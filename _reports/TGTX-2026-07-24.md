**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TG Therapeutics is a single-product commercial-stage biotech whose entire equity story rests on BRIUMVI (ublituximab-xiiy), an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody for relapsing multiple sclerosis. The commercial ramp has been among the strongest specialty-pharma launches of the past several years: US net product revenue went from $310M in FY2024 to $594M in FY2025, and Q1 2026 total revenue of $204.9M grew roughly 70% year over year. The company turned GAAP-profitable, and consensus EPS estimates have been revised up meaningfully across every window — the current-year figure moved from $1.24 to $1.61 (+29.7%) and next year from $2.46 to $3.28 (+33.2%) over 90 days. That combination of ~70% revenue growth and >30% estimate revisions historically precedes further re-rating, and it is the central reason this is not a bearish call.

The offsetting concern is quality-of-earnings and balance-sheet posture. Despite reporting positive net income, operating cash flow remained negative at -$17.9M in Q1 2026, driven by an accounts-receivable balance that has grown to $392M as the company extends payment terms to specialty distributors. In March 2026, management repaid its $250M term loan and drew a new $750M facility from Blue Owl, a net ~$500M non-dilutive raise that lifted debt/equity from ~0.64x to ~1.28x, and simultaneously raised its buyback authorization from $100M to $300M, executing $100M immediately at ~$30. Levering a single-product cash-flow base to repurchase stock signals conviction but removes the cushion a one-product biotech typically wants to carry.

The near-term price driver is the subcutaneous ublituximab Phase 3 program — enrollment complete, topline data expected around year-end 2026 or Q1 2027, with encouraging early data reported mid-2026. This is a genuine binary. Success defends BRIUMVI's franchise against self-administered competitors (Kesimpta, subcutaneous Ocrevus); a pharmaceutical-equivalence miss (a real technical risk per the 10-Q) removes a key growth leg. With the stock at $54.87, up ~48% over the trailing year, a $73 median analyst target, and a BofA Underperform at $20, this is a high-dispersion name where the durability, pricing, and valuation debate is unresolved. We rate it a growth-oriented HOLD.

## Company Overview

TG Therapeutics operates as a fully-integrated, single-segment commercial biotech headquartered in Morrisville, NC, with roughly 399 employees. It is effectively a one-product company: virtually all revenue derives from BRIUMVI, FDA-approved in December 2022 and launched in January 2023 for relapsing forms of MS.

BRIUMVI's clinical differentiation is administration convenience within the infusion segment — a one-hour IV twice yearly after the starting dose, versus the longer infusion time for Roche's Ocrevus. Five-year open-label ULTIMATE I/II extension data published in *JAMA Neurology* (February 2026) support durable efficacy for long-term positioning.

The business model carries several structural features that shape the investment case. BRIUMVI is in-licensed from LFB Group (2012), with mid-to-high-single-digit royalties on worldwide net sales flowing through COGS (~$20.6M in Q1 2026 alone). TGTX does not manufacture the drug; it relies on Samsung Biologics (primary) and FUJIFILM Diosynth (secondary), both sole-source and ex-US. Ex-US commercialization is licensed to Neuraxpharm in a deal worth up to $645M plus tiered royalties, which means TGTX does not control international pricing — a vulnerability under most-favored-nation pricing frameworks. CEO and Executive Chairman Michael S. Weiss has led the company since 2012.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** The commercial ramp has been exceptional:

|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $2.8M | — (pre-launch) |
| FY2023 | $233.7M | — |
| FY2024 | $329.0M | $310.0M |
| FY2025 | $616.3M | $594.1M |
| Q1 2026 | $204.9M | $194.8M |

Q1 2026 product revenue was $201.3M ($194.8M US plus $6.5M Neuraxpharm).

**Profitability requires care in interpretation.** FY2025 GAAP net income of $447.2M was inflated by a one-time $339.8M deferred-tax-asset valuation-allowance release booked in Q3 2025; underlying pre-tax income was roughly $107M. Q1 2026 is the cleaner picture: operating income of $34.8M (~17% operating margin), net income of $19.8M ($0.12 diluted), after a $9.2M debt-extinguishment loss. The Q1 effective tax rate was only 2.9% due to R&D credits and timing; with the DTA released, GAAP tax expense will normalize toward statutory levels over the coming years, which will dampen reported EPS growth even as pre-tax profit rises. Investors should anchor on operating income rather than headline net income.

**Margin dynamics are the core operational story.** COGS is normalizing higher: pre-approval inventory expensed to R&D before 2022 approval was depleted mid-Q1 2025, so gross margin is stepping down to reflect true manufacturing plus LFB royalties (Q1 2026 COGS ~16.6% of product revenue). Meanwhile SG&A surged to $88.2M from $50.3M a year earlier (+75%), driven by aggressive direct-to-consumer and TV advertising and commercial expansion. This is a deliberate land-grab that compresses margins now in pursuit of durable share.

**Cash flow is the yellow flag.** Despite GAAP profitability, operating cash flow was -$17.9M in Q1 2026 (improved from -$28.7M) and -$24.8M for FY2025. The driver is a relentless accounts-receivable build — $392M at 3/31/26 versus $306M at YE2025 and $129M at YE2024 — because TGTX offers extended payment terms to align distributor payments with payer reimbursement timing. Profits are real, but cash conversion is the metric to monitor. The company can carry this given its $572.8M cash-and-investments position, but a deterioration in distributor credit or reimbursement timing would pressure liquidity.

**Balance sheet re-levered in March 2026.** The refinancing drew a term loan maturing March 2031 at SOFR + ~4.75% floating, with $37.5M quarterly amortization beginning Q1 2030 (deferrable if leverage is low) and total facility capacity up to $1B. Loan payable now stands at $745M against equity of $583M. The facility is secured by substantially all assets and prohibits dividends. The FY2025 Altman Z of ~3.3 is comfortable, but the leverage rests on a single product's cash flows.

**Earnings surprise track record.** The company has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 -40.1%, Q4 2025 -60.9%, Q3 2025 -28.7%, Q2 2025 -23.3%). The misses reflect heavy commercial reinvestment (DTC advertising) and the model's evolving cost structure rather than revenue weakness — top-line growth has been robust throughout. The recurring pattern warrants caution: current consensus assumes a step-up in profitability that management has repeatedly undershot on the bottom line.

Revisions of this magnitude are among the stronger forward indicators available. The next-year figure is accelerating (+14.2% in the last 30 days alone), which typically signals consensus is still catching up to reality. We weight this positive signal materially in the growth score.

The pipeline provides additional optionality, all early relative to the core franchise:
- **SubQ ublituximab Phase 3** — enrollment complete (April 2026); topline data expected YE2026/Q1 2027, with positive early data reported June 2026. This is the pivotal near-term catalyst.
- **ENHANCE** (simplified IV dosing) Phase 3b — enrollment complete October 2025.
- **Azer-cel CAR-T** in progressive MS — first patient dosed August 2025; Milestone Event 1 achieved February 2026.
- **Ublituximab in myasthenia gravis** — enrolling.
- **Schizophrenia Phase 2** — initiated July 2026, a novel indication expansion for the anti-CD20 mechanism.

The strategic logic is to build a broader B-cell/CNS franchise beyond a single indication. That optionality is real but unproven and years from revenue. The durable near-term growth case rests on BRIUMVI's US ramp and defending share via the SubQ program.

## Valuation Assessment

At $54.87, TGTX trades at roughly 17x current-year consensus EPS, and the profile cites a trailing/forward P/E of ~19.3x. On next-year consensus of $3.28, the forward multiple compresses to ~17x — reasonable, arguably cheap, for a company growing revenue ~70% with rising estimates. On a PEG basis the stock screens attractively given the growth rate.

Peer comparison is of limited use here because the biotech comp set is dominated by pre-profit names — RGEN at 146.5x, and IBRX, APGE, PRAX, and KYMR all with negative earnings. TGTX is one of the few profitable names in the group, so its multiple is not directly comparable to a peer median distorted by loss-makers. The more relevant frame is a profitable specialty pharma growing at this clip, against which ~17x forward looks undemanding on the surface.

We temper enthusiasm for three reasons. First, cheapness on forward EPS assumes the profitability step-up materializes, and the company has missed EPS four quarters running while spending aggressively on DTC. Second, GAAP tax normalization toward statutory rates will erode reported EPS growth relative to the pre-tax trajectory, so the "17x forward" figure flatters the story if consensus underweights the tax headwind. Third, the reported earnings are not converting to cash — a stock that looks cheap on accrual EPS but burns operating cash on a growing receivables base is a lower-quality version of "cheap." The analyst dispersion captures the debate: median target $73, high $83, mean $64, but a BofA Underperform at $20. The large gap between the low and median target signals that at least one serious shop believes the pricing/durability risks dwarf the growth. We do not treat the compressed multiple as an unambiguous buy signal, and we assign a mid-range valuation score reflecting genuine two-sidedness rather than clear mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The RMS disease-modifying-therapy market is large but crowded, and the competitive battleground is shifting toward route and frequency of administration. The anti-CD20 class (Ocrevus, Kesimpta, BRIUMVI) is the standard-of-care growth engine. Kesimpta (self-injected monthly at home) and subcutaneous Ocrevus are pulling patients toward self-administration, which is why TGTX's SubQ ublituximab program is strategically central — the IV twice-yearly profile is competitive for infusion-preferring patients but leaves the franchise exposed if it cannot offer a self-administered option.

Longer-term, BTK inhibitors and CD40L agents could disrupt the entire anti-CD20 class if they demonstrate superior efficacy or safety. Competitors (Roche, Novartis) have vastly greater resources. BRIUMVI's share gains to date are real and impressive, but the company is a challenger defending against much larger incumbents, and its competitive trajectory hinges on the SubQ readout. The pipeline pivot into MG, progressive MS, and schizophrenia is an attempt to broaden the moat, but these are early and speculative. We score competitive position slightly above neutral: the trajectory is favorable given share capture, but the moat is neither wide nor secure against well-funded rivals and a possible mechanism shift.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-product concentration.** 100% of revenue is BRIUMVI. Any safety signal, competitive displacement, manufacturing disruption, or reimbursement setback is existential.

**Patent/exclusivity.** Composition-of-matter patent expires 2029 (US); newer patents claim protection to 2042; BPCIA biologic exclusivity runs to December 2034. The enforceability of the post-2029 patents against biosimilars is the key long-term uncertainty.

**Drug-pricing overhang, newly elevated and MS-specific.** The 10-Q explicitly flags that CMS's proposed GUARD (Part D) and GLOBE (Part B) models name MS CNS agents for additional rebates. IRA Medicare negotiation, MFN/international reference pricing (April 2026 executive order), and Neuraxpharm's lower ex-US prices that could become MFN reference points are direct threats to US net pricing. TGTX has less control over its pricing narrative than a typical US-centric biotech.

**Tariffs/supply chain.** The April 2026 executive order threatening up to 100% tariffs on imported patented pharmaceuticals is a genuine risk — Samsung Biologics, the sole bulk-drug-substance supplier, is ex-US, and tariffs are an inventoriable cost. Not yet material, but explicitly disclosed.

**SubQ technical risk.** Subcutaneous ublituximab faces viscosity, stability, immunogenicity, and PK/PD-equivalence hurdles. Even if the exposure endpoint is met, regulators may not deem it pharmaceutically equivalent. This is the fulcrum of the thesis and a binary event in the next two to four quarters.

**Customer concentration.** Sales flow through ~4 specialty distributors: Customer 1 = 42%, Customer 2 = 27%, Customer 3 = 20% of gross revenue (top three ~89%). Concentrated receivables and channel risk.

**Negative operating cash flow.** Extended payment terms delay cash conversion; a deterioration in distributor credit or reimbursement timing could pressure liquidity.

**Leverage.** The $750M secured loan restricts M&A, additional debt, and dividends, and could accelerate on covenant breach — heightened by single-product cash flows.

**Manufacturing quality.** A $6.2M inventory reserve was taken in Q4 2025 for a manufacturing deviation on one bulk-drug-substance batch, a reminder of CMO-dependency risk.

The concentration of existential single-product, pricing, and binary-catalyst risks, several of which are freshly elevated and not fully priced given the wide analyst dispersion, supports a below-neutral risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 67.1% against 47.3% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 82nd percentile within the current chain, and IV ticked up 2.9 points over the last week even as the price held. The term structure is in backwardation (67.1% near-term vs 56.8% long-term), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±14.6%, consistent with an event-driven setup. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call volume ratio of 0.16, put/call OI of 0.38, and a -4.1% put/call skew, indicating traders are leaning toward upside rather than hedging downside. The combination of rich IV, backwardation, and call-heavy positioning suggests the options market expects a near-term catalyst with an upside bias, but the elevated volatility argues against chasing entry ahead of the event; waiting for a post-catalyst IV crush would be a better entry mechanic for a new position.

Separately, short interest sits at 28.5% of float with 9.8 days to cover. This is a crowded book that creates two-sided reflexivity: a positive SubQ readout could force a violent squeeze, while a disappointment would validate the bears and accelerate downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$83).** BRIUMVI's US ramp continues at a strong pace, DTC spending converts to durable share, and consensus continues to revise upward as the profitability inflection materializes. Positive SubQ pivotal data lands YE2026/Q1 2027, defending the franchise against Kesimpta and subcutaneous Ocrevus and extending the growth runway. Debt-funded buybacks at ~$30 prove accretive, the receivables build normalizes as distributor terms stabilize, and the 28.5% short base is forced to cover. At ~17x a rising forward EPS base with a de-risked SubQ leg, the multiple re-rates toward the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$20).** The SubQ program misses pharmaceutical equivalence, removing a key growth leg and exposing BRIUMVI to accelerating share loss as the market shifts to self-administration. CMS GUARD/GLOBE and MFN reference pricing compress US net prices for MS agents, GAAP tax normalization erodes reported EPS, and the persistent operating-cash burn on a $392M-and-rising receivables base forces a liquidity reassessment against $745M of secured debt. The four-quarter EPS miss streak continues, and the market re-rates to reflect that a single-product biotech levered to buy back its own stock carries no margin for error. This is roughly the BofA view.

The base case sits between these: continued strong BRIUMVI growth with a mixed-to-positive SubQ outcome, tempered by pricing and cash-conversion headwinds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** The forward growth signal — ~70% revenue growth plus +30% estimate revisions — is strong enough to preclude an AVOID, but the binary SubQ catalyst, the re-levered balance sheet funding buybacks, negative operating cash flow, MS-specific pricing risk, and extreme analyst dispersion preclude a clean BUY at $54.87 near the 52-week high.

**Reassessment timeline.** The pivotal reassessment point is the SubQ ublituximab topline data, expected YE2026/Q1 2027. The next quarterly print is the interim checkpoint for revenue growth, SG&A trajectory, and — critically — operating cash flow.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive, unambiguous SubQ pivotal data demonstrating pharmaceutical equivalence, de-risking the franchise's defense against self-administered competitors.
- Operating cash flow turning positive or the receivables build stabilizing (AR growth decelerating materially below revenue growth), confirming the earnings are converting to cash.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that widens the risk/reward while fundamentals hold, or a first EPS beat that ends the four-quarter miss streak.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SubQ data missing the equivalence endpoint or a regulatory setback on the SubQ path.
- CMS finalizing GUARD/GLOBE or MFN pricing in a manner that quantifiably compresses BRIUMVI US net pricing.
- Revenue growth decelerating below ~25% YoY, or a covenant/liquidity concern emerging from the Blue Owl facility as cash burn persists.
- Estimate revisions turning negative, signaling the profitability inflection is stalling.

## Conclusion

TG Therapeutics offers one of the more compelling top-line growth profiles in profitable biotech, with BRIUMVI compounding at ~70% and consensus estimates rising more than 30% across every window — a forward signal we weight heavily and one that keeps this out of AVOID territory despite the risks. The offsets are substantial and specific: a binary SubQ readout that the market is clearly positioning around, a balance sheet re-levered to ~1.28x D/E partly to fund buybacks, four consecutive EPS misses, negative operating cash flow on a swelling receivables base, and an MS-specific drug-pricing overhang that CMS has named explicitly. The gap between the BofA $20 Underperform and the $73 median target, alongside 28.5% short interest, captures a genuinely unresolved debate.

At ~17x rising forward EPS the stock is not expensive on accrual earnings, but the cheapness is contingent on a profitability inflection the company has repeatedly undershot and on cash conversion that has not yet arrived. We recommend HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. Long-oriented investors comfortable with the SubQ binary and the leverage should look to accumulate on weakness toward the low-$40s or after a de-risking data event rather than chase near the 52-week high into rich, backwardated implied volatility.
