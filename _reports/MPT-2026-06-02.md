**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medical Properties Trust is a hospital-focused net-lease REIT working through the aftermath of the Steward Health Care and Prospect Medical bankruptcies that drove a ~$2.4B net loss in 2024 and forced a ~60% dividend cut in 2023. The Q1 2026 supplemental shows the recovery thesis is now visible in the numbers: total revenue rose 12.6% YoY to $252.1M, cash rent billed climbed roughly 20% as re-tenanted properties resumed paying, impairment charges shrank to $19M from $76M a year earlier, and the dividend was raised to $0.09/quarter. The company posted its first GAAP-positive quarter ($0.05 EPS) in some time, though that headline was aided by a $32.8M income-tax benefit against just $0.3M of pre-tax income; the cleaner normalized FFO figure was roughly flat YoY at $0.14/share.

The binding constraint is the balance sheet, not operations. Net debt is $9.66B against EBITDAre that implies ~9.3x leverage, adjusted interest coverage is just 1.8x, and the Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone. There is a $1.24B maturity in 2026 and $1.6B in 2027, and recent capital was raised at punitive rates (7.0% and 8.5% secured notes due 2032). The equity is effectively a leveraged claim on the company refinancing and de-levering successfully while the surviving tenant book continues to stabilize.

This report carries two prior AVOID ratings. The case for moving to HOLD is the convergence of three things: the operational trajectory has turned constructive (growing revenue, rising estimates, falling impairments, a raised dividend, three beats in four quarters), the price-target distribution is centered on the current price (analyst median $5.00 = spot), and the bear thesis is heavily crowded (29.7% short interest, 23.9 days to cover) and difficult to monetize from here. The setup is genuinely two-sided rather than negatively asymmetric, which is the definition of a HOLD. We do not see enough to underwrite a BUY given the leverage and refinancing binary, but the prior negative-asymmetry call is no longer supported by the evidence.

## Company Overview

Medical Properties Trust (NYSE: MPT) is a self-advised REIT founded in 2003 and headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama. It is one of the largest owners of hospital real estate globally, operating a net-lease and mortgage-financing model that lets hospital operators monetize their real estate to fund operations.

As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio comprised 378 properties, roughly 38,000 licensed beds, and 51 operators across 30 U.S. states and 9 countries, with total assets of $14.76B. By asset type, general acute care hospitals are 58.8% of assets (62% of revenue), behavioral health is 16.3%, and post-acute care is 11.3%. The portfolio is roughly 50/50 U.S./international by assets. The single largest geography is the UK at 27.7% of assets and 38% of revenue, with additional exposure to Switzerland, Germany, Spain, Finland, and Italy. Top operators include Circle Health (UK, 14% of assets, 21.8% of revenue), Priory Group (UK behavioral, 8.6%), Healthcare Systems of America (8.2%, which absorbed former Steward hospitals), Swiss Medical Network (5.9%), and Lifepoint Behavioral (5.5%).

MPT's stated competitive edge is hospital-specific underwriting (it underwrites the real estate for re-tenantability by any competent operator rather than the current tenant) layered with absolute net master leases. That model was tested by the Steward and Prospect failures, and the surviving book is the result of an extensive re-tenanting process.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (Q1 2026).** The revenue increase reflects re-tenanted Steward/Prospect hospitals coming back online, with rent billed rising to $197.5M from $165.2M. Net income was $32.8M versus a loss a year earlier, but pre-tax income was only $0.3M; the GAAP profit was driven by the income-tax benefit. Normalized FFO was $82.2M, essentially flat against $81.1M a year ago. Cash rent is recovering while normalized earnings power has stabilized rather than begun to grow.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is $9.79B, with a weighted-average rate of 5.36% and 91% fixed. Financial leverage is 59.4% of gross assets. Cash is $425M. Total equity of $4.55B sits against a retained deficit of $4.16B, the accumulated scar from years of impairments. Currency mix is 59% USD, 21% GBP, 19% EUR, and AOCI swung -$44.5M in the quarter on FX.

**Cash flow quality.** The improvement to focus on is cash rent billed (+20% YoY), not headline EPS. Two earnings-quality flags warrant monitoring: the $904M straight-line rent receivable, which has been a recurring source of write-offs when tenants fail, and the reliance on tax benefits and FFO adjustments in the GAAP headline.

**Deleveraging execution.** Management repaid $200M of the term loan on April 1, 2026, and continues asset sales, loan repayments, and capital recycling. The raised dividend (~$0.36 annualized) sits against a NFFO run-rate near $0.56, a ~64% payout — covered, and a deliberate signal of confidence after the prior cut from $0.29.

## Growth Analysis

The growth here is recovery, not organic expansion. Revenue growth of 12.6% and cash rent up 20% reflect previously non-paying re-tenanted assets resuming rent, plus contractual escalators on the surviving book. Impairments collapsing from $76M to $19M suggests the worst of the write-down cycle is passing.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year consensus EPS moved to $0.11 from $0.08 thirty days ago (+37.5%) and next year to $0.17 from $0.14 (+22.2%), although the very-near-term quarter was trimmed to roughly breakeven. Three of the last four quarters beat (one large miss in Q3 2025 driven by a -$0.13 print). The development pipeline is $177M committed (IMED Spain, HSA Florida, NOR California), modest relative to a $14.76B asset base.

The signal to weigh against the recovery is that normalized FFO is flat. The re-tenanting fills the revenue line, but until refinancing is resolved on acceptable terms and leverage falls, per-share cash earnings power is unlikely to inflect materially. This is a stabilization story with a path to growth contingent on the balance sheet, not a clean earnings inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $4.99, MPT trades near 9x a ~$0.56 NFFO run-rate and offers a ~7.0% dividend yield at a ~64% payout. The peer set in Health Care REITs shows a median P/E of 32.1 and a median yield of 6.0% (LTC 14.7x/6.1%, SBRA 31.6x/6.0%, UHT 32.1x/7.2%, HR -34.1x/4.8%, AHR 84.9x/2.0%). MPT's above-peer yield and depressed multiple reflect its leverage and refinancing risk; the discount is rational, not a clean mispricing. On price-to-book, the ~$3.0B market cap against $4.55B of equity is roughly 0.66x, but book is materially impaired and not a reliable anchor.

The analyst distribution is informative: median target $5.00 (equal to spot), mean $5.79, high $8.00, low $5.00. A bull outcome (successful refinancing at manageable cost, continued de-levering, the empty hospitals leased) supports a re-rating toward $6.50-7.50 (recovering NFFO plus modest multiple expansion). A bear outcome (refinancing at distressed rates, renewed tenant stress, or a dividend reset) pushes the equity toward $3.00-3.50. Those scenarios are roughly symmetric around the current price, and the center of the distribution sits closer to $5 than to either tail. That balance is why the valuation score lands at the midpoint rather than supporting either a BUY or an AVOID.

## Competitive Landscape

Hospital REITs are entirely dependent on tenant operating health, and the 2023-2025 period exposed the structural weakness of financing thinly-capitalized operators (Steward, Prospect) at lease coverage that proved unsustainable. The surviving book looks healthier: TTM EBITDARM rent coverage is now ~2.5x portfolio-wide, with general acute at ~3.0x and behavioral the weakest segment. The international assets (Circle Health and Priory in the UK, the Swiss Infracore JV, the German MEDIAN JV) have performed more steadily and now anchor revenue.

Versus peers (Welltower, Ventas, Healthpeak, Omega, Sabra), MPT is distinct in its concentrated hospital focus and heavy international footprint. The footprint diversifies away from U.S. Medicaid policy risk (the newly-cited OBBBA Medicaid funding cuts) but adds FX volatility. Tenant concentration remains a watch item, with Circle Health at 21.8% of revenue. The competitive trajectory is improving off a low base, though the model's vulnerability to operator distress is now a permanent part of how the market prices the equity.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing and maturity wall.** The single biggest swing factor. The 2026 maturity includes €500M notes and the $666M revolver; the 2026 facility was extended to June 2027 "subject to conditions." With 1.8x coverage and a distress-zone Z-Score, the cost and availability of refinancing dominates the equity outcome. The recent secured issuance illustrates the distressed cost of capital.

**Tenant credit and concentration.** Post-Steward/Prospect re-tenanting has stabilized the book, but Domestic Operator 3 (behavioral, $806M investment) shows weak 1.2x coverage, and two hospitals are being held with no identified tenant, marketed for sale or lease. Prospect collateral recovery depends on litigation outcomes with uncertain timing and value.

**Earnings quality.** The $904M straight-line rent receivable and the tax-benefit-driven GAAP profit mean headline figures overstate the underlying cash story; track cash rent and NFFO.

**Governance and alignment.** CEO 2025 total comp of $15.9M ("comp actually paid" $16.6M) rose even as 5-year indexed TSR badly lagged peers (37.95 vs. 163.81) against cumulative losses. There is no open-market insider buying at a $5 price — conspicuous for a recovery thesis — with recent Form 4 activity limited to tax-withholding on vesting.

**What is already priced.** Much of the above is reflected. The stock is down roughly two-thirds from pre-2022 levels, short interest is 29.7% of float with 23.9 days to cover, and the Z-Score already signals distress. The incremental downside surprise from known risks is therefore limited, and the crowded short with demonstrated squeeze capacity (a Feb 2026 spike to $6.47) makes the bear case hard to monetize.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute distress. Near-term ATM IV is 32.2% against 36.1% historical volatility (cheap, -3.9% spread), the term structure is in contango (32.2% near vs. 38.4% at six months), put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.7%), and put/call open interest is 0.60 (call-tilted). The implied move at the nearest expiry is only ±2.5%. A 3-month put/call volume ratio of 10.0 is a small-sample outlier not corroborated by the broader skew or OI picture. The derivatives market is treating MPT as a stabilizing, range-bound situation, consistent with a balanced HOLD rather than a stock the market expects to break lower.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($6.50-7.50).** Refinancing of the 2026-2027 maturities closes at manageable cost, de-levering continues, the two empty hospitals are leased, and cash rent keeps recovering toward a growing NFFO. The sustained 7% yield plus continued re-tenanting drives a re-rating, and the 29.7% short base provides squeeze fuel on any positive surprise.

**Bear case ($3.00-3.50).** Refinancing comes at punitive rates that further compress coverage from 1.8x, a tenant in the soft-spot cohort (the 1.2x-coverage behavioral operator, or an unleased asset) deteriorates, the straight-line receivable produces another write-off, and the dividend is reset. OBBBA Medicaid cuts pressure U.S. tenant economics, and the distress-zone Z-Score proves prescient.

The center of the distribution is closer to the current price than to either extreme, and the analyst median target equals spot. Positive and negative roughly cancel, the defining feature of a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026) and any 2026/2027 refinancing announcement are the natural checkpoints. The maturity wall makes the next 12-18 months decisive.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Refinancing or extension of the 2026-2027 maturities at a blended rate at or below the current 5.36% weighted-average cost, demonstrating restored capital-markets access.
- Net debt/EBITDAre falling below ~8x through asset sales and de-levering, with adjusted interest coverage rising above ~2.2x.
- Portfolio EBITDARM coverage holding above 2.2x with the soft-spot operators (the 1.2x behavioral tenant, the two unleased hospitals) resolved, plus open-market insider buying as a conviction tell.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Refinancing only achievable at rates near or above the recent 7.0-8.5% secured issuance, signaling a deteriorating cost-of-capital spiral.
- A new tenant default or a material straight-line rent write-off, or NFFO turning down sequentially.
- A dividend reset, which would signal management no longer views cash flow as covering the distribution.

## Conclusion

MPT is a deleveraging turnaround in which operations have genuinely stabilized while the balance sheet remains the dominant, unresolved variable: 9.3x leverage, 1.8x coverage, a $2.84B 2026-2027 maturity wall, and a distressed Z-Score. The equity is a leveraged claim on refinancing and continued de-levering at a distressed cost of capital.

The two prior AVOID ratings asserted negative asymmetry. The current evidence does not support that. Revenue is growing rather than declining, estimates are broadly rising, the price-target distribution is centered on spot, the bull/bear outcomes are roughly symmetric around $5, and the bear thesis is crowded with options pricing no acute distress. The honest call is **HOLD**: the balance-sheet tail risk is real but heavily reflected, and the operational trajectory has turned constructive. We would need refinancing clarity and de-levering below ~8x to move constructive, and a refinancing at punitive rates or a new tenant default to turn negative.
