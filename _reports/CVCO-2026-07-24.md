**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cavco Industries is one of three producers that together control more than 86% of U.S. factory-built (manufactured and modular) housing production, alongside Berkshire's Clayton Homes and Skyline Champion. The company runs a vertically integrated model: 33 production lines feeding independent dealers and 99 company-owned retail stores, complemented by a captive mortgage originator (CountryPlace) and a property-and-casualty insurer (Standard Casualty). FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) revenue grew 11.4% to $2.24B, net income rose to $190.6M, and the company generated $232M of free cash flow while absorbing a $179.9M all-cash acquisition without touching its revolver. This is a high-quality business in an attractive industry structure.

The near-term investment case is more balanced than the quality of the franchise suggests. Roughly two-thirds of FY2026's pretax income growth came not from core homebuilding but from a weather-driven doubling of the financial-services segment margin (28.9% to 58.0%) on benign catastrophe experience, a swing factor that could reverse in a normal weather year. Core factory-built gross margin compressed (22.9% to 22.1% full-year; 23.6% to 21.7% in Q3) as unit costs outran pricing. Mid-year backlog fell roughly 29% year-over-year to $160M before recovering to $195M by fiscal year-end, an unresolved tension between reported double-digit growth (flattered by M&A and price) and softer organic order pace. Consensus EPS estimates have been trimmed across every window over the past 90 days.

At $562-573, CVCO trades at roughly 23x forward earnings against homebuilder peers at a 10-14x median, with no dividend. That premium is defensible on the strength of the balance sheet, the oligopoly structure, and higher returns on capital, but it is not a mispricing. The stock has round-tripped from a ~$713 January high through a ~$397 low and back to the mid-$500s, and the current level offers no clear asymmetry. We maintain a HOLD, framed as a growth hold: the durable compounding and debt-free consolidation optionality are worth owning, but the near-term data do not support upgrading ahead of the July 30 Q1 FY2027 print.

## Company Overview

Cavco operates two segments. **Factory-built housing (~96% of revenue)** produces HUD-code manufactured homes, modular homes, park-model RVs, cabins, and commercial modular structures across 33 lines (31 U.S., 2 in Ojinaga, Mexico), each serving a roughly 350-mile radius. Distribution is ~77% through independent dealers and the balance through 99 company-owned stores, 62 of them in Texas. The target buyer earns under ~$90K, skews first-time/move-up or 55+, and is highly rate-sensitive. **Financial services (~4% of revenue, high margin)** consists of CountryPlace (Fannie/Freddie/Ginnie-approved mortgage origination and servicing, including chattel/home-only loans) and Standard Casualty (P&C insurance concentrated in TX/AZ/NM/NV).

The industry structure is the central asset. The top three producers control more than 86% of production, a consolidated oligopoly with scale-driven procurement, distribution breadth, and captive finance economics that create high entry barriers. Cavco names Clayton and Champion as larger, better-capitalized rivals, and the sector's dominant financing chokepoint, 21st Mortgage/Vanderbilt, is a Clayton/Berkshire affiliate that is simultaneously a competitor and a floor-plan-financing supplier to Cavco's independent dealers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2023 $2.14B → FY2024 $1.79B (rate-driven downturn) → FY2025 $2.02B → FY2026 $2.24B (+11.4%). The FY2026 recovery came from +5.5% homes sold (20,842 units), +5.8% revenue per home ($103,510), and ~$90.5M contributed by American Homestar (acquired September 29, 2025).

**Margins and profitability.** Consolidated gross margin was 23.5%, up from 23.1%. The composition matters more than the headline. Factory-built gross margin compressed to 22.1% from 22.9% as per-unit costs outran price. Financial-services gross margin nearly doubled to 58.0% from 28.9%, and that segment's pretax income swung from $1.5M to $23.3M, roughly two-thirds of the total $33.6M year-over-year pretax income increase. The primary driver was favorable weather reducing Standard Casualty catastrophe claims, which is not a durable structural gain. Net income was $190.6M (net margin 8.5%), ROE 17.3%, ROA 12.8%.

**Balance sheet.** This is the strongest part of the story and the source of the elevated Financial Health score. Altman Z-Score 4.48 (safe zone), D/E 0.35, equity/assets 0.74. There is essentially no funded debt: the $75M revolver (expandable to $150M) is undrawn, and interest expense is negligible (~$0.5M, all finance leases). Covenant capacity (3.25x leverage) is enormous against zero borrowings. Cash ended at $236.7M (down from $356M) after paying $172.8M cash for American Homestar with no debt. Negative working-capital-to-assets is structural and benign (customer deposits, warranty reserves, unearned premiums, and volume rebates in current liabilities) with a current ratio near 2.4x.

**Cash flow quality.** Operating cash flow of $267.5M exceeded net income, a positive earnings-quality signal. FCF was $232M (10.3% FCF margin), roughly a 5.3% FCF yield on the ~$4.4B market cap. Capital return is entirely buyback-driven: ~$130M repurchased in the first nine months of FY2026, with $98M remaining on the $150M authorization as of late December. There is no dividend, and diluted share count has fallen from 8.9M (FY2023) to ~7.9M, amplifying EPS growth above net-income growth.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Over the last four quarters, three beats and one miss: +13.4% (Jun-25), +5.4% (Sep-25), then a -12.6% miss (Dec-25), then a modest +1.0% beat (Mar-26). The December miss coincides with the mid-year backlog trough and warrants attention to whether forward visibility is deteriorating.

## Growth Analysis

Cavco is outgrowing a flat industry. MHI shipments held near 103,000 units in CY2025, roughly unchanged year-over-year, so reported double-digit revenue growth is being manufactured through price, share gains, and acquisition rather than end-market expansion. That is a legitimate strategy in a fragmented long tail below the top three, and the debt-free balance sheet gives Cavco ample consolidation firepower.

The near-term growth signals are mixed, which is why Growth Prospects sits in the mid-5s rather than higher. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across all windows: current quarter -3.4% (30d and 90d), next quarter -7.4%, current year -4.2% (90d), next year -2.0%. This is negative earnings momentum, not the positive revision velocity that would signal re-acceleration. The unresolved question is whether organic demand is genuinely re-accelerating or reported growth is flattered by American Homestar and pricing while unit volume softens. The July 30 Q1 FY2027 print is the key data point.

Longer-term structural tailwinds remain intact. Manufactured housing is ~13.1% of new single-family homes sold and is the affordability answer to a national housing shortage; both the young-adult and 55+ demographics are growing. The persistent structural lid is the absence of a secondary market for chattel/home-only loans and continued GSE reluctance to purchase them, the single biggest constraint on the sector's addressable market. Cavco's push to expand CountryPlace's own home-only lending, including a forward-flow agreement selling ≥$25M/quarter to a third party, is a deliberate effort to capture more of the value chain and reduce dependence on competitor-controlled financing.

## Valuation Assessment

CVCO trades at roughly 23x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $23.98) at ~$573, against a homebuilding peer median P/E of 13.4x. Same-industry comparables: SKY 22.0x, TPH 22.2x, MTH 13.4x, MHO 10.7x, KBH 10.4x. Even against the higher-multiple factory-built peer (Skyline Champion at 22.0x), Cavco carries no discount and no dividend, versus MTH (2.7% yield) and KBH (1.9%).

The premium to the pure-play production homebuilders (MTH/MHO/KBH) is partly justified: those are land-and-build cyclical developers with different balance-sheet risk, whereas Cavco is a debt-free, capital-light factory operation with captive finance and insurance economics and higher, more stable returns on capital. But the closest structural comps (SKY, TPH) already sit at ~22x, so Cavco is fully valued within its own cohort rather than mispriced.

A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is wrong. Here, the evidence points the other way: falling estimates, weather-flattered FY2026 earnings that inflate the "E" in the denominator, and core homebuilding margin compression. The forward multiple is not compressed relative to a rising estimate stream; estimates are declining. Analyst targets (mean $625, median $600, low $575, high $700) sit only modestly above the current price and are not a reliable floor given the negative revision trend; on a stock with falling estimates and no clear re-acceleration, treating proximity to targets as upside is unwarranted. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a fairly-to-fully-priced stock without an identified catalyst, not a value opportunity. There is a value-trap caution embedded here: cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way, and CVCO is not even cheap, so there is no margin of safety on the multiple.

Normalizing for weather (backing out the ~$22M pretax swing in financial services), consolidated earnings power is somewhat lower than the reported figure, which pushes the true forward multiple modestly higher than 23x. That argues for patience rather than deployment at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is genuine. Three producers control >86% of output, with rational pricing, scale procurement, and distribution breadth creating durable barriers. Cavco's vertical integration, with CountryPlace and Standard Casualty capturing financing and insurance economics that competitors' buyers otherwise hand to third parties, is a real differentiator and doubles as a partial hedge against the industry's chronic chattel-financing gap. The proxy's cumulative TSR of 201 vs. a peer index of 128 evidences that the quality has compounded.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than sharply strengthening. Cavco is gaining share through M&A and price in a flat industry, and the American Homestar deal deepened its South-Central footprint (2 plants, 19 retail locations). Offsetting this: Clayton and Champion are larger and better capitalized, and the Berkshire/Clayton finance affiliate controls a financing chokepoint that Cavco's dealers depend on. Core factory-built margin compression indicates that pricing power is not currently expanding. The 6.8 competitive-position score reflects a strong, defensible position that is improving at the margin through consolidation, tempered by the fact that the two larger rivals set the competitive terms and the financing constraint caps the whole industry's ceiling.

## Risk Assessment

**Chattel financing bottleneck.** The absence of an efficient secondary market for home-only loans, and GSE plans through 2027 that still exclude such purchases, structurally caps industry demand. That a competitor (21st Mortgage/Berkshire) controls a key floor-plan financing channel to Cavco's dealers is a competitive dependency.

**Weather-normalization risk.** A normal weather year could pressure consolidated earnings even if home sales hold, a risk the market's forward multiple may not fully discount.

**Backlog and order-pace visibility.** The mid-year backlog decline followed by fiscal-year-end recovery, combined with the December earnings miss and falling forward estimates, points to demand visibility that is less firm than the reported growth suggests.

**Contingent repurchase obligations.** Maximum exposure ~$141-148M under distributor floor-plan repurchase agreements against only a ~$3.7M reserve, a risk that scales in a declining-price or dealer-distress scenario.

**Concentration.** Texas houses 62 of 99 retail stores, multiple plants, 47% of the consumer-loan book, and a TX-heavy insurance book, creating hurricane/storm exposure. Goodwill is now ~14% of assets ($207.8M post-acquisition), with impairment risk if manufacturing cash flows weaken.

**Other.** Mexico/tariff exposure on two production lines; Ginnie/Freddie servicing-advance obligations; and an unretrieved October 28, 2025 8-K (Item 5.02 officer/director change) that should be confirmed for whether it was a routine board matter or an operational-leadership departure.

The 5.5 risk score reflects that most of these are known and partly priced, but the combination of weather-normalization, backlog softening, and falling estimates introduces near-term downside that the full multiple does not obviously cushion. There is no single binary catalyst, which keeps the score from falling into the ≤4 range.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.1% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 47.6% (fair, spread 1.5%), so options are not richly or cheaply priced on an absolute basis. The term structure is in backwardation (49.1% at one month versus 41.8% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the July 30 Q1 FY2027 print and its ±11.2% implied one-month move. Put/call skew is balanced (1.8%), and open interest is thin (P/C OI ratio 0.01). The earnings report is a genuine binary for the near term; the elevated near-term IV argues against expecting a quiet reaction and favors patience over pre-positioning ahead of the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$700).** Q1 FY2027 confirms organic order re-acceleration (backlog rebuilds above $195M), factory-built margins stabilize, and the industry re-accelerates off a flat base as rates ease. The debt-free balance sheet funds further accretive consolidation of the fragmented long tail, buybacks continue shrinking the float, and the market re-rates the quality/oligopoly premium. On ~$29.40 next-year EPS at ~24x, this supports the analyst-high $700 area.

**Bear case (~$450).** Weather normalizes and financial-services profit reverts toward its FY2025 level (~$1.5M pretax), erasing much of FY2026's earnings growth; core homebuilding margin compression continues; backlog softening proves to be genuine organic demand weakness rather than timing; and estimates keep falling. On normalized earnings the ~23x multiple looks expensive, and a de-rating toward the higher end of the pure-play homebuilder range (14-16x) plus lower earnings pulls the stock toward the ~$450 area (roughly the January-February trough), a ~20% decline from current levels.

**Base case (~$600).** Modest revenue growth continues through price, share, and M&A against a flat industry; earnings normalize partially for weather but the franchise compounds; the multiple holds near ~23x on ~$25-26 normalized EPS power. This lands near the analyst median of $600, roughly 5% above current levels plus continued buyback support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, owned for durable compounding and consolidation optionality, not for near-term re-rating.

**Reassessment timeline:** July 30, 2026 (Q1 FY2027 results, call July 31). This print is the decisive test of the backlog/organic-demand tension and the near-term binary the options market is pricing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q1 FY2027 shows backlog rebuilding above ~$195M and homes-sold growth accelerating, with consensus EPS revisions turning positive after the current -4% to -7% trend.
- Factory-built gross margin stabilizes or expands off the 22.1% FY2026 level, demonstrating that FY2026's growth is not solely weather- and price-driven.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid-$400s (near the FY2026 trough) that compresses the forward multiple below ~16x on normalized earnings, restoring a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarterly earnings miss combined with continued backlog contraction, confirming genuine organic demand deterioration.
- Financial-services margin reversion to normalized weather levels pressuring consolidated earnings while the multiple stays above ~22x, indicating a value trap.
- A goodwill impairment or evidence that American Homestar synergies are not materializing, or clarity that the October 2025 executive change was an operational-leadership loss.

## Conclusion

Cavco is a high-quality business (debt-free, cash-generative, and structurally advantaged within a consolidated oligopoly) trading at a full but defensible multiple. The trajectory data captures the current standoff: Financial Health has strengthened (7.2 → 8.0) on the fortress balance sheet and clean M&A execution, while Growth Prospects (6.5 → 5.5) and Valuation (5.8 → 5.0) have softened on falling estimates and a fuller multiple. New data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: the estimate cuts persist, the FY2026 earnings beat is partly weather-flattered, and core homebuilding margins compressed even as reported revenue grew double digits.

The stock offers no clear asymmetry at ~$573. It is neither cheap nor showing the positive-revision re-acceleration that would justify chasing it. The right posture is to hold for the compounding and the debt-free consolidation optionality, and to let the July 30 print resolve the backlog/organic-demand question before adding. We reiterate HOLD. A BUY case requires either an estimate-revision turn confirming organic re-acceleration or a pullback toward the low-$400s that restores a margin of safety on normalized earnings.
