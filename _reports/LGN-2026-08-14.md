## Executive Summary

Legence has transformed its business faster than most investors have updated their model of it. Data centers and technology were 35.9% of revenue in Q2 2025 and 62.5% in Q2 2026. New-build work went from 31.5% to 62.2% of revenue over the same period, while existing-building retrofit work — the energy-efficiency and ESPC franchise that defined the pre-IPO equity story — fell from 68.5% to 37.8%. Revenue in the quarter rose 110.7% to $1,262.1M, roughly 60% of it excluding the January 2026 Bowers acquisition, and management raised FY2026 guidance on August 13 by approximately $600M of revenue and $95M of adjusted EBITDA, to $4.7-4.8B and $565-585M. That is a genuine operating inflection with confirming evidence from the wider data center construction chain.

The problem is that the stock already reflects most of it, and the way it is commonly screened understates how much. Legence is an Up-C: 76.9M Class A shares plus 31.2M Class B shares paired with LGN B Units carried as noncontrolling interest, 108.05M total economic units. The $5.2B market capitalization in circulation is Class A only. On the full unit count, equity value at $67.50 is $7,293M, enterprise value $8,042M including finance leases, and $8,393M including the $350.9M Tax Receivable Agreement liability. That is 14.0x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, or 14.6x including the TRA, versus 10.4x on the Class A-only shortcut. Consensus next-year EPS of $1.78 puts the shares at 37.9x, with a PEG near 1.7 on the $1.46-to-$1.78 step.

The risk stack has hardened since our July review even as the multiple improved. The Q2 10-Q disclosed one Installation & Maintenance customer at 17.2% of quarterly revenue, against a FY2025 10-K statement that no client exceeded 10% in any of 2023-2025; the customer is unnamed and the risk factors were not updated. Material weaknesses in general IT controls and in journal-entry review remain unremediated at June 30, 2026, at a company that recognizes primarily fixed-price revenue on the cost-to-cost method, has no single ERP, and whose surety bonding rose 158% to $1,449.5M in six months. The first auditor SOX 404 attestation arrives with the FY2026 10-K. Guidance implies Q3 revenue flat against Q2 and Q4 down sequentially, after two quarters of $224-300M sequential gains. Our probability-weighted return from $67.50 is approximately flat. HOLD, with a defined accumulation zone in the mid-$50s.

## Company Overview

Legence provides engineering, consulting, installation, fabrication, and maintenance services for mission-critical building systems — HVAC, process piping, and broader mechanical/electrical/plumbing scope. It is a Blackstone-sponsored roll-up that IPO'd on September 15, 2025 at $28.00.

Two segments. **Installation & Maintenance** generated Q2 2026 revenue of $1,055.2M (83.6% of total) at 15.5% GAAP / 16.1% adjusted gross margin. **Engineering & Consulting** generated $206.9M (16.4%) at 27.1% GAAP / 31.1% adjusted gross margin. Within E&C, Engineering & Design revenue declined 4.1% in Q2 and 6.2% in H1 2026; Program & Project Management grew 16.9%.

The operating model is a deliberately decentralized federation of acquired brands, each a separate legal entity with local decision authority. The 10-K discloses the absence of a single enterprise resource planning system and cites it as a reason the company "may be slower or less effective" in identifying problems at an important brand. Services are primarily fixed-price, and the filing states clients have increased their use of fixed-price contracts. Approximately 64.1% of full-time employees were covered by collective bargaining agreements at December 31, 2025, with $75.6M of FY2025 multiemployer pension contributions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 revenue was $2,550.5M. The quarterly path — $505.9M, $598.9M, $708.0M, $737.7M in 2025, then $1,037.9M and $1,262.1M in 2026 — shows the step change. H1 2026 revenue of $2,300.0M compares to pro forma H1 2025 of $1,478.1M (Bowers as if owned from January 1, 2025), or +55.6%. Underlying growth of 55-60% is real and independent of the acquisition accounting.

**Margins.** Adjusted gross margin fell 330bp year over year, from 21.8% to 18.5%. Roughly 281bp of that is segment mix — I&M rose from 67.3% to 83.6% of revenue while segment margins were nearly flat (I&M 16.2%→16.1%, E&C 33.2%→31.1%) — leaving only about 49bp of actual margin erosion. Adjusted EBITDA margin nonetheless rose from 12.1% to 12.2%, because adjusted SG&A fell from roughly 11.1% to 6.9% of revenue as a largely fixed corporate overhead base was spread over 2.1x the revenue.

The mechanism matters more than the outcome. Pricing and project execution are holding; the P&L is being reshaped by mix and carried by scale. That makes the EBITDA margin structurally dependent on continued volume growth. If revenue plateaus, the SG&A leverage reverses while the adverse mix persists. Guidance implying roughly 12.3% Q4 adjusted EBITDA margin on lower sequential revenue is mildly inconsistent with that mechanic, which is weak evidence the revenue guide is conservative rather than the margin guide aggressive.

**GAAP versus adjusted.** Q2 2026 showed a loss from operations of $(6.6)M against income of $31.3M a year earlier, despite revenue more than doubling, and net loss attributable to Legence of $27.8M, or $(0.37) per share, against adjusted EBITDA of $154.6M. The $189.2M gap is dominated by $73.9M of stock-based and other non-cash compensation (overwhelmingly legacy Series A and Restricted Series C Interests), $21.6M of goodwill plus $19.5M of long-lived asset impairment in an E&C reporting unit attributed to "lower customer demand for sustainability services," and $44.2M of depreciation and amortization.

The compensation item requires precision. Series A and Restricted Series C Interests were issued by Blackstone-controlled Management Aggregators sitting *above* Legence Corp, and the Parent Entities — not Legence — settle them. Legence records the expense with an offsetting capital contribution. The expense is a real GAAP cost of labor, it is not a Legence cash outflow, and it is not dilutive to Class A. It is also nearly finished: only $24.6M of Time Interest expense remains unrecognized over 3.2 years, plus $2.0M of Restricted Series C. GAAP earnings should improve sharply as it rolls off, with no operational change. Exit Interests (20% of Series A) carry no accrued expense and would trigger an unquantified non-cash charge on a future liquidity event.

**Cash flow, with a caveat that matters for FY2027.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $194.5M against $62.5M a year earlier; capex was $41.9M, so free cash flow was $152.6M. But $112.0M of the operating cash flow is the non-cash compensation add-back. That is genuine cash retention for Legence today — because Blackstone's parent entities are compensating Legence employees out of their own vehicles, having distributed $47.5M to holders in Q2 alone. As the legacy interests complete, ongoing employee compensation reverts to Legence's own RSU/option/ESPP program, which is far smaller but is either cash or Class A dilution. The LTM add-back of $179.1M will not repeat at that scale. Naive extrapolation of H1 cash generation into FY2027 is the single easiest way to overstate this business.

Working capital was a $16.7M net drag in H1: accounts receivable −$151.1M and contract assets −$53.7M (including $158.0M of retentions) against accounts payable +$105.3M, accrued compensation +$42.7M, and contract liabilities +$51.4M. The offsetting inflows are a function of continued volume growth, which is precisely what guidance says stops in H2. Management states second-half working-capital needs are higher; capex is stepping up ($41.9M in one half against $37.9M for all of FY2025); and $44.9M of Bowers deferred consideration is due December 31, 2026, payable in cash or stock at the company's election.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $3,739.7M, cash $292.0M, total equity $977.5M including $377.1M of noncontrolling interest, current ratio 1.35x. Debt of $1,041.4M comprises a $992.8M term loan, $33.6M notes payable, and $15.0M finance leases. Company-defined net debt is $734.4M, giving net leverage of 1.6x on LTM adjusted EBITDA of $448.5M and 1.5x on the $499.8M combined base including Bowers' pre-ownership contribution. Interest coverage is 5.9x LTM and 9.1x in Q2 alone. Adding the TRA as a debt-like related-party claim takes leverage to about 2.2x combined.

Credit terms improved twice: a May 27, 2026 amendment cut the term-loan spread from SOFR+2.25% to SOFR+2.00%, and S&P (June 5) plus Moody's (June 24) upgrades earned a further 25bp step-down to SOFR+1.75%. Quarterly interest expense fell to $16.9M from $30.4M as IPO proceeds cut floating-rate debt from $1,590.4M to $797.8M. The term loan matures December 16, 2031 — no maturity wall. The revolver is $200M with $169.3M available, and its only financial covenant is a springing First Lien Net Leverage test of ≤8.50x, tested only above 35% utilization; at 15.4% it is untested. This is effectively covenant-lite.

On the distress screens that circulate for LGN: recomputing Altman's original formula with the market value of equity — 108.05M units at $67.50 against total liabilities of $1,890.6M — produces Z in the safe zone. The distress reading appears to substitute book equity for market equity. The metric travels poorly to goodwill-heavy services roll-ups anyway; 1.5-1.6x net leverage, 5.9x coverage, a 2031 maturity, and an untested covenant are the more informative solvency evidence.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is corroborated by Legence's own end-market disclosure: data centers and technology rose to 62.5% of Q2 2026 revenue from 35.9% a year earlier, and I&M installation and fabrication revenue rose 189% year over year. Broader macro corroboration of the data-center construction cycle is referenced in the wider market but is not established in the provided sources and should be treated as unverified.

Forward work: backlog, defined as remaining performance obligations under contracted fixed-price projects, was $4,017.8M at June 30, plus $1,654.0M of awarded contracts, for a $5,671.8M headline that grew 104.6%. Three definitional points. Roughly 29% of the headline is awarded but not contracted — management estimates dependent on scope holding, clients obtaining financing, and definitive documentation. The measure excludes cost-plus and time-and-materials work, understating total workload in the other direction. And management expects only 65-75% of RPO to convert within twelve months. Book-to-bill was 1.2x, down from 1.3x. I&M forward work grew 141.2%, E&C only 26.6%, and the company attributes I&M growth primarily to Bowers plus data center and education demand.

Estimate revisions are strong: current-year consensus EPS rose 37.1% over 90 days to $1.46 and next year 26.1% to $1.78, with the 30-day change flat, indicating the revision cycle followed the August guidance raise and has not yet extended. Revisions of that magnitude are usually a durable positive signal, but two things attenuate it here. First, the stock is not cheap against the revised numbers — 37.9x next year, PEG ~1.7 — so consensus catching up does not mechanically re-rate the shares. Second, the reported surprise record is three misses in four quarters, including a −51.1% variance in the June quarter ($0.17 reported against $0.35). The company published no adjusted EPS in its release and reported GAAP $(0.37), so the $0.17 basis is not verifiable, and the comparison should not be treated as an established beat/miss. What that pattern does establish is that consensus and company reporting are not reconciling on a common basis — which is a reason to hold forward estimates loosely rather than to lean on them.

The most important item in the guidance is the implied shape. Q3 midpoint of $1,250M is essentially flat against Q2's $1,262M, and the implied Q4 midpoint of roughly $1,200M is down sequentially, after gains of $300M and $224M in the prior two quarters. FY2025 revenue rose sequentially in every quarter including Q4, so seasonality does not obviously explain it. Two readings compete: conservatism from a newly public company that just raised the year by $600M, or specific large projects completing without equivalent replacement — plausibly Bowers-related work, whose acquired backlog intangible is amortized over just two years. The margin arithmetic weakly favors conservatism. This is the first question for the next call.

Two structural headwinds inside the legacy business. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act removed the Section 179D deduction for energy-efficient commercial buildings where construction begins after June 30, 2026 — a client-side incentive whose withdrawal has just taken effect for the retrofit franchise. And goodwill impairment has now occurred in four consecutive reporting periods ($5.1M FY2023, $17.8M FY2024, $25.0M FY2025, $21.6M Q2 2026), with two reporting units holding a combined $88.3M of goodwill carrying less than a 10% fair-value cushion at year-end.

Options (845k), RSUs (1,417k), and potential Bowers stock settlement (612k) total roughly 2.87M shares, or 3.7% of Class A. The real economic cost of exchange is the TRA, under which Legence owes 85% of net cash tax savings to Blackstone-affiliated members. That liability grew from $207.4M at year-end to $350.9M at June 30, with the April 9 exchange of 9.53M units alone adding $133.8M. Applying that $14.04-per-unit rate to the remaining 31.17M units implies roughly $438M of additional liability, or ~$790M total, about 11% of equity value. That extrapolation holds basis and rates constant and the recorded liability is undiscounted, so NPV is materially lower — treat it as a sensitivity, not a forecast. First payments are expected late 2026 or early 2027.

Peer context is imperfect. The supplied same-sector comparables are heterogeneous (UFPI 21.6x, TPC 42.7x, MYRG 32.0x, LPX 102x, SYM 681x P/E) and none is a close functional comp. The relevant set is mechanical/electrical contractors with data-center exposure, and no verified multiple for that group was obtainable in this cycle. What can be said from primary sources: 14x forward EV/EBITDA on a business whose EBITDA margin is scale-dependent, whose forward-work ratio is decelerating, and whose GAAP earnings are negative sits at a full price. The multiple is being asked to be justified by the +86% guided growth rate, most of which will not repeat in FY2027.

Valuation has improved since our July review, and that improvement is real: at approximately $70.80 on July 26 against the prior $470-490M EBITDA guide, the same arithmetic produced roughly 17.5x. A lower price against a $95M higher guide is a multiple compression of roughly 3.5 turns. That moves the valuation score from 4.5 to 5.0 and rules out a REDUCE on price alone. It does not make the stock cheap.

**On the drawdown from the $107.24 May 14 high.** Some of it is identifiable: the May 14 session opened at $104.38 and closed at $89.00; late July saw a violent three-day decline to a $53.08 close on July 29 on heavy volume. But the sellers are not obviously wrong at the level: at 14x forward EBITDA on guidance implying a sequential revenue plateau, with an unremediated control weakness and a newly concentrated customer, the derating has a fundamental rationale.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own framing is unglamorous: a highly competitive industry with many firms of varying size, some with greater resources, others "smaller, hav[ing] a lower cost structure, employ[ing] a non-unionized workforce." Two named pressures deserve more attention than generic competition language.

First, channel disintermediation. Legence frequently works as a subcontractor to other contractors, and the filing warns revenue could be hurt if those contractors reduce subcontracts "including due to expansion of their internal HVAC or MEP capabilities." In a hyperscaler build, Legence's contractual customer is often a general contractor or developer rather than the end owner, and those parties have both incentive and balance sheet to insource mechanical trades in a tight-capacity market. Second, pay-when-paid subcontract terms create payment-timing exposure to prime contractors' collection cycles — directly relevant with receivables up $151.1M in a half and $158.0M of retentions in contract assets.

Trajectory is the right lens. Legence is clearly gaining position in data center mechanical work: I&M installation and fabrication revenue rose 189% year over year and forward work 141%. The company claims over 60% of the Nasdaq-100 as clients, which is a marketing assertion with no supporting revenue attribution. Simultaneously, the differentiated part of the business — energy-efficiency retrofit, ESPC, sustainability consulting, where technical advisory work creates switching costs — is shrinking, with Engineering & Design revenue down 6.2% in H1 and four consecutive impairment periods. The mix is moving from sticky retrofit relationships toward bid-driven new construction. That raises near-term growth and lowers the durability of the franchise. Net: 5.5.

The Bowers acquisition deserves credit and scrutiny in equal measure. Consideration was $426.7M against disclosed EBITDA of $51.3M for the six months to December 31, 2025 — roughly 4.2x annualized, exceptionally accretive if durable. Bowers contributed $303.8M of Q2 revenue and appears to be growing standalone (derived H1 revenue of ~$373M in 2025 versus $547.1M in 2026). But a 4.2x headline on a six-month annualization is also what one would see if that half was a peak driven by one or two very large projects, if the earnings sit with the customer now at 17% of consolidated revenue, or if the seller discounted for liquidity and stock consideration. Bowers' $885K of six-month D&A indicates an asset-light, project-driven earnings stream with no installed-base recurrence. H2 2025 standalone revenue is not disclosed, so the acquired margin cannot be computed.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control weakness intersecting judgment-heavy accounting.** Both management and the auditor identified material weaknesses: ineffective general IT controls across multiple business units' systems, and COSO deficiencies including insufficient journal-entry review with personnel able to both prepare and post entries. Both were unremediated at December 31, 2025 and again at June 30, 2026; disclosure controls were concluded not effective. The company recognizes primarily fixed-price revenue on the cost-to-cost input method, and the 10-K states the specific hazard directly: profit reported over several periods may later be determined to have been overstated, with the reversal landing in one period. Revenue roughly doubled in a year and surety bonding rose 158% to $1,449.5M, indicating far larger bonded commitments. The first management SOX 404 report and first auditor attestation arrive with the FY2026 10-K — a dated binary. Mitigating: GITC weaknesses are common among recent IPOs and serial acquirers, a new CIO has been hired, and no misstatement has been identified. A material weakness speaks to possibility, not occurrence.

**Customer concentration that appeared after the 10-K.** The FY2025 filing stated no client exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024, or 2025. The Q2 10-Q discloses one I&M customer at 17.2% of quarterly and 17.6% of half-year revenue, against 5.7% and 5.9% prior year — roughly $217M in the quarter. The customer is unnamed, and the 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors. Whether the counterparty is a hyperscaler end user, a general contractor, or a developer intermediary is unresolved, and that distinction determines whether underlying demand is more or less diversified than the contractual exposure implies. Note that broadening service-line mix is not diversification when direct customer concentration triples.

**Sponsor supply and a leveraged holder.** Blackstone has sold 8,402,178 shares at $45.00 (December 2025), 1,260,326 at $45.00 (January 2026), and 15,394,112 at $54.00 (April 2026) — approximately $1.27B, none of it to Legence — and retains 50,468,515 units, about 46.7% of the total. Separately, as of March 24, 2026, approximately 61% of shares on an as-exchanged basis were pledged to secure margin loans of the Legence Parent entities with Goldman Sachs Bank USA as administrative agent, and the 10-K warns foreclosure "could result in sales of a substantial number of shares." That is a forced-selling channel disconnected from operating results. The pledge figure predates the April secondary and has not been updated. Each tranche to date has cleared, and the stock trades above the last institutional clearing price of $54.

**Concentrated end-market and client financing.** With 62.5% of revenue in data centers, Legence is levered to one capital cycle. The 10-K flags that clients often obtain third-party financing and that unavailability of water or utility services may delay projects or cause deferred investment. Tightening project-financing conditions transmit to contractor order books with a lag; the specific macro credit-market details are not established in the provided sources and are omitted here.

**Other.** Tariff exposure on imported steel and aluminum lands on Legence under fixed-price contracts absent change orders. 64.1% union coverage with joint-and-several multiemployer withdrawal liability the company states it cannot estimate. TRA early-termination and change-of-control provisions require an immediate lump sum on assumptions that could "materially exceed" realized benefits and function as an anti-takeover mechanism, with the 10-K acknowledging Class A holders could receive "substantially less consideration" in a change of control. Controlled-company status with a corporate opportunity waiver covering "any aspect of the energy equipment or services business." Blackstone affiliates also hold $97.2M of the term loan.

**Management alignment.** Insider activity is uninformative to negative: the only open-market purchase on record is a director's 8,928 shares at the $28.00 IPO price on the IPO date; there has been no officer open-market selling either. The compensation plan uses Adjusted EBITDA, EBITDA, and Safety TRIR — no revenue, margin, cash-flow, EPS, or return-on-capital metric. For a serial acquirer that issued 2.7M Class A shares for acquisitions in 2026 and drew a $200M incremental term loan for Bowers, an EBITDA-only financial metric rewards acquired growth without testing price paid or capital consumed. Safety TRIR is genuinely material in this business and the plan is one year old, so treat this as a design gap to monitor. Offsetting positives on capital allocation: the Bowers purchase looks accretive, IPO proceeds went to deleveraging, and two spread reductions plus two ratings upgrades were earned.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 72.8% matches one-year realized volatility of 72.8%, so vol is fairly priced in relative terms while absolute uncertainty is high — the three-month tenor implies a ±30.7% move. The term structure is flat (72.8% near, 73.5% far), distributing uncertainty evenly rather than pointing at a specific catalyst. Positioning is the notable item: put/call skew is −7.0% (call-heavy), put/call open interest collapsed from 0.83 on July 26 to 0.05, and ATM IV fell 28.1 points over the same window. Effectively no hedging demand exists against a risk stack that includes an unremediated control weakness, a first auditor attestation, an unnamed 17% customer, and a pledged controlling stake. Combined with 16 of 17 analysts at Buy or better and a low target of $80, positioning reads complacent. Short interest at 7.4% of float and 2.6 days to cover is moderate and not a squeeze constraint. This divergence argues for patience rather than for adding at $67.50.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $96 (+42%)

Backlog and awarded contracts of $5.67B with 65-75% of RPO converting within twelve months mechanically supports revenue well above the implied $1.2B Q4, so the H2 guide proves conservative and FY2026 lands near or above the top of the range. Bowers' apparent 4.2x purchase multiple is durable, validating the roll-up playbook and supporting further accretive tuck-ins funded within 1.5x leverage. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$730M as the data-center pipeline converts, and the multiple holds at 15x as legacy compensation expense rolls off ($24.6M remaining) and GAAP earnings turn positive, removing the "loss-making" screen. Consensus targets ($105.71 mean, $125 high) become achievable. Getting there requires no control failure, no contract profit reversal, retention of the 17% customer, and orderly absorption of remaining sponsor supply.

### Bear Case — $38 (−44%)

The implied Q4 sequential decline is real rather than conservative, as a large Bowers-related project completes without replacement. SG&A leverage — 420bp of it — reverses while the adverse I&M mix persists, so the EBITDA margin compresses toward 11%. FY2027 EBITDA settles near $525M and the multiple compresses to 9-10x as the market reprices a bid-driven mechanical contractor rather than a structural AI beneficiary. Any one of three specific events accelerates this: an adverse SOX 404 attestation or a cost-to-cost profit reversal, loss or reduction of the unnamed 17% customer, or margin-loan foreclosure adding forced supply on top of Blackstone's remaining 46.7%. Working capital turns from a modest drag into a significant one as receivables and retentions build without the offsetting payable growth that volume expansion provides, and TRA payments begin.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $70 (+3.7% from $67.50).** FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $655M — FY2026 guidance midpoint of $575M growing mid-teens as backlog converts and tuck-ins contribute — at 12.75x EV/EBITDA, less approximately $1,100M of net debt plus TRA, divided by 108.05M units. That multiple is a modest discount to today's 14.0x, reflecting the deceleration from +86% to mid-teens growth and unresolved control and concentration issues.

**Bull case: $96 (+42%).** $730M FY2027 EBITDA at 15x, net debt and TRA of ~$1,000M, 108.05M units.

**Bear case: $38 (−44%).** $525M FY2027 EBITDA at 9.5x, net debt and TRA of $1,100M.

**Probability-weighted return:** 25% bear / 50% base / 25% bull produces approximately +1%. That is a fair-value outcome from the current price and does not clear the bar for new capital.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on three dated checkpoints — the Q3 2026 print (early November, testing whether the sequential plateau is conservatism or project completion), the FY2026 10-K with the first auditor SOX 404 attestation (approximately March 2027), and the initial TRA payment (late 2026 / early 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if:**
1. Price reaches $52-56, which is 11.0-11.5x our $655M FY2027 EBITDA estimate net of debt and TRA — roughly where the shares traded on July 29 — with backlog and guidance intact.
2. Q3 revenue exceeds $1,300M with Q4 guidance not implying sequential decline, resolving the plateau question, **and** book-to-bill holds above 1.2x.
3. Management discloses either remediation of the GITC and journal-entry weaknesses or the nature of the 17.2% customer relationship and its contracted duration.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. Any cumulative catch-up adjustment reverses previously recognized fixed-price contract profit, or the FY2026 attestation identifies a material weakness that persists into FY2027.
2. Single-customer concentration exceeds 20% of revenue, or that customer's contribution declines without replacement — visible in I&M revenue falling sequentially for two consecutive quarters.
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin falls below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the SG&A-leverage reversal.
4. A margin-loan foreclosure event or an unmarketed block sale by the Parent entities.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected early November 2026.

Note that the scenario arithmetic uses the authoritative $67.50 basis. On the prior official close of $63.24, base-case upside is +10.7%, bull +52%, bear −40% — a materially better entry, and an illustration of how much a 6.7% intraday move matters in a name with 72.8% implied volatility. Prospective buyers should be disciplined about the level, not the ticker.

## Conclusion

This is our third consecutive HOLD on LGN (scores 5.0 → 5.3 → 5.3), and the composition has changed even though the conclusion has not. Valuation improved materially — 14.0x forward EBITDA versus roughly 17.5x in late July, on a lower price and a $95M higher guide — which removes the case for reducing on price. Financial health improved on two ratings upgrades, a 50bp cumulative spread reduction, 1.5-1.6x net leverage, and 5.9x coverage against a 2031 maturity. Those gains were offset by a harder risk stack: a newly disclosed 17.2% single customer against a 10-K stating none exceeded 10%, a second consecutive period of unremediated auditor-confirmed material weaknesses ahead of the first SOX 404 attestation, a fourth consecutive impairment in the legacy sustainability business against $88.3M of goodwill carrying a sub-10% cushion, and guidance that implies the sequential revenue ramp stops.

The growth is real, the demand backdrop is corroborated by the company's own end-market disclosure, and the balance sheet can absorb a downturn. What is missing is a margin of safety. At 14.0x guided FY2026 EBITDA and 37.9x next-year consensus EPS on the full 108.05M-unit count, the price pays for successful execution of the plateau-then-reaccelerate path while an options market with essentially no put open interest and a sell-side book of 16 Buys prices nothing for the control, concentration, and sponsor-supply tails. Existing holders should stay, monitor the Q3 print for resolution of the H2 shape, and treat the FY2026 attestation as a genuine binary. New capital should wait for $52-56 or for evidence that the control environment and the customer relationship are resolved.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the analyst price-target figures were materially wrong (report cited a stale ~$63.29 mean and "low $80"; Phase 1 shows mean $105.71 / median $105 / low $80 / high $125), and several macro/market claims (hyperscaler capex, Caterpillar, utility load, CoreWeave, 30-year Treasury) were unsupported by the sources and have been removed — none of these changes the valuation math underpinning HOLD, but the corrected consensus targets sit well above the current price.
