**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rogers Corporation has completed a multi-year decline and is now in the early innings of an earnings recovery. Revenue fell from $971M in 2022 to a trough of $811M in 2025, and the company posted a $61.8M net loss last year driven by $97.1M of restructuring and impairment, including a $71.8M write-off tied to the curamik ceramic-substrate goodwill (a $67.3M full goodwill write-off of the reporting unit). Q1 2026 marked the inflection: revenue rose 5.2% to $200.5M, gross margin expanded 230bps to 32.2%, the company returned to operating profit ($10.7M) and net income ($4.5M), and adjusted EPS more than doubled to $0.75, beating the $0.68 consensus. This was the third consecutive quarter at or above the guidance midpoint, and management guided Q2 to $210–220M revenue and $0.90–1.10 adjusted EPS against $0.34 in the prior-year quarter.

The recovery rests on a quantified, dated cost-savings program of roughly $45M run-rate (about $25M realized in 2025, $7M annualization carryover, and $13M from the curamik/Germany consolidation expected to fully land by Q4 2026), layered on a stabilizing top line. The balance sheet is a genuine asset: zero drawn debt on a $450M revolver, ~$196M cash, a 3.97 current ratio, and 84% equity/assets. The headline negative EPS (-$3.40 trailing) and depressed Z-Score (2.45) reflect the 2025 operating loss from non-cash impairments, not solvency risk. There is a credible AI-adjacent call option: the same curamik unit that was impaired is being repositioned around microchannel-cooler technology for high-power chip cooling in data centers, though management is explicit this is 2027+ revenue with 2026 limited to sampling and prototypes.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated 134% over twelve months, from ~$68 to ~$158, and now trades around the ~$153 average analyst target on roughly 44x current-year EPS ($3.64). The fundamental bottom is confirmed and the margin-recovery thesis is intact, but the equity has already re-rated to price most of the visible recovery. With much of Q1's revenue growth attributable to a $7.9M FX tailwind while automotive end-markets were still declining high-single-digits, the top-line inflection remains partly unproven. We rate ROG a HOLD on a growth thesis: own it for the margin ramp and the 2027 optionality, but the asymmetry that existed at $90–110 is gone, and we would wait for a pullback toward the low-$130s before adding.

## Company Overview

Rogers Corporation (Chandler, AZ) designs and manufactures high-performance engineered materials and components, selling to roughly 2,800 customers worldwide with no single customer exceeding 10% of sales. The business runs through two core segments plus a small "Other":

- **Advanced Electronics Solutions (AES)** — approximately 55% of sales. High-frequency circuit materials (RO4000, RT/duroid), ceramic substrates (curamik), busbars (ROLINX), and cooling solutions. End markets: EV/HEV, ADAS, aerospace and defense (phased-array radar, antennas), renewable energy, and wireless infrastructure.
- **Elastomeric Material Solutions (EMS)** — approximately 44% of sales. Polyurethane and silicone materials (PORON, BISCO, DeWAL) for cushioning, gasketing, sealing, vibration management, and thermal applications. End markets: EV batteries, industrial, portable electronics, footwear, A&D, and medical.

The moat rests on "design-in" wins: embedding proprietary materials into customer product specifications, which creates switching costs once a material is qualified into a design. The offset is that products are sold on short-term, cancellable purchase orders with no long-term volume guarantees. The business is highly international, with ~72% of sales foreign (41% APAC, 29% EMEA) and ~63% of employees outside the U.S. China is the single largest country, at roughly $62M per quarter.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a multi-year decline that bottomed in 2025 and is now inflecting:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $971M | $908M | $830M | $811M | $200.5M (+5.2%) |
| Operating income | $144M | $85M | $25M | –$45M | $10.7M (vs –$0.3M) |
| Net income | $117M | $57M | $26M | –$61.8M | $4.5M (vs –$1.4M) |
| Gross margin | 33.1% | 33.8% | 33.4% | 31.7% | 32.2% (+230bps) |

**2025 was the trough.** The net loss was driven by the restructuring and impairment charge: the curamik goodwill write-off plus $23.4M of restructuring tied to the Belgium plant wind-down, German footprint consolidation, workforce reduction, and executive transition. A $16.7M tax expense on a pre-tax loss (a -37% effective rate) reflects a valuation allowance against deferred tax assets in loss jurisdictions, a sign of how concentrated the operating stress was. The Q1'26 effective tax rate of 60.2% from the same valuation allowance means GAAP earnings will lag the operating recovery until those jurisdictions return to consistent profitability.

**Q1 2026 confirmed the inflection.** Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 580bps to 16%. Management guided gross margin to 32.5–33.5%. The track record supports confidence in these estimates: four consecutive beats, including +46.9% in Q4'25 and +27.3% in Q3'25.

**The balance sheet is the standout.** The $450M revolver is expandable to $675M. The Altman Z-Score of 2.45 and Piotroski 3/5 are dragged down purely by the 2025 operating loss, which was largely non-cash. This is an earnings-distressed, balance-sheet-safe company. Cash flow quality is solid: 2025 FCF of $71.1M (8.8% margin) despite the net loss, since the impairments were non-cash. Capital intensity is falling (capex/sales ~4% in 2025 vs. ~7% in 2024) as the multi-year capacity build-out completes. The company deployed $52.4M on buybacks in 2025 (738K shares), reducing share count from 18.6M to 17.8M, though it repurchased none in Q1'26, preserving capital and optionality.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth thesis is margin recovery, not top-line expansion. The cost-savings program flows directly into the EBITDA margin trajectory. Estimate revisions confirm the direction: current-year EPS consensus has risen 12.3% over 90 days to $3.64, and current-quarter estimates rose 24.4% over 90 days to $0.99, though revisions have been flat over the most recent 30 days, suggesting consensus has caught up to the trajectory for now.

The 90-day revision velocity is meaningful at both the current-quarter (+24.4%) and full-year (+12.3%) levels. That signal carries the most weight when a stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates. Here the multiple is the constraint: at ~44x current-year EPS, the rising estimates are already reflected in the price, so the revision signal does not carry the same buy implication it would at 15–20x forward earnings.

The top-line picture is more nuanced than the +5.2% headline suggests. Q1 growth was flattered by the FX tailwind, automotive end-markets were still declining at a high-single-digit rate, and the durable demand recovery in EV/HEV and wireless infrastructure has not yet materialized. The industrial recovery is tracking U.S./European PMI improvement, and semiconductor and high-end smartphone content gains are tailwinds, but the revenue inflection itself remains partly unproven.

The longer-dated growth optionality is the curamik reinvention. The same ceramic-substrate unit that was fully impaired in 2025 is being repositioned around microchannel-cooler technology for cooling high-power chips in data centers and AI applications, plus high-frequency materials for data centers. This places Rogers in the materials/component layer of the AI infrastructure buildout. Management is explicit this is 2027+ revenue, with 2026 limited to sampling and prototyping, and declined to quantify TAM. Rogers is a component supplier into AI hardware, not an AI company, and the optionality is real but unquantified.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing earnings, ROG screens as loss-making (-$3.40 EPS, -51x P/E), an artifact of the 2025 impairment. The relevant lens is forward and normalized. At ~$158 against current-year consensus of $3.64, the stock trades at roughly 44x, and against next-year $4.37 at roughly 36x. These are full multiples for a business whose top line grew 5.2% in its best recent quarter, much of it FX, with automotive still contracting.

Peer comparison is messy because the Electronic Components group is heterogeneous: Knowles at 55.4x, Belden at 18.9x, Corning at 85x, with Vishay and Littelfuse showing distorted or negative trailing multiples. The peer median P/E of 85x is not a usable anchor given the dispersion. Adjusting for growth, ROG's forward multiple sits above what its current revenue trajectory supports. The PEG is unfavorable: ~44x forward earnings against a high-single-digit normalized revenue growth profile and a margin-recovery story dated to conclude by Q4 2026. Once the cost savings fully land, the incremental margin lever diminishes, and the multiple would then need to be supported by genuine top-line acceleration or the 2027 curamik optionality converting to revenue.

The clearest valuation tell is that the stock now trades around the average analyst target, even after analysts raised targets 14.5%. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle applies to cheap inflection stocks with reasonable multiples; at ~44x forward earnings with the stock near consensus, the targets are not an independent floor, they reflect the same recovery extrapolation embedded in the price. The valuation is full because the current price discounts most of the visible, dated margin recovery while leaving the unproven top-line inflection and the 2027 optionality as the remaining upside drivers; prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input. Our base case fair value of ~$165 sits close to current price; the asymmetry that justified ownership in the low-$100s has narrowed to roughly balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

Rogers occupies a high-performance electronic-materials niche with structural exposure to vehicle electrification, ADAS, A&D radar and communications, renewable energy, and AI/data-center thermal management. The design-in model insulates it somewhat from pure price competition, and switching costs are real once a material is qualified into a customer's design.

The competitive trajectory is mixed, which is why we score this dimension at the midpoint rather than higher. The curamik impairment is the key data point: the write-off was triggered by "changing market competition and supply dynamics," which reads as encroachment from lower-cost Asian commodity producers in ceramic substrates and a structural setback in part of the AES segment. The "local-for-local" regional manufacturing strategy is a deliberate response to tariff and decoupling pressure, and the consolidation of the German footprint is a rationalization, not an expansion. Against that, the EMS franchise (PORON, BISCO) remains differentiated, and the curamik repositioning toward data-center cooling could turn a competitively eroded business into a growth vector if the AI thermal-management opportunity converts. The net read is a company defending a specialized but contested position, with a credible but unproven path to upgrading its mix.

## Risk Assessment

- **Curamik competitive erosion.** The 2025 impairment crystallized a real, recent setback in ceramic substrates. The data-center cooling pivot is the bull's answer, but it is 2027+ and unquantified.
- **End-market cyclicality / EV-HEV weakness.** Automotive was still declining high-single-digits in Q1'26 as customers manage inventory and U.S. EV incentives soften. The durable demand recovery is not yet in the numbers.
- **China and tariff exposure.** ~41% of sales are APAC; China is the largest country at ~$62M/quarter. Risk factors flag U.S.-China decoupling, China local-supplier substitution, and 2025 tariff actions.
- **FX translation.** ~72% foreign sales; the Q1'26 FX tailwind flattered reported growth and could reverse.
- **PFAS and asbestos legacy.** Several products contain PFAS under evolving regulation; the asbestos liability is $57.3M projected through 2064, substantially insured (net ~$4.5M exposure).
- **Governance and activist risk.** A near-complete executive turnover in 2023–2025, a CEO transition resolved only in May 2026, and the explicit addition of an "activist shareholder" risk factor alongside two semiconductor ex-CEO board appointments suggest the company is operating under or anticipating shareholder pressure. This is a two-sided risk: it could catalyze portfolio rationalization or M&A, or signal continued instability.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options chain is distorted: 1-week ATM IV of 181%+ against 1-year historical vol of 38% reflects a thin, single-day expiry artifact rather than a clean fundamental signal, and the put/call OI ratio of 0.01 with balanced skew shows no directional bearish positioning. The more informative reads are the 3-month skew of -5.8% and 6-month skew of -8.0%, indicating modest downside hedging demand building into the back half of 2026, consistent with a market that has priced the near-term recovery and is hedging the unproven top-line inflection and the 2027 optionality. The term-structure backwardation overstates imminent stress given the expiry artifact, but the steady put-skew at the 3–6 month tenors aligns with our view that the easy re-rating is done and the risk is now more balanced. Short interest is low at 3.1% of float (1.3 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded long nor a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$200):** The cost-savings program lands fully, driving EBITDA margins higher. Automotive and wireless end-markets recover in H2 2026, turning the FX-flattered top line into genuine organic growth. The curamik data-center cooling and high-frequency AI materials begin generating revenue in 2027, re-rating Rogers as an AI-infrastructure materials beneficiary. The fortress balance sheet funds accretive M&A and continued buybacks. Multiple holds at ~44x on rising EPS, supporting the $200 high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$120, ~24% downside):** The top-line inflection stalls as automotive weakness persists and FX reverses, exposing that much of the Q1 "growth" was non-operational. The cost-savings benefit fully laps by late 2026, removing the margin lever with no organic growth to replace it. The 2027 curamik optionality slips or fails to scale against Asian competition, and the ~44x multiple compresses toward 25–30x as the market re-rates a high-single-digit grower without a yield. Activist pressure proves disruptive rather than catalytic.

**Base Case (~$165):** Margins continue to recover on the dated cost program, revenue grows mid-single-digits with the FX tailwind fading, and the stock consolidates near current levels as the realized recovery is offset by a full multiple and the long wait for curamik revenue. EPS reaches the ~$3.64 current-year and ~$4.37 next-year consensus, and the multiple holds roughly steady, leaving the stock range-bound until the 2027 optionality becomes more tangible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is the margin-recovery ramp combined with the dated 2027 curamik data-center cooling optionality. This is a confident growth hold for existing positions, not a buy at current levels, because the price has re-rated to capture most of the visible recovery.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July), which will test the guided revenue and EPS and reveal whether organic (ex-FX) growth is materializing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$130s (near the $135 analyst low and a ~36x current-year multiple) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry.
- Evidence of genuine organic top-line acceleration ex-FX, specifically automotive returning to growth or wireless infrastructure recovering, lifting forward revenue estimates.
- A concrete, quantified curamik/data-center cooling design-win or revenue guide that converts the 2027 optionality into a modelable contribution.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance miss or cut at Q2 2026, breaking the four-quarter beat streak and signaling the recovery is stalling.
- Margin progression stalling below the guided gross margin while the cost-savings program laps, with no organic growth to offset.
- A disruptive activist outcome or renewed executive instability following the recently resolved CEO transition.

## Conclusion

Rogers is a clean turnaround that has done most of its visible work already. The bottom is confirmed: three straight guidance beats, EPS doubling, a 580bps EBITDA margin expansion, and a quantified cost-savings ramp on a debt-free balance sheet. The curamik data-center cooling pivot is genuine optionality that could re-rate the equity if it converts to 2027 revenue. These are real positives and the reason the stock is not an AVOID.

The constraint is valuation. At ~$158, the stock trades around the average analyst target on ~44x current-year earnings, having appreciated 134% over twelve months. The margin-recovery thesis is largely priced, the top-line inflection is partly an FX artifact with automotive still contracting, and the curamik upside is two-plus years away and unquantified. The risk/reward, asymmetric in the low-$100s, is now roughly balanced. We rate ROG a HOLD and would look to add on a pullback toward the low-$130s, where the multiple and the durability of the recovery come back into better alignment.
