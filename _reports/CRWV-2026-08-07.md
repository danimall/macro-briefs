## Executive Summary

CoreWeave sells contracted GPU cloud capacity to a small number of very large AI buyers. Q1 2026 revenue was $2,078M, up 112% year over year, 98% of it tied to customer commitments, against remaining performance obligations of $98.8bn. That demand side is real and verifiable in the filings. The problem sits below the revenue line: an operating loss of $144M became a net loss of $740M because net interest expense was $536M, cash capex was $7,695M against $2,984M of operating cash flow (two-thirds of which was working capital), and total principal debt of $25.1bn at March 31 rose to roughly $30.3bn after the April financings. Adding $10.05bn of recognized operating lease liabilities and roughly $60bn of undiscounted lease payments not yet on the balance sheet, contractual fixed obligations approach the same order of magnitude as the RPO they are meant to service. The spread between contracted receipts and contracted outflows is thin relative to the absolute numbers, which is why the equity moves 40% in four sessions.

Two things changed since the last review, and both cut against the stock. First, consensus loss estimates deteriorated further and the deterioration accelerated: current-quarter EPS went from -$1.26 thirty days ago to -$1.45 (-14.6%), next quarter from -$1.12 to -$1.36 (-21.6%), full-year 2026 to -$5.02 from -$4.60 ninety days ago. Second, the share price rose 11.5% from $79.99 at the prior snapshot to $89.05. Falling estimates with a rising price is the inverse of an improving entry point. Separately, the company's own cost of capital is visible in the April 2026 issuance of 9.75% unsecured notes alongside a 1.75% convertible and equity sold at $109.

This is the third consecutive AVOID (4.2 → 4.0 → 3.9), so the bar for repeating it should be higher. It is met: pro forma leverage is roughly $5bn higher than at the last review, the loss estimates are materially worse, the three material weaknesses and ineffective disclosure controls remain unremediated as of March 31, 2026, and the price is higher. The rating does not claim that the business fails; it claims that at $89.05 the equity offers a negative probability-weighted return with a live path to permanent capital loss. It is also not a short: 21.4% short interest, 2.2 days to cover, and a rally off the July 29 low of $60.82 make the mechanics hostile.

## Company Overview

Formed in 2017, incorporated in 2018, platform launched 2020, IPO'd March 2025 at $40.00. CoreWeave operates a purpose-built AI cloud: GPU clusters with high-speed interconnect, AI-optimized storage, bare-metal control, the Mission Control orchestration and observability layer, and developer tooling augmented by the May 2025 Weights & Biases acquisition ($1,029M, of which $793M goodwill; results immaterial). One reportable segment, approximately 2,189 employees at FY2025, headquarters in Livingston, New Jersey.

The economics are those of a leveraged infrastructure lessor with a software wrapper. Property and equipment net was $36,424M at March 31, 2026 (technology equipment $26,600M gross, construction in progress $9,600M gross) against $4,759M of book equity. Revenue is 91.4% U.S. and 98% contract-committed. Every GPU in the fleet is NVIDIA's, and the filings state customers have contractually specified NVIDIA silicon. NVIDIA is simultaneously the sole supplier and, since a January 2026 private placement of ~23M Class A shares at $87.20, a $2.0bn shareholder. NVIDIA does not guarantee CoreWeave debt and is not disclosed as a purchaser of CoreWeave services.

A reconciliation item matters for every multiple in this report. The supplied market capitalization of $39.9bn equals 447.6M Class A shares at $89.05. Total shares outstanding are 545.6M (447.6M Class A plus 98.0M Class B, ten votes each, held entirely by the co-founders). Total equity value at the frozen price is therefore approximately $48.6bn, and I use that figure throughout.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement, Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025.** Revenue $2,078M vs $982M (+112%). Cost of revenue $716M, rising to 34% of revenue from 27%, driven by rent (+$249M), utilities and power (+$85M) and power-infrastructure depreciation (+$60M). Technology and infrastructure $1,273M (61% of revenue) vs $561M (57%), of which roughly $642M of the $712M increase was depreciation. Sales and marketing $69M from $11M. G&A $164M, down 6%. Operating loss $144M (-6.9% margin). Net interest expense $536M, up 103%. Tax provision $84M on a pre-tax loss — an effective rate of -13%, driven by a U.S. valuation allowance and rising tax depreciation. Net loss $740M; diluted EPS -$1.40 on 527M weighted-average shares.

Depreciation and amortization of $1,147M equals 55% of revenue. Add back D&A and $153M of net stock-based compensation to the operating loss and quarterly adjusted EBITDA is approximately $1,156M, a 55.6% margin, or roughly $4.6bn annualized. Annualized net interest of about $2.1bn (higher on the pro forma debt stack) consumes nearly half of that before any capital spending.

**Cash flow.** Reported operating cash flow of $2,984M decomposes into $968M before working capital and $2,016M from working capital — principally a $1,042M accounts-receivable decline (from $3,169M to $2,120M) and a $960M increase in payables and accruals. The receivable drawdown cannot repeat at that magnitude on a $2.1bn balance. Cash capex was $7,695M, giving free cash flow of **negative $4,711M in one quarter**; FY2025 was approximately negative $7.25bn. Cash interest paid net of capitalization was $364M, and capitalized interest was $97M versus $13M a year earlier, so reported operating loss understates the all-in current cost of the build and that cost will surface later as depreciation.

Gross capex is larger than the cash figure implies in a different sense: gross property and equipment rose $6,992M in the quarter while accrued purchases fell $3,243M and payables rose $1,748M. The pattern is consistent with cash settlement in Q1 of Q4 2025 equipment purchases alongside continued additions, plus $4,592M of non-cash liabilities for equipment and OEM-financed additions and $1,469M reclassified into debt on execution of OEM financings.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets $55,573M; total liabilities $50,814M; equity $4,759M with an accumulated deficit of $3,383M. Current assets $5,609M against current liabilities $17,818M — negative working capital of $12.2bn and a 0.31x current ratio, with $7,547M of that being current debt. Total principal debt $25,149M across twelve facilities at effective rates from 2% (2031 convertible) to 15% (DDTL 1.0). Adding $10,050M of operating lease liabilities gives $35.2bn of contractual financial obligations against $4.8bn of equity, roughly 7.4x. Maturities: $6,066M in the remainder of 2026, $5,652M in 2027 — $11.7bn inside 21 months.

April 2026 raised $4.0bn of 1.75% converts (with $492M of capped calls), $2.8bn of 9.75% senior notes due 2031, and $1.0bn of equity at $109.00, while repaying $1.5bn on the revolver and settling $172M of promissory notes in shares at $106.61. Pro forma principal debt is therefore approximately $30.3bn, roughly $40bn including operating leases. Liquidity at March 31 was $11,091M, of which $8,825M was undrawn commitments, and $1,076M of cash is restricted, largely for DDTL 4.0 forward interest, principal, swap and operating-expense coverage. The majority of Credit Facility borrowings are floating and, per the filing, largely unhedged; swap notional grew from $319M to $1,285M in the quarter.

**Off-balance-sheet obligations.** Executed-but-not-commenced leases carry $40.7bn of undiscounted payments (commencing 2026-2029, 5-16 year terms). Explicitly excluded from that figure is a single 525 MW site with total contractual rent of $18.7-19.6bn over 16 years, plus 363 MW of leases where rent is a percentage of lessor construction cost, plus $0.9-1.6bn of committed lessee-owned equipment. Recognized operating leases add $16,978M of undiscounted payments discounted at 10%. Gross contracted lease payments are therefore on the order of $77bn against $98.8bn of RPO. These are not directly comparable — different discounting, horizons, and RPO is net of variable-consideration haircuts — but the proximity of the two magnitudes is the entire equity story.

**Financial health: 2.5/10.** Altman Z near zero, 0.31x current ratio, 7.4x obligations-to-equity, $11.7bn of maturities inside 21 months, negative $4.7bn quarterly FCF, mostly unhedged floating exposure, and a 9.75% marginal unsecured coupon. Genuine mitigants: $11.1bn of liquidity, contract-collateralized amortizing DDTL structures with pledged cash flows "generally from investment grade counterparties," DDTL 4.0 non-recourse to the parent, falling effective rates across facility vintages (15% → 7%), and customer prepayments of 15-25% of committed contract value. The mitigants keep this from a 1-2; they do not make the structure resilient to a financing window closing.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 112% is among the highest in any $8bn-run-rate business. Management attributes approximately 38% of the increase to expansion within existing customers. Named commitments are large and dated: OpenAI ~$11.9bn through October 2030 (March 2025 MSA) and ~$6.5bn through May 2031 (May 2025 MSA / September 2025 order form) — reported separately because the filings do not establish they are additive; Meta ~$14.2bn through December 2031 (September 2025), escalating to an "initially committed to pay us up to approximately $21 billion" order form in March 2026 running to December 2032. FY2025 revenue was $5,131M.

Three constraints temper the growth score. First, RPO conversion requires a step-change in delivery: 36% of $98.8bn recognized in the 24 months to March 2028 implies roughly $4.45bn per quarter against $2,078M in Q1 2026 — quarterly revenue must more than double simply to consume the near-term layer. Second, RPO is defined net of estimated variable consideration including "amounts that may not be recognized as revenue due to delivery delays," and a delivery delay has already occurred (the November 2025 third-party data-center delay referenced in the risk factors). Third, earnings estimates are moving the other way and accelerating downward: current quarter -14.6% in 30 days, next quarter -21.6%, FY2026 -8.2%, FY2027 -9.4%. Three of the last four quarters missed consensus, including the most recent two (-11.2% and -22.3%).

The estimate-revision signal here is negative. There is no inflection buy signal to weigh.

**Growth prospects: 6.5/10.** Revenue trajectory and contracted pipeline are exceptional; the earnings trajectory, the delivery-execution requirement, and management's own statement that the growth rate "is expected to decline" cap the score well below what the top line alone would imply.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no meaningful P/E: trailing and forward EPS are negative (-$2.81 trailing; -$5.02 consensus for 2026, -$3.68 for 2027). Enterprise value is the only workable frame.

Equity value $48.6bn (545.6M shares at $89.05) plus pro forma debt of ~$30.3bn less cash of roughly $5bn post-April raises gives EV of approximately **$74bn**, or **$84bn** including the $10.05bn of recognized operating lease liabilities. Against annualized Q1 revenue of $8.3bn that is 8.9x to 10.1x EV/sales. Against annualized adjusted EBITDA of $4.6bn it is 16.1x to 18.3x. Neither figure adjusts for the roughly $60bn of undiscounted lease payments not yet recognized.

A forward frame, using a 2027 revenue path consistent with continued RPO conversion (call it $15-16bn) and a 55% adjusted EBITDA margin, produces roughly $8.3bn of 2027 adjusted EBITDA. At 12x, EV of $99.6bn less net debt and lease liabilities of roughly $52bn by then leaves $47.6bn of equity, or about **$85 per share on ~560M shares** — approximately today's price. At 10x the same EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $55; at 16x, roughly $164. The valuation is therefore not obviously wrong in either direction; it is a multiple-and-leverage bet where a two-turn change in the EBITDA multiple swings the equity by more than 50% because $52bn of obligations sit ahead of it.

Three market-based reference points bracket the price. NVIDIA bought equity at $87.20 in January 2026; an institutional placement cleared at $109.00 in April; insiders sold between $65.33 and $94.01 in July and early August. The stock at $89.05 is 42% below the October 2025 high of $153.20 and 47% above the July 29, 2026 low of $60.55. Analyst dispersion is extreme — low $36, high $303, mean $138.37, median $142 — which is the honest expression of a bimodal outcome set rather than a target to anchor on. At a 9.75% marginal unsecured coupon, the credit market's implied view is materially more cautious than the equity mean target.

**Valuation: 4.0/10.** No margin of safety at ~12x forward adjusted EBITDA when the capital structure is 7x equity, free cash flow is negative $4.7bn per quarter, and the loss estimates are being cut. The optionality is real but the price already embeds a successful RPO conversion.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural weakness is that CoreWeave's largest customers compete with it. Microsoft was approximately 67% of FY2025 revenue and operates Azure; Meta builds its own AI infrastructure at scale. CoreWeave sells specialized and overflow capacity to entities constructing the same capability internally, and an insourcing decision is a demand cliff rather than a gradual share shift. The mitigant is contract duration — roughly five-year weighted-average committed terms, with 39% of RPO landing in months 25-48 and the remainder in months 49-84.

Concentration has been re-shaped rather than reduced. The largest customer fell from 72% of revenue in Q1 2025 to 45% in Q1 2026, but the top two are 65%, and receivables are approximately 78% concentrated in three names (39% / 22% / 17%). Microsoft, OpenAI and Meta are all levered to the same AI capital-expenditure cycle, so counterparty correlation is high even as counterparty count rises. Counterparty credit quality has plausibly improved — two of the three are investment grade — which is a real offset for the DDTL collateral pools.

The genuine differentiators visible in filings are speed to deploy new NVIDIA architectures, the Mission Control orchestration layer, and the financing machine that converts contracted investment-grade cash flows into asset-level, amortizing, partly non-recourse debt. The first two are narrow and replicable; the third is differentiated and is also the primary risk. The filings assert first-to-market positioning and total-cost-of-ownership efficiency; they do not assert switching costs, and 98% contract-committed revenue with fixed capacity means renewals, not stickiness, determine the outcome.

Two trajectory items are negative. The company now expects to commit long-term power supply contracts in advance of securing customer contracts for new sites — a change from a purely contract-backed build. And the filings name "advancements in AI technology that may lead to further compute efficiencies" as a demand risk, which for a fleet carrying $26.6bn of gross technology equipment against $4.5bn of accumulated depreciation is a stranded-asset exposure.

**Competitive position: 4.3/10** (from 4.5). Power is the binding constraint and the company's first-listed risk; supply is single-vendor by contractual requirement; customers are competitors; and the marginal cost of the capital that funds the moat is rising.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing and solvency.** $11.7bn of principal due within 21 months of the balance-sheet date, against $11.1bn of liquidity of which $8.8bn is undrawn commitments subject to covenants. Debt service in Q1 alone was $1.8bn. Management states one-year sufficiency while also stating future investment "may require significant debt and/or equity financing." Interest-rate sensitivity is disclosed as approximately $32M per 100bp, on a period basis the filing leaves ambiguous — the implied unhedged floating balance differs roughly fourfold depending on whether that figure is quarterly or annual.

**Internal control.** The CEO and CFO concluded disclosure controls were **not effective** as of March 31, 2026, with three material weaknesses continuing: IT general controls, segregation of duties, and insufficient qualified accounting personnel. No remediation completion date. The company states no material misstatement resulted. The judgments at stake are unusually consequential: lease classification across ~$77bn of gross lease payments, VIE primary-beneficiary determinations across at least five off-balance-sheet vehicles, the ASC 210 netting of a $303M note receivable against $232M of financing obligation in the DCSP arrangement, $1,469M of OEM-financing reclassifications, placed-in-service dating that drives $1.1bn of quarterly depreciation, and $97M of capitalized interest. Material weaknesses are a statement about control design, not evidence that any figure is wrong; they widen the confidence interval around every number in this report.

**Unexplained accounting movement.** Deferred revenue fell from $8,185M to $7,523M, attributed primarily to a $1.3bn reclassification into "customer liabilities," which rose from $137M to $1,469M. The filing identifies neither counterparty nor reason. Competing readings — prepayments becoming refundable or subject to credit, a presentational change following contract restructuring, a step ahead of conversion into a financing arrangement, or amounts tied to capacity resale rights — all survive the evidence. This is the single largest disclosure gap in the file.

**Litigation.** A putative securities class action (*Masaitis v. CoreWeave*, D.N.J., filed January 12, 2026) and three derivative actions consolidated April 1, 2026. No loss accrued; possible loss not estimable. The specific allegations were not obtainable, so any linkage to the November 2025 delivery delay is hypothesis.

**Power.** The first-listed risk factor: grid constraints, planned utility outages in California, Colorado and Texas, and limited control of generators and fuel at leased sites.

**Dilution and insider supply.** 545.6M shares outstanding; 28M options at a $1.78 weighted-average strike and 30M unvested RSUs/RSAs add 58M shares, equal to 10.6% of the current base or 9.6 points of ownership dilution post-issuance. The 2031 and 2032 convertibles ($2.6bn and $4.0bn) create further contingent dilution that cannot be quantified without conversion prices; $492M of capped calls blunt part of it. The 2025 Plan carries a 5%-of-outstanding annual evergreen through 2035. Two disclosed 10b5-1 plans alone authorize up to ~12M shares (~2.7% of Class A) starting around June 8, 2026.

**Risk factors: 2.0/10.** Multiple live binaries — refinancing windows, a customer insourcing decision, control remediation, litigation, the unexplained $1.3bn reclassification — each capable of a large move, in a capital structure with 7x obligations to equity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$150, +68%).** RPO converts on schedule. Quarterly revenue doubles toward $4.4bn by 2027-2028, adjusted EBITDA margin holds near 55%, and the margin compression from 27% to 34% cost of revenue proves to be a capacity-cadence timing effect that reverses as sites reach full utilization. Depreciation stops growing faster than revenue, DDTL vintages keep repricing tighter (15% → 7%), the non-recourse structure ring-fences project risk, NVIDIA's equity position secures privileged Rubin-generation allocation, and Meta's escalation from $14.2bn to $21bn establishes that hyperscalers will keep outsourcing at the margin rather than insourcing everything. At 16x $9bn of 2027 adjusted EBITDA the equity clears $160.

**Bear case (~$38, -57%).** One of three things closes the financing window: a rate backup at the long end, an AI-credit repricing, or a demand disappointment at a frontier lab. CoreWeave then faces $11.7bn of maturities and multi-billion quarterly capex with negative free cash flow, and raises equity at a distressed price or renegotiates leases on $60bn of undiscounted future commitments. Alternatively, the cost-of-revenue rise from 27% to 34% turns out to be permanent repricing on the newer Meta and OpenAI contracts rather than timing, in which case the 55% adjusted EBITDA margin is the peak and the depreciation load never gets covered. Analyst low target is $36.

**Base case (~$80, -10%).** Revenue roughly doubles over two years, adjusted EBITDA reaches $8-8.5bn in 2027, and the market applies ~12x to it — leaving equity value approximately where it trades today after another $20bn+ of obligations are layered ahead of shareholders. Probability-weighting 45% base, 25% bull, 30% bear gives an expected return of roughly -5% with a standard deviation far wider than any portfolio should accept for that payoff.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 2026 earnings [unverified — the research brief does not establish the Q2 2026 report date; no earnings-call transcript or forward date was retrievable]. The 13-day option tenor prices ±17.6%, which is consistent with an event before August 21. The specific short thesis would be a third consecutive miss with widening losses consistent with the 30-day estimate cuts, plus any disclosure on the $1.3bn customer-liability reclassification or further delivery delays. Secondary catalyst: the next Item 2.03 financing, which on the observed cadence of roughly every 6-10 weeks is due imminently and would reveal the current cost of capital.

**Timing window.** Days, not months, for the earnings catalyst — attractive on carry. But the structural thesis (refinancing wall) resolves over 2026-2027, well beyond a tolerable carry horizon at these borrow and volatility levels.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 21.4% of float, 64.4M shares short, but only 2.2 days to cover against 28.8M average daily volume. The name demonstrated its squeeze capacity in the past two weeks: $60.82 close on July 29 to $94.30 intraday on August 4, a 55% swing in four sessions.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options do not confirm the bearish case. ATM IV fell 23 points since July 21, put/call open interest halved to 0.58, and skew is call-heavy at -16.7%. Put protection at a 95% IV costs roughly 8-9% of notional for a one-month hold — a heavy premium against a stock whose base case is -10%.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear $38 (-57%); realistic upside risk on a beat or a further NVIDIA-adjacent announcement is $115-125 (+30-40%). That is roughly 1.5:1 reward-to-risk before borrow cost, below the 2:1 threshold, and the tail on the short side is uncapped in a name backed by its own chip supplier.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish analysis is sound and the catalyst is dated, but 21.4% short interest, sub-3-day cover, demonstrated 40%+ squeeze behavior, call-heavy options positioning and a $303 high target make the mechanics unfavorable. Avoid the equity; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: AVOID. No position, long or short.**

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move this to HOLD, and a combination of the first, third and fourth would justify ACCUMULATE:**

1. **Free cash flow inflection.** Two consecutive quarters with cash capex covered by pre-working-capital operating cash flow, or a stated capex plan that closes the current ~$4.7bn quarterly gap. This is the single most important condition.
2. **Cost of capital confirmation.** A new unsecured or DDTL issuance priced materially inside the April 2026 9.75% coupon, demonstrating that credit-market access has improved.
3. **Control remediation.** Disclosure controls concluded effective, with all three material weaknesses remediated and a clean ICFR opinion.
4. **Margin resolution.** Cost of revenue retracing from 34% toward the prior 27% of revenue for two consecutive quarters, which would settle the timing-versus-permanent-repricing question that filings cannot currently answer.
5. **Disclosure of the $1.3bn reclassification** — counterparty and reason — with a benign explanation.
6. **Price.** Below roughly $45-50, where the equity trades near 7-8x forward adjusted EBITDA on the same obligation stack and the bear case is substantially priced.

**Conditions that would harden the AVOID toward an active short (i.e., break the crowded-short constraint):** short interest falling below 12% while the fundamental picture deteriorates; a customer insourcing announcement or contract renegotiation from Microsoft, Meta or OpenAI; a covenant waiver request; or an equity raise below $70.

**Expected duration of headwinds:** through at least mid-2027, tied to the $11.7bn maturity ladder and the RPO delivery ramp. Next natural reassessment: the Q2 2026 print, and then any Item 2.03 filing.

## Conclusion

CoreWeave has built an $8bn revenue run-rate in five years and holds $98.8bn of contracted performance obligations, which is a genuine commercial achievement. It has done so by assembling roughly $30bn of debt, $10bn of recognized lease liabilities and roughly $60bn of undiscounted lease payments not yet on the balance sheet, funded by returning to the capital markets every six to ten weeks, most recently at a 9.75% unsecured coupon. Free cash flow was negative $4.7bn in a single quarter. Book equity is $4.8bn behind $50.8bn of liabilities. Three material weaknesses remain unremediated and the certifying officers state disclosure controls are not effective.

The score declines from 4.0 to 3.9 not because the price moved but because the loss estimates were cut 15-22% over thirty days while pro forma leverage rose roughly $5bn and the price rose 11.5%. At $89.05 the base case is approximately flat to modestly negative, the bull case requires a doubling of revenue and a multiple expansion, and the bear case is a distressed recapitalization. That distribution belongs in a venture allocation, not an equity book, and the short side is closed off by 21.4% short interest and a demonstrated ability to rally sharply in a handful of sessions. AVOID.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: repeated references to a "$2.6bn loan reprice 100-125bps wider with maintenance covenants restored" are not supported anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed/tagged; and the Q2 2026 earnings date used as the primary short catalyst is not established in the sources. The bear/credit-signal argument leaned partly on the unsupported loan-reprice claim.
