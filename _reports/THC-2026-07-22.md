**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tenet Healthcare is a two-segment healthcare operator that has transformed itself from a leverage-laden acute-care hospital chain into an ambulatory-surgery-led business with a high-margin growth engine. The crown jewel is United Surgical Partners International (USPI), the largest US ambulatory surgery center operator, holding interests in 541 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals across 37 states. In Q1 2026, USPI grew revenue 10.6% at a 31.7% operating margin, against +0.5% revenue growth and a 21.7% margin at the legacy hospital business. The secular migration of surgical procedures from inpatient to lower-cost outpatient settings favors this mix shift, and management is compounding it through de novo ASC development, tuck-in M&A, and buying in physician-partner minority stakes.

The stock trades at roughly 11.0x forward earnings against current-year consensus EPS of $17.85, which has risen 3.9% over the past 90 days. Tenet has beaten estimates by 14.1%, 14.8%, and 10.9% over the last three quarters — a 100% beat rate with double-digit magnitude. The capital-return program has cut share count ~9% in 15 months (95.1M to 86.1M shares), with $1.172B remaining on the buyback authorization. Operating cash flow was $1,641M in Q1 2026, the balance sheet carries a fully undrawn $1.9B revolver, and debt is 100% fixed-rate with staggered maturities out to 2033.

The dominant risk is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), whose Medicaid provisions — caps on state-directed payments, provider-tax limits, and DSH cuts beginning October 1, 2027 — threaten the government-payer cash flows that partly service Tenet's debt. Sizing that exposure rather than treating it as unknowable implies a phased ~8-15% earnings headwind over 2027-2029, material but partly offset by OBBBA-insulated ambulatory growth and the buyback flywheel. Against a cheap multiple, rising estimates, and a street where even the lowest price target ($210) sits above the current $196.49, the risk/reward favors ownership. I rate THC a BUY.

## Company Overview

Tenet operates through two reportable segments. **Ambulatory Care (USPI)** held interests in 541 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals across 37 states as of Q1 2026, with 407 ASCs consolidated and 152 facilities held via the equity method with non-controlling stakes. USPI's model pairs scale with physician joint-venture ownership, which aligns physician incentives and concentrates the portfolio in attractive growth markets (Texas, Florida, California). Procedure focus skews toward orthopedics, total joints, spine, GI, ophthalmology, urology, ENT, and pain management — elective, commercially-oriented cases with structurally higher margins than inpatient work.

**Hospital Operations & Services** comprises 50 acute-care and specialty hospitals across 8 states, 132 outpatient facilities (urgent care, imaging, off-campus EDs, microhospitals), an employed physician network, and Conifer Health Solutions, a revenue-cycle management (RCM) business. This segment is the more policy-exposed, government-payer-heavy part of the company, and it is where the OBBBA and ACA-subsidy headwinds bite hardest.

The strategic direction over the past two years has been deliberate: divest capital-intensive, lower-margin hospitals (roughly 14 sold in 2024), deleverage, and redeploy toward the higher-return ambulatory franchise. The Conifer restructuring completed in early 2026 (detailed below) simplifies the corporate structure further.

## Financial Analysis

Tenet's reported income statement is distorted by large one-time items in consecutive years, and reading the headline numbers naively produces the wrong conclusion.

FY2024 operating income of ~$5.96B was inflated by a ~$2.9B one-time gain from divesting hospitals. FY2025 operating income of ~$3.5B looks like a collapse against that but is actually the cleaner, normalized figure — underlying operations improved. The same pattern recurred in Q1 2026: diluted EPS jumped to $8.01 from $4.27 a year earlier (+88%), but that includes a $413M "revenue from contract termination" gain tied to the Conifer/CHI deal. Adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat at ~$1,162M, with the $413M gain sitting below the EBITDA line, and Ambulatory EBITDA grew to $484M from $456M. A standardized data pull would misread both years; the underlying operating momentum (USPI +10.6% revenue, hospital pricing and acuity gains) is genuinely positive, not deteriorating.

Q1 2026 net operating revenue was $5,368M, up 2.8% YoY, with Ambulatory at $1,320M versus Hospital Operations at $4,048M. Operating cash flow surged from $815M, boosted by a $540M CHI contract-termination payment and $75M lower interest expense.

On leverage: the Altman Z-Score of ~1.57 nominally sits in the distress zone, and D/E is ~4.9x on ~$13.2B long-term debt against ~$4.8B equity. Read in isolation this looks alarming, but the context substantially defuses it. The debt is 100% fixed-rate (4.25%-6.875%) with staggered maturities from November 2027 through 2033 and no floating-rate exposure. Cash is ~$2.97B, the company is in compliance with all covenants, ROE is ~56%, and quarterly operating cash flow runs ~$1.6B. This is a deliberately-leveraged, high-cash-generative operator, not a distressed credit. The genuine leverage risk is event-driven — OBBBA cutting the government-payer cash flows that service the debt — rather than near-term refinancing risk.

Capital allocation is aggressively shareholder-friendly. Tenet repurchased $318M in Q1 2026 at ~$236/share. It also spent $120M on 7 ASCs in Q1 and $549M buying in NCI minority stakes (mostly the Conifer/CHI redemption). There is no dividend; capital returns run entirely through buybacks. FY2026 capex is guided to $700-800M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on USPI. The ambulatory segment is compounding revenue in double digits at a 31.7% operating margin, driven by the secular shift of surgical procedures to outpatient settings, de novo center development, tuck-in acquisitions, and the consolidation of physician-partner minority stakes that pull more segment earnings onto Tenet's income statement. This is a durable, multi-year tailwind that is largely commercial-payer and elective, which insulates it from the Medicaid-focused OBBBA provisions.

The hospital segment tells a more mixed story. Q1 2026 same-hospital metrics showed total ED visits -3.9%, total surgeries -0.9%, patient days -1.3%, and licensed-bed utilization down to 53.4%. Hospital growth is being carried by pricing and acuity, not volume, and the Street is watching Q2 patient days closely. This volume softness is the near-term operational question mark, though it is offset by mix and pricing.

Estimate revisions are supportive rather than warning. Next-year consensus sits at $17.79, up 1.7%; the current-quarter estimate rose 1.9% over 30 days. The three-quarter beat streak gives me confidence the current consensus is more likely a floor than a stretch. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a trough-earnings inflection, but it is steady positive momentum on an already-profitable base, which is what growth_prospects should reward when confirmed by a strong beat record and a real structural growth engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $196.49, THC trades at ~11.0x forward EPS of $17.85. The stock is up ~12.5% over the trailing year but sits well below its 52-week high of $247.21, having retraced through spring and early summer 2026 on OBBBA and volume concerns. The trailing multiple is not distorted by a trough — earnings are near normalized, and the low-teens multiple reflects the market's discount for the 2027 policy cliff rather than a value trap on structural impairment.

The valuation dimension deserves particular scrutiny here, because a cheap multiple is more often a warning than a signal. The case for THC being genuinely mispriced rather than a value trap rests on three legs. First, there is an identifiable reason the discount exists (OBBBA), and when sized it is manageable relative to the earnings base — a diffuse, well-telegraphed, 15-months-out headwind, not a structural erosion of the franchise. Second, the ambulatory growth engine is real and OBBBA-insulated, so the business is not in secular decline. Third, the buyback flywheel converts the cheap multiple into per-share accretion: the share-count reduction at a low-teens multiple compounds EPS mechanically.

On the analyst set, the entire street sits above spot: LOW target $210 (~+7%), median $245, mean $244 (~+24%), high $288, with 6 strong-buy, 14 buy, 2 hold, and zero sells. On a name at 11.0x forward with rising estimates, analyst targets function as a floor rather than a ceiling — the street is already looking through the policy overhang and finding upside. BofA's July 2026 reaffirmed Buy at a trimmed $230 target captures the constructive-but-cautious consensus. I score valuation a 7.0: there is a specific, sizable reason the market is discounting the stock, and my own sizing of that reason shows it does not justify the full discount.

My base case is $245, applying ~13.5x to a modestly de-risked ~$18 forward EPS as OBBBA visibility improves and the buyback continues — in line with the street median and consistent with a company whose growth engine and cash generation support a higher multiple than the current 11.0x.

## Competitive Landscape

In acute care, Tenet competes against HCA Healthcare (the scale leader), Community Health Systems, and Universal Health Services. In ambulatory, USPI competes with Surgery Partners, HCA's ASC network, and hospital-affiliated outpatient centers. USPI's scale as the largest US ASC operator, combined with its physician-partnership model, gives it a structural advantage in the fastest-growing part of the industry.

The competitive trajectory is improving. Tenet's deliberate pivot toward higher-margin, commercially-oriented ambulatory revenue is a partial hedge against the sector-wide headwind that hits all hospital operators uniformly: ACA subsidy expiry (2025) and OBBBA Medicaid cuts (2027) threaten volumes and payer mix across the group. Because ASCs skew toward commercially-insured elective procedures, Tenet's mix shift positions it better than pure acute-care peers. The +1.0% proposed FY2027 IPPS update for proprietary hospitals keeps margin pressure on the legacy segment, reinforcing the logic of the ambulatory tilt. I score competitive position a 7.0 on the improving trajectory of the mix shift and USPI's widening scale advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**OBBBA / Medicaid overhang (dominant risk).** The Act brings Medicaid work requirements, caps on state-directed payments, provider-tax limits, and stricter eligibility checks, with the most consequential provisions taking effect in 2027. Management states it cannot estimate the impact. Sizing it against the disclosed exposure: Q1 2026 included $304M of supplemental/DSH revenue (~5.7% of revenue), or roughly $1.2B annualized. A plausible 20-35% erosion over 2027-2029, treated as high-margin flow-through, implies roughly $1.50-2.60 of phased annual EPS risk against a ~$17.85 base — an ~8-15% headwind, partly offset by ambulatory growth and buybacks. This is material and warrants a below-neutral risk score, but it is neither thesis-breaking nor imminent.

**ACA subsidy expiration (already biting).** Enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired at end-2025. Q1 2026 charity/uninsured admissions rose 9.7% same-hospital, and charity care costs doubled ($17M to $34M YoY). This is the leading edge of payer-mix deterioration and the metric to watch in Q2/Q3 2026 prints.

**Minority-interest cash drain.** USPI's JV structure sends a meaningful chunk of segment earnings to physician partners ($197M of NCI distributions in Q1), plus periodic put-option buy-in obligations.

**Conifer roll-off.** The CHI RCM revenue rolls off after 2026, muddying 2027 comparisons. Netted against the cash inflow redeployed into buybacks and the full NCI consolidation of Conifer, the normalized 2027 EPS drag is low-single-digit percent.

**Standard sector risks:** qui tam/litigation exposure, cybersecurity, and ~$1.24B malpractice self-insurance reserves.

I score risk factors a 5.0 — the OBBBA cliff is a real, sized, specific catalyst for material downside, but it is well-telegraphed, 15 months out, and partly priced into the low-teens multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 57.6% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 40.4% (a 17.2pp spread) and sits at the 83rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (57.6% near-term versus 49.2% at six months), consistent with an imminent catalyst — the upcoming Q2 earnings print, where the Street is focused on patient-day softness. The put/call volume ratio of 3.50 looks bearish, but the put/call skew is only -1.6% (roughly balanced), and the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 3.14 to 1.35 in recent days, suggesting the earlier put-heavy positioning is unwinding rather than building. The implied move of ±13.5% into the near-term expiry is large, and the rich IV means the market is charging for uncertainty into the print. For a fundamentally-driven buyer, the elevated near-term vol argues for scaling entry and letting the post-earnings IV crush work in your favor rather than paying up for optionality ahead of the event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-288):** USPI continues compounding double-digit ambulatory revenue at 30%+ margins, hospital volumes stabilize and pricing/acuity carries the segment, and the buyback retires another ~8-10% of shares over the next 12-18 months. OBBBA implementation proves less severe than feared (state-directed payment programs partly preserved through waivers/redesign), and the multiple re-rates from 11.0x toward the mid-teens as the 2027 overhang clears. At ~14.5x a ~$19 forward EPS, the stock reaches the high street targets.

**Bear Case ($150-165):** Hospital volume softness deepens (ED visits and patient days keep declining), ACA-driven uninsured counts accelerate payer-mix deterioration, and OBBBA lands at the harsher end of the range, taking the full ~15% earnings bite. The market re-rates the legacy hospital business lower on structural government-payer risk, and the stock compresses toward ~9x a lower normalized EPS. This is roughly -16% to -24% from current levels.

The asymmetry favors the bull case: the downside is a sized, partly-priced policy headwind, while the upside combines a genuine growth engine, a cheap multiple, rising estimates, and a mechanical buyback tailwind.

3. Management guiding to a quantified OBBBA earnings impact materially above the ~15% ceiling in my sizing, or drawing on the revolver to fund buybacks (a capital-allocation red flag).

## Conclusion

Tenet is a cheap, cash-generative compounder with a genuine structural growth engine (USPI) trading at ~11.0x forward earnings against rising estimates, a 100% recent beat rate, and an aggressive buyback that has cut share count ~9% in 15 months. The balance sheet, while nominally leveraged, is 100% fixed-rate with staggered maturities, an undrawn $1.9B revolver, ~$3B cash, and ~$1.6B quarterly operating cash flow — a deliberately-leveraged operator, not a distressed credit.

The bear case rests almost entirely on the 2027 OBBBA Medicaid cliff. When sized against the disclosed ~$304M/quarter of supplemental exposure, that cliff implies a phased ~8-15% earnings headwind over 2027-2029, partly offset by OBBBA-insulated ambulatory growth and the buyback flywheel. It lands 15+ months from now, is sector-wide rather than THC-specific, and is already partly discounted in the low-teens multiple and the analyst price-target floor of $210 that sits above spot. Neutralizing a name with this much positive operating momentum, cheap valuation, rising estimates, and a supportive street on a diffuse, well-telegraphed, distant policy overhang is the wrong call. I rate THC a **BUY** with a base-case target of $245.
