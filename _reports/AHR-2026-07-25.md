**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Healthcare REIT is an internally managed, diversified healthcare REIT that sits at the operating-intensive end of the sector. Roughly 76% of its portfolio is senior housing and skilled nursing, and much of that income is earned through RIDEA/operating structures rather than triple-net leases. The Trilogy integrated-senior-health-campus platform (ISHC) is the crown jewel: about 45% of the portfolio by purchase price but roughly 55.5% of annualized NOI. This gives AHR outsized leverage to the senior-housing occupancy recovery, where rising occupancy and pricing power flow directly to the landlord-operator rather than being capped by fixed rent.

The setup that produced the stock's ~52% twelve-month gain is genuine. Revenue grew from ~$1.41B (FY2022) to ~$2.09B (FY2025), the company turned GAAP-profitable in FY2025 (net income ~$69.8M versus a ~$(37.8)M loss in FY2024), leverage was cut to below 30% of total (equity + debt) market cap, and all variable-rate debt is hedged with $550M of swaps. Same-store NOI at senior-housing operators has been running well above the ~2.6% REIT sector median as occupancy recovers and new supply stays starved by high construction costs. AHR is a direct, high-beta beneficiary of that imbalance.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the intersection of price, estimates, and governance. At $57.44 the stock trades near the middle of the published analyst target range on a per-share funds-from-operations basis (trailing/forward P/E near 98x on GAAP EPS is not the right lens for a REIT, but the dividend yield has compressed to 1.75% versus a 5.8% peer median, and current-year FFO/NFFO estimates are being revised down ~22-25% over 90 days). Layered on top is an unresolved CEO medical leave, a concentrated dependence on a single external manager for the majority of NOI, and a deliberate multi-year dilution strategy that the market must be paid to accept. The growth thesis is intact; the entry point is not attractive.

## Company Overview

AHR owns and operates senior housing, skilled nursing facilities (SNFs), outpatient medical (OM) buildings, and other clinical real estate across the U.S., the UK, and the Isle of Man. Portfolio composition as of 12/31/2025 (by aggregate contract purchase price): senior housing 49.1%, SNFs 26.9%, outpatient medical 19.8%, international 1.1%. The company traces to the Griffin-American Healthcare REIT franchise and listed on the NYSE in early 2024.

The defining structural feature is the operating model. Two segments carry the operational risk and reward:

- **Trilogy (ISHC):** Integrated senior health campuses combining senior housing, assisted living, memory care, and skilled nursing with regional density. All Trilogy campuses are managed by Trilogy Management Services, LLC. This segment contributes roughly 55.5% of annualized NOI. AHR has been buying in the minority interests to move toward full ownership.
- **SHOP (Senior Housing Operating Portfolio):** RIDEA-structured senior housing where AHR bears operating economics directly.

The mechanism matters for both the bull and bear case. In a recovering cycle, an operator captures the full spread between rising revenue-per-occupied-room and slower-growing costs, upside a triple-net landlord would forgo. In a downturn, or when nursing labor re-inflates, that same operator absorbs the margin compression that a NNN tenant would have borne. AHR is a levered play on the direction of senior-housing operating fundamentals rather than a bond-like rent collector.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue growth over FY2022–FY2025 was driven by occupancy recovery and consolidation of Trilogy and SHOP. GAAP net income is a poor proxy for a growth-phase REIT — depreciation, interest, and acquisition costs suppress the bottom line — so FFO/NFFO and same-store NOI are the operative metrics. The GAAP EPS of $0.42 and the resulting ~98x headline P/E should be disregarded; they are accounting artifacts of a heavily-depreciated real estate balance sheet, not a signal of overvaluation on their own.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$1.5B ($550M unsecured revolver/lines plus ~$986M secured mortgages). Sub-30% leverage is conservative for the sector and the product of deliberate deleveraging. All variable-rate debt is hedged via interest-rate swaps, materially limiting near-term rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment. This is one of the cleaner balance sheets among healthcare REITs and is the primary support for the financial-health score.

**Capital allocation and dilution.** Shares outstanding expanded from ~66M to ~166M+ over recent periods (192.7M on the latest count) through an ATM program and follow-on offerings. The proceeds funded deleveraging and the Trilogy minority buy-in. This is a coherent strategy — dilute to own more of the highest-NOI-margin asset and clean the balance sheet — but it imposes a real cost. Per-share NFFO growth must be judged net of that dilution, and the bull case rests on the claim that incremental Trilogy ownership at high NOI margins more than offsets the share issuance. That is plausible but not yet proven at scale in reported per-share numbers.

**Earnings surprise and estimate trend — the caution.** The beat/miss record is noisy (three beats, one miss over four quarters, with the Q1 2026 print of $0.50 far above a near-zero estimate, a sign estimates are poorly calibrated to a lumpy REIT earnings stream rather than a clean beat signal). Consensus is moving the wrong way: current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 25% over 30 days, current-year estimates down ~25% over 90 days, and next-quarter down ~15-21%. Only the next-year estimate is flat-to-slightly-positive (+1.8% over 90 days). Falling near-term estimates into a rising stock price is the classic warning that price has run ahead of fundamentals. This is the single most important reason not to chase the name here.

**Cash flow quality.** Operating cash flow supports the distribution, but as an operator-heavy REIT, FCF is exposed to Trilogy/SHOP labor costs. Distributions are board-discretionary and restricted under the 2024 credit facility (up to $1.15B) in a default scenario.

## Growth Analysis

The demographic and supply backdrop is the durable part of the thesis. The 80+ cohort is inflecting upward, new senior-housing development has been starved by elevated rates and construction costs, and the resulting occupancy and pricing recovery flows disproportionately to operating landlords. AHR's RIDEA/ISHC tilt makes it a high-beta expression of exactly this trend.

Three signals moderate the growth score from a higher reading:

1. **Estimate revisions are negative in the near term.** The +30%-over-90-days inflection signal that would argue strongly for a BUY is absent here — current-year estimates are being cut, not raised. Only the out-year holds up.
2. **Growth is partly bought with equity.** Revenue growth is impressive in absolute terms but is amplified by consolidation funded through dilution. The relevant metric is per-share NFFO, and the market has not yet seen a clean multi-quarter run of accelerating per-share growth net of the share count expansion.
3. **CMS reimbursement headwind.** The 10-K flags November 2025 CMS changes reducing reimbursement for certain outpatient products/services beginning in 2026, a modest drag on the ~20% OM segment, plus federal proposals to cut Medicaid funding that would hit the SNF book.

The senior-housing recovery has multi-year runway and multiple confirming operators, which gives the top-line growth thesis durability. But the near-term earnings trajectory is being marked down, and that tension keeps growth prospects at a solid-but-not-standout level.

## Valuation Assessment

On a REIT-appropriate basis, AHR looks fully valued to modestly expensive at $57.44:

- **Dividend yield of 1.75% versus a 5.8% peer median** (CTRE 4.1%, OHI 5.8%, HR 4.7%, DOC 6.0%, SBRA 6.4%). AHR's yield is the lowest in the comp set by a wide margin, reflecting the market's willingness to pay a growth premium for the RIDEA/Trilogy operating leverage. That premium is defensible given superior NOI growth, but it means there is no yield support cushioning the stock.
- **Price near the middle of the analyst target range.** The analyst target band is $55 low / $60 median / $70 high, mean $60.07. At $57.44 the stock sits between the low and median target — roughly 4.4% below the mean and 4.4% above the low. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap inflection stocks with rising estimates; this is the opposite case, with estimates falling and the stock near the middle of the range. The targets here are not a signal of trapped upside.
- **The trailing/forward P/E of ~98x is a GAAP artifact** and should be set aside; the correct read is that the growth premium is already embedded in a compressed cap rate / low yield relative to peers.

The valuation dimension scores below the midpoint. This is not a value trap in the impaired-fundamentals sense — the business is improving — but the stock is not cheap, and there is no identifiable catalyst that the market is mispricing at the current level. The senior-housing recovery is broadly understood and reflected in the premium multiple. A high valuation score would require either a compressed multiple with a catalyst or cycle-adjusted earnings power well above what is priced; neither is present. Cheapness signals in this sector have historically sorted poorly, and AHR is not even cheap on the relevant metrics.

## Competitive Landscape

AHR competes with Welltower, Ventas, Omega Healthcare, National Health Investors, and Sabra. Its differentiation is the operational (RIDEA) tilt and the Trilogy platform, a needs-driven, higher-acuity, integrated-campus model with regional Midwest density. In a recovering cycle this positioning is a genuine advantage: AHR grows NOI faster than NNN-focused peers.

The competitive trajectory is improving but not without friction. AHR competes for acquisitions against larger players with lower cost of capital (Welltower, Ventas), which pressures cap rates on new deals and constrains accretive external growth. The moat is the Trilogy platform and its integrated campuses, which are hard to replicate, but the reliance on a single external manager for that platform is both the source of the edge and a structural vulnerability. On balance, the trajectory favors AHR through this cycle as occupancy and pricing gains accrue to the operator model, which supports an above-average competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

Several risks are specific, current, and not fully priced:

1. **Trilogy Manager dependency — the dominant risk.** One external manager, Trilogy Management Services, runs all ISHC. AHR depends entirely on Trilogy's personnel, systems, and campus-level reporting. Financial distress, key-personnel loss, staffing shortage, or a management-agreement termination would be extremely difficult to replace (requiring regulatory and lender approvals) and could be materially adverse. AHR is simultaneously increasing this exposure (buying in minority interests) while remaining reliant on the manager.

2. **RIDEA operating risk.** Labor inflation and nursing shortages flow straight through to margins on RIDEA/SHOP/ISHC properties, unlike triple-net leases. A re-acceleration in wage growth compresses NOI directly.

3. **Geographic concentration.** Indiana (~33.4%) and Ohio (~11.5%) together are ~45% of annualized base rent/NOI. A state-level reimbursement, regulatory, or economic shock would be magnified.

4. **CEO medical leave — live governance overhang.** The 10-K discloses in unusually candid language that CEO Danny Prosky is on medical leave and that the company "cannot predict when Mr. Prosky may be able to resume his duties," with Chairman and former CEO (2015–2021) Jeffrey T. Hanson serving as Interim CEO. Hanson's deep history partially mitigates the disruption, but the open-ended nature of the leave is an unresolved risk.

5. **Reimbursement/regulatory risk.** SNFs and senior housing depend on Medicare/Medicaid. November 2025 CMS outpatient reimbursement cuts and proposed Medicaid funding reductions are dated, specific headwinds.

6. **Dilution.** The strategy relies on continued equity issuance, which the market must be compensated for through per-share NFFO growth that outpaces the share count.

7. **REIT-status complexity.** The RIDEA structure requires properties leased to TRSs operated by eligible independent contractors; mis-classification is a technical but existential tax risk.

Offsetting these: sub-30% leverage and fully hedged variable debt make balance-sheet and rate risk genuinely low, which is the primary reason risk factors are not scored lower.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 41.2% versus 24.7% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 69th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 41.2% falling to 29.0% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress or a catalyst, with an implied move of roughly ±9-11.8% into the nearest expiry (likely the next earnings print). The put/call skew is modestly call-heavy (-4.7%), which does not confirm a sharply bearish tilt in options positioning despite the falling estimates. IV rose sharply (31.4% → 44.5%) alongside the +22% price move since late June, consistent with a stock that has run into an event. For a holder, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors waiting for the post-event volatility crush before adding. Short interest at 11.8% of float (4.7 days to cover) reflects moderate institutional bearishness — meaningful but not at squeeze-risk levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$68-72).** The senior-housing occupancy recovery has multiple years of runway against structurally constrained supply. AHR's RIDEA/Trilogy operating leverage captures that recovery faster than NNN peers, and the ongoing Trilogy minority buy-in increases ownership of the highest-margin asset. Per-share NFFO accelerates as consolidation completes and dilution slows, the market re-rates the operating model, the balance sheet supports opportunistic acquisitions, and the yield compression proves justified. Analyst high target of $70 is achievable if next-year estimates (the one revision cohort still positive) prove correct.

**Bear case (~$46-48).** Nursing labor re-inflates and flows straight through RIDEA margins, occupancy recovery stalls, and CMS/Medicaid cuts bite the OM and SNF books. The CEO leave becomes prolonged or the Trilogy manager relationship encounters friction, forcing a governance discount. Falling current-year estimates continue, the market re-prices the growth premium toward the peer yield, and the yield offers no downside cushion. A move back to the low-to-mid $40s (where the stock traded as recently as early June 2026) is plausible on any of these, roughly 15-20% downside.

The base case is that the growth cycle continues but the current price already reflects most of the near-term upside, with estimate cuts and governance overhang capping the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth hold** over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the high-$40s / low-$50s that restores an asymmetric entry (a ~10-15% decline on unchanged fundamentals would materially improve risk/reward).
- Current-year FFO/NFFO estimate revisions turn positive and same-store SHOP/ISHC NOI growth is confirmed above the sector median in the next two prints.
- Resolution of the CEO succession (Prosky's return or a clean permanent appointment) removing the governance overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of nursing-labor re-inflation compressing SHOP/ISHC operating margins in reported results.
- Any disruption to the Trilogy management relationship, or a material step-down in Trilogy NOI.
- A dividend that fails to grow while dilution continues, signaling per-share value leakage.
- Continued negative estimate revisions extending into the out-year (which is currently the one supportive cohort).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (the near-term event the options market is pricing), with the CMS 2026 reimbursement impact and updated full-year NFFO guidance as the specific data points to check. The rich, backwardated near-term IV argues for patience on any addition until after that print.

## Conclusion

AHR is a well-run, defensively-financed operating REIT with a real structural tailwind. The balance sheet is clean, the senior-housing recovery is durable, and the Trilogy platform is a genuine differentiator that captures operating leverage NNN peers cannot. The trajectory of the underlying business justifies the improvement in the financial-health and valuation scores over the past several reports.

The problem is the price. At $57.44 — near a 52-week high after a ~52% twelve-month run — the stock trades near the middle of the analyst target range on the metrics that matter, offers a 1.75% yield versus peers, and is seeing current-year estimates cut even as the shares rally. Layer on an open-ended CEO medical leave and a majority-of-NOI dependence on a single external manager, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. I would own AHR for exposure to the senior-housing cycle but would not add at this level. **Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Reassess for a BUY on a pullback toward the low-$50s, a positive turn in current-year estimate revisions, or resolution of the CEO succession.
