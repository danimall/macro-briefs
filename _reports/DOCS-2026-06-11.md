**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Doximity operates the dominant professional network for U.S. physicians, with more than 3 million registered members covering over 85% of U.S. doctors. The business monetizes that network primarily through pharmaceutical and health-system marketing, supplemented by hiring and workflow/clinical-AI tools. On a static basis it is among the highest-quality software franchises in healthcare: ~89% gross margin, ~55% adjusted EBITDA margin, $317M of FY26 free cash flow, $748.6M of cash and securities, and effectively no debt. The problem is the trajectory. Revenue growth has stepped down from 20% (FY25) to 13% (FY26) to +5.1% in the March quarter, net revenue retention fell from 119% to 109%, GAAP net income declined 12% as stock-based compensation jumped 68%, and FY27 guidance landed below consensus. The May 13 print drove the stock down roughly 24% to a record low and crystallized a decline of roughly 74% from the September 2025 high of $76.51.

The prior view here was a BUY at $26 issued shortly before that print; the deceleration confirmed at the report invalidated the constructive case, and the rating is reset accordingly. The change is fundamental: the saturation of the U.S. physician market, the slowing of customer expansion (NRR), and the margin compression from a self-described "AI investment year" are visible in the actuals and the guide, and the Street cut estimates ~12% across horizons.

This is a HOLD rather than an AVOID because the bear case is now largely in the price. At ~$20.24 the stock trades at ~12.7x next-year consensus EPS and ~6x free cash flow net of cash, with net cash equal to roughly 28% of market capitalization and no refinancing or solvency risk. The downside to a net-cash-supported floor in the mid-teens is on the order of 25%, against a credible reacceleration/short-squeeze path toward the low-$30s. The most aggressive competitive claim, that a clinical-AI rival already reaches two-thirds of U.S. physicians, comes from company self-reporting and unaudited press coverage, which is too thin a foundation for a structural-impairment short. The honest classification is an uncertain hold: the deceleration is real, the floor is real, and the decisive competitive variable is unverified.

## Company Overview

Doximity is a digital professional network for medical professionals, frequently described as "LinkedIn for doctors." The platform is free for clinicians and monetizes through three customer-paid solution lines:

- **Marketing Solutions**: the dominant revenue driver, largely subscription-based; pharmaceutical manufacturers and health systems pay for targeted, verified-physician reach on a brand/service-line basis.
- **Hiring Solutions**: job posts, direct messaging, and the higher-touch Curative Talent staffing business.
- **Workflow Solutions**: telehealth (Dialer), secure messaging, digital fax, on-call scheduling (AMiON), and the Clinical AI Suite: *Ask* (with PeerCheck verification), *Scribe* (ambient documentation), and *Dialer*. Management cites 140+ health systems that have approved the Clinical AI Suite.

The moat rests on genuine network effects, verified-physician trust, deep workflow integration, and a proprietary physician dataset. The economic engine is digital pharma advertising delivered through a clinical-utility network: high physician engagement, subsidized by free tools, monetized via cyclical pharma marketing budgets. Fiscal year ends March 31. Class B shares (10 votes each), held by CEO Jeff Tangney and affiliates, control roughly 79% of voting power, leaving public holders with effectively no governance influence near-term.

Balance sheet quality is unambiguous: Altman Z-Score 6.53, current ratio 6.1x, debt/equity 0.18, ~$748.6M of cash and marketable securities, negligible debt, ROE 20.6%, gross margin ~89%. Net cash is roughly $5.67 per share, about 28% of the $2.64B market capitalization. There is no covenant, maturity, or liquidity risk to model.

Two tensions a headline read would miss:

1. **Net income fell 12% in FY26 despite +13% revenue.** The driver was stock-based compensation rising to $122M (+68% YoY from $72M, with R&D SBC up ~148%) plus a higher tax provision. GAAP operating margin compressed ~600bps (40%→34%). The margin compression is a deliberate "AI investment year" choice (escalating R&D, compute/inference costs, and brand spend), and management's framing implies it persists into FY27. Free cash flow held up because SBC is non-cash, but diluted share count near ~199M and the buyback timing (below) mean per-share economics are weaker than the cash figure suggests.

2. **Net revenue retention dropped from 119% to 109%.** Management states NRR is directly tied to the revenue growth rate, making this the clearest single signal that customer expansion, not just new logos, is slowing. The most recent quarter's revenue and the sub-consensus FY27 guide are consistent with that.

Capital allocation is buyback-heavy: a completed $500M repurchase (11.6M shares) plus a second $500M authorization (Feb 2026), which is why retained earnings swung from +$187M to –$51M and equity declined despite profitability. FY26 repurchase spend was approximately $432M. There is no dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has structurally downshifted, and this is the core of the rating change.

- **Top-line trajectory:** 20% (FY25) → 13% (FY26) → +5.1% in the March quarter, with next-quarter guidance near +4-5% (~$151.5M vs the $145.4M just reported). The U.S. physician market is approaching saturation (>85% of physicians already on platform), so future growth depends on monetization per physician rather than reach.
- **Estimate revisions:** consensus EPS was cut ~12% across horizons — current quarter $0.35→$0.30 (-12.4%), current year $1.62→$1.43 (-11.6%), next year $1.79→$1.59 (-11.1%). The 30-day and 90-day revision magnitudes are identical, which means essentially all of the cuts occurred at or around the May 13 print and there has been no incremental deterioration in the month since. This is a completed reset, not an ongoing slide. It removes the "estimates still falling" amplifier from the bear case, though it does not by itself argue for reacceleration.
- **Swing factor:** the bull case requires converting free AI engagement (Ask, Scribe) into paid enterprise/marketing revenue faster than competitors capture physician AI attention. Management has announced integrations with Aledade and Photon, took PeerCheck live on Ask, and acquired Pathway Medical ($26.5M cash plus contingent consideration, July 2025) to bolster clinical AI. None of this has yet produced visible monetization that offsets the marketing-side deceleration. The earnings track record is mixed (two beats, two misses over four quarters, including a -10% miss in the March quarter), which lowers confidence in near-term upside surprises.

Net assessment: residual double-digit FY revenue growth with strong cash conversion, but a clear and confirmed deceleration with the next leg of growth dependent on unproven AI monetization.

## Valuation Assessment

After the drawdown, valuation is supportive rather than stretched, the principal reason this is not an AVOID.

- **Forward earnings:** ~14.2x current-year consensus ($1.43) and ~12.7x next-year ($1.59). For an 89%-gross-margin network business throwing off $317M of FCF, this is undemanding even against a decelerating growth rate.
- **Cash-flow basis:** enterprise value of roughly $1.89B (market cap less net cash) against $317M FCF is ~6x EV/FCF. That multiple bakes in continued deceleration.
- **Net-cash support:** ~28% of market cap is cash with no debt. A reasonable downside scenario marks the equity toward the mid-teens (a net-cash-plus-modest-multiple floor), roughly 25% below spot.
- **Peer context:** Health Care Technology peers are heterogeneous and largely lower-quality on margins — peer median P/E ~37.7 (OMCL 97.7x, WAY 29.7x, HSTM 37.7x, CERT negative, SOLV 9.9x). Doximity's profitability and cash generation are well above this set, yet it trades at a discount to the median on forward earnings. Some discount for decelerating growth is warranted; the magnitude looks excessive.

The asymmetry is the key point: roughly -25% to a cash-supported floor against a reacceleration/squeeze path toward the low-$30s (the analyst high target sits at $42, mean $25.15, median $24, all above spot). That risk/reward does not support a short, but it also does not support a high-conviction long while growth is still falling and AI monetization is unproven. Valuation earns a constructive but not aggressive score.

## Competitive Landscape

Doximity's network position among physicians is dominant and defensible: verified-physician trust, deep workflow integration, and a proprietary dataset that is hard to replicate. The competitive center of gravity, however, is shifting to clinical AI, where the threat is genuine:

- **OpenEvidence** is named in the 10-K as a Marketing competitor and is the most direct threat to *Ask*. NBC News and Fierce Healthcare coverage characterizes it as reaching roughly two-thirds of U.S. physicians and branding itself the most widely used medical AI platform. This figure is material to the bear case, but it is self-reported and relayed through press coverage rather than independently audited; it should be treated as a directional risk indicator, not an established fact. The two companies are also in active, two-sided litigation: OpenEvidence sued Doximity, its CTO, and its Director of AI Products (June 2025, D. Mass.) over alleged unauthorized platform access; Doximity counterclaimed for false advertising and defamation; in January 2026 the court let most claims on both sides proceed. The dispute is unresolved and amplifies the competitive overhang.
- **Abridge** (ambient AI scribe) and **OpenAI/Anthropic** (foundation models) threaten *Scribe* and *Ask*.
- Legacy competitors persist across each line: WebMD/Medscape (marketing), Teladoc/Amwell (telehealth), QGenda (scheduling), LinkedIn (networking).

The trajectory question dominates the level question here. Doximity's reach is enormous, but NRR decline plus well-funded pure-play AI entrants competing for the same physician attention and the same pharma dollars create a credible erosion risk. Because the most alarming competitive datapoint is unverified, the appropriate stance is to treat the threat as real and unsettled rather than decisive.

## Risk Assessment

- **Clinical-AI disruption (primary strategic risk):** specialist AI entrants could capture physician mindshare and, over time, pharma marketing budgets. Partly priced; magnitude uncertain because the key competitor metric is self-reported.
- **Pharma policy/budget headwind:** the 10-K specifically flags "Most Favored Nation" drug-pricing negotiations as causing "short-term budget caution" that affected Marketing Solutions bookings timing in FY26. This is a named, direct overhang on the core revenue engine and is cyclical/policy-driven rather than under company control.
- **Customer concentration:** for the first time, one customer exceeded 10% of revenue in FY26; 125 customers above $500K account for 83% of revenue; pharma contracts are largely short-term and agency-intermediated.
- **Metric-disclosure history:** the just-settled securities class action ($31M, fully insurance-covered, final hearing June 10, 2026) concerned the reliability of user-count/engagement metrics, the same figures the thesis relies on. Six derivative suits remain outstanding with loss not estimable. The 10-K's expanded caveats on unverified internal metrics should be read in that context.
- **Margin air pocket:** the "AI investment year" implies continued margin compression into FY27.
- **Governance:** dual-class structure concentrates ~79% voting power with insiders through at least 2031.

Offsetting these: net cash of ~28% of market cap and zero leverage contain the downside; the largest litigation item is settled and insured; and the estimate reset appears complete. The risks are real and multiple, but the magnitude of equity downside is structurally limited by the balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility is 66.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 56.4% (IV rich by ~10 points), but IV percentile within the current chain is only 30%, and put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.5% near-term) with a put/call OI ratio of 0.99. The term structure is in contango (66.3% near vs 69.5% long-dated), a normal, non-panicked configuration. ATM IV collapsed from 223.1% to 66.3% over the past month as the May 13 event resolved, with the P/C OI ratio rising from 0.59 to 0.99. The options market is no longer pricing acute event risk; it has digested the print and now reflects ordinary elevated uncertainty rather than directional fear. The longer-dated put skew steepening to -5.4% at six months indicates lingering downside hedging into the AI-monetization question, but nothing that signals an imminent informed bearish catalyst. This is consistent with a HOLD: the binary event has passed, and the market is in wait-and-see mode.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The franchise generates $317M of FCF at 89% gross margins on a dominant physician network. The estimate reset is complete (30-day cuts equal 90-day cuts), removing the falling-knife dynamic. If AI monetization (Ask, Scribe, enterprise Clinical AI Suite across 140+ systems) begins converting engagement to revenue, growth can stabilize above the depressed +5% quarter, the multiple re-rates toward peer levels, and the 12.7% short interest (3 days to cover) provides squeeze fuel on any positive surprise. Bull target ~$32-34 (≈18-20x next-year EPS on stabilized double-digit growth plus net cash).

**Bear case.** The U.S. physician market is saturated, NRR has fallen to 109% and may keep sliding, MFN pharma-pricing pressure persists on the core marketing engine, and clinical-AI entrants (OpenEvidence, Abridge, foundation-model players) erode physician attention and eventually pharma share. The "AI investment year" compresses margins for multiple quarters with no guaranteed payoff, and management continues buying back stock and granting heavy SBC while insiders sell into the decline. In this scenario growth stalls near mid-single digits, the multiple stays depressed, and the stock drifts toward its net-cash-supported floor in the mid-teens (~-25%).

The realistic path sits between these: a cheap, cash-rich, decelerating business whose next leg depends on an unproven AI monetization ramp against a real but unquantified competitive threat. That is the definition of an uncertain hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** No dividend, so this is not an income hold; growth is decelerating, so it is not a confident growth hold. The call reflects a genuine inability to determine whether the cash-supported value floor or the competitive/deceleration erosion dominates over the next 12 months, with most of the bear case already in the price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue growth re-accelerating above ~10% YoY, or NRR stabilizing/recovering above 112%, evidencing that AI engagement is converting to revenue.
- Positive consensus EPS revisions (reversing the -12% reset) alongside evidence that AI investment-year margin compression is troughing.
- A pullback toward the net-cash floor (high-teens / ~$15-17) that widens the margin of safety while fundamentals hold, improving the risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth decelerating below ~3% YoY or NRR falling below ~105%, indicating saturation plus active churn rather than slowing expansion.
- Independently verified evidence (not company self-reporting) that a clinical-AI competitor is displacing physician engagement or capturing pharma marketing dollars at Doximity's expense.
- Gross margin compression below the low-80s or a further leg of estimate cuts, signaling the margin air pocket is structural rather than investment-driven.

**Reassessment timeline:** next quarterly print (August 2026, fiscal Q1 FY27), which will be the first clean read on whether the post-reset growth rate stabilizes and whether AI monetization shows up in the numbers. The June 10, 2026 securities-settlement approval hearing is a minor cleanup item, not a thesis driver.

## Conclusion

Doximity is a high-quality, cash-generative physician network experiencing a confirmed structural deceleration across revenue growth, NRR, GAAP net income, and FY27 guidance. Those are real fundamental developments, and they justified moving off the prior constructive view. They do not, however, justify an AVOID. The stock trades at ~12.7x next-year EPS and ~6x free cash flow net of cash, with ~28% of market capitalization in net cash, no leverage, and a downside floor roughly 25% below spot, against a reacceleration/squeeze path and a full sell-side panel carrying zero sell ratings with targets above spot. The estimate cuts are concentrated at the May print rather than ongoing, and the most dramatic competitive datapoint (a rival's two-thirds physician reach) is unaudited self-reporting that cannot anchor a structural-impairment thesis.

The result is an uncertain HOLD: own it only if you already do, monitor the August print for evidence that AI monetization is offsetting marketing-side deceleration, and require either a clean reacceleration or a deeper pullback toward net-cash support to upgrade. Overall score 5.75, reflecting elite financial health, genuinely impaired growth, supportive valuation, an eroding-but-still-dominant competitive position, weak recent management signals, and contained idiosyncratic risk.
