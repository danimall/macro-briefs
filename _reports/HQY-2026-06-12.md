**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HealthEquity is the largest U.S. Health Savings Account custodian by number of accounts and #2 by assets, administering 10.6 million HSAs holding $37.1 billion in HSA assets plus 7.2 million complementary consumer-directed benefits, for 17.8 million total accounts as of April 30, 2026. The business is fundamentally a rate-spread fintech rather than a managed-care insurer: roughly 48% of FY26 revenue came from the custodial spread HQY earns on ~$17.5 billion of HSA cash placed with insurance and depository partners. That spread has been the dominant earnings driver, with the HSA-cash yield climbing from 3.11% to 3.53% in FY26 and reaching 3.84% in Q1 FY27. The result is a clean, capital-light compounder: FY26 revenue of $1,313M (+9.5%), net income of $215M (+123%), Adjusted EBITDA margin of 43% (rising to 46% in Q1 FY27), and ~$455M of free cash flow at a 35% margin.

The fundamentals are intact and improving. The company has beaten consensus for four straight quarters, raised FY27 revenue guidance to $1.41-1.42B, and authorized an incremental $1.0B buyback in May 2026 on top of prior repurchases (~$422M deployed across FY26 and Q1 FY27, at an average below ~$83). At $88.18, the stock trades at roughly 18.7x FY27 non-GAAP consensus EPS of $4.72 and 16.1x FY28 of $5.48, reasonable for a 35% FCF-margin business growing EPS in the mid-teens. The headline 33x trailing GAAP P/E overstates the multiple because it ignores ~$1.07B of acquisition-related intangible amortization and a heavy stock-comp load. The stock is down ~15% from a year ago and ~18% from its 52-week high, which on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward rather than signaling deterioration.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. The earnings engine is rate-levered, and the first concrete signs of rate-cut pressure are appearing: the AOCI unrealized hedge loss grew from $5.7M to $31.8M in a single quarter as rates fell. The recent margin expansion is also partly the absence of one-time fraud and breach costs, not purely structural. Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+4.0% over 90 days, far below an inflection-grade move), there is no discrete re-rating catalyst within the next 60 days, and the bear-case path back toward the $73-75 trough is roughly -15% to -18% from current levels. This is a quality compounder worth holding with constructive 12-month upside, but not one that clears the bar for a high-conviction long.

## Company Overview

HealthEquity (fiscal year ending January 31) operates an integrated platform for tax-advantaged consumer health accounts. Its market share by HSA assets grew from 4% in 2010 to 20% by December 2025. It is an IRS-approved non-bank custodian, a structural credential that limits the competitive set to a small group of qualified providers.

The revenue model has three streams (FY26 figures):

- **Custodial revenue — $636.8M (48%):** the economic core. HQY places ~$17.5B of HSA cash with insurance-company partners ("Enhanced Rates," group annuity contracts that are the default, higher-yielding option) and federally insured bank/credit-union "Depository Partners" ("Basic Rates"). HQY retains most of the yield; members keep roughly 0.24-0.25%. The contracts run 3-5 years and reprice on a laddered schedule, which is why the realized yield moves with a lag to Fed policy.
- **Service revenue — $485.0M (37%):** per-account administrative fees (largely fixed/tiered) plus asset-based fees on $18.5B of invested HSA assets and marketplace fees.
- **Interchange revenue — $191.6M (15%):** card-swipe fees on member spending.

Distribution is B2B2C, reaching employers through 200+ network partners (health plans, benefits administrators, retirement recordkeepers), brokers, and direct sales. The competitive advantages are scale leadership, a bundled HSA+CDB single-source offering most employers prefer, a proprietary multi-tenant cloud platform, high switching costs on individually owned trust accounts, and a roll-up acquisition strategy targeting competitors who treat HSAs as non-core (WageWorks, Further, BenefitWallet).

The FY26 net-income swing reflects three things: custodial revenue up 17% on the rising HSA-cash yield, operating leverage as invested-asset and custodial revenue scale without proportional cost, and the non-recurrence of a $30M FY25 lease-settlement charge. Q1 FY27 extended the trend — revenue +7% to $354.6M, net income +29% to $69.4M, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.24 versus $1.11 consensus.

Cash flow quality is excellent. FY26 operating cash flow was $457M against software-development and IT capex of roughly $48.5M, producing the ~$455M of free cash flow. The reported XBRL "capex" of $2M understates true capital intensity because software development is classified separately; on either measure this is a capital-light, scalable platform.

The balance sheet carries $947M of debt — a fixed 4.50% $600M senior note due 2029 plus ~$347M on a variable-rate revolver at 5.00% — against $2.0B of equity. Net leverage is modest at ~1.7x EBITDA versus a 5.0x covenant; the current ratio is 3.3x and interest coverage is robust. The Altman Z of 2.7 ("grey zone") is depressed by an intangible-heavy balance sheet rather than distress: goodwill ($1.65B) plus intangibles ($1.07B) total ~81% of assets, a legacy of the acquisition roll-up. This is the principal balance-sheet caution: not solvency, but the impairment risk that sits behind that asset base.

Capital allocation has shifted decisively to buybacks. The company repurchased $299M in FY26 and $123M in Q1 FY27, prepaid $115M of debt across FY26/Q1, and in May 2026 added $1.0B to the repurchase authorization. With repurchases executed below ~$83 on average, this is disciplined deployment at prices below current levels and a credible signal of management's view of intrinsic value.

## Growth Analysis

The structural backdrop is favorable. KFF data shows family health-insurance premiums up 53% since 2015, pushing consumers into high-deductible plans and the HSAs that accompany them. HQY is the scale beneficiary, and the July 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" expanded HSA eligibility (Bronze/Catastrophic plans, broader qualified services), enlarging the addressable market that management is now actively marketing into.

Three quantitative growth drivers are working concurrently:

1. **Custodial yield repricing.** The HSA-cash maturity schedule shows $3.2B repricing through the rest of FY27 at only ~1.8%, well below the current ~3.6% portfolio yield. Legacy low-rate contracts are still rolling up, which supports continued yield expansion even through modest Fed cuts. This is the durable component of the earnings story.
2. **Invested-asset growth.** HSA investments rose 38% YoY to $19.6B (market appreciation plus cash-to-investment transfers), with HSAs-with-investments up 18%. This grows high-margin asset-based service fees independent of rate spread.
3. **Operating leverage and cost discipline.** Q1 service costs fell 11% YoY, aided by AI tooling (Expedited Claims, HSAnswers) and platform modernization, plus the abatement of the prior-year fraud spike.

Consensus reflects this: FY28 EPS of $5.48 is +16% over FY27. Estimate revisions are positive across all horizons — current year +4.0% over 90 days, next year +4.0% — but moderate. This is solid earnings momentum, not the +30%-over-90-days move that would force a stronger directional call. Revenue growth is high-single-digit, not the +40% acceleration that justifies treating trailing multiples as trough-distorted. The story is durable mid-teens EPS compounding, supplemented by buyback accretion.

The honest caveat: a meaningful slice of the recent margin and net-income gains is the absence of one-time items (the $30M lease-settlement charge in FY25 and the fraud/breach-driven cost spike), not purely structural improvement. The structural component — yield repricing, invested-asset fees, automation — is real and the larger share, but the headline growth rates overstate the recurring run-rate.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported 33.4x trailing GAAP P/E is misleading because it is computed on $2.46 of GAAP EPS, which absorbs the acquisition-related intangible amortization and heavy stock-based compensation. On the basis analysts and management actually use, the stock trades at:

- ~18.7x FY27 non-GAAP consensus EPS of $4.72
- ~16.1x FY28 of $5.48
- PEG of ~1.2 on non-GAAP forward earnings and ~16% growth — below the 1.5 threshold

For a 35% FCF-margin platform with 46% incremental EBITDA margins, durable structural demand, and a still-positive yield tailwind, that multiple is reasonable to modestly attractive. The free-cash-flow yield is roughly 6% on a $7.4B market cap, with that cash being returned through the buyback program.

The peer table (MOH 53x, CNC negative, HUM 37.4x, ELV 17.7x, UNH 30.2x; median 37.4x) is not a clean comp because those are managed-care insurers carrying medical-loss-ratio and policy risk that HQY does not. The more relevant frame is a specialty-financial/payments compounder, against which ~18.7x forward for mid-teens growth and high FCF conversion screens fairly, not cheap.

Analyst price targets imply meaningful upside: mean $114.6 (+30%), median $110.5 (+25%), high $135 (+53%), low $88 (flat). The targets are constructive, but because the stock is a quality compounder rather than a cheap inflection, I treat them as directional support, not a floor that guarantees re-rating.

The valuation case is genuine but not asymmetric enough for a high-conviction long. The market applies a discount for rate-cut risk to the custodial spread plus the GAAP optics, which is rational, and the bear path is not trivial. I score valuation 6.8: attractive on forward fundamentals, capped by the rate-dependence of those fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

HQY's competitive position is stable-to-improving with one real threat. As the scale leader (20% of HSA assets, up from 4% in 2010), it benefits from network effects, a bundled HSA+CDB single-source value proposition most employers prefer, high switching costs on individually owned trust accounts, and its non-bank custodian status. It also functions as a consolidator: many banks and insurers treat HSAs as non-core and face depository capital requirements that push them to divest, feeding HQY's acquisition pipeline (BenefitWallet's ~616k accounts/$2.7B for $425M in FY25). No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

The principal competitive tension is Fidelity, which is gaining share through brand strength and superior investment/recordkeeping infrastructure, alongside Optum Bank (UnitedHealth) and Webster Bank with banking scale. There is also channel risk that network partners in-source HSAs/CDBs directly. HQY's defense — the bundled single-source offering and embedded ecosystem integrations — is durable but not impregnable, particularly as members increasingly value investment platform quality where Fidelity is strong. The trajectory remains positive on share, with a watchable erosion vector. Score: 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

This is the dimension that constrains the recommendation.

1. **Rate-cut spread compression (central risk).** With ~48% of revenue from the custodial spread, a sustained rate-cut cycle eventually reprices depository contracts down and compresses the dominant earnings driver. The near-term schedule is protective ($3.2B repricing at ~1.8% versus a 3.6% portfolio yield), so the tailwind persists through modest cuts. The AOCI unrealized hedge loss grew from $5.7M to $31.8M in a single quarter as rates fell, a quantified, observable signal that the rate environment is already pressuring the $3.5B-notional Treasury-forward hedge book (weighted rate 3.91%). This is not priced as benignly as the company's recent results suggest.

2. **Tax/regulatory dependence.** Substantially all revenue derives from accounts created by the Internal Revenue Code. Curtailment of HSA tax benefits or structural health reform would be materially adverse. The offsetting positive is the July 2025 OBBBA expansion of HSA eligibility, a genuine near-term TAM tailwind.

3. **Cybersecurity and fraud (materialized, ongoing).** A 2024 data breach (via a business partner's user account) has produced a consolidated putative class action in the District of Utah plus regulatory inquiries; HQY refiled a motion to compel arbitration in May 2026 and cannot estimate the loss. A FY25/Q1 FY26 spike in account-takeover fraud inflated service costs and is now receding. The abatement of these costs is part of recent margin gains, but the litigation and regulatory exposure remain open with unestimable loss.

4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** At 81% of assets, any deterioration in the acquired businesses could trigger large non-cash charges. None to date.

5. **Member-yield-split lever.** Members retain only ~0.24-0.25% of the ~3.6-3.8% yield. Competitive pressure could force HQY to pass more yield to members, compressing the spread independent of headline rates.

Several of these are partially priced (sector tax-dependence, breach existence). The under-priced elements are the rate-cut downside (quantified by the rising AOCI loss) and the breach litigation's open-ended outcome. Score: 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is constructive and not flagging stress. Near-term ATM IV of 37.7% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 35.4% (a 2.3% spread, "fair"), at the 23rd percentile of the current chain. The put/call OI ratio is 0.37 (call-heavy), and at the front the 1-week skew is +21.0% while the 1-month-and-out skew turns negative (-1.0% to -4.2%, call-favored). The term structure is in contango (37.7% near versus 41.2% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±4.2%. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.08 to 0.37 since late May, but absolute positioning remains skewed toward calls. There is no bearish options positioning that would contradict a constructive fundamental view; if anything, options sentiment is mildly supportive. This argues against the rate-cut bear case being imminent in the market's pricing, consistent with holding rather than avoiding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($110-135).** Rates stay higher-for-longer or cut only modestly, so the laddered repricing of low-1.8% legacy contracts continues lifting the custodial yield above 3.84% through FY27-28. Invested-asset growth (+38% YoY) compounds high-margin service fees, OBBBA expands the eligible HSA base, and automation holds the EBITDA margin near 46%. The $1B+ buyback, executed below current prices, drives mid-teens EPS growth toward $5.48+ in FY28. On 22-24x non-GAAP forward, the stock reaches $110-130, matching the analyst mean-to-high range. The 4-for-4 beat streak and beat-and-raise cadence support consensus reliability.

**Bear case ($72-78).** The Fed cuts more aggressively than priced, the custodial yield rolls over as contracts reprice down, and the AOCI hedge loss (already $31.8M and growing) signals the turn. Custodial revenue, ~48% of the total, decelerates and the operating-leverage story reverses. The breach class action resolves adversely with quantified liability, and the market de-rates the rate-dependent earnings to ~15-16x on a lower EPS base. The stock retraces to its $72.76 trough, roughly -15% to -18% from current.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-teens EPS growth supported by the still-positive yield tailwind and buyback accretion, but with the rate-cut overhang capping the multiple. Fair value of ~$100, ~13% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a genuine positive 12-month thesis — buyback-supported mid-teens EPS growth, a still-positive custodial-yield tailwind, and analyst targets 25-30% above the current price — but the rate-cut sensitivity, modest (non-inflection) estimate revisions, absence of a 60-day catalyst, and a bear case that breaches the 15% downside guardrail keep it below the bar for a high-conviction long.

**Rebuttal to a BUY upgrade (concrete and falsifiable):**
- **Quantified rate risk.** The AOCI hedge loss expanded from $5.7M to $31.8M in one quarter as rates fell, an observable signal that the rate-cut cycle is already pressuring the hedge book on a business where ~48% of revenue is the custodial spread. This is testable: if the FY27 custodial yield holds above 3.84% and the AOCI hedge loss stabilizes or reverses over the next two quarters, the risk recedes and the name upgrades.
- **Not inflection-grade.** Estimate revisions are +4.0% over 90 days, well below the +30% that signals a true earnings inflection, and revenue growth is high-single-digit rather than the +40% that would justify trough-multiple treatment. Roughly half the FY26 net-income doubling was non-recurring (the $30M lease-settlement charge plus the absence of one-time fraud/breach costs).
- **Bear-case downside exceeds 15%.** The $72-78 bear path is -15% to -18% from $88, above the contained-downside threshold for a high-conviction long.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY27 custodial yield holds above 3.84% through Q2 and Q3 FY27 *and* the AOCI hedge loss stabilizes or narrows — confirming the rate tailwind is durable.
2. Estimate revisions accelerate to >+10% over a subsequent 90-day window, or the stock pulls back toward $78-80 on unchanged fundamentals, improving the asymmetry to a contained (<15%) bear case.
3. Favorable resolution or quantified, immaterial settlement of the 2024 breach class action.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Custodial yield declines QoQ for two consecutive quarters with the AOCI hedge loss expanding materially beyond $50M.
2. A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or breach litigation crystallizing a material, quantified liability.
3. Net new HSA additions or invested-asset growth decelerating below mid-single digits, signaling competitive share loss to Fidelity/Optum.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY27 earnings (expected late August 2026) is the next natural checkpoint, with the custodial-yield disclosure and AOCI hedge mark as the key data points.

## Conclusion

HealthEquity is a high-quality, capital-light compounder with the leading position in a structurally growing market, excellent free-cash-flow conversion, and disciplined capital return through a buyback executed below current prices. At ~18.7x forward non-GAAP EPS with mid-teens growth, the valuation is reasonable and the analyst targets imply 25-30% upside. The 15% pullback over the past year, on improving fundamentals, has improved the risk/reward.

The earnings engine's heavy dependence on the interest-rate-driven custodial spread keeps this from being a high-conviction long. The first quantified evidence of rate-cut pressure is visible in the AOCI hedge loss tripling-plus to $31.8M, the estimate-revision momentum is positive but not inflection-grade, there is no discrete catalyst inside 60 days, and the bear-case path to the $72-78 trough modestly exceeds the downside tolerance for a buy. The appropriate posture is to hold for the constructive 12-month growth-and-buyback thesis while watching the custodial yield and hedge mark at the next print. **HOLD, overall score 6.8.**
