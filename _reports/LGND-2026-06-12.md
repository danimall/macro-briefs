**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ligand Pharmaceuticals is a biopharmaceutical royalty aggregator that funds mid-to-late-stage drug development and purchases royalty rights in exchange for passive, high-margin economics. The underlying business is performing well: Q1 2026 royalty revenue grew 56% to $43.0M, operating income swung from -$36.2M a year ago to +$17.4M, and operating cash flow was +$48.7M against -$25.4M in Q1 2025. The company carries roughly $779M in cash and short-term investments against $460M of 0.75% convertible notes due 2030, structured with a capped call that lifts the effective conversion price to ~$294. This is a financially healthy, cash-generative platform with genuine therapeutic diversification across 12+ commercial royalties and 90+ development programs.

The investment question is whether the current price offers enough asymmetry to warrant a BUY. The stock has risen roughly 121% over twelve months, from about $115 to $255, and now trades below the consensus mean target of $277.6, with the analyst low target ($247) sitting only about 3% below current. The most recent quarter broke a three-quarter beat streak with a 12% EPS miss, and 30-day estimate revisions have turned negative across every horizon (Current Quarter -2.8%, Next Quarter -5.3%, Current Year -1.6%, Next Year -4.2%). The 90-day revisions remain positive, so the medium-term trajectory is intact, but recent momentum is decelerating rather than accelerating.

We rate LGND a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The royalty engine and catalyst slate (Filspari FSGS ramp, XOMA close in Q3, Palvella NDA in 2H 2026) support continued ownership for investors already in the name. Near-term options structure is pricing imminent stress (term-structure backwardation, IV rich to realized vol by 9.4pp, elevated put skew), and the upside-to-downside profile at $255 is roughly balanced. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$200s or on confirmation that the XOMA integration and Filspari ramp are driving estimates back to upward revision.

## Company Overview

Ligand's model is infrastructure-light. Rather than developing and commercializing drugs, it provides development capital and acquires royalty streams, letting larger partners handle clinical trials, regulatory work, and commercialization. Revenue comes from three sources: royalties on partnered commercial products, sales and licensing of two proprietary formulation platforms (Captisol, a solubility-enhancing cyclodextrin used by Amgen, Merck, Pfizer, Jazz, Gilead, and Baxter; and NITRICIL, a drug-release platform), and milestone/contract payments.

The Q1 2026 royalty mix illustrates both the diversification and the concentration. Filspari (sparsentan, partnered with Travere) contributed $11.3M at roughly a 9% effective rate and is the largest and fastest-growing stream (+113% YoY). Kyprolis (Amgen) added $6.7M, Qarziba $6.3M, Rylaze (Jazz) $3.2M, and Ohtuvayre (Verona) $3.0M, with additional contributions from Merck's pneumococcal vaccines and teriparatide. The portfolio spans oncology, rare disease, nephrology, respiratory, and vaccines.

CEO Todd Davis, who carries a royalty-finance pedigree, now leads the company following the transition from longtime CEO John Higgins. The strategy emphasizes smaller development-funding checks ($20–75M) on mid/late-stage and rare-disease assets, a niche where larger competitor Royalty Pharma's scale is less of an advantage.

## Financial Analysis

On a full-year basis the numbers are strong: FY2025 revenue of roughly $268M was up about 60% YoY, with net income of $124.5M and a 46.4% net margin. The quarterly picture requires careful reading because GAAP earnings are dominated by non-cash marks.

Q1 2026 total revenue was $51.7M (+14% YoY), with royalties offsetting a 36% decline in Captisol sales (order timing) and a 97% collapse in contract revenue (lumpy milestones). The company reported a GAAP net loss of $(13.3)M despite the positive operating income. The loss was driven by a $49.2M non-cash fair-value markdown on its Pelthos holdings, where the common and Series A preferred stakes fell from $152.8M to $103.5M in one quarter. The prior-year Q1 loss stemmed from a different non-cash item, a $44.3M upfront R&D-funding charge under ASC 730 tied to the Castle Creek deal.

The signal that matters is cash. Adjusted core EPS was $1.63, up 23% YoY. The GAAP headline loss does not reflect business deterioration; it reflects mark-to-market swings on a growing book of equity stakes (Pelthos, Viking, Palvella) and lumpy expensing of development funding.

The balance sheet is robust. Cash plus short-term investments, an undrawn $124.4M revolver, and the convertible notes at a 0.75% coupon give substantial deployment capacity. Debt/equity is ~0.53 and the current ratio is 22.2x. The Altman Z-Score of 2.91 (grey zone) and Piotroski 4/5 understate quality because the model's asset-heavy royalty/investment structure depresses asset-turnover and EBIT/asset ratios that traditional scores penalize.

One genuine earnings headwind worth modeling: Section 162(m) limits on executive compensation deductibility, amplified by stock-driven "comp actually paid," pushed the effective tax rate to 45% in Q1 2026. This is a real drag on net income that does not show up in operating metrics.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the royalty book compounding through new asset deployment plus organic ramp of existing royalties. Management targets ~$150–250M/year in new royalty deployment, and recent activity supports the cadence: Castle Creek ($50M), Orchestra BioMed (up to $40M), Arecor ($7M), and the pending $739M XOMA acquisition.

Three near-term catalysts anchor the forward case:

- **Filspari FSGS approval (April 13, 2026).** Travere won FDA approval for FSGS, the first and only approved therapy, opening a ~30,000-patient second indication on top of the IgAN franchise that grew 88% YoY. This is the single largest organic growth driver.
- **XOMA acquisition (announced April 27, expected Q3 close).** At $39.00/share cash (~$739M equity value, ~14% premium), this adds 120+ assets including royalties on Roche's Vabysmo, Servier's Ojemda (tovorafenib), Zevra's Miplyffa, and 14 late-stage programs. It materially broadens the partner base into ophthalmology and CNS and reduces reliance on Filspari.
- **Palvella Qtorin rapamycin.** Positive Phase 3 SELVA data (Feb 2026) with an NDA planned for 2H 2026, adding another royalty stream.

The estimate revision data complicates the timing. The 90-day trend is positive and meaningful: Next Year +17.6% to $11.32, Current Year +7.7% to $9.10. But the 30-day trend is negative across every horizon, and the Q1 EPS miss (-12.0%, $1.63 vs $1.85 estimate) broke a streak that included a +57.3% surprise in Q3 2025 and +26.8% in Q4 2025. This is decelerating revision momentum following a miss, not the accelerating trajectory that would carry a high-conviction growth call. The medium-term thesis is intact; the near-term earnings visibility has softened.

## Valuation Assessment

At $255, LGND trades at roughly 28x the $9.10 current-year EPS estimate and about 22.5x the $11.32 next-year estimate. For a business growing royalties at this rate with a high-margin, cash-generative model, neither multiple is egregious. The problem is the asymmetry at the current price.

The consensus mean target is $277.6 (median $278.5), implying roughly 9% upside to the midpoint. The high target is $330 (+29%) and the low is $247, only about 3% below current. The stock has narrowed much of the gap to consensus after its twelve-month run. On stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, consensus targets often lag reality and should be treated as floors. That principle applies most cleanly when estimates are accelerating; here the 30-day revisions are negative, which weakens the case that analysts will be chasing the price higher in the next few weeks.

Peer comps are not directly instructive because the listed biotech peers (TGTX at 14x, CPRX at 18x, RGEN at 134x, with PTGX and RCUS unprofitable) are heterogeneous drug developers, not royalty aggregators. The more relevant comparison is Royalty Pharma, against which Ligand commands a premium for higher growth and smaller-deal agility. That premium is defensible but already reflected in the price.

The valuation score of 5.2 reflects a fairly-valued situation: a quality business at a not-unreasonable forward multiple, but with the analyst floor only ~3% below current and the recent revision trend providing no near-term catalyst for multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

Ligand competes in biopharma royalty financing against Royalty Pharma, HealthCare Royalty, and DRI Healthcare. Its differentiation is the smaller-check focus on mid/late-stage and rare-disease assets, where the largest players are less competitive. The XOMA acquisition is itself a consolidation move, absorbing a direct competitor and broadening the partner roster to include Roche, Servier, Takeda, and Zevra.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the platform level: the model offers diversified biotech upside with profitability and no single-molecule binary risk, and the XOMA deal diversifies therapeutic exposure. The counterweight is that current growth is concentrated in Filspari, so the diversification is more advanced on paper than in the actual growth contribution. Until the XOMA assets and newer royalties contribute meaningfully, the moat is real but the growth driver is narrow.

## Risk Assessment

**Filspari concentration.** Filspari contributes the largest royalty and the bulk of growth. Nephrology competition and reimbursement dynamics at a single partner represent a concentrated exposure that the XOMA close will dilute but not eliminate near-term.

**GAAP earnings volatility.** The Pelthos stake (Ligand owns ~45% common / ~62% preferred, capped at 49.9%) is an outsized, illiquid, marked-to-market position in a sub-scale public company (PTHS). It swung Q1 to a loss and will continue distorting reported results. The broader portfolio (Viking, Palvella, Agenus, Arecor, Orchestra, Castle Creek) makes Ligand increasingly an investment company underwriting management's capital-allocation skill across partly illiquid, Level 3 marks.

**Viking TR-Beta dispute.** Ligand is attempting to terminate Viking's license to the VK2809/VK0214 (MASH) program, alleging insufficient commercially reasonable efforts, with up to $375M in milestones/royalties at stake. Viking is publicly disputing the termination. This is a contested, potentially protracted, binary situation with adversarial outcomes.

**Captisol patent challenge.** An Endo subsidiary filed an ANDA challenging the Captisol-enabled Nexterone patent, with trial targeted for 2027. A favorable procedural development (defendants precluded from asserting invalidity) reduces but does not eliminate generic-entry risk to a legacy stream.

**Development-stage assets on non-accrual.** Many financial royalty assets (Bot/Bal, Tzield, AT292, Castle Creek D-Fi) are pre-commercial at 13–35% discount rates (weighted ~17%). Ligand already took a full impairment on Agenus partner programs in Q4 2025, evidence these bets can go to zero.

**XOMA integration.** The deal is funded via cash plus revolver and adds operating complexity plus a CVR tied to XOMA litigation.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 45.9% is rich to one-year realized volatility of 36.5% (a 9.4pp spread, 69th percentile within the current chain). The term structure is in backwardation (45.9% near-term vs 43.8% at six months), which typically signals an expected near-term event. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.9% on the front-week expiry, and the implied move into the June 18 expiry is ±4.9%. IV rose 2.5pp since late May even as the stock climbed 8.7%, indicating the market is hedging rather than growing complacent. For a BUY thesis this would be a meaningful divergence; for a HOLD it reinforces caution about chasing the stock at $255. The put/call OI ratio of 0.12 remains low, so the positioning is hedging-driven rather than an outright bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($330)

The royalty engine compounds as guided. Filspari's FSGS launch ramps the 30,000-patient market on top of IgAN growth, XOMA closes in Q3 and adds Vabysmo/Ojemda/Miplyffa royalties plus 14 late-stage programs, and the Palvella NDA advances. Next-year EPS revisions resume their upward trajectory toward and beyond $11.32, the Viking claw-back succeeds, and the market re-rates a diversified, profitable royalty platform toward 29x next-year earnings. This matches the $330 high analyst target.

### Bear Case (~$200, -22%)

Front-week options stress proves prescient: a near-term disappointment (further Pelthos markdown, soft Filspari script data, or XOMA-integration friction) extends the negative 30-day revision trend. The Viking dispute becomes protracted litigation with no recovery, the Captisol generic challenge progresses, and GAAP losses from equity marks weigh on sentiment. The multiple compresses toward 18–20x next-year EPS as the stock gives back part of its run, landing near $200.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings report and the XOMA close, both expected in Q3 2026. The June 18 options expiry and any near-term catalyst pricing flagged by the backwardated term structure are nearer markers.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- 30-day estimate revisions turn positive again across at least Current Year and Next Year horizons, confirming the Q1 miss was a one-off and the medium-term +17.6% next-year revision trend is reasserting.
- A pullback toward the low-$200s that restores meaningful asymmetry (bull/base upside materially exceeding bear downside) without any fundamental deterioration.
- XOMA closes on terms and integrates without margin or guidance disruption, materially diversifying away from Filspari concentration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Filspari quarterly royalty growth decelerates sharply (below ~20% YoY) signaling the core driver is fading.
- The Viking dispute escalates into protracted litigation with an adverse procedural ruling, or the Captisol Nexterone patent challenge turns unfavorable.
- A material further impairment or markdown on the equity-stake portfolio (Pelthos, Palvella, Agenus successors) combined with continued negative revisions.

## Conclusion

Ligand is a fundamentally healthy royalty aggregator with a cash engine growing strongly, fortress liquidity, a shareholder-friendly convertible structure, and a credible near-term catalyst slate. The trajectory of the underlying business justifies the improvement in scores over the past several months, and the GAAP losses are non-cash artifacts that a casual screen would misread.

What prevents a BUY at $255 is specific and falsifiable: the stock sits below the consensus mean target with the analyst low only ~3% below current; the most recent quarter missed by 12% and broke a beat streak; 30-day estimate revisions have turned negative across all four horizons; and the options market is pricing imminent stress through term-structure backwardation, rich IV, and elevated put skew. The risk/reward at the current price is roughly symmetric (bull +29% vs bear -22%), not the asymmetric setup a high-conviction long requires. We rate LGND a HOLD on a growth basis. For existing holders the thesis remains intact and worth riding through the XOMA close and Filspari ramp; new capital is better deployed on a pullback toward the low-$200s or on confirmation that estimate revisions have turned back up.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report twice stated the stock trades "essentially at" the consensus mean target ($277.6), but at ~$255 there is ~9% upside to the mean and ~9–13% to the high/median range — the price is below, not at, consensus mean, which modestly improves the asymmetry the HOLD rests on.
