**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northrop Grumman is one of the five U.S. defense primes, generating $41.95 billion in FY2025 revenue with 84% of sales to the U.S. government and a record backlog of roughly $96 billion (about 2.3x revenue). Its competitive core is a set of sole-source strategic-deterrent franchises that no competitor can execute: the B-21 Raider stealth bomber, the Sentinel ICBM, submarine propulsion for the Virginia and Columbia classes, and Trident solid-rocket motors. These programs anchor multi-decade revenue visibility and a classified/restricted portfolio (~35% company-wide) that is structurally difficult for new entrants to contest.

The stock has declined roughly 30% from its 52-week high of $774 (early March) to $521.57, sitting close to its 52-week low of $493.84. The proximate triggers were a Q1 FY2026 print that matched on revenue but trailed profit expectations, and a genuinely new company-specific risk surfaced in the FY2025 10-K: an executive order that could bar share repurchases and dividends for defense contractors judged to have underperformed. Because buybacks contribute roughly 2 percentage points to NOC's annual EPS growth, this overhang strikes directly at the per-share growth engine. Against this, the emerging bull argument is that the B-21 fixed-price loss reservoir — the source of ~$2.0 billion in cumulative charges — is flipping to profit as the program exits development into low-rate production. Secondary reporting cites a swing from a ~$183 million loss to ~$305 million in profit, though this is not confirmed in NOC's primary filings and must be validated at the next 10-Q.

We rate NOC a HOLD (growth_hold). The franchise quality is high and the drawdown has restored a reasonable valuation (~17.3x forward EPS, ~2% above the analyst target floor of $533), but the setup lacks a dated near-term catalyst, estimate revisions are flat, insiders have sold near the highs with no offsetting buying, and the score trajectory has been declining (6.7 in April to 5.6 in late June) on legitimate deterioration in risk, management-alignment, and balance-sheet signals rather than on price alone. The name is worth owning for the eventual B-21 production re-rating, but the catalyst is not yet in hand.

## Company Overview

Northrop operates four reporting segments. **Mission Systems** ($12.5B revenue, 14.6% margin) is the highest-margin and fastest-growing business, spanning radar, electronic warfare, C4ISR, microelectronics, F-35 fire control/DAS/CNI, and submarine propulsion; roughly 30% is restricted, and it grew about 10% in 2025. **Aeronautics Systems** ($13.0B, 6.3% margin) houses the B-21, B-2 sustainment, F-35 fuselage, E-2D, Triton, and TACAMO; its depressed margin reflects the B-21 fixed-price drag, and ~40% is restricted. **Space Systems** ($10.8B, 11.0% margin) covers satellites, SDA layers, missile defense, GEM boosters, and SLS motors; revenue fell 8% in 2025 on the wind-down of a restricted program and NGI. **Defense Systems** ($8.0B, 10.9% margin) contains Sentinel, IBCS, munitions, solid rocket motors, and hypersonics.

The company runs on long-term percentage-of-completion contracts, split roughly 50/50 between cost-type and fixed-price arrangements. This mix is central to the investment case: cost-type work protects margins, but the fixed-price development contracts — which customers increasingly favor — are where cost overruns bite, as the B-21 experience demonstrated. NOC employs about 95,000 people, many holding security clearances, which is itself a barrier to competitive entry.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded at a steady mid-single-digit pace: $36.6B (2022), $39.3B (2023), $41.0B (2024), $42.0B (2025), with 2025 organic growth around 3% after adjusting for the training-services divestiture. The dominant swing factor in profitability has been the B-21 fixed-price loss. Operating income collapsed to $2.54B (6.5% margin) in 2023 after a $1.56B pre-tax loss provision on the five LRIP options, recovered to $4.37B in 2024, and reached $4.51B (10.8% margin) in 2025. Q1 2025 absorbed an additional $477M B-21 charge; the Q4 2025 review found no further adjustment. Net income was $4.18B in both 2024 and 2025, with reported EPS of $29.08 (aided by a ~2% share-count reduction) and management's MTM-adjusted EPS at $26.34.

Cash generation is the clear strength. Operating cash flow rose to $4.76B in 2025 and free cash flow to $3.31B, up 26% year over year and helped by $317M lower capex. Operating cash flow exceeds net income, ROA is 8.1%, and ROE is a strong 25.1%. The earnings-surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats through Q1 FY2026, including a 17.6% beat in Q3 2025 — which supports confidence in the consensus estimate track.

The balance sheet is where caution enters. Long-term debt has risen to $15.2B from $11.8B in 2022, D/E is 2.08, and equity of $16.7B sits against $51.4B of assets, of which $17.4B (34%) is goodwill from the Orbital ATK, TRW, and Litton legacy deals. The current ratio is a thin 1.1, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.88 places the company in the grey zone. This reflects an asset-heavy, leveraged, goodwill-laden structure rather than distress — investment-grade credit and government-backed revenue mitigate genuine bankruptcy risk — but the thin equity base means any impairment or a prolonged budget/B-21 shock would pressure reported equity. NOC returned $2.9B to shareholders in 2025 (down from $3.7B in 2024) and issued $1.0B of senior notes in May 2025. Share count fell from 155M in 2022 to 143.5M in 2025, a consistent ~2%/yr reduction that is a structural part of the EPS story and precisely what the new capital-return executive order threatens.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is favorable and confirmed across multiple channels. Rising global defense budgets, active conflicts in Ukraine, the Middle East, and Pacific deterrence needs, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's ~$150B defense allocation (available through FY2034, aimed at missile defense, munitions, strategic deterrence, shipbuilding, and Pacific deterrence) all map onto NOC's portfolio. Procurement-reform pressure (fixed-price mandates, efficiency reviews) cuts the other way for legacy execution.

The forward EPS path is anchored by stable consensus estimates: current-year EPS of $27.95 and next-year of $30.24, with 90-day revisions essentially flat (+0.0% current year, +0.2% next year). This is not the >30% estimate-revision signal that would flag a decisive buy; it is a steady-compounder profile. The company reaffirmed full-year guidance at Q1 FY2026, with sales +4% to $9.9B (organic +5%) and 10.8% margin.

The single most important growth variable is the B-21 transition. As the program moves from development into production, the fixed-price loss drag should reverse, mechanically lifting Aeronautics Systems margins from the depressed 6.3% level. If the reported profit swing holds, this is the re-rating catalyst. Offsetting factors: Space Systems shrank 8% in 2025 and needs SDA and missile-defense wins to refill it, and the buyback lever that has historically added ~2 points to EPS growth is now under regulatory threat. The growth thesis is real but not accelerating — a mid-single-digit revenue compounder with a specific margin-recovery option embedded in B-21.

## Valuation Assessment

At $521.57, NOC trades at roughly 17.3x forward consensus EPS ($30.24 next-year) and about 16.3x trailing. That is a substantial discount to the aerospace/defense peer median P/E of 39.3, though the median is distorted by the aftermarket-heavy TransDigm (39.3x), high-growth Elbit (73.9x), and unprofitable Rocket Lab. The cleaner comparables are the diversified primes: General Dynamics at 22.6x and L3Harris at 33.9x. On that basis NOC trades at a meaningful discount to GD despite comparable franchise quality, and a wide discount to LHX.

The discount is partly justified: NOC carries higher leverage than GD (D/E 2.08 vs. GD's cleaner structure), a lower-margin segment mix while B-21 drags, and the newly surfaced capital-return risk. But at forward levels with the current price already slightly above the lowest analyst target ($533) and a mean target of $670, the market is pricing a fairly cautious scenario. The analyst sentiment trend is degrading (one Strong Buy moved to Buy, and holds rose from 9 to 12 over three months), which tempers the "targets are floors" argument; on a name with flat estimates and a degrading rating mix, consensus targets are less reliable as forward markers than they would be on an accelerating story.

This is a fair-to-slightly-attractive valuation, not a compelling one. The stock is cheap enough to own for the franchise and the B-21 optionality, but "cheap" here reflects genuine overhangs rather than pure mispricing. We do not treat the low multiple as a standalone buy signal: the discount to peers requires the B-21 profit swing and capital-return clarity to close, and neither is confirmed.

## Competitive Landscape

NOC's moat is among the most durable in the sector. The B-21 and Sentinel are national monopolies — no other company can execute them — and submarine propulsion and Trident motors are similarly sole-source and central to the nuclear triad. This gives multi-decade revenue visibility that new entrants cannot contest. The large restricted/classified portfolio compounds the barrier: these programs are invisible to competitors and require established clearances and infrastructure.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving at the franchise level. Mission Systems is growing double-digits and gaining in radar, EW, and microelectronics. The primary competitive threat flagged repeatedly in the 10-K is the rise of new entrants and startups using OTA (non-FAR) procurement, which pressures pricing and margins on newer, contestable programs, but does not reach the strategic-deterrent core. The one negative segment trajectory is Space Systems (-8%), which faces both program wind-downs and heightened competition from newer space-focused players. On balance the competitive position is strengthening at the high-margin/sole-source end and eroding at the contestable margin.

## Risk Assessment

**B-21 fixed-price exposure** remains the defining company-specific risk. The five LRIP lots are largely fixed-price with NTE pricing extending to unit 40. Management is negotiating an accelerated production rate that "would require future investment by the company to expand production capacity." Any further cost growth or unfavorable supplier terms could materially affect results. The bull case that the program is turning profitable is plausible but not confirmed in primary filings.

**Capital-return executive order (new and material).** The FY2025 10-K adds an executive order that could prohibit buybacks and dividends for contractors deemed to have underperformed, insufficiently prioritized contracts, or under-invested in production. This is NOC-specific and strikes at the share-reduction EPS lever. It is a low-base-rate but binary risk with no dated resolution.

**Sentinel** breached Nunn-McCurdy cost thresholds in January 2024, was certified to continue but directed to be restructured; the Production and Deployment phases remain unpriced, leaving a large open negotiation. **Budget/appropriations risk** persists — FY2026 appropriations were not enacted, with a shutdown Oct 1–Nov 12, 2025 and a CR through Jan 30, 2026 — though the OBBBA injection is a strong offset. **Pension MTM** adds Q4 volatility (a $527M benefit in 2025, a $422M charge in 2023; a 100bp asset-return miss swings ~$314M). The thin equity base amplifies the consequence of any goodwill impairment.

Insider activity offers no bullish confirmation: no open-market executive buying, and sales near the highs (General Counsel at $745, Mission Systems President at $720). Post-vesting sales are routine and estimate revisions are stable, which limits the bearish weight of the selling, but the absence of any buying near a 52-week low is a mild negative on the management-alignment read. Governance is otherwise credible: comp is weighted to Adjusted FCF, ROIC, and TSR, and "comp actually paid" fell during the 2023 B-21 loss, showing the plan works.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility has jumped from 25.8% to 35.6% over the past month while the price barely moved, and the term structure is in backwardation (35.6% near-term vs. 31.6% at six months), so the market anticipates an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print and any B-21 disclosure. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.89 to 1.27, and IV is rich relative to 27.4% historical vol. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±8.8%. Put/call skew is only mildly negative (-1.6% near-term, widening to -4.1% at six months), so positioning is not one-sidedly bearish but does reflect elevated uncertainty into the next report. Short interest is negligible (1.7% of float, 2.1 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in play. The rich IV argues against paying up for the stock ahead of the print and favors patience.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$700).** The B-21 profit swing is confirmed at the next 10-Q, Aeronautics margins recover from 6.3% toward the low double digits, the capital-return EO does not apply to NOC, and buybacks resume at the ~2%/yr pace. Mission Systems continues double-digit growth, the OBBBA funding flows into missile defense and strategic deterrence, and the multiple re-rates toward GD's 22.6x on ~$32 forward EPS. This maps to roughly $700, near the analyst mean of $670 — about 34% upside.

**Bear case (~$430).** B-21 cost growth resurfaces or accelerated-rate negotiations require heavy uncompensated investment, the EO restricts buybacks and removes the EPS lever, appropriations dysfunction delays awards, and Space Systems continues to shrink. On a de-rated ~14x forward and pressured estimates, the stock falls toward $430 — about 18% downside from current levels.

The asymmetry is favorable but not decisive: roughly 34% to the base/bull versus 18% downside. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is timing and confirmation. The estimate-revision signal is flat, the B-21 profit figures are unconfirmed in primary filings, the capital-return risk is unresolved, insiders are net sellers, and the score has been trending down on real signal deterioration. This is Industrials, a sector where BUY calls have historically underperformed the market on alpha, which raises the bar for a company-specific catalyst that NOC does not yet have on a dated basis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next 10-Q / Q2 FY2026 earnings (expected late July) is the key checkpoint — it should confirm or refute the B-21 loss-to-profit swing and give visibility on the capital-return EO's applicability.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 filing confirms the B-21 program has flipped to profit (Aeronautics segment margin recovering toward double digits), validating the margin-recovery thesis.
- Clarity that the capital-return executive order does not restrict NOC's buyback/dividend, preserving the ~2%/yr EPS lever, OR a resumption of buybacks at prior pace.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (next-year EPS revised up >5% over 90 days) combined with the stock holding above the low-$500s.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new B-21 loss provision or disclosure that accelerated-rate production requires material uncompensated capital investment.
- The capital-return EO is applied to NOC, removing the buyback engine and cutting the EPS growth path.
- Sentinel Production/Deployment pricing lands unfavorably, or a goodwill impairment given the thin equity base.

**Base-case anchor:** At ~18x forward on $30.24 next-year EPS, fair value sits near $545; the B-21 confirmation is what closes the gap to the bull case. Downside is anchored near $430 (14x on pressured estimates).

## Conclusion

Northrop owns some of the most durable franchises in defense: sole-source strategic-deterrent programs, a record backlog, and a demand backdrop reinforced by the OBBBA and multi-front conflict. The ~30% drawdown to near the 52-week low has restored a reasonable valuation and improved the risk/reward. But the drawdown is not purely sentiment: it reflects a real Q1 profit-vs-estimate miss, a novel executive-order threat to the buyback-driven EPS lever, and a B-21 profit swing that is reported by secondary sources but unconfirmed in filings. Estimate revisions are flat, insiders have sold near the highs with no buying, and the internal score has declined steadily since April on legitimate deterioration in the risk and management-alignment reads.

We rate NOC a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale. The path to a BUY is clear and near-dated — confirmation of the B-21 production margin recovery and clarity on the capital-return EO at the Q2 report — but until those resolve, the setup is a high-quality franchise at a fair price without a confirmed catalyst. Rich near-term implied volatility argues for patience over chasing ahead of the print. Reassess at Q2 FY2026 earnings.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's valuation claim of a "~24% gap to the lowest analyst target ($533)" was incorrect — the current price of $521.57 is actually ~2% *below* the low target, so the "margin of safety to the target floor" argument supporting the HOLD's valuation cushion is materially weaker than stated.
