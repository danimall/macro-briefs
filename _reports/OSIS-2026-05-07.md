## Executive Summary

OSI Systems is a $4B security screening and detection company in the midst of a strategic pivot from dependence on large international turnkey contracts (primarily Mexico's SEDENA military) toward U.S. homeland defense and military applications. The company's recent $235M Over-the-Horizon Radar contract and September 2024 military/space acquisition demonstrate execution against this shift, while a record $1.9B backlog with 1.3x book-to-bill provides multi-year visibility.

Multiple risk vectors converge simultaneously: a DOJ investigation broadly scoped around the company's Mexico business dealings, accounts receivable at 178 days sales outstanding (49% of revenue), declining estimate revisions (-3.5% for the current quarter over 30 days), 15.4% short interest, and a sharp 17% post-earnings decline on May 5. The options market confirms near-term stress with backwardated term structure (45.8% near-term vs. 41.4% at 6 months) and OI put/call ratio of 5.67.

The stock trades at 27.5x trailing earnings and approximately 23x forward FY2026 estimates of $10.42. While the growth trajectory and competitive positioning are legitimate, the magnitude of unresolved downside risks (DOJ outcome, Mexico collection timing, convertible dilution) warrants caution until at least one of these overhangs clears.

## Company Overview

OSI Systems operates across three segments: Security (70% of FY2025 revenue, $1.196B), Optoelectronics & Manufacturing (20%, $349M), and Healthcare (10%, $168M). The Security division is the growth engine, providing cargo/vehicle inspection systems, airport screening technology, and turnkey checkpoint operations to governments worldwide.

The company's vertical integration — manufacturing its own optoelectronic components — provides cost advantages and supply chain control that pure-play security software or systems integrators cannot replicate. OSI serves customers in 35+ countries, though revenue concentration remains a persistent issue, with one Security customer (almost certainly Mexico's SEDENA) representing 42% of accounts receivable.

The strategic pivot is clear: Mexico revenue declined from $423M (FY2024) to $276M (FY2025) and is projected to decline further as equipment deliveries are substantially complete. Replacement revenue is coming from U.S. defense (OTHR radar contract, military/space acquisition contributing ~$80M run rate) and growing service revenue ($390M in FY2025, +18% YoY).

Revenue has grown 45% since FY2022, with operating margins expanding 240bps over the same period. SG&A leverage (declining from 17.9% to 17.0% of revenue) and steady R&D investment (~4.3%) demonstrate operational discipline. FY2026 guidance of $1.825B–$1.867B implies 6.5%–9.0% growth, representing continued progress despite the Mexico headwind.

**Cash Flow Quality — The Central Concern:**

Operating cash flow has been erratic: $95M (FY2023) → negative $88M (FY2024) → $98M (FY2025). The FY2025 recovery was attributed to improved receivable collections, but accounts receivable still grew by $190M to $838M — representing approximately 178 days of revenue. This is roughly triple what would be considered normal for a manufacturing/services company.

Free cash flow of $74M against $150M in net income implies only 49% cash conversion, far below the 80%+ typically expected. Management noted $74M in post-quarter collections from Mexico expected to boost Q4 FY2026 cash flow, confirming ongoing collection challenges.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of $650M includes $350M in convertible notes due 2029 (at 2.25%, conversion price $191.98) and a $725M revolving facility extended to July 2030. Net debt of approximately $543M against $218M operating income yields net leverage of ~2.5x — manageable for the industry but leaving limited flexibility if collections deteriorate further. The July 2025 credit facility expansion to $725M and new sub-limits suggest banks remain comfortable.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Growth Drivers:**

1. **Record backlog of $1.9B** with 1.3x book-to-bill in Q3 FY2026 provides approximately 12-14 months of revenue visibility at current run rates.

2. **U.S. defense expansion:** The $235M OTHR contract (initially funded at $46M) represents OSI's entrance into a new customer segment with recurring upgrade/maintenance potential. Combined with the September 2024 military/space acquisition ($81M revenue, $8.9M net income contribution from acquisition through June 2025), U.S. defense could represent 15-20% of revenue within 2-3 years.

3. **Service revenue growth:** $390M in FY2025, up 18% YoY. As the installed base of screening systems expands, service revenue becomes a higher-margin recurring stream.

4. **Global security spending tailwinds:** Border enforcement, airport upgrades, and counter-terrorism spending are secular growth drivers across both developed and developing markets.

**Growth Headwinds:**

1. **Mexico revenue cliff:** From $423M (FY2024) to an estimated $150-200M in FY2026 and declining further. Management must replace $200-250M in annual revenue.

2. **Estimate revisions turning negative:** Current quarter EPS estimate fell 3.5% in 30 days ($3.90 → $3.76). Full-year FY2026 declined marginally (-0.4%). This represents a directional change from the positive momentum that drove the stock from $220 to $310 between August 2025 and April 2026.

3. **Healthcare segment weakness:** At 10% of revenue and declining, this segment is a drag. Without a turnaround or divestiture, it consumes management attention disproportionate to its contribution.

## Competitive Landscape

OSI's competitive position is strong within security screening, supported by:

**Structural advantages:**
- Vertical integration (Optoelectronics division supplies components)
- Turnkey capability (build, staff, operate entire screening operations)
- Breadth of technology (X-ray, CT, trace detection, RF/radar, now OTHR)
- 25+ year relationships with sovereign customers

**Competitive dynamics:**
- Nuctech (Chinese state-owned) is the primary competitor in emerging markets. Growing Western concerns about Chinese security equipment in allied nations creates an opportunity for OSI to win market share in countries previously open to Nuctech.
- Smiths Group and Leidos are competitors but with different focus areas (Leidos at $15B+ is far larger and more diversified).
- The OTHR contract signals OSI's entry into a space dominated by larger defense primes, potentially indicating price/capability advantages that attracted the customer.

**Trajectory:** The shift from pure security screening toward defense electronics broadens the addressable market and reduces reliance on a small number of massive international contracts, improving competitive positioning over time.

## Risk Assessment

**1. DOJ Investigation (Probability of Material Impact: 25-35%)**

The February 2023 subpoena requesting documents about "the Company's business dealings in Mexico since 2020" is broad enough to encompass the SEDENA contracts. While management characterizes this as related to a former employee's pre-hire conduct, the scope and follow-up subpoena (February 2024) suggest continuing investigative interest. A negative finding could result in fines, debarment from U.S. government contracts, or forced disclosure of contract irregularities. This is the highest-severity risk and the most likely explanation for the 15.4% short interest.

**2. Accounts Receivable/Working Capital Risk**

At 178 DSO and $838M in receivables against $1.7B revenue, OSI carries unusual balance sheet risk. If a major sovereign customer delays or disputes payment, the company would face simultaneous earnings recognition questions and liquidity pressure. The $74M in post-quarter Mexico collections mitigates near-term concern but confirms the structural challenge.

**3. Convertible Note Dilution**

The $350M in 2029 notes at a $191.98 conversion price are deeply in-the-money. Full conversion would add ~2.3M shares (14% dilution). While OSI settles principal in cash and only delivers shares for the excess, a sustained stock price above conversion creates ongoing EPS headwind through diluted share count.

**4. Revenue Concentration Transition Risk**

The company must replace $200M+ in declining Mexico revenue with new contracts. The $1.9B backlog suggests this is achievable, but the timing mismatch between Mexico wind-down and new contract ramp creates potential for a revenue air pocket in FY2027.

**5. Short Interest and Activist Exposure**

The 15.4% short interest with 7.0 days to cover is substantial. Short seller Culper Research published a report in March 2025 targeting the Mexico contracts and DOJ investigation. Elevated short interest creates potential for both: (a) violent short squeezes if positive catalysts emerge, and (b) continued analytical scrutiny that surfaces additional concerns.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term IV of 45.8% exceeds 1-year historical vol of 42.7%, with term structure in backwardation (near > far). The put/call OI ratio of 5.67 indicates overwhelming institutional put positioning. Combined with the short interest data, the derivatives market aligns with a cautious or bearish view on the next 1-3 months. This does not confirm fundamental deterioration, but any additional negative catalyst (DOJ update, collection write-off, guidance reduction) would face amplified selling pressure from delta-hedging flows.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300-345, +25-43%):**
- DOJ investigation resolved without material findings
- Mexico collections normalize, FCF converts at 80%+
- U.S. defense revenue accelerates (OTHR follow-on, additional military contracts)
- Backlog converts cleanly, FY2027 revenue grows 8-12%
- Re-rating toward defense contractor multiples (25-30x forward)
- Short squeeze potential if DOJ clears

**Bear Case ($180-210, -13-25%):**
- DOJ investigation produces adverse findings (fines, debarment, accounting restatements)
- Mexico receivables require partial write-down
- Backlog conversion disappoints (contract delays, customer cancellations)
- Revenue air pocket in FY2027 as Mexico fully winds down before defense scales
- Short sellers publish follow-up research with additional findings
- Stock trades to 20x forward on reduced estimates (~$9 EPS × 20x = $180)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. DOJ investigation formally closed with no adverse findings
2. Two consecutive quarters of operating cash flow > $80M demonstrating normalized collections
3. Stock price below $210 on unchanged/improving fundamental trajectory (would imply <20x forward P/E)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DOJ issues formal enforcement action, subpoena to officers/directors, or requests expanded document production
2. Management discloses material receivable impairment or write-down
3. FY2026 revenue guidance reduced below $1.80B (would indicate backlog conversion failure)

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural assessment point is Q4 FY2026 earnings (likely August 2026), which should show: (a) whether Mexico $74M collections materialized, (b) OTHR contract revenue recognition trajectory, (c) full-year FY2026 results vs. guidance. The DOJ situation lacks a defined timeline but would likely surface if proceeding to enforcement.

## Conclusion

OSI Systems is executing a genuine strategic transformation from a company dependent on lumpy international security contracts toward a diversified security/defense electronics provider with growing U.S. government exposure. The $1.9B backlog, OTHR contract, and service revenue growth provide credible multi-year visibility.

However, the convergence of DOJ investigation risk, extreme working capital characteristics (178 DSO), negative estimate revision momentum, and a post-earnings collapse creates too much unresolved uncertainty for a constructive call. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric: 25-43% upside if the bull case plays out, 13-25% downside in the bear case, with both scenarios having meaningful probability.

The correct posture is patience. Wait for the DOJ overhang to resolve or for the stock to decline to levels that provide sufficient margin of safety (~$210 or below, representing <20x forward earnings). At current levels, the investment requires conviction in unknowable legal outcomes — conviction that the options and short interest positioning suggest the market lacks.
