**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sealed Air's common stock now trades as a cash-merger instrument rather than a fundamental packaging equity. On November 16, 2025, the company signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by affiliates of Clayton, Dubilier & Rice (CD&R) for $42.15 per share in cash, valuing the enterprise at $10.3 billion. The board approved unanimously, shareholders approved at a special meeting on February 25, 2026, and U.S. antitrust review was resolved when HSR early termination was granted on December 23, 2025. Expected close is mid-2026. Any position in SEE common is a bet on deal completion, with the operating business a secondary consideration relevant only in a break scenario.

The deal indicators are favorable. The two hardest gates — a shareholder vote and U.S. antitrust — are cleared. The residual risks are the standard tail set for a leveraged buyout: remaining non-U.S. antitrust and foreign-investment approvals, no Material Adverse Effect, and CD&R securing debt financing to layer additional leverage onto a company that already carries $4.01 billion of debt at sub-investment-grade ratings. S&P placed SEE on CreditWatch negative and Moody's put ratings under review for downgrade the day after announcement, reflecting the LBO's incremental leverage. A $205.1 million termination fee is payable by SEE under certain circumstances, and putative stockholder class actions challenging the merger have been filed (routine for deals of this size).

The critical missing input is the current market price relative to $42.15, which we could not source from live market data. The arbitrage spread — deal price versus trading price — is the single most important variable for an actionable decision, and it determines whether the annualized return on the remaining spread compensates for deal-break risk. Absent that input, the honest classification is an uncertain hold-to-catalyst: the deal appears highly likely to close, but position sizing and the go/no-go decision hinge on a spread we cannot observe. If the deal terminates, this reverts to AVOID: the underlying business is slow-growing, highly leveraged, and structurally challenged in one of its two segments.

## Company Overview

Sealed Air is a global packaging solutions provider operating through two reportable segments. The Food segment (roughly 67% of sales, $3.59 billion in 2025) sells largely direct to industrial food processors — fresh red meat, poultry, processed meats, seafood, cheese, and liquids — anchored by the CRYOVAC brand and the LIQUIBOX liquids systems acquired in 2023. This is the higher-margin, more defensive segment, with adjusted EBITDA margin of 23.1%, up from 22.5% in 2024. The Protective segment (roughly 33% of sales, $1.77 billion in 2025) provides transit packaging for e-commerce, industrial, and fulfillment markets, including BUBBLE WRAP, AUTOBAG, and Instapak, sold roughly 60% through distributors. Protective carries a lower and declining margin (17.6% adjusted EBITDA) and has been the persistent drag on the consolidated business.

The company operates in 46 countries and distributes to 119, employs approximately 16,100 people, and generates 49% of sales outside the United States. No customer or affiliated group exceeds 10% of sales, a genuinely diversified base. The competitive moat rests on iconic brands, materials-science and automation intellectual property (roughly 2,320 patents), and global scale, set against a fragmented and consolidating competitive field.

On transaction structure: CD&R is the private-equity acquirer; Sword Purchaser LLC and Sword Merger Sub are CD&R's acquisition vehicles, not operating businesses. The $42.15 per share is the equity consideration; the $10.3 billion is total transaction value including assumed debt.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years: $5.64 billion (2022), $5.49 billion (2023), $5.39 billion (2024), and $5.36 billion (2025). The 2025 result was essentially flat at -0.6% headline, but that includes an FX tailwind masking a -1.2% constant-currency decline. The Protective segment is the source of the erosion — down 8% in 2024 and another 3% constant-currency in 2025 on North American fulfillment-customer churn and industrial weakness.

Operations are stable even as the top line softens. Operating margin has held near 13.5% with gross margin around 29.8%. Adjusted EBITDA has been steady and slightly growing: $1,107 million (2023), $1,111 million (2024), and $1,134 million (2025), with margin expanding to 21.2% on the completed CTO2Grow cost program, which delivered its full $160 million annualized savings target by year-end 2025.

GAAP net income is misleadingly volatile. Reported net income moved from $342 million (2023) to $265 million (2024) to $506 million (2025). The 2025 jump is overwhelmingly a tax event, not operating improvement: the effective tax rate collapsed to 7.4% from 41.2% in 2024, driven by a $170 million reversal of uncertain tax positions from resolving IRS audits (2017–2019) and international matters, plus a $64 million discontinued-operations gain tied to the earlier Diversey sale. Adjusted EPS was $3.34 in 2025 versus $3.14 in 2024, a roughly 6% underlying improvement. A screen reading "net income up 91%" would badly misinterpret this business.

Cash generation is solid but declining. Operating cash flow was $628 million in 2025 versus $728 million in 2024; free cash flow was $458 million (8.6% margin) versus $508 million, with the decline reflecting higher tax and incentive-comp payments. Capex was cut to $170 million from $220 million.

The balance sheet is where the distress screen originates. The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 sits in the "distress zone," but this is a capital-structure artifact, not an operating-distress signal. Debt-to-equity is 4.67x, total debt $4.01 billion, net debt $3.66 billion. Equity is thin at $1.24 billion, compressed by a history of special dividends and buybacks and by $2.9 billion of goodwill. Deleveraging is working: total debt fell $394 million in 2025 and net debt declined from $4.03 billion to $3.66 billion. The covenant leverage ratio is 2.95x against a 4.50x maximum, comfortable headroom. The current ratio of 0.91 (negative working capital) is distorted by $625 million of long-term debt reclassified to current (the October 2026 Senior Secured Notes); the $1 billion revolver is fully undrawn. Corporate credit ratings are sub-investment-grade (Moody's Ba1 / S&P BB+), with senior secured notes at the investment-grade cusp (Baa2/BBB-). No share repurchases occurred in 2024 or 2025 (versus $80 million in 2023); the ~$120 million annual dividend was maintained, with deleveraging the explicit priority.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, growth prospects are muted. The bifurcation between the two segments defines the operating story: Food benefits from durable secular tailwinds — labor scarcity driving automation, urbanization, ship-to-home food consumption, and demand for sustainable packaging — which support its roughly 23% EBITDA margins. Protective faces structural headwinds as e-commerce packaging commoditizes, with SEE losing fulfillment volume to price competition and to molded-pulp and corrugate substitution.

The 2025 earnings improvement was substantially cost-driven (CTO2Grow) rather than volume-driven, and that lever is now largely exhausted with the program complete. The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses across the four disclosed quarters, with strong recent beats (23.5% in Q3 2025, 4.3% in Q4 2025) — but this is beside the point once the deal is signed. As a going concern, forward growth would depend on stabilizing Protective and continued Food margin expansion, neither of which supports a compelling standalone re-rating case. The likely PE thesis is to reshape the portfolio away from public-market scrutiny, possibly separating Protective, which is why the growth question matters less than deal completion.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation here is not a discounted-cash-flow or peer-multiple exercise. The stock is a claim on $42.15 per share in cash, and its fair value is that figure discounted for time to close and deal-break probability. With shareholder approval secured, HSR cleared, and a unanimous board, the deal probability is high, which anchors intrinsic value near the deal price less a modest arbitrage discount.

We could not source the current trading price, so we cannot quantify the spread or the implied annualized return. This is a material gap. If the stock trades at a wide discount to the offer (say, several percent), the annualized return on a mid-2026 close could be attractive relative to the residual risk. If it trades within a point of the deal price, the remaining spread offers little compensation for the tail risk of a financing failure or foreign regulatory delay, and the risk/reward favors avoidance.

The valuation score reflects this ambiguity. On a fundamental basis, the underlying equity would carry a low score: a slow-declining, highly leveraged business is a value trap absent a catalyst, and cheapness on trailing metrics historically sorts the wrong way. The merger provides a specific, dated catalyst, and the deal price sits at a premium to the pre-announcement fundamental level. The company's total shareholder return had lagged its peer group materially (FY2024 TSR of 92.83 versus peer 142.41; FY2023 of 97.98 versus 132.10), which is part of the backdrop to accepting a take-private. The mid-range score captures a probable but unquantified positive spread against a genuine, if low-probability, downside if the deal breaks.

## Competitive Landscape

Sealed Air's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding. The Food franchise is durable — CRYOVAC and LIQUIBOX hold strong brand and IP positions in industrial food packaging, with margins expanding and secular demand tailwinds. Protective is losing ground: fulfillment-volume churn, commoditization of e-commerce packaging, and substitution toward molded-pulp and corrugate alternatives have driven multi-year volume declines. The overall trajectory is a strong segment offsetting a structurally challenged one, netting to flat-to-down consolidated revenue.

The packaging industry is fragmenting and consolidating, a dynamic SEE acknowledges explicitly. CD&R taking the company private fits that theme: a stable, cash-rich Food business paired with an underperforming Protective segment ripe for restructuring is a classic private-equity reshaping thesis. Regulatory pressure on plastics — the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, Extended Producer Responsibility laws, plastic taxes, and PFAS litigation and regulation — is a long-term secular headwind to the entire flexible-plastics category, and applies whether the company is public or private.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is deal completion. Two major gates are cleared (shareholder vote, U.S. antitrust), but the merger remains subject to certain non-U.S. antitrust and foreign-investment approvals, absence of a Material Adverse Effect, and CD&R securing debt financing. The financing risk is the most substantive residual concern: this is a leveraged buyout adding debt to an already-levered, sub-investment-grade company, and the 10-K explicitly flags financing execution given credit-market conditions. Interim-period restrictions prevent SEE from pursuing alternatives (the go-shop expired December 16, 2025), issuing securities, or making major capex without CD&R approval, and create customer and employee uncertainty during pendency.

If the deal breaks, the stock would likely fall sharply toward its pre-deal fundamental level, and the operating risk set becomes relevant: high leverage and refinancing risk with sub-IG ratings on negative watch, resin-cost cyclicality (petrochemical resins are roughly one-third of COGS, with ~45% of Food sales on formula pricing that lags raw-material moves by about six months), Protective's secular softness, plastics regulation and PFAS exposure, and FX (49% international, recurring Argentina hyperinflation losses, ~$30 million cash trapped in Russia/Ukraine).

The 10-K filed March 2, 2026 added an entirely new "Pending Merger Risks" section and expanded AI-adoption, 2025 tariff-regime, and OBBBA/OECD Pillar Two tax-law risks.

The risk score is set above midpoint because the two largest binary risks (shareholder vote, U.S. antitrust) are already resolved, and the downside scenario is well understood and low-probability. The asymmetry is characteristic of a late-stage merger arb: modest upside to the spread, sharp downside on a break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case:** The merger closes at $42.15 in cash by mid-2026. Shareholder approval and HSR clearance are done; the board is unanimous; the go-shop expired without a competing bid; and CD&R announced the deal with committed financing. Remaining foreign approvals for a well-diversified packaging company with no single geography dominating are routine, and CD&R — an experienced sponsor in industrial and packaging assets — completes the LBO financing. An investor buying below $42.15 captures the remaining spread plus any interim dividend, with an annualized return that could be attractive if the discount is wide enough. Upside to $42.15.

**Bear case:** CD&R's LBO financing fails to close in a stressed credit market, or a foreign regulator delays or blocks approval, or a Material Adverse Effect is invoked. The deal terminates, and the stock reverts toward its pre-announcement fundamental value — a slow-declining, highly leveraged packaging business with a distressed Z-Score, a structurally challenged Protective segment, and a tax-driven 2025 earnings inflation that unwinds in the market's perception. A break could take the stock materially below the current level, depending on how much of the deal premium is currently embedded in the price. (The precise magnitude cannot be quantified without a current market price and a pre-announcement price reference, neither of which was available.)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, driven by the missing arbitrage-spread input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Confirmation that the stock trades at a discount to $42.15 wide enough to deliver an attractive annualized return to a mid-2026 close (roughly 3%+ absolute spread would merit a fresh look given the low break probability); (2) receipt of the remaining non-U.S. regulatory approvals; (3) public confirmation that CD&R's debt financing is committed and funded. Any two of these, combined with a favorable spread, would support a BUY as a defined-return arbitrage position sized as a binary event bet.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) Termination of the merger agreement for any reason; (2) a formal challenge or extended review from a foreign regulator that materially delays the timeline; (3) any indication that CD&R's financing is at risk or that a Material Adverse Effect is being contemplated. Deal termination immediately reverts this to AVOID on the standalone fundamentals.

**Reassessment timeline:** Continuous through the expected mid-2026 close. The next natural checkpoints are announcements of remaining regulatory clearances and financing confirmation. The prior standalone weakness supports the pre-deal caution; the signed transaction justifies moving to HOLD rather than continuing AVOID, but the score remains sub-5 because the actionable go/no-go decision depends on a spread we cannot observe.

## Conclusion

Sealed Air common is a merger-arbitrage instrument priced against a $42.15 all-cash offer from CD&R, with shareholder approval and U.S. antitrust clearance already secured and close expected mid-2026. The operating business — flat-to-declining revenue, a durable Food franchise, a structurally weak Protective segment, a tax-inflated 2025 net income, and a leverage-driven distress Z-Score — is a secondary consideration relevant only if the deal breaks. Deal-completion indicators are favorable, with residual risk concentrated in remaining foreign approvals and CD&R's LBO financing.

The recommendation is HOLD (uncertain), because the single most important variable — the current market price relative to $42.15 — could not be sourced. If the spread is wide, this is an attractive defined-return arbitrage worthy of a BUY; if the stock trades near the deal price, the remaining spread does not compensate for the break tail. Source the current price before acting. If the deal terminates, revert to AVOID: the underlying equity does not stand on its own fundamentals.
