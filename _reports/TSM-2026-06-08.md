**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TSMC is the single most important node in the global semiconductor supply chain, manufacturing the leading-edge silicon (2nm/3nm) that underpins nearly all advanced AI accelerators and premium mobile processors. The investment case rests on three confirmed facts: 2nm capacity is fully pre-sold through end-2026, Q1 2026 gross margin held at 66.2% despite the dilutive ramp of higher-cost overseas fabs, and consensus EPS estimates have risen roughly 9-10% over the last 90 days while the company beat its most recent quarter by 4.4% (the fourth consecutive beat). Forward FY26 EPS of ~$15.62 against the current $415-427 ADR price puts the stock near 27x forward earnings for a business guiding to "30%+" revenue growth with ~45% net margins and self-funded multi-billion-dollar capex. That is a reasonable, not stretched, multiple for the quality and growth on offer.

The reason this is a measured BUY rather than a high-conviction one is the risk profile, which is genuinely binary in one dimension and structurally degrading in another. The defining risk is geopolitical: the overwhelming majority of leading-edge capacity sits in Taiwan, and a renewed U.S.-China diplomatic thaw has reopened debate over Taiwan's status. This is a tail risk no other foundry shares, and it caps the multiple the market will pay regardless of fundamentals. The slower-burning risk is that TSMC's own scarcity-driven pricing power (customers facing 80-90% higher spot pricing on advanced nodes) is precisely what is pushing Apple, Nvidia, and Broadcom to court Samsung and Intel for a second source. The company's near-term dominance is intact and arguably strengthening; the multi-year strategic counter-current is real.

The stock has appreciated ~104% over the trailing twelve months, driven by fundamental improvement (record revenue, +58% Q1 net profit, raised guidance) rather than multiple expansion alone. Prior appreciation is not a reason to avoid the name. The constraints are the valuation, which is full but not excessive, and the Taiwan tail, which is unhedgeable. We rate TSM a BUY with deliberate position sizing of 2-3% of a diversified book, reflecting both the quality of the franchise and the concentration risk that makes maximum sizing inappropriate.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing chips designed by fabless customers including Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm, and MediaTek. Its moat is a multi-year process-technology lead at the leading edge combined with manufacturing yields no competitor has matched at scale. For roughly a decade, the world's most advanced silicon has been produced almost exclusively by TSMC because neither Samsung Foundry nor Intel Foundry could match its yields at cutting-edge nodes.

The mix is heavily weighted to advanced process technology: chips at 7nm and below accounted for approximately 75% of total wafer revenue in Q1 2026. The 2nm (N2) node is fully pre-sold through end-2026, primarily to AI data-center customers, with Apple alone reportedly locking up over half of 2nm output. TSMC is building five 2nm fabs in Kaohsiung, Taiwan, alongside major overseas expansion in Arizona (Fab 21, part of a stated ~$165B U.S. investment commitment), Japan, and Germany.

The company's scale has reached a point where TSM represents over 40% of the entire Taiwan TAIEX benchmark, which is both a measure of its strength and a transmission mechanism for company-specific shocks into country-level index risk. TSMC trades in the U.S. as an ADR on the NYSE.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 reported revenue exceeded US$120 billion with net income of approximately US$54.55 billion, implying a ~45% net margin that is exceptional for a capital-intensive manufacturer.

Q1 2026 was the fourth consecutive quarterly record. Revenue of NT$1.13 trillion (roughly US$35.6-35.9B) rose ~35-41% year over year, net profit increased 58%, and gross margin came in at 66.2%, beating guidance. The gross margin figure is the most important data point in the quarter: it demonstrates that pricing power at 2nm/3nm is more than offsetting the cost dilution from ramping higher-cost fabs in Arizona, Japan, and Germany. Advanced nodes are supply-constrained and priced accordingly, so the marginal wafer carries margin high enough to absorb overseas cost structure.

The earnings beat record is consistent: 4.4% (Q1 2026), 5.2% (Q1 2025), 8.0% (Q3 2025), 3.7% (Q2 2025) — four beats in four quarters averaging ~5.3%. This track record supports above-average confidence in forward consensus.

On capital allocation, the board approved a $51B expansion in May 2026, comprising ~$31.28B for advanced manufacturing plus up to $20B in capital injection into the wholly owned TSMC Arizona subsidiary. Arizona turned a $514M profit in its first full year of mass production, with Q1 2026 alone reportedly exceeding that figure. This materially weakens the long-standing bear thesis that overseas fabs would structurally crush margins.

Balance sheet detail could not be extracted directly from the 20-F (TSMC files as a foreign private issuer; the standard domestic-filer tooling returned null and direct EDGAR fetches were blocked). TSMC historically carries low leverage, a large net cash position, and very strong free cash flow even after heavy capex. The combination of ~45% net margins, 66% gross margins, and self-funded capex points to an extremely healthy financial profile. The key swing factor on future free cash flow is rising overseas capex intensity, which compresses FCF conversion in the near term even as the income statement stays robust. We score Financial Health 9.0, reflecting best-in-class margins and balance-sheet strength, with the capex intensity the only meaningful qualifier.

## Growth Analysis

Management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to "30%+" and lifted capex to the high end of original expectations, citing a "multiyear AI megatrend." Consensus estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-year EPS has risen to $15.62 from $14.24 ninety days ago (+9.7%), and next-year EPS has risen to $19.53 from $17.91 (+9.0%). The 30-day revisions are smaller (+1.1% current year, +1.2% next year), indicating the bulk of the upward revision came earlier and is now stabilizing rather than accelerating.

The +9-10% 90-day revision pace combined with 30%+ guided revenue growth is a genuine positive signal, but it sits below the +30% revision velocity that marks a true earnings acceleration. TSMC is a high-quality compounder with rising estimates, not a trough-earnings recovery story, so the estimate-revision dynamic supports the thesis without being the dominant driver.

The one data point that warrants monitoring is the monthly revenue cadence. April 2026 sales rose +17.5% YoY to NT$410.7B, the slowest monthly growth since October. This is the first quantitative hint that the AI-driven super-cycle growth rate is moderating off record bases. It is still strong growth in absolute terms, and a single month against an extreme comparison base is not a trend, but it is the kind of marginal deceleration that, if it continues, would compress the multiple the market is willing to pay.

The durability of the growth is supported by the breadth of the AI-infrastructure theme rather than a single customer. Multiple hyperscalers are guiding to record capex, Nvidia reportedly plans ~$150B/year of Taiwan spending as the Vera Rubin ramp strains supply, and TSMC's own May symposium raised its global semiconductor market forecast to $1.5T by 2030 from a prior $1T. The demand confirmation is broad-based, which raises the durability of the growth thesis relative to a single-company story. We score Growth Prospects 8.0, down modestly from prior reports to reflect the monthly deceleration signal against an otherwise intact super-cycle.

## Valuation Assessment

At the current ADR price of roughly $415-427 against forward FY26 EPS of $15.62, TSM trades at approximately 27x forward earnings. On next-year (FY27) consensus EPS of $19.53, the forward multiple drops to ~21x. For a business compounding revenue at 30%+ with ~45% net margins and a widening process-technology lead, a PEG of well under 1.0 on the near-term growth rate makes the valuation reasonable rather than stretched.

The reported trailing P/E of ~36x is misleading because it captures a period of lower earnings than the forward trajectory implies; the forward and growth-adjusted metrics are the relevant anchors. The stock is not at a premium multiple in the way a 50x+ software name would be, so the estimate-revision signal retains some predictive value — analysts have revised up 9-10% in 90 days and have a documented pattern of lagging actual delivery, given four straight beats.

Analyst price targets bracket a mean of $467.84 and a median of $472.50, with a high of $600 and a low of $354. The current price sits roughly 9-12% below the mean target. Given that estimates are rising and the company keeps beating, the consensus target is more likely a floor than a ceiling on a 12-month view, though the Taiwan tail risk is the reason the low target sits ~17% below spot.

The valuation does not offer the deep asymmetric mispricing that would justify a top-decile valuation score. The market understands TSMC's quality and growth, and the multiple reflects it. The multiple also reflects, and arguably over-discounts, the geopolitical tail. We score Valuation 6.0 — full but fair, with the upside coming from earnings delivery and consensus catch-up rather than from a re-rating off a depressed multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's near-term competitive position is intact and arguably strengthening. The scale gap versus competitors is stark: Intel Foundry generated only $174M from external customers in Q1 2026 against TSMC's total revenue. Intel still trails on yields and customer trust despite political tailwinds for U.S. fabrication and a large stock run. Samsung Foundry is pursuing creative bundling, including a rumored memory-for-foundry pitch to MediaTek that ties priority HBM/DRAM allocation to Samsung 2nm orders, but it has not closed the yield gap at the leading edge.

The competitive counter-current is the more important medium-term consideration. TSMC's scarcity-driven pricing power is the direct cause of its largest customers seeking a second source. Customers facing 80-90% higher spot pricing on advanced nodes — Google and others reportedly left on 3nm — are why Apple is in early-stage talks with Intel and Samsung for some M-series/A-series production, and why Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom increasingly want a second source ahead of the next supply crunch. Reporting suggests Samsung and Intel are "suddenly winning AI-chip business once reserved for TSMC."

This dynamic pulled the Competitive Position score down from 9.5 to 8.0 across prior reports, and we hold it at 7.5 here. TSMC is a market leader whose customers are actively funding alternatives to reduce single-source dependency. That is a slow erosion of an extraordinary moat, not an imminent one. The yields and trust that built the lead are not replicable on a 12-month horizon, but the strategic intent of the customer base is a genuine multi-year headwind that did not exist two years ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical / Taiwan-China (the defining risk).** A May 2026 U.S.-China summit reignited debate over Taiwan's unresolved status, with at least one outlet noting sentiment shifting on whether the diplomatic thaw makes TSM more vulnerable. This is an existential tail risk no other foundry shares, and it is unhedgeable at the company level. It is the single largest reason to cap position sizing and the reason the valuation multiple stays below where the fundamentals alone would support it.

**Customer diversification.** Apple, Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom all have strategic intent to reduce single-source dependency. This is a multi-year structural risk, not a near-term earnings risk.

**U.S. tariff / trade policy.** Potential Section 232 semiconductor tariffs loom, though Taiwan officials reported "no timetable" and "preferential terms" agreed under a January trade deal as of late May 2026. The $165B U.S. investment commitment functions as political insurance. The worst tariff scenario may be partially negotiated away.

**Margin dilution from overseas expansion.** Arizona, Japan, and Germany carry higher costs than Taiwan. Q1's gross margin shows this is being absorbed via pricing for now, and the Arizona profitability surprise weakens the bear case, but sustained scale-up remains a headwind to watch.

**AI demand concentration / cyclicality.** Growth is heavily levered to AI data center. The April monthly print is the first quantitative hint of normalization. Any hyperscaler capex pullback would hit TSMC's most profitable node mix first. TSMC's diversification across the entire leading-edge silicon complex insulates it from any single customer's cut, which makes it a cleaner expression of the theme than a single-ASIC supplier.

We score Risk Factors 5.0. The geopolitical tail dominates and is not priced away; it is the binary that defines the name.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 56.0% sits well above the 36.1% one-year historical volatility (a 19.9-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (56.0% near-term vs 48.0% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress. The put/call skew is roughly balanced at -1.6% on the front week, becoming more put-heavy at longer tenors (-4.7% at six months), which reflects structural hedging of the Taiwan tail rather than acute directional bearishness. The put/call open-interest ratio rose to 1.14 from 1.03 as the price fell ~3.8%, and a single block of 1,954 June-18 $418 calls (18.6x volume/OI) suggests some positioning for a near-term bounce. The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty and longer-dated tail hedging, not a fundamental breakdown. The rich near-term IV makes this a poor moment to buy short-dated upside but does not contradict the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$540-600).** AI-infrastructure demand stays broad and durable through 2027, 2nm and the subsequent A16 node command premium pricing, gross margins hold near 65%+ as Arizona scales profitably, and consensus catches up to the company's repeated beats. FY27 EPS reaches the high teens to ~$20+, and a ~28-30x multiple on rising earnings plus the diminished tariff overhang drives the ADR toward $540-600 (the analyst high). This requires the Taiwan tail to stay dormant and the customer-diversification effort to remain years from materially denting TSMC's leading-edge share.

**Bear Case (~$280-300, roughly -30%).** A genuine AI capex normalization (the April monthly deceleration extends into a trend), combined with accelerated second-sourcing wins for Samsung/Intel and a tariff or geopolitical shock, compresses both the multiple and the growth rate. Forward EPS estimates roll over, the multiple de-rates toward ~18-19x on flat-to-down estimates, and the ADR falls toward $280-300. A severe Taiwan-China escalation is a separate, lower-probability scenario with far larger downside that no fundamental model captures.

**Base Case (~$470).** Revenue grows ~30% in FY26 and decelerates to ~20% in FY27, margins hold near current levels, FY27 EPS reaches ~$19.50, and the stock trades at ~24x forward earnings as consensus targets drift up toward the current mean. This places the ADR near $470, roughly 10-13% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $470.** Derived from ~24x forward FY27 EPS of ~$19.53. This sits between the bear case ($280-300) and bull case ($540-600) and aligns with the current consensus mean ($467.84) and median ($472.50).

**Bull case target: $540-600.** ~28-30x on rising FY27 earnings with diminished tariff overhang.

**Bear case target: $280-300**, approximately -30% from the current price, on AI normalization plus competitive share loss plus a multiple de-rate.

**Timeframe: 12 months.** Key catalysts are the next two quarterly prints (the decisive test of whether the April monthly deceleration is signal or noise), the resolution of Section 232 tariff timing, and continued monthly revenue cadence data.

**Upside/downside from current (~$415-427):** roughly +10-13% to base case, +27-44% to bull case, against approximately -30% to the fundamental bear case (with a separate, lower-probability geopolitical tail of materially larger magnitude).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Monthly revenue growth decelerating below +10% YoY for two consecutive months, signaling the super-cycle is normalizing faster than guided.
2. A confirmed, material second-source win — Apple or Nvidia moving a leading-edge product to Samsung or Intel for volume production, not a trial.
3. Gross margin falling below ~60% on a quarterly basis, indicating overseas dilution is no longer being offset by pricing, or a guidance cut to FY26 revenue growth below ~25%.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the highest-quality, cleanest expression of the AI-infrastructure buildout available in public markets: an irreplaceable leading-edge manufacturer with ~45% net margins, 66% gross margins, fully pre-sold 2nm capacity, and consensus estimates that keep rising as the company keeps beating. At ~27x forward FY26 and ~21x forward FY27 earnings against 30%+ revenue growth, the valuation is full but fair, and analyst targets sitting 9-12% above spot are more likely floors than ceilings given the revision and beat record.

The two reasons this is a measured BUY at 2-3% sizing rather than a maximum-conviction position are the unhedgeable Taiwan geopolitical tail and the slow-burn customer-diversification dynamic that TSMC's own scarcity pricing is accelerating. Neither is a near-term earnings risk, but both cap the multiple and the appropriate position size. The April monthly revenue deceleration is the single data point worth watching most closely over the next two quarters. We recommend BUY, sized for the tail risk, with the next two earnings prints as the decisive checkpoints.
