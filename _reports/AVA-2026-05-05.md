## Executive Summary

Avista Corporation is a small-cap regulated utility ($3.4B market cap) serving approximately 417,000 customers across eastern Washington, northern Idaho, and parts of Oregon, with a subsidiary in Juneau, Alaska. The stock trades at $40.56, yielding 4.8% and carrying a forward P/E of ~15.5x on 2026 utility EPS guidance midpoint of $2.62. This represents a meaningful discount to multi-utility peers trading at a median 20.4x.

The discount is partially justified. Avista faces a structural power supply cost gap ($78M excess in 2025, expected to persist in 2026), mixed earnings execution (2 beats, 2 misses in recent quarters), declining estimate revisions (-6.3% on current year over 90 days), and continuous equity dilution (~11% share count growth over three years). The company's January 2026 multi-year rate plan requesting a 13.9% first-year electric rate increase will be the defining catalyst — approval unlocks earnings growth acceleration and potential multiple re-rating; significant reduction would validate the market's skepticism.

For income-oriented investors, the 4.8% yield with 3% expected dividend growth, investment-grade credit, and no near-term refinancing wall provides reasonable total return. For growth-oriented investors, the thesis depends on regulatory outcomes that won't resolve until late 2026 or early 2027.

## Company Overview

Avista operates as a regulated electric and natural gas utility through two segments:

**Avista Utilities** ($201M net income in 2025): Electric and gas distribution in the Pacific Northwest. Generation portfolio includes hydro, natural gas, wind, and biomass. The company exited coal generation with the Colstrip transfer to NorthWestern Energy effective January 1, 2026.

**AEL&P** ($6M net income): Hydroelectric utility serving Juneau, Alaska. Not interconnected to other grids.

**Other** (-$14M net loss): Venture capital/clean technology investments that have been a drag on earnings, particularly after policy shifts reduced clean-tech valuations in 2025.

The company operates under cost-of-service regulation across four jurisdictions: Washington (WUTC), Idaho (IPUC), Oregon (OPUC), and Alaska (RCA), with approved ROEs ranging from 9.4% to 11.45%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Revenue ($M) | $1,710 | $1,752 | $1,938 | $1,964 |
| Net Income ($M) | $155 | $171 | $180 | $193 |
| EPS (diluted) | $2.12 | $2.24 | $2.29 | $2.38 |

Revenue CAGR: ~4.7%. Net income CAGR: ~7.5%. EPS growth is slower (~3.9%) due to share count expansion from 73M to 81M over three years. Q1 2026 delivered $1.11 EPS versus $0.98 in Q1 2025 (+13%), and management confirmed full-year guidance of $2.52-$2.72 non-GAAP utility EPS.

**Capital Structure:**

- Total debt: $3.3B; equity: $2.7B; D/E: 2.09x
- Debt-to-total-capital: 54.9% (covenant limit: 65%)
- Weighted average long-term debt rate: 4.43%
- No significant maturities until 2028-2030 ($60M); $2.7B maturing post-2030
- Credit ratings: BBB/Baa2 (investment grade)
- Available liquidity: $146M

The leverage is typical for regulated utilities and the maturity profile is favorable in the current rising-rate environment. However, the 2026 financing plan requires $230M new long-term debt plus $90M common equity issuance, continuing the dilution trend.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow declined from $534M (2024) to $469M (2025), primarily due to a $158M increase in deferred power supply costs. Free cash flow was -$101M after $570M in capital expenditures. The negative FCF is structural given the capital intensity of the business — recovery depends entirely on regulatory approvals allowing these investments into rate base.

**Power Supply Cost Gap:**

Actual net power supply costs exceeded base retail rates by $78M in 2025. Under the Energy Recovery Mechanism (ERM), Avista absorbs approximately 10% of excess costs once cumulative deferrals exceed $30M. This produced a $14M pre-tax ERM expense in 2025 ($0.10/share impact expected in 2026). The WUTC rejected Avista's request to modify the ERM calculation in its 2024 rate case, making the 2026 MYRP filing the next opportunity to address this structural issue.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**

1. **Rate base expansion:** 5-year base capital CAGR of 5% ($585M expected in 2026). Incremental capex of up to $350M possible if a large load customer (likely data center) materializes.
2. **Multi-year rate plan:** If approved, locks in revenue growth through 2030.
3. **Clean energy transition:** 100 MW battery storage (2028), 200 MW wind PPA (2029), demand response programs.
4. **Load growth:** Regional electrification mandates and potential large industrial/tech customer.

**Earnings Growth Trajectory:**

Management targets 4-6% long-term EPS growth. However, estimate revisions are currently negative: full-year 2026 estimates have declined 6.3% over 90 days from $2.76 to $2.59. The current quarter estimate dropped 11.9% from $1.16 to $1.03. This negative revision momentum reflects the market adjusting downward for the large customer loss ($9M net income impact) and persistent ERM headwinds.

**Headwinds to Growth:**

- Large industrial customer departing in April 2026 (-$9M net income)
- Ongoing equity dilution (~3% annually)
- Oregon HB 3179 restricting rate increase frequency and magnitude
- Clean technology investment losses (-$14M in 2025)

The net growth profile is modest: 4-6% utility EPS growth, partially offset by 3% dividend growth target (below earnings growth to reduce payout ratio from current levels above the 60-70% target).

AVA trades at a 17-24% discount to multi-utility peers on P/E, with a 120bps yield premium. The discount reflects: (1) power supply cost deferral-driven earnings volatility, (2) 5-year TSR underperformance versus peers (121% vs. 145%), (3) mixed quarterly execution record, and (4) higher-than-peer dilution.

The median analyst target of $41.00 implies minimal upside (~1%) from the current $40.56. The high target of $50.00 (~23% upside) likely reflects full MYRP approval plus large customer load addition. The low target of $37.00 (~9% downside) reflects regulatory risk.

At 15.5x forward utility EPS, the valuation is not expensive for a utility with investment-grade credit and a 4.8% yield, but the discount exists for identifiable reasons rather than market error. The stock would need the MYRP to be approved at or near requested levels to justify re-rating toward peer multiples. Applying 18x to the $2.62 midpoint guidance yields $47; applying 16x to $2.52 (low end) yields $40. The current price essentially prices in low-end execution.

## Competitive Landscape

Avista operates as a natural monopoly within its service territory. Competitive dynamics are mediated through the regulatory process rather than direct market competition.

**Favorable:** Regulated monopoly position ensures revenue stability. Pacific Northwest load growth from electrification mandates provides secular tailwind. Potential large customer load addition suggests the service territory is attracting industrial/tech demand.

**Unfavorable:** Multi-jurisdictional complexity creates execution risk and regulatory lag. Washington's building electrification mandates threaten the natural gas distribution business long-term. The company's smaller scale (vs. $10-13B peers like PNW and OGE) provides less regulatory influence and lower operating leverage. Five-year relative TSR underperformance of 24 percentage points versus the peer group signals below-average operational execution.

**Colstrip Exit:** The transfer of coal assets was strategically necessary but created short-term friction ($9M customer refund ordered by WUTC, ongoing environmental remediation liability of $14M in surety bonds). The company is now cleaner from an ESG perspective, which could reduce cost of capital over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Power Supply Cost Volatility (High):** The $78M gap is structural in a tightening Pacific Northwest capacity market. If the MYRP doesn't fix the baseline calculation, Avista will continue absorbing ~$10-14M annually in ERM losses. Regional resource adequacy concerns are worsening as thermal retirements outpace clean energy additions.

**Regulatory Risk (High):** The 13.9% first-year electric rate increase is unusually large. Political and regulatory pushback could reduce the authorized amount meaningfully. Oregon HB 3179 additionally constrains rate recovery in that jurisdiction. The WUTC has 11 months from January 2026 to decide.

**Equity Dilution (Moderate):** $90M annual common stock issuance on a $3.4B market cap equates to ~2.6% dilution. This is higher than average for utilities and will continue suppressing per-share growth relative to total earnings growth.

**Wildfire Risk (Moderate-Low):** Pacific Northwest fire risk is increasing. The company has regulatory mechanisms (wildfire balancing accounts) but a catastrophic event could produce uninsured losses. Insurance coverage adequacy is never fully transparent.

**Large Customer Concentration:** The departure of one industrial customer produces a $9M net income hit (~4.7% of 2025 utility net income). This demonstrates concentration risk — conversely, adding a large load customer would be highly accretive.

**Rate Environment:** With long-term rates elevated, Avista's $230M planned debt issuance in 2026 will carry higher coupons than its portfolio average of 4.43%. However, the maturity wall is well-managed (minimal maturities through 2030), limiting near-term refinancing pressure.

**Earnings Volatility:** The 2-for-4 quarterly beat/miss record includes a -44.5% miss in Q2 2025 and a -14.6% miss in Q4 2025, which erodes confidence in management's ability to deliver guidance consistently. The beats were also modest (+6.2% and +25% on a very small seasonal quarter).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($47-50):**
- MYRP approved at or near requested levels, fixing the power supply cost baseline and locking in multi-year revenue growth
- Large load customer (data center) materializes, adding up to $350M in incremental capital investment and accelerating rate base growth
- Stock re-rates toward peer median of 18-20x as earnings predictability improves
- Path: 18x × $2.72 (high guidance) = $49; 19x × $2.62 = $50

**Bear Case ($34-37):**
- MYRP significantly reduced by WUTC (50-60% of request approved)
- Power supply cost gap persists, producing continued ERM losses
- Clean technology investments continue to drag
- Rising rates increase financing costs on new debt issuances
- Stock de-rates to 14x on execution concerns: 14x × $2.52 = $35

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price falls to $36-37 (yield approaches 5.4%, valuation at ~14x forward), providing sufficient margin of safety relative to the regulatory uncertainty
- MYRP first-year approval at ≥80% of request with constructive language on ERM reform
- Large load customer commitment announced with approved rate recovery mechanism

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- MYRP approved at <50% of Year 1 request, signaling hostile regulatory environment
- Second consecutive large customer loss or industrial load departure
- Dividend cut or suspension (would indicate financing stress given negative FCF)
- Credit rating downgrade below investment grade

**Reassessment timeline:** Late 2026/early 2027 when MYRP decision arrives. Interim check at next earnings (~August 2026) for Q2 seasonal weakness patterns and updated power supply cost commentary.

## Conclusion

Avista is a fairly priced small-cap utility offering a 4.8% dividend yield with modest growth prospects. The valuation discount to peers reflects identifiable operational challenges rather than market inefficiency. The power supply cost gap, ongoing equity dilution, and mixed execution record justify a discount to the peer median P/E of 20.4x. The MYRP outcome will be the defining catalyst — until it resolves, the risk-reward is balanced. Income investors can collect a well-covered dividend while waiting; total-return investors should await either a lower entry price or regulatory clarity.

HOLD for income — collect the yield, reassess after MYRP decision.
