**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Live Holdings is a Liberty Media–style holding vehicle, not an operating company. Its value is overwhelmingly a single position: approximately 69.6 million shares of Live Nation Entertainment (LYV), about 30% of LYV, carried under the equity method at roughly $490M of book value but worth approximately $10.6 billion in market value as of March 31, 2026. The only consolidated operating business is Quint (QuintEvents), a small experiential-hospitality company generating about $382M in FY2025 revenue and running near breakeven at the OIBDA level. The reported GAAP figures (negative book equity of −$318M, a Q1 2026 net loss of −$294M, EPS of −$3.20, and a negative trailing P/E) are accounting artifacts of equity-method treatment and fair-value debt marks that move inversely to the value of the LYV stake. They do not describe the economic reality.

The economic reality is a company holding a $10.6B liquid asset against $1.15B principal of low-coupon (2.375%), long-dated (2053) debt and roughly $524M of cash. On a look-through basis, net asset value is approximately $113 per share using debt at principal (about $105 using debt at fair value). At a recent price near $91-96, LLYVA trades at an estimated 15-20% discount to look-through NAV. The investment case is a classic Liberty "stub" / discount-narrowing play. The Split-Off from Liberty Media, completed December 15, 2025, was designed to narrow the discount that applied to the prior tracking stock.

We rate the stock HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The 12-month thesis is a discount-narrowing catalyst (most plausibly a buyback authorization, the standard Liberty tool) combined with a resolution or limitation of the Live Nation antitrust overhang following the April 15, 2026 jury verdict. We stop short of BUY because two of the dominant variables are outside management's control: whether the discount actually narrows (Liberty discounts have persisted for years historically) and the severity of the antitrust remedy, where a forced Ticketmaster divestiture remains a tail risk to LLYVA's sole material asset. The embedded leverage on a single stock makes the downside non-trivial.

## Company Overview

LLYVA (Series A, one vote, Nasdaq-listed) is one of three share series of Liberty Live Holdings, Inc., a Nevada-incorporated entity split off from Liberty Media on December 15, 2025. The other series are LLYVB (10 votes, ~97.4% owned by John Malone, sparsely traded) and LLYVK (Series C, non-voting, Nasdaq). Total shares across series number roughly 91.9 million (25.6M Series A + 2.5M Series B + 63.9M Series C). The headline $2.3B "market cap" in standard data feeds reflects only the LLYVA float (25.6M × ~$91), not the full enterprise, which is closer to $8.4B in equity across all series.

The asset base consists of: (1) the ~30% Live Nation stake, equity-method accounted; (2) Quint, a wholly-owned consolidated business selling official ticket-inclusive hospitality packages for Formula 1, MotoGP, NBA and similar events; (3) corporate cash and small private-equity investments of ~$164M; offset by (4) corporate debt. Management is shared with the broader Liberty complex under a services agreement (~$3.3M/quarter reimbursable to Liberty Media).

## Financial Analysis

The consolidated GAAP statements require decomposition to be interpretable.

**Quint (the only consolidated operating business).** FY2025 revenue was ~$382M. Q1 2026 revenue rose to $63.6M from $47.1M (+35% YoY), driven by Formula 1 (+$25.7M on three events versus two) and MotoGP (+$3.2M on a new hospitality agreement), partially offset by NBA (−$12.0M on venue/team mix). Quint Adjusted OIBDA loss narrowed to −$2.65M in Q1'26 from −$6.71M in Q1'25. The business is seasonally weighted to Q2/Q4 and is roughly breakeven on a full-year basis. It is small and lumpy relative to a $10.6B securities holding.

**Holding-company "noise."** The Q1 2026 net loss was driven by two non-cash items. First, the share of LYV losses of −$122.6M, almost entirely because LYV booked a $450M antitrust litigation charge in the quarter. Second, realized/unrealized losses of −$229.6M, mostly a −$185.6M mark-to-market on the exchangeable debentures (which rose in value because LYV's stock rose, since the debt is exchangeable into LYV and carried at fair value) plus −$46M on the 2025 forward contracts. The largest reported "losses" largely reflect the underlying LYV stake gaining value. Reported earnings will swing with LYV's stock price in counter-intuitive directions, and should be looked through entirely.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Cash breaks down as Quint $99M and corporate $426M. Operating cash flow was modestly negative (−$8.6M in Q1'26), consistent with a holding company plus a seasonally back-weighted Quint. Debt is 2.375% Exchangeable Senior Debentures due 2053, carried at fair value of $1.82B. The $400M Live Nation margin loan is fully undrawn. There is no drawn variable-rate debt. In March 2026, the company closed a privately negotiated exchange of ~$1,116M of old debentures for new 2.375% debentures due 2053, pushing the first put/call from September 2028 to September 30, 2032 and making them cash-settle-only, which pushes the maturity wall comfortably distant.

The negative stockholders' equity is a pure artifact: the LYV stake sits at ~$490M of equity-method book value versus ~$10.6B of market value. Any screen flagging negative equity, low Altman Z-Score, or weak Piotroski F-Score is structurally meaningless for this entity.

**Look-through NAV.** Using debt at principal: LYV ~$10.6B + cash $0.52B + Quint (~$0.25–0.4B) + other equity $0.16B − debt $1.15B ≈ ~$10.4B, or ~$113/share. Using debt at fair value of $1.82B narrows this to ~$105/share. Against a recent price of ~$91-96, the discount is in the 15-20% range using principal debt, narrower using fair-value debt.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is not an operating-fundamentals question; it is a function of two variables. First, the trajectory of the underlying LYV business, which remains healthy: Q1'26 LYV revenue was $3.79B (+12% YoY), with Concerts +$292M, Ticketing +$70M, and Sponsorship +$43M. Underlying live-entertainment demand (805M+ fans across 55 countries in 2025) is robust; the Q1'26 LYV operating loss was driven almost entirely by the $450M legal charge, not by demand deterioration. Second, the closing of the NAV discount, which contributes "growth" to LLYVA shareholders independent of LYV's intrinsic value.

The earnings-surprise record is largely uninformative for this entity because reported EPS is dominated by non-cash marks. The four-quarter record (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss, with the Q2'25 +128% and Q1'25 +163% surprises both driven by mark-to-market swings) reflects the volatility of fair-value accounting, not operating delivery.

Quint's revenue growth and narrowing OIBDA loss are positive at the operating level, but the business faces a 2026 headwind: the Iran conflict led to the cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi F1 GPs in April 2026 and the postponement of MotoGP Qatar to November 2026, flagged as adverse to Quint's 2026 results. Quint is too small to move the consolidated thesis materially in either direction.

The growth thesis that justifies holding is concrete and 12-month-actionable: (a) a buyback authorization at a discount to NAV, the most reliable Liberty discount-narrowing lever, would be immediately accretive; and (b) a remedies-phase outcome or appellate process that caps LYV's downside would remove the overhang that has weighed on the stock since the April verdict.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing and forward P/E of −24.9x are meaningless given negative GAAP earnings driven by non-cash marks. The only relevant valuation framework is discount-to-NAV.

At ~$91-96 against a look-through NAV of ~$105-113/share, LLYVA carries a 15-20% discount (principal basis). For a single-asset vehicle holding a liquid, exchange-listed security, that discount is the entire valuation argument. It is wide enough to be interesting but well within the historical range of Liberty discounts, which have persisted for multiple years. The Split-Off was designed to narrow it, but management warns there is "no assurance" it will.

There is no peer-multiple framework that applies cleanly here; the closest comparison is to other Liberty tracking/stub structures, which have traded at discounts ranging from negligible to 30%+ over time. The asymmetry is moderately favorable: if the discount fully closes, the upside to NAV is ~15-20% before any move in LYV itself; if the discount widens back toward historical extremes, downside is comparable. The valuation score reflects a real but unexceptional mispricing with no forcing mechanism to close it within a defined window.

We do not treat the absence of published analyst targets as a constraint; the single data point provided (current price $91.18 as the "target") carries no information.

## Competitive Landscape

Because LLYVA is a leveraged proxy on Live Nation, the relevant competitive dynamics are LYV's. Live Nation is the dominant global live-entertainment platform across concerts, ticketing, and sponsorship. Its competitive position is simultaneously its greatest strength and its central legal vulnerability: Ticketmaster's market dominance is precisely what the DOJ, FTC, and state attorneys general are attacking. The moat is wide, but the regulatory attack on it is the swing factor for intrinsic value.

Quint competes in premium experiential hospitality, dependent on rightsholder relationships, notably F1 and MotoGP. Those relationships are Liberty-affiliated related parties through Malone's common ownership, giving privileged access while creating a related-party dependency and a governance consideration.

## Risk Assessment

**Live Nation antitrust/regulatory overhang (dominant risk).** The DOJ and states sued LYV in May 2024 seeking Ticketmaster divestiture. LYV settled with the DOJ and some states (term sheet public March 9, 2026: structural/injunctive relief plus a $280M settlement fund, with an additional $18.6M from six more states). But on April 15, 2026, a jury returned a verdict for the remaining "Litigating States" on all claims, with per-ticket damages not yet quantified. LYV intends to appeal. A separate FTC and seven-state suit (September 2025) alleges deceptive ticket pricing under the BOTS Act. Forced Ticketmaster divestiture remains a tail risk, and LLYVA is essentially 100% levered to LYV's value.

**NAV-discount persistence.** The thesis depends on the discount narrowing; Liberty discounts have historically persisted for years.

**Embedded single-stock leverage.** ~$1.15B of debt plus 2025 forward contracts; roughly 10.5M and 9.0M LYV shares pledged as collateral across instruments. A sharp LYV decline could pressure collateral and refinancing even though the margin loan carries no financial covenants.

**Uncapped tax-sharing indemnity to Liberty Media.** If the Split-Off (or the related Liberty SiriusXM split-off) is deemed taxable under §355(e) — e.g., via a >50% ownership change — Liberty Live must indemnify Liberty Media with no cap. This constrains M&A and change-of-control activity post-split, limiting one avenue of value unlocking.

**Investment Company Act risk.** If the LYV stake were deemed passive (loss of board seats / significant dilution), LLYVA could be forced to register as an investment company, which would be costly and restrictive.

**Quint-specific.** ~$25.6M accrued VAT/sales-tax compliance liability with possible further exposure; 2026 event cancellations/postponements tied to the Middle East conflict.

**Governance.** Malone controls >50% of votes via super-voting LLYVB; classified board; Nevada anti-takeover provisions; Emerging Growth Company status with reduced disclosure including no say-on-pay and limited executive compensation disclosure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 47.7% against one-year historical volatility of 30.3% — rich, with a 17.3-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (47.7% at one month versus 31.7% at six months). This signals the market is pricing imminent event risk, consistent with the open-ended remedies/appeal timeline on the Live Nation litigation. Put/call skew of −13.7% is call-heavy at the near tenor, which leans mildly constructive, though the six-month skew turns modestly negative (−5.4%). The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±8.7%. The options market expects a near-term catalyst with two-sided potential, and the rich near-term IV makes long-premium hedging expensive while favoring patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The discount to look-through NAV closes from ~15-20% toward zero, driven by a buyback authorization (the classic Liberty discount-narrowing tool) and/or a credible monetization path. LYV wins or materially limits the antitrust appeal, removing the overhang and allowing the $10.6B stake to re-rate. The 2.375% debt due 2053, recently extended, provides cheap, long-dated, low-risk financing. Quint inflects to positive OIBDA as F1/MotoGP volumes normalize. In a favorable scenario, NAV itself grows with LYV's +12% revenue trajectory, and LLYVA captures both NAV growth and discount compression. Bull-case value: ~$115-125/share (NAV at current LYV price with full discount closure, plus modest LYV appreciation).

**Bear Case.** Liberty discounts persist for years, as they historically have, and the gap to NAV stays wide or widens. The antitrust remedies phase produces severe relief, up to forced Ticketmaster divestiture, impairing LYV's intrinsic value; embedded leverage amplifies the drawdown at the LLYVA level. Malone control plus the uncapped tax indemnity handcuff value-unlocking M&A. Bear-case value: ~$70-75/share (wider discount on a lower LYV mark), roughly −20% to −25% from current levels and near the 52-week low of $71.06.

The two cases are roughly symmetric in magnitude, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY. The mispricing is real but unexceptional, and the dominant catalysts are externally determined.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment points are the next quarterly print (look-through NAV update and any capital-allocation announcement) and any material development in the Live Nation remedies/appeal process. A 12-month horizon is appropriate given the discount-closure thesis and the litigation timeline.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of a meaningful LLYVA/LLYVK buyback authorization executed at a discount to NAV (the single most reliable discount-narrowing signal in the Liberty playbook).
- A remedies-phase ruling or appellate development that caps LYV's antitrust exposure without structural divestiture, removing the dominant overhang.
- The discount to look-through NAV widening beyond ~25% on no fundamental deterioration in LYV, which would improve the asymmetry enough to warrant a higher-conviction call.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A remedies ruling ordering Ticketmaster divestiture, or damages quantification materially above the $450M reserve, impairing the underlying asset.
- A collateral/refinancing event on the pledged LYV shares or forward contracts that forces dilutive action.
- Confirmation that the post-split discount is structurally persistent (e.g., management explicitly deprioritizing buybacks while the discount remains wide for several quarters).

## Conclusion

LLYVA is a controlled-company, single-asset NAV play on Live Nation, trading at an estimated 15-20% discount to look-through NAV with a clean (low-coupon, long-dated, recently extended) balance sheet that GAAP makes look distressed. The standardized "distress" signals — negative equity, large net losses, negative P/E — are false positives produced by equity-method accounting and inverse-moving fair-value debt marks.

We rate the shares HOLD on a growth_hold basis. The 12-month thesis is specific: a buyback-driven discount narrowing and a limitation of the Live Nation antitrust overhang would deliver meaningful appreciation toward look-through NAV. We do not upgrade to BUY because the two governing variables — discount persistence and the antitrust remedy — are outside management's control, the embedded leverage cuts both ways, and the risk/reward is roughly symmetric around current levels. A buyback authorization or a favorable remedies development would warrant a re-rating to BUY. Berkshire Hathaway's modest January 2026 trimming of its legacy LLYVK position and the rich near-term implied volatility argue for patience rather than aggressive accumulation.
