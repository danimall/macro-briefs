## Executive Summary

Murphy USA operates 1,803 fuel and convenience stores predominantly adjacent to Walmart Supercenters. The business delivered an exceptional Q1 2026 — EPS of $7.28 crushed the $5.14 consensus by 42% — driven by extreme oil price volatility ($56-$105/bbl WTI range) that amplified the company's structural cost advantages in fuel procurement, RIN generation, and wholesale supply. This quarter catalyzed a massive re-rating: the stock moved from ~$514 pre-earnings to $576 currently (+12%), and from $380 at the February 2026 low (+51%).

The re-rating is directionally justified. Murphy USA possesses genuine embedded optionality to commodity volatility — the fuel supply/PS&W business swung from -$15.3M in Q1 2025 to +$39.0M in Q1 2026, and RIN revenue doubled. Combined with aggressive share repurchases (21% float reduction since 2022), EPS power in volatile environments is substantially higher than trailing figures suggest.

The challenge is valuation and sustainability. At $576, the stock trades at ~18x current-year consensus ($32.17), which itself embeds a 25% upward revision in 30 days. The peer median P/E is 12.2x. The premium is warranted given Murphy's superior ROIC and buyback-driven EPS growth, but it now prices in continued elevated fuel margins through 2026 — an assumption that is inherently commodity-dependent and could reverse rapidly if oil volatility subsides or if WTI stabilizes in a narrow band.

## Company Overview

Murphy USA operates a low-cost, high-volume fuel retail model with three brands: Murphy USA (near Walmart), Murphy Express (standalone), and QuickChek (food-focused, NJ/NY). The company sells approximately 4.8 billion gallons of fuel annually across 1,655 Murphy-branded and 148 QuickChek locations in 27 states.

The business model centers on: (1) strategic Walmart adjacency driving customer traffic without bearing proportional real estate cost, (2) a lean operating structure (1-2 employees per shift, 1,400-2,800 sq ft footprint), (3) supply chain advantages from pipeline positions, terminal ownership, and shipper status, and (4) aggressive capital return via buybacks ($4.1B since 2013 spin-off).

Revenue is declining in absolute terms ($23.4B FY2022 → $19.4B FY2025) primarily due to lower fuel prices, but EPS has remained remarkably stable at ~$24 due to the 21% share count reduction. The Q1 2026 blowout ($7.28 EPS) demonstrated that in volatile environments, earnings power can far exceed the trailing run-rate.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 revenue of $19.4B, operating income $719M, net income $471M. The business generates ~$1B in adjusted EBITDA annually in "normal" environments. Q1 2026's $277.9M adjusted EBITDA (+77% YoY) shows the upside in favorable conditions.

**Fuel Economics:** FY2025 retail fuel margin: 28.1 cpg. Q1 2026 total fuel contribution: 35.0 cpg (vs. 25.4 cpg in Q1 2025). The 9.6 cpg differential in fuel supply/RIN contribution alone adds ~$460M annualized gross profit when conditions are favorable.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt/equity of 6.58x is optically high but intentional — the company operates a leveraged return model. Leverage ratio of 1.87x (vs. 5.0x covenant) provides substantial headroom. The April 2025 refinancing extended maturities (new $600M term loan to 2032, revolver to 2030). ROE of 75.5% reflects the capital efficiency.

**Cash Flow & Capital Return:** FY2025 FCF of $374M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $320M (annualizing >$1B). Share repurchases: $652M in FY2025, $70.5M in Q1 2026. Remaining authorization: $221M + $2B new program through 2030. At the current price, the $2.2B authorization represents approximately 21% of the current market cap.

**Earnings Surprises:** 3 beats in 4 quarters. The Q1 2026 beat (42%) was transformational. Q3 2025 miss (-5%) occurred during a low-volatility environment, illustrating the cyclicality.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is structurally limited — fuel volumes are declining 1-3% annually on a same-store basis due to fuel efficiency improvements. Total growth depends on new store openings (45-55 NTI + up to 30 raze-and-rebuilds annually).

EPS growth is driven by three mechanisms: (1) share count reduction (5-6% annually at current buyback pace), (2) merchandise margin expansion (20.2% in FY2025, up from 19.1% in 2021), and (3) episodic fuel margin windfalls in volatile environments.

Current-year EPS consensus of $32.17 (revised up 27% in 30 days) implies full-year earnings roughly matching Q1's elevated run-rate. Next-year consensus at $29.22 suggests some normalization but still above FY2025's $24.10. The revision velocity is strong but partly reflects a single exceptional quarter being annualized.

The 45-55 store/year growth pipeline provides low-single-digit unit growth. Combined with buybacks, Murphy USA can compound EPS at 8-12% annually in "normal" environments, with significant upside optionality in volatile markets.

## Valuation Assessment

At $576, Murphy USA trades at:
- 19.9x trailing EPS ($24.10 — understated given Q1 2026 blowout)
- ~17.9x FY2026 consensus ($32.17)
- ~19.7x FY2027 consensus ($29.22)

The peer median P/E is 12.2x (PAG, AN, LAD). Murphy USA's premium is partially justified by: (1) superior ROIC (75% ROE vs. typical 15-25% for auto retailers), (2) aggressive buyback-driven EPS compounding, (3) embedded volatility optionality, and (4) better competitive positioning (no used car/new car cycle exposure).

However, the current multiple assumes the elevated earnings power persists. If FY2026 EPS normalizes closer to $26-28, the stock trades at 20-22x — stretched for a fuel retailer regardless of quality advantages. The market is pricing Murphy at 1.5x its peer group multiple on estimates that were revised up 27% in one month based on one quarter's results.

The analyst consensus target of $558 (mean) is below the current price of $576, suggesting the stock has already traded through the average target. The median target of $600 provides limited upside (4%). The high target of $655 implies ~14% upside but requires sustained elevated margins.

## Competitive Landscape

Murphy USA's competitive advantages are structural and durable:
- **Cost leadership in fuel procurement** — pipeline positions, terminal ownership, RIN generation
- **Walmart adjacency** — generates traffic that would cost billions to replicate
- **Fee-simple land ownership** (73% of sites) — eliminates landlord risk
- **Scale** — 1,800+ stores provide purchasing leverage (Core-Mark contract through 2031)

Competitive position is stable-to-improving. The fragmented industry (~150,000 stores) continues consolidating in favor of scaled operators. Murphy's low-cost model performs best during volatility and high-price environments — consistent with the current macro regime of elevated oil volatility and geopolitical disruptions.

Weakness: QuickChek (148 stores, declining from 158) faces competitive pressure in Northeast markets. The food & beverage strategy hasn't scaled across the Murphy network. Non-nicotine merchandise SSS are slightly declining.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel Margin Normalization (Primary Near-Term Risk):** Q1 2026's 35.0 cpg total fuel contribution vs. FY2025's 30.7 cpg average. If oil volatility subsides, H2 2026 margins could compress materially. Current consensus embeds continued elevated volatility.

**Nicotine Regulation:** Nicotine SSS margin grew 10.4% in Q1 2026. This category drives a disproportionate share of merchandise profitability. Regulatory action (flavor bans, tax increases) would hit Murphy USA harder than food-forward peers like Casey's.

**Leadership Transition:** New CEO (Mindy West) and CFO (Donald Smith Jr.) with restructuring charges suggesting organizational realignment. Q1 was market-driven rather than strategy-driven. Execution risk on 50+ stores/year pipeline under new team is elevated until demonstrated.

**Walmart Dependency:** Master lease and ECR agreements give Walmart significant control. Any deterioration in Walmart traffic directly impacts Murphy.

**Refinancing:** $300M senior notes maturing 2027 at below-investment-grade. Manageable given cash generation and revolver capacity, but elevated rate environment increases cost.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($620-$655, +8-14%):** Oil volatility persists through 2026 driven by geopolitical disruptions, maintaining elevated fuel margins (33-35 cpg total contribution). FY2026 EPS reaches or exceeds $32 consensus. Continued buybacks ($500-700M annually) reduce share count 4-5%. New store openings execute on plan. The market re-rates Murphy as a structural volatility beneficiary at 20-22x earnings. Nicotine growth continues.

**Bear Case ($430-$470, -18-25%):** Oil volatility subsides (diplomatic progress, geopolitical de-escalation). Fuel margins normalize toward 28-30 cpg. FY2026 EPS comes in at $26-28, well below the $32.17 consensus. Multiple compresses from 18x back toward 16-17x on lower earnings. Consumer weakness reduces fuel volumes. Nicotine regulatory action. Stock reverts toward pre-earnings range.

**Base Case ($540-$580, -6% to flat):** Moderate ongoing volatility supports Q2-Q3 fuel margins above FY2025 levels but below Q1 2026 peaks. FY2026 EPS of $28-30. Stock consolidates at 18-20x forward earnings. Buybacks provide floor. The stock has largely priced in the good news from Q1.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $470-$500 range (~15% decline from current) while FY2026 EPS estimates remain above $30 — would create a 15-17x forward P/E entry
2. Q2 2026 earnings demonstrate fuel margins remained elevated (total contribution >32 cpg), confirming Q1 was not one-off
3. Oil volatility increases further, extending the elevated-margin regime

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimates revised below $26 (20%+ downward revision from current $32.17) — would indicate fuel margin normalization
2. Stock rallies above $630 without corresponding estimate increases — would push forward P/E above 22x, clearly stretched for a fuel retailer
3. Significant Walmart traffic deterioration or nicotine regulatory action

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August) is the critical data point. This will reveal whether Q1's elevated fuel margins were sustained or anomalous.

## Conclusion

Murphy USA is a high-quality fuel retailer with structural competitive advantages, aggressive capital return, and genuine embedded optionality to commodity volatility. The Q1 2026 results demonstrated this optionality conclusively, and the current oil market environment (elevated prices, geopolitical disruption) is favorable for continued above-normal margins.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation is quantifiable: current-year EPS consensus of $32.17 requires H2 2026 fuel margins to remain elevated at levels that are historically unsustainable on a multi-quarter basis. If H2 reverts toward the FY2025 average, full-year EPS would be $26-28, putting the stock at 20-22x actual earnings — 60-80% above the peer median. At $470-$500, the asymmetry would favor buyers. At $576, the upside ($620-655, +8-14%) is roughly symmetric with the downside ($430-470, -18-25%), and the stock already trades above the analyst consensus mean target of $558.

The macro environment does support continued elevated margins as a central case. But the stock has already moved 51% from its February low to price this in. The right posture is to hold for shareholders who own it (the buyback-driven compounding story is intact), while waiting for either a pullback or a second quarter of confirmed elevated margins before adding.
