## Executive Summary

IQVIA's second-quarter 2026 report, released July 28, delivered the operating improvement bulls had been waiting for since the R&D Solutions slowdown of 2024–25. Revenue rose 8.7% to $4,368M, segment profit grew roughly 11%, R&D Solutions net new bookings hit a record $3.15B (+19%) for a 1.22x book-to-bill, and management raised FY2026 guidance across all three headline metrics. The shares gained 12.9% intraday and have now traveled from $154.50 on April 30 to $235.41, a 52% move that recaptured essentially all of the February–April derating.

The complication is that the improvement is visible in segment profit and adjusted metrics but not yet in GAAP earnings. Income from operations was $506M in Q2 2026 against $506M in Q2 2025, flat to the dollar, and GAAP diluted EPS was $1.53 versus $1.54. The reconciliation is fully explainable: general corporate and unallocated expense swung from roughly a $46M quarterly tailwind to a headwind as the 2024–25 corporate cost harvest reversed, restructuring nearly doubled to $63M, and depreciation rose $16M. But it means that two consecutive years of reported earnings growth have been produced by capital structure rather than by profit. FY2025 net income attributable fell 0.9% to $1,360M while diluted shares fell 5.4%, generating +4.7% diluted EPS. Gross margin excluding depreciation has now declined in both periods (34.9% to 33.3% in FY2025, and a further 66bp in H1 2026), with part of that pass-through mix (R&DS revenue grew 8.8% reported but 6.7% excluding reimbursed expenses) and the remainder genuine delivery-cost pressure.

At $235.41 the stock is 18.3x the $12.90 midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 16.3x the $14.44 FY2027 consensus, 13.1x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA on enterprise value, and roughly 29x trailing GAAP diluted earnings, in line with the 29.9x median of its Life Sciences Tools peer set. Adjusting only for purchase-accounting amortization while expensing stock compensation and the restructuring charge that has recurred in each of the last four years produces an economic FY2026 EPS near $10.20, or 23x. That is a fair multiple for a business guiding to 6.5% revenue growth (of which ~200bp is acquired), running 3.59x net leverage with 2.85x EBIT interest coverage, and facing a mechanical interest-cost step-up as sub-3% euro notes mature. Base case $255 offers ~8% upside against ~24% downside to $180, an approximately fair risk-adjusted return. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

IQVIA is the product of the 2016 Quintiles–IMS Health merger, and the two heritages still define it: the largest contract research organization by revenue, bolted to the incumbent owner of pharmaceutical prescription and sales data plus commercial analytics. FY2025 revenue was $16,310M across roughly 94,000 employees in more than 100 countries.

Effective January 1, 2026 the company collapsed three reportable segments into two, with prior periods recast:

- **Commercial Solutions** (former Technology & Analytics Solutions plus Contract Sales & Medical Solutions, less Real-World Late Phase moved out): $1,793M of Q2 2026 revenue, +8.6% (+8.4% constant currency). Predominantly fixed-price, generally non-cancelable one- to three-year information subscriptions recognized ratably, plus technology services, SaaS and consulting. The higher-margin, more annuity-like half; the legacy T&AS segment ran a 24.1% margin in FY2025.
- **R&D Solutions** (which absorbed Real-World Late Phase): $2,575M of Q2 2026 revenue, +8.8% reported and +6.7% excluding reimbursed expenses. Clinical trial contracts treated as a single performance obligation and recognized over time using a cost-based input method. Most contracts are cancelable on 30 to 90 days' notice. FY2025 segment margin 21.1%.

Geographically, FY2025 revenue split Americas $7,745M (47.5%), Europe & Africa $5,185M (31.8%), Asia-Pacific $3,380M (20.7%), with ~42% of revenue attributable to services performed in the United States. No individual customer reached 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025, which establishes only that the largest customer is below the disclosure threshold; top-5 and top-10 share are not disclosed.

PwC (auditor since 2002) designated a single critical audit matter in the FY2025 audit: estimating costs to complete for clinical research services, covering the $8,896M of R&DS revenue. That is the correct focus for accounting attention, since revenue recognition on that half of the business depends on management's estimate of total contract cost.

### Cash generation

FY2025 operating cash flow was $2,654M against $1,361M of net income (1.95x), bridged mainly by $1,144M of depreciation and amortization and $247M of SBC, with only ~$125M from working capital. Cash flow is not being manufactured by receivable release. Free cash flow (OCF less $603M capex) was $2,051M, a 12.6% margin, a 5.3% yield on today's equity value and 3.9% on enterprise value. Two disclosure items warrant note without implying impropriety: approximately $704M of invoices were factored non-recourse in 2025 (versus $678M in 2024, flat scale rather than a growing tailwind), and $1,565M of receivables and unbilled services were pledged to the $550M receivables facility.

H1 2026 OCF was $1,176M (+16.3%) with FCF of $851M (+18.5%). Cash generation is back-half weighted (FY2025 H2 was 62% of the year), so H1 should not be doubled. As a sensitivity rather than a forecast, repeating the FY2025 H2/H1 ratio would produce ~$3.09B of FY2026 OCF and ~$2.42B of FCF. Offsets that could pull it lower: a rising tax rate, rising interest, and a +$239M H1 receivable/unearned inflow that may not repeat.

### Balance sheet

At June 30, 2026: cash $1,909M, total debt $15,999M, net debt $14,090M, net leverage 3.59x trailing adjusted EBITDA (company-stated, implying ~$3,924M of TTM adjusted EBITDA). Equity attributable to shareholders $6,171M against goodwill of $16,604M and other intangibles of $4,749M, together 71% of the $29,881M asset base.

Derived EBIT interest coverage is ~2.85x (TTM EBIT ≈ $2,200M / TTM interest ≈ $771M); adjusted EBITDA to interest is ~5.1x. The maturity ladder at December 31, 2025 was $1,840M (2026), $1,794M (2027), $2,574M (2028), $2,451M (2029), $3,275M (2030), $3,866M thereafter, against $1,195M of undrawn revolver capacity. Covenants include a maximum senior secured net leverage test and a minimum interest coverage test; the company was compliant and the debt agreements restrict dividends (IQVIA pays none).

The cost-of-debt path is a quantifiable, largely operating-independent headwind. Roughly $3,383M of euro notes carry coupons of 1.750%–2.875% and mature 2026–2029, while the June 2025 issue priced at 6.250%. Refinancing that block at ~6% would add ~$125M of pretax interest, or about $0.63 per share after tax on 167M shares, roughly 5% of guided adjusted EPS, phasing in through 2029. Cross-currency swaps have cut effective rates on hedged tranches to ~4.06–4.11%, and secured refinancing could price tighter, so treat the magnitude as a sensitivity. The direction is not in doubt: interest expense went $670M → $729M → $389M in H1 2026 alone versus $347M.

A classic Altman Z recomputed on the June 2026 balance sheet lands at ~2.07 (grey zone), and the private-firm variant at ~1.07. The gap is entirely the equity term, which tells you the model is doing little discriminating work here. Negative working capital of $2,589M is largely $2,281M of unearned income (customer prepayments that function as float in a services business) plus current maturities against an undrawn revolver. The honest characterization is structurally leveraged with thin tangible equity and adequate coverage. The practical consequence matters more than the score: if ratings or covenant pressure ever forced deleveraging over repurchases, the primary driver of recent EPS growth would disappear.

**Financial Health: 5.7.** Genuinely strong and clean cash conversion, offset by 3.59x leverage, 2.85x EBIT coverage, a $3.6B two-year maturity stack, a rising cost of debt, and $16.6B of goodwill on $6.2B of book equity.

## Growth Analysis

The forward evidence is better than the trailing evidence, and that is the crux of the bull case.

**Bookings.** Q2 net new bookings of $3.15B were up 19%, a 1.22x book-to-bill, with trailing-twelve-month bookings of $11.3B (+13%). Management characterized the environment as "strengthening" and pointed to "favorable forward-looking indicators across both segments" for the balance of 2026 and into 2027.

**Backlog, and the three metrics that must not be conflated.** R&DS contracted backlog was $34.2B at June 30, 2026, of which ~$9.2B is expected to convert to revenue in the next twelve months, up 7.5%. Separately, ASC 606 remaining performance obligations were $34.2B at December 31, 2025 with ~30% expected within a year, a different construct that excludes wholly unperformed cancelable contracts. Backlog is also not assured: most R&DS contracts terminate on 30 to 90 days' notice with payment for work performed plus close-out costs.

The most decision-useful figure is the $9.2B twelve-month conversion, because it is the piece management attaches a time frame to. Its 7.5% growth is slower than bookings (+19%) and slower than current revenue (+8.7%), which tempers the record-backlog headline. Bookings convert to revenue with a lag and lumpiness; one quarter of 19% growth can reflect a handful of large awards.

**Guidance.** FY2026 revenue $17,275–17,475M, adjusted EBITDA $4,000–4,050M, adjusted diluted EPS $12.80–13.00. The revenue-growth midpoint moved from 5.8% to 6.5%: roughly +100bp organic, +50bp M&A (total M&A contribution now ~200bp), offset by −80bp of FX as the tailwind was cut to ~20bp at July 27 rates. Stripping acquired contribution leaves organic growth around 4.5%.

The guide embeds a second-half margin step-up. Implied H2 adjusted EBITDA of ~$2,099M on ~$8,856M of revenue is a 23.7% margin against 22.61% in H1. That is a 110bp sequential-half improvement and constitutes the main execution risk in the numbers.

**Consensus and revisions.** Current-year consensus is $12.96 and next-year $14.44, revised +1.2% and +1.8% respectively over 90 days, with current-quarter estimates up 2.9%. Directionally positive and consistent with the guidance raise, but an order of magnitude below the revision velocity that would signal a systematic underestimate. The four-quarter surprise record is modest: +2.9%, +1.9%, −0.3%, −0.0%. This is a company that lands close to consensus, not one that serially blows through it.

**Where the clinical growth is coming from.** R&DS revenue by geography: Americas $4,157M (2023) → $3,894M (2024) → $4,105M (2025), still 1.3% below 2023; Asia-Pacific $2,135M → $2,518M, up 17.9% and now 28% of R&DS. Two years of clinical growth have been delivered disproportionately outside the United States. Whether that reflects delivery-cost arbitrage or indication-specific patient availability, the consequence is the same and is not addressed in the MD&A: rising exposure to Asia-Pacific regulatory regimes, cross-border health-data transfer rules, currency, and U.S. legislative sentiment about clinical work performed in China.

**Growth Prospects: 5.8.** A real recovery from a genuine trough, evidenced in segment profit dollars rather than only in adjusted metrics, but organic growth of ~4.5%, twelve-month backlog conversion of +7.5%, flat GAAP operating income, and 3.4 points of the 12.1% adjusted EPS growth coming from share count keep this a mid-single-digit compounder with a non-GAAP overlay.

Note two data conflicts. The supplied profile lists both trailing and forward P/E at 28.78, which cannot both be right for a company guiding to 12% adjusted EPS growth; the trailing GAAP figure is the reconcilable one. Separately, third-party sources have circulated a 16.33x forward P/E and 14.94x EV/EBITDA for IQV; neither reconciles to $235.41 with FY2026 guidance, though 16.3x matches the FY2027 consensus. Multiples should be quoted with their basis attached.

**The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is unusually wide and determines the conclusion.** In H1 2026 adjusted net income was $1,019M against $530M of GAAP net income attributable, so 48% of adjusted earnings are add-backs. The pretax composition: purchase-accounting amortization $273M (44%), stock-based compensation $160M (26%), restructuring and related $120M (19%), acquisition-related $21M, other $41M, debt extinguishment $3M.

These deserve different treatment. Purchase-accounting amortization is a defensible non-cash exclusion for a serial acquirer, since revenue from acquired intangibles stays in the numerator. Stock compensation is non-cash but genuinely dilutive and growing 21% year over year. Restructuring is cash and has recurred every year: $84M (2023), $67M (2024), $105M (2025), $114M in H1 2026 alone, roughly $370M cumulative, with the 10-K stating actions "are expected to continue into 2026." Calling a charge that appears four consecutive years non-recurring is a stretch, even allowing that the 2026 step-up has an identifiable cause in the January segment consolidation.

A middle path adds back only acquired-intangible amortization (~$546M annualized pretax, ~$2.75/share after tax) and expenses the rest. That produces FY2026 economic EPS of roughly $10.20 ($12.90 less ~$1.60 of SBC and ~$1.11 of restructuring, after tax), or **23.1x** at the current price. On FY2027, with restructuring normalizing, economic EPS is near $11.90, or **19.8x**.

**Peer context.** Median Life Sciences Tools P/E is 29.9x (WAT 52.2, A 26.0, ILMN 30.1, MTD 26.8, MEDP 29.9), which are broadly GAAP-basis trailing figures. On the like-for-like GAAP basis IQV's ~29x sits at the median, not below it. On adjusted earnings IQV screens cheaper than Medpace at 29.9x, but Medpace is unlevered, asset-light, and higher-growth, so the discount is doing real work rather than signalling mispricing. The peer set is also heterogeneous enough (26x to 52x) that median comparison carries limited weight.

**The catalyst question.** For a high valuation score I would need a specific reason the market is wrong. The candidates are: (a) the 1.22x book-to-bill converts to 8%+ organic revenue in 2027, which would justify multiple expansion toward 20x adjusted; (b) H2 margin delivery at 23.7% proves the reorganization is producing structural, not cosmetic, savings; (c) continued buyback at ~$1.9B annualized shrinks the count 4-5% a year. Against that: the same forward-looking indicators are already in a raised guide and in the 19-of-21 buy-rated consensus, and the stock has re-rated 52% off the April low. Twelve-month conversion growth of 7.5% is the number that argues the 2027 acceleration will be more modest than the bookings headline implies.

**Valuation: 5.3.** Neither clearly cheap nor stretched. 18.3x guided adjusted looks inviting until it is restated for the 48% add-back content, at which point 23x on 6.5% revenue growth with 3.6x leverage is approximately fair. The apparent cheapness on adjusted metrics is the kind that has historically failed to convert without an independent earnings catalyst, and prior appreciation, while irrelevant as a valuation input, has removed the margin of safety that existed at $160.

## Competitive Landscape

**Scale is the asset.** $34.2B of contracted backlog, ~94,000 employees, a global site and investigator network, and a de-identified patient and prescription data estate licensed from thousands of suppliers. On the clinical side, that permits bidding large multi-year, multi-country programs mid-caps cannot staff. On the commercial side, the data estate is expensive to replicate and embedded in customer workflows.

The trajectory is mixed, and the more important half is the more threatened one. In R&D Solutions, IQVIA competes with Thermo Fisher's PPD, ICON, Fortrea, Medpace, Parexel and sponsor in-house development groups. That competition is cyclical and pricing-driven; IQVIA's segment profit fell 3.9% in FY2025 and grew 11.2% in Q2 2026, which reads as sector cycle more than share dynamic. The unresolved question is whether the 1.22x book-to-bill reflects industry-wide recovery or company-specific share gain; peer Q2 book-to-bill data was not obtainable, and this is the single most important open item for 2027 revenue durability. A 1.22x accompanied by broad recovery is a durable input; the same number driven by one or two very large awards is not.

In Commercial Solutions the competitive set is more dynamic: Veeva Systems in commercial software and increasingly data, plus Definitive Healthcare, Clarivate, Komodo Health, Truveta, sponsor in-house analytics teams, and, per IQVIA's own risk factors, third-party and open-source foundation models. The FY2025 10-K now states that generative, agentic and foundation models "increasingly accessible through third-party or open-source platforms" may "reduce the perceived differentiation of certain of our information, analytics and insight-based offerings." The company is flagging substitution risk on the higher-margin half of its business. The defensible asset is the underlying licensed data estate and its privacy plumbing, not the analytical layer above it. If the analytical layer commoditizes, pricing power migrates to raw data ownership, and IQVIA licenses rather than owns its raw source data, a dependency the forward-looking-statement list places first.

The counter-evidence so far runs the other way: Commercial Solutions grew 8.6% in Q2 with management citing increased adoption of its AI solutions. The risk language may be conservative updating rather than an observed demand change. This matches the pattern across application-layer software over the past eighteen months, with infrastructure and proprietary data assets holding up while analytical and workflow layers compress, and the market will reprice the multiple on evidence, not on disclosure.

**Competitive Position: 5.7.** Improving from a cyclical trough on bookings and segment profit, with unresolved structural questions on the Commercial half and R&DS Americas revenue still below 2023 levels.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Backlog cancelability against decelerating conversion.** Most R&DS contracts terminate on 30–90 days' notice. Bookings +19% and book-to-bill 1.22x sit alongside twelve-month backlog conversion growth of +7.5%. If sponsors defer program starts as they did in 2024–25, bookings strength does not arrive on schedule.

**2. Required 2026 goodwill impairment test.** Note 8 states the segment reallocation "necessitates the reallocation of goodwill between the two reportable segments and the performance of a goodwill impairment test, which the Company will perform in 2026." FY2025 was assessed qualitatively only. Goodwill is $16.6B against $6.2B of book equity, and the shares traded near $160 in April. No impairments occurred in 2023–2025 and, with enterprise value near $52.8B against a $29.9B asset base, there is no evident company-level trigger; the exposure is reporting-unit-specific and post-reallocation carrying values are undisclosed. A write-down would hit GAAP earnings and book equity, not cash flow, covenant EBITDA as typically defined, or the adjusted metrics on which guidance is set. It is nonetheless a dated event a screen would not surface.

**3. H2 margin step-up.** Guidance requires 23.7% H2 adjusted EBITDA margin versus 22.61% in H1 while gross margin is still eroding. Miss it and the adjusted EPS guide is at risk even with revenue on track.

**4. Rising interest and tax.** The euro note refinancing is worth roughly $0.63/share by 2029. The effective tax rate rose to 19.9% from 17.3%; a 400bp increase costs ~$64M, or ~$0.38/share. Of $1,591M FY2025 pretax income, only $94M was domestic, with the 15.8% rate depending on FDII (−3.6%) and foreign earnings net of credits (−3.5%). Undistributed foreign earnings of $6,689M are not treated as indefinitely reinvested.

**5. Self-disclosed AI substitution on Commercial Solutions,** discussed above, plus data-supplier and privacy dependency: IQVIA licenses rather than owns its raw data.

**6. Cost-to-complete estimation.** The sole critical audit matter. Adverse revisions to total expected contract cost flow straight through revenue in the period of change. Internal control was assessed effective with clean opinions, so there is no indication of a problem, but this is the mechanism through which a negative surprise would most plausibly arrive.

**7. Asia-Pacific clinical concentration** — 28% of R&DS revenue and the source of nearly all two-year clinical growth, carrying regulatory, data-transfer, FX and U.S. political exposure.

**8. Unconfirmed senior finance transition.** An April 23, 2026 8-K carried Item 5.02; the Q2 call participant list names an executive other than the signing CFO. No confirming release was retrievable. This is unverified and should not be treated as established, but it belongs on the monitoring list.

**9. Macro overlay.** Higher-for-longer policy rates compress the equity multiple on a 3.6x-levered business and raise the refinancing cost quantified above. Biopharma R&D budgets are the demand driver, and while the current read is recovery, sponsor capital availability is sensitive to credit conditions.

### Options Market Signal

The event has been resolved and the market is not pricing another one nearby. ATM IV collapsed from 47.4% to 35.4% between July 19 and August 7 as the price rose 14.2%, and near-term IV of 35.4% sits below 41.1% trailing realized volatility, the 6th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (35.4% at 13 days, 37.5% at 41 days, 43.6% at 104 days), the normal complacent shape. Skew is call-heavy at −3.8% near-dated, and put/call open interest is 0.26, the most call-tilted reading in the chain's recent history (up from 0.02). Implied move is ±5.8% to August 21 and ±17.8% to November 20.

Two readings. Constructively, cheap near-term vol against 41% realized means holders can monetize the position through covered calls at attractive prices, and there is no near-term catalyst the market fears. Cautiously, a 6th-percentile IV with call-tilted open interest six days after a 12.9% earnings pop is complacent positioning, and the longer-dated 43.6% suggests the market does see genuine uncertainty further out, plausibly around 2027 guidance and the goodwill test. The elevated put/call *volume* ratio of 6.74 conflicts with the call-tilted open interest; single-session volume ratios on a low-IV day are noisy and I would not read direction from it. Short interest is only 3.7% of float with 3.4 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic on either side.

**Risk Factors: 5.3.** No existential exposures and no named litigation, but a cluster of identifiable, dated items — the 2026 impairment test, the H2 margin step-up, cancelable backlog, mechanical interest and tax escalation, and self-flagged AI substitution — that are not obviously reflected at 18.3x guided adjusted earnings.

## Management Quality

**Capital allocation has been well-timed but is structurally entangled with the incentive metric.** FY2025 repurchases were $1,244M — 7.4M shares at a $169.13 average, accretive against today's price and better than 2024's $209.68 average. H1 2026 added $950M. Remaining authorization rose from $1,769M to $2,819M despite that spend, implying roughly $2.0B of new Board capacity in H1 and signalling continued buyback intent over accelerated deleveraging at 3.59x.

The tension: the company-selected performance measure in the proxy is Adjusted Diluted EPS Growth, and both leverage and buybacks mechanically lift it. FY2025 is the clean illustration — net income −0.9%, share count −5.4%, metric +7.1%. I am not inferring intent. Revenue, adjusted EBITDA and relative TSR are also plan metrics, and realized CEO "compensation actually paid" has swung from $88.4M (2021) to $6.0M (2022), which is evidence the plan transmits shareholder outcomes.

Alignment is the weaker part of the record. Cumulative five-year TSR indexed at 125.81 against a company-selected peer index of 186.82 — a 61-point shortfall — while CEO total compensation was between $26.7M and $30.1M in every one of those five years, 3.2x the average of other named executives, with the Chairman and CEO roles combined.

**Insider activity is one-sided but low-signal.** Form 4s through August 3 show exercise-and-sell and disposition activity by senior executives, concentrated in late July at $244–249 immediately after the 12.9% move; the CEO exercised a large block of appreciation rights and sold ~106,000 shares while retaining ~836,000. Earlier 2026 reference prices were ~$160 (April) and ~$179 (May). No open-market purchases were observed. Form 4 data cannot separate discretionary sales from 10b5-1 execution, SAR expiry management or tax withholding, and the retained position is large, so this is a mild negative rather than a view on value. ## Conclusion

IQVIA has done what the bull case required: bookings rose 19% to a record $3.15B, book-to-bill reached 1.22x, segment profit grew roughly 11% after a year of being flat in dollars, and management raised FY2026 guidance on all three headline metrics. The score trajectory that had declined from 6.4 in February to 5.4 in July stabilizes at 5.5 on this evidence; the operating deterioration reversed, and that is a genuine change rather than a price artifact.

What has not changed is the quality of the earnings and the price now being asked for them. GAAP operating income was flat at $506M for a second consecutive comparison, GAAP diluted EPS declined a penny, gross margin excluding depreciation fell another 66bp in H1, and 48% of adjusted net income consists of add-backs, of which the restructuring component has recurred four years running. Adjusted EPS growth of 12.1% in the quarter included 3.4 points from share count at 3.59x net leverage, against 2.85x EBIT interest coverage and a euro refinancing that mechanically removes about $0.63 per share by 2029. A reallocation-triggered goodwill test on $16.6B of goodwill supported by $6.2B of book equity lands sometime in 2026.

At 18.3x guided FY2026 adjusted earnings, 16.3x FY2027 consensus, and roughly 23x an economic EPS that expenses stock compensation and recurring restructuring, the stock is priced approximately where a mid-single-digit organic grower with this balance sheet should be priced. Base case $255 offers 8% upside; the bear case at $180, a level the shares traded through as recently as May, implies 24% downside. Nineteen of twenty-one analysts are positive with a low target of $240 above spot, and no insider bought at $160 in April while several sold at $245 in July. Own it if you own it; wait for $195–205, or for the Q3 print to show that the second-half margin step-up is structural, before adding.
