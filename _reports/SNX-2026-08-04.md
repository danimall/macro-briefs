## Executive Summary

TD SYNNEX is two businesses that the company only began reporting separately in Q1 FY2026, and the disaggregation is the most important development in the equity story. A low-margin, high-turnover global IT distribution utility (Americas 2.55% operating margin, Europe 1.19%, APJ 2.47%) is bolted to Hyve Solutions, a hyperscale ODM/contract manufacturer that generated 15.2% of Q2 FY2026 revenue and 34.4% of consolidated operating income at a 6.03% reported margin. The segments sum exactly to consolidated operating income, so there is no unallocated corporate layer distorting the comparison. Hyve competes against Jabil, Celestica, Flex, Quanta and Wiwynn, a comparable set that has re-rated substantially through the AI infrastructure cycle, and it is the reason SNX has compounded 78.5% over the trailing twelve months.

The Q2 print (June 25) was strong on every income-statement line: revenue $19,574.8M (+31.0%, +29.1% constant currency), operating income $519.4M (+58.3%), non-GAAP diluted EPS $4.85 versus $2.99. Consensus has followed: FY2026 non-GAAP EPS now sits at $18.97 and FY2027 at $21.41, up 13.3% and 17.6% respectively over 90 days, and three consecutive quarters have beaten (16.0%, 41.6%, 1.7% surprises). Against that, the July 2 10-Q disclosed information not available at our prior review: first-half free cash flow of -$1,261.4M versus -$246.6M a year earlier, driven by a $4,363.9M inventory build that lifted days inventory outstanding from 53 to 70. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow computes to $374.6M, a 1.8% yield on the current market capitalization against 6.6% on the FY2025 figure alone.

We keep the HOLD. The multiple has compressed since our June review (price down roughly 9-10% from the June 24 high of $296.47 while FY2027 estimates rose), which improves risk/reward on the forward P/E. But the earnings are increasingly memory-inflation-assisted, roughly $3.4bn of inventory sits above the prior run-rate as a directional bet on component prices, off-balance-sheet receivable financing has grown to $2.3bn on *uncommitted* facilities, and six insiders sold into the strongest fundamental stretch in the company's history with no offsetting purchases. A sum-of-parts at $266.17 already credits Hyve with roughly a mid-teens EBIT multiple. The thesis is intact; the entry is not compelling.

## Company Overview

TD SYNNEX distributes more than 200,000 active SKUs from approximately 2,500 OEM suppliers to more than 150,000 reseller and integration customers across 100+ countries, from 168 facilities, with roughly 24,000 full-time employees. About 48% of FY2025 revenue was non-US. The company was formed by SYNNEX's September 2021 acquisition of Tech Data; integration completed in the first half of FY2024. That history explains the accounting: $4.12bn of goodwill and $3.64bn of net intangibles at May 31, 2026 together equal 87% of $8.95bn book equity, and intangible amortization of $75.7M per quarter ($0.94 per diluted share in Q2) keeps GAAP earnings persistently below non-GAAP.

Offerings split between Endpoint Solutions (PCs, peripherals, mobile, print) and Advanced Solutions (hybrid cloud, security, storage, networking, servers, software, hyperscale infrastructure via Hyve), wrapped in integration, logistics, depot repair, a cloud marketplace platform and financial services including floor-plan financing and Device-as-a-Service.

Two disclosure points frame everything downstream. First, gross billings versus revenue: under ASC 606 the company recognizes only net margin on agent-role transactions, so Q2 gross billings of $28,879.1M netted down to $19,574.8M of GAAP revenue, a 32.2% gross-to-net rate versus 31.0% a year earlier. Reported revenue understates volume growth and the gap is widening, while reported margins are mechanically flattered. The company quantifies the effect: net presentation reduced Hyve's reported revenue growth by roughly 68% in Q2 and lifted Hyve's reported operating margin by about 189 basis points, implying a gross-billings-equivalent Hyve margin near 4.1%. Second, concentration: Apple was 11% of Q2 revenue, HP Inc. fell below 10%, and no single customer exceeded 10% of total revenue in Q2 or the six months, versus one customer at 11% of FY2025 revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue moved $62.3bn (FY2022) → $57.6bn (FY2023) → $58.5bn (FY2024) → $62.5bn (FY2025), with operating margin climbing 1.69% → 1.87% → 2.04% → 2.26% and non-GAAP diluted EPS reaching $13.19 in FY2025 against GAAP $9.95. FY2025 gross margin expanded 18bps to 6.99%, but management disclosed that roughly 30bps of that came from the net-presentation shift; underlying gross margin was modestly lower on a like-for-like basis. Cost control was real: SG&A fell to 67.5% of gross profit from 68.2%.

**First-half FY2026.** Six-month revenue $36,736.0M (+24.6%, +21.3% cc), operating income $1,008.7M (+59.5%) at a 2.75% margin, non-GAAP diluted EPS $9.58 versus $5.79. Decomposing the Q2 operating income increase of $191.2M: Hyve contributed roughly $84.3M, or about 44% of the incremental profit on 15% of revenue, with the three distribution segments supplying the balance. Europe distribution nearly doubled its GAAP operating margin (0.70% → 1.19%), so the improvement is broader than Hyve alone, but Hyve is doing disproportionate work. Separately, diluted weighted-average shares fell 3.8% to 79,755K, which accounts for roughly 4.1 percentage points of the 62.2% non-GAAP EPS growth.

**Cash flow is the analytical crux.**

Six-month drivers: receivables -$1,283.9M, inventories -$4,363.9M, payables +$3,488.2M. Trailing FCF = FY2025 $1,389.4M − 1H FY2025 (-$246.6M) + 1H FY2026 (-$1,261.4M) = **$374.6M**, or 1.8% of market capitalization.

Three qualifications matter before drawing conclusions. First, seasonality: 1H FY2025 free cash flow was also negative and the full year still produced $1,389.4M, so we do not annualize the half-year outflow. Second, funding mix: accounts payable financed roughly 80% of the inventory build, with DPO extending from 98 to 107 days and $3.8bn of supplier finance obligations embedded in payables. A vendor-financed inventory position is a materially less alarming configuration than a debt-financed one. Third, magnitude: inventories rose 46.2% in six months against 24.6% revenue growth, and using Q2 daily cost of revenue of $200.4M, the 17-day DIO increase equates to roughly **$3.4bn of inventory above the prior run-rate**. That is both the cash that would be released on normalization and the exposure base if component prices reverse.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets $38,506.0M, equity $8,950.4M, cash $1,094.2M (down from $2,435.4M at November 30, 2025), current ratio 1.20. Total borrowings approximately $4,720M against cash gives net debt of $3,625.8M and enterprise value near $24.8bn. On trailing non-GAAP operating income of $2,176.6M plus roughly $112M of depreciation, adjusted EBITDA is about $2,289M: gross leverage 2.06x, net leverage 1.58x, with first-half interest coverage of 5.47x versus 3.97x in FY2025. Covenant compliance is stated.

Two adjustments qualify those ratios. Accounts receivable sold to and held by financial institutions under *uncommitted* purchase agreements rose to $2.3bn from $1.8bn at November 30, 2025 and $1.2bn a year before that, with discount fees in interest expense nearly doubling to $23.4M in the quarter. Treating that as debt-like pushes gross leverage toward 3.1x. Committed liquidity remains large and largely unused: a $3.5bn revolver undrawn to April 2029, a $1.5bn US securitization with $0 drawn against roughly $4.0bn of eligible receivables, and a new €650M BNP Paribas European receivables program entered June 26, 2026. Adding committed capacity as working-capital intensity rose reads as prudent sequencing rather than distress, though it also confirms management expects the intensity to persist.

One near-dated item: $700M of 1.750% Senior Notes mature August 9, 2026. Refinancing at something near the 5.300% coupon on the October 2025 issue adds roughly $25M of annual pretax interest, about $0.23 of annual EPS. It is a real step-up in a higher-for-longer rate environment but manageable.

**Capital allocation.** First-half dividends of $77.3M and buybacks of $192.1M (1,095K shares at a $175.39 average, including 183K in March at $156.71) were funded from cash and incremental borrowings given the free cash outflow. The March repurchases were well timed against today's price; the pace slowed to 112K shares in May as the stock rose. The quarterly dividend was raised 9.1% to $0.48 (annualized $1.92, a 0.72% yield, roughly 11% of trailing non-GAAP EPS). $1.0bn remains on the March 2024 authorization.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is real, decelerating, and shifting in composition.

**What is driving it.** Hyve is the standout, with Q2 revenue $2,965.6M, operating margin expanding from 4.75% to 6.03%, and secondary reporting citing gross billings up 117% year over year. This is a direct read on hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending. Management states Hyve runs programs with all five major hyperscalers, with more than three programs at three of them. On the distribution side, two concrete vendor-consolidation wins support share gains: selection as one of two HPE global distributors (with Ingram Micro), which opens countries in Europe and APJ and brings access to the Juniper portfolio; and Dell's reported termination of its enterprise computing distribution agreement with Arrow ECS, freeing partners. Both are consistent with OEMs shrinking distributor counts in favor of global, end-to-end players. The HPE item is externally reported; the Dell/Arrow item rests on trade-press sourcing and management confirmation of TD SYNNEX's own Dell status.

**What restrains it.** Q3 guidance implies sequential declines at the midpoint: revenue $18.6bn versus $19.575bn (-5.0%), gross billings -4.1%, non-GAAP EPS $4.50 versus $4.85 (-7.2%). Q4 is seasonally the strongest quarter, so a Q3 dip is normal, but the year-over-year growth rate is stepping down from +31% toward roughly +17-20% on reasonable assumptions about the FY2025 seasonal split. Guidance also assumes PC units decline modestly, which matters because Endpoint Solutions is the volume backbone and Apple is 11-12% of revenue.

**Composition is shifting toward price.** The CEO stated only 300-400bps of the 31% Q2 growth came from ASP increases and pull-ins, that the price effect was small in Q1, larger in Q2, and will be "even more significant" in Q3, with another wave of increases expected in July and component tightness persisting at least through mid-2027. Volume and share are therefore doing most of the current work, which is favorable, but the mix is moving toward price as the year progresses, and price-driven revenue is the lower-quality kind for a distributor because it inflates working capital and reverses.

**Estimate momentum.** The 90-day revisions (+13.3% current year, +17.6% next year) are strong. The 30-day figures are flat at the annual level, with the +15.4% current-quarter revision reflecting reallocation across quarters rather than a net upgrade. Consensus of $4.63 for Q3 sits in the upper half of the $4.25-4.75 guide. Revision velocity is meaningfully positive but below the threshold at which it becomes a standalone signal of systematic underestimation.

## Valuation Assessment

Three lenses give three different answers, and the spread between them is the reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

**Forward P/E looks cheap.** At $266.17, SNX trades at 14.0x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $18.97 and 12.4x FY2027 of $21.41. On the implied FY2026-to-FY2027 growth of 12.9%, that is a PEG near 1.0. Peer median P/E is 17.8 (CDW 15.7, ARW 15.9, NSIT 19.3, PLUS 17.8, AVT 34.6), so SNX trades at a 22-30% discount to the median on forward numbers while growing faster than any of them. Analyst targets run $278 low, $330 median, $333.55 mean, $374 high; the low target sits 4.4% above the current price, and the recommendation distribution (4 strong buy, 6 buy, 1 hold) has been stable for four months.

**Trailing GAAP does not look cheap.** Trailing GAAP diluted EPS computes to $13.95 (FY2025 $9.95 − 1H FY2025 $4.19 + 1H FY2026 $8.19), putting the stock at 19.1x. Non-GAAP excludes roughly $303M of annual intangible amortization, so the 15.7x trailing non-GAAP multiple flatters the economics of a business whose equity is 87% goodwill and intangibles (tangible book value is approximately $14.90 per share). The supplied trailing and forward P/E fields (both 18.19) are internally inconsistent with the supplied EPS field of $9.95; we use our own computed figures throughout.

**Sum-of-parts brackets the current price.** Run-rating Q2 as a sensitivity (not a forecast, and noting Q4 is seasonally stronger while Q3 is guided lower): distribution at roughly $66.4bn revenue and $1,362M operating income; Hyve at roughly $11.9bn revenue and $715M operating income. Capitalizing distribution at 9-11x EBIT gives $12.3-15.0bn; Hyve at 12-16x EBIT gives $8.6-11.4bn. That totals $20.9-26.4bn of enterprise value against the current $24.8bn. To get materially above today's price the market must award Hyve a genuine AI-EMS multiple: at 20x EBIT, Hyve is worth $14.3bn and the sum-of-parts implies $287-321 per share. At $266.17 the market is already paying something like a mid-teens EBIT multiple for the hyperscale business. This is a reasonable price for a business whose fastest-growing segment is being credited fairly rather than a deep mispricing.

**Earnings quality is the offset.** EV/trailing revenue of 0.356x and EV/trailing non-GAAP EBIT of 11.4x look undemanding, but trailing FCF yield is 1.8%. A distributor earning 14x forward while converting negative cash is cheap conditional on the inventory converting back. On the cycle question, memory-driven ASP inflation is currently additive to revenue, gross profit and inventory carrying value simultaneously, the configuration in which a distributor's multiple compresses ahead of its earnings. We score valuation 6.2: there is an identifiable mispricing argument (peer discount adjusted for growth, Hyve re-rating optionality, analyst targets clustered above spot), but the cheapness rests on non-GAAP earnings at what may be a cycle-assisted peak.

## Competitive Landscape

The distribution side is consolidating in TD SYNNEX's favor, and the evidence is behavioral rather than statistical. HPE narrowing to two global distributors and Dell terminating Arrow ECS both indicate OEMs rewarding global scale and end-to-end portfolio breadth. Arrow (enterprise-only, no PC business) and regional players (ScanSource, ALSO, Esprinet, VSTECS) are structurally disadvantaged if the pattern continues, leaving TD SYNNEX and Ingram Micro as the beneficiaries. Distribution economics remain thin (Europe at 1.19% GAAP operating margin), so share gains matter mainly for scale and vendor rebate leverage rather than for margin.

Hyve competes in a different arena against Jabil, Celestica, Flex, Quanta and Wiwynn. One structural disclosure deserves weight: for Hyve, hyperscaler customers negotiate component quantities and prices directly with component manufacturers rather than through TD SYNNEX. That explains the shift toward customer-owned procurement (and thus net revenue presentation), and why Hyve reported no meaningful component shortage impact. It also means Hyve's economics are increasingly fee-for-service: lower exposure to component price movement, but narrower value capture and, plausibly, greater substitutability. Hyve's Q2 segment payroll of $11.7M against Americas distribution's $250.8M illustrates how asset- and labor-light the reported model is relative to its profit contribution.

The Amazon warrant belongs here rather than in the bull case. On May 30, 2026 the company issued Amazon a warrant for up to 3,238,066 shares (215,871 vested at $0.01; 3,022,195 vesting in tranches at $191.10 on aggregate qualifying-payment thresholds, expiring May 30, 2033), accounted for as contra-revenue. Maximum contingent issuance equals 4.05% of shares outstanding, or 3.89% post-issuance ownership dilution; full exercise would bring roughly $577.6M of cash. Management states this is established practice ("we are one of the last contract manufacturers to sign this warrant. Jabil and Flex already had it"), which we treat as an unverified assertion but which, if accurate, reframes the instrument as a sector-standard AWS volume incentive rather than a company-specific endorsement. The mechanical implications are concrete: the performance tranches carry a grant-date fair value of $116.15 each, roughly $351M in aggregate, which will flow through future revenue as a reduction as tranches vest, dampening reported Hyve revenue and margin by an amount not yet visible in any period. There is also full acceleration on an acquisition transaction, an embedded change-of-control cost any acquirer would price.

## Risk Assessment

**Inventory and component-price reversal.** Approximately $3.4bn of inventory sits above the prior run-rate on a deliberate bet that memory and component prices keep rising. Management is explicit that the build was intentional and that ASP increases will smooth the transition. If tightness resolves earlier than the mid-2027-to-2028 window management's vendor contacts describe, the exposure runs through both gross margin and carrying value.

**Undisclosed Hyve profit concentration.** The improvement in the consolidated concentration statistic (no customer above 10% of revenue) is plausibly an artifact of net presentation shrinking Hyve's reported revenue by 68-70%. A hyperscaler can be an enormous share of gross billings and operating profit while falling below 10% of GAAP revenue. Segment-level customer concentration is not disclosed. The Amazon warrant's existence (up to 4.05% of the share count granted against one customer's payment thresholds) implies at least one relationship large enough to justify that transfer.

**Funding structure.** $2.3bn of receivables sold under uncommitted facilities and $3.8bn of supplier finance obligations in payables are the least reliable liquidity in a stress scenario, and payables funded roughly 80% of the inventory build. Committed capacity ($3.5bn revolver undrawn, $1.5bn US securitization undrawn, new €650M European program) is substantial and offsets this, but reported net leverage of 1.58x understates economic leverage.

**Consumer/PC exposure.** Guidance assumes modest PC unit declines. Endpoint Solutions is the volume backbone and Apple at 11-12% of revenue is directly tied to it, making it the most exposed part of the portfolio if B2B PC demand undershoots the modest-decline assumption.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Insider activity from April through July 2026 was one-directional: Dennis Polk (Chair, Hyve Solutions) sold in every month including roughly 25,000 shares of exercise-and-sell at $264.75-$269.03 on June 29-30; Richard Hume cut holdings from 38,444 to 18,537; Saintil, Vezina, Thompson and Murphy also sold. No open-market purchases were identified. The regular, equal-sized tranches are consistent with pre-arranged plans (unconfirmed), and a cumulative TSR index of 207.61 against a 113.58 peer group gives ample non-informational reason to diversify. Rising estimates are the dominant signal here, so we do not treat this as strongly bearish, but the complete absence of offsetting purchases during record results is a legitimate negative for management-alignment scoring. Separately, Item 5.02 8-Ks filed July 16 and August 3, 2026, an Item 8.01 filed June 29, and the May 5 Polk offer letter are unread; if any involves segment leadership, execution risk changes. The management bench has turned over nearly completely in 24 months (CEO September 2024, CFO October 2025, Europe and North America presidents 2024).

**Other specifics.** Recurring cyber intrusions in July 2021, July 2022 and September 2023 attributed to the same or related actors, plus a November 2024 partner breach; significant IT/software operations in China and Hyve manufacturing in China and Taiwan (tariff and geopolitical exposure); the French Autorité de la Concurrence matter (€24.9M paid, further appeal pending, loss assessed remote); and perpetual, unquantified inventory repurchase obligations to flooring companies. The Q2 10-Q added no new risk factors despite the segment recast, the warrant and the inventory build.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.7% against 31.4% trailing realized volatility is rich (11.3pp spread), with a ±7.5% implied move into the August 21 expiry and a modest 3.3% put/call skew indicating some near-dated put demand. Structure is otherwise not bearish: term structure is in contango (42.7% near, 45.7% at six months), skew turns negative at three and six months (-0.5% and -3.8%), put/call open interest is 0.20 and put/call volume 0.03, both call-tilted. Since June 24, ATM IV compressed from 51.3% to 42.7% while the price fell 9.6%, and put/call OI fell from 0.26 to 0.20. Uncertainty is being priced *down* as the stock corrects, which argues the drawdown is multiple digestion rather than a positioning-driven repricing of risk. Short interest of 2.4% of float and 1.6 days to cover leave no squeeze dynamic in either direction. For holders, rich near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting into the September quarter more attractive than buying protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($374, +40.5%).** Hyve keeps compounding on hyperscaler AI capex, and the market re-rates it toward the 18-20x EBIT the EMS/ODM comparable set commands rather than the mid-teens embedded today. The HPE global-distributor selection and Arrow's displacement at Dell convert into multi-year share gains across Europe and APJ. Component tightness persists into 2027 as management's vendor contacts indicate, so the $3.4bn inventory position produces holding gains and pre-buy demand rather than markdowns, and Q4 seasonal cash generation converts the first-half build back to cash as it did in FY2025 (+$1,389.4M full-year FCF against a negative first half). FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches or exceeds $21.41 with buybacks continuing against the $1.0bn remaining authorization; roughly 17.5x on that number reaches the $374 analyst high.

**Bear case ($195, -26.7%).** Memory and component prices normalize in the first half of FY2027, exposing above-run-rate inventory to margin compression and write-downs while removing the ASP tailwind from revenue. PC unit declines exceed the "modest" assumption as the consumer weakens, hitting the Endpoint volume base and Apple-linked revenue. Hyve growth decelerates as hyperscalers reallocate programs among Jabil, Celestica, Flex, Quanta and Wiwynn while warrant contra-revenue begins reducing reported Hyve revenue and margin. Working-capital intensity proves structural rather than seasonal, forcing a choice between buybacks and the balance sheet; uncommitted receivable facilities tighten. Estimates fall toward $16 and the multiple compresses to 12x on cycle-peak concern, giving $195. For reference, the stock traded $150-165 for most of the second half of calendar 2025.

**Base case ($320, +20.2%).** 15x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $21.41. That multiple sits below the 17.8x peer median (justified by the earnings-quality gap between non-GAAP and GAAP/FCF) and above the low end of distributor history (justified by Hyve's mix contribution and the vendor-consolidation wins). It requires cash conversion to normalize through the seasonally strong fourth quarter without an inventory markdown, and Hyve to hold roughly current profit contribution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is positive and specific: Hyve on hyperscaler capex, HPE/Juniper franchise expansion, Arrow displacement at Dell, and 12.4x FY2027 consensus. What prevents a BUY at $266.17 is that the sum-of-parts already pays a mid-teens EBIT multiple for Hyve, trailing FCF yield is 1.8%, and the earnings are partly memory-cycle-assisted with $3.4bn of inventory as the offsetting exposure.

**Trajectory reconciliation.** Scores have drifted from 6.7 (March-May) to 6.4 (June 18) to 6.2 (June 24), and we mark 6.1 now. The composition of that drift has changed. Valuation improves at the margin from the prior review: the price is roughly 10% below the June 24 high while FY2027 consensus rose 17.6% over 90 days, so the forward multiple has compressed on rising estimates, a better entry than five weeks ago. Offsetting that, the July 2 10-Q disclosed genuinely new negative information that was not available at the prior review: -$1,261.4M of first-half free cash flow, DIO at 70 days, and $2.3bn of uncommitted off-balance-sheet receivable sales. This is not a case of lowering scores because the price fell; the price decline is modest (10%, well short of a drawdown that would shift the burden of proof) and the score adjustment reflects specific new cash-conversion and off-balance-sheet disclosures. Growth stays strong at 7.0 but below the prior 7.5 given the guided sequential decline and the shift in growth composition toward price.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Cash conversion turns.** Q3 (reporting late September) operating cash flow positive with DIO retreating below 62 days and inventories down sequentially, confirming the build is self-liquidating rather than a permanent step-up.
2. **Price at $225-235.** That level is roughly 10.5-11.0x FY2027 consensus and would restore a 35-45% gap to base case with the Hyve contribution unpriced.
3. **Hyve concentration disclosure or a second-hyperscaler program disclosure** that quantifies segment-level customer mix, removing the largest unquantified risk in the file.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Inventory markdown or gross margin below 6.5%** in a quarter, indicating the component-price bet has turned against the position.
2. **FY2027 consensus cut below $19.00** (a 11%+ reduction from $21.41), or two consecutive quarters of negative revisions.
3. **Contraction in uncommitted receivable facilities or supplier finance programs**, or revolver drawdown above $1bn, signs that the vendor-financed working-capital structure is tightening.
4. **Departure of Hyve segment leadership** (a live possibility given three unread Item 5.02 filings since June 22).

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 FY2026 results in late September 2026 are the decisive checkpoint, the first print that tests inventory normalization, the guided sequential decline, the first material warrant contra-revenue, and the post-refinancing interest run-rate following the August 9 maturity. The August 21 options expiry prices a ±7.5% move but carries no fundamental event.

## Conclusion

TD SYNNEX is executing well against a favorable backdrop. The segment recast made visible a hyperscale manufacturing business generating a third of operating profit on 15% of revenue, growing gross billings triple digits, and attached to the AI capex cycle. Distribution is simultaneously consolidating in the company's favor, with the HPE selection and Arrow's Dell loss as concrete evidence. Consensus has moved 17.6% higher on FY2027 in 90 days, three consecutive quarters have beaten, and every published analyst target sits above the current price.

The reasons to wait are equally specific. Trailing free cash flow of $374.6M against a $21.3bn market capitalization means the earnings inflection has not yet converted to cash, and the $3.4bn of above-run-rate inventory that explains the gap is a directional bet on component prices continuing to rise. Roughly $2.3bn of receivable financing sits on uncommitted facilities and $3.8bn of supplier finance sits inside payables, so economic leverage exceeds the reported 1.58x. Six insiders sold and none bought during the strongest fundamental stretch in the company's post-merger history. And at $266.17 a sum-of-parts already awards Hyve a mid-teens EBIT multiple, so the upside case requires either further estimate revisions or a further Hyve re-rating rather than the correction of an obvious mispricing.

Hold existing positions with an eye on covered-call overwriting given rich near-term implied volatility. New capital should wait for the late-September print to show inventory days retreating and operating cash flow positive, or for a price near $225-235.
