**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Albertsons is the second-largest traditional food-and-drug retailer in the United States, operating 2,244 stores across 35 states under 22 banners. The investment case has deteriorated on three axes over the past year: profitability, capital allocation discipline, and forward momentum. The headline GAAP collapse (net income down to $217M in FY2025 from $959M) is largely a one-time $773.8M pre-tax opioid settlement charge, but the more important story is that adjusted EBITDA has fallen for three consecutive years ($4.32B → $4.01B → $3.90B) and adjusted EPS has slid from $2.88 to $2.18. Operating margin has compressed from 2.6% to 0.9%. This is structural, driven by mix shift into two of the lowest-margin, highest-cost parts of the business (pharmacy, now 13.7% of sales, and digital delivery, +21% YoY).

The July 23, 2026 Q1 FY2026 print crystallized the concern: revenue grew only 0.2% and management lowered the full-year profit outlook, with the stock falling on the day. Kroger declined in sympathy, confirming a sector-wide pharmacy-reimbursement and digital-cost drag rather than a purely idiosyncratic problem, but a shared sector problem is not a bull case for a levered, standalone grocer with a compressing margin structure. Estimate revisions are negative across every window, and the capital-return program (a $750M accelerated share repurchase completed at ~$17.57, with the stock now at ~$14.60) was partly debt-funded while EBITDA and free cash flow declined.

The stock has fallen ~29% from where our prior review sat and ~30% from its 52-week high, and it screens optically cheap at ~6.4x the $2.27 current-year adjusted EPS with a 4.6% dividend yield. Neither is sufficient. Cheapness on a business with falling normalized earnings, rising capex, and no catalyst reflects a value trap, not an opportunity. The one genuine asymmetric event, the October 19, 2026 Delaware trial over the $600M Kroger termination fee, is binary and unpredictable. We rate ACI AVOID, while noting it is not an active short given elevated short interest and a resolvable dividend/litigation floor.

## Company Overview

Albertsons operates 2,244 stores under banners including Albertsons, Safeway, Vons, Jewel-Osco, ACME, Shaw's, Tom Thumb, and Randalls. The footprint includes 1,713 in-store pharmacies, 405 fuel centers, 22 distribution centers, and 19 manufacturing facilities, serving ~36.5 million customers weekly with roughly 280,000 employees, of whom ~190,000 are unionized. It reports as a single segment organized by geographic retail division.

Strategy is branded "Customers for Life," anchored in four digital platforms (eCommerce, loyalty, pharmacy/health, mobile app) and a growing retail-media unit ("Media Collective"). Competitive positioning rests on scale, fresh and Own Brands, a 51.2M-member loyalty program (+12% YoY), and an accelerating technology/AI investment push. It competes against larger and often non-unionized rivals (Walmart, Costco, Kroger) plus discounters, club stores, and online grocers, in a structurally low-margin industry.

Cerberus Capital Management owns roughly 30% and holds governance rights through a Stockholders' Agreement, which introduces both board-influence overhang and future share-supply risk.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trend is the core of the analysis:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $77.6B | $80.4B | $83.2B |
| Operating income | $2,307M | $1,546M | $728M |
| Net income (GAAP) | $1,514M | $959M | $217M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $4,318M | $4,005M | $3,902M |
| Adjusted net income | $1,694M | $1,382M | $1,209M |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $2.23 | $1.64 | $0.40 |
| Adjusted EPS | $2.88 | $2.34 | $2.18 |

Revenue rose modestly, but the quality of that growth is poor. Identical sales grew 2.0% and are increasingly pharmacy-driven. The pharmacy and digital growth noted above is dilutive to margin because pharmacy carries reimbursement pressure and digital carries elevated delivery/handling costs. Gross margin ex-fuel/LIFO fell 59 bps in FY25. The mechanism is straightforward: the parts of the business that are growing are the parts that earn the least, so top-line growth coincides with a shrinking profit pool. Fresh and non-perishable categories, which carry better margins, are flat-to-declining as a share of sales.

Cash generation is weakening. Operating cash flow fell to $2,367M from $2,681M, and free cash flow was ~$533M. Capex is guided up to $2.0–2.2B in FY2026 from $1.83B, which will pressure FCF further at the same time EBITDA is falling. Total debt (including finance leases) rose ~$1.1B to $8.95B, with the current ratio weak at 0.86. The reported debt/equity of 14.6x is distorted by buybacks depleting equity; the Altman Z-Score of 3.28 sits in the safe zone, supported by grocery's characteristically high asset turnover (~3.1x), and no financial maintenance covenants were triggered, with $3.5B+ of ABL availability. This is not an imminent solvency problem but a slow erosion of the earnings base while the balance sheet takes on more debt.

Capital allocation is the sharpest concern. The company repurchased $1,492M of stock (78.7M shares, including a $750M ASR at ~$17.57 average) and paid $323M in dividends in FY25, then raised the dividend 13% ($0.15 → $0.17) and lifted buyback authorization to $2.0B remaining. Buying back stock at ~$17.57 that now trades at ~$14.60 destroyed value, and the ASR was partly ABL-funded. Returning cash aggressively, partly with debt, while EBITDA and FCF decline and capex rises is a poor use of a shrinking balance sheet.

The earnings track record shows four consecutive beats (Q1 FY26 +10.3%, prior quarters +5.2%, +9.0%, +2.1%), which lends some confidence to the current-quarter consensus. But beating a lowered bar is different from growing the underlying earnings power, and the FY26 guidance cut resets the trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

There is no credible engine for earnings growth at present. Revenue growth has slowed to essentially flat (Q1 FY26 +0.2%), identical sales growth is thin and margin-dilutive, and the two areas actually expanding (pharmacy, digital) subtract from profitability. Management's articulated path (higher-margin retail media and pharmacy health services plus productivity/AI to offset core margin compression) is plausible in theory but unproven in the numbers, and it requires elevated capex to build.

Estimate revisions confirm the negative momentum. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $2.27, revised down 0.6% over both 30 and 90 days; next-year at $2.45, down ~0.6–0.7%; and both quarterly estimates are edging lower. These are not collapsing, but they are unambiguously falling, the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated turn. The guidance cut is the confirming event: management lowered the fresh-fiscal-year profit outlook one quarter into the year.

The Medicare Drug Negotiation Program (IRA) became effective in 2026, expanding to up to 100 drugs by 2031, which directly pressures the pharmacy growth engine's economics. PBM consolidation compounds it. The company is growing its most exposed category into a tightening reimbursement environment.

## Valuation Assessment

ACI trades at ~6.4x current-year adjusted EPS ($2.27) and ~6.0x next-year ($2.45), with a 4.6% dividend yield. The peer set is heterogeneous and not directly comparable: Sprouts at 17.0x, Casey's at 47.8x, Instacart (CART) at 23.5x, with UNFI and Grocery Outlet at negative multiples. Peer median P/E of 23.5 is not a fair anchor given those are faster-growing or structurally different formats; a traditional levered grocer with declining EBITDA deserves a discount, and it has one.

The forward P/E of 36.6x in the profile reflects GAAP EPS depressed by the opioid charge and is not the relevant figure. On adjusted earnings, the multiple is genuinely low. The problem is that the low multiple is doing exactly what a low multiple should do when earnings power is eroding: reflecting a business whose normalized profit is falling, whose FCF is being squeezed by rising capex, and whose growth areas are margin-dilutive. There is no identified catalyst to reverse the compression; retail media and pharmacy health services are aspirational, not delivering.

Cheapness alone is not a thesis, and in this environment a compressed multiple more often marks a value trap than a mispricing. To score valuation highly, we would need a specific reason the market is wrong: a demonstrable earnings inflection, a hidden asset, or a catalyst that resegments the story. The Kroger $600M termination fee plus potential damages is the only such candidate, but it is binary and undated in outcome (trial October 19, 2026), and the June 2026 document-production ruling against Albertsons is a modest negative tell. On base-case fundamentals, the multiple is fair for what it is: a low-growth, levered grocer with a declining margin structure. We score valuation 4.5; the yield and discount provide some floor, but there is no asymmetric upside case at current fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

Albertsons is the standalone #2 traditional grocer after the collapse of the $24.6B Kroger merger. That leaves it competing against scale-advantaged, largely non-unionized rivals (Walmart, Costco) and against Kroger, which is now unshackled and pursuing its own consolidation (reported Giant Eagle deal, July 2026). The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, not stable: Albertsons' chief rival is growing scale while ACI defends itself in litigation and absorbs the cost of two years operating under merger constraints.

The loyalty program (51.2M members) and Own Brands are genuine assets, and the Uber One partnership extends the loyalty value proposition. But these have not arrested the margin erosion. The 10-K's newly emphasized language about agentic AI shopping tools eroding direct customer relationships and pricing power is a differentiated, forward-looking risk for grocers: AI agents optimizing purely on price and speed would disintermediate exactly the loyalty and relationship advantages Albertsons is investing in. The competitive position is stable-to-eroding on a level basis but deteriorating on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Kroger litigation (binary, dated).** The Delaware Chancery trial over the $600M termination fee plus damages is set for October 19, 2026. Upside if ACI collects; downside is continued legal costs and no fee. The June 2026 ruling rejecting Albertsons' bid to force Kroger's planning documents is a small procedural setback. This is an unusual, asymmetric, but genuinely unpredictable event.

**Pharmacy reimbursement.** IRA drug negotiation and PBM dynamics squeeze the growth engine's margins. This is the structural driver of the compression thesis and is not fully arrested.

**Opioid liability.** Beyond the $773.8M framework charge, ACI faces ~81 suits plus potential ~108 more, ongoing DOJ Controlled Substances Act / FCA investigations, and a Washington State trial set for May 2026. Additional charges are possible.

**Multiemployer pension and labor.** Contributions to 28 plans (~$583M FY25, rising to ~$610M), of which 16 are "Critical" or "Critical and Declining"; exiting markets could trigger withdrawal liabilities. Contracts covering ~22,000 employees expire in FY2026, carrying strike risk.

**Leverage and refinancing.** $8.6–8.95B debt with heavy refinancing cadence at 5.5%–6.25% coupons; higher-for-longer rates and any downgrade raise costs.

**Governance/transition instability.** CEO transition to Susan Morris is complete, but continued C-suite churn (a 5.02 filed alongside the July 23 earnings 8-K), the 66% pay hike for the legal chief on a retention/stabilization award, and negative CEO "comp actually paid" all point to a company managing through instability with underwater equity comp.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has jumped to 85.0% (1-week) from 50.2% in early June, against 1-year historical volatility of 30.2%, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (85.0% near-term vs. 42.8% at six months), signaling an imminent catalyst around the July 23 earnings event. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.44 to 0.45 and skew is call-heavy (-20.2%), which reads as speculative positioning for a bounce or event resolution rather than fresh downside hedging. Short interest is elevated at 15.2% of float with 6.0 days to cover, meaningful bearish institutional positioning that also creates squeeze risk against any short thesis. The mechanics do not favor initiating a fresh short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** ACI is optically cheap at ~6x adjusted EPS with a well-covered dividend and a $2.0B buyback authorization. If the Delaware trial delivers the $600M fee plus damages, that is a meaningful cash inflow relative to a ~$5.9B market cap. Retail media and pharmacy health services could scale into higher-margin revenue that offsets core compression, and productivity/AI initiatives could stabilize margins. A sector-wide problem shared with Kroger implies the discount is not company-specific and could re-rate if pharmacy reimbursement pressure stabilizes. Bull case ~$20 (roughly consensus mean, requiring margin stabilization and a favorable litigation outcome).

**Bear Case.** Adjusted EBITDA and EPS continue their three-year decline as pharmacy and digital keep growing at the expense of margin, while rising capex suppresses FCF. Estimate revisions keep drifting lower, the July guidance cut proves to be the first of several, and debt-funded buybacks at prices well above the current stock continue to destroy value. The Kroger trial yields no fee plus ongoing costs, and IRA/PBM pressure intensifies through 2031. Bear case ~$11–12, roughly 15-20% downside from current, toward the low end of analyst targets ($14) and below.

The distribution is skewed unfavorably: the base case is continued grind at a low but justified multiple, the bear case is a further leg down on sustained EBITDA erosion, and the bull case depends on an unproven margin turn plus a binary court outcome. That is not the setup for ownership.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated negative catalyst, the July 23, 2026 guidance cut, has already occurred and is reflected in the price. The next dated events are the October 19, 2026 Kroger trial (binary, and arguably a positive-skew catalyst given a potential $600M inflow) and subsequent quarterly prints where continued margin compression could disappoint. There is no clean, dated negative catalyst on a near-term horizon; the thesis is a structurally eroding grocer, which is a slow bleed rather than an imminent break.

**Timing window.** The trial is roughly three months out and skews positive for ACI, which is adverse for a short. There is no near-term event that reliably drives the stock materially lower within a favorable carry window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is already elevated at 15.2% of float with 6.0 days to cover. This is a moderately crowded short, and 6.0 days to cover creates real squeeze risk on any positive surprise (a favorable trial ruling, a beat, or M&A interest given Cerberus's stake).

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is not confirming a bearish setup for shorts. Near-term IV is very rich (85% vs. 30% HV), so buying puts is expensive, and the call-heavy skew with a P/C OI ratio of 0.45 suggests positioning is tilted toward upside/event resolution, not downside. Rich IV increases the carrying cost of any options-based short expression.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case downside is ~$11–12, roughly 15-20% from ~$14.60. Upside risk includes a squeeze, a favorable $600M+ litigation outcome, and takeout interest. Reward:risk is well below 2:1 given the modest remaining downside, the positive-skew litigation catalyst, and squeeze mechanics.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a mediocre, structurally challenged business, not an actively collapsing one. The next dated catalyst (the trial) skews positive, short interest is already elevated, and the options market is not confirming downside. Avoid ownership; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The core headwinds (pharmacy/digital margin dilution, IRA reimbursement pressure, rising capex against falling EBITDA, and negative estimate revisions) are structural and likely to persist through FY2026 and beyond.

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view):**
- **Demonstrated margin stabilization**: two consecutive quarters of flat-to-rising adjusted EBITDA margin and a halt to the three-year EBITDA decline, evidencing that retail media/pharmacy health services are offsetting core compression.
- **Positive estimate-revision turn**: current-year and next-year consensus EPS rising rather than falling over a 90-day window.
- **Capital allocation discipline**: buybacks paused or executed at prices consistent with intrinsic value, and dividend coverage clearly maintained without incremental ABL draws.
- **Litigation clarity**: a favorable Delaware ruling delivering the $600M fee (plus damages) would materially improve the balance-sheet and re-rating case; conversely, a loss removes a source of upside.
- **Valuation floor with catalyst**: a further de-rating toward ~$11 combined with any of the above operational turns would create a genuine risk/reward case.

**Timeline.** Expect the margin and reimbursement headwinds to persist for at least the next 12 months. The October 19, 2026 trial is the nearest structural swing factor. Reassess at each quarterly print, with particular focus on adjusted EBITDA margin trajectory and the direction of estimate revisions.

## Conclusion

Albertsons is a large, scaled grocer whose profitability is eroding structurally, not cyclically. Adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS have declined for three straight years, the growth is concentrated in the lowest-margin parts of the business, capex is rising as FCF weakens, and management has cut FY2026 guidance one quarter in. The debt-funded buyback executed well above the current price compounds the impression of poor capital discipline. The stock is optically cheap, but the multiple is appropriate for a business with falling normalized earnings and no identified catalyst, presenting value-trap risk more than opportunity. The 4.6% dividend and the binary Kroger litigation provide a partial floor, but neither offsets the negative momentum. The score trajectory (5.3 → 4.5 → 3.9 → 4.7) has been volatile and the recommendation has oscillated; the July 23 guidance cut and continued estimate erosion tip the balance decisively negative this cycle. We rate ACI **AVOID**, and specifically NOT a short given elevated short interest, rich options pricing, and a near-term litigation catalyst that skews positive.
