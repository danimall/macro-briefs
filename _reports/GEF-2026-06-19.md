**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Greif has spent the past 12 months reshaping itself from a debt-heavy, paper-and-timber-centric industrial conglomerate into a more focused industrial-packaging operator. The two defining transactions, the $1,804.7M sale of the containerboard business (closed August 31, 2025) and the $462.0M sale of ~173,000 acres of Soterra timberland (closed October 1, 2025), generated roughly $2.27B gross and were used primarily to retire debt. Long-term debt fell from $2,626M at FY2024 year-end to $701.2M as of March 31, 2026, and management now describes net leverage at 1.1x as "the strongest balance sheet in our nearly 150-year history." This is the central fact that any analysis must anchor to; screening metrics computed off the FY2024 10-K (a 3.19x debt/equity ratio, an Altman Z near 1.77) describe a company that no longer exists.

The operating story is more mixed. Greif is generating Adjusted EBITDA growth (Q2 FY26 +7.5% YoY, H1 +14%) and a sharp improvement in free cash flow (Q2 adjusted FCF $179.3M vs $86.6M) entirely through cost-out and value-based pricing, not volume. The company is operating through what management explicitly calls an "industrial recession," with volumes guided down mid-single digits across metals, fiber, and closures for the second half of FY26 and no demand inflection visible. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 6% over 90 days (and the current-quarter estimate cut 17%), and the only reason FY26 EBITDA guidance was trimmed to a $610M floor was exogenous: intermittent Middle East facility shutdowns.

We rate $GEF a HOLD on an income basis. The balance-sheet repair is real and the recently raised dividend (+10.7% in June 2026, yielding ~3.5%) is well covered, but at ~22x trailing earnings near a cyclical trough, with falling near-term estimates, soft volumes, and a permanent dual-class governance discount, the setup lacks the asymmetric upside required for a BUY. The stock has recovered ~9% over the trailing year and sits roughly mid-range between its 52-week low ($55.75) and high ($77.14).

## Company Overview

Greif is a ~150-year-old global producer of rigid industrial packaging: the steel and plastic drums, intermediate bulk containers (IBCs), fibre drums, and closure systems used to move bulk chemicals, food ingredients, paints, pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and petroleum products. The company operates roughly 250 plants across 40+ countries.

Following the portfolio overhaul, Greif reports in four segments effective FY2026:
- **Customized Polymer Solutions** — plastic drums, rigid IBCs, small plastics, sold to chemicals, food/beverage, ag, and pharma. Designated as the growth engine given its resin base, lower cyclicality, and ag-chem strength.
- **Durable Metal Solutions** — steel drums and metal packaging plus paints/linings; the largest revenue segment (~$735M in H1 FY26).
- **Sustainable Fiber Solutions** — fibre drums, recycled board, tubes and cores, specialty partitions, and adhesives. This is what remains of the former Paper Packaging business after the containerboard exit.
- **Innovative Closure Solutions** — closure systems for industrial packaging; small, higher-margin (~27% gross margin), and growing.

The dual-class equity structure is a defining governance feature. Class A shares (GEF) are largely non-voting unless dividends fall into arrears; Class B shares carry full voting control and are historically family/insider-aligned. Dividends are split 40% to Class A and 60% to Class B by charter (Class B receives 1.5 cents for every 1 cent to Class A). Class A holders have essentially no control over the company.

## Financial Analysis

**Deleveraging is the headline.** The trajectory of long-term debt ($2,626M FY2024, $914.8M Sept 2025, $701.2M Mar 2026) captures the transformation. Total debt including short-term and AR facilities sits near $1.0B versus $2.7–3.0B in FY2024. Cash was $286.1M at March 2026; total shareholders' equity was $2,981M against total assets of $5,597M, an equity/assets ratio of ~53% versus the ~31% embedded in the stale distress screen. The February 27, 2026 refinancing into the "2026 Credit Agreements" extended maturities to 2031 at a weighted-average interest rate of ~3.14%, aided by access to the Farm Credit System (CoBank), a genuine and unusual cost-of-capital advantage for a packaging company. Interest expense fell accordingly: H1 went from $31.4M to $19.7M.

**Operating performance is improving on cost, not volume.** Q2 FY26 (quarter ended March 31, 2026): net sales $1,072.8M (roughly flat, −$5.6M YoY); gross margin 23.0% (flat); Adjusted EBITDA $156.8M, up 7.5% YoY with margin +110bps. Reported net income of $16.3M was depressed by a $40M non-cash charitable share donation that ran through SG&A. H1 FY26 net sales were $2,067.6M (−1.3% YoY), with operating profit of $292.0M inflated by the $216M one-time Soterra gain; the cleaner read is H1 Adjusted EBITDA of $279.3M versus $244.7M (+14%). Adjusted free cash flow improved more than 100% YoY, even though the prior-year period still included ~$30M from the now-divested containerboard business.

**Cost-out is the engine.** Management has achieved $75M of an $80–90M FY26 cost-savings target, with $120M committed by FY27. They describe a ~12% reduction in the salaried workforce plus footprint and mill closures as structural — costs that "are not coming back." Combined with the index-linked contract pricing architecture (see Competitive Landscape), this is what allowed margins to expand through both input inflation and the Middle East cost shock while volumes were soft.

**Quality-of-earnings notes.** Three items distort reported figures: the $216M Soterra gain inflates H1 net income; the $40M charitable charge depresses Q2 reported operating profit and net income; and a $49.3M discrete tax expense was triggered by the Soterra disposition. Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted FCF are the cleaner run-rate measures.

**Capital returns.** The dividend was raised 10.7% in June 2026 to $0.62/quarter on Class A. A $150M buyback (Class A + B) was completed in early FY26, and a $300M authorization from December 2025 remains available but unused. FCF guidance of $315M was maintained even as the EBITDA floor was cut, underscoring that the cut is exogenous.

## Growth Analysis

Greif's growth profile is the weakest part of the story and the primary reason this is not a BUY. The company is delivering EBITDA and FCF growth in a contracting volume environment, which is impressive operationally but not the same as durable top-line growth. Net sales are flat-to-down, and management guides volumes down mid-single digits across metals, fiber, and closures for H2 FY26, with polymer flat. The explicit management framing, "demand remains soft, and we are not yet seeing an inflection," leaves the growth thesis dependent on either continued cost-out (a finite lever, largely realized by FY27) or an eventual volume recovery.

The forward-looking signal is negative. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 17.2% over 30 days (to $1.08 from $1.30) and current-year EPS cut 6.0% over 90 days (to $3.88 from $4.15); next-year estimates are down 4.1%/5.6%. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate momentum that would justify paying up for a cyclical name. Estimate revisions are among the most reliable forward indicators available, and here they are pointing down.

The one genuine upside lever is operating leverage on recovery. Management claims greater than 50% incremental margins on the first tranche of any volume recovery, given the structural cost reductions already taken. If the industrial cycle turns, the earnings response could be sharp. That is real optionality, but it is unproven at scale and outside management's control — it is a call on the global industrial cycle, not on company execution.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.91, $GEF trades at ~22.4x trailing earnings, a meaningful premium to the peer median P/E of 19.9x (ATR 19.9x, SLGN 15.1x; OI is loss-making). On forward consensus of $3.88 (FY26) and $4.50 (FY27), the multiple compresses to ~17.8x and ~15.3x, more reasonable, but the FY26 estimate is still falling, which undercuts the case that forward multiples represent a clean discount. Analyst price targets span $68.00 (low) to $90.00 (high) with a $76.20 mean and $75.00 median, putting the current price near the low end of the range with ~11% to the median.

We do not treat the elevated trailing multiple as a value-trap signal, but neither do we see a specific mispricing to reward. The case for a higher valuation score would rest on cycle-adjusted earnings power above trailing, and that case has some merit (cost savings are structural, and recovery optionality is real). But the offsetting facts are concrete: estimates are being revised down, not up; volumes are contracting; and the dual-class structure with non-voting Class A shares warrants a permanent governance discount that limits any re-rating. The dividend yield of ~3.5%, well covered against the FCF guide, provides absolute support and is the primary reason to hold. Against the peer set, GEF deserves a modest premium for its de-levered balance sheet and pricing architecture, but the premium is largely in the price.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5: not cheap enough to be asymmetric, not stretched enough to flag as a trap. The income support and forward multiple keep it balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

Greif competes in fragmented, cyclical, price-sensitive industrial-packaging markets where no single company dominates. Competition is on price, quality, service, and on-time delivery against both large vertically-integrated players and many small regional firms. The entire sector is in a multi-year industrial contraction, and competitors may cut prices to defend volume.

Two structural advantages stand out. First, global scale: few competitors can serve multinational chemical customers across 40+ countries, which supports customer retention and pricing in the larger accounts. Second, and underappreciated, is the contractual pricing architecture. Over roughly seven years, Greif has converted most global customer contracts to monthly, index-linked price-adjustment mechanisms with "openers" for non-raw-material cost increases. This is the mechanism that allowed margins to hold and expand through both input inflation and the Middle East cost shock, and it is a genuine, durable moat improvement rather than a cyclical artifact.

The strategic mix-shift toward less-cyclical resin/small-polymer and closures end-markets (with ag-chem showing growth) and away from commoditized, capital-intensive paper assets is sensible. Whether it improves through-cycle returns enough to justify a sustained premium is unproven, particularly while volumes are contracting and the segment that remains in fiber is the residual of a business Greif chose to shrink.

## Risk Assessment

**Persistent industrial demand recession.** The most material risk is the absence of a volume inflection. The cost-out lever is largely realized by FY27; beyond that, EBITDA growth requires volume recovery that management does not yet see.

**Middle East conflict.** Intermittent regional facility shutdowns have already caused a <$5M Q2 EBITDA hit, the sole driver of the FY26 EBITDA guidance cut. Steel/metal volumes are most affected, and the conflict is also driving input-cost inflation.

**Russia exposure.** ~3% of net sales but ~9% of operating profit and 2% of assets (FY2024). Cash is largely trapped under currency controls; a forced exit would trigger an impairment.

**Goodwill impairment.** Goodwill of $1,693M (Mar 2026) is concentrated in Polymer ($622M) and acquired businesses (Ipackchem). A demand-driven downgrade to reporting-unit cash flows could force write-downs.

**Acquisition integration and regulation.** The recent acquisition spree (Ipackchem $569M; Lee Container, Centurion, ColePak) carries integration risk. EU Packaging & Packaging Waste Regulation mandates post-consumer resin content, a potential supply/cost risk for the polymer growth engine.

**Governance.** The dual-class structure means Class A holders have no effective voting control, and activist-driven change is not a catalyst. This is a permanent discount, not an event risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 29.0% sits modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 24.6% (a 4.4% spread, "rich"), but at only the 20th percentile within the current chain — the market is not pricing an imminent binary event. Put/call skew is mildly elevated at 3.2%, indicating a slight preference for downside protection, and the term structure is in normal contango (near 29.0% vs long-term 31.2%). Days-to-cover is just 1.1 on minimal short interest (210,087 shares), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The options market reads as complacent-to-neutral, consistent with our balanced fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$85–90):** The cost-out proves fully structural, the index-linked pricing architecture sustains margin expansion, and the industrial cycle turns — triggering the claimed >50% incremental margins on early volume recovery. EBITDA re-rates [unverified — actual: N/A], FCF supports continued buybacks against the unused $300M authorization, and the market awards a premium for the de-levered balance sheet and lower-cyclicality mix. This aligns with the $90 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$56):** The industrial recession deepens, volumes fall faster than the mid-single-digit guide, the Middle East disruption persists or worsens, and cost-out runs out as a lever by FY27 with no volume offset. Falling estimates continue, a goodwill write-down surfaces in Polymer, and the stock retests its 52-week low of $55.75 (~−19% from current). The dividend remains covered, limiting downside below that level.

**Base Case (~$75):** Cost savings deliver as guided, margins hold through the soft patch, the dividend grows, and FCF funds modest buybacks. The stock re-rates toward the analyst median of $75 as the balance-sheet quality is recognized, but absent a volume inflection the multiple does not expand materially. This is roughly +9% from current plus the ~3.5% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus FY26/FY27 EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive over two consecutive months (the current −6% to −17% revision trend reverses).
- Evidence of a volume inflection — sequential volume growth in Durable Metal or Customized Polymer, or management explicitly confirming demand has bottomed.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s that pushes the forward multiple below ~14x and the yield above ~4%, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment in the Polymer reporting unit, or a forced Russia exit impairment.
- A dividend coverage problem — FCF guidance cut below the level that sustains the dividend plus committed cost-out.
- Volumes deteriorating beyond the guided mid-single-digit decline with cost-out exhausted, signaling EBITDA contraction in FY27.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with particular attention to whether the Middle East drag persists into the FY26 EBITDA print and whether estimate revisions stabilize.

## Conclusion

Greif executed a genuine and well-timed transformation, exiting commodity-paper and timberland to become a de-levered, more focused industrial-packaging company with the strongest balance sheet in its history and a real pricing-architecture moat. Those facts justify holding the stock and collecting a well-covered, growing ~3.5% dividend. They do not, however, add up to a BUY at ~22x trailing earnings while volumes contract, current-year estimates fall, and the upside lever (volume recovery) sits outside management's control. The dual-class governance structure caps any re-rating, and the stock already sits mid-range against its 52-week band with the analyst mean only ~11% above. We rate $GEF a HOLD (income), with an upgrade path that requires either stabilizing estimates plus a volume inflection or a cheaper entry point.
