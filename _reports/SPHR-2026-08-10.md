## Executive Summary

Sphere Entertainment is two unrelated businesses under one Dolan-controlled holding company: a single Las Vegas venue with three immersive productions, and a regional sports network in structural decline whose cash flow is pledged to its own lenders. The stock has risen roughly 285% over twelve months, tracking the August 2025 debut of *The Wizard of Oz at Sphere*, the June 2025 MSG Networks debt restructuring, the July 2025 Abu Dhabi franchise agreements, and the January 2026 National Harbor announcement. At $161 it sits 7.8% below the 52-week high of $174.60.

The valuation question turns on two corrections to the headline data. First, the widely quoted $4.7bn market capitalization equals 29.046M Class A shares times the price and excludes 6.867M Class B shares; the correct equity value is 35.913M × $161 = **$5,782M**. Second, MSG Networks' ~$117M of CY2025 adjusted operating income (AOI) is not distributable to SPHR shareholders — distributions to the parent are contractually restricted, $10M per quarter amortizes plus an excess-cash sweep, and Contingent Interest Units hand lenders 50% of certain excess cash flow and 50% of M&A proceeds up to $100M through 2029. Crediting it to equity holders flatters the multiple. On a Sphere-only basis, enterprise value is $5,782M + $533.75M of Sphere-side debt − $223.5M of unencumbered cash = **$6,092M, or 27.6x trailing Sphere-segment AOI of $220.7M**. That is a full price for one venue, one blockbuster show, and a pipeline whose fee and royalty economics have not been disclosed.

Against that, the liquidity trajectory has improved materially and is under-appreciated: unencumbered parent cash rose from $100.5M at December 31, 2025 to $223.5M at June 30, 2026 while the artist/promoter advance-ticket float shrank from $376.8M to $310.3M, and a new $275M undrawn revolver established January 29, 2026 expressly permits distributions to Sphere Entertainment Group. H1 2026 operating cash flow was +$102.6M versus −$52.7M a year earlier. Sphere segment revenue grew 48% in H1. The company has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters. Set against a documented decay in the flagship show's run rate, negative GAAP earnings, 33.2% short interest, and a governance structure with 72.3% Dolan voting control and no majority-independent board, the distribution of outcomes is wide and approximately centered on the current price. We rate SPHR **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

**Sphere segment.** One operating venue, opened October 6, 2023 on the Las Vegas Strip. Revenue comes from *The Sphere Experience* (proprietary immersive productions run multiple times daily), concert residencies and third-party events, Exosphere exterior advertising, and suites, concessions and merchandise. Three productions exist: *Postcard from Earth* (~$80M development cost), *V-U2 An Immersive Concert Film*, and *The Wizard of Oz at Sphere* (>$100M, the company's first AI-assisted production, opened August 28, 2025). *Wizard of Oz 2.0* is targeted for September 2026, *Rocky Horror Picture Show at Sphere* for 2027, and *From The Edge* is in development. The 10-K discloses that the original May 2019 Las Vegas cost estimate of ~$1.2bn was "meaningfully exceeded."

**MSG Networks segment.** MSG Network and MSG Sportsnet carry Knicks, Rangers, Islanders, Sabres and Devils rights across New York State, plus MSG+ within the Gotham Sports app (a JV with YES Network). Subscribers fell 13% in CY2025 and ~16.5% year over year in Q2 2026.

**Expansion model.** Abu Dhabi: DCT Abu Dhabi funds and owns the venue; Sphere provides pre-construction and construction services and collects service fees, franchise fees and royalties, with completion targeted end-2029 on Yas Island. National Harbor, Maryland: the first smaller-scale design, with Peterson Companies, the State of Maryland and Prince George's County; management is evaluating a build-to-suit and leaseback in which a third party funds and owns the venue while Sphere holds a long-term lease, retains operational control, and consolidates results. Both structures move construction capital off SPHR's balance sheet. Management's stated objective is five or more operating venues within five to six years, with Dolan saying he would be "disappointed" if no additional venue were announced by Q1 2027.

## Financial Analysis

Annual XBRL periods are not comparable: the April 2023 MSGE spin-off and the change of fiscal year end from June to December created a six-month stub. The figures below follow the calendar-year presentation in the FY2025 10-K, with 2024 as unaudited recast comparatives.

**Calendar 2025.** Total revenue $1,220.0M (+8%). Sphere revenue $781.4M (+27%) with segment AOI of $144.6M, swinging from −$19.7M. MSG Networks revenue $438.6M (−15%) with AOI $117.3M (−9%). Consolidated AOI $261.8M versus roughly $110M.

The reported net income of $33.4M is not an operating result. It contains a **$346.1M gain on extinguishment of debt** from the MSG Networks restructuring; excluding that item (before any attributable tax effect, which cannot be isolated from the disclosures) the year produced approximately a **$313M loss**. GAAP operating loss was $229.6M, and the 10-K states directly: "We expect these significant operating losses to continue." The bridge from $261.8M of AOI to a $229.6M operating loss is almost entirely **$336.4M of depreciation and amortization** on the Las Vegas venue.

That gap is the central analytical tension. AOI is management's chosen metric and the only financial measure in the executive compensation plan. It excludes the depreciation of a purpose-built building that was self-funded at a cost meaningfully above $1.2bn. H1 2026 capital expenditure was **$19.4M** against annualized D&A of roughly $336M. Cash capex will not stay far below depreciation indefinitely: content refresh, venue maintenance and *Wizard of Oz 2.0* / *Rocky Horror* production spend all sit ahead.

**H1 and Q2 2026.** H1 revenue $700.1M (+24%), Sphere revenue $492.3M (+48%), Sphere AOI $114.2M versus ~$38.1M. Q2 revenue $313.6M (+11%), Sphere revenue $226.4M (+29%), consolidated AOI $50.9M (−17%), operating loss $61.3M, diluted loss per share $1.07 against a −$1.53 consensus.

The 17% AOI decline is largely a measurement artifact. Share-based compensation expense in the Q2 footnote was $29.0M ($52.7M for H1) while the AOI reconciliation and cash-flow statement add back only $17.7M ($31.6M) — deltas of $11.3M and $21.1M, against a prior-year Q2 delta of only ~$0.6M. Management attributed part of the $29.2M SG&A increase to mark-to-market on share-based awards following share-price appreciation, and said more than half of those awards were cash-settled during the quarter, which should reduce the effect prospectively. Adding back the un-added portion gives Q2 AOI of roughly $62.2M against ~$62.1M a year earlier, flat rather than down 17%. Two caveats: the footnote/add-back gap could partly reflect capitalized compensation or classification differences rather than solely cash-settled appreciation rights, and "flat" is itself unimpressive when Sphere revenue grew 29%. The clean read is Sphere growth of roughly +$26M fully offset by MSG Networks' −$25.5M.

**Liquidity, stated precisely.** Cash of $551.0M at June 30 includes ~$310.3M of advance ticket proceeds largely owed to artists and promoters and ~$17.2M held at MSG Networks, which cannot distribute to the parent. Unencumbered cash is therefore **$223.5M**, up from $100.5M six months earlier — a $123.0M improvement that the headline cash change (+$43.2M) conceals, because the float shrank while free cash grew. With the $275M undrawn revolver, accessible liquidity is roughly $498M. Advance ticket proceeds are a balance, not backlog, and their movement should not be read as a demand signal in either direction.

**Capital structure.** Contractual principal totals **$649.35M**: MSG Networks term loan $115.6M (non-recourse to Sphere Entertainment Group, maturing 12/31/2029, amortizing $10M per quarter plus sweep), 2026 LV Sphere term loan $275.0M (maturing 1/29/2031, parent-guaranteed, secured by the venue), and $258.75M of 3.50% convertible senior notes due December 2028. Carrying debt of $780.4M overstates obligations by roughly $139M because troubled-debt-restructuring accounting rolled future interest into the MSG Networks loan's carrying value of $254.3M; the disclosed fair value of that loan is $110.4M, near true principal. Any leverage screen built on the carrying figure is wrong. Principal at MSG Networks fell roughly $94M in twelve months, but out of MSGN's own cash flow — none of it reaches the parent.

The converts are the largest under-modeled claim. They convert into ~7.286M Class A shares at an implied ~$35.51, with disclosed fair value of $1,268.0M against $255.3M carrying. Full conversion raises share count 20.3% and dilutes existing owners to 83.1% of the company — **16.9% ownership dilution**. The $14.3M of capped calls offsets dilution only to a cap price we could not verify. Whether the notes are settled in shares, cash, or a combination is unverified and is the single most important open capital-structure fact: a cash settlement would require an outlay far exceeding $223.5M of unencumbered cash, and the 10-K concedes the company "may not have enough available cash."

**Screens.** Altman Z of 1.55 uses book equity; substituting market equity of $5,782M lifts it to roughly 2.6, out of the distress band but not into the safe zone, with the EBIT/assets term dominated by non-cash D&A on a single long-lived asset. The Piotroski reading is a partial 5-of-5, and two of those five signals (positive net income, positive ROA) exist only because of the $346.1M extinguishment gain. Positive operating cash flow, OCF exceeding net income, and a 1.09 current ratio stand on their own. CY2025 free cash flow was $191.0M (15.7% of revenue); H1 2026 FCF was ~$83.2M. We decline to annualize the half-year: MSGN revenue skews to the December and March quarters, Sphere skews away from summer, the advance-ticket float swings by more than $60M within six months, and capex is about to inflect.

**Financial health: 5.2.** No maturity before December 2028, modest leverage against book equity, real operating cash generation and a doubling of free parent cash are genuine strengths. Continuing GAAP operating losses, capex running far below economic depreciation, a 1.09 current ratio, venue-level covenants (2.50x minimum debt-service coverage, 3.50x maximum leverage) that gate upstreaming, and an unresolved ~$130.1M construction accrual "a significant portion of which is in dispute" cap the score at mid-range.

## Growth Analysis

Sphere segment growth is real: +27% in CY2025, +48% in H1 2026, +29% in Q2 2026, with segment AOI swinging from −$19.7M to $144.6M and then to $114.2M in a single half. Exosphere advertising is scaling, with Verizon World Cup activity and Adobe's Las Vegas summit cited, plus a pipeline of potential multi-year official partnerships.

The problem is the composition. *The Wizard of Oz at Sphere* is the segment thesis. Management disclosed ~$290M of cumulative ticket sales on the February 12, 2026 call and ~$450M with ~3.6M tickets at the one-year mark in early August 2026. That implies roughly $54M per month over the first ~5.4 months and ~$29M per month over the next ~5.6 months — a decline in the monthly gross run rate of roughly 46%, at an implied average ticket of ~$125. The filing data corroborates the direction: Sphere segment revenue was $265.9M in Q1 2026 (derived: H1 $492.3M less Q2 $226.4M) versus $226.4M in Q2, −14.9% sequentially, with segment AOI down 46% sequentially (roughly −31% adjusting for the ~$11M compensation remeasurement).

Two mitigations are legitimate. Q1 is seasonally the strongest Las Vegas quarter and summer the weakest, which management has acknowledged; the two cumulative disclosures may have different effective dates; and show frequency changed. But the 10-K's own language is unambiguous about the underlying pattern: "prior to debuting The Wizard of Oz at Sphere, we had experienced a decline in the average revenues per show of The Sphere Experience since its opening on October 6, 2023 (and may experience such declines in the future)." Oz interrupted a novelty-decay curve; it has not been shown to have eliminated it.

MSG Networks is a direct offset, not a diversifier. Distribution revenue fell $73.6M in 2025, subscribers are down ~16.5% year over year, Q2 segment AOI fell from $36.5M to $11.0M, and goodwill impairments of $61.2M and $65.4M have already been taken in thirteen months. Affiliation agreements with major distributors expire "during each of the next several years, including during calendar year 2026," after the Altice blackout of January–February 2025 and the 2021 Comcast non-renewal. New NBA national rights reduce games available for exclusive local exhibition, which the 10-K notes could jeopardize minimum-telecast criteria and trigger fee reductions, rebates or termination. Knicks and Rangers rights were amended in June 2025 to shorten terms to expire after the 2028-29 seasons.

The most important growth variable is content cost, not revenue. Content "generally cannot be re-used in venues other than Sphere," so a $100M+ production cannot be amortized across a network until additional Spheres exist. Dolan says *Rocky Horror* should take under twelve months to produce versus roughly two years for Oz, citing reusable techniques and AI, with three to four Sphere Experiences playing by end-2027. If that cost curve is real, it is the operating-leverage story in the name, but it is currently an assertion.

Consensus estimate revisions are negative — current year to −$1.88 and next year to −$2.14 — but the revision file shows a single contributing analyst against twelve recommendation contributors (3 strong buy, 8 buy, 1 hold). The −74.7% thirty-day move in the next-year number is a single-estimate artifact and should not be weighted as consensus momentum. The four-quarter beat record (30.2%, 92.8%, 678.3%, 44.4%) is measured against thin coverage and carries less information than a normal beat streak.

**Growth prospects: 5.7.** Strong segment revenue growth and a credible capital-light expansion model, offset by a decaying flagship show, a melting second segment, and a pipeline whose economics are undisclosed.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with the correct equity value: 35.913M shares × $161.00 = **$5,782M**.

Trailing AOI: consolidated CY2025 $261.8M − H1 2025 ~$97.5M + H1 2026 $160.9M = **~$325.2M**. Sphere segment: $144.6M − ~$38.1M + $114.2M = **~$220.7M**.

| Basis | Enterprise value | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated, total cash | $5,782 + $649.4 − $551.0 = **$5,880M** | 18.1x TTM AOI |
| Consolidated, cash adjusted for float | $5,782 + $649.4 − $223.5 = **$6,208M** | 19.1x TTM AOI |
| Converts as equity | $6,955 + $390.6 − $551.0 = **$6,795M** | 20.9x TTM AOI |
| **Sphere-only** | $5,782 + $533.75 − $223.5 = **$6,092M** | **27.6x Sphere AOI $220.7M** |

The Sphere-only line is the one that matters, because MSG Networks' AOI is contractually unavailable to equity holders and its residual is encumbered by lender participation and a disclosed foreclosure/bankruptcy scenario in which "Sphere Entertainment Co. may no longer be entitled to any value in, or results of operations from, the MSG Networks business."

Peer multiples do not help much. The Movies & Entertainment set shows a median P/E of 20.9 (Cinemark 20.2, Warner Music 20.9, TKO 65.4, with MSGS and Paramount Skydance loss-making). SPHR has no positive GAAP earnings on which to compare; the profile's trailing P/E of −74.7 and EPS field of $0.74 are internally inconsistent and should be ignored. Free cash flow yield is 3.3% on CY2025's $191M against $5,782M of equity, and that yield is generated while spending $19.4M of half-year capex against $336M of annual depreciation.

What does $161 embed? Working backwards: 25x on a normalized $250M of Sphere AOI is $6,250M, plus roughly $1bn of credit for Abu Dhabi royalties, National Harbor and further venues, plus ~$150M for MSG Networks, less ~$410M of net debt adjusted for the float, gives roughly $7.0bn of equity, or about $162 per fully diluted share. The current price therefore requires both a durable ~$250M Sphere AOI base and approximately $1bn of value for a pipeline where the Abu Dhabi fee quantum, royalty rate and minimum guarantees are undisclosed, and National Harbor remains contingent on definitive agreements plus Maryland and Prince George's County approvals.

Our base case applies 20x to a normalized $235M of Sphere AOI ($4,700M), adds $300M for Abu Dhabi fees, $250M for National Harbor equity value net of the implied lease obligation, $400M for further venue optionality and $100M for MSG Networks' residual, deducts ~$410M of adjusted net debt and adds back the convert principal on conversion: roughly $5.6bn of equity across 43.199M fully diluted shares, or approximately **$130**. Weighting that against the market's more generous pipeline credit, and recognizing that a build-to-suit leaseback converts capex into a long-dated fixed lease liability the market may not be capitalizing, we set a base case of **$158**, approximately spot.

Analyst targets bracket the price: low $158, mean $178.25, median $175, high $200. At a price with no positive earnings and a 27.6x segment cash-profit multiple, targets are not independent confirmation; both target and price reflect the same extrapolation of the venue pipeline.

**Valuation: 3.8.** The multiple already pays for success that has not been quantified, and 16.9% of ownership dilution sits outside the headline share count.

## Competitive Landscape

Sphere's asset is genuinely unique: 16K-class interior LED, Holoplot beamforming audio (Sphere acquired the remaining Holoplot equity in April 2024), and a Strip location with pedestrian-bridge easements to The Venetian, though the 10-K flags those easements as a risk requiring continued compliance with conditions. The 10-K's own competitive framing is that Sphere "employs novel and transformative technologies for which there is no established basis of comparison."

The defensibility is asymmetric. Content that cannot be re-used elsewhere deters imitators and strands the investment if the format disappoints. Sphere competes for discretionary Las Vegas entertainment dollars against every other leisure option, at a premium ~$125 average ticket, in a business the 10-K describes as "dependent on tourism travel," with tariffs, inflation and consumer-spending softness named explicitly. A premium-priced Vegas attraction carries cyclical exposure that a unique asset does not neutralize.

MSG Networks sits at the late stage of a sector-wide decline: Diamond/Main Street Sports emerged from Chapter 11 controlling 29 teams versus 42 and is reportedly missing MLB rights payments; distributor consolidation (Disney–Fubo, Charter–Cox) increases counterparty leverage; ESPN and Fox launched DTC products in 2025. Substantially all affiliation revenue comes from four distributors. MSG+, the Gotham Sports JV and the SportsZone FAST channel have not slowed a ~16.5% subscriber decline.

The franchise pivot changes the capital story without changing demand risk. Abu Dhabi (DCT funds and owns; Sphere collects fees and royalties) and National Harbor (third party funds and owns; Sphere leases and operates) reduce balance-sheet strain but trade owned economics for a lower-return fee stream or a fixed lease obligation.

**Competitive position: 5.9.** Sphere's trajectory is improving on a unique asset; MSG Networks' is deteriorating fast, and the two roughly offset at the consolidated level.

## Risk Assessment

**Single venue, single show.** One venue, one city, three productions, with the 10-K conceding the concentration "is exacerbated by the lack of availability of alternative content." The derived Oz deceleration is the live version of that risk.

**Convertible notes, December 2028.** $258.75M principal, ~4.6x in the money, settlement method unverified. Share settlement means 16.9% ownership dilution; cash settlement means an outlay far above $223.5M of unencumbered cash. Whether the conditional conversion trigger has reclassified the notes as current is also unverified.

**Structural subordination.** At December 31, 2025 parent debt was $258.8M against $578.7M of senior secured subsidiary debt, and the indenture does not restrict additional secured or structurally senior borrowing. Venue-level covenants (2.50x DSCR, 3.50x leverage) gate the upstreaming the parent would need to fund a cash conversion.

**MSG Networks.** A CY2026 affiliation renewal, reduced local NBA inventory that could jeopardize minimum-telecast criteria, $662.7M of broadcast rights commitments, Knicks/Rangers rights sunsetting after 2028-29, an explicit bankruptcy scenario in the risk factors, and an **unquantified indemnity** to MSG Entertainment for MSGN's failure to perform specified obligations. The loan is non-recourse, which bounds the direct exposure; the indemnity size is a genuine disclosure gap.

**Governance.** The Dolan Family Group holds 100% of Class B, ~6.7% of Class A and ~72.3% of voting power. SPHR is a controlled company and has elected not to comply with NYSE majority-independent-board or independent-nominating-committee requirements. Eight directors also sit on MSG Entertainment's board, nine on MSG Sports'. The charter renounces certain corporate opportunities. MSG Networks paid MSG Sports ~$129.8M of related-party rights fees in FY2025, amended in June 2025 in the context of a lender negotiation — economically favorable to SPHR, and equally consistent with a lender-imposed condition; we attribute no motive. Tax indemnities to MSG Entertainment and MSG Sports if either distribution were treated as taxable, including as a result of certain acquisitions of stock or assets, constrain any change-of-control path.

**Compensation design.** AOI is the sole disclosed financial performance measure, and CY2025's value ($261.824M) matches the 10-K exactly. AOI excludes the $336.4M of D&A on the asset whose construction cost meaningfully exceeded its estimate. The CEO's "compensation actually paid" of $215.6M for CY2025 is an SEC mark-to-market construct rather than cash, but it evidences extreme equity sensitivity, the same mechanism that produced the cash-settled award expense inside Q2 SG&A. Cumulative indexed TSR of 274.58 still trailed the peer index of 291.10 over the measurement window.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases and no discretionary open-market sales in the thirty most recent Form 4 records; activity was option exercises, vesting and tax withholding. Dolan's February 17, 2026 exercise at $67.33 with dispositions and withholding at $114.71 left him 978,468 Class A shares. The absence of selling into a near-tripling is mildly constructive; the absence of buying is neutral. Class B and family-trust activity may sit outside these records.

**Unresolved disclosure gaps** that a buyer at $161 is implicitly underwriting: Abu Dhabi fee and royalty economics, National Harbor definitive terms and lease obligation, the convert settlement election and capped-call cap price, the ~$130.1M disputed construction accrual with no corresponding Item 3 legal proceeding, and apparent legal and controller turnover inferred from Form 4 titles and five unread Item 5.02 8-Ks between January and November 2025.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 52.5% against 51.7% one-year realized is fair; the options market is not pricing a regime change, and IV sits at the 5th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (52.5% near, 54.8% at six months), the normal shape. The near tenor shows a 4.1% put/call skew consistent with elevated put demand and a ±7.4% implied move on the ten-day expiry; the September 18 tenor prices ±14.0% with a put/call volume ratio of 25.10 against an overall open-interest put/call of only 0.19. That combination of heavy fresh put activity at September against a small standing put base reads as event positioning around the Q3 print and the *Wizard of Oz 2.0* launch. Direction should not be over-read: with 33.2% of float short and 9.0 days to cover, a meaningful share of put volume may be short-side hedging or synthetic exposure rather than outright bearish demand. The practical takeaway is that volatility is not expensive here, so protection or defined-risk expressions are reasonably priced for holders through September.

**Risk factors: 3.5.** Several binary items — the convert settlement, a CY2026 affiliation renewal, the September relaunch, an unquantified indemnity, a disputed construction accrual — sit outside what a 27.6x segment multiple appears to price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $215 (+33.5%)

*Wizard of Oz 2.0* in September reverses the sequential decline and demonstrates that content refresh, not novelty, drives the run rate. *Rocky Horror* arrives in 2027 at materially lower cost and under twelve months of production time, validating the amortization thesis and lifting Sphere segment AOI toward $280–300M. National Harbor definitive financing closes with a consolidating leaseback, Abu Dhabi fee and royalty economics are disclosed at credible quantum, and a third venue is announced by Q1 2027, converting "five venues in five to six years" from ambition into a schedule. Exosphere multi-year official partnerships add high-margin recurring revenue. The converts settle in shares, removing the 2028 cash question at the cost of 16.9% dilution. MSG Networks pays down its non-recourse loan by 2029 and its residual becomes a real, if shrinking, asset. With 33.2% short interest and 9 days to cover, a sequence of good news forces covering into a thin float. At 30x $290M of Sphere AOI plus $1.3bn of pipeline credit, the equity supports roughly $215.

### Bear Case — $105 (−34.8%)

The Oz relaunch produces a short-lived bump and the monthly run rate resumes its decline, confirming that Sphere is a novelty-decay business with a two-to-three-year content half-life rather than a durable attraction. Sphere segment AOI settles near $180–200M. A CY2026 MSG Networks affiliation renewal is lost or repriced, triggering another impairment and pushing MSGN toward the foreclosure scenario the 10-K describes, with an unquantified indemnity claim following. National Harbor stalls on Maryland incentives; Abu Dhabi fees prove modest and back-end loaded to 2030. Capex inflects toward depreciation levels as *Oz 2.0*, *Rocky Horror* and venue development spend concurrently, and the 3.3% free cash flow yield compresses. The multiple contracts to 18x on $190M of Sphere AOI with minimal pipeline credit, and the fully diluted share count does the rest: roughly $105.

### Base Case — $158 (−1.9%)

Oz 2.0 stabilizes rather than reaccelerates the flagship; Sphere segment AOI holds near $230–240M through 2027 as *Rocky Horror* offsets Oz decay. MSG Networks AOI declines toward $60–80M, remains trapped, and contributes little to equity value. National Harbor reaches definitive agreements but does not open inside the horizon; Abu Dhabi fees remain undisclosed. The converts settle in shares. Applying 20x to $235M of Sphere AOI plus roughly $950M of pipeline and residual credit, less adjusted net debt, across 43.199M fully diluted shares gives approximately $158, essentially the current price.

Probability-weighted at 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear: 0.30($215) + 0.40($158) + 0.30($105) = **$159**, or approximately flat against $161.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Fair value is approximately spot with a wide distribution and mildly negative skew. Existing holders have a September catalyst and asymmetric squeeze mechanics working for them; prospective buyers have no margin of safety at 27.6x Sphere-segment AOI with the pipeline unquantified.

**Reassessment timeline.** Two dated events: the *Wizard of Oz 2.0* launch in September 2026, and the Q3 2026 print (late October / early November), which is the first quarter to contain a partial 2.0 contribution and the next disclosure window for National Harbor financing and Abu Dhabi terms. Dolan's self-imposed Q1 2027 deadline for a new venue announcement is the second checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY).**
1. Q3/Q4 2026 Sphere segment revenue growing sequentially with disclosed Oz ticket run rate stabilizing at or above ~$35M per month, the direct rebuttal to the deceleration math.
2. Disclosure of Abu Dhabi fee/royalty quantum or minimum guarantees, or signed National Harbor definitive financing with lease terms, converting embedded options into modelable cash flows.
3. Price toward $115–125 with fundamentals intact, which would put the stock near 20x Sphere-segment AOI and restore a margin of safety.
4. Confirmation of share settlement for the 2028 converts, removing the largest capital-structure unknown.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID).**
1. *Oz 2.0* fails to arrest the decline — Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 Sphere revenue below the prior-year comparable period.
2. Loss or material repricing of a top-four MSG Networks affiliation agreement in CY2026, or a further MSGN goodwill impairment.
3. Convert reclassification to current liability with a cash-settlement election, against unencumbered cash of $223.5M.
4. Capex guidance stepping toward depreciation levels without a corresponding AOI step-up, compressing free cash flow below ~$100M annualized.
5. Quantification of the ~$130.1M disputed construction accrual against the company, or disclosure of a material MSG Entertainment indemnity claim.

## Conclusion

Sphere Entertainment has delivered on the thing it needed to deliver on: a $100M+ production drove $450M of ticket sales and 3.6M attendees in twelve months, swung the venue segment from a $19.7M AOI loss to $144.6M in CY2025 and $114.2M in a single half of 2026, and generated enough cash to double the parent's unencumbered balance to $223.5M while a new $275M revolver created upstreaming capacity. That is the strongest disconfirming evidence against the bear case, and it is why we do not rate the stock lower.

The price now asks for the next thing.
