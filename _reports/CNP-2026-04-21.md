## Executive Summary

CenterPoint Energy is a Houston-based regulated utility holding company with ~$9.4B in annual revenue, $20.6B in long-term debt, and a $65B+ 10-year capital investment plan. The company's core asset is Houston Electric, a transmission and distribution franchise positioned in one of the largest electricity demand growth areas in the country, driven by data center buildout, population migration, and electrification in the ERCOT market. Management has delivered 8-9% annual EPS growth four out of the past five years, placing CNP at the high end of regulated utility peers.

The investment case is straightforward: massive rate base growth funded by a $65B+ capital plan translates to sustained above-peer EPS growth, supported by constructive regulation and structural demand tailwinds. The problem is that at all-time highs (~16x forward EPS of ~$1.90), the market has already priced a significant portion of this story. The company runs a structural ~$2.4B annual free cash flow deficit, requiring continuous access to debt and equity markets. A recent $600M convertible notes offering illustrates both the creativity and the necessity of this financing strategy. Hurricane exposure in Houston, interest rate sensitivity on a $20.6B debt stack, and uncertainty around actual data center demand materialization in ERCOT create meaningful downside scenarios that the current valuation does not adequately compensate for.

CNP is a well-run utility executing a credible growth strategy, but the risk/reward at current levels favors patience. A pullback toward $39-41 (14x forward EPS) or clarity on data center demand materialization would create a more attractive entry point.

## Company Overview

CenterPoint Energy operates through three segments: Electric (Houston Electric T&D and Indiana Electric integrated utility), Natural Gas (distribution across six states: Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Mississippi, Ohio, Texas), and Corporate/Other. The company has approximately 8,300 employees and a 140+ year operating history.

Houston Electric is a wires-only T&D business operating within ERCOT, the deregulated Texas wholesale market. CenterPoint does not own generation in Texas; it earns regulated returns on transmission and distribution infrastructure investment. This asset structure means the company captures demand growth through increased capital deployment into rate base, without commodity price exposure.

The natural gas distribution business provides geographic and regulatory diversification across six state jurisdictions, though it contributes less to the growth story than Houston Electric. Indiana Electric adds a small vertically integrated utility with generation assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 operating revenues reached $9.36B (+8.3% YoY), with adjusted EPS of $1.76 (+8.6% YoY). Q4 2025 adjusted EPS of $0.45 missed consensus by $0.01, though the full-year delivery was strong. CEO Jason Wells has emphasized that 9% EPS growth has been achieved "four out of the last five years," which is strong execution for a regulated utility.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow improved 16.4% to $2.49B in FY2025. However, capital expenditures of $4.87B resulted in a free cash flow deficit of approximately $2.4B. This deficit is structural and intentional: management is deploying capital aggressively to grow rate base, which flows into regulated earnings. But it means the company must raise $2-3B annually in external capital (debt, equity, convertibles) for the foreseeable future.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt of $20.6B against operating income of $2.11B yields a debt-to-operating-income ratio of ~9.8x. Interest expense of $882M consumed approximately 35% of operating income and grew 7.8% YoY, reflecting the cost of the capital plan. Cash on hand of $38M is negligible; the company is entirely dependent on credit facilities and capital market access for liquidity. This is typical for large regulated utilities but creates acute vulnerability to any disruption in capital market access.

**2026 Guidance:** Management guided non-GAAP EPS to at least the midpoint of $1.89-$1.91, representing ~8% growth over FY2025. The 10-year capital plan was increased by $500M to $65B+, signaling continued confidence in the Houston Electric growth opportunity.

**Capital Markets Activity:** In late February 2026, CenterPoint priced an upsized $600M offering of 2.875% convertible senior notes due 2029. The low coupon reduces near-term cash interest expense but introduces equity dilution risk upon conversion. Management needs capital but wants to limit immediate dilution.

**Dividend:** The quarterly dividend of $0.23/share ($0.92 annualized) implies a yield of approximately 2.0-2.2% at recent prices. For a utility, this is below-average yield, reflecting the company's prioritization of reinvestment over shareholder distributions.

## Growth Analysis

CenterPoint's growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Houston Electric Data Center Demand:** ERCOT's latest forecasts project peak demand could quadruple by 2032, from ~85 GW to ~368 GW, driven primarily by data centers. ERCOT itself has cautioned this estimate is "likely too high and must be revised," but even a fraction of this growth would be transformational for Houston Electric's rate base. Houston is a major data center hub due to Texas's business-friendly regulation, low energy costs, abundant land, and increasingly robust fiber/power infrastructure. As the T&D provider, CenterPoint captures every megawatt of incremental demand through additional infrastructure investment.

**2. Population and Electrification Growth:** Houston continues to attract population growth, with the metro area among the fastest-growing in the country. Transportation electrification (EVs), building electrification, and general economic expansion in Texas provide demand tailwinds independent of data centers.

**3. Rate Base Compounding:** The $65B+ 10-year capital plan translates to sustained 8-10% rate base growth. In a regulated utility framework, rate base growth directly translates to earnings growth, assuming regulatory outcomes remain constructive. Management has a track record of securing timely rate relief across multiple jurisdictions.

The key uncertainty is magnitude: if even 25-30% of ERCOT's projected data center demand materializes in CenterPoint's service territory, the $65B plan could prove conservative. If data center demand disappoints (projects are delayed, relocate, or demand growth is absorbed by existing capacity), the latter years of the capital plan could see diminishing returns.

## Valuation Assessment

At a market capitalization of ~$28-29B and FY2026 guided EPS of ~$1.90, CNP trades at approximately 16x forward earnings. For context:

- **Utility sector median forward P/E:** 14-16x for regulated peers growing EPS at 5-7%
- **Premium growth utilities (Entergy, NextEra):** 17-20x forward
- **CenterPoint's growth rate (8-9%):** Justifies some premium to sector median

At 16x forward, CNP is priced at the upper boundary of fair value for its growth profile. The stock hit all-time highs in March 2026, up ~24% over the trailing 12 months. The Zacks Rank #4 (Sell) rating as of early 2026 suggests near-term valuation concerns from quantitative models.

The convertible notes add a dilution overhang. If the stock remains above the conversion price, existing shareholders face ~2-3% dilution, a real cost that reduces per-share value of the growth story.

On a DCF basis, 8% EPS growth for 5 years from $1.90, discounted at an 8-9% cost of equity with a 4% terminal growth rate, suggests fair value of approximately $44-48 per share. The stock at ~$44-46 is within this range, leaving limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

CenterPoint's primary competitive advantage is geographic: Houston Electric is a regulated monopoly franchise in one of the fastest-growing load centers in the United States. No competitor can replicate this asset.

Among regulated utility peers (DTE Energy, Duke Energy, Entergy, AES), CenterPoint's 8-9% EPS growth target sits at the high end. Most large regulated utilities target 5-7% growth. This premium growth rate justifies a valuation premium, but at 16x forward, that premium appears largely priced.

The natural gas distribution business is a solid but undifferentiated portfolio across six states. Multi-state regulatory complexity adds operational overhead but provides geographic diversification. The gas business will likely face longer-term secular headwinds from electrification and building decarbonization, though the transition timeline is measured in decades, and gas infrastructure will be needed throughout.

## Risk Assessment

**Hurricane/Weather Exposure:** CenterPoint's most acute risk. The 2024 derecho and Hurricane Beryl left parts of Houston without power for days, generating severe reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny. The Climavision partnership and grid hardening investments are constructive, but Houston's Gulf Coast location creates permanent, recurring exposure to tropical systems. A major hurricane striking Houston before resilience investments are complete could cause extended outages, regulatory backlash, and storm cost recovery disputes. Current elevated oil prices and geopolitical instability add complexity to post-storm recovery logistics.

**Financing Risk:** The structural FCF deficit requires continuous capital market access. With only $38M in cash, any disruption (credit downgrade, capital market freeze, or sustained rate increases) would directly impair the company's ability to execute its growth plan. In the current macro environment where the Fed is holding at 3.64% with inflation trending above 4%, refinancing maturing debt at higher rates would compress earnings. Every 50bp increase in the average cost of debt on the $20.6B stack adds ~$100M in annual interest expense.

**Data Center Demand Uncertainty:** ERCOT's own quadrupling forecast has been characterized as "likely too high." The capital plan's later years embed assumptions about demand growth that may not fully materialize. Data center construction timelines, power availability constraints, and evolving AI hardware efficiency could all reduce the magnitude of actual demand growth.

**Regulatory Risk:** Texas political dynamics remain volatile post-Winter Storm Uri. While CenterPoint's T&D operations are regulated, broader ERCOT market changes could affect cost recovery, reliability requirements, or competitive dynamics. Operating across six state regulatory jurisdictions adds complexity and execution risk.

**Equity Dilution:** The convertible notes offering is the visible portion of what will be years of additional capital raises. Investors should expect periodic equity dilution (ATM programs, secondary offerings, additional convertibles) as a permanent cost of the high-growth strategy.

**Inflation Pass-Through Lag:** While regulated utilities can eventually recover cost increases through rate cases, the lag between cost increases (materials, labor for construction) and rate relief approvals creates periods of margin compression. In a sustained >4% inflation environment, construction cost escalation on the $65B capital plan could meaningfully exceed initial estimates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$52-55, ~15-20% upside from ~$45)
- Data center demand in ERCOT materializes at or above current forecasts, validating and potentially expanding the $65B capital plan
- Houston Electric rate base grows at 10%+ annually, driving EPS above the 8-9% guidance range
- Regulatory outcomes remain constructive across all jurisdictions
- Interest rates decline, reducing financing costs and lowering the cost of capital for the growth plan
- No major hurricane disruption through 2027-2028, allowing grid resilience investments to mature
- The stock re-rates to 18-19x forward earnings, consistent with premium growth utility multiples

### Bear Case (~$35-37, ~18-22% downside)
- Data center demand disappoints: projects delay, relocate, or demand is absorbed by existing capacity
- A major hurricane strikes Houston before resilience investments are complete, causing regulatory backlash and storm cost recovery disputes
- Interest rates rise further, compressing returns on incremental capital deployment and increasing financing costs
- Regulatory environment becomes less constructive (e.g., lower authorized ROEs, disallowance of capital spending)
- Capital market disruption limits access to financing, forcing capital plan reductions
- The stock de-rates to 12-13x forward earnings, reflecting reduced growth expectations and higher perceived risk

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $39-41 range (14x forward EPS), providing a margin of safety that compensates for financing and weather risk
- ERCOT releases revised demand forecasts showing validated data center interconnection queues converting to actual load at rates above 50%
- Interest rates decline meaningfully (10-year Treasury below 3.5%), reducing financing costs for the capital plan

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- A major hurricane causes extended Houston outages (>5 days for >500K customers), triggering regulatory investigations or rate case disallowances
- Management reduces or defers the capital plan by >15%, signaling demand disappointment or financing constraints
- Credit rating downgrade to below investment grade, or loss of capital market access for >60 days
- FY2026 EPS comes in below $1.80 (more than 5% below guidance midpoint)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next reassessment point is Q1 2026 earnings (late April/May 2026), followed by Atlantic hurricane season (June-November 2026), which will test both physical infrastructure and investor confidence.

## Conclusion

CenterPoint Energy is executing a credible, differentiated growth strategy anchored by Houston Electric's structural demand tailwinds. The 8-9% EPS growth trajectory is well above regulated utility peers, and the $65B capital plan provides a clear path to sustained rate base compounding. Management has delivered consistently.

The challenge is price. At ~16x forward earnings and all-time highs, the stock reflects a significant portion of the growth story. The structural free cash flow deficit, hurricane exposure, and data center demand uncertainty create downside scenarios that the current valuation does not adequately buffer against. In an environment where inflation is running above 4% and rates are held at 3.64% with the possibility of further increases, a company that must raise $2-3B annually in external capital faces meaningful cost-of-capital headwinds.

The appropriate posture is to recognize the quality of the franchise and the growth opportunity while waiting for a more attractive entry point. A pullback into the $39-41 range, driven by weather events, broader market weakness, or data center demand uncertainty, would create a compelling risk/reward. Until then, existing holders should maintain positions for the growth trajectory, but new capital is better deployed elsewhere.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Growth Hold)**
