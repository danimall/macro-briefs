## Executive Summary

IAC is no longer meaningfully valued as an operating company. At ~$3.14B of market capitalization, the company's 26.1% stake in MGM Resorts International — worth approximately **$3.24B** at the $48.30 per share cash price IAC itself proposed on June 1, 2026 — accounts for roughly 103% of the equity value. Adding back $441M of year-end net debt and subtracting the stake leaves an implied enterprise value of roughly **$347M** for a portfolio comprising the People Inc. publishing business (People, Better Homes & Gardens, Allrecipes, Investopedia, Verywell, Southern Living), the residual Search arbitrage segment, Care.com, Vivian Health and The Daily Beast — about 0.15× FY2025 consolidated revenue of $2,393.2M. The stake is encumbered by an unquantified deferred tax liability (cost basis dates to a 2020 purchase); applying an assumed ~$1.0B basis and ~25% effective rate lifts the implied stub to roughly **$883–933M**, or ~0.37× revenue. Either way, the operating businesses are valued at a level that implies limited terminal value.

The problem is that the discount now has a structural explanation rather than being an unexplained anomaly, and the mechanism that would close it may transfer value away from IAC's minority holders. IAC has proposed acquiring the 73.9% of MGM it does not own at a total transaction value of ~$18B including assumed debt. The derived third-party cash requirement is roughly **$9.16B** against $960.2M of parent cash at year-end 2025. Financing is described as a combination of cash at both companies, incremental debt and equity funding, with only "preliminary conversations" held with equity investors as of the proposal. Whether new equity is issued at the IAC level (diluting shareholders directly) or at an acquisition-vehicle level (diluting IAC's proportional claim on MGM) is not established. Barry Diller and former IAC CEO Joey Levin both sit on MGM's board; MGM's board formed a special committee of independent directors on or about July 31, 2026 and has said it views the bid as undervalued.

Compounding this, consensus 2026 EPS has moved from **+$0.49 to -$0.54** over 90 days across eight analysts, with the current quarter cut from -$0.15 to -$0.33 in the last 30 days alone, while 2027 estimates were left effectively unchanged at $1.17. That pattern — this year impaired, next year intact — is consistent with front-loaded rebranding, consolidation and transaction costs, but it also carries the familiar risk that the recovery year is the one that has not yet been tested. At $44.53 against $1.17 of 2027 consensus, the stock trades at ~38x next-year earnings. There is no earnings-based valuation support here; the case is entirely asset-based. That asset backing is substantial enough that the stock is not shortable, and unresolved enough that new capital has no edge. **HOLD, no new capital ahead of the Q2 disclosure.**

## Company Overview

IAC is Barry Diller's holding company, historically a serial spin-off vehicle (Expedia, TripAdvisor, Match Group, Vimeo, and most recently Angi in 2025). Following the Angi separation, the reporting entity comprises:

1. **People Inc.** (formerly Dotdash Meredith) — scaled digital and print publishing across home, food, health, finance and lifestyle verticals. Renamed in October 2025; the parent announced in April 2026 that it would adopt the same name by its Q2 2026 earnings, consolidate corporate functions and reduce staff. Neil Vogel, who built the digital business, becomes CEO of the renamed parent.
2. **Search** — residual paid-search arbitrage properties (Ask.com, Reference.com, Shopping.net) plus downloadable desktop applications. Structurally declining.
3. **Emerging & Other** — Care.com, Vivian Health, The Daily Beast, IAC Films.
4. **A 26.1% equity interest in MGM Resorts International**, acquired beginning in 2020, plus other equity securities (a Turo position has been previously disclosed; current status unverified).

Employee count is 5,156. The headline revenue series — $5,235.3M (2022), $4,365.2M (2023), $3,807.2M (2024), $2,393.2M (2025) — reads as a four-year collapse but is dominated by changes in the reporting entity. The FY2024 figure as originally filed includes Angi; FY2025 excludes it. Corroborating balance-sheet evidence for deconsolidation rather than deterioration: goodwill fell $1,085.6M (from $2,877.1M to $1,791.5M) and long-term debt fell $530.5M (from $1,931.8M to $1,401.3M) year over year, magnitudes consistent with removing a subsidiary that carried its own goodwill and its own notes. **The -37.1% headline is predominantly a change in what is being consolidated.** Any screen ranking IAC on reported revenue trend is measuring the spin-off.

No Adjusted EBITDA figure, leverage multiple or EV/EBITDA metric in this report is constructed from it, and the market's frequently cited "EBITDA" for this name should be sourced from the actual reconciliation rather than inferred.

## Growth Analysis

The organic signal in the largest business is positive and is the strongest single fact in the bull case. Per reporting on the FY2025 release, **People Inc. Q4 2025 digital revenue rose 14% year over year to $354.8M, and full-year digital revenue rose 10%** (implied Q4 2024 digital ≈ $311.2M, derived). Achieving double-digit digital growth in 2025 while the referral-traffic base for scaled publishers contracted is an outlier result, and reporting attributes it to Vogel having built the business without reliance on Google traffic.

Two constraints on how much weight that deserves. First, revenue growth with unknown session trends is consistent with either traffic holding up **or** traffic declining while revenue per session rises through premium direct-sold advertising, first-party data targeting, content licensing and commerce. Those two worlds have very different durability, and no session, pageview or unique-visitor data is available. Traffic stability is not inferred from revenue growth here. Second, all operating datapoints are from FY2025 or earlier; Q1 2026 (8-K filed May 4) and what is likely Q2 2026 (8-K filed August 3) were not available for review.

Against that, the consensus revision pattern is the clearest quantitative growth signal available and it is negative:

The benign reading is that rebranding, corporate consolidation, restructuring and bid-related expenses are being recognized in 2026 with no read-through to run-rate earnings, which is consistent with 2027 estimates holding. The adverse reading is that the operating businesses are absorbing more cost than expected while the recovery year remains an estimate. Analyst sentiment has drifted with it: strong buys 3→2 and buys 7→6 over three months, with holds 3→4.

Structural composition also caps the growth rate. Search is in secular decline as an arbitrage model; print is in secular decline; Care.com and Vivian Health are subscale relative to the publishing business. Consolidated growth will therefore lag digital growth even if digital sustains 8–10%. Growth prospects score **4.5** — a genuinely good digital franchise trend, offset by declining segments, a collapsed current-year consensus, and no verifiable 2026 data.

## Valuation Assessment

**The reported multiples do not reconcile and should not be used as shown.** A forward P/E of 16.3 implies EPS of roughly $2.73 at $44.53 — a figure inconsistent with both the -$0.54 FY2026 and $1.17 FY2027 consensus. On consensus, the stock trades at a loss this year and **~38x FY2027 EPS**. Trailing P/E of 25.9 is likewise unreliable against a GAAP net loss of $104.0M. The peer table supplied is not usable for relative valuation: Zillow at 134.6x, Kanzhun at 13.2x, Baidu at negative earnings and Nebius at 76.8x describe four unrelated business models, and the 76.8x median carries no information for a print-and-digital publisher with a casino stake.

The valuation case is therefore sum-of-the-parts, and it is genuinely wide:

**Gross calculation.** MGM stake of 26.1% × $12.4B equity value at the $48.30 proposal = **$3.24B**, versus $3.14B of market capitalization — roughly 103%. Implied MGM shares held ≈ 67.0M (derived from $12.4B ÷ $48.30 × 26.1%; both inputs are rounded press figures and could be off by several percent). Implied stub enterprise value = $3,142M + $441M − $3,236M ≈ **$347M**, or 0.15× FY2025 revenue.

**Three haircuts materially change the picture:**

1. **Deferred tax.** The stake was built beginning in 2020 and carries a large embedded gain. Using an assumed ~$1.0B cost basis and ~25% combined effective rate — both assumptions, not facts — deferred tax of roughly $550–600M reduces the after-tax stake value to ~$2.65–2.70B and lifts the implied stub to **~$883–933M**, or ~0.37× revenue.
2. **MGM's price is bid-inflated.** MGM closed at $50.64 on June 2, above the offer, and traded as high as only $48.25 by July 13. Neither price is a clean standalone mark.
3. **Net debt is as of December 31, 2025** — seven months stale, excluding H1 2026 buybacks, restructuring cash costs and bid expenses.

**A base-case build.** Taking the after-tax stake at ~$2.68B, valuing the operating portfolio at 0.6–0.8× FY2025 revenue ($1.4–1.9B; midpoint $1.65B) and deducting $441M of net debt gives equity of roughly **$3.9B**, or **$52–55 per share** at 71–74M shares. Applying a holding-company discount for the controlled structure, the non-controlled nature of the MGM position and the absence of a stated monetization path, a defensible base case is **$50** — which happens to be the analyst median. Consensus mean is $52.18; the range runs $44 to $66, with the low target essentially at spot.

Where this stops being a clean value case is the mechanism. A discount closes when the holder sells the asset, distributes it, or is bought. Here the controlling shareholder is proposing to do the opposite — to increase concentration in the discounted asset, using a structure that leaves People Inc. at just over 50% post-close, funded partly by MGM's own balance-sheet cash, partly by incremental debt, and partly by co-investors whose commitments were preliminary at the time of the proposal. If a raised price is required to satisfy MGM's special committee, IAC overpays for the 73.9% it does not own. If equity is issued at the IAC level, current holders are diluted directly. If it is issued at the vehicle level, IAC's proportional claim on MGM is diluted instead. None of these paths obviously narrows the discount, and two of them widen it. If the bid fails, the stake reverts to a passive, tax-encumbered, non-controlled position and the strategic pivot stalls.

Valuation scores **5.5** — down from the prior review's 6.5. The asset backing is real and the stub is cheap, but the earnings basis is unsupportive at ~38x FY2027, the discount has persisted for years without a closing mechanism, and the mechanism now under way carries an unquantified risk of transferring value away from minority holders. Cheapness without a value-realization path has a poor record.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining industry dynamic is mechanical: Google AI Overviews and conversational assistants answer informational queries inline, so the search result that historically produced a click to a publisher's page increasingly does not. Corroborating datapoints from the past twelve months: Google was reported to have internally described the open web as in "rapid decline" while publicly disputing that framing; Google has been reported to condition publisher participation in a new AI partnership on granting AI training rights to their content (Google is the party offering access and seeking rights; publishers supply content and decide whether to grant them); and major publishers including Reddit and Politico have been reported weighing whether they can work with Google at all as AI substitutes for traditional search.

People Inc.'s +10% FY2025 and +14% Q4 digital revenue growth is a genuine outperformance against that backdrop, and the owned-and-operated brand portfolio (People, Better Homes & Gardens, Allrecipes, Investopedia, Verywell) supports direct traffic, email and app relationships plus premium direct-sold inventory that is less dependent on search referrals than a pure SEO publisher. Those advantages are asserted from reporting and prior disclosure rather than confirmed against the current Business section.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is ambiguous and arguably negative for two reasons. First, the revenue-versus-traffic ambiguity described above means the growth could be yield expansion on a shrinking audience, which is a less durable configuration. Second, and more telling, the company's own capital allocation implies a view. Diller's June 1 filing praised MGM as "a rare kind of business" with real-world assets that artificial intelligence "cannot easily replicate or disintermediate," while People Inc. cut 6% of publishing headcount in October 2025 and the parent announced further corporate consolidation and layoffs in April 2026 — and proposed deploying ~$9.2B into physical casino assets. The parsimonious reading is a rotation out of AI-exposed digital media into AI-resistant physical assets. The alternative reading is that Diller has been an MGM shareholder since 2020, sees a cheap gaming asset in a hot M&A market (Fertitta agreed to acquire Caesars for $17.6B including debt days earlier), and the AI framing is deal advocacy directed at MGM's board. The countervailing signal is that the publisher's CEO was elevated to run the parent, which points the other way.

Search is the clearest deteriorating franchise — paid-search arbitrage economics compress as platform economics change — and print declines structurally. Competitive position scores **4.0**, unchanged from the prior review: a strong relative performer inside an industry facing platform-driven demand contraction, with no ability to verify whether the outperformance is audience durability or monetization intensity.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Binary transaction outcome with uncommitted financing (highest-magnitude, unpriced).** The derived third-party cash requirement to acquire the 73.9% not owned is ~$9.16B against $960.2M of parent cash. Financing was described as cash at both companies plus additional debt and equity, with only preliminary conversations held. MGM already carries substantial net debt (implied ~$5.6B from the $18B-versus-$12.4B gap) plus large triple-net master-lease obligations that rating agencies treat as debt-like. Pro-forma leverage on any completed structure would be far above IAC's current 0.18× net-debt-to-revenue profile. Whether that financing can be assembled on acceptable terms is unverified, and the market environment for such a package is not established from the provided sources.

**2. Undisclosed dilution path.** Whether equity is raised at the IAC level or at an acquisition vehicle determines whether IAC shareholders are diluted directly or see their proportional claim on MGM diluted. No share-issuance figure exists. No dilution percentage should be treated as established, and this is the most consequential open item in the file.

**3. Affiliated-bidder governance conflict.** Diller and Levin both serve on MGM's board while People Inc. bids to take MGM private, with MGM's own cash contemplated as a source of funds for its own buyout. MGM's formation of a special committee of independent directors on/about July 31, 2026 is the standard procedural remedy and is itself evidence that MGM's board treats the bidder as an affiliate. MGM has stated it views the bid as undervalued.

**4. Concentration in a single non-controlled investee.** Approximately the whole of IAC's equity value corresponds to one 26.1% position in a Las Vegas–centric gaming operator, embedding Las Vegas RevPAR cyclicality, Macau regulatory exposure and the Osaka development project (2030 target) into a Communication Services stock. The reported beta of 1.055 understates the forward risk profile if the deal closes. This exposure lands into a consumer backdrop that would affect both Las Vegas cash flows and advertising budgets, though the specific macro conditions are not established from the provided sources.

**5. Shareholder-return and compensation record.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested in IAC worth **$37.67** at FY2025 year-end (-62.3%) against a peer index at $263.67 (+163.7%) — roughly 226 points of five-year underperformance. SEC TSR methodology across three spin-offs may overstate the loss to a continuous holder, but the relative record is poor by any adjustment. Cumulative PEO "compensation actually paid" of **-$273.8M** across 2021–2025 shows the equity-based structure did transmit losses to the CEO, which favors alignment. Against that, FY2025 PEO total compensation rose to **$17,601,042** from $4,975,418 (+254%) in a year with a $104.0M net loss. The plausible explanation is transition or separation-related equity treatment tied to Levin's departure from the IAC CEO role alongside the Angi separation, but that is unverified, and no motive is attributed on the basis of the figure.

**6. Cash-conversion risk.** FY2025 OCF of $64.0M against $120.0M of gross interest expense means the business did not, in that year, generate operating cash meaningfully in excess of its gross interest burden. Whether that is transitional or structural cannot be determined without the cash-flow statement and segment data.

**7. Information risk.** Segment results, risk-factor changes, debt structure and maturities, the MGM carrying value and accounting method, the goodwill rollforward, and Q1/Q2 2026 results were not available for review. An 8-K was filed August 3, 2026 — likely Q2 results and/or an MGM update — and has not been read. Where the $1,401.3M of long-term debt sits in the structure is unknown; if it resides at the People Inc. level it is structurally senior to the holdco and does not encumber the MGM stake, which would matter considerably for the bid's feasibility.

**Short Interest Signal.** Short interest stands at **19.0% of float** (8.28M shares, 5.7 days to cover), which is high for a company trading at 0.66× book with asset backing approximating market cap. Two readings are consistent with the data. The first is merger-arb mechanics: participants long MGM and short IAC against the affiliated bid, which would make the position technically driven rather than a fundamental judgment. The second is a genuine bearish view that the publishing and Search assets have limited terminal value and that the MGM stake is tax-encumbered and non-controlled, making the SOTP unrealizable. The absence of options data prevents cross-checking whether skew confirms directional conviction. Either way, 19% SI with 5.7 days to cover creates meaningful squeeze risk on a definitive-agreement headline or a raised bid, which is one of the reasons the bearish case does not translate into a shortable setup.

Risk factors score **3.5**: multiple concurrent binary events (deal completion, deal price, financing structure, dilution location), a controlling-shareholder conflict with directors on both sides, and asset concentration in a single non-controlled investee — none of which is resolvable from available disclosure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $65 (+46%)

The MGM position is worth $3.24B at the proposal price against a $3.14B market capitalization, so a buyer at $44.53 acquires the publishing, Search, Care.com and Vivian Health businesses for approximately nothing on a gross basis and for ~0.37× revenue after a tax haircut. The publishing business grew digital revenue 10% in FY2025 and 14% in Q4 while the industry contracted, evidence of a portfolio with direct audience relationships rather than search dependence. The parent balance sheet is conservative — net debt of $441M against $4.73B of book equity — and the stock trades at 0.66× book with no dividend obligation and demonstrated buyback capacity.

For the bull case to pay, one of three things must happen: MGM's special committee agrees a transaction on terms that create rather than transfer value (financing raised at the vehicle level, minimal IAC-level dilution, price not materially above $48.30); the bid is withdrawn and management redeploys cash into repurchases below SOTP, with FY2027 EPS of $1.17 confirming the operating businesses earn; or MGM re-rates independently in a sector where sponsors are actively paying up (Caesars at $17.6B). In a favorable combination — MGM value at ~$55 per share, operating businesses at 0.9× revenue, net debt unchanged — equity value approaches $4.7B, or roughly **$65 per share**, consistent with the top of the analyst range at $66.

### Bear Case — $34 (-24%)

The special committee rejects $48.30 as inadequate, IAC either walks away or raises the price into a levered structure that requires IAC-level equity issuance. MGM de-rates toward $40 as the bid premium evaporates and Las Vegas RevPAR softens, cutting the after-tax stake value to roughly $2.0B. The FY2026 loss of -$0.54 proves to reflect operating cost absorption rather than one-time transaction expense, and the FY2027 $1.17 estimate follows the current year down. Digital revenue growth decelerates as the traffic-versus-yield question resolves in favor of yield on a shrinking audience, and the operating businesses are re-rated to 0.4× revenue (~$0.95B). Equity value falls to roughly $2.5B, or **$34 per share** — above the 52-week low of $29.56, which marks a deeper stress case if the deal collapses alongside a broader advertising contraction.

### Base Case — $50 (+12%)

The proposal remains under special-committee review through Q4 2026 without a definitive agreement, MGM trades in the mid-to-high $40s, and the operating businesses hold a mid-single-digit consolidated revenue trajectory with digital growing high single digits. After-tax stake of ~$2.68B plus operating businesses at 0.7× revenue (~$1.65B) less $441M net debt gives ~$3.9B of equity, discounted for the controlled holding-company structure to approximately **$50** — the analyst median. Roughly +12% upside against -24% downside is not an attractive asymmetry for new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders are compensated by asset backing that approximates the market price; new capital should wait for the deal structure and Q2 disclosure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate. The August 3, 2026 8-K likely contains Q2 2026 results and possibly an MGM update, and the parent rename to People Inc. was scheduled to coincide with Q2 earnings. The next hard checkpoints are (a) the Q2 filing and any segment disclosure showing 2026 digital revenue and margin, (b) any definitive agreement or withdrawal on the MGM proposal, and (c) MGM's special-committee conclusion.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. A definitive MGM agreement with **committed** financing where new equity is raised at the acquisition-vehicle level and IAC-level share issuance is nil or below 5% of outstanding — or, alternatively, bid withdrawal accompanied by an announced buyback of ≥$300M with the stock below $48.
2. Disclosure confirming FY2026 digital revenue growth of ≥8% **together with** session or audience metrics showing traffic stable to better, resolving the yield-versus-traffic ambiguity that currently caps the competitive score.
3. Operating cash flow normalizing to ≥$250M on a trailing four-quarter basis, demonstrating that FY2025's $64.0M was transitional.
4. Absent the above, a price of $34–36, which would put the stock at or below the after-tax MGM stake alone and offer asset-backed downside protection.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Announced equity issuance at the IAC/People Inc. level to fund the MGM acquisition, or a raised offer above ~$55 per MGM share financed with IAC-level leverage.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $0.80 (from $1.17), which would indicate the current-year deterioration is structural rather than transaction-related.
3. Digital revenue growth decelerating below ~3% year over year in any reported quarter.
4. MGM trading below $40, which would compress the after-tax stake to approximately two-thirds of the current market capitalization and remove the asset-backing floor.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on IAC, and the score declines to 4.5 from 5.0. The reason is not the share price — at $44.53 the stock sits 3.9% below its 52-week high and well above the $29.56 low, so this is not a drawdown story requiring a defense of mispricing. The reason is that two specific things deteriorated since the prior review while nothing verifiable improved. Consensus FY2026 EPS moved from +$0.49 to -$0.54, with more than a third of that cut occurring in the last 30 days, and the MGM proposal advanced from a nonbinding letter to a special-committee review in which the target has publicly called the price inadequate — raising both the probability of completion and the probability of a raised price funded by mechanisms whose effect on IAC minority holders is undisclosed.

The valuation gap is real. On a gross basis the market pays for the MGM stake and assigns roughly $347M to a $2.4B-revenue portfolio; on a tax-adjusted basis it assigns roughly $883–933M, or 0.37× revenue. What is missing is the mechanism that converts that gap into shareholder return. Management's stated intention is to increase concentration in the discounted asset rather than monetize it, using ~$9.2B of third-party capital that was not committed as of the proposal. The five-year relative shareholder return of -226 percentage points against the peer index is the historical record of this capital-allocation function, and the affiliated structure — with the controlling shareholder and the former CEO on the board of the company being acquired — is the governance context in which the next allocation decision is being made.

Existing holders are reasonably protected by asset value and have a specific catalyst to wait for. New capital is being asked to underwrite an unquantified dilution path, a non-controlled and tax-encumbered principal asset, and a core business whose growth cannot be distinguished from monetization intensity, all at 38x FY2027 consensus. The 19% short interest and 5.7 days to cover make an active short indefensible against squeeze risk on any definitive-agreement headline. Hold what you own; do not add before the deal structure and 2026 segment economics are on paper.
