## Executive Summary

Terex Corporation is executing an aggressive portfolio transformation, having closed the REV Group merger on February 2, 2026 while simultaneously pursuing a strategic review (potential divestiture) of its ~$2B Aerials segment. The result is a company that has shifted from approximately 50% international/highly-cyclical revenue to ~80% North American, ~85% U.S.-manufactured, with a more defensive end-market profile spanning waste/recycling, utilities, fire/emergency vehicles, and infrastructure equipment.

The transformation creates genuine upside optionality: a successful Aerials sale at 8-10x normalized EBITDA could yield $1.6-2.5B in proceeds, materially deleveraging the balance sheet and leaving a higher-quality residual portfolio. Q1 2026 showed encouraging execution (11% pro forma growth, SV at 14.2% EBITDA margins, MP bookings +38% YoY). However, the stock's 74% share dilution from the REV deal, elevated debt ($2.6B+ long-term), compressed Aerials margins (5%), and the current macro environment for industrial equipment create substantial near-term headwinds that prevent a full conviction buy recommendation.

At ~$58.56, the stock trades at roughly 9.5-9.9x guided 2026 EBITDA and ~12x forward P/E on FY2026 estimates — reasonable for an industrial in transition but not cheap enough to compensate for execution risk given the company is simultaneously integrating a $2B+ acquisition and divesting a $2B segment.

## Company Overview

Terex operates four segments following the REV Group merger:

- **Environmental Solutions (ES)**: $1.7B revenue, 13.8% operating margins. Refuse collection bodies (Heil brand), compaction/recycling, utility equipment, 3rd Eye AI digital solutions. Acquired from Dover in late 2024; integration ahead of schedule.
- **Materials Processing (MP)**: $1.7B revenue, 13.9% operating margins. Crushers, screens, washing systems under Powerscreen and related brands. Manufactured primarily in Northern Ireland and India.
- **Aerials**: $2.1B revenue, 5.0% operating margins (collapsed from 11.2% prior year). Genie® brand MEWPs, scissor lifts, telehandlers. Under strategic review for divestiture.
- **Specialty Vehicles (SV)**: ~$2.2B annualized revenue, 14.2% EBITDA margins. Fire/emergency vehicles, ambulances, terminal trucks, motorized RVs. Acquired via REV merger.

No single customer exceeds 6% of sales. Distribution is through independent dealers, rental companies, and direct channels.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue grew modestly to $5.42B in 2025 (+5.7% YoY), with 2026 guided at $7.5-8.1B (reported growth of 38-49%, but only ~5% on a pro forma basis including REV). The margin profile shows divergence:

- Gross margin: 19.4% (2025) vs. 22.8% (2023) — consistent compression
- Operating margin: 8.8% (2025) vs. 12.4% (2023) — driven almost entirely by Aerials deterioration
- Interest expense: $177M (2025) vs. $63M (2023) — nearly tripled on acquisition financing

Q1 2026 revenue of $1.73B (+41% reported, +11% pro forma) demonstrated that organic growth remains positive across all four segments. SV contributed 20% growth versus the prior-year comparable period.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

The balance sheet has been fundamentally reshaped:
- Long-term debt: $2.58B (vs. $620M in 2023)
- Debt-to-equity: 1.93x
- Net leverage: 2.4x (improving trajectory)
- No maturities until 2029
- Total liquidity: $1.57B ($772M cash + $800M undrawn revolver)
- Goodwill/intangibles: $2.1B (vs. $310M in 2023)

The Altman Z-Score of 2.23 places Terex in the grey zone, reflecting the rapid leverage increase. However, no near-term refinancing risk exists given the maturity wall starts in 2029.

### Cash Flow Generation

Free cash flow was $322-325M in 2025 (147% FCF conversion ratio), with operating cash flow of $440M in 2025 up from $326M in 2024. Working capital discipline has been strong: NWC/sales improved from 24.0% to 16.7% by Q1 2026. SV's more favorable working capital profile with less seasonality should further improve cash generation stability.

Q1 2026 showed a seasonal $57M FCF outflow — consistent with historical patterns of back-half weighted cash generation.

### Share Dilution

Pre-REV: ~66M shares. Post-REV: ~115M shares — a 74% dilution. This is the single most important factor for per-share valuation. 2026 EPS guidance of $4.50-$5.00 on the expanded share base implies $520-575M net income, but the trailing EPS of $3.33 reflects the dilution occurring mid-year.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

1. **Waste/recycling**: EPA 2027 regulation changes driving pre-buy cycles; population-driven fleet replacement
2. **Utilities**: Data center construction driving 8-15% annual capex growth through 2030; grid modernization tailwind
3. **Infrastructure**: IIJA federal funding providing multi-year volume support
4. **Fire/emergency**: Municipal fleet aging; 2-year backlog provides visibility
5. **MP recovery**: Q1 bookings +38% YoY, suggesting cyclical trough is passing

### Acquisition-Driven Growth

EBITDA guidance of $930M-$1B for 2026 represents the first full year of the combined entity. REV synergy target: $75M run-rate within 24 months ($28M expected in 2026). ESG synergies were delivered ahead of schedule, providing credibility for the REV timeline.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year estimates have been modestly revised upward (+1.7% over 30 days, +0.7% over 90 days), while current-quarter estimates have been revised down (-6.7% over 30 days). The divergence suggests near-term timing uncertainty but stable full-year expectations. Next-year estimates ($5.82 EPS) are essentially flat over 90 days. This is not a high-momentum estimate revision story.

## Competitive Landscape

**Aerials (Genie)**: #2 global MEWP maker behind JLG (Oshkosh). Facing structural competitive pressure from Chinese manufacturers (Dingli, Sinoboom, XCMG). Anti-dumping duties in US and EU provide temporary protection, but the margin collapse (11.2% → 5.0%) suggests pricing power is limited. This competitive deterioration supports the divestiture thesis — better to exit before further margin erosion.

**ES (Heil/Utilities)**: Leading refuse collection body manufacturer competing with McNeilus (Oshkosh) and Federal Signal. End-market demand is non-discretionary (waste must be collected), creating revenue stability. The 3rd Eye AI camera technology provides a digital differentiation angle.

**MP (Powerscreen)**: Global leader in mobile crushing/screening competing against Sandvik and Metso. Strong brand recognition among contractors. Recovery in bookings (+38% YoY) suggests cyclical trough is behind.

**SV (REV brands)**: Fire/emergency vehicles compete with Pierce (Oshkosh). REV has strong positions in ambulances and terminal trucks with 2-year backlogs indicating solid competitive positioning.

The portfolio transformation is designed to shift toward segments with structural demand growth (waste management, utilities, emergency services) and away from the highly cyclical, competitively intense Aerials market.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **REV Integration Execution**: The merger nearly doubled the company. While ESG integration went well, REV is larger and more complex. Early indicators (14.2% SV margins, on-track synergies) are positive, but 18-24 months of integration risk remain.

2. **Aerials Divestiture Uncertainty**: Management stated they are "engaging with multiple interested parties" but no timeline exists. The 10-K risk factors explicitly state the divestiture "may not happen on favorable terms or at all." If it fails, the company retains a structurally challenged segment requiring ongoing capital allocation.

3. **Leverage Sensitivity**: At 2.4x net leverage with ~$190M annual interest expense, any cyclical downturn in the construction/equipment markets could pressure coverage ratios. The credit agreement triggers restrictions at <2.0x cash flow coverage.

4. **Tariff Exposure**: Primarily affects Aerials (China, Italy, Mexico imports). Management expects price/cost neutrality for 2026, but prolonged tariff escalation could pressure margins further.

5. **Macro Cyclicality**: Despite the portfolio shift toward more defensive end markets, Terex remains exposed to construction activity, municipal budgets, and infrastructure spending.

6. **Antitrust Litigation**: Early-stage industry class action lawsuits disclosed. Magnitude unknown but potential for material settlement or distraction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.3% is modestly above historical volatility of 47.3% (3.0% spread), suggesting the market prices slightly elevated uncertainty but no extreme event risk. The flat term structure (50.3% near vs. 51.4% far) indicates uncertainty is distributed evenly across time horizons rather than concentrated around a specific catalyst. The put/call OI ratio of 0.43 (call-heavy) and negligible put volume suggest options participants are positioned for upside — potentially related to the Aerials divestiture catalyst. IV has declined 7.8 percentage points since early May, indicating post-earnings uncertainty has partially resolved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75-85, +28-45%)

- Aerials divested at 9-10x EBITDA ($1.8-2.5B), with proceeds used to reduce debt from $2.6B to sub-$500M
- REV synergies exceed target ($75M+ vs. ESG precedent of upside delivery)
- Residual portfolio re-rates from 9.5x to 12-13x EBITDA as cyclicality profile improves
- 2027E EPS of $5.82 at 14-15x P/E = $82-87
- Utilities demand accelerates on data center buildout; SV backlog converts at high margins

### Bear Case ($42-48, -18-28%)

- Aerials divestiture fails or proceeds at depressed valuation (6-7x) due to margin deterioration
- REV integration hits operational snags; synergies delayed or reduced
- Rising rates + stagflationary environment compress equipment demand across construction and municipal end markets
- Net leverage remains elevated above 2x through 2027, limiting capital return and multiple expansion
- Aerials margins deteriorate further (sub-5%) requiring restructuring charges

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Aerials divestiture announced with specific terms at 8x+ EBITDA — this crystallizes the sum-of-parts value and eliminates the largest uncertainty
2. Stock pulls back to $48-50 (forward P/E of ~8.5x on FY2027) while EBITDA guidance maintained — would provide asymmetric risk/reward
3. Two consecutive quarters of SV EBITDA margins above 14% AND REV synergy run-rate tracking above $40M annualized — confirms integration success

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Aerials strategic review terminated without a transaction, combined with margins below 3%
2. Net leverage rises above 3.0x or management cuts EBITDA guidance below $850M
3. MP bookings reverse course (below 1.0x book-to-bill for two consecutive quarters)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August 2026). Any Aerials divestiture announcement would warrant immediate reassessment regardless of timing.

## Conclusion

Terex presents a compelling transformation narrative with identifiable catalysts (Aerials divestiture, REV synergy realization, utilities/infrastructure demand). The forward valuation of 10x FY2027 earnings is genuinely undemanding for a company with this much positive optionality. However, the transformation is mid-execution with 18-24 months of integration risk ahead, elevated leverage constraining the balance sheet, and a degrading analyst sentiment trend.

The risk/reward is approximately balanced at $58.56: meaningful upside if execution continues (~28% to consensus median target) but meaningful downside if macro deteriorates or the Aerials divestiture disappoints. The current price reflects neither the full bull case nor the bear case — appropriately. This warrants a HOLD with a positive directional bias, upgrading to BUY on either a price pullback to the $48-50 range or specific confirmation of the Aerials divestiture on favorable terms.
