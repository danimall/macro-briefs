## Executive Summary

Nexstar Media Group is the largest local television broadcaster in the United States, operating 201 stations reaching ~70% of U.S. TV households. The company generates approximately $900M-$1B in normalized annual free cash flow on a $6.1B market cap (12-15% FCF yield), driven by structurally advantaged retransmission revenue and highly profitable political advertising cycles. This is one of the cheapest cash-flow-generative media assets in public markets.

The critical complication is the TEGNA acquisition. Nexstar closed the $6.2B deal on March 20, 2026, adding ~$5.7B in debt. A federal judge issued a preliminary injunction on April 17, 2026 (with 8 state AGs and DirecTV as plaintiffs, subsequently joined by 5 additional states). The injunction creates a genuine binary outcome: if Nexstar prevails on appeal, the combined entity generates massive cash flow with dominant scale; if the deal is unwound, the company faces potentially $12B+ in debt without proportional revenue and forced asset sales. This binary should resolve within 6-12 months via the Ninth Circuit.

The stock at $197 trades at approximately 7x normalized 2-year average standalone FCF, which is objectively cheap for the quality of the cash flows. But the injunction overhang, combined with 60% near-term implied volatility (26pp above historical), signals the market is correctly pricing substantial uncertainty. I rate NXST a HOLD based on the specific growth catalyst of TEGNA resolution: a favorable outcome would justify $250-290 targets; an unfavorable one would send shares toward $140-160. Initiating or exiting ahead of this resolution sacrifices the most informative data point.

## Company Overview

Nexstar Media Group operates the largest portfolio of local television stations in the U.S., with affiliations spanning all major broadcast networks (CBS, FOX, NBC, ABC, The CW). Revenue breaks down into distribution fees from cable/satellite/streaming distributors (59% of FY2025 revenue, $2.92B) and advertising (40%, $1.96B). The company also operates NewsNation (national cable news), The CW network (80.8% owned, repositioned toward sports), and digital properties including The Hill and 125+ local news websites.

The business model benefits from two structural moats: (1) FCC-licensed broadcast spectrum is a finite, government-protected asset creating barriers to entry; and (2) local TV remains the only medium legally eligible for the lowest-unit-rate pricing from political candidates, making it the default channel for campaign spending in even-numbered years.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, Nexstar faces modest headwinds: MVPD subscriber attrition partially offset by rate escalators, and secular digital advertising migration away from linear TV. Distribution revenue was flat in FY2025 (-0.1%). Organic growth without M&A is approximately 0-3% annually, driven primarily by pricing power in retransmission negotiations.

**TEGNA Synergy Case (if deal upheld):**
- Combined revenue: ~$8B (Nexstar $4.95B + TEGNA ~$3B)
- Expected cost synergies: $250-400M (based on historical broadcast M&A precedents of 8-12% of target revenue)
- Combined entity reaches ~80% of U.S. TV households — unprecedented negotiating leverage with MVPDs
- 2026 is an election year: combined political advertising revenue could approach $700-800M

**Growth Catalysts:**
1. Ninth Circuit ruling on TEGNA injunction (expected H2 2026) — binary positive catalyst
2. 2026 election cycle — political advertising tailwind ($400-500M incremental vs. 2025)
3. ATSC 3.0 (NextGen TV) spectrum monetization — nascent but could unlock $200-300M in new data/datacasting revenue over 3-5 years
4. CBS/FOX affiliation renewals (July-August 2026) — risk but also potential to secure improved economics given Nexstar's scale

**Secular Headwinds:**
- Cord-cutting: MVPD subscribers declining
- Digital advertising share gains by Google, Meta, TikTok
- Cable network decline (TV Food Network impairment = -3% terminal growth rate assumption)

## Competitive Landscape

Nexstar occupies a structurally advantaged position in local broadcasting. With 201 stations across 116 markets, it is approximately 3x larger than its nearest competitor (Sinclair at ~185 stations, but smaller markets). This scale creates:

- **Retransmission negotiating leverage:** MVPDs cannot practically black out Nexstar stations across 70% of the country
- **Political advertising dominance:** Campaigns in 40 states must advertise on Nexstar stations
- **Cost efficiencies:** Shared services across stations (sales, engineering, news production)
- **Network leverage:** No broadcast network can afford to lose 40-50+ affiliates simultaneously, giving Nexstar power in affiliation renewals

The July 2025 court decision vacating the FCC's "top four" duopoly restriction potentially enables further consolidation. However, the TEGNA injunction suggests regulatory/antitrust barriers remain real even in a deregulatory environment.

**Competitive threats** come primarily from digital platforms rather than other broadcasters. Google and Meta capture the majority of incremental advertising dollars. YouTube in particular competes for local news viewership. Over time, if retransmission consent economics deteriorate (MVPDs lose too many subscribers), the business model faces structural pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: TEGNA Injunction (Binary, Near-Term)**
Judge Nunley found plaintiffs "likely to prevail" on antitrust grounds. Eight state AGs plus DirecTV originally challenged the merger, with five additional states joining in May 2026. The Ninth Circuit appeal is the key next event. If the injunction is upheld:
- Nexstar faces potential forced divestiture of TEGNA stations
- Billions in acquisition debt would remain on the balance sheet without corresponding revenue
- Integration costs already incurred become sunk
- Stock could trade to $140-160 reflecting impaired standalone value plus deal friction costs

**Secondary Risk: Leverage/Interest Rate Exposure**
$3.6B in floating-rate debt means every 100bps increase in SOFR adds ~$36M in annual interest expense. At current rates (~5.3% SOFR), the post-TEGNA interest burden could consume a large share of combined cash flow.

**Tertiary Risk: Affiliation Renewals**
CBS (49 stations, July 2026) and FOX (41 stations, August 2026) expirations create near-term cost uncertainty. Networks have historically extracted increasing reverse compensation from affiliates.

**Tail Risk: IRS Tax Dispute**
$225M in taxes + $271M in interest/penalties at stake. Tax Court ruled for Nexstar; IRS appealed to Seventh Circuit (ruling expected H1 2026). Adverse outcome would be material but not existential.

- Combined Nexstar-TEGNA generates $1.5-1.8B in annual FCF
- 2026 election cycle drives $700-800M in combined political advertising

The specific growth catalyst justifying a HOLD on a 12-month horizon is the TEGNA deal resolution. A favorable outcome transforms NXST from a modestly declining standalone broadcaster into a dominant platform with $1.5-1.8B in FCF trading at under 5x that cash flow. The probability-weighted expected value — even accounting for the bear case — favors maintaining exposure through resolution rather than selling at current levels, which already discount substantial deal risk (stock is 22% below the February high of $254.30 and well below the $259 analyst mean target).

## Conclusion

Nexstar presents a compelling cash-flow story obscured by binary deal risk. The underlying business generates $900M-$1B in normalized FCF annually — a 12-15% yield on the current market cap that compares favorably to any media company. Analyst consensus overwhelmingly favors the stock (7 buy/strong buy vs. 1 hold, 0 sell, 1 strong sell) with a median target of $270.

I rate NXST a HOLD because: (1) the probability-weighted outcome favors the stock at current levels given the FCF protection on the downside and deal upside; (2) a specific, identifiable catalyst (Ninth Circuit ruling) will resolve the uncertainty within 6 months; and (3) selling at $197 — after a 22% decline from highs and with 31% upside to consensus target — would mean crystallizing losses at the point of maximum uncertainty. The appropriate action is to wait for information, not react to volatility.

Position sizing should reflect the binary nature: 1-2% for new capital, with a hard stop at $155 (approximately where standalone value with impaired deal economics bottoms). Re-evaluate after the Ninth Circuit rules.
