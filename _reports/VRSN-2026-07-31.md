**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Verisign operates the authoritative registries for `.com` and `.net` under contracts running to November 30, 2030 and July 1, 2029 respectively, plus the Root Zone Maintainer agreement with ICANN through October 2032. The economics are close to unique in public markets: FY2025 revenue of $1,656.6M converted to $1,121.0M of operating income (67.7% margin), $825.7M of net income and $1,068.3M of free cash flow, a 64.5% FCF margin. Registrars pay at registration and revenue is recognized ratably, producing $1.45B of deferred revenue at June 30, 2026 that functions as permanent interest-free float. Gross debt of ~$1.8B against ~$1.15B of EBITDA is roughly 1.1x net leverage post the July 20 redemption, with interest coverage near 15x.

Two things changed materially since our last review. First, `.web` was delegated into the DNS root zone on July 22, 2026 with Verisign as registry operator, and the Independent Review Process against ICANN was resolved (terms confidential, characterized as not material). Management confirmed that `.web` sits under a standard ICANN registry agreement rather than the Commerce Department Cooperative Agreement, which means full wholesale pricing flexibility subject to six months' notice, the ability to sell premium names, and far fewer channel-marketing restrictions. General availability is guided to late 2026 or very early 2027, preceded by a limited registration period in which every `.com` registrant can claim the matching `.web`. Second, the `.com` pricing lever re-engaged: after a two-year contractual dead zone, the wholesale fee rises 6.9% from $10.26 to $10.97 on November 1, 2026, with management modeling roughly half of that flowing into 2027 revenue.

The problem is price. At $286.58 the stock trades at 30.0x consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.56 and 26.6x FY2027 consensus of $10.76, with a ~4.1% free-cash-flow yield and a ~4.3% total shareholder yield. It has moved from ~$255.90 on July 1 to a $295.36 intraday high on July 29; the `.web` delegation and the beat-and-raise are substantially reflected. Consensus mean and median targets of $318 and $324 imply 11-13% upside, and against a plausible bear case near $225 the risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD, held on a specific 12-month growth thesis: the pricing lever, base-growth acceleration and `.web` optionality are real, but they are approximately priced at 30x forward for a business whose top line is contractually capped near 6%.

## Company Overview

Verisign is a wholesaler. Registrants contract with registrars — GoDaddy, Namecheap, Tucows and hundreds of others — and those registrars are Verisign's direct customers, paying a per-annum registry fee for each name registered or renewed. Verisign never touches the end registrant, does not set retail pricing, and does not hold personal registrant data for `.com`/`.net`, which the 10-K cites as the reason GDPR-type regimes have had limited impact.

The asset base: the `.com` registry under both an ICANN Registry Agreement and a Cooperative Agreement with the Department of Commerce (auto-renewed on identical terms in November 2024 for a six-year term); `.net` through July 1, 2029; `.name`, IDN transliterations, the `.cc` ccTLD, back-end services for `.edu`; and the Root Zone Maintainer role, under which Verisign publishes the authoritative root zone file and operates two of the thirteen global root servers. `.web` joined this set in July 2026.

The company runs this with 928 employees (December 31, 2025), 93% U.S.-based, average tenure ~11 years, across data centers in Dulles, Ashburn and New Castle plus 200+ points of presence. Management extended its record of 100% `.com`/`.net` resolution availability to 29 consecutive years. There is one reportable segment; there is no product, geographic or customer diversification to analyze. Revenue by registrar domicile in FY2025 was U.S. $1,093.1M (66%), EMEA $279.4M (17%), APAC $184.6M (11%), other $99.5M (6%), a split the company warns may not reflect where registrants actually sit.

The moat is contractual rather than technological, and it cuts both ways. The same agreements that grant sole-source status cap pricing, restrict bundling and marketing methods, and gate new registry services. The 10-K states plainly that these restrictions do not apply to ccTLDs and other gTLDs and "therefore may create a competitive disadvantage."

The capex increase deserves attention because management attributed it specifically to AI-driven hardware inflation. The CFO said server and memory chip prices have "had a meaningful impact," that the company pulled forward spending to front-run known increases, and that 2027 prices are expected to stay elevated because "we're competing with a lot of data center capacity that's trying to be built right now." The CEO stated the company will make the investment "without hesitation" regardless of market price, which is rational when franchise value rests on an unbroken availability record. In proportion, $65M of capex is 3.7% of guided revenue versus 1.4% in 2025 — roughly $40M of annual FCF, under 4%. It is a cost-discipline data point and an early signal of a longer capital-intensity cycle, not a thesis-breaker.

Additional paid-in capital of $9,092.7M sits against 355.9M shares issued but only 90.4M outstanding, because Verisign has repurchased 265.4M shares since inception for $16.19B, charged against APIC. The accumulated deficit primarily reflects dot-com-era write-offs. The current ratio of ~0.59 is arithmetically driven by $1,084.8M of current deferred revenue, a performance obligation satisfied by running servers rather than a cash claim; excluding it, current liabilities were $814.3M (including $549.3M of notes since retired) against $1,122.3M of current assets.

Standardized book-equity screens are non-informative on this structure — retained-earnings and working-capital inputs are dominated by two-decade-old write-offs and prepaid revenue — and should not be cited in either direction. Solvency should be judged on cash flow, leverage and coverage. Post the July 20 redemption of the $550M 4.75% notes, gross debt is ~$1.8B ($750M 2.70% 2031, $500M 5.25% 2032, $550M 5.10% 2031 issued June 26, 2026 at 99.961), with an undrawn $200M revolver to 2028. Gross debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.56x on FY2025 EBITDA of ~$1,152M; pro-forma net leverage ≈ 1.1x. The June refinancing raised that tranche's coupon 35bp and extended maturity four years. The genuine implication of negative book equity is dependence on continuous debt-market access to refinance ~$1.8B of notes, accessed successfully in June 2026 (and, per the brief's balance-sheet note, again earlier in 2026).

In Q2 the company returned $270.4M ($196.8M buyback + $73.6M dividend) against ~$213M of FCF, above 100% and partially funded from the balance sheet. RSU dilution is genuinely minimal: 0.7M unvested units at year-end 2025, characterized as under 1.0% of shares outstanding, with diluted exceeding basic by only ~0.3M shares.

**Earnings delivery.** Four quarters: one beat, three misses, all small (–1.4%, +2.1%, –4.3%, –1.1%). This is a low-variance business where consensus should be closely tracked, so a 1-in-4 beat rate argues against paying a premium for consensus rather than signaling deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

The domain base is the operating variable that matters, and it inflected hard.

| Period | `.com`+`.net` base | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 31, 2023 | 172.7M | — |
| Dec 31, 2024 | 169.0M | –2% |
| Dec 31, 2025 | 173.5M | +2.6% |
| Jun 30, 2026 | 179.1M | +5.1% |

Q2 2026 added 3.05M net names. New registrations were 12.7M versus 10.4M a year earlier, with management characterizing new-registration unit growth as +14% in Q1 and +21% in Q2. The blended final renewal rate improved to 76.3% in Q1 2026 from 75.5%. Management disclosed the decomposition: first-time renewal in the mid-40% range, stable for several quarters; previously-renewed cohorts in the mid-80% range.

That decomposition is the key to reading the surge, and it argues for measured enthusiasm. More than half of new registrations do not renew even once. A volume surge mechanically inflates the base before churning, and the blended renewal rate must decline as the first-time-renewing mix grows. The CFO conceded exactly this, guiding to "a little bit of a tick down... in the remainder of this year and then possibly into next year." So the +5.1% base growth is partly a mix artifact that will partially unwind.

**The quality point about recent growth is that it was achieved on volume, not price.** The `.com` wholesale fee has been $10.26 since September 2024 and cannot change until November 1, 2026, because the Registry Agreement permits up to 7% increases only in each of the final four years of a six-year period, and the current period began October 26, 2024. Revenue grew 6.4% in FY2025 and 6.3% in H1 2026 with the price lever switched off. It now switches on for four consecutive years through 2030.

Sizing the 2027 contribution: assuming `.com` is ~92% of revenue (the company does not disclose the split), 0.92 × $1,750M × 6.9% × 50% ≈ $56M, roughly 3.2 points of 2027 growth before any volume. If units moderate to 3-4%, 2027 revenue growth frames in the 6-8% range before `.web`. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $10.76 against FY2026's $9.56 implies +12.6% EPS growth, the gap over revenue growth coming from buyback and modest operating leverage. Estimate revisions support this: current year +0.9% over 30 days and +1.1% over 90; next year +3.9% and +4.3%. Positive and accelerating at the margin, but an order of magnitude below the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection.

**`.web`.** Strategically the most important development in a decade, because it is the first namespace where Verisign controls price. Management confirmed complete wholesale pricing flexibility subject to six months' notice, the right to sell premium names (prohibited in `.com`/`.net`), and materially fewer channel restrictions. There are no incremental registry build costs — the CFO called integration "as natural as one could imagine" — and 2026 marketing expense is immaterial and inside guidance. Guidance explicitly "does not anticipate meaningful revenue or expense related to `.web`."

The launch sequence is 90 days of security testing, then a minimum 30-day Sunrise for trademark holders, then an optional limited registration period letting existing `.com` registrants claim matching `.web` names, then general availability late 2026 or very early 2027. The LRP addresses roughly 167M `.com` names with a defensive-registration pitch, which is the single most interesting lever. Arithmetically, 2M names at a $20 wholesale fee is ~$40M of revenue (2.3% of 2026 revenue) at near-100% incremental margin; 5M at $25 is ~$125M. Those are illustrations, not forecasts — there are zero `.web` registrations today and management declined to discuss pricing.

The relevant cautionary comparable is internal: `.net` has stagnated at roughly 12-13M names despite Verisign's own operation, brand and channel. New gTLD history generally has disappointed. We would not underwrite `.web` as a growth engine on current disclosure; we treat it as a call option worth 1-3% of revenue in a base case, with genuine upside if the LRP defensive pitch lands.

**AI cuts both ways, and both directions are in the filings.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors state that AI "could be transformational in ways that cannot be anticipated fully" and that reduced domain demand "could have a material impact." The Q2 10-Q MD&A attributes base growth partly to "the evolution of AI tools used in content and website creation," and the CEO said AI "is definitely enhancing demand." Both are true: AI lowers the cost of building a site (near-term demand tailwind, currently visible in +21% new registrations), and AI-mediated navigation could reduce the need to type addresses (longer-dated, unquantified). Management is monetizing the first and has disclosed the second. The CEO declined to separate AI from the registrar cycle turning back toward volume acquisition and conceded "there's a bit of good fortune here."

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names CentralNic, CNNIC, DENIC, GoDaddy, Google, Identity Digital, Nominet, Public Interest Registry, Radix and `.xyz`. GoDaddy appears as a registry-level competitor while also being, as the largest registrar, among Verisign's most important distribution channels — a relationship the 10-K addresses directly, noting registrars sell competing registries' names and "may be more motivated to market to registrants to whom they can also market their own services."

Market structure is the paradox worth understanding. There are now well over 1,000 TLDs, approaching 2,000 by the CEO's count, and yet `.com` alone accounts for roughly 167M of Verisign's ~179M names. `.com` remains, in management's own characterization, one of the cheapest TLDs available at $10.26 wholesale. Dominant share plus low relative price plus a contractual cap on increases is the structural bull case: a monopolist contractually prevented from monopoly pricing, with an embedded and unexercised right to petition Commerce to remove pricing restrictions if market conditions no longer warrant them.

Trajectory is modestly improving: the base went from –2% to +5.1% YoY, new-registration units are up 14-21%, and `.web` adds an unregulated-price namespace at zero incremental infrastructure cost. Against that, ICANN's New gTLD Program 2026 Round opened for applications around April-May 2026, and the 10-K's registrar risk factor anticipates precisely the consequence — new TLDs competing for finite registrar shelf space and marketing attention, with registrars potentially reducing "the prominence or visibility of our products." Verisign sits on both sides of that: it faces new entrants, and `.web` is itself a new TLD competing for the same shelf.

Regulatory architecture is a distinct dependency. Verisign remits $0.2575 per annual `.com` registration to ICANN and $0.75 per `.net`, and the 10-K flags weakening of the multi-stakeholder governance model — UN oversight proposals, governance by treaty, national legislation supplanting the process — as a material risk. That is not a near-term catalyst but it is the channel through which the franchise could be structurally altered.

Score: 7.0. The level is elite and the trajectory is improving, but the moat is contractual with a dated expiry rather than self-renewing.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract renewal and the most-similar-terms reset.** Both Registry Agreements carry only presumptive renewal rights, and both contain a provision under which, if certain terms are not similar to those generally in effect at the five largest gTLDs, renewal must conform on terms "reasonably necessary" — which the 10-K states "could be unfavorable to us." `.net` expires July 1, 2029; `.com` November 30, 2030. The Cooperative Agreement auto-renews in 2030 unless Commerce gives written notice at least 120 days prior. This is the dominant terminal-value risk and the primary reason a 68%-margin monopoly trades at 30x rather than 40x. As 2029 approaches, renewal-cycle publicity risk — which the 10-K explicitly says intensifies "particularly when these agreements are being renewed," and which it warns can be material "even when unmeritorious and/or unsuccessful" — becomes a live multiple headwind.

**Concentration approaching absolute.** "Substantially all" revenues come from `.com` and `.net`. One segment, no product diversification, no geographic hedge that matters. Any single adverse contractual, regulatory or political outcome on `.com` is existential in a way unusual for a $26B company.

**Renewal-mix decay.** Mid-40% first-time renewal means the current volume surge mechanically inflates the base and then partially unwinds. Management has pre-guided the blended rate lower into 2027. A related risk: AI-assisted site building may be generating a cohort of speculative or low-intent names whose second renewal disappoints even if first-time renewals look normal.

**Pull-forward ahead of the November 1 price increase.** Asked directly whether the registration surge reflects registrars front-running the increase, the CEO said "you can't say that that's not a factor... we just don't see it as anything coming close to a material factor." That is a hedged denial. Pull-forward would borrow from H2 2026 and H1 2027 volumes.

**Uninsured operational tail.** The 10-K states the company maintains no specific reserves for security breaches, cyber-attacks or DDoS, that insurance "may be inadequate," and that it carries no insurance or designated reserves for data-center interruption. It also flags dependence on RPKI administered by regional internet registries, where Verisign has "limited visibility into the maturity of and investment in the RIRs' operational and security controls," and where a compromise could cause "complete loss of availability." A multi-day outage would strike directly at the 29-year availability record that underpins the brand and, ultimately, the renewal case.

**China licensing.** Demand declined substantially in China in 2023-2024 on regulatory mandates. Verisign must hold a government-issued license per TLD under MIIT rules; failure to obtain or renew "could result in... the suspension of some or all of our registry services" there.

**Governance and succession.** D. James Bidzos, 70, is simultaneously Executive Chairman, President and CEO, and has held the Chairman or Vice Chairman seat continuously since 1995. The filings reviewed disclose no named CEO successor, though the 10-K describes generic strategic talent reviews and succession planning for the workforce. CEO total compensation rose from $10.12M (2021) to $15.54M (2025), +54% over four years, against a cumulative TSR index of 113. There is a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (October 8 and November 25, 2025; May 21, 2026, plus four in H1 2025) whose substance we have not verified. Concentration of board and executive authority in one 70-year-old, with pay growth well ahead of shareholder returns, is a legitimate discount factor.

**Insider activity.** From May through July 28, 2026, Bidzos sold in near-weekly 100-1,500 share tranches at $246-302, reducing disclosed holdings from ~441,439 to 415,939 — roughly 25,500 shares, ~5.8% of the position, ~$6.9M. The General Counsel sold repeatedly in 250-500 share lots; the CFO sold 500 shares at $281.56 on July 28. Director open-market purchases were de minimis (13.8 and 2.3 shares), consistent with plan mechanics rather than conviction. The metronomic cadence and price laddering are characteristic of a pre-established 10b5-1 program, and the 10-Q confirms no plan was adopted, terminated or modified during Q2 2026, which weakens any inference that insiders sold into `.web` news they alone knew. We treat this as programmatic diversification and a mild negative — one-way selling with no offsetting purchases of size, in a period when estimate revisions are only modestly positive. It is not a red flag.

**What is not a risk here:** leverage, refinancing capacity, covenant pressure, litigation of size (the `.web` disputes are resolved), or short-squeeze mechanics. Short interest is 2.0% of float, 2.2 days to cover.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $355

Two dormant levers turn on simultaneously against a base that has re-accelerated. The November 1 `.com` increase contributes roughly 3.2 points to 2027 revenue on management's own 50%-recognition modeling, with three more annual increases available through 2030. `.web` general availability arrives in early 2027 into a 167M-name installed base with a defensive-registration pitch, at zero incremental infrastructure cost, full pricing freedom and the ability to sell premium names — if the limited registration period converts even 2-3% of `.com` holders, that is $40-80M of near-100%-margin revenue, $0.35-0.70 of EPS. Volume growth persists at 4-5% as the registrar cycle stays oriented toward customer acquisition and AI-assisted site creation keeps lowering the cost of starting a web presence. Revenue grows 7-8% in 2027, margins hold near 68%, and the $1.50B buyback authorization retires 3-4% of shares annually. FY2027 EPS reaches $11.00 and the multiple re-rates to 32x as the market recognizes a second franchise with unregulated pricing: $355, matching the high analyst target, ~24% above current with a 1.1% dividend on top.

### Bear Case — $225

The registration surge proves substantially cyclical and partly pull-forward. As the first-time-renewing cohort grows, the blended renewal rate declines more than management guided — arithmetically inevitable given a mid-40% first-time rate — and net adds decelerate through 2027 to 2-3%. `.web` follows `.net`: a modest, high-margin adjacency at 1-2M names, worth under 2% of revenue, because registrar shelf space is now contested by the 2026 gTLD round and because 1,000+ existing TLDs demonstrated that new namespaces rarely scale. The November price increase meets the resistance the 10-K itself warns about: "to the extent we increase our prices, there could be a decrease in the demand and/or renewal rates." Capex inflation persists as management pre-committed to buy hardware at any price, depreciation rises off the $13.0M H1 base, and margins slip toward 66-67%. As `.net`'s July 2029 expiry approaches, renewal-cycle publicity and the most-similar-terms reset become an active overhang. FY2027 EPS lands near $10.20 and the multiple compresses to 22x: $225, roughly 21% below current and above the February 2026 low of $208.86.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive and specific — pricing flow-through, `.web` launch, base growth, 4%+ shareholder yield — but the base-case return does not compensate for a 21% bear case.

**Base case: $312 (+8.9% from $286.58, ~10% total return with the dividend).** Math: 29x FY2027 consensus EPS of $10.76. The 29x anchor is the midpoint of the trailing three-year range for this business and consistent with an approximately 4% FCF yield on ~$1.10B of 2027 free cash flow. Sits between the bear and bull cases above.

**Bull case: $355 (+23.9%)** — 33x on ~$11.00 of FY2027 EPS with `.web` traction confirmed.

**Bear case: $225 (–21.5%)** — 22x on ~$10.20 of FY2027 EPS with base growth decelerating to 2-3%.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, gated by three dated events — the Q3 2026 10-Q (reclassification of the $145.2M `.web` deposit into intangibles and any amortization schedule), the November 1 `.com` price increase, and `.web` general availability in late 2026/early 2027 followed by the first quarter of disclosed `.web` volumes.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**

2. **`.web` evidence** — disclosed registrations above ~3M within two quarters of general availability, or an announced wholesale price materially above `.com`'s $10.97, either of which would convert the option from illustrative to quantifiable and support $60M+ of high-margin incremental revenue.
3. **Base durability** — two consecutive quarters in which the blended final renewal rate holds flat or improves *while* the first-time-renewing mix grows, which would falsify the mix-decay bear case and justify underwriting 4%+ volume growth alongside four years of available price increases.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Volume break** — net adds turning negative in any quarter, or first-time renewal falling below the low-40% range, signalling the 2025-26 cohort was low-intent.
2. **Contract deterioration** — any Commerce Department or ICANN action signalling non-renewal, an early most-similar-terms negotiation, or a formal pricing challenge on `.com`; alternatively a `.net` renewal proposal in 2028-29 with materially worse economics.
3. **Operational or margin break** — a multi-day `.com`/`.net` resolution outage, or operating margin sustained below 65% as capex-driven depreciation and hardware inflation compound.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 release (late October 2026), which carries the `.web` deposit reclassification, the first commentary on the November 1 increase, and updated renewal-rate decomposition.

## Conclusion

Verisign is one of the highest-quality cash-generating franchises in public markets: 68% operating margins, 64% free-cash-flow margins, ~1.1x net leverage, 15x interest coverage, minimal dilution, and $1.45B of prepaid customer cash functioning as permanent float. The operating news over the past two quarters has been good: the domain base re-accelerated from –2% to +5.1%, guidance was raised, the `.web` litigation resolved and the namespace was delegated, and the `.com` pricing lever re-engages November 1 after a two-year contractual freeze.

Our scores reflect both sides of that. Financial health at 8.2 is where it belongs for a business with this cash conversion and leverage profile, tempered slightly by a >100% quarterly payout funded from the balance sheet, a capex step-up from $22.8M to $55-65M, and no book-equity cushion behind ~$1.8B of notes. Competitive position at 7.0 recognizes an elite sole-source franchise on an improving trajectory, discounted for the fact that the moat is a contract with a 2030 expiry rather than a self-renewing advantage. Growth at 6.2 credits the pricing lever and the `.web` option without underwriting either at more than a modest outcome. Management quality at 5.8 nets disciplined buybacks and minimal dilution against a triple-hatted 70-year-old CEO with no named successor, compensation up 54% over four years against a 13% five-year TSR, and one-way insider selling. Risk factors at 5.5 reflect near-absolute revenue concentration and a 2029/2030 renewal reset only partially priced by the compressed multiple, offset by the removal of the `.web` litigation and the absence of any near-term binary.

At $286.58 — 30.0x FY2026E, 26.6x FY2027E, ~4.1% FCF yield, after a ~12% move in five weeks that spanned the delegation and the beat-and-raise — the shares are fairly valued. The two structural levers are real, and they are approximately what the market is paying for. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 6.4, and would move to BUY in the $255-265 range or on disclosed `.web` volumes above roughly 3 million names.
