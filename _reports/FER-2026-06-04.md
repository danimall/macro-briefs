**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ferrovial SE is one of the world's premier private infrastructure developers, operating a concentrated portfolio of high-quality toll-road, managed-lane, and airport-development concessions across North America and Europe. The investment case is straightforward in concept: a handful of irreplaceable, inflation-linked, long-duration assets (the 407 ETR in Toronto, US managed lanes in Texas and Virginia) throw off growing cash flows, and management recycles capital out of mature assets into a US growth pipeline. The 2023 Dutch re-domiciliation and 2024 Nasdaq listing were engineered specifically to tilt the portfolio toward USD-denominated North American infrastructure and access deeper capital markets.

The numbers require careful framing. Revenue compounded roughly 8.4% annually from €7.55B (2022) to €9.63B (2025). The headline 2024 net profit of €3.49B (€3.24B attributable) is a disposal mirage, driven overwhelmingly by the €2.0B sale of the 19.75% Heathrow stake closed in December 2024. Normalized 2025 attributable profit was €888M. Against a €47.8B market capitalization, the stock trades at roughly 50x normalized earnings and 46.8x reported EPS. Ferrovial does not trade on accounting EPS — the most valuable assets (407 ETR, managed-lane stakes) are equity-accounted, so consolidated financials understate economic scale. The company is valued on a sum-of-the-parts of its look-through concession stakes, which is why the optically high P/E coexists with a defensible market value.

We rate the stock HOLD with a growth bias. The asset quality is genuine and the capital-recycling discipline is demonstrated, but the price at $66.39 captures most of the visible NAV, the stock has advanced about 30% over the trailing period and sits 11% below its 52-week high of $74.79, and the near-term options curve is in backwardation pricing event stress. With a base case roughly in line with current levels and analyst targets clustered tightly at the low end ($69.99 low, $70.05 median), the asymmetry does not support a high-conviction long here. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$50s or on confirmation that look-through concession value is re-rating.

## Company Overview

Ferrovial SE (incorporated in the Netherlands, operationally headquartered in Madrid) develops, finances, and operates infrastructure concessions. As a foreign private issuer, it files Form 20-F annual reports under IFRS in euros rather than 10-Ks under US GAAP. Its portfolio breaks into four areas:

- **Toll roads / managed lanes**: The 407 ETR, a high-margin, inflation-linked Toronto electronic toll highway held as an equity-method associate, is one of the most valuable toll-road assets globally. The US managed lanes (NTE, LBJ, NTE 35W in Texas; I-66 and I-77 in Virginia) use dynamic congestion pricing, providing structural inflation protection and the company's primary growth engine.
- **Airports**: Historically a major Heathrow shareholder (exited December 2024), now pivoting toward US airport development including the New Terminal One at JFK.
- **Construction**: Webber and Ferrovial Construction, a lower-margin fixed-price business.
- **Energy / Mobility**: An emerging vertical.

The company consolidates only some assets; the crown jewels are equity-accounted. Reported revenue therefore understates economic scale, and net income is heavily influenced by disposals and equity-method swings. This structural feature must be understood before interpreting any of the financials.

Revenue growth has been steady and broad-based, reflecting traffic growth on managed lanes, contractual toll escalators, and construction activity. The 2024 earnings spike is not run-rate; it reflects the Heathrow disposal gain and normalizes lower in 2025. Any screen anchoring on 2024's attributable profit shows a deceptively cheap ~5x trailing P/E that bears no relation to economic reality.

The most constructive signal is the cash-flow improvement: operating cash flow rose roughly 49% to €1.93B in 2025 from approximately €1.3B, even as headline profit fell. This divergence indicates the core concession portfolio is maturing into stronger underlying cash generation, distinct from disposal-driven accounting gains. Equity declined to €7.67B despite positive earnings, consistent with continued shareholder returns (buybacks plus dividends). Total assets fell to €27.4B, consistent with deconsolidation as mature assets are sold.

On leverage, the standard caveat for concession holding companies applies. Ferrovial runs substantial non-recourse project debt at the SPV level, structurally ring-fenced from the parent. Headline consolidated debt overstates recourse leverage, and traditional distress metrics (Altman Z, Piotroski) are not meaningful for this structure. The recourse-versus-non-recourse net-debt split is the key item we would want to confirm from the 20-F to finalize the financial-health read; based on the available cash-flow strength and the Heathrow proceeds de-risking the parent balance sheet, we assess parent-level leverage as manageable. The trailing dividend yield of 1.95% is modest and well-covered by operating cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is multi-year and rests on the US managed-lanes franchise and airport-development pipeline. Managed lanes monetize congestion through dynamic pricing, which delivers both volume growth (as US road traffic recovers and grows) and price growth (inflation-linked escalators plus congestion-driven peak pricing). This is a structurally attractive model because few competitors replicate it at scale and because chronic US infrastructure underinvestment combined with growing political appetite for public-private partnerships creates a long greenfield runway.

The four-quarter earnings record shows consistent beats (5.3% in Q4 2025, 10.4% in Q3 2025), confirming reasonable forward visibility on the operating book even though the 2024 numbers are distorted by the disposal. Analyst sentiment has been improving, with one recent upgrade to strong-buy and a reduction in hold ratings.

The constraint on the growth score is twofold. First, growth is partly a function of recycling — selling mature assets to fund development — so reported consolidated growth understates and overstates economic growth in different periods depending on disposal timing. Second, the present value of long-duration concession cash flows is highly rate-sensitive. In a higher-for-longer environment, even strong underlying traffic and toll growth can be offset by a higher discount rate compressing NAV. The mechanism is direct: higher rates lead to a higher discount applied to 20-to-50-year concession cash flows and lower present value, plus higher refinancing costs on project debt and higher P3 financing costs that compress returns on new development.

## Valuation Assessment

Ferrovial cannot be valued on conventional earnings multiples. The trailing and forward multiples look extreme, but they are artifacts of equity-method accounting that excludes the economic earnings of the most valuable assets from the consolidated P/L. The correct framework is sum-of-the-parts on look-through stakes: the 407 ETR equity stake, the managed-lane concessions, the construction book, and net parent cash/debt.

The challenge for the valuation score is that the SOTP framework is precisely what the market already applies, and the price appears to capture most of the visible NAV. The analyst price-target distribution is informative here: low $69.99, median $70.05, mean $77.68, high $92.99. The tight clustering of the low and median targets just above the current price (roughly 5-6% upside to median) indicates that the consensus does not see deep mispricing. The mean is pulled higher by a single bullish outlier at $92.99. The company's earnings are normalizing, not accelerating, and the valuation is rich on every conventional metric, so we do not treat the targets as conservative floors lagging a re-rating.

This is a high-quality compounder fairly priced for its quality. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects balanced asymmetry: the assets justify a premium, but the premium is largely in the price. A re-rating requires either a fall in rates that lifts the present value of the concession book, a value-crystallizing disposal at a premium to carrying value, or evidence that managed-lane traffic and pricing are running materially ahead of consensus. None of these is imminent.

## Competitive Landscape

Ferrovial's competitive position is genuinely strong and, on trajectory, improving. It competes against Vinci, ACS/Abertis, Mundys (formerly Atlantia), and large infrastructure funds (IFM, GIP, Macquarie, Ardian). Its differentiation is a demonstrated ability to develop greenfield toll and managed-lane assets in the US, a capability few peers possess at scale. The managed-lanes model gives pricing power and inflation protection that are difficult to replicate.

The Heathrow exit and US pivot represent a deliberate upgrade of portfolio quality: out of a mature, regulated European airport with capped returns, into higher-growth USD development assets with pricing power. The 407 ETR remains a near-irreplaceable franchise asset with decades of concession life and contractual escalators. Customer concentration is not a meaningful issue for a toll operator; the relevant concentration risk is asset-level (a small number of crown jewels carry most of the value), which we treat under risk rather than competitive position. On trajectory — share of the US P3 market, pipeline depth, and asset quality post-Heathrow — the position is improving, supporting a 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are specific and material, which caps the risk score at 5.5 despite the balance-sheet strength.

1. **Asset concentration.** A disproportionate share of intrinsic value sits in 407 ETR and the US managed lanes. A traffic shock, an adverse concession renegotiation, or a regulatory cap on toll escalation would have an outsized valuation impact.
2. **Rate sensitivity.** Long-duration concession cash flows are the dominant value driver, and their present value moves inversely with rates. In a higher-for-longer environment, the discount-rate risk is live and asymmetric to the downside if yields back up.
3. **Equity-method opacity.** Reported earnings are volatile and disposal-driven, which makes the stock prone to misinterpretation and to sentiment swings around quarterly prints that do not reflect underlying economics.
4. **Construction-segment execution.** Fixed-price construction is lower-margin and exposed to cost overruns and claims, a persistent drag on the consolidated picture.
5. **Possible auditor transition (EY → PwC)** between the FY2024 and FY2025 20-Fs. Auditor changes warrant scrutiny; we flag this for confirmation but assign it low probability of indicating an accounting problem given the clean cash-flow trajectory.
6. **Governance and re-domiciliation scrutiny.** The Dutch move drew political criticism in Spain; cross-border tax and governance complexity remains. The del Pino family anchor stake aligns long-term interests but concentrates control.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 32.2% against 23.2% trailing realized volatility — rich by 9 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (32.2% at one month versus 26.5% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst or elevated event risk. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.7%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%) and put/call open interest is mildly elevated at 1.16, so the positioning is not aggressively bearish, but the backwardation argues against initiating a long here at full valuation. Short interest is negligible (0.6% of float, 1.9 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic to consider in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$93, the high analyst target):** Rates ease, lifting the present value of the long-duration concession book. US managed-lane traffic and dynamic pricing run ahead of consensus, the JFK and broader US airport-development pipeline crystallizes value, and a premium disposal or a 407 ETR revaluation forces the market to mark up the SOTP. Continued buybacks at sensible prices compound per-share NAV. The stock re-rates toward $93 over 18-24 months.

**Bear case (~$56, roughly -16%):** Higher-for-longer rates compress the present value of concession cash flows and raise refinancing and P3 financing costs. A traffic or regulatory shock at a crown-jewel asset impairs NAV. Construction claims or cost overruns drag consolidated results, and the equity-method opacity amplifies a sentiment de-rating. The stock retraces toward the high-$50s.

**Base case (~$70, roughly +5%):** Underlying cash generation continues to strengthen (operating cash flow held near or above €1.9B), managed-lane and toll-road growth proceeds in line, and capital recycling continues. The stock drifts modestly higher toward the median analyst target as NAV compounds, but the premium valuation and rate backdrop cap near-term upside.

The net of these scenarios is roughly symmetric around the current price, with the bull case requiring a rate tailwind largely outside management's control. That symmetry, against a rich and backwardated options curve, is why this is a HOLD and not a BUY despite genuine asset quality. The specific, falsifiable concern that prevents a BUY: at $66.39 the stock trades at the consensus low/median target band with only ~5% to the median, the price already captures the visible look-through NAV, and the dominant value driver (present value of long-duration concession cash flows) is exposed to a live higher-for-longer rate risk with asymmetric downside. A BUY here would require either a discount to SOTP we cannot currently identify or an estimate-revision inflection, neither of which is present: 2025 earnings normalized lower, they did not accelerate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next 20-F / interim results and the upcoming FOMC decision, which will clarify the rate path that drives concession-NAV discounting.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$50s that opens a clear discount to sum-of-the-parts NAV (downside-to-base asymmetry of 2:1 or better).
- A material easing in long-end yields that lifts the present value of the concession book, OR a value-crystallizing disposal/revaluation (e.g., a managed-lane or 407 ETR transaction marked above carrying value).
- Confirmation that operating cash flow is sustaining above €2.0B with managed-lane traffic and pricing running ahead of consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of an adverse concession renegotiation or toll-escalation cap at a crown-jewel asset.
- A confirmed accounting problem tied to the auditor transition, or a deterioration in parent-level (recourse) leverage.
- A sustained back-up in long-end yields that structurally compresses concession NAV without offsetting traffic/pricing growth.

## Conclusion

Ferrovial is a high-quality infrastructure operator with irreplaceable assets, a demonstrated capital-recycling discipline, improving underlying cash generation, and a structurally attractive US growth runway. The constraint is price: at $66.39 the stock trades at a full NAV-driven valuation, the consensus sees only mid-single-digit upside to the median target, and the dominant value driver is exposed to rate risk that is currently a headwind. The near-term options curve confirms event stress. We rate FER a HOLD with a growth bias, recognizing the quality of the franchise while declining to chase it at a level that offers limited asymmetric upside. The right entry is a pullback toward the high-$50s or a rate-driven re-rating of the concession book.
