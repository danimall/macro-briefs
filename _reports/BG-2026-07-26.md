**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bunge Global is a top-tier global agribusiness ("ABCD" major) whose July 2, 2025 acquisition of Viterra roughly doubled its balance sheet and reorganized it into a four-segment value chain spanning soybean and softseed crush, tropical oils/specialty ingredients, and grain merchandising. The stock has risen ~58% off its $74.95 fifty-two-week low to ~$121.53, and the central question is whether that move reflects a durable earnings recovery or a cyclical head-fake.

The evidence has moved enough to warrant an upgrade to BUY from a prior HOLD. The most important reconciliation: headline GAAP Q1 2026 EPS of $0.35 badly understates the operating result. On the adjusted basis consensus tracks, Q1 2026 EPS was $1.83 versus a $0.90 estimate, a 103% beat and the fourth consecutive beat. The GAAP figure was depressed by ~65.6M shares of Viterra dilution, a one-time $118M pension-settlement loss, a $30M investment impairment, and the absence of a prior-year $195M divestiture gain. Segment EBIT rose year-over-year ($2,159M to $2,329M). Consensus EPS has been revised up +13.1% for the current year (to $9.49) and +7.6% for next year (to $11.29) over 90 days, and the trailing 32x P/E dissolves to ~11-13x on those forward figures.

The offsets are real but largely bounded. Rating agencies affirmed investment grade through the deal (S&P upgraded to A-), and ~$13.4B of the balance sheet is readily marketable inventory carried at fair value, so leverage optics overstate economic risk. The durable cautions are negative 2025 free cash flow into a rising interest burden ($628M and climbing), a thin ~9% goodwill cushion in a Grain Merchandising unit that just posted a quarterly loss, and a 34% shareholder bloc (Glencore ~17%, CPP ~14%) that both aligns and constrains. With ~16% upside to the $141 mean target and only ~4% downside to the $116 analyst low, the asymmetry favors accumulation, sized with discipline.

## Company Overview

Bunge is Swiss-domiciled (Geneva registered office, corporate operations in Chesterfield, Missouri) and connects farmers to food, feed, and fuel markets. It competes in the global grain/oilseed oligopoly alongside ADM, Cargill, and Louis Dreyfus, where the durable advantage is the geographic footprint of origination, processing, and logistics assets rather than margin.

Effective Q3 2025, Bunge realigned into four reportable segments:

1. **Soybean Processing & Refining** — the largest (FY25 net sales $36.3B; EBIT $1,225M, +40% YoY), including the Bunge Chevron renewable-diesel JV.
2. **Softseed Processing & Refining** — canola/rapeseed and sunflower (FY25 net sales $11.3B; EBIT $521M, −21%), a large Viterra contributor.
3. **Tropical Oils & Specialty Ingredients** — palm/tropical oils and specialty products (FY25 net sales $4.6B; EBIT $118M).
4. **Grain Merchandising & Milling** — corn, wheat, barley, cotton, pulses, sugar, milling, ocean freight and financial services (FY25 net sales $18.1B; EBIT $465M, +14%). Viterra roughly doubled grain volumes (67.2M MT vs 36.7M MT).

The Viterra combination is a scale play to close the gap with Cargill and ADM in grain origination and handling, particularly in Canada (canola), Australia, and the Black Sea, complementing Bunge's traditional strength in oilseed crush and South America.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings — cyclical normalization plus deal transition.** Revenue moved $59.5B (2023) → $53.1B (2024) → $70.3B (2025), with Q1 2026 at $21.9B (+88% YoY). The step-up is Viterra, consolidated from July 2, 2025 — only six months in 2025, so 2026 carries a full-year run-rate. Net income attributable fell to $816M in 2025 from $1,137M (2024) and $2,243M (2023), and GAAP diluted EPS dropped to $4.91 from $7.99, absorbing both lower cyclical earnings and Viterra share issuance.

**Reconciling the Q1 optics.** The GAAP Q1 2026 result ($68M net income) reads as a weak start, but the gap between GAAP and adjusted is entirely non-operational and transition-related (dilution, the pension settlement, the impairment, and the missing prior-year gain). Beat quality is mixed rather than pristine: Tropical Oils EBIT surged to $110M (from $5M) partly on mark-to-market gains that the filings flag as reversible, and Grain Merchandising swung to a −$76M EBIT loss on ocean-freight losses tied to higher oil prices. This is a cyclical trough with deal-transition noise, not a structural collapse.

**Track record.** Four consecutive beats: Q2 2025 +11.5%, Q3 2025 +5.3%, Q4 2025 +6.5%, Q1 2026 +103.4%. The streak raises confidence that current consensus is achievable-to-conservative.

**Margins** are structurally thin (FY25 gross ~4.8%, net ~1.2%, operating ~0.4%), which is normal for a commodity trader where value comes from asset turnover (~1.58x) and volume, not price spread.

**Cash flow — the near-term soft spot.** Operating cash flow fell to $844M in FY25 (from $1,900M), against $1,723M of capex — negative free cash flow for the year. Q1 2026 saw a −$541M operating outflow on a seasonal South American working-capital build (larger than the −$285M prior-year quarter). 2026 capex is guided to $1.5-1.7B. Cash generation is the weakest link in the current profile.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt rose from $6.24B (end-2024) to $14.05B (end-2025) and $14.55B at Q1 2026, funding Viterra ($1.88B cash + $3.55B Viterra debt repayment + assumed notes). Interest expense reached $628M in FY25 (~$181M in Q1 2026 alone). Current ratio fell to 1.61, D/E is ~2.8x, and Altman Z is 2.56 (grey zone). All three agencies reviewed pro-forma leverage and affirmed investment grade — S&P upgraded to A-, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+, all Stable — with no rating-downgrade acceleration triggers and full covenant compliance. Roughly $13.4B of inventory is readily marketable and carried at fair value, and much of the debt funds hedged, marketable stock, so the leverage looks worse than the economic reality. The more relevant risk is the rising cash interest burden landing during an earnings trough with negative FCF; a 100bp rate move is roughly a $99M interest impact.

**Capital allocation.** A new $3.0B buyback was authorized March 9, 2026 (~$3.2B total remaining), with zero shares repurchased in Q1, a disciplined signal that management is prioritizing deleveraging and integration over financial engineering, consistent with the AROIC-based compensation design. The quarterly dividend is $0.70 with a proposed raise to $0.72 ($2.88 annualized), a ~2.3% yield.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth vectors are in play. First, the mechanical full-year Viterra consolidation lifts 2026 revenue and earnings simply from twelve months versus six. Second, synergy capture — origination reach in Canada, Australia, and the Black Sea combined with crush and biofuel feedstock demand — is the 2026-2028 value lever, now explicitly tied to management compensation through a new Executive Integration Incentive plan.

The estimate-revision picture is constructive but moderate rather than extreme. Current-year EPS was revised up +13.1% over 90 days ($8.39 → $9.49), next-year +7.6% ($10.50 → $11.29), and current-quarter +11.4%. These are firmly positive and support the recovery path, but they fall short of the +30% velocity that flags the strongest inflection archetype, and much of the revenue "growth" is acquisition-driven rather than organic. Adjusted EPS remains well below the 2021-22 super-cycle peak (12.93 → 7.57 in 2025), so the durability of the recovery depends on synergy execution and where the crush/grain cycle settles.

Structural industry tailwinds — biofuel/renewable-diesel feedstock demand, global protein and vegetable-oil consumption — support the multi-year case. Headwinds include crush margins normalizing off super-cycle highs, on-farm storage giving producers pricing power, petroleum majors entering renewable feedstock, and ocean freight as a newly-visible earnings swing factor (evident in the Q1 grain loss).

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 32x P/E reported on the profile is a trough-earnings artifact — depressed GAAP net income compounded by Viterra dilution and one-time charges. On the adjusted forward estimates that the sell-side and management use, Bunge trades at roughly 12.8x current-year and 10.8x next-year EPS. For a commodity trader emerging from a cyclical low with rising estimates and an IG-rated, marketable-inventory-backed balance sheet, that is inexpensive.

Peer context is muddied by the same GAAP distortion — ADM screens at 35.3x and Darling at 41.4x on trailing figures, while Ingredion, the cleanest comparable, sits at 9.7x. Against a peer-median 35.3x P/E and Ingredion's 9.7x, Bunge's forward multiple is reasonable-to-cheap for a business with a full-year Viterra step-up ahead and a defined synergy catalyst. That catalyst — synergy capture, deleveraging, and full-year Viterra flow-through — is the specific reason the market is under-crediting the forward earnings power, which distinguishes this from a pure low-multiple value trap.

The sell-side is constructive without being frothy: 3 strong-buy, 5 buy, 1 hold, zero sells; mean target $141.44, median $142, high $150, low $116. That implies ~16% upside to the mean and only ~4% downside to the low. Commodity traders are the archetypal setting where a cheap trailing/forward multiple on peak-of-cycle crush earnings becomes a value trap when the cycle rolls, so the valuation deserves a constructive rather than maximum score. Modeling ~14x on current-year adjusted EPS yields ~$133, and ~12x on next-year yields ~$135, supporting a base target in the mid-$130s.

## Competitive Landscape

Bunge operates in a consolidating oligopoly where scale in origination and logistics is the moat. Viterra materially improves that scale — doubling grain volumes and adding Canadian canola, Australian, and Black Sea origination — narrowing the gap with Cargill and ADM. The strategic logic is a more integrated value chain (origination + crush + biofuel feedstock) that should dampen quarter-to-quarter volatility over time.

The competitive trajectory is improving on paper, but the improvement is unproven in the numbers: the newly-scaled Grain Merchandising segment is the one that just posted a loss, and integration is explicitly a multi-year undertaking. Bolt-on moves up the value chain (IFF's lecithin/soy-protein businesses for $105M, closed March 2026) and portfolio pruning (North America corn milling, BISA, BP Bunge Bioenergia, pending European margarines sale) suggest a coherent reshaping toward higher-value specialty ingredients. Net trajectory is positive, tempered by execution risk and the fact that scale advantages in a price-competitive commodity market translate to earnings only if synergies land.

## Risk Assessment

**Grain Merchandising goodwill cushion (the sharpest binary).** The Grain Merchandising reporting unit carries only ~9% excess fair value over carrying value (Global Cotton ~15%), and the segment posted a Q1 2026 EBIT loss. Management flagged that "market downturns and higher interest rates negatively impacted the fair value." A non-cash impairment is a real risk if grain and ocean-freight conditions stay weak — a contained, monitorable risk rather than a structural solvency issue, but a live one.

**Cash flow and interest burden.** Negative 2025 FCF, the Q1 operating outflow, and a climbing interest burden all land during an earnings trough. This constrains the pace of deleveraging and the timing of buyback resumption.

**Governance concentration.** Former Viterra owners hold ~34% (Glencore ~17%, CPP ~14%, BCI ~3%), with Glencore and CPP each holding board-nomination rights. Glencore is simultaneously a large shareholder with board seats and a commodity-trading competitor/counterparty — an atypical related-party structure that both aligns interests and could deter takeover premiums. Two unexplained Item 5.02 executive/director changes (April 1, 2026 and December 12, 2025) are open items.

**Integration execution.** Management states integration will "continue in phases over the next several years"; acquisition/integration costs were $223M in 2025 with more to come. Synergy capture is the central thesis risk.

**Commodity, policy, and geographic.** Crush-margin and mark-to-market volatility drive quarterly noise; ocean freight is now a visible swing factor. EUDR deforestation regulation is effective December 2026 (palm and soy exposure). Emerging-market exposure includes Brazil (PIS/COFINS claims risen to $594M; ~$835M farmer credit), Argentina FX controls, and Ukraine (four crush plants, two export terminals, <2% of assets but strategic). Swiss-domicile mechanics impose 35% withholding-tax constraints on capital returns.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress and modest downside hedging. Near-term ATM IV is 36.4% versus 30.4% trailing historical volatility (rich by ~6pp), with a 3.7% put/call skew (elevated put demand) and an implied ±8.3% move into the next expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (36.4% near-term versus 31.1% at six months), and the P/C open-interest ratio has risen from 0.49 to 0.91 since late May. This is consistent with hedging into a catalyst — the next earnings print and the goodwill-impairment watch — rather than a directional collapse thesis, but it argues for patience on entry and for sizing that can tolerate an event-driven move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Full-year Viterra consolidation plus synergy capture lifts adjusted EPS toward and beyond the $11.29 next-year consensus. The market re-rates the forward multiple from ~11-13x toward the mid-teens as integration proceeds and FCF turns positive on deleveraging, and management resumes buybacks against the $3.2B authorization. The IG balance sheet, RMI-backed inventory, and a ~2.3% dividend cushion the downside. Target $150-165 (roughly the high analyst target and above), or +23% to +36%.

**Bear case.** The crush/grain cycle rolls over, Tropical Oils MTM gains reverse, and Grain Merchandising takes a goodwill impairment. Negative FCF persists, the rising interest burden compresses earnings, and integration synergies disappoint. Adjusted EPS reverts toward ~$8, and the multiple compresses to ~10x, taking the stock toward $95-105 (−14% to −22%), below the analyst low of $116, with the dividend and IG rating limiting further downside.

**Base case.** Bunge delivers a full-year Viterra step-up with moderate synergy progress, adjusted EPS lands near $9.49 for the year and builds toward $11.29, and the market applies ~14x current-year adjusted earnings. Target ~$135, roughly +11%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$135** — ~14x current-year adjusted EPS, consistent with a partial re-rating as full-year Viterra flows through and deleveraging proceeds. Upside of ~11% from $121.53.

**Bull case target: $150-165** — mid-teens multiple on next-year adjusted EPS with meaningful synergy capture and positive FCF inflection. Upside of ~23% to ~36%.

**Bear case target: $95-105** — ~10x on ~$8 reverted adjusted EPS with a Grain Merchandising goodwill impairment and cycle rollover. Downside of ~14% to ~22%.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to the full-year Viterra P&L flowing through 2026, quarterly synergy commentary, and the goodwill re-test in subsequent 2026 quarters.

**Upside/downside from current ($121.53):** +11% to base, +23-36% to bull, −14-22% to bear. The ~4% cushion to the $116 analyst low anchors near-term downside.

**Thesis invalidation triggers (exit or downgrade to HOLD/AVOID):**
1. A goodwill impairment in the Grain Merchandising unit, or a second consecutive Grain Merchandising quarterly EBIT loss, signaling the cushion is eroding.
2. Current-year adjusted EPS estimates revised down more than 10% from the current $9.49, or a quarterly adjusted miss reversing the beat streak.
3. FCF remaining negative through 2026 with the interest burden climbing and no buyback execution, indicating deleveraging is stalling.

## Conclusion

Bunge earns a BUY, reversing the prior HOLD, on a reconciliation the earlier read got wrong: the Q1 2026 GAAP EPS was a transition artifact, while the adjusted result reflects the actual operating trajectory. On forward adjusted earnings, the stock trades at ~11-13x with estimates rising, an IG balance sheet upgraded to A- through the Viterra close, and marketable inventory backing the leverage. The risk/reward is asymmetric: ~16% to the mean consensus target against ~4% to the analyst low.

This is a constructive, disciplined-sizing BUY rather than a maximum-conviction call. The earnings are inherently reversible (commodity/MTM), the growth is largely acquisition-mechanical, 2026 is the true test as full-year Viterra and integration costs flow through, and the Grain Merchandising goodwill cushion is a live, monitorable risk. Accumulate on weakness, particularly given the backwardated options market pricing near-term stress, and monitor the invalidation triggers; a goodwill impairment or an FCF/deleveraging stall would return this to a HOLD.
