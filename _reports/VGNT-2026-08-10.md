## Executive Summary

Versigent is the former Electrical Distribution Systems segment of Aptiv PLC, separated on April 1, 2026 via tax-free spin-off at one Versigent share per three Aptiv shares. It designs and manufactures low- and high-voltage electrical architectures (wire harnesses and power/signal/data distribution systems) for automotive OEMs, with approximately 138,000 employees across more than 25 countries. The stock has re-rated from a $27.85 first-day close to $43.81, and now trades at 4.82x guided FY2026 EV/Adjusted EBITDA (5.02x including the $199M noncontrolling interest) and roughly 5.7x annualized Q2 adjusted earnings, with a guided free-cash-flow yield of 8.1% at the midpoint.

The August 4 report was operationally solid: Q2 net sales of $2,444M rose 10.8%, Adjusted EBITDA rose 24.8% to $272M with margin up 120bp to 11.1%, and management raised FY2026 sales guidance to $9,400-9,600M from $9,100-9,400M. Three facts constrain how much of that should be capitalized. First, the sales range moved up $300M at both ends while the Adjusted EBITDA range ($950-1,030M) and FCF range ($200-300M) were left unchanged; at the guided ~10.4% midpoint margin, $300M of revenue should carry roughly $31M of EBITDA, so the incremental revenue carries approximately zero margin. Management attributed the raise to "higher commodity pass-throughs and FX impacts," which is internally consistent. Second, the implied second half backs out to $4,844M of sales and $515M of Adjusted EBITDA, a 10.6% margin below the 11.1% just delivered. Third, H1 net income attributable fell to $196M from $202M and H1 diluted EPS fell to $2.75 from $2.85, because $41M of new interest expense and $48M of separation costs are real cash and are excluded from Adjusted EBITDA but not from earnings.

We rate the shares HOLD. The multiple is low in absolute terms, but the discount is explained rather than mispriced: 12-13% gross margins, top-ten customers at 83% of Q2 sales with three individually at 20%, 18% and 14%, no production-part contracts beyond one year and no enforceable right to payment on customized parts, a high-voltage product line contracting 14.9%, an AOCI hedge buffer that largely exhausts during FY2027, and Mexican statutory minimum wages compounding roughly 52% over 2024-2026 against a business with approximately half the revenue-per-employee of its former parent. Our probability-weighted expected return from $43.81 is roughly +7%, with a base case of $49, a bull case of $62 and a bear case of $27. That asymmetry is not adequate on a 1.7x net-levered cyclical whose first audited standalone annual financial statements do not yet exist.

## Company Overview

Versigent PLC is incorporated in Jersey and tax-resident in Switzerland (Schaffhausen), listed on the NYSE under Commission file 001-42957, and operates as a single reportable segment with revenue disclosed across two product lines. Aptiv distributed all 70,893,660 shares and retained none, leaving no parent overhang and no parent backstop.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q2 gross margin was 13.1% (up from 11.9%); H1 was 12.1%, flat year over year. SG&A was 4.1% of Q2 sales versus 4.7%. Operating income rose to $199M from $131M in Q2 but only to $273M from $254M for H1, because H1 carried $46M of restructuring and $48M of separation costs. Interest expense went from $3M in H1 2025 to $41M in H1 2026.

**Growth composition.** The Q2 dollar bridge is the most important disclosure in the filing: of the $238M revenue increase, volume contributed +$120M, pricing −$18M, FX +$40M and commodity pass-through +$96M. FX plus pass-through therefore accounted for $136M, or 57% of the increase; for H1 the figure is $258M of $426M, or 61%. Management's own Adjusted Net Sales Growth measure, which strips FX and commodity, was approximately 5% in Q2 and 4% in H1.

Pass-through is margin-dilutive in transit. Q2 commodity pass-through added $96M to sales and $105M to cost of sales, a $9M reduction in gross profit; H1 added $152M and $192M respectively, a $40M reduction. Aptiv's pre-spin 10-K is explicit that copper contract clauses have enabled the segment to pass on "some" of price increases, which is partial and lagged, not complete.

Margin improvement came from elsewhere. The Q2 gross-profit bridge shows "Operational performance and other" at +$44M (+$64M for H1), the largest single driver and the least decomposable line in the filing.

**The hedge buffer.** At separation Aptiv novated FX and commodity forwards and FX options to Versigent, producing a $97M gain recorded to AOCI in Q1 2026. Net cash-flow hedge gains in AOCI at June 30 were $125M pre-tax, of which approximately $102M is scheduled to be recognized in cost of sales within twelve months and only approximately $23M thereafter. Q2 reclassifications into cost of sales totalled $35M ($16M commodity, $19M FX), equal to 1.43% of Q2 net sales. Notionals: MXN 17,902M (≈$1,022M), copper 57,556 thousand pounds (≈$354M), CNY 1,238M (≈$182M), extending to June 2028. Interest-rate exposure is not hedged with derivatives.

These gains offset higher copper and peso costs already inside cost of sales; the net commodity effect on gross profit was negative $9M in Q2 and negative $40M in H1 despite them. The buffer has a defined runway that substantially exhausts during FY2027, and replacement hedges reset at prevailing copper and peso levels while pass-through recovery continues to lag.

**Balance sheet.** Versigent paid a $1,894M cash dividend to Aptiv funded by $2,063M of net new debt. Shareholders' equity fell from $1,657M at 12/31/25 to negative $(48)M at 3/31/26 and $85M at 6/30/26. There is no goodwill and only $6M of intangibles. Tangible equity excluding the $443M deferred tax asset is negative $(165)M, so book equity is more than fully accounted for by a tax asset. Equity-based screens (ROE, P/B, and the retained-earnings term in Altman-type scores) are uninformative here because retained earnings reset to zero on April 1.

Debt: $800M 6.125% notes due 2031, $800M 6.375% notes due 2034, a $500M Term Loan A due 2031 at benchmark +1.50%, and $151M of finance leases, totalling $2,224M. Cash $554M (approximately $545M offshore) gives net debt of $1,670M. The $850M revolver is fully undrawn; total liquidity $1,404M.

The credit-relevant ratios are comfortable. Against a derived FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA base of roughly $894M (built from Aptiv's disclosed EDS segment Adjusted Operating Income of $674M plus estimated depreciation of ~$220M, and cross-checked against Versigent's own disclosed H1 2025 margin of 9.8%, the two derivations reconcile within ~30bp), net leverage is approximately 1.87x; on the FY2026 guided midpoint of $990M it is roughly 1.69x, against covenant maximums of 4.25x stepping to 3.75x. Interest coverage of roughly 6.6x compares with a 3.00x minimum. We have not verified that the Credit Agreement's "Consolidated EBITDA" definition equals reported Adjusted EBITDA; add-back baskets typically make covenant measures more permissive.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $194M against $190M, with capex up 48% to $117M from $79M, producing free cash flow of $77M versus $111M. Accounts receivable consumed $278M as AR rose from $1,567M to $1,877M (DSO approximately 70 days), partly reflecting a mix shift into bank notes receivable ($51M at 6/30/26 versus $0 at year-end). Two timing items prohibit annualizing: cash paid for interest in H1 was only $3M because the notes pay semi-annually beginning October 15, 2026, and H1 absorbed $37M of cash restructuring plus $48M of separation charges. As a labelled sensitivity, FY guidance of $200-300M implies H2 FCF of $123-223M against roughly $80-90M of H2 cash interest, which is demanding but achievable given the seasonal Q4 working-capital release.

Capital returns: an inaugural $0.13 quarterly dividend was declared August 3 (≈$36.8M annually, 1.19% yield at $43.805, roughly 15% of the FCF midpoint) and a $250M repurchase authorization was approved in April, equal to 8.1% of market capitalization, with no executions disclosed through June 30. Full use of the authorization plus the dividend would total approximately $287M against a $250M FCF midpoint, requiring balance-sheet cash or incremental borrowing.

Financial health scores 5.4: leverage and liquidity are genuinely comfortable, but FCF conversion of 39% of net income in H1, negative tangible equity, an unhedged floating-rate tranche in a higher-for-longer rate regime, and first-year reporting without a 404(b) attestation offset the covenant headroom.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying volume growth of 4-5% against global automotive production that was roughly flat to slightly down in H1 2026 implies genuine content and share gains. That is the strongest element of the growth case. Guided FY2026 sales of $9,400-9,600M represent +6.6% to +8.9% off the $8,818M FY2025 EDS base, of which approximately 6pts is FX and pass-through. Guided Adjusted EBITDA of $950-1,030M is roughly +6% to +15% off the derived ~$894M base, midpoint +10.7%.

Estimate revisions are two-sided and internally inconsistent. Current-year consensus rose 5.4% in 30 days and 7.5% in 90 days to $7.29, and next year rose 2.9% to $7.81. But the current and next quarter were each cut 7.8% in 30 days, to $1.73 and $1.72. The full-year consensus of $7.29 sits above the sum of the disclosed H1 GAAP diluted EPS ($2.75) plus the two revised forward quarters ($1.73 + $1.72), and the mix of GAAP and adjusted figures across these vendor series does not cleanly reconcile; we therefore work with a forward earnings band rather than a point estimate. The full-year raise is mechanically the H1 beat; the forward-quarter cuts are the more forward-looking signal and align with management's implied H2 margin give-back.

The growth story's structural leg is currently contracting. High voltage at 9.1% of sales and falling means the reported top line is legacy low-voltage harness plus pass-through. Adjacent markets (agriculture, construction, grid and infrastructure, off-grid storage, robotics) carry 20% of the 2026 performance-share metric, a real strategic signal, though Aptiv's own 2025 grant carried a higher 30% weight, but Versigent discloses no adjacent-market revenue, so there is nothing to underwrite.

There is also no backlog anchor. The 10-Q states there are no contract assets or contract liabilities and no production-part contracts beyond one year, and contains no remaining-performance-obligation disclosure. Management's "expanding book of business" is an unquantified issuer characterization with no filed metric behind it and should not be treated as backlog.

Growth prospects score 4.8: mid-single-digit volume growth outpacing production, with the higher-content product line shrinking, pass-through inflating optics, and forward quarters being revised down.

## Valuation Assessment

All multiples below are derived from the frozen $43.805 basis.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market capitalization | $3.103B |
| Net debt | $1,670M |
| Enterprise value (ex-NCI / incl NCI) | $4.773B / $4.972B |
| EV / FY26 guided Adj. EBITDA ($990M) | **4.82x / 5.02x** |
| EV / derived FY25 Adj. EBITDA (~$894M) | 5.34x |
| P/E on 2× H1 GAAP diluted EPS ($5.50) | 8.0x |
| P/E on Q2 adjusted diluted EPS annualized ($7.68) | 5.7x |
| FCF yield on $250M guidance midpoint | **8.1%** |
| Dividend yield ($0.52 annualized) | 1.19% |
| Buyback authorization / market cap | 8.1% |

The supplied peer median P/E of 21.2 is drawn from specialty retailers and is not a valid benchmark. Judged on its own economics, a 4.8x EV/EBITDA multiple for a harness manufacturer is broadly consistent with where labor-intensive, low-margin, OEM-concentrated auto suppliers have historically been capitalized, and the reason for that convention is the same set of facts present here: 12-13% gross margins, annual contractual price step-downs that Aptiv quantifies at 1-3% of sales industry-wide, and demand controlled by a handful of counterparties who can cancel programs without notice. Aptiv spun this business and kept the 26%-gross-margin connector franchise and the software/sensing business while stating a strategy of "focusing our portfolio in high-technology, high-growth spaces." The multiple is low because the asset is structurally low-multiple, not because the market has made an error.

What argues the discount is nonetheless too wide: an 8.1% FCF yield with a 1.19% covered dividend and an untapped 8.1%-of-cap buyback provides absolute return support that does not require multiple expansion; $94M of H1 restructuring and separation charges (2.0% of sales) contains a genuinely non-recurring separation component that rolls off; capex up 48% is consistent with the automation response required by Mexican wage inflation, funded by a standalone owner rather than a parent allocating capital elsewhere; and an incentive plan weighting ROIC at 80% with net debt in the denominator is well-constructed for a leveraged converter.

What argues against it: 4.8x is measured against management guidance, not against audited standalone annual results, because Versigent has never filed a 10-K. The FY2025 denominator is our derivation. The FY2027 earnings path faces roughly $80M of hedge-support fade, compounding Mexican wages, and a possible statutory reduction of the Mexican work week from 48 to 40 hours as early as January 1, 2027. And on GAAP earnings the multiple is roughly 6.7-8x (Q2 annualized to Q2×2 basis), not the adjusted 5.7x.

Analyst coverage is uniformly positive (3 strong buy, 8 buy, 0 hold, with the hold rating removed in the last month) and the target range of $52-60 (mean $56.20) sits 18.7% to 37.0% above spot. On a low-single-digit-EBITDA-multiple stock those targets are not evidence of a stretched multiple, but they are also from a coverage group four months into following a first-year spin without an audited annual report, and we treat them as corroborating rather than confirming.

Valuation scores 5.8: cheap in absolute terms with real cash-yield support, but the discount is substantially explained by business quality rather than by identifiable mispricing, and the FY2027 earnings base is contested.

## Competitive Landscape

Aptiv's pre-spin 10-K names the segment's competitors as Furukawa Electric, Lear Corporation, Sumitomo Electric Industries and Yazaki Corporation, a concentrated oligopoly in which two of the largest participants are Japanese groups and Yazaki is private, so a meaningful share of industry capacity is not managed to quarterly public-market margin targets. That limits the scope for price-led share gains and also limits the risk of a new entrant.

Versigent's positional strengths are scale, an established best-cost regional footprint (North America from Mexico, South America from Brazil, Europe from Eastern Europe and North Africa, Asia Pacific from China), roughly 34% contingent labor providing variable-cost flexibility, and customer co-investment in engineering (Aptiv quantifies co-investment at 25-35% of engineering expense industry-wide, with increasing volume-independent cost-recovery payments). The OEM shift toward pre-assembled, fully engineered systems and in-region-for-region supply favors incumbents of this size.

Trajectory is mixed. Volume outgrowing production is a share/content positive. Against that: gross margin at 12-13% is the thinnest link in the supply chain; the highest-content product line is shrinking; realized pricing was −0.7% in Q2 and −0.26% in H1, better than the 1-3% industry norm, most plausibly because inflation and commodity recoveries are temporarily offsetting contractual step-downs rather than because the pricing structure has improved; APAC is both the fastest-growing region (+23.7%) and the one where domestic Chinese OEMs are displacing the non-Chinese OEMs that anchor this business; and EMEA is contracting 5.6% with a European site closure driving ~$33M of H1 restructuring.

Competitive position scores 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and it worsened.** Top-ten customers were approximately 83% of Q2 sales versus approximately 79% a year earlier, with three individual customers at 20%, 18% and 14% (from 19%, 14% and 12%). Aptiv's ten largest customers were approximately 56% of sales with a maximum of approximately 10% to any single OEM, so Versigent is materially more concentrated than the parent it came from. Compounding this, the 10-Q discloses no production-part contracts beyond one year, customer ability to cancel a program without any notification period, and no enforceable right to payment for highly customized parts with no alternative use. A $6M bad-debt charge in H1 2025 from a Chinese customer that ceased operations demonstrates the tail is live. The regional mix shift toward APAC changes geographic composition; it does not diversify the customer base, which moved the other way.

**Mexican labor cost inflation is the least visible risk in Versigent's own filings.** Statutory minimum wage increases of approximately 20% (Jan 2024), 12% (Jan 2025) and 13% (Jan 2026) compound to roughly 52%, and the government has signalled a possible reduction of the work week from 48 to 40 hours as early as January 1, 2027. Aptiv's own words: these increases are "necessitating a strategic review of more cost-competitive jurisdictions and a greater acceleration in manufacturing automation." Versigent's revenue per employee of roughly $69K compares with roughly $146K Aptiv-wide, so its earnings are materially more levered to wage inflation. Its MXN hedge notional is 2.9x its copper notional, so the peso, not copper, is the largest hedged exposure. Hedging to June 2028 defers rather than resolves a structural wage trend. The statutory minimum is not Versigent's average wage, and the 40-hour proposal is not enacted, so the magnitude is uncertain rather than absent.

**Input costs.** Copper is the dominant material input with partial, lagged pass-through. The mechanical result in H1 2026 was +$152M of pass-through revenue against +$192M of pass-through cost, a −$40M hit to gross profit: rising copper inflates the revenue line while compressing both margin percentage and absolute gross profit. For a supplier at 83% top-ten concentration, softer end-market vehicle demand would convert directly into volume and mix risk. [unverified] Broader macro claims about mortgage rates, Fed policy dissents, ocean-freight repricing and energy-driven input inflation are not supported by the research brief and have been removed.

**Governance and structural items.** Kevin P. Clark is chair and CEO of Aptiv and a director of Versigent, and adopted a Rule 10b5-1 plan on June 3, 2026 for the sale of up to 220,000 of his 247,571 Versigent shares (approximately 89% of the disclosed holding). Routine diversification of passively received spin shares by a non-operating director is the most likely explanation and we draw no inference about his view of the business. What is objectively relevant is the structural position: Versigent purchases from Aptiv at roughly a $774M annual run-rate (approximately 8% of revenue) under agreements negotiated pre-separation, while the supplier's CEO sits on Versigent's board. CEO target compensation of approximately $11.4M, including a $9.0M annual equity target, is aggressive relative to a $3.1B market capitalization. The LTIP authorizes 6,380,429 shares (9.0% of shares outstanding), against actual nonvested RSUs of 2,332 thousand (3.29%) and actual Q2 dilution of 0.98M shares (1.4%); full utilization of the authorization would take existing holders from 100% to approximately 91.7% ownership, but that is a maximum-authorization scenario across many years, not current fully diluted capitalization.

**Verification gaps that constrain confidence.** No 10-K and no audited standalone annual financial statements exist in Versigent's EDGAR file; the audited FY2025 carve-out statements reside in the March 6, 2026 Information Statement, and Versigent's own risk factors were not read directly. No SOX 404(b) attestation exists yet for a business with 138,000 employees in 25+ countries operating partly on shared systems under a Transition Services Agreement. The Q2 earnings call transcript is unavailable, so management's explanation for the unchanged EBITDA guidance is unverified. No credit ratings were obtained; the 6.125%/6.375% coupons are consistent with a sub-investment-grade profile but no rating is confirmed. A collateral-description discrepancy between Versigent's 10-Q and Aptiv's 10-K is unresolved.

Risk factors score 4.0. Much of the above is arguably reflected in a 4.8x multiple, but the binary items (one program loss at a 20% customer, enactment of the 40-hour week) are large relative to a $85M equity base and are not quantifiable from available disclosure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($62, +41.5%).** Volume growth sustains at 5-6% as content per vehicle rises and platform standardization favors scale suppliers. Automation capex (H1 capex $117M now exceeding H1 depreciation of $111M) offsets Mexican wage inflation, and "operational performance" continues to contribute at the +$44M-per-quarter pace seen in Q2, allowing FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1,120M despite hedge fade. The $250M repurchase authorization is executed at 5-6x EBITDA, retiring roughly 7-8% of shares. Separation costs cease entirely and restructuring normalizes, lifting GAAP earnings toward adjusted. Credit ratings improve enough to suspend collateral requirements. The multiple re-rates to approximately 8x adjusted earnings as a standalone operating record accumulates. Recovering EV demand turns the high-voltage line from a 15% decline into a mix and operating-leverage tailwind.

**Bear case ($27, −38.4%).** Global auto production declines 3-5% into 2027 as a weakening consumer compresses SAAR. The AOCI hedge buffer runs out with only $23M scheduled beyond twelve months, replacement hedges reset at higher copper and peso levels, and the 40-hour Mexican work week is enacted. One of the 20%/18%/14% customers reallocates a major program (permitted contractually with no notice and no payment right) and EBITDA falls to roughly $820M. At 1.7x net leverage rising toward 2.1x on lower EBITDA, a 4.25x multiple on impaired earnings leaves equity near the 52-week low of $26.34. Thin equity ($85M book, negative tangible ex-DTA) provides no cushion, and the company has no audited standalone downturn history to reassure lenders or equity holders.

**Base case ($49, +11.9%).** FY2026 lands near the guided midpoint ($9,500M sales, $990M Adjusted EBITDA, $250M FCF). FY2027 volume grows approximately 4%; roughly $80M of hedge-support fade and Mexican wage inflation are largely offset by continued cost execution and automation benefit, holding FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA near $1,000-1,040M. Partial buyback execution and modest debt paydown take net debt to roughly $1,600M. At 5.0x EV/EBITDA on $1,020M: $5,100M EV less $1,600M net debt = $3,500M equity, or $48.9 per share on ~71.5M shares. Cross-check at 6.4x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$7.68: $49.2.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: 0.5(+11.9%) + 0.25(+41.5%) + 0.25(−38.4%) = **+6.7%** expected return. That is a positive number, but the dispersion is wide, the confidence is limited by the absence of audited annual financials, and the downside path is amplified by leverage against a near-zero equity base.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Hold rationale: uncertain.** The expected return is modestly positive, but the FY2027 margin path is genuinely two-sided and material primary disclosures (10-K, audited standalone annuals, risk factors, earnings call, credit ratings) are not yet available.

**Targets from the $43.805 basis:**
- Base case **$49** (+11.9%): 5.0x EV/EBITDA on ~$1,020M FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA, cross-checked at 6.4x ~$7.68 adjusted EPS
- Bull case **$62** (+41.5%): approximately 8.0x adjusted EPS on demonstrated standalone execution plus buyback
- Bear case **$27** (−38.4%): 4.25x on ~$820M impaired EBITDA at higher net debt, near the 52-week low of $26.34
- Timeframe: 12-18 months, with decision points at the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November), the first 10-K (Q1 2027, including the FY2026 audited standalone financials and the full risk-factor set), and January 2027 for Mexican work-week legislation

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if two or more of the following occur:**
1. Q3 or Q4 Adjusted EBITDA margin holds at or above 11.0% while the AOCI reclassification into cost of sales declines sequentially, separating durable cost execution from hedge support
2. Meaningful buyback execution disclosed (≥$75M within two quarters) alongside FY2026 FCF landing at or above the $250M midpoint
3. Top-ten customer concentration stabilizes at or below 83% with the largest customer at or below 20%, or management begins disclosing an adjacent-market revenue figure showing a credible diversification path
4. Price weakness to **$33-36** on unchanged guidance, which would take EV/guided EBITDA to roughly 4.1-4.3x and the FCF yield above 10%; that entry level would justify a full BUY-sized commitment

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if any of the following occur:**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA or FCF guidance is cut, or FY2027 initial guidance implies Adjusted EBITDA below $950M
2. Any customer at ≥14% of sales is disclosed as reducing or cancelling a major program, or top-ten concentration rises above 86%
3. Net debt / Adjusted EBITDA rises above 2.75x, the revolver is drawn for operating purposes, or interest coverage falls below 4.5x
4. Adjusted quarterly EBITDA margin falls below 10.0% with the hedge reclassification into cost of sales still positive, indicating wage and copper pressure has overwhelmed cost execution before the buffer even ends
5. Any material weakness in internal control is disclosed in the first 10-K

**Reassessment timeline:** next scheduled review at the Q3 2026 report; a hard review at the first 10-K, which is the first opportunity to test the ~$894M FY2025 EBITDA base, the covenant EBITDA definition, and the risk-factor set against our derivations.

## Conclusion

Versigent trades at 4.82x guided FY2026 EV/Adjusted EBITDA, roughly 5.7x annualized Q2 adjusted earnings, and an 8.1% guided free-cash-flow yield, with 1.7x net leverage against a 4.25x covenant and $1.4B of liquidity. Underlying volume growth of 4-5% against flat global production is real, cost execution contributed $44M to Q2 gross profit, SG&A is being managed down, and the incentive plan is sensibly built around ROIC with net debt in the denominator. Those are the reasons not to be short.

The reasons not to be long at $43.81 are equally concrete. Base-case upside of roughly 12% against bear-case downside of 38%, on a leveraged cyclical whose first full audited reporting cycle is still ahead, is a fair-value proposition rather than an asymmetric one.
