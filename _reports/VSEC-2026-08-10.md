## Executive Summary

VSE Corporation has finished a three-year reconstruction. Having sold its Federal & Defense segment (February 2024, $42.9M cash, $15.2M pre-tax loss) and its Fleet distribution business (April 2025, $136.2M cash plus a $25M note), the company now reports a single segment: Aviation. Continuing-operations revenue went from $544.0M in FY2023 to $1,112.3M in FY2025, and Q2 2026 revenue reached $449.1M, up 65% year over year. Operating margin expanded from 7.5% in FY2024 to 10.9% in Q2 2026 while absorbing $18.5M of intangible amortization and $8.0M of acquisition expense in the quarter. Management raised FY2026 guidance to 61–64% revenue growth and 18.7–19.0% adjusted EBITDA margin, from 57–61% and 18.1–18.5%. Consensus FY2026 EPS moved from $4.37 to $5.49 over 30 days (+25.5%) and FY2027 from $5.68 to $6.93 (+22.0%). The operating story is confirmed in filed numbers, not just guidance.

The valuation and the funding structure are where the case narrows. The quoted $6.14B market capitalization counts only the 28,062,020 common shares outstanding at June 30, 2026 and omits 1,415,752 exchangeable subsidiary shares issued as PAG in-kind consideration and roughly 2,101,000 shares embedded in the 9.2 million tangible equity units sold in February 2026. Both blocks are already sold and funded; VSE itself includes them in *basic* EPS (reported basic weighted shares 30,941,788 versus 28.06M outstanding). Corrected share equivalents of ~31.58M imply equity value of ~$6.91B and enterprise value of ~$7.83B against FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of ~$341M, or **23.0x** — 2.3 turns higher than the 20.7x the vendor share count produces. On adjusted EPS the stock is ~39.8x FY2026 consensus and ~31.6x FY2027; on our GAAP estimate of ~$3.86 it is ~57x.

Cash conversion is the second constraint. Operating cash flow less capex was −$40.5M (FY2023), −$51.7M (FY2024), +$5.7M (FY2025) and −$50.0M (H1 2026). Over FY2023–FY2025 the company raised $898.2M of equity and paid $895.8M cash for acquisitions while generating roughly −$86.5M of cumulative free cash flow; weighted diluted shares rose from 14.18M to 31.39M. Inventory stood at $867.1M at June 30, 2026, of which $115.6M was an organic six-month build. Growth has been real and externally financed, and the compensation plan's disclosed metrics — revenue, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted free cash flow — contain no per-share measure. We rate VSEC **HOLD** with a base case of $232 (+6%), bull $280 (+28%) and bear $155 (−29%).

## Company Overview

VSE is an aviation aftermarket parts distributor and MRO operator. Two revenue lines: Distribution (reported as Products) at $703.9M in FY2025, and Repair/MRO (Services) at $408.4M. The mix has inverted quickly — repair was 30.8% of revenue in FY2023, 36.7% in FY2025, and **54.7% of Q2 2026 revenue** ($245.7M of $449.1M). Repair carries higher margins but is labor- and capacity-constrained; management is building one new engine-shop facility and expanding two others.

The differentiating asset is OEM-licensed exclusivity rather than scale. VSE pays OEMs cash for legally exclusive rights over specific part-number families: $105.0M for Honeywell fuel control systems (September 2023), a $7.8M amendment expanding that scope (March 2026), and $10.8M for exclusive rights over certain Pratt & Whitney PT6 fuel pumps (December 2025). VSE is the licensee, not the platform owner — Honeywell and Pratt & Whitney design and certify; VSE holds the aftermarket commercial position. The same OEMs are simultaneously licensors, suppliers, and customers; the Q2 call attributed part of MRO growth to "increased OEM customer spending."

FY2025 revenue was 99.6% commercial ($1,108.1M) and 0.4% government, despite the Aerospace & Defense sector label — the defense exposure departed with FDS. Geographically, the United States was 57% of FY2025 revenue, Canada 13%, other countries 30%. Headcount was approximately 1,600 at December 31, 2025, before PAG.

Three acquisitions closed in seven months: Aero 3 (December 23, 2025, $346.4M cash, wheel and brake MRO), NorthStar Technologies (April 1, 2026, $10.1M, engine MRO and 3PL), and Precision Aviation Group (May 5, 2026, $2,020.0M total consideration plus an earn-out of up to $125M). PAG brought 29 locations, plus rotorcraft, defense and international exposure across Europe, Australia and Brazil.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 2026 growth decomposition matters more than the headline. Revenue rose 65%, of which management characterized approximately 14% as organic — implying roughly 51 points from acquisitions. Within the total, repair revenue rose 149% and distribution 17%. Distribution, the legacy engine, is compounding at a mid-teens rate; the headline is largely acquisition arithmetic.

Fourteen percent organic growth with 320 basis points of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion is nonetheless a solid result in a fragmented aftermarket. The demand drivers management cites are well documented across the industry: resilient global air traffic, an aging installed fleet, and constrained new aircraft and engine availability that extends the service lives of in-fleet assets and shifts spend toward aftermarket. VSE's purchase of seven CFM56 engines for in-house repair and teardown is a direct expression of that setup — capturing both repair margin and used-serviceable-material margin, and consistent with the asset intensity showing up in inventory.

FY2026 guidance implies revenue of $1,791M–$1,824M and adjusted EBITDA of approximately $335M–$347M. Notably, management attributed the raised margin guidance "predominantly to the core business rather than accelerated PAG synergies," and stated that most PAG synergy realization is expected in 2027 rather than 2026. Run-rate synergies were originally guided at approximately $15M — 0.7% of a $2.02B purchase price. Management is explicitly not underwriting PAG on cost-out; the case rests on PAG's standalone growth, cross-selling proprietary content, and linking repair operations to exchange inventory pools. That is a less circular case than a synergy case, and a harder one to verify.

The estimate revision picture is positive but requires care. FY2026 consensus rose 25.5% and FY2027 22.0% over 30 days; the 90-day columns are blank, so the strongest form of the revision signal (>30% over 90 days) cannot be established from this data. Much of the 30-day move is mechanical — analysts adding PAG to models after the May 5 close, plus VSE's disclosed change in non-GAAP definition to exclude stock-based compensation. The same caveat applies to the 4-for-4 beat record and the 84.8% Q2 surprise ($1.75 actual versus $0.95 estimate): a beat measured across a shifting non-GAAP basis and a mid-quarter consolidation is not a clean read on estimate reliability. The underlying trend is genuinely positive; the magnitude overstates it.

PAG's own economics are the largest unverified input. The completion release said PAG increases VSE revenue by roughly 50% on a pro forma 2025 basis, implying ~$556M of PAG 2025 revenue; the 10-Q pro forma tables corroborate roughly $554M annualized and imply ~14% H1 2026 growth. **VSE did not disclose PAG's EBITDA.** If PAG runs near VSE's ~19% consolidated margin on ~$630M of 2026 annualized revenue, that is ~$120M of EBITDA and ~16.8x upfront, ~15.0x with the $15M of synergies. The release's claim that the deal is immediately accretive to adjusted EBITDA margin implies PAG's margin exceeds 19%, which would reduce the multiple. Either way, the price paid for half the company's revenue base cannot currently be verified against a disclosed earnings figure.

The gap between adjusted and GAAP EPS is the crux. On EV/EBITDA, 23.0x sits toward the premium end of aerospace aftermarket comparables — closer to HEICO-type multiples than to AAR-type multiples — for a company that has not yet demonstrated self-funding cash flow.

Sell-side targets run $245–$280 with a $265 mean, all above spot. Where a stock trades at a premium multiple, targets and price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation, so we treat the range as context rather than as a floor.

Applying 20x — a modest discount to today's 23.0x, reflecting leverage, the amortization-heavy earnings bridge and unproven cash conversion — gives EV of $8,300M, less ~$880M net debt and $34M earn-out, or $7,386M of equity across ~31.9M shares: **$232**, or +6.1% from $218.70.

## Competitive Landscape

VSE's own 10-K describes the aviation parts industry as "highly fragmented," with "several highly visible leading companies," and concedes that many competitors are "larger in size and have greater name recognition, financial resources, and larger technical staff." Named competitive factors — customer knowledge, product availability, turnaround time, sales force initiatives and price — describe an industry where structural advantage must be manufactured.

VSE's answer is to become the OEM's outsourcing partner rather than its competitor, converting distribution relationships into quasi-proprietary positions through licensed exclusivity. The Honeywell fuel-control and PT6 fuel-pump agreements do exactly that; NorthStar was framed as enhancing integration within an engine OEM's aftermarket supply chain. Directionally this moves VSE toward the proprietary-content model that supports premium multiples elsewhere in the sector.

The vulnerability is symmetrical. Exclusivity granted by an OEM can be renegotiated or allowed to lapse; VSE holds licenses rather than certifications it owns outright. And the OEM counterparties may sit inside the ~20% affiliated customer group discussed below, which makes the relationship both harder to replace and harder to price.

On trajectory — the dimension that matters more than absolute position — the evidence is favorable: repair mix rising, margins expanding faster than revenue in the core, exclusivity portfolio growing, geographic footprint extended into Europe, Australia and Brazil via PAG, and a re-entry into rotorcraft and defense. Distribution growth of 17% suggests share gain rather than share loss.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, disclosed and not improving.** The FY2025 10-K added a standalone risk factor naming a "Customer Group" — affiliated customers under common ownership — at approximately 20% of FY2025 revenue. Note 14 gives the history: 14% (2023), 21% (2024), 20% (2025). Product, platform and geographic mix diversified substantially over that period through roughly $1.5B of acquisitions; direct counterparty concentration did not. These measure different exposures and the first does not offset the second. The risk factor's language is unusually specific, enumerating "liquidity constraints, or financial distress" and the customer seeking "relief from its contractual obligations." The group is not named in any filing. A counter-reading is available: the group's absolute spend rose from roughly $76M to roughly $222M, which is consistent with share gain in a deepening relationship, and PAG's consolidation will mechanically dilute the percentage in FY2026.

**Inventory valuation is the auditor's flagged concern.** Grant Thornton designated the write-down of slow-moving inventory at certain locations as the sole critical audit matter, citing recovery rates, "the identification of specific inventories associated with aircraft with declining usage trends," and "the impact of exclusive distribution agreements." That describes precisely the downside of the strategy: exclusivity requires stocking parts for specific platforms, and a faster-than-modeled decline in a platform's fleet utilization converts that inventory into a reserve question. With inventory at $867.1M and roughly 20% of assets, and implied book reserves in the $90–95M range derived from the $22.8M inventory-reserve deferred tax asset, the sensitivity is material.

**Integration and purchase-accounting risk at unprecedented scale.** The 10-K added an entire "Risks Related to the PAG Acquisition" subsection, including candid language about "potential differences between PAG's private-company control environment and public company Sarbanes-Oxley requirements" and a statement that synergy estimates are "speculative in nature." The Q2 10-Q discloses that certain control structures remain in operation at Aero 3, NorthStar and PAG, "primarily related to information technology, inventory management, human resources, processing and billing of revenues, and collection of those revenues." Disclosure controls were concluded effective as of June 30, 2026. Three acquisitions in seven months, one roughly the size of the pre-existing company, with legacy controls still running at all three, in a business whose critical audit matter is inventory valuation, concentrates control risk into the FY2026 ICFR assessment.

**Goodwill.** Goodwill of $1,810.4M equals 62% of book equity. The FY2025 test was qualitative only and passed; there have been no impairments in FY2023–FY2025. The first-day-of-Q4 2026 test will be the first to include PAG's $1,167.0M of goodwill, after roughly five months of ownership and inside the measurement period during which the purchase price allocation remains preliminary (open to May 2027). Ninety percent of PAG's $2,020.0M consideration was allocated to goodwill ($1,167.0M) and a single 12-year customer-relationship intangible ($650.0M), leaving ~$203.0M of net tangible assets. The economic case rests entirely on customer-relationship durability.

**Cyclicality with no ballast.** Continuing operations are 99.6% commercial. Having sold both the government-services and truck-fleet businesses, VSE has removed the segments least correlated to commercial air travel. Management stated it has not seen macro or geopolitical uncertainty translate into a meaningful change in customer demand — a real-time observation with limited forward content. As a discretionary-travel-linked cyclical with essentially no defensive ballast, the business carries more beta than the multiple credits should the commercial aftermarket cycle turn.

**Tariffs and cross-border exposure.** With 43% of FY2025 revenue billed outside the US, the 10-K's dedicated tariff risk factor is live rather than boilerplate; management cites pass-through pricing and productivity as mitigants. Legal proceedings: none.

**Management alignment.** Five-year TSR of +364% against a peer group's +130% is a wide margin, and comp actually paid tracked it ($21.71M for the CEO in 2025). The disclosed performance metrics — revenue, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted free cash flow — contain no per-share measure, while adjusted EBITDA rose from $73M (2021) to $181M (2025) and weighted diluted shares rose roughly 2.5x. The inclusion of adjusted free cash flow is a meaningful partial offset and, given the conversion pattern, the most relevant of the three. Section 162(m) disallowance added 5.5 percentage points ($3.8M) to the FY2025 effective tax rate. On insider activity, we found no open-market purchases by officers or directors between December 2025 and July 2026 — including through offerings priced at $170.00 (October 2025) and $188.00 (February 2026), both well below the current price. The COO sold 6,500 shares at $196.82 on June 12, 2026; the CEO adopted a Rule 10b5-1 plan on May 11, 2026 for up to 35,000 shares (~19% of his 187,037-share position) with sales permitted from **August 17, 2026**. A diversification plan after a 364% run is defensible and is what such plans exist to permit; the absence of any offsetting open-market buying across five equity raises removes the strongest form of positive insider signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 57.4% sits essentially in line with 55.8% one-year realized (spread 1.6%), so options are neither cheap nor rich in absolute terms, though IV sits at the 73rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation — 57.4% at 10 days against 51.3% at six months — indicating the market prices more near-term than far-dated uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced at −2.3% near-term but steepens to −5.9% at the January 2027 tenor. Put/call ratios are heavy in both volume (2.25) and open interest (2.37), and short interest is 11.0% of float with 5.4 days to cover. The combination of put-heavy open interest, elevated short interest and near-dated volatility backwardation describes hedged and partly bearish institutional positioning around the next one to two quarters — consistent with the specific unresolved items we identify (Q3 cash flow, the Q4 goodwill test, the CEO's selling window opening August 17). The ±8.2% implied move on the 10-day tenor and ±14.2% at three months bracket a market that expects meaningful two-way movement rather than a one-directional break, which supports a HOLD rather than either an add or a short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $280 (+28%)

The aftermarket cycle is durable: aging fleets, constrained new aircraft and engine deliveries, and operators holding assets in service longer shift spend from new-build to aftermarket for several years. VSE's repair mix reached 54.7% of revenue in Q2 and repair carries higher margins, so mix alone supports continued expansion beyond the 19.2% adjusted EBITDA margin already achieved. PAG synergies of ~$15M sit almost entirely in 2027 and are not in FY2026 numbers; the raised margin guidance came from the core business, leaving PAG contribution as incremental. Full-year PAG plus 7–9% organic growth supports roughly $2.15B of FY2027 revenue and ~$430M of adjusted EBITDA. At 23x — today's multiple, sustained on a proven integration — that is ~$281 per share. The December 9 investor day is the first with the full post-PAG portfolio and the likely venue for medium-term targets that anchor 2028 estimates. Working-capital intensity normalizes in H2 as guided, and the FCF question resolves in VSE's favor.

### Bear Case — $155 (−29%)

The cash flow pattern proves structural rather than transitional: exclusive distribution rights require permanent, growing inventory, so free cash flow stays near zero and growth continues to require equity issuance at whatever multiple the market allows. A cycle turn in commercial air travel — VSE is 99.6% commercial with no defensive ballast — slows organic growth from 14% toward mid-single digits while the fixed costs of 29 additional PAG locations remain. The slow-moving inventory reserve requires a step-up, or the ~20% Customer Group renegotiates. The FY2027 interest and amortization step-up removes roughly $0.76 of GAAP EPS. The Q4 2026 goodwill test, the first including PAG's $1,167M, produces a headline impairment against tangible book equity of only ~$171M. At 16x a reduced ~$360M EBITDA, equity is $4.8B, or approximately $152–158 — the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Rationale: uncertain.

- **Base case: $232 (+6.1%)** — 20x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of $415M, less $880M net debt and $34M earn-out, across ~31.9M share equivalents.
- **Bull case: $280 (+28.0%)** — 23x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of $430M on demonstrated PAG integration and confirmed FCF conversion.
- **Bear case: $155 (−29.1%)** — 16x a reduced $360M EBITDA on organic deceleration, an inventory reserve step-up, or a goodwill charge.
- **Probability-weighted return** (25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear): approximately **+2.75%** over 12 months — approximately fair, which is what separates HOLD from ACCUMULATE here.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with three discrete checkpoints: Q3 2026 results (late October/early November), the December 9 investor day, and the FY2026 10-K including the first PAG-inclusive goodwill test and ICFR assessment.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 operating cash flow less capex turns positive, with inventory growing slower than revenue sequentially — the direct test of management's stated H2 working-capital claim and the single most important open item.
2. Disclosure of PAG's standalone EBITDA or margin at the December investor day, confirming a purchase multiple at or below ~15x post-synergy, together with organic growth sustained at or above 12%.
3. A price near $180–190 on unchanged fundamentals, which would place the stock near 19x FY2027E EBITDA and ~27x FY2027 consensus EPS, restoring a margin of safety.
4. Open-market insider purchases by two or more C-suite executives, which have been absent through five equity offerings.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any goodwill or intangible impairment at the Q4 2026 test, or a material adverse purchase-price-allocation revision to PAG before May 2027.
2. Organic revenue growth decelerating below 8% for two consecutive quarters, or adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 18%.
3. A material inventory reserve increase, an ICFR material weakness at any of Aero 3, NorthStar or PAG, or a disclosed change in the Customer Group relationship.
4. Another equity issuance to fund acquisitions before free cash flow turns positive, which would confirm that the model requires perpetual external financing.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings release. The CEO's 10b5-1 sales window opens August 17, 2026; observed sale volume against the 35,000-share authorization is informational but should not be weighted heavily on its own.

## Conclusion

VSE has executed a difficult transformation well. Selling two of three segments and rebuilding around aviation aftermarket has produced revenue growth from $544M to a guided $1.8B in three years, operating margin expansion from 7.5% to 10.9%, four consecutive earnings beats, two guidance raises in 2026, and a 25.5% upward revision to FY2026 consensus over 30 days. The strategic logic — buying legally exclusive positions on specific OEM part families and pairing them with certificated repair capacity — is coherent and moves the company toward proprietary content.

The reason we stop at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE is arithmetic rather than skepticism about the business. Correcting the share count for two blocks of already-funded equity that the vendor market cap omits takes the multiple to 23.0x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA and 31.6x FY2027 adjusted EPS, against a company that has generated roughly negative $86.5M of cumulative free cash flow across FY2023–FY2025, carries 62.8% of assets in goodwill and intangibles against ~$5.41 of tangible book per share, faces roughly $32M of scheduled incremental interest and amortization expense in 2027, and has not disclosed the EBITDA of the asset that represents half its revenue base. A ~20% affiliated customer group has not diluted through $1.5B of diversifying acquisitions, and its risk factor was escalated to standalone status with language about financial distress. The compensation structure rewards revenue and adjusted EBITDA with no per-share metric while the share count has grown 2.5x, and no insider has bought in the open market through five offerings, including two struck well below the current price.

Existing holders have a business with real momentum and identifiable catalysts in Q3 cash flow and the December investor day. Prospective buyers are paying a full price for an integration that will not be verifiable until 2027. We would become constructive at $180–190, or on a single quarter of positive free cash flow with inventory growing slower than revenue.
