## Executive Summary

Old National Bancorp has completed a multi-year transformation from a $23B community bank into a $72B top-25 U.S. banking franchise through three consecutive acquisitions (First Midwest 2022, CapStar 2024, Bremer 2025). Integration execution has been exceptional: management has stated that Bremer's $111M in targeted annual cost saves were fully realized within one year of close, driving improved operating efficiency. With a 15-18% return on average tangible common equity, peer-leading deposit costs (170 bps spot rate), and a $5.5B record loan pipeline, ONB is entering its first period of pure organic harvesting.

At $24.57 per share, the stock trades at approximately 9.4x forward P/E on consensus $2.62 FY26E and 1.79x tangible book value. This valuation is below the peer median, despite ONB's profitability metrics (ROATCE, efficiency, deposit costs) being materially above the peer set. As the market gains confidence that integration risk has passed and organic earnings power compounds, re-rating toward a peer-appropriate multiple is realistic, implying upside within 12 months.

The primary risk is CRE concentration (45% of loans, with commercial + CRE representing approximately 76% of total loans), where charge-offs tripled from 0.07% to 0.20% over two years. While still low in absolute terms and below industry averages, the trajectory requires monitoring—particularly the $2.7B office portfolio and $828M of sub-4% maturing CRE loans.

## Company Overview

Old National Bancorp operates as the sixth-largest commercial bank headquartered in the Midwest, with $72.2B in total assets and 346 banking centers spanning Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, North Carolina, North Dakota, Tennessee, and Wisconsin. The company generates revenue through:

- **Net interest income** ($2.06B in 2025): Primary driver, benefiting from 3.54% NIM
- **Wealth & investment services** ($144M): Stable fee revenue
- **Capital markets** ($37M): Growing 84% YoY, still small but accelerating
- **Mortgage banking** ($38M): Growing 46% YoY

The loan book is approximately 76% commercial/CRE and 24% consumer/residential. Deposits total approximately $55B with a loan-to-deposit ratio of 89%, providing ample room for organic balance sheet growth without wholesale funding dependency.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

The Bremer acquisition closed in May 2025 and system conversions completed in October 2025. Q4 2025 is the first clean quarter representing full post-integration run-rate:

- Q4 2025 adjusted EPS: $0.62 (annualized: $2.48)
- Q1 2026 adjusted EPS: $0.61 (slight seasonal softness)
- Full-year 2026E consensus: $2.62 (9 analysts)
- Full-year 2027E consensus: $2.90 (11 analysts)

NIM expanded from 3.31% (FY24) to 3.54% (FY25) and further to 3.65% in Q4 2025. Management guided for "stable-to-improving" NIM through 2026. The efficiency ratio improved from 55.1% (FY25) to 51.6% in Q4 2025 (with Q4 2025 adjusted efficiency ratio reaching a record-low 46.0%), and Q1 2026 reported an efficiency ratio of 48.25%, reflecting continued strong operating leverage from realized merger synergies.

**Capital Generation:**

ONB generated operating cash flow of approximately $682M with minimal capex ($44M). While free cash flow is not a standard valuation metric for banks given the nature of their balance sheet operations, the strong capital generation supports robust capital return. The 31% dividend payout ratio ($0.56/share, 2.3% yield) leaves substantial capacity for buybacks. Management repurchased 6.1M shares over the past year with $383M remaining on the authorization.

**Credit Quality:**

Net charge-offs rose to 0.25% in FY2025 (vs. 0.17% in FY24), driven primarily by legacy Bremer loans. Q1 2026 NCOs were 26 bps (19 bps excluding purchased credit-deteriorated loans). Non-accrual loans have improved for four consecutive quarters. The allowance for credit losses covers 109% of non-accruals.

## Growth Analysis

ONB's growth story has two distinct phases:

**Phase 1 (2020-2025): Transformational M&A.** Assets grew from approximately $23B to $72B over five years. This phase is complete—CEO Ryan stated in Q1 2026 that "we don't need acquisitions to meet our objectives."

**Phase 2 (2026+): Organic Harvesting.** Key organic growth indicators:

1. **Record loan pipeline:** $5.5B (up 14% from year-end), supporting 4-6% annual loan growth guidance
2. **C&I momentum:** 16.9% annualized growth in Q1 2026
3. **Capital markets scaling:** Revenue grew 84% YoY to $37M, still a small contributor with room to expand
4. **Wealth management:** $144M in stable fee revenue from the expanded client base
5. **Deposit cost optimization:** Spot rate of 170 bps with "back book opportunity" for further reductions

Consensus projects EPS growing from $2.62 (2026E) to $2.90 (2027E), representing 10.7% growth. Estimate revisions over 90 days are modestly positive: current year +0.2%, next year +0.8%. The revision velocity is below the 30% threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal, but the direction is positive and accelerating (30-day revisions stronger than 90-day).

The earnings beat track record (3 of 4 quarters, with the miss being a negligible $0.002) supports confidence in consensus estimates.

| Price/52-wk High | 94% | Near highs |

**Relative Valuation:**

ONB trades at a material discount to the peer median forward P/E. Its profitability metrics are above-median:
- ROATCE of 15-18% vs. regional bank average of 12-14%
- Adjusted efficiency ratio of 46-48% vs. industry average of 55-60%
- Deposit cost of 170 bps vs. estimated industry average of 200-250 bps

A bank generating 15-18% ROATCE with a sub-50% efficiency ratio trading at a discount to peers generating 12-14% ROATCE represents a potential mispricing. The market appears to be discounting the sustainability of post-merger margins, or pricing in residual integration/credit risk that Q1 2026 results suggest is dissipating.

**Fair Value Estimate:**

Using 11.0x forward earnings on $2.62 FY26E EPS = $28.82. On blended FY26/27 EPS ($2.76), 10.0-10.5x = $27.60-$28.98. Analyst consensus median target is $28.00 (mean $27.91). The base case target of $28 represents 14% upside, achievable through modest multiple expansion as integration risk fades from investor consciousness.

**Why the market is underpricing ONB:** Investors are anchoring to the bank's pre-transformation identity as a small Midwest community bank and to integration risk that has now been resolved. Four consecutive quarters of non-accrual improvement and full cost save realization should progressively remove this discount.

## Competitive Landscape

ONB occupies an advantageous position as a scaled regional bank ($72B) in the Midwest/Southeast:

**Strengths:**
- Peer-leading deposit franchise with 170 bps cost (provides structural NIM advantage)
- Diversified geographic footprint post-Bremer (reduced Indiana concentration)
- Scale advantages in compliance, technology, and treasury management vs. smaller community banks
- Relationship-banking model less vulnerable to FinTech disintermediation than money-center banks

**Competitive Threats:**
- Larger regionals (USB, PNC, Huntington) compete for the same commercial borrowers
- GENIUS Act stablecoin provisions could create new deposit competition (identified as an emerging sector-level risk for bank deposits)
- CRE concentration creates sector-level vulnerability if a broad downturn emerges
- Credit unions gaining consumer market share in Midwest markets

The competitive position is stable to improving. ONB's transformation from a small community bank to a top-25 franchise provides scale economics that didn't exist five years ago, while maintaining the relationship-driven model that community banks are known for.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Concentration (Primary Risk — Weight: HIGH):**
At 45% of total loans ($22.1B), CRE is the dominant risk factor. Combined with commercial loans ($15.0B), commercial/CRE represents approximately 76% of the total loan book. Specific exposures:
- Office: $2.7B (12% of CRE) with $49M non-accrual
- Multifamily: $6.6B (30% of CRE) — well-performing but interest-rate sensitive
- $828M of sub-4% CRE maturing within 18 months — potential refinancing stress

If 30Y rates remain above 5% and office/retail vacancy continues rising, CRE losses could accelerate further. A doubling of CRE NCOs to 0.40% would reduce EPS by approximately $0.15-0.20.

**2. Otto Bremer Trust Overhang (Moderate):**
The former Bremer Financial owner holds a significant ONB position. While ONB has a right of first refusal and already repurchased $50M, further sales could create technical pressure. The 7.9% short interest (7.5 days to cover) may partly reflect this overhang.

**3. Rate Sensitivity:**
The bank is near-neutral but slightly asset-sensitive (+100 bps = +0.53% NII; -100 bps = -0.91% NII). In the current elevated-rate environment with potential policy uncertainty, this positioning is defensive. Risk is modest.

**4. Regional Economic Exposure:**
Concentration in Midwest/Southeast exposes the bank to agricultural stress (North Dakota/Minnesota) and tariff-related manufacturing headwinds (Indiana, Michigan). These are manageable given geographic diversification post-Bremer.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV surged from 58.2% to 79.0% (+20.7pp) over the past 10 days while the stock rose 5.2%. This divergence—rising vol alongside rising price—likely reflects the Q1 earnings event resolution combined with increased uncertainty around its implications for rates/credit. The elevated IV suggests the market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty, which could create volatility but doesn't invalidate the fundamental thesis. Short interest at 7.9% with 7.5 days to cover is somewhat elevated for a regional bank, potentially reflecting the Bremer Trust overhang or sector-level CRE concerns.

## Conclusion

Old National Bancorp represents a well-executed post-transformation story at an attractive price. The bank has nearly tripled in size through disciplined M&A over five years, achieved full integration synergies, and now possesses peer-leading operational metrics (46-48% adjusted efficiency, 170 bps deposit costs, 15-18% ROATCE). At approximately 9.4x forward earnings—materially below the peer median—the stock does not reflect this above-average profitability. CRE concentration (76% of loans in commercial/CRE) and the Bremer Trust overhang create near-term headwinds, but four quarters of improving non-accruals and proactive share repurchases demonstrate management's ability to navigate both.

The stock warrants a BUY at current levels with a 12-month base case target of $28.00, representing 14% upside plus a 2.4% dividend yield. The key monitoring items are CRE credit trends and whether the record $5.5B loan pipeline converts into sustained mid-single-digit organic growth.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected forward P/E of ~9.4x (vs. originally stated 12.4x) and the higher commercial/CRE concentration of ~76% of loans (vs. originally stated 57%) pull in opposite directions for the recommendation: the lower multiple strengthens the valuation case for upside, while the higher CRE concentration elevates the risk profile. The $28 target is supported by analyst consensus but relies on modest multiple expansion rather than the originally described "peer premium re-rating."
