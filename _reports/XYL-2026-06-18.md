**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Xylem is the largest pure-play water-technology company globally (~$9.0B revenue, ~150 countries), built around four segments spanning the full water cycle and reshaped by the $7.5B all-stock Evoqua acquisition in 2023. The investment debate today is straightforward: the company is delivering an excellent operating-margin transformation while its top line has gone flat. In 2025 adjusted operating margin expanded 180bps to 17.8% and adjusted EPS grew 19% to $5.08, but organic revenue grew only 4.9% and turned slightly negative (-0.4%) in Q1 2026, with backlog down 7.5% YoY.

The stock has fallen roughly 28% from its October 2025 high of $154.27 to ~$110, and now trades below the entire analyst target range (low $124, median $155). On forward FY26 consensus EPS of $5.53, the multiple sits around 20x — a discount to the ~27x peer median and well below where Xylem traded for most of 2025. That compression is the bull's entry point. The bear's counter is that the multiple compressed for a reason: organic orders are soft, the margin self-help program has a finite runway, and the headline EPS growth rests on adjustments (~$220M intangible amortization, multi-year restructuring) that flatter the picture versus GAAP EPS of $3.92.

We rate XYL a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. This is a structurally advantaged business with durable secular tailwinds (water scarcity, aging infrastructure, PFAS remediation) and a clean balance sheet, and the valuation is no longer demanding. But there is no dated catalyst to re-accelerate organic growth, and we would want either a cheaper entry (~$105 or below) or evidence that organic order intake has inflected before moving to a buy. The prior trajectory — scores drifting from 6.4 to 6.3 with growth prospects falling from 6.5 to 5.5 while valuation improved — captures the situation: the fundamentals have not deteriorated so much as stalled, while the price has come to us.

## Company Overview

Xylem designs, manufactures, and services engineered water products across the full cycle: extraction, delivery, measurement, treatment, and return to the environment. The company estimates the global water TAM (excluding labor, energy, and chemicals opex) at roughly $800B, and its franchise rests on century-old brands, a large installed base generating recurring parts/service revenue, deep distribution, and exposure to regulatory tailwinds.

Four reportable segments (2025 revenue):

- **Water Infrastructure** — $2,636M (29%), water/wastewater transport and treatment (Flygt, Wedeco, Wallace & Tiernan). Adjusted operating margin 22.1%, the highest in the portfolio.
- **Water Solutions & Services** — $2,464M (27%), largely Evoqua-derived outsourced water, dewatering rental (Godwin), industrial process water, and O&M. Adjusted operating margin 17.1%.
- **Measurement & Control Solutions** — $2,086M (23%), smart meters, the FlexNet 900MHz communication network, and Xylem Vue software (Sensus, YSI, WTW). Adjusted operating margin 16.9%.
- **Applied Water** — $1,849M (20%), building/commercial pumps, valves, HVAC, fire protection (Bell & Gossett, Goulds, Lowara). Adjusted operating margin 18.4%.

No single customer exceeds 5% of revenue; the US is 58% of sales, emerging markets ~14%. CEO Matthew Pine, who succeeded Patrick Decker (architect of the Evoqua deal), is running an active capital-allocation program: ~$1B/year of M&A combined with portfolio pruning under an 80/20 simplification framework.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is conservatively positioned. Total debt is ~$1.94B against $11.5B equity (D/E ~0.51), net leverage near 1x, and the term loan that funded Evoqua was fully repaid in April 2024, dropping interest expense to $29M. Current ratio is 1.62; liquidity is ~$1.8–2.5B. The Altman Z of 2.94 sits in the grey zone but reflects low asset turnover typical of an acquisitive industrial rather than distress; the goodwill/intangible load (~$9B, roughly half the $17.6B balance sheet) depresses turnover.

Revenue stepped from $5.5B (2022) to $9.0B (2025), but the jump was almost entirely the 2023 Evoqua acquisition. The operating story since 2024 is margin, not volume:

- 2026 guidance +2–4% organic, total raised to 2–3% in Q1.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin 22.2% (+160bps); GAAP operating margin +170bps to 13.5%.
- Drivers: productivity savings (+330bps), price realization (+190bps), partially offset by ~240bps inflation. The 2026 restructuring program targets $80–120M of annualized net savings.
- The GAAP-to-adjusted gap stems from $220M intangible amortization, $133M restructuring, and special charges.

Cash generation is healthy: 2025 OCF $1,241M, FCF $910M (10.1% margin), against the ~$26.7B market cap a ~3.4% FCF yield. Capital return is accelerating and increasingly leans on balance-sheet capacity: the Board authorized a new $1.5B buyback in February 2026, and Q1 2026 alone repurchased $595M (treasury stock rose from $768M to $1.37B), drawing cash from $1.48B to $808M. The May 29, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01, 2.03, 8.01) signals a new financing facility, likely to fund the M&A pipeline and buyback; specific terms were not retrievable and should be confirmed.

Q1 2026: revenue $2,125M (+2.7% reported but -0.4% organic, FX did the lifting), adjusted EPS $1.12 (+8.7%), backlog $4,712M. MCS orders surged +15.4% organically on US energy/smart-metering strength, while WSS orders fell 14.6% organically against a tough comp. The earnings-surprise record is solid — three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with Q3 2025 beating by 10% — supporting reasonable confidence in consensus.

## Growth Analysis

This is the crux of the score. Headline EPS growth is double-digit, but it is being manufactured by cost transformation and capital return on a flat organic base. Consensus EPS revisions confirm the stall: current-year estimate $5.53 (essentially unchanged over 30 days, +0.4% over 90d), next-year $6.09 (flat). There is no estimate-revision momentum to force a higher growth score. Estimates are stable-to-flat, which is the honest read.

The secular case remains intact: a projected ~40% global water supply-demand gap by 2030, US non-revenue water losses of roughly one in five gallons, tightening regulation, and PFAS remediation needs. The MCS smart-metering momentum (+15.4% organic orders) and CEO Pine's flagging of data-center cooling water demand are genuine forward vectors. But these are multi-year tailwinds that have not yet translated into accelerating consolidated organic growth, and emerging markets (14% of revenue) are weak.

The margin runway is the more immediate lever. With adjusted operating margin at 17.8% versus best-in-class industrial peers in the low-20s, there is room, and the $80–120M restructuring target plus continued 80/20 simplification should support a few more years of margin-led EPS compounding even on low-single-digit organic growth. The risk is that this is a depleting resource: once the self-help is harvested, EPS growth converges toward organic revenue growth plus buyback, which on current trends is high-single-digit at best. I score growth prospects 5.0: durable secular backdrop and credible near-term EPS compounding, offset by genuinely stalled organic demand and flat estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$110 the stock trades at roughly 20x forward FY26 EPS and ~18x FY27 of $6.09 — a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 27.3 (Dover 27.3, Hubbell 27.7, Ingersoll Rand 49.6; cheaper names Otis 18.6 and Snap-on 19.9 carry their own issues). The trailing P/E of 27.4 on GAAP EPS overstates the multiple because GAAP earnings are depressed by recurring amortization; the more representative figure is the ~20x forward on adjusted earnings.

The stock sits below the entire analyst target range (high $183), implying ~12% upside even to the most conservative target. On inflection names, analyst targets lag reality; here, with flat estimates, the targets are more likely stale on the downside, meaning consensus may drift lower toward the price rather than the price rising to meet targets. That tempers the "targets are floors" framing.

This is a quality compounder that has de-rated to a reasonable, no-longer-demanding multiple, but cheapness alone is not a catalyst. A ~3.4% FCF yield and 1.5% dividend yield provide modest support. For the multiple to re-rate back toward the peer median, organic growth needs to inflect or the margin program needs to keep surprising. Absent that, ~20x forward on a flat-organic industrial is fair, not cheap. I score valuation 5.7: the discount to peers and to targets is real, but there is no independent re-rating catalyst dated on the calendar. A move toward $105 would shift this to clearly attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Xylem's position is strong and stable-to-improving in pockets. The water industry is highly fragmented with no competitor spanning all of Xylem's markets, and the company competes on brand, application expertise, energy efficiency, and channel reach. The large installed base drives recurring parts/service revenue, a structural moat that smooths cyclicality. MCS smart metering is gaining share, and the FlexNet network plus Xylem Vue software layer adds a recurring, higher-margin digital element.

The competitive risks are specific rather than existential: the FlexNet franchise depends on FCC-licensed 900MHz spectrum (non-renewal or interference would impair both the metering business and its goodwill), and the 2026 10-K added AI as both a competitive threat and a product feature. Net, the trajectory is favorable — share gains in metering, a widening services moat, and durable regulatory tailwinds — which supports a competitive-position score of 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/intangible impairment (~$9B, half the balance sheet).** Concentrated in Evoqua-related units. The 2025 annual test passed, but deterioration in WSS/treatment fair value could trigger material charges. This is the single largest balance-sheet risk and a structural counterweight to the clean operating story, especially as the company simultaneously buys (~$1B/yr) and prunes.

**Decelerating organic growth.** The thesis increasingly rests on margin self-help; if simplification stalls or demand stays soft, the story weakens and the multiple has further to fall.

**Tariff/supply-chain exposure** (China, Mexico, Taiwan; steel, copper, rare earths, semiconductors). The February 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of IEEPA tariffs could yield refunds Xylem has not booked, a possible non-modeled upside option.

**Smaller items:** PFAS/environmental strict-liability exposure (CERCLA/RCRA) in treatment/WSS; large-project execution and performance-guarantee risk; the Swedish tax dispute (~SEK 837M / ~$88M, no reserve, management has prevailed at intermediate levels but the STA is appealing).

On balance these risks are partly priced after the drawdown, but the impairment overhang and the organic-growth question are genuine open items. I score risk factors 5.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150–165).** The margin transformation has multiple years left; productivity and pricing carry adjusted EPS to ~$6.50+ by FY27 even on low-single-digit organic growth. Organic orders inflect as US infrastructure spending, smart-metering adoption, and data-center cooling demand accelerate, and the multiple re-rates back toward the 27x peer median. The ~$1B/yr M&A program adds accretive growth, the buyback retires shares at a depressed price, and the IEEPA tariff refund provides a non-modeled upside surprise. At 25x on $6.50 EPS, the stock approaches $160.

**Bear case ($90–95).** Organic growth stays flat-to-negative, backlog keeps eroding, and the margin program reaches diminishing returns. EPS growth decelerates toward mid-single-digit as the self-help fades, a goodwill impairment charge on the Evoqua units surfaces, and the multiple compresses to ~16x on $5.70 EPS as the market prices a low-growth industrial. That implies roughly -15% to current levels.

**Base case ($122–130).** Margin expansion continues at a slower pace, organic growth recovers to the 2–4% guided range, EPS reaches ~$6.00 in FY27, and the multiple holds around 21x. This brackets between the scenarios and aligns with the low end of the analyst range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), with organic order intake and backlog trajectory the key data points. The full-year picture clarifies at Q3/Q4.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth re-accelerates above 4% YoY for two consecutive quarters, or organic order intake (ex-MCS) turns clearly positive with backlog stabilizing.
- The stock pulls back to ~$105 or below on unchanged fundamentals, pushing the forward multiple under 19x and widening the discount to peers and targets.
- Confirmation that the IEEPA tariff refund will be booked, providing a quantifiable earnings boost.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge against the Evoqua reporting units.
- Organic revenue declining for two-plus consecutive quarters with backlog falling below ~$4.3B, signaling demand deterioration rather than execution outpacing orders.
- Adjusted operating margin expansion stalling (flat or down YoY), removing the core EPS-growth lever while organic growth remains soft.
- Consensus FY26/FY27 EPS estimates revised down more than 5%.

## Conclusion

Xylem is a high-quality, structurally advantaged water-technology franchise that has executed a genuine margin transformation on a top line that has gone flat. The drawdown has removed the valuation excess that prevailed in 2025 and put the stock below the entire analyst target range at ~20x forward earnings, a discount to a 27x peer median. The reason the multiple compressed is equally clear: organic growth is negative, backlog is shrinking, estimates are flat, and the EPS compounding leans on adjustments and capital return rather than demand.

That leaves a balanced setup. The downside is buffered by a clean balance sheet, recurring service revenue, durable secular tailwinds, and a now-reasonable valuation; the upside requires an organic-growth inflection or sustained margin surprise that is not yet visible in the data. With no dated catalyst and flat estimate revisions, the asymmetry is not yet compelling enough for a buy. We rate XYL a HOLD (growth-hold), and would become constructive either below ~$105 or on confirmation that organic orders are re-accelerating. The prior declining score trajectory is confirmed by the data — growth has stalled — but the offsetting valuation improvement keeps this a reasonable, if uncatalyzed, name to own rather than one to sell.
