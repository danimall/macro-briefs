## Executive Summary

Performance Food Group is the second-largest US foodservice distributor with $63.3B in FY2025 revenue, operating across Foodservice (53%), Convenience (39%), and Specialty (8%) segments. The company is executing well on its core strategy of growing high-margin independent restaurant cases (organic growth of 6.5% in Q3 FY2026) while integrating the $2.0B Cheney Brothers acquisition. Adjusted EBITDA reached $1.77B in FY2025 (+17.3%) with FY2026 guidance of $1.9-1.93B.

The dominant variable for PFGC shares is the disclosed "clean team agreement" with US Foods, which has already consumed $20.2M in legal and professional fees. A combination would create a ~$100B revenue entity to rival Sysco. However, antitrust approval in a three-player oligopoly would face intense FTC scrutiny, and the stock at 45x trailing EPS appears to partially capitalize some deal premium. With estimates declining modestly across the forward curve, leverage at ~3.5x net debt/EBITDA, and the consumer spending environment deteriorating under 4.5%+ inflation, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling at $95.59.

The company's fundamental trajectory is positive: organic growth is strong, Cheney Brothers integration is progressing, and the shift toward higher-margin independent customers is lifting gross margins. But the near-term setup requires patience: GAAP earnings are suppressed by acquisition-related interest and amortization, the Convenience segment faces secular cigarette decline, and any macro-driven pullback in food-away-from-home spending would disproportionately impact this thin-margin business.

## Company Overview

Performance Food Group markets and distributes over 250,000 food and food-related products from approximately 155 distribution centers to over 300,000 customer locations across the United States and Canada. The company operates three segments:

**Foodservice (53% of sales):** Broadline distribution to independent and chain restaurants, schools, and healthcare facilities through 89 distribution centers. Independent customers represent 41.5% of segment sales and are the key profit driver due to higher gross profit per case from value-added services and proprietary "Performance Brands."

**Convenience (39% of sales):** One of the largest wholesale distributors to convenience stores, distributing cigarettes, snacks, beverages, and food through 39 distribution centers. Revenue growth is structurally limited by cigarette volume declines.

**Specialty (8% of sales):** Distribution of candy, snacks, and beverages to vending, office coffee, theater, and retail channels through 27 distribution centers.

The $2.0B Cheney Brothers acquisition (closed October 2024) expanded Southeast US foodservice operations, contributing $2.7B in revenue and $485M in gross profit in FY2025 (partial year).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** FY2025 revenue reached $63.3B (+8.6% YoY), with first nine months of FY2026 at $49.8B (+7.4% YoY). FY2026 guidance is $67.7-68.0B. Organic case growth of 3.7% total with 6.5% in the critical independent channel demonstrates underlying demand health.

**Profitability:** Operating margin is 1.3%, net margin 0.5%. Adjusted EBITDA provides better insight: $1.77B FY2025, $1.34B through 9 months of FY2026 (+10.0%), with full-year guidance of $1.9-1.93B. Gross profit margin improved to 11.7% from 11.3% in FY2024, driven by mix shift toward higher-margin independents.

GAAP net income declined 22% in FY2025 to $340.2M ($2.18/diluted share), driven by the $126M increase in interest expense and $100M increase in D&A from Cheney Brothers. This acquisition overhang should diminish as synergies materialize.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $1.21B in FY2025 and $1.07B through 9 months of FY2026. Free cash flow of $704M in FY2025 (after $506M capex) provides adequate coverage, though FCF margin is thin at ~1.1%.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of approximately $6.88B (including $1.76B finance leases), with net debt/Adjusted EBITDA of ~3.5x. The company successfully refinanced $1.06B of 2027 maturities into 2034 at 5.625%, extending the maturity profile. The $5.0B ABL facility provides $2.75B in availability. Interest expense of $312M through 9 months of FY2026 is a meaningful drag. Approximately 68% of debt is fixed-rate, limiting near-term exposure to the current rate environment.

The Altman Z-Score of 4.42 indicates no bankruptcy risk, but leverage at ~3.5x constrains flexibility for additional M&A or shareholder returns. The essentially untouched $500M buyback authorization ($498.8M remaining) suggests management is preserving capacity for strategic purposes.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Engine — Independent Customers:** The 6.5% organic independent case growth in Q3 FY2026 is the company's most important metric. Independent restaurants generate significantly higher gross profit per case than chain accounts due to value-added services, Performance Brands penetration, and lower price transparency. The steady improvement in independents as a percentage of Foodservice segment sales (now 41.5%) is the primary margin expansion mechanism.

**Acquisition-Driven Growth:** PFG has been a serial acquirer — Core-Mark (2021), Cheney Brothers (2024), plus three additional foodservice acquisitions in FY2026 for $384M. This capital allocation strategy has built scale advantages in procurement and distribution density. Integration execution is the primary risk.

**Convenience Segment Headwinds:** Organic revenue growth of just 1.4% in FY2025 reflects the secular decline in cigarette volumes. The company is diversifying into food and fresh categories within this channel, but the cigarette drag is structural and ongoing.

**Industry Tailwinds:** Food-away-from-home spending share has increased secularly over decades. The consolidation trend favors large broadline distributors over small independents due to scale advantages in procurement, logistics, and technology. The addressable market is approximately $400B.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter estimates have declined 5.2% over 30 days, and next-year estimates have fallen 2.7% over 30 days. This negative revision trend is a concern that must be weighed against the strong organic growth data. The slight guidance tightening (Q4 revenue slightly below expectations) drove the recent selloff.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.59, PFGC trades at 45.7x trailing EPS ($2.18), which significantly overstates the "true" multiple because FY2025 GAAP earnings were depressed by the Cheney Brothers acquisition ($126M incremental interest, $100M incremental D&A). On FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.62, the forward P/E is approximately 20.7x. On FY2027 estimates of $5.78, it's 16.5x.

**Peer comparison:** Sysco trades at 20.3x, US Foods at 28.2x, and the peer median is 28.2x. PFGC's ~20.7x forward P/E is at a discount to US Foods and at a slight premium to Sysco. Given PFG's faster organic growth (6.5% independent vs. Sysco's more mature base) and the potential deal premium, the discount to USFD makes sense while the premium to SYY is modest.

**Adjusted EBITDA basis:** At $15B market cap + $6.88B net debt = ~$21.9B EV. On FY2026 Adj. EBITDA guidance midpoint of $1.915B, EV/EBITDA is ~11.4x. This is reasonable for a food distribution company with improving mix and clear margin expansion levers, though it incorporates some optimism about continued improvement.

**Deal premium consideration:** If a PFG-USFD combination were to proceed, historical foodservice M&A suggests 20-30% premiums. However, the antitrust risk in a 3-player market is severe — the FTC blocked Sysco's attempted acquisition of US Foods in 2015 under similar market concentration arguments. The $20.2M in clean team costs confirms serious engagement, but the regulatory path is far from clear.

The valuation is neither cheap enough to be compelling on standalone fundamentals nor expensive enough to sell, given the deal optionality.

## Competitive Landscape

PFG has strengthened its competitive position over the past several years through scale acquisitions and organic share gains in the independent channel. The company is now firmly the #2 US foodservice distributor with meaningful advantages:

**Scale benefits:** 155 distribution centers provide geographic density that smaller competitors cannot replicate. Procurement leverage improves with each acquisition.

**Performance Brands:** Proprietary products drive higher margins and customer stickiness. Independents value the margin improvement these brands deliver vs. national brands.

**Customer diversification:** No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, reducing concentration risk.

**Competitive threats include:** Group Purchasing Organizations (GPOs) that aggregate buying power for independents, potentially compressing distributor margins; regional distributors that can offer more personalized service in specific geographies; and Sysco's continued dominance with ~$78B in revenue and superior margins.

The trajectory is positive: 6.5% independent case growth versus industry growth of 3-4% indicates share gains. The Cheney Brothers acquisition added important Southeast density.

## Risk Assessment

**1. US Foods Combination Uncertainty (HIGH):** Outcomes are highly binary: successful combination (20-30% upside) vs. regulatory block (potential 10-15% downside if deal premium deflates) vs. no deal attempted (neutral to modest negative from wasted resources). FTC precedent from the 2015 Sysco-USFD block is directly relevant.

**2. Leverage (MODERATE-HIGH):** ~3.5x net debt/EBITDA constrains flexibility. The $312M interest burden (first 9 months of FY2026) consumes substantial operating cash flow. While the February 2026 refinancing extended maturities, total debt service remains heavy.

**3. Consumer Spending Deterioration (MODERATE):** With product cost inflation running at 4.5%+ and real wages under pressure, food-away-from-home spending is vulnerable. Restaurant traffic declines would directly reduce case volumes. PFG's thin margins amplify the impact of even modest volume declines.

**4. Convenience Segment Secular Decline (MODERATE):** Cigarette volumes declining structurally limits the 39% of revenue derived from the Convenience segment. Diversification into food/fresh is progressing but cannot fully offset tobacco headwinds.

**5. Cheney Brothers Integration (MODERATE):** The acquisition generated a $19.1M net loss in its first 9 months due to purchase accounting. Integration of a $2B acquisition in the logistics-intensive foodservice business carries execution risk.

**6. Insurance Cost Inflation (LOW-MODERATE):** $28M increase in FY2025 with further increases expected. In a 1.3% operating margin business, $28M is material (~8-9% of operating income).

### Options Market Signal

The flat term structure (29.7% near, 30.0% far) indicates no market expectation of a near-term catalyst. Put/call OI ratio of 0.43 leans call-heavy, consistent with bullish positioning from the analyst community (12 of 13 at Buy/Strong Buy). ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115-135):**
- US Foods combination proceeds with regulatory approval, delivering 20-30% premium to current levels
- Organic independent case growth sustains 5-6%+, driving continued gross margin expansion toward 12%+
- Cheney Brothers synergies fully materialize, reducing integration losses and adding $50-75M in incremental EBITDA
- Food-away-from-home spending remains resilient despite macro headwinds
- FY2027 EPS of $5.78+ × 20-23x P/E = $115-133

**Bear Case ($72-82):**
- USFD combination blocked by FTC (Sysco-USFD 2015 precedent), deal premium deflates
- Consumer recession reduces food-away-from-home traffic by 3-5%, compressing thin operating margins
- Leverage becomes problematic if EBITDA declines — ~3.5x could rise toward 4.5x
- Insurance costs and inflation continue to pressure margins while pricing power is limited
- FY2027 EPS misses at $4.50-5.00 × 16-18x P/E = $72-90

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $82-85 range (forward P/E <18x on FY2027 estimates), providing adequate margin of safety for standalone valuation
2. Definitive agreement announced with US Foods with plausible antitrust path (e.g., significant divestitures pre-agreed)
3. Estimates reverse upward — specifically, if FY2027 EPS consensus rises above $6.00, implying accelerating earnings power

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic independent case growth declines below 3% for two consecutive quarters, indicating competitive position deterioration
2. Net leverage rises above 4.0x without clear path to reduction
3. USFD combination announced and subsequently challenged by FTC with no resolution path within 12 months

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely August 2026 for Q4 FY2026 / full FY2026 results). Key data: FY2026 final results vs. guidance, FY2027 guidance, any update on USFD strategic engagement.

## Conclusion

Performance Food Group is a well-managed #2 player in a consolidating industry with genuine organic growth momentum in its highest-margin customer segment. The clean team agreement with US Foods adds meaningful optionality. However, at 20.7x forward earnings with negative revision momentum, ~3.5x leverage, and severe antitrust uncertainty around the most significant potential catalyst, the current price represents fair value for the standalone business with modest deal premium embedded.

The stock is appropriate to hold for investors with existing positions, given the positive competitive trajectory, potential strategic value creation, and reasonable forward valuation relative to peers. New positions should wait for either a more attractive entry point (~$82-85) or clarity on the USFD engagement. The score trajectory improvement from 4.8 to the current assessment reflects genuine fundamental improvement (Cheney integration progressing, strong independent growth, successful refinancing), but the stock price has also recovered substantially from its March lows, capturing much of this improvement.
