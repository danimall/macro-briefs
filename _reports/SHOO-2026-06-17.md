**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Steven Madden is a fashion-forward footwear and accessories company built on a test-and-react design engine, speed-to-market, and accessible price points across wholesale, direct-to-consumer, and licensing. FY2025 reported results looked alarming on a screen: net income fell 74% to $45M and operating margin compressed from 9.9% to 3.2%, but the decline is concentrated in identifiable, largely one-time items tied to the $403M Kurt Geiger acquisition (closed May 2025) and a tariff shock, not in deteriorating brand economics. Roughly $70M+ of the EBIT decline came from acquisition-related compensation ($38.8M seller-proceeds reallocation), purchase-accounting inventory step-up (~$30.9M through COGS), transaction/legal/severance costs (~$25M), plus an acquisition-driven tax-rate spike to 36.9%. Underlying Wholesale Footwear margins held near 15%.

The central debate is whether the recovery the market is pricing — the stock has run from the low-$20s to ~$45, near its 52-week high — is real. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.00 was flattered by a $55.1M IEEPA tariff-refund benefit run through COGS; on an adjusted basis EPS was ~$0.45 versus ~$0.60 a year earlier. That $0.45 adjusted figure beat the $0.39 consensus by 14.3%. Consensus had already modeled the year-over-year decline and the company exceeded it. Estimate revisions are positive on every horizon (FY26 $2.12, +1.4% over 90 days; FY27 $2.69, +2.7%), the DTC operating loss narrowed to $1.6M from $6.2M, and a court-ordered ~$90M tariff refund is still to be collected in cash. This is a normalizing business absorbing transitory drag, not a structurally impaired one.

The constraint is valuation, not fundamentals. At ~21x forward FY26 EPS against a footwear peer median near 16x, with the stock near its 52-week high and analyst targets clustered around the current price, near-term upside is capped. There is no balance-sheet distress (covenant-compliant, voluntary $60M debt prepayment), the dividend is FCF-covered, and the FY26→FY27 earnings path (~27% growth) provides a credible appreciation thesis as tariff normalization and Kurt Geiger synergies flow through. We rate SHOO HOLD with a constructive bias.

## Company Overview

Steven Madden designs, sources, and markets branded and private-label footwear, handbags, accessories, and apparel. The model emphasizes rapid trend identification rather than long-lead-time fashion bets, and the company sells across all price tiers and channels. Four reportable segments structure the business:

- **Wholesale Footwear (~41% of FY25 revenue):** the historical core (Steve Madden, Dolce Vita, Blondo), sold to department stores, mass merchants, off-price, and clubs. Still earns ~15% operating margin.
- **Wholesale Accessories/Apparel (~25%):** handbags and apparel, expanded by the 2023 Almost Famous acquisition.
- **Direct-to-Consumer (~33%):** retail, e-commerce, and international concessions, the fastest-growing mix and now the largest segment post-Kurt Geiger.
- **Licensing (~0.5% of revenue, ~84% operating margin):** Steve Madden®, Betsey Johnson®, Kurt Geiger® trademarks.

The largest event of the year was the May 2025 acquisition of Kurt Geiger (Kurt Geiger London, KG, Carvela), a UK premium footwear/accessories retailer, funded with a new $300M term loan and $250M revolver. The company also consolidated JVs in Australia/NZ, Malaysia, and Greater China, accelerating its international/DTC pivot. Founder Steve Madden remains Creative and Design Chief; Edward Rosenfeld is Chairman & CEO. The moat is execution speed and brand relevance rather than structural barriers; fashion risk is constant and entry barriers are low.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 earnings decline reads worse than the underlying business performed.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,111M | $1,971M | $2,283M | $2,534M (+11%) |
| Operating income | $282M | $213M | $225M | $81M |
| Operating margin | 13.3% | 10.8% | 9.9% | 3.2% |
| Net income (to SHOO) | $216M | $172M | $169M | $45M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $2.35 | $0.63 |

The net-income decline despite rising revenue decomposes into identifiable items: $38.8M acquisition-related compensation, ~$30.9M purchase-accounting inventory step-up through COGS, ~$13.3M transaction costs, $7.3M legal settlements, $4.0M severance, U.S. tariff drag (China 56.1% of purchases, Cambodia 24.6%; U.S. 66.2% of sales), a tax-rate spike to 36.9% from 23.7%, and a DTC segment that swung to a $34M operating loss on purchase accounting and lower-margin concessions mix. Strip these out and the operating franchise is intact.

Q1 2026 revenue rose 18% to $653M; GAAP net income was $71.8M ($1.00 diluted) versus $40.4M ($0.57). Adjusting for the tariff-refund benefit, EPS was ~$0.45 versus ~$0.60 prior year, beating the $0.39 consensus by 14.3%. Analysts had already modeled the year-over-year decline; the company exceeded it. Steve Madden full-price U.S. DTC was strong.

Balance sheet and cash flow: the company moved from zero long-term debt to a $240M term loan plus revolver, with $292M drawn by Q1 2026, voluntarily prepaying $60M of the term loan in 2025. Altman Z sits at 2.9 (grey zone), driven by new leverage and depressed EBIT rather than distress. The net-leverage covenant ceiling is 3.0x and the company is in compliance. Current ratio 1.9, gross debt/equity ~1.17, equity $948M. FY25 operating cash flow was $162M with $120M FCF; Q1 2026 OCF was negative (-$55M) on a working-capital build that includes the $90M tariff receivable and inventory. The dividend is maintained at $0.21/quarter (~$61M/year) and FCF-covered on a normalized basis; $85M remains on the buyback authorization, with zero repurchases in Q1 as capital is conserved for debt paydown and integration. One structural note: ~93% of cash ($72M of $77M) sat offshore at Q1 2026, which could create repatriation friction for U.S. debt service and dividends.

## Growth Analysis

Reported FY25 revenue grew to $2,534M, with Kurt Geiger and JV consolidation as the primary drivers; Q1 2026 revenue accelerated to +18%. The forward earnings path is the constructive element: consensus FY26 EPS of $2.12 grows to FY27 $2.69, roughly 27%, as three drivers normalize:

1. **Tariff normalization.** The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs (Feb 20, 2026), the Court of International Trade ordered CBP to refund (March 4), and SHOO filed its claim April 20. The ~$90M receivable is a genuine court-ordered cash catalyst. The caveat is that relief is partial: the administration replaced IEEPA tariffs with a new 10% Section 122 global tariff and launched Section 301 investigations, so the net forward tariff impact is not a clean permanent tailwind.

2. **Kurt Geiger margin accretion.** KG adds a higher-margin premium brand and accelerates the DTC/international pivot, the company's stated largest long-term growth initiative. Synergies have not yet appeared in adjusted earnings, which is the bull/bear crux.

3. **DTC and international expansion.** The DTC loss nearly disappeared sequentially, and consolidated JVs extend international reach.

Estimate revisions support the recovery framing: every horizon is rising (current quarter +2.7% over 90 days, next quarter +1.9%, FY26 +1.4%, FY27 +2.7%). These are modest in magnitude rather than the +30% velocity that would force an aggressive re-rating, but they are directionally consistent with a normalizing business. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 small misses over four quarters), which tempers confidence in the precise consensus path but does not contradict the trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$44.61 the stock trades at ~21x forward FY26 EPS and ~17x FY27. The PEG on the FY26→FY27 path is below 1, which on its own looks reasonable. The constraint is peer context and price level.

| CROX | Crocs Inc | $6B | neg | — |

The footwear peer median sits near 16x. SHOO's ~21x forward multiple carries a premium that is partly justified by the KG-driven growth and DTC mix shift, but the premium leaves little margin for execution slippage. Analyst targets — high $54, low $31, median $50, mean ~$47 — bracket the current price; the consensus Strong Buy and ~$47-49 average target imply roughly 5-10% upside to the mean, with downside to the low of ~$31 (-30%). The trailing P/E of ~42x is meaningless here given the depressed FY25 base; forward and normalized metrics are the right lens, and on those the stock is fully valued rather than cheap or stretched.

The valuation score is anchored at the midpoint: the multiple genuinely caps near-term upside, but the stock is not so expensive that any disappointment is catastrophic. A cheap headline screen would mislead, and we treat the proximity-to-target dynamic with skepticism; for a name trading at a premium-to-peer forward multiple, analyst targets are not an independent floor, they reflect the same recovery extrapolation embedded in the price.

## Competitive Landscape

The footwear and accessories space is highly competitive with low entry barriers; SHOO competes against Wolverine, Skechers, Deckers, Caleres, and accessories players like Tapestry and Capri. Industry-wide pressures in 2025-26 include tariff volatility, cautious wholesale ordering, elevated rates pressuring discretionary spend, and the structural shift to omnichannel/DTC. SHOO's strategic response — growing DTC and international, expanding non-footwear categories, and adding the higher-margin Kurt Geiger brand — is aligned with where margin and growth are migrating.

The competitive position is stable rather than clearly improving or deteriorating. The margin-compression event was a mix of company-specific acquisition accounting and sector-wide tariffs, not brand erosion; Wholesale Footwear still earns ~15% operating margins. KG adds a premium brand and accelerates the DTC pivot, but at the cost of integration risk and leverage. The moat of speed-to-market and brand relevance is execution-dependent and structurally thin, which keeps competitive position scored in the middle.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Tariff/sourcing concentration** is the dominant swing factor. This caused FY25 compression and is now generating a refund windfall, but the post-IEEPA backdrop (new 10% Section 122 global tariff plus pending Section 301 actions) leaves forward net impact uncertain.

2. **Kurt Geiger integration and leverage.** New $240M+ variable-rate debt (100bp ≈ $2.9M added interest), $240M+ of added goodwill/intangibles subject to impairment if KG underperforms, GBP/FX translation exposure, and KG controls excluded from SOX assessment for up to a year (13.8% of assets, 19.5% of Q1 revenue).

3. **A building second comp liability.** The KG Transaction Incentive Plan ("Growth Shares") is a cash-settled, EBITA-target plan ($10.59M grant-date fair value per class, six classes, through 2030), liability-classified and remeasured each period; no expense recognized yet but it could create future P&L charges.

4. **Founder/key-person risk** on Steve Madden as Creative Chief, the design engine being the moat.

5. **Wholesale customer concentration** among department stores/off-price, partly mitigated by factoring.

6. **Fashion/inventory risk** inherent to the model; turns fell to 3.4x (3.8x ex-KG seasonal).

A new AI-deployment risk factor was added in the FY25 10-K; the Q1 10-Q reported no material risk-factor changes. The refund timing depends on CBP/Treasury execution; cash was not yet received as of Q1.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 86.4% versus 43.6% historical vol (rich, 82nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (86.4% near-term versus 52.8% six-month), pricing imminent stress around the next event. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.8% (and ±11.9% on the 1-week tenor). Put/call skew is balanced (2.0%) and the put/call OI ratio is very low (0.03), so this is event-volatility pricing rather than a directional bearish bet. The options-delta history shows ATM IV jumping from 55.2% to 103.7% as price rose 10.2%; rising vol alongside a rising stock typically flags an approaching catalyst (likely earnings). For a holder, the rich near-term vol argues against chasing here and favors patience; for the thesis, the balanced skew does not contradict the constructive fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$54)
Tariff relief proves durable net of Section 122/301, the ~$90M refund converts to cash, Kurt Geiger synergies begin flowing into adjusted earnings, and DTC/international continue to scale with the DTC segment returning to profitability. FY27 EPS reaches or exceeds $2.69, and a ~20x multiple on rising estimates supports the high analyst target near $54 (+21% from current). Deleveraging restores buyback optionality.

### Bear Case (~$31)
The new 10% Section 122 tariff plus Section 301 actions offset most of the IEEPA relief, KG integration stalls and synergies disappoint, the Growth Shares plan and acquisition comp pressure the P&L, and discretionary consumer spending weakens. Adjusted earnings fail to inflect, the premium-to-peer multiple compresses toward the ~16x footwear median on FY26 EPS, and the stock retraces to the low analyst target near $31 (-30%).

### Base Case (~$47)
Adjusted earnings stabilize and begin a modest recovery as tariff drag eases partially and KG contributes incrementally. FY26 EPS lands near $2.12, estimates continue their slow upward drift, and the stock holds a ~21-22x forward multiple, settling near the consensus mean of ~$47 (+5%). The dividend remains covered and leverage declines.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY26, expected early August 2026) is the natural reassessment point, particularly for adjusted-earnings trajectory and refund-cash receipt. The 12-month horizon ties to FY26→FY27 earnings delivery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EPS inflects positively year-over-year (e.g., Q2/Q3 adjusted EPS above prior-year comparables), confirming the recovery is in the underlying numbers and not just GAAP refund accounting.
- Forward multiple compresses toward ~16x peer median on stable/rising estimates (e.g., a pullback to ~$34-36 with FY27 estimates intact), restoring asymmetric upside.
- The tariff refund converts to cash and net leverage falls meaningfully below 2.0x, reopening buyback capacity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY26/FY27 estimate revisions turn negative for two consecutive periods, signaling the recovery is failing.
- Section 301 actions or expanded tariffs materially re-impair gross margin, or KG goodwill/intangibles are written down.
- DTC segment reverts to widening losses or covenant headroom compresses below 0.25x of the 3.0x ceiling.

## Conclusion

Steven Madden is a sound, cash-generative footwear franchise whose FY2025 GAAP results were distorted by a debt-funded acquisition and a tariff shock, both of which are partially reversing. The bear case that anchored prior caution — that declining adjusted earnings disprove the recovery — does not survive scrutiny: the adjusted Q1 EPS beat consensus, meaning analysts already modeled the year-over-year decline and the company exceeded it. Estimate revisions are positive on every horizon, the dividend is FCF-covered, the balance sheet is covenant-compliant with a voluntary debt prepayment, and a court-ordered cash refund is still to be collected. This is not a structurally impaired business or a balance-sheet distress story.

It is, however, fully valued. At a premium to footwear peers, near a 52-week high, with analyst targets clustered around the current price and rich near-term options vol pricing imminent event risk, the near-term upside is capped and the burden is on KG synergies and durable tariff relief that have not yet appeared in adjusted earnings. The ~27% FY26→FY27 earnings growth provides a credible appreciation path that justifies holding rather than selling, but the premium multiple and partial nature of the tariff relief argue against chasing here. We rate SHOO **HOLD** (growth_hold), with a constructive bias and a preference to add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s where the forward multiple would compress to peer-level on intact estimates.
