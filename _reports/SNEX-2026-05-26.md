## Executive Summary

StoneX Group delivered an extraordinary Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) with net operating revenues up 70% YoY to $829M and net income up 143% to $174M, primarily driven by physical commodities trading that benefited from tariff-related market dislocations. The RJO acquisition (closed July 2025) is integrating ahead of schedule and approximately doubled StoneX's FCM scale. The company occupies a structurally advantaged position as the largest non-bank FCM in the U.S., filling a gap left by post-Dodd-Frank bank retrenchment.

The stock has appreciated ~100% over the past 12 months, from ~$56 to ~$114. The fundamental question is whether current elevated earnings represent a new baseline or a cyclical peak driven by one-time tariff disruptions in precious metals and agricultural commodities. At ~17.8x forward consensus EPS of $6.41, valuation is reasonable if current conditions persist but expensive relative to normalized mid-cycle earnings. The physical precious metals arbitrage that drove Q2's blowout results — carrying U.S. inventory to mitigate tariff risk, generating 239% YoY growth in physical contract revenues — is directly tied to trade policy uncertainty that could resolve materially in the coming months.

The specific risk preventing a higher-conviction call is the combination of: (1) cyclical peak earnings dependence on tariff volatility, (2) uniform C-suite insider selling totaling $48M+ in May 2026, and (3) a material weakness in internal controls. These factors, against a stock at 91% of its 52-week high after a 100% run, create asymmetric downside risk relative to remaining upside potential.

## Company Overview

StoneX Group operates as a global financial services network connecting 80,000+ commercial/institutional/payments clients and 400,000+ retail accounts across 180+ countries. The business model has four segments:

- **Commercial** (largest profit contributor): Commodities hedging, physical trading and logistics in precious metals, energy, and agricultural products. OTC derivatives. This segment's income grew 151% YoY in Q2 FY2026.
- **Institutional**: Securities and derivatives clearing, prime brokerage, correspondent clearing for ~100 introducing brokers.
- **Self-Directed/Retail**: Forex, CFDs, and wealth management through platforms including City Index and GAIN Capital.
- **Payments**: Cross-border payment solutions in 140 currencies across 180 countries via 375 correspondent bank relationships.

The RJO acquisition (closed July 31, 2025, for $652M cash + 3.1M shares) made StoneX the largest non-bank FCM in the U.S. with $13.7B in combined client segregated assets. The company employs 5,400+ people globally.

### What Drove the Blowout Quarter

The Commercial segment accounted for the majority of upside. Physical contracts net operating revenue reached $164.6M in Q2, up 239% YoY from $48.6M. Management explicitly attributed this to "tariff-related disruptions" in precious metals markets — specifically, carrying U.S. precious metals inventory to service clients seeking to mitigate tariff risk. OTC derivatives within Commercial also grew 98% YoY.

The single largest earnings driver is directly correlated with trade policy uncertainty. If tariff uncertainty resolves (whether through deals or permanent tariff implementation that normalizes trading patterns), this specific revenue stream contracts materially.

The business generates ample free cash flow for a financial services firm, evidenced by the ability to fund $777M in acquisition consideration (RJO) within the year.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Vectors

1. **Market share gains in clearing/execution**: Securities ADV up 35%, listed derivatives volumes up 59% in Q2 FY2026. Part RJO-driven, part organic.
2. **Payments expansion**: $32B+ in payments processed in FY2025 across 140 currencies. Growing through new correspondent relationships and geographic expansion.
3. **Retail/wealth management**: 400,000+ accounts with opportunities for cross-selling and AUM growth.

### Acquisition-Driven Growth

StoneX has completed 8+ acquisitions since 2024 (RJO, Benchmark, Octo, JBR Recovery, Bamboo, Intercam, Plantureux, Right Corporation). The acquisition strategy focuses on adding client relationships, geographic footprint, and product capabilities. Management's track record of integrating smaller bolt-ons (Benchmark, Gain Capital historically) is solid, though RJO is by far the largest acquisition in company history and represents meaningful execution risk.

### Forward Estimates

Consensus (single analyst) estimates $6.41 for FY2026 and $6.65 for FY2027. Estimates were revised up 15.7% over the past 90 days, reflecting the Q2 beat. However, with only 1 analyst providing estimates, consensus is thin and potentially stale.

At $6.41 FY2026 EPS, the forward P/E is ~17.8x. If the current quarterly run-rate were sustained ($2.07 x 4 = $8.28 annualized), the stock trades at ~13.8x. The valuation question reduces to which earnings level is appropriate to capitalize.

### What the Market Is Pricing

At $114 and ~$6.41 consensus, the market is pricing in continued elevated earnings driven by tariff volatility. The stock was at ~$56 a year ago when it earned $5.89 — it traded at ~9.5x earnings then. The re-rating from 9.5x to 17.8x reflects both the earnings acceleration and increased market recognition of StoneX's structural advantages.

If tariff-driven physical commodities trading normalizes (plausible within 2-3 quarters), FY2027 earnings could revert toward $5.50-6.00, which at 17.8x implies a stock price of $98-107 — 7-14% below current levels. At a more normalized 12-14x multiple on normalized earnings of $5.50-6.00, the stock would be worth $66-84.

### My Assessment

The stock is fairly valued at current levels if elevated volatility persists through FY2027. It is modestly expensive if physical commodities revenues revert to historical trend within 6-9 months. The 100% appreciation already reflects the earnings inflection — remaining upside requires either sustained tariff volatility or further multiple expansion, both of which face headwinds.

## Competitive Landscape

StoneX's competitive position is genuinely strong and improving:

1. **Largest non-bank FCM in the U.S.**: Post-RJO, with $13.7B in client segregated assets. This scale creates regulatory capital advantages and client trust.
2. **Only 8 Category One ring-dealing members of the LME**: Structural barrier to entry.
3. **Global payments network**: 375 correspondent banks, 140 currencies, 180 countries — built over decades and extremely difficult to replicate.
4. **Post-Dodd-Frank structural tailwind**: Banks raising minimum client thresholds creates ongoing market-share transfer to well-capitalized non-bank intermediaries.

The competitive moat is widening through scale (RJO doubled FCM operations), geographic reach (Intercam for Latin America, Plantureux for European agriculture), and product breadth. Client switching costs are high in clearing/prime brokerage relationships due to margin documentation, collateral arrangements, and workflow integration.

Threats include: large banks competing for the most profitable institutional clients, fintech payment providers (though StoneX's multi-currency/multi-country breadth is a significant barrier), and periods of low volatility compressing OTC spreads.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Cyclical Earnings Peak (HIGH)**: The single largest driver of the Q2 beat — physical precious metals trading amid tariff disruptions — is directly tied to U.S. trade policy uncertainty. If tariff policy normalizes (either through resolution or permanent implementation that allows hedging patterns to stabilize), this revenue stream could contract materially. Bad debts also spiked to $12.4M in Q2 (from $0.1M prior year), indicating higher credit risk accompanies higher revenue.

2. **Insider Selling (MODERATE-HIGH)**: Over $48M in C-suite selling in May 2026. While these are option exercises (strike $13.34 from ~2017-2018 grants) rather than open-market sales, the uniformity across Sean O'Connor ($33.2M), William Dunaway ($7.1M), Philip Smith ($3.7M), and Diego Rotsztain ($3.8M) is notable. No insider is buying. This suggests management collectively views the stock as at least fairly valued at current levels.

3. **Material Weakness in Internal Controls (MODERATE)**: A newly disclosed material weakness related to balance sheet presentation (repo/reverse repo netting). While this is a presentation issue rather than an earnings integrity concern, it creates regulatory scrutiny risk and signals governance gaps.

4. **Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)**: $478M in FY2025 operating revenues came from interest/fees on client balances. Rate cuts would compress this. However, the RJO acquisition added $5.6B in client float as a volume offset.

5. **Acquisition Integration (MODERATE)**: RJO at $777M total consideration is the largest acquisition in company history. Integration is "on track" per management, but the risk of client attrition from former RJO introducing brokers remains.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 42.6% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 41.6% (1% spread), suggesting the options market views current risk as fairly priced. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.4%), and the put/call volume ratio is extremely low (0.03), indicating minimal informed bearish positioning. Implied moves of 8.4% (1-month) and 11.7% (3-month) are consistent with a stock that just had a major earnings event and may consolidate. Short interest at 5.4% with 4.2 days to cover is moderate.

The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis, but is also not pricing in significant additional upside. The flat skew and fair IV level suggest a consensus view of appropriate valuation with normal uncertainty.

## Conclusion

StoneX occupies a structurally advantaged competitive position that has been validated by the RJO acquisition and strong organic volume growth. The business model's variable cost structure provides meaningful downside protection, and the 26.5% Q2 ROE demonstrates the earnings power potential when market conditions align.

However, the specific concern preventing a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: the physical precious metals trading arbitrage that drove the majority of the Q2 earnings surprise is directly linked to tariff-related market dislocations. With active U.S. trade negotiations ongoing and potential for policy normalization within 2-3 quarters, this revenue stream faces material contraction risk. Combined with uniform C-suite insider selling and a stock that has already doubled in 12 months, the risk/reward at current prices is approximately symmetric rather than asymmetrically favorable.

The appropriate posture is to hold existing positions (the structural thesis is intact and improving) while waiting for either: (a) a price pullback to $90-95 that creates asymmetric entry, or (b) evidence that elevated physical commodities revenue persists through trade policy resolution, confirming a higher normalized earnings base.
