## Executive Summary

DT Midstream is a premium midstream operator with an integrated portfolio of natural gas pipeline and gathering assets connecting Appalachian and Haynesville supply basins to Midwest demand centers and Gulf Coast LNG terminals. The company has delivered 17% adjusted EBITDA growth in FY2025 ($1.138B), achieved investment-grade credit ratings from all three agencies, and assembled a differentiated Midwest pipeline network through the $1.2B Midwest Pipeline Acquisition. The organic growth pipeline, anchored by the $850M-$930M Guardian G3 expansion with 20-year utility contracts, provides visible, high-quality earnings growth through 2028 and beyond.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. At $148.89, DTM trades at ~31.7x consensus FY2026E EPS ($4.70), representing an 80%+ premium to peer median P/E of 18.9x. The consensus analyst mean target of $144.93 sits below the current price. While the data center demand thesis and LNG tailwinds justify a premium, the current multiple embeds execution perfection on multiple expansion projects simultaneously, leaves no margin of safety for the company's 45% revenue concentration in Expand Energy, and offers limited upside even in a favorable scenario. The stock has appreciated 52% over the trailing twelve months and is within 1% of its 52-week high.

The investment case is constructive on a 12-18 month horizon given the visible growth pipeline and structural demand tailwinds, but the risk/reward at current levels favors patience. A pullback to $125-130 would restore an adequate margin of safety and offer a more favorable entry point.

## Company Overview

DT Midstream, spun off from DTE Energy in July 2021, operates through two segments: **Pipeline** (~55% of revenue), consisting of FERC-regulated interstate pipelines, intrastate pipelines, and storage, and **Gathering** (~45% of revenue), comprising gathering systems and treatment plants in the Haynesville and Appalachian basins. The company employs just 588 people, making it one of the leanest operators relative to its asset base in the midstream sector.

Key wholly-owned pipeline assets include Guardian (263 mi, 1.3 Bcf/d capacity, IL/WI), Midwestern Gas Transmission (402 mi, 1.5 Bcf/d, KY/IL/IN/TN), Viking (674 mi, 1.0 Bcf/d, MN/ND/WI), LEAP (221 mi, 2.1 Bcf/d, LA), and Stonewall (68 mi, 1.5 Bcf/d, WV). Joint venture interests include Millennium (52.5%), NEXUS (50%), and Vector (40%). On the gathering side, the Blue Union system (443 mi, 2.6 Bcf/d) serves the Haynesville, while Susquehanna and Ohio Utica systems serve Appalachia.

The strategic thesis centers on DTM's positioning at the intersection of two structural demand drivers: (1) Midwest data center power generation requiring gas pipeline capacity, and (2) Gulf Coast LNG exports requiring Haynesville gathering-to-pipeline connectivity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew 27% YoY in FY2025 to $1.243B, driven by $212M from the Midwest Pipeline Acquisition and organic expansions including LEAP Phase 4 and new Haynesville gathering contracts. Net income reached $441M ($4.30 diluted EPS), up 25% from $354M in FY2024. Adjusted EBITDA grew 17% to $1.138B, with the Q1 2026 run-rate of $308M annualizing to ~$1.23B.

**Margins:** Operating margin of 49.4% and net margin of 36.5% are exceptional, reflecting the fee-based, contracted nature of the business. Approximately 92% of Pipeline revenue and 99% of JV revenue is firm service (fixed demand charges or minimum volume commitments). In the Gathering segment, ~57% is firm and ~36% is flowing gas (volume-dependent).

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow increased 14% to $867M in FY2025. Capex of $426M yielded $441M in free cash flow. Dividends of $324M consumed 73% of FCF, leaving $117M of excess cash flow. Capital guidance for 2026 is $490M-$570M, which will likely reduce FCF unless EBITDA growth accelerates proportionally.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $3.324B in senior notes with no revolver borrowings and $1B of available liquidity. Leverage is approximately 2.9x net debt/EBITDA, appropriate for an investment-grade midstream company. There are no debt maturities until 2029 ($1.1B), providing significant financial flexibility during the heavy capital deployment period. The achievement of investment-grade ratings from all three agencies in 2025 reduced cost of capital and broadened the investor base.

**Earnings Quality:** Q1 2026 EPS of $1.27 beat consensus of $1.16 by 9.8%. The trailing four-quarter record shows 3 beats and 1 miss (Q4 2025 missed by 8.7%), suggesting reasonable but not exceptional earnings visibility. The Q4 miss warrants attention as it disrupts an otherwise clean execution narrative.

## Growth Analysis

DTM's growth pipeline is among the most visible in the midstream sector:

**Guardian G3 Expansion ($850M-$930M, Q4 2028 in-service):** Increases Guardian capacity by 537 MMcf/d (~40%) to 1.8 Bcf/d. Anchored by 20-year negotiated rate precedent agreements with investment-grade utility customers. This is the highest-conviction growth project: long-duration, creditworthy counterparties, expanding into a demand corridor where data center and other industrial operators are building capacity near the Upper Midwest.

**Vector Pipeline 2028 Expansion (+400 MMcf/d):** Approved in Q1 2026 with non-binding open seasons oversubscribed. Serves the Michigan-to-Chicago corridor.

**Midwestern Gas Transmission Expansion:** Open season oversubscribed with customer interest exceeding offered capacity, validating the demand thesis for Midwest gas infrastructure.

**LEAP System:** Reached 2.1 Bcf/d capacity in September 2025 (on budget), connecting Haynesville production to Gulf Coast LNG terminals. Potential for further expansion as LNG export approvals accelerate.

**Carbon Capture:** Class VI permit pending. Early-stage but optionally valuable.

Consensus estimates project EPS of $4.70 for FY2026 and $5.00 for FY2027, implying 9% and 6% growth respectively. However, FY2027 estimates have been revised down 5.8% over the past 90 days ($5.31 → $5.00), which is a cautionary signal. The 30-day revision for the current quarter is modestly positive (+1.1%), but next-year estimates declining at this pace suggests analysts are becoming less confident in the growth trajectory's magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the investment case encounters its primary obstacle.

At $148.89, DTM trades at:
- **34.4x trailing EPS** ($4.30)
- **31.7x FY2026E EPS** ($4.70)
- **29.8x FY2027E EPS** ($5.00)
- **~13.1x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA** ($1.138B)
- **~2.5% dividend yield**

The peer median P/E is 18.9x with a peer median dividend yield of 4.3%. DTM's 80%+ P/E premium and sub-peer dividend yield require substantial growth outperformance to justify. At this price, the stock prices in the Guardian G3 expansion, the Vector expansion, and continued Haynesville momentum with limited allowance for delay, cost overruns, or demand deceleration.

The consensus analyst mean price target is $144.93 with a median of $143.50, both below the current trading price. The high target of $165 offers only 11% upside, while the low target of $127 implies 15% downside. The distribution of analyst ratings shows 1 Strong Buy, 5 Buys, 8 Holds, and 1 Sell (15 analysts total).

Morgan Stanley's April 2026 upgrade to Equal-Weight with a $165 target (the high-water mark among analysts) reflects improved growth visibility, but the timing after a 50%+ run means much of the thesis is already in the price.

To justify the current valuation purely on fundamentals: at 25x earnings (a generous premium for a midstream company), DTM would need to deliver $5.96 EPS, approximately 19% above FY2027 consensus. That requires sustained EBITDA growth of 10%+ annually with no execution missteps, which is possible but demands conviction the market does not appear to share given the consensus median target below current price.

## Competitive Landscape

DTM's competitive position has strengthened materially. The Midwest Pipeline Acquisition created an integrated system (Guardian, Midwestern, Viking, with Vector and NEXUS JV connections) that forms a near-monopoly position for serving certain Upper Midwest demand centers. TC Energy (Columbia Gas, ANR) is also pursuing data center power demand, but DTM's geographic positioning along key Appalachian-to-Midwest corridors provides a durable advantage.

In the Haynesville, competition from Williams (Transco), Energy Transfer, and Momentum Midstream is more intense, and the gathering segment's ~36% flowing gas exposure makes it more sensitive to producer activity levels. Haynesville volumes hit record highs in 2025 as LNG demand signals strengthened, benefiting DTM's Blue Union gathering system and LEAP pipeline.

Oversubscribed open seasons across multiple expansion projects confirm that DTM's assets are in demand, and the 20-year Guardian G3 contracts provide visibility that few midstream competitors can match.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (Critical):** Expand Energy (formed from the Chesapeake-Southwestern merger in October 2024) accounts for approximately 45% of DTM's operating revenues, among the highest single-customer concentrations in the midstream sector. Within the Gathering segment specifically, the concentration is even higher. While the relationship is underpinned by long-term firm contracts with MVCs and acreage dedications, any material change in Expand Energy's production strategy, financial health, or contract renewal posture would disproportionately affect DTM.

**Execution Risk:** The simultaneous pursuit of Guardian G3, Vector expansion, Midwestern expansion, BEST project, and carbon capture initiatives represents a significant step-up in capital deployment from DTM's historical levels. 2026-27 committed capital of $400M-$440M annually is elevated relative to historical FCF generation. Cost overruns, permitting delays (particularly FERC and NEPA), or construction challenges on aging infrastructure segments could delay or reduce returns.

**Estimate Revision Momentum:** FY2027 EPS estimates have declined 5.8% over the past 90 days. This negative revision trend is concerning for a stock trading at 30x+ forward earnings and suggests the market may be recalibrating growth expectations.

**Natural Gas Price Sensitivity:** Though substantially contracted, the ~36% flowing gas revenue in the Gathering segment is exposed to producer activity decisions driven by gas prices. Sustained low Henry Hub prices could reduce Haynesville drilling and volumes.

**Macro Environment:** The stagflationary macro backdrop with rising rates creates headwinds for capital-intensive businesses with large expansion capex pipelines. If 10-year yields approach 5%, the relative attractiveness of DTM's 2.5% dividend yield diminishes further against a risk-free alternative.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($165-$175, 25-30% probability):** Guardian G3 completes on schedule and on budget, Vector and Midwestern expansions secure firm contracts at attractive returns, Haynesville volumes continue rising on LNG demand, data center buildout accelerates beyond current projections, and consensus FY2027 EPS revises upward to $5.50+. At 30x forward earnings, this implies ~$165-$175.

**Base Case ($140-$155, 45-50% probability):** Growth projects execute roughly as planned with modest delays or cost overruns, EBITDA reaches $1.35B-$1.40B by FY2027, EPS reaches $5.00-$5.20, and the valuation multiple compresses to 28-30x as rate uncertainty persists. This implies roughly current levels or modest upside.

**Bear Case ($110-$125, 20-25% probability):** FY2027 estimate revisions continue downward, Expand Energy renegotiates gathering contracts at lower rates upon renewal, data center buildout faces permitting delays or power procurement shifts (small modular reactors gain traction), rising rates compress the valuation multiple to 22-25x, and/or a macro recession reduces industrial gas demand. At 24x a $4.80 EPS scenario, the stock trades to ~$115.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $125-$130, restoring a risk/reward profile where base case upside to $155+ offers 20%+ return with 12-15% downside to bear case
2. FY2027 consensus EPS stabilizes and begins revising upward; specifically, 30-day FY2027 revision turning positive
3. Guardian G3 receives full FERC approval with no material conditions or timeline changes

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Expand Energy announces production curtailments or contract renegotiations that would reduce DTM revenue by >5%
2. FY2027 EPS consensus falls below $4.50 (another 10% downward revision from current $5.00)
3. Stock exceeds $165 without corresponding upward EPS revisions (multiple expansion beyond 33x FY2027 without fundamental support)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late July/early August 2026) for Q2 results and updated guidance on Guardian G3 permitting progress and Midwestern/Vector expansion contract announcements.

## Conclusion

DT Midstream is a high-quality midstream company with improving competitive positioning, visible multi-year growth driven by data center demand and LNG exports, and a clean balance sheet with no near-term debt maturities. The management team has executed well since the 2021 spin-off, delivering cumulative TSR of 348% vs. 206% for peers. The business fundamentals deserve a premium multiple.

The premium is already in the price. At 31.7x FY2026E and 29.8x FY2027E with declining forward estimates, the stock offers limited margin of safety. The 45% Expand Energy concentration risk, while managed through contracts today, represents a structural vulnerability that the market appears to underweight at this multiple. In the current macro environment, paying 30x+ for a midstream company requires either higher growth visibility or a lower entry price than currently available.

Maintain HOLD with a growth orientation. The assets are well-positioned for secular demand themes. Wait for either a price correction to $125-130 or upward estimate revisions that would validate the current multiple.
