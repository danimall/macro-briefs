## Executive Summary

Fluor Corporation is a large-cap engineering and construction (E&C) company with a $6.6B market capitalization, currently trading at $45.64. The company ended 2025 with a $25.5B backlog and announced a $1.4B stock buyback program for 2026, signaling management confidence in forward cash generation. However, the Q4 2025 results were ugly: a $1.57B net loss ($9.87 per share) driven by non-recurring items, and full-year 2025 revenue of $15.5B alongside a $51M net loss. The trailing P/E is negative at -146.8x.

The stock has been volatile — falling from $57.50 (52-week high) to $29.20 (52-week low in early April 2025), then recovering to the mid-$40s. The February 2026 earnings release triggered a sharp rally from $45.48 to $53.69, but the stock has since retraced to $45.64. The NuScale share sale generated $1.35B in gross proceeds, a material liquidity event that funds the buyback program. Analyst fair value estimates have been revised up to approximately $54.22, with price targets in the high-$50s to low-$60s range. In the current stagflationary macro environment with oil at $119, Fluor's heavy exposure to energy infrastructure could be a tailwind for new awards but a headwind for project cost management.

## Company Overview

Fluor is one of the world's largest publicly traded engineering, procurement, construction, and maintenance (EPCM) companies. The company operates across energy, chemicals, mining, infrastructure, and government services. It has historically been a contractor of choice for mega-projects in the oil & gas, petrochemical, and nuclear sectors. The NuScale Power relationship (small modular reactor technology) had been a key growth narrative, though Fluor has now divested 71 million NuScale shares and is liquidating its remaining 40 million share position.

Key business drivers: (1) energy infrastructure spending, particularly in LNG, refining, and petrochemicals; (2) U.S. government/defense infrastructure; (3) mining and metals; (4) nuclear (now primarily through NuScale royalties/residual stake).

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** Fluor reported a $1.57B net loss in Q4 2025, resulting in a full-year 2025 net loss of $51M. EPS for Q4 was -$9.87, though adjusted EPS was $0.33 (missing the $0.34 estimate by 2.94%). The massive Q4 loss appears driven by non-recurring items — likely write-downs, litigation settlements, or project-related charges — rather than operational deterioration. The company had beaten EPS estimates by $0.23 in the prior quarter, suggesting the underlying business generates positive adjusted earnings.

**Revenue:** Q4 2025 revenue was $4.173B, down $85M year-over-year. Full-year 2025 revenue was $15.5B. Revenue flatness is consistent with E&C industry patterns where backlog conversion takes 2-4 years.

**Backlog:** $25.5B at year-end 2025 — a strong indicator of future revenue. At a $15.5B annual revenue run rate, this represents approximately 1.6 years of revenue visibility. The quality of this backlog matters significantly; E&C companies have historically booked projects that later proved unprofitable (Fluor itself has experienced this).

**Capital Returns:** The $1.4B buyback target for 2026 represents approximately 21% of the current market cap. Funded primarily by the $1.35B NuScale share sale proceeds, this is aggressive capital return that should provide meaningful per-share value accretion if executed near current prices.

**NuScale Divestiture:** Fluor received $1.35B for 71M NuScale shares. It retains 40M shares being liquidated via a trading program. At NuScale's recent prices (roughly $19/share based on the $1.35B/71M share implied price), the remaining 40M shares represent approximately $760M in additional potential proceeds. Combined, Fluor will have extracted over $2B from its NuScale position.

**Balance Sheet:** Detailed balance sheet data was not available in the provided financials, but the NuScale sale and buyback program imply management believes the company has sufficient liquidity and leverage capacity. The 10-K/A amendment filed March 2, 2026, warrants attention — amendments to annual reports sometimes signal accounting adjustments or restatements.

**Valuation Multiples:** Forward P/E of -146.8x reflects the trailing losses. On an adjusted earnings basis, assuming $0.33 quarterly adjusted EPS annualizes to roughly $1.32, the stock trades at approximately 34.6x adjusted earnings — expensive for an E&C company. The analyst fair value estimate of $54.22 implies approximately 19% upside from current levels.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive catalysts:** The $25.5B backlog provides substantial revenue runway. In the current macro environment, several of Fluor's end markets benefit from elevated spending:

1. **Energy infrastructure:** Oil at $119 and the Gulf-wide energy infrastructure disruption drive demand for refinery, LNG, and petrochemical construction in the Americas. The JERA buyer-shift statement (Asian buyers permanently reducing Middle East procurement, favoring US/Canadian producers) would directly benefit Fluor's energy project pipeline in North America.

2. **Government/defense infrastructure:** The $200B Pentagon supplemental request and broader defense spending cycle benefit Fluor's government services business.

3. **Nuclear:** While Fluor is divesting its NuScale equity, the nuclear renaissance supports new project awards for nuclear construction services.

**Negative factors:** Revenue was flat to slightly down in 2025. The E&C industry is characterized by lumpy project awards, execution risk, and thin margins (typically 3-6% operating margins). In a stagflationary environment, labor costs and materials inflation compress margins on fixed-price contracts. Fluor's history includes significant project write-downs and cost overruns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.64, Fluor trades at roughly 0.43x trailing revenue ($15.5B) — reasonable for E&C but not cheap given margin uncertainty. The adjusted earnings multiple of ~34.6x (based on annualized Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.33) is elevated. Analyst consensus fair value near $54.22 and price targets in the high-$50s to low-$60s suggest 19-32% upside.

The buyback program provides a valuation floor: $1.4B in buybacks against a $6.6B market cap absorbs a substantial portion of the float. However, buyback execution depends on NuScale share liquidation proceeds and operating cash flow. If NuScale shares decline or project cash flows disappoint, the buyback program could be curtailed.

Compared to peers (Jacobs, AECOM, KBR), Fluor's valuation on a revenue multiple basis appears in-line, but its profitability metrics (negative GAAP earnings) are weaker.

## Competitive Landscape

Fluor competes with Jacobs Solutions (J), AECOM (ACM), KBR (KBR), Bechtel (private), and Worley (ASX: WOR). Among publicly traded E&C firms, Fluor is distinguished by its heavy weighting toward energy and chemicals construction, which is cyclically advantageous in the current environment but increases project execution risk.

Competitive advantages: (1) scale and global presence; (2) long-standing relationships with major oil companies and governments; (3) demonstrated ability to manage mega-projects; (4) nuclear capabilities through NuScale relationship.

Competitive disadvantages: (1) historical project write-downs erode credibility; (2) thinner margins than more asset-light engineering peers; (3) NuScale equity divestiture removes the highest-growth narrative from the story.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk (HIGH):** Fluor's history of project write-downs and cost overruns is the dominant risk. The Q4 $1.57B loss, while attributed to non-recurring items, underscores the binary nature of E&C project outcomes. In a stagflationary environment with labor inflation and $119 oil driving input costs higher, fixed-price contracts become especially dangerous.

**Macro Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Recession probability at 40-45% and the Fed discussing rate hikes create a hostile environment for construction spending. While energy infrastructure spending may be insulated, government infrastructure budgets could face sequestration pressure, and private-sector capital projects get deferred in recessions.

**NuScale Liquidation Risk (MODERATE):** The remaining 40M NuScale shares represent ~$760M in value. NuScale is a volatile small-cap nuclear technology company; a significant decline in its share price before liquidation would impair Fluor's buyback capacity.

**Insider Activity (NEGATIVE):** Multiple C-suite executives disposed of shares in early March 2026. While most were coded as tax withholding (code "F"), Group President Alvin Collins sold 15,610 shares at $45.62 on March 10 — a discretionary sale. Executive Chairman David Constable's tax withholding of 92,243 shares ($4.16M) was the largest disposition. Combined insider dispositions in early March exceeded $6M.

**10-K/A Amendment (MONITORING):** The March 2, 2026, 10-K/A filing warrants investigation. Amendments to annual reports can signal accounting adjustments, restatement risk, or disclosure corrections. This has occurred in consecutive years (March 2025 and March 2026), which could indicate systematic issues with initial filings.

**Governance Transition:** Executive Chairman David Constable is retiring at the May 2026 shareholder meeting, with Lead Independent Director Jim Hackett assuming the chairmanship. Leadership transitions introduce uncertainty.

- Energy infrastructure super-cycle driven by $119 oil, JERA buyer shift to Americas, and Gulf energy disruption benefits Fluor's core competency

- Stagflationary macro (recession at 40-45%, rate hikes discussed) threatens project deferrals and margin compression
- Insider selling across C-suite in March 2026 suggests limited insider confidence at current prices
- 10-K/A amendments in consecutive years raise accounting quality concerns
- NuScale equity sale removes the highest-growth optionality from the investment case
- Historical pattern: E&C companies with large backlogs have repeatedly disappointed when backlog margins prove lower than initially booked

## Conclusion

Fluor presents a mixed picture: a large, well-positioned E&C company with a substantial backlog and aggressive capital return program, offset by GAAP losses, execution risk, insider selling, and cyclical vulnerability in a stagflationary environment. The stock's 22.7% price appreciation over the past year has already captured much of the NuScale monetization catalyst. At $45.64, with analyst fair value near $54, there is potential upside but the risk-reward is balanced rather than compelling.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The backlog and buyback provide downside support, but the negative GAAP earnings, insider selling, 10-K/A amendments, and macro headwinds warrant waiting for better visibility on margin normalization. Investors already holding FLR should retain the position; new capital should wait for either a pullback to the $38-40 range (where the buyback provides a harder floor) or evidence of sustained GAAP profitability improvement.
