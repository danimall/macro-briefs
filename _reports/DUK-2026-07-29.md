**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Duke Energy is one of the largest regulated electric utility holding companies in the U.S., with ~55,757 MW of net proportional capacity serving high-growth service territories in the Carolinas, Florida, and the Midwest. The investment case rests on a single dominant dynamic: after two decades of flat load, electricity demand in Duke's territories is inflecting sharply on data-center/AI buildout, manufacturing reshoring, and population growth. Management describes power-demand growth at "10x the historic pace" and has converted the abstraction into contracts: 7.6 GW of signed Energy Service Agreements (ESAs) now underpin its confidence in the top half of a reaffirmed 5–7% long-term EPS growth algorithm. This is the same demand signal now visible across the regulated-utility complex (peer data centers, transmission rate base, electrical equipment orders), which argues the load surge is real and broad-based rather than a single-company narrative.

The complication is that Duke must fund an enormous multi-year capital plan (Q1 capex $4.09B, up from $3.15B) against rising interest expense ($968M/quarter and climbing) and a deeply negative free-cash-flow profile: operating cash flow fell to $1,512M in Q1 from $2,177M. Management has responded with sophisticated capital sourcing: a $6B Brookfield minority investment into Florida Progress (recorded to equity, control retained, no dilutive common raise), the ~$2.5B Piedmont Tennessee sale to Spire, a $3.1B tax-credit monetization, and a $6B ATM forward-sales program. These transactions are designed to displace planned debt and equity issuance through 2029 while protecting credit metrics. They are the crux of the thesis: whether Duke can grow rate base 7%+ without breaking its balance sheet or over-diluting.

At $129.16, near the 52-week high of $134.49 and roughly 13% above the low, the stock trades at ~20x current-year EPS, a discount to the peer median of 23.8x, but a discount largely justified by Duke's higher leverage and the affordability/regulatory friction attaching to its data-center strategy, which turned adversarial in Florida in July 2026. Analyst consensus targets ($131 low / $146 high, $139 median) offer limited near-term upside, and Mizuho recently trimmed its target to $135 from $139 while reiterating Outperform. We rate DUK a HOLD (growth_hold). The dividend is well-covered at ~3.4%, the load-growth thesis is genuine and de-risking, but the entry point offers no asymmetry and the higher-for-longer rate path directly taxes a negative-FCF financing model. This maintains the prior three-report HOLD; the score has drifted lower (6.4 → 6.6 → 6.1) as valuation caught up to fundamentals and rate risk stayed elevated.

## Company Overview

Duke Energy operates through two segments. **Electric Utilities & Infrastructure (EU&I)** is the dominant unit — ~$175.6B of $198B total assets and roughly $1.25B of $1.79B in quarterly segment income. It comprises regulated electric utilities in the Carolinas (Duke Energy Carolinas, Duke Energy Progress), Florida (Duke Energy Florida), and the Midwest (Duke Energy Indiana, Ohio, Kentucky), running a large Carolinas nuclear fleet alongside coal, natural gas, solar, and storage. **Gas Utilities & Infrastructure (GU&I)** houses Piedmont Natural Gas plus Ohio/Kentucky gas distribution and midstream/RNG investments.

The economics are a classic rate-base growth model: Duke earns regulated returns (authorized ROEs ~9.75%–10.1%, equity ratios ~53%) on capital deployed into its monopoly franchise. The competitive moat is the regulated franchise across fast-growing Southeast/Midwest territories that happen to be prime data-center siting locations. A structural efficiency positive is the combination of Duke Energy Carolinas and Duke Energy Progress into a single utility effective January 1, 2027, carrying a 14-year customer-savings guarantee.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 revenue rose 11.3% to $9,178M (from $8,249M), driven by rate-case pricing (+$202M), higher fuel pass-through revenues (+$286M), improved weather, and customer growth. GAAP EPS was $1.97 (vs $1.76 prior); adjusted EPS $1.93 (vs $1.76). The quarter benefited from ~$171M of asset-sale gains (Piedmont TN) offset by −$150M of legal/regulatory settlement charges. Net income to common was $1,536M (vs $1,365M). The earnings-surprise record is steady and modestly positive — three beats and one in-line across the trailing four quarters, with the largest surprise +5.3% (Q2 2025) — supporting confidence in the reaffirmed FY2026 EPS guide of $6.55–$6.80.

The balance sheet reflects the capital intensity of the model: total assets $198.0B, net PP&E $132.3B, goodwill $19.0B, total book debt ~$88B (LT debt $80.5B plus $7.4B current maturities), against $56.5B equity (including $2.0B noncontrolling interest). Interest expense of $968M/quarter is rising (from $889M) and is the key sensitivity in a capital-heavy, higher-rate environment. Q1 capex against operating cash flow underscores the reliance on external financing: this is a structurally negative-FCF business dependent on continuous, timely regulatory cost recovery and market access.

The Altman Z-Score screens in the "distress" zone (~0.8), but this is expected and not meaningful for a regulated utility; the model penalizes the high leverage and low asset turnover inherent to the business. The genuine question is balance-sheet capacity to fund the plan without credit downgrades or excessive dilution, which is precisely why the funding transactions below are strategically central.

The quarterly dividend was raised to $1.065 (from $1.045), ~$846M/quarter, a ~3.4% yield. Coverage is comfortable against the guided EPS range.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is stronger than a generic utility rate-base story because it has been partly contractualized. The shift from "projected" data-center load to signed ESAs is the single most important de-risking event, and it is why KeyBanc upgraded (July 2026), Morgan Stanley raised its target, and Mizuho reiterated Outperform. Duke's Carolinas and Florida territories are among the fastest-growing in the nation for both population and data-center siting.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-quarter consensus was revised +4.4% over 30 days (+4.7% over 90 days), but current-year and next-year estimates are flat (+0.1% over 90 days), and next-quarter estimates ticked down 2.4%. This does not meet the threshold of an earnings-inflection signal; it is a steady mid-single-digit compounder, not a re-rating candidate on revision velocity.

The demand backdrop is corroborated across the regulated-utility complex. Data-center load is now visible in hard commercial-KWh data at peers, in electrical-equipment order books (data-center orders up materially), and in structural-steel demand into transmission and substations. The breadth of that confirmation raises the durability of Duke's growth thesis at the infrastructure layer, even as the chip layer has shown isolated deceleration signals. The offset is conversion risk: the 10-Q explicitly flags that signed capacity is not delivered load, cautioning "particularly if usage of electricity by data centers is less than currently projected."

## Valuation Assessment

At $129.16, DUK trades at ~19.9x trailing/forward EPS and ~19.3x the $6.70 current-year consensus. Against the Electric Utilities peer median P/E of 23.8x, that is a meaningful discount, but the discount is largely earned: peers such as Southern (23.8x), Entergy (28.1x), and AEP (19.0x) span a wide range, and Duke's higher leverage plus the affordability/regulatory friction attaching to its data-center strategy justify a below-median multiple. The ~3.4% dividend yield sits above the peer median of 2.3%, providing yield support.

We do not treat the low relative multiple as a buy signal in isolation. Cheapness without an independent catalyst is a value-trap flag, and here the identifiable catalysts (rate-case outcomes, FERC/PJM ruling, Florida PSC posture) are two-sided or adverse near-term. On forward earnings, the stock offers modest upside to the $138.84 mean analyst target (~7%) and to the $146 high (~13%), with the $131 low target only ~1% above spot. Mizuho's trim to a $135 target implies the stock is already close to fair value on some sell-side models.

There is no cycle-adjustment argument to make here: utility earnings are not at a trough, estimates are flat, and the multiple already reflects the load-growth optimism. The valuation is fair, not cheap — enough support from yield and rate-base visibility to hold, insufficient asymmetry to justify a BUY at these levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Duke's competitive position is structurally strong and, on trajectory, improving. As a regulated monopoly it faces no meaningful threat from other utilities; the relevant "competition" is with regulators, consumer advocates, and state legislatures balancing growth capex against residential affordability. Duke is uniquely positioned in that its Carolinas and Florida franchises are prime data-center corridors, and the shift to signed ESAs converts a demand narrative into bankable load. Its "Customer Protection Plus" framework, structuring long-term data-center contracts to protect other ratepayers, is emerging as the industry template, a competitive-position positive if it clears regulators.

The trajectory qualifier is Florida. The state has turned adversarial: the FL PSC (July 2026) cast doubt on Duke's data-center plan in the first test of DeSantis's new consumer-protection law, and North Carolina hearings have questioned Duke's role in attracting data centers amid affordability concerns. Because Florida is both the growth engine and the Brookfield-partnered entity, regulatory posture there is the swing factor for whether the competitive advantage converts to shareholder value.

## Risk Assessment

**Financing/execution risk on the capital plan.** Rising interest expense, dependence on capital markets, and reliance on staged Brookfield closings ($200M by 12/31/26, $500M by 6/30/27, $1.5B by 12/31/27, $1B by 6/30/28) plus $6B of ATM equity forwards. A credit downgrade or market disruption would raise financing costs and pressure the growth algorithm. The higher-for-longer rate path directly taxes this negative-FCF model.

**Data-center load conversion.** The top-half-of-guidance thesis leans on the ESAs converting to actual usage. The 10-Q's explicit hedging language shows management is aware signed capacity ≠ delivered load.

**Regulatory cost-allocation, bifurcating by state.** Florida (PSC skepticism under the new consumer-protection law) is the adversarial front; the Carolinas remain constructive (SB266 fuel recovery, DEC/DEP merger settlements with savings guarantees, resource-plan acceptance). Multiple large active rate cases (2025 NC: DEC ~$952M/2yr, DEP $729M, both requesting 10.95% ROE) plus a pending **NC Supreme Court decision (expected Q2 2026)** on 2023 rate-case ROE appeals.

**PJM DFAX ruling (FERC, March 2026).** Could impose retroactive transmission cost refunds/surcharges back to June 2015 at Duke Energy Ohio; financial impact "unable to reasonably estimate" — an unquantified tail.

**Environmental and weather.** 2024 CCR (coal ash) Rule expansion with a final rule expected Q4 2026 (impact "could be material"); asbestos reserves $386M; Winter Storm Fern (Jan 2026) drove $287M restoration plus ~$809M in recovery filings; recurring Florida hurricane exposure. **Goodwill impairment risk** at the Duke Energy Ohio GU&I unit, the only unit whose fair value did not materially exceed carrying value at the August 2025 test.

**Cash-flow quality caveat.** Q1 GAAP earnings were flattered by the asset-sale gains while underlying operating cash flow declined YoY on deferred fuel/storm costs.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 21.2% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 15.7% (a 5.6pp spread), consistent with event-hedging into rate-case and regulatory rulings rather than directional stress. The put/call skew is essentially balanced (−0.5%), the term structure is flat (near 21.2% ≈ far 21.8%), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.2%. The June-to-July snapshot shows ATM IV collapsing from an anomalous 224.5% reading to 21.2% and the P/C OI ratio easing from 0.85 to 0.51 (call-tilted). Nothing in the options complex contradicts the fundamental view: the market is pricing ordinary utility uncertainty, not an imminent binary. The 6-month tenor carries a slightly negative skew (−2.9%), reflecting some longer-dated hedging demand into the regulatory calendar. Short interest is low at 2.5% of float (4.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-positioning overhang either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$146–$173).** Load growth converts: the ESAs deliver actual load, additional data-center contracts sign, and Duke delivers the top half of 5–7% EPS growth (compounding toward ~$7.17 next year and beyond). The Brookfield/Piedmont/tax-credit funding stack proves it can grow rate base without a dilutive common raise or a downgrade. Carolinas rate cases clear near requested ROEs, Florida regulatory friction resolves via the Customer Protection Plus framework, and the market re-rates the stock toward the utility peer median (23.8x) on de-risked visible growth. At 22x $7.17 that is ~$158; the $146 street high and beyond becomes reachable if rates ease and the load thesis fully validates.

**Bear case (~$108–$115).** A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs on a negative-FCF plan; the ATM forwards and staged Brookfield closings dilute per-share growth more than expected; data-center load undershoots ESAs; Florida regulators reject or gut the data-center cost-allocation model; the NC Supreme Court cuts authorized ROE; and the PJM DFAX ruling and CCR rule crystallize into material liabilities. The multiple compresses toward 16–17x on ~$6.70–$7.00 EPS, taking the stock to the low $110s, near the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings and the Q2 2026 NC Supreme Court ROE decision; the FL PSC data-center rulings; and the NCUC Carolinas Resource Plan order expected by 12/31/26.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$120s or below, restoring a margin of safety (forward P/E ~17–18x) against the mid-single-digit growth algorithm.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity on current-year/next-year EPS (currently flat) as ESAs convert to load, plus constructive Carolinas rate-case ROE outcomes at or near the 10.95% request.
- Clear resolution of the Florida data-center cost-allocation dispute in Duke's favor, de-risking the growth engine and the Brookfield-partnered entity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit-rating downgrade or a material widening of the equity-issuance need beyond the current ATM/Brookfield plan, signaling the funding stack is failing to protect metrics.
- An adverse FL PSC ruling that materially caps data-center cost recovery, or an NC Supreme Court decision cutting authorized ROE.
- A material adverse PJM DFAX true-up or CCR-rule liability crystallizing, or a goodwill impairment at the Ohio GU&I unit.
- Evidence that signed ESAs are not converting to load (delayed or cancelled interconnections).

**Base-case fair value: ~$135–$140** (roughly 20x the $6.70–$7.17 forward EPS range with modest yield support), consistent with the analyst mean/median of $138.84/$139.00 and implying ~5–8% total return including the dividend over 12 months. Upside to the bull case (~$146+) is ~13%+; downside to the bear case (~$110–$115) is ~11–15%. The near-symmetric risk/reward, with the low analyst target ($131) barely above spot, is what keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Conclusion

Duke Energy is a high-quality regulated utility positioned at the center of the most important secular theme in its sector, and the conversion of data-center demand into signed ESAs has genuinely de-risked the growth algorithm relative to a year ago. The capital-sourcing architecture — Brookfield's minority stake in Florida Progress, the Piedmont Tennessee sale, the tax-credit monetization, and the ATM forwards — is sophisticated and, so far, effective at funding a ~7% rate-base plan while avoiding a dilutive common raise. Management execution has been steady, with three of the last four quarters beating.

The stock, however, sits near its 52-week high at ~20x earnings, offering a modest discount to peers that is largely justified by higher leverage and by regulatory friction that has turned adversarial in Florida, its growth engine. The business remains deeply free-cash-flow negative and exposed to a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the cost of the financing model on which the entire thesis depends. Estimate revisions are flat, not accelerating, and consensus targets imply only single-digit upside with the low target essentially at spot. This is a stock to own for its ~3.4% covered dividend and mid-single-digit compounding, monitored for the rate-case, FERC, and Florida regulatory catalysts that will determine whether the load growth converts to shareholder value. We maintain HOLD and would become a more active buyer on weakness toward the low-$120s or on a positive turn in estimate-revision velocity alongside constructive regulatory outcomes.
