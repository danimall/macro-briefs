# Investment Research Report: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co Ltd (TSM)

**Recommendation: BUY** | **Price: $403.31** (July 30, 2026 close; $404.25 July 31) | **Overall Score: 7.3/10**
**Base case $475 | Bull $560 | Bear $320 | 12-month horizon**

## Executive Summary

TSMC reported its fifth consecutive record quarterly profit in Q2 2026 and raised guidance on both revenue and capital spending. Revenue of $40.20B landed at the top of the guided $39.0–40.2B range; gross margin of 67.7% exceeded the 65.5–67.5% guide, and operating margin of 60.3% came in 180bp above the guided high end. Net income of NT$706.56bn beat the LSEG consensus of NT$632.64bn by 11.7% and rose 77.4% year over year. Management lifted the full-year 2026 revenue growth outlook to "slightly above 40%" and guided Q3 revenue to $44.6–45.8B, a 12.4% sequential increase at the midpoint. This is a supply-constrained business operating at 64% trailing-twelve-month gross margin and above 50% net margin at roughly $140B annualized scale.

The stock trades at $403, which is 15.8% below the $479 intraday high set June 30 and roughly flat with where it traded in early May. Over the same window consensus EPS estimates moved the other way: FY2026 up 8.8% in 90 days to $16.82 per ADR, FY2027 up 12.0% to $21.61, with 30-day revisions (+6.3% and +7.3%) running at a pace consistent with continued upward drift. That combination of price down and estimates up has compressed the forward multiple from roughly 24.8x next-year earnings at the June high to **18.7x today**, against approximately 28% expected FY2026-to-FY2027 EPS growth (PEG ≈ 0.66). The lowest published analyst target in the coverage set is $430, 6.4% above spot; the mean is $540.

The bear case rests on where the cash goes, not on demand. 2026 capex was raised to $60–64B from $52–56B, taking capital intensity from roughly 33.5% of revenue in FY2025 to about 36.5% this year, and the Arizona commitment was expanded by a further $100B to $265B in total. Net margin rose from 44.50% (FY2025) to 50.37% (TTM June 2026) while free cash flow margin *fell* from 26.32% to 25.75%. Holding the trailing OCF-to-revenue ratio of 59.3% and applying $62B of capex to roughly $170B of 2026 revenue puts FCF near $39B, broadly flat despite ~40% revenue growth, and a ~1.9% FCF yield on a ~$2.09 trillion market capitalization. The P&L is a poor proxy for owner cash returns in 2026. The reinvestment is being made into demand the company cannot currently serve, which is the value-maximizing choice, but investors must own the security on that judgment rather than on the 18.7x headline.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing logic chips designed by others. Its economic position rests on being the only merchant manufacturer able to volume-produce logic at the current leading edge, which makes it the physical chokepoint for most advanced AI, smartphone, and high-performance computing silicon.

Q2 2026 composition (company disclosure): advanced technologies at 7nm and below were 77% of wafer revenue; 5nm was 33% of total revenue and 3nm 30%, so the two leading nodes together are roughly 63%. The 2026 platform mix is High-Performance Computing 66%, smartphone 22%, IoT 5%, with the residual across automotive, digital consumer electronics, and other.

The 66% HPC share is concentration, not diversification. Two-thirds of revenue now depends on one end-market's capital-spending cycle, and nearly two-thirds on two process nodes. Named customers reported by CNBC include Nvidia, Apple, and Broadcom; those firms design chips and pay TSMC to fabricate them. TSMC does not own or design its customers' products. We have no verified top-customer concentration percentage; that disclosure sits in the Form 20-F, which we could not access, and we decline to infer customer concentration from platform mix in either direction.

Segment data from a third-party aggregator splits FY2025 revenue into Wafer NT$3,272,553m (85.9%) and Other Product NT$536,501m (14.1%). The "Other" line, which includes advanced packaging (CoWoS), mask-making, and testing, grew 2.95x from FY2021 to FY2025 versus 2.33x for wafer revenue, consistent with advanced packaging becoming a profit center rather than a service adjunct. These segment definitions are unaudited and were not reconciled to the annual report.

**A material disclosure on data quality.** TSMC is a foreign private issuer: it files Form 20-F and Form 6-K, not 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, or Form 4. The 20-F content — risk factors, business description, audited statements and footnotes — was not read. As a result Altman Z-Score, Piotroski F-Score, ROE, ROA, current ratio, and asset turnover could not be computed and are not estimated here. Multi-year income statement, cash flow, and balance sheet history below comes from a third-party aggregator; the quarterly guidance-versus-actual table is company-primary from TSMC's investor relations site. There is no insider transaction or compensation data because none exists in US filings for this issuer; "no insider signal" means "not observable," not "neutral."

We also flag an internal inconsistency in the supplied profile data: it reports trailing and forward P/E both at 150.1 on EPS of $2.086, which reconciles neither to the $403 price (193.7x) nor to the $4.31 per-ADR Q2 EPS actual. We treat those fields as unreliable and build valuation from the consensus per-ADR estimates ($16.82 FY2026, $21.61 FY2027), which cross-check cleanly against a bottom-up build.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** (aggregator, NT$m):

| Fiscal year | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | 1,587,415 | +18.5% |
| FY2022 | 2,263,891 | +42.6% |
| FY2023 | 2,161,736 | **−4.5%** |
| FY2024 | 2,894,308 | +33.9% |
| FY2025 | 3,809,054 | +31.6% |
| TTM Jun'26 | 4,440,492 | +30.6% |

FY2023 is the most instructive row. Revenue fell 4.5%, gross margin compressed from 59.56% to 54.36%, EPS fell 14.2%, and free cash flow collapsed 44.7% to a 13.51% FCF margin, with capex essentially flat. TSMC is not immune to cycles; the current 30%+ growth is cyclically amplified, and the FY2023 pattern is the right template for what a demand pause looks like on a now-larger fixed-cost base.

**Margin progression:**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 54.36% | 56.12% | 59.89% | **64.23%** |
| Operating margin | 42.63% | 45.68% | 50.83% | **56.10%** |
| Net margin | 39.37% | 39.99% | 44.50% | **50.37%** |
| FCF margin | 13.51% | 30.06% | 26.32% | **25.75%** |

Gross margin expanded 377bp in FY2025 (from 56.12% to 59.89%) and roughly another 434bp on a trailing basis. Net margin above 50% at this scale is the clearest available evidence of pricing power in a capital-intensive manufacturing business. The divergence between the net margin gain and the FCF margin decline is the analytical crux and is addressed below.

**Quarterly guidance versus actuals** (company-primary):

| | 4Q25 actual | 2Q26 actual | 2Q26 guide | 3Q26 guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (US$B) | 33.73 | **40.20** | 39.0–40.2 | **44.6–45.8** |
| USD/NTD assumption | 31.01 | 31.60 | 31.7 | **32.0** |
| Gross margin | 62.3% | **67.7%** | 65.5–67.5% | **65.0–67.0%** |
| Operating margin | 54.0% | **60.3%** | 56.5–58.5% | **56.0–58.0%** |

4Q25 gross margin of 62.3% beat its own 59.0–61.0% guide by 130bp above the high end; 2Q26 beat again. That systematic conservatism matters for reading the Q3 guide.

**The FX-adjusted margin guide-down.** Q3 revenue is guided up 12.4% sequentially while gross margin is guided down about 170bp to a 66.0% midpoint and operating margin down 330bp to 57.0%. The Q3 FX assumption is 32.0 USD/NTD against 31.60 realized in Q2, a weaker New Taiwan dollar. Because TSMC bills predominantly in USD while a substantial share of its cost base is NT$-denominated, a weaker NTD is normally a tailwind to reported gross margin. Guiding margins down *against* a favorable currency assumption and *with* 12%+ sequential volume growth implies the underlying operational headwind is larger than the headline. The plausible drivers are consistent with management's stated strategy: dilution from ramping higher-cost overseas fabs, initial N2 ramp and yield learning, and a step-up in depreciation from the accelerating capex program.

The alternative explanation must be stated fairly: TSMC beat its gross margin guidance range in both 4Q25 and 2Q26, so habitual conservatism could fully explain the guide-down. We cannot distinguish these from the available evidence. The FX cross-check narrows the ambiguity but does not close it. We also have not measured the actual NT$ share of the cost base, so the magnitude of the FX benefit is assumed rather than quantified.

**Capital allocation:**

| (NT$m) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 1,241,967 | 1,826,177 | 2,274,976 | 2,634,679 |
| Capex | (949,817) | (956,007) | (1,272,411) | (1,491,123) |
| Free cash flow | 292,151 | 870,171 | 1,002,565 | 1,143,557 |

FY2025 capex of NT$1,272,411m is roughly $40.5B at a ~31.4 average rate. The 2026 guide of $60–64B moved up $8B (~15%) in a single quarter. A labeled sensitivity, with inputs shown so it can be rejected: FY2025 revenue of NT$3,809,054m ÷ 31.4 ≈ $121B; "slightly above 40%" growth → ~$170B (cross-checked: 1H26 revenue NT$2,404.48bn ≈ $76B, plus a $45.2B Q3 midpoint = ~$121B nine-month, plus a ~$49B Q4 ≈ $170B). Capex intensity therefore rises to ~36.5% from ~33.5%. Holding the TTM OCF/revenue ratio of 59.3%, 2026 OCF ≈ $101B; less $62B capex → FCF ≈ $39B, an FCF margin near 23%. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast: working capital at 40% revenue growth is more likely a drag than neutral, which makes it optimistic, and it ignores cash-tax timing.

**Balance sheet:**

| (NT$m) | FY2025 | Jun 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash & investments | 3,128,298 | **3,518,013** |
| Total debt | 1,064,583 | **982,447** |
| Net cash | 2,063,715 | **2,535,566** |

Net cash of NT$2,535,566m converts to roughly $80–85B; total debt of NT$982,447m to roughly $31–33B. Debt *declined* while cash rose. Total debt ÷ TTM operating cash flow = 0.37x, and net cash equals roughly 1.0x TTM OCF. On these figures solvency is not a live question. We stress that a formal distress screen was not run; the reader should not substitute the net-cash observation for one.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** Four consecutive beats, by 8.0%, 5.2%, 4.4%, and 10.9% (NT$ per ordinary share basis). The Q2 profit beat of 11.7% dwarfed the ~0.5% revenue beat against the NT$ consensus, so the upside was margin-driven rather than volume-driven. That is higher-quality upside in one sense (operating leverage) and more fragile in another, because it reverses fastest on a demand pause.

## Growth Analysis

Three independent strands point the same direction. Management calls AI demand "extremely robust" (C.C. Wei). Capex was raised mid-year by ~15%, which is a costly signal that only makes sense if order visibility improved. And ASML — TSMC's lithography supplier, therefore up the supply chain and paid by TSMC — raised its own 2026 revenue and capex outlooks in the same window. Simultaneous upward revisions from the equipment supplier and its largest customer are meaningful corroboration that this is order flow rather than announcement inflation.

The consensus build cross-checks the company guidance closely. Bottom-up from Q2 actual plus guided Q3 gives FY2026 net income near NT$2,859bn ≈ $89B; consensus FY2026 EPS of $16.82 × ~5,186m ADR-equivalent units = $87.2B. The two agree within 2%, which raises confidence that the $16.82 and $21.61 figures are not stale.

Estimate revision velocity: +9.1% (current quarter), +13.8% (next quarter), +8.8% (current year), +12.0% (next year) over 90 days, with 30-day moves of +6.7%, +11.3%, +6.3%, +7.3%. The 30-day next-quarter revision of +11.3% is running faster than the 90-day pace, an accelerating pattern. These revisions do not reach the +30% magnitude that constitutes the strongest inflection signal, so we weight them as solid confirmation rather than as a standalone buy trigger. What matters more is that the revisions are rising while the multiple compressed.

Roadmap: N2 is in mass production and is the technology destined for the new Arizona fabs, with A16 and A14 behind it. Node mix — 5nm 33%, 3nm 30% — shows N3 nearly co-equal with N5, with N2 revenue still ahead of the reported base. We gathered no filing-grade evidence on Intel 18A/14A or Samsung SF2 status and make no claim about them.

The non-AI base is the growth question the bulls underweight. SemiAnalysis's Sravan Kundojjala, quoted by CNBC, argues that "the memory boom is now squeezing TSMC's non-AI business… Consumer and price-sensitive end markets took a hit from rising memory prices and tight component supply." The mechanism: TSMC's logic wafers only ship inside finished products that also require DRAM and NAND; a memory price spike breaks OEM bills-of-materials, units get cut, and TSMC's mature-node and consumer demand falls even though TSMC sells no memory and captures none of the memory cycle. This would mean part of the HPC mix shift to 66% reflects a shrinking non-AI denominator rather than pure AI numerator growth. It is one analyst's view, unquantified and not confirmed by the company, and we cannot distinguish the two explanations without segment unit detail. We treat the channel as plausible and unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

Market capitalization: ~5,186m ADR-equivalent units × $403.31 ≈ **$2.09 trillion**. Enterprise value ≈ $2.01 trillion after netting $80–85B net cash.

| FY2026 P/E | **24.0x** | $403.31 / $16.82 consensus |
| FY2027 P/E | **18.7x** | $403.31 / $21.61 consensus |
| FY2026→27 EPS growth | +28.5% | $21.61 / $16.82 |
| PEG (FY2027) | **~0.66** | 18.7x ÷ 28.5 |
| P/S | ~13.9x | Aggregator |
| P/FCF (TTM) | **~55x** | Aggregator |
| FCF yield (2026E) | ~1.9% | $39B sensitivity ÷ $2.09T |
| Dividend yield | ~0.7% | TTM $2.76/ADR; payout ~19-20% |

The trailing P/E is itself FX-sensitive by roughly 1.5 turns (28.1x at a 30.0 rate, 29.6x at 31.6). Any TSM model must fix its convention explicitly; cross-source USD comparisons for this company generate spurious 1–2% beats and misses. The reliable comparison is always the NT$ one.

**The case that the market is mispricing this.** At the June 30 high of $479 with the then-current FY2027 estimate of $19.30, the stock traded at 24.8x next-year earnings. Today at $403 with FY2027 at $21.61, it trades at 18.7x. The multiple has compressed roughly 25% in a month on rising estimates and a raised revenue outlook. Nothing in the Q2 report or the Q3 guide cut forward earnings; consensus went up. The identifiable catalyst set over the next twelve months is concrete: the October Q3 print against a guide that carries demonstrated conservatism (two consecutive quarters of beating its own gross margin range), the N2 revenue ramp, and continued monthly revenue disclosures. Against a 28% expected earnings growth rate and a coverage set whose *lowest* published target is $430, 18.7x is not a demanding multiple for the only merchant supplier of leading-edge logic.

**The case against.** The 55x trailing P/FCF is the honest counterweight to the 18.7x forward P/E, and the gap is capital intensity made visible in valuation terms. If a buyer's horizon is owner cash flow rather than accounting earnings, this security is expensive at a ~1.9% 2026 FCF yield. The reinvestment defence — that capex is a cash outflow building a revenue-generating asset base, and that depressed FCF during a demand-constrained expansion is the correct value-maximizing choice — is the one we find more persuasive, because gross margin is expanding while capacity is added, which is the signature of returns above cost of capital rather than capacity gluts. But it is a judgment, and FY2023 shows what happens when the demand assumption breaks: FCF margin fell to 13.5% with capex flat.

A compressed multiple is not by itself a reason to buy. Here it is paired with rising estimates, a raised revenue outlook, and expanding margins, so the discount is not a value-trap signature. We score valuation 6.8 — constructive, not maximal, with the FCF decoupling as the specific reason we stop short of higher.

Sector context: semiconductors are re-rating on a structural demand shift, with ASML's raised outlook one confirming signal up the supply chain. At 18.7x next-year TSM trades below the mean analyst-target-implied multiple for the coverage set.

## Competitive Landscape

The proof of competitive position is in the pricing. A 67.7% gross margin and 60.3% operating margin in capital-intensive manufacturing are not achievable in a contested market. SemiAnalysis's observation that TSMC has "far more pricing power than they are currently exercising" and is "deliberate rather than opportunistic to keep margins healthy without squeezing customers" reinforces the point: a company facing credible leading-edge alternatives could not choose restraint. If accurate, that describes a latent, un-booked pricing asset, though it is one analyst's assessment rather than company language.

The binding constraint is supply. Management describes demand as extremely robust, raised capex ~15% mid-year, raised the full-year revenue outlook, and is building advanced packaging fabs in Arizona, with the CoWoS bottleneck being the constraint on Nvidia and AMD accelerator delivery timelines, per Tech Times' characterization (TSMC's own language was the more general "advanced packaging fabs").

Trajectory is the relevant question, and here we mark the position down slightly from prior assessments. Node leadership and margin trajectory are both improving. But the mix is becoming more concentrated (66% HPC, 63% in two nodes), and the geographic build-out is deliberately moving marginal capacity into the highest-cost jurisdiction TSMC operates in for policy rather than economic reasons. The competitive moat is intact; the *economics* of exercising it are being diluted at the margin by where the new capacity sits. Score 8.5, down from 8.7 in the prior review and 9.5 two months ago.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Structural margin dilution from policy-driven capacity relocation (high probability, moderate magnitude).** The Arizona commitment is now $265B in total after a $100B increment, and TSMC has announced roughly $200B of US investment since 2025. CNBC's July 22 headline — "Trump's push for American-made AI chips hits TSMC's margins" — was available to us as headline and snippet only; we did not read the body and do not assert any specific management-quantified dilution figure. The evidence we can stand behind is the guidance mechanics: margins guided down against a favorable FX assumption. This compresses terminal margin, not the ability to operate.

**2. Capital intensity outrunning cash generation (moderate probability, high magnitude).** Capex intensity rising to ~36.5% of revenue while revenue grows 40% means the absolute commitment escalates steeply, and depreciation from a $60–64B capex year flows through the P&L in 2027–28. If AI demand normalizes into that depreciation step-up, the operating leverage that produced 60.3% reverses hard. FY2023 is the analogue.

**3. End-market and customer concentration (unquantified).** HPC at 66% of 2026 revenue ties two-thirds of the business to one capital-spending cycle. We have no verified customer-concentration percentages. Whether AI capex ROI eventually fails to match confirmed spend bears directly here, and argues for scaled rather than full entry.

**4. Memory-driven demand destruction in the non-AI base (see Growth Analysis).** Unquantified, externally sourced, and a genuine analytical gap.

**5. Taiwan concentration (low probability, extreme magnitude).** The overwhelming majority of leading-edge capacity sits on an island subject to cross-Strait tension and natural-hazard exposure; Typhoon Bavi delayed the June revenue release by two days, which evidences the operational-disruption channel if not a production event. We could not read TSMC's own characterization of Taiwan risk, export-control exposure, or China restrictions and will not paraphrase language we have not seen. This is a discontinuous scenario outside our bear-case price range.

**6. FX translation is first-order.** The identical NT$1.27tn quarter was published as $40.20B and $39.45B; the identical NT$706.56bn profit as $21.99B and $22.36B. USD-based investors bear a translation exposure worth more than a full multiple turn.

**7. Audit-quality gap.** No 20-F content read; no distress screen computed; no governance, compensation, or insider data available. The reader should discount our balance-sheet and risk-factor confidence accordingly.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 58.5% against 40.6% one-year historical volatility looks rich, and the term structure is in backwardation (58.5% near versus 45.3% at three and six months). The 58.5% print belongs to a one-day expiry with an implied move of only 0.4%, which is same-day pinning mechanics rather than a stress signal. The informative tenors are the 1-month at 48.8% (7.5% implied move) and the 3-month at 45.3% (12.7% implied move), elevated versus realized but consistent with a semiconductor name in an August payrolls-plus-September-FOMC window.

The headline put/call skew of 257% (elevated put demand) is likewise concentrated in the one-week tenor at 215%; at one month skew is 0.8%, at three months −1.0%, and at six months −4.2%. We do not infer directional meaning from those signs without a stated convention, but the collapse of the skew magnitude beyond one week means there is no sustained protection bid in the structure. Put/call open-interest ratio of 1.08 and volume ratio of 1.06 are close to balanced. Short interest is 0.7% of float with 2.3 days to cover, showing no crowding and no squeeze dynamic in either direction. Unusual activity is two-sided and small: 450 contracts of the August 7 $442 call (3.5x open interest) and 574 of the August 7 $405 put (3.4x).

Net read: the options market is pricing event risk around the near-term calendar, not a regime change, and does not contradict the fundamental thesis. The rich 1-month vol argues for scaling in rather than buying in one clip, or for selling puts to establish the position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($560, +38.8%).** AI demand visibility holds through 2027, N2 ramps on schedule with yields tracking N3's learning curve, and TSMC's demonstrated guidance conservatism produces further beats against the 65.0–67.0% Q3 gross margin guide. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the current $21.61 consensus, which itself has risen 12% in 90 days and would likely be revised higher. Advanced packaging revenue — already growing 2.95x over four years versus 2.33x for wafers — becomes a second margin engine as CoWoS scarcity persists. At 26x FY2027 EPS the stock reaches $560, below the mean analyst target of $540 plus a further year of estimate revision. The pricing-power argument (that TSMC is deliberately under-charging) provides optional upside not in any model.

**Base case ($475, +17.8%).** Revenue grows slightly above 40% in 2026 and decelerates to a high-teens-to-twenties pace in 2027 as the comparison base normalizes. Gross margin settles in the mid-60s as overseas dilution and depreciation offset mix and pricing, roughly in line with the Q3 guide rather than beating it materially. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $21.61. At 22x — a discount to the current trailing multiple, reflecting the FCF decoupling and the policy-driven margin dilution — the stock is worth $475. Free cash flow stays near $39–45B, so the equity is carried by earnings growth rather than cash returns.

**Bear case ($320, −20.7%).** AI capex enters a digestion phase in late 2026 or 2027. Hyperscaler capex growth decelerates, order lead times shorten, and utilization on the newest nodes slips just as depreciation from a $62B capex year lands. Memory-driven demand destruction deepens in the non-AI base. FY2027 estimates are cut roughly 10% toward $19.50 and the multiple compresses to about 16.5x, giving $320, approximately where the stock traded in March 2026. This is not a solvency scenario; net cash of $80–85B and 0.37x debt-to-OCF make it a valuation and growth-rate scenario. A Taiwan escalation is a separate, discontinuous outcome that sits outside this range.

**Why we stay with BUY after four consecutive BUY calls.** The bar for repeating a recommendation should rise with repetition, and the specific new evidence that clears it is the change in entry price relative to estimates. The forward multiple has compressed from 24.8x to 18.7x next-year earnings in a month, driven by a 15.8% price decline into a 12.0% ninety-day upward revision to FY2027 consensus. No forward estimate was cut. The offsetting new negative — the FX-adjusted margin guide-down and the capex raise — is real and is why the overall score moves down to 7.3 from 7.4 and why valuation stays at 6.8 rather than moving higher. Risk/reward at $403 is better than at any point in the prior four reviews.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the mid-October Q3 print, the January Q4 print with FY2027 capex guidance, and monthly revenue disclosures in between.

**Base case target: $475 (+17.8%).** Math: 22x FY2027 consensus EPS of $21.61 per ADR = $475. The 22x multiple is a discount to the ~24x current-year and ~28x trailing multiples, applied to reflect capital intensity at ~36.5% of revenue and policy-driven margin dilution.

**Bull case target: $560 (+38.8%).** 26x FY2027 $21.61, or 22x on upward-revised FY2027 EPS of ~$25.

**Bear case target: $320 (−20.7%).** 16.5x on FY2027 EPS cut ~10% to $19.50.

**Position construction:** scale in. One-month implied volatility of 48.8% versus 40.6% realized is rich, which argues against a single-clip entry. Add on any move toward $360-375, which would put the stock near 17x FY2027 with estimates unchanged.

**Thesis invalidation triggers** (each specific and checkable):
1. **Q3 gross margin prints below the guided 65.0% floor**, or Q4 gross margin is guided below 63%. That would confirm the FX-adjusted guide-down reflects a genuine margin inflection rather than habitual conservatism, and would break the operating-leverage leg of the thesis.
2. **FY2027 consensus EPS is revised down more than 5% from the current $21.61**, or the 30-day revision turns negative for two consecutive months. Rising estimates are load-bearing for the valuation argument.
3. **FY2027 capex is guided above $70B without a commensurate revenue outlook increase.** That would push capital intensity above 38% and confirm reinvestment is running ahead of demand rather than behind it.
4. **Monthly revenue growth decelerates below 15% year over year** for two consecutive months, which would signal the AI order book is normalizing.

**Reassessment date:** mid-October 2026 (Q3 results and Q4 guidance).

## Conclusion

**BUY.** TSMC is compounding revenue above 30%, net margin above 50%, and gross margin at 64% trailing, holds $80–85B of net cash with debt declining, has beaten consensus four consecutive quarters and its own gross margin guidance in each of the last two, and trades at 18.7x next-year consensus earnings growing about 28%. The lowest published analyst target is 6.4% above spot. The stock is 15.8% below its June high while forward estimates have risen, which is improving risk/reward, not thesis deterioration.

Three things keep this from being a maximum-conviction position. First, free cash flow is decoupling from earnings: at ~55x trailing P/FCF and a ~1.9% 2026 FCF yield, the equity requires the reinvestment thesis to be right, and the FY2023 precedent shows how quickly the cash-flow profile deteriorates when demand pauses. Second, margins are guided down in Q3 against a currency assumption that should have helped, which means the underlying operational headwind from overseas fabs, the N2 ramp, and depreciation is larger than the −170bp headline; we cannot fully separate that from management's documented conservatism. Third, and unquantifiable, the leading-edge capacity concentration in Taiwan is a discontinuous risk we cannot model and did not verify from the company's own filings.

Score 7.3, down marginally from 7.4, with the decline located in competitive position (mix concentration, policy-driven cost relocation) and offset by an improved entry price. Own it, scale in, and reassess at the October print.
