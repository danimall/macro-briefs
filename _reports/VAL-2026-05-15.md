## Executive Summary

Valaris Ltd is one of the world's largest offshore contract drilling companies, operating 45 rigs (including 12 premium 7th-generation drillships) across six continents. The company's investment thesis has shifted fundamentally since February 9, 2026, when it entered into a Business Combination Agreement with Transocean Ltd at an all-stock exchange ratio of 15.235 RIG shares per VAL share. At Transocean's recent trading price (~$6.50), this implies ~$99/share for Valaris — roughly in line with the current price of $103.

The stock's 161% appreciation over the past year is overwhelmingly merger-driven (from ~$39.50 a year ago to $103 today). Analyst consensus is uniformly bearish relative to current levels — 7 HOLDs, 1 SELL, 1 STRONG SELL with a mean target of $73 and a high target of $99, both below the current trading price. Options markets confirm extreme uncertainty: near-term IV of 114.6% vs. 56% historical vol, with severe backwardation pricing imminent binary risk.

On standalone fundamentals, Valaris has a compelling medium-term position: $4.93B backlog, rising day rates ($436K average for floaters, a company record), structural supply constraints (global floater fleet down 45% from peak with zero newbuilds), and no debt maturities until 2030. But Q1 2026 revealed the business's inherent lumpiness — revenue fell 25% YoY on temporary contract gaps, producing a $(0.24) EPS loss. The recovery depends on four drillships returning to work in H2 2026.

## Company Overview

Valaris emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in April 2021 and has since rebuilt into a focused, premium offshore drilling operator. The fleet consists of 13 drillships (12 seventh-generation), 1 semisubmersible, and 31 jackups. Additionally, the company holds a 50% interest in ARO, a joint venture with Saudi Aramco operating 9 jackups under long-term contracts.

The business model is straightforward: Valaris contracts rigs to oil and gas companies on a day-rate basis. Revenue is driven by three variables: rig count, utilization, and day rates. Current industry dynamics favor day rate expansion — global drillship utilization is ~92%, jackup utilization ~88%, and no new ultra-deepwater rigs are being built given $400M+ construction costs and limited shipyard capacity.

Top customers include Petrobras (17% of Q1 2026 revenue), Azule Energy (14%), and BP (11%). Geographic diversification spans the Gulf of America, Brazil, North Sea, Middle East, and West Africa.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
- 2022: $1.60B → 2023: $1.78B → 2024: $2.36B → 2025: $2.37B
- Q1 2026: $465M (-25% YoY, driven by floater utilization dropping to 45% from 81%)

The Q1 2026 decline was anticipated and reflects contract timing gaps — four drillships were between contracts. Average floater day rates actually rose to $436K from $384K YoY, confirming pricing power even during the utilization trough.

**Profitability:**
- Full-year 2025 operating income: $477M (20% margin)
- 2025 net income of $983M is misleading — $523M came from a non-cash deferred tax asset valuation allowance release
- Adjusted pre-tax operating earnings: ~$455M
- Q1 2026: Operating income collapsed to $20M on lower utilization

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**
- 2025 operating cash flow: $546M; CapEx: $344M; FCF: $203M
- Q1 2026: OCF $75M vs. CapEx $101M (negative FCF, typical for transitional quarters)
- Cash: $578M, Revolver: $375M undrawn, Long-term debt: $1.1B (8.375% notes due 2030)
- Net debt/equity: ~0.16 — conservative leverage
- $1.36B deferred tax asset (from pre-bankruptcy NOLs)

The Altman Z-Score of 2.19 (grey zone) is largely an artifact of the capital-intensive business model. With no maturities until 2030 and ~$950M of liquidity, near-term solvency risk is negligible.

**Capital Allocation:**
- $425M in share repurchases since 2023 (now suspended pending merger)
- 2026 CapEx guidance: $425-475M (maintenance and upgrades)
- No dividend; no buyback capacity until merger resolves

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is constrained by two factors: the merger's completion timeline (expected by February 2027) and the cyclical nature of offshore drilling.

**Near-term (H2 2026):** Revenue should recover meaningfully as four drillships return to work. Consensus estimates $3.44 EPS for FY2026 (with $0.33 current quarter, $1.34 next quarter), implying substantial H2 acceleration.

**Medium-term standalone thesis (if merger fails):**
- Backlog of $4.93B provides 2+ years of revenue visibility
- Day rates rising (with some fixtures above $500K)
- Global floater supply structurally constrained — 29% of jackup fleet is 40+ years old
- Deepwater project economics remain attractive at sub-$50 breakevens

**Headwinds:**
- Oil market currently in oversupply (per management's own 10-K language)
- Customer spending decisions lag oil price moves by 6-12 months
- Tariff/trade tensions adding operational cost pressure
- Estimate revisions for FY2026 are down 4.2% over 90 days, though current quarter estimates rose 54%

The growth profile is fundamentally lumpy and commodity-dependent. Revenue growth from $1.6B (2022) to $2.37B (2025) was impressive, but the Q1 2026 trough illustrates how quickly utilization gaps compress earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $103, Valaris trades at:
- 7.2x trailing EPS ($13.86, heavily inflated by tax benefits)
- ~30x FY2026 consensus EPS ($3.44)
- ~14.5x FY2027 consensus EPS ($7.09)
- Approximately at merger-implied value (15.235 × ~$6.50 RIG = ~$99)

**Peer comparison:**
- Noble Corp (NE): 35.8x P/E, 3.9% dividend yield
- Patterson-UTI (PTEN): Negative earnings, 3.4% yield
- Helmerich & Payne (HP): Negative earnings, 2.6% yield

VAL appears optically cheap on trailing metrics, but trailing EPS is distorted by the $523M non-cash tax benefit. On FY2027 forward estimates ($7.09), the stock trades at 14.5x — reasonable for an offshore driller with improving fundamentals but not dramatically cheap given cyclical risk and the Q1 miss.

The stock has already re-rated from ~$50 to $103 on the merger announcement. Analyst consensus targets range from $47 to $99 — all below the current price. The stock is trading above every analyst's price target, reflecting pure merger premium.

If the deal breaks, a return to $65-80 (standalone value reflecting the fundamental recovery but discounting merger premium) represents 22-37% downside. If the deal closes at current RIG prices, there is approximately 0-4% downside. This asymmetric risk profile favors caution at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Valaris holds a strong competitive position within offshore drilling:

**Structural advantages:**
- 12 seventh-generation drillships represent the highest-specification fleet segment
- Geographic diversification reduces regional concentration risk
- ARO JV provides locked-in Saudi Arabia exposure
- Post-bankruptcy balance sheet with no near-term debt issues

**Industry structure favors incumbents:**
- 45%+ fleet attrition since 2014 peak with zero newbuilds planned
- Remaining rigs gaining pricing power as utilization rises above 90%
- Capital discipline across the industry (no speculative builds)

**Competitive risks:**
- The Transocean merger, if completed, will create the dominant player — but if blocked, it signals regulatory skepticism about industry concentration
- Customer consolidation (NOC mega-projects) gives counterparties bargaining power on multi-year contracts
- ESG/energy transition pressure on upstream capex allocation decisions

Valaris's competitive moat is medium-duration: it depends on supply discipline continuing and customers maintaining deepwater spending plans. Both conditions appear satisfied for the next 2-3 years but are ultimately oil-price dependent.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks ranked by impact:**

1. **Merger failure (DOJ block):** A DOJ antitrust inquiry is underway. The combined entity would control 37+ floaters in a market of ~150 total — significant market share. If blocked, VAL likely trades to $65-80 (standalone value), representing 22-37% downside from current levels. The $173M termination fee applies only if Valaris causes the break, providing no downside protection for shareholders in a regulatory block scenario.

2. **Cyclical downturn/oil price decline:** Management explicitly acknowledges "current oversupply" in the 10-K. A sustained oil price below $70 would pressure E&P capex budgets, eventually flowing to lower drilling demand and day rates within 12-18 months.

3. **Utilization lumpiness:** Q1 2026 demonstrated that 4 rigs going idle simultaneously can cut revenue 25% and swing earnings to a loss. Contract gaps are inherent to the business and create volatile quarterly earnings.

4. **ARO contingent obligation:** Up to $1.1B of potential funding obligation for ARO's 20-rig newbuild program. The JV has never paid a distribution, and $401M in notes receivable are subordinated to ARO's third-party debt. This is trapped capital with uncertain recovery.

5. **Geopolitical (Hormuz/Middle East):** $7.5M in incremental costs in Q1 2026 alone from war-risk insurance. Several jackups operate in the Persian Gulf. Escalation risk is directly relevant to operations and insurance costs.

### Options Market Signal

This structure prices an imminent binary event — almost certainly the merger's regulatory outcome. The P/C OI ratio of 0.56 suggests moderate bullish lean (more call OI than put OI), but the IV level itself indicates the market expects a large move. The ±7.9% implied move on the nearest expiry is consistent with a potential DOJ decision window.

Short interest at 13.6% of float with 7.3 days to cover confirms institutional bearish positioning. This is elevated but not at squeeze-risk levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-120):**
- Transocean merger closes as planned, potentially at improved terms or with RIG share price appreciation
- If deal breaks, standalone Valaris benefits from H2 2026 utilization recovery, rising day rates, and oil above $100
- $4.93B backlog provides revenue certainty through 2028
- Supply constraints enable continued day rate expansion above $450K for premium drillships
- NOL-driven tax shield ($1.36B DTA) provides years of reduced cash tax payments

**Bear Case ($65-80):**
- DOJ blocks the merger on antitrust grounds — stock immediately reprices to standalone value
- Oil prices decline on recession/demand destruction, pressuring E&P budgets with 12-month lag
- H2 2026 rig deployments face further delays, extending the utilization trough
- ARO funding obligation materializes, straining liquidity
- Industry oversupply re-emerges if OPEC+ unwinds cuts

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $75-85 on merger uncertainty without fundamental deterioration (creating standalone value entry point)
- DOJ unconditionally approves merger AND RIG appreciates above $7.50 (implying VAL value of $114+)
- Merger breaks AND oil remains above $100 with standalone re-rating to 12-15x FY2027 EPS ($85-106 range with potential upside)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- DOJ signals intention to block (2nd request with structural remedies demanded)
- Oil drops below $80 sustained for 4+ weeks
- Q2 2026 reveals further rig deployment delays beyond current guidance

**Reassessment timeline:** DOJ decision expected H2 2026 (potentially Q3). Next earnings report will clarify H2 2026 utilization recovery trajectory.

## Conclusion

Valaris is a high-quality offshore driller with structural competitive advantages, but at $103 the stock is priced entirely for merger completion. Every Wall Street analyst target ($47-99) sits below the current price. The investment case is binary: if the Transocean deal closes, shareholders receive approximately current value in RIG shares; if it breaks, a 22-37% drawdown is the base case.

The prior score trajectory (declining from 6.6 to 5.8) reflected deteriorating valuation attractiveness as the stock appreciated on merger speculation — that dynamic has continued with the stock now 3% above the 52-week high and above all analyst targets. The fundamental business is sound (growing backlog, rising day rates, constrained supply), but the risk/reward at current levels is unattractive for new capital. The appropriate stance is HOLD for existing positions with the understanding that this is primarily a merger-arbitrage situation, not a fundamental equity investment.
