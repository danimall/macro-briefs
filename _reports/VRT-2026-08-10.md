## Executive Summary

Vertiv is one of the few pure-play beneficiaries of AI data center construction where the demand is visible in billed revenue rather than in guidance language. First-half 2026 net sales were $5,923.8M, up 26.7% reported and 19.9% organic; gross margin expanded 372 basis points year over year in Q2 to 37.7%; adjusted operating margin reached 22.6% in Q2 against 18.5% a year earlier; and incremental adjusted operating margin on the 1H revenue increase was 37.2%. The company earned its way from roughly $1.1B of net debt at year-end 2025 to a $170.8M net cash position by June 30, refinanced a $2,076.1M secured term loan into four tranches of unsecured notes maturing 2036 through 2066, and replaced an $800M asset-based revolver with a $2.5B unsecured facility carrying a single 4.00x net-leverage covenant. Four consecutive quarterly EPS beats (5.7%, 16.0%, 5.1%, 24.3%) and consensus estimates rising 3.7% for the current year and 5.7% for next year over 90 days confirm operating momentum.

The problem is the shape of the guidance rather than its direction. Organic growth decelerated from 22.6% in Q1 to 17.8% in Q2, Q2 revenue came in roughly $110M below sell-side expectations, and yet full-year revenue guidance was raised to $13.8–14.2B. The arithmetic that follows is unavoidable: 2H26 implied revenue of $8,076M against 2H25 of $5,556M is +45.4% reported, requiring two consecutive sequential steps of roughly 15% (Q2 $3,274M → Q3 guide midpoint $3,750M → Q4 implied $4,326M) and a Q3 organic growth rate of 34–36% against Q2's 17.8%. Management did not publish a Q2 orders or backlog figure — the $15.0B backlog and 2.9x book-to-bill widely quoted in secondary coverage are December 31, 2025 metrics — so the single datapoint that would underwrite that acceleration is not in the public record. Meanwhile, 1H26 operating cash flow of $1,866.6M was almost entirely explained by a $1,822.7M deferred-revenue inflow, and guided 2H26 adjusted free cash flow of $822–1,022M sits roughly 31% below derived 2H25 levels even as 2H revenue is guided ~45% higher.

At 40.8x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $6.70 and 30.1x FY2027 consensus of $9.10 — a PEG of roughly 0.85 on 35% forward earnings growth — Vertiv is not expensive relative to what it is delivering, and it trades below the trailing multiples of peers growing high single digits (peer median P/E 41.3x). But at a 2.37% guided free cash flow yield with a 28% drawdown already behind it and a 22.6% bounce off the July 29 low, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: base case $310 (+13%), bull $400 (+46%), bear $175 (−36%). Own it; wait for the October print or a materially better price before adding.

## Company Overview

Vertiv designs, manufactures and services the physical infrastructure inside data centers: uninterruptible power supplies, switchgear, busway, power distribution units and integrated modular power skids on the electrical side; liquid cooling, coolant distribution units, chillers, air handling and heat rejection on the thermal side; plus prefabricated modular data centers, monitoring software and a lifecycle services organization covering commissioning, maintenance, spares and remote monitoring. It operates in more than 130 countries from Westerville, Ohio, and reports three geographic segments — Americas, APAC and EMEA — plus Corporate.

Two structural features matter. First, Vertiv sells power and thermal as one specification. As rack densities rise with each accelerator generation, direct-to-chip liquid cooling stops being optional and the cooling loop becomes physically entangled with the power train; a vendor that can specify, build, install and then service both has an advantage over point-product suppliers. Second, services and spares were $667.9M of Q2 net sales, or 20.4% of the total — recurring, higher-margin revenue anchored to an installed base and a global field organization that a new entrant cannot replicate quickly.

The mix is heavily Americas-weighted: AMER was $3,885.2M of 1H26's $5,923.8M (65.6%) at a 27.3% adjusted operating margin, versus 13.2% in APAC and 22.1% in EMEA. Vertiv is a supplier to data center owners, colocation providers, hyperscalers and specialized AI cloud operators. It does not own, operate or monetize data centers or the workloads inside them — a distinction worth holding onto when reading headlines describing "partnerships" with developers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue was $10,229.9M, up 27.7% from $8,012.0M. 1H26 was $5,923.8M (+26.7% reported, +19.9% organic), split $2,649.5M in Q1 (+30.1% reported, +22.6% organic) and $3,274.3M in Q2 (+24.1% reported, +17.8% organic).

| Metric | Q2 25 | Q2 26 | 1H25 | 1H26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 34.0% | 37.7% | 33.9% | 37.7% |
| GAAP operating margin | 16.8% | 19.5% | 15.7% | 18.2% |
| Adjusted operating margin | 18.5% | 22.6% | 17.7% | 21.8% |
| SG&A / sales | 15.0% | 15.1% | 15.9% | 16.1% |

The composition of the margin gain deserves attention: essentially all of it came from gross margin, while the SG&A ratio actually rose about 11 basis points in Q2 and 20 basis points in 1H. Management attributes the gross-margin improvement to productivity and favorable price-cost inclusive of tariff recovery. That is a higher-quality source than cost cutting, but it is also partly a scarcity margin — Vertiv is pricing into a market where capacity is the constraint — and scarcity margins compress when capacity arrives.

FY2026 guidance implies adjusted operating margin of 23.3–24.3% against FY2025's 20.4%, roughly +340 basis points in a single year, with Q3 guided at 24.0–25.0%. That is a steep ramp to underwrite on top of a revenue ramp.

**Earnings.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $3.41 and adjusted approximately $4.19. FY2026 guidance is GAAP $5.82–5.92 and adjusted $6.65–6.75. The GAAP-to-adjusted wedge is widening and largely structural: FY2026 adds back $306.8M of intangible amortization ($0.78/share, up from roughly $190M in FY2025) plus $62.0M of contingent-consideration remeasurement ($0.16/share). Investors should expect a $0.90+ per-share gap to persist and grow with further acquisitions. The supplied trailing P/E field (61.65x paired with EPS of $3.41) is internally inconsistent — $273.46/$3.41 is 80x — so all valuation work here uses guidance and consensus rather than that field.

**Cash flow — the most important nuance.** Headline 1H26 operating cash flow was $1,866.6M versus $626.2M in 1H25, and adjusted free cash flow $1,578.1M versus $541.5M. But the deferred-revenue line contributed $1,822.7M of that, with the balance sheet figure doubling from $1,814.7M to $3,633.7M in six months. Excluding that single line, all other 1H26 operating sources and uses netted to roughly $44M; for Q2 in isolation, operating cash flow less the deferred-revenue inflow was approximately negative $72M. Inventories rose 73.2% to $2,522.7M, with raw materials up roughly 85%.

Customer prepayment on large multi-phase projects is economically a strength — interest-free financing plus contractual commitment. But the rate of change cannot compound, and management's own guidance says so: FY2026 adjusted FCF of $2,400–2,600M less 1H26 actual of $1,578.1M implies 2H26 of $822–1,022M, against derived 2H25 adjusted FCF of roughly $1,346M. Guided 2H cash flow is therefore about 31% below the prior year's second half while 2H revenue is guided ~45% higher. Some of that reflects the capex step-up (roughly $274M in 2H on the same derivation) and higher cash taxes, and the 2H25 comparison is derived by subtraction rather than disclosed. The direction is nonetheless robust: annualizing Q2's $925.3M of adjusted FCF to ~$3.7B would overstate the guided full year by roughly 48%.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and short-term investments of $3,110.6M against total debt of $2,939.8M leaves $170.8M of net cash; liquidity was $5.6B including the undrawn $2.5B revolver. Debt/equity is 0.62x. The rise in liabilities-to-equity from 2.10x to 2.34x is driven by deferred revenue and payables, not borrowings. The March 3, 2026 refinancing — $2,100M of unsecured notes at 4.85–5.95% across 2036/2046/2056/2066 maturities, full repayment of the secured term loan, and an unsecured revolver with a lone 4.00x net-leverage covenant — is the capital-structure signature of investment-grade market access. Net interest expense fell to $13.0M in 1H26 from $46.6M; guided FY2026 net interest is $49.5M. Vertiv correctly excludes the $22.9M swap-reclassification benefit from adjusted EPS.

One screening caveat: a standardized Altman Z-Score of 2.31 for Vertiv is a calculation artifact from substituting book equity into the market-value term. Recomputed with $105.3B of market capitalization, the score is approximately 9.3, deep in the safe zone. With net cash and a single leverage covenant against zero net debt, there is no solvency or covenant issue. Against that, 86% of book equity ($2,283.3M goodwill plus $1,800.8M net intangibles against $4,757.6M of equity) sits in acquired intangible assets — a real impairment exposure if AI capex pauses, though not a liquidity risk.

**Capital allocation.** Capex is inflecting: $285.9M in 1H26 versus $81.5M in 1H25, guided to roughly 4.0% of revenue (~$560M) for the year, with US net PP&E up from $340.7M to $492.9M in six months. M&A has become a core strategy, with roughly $1.67B of consideration in about eleven months, headlined by PurgeRite at $1,152.7M gross plus up to $250M of earn-out. The dividend is immaterial at ~0.09% yield. Share repurchases stopped in 1H26 despite roughly $2.4B of remaining authorization and a share price 28% below the May high. Management frames capacity and acquisitions as the higher-return use; preserving the newly achieved net-cash position is an equally plausible explanation. I would not read a valuation view into the absence of buybacks, but the combination of halted repurchases and zero open-market insider buying at a 28% drawdown is not a positive alignment signal either.

## Growth Analysis

The forward path is exceptional on any absolute measure and unusually back-loaded on a quarterly basis.

| Period | Revenue | Reported growth | Organic growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 26 | $2,649.5M | +30.1% | +22.6% |
| Q2 26 | $3,274.3M | +24.1% | +17.8% |
| Q3 26 guide | $3,650–3,850M | — | +34–36% |
| FY26 guide | $13,800–14,200M | +36.8% at mid | +30–32% |
| 2H26 implied | $8,076M | **+45.4%** | — |

Consensus has been moving with management: current-quarter estimates +2.1% over 30 days, next quarter +7.7%, current year +3.7% to $6.72, next year +3.3% over 30 days and +5.7% over 90 days to $9.10. Twenty-eight analysts cover FY2027. The revision trend is positive and broad, though the magnitudes are in the mid-single digits rather than the +30% velocity that historically marks a violent inflection. The earnings beat record (four for four, average surprise 12.8%) supports confidence that $6.70 is achievable and that $9.10 is not a stretch target.

Three pieces of evidence underwrite the 2H acceleration independent of the undisclosed backlog. First, deferred revenue of $3,633.7M is cash already collected against work to be delivered; that is roughly 45% of the implied 2H revenue figure sitting on the balance sheet in advance. Second, management attributed the Q2 shortfall to "temporary supply chain congestion and multi-phased project execution," which mechanically shifts revenue into 2H rather than eliminating it. Third, the capex ramp to ~4% of revenue and the 73% inventory build are money spent in anticipation of volume; management is not hedging with words.

Three pieces cut the other way. Organic growth is decelerating in the reported numbers even as guidance accelerates. The July 29 release omitted any orders or backlog figure, in contrast to the February release that headlined 252% organic order growth and a $15.0B backlog — an asymmetry in disclosure practice in the quarter revenue missed. And the $15.0B backlog itself is defined in the 10-K as majority-firm, expected to ship in 12–18 months, and explicitly subject to cancellation or rescheduling, with acquisitions having contributed to its 108% growth.

On segment geography, the EMEA headline is misleading. EMEA 1H26 net sales fell 8.4% reported and 14.8% organic to $805.0M, but intersegment sales rose from $264.4M to $400.4M; including intersegment, EMEA total sales grew roughly 5.4%, and EMEA adjusted operating margin improved to 22.1% from 20.8%. APAC shows the pattern more sharply, with intersegment sales up from $94.2M to $332.5M. European and Asian plants are supplying Americas AI demand. European end-market demand is genuinely soft, but treating the −14.8% organic print as franchise deterioration misreads the structure. These intersegment derivations come from the segment note rather than disclosed metrics, and transfer pricing can distort them.

Services deserve a caution. 1H26 organic growth was +22.0% for products versus +11.7% for services and spares; in the Americas, of the $234.7M services increase, $150.4M came from acquisitions and only $80.1M was organic. The services acceleration is substantially bought. Product installations lead service attachment by quarters, so a product-led boom should produce a services tail — but for now the revenue mix is becoming more equipment-weighted and therefore more cyclical, at the point in the cycle where investors are paying a services-quality multiple. Note also that a mix shift toward services via acquisition says nothing about customer concentration, which is not disclosed anywhere in the reviewed material.

## Competitive Landscape

Vertiv occupies a structurally advantaged niche: power and thermal are the binding physical constraints on data center throughput, and they cannot be engineered away by architectural cleverness the way compute bottlenecks sometimes can. Successive accelerator generations force wholesale redesign of power delivery and heat rejection, and operators making 800VDC and liquid-cooling architecture choices are making decisions with 10–15-year consequences — which creates lock-in for whoever specifies the architecture.

What is visible in the filings as genuine protection: the field-service installed base at 20.4% of Q2 sales, which competitors cannot buy quickly; manufacturing capacity itself, which is the scarce asset in a supply-constrained market and which Vertiv is expanding at ~4% of revenue; and the multi-region footprint demonstrated by the EMEA/APAC intersegment export data, which allows Vertiv to redirect plants toward wherever demand and tariff economics are most favorable.

What is not visible: any proprietary technology so differentiated that a well-capitalized competitor with adequate capacity could not compete on price. The competitive set — Schneider Electric, Eaton, ABB, Legrand, nVent, Munters, Delta, Huawei, Mitsubishi Electric, Stulz — is consolidating toward Vertiv's power-plus-thermal bundle, which validates the strategy and threatens it simultaneously. Secondary reporting describes Eaton assembling a comparable bundle via Boyd Thermal; I could not verify that against Eaton's filings and treat it as unconfirmed. nVent has been positioned by commentators as a liquid-cooling alternative, which is narrative rather than share data but indicates capital flowing into the same niche.

The trajectory is favorable: Vertiv is growing revenue several times faster than its electrical-equipment peers and expanding gross margin 372 basis points while doing it, which is the signature of share gain into a supply-constrained market. The moderate score reflects that a meaningful part of the 37.7% gross margin is scarcity rent, and scarcity rents mean-revert as capacity arrives — including Vertiv's own.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Backlog conversion and order cancellability — the load-bearing risk.** The $15.0B backlog is defined as estimated combined order backlog, majority firm, expected to ship within 12–18 months, and explicitly subject to cancellation or rescheduling. Guidance requires +45% reported growth in 2H26; any slippage compounds. The absence of a Q2 orders or backlog disclosure means investors cannot check bookings against that requirement until the October print. Management may well have provided the figures on the call, and the Q4 comparison (252% organic order growth) was exceptionally easy; this is a verification gap, not evidence that backlog declined.

**2. Customer credit, specifically specialized AI cloud operators.** The FY2025 10-K flags the credit profile of "neocloud" providers as a distinct risk. Vertiv carries $3,750.3M of receivables and $2,522.7M of inventory staged against these orders. A levered AI cloud operator re-phasing or failing leaves Vertiv holding both. Customer concentration is not disclosed in the material reviewed — whether any customer exceeds 10% of revenue is unresolved, which is a material gap given the receivable balance.

**3. Buyer power and fixed-price exposure.** Named risk factors include "less leverage with large customer contract terms" and long-term fixed-price contract risk. Hyperscalers and large colocation buyers negotiate hard on price, liquidated damages and schedule, and Vertiv is taking multi-year fixed-price exposure into volatile input and tariff costs.

**4. Execution constraints, already manifesting.** The Q2 shortfall was attributed to supply-chain congestion and multi-phase project complexity — an admission that Vertiv's own delivery machine is straining, not a demand excuse. The capex ramp and inventory build are the response, but they take quarters to land.

**5. Goodwill and intangibles at 86% of book equity.** The 2026 deals booked $260.1M of goodwill on $331.8M of consideration; PurgeRite booked $589.3M of goodwill and $445.2M of intangibles on $1,152.7M. An AI capex pause would put impairment testing under pressure and would land on a GAAP earnings base already carrying $306.8M of annual amortization.

**6. Permitting, zoning and local moratoria.** A newer risk factor in the FY2025 10-K: local zoning decisions, community opposition and temporary or permanent moratoria affecting data center development. This is an end-market throughput constraint entirely outside Vertiv's control.

**7. Contingent consideration and adjusted-earnings presentation.** The PurgeRite earn-out liability was marked from $139.2M to $206.1M against a $250M maximum, producing $62.0M of 1H expense that Vertiv adds back. Marking toward the maximum most naturally reads as PurgeRite tracking ahead of plan — a useful proprietary signal on liquid-cooling deployment velocity — though earn-out remeasurement also moves with discount rates and the unwinding of time value. The add-back is symmetric with Vertiv's treatment of a restructuring credit, but it means part of the cost of acquired growth sits outside adjusted EPS.

**8. Tariffs, cutting both ways.** Following the February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs, Vertiv has submitted refund claims but recognized no gain, treating it as a gain contingency. That is unquantified upside not in guidance. Separately, favorable price-cost inclusive of tariff recovery is a stated Q2 margin driver; that recovery is not guaranteed to persist.

**9. Governance and disclosure items.** A 2022 securities class action remains pending with the motion to dismiss undecided as of the Q2 10-Q and no loss accrual — immaterial against a $105B market cap, though the duration is unusual. Forward-looking-statement boilerplate still references failure to remediate material weaknesses; I could not confirm whether that is legacy language or reflects a current deficiency. A CFO transition (Item 5.02 8-K dated October 14, 2025) has unverified terms.

**10. Macro.** AI-purpose debt supply is expanding, and financing terms for AI credit may reprice at issuance. Vertiv itself has no refinancing need, but its customer base is increasingly debt-funded, which is the transmission channel from higher long rates to Vertiv's order book. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $400 (+46%)

Vertiv is the purest listed expression of the physical constraint on AI compute, and the constraint is confirmed rather than speculative by Vertiv's own numbers — 37.7% gross margin, 37.2% incremental operating margin, a 73% inventory build and a 3.5x capex step-up that management does not make unless it sees the volume. Deferred revenue of $3,633.7M is cash in hand against a portion of implied second-half revenue, which underwrites part of the ramp independent of any backlog disclosure. Four straight beats averaging 12.8% suggest $6.70 is conservative; the PurgeRite earn-out being marked toward its maximum says liquid-cooling services are deploying faster than underwritten. At 30x FY2027 consensus with estimates up 5.7% in 90 days, a PEG of 0.84, and a balance sheet with net cash and roughly $2.4B of unused repurchase authorization, a re-rating toward the peer trailing multiple of 41x on $10.00 of FY2027 adjusted EPS produces $400+.

### Bear Case → $175 (−36%)

The guidance requires the growth rate to double in one quarter — Q3 organic of 34–36% against Q2's 17.8% — while the company has just told investors its own delivery machine is congested. If the second half converts at even 30% growth rather than 45%, FY2026 revenue lands near $13.2B, adjusted EPS near $6.10, and the 2027 consensus of $9.10 resets toward $7.50. The cash flow tells the same story earlier: strip the $1,822.7M deferred-revenue inflow and 1H26 generated essentially no cash, and management's own 2H free cash flow guide is 31% below the prior year on 45% more revenue. Deferred revenue that has doubled in six months becomes a use of cash as it unwinds, and if projects slip Vertiv holds both the inventory and the delivery obligation. Layer on customer credit exposure to levered AI cloud operators against $3.75B of receivables, 86% of book equity in goodwill and intangibles, buyer power that compresses a scarcity-driven 37.7% gross margin as competitors add capacity, and Eaton assembling a comparable bundle. At 23x a reset $7.50, the stock is $175 — below the July 29 intraday low of $220.92 but consistent with published bear ranges near $180.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth_hold).** The twelve-month growth thesis is intact and specific, but base-case upside of 13% against a 36% bear case does not clear the bar for adding capital ahead of the adjudicating event.

**Targets and math from $273.46:**
- **Base case $310 (+13.4%):** 34x FY2027 consensus EPS of $9.10.
- **Bull case $400 (+46.3%):** 40x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$10.00.
- **Bear case $175 (−36.0%):** 23x a reset FY2027 EPS of ~$7.50.
- Probability-weighted (50/25/25): ~$299, or roughly +9%.
- Analyst consensus mean $338.15 (+23.7%), median $345.00, range $236.00–$427.00.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026. That report tests whether the guided 15% sequential step and 34–36% organic growth materialize, and whether management restores orders/backlog disclosure.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Q3 revenue lands at or above the $3,750M guidance midpoint **with** an orders or backlog figure disclosed showing year-over-year growth, confirming the 2H ramp is booked rather than hoped.
2. Q3 adjusted free cash flow tracks toward the implied $822–1,022M second-half range **without** further reliance on deferred-revenue inflows — that is, receivables and inventory growing no faster than revenue.
3. Price weakness toward $230–240 (roughly 26x FY2027 consensus, a PEG near 0.73) absent fundamental deterioration. A resumption of buybacks at these levels would corroborate.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. Q3 revenue misses the $3,650M low end, or FY2026 revenue guidance is cut, which would invalidate the second-half conversion assumption underpinning the FY2027 consensus.
2. Adjusted operating margin falls below 22% in Q3 against the 24.0–25.0% guide, indicating the price-cost and productivity tailwind has turned.
3. Receivables materially deteriorate — a specific-reserve charge or a disclosed customer re-phasing among AI cloud operators — or any goodwill impairment testing commentary appears.

Vertiv is a high-quality operator in a strong capex cycle, and its financial position has improved materially over the past six months: net cash, $5.6B of liquidity, an unsecured capital structure with 40-year paper, a single leverage covenant against zero net debt, gross margin up 372 basis points, and incremental operating margins near 37%.
