**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cohu is a global supplier of semiconductor test handlers, testers, inspection/metrology systems, and the consumables and services that attach to the installed base. The business is emerging from a severe multi-year downcycle — GAAP revenue fell from ~$813M in FY2022 to ~$402M in FY2024 before recovering to ~$453M in FY2025 — and the March-quarter print (Q1 FY26) is the clearest evidence yet that the cycle has turned. Revenue grew 29.3% YoY to $125.1M, gross margin expanded to 46.3% from 43.7%, the net loss more than halved to $(12.1)M, and operating cash flow flipped positive to +$10.3M. The recovery is concentrated in AI/HPC/data-center and mobile test demand, while automotive, industrial, and consumer end markets remain in inventory correction, though auto/industrial appears to be bottoming (+45% in 2025 after -65% in 2024).

The valuation problem is that the equity has already priced much of this. The stock is up 166% from its 52-week low of $17.80 and, despite a pullback from the June 30 intraday high of $74.60, still trades at $50.70 against negative trailing EPS and a forward path that consensus pegs at $0.58 for FY26 and $1.48 for FY27. On next-year numbers that is roughly 34x forward earnings — not obviously cheap for a company still posting GAAP losses, and reliant on the AI-testing narrative continuing to build. The 90-day estimate revisions are modestly positive further out (+20.3% next-year, +8.2% current-year) but current-quarter estimates have drifted lower (-8.5%), and the two most recent quarters both missed consensus.

We rate COHU a HOLD (growth_hold). The cyclical inflection and net-cash balance sheet are genuine, and the AI test-intensity theme is well-supported across the semiconductor infrastructure complex. But at this price the setup is balanced rather than asymmetric: a specific IS-reporting-unit goodwill impairment risk that management itself flags as having "limited headroom," net insider selling into the rally with zero open-market buying, 20.3% short interest, and rich near-term implied volatility pricing an imminent catalyst all argue for patience over conviction. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$40s (the capped-call cap and prior consolidation zone) or on confirmation that the recurring-revenue base and systems backlog are compounding through the next earnings print.

## Company Overview

Cohu, founded in 1947 and headquartered in San Diego, sells semiconductor test, handling, inspection, and metrology equipment plus consumables and services to integrated device manufacturers (IDMs) and outsourced assembly-and-test (OSAT) subcontractors. The company runs three product lines — Test Handlers, Semiconductor Testers, and Interface Solutions — but reports as a single segment under ASC 280.

The business has two distinct halves. The capital-equipment side (testers, handlers, inspection systems) is highly cyclical, tied to customer capex and fab utilization; it was roughly 40% of Q1 FY26 sales ($49.4M of systems revenue). The recurring/non-systems side (test contactors, sockets, spares, kits, services) runs 60-63% of revenue and is tied to installed-base production volume, which management positions as the more stable anchor. Roughly 90% of FY25 sales ship internationally, dominated by Asia (Malaysia, Philippines, Taiwan, China). Manufacturing is heavily outsourced; most test systems are built by Jabil in Malaysia, a single-source manufacturing dependency.

Two structural developments shape the current story. In January 2025 Cohu closed the ~$36.6M acquisition of Tignis, an AI process-control/analytics software company (PAICe Monitor/Maker), acquired outright to deepen its data-science and process-monitoring capabilities. In September 2025 Cohu issued $287.5M of 1.50% convertible senior notes due 2031, transforming a nearly debt-free company into one carrying ~$305M of debt against ~$489M of cash and investments.

## Financial Analysis

The most reliable lens on Cohu is cyclical earnings power and liquidity, not solvency or trailing profitability. Standardized screens that flag "weak profitability" reflect trough losses in a highly cyclical business, not a deteriorating franchise.

**Revenue and margins.** Q1 FY26 net sales were up 2.3% sequentially. Gross margin reflects a favorable systems mix plus improved factory utilization. R&D was $26.4M (21% of sales) and SG&A $34.6M (27.7%), both improving as operating leverage returns. Restructuring charges fell to $0.8M from $6.6M as the 2025 program winds down.

**Balance sheet.** Cash plus short-term investments stood at $488.7M at quarter-end, with working capital of roughly $637M and a historically high current ratio near 6-7x. Even after the September 2025 convert issuance, Cohu remains net-cash positive by roughly $184M (~$489M cash and investments less ~$305M total debt). The Altman Z-score is well above distress thresholds; bankruptcy risk is negligible. Interest income (Q1 FY26 $3.8M) currently exceeds interest expense ($1.6M) on the notes, so the convert is cash-flow-accretive at the pretax line despite adding ~$4.3M of annual coupon.

**Capital structure detail.** The $287.5M converts carry a 1.50% coupon (2.2% effective rate), mature 2031, and have a $27.18 conversion price (a 32.5% premium over the $20.51 September reference price). With the stock at $50.70, the notes are deeply in-the-money, so conversion/dilution mechanics matter to the equity story. Cohu bought $31.4M of capped calls with a cap near $41.02; above that level, dilution offset is exhausted. The convert's fair value had climbed to $382.7M by March 2026 as the stock recovered, a marker of how much equity upside has already accrued to noteholders.

**Earnings delivery.** The four-quarter surprise record is mixed: beats in the June and September 2025 quarters (+212%, +70% on small absolute numbers), then misses in the December 2025 (-337%) and March 2026 (-69%) quarters. The magnitudes are exaggerated by near-zero denominators, but the pattern signals that forward visibility at a cyclical trough is limited and consensus is being reset frequently.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on two legs. First, cyclical recovery: revenue is inflecting off a trough, and with the cost base rightsized through the 2025 restructuring (Switzerland/Germany consolidation, Poway-to-Asia manufacturing transition, and the January 2026 IS/ST consolidation), incremental margins on returning volume should be high. Second, secular test intensity: AI/HPC chips, advanced packaging, and HBM require more test and inspection per device, and Cohu cited a multi-unit HBM inspection system order as early evidence of design-in traction.

The demand picture is bifurcated. Mobile and AI/data-center test demand is driving the recovery, while auto/industrial/consumer remains subdued. The multi-year end-market data shows auto/industrial falling -24% (2023) and -65% (2024) before turning +45% in 2025, suggesting that segment is bottoming and could add a second recovery leg if the pattern holds.

Estimate revisions support the medium-term case without confirming a near-term acceleration. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates rose +8.2% to $0.58, next-quarter +19.2% to $0.22, and next-year +20.3% to $1.48. But over 30 days the trend flattened (current quarter +1.2%, next quarter -0.8%, current year -0.5%, next year -0.1%), and the current-quarter estimate is down -8.5% over 90 days. This is a stable-to-positive revision profile with the strongest momentum in the out-years, directionally encouraging, but it falls short of the sustained +30% velocity that would signal the market is systematically underestimating an imminent inflection. The revision signal here is a supporting factor, not a standalone BUY trigger, particularly because the stock is no longer cheap against the improving estimates.

The Tignis earnout missing its FY25 targets (the $5M contingent consideration lapsed) is a modest negative on the AI-software execution, though the capability is now Cohu-owned and its consolidated impact remains immaterial.

## Valuation Assessment

The company operates in a re-rating theme (AI-driven semiconductor test intensity), so pre-cycle historical multiples are the wrong anchor. But the appropriate cycle-adjusted lens still leaves the stock closer to fair than cheap.

Trailing metrics are meaningless — EPS is -$1.59 and the trailing P/E is negative. On forward consensus, $0.58 for FY26 implies ~87x and $1.48 for FY27 implies ~34x. Peer comparables in the semiconductor materials & equipment cohort show a wide dispersion (PDFS 352x, ACLS 54x, PLAB 11x, with negative multiples at SEDG and ICHR), and a peer-median P/E of 53.7. On that basis, COHU at ~34x next-year is at a discount to the noisy peer median, but the peer set is heterogeneous and several members carry their own trough distortions, so the comparison is not clean confirmation of undervaluation.

The more disciplined framing: Cohu is still generating GAAP losses, the recovery is early and concentrated, and the stock has already captured most of the cyclical re-rate — from ~$20 at the September 2025 note reference price to $50.70 today, a 2.5x move. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $53, mean $64, median $65, high $80), which puts the stock roughly 5% below the low end of the range and ~26% below the mean. On a genuinely cheap inflection stock, analyst targets often lag and act as floors; here, with the stock at ~34x next-year earnings and still loss-making today, the targets are better read as a range that encompasses the current price rather than a floor that guarantees upside.

We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — neither a value-trap flag nor a clear mispricing. The upside case exists if FY27 earnings power proves conservative and the recurring base compounds, but at today's price the entry does not offer the asymmetry a higher score would require. A pullback toward the low-$40s would materially improve the risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Cohu competes in the fragmented, price-competitive test-handling and interface niche of semiconductor capital equipment — smaller than the wafer-fab-equipment space (AMAT/LRCX/KLA) and adjacent to ATE leaders Teradyne and Advantest. Its competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly widening.

Positives: the recurring-revenue mix provides an installed-base annuity that dampens cyclicality and rewards long-tenured design-ins; the HBM/advanced-packaging inspection push aligns Cohu with the highest-growth test-intensity applications; and the Tignis-enabled analytics capability, if it converts, could extend the moat toward smart-manufacturing/process-control adjacencies.

Negatives and structural pressures: customer consolidation shrinks the buyer base and raises pricing pressure (top 10 customers = 60% of 2025 revenue); China export controls (40-50 material BIS rule changes in 2025) both restrict direct sales and push Chinese customers toward non-U.S. competitors, eroding long-term positioning; and the single-source Jabil manufacturing dependency concentrates operational risk. Company total shareholder return badly lagged the semi-equipment peer group through the downturn (indexed 60.6 vs. peer 138.3 in FY25), which is why bulls frame Cohu as an underfollowed catch-up play, but it also signals the franchise has not been a share-gainer relative to the complex.

## Risk Assessment

**IS goodwill impairment (specific, elevated, underappreciated).** Total goodwill is ~$280M (29% of assets). The 10-K explicitly discloses that the Interface Solutions reporting unit — 39% of total goodwill — had "limited headroom" at the October 2025 annual test, with fair value only narrowly exceeding carrying value. Management warns that "even modest adverse changes" could trigger a future non-cash impairment. No triggering event occurred through Q1 FY26, but this is a live risk if the recovery stalls.

**Extreme cyclicality.** Revenue nearly halved peak-to-trough with limited backlog and unpredictable timing. The rebound is real but early and concentrated in AI/mobile; an AI-capex pause or continued auto/industrial weakness could cap the recovery.

**Customer and manufacturing concentration.** Top 10 customers = 60% of revenue; most test systems are built by a single contract manufacturer (Jabil, Malaysia). Either concentration reversing would be material.

**China/export controls.** China was $13.5M of Q1 FY26 sales; the regulatory regime is fluid and structurally erosive to the addressable market.

**Convertible dilution.** Non-investment-grade credit; notes are deeply in-the-money at $50.70 versus the $27.18 conversion price, and capped-call protection is exhausted above ~$41. Conversion mechanics dilute equity holders on the upside.

**Insider selling.** After the re-rate, the CEO (~$2.1M at $46.62), CFO (~$0.6M at $46.84), and multiple directors sold in May 2026, with zero open-market buying. These appear to be diversification/10b5-1 sales, but the absence of conviction buying during a recovery narrative is a caution.

**Geopolitical.** The Q1 FY26 10-Q added a new Iran Conflict risk factor (energy prices, supply chain, state-sponsored cyber), the primary change versus the prior 10-K risk set.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. ATM implied volatility is 109.9% versus 60.6% one-year historical volatility — a 49-point rich spread at the 83rd percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 109.9% > long-term 92.4%), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst. IV also rose sharply (79.9% → 109.9%, +30pp) even as the stock fell 7.7% since late May. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.8%) and put/call volume/OI ratios are low, so there is no acute directional bearish tilt in positioning; but the ±24.4% implied move on the nearest expiry, combined with 20.3% short interest and 5.5 days to cover, frames a highly binary near-term setup. Rich IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for the volatility crush after the catalyst resolves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($80).** The AI/HPC test-intensity tailwind proves durable and broadens; auto/industrial completes its recovery from the +45% 2025 base; the rightsized cost structure delivers high incremental margins as volume returns, and FY27 EPS beats the $1.48 consensus. The recurring-revenue base compounds, the market rewards a re-rate toward the high end of the peer range, and Cohu closes the TSR gap to the semi-equipment complex. At ~40-50x a beat-and-raise FY27 number, the stock reaches the $80 analyst high.

**Bear case (~$32-36, roughly -30% to -35%).** AI-capex enthusiasm cools or the recovery stalls; auto/industrial demand stays soft; the IS reporting unit underperforms and triggers a material goodwill impairment; and the stock de-rates back toward the pre-June breakout zone in the mid-$30s. The short interest and rich near-term IV amplify downside if the next print disappoints against elevated expectations.

**Base case ($60-65).** The cyclical recovery continues at a measured pace, FY27 EPS lands near the $1.48 consensus, and the stock trades toward the analyst mean/median. This implies roughly 18-28% upside from $50.70 — attractive in isolation, but the path runs through a binary earnings catalyst with a ±24% implied move and a specific impairment risk, which is why we do not treat the base case as a high-conviction BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings print (the catalyst the options market is pricing).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (the capped-call cap zone and prior consolidation), which would restore asymmetric risk/reward on the same forward estimates.
- Confirmation of sustained estimate-revision velocity (a durable +30% next-year revision path) alongside evidence the recurring base and systems backlog are compounding, rather than the current stable-to-flat 30-day trend.
- A clean quarter that beats consensus and demonstrates auto/industrial recovery broadening, removing the near-term expectations risk baked into the elevated multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An IS reporting-unit goodwill impairment charge, or a triggering-event disclosure indicating one is imminent.
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue deceleration or a guidance cut signaling the AI-driven recovery is stalling.
- Consensus next-year EPS estimates rolling over (a shift from the current +20% 90-day trend to sustained negative revisions).

**Score trajectory note.** COHU has declined from 5.8 (May 05) to 5.5 (May 27), with management quality, growth, and competitive position all ticking down. Our current 5.6 broadly holds that trajectory. The stock has fallen from its late-June peak since the last report, but the fundamentals are unchanged-to-slightly-improving (estimates stable-to-positive, balance sheet intact), so the lower price improves risk/reward at the margin without changing the balanced HOLD conclusion. What keeps us from upgrading on the decline is that the price is still ~34x next-year earnings against GAAP losses, the drawdown from the June high was a reversal of speculative excess rather than a fundamental re-rate, and the specific impairment and expectations risks remain unresolved ahead of a binary catalyst.

## Conclusion

Cohu is a legitimate cyclical inflection with a fortress balance sheet ($489M cash, net-cash even after the convert), a genuine AI/HPC test-intensity tailwind, and a recurring-revenue anchor that dampens the worst of the cycle. Q1 FY26 confirmed the turn: revenue +29% YoY, gross margin 46.3%, positive operating cash flow, and a loss more than halved. The problem is price. After a 166% move off the low, the equity trades at ~34x next-year consensus while still posting GAAP losses, with analyst targets bracketing rather than anchoring above the current price, and with a specific IS-goodwill impairment risk, net insider selling, 20.3% short interest, and rich backwardated implied volatility all layered onto a binary near-term catalyst.

We rate COHU a HOLD (growth_hold) and would look to accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$40s or on confirmation that the recovery is compounding through the recurring base and auto/industrial recovery, rather than chasing the AI-testing narrative at current levels into an elevated-expectations earnings print.
