**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and the dominant North American nitrogen manufacturer, controlling roughly 40% of regional ammonia, 41% of granular urea, and 44% of UAN capacity. FY2025 was a strong recovery year: net sales of $7.08B (+19% YoY), gross margin of 38.5% (up from 34.6%), diluted EPS of $8.97 (+33%), and free cash flow near $1.8B against a balance sheet carrying only ~0.5x net leverage. Q1 FY2026 extended the strength: adjusted EBITDA of $983M and EPS of $3.98 on tight global nitrogen markets. Near-term consensus estimates have risen sharply (current-quarter EPS revised +173.6% over 90 days, current year +91.9%), reflecting the price spike that tight supply has produced.

The complication is that the source of CF's elevated earnings is a global nitrogen price spike driven substantially by supply disruption (Russia, Iran, Egypt outages). Set against the price tailwind are three identifiable headwinds: early-2026 Henry Hub gas averaging ~$6.32/MMBtu (nearly double the 2025 average of $3.53), the Yazoo City outage idling the complex until Q4 2026 at the earliest (cutting guided 2026 ammonia output to ~9.5M tons from 10.1M), and a December 2025 Trump executive order directing food-supply-chain price-fixing investigations that explicitly names fertilizer.

The stock has fallen roughly 27% from its 52-week high of $141.96 to $102.93, with degrading analyst sentiment. The valuation looks superficially cheap (~9.3x trailing), but that multiple is built on peak-cycle earnings that the macro setup makes two-sided rather than asymmetric. We rate CF a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the business quality and balance sheet are genuine, but there is no directional edge here until the nitrogen-price cycle resolves or the price retraces to a level offering downside protection.

## Company Overview

CF operates nine nitrogen complexes: six in the U.S. (including Donaldsonville, Louisiana, the world's largest ammonia complex at roughly 40% of CF's capacity), two in Canada (Medicine Hat, Courtright), and one in the U.K. (Billingham, whose ammonia unit has been idled since 2022, now importing ammonia to upgrade), plus a 50% JV in Trinidad (PLNL). The company uses the Haber-Bosch process to convert natural gas into anhydrous ammonia, then upgrades it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and industrial products including diesel exhaust fluid.

The FY2025 segment gross-margin mix was UAN ($921M, 42.6% margin), granular urea ($837M, 47.0%), ammonia ($682M, 31.3%), other ($205M), and AN ($79M, 18.8%). Distribution scale is a meaningful asset: roughly 5,000 leased railcars, a river barge fleet, access to the 2,000-mile Sunoco ammonia pipeline, and 39 in-market terminals. CHS Inc. holds ~11% of subsidiary CFN and is CF's largest customer at ~13% of net sales.

A leadership transition appears to have completed around year-end 2025/early 2026. Form 4 data shows long-time CEO W. Anthony ("Tony") Will reclassified as "Director" rather than Officer, with Andrew Scribner appearing as EVP & CFO, alongside a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks in January 2026. The specific identity of the new CEO is unconfirmed from available filings.

## Financial Analysis

CF's profile is highly cyclical and tracks nitrogen prices closely. Revenue ran $11.19B in 2022 (the post-Russia/Ukraine super-spike), fell to $6.63B in 2023, troughed at $5.94B in 2024 (compounded by winter-storm outages), and recovered to $7.08B in 2025.

The 2025 recovery was almost entirely price-driven: average selling price rose 19% to $372/ton from $313, while volume was essentially flat (+1%). Gross margin expanded $668M to $2.72B, with price gains of ~$1.06B partly offset by a $316M natural gas cost headwind (gas cost up 38% to $3.31/MMBtu). Net earnings to common were $1.46B (+19%); diluted EPS outgrew net income because of buybacks. Operating cash flow was $2.75B, capex $950M, leaving free cash flow near $1.8B (a 25% FCF margin).

The balance sheet is robust. Cash stood at $1.98B against total debt of $3.25B, all unsecured senior notes maturing 2034–2044 after the November–December 2025 refinancing cleared the $750M 4.50% 2026 notes (issuing $1B 5.30% notes due 2035). ROE of 37% and ROA of ~13% are exceptional for a materials name, though ROE is flattered by buyback-driven equity shrinkage. The Altman Z-Score of 1.37 ("distress") is a model artifact: it penalizes CF for a high debt/equity ratio (2.91) that reflects buyback-driven equity contraction, not financial stress. With $1.8B FCF, $2B cash, and a 3.37 current ratio, the company is financially sound.

The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, including a 24.9% beat in Q4 2025 and an 8.2% beat in Q1 2026, against a -10.3% miss in Q2 2025. The pattern is consistent with a price-sensitive cyclical where consensus lags fast-moving nitrogen markets in both directions.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth signal is striking: current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised +48.5% over 30 days and +173.6% over 90 days; next-quarter +92.0% / +286.1%; current-year +91.9% over 90 days. On the standard inflection screen, revision velocity of this magnitude is a strong forward signal. But the guardrails cut against treating this as a clean BUY signal.

First, this is a commodity price spike in a cyclical, not a structural inflection from a depressed base. Tight global nitrogen supply in 2025 reflected outages in Egypt, Iran, and Russia. A meaningful portion of the price strength is contingent on global supply remaining constrained, and a normalization of that supply tightness would pull nitrogen prices back toward normalized levels. The estimate revisions are therefore extrapolating a peak that has identifiable reversal mechanisms.

Second, the offsetting near-term swing into 2026 is real. Gas is ~34% of production cost, so a sustained doubling erodes CF's cost advantage and compresses margins even if selling prices hold.

The longer-term growth option is the Blue Point, Louisiana greenfield ATR + CCS plant (~$3.7B total, CF 40% / JERA 35% / Mitsui 25%, targeted for 2029) and low-carbon ammonia more broadly. The Donaldsonville CCS unit came online in July 2025, earned ~$42M of 45Q credits in 2025, and supports first low-carbon ammonia sales at a premium to Europe and Africa. EU CBAM (live January 2026) and UK CBAM (2027) are structural tailwinds for low-carbon demand, but this is a multi-year, capital-intensive, policy-dependent bet, not a 12-month driver. Growth prospects score 5.5: the near-term revision velocity is offset by its peak-cycle, reversible source and the gas-cost/outage headwinds, while the durable growth option is years out and execution-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at ~9.3x trailing EPS of $8.97 and ~9.3x forward. Against the peer median P/E of 40.7 (Fertilizers & Agricultural Chemicals), the discount looks dramatic, but the peer set is distorted: Mosaic at 148.8x and FMC negative reflect their own trough earnings, while CTVA at 40.7x and SMG at 17.7x are different business mixes. The cleaner read is that CF's multiple is compressed because the market is correctly recognizing that current EPS reflects peak-cycle nitrogen prices, not a normalized run-rate.

The cycle-adjusted framework cuts in the unfavorable direction here: trailing and forward EPS sit near a cyclical peak inflated by supply tightness, not a trough. Applying a low multiple to peak earnings is the classic cyclical value trap. If nitrogen prices normalize and global capacity additions arrive (the 10-K explicitly warns new capacity is coming online over the next 12 months), normalized EPS could be materially below current levels. A history reference in the filing, a 34% nitrogen price decline 2015–2017, frames the downside.

Analyst targets span $100 low to $150 high (mean $126, median $128). That dispersion of roughly 50% around the current price mirrors the two-sided macro setup. We do not treat the consensus targets as floors here, because this is a peak-cycle commodity producer where the estimate revisions themselves are the contested variable, not an inflection stock cheap relative to durable rising estimates.

The ~1.9% dividend yield ($2.00/share, ~$326M, well-covered) provides modest support but does not anchor a valuation case. The valuation score is 5.0: the low headline multiple is offset by the peak-earnings denominator and the absence of a catalyst that resolves favorably with confidence. A genuinely cheap entry would require either a supply normalization that clears the overhang while prices hold (unlikely to coincide) or a retracement toward the mid-$70s/low-$80s where the trough-multiple math turns protective.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's moat is structural cost advantage plus scale. Its North American shale gas feedstock advantage versus the global marginal producer (often European) means CF's margins expand when global gas and nitrogen supply are tight, and compress when North American gas spikes. The early-2026 gas doubling is the unfavorable side of that dynamic. The ~40% North American capacity share across ammonia, urea, and UAN, combined with logistics (railcars, barges, the Sunoco pipeline, 39 terminals), is a genuine barrier.

Primary competitors include Nutrien, Koch Fertilizer, Yara International, LSB Industries, and CVR Partners, plus continuous import competition from the Middle East, Trinidad, Africa, and Russia. Product differentiation is limited; competition is on delivered price. CF's first-mover position in low-carbon ammonia (Donaldsonville CCS online, Blue Point under construction) is a forward differentiator that CBAM regimes increasingly reward.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the low-carbon front, but the structural tailwind is partly offset by the near-term cost-position erosion from high domestic gas and the announced global capacity additions that will pressure pricing. Competitive position scores 6.5: a durable moat with a modestly improving low-carbon angle, tempered by the cyclical cost-position swing.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic and macro risks here are material and not fully priced, which drives a below-average risk score of 4.5.

**Nitrogen-price cyclicality.** The single largest risk to the earnings thesis is that the supply-driven nitrogen price spike reverses. A normalization of the global supply tightness underpinning current pricing would pull selling prices back toward normalized levels and compress the margins driving the estimate revisions.

**Natural gas cost.** Henry Hub at ~$6.32/MMBtu early 2026 versus $3.53 in 2025 is a quantified, acute margin headwind if sustained.

**Yazoo City outage.** The November 2025 incident idled the complex (a $25M impairment), with production not expected to resume until Q4 2026, cutting roughly 600K tons of 2026 ammonia output. This highlights concentration risk across a limited number of key facilities.

**Litigation and policy.** The December 2025 price-fixing executive order is an unquantified overhang. Add EU CBAM exemption lobbying (France/Italy), the Louisiana Class VI well moratorium (October 2025, threatening Blue Point's CCS dependency), the expired PLNL Trinidad gas contract (on short-term extensions), the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs (with the administration re-imposing 10% under other authority), the EPA's February 2026 rescission of the GHG endangerment finding, and 45Q credit modifications under the July 2025 OBBBA.

**Leadership transition.** A CEO handoff completing around year-end 2025/early 2026 with an unconfirmed successor adds execution uncertainty during a capital-intensive build phase.

**Capital intensity.** 2026 consolidated capex is guided to ~$1.3B (including Blue Point), up sharply from $950M, with Blue Point representing both the biggest growth option and the biggest execution risk (unproven ATR-at-scale, third-party sequestration dependency, a market that "may not develop at all").

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is two-sided and shows resolving near-term uncertainty rather than directional conviction. Near-term ATM IV is 48.8% versus 41.6% historical vol (rich by 7.2 points), at the 69th percentile of the current chain, implying a ±5.7% move on the nearest expiry. ATM IV collapsed from 64.0% to 48.8% over the prior reporting window and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.47 to 0.65, with skew turning call-heavy (-6.4%). That shift (falling IV, falling put demand, call-heavy skew) reads as a market that has partly stepped back from hedging downside. The flat term structure (near 48.8% ≈ far 48.7%) shows uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors. There is no clear options-implied directional edge to override the fundamental two-sidedness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($138–$150).** Global nitrogen supply stays tight through 2026 (Russian/Iranian/Egyptian supply remains constrained), domestic gas costs ease back toward 2025 levels, and CF executes the buyback against a low share count. Estimate revisions prove correct, FY2026 EPS lands well above the prior year, and the trough multiple re-rates modestly on sustained earnings. The low-carbon ammonia premium and CBAM tailwinds add an option value the market is not paying for. The $150 analyst high anchors this scenario.

**Bear Case ($75–$85).** Global nitrogen supply normalizes, nitrogen prices fall toward historical levels (recall the 34% decline 2015–2017), and the early-2026 gas cost doubling compresses margins simultaneously. Estimate revisions reverse, the peak-earnings denominator collapses, and the "cheap" 9x multiple is revealed as a value trap on peak EPS. The Yazoo City output cut and the price-fixing probe add pressure. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low region near $75.

The base case sits between these at roughly $108–$112, reflecting a stock fairly valued for a two-sided cycle with a fortress balance sheet but no asymmetric edge at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** This is a HOLD because the business quality is genuine but the risk/reward is symmetric, not asymmetric. The recommendation has been HOLD across the prior reports, and the score trajectory (5.6 → 5.9, declining velocity, with Management Quality, Valuation, and Growth Prospects all derated over the period) reflects the recognition that the supply-driven earnings spike is reversible. New data does not change that read: the ~27% decline from the 52-week high tracks softening sentiment rather than fundamental deterioration, but because the lower price coincides with a peak-earnings denominator that is itself at risk, the lower price does not, in this case, improve the risk/reward the way it would for a company whose fundamentals were independent of the cycle.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026) and any durable evidence of nitrogen-supply normalization are the key reassessment points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A retracement to the mid-$70s/low-$80s, where the trough-multiple math turns protective and downside is bounded by the balance sheet.
- Durable evidence of structural nitrogen tightness holding even after a supply normalization, alongside domestic gas costs easing back below ~$4/MMBtu.
- Resolution of the fertilizer price-fixing probe without material liability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A normalization of global nitrogen supply coinciding with sustained Henry Hub above ~$6/MMBtu (the double margin squeeze).
- A reversal in estimate-revision velocity (current-quarter or next-quarter estimates cut >15% over 30 days).
- An adverse development in the price-fixing investigation.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, low-cost, scale-advantaged cyclical with a fortress balance sheet, disciplined capital allocation (~20% share-count reduction in three years, the electrolyzer write-off in favor of CCS economics), and a credible long-term low-carbon ammonia option. None of that is in question. What is in question is whether the current earnings level, and the rapidly rising estimates built on it, survives a normalization of the supply-driven nitrogen price spike. With a ~27% drawdown already behind it, a ~9x multiple on peak earnings, and a roughly 50%-wide analyst target band ($100–$150) that mirrors the macro two-sidedness, the setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. We maintain HOLD and would look to add only on a supply normalization that clears the overhang while prices hold, or a retracement toward the mid-$70s/low-$80s where the cycle-trough math turns protective.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's central "Hormuz/Iran de-escalation binary" narrative — including the draft US-Iran MoU, cancelled strike, and Hormuz-reopening trigger — is not supported anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and has been removed/reframed as generic nitrogen-supply cyclicality; the March 2026 Sherman Act class action was likewise unsupported and removed. The exit/upgrade/downgrade logic that hinged on a "verified Hormuz reopening" now rests on the more general (and weaker-edged) supply-normalization thesis.
