**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

XP Inc. is a Brazilian open-architecture investment platform that has compounded diluted EPS at roughly 12% annually since FY2022 (R$6.25 → R$9.90 TTM) while generating a low-20s return on average equity and maintaining a 20.7% regulatory capital ratio. At $17.06, the shares trade at approximately 9.0-9.6x trailing EPS and 8.3-8.9x FY2026 consensus of R$10.74. For a business earning 21.7% adjusted ROAE and retiring 3-4% of its net share count annually, that is a demonstrably low multiple.

The reason it is low is visible in the operating data. XP's retail take rate — the revenue it captures per unit of client assets — fell from 1.25% (2Q25, described by the company as stable) to 1.18% annualized in 1Q26, a 7bp or 5.6% relative decline in three quarters. On R$1.5 trillion of client assets, each basis point is roughly R$150m of annualized retail revenue, so 7bps approximates R$1.05bn, about 5.7% of FY2025 revenue. The consequence is that 15% client-asset growth converted into only 10% retail revenue growth in 1Q26, and adjusted net income growth decelerated along a clear path: +18% (2Q25) → +12% (3Q25) → +7% (1Q26). Reported EPS growth decelerated more slowly (+22% → +13% → +9%) because buybacks absorbed the difference. The franchise's underlying momentum is the 7%, not the 9%.

The pivotal question is whether take-rate compression is cyclical or structural. XP attributes the 1Q26 shift to weaker fixed income offset by equities, funds, cards and credit — consistent with a high-Selic environment crowding clients into low-margin products, which would reverse mechanically on a rate-cutting cycle. It is equally consistent with competitive fee compression and rising adviser retention costs in a market where incumbent banks have built their own open platforms and Brazilian regulation has loosened adviser exclusivity. Available disclosure does not adjudicate between these, and the difference is worth several turns of multiple. Combined with 95.8% revenue concentration in Brazil, BRL translation risk on a USD-priced security, and an unseasoned loan book growing 33% annually, we rate XP HOLD with a base case of roughly $20.50 (+20%) and a bear case of $13.50 (-21%).

## Company Overview

XP distributes third-party and proprietary investment products to Brazilian retail and institutional clients through a large network of financial advisers, having pioneered open architecture in a market historically dominated by universal banks selling in-house shelves. The business now spans four broad areas:

- **Retail** (~77% of gross revenue, derived from 2Q25: R$3.58bn retail against R$4.67bn gross): equities and trading, fixed income distribution, funds, pension/retirement products, credit cards, and collateralized lending.
- **Corporate & Issuer Services / Wholesale Bank**: investment banking, DCM/ECM, corporate lending. Fastest-growing line at +32% YoY in 3Q25.
- **Institutional and Asset Management**: XP Asset Management.
- **Insurance (XP V&P)**: gross written premiums +45% YoY in 2Q25.

Total client assets reached R$1.5 trillion in 1Q26 (+15% YoY), with a broader assets-plus-AuM-plus-AuA measure of R$1.9 trillion at 3Q25. Reported NPS was 74 at 3Q25.

Geographic concentration is near-total: Brazil generated R$17,933m of R$18,728m TTM revenue (95.8%), essentially unchanged from FY2024's 95.6%. The international build — including an inaugural XP Asset Management Global Conference in Miami in March 2026 — is real but immaterial to earnings.

Two mechanical items on the data require correction before any multiple is computed. First, the market capitalization shown ($7.05B, derived from 413.1m shares × $17.06) appears to capture Class A shares only. Total disclosed shares after the November 17, 2025 cancellation were 519,889,007, implying a capitalization near $8.87B; a $7.0B figure understates every derived multiple by roughly 21%. Second, XP is a Cayman-incorporated foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K reports, not 10-K/10-Q. It files no proxy statement and its insiders are exempt from Section 16, so no Form 4 insider-transaction record exists. The absence of insider buying carries no information here.

This is a correlated exposure: in a Brazilian market drawdown, both the asset base and the take rate (via flight to low-margin fixed income) can move against XP simultaneously.

### Balance sheet and why conventional cash-flow metrics mislead

Reported cash and investments of R$321.0bn (Mar-26) against total debt of R$179.3bn produces an apparent "net cash" of R$141.7bn, or R$267 per share against a share price near R$89. Reported TTM operating cash flow of R$19,368m against capex of R$209m produces a "FCF margin" above 100% and a P/FCF near 2.3x.

None of these figures should be used. For a broker-dealer and bank holding company, cash and investments are largely client cash, securities inventory and repo-financed positions; total debt is largely funding; and operating cash flow is dominated by period changes in client balances and the trading book. The FY2021 figure of −R$4,020m operating cash flow followed by a path to +R$19,368m reflects balance-sheet expansion, not an earnings-quality turnaround. The Altman Z-Score and Piotroski F-Score frameworks are structurally inapplicable — XP's revenue/assets ratio would mechanically register distress for a fully solvent institution.

The relevant solvency measure is the BIS ratio, which rose from 20.1% (2Q25) to 20.7% (1Q26). Capital is not the binding constraint on this story; revenue per unit of client assets is.

### Capital allocation

Dividends per share fell from R$6.357 (FY2023) to R$4.020 (FY2024) to R$0.991 (FY2025). This is substitution, not distress, and the evidence is specific:

- **May 20, 2025**: cancelled 12,053,924 Class A treasury shares (540,052,383 → 527,998,459).
- **November 17, 2025**: cancelled 10,970,754 Class A shares (530,859,761 → 519,889,007); declared US$0.18/share (≈R$500m); authorized a new R$1.0bn buyback through November 2026.
- **May 18, 2026**: declared US$0.20/share (≈R$500m) payable June 18, 2026; authorized another R$1.0bn buyback through May 2027, funded from existing cash.

Two derived points. Net share reduction over the twelve months from May 2025 to November 2025 cancellations was 3.73%. But the count rose from 527,998,459 to 530,859,761 between the two cancellations — an increase of 2,861,302 shares (+0.54%) in roughly six months, most plausibly equity compensation, though the source was not verified. Net accretion to shareholders is therefore 3-4% annually, not the gross repurchase figure.

Dividends of roughly R$1.0bn annually plus a R$1.0bn buyback authorization against FY2025 net income of R$5,170m implies a total distribution near R$2.0bn, a payout ratio near 39%, comfortably funded while the capital ratio rose. Repurchasing stock at 9x earnings with a 20.7% BIS ratio and a low-20s ROE is the correct decision.

The dividend yield shown (1.19%) does not reconcile with declared payments; two semi-annual dividends of US$0.18 and US$0.20 imply a trailing cash yield near 2.2%. Combined with net share shrinkage, total shareholder yield is roughly 5-6%.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus expects R$10.74 for the current year (+8.5% over TTM R$9.90) and R$12.05 for next year (+12.2%). Revisions have drifted modestly negative: current year -1.1% over 30 days and -1.0% over 90 days; next year -1.6% and -2.4%. Current-quarter estimates rose 2.2% over 30 days but remain 2.5% below where they sat 90 days ago. There is no estimate-revision signal here in either direction of consequence, and certainly nothing resembling the >30% upward velocity that would justify overriding valuation discipline.

The delivery record reinforces caution: three misses in the last four quarters (-1.4%, -1.2%, -5.6%) after one small beat (+1.9%). The magnitudes are modest, but the direction has been consistently below consensus and the miss widened in the most recent quarter.

Where growth is coming from has changed. Retail brokerage fees are compressing; the growth leaders are Corporate & Issuer Services (+32% YoY in 3Q25), the loan portfolio (R$67bn, +33% YoY), insurance premiums (+45% YoY), cards, and retirement products. Management is substituting balance-sheet and capital-markets income for compressing distribution fees, which is the right strategic response and is working at the revenue line.

The mix shift also changes the character of the business. A capital-light, fee-based distribution platform earns a higher multiple than a capital-consuming, credit-exposed bank. Investors buying XP at 8-9x on the argument that it is a cheap platform should recognize that the mix is drifting toward the model that justifies 8-9x. The 33%-growing loan book has not seasoned through a Brazilian credit downturn, and while XP describes the lending as collateralized, collateral consisting of client investment portfolios can decline in the same stress event that triggers defaults.

The cyclical case for reacceleration is legitimate. Brazil's policy rate determines whether household savings sit in low-take fixed income or rotate into higher-take equities, funds, structured products and real-estate funds. XP's 1Q26 commentary — weaker fixed income offset by equities, funds, cards and credit — is the first observable evidence of that rotation beginning. If a rate-cutting cycle is underway, the take rate has a cyclical tailwind and 8.3x forward embeds a pessimistic mix assumption. The company's April 2026 "Expert Trader XP" event, aimed at the active-trading cohort, is consistent with a deliberate push to defend take rate through mix.

We cannot verify a first-hand 2026 guidance figure. A third-party summary characterized management as maintaining 2026 guidance on the 1Q26 call; the transcript was not available for confirmation and no guidance range should be treated as established.

## Valuation Assessment

Using the disclosed 519.9m share count and $17.06, market capitalization is approximately $8.87B. Back-solving the reported P/E gives an implied FX rate near 5.20 BRL/USD, so:

| Basis | BRL EPS | USD EPS | P/E at $17.06 |
|---|---|---|---|
| TTM Mar-26 | R$9.90 | $1.90 | ~9.0x |
| FY2026 consensus | R$10.74 | $2.07 | ~8.3x |
| FY2027 consensus | R$12.05 | $2.32 | ~7.4x |

Those conversions inherit FX uncertainty; the rate was derived from reported ratios rather than observed, and BRL depreciation would compress USD EPS one-for-one with no operational change.

Against Brazilian financial peers, this is not an obvious dislocation. Brazilian large-cap financials trade at compressed multiples as a group because Brazil's cost of equity is high, its policy rate is high, and October 2026 brings a general election. XP's premium to a pure incumbent bank has largely closed, which is itself the market's judgment that XP is becoming more bank-like. [unverified — specific peer multiples for Itaú, Bradesco, BTG and Nu are not in the provided sources]

The asymmetry case rests on three specifics: a 21.7% adjusted ROAE against a 20.7% capital ratio, meaning the return is earned on an over-capitalized balance sheet; a 5-6% total shareholder yield with distributions covered near 39% of earnings; and a linear take-rate sensitivity that cuts both ways — 7bps of recovery would add roughly R$1.05bn of annualized retail revenue, approximately 5.7% of revenue at high incremental margin.

The offsetting case is that the multiple has compressed for identifiable reasons rather than sentiment: three consecutive misses, net income growth halving from +18% to +7%, the take rate down 7bps, inflows characterized as slowing, and consensus drifting lower. The shares are 26% below the February 2026 high of $23.13. That decline coincided with the 1Q26 print and the CFO announcement — sellers are responding to disclosed deterioration, not mispricing volatility. A cheap multiple on decelerating fundamentals with an unresolved causal question is exactly the configuration in which apparent value fails to convert.

Analyst positioning is constructive (3 strong buy, 8 buy, 2 hold, with one hold moving to buy in the past month) and the consensus target range is $19.38-26.38 with a mean of $23.10 — 35% above spot. We do not treat that as independent confirmation. For a stock at 8x forward earnings, targets typically embed a multiple re-rating assumption that is itself contingent on the take-rate question resolving favorably.

We score valuation 6.0: the multiple is genuinely low relative to returns and there is a defined catalyst (mix rotation on Selic cuts) that could unlock it, but the discount is explained by real deterioration and the catalyst is macro-contingent rather than company-controlled.

## Competitive Landscape

XP's moat is the adviser network and the scale of client assets it distributes to. That moat is expensive and slow to replicate, and NPS of 74 supports genuine client stickiness. The trajectory, however, is the relevant measure.

Two pressures are working against the core. Incumbent universal banks have built their own open-architecture platforms and digital investment arms, competing for the same clients with balance sheets XP cannot match. Second, Brazilian regulation has moved toward permitting advisers to serve multiple platforms rather than operating exclusively, which erodes lock-in and raises the price of retaining producers. We could not verify the current regulatory specifics, so treat the mechanism as inferred rather than confirmed; XP's own language about "continued investment in technology and advisers" (4Q25), combined with the take-rate trend, is consistent with a market where producer economics are contested and their cost is rising.

Against that, the response is coherent and measurable: wholesale banking +32%, credit +33%, insurance premiums +45%. XP is winning share in adjacent fee pools even as its founding fee pool compresses.

One discipline point on diversification. Revenue mix is genuinely broadening away from equity brokerage, and that reduces exposure to any single fee pool. It does not reduce concentration risk in the dimensions that matter most for a USD-priced security: geographic concentration is unchanged at 95.8% Brazil, and retail remains roughly 77% of gross revenue. XP is diversifying its product set within one country and one client franchise. Exposure to Brazilian macro, Brazilian rates, the BRL, and Brazilian investment-product tax policy is essentially unchanged.

Score: 5.5. Improving in adjacencies, deteriorating in the core, with the net trajectory unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Take-rate compression (dominant, evidenced).** 1.25% → 1.18% in three quarters. If compression continues at ~2bps per quarter, roughly half of client-asset growth is consumed before reaching the revenue line. The cyclical and structural explanations are both consistent with disclosed data, and the difference determines whether the stock is worth 8x or 12x.

**Brazil concentration ahead of the October 2026 election (95.8% of TTM revenue).** Three transmission channels: policy rate determines product mix and therefore take rate; BRL/USD determines USD earnings for a dollar-based holder; and tax policy on previously exempt instruments (LCI/LCA, incentivized debentures, real-estate funds) directly shapes demand for products XP distributes. Brazil has actively debated raising taxation on exempt instruments; we could not confirm the current status of any measure, but the exposure is structural.

**Unseasoned credit book.** R$67bn growing 33% annually, collateralized but never tested through a Brazilian downturn, with potential wrong-way correlation if collateral consists of client portfolios. The 20.7% BIS ratio is the mitigant.

**Net-inflow volatility.** A 70% YoY quarterly inflow decline (2Q25) in a take-rate business is material, and asset growth is partly beta to Brazilian markets.

**CFO transition.** Victor Mansur departed May 31, 2026; Gustavo Viviani starts August 3, 2026; CEO Thiago Maffra served as interim CFO for the nine-week gap. A named successor with a fixed start date and a CEO bridge is the signature of planned succession, and we found no evidence of disagreement, restatement or accounting concern — none should be inferred. The narrower fair statement is that XP is changing its principal financial officer at the moment its key operating metric is compressing, removing an experienced voice from investor communications when the take-rate narrative most needs credible explanation. IR Officer André Parize continuing supports disclosure continuity.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure with buybacks and cancellations exclusively of Class A. The mechanical consequence is that Class B voting weight rises over time — public shareholders fund a repurchase that concentrates control. No proxy, compensation, pay-for-performance, or board-composition data exists to evaluate, and no insider-transaction record is available.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 42.4% sits below one-year realized volatility of 46.9% and in the 2nd percentile of the current chain — near-dated optionality is cheap, which favors buying protection over selling it and argues against paying up for near-term upside exposure. The term structure is in steep contango (42.4% at 13 days, 67.6% at 41 days, 52.9% at 111 days), and the September 11 tenor carries a 21.8% put/call skew with a 19.3% implied move. The market is pricing concentrated event risk in the 4-6 week window covering the 2Q26 print and the run-up to Brazil's October election, while treating the immediate period as calm. The 5.0% near-term put/call skew indicates elevated put demand; put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.63 to 0.02 since early June, so protection is being expressed in newer, dated positions rather than accumulated inventory. Short interest at 4.7% of float with 3.2 days to cover is uncrowded — informed capital is not pressing a bearish view, but neither is it defending the name.

Net read: options positioning corroborates our HOLD. The market is not pricing a collapse, but it is pricing a specific, dated risk event around the next print and the Brazilian political calendar, and near-term vol is cheap enough that patience costs little.

Score: 4.8. The concentration of unresolved binaries — take-rate causality, election, tax policy, FX, credit seasoning — is high relative to what an 8x multiple compensates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($25.50, +49%)

Brazil's rate-cutting cycle deepens, and household savings rotate out of low-margin fixed income into equities, funds, structured products and real-estate funds. The retail take rate recovers toward 1.25%, adding roughly R$1.05bn of high-margin retail revenue against R$18.4bn of total revenue. Client assets compound in the low-to-mid teens on both inflows and market appreciation. Wholesale banking sustains 25-30% growth and the credit book continues to convert client relationships into net interest income without a credit event. Net income growth reaccelerates from 7% toward the mid-teens, and buybacks continue reducing the share count 3-4% annually. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds consensus R$12.05, and the multiple re-rates toward 11x as investors again pay for a fee platform rather than a bank. 11x on $2.32 = $25.50. Election resolution without a policy shock supporting Brazilian financial assets would be a secondary accelerant.

### Bear Case ($13.50, -21%)

Take-rate compression proves structural. Adviser multi-platform economics raise retention costs permanently, incumbent bank platforms compete distribution fees lower, and the take rate continues declining 1-2bps per quarter toward 1.10%. Client-asset growth converts to revenue growth at a 50-60% rate, and net income growth flattens to 3-5%. The BRL depreciates 10-15% on election uncertainty or EM stress, compressing USD EPS mechanically. The loan book seasons badly in its first downturn, forcing provisions that consume the margin the credit strategy was meant to defend. FY2027 EPS lands near R$10.50 rather than R$12.05, and the multiple compresses to 7x on a $1.93 USD EPS = $13.50. This is roughly the level the shares reached in June 2026 ($14.80 52-week low), so the market has recently expressed a version of this view.

### Base Case ($20.50, +20%)

Take-rate compression moderates but does not fully reverse — stabilizing near 1.15-1.18% as mix rotation offsets competitive pressure. Net income grows 8-10%, EPS 11-13% with buyback contribution, landing FY2027 EPS near R$11.60-12.05 ($2.16-2.32). The multiple holds at 9x, in line with where the stock trades today and with Brazilian financial peers. 9x on $2.28 = $20.50.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). We do not have sufficient evidence to underwrite the take-rate question, and that question dominates the outcome distribution.

**Base case target: $20.50** — 9x FY2027 consensus EPS of R$12.05 at 5.30 BRL/USD ($2.27), consistent with the current multiple and Brazilian financial peer range. **+20% from $17.06.**

**Bull case: $25.50** (+49%) — 11x on $2.32, requiring take-rate recovery toward 1.25% and net income reacceleration to the mid-teens.

**Bear case: $13.50** (-21%) — 7x on $1.93, requiring structural fee compression plus BRL depreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** the 2Q26 release, expected mid-to-late August 2026, is the first hard test. The October 2026 Brazilian general election is the second.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Retail take rate stabilizes at 1.18% or higher for two consecutive quarters**, with the company attributing stabilization to mix rotation rather than one-off items. This is the single decisive datapoint.
2. **Adjusted net income growth reaccelerates above 10% YoY** with retail revenue growth converging toward client-asset growth (currently 10% vs 15%).
3. **Price declines to $14.00-14.50** without further fundamental deterioration — roughly 7x FY2026 consensus, at which point the bear case is largely in the price and the 5-6% shareholder yield plus 20.7% capital ratio provide support.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Take rate falls below 1.13%** (a further 5bps), confirming continued 2bps-per-quarter structural compression.
2. **Credit provisions or non-performing loans rise materially** on the R$67bn book, indicating the collateralized-lending margin defense is generating losses rather than income.
3. **Consensus FY2027 EPS revisions turn down more than 5% over 90 days** (currently -2.4%), or a fourth consecutive miss with widening magnitude.
4. **Brazilian tax legislation on exempt fixed-income instruments (LCI/LCA, incentivized debentures, real-estate funds) advances**, directly impairing the product set XP distributes.

**Monitoring items:** whether the incoming CFO's first communications (2Q26 call, following the August 3 start) provide a credible, quantified explanation of take-rate dynamics; net inflow disclosure on a consistent quarterly retail basis; BRL/USD; and whether share cancellations continue to outpace equity-compensation issuance.

## Conclusion

XP is a well-capitalized franchise earning 21.7% on equity, distributing roughly 39% of earnings through dividends and genuinely cancelled buybacks, trading at 8.3-8.9x forward earnings. Those facts describe a stock that should be cheap only for a reason, and the reason is identifiable: the revenue XP captures per unit of client assets has fallen 5.6% in three quarters, adjusted net income growth has more than halved, three of the last four quarters missed consensus, and estimates are drifting lower.

Whether that compression is a high-rate mix artifact that reverses with Selic cuts or permanent fee erosion from adviser competition determines whether the shares are worth $25 or $13. Disclosed information does not answer it, and the next credible answer arrives from a new CFO at the 2Q26 print. With 95.8% of revenue in Brazil ahead of an October general election, a loan book growing 33% annually that has never seen a downturn, and options markets pricing a 19% move around the September expiry, the correct posture is to hold existing exposure, size it modestly, and wait for the take rate to give a second reading before committing new capital. The upgrade trigger is specific and near-dated: take rate at or above 1.18% for two consecutive quarters, or a price near $14 that pays for the bear case.
