**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Evercore is the largest of the independent investment-banking advisory firms, and it is participating fully in the 2025-2026 reopening of the M&A cycle. Q1 2026 was a record quarter: net revenue of $1.39B (+100% YoY), advisory fees of $1.24B (+123%), diluted EPS of $7.20 versus $3.48 a year earlier, and a comp ratio that improved to 65.0% as revenue outran compensation. The firm earned 148 fees of at least $1 million (up 54% YoY) and executed on 313 total advisory/underwriting mandates, confirming the franchise is winning outsized share of the largest transactions. Full-year consensus has moved from $18.17 to $19.85 over 90 days, and the earnings-surprise record is four straight beats.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the near-term setup is balanced, not asymmetric. The Q1 print is not a clean run-rate: management explicitly attributed a large portion of the quarter to an unusually high volume of *large* deal closings that pulled revenue forward, and it guided Q2 to be merely "closer to last year's record" — a sequential step-down. The headline EPS was further flattered by a 2.7% effective tax rate, driven by $88.5M of share-vesting excess tax benefits that cut the rate by 26.7 points; anyone annualizing $7.20 would badly overstate normalized earnings. The market recognized this, selling the stock down on a ~47% adjusted-EPS beat. Underneath the buyback headline, roughly half of the $621M repurchased in Q1 was net-settlement of vesting equity awards, so real share-count reduction is far smaller than it appears.

At $339.63 the stock trades at ~17x current-year consensus EPS and near the middle of its 52-week range ($265.87–$388.71), a slight premium to boutique peers Moelis and Houlihan Lokey on adjusted metrics but discount on trailing. The valuation is neither a value trap nor a clear mispricing; it fairly reflects a quality franchise in the middle innings of an advisory upcycle whose earnings power is genuine but whose quarterly cadence is lumpy and whose one-off distortions are large. We rate EVR HOLD (growth_hold) with a base-case target of $375, accumulating on weakness toward the low-$300s.

## Company Overview

Evercore operates two segments. Investment Banking & Equities generates roughly 98% of net revenue and comprises success-based M&A, divestiture, activism/defense, capital-raising, and restructuring/liability-management advisory, plus a smaller equities business (research, agency trading, underwriting). In Q1 2026 this segment produced $1.37B of net revenue (+103% YoY) and $326.9M of pre-tax income. Investment Management (~2% of revenue) houses Evercore Wealth Management and Evercore Trust, plus passive private-equity interests and a 49% stake in Atalanta Sosnoff; wealth AUM was $15.1B at 3/31/26, down 3% in the quarter on market depreciation and net outflows. Investment Management is a stable modest contributor, not the growth engine.

The competitive model rests on senior banker talent (Senior Managing Directors). Scale within the independent-advisory niche gives Evercore credibility on the largest, most complex transactions, and the absence of a lending balance sheet or trading conflicts is a genuine selling point for advisory mandates. The firm employed roughly 2,635 people at quarter-end, up from 2,395 a year earlier, reflecting a deliberate, front-loaded talent-expansion strategy. John Weinberg is Chairman and CEO; Roger Altman is the founder. In H1 2025 league tables ranked Evercore #1 by deal value in financial-services M&A (~$38.6B advised), consistent with its positioning at the top of the mega-deal tier rather than the volume tier.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue rebounded ~29.5% to ~$3.88B, with net income up ~56% to ~$592M and ROE of ~31.5% — a capital-light, low-leverage franchise producing top-tier returns on equity. Q1 2026 accelerated that recovery sharply: net revenue $1.39B (+100% YoY), net income attributable to Evercore $301.2M (versus $146.2M), diluted EPS $7.20 (+107%).

Three items require adjustment before reading the quarter as run-rate. First, the effective tax rate was 2.7%, flattered by $88.5M of excess tax benefits from equity awards vesting above grant price — a recurring but volatile Q1 item that reduced the rate by 26.7 points. On a normalized ~27-28% rate, underlying profitability is materially lower. Second, management stated a disproportionate share of the quarter came from large-transaction closings that are inherently hard to repeat. Third, the comp ratio of 65.0% is a byproduct of revenue outrunning compensation in a strong quarter; comp is 65%+ structurally and rising in absolute dollars.

The balance sheet is conservative. Cash and investment securities were ~$2.0B at quarter-end against only ~$540M of private-placement senior notes laddered 2028-2033, all covenants in compliance, with no credit-facility draw (the PNC revolver was expanded from $85M to $225M). Q1 operating cash flow was negative $225.9M, which is normal seasonality — bonuses and deferred comp are paid in Q1 every year — and should not be read as a cash-quality concern.

Capital allocation is aggressive but partly a treadmill. Evercore repurchased 1.93M shares for $621M in Q1 (avg $322), split between $310M of open-market buybacks and $311M of net-settlement of vesting equity awards. With 1.63M RSUs granted in Q1 (~$533M grant value), the firm must buy back continuously just to hold Class A shares roughly flat near 38.7M. The dividend was raised to $0.89/quarter in April 2026 from $0.84, but the ~1.1% yield is a rounding item in the total-return case. The remaining authorization is ~6.2M shares of an 8.0M / $1.6B program.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is a cyclical advisory recovery layered on secular share gains by independent advisors. The 2026 rebound follows the 2022-2023 rate-shock M&A slump, and Evercore is capturing outsized share of the largest, most complex mandates. The estimate-revision picture is constructive but not uniform: current-year consensus has risen +9.3% over 90 days to $19.85, and next-year sits at $23.27. Notably, next-quarter estimates have been cut -8.2% over 90 days and -1.1% over 30 days, consistent with management's guidance that Q2 steps down from the Q1 blowout. This is a positive full-year trajectory alongside a soft near-term quarter — the definition of lumpiness in this business.

The earnings-surprise record is four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +46.6%, Q4 2025 +25.7%, Q3 2025 +5.9%, Q2 2025 +34.5%), which lends confidence to the full-year estimates but reflects deal-timing volatility rather than smooth compounding. Structural tailwinds support durability: independent advisors continue taking wallet share from bulge brackets on conflict-free positioning; restructuring/liability-management provides a natural counter-cyclical hedge within advisory; and talent expansion (240 net headcount adds YoY) plus the Robey Warshaw UK addition widen the mandate funnel. The offset is that new senior hires "do not begin to generate significant revenue in the year they are hired," so the expansion is a front-loaded cost that pressures near-term margins.

I do not apply the high-conviction inflection BUY signal here. While current-year revisions cleared +9% over 90 days, this is a cyclical recovery in a lumpy fee business rather than a structural earnings inflection off a trough, and the strongest revision signal (>30% over 90 days combined with accelerating revenue) is not present — the near-quarter estimate is actually falling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $339.63 the stock carries a ~17x multiple on current-year consensus EPS of $19.85 and ~14.6x on next-year $23.27. Peer medians in Investment Banking & Brokerage sit at a P/E of 21.8 (Stifel 13.9, StoneX 23.0, Houlihan Lokey 21.8, Piper Sandler 19.7, Moelis 23.2). On forward earnings, Evercore screens roughly in line with to modestly cheaper than the boutique cohort, with a lower dividend yield (~1.1% vs. peer median 1.9%).

The critical judgment is what earnings base to capitalize. The trailing multiple is distorted by the record Q1, and the reported EPS is inflated by the 2.7% tax rate. Capitalizing normalized rather than headline earnings, the stock is fairly valued — not a value trap, not an obvious mispricing. An external note in June 2026 pegged EVR near 18x, above its 10-year average, framing it as a quality business with limited upside after a record quarter, which aligns with our read.

I treat a cheap-looking trailing multiple with skepticism here; in this sector a low P/E on peak-cycle advisory earnings is more warning than opportunity, because the "E" mean-reverts as deal volumes normalize. Analyst targets (low $325, mean $383.6, median $390, high $435) place the current price near the low end of the range, offering ~15% upside to the median. Given that the stock trades below 30x forward with rising full-year estimates, I give some weight to the consensus target as a directional floor, but I do not treat it as a strong mispricing signal because the Q2 step-down is a known headwind that will pressure the print before the full-year picture reasserts. The valuation score reflects a balanced setup: modest peer discount and full-year momentum against a lumpy, partly-distorted earnings base with a treadmill buyback.

## Competitive Landscape

Evercore holds a strong and arguably improving position at the top of the independent-advisory tier. The #1 H1 2025 ranking by financial-services M&A deal value, 148 fees of ≥$1M (+54% YoY), and the surge in EMEA revenue to $219M (from $73M) on Robey Warshaw plus UK strength all point to franchise share gains at the high end of the market. The conflict-free, no-balance-sheet model remains a durable differentiator against bulge brackets for the largest mandates.

The competitive battleground is talent, and here the trajectory is favorable but expensive. The peer set (Lazard, PJT, Moelis, Centerview) competes for the same limited pool of rainmaker SMDs, keeping comp ratios structurally near 65%. Evercore's aggressive hiring (2,635 employees, up 10% YoY) signals confidence in the cycle but front-loads cost. Wealth Management is the one area of relative weakness — EWM lagged the S&P 500 by ~5-6% on 1- and 3-year bases with net outflows in Q1 — but it is a 2% segment and does not move the thesis. On balance, competitive trajectory is positive within a heterogeneous sector where quality is priced.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant near-term risk is the record-quarter-then-normalize dynamic. Advisory fees are success-based and lumpy, recognized on transaction close, and management explicitly flagged that an unusual concentration of large closings drove Q1 and won't repeat in Q2. The stock's negative reaction to a ~47% beat shows the market is already alert to the pull-forward and the unsustainable tax rate. This is a priced-in but genuine catalyst for a soft Q2 print.

Talent and compensation economics are the second axis. The entire model rests on retaining and hiring SMDs at a 65%+ comp ratio, with an elaborate deferred-comp web: Class K-P Units (~$254M probable-to-vest fair value), the 2025 LTIP (~$272M remaining expense through 2031), a deferred cash program (~$221M unrecognized), and ~$87M of employee loans. Robey Warshaw is effectively an acqui-hire structured as an acquisition — ~$84.3M of equity consideration is forfeitable over four years and treated as comp, plus a $100.8M first-anniversary payment and up to $25M+ contingent consideration through 2031, with an early $1.8M lease impairment already booked. Value creation there depends on retaining a handful of star UK bankers.

Two newer items warrant monitoring. First, the Q1 10-Q added disclosure of a UK judicial matter challenging the employment-tax treatment of partnership members; Evercore is not a party, but the outcome "may adversely impact the Company's tax position" given its LP structure and UK exposure. Second, a February 2026 Item 5.02 8-K indicated an executive/officer change that could not be independently confirmed within this research scope — a governance item to verify. The complex share structure (multiple LP unit classes, Class L Interests routing NEO comp through the partnership, EWM noncontrolling interests) creates ongoing exchange/dilution dynamics requiring continuous buyback offset. Litigation is otherwise clean — no material proceedings disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-complacent. Near-term ATM IV of 37.1% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical vol of 36.6% (spread +0.4%, "fair"), and IV percentile within the current chain is a low 4th percentile — the market is not pricing an imminent shock. The term structure is in contango (37.1% near vs. 41.5% at six months), consistent with normal, non-stressed positioning. One flag for a bull thesis: near-term put/call skew of 8.4% shows elevated put demand at the front expiry, though the put/call volume ratio (0.22) and OI ratio (0.11) are heavily call-tilted overall. IV collapsed from 181.6% to 37.1% since the prior snapshot, reflecting the passing of the Q1 earnings event. The implied ±8.5% move at the nearest expiry captures the market's expectation of a lumpy quarterly print. Net, options are not contradicting a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($435 / +28%).** The M&A cycle broadens and deepens through 2026-2027, independent advisors continue taking bulge-bracket wallet share, and Evercore's mega-deal franchise sustains elevated large-fee counts. Robey Warshaw retention holds and EMEA compounds off the new higher base. Talent adds from the last two years convert to revenue, the comp ratio grinds toward the low-60s on operating leverage, and full-year EPS beats the $19.85 consensus, driving continued upward revisions and a re-rating toward peer multiples.

**Bear case (~$275 / -19%).** Q2 disappoints against the pulled-forward Q1, deal timing slips as financing costs and macro uncertainty (rate-path ambiguity, stagflation risk) delay closings, and full-year revisions roll over. The normalized tax rate reasserts, compressing reported EPS, and the market re-prices the stock toward the low end of its range as the "peak-cycle earnings" concern crystallizes. Comp inflation from front-loaded hiring pressures margins in a softer revenue year.

**Base case ($375 / +10%).** The advisory recovery is real and durable, but the near-term cadence is choppy. Q2 steps down as guided, the full year lands near or modestly above consensus, and the stock drifts toward the analyst median of $390 as the year progresses, tempered by the known Q2 air pocket and the treadmill buyback. This is a quality franchise fairly priced, worth holding for the cycle without an asymmetric near-term edge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Horizon: 12 months, with the key checkpoints being the Q2 2026 print (the guided sequential step-down), and subsequent quarters confirming whether full-year estimates hold above $19.85.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 prints demonstrate the large-fee run-rate is durable rather than a Q1 pull-forward (advisory fees holding above prior-year comps ex the tax windfall), with current-year estimate revisions continuing to rise.
- A pullback toward the low-$300s or high-$290s, restoring asymmetric risk/reward (a director bought ~2,000 shares at ~$290 in March 2026, a reasonable reference for value).
- Comp ratio sustaining below 64% on operating leverage, confirming margin expansion into the cycle.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Q2 miss coupled with full-year estimate cuts and evidence deal closings are slipping structurally (not just seasonally).
- Departure of key SMDs or Robey Warshaw principals, or the UK partnership-tax matter resolving adversely with a quantified hit to the effective tax rate.
- Comp ratio inflecting back toward 67%+ as front-loaded hiring outpaces revenue.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, then the Q3 print to confirm the full-year trajectory.

## Conclusion

Evercore is a high-quality franchise executing well in a genuine advisory upcycle, and the score trajectory (6.5 → 7.3 → 6.5) reflects a stock that briefly looked cheap on rising estimates and now looks fairly priced after the run. Current data confirms the improving financial health (ROE ~31.5%, fortress balance sheet, rising full-year estimates) but also validates the pullback to HOLD: the record Q1 is not a clean run-rate given the 2.7% tax rate and large-closing concentration, management guided Q2 lower, next-quarter estimates are falling, and the buyback is partly a dilution treadmill. Management Quality has eased from its prior peak, appropriately, on the treadmill buyback, the LP-routed Class L comp mechanism, and the mixed insider signal (a small director buy at $290 against routine CFO-level selling). At ~17x forward with ~10% upside to our $375 base case and a bear case ~19% lower, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate EVR HOLD (growth_hold) and would become more constructive on a pullback to the low-$300s or on evidence the elevated large-fee run-rate is durable.
