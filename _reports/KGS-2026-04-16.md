## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest independent provider of large horsepower contract compression infrastructure in the United States, with 4.46 million HP deployed at 97.7% utilization across the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. The underlying business quality is strong: adjusted EBITDA margins of 54.7%, operating cash flow that nearly doubled to $600M in 2025, and pricing power supported by equipment scarcity and outsourcing trends. Management has executed well, clearing the EQT private equity overhang and compounding total shareholder return of 271% since IPO versus 174% for peers.

The problem is valuation and leverage. KGS trades at ~69x trailing earnings and approximately 23x FY2026E EPS ($2.71), a 4-5x premium to the OFS peer median P/E of 14.2x. The stock has appreciated 84% in 12 months, with a near-vertical move from $42 to $63 following the February DPS acquisition announcement and Q4 earnings. Pro-forma debt stands at approximately $3.6B+ after the $1B March 2026 notes offering, placing the Antman Z-Score at 1.17 (distress zone) and leverage at 4.5-5.0x adjusted EBITDA. An open DOJ/OFAC investigation into cartel-linked payments from its former Mexican subsidiary introduces unquantifiable tail risk.

The DPS acquisition adds a compelling AI/data center distributed power thesis, and estimate revisions are sharply positive (+31.9% for FY2026E in 30 days). These are real catalysts. But at the current price, the stock has already moved to price in significant upside from DPS and continued compression demand strength, leaving minimal margin of safety against the balance sheet risk, the DOJ investigation, and the cyclical nature of the energy services sector.

## Company Overview

Kodiak Gas Services operates compression infrastructure — equipment that enables the production, gathering, and transportation of natural gas and oil — under fixed-revenue, long-term contracts. The Contract Services segment generates approximately 90% of revenue, with the remaining 10% from station construction, maintenance, and other ancillary services.

The company's fleet consists of 4,736 compression units averaging 970 HP per unit (up 4.7% YoY), reflecting a strategic focus on large horsepower equipment that commands higher pricing and presents higher barriers to entry. Geographic concentration is high: 82.8% of assets sit in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale.

On April 1, 2026, KGS completed the acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions (DPS) for approximately $675M ($587M cash + 2.4M shares), adding ~395 MW of distributed power generation capacity. Rebranded as Kodiak Power Solutions, this business extends the platform into data centers, microgrids, and manufacturing — a strategic bet on the convergence of energy infrastructure and AI-driven power demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

Revenue grew from $850M (2023) to $1,159M (2024, +36.3%, driven by CSI acquisition) to $1,308M (2025, +12.8%, organic). The 2025 growth was driven by pricing increases and higher revenue-generating HP per unit. Adjusted EBITDA reached $715M in 2025 (+17.3%), with margins expanding to 54.7% from 52.6%. Q4 2025 Contract Services adjusted gross margin hit a record 69.2%.

Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $600M from $328M, driven by $90.6M higher operating income and a $165M favorable working capital swing. GAAP free cash flow was approximately $284M after $316M of total capex ($200M growth + $76M maintenance + $40M other).

**Earnings Quality Concern**

GAAP net income was $81.1M in 2025, or $0.89 per diluted share. Yet adjusted EPS was $1.54, reflecting substantial add-backs — primarily stock-based comp, amortization of intangibles, and non-cash items. The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is material and growing. The trailing P/E of 69x is based on GAAP EPS; on adjusted EPS, the multiple is approximately 41x trailing. Neither is cheap.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats followed by two misses in the most recent quarters (Q3 2025: -29.6%, Q4 2025: -19.2%). This deterioration in forecast accuracy raises questions about forward estimate reliability despite the strong upward revision trend.

**Balance Sheet**

This is the central risk. Pre-DPS debt was $2.6B. The $1B March 2026 notes offering (upsized from $750M) pushed pro-forma long-term debt to approximately $3.6B+. Cash on hand is negligible ($3.2M), though $1.5B of ABL availability provides liquidity. The debt/equity ratio of 2.58x and Altman Z-Score of 1.17 place KGS in statistically elevated risk territory.

Interest expense was $198M in 2025 and will likely rise to $240-260M+ annually on the larger debt stack. Against guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $750-780M (excluding DPS), interest consumes approximately 31-35% of EBITDA. Maturity profile: $750M at 7.25% due 2029, ~$465M due 2030, $770M due 2033, $630M due 2035, plus the new 2026 issuance. The 2029 maturity will require refinancing at rates that may be higher than current levels given the macro trajectory.

**Capital Return**

KGS returned $264M to shareholders in 2025 ($160M dividends + $104M buybacks). The current quarterly dividend of $0.49/share ($1.96 annualized) yields approximately 3.1%. This is a meaningful payout, but given the leverage, the sustainability of both dividends and buybacks depends on continued EBITDA growth.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Catalysts**

1. **DPS/AI Power Thesis:** The Kodiak Power Solutions platform positions KGS at the intersection of energy infrastructure and data center power demand. Management expects immediate EPS and DCF/share accretion. The AI-driven power demand cycle is real — hyperscaler and semiconductor earnings confirm accelerating hardware buildout, which translates directly to data center power consumption.

2. **Estimate Revisions:** FY2026E EPS revised up 31.9% in 30 days (from $2.05 to $2.71). Next quarter estimates up 54.9% in 30 days. This momentum reflects DPS accretion being modeled in and improved compression economics.

3. **Pricing Power and Utilization:** 97.7% utilization at record margins demonstrates genuine pricing power in a supply-constrained market. Management guided $750-780M EBITDA for 2026 (ex-DPS), implying 5-9% organic growth.

4. **LNG Export Buildout:** Growing Gulf Coast LNG capacity drives incremental compression demand for gas gathering and processing.

5. **Tax Tailwinds:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) reinstated full capital expense deductions, materially benefiting KGS's capital-intensive model.

**Growth Risks**

1. The DPS acquisition takes KGS into a new market with different competitive dynamics. Integration risk is real.
2. Two consecutive earnings misses (Q3 and Q4 2025) undermine confidence in forward estimates despite the revision trend.
3. ~9% of revenue-generating HP is on month-to-month contracts, creating vulnerable revenue.

Guidance for 2026 growth capex of $235-265M with ~150,000 new HP deployments is credible given utilization levels.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis breaks down. KGS trades at:

- **69.2x trailing GAAP P/E** — well above even growth software multiples
- **~23x FY2026E adjusted EPS** ($2.71) — still premium
- **~18.6x FY2027E adjusted EPS** ($3.31) — reasonable only if estimates are met
- **Peer median P/E: 14.2x** — KGS trades at approximately 4.9x the peer median on trailing, and approximately 1.6x on FY2027E estimates

The peer comparison is instructive. Archrock (AROC), the closest direct competitor, trades at 14.2x. Weatherford (WFRD) at 13.2x. Tidewater (TDW) at 7.6x. Even NOV at 40.1x is well below KGS's trailing multiple. The only justification for this premium is the DPS/data center optionality — investors are paying a "tech multiple" for what remains primarily an oilfield compression company.

On EV/EBITDA: with an enterprise value of approximately $9.2B ($5.6B market cap + $3.6B debt) against $750-780M guided EBITDA (ex-DPS), KGS trades at approximately 11.8-12.3x EV/EBITDA. If DPS adds $100-150M of incremental EBITDA over time, pro-forma EV/EBITDA drops to ~9.5-10.5x, which is more reasonable but still above peers.

The analyst consensus target of $60.33 (median $62.50) offers essentially no upside from the current $61.61 price. The low target of $36 implies 42% downside. The stock is at the 52-week high of $63.62.

The 84% appreciation in 12 months has consumed most of the upside from both the operational improvement and the DPS acquisition. At current prices, investors are paying full price for success and receiving no compensation for the leverage risk, the DOJ investigation, or the integration uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

KGS operates in an oligopoly-like U.S. contract compression market alongside Archrock (AROC) and USA Compression Partners (USAC). Barriers to entry are substantial: long equipment lead times, high capital requirements for large HP units, established customer relationships, and specialized engineering expertise.

KGS's competitive advantages are genuine:
- Largest independent fleet by HP
- Focus on large HP units (970 avg, growing) commands premium pricing
- 97.7% utilization is near theoretical maximum
- Fixed-revenue contracts provide cash flow visibility
- DPS adds a differentiated angle competitors lack

The outsourcing trend favors third-party compression providers as E&P companies prioritize capital discipline. Equipment scarcity supports continued pricing power through at least 2027.

The risk is that the DPS business competes in a different arena — distributed power generation — where the competitive dynamics are less favorable and KGS has no established track record.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ/OFAC/SEC Investigation (Material, Unquantifiable)**

During 2025, KGS discovered that its Mexican subsidiary (acquired via the CSI acquisition) made payments to persons associated with Foreign Terrorist Organizations and Specially Designated Global Terrorists — specifically criminal cartel organizations. KGS self-reported to the DOJ, OFAC, and SEC. The Mexico operations were divested at a $33.3M loss. The investigation is described as "substantially complete" but remains open.

Under U.S. anti-terrorism finance and OFAC sanctions laws, penalties can be severe. The company states it "does not expect" significant adverse impact but "cannot provide assurance as to the ultimate outcome." This risk is essentially unmentioned in sell-side coverage.

**Leverage and Credit Risk**

The contracted cash flow profile provides more stability than a typical Z-Score would suggest, but the degree of leverage leaves no margin for error in a downturn.

**Geographic Concentration (82.8% Permian/Eagle Ford)**

A regional production decline, water scarcity issue, or regulatory change concentrated in these basins would disproportionately impact KGS.

**Texas Sales Tax Exposure**

$102.3M in accrued sales tax contingent liabilities, up from $70.1M in 2024. Recent settlement of $28M in penalties/interest. Additional audit periods under review. This is a meaningful ongoing liability.

**Integration Risk**

DPS takes KGS into data center power, a new market. Management has successfully integrated CSI, but DPS represents a strategic pivot into unfamiliar territory.

Given two consecutive earnings misses and estimates revised up 31.9% in 30 days, the bar is high. A miss on the first DPS-inclusive quarter would likely trigger a meaningful correction. **Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Bear case of $35-42 from current $61.61 represents 32-43% downside. Bull case of $70-75 represents 14-22% upside. Risk/reward ratio is approximately 2:1 to 3:1 in favor of the short if the bear case materializes. However, the stock has strong momentum — up 84% in 12 months and just set a 52-week high.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** Despite favorable fundamental risk/reward, the combination of strong price momentum (stock at 52-week high), thin options liquidity preventing efficient expression, expensive borrow implied by elevated IV, and absence of a dated catalyst with clear timing makes this an avoid rather than an active short. The DOJ investigation is a latent risk without a timeline. The earnings setup is potentially favorable for shorts but is 4-6 weeks away. Revisit after Q1 earnings if estimates are missed.

## Conclusion

Kodiak Gas Services operates a high-quality compression infrastructure business with strong competitive positioning, expanding margins, and a potentially transformative AI/data center power platform in DPS. The operational execution has been strong, and the growth narrative is compelling.

The stock price already reflects this. At 69x trailing GAAP P/E, 23x FY2026E adjusted EPS, and a 4.9x premium to the OFS peer median, KGS offers essentially no margin of safety. Pro-forma leverage of ~$3.6B (4.5-5.0x EBITDA) in a higher-for-longer rate environment, an unresolved DOJ/OFAC investigation with potentially severe consequences, a recent pattern of earnings misses, and thin options liquidity all argue against initiating or holding a position at current levels. The unanimous sell-side Buy consensus (12/12 analysts) with a mean target of $60.33 — below the current price — underscores that the market has already overshot consensus estimates.

The underlying business deserves monitoring. A pullback to the $40-45 range (15x forward earnings, in line with peers) would present a more attractive entry point where the growth optionality from DPS and the contracted compression cash flows could compensate for the leverage and legal risks.
