## Executive Summary

Capri Holdings is a two-brand fashion company (Michael Kors, Jimmy Choo) trading at $18.48 with a $2.2B market cap following the December 2025 divestiture of Versace to Prada for $1.375B. The balance sheet has been materially de-leveraged (net debt declined from ~$1.6B to $80M), eliminating the most acute financial distress risk. However, the remaining business confronts a multi-year revenue decline (FY2022 $5.65B → FY2026 guidance ~$3.45B), structural brand positioning challenges at Michael Kors, and severe tariff headwinds from Southeast Asian manufacturing concentration.

The stock has been rated AVOID for three consecutive assessments, with scores gradually improving (3.6 → 4.1 → 4.3) primarily on balance sheet improvement. This report continues the AVOID recommendation. While the valuation of ~13x FY2026 EPS ($1.30-$1.40 guided) appears optically undemanding, Michael Kors' competitive position continues to deteriorate, and the turnaround thesis depends on management's ability to reverse years of over-distribution and promotional activity — a process that historically takes 3-5 years with no guarantee of success. The tariff overhang from Vietnam/Cambodia/Indonesia manufacturing creates an additional margin headwind precisely when the brand cannot afford to raise consumer prices further.

CEO John Idol's $989K open-market purchase at $17.98 in March 2026 represents genuine insider conviction, but single-executive buys at distressed companies have mixed predictive records. The 10.6% short interest and options market pricing (79.2% near-term IV vs. 48.4% historical) reflect institutional skepticism that appears warranted.

## Company Overview

Capri Holdings operates two fashion brands following the Versace divestiture:

**Michael Kors** (~85-90% of remaining revenue): Accessories, ready-to-wear, and footwear positioned as "accessible luxury." Operates 711 stores globally (399 full-price, 312 outlet) plus wholesale and e-commerce (~19% of revenue). Revenue has declined from ~$3.7B (FY2023) to ~$3.0B run-rate, with the Q3 FY2026 segment reporting -5.6% YoY.

**Jimmy Choo** (~10-15% of remaining revenue): Luxury shoes and accessories across 219 stores. Showed relative strength in Q3 FY2026 with +5% revenue growth ($167M). Better positioned in luxury price tiers but small in absolute contribution.

The company is BVI-incorporated, London-headquartered, with revenue split approximately 51% Americas, ~25% EMEA, ~24% Asia. Manufacturing is concentrated in Vietnam, Cambodia, Indonesia, and Bangladesh — all directly targeted by 2025 U.S. tariff actions.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance Sheet — Materially Improved:**
The Versace sale transformed the capital structure. Net debt fell from $1.6B to ~$80M, and the company is well within its 4.0x maximum net leverage covenant under the February 2025 credit facility ($1.5B revolver + $706M term loans). The Altman Z-Score of 0.56 reflects the pre-sale balance sheet and is no longer representative.

**Revenue — Still Declining:**
- FY2023: $5.62B
- FY2024: $5.17B (-8.0%)
- FY2025: $4.44B (-14.1%)
- FY2026 guidance: $3.45-$3.475B (reflects Versace removal + ~5-6% organic decline)

The remaining business is shrinking. Michael Kors declined 5.6% in Q3 FY2026, and management's stated expectation of "return to growth in FY2027" lacks specificity. Same-store sales trends remain negative.

**Profitability — Recovering from Impairment-Driven Losses:**
- FY2025 gross margin: 63.6% (down from 66.3% FY2023 due to promotional activity)
- Michael Kors operating margin: 11.3% FY2025 (down from 18.0% FY2024)
- $1.37B cumulative impairments in FY2024-2025 have cleared goodwill/intangible bases
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guided at $1.30-$1.40; Q3 came in at $0.81 vs. $0.79 consensus

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $281M
- FY2025 free cash flow: $153M (3.4% margin)
- Capex expected to decline to ~$110M in FY2026
- Cross-currency swap portfolio generates ~$117M in annual interest income (obscures true interest cost)

**Key Concern:** The $1.41 FY2026 EPS estimate relies on an already-declining revenue base. If Michael Kors revenue declines accelerate (tariffs, consumer weakness), the earnings base compresses further. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.87 implies organic margin expansion while the top line is under pressure — a combination that requires flawless execution.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are poor for the intermediate term:

1. **Michael Kors revenue has declined for 3 consecutive years.** The brand's strategy of reducing promotions and raising average unit retail is intentionally sacrificing near-term volume for long-term margin recovery. This creates a J-curve where revenue declines before margins expand — and the risk is that the revenue never recovers because consumers shift permanently to competitors.

2. **Management targets FY2027 for return to growth** (starting after March 2026). No quantified growth rate has been provided. The strategy involves: renovating 50% of stores over 3 years, increasing marketing to >8% of sales, reducing SKU count, and elevating product positioning.

3. **Jimmy Choo provides a modest positive offset** (+5% in Q3 FY2026) but at $600-700M annualized revenue, cannot compensate for Michael Kors declines.

4. **No new brand acquisition is on the horizon.** The company went from a 3-brand portfolio to 2, reducing diversification.

5. **Estimate revisions are modestly positive but negligible in magnitude:** Current quarter +5.3% (30-day), current year +0.4%. This does not trigger any inflection signal; it confirms the business isn't deteriorating faster than expected.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.48, CPRI trades at approximately:
- 13.1x FY2026 guided EPS ($1.41 consensus)
- 9.9x FY2027 EPS ($1.87 consensus)
- ~0.6x trailing revenue (estimated $3.5B)
- No dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 19.5x. CPRI's 13x looks cheap, but the discount reflects: (a) declining revenues vs. peers, (b) a turnaround execution premium, and (c) tariff headwinds that peers face to varying degrees. Carter's (CRI) at 15.1x with a 2.7% dividend and stable cash flows represents a more investable value proposition in the same sector.

The analyst consensus median target is $24 (30% upside), with a mean of $26.59 and a range of $20-$45.50. The low target of $20 being only 8% above the current price suggests limited downside consensus, while the wide range ($20-$45.50) reflects extreme uncertainty about the turnaround's success.

**Why the market may not be wrong at $18.48:** The FY2027 EPS estimate of $1.87 requires both revenue stabilization AND margin expansion. If Michael Kors revenue declines 5-8% instead of stabilizing, and tariffs compress gross margins by 200-300bp, FY2027 EPS could be $1.00-$1.20, making the current 13x multiple actually 15-18x a more realistic earnings estimate. The market may be correctly pricing execution risk rather than offering a discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Michael Kors occupies the most challenged segment of the fashion market — accessible luxury where brand equity is fragile and switching costs are zero.

**Competitive position is deteriorating:**
- Coach (Tapestry) has executed a successful brand elevation under creative director Stuart Vevers, gaining share in the segment Michael Kors is losing
- Ralph Lauren has re-established premium positioning with strong same-store sales growth
- Tory Burch, Kate Spade (Tapestry), and digitally-native brands (Polène, Mansur Gavriel) are taking share from below and above

**The core problem:** Michael Kors has 312 outlet stores — nearly as many as full-price locations (399). This level of outlet presence has historically been incompatible with brand elevation in fashion. Coach spent 5+ years (2015-2020) closing outlets and rebuilding brand perception. Michael Kors is earlier in an equivalent process.

**Jimmy Choo is better positioned** in luxury footwear with genuine competitive differentiation, but its small scale limits portfolio impact.

**Quantitative evidence of share loss:** Michael Kors Americas revenue declined from $2.3B (FY2023) to ~$1.7B (FY2025 estimate) — a 26% decline in the brand's home market over two years. This represents actual consumer abandonment, not merely sector headwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**Tier 1 Risks (High probability, material impact):**

1. **Tariff exposure:** At current tariff levels, gross margin compression of 200-400bp is plausible unless offset by price increases — which the brand cannot support given existing consumer softness. No guidance has been provided on tariff mitigation.

2. **Consumer spending compression (global):** Accessible luxury is historically the first discretionary category consumers cut during periods of macro uncertainty.

3. **Continued Michael Kors revenue decline:** If the "return to growth in FY2027" thesis fails and revenue declines another 5-8%, the entire earnings framework breaks down.

**Tier 2 Risks:**

4. **E-commerce platform instability:** "Unanticipated challenges" from the new platform have hurt results. Digital is ~19% of revenue.

5. **Wholesale channel deterioration:** Saks Global bankruptcy ($33M exposure), department store consolidation, and promotional pressure.

6. **Shareholder litigation:** Two class action complaints related to Tapestry merger statements. Uncertain financial exposure.

7. **Full valuation allowance on DTAs:** Limits near-term tax benefits from accumulated losses.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (79.2% near-term vs. 59.7% 6-month), indicating the market expects a near-term catalyst to resolve uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio of 0.12 (extremely low — dominated by calls) likely reflects speculative call positioning rather than institutional conviction. The implied move of ±16.4% on nearest expiry (~$15.50 to $21.50 range) confirms the market views this as a binary-outcome stock in the near term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($24-$28, +30-50%):**
- Michael Kors revenue stabilizes in FY2027 as reduced promotions + store renovations attract higher-value consumers
- Jimmy Choo growth accelerates to high-single-digits
- Tariff impact mitigated through supply chain shifts to non-targeted countries
- Gross margins recover 200bp+ as promotional activity normalizes
- FY2027 EPS reaches $1.80-$2.00, stock re-rates to 14-15x = $25-$30
- CEO insider purchase proves prescient

**Bear Case ($12-$14, -25-35%):**
- Michael Kors revenue decline continues at -5-8% through FY2027 as brand elevation fails
- Tariffs compress gross margins 300bp+, EPS falls to $0.80-$1.00
- Consumer spending weakness accelerates accessible luxury declines
- Store renovation capex consumes FCF without revenue payback
- Stock de-rates to 12-14x lower earnings = $10-$14
- Short thesis proves correct; 10.6% SI intensifies

**Base Case:** The turnaround is slow and uneven. FY2027 shows marginal improvement but not the acceleration bulls need. Revenue declines moderate to -2-3%, margins are flat due to tariff/marketing cost offsets. EPS of $1.40-$1.60. Stock oscillates between $16-$22 for 12-18 months.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q4 FY2026 earnings (likely May-June 2026) and initial FY2027 guidance will test the "return to growth" thesis. If guidance disappoints — particularly if tariff impacts are quantified — the stock has 20-25% downside to the $14-15 range.

**Timing window:** 1-3 months. The next earnings release is the nearest dated catalyst.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 10.6% with 4.1 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. Squeeze risk is moderate — not high enough to deter positioning, but requires active risk management.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV at 79.2% increases the cost of protective positioning. Put/call OI at 0.12 (call-dominated) suggests minimal put hedging — the short would be somewhat uncrowded in options space despite elevated SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $14 = 24% downside. Upside risk to $22 on positive news = 19% against. Risk/reward approximately 1.3:1 — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The risk/reward is insufficient for an active short. The balance sheet improvement limits catastrophic downside (no liquidity crisis), and the CEO insider purchase + analyst consensus of $24 create squeeze fuel. The correct positioning is to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive Michael Kors same-store sales growth — demonstrating the brand turnaround is gaining consumer traction
2. Quantified tariff mitigation plan showing gross margin preservation within 100bp of current levels
3. Stock price below $14 (creating 15x+ upside to base case on current estimates)

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist for at least 12-18 months. The turnaround, if successful, likely requires through FY2028 (March 2028) to demonstrate consistent growth. The tariff environment is unlikely to resolve before mid-2027 at earliest.

**Reassessment points:** Q4 FY2026 earnings (May-June 2026), FY2027 guidance release, any tariff policy clarification.

## Conclusion

Capri Holdings has improved from "distressed" to "challenged." The Versace sale eliminated balance sheet risk, and CEO Idol's insider purchase signals genuine management conviction. However, the core business faces structural competitive deterioration that pre-dates the current macro environment, compounded by tariff exposure that directly targets its manufacturing base. The consumer spending environment is hostile to accessible luxury positioned between mass-market and true premium.

At $18.48, the stock prices in modest pessimism but not the downside scenario where the turnaround fails. The 13x forward multiple on declining earnings is not a genuine discount when the earnings base is at risk of further compression. This remains an AVOID — the improving trajectory of prior scores reflects the balance sheet cleanup, but the competitive position score (3.0) is the binding constraint, and nothing in recent data suggests that trajectory reverses within the next 12 months.

The bar for upgrading to HOLD is specific: positive same-store sales at Michael Kors for two quarters. Until then, capital deployed here carries unfavorable risk/reward relative to opportunities in sectors where the system has demonstrated analytical edge.
