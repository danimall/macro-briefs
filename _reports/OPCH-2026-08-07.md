## Executive Summary

Option Care Health is the largest independent home and alternate-site infusion provider in the United States, operating 196 locations across 43 states with an in-house nursing network of roughly 2,700 clinicians. The economics are a hybrid of drug distribution and clinical service: the company is reimbursed on a percentage discount to average wholesale price or a percentage premium to average sales price. That architecture is the central fact for the investment case, because it means a decline in a drug's reference price compresses revenue *and* gross profit dollars per patient even when patient volume is unchanged. Three forces are pressing on that spread simultaneously: biosimilar entry, IRA Medicare price negotiation (the FY2025 10-K discloses a 66% list-price reduction on one portfolio therapy effective January 2026), and migration of biologics from IV to subcutaneous self-administration.

The consequence shows up cleanly in the numbers. Revenue growth decelerated from 13.0% in FY2025 to 1.6% in H1 2026. Gross margin has compressed 428 basis points from the FY2023 peak of 22.81% to 18.53% in Q2 2026. Gross profit dollars are now shrinking: $267.3M in Q2 2026 versus $269.0M, and $529.3M in H1 versus $532.1M. Management's disclosed chronic-inflammatory-disease gross-profit headwind for 2026 rose from a $25-35M range in the February 10-K to approximately $55M in the July 10-Q. The Q2 adjusted beat ($0.45 versus $0.42 non-GAAP consensus) was assembled from a $5.4M SG&A reduction attributed primarily to lower performance-based compensation, plus roughly $2.0M from "Other, net" swinging on the non-repeat of a prior-year unclaimed-property accrual. Neither is a repeatable source of earnings growth.

Against that, the balance sheet and capital allocation are genuinely good. Net debt of $979.1M is roughly 2.0x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, the term loan runs to 2032 at SOFR+1.75%, nothing material matures before the $506.8M of notes in October 2029, and the revolver was upsized to $850M in March 2026 and sits undrawn. Management repurchased 7.35 million shares in May 2026 at an average $20.41, the lowest prices of the cycle, while the CEO, CFO and two directors bought approximately $1.88M of stock on the open market in a four-day window. Shares outstanding have fallen 9.9% in 18 months. At 12.6x guided adjusted EPS and 9.3x EV/EBITDA the stock is not expensive. The problem is that FY2026 guidance requires an H1-to-H2 adjusted EBITDA step-up of 16-23% against roughly 7% seasonality the prior year, the working-capital cash tailwind that flattered H1 free cash flow is nearly exhausted, and the price has already recovered off the April low. We stay at HOLD, with the balance of evidence sitting closer to the REDUCE boundary than in prior reviews.

## Company Overview

Option Care Health operates a single reportable segment, infusion services. The Q2 2026 10-Q describes 86 full-service pharmacies and 187 ambulatory infusion suites, 32 of which have advanced practitioner capabilities, up from 27 in the FY2025 10-K, while pharmacy count fell by one. The suite counts are presented on different bases between the two filings, so the year-over-year suite comparison should be read directionally rather than precisely; the direction is a deliberate tilt toward higher-acuity, higher-margin sites of care.

The therapy book splits into acute (anti-infectives, parenteral and enteral nutrition, which are shorter duration, service-weighted, and less exposed to drug price) and chronic (immunoglobulin and neurology, chronic inflammatory disorders, rare and orphan, bleeding disorders, which are longer duration and drug-cost-weighted). Two assets look genuinely differentiating. Naven Health, the in-house national nursing network of roughly 2,700 nurses, gives OPCH control over the highest-friction part of home infusion, which competitors that subcontract nursing lack. Second, procurement scale: the 10-K states the national platform negotiates volume purchase rebates and vendor administration fees, and rebates receivable from manufacturers stood at $37.7M at 6/30/2026, recorded as a reduction of inventory and released to cost of revenue as inventory sells. That mechanism makes gross margin partly a function of purchasing behavior, not only pricing, which is relevant to the working-capital discussion below.

The company also holds two 50%-owned health-system infusion joint ventures carried at $30.2M, contributing $3.4M of equity earnings and $3.9M of management fee income in H1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and gross profit

| Period | Net revenue | YoY | Gross profit | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $4,302.3M | +9.1% | $981.2M | 22.81% |
| FY2024 | $4,998.2M | +16.2% | $1,013.0M | 20.27% |
| FY2025 | $5,649.5M | +13.0% | $1,087.9M | 19.26% |
| Q1 2026 | $1,350.7M | +1.3% | $262.0M | 19.40% |
| Q2 2026 | $1,442.4M | +1.9% | $267.3M | 18.53% |
| H1 2026 | $2,793.1M | +1.6% | $529.3M | 18.95% |

Two deteriorations happened at once: an approximately 11-point revenue growth deceleration in two quarters, and 428 basis points of gross margin compression from the FY2023 peak. The combination pushed gross profit dollars into decline for the first time in the period examined.

The Q2 10-Q decomposes it: acute revenue grew high-single-digit (down from mid-teens in FY2025), and chronic revenue was flat year over year, with growth in immunoglobulin/neurology and rare-and-orphan offset by a decline in the chronic inflammatory disease portfolio "primarily due to patient attrition and unfavorable therapy mix." The FY2025 acute surge was credited in the 10-K to "shifts in the competitive landscape, which increased the volume of patient service," a competitive-disruption windfall that appears to be annualizing out of the base. An alternative reading is ordinary capacity constraint; the filings do not resolve which.

### How the beat was constructed

SG&A fell from 12.6% of revenue in FY2024 to 12.1% in FY2025 to 11.4% in Q2 2026, a $5.4M (−3.2%) absolute reduction in Q2, attributed primarily to lower performance-based compensation plus efficiency initiatives. Reconciling Q2 pretax income of $73.99M against $68.86M a year earlier (+$5.13M): roughly $2.4M came from the operating line (the $5.4M SG&A cut less a $1.8M gross profit decline less $1.2M higher D&A), and roughly $2.0M came from "Other, net" swinging from −$0.8M to +$1.2M because a prior-year unclaimed-property accrual did not repeat. Essentially none of the improvement came from the gross profit engine. Lower bonus accruals reset when targets reset, and a non-repeating accrual is by definition non-repeating.

The bull-side reading deserves fair weight: 11.4% SG&A on a scaled national platform may be durable operating leverage rather than a one-year lever, and if gross profit merely stabilizes that leverage becomes a genuine earnings tailwind.

### Adjusted EBITDA and the H2 requirement

The proxy discloses a five-year adjusted EBITDA series: $342.9M (2022), $425.2M (2023), $443.8M (2024), $471.3M (2025). Margin compressed 154bp from the 2023 peak to 8.34%. FY2026 guidance of $480-495M implies roughly flat margin, i.e. SG&A savings offsetting gross margin erosion.

The critical arithmetic: Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $104.77M (filing fact, −6.3% YoY). Q2 2026 reconstructs from the 10-Q to approximately $117.0M (net income $53.913M + net interest $14.020M + tax $20.074M + D&A $18.382M + stock compensation $8.382M + roughly $2.2M restructuring and other), consistent with the externally reported figure. H1 2026 is therefore approximately $221.8M, and the $480-495M guide requires H2 of $258.2-273.2M, a **16.4% to 23.2% step-up over H1**. The prior year's comparable pattern was roughly +7% (H1 2025 approximately $227-228M against H2 2025 approximately $243-244M). Framed year over year, the guide needs H2 adjusted EBITDA growth of roughly +6% to +12% after H1 declined roughly 2.7%.

That is an inflection assumption, not an extrapolation. The bottom end ($480M, implying H2 +6% YoY) is defensible if CID has genuinely troughed and SG&A savings annualize; the top end looks difficult. The top of the range was already trimmed $10M at the Q2 print, from $505M to $495M.

### Cash flow quality

H1 2026 operating cash flow of $171.5M was up $88.4M year over year. The inventory line in the cash flow statement swung from a $12.7M use to a $71.5M source, an $84.3M swing that by itself more than accounts for the entire improvement. The balance-sheet trail confirms it: inventories went $388.1M (12/31/24) to $471.1M (12/31/25, a $83.0M build the 10-K attributed to "strategic purchases and a focus on maximizing the impact from volume based rebates") to $399.6M (6/30/26). Roughly 86% of the 2025 build has been unwound, leaving about $11M of further release available. Ex-inventory, H1 operating cash flow was approximately $99.9M.

Management frames this as durable supply-chain improvement. The balance-sheet arithmetic is equally consistent with reversing a one-time rebate-capture purchase, and unwinding it may carry a small gross-margin cost since the build served to capture rebates and prompt-pay discounts. Either way, the guided "at least $320M" FY2026 operating cash flow versus $258.4M actual in FY2025 is substantially a working-capital event. Normalizing the $71.5M release, free cash flow is closer to $205M than $277M, taking the yield from 7.8% to 5.8% on market cap.

Receivables warrant monitoring: implied DSO moved from 29.9 days (FY2024) to 30.6 (FY2025) to roughly 32.4 at 6/30/2026. Earned-but-unbilled gross receivables were $154.9M, essentially flat versus year-end, so the unbilled component is not the source.

### Balance sheet

Term loan $672.9M at SOFR+1.75% (5.37% at 6/30/26) maturing September 2032; $500M of 4.375% notes due October 2029; $850M revolver undrawn with $846.0M available. Total principal $1,172.9M against $193.8M cash for net debt of $979.1M, or approximately 2.0x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA. The maturity ladder is $3.4M for the rest of 2026, $6.8M each in 2027-2028, then $506.8M in 2029. Altman Z of 3.02 and a current ratio of 1.47 at 6/30/2026 are consistent.

The offsets are that goodwill of $1,606.7M is 47.6% of total assets and exceeds book equity of $1,259.9M; adding $290.6M of other intangibles puts tangible book equity at approximately −$637M. Goodwill is tested annually in Q4 on a qualitative basis, and that test now follows a roughly 30% decline in the share price. Any charge would be non-cash, affecting GAAP net income, book equity and GAAP EPS but not adjusted EBITDA, cash flow, or covenant compliance under typical credit-agreement definitions. Market capitalization of $3.54B still substantially exceeds book equity of $1.26B, so market evidence does not currently point to entity-level impairment.

Cash has fallen from $412.6M to $193.8M over 18 months as buybacks (approximately $733M cumulative FY2024 through H1 2026) exceeded cumulative net income (approximately $519M), with the gap funded from the cash balance and a $49.6M incremental term loan. The $450M revolver upsize now supplies the flexibility cash formerly did. Score: **financial health 6.2** — comfortable leverage and maturity profile, offset by a thinner cash cushion, declining gross profit dollars, and headline free cash flow that overstates the run rate.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus EPS estimates are $1.88 for the current year (−3.2% over 90 days but +2.4% over 30 days) and $2.08 for next year (+0.7% over 90 days, +1.4% over 30 days). The pattern is a large downward reset in the spring followed by a modest upward drift after the Q2 print. This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection; it is stabilization at a lower level.

The composition of the $1.88 to $2.08 progression matters more. With adjusted EBITDA guided roughly flat and share count falling 6-7% annually, most of the per-share growth is arithmetic from the buyback rather than operating expansion. Q1 2026 illustrates the mechanism precisely: GAAP net income −3.0% but GAAP diluted EPS +3.6%; adjusted EBITDA −6.3% but adjusted EPS flat. Q2 2026: net income +6.7%, GAAP diluted EPS +12.9%, on diluted shares down 6.5%. Investors tracking this business should follow adjusted EBITDA, not adjusted EPS.

The structural growth question is whether the chronic book stabilizes. Three forces are operating in the chronic inflammatory category: biosimilar entry compressing reference prices, IRA Medicare negotiation (the disclosed 66% list-price reduction effective January 2026), and IV-to-subcutaneous conversion. The first two compress margin per patient; the third removes the patient entirely. The 10-Q cites "patient attrition" without identifying the cause, and the filings do not disambiguate between subcutaneous conversion, share loss to vertically integrated competitors, and prescriber migration to agents outside OPCH's channel. Subcutaneous conversion is the most consequential because it is permanent volume destruction rather than a price reset, and the 10-K risk factors flag exactly that mechanism.

The offsetting engines are acute therapies (service-weighted, less drug-price-exposed) and rare/orphan. But the 10-K describes rare and orphan as "certain higher cost therapies" contributing to margin compression, so the growth categories carry lower percentage margins. OPCH must add volume faster simply to hold gross profit flat. Management's characterization of CID as "stabilizing" on the Q2 call is the single most important forward claim in the story, and it is currently unverified by the gross profit line. Score: **growth prospects 4.5**, unchanged from the prior review; the acute line holding at high-single-digit and the 30-day estimate uptick are marginal positives, but gross profit dollars are still declining.

## Competitive Landscape

OPCH's advantages are national scale (196 locations, 43 states, roughly 5,000 clinicians) that supports national payer contracts and limited-distribution launch partnerships with manufacturers; the in-house Naven Health nursing network; health-system referral access through partnerships and the two joint ventures; and the ambulatory infusion suite footprint, particularly the 32 with advanced practitioner capability, which is the site of care payers most want to steer toward because it is cheaper than hospital outpatient. The company also earns fees from biotech manufacturers for bona fide clinical-outcomes services, a higher-margin, non-drug revenue stream.

The structural disadvantage is stated plainly in the 10-K. Named competitors include Optum Infusion Pharmacy (UnitedHealth), Coram CVS (CVS Health), Amerita (BrightSpring), plus specialty pharmacies Accredo, CVS Caremark and Optum Rx. The risk factor concedes that competitors under common control with wholesalers, managed care organizations, PBMs or retail chains "may be better positioned with respect to the cost-effective distribution of pharmaceuticals" and may have supply arrangements "on price terms substantially more favorable." Being the largest *independent* provider is simultaneously the differentiator and the vulnerability: its largest competitors are owned by the payers that set its rates. When a vertically integrated payer moves a patient in-house, OPCH loses the patient without a pricing negotiation ever occurring.

Concentration is not improving. The largest payer was 14% of revenue in Q2 2026 and FY2025 versus 15% in Q2 2025, flat to marginally lower rather than diversification. The payer mix is shifting toward government: government revenue grew 11.0% in Q2 ($186.4M) while commercial grew 0.4% ($1,236.6M), taking direct government programs to 13% of revenue from 12%. That is a margin consideration, since government rates are typically lower, and should not be read as diversification. Supplier concentration disclosures are not comparable across filings (68% from four vendors in FY2025 versus 57% from three in Q2 2026), so direction cannot be determined.

Chronic revenue running flat while acute decelerates from mid-teens to high-single-digit is the observable trajectory. Score: **competitive position 5.0** — defensible scale assets, deteriorating trajectory, and a structural asymmetry against payer-owned rivals.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement spread compression.** The 10-K discloses the IRA outcome as a filing fact: a 66% reduction from 2023 list price for one portfolio therapy effective January 2026, with the direct and indirect impact expected to hurt results. Because payment is a percentage of AWP or a premium to ASP, reference-price cuts reduce gross profit dollars at unchanged volume. Future negotiation rounds extend this open-endedly. The specific therapy is not named.

**CID headwind revision.** The disclosed 2026 gross-profit headwind moved from "$25 million to $35 million" (February 10-K) to "approximately $55 million" (July 10-Q). The scopes differ — the 10-K figure covered chronic mix including rare/orphan *plus* biosimilar adoption, while $55M is CID-specific — so this is a materially larger disclosed headwind rather than a verified doubling. The 10-Q's "continues to expect" phrasing suggests the figure was set at the April guidance revision, which coincided with a roughly 29% price decline.

**H2 guidance risk (the live binary).** The required H1-to-H2 adjusted EBITDA step-up resolves at the Q3 print in late October. A shortfall to $470M full-year would imply adjusted EPS near $1.82 and likely multiple compression.

**Goodwill test.** The Q4 2026 annual test follows a 30% price decline, raising the odds of a quantitative test and, at the margin, a charge. Non-cash, but a headline event against already-negative tangible book equity.

**Legacy escheatment liability.** A voluntary disclosure agreement program relating to pre-merger BioScrip operations drove "Other, net" from +$4.8M in FY2024 to −$7.9M in FY2025, implying roughly $8M of accrual, and both filings state the matters are ongoing with undisclosed ultimate size.

**Interest rate cap expiry.** The $300M notional cap rolls off around November 2026, having contributed $3.4M of benefit in H1 2026. After that the full $672.9M term loan is unhedged floating, modest in scale, and offset if SOFR falls.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** Note 14 of the FY2025 10-K (commitments and contingencies) was not read, and the risk factors reference material proceedings described there. An 8-K filed 2026-05-05 under Item 5.02 (director/officer departure or election) three days before the insider buying cluster, and an 8-K filed 2026-05-12 under Item 7.01 with exhibits, were also not retrieved. The FY2026 operating cash flow guidance of "at least $320M" is a filing fact only as of April 30; whether it was reaffirmed on July 29 is unconfirmed, and the free cash flow yield calculation depends on it. These are genuine verification gaps, not established negatives.

The company did **not** add a risk factor for the CID/attrition dynamic in the Q2 10-Q despite raising the quantified headwind to $55M, relying instead on the existing generic factor covering lower-margin drug adoption.

Score: **risk factors 4.8** — the dated H2 guidance binary, an undiagnosed attrition cause with materially different durability implications, and the year-end goodwill test are specific downside catalysts. Much of the reimbursement risk is already in a drawdown of roughly 36% from the 52-week high, which prevents a lower score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.4% sits essentially on top of 44.1% one-year realized (spread −0.7%), and the term structure is flat (43.4% near versus 44.1% at six months). Event volatility has been crushed: ATM IV fell from 91.1% on July 19 to 39.7% on August 7 as the earnings print resolved. Options are therefore neither cheap nor rich, and the market is not pricing a near-term catalyst; the H2 guidance risk resolving in late October falls outside the heavily traded near tenors and is priced at 38.1% for the September expiry.

Positioning is mixed and should be read with care. The 3-month put/call volume ratio of 9.33 and a 4.46 aggregate ratio indicate active put buying, and the reported 4.1% put/call skew is characterized as elevated put demand. Against that, put/call open interest of 0.03 is negligible, and the chain is thin (32 contracts across four expirations), so flow ratios on a low base carry limited information. Short interest of 12.5% of float with 5.3 days to cover is the more substantive bearish signal, and it is consistent with the fundamental concern rather than contradicting it. Nothing in the options data challenges a HOLD; the elevated put flow and short interest are consistent with the view that H2 guidance is the unresolved item.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $31 (+31%)

CID has genuinely troughed, as management asserted on the Q2 call. Acute holds high-single-digit growth, immunoglobulin/neurology and rare/orphan continue to add gross profit dollars, and the CID drag annualizes out of the base by H1 2027, allowing gross profit dollars to resume growth in 2027. The 11.4% SG&A ratio proves durable operating leverage rather than a bonus-accrual artifact, delivering adjusted EBITDA at or above $490M in 2026 and $520M+ in 2027. The company continues deploying the $525M remaining authorization at prices in the low-to-mid $20s, taking share count toward 140M. On that path FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $2.20 and the multiple re-rates to 14x, a level appropriate for a scaled national platform with 2.0x leverage and growing gross profit, producing $31. The insider cluster buy and the $150M repurchase at an average $20.41 are the two parties with the best visibility into CID acting on exactly this view.

### Bear case — $18 (−24%)

The CID attrition is IV-to-subcutaneous conversion, which is permanent volume destruction rather than a price reset. Chronic revenue turns negative in H2, the acute competitive windfall fully annualizes out, and gross profit dollars decline through 2027. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands at $470-478M, below the guided range, forcing another reset in October. Working capital provides no further help — only about $11M of inventory release remains — so free cash flow reverts toward $200-210M, and the buyback pace slows or is funded on the revolver. Further IRA negotiation rounds extend the reference-price compression, and the Q4 goodwill test produces a non-cash charge against already-negative tangible book equity. On $1.80 of adjusted EPS at 10x, the stock returns to $18, near the April low.

### Base case — $26 (+10%)

CID stabilizes but does not recover; gross profit dollars run roughly flat through 2027. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands in the lower half of guidance, $480-487M, with adjusted EPS at $1.85-1.88. Continued buybacks at 6-7% annually deliver FY2027 adjusted EPS near consensus $2.08. The multiple holds at 12.5x, producing $26. Probability-weighting the three cases (50% base, 20% bull, 30% bear) gives an expected return of roughly +4%, below a reasonable cost of equity, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE.

**Rating: HOLD (fifth consecutive). **Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026. Secondary checkpoints: retrieval of the FY2025 10-K Note 14 contingencies detail and the two unread May 8-Ks; the Q4 goodwill test disclosure with the FY2026 10-K in February 2027; interest rate cap expiry around November 2026.

## Conclusion

Option Care Health is a well-financed, well-run business operating in a structurally compressing revenue model. The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case: 2.0x net leverage, nothing material due until October 2029, an $850M undrawn revolver, and a management team that repurchased 7.35 million shares at the cycle low while the CEO and CFO added personal capital in the same week. That combination is the most credible piece of forward-looking evidence available.

The weakness is equally clear.
