**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Certara is a biosimulation software and services company whose products are embedded in the drug-development workflows of roughly 160,000 users and 20 global regulatory agencies, including the FDA, MHRA, PMDA, and NMPA. The core asset is regulatory entrenchment: customers using Certara tools have been involved in over 90% of FDA new drug approvals since 2014, and the models must be transparent and explainable to survive regulatory review, which favors a validated incumbent over open-source or unvalidated AI alternatives. The financial signature is a cash-generative business masked by GAAP losses: FY2025 produced $94.6M of free cash flow (22.6% margin) and ~$134.5M of adjusted EBITDA, while reported net income was -$1.6M because of ~$56M in acquisition-related intangible amortization and a distorted 120.9% effective tax rate from valuation allowances.

The problem is the growth trajectory. FY2025 revenue grew 9% to $418.8M, but roughly 3 points came from the Chemaxon acquisition; organic growth was ~6% and decelerating. Q1'26 revenue rose only 1% YoY, with Software +7% but Services -4%, adjusted EBITDA contracting to $31.7M from $34.8M, and adjusted diluted EPS falling to $0.09 from $0.14. Software net retention slipped from 108.8% in 2024 to 105.3% in 2025. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen roughly 15% over the past 90 days across all forward periods, and the company missed EPS in three of the last four quarters. The stock is down 34% since the prior report window and sits at $6.93 versus a 52-week high of $13.88.

FY2026 is a reset year. New CEO Jon Resnick and new CFO John Gallagher took over by mid-2026 after a string of officer-change filings, and the company sold its medical-writing and regulatory-services business to Veristat for $85M cash plus escrow and earn-out, booking an expected loss of $28.9M–$53.7M. The divestiture sharpens focus on higher-margin biosimulation software and reduces direct FDA-disruption exposure, but confirms the divested asset was underperforming and removes revenue. The bull case rests on durable FCF, a regulatory moat, and buybacks at depressed prices; the bear case rests on slowing organic growth, FDA/NIH funding disruption threatening customer demand, goodwill concentration, and PE-sponsor overhang. With the growth story deteriorating and no identifiable near-term catalyst to reverse it, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CERT a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Certara, headquartered in Radnor, PA, operates in Model-Informed Drug Development (MIDD): the use of mathematical and biological models to simulate drug behavior in the body, reducing the cost and raising the success probability of pharmaceutical R&D. The company reports as a single operating segment across three reporting units: Certara Data Science Software, Certara Predictive Technologies, and Certara Drug Development Services.

Revenue splits into two streams. Software generated $183.3M in FY2025 (44% of revenue, ~18% growth), sold as multi-year subscriptions and licenses with recurring characteristics. Services generated $235.6M (56%, ~3% growth) and is the lower-margin, more cyclical component; the Veristat divestiture removes a portion of this. The workforce is unusually scientific, with 414 employees holding PhDs or MDs, and scientists doubling as sales and technical resources. Arsenal Capital Partners, the private-equity sponsor, owns approximately 22.8% with two board seats; insiders plus Arsenal hold roughly 25.2%.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year revenue path shows steady top-line growth flattered by acquisitions: $335.6M (2022), $354.3M (2023, +5.6%), $385.1M (2024, +8.7%), $418.8M (2025, +9%). GAAP net income was volatile and mostly negative: +$14.7M (2022), -$55.4M (2023, including a $47M goodwill impairment), -$12.1M (2024), -$1.6M (2025). Adjusted EBITDA has been stable at $120M–$134.5M across the period, and operating income turned positive in 2025 (+$21M) for the first time since 2022.

The critical interpretive point is that GAAP losses do not reflect cash economics. FY2025 free cash flow was $94.6M (22.6% margin), adjusted EBITDA margin was ~32%, and the reported loss stemmed from ~$56M/year of acquisition-related intangible amortization plus a 120.9% effective tax rate driven by a $29.4M valuation allowance and non-deductible items. An investor screening on the -$0.01 trailing EPS or the negative headline P/E would misjudge the underlying business. This is the strongest element of the story and supports a financial-health score above the sector median.

Q1'26 is where the deterioration is visible. Revenue was $106.9M, up only 1% YoY, with Services down 4%. Net loss was -$8.8M versus +$4.7M in Q1'25, driven by a $7.4M contingent-consideration expense and a 49% jump in G&A. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $31.7M from $34.8M, and adjusted diluted EPS fell to $0.09 from $0.14, a genuine step-down in profitability rather than an accounting artifact. Bookings were $115.3M versus $118.2M a year earlier.

The balance sheet is manageable. Cash was $149.5M at Q1'26 (down from $189.4M at YE25 on buybacks), against a $294.8M term loan maturing June 2031 at SOFR + 2.75% (refinanced October 2025 with a reduced margin) and an undrawn $100M revolver. Debt/equity is 0.46, current ratio 2.05, and the Altman Z-score of 2.69 sits in the grey zone. Interest expense is roughly $20M/year, a real cash cost but well covered by FCF. The concern is asset composition: goodwill ($773M) plus intangibles ($447M) equal about 78% of total assets, a legacy of 21 acquisitions since 2013. With the stock near multi-year lows, further impairment (a $47M charge already occurred in 2023) is a live risk.

Capital allocation has been aggressive. A $100M buyback was authorized April 2025; the company repurchased $42.6M in 2025 and $40M in Q1'26 (5.8M shares at an average $6.86), leaving only ~$17.4M authorized. Management is buying stock at depressed prices, which is defensible given the FCF profile, but the repurchases have drawn cash down without arresting the share decline.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the weakest link and the reason this is not a BUY. Three data points converge. First, organic growth is decelerating: FY2025 organic was ~6% (versus 9% headline), and Q1'26 total revenue growth was just 1% with Services contracting. Second, software net retention fell from 108.8% (2024) to 105.3% (2025), suggesting the biosimulation adoption S-curve may be flattening or that existing customers are expanding more slowly. Third, and most telling, consensus estimates are falling across every window: current-year EPS revised from $0.45 to $0.38 (-14.9% over 90 days), next-year from $0.50 to $0.41 (-17.0%), current quarter -15.7% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces low confidence in forward estimates: three misses in the last four quarters (Q1'26 -17.4%, Q4'25 -21.1%, Q2'25 -33.2%), with only one beat (Q3'25 +25.8%). Software remains the healthier stream (+7% in Q1'26), and the post-Veristat business is a cleaner, higher-margin software-plus-biosimulation franchise, which is strategically sensible. But the divestiture removes revenue, and the reset creates a discontinuity that makes FY2026 comparisons noisy. There is no evidence yet that the software growth engine can accelerate enough to offset the loss of Services revenue and a decelerating retention curve.

The secular tailwind of regulatory encouragement of in-silico methods and ~$290B/year global biopharma R&D remains intact, but it is being partially offset by FDA workforce reductions and NIH/NSF funding pullbacks that pressure both academic and small-biotech demand.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here given the GAAP loss, so the relevant lens is cash-based multiples and forward EPS. At $6.93 and ~155.6M shares, market cap is ~$1.08B. Against $94.6M FY2025 FCF, that is an ~8.8% FCF yield, genuinely attractive on a standalone basis and above most software peers. On forward EPS, current-year consensus of $0.38 implies ~18x and next-year $0.41 implies ~17x. Adding net debt of roughly $145M (term loan $294.8M less cash $149.5M), EV/adjusted-EBITDA on ~$134M is roughly 9x.

Peer context is mixed. Health Care Technology comparables show a median P/E of 27.4 (HSTM 39.2, OMCL 85.9, WAY 27.4, DOCS 20.4, SOLV 9.7), so on forward earnings CERT screens cheaper than the median. The FCF yield and EV/EBITDA support downside: analyst targets range $6.00–$10.00 with a $7.53 mean and $7.65 median, placing the current price near the low end.

The caution here is twofold. First, a cheap multiple is not a buy signal without an independent catalyst that corrects the mispricing, and cheapness has been an anti-predictive marker. CERT is cheap for identifiable reasons: falling estimates, decelerating organic growth, and a transition year. Second, the estimate-revision signal cuts against re-rating: analysts are lowering targets, not raising them, so proximity to consensus targets does not imply upside catch-up here. The FCF yield anchors a floor, but there is no specific reason the market is mispricing the equity upward. We score valuation at neutral: the yield support is real, but it is offset by deteriorating fundamentals with no re-rating catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Certara's moat is regulatory entrenchment. Its software is embedded in FDA/EMA/PMDA workflows, its models are the reference standard for MIDD submissions, and academic users become industry advocates, a reference-standard network effect with high switching costs because a validated, explainable model is what regulators demand. This is durable but not impregnable. The competitive set is broadening: open-source tools (R, PK-Sim) are free, large pharma can build in-house, and generative/agentic AI could enable lower-cost entrants. Certara's "expert-in-the-loop, validated-model" positioning is a defensible response consistent with regulatory explainability requirements, and the company frames AI as an enhancer rather than a threat.

The trajectory is the concern. The moat remains intact at the software layer, but net retention decay suggests the company is capturing less incremental spend from its installed base, and the AI-disruption question is unresolved. The prior report cycle already marked competitive position down from 6.5 to 5.5 on this dynamic; the moat is real but the trajectory is stable-to-slightly-softening rather than widening.

## Risk Assessment

**FDA/HHS workforce disruption** is the most acute company-specific risk and is not boilerplate. The FY2025 10-K details HHS's DOGE-driven reorganization cutting ~20,000 employees including ~3,500 FDA positions (EO 14210), with layoffs implemented April–July 2025. Certara's services and its customers' approval timelines depend on a functioning FDA, and NIH/NSF/CDC funding pullbacks directly threaten academic and small-biotech demand. This is a live 2025–2026 event with direct revenue implications, not a priced sector truism.

**Growth deceleration and retention decay** (organic growth ~1% in Q1'26, net retention 105.3% and falling) is the risk most likely to drive further estimate cuts.

**Goodwill/intangible concentration** at ~78% of assets, against a depressed stock, raises impairment probability; a 2023 charge already occurred, and contingent-consideration remeasurements ($7.4M in Q1'26) create earnings volatility.

**Divestiture execution and loss:** the Veristat sale books a loss that confirms the asset was underperforming and removes revenue during a transition.

**Management turnover:** four Item 5.02 filings since early 2025 and a simultaneous CEO/CFO change create execution risk during the strategic pivot.

**PE-sponsor overhang:** Arsenal's stake is a potential future secondary-selling source.

**Leverage:** the ~$295M floating-rate term loan is a real cash cost in a higher-for-longer environment, though covered by FCF.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility has collapsed from 164.9% to 88.4% since the May snapshot as the Q1 print and divestiture news resolved, while the price rose ~11%. IV remains elevated in absolute terms, consistent with a small-cap in transition, but the sharp decline signals reduced near-term event risk being priced. The more important signal is short interest: 29.3% of float short with 6.0 days to cover. That is very high and cuts both ways: it reflects genuine skepticism about the growth trajectory, but it also creates squeeze risk on any positive surprise, which partly explains the recent bounce from the $4.45 low. For a HOLD, the high SI is a reason not to establish a bearish position and a source of upside volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($10–$12):** The post-Veristat business is a focused, high-margin biosimulation software franchise generating ~$95M FCF at an 8.8% yield. Software continues to compound at high-single/low-double digits, net retention stabilizes as the new management team executes, buybacks retire shares at depressed prices, and the regulatory moat proves durable against AI entrants. Estimate revisions turn positive, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median of ~27x forward, and the stock closes the gap to its prior highs. On restored ~$0.45 EPS at ~22x, ~$10; the high analyst target is $10.00, so the upper end of this range assumes multiple expansion beyond current sell-side targets.

**Bear Case ($4.50–$5.00):** Organic growth stays near zero or turns negative as Services contracts and net retention keeps decaying below 105%. FDA/NIH disruption reduces small-biotech and academic demand. A goodwill impairment materializes given the depressed stock and intangible-heavy balance sheet. Estimates keep falling, the leadership reset stumbles, and the stock retests the $4.45 52-week low. On $0.35 EPS at ~13x, roughly $4.50, a 35% decline from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and the first full quarter reflecting the post-Veristat revenue base. FY2026 is explicitly a transition year; the Q2 and Q3 prints will show whether software growth can offset the Services divestiture and whether net retention stabilizes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Software net retention stabilizes at or above 105% and organic revenue growth reaccelerates above 6% for two consecutive quarters.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (any window) after the current -15% cut, confirming the growth reset has bottomed.
- Post-divestiture adjusted EBITDA margin holds ≥30% with FCF conversion intact, and the multiple compresses further to <12x forward while fundamentals stabilize.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic revenue growth turns negative or software net retention falls below 103%.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge is announced, or a further guidance cut accompanies the next print.
- FDA/NIH funding disruption produces a named customer or bookings decline (bookings already slipped to $115.3M from $118.2M; watch for continuation).

**Current levels:** Base case ~$7.50 (near consensus mean, ~8% upside); bull ~$10–$12 (44%–73% upside); bear ~$4.50–$5.00 (28%–35% downside). The roughly symmetric distribution, with negative estimate momentum on the downside and squeeze/re-rating optionality on the upside, keeps this a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Conclusion

Certara is a fundamentally sound cash generator reporting a GAAP loss: 22.6% FCF margin, ~$134M adjusted EBITDA, a genuine regulatory moat, and management repurchasing stock at ~$7. Those strengths keep it off the AVOID list and support the improving trajectory reflected in the prior score history (5.1 → 5.4). But the growth engine is decelerating in real time (1% Q1'26 revenue growth, retention slipping from ~109% to ~105%, estimates down ~15% over 90 days, and three misses in four quarters), and the business is mid-reset, with new leadership and a loss-making divestiture reshaping the FY2026 base. Cheapness alone is not a catalyst, and the estimate-revision trend argues against near-term re-rating.

The stock's 34% decline since the prior report reflects real fundamental softening (falling estimates, contracting Services, retention decay), not just volatility, so the lower price does not by itself improve the risk/reward. With a balanced distribution, high short interest cutting both ways, and no identifiable catalyst to reverse the growth deceleration, we maintain a **HOLD**. We would turn constructive on evidence of retention stabilization and positive estimate revisions, and negative on an impairment charge or continued organic contraction.
