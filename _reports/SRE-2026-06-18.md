**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sempra is a ~$59B multi-utility holding company whose value sits in three businesses with sharply divergent risk profiles: the regulated California utilities (SDG&E, SoCalGas), an equity-method interest in Oncor (the largest transmission-and-distribution utility in Texas), and a large LNG/energy-infrastructure arm now being substantially deconsolidated through a ~$10B sale to KKR. The defining tension is structural: the high-multiple, high-growth Texas and LNG franchises are trapped inside a holding company whose largest segment is the low-multiple, wildfire-exposed California complex. That gap is the explicit target of Voss Capital, which on May 28, 2026 publicly urged a spin-off of Oncor to unlock a sum-of-the-parts discount.

The growth case is real and specific. Sempra Texas Utilities posted +17% segment earnings in Q1 2026; Oncor won an April 2026 rate settlement lifting its annual revenue requirement ~$560M (+8.7%); and in June 2026 Oncor received ERCOT approvals for new transmission projects expected to exceed $7B, driven by data-center and electrification load growth in ERCOT. On consensus EPS of $5.11 (current year) and $5.54 (next year), the stock at ~$90 trades at roughly 17.6x and 16.2x forward — a discount to the multi-utility peer median of 19.7x — despite arguably the strongest organic load-growth profile in the group. The reported forward P/E of 30.7x in the headline data reflects a stale trailing EPS figure and materially overstates the multiple.

We rate Sempra a HOLD with a growth bias. The reason it is not a buy is the California wildfire overhang, which is crystallizing rather than receding. In April 2026, Edison disclosed ~$295M of Wildfire Fund reimbursements tied to the January 2025 Eaton fire, confirmed as a "covered wildfire," and Sempra's own filings warn this "could materially reduce or exhaust" the AB 1054 fund that backstops every California investor-owned utility, including SDG&E, regardless of SDG&E's clean fire record. Combined with a structurally free-cash-flow-negative model funded by ATM equity dilution and negative credit-rating outlooks at both Moody's (Baa2) and S&P (BBB+), the asymmetry is balanced rather than clearly favorable. The Voss catalyst and the KKR close are the path to a re-rating; the wildfire-fund mechanics are the path to a derating.

## Company Overview

Sempra reports three segments:

- **Sempra California** — SDG&E (electric for San Diego and southern Orange counties; gas for San Diego County) and SoCalGas (the largest U.S. gas distribution utility, serving most of Southern California). Combined California assets total roughly $61B. This is the largest, most stable segment, and the one carrying the country's harshest wildfire-liability and affordability/regulatory regime.
- **Sempra Texas Utilities** — an equity-method investment in Oncor Holdings (which owns 80.25% of Oncor) plus Sharyland. Because of PUCT ring-fencing, Oncor is not consolidated; Sempra recognizes equity earnings and does not control Oncor's operations or distributions. This is the highest-growth piece, riding ERCOT load growth.
- **Sempra Infrastructure (SI Partners)** — LNG (Port Arthur, ECA, Cameron), Mexican pipelines, and renewables. Sempra currently owns 70%, with a pending sale of an additional 45% economic interest to KKR (post-close: Sempra 25%, KKR 65%, ADIA 10%), after which Sempra deconsolidates and moves to equity-method accounting.

The franchise quality is genuine: large, hard-to-replicate rate-based positions in two of the strongest U.S. utility growth markets, plus one of the largest U.S. LNG export development platforms.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (10-Q, filed May 7, 2026) vs Q1 2025:**

- Earnings attributable to common rose to $1,037M ($1.58 diluted EPS) from $906M ($1.39), up 14% YoY. Net income was $1,150M vs $919M.
- Segment earnings: Sempra California $720M (–1%, essentially flat); Sempra Texas Utilities $171M (+17%); Sempra Infrastructure $262M (+80%); Parent & other –$116M.
- Revenue fell to $3,655M from $3,802M (–4%), but the decline is almost entirely pass-through (lower commodity gas cost recovered in rates, –$158M cost of gas). Utility margins are set by rate base, not commodity volume, so the top-line decline does not reflect business weakness.

The quality of the headline beat is mixed. A meaningful chunk of the +80% Infrastructure improvement is non-recurring or optical: roughly +$58M from unrealized commodity derivative swings, +$36M from ceasing depreciation on held-for-sale assets, and a +$35M one-time tax benefit from the held-for-sale reclassification. Normalizing these out, underlying operating improvement is more modest than reported. The consolidated effective tax rate of just 7% reflects flow-through regulatory treatment, ITCs, and large non-controlling/partnership income on which Sempra owes no tax.

The cash-flow picture is the central financial-health constraint. Operating cash flow was $1,809M (+$327M YoY), but capex plus investments totaled $3,337M in the quarter (California $967M, Oncor equity contributions $876M, SI Infrastructure $1,493M). Sempra is structurally free-cash-flow negative and funds growth with a combination of debt, equity (a $3B ATM program with ~$2.6B remaining), and asset sales. The balance sheet carries long-term debt of $30.8B plus $1.9B current and $3.7B short-term, with another $9.1B classified in liabilities held for sale. Leverage is normal for a capital-intensive utility, but the financing mix relies on continued capital-market access and successful asset-sale execution.

Credit ratings are a live constraint. Moody's rates Sempra Baa2 with a negative outlook and S&P BBB+ with a negative outlook. Against a multi-year capex program of this scale, a downgrade would raise borrowing costs and could trigger collateral postings, which is why the timing of the KKR close (the largest single source of incoming cash) matters to the 2026 financing plan.

**Earnings track record:** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters. The most recent quarter (March 2026) missed by 1.4% ($1.51 vs $1.53), breaking a streak that included a +20.5% surprise in September 2025 and +8.2% in December 2025. The narrow miss is not alarming in isolation, but it interrupts a positive surprise pattern.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is Sempra's strongest dimension and rests on three durable tailwinds.

**Texas/Oncor.** ERCOT load growth driven by data centers, electrification, and population is the cleanest growth lever in the U.S. utility space, and Oncor is the dominant pure-play T&D beneficiary. Three concrete data points anchor this: the April 2026 PUCT rate settlement raising Oncor's annual revenue requirement ~$560M (+8.7%) to ~$6.97B with a 9.75% ROE and an improved 43.5% equity layer; Oncor's first Unified Tracker Mechanism filing for ~$4.4B of 2025 T&D capital; and the June 2026 ERCOT approval of new transmission projects expected to exceed $7B. The data-center-driven load surge is visible across the regulated-utility group, which strengthens the durability of this thesis beyond a single-company story.

**LNG.** Port Arthur (Phase 1 ~$13B, Phase 2 ~$14B) and Cameron position Sempra among the largest U.S. LNG developers, benefiting from structural global demand and a friendlier DOE permitting posture (multiple non-FTA and construction-deadline extensions granted in 2026). ECA LNG Phase 1 introduced gas in April 2026, and as of May 7 no customers had exercised the SPA termination right tied to a February 24, 2026 commercial-operations deadline.

**California.** A large decarbonization rate-base opportunity, but the growth is capped by a 2026–2028 authorized-ROE step-down (SDG&E to 9.93% from 10.23%; SoCalGas to 9.78% from 10.08%) and the affordability/wildfire regime.

On consensus, EPS grows from $5.11 (current year) to $5.54 (next year), roughly +8.4%, consistent with a high-single-digit utility grower. Estimate revisions are modestly positive at the quarterly level (+3.6% current quarter, +6.3% next quarter over 90 days) but essentially flat at the full-year level (+0.4%). This is steady, durable utility growth with the SOTP optionality layered on top, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a re-rating.

The headline 30.7x P/E is misleading; it reflects a stale trailing EPS input. On consensus forward EPS of $5.11, the stock at ~$90 trades at 17.6x current-year and 16.2x next-year earnings, below the multi-utility peer median of 19.7x.

The discount is defensible only if there is a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock, because a low multiple in utilities frequently signals deserved risk rather than opportunity. Here there are two: first, the California-wildfire overhang is suppressing the multiple on the entire company even though roughly half the earnings power (Texas/LNG) carries no such risk; second, the trapped-asset structure is exactly what Voss Capital is targeting, and a clean Oncor separation would let the Texas business trade closer to a high-growth Texas-utility multiple. The KKR close and Oncor spin-off are the catalysts that could narrow the gap.

The countervailing risk is that the discount is partly deserved: the closest comparable, PG&E, trades at 13.0x precisely because California wildfire liability is unbounded under inverse condemnation, and an Eaton-driven depletion of the AB 1054 fund would push SRE's California risk profile toward PG&E's. So the cheapness is conditional, not unconditional, and we weight it accordingly. Even the low analyst target of $93 sits above the current price, with a mean of $103.5 (+14.9%), which supports a modestly positive skew, but analyst targets here reflect the same SOTP optionality rather than independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Sempra's moat is a portfolio of large, capital-intensive, regulated franchises that are extremely difficult to replicate. Oncor is the dominant T&D operator in ERCOT, structurally advantaged by the same load growth driving the >$7B transmission build. SoCalGas is the largest U.S. gas distributor. The LNG platform is among the largest U.S. export-development positions.

Trajectory matters more than level. In Texas, the competitive position is improving: rising rate base, a favorable rate settlement, and transmission approvals that compound the growth runway. In California, the position is stable-to-pressured: a large investment opportunity offset by a declining authorized ROE and a regulatory/affordability backdrop that is the most hostile in the country. In LNG, the position is improving on permitting tailwinds, tempered by construction-execution risk. The net trajectory is positive, weighted toward the Texas and LNG franchises that Voss argues are undervalued inside the holding-company wrapper.

## Risk Assessment

**California wildfire liability — the dominant, crystallizing risk.** California's inverse-condemnation regime imposes liability regardless of fault. The single most important watch item is the AB 1054 Wildfire Fund, which Sempra's 10-Q warns the Eaton-fire reimbursements could materially reduce or exhaust. If the LA Fires materially deplete the fund, every California IOU — including SDG&E — loses a key liability backstop, regardless of its own fire record. SDG&E carries a $253M Wildfire Fund asset that could be written down. SoCalGas and Sempra are named defendants in both the Palisades and Eaton fire litigation. The 2025 creation of an $18B Continuation Account (SB 254) is itself evidence the original fund is viewed as inadequate. This sector-wide overhang is moving from theoretical to crystallizing, and it is not fully priced into a 17.6x multiple.

**CPUC regulatory disallowance.** SDG&E/SoCalGas have $522M of WMP/drone-inspection costs and $739M of PSEP costs in pending 2024 GRC Track 3 requests; disallowances flow straight to earnings. The 2026–2028 authorized ROE step-down is already a modest headwind.

**FERC ISO adder refund.** FERC ruled SDG&E ineligible for the 0.50% California ISO ROE adder, requiring a refund retroactive to June 2019 (under appeal).

**Credit and financing.** Negative outlooks at both rating agencies against a large capex plan funded partly by ATM dilution. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the structural free-cash-flow deficit compounds the financing risk, and continued equity issuance is a mild but persistent dilution headwind.

**LNG execution.** Port Arthur and ECA carry construction risk, including an April 2025 fatal construction incident at Port Arthur now in multidistrict litigation (Sempra named, Bechtel indemnifying).

**Mexico/FX.** ECA land disputes, permit challenges, CFE pipeline disputes, and peso exposure — risks that decline materially post-KKR sale. Sempra layered on ~$767M-equivalent of peso hedges in Q1–Q2 2026 to lock in the Mexican capital-gains tax on the KKR sale, signaling firm intent to close.

**Execution/timing.** The 2026 financing plan depends on closing ~$10B of KKR proceeds plus ~$0.5B from Ecogas on schedule (Q2/Q3 2026). A ticking fee and $414M termination fee provide protection, but a delay would stress the ratings and the capex funding plan.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 160.9% is a thin-options artifact tied to a one-day expiry (the implied move is only ±1.0% and one-year historical volatility is 19.7%); it should be treated as non-informative. The meaningful read is the 1m–6m term structure at 23.7%–24.6%, modestly elevated versus realized vol, with a 4.4% one-month put skew and unusual activity in the July $85 put (volume 4.5x open interest). This reflects measured downside hedging consistent with the wildfire and regulatory overhangs, not acute stress. Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.53 is actually call-tilted. Short interest is low at 1.8% of float (2.8 days to cover), so positioning is uncrowded in both directions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$118):** The KKR sale closes on schedule, de-risking the balance sheet, removing Mexico/FX volatility, and defending the credit ratings. The Voss campaign gains traction and management moves toward an Oncor separation, allowing the Texas business to re-rate toward a high-growth Texas-utility multiple. ERCOT load growth converts the >$7B transmission pipeline into approved, recoverable rate base, and the AB 1054 fund proves adequate without forcing SDG&E write-downs. EPS compounds high-single-digits with the SOTP discount narrowing — high-$110s on a re-rating plus growth.

**Base case (~$104):** Asset sales close, Oncor growth continues, the multiple stays near peers, and the wildfire overhang persists but does not crystallize into a Fund-depletion event. EPS grows ~8% and the SOTP discount narrows modestly. ~18.8x on next-year EPS of $5.54 with a partial re-rating supports the low-$100s.

**Bear case (~$73, –19%):** The Eaton fire materially depletes the AB 1054 fund, forcing a write-down of SDG&E's $253M Fund asset and re-rating California risk toward PG&E's 13x multiple. A credit downgrade raises borrowing costs against the large capex plan; the Voss campaign fails to move management; and continued ATM dilution pressures per-share growth. This pushes the stock back toward the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD with a 12-month horizon tied to two dated catalysts: the KKR/Ecogas asset-sale closings (expected Q2/Q3 2026) and any management response to the Voss Capital Oncor-spin-off campaign. The Texas transmission build and Oncor rate progression provide the durable per-share growth engine underneath.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- KKR sale closes on schedule with the credit-rating outlooks stabilized to neutral at Moody's and/or S&P, removing the financing overhang.
- Management endorses or initiates a structural separation of Oncor, validating the SOTP thesis with a clear path to value realization.
- The AB 1054 Wildfire Fund draw from the LA Fires resolves without materially impairing SDG&E's $253M Fund asset, removing the dominant overhang and allowing the discount to peers to close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit downgrade at Moody's or S&P, or a delayed/terminated KKR close that strains the 2026 financing plan.
- Confirmation that the Eaton fire materially depletes the AB 1054 fund, forcing SDG&E Fund-asset write-downs or expanding inverse-condemnation exposure.
- A material CPUC disallowance on the pending $522M WMP / $739M PSEP requests, or evidence the ATM dilution accelerates beyond the current pace.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings, and on confirmation of the KKR close. The Q2/Q3 2026 asset-sale window and the trajectory of the Voss campaign are the natural decision points.

## Conclusion

Sempra is a high-quality utility holding company trading at a justified-but-conditional discount to peers, with a genuine high-single-digit growth engine in Oncor/Texas and two live catalysts (the ~$10B KKR de-risking and the Voss SOTP campaign) that could narrow the gap over the next 12 months. We rate it HOLD rather than BUY because the California wildfire overhang is crystallizing rather than receding — the Eaton-fire draw on the AB 1054 fund is the single most important watch item and is not fully reflected in the multiple — and because the structurally free-cash-flow-negative, dilution-funded model carries negative credit-rating outlooks into a higher-for-longer rate environment. The growth thesis is concrete and the analyst floor ($93) sits above the current price, but the asymmetry is balanced, not clearly favorable. We would upgrade on a clean KKR close with stabilized ratings or a credible Oncor-separation step, and downgrade on a credit downgrade or a confirmed Wildfire Fund impairment.
