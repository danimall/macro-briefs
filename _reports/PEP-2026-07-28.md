**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PepsiCo is one of the highest-quality consumer staples franchises in the world: a complementary food-and-beverage portfolio spanning 200+ countries, a ~40% return on equity, and a 50-plus-year record of dividend increases. The investment case today is defined less by that quality than by a stalled core business. In Q2 FY2026, PepsiCo Foods North America (Frito-Lay) posted revenue down 2% with flat unit volume and *negative* net pricing, indicating that the pricing-led growth model of 2021-2023 has run out of road. PepsiCo Beverages North America saw organic volume fall 4%. Consolidated core operating profit grew roughly 4% (only ~1% constant-currency), with the headline GAAP surge (+125% operating profit) being an artifact of last year's $1.86B Rockstar/Be&Cheery impairment base effect. Net income has declined from $9.63B in 2024 to $8.30B in 2025.

The stock sits at $139.79, down ~18% from its 52-week high of $171.48 and roughly flat over the trailing twelve months. At ~16x forward EPS ($8.56 consensus for the current year) and a ~4.3% dividend yield, PEP trades at a meaningful discount to the beverage peer median (~26x), reflecting genuine earnings stagnation rather than pure sentiment. The bull case is a self-help story: Elliott Management disclosed a ~$4B stake in September 2025 arguing for >50% upside via improved operations, cost discipline, and potential refranchising of low-margin company-owned bottling (~36% of consolidated revenue). The bear case is that North American volume erosion is structural — driven by value-seeking consumers, private-label share gains, GLP-1 appetite suppression, and health/regulatory scrutiny — and that the FX and Russia tailwinds currently flattering international results can reverse.

We rate PEP a HOLD (income). The dividend is well-covered and the valuation is undemanding, providing a floor, but we cannot underwrite appreciation until either core North American volumes stabilize or the Elliott-era margin plan produces visible results. Near-term estimate revisions are negative (current-quarter EPS cut 5.5% over 30 days), the stock's multi-year score trajectory has been declining, and this is now the fourth consecutive HOLD. We see no reason to reverse that call, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than negative.

## Company Overview

PepsiCo operates a global beverage and convenient-food business organized into six segments: PepsiCo Foods North America (PFNA), PepsiCo Beverages North America (PBNA), International Beverages Franchise, EMEA, Latin America Foods, and Asia Pacific Foods. Revenue splits roughly 57% convenient foods / 43% beverages. The food-plus-drink combination is the structural differentiator versus pure-play Coca-Cola.

Marquee brands include Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Ruffles, Tostitos, Quaker, Gatorade, Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Starry, and Aquafina, plus recent "better-for-you" additions poppi (prebiotic soda, acquired for ~$2.1B) and Siete (Mexican-American foods, ~$1.2B). PepsiCo also distributes Celsius, Alani Nu, and Rockstar, though it wrote down the Rockstar brand in 2025 and is the distributor — not owner — of Celsius/Alani.

Two structural features matter for the thesis. First, ~36% of consolidated revenue comes from company-owned bottling operations, which carry higher revenue but lower margin, the likely target of any Elliott refranchising push. Second, customer concentration is meaningful: Walmart/Sam's Club accounts for ~14% of consolidated revenue, and the 10-K states that its loss would have a material adverse effect on PFNA and PBNA. PepsiCo employs ~306,000 people. In the U.S., Coca-Cola holds ~20% of liquid refreshment beverage retail sales versus Pepsi's ~16%; in snacks, Frito-Lay is dominant but confronting weak domestic volumes.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 FY2026 (12 weeks ended 6/13/2026).** Net revenue was $24.18B, up 6% reported but only 2% organic (+1 volume, +2 pricing, with FX and M&A largely offsetting). H1 revenue reached $43.62B, up 7% reported / 2.5% organic. Reported operating profit of $4.02B (+125%) and GAAP diluted EPS of $2.18 (versus $0.92) overstate the underlying trajectory: the prior-year quarter carried a $1.86B impairment. On a core basis, operating profit grew ~4% (+1% constant-currency), and core EPS of $2.20 rose 4% (only +1% cc). The financial press characterized the print as an earnings miss, with management attributing U.S. softness to higher gas prices squeezing consumers. Consensus EPS estimates confirm the deceleration: current-quarter estimates have been cut 5.5% over 30 days and 5.8% over 90 days, while the trailing four-quarter record shows 2 beats, 1 in-line, and 1 miss — the most recent being a 1.3% Q2 miss.

**The North American problem.** PFNA revenue fell 2% in Q2 with flat unit volume and operating profit down 3.5% on negative net pricing. PBNA organic volume fell 4% (CSD -3%, non-carb -4%), and its reported profit "recovery" is almost entirely the absent prior-year impairment plus poppi contingent-consideration credits. Growth is carried by international: IB Franchise +9% organic, EMEA +6%, LatAm +4% (peso-aided), and Asia Pacific +8-10% led by India.

**Multi-year trend.** Net income has flatlined-to-declined: $9.16B (2023) → $9.63B (2024) → $8.30B (2025), despite modest revenue growth. This margin/earnings stagnation is the crux of the activist thesis.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Total assets are $112.2B against total debt of ~$53.2B ($10.6B short-term + $42.6B long-term) and equity of only $22.3B, producing elevated leverage that pushes the Altman Z-score into the grey zone (~2.0) despite the ~40% ROE. Cash is $10.3B, but 21% is trapped in Russia behind currency controls. H1 free cash flow improved to $1.17B (from -$0.34B), aided by favorable working capital. Capital return is aggressive relative to FCF — ~$8.9B planned for 2026 (~$7.9B dividends + ~$1.0B buybacks). The dividend was raised 4% to $5.92/share in February 2026, extending a five-decade streak, with a new $10B buyback authorization through Feb 2030 but only ~$1B budgeted for 2026. Capital is prioritized toward the dividend, which limits buyback firepower and constrains per-share earnings support in a way a less-levered peer would not face.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated and, on balance, weak. Organic revenue growth of 2% in Q2 is well below the mid-single-digit algorithm PepsiCo delivered during the 2021-2023 pricing cycle, and the composition has deteriorated: the pricing lever that drove that growth has now reversed to *negative* in snacks. Volume is flat-to-declining across the entire North American footprint, which represents roughly half the business. The regulatory and secular backdrop — sugar/sodium taxes, "MAHA"-style ingredient scrutiny, GLP-1 appetite suppression, and continued private-label share gains — argues that at least part of this volume weakness is structural rather than cyclical.

International remains the bright spot, with India and China volume growth and better-for-you innovation (poppi, zero-sugar, protein-rich snacks) resonating in some categories. But the reported strength internationally is materially FX-aided (ruble/peso appreciation), and a reversal would flatter-to-hurt. The $6.15B "2019 Productivity Plan," now extended to 2030 with $3.8B incurred, is the primary cost-response vehicle, but productivity savings offset margin pressure rather than drive top-line growth.

Consensus reflects the deceleration: current-year EPS of $8.56 and next-year $8.99 imply ~5% forward growth, both revised down over the trailing 30 and 90 days. There is no evidence in the data of an earnings inflection; estimates are broadly falling. This is a stable-but-stalling franchise with negative near-term earnings momentum, not a case of a high trailing multiple masking trough earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $139.79, PEP trades at ~18.3x trailing EPS, with the ~16x forward multiple and ~4.3% yield noted above. Against the beverage peer median P/E of 26.0, this is a wide but well-earned discount: Coca-Cola (26.0x) and Monster (44.5x) are growing organically, while PEP's core earnings are roughly flat. Keurig Dr Pepper at 23.2x is the closest comparable on growth profile, and PEP's discount to KDP reflects both the North American stagnation and PepsiCo's higher leverage.

We resist the temptation to score cheapness as a buy signal. Across our framework, low headline multiples in staples have repeatedly proven to be value traps absent a specific catalyst, and the correct question is whether the market is mispricing PEP *today*. Two things anchor the fair value. First, the dividend yield, now near the high end of its multi-year range, provides absolute return support and downside cushioning. Second, the Elliott campaign creates genuine, if unquantified, optionality: bottling refranchising and a North American cost reset could re-rate the multiple if execution materializes. Analyst targets span $124 to $183 with a mean of $155.9, and the sentiment trend is "degrading" (one buy-to-hold downgrade recently, with Citi cutting its PT to $170 from $182 while maintaining Buy).

On balance we see PEP as roughly fairly valued rather than deeply mispriced. The stock is cheap enough that further downside is limited by the yield, but the absence of an earnings inflection means the multiple has little reason to expand until the self-help story is proven. We do not treat the consensus targets as floors here — those apply to inflection stocks with rising estimates, and PEP's estimates are falling.

## Competitive Landscape

PepsiCo's moat remains wide in absolute terms but is compressing at the trajectory that matters for scoring. In snacks, Frito-Lay retains dominant U.S. share, but the negative pricing and flat volume in PFNA signal that pricing power has eroded as consumers trade down to private label and value formats. In beverages, PepsiCo is structurally #2 to Coca-Cola in U.S. CSD and further behind internationally, and it ceded the energy category (Rockstar impaired) while now merely distributing Celsius/Alani rather than owning them.

The retail landscape — consolidation, hard discounters, e-commerce, and private-label expansion — is explicitly flagged in the MD&A as squeezing shelf space and pricing power. The bright spots are emerging-market volume growth and better-for-you innovation, but the poppi contingent-consideration markdown (from $300M to $117M) is an early indicator that at least one flagship 2025 acquisition is tracking below its deal model. The competitive trajectory in the ~50% of the business that is North America is deteriorating, which is why we score this dimension below the level PepsiCo's brand quality alone would suggest; direction matters more than level here, and the direction in the core market is negative.

## Risk Assessment

**North American volume/pricing erosion** is the central operational risk — snacks now showing negative pricing, beverages losing share in both CSD and non-carb. If this proves structural rather than cyclical, the entire earnings algorithm is impaired.

**Russia exposure** is larger than the 5-6% revenue headline suggests: 6% of assets, 21% of cash (behind currency controls), and 38% of accumulated currency-translation loss. FX tailwinds are currently flattering reported international results; a reversal would hurt.

**Regulatory/health backlash** spans sugar/sodium/saturated-fat taxes on both beverages and snacks, ingredient scrutiny, and packaging rules (EPR, PFAS, Baltimore plastics litigation). **Leverage and credit ratings**: the MD&A explicitly flags that a downgrade — especially of short-term ratings — would impair Tier-1 commercial paper access ($6.1B CP outstanding). The **OECD 15% global minimum tax** is now in effect, raising the effective tax rate to 22.0% (from 18.6%). **Impairment risk** on recently acquired brands (poppi $1.7B, Siete $470M) bears watching after the 2025 Rockstar/Be&Cheery write-downs.

Most of these are known and partly priced, which is why we score risk near neutral rather than low. The one genuinely asymmetric idiosyncratic factor is the Elliott campaign, whose outcome (board settlement terms, structural demands) could not be fully confirmed from available sources — a two-sided catalyst that could re-rate the stock up on a credible plan or disappoint if the turnaround stalls further.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 29.7% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 21.5% (an 8.3-point rich spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (29.7% near-term versus 22.2% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress. Put/call skew is balanced-to-slightly-put-weighted (-1.4% near-term, steepening to -8.7% at three months), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.45 is not bearish. ATM IV collapsed from 66.9% to 29.7% since the prior snapshot — the earnings binary has passed and uncertainty has normalized. The implied ±2.4% near-term move and modest 2.7% short interest (3.2 days to cover) argue against any acute positioning signal. The rich-but-declining IV suggests the market is not pricing a directional break, consistent with our balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($170-183).** North American volumes stabilize as consumer pressure eases and better-for-you innovation gains traction; the productivity plan and an Elliott-driven cost/margin reset lift operating margins; bottling refranchising sheds low-margin revenue and re-rates the multiple toward the beverage-peer range. The dividend is well-covered throughout, and a credible activist plan closes the valuation gap Elliott identifies. CEO comp actually paid collapsing to $1.4M in 2025 (from $36.5M in 2023) shows the pay plan is self-correcting and management is motivated to deliver.

**Bear Case ($124).** North American volume decline proves structural (health/GLP-1/value trends), negative snack pricing persists, and Frito-Lay margins compress further. Leverage limits buyback firepower and keeps EPS growth muted. FX and Russia tailwinds reverse, exposing the weakness of core constant-currency growth (barely positive). poppi and Siete take further impairments, and estimates continue to grind lower. In this scenario the stock drifts toward the low end of the analyst range with only the dividend providing return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Reassessment timeline: next earnings release (early October 2026) and any Elliott board-settlement announcement.**

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has declined from 6.7 (Feb) to 5.0 now, driven by cuts to Competitive Position (8.5 → 4.5) and Management Quality (7.5 → 5.5) as the North American stagnation became visible in the numbers. New data confirms rather than reverses that trend — negative snack pricing and a Q2 miss validate the caution. The dividend and undemanding multiple justify holding for income; the absence of an earnings inflection and falling estimates prevent a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PFNA net pricing returns to positive AND North American unit volume inflects to flat-or-better for two consecutive quarters.
- A credible, dated Elliott settlement with a specific margin/refranchising plan and quantified savings targets, OR current-year EPS estimates turn positive (rising) on a 90-day basis.
- Evidence the productivity plan is expanding operating margins rather than merely offsetting cost pressure.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- North American organic volume declines accelerate below -4% with continued negative pricing, signaling structural rather than cyclical erosion.
- A dividend-coverage strain emerges (FCF/dividend materially below 1.0x on a trailing basis) or a credit-rating downgrade impairs commercial paper access.
- A material impairment of the poppi/Siete brand portfolio confirms the better-for-you pivot is failing.

## Conclusion

PepsiCo is a durable, cash-generative franchise trading at a defensible discount to lower-quality but faster-growing beverage peers, with a well-covered dividend anchoring the downside. That combination makes it a reasonable income hold. On current data it is not an appreciation call: the North American engine that represents half the business is stalling, snack pricing has turned negative, near-term estimates are falling, and the reported GAAP "surge" is an impairment base effect that masks barely-positive constant-currency growth. The Elliott campaign supplies genuine optionality, but until refranchising or a margin reset produces visible results, or until North American volumes stabilize, the fair value sits close to where the stock trades today. We maintain HOLD with an income rationale and would revisit on the triggers above.
