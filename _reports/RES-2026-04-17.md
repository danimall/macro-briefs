## Executive Summary

RPC, Inc. is a mid-tier oilfield services company whose core pressure pumping business has declined 37% over two years, compressing operating margins from 18% (2022) to 2.8% (2025) and reducing net income 85% to $32M. The stock has rallied 48% from its April 2025 lows to $7.20, driven by the broader energy rally tied to Middle East supply disruptions, but this move has pushed the valuation to ~49x trailing earnings — nearly double the peer median of 25.4x — with no fundamental improvement to justify the premium.

The April 2025 Pintail acquisition added ~$395M in annualized wireline revenue and diversified the business away from pressure pumping, but it also brought $65M in acquisition-related employment costs, elevated D&A, and $83M in goodwill that requires impairment testing in a deteriorating market. Analyst EPS estimates have been cut 57% at the current-quarter level and 23% at the full-year level over the past 90 days, with no sign of stabilizing. With 38.3% of the float sold short, the stock sits at the intersection of a deteriorating fundamental story and a crowded short that has been squeezed by the energy rally.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong ($210M cash, minimal debt, 3.24x current ratio, 4.44 Altman Z-Score), and RPC is not at risk of financial distress. But at 49x trailing earnings with declining estimates, no electric frac capability, and structural oversupply in the core service line, the risk-reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

RPC, Inc. provides specialized oilfield services to oil and gas companies, primarily in the U.S. (>98% of revenue), with operations concentrated in the Permian Basin, mid-continent, Gulf of America, Rocky Mountain, and Appalachian regions. The company operates through two segments: Technical Services (94.4% of 2025 revenue) and Support Services (5.6%).

Following the Pintail acquisition in April 2025, the revenue mix shifted meaningfully: Pressure Pumping dropped from 47.7% (2023) to 29.8% (2025), while Wireline surged from 1.3% to 19.4%. Downhole Tools (Thru Tubing Solutions) at 24.2% is RPC's most differentiated business, offering proprietary downhole motors and tools with active patent protection.

The company is controlled by the Rollins family, which holds ~61% of voting power and has elected the NYSE Controlled Corporation exemption, exempting it from majority-independent board requirements. RPC had 2,893 employees at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

Revenue in 2025 was $1.627B, nominally the highest in the four-year period, but entirely attributable to Pintail's $295.8M contribution over nine months. Organic revenue declined, particularly in pressure pumping ($485M in 2025 vs. $587M in 2024, down 17.4%, and down 37% from $772M in 2023).

The margin trajectory:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Operating Margin | 18.0% | 15.1% | 6.9% | 2.8% |
| Net Margin | 13.6% | 12.1% | 6.5% | 2.0% |
| Diluted EPS | ~$1.01 | ~$0.91 | $0.43 | $0.15 |

Operating income fell from $288M (2022) to $45M (2025). Net income declined from $218M to $32M. The 2025 effective tax rate spiked to 43.3% (vs. 18.9% in 2024) due to $20.3M in non-deductible acquisition employment costs on a thin pretax income base — this should partially normalize as costs amortize, but the underlying pretax deterioration ($168M → $57M over two years) is structural, not tax-driven.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow declined from $395M (2023) to $349M (2024) to $201M (2025). Free cash flow was $53M in 2025, down from $129M in 2024. Working capital was a $37.4M use of cash (vs. a $116.7M source in 2024), partly driven by a $45.4M unearned revenue drawdown. FCF remains positive but is clearly deteriorating.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the company's clearest strength. $210M in cash, only $50M in debt (a seller note from Pintail, SOFR + 2%, maturing April 2028), zero revolver borrowings against a $100M facility, and stockholders' equity of $1.1B. Current ratio is 3.24x and the Altman Z-Score is 4.44, firmly in safe territory. There is no near-term solvency risk.

### Earnings Quality Flags

Q4 2025 EPS of $0.04 missed the consensus estimate of $0.07 by 32%. The last four quarters show a mixed 2-for-4 beat/miss record. Adjusted EBITDA of $233M in 2025 was flat with 2024 despite 15% higher revenue, implying the Pintail acquisition's contribution was offset by continued pressure pumping margin compression.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak across multiple dimensions:

**Estimate Revisions:** Every time horizon shows negative revisions. Current quarter EPS estimates have been cut 57.4% over 90 days (from $0.06 to $0.03). Full-year 2026 estimates are down 22.6% (from $0.27 to $0.21). Next-year estimates are down 14.6% ($0.35 to $0.30). This is broad-based, accelerating negative earnings momentum with no stabilization visible.

**Structural Headwinds:** The U.S. rig count declined 6.3% in 2025 to an average of 562, and efficiency gains (20-22 pump hours/day, longer laterals) mean each rig generates more completion activity with fewer OFS assets. Management explicitly describes the pressure pumping market as "over-supplied" and has no plans to add frac fleets.

**No Electric Frac Fleet:** RPC does not offer electric frac services and is only "considering future investments." Larger E&P customers increasingly require electric or dual-fuel equipment for emissions compliance. Competitors like Liberty Energy and ProFrac have invested aggressively. This gap risks share loss with blue-chip E&P clients.

**Potential Positives:** Natural gas prices rose 60%+ in 2025, and management sees favorable long-term gas demand from LNG exports and AI-related energy consumption. Higher gas activity could benefit RPC's non-Permian operations. The Pintail acquisition also provides a growth vector in wireline services, where the equipment is among the newest in the industry. These positives would need to overcome the structural pressure pumping headwinds, and the timing is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

RES trades at approximately 49x trailing earnings on $0.15 diluted EPS. Against the peer median P/E of 25.4x, RES commands a 93% premium. This premium is difficult to justify given declining margins, falling estimates, and absence of the technology differentiation (electric frac) that would warrant a growth multiple.

On forward estimates: consensus expects $0.21 for FY2026, implying ~34x forward earnings — still well above the peer median. Even assuming some recovery to the $0.30 next-year estimate, the stock at $7.20 prices at ~24x, roughly in line with peers, requiring flawless execution of both the Pintail integration and a pressure pumping stabilization that management itself does not project.

The analyst consensus target is $6.44 mean / $6.20 median, both below the current price of $7.20. The range is $5.00 to $8.00. Current analyst recommendations: 1 Strong Buy, 3 Holds, 1 Strong Sell. The stock has traded through the mean and median targets, suggesting the recent rally has front-run any near-term positive catalysts.

EV/EBITDA: With $233M in adjusted EBITDA and an enterprise value of approximately $1.27B ($1.43B market cap + $50M debt - $210M cash), the stock trades at ~5.5x EBITDA. This is more reasonable on an absolute basis but reflects the low-quality EBITDA trajectory (flat YoY despite the Pintail boost).

The dividend yield of 2.3% ($0.16/year) is modest, and management has explicitly stated no intention to grow it or return excess cash through special dividends.

## Competitive Landscape

RPC occupies an uncomfortable middle position in the OFS market. Halliburton, Baker Hughes, and SLB offer scale, technology, and global diversification. Liberty Energy and ProFrac lead on electric frac innovation. Smaller competitors undercut on price. RPC's most defensible asset is Thru Tubing Solutions (Downhole Tools), which generates $394-397M in stable annual revenue and benefits from proprietary IP. This line alone cannot sustain the current valuation.

E&P consolidation is reducing the customer base and concentrating buying power. One private E&P customer now represents ~15% of revenue (up from 13% in 2024), creating meaningful concentration risk. Management acknowledges that consolidation "has resulted in the loss of some customers."

The Pintail acquisition brought newer wireline equipment and strong Permian Basin positioning, which is tactically sensible. Wireline is a competitive market, however, and the integration introduces execution risk, goodwill impairment risk ($83.4M), and near-term earnings drag.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Structural Pressure Pumping Oversupply:** The dominant risk. Efficiency gains reduce demand for OFS assets per unit of production. RPC's 10 horizontal frac fleets face pricing pressure with no planned additions or technology upgrades to electric. This is a multi-year headwind.

**2. No Electric Frac Capability:** Increasingly disqualifying for large E&P contracts. The longer RPC delays this investment, the more market share it cedes.

**3. Customer Concentration:** 15% of revenue from one private E&P. Loss of this customer would be immediately material to revenue and earnings.

**4. Controlled Company Governance:** The Rollins family controls 61% of voting power. CEO compensation nearly doubled ($1.85M to $3.19M) while net income fell 65%. Minority shareholders have limited recourse.

**5. Falling Estimates:** EPS estimates have been cut at every horizon, with accelerating declines at the nearest quarters, signaling deteriorating sell-side confidence in near-term results.

**6. Pintail Integration Risks:** $65M in remaining acquisition employment costs, elevated D&A from $97M in purchased intangibles (10-year amortization), and $83.4M in goodwill on a business acquired during a market downturn.

**7. 38.3% Short Interest:** While this creates squeeze risk (which may partially explain the recent rally), sustained high short interest reflects broad institutional skepticism about the fundamental trajectory. Days to cover at 5.8 adds mechanical upward pressure during rallies but compounds any fundamental improvement.

**8. Oil Price Sensitivity:** Average oil prices fell 14% in 2025 to $65.58/bbl. Further oil price declines reduce E&P activity and pricing power for OFS providers.

### Options Market Signal

No options data was provided. The 38.3% short interest functions as a proxy for directional positioning — institutional investors with conviction are positioned for downside. The rally from $4.18 to $7.20 (72% from the October 2025 low) occurred on rising short interest, suggesting shorts have not covered despite the move. This either indicates the squeeze still has legs (if oil continues rising) or that informed investors see the current price as disconnected from fundamentals.

- Pressure pumping revenue continues declining 10-15% annually as oversupply persists
- Large E&P customer (15% of revenue) reduces activity or switches to a competitor with electric frac capability

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Next earnings report (estimated late April/early May 2026 for Q1 2026 results). Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 57% over 90 days to $0.03. If Q1 results miss again (as Q4 2025 did), the combination of a second consecutive miss and further estimate cuts would pressure the stock. Oil price weakness from the 10-day ceasefire could also accelerate E&P activity cuts, becoming visible in Q2 guidance.

**Timing window:** 2-8 weeks. Q1 2026 earnings is the nearest hard catalyst. If oil remains below $85 through April, management guidance will likely reflect reduced activity expectations.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 38.3% of float with 5.8 days to cover, squeeze risk is substantial and is the dominant risk for any new short position. The stock has already rallied 72% from its October 2025 low, partly on squeeze mechanics. Any further oil price spike (ceasefire failure, re-escalation) could produce violent upside.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data available. The extremely high short interest implies elevated borrow costs. The 48% YTD move on limited fundamental improvement suggests momentum traders may be driving price action independent of fundamentals.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $5.00-$5.25 (analyst low target: $5.00) implies 28-31% downside from $7.20. Upside risk to $8.50 on a squeeze or oil spike implies ~18% upside risk. The ratio is approximately 1.6:1 to 1.7:1 — below the 2:1 threshold for a clean short, and the 38.3% short interest makes the mechanics dangerous.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bear thesis on fundamentals is sound — declining earnings, falling estimates, no technology pivot, and a rich valuation. But 38.3% short interest creates acute squeeze risk, and the geopolitical environment can deliver oil price spikes that overwhelm the fundamental deterioration for weeks or months. The correct expression is to avoid owning the stock, not to actively short it.

## Conclusion

RPC's balance sheet strength and the strategic logic of the Pintail acquisition are real positives, but they are insufficient to overcome: (1) a 49x trailing P/E nearly 2x the peer median, (2) EPS estimates declining at every horizon, (3) structural pressure pumping oversupply that management acknowledges, (4) absence of electric frac capability in an industry shifting that direction, (5) a controlled company governance structure with misaligned executive compensation, and (6) a stock price already above the analyst consensus target. The 38.3% short interest may create periodic squeeze-driven rallies, but it also reflects the breadth of institutional skepticism. At $7.20, the stock prices in a recovery that the data does not yet support. Avoid.
