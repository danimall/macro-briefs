**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GE HealthCare is a $29B-market-cap medical-technology company spun from General Electric in January 2023, generating $20.6B in FY2025 revenue across imaging, advanced visualization, patient monitoring, and pharmaceutical diagnostics. After trading above $89 in early January 2026, the stock has fallen roughly 30% to ~$63, driven by a disappointing Q1 2026 print (April 29), tariff pressure, China weakness, and a sharp margin collapse in the Patient Care Solutions segment. Consensus EPS estimates have been trimmed 3-4% over the past 90 days, and analyst sentiment is degrading. The stock now trades at roughly 13x forward EPS (~$4.87 current-year consensus), a meaningful discount to the Health Care Equipment peer median of ~26x.

A broad cluster of open-market insider purchases occurred in late April through May 2026 at ~$60-64, led by Director Larry Culp (Chairman/CEO of GE Aerospace) buying ~80,805 shares (~$5.0M), joined by CEO Peter Arduini, CFO Jim Saccaro, the General Counsel, and several other directors. Insiders deploying personal capital at multi-year lows signals that management views the current level as a floor. This is the strongest single element of the bull case and supports a Management Quality score of 7.0.

We rate GEHC a HOLD (growth_hold). The valuation is inexpensive on both an absolute and relative basis, the franchise is durable, and the insider cluster is compelling. But the operating trend is deteriorating: Q1 operating income fell 18% and adjusted EBIT margin compressed 150bps, and estimate revisions are negative. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on GEHC, and with the score trajectory declining (6.7 in February to 6.0 in June), we require evidence of margin stabilization or a tariff resolution before upgrading.

## Company Overview

GE HealthCare develops and sells medical imaging, patient monitoring, diagnostics, and healthcare software. FY2025 revenue was $20.63B, up 4.8% reported and 3.5% organic. The business is being reorganized from four segments into three effective Q2 2026:

- **Imaging** ($9.25B FY25, +4.4%): MRI, CT, molecular imaging, X-ray. The largest segment, being combined with Advanced Visualization into "Advanced Imaging Solutions" (AIS) under Philip Rackliffe.
- **Advanced Visualization Solutions** ($5.35B, +4.3%): Interventional/procedural guidance and specialized ultrasound. The highest-margin segment at ~22% EBIT.
- **Patient Care Solutions** ($3.09B, −1.2%): Monitoring and life support. Segment EBIT collapsed 40% in FY25 to $209M (6.8% margin) and 80% YoY in Q1 2026 to $10M (1.4% margin), the clear operational problem area.
- **Pharmaceutical Diagnostics** ($2.90B, +15.6%): Contrast agents and radiopharmaceuticals. The fastest grower, boosted by the NMP acquisition, at ~30% EBIT margin.

The competitive moat rests on a large global installed base, ~$7B of high-margin recurring service revenue (with $10.7B of services remaining performance obligations), scale in imaging, and the GE brand, which is licensed from General Electric, not owned, a subtle long-term risk. Total RPO backlog is roughly $15.8B.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily but slowly: $19.55B (2023), $19.67B (2024), $20.63B (2025). Q1 2026 revenue was $5.13B, up 7.4% reported but only 2.9% organic; the reported figure was FX-aided.

The margin story is the concern. FY25 operating margin was 13.4%, with net income of $2.08B (10.1% net margin) and diluted EPS of $4.55 ($4.59 adjusted). Adjusted EBIT margin declined 100bps to 15.3% on tariffs and cost inflation. Q1 2026 deteriorated further: operating income fell 18% to $515M, net income fell 31% to $389M (net margin down 420bps to 7.6%), and adjusted EBIT margin fell 150bps to 13.5%. The headline EPS drop overstates the trend; much of the YoY decline reflects the non-repeat of a $97M NMP one-time gain in Q1'25, and adjusted EPS was only down $0.02 ($0.99 vs $1.01). But the underlying pressure from tariffs (~$90M operating / ~$110M cash in Q1 alone), engineering "design follow-through" investment, and a transient PDx supplier issue is real.

Cash flow remains solid: FY25 operating cash flow was $1.99B and FCF $1.51B (down 3.2% YoY), a reasonable conversion rate. The balance sheet carries the spin-off's imprint. Total assets are $37.1B, of which goodwill is $15.1B (~41%). Total debt is ~$10.1B against stockholders' equity of $10.7B, for D/E of ~2.5x. Cash fell from $4.5B to $2.3B in Q1 2026 after funding the $2.3B Intelerad acquisition. The current ratio is 1.37.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.55 nominally places GEHC in the "distress zone," but this reading is misleading. It is driven by high leverage and thin equity relative to a goodwill-heavy post-spin balance sheet, not operational distress. ROE is 20.7%, ROA 5.8%, the company is solidly profitable and FCF-positive, and it is compliant with all debt covenants. The genuine financial watch items are leverage (~$10B and rising to fund M&A) and a pension deficit of ~$4.4B net (US plans $3.84B underfunded on an $18.6B PBO, with ~$350M expected contributions in 2026), not solvency.

Capital allocation is modest: a token $0.035/quarter dividend (0.2% yield), a $1.0B buyback authorized April 2025 with ~$700M remaining ($100M repurchased in Q1'26), and a stated priority on debt paydown and tuck-in M&A.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is mid-single-digit — 3.5% in FY25, decelerating to 2.9% in Q1 2026. This is a durable but unexciting growth profile that depends on hospital capital budgets, the shift to ambulatory/outpatient care, and the mix uplift from faster-growing PDx and AI/software.

The growth levers are: (1) PDx and radiopharmaceuticals compounding in the mid-teens; (2) the Intelerad acquisition (closed March 18, 2026, $2.3B cash), which extends imaging software into the higher-growth outpatient/ambulatory workflow market; (3) a steady cadence of AI/software tuck-ins (icometrix, MIM Software, Intelligent Ultrasound); and (4) new long-term commercial wins such as the $500M, 10-year Care Alliance with Catholic Health announced in July 2026 (>1,300 devices).

The near-term data cuts against acceleration. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from $5.06 to $4.87 over 90 days (−3.7%), and next-year from $5.56 to $5.36 (−3.6%). The current-quarter estimate was cut 11.8% over 90 days to $1.03. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats, one in-line, one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) missing by 7.5% ($0.99 vs $1.07 estimate). Given negative revisions and a recent miss, we score Growth Prospects at 5.5 — durable but decelerating, with no confirmed re-acceleration catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$63, GEHC trades at roughly 13x forward consensus EPS ($4.87) and ~15x trailing ($4.55). The peer set — Dexcom (32.2x), ResMed (18.6x), Steris (26.1x), Zimmer Biomet (22.6x), Hologic (31.3x) — carries a median P/E of 26.1x. GEHC trades at roughly half the peer median.

The discount is not unjustified. GEHC's organic growth sits below the higher-growth device names, its balance sheet carries more leverage than most peers, and it faces active tariff and China headwinds those peers largely do not. A 13x multiple for a low-single-digit organic grower with margin pressure is defensible rather than obviously mispriced. Our valuation lessons caution against treating cheapness as a buy signal absent a specific catalyst; cheap names have reliably been value traps in this regime.

There is genuine asymmetry here that keeps the valuation score above neutral at 6.0. First, absolute yield support is limited, but FCF yield of ~5% on the current market cap provides a floor. Second, there is a specific, underappreciated potential catalyst: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariff authority and the subsequent April 2026 refund process. If refunds materialize against the ~$245M/year tariff drag, current-year EPS estimates would be revised upward, and the multiple would re-rate. Third, analyst targets (mean $79, median $80, high $98, low $65) sit well above the current price, but with degrading sentiment and negative revisions, we treat these as aspirational rather than as floors. The catalyst that would unlock the valuation — tariff refunds, PCS recovery — is real but uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

GEHC competes with Siemens Healthineers, Philips, and Canon Medical in imaging; Bracco and Guerbet in contrast agents; and numerous players in monitoring. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving in the aggregate, but with divergent segment trajectories.

On the positive side: the recurring service base and RPO backlog provide revenue durability; PDx is gaining share; and the Intelerad acquisition strengthens GEHC's position in the structurally growing outpatient imaging-software market. TSR of 136.2 vs a peer benchmark of 122.0 indicates the franchise has outperformed its comparison group over the measurement period.

On the negative side: China revenue fell 4.6% in FY25 and 4.4% in Q1'26 under volume-based procurement price controls, local competition, and the ongoing anti-corruption campaign delaying orders. The PCS segment's margin collapse suggests competitive and mix pressure in monitoring/life support that the reorg is explicitly trying to address. Right-to-repair and independent-service-organization pressure threatens the high-margin service annuity over time. On balance, the competitive position is holding, with the imaging/PDx strength offsetting PCS erosion, so we score this dimension at 6.3 — consistent with the recent trajectory of gradual decline from 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — the dominant near-term headwind.** ~$245M FY25 operating-income impact (~$285M cash), primarily bilateral US-China tariffs, running ~$90M/$110M in Q1 2026. Management explicitly states mitigation will not fully offset. The IEEPA Supreme Court ruling and April 2026 refund process are a two-sided catalyst; the timing and amount of any refunds are uncertain, but they represent genuine upside if they materialize.

**China weakness.** Volume-based procurement, local competition, an anti-corruption campaign delaying orders, and 2025-tightened rare-earth export controls that threaten imaging supply chains.

**PCS margin collapse.** Segment EBIT down 40% in FY25 and 80% YoY in Q1'26; the reorg is partly an attempt to fix execution.

**Leverage and pension.** ~$10.1B debt rising to fund M&A; ~$4.4B net pension deficit sensitive to discount rates.

**GE Tax Matters Agreement overhang.** The IRS may assert material additional taxes on GE's 2016-2020 consolidated returns, with GEHC potentially owing indemnification — not accrued and not currently estimable. Spin-off tax-free status also carries residual risk.

**Goodwill impairment risk** given $15.1B of goodwill (~41% of assets).

**Legal.** The Iraqi Ministry of Health / Anti-Terrorism Act case was revived by the DC Circuit in January 2026, with en banc review denied in April 2026; the case is proceeding.

**GE brand licensed, not owned** — termination would force costly rebranding.

The concentration of specific, unpriced risks — tariff timing, the GE tax indemnification tail, PCS execution, and pension — justifies a Risk Factors score of 5.3. Several of these are genuinely binary or asymmetric rather than sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-slightly-constructive and offers no strong directional signal. Near-term ATM IV is 31.9% versus 1-year historical vol of 34.0% — fair to slightly cheap, indicating the market is not pricing elevated near-term stress. The prior snapshot showed IV of 243.9% (an earnings-driven spike that has fully collapsed), so the current calm reflects resolved event risk after the Q1 print. Put/call skew is balanced (+1.8%), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 1.06 to 0.77, a modest bullish shift. The 6-month tenor shows a −3.5% put skew (mild call preference on the longer horizon). One unusual print — 653 contracts of the August $65 call at 4.7x open interest — hints at some upside positioning. The fading of downside hedging aligns with the insider-buying floor thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$90-98)

Tariff refunds materialize under the post-IEEPA process, reversing the tariff drag and driving upward EPS revisions. PCS margin recovers as the reorg takes hold, restoring segment EBIT toward historical levels. PDx and Intelerad-driven imaging software compound in the mid-teens, lifting the growth profile. China stabilizes. The stock re-rates from ~13x toward the low end of the peer range (18-20x) on normalized ~$5.50+ EPS, delivering $90-98, consistent with the analyst high target. The insider cluster proves prescient, with ~$60 confirmed as a durable floor.

### Bear Case (~$52-55, ~13-18% downside)

Tariff refunds fail to materialize or slip indefinitely, and China deteriorates further under continued procurement pressure. PCS margins stay depressed and drag consolidated margins. Estimate revisions continue downward, and the stock de-rates further on ~$4.60 EPS at ~11-12x, reaching $52-55. The GE tax indemnification or pension deficit crystallizes as a cash drain. Leverage constrains capital return.

### Base Case (~$72)

Organic growth holds at mid-single-digit, margins stabilize but do not fully recover in 2026, and tariff refunds provide modest partial relief. The stock recovers toward the middle of its trading range as the Q1 shock fades and the insider signal supports sentiment. A modest re-rate to ~14-15x on ~$4.90-5.00 EPS supports ~$72, roughly 14% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold) — we see a plausible 12-month path to appreciation supported by the insider buying and cheap valuation, but require confirmation before upgrading.

**Base case target: ~$72** (roughly 14x forward on ~$5.00 EPS, ~14% above the current ~$63). Math: modest margin stabilization plus partial tariff relief lifts consensus back toward $5.00, and a re-rate from 13x toward the low-14x range yields ~$72.

**Bull case target: ~$90-98** (18-20x on normalized ~$5.50 EPS after full tariff reversal and PCS recovery).

**Bear case target: ~$52-55** (~11-12x on ~$4.60 EPS, ~13-18% downside).

**Timeframe:** 12 months, keyed to the Q2 2026 print (first quarter under the three-segment structure, a read on PCS recovery), tariff-refund developments, and China trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Positive EPS estimate-revision velocity for two consecutive quarters, reversing the current −3.7% 90-day trend.
2. Evidence of PCS margin recovery — segment EBIT margin rebuilding toward mid-single-digit or better in Q2/Q3 2026.
3. Confirmed tariff refunds or a durable de-escalation reducing the ~$245M/year drag.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A further leg-down in China revenue (worse than the −4.4% Q1 run-rate) or a China supply disruption from rare-earth controls.
2. A goodwill impairment charge or crystallization of the GE tax indemnification liability.
3. Consolidated adjusted EBIT margin compressing below ~12% on a sustained basis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026), plus any material tariff-refund or GE Tax Matters Agreement development.

## Conclusion

GE HealthCare is a durable, scaled medical-imaging franchise trading at an unusually low ~13x forward multiple after a ~30% drawdown — a discount that reflects genuine tariff, China, and PCS-execution headwinds rather than pure market irrationality. The standout positive is the broad, dollar-significant cluster of open-market insider purchases, led by Larry Culp's ~$5M buy, which signals that management and the board see current prices as a floor. This is the single most reliable forward indicator in the setup and it earns a Management Quality score of 7.0.

The offsetting reality is that operating momentum is deteriorating and consensus estimates are being cut, not raised. The Q1 miss, the margin compression, and the PCS collapse are current facts, while the bullish catalysts — tariff refunds, PCS recovery, China stabilization — remain prospective and uncertain. With the score trajectory declining across four prior HOLDs and negative estimate revisions, we require confirmation before paying up for the cheapness. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, a base-case target of ~$72, and a clear upgrade path tied to estimate-revision velocity and PCS margin recovery. Investors who share the insiders' conviction can begin accumulating at these levels with a wide stop below the bear case; those seeking confirmation should await the Q2 print.
