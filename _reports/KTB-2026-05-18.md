## Executive Summary

Kontoor Brands is divesting its declining Lee brand to concentrate on the growing Wrangler franchise and the rapidly-scaling Helly Hansen acquisition. At $64.25, the stock trades at approximately 12.3x FY2026 continuing-operations adjusted EPS ($5.15-$5.25 guided), a modest multiple for a company reporting 470bps of gross margin expansion and 16% growth in its second-largest brand. The announced $750M share repurchase program (~21% of current market cap) provides meaningful capital return support.

Several material risks constrain conviction: estimate revisions are sharply negative (current-year EPS revised down 17% in 30 days), the most recent quarter missed EPS by 6.5%, leverage remains elevated at 3.57x D/E post-Helly Hansen acquisition, and 10% short interest signals institutional skepticism about the transition's execution. The Lee divestiture — reportedly with Authentic Brands Group in exclusive discussions — carries meaningful execution risk that could delay the buyback program and deleveraging timeline.

The thesis resolves around whether Helly Hansen's growth trajectory (+16% organic, +100% China) can be sustained while Wrangler maintains its 16-quarter market-share-gain streak through an environment of weakening U.S. consumer spending. The September 2026 Investor Day in Norway should provide more granular Helly Hansen targets. This is a stock to hold for investors already positioned, with the understanding that the upside case requires successful execution across multiple workstreams simultaneously.

## Company Overview

Kontoor Brands (spun off from VF Corp in 2019) operates three brands: Wrangler ($1.91B, 61% of segment revenue), Helly Hansen ($460M in 7 months post-acquisition, acquired May 2025 for ~$958M USD), and Lee (~$750M, now classified as discontinued operations pending divestiture). Products are sold in 90+ countries, ~150 million units annually, through U.S. Wholesale (64%), International Wholesale (21%), and Direct-to-Consumer (15%).

The company is transforming from a heritage denim house into a function-based outdoor/work/Western platform. Wrangler targets the Western lifestyle consumer with 79 years of brand equity. Helly Hansen (149 years old) addresses premium outdoor and professional workwear with significant U.S. underpenetration (<30% aided awareness). Lee, after 137 years, is being divested due to persistent declines (-5% in FY2025) and compressing margins.

Critical customer concentration: Walmart accounts for 30% of total revenues. Top 10 customers represent 53%.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trends:**

FY2025 (ended Jan 3, 2026) revenue reached $3.15B, up 21% from $2.61B, though organic growth was only ~2.4% ($61M). The remainder came from Helly Hansen's 7-month contribution and a 53rd reporting week. Net income declined 7% to $227M despite the revenue increase, burdened by $131M in acquisition/integration/restructuring charges and a higher tax rate (24.3% vs. 18.5%).

Adjusted EPS grew to $5.59 from $4.89, representing genuine operating improvement. GAAP EPS fell to $4.05 from $4.36.

**Q1 2026 (reported May 7):**

- Revenue from continuing operations: $613M
- Adjusted EPS from continuing ops: $1.06 (missed consensus $1.13 by 6.5%)
- Adjusted gross margin: 50.6% (+470bps YoY)
- Helly Hansen pro-forma revenue growth: +16% (China JV: +100%)
- Wrangler: +2% reported (+4% on global basis)

The gross margin expansion is the most important signal in the quarter. The combination of Project Jeanius (multi-year supply chain transformation), Helly Hansen's premium mix, and DTC shift is producing structural margin improvement, not cyclical tailwinds.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Cash: $108M (down from $334M pre-acquisition)
- Total debt: ~$1.15B (up from $740M)
- D/E ratio: 3.57x — elevated but being actively reduced ($250M voluntary prepayments in FY2025)
- Goodwill + intangibles: $981M (38% of assets), up from $220M pre-acquisition
- Altman Z-Score: 2.35 (grey zone)
- ROE: 40.3% (inflated by leverage)
- Interest expense: up 52% YoY

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow of $455.8M (up from $368.2M) is robust and well above the $116M dividend obligation. FCF conversion is strong relative to reported earnings, though partially masked by one-time acquisition costs.

**Forward Guidance (FY2026):**

- Continuing operations adjusted EPS: $5.15-$5.25 (before $0.55 stranded overhead)
- Total adjusted EPS (including discontinued Lee): $6.60-$6.70
- Revenue: $3.41-$3.46B

## Growth Analysis

**Positive catalysts:**

1. *Helly Hansen* is the primary growth engine: +16% pro-forma revenue growth, operating margins "nearly doubled" YoY, with a path to mid-teens margins from current high-single-digits. U.S. distribution just beginning (Dick's Sporting Goods signed). China JV growing 100%. Addressed market is massive relative to current $460M/7-month run rate.

2. *Wrangler* continues gaining share: 16 consecutive quarters of market share gains per Circana. Women's business is only 10% of revenue — a meaningful expansion vector. Western lifestyle category benefits from cultural tailwinds (country music, ranch aesthetic).

3. *Margin expansion* is structural: 50.6% gross margin in Q1 2026 vs. mid-44% range a year ago. Project Jeanius, Helly Hansen mix, and DTC growth are permanent mix shifts.

**Negative signals:**

1. *Estimate revisions are sharply negative*: Current-year EPS revised from $6.48 → $5.35 (-17.4% in 30 days); next quarter from $1.90 → $1.36 (-28.4%). This reflects analysts incorporating Lee discontinuation confusion, tariff uncertainty, and macro headwinds.

2. *Most recent quarter missed*: Q1 2026 EPS of $1.06 vs. $1.13 estimate (-6.5%), breaking a 3-quarter beat streak. Misses after a streak of beats can signal deteriorating visibility.

3. *Organic growth is tepid*: Excluding Helly Hansen, Wrangler grew only 2-4%. In a stagflationary environment with negative real wages and gasoline above $4.50, Wrangler's mass-market consumer (30% Walmart exposure) faces direct pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

At $64.25, KTB trades at:
- ~12.3x FY2026 continuing-ops adjusted EPS ($5.15-$5.25)
- ~9.6x total adjusted EPS including Lee ($6.60-$6.70)
- ~12.5x forward P/E (per Yahoo data)
- ~3.3% dividend yield ($2.12/share annualized)

**Peer context:** The peer median P/E is 19.6x (COLM 18.7x, GIII 19.6x, CRI 13.4x). KTB at 12.3x continuing-ops represents a ~37% discount to the peer median. Carter's (CRI) is the closest comp at 13.4x with similar mass-market consumer exposure and 3.1% yield.

**What the market may be mispricing:** The $750M buyback (~21% of market cap) funded by Lee divestiture proceeds represents extraordinary potential EPS accretion. If Lee sells for $500-700M (reasonable for a $750M-revenue brand at 1.0-1.2x revenue given declining trajectory), combined with ongoing FCF, the buyback is executable over 2-3 years. This could drive 5-7% annual EPS accretion from share count reduction alone, on top of organic earnings growth from Helly Hansen.

**Why the discount may persist:** Execution risk on Lee sale, elevated leverage, negative estimate revisions, and macro headwinds for the mass-market consumer all justify some discount. The market is pricing "show me" — correct execution gets you to $80+; stumbles keep you at $55-65.

The valuation is genuinely inexpensive relative to the growth profile of the continuing business (Helly Hansen at +16%, structural margin expansion), but the discount reflects real risks rather than market ignorance.

## Competitive Landscape

**Wrangler** occupies a defensible niche: the Western lifestyle category has limited direct competition at Wrangler's price point in the mass channel. Levi's competes in broader denim but not authentically in Western. Ariat competes in Western but at premium price points. Wrangler's 16-quarter share gain streak suggests the brand is strengthening relative to private label and secondary competitors.

**Helly Hansen** competes against Arc'teryx, Patagonia, The North Face, Columbia, and Fjällräven in outdoor, and against Carhartt, Dickies, and Snickers in professional workwear. Its competitive advantage lies in 149 years of Scandinavian heritage and genuine professional-use credibility (used by actual sailors, oil workers, and mountaineers). U.S. penetration is minimal (<30% aided awareness) vs. 90%+ in Norway/Scandinavia — creating a long runway.

**Competitive position trajectory:** The divestiture of Lee removes a declining drag and allows management focus on the two brands with positive momentum. However, Helly Hansen's U.S. expansion requires building wholesale relationships and consumer awareness from a low base against entrenched competitors with larger marketing budgets. This takes years, not quarters.

**Score context:** Prior score declined from 6.5 to 6.0. I maintain 6.0 — the strategic repositioning improves the forward-looking portfolio quality, but Helly Hansen's U.S. market position is still embryonic, and Wrangler's mass-channel exposure carries consumer spending sensitivity.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Customer concentration (Critical):** Walmart = 30% of revenues. In the current consumer environment (real wages negative, confidence collapsing, gas >$4.50), Walmart may pressure suppliers on pricing, shift shelf space to private label, or see traffic declines in discretionary categories. This is KTB's single largest risk factor and is partially reflected in the valuation discount.

2. **Lee divestiture execution (High):** If the Authentic Brands Group deal falls through or yields a price below expectations, the $750M buyback program loses funding. Stranded overhead ($0.55/share) persists longer. The stock would likely sell off 10-15% on a failed sale.

3. **Leverage and rate environment (High):** 3.57x D/E with 30Y >5.1% makes refinancing expensive. Interest expense already up 52% YoY. The company is prioritizing deleveraging, but $1.15B in debt with rates elevated creates a persistent drag on earnings power.

4. **Tariff uncertainty (Moderate-High):** 77% of units sourced internationally (primarily Bangladesh). The IEEPA tariff reversal ($54M receivable) was a tailwind, but future tariff regimes remain unpredictable. Mexico sourcing (23%) provides partial hedge.

5. **Consumer spending deterioration (High in current macro):** With PPI at 6%, real wages negative, and gas prices elevated, the mass-market consumer that drives Wrangler sales (via Walmart, Target, etc.) faces material pressure. HD/TGT earnings this week will provide a real-time read.

6. **Helly Hansen integration risk (Moderate):** Still early-stage (12 months post-acquisition). Current 6.9% operating margins need to reach mid-teens for the acquisition math to work. Progress is encouraging but not proven over multiple cycles.

- Helly Hansen sustains 15%+ growth and achieves 12-14% operating margins within 18 months

- Lee sale fails or produces <$400M; buyback delayed/reduced

- Tariff regime reasserts, compressing margins for Asia-sourced goods
- Leverage becomes problematic if earnings decline — 3.57x D/E with falling EBITDA

## Conclusion

Kontoor Brands is executing a strategically sound transformation — shedding a declining brand to concentrate capital and management attention on two growing franchises with structural margin expansion. The valuation at ~12x forward earnings with a 3.3% yield provides reasonable downside protection, and the $750M buyback (if funded) represents genuine catalytic upside.

However, the current environment presents meaningful headwinds: sharply negative estimate revisions, elevated short interest, leverage at 3.57x, and a macro backdrop that directly threatens the mass-market consumer who drives Wrangler's largest channel. The stock needs to demonstrate that the strategic pivot produces tangible results — a successful Lee sale, sustained Helly Hansen growth, and stabilizing estimates — before warranting higher conviction.

This is a correctly-priced transformation story with genuine upside optionality, but the risk/reward requires execution confirmation before upgrading. Hold for the September Investor Day and Lee divestiture resolution.
