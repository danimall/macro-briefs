## Executive Summary

Certara sells biosimulation software and scientific services that substitute computational modeling for physical experimentation in drug development. The franchise is genuine: software licensed by 20 regulatory agencies including FDA, MHRA, PMDA and NMPA; more than 2,600 customers; 414 PhD/MD staff out of ~1,576 employees. The problem is that this franchise is currently growing 1-2%, and the market has just paid 18% more for it than it did three weeks ago while consensus earnings estimates fell.

The specific arithmetic that drives our call: continuing-operations revenue grew 1.0% in Q2 2026 and 1.6% in H1; the Americas, roughly 65% of revenue, contracted 1.8%; quarterly net revenue retention printed 101.5%, the weakest observation in the disclosed series against an annual trend of 108.4% (2023) → 108.8% (2024) → 105.3% (2025). Adjusted diluted EPS of $0.08 missed the $0.10 consensus, the third miss in four quarters, and consensus for FY2026 has been cut from $0.45 to $0.34 over 90 days (-23.6%) and from $0.38 to $0.34 in the last 30 days alone (-11.0%). Comparable-period free cash flow, computed properly with capitalized software development included, fell from $10.6M in H1 2025 to $1.1M in H1 2026. Against that, the stock is up 85% from its May 15 low of $4.45 and now trades above the $8.04 analyst mean target, inside the $6.00-$9.00 range.

We do not lower a rating because a stock has risen. We lower it because the price has moved one way and the estimates the other, so the margin of safety has been consumed twice over. On our base case of $7.25 — 12x EV/adjusted EBITDA on a modest FY2027 build, cross-checked against a 4.5-5.5% free-cash-flow yield — the shares carry roughly 12% downside to fair value, 36% downside in the bear case, and 27% upside only if the cost program overdelivers and software bookings convert into accelerating software revenue. Probability-weighted expected value sits near $7.56, below spot. The balance sheet is not the issue: net debt of $110M against ~$112M of guided adjusted EBITDA is under 1.0x, the term loan matures in 2031 with roughly 1% annual amortization, and the $100M revolver is undrawn. This is a REDUCE on valuation and execution risk, not a solvency call, and the 29.6% short interest is a specific reason not to press it as a short.

## Company Overview

Certara's product set spans physiologically-based pharmacokinetic modeling (Simcyp), PK/PD and population-PK (Phoenix, Pinnacle), regulatory writing and submissions (CoAuthor, GlobalSubmit), discovery and cheminformatics (D360, Chemaxon), data (CODEx) and quantitative systems pharmacology (Certara IQ). Revenue is now roughly evenly split: Q2 2026 software $48.8M (52.3%) and services $44.5M. Software recognition is itself mixed — $18.6M point-in-time license revenue versus $30.2M recognized over time in Q2 — which explains why quarterly software growth is choppy and why bookings are the better read on demand.

Two structural changes define the current period. First, in May 2026 the company sold its global medical writing and related regulatory services business to Veristat for $69.435M cash at close, a $15.0M contingent escrow and an earn-out of up to $35.0M, recognizing a $65.481M pretax loss ($48.576M after tax). Second, the company reorganized into two units — MID3 (Model Informed Discovery and Drug Development) and ACE (Accelerated Clinical Evidence) — and executed a reduction in force affecting approximately 5% of global headcount, predominantly overhead, targeting ~$13M of run-rate savings.

The most useful insight in management's own commentary concerns where competition comes from. At the June 2026 Jefferies conference, CEO Jon Resnick said Certara "often loses business not to competitors or on price, but because clients choose traditional approaches." If accurate, the binding constraint is category adoption rather than share loss. That framing cuts both ways: it implies a large runway if simulation substitutes for wet-lab and clinical work at scale, and it implies that near-term revenue is hostage to a behavioral shift Certara does not control and cannot forecast reliably.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 total revenue was $418.838M, +8.7%, of which roughly a third came from the Chemaxon acquisition (organic ~6%). Continuing operations after the divestiture ran ~$367M in FY2025. H1 2026 continuing revenue was $187.363M versus $184.455M, +1.6%. Geographic decomposition: Americas $122.255M (-1.8%), EMEA $46.256M (+5.2%), APAC $18.852M (+18.2%). APAC growth is strong but at 10% of revenue cannot offset a contracting 65% base.

**Bookings.** Total H1 bookings were flat at $195.5M versus $195.7M, with software bookings +13.9% to $99.4M and services bookings -11.3% to $96.1M. This is the most encouraging datapoint in the filings — mix shifting toward the higher-margin, more recurring business — but it is composition improvement without aggregate growth. Software bookings growth of 13.9% has so far produced software revenue growth of 5.8%, so conversion is either lagging or the bookings are longer-duration than the revenue they replace.

**Margins.** Gross margin held at 62.75% in H1 2026 versus 62.59%. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 181bp to 28.23% from 30.04%. GAAP operating margin swung to -3.4% from +6.5%. The GAAP swing is dominated by a contingent-consideration fair-value reversal ($7.230M charge in 2026 against $5.901M of income in 2025 — a $13.1M non-cash swing inside a $14.9M G&A increase) plus ~$21.6M per half of intangible amortization. The adjusted EBITDA decline has a different and more relevant cause: deliberate spend ahead of revenue, with R&D +12.8% to $21.991M and S&M +7.2% to $27.928M against 1.6% revenue growth.

**Development spend is understated by the income statement.** Capitalized software development was $13.223M in H1 2026 against reported R&D of $21.991M. Total product investment is therefore $35.214M, or 18.8% of revenue, not the 11.7% the P&L shows. This matters for two reasons: it is real cash out the door, and it means the "we are investing to reaccelerate" story is already being funded at nearly a fifth of revenue without visible revenue response.

**Free cash flow — the number most screens get wrong.** A conventional screen takes FY2025 operating cash flow of ~$95M less ~$0.5M of PP&E capex and reports ~$94.6M of FCF, a 22.6% margin and a 7.5% yield. Including capitalized software (~$24.4M for FY2025, annualized from the $12.199M H1 actual), total-company FCF was ~$70M. On continuing operations: $72.180M operating cash flow − ~$0.5M PP&E − ~$24.4M capitalized software ≈ **$47M**, a 12.9% margin on ~$367M. At $8.235 that is a **3.7% FCF yield**, roughly half what the naive calculation implies.

H1 2026 continuing FCF was $15.584M − $1.252M − $13.223M = **$1.109M**, versus $10.594M in H1 2025. This should not be annualized — H1 2025 operating cash flow was only 32.3% of the full-year continuing total, so cash generation is genuinely H2-weighted on Q4 renewal billing. But the $9.5M comparable-period gap is real, and the drivers are identifiable: accrued expenses fell from $59.658M to $36.211M (a ~$23.4M drain from bonus, severance and divestiture settlements) and cash taxes paid doubled to $12.137M from $6.039M.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $184.138M; current ratio 2.55x; term loan $294.028M at SOFR+2.75% maturing June 2031 with a $280.7M balloon and ~1%/year amortization until then; $100M revolver undrawn to 2029; $230M notional of swaps (78% of the loan) fixed at 3.62-3.64% to 2029. Net debt $109.890M against guidance-midpoint adjusted EBITDA of ~$112.4M is **0.98x net / 2.62x gross**, with interest coverage near 5.6x. Refinancing risk is effectively absent for five years.

The offset: goodwill $718.125M plus intangibles $345.207M equal 76.0% of total assets, tangible book value is **negative $96.8M**, accumulated deficit is $193.9M, and GAAP continuing operations lose money at the operating line. Property and equipment net is $1.673M. There is no asset backing beneath the equity, which caps how far a value floor can be argued.

**Tax.** FY2025 GAAP effective rate was 120.9% with a $29.4M valuation allowance; H1 2026 continuing-ops rate was -10% (a $1.614M provision on a $16.292M pretax loss). Foreign NOLs of $87.3M begin expiring in 2026 and $14.6M of foreign tax credits from 2027, so some deferred assets will likely lapse. The adjusted-EPS reconciliation tax-effects add-backs at statutory rates (-$11.132M in H1), which flatters adjusted EPS relative to the $12.1M of cash taxes actually paid against $21.6M of adjusted net income. Free cash flow, not adjusted EPS, is the honest anchor here.

**Earnings delivery.** Four quarters: +25.8% (Sep-25), -21.1% (Dec-25), -17.4% (Mar-26), -16.1% (Jun-26). Three consecutive misses of similar magnitude is a visibility problem, not noise, and it argues for discounting rather than trusting the upper half of guidance.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance, reaffirmed on August 4: revenue $367-382M (0-4%), adjusted EBITDA margin 29-31%, adjusted diluted EPS $0.31-0.36 on 155-157M shares.

The critical stress test is the H2 earnings ramp. H1 delivered $0.14 ($21.574M ÷ 156.480M shares). Reaching $0.31-0.36 requires FY adjusted net income of $48.4-56.2M, implying **H2 adjusted net income of $26.8-34.6M, or +24% to +60% versus H1**. Guidance implies H2 revenue of $179.6M to $194.6M, that is -4.1% to +3.9% versus H1 — and because FY2025 continuing revenue split almost exactly in half (H1 was 50.3% of the year), there is no revenue seasonality to supply the lift. The ramp must come from cost and tax.

Bridging the low end: ~$13M of run-rate savings contributes roughly $6.5M in H2; tax-effected at ~25% that is ~$4.9M, taking H1's $21.574M to ~$26.5M against a low-end requirement of $26.8M. So **the low end is approximately bridgeable on cost savings alone; the midpoint and high end require another ~$4-8M of after-tax improvement** from revenue upside, deeper cuts or a lower rate. The same holds for margin: 29% needs H2 at ~29.8% (+160bp, comfortable), the 30% midpoint needs ~31.8% (+360bp, requiring full realization with zero cost creep). The guided share count of 155-157M is flat against H1's 156.5M, so the buyback contributes nothing to the EPS ramp.

There is an unquantified complication. Management stated the ~$13M includes absorbing stranded costs from the divestiture; in June the then-CFO described "about $10 million." The net incremental margin benefit is therefore less than $13M and has not been disclosed. Consensus at $0.34 sits just below the guidance midpoint, which is the right posture.

**The longer-dated growth case is real but undated.** The FDA's April 2025 roadmap to reduce reliance on animal testing, the March 2026 draft guidance on New Approach Methodologies, and follow-on guidance in May 2026 all push in Certara's direction, and the company launched a "Non-Animal Navigator" product in April 2025. Independent commentary is more measured — FierceBiotech (April 2026) reported the agency declaring first-year goals met while experts noted "still a long way to go," and RAPS (June 2026) cited scaling and harmonization challenges. Fifteen months after the roadmap, continuing revenue grows 1-2%. Cutting the other way, the FY2025 10-K itself flags the HHS reduction in force (~20,000 positions including ~3,500 at FDA) and 2025 FDA layoffs; agencies are both customers and demand gatekeepers, so disruption defers purchases through two channels.

Growth score of 4.3 reflects a 0-4% revenue guide, a contracting Americas base, deteriorating retention, and estimate revisions of -23.6% for the current year — partially offset by software bookings up 13.9%, APAC up 18.2%, and a credible multi-year regulatory tailwind with no dated conversion path.

Revenue growth of 1.6% is not depressed trough revenue awaiting recovery; it is the guided run rate. The conditions under which rising estimates and momentum justify paying up are absent here — estimates are falling while the price rose.

Two further reference points. The company sold ~$50M of annual revenue to Veristat for $69.4M cash, roughly 1.4x revenue (2.4x if the escrow and full earn-out land), against Certara's own 3.65x EV/revenue. That asset was lower quality — it had already absorbed a $47M goodwill impairment in 2023 — but a private buyer paying 1.4x for a services business is a data point on where non-software revenue clears. And management's own H1 buyback at a $6.37 average, versus the new $50M authorization approved August 3 at ~$8.23, tells you where the insiders with the most information found the shares attractive.

Valuation score 4.2: the multiple is fair-to-full on adjusted metrics and demanding on cash, the stock sits above the $8.04 analyst mean, and there is no identified catalyst for the market being wrong to the upside on price.

## Competitive Landscape

Certara is the category leader by penetration — 20 regulatory agencies as licensees, customers receiving over 90% of FDA new drug approvals since 2014, over 160,000 users. That penetration statistic establishes breadth, not value capture per approval or pricing power, and the revenue evidence is the better test.

On trajectory, the picture is mixed rather than deteriorating outright. Positive: software bookings +13.9%, modality mix now roughly 60% small molecule / 40% large molecule per management (Simcyp ~30% large molecule, QSP almost exclusively biologics), which aligns the portfolio with where incremental pharma R&D dollars go. The closest public pure-play, Simulations Plus, is in materially worse condition — a revenue guidance cut, accounting disclosures, and a plaintiff-firm investigation (which establishes nothing about wrongdoing but does signal category stress). Certara reaffirmed guidance, discloses no accounting issue, generates positive operating cash flow and carries ~1.0x net leverage.

Negative: net retention at 101.5% in Q2 (annual series 108.4% → 108.8% → 105.3%) means expansion within the installed base has nearly stopped. Services bookings -11.3% suggests share or relevance loss in that channel, or divestiture-related commercial disruption that management has not disentangled. The FY2025 risk factors name AI-enabled entrants able to compete "at lower investment points," established technology companies, and free open-source alternatives (R, PK-Sim) that cap PBPK/PopPK pricing. The 10-K goes further than most, attributing share-price volatility explicitly to "market perceptions regarding the impact that developments in AI and machine learning technologies may have on our business, industry and competitive position."

Management's counter-argument — that frontier models will handle reasoning while Certara owns the "vertical last mile" of proprietary data, embedded regulatory workflows, publication record and regulator relationships — is coherent and unproven. The July 2026 integration of NVIDIA's BioNeMo Agent Toolkit into Certara's platform is a supplier arrangement in which Certara licenses a toolkit for products it owns; no SEC filing confirms or quantifies it, and it should carry no valuation weight.

There is a notable tension between management's read of end markets ("in good shape," biotech funding positive, big pharma healthy, per the June conference) and Americas revenue down 1.8% with services bookings down 11.3%. One of those is wrong, and the reported numbers have the stronger claim.

Competitive position 5.3: leadership intact, moat narrative unresolved against a disclosed AI threat, retention trajectory clearly negative, mix trajectory positive.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill and intangible carrying values.** $718.1M of goodwill and $345.2M of intangibles against $966.5M of book equity. Management tested immediately before the disposal and concluded each reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value — but at May's $5.34 average repurchase price the market cap was near $820M, below book equity, a conventional trigger indicator. The track record on the one unit that was tested by an arm's-length sale is poor: a $47M impairment in 2023 followed by a $65.5M pretax loss on sale in 2026, roughly $112M of cumulative recognized destruction. An impairment would be non-cash and would not touch adjusted EBITDA or cash flow, but it would deepen already-negative tangible book and would likely be read as confirmation of the pricing skepticism.

**Leadership vacancy.** Four 8-Ks with Item 5.02 between December 2025 and August 2026. A new CEO from January 1, an **interim CFO (Faiz Mohammed) signing the Q2 10-Q with no permanent successor announced**, a new external Chief Commercial Officer effective August 1, a new CIO and a new Chief AI Officer. Executive recruiting expense rose 180% to $1.851M and reorganization expense to $4.187M from $1.085M. An unfilled CFO seat during a guidance ramp that depends on cost execution is a specific, identifiable risk to Q3 and Q4 delivery.

**Guidance ramp concentration.** As shown above, the low end is bridgeable on cost savings; the midpoint requires H2 adjusted EBITDA margin near 31.8% with no cost creep, against three consecutive quarterly misses and unquantified stranded costs inside the $13M savings figure.

**Dilution from the equity reset.** H1 2026 grants of 4,231,181 units equal 2.77% of shares outstanding in six months, struck at $5.13 (RSUs) and $3.11 (PSUs) after a -73.9% cumulative TSR since IPO versus -22.1% for management's own peer group. The CEO's May 11 grants of 2,411,220 units equal 1.58% of shares outstanding (1.56% post-issuance ownership dilution if all vest), with an estimated grant-date value near $10.3M using H1 blended averages. Non-vested RSUs plus PSUs at 6/30/26 total 6,757,516 units, 4.43% of shares — contingent on service and, for PSUs, on unmet performance conditions, so scenario dilution rather than current dilution. Equity compensation expense actually fell 12% to $13.414M because high-priced legacy awards rolled off, which understates the share-count consequence.

**Insider signal.** One open-market purchase in the reviewed Form 4 record: then-CEO Feehery, November 2025, $200,000 at $8.30. No insider bought in H1 2026 while the stock traded between $5.34 and $6.88 and the company repurchased $57.4M. This is weak evidence given blackout windows around the April-May divestiture and the CEO/CFO transitions, plus the large grants that conferred exposure without cash outlay — but it is not a bullish tell either. Leif Pedersen adopted a 10b5-1 plan on June 4 to sell up to 36,066 shares in early September; small and pre-planned.

**Ownership.** Arsenal Capital holds ~22.8%, with rights to nominate up to two directors. That is simultaneously a supply overhang and a plausible route to a strategic transaction — the clearest identifiable upside catalyst in the bull case.

**Other:** IRA price negotiation and most-favored-nation proposals compressing customer R&D budgets; 31% of FY2025 revenue in foreign currency (up from 28%); EU AI Act obligations from August 2, 2026 for a company embedding AI in regulatory-facing products; customer concentration low at top-10 = 24% of revenue with none above 10%.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped from 88.4% to 129.8% between July 20 and August 7 while the stock rose 17.7%. Put/call open interest is 0.01 — effectively no put open interest at all. Read literally, the listed market shows no hedging demand and near-exclusive call positioning into a 129.8% IV, which is consistent with speculative upside participation rather than informed downside positioning. Combined with 29.6% short interest and 6.1 days to cover, this is the mechanical setup for continued squeeze-driven volatility in either direction.

For a REDUCE, the honest statement is that the options market is not corroborating our view. Rich IV does, however, favor selling upside calls against retained exposure for holders who prefer a staged exit, and it argues strongly against expressing this view as a short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $10.50 (+27.5%)

Software bookings growth of 13.9% converts to accelerating software revenue in H2 and FY2027 as the point-in-time license component normalizes. The ~$13M cost program lands with more net benefit than skeptics assume, pushing FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin to the 31% high end and setting FY2027 up above $120M. FDA New Approach Methodologies guidance begins converting into paid work in 2027, and the Non-Animal Navigator plus Certara IQ capture it. The new CEO, CCO and Chief AI Officer execute a commercial reset that returns net retention above 105%. APAC compounds at 15%+ off a 10% base. On $120M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA at 14x, EV of $1,680M less $110M net debt gives $1,570M ÷ 155M shares = $10.13; a strategic transaction with Arsenal's 22.8% stake as fulcrum, or a squeeze of the 29.6% short base, could overshoot toward $11-12. Contingent consideration is now fully settled at zero, removing recurring P&L noise, and $184M of cash plus an undrawn revolver funds the transition.

### Bear Case — $5.25 (-36.2%)

The H2 ramp misses because stranded costs absorb more of the $13M than disclosed and because the interim CFO transition slows execution. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or below $0.31 and FY2027 consensus of $0.40 gets cut toward $0.32-0.35, as the 30- and 90-day revision trends already imply. Americas revenue keeps contracting, net retention drops below 100%, and the market re-rates a 0-2% grower with negative tangible book toward a 6% free-cash-flow yield: $45M ÷ 0.06 = $750M market cap = $4.85/share. A goodwill impairment against the $718M balance, following the pattern already established on the divested unit, provides the trigger. This is not a distress case — leverage remains under 1.0x and there is no maturity before 2031 — it is a multiple case, and the stock traded at $4.45 as recently as May 15.

### Base Case — $7.25 (-12.0%)

FY2026 lands near the guidance low-to-mid range at $0.32-0.34 adjusted EPS and 29-30% margin. FY2027 revenue grows ~3% to ~$386M with adjusted EBITDA near $116M. At 12x EV/adjusted EBITDA — a fair multiple for a 0-4% grower with 62% gross margins and 1.0x leverage — EV is $1,392M, less $110M net debt gives $1,282M, or $8.27 per share on 155M shares. At 11x it is $7.52. Cross-checking on cash: normalized continuing free cash flow of $45-50M at a 5.5% required yield implies $818-909M market cap ($5.35-5.95); at 4.5% it implies $1,000-1,111M ($6.55-7.25). Blending the EBITDA and FCF approaches, with weight to the cash view because adjusted EPS is flattered by the tax convention, gives **$7.25**.

Probability-weighted: 25% bull at $10.50, 50% base at $7.25, 25% bear at $5.25 = **$7.56**, or -8% from $8.235.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Action: REDUCE.** Trim exposure into current strength rather than around a dated catalyst — the stock is above the $8.04 analyst mean and the next hard datapoint (Q3 results, expected early November 2026) is the most likely source of downside given the H2 ramp requirement. Holders who want to stage the exit can sell into the elevated 129.8% implied volatility via covered calls, but should not maintain full weight through the Q3 print. Appropriate residual exposure for existing holders: no more than half of a normal position size, retained on the software-bookings and NAM-adoption optionality.

**Base case target: $7.25** (-12.0%). 12x EV/adjusted EBITDA on ~$116M FY2027 adjusted EBITDA blended with a 4.5-5.5% yield on $45-50M of continuing free cash flow.
**Bull case target: $10.50** (+27.5%). 14x on $120M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA, requiring software revenue acceleration and full cost realization.
**Bear case target: $5.25** (-36.2%). 6% free-cash-flow yield on $45M following an FY2027 estimate reset.
**Timeframe:** 6-12 months, anchored on Q3 2026 results (early November) and FY2027 guidance (late February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. Price at or below $6.25-6.50 with guidance intact — this restores roughly a 5% free-cash-flow yield and closes most of the gap to base case.
2. Q3 2026 delivering adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 30.5% with quarterly net revenue retention recovering above 104%, evidencing that the cost program nets through and expansion has resumed.
3. A named permanent CFO plus disclosure quantifying the net incremental benefit of the $13M program after stranded costs.
4. Software revenue growth accelerating above 10% year over year, confirming that the 13.9% bookings growth converts.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 revenue or adjusted EPS guidance cut on the Q3 call, or a fourth consecutive adjusted-EPS miss.
2. Quarterly net revenue retention printing below 100%.
3. A goodwill impairment charge against any of the three reporting units, which would validate the carrying-value skepticism the divestiture already supports.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS cut below $0.35 (currently $0.40, already -19.5% over 90 days).

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, early November 2026.

## Conclusion

Certara owns a defensible position in a category with a credible multi-decade tailwind: regulators are actively pushing toward computational methods, and Certara's software sits inside 20 agencies and the workflows behind the large majority of FDA approvals. The balance sheet gives management five years to execute — $184M of cash, an undrawn $100M revolver, sub-1.0x net leverage, and a 2031 maturity with 1% annual amortization. Contingent consideration is settled to zero, the low-margin medical writing business is gone, and the H1 buyback at a $6.37 average shrank the share count 4.2% in six months at prices that now look well-judged.

None of that makes the stock attractive at $8.235. Revenue grows 1.6% with the 65% Americas base contracting. Net retention has fallen to 101.5%. Comparable-period free cash flow dropped $9.5M. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 23.6% in 90 days and 11.0% in the last 30, and three of the last four quarters missed. The H2 earnings ramp requires 24-60% sequential half-over-half growth in adjusted net income with no revenue seasonality to help, executed by a management team with an interim CFO. And the price paid for all of this — 3.65x EV/revenue, 12.2x EV/adjusted EBITDA, and 26-28x properly computed free cash flow — is above the analyst mean target, having risen since our last review while the estimates fell.

The right response to a higher price on lower estimates is to reduce, not to rationalize. We would re-engage in the low $6s, or on evidence that the software bookings and cost program convert into the margin and retention numbers the current multiple already assumes. With 29.6% of the float short, 6.1 days to cover, and implied volatility at 129.8% against effectively zero put open interest, this is a position to trim, not to short.

**Thesis:** Certara's biosimulation franchise is genuine but currently growing 1-2%, with the ~65% Americas base contracting 1.8% and quarterly net revenue retention at 101.5%. Adjusted EPS guidance requires a 24-60% H2 ramp in adjusted net income with no revenue seasonality, executed under an interim CFO. Properly computed continuing free cash flow (~$47M, including capitalized software) yields only 3.7%, so the price already assumes the cost program and software bookings convert.

**Variant view:** Consensus screens see ~7.5% FCF yield and a reaffirmed guide. Including $24M+ of capitalized software halves the yield, and guidance's low end is bridgeable only on cost savings whose net benefit after stranded costs is undisclosed. Software bookings +13.9% is real but has produced only 5.8% software revenue growth.
