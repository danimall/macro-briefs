## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the largest acreage holder and a top producer in the Williston Basin, with 1.3 million net acres and average daily production of 276,620 Boepd. The company generated $2.04 billion in operating cash flow in FY2025 despite a 15% decline in average realized crude prices, demonstrating the resilience of its low-cost production base. The reported 95% collapse in net income (from $849M to $44M) is almost entirely explained by a $539M non-cash goodwill impairment from the Enerplus acquisition; underlying cash generation was essentially flat year-over-year.

The stock has rallied 61% from its late-October 2025 lows near $84 to $143.47, driven by the surge in Brent crude above $125 following Hormuz disruptions. With only 17% of 2026 oil production hedged, Chord is among the most leveraged E&P names to sustained elevated oil prices. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up 198% over the past 90 days, reflecting oil price pass-through. However, the stock now trades above the Benzinga analyst consensus target of $132.80 (which was set when oil was lower) and within 3% of its 52-week high of $148.42. Multiple insiders sold shares at prices 15–25% below the current level, and the options market is pricing near-term stress with IV in backwardation (46.9% near-term vs. 43.6% long-term) and elevated put skew. The risk/reward at current prices favors patience.

## Company Overview

Chord Energy Corporation was formed through the 2022 merger of Oasis Petroleum and Whiting Petroleum, and has since become the dominant Williston Basin consolidator through the $5.4B Enerplus acquisition (May 2024) and two XTO Energy asset purchases (2023 and October 2025 for $542M). The company operates 5,025 gross producing wells with an average 78% working interest. Proved reserves stand at 917.5 MMBoe (56% crude oil, 69% proved developed) with a PV-10 of $9.1 billion. The company plans to run 4–5 operated rigs in 2026 and turn-in-line 135–165 gross operated wells.

Capital allocation targets returning 75%+ of adjusted free cash flow to shareholders (when leverage is below 0.5x at $65 WTI/$3 HH) through a $5.20/share annualized base dividend and a $1.0 billion share repurchase program ($952M remaining). Chord operates with 676 employees from its Houston headquarters with field offices in North Dakota.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement — Headline Distortion vs. Cash Reality

The headline financials require significant adjustment. FY2025 net income of $44M and the apparent ~197x trailing P/E are artifacts of a $539M non-cash goodwill impairment recorded in Q2 2025, which also inflated the effective tax rate to 81.7% (since goodwill impairment is non-deductible). Adjusting for the impairment, pre-tax income was approximately $783M.

Revenue declined 7% from FY2024 despite a 19% increase in production volumes, entirely attributable to the 15% decline in average realized crude prices ($73.67 → $62.78/Bbl). Per-unit operating costs remained well-controlled: LOE at $9.73/Boe (flat YoY), GPT improved to $2.88/Boe from $3.14, and production taxes declined to $2.89/Boe from $3.91 (partially aided by a North Dakota extraction tax exemption refund). DD&A increased to $14.12/Boe from $12.30, reflecting higher acquisition-based depletion from Enerplus and XTO assets.

The FCF margin of 14.2% ($693M on $4.88B revenue) is solid for an E&P company operating in a year where WTI averaged roughly $62–69/Bbl. Operating cash flow of $2.04B exceeded net income by a factor of 46x, reflecting the dominance of non-cash charges in GAAP earnings and high-quality underlying cash generation.

### Balance Sheet

- Total liquidity: $2.16 billion ($190M cash + $1.97B undrawn revolver)
- Long-term debt: $1.50 billion ($750M 6.75% notes due 2033, $750M 6.00% notes due 2030)
- No near-term maturities (earliest: October 2030)
- Debt-to-equity: 0.62x
- Current ratio: 1.06x (tight but above the 1.0x covenant minimum)

Debt roughly doubled during FY2025 as Chord issued $1.5B in new senior notes to fund the XTO acquisition and refinance the $400M of 2026 maturities. Interest expense rose 42% to $80.2M. The maturity profile is now clean, but the higher debt load increases breakeven oil prices. At current elevated oil prices, leverage is manageable. At $60 WTI, the $80M+ in annual interest expense becomes more burdensome relative to reduced operating cash flow.

### Capital Return

In FY2025, Chord paid $5.20/share in dividends (~$303M aggregate) and repurchased $364.5M of stock at a weighted average price of $104.39/share (3.49M shares), for total capital return of approximately $668M. This compares to approximately $974M in FY2024, when the company paid $10.15/share in base-plus-variable dividends before eliminating the variable component as oil prices weakened. The $1.0B buyback program authorized in August 2025 has $952M remaining.

### Earnings Beat/Miss Record

The quarterly track record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters. The Q1 2025 beat (+12.7%) occurred during a higher price environment, while the Q2 2025 miss (-6.1%) coincided with the oil price decline and goodwill impairment. This inconsistency reduces confidence in forward estimates, though the estimate revisions data suggests the consensus is now recalibrating to the current oil price environment.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Growth

Chord has delivered substantial production growth through acquisition: 173,425 Boepd (FY2023) → 232,737 (FY2024) → 276,620 (FY2025), a 59% increase over two years. The 2026 program of 4–5 operated rigs and 135–165 well TILs should sustain production at or slightly above current levels, though organic volume growth will be modest. The growth story now centers on capital efficiency and cost-per-barrel improvement rather than headline volume increases.

## Valuation Assessment

At $143.47, Chord trades at approximately 8.0x the FY2026E EPS of $17.98 and 9.7x FY2027E EPS of $14.74. Against the peer median P/E of 11.4x, this appears optically cheap. However:

1. **The earnings estimates embed $100+ oil.** If Brent sustains at $125, the 8x multiple is genuinely cheap. If oil normalizes to $85–95 (the diplomatic resolution scenario at 5–8% probability, or a broader demand slowdown), FY2026 EPS likely compresses to $10–12, putting the stock at 12–14x — at or above the peer median.

2. **The stock is above one analyst consensus but below another.** The Benzinga consensus (from March 2026, 10 analysts) shows an average of $132.80. The Yahoo Finance consensus (17 analysts) shows a mean target of $165.35 and median of $165.00, with a range of $129–$226. At $143.47, the stock has already captured a substantial portion of the commodity repricing relative to the earlier consensus but remains below the more current Yahoo Finance consensus.

3. **Dividend yield at 3.6%** ($5.20/share base dividend) provides some income support. This was reduced from $10.15/share in FY2024 as oil weakened. At current oil prices, Chord could reinstate variable dividends or accelerate buybacks but has not yet signaled this.

4. **Enterprise value perspective.** Market cap of ~$8.1B plus ~$1.3B net debt ($1.5B debt less $190M cash) = ~$9.4B EV against PV-10 of $9.1B (calculated at SEC pricing, which uses a 12-month trailing average that lags current spot). At sustained $100+ WTI, reserve values would be substantially higher. At $60 WTI, PV-10 shrinks by roughly 20%.

The valuation is fair-to-full at current prices if oil sustains above $100; cheap if oil moves higher; stretched if oil reverts to $70–80. The binary commodity exposure makes a high valuation score inappropriate when the stock is within 3% of its 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

Chord holds a commanding competitive position within the Williston Basin:

- **Largest acreage position** (1.3M net acres) of any Williston operator
- **Scale advantages** enabling 4-mile laterals, centralized infrastructure, reduced per-unit costs
- **In-house marketing** optimizing pipeline vs. rail transportation
- **Low breakeven.** XTO acquired assets have ~$40/Bbl breakevens; the broader portfolio is competitive at sub-$50 WTI

The consolidation strategy has been well-executed. The Enerplus and XTO acquisitions provide overlapping acreage that enables longer laterals and shared infrastructure, reducing well-level costs. The company's 78% average working interest provides operational control.

Against E&P peers, Chord's geographic concentration is both a strength (scale, cost, efficiency) and a vulnerability (single-basin risk). Competitors like SM Energy and Matador Resources operate across multiple basins, providing diversification that Chord lacks.

The DAPL pipeline risk is a uniquely Chord-specific competitive concern. Three of five Army Corps alternatives analyzed would require abandonment, removal, or rerouting of the disputed segment. Loss of DAPL would materially weaken Williston Basin crude pricing relative to other domestic basins.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Commodity Price Sensitivity (Extreme).** With 83% of 2026 oil production unhedged, a $10/Bbl decline in WTI reduces annualized cash flow by approximately $350–400M. A 10% decline in SEC pricing reduces proved reserves by 30.4 MMBoe and PV-10 by $1.8 billion (nearly 20% of total).

**2. Geographic Concentration.** Essentially all production derives from the Williston Basin. Weather events (North Dakota winter operations), transportation constraints, or regulatory changes specific to the region would impair the entire production base.

**3. DAPL Pipeline Uncertainty.** The Army Corps completed its final Environmental Impact Statement in December 2025, with a formal decision expected in 2026. Three of five alternatives analyzed could require pipeline abandonment. Chord states that DAPL cessation would cause Williston crude prices to "weaken materially."

**4. Elevated Debt Load.** Long-term debt doubled to $1.5B during FY2025. Interest expense of $80M+ per year is manageable at current oil prices but becomes a significant fixed cost if oil declines.

**5. Tariff & Trade Policy Uncertainty.** The 10-K specifically highlights evolving U.S. tariff and trade policies during 2025 as creating uncertainty, with potential impacts on costs and commodity demand.

**6. Fort Berthold Regulatory & Litigation Risk.** The ownership of riverbed minerals on the Fort Berthold Reservation is actively litigated, with the case on remand before a D.C. Federal District Court. The MHA Nation can impose independent taxes, fees, and approvals on Chord's operations.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 46.9% is 9.5pp above 1-year historical volatility of 37.4%, and the term structure is in backwardation (46.9% near-term vs. 43.6% long-term), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst or elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew is elevated at 4.6%, and the put/call volume ratio of 1.08 confirms incremental put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.38 is lower, suggesting the near-term put activity is fresh positioning rather than structural hedging.

IV has compressed 10pp over the past two weeks (from 56.9% to 46.9%) as the stock rallied 7.5%, suggesting some resolution of uncertainty, but the backwardation structure and put skew indicate the market views current prices as potentially unstable. The implied near-term move of ±7.3% ($133–$154 range) brackets both the analyst consensus and a meaningful pullback.

Short interest at 6.6% of float with 2.6 days to cover is moderate — not crowded but present.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($170-$190)

Oil sustains above $100 WTI / $120+ Brent through 2026 as Hormuz disruptions persist and US shale production discipline continues (supported by 7 independent confirmations of supply inelasticity). At $100+ WTI, Chord generates approximately $2.5–3.0B in operating cash flow, enabling accelerated buybacks from the $952M remaining program, potential reinstatement of variable dividends, and rapid deleveraging. FY2026E EPS of $17.98 at 10x = $180. The DAPL decision resolves favorably. Chord's low-cost, high-working-interest Williston position makes it a preferred domestic operator capturing the oil supply premium without international exposure risks.

### Bear Case ($85-$100)

Oil normalizes to $75–85 WTI on diplomatic resolution, OPEC+ production increases, or global demand weakness from the confirmed stagflationary environment. Chord's unhedged production base means EPS compresses to $6–9, putting the stock at 15–17x at current prices. DAPL pipeline ruling goes against the industry, weakening Williston Basin netbacks by $3–5/Bbl. The $1.5B debt load creates cash flow pressure at lower prices. Buyback and dividend reduction probable. At $80 WTI, fair value approximates 8–9x normalized EPS of $10–12 = $80–108.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Stock pulls back to $115–120, providing 15%+ upside to the $165 median analyst target and a more favorable entry point relative to commodity risk.
2. DAPL ruling resolves favorably, removing the single largest idiosyncratic overhang.
3. Management reinstates variable dividend or announces accelerated buybacks at current oil prices, signaling internal confidence in sustained commodity strength.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. Oil declines below $85 WTI for more than 2 consecutive weeks without a proportionate stock correction, creating a valuation gap.
2. Q1 2026 production misses guidance (below 270,000 Boepd) or per-unit costs rise above $13/Boe (ex-DD&A), signaling operational deterioration.
3. DAPL adverse ruling with no viable transportation alternative articulated by management.

### Reassessment Timeline
- Q1 2026 earnings (expected late May 2026) — will confirm realized pricing at elevated levels and provide updated 2026 guidance.
- DAPL Army Corps decision — expected in 2026; binary risk event.
- Oil price trajectory over next 30–60 days — if Brent sustains above $120, the bull case strengthens materially.

## Conclusion

Chord Energy is a well-run, low-cost Williston Basin producer that directly benefits from the current elevated oil price environment. Operating cash flow proved resilient through the FY2025 oil downturn, the balance sheet has no near-term maturity risk, and the consolidation strategy has created meaningful scale advantages. However, the stock has already rallied 61% from its lows and trades above the Benzinga analyst consensus target, within 3% of its 52-week high. Insider selling at materially lower prices, a mixed earnings beat/miss record, and extreme commodity price sensitivity at 83% unhedged leave the risk/reward unattractive at $143.47. The options market confirms this assessment with IV backwardation and elevated put demand.

The stock is a HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis: Chord would be a compelling BUY at $115–120, where the risk/reward properly compensates for commodity tail risk and the DAPL overhang. At current prices, the positive estimate revision momentum and structural oil thesis are largely priced in.
