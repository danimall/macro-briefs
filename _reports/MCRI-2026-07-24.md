**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Monarch Casino & Resort is a small, focused gaming operator with two assets: the Atlantis in Reno, Nevada, and the Monarch Casino Resort Spa in Black Hawk, Colorado. It runs an amenity-led model (high-end hotel, spa, dining used to drive higher-margin gaming) with an essentially debt-free balance sheet, high free-cash-flow conversion, and a founder-family management team that owns roughly 35% of the shares. The business has compounded through a multi-year Black Hawk expansion completed in 2022 and now enters a phase where capex is normalizing and depreciation is rolling off, both of which mechanically lift reported margins and EPS.

The stock has re-rated sharply. From roughly $99 in mid-April 2026, MCRI ran to a 52-week high near $136 on the back of a record Q1 (revenue +8.9%, EPS +44.8%) and a record Q2 (EPS $1.78, ~4.8% above consensus). It sold off after the July 21 print to ~$117 and now trades near $119, still up more than 20% from spring levels. At ~18x consensus current-year EPS of $6.64 and ~17x next-year $6.89, the multiple is near the higher end of the small-cap regional-casino cohort. The underlying quality is real, but a large part of the recent EPS acceleration is optics: 2024 was depressed by a $27.6M litigation charge, and the Black Hawk hotel tower became fully depreciated in Q4 2025, cutting quarterly D&A by ~$2.7M without any change in operating performance.

The dominant company-specific issue is the PCL construction litigation, which in February 2025 produced a $74.6M principal judgment against Monarch (accrued at $78.5M as of Q1'26 and growing ~$1.1M/quarter). The appeal will likely take another 12-24 months, and PCL's cross-appeal for interest, fees, and costs could push the total higher. Monarch has the liquidity to pay ($120M cash, $99.4M undrawn revolver, zero drawn debt), so this is a cash overhang rather than a solvency threat, but it constrains the special-dividend/M&A optionality that forms part of the bull case. We rate MCRI a HOLD (growth-hold) at $119 with a $126 base-case target: a quality compounder at a fair price, not an asymmetric entry.

## Company Overview

Monarch operates exactly two properties, which makes it a rare pure two-asset gaming company:

**Atlantis Casino Resort Spa (Reno, NV)** — ~61,000 sq ft casino, 817 rooms, ~1,200 slots, ~33 table games, eight restaurants, a 30,000 sq ft spa. Its structural differentiator is being the only hotel physically connected via enclosed skywalk to the Reno-Sparks Convention Center, which supports the higher-ADR convention segment. Reno is a mature, competitive local market (roughly 13 casinos above $12M in revenue, a ~$968M market) under sustained pressure from California tribal gaming.

**Monarch Casino Resort Spa Black Hawk (Black Hawk, CO)** — ~60,000 sq ft casino, 516 rooms, ~1,000 slots, ~43 table games. It is the first property encountered when entering Black Hawk from Denver via Highway 119. This is the growth engine, transformed by a multi-year expansion (completed early 2022) into a high-end resort targeting Denver Front Range high-value players who previously traveled to Las Vegas.

The moat sits in Colorado. Gaming there is constitutionally restricted to three mountain towns, with Black Hawk accounting for roughly 76% of state gaming revenue, and scarce developable land creates a high barrier to new entrants. Amendment 77 (2020) removed betting limits and added table game types, widening the high-end opportunity. The strategy is amenity-led: use superior non-gaming facilities to pull higher-margin play, supported by a cross-property Monarch Rewards loyalty program. The Farahi family runs a hands-on, cost-focused operating model and controls roughly 35% of the equity.

## Financial Analysis

Monarch is a high-quality, cash-generative, essentially debt-free operator.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $477.9M | $501.5M | $522.2M | $545.1M | $136.6M (+8.9%) |
| Net income | $87.5M | $82.4M | $72.8M* | $101.4M | $27.6M (+38.9%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $3.84 | $5.43 | $1.52 (+44.8%) |

*2024 net income absorbed a $27.6M ($1.14/sh) PCL litigation loss.

Balance-sheet resilience is a genuine strength. Altman Z-Score is 3.64 (safe zone), debt/equity is 0.33 with zero drawn debt, and the $100M Wells Fargo revolver (maturing 1/1/2028) sits undrawn. Total Leverage Ratio was 0.0x against a 1.5x covenant limit; Fixed Charge Coverage was 144.9x against a 1.1x minimum. Returns are solid: ROA 14.2%, ROE 18.9%, net margin 18.6%, FCF margin 23.6% (FCF ~$128M in 2025). Cash built from $58.8M at the start of 2025 to $96.5M at year-end to $120.1M in Q1'26, with Q1'26 operating cash flow of $48.5M.

Two mechanical tailwinds warrant separating from operating performance. First, D&A is rolling off: Q1'26 D&A fell to $10.5M from $13.2M as the Black Hawk hotel tower (in service since Q4 2020) became fully depreciated in Q4 2025. That ~$2.7M/quarter reduction flows straight to reported earnings without any incremental investment. Second, capex is normalizing, from $51M in 2023 to $37M in 2025, and just $7.5M in Q1'26 versus $16M a year prior, as the expansion cycle ends. The combination structurally steps up free-cash-flow generation. The flip side is that a meaningful portion of the EPS growth is non-operating: the 2024→2025 net income surge is inflated by the litigation base effect, and forward EPS growth is flattered by the depreciation cliff.

The current ratio of 0.86 looks weak but is an artifact of the $78.5M PCL liability sitting in current liabilities, not a liquidity problem. Adjusting for it, liquidity is ample.

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly: 2025 saw $72.7M in buybacks plus $21.9M in dividends (dividend initiated Q2 2023, currently $1.20/yr), and Q1'26 added $17.6M in buybacks with 971,503 shares still authorized. Share count has fallen from ~19.2M in 2023 to ~17.7M in Q1'26.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is credible but modest in scale. Q1'26 revenue grew 8.9% on casino market-share gains at both properties plus F&B and hotel margin improvement; Q2'26 grew ~4.2% to ~$142.6M with EPS of $1.78. The four-quarter earnings-surprise record is 3 beats and 1 miss, and Q2 beat by ~4.8%, supporting reasonable confidence in near-term consensus.

Estimate revisions temper the enthusiasm. Current-quarter EPS was cut 5.0% over 90 days (to $1.70), next-quarter down 1.2%, and current-year down 0.5% to $6.64; only next-year edged up 1.2% to $6.89. This is broadly flat-to-slightly-negative revision momentum, not the +30% velocity that would signal the market is systematically under-modeling an earnings acceleration. The growth here is a mid-single-digit organic compounder augmented by margin tailwinds (D&A roll-off, expansion capex ending), not a re-rating catalyst.

The larger growth question is the absence of a defined next project. Black Hawk is the structurally advantaged asset with room to keep trading up-market to Front Range high-end players, and Colorado sports betting (live since 2020, Monarch holds a Master License) is a modest tailwind. But there is no announced third property, and the mature Reno market can only hold or gain incremental share against California tribal competition; hotel occupancy at Atlantis dipped to 82.6% in 2025 from 84.5% in 2023, and to 79.6% in Q1'26, as competitors cut mid-week rates. The building cash pile creates optionality for a third property, special dividend, or accelerated buyback, but nothing is committed, and the PCL overhang ties the family's hands in the near term.

The score trajectory shows growth prospects improved from 5.5 to 6.5 over prior reports. The current data neither confirms an acceleration nor reverses the improvement; the slightly negative near-term revisions argue for settling at 5.8 rather than extending the upgrade.

## Valuation Assessment

At $119.17, MCRI trades near the upper end of the small-cap regional-casino cohort. The trailing multiple of ~19x sits against the casinos & gaming peer median P/E of 19.9x, but that median is distorted: several peers (PENN, GDEN, CZR) carry negative or non-meaningful P/Es. The cleaner comparables are Red Rock Resorts (RRR) at 19.9x and Churchill Downs (CHDN) at 15.9x. On that basis, Monarch is fairly valued to modestly rich for a mid-single-digit organic grower, though the pristine balance sheet (zero net debt versus levered peers) justifies a premium on an enterprise-value basis.

The analyst target range is $114 low / $144 high, mean $126.5, median $125. The stock is trading essentially at consensus, and the analyst mix has degraded: buy ratings fell from 2 to 1 and holds rose from 4 to 5 over the past month. On a name where estimate revisions are flat-to-negative and the multiple already prices in the D&A-driven margin lift, we do not treat these targets as stale floors; consensus optimism and the price are broadly aligned rather than the market lagging an accelerating fundamental. The dividend yield of ~1.0% offers little valuation support on its own.

The bull-case valuation argument rests on the structurally higher free-cash-flow generation as capex normalizes (FCF yield already ~6% on ~$128M against a ~$2.2B market cap) plus the optionality of a large cash balance eventually deployed. That is real, but it is a quality-at-a-fair-price case, not a mispricing. The below-peer-median headline P/E reflects a small, concentrated, mature-market business, and the burden is on identifying why the market is wrong, which the current evidence does not meet. Valuation scores 5.2: fairly priced with balanced risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Monarch's competitive position is stable-to-improving, anchored by the Black Hawk moat. Colorado's constitutional three-town restriction and land scarcity in Black Hawk create durable barriers to new supply, and Monarch's first-property-from-Denver location plus its high-end amenity build-out let it capture share of the growing Front Range high-value segment. Amendment 77's removal of betting limits expanded the addressable high-roller opportunity. The company has posted casino market-share gains at both properties in recent quarters, and the five-year TSR (156 indexed versus a peer group at 89) reflects meaningful operating outperformance.

Reno is the weaker leg. The Atlantis holds a genuine niche via its physical connection to the convention center, but the local market is mature and under sustained promotional pressure from California tribal casinos, with occupancy trending down. Monarch is defending share here, not expanding.

The long-term structural threat is iGaming/online gaming, which competes with physical casinos for wallet share; Monarch actively lobbies against iGaming expansion. A future legalization of Denver-metro gaming would be the most damaging Black Hawk scenario, though such initiatives (most recently at the Arapahoe racetrack) have been repeatedly voted down. The trajectory is favorable in Colorado and defensive in Nevada, supporting a competitive-position score of 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**PCL litigation (the defining risk).** The Denver District Court's February 2025 judgment awarded PCL $74.6M in principal against $144,894 for Monarch on counterclaims. Monarch has appealed (Notice filed 5/30/2025); PCL cross-appealed on interest, fees, and costs, with briefing active into mid-2026 and resolution likely 12-24 months out. Wells Fargo waived the resulting event-of-default conditionally. The liability could grow via the cross-appeal, adds a few million per quarter in legal/interest cost, and constrains near-term capital-return optionality. A favorable appeal outcome would be a positive catalyst; an adverse one raising the total would be a negative.

**Two-asset concentration.** All cash flow depends on two properties in two markets. Any localized shock (Denver-area gaming legalization, Reno competitive escalation, or severe weather closing I-70/US-6 into Black Hawk) hits disproportionately.

**Competitive/promotional pressure at Atlantis:** California tribal growth and an aggressive local promotional environment, reflected in declining occupancy.

**Governance and key-person risk.** The Farahi family (~35%) controls board elections and major transactions; minority holders are effectively partners to an entrenched founding family. Related-party leases (the Atlantis parking lot and shared driveway from Biggest Little Investments, plus billboard/storage leases from Farahi affiliates, together roughly $0.56M/quarter) are modest but ongoing. Monarch operates without a separately titled CFO, with the Chief Accounting Officer serving that function. A December 19, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K flagged a director/officer change whose specifics could not be confirmed, a governance item to verify. Explicit dependence on CEO John Farahi and President Bob Farahi.

**Regulatory:** Colorado can raise gaming taxes to a 40% statutory cap (currently graduated, max tier 20%); smoking-ban and iGaming-expansion dynamics.

Idiosyncratic risk is concentrated and partly binary (the appeal), keeping the risk-factors score at 5.0 despite the strong balance sheet that caps downside severity.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and reflects post-earnings normalization. ATM IV collapsed from 83.6% to 30.8% (down ~53 points) as the July 21 earnings event resolved, and the put/call OI ratio fell to a very light 0.08 (call-heavy, with a -7.8% one-month skew). Near-term IV (30.8%) sits modestly above one-year historical vol (25.4%), so options are slightly rich, and the term structure is in mild backwardation (30.8% near-term versus 25.6% at six months), pricing some residual near-term uncertainty. Short interest is low at 4.3% of float (2.4 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded-short setup nor a squeeze dynamic. The signal is neutral-to-mildly-constructive and does not challenge the fundamental HOLD view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Monarch is a debt-free, ~24% FCF-margin operator with a genuine Colorado moat, entering a phase where capex normalization and the D&A roll-off structurally lift free cash flow. The building cash balance (approaching $120M and growing) creates optionality for a third property, special dividend, or accelerated buyback. Continued casino market-share gains and hotel/F&B margin improvement drive mid-single-digit revenue growth with operating leverage. A favorable PCL appeal outcome removes the cash overhang and unlocks that optionality. Fair value $140-150 (~21-22x forward EPS on a de-risked, cash-rich compounder), roughly matching the $144 analyst high.

**Bear case.** The recent EPS acceleration is substantially optics (litigation base effect plus depreciation cliff), the Reno market keeps eroding under California tribal and promotional pressure, and there is no defined next growth project. The PCL cross-appeal raises the total liability, legal costs persist, and the ~35% family control with related-party leases limits the independent check on capital allocation. On disappointing organic growth and multiple compression toward the low end of the peer range (~15-16x), the stock derates to $95-100, roughly -17% to -20% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold). Horizon: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: $126** — ~18.5x next-year consensus EPS of $6.89, holding roughly the current multiple as mid-single-digit organic growth and margin tailwinds carry earnings modestly higher. This sits at the consensus mean ($126.5) and implies ~6% upside plus the ~1% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $144** — ~21x forward EPS on a favorable PCL resolution plus deployment of the cash balance; +21% from current.
- **Bear case target: $98** — ~15x forward EPS on Reno erosion, no growth project, and an adverse cross-appeal; -18% from current.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Favorable PCL appeal ruling that materially reduces or eliminates the ~$78.5M liability, freeing capital-return optionality.
2. Announcement of accretive capital deployment (a third property at a sensible multiple, or a special dividend/accelerated buyback) alongside a pullback toward the low-$100s.
3. Estimate-revision inflection turning positive (current-year and next-quarter revisions moving up) confirming organic acceleration beyond the D&A-driven optics.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Adverse PCL outcome that raises the total liability materially and forces a draw on the revolver or halts buybacks.
2. Organic revenue growth decelerating below ~2% YoY at either property, or Atlantis occupancy falling below ~78% on structural share loss.
3. A governance red flag surfacing from the unconfirmed December 2025 officer change, or expansion of related-party dealings.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 2026, roughly late October), and any PCL appeal docket developments.

## Conclusion

Monarch is a well-run, financially pristine gaming operator with a real structural moat in Black Hawk and a disciplined capital-return record. The recent EPS strength is genuine at the operating level but flattered by the litigation base effect and the depreciation roll-off, and the stock's ~20% re-rating since April has moved it to a fair-to-full ~18x forward multiple sitting right at consensus. Near-term estimate revisions have turned slightly negative, analyst sentiment has degraded, and the ~$78.5M PCL overhang caps the optionality that anchors the bull case. The balance sheet limits downside, but there is no asymmetric setup: no cheap entry, no identified inflection catalyst, and a binary appeal that could cut either way. We would turn constructive on a favorable PCL ruling, a value-accretive capital deployment, or a pullback into the low-$100s. Consistent with prior reports, the recommendation remains HOLD; the reiteration reflects an unchanged quality-at-fair-price profile rather than any deterioration.
