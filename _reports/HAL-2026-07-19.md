**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Halliburton sits at the intersection of two conflicting forces. It is a technology-differentiated leader in oilfield services, the most North America-weighted of the "Big Three," with a leading electric-fracturing platform (Zeus IQ, now ~50% of its NAM frac fleet) and a diversified international/offshore franchise. At the same time, it is in the middle of a real cyclical earnings decline: 2025 revenue fell 3% to $22.2B, operating income dropped 41% to $2.26B (roughly $715M of which was incremental impairment, but core "total operations" income still fell 19%), and net income nearly halved to $1.28B. Management's own primary compensation metric, ROCE, fell to 8.9% from 16.1%. This is genuine deterioration concentrated in North American land pressure pumping, where the company has been stacking uneconomic fleets.

The near-term picture is stabilization rather than recovery. Q1 2026 revenue was flat YoY at $5.40B with operating income of $679M and no impairments, but the geographic mix turned adverse: Latin America +22% and Europe/Africa/CIS +11% were offset by a 13% decline in Middle East/Asia driven by conflict-related work cancellations and force majeure in Saudi Arabia and Qatar. That Middle East disruption hit HAL's highest-margin international business and is a fresh drag not captured in the FY2025 numbers. Consensus estimates have risen 7-8% over the past 90 days (current-year EPS to $2.38, next-year to $2.94), and the company has a three-of-four-quarters beat record, so forward visibility is improving even as trailing results remain soft.

The stock has fallen from a $43.59 high in May to $35.22, roughly 19% off its peak, while fundamentals have modestly improved, a setup that ordinarily strengthens the risk/reward. What tempers this is the absence of any insider vote of confidence (executives sold at $40-42; none have bought at $35), a two-sided Q2 catalyst on July 21, and a macro backdrop where the Middle East normalization that would reverse the conflict drag would also compress the oil-price support the entire sector rides on. We rate HAL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the July 21 print is the natural reassessment point.

## Company Overview

Halliburton is one of the world's largest diversified oilfield services providers, with ~46,000 employees operating in more than 70 countries. It serves major, national, and independent producers across the reservoir lifecycle through two segments:

- **Completion & Production (C&P)** — ~58% of 2025 revenue ($12.8B). Cementing, stimulation (hydraulic fracturing), specialty chemicals, artificial lift, completion tools, and well intervention. This is the segment most exposed to North American land activity and pressure pumping, and the one that led the 2025 decline (C&P operating income fell 21%).
- **Drilling & Evaluation (D&E)** — ~42% of revenue ($9.4B). Directional drilling (Sperry), drilling fluids (Baroid), wireline, testing/subsea, and Landmark software.

The company's historical strength is North American completions, where it holds a leading share. Its differentiation is increasingly technology-led: the Zeus IQ electric frac platform, iCruise rotary steerable systems, and LOGIX automation, deployed to defend margins as commodity pricing pressures the base business. US revenue has fallen to 39% of the total from 44% in 2023, reflecting both the North American downturn and a deliberate geographic diversification push.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins:** Revenue moved from $23.0B (2023) to $22.9B (2024) to $22.2B (2025), a 3% decline, with North America down 6% and international down 2%. Operating income collapsed 41% to $2.26B, driven by an $831M impairment/other charge (versus $116M in 2024) layered on top of a 19% decline in underlying "total operations" income to $3.5B. Net margin fell to 5.8% from ~10.9%. Segment margins held up better than the headline suggests, with C&P ~17% and D&E ~15% full-year, indicating the operational deterioration is a cyclical volume/pricing effect in North America rather than a structural collapse.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue was essentially flat YoY at $5.40B; operating income was $679M (versus $431M a year earlier, which had been depressed by a $356M impairment). No impairments this quarter. The geographic divergence is the story: Latin America +22%, Europe/Africa/CIS +11%, Middle East/Asia −13% on conflict-related cancellations/force majeure, North America −4%. Operating cash flow was weak at $273M (down from $377M) on a $252M working-capital drag, mostly a $293M receivables build. This is seasonally typical for HAL but worth watching given the emerging-market collectability issues discussed below.

**Balance sheet and capital return:** Altman Z-Score of 2.48 places HAL in the grey zone: not distressed, but leverage is meaningful. D/E is ~1.39; long-term debt of $7.1B sits against $10.5B equity and $2.0B cash. Liquidity is solid: a $3.5B undrawn revolver maturing 2030, no financial covenants, laddered maturities (only ~$90M due Feb 2027), and investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+, Moody's A3, stable). The capital-return framework targets ≥50% of free cash flow; HAL returned $1.6B in 2025 ($579M dividends at $0.17/quarter plus ~$1.0B buybacks, retiring 42.4M shares). Diluted share count has fallen to 853M from 902M in 2023.

The 2026 posture is defensive: capex guided down to ~$1.1B (5% of revenue) from $1.25B, and Q1 buybacks slowed to $100M from $250M a year earlier. Management is preserving flexibility against the North American downturn and the potential ~$640M IRS liability. This is prudent, but it also signals limited internal confidence in a rapid activity rebound. The ongoing SAP S4 migration is over budget and now running ~$45M/quarter (~$180M annualized) through Q4 2026, a persistent cost drag.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated and, on balance, modest. North America, HAL's largest and most cyclical exposure, is guided down again in 2026 as management stacks uneconomic fleets against a US land rig count that fell from 669 (2023) to 546 (2025). This is the core structural headwind and it directly pressures the C&P segment.

Against that, international momentum is real, and management guides international to flat-to-modestly-higher for 2026 led by Latin America. The wild card is the Middle East, where the conflict-driven cancellations cost $0.02-0.03 of EPS. A normalization would restore the highest-margin franchise, but the timing is outside management's control.

Estimate revisions are constructive: current-year EPS has been revised up 7.5% over 90 days, next-year up 7.7%, and both next-quarter and current-year revisions turned positive. This is meaningful positive earnings momentum, though it falls short of the +30% velocity that would flag an aggressive buy signal. The revisions are consistent with a company grinding out a modest recovery from a soft 2025, not one at a violent trough.

The longer-dated optionality is the VoltaGrid investment (May 2026): Blackstone and Halliburton jointly committed ~$1B into an independent gas-microgrid startup that powers data centers, valuing it at >$10B. HAL's role is that of a co-investor and manufacturing partner, having secured capacity for 400 MW of modular natural gas power systems for 2028 delivery. This reframes part of the HAL story around structural natural gas demand from LNG, electrification, and data centers, but it is 2028-weighted optionality that is modest against a $22B revenue base, and the microgrid IP belongs to VoltaGrid, not Halliburton. It is a directional positive for diversification, not a near-term earnings driver.

## Valuation Assessment

HAL trades at ~$35.22, roughly 23x trailing EPS of ~$1.50 (a figure depressed by the 2025 impairment), ~14.8x consensus current-year EPS of $2.38, and ~12x next-year $2.94. On forward multiples the stock does not look expensive, and the earnings base is somewhat cyclically depressed given the 2025 impairment and North American softness.

Against peers in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, the median P/E is 27.0 (skewed by NOV at 85x and KGS at 89x, which are not clean comps). The more relevant reads are Weatherford at 15.8x and Archrock at 19.6x. HAL's ~23x trailing / ~15x forward is not a standout discount, and the trailing figure is inflated by the impairment. Analyst targets bracket the stock at $28 low, $55 high, $44.04 mean, $45 median, placing the current price roughly 20% below consensus mean. That gap is real, but it reflects the same recovery expectations embedded in the rising estimates rather than an independent mispricing.

The cheapness case here is the classic energy value-trap pattern: a low forward multiple that depends entirely on a cyclical recovery materializing. Cheapness in this sector has historically sorted the wrong way, and HAL's core discount is warranted by genuine North American deterioration, the ROCE halving to 8.9%, and TSR lagging its peer group (163.76 vs 181.72). We do not treat the sub-consensus price as a strong buy signal absent an identified catalyst; the rising estimates provide some support, but they price a modest recovery rather than an inflection. A score modestly above midpoint reflects the reasonable forward multiple offset by the absence of a clear catalyst and the anti-predictive nature of energy cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

HAL is one of the Big Three OFS players alongside SLB and Baker Hughes, and it remains the most North America / completions-levered of the three. In the current NAM downturn this is a liability; in a NAM recovery it provides the most torque. Its technology moat is holding, and automation/directional differentiation (iCruise, LOGIX) helps defend C&P margins as pricing compresses. Segment margins even in a soft year indicate the competitive position is stable rather than eroding.

The trajectory, though, is more mixed than the level. ROCE fell by roughly half in 2025, and TSR has lagged peers over five years, suggesting HAL has not out-executed SLB (more international/offshore-weighted, better positioned in the current cycle) or Baker Hughes (more diversified into LNG equipment). The VoltaGrid manufacturing partnership is an attempt to open a new competitive front in gas-powered data-center microgrids, but that is a supplier role rather than an owned-IP moat. On balance, HAL's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-softening: it retains its NAM completions leadership and technology edge, but is currently on the wrong side of the geographic cycle relative to its most international peer.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully reflected at current levels:

1. **North American structural downturn.** Management is stacking uneconomic fleets and expects NAM revenue down again in 2026. The US land rig count has fallen ~18% from 2023. This is the central drag on C&P and the base business.

2. **Middle East disruption is live and escalating.** Q1 2026 saw actual work cancellations and force majeure in Saudi Arabia and Qatar. This hit the highest-margin international franchise and is a fresh deterioration not in the FY2025 base. The Q2 print (July 21) will show whether it deepens.

3. **IRS NOPA on the Baker Hughes termination fee.** The IRS proposes reclassifying ~95% of the 2016 $3.5B termination fee from ordinary deduction to capital loss; an adverse outcome could mean ~$640M cash taxes plus interest. Multi-year resolution; management says it would not impair capital return, but it is a real contingent liability.

4. **Concentrated emerging-market receivable risk.** The allowance for credit losses is $805M (14.9% of gross receivables), primarily tied to Venezuela. Pemex is ~7% of receivables with chronic payment delays, managed via $374M notional of credit default swaps. Venezuela growth is on hold pending payment certainty.

5. **Tax/tariff overhangs.** The OBBBA triggered a $125M valuation allowance on foreign tax credit carryforwards; tariffs cost ~$89M incremental in 2025 with the regime under Supreme Court review.

6. **Governance and leadership churn.** A broad late-2025/early-2026 reorganization (new COO, new CAO, reshuffled hemisphere presidents) coincides with the succession risk flagged in the 10-K.

The macro overlay cuts both ways. A Middle East normalization would reverse the work stoppages, but a sustained conflict-driven supply disruption tends to support crude prices [unverified — no specific oil-price scenario in sources], keeping the sector pricing backdrop firmer while the Middle East drag stays live. This offsetting structure is why the risk score sits below midpoint despite the balance sheet not being distressed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has jumped to 47.6% from 36.3% a month ago, versus 34.9% historical vol (a rich 12.7-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 47.6% > long-term 35.1%), consistent with the imminent July 21 earnings print. The nearest expiry implies a ±5.3% move. Skew is roughly balanced (0.9%) and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply to 0.34 from 0.81, so positioning is not aggressively bearish. Unusual activity shows call buying at the $36 and $39 July 24 strikes (7-8x volume/OI). The market expects a meaningful earnings reaction but is not positioned decisively for a downside miss, consistent with the two-sided nature of the setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($45-55):** North American activity troughs in 2026 and HAL's operating leverage snaps back on a recovery, aided by its electric-frac technology edge. International momentum broadens, the Middle East normalizes and restores high-margin work, and rising estimates drive a re-rating toward the $44-45 consensus and beyond. The VoltaGrid gas-microgrid optionality gains credibility as a 2028+ diversification lever. On a 15-18x multiple applied to $2.94-3.20 forward earnings power, the stock reaches the mid-$40s to mid-$50s.

**Bear case ($26-30):** North American fleet stacking deepens, the Middle East conflict drag persists into H2 2026, and the modest 2026 recovery stalls. Working-capital drags and emerging-market receivable write-downs (Venezuela, Pemex) pressure free cash flow, and the IRS NOPA crystallizes toward the ~$640M end. Estimates roll over, and the stock de-rates toward the $28 analyst low and the low-to-mid $20s. This maps to roughly 20-25% downside.

The base case sits between: a modest recovery playing out through 2026-2027, with the stock grinding toward the low-$40s as estimates are met and revised up, against a backdrop where the macro and the Middle East disruption keep the path choppy and two-sided.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The risk/reward is roughly balanced. The forward multiple is reasonable and estimates are rising, but the core business is in a genuine (if cyclical) decline, insiders are one-directional sellers with no dip-buying, and the near-term catalyst is two-sided.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings report on July 21, 2026 is the natural next checkpoint. It will show whether the Middle East disruption is deepening or abating and whether international momentum is broadening.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 confirms the Middle East drag is stabilizing/reversing and international growth broadens beyond Latin America, with current-year EPS estimates continuing to revise up (toward $2.50+).
- Any clustered open-market insider purchasing at current levels, absent today.
- North American activity shows a durable inflection (rig count stabilizing, C&P margins expanding sequentially) rather than continued fleet stacking.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 revenue growth turns negative YoY with the Middle East decline widening beyond the Q1 13% run-rate.
- Estimate revisions turn negative (current-year EPS revised below $2.30) alongside deteriorating free cash flow / rising receivable write-downs.
- An adverse IRS NOPA resolution toward ~$640M combined with a capital-return cut.

## Conclusion

Halliburton is a well-run, technology-differentiated oilfield-services leader working through a real cyclical downturn in its most-exposed North American completions business, with a fresh Middle East conflict drag hitting its highest-margin international franchise. The 2025 earnings decline was part impairment and part genuine operating deterioration; ROCE halving to 8.9% and TSR lagging peers underscore that the burden of proof is on the 2026-2027 international/gas recovery.

The ~19% pullback from the May high, combined with 7-8% upward estimate revisions over 90 days, has restored some risk/reward, and the stock trades at a reasonable ~12x next-year EPS with a consensus mean ~20% above the current price. But the cheapness depends on the recovery materializing, insiders are selling with none stepping in at $35, and the Middle East normalization that would help HAL's international business could also relieve the supply-driven support crude prices have drawn from the conflict. We rate HAL a HOLD and look to the July 21 print for confirmation of whether the international/gas recovery is offsetting the North American fleet stacking. This is a genuinely uncertain call rather than a conviction-driven one, and the exit criteria above define the specific data points that would move it in either direction.
