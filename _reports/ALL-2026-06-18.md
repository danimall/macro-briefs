**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allstate has completed one of the more impressive turnarounds in personal-lines P&C insurance. After consecutive net losses in 2022–2023 driven by post-pandemic auto loss-cost inflation outrunning rate, the company pushed through large rate increases and rebuilt underwriting margins. The 2025 result: revenue of $67.7B, net income of $10.28B, a property-liability combined ratio of 85.2% (auto 85.0%, homeowners 84.4%), book value per diluted share up 49.9% to $108.45, and a 42.3% return on average common equity. The balance sheet is materially stronger: debt-to-equity fell to 24.5% from 44.7% in 2023, statutory surplus rose to $22.95B, and all three rating agencies affirmed their ratings with the outlook upgraded from negative to stable.

The complication is that the 2025 headline overstates run-rate earnings power. Roughly $1.96B of favorable prior-year reserve releases (22.6% of underwriting income) plus ~$1.14B of after-tax disposition gains from the EVB and group-health divestitures are non-repeatable. A 4.9x trailing P/E is arithmetically cheap, but it sits on peak-cycle earnings that the company itself will not match on a normalized basis. Forward consensus tells the real story: $29.90 for the current year and $26.28 for next year, which puts the stock closer to 7.4x current-year EPS — still inexpensive, but the multiple is doing less work than the trailing number suggests, and the year-over-year EPS decline embedded in those estimates reflects the reserve-release tailwind fading.

We move to HOLD from the prior BUY. The fundamentals are sound and the capital-return acceleration (a new $4.0B buyback authorized in February 2026, a ~9% dividend raise to $1.08/quarter) is shareholder-friendly. But the stock trades at $221.66, roughly 2.6% below its 52-week high of $227.62 and up ~12% over the trailing year, into a softening rate cycle, structural homeowners constraints in California and Florida, rising regulatory profit-cap risk at a 42% ROE, and the perennial catastrophe swing factor. This is a quality business at a fair price.

## Company Overview

Allstate is the third-largest personal P&C insurer in the U.S. by direct premiums, operating through four segments:

- **Allstate Protection (93.9% of insurance premiums)** — personal auto ($38.6B premiums written), homeowners ($16.6B), and select commercial lines, distributed through ~27,400 exclusive agents, ~58,700 independent agent locations, and a growing direct channel under the Allstate, National General, Direct Auto, and Answer Financial brands.
- **Protection Services (5.0% of revenue, 81.6% of total policies-in-force)** — Protection Plans, Roadside, Dealer Services, Identity Protection, and the Arity telematics/mobility-data platform. High policy counts, modest dollars, $218M adjusted net income.
- **Run-off Property-Liability** — legacy asbestos/environmental exposures, net reserves $1.43B.
- **Corporate** — holding company and the $83.2B investment portfolio.

The strategic thrust is "Transformative Growth," a multi-year repositioning from a primarily exclusive-agent model toward a lower-cost, multi-channel, digitally-enabled platform. Differentiators include a proprietary underwriting/loss database, telematics products (Drivewise, Milewise, DynamicDrive, Arity), and the rollout of "Affordable, Simple, Connected" auto (43 states) and home (31 states) products plus Custom360 for the independent channel (36 states). Allstate competes against GEICO, Progressive, State Farm, and others in a highly price-sensitive market.

## Financial Analysis

The three-year trajectory is the core fact pattern:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $51.4B | $57.1B | $64.1B | $67.7B |
| Net income | -$1.31B | -$0.19B | $4.67B | $10.28B |
| P-L combined ratio | — | 104.5% | 94.3% | 85.2% |
| Auto combined ratio | — | 103.4% | 95.0% | 85.0% |
| Homeowners combined ratio | — | 106.8% | 90.1% | 84.4% |
| Shareholders' equity | $17.5B | $17.8B | $21.4B | $30.6B |
| Book value/diluted share | — | — | $72.35 | $108.45 |
| ROE (avg common equity) | — | — | 25.8% | 42.3% |

Revenue growth was driven by rate (auto +3.5% implemented in 2025, homeowners +7.6%) and modest PIF growth (auto PIF +2.3% to 25.5M, homeowners +2.5%). Net investment income rose 11.5% to $3.45B on higher reinvestment yields against a 5.1-year duration; the portfolio is 92.1% investment-grade fixed income with ~$9.8B in performance-based limited partnerships. Operating cash flow was $10.1B and FCF $9.9B, supporting $1.24B of repurchases in 2025 and a holding-company deployable-asset base of $7.52B.

The quality-of-earnings caveat is central to the rating. The favorable prior-year reserve releases were mostly favorable auto bodily-injury severity development and contributed 22.6% of underwriting income and 14.1% of net income. Strip the releases and disposition gains out and the underlying combined-ratio improvement of roughly nine points is genuine, but normalized earnings power runs meaningfully below the $10.28B headline. Consensus reflects this: current-year and next-year EPS imply a deliberate step-down as the reserve tailwind fades, even with continued underlying margin and a shrinking share count.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.49 flagging "distress" is a model artifact. The Z-Score assumes a working-capital-driven balance sheet and is not designed for insurers, whose balance sheets are dominated by investments and reserves. With A3/BBB+/a- ratings on stable outlook, record surplus, and 24.5% debt-to-equity, Allstate is financially strong. The Piotroski flag that operating cash flow did not exceed net income is similarly expected when net income is inflated by reserve releases and one-time gains.

## Growth Analysis

The most important emerging positive is the growth inflection. After years of deliberately shrinking PIF to repair profitability — auto PIF fell in 2023–2024 — 2025 produced PIF growth in both auto and home, and new issued auto applications surged +25.3% across all channels. This is the pivot from "earn back profitability" to "profitable growth," and it is the central bull case.

Estimate revisions corroborate the momentum. Current-year EPS consensus has risen +19.3% over 90 days and next-year +4.3%, with positive 30-day drift across all periods. The earnings-surprise record is strong — four straight beats, all 43–79% above estimate — which reflects how hard the catastrophe and reserve-development lines are to model rather than persistent sandbagging on the underlying book.

The durability question is the offset. The +19.3% current-year revision is partly the market catching up to a benign 2026 catastrophe cadence (May cat losses of $289M, down 63% from an active April) rather than a structural step-change in earning power. As the auto-rate cycle softens and Progressive continues to compete aggressively on price, Allstate must grow PIF without giving back the 85% combined ratio. Homeowners growth is structurally capped by the company's retreat from new business in California (since 2022) and Florida (since 2023) absent regulatory reform. The growth thesis is real but cyclically and geographically constrained, which is why we score growth at 6.0 — improving trajectory, bounded ceiling.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 4.9x trailing P/E overstates cheapness because it capitalizes peak earnings. The more honest frame is forward: at $221.66 against current-year and next-year consensus, the stock trades at roughly 7.4x current-year and 8.4x next-year EPS. Against the P&C peer median P/E of 9.0 (TRV 9.0, HIG 9.0, CINF 9.6, ACGL 7.0, WRB 14.3), Allstate is modestly cheap to peers — but those peers are also being valued on cyclically elevated earnings, so the comparison is apples-to-apples and does not reveal a standalone mispricing.

The more meaningful support is book value. At $108.45 book per share, the stock trades around 2.0x book, a premium to most P&C peers that reflects the 42% ROE the market knows is unsustainable. As ROE normalizes toward the mid-to-high teens, the price-to-book multiple should compress, which caps upside even if book value compounds. A 1.6x–1.8x multiple on a normalizing ~$120 book a year out frames fair value in the low-to-mid $200s, broadly where the stock already trades.

Analyst targets bracket this: low $176, mean $240.55, median $242.50, high $295. With the stock at $221.66, the median target implies ~9% upside — reasonable but not the asymmetric setup that warrants a high valuation score. The principle that analyst targets are floors on cheap inflection stocks applies less here: the stock is at a forward multiple that already reflects the cycle, and the EPS line is set to decline year-over-year, so the targets are not stale-lagging a hidden re-rating. We score valuation at 6.0 — inexpensive on forward earnings and book-supported, but with a normalizing ROE and a faded reserve tailwind capping the re-rate case.

## Competitive Landscape

Allstate's position is stable-to-improving on the strength of the brand, a proprietary loss/pricing database, and the multi-channel digital pivot. The new-application surge suggests the Transformative Growth strategy is gaining traction after years of policy attrition, and the National General/independent-agent expansion broadens distribution beyond the legacy exclusive-agent base.

The competitive pressures are well-defined. Progressive has been the share gainer in personal auto, leaning on direct distribution and telematics scale, and a softening rate cycle will test whether Allstate can retain growth without sacrificing the auto combined ratio. In homeowners and catastrophe-exposed geographies, the retreat from California and Florida is sector-wide (State Farm and others have done the same), and Allstate manages residual exposure via reinsurance, E&S writing through North Light, the Ivantage brokerage ($2.86B non-proprietary premium), roof-based pricing, and FAIR Plan participation. Over the long term, ADAS/autonomous-vehicle adoption and ride-sharing could shrink the auto market, and Allstate is more exposed than diversified commercial insurers given auto's weight in its book. The trajectory is positive, but the auto-share battle is contested and homeowners growth is geographically boxed in, supporting a 6.5 competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this name, weighted by how much they are already priced:

1. **Catastrophe/climate concentration (the defining risk).** 2025 cat losses were $4.96B, 8.6 points of combined ratio; homeowners cats alone added 26.6 points to the homeowners loss ratio. The January 2025 California wildfires drove a $523M jump in cat-reinsurance recoverables. Modeled 1-in-100 PML is ~$3.1B net of reinsurance, and reinsurance cost is rising ($1.23B in 2025 vs $1.11B in 2024). The benign 2026 cat experience is currently propelling earnings and can reverse violently.

2. **Reserve-release reversal.** The favorable 2025 releases mechanically inflate the base. If loss-cost inflation re-accelerates — auto-parts tariffs, medical inflation, attorney representation, social inflation — development could turn unfavorable. Management's stochastic model shows a 7.0% standard deviation on the $27.8B auto-liability reserve (~$1.95B pre-tax sensitivity).

3. **Regulatory profit-cap risk, rising precisely because ROE is 42%.** P&C rates require approval (prior-approval in ~21 states for auto), and some states are weighing profit caps that could force premium credits or refunds when returns exceed thresholds. Peak profitability is exactly when this risk is highest. The CA Insurance Commissioner has imposed a one-year non-renewal moratorium in wildfire ZIP codes.

4. **Tariff-driven loss-cost inflation** — flagged as new in the 2025 MD&A, feeding both auto and home claim severity.

5. **MCCA concentration** — $5.83B of recoverables from the Michigan Catastrophic Claims Association, where 64 claims (>$15M each) comprise ~25% of gross reserves, creating single-claimant volatility.

6. **Governance concentration** — an 18-year, 68-year-old combined Chairman/President/CEO with no publicly identified successor, and a freshly reshuffled C-suite (CFO transition to investment-background John Dugenske in October 2025, plus other Item 5.02 changes).

The combination of a faded reserve tailwind, peak-cycle ROE that invites regulatory profit caps, and the inherent catastrophe swing places several of these risks only partially in the price at a 52-week high, supporting a risk score of 5.0.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($265–295).** The auto growth inflection holds — PIF compounds at mid-single digits while the combined ratio stays in the high-80s as rate outruns moderating loss costs. The $4.0B buyback retires a meaningful portion of the float at a discount to intrinsic value, book value compounds toward $120+, and ROE settles in the high teens rather than collapsing. A benign catastrophe year lets the market extrapolate normalized earnings power above current consensus, and the stock re-rates toward 1.8–2.0x a growing book. Upside to ~$265 on the median-plus scenario, $295 at the analyst high.

**Bear Case ($176–185).** The reserve tailwind reverses as tariff- and social-inflation re-accelerate auto severity; a major catastrophe year (the Jan-2025 wildfire profile recurs) drives cat losses well above plan; a state imposes profit caps that force premium refunds at peak ROE; and the auto-rate cycle softens faster than expected as Progressive presses on price, forcing Allstate to choose between margin and growth. EPS normalizes toward the low-$20s and the price-to-book multiple compresses, taking the stock to the analyst low of $176, roughly -21% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — the growth pivot is real, capital return is accelerating, and the balance sheet is the strongest in years — but the risk/reward at a 52-week high, with a normalizing ROE and faded reserve tailwind, is balanced rather than compelling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (late July 2026) for the underlying combined ratio and PIF trajectory; the monthly Item 7.01 catastrophe disclosures as a real-time read; and state regulatory developments on profit caps.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the auto growth inflection is durable through a softening cycle — PIF growth sustained at mid-single digits with the underlying (ex-cat, ex-reserve-release) auto combined ratio holding below 90%.
- A pullback toward the high-$190s/low-$200s (roughly 7x forward EPS / ~1.8x book) that restores asymmetric upside without fundamental deterioration.
- A benign catastrophe year confirming normalized EPS power above the current ~$26–30 consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Unfavorable prior-year reserve development emerging on the auto book, signaling the 2025 releases were over-stated relief.
- A state imposing binding profit caps/premium-refund mandates that materially compress the personal-lines margin.
- The underlying auto combined ratio deteriorating back above 95% as the rate cycle softens, or a major catastrophe event materially impairing surplus.

## Conclusion

Allstate is a financially strong, well-capitalized P&C leader that has executed a genuine underwriting turnaround and is now leaning into profitable growth with an accelerating capital-return program. The operational improvement is substantial: a combined ratio cut from 104.5% to 85.2% in two years, book value up nearly 50%, and leverage roughly halved. The reason for moving from BUY to HOLD is not fundamental deterioration but that the most attractive part of the setup is behind the stock. The 2025 earnings base is inflated by ~$3B of non-repeatable reserve releases and disposition gains; ROE is at a 42% level that the company will not sustain and that invites regulatory profit caps; homeowners growth is structurally constrained by the CA/FL retreat; and the stock trades near its 52-week high at a forward multiple that, while inexpensive against peers, already reflects the cycle. The score trajectory has been declining (7.7 → 7.3 → 7.1), and this report continues that drift to 6.15 for consistent, evidence-based reasons rather than price action alone. At ~9% to the median target with a -21% bear case, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. We would re-engage as a BUY on a pullback toward the high-$190s/low-$200s or on confirmation that the auto growth inflection holds through a softening rate cycle.
