**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hancock Whitney is a ~$35.5 billion-asset Gulf South regional bank in the middle of a deliberate strategic pivot toward faster-growing Florida metros. Two acquisitions in twelve months (the May 2025 Sabal Trust wealth deal, ~$3B AUM in central Florida, and the pending $377.6M all-cash purchase of OFB Bancshares/One Florida Bank in Orlando, $2.1B assets, HWC's first bank acquisition in seven years) define the forward thesis. Underneath the M&A, the operating business is performing well: FY2025 net income was $486.1M ($5.67 diluted EPS, +5.5% YoY), ROA reached 1.40%, ROTCE hit 14.49%, and net interest margin expanded 10 bps to 3.47% as deposit costs repriced down faster than loan yields in the Fed's easing cycle.

The central analytical adjustment this quarter is normalizing reported Q1 2026 EPS of $0.57. That number reflects a deliberate $98.5M pre-tax securities-restructuring loss (~$0.93/share after-tax), where the bank sold $1.5B of 2.49%-yielding securities and reinvested at 4.35% for a ~50-month payback and +7bps of NIM. Underlying quarterly run-rate earnings power remains near $1.40+, and the March quarter's normalized EPS of $1.52 beat consensus. Full-year 2026 consensus sits around $6.38–6.42 (+12.5% over 2025), and next-year estimates have been revised up +7.5% over 90 days.

The problem for the recommendation is price. HWC has rallied roughly 23% over the trailing twelve months to $74.61, sitting within 2% of its 52-week high. At ~15.3x trailing and ~11.7x forward EPS with a 2.7% yield, the stock trades roughly in line with regional-bank peers (median P/E 16.8, yield 2.8%). The fundamental improvement is real and the scores have been trending up (5.9 → 6.8), but the valuation gap that would have made this asymmetric has largely closed. This is a growth-hold: a quality compounder worth owning through the Florida build-out, but not a stock offering clear mispricing at a record price.

## Company Overview

Hancock Whitney operates through Hancock Whitney Bank from Gulfport, Mississippi, with 186 branches concentrated in Louisiana (~100), Mississippi, Alabama, Texas, and a growing Florida franchise (32 branches). The lineage traces to 1899, and the company has paid uninterrupted quarterly dividends since 1967.

The lending book ($24.0B) is weighted toward commercial: 55% C&I including owner-occupied CRE, 18% income-producing CRE and construction, 17% residential mortgage, and 6% consumer. Management is deliberately shrinking its shared national credit book (down to $2.0B / 9% of loans from $2.3B / 10%) in favor of granular, full-relationship local lending, a de-risking move. The deposit base ($29.3B) is high quality: 35% noninterest-bearing, 95% core, average account size only ~$37,700, with a conservative ~81% loan/deposit ratio. Fee businesses (trust/wealth of $14.0B AUM, service charges, card, and investment/annuity) now generate 26.6% of revenue, up from 20.7% in 2023, an increasingly meaningful diversification lever.

The bank competes against megabanks (Truist, BofA, JPMorgan, Wells), other regionals (Regions, Bank OZK), tax-advantaged credit unions, and fintechs. Its edge is a low-cost granular deposit franchise, strong capital, and a credit-conservative culture.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 results were solid across the board. Net income of $486.1M rose 5.5% from $460.8M ($5.28). NIM expansion was driven by a 48% deposit beta versus a 34% loan beta in the down-rate cycle; cost of funds fell 31 bps to 1.57%. The efficiency ratio improved to 54.78% from 55.36%, reflecting a well-managed cost base. Noninterest income grew 12% to $406.4M, with trust fees up 25% on the Sabal acquisition plus organic growth.

The multi-year EPS path shows the securities-restructuring pattern clearly: 2021 $5.22 → 2022 $5.98 → 2023 $4.50 (depressed by a $65.4M securities-restructuring loss plus FDIC special assessment) → 2024 $5.28 → 2025 $5.67. The January 2026 restructuring repeats the same playbook: take a one-time mark to lift forward NIM. This is why reported Q1 2026 net income of $47.4M is misleading; on a normalized basis the quarter's EPS was $1.52, a small beat.

The balance sheet is strong. CET1 stands at 13.65% (down 49 bps YoY intentionally from capital return), TCE at 10.06% (up 59 bps), total capital at 15.45%; capital exceeds well-capitalized minimums by over $1.1B. Management stress-tests capital marking both AFS and HTM to market and states ratios would remain above well-capitalized minimums even then, which differentiates HWC from the regionals scarred in the 2023 AOCI panic. Liquidity is ample: $18.9B available versus ~$11.3B noncollateralized uninsured deposits (~1.7x coverage), with wholesale funding only 4.4% of core deposits.

Credit quality is benign. ACL/loans of 1.43% (down 4 bps), net charge-offs of 0.22%, nonaccruals of 0.45%, and criticized commercial loans down 14% to $535.4M. The allowance covers nonaccruals ~288%. The one deteriorating metric worth tracking: modified loans to borrowers in financial difficulty jumped to $162.8M from $99.5M, which management attributes to prolonged high rates, inflation, and insurance-cost stress. It remains small relative to a $24B book, but the trajectory bears monitoring, particularly in hospitality and CRE.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth story is the Florida land-grab. The two 2025-2026 acquisitions plus 32 existing Florida branches signal a deliberate pivot from the mature Gulf South toward Sun Belt metros. The OFB deal increases HWC's Florida loans and deposits by more than 30% each, taking pro forma assets above $37B, and hands the bank an Orlando platform (currently the #10 deposit-holder there) to build organically around. Piper Sandler characterized OFB's asset quality as "pristine" with rising ROA (0.80% in 2023 → 1.18% Q1 2026).

Organic drivers support mid-single-digit compounding. Management guides to mid-single-digit loan growth, continued NIM support from the securities repositioning (+7bps annualized, +$23.8M NII), and fee-income growth from the expanded trust franchise. The +12.5% implied 2026 growth and +7.5% out-year revision are positive but not extreme revision signals. Current-year and near-quarter revisions are essentially flat (+1.0%/+0.2%), so the acceleration is concentrated in the out-year, consistent with the M&A accretion timeline rather than a broad turn.

Growth prospects earn a moderate score. The Florida strategy is credible and additive, and the balance sheet funds continued bite-sized M&A. But this is a regional bank compounding in the high-single to low-double digits: the +7.5% out-year revision does not clear the threshold that would flag a systematic underestimation of a fundamental turn. Growth here is durable rather than explosive.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.61, HWC trades at ~15.3x trailing EPS ($5.67), ~11.7x forward consensus ($6.38), and yields 2.7%. Against the regional-bank peer set (median P/E 16.8, median yield 2.8%; AUB 16.8x, ABCB 13.7x, UBSI 12.6x, GBCI 22.8x), HWC sits roughly in line to slightly cheap on a trailing basis and reasonable on forward. The forward multiple looks attractive relative to ~12.5% projected earnings growth, but that growth partly reflects the mechanical NIM lift from the securities restructuring rather than a durable structural re-rating.

The valuation case that existed at prior reads (when the stock was in the low-$60s) has largely played out. On the frameworks that matter for banks, HWC trades at a modest premium to tangible book given a 14.5% ROTCE, which is fair rather than cheap; the market is appropriately paying up for above-peer profitability and capital strength. There is no identifiable catalyst forcing a re-rating from here: the OFB deal is accretive but incremental, estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating, and the profitability metrics are already recognized in the multiple.

I score valuation at the midpoint rather than high. Regional-bank cheapness has repeatedly proven to be a value-trap signal in this environment rather than a buy trigger, and HWC is not even notably cheap; it trades in line with peers at a full price. Analyst targets (mean $78.9, median $79.5, high $86, low $72) imply ~6% upside to mean and put the current price above the low target, consistent with a stock that has caught up to fair value. The prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark down valuation; the reason is that current price now reflects current fundamentals with limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

HWC's competitive position is stable to modestly improving. Its structural advantages (a granular, low-cost deposit franchise, strong capital, and disciplined credit) are durable in a sector where funding cost and credit culture separate winners from losers. The bank is a consolidator, not a target, using its strong stock currency (213 TSR versus peer 152) and excess capital to execute bite-sized deals. The Florida expansion adds exposure to superior demographics and metro growth.

The trajectory is a genuine positive: management is simultaneously growing the balance sheet while de-risking it (shrinking SNCs, exiting indirect auto, tying compensation to criticized-loan and NPL metrics). The offsetting reality is that in Orlando, HWC is buying a #10 foothold in a market dominated by megabanks, which is a build-out bet rather than scale. Fee-income diversification is a structural improvement that reduces rate sensitivity. On balance the competitive position is improving at the margin, but not dramatically enough to command a high score, and competitive-position signals in this environment have decayed to near-noise as a standalone discriminator.

## Risk Assessment

The most HWC-specific risks:

1. **Rate-path sensitivity.** HWC is modestly asset-sensitive (+100bp → +1.5% NII year one; -100bp → -1.9% NII). Further Fed cuts are a mild headwind to net interest income, partially offset by continued deposit repricing. EVE declines 2.6% at +100bp. The recent NIM expansion is partly cyclical and reverses if the down-rate deposit tailwind exhausts before loan yields catch up.

2. **Securities-portfolio drag and earnings-management optics.** The AFS book still yields ~3.04%. The January 2026 restructuring was accretive but recurring restructurings signal a legacy portfolio that continues to weigh on reported results and complicates clean earnings comparisons.

3. **Integration and capital-allocation tension.** The OFB deal enters a new Orlando market with attendant deposit/employee-retention risk. The $378M cash deployment likely comes in lieu of buybacks in 2026. Management is juggling three capital uses (dividends raised twice, a 5% buyback authorization, and cash M&A) against a 12.0–12.5% CET1-by-2028 target that implies continued aggressive deployment and a thinner cushion over time.

4. **Geographic and CRE concentration.** Gulf South exposure to hurricanes, flooding, and energy (though direct energy lending is now ~1% of C&I). Income-producing CRE grew 13%; office is a manageable ~9% of the CRE/construction book. The modified-loan trend is the fastest-deteriorating asset-quality metric.

5. **Macro/regulatory overhang.** The FY2025 10-K added language on the Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA-tariff ruling, government-shutdown references, and AI risks. The ACL is now weighted 50% baseline / 50% mild-recession, with the S-2 scenario implying ~36% higher expected losses.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 42.2% sits well above 1-year historical vol of 26.1% (a rich 16.1pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.2% near-term versus 24.5% at six months), signaling an imminent catalyst, most likely the mid-July OFB shareholder vote and the Q2 earnings cadence. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%) and put/call volume light (0.17), so the elevated near-term vol reflects event uncertainty rather than directional bearish positioning. Short interest is elevated at 11.4% of float with 7.6 days to cover, indicating meaningful institutional skepticism that diverges from the constructive fundamental picture, though the balanced options skew suggests the shorts are not being confirmed by directional put demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$86, +15%):** The Florida strategy compounds — OFB closes cleanly in Q3 2026, integrates without deposit runoff, and Orlando organic build-out plus continued bite-sized M&A accelerates EPS toward the $7.26 next-year consensus. NIM holds above 3.45% as the securities repositioning offsets rate cuts, credit stays benign, and the bank re-rates modestly toward the peer high (GBCI at 22.8x) on above-peer ROTCE. The stock reaches the analyst high target of $86.

**Bear Case (~$60, -20%):** Fed cuts compress NIM faster than deposit repricing offsets, the modified-loan trend metastasizes into hospitality/CRE charge-offs, OFB integration disappoints or diverts capital from accretive buybacks, and the stock de-rates toward the low-teens forward multiple that regional banks command in a credit-stress regime. A drawdown to the mid-$60s (near where it traded in early 2026) is plausible.

**Base Case (~$79, +6%):** Mid-single-digit loan growth, NIM stability, benign-to-slightly-elevated credit, and modest OFB accretion deliver ~$6.40 in 2026 EPS and progress toward $7+ in 2027. The stock tracks earnings growth plus dividend, reaching the analyst mean/median of ~$79.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the OFB shareholder vote (mid-July 2026), Q3 2026 deal close, and the quarterly earnings cadence.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$60s that restores forward-multiple margin of safety below ~10x, OR
- Evidence the OFB integration is running ahead of plan (accretion above guidance, deposit retention >95%) with NIM sustained above 3.50%, OR
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +10% on the current year, signaling a fundamental inflection the multiple hasn't captured.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Modified/troubled loans continuing to climb past ~$250M with rising net charge-offs above 0.40%, indicating the Gulf South CRE/hospitality book is cracking, OR
- A guidance cut on loan growth or NIM, OR
- OFB integration missteps (material deposit runoff, key-staff attrition) that impair the Florida thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings and the OFB vote in mid-July, followed by the Q3 close.

## Conclusion

Hancock Whitney is a well-run, well-capitalized regional bank executing a credible pivot into higher-growth Florida markets, with expanding NIM, strong ROTCE, benign credit, and a balance sheet that comfortably survives AOCI/HTM stress tests. The fundamental trajectory has been improving, and the scores reflect that: financial health is genuinely strong, and the Florida build-out gives durable mid-single-digit-plus compounding potential.

The recommendation is HOLD (growth-hold). The reasons to own it are real, but the rally to within 2% of the 52-week high has closed the valuation gap. HWC is fairly priced rather than mispriced, with ~6% upside to the analyst mean and no forcing catalyst for a re-rating. The Financials sector carries a structural negative-alpha history, which raises the bar for a BUY that this name does not clear on valuation asymmetry. Own it for the compounding and the growing dividend; add on a pullback into the low-$60s or on evidence of a genuine earnings inflection. The elevated near-term options vol and short interest argue for patience around the July catalysts rather than chasing at a record price.
