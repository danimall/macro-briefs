## Executive Summary

Westamerica Bancorporation is a $1.3 billion market cap community bank operating across 19 counties in Northern and Central California with $6.0 billion in assets. Only 12% of assets are in loans, with 72% deployed in investment securities. It functions as a bond portfolio attached to one of the cheapest deposit franchises in US community banking (0.50% interest-bearing deposit costs, 46% noninterest-bearing mix).

The core investment tension: earnings are declining structurally as a $1.5B+ CLO portfolio that yielded 7%+ runs off and gets replaced with 4-5% agency securities. Net income fell from $161.8M (2023) to $116.2M (2025), and NIM compressed from 4.37% to 3.82%. However, management is aggressively returning capital — $104M in buybacks (8% of shares) plus $47M in dividends in 2025 alone — providing an 11.6% combined shareholder yield. The buyback-driven share count reduction partially offsets per-share earnings pressure.

At $55.68, the stock trades at 12.5x forward earnings with a 3.3% dividend yield. This is a reasonable valuation for a bank with no growth, declining revenue, and fortress capital — but also one with pristine credit quality, extraordinary efficiency (39.3% efficiency ratio), and demonstrated willingness to return excess capital aggressively. The investment case is primarily income-oriented: total return through dividends plus continued buyback-driven EPS support.

## Company Overview

Westamerica Bancorporation, through its subsidiary Westamerica Bank, provides commercial and retail banking services to small businesses, local professionals, and retail customers in Northern and Central California. Founded in 1884, the bank operates one of the leanest models in community banking with only 627 employees managing $6.0 billion in assets.

The bank's strategy is explicitly conservative: no interest-only or negative-amortization mortgage products, no brokered deposits, no aggressive loan growth. Management deliberately chose not to pursue loan portfolio expansion during the low-rate era, instead deploying funds into CLOs and other securities. This produced exceptional returns during the 2022-2024 rate hiking cycle but now creates headwinds as those high-yielding assets mature.

The geographic footprint spans from Mendocino and Lake counties in the north to Kern County in the south, concentrated in smaller towns and rural communities where relationship banking still dominates.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited by design. The bank is in explicit harvest mode:

- Loans declining ~10% annually as the bank does not actively pursue originations
- Total deposits contracting 2-3% annually as competitive pressures and rate dynamics drive outflows
- Securities portfolio composition shifting from high-yield CLOs to lower-yield agency MBS
- No M&A activity since 2010 (two FDIC-assisted acquisitions)

The only growth vector is per-share earnings stabilization through buybacks. If the bank maintains $100M+ annual repurchases at current prices, it removes approximately 1.7-1.8M shares annually (7-8% of float). On $4.25-4.45 consensus EPS for FY2026, this buyback pace could stabilize EPS around $4.30-4.50 even as total net income continues declining 5-10% annually.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year consensus rose 4.5% over 30 days (from $4.25 to $4.45), likely reflecting the Q1 2026 beat. This reflects better-than-feared decline, not recovery.

The valuation is neither cheap enough for a conviction buy (no catalyst to re-rate a structurally declining earner) nor expensive enough to avoid (yield and buyback support provide a meaningful total return floor).

## Competitive Landscape

Westamerica's competitive position is stable but static. Strengths include:

1. **Deposit franchise moat:** The 46% noninterest-bearing mix and 0.50% cost of interest-bearing deposits took decades to build through community relationships. This is not replicable by fintech or new entrants.

2. **Operational efficiency:** At 39.3%, the efficiency ratio is in the top decile of community banks nationally. Only 627 employees manage $6B in assets — peers of similar size typically employ 1,500-2,500.

3. **Capital fortress:** 23% total capital ratio provides enormous buffer against any conceivable stress scenario and enables aggressive capital return.

4. **Rural/small-town positioning:** Less competitive intensity than metropolitan markets, though also slower deposit growth.

Weaknesses:

1. **No growth engine:** Loans declining ~10% annually without replacement means the earning asset base shrinks organically.
2. **Geographic concentration:** 100% California, with significant Central Valley exposure.
3. **Technology investment gap:** Small community banks increasingly struggle to match digital offerings of larger competitors, driving deposit outflows among younger demographics.

The competitive position is deteriorating at the margin (deposit outflows, loan contraction) but the core franchise remains intact and valuable.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Structural NII decline (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact):** The remaining $425M in CLOs will likely be substantially called within 12-18 months. Each quarter brings ~$40-60M of reinvestment at lower yields. This risk is partially priced (stock well below 2023 highs) but the trough in EPS has not yet been reached.

2. **Asset sensitivity in falling rate environment:** The bank's simulation shows a 200bp rate decline reduces NII by 12.2%. With the Fed potentially cutting further if recession materializes, this creates additional downside beyond CLO runoff. Conversely, rate hikes would help.

3. **Deposit attrition continuation:** Average deposits fell 16% from $5.79B (2023) to $4.86B (2025). If this pace continues, the earning asset base shrinks faster than buybacks can offset on a per-share basis.

4. **Key person risk:** CEO Payne has led the bank 30+ years. His departure could disrupt the conservative culture that defines the bank.

5. **Geographic and CRE concentration:** $482M (66% of loans) in commercial real estate, concentrated in California communities. CRE stress in the current high-rate environment could create credit losses, though the bank's track record of zero provisions suggests conservative underwriting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**

- NIM stabilizes for two consecutive quarters (indicating CLO runoff has been fully absorbed)
- Insider open-market purchases exceeding $500K (signaling management conviction in trough)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM declines below 3.50% for two consecutive quarters
- Deposit attrition exceeds 5% annually
- Management suspends or reduces buyback program
- Credit losses emerge (any provision for loan losses exceeding $5M)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (July 2026, Q2 results). Key data points: NIM trajectory, CLO balance remaining, buyback volume, deposit trends.

## Conclusion

Westamerica Bancorporation is a well-run, uniquely conservative bank in structural earnings decline. The deposit franchise is exceptional, credit quality is pristine, capital ratios provide enormous safety margins, and management is aggressively returning capital to shareholders. At 12.5x forward earnings with an 11.6% combined shareholder yield, the stock offers reasonable income characteristics.

However, the lack of any growth catalyst, ongoing NIM compression from CLO runoff, and gradual deposit attrition prevent a more constructive stance. The stock is fairly valued for what it is: a high-quality, declining-revenue income vehicle. The 4.2% upside to the sole analyst target ($58) confirms limited near-term re-rating potential. Investors collect 3.3% in dividends, benefit from 7-8% buyback-driven EPS support, and wait for either valuation compression (creating a buy) or NIM stabilization (creating a catalyst).
