## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the dominant E&P operator in the Williston Basin, producing 275,600 Boepd (57% oil) following three transformative acquisitions that consolidated its basin leadership. Q1 2026 delivered $4.56 adjusted EPS versus approximately $3.35–$3.49 consensus, driven by oil prices rising more than 50% by the end of Q1 on Middle East escalation. At current prices, the company projects ~$1.4B in free cash flow at $80 WTI for FY2026, implying a 17% FCF yield on an $8.1B market cap.

The stock trades at approximately 7x forward earnings with PV-10 reserves of $9.1B (a non-GAAP measure that excludes future income taxes). The capital return framework targets 75%+ of adjusted FCF returned to shareholders at current leverage levels, which management estimates could produce 12-15% total yield in 2026 assuming sustained commodity prices and continued buyback execution. The May 21 DAPL easement grant removes a significant idiosyncratic overhang the basin has faced for nearly a decade.

The principal risk is binary oil price exposure. If oil reverts toward $85–95 (consistent with EIA's base case of Brent declining to $89/bbl by 4Q26), earnings compress meaningfully but the company maintains strong liquidity and conservative leverage. The valuation at 7x forward earnings already discounts significant commodity price risk, creating asymmetric upside if prices sustain or limited additional compression if they moderate.

## Company Overview

Chord Energy Corporation was formed from the 2022 merger of Oasis Petroleum and Whiting Petroleum, subsequently acquiring Enerplus Corporation (approximately $4.6B total consideration including stock and cash, May 2024) and ExxonMobil's XTO Williston Basin assets ($542M, October 2025). The company controls 1.3 million net leasehold acres in the Williston Basin, operates 5,025 producing wells, and holds proved reserves of 917.5 MMBoe (56% oil, 69% proved developed).

The company operates 4-5 rigs targeting 135-165 gross operated wells per year, running an increasingly efficient program with 4-mile lateral capabilities. Operations are 100% domestic, predominantly in North Dakota and Montana, with minimal non-operated Marcellus Shale interests.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Dynamics:**

FY2025 headline net income of $44M is distorted by a $539M non-cash goodwill impairment triggered during Q2 2025 when oil prices declined. Adjusting for this write-off, underlying operating profitability remained solid with $2.04B operating cash flow and $693M free cash flow on $4.9B revenue.

Q1 2026 shows meaningful recovery: total GAAP revenue reached $1.67B (+37% YoY), though this increase was heavily driven by purchased oil and gas sales ($515M, up from $112M YoY), which are marketing/optimization transactions recorded gross and largely offset by corresponding purchased oil and gas expenses ($510M). Core oil, NGL, and gas revenues were $1.15B, up approximately 4% YoY. Operating income was $333M (vs. $92M in Q4 2025), and adjusted EPS of $4.56 beat consensus estimates. However, GAAP net income was $109M, down 51% YoY from $220M, primarily due to $241M in unrealized derivative losses as the crude-oil futures curve shifted higher.

Average crude realization improved to $70.05/bbl from $56.90 in Q4 2025 (+23% sequentially), though it was only modestly above Q1 2025's $69.11/bbl. WTI spot prices rose more than 50% by the end of Q1 2026 on Middle East escalation, benefiting forward curves and sentiment more than Q1 average realizations.

**Cash Flow Generation:**

|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Operating CF | $2.10B | $2.04B | ~$2.0B |
| Capex | $1.24B | $1.36B | ~$1.38B |

At $80 WTI, management guides to $1.4B FCF for FY2026 — approximately $25/share. At the current $144 price, that is a 17% FCF yield.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt stands at $1.5B ($750M 6.00% notes due 2030, $750M 6.75% notes due 2033). No revolver borrowings outstanding against a $2.75B borrowing base reaffirmed in May 2026. Total liquidity is $2.19B. Debt-to-equity is approximately 0.19x ($1.5B debt / $8.0B stockholders' equity), which is exceptionally conservative for an E&P company. Goodwill is zero (fully impaired in 2025), removing future impairment risk from that source.

**Capital Returns:**

Since 2021, Chord has returned $61.19/share in aggregate dividends plus $1.3B in buybacks. The base dividend is $5.20/year (3.6% yield). An $881M buyback authorization remains outstanding, though the program does not require the company to purchase shares within any particular timeframe. Q1 2026 actual repurchases were $70.7M. FY2025 saw $682M returned on $693M FCF — essentially 100% return. Average buyback price in 2025 was $104.39, well below the current $144 price.

## Growth Analysis

Chord operates a mature asset base with maintenance-level production growth. FY2025 production averaged 276,620 Boepd; Q1 2026 was 275,615 Boepd. Management raised 2026 oil guidance by 2,000 bpd after Q1 outperformance.

Growth vectors are primarily:
1. **Capital efficiency improvements** — 4-mile lateral program and operational optimization driving per-well productivity gains
2. **Acquisition integration** — XTO assets (October 2025) adding incremental production with zero goodwill and proven geology
3. **Commodity price leverage** — at $80+ WTI, FCF expansion is dramatic without volume growth

The company has $3.0B in projected PUD development capital over 5 years, supporting long-term inventory depth. However, rising DD&A ($12.30/Boe in 2024 → $14.12/Boe in 2025 → $15.20/Boe in Q1 2026) reflects the cost basis of acquisitions flowing through the income statement.

**Estimate Revisions — Critical Signal:**

Current quarter EPS estimates have risen from $1.02 to $5.79 over 90 days (+467%). Full-year 2026 has moved from $4.91 to $20.47 (+317%). While much of this reflects oil price pass-through rather than operational outperformance, the magnitude of revisions demonstrates the operating leverage inherent in the business model. Next-year estimates at $15.58 (implying some oil price moderation) still produce a forward P/E under 10x.

## Valuation Assessment

At $143.98 with FY2026 consensus of $20.47, CHRD trades at 7.0x forward earnings. The company offers a 3.6% dividend yield and approximately 17% FCF yield at $80 WTI, which compares favorably to E&P peers on cash-return metrics.

The discount to peers is partially explained by single-basin concentration and commodity price uncertainty, but the magnitude appears excessive given: (a) dominant basin position, (b) DAPL resolution removing a primary idiosyncratic risk, (c) conservative balance sheet (0.19x debt/equity), and (d) aggressive capital return program.

PV-10 of proved reserves is $9.1B versus $8.1B market cap. However, PV-10 is a non-GAAP measure that excludes future income taxes; the GAAP standardized measure of discounted future net cash flows was $7.45B. As the company discloses, PV-10 "does not purport to represent the fair value" of reserves. Nonetheless, both measures suggest the market is ascribing limited value to upside optionality beyond proved reserves.

Analyst consensus target is $172.75 (median $175), with a range of $135-$224. Given that the most recent quarter delivered a significant earnings beat and estimates have been revised up 9-17% in the last 30 days alone, current consensus targets likely lag the fundamental reality.

**What the market is mispricing:** The market appears to apply a permanent discount for oil price uncertainty (Hormuz, OPEC+ decisions) that does not adequately account for (1) the balance sheet's strong liquidity and conservative leverage providing resilience across commodity cycles, (2) the capital return mechanism that converts high prices into direct shareholder value, and (3) the structural de-risking from DAPL resolution (May 21, 2026) that has yet to be fully reflected in valuation multiples.

## Competitive Landscape

Chord is the largest operator in the Williston Basin by acreage and production. Post-consolidation, it has meaningful scale advantages:

- **Operational efficiency:** Controlling 89% of proved reserves through operated positions enables drilling optimization, longer laterals, and coordinated development
- **Infrastructure:** Extensive pipeline/gathering ownership reduces reliance on third parties and supports narrower differentials (currently ~$2/bbl to WTI)
- **Acquisition accretion:** Integration of Enerplus and XTO completed clean (no goodwill on XTO; Enerplus goodwill already written off)
- **Basin maturity advantage:** Decades of production data enable targeted development of remaining inventory with high confidence

Q1 2026 per-unit costs (LOE $9.87 + GPT $2.70 + production taxes $3.50 + DD&A $15.20 = approximately $31/Boe in key operating costs) generate substantial margins at current oil prices.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Oil Price Exposure (HIGH — primary risk):**
The business is 56% oil-weighted with zero geographic diversification. FY2025 demonstrated the impact: a 14% WTI decline compressed operating income significantly (excluding impairment). At current elevated prices (~$105-110 WTI spot), the risk skews asymmetric — the EIA's May 2026 base case projects Brent declining to $89/bbl by 4Q26 and $79/bbl in 2027 as Hormuz tensions ease, suggesting meaningful downside risk to current spot.

**Specific downside scenario:** If Hormuz de-escalation succeeds and oil reverts to $85-95, FY2026 EPS would compress from ~$20 to ~$12-14. At 7x forward earnings, the stock would need to decline to $85-100 to fully reflect this scenario. Current price near $144 therefore embeds significant pricing of elevated oil persisting.

**2. Hedge book caps upside:**
As of December 31, 2025, Chord had approximately 4.2 MMBbl of three-way collars (weighted-average ceiling ~$77.51), 1.1 MMBbl of two-way collars (ceiling ~$71.23), and 1.9 MMBbl of fixed-price swaps at $65.05 for 2026, totaling approximately 19,800+ bbl/d of hedged volumes before subsequent additions with ceilings around $66-68. These positions limit full participation if prices remain above $100. The $165M net derivative liability in Q1 2026 demonstrates this constraint.

**3. Geographic concentration:**
Single-basin exposure creates vulnerability to regional disruptions. The DAPL easement grant (May 21, 2026) removes the largest specific manifestation of this risk.

**4. Rising depletion base:**
DD&A increasing from $13.00 to $15.20/Boe over two years compresses margins at lower oil prices. This is a structural cost of the acquisition-driven growth strategy.

**5. Insider selling:**
CEO and CSO adopted 10b5-1 selling plans in March 2026. CFO sold $609K, CSO sold $1.88M, COO sold $186K. No open-market purchases observed. Given the pre-arranged nature of sales during a period of rapidly rising estimates, this is standard diversification rather than a bearish signal, but the absence of any buying is noted.

## Conclusion

Chord Energy warrants a BUY recommendation at $143.98. The stock trades at 7x forward earnings with the dominant competitive position in the Williston Basin, $2.19B of liquidity, a management framework targeting aggressive capital returns (though buyback execution is discretionary and not guaranteed), and a materially de-risked transportation corridor via the DAPL easement grant.

The sector-level concern is legitimate: why BUY an Energy stock given the system's -1.24% average return in the sector? The answer is specific and falsifiable: (1) estimate revisions have risen 317% over 90 days with 30-day acceleration continuing at +16.7%; (2) the stock trades at 7x forward earnings — the market is pricing in significant commodity price risk that may be excessive given the company's balance sheet strength and capital-return framework; (3) at 17% FCF yield, the company is buying back stock at potentially accretive prices, creating a self-correcting mechanism. The catalyst is immediate: Q1 beat with guidance raised, DAPL easement granted, and $881M buyback authorization outstanding. If oil falls below $75 sustained, the thesis breaks — but at current WTI levels, cash generation is exceptional and the multiple discount appears unjustified.

Position sizing should be 2-3% given binary oil price exposure and the meaningful probability of oil price reversion consistent with EIA base-case projections (Brent to $89/bbl by 4Q26).

Additionally, the projected 12-15% capital-return yield assumes discretionary buyback execution that is not guaranteed, and peer P/E comparisons could not be fully validated against consistent data sources.
