## Executive Summary

RPM International is a $12.6B diversified specialty chemicals company trading at $97.97, down 24% from its 52-week high of $129.12. The stock sits below every analyst's price target (low: $108, high: $149), and the forward P/E of 16.7x is a material discount to the specialty chemicals peer median of 31.7x. This looks like a beaten-down quality compounder at an attractive entry point.

The problem is that the earnings trajectory has deteriorated meaningfully. RPM has missed consensus EPS in three of its last four quarters, including a -16.0% miss in the most recent December quarter. Analyst estimates are falling across every time horizon: current-quarter EPS has been cut 25% over 90 days, and full-year estimates are down 6.9%. The Consumer segment — roughly a third of revenue — is in organic decline (-4.7% in Q2 FY2026), and a $152.5M contingent litigation liability from an Oregon trade secrets verdict remains largely unaccrued. With the macro backdrop of stagflation, elevated oil prices feeding through to petrochemical raw material costs, and no rate relief through 2026, the near-term earnings path has more downside risk than the current consensus reflects.

The business has genuine competitive advantages: strong brands, niche market positions, a proven acquisition model, and a management team with a long track record. But negative earnings momentum, multiple unresolved headwinds, and a litigation wildcard make the risk/reward ambiguous at current levels. No specific catalyst resolves these issues within a 6-month horizon.

## Company Overview

RPM International manufactures and sells specialty chemicals across construction, industrial, and consumer markets. Revenue for FY2025 (ending May 31, 2025) was $7.37B, generated through three reporting segments (reorganized from four effective June 2025):

- **Construction Products Group (CPG, ~40% of sales):** Roofing systems, sealants, adhesives, concrete admixtures, building envelope solutions. Key brands include Tremco, Euclid, Dryvit, and Nudura.
- **Performance Coatings Group (PCG, ~27% of sales):** High-performance flooring, corrosion control coatings, fireproofing, FRP structures. Key brands include Stonhard, Carboline, and Fibergrate.
- **Consumer (~33% of sales):** DIY and professional paints, caulks, sealants, cleaners. Key brands include Rust-Oleum, DAP, Zinsser, and the recently acquired The Pink Stuff.

The company operates 118 manufacturing facilities in 22 countries, selling to ~163 countries. Approximately 71% of revenue is domestic, 29% international. The top 10 Consumer segment customers (Home Depot, Lowe's, Menards, Amazon) represent ~22% of consolidated sales and ~65% of Consumer segment sales.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Growth Has Stalled

Revenue growth has decelerated sharply from the post-COVID period:

| FY | Revenue ($B) | Growth |
|----|-------------|--------|
| 2022 | $6.71 | +9.8% |
| 2023 | $7.26 | +8.2% |
| 2024 | $7.34 | +1.1% |
| 2025 | $7.37 | +0.5% |

FY2025 organic growth was only 0.8%. H1 FY2026 total revenue grew 5.5%, but organic growth was just 1.3%, with acquisitions contributing 3.4%. The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, ended November 30, 2025) showed organic sales declining 0.5%, with Consumer organic sales falling 4.7%.

### Profitability: Margin Gains Under Pressure

The MAP 2025 restructuring program delivered real margin improvement: gross margin expanded from 37.9% in FY2023 to 41.4% in FY2025 (~350bps over three years). This trajectory has reversed. H1 FY2026 gross margin compressed to 40.8% from 41.4%, driven by tariff-related raw material inflation, reduced absorption from lower volumes, and temporary plant consolidation inefficiencies.

FY2025 diluted EPS of $5.35 was materially inflated by a one-time $43.9M deferred tax adjustment (U.S. foreign tax credit carryforwards), which dropped the effective tax rate to 12.9% vs. 25.2% in FY2024. This boosted EPS by approximately $0.34. Normalizing for this, underlying FY2025 EPS was closer to ~$5.00. H1 FY2026 diluted EPS of $3.03 represents a 5% decline from $3.19, with the effective tax rate normalizing to 23.5%.

### Balance Sheet: Adequate but Leveraged

- Total debt: $2.51B (November 2025), down from $2.64B at May 2025
- Leverage: 1.86x EBITDA (well within 3.75x covenant)
- Cash: $317M; available liquidity: $1.10B
- Goodwill + intangibles: $2.4B (~31% of total assets)
- $1.38B in debt matures in FY2027-2028, including $400M of 3.75% notes due 2027 requiring near-term refinancing

Debt increased meaningfully to finance the $487M Star Brands Group acquisition, and interest expense rose 20% in H1 FY2026 ($57.3M vs. $47.6M). In a hold/hike rate environment, refinancing the 2027 maturities will likely occur at higher rates, creating an incremental EPS headwind.

### Cash Flow: Strong Underlying Generation

FY2025 operating cash flow was $768M (depressed by $214M of strategic tariff-related inventory purchases). H1 FY2026 OCF improved to $583M from $528M. Free cash flow in FY2025 was approximately $538M after $230M in capex. The company returned $256M in dividends and $70M in buybacks. This cash generation capacity is one of RPM's genuine strengths.

### Acquisition-Driven Growth Model

RPM's primary growth engine is acquisitions — six completed in FY2025, with Kalzip GmbH (~€75M revenue) pending. The model has worked historically, but it requires sustained capital deployment and integration execution. An early signal from The Pink Stuff — a $12.7M reduction in earnout fair value just six months post-close — suggests performance is tracking below acquisition-date projections.

### Macro Headwinds

RPM's end markets are broadly cyclical and sensitive to the current macro environment:
- **Construction:** Elevated interest rates suppress new residential construction and dampen R&R spending. No rate relief through 2026 per IMF/OECD.
- **Consumer DIY:** Post-COVID normalization, weakening consumer sentiment (56.6, -12.5% YoY), and depressed housing turnover reduce demand.
- **Industrial:** More resilient, but overall business activity at 11-month lows.
- **Raw materials:** Oil at ~$107 (dual-chokepoint disruption) feeds through to petroleum-based derivatives, epoxies, and polyurethanes. The 14-channel CPI inflation model in the world model suggests petrochemical and industrial passthrough of +0.1-0.3% over 2-6 months. RPM has explicitly flagged tariff-related raw material inflation as a FY2026 headwind.

If consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.26 proves too high (given the miss pattern and negative revisions), the effective forward multiple could be 19-20x on a more realistic $4.90-5.10 EPS range. At that level, RPM is fairly valued, not cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

RPM's competitive position rests on several durable advantages:
- **Brand portfolio:** Rust-Oleum and DAP have strong consumer recognition. Professional brands (Tremco, Stonhard, Carboline) have installed-base loyalty.
- **Niche market leadership:** RPM targets fragmented sub-markets where it can be #1 or #2 rather than competing head-on with Sherwin-Williams or PPG across broad categories.
- **Services/installation capability:** CPG and PCG both have meaningful services revenue (roofing maintenance, flooring installation), creating stickier customer relationships.
- **Acquisition flywheel:** A decades-long track record of acquiring niche specialty chemicals companies and extracting margin through the MAP program.

The moat is moderate, not wide. The company faces competition from both larger (Sherwin-Williams, Sika, Akzo Nobel) and smaller regional players. Customer concentration in Consumer (Home Depot at 24% of segment sales) creates bargaining power asymmetry. And the acquisition model requires constant capital deployment to sustain growth rates.

## Risk Assessment

### Oregon Litigation ($152.5M Maximum Exposure)
The Consumer segment subsidiary lost a $190M jury verdict (breach of contract, trade secrets, fraud) in September 2024, reduced to $110.8M plus prejudgment interest and fees. RPM has accrued only $0.5M and is pursuing appeal, claiming "strong grounds" citing "significant trial error." The full range is $0.5M to $152.5M. The maximum represents approximately 22% of annual net income. This is a binary risk — an adverse appellate outcome would require a material charge.

### Consumer Segment Structural Weakness
Three consecutive periods of organic decline, customer destocking, product rationalization, and operational disruptions from system implementations and distribution center integration suggest problems beyond cyclicality. The 65% revenue concentration to 10 customers amplifies vulnerability to big-box order reductions.

### Raw Material / Tariff Inflation
Petroleum-based derivatives, minerals, and metals are core inputs. With oil at ~$107 and tariff-related inflation explicitly flagged by management, gross margin compression could extend. RPM implements pricing actions, but the lag between cost increases and price recovery compresses near-term margins.

### Debt Refinancing in Rising-Rate Environment
$1.38B in debt maturing FY2027-2028, including $400M of 3.75% notes due 2027. In a hold-or-hike rate environment, refinancing will likely increase interest expense further, creating an incremental EPS headwind of potentially $0.10-0.20.

### Acquisition Integration
The Pink Stuff earnout reduction ($12.7M) just 6 months post-close is an early warning signal. Integration execution risk is inherent in RPM's acquisition-dependent growth model.

### Options Market Signal
No options sentiment data was provided for RPM. The stock's beta of 1.093 and low short interest (2.4% of float, 2.8 days to cover) suggest the market is not positioning for a dramatic move in either direction. Short interest is unremarkable and does not indicate concentrated bearish conviction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- **Valuation floor:** Trading at 16.7x forward with all 14 analyst targets above current price. Even the lowest target ($108) implies 10% upside.
- **Cash flow:** Underlying FCF generation of ~$500M+ supports the 2.2% dividend yield, continued bolt-on acquisitions, and moderate buybacks regardless of near-term earnings softness.
- **MAP 2025 benefits materialize:** The plant consolidation inefficiencies creating near-term margin pressure are temporary. As operations stabilize, the 350bps gross margin improvement since FY2023 should reassert.
- **Construction cycle turn:** When rates decline, RPM's CPG segment and consumer R&R exposure provide significant earnings leverage.
- **The Pink Stuff long-term value:** If the brand scales globally beyond its UK base, it could become a meaningful earnings contributor over 3-5 years.

### Bear Case
- **Estimates are still too high:** Three misses in four quarters and uniformly negative revisions suggest consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.26 has further downside. A more realistic range of $4.90-5.10 makes the stock less cheap than it appears.
- **Consumer segment in structural decline:** The combination of weak DIY, big-box customer concentration, product rationalization, and operational disruptions could persist for several more quarters. Consumer is a third of revenue.
- **Stagflationary cost pressure:** Elevated oil prices → higher petrochemical costs → margin compression. No rate relief → no housing/construction demand recovery → no volume recovery. Both channels work against RPM.
- **Litigation wildcard:** A $152.5M adverse outcome (22% of net income) is a low-probability but high-impact event that isn't priced in.
- **Acquisition model risk:** Rising interest costs + depleted acquisition pipeline quality + Pink Stuff underperformance could challenge the growth model.

## Conclusion

RPM is a well-managed specialty chemicals company with genuine competitive advantages, trading at a material discount to its historical valuation and analyst targets after a 24% drawdown. The 2.2% dividend yield provides modest income while waiting.

Three consecutive earnings misses, uniformly declining estimate revisions, a Consumer segment in organic decline, raw material cost headwinds from the current stagflationary environment, and a $152.5M litigation contingency create too much uncertainty for a BUY recommendation. Consensus is still falling, and the risk is that the stock is cheap for a reason rather than a contrarian opportunity.

Catalysts that would convert this to a BUY: (1) stabilization of Consumer segment organic sales, (2) a reversal in estimate revision trends, (3) resolution or favorable update on the Oregon litigation, or (4) gross margin re-expansion demonstrating MAP 2025 benefits are reasserting. None of these are visible in the near-term data.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Uncertain).** The valuation provides reasonable downside protection (analyst low target 10% above current price, 2.2% yield), but negative earnings momentum and multiple unresolved headwinds prevent a higher-conviction call in either direction.
