## Executive Summary

Bloom Energy has undergone the fastest operating transformation in its 25-year history. Revenue grew 37.3% in FY2025 to $2,024.0M and then 149.8% in the first half of 2026 to $1,816.4M, with Q2 revenue of $1,065.4M up 165.5% year over year. Gross margin has moved from 14.8% (FY2023) to 33.4% (Q2 2026) and GAAP operating margin from −15.7% to 17.1% over the same span. Q2 GAAP operating income was $182.2M against a $3.5M loss a year earlier. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $3.9-4.2B of revenue, $800-900M of operating income and $2.55-2.85 of non-GAAP EPS. Consensus current-year EPS has risen 27.9% in 90 days to $2.72 and next-year 18.6% to $4.90. The mechanism is straightforward: grid interconnection queues and transmission constraints prevent hyperscalers and AI labs from securing utility power on their construction timelines, and Bloom's solid-oxide fuel cells can be energized faster. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act restored a 30% Section 48E investment tax credit for fuel cell property beginning construction after December 31, 2025 through 2033, removing what had been a 2025 policy overhang.

The demand thesis is well corroborated outside Bloom's own disclosures — data-center load is now visible in regulated-utility kWh data, in industrial equipment order books, and in hyperscaler capex guidance. The problem is not the theme. It is that $214.63 requires the theme to convert into durable, cash-generating, broadly-sourced earnings, and the Q2 10-Q shows the opposite pattern on three measures that matter. One customer accounted for 73% of Q2 revenue, up from a prior worst case of 43%; three customers hold 87% of accounts receivable. Reported 1H26 operating cash flow of $300.0M includes a $301.2M inflow from deferred revenue and customer deposits, so ex that single line the figure is approximately −$1.2M. And $306.5M of Customer Consideration Asset created by the Oracle warrant will amortize as a reduction of future reported revenue, of which only $17.9M has flowed through to date.

At 43.8x next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS, roughly 74x guided FY2026 operating income and about 100x estimated FY2026 GAAP EPS, the multiple embeds sustained hypergrowth with no allowance for the concentration reversing, the warranty issue recurring, or the scandium-sourcing dispute reaching the tax-credit indemnification channel management itself flagged in the 10-K. Our base case of $206 sits marginally below spot; bull $330; bear $105. On a 30/40/30 weighting expected return is approximately −1% against a six-month implied move of 45.6%. We move to **REDUCE**: trim exposure, do not initiate.

## Company Overview

Bloom designs and manufactures the Bloom Energy Server, a solid-oxide fuel cell platform generating on-site electricity from natural gas, biogas or hydrogen through an electrochemical rather than combustion process. Combined heat-and-power configurations reach roughly 90% efficiency; servers carry a 15-21 year depreciable life. A second line, the Bloom Electrolyzer, runs the same stack in reverse to produce hydrogen using 20-25% less electricity than low-temperature alternatives. The company reports one segment, with non-GAAP gross margin as the primary profitability measure and the CEO as chief operating decision maker.

Deployments span roughly 1,100 sites in nine countries, with manufacturing in Fremont, California and Delaware. Installed factory capacity is 1 GW, with a stated plan to reach 2 GW by end-2026.

Two structural features of the business model materially affect how the financials should be read. First, because the equipment carries large upfront cost, Bloom frequently does not sell to the end user. The Q2 10-Q defines "customer" as the contractual counterparty, "which in certain transactions may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." In FY2025, $862.1M — 42.6% of revenue — was recognized on sales to Brookfield-affiliated joint ventures in which Bloom holds only 9.9%, 9.9% and 15.0% passive interests. Brookfield is the primary beneficiary; the JVs are unconsolidated; Bloom does not guarantee their debt and maximum exposure to loss was $68.8M at June 30, 2026. Second, geographic mix has flipped decisively toward the United States: 90% of both Q2 and 1H26 revenue, versus 59% and 58% in the comparable 2025 periods. Bloom is now substantially a U.S. AI-infrastructure supplier.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** The progression is unambiguous:

| Period | Revenue | Growth | Gross margin | Operating margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $1,333.5M | — | 14.8% | −15.7% |
| FY2024 | $1,473.9M | +10.5% | 27.5% | +1.6% |
| FY2025 | $2,024.0M | +37.3% | 29.0% | +3.6% |
| 1H2026 | $1,816.4M | +149.8% | 32.0% | +14.0% |
| Q2 2026 | $1,065.4M | +165.5% | 33.4% | +17.1% |

Q2 product revenue alone was $935.4M against $296.6M a year earlier, 87.8% of total. Product is higher margin than the legacy service and electricity annuity but far lumpier: revenue recognizes only on delivery or completed installation, and the 10-K warns that "a small fluctuation in the timing of the sales transaction's completion could cause our operating results to vary materially from period to period."

FY2025 illustrates the distance between operating and net results. Operating income was $72.8M but the pretax loss was $84.4M, driven below the line by $53.9M interest expense, $40.4M equity in loss of unconsolidated affiliates, a $32.3M loss on debt extinguishment and $66.2M of debt conversion inducement expense, offset by $34.1M interest income. Those items were largely non-recurring, and Q2 2026 net income to common was $196.3M with GAAP diluted EPS of $0.62 (1H26: $266.9M and $0.85).

**Cash conversion is the weakest link.** Reported 1H26 operating cash flow was +$300.0M versus −$323.8M in 1H25, with $77.8M of capex implying $222.2M of free cash flow. Line-by-line, the working-capital section was a net drag of roughly $129.5M composed of large outflows — contract assets −$187.7M, prepaids −$132.3M, inventories −$115.1M, other long-term assets −$96.6M, receivables −$89.6M, deferred cost of revenue −$40.0M — offset by payables +$100.4M, accrued expenses +$89.4M, accrued warranty +$57.8M, and critically deferred revenue and customer deposits +$301.2M. Customer deposits alone rose $282.4M to $360.6M while deferred revenue rose only $18.9M. Subtracting that single line from reported OCF leaves approximately −$1.2M.

The charitable reading is correct as far as it goes: a company scaling revenue 150% mechanically absorbs working capital, and customer prepayments are the appropriate funding source and evidence of pricing power and scarce delivery slots. But the arithmetic means 1H26 cash generation is funded by the order book rather than by conversion of recognized profit, and it will not repeat at this magnitude unless bookings keep accelerating at a similar rate. As a labeled sensitivity only, a neutral-working-capital construction (1H net income $272.5M + SBC $100.4M + D&A $27.0M − capex $77.8M = $322.1M for the half) requires seasonality and cash-tax adjustment before it can be used as a forecast. We do not annualize either figure.

**Solvency is strong and screening scores mislead.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $2,666.9M against $2,530.3M of debt principal, so net cash of roughly +$136.6M; current ratio 4.09; debt/equity 2.43, improved from 4.55 at year-end. The maturity ladder is $7.4M in the remainder of 2026, zero in 2027 and 2028, $23.0M in 2029, and $2,500.0M in November 2030 — no refinancing wall for more than four years, and the 2030 notes carry a 0% coupon. A $600M senior secured revolver entered December 2025 is undrawn with $510M available after $90M of standby letters of credit, and the company reports compliance with minimum interest coverage and maximum leverage covenants. A distress-zone Altman Z-score on this balance sheet reflects a $3.99B accumulated deficit from 25 years of losses rather than solvency risk; recomputed with market equity the score lands firmly in the safe zone. The capital-structure question here is dilution, not default.

**Dilution.** Shares outstanding went from 229.1M (12/31/24) to 280.0M (12/31/25) to 293.4M (6/30/26), a 28% increase in eighteen months, driven mainly by the November 2025 induced conversion in which $975.9M of 3.0% notes were exchanged for $988.4M cash plus 42.4M shares at $66.2M of inducement expense. Reported diluted shares are 323.33M. Instruments outstanding at June 30 add roughly 30.5M further base-case shares (0% notes 12.82M at a $194.97 conversion price, remaining 3.0% notes 1.71M, unsettled converted shares 0.88M, options 5.01M, unvested RSU/PSU 10.10M), equal to 10.4% of current shares or 9.4% of post-issuance ownership. A further 2.69 shares per $1,000 on the 0% notes applies only on a make-whole fundamental change and should be probability-weighted rather than assumed. The 0% notes' closing-price condition (20 of 30 trading days above $253.46) was not met in Q1 2026, so they were not convertible during Q2.

**Tax.** The effective rate was 0.7% in Q2 and 1H26 on $274.5M of pretax income, because of a full valuation allowance against $872.6M of U.S. deferred tax assets. NOLs are $2.3B federal and $1.5B California. Near term this makes Bloom a near-zero-cash-tax company, which flatters free cash flow relative to a normalized taxpayer. Medium term, a valuation allowance release would produce a large one-time non-cash GAAP benefit, and NOL exhaustion would raise the cash rate materially. Given the 28% share-count increase and convertible activity, Section 382 limitation is a live question rather than a settled one despite management's stated conclusion.

**Stock compensation.** 1H26 SBC was $113.4M, 6.2% of revenue, versus $62.4M in 1H25. Unrecognized SBC on unvested awards is $401.1M plus $106.0M on the 2026 executive and CEO awards. The weighted-average grant-date fair value on 1H26 grants was $177.23 against $25.74 on the year-end unvested balance — a roughly 7x increase in cost per share granted that will compound reported expense.

## Growth Analysis

The demand driver is durable and externally confirmed. Hyperscaler capex guidance has risen through three consecutive reporting cycles, regulated utilities are reporting data-center load in kWh rather than in pipeline announcements, and industrial equipment suppliers are booking record data-center-driven revenue. Interconnection queues and transmission build times are physical constraints that do not resolve on an AI construction schedule, and OBBBA's restoration of the 30% ITC through 2033 removes a policy discontinuity that had threatened 2025 economics.

Estimate revisions confirm the inflection with unusual velocity. Current-quarter consensus rose 31.5% in 30 days to $0.67; current-year rose 25.6% in 30 days and 27.9% in 90 days to $2.72; next-year rose 18.6% in 90 days to $4.90. All four reported quarters beat, by 55.1%, 40.7%, 228.6% and 86.6%. Q2 revenue of $1,065.4M exceeded roughly $827M of consensus by 29%.

Three qualifications belong in the growth assessment.

First, the FY2026 guide is more demanding on margin than on revenue. The $4.05B midpoint implies 2H revenue of $2,234M against 1H's $1,816M, a manageable 1.23x step-up. But $850M of operating income less 1H's $254.4M leaves $595.6M on $2,234M, a 26.7% second-half operating margin against Q2's actual 17.1% — roughly 960bp of implied expansion in two quarters. Whether the $800-900M figure is stated on a GAAP or non-GAAP basis is not resolvable from available sources; at roughly $227M of annualized SBC the distinction is worth about $0.70 per share and materially changes the implied margin path. This is the single most aggressive element of the guide.

Second, $306.5M of Customer Consideration Asset ($91.0M current, $215.5M non-current) will amortize as a reduction of revenue as Oracle-arrangement servers are delivered. Only $17.9M has flowed through cumulatively. This is non-cash to Bloom — shareholders paid via dilution — but it suppresses reported revenue and gross margin on those deliveries in a way that a revenue-multiple valuation does not anticipate. The amortization period is undisclosed.

Third, capacity. Bloom must double factory throughput from 1 GW to 2 GW by end-2026 while roughly doubling revenue, and its own risk factors flag cost overruns, labor shortages and quality-control degradation during production upgrades. The company's July 2026 rebuttal to short sellers claimed a supply chain capable of supporting 25 GW per year of fuel cell production, a figure that cannot be reconciled with the 10-K's 1-2 GW factory plan and which the source rendered with an editorial bracket. We do not treat it as established.

On backlog: GAAP unsatisfied performance obligations at June 30 were $442.4M (product/installation) plus $51.7M (service) = $494.1M, with disclosed exclusions for sub-one-year contracts and right-to-invoice arrangements. Company-cited figures of "$20 billion total backlog, including product backlog approaching $6 billion" use a different, company-defined construct that includes ITC value. The exclusions are disclosed and GAAP-permitted, and part of the gap is legitimate, but a roughly 40x difference means the $20B figure carries far less evidentiary weight than the $494.1M and should not anchor a growth forecast.

## Valuation Assessment

At $214.63 with $2,530.3M debt and $2,666.9M cash, enterprise value is approximately $62.82B. Against that:

| Metric | Multiple |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026 guided revenue ($4.05B midpoint) | 15.5x |
| EV / FY2026 guided operating income ($850M) | 73.9x |
| P/E on current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS $2.72 | 78.9x |
| P/E on next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS $4.90 | 43.8x |
| P/E on estimated FY2026 GAAP EPS (~$2.00-2.20) | ~98-107x |
| EV / FY2025 revenue | 31.0x |

The forward multiple on next-year non-GAAP earnings, 43.8x against roughly 80% implied EPS growth, is not prima facie unreasonable and is the strongest element of the bull case. Three considerations reduce its weight.

The non-GAAP measure excludes roughly $0.70 per share of annualized stock compensation that is a real transfer of ownership, and that cost per share granted has risen sevenfold. On GAAP earnings the stock trades near 100x. Second, the estimate-revision signal, powerful as it is, applies with greatest force to inflection stocks that are cheap relative to improving estimates — below roughly 30x forward earnings or a PEG under 1.5. At 78.9x current-year and near 100x GAAP, beats are already embedded in the price; the asymmetry runs the other way, with disappointments repricing violently. The stock fell from $351.28 on June 25 to $163.75 on July 29, a 53% decline in five weeks, which demonstrates the point empirically. Third, the analyst distribution is unusually wide: low $97, high $390, mean $273.51, median $288.50. A 4x spread between the extremes is not consensus confirmation of anything; it reflects a genuinely binary set of outcomes, and at a premium multiple targets and price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent verification.

Our scenarios:

- **Base $206** (−4.0% from $214.63). 42x next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS of $4.90. Cross-checked against roughly 9.5x EV/FY2027 revenue of $6.3B. This assumes the guide is delivered near the midpoint, concentration eases without breaking, and the multiple compresses modestly as growth decelerates from triple digits.
- **Bull $330** (+53.8%). FY2027 revenue above $7B, operating margin sustained near 25%, non-GAAP EPS approaching $6.00 at 55x, or roughly 12x EV/FY2027 revenue. Requires the 2 GW expansion on schedule, the scandium dispute to resolve in Bloom's favor, and the customer base to broaden.
- **Bear $105** (−51.1%). The 73% counterparty defers or slips deliveries, the specific warranty reserve proves to be the first tranche of a fleet issue, or an ITC/FEOC finding triggers indemnification. FY2027 revenue near $4.5B with margins reverting toward FY2025 levels and the multiple compressing to 6-7x EV/sales. This band contains the $97 low analyst target.

Probability-weighted at 30/40/30: 0.30(+53.8%) + 0.40(−4.0%) + 0.30(−51.1%) ≈ −1%. There is no margin of safety at the current price, and the variance around that near-zero expectation is very large.

## Competitive Landscape

The relevant competition is on-site generation versus the interconnection queue rather than Bloom versus other fuel cell makers. Bloom's advantage is time-to-power, supported by 1,100 deployed sites, solid-oxide efficiency, and demonstrated ability to warehouse and transfer project rights — in May 2026 Bloom paid $50.0M for option rights on a third-party project entity, then in July assigned those rights to a Brookfield vehicle which will pay Bloom $50.0M on exercise, with a put back to the original holder, Oracle, as fallback. That origination capability is a genuine moat element, and it is also an expanding balance-sheet commitment if the $25B Brookfield framework scales.

The trajectory is improving: gross margin has expanded 1,860bp in three years, U.S. share of revenue has risen from 70% to 90% as the AI channel opened, and Bloom outperformed fuel-cell peers by a wide margin in 2026 (+248% in 1H26 versus FuelCell Energy +169% and Plug Power roughly flat). Alternatives named in the 10-K include utilities, co-generation, nuclear including SMRs, hydro, geothermal, renewables plus storage, other fuel cell companies and diesel gensets. Gas turbines with multi-year order books are the nearest substitute at scale; SMRs are a later-decade threat.

Two competitive constraints deserve weight. FERC, at DOE direction, began considering reforms to the interconnection of on-site generation co-located with large loads in 2025 — a two-sided regulatory process that could either institutionalize or erode the speed-to-power advantage. And scandium supply is a real bottleneck question rather than pure short-seller noise: global consumption is only 30-40 metric tons annually per USGS, one Chinese producer claims over half of fuel-cell-grade supply, and Beijing added scandium to its export-license list in 2026. Non-Chinese sources are small. Bloom "categorically rejects" dependence on China for scandium and asserts sufficient supply for current demand and backlog. We cannot resolve the intensity assumptions on either side. Vendor advances rising from $18.1M to $143.5M in six months is consistent with an aggressive campaign to lock up constrained inputs, though the filings do not identify the materials or counterparties.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, worsening.** FY2023: two customers 63%. FY2024: three at 53%. FY2025: three at 68%. 1H26: two at 65%. Q2 2026: **one non-related-party customer at 73% of total revenue.** Accounts receivable concentration reached 36%/34%/17% = 87% across three counterparties versus 73% at year-end, and the allowance for credit losses rose from $0.46M to $3.00M. Related-party revenue fell from $376.1M in 1H26 to $2.8M in Q2, so the channel composition rotated away from the Brookfield JVs toward a single large direct counterparty. The mix changed; measured concentration deteriorated. Because revenue recognizes on delivery or completed installation, a single quarter can be dominated by whichever project reached acceptance, and full-year concentration may look different. That is a real mitigant but it does not convert the disclosed figure into evidence of diversification, and management's public framing of growth driven by "a broad mix of customers and projects rather than a few large deals" does not reconcile with 73%.

**Channel dependency.** Bloom's largest historical revenue channel requires Brookfield to approve and fund individual projects; Brookfield holds exclusivity over certain Bloom fuel cell projects and periodic pipeline review rights. The $5B framework was expanded to $25B on June 30, 2026 (well corroborated by Reuters and others), but a framework is financing capacity subject to per-project approval, not a purchase order. Deloitte flagged the consolidation assessment of the Fund JVs as a critical audit matter and issued unqualified opinions on both financials and ICFR; the risk is dependency rather than accounting impropriety.

**Warranty.** Accrued warranty and product performance liabilities rose from $20.0M to $77.8M between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026, with the product warranty component going from $3.2M to $61.0M on $71.6M of additions against $13.8M of expenditures. The footnote discloses "a specific warranty reserve of $58.3 million... recognized within cost of product revenue." The word "specific" ordinarily indicates an identified, quantified issue in a defined population of units. At $58.3M it equals 3.7% of 1H26 product revenue, meaning underlying product margin is better than reported — and that a known fleet issue exists. Cause, scope, affected fleet and forward exposure are all undisclosed. The 10-K separately acknowledges latent defects discovered after field deployment and rising defect probability as volume scales.

**Litigation and the short-seller dispute.** On July 8, 2026 Hunterbrook Media published a report alleging continued reliance on Chinese scandium, with an affiliate disclosing a short position; Bloom rejected the claims on July 9 in a Reg FD filing. A securities class action covering purchasers from February 27, 2025 to July 8, 2026 followed, known so far only from a plaintiff-firm notice with no verified docket or claim detail. The financially consequential channel is not reputational. The 10-K warns that failing to interpret OBBBA "prohibited foreign entities" or "material assistance" requirements correctly may cause "a partial or full reduction in the related federal tax benefit, and our customers, financiers and equity investors may require us to indemnify them." Because Bloom sells into ITC-financed project vehicles, a FEOC finding could reduce project-level credits and route back as indemnity claims. Separately, the Plansee/Global Tungsten arbitration produced a partial award in February 2026 with the parties disputing whether first-phase issues are resolved and no timeline for further phases.

**Governance.** KR Sridhar is Founder, CEO and Chairman, alongside a classified board, supermajority amendment requirements, blank-check preferred and no action by written consent. Every disclosed open-market insider transaction in the reviewed window was a sale: director John Chambers 55,000 shares at $297.69 (May 28) and 15,000 at $205.58 (August 3, after the drawdown); CCO Aman Joshi, COO Satish Chitoori, CAO Maciej Kurzymski and Shawn Soderberg all selling at $259-$300 in May-July. No open-market purchases by anyone, including after a drawdown from the high. Form 4 data does not disclose whether these were 10b5-1 executions, and selling into record results is often routine wealth management. But five insiders selling within roughly two months, including a repeat sale below the earlier price, with zero offsetting purchases, is a clustered pattern rather than noise. The offsetting positive is compensation design: the June 15, 2026 CEO award is PSUs cliff-vesting over a 3.5-year performance period with net shares subject to a mandatory hold through December 31, 2031.

Two further unresolved items: the $54.5M capped calls covering 33.55M shares ($18.85 strike, $26.46 cap, June 2028 expiry, maximum intrinsic value roughly $255.3M) whose status after full extinguishment of the underlying 2028 notes on July 10 is not updated in the 10-Q; and an Item 5.02 8-K filed June 17, 2026 amended by an 8-K/A on June 22, which we did not read. Amendments to officer/director 8-Ks are uncommon.

**The Oracle inducement.** Bloom committed to a warrant for 3,531,073 shares at $113.28 to Oracle, described in filings as a "customer's customer" — an end user of servers sold under the arrangement rather than Bloom's direct contractual counterparty. On cashless exercise Bloom issued 1,905,433 shares and, because net settlement produced 1.4M fewer shares than gross, agreed to issue an additional 248,798 shares valued at $72.3M as an inducement. Reconstructing both paths at the implied ~$246 exercise price, gross and net settlement are economically equivalent to Bloom, so the $72.3M is an incremental cost whose identified benefit is a lower share count. Oracle may have had independent tax or regulatory reasons to prefer gross settlement and extracted payment for switching, and Bloom may have had index, registration or relationship reasons to minimize issuance; the disclosure states only that the shares induced net settlement, and we attribute no motive.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 115.3% is essentially fair against 112.8% one-year realized (2.5-point spread), but the term structure is in backwardation — 115.3% at one week versus 111.2% at six months — with implied moves of ±9.9% at three days, ±18.3% at one month, ±27.8% at three months and ±45.6% at six months. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (−1.2%) and widens through the curve, while the put/call volume ratio rises sharply with tenor (0.86 at one week, 2.31 at three months, 3.13 at six months). Read together: the market prices no directional view near term but substantial and increasing two-sided event risk into the autumn, with hedging demand concentrated in longer tenors — consistent with the litigation, guidance-delivery and concentration questions resolving over quarters rather than weeks. Short interest is only 7.5% of float at 1.3 days to cover, so the bear thesis is uncrowded and there is little squeeze cushion supporting the price. A single unusual print (563 August-14 $268 calls, 5.3x open interest, 24% out of the money) is too small to inform. The overall read corroborates our high risk score and argues against sizing this as a core position at any rating.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($330, +53.8%).** AI power scarcity is a multi-year physical constraint and Bloom is the fastest available on-site solution. OBBBA guarantees 30% ITC economics through 2033. The Brookfield framework at $25B provides financing capacity far beyond current throughput, and Bloom is capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained, which is why customers are prepaying $360.6M to hold delivery slots. Factory capacity doubles to 2 GW by end-2026 and revenue follows; FY2027 revenue exceeds $7B at 25% operating margin, non-GAAP EPS approaches $6.00, and the near-zero cash tax rate from $2.3B of federal NOLs converts more of that into cash than a normalized taxpayer would. The 0% coupon on $2.5B of five-year money is exceptionally cheap capital, the notes are not currently convertible, and no maturity arrives before November 2030. The scandium allegation resolves as a scoping dispute; the specific warranty reserve proves to be a one-time true-up, meaning underlying product margin is 370bp better than reported. At 55x $6.00 the stock revisits its June high.

**Bear case ($105, −51.1%).** The 73% counterparty defers deliveries by a quarter and revenue recognition — which requires delivery or completed installation — collapses in that period, taking the multiple with it. Operating cash flow reverts negative as prepayment inflows normalize and the $187.7M contract-asset and $115.1M inventory builds fail to convert on schedule. The $58.3M specific warranty reserve proves to be the first tranche of a fleet issue on the newly deployed AI-scale installations, and the 2 GW capacity ramp degrades quality control exactly as the 10-K warns. A FEOC or material-assistance finding on sourcing reduces 48E credits at project level and generates indemnification claims, simultaneously validating the securities class action. The $306.5M contra-revenue asset amortizes into a decelerating revenue base, compressing reported growth and margin. FY2027 revenue lands near $4.5B, margins revert toward FY2025 levels, the multiple compresses to 6-7x EV/sales, and the stock trades toward the $97 low target.

The bear case does not require the AI power theme to fail. It requires Bloom-specific execution, concentration, quality or compliance to disappoint, and at 43.8x next-year non-GAAP earnings the price allows for none of those.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now rather than waiting for a catalyst. The stock has recovered from its July 29 low of $163.75 without resolving any of the three questions that caused the decline — concentration, cash conversion and the sourcing dispute — which makes the current level a better exit than the post-print low. For holders with a large embedded gain, reduce to a residual position no greater than a third of prior weight, sized to survive the ±45.6% six-month implied move without forced action. Do not initiate new capital here.

(3) Q3 operating margin at or above 22%, putting the implied 26.7% second-half margin within reach rather than requiring a step-change in Q4. (4) A price near $150-160, which would take next-year non-GAAP P/E to roughly 31x and restore genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers.** (1) Any additional specific warranty reserve, or disclosure that the $58.3M reserve relates to a systemic fleet defect. (2) A regulatory or IRS finding on prohibited-foreign-entity or material-assistance status affecting 48E eligibility on financed projects, or disclosure of a customer indemnification claim. (3) FY2026 revenue guidance cut, or a quarter in which revenue misses consensus materially because a single large project slipped. (4) Operating cash flow turning materially negative alongside a decline in customer deposits, which would indicate the order book is no longer funding the working-capital build. (5) Clustered insider selling continuing at prices below $200 with still no open-market purchases.

**Reassessment date.** The Q3 2026 report in late October, which is the first opportunity to observe whether Q2's 73% concentration was a recognition artifact or a structural fact, and whether the implied second-half margin expansion is materializing.

## Conclusion

Bloom Energy has converted a decade of losses into a genuinely profitable, fast-growing business by supplying power on a timeline the grid cannot match. The operating evidence — 165% Q2 revenue growth, 33.4% gross margin, 17.1% GAAP operating margin, four consecutive beats, guidance raised to $3.9-4.2B, and consensus current-year EPS up 27.9% in 90 days — is real and the demand backdrop is corroborated well outside the company's own disclosures. The balance sheet has been repaired: net cash, a 4.09 current ratio, a $510M undrawn revolver and no maturity before November 2030 on 0% paper.

The price does not leave room for what remains unresolved. One customer supplied 73% of Q2 revenue and three hold 87% of receivables, both worse than any prior period. Reported first-half operating cash flow is approximately zero once customer prepayments are removed. A $58.3M "specific" warranty reserve appeared with no disclosed cause. $306.5M of contra-revenue sits on the balance sheet awaiting amortization against future reported growth. A securities class action is pending, the sourcing dispute has a direct tax-credit indemnification channel management itself flagged, and every disclosed insider transaction across five individuals over two months was a sale, including one below an earlier sale after the drawdown. At 43.8x next-year non-GAAP EPS that excludes roughly $0.70 per share of stock compensation, and near 100x GAAP, none of that is priced.

Probability-weighted expected return is approximately zero against a 45.6% six-month implied move, with three of the four most reliable evaluative dimensions — financial health, management quality and risk — reading neutral to poor. **REDUCE.** Trim into the recovery from the July low, keep any residual position small, and require the concentration and cash-conversion disclosures in the October report before considering re-engagement.
