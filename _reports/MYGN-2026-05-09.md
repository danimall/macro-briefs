## Executive Summary

Myriad Genetics is a molecular diagnostics company with $825M in revenue, 70% gross margins, and a market capitalization of just $407M (~0.49x revenue). The company has generated cumulative net losses of $868M over the past four fiscal years, took $319M in impairment charges in FY2025 alone (acknowledging its own previously optimistic internal forecasts were wrong), and carries a $125M term loan at 10.4% interest with revenue covenants that escalate to $974M by 2029.

The most recent quarter (Q1 CY2026, reported May 5) missed estimates with adjusted EPS of -$0.09 vs. -$0.07 expected, and revenue of $200.4M grew only 2.3% YoY. The stock fell 16.2% to $4.11 and currently sits at $4.29. UnitedHealthcare's discontinuation of GeneSight coverage eliminated approximately $45M in annual revenue with no confirmed path to restoration. Insider open-market purchases by the CEO ($200K) and a Director (~$495K) at $4.66-$5.00 represent genuine conviction signals, but they purchased before the Q1 miss.

The fundamental question is whether Myriad can convert its strong hereditary cancer franchise (45% of revenue, volumes growing 10-16%) and pipeline products (Precise MRD, FirstGene, PATHOMIQ) into sustained profitability before debt covenants and cash consumption force dilutive capital raises. At current trajectory, with FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of just $37M-$49M against $13M+ in annual interest expense, the margin for error is negligible.

## Company Overview

Myriad Genetics develops and commercializes genetic tests across four segments: Hereditary Cancer (45% of revenue, $372M), Prenatal Health (23%, $186M), Mental Health (17%, $144M), and Tumor Profiling (15%, $122M). The company employs ~2,700 people and processes tests at single-site laboratories in Salt Lake City, South San Francisco, Mason OH, and San Diego.

Key products include MyRisk (63-gene hereditary cancer panel), BRACAnalysis CDx (the only FDA-approved germline BRCA companion diagnostic for breast cancer), GeneSight (pharmacogenomic test for psychiatric medication selection), Prequel (non-invasive prenatal screen), and Prolaris (prostate cancer prognostic test).

The company is in a leadership transition following a complete C-suite refresh in 2025: new CEO Samraat Raha (April 2025), new COO, new CCO, and new CFO (August 2025).

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance of $860-880M represents 4-7% topline growth. Current quarter estimate revisions are mixed: current year EPS revised down 18.3% over 30 days and 31.0% over 90 days, while next year estimates are up 31.2% over 90 days.

**Growth drivers:**
- Hereditary cancer volumes growing 10-16% YoY (strongest segment)
- MyRisk expansion to 63 genes increases test utility
- Pipeline: Precise MRD (MRD market potentially $3B+), FirstGene (4-in-1 prenatal), PATHOMIQ AI prostate diagnostic
- SOPHiA Genetics liquid biopsy CDx collaboration

**Growth headwinds:**
- UNH GeneSight coverage loss (-$45M annually)
- Revenue per test declining ~5% in hereditary cancer (pricing pressure)
- EndoPredict US discontinuation
- PAMA-driven Medicare rate compression
- Other payors could follow UNH's decision

The pipeline products (MRD, FirstGene) are 12-24+ months from meaningful revenue contribution. Natera has substantial first-mover advantage in MRD with Signatera. The growth thesis requires both sustaining hereditary cancer momentum and successfully commercializing pipeline products — a dual execution challenge with no margin for stumbles.

## Valuation Assessment

At $4.29/share and $407M market cap:
- Price/Revenue: 0.49x
- Forward P/E: negative (not meaningful)
- EV/Revenue: ~0.6x (adding net debt)
- FY2026 EV/EBITDA: ~12-15x on $37-49M adjusted EBITDA guidance

The 0.49x revenue multiple on 70% gross margins appears cheap in isolation. However, the company has never demonstrated ability to convert revenue into sustained positive earnings or free cash flow. A company that grows revenue 6% annually but generates zero FCF is worth its liquidation value plus option value on future profitability.

Peers are not directly comparable given Myriad's unique profitability challenge, but profitable diagnostics companies trade at 3-5x revenue. Applying even 1x revenue (implying a path to modest profitability) yields ~$8.75/share, which aligns with the $7.17 analyst mean target. The current price implies the market assigns ~50% probability to a scenario where Myriad achieves sustained profitability vs. scenarios involving dilutive capital raises, covenant violations, or continued value destruction.

Analyst consensus: mean target $7.17, median $6.00, range $4.00-$18.00. The distribution is wide, reflecting genuine uncertainty about the trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

**Hereditary Cancer (strength):** Myriad retains a strong position with the broadest panel (63 genes), the only FDA-approved germline BRCA CDx, and deep community oncology relationships. Competitive moat is moderate — Invitae (now bankrupt/acquired), Quest, LabCorp, and smaller labs compete, but Myriad's clinical integration and brand remain durable. Volume growth of 10-16% confirms competitive health.

**GeneSight (weakening):** The UNH coverage loss signals broader payor skepticism about pharmacogenomic testing evidence. Competitors (Genomind, Tempus, Quest, LabCorp) offer similar tests. Patents begin expiring in 2027. This segment's competitive position is deteriorating.

**Prenatal (moderate):** Natera's Panorama and BillionToOne's UNITY compete directly. FirstGene's 4-in-1 design could differentiate, but it's still in clinical study. No clear competitive advantage today.

**Tumor Profiling/MRD (challenged):** Natera's Signatera has significant head start in MRD. Foundation Medicine and Guardant are better-capitalized competitors in genomic profiling. Myriad is a late entrant against more established players.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural risks:**

1. **Covenant escalation risk.** The $974M revenue requirement by 2029 demands ~4% CAGR from current levels. Any acceleration of payor coverage losses or competitive market share erosion could trigger covenant concerns as early as 2027-2028.

2. **Capital raise probability.** With FCF negative, minimal operating cash flow, and debt carrying costs of $13M+/year, the company will likely need to raise capital within 12-18 months if the adjusted EBITDA ramp stalls. At $407M market cap, equity raises would be highly dilutive.

3. **Single-payer concentration.** One unnamed payor represents 13% of A/R. Another coverage decision by a major payor could rapidly change the revenue trajectory.

4. **Single-lab risk.** No redundancy in laboratory operations — any site disruption halts the associated product line.

5. **Complete management team replacement.** While individually qualified, the entire C-suite turned over in 2025. Integration and strategic coherence take time.

6. **Federal healthcare policy headwinds.** Potential Medicaid and ACA marketplace enrollment reductions from recent legislation could reduce testing volumes.

**Mitigating factors:**
- OrbiMed (sophisticated healthcare lender) extended 5-year credit, implying they see sufficient asset value
- Hereditary cancer franchise generates stable, growing volumes
- Insider buying signal (pre-Q1 miss, but still meaningful directional conviction)

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The revenue covenant becomes progressively more binding, and FY2026 EBITDA guidance leaves minimal room for operational stumbles.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 8.9% of float with 3.9 days to cover. Moderate but not extreme — low squeeze risk. The stock is illiquid (avg volume 1.6M shares on a $400M market cap), which could amplify moves in either direction.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data provided. The stock's high beta movement on earnings (-16.2% on the Q1 miss, +46% on Aug 2025 results) indicates binary event risk for shorts. Carrying cost is likely manageable at <9% SI.

Upside risk to $5.50-6.00 on a strong quarter = ~28-40% upside. Risk/reward approximately 1:1.3, unfavorable for a short given the binary earnings outcomes.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The combination of binary earnings events (+46% moves on beats), low absolute price, insider buying, and unfavorable risk/reward (1:1.3) makes this unsuitable for active shorting. The stock is cheap enough that any positive surprise generates outsized moves. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Conclusion

Myriad Genetics presents an optically cheap stock (0.49x revenue, 70% gross margins) masking deep operational and financial challenges. The company has destroyed $868M in shareholder value over four years, carries expensive debt with escalating covenants, faces structural reimbursement headwinds, and just missed its most recent quarterly estimates. The insider buying signal is the strongest bullish indicator, but it preceded the Q1 miss and has not been followed by any positive fundamental development.

The stock's current price represents a call option on successful execution of a multi-year turnaround by an entirely new management team, in an increasingly competitive market, while carrying expensive debt that requires revenue growth to remain in compliance. The wide analyst target range ($4.00-$18.00) accurately reflects the binary nature of outcomes. At $4.29, the downside to $3.00-3.50 in a capital-raise scenario represents proportionally larger risk than the current distance from the analyst median target of $6.00 would suggest as upside, particularly given the Q1 miss established negative momentum.

Recommendation: **AVOID.** Monitor for re-entry if the company demonstrates two consecutive quarters of beats with positive free cash flow, or if a specific reimbursement catalyst emerges for GeneSight.
