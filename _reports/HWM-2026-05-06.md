## Executive Summary

Howmet Aerospace has executed one of the most impressive operational transformations in industrial manufacturing over the past four years, tripling net income from $469M (2022) to $1,508M (2025) while expanding Total Segment Adjusted EBITDA margins from 22.5% to 30.4%. The company holds leading market positions on critical aerospace engine programs, with pricing above inflation — a rare quality in capital goods that has driven margin expansion well beyond volume leverage alone. Revenue compounded at 13.4% annually from 2022-2025, with management guiding to ~$9.1B in 2026 (~10% growth).

The central tension is valuation: at $256.43 (May 6, 2026 close), HWM trades at approximately 56-59x company-guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.35-$4.55 (midpoint $4.45). These are technology-company multiples applied to an industrial manufacturer. However, the market is paying for three features that justify a premium: (1) pricing power that expands margins even in inflationary environments, (2) multi-year visibility from aerospace certification barriers and long-term engine program positions, and (3) emerging demand from gas turbine/data center power generation that adds a structural growth vector outside traditional aerospace cycles. Third-party analyst consensus estimates have been rising, and HWM has consistently beaten earnings expectations in recent quarters.

The BUY thesis rests on continued earnings compounding, supported by commercial aerospace production ramp, aftermarket engine demand, defense spending, and data center power buildout. The primary risk is valuation compression if growth decelerates or if the aerospace cycle turns.

## Company Overview

Howmet Aerospace is the world's leading producer of advanced engineered metal components for jet engines, airframe structures, and industrial gas turbines. Created from the 2020 separation of Arconic Inc., the company traces its materials science heritage to Alcoa (founded 1888). Operations span four segments:

- **Engine Products** (52% of revenue, $4.32B): Investment castings (airfoils), seamless rolled rings for jet engines and gas turbines. 33.3% EBITDA margins.
- **Fastening Systems** (21%, $1.75B): Aerospace/industrial fasteners. 30.4% EBITDA margins (up from 20.6% in 2023).
- **Engineered Structures** (14%, $1.15B): Titanium ingots, forgings, machining. 21.2% EBITDA margins (up from 12.9% in 2023).
- **Forged Wheels** (13%, $1.04B): Commercial truck wheels. 28.5% EBITDA margins.

Key customers include RTX Corporation and GE Aerospace (each ~11% of revenue). Geographic mix is 72% North America, 22% Europe. The company operates across 19 countries with approximately 1,020 active patents.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (12-18 months):**
1. Commercial aerospace production ramp: Boeing 737 MAX gradually recovering from 2024 strike/regulatory delays; Airbus A320neo production increasing. OEM build rates directly drive Engine Products and Fastening Systems demand.
2. Engine aftermarket: Aging fleet requires increasing maintenance/replacement parts — management has explicitly cited engine spares growth as a key demand driver.
3. Gas turbine/data center power: Management explicitly identified AI-driven data center electricity demand as a new growth vector for turbine components.
4. Defense spending: ~17% of revenue, growing with elevated global defense budgets.
5. CAM acquisition: Adds fastener revenue with margin expansion potential under Howmet's operational playbook.

**Medium-term drivers (2-4 years):**
- Engine program ramps (LEAP, GTF, GE9X) have decades of aftermarket demand locked in
- Capacity expansion underway (machine tool lead times >2 years create barriers)
- Pricing power sustainability: leading market positions on qualified programs persist through certification barriers

**Estimate revision momentum:** Third-party analyst consensus estimates for FY2026 and FY2027 have been rising over recent months. Howmet's own FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is $4.35-$4.55 (midpoint $4.45), while third-party consensus estimates appear modestly above company guidance. Howmet has beaten EPS estimates in recent quarters, suggesting consensus may remain conservative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $256.43 (May 6 close) with company-guided FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $4.45, HWM trades at approximately 58x forward earnings. For estimated FY2027 EPS (if third-party consensus is approximately $5.50-$5.60), the forward P/E drops to approximately 46x. These are elevated multiples by any industrial standard — peer median P/E is 24.7x.

**EV/EBITDA:** Using pre-CAM net debt of $2,308M and a market cap of approximately $103.6B (at ~$256), enterprise value is approximately $105.9B. Divided by FY2025 Total Segment Adjusted EBITDA of $2,507M, EV/EBITDA is approximately 42x. (Note: Howmet also reports Adjusted EBITDA excluding special items of approximately $2.4B with a 29.3% margin; using this figure yields a modestly higher multiple.)

**Why the premium is partially justified:**
- Margin expansion trajectory is still active (30.4% Total Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin and rising)
- Pricing power above inflation is rare in industrials and more comparable to technology/software moats
- 25%+ EPS growth rate with multi-year visibility from qualification barriers
- Sub-1x leverage provides balance sheet optionality

**Why it's still expensive:**
- ~42x EV/EBITDA vs. peer median of ~15-18x
- At these multiples, a single quarter of disappointment (Boeing production delay, customer pushback on pricing) would drive 15-20% downside
- Industrial companies historically de-rate when growth moderates — HWM is priced for perpetual expansion
- No dividend yield support (0.2%)

The valuation prevents a high score on this dimension. The stock is priced for continued excellence. However, analyst targets (consensus mean reportedly around $281, high ~$315) imply upside from current levels, and rising estimates suggest the current multiple may compress naturally through earnings growth rather than stock price decline.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet's competitive position is the strongest dimension of this investment case:

1. **Certification barriers:** Aerospace qualification processes take years. Once a supplier is certified on an engine program, switching costs are prohibitive. Howmet holds long-standing customer relationships and significant technology/process advantages that support leading positions on major programs.

2. **Pricing above inflation:** The company's 10-K explicitly states product prices are increasing faster than cost pass-through to customers. This has driven COGS as a percentage of sales from 72% to 66% in three years — operating leverage amplified by genuine pricing power.

3. **Vertical integration:** In its Engineered Structures segment, Howmet produces titanium ingots and mill products and is vertically integrated into forgings, extrusions, forming, and machining. However, the company does depend on limited- or sole-source suppliers for certain raw materials, including titanium sponge.

4. **Patent portfolio:** 1,020 active patents in advanced alloys, coatings, and manufacturing processes.

5. **Machine tool lead times >2 years:** Even with capital, new entrants face multi-year delays in acquiring production capacity. Combined with qualification timelines, new competition for Howmet's positions is unlikely within this decade.

Competitors include Precision Castparts (Berkshire), ATI, and Lisi Aerospace. Precision Castparts competes across multiple product categories including titanium alloys, precision forgings, investment castings, and aerospace fasteners, making it the most comparable multi-segment competitor. VSMPO (Russia) remains listed as a competitor in titanium and titanium-based alloys; geopolitical tensions and sanctions have created uncertainty around Western supply chains for Russian titanium, which may benefit Howmet's competitive positioning.

## Risk Assessment

**Boeing Dependency (Moderate-High):** Boeing production rates directly affect Howmet volumes. The 737 MAX recovery has been slower than expected, and any further delays compress near-term revenue. However, this is partially offset by aftermarket demand from the existing fleet and Airbus production growth.

**Valuation Risk (High):** At approximately 58x company-guided forward P/E, the stock has zero margin for error. A single quarter miss or guidance reduction could trigger a 15-20% correction.

**CEO Succession (Moderate):** John Plant (72) has driven the transformation. Recent executive departures (Chief Legal Officer resigned September 2025, new CFO appointed from Hexcel) suggest transition planning may be underway, but key-person risk is real.

**Tariff/Trade Uncertainty (Low-Moderate):** 72% North American production provides insulation, and reshoring trends may benefit the company. However, titanium sourcing and international operations (19 countries) create exposure to trade disruptions. The 10-K specifically notes uncertainty around "the timing, extent, application, and level of tariffs by various governments."

**Aerospace Cyclicality (Low near-term, Moderate medium-term):** Current airline demand, defense spending, and data center power generation provide multi-vector support. A synchronized global recession that reduced air travel would be the primary cyclical threat.

**CAM Integration (Low-Moderate):** $1.8B acquisition represents modest leverage increase. Integration risk exists but Howmet has a track record of margin improvement in acquisitions.

## Conclusion

Howmet Aerospace is a high-quality industrial compounder with genuine competitive advantages: certification barriers, pricing power above inflation, and leading market positions on multi-decade engine programs. The financial trajectory is exceptional: ~50% EPS CAGR from 2022-2025, 17% FCF margins, sub-1x leverage, and rising estimates across all periods. The emerging gas turbine/data center demand vector adds a structural growth driver that most industrials lack.

The primary constraint is valuation at approximately 58x company-guided forward earnings — expensive by any standard and leaving minimal room for execution shortfalls. However, with 20%+ EPS growth expected through FY2027, consistent earnings beats, and estimates still rising, the stock is more likely to grow into its multiple than to compress from current levels absent a specific negative catalyst. The combination of structural demand visibility and rising estimates supports maintaining a BUY at current levels with the understanding that position sizing should be moderate given the elevated multiple and narrower upside from the May 6 price level.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The stock's May 6 move to $256.43 materially compresses the base-case upside to ~9% while bear-case downside is ~20%, resulting in an unfavorable risk/reward ratio that weakens the BUY recommendation at current prices.
