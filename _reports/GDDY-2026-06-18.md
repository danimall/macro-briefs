**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GoDaddy has fallen roughly 57% from its 52-week high of $181.49 to about $76, a drawdown that has compressed the stock to ~10.8x consensus FY26 EPS ($7.12) and ~8.6x FY27 EPS ($8.98), with a trailing-twelve-month free cash flow yield of approximately 15%. The unusual feature of this decline is that it occurred while reported fundamentals improved. First-quarter 2026 revenue rose 6.1% to $1,266.9M, operating income rose 25.6% to $310.5M, operating margin expanded 380 basis points to 24.5%, normalized EBITDA rose 13.5% to $413.5M, and pre-tax income rose 28%. The company has beaten EPS estimates in four consecutive quarters and consensus estimates have been revised modestly higher over the past 90 days (current year +2.0%, next year +1.7%). The decline is being driven by a forward-looking concern that artificial intelligence will commoditize GoDaddy's core domain, hosting, and website-builder franchises, not by any deterioration visible in the trailing or current numbers.

That distinction matters for the recommendation. An outright avoid would require either realized erosion (declining revenue, falling estimates, guidance cuts) or a structural-impairment case grounded in the actual financials. Neither is present: revenue is growing, estimates are rising, FY26 guidance was reaffirmed, and the balance sheet is serviceable (net debt/FCF ~1.6x, covenant compliant, well-laddered maturities). The "distress" screening signals (Altman Z-score 0.96, D/E 37x, ROE 407%) are artifacts of $2.49B of current deferred revenue and years of buyback-driven equity erosion, not solvency risk. At the same time, an outright buy is hard to justify: customer count is flat at ~20.4M, growth is now entirely ARPU-driven, the moat in domains and easy website-building is genuinely exposed to AI substitution that management itself has flagged with expanded risk-factor language, and the overall quality/growth profile does not clear the bar for high conviction in this sector.

The result is a HOLD with a constructive lean. The 12-month appreciation thesis is concrete: ~$1.6B of free cash flow retiring 6-8% of the float annually at these prices, combined with mid-single-digit revenue growth and operating leverage, can drive low-double-digit EPS growth even on a flat multiple, supporting a path toward the high-$80s to low-$90s. The cap on that thesis is the unresolved question of whether AI accelerates GoDaddy's monetization (via Airo) or erodes its long-tail small-business base. Until customer and ARPU data resolve that debate, the cheapness is best characterized as a depressed but uncertain situation rather than a clear mispricing.

## Company Overview

GoDaddy is the world's largest domain registrar and an integrated digital-presence platform serving roughly 20.4 million customers (predominantly entrepreneurs and small/medium businesses) and managing approximately 81.4 million domains. The business reports in two segments:

- **Core Platform (~61% of revenue, $768.7M in Q1'26):** domain registrations and renewals, aftermarket domain sales, hosting, and security. This is the legacy cash engine, growing slowly (+2.8% YoY) at roughly a 33% segment EBITDA margin. It is sticky, high-renewal, recurring-revenue, and the most exposed to AI-driven substitution risk.
- **Applications & Commerce (A&C) (~39% of revenue, $498.2M in Q1'26):** proprietary website builders, commerce/payments (GoDaddy Payments), and third-party productivity resold via Microsoft 365. Growing +11.6% YoY at a ~45% segment EBITDA margin, the growth and margin engine.

The competitive assets are scale in domains, a recognizable consumer brand, a large guided-sales/care force ("GoDaddy Guides," ~9% of bookings), and a growing AI-native layer branded Airo (including Airo for WordPress, launched May 2026). Management describes the core domain and website-builder markets as "increasingly characterized by commoditization," which is the central tension in the stock.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Total Q1'26 revenue grew 6.1% (5.6% constant currency), with international ~33% of the mix. Margin expansion was driven by disciplined cost control (marketing -7.8%, G&A -6.3%, D&A -21.4%). Reported net income of $214.6M was down 2.2%, but this is a tax-comparison artifact: the prior-year quarter included a one-time $34.6M uncertain-tax-position benefit, while Q1'26 carried a normal $67.3M provision at a 23.9% rate. Pre-tax income rose to $281.9M from $220.0M. ARPU climbed 9% to $246 from $225 while customer count was essentially flat (+13k QoQ), confirming that the operating strategy is monetization of the existing base rather than customer acquisition.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** This is the core of the long thesis. Operating cash flow was $471.5M in the quarter (+16.5%) against just $4.6M of capex, an extremely capital-light model. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is approximately $1.58B at a ~32% margin. Capital is returned almost entirely through buybacks: $279.7M (~3.0M shares) repurchased in Q1'26, against a $3.0B authorization through end-2027 with $1.89B remaining. Share count has fallen from ~141.7M (Q1'25 basic) to ~133.6M. There is no dividend. One caveat: recent repurchases were executed at $88-105, above today's ~$76, so the most recent tranches are underwater. The continued ~$280M/quarter pace at the current depressed price is more accretive going forward.

**Balance sheet and the false distress signal.** Screening tools flag GoDaddy as distressed (Altman Z-score 0.96), but this is a false positive. The negative working capital (current ratio 0.61) is driven by $2.49B of current deferred revenue, prepaid subscriptions that are a funding source, not a cash liability. Near-zero book equity ($237M, against a $2.86B accumulated deficit) is the cumulative result of buybacks, which mechanically produces the headline D/E of 37x and ROE of 407%. The economic picture: $1.26B cash, $3.82B total debt, ~$1.6B FCF, net debt/FCF roughly 1.6x. Debt is well-laddered (2027/2029/2031), partially swapped to fixed (effective 3.7%-6.0%), with a fully available $1.0B revolver and covenant compliance. Leverage is moderate and serviceable. The Piotroski score of 4/5 confirms positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF above net income.

The trajectory of prior scoring captured this correctly: financial health was revised up from 6.0 to 7.0 over the last two reviews. The current read sustains a strong financial-health score, with the moderate leverage and thin book equity the only restraints on a higher mark.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is modest and monetization-driven. A&C (+11.6%) carries the mix against a slow Core (+2.8%). The customer count is flat year-over-year; the entirety of revenue growth is coming from the ARPU increase and rising attach rates of payments, commerce, and productivity products. This is durable if A&C and Airo attach rates keep rising, but it is structurally vulnerable: a monetization-only algorithm has a ceiling, and the long tail of small-business customers is precisely the cohort most exposed to cheaper or free AI-built alternatives.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +2.7% over 90 days (+2.5% over 30 days). These rising estimates, combined with four consecutive beats, support confidence in the consensus path and contradict the bear precondition that estimates would need to begin falling. They do not constitute the >30% revision velocity with >30% revenue growth that would mark a fundamental inflection; this is a steady, mid-single-digit revenue grower with operating leverage and buyback accretion, not an accelerating one.

The growth wildcard is Airo. Airo AI Builder is cited at a $10M+ annualized bookings run-rate, which is immaterial against ~$5.8B of revenue but directionally important as evidence GoDaddy can monetize an integrated, managed AI experience rather than be displaced by standalone tools. Airo for WordPress targets the gap between "easy-but-limiting" and "powerful-but-complex" site building. The CFO's framing of GoDaddy as "going through a transformation" (Evercore, June 2026) acknowledges that the AI pivot is a multi-year reshaping with uncertain payoff. Weighting growth heavily, as the evidence base warrants, this is a moderate score: the operating leverage and EPS algorithm are real, but top-line growth is unexciting and faces a genuine structural question.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$76, GoDaddy trades at roughly 10.8x FY26 consensus EPS and ~8.6x FY27 EPS, with a ~15% trailing FCF yield. On an absolute and growth-adjusted basis the stock is cheap. The IT peer set is heterogeneous (Guidewire at 63.8x, Cognex at 73.1x, Zebra at 27.0x, Gartner at 14.8x), but even against the cheaper, slower-growing names (Gartner), GoDaddy sits at a meaningful discount despite mid-single-digit growth, ~32% FCF margins, and an aggressive buyback. A 14-15x forward multiple, in line with mature recurring-revenue software/infrastructure, would imply a stock in the $120s-$130s.

The reason for caution on the valuation score is not the multiple itself but the absence of a hard catalyst to unlock it and the presence of a legitimate reason for the discount. Cheap multiples on names where the market is pricing a structural threat are frequently value traps, and rewarding cheapness alone has been a reliable way to be wrong. Here the market is discounting a credible AI-commoditization risk to the core franchise; that risk is real, management-acknowledged, and could plausibly manifest as customer erosion and falling estimates over a multi-year horizon. The stock is better described as inexpensive with an embedded option on AI outcomes than as a clean mispricing.

Two valuation supports keep the score above neutral. First, the FCF yield plus the remaining buyback authorization mechanically retires 6-8% of the float per year at current prices, providing a structural per-share tailwind and a soft price floor. Second, the depressed multiple already embeds substantial pessimism: the bear thesis is largely in the price after the decline, which limits incremental downside relative to a name trading at peak optimism. Sell-side targets (low $83, mean $114, median $100, high $190) sit entirely above the current price, with Morgan Stanley raising its Equal Weight target to $83 from $77 after Q1. Those targets are not aggressive growth extrapolation on a premium multiple; they reflect the same view: cheap, but capped by AI uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

GoDaddy competes across a fragmented stack: domain registrars, commoditizing hosting, website/e-commerce builders (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify-adjacent), payments (Stripe, Block, PayPal, also partners), and productivity (Microsoft, on which its email offering depends). The competitive moat (scale in domains, brand recognition, a sticky recurring-revenue base, and a guided-sales force) is genuine but its trajectory is the concern. Management's 10-K language was materially expanded around AI: competitors "may seek to disrupt the market by offering services at low or no cost," and a shift of customers to third-party AI platforms or closed ecosystems (natural-language site builders, ChatGPT-style discovery reducing the need for typed domains and search) could reduce demand for GoDaddy's website-building, hosting, and domain products or compress pricing.

The defensive posture is breadth plus an integrated, managed AI experience (Airo handles build, manage, and grow, not just creation) leveraged across a large installed base. The risk is that AI lowers switching costs and commoditizes the "easy website" value proposition that GoDaddy historically sold to the long tail. Flat customer count is the early data point worth watching: it has not yet declined, but the absence of net adds in a period of heavy AI marketing by competitors is consistent with intensifying competition for new small-business formation. The competitive position is stable today with a deteriorating long-run trajectory that the market is actively pricing, a level that warrants a below-neutral score given the weight of the AI threat to the core anchor.

## Risk Assessment

**AI substitution (the central debate).** The most material idiosyncratic risk is that AI commoditizes domains, hosting, and website-building. This is partly priced after the drawdown, but it is not fully resolved: if erosion shows up in customer counts or ARPU, estimates would fall and the multiple could compress further. This is a genuine two-sided binary playing out over years, not quarters.

**Customer-count stagnation.** Growth is ARPU-driven on a flat base. Management's own stated primary risk is the inability to increase sales to new and existing customers. A monetization-only algorithm has a finite runway.

**Partner concentration.** Email/productivity depends heavily on Microsoft 365; adverse repricing or loss would force costly customer migrations. Infrastructure depends on AWS amid a public-cloud migration that added $4.5M of T&D cloud cost YoY.

**Regulatory/ICANN.** ICANN accreditation, Verisign price increases (.com fee rising to $10.97 on 11/1/2026), EU NIS2/DSA, and a 20-year FTC security settlement (approved 5/21/2025) create compliance obligations. The ICANN "Next Round" of new gTLDs (applications opening ~4/30/2026) is a potential Core catalyst or cost.

**Balance-sheet optics.** Continued large buybacks keep book equity minimal and amplify ratio volatility, a screening artifact rather than a solvency concern, but one that produces persistent false distress signals.

**Capital-allocation timing.** Recent repurchases are underwater at $76, a mild critique if the stock stays depressed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.0% is modestly rich versus 38.8% trailing realized vol (a 4.2pp spread), and sits at only the 11th percentile of the current chain: the market is not pricing acute near-term event risk. The put/call skew is balanced (1.1%) and the put/call volume ratio is low (0.20), inconsistent with aggressive directional bearish positioning ahead of a catalyst. The elevated put/call open-interest ratio (2.79) reflects accumulated hedging/yield-overwrite positioning rather than a fresh bearish signal. The term structure is in normal contango (1m 43.0% vs 6m 49.6%), a complacent rather than stressed shape. The reported "ATM IV 803%" delta versus the prior snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded. The options market is not corroborating an imminent collapse, consistent with a slow secular debate rather than a dated catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

GoDaddy is a capital-light, ~$1.6B-FCF cash machine trading at ~8.6x next-year EPS and a ~15% FCF yield after a sentiment-driven decline. Operating metrics are improving, not deteriorating: margins expanded 380bps, pre-tax income grew 28%, ARPU rose 9%, and estimates are rising. The AI fear is overstated: Airo turns the threat into an upsell engine, A&C compounds at low-double-digits, and the buyback retires 6-8% of the float per year. If AI fears fade and the multiple normalizes toward 13-15x forward on ~$9 EPS, the stock re-rates toward $120-$135 (+60% to +75%).

### Bear Case

The AI-commoditization risk is real and the market is right to price it. Flat customer count signals that competitors are already winning the long tail; over time, customer attrition and ARPU pressure cause revenue growth to stall and estimates to roll over. The buyback masks a maturing, structurally threatened franchise, and repurchases at $88-105 destroy value if the stock keeps falling. The multiple stays compressed or contracts further as growth decelerates, and the stock drifts to $60-$65 (-15% to -20%). The bear precondition (estimates beginning to fall) is not yet met, which is why this is a multi-year grind rather than an imminent break.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month appreciation path rests on buyback-driven EPS accretion and continued ARPU monetization, capped by unresolved AI risk.

**Base case target: ~$90 (+18% from ~$76).** Roughly 10x FY27 EPS of ~$8.98 plus the per-share tailwind from ~6% annual share-count reduction; consistent with steady mid-single-digit revenue growth, operating leverage, and a flat-to-slightly-higher multiple.

**Bull case target: ~$125 (+64%).** ~14x forward on ~$9 EPS if AI fears recede and Airo monetization scales.

**Bear case target: ~$62 (-18%).** Multiple holds at ~7x as growth decelerates and customer erosion begins to appear in the data.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the FY26 earnings cadence (next print expected late July 2026) and full-year guidance updates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Customer count returns to net growth (>1% YoY) alongside sustained ARPU gains — evidence the AI threat is not eroding the base.
2. Airo bookings run-rate scales from ~$10M toward a material figure (e.g., >$100M annualized), demonstrating the AI accelerant case.
3. Forward EPS estimate revisions accelerate to >10% over 90 days, or the multiple compresses further (<8x forward) on continued operating beats, widening the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below ~3% YoY or A&C growth drops below mid-single digits — the first hard evidence of AI-driven erosion.
2. Consensus EPS estimates begin falling (negative 30-day and 90-day revisions) — the bear precondition currently absent.
3. FY26/FY27 guidance reduction, or sequential customer-count declines.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for the Q2 customer/ARPU and Airo updates; monitor the June 2026 8-K director/officer change (Item 5.02) and the ICANN new-gTLD round for Core-segment developments.

## Conclusion

GoDaddy is a depressed, cash-generative business where the share price reflects a forward-looking AI-disruption fear rather than any realized fundamental deterioration. Every reported operating metric improved last quarter, estimates are rising, the balance sheet is serviceable, and the FCF yield plus an aggressive buyback provide a structural per-share tailwind and a soft floor. That combination rules out an avoid: the prerequisite for a bearish terminal-risk call (declining revenue, falling estimates, or a guidance cut) is not present, and the screening-based "distress" signals are accounting artifacts.

It also stops short of a buy. Growth is modest and monetization-only on a flat customer base, the moat in domains and easy website-building is genuinely contested by AI in ways management itself has flagged, and there is no insider conviction buying to corroborate the optimism (the routine 10b5-1 sales at $80-90 are low-signal given improving fundamentals, but the absence of open-market purchases stands out). The valuation is cheap, but cheapness without a hard catalyst and against a legitimate structural overhang is best held, not chased. The risk/reward is favorably skewed (base +18%, bull +64%, bear -18%), and the buyback-driven EPS algorithm offers a credible appreciation path, which is why the lean is constructive. The defining variable is whether AI proves an accelerant or a commoditizer for GoDaddy's franchise; until the customer and ARPU data resolve that, HOLD is the disciplined call.
