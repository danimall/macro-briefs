**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Manhattan Associates is a market-leading supply-chain and omnichannel commerce software vendor mid-way through a cloud transformation that is de-risking its business even as headline revenue growth looks pedestrian. FY2025 total revenue grew only 4% to $1,081M, but that number is the arithmetic result of runoff license and maintenance revenue netting against a 21% increase in cloud subscriptions. The forward signal is remaining performance obligations (RPO), which reached ~$2.3B and grew 24% year-over-year, a contracted backlog that points to durable double-digit revenue growth as legacy drag rolls off and cloud becomes the dominant line. Cloud subscription growth accelerated to +24% in Q1 2026, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance during that print.

The business quality is exceptional. Zero debt, no credit facilities, a ~35% FCF margin ($374M FCF on $1,081M revenue in FY2025), 25.9% operating margins, and an asset-light model requiring only ~$15M of annual capex. Management is returning nearly all of it through buybacks and raised the repurchase authorization fivefold to $500M in March 2026. Against that, the stock trades at roughly 28x the $5.36 current-year consensus EPS and ~25x the $5.97 next-year estimate, a premium the market has been unwilling to sustain, with shares down 31% from the July 2025 high near $230 to $151.67.

The reasons for the derating are concrete rather than sentiment-driven: a services-revenue disappointment in early 2025 triggered a guidance reset, a ~24-point relative TSR underperformance, a CEO change, a January 2025 restructuring, and a securities class action whose alleged class period (July 2024–Feb 2025) maps directly onto that stress event. Add a CFO retirement in early 2026, a second restructuring in June 2026, and estimate revisions that are flat rather than rising, and the setup is a high-quality compounder priced for continued execution with a discrete legal overhang. We rate MANH a HOLD (growth_hold): the RPO-backed growth thesis is intact and worth owning, but there is no identified catalyst for near-term multiple expansion and the valuation leaves little margin for a bookings stumble.

## Company Overview

Manhattan Associates develops and services supply-chain execution, inventory optimization, and omnichannel commerce software for retailers, wholesalers, manufacturers, logistics providers, and government agencies. It is recognized by Gartner and ARC Advisory Group as a leader in supply chain execution and omnichannel software. The company's strategic core is Manhattan Active, a cloud-native, "versionless" SaaS suite spanning Warehouse Management, Transportation Management, Order Management/POS, and Inventory Optimization, now augmented with agentic AI ("Manhattan Active Agents").

FY2025 revenue of $1,081.4M broke down as Services 47% ($503M), Cloud subscriptions 38% ($408M), Maintenance 12% ($130M), License 1% ($15M), and Hardware 2% ($25M). Cloud now represents roughly 96% of software revenue, confirming the transition is nearly complete on the software side. Geographically, the Americas contribute ~75% of revenue, EMEA ~20%, and APAC the remainder. The company employs ~4,390 people, with a large R&D center in Bangalore.

The moat rests on domain expertise as a barrier to entry, a single extensible code base with zero-downtime updates, a deep installed base of premier brands, and non-cancelable cloud contracts of 5+ years that produce high forward visibility. The company is singularly focused on supply-chain commerce, a source of both depth and concentration risk.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is among the cleanest in application software. FY2025 operating cash flow rose 32% to $389.5M, and free cash flow of $374M represented a 34.6% FCF margin. The balance sheet carried $328.7M cash at year-end 2025 (drawn down to $226M by Q1 2026 after buybacks), zero debt, and no credit facilities. The Altman Z-Score of 3.32 sits comfortably in the safe zone. The reported debt-to-equity of 2.67 is a bookkeeping artifact of cumulative buybacks shrinking the equity base to ~$315M and a deferred-revenue-heavy current liability structure; it is not financial leverage. Return on equity of 69.9% is similarly inflated by the diminished equity base.

Profitability held up well. FY2025 operating margin expanded to 25.9% from 25.1%; net margin was 20.3%. Q1 2026 operating margin compressed modestly to 23.0% from 24.0%, a deliberate consequence of a 32% jump in sales-and-marketing spend under new CEO Eric Clark, a bet on accelerating bookings that pressures near-term margins in exchange for pipeline. The effective tax rate has stepped up to 23.1% for FY2025 and 28.8% in Q1 2026, from ~18% previously, driven by lower excess tax benefits on RSU vesting and executive compensation deduction limits. This is a modest EPS headwind that investors should model forward rather than treat as one-time.

The earnings-surprise record is consistent: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters, ranging from +5.7% to +13.9%. That track record supports confidence in current consensus, though the beats have narrowed from the mid-teens toward high single digits, consistent with a maturing base.

Capital allocation is almost entirely buyback-driven. FY2025 repurchases totaled $274.5M (1.45M shares); Q1 2026 added $150M. The board's March 2026 decision to raise the authorization from $100M to $500M is an aggressive signal of confidence, though at ~28x forward earnings the buybacks are being executed at a full multiple rather than an opportunistic one. There is no dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The central analytical task with MANH is separating reported revenue from underlying momentum. FY2025's 4% total growth understates the business because it nets 21% cloud subscription growth against declining license, maintenance, and (in FY2025) services revenue. The right leading indicator is RPO, a contracted backlog that will convert into revenue as the ratable cloud model recognizes it over 5+ year terms. Services returned to +4% growth after the FY2025 decline, addressing one of the prior year's soft spots.

Management raised full-year 2026 guidance during the Q1 print, to a revenue midpoint of ~$1.15B (roughly 0.5% above consensus) with higher operating margin and EPS. EMEA is a bright spot, up 13% in FY2025 revenue and 21% in operating income. The AI vector, embedding agentic features into the platform, is a genuine growth lever, though it carries newly expanded IP and regulatory risk disclosures.

The tempering factor for the Growth Prospects score is that estimate revisions are flat, not rising. Current-year consensus of $5.36 and next-year of $5.97 have moved less than 1% over both 30- and 90-day windows. The strongest forward buy signals in this system, estimate revisions exceeding +30% in 90 days, are absent here. MANH is a steady double-digit forward compounder on the strength of RPO, but the market is not being handed accelerating consensus to chase. Growth durability is high; growth velocity is not inflecting.

## Valuation Assessment

At $151.67, MANH trades at ~28.3x the $5.36 current-year EPS estimate and ~25.4x the $5.97 next-year estimate. Against the Application Software peer set, the peer median P/E is 40.8 (BSY 35.0, DOCU 28.9, GWRE 63.8, DT 74.8, TYL 40.8). On a trailing basis MANH sits below the peer median, and on forward EPS the discount is meaningful. That relative cheapness is real, but the peer set is heterogeneous, and several of those names carry richer multiples on faster or more inflecting growth profiles.

MANH is not a cyclical trough story where trailing P/E overstates expense; its earnings are stable and growing steadily, so forward multiples are the right lens and they show a stock at a full but not extreme price for a ~4%-reported / mid-teens-underlying grower. The 31% drawdown on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals is an improving risk/reward, not a broken thesis. At a >30% drawdown the burden is on the bull to identify what the market is missing, and the honest answer is that the market is pricing a legitimate combination of decelerated reported growth, management transition risk, and an unresolved securities case, not an obvious mispricing. Analyst targets (mean $185, median $190, high $240, low $145) imply ~22% upside to the mean, but with stable estimates rather than rising ones, those targets are not a floor that consensus is about to chase higher; they reflect the same RPO-conversion optimism already embedded in the current price.

We score valuation a shade below neutral. The absolute multiple is defensible for the quality and forward growth, but there is no independent catalyst to argue the market is wrong today, and cheapness relative to a heterogeneous peer median is not on its own a reason to expect re-rating. This is a fairly-valued quality compounder without an asymmetric setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Manhattan competes against ERP majors (Oracle, SAP, Infor), supply-chain execution specialists (Blue Yonder/Panasonic, E2Open, Körber, Relex, SAS, IBM Sterling), and POS vendors (Aptos, Oracle, Jumpmind, Salesforce). Its competitive position is stable-to-improving on the strength of a single extensible code base, zero-downtime cloud updates, and a premier installed base with long non-cancelable contracts. The 24% RPO growth is direct evidence the moat is converting into contracted revenue rather than eroding.

The trajectory is positive at the product layer: cloud is displacing legacy, EMEA is outperforming, and the AI features are a forward differentiator. The offsetting pressure is competitor consolidation (Panasonic/Blue Yonder, Körber/HighJump), which the company explicitly flags as intensifying competition, and structural disruption in retail, its largest vertical. The single-line-of-business focus is both the source of its depth and a concentration exposure. On balance the competitive position is a modest positive: share and moat trajectory favor Manhattan within its niche, but it is a leader in a maturing category rather than a challenger gaining ground in an expanding one.

## Risk Assessment

The defining company-specific risk is the securities litigation. Two putative securities class actions (Prime, Feb 2025; City of Orlando Police Pension, April 2025) were consolidated, alleging false or misleading statements over a July 24, 2024–Feb 7, 2025 class period tied to the guidance disappointment around Q4 2024 services weakness. A derivative suit (Ayers v. Capel) followed in September 2025. The motion to dismiss was argued April 9, 2026, with the outcome pending, a discrete binary that could resolve favorably (dismissal removes the overhang) or unfavorably (proceeds to discovery, extending the reputational and financial tail). The company carries D&O insurance but cannot estimate potential loss.

Secondary risks: (1) governance continuity — two C-suite seats (CEO in early 2025, CFO in early 2026) turned over within ~12 months, alongside two restructurings (January 2025 and a fresh June 2026 Item 2.05 action even amid raised guidance); (2) the ratable revenue model masks near-term inflection, so a bookings downturn would not surface in reported revenue for several quarters, reducing early-warning visibility; (3) single-line-of-business concentration in a retail-heavy vertical undergoing structural change; (4) expanded AI risk disclosures (IP protectability, EU/Colorado AI regulation, regulated-customer adoption reluctance); (5) ~$188–203M of non-cancelable cloud infrastructure commitments that could pressure margins on renewal; and (6) a newly added Israel–Iran geopolitical risk factor in the Q1 2026 10-Q.

Most of these are at least partially priced given the 31% drawdown, but the litigation is a genuine unpriced binary and the management transition reduces the credibility premium the stock previously enjoyed. We score risk factors modestly below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 61.1% sits well above the 40.7% one-year historical volatility (a 20.4-point spread) and the term structure is in backwardation (61.1% near-term versus 51.8% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the next earnings print and any litigation development. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.4% near-term), so the market is not aggressively hedging downside despite the elevated overall vol. IV has collapsed from 101.6% in mid-June to 61.1%, alongside a 14.8% price recovery, suggesting some resolution of the acute uncertainty that peaked earlier. For a prospective owner, rich IV argues against buying premium and favors waiting for the post-earnings vol crush before adding, or accumulating in the equity directly on weakness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200–230):** RPO conversion drives reported revenue growth to the low double digits as legacy drag rolls off and cloud dominates the mix. New CEO Clark's S&M investment yields accelerating bookings, the securities case is dismissed removing the overhang, and the larger buyback shrinks the share count into a rising earnings base. On $6.50–7.00 of forward EPS at a 30–33x multiple justified by a debt-free ~35% FCF-margin franchise with double-digit growth, the stock re-rates toward its prior highs and the analyst high target of $240 comes into view.

**Bear Case ($120–135):** Bookings decelerate, masked for several quarters by the ratable model, and reported growth stays stuck near mid-single digits, forcing the market to conclude the 28x forward multiple is unjustified for the delivered growth. The securities case survives the motion to dismiss and proceeds to costly discovery. Management transitions and the second restructuring signal execution friction rather than optimization. Multiple compresses toward 20x forward on $5.97 EPS, taking the stock back toward its April 2026 low of $119.

**Base Case ($165–180):** Cloud sustains 20%+ growth, RPO continues converting, and reported revenue climbs toward high-single-digit growth over the next year while margins stabilize after the S&M investment matures. The litigation resolves without material financial impact. The stock re-rates modestly toward the analyst mean of $185 as consensus estimates edge higher, delivering high-single to low-double-digit returns plus the buyback tailwind.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print (~late July, given the recent July 22–23 price action around the report), followed by the resolution of the motion to dismiss. Twelve months is the horizon for the RPO-conversion thesis to demonstrate itself in reported revenue acceleration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Cloud subscription growth sustained at 24%+ with RPO growth holding above 20% AND consensus EPS estimates turning positive (a shift from the current flat revisions to +5% or better over a 90-day window).
- A favorable resolution of the securities class action (motion to dismiss granted), removing the discrete overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$130s that compresses forward P/E toward 22x, restoring asymmetry to the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cloud subscription growth decelerating below 15% or a sequential decline in RPO — the earliest available signals of a bookings downturn given the ratable revenue lag.
- Consensus current-year EPS revised down more than 5% over a 90-day window, or a guidance cut.
- An adverse litigation development (motion to dismiss denied with a material damages exposure surfacing) or a third C-suite departure signaling deeper organizational instability.

## Conclusion

Manhattan Associates is a genuinely high-quality business: debt-free, ~35% FCF margins, a widening cloud moat, and a contracted backlog that de-risks the forward revenue model far more than the 4% reported growth suggests. The financial health is best-in-class and the competitive trajectory within supply-chain software is positive. Those attributes justify owning the stock.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the absence of an asymmetric setup. The forward multiple is full for the delivered growth, estimate revisions are flat rather than the +30% inflection that historically precedes re-rating, and the stock carries a live securities-litigation overhang plus two recent C-suite transitions that have eroded the credibility premium it once commanded. The 31% drawdown improves the risk/reward versus a year ago, but the market is pricing legitimate concerns, not an obvious mistake. This is a growth_hold: own it for the durable RPO-driven compounding and the larger buyback, reassess at the Q2 print and the litigation ruling, and look to add on a pullback toward the low-$130s or on evidence that bookings and consensus estimates are inflecting higher.
