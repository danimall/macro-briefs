## Executive Summary

Takeda is a ¥4.6tn-revenue Japanese biopharmaceutical company in the middle of two simultaneous transitions: the tail end of the VYVANSE loss of exclusivity and the amortization tail of the 2019 Shire acquisition, against the front end of three product launches that management has explicitly designated as the near-term equity story. Fiscal 2026 (year ending March 2027) is a guided down year. Management forecasts Core revenue declining low single digits at constant exchange rates, Core operating profit down 5–8%, and Core EPS down mid-teens percent. That guidance was reaffirmed unchanged on July 30, 2026, alongside a Q1 print that showed revenue up 10.2% in yen but down 0.5% at constant currency — the entire reported topline gain was a currency effect of roughly 10.7 percentage points.

Two items dominate the risk side. First, a Boston federal jury found Takeda liable in May 2026 for approximately $885m in an Amitiza pay-for-delay antitrust case; Takeda booked a ~$2.52bn provision in June and restated FY2025 from a reported ¥191.8bn net profit to a net loss of roughly ¥152bn. Takeda has said the FY2026 forecast is unaffected except for adjusted free cash flow, which locates the exposure squarely in cash. At ~¥152.5/USD the provision is ~¥384bn, or roughly 55% of one year's guided adjusted FCF. Second, Q1 adjusted FCF of ¥68.6bn was 9.8% of the ¥700bn full-year midpoint, versus 27.8% in the comparable quarter last year, and neither the CEO nor the CFO addressed the 40.8% decline in operating cash flow in prepared remarks. Working-capital build ahead of three launches and cash-tax timing are the likely explanations, and the reaffirmed annual range is evidence for that reading, but Q2 is a genuine test.

On the other side, the pipeline evidence is better than the financials. Zasocitinib beat Bristol Myers Squibb's deucravacitinib on the primary and all key secondary endpoints in a head-to-head Phase 3 in plaque psoriasis, with more than 35% of patients achieving complete skin clearance at week 16 — the strongest available basis for payer access and prescriber switching in oral immunology. Oveporexton has its first approval anywhere (China, as ORZEYFUL) with US and Japan under review. Rusfertide carries FDA Priority Review with a 2H 2026 US launch. The problem for a 12-month view is timing and ownership: zasocitinib launches in H1 2027 and contributes nothing to FY2026 while absorbing launch spend, and rusfertide's retained economics — the asset originated outside Takeda — are not established in the disclosure available. At 11.5x forecast Core EPS with a ~3.7% gross dividend yield, the stock is priced for a difficult year rather than for a structural decline, which is a fair place for it to sit given what is and is not knowable today.

## Company Overview

Takeda operates in approximately 80 countries across six self-defined areas: gastrointestinal and inflammation (anchored by ENTYVIO), rare diseases (including TAKHZYRO in hereditary angioedema), plasma-derived therapies, oncology, neuroscience (historically VYVANSE), and vaccines (including QDENGA). Fiscal year ends March 31. As a foreign private issuer it files a Form 20-F (most recent filed June 17, 2026) and Form 6-K furnishings rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, and its officers and directors are exempt from Section 16 reporting. There is therefore no Form 4 insider data for this issuer, and that absence carries no informational content.

Julie Kim became President and CEO in June 2026, completing an 18-month telegraphed transition ratified at the 150th annual general meeting; she succeeds Christophe Weber, who led the Shire acquisition and integration. Milano Furuta is CFO. Kim's stated framework is a "two-horizon growth strategy": Horizon One is the launch of oveporexton, rusfertide and zasocitinib plus preservation of the core in-line portfolio and delivery of transformation savings; Horizon Two is a subsequent cohort of blockbuster brands, the detail of which is reserved for a Capital Markets Day in Tokyo on December 11, 2026.

A material limitation applies to this report. The 20-F text, the audited balance sheet, product-level revenue disclosure, and the compensation and board sections were not obtainable in the underlying research. Every solvency statement below rests on a single issuer-stated non-IFRS ratio. Where a figure is derived, the inputs are shown so the arithmetic can be checked.

## Financial Analysis

### Reported versus Core

The defining feature of Takeda's financial profile is the gap between IFRS and Core results.

| FY2025 (yr to Mar-2026), ¥bn | Reported | Core |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 4,505.7 (−1.7% AER) | 4,505.7 (−2.6% CER) |
| Operating profit | 408.8 (+19.3%) | 1,172.5 (−0.9% CER) |
| OP margin | 9.1% | 26.0% |
| Net profit (as first announced) | 191.8 | 814.1 |
| EPS (¥) | 122 | 517 |
| Operating cash flow | 1,041.4 | — |
| Adjusted free cash flow | — | 684.5 (−11.0%) |

Core operating profit of ¥1,172.5bn less reported operating profit of ¥408.8bn is a **¥763.7bn wedge — 65% of Core OP is excluded from the reported line.** Takeda attributes the reported improvement to a step-down in VYVANSE amortization and lower restructuring expense. Amortization of acquired intangibles is non-cash and its exclusion is a defensible convention for a company that paid roughly $59bn for Shire. Restructuring cash costs are not. The composition of the wedge was not disclosed in the releases available, and it could not be verified against the 20-F; a scoring analyst should treat the size of the wedge as fact and its composition as unresolved.

### The restatement

On May 18, 2026 a Boston jury found Takeda liable for ~$885m over an anticompetitive patent settlement delaying generic lubiprostone (Amitiza), with damages allocated across direct purchasers ($474.9m), end payors ($63.2m), CVS ($191m) and Walgreens ($121m). US antitrust law permits trebling. On June 5 Takeda booked a ~$2.52bn provision and revised FY2025 to a net loss of approximately ¥152bn — a swing of roughly ¥344bn after tax against a gross provision of ~¥384bn at ¥152.5/USD, which reconciles broadly.

Two observations. The provision at 2.85x the verdict is close to a fully-trebled outcome (3.0x), but it may equally include prejudgment interest, plaintiff fees, and reserves for opt-out or state-law claims; no inference about management's view of the appeal should be drawn from provision size, since IFRS requires a best-estimate standard. More usefully, Takeda has framed the impact as confined to adjusted free cash flow rather than to earnings guidance. The provision is therefore a claim on the same cash that funds the ¥321bn dividend (¥204 × ~1,575m shares) and the stated deleveraging from 2.6x adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA.

A practical note for anyone screening this name: the widely syndicated May 13 release showing FY2025 net profit of ¥191.8bn and EPS up 78% has been superseded. The reported EPS field in the market-data feed (−$0.6096 per ADS, producing a −27.4x P/E shown identically as both trailing and forward) reconciles with neither the restated loss (~−¥97/ordinary share ≈ −$0.31/ADS at ¥155) nor the trailing four quarters of reported EPS from the surprise history. Reported EPS is not usable for valuation here; Core earnings and cash flow are.

### Q1 FY2026 (quarter to June 30, 2026)

| ¥bn | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,219.9 | 1,106.7 | +10.2% AER / **−0.5% CER** |
| Reported OP | 201.4 | 184.6 | +9.1% |
| Net profit | 113.2 | 124.2 | **−8.9%** |
| Core OP | 358.9 | 321.8 | +11.5% AER / −0.5% CER |
| Core OP margin | 29.4% | 29.1% | +0.3pp |
| Core EPS (¥) | 154 | 151 | +1.5% AER / **−11.8% CER** |
| Operating cash flow | 127.6 | 215.4 | **−40.8%** |
| Adjusted FCF | 68.6 | 190.1 | **−63.9%** |

Four derived points matter.

**The topline growth is currency.** +10.2% AER against −0.5% CER implies ~10.7pp of FX tailwind on Takeda's revenue basket. Underlying revenue was flat to slightly down.

**The decline sits below the operating line.** Core OP fell 0.5% CER while Core EPS fell 11.8% CER — an 11.3pp wedge. On the reported side, OP rose 9.1% while net profit fell 8.9% — 18pp. Full-year guidance replicates the shape (Core OP −5% to −8%, Core EPS mid-teens decline). The most plausible drivers are a higher effective tax rate on Core earnings and higher net finance expense, the latter potentially amplified by yen weakness inflating the yen cost of USD-denominated interest. A prior-year non-recurring tax benefit creating an unflattering base is an equally plausible explanation with very different persistence. The cause is not established, and the distinction matters: a financing-and-tax trough is more transient than an operating-margin problem.

**Margins and cash are heavily back-loaded, more so than last year.** Q1 Core OP margin of 29.4% against a full-year implied 25.0% (1,160.0 ÷ 4,640.0) requires a 4.4pp step-down, versus a 3.1pp step-down in FY2025. That is consistent with management's plan to fund three launches in H2 and with the CFO's comment that transformation savings are being "strategically reinvested," but it puts the risk of a miss in the second half. On cash, ¥68.6bn is 9.8% of the ¥700bn midpoint, requiring ¥581–681bn (85–91% of the annual guide) over nine months, against 27.8% delivered in the comparable quarter last year.

**Revenue guidance implies deceleration or a stronger yen.** Q1 annualized at 4x is ¥4,879.6bn, 5.2% above the ¥4,640.0bn forecast, and Q1 is not seasonally depressed (Q1 FY2025 was 24.6% of the full year). Presented as a sensitivity, not a forecast: the forecast implies +3.0% AER against a low-single-digit CER decline, i.e. roughly 4–6pp of assumed FX benefit versus 10.7pp realized in Q1. Either the yen is assumed to strengthen, or operational erosion is expected to accelerate, or the forecast is conservative.

### Leverage and dividend coverage

The only leverage datapoint available is issuer-stated and non-IFRS: adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA of 2.6x at March 31, 2026, published May 13 — before the June 5 provision. Litigation provisions are typically excluded from net-debt definitions, so including the ~¥384bn would add roughly 0.25 turns. Gross debt, the maturity ladder, covenants and ratings are unverified; the implied ¥3.6–3.9tn of adjusted net debt is an estimate from an unverified EBITDA denominator.

Dividend coverage, derived: ¥204 × 1,575m = ¥321bn, equal to 43–49% of guided adjusted FCF (¥650–750bn), 43% of forecast Core net profit (¥743bn), and **194% of forecast reported net profit (¥166bn).** The dividend is covered by cash and by Core earnings, not by IFRS earnings. For a business carrying a large non-cash amortization load that is defensible, but it makes the dividend's safety a function of cash conversion holding up — which is precisely what Q1 called into question and what the Amitiza cash timing could pressure. Note also that the ¥204 is a forecast, not a declared dividend, and represents an increase from FY2025's proposed ¥200.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 is a decline year by management's own guidance, and the composition matters more than the headline. VYVANSE loss of exclusivity remains the dominant revenue headwind; the issuer's language has progressed from "partially mitigated by Growth and Launch Products" (FY2025) to "largely offset by growth from core in-line brands" (Q1 FY2026), consistent with erosion maturing. Reported operating profit is being flattered by the step-down in VYVANSE amortization, which is non-cash.

Against that, three launches:

- **Oveporexton** (ORZEYFUL in China) — a first-in-class orexin receptor agonist addressing the orexin deficiency underlying narcolepsy type 1, first approval secured in China, NDAs under review in the US and Japan, Phase 3 data on daily functioning, cognition and nighttime sleep presented at SLEEP 2026. A mechanism-of-disease agent in a specialist-concentrated market is efficient to commercialize, but NT1 is a small addressable population; blockbuster status depends on pricing and label expansion, neither disclosed. Competing orexin-2 agonists are in development elsewhere; their stage was not verified.
- **Rusfertide** — a potential first-in-class hepcidin mimetic in polycythemia vera with FDA Priority Review and a 2H 2026 US launch. The asset originated outside Takeda. The retained economic split is not established in the available disclosure, and it should not be modelled as wholly-owned revenue and profit. This is the single largest unresolved variable in the Horizon One arithmetic.
- **Zasocitinib** — head-to-head Phase 3 superiority over deucravacitinib on the primary and all key secondary endpoints, >35% PASI 100 at week 16, once-daily oral with no fasting restrictions. Filing in 2026, launch H1 2027. Safety and tolerability data were not reviewed, and the 2027 oral psoriasis competitive set, including oral IL-23 agents in late-stage development elsewhere, was not verified.

The structure of this pipeline is that FY2026 absorbs the cost of all three and books revenue from at most one and a half of them. Growth, if it arrives, is an FY2027–2028 event contingent on rusfertide economics and zasocitinib's label. With no visible fourth pillar before the Horizon Two cohort, and the Horizon Two case reserved for December 11, the growth score reflects a guided decline with credible but unquantified optionality rather than an inflection.

The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, including a 28.4% beat in the June quarter and a −121.9% miss in September 2025) supports moderate confidence in near-term Core delivery but says little about the underlying trajectory, since the volatility is driven by non-core items.

|---|---|---|---|
| Implied ordinary price | ¥5,235 | ¥5,410 | ¥5,584 |
| Core P/E (FY26F Core EPS ¥472) | 11.1x | **11.5x** | 11.8x |
| Reported P/E (FY26F EPS ¥104) | 50.3x | 52.0x | 53.7x |
| Gross dividend yield (¥204) | 3.90% | 3.77% | 3.65% |
| Market cap | ¥8.24tn | ¥8.52tn | ¥8.79tn |

Market capitalization is ~$55bn across the range. Adjusted FCF yield: ¥700bn ÷ ¥8.52tn = **8.2%**.

Note a discrepancy worth flagging: the quoted ADR dividend yield is 2.83% against a derived gross yield of ~3.8%. The gap is most likely Japanese withholding tax (15.315% would take 3.77% to ~3.19%) plus FX timing on dividends already paid at a stronger yen. For a US holder the realized net yield is therefore probably in the low-3% range, which against a prevailing 10-year Treasury yield does not constitute a standalone income case.

**How to read 11.5x.** European large-cap pharma trades roughly 9–16x forward earnings, with the low end occupied by companies facing near-term patent cliffs. Takeda at 11.5x Core is inside that band rather than below it, and the Core measure excludes 65% of reported operating profit. On reported IFRS earnings the stock is at 52x. The most honest single metric is cash: an 8.2% adjusted FCF yield is genuinely attractive in absolute terms, but the claims on that cash are heavy — ¥321bn of dividend (43–49%), potentially ¥384bn of litigation payment (~55%), and a stated intention to delever from 2.6x. Those three cannot all be satisfied from one year of guided FCF.

Consensus targets (3 analysts, all Buy, sentiment stable) span $19.52–$23.02 with a $21.38 mean, implying 12–32% upside. Consensus targets are more reliably read as floors on inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; that is not the setup here, since management guides earnings down mid-teens at constant currency. The targets are treated as corroboration of a discounted-valuation view, not as independent evidence.

The stock has recovered 16% from the June 3 low of $15.03 to $17.45 and sits 7.7% below the 52-week high of $18.895. Prior appreciation is not a valuation input, but the recovery does mean the margin of safety available in June has largely closed while the two open questions (Q2 cash conversion, appellate outcome) have not been answered. A valuation score of 5.5 reflects a modest discount with identified catalysts, offset by declining earnings, an unverified balance sheet, and competing claims on the cash that generates the yield.

## Competitive Landscape

The defensible structural advantages visible in the disclosure are the plasma-derived therapies franchise, an entrenched Japanese domestic position, and a commercial footprint across ~80 countries that can be leveraged for the three launches. Plasma is a scale business where the binding constraints are donor-collection capacity and fractionation throughput rather than patent life, which makes PDT the most LOE-resistant part of the revenue base; the competitive set is CSL Behring, Grifols and Octapharma. The share of Takeda revenue PDT represents could not be quantified — product-level revenue was not disclosed in the material available, which is the largest single analytical hole here. Management's statement that core in-line brands "largely offset" mature-portfolio decline describes a revenue mix shift and establishes nothing about customer, payer or geographic concentration.

The trajectory read is mixed. On the negative side, the largest historical franchise (VYVANSE) is in erosion, and the Amitiza verdict — the first plaintiff jury win in class-action pay-for-delay litigation after three prior defense verdicts — raises the question of whether other Takeda patent settlements carry similar exposure. No such suits were found, but no systematic search was conducted; that is an absence of evidence.

On the positive side, zasocitinib's head-to-head superiority over the incumbent oral TYK2 is the strongest form of commercial differentiation available in a crowded immunology market and materially de-risks payer positioning. Winning a head-to-head against Sotyktu on skin clearance does not establish superiority on safety, tolerability or long-term durability, and the safety dataset was not reviewed. Oveporexton's first-in-class status in NT1 is a rare setup, with an unverified first-mover window.

Net: a stable-to-slightly-eroding in-line base with a genuinely differentiated replacement cohort whose economics are partly unknown. That supports a score just above the midpoint, and no more, given the trajectory of the largest disclosed franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Amitiza cash timing (largest discrete risk).** ~$885m verdict, ~$2.52bn provision, FY2025 restated to a loss, trebling exposure, appeal pending. The provisioned amount equals ~4.6% of market capitalization. Takeda's defense — that final patents ran to October 2027, so the 2014 settlement permitting January 2021 generic entry accelerated competition — is substantive and likely the core of the appeal. Whether a supersedeas or appeal bond is required, and its size, is unresolved. An appellate win, reduction or settlement could release part of the provision; a loss converts ~55% of one year's adjusted FCF into cash out.

**Cash conversion.** Q1 adjusted FCF at 9.8% of the annual midpoint against 27.8% last year, with a dividend consuming 43–49% of the guide. Management reaffirmed the range but did not address the shortfall in prepared remarks. Q2 adjusted FCF is the single most informative near-term datapoint for the dividend and deleveraging thesis.

**Currency, in both directions.** Reported results are inflated by yen weakness (10.7pp of Q1 revenue growth) while Core EPS declines double digits at CER. A yen reversal strips the reported-growth optics. For a USD holder of the ADR the effect is partly offsetting — a stronger yen raises the USD translation of a stable yen share price while compressing Takeda's reported yen earnings on USD-denominated revenue — so the ADR is less levered to the yen than the ordinary share is. The interest-and-currency-rate risk is one the issuer itself enumerates in its forward-looking-statements language, so this two-sidedness is worth holding in mind rather than treating as a one-way headwind.

**Launch concentration and the December reset.** Three assets in a 12–24 month window with no visible fourth pillar. A new CEO's first Capital Markets Day is a conventional venue for rebasing guidance or announcing restructuring; investors should not assume the current framework survives December 11 unchanged.

**US policy.** The announced generic-drug tariff schedule (0% for two years from August 1, then 100%, then 200%) appears to be an indirect exposure for a predominantly branded and biologic portfolio, though that is an inference from reported policy design rather than a company disclosure. Most-favored-nation pricing is the more relevant channel for a branded portfolio and remains unsized, with no Takeda-specific quantification available.

**Transformation delivery.** The issuer's own forward-looking-statement language explicitly flags the risk that efficiency and cost-saving efforts, including AI integration, may not deliver expected benefits. FY2026 guidance depends on those savings funding launch spend; if savings underdeliver, either margins or launch investment gives.

**Verification gaps that must not be scored as clean.** The 20-F risk factors, business and MD&A sections were not read, so risk factors added or modified versus the prior year cannot be identified. No balance sheet data was obtained — no total assets, equity, gross debt, cash, goodwill, maturity ladder, covenants or ratings. Product-level revenue, rusfertide partner economics, and the restated FY2025 line items are all unverified. Altman Z and Piotroski F could not be computed because Takeda reports under IFRS on Form 20-F; that is a data-availability artifact, not a credit signal.

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.2% against 20.6% one-year realized (rich by 11.6 points), with the term structure in backwardation — 32.2% at the August 21 tenor and 38.4% at September 18 (18.5% implied move), falling to 23.2% by January 2027. Positioning is strongly call-tilted (put/call volume 0.03, open interest 0.10) and short interest is negligible at 0.1% of float with 1.3 days to cover. The 6-month tenor shows a −4.4% skew reading; the sign convention is not defined in the data, so no directional inference is drawn from it.

Read literally, the chain prices elevated near-term event risk with no accumulated downside protection — consistent with positioning around US regulatory decisions on oveporexton and, on the September tenor, the Q2 print. The caveat is decisive: 27 contracts across four expirations is a very thin market, and the September 18.5% implied move for a $55bn diversified pharma is not credible as a genuine risk estimate. This is low-confidence data and it neither confirms nor challenges the fundamental view. The absence of short interest does confirm that no organized bearish position exists in the name.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $23.00 (+31.8%)

FY2026 marks a trough driven by items below the operating line (tax rate, finance expense) rather than operating deterioration, and those items normalize. Q2 confirms the Q1 cash-flow weakness as working capital and cash tax timing, with adjusted FCF landing inside the ¥650–750bn range. The Amitiza appeal succeeds or settles well below the provision, releasing cash and part of the reserve. Oveporexton wins US and Japanese approval; rusfertide launches into polycythemia vera with better-than-assumed retained economics; zasocitinib's H1 2027 launch converts head-to-head superiority into rapid payer access, and December 11 delivers a credible Horizon Two roadmap that reframes FY2027–2029 as a growth period. Core EPS troughs at ¥472 and recovers toward ¥540; the market pays 14x for a re-accelerating franchise, giving ¥7,560 per ordinary share ≈ $24.4/ADS at ¥155. Round to $23.00 to allow for FX and for the pipeline optionality not fully verified.

### Bear case — $13.50 (−22.6%)

The below-the-line wedge is structural — higher interest expense on USD debt in a higher-for-longer rate regime plus a permanently higher Core tax rate — and Core EPS declines again in FY2027 rather than recovering. VYVANSE erosion continues while rusfertide economics prove substantially shared, oveporexton's NT1 population proves too small to matter at the group level, and zasocitinib meets oral IL-23 competition on launch. Q2 cash flow disappoints, the Amitiza appeal fails and cash leaves in FY2027, deleveraging stalls at 2.6x, and the progressive dividend is reset. The multiple compresses to 9x on ¥440 Core EPS, giving ¥3,960 per ordinary share ≈ $12.8/ADS; the 52-week low is $12.99. Bear case $13.50.

### Base case — $19.50 (+11.7% price; ~+15% with dividend)

FY2026 lands within guidance, Q2 cash flow substantially recovers, the appeal defers the Amitiza cash outflow beyond FY2027, and FY2027 Core EPS recovers modestly to ~¥500 as one full year of oveporexton and rusfertide offsets residual VYVANSE erosion and as launch spend normalizes. The market pays 12x — a modest re-rating from 11.5x on visible launch execution — giving ¥6,000 per ordinary share ≈ $19.35/ADS at ¥155. Round to $19.50, marginally below the analyst low of $19.52.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives an expected price return of roughly +3.7%, or ~+7% including the net dividend. That is approximately a fair risk-adjusted return, with the distribution wide on both sides and the two decisive variables (Q2 cash conversion, appellate outcome) unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Horizon: 12 months, spanning the Q2 FY2026 print (late October/November 2026), the December 11 Capital Markets Day, and FY2026 full-year results in May 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Q2 adjusted FCF of ¥180bn or more**, putting the half-year at ~¥250bn and the annual guide back on a credible path (≥35% of the ¥700bn midpoint at the half). Combined with an explicit management explanation of the Q1 shortfall, this closes the largest open question and would support a move to ACCUMULATE.
2. **Disclosure at or before December 11 that quantifies rusfertide's retained economics and product-level Growth & Launch revenue**, allowing the Horizon One contribution to be modelled rather than assumed. Confirmation that Takeda retains majority US economics on rusfertide would be a material positive.
3. **A favorable appellate ruling, settlement below ~$1bn, or partial provision release on Amitiza**, removing ~¥384bn of claims on cash and restoring the deleveraging path.
4. **Price weakness toward $15.00–15.75** on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a Core P/E near 10x and a gross dividend yield above 4.2%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q2 adjusted FCF below ~¥120bn** (half-year under ¥190bn, i.e. under 27% of the midpoint), or any reduction to the ¥650–750bn range. That would convert the cash question from timing to deterioration and put the ¥204 dividend forecast at issue.
2. **A cut to FY2026 Core OP or Core EPS guidance**, or an H2 Core OP margin materially below the implied ~22–23% required to reach the 25.0% full-year figure, indicating that launch spend is not being funded by transformation savings.
3. **A guidance rebase or restructuring announcement at the December 11 Capital Markets Day** that pushes the Core EPS recovery beyond FY2028, or an FY2027 Core EPS outlook below ¥470.
4. **Evidence of additional pay-for-delay claims against other Takeda patent settlements**, or an appellate ruling affirming trebled damages with a near-term payment requirement.

**Next natural reassessment point:** Q2 FY2026 results, then December 11, 2026.

For existing holders: maintain exposure. The 8.2% adjusted FCF yield and 11.5x Core multiple provide reasonable support against a guided down year, and the pipeline evidence is better than the earnings trajectory. For prospective buyers: there is no urgent entry signal at $17.45, 7.7% below the 52-week high, with the Q2 cash-flow test and the appellate outcome both ahead. Position size should reflect that four material items — product-level revenue, the full balance sheet, the 20-F risk factors, and rusfertide's economics — remain unverified.

## Conclusion

Takeda presents a narrow, testable question: is FY2026 a financing-and-tax-driven earnings trough ahead of three launches, or the start of a structural decline in which the launches are too small and too shared to replace the mature portfolio? The evidence available does not resolve it. What is established is that operating performance is roughly flat at constant currency, that reported growth is a currency effect, that the earnings decline sits below the operating line for reasons nobody has explained, that Q1 cash generation was far weaker than the guidance path requires and went unaddressed by management, and that a ~$2.52bn litigation provision has an unresolved cash timeline against a dividend that already consumes nearly half of guided free cash flow. Also established is that zasocitinib beat the incumbent oral TYK2 head-to-head, that oveporexton is approved in China with US and Japanese decisions pending, that rusfertide carries Priority Review, and that a new CEO has a dated venue on December 11 to make the case for what follows.

At 11.5x forecast Core EPS and an 8.2% adjusted FCF yield, the price is neither demanding nor a bargain relative to a European pharma peer group where similar patent-cliff situations trade at comparable multiples.
