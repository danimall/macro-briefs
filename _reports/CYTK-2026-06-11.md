**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

In December 2025 the FDA approved MYQORZO (aficamten), an oral cardiac myosin inhibitor for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM); first US sales began the last week of January 2026; and the EU launch followed in Germany on June 1, 2026. On May 5, 2026 the company reported positive topline ACACIA-HCM data in non-obstructive HCM (nHCM): both dual primary endpoints met, p<0.001 secondaries, which opens an indication roughly the size of the oHCM market and where the incumbent rival, Bristol Myers Squibb's Camzyos, has not succeeded. The stock has roughly doubled over the trailing year and sits at $69.81, down from a 52-week high of $80.20 set the day of the ACACIA print.

The analyst community is constructive: 19 of 22 covering analysts rate the stock Buy/Strong Buy, with a median target of $104 against the current $69.81. This is not a clean long. The equity is structurally diluted by a Royalty Pharma financing stack that takes 4.5% of worldwide aficamten net sales up to $5B, layered on top of $891.6M of convertible notes, $300.6M of term loans, and $539.1M of revenue-participation liabilities accreting at a 26% imputed rate. Stockholders' equity is negative $826.6M and worsening each quarter. Operating cash burn ran $145.5M in Q1 2026 alone (~$580M annualized) against $1.07B of liquidity. A securities class action now names both CEO Robert Blum and CFO Sung Lee, and a freshly-issuing BMS method-of-treatment patent could become a litigation flashpoint.

The net read is a growth-oriented HOLD. The 2H 2026 catalyst calendar (MAPLE-HCM sNDA PDUFA November 14, commercial ramp datapoints, planned nHCM sNDA) and the consensus upside justify holding. The combination of structural economic dilution, refinancing/dilution overhang, single-product commercial risk against an entrenched incumbent, and live litigation keeps us short of a BUY at this price. We would look to add on either a pullback toward the low-$60s with the commercial ramp confirming, or on a clean MAPLE-HCM approval.

## Company Overview

Cytokinetics is a South San Francisco biopharmaceutical company founded in 1997, focused on muscle biology — small-molecule activators and inhibitors of the cardiac and skeletal sarcomere. With 673 employees and a $9.5B market capitalization, the company has just crossed from 28 years of clinical-stage operation into commercial-stage specialty cardiology.

The lead asset is MYQORZO (aficamten), an oral, allosteric, reversible cardiac myosin inhibitor that reduces hypercontractility and left-ventricular outflow tract obstruction in oHCM. It is approved in the US (December 2025), EU (February 2026), and China (December 2025, via Sanofi), and is sold in the US through a small number of specialty pharmacies under a REMS program requiring echocardiographic monitoring for systolic dysfunction. Management has stated an ambition to capture more than 50% US share among cardiac myosin inhibitors while growing the overall category. A supplemental NDA based on MAPLE-HCM (aficamten monotherapy versus metoprolol) has been accepted with a PDUFA date of November 14, 2026.

The broader pipeline includes aficamten in nHCM (ACACIA-HCM, positive topline May 2026), omecamtiv mecarbil — a cardiac myosin activator for HFrEF now in the confirmatory COMET-HF Phase 3 after a 2023 Complete Response Letter — ulacamten (CK-586) for HFpEF in Phase 2, and CK-089, a fast skeletal troponin activator for muscular dystrophy in Phase 1. Geographic economics are partially de-risked through out-licensing to Sanofi (China/Taiwan) and Bayer (Japan), while CYTK retains direct US and EU economics.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 income statement reflects the earliest stage of the commercial pivot. Total revenue was $19.4M (versus $1.6M a year earlier), composed of net product revenue of $4.8M (roughly two months of MYQORZO sales ramping through a specialty channel), collaboration revenue of $2.6M, and license/milestone revenue of $11.9M — the latter driven by a Bayer milestone triggered by the first US commercial sale. The $4.8M product figure is the only durable, recurring line, and it is too early to read a trend from it.

Net loss widened to $206.0M from $161.4M, with EPS of −$1.67, essentially in line with consensus. The cost structure tells the launch story: R&D fell modestly to $95.5M (down ~$2.7M as MAPLE-HCM and ACACIA-HCM wound down), while SG&A jumped 83% to $104.9M as the commercial infrastructure scaled. SG&A now exceeds R&D for the first time, the clearest financial marker of the commercial transition. Stock-based compensation runs a heavy $30.4M per quarter.

The balance sheet is the most uncomfortable part of the analysis. Cash and investments stood at $1.07B, down from $1.22B at year-end 2025. Management asserts at least 12 months of runway and reaffirmed 2026 GAAP R&D-plus-SG&A guidance of $830M–$870M. Total stockholders' equity is negative $826.6M, with an accumulated deficit of $3.69B. The negative equity is structural, a product of royalty- and debt-financed operations, and is not an imminent solvency signal given liquidity, but it is severe and deteriorating each quarter from both operating losses and non-cash royalty-liability accretion ($18.8M in Q1).

The financing stack totals roughly $1.87B: convertible notes of $891.6M net (4% notes due 2026, 3.5% due 2027, 1.75% due 2031), term loans of ~$300.6M, RPI fair-value liabilities of $138.8M, and revenue-participation (royalty-sale) liabilities accreting at a punishing 26% imputed rate. The September 2025 issuance of $750M of 1.75% 2031 convertibles, used partly to repurchase ~$399.5M of 2027 notes (with a $121.2M debt-conversion expense), pushed out the maturity wall while adding dilution potential. An undrawn Jefferies ATM equity facility (February 2025) remains a dilution lever held in reserve. Roughly 30.6M shares are issuable on convertible and equity instruments, and the 2026 4% notes are currently convertible and sit in current liabilities.

The defining feature of this equity is the Royalty Pharma structure. RP is entitled to 4.5% of worldwide MYQORZO/aficamten net sales up to $5B (1% above that), plus interests on omecamtiv mecarbil (up to ~7.5%) and ulacamten. The RP Multi-Tranche Loan carries a 190% repayment multiple, the royalty interests cannot be repurchased, and the agreements impose restrictive operating covenants. A large fraction of MYQORZO's eventual gross profit is therefore pre-committed. The practical implication: a "modest success" commercial outcome is financially mediocre for equity holders, because the royalty and debt drag consumes much of the upside. The bull case requires MYQORZO to be genuinely large.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs, and both improved this quarter. The first is the oHCM commercial launch. HCM epidemiology per the 10-K cites more than 300,000 diagnosed US patients and 400,000–800,000 undiagnosed, split roughly evenly between obstructive and non-obstructive forms, with nHCM diagnosis growing at low-double-digit annual rates. MYQORZO enters as the second cardiac myosin inhibitor behind Camzyos but with claimed clinical advantages: faster titration (as early as two weeks), echo flexibility, and a potentially cleaner functional profile, with MAPLE-HCM demonstrating superiority versus metoprolol.

The second, more strategic leg is ACACIA-HCM in nHCM, an indication where Camzyos has not succeeded. This roughly doubles the addressable HCM population and creates a genuine point of differentiation. The safety caveat warrants monitoring — LVEF below 50% occurred in 10% of aficamten patients versus 1% on placebo, with two serious heart-failure adverse events — and FDA/EMA approval in nHCM will hinge on interpretation of "minimally important" functional benefit. A positive Phase 3 readout in a doubling of the market is the most important fundamental development of the year.

Estimate revisions are constructive over the near term and bear directly on the growth read. Current-quarter consensus EPS improved to −$1.64 from −$1.68 thirty days ago (+2.1%), next-quarter to −$1.59 from −$1.65 (+3.6%), and current-year to −$6.43 from −$6.58 (+2.2%). Next-year estimates rose +4.4% over 30 days. The 90-day picture is more mixed — next-year estimates are down 21% over that window — reflecting the heavier launch spend and dilution baked in following the September financing. For a pre-profit commercial-launch biotech, losses widening alongside revenue ramp is the expected pattern; the relevant signal is the recent 30-day stabilization and upward drift, which suggests the launch and ACACIA news are nudging the loss trajectory in the right direction rather than deteriorating.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over four quarters, including a large −58.2% miss in Q3 2025 (the quarter with the $121M debt-conversion expense) but in-line prints the last two quarters. Visibility into the commercial ramp is still low, which argues for treating consensus product-revenue assumptions with caution.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are not meaningful here. Trailing P/E is undefined (the company is loss-making), forward P/E is negative (−14.7x on a loss), there is no dividend, and the peer set — KRYS at 40.3x, BMRN at 40.7x, HALO at 25.2x, RGEN at 134.4x, ARWR negative — is heterogeneous and not directly comparable to a single-product commercial-launch story with a unique capital structure. The peer median P/E of 40.7x is informational background, not a usable anchor.

The relevant valuation framework is a probability-weighted view of MYQORZO's peak sales potential against the encumbered economics, sanity-checked against analyst targets. The sell-side consensus is constructive and reasonably tight on the bullish side: median target $104, mean $104.7, range $69–$146. The current price sits essentially at the low end of the analyst range, which on a stock with positive near-term estimate revisions and a confirmed Phase 3 readout argues the downside is reasonably priced and the consensus skews to upside. Nineteen of 22 analysts rate Buy or Strong Buy, with the Buy count rising over the past three months (12 from 10), and zero Sells.

The valuation discipline that keeps this from a higher score is the Royalty Pharma drag. A growth-adjusted view that ignored the financing stack would point to a higher fair value; the reality is that 4.5% of every aficamten sales dollar (to $5B), plus the 190% loan repayment multiple and 26%-accreting participation liabilities, materially compress the equity's claim on the cash flows the top-line story generates. Combined with the annual burn and an undrawn ATM that signals future dilution, the discount to consensus is partly deserved. We assign a moderate valuation score: the consensus upside and recent revision support are real, but the structural economic leakage and dilution overhang prevent treating the gap to $104 as clean asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

The cardiac myosin inhibitor class was created by BMS/MyoKardia's Camzyos (mavacamten), the first-mover in oHCM. MYQORZO enters second but with a differentiated profile and a positive nHCM readout that the incumbent lacks. The platform — deep, specialized expertise in sarcomere and myosin mechanics — is a niche few competitors occupy, which supports a durable competitive position.

The trajectory is improving. The ACACIA-HCM result is share-expanding: it lets CYTK address a patient population BMS cannot currently reach, and the MAPLE-HCM monotherapy superiority versus metoprolol strengthens the within-oHCM positioning. Out-licensing to Sanofi (China/Taiwan, low-to-high-teens royalties plus up to $135M remaining milestones) and Bayer (Japan, high-teens-to-low-30s royalties plus up to ~€490M commercial milestones) de-risks geography while retaining the most valuable US and EU economics in-house.

The competitive position is bounded by execution uncertainty. CYTK concedes "limited" historical payor relationships, just $4.8M of product revenue is on the board, and the >50%-share ambition is unproven against an entrenched incumbent with a multi-year head start in physician familiarity and payor coverage. The freshly-issuing BMS method-of-treatment patent (US 12,616,697, expected issuance ~May 5, 2026), which claims treating HCM/HFpEF patients on beta-blocker therapy with a myosin inhibitor, reads on a common clinical-use scenario and could become a competitive-litigation flashpoint. EU composition and crystalline-form patents face opposition proceedings.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is concentrated and asymmetric, and the trailing beta of 0.43 materially understates it: a single-product, deeply-levered, negative-equity biotech with binary catalysts whose realized volatility (a 21% single-day move on ACACIA, a 2024 range of $46–$108) is the truer measure.

Specific risks not fully reflected in valuation:

1. **Commercial execution against Camzyos.** The entire equity thesis rests on a ramp that has barely begun, into a market with an entrenched incumbent and limited established payor relationships.

2. **Structurally encumbered economics.** The Royalty Pharma stack permanently caps a slice of every sales dollar and cannot be unwound (no repurchase right, 190% loan multiple, 26% accretion). This is the single most important structural risk.

3. **Dilution and refinancing.** Shares issuable on convertible/equity instruments; 2026 4% notes currently convertible and in current liabilities; undrawn ATM held in reserve. Negative equity heals only on a sharp sales inflection.

4. **Securities litigation.** *In re Cytokinetics Securities Litigation* (N.D. Cal., filed September 2025) alleges misleading statements on the aficamten NDA timeline, with CEO Blum named and CFO Lee added in March 2026. Because approval ultimately occurred and ACACIA read out positive, it may prove more reputational than financially material, but it remains an active governance watch item.

5. **BMS patent overhang.** US 12,616,697 could read on real-world MYQORZO use.

6. **Single-source manufacturing and tariff exposure.** No in-house manufacturing; all API and finished product rely on single-source CMOs, against the April 2026 Pharmaceutical Tariffs Proclamation (up to 100% on imported branded pharma).

7. **Customer concentration.** Three specialty pharmacies account for the entire product channel (45% / 31% / 24%).

8. **Pricing-policy macro.** IRA Medicare negotiation, inflation rebates, and a proposed Most-Favored-Nation policy weigh on long-term specialty-cardiology pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The options data point to resolved near-term uncertainty rather than mounting stress. Between April 27 and May 22, 2026, ATM implied volatility collapsed from 196.8% to 38.5% (−158.3pp) as the ACACIA-HCM binary resolved positively and the stock rose 23.7% to $76.86. The put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.44 to 0.21, indicating reduced hedging demand and a tilt toward calls post-readout. The 38.5% IV is now in a more normal range for a single-product biotech and does not signal cheap optionality or elevated fear. Short interest of 17.6% of float (4.4 days to cover) reflects meaningful bearish institutional positioning, consistent with skepticism on the encumbered economics and dilution, and creates moderate squeeze potential on a clean MAPLE-HCM approval, though the days-to-cover figure suggests the short side is not dangerously crowded.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
MYQORZO becomes a large oHCM franchise on its faster-titration and echo-flexibility advantages, the MAPLE-HCM sNDA is approved in November 2026, and the planned nHCM filing succeeds on the back of ACACIA-HCM. US plus EU revenue inflects sharply through 2026–2027, payor coverage builds, and the company crosses into operating leverage. In this scenario the top line is large enough to absorb the Royalty Pharma drag and still leave substantial equity value, and the negative balance sheet heals. The stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull target ~$130–$146.

### Bear Case
The commercial ramp disappoints against an entrenched Camzyos, payor coverage is slow, the >50%-share ambition proves optimistic, and product revenue stays well below the level needed to outrun the annual burn. The company taps the ATM and/or issues further convertibles, diluting holders; the BMS patent or securities litigation creates an overhang; and the nHCM safety signal complicates the label. A "modest success" outcome leaves equity holders with little after the royalty and debt stack. The stock drifts back toward the low end of the range. Bear target ~$48–$55.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias and a 12–18 month horizon framed around the 2H 2026 catalyst calendar.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- MYQORZO US product revenue confirms a steep ramp over the next two quarters (e.g., quarterly product revenue scaling through the high tens of millions with payor coverage broadening), demonstrating the >50%-share thesis is tracking.
- A clean MAPLE-HCM approval at the November 14, 2026 PDUFA, particularly if paired with favorable nHCM regulatory progress.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry to the $104 consensus median.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dilutive equity raise via the ATM at depressed prices, or further convertible issuance materially expanding the share base.
- A commercial-ramp stall (product revenue flat or decelerating over consecutive quarters) signaling Camzyos is holding share.
- Adverse developments in the BMS patent or securities litigation, or an FDA setback on the nHCM/MAPLE filings.
- Gross-margin or burn trajectory indicating runway compression below 12 months without a financing solution.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (first full quarter of US product revenue) and the November 14, 2026 MAPLE-HCM PDUFA date.

## Conclusion

Cytokinetics offers a genuine, improving growth story: first commercial approval after 28 years, a strategically important nHCM Phase 3 win, and constructive analyst support with median upside to $104. The positive near-term estimate revisions confirm the fundamental direction is up, and the recent ~13% pullback from the $80 high on unchanged fundamentals modestly improves the entry. We do not lower our view on the price action alone.

The reason this stops at HOLD rather than BUY is structural, not directional. The Royalty Pharma stack pre-commits a large fraction of every sales dollar, the $1.87B financing burden and negative equity heal only on a sharp inflection, the annual burn against liquidity keeps dilution risk live, and the securities litigation now names both the CEO and CFO. The equity is leveraged to a commercial ramp that has barely started, so the asymmetry to consensus is partly deserved discount rather than clean mispricing. We rate CYTK a HOLD with a growth bias, and would move to BUY on confirmed commercial traction, a clean MAPLE-HCM approval, or a pullback into the low-$60s.
