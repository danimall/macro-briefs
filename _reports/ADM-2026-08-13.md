## Executive Summary

ADM has delivered a genuine and large earnings inflection. Adjusted diluted EPS was $2.56 in H1 2026 against $1.63 in H1 2025; total segment operating profit rose to $2,214M from $1,577M; and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance on August 4 to $5.15–$5.60 from $4.15–$4.70, the second raise of the year. Consensus current-year EPS has moved to $5.49 from $4.69 ninety days ago, a +17.1% revision, and the company has beaten non-GAAP consensus in four consecutive quarters, most recently by 26.8%. The balance sheet improved alongside earnings: funded debt fell to $8,011M at 6/30/2026 from $9,228M a year earlier, commercial paper outstanding is $30M versus $715M at year-end, and net funded debt sits at roughly 1.65x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $4,203M against $11.1B of available liquidity.

The problem is attribution. ADM's own filings locate both the FY2025 collapse and the 2026 recovery in the same place: U.S. biofuel policy. FY2025 was hurt by deferral of Renewable Volume Obligations and the absence of a functioning replacement for the blender's credit ($316M of FY2024 benefit that did not repeat). H1 2026 was helped by EPA's final 2026–2027 RVOs in March 2026 and by proposed Section 45Z regulations in February 2026, the latter flowing through the tax line — the effective rate was 16.2% in Q2 2026 against 22.2% a year earlier, worth roughly $0.14 of the $1.84 quarter. Those conditions are industry-wide. Every U.S. crusher and ethanol producer is operating in the same margin environment, which means peer capacity response, rather than competitive displacement, is the natural governor. Adjusted ROIC — management's own selected performance measure — recovered only to 7.8% on a trailing-four-quarter basis from 6.3% in FY2025, against 13.6% in FY2022, and remains plausibly at or below cost of capital.

At $80.49 the stock trades at 15.0x the guidance midpoint of $5.375, 14.2x FY2027 consensus of $5.68, 1.66x book value of $48.62 per share, and roughly 10.9x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of $46.0B. That is not expensive for a franchise of this asset quality, but neither is it a margin of safety when the earnings base is a policy derivative at what may be a cycle high, when management's implied H2 guidance ($2.82 at the midpoint, roughly $1.41 per quarter) decelerates 23% from Q2's $1.84, and when the stock already trades above the $78.70 consensus mean and $78.00 median targets with two strong-sell ratings outstanding. We rate ADM HOLD.

## Company Overview

ADM originates, transports, stores, processes and merchandises agricultural commodities and sells food and feed ingredients, with consolidated subsidiaries in approximately 75 countries and sales into more than 180. Three reporting segments carry the business:

- **Ag Services & Oilseeds** — FY2025 revenue $61.6B (76.7% of total), segment operating profit $1,614M (49.8%). Comprises Ag Services (origination, elevators, freight, export terminals, $40.4B), Crushing ($10.4B), Refined Products & Other ($10.9B), and a 22.5% equity stake in Wilmar International.
- **Carbohydrate Solutions** — FY2025 revenue $10.7B (13.4%), operating profit $1,211M (37.4%). Starches & Sweeteners including wet-mill ethanol ($8.0B) and Vantage Corn Processors dry-mill ethanol ($2.8B).
- **Nutrition** — FY2025 revenue $7.5B (9.4%), operating profit $417M (12.9%). Human Nutrition (flavors, specialty proteins) and Animal Nutrition.

The revenue and profit mixes are close to inverted: Carbohydrate Solutions produces 13% of revenue and 37% of segment profit. Screening ADM on revenue mix materially misreads where the earnings sit. In H1 2026 the profit split was AS&O 51.5%, CS 34.6%, Nutrition 13.9%.

A related point matters for interpreting the four-year revenue decline from $101.6B (FY2022) to $80.3B (FY2025), down 21.0%. Processing volumes did not fall. FY2025 oilseeds processed were 36,324 thousand metric tons, up 605kt year over year; corn was essentially flat at 18,525kt. H1 2026 oilseeds rose 3.5% to 18,776kt and corn 0.9%. The top-line decline is commodity price pass-through. Revenue is close to uninformative for this business, and the meaningful metrics are volumes, spreads, and segment operating profit.

### Quality of the Q2 print

Two adjustments reduce the underlying step-up. First, Q2 included approximately +$100M of net positive mark-to-market and timing impacts, primarily in Crushing — roughly 28% of that sub-segment's print. After tax at the reported 16.2% rate and across 485M shares, that is about $0.17 of the $1.84. Second, the tax rate itself contributed roughly $0.14 versus the prior-year rate. Underlying Q2 adjusted EPS ex-marks was therefore closer to $1.67, and the policy-driven tax benefit is a further layer. Note the offset: H1 2026 as a whole carried net *negative* marks of approximately $200M across RPO and Crushing, so over a full year these largely wash. They should not be extrapolated quarter to quarter.

Corporate costs are absorbing part of the upside. Unallocated corporate function costs rose 27% in Q2 to $374M and 11% in H1 to $718M on higher performance-based compensation; stock compensation expense rose to $138M in H1 from $72M. A charitable reading is normalization after a year of missed targets rather than inflation.

### Cash flow — headline numbers mislead in both directions

FY2025 operating cash flow of $5,452M against $1,248M of capex produced $4,204M of free cash flow, a figure that exceeds FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $3,657M. That is a working-capital release — the 10-K attributes it to roughly $1.5B of inventory inflow and $1.1B from payables to brokerage customers. It is not a repeatable run rate, and part of the release reflects falling commodity prices, which shrink working-capital requirements and revenue simultaneously.

The mirror image applies to 2026. H1 operating cash flow fell to $1,299M from $3,956M, which reads as deterioration but is not. ADM's disclosed "cash flows from operations before working capital" was $1.8B in H1 2026 versus $1.2B in H1 2025 — reproducible as $1,299M less a $(460)M working-capital change, and $3,956M less $2,723M. Underlying cash generation improved roughly 43%. H1 capex was $466M against $1.3–1.5B full-year guidance (capex is H2-weighted), and dividends paid were $510M.

One genuine cash-conversion watch item: the $(549)M H1 outflow in other current assets is attributed in part to "unmonetized Section 45Z credits." ADM is recognizing 45Z benefits in earnings and in a lower tax rate before converting them to cash. Disclosed, not hidden, but reported EPS is currently running ahead of cash on the single largest driver of the upgrade cycle.

### Balance sheet

At 6/30/2026: short-term debt $407M plus long-term debt including current maturities $7,604M equals $8,011M funded debt; cash and equivalents $1,060M plus $33M of short-term marketable securities gives net funded debt of $6,918M. Against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $4,203M, net leverage is 1.65x. Funded debt to equity is 0.34x ($8,011M / $23,580M); total liabilities to equity 1.26x; current ratio 1.39x. Lines of credit total $12.6B with $10.0B unused and total available liquidity $11.1B. Commercial paper outstanding is $30M.

A standardized screen returning debt/equity of 2.30x for ADM is not reproducible from the balance sheet on either a funded-debt or total-liabilities basis; the same applies to a grey-zone Altman Z reading built on an EBIT/assets input of 0.003 when FY2025 pretax earnings of $1,255M plus $612M of interest imply roughly 0.036. Recomputed, Z lands near 3.2. The broader point is that Z-Score terms are distorted here by roughly $8.5B of segregated futures-commission-merchant customer funds offset by $9.2B of payables to brokerage customers, and by $6.6B of fair-valued, readily marketable commodity inventories. Liquidity, net leverage, and commercial-paper access are the informative measures, and all three are sound.

Two constraints qualify the strength. First, all three rating agencies rated ADM investment grade **with a negative outlook** as of 12/31/2025, and a dedicated risk factor warns of potential loss of tier-one commercial paper access; we have not verified whether outlooks have been revised after the two 2026 guidance raises. Second, the receivables-securitization program: $2.2B of transferred receivables were derecognized at 6/30/2026 with only $722M of unused capacity, and the Second Program (up to $1.3B / €1.1B) terminates 11/18/2026 unless extended. Non-renewal in a tight credit environment would be a working-capital financing event, so renewal terms are worth watching even for an investment-grade name.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth number is large and the driver is identifiable. FY2026 guidance midpoint of $5.375 is 57% above FY2025's $3.43. Consensus revisions confirm the direction: current-quarter estimates +18.6% in 30 days and +22.2% in 90 days; current-year +19.4% and +17.1%; next-year +5.8% and +9.5%. This is real positive earnings momentum, though it falls short of the >30% ninety-day threshold that historically marks the strongest inflection setups, and — critically — it is a recovery from a trough rather than a structural acceleration.

The forward shape is the constraint. Consensus FY2027 of $5.68 is only 3.5% above the FY2026 consensus of $5.49. The Street models the recovery as a plateau, not a ramp. Management's own guidance implies the same: H1 delivered $2.56, so the midpoint implies H2 adjusted EPS of $2.815, roughly $1.41 per quarter against Q2's $1.84 — a 23% sequential per-quarter step down. An investor annualizing Q2 at $7.36 would sit 37% above management's own midpoint.

Underneath the cycle, structural growth is low-single-digit. Volumes grew 0.9–3.5% in H1. The one line genuinely improving on self-help is Nutrition: operating profit rose from $386M (FY2024) to $417M (FY2025) to $307M in H1 2026 alone versus $210M, with Human Nutrition growth attributed to Flavors (energy and ready-to-drink beverages), Dietary Supplements, and progress at the Decatur East plant, and Animal Nutrition profit up 50% in Q2 to $33M. Nutrition operating margin improved from 5.6% (FY2025) to 8.3% (H1 2026). A reported $80–100M operating profit ambition in natural colors tied to synthetic-dye reformulation is unconfirmed in filings and should not be modeled.

Working against Nutrition growth optics: revenue declined year over year partly because of the Akralos JV deconsolidation, not organic weakness. ADM's portfolio strategy is capital-light JV consolidation — Plainsman (40%), Two Rivers Premium Oils (60%), Akralos (49%) — which is efficient for the rating but reduces reported revenue and shifts earnings into the less transparent equity-method line. A >$500M cumulative cost program announced 2/4/2025 runs three to five years.

Structurally challenged: Refined Products & Other, the only declining sub-segment in H1 2026 ($237M vs. $291M, –19%), plus persistent softness in North American liquid sweetener volumes and margins and EMEA Starches & Sweeteners.

Cheapness on forward P/E deserves skepticism here for a specific reason rather than a generic one. The multiple is compressed because the market discounts policy-derived earnings — the RVO and 45Z framework that produced the upgrade is regulatory rather than contractual, and the 45Z regulations were only *proposed* as of the Q2 filing while the RVOs are final. A 15x multiple on earnings whose durability the company itself attributes to regulation is not the same asset as 15x on contracted earnings. Sell-side positioning reflects this: 1 buy, 8 holds, 0 sells, 2 strong sells, with mean target $78.70 and median $78.00 — both *below* spot. The general principle that consensus targets lag rising estimates on cheap, high-momentum names applies with less force when the distribution contains two strong sells and the price has already crossed the mean.

## Competitive Landscape

ADM's advantage is physical: owned elevators, rail and river assets, export terminals and processing plants that capture origination-to-destination spreads. The FY2025 risk factors are unusually explicit that this is a volatility-levered rather than volume-levered franchise — if crop production outpaces demand for more than one or two crop cycles, "volatility diminishes," reducing both dislocation earnings and basis/spread opportunities. That is the inverse of how most staples businesses describe commodity risk, and it means a benign, well-supplied, low-volatility global grain market is bad for ADM. The same section names competitors including state-owned enterprises "with different return objectives."

On trajectory, the evidence splits three ways:

- **Carried by the environment:** Crushing (+$330M in Q2 year over year), Vantage Corn Processors (+$52M), Starches & Sweeteners ethanol. The economics ADM describes — final RVOs, elevated global energy prices, lower North American corn prices giving ethanol an advantage over competing blendstocks, favorable industry exports — are available to every U.S. processor.
- **Plausibly execution:** Ag Services +159% in Q2 on asset-network utilization, Barcarena back to full operations, higher freight rates on strong corn demand.
- **Genuinely self-help:** Nutrition, where margin improved from 5.6% to 8.3% and Animal Nutrition profit rose 50%.

What is absent is evidence of share gain. Volumes are flat to modestly up while global crush capacity has been expanding, so flat ADM volumes in a growing market would still constitute share erosion; no market-share data appears in the filings. Nutrition remains the unresolved capital-allocation question: $4.65B of goodwill and $1.85B of other intangibles at 6/30/2026, much of it from Nutrition-building acquisitions, against a 13.9% share of segment operating profit. The FY2025 write-downs of $372M in alternative-protein and startup investments suggest the most speculative parts of that build-out have been cleaned out.

Trade policy has whipsawed in ADM's favor. The Supreme Court's 2/20/2026 holding that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs at issue invalidated them; CBP began refunds in Q2 2026, and ADM does not expect material tariff impact. Resumed U.S. soybean sales to China reversed the specific dynamic ADM blamed for FY2025 crush weakness. Both are policy reversals rather than competitive achievements, and both can reverse again — USMCA review is flagged separately.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The earnings base is substantially a U.S. biofuel-policy derivative.** This is the dominant risk and it is specific, not generic. Note the asymmetry in the current setup: the 2026–2027 RVOs are final, but the Section 45Z regulations are only proposed, and 45Z is what compresses the effective tax rate to 16.2%. Risk factors flag 45Z, RFS mechanics, and small refinery exemptions specifically, and note that ADM's ability to generate significant 45Z credits from carbon capture is itself uncertain.

**2. Unquantified securities litigation.** A putative securities fraud class action filed 1/24/2024 in the Northern District of Illinois survived — the court denied motions to dismiss on 3/12/2025. ADM discloses no accrual and states it cannot estimate a range. A denied MTD is a materially worse posture than a pending one: it moves the case into discovery and raises settlement expectations. Derivative suits are consolidated in Delaware federal court and Chancery, with a separate unconsolidated Litigation Demand Action from 3/28/2025.

**3. Ethanol price-manipulation class actions with a stated damages range of "approximately $500 million to over $2.0 billion."** The upper bound is ~5.2% of market capitalization. The trend of rulings favors ADM — Green Plains dismissed with prejudice 3/12/2026, the Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of the UWGP amended complaint, the parallel Wisconsin case dismissed with prejudice 4/28/2026 (appealed) — but AOT Holding and Maize Capital remain in pretrial.

**4. Wilmar carried above market.** The 22.5% stake was carried at $4.1B against $3.9B of market value at 6/30/2026, narrowed from $4.0B versus $3.4B at year-end. ADM has not deemed the shortfall other-than-temporary. Precedent exists: a $461M Wilmar impairment in FY2024. Wilmar equity earnings fell 22% in Q2 and 15% in H1.

**5. Animal Nutrition goodwill headroom of approximately 15%** on $958M of allocated goodwill, with disclosed sensitivities of roughly $181M of impairment from a 100bp rise in the capex-to-revenue assumption. Improving results reduce near-term risk but this is the most fragile line in the $4.65B goodwill balance.

**6. Financing mechanics.** The receivables-securitization Second Program terminates 11/18/2026 unless extended, with only $722M of unused capacity across both programs against $2.2B derecognized. Negative rating outlooks from all three agencies as of 12/31/2025.

**7. Control environment.** ADM took a $179M impairment of capitalized internal-use software in FY2025 on pivoting away from global ERP implementations, and risk factors state technology difficulties "in the past have caused and in the future may cause" disruptions that may affect internal control over financial reporting. Against the 11/5/2024 Item 4.02 non-reliance filing and the SEC's January 2026 action against former CFO Vikram Luthar, an admitted ERP retrenchment is a governance consideration rather than a write-off. Offsetting: the FY2025 10-K no longer carries a standalone material-weakness risk factor, and disclosure controls were concluded effective at 6/30/2026 with no material ICFR changes in Q2.

**8. Governance.** Juan Luciano serves as both Chair and CEO. CEO grant-date compensation was flat at $21.6–24.8M in every year from 2021 through 2025, including a year in which net income fell 40% and adjusted ROIC fell 200bp; realized "compensation actually paid" did track the stock down hard, at $(0.27)M in 2023 and $(13.22)M in 2024, so alignment functioned in the drawdown even as target-setting did not adjust. Five-year cumulative TSR indexed from 12/31/2020 was 131.07 versus a peer group at 214.08 — an 83-point shortfall that widened in each of 2024 and 2025. Four Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed in 2026; only the Jeff Rowe appointment (ex-Syngenta CEO, EVP & COO in a newly created role effective 8/17/2026, reporting to Luciano) is confirmed in content. Whether that is succession preparation is inference, not disclosure.

**9. Insider activity.** No open-market purchases and no discretionary open-market sales in the most recent 25 Form 4s; activity is routine director unit grants plus one CFO tax-withholding disposition (6,720 shares at $79.27, 218,253 held). With the stock at $80 after a guidance raise, no insider chose to buy. Neutral to mildly unhelpful on a one-quarter sample.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $100, +24%)

Final RVOs for 2026 and 2027 give visibility on blending economics through next year, which is unusual for this business — the policy anchor is dated rather than open-ended. If Treasury finalizes 45Z at or above the proposed terms, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act delivers the higher future 45Z benefits ADM references, the tax-rate benefit persists and the unmonetized credits convert to cash, resolving the current EPS-ahead-of-cash gap. Crush and ethanol margins hold as U.S. ethanol retains its advantage over competing blendstocks at elevated global energy prices — a condition the current oil regime supports. Nutrition continues its self-help trajectory from 8.3% H1 margin toward double digits, adding a non-cyclical earnings layer against the $4.65B intangible base. With net leverage at 1.65x and $11.1B of liquidity, rating agencies revise outlooks to stable and ADM restarts buybacks against a 114.8M-share authorization — at 15x forward, retiring even a third of that authorization over two years compounds EPS meaningfully. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $6.25 at 16x supports $100.

### Bear Case (→ $59, –27%)

The margin environment ADM is enjoying is industry-wide, so industry capacity responds. Crush spreads normalize, and ADM's own risk factor mechanism engages: sustained abundant supply compresses volatility, cutting both dislocation earnings and basis opportunities in the 77%-of-revenue AS&O segment. 45Z is finalized less favorably than proposed, or credit monetization drags, pushing the effective tax rate back toward 22% and reversing roughly $0.14 per quarter. Management's own guidance already implies $1.41 quarterly in H2 versus $1.84 in Q2, so the deceleration is not hypothetical. Adjusted EPS reverts to $4.00–$4.25, and at 13–14x — the multiple ADM commanded through 2024–2025 — that is $53–$59. Layered risks compound the tail: an unaccrued securities settlement, the $500M–$2.0B ethanol range, a Wilmar impairment following the $461M FY2024 precedent, or an Animal Nutrition goodwill charge against 15% headroom. The 7.8% adjusted ROIC says the recovery has restored less than half of the lost economic return, and five-year relative TSR 83 points behind peers says the market has been right about this business for a while.

### Where we land

Both cases are credible and the probability-weighted expected return is close to fair. The score trajectory (4.8 → 4.0 → 5.0 → 4.5 → 5.1 → 4.7) has been noisy, and the April AVOID at 4.0 was too bearish — the stock traded from roughly $68 that month to $80 today on genuinely improving fundamentals. Our overall score rises to 5.16, driven by Financial Health moving to 6.0. That is an explicit upward revision from prior work, justified by verified facts rather than price action: funded debt down $1,217M year over year, commercial paper at $30M, net leverage 1.65x, $11.1B liquidity, and the reconciliation showing the third-party 2.30x debt/equity figure and grey-zone Z-Score are not reproducible from the filings. Risk Factors stays low at 4.2 because the litigation, policy, impairment and securitization-renewal stack is dense and partly unaccrued. ## Conclusion

ADM's 2026 recovery is real, verifiable in filings, and larger than the market expected — four consecutive beats, two guidance raises, adjusted EPS guidance of $5.15–$5.60 versus $3.43 delivered in FY2025, and a balance sheet that has deleveraged to 1.65x net funded debt with $11.1B of liquidity. Prior work in this coverage understated the financial-health improvement, and that is corrected here.

What the recovery does not establish is a re-rating case. The margin environment is industry-wide and policy-created, with the RVO leg final but the 45Z leg still in proposed form and its credits not yet converted to cash. Management's own implied H2 guidance decelerates 23% per quarter from Q2, and roughly $0.17 of Q2's $1.84 came from unrealized marks that reverse on settlement. Adjusted ROIC of 7.8% remains plausibly at or below cost of capital, having recovered less than half the ground lost since 2022, and five-year relative TSR trails the company's own selected peer group by 83 percentage points. The buyback — historically the mechanism that took diluted shares down 14% between FY2022 and FY2025 — is switched off, with 23.7% of shares outstanding authorized and unused.

At $80.49 the stock trades at 15.0x the guidance midpoint, 14.2x FY2027 consensus, 1.66x book and 10.9x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, above the $78.70 consensus mean target with two strong-sell ratings outstanding. Base case $83, bull $100, bear $59; probability-weighted total return of roughly 3–6% including the dividend. That is fair value with genuinely two-sided outcomes, and the honest classification is uncertainty about the durability of a policy-derived earnings base rather than confidence in either direction. **HOLD.** We would become constructive on evidence that 45Z credits are converting to cash and that the buyback has restarted, or on a price near $68–70.
