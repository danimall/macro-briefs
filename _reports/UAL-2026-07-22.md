**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Airlines is running the strongest commercial performance in its history while its bottom line is being squeezed by a fuel shock it chose not to hedge. Q2 2026 revenue rose 16.0% YoY to $17.67B on a 12.1% yield increase and 12.5% PRASM gain, validating the premium/international/loyalty strategy that has separated United from the domestic-heavy discounters. Yet operating income fell 17.3% to $1.10B and net income fell 17.3% to $805M, entirely because aircraft fuel expense surged 84.1% to $5.11B as jet fuel ran $4.19/gal against $2.34 a year earlier. The demand side beat, the input-cost side overwhelmed it, and United does not hedge fuel, making the stock a high-beta play on both jet fuel and travel demand.

The setup is two-sided and tied to the Middle East. A verified reopening of the Strait of Hormuz pulls crude lower and directly reverses the component that drove the fuel spike, restoring United's earnings power toward the January guide of $12-14 adjusted EPS. A grinding conflict sustains the compression that already forced management to walk down full-year guidance to roughly $9-11 and issue Q3 numbers below consensus. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 10.4% over 90 days to $10.38, a positive momentum signal, but that revision reflects the strong demand read partly offsetting fuel rather than a clean recovery. The revision is real, but the underlying earnings remain volatile in a way that caps conviction.

We rate UAL a HOLD (growth_hold). The balance sheet has deleveraged materially ($28.3B to $20.6B long-term debt since 2022), liquidity is robust at $16.6B, and three agencies upgraded UAL deeper toward the investment-grade line in 2025. At ~11.3x current-year EPS the stock is not expensive against a $162 consensus target. But the earnings are hostage to unhedged fuel, reported results are flattered by ~$796M of non-recurring sale-leaseback gains, and the fuel binary has to resolve favorably before the risk/reward turns asymmetric. Own this into a verified de-escalation, not before it.

## Company Overview

United Airlines Holdings is the parent of United Airlines, the largest airline in the world by available seat miles, serving roughly 181 million passengers across 380+ destinations on six continents. The network is anchored in the most slot- and gateway-constrained U.S. airports: Chicago, Denver, Houston, Los Angeles, Newark, San Francisco and Washington Dulles. That hub geography is both a moat (scarce slots, premium international gateways) and an operational liability (congestion-driven delays, as the 2025 Newark meltdown demonstrated).

The strategy ("United Next") rests on four pillars. Fleet transformation involves firm orders for 600+ aircraft with deliveries through 2034, including 146 787s, 167 737 MAX 10s (not yet certified), 111 A321neos, 49 A321XLRs and 45 A350s. Premium segmentation de-commoditizes travel through Polaris, signature interiors (530+ aircraft retrofitted), and a Starlink Wi-Fi rollout. Loyalty and ancillary monetization runs through MileagePlus and the JPMorgan Chase co-brand card, plus the Kinective Media advertising network. "Other operating revenue," largely high-margin Chase mileage sales, is a structurally growing profit engine that most industry screens under-weight.

The economic logic is a network-premium-loyalty flywheel: scarce hub slots and a global network support premium pricing, which feeds a valuable loyalty franchise monetized through the Chase relationship at margins far higher than flying itself.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has recovered cleanly post-COVID: $44.96B (2022) → $53.72B (2023) → $57.06B (2024) → $59.07B (2025). FY2025 net income was $3.35B, a 5.7% net margin, up from $3.15B in 2024. But FY2025 operating income of $4.71B (8.0% margin) was down from $5.10B in 2024 as costs (salaries +5.8%, landing fees +12%, maintenance +7.5%) outpaced 3.5% revenue growth. Operating cash flow was $8.43B against $5.87B capex, generating roughly $2.56B of free cash flow. ROE ran ~21.9%.

Q2 2026 revenue growth was led by Atlantic (+10.6%) and Pacific (+10.9%), with Pacific revenue growing fastest at +18.7%, validating the long-haul international premium recovery. First-half net income of $1.50B (+10.5% YoY) was helped by $534M of net operating special credits, dominated by ~$796M of aircraft sale-leaseback gains partly offset by $184M of labor ratification bonuses. Stripped of these non-recurring items, underlying operating performance is weaker than the headline suggests.

The balance sheet reads worse than it is. Reported D/E is ~5.0x and the Altman Z-Score of 1.23 sits in the "distress zone," but both are artifacts of the airline model. The negative working capital (current ratio 0.65) is structural: $10.75B of advance ticket sales and $7.97B of frequent-flyer deferred revenue are customer prepayments, not debt-like obligations. The real credit story is the opposite of distress: three ratings upgrades in 2025 (S&P to BB+ positive, Moody's to Ba1 stable, Fitch to BB+ stable), and liquidity of $16.6B unrestricted cash plus an undrawn $3.0B revolver against a $2.0B covenant floor. In early 2026 United termed out debt opportunistically: $1.0B 5.375% notes due 2031, $1.0B 4.875% notes due 2029, redemption of $2.0B 4.375% secured notes, and $3.9B of aircraft-secured financing at ~SOFR+1.4%.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. United repurchased $640M of stock in 2025 at an average $78.75 but paused buybacks in Q2 2026 (only $27M in H1), a self-aware move given the fuel shock; ~$755M remains on the $1.5B authorization. The priority is clearly deleveraging plus fleet capex (<$8.0B adjusted capex guided for 2026, reflecting Boeing/Airbus delays). No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The demand and revenue trajectory is the strongest part of the case. A 12% yield gain during a period of capacity discipline reflects genuine pricing power in premium and international cabins, not fare-led recovery from a depressed base. The Chase co-brand and "other operating revenue" stream compounds double-digit at high margin and is structurally insulated from the fuel/fare cyclicality that governs the flying business.

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum. Current-quarter EPS was revised +17.2% over 30 days to $3.21; current-year to $10.38 (+10.4% over 90 days); next-year to $15.50 (+9.1% over 30 days, +8.4% over 90 days). United has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (by 4.5-10%), supporting confidence in forward estimates. The current-year revision falls short of the velocity that would flag a clean recovery, but combined with 16% revenue growth it signals the market has been underestimating the demand resilience even through the fuel shock.

The qualifier is that this is a revision recovering against a mid-year guidance cut, not a fresh recovery off a trough. January 2026 guidance called for $12-14 adjusted EPS; by July that range had effectively become ~$9-11, a downgrade driven by the ~$6B incremental full-year fuel headwind. Management framed the July update as "raising to the high end of the forecast," but versus the start-of-year plan it was a walk-down, and Q3 guidance came in below consensus. Growth durability therefore depends on the fuel binary: at lower crude the January earnings power returns; at sustained elevated crude the compressed profile persists. The fleet plan itself faces slippage: the 737 MAX 10 (167 firm orders) remains uncertified and deliveries are being pushed, complicating the United Next capacity story and forcing older aircraft to stay in service longer at higher maintenance cost.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$118 and current-year EPS of $10.38, UAL trades at ~11.3x, in line with Delta (11.9x) and well below the peer median of 29.0x, which is inflated by AAL (47.9x) and ALK (75.6x) trading on depressed trough earnings. Against Delta, United's closest structural comparable, the two trade at essentially the same multiple; United's stronger Q2 yield performance and faster international recovery argue for at least parity, and arguably a modest premium.

The consensus price target framework is instructive. The mean target is $162.15 (median $162), with a high of $205 and a low of $102, against a current price of $117.26. That implies roughly 38% upside to the mean. Under the guidance recognized in the "Analyst Targets Are Floors" principle, these targets on a sub-12x-forward stock with rising estimates would suggest meaningful mispricing, but I temper that here because the target dispersion (a $102 low, a $205 high) reflects exactly the fuel binary that governs the earnings. The high target implicitly assumes fuel normalizes and the January $12-14 EPS power returns; the low target assumes the fuel compression persists. The consensus mean is a blended bet on de-escalation, not an independent confirmation of value.

Cycle-adjusted, the case is two-sided. On normalized earnings power ($12-14 EPS in a lower-crude world), an 11-13x multiple supports $130-180, the bull range. On the current fuel-compressed run-rate (~$9-11 EPS at sustained elevated crude), 11x supports $100-120, roughly where the stock trades. The market is pricing the current run-rate, not the normalized power, which means the upside is real but contingent on a specific external event resolving favorably. Cheapness alone does not earn a high valuation score here; the multiple is a fair reflection of unhedged fuel risk, and the re-rating catalyst sits outside the company's control. I score valuation 6.0: cheap enough to be constructive, contingent enough not to be a standalone buy.

## Competitive Landscape

United competes against legacy network peers (Delta, American), point-to-point and low-cost carriers (Southwest, JetBlue), ultra-low-cost carriers (Frontier, Spirit), subsidized Gulf carriers on international routes, and foreign carriers under Open Skies arrangements. Its competitive position is improving relative to the domestic discounters. The premium and international-gateway strategy is differentiating United from the domestic-heavy carriers whose fare discounting pressures yields. The Q2 yield gain, led by Atlantic and Pacific, is direct evidence the premium-demand thesis is working, and Reuters coverage noting United outperformed Delta in Q2 on yield strength supports a share-of-value narrative even where absolute size favors larger rivals.

The scarce-slot hub geography is a durable moat: Newark, San Francisco, and Chicago slots cannot be replicated, and they anchor the premium international network. The loyalty franchise and Chase co-brand deepen the flywheel. United also locked in regional feed through the Republic/Mesa merger (now a ~22% Republic stake, closed November 2025) and a 40% CommuteAir interest, though this concentrates dependence on a few regional partners.

The industry backdrop is bifurcated: robust premium and international demand against fuel-cost pressure and constrained domestic pricing from low-cost competition. United is positioned on the favorable side of that split, which is why competitive position (6.5) scores above the company's fuel-driven earnings profile.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel price volatility (the dominant risk).** Fuel is the largest and most volatile cost, and United does not hedge. The Q2 fuel-cost spike and the ~$6B incremental full-year fuel guidance are the single biggest earnings swing factor, driven directly by Middle East conflict. This is not a priced-in sector truism; it is an active, quantified headwind whose resolution is binary and external.

**Supplier concentration and certification risk.** Substantially all aircraft come from Boeing/Airbus. The 737 MAX 10 remains uncertified and deliveries are being pushed, complicating the fleet plan and raising maintenance cost as older aircraft stay in service.

**Hub congestion and regulatory capacity caps.** The FAA cap on Newark operations through October 2026 (following the 2025 EWR meltdown) constrains one of United's most valuable premium/international gateways, a UAL-specific risk a generic screen would miss.

**Leverage and non-IG ratings.** Despite deleveraging, ~$31B of fixed obligations and BB+/Ba1 ratings remain, with covenants requiring $2.0B minimum liquidity and 1.6:1 collateral coverage. The direction is favorable (upgrade path) but the balance sheet still carries cyclical fragility.

**Labor cost inflation.** 83% unionized; the new AFA contract ratified May 2026 carries a $742M ratification-bonus liability and ongoing pay increases. Labor cost is structurally rising.

**MileagePlus/Chase dependence.** The high-margin loyalty engine is exposed to regulatory reshaping of credit-card rewards economics, a low-probability, high-impact tail risk to a crown-jewel cash flow.

**Government shutdown exposure.** Late-2025 federal shutdowns forced FAA-directed schedule reductions at ~40 airports, a recurring operational/cost risk flagged in both recent filings.

The risk_factors score of 4.5 reflects the concentration of the unhedged-fuel binary plus the EWR cap plus mid-year guidance vulnerability: these are specific, material, and not fully priced given the two-sided target dispersion.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 59.5% is rich against 1-year historical vol of 47.5% (a 12.1pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (59.5% near vs 49.3% long-term), so the market expects an imminent move. ATM IV collapsed from 172.7% on June 18 to 59.5% now, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.24 to 0.91, indicating the extreme event-vol premium (around the June Hormuz escalation) has substantially unwound while positioning has normalized toward two-sided. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-22.1%), suggesting no acute downside hedging demand despite the fuel overhang, consistent with the market treating the fuel risk as a two-way bet on de-escalation rather than a one-directional threat. The elevated but declining IV argues against chasing entry; a further vol crush post the July earnings and any Hormuz verification would offer a cleaner entry point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($160-180).** A verified, held reopening of the Strait of Hormuz pulls crude lower, reversing the fuel component that compressed 2026 earnings. Adjusted EPS reverts toward the January $12-14 guide as the demand side (12% yields, 18.7% Pacific growth, Chase compounding) is already firing. Continued deleveraging pushes United toward investment grade, lowering financing cost and expanding the multiple. At 12-13x $13-14 normalized EPS, the stock reaches $160-180, roughly the consensus mean-to-high. The premium/loyalty flywheel proves it can grow yields faster than costs once fuel normalizes.

**Bear Case ($95-105).** Hormuz remains closed or the conflict grinds at sustained elevated crude, sustaining the fuel compression. Full-year EPS holds in the $9-11 range, Q3 disappoints as guided, and any softening of domestic demand in H2 2026 compounds low-cost competitive pressure on fares. The 737 MAX 10 certification slips further, raising maintenance cost. At 10-11x $9-10 EPS the stock falls to $95-105, about 10-19% below current, with the low analyst target of $102 as an anchor.

**Base Case ($125-135).** The most probable oil scenario keeps fuel elevated but off the peak. Demand strength partly offsets, EPS lands around $10.50-11.50, and the stock holds an 11-12x multiple for $125-135, modestly above current price. The stock re-rates only partially as the fuel binary stays unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $130.** 11.5x on ~$11.30 blended-scenario EPS (weighting a sustained-elevated-crude grind as most probable with partial demand offset). This sits between the bear ($95-105) and bull ($160-180) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $170.** 12.5x on ~$13.50 EPS in a lower-crude world.

**Bear case target: $100** (~15% below current $117.70). 10.5x on ~$9.50 EPS in a sustained elevated-crude environment.

**Timeframe: 6-12 months,** tied to (a) resolution of the Hormuz fuel binary and (b) the Q3 earnings print in October 2026, which tests the below-consensus guidance.

**Upside/downside from current (~$117.70):** +10% to base, +44% to bull, -15% to bear. The reward/risk skews modestly positive but is contingent on the fuel binary; the asymmetry is not yet compelling enough for a BUY at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening (72+ hours sustained commercial transit) that pulls crude lower, directly reversing the fuel headwind.
- Full-year adjusted EPS guidance re-raised back toward the $12-14 January plan, confirming the demand engine can carry earnings without a fuel tailwind.
- An investment-grade rating upgrade (from BB+/Ba1), which structurally lowers financing cost and supports multiple expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Sustained crude at levels forcing further capacity cuts and a guidance cut below $9 EPS (Hormuz failure scenario).
- H2 2026 demand weakening in yields or negative PRASM, signaling demand can no longer offset fuel.
- A material 737 MAX 10 certification failure or fleet-plan disruption that raises structural cost.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (October) and ongoing Hormuz verification are the natural checkpoints.

## Conclusion

United is executing a strong commercial strategy (16% revenue growth, 12% yield gains, a compounding Chase co-brand, three ratings upgrades, and real deleveraging) while its earnings sit hostage to an unhedged fuel cost that spiked 84% in Q2 and compressed net income 17%. The stock at ~11.3x current-year EPS is not expensive, and the $162 consensus mean target implies meaningful upside, but that upside is a blended bet on Middle East de-escalation rather than an independent confirmation of value. Reported results are flattered by ~$796M of non-recurring sale-leaseback gains, and management effectively cut full-year guidance mid-year despite upbeat framing.

The prior trajectory (AVOID → HOLD, scores drifting from 5.8 to 5.7) reflected this tension, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it: the demand thesis strengthened while the fuel overhang and guidance walk-down offset the improvement. This is a HOLD with a growth bias: the demand engine is real and the balance sheet is repairing, but the risk/reward turns asymmetric only on a verified fuel-shock reversal. Own it into a confirmed Hormuz reopening; wait for that resolution or a pullback toward the low-$100s before committing capital.
