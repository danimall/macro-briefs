**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ADMA Biologics is a vertically integrated plasma-derived biologics company whose flagship product, ASCENIV, is a differentiated RSV high-titer IVIG protected by a patented donor-screening assay that qualifies fewer than 10% of plasma samples. On backward-looking metrics the company looks like a clean compounder: revenue grew from $154M (FY2022) to $510M (FY2025), gross margin expanded from 34% to 57.4%, FY2025 net income was $146.9M, and the balance sheet carries an Altman Z near 5.6 with a current ratio of 6.7. A standardized data pull would score this name well.

The live situation is materially worse than those numbers suggest. Within roughly three months of the FY2025 10-K, ADMA cut FY2026 revenue guidance to $530–560M and withdrew prior long-term targets (>$635M for 2026, >$775M for 2027), watched BIVIGAM revenue collapse 54% YoY in Q1 2026 (holding total revenue flat at $114.5M despite ASCENIV growing 28%), changed CFOs concurrent with a short-seller report, and became the defendant in multiple securities class actions alleging channel-stuffing and undisclosed related-party transactions. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 30.7% for the current quarter and 19.6% for next year over 90 days. The stock is down 58% from its 52-week high of $20.97.

The investment case now hinges less on the still-attractive product economics and more on whether the short-seller allegations are substantiated and whether ASCENIV's growth reflects genuine end-patient demand or partly distributor stocking. With two distributors representing 71% of revenue and 82% of accounts receivable, that question is not academic. Until the litigation overhang clears and demand visibility is restored, the risk/reward is unfavorable. We rate ADMA AVOID. This is a demand-quality and credibility call, not a solvency call: the balance sheet is not distressed and there is no going-concern qualification.

## Company Overview

ADMA operates two segments. ADMA BioManufacturing runs an FDA-licensed plasma fractionation facility in Boca Raton, Florida with up to ~600,000 liters/year peak capacity and three approved products:

- **ASCENIV** — an RSV high-titer IVIG for Primary Immunodeficiency, the flagship growth engine. Its moat is a patented microneutralization assay that screens for RSV high-titer plasma found in <10% of donor samples, a real supply-side differentiator. FDA approved a pediatric label expansion (ages 2+) in May 2026, expanding the addressable market.
- **BIVIGAM** — a standard/commodity IVIG, now under acute pricing and destocking pressure (revenue -14% in FY2025, then -54% in Q1 2026).
- **Nabi-HB** — a mature Hepatitis B hyperimmune globulin, <10% of revenue and declining.

The second segment, ADMA BioCenters, operates 7 FDA-licensed plasma collection centers (down from 10 after a Q1 2026 divestiture). ADMA is one of only four U.S. plasma fractionators alongside CSL Behring, Grifols, and Takeda. The pipeline includes SG-001, a preclinical S. pneumoniae hyperimmune globulin with a planned pre-IND in FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory through FY2025 was strong. Revenue rose 20% to $510.2M, with ASCENIV at $362.5M (+51%), BIVIGAM at $122M (-14%), and plasma/other at ~$25M. Gross margin expanded to 57.4% from 51.5% the prior year, aided by an FDA-approved yield enhancement process (April 2025) that lifts output ~20% from the same plasma input. The prior year's $197.7M net income was inflated by an $84.3M deferred-tax benefit and a $12.6M Medicaid rebate true-up, so the underlying earnings comparison is more favorable than the headline decline implies.

The Q1 2026 print is where the story breaks. ASCENIV grew 27.7% to $97.5M, but BIVIGAM fell to $15.4M and intermediates fell 78%. Gross margin rose to ~70.5% on favorable mix and yield enhancement, and net income rose to $45.3M, helped by an $8.0M one-time gain on the plasma-center sale. Operating cash flow turned strongly positive (+$58.2M), driven by $22.6M of AR collection, though FY2025 full-year OCF was weak (~$50M) on large AR and inventory builds, a recurring working-capital watch item.

The balance sheet is not distressed. Q1 2026 cash was $138M, inventory $222M, AR $136M, total debt $196.9M, equity $390M. ROE is ~31% and ROA ~24%. In August 2025 ADMA refinanced into a cheaper JPMorgan facility — $300M ($75M term + $225M revolver), ~6.2% rate, maturing August 2028 — replacing prior double-digit Ares/Hayfin debt. Covenants (max leverage 2.5x, min FCCR 1.2x) are in compliance, and dividends are prohibited. Management states cash plus AR plus operating cash flow funds operations "through Q1 2027 and beyond." The financial-health concern is about earnings quality, not liquidity.

The capital allocation pivot is a flag. The board authorized a $500M buyback in May 2025. In Q1 2026 ADMA executed a $125M accelerated share repurchase funded by drawing the revolver — it levered up to buy back stock at roughly $16/share, immediately before a ~50% decline. Treasury stock rose to $143M (8.67M shares). Bulls read aggressive repurchase as management confidence; the bearish read is that ADMA converted balance-sheet cash into debt at a peak valuation, and buybacks can flatter EPS while masking demand softness.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has reversed. The most important forward indicator — consensus estimate revisions — is uniformly negative: current-quarter EPS revised down 30.7% over 90 days, next-quarter down 26.2%, current-year down 14.8%, and next-year down 19.6%. This is the inverse of the rising-estimates, accelerating-revenue signal that would support a growth thesis. The mechanism is straightforward: management cut guidance and withdrew long-term targets, and the Street has marked down accordingly.

ASCENIV's growth remains the bull's anchor, and the pediatric label expansion genuinely enlarges the TAM. But total revenue was flat in Q1 because BIVIGAM cratered, and the central analytical question — whether ASCENIV's growth is end-patient demand or partly distributor stocking — is exactly what the securities litigation contests. Management relies on purchased distributor data for demand estimates (disclosed in risk factors), which is the visibility gap a channel-stuffing allegation exploits. With two distributors at 82% of AR, a one- to two-quarter destocking episode could turn reported ASCENIV growth negative even if underlying demand is intact.

The standard IVIG market is in a pricing-pressure and inventory-destocking cycle, with new FDA-approved entrants using aggressive pricing, extended payment terms, and rebates, and FcRn inhibitors threatening IVIG demand in adjacent indications. ASCENIV is more insulated than BIVIGAM, but management acknowledges the pressure runs "broadly across the IVIG complex." The growth thesis is product-specific (ASCENIV) rather than franchise-wide, and even the product-specific case is now clouded by demand-quality questions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $8.58, ADMA trades at roughly 12.6x trailing EPS of $0.60 and about 8.6x next-year consensus EPS of ~$1.00. On the surface that is cheap against a biotech peer median P/E of 23.5x (VCEL 91.5x, HRMY 14.2x, SRPT 23.5x; FTRE and VIR negative). For a company with 57%+ gross margins and a differentiated lead product, single-digit forward earnings multiples look like a value opportunity.

The discount is earned, not a mispricing the market has overlooked. Three factors justify it. First, the earnings base is in question: if channel-stuffing allegations have merit, reported earnings overstate sustainable run-rate, and the "cheap" multiple is calculated on numbers that may revise lower. Second, estimates are falling 15–30% across horizons, so the forward multiple is a moving target biased downward. Third, an unresolved binary litigation overhang carries asymmetric downside that a clean P/E does not capture. A low multiple on contested earnings with a fraud allegation pending is a value trap profile, not an asymmetric-upside setup.

The peer comparison is also less flattering than the headline suggests. SRPT trades at 23.5x but sits 62% below its 52-week high, illustrating how quickly biotech multiples compress when a thesis breaks. ADMA's own analyst targets ($12 low, $21 high, $17.6 mean) sit far above the current price, but those targets predate full digestion of the guidance cut and litigation; Cantor already downgraded to Neutral (May 21) and Mizuho cut its target to $20 from $24. Treating those stale targets as floors would be a mistake here, because the conditions for that treatment (rising estimates, clean fundamentals) are absent. We assign a valuation score of 5.0: the price is genuinely low, but there is no identified catalyst to close the gap, and the earnings base is contested.

## Competitive Landscape

ADMA's structural position is attractive in the abstract: one of four U.S. fractionators in an oligopoly, with a differentiated niche product protected by a scarce-plasma screening assay and reinforced by long-term supply agreements (Grifols to 2039, KEDPlasma to 2031, each with 35,000 L/yr minimums). The yield enhancement process is a real, FDA-approved margin tailwind, and the pediatric approval extends ASCENIV's reach.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than improving. ASCENIV's moat is product-specific and appears intact; BIVIGAM's commodity position is deteriorating rapidly under new-entrant pricing pressure, as the 54% Q1 decline demonstrates. FcRn inhibitors represent a structural threat to IVIG demand in adjacent indications over the medium term. The franchise is bifurcating into a defensible specialty product and an eroding commodity product. RSV high-titer plasma supply dependence (third-party reliant, <10% donor qualification) is a genuine constraint on scaling ASCENIV. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating at the franchise level, which supports a middling score.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the securities-fraud overhang. A Culper Research short report (~May 13, 2026) alleged financial misrepresentation, channel stuffing, and undisclosed related-party transactions. Multiple securities class actions have been filed (class period August 9, 2024–March 25, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline August 10, 2026), with numerous law firms running investigations. ADMA publicly refuted the claims (Reg FD 8-Ks on 3/2, 3/27, 5/4/2026; stock bounced ~15% on the rebuttal). The allegations are unproven and contested, but the BIVIGAM collapse and 82%-of-AR concentration in two distributors give the channel-stuffing narrative superficial plausibility. The Q1 AR decline (collections) cuts slightly against the most aggressive version but does not resolve it. This is an unresolved binary that the current valuation only partly prices.

Compounding governance flags: the CFO changed (Brad Tade departed via separation agreement 2/25/2026; Paul Kohler appointed) concurrent with the short report, and ADMA changed its certifying accountant in late 2024. Related-party transactions are real and now litigated — ADMA leases from Areth, LLC and historically purchased equipment from GenesisBPS, both controlled by CEO Adam Grossman and his father, Vice Chairman Dr. Jerrold Grossman (GenesisBPS sold by the Grossmans only in September 2025). The proxy uses no financial performance measure in its pay-versus-performance disclosure, a governance soft spot. The debt-funded buyback at the highs is a capital-allocation concern. Other risks: extreme customer concentration, single-facility manufacturing, RSV plasma supply dependence, and $265.6M of federal NOLs subject to Section 382 limits.

The June 2, 2026 annual meeting saw the board re-elected, the auditor ratified, and say-on-pay approved — no shareholder revolt, which modestly cuts against the most severe governance read. But the cluster of CFO change, auditor change, related-party web, and active litigation, all surfacing within a short window, is exactly the profile that warrants a low risk score because the downside is specific and not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates the caution. Near-term ATM IV (56.2%) is roughly in line with one-year historical volatility (56.1%), so front-month options are fairly priced rather than cheap. The put/call open-interest ratio is 6.54 (up sharply from 1.44 three weeks prior), and put/call skew is elevated at 36.5%, so the market is paying up for downside protection. The term structure is in contango (near 56.2% vs long-term 64.7%), with the 3-month tenor showing a steep 82.8% IV and a -39.2% skew, consistent with hedging around the August litigation timeline. Short interest is 10.5% of float (3.1 days to cover), elevated but not at squeeze-risk levels. The options market is positioned for continued downside and event risk, aligned with the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The fraud allegations prove unfounded, the IG destocking cycle normalizes within two to three quarters, and ASCENIV resumes 25%+ growth with pediatric expansion lifting the TAM. Yield enhancement holds gross margins above 60%, the JPM refinancing keeps cost of capital low, and the post-crash valuation re-rates toward the peer median. In this scenario the stock recovers toward the $12 analyst low target and potentially higher as litigation clears. Bull case ~$14 (+63%).

### Bear Case
Discovery in the securities litigation substantiates channel-stuffing or related-party concerns, forcing earnings restatement or revealing that reported ASCENIV growth included distributor stocking that now reverses. BIVIGAM continues to erode, FY2026 revenue lands at or below the low end of the cut range, and estimates fall further. A credibility discount persists, and the multiple compresses on lower, lower-quality earnings. Bear case ~$5.50 (-36%).

The asymmetry is unfavorable on a probability-weighted basis because the bear case has a concrete, dated catalyst (litigation progression) while the bull case requires the absence of bad news plus a demand-cycle recovery that management cannot yet confirm.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The securities litigation timeline provides the clearest dated path — lead-plaintiff deadline August 10, 2026, after which consolidated complaints and the early discovery posture become visible. Beyond that, each quarterly print (next earnings ~early August 2026) tests whether ASCENIV growth holds or whether destocking reverses reported revenue. A further guidance cut or any restatement language would drive the stock materially lower.

**Timing window**: The litigation catalyst is roughly two months out (August), with quarterly demand confirmation on a similar cadence. This is a reasonable window, not a distant 6+ month wait.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is elevated at 10.5% of float but below the >20% threshold that creates serious squeeze risk, and days-to-cover is low at 3.1. Institutional ownership (BlackRock/State Street/Invesco + insiders ~30%) is moderate. Squeeze risk is manageable, but the ~15% rebound on the company's refutation shows the stock can spike sharply on any favorable headline.

**Borrow and options signal**: The options market confirms the bearish thesis via the put/call OI of 6.54, 36.5% put skew, and steep 3-month IV (82.8%) around the litigation window. This rich downside pricing raises the cost of expressing the short via options.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$5.50 (-36%) against a squeeze/recovery upside risk toward $12–14 (+40% to +63%) if the allegations are dismissed and demand normalizes. That is roughly a 1:1 to sub-1:1 reward:risk on a short, below the 2:1 bar. The contested nature of the allegations (genuinely differentiated product, profitable, not distressed) means the downside is not assured, while a favorable litigation or earnings surprise carries large upside risk.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a credibility crisis with real downside, but the company is profitable with a defensible lead product and a healthy balance sheet, so it is not a collapsing business with assured downside. The reward:risk is below 2:1, the allegations are unproven and could be dismissed, and the rich put pricing plus rebound risk make the short expensive and asymmetric against the seller. Avoid owning it; do not press it short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a 9–12 month window over which the litigation and demand-quality questions should resolve.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- The securities litigation is dismissed, settled on immaterial terms, or discovery clearly fails to substantiate the channel-stuffing/related-party allegations.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or reaccelerating consensus estimate revisions (reversing the current -20% to -30% trend), confirming end-demand rather than channel dynamics.
- Evidence that BIVIGAM has bottomed and that ASCENIV growth is verifiable end-patient demand (e.g., management restoring long-term guidance with disclosed end-demand metrics, reduced distributor concentration).
- Valuation that compensates for residual risk — a forward multiple in the 6–8x range on confirmed, uncontested earnings would offer asymmetry once the overhang clears.

**Timeline**: The headwinds (litigation, IG destocking cycle) should persist through at least the August earnings print and the August 10 lead-plaintiff deadline. We would reassess after Q2 2026 results and any litigation developments.

## Conclusion

ADMA is a fundamentally differentiated, profitable plasma-biologics niche leader whose backward-looking financials — 20% revenue growth, 57%+ gross margins, $147M net income, Altman Z of 5.6 — would normally support a constructive view. The forward picture has deteriorated sharply: withdrawn long-term guidance, a 54% BIVIGAM collapse, consensus estimates down 15–30% over 90 days, a debt-funded buyback executed near the highs, a CFO change concurrent with a short report, and active securities litigation alleging channel-stuffing and undisclosed related-party transactions. The central question is no longer how fast ASCENIV grows but whether reported growth reflects genuine end-patient demand, and that question is precisely what is in litigation.

The balance sheet is sound and there is no going-concern qualification, so this is a credibility and demand-quality problem rather than a solvency one, and on those dimensions the burden of proof now sits with the bulls. The 58% drawdown reflects sophisticated sellers responding to confirmed fundamental reversals (guidance cut, BIVIGAM collapse) plus an unresolved fraud allegation, not mere volatility. We rate ADMA **AVOID** with an overall score of 4.6, and we would revisit only on resolution of the litigation overhang combined with two quarters of stabilizing estimates and verifiable end-demand. The cheap-looking multiple is a value-trap signature until the earnings base is no longer contested.
