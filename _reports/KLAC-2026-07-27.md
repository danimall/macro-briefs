**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

*Prices adjusted for the 10-for-1 forward stock split effective June 11, 2026. Current price $210.52 is post-split (~$2,105 pre-split equivalent). SEC filings cited below predate the split and show pre-split per-share figures.*

## Executive Summary

KLA sits at one of the most advantaged nodes of the semiconductor value chain. It supplies the inspection, metrology, and yield-management tools that chipmakers use to detect nanometer-scale defects across the fab process, and it holds a commanding, near-monopolistic position in optical wafer and reticle inspection. The economics are exceptional: FY2025 revenue of $12.16B (+24% YoY), ~33% net margins, ~87% ROE, gross margins near 61%, and prodigious free cash flow (9M FY26 operating cash flow of $3.24B against capex of just $287M). The balance sheet is strong once the accounting artifacts are stripped away: net leverage is 0.54x against a 3.25x covenant, and the company carries A-band credit ratings. The structural thesis is real: process-control intensity rises as nodes shrink to 2nm and structures grow more complex, so KLA can grow faster than the broader wafer-fab-equipment market during a leading-edge-driven cycle.

The problem is price. The stock has risen roughly 2.5x from its July 2025 low of $83.22 to a peak of $307.37 on June 30, 2026, before correcting to $210.52, a 31% drawdown from that peak. At the current price, KLA trades at ~57x current-year (FY26) consensus EPS of $3.70 and ~41x next-year (FY27) EPS of $5.13. That FY27 figure embeds a ~39% earnings growth expectation, well above the 10–11% revenue growth the company is actually delivering (Q3 FY26 revenue +11%, 9M FY26 +10%). Analysts are still nudging estimates higher (next-year EPS revised +6.4% over 90 days), so the multiple is not obviously stretched relative to the semiconductor-equipment cohort (peer median P/E 68.5). But it does require the AI/2nm cycle to drive a material revenue and margin re-acceleration that has not yet appeared in the top line.

We rate KLAC a HOLD with a constructive lean. This is a premium-multiple stock rather than an earnings-trough story; the estimate-revision momentum, while positive, is far short of the velocity that would justify chasing a name at 57x current-year earnings. Layer on a live sector-correction risk, a binary Q4 FY26 earnings event that the options market is pricing at ±21%, and one-directional China regulatory tightening, and the risk/reward at $210 does not support high-conviction accumulation. The franchise quality and structural tailwind keep this well clear of AVOID.

## Company Overview

KLA supplies process control and yield management equipment: the tools chipmakers use to inspect and measure their production at the nanometer scale. The company was formed via the 1997 merger of KLA Instruments and Tencor and holds a dominant share in optical wafer inspection and reticle inspection.

Three reportable segments:

- **Semiconductor Process Control (~90% of revenue):** inspection, metrology, and data analytics. Wafer Inspection alone was 51% of Q3 FY26 revenue (+16% YoY); Patterning ~18–20%.
- **Specialty Semiconductor Process (~4%):** vacuum deposition/etch tools for MEMS, RF, and power semiconductors.
- **PCB and Component Inspection (~5%):** PCB, IC substrate, and advanced-packaging inspection. KLA exited the Display business in March 2024.

Services represents ~23% of revenue and is growing. Management describes these as recurring, subscription-like contracts tied to an expanding installed base, a high-margin annuity that cushions the historical cyclicality of tool sales. Legacy-node longevity keeps installed equipment in service for years, generating a steady service stream.

The moat rests on technology leadership (>8,500 active patents) and very high switching costs. Once a customer qualifies KLA equipment for a production line, it stays qualified for years. Competitors (Applied Materials, ASML, Hitachi High-Tech, Onto Innovation, Lasertec) compete around the edges, but none match KLA's breadth in process control. International revenue is ~89% of total; the workforce is ~15,000.

## Financial Analysis

KLA's financials are among the strongest in large-cap technology.

**Profitability and growth:**
- FY2025 (ended June 30, 2025): revenue $12.16B (+24% YoY), net income $4.06B, net margin ~33%.
- Q3 FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026): revenue $3.42B (+11% YoY), net income $1.20B (+10%).
- 9M FY2026: revenue $9.92B (+10%), net income $3.47B.
- Gross margin ~61%, down modestly from 61.6% to 61.1% YoY, pressured by DRAM component cost inflation and tariffs. Management calls the DRAM impact transitory.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- 9M FY26 operating cash flow of $3.24B against capex of only $287M produces very high free cash flow conversion. This is a capital-light, cash-generative model.
- Cash and marketable securities of $4.96B (March 31, 2026); total debt of $5.95B in laddered fixed-rate Senior Notes maturing 2029–2063.
- Net leverage of 0.54x versus a 3.25x covenant maximum. Credit ratings sit at A (Fitch) / A2 (Moody's) / A- (S&P).

On the balance sheet's optics: a naive Altman Z-Score screen flags KLA at ~1.94, in the "distress zone." This is a false signal. KLA's book equity (~$5.83B) is structurally thin because the company has returned nearly all of its free cash flow to shareholders through buybacks and funds some of those returns with debt. With ~33% net margins, ~87% ROE, 0.54x net leverage, and A-band credit, there is no bankruptcy risk. The low Z-Score reflects deliberate capital-structure engineering, not financial weakness.

**Capital allocation:**
- 17th consecutive annual dividend increase, raised to $2.30/quarter (pre-split) in March 2026.
- Buyback authorization increased by $7.0B in Q3 FY26; $10.31B remaining (~3.6% of market cap). 9M FY26 returned $1.72B in buybacks and $752M in dividends.
- Diluted share count is steadily declining.

One caveat: repurchasing stock at ~57x current-year earnings near all-time highs is not obviously value-accretive. The buyback program is running its largest authorization at the richest valuation in the company's history, a mild concern about capital-allocation discipline even as the shareholder-return posture is admirable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is durable but decelerating from the pandemic surge. Revenue grew 24% in FY25 but has settled to 10–11% in recent quarters. The structural drivers are genuine:

- **Process-control intensity rises as nodes shrink.** At 2nm, with gate-all-around transistors, EUV in logic and DRAM, high-bandwidth memory, and advanced 2.5D/3D packaging for AI accelerators, chipmakers spend a higher share of wafer-fab-equipment budgets on inspection and metrology. This lets KLA grow faster than overall WFE during a leading-edge cycle.
- **Memory/HBM leadership in the current cycle.** Korea revenue rose 50% (9M) and 80% (Q3) YoY on memory and HBM investment.
- **Services annuity** (~23% of revenue) compounds with the installed base and extends with legacy-node longevity.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +1.8% over 90 days, next quarter +2.8%, current year +1.2%, and next year +6.4%. The next-year figure is the most encouraging: analysts are getting more confident about FY27, not less. But these are single-digit revisions, not the +30% velocity that signals an earnings trough turning up. KLA is a premium-multiple, moderate-growth compounder, not a cheap inflection stock.

The FY27 consensus of $5.13 EPS embeds ~39% growth off the FY26 ~$3.70 base. With revenue only growing ~10–11% and buybacks contributing ~2–3% to EPS, the balance of that expected growth must come from a revenue re-acceleration on the 2nm/HBM cycle plus margin recovery once the DRAM component headwind passes. This is plausible given the confirmed breadth of AI-infrastructure demand, but it is an aggressive consensus that the late-July Q4 FY26 print and FY27 guidance will begin to test.

The main structural counterweight is mix. Memory customers historically adopt less process control than foundry/logic. A shift toward lower-intensity memory spend, or a slowdown in China legacy-node investment, would pressure the growth premium.

At $210.52, KLA trades at ~56.9x current-year EPS ($3.70) and ~41x next-year EPS ($5.13). On next-year growth of ~39%, the PEG is roughly 1.05, defensible for a franchise of this quality if the growth materializes. Relative to peers, KLA is not the most expensive name in a re-rated cohort:

Against a peer median of 68.5x, KLA at ~57x current-year screens as reasonably valued for the highest-quality process-control franchise in the group. But this comparison is only reassuring if the entire cohort's multiples are sustainable, and the semiconductor-equipment complex has re-rated dramatically: the SOX index is up roughly 99% year-to-date in 2026. When an entire sector re-rates on a demand thesis, "cheaper than peers" can mean "less overvalued," not "undervalued."

We score valuation neutral. The stock is a fortress business rather than a value trap, but there is no clean mispricing to exploit at 57x current-year earnings after a 2.5x move. The bull case rests on the FY27 re-acceleration validating the 41x next-year multiple; the bear case is that revenue stays at ~10% and the multiple compresses toward the low-to-mid 30s. Consensus targets ($234 mean, $227.5 median, $150–$325 range) imply modest upside from here, and on a stock trading above 50x forward, we do not treat analyst targets as floors; at premium multiples, targets and price often reflect the same growth extrapolation.

## Competitive Landscape

KLA's competitive position is one of the strongest in technology, and its trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. The company holds a near-monopoly in optical wafer inspection and reticle inspection, protected by >8,500 patents and by switching costs that lock in customers once tools are qualified for a production line. The structural tailwind, rising process-control intensity as the industry moves to 2nm, EUV, gate-all-around, and advanced packaging, is precisely the direction leading-edge manufacturing is heading, and it accrues disproportionately to KLA.

The service business deepens the moat. At ~23% of revenue and growing, it converts an ever-larger installed base into recurring, high-margin revenue and dampens the cyclicality that historically made the sector risky. The company has grown revenue 10–24% despite a sharp decline in China exposure (from 43% of revenue in FY24 to 31–33% now), which demonstrates that leading-edge and AI demand more than offset Chinese softness.

The competitive counterweights are secondary. Applied Materials competes in metrology, Onto Innovation and Lasertec in select inspection niches, and ASML holds the EUV lithography position. None threaten KLA's core process-control franchise in the near term. The weaker segments (PCB, which took a $230.4M goodwill impairment in FY25 Q2) are non-core and small.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are meaningful and, in several cases, not fully priced.

1. **China export controls — the single largest risk.** China was 33% of FY25 revenue. Successive BIS rules, Entity List additions, revised advanced-DRAM definitions, and the September 2025 "Affiliates Rule" (suspended until November 2026) restrict what KLA can ship and service. License failures have already forced customer-deposit refunds and hurt backlog. Chinese counter-measures (rare-earth export controls) also threaten KLA's own component supply. The risk is asymmetric and one-directional: continued legacy-node demand is a tailwind, but any new rule or reinstatement of the Affiliates Rule in November 2026 could force further refunds and backlog cuts.

2. **DRAM component cost headwind (new).** Large suppliers discontinued certain DRAM chips embedded in KLA's own image computers, spiking prices and pressuring gross margin through calendar 2026. This drove purchase commitments to $4.83B. Management deems it transitory, but it is a live margin drag and an ironic vulnerability for the company that inspects DRAM production.

3. **Customer concentration.** TSMC and Samsung are the >10% customers; in Q3 FY26 two customers were 19% and 11% of revenue. Custom-configured tools make any single large customer difficult to replace.

4. **Sector cyclicality and correction risk.** Semiconductor-equipment demand is cyclical, and the group has run hard. There is a real near-term risk of a broad semiconductor correction after the 2026 rally, independent of KLA's fundamentals.

5. **Backlog normalization.** Backlog fell from $9.83B (FY24) to $7.86B (FY25) as lead times normalized. Not a demand signal per se, but it reduces forward visibility.

6. **Tariffs, Israel geopolitical exposure, and an ERP upgrade** (completion expected Q1 FY27, carrying reporting-transition risk) round out the list.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 101.5% against a one-year historical volatility of 58.1%, rich by 43 points, in the 83rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (101.5% near-term vs 87.9% at six months), which the market uses to price an imminent binary event, the late-July Q4 FY26 earnings report. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±21%. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.66 to 1.73 since the prior snapshot, alongside a 19% price decline, indicating a shift toward defensive/bearish positioning. IV collapsed from 195.9% to 101.5% over that window, a large crush from the post-split peak, though the level remains extremely elevated. Skew is balanced (-2.2%), so this is not a one-sided crash bet; the market is pricing a wide two-sided outcome into earnings. For a HOLD, this reinforces patience: the earnings event could resolve in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

KLA is the highest-quality way to own the AI-driven leading-edge capex cycle. Process-control intensity rises structurally at 2nm, EUV, and advanced packaging, so KLA grows faster than WFE. The FY27 consensus of $5.13 EPS (+39%) proves conservative as the 2nm ramp and HBM buildout re-accelerate revenue and the DRAM component headwind fades, restoring 61%+ gross margins. Estimate revisions continue rising (next-year already +6.4% over 90 days). At 45–50x on $5.13, the stock reaches $231–$257; if the AI cycle re-rates the group again and BofA's constructive stance is right, ~$317. Bull case: **~$317 (+51%).**

### Bear Case

Revenue stays at ~10% and the aggressive FY27 EPS growth expectation fails to materialize. China regulatory tightening (Affiliates Rule reinstatement in November 2026) forces further deposit refunds and backlog cuts. The DRAM component squeeze proves less transitory than management claims, compressing gross margins through 2026. A broad semiconductor correction de-rates the group from 57x toward the low-to-mid 30s. On $5.13 next-year EPS at ~30x, the stock reaches the analyst low of ~$150. Bear case: **~$150 (-29%).**

### Base Case

The AI/2nm tailwind supports mid-teens earnings growth and the multiple settles into the low-to-mid 40s on next-year earnings as consensus catches up. Base case ~$235 (roughly consensus mean), **+12%** from current, over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact (a genuine structural tailwind, positive next-year estimate revisions, and a best-in-class franchise), but the premium multiple, the live sector-correction risk, and the binary earnings event prevent a high-conviction BUY at $210.

- **Reassessment timeline:** The late-July Q4 FY26 earnings report and FY27 guidance. The November 2026 Affiliates Rule reinstatement decision is the next major regulatory checkpoint.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. A pullback toward the low-$180s or below (roughly 35x next-year EPS), which would restore asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
  2. Q4 FY26 revenue growth re-accelerating above the mid-teens with FY27 guidance corroborating the $5.13 consensus, confirming the growth premium.
  3. Constructive resolution or extension of China export-control uncertainty that removes the asymmetric regulatory tail.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. Revenue growth decelerating below 5% YoY combined with a gross-margin decline below 60% on a non-transitory DRAM/component squeeze.
  2. Reinstatement of the China Affiliates Rule forcing material deposit refunds and a backlog cut below ~$7B.
  3. FY27 consensus estimates rolling over (falling more than 5% over 90 days), which would break the current positive-revision underpinning of the multiple.

Current-price context: base case +12%, bull case +51%, bear case -29%. The roughly balanced upside/downside is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY at the current price.

## Conclusion

KLA is an exceptional business: a near-monopoly in semiconductor process control with 61% gross margins, 33% net margins, prodigious free cash flow, a fortress balance sheet, and a genuine structural tailwind as inspection intensity rises at the leading edge. The competitive position and financial health are among the best in large-cap technology, and the AI/2nm/HBM demand cycle is broad and confirmed across the infrastructure layer.

The constraint is valuation. After a 2.5x run in twelve months, the stock trades at ~57x current-year and ~41x next-year earnings, embedding an aggressive ~39% FY27 growth expectation against actual revenue growth of 10–11%. The estimate-revision momentum is positive but modest, well short of the velocity that would justify chasing a premium-multiple name. Add a live sector-correction risk, a binary earnings event that the options market is pricing at ±21%, and one-directional China regulatory risk, and the risk/reward at $210 is roughly balanced.

We rate KLAC a **HOLD** with a constructive lean. This is a name to own for the durable, high-quality growth thesis and to accumulate on weakness toward the low-$180s or on confirmation that the FY27 re-acceleration is real.
