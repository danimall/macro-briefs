**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Marriott Vacations Worldwide is a leveraged, brand-licensed timeshare developer in the middle of a management-led turnaround following a fiscal 2025 net loss of roughly $308 million, a full C-suite overhaul, and deteriorating unit economics. The FY2025 loss was overwhelmingly non-cash — some $577 million in impairments tied to goodwill, trade names, inventory write-downs, and an Asia-Pacific pullback — so the balance sheet is not in imminent crisis. But the impairments expose that MVW overpaid for the ILG and Welk acquisitions: goodwill of ~$2.96 billion now exceeds book equity of ~$2.0 billion, leaving negative tangible equity and a live risk of further write-downs. Underneath the impairment noise, the operating story is weakening: Q1 2026 development profit margin collapsed 610 basis points to 16.1%, contract sales fell 2%, and adjusted EBITDA declined 16% year over year.

The stock has recovered from a November 2025 low of $44.58 to roughly $94 today, a gain of more than 110%. The rally has been fueled by a Q1 2026 report that beat lowered expectations (the shares rose ~9% post-print despite a headline EPS miss), a June 1 Goldman Sachs upgrade to Buy, and a market willing to underwrite the recovery narrative. That leaves the "cheap distressed asset" thesis largely priced in. Management is explicitly telling investors that the benefits of its modernization and quality-over-volume repositioning will not show up until the second half of 2026 and into 2027, from a team that has been in place for a matter of months.

We rate VAC a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The score has trended up over three reports (4.3 → 4.9 → 5.1), and the improvements in risk (Spain litigation reversal) and valuation are real. But the Q1 EPS miss of -27.9% broke a three-quarter beat streak, development margins are still compressing, and insiders are not buying with their own cash at a valuation the bulls call cheap. The evidence does not yet support a confident BUY, and the balance-sheet cushion and healthy resort occupancy do not support an AVOID.

## Company Overview

MVW operates two segments. Vacation Ownership (~95% of revenue) is the exclusive worldwide developer, marketer, seller, and manager of timeshare products under licensed brands: Marriott Vacation Club, Sheraton, Westin, The Ritz-Carlton Club, Grand Residences by Marriott, and Hyatt Vacation Club. These brands are licensed from Marriott International and Hyatt under long-term agreements expiring 2090–2095; MVW does not own them. Revenue derives from selling vacation ownership interests (VOIs), resort and HOA management fees, consumer financing (MVW finances roughly 55% of VOI sales), and rental of unsold inventory. The second segment, Exchange & Third-Party Management, comprises the Interval International exchange network (3,200+ resorts across 90+ countries, ~1.5 million active members) and Aqua-Aston property management. This segment is fee-based and high-margin (~45% EBITDA margin) but slowly shrinking, with members down 2% year over year.

The moat rests almost entirely on the licensed brand relationships. The Marriott and Hyatt affiliation drives customer acquisition through marketing to loyalty-program members and hotel guests, with roughly a quarter of resorts co-located with branded hotels. That is both the strength and the central vulnerability: the brands can be lost for breach or failure to meet brand standards, and Marriott/Hyatt retain the ability under certain conditions to compete in timeshare. Primary competitors are Hilton Grand Vacations and Travel + Leisure, the latter of which owns Interval's main exchange rival, RCI.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** The headline is a net loss of approximately $308 million versus +$217 million of net income in FY2024. The swing was driven by ~$577 million of impairment charges plus rising loan-loss reserves and ~$122 million of "modernization" charges. Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $751 million was essentially flat against $736 million in 2024, so the loss is largely non-cash. But flat adjusted EBITDA masks weakening earnings quality, and the impairments confirm that prior M&A destroyed value.

**Q1 2026.** Total revenue rose 5% to $1,257 million, but the increase is inflated by $57 million of pass-through cost reimbursements. Core VOI product sales fell 3% to $343 million. Net income dropped to $22 million from $56 million; diluted EPS was $0.64 versus $1.46. Adjusted EBITDA fell 16% to $161 million, with margin compressing 370 basis points to 19.5%. The clearest operating tell is development profit margin, which collapsed 610 basis points to 16.1% from 22.2%; marketing and sales costs rose to 70% of VOI revenue from 66% while contract sales fell 2% to $411 million. Tours declined 3%; volume per guest (VPG) rose a modest 1% to $4,016. Rental profit fell 27% on higher unsold-inventory maintenance fees.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Corporate net debt to adjusted EBITDA sits at 4.2x, which management calls manageable while targeting deleveraging. The structure comprises ~$3.6 billion of corporate debt plus ~$2.3 billion of non-recourse securitized debt. Cash fell to $268 million from $406 million as $575 million of 2026 convertibles were repaid. The refinancing came at a steep cost: the 0% converts were replaced in part by new 6.5% 2033 notes, and interest expense rose 9% year over year. The near-term maturity wall includes $575 million of 2027 convertible notes (December 2027) and $350 million of 2028 notes. S&P downgraded MVW to B+ in 2025, sub-investment grade, raising future funding costs. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was slightly negative at -$4 million, seasonally weak but a stated priority precisely because it is under pressure. The reported Altman Z-Score of ~0.9 flags a distress zone, though that metric is distorted for a finance-heavy timeshare model carrying large securitized receivables and matched debt.

**Loan book.** The consumer financing book is a core risk. The estimated average remaining default rate reached 13.36% in Q1 2026, with a reserve balance of $534 million. Weighted-average FICO is 730, but ~$65 million of loans carry FICO below 600 and ~$420 million carry no score. Management is intentionally reducing sub-640 FICO originations, a quality-over-volume shift that pressures near-term sales while improving forward credit quality. A 0.5-point increase in the default rate adds roughly $15 million to reserves, and all 12 securitization pools are currently in compliance with their performance triggers. Post-quarter, MVW completed a $470 million receivables securitization (2026-1) at a ~4.86% blended rate, demonstrating continued ABS-market access.

The financial-health score of 4.3 reflects a balance sheet under stress but not at the edge: 4.2x leverage against a B+ rating, negative tangible equity, negative Q1 operating cash flow, and a rising cost of debt, offset by continued ABS access, non-core asset sales, and adjusted EBITDA that remains healthy in absolute terms.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the crux of the debate, and the current data reads as cyclical softness compounded by self-inflicted transition pain. The Q1 contraction in contract sales and tours is driven partly by the deliberate pullback from sub-640 FICO customers and lower-return Asia-Pacific operations. Management frames this as trading near-term volume for quality, with the benefits deferred to H2 2026 and 2027.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than clearly inflecting. Current-year EPS consensus of $7.55 is essentially flat over 90 days (-0.5%), and current-quarter estimates have drifted down 4.0% over the same window. The one positive is next-year EPS, revised up 2.4% over 90 days to $8.54, and next-quarter up 12.0% to $1.95. This is not the >30% 90-day revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection; it is modest, back-half-weighted optimism. The Q1 EPS actual of $1.24 missed the $1.72 estimate by 27.9%, breaking a three-quarter streak of beats (17.3%, 3.4%, 9.7%) and signaling that near-term visibility has deteriorated.

Sector context matters. The whole timeshare industry faces first-time-buyer softness and consumer-credit stress, and HGV management stated "2026 is definitely not a normal year." Travel + Leisure is responding by acquiring customer bases (Yes& Vacations closed, Spinnaker at ~$343 million signed in July 2026). Underlying resort utilization stays healthy — MVW resort occupancy is 90.5% and rental occupancy 75% — which suggests the demand base is intact and the problem is customer acquisition economics rather than a collapse in the underlying product. The growth score of 4.8 reflects a business where the top line is contracting on a deliberate repositioning, the earnings inflection is promised rather than demonstrated, and the revision trend is only mildly positive at the out-year.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of -9.4 is meaningless given the impairment-driven loss, so forward and normalized earnings are the relevant lens. At ~$94 against consensus current-year EPS of $7.55 and next-year of $8.54, VAC trades at ~12.5x current-year and ~11.1x next-year earnings. Against the peer group — HGV at 27.8x, TNL at 20.6x, CHH at 14.5x, MTN at 31.2x, with a peer median of 27.8x — VAC screens as the cheapest name in the cohort by a wide margin.

That discount is not a free lunch. The peer set trades at hotel-quality multiples; VAC trades at a distressed multiple because it carries 4.2x leverage, a B+ rating, negative tangible equity, chronic execution problems (5-year TSR of 51.4 versus a peer group at 217.5, roughly a 4x underperformance), and a full leadership transition. The relevant comparable within timeshare is HGV, and even adjusting for VAC's higher leverage and lower quality, a ~55% multiple discount is steep. A re-rating to even 15x next-year EPS ($8.54) implies ~$128; sustaining the current ~11x implies little upside from here.

The valuation score of 5.2 reflects a name that is genuinely cheap on forward earnings but where cheapness has been a persistent feature, not a temporary anomaly. History cautions against treating a low multiple as a buy signal when the discount reflects structural concerns — leverage, negative tangible equity, and unproven margin recovery — rather than a clean mispricing. The stock has already closed much of the gap between distress and recovery pricing. The remaining upside requires the H2-2026/2027 margin inflection to materialize, and the market has already begun paying for it. Analyst targets bracket the uncertainty: low $52, high $119, mean $90.3, median $96. The mean sits below the current price, which is unusual and signals that consensus does not view the stock as obviously cheap after the run.

## Competitive Landscape

MVW's competitive position is stable-to-eroding. On the positive side, resort occupancy confirms the underlying product remains desirable, and the licensed Marriott/Hyatt brand affiliation is a durable customer-acquisition engine with agreements running to 2090–2095. The Interval International exchange network is a high-margin fee franchise, though membership is declining ~2% annually.

The negatives outweigh. The 5-year TSR underperformance of roughly 4x versus peers is a chronic signal that MVW has lost ground competitively, whether through integration missteps, cost bloat, or leadership instability. The Exchange segment is shrinking. Development margins compressing in a single year, driven by rising marketing spend against falling tours, indicate that customer acquisition is becoming more expensive relative to competitors. The sector is consolidating, and MVW is not the aggressor: Travel + Leisure is buying customer bases while MVW retrenches from Asia-Pacific and low-FICO segments. The brand-license dependence is a structural vulnerability rather than an owned moat. The competitive score of 4.7 reflects a business of real scale whose trajectory is flat-to-down: healthy occupancy and durable brand access, offset by shrinking exchange membership, deteriorating unit economics, and a multi-year record of trailing its peers.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are material and only partly priced. Consumer-credit deterioration is the most direct threat: the remaining default rate feeds a ~$50 million-per-quarter reserve drag, and a further 0.5-point rise adds ~$15 million to reserves. Securitization performance triggers could redirect cash flow if breached, though all pools are currently compliant. Leverage and refinancing risk are elevated: 4.2x net leverage, a B+ rating, a $575 million 2027 convert and $350 million 2028 note wall, and a cost of debt that stepped from 0% to 6.5% on the latest refinancing. Impairment risk remains live: goodwill exceeds equity, tangible equity is negative, and any further write-down of ILG/Welk-related goodwill is possible. Geographic concentration in Florida, California, South Carolina, and Hawaii carries hurricane and wildfire exposure amid rising insurance costs.

Two developments have reduced risk. The Spanish Supreme Court overturned the 2015 contract-voiding rulings in November 2025 and January 2026, removing the principal legal basis for contract-cancellation suits — a genuine and possibly under-followed reduction in tail risk, reflected in the risk-score improvement from 3.0 to 4.8 across prior reports. And non-core asset sales ($50 million Cancún hotel closed, $200–250 million targeted by end-2027) plus continued ABS access provide deleveraging cover. The dividend is a point of tension: MVW maintains a $0.80/quarter payout (~3.3% yield, ~$220 million/year) at 4.2x leverage with negative Q1 operating cash flow, and did no buybacks in Q1 despite $322 million of remaining authorization. The risk score of 4.5 balances the eased Spain overhang against still-elevated credit, leverage, impairment, and dividend-sustainability concerns.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM IV of 58.9% sits above one-year historical volatility of 55.6% (a modest 3.3-point rich spread) and in the 85th percentile of the current chain, with an implied move of ±13.1% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (58.9% near-term versus 52.1% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst — here, the next earnings report. Put/call skew of 2.7% is balanced, and the put/call OI ratio of 0.57 is not bearish. ATM IV has collapsed from 86.9% to 58.9% since mid-June, indicating that a prior spike in expected volatility has partly resolved. The 12% short interest and 5-day cover ratio confirm elevated institutional bearishness, but not at squeeze-risk extremes. The net read: the market expects a sizeable move around the next print but is not directionally leaning short via options.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$128, +36%).** The Avril-led team executes the modernization program, development margins recover toward the low-20s% as marketing efficiency improves and the low-FICO/APAC drag rolls off, and H2 2026/2027 delivers the promised inflection. Next-year EPS of $8.54 proves conservative, the ABS market stays open, non-core sales bring leverage below 4x, and the stock re-rates toward 15x next-year earnings as the distressed discount to HGV narrows. Goldman's Buy thesis on healthy U.S. travel demand and stable resort fundamentals plays out. Spain tail risk stays resolved, and the dividend is comfortably funded by recovering cash generation.

**Bear case (~$65, -31%).** Consumer-credit stress deepens, the default rate breaks above 14% and pressures reserves and securitization triggers, and first-time-buyer softness proves more secular than cyclical. Development margins fail to recover on schedule, the "H2 gets better" story slips into 2027 and beyond, and the new team — incentivized almost entirely on stock-price thresholds but with no demonstrated track record here — cannot arrest the ~4x peer underperformance. Further goodwill impairment hits negative tangible equity, the 2027 maturity wall refinances at punitive rates, and the market reverts to pricing VAC as a distressed, over-levered operator, unwinding much of the rally.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For this HOLD:**

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Development profit margin recovers to ≥20% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the promised unit-economics inflection; (2) contract sales return to positive year-over-year growth alongside a stabilizing or falling remaining default rate below 13%; (3) clustered open-market insider buying by the new C-suite — currently absent — which would signal management conviction at these levels; or (4) evidence of deleveraging below 4.0x net debt/EBITDA from executed non-core sales without a dividend cut.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) Remaining default rate rising above 14% with any securitization pool breaching a performance trigger; (2) a further material goodwill/inventory impairment against already-negative tangible equity; (3) the H2-2026 margin recovery failing to appear in the Q3 2026 print, confirming the turnaround is slipping; or (4) a dividend cut or a distressed refinancing of the 2027 converts.

- **Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report (roughly early August 2026) is the near-term catalyst, given the options market is pricing a ±13% move and backwardation flags an imminent event. The H2 2026 prints are the decisive test of the management-promised inflection.

## Conclusion

Marriott Vacations Worldwide is a genuinely cheap timeshare operator on forward earnings, but the discount reflects real structural problems — 4.2x leverage, negative tangible equity, a B+ rating, compressing development margins, elevated consumer-credit stress, and a chronic 4x peer underperformance — rather than a clean mispricing. The bull case is coherent and has external validation (Goldman's Buy, healthy resort occupancy, eased Spain litigation, and improving next-year estimates), but it hinges almost entirely on an H2-2026/2027 margin recovery that a brand-new management team has yet to demonstrate. The stock has already rallied more than 110% off its November low, pricing in a meaningful share of that recovery, and the mean analyst target now sits below the current price.

The improving score trajectory (4.3 → 4.9 → 5.1) is warranted by the reduced Spain tail risk and the deleveraging optionality, but the recent Q1 EPS miss, still-negative operating cash flow, and the absence of any open-market insider buying at a "cheap" valuation argue against extrapolating the improvement into a BUY. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the risk/reward is roughly symmetric, and the evidence needed for a stronger call in either direction — proof that the margin inflection is real, or proof that it is slipping — will arrive with the next two prints. We would turn constructive on demonstrated development-margin recovery or a pullback that widens the discount to peers; we would turn negative on a credit-quality break or a failed H2 recovery.
