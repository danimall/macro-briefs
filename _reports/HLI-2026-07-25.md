**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Houlihan Lokey is one of the highest-quality franchises in the independent advisory space: a pure-advisory investment bank with no lending, trading, or research book, a diversified three-segment model that smooths results across M&A cycles, and a balance sheet carrying $1.36B in liquidity against zero drawn debt. At $140.23 the stock sits 29% below its 52-week high of $211.78 and near its 52-week low of $130.72, having de-rated even as FY26 revenue grew 10% to $2.62B and net income reached $426M. The drawdown reflects near-term estimate cuts (current-quarter EPS revised down 14.9% over 90 days) driven by the timing of contingent completion fees, not any structural deterioration in the franchise.

The central analytical question is whether the price decline signals a fundamental crack or an over-reaction to the inherent lumpiness of a contingent-fee business. The evidence favors the latter. All three operating segments were growing simultaneously by Q3 FY26 (Corporate Finance +12–14%, Financial Restructuring +19%, Financial & Valuation Advisory +6–8%), the balance sheet funds a coherent internally-financed roll-up strategy (three acquisitions in 18 months), and FY27 consensus EPS of $9.42 implies double-digit growth off the FY26 base. At ~15x forward FY27 earnings, HLI trades at a meaningful discount to its own historical premium multiple and below the advisory-boutique peer group, unusual for a name whose total shareholder return has consistently beaten its peers.

The recommendation is BUY, upgraded from the prior HOLD sequence and consistent with the improving score trajectory (6.3 → 6.3 → 6.7). The bar for continuing to raise conviction on a name is higher after repeated reports, but here the case is strengthened rather than repeated: the price has fallen further while the balance sheet and franchise quality are unchanged, improving the risk/reward. Position sizing should account for genuine near-term earnings-momentum headwinds and the quarter-to-quarter volatility this business structurally produces.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1972 and public since August 2015, Houlihan Lokey is a leading global independent investment bank serving over 2,000 clients annually from 30+ offices with roughly 1,900 financial professionals and 354 Managing Directors as of March 31, 2026. The business runs through three segments:

- **Corporate Finance (CF)** — M&A and capital-raising advisory, ~67% of FY26 revenue ($1.74B), 251 MDs. HLI is the most prolific sell-side advisor in the sub-$1B "mid-cap" deal segment, which it argues is underserved by bulge-bracket banks. This is the growth engine.
- **Financial Restructuring (FR)** — one of the largest restructuring and liability-management practices in the industry, ~20% of revenue ($529M), 59 MDs. FR is explicitly positioned as a counter-cyclical hedge: demand rises when M&A falls.
- **Financial & Valuation Advisory (FVA)** — fairness/solvency opinions, valuation, disputes, and tax, ~13% of revenue ($344M), 44 MDs. A reputation-driven, recurring fee-event model.

The strategic core is the CF/FR counterbalance. During the 2022–2024 M&A slump, FR and FVA cushioned results, which is the primary reason HLI has historically commanded a premium multiple to pure-M&A boutiques. The firm operates a conflict-free advisory model (no balance sheet lending or proprietary trading) and maintains broad employee ownership (~850 employee-shareholders own ~21% of equity, none more than 2%), which underpins retention.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is best-in-class for the sector. Revenue has compounded from $1.81B in FY23 to $2.62B in FY26, with net income attributable to the company rising from $254M to $426M over the same period.

| Operating income | $342M | $363M | $502M | $527M |

| Operating cash flow | — | — | $849M | $704M |

Three points stand out. First, the balance sheet is genuinely fortress-like for a financial: $1.19B cash plus $170M investment securities, roughly $1.36B liquid, against zero drawn debt and an undrawn $150M revolver. The Altman Z-score of 2.53 ("grey zone") is a model-misfit artifact — the manufacturing-oriented formula penalizes an asset-light advisory firm on working-capital-to-assets — and should be disregarded as a distress signal. ROE of 18%, ROA near 10%, and a 26% FCF margin are the accurate reads. This directly supports the highest score in this report; financial health is the single most reliable discriminator of winners in this analytical framework, and HLI's is unambiguous.

Second, the compensation ratio held steady near 64% of revenue, the key operating lever in advisory. Its stability signals disciplined pay management and is the reason operating income scaled cleanly with revenue.

Third, the FY26 segment story is more favorable than the headline. CF revenue grew 14% with segment profit up 23% on the M&A recovery. FR revenue fell 3% and profit fell 14% for the full year as the counter-cyclical hedge acted as a drag while credit markets normalized. But FR rebounded sharply intra-year: Q3 FY26 (December quarter) FR profit was +30%, and 9-month FR revenue was +10%. By Q3, all three segments were growing simultaneously — Q3 total revenue rose 13% to $717M and net income rose 22% to $117M ($1.70 diluted EPS vs. $1.39). This configuration, in which the hedge grows alongside the engine, is atypical and represents a peak-earnings-power moment.

Capital return is disciplined and well-covered. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.70 from $0.60 post-FY26. A $500M buyback is active with roughly $280M remaining as of December 2025. One caution worth flagging honestly: FY26 repurchases were executed at ~$185 average, versus the current $140 price — buying back stock at prices well above where it now trades is a value-destroying pattern, and it tempers the management-quality read.

The earnings surprise record is a genuine mixed signal. Over the trailing four quarters the firm beat three times (by 26.3%, 7.0%, and 2.9%) but missed the most recent print (March 2026) by 11.7% ($1.63 vs. $1.85). Combined with a tier-3 report of a Q1 FY27 revenue miss (single low-tier source, directional only), this argues for caution on the near-term earnings cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The medium-term growth thesis rests on the M&A recovery extending, the FR practice remaining structurally busy given elevated corporate leverage and rates, and the roll-up strategy adding bankers in new sectors and geographies. Management characterizes the environment as "relatively stable" for M&A while flagging that elevated rates, record corporate leverage, software-sector disruption, geopolitics, and trade policy sustain FR/liability-management demand over the short-to-medium term.

The acquisition cadence is coherent and repeatable, funded entirely from internal cash: Waller Helms Advisors (financial services, December 2024), Audere Partners (formerly Natixis Partners, French corporate finance, closed February 2026), and Intrepid Financial Partners (energy advisory, signed June 27, 2026, expected to close before September 2026). Each adds MDs in a distinct sector or region, extending the CF footprint without balance-sheet strain.

The offsetting reality is negative near-term earnings momentum. Estimate revisions are broadly falling: current-quarter EPS was cut 10.4% over 30 days; current-year EPS was cut 3.8%/7.3% (30d/90d) to $7.90; next-year EPS is essentially flat at $9.42 (down 0.2%/1.1%). This is the primary reason growth prospects earn only a middling score. The revisions do not meet the strong-positive threshold that would independently support a growth-driven upgrade; the near-term direction is a headwind. The pattern is consistent with contingent-fee timing rather than demand destruction — the far-out FY27 estimate ($9.42, implying ~19% growth over the depressed current-year figure) has barely moved, indicating analysts view the near-term cuts as timing, not trajectory. Two rising contingent-consideration revaluation items (WHA revalued up to $47.9M from $30M) also inflate corporate expenses and add earnings volatility that muddies quarter-to-quarter comparisons.

Applying the framework's discipline: a "cheap" multiple is treated as a value-trap warning unless there is an identifiable catalyst, because cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way. Here the catalyst is concrete — the near-term estimate cuts reflect contingent-fee timing on a business where completion-fee lumpiness is well-documented, not structural impairment, and FY27 estimates have held. This is a quality compounder that de-rated on a lumpy print rather than a broken-thesis value trap, which is what supports a moderately favorable valuation score rather than a low one. The multiple is not deeply cheap on an absolute basis, which caps the score below 7.

## Competitive Landscape

HLI competes against bulge-bracket banks with deeper capital and financing capabilities it lacks, and against a growing field of independent boutiques (Evercore, Moelis, PJT, Perella Weinberg, Lazard). The 10-K explicitly flags pricing pressure as new entrants chase share and acknowledges the structural disadvantage versus competitors who can provide financing.

The differentiation is real and durable. HLI holds a genuine leadership position in mid-cap sell-side M&A, runs one of the industry's largest restructuring practices, and operates a respected valuation/opinions franchise. The combination produces a more diversified, less-cyclical profile than pure-M&A boutiques, which is precisely what cushioned results during the last M&A slump. The roll-up strategy is expanding the addressable footprint into financial services, France, and energy.

The competitive-position score is trimmed slightly from the prior 7.5→7.0 trajectory to 6.8, reflecting two genuine erosion vectors: intensifying pricing pressure flagged in the filings, and the rising restriction on non-compete enforceability in California (HLI's HQ) and other states, which directly threatens the retention model underpinning a people-based franchise. Trajectory matters more than level in this dimension, and while HLI remains a leader, the moat's key defensive mechanism (talent lock-in) is facing structural pressure the market may not fully price. This dimension has been a weak recent predictor and should not carry disproportionate weight regardless.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are specific and, in several cases, only partially priced:

1. **Contingent/completion-fee volatility.** The majority of CF and FR revenue is tied to deal closings HLI does not control. Quarterly results are inherently lumpy, and management explicitly states a strong quarter is not predictive. This is the dominant driver of the recent estimate cuts and the stock's quarter-to-quarter behavior.
2. **The hedge cuts both ways.** In strong M&A markets FR shrinks; in downturns CF/FVA shrink. HLI cannot benefit from all regimes at once, and the "diversification" can mask softness in the dominant CF segment.
3. **Talent retention and non-compete erosion.** The franchise is its people; MD departures directly threaten client relationships. The stealth risk is that California and other states are restricting non-compete enforceability, weakening the retention model at its foundation.
4. **Litigation exposure, especially in FVA.** Fairness/solvency opinions carry material legal liability.
5. **Dual-class control and the "Final Conversion" dynamic.** The HL Voting Trust controls ~74% of voting power on ~22% economic ownership. HLI is a controlled company, so Class A holders have limited influence. As insiders convert/sell toward the 20% auto-conversion trigger, the structure approaches a mechanical flip that could unlock activist/takeover optionality but also introduce governance uncertainty and volatility. This is a slow-moving, consequential, and underappreciated thread.
6. **Client credit/bankruptcy risk** ($7.7M bad-debt expense in FY26) and **~32% international revenue** (FX swings drove a $33.8M OCI move in 9M FY26).
7. **Governance data point:** a resolved August 2023 SEC settlement with a $15M penalty over off-channel recordkeeping.

The risk score of 5.5 holds the prior improvement (4.5 → 5.5). The fortress balance sheet and conflict-free model materially reduce systemic and liquidity risk, but the concentration of quarter-to-quarter outcomes in contingent fees, the non-compete erosion, and the slow-moving control-structure catalyst are real, unpriced-to-partially-priced idiosyncratic exposures that keep this dimension in the middle of the range.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.9% sits well above the 27.6% one-year historical volatility (a rich 13.4-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (40.9% near-term vs. 35.7% at six months), which the market uses to price an imminent event — consistent with an upcoming earnings print given the lumpy-revenue profile. The put/call skew is balanced at -1.1% near-term, widening only modestly to -5.3% at six months, so there is no acute directional bearish positioning. Short interest is negligible at 3.3% of float (1.9 days to cover). ATM IV has risen 3.3 points since late June while the price barely moved, indicating rising uncertainty rather than resolved complacency. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing into the print; a better entry likely comes after the event-driven volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200–210):** The M&A recovery extends through FY27, FR demand stays elevated on record corporate leverage and refinancing walls, and the roll-up strategy (Intrepid energy advisory closing, Audere ramping) adds banker productivity. FY27 EPS reaches or exceeds consensus, and the multiple re-rates back toward the peer median (22–23x) as the near-term timing noise clears, supporting a return to the low-$200s. The improving analyst sentiment and $200 high target frame this scenario.

**Base Case ($170):** FY27 EPS lands near $9.00–$9.42, and the stock re-rates modestly to ~18–19x forward as estimate revisions stabilize and the completion-fee timing normalizes. This is ~$170, near the analyst mean of $169.75, implying roughly 21% upside from current price.

**Bear Case ($115–125):** M&A activity stalls, the near-term revenue misses compound into FY27, and the counter-cyclical hedge fails to fully offset a CF slowdown. Estimates continue to fall, the multiple compresses to ~13–14x on lower earnings, and the stock retests the low-$120s, roughly 11–18% below current price. Non-compete-driven MD attrition or a disorderly Final Conversion outcome would deepen this.

The asymmetry favors the long side: roughly 21% to base case and 43% to bull versus 11–18% downside to bear, with a fortress balance sheet and dividend support underpinning the floor.

## Conclusion

Houlihan Lokey is a high-quality, zero-debt independent advisory franchise trading at ~15x forward FY27 earnings after a 29% drawdown that reflects contingent-fee timing rather than franchise deterioration. The fortress balance sheet, 26% FCF margin, disciplined comp management, and the H2 FY26 configuration in which all three segments grew simultaneously support the quality case; the discount to peers and to the firm's own historical premium multiple, combined with improving analyst sentiment and a well-covered dividend, support the value case. The near-term estimate cuts and lumpy earnings cadence are the legitimate reasons for caution and argue for disciplined sizing and patience on entry, ideally after the rich near-term implied volatility resolves through an earnings print.

The recommendation is **BUY** with disciplined sizing. This upgrades the trajectory (HOLD → HOLD → BUY at 6.3 → 6.3 → 6.7 → 6.6) on improved risk/reward: the price fell while the balance sheet and franchise stayed intact. Financials-sector BUYs carry a negative-alpha default in this framework, so the call rests on the company-specific catalysts (M&A recovery extension, roll-up execution, multiple normalization) rather than sector beta. The strongest scoring dimension — financial health at 8.0 — is also the most reliable predictor, which raises confidence in the underlying quality even as growth momentum runs temporarily negative.
