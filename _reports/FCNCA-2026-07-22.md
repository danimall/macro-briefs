**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Citizens BancShares has transformed itself over four years, through the CIT merger (2022) and the FDIC-brokered acquisition of Silicon Valley Bridge Bank (March 2023), into a top-20 U.S. bank with $229.7B in assets, a dominant venture/PE fund-banking franchise inherited from SVB, and diversified fee streams spanning equipment leasing, factoring, and wealth. The core investment question today concerns earnings trajectory rather than balance-sheet quality, which is strong. Reported diluted EPS fell 20% in FY2025 to $165.24, and consensus expects further year-over-year declines through 2026 as the purchase-accounting accretion tailwind that flattered post-SVB results fades and net interest income compresses.

The bull-case anchor is the company's capital position and capital return: CET1 of 11.15%, a fund-banking moat that competitors cannot easily replicate, $3.0B of Class A buybacks in 2025 (roughly 11% of the share class retired), and the pending acquisition of 138 BMO branches (~$5.7B of low-cost deposits) that would shift the funding mix toward stickier retail. The bear-case anchor is concentration: capital-call lines alone are $31.8B (~21% of total loans) and grew 25% in a single year, layering the company's growth squarely into the non-depository-financial-institution (NDFI) and private-credit exposure that regulators and investors are stress-testing in 2025-2026. Accretion income has collapsed from $733M (2023) to $289M (2025), and that headwind persists as acquired loans run off.

At ~$2,061, FCNCA trades at ~11.7x forward consensus EPS of $176.87 and ~1.0x tangible book, a discount to large-cap bank peers trading at 12-15x. That discount is partly deserved: declining reported earnings, an NDFI concentration the market is repricing across the sector, and a $33.4B Purchase Money Note maturing in March 2028. We rate the stock HOLD. The valuation is reasonable and the balance sheet is durable, but with the current-quarter EPS estimate having fallen 6.3% over 90 days and no near-term catalyst to close the multiple gap, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

First Citizens is a Raleigh, NC-based financial holding company operating through First-Citizens Bank & Trust Company. It is family-influenced (the Holding family controls voting power through high-vote Class B shares) and has a long record of value creation via disciplined acquisitions. The current structure reflects three transformational transactions:

- **CIT Merger (January 2022):** Added equipment leasing, commercial finance, factoring/supply-chain finance, and the Rail lessor business.
- **SVBB Acquisition (March 27, 2023):** Acquired substantially all loans and deposits of Silicon Valley Bridge Bank from the FDIC, generating a one-time $9.81B bargain purchase gain and nearly doubling the balance sheet. This event defines the recent financial history and must be stripped out of any earnings comparison.

The company reports three segments (FY2025 net income):
- **General Bank ($1,175M NI)** — Consumer/small-business branch banking, residential mortgage, wealth/private banking. $65.0B loans, $74.8B deposits.
- **Commercial Bank ($975M NI)** — Middle-market lending, leasing, factoring, and the legacy SVB "innovation economy" franchise: Global Fund Banking (capital-call lines), Technology & Healthcare Banking, and investor-dependent lending. $82.9B loans.
- **Rail ($87M NI)** — Owns and leases railcars/locomotives (~$8.9B of operating-lease equipment). Long-duration, cyclical, capital-intensive.

The company occupies an unusual position: simultaneously a Southeast super-regional retail bank, the de facto successor to SVB's fund-banking franchise, and an equipment lessor/factoring house. This makes FCNCA more of a levered play on the health of the private-capital ecosystem than a typical regional bank.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings normalization is the central story.** Reported net income has stepped down as the SVB gain rolls off:
- FY2023: $11,466M NI (includes the $9.81B non-taxable acquisition gain), not repeatable.
- FY2024: $2,777M NI; diluted EPS $189.41.
- FY2025: $2,206M NI; diluted EPS $165.24.

**Net interest income is the concerning line.** NII fell from $7,143M (2024) to $6,814M (2025), down 4.6%, reflecting elevated deposit costs and declining purchase-accounting discount accretion. Accretion income fell from $505M (2024) to $289M (2025). As acquired loans run off, this benefit shrinks further, and it explains most of the reported NII decline and much of why consensus expects EPS to keep falling despite loan growth.

**Profitability is solid but unexceptional for the size.** ROA is roughly 0.96% and ROE roughly 10%. Total revenue (NII + noninterest income) was ~$9.54B in 2025 versus $9.76B in 2024. (The ~$1.4B "revenue" figure appearing in standardized data pulls is a mis-tagged line item and should be disregarded; actual NII is $6.8B and total revenue ~$9.5B.) Efficiency deteriorated: personnel expense rose to $3,294M from $3,078M, and total noninterest expense grew 5.6% to $6,056M.

**Balance sheet and capital are the genuine strength.** BancShares CET1 was 11.15% and total risk-based capital 13.71%, comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds, though CET1 fell from 12.99% in 2024 as buybacks consumed capital. Loans grew 5.5% to $147.9B, driven almost entirely by capital-call lines (+25% to $31.8B). Deposits rose 4.1% to $161.6B, with a healthy $40.7B (25%) noninterest-bearing. The allowance for credit losses is $1.57B (1.06% of loans), down $110M YoY, partly because low-loss capital-call lines grew as a share of the book, a legitimate mix effect but also the reason one external analyst flagged the portfolio as potentially "under-reserved."

**Capital allocation is aggressive and financially engineered.** Operating cash flow was ~$2.9B. The company retired $3.0B of Class A stock in 2025 (1.58M shares, ~11% of the class), on top of $1.65B in 2024, while issuing preferred (Series D $500M at 7.00%, Series E $400M at 6.625%) and sub/senior debt. This boosts EPS and ROE optically but drew CET1 down roughly 180 bps and adds fixed-cost obligations. Total share count fell from 15.5M (2022) to ~12.1M total (2025).

**The Purchase Money Note** — a $36.07B, 3.5% fixed note payable to the FDIC — matures March 27, 2028, with ~$33.4B outstanding at YE2025 after a $2.49B prepayment. It is a large, low-cost but concentrated funding source; repayment or refinancing at market rates ahead of 2028 is a structural funding-cost headwind.

The earnings-surprise track record is reassuring on execution: the company beat in three of the last four quarters, including a 10.5% beat in Q1 2026 ($44.86 vs. $40.59 estimate). This supports confidence in near-term consensus even as the year-over-year trend declines.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are muted on a reported basis and mixed on the underlying drivers. Consensus expects current-year EPS of $176.87 and next-year of $200.42, a return to growth in 2027, but the current-quarter estimate of $40.16 is down meaningfully year over year, and Zacks' Q2 preview (~$40.45, down ~9.7% YoY) is consistent with continued near-term declines driven by accretion runoff and NIM compression.

The 90-day estimate revision picture is mixed rather than clearly positive: current-quarter estimates fell 6.3% over 90 days (though up 1.1% over the last 30), next-quarter fell 1.5%, current-year is essentially flat (+0.7%), and next-year fell 0.8%. This does not meet the threshold of a strong forward earnings-momentum signal; it reads as stabilization near a trough.

The organic growth that exists is disproportionately concentrated. Capital-call lines added $6.3B in 2025 and now represent the largest single driver of loan growth. This book carries very low historical losses (zero nonaccruals) because repayment depends on contractual LP capital commitments, but its growth deepens a single concentration. The rest of the loan book grew modestly.

The clearest forward catalyst is the **BMO branch acquisition** announced October 16, 2025: 138 branches across the Midwest, Great Plains, and West, assuming ~$5.7B of deposits and ~$1.1B of loans, expected to close in 2H 2026 subject to regulatory approval. Strategically, this diversifies the funding base toward stable low-cost retail deposits, a sensible offset to the wholesale-heavy, fund-banking-concentrated balance sheet. It is a deposit-franchise acquisition rather than an earnings-accretive growth engine, so the near-term EPS impact is modest.

## Valuation Assessment

FCNCA trades at ~11.9x trailing and ~11.7x forward consensus EPS, and roughly 1.0x tangible book value (equity ~$22.2B against a $21.8B market cap, adjusted for the ~$1.16B unrealized HTM loss not reflected in equity). This is a discount to large-cap and super-regional bank peers, which generally trade in the 12-15x forward range. The analyst target set — low $2,050, mean $2,258, median $2,305, high $2,475 — implies mid-single-digit to ~10% upside to the mean.

The valuation case is two-sided. The discount reflects three legitimate concerns that the market is not wrong to price: (1) declining reported earnings as accretion fades, a real, mechanical NII drag; (2) NDFI/capital-call concentration that is being repriced sector-wide amid heightened regulatory and investor scrutiny of bank exposure to private credit; and (3) the 2028 Purchase Money Note refinancing overhang. A low P/E on a bank whose reported EPS is contracting and whose fastest-growing loan bucket sits in the exact category the market is stress-testing is a discount that has to be earned back through execution rather than one that self-corrects.

The offset is real book-value support and a durable capital-return program. At ~1.0x tangible book with ROE near 10% and a well-capitalized balance sheet, downside is cushioned. The buyback continues to retire shares at reasonable multiples, mechanically supporting EPS. The BMO deal, once closed, improves deposit mix and funding cost. On normalized 2027 earnings power (~$200 consensus), the stock trades near 10x, a full turn or two below where it should trade if the accretion drag proves to be a trough and the NDFI concern proves overblown.

On balance, this warrants a moderate valuation score. The stock is not expensive, but the discount is defensible given the earnings direction and concentration profile, and there is no identifiable catalyst that forces a re-rating within the 6-12 month window. Absolute yield support is thin: the dividend yield is only ~0.4%, so this is not an income story.

## Competitive Landscape

FCNCA's competitive position is stable-to-improving at the franchise level but concentrated. Its fund-banking moat — capital-call lines and innovation-economy lending inherited from SVB — is genuine and difficult to replicate; few banks have the underwriting capability and client relationships in venture/PE fund finance. That franchise now competes with remnants of the SVB model dispersed across other banks and private lenders, and with an industry that has learned the lessons of 2023. The stable, low-cost Southeast retail deposit base (soon augmented by the BMO branches) is a durable funding advantage, and the CIT-legacy leasing/factoring businesses provide diversified, cycle-varied fee streams.

The trajectory signal is mixed. Loan growth suggests the company is taking share in fund banking, a positive competitive signal. But that same growth increases the company's beta to the private-capital ecosystem, and NII compression is a symptom of both the accretion runoff and industry-wide deposit-cost pressure; the latter is not company-specific competitive weakness, but it does cap the segment's economics near-term. The franchise is well-positioned but not obviously widening its overall moat; the score reflects a solid, defensible position without a clear share-gain-driven re-rating case at the consolidated level.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NDFI / private-credit / capital-call concentration (the dominant risk).** Capital-call lines represent ~26% of commercial loans and are growing 25% annually. Beyond that, C&I includes NAV loans, leveraged fund lines to private-credit funds, warehouse lines, and specialty finance to first-lien lenders. This is precisely the bank-to-NDFI exposure under regulatory and investor scrutiny in 2025-2026. Repayment depends on LP capital commitments (currently zero nonaccruals), but a severe VC/PE downturn or LP funding stress is a tail risk, and reserve adequacy has been questioned externally.

**2. Innovation-economy / investor-dependent credit.** The legacy-SVB investor-dependent book ($2.8B) carries elevated charge-offs ($125M in 2025, a 4.5% annualized rate) and $500M of combined substandard/doubtful classifications. Small in absolute terms but the highest-loss portfolio.

**3. Commercial real estate.** $23.8B of CRE with $1.36B substandard/doubtful, $418M nonaccrual, and $157M of 2025 collateral-dependent write-downs. An industry-wide watch item, particularly office.

**4. AOCI / securities rate risk.** HTM securities carry a $1.16B unrealized loss (fair value $8.49B vs. $9.65B amortized cost) not reflected in equity. AFS marks improved in 2025 (AOCI swung to +$83M from -$445M), but a rate back-up would pressure tangible book.

**5. Purchase Money Note maturity (2028) and fading accretion.** Both are structural earnings/funding headwinds. Refinancing the note at market rates versus its current 3.5% coupon would raise funding cost materially.

**6. Governance/control.** The dual-class structure (Class B = 16 votes/share) concentrates voting power with the founding family, limiting outside shareholder influence. A January 14, 2026 Item 5.02 executive departure (and a June 2025 change) are worth monitoring for succession/continuity.

A partial mitigant: the termination of the FDIC Shared-Loss Agreement (April 7, 2025) removed downside protection on ~$60B of covered SVB assets with no P&L impact, signaling management confidence in the acquired book's credit. Still, the combination of concentrated, fast-growing NDFI exposure that the market is actively repricing, plus a large 2028 funding cliff, justifies a below-average risk score; these are specific, potentially material downside catalysts not fully reflected at a 1.0x tangible book multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 32.8% sits above the 27.8% one-year historical vol (a ~4.9% rich spread) and in the 59th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (32.8% near-term vs. 28.4% at six months), consistent with the earnings window. Put/call skew is balanced (1.5%) and the put/call volume ratio (0.67) and OI ratio (0.87) are not bearishly extreme. The implied one-month move is ±7.8%. Short interest is modest at 5.7% of float (5.7 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish setup. The read: elevated but not one-sided uncertainty into the next print, with no directional conviction embedded in positioning, consistent with a balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($2,300-2,475):** Accretion runoff proves to be a trough rather than a permanent decline; loan growth (led by the durable fund-banking franchise) and the BMO deposit acquisition stabilize and then re-grow NII into 2027, when consensus already sees ~$200 EPS. Continued buybacks at ~1.0x tangible book compound per-share value. The NDFI concentration proves benign (contractual LP-backed repayment holds through a soft private-capital cycle), and the market re-rates the stock toward peer multiples of 13-14x on normalized earnings. At 12x $200 = $2,400.

**Bear Case ($1,600-1,750):** A private-capital downturn stresses the capital-call and investor-dependent books, forcing reserve builds against a portfolio some consider under-reserved. Accretion and NIM compression continue, keeping reported EPS in decline. The 2028 Purchase Money Note refinancing at market rates raises funding cost. CET1, already down 180 bps on buybacks, constrains capital return. The stock retests its 52-week low near $1,624, roughly 21% below current levels.

**Base Case (~$2,150-2,250):** Earnings stabilize near a trough, the BMO deal closes and modestly improves the funding mix, buybacks continue, and the stock grinds toward the analyst mean of ~$2,258 as consensus normalizes on 2027 earnings power. Roughly 4-9% upside, offset by a comparable bear-case drawdown; balanced risk/reward.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (balanced risk/reward without a near-term catalyst to force a re-rating).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that NII has troughed — two consecutive quarters of stable or rising net interest income excluding accretion, confirming that the accretion drag is offset by loan growth and repricing.
- BMO deal closes on terms that visibly improve deposit cost and funding mix, with management guiding to accretion.
- Estimate revisions turn decisively positive (current-quarter and next-year both rising >2% over 90 days), or the stock pulls back toward tangible book (~$1,850-1,900), improving the risk/reward asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Reserve build or rising nonaccruals in the capital-call / investor-dependent books, validating the "under-reserved" concern.
- CRE deterioration accelerating beyond the current $418M nonaccrual and $157M annual write-down pace.
- A capital-return pace that draws CET1 below ~10.5%, or a materially unfavorable refinancing of the Purchase Money Note.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report is the immediate checkpoint, followed by the expected 2H 2026 close of the BMO acquisition. The Purchase Money Note maturity (March 2028) is the key structural date to monitor over the medium term.

## Conclusion

First Citizens is a well-run, well-capitalized top-20 bank with a genuine fund-banking moat and a shareholder-friendly capital-return program. The problem for the equity is that reported earnings are declining as the SVB purchase-accounting accretion tailwind fades, and the discount valuation is largely justified by that earnings direction plus a fast-growing NDFI concentration the market is actively repricing across the sector. The balance sheet cushions downside, and the BMO deposit deal and buybacks provide support, but there is no identifiable catalyst to close the peer-multiple gap within the next 6-12 months, and current-quarter estimates remain below levels of 90 days ago. We rate FCNCA HOLD and would become more constructive on confirmation that NII has troughed, on a clean BMO close, or on a pullback toward tangible book that restores asymmetry to the risk/reward.
