## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG is a pure-play VLGC operator with 25 vessels (as of March 31, 2025) generating $206M in adjusted EBITDA at cyclically depressed rates ($39,778/day TCE vs. the prior-year peak of $62,129). The stock has appreciated ~95% from its May 2025 low of ~$20 to $39.04, driven by improving rate forward indicators and analyst estimate revisions. Next-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up significantly over recent months according to market data vendors, signaling a rate recovery cycle accelerating faster than consensus expected as recently as February 2026.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a balance sheet with substantial cash reserves, predominantly fixed/hedged debt, and fleet value exceeding book by approximately $590M (~$14/share as of March 2025); (2) a structural demand story driven by US shale LPG exports and Asian PDH plant buildout; and (3) a valuation that remains attractive relative to mid-cycle earnings power. The primary risk is the VLGC orderbook (approximately 111 vessels, ~27% of fleet) that could suppress rates through 2027, compounded by US-China tariff disruptions to the dominant trade lane. However, the pace of estimate revisions, the CEO's open-market purchase at $27 in January 2026, and improving FY2026 results (nine-month net income of $112.7M through December 2025) tilt the probability distribution toward continued upside.

## Company Overview

Dorian LPG operates one of the world's largest fleets of Very Large Gas Carriers. As of the FY2025 10-K (March 31, 2025), the fleet comprised 25 VLGCs with aggregate capacity of approximately 2.1 million cbm. The fleet has since expanded — the company's FY2026 Q3 filing (as of January 31, 2026) reported twenty-seven VLGC carriers with aggregate capacity of approximately 2.3 million cbm, and a March 2026 press release described twenty-eight modern VLGCs, including six dual-fuel ECO VLGCs, twenty ECO VLGCs, and two modern VLGCs. As of January 31, 2026, the fleet averaged 10.0 years — still younger than the global VLGC average of approximately 11.4 years. Sixteen vessels are scrubber-equipped, providing a fuel cost advantage over non-scrubber peers.

All vessels operate in the Helios Pool, a 50/50 joint venture with MOL Energia (a subsidiary of Mitsui OSK Lines). As of March 31, 2025, the Pool operated 29 VLGCs. Revenue is allocated by pool points tied to vessel attributes and on-hire days. This structure means Dorian is overwhelmingly spot-rate exposed — 97% of FY2025 revenue derived from the Helios Pool with no meaningful time charter coverage. The company is headquartered in the U.S. (Stamford, CT) and incorporated in the Marshall Islands.

The dominant trade route is US Gulf-to-Asia via the Panama Canal, carrying propane and butane primarily to petrochemical feedstock buyers and residential heating/cooking markets in India, China, Japan, and Southeast Asia.

FY2025 represents a cyclical trough: revenue declined 37% YoY as the Baltic LPG Index (Ras Tanura-Chiba) averaged $57.95/MT versus $104.95/MT in FY2024. Dorian's FY2026 results through December 31, 2025 already show meaningful recovery: nine-month revenue of $328.2M, net income of $112.7M, adjusted EBITDA of $198.5M, and Q3 FY2026 TCE of $50,333/day. The key question is whether FY2027 (ending March 2028) sustains this recovery trajectory or whether orderbook deliveries create renewed rate pressure.

**Balance Sheet Strength (as of December 31, 2025):**
- Cash: $294.5M
- Long-term debt (net of current portion and deferred fees): $415.4M; current debt: $97.7M
- Current ratio: approximately 2.4x
- D/E: approximately 0.47x (based on total debt of ~$513M vs. equity of $1.086B)
- Fleet market value exceeded book by approximately $590M as of March 31, 2025 (~$14/share unrealized NAV)

The balance sheet is defensively positioned. Even at trough rates in FY2025, Dorian generated $154M in free cash flow (45% FCF margin), demonstrating the fleet's low operating leverage at the vessel level. Approximately 93% of debt is hedged or fixed.

**Capital Return:**
- Most recent declared dividend: $0.70/share (January 2026), payable February 2026
- Dorian's dividends are explicitly irregular and subject to board discretion; annualizing any single quarter's dividend can be misleading
- $100M buyback authorized; only $5.6M utilized
- Dividend trajectory: $1.00/qtr → $0.70 → $0.50 → $0.70, tracking rate fluctuations

The dividend is variable and directly linked to spot earnings. At mid-cycle rates (~$50,000/day TCE), the company could comfortably support $3.00-3.50/share in annual dividends based on historical payout patterns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.04 with trailing FY2025 EPS of $2.14, the trailing P/E is approximately **18.2x** — reflecting trough earnings rather than normalized earning power. More relevant forward metrics:

- **Forward P/E on FY2027E ($4.33):** 9.0x
- **Forward P/E on FY2028E ($3.82):** 10.2x
- **EV/EBITDA on FY2025 ($206M):** approximately 9.3x (using $1.67B market cap + $513M debt - $295M cash ≈ $1.9B EV)
- **Price/NAV:** Fleet value exceeds book by ~$590M (as of March 2025); adjusted NAV approximately $1.64B or ~$38/share — stock trades near 1.0x adjusted NAV

**Peer Context:** Direct peer comparisons are complicated by differences in fleet type, contract coverage, and business model. International Seaways (INSW) is sometimes cited as a comparable, though it is primarily a crude/product tanker company rather than a VLGC pure-play. Dorian's spot exposure and cyclicality justify some valuation discount to peers with more time charter coverage.

**Valuation Floor:** At worst-case sustained low rates, fleet liquidation value provides a floor around $28-30/share based on the excess of market over book value plus cash minus debt.

The stock is not cheap relative to trough earnings (18.2x trailing), but it is attractively valued relative to the rate recovery already visible in FY2026 results. At 9x forward P/E with estimates rising materially, the market has not fully priced the cyclical recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian ranks among the top 4-5 global VLGC operators. Competitive advantages:

1. **Fleet youth/efficiency:** 10.0-year average age (as of January 2026) vs. ~11.4-year global average; 16 scrubber-equipped vessels
2. **Helios Pool scale:** Pooling arrangement provides scheduling flexibility, cargo access, and utilization optimization
3. **Ammonia capability:** "Areion" (93,000 cbm VLGC/AC) provides first-mover positioning in a nascent market
4. **Balance sheet flexibility:** ~$295M cash enables opportunistic fleet growth during cyclical troughs

Competitive risks:
- BW LPG is larger and more diversified (product trading arm)
- New entrants via the large orderbook could increase competition and compress margins
- Dorian's lack of time charter coverage means it outperforms in strong markets but underperforms in weak ones relative to peers with contract backlogs

The competitive position is stable but not improving — the orderbook represents the primary threat to rates and, by extension, to Dorian's relative position.

## Risk Assessment

**1. VLGC Orderbook Oversupply (HIGH):** Fleet growth could overwhelm demand growth through 2027-2029. This is the central bear case and the reason the stock deserves a cyclical discount. Delivery slippages and ammonia-capable diversion partially mitigate but do not eliminate this risk.

**2. US-China Tariff Disruption (MODERATE-HIGH):** China extended a 10% tariff on US-sourced propane and butane for a further 12 months. Since the US Gulf-to-Asia route is the dominant VLGC trade lane, sustained tariffs could redirect trade flows and reduce ton-miles. Separately, USTR port fees on Chinese-built vessels were implemented in October 2025 but subsequently suspended for one year beginning November 10, 2025 (along with China's retaliatory port-service fees). Dorian stated it does not expect itself or its charterers to be materially impacted by such port fees at this time, though trade relations remain volatile. Two of Dorian's time-chartered vessels are owned by a Chinese financial institution, creating potential exposure if the suspension lapses.

**3. Earnings Volatility (STRUCTURAL):** Three misses in the last four quarters (-7.4%, +1.2%, -61.4%, -67.2%). The Q1-Q2 FY2025 misses were massive. This volatility is inherent to spot-exposed shipping and makes forward estimates inherently uncertain despite their recent upward trajectory.

**4. Panama Canal Constraints:** >90% of US-to-Asia LPG transits use the Canal. Toll increases, slot limitations, and drought risk add costs. Already partially embedded in rates.

**5. EU ETS / Environmental Costs:** Escalating from 40% to 100% of emissions coverage by 2026. Quantifiable operating cost increase, partially offset by scrubber fleet advantage.

## Conclusion

Dorian LPG presents a favorable cyclical entry point. The stock trades at approximately 9x forward earnings with estimates revised materially higher over recent months — a combination that historically precedes further re-rating in cyclical names. The ~$295M cash balance and fleet value premium provide meaningful downside protection, while the CEO's open-market purchase at $27 in January 2026 suggests management confidence in the rate recovery.

The specific risk preventing a higher-conviction call is the ~27% VLGC orderbook overhang, which creates genuine uncertainty about whether the rate recovery can be sustained through 2027-2028. Additionally, the earnings track record (3 misses in 4 quarters) warrants caution on the reliability of forward estimates. However, actual FY2026 results already demonstrating recovery, combined with structural demand growth from US LPG exports, tilt the probability distribution toward the base-to-bull range.

BUY with 2-3% position sizing, acknowledging the elevated cyclical volatility inherent in a spot-rate-exposed shipping name.

Additionally, estimate-revision claims central to the thesis could not be independently validated from primary sources and should be treated as indicative rather than authoritative.
