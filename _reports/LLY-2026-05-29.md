**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is delivering one of the largest-scale growth accelerations in pharmaceutical history. Revenue rose from $45.0B in 2024 to $65.2B in 2025 (+45%), Q1 2026 revenue grew 56% YoY to $19.8B, and management raised FY2026 guidance by $2B to $82-85B, implying roughly 28% growth at the midpoint. The engine is the incretin (GLP-1/GIP) franchise — tirzepatide marketed as Mounjaro (diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity) — which moved from 56% of 2025 revenue to 65% in Q1 2026. Diluted EPS grew 96% in 2025 to $22.95 and 170% in Q1 2026 to $8.26. The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters, most recently by 27%, and current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 5.7% in the past 30 days.

The investment debate is what is priced in, not whether Lilly is a great business. It plainly is, with 83.0% gross margins, 77.8% ROE, and a next-generation pipeline (oral orforglipron/Foundayo, triple-agonist retatrutide delivering ~28% weight loss in TRIUMPH-1) that few peers can match. At ~$1,105, the stock trades at roughly 30.5x consensus current-year EPS of $36.18 and ~24.8x next-year $44.47. For a company growing earnings near 28% with rising estimates and a 4-for-4 beat record, that multiple sits below the growth rate (PEG roughly 1.1 on forward), which does not meet the definition of an expensive stock. The prior decision to hold this name at a 6.9 overall score is inconsistent with the framework: a high-growth name scoring above the actionable threshold with positive estimate revisions and a reasonable forward multiple is a BUY.

The genuine risks are specific: single-franchise concentration (tirzepatide is 65% of revenue), accelerating price erosion (consolidated realized price -13% in Q1 2026), and a converging set of government-pricing pressures (IRA selections for Trulicity and Verzenio in 2028, the November 2025 U.S. pricing agreement, China NRDL pricing). These cap conviction and warrant a measured position size, but they do not invalidate the thesis at the current price.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a ~150-year-old Indianapolis-based pharmaceutical company operating as a single human-pharmaceuticals segment across ~90 countries with roughly 50,000 employees (~12,000 in R&D). It is organized around four therapeutic areas: Cardiometabolic Health, Oncology, Immunology, and Neuroscience.

The defining asset is the incretin franchise. Tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, is widely regarded as the most efficacious approved GLP-1, giving Lilly a clinical edge over Novo Nordisk's semaglutide. The top-6 portfolio (Mounjaro, Zepbound, Verzenio, Trulicity, Taltz, Jardiance) was 82% of 2025 revenue. Beyond the marketed franchise, Lilly is building a deep next-generation pipeline: orforglipron (oral GLP-1, brand Foundayo, FDA-approved April 2026, licensed from Chugai), retatrutide (triple agonist), eloralintide (amylin), insulin efsitora, donanemab (Kisunla, Alzheimer's), imlunestrant, olomorasib, and pirtobrutinib.

The company is investing heavily in manufacturing scale (new sites in NC, WI, IN, VA, TX, AL, PA, Ireland, Germany, Netherlands) and has built a direct-to-patient channel, LillyDirect, to capture cash-pay demand and bypass PBM friction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** The growth trajectory is exceptional in both rate and absolute scale: $28.5B (2022) → $34.1B (2023) → $45.0B (2024) → $65.2B (2025). EPS of $8.26 in Q1 2026 was up from $3.06. Net income was $20.6B in 2025 (+95%).

**Margins and returns.** Gross margin reached 83.0% in 2025 (up from 81.3%), with Q1 2026 at 81.9%. Net margin is ~31.7%, ROE 77.8%, and ROA 18.4% — elite by any standard.

**A note on screening artifacts.** Automated financial-health screens flagged an Altman Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress zone") and a negative operating margin. Both are false signals. Lilly generated $25.7B of pretax income on $65.2B of revenue; the negative EBIT/assets component is a classification quirk (likely acquired-IPR&D and "other" charges miscaptured), and the low Z-Score reflects thin book equity relative to a leveraged, buyback-heavy capital structure, not operating fragility. This is among the most profitable large-cap businesses in the market.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is $43.4B (Q1 2026), with book D/E of ~4.2x because equity is structurally thin from buybacks (equity grew to $31.2B by Q1 2026). Cash is modest at $5.3B, with the company relying on operating cash flow plus commercial paper. The current ratio of 1.58 is adequate. The leverage is appropriate for a business generating $16.8B of operating cash flow with 83% gross margins; it is not a refinancing-risk story.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $16.8B in 2025. FCF was $9.0B, with the 13.8% FCF margin deliberately compressed by a capex ramp to $7.8B (from $5.1B in 2024) to build incretin capacity. Reported FCF therefore understates underlying earnings power. The dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized), and $2.3B was repurchased in Q1 2026 ($8.6B remaining on the $15B program). Working capital has swelled — inventories of $13.7B, ~$12-13B of prepaid taxes, and rebate/discount liabilities near $15B — reflecting the scale and rebate intensity of the U.S. incretin business.

The 4-for-4 beat record (27.0%, 11.9%, 22.0%, 14.5% over the last four quarters) supports above-average confidence in forward estimates. The one caveat: Q1 2026 was flattered by a favorable one-time adjustment to rebate/discount estimates on Mounjaro/Zepbound, so sustainability of the magnitude of beats bears watching.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has the highest possible durability characteristics. EPS estimate revisions are firmly positive: current-quarter EPS revised +9.0% over 30 days and +8.8% over 90 days; current-year +5.7% (30d) and +4.8% (90d); next-year +5.6% (30d). The combination of revision velocity, revenue growth, and a guidance raise is the strongest forward profile in large-cap pharma.

Three drivers underpin durability:

1. **Incretin volume.** Tirzepatide is scaling globally with new indications (OSA approved) and a manufacturing build-out designed to relieve supply constraints that previously capped demand capture.

2. **Oral GLP-1 (orforglipron/Foundayo).** Oral dosing dramatically expands the addressable market and eases the manufacturing bottleneck inherent to injectables. The strategic importance is high. The live debate is competitive: Lilly lost first-mover position to Novo's oral semaglutide (approved ~3 months earlier), and early prescriber-uptake data is contested. This is the single most important near-term swing factor.

3. **Retatrutide.** The TRIUMPH-1 Phase 3 readout of ~28% average weight loss at the highest dose (with 45% of patients losing ≥30%) approaches bariatric-surgery territory and resets the efficacy benchmark. This extends the franchise into the next product cycle and supports the long-duration bull case even as pricing pressure builds on legacy products.

The obesity TAM remains structurally enormous and underpenetrated, and Lilly's pipeline breadth across obesity, T2D, CV outcomes, OSA, and hypertension means the growth story does not depend on a single molecule's launch curve.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,105, Lilly trades at ~30.5x consensus current-year EPS of $36.18 and ~24.8x next-year EPS of $44.47. With consensus growth near 28% and rising, the forward PEG is roughly 1.0-1.1.

Against pharmaceutical peers, the headline multiple looks rich: peer median P/E is 20.0 (JNJ 26.8, MRK 33.6, PFE 20.0, BMY 16.1). But that comparison is unhelpful because none of those peers is growing earnings at anywhere near 28%, and several face declining or flat trajectories. The relevant frame for a stock with this estimate revision support and accelerating revenue is forward/growth-adjusted, not trailing P/E against slow-growth peers. On that basis the stock is reasonably valued: a 24.8x next-year multiple on a business compounding earnings near 25-28% with 83% gross margins and an unmatched pipeline is not stretched.

This is also a case where analyst targets function as floors rather than ceilings. The mean target is $1,215 and median $1,251, both above the current price, with a high of $1,500. With estimates rising, those targets are stale and likely to be revised upward as consensus catches up to delivered results.

The market is mispricing something specific: it is anchoring on the pricing-erosion narrative and the contested oral-GLP-1 launch, applying a discount that the volume trajectory and pipeline breadth do not yet justify. The stock is below its 52-week high of $1,149 even as forward estimates have risen, which is improving risk/reward. I score valuation 5.5 — not higher, because the multiple genuinely embeds continued execution and leaves limited margin for a demand or pricing disappointment, but the price offers asymmetric upside given the growth and quality.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity/GLP-1 market is effectively a Lilly-Novo Nordisk duopoly. Lilly holds the efficacy lead via tirzepatide and a deeper next-generation pipeline (oral orforglipron, triple-agonist retatrutide, amylin eloralintide). Novo's stock fell ~70% off highs per recent commentary, repositioning the relative bull/bear debate in Lilly's favor.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable: Lilly is widening its efficacy moat, building a direct-to-patient channel that bypasses PBM friction, and scaling manufacturing to convert latent demand into revenue. The main competitive watch items are the contested oral-GLP-1 launch versus Novo and the persistent threat from mass-compounded and counterfeit tirzepatide. Both companies were fined by French regulators in May 2026 over obesity-drug promotion, a sign of intensifying marketing competition and scrutiny.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where conviction is bounded, and it justifies the 5.0 risk score.

**Product concentration.** Any safety signal, demand shock, supply disruption, or access setback in tirzepatide hits results disproportionately. Management explicitly flags that incretin channel dynamics can have a "disproportionate impact" in any period.

**Pricing and government policy.** This is the dominant emerging risk and it is converging precisely as Lilly's biggest products scale. Consolidated realized price fell 6% in 2025 and 13% in Q1 2026 (China NRDL drove OUS price -25%). The November 2025 voluntary U.S. government agreement (finalized Q1 2026) commits Lilly to lower Medicaid and certain prices, more balanced developed-nation launch pricing, and a Medicare GLP-1 "Bridge Program," in exchange for a 3-year Section 232 tariff grace period. IRA price-setting hit Jardiance in 2026 (-66%) and selected Trulicity and Verzenio for 2028, with management expecting additional selections. CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound as a preferred obesity drug on some plans in July 2025, a concrete demonstration of PBM/formulary power. The thesis depends on volume continuing to overwhelm price cuts.

**IP/LOE.** Trulicity loses U.S. protection in the next few years; tirzepatide's compound patent runs to 2036 (U.S.) but data protection expires 2027, exposing it to IP challenges.

**Manufacturing execution.** Bidirectional risk: underbuilding caps growth, while overbuilding (with up to ~$10B in take-or-pay supply commitments) risks stranded capacity if demand disappoints or a competitor disrupts.

**Litigation.** Two federal MDLs over alleged GI injuries and NAION tied to the franchise, with Lilly predominantly self-insured. Recent appellate losses (Emgality patent reversal, AMP qui tam) generated charges in Q1 2026; monitor, but not thesis-changing yet.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation, with near-term ATM IV of 40.1% above the 6-month tenor at 34.3%, indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk. The 1-week skew is heavily distorted (232.9%) by very low OI and concentrated single-day call activity (the $1105 call shows 54.9x volume/OI), so it is noise rather than informed positioning. More telling: the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.70 to 0.49 over the past few sessions as the stock rose 3.8%, and the 1-month and 3-month skews are roughly balanced to slightly call-tilted. IV sits fair to historical vol (40.1% vs 37.9% HV, +2.3pp spread). Short interest is negligible at 0.9% of float (2.4 days to cover). The options market is not signaling a bearish setup; it is pricing event-driven near-term uncertainty consistent with pipeline/launch catalysts, with no put-skew warning to contradict the long thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,500).** Volume continues to outrun price erosion; orforglipron's oral profile expands the addressable market and uptake stabilizes against Novo; retatrutide's surgery-level efficacy resets the benchmark and extends the franchise into the next cycle. Estimates continue to revise upward, and the stock re-rates as consensus catches up. At ~33x a next-year EPS approaching $45+, this implies the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case ($850).** Pricing erosion accelerates faster than volume can offset; the U.S. government deal and IRA selections compress realized prices; orforglipron loses the oral race to Novo and uptake disappoints; the Q1 rebate adjustment proves non-recurring and beats normalize lower. The stock derates toward 19x next-year EPS, ~23% below the current price. This maps to the analyst low of $850.

**Base Case ($1,300).** Volume growth remains robust enough to keep net revenue compounding in the high-teens-to-twenties despite double-digit price declines; orforglipron carves out a meaningful oral share even as the second mover; retatrutide advances on schedule. ~29x next-year EPS of ~$44.50 = ~$1,290-1,300. Estimates drift higher and analyst targets follow.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $1,300.** ~29x next-year consensus EPS of $44.47, sitting between the bear ($850) and bull ($1,500) cases. Upside of ~18% from $1,105.
- **Bull case target: $1,500.** ~+36% from current.
- **Bear case target: $850.** ~-23% from current.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to: orforglipron/Foundayo launch trajectory data through 2H 2026; the July 1, 2026 Bridge Program access milestone; quarterly prints (next ~late July 2026) testing whether volume continues to outrun price; and retatrutide regulatory progress.
- **Upside/downside:** +18% to base, +36% to bull, -23% to bear. The asymmetry is favorable given rising estimates and a forward PEG near 1.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Consolidated realized price decline accelerates beyond ~-15% YoY for two consecutive quarters without offsetting volume growth keeping net revenue growth above ~15%.
2. Orforglipron prescription share clearly stalls below Novo's oral product through 2H 2026 launch data, signaling the oral franchise is structurally capped.
3. Next-year EPS estimates begin falling (negative 90-day revisions) — the reverse of the current +5.6% trend — or a guidance cut from the $82-85B FY2026 range.

## Conclusion

Lilly is a category-defining growth compounder with elite profitability, an unmatched obesity/GLP-1 franchise, and a pipeline deep enough to extend the moat into the next product cycle. At ~24.8x next-year EPS with a forward PEG near 1.0-1.1, the stock is reasonably valued rather than stretched, and analyst targets (mean $1,215, median $1,251) are likely to lag rising estimates.

The risks — single-franchise concentration, accelerating price erosion, and converging government-pricing pressures — are specific and material, and they cap conviction and warrant disciplined position sizing within a sector permitted at this score level. They do not invalidate the thesis at the current price. The prior hold posture at a score above the actionable threshold understated the risk/reward. Recommendation: **BUY**, base-case target $1,300 over 12 months, with the orforglipron launch and price-versus-volume dynamics as the key items to monitor.
