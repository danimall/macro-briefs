**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fortive is a diversified industrial-technology company that, following the June 28, 2025 tax-free spin-off of its Precision Technologies segment as Ralliant (RAL), now operates two segments: Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS, ~69% of continuing revenue) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS, ~31%). This is the company's third major separation in a decade, after its own 2016 spin from Danaher, the 2018 Altra reverse Morris trust, and the 2020 Vontier spin. The residual business is asset-light (capex ~2.5% of sales), software-and-brands-heavy (recurring revenue ~20% of quarterly sales and rising), and generates high-quality free cash flow: FY25 FCF of $978M on $4.16B of continuing revenue, a 23.5% FCF margin, with operating cash flow consistently exceeding net income.

The central near-term question is whether the Q1 FY2026 reacceleration is durable. FY2025 was carried entirely by price: total core growth of just +1.7% with volume declining 0.6%. Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended April 3) inflected to +7.7% total / +5.3% core revenue with +3.1% volume growth, and AHS operating margin jumped +280bps to 10.0% as the healthcare-policy-driven sterilization/biomedical softness of 2025 reversed. If this holds, the "cleaner, faster-growing Fortive" thesis has real support; if it fades on easy comps or extra selling days, the thin AHS goodwill cushions (some reporting units at 1.5x carrying value) become a live impairment concern.

We rate FTV a HOLD (growth_hold). The setup is a quality business at a full multiple with flat estimate revisions, a fully-turned-over C-suite (CEO, CFO, and Chief People Officer all changed in 2025/early 2026), and a self-disclosed cumulative TSR of 105.7 versus a peer group at 189.7, an ~80-point relative shortfall that is the crux of the bull/bear debate. At ~21x forward EPS the risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric. The July 29 Q2 print is the decisive near-term catalyst.

## Company Overview

Fortive (HQ Everett, WA; ~10,000 employees; operations in ~50 countries) runs on the Fortive Business System (FBS), a Danaher-derived lean/Kaizen operating playbook that is the company's core competitive asset and is licensed to Ralliant post-spin. The two segments:

- **Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS)** — $2.86B FY25 revenue, 25.8% segment operating margin. Professional instrumentation (Fluke), facility/asset-lifecycle software (ServiceChannel, Gordian, Accruent), and connected-worker safety/compliance (Industrial Scientific, Intelex). Increasingly a facilities-and-safety software franchise, which supports the 63%+ gross margins.
- **Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS)** — $1.30B FY25 revenue, 10.6% segment operating margin. Instrument sterilization (ASP), sterile-processing tracking (Censis), biomedical test (Fluke Biomedical), radiation/dosimetry (Landauer), and clinical productivity software (Provation). The lower-margin, amortization-heavy segment (~14% of AHS sales is amortization), and the source of 2025's volume weakness.

Because Ralliant is now discontinued operations, FY2025 continuing revenue ($4.16B) sits well below the as-reported FY2024 figure ($6.23B, which included Ralliant). This is an accounting-presentation effect of the spin, not organic contraction.

## Financial Analysis

**Continuing-operations trend (post-spin lens):**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (cont. ops) | $3.91B | $4.08B | $4.16B | $1.069B |
| Operating margin | 14.7% | 17.6% | 17.3% | 17.9% |
| Gross margin | 63.3% | 64.2% | 63.5% | 63.2% |
| Operating cash flow | $833M | $1,029M | $1,036M | $220M |
| FCF | — | — | $978M | ~$194M |

Margins are healthy and stable. Consolidated operating margin rose to 17.9% in Q1'26. FCF conversion is a genuine strength: OCF exceeds net income (a positive earnings-quality signal), and capex intensity of ~2.5% of sales reflects the software-and-brands model.

**The Altman Z-Score of 1.59 ("distress zone") is misleading and should not drive the risk read.** Three structural factors explain it: (1) the current ratio of 0.71 and negative working capital are driven by ~$900M of current-portion long-term debt (2026 notes) plus large deferred-revenue balances ($448M current) that reflect favorable cash-collected-upfront economics, not liquidity stress; (2) equity fell from $10.2B (FY24) to $6.45B (FY25) and $6.08B (Q1'26) because Ralliant equity was distributed to shareholders and because of aggressive buybacks, not losses; (3) total principal debt of only ~$3.2–3.5B sits against >$1B annual OCF and $978M FCF, with investment-grade ratings and a $2.0B undrawn revolver extended to March 2031. Net leverage is comfortably inside the 3.75x covenant.

**Capital allocation is the standout.** Fortive received a $1.15B dividend from Ralliant at separation and has repurchased stock aggressively: 30M shares for ~$1.61B in FY25, then 8.9M shares for $500M at an average of $56.21 in Q1'26. Share count fell from ~349M (FY24) to ~306M (Q1'26), a ~12% reduction, with buybacks below management's apparent intrinsic-value estimate. The subtler concern: Q1'26 buybacks were funded largely by drawing commercial paper (CP rose from $650M to $1.24B), and a $125M treasury rate-lock entered in March 2026 signals upcoming term-debt issuance to term out CP and/or fund M&A. Balance-sheet-funded buybacks are worth monitoring against the covenant if rates rise or M&A competes for capital.

## Growth Analysis

The 2025→2026 volume inflection is the whole story. FY2025 growth was entirely price-driven (+2.2% price, −0.6% volume), reflecting the healthcare air-pocket: H.R.1 / "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA) federal-spending cuts hit AHS sterilization and biomedical demand. Q1'26 reversed this: +3.1% consolidated volume (partly extra selling days), with AHS volume swinging to +3.5% and both segments accelerating (IOS core +5.2%, AHS core +5.8%).

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one small miss over four quarters, including +8.0% in Q1 FY26 and +17.9% in Q3 FY25. Estimate revisions, however, are flat rather than rising: current-year consensus of $3.00 has moved +1.1% over 90 days and +0.0% over 30 days; next-year $3.22 is up +1.0%/90d. This does not clear the +30%/90-day threshold that would flag an inflection-driven re-rating. The growth thesis is real but incremental, not a step-change the market is systematically underestimating.

Structurally, the shift toward recurring software revenue (Accruent, Gordian, ServiceChannel, Intelex, plus dosimetry services and extended warranties) is the durable growth lever, supporting mid-single-digit core growth over a cycle. The healthcare recovery adds upside if reimbursement/policy stabilizes, but AHS remains exposed to hospital capex cyclicality and OBBBA-driven federal spending (~$417M of FY25 sales were to the U.S. federal government).

Cheapness that reflects legitimate structural concerns is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and Fortive's own proxy admits ~80 points of relative TSR underperformance over the measurement window. The bull framing, a "simplified special situation" that is under-owned and should re-rate toward peers, requires the Q1 volume inflection to prove durable and management to demonstrate FBS still works post-transition. Neither is yet confirmed.

Analyst targets bracket a mean of $64.23 and median of $64.00, with a range of $51 to $72. The stock sits just below consensus mean. With flat estimate revisions and a sub-30x multiple, targets are neither a clear floor nor ceiling here; they reflect a genuinely two-sided debate. We do not reward the discount as asymmetric upside until the volume reacceleration is confirmed. Valuation scores 4.5: the discount to peers is real but justified by identifiable structural concerns, not an obvious mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Fortive competes across fragmented niches (test & measurement, EHS/safety, facilities software, medical sterilization) against a non-overlapping competitor set, with no single peer matching the portfolio. The durable edge is FBS-driven continuous improvement plus a growing base of sticky software and recurring services (SaaS, dosimetry, extended warranties). High and stable gross margins (63%+) and 25%+ IOS operating margins are evidence of pricing power and mix quality.

The competitive-position trajectory is mixed. IOS is structurally advantaged and gaining recurring-revenue share; AHS faces capital-equipment cyclicality and policy-driven demand swings that dented 2025 volume. The larger overhang is that FBS is a management/culture asset now being tested by simultaneous CEO/CFO/Chief People Officer turnover. The moat is intact but its execution is less proven under the new team than it was under the prior long-tenured leadership. We score competitive position 6.0, solid, with a slight negative bias versus the prior 6.5 given the transition and the TSR gap that suggests the market questions whether serial spins have compounded value.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Healthcare-policy / government-spending exposure (most company-specific).** OBBBA-driven federal spending cuts are explicitly cited in both the 10-K and 10-Q as hitting AHS sterilization/biomedical demand. Q1'26 recovered, but this is a live, trackable headwind, not a resolved one.
2. **Goodwill/intangible impairment tail.** $7.3B goodwill + $2.2B intangibles = ~$9.5B, ~82% of total assets ($11.7B). The 10-K discloses some reporting units with fair-value cushions as thin as 1.5x carrying value (1.3x under a 10% haircut). If AHS demand stalls again, impairment risk becomes real.
3. **Leadership transition (elevated).** The 10-K added a specific risk factor on managing concurrent CEO/CFO/Chief People Officer transitions, an unusual full-C-suite refresh in one year, execution-sensitive against an FBS-dependent model.
4. **Tariffs / IEEPA Supreme Court ruling.** Two-sided uncertainty: potential refunds to Fortive, possible refund demands from customers, and risk of reimposed tariffs via other channels. Management has offset costs with pricing (~105bps favorable in FY25).
5. **Balance-sheet discipline.** Buybacks increasingly funded with commercial paper (CP nearly doubled to $1.24B in Q1'26), with new term debt likely coming. Watch net leverage against the 3.75x covenant as buybacks plus potential M&A consume capacity.
6. **Separation/tax indemnification.** Retained exposure to Ralliant and Vontier; any of the Danaher/Vontier/Ralliant spins could be retroactively deemed taxable.
7. **FX.** ~44% of sales are non-U.S. and transactional FX is largely unhedged; FY25 recognized $161M after-tax FX losses on Euro notes (deferred in AOCI as a net-investment hedge).

Insider activity is a neutral signal: no open-market C-suite purchases in the recent window, only routine grants, tax-withholding dispositions, and director accumulation. The absence of clustered buying at ~$56 is a missed confirmation for the "cheap and under-owned" bull thesis.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.1% is rich versus 25.9% one-year historical vol (a 15.2pp spread) and sits in the 63rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (41.1% near-term vs 30.1% at six months), with an implied ±9.4% move on the nearest expiry, consistent with the July 29 earnings binary. Skew is mildly call-heavy (−3.4%) and put/call volume is low at 0.40, so directionally the near-term positioning is not bearish, though the put/call OI ratio of 1.52 (up from 1.38) shows accumulated hedging into the print. The IV/HV richness argues against chasing the stock ahead of earnings; better entry would follow the post-event volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($72):** The Q1'26 volume inflection is durable, not a selling-day artifact. AHS reimbursement/policy normalizes, sustaining +5%+ core growth and mid-teens AHS margins toward the mid-cycle. FBS execution proves intact under Soroye/Okerstrom, ~12% share-count reduction compounds, and the stock re-rates toward the peer-median multiple as the "simplified special situation" thesis is validated. ~19% upside.

**Bear case ($51):** Q1's volume reacceleration fades on easy comps; healthcare demand stalls again on OBBBA-driven federal cuts. Thin AHS goodwill cushions (1.5x) trigger an impairment. The full C-suite turnover disrupts FBS execution, the TSR gap persists, and CP-funded buybacks compress the balance-sheet cushion into a rising-rate environment. ~16% downside from current.

**Base case ($64):** The volume recovery holds at a moderated pace, core growth settles at mid-single digits, buybacks continue, and the multiple holds near current levels as the market waits for durable proof. Roughly consensus, ~5% upside from current.

The risk/reward is balanced. This is a quality asset-light compounder at a full-but-not-stretched multiple, where the discount to peers reflects legitimate structural concerns rather than an obvious mispricing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis exists (durable software mix-shift plus a healthcare recovery) but lacks the estimate-revision velocity or valuation asymmetry to warrant a BUY today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY26 (July 29) confirms core/volume growth holding above +4% with continued AHS margin expansion, showing Q1 was not a selling-day artifact.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>+5% over 90 days), or the stock pulls back into the low-$50s where the peer-multiple discount becomes an asymmetric entry.
- Clustered open-market C-suite buying, confirming management views the stock as cheap.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 volume growth relapses toward flat/negative, re-exposing the price-only growth profile.
- A goodwill impairment charge in AHS (the 1.5x cushion breaks).
- Net leverage approaches the 3.75x covenant as CP-funded buybacks plus M&A stretch the balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 29 Q2 FY26 earnings call is the decisive near-term checkpoint, with the future debt-issuance/CP-terming decision as a secondary capital-allocation signal.

## Conclusion

Post-Ralliant Fortive is a cleaner, higher-quality business than its as-reported financials suggest: asset-light, 63%+ gross margins, ~23.5% FCF margin, and disciplined buybacks that have cut share count ~12%. The Altman Z-Score "distress" flag is a post-spin balance-sheet artifact, not a solvency concern. The Q1'26 volume reacceleration is the most important positive datapoint in eighteen months and, if durable, supports the under-owned special-situation thesis.

The case is not yet asymmetric. The ~21x forward multiple sits at a discount to peers for identifiable reasons: a fully-turned-over C-suite that must re-prove FBS, a self-disclosed 106-vs-190 TSR gap, thin AHS goodwill cushions, and flat estimate revisions that fall short of an inflection signal. Insiders have not bought the dip. We maintain HOLD, consistent with the prior three reports, with the score essentially flat at 5.6. The bar for continuing an unchanged HOLD is met by the balanced risk/reward and the imminent July 29 catalyst that will resolve whether Q1's growth was real or noise. We would turn constructive on confirmed durable volume growth or a pullback into the low-$50s; we would turn cautious on a volume relapse or AHS impairment.
