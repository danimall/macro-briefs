## Executive Summary

TD's reported financials over the last two years are almost unusable at face value, and understanding why is the starting point for any view on the stock. Fiscal 2024 was depressed by roughly C$4.2B of provisions tied to the U.S. anti-money-laundering resolution; fiscal 2025 was inflated by the gain on exiting the Charles Schwab stake, visible in the Corporate segment's revenue jump from C$2,899M to C$11,832M. The trailing-twelve-month window to April 30, 2026 contains neither item, which is why headline TTM comparisons show revenue down 4.9%, net income down 14.2% and EPS down 12.1% while underlying earnings are up roughly 25%. Backing the net one-off swing (approximately +C$5.3B, or US$3.9B) out of the prior TTM base gives underlying earnings of about US$8.3B versus US$10.53B now. Any screen ranking TD on reported earnings momentum is mis-ranking it in both directions.

The operating trajectory is therefore better than the headline suggests. Four consecutive quarterly beats (+2.3%, +4.8%, +4.3%, +4.0%), consensus adjusted EPS revised up 3.4% for the current fiscal year and 4.0% for next over 90 days, U.S. Retail revenue up roughly 20% on a rolling basis despite a balance sheet that cannot grow, and a shareholder yield near 5.7% all support the bull framing. Every major covering firm raised its target after fiscal Q2 2026: Scotiabank to C$169, CIBC to C$164, RBC to C$156, Jefferies to C$151, Barclays to C$140.

The stock outran all but one of those raises. At US$121.08 TD trades at approximately 2.2x book value and 2.74x tangible book on a TTM ROE of 11.9%, and at roughly 17.0x current-year and 15.3x next-year consensus adjusted EPS (C$9.69 and C$10.77 respectively). Solving the standard justified price-to-book relationship, (ROE − g)/(COE − g), a 2.2x multiple requires sustainable ROE of about 15.0% at a 9% cost of equity and 4% terminal growth, and 17.3% at 9.5% COE and 3% growth. Even generous parameters demand that TD exceed its own pre-scandal adjusted ROE of 14-15%. The dividend yield has compressed from 5.29% in FY2024 to 2.56%, and the price sits about 6% above the US$113.98 mean target. The re-rating that would follow relief from the OCC asset cap appears to have been paid in advance. That produces a probability-weighted total return near +2% against a bear case roughly 23% below spot, which is why the rating is REDUCE rather than HOLD.

## Company Overview

TD is a G-SIB-scale North American universal bank operating four reportable segments plus Corporate. Derived from disclosed ratios (TTM net income of US$10.53B at a 0.72% ROA), total assets are approximately US$1.46 trillion (roughly C$2.0 trillion). Common and preferred book equity of US$91.33B implies equity/assets near 6.1% and balance-sheet leverage of roughly 16x, consistent with a deposit-funded universal bank. Headcount is 94,867.

Rolling segment revenue (C$ millions, TTM to April 30, 2026): Canadian Personal & Commercial C$21,223 (+2.6% versus FY2025), Wealth Management & Insurance C$15,145 (+4.0%), U.S. Retail C$14,799 (+20.3%), Wholesale Banking C$9,126 (+8.7%), Corporate C$2,880 (−75.7%, the Schwab-gain reversal). Two disciplinary caveats: the segment lines exceed consolidated revenue by roughly C$4.0-4.5B in both periods, almost certainly intersegment eliminations or taxable-equivalent adjustments in the source presentation, so segment figures are directional rather than an audited reconciliation; and the comparison is a trailing-twelve-month window against a fiscal year that overlaps it by two quarters, not a clean year-over-year growth rate.

Approximately 56% of revenue comes from the Canadian domestic franchise (Canadian P&C plus Wealth & Insurance), which operates inside a six-bank oligopoly with high regulatory barriers to entry. That is the earnings ballast, growing at mature mid-single-digit rates. The U.S. Retail bank, a Maine-to-Florida branch franchise, is the second pillar and the source of both the constraint and the option value discussed below. TD exited its Schwab equity stake entirely in February 2025, so any model still carrying associate income from that holding is stale.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported trajectory (C$ millions).** Revenue: FY2023 C$47,757 → FY2024 C$52,970-53,240 (two source values, unresolved) → FY2025 C$63,271 → TTM C$59,180. Net income: FY2023 C$10,071 → FY2024 C$8,316 → FY2025 C$19,973 → TTM C$14,328. Diluted EPS: C$5.52 → C$4.72 → C$11.56 → C$8.48.

**Normalization.** The FY2025 figure less roughly C$8.9B of Corporate one-off revenue gives about C$11.0B of ex-Schwab net income, before adjusting for restructuring, AML remediation spend, intangible amortization and a U.S. securities-portfolio repositioning loss believed to have been taken in fiscal Q1 2025. Underlying FY2025 earnings power was therefore somewhat above C$11.0B. The TTM figure of C$14,328M is comparatively clean of both mega-items, though it still absorbs restructuring and remediation cost. The move from roughly C$11.0B to C$14.3B is the real trend line.

One alternative explanation deserves weight: if the FY2025 base was reduced by realized losses on repositioning the U.S. securities book, part of the "improvement" is the absence of a loss rather than new earning power. The size and segment location of that loss could not be verified, so the +25% underlying growth estimate should be read as an upper-bound-flavored figure.

**Returns.** TTM ROE 11.91%, ROA 0.72%. TD's pre-2024 adjusted ROE ran in the 14-15% area. The bank is under-earning on equity, and that gap is the entire valuation debate. Net profit margin 25.19%, effective tax rate 19.58% on US$2.67B of TTM tax. Revenue per employee US$458,281; profit per employee US$110,954.

**Cash flow metrics should be ignored here.** TTM operating cash flow of US$3.94B against US$10.53B of net income produces a "free cash flow" of US$2.24B, a 1.12% FCF yield and an 89x P/FCF. For a deposit-taking institution these are balance-sheet flow artifacts, the same framework that reports working capital of −US$590B and no meaningful current ratio. The Altman Z-Score is correctly undefined. The Piotroski F-Score of 3 is driven substantially by the ROA-decline test (a Schwab base-effect artifact) and the operating-cash-flow-below-earnings test (structurally normal for a growing bank), plus several inputs that do not apply to banks. Those two flags are real observations that deserve explanation rather than dismissal, but the composite score carries little information for this issuer.

**Capital and capital return.** Book value per share US$53.88; at US$121.08 that is roughly 2.25x book, and tangible book per share of approximately US$44.19 gives 2.74x tangible. Implied goodwill and intangibles of roughly US$9.69/share (about US$16.0B) is consistent with TD's U.S. retail and Cowen acquisition history. The dividend is now approximately C$1.12 quarterly (C$4.48 annualized, ≈US$3.10), up roughly 6.7% from FY2025's C$4.20 declared. Share count of 1.65B is down 3.15% year-over-year and 1.14% quarter-over-quarter.

Combining the two: dividends at a 49.8% payout on US$10.53B ≈ US$5.25B, plus 3.15% of a US$200B market cap ≈ US$6.30B of repurchases, totals about US$11.55B, or roughly **110% of TTM net income**. Shareholder yield of 5.71% exceeds the earnings yield of about 5.26%. This is a rational deployment of the Schwab windfall by a bank that cannot grow its U.S. balance sheet, not distress. But it means roughly 3.3 percentage points of recent EPS growth is share-count arithmetic that decelerates as the windfall is exhausted, and a model extrapolating both current EPS growth and the current buyback pace is double-counting.

**Two unresolved data conflicts worth flagging.** The supplied profile reports an EPS field of 8.24 alongside a trailing P/E of 19.46; at the quoted price the P/E implies USD EPS near 6.22, consistent with the US$6.22 TTM figure from statement data. The 8.24 field does not reconcile to either the USD reported or the CAD reported basis and has been set aside. Separately, the quarterly EPS actuals ($2.38, $2.44, $2.18, $2.20, summing to $9.00) and consensus estimates (C$9.69 current year, C$10.77 next year) are on a Canadian-dollar adjusted basis; comparing them to IFRS reported EPS of C$8.48 would be an apples-to-oranges error.

**Financial health score: 6.0.** Deposit-funded, systemically scaled, demonstrably beating estimates, and carrying excess capital. Offsetting that: ROE below peer norms and below its own history, distributions above earnings, and, the reason this is not a 7, no verified CET1 ratio, leverage ratio, LCR, provision-for-credit-loss data, gross impaired loan trend or allowance coverage. For a bank, credit is the primary swing factor in earnings, and the direction of TD's credit cycle is not established by the available evidence.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth profile is mature. Canadian P&C at +2.6% and Wealth & Insurance at +4.0% on the rolling basis are consistent with a high-return domestic annuity in a saturated market. Wholesale at +8.7% is off a small base and is being actively restructured. Consensus implies FY2026 adjusted EPS of C$9.69 and FY2027 of C$10.77, or +11.1%, respectable, but roughly 3pp of that is buyback, leaving underlying growth near 8%.

The U.S. Retail line stands out: revenue from C$12,305M in FY2025 to C$14,799M on a TTM basis, +20.3%, corroborated by trade-press coverage of fiscal Q2 2026 attributing bottom-line growth to strong U.S. results. Two explanations compete and cannot be adjudicated without segment MD&A. The constructive read is margin remix under a balance-sheet constraint: unable to grow assets, TD sells low-yielding legacy securities, redeploys into higher-yielding loans and reprices deposits, producing revenue growth without asset growth and a genuine improvement in U.S. Retail ROA. The deflationary read is base effect: the FY2025 comparison was depressed by portfolio-repositioning losses and by the removal of Schwab-related income. Both are consistent with the aggregate data. "U.S. Retail is inflecting" should not be treated as established.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: +0.4% for the current quarter and +0.1% for the next over 30 days; +3.4% current year and +4.0% next year over 90 days. This is directional confirmation, not the kind of revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimation of an inflection. The characterization in the data (estimates stable, no significant directional shift) is the right one.

A sensitivity worth stating and then discounting: annualizing fiscal H1 2026 net income of roughly C$8.4B gives C$16.8B, or about C$10.10 of reported EPS on 1.66B shares, roughly 16.3x the C$165 share price. That is not a forecast. Canadian bank quarters differ on day count, insurance seasonality, trading revenue and the timing of restructuring and remediation charges, and consensus of C$9.69 for the full year sits about 9% below that annualization. The gap itself indicates consensus expects second-half charges, higher provisions, or normalization that the first half did not contain.

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.** Mid-single-digit organic earnings growth in the domestic core, a second-largest franchise that cannot grow assets by regulatory order, and a genuine but unproven U.S. margin story.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiples at US$121.08 (C$165 equivalent).** Trailing P/E on IFRS reported USD EPS of US$6.22: 19.5x. On TTM adjusted CAD EPS of approximately C$9.00: 18.3x. On FY2026 consensus C$9.69: 17.0x. On FY2027 consensus C$10.77: 15.3x, giving a PEG of about 1.38 on headline growth and closer to 1.9 on growth excluding buyback contribution. Price to book approximately 2.2-2.25x; price to tangible book 2.74x. Dividend yield 2.56%, down from 5.29% in FY2024.

**The implied-ROE test.** Justified price-to-book for a bank is (ROE − g)/(COE − g). Solving for the ROE that supports 2.2x:

| Assumptions | Required sustainable ROE |
|---|---|
| COE 9.5%, g 3% | 17.2% |
| COE 10.0%, g 3% | 18.3% |
| COE 9.0%, g 4% | 15.0% |
| COE 8.5%, g 4% | 13.9% |

Delivered TTM ROE is 11.91%. Pre-scandal adjusted ROE was 14-15%. Only the most generous parameterization, an 8.5% cost of equity for a bank still under a criminal-plea monitorship paired with 4% terminal growth, brings the required return down to the top of TD's historical range. The COE and terminal-growth inputs are my assumptions, and a reader who genuinely believes TD's COE is 8% and terminal growth 4.5% can justify the current multiple on roughly 12% ROE. The conclusion is not that TD is definitively overvalued; the price embeds a large, specific and testable recovery in profitability, and the burden of proof sits with the bull.

**Peer and historical context.** TD at 15.3x next-year and 17.0x current-year sits above the range at which Canadian large banks have historically traded. TD is being valued more richly than its current profitability supports relative to peers with equal or better ROEs. Investors are paying for the option on asset-cap removal plus U.S. ROE normalization rather than for delivered returns. That is a legitimate thesis; it is not a "cheap bank" thesis, and the compressed dividend yield removes the absolute-yield support that historically underpinned Canadian bank valuations. [Note: specific historical Canadian bank forward-multiple bands and U.S. money-center forward multiples/ROTE are not in the provided sources and have been removed.]

**The sell-side evidence corroborates rather than contradicts.** Mean target US$113.98 (low US$97.67, high US$130.28) sits 5.9% below spot. The rating distribution is 5 strong buy, 4 buy, 2 hold, 2 strong sell, less enthusiastic than a headline "Buy" implies, and unchanged over four months. Every Canadian-dollar target listed was raised after fiscal Q2 2026 and the stock still cleared all but Scotiabank's C$169. The usual argument that analyst targets are stale floors on rising-estimate names applies to stocks that look cheap against improving estimates; TD does not. When price exceeds a freshly-raised target set at a premium multiple, targets and price are not independent confirmations of upside.

**Valuation score: 3.5.** The multiple is stretched against delivered profitability with no absolute-yield floor, and the identifiable catalyst appears substantially pre-paid. Not lower, because the earnings trajectory is genuinely improving and a cap-relief outcome would validate a portion of the current multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

**Canada.** The Big Six control the overwhelming share of Canadian banking assets under OSFI supervision, with foreign-entry restrictions, national branch and deposit scale, and integrated wealth and insurance distribution. TD's position here is stable and highly profitable. Competitive pressure is domestic (RBC's scale advantage post-HSBC Canada, National Bank post-CWB) plus fintech at the payments and direct-investing margins. The response to that margin pressure is visible in the mid-2026 arrangement to distribute Fiserv's Clover merchant platform in Canada. Attribution matters: Clover is Fiserv's product; TD is the Canadian bank distribution partner and merchant-acquiring counterparty, not the owner or developer. It is a sensible, capital-light defense of small-business acquiring share against Square, Stripe and Global Payments, with undisclosed economics.

**United States.** Following the October 2024 Bank Secrecy Act resolution, TD's U.S. insured-depository assets are subject to an OCC limitation at approximately the September 30, 2024 level, alongside a requirement for regulatory non-objection before launching new products, services or markets, plus multi-year independent monitorships. TD is therefore the only large U.S. bank that cannot grow its balance sheet while regionals consolidate and money-centers gain deposit share. The concrete consequences: declining or repricing away marginal loan and deposit business, no U.S. M&A, and a product pipeline gated by a regulator. The counterintuitive offset is that a capped bank is forced to optimize mix, consistent with the U.S. Retail revenue pattern observed above. The status of the cap as of August 2026 could not be verified in this cycle, and it is the single largest unresolved item in the analysis.

**Wholesale.** Roughly 14% of segment revenue (C$9.1B TTM), built out with the 2023 Cowen acquisition, sub-scale against the U.S. bulge bracket and capital-intensive. Three senior appointments inside roughly six weeks in mid-2026 (president of the securities business, head of corporate and investment banking, head of global research), plus a U.S. commercial banking reorganization, indicate an active platform reset. Whether TD leans in or trims is an unresolved strategic question.

**Competitive position score: 5.5.** A stable, protected, high-return domestic core paired with a second pillar under a growth prohibition that peers do not face. Trajectory is mixed rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The U.S. asset cap and monitorships.** Thesis-critical and unresolved in both directions. Relief would be a material re-rating catalyst; an extension, adverse monitor finding or new enforcement action would be a material negative. The October 2024 resolution parameters (approximately US$3.09B across DOJ, FinCEN, OCC and the Federal Reserve; an asset limitation near US$434B; non-objection requirements; four-year FinCEN and three-year DOJ monitorships) are background knowledge that could not be re-verified against the primary filing, and the current status is unknown.

**2. Valuation risk.** At 2.2x book on 11.9% ROE, with the yield compressed to 2.56% and the price above the mean target after a 65% twelve-month advance, the cushion for disappointment is thin. This is the risk most likely to be understated by a momentum- or revision-driven screen.

**3. Canadian consumer and mortgage credit.** With roughly 56% of revenue from Canadian retail and wealth, TD is levered to household credit through the fixed-rate mortgage renewal cycle repricing through 2027 and elevated debt-to-income. No verified provision data, PCL ratio, impaired-loan trend or allowance coverage is available, so the direction of TD's credit cycle is unknown. That is the most consequential omission in this analysis after capital ratios. [Note: specific claims about current North American mortgage-rate levels and application demand are not supported by the provided sources and have been removed.]

**4. Cross-border trade and tariff exposure.** Canadian commercial borrowers with U.S. export exposure face tariff-related uncertainty, which flows into commercial provisions and loan demand. TD's footprint is both exposed and partially naturally hedged.

**5. Senior leadership turnover.** Three TD Securities appointments plus a U.S. commercial reorganization inside roughly six weeks is a high rate of change. The most plausible reading is a deliberate reset of the Wholesale and U.S. commercial platforms under a CEO 18 months into the role. An alternative reading, unplanned attrition or dissatisfaction with Wholesale returns, is equally consistent with the available evidence, and no filing evidence distinguishes them. No inference about motive is drawn.

**6. Retention in the financial-crimes function.** Reported deployment of work-tracking software on financial-crimes and risk-management staff is consistent with pressure to demonstrate remediation throughput to monitors. It also carries retention, morale and privacy-regulator risk in the one function TD cannot afford to lose people from.

**7. Capital-return durability.** Distributions at roughly 110% of earnings and approximately 3.3pp of EPS growth from share-count shrinkage. Not a solvency question; a growth-durability question. Note that the two bull cases here partially cancel: if the cap is lifted, capital redirects from buybacks into U.S. loan growth, reducing the buyback support for EPS even as revenue accelerates.

**8. Currency translation.** TD reports in CAD; a USD-based holder of the NYSE line can see reported results and share-price performance diverge on FX alone. The 0.7335 rate embedded above is a snapshot.

**Risk factors score: 4.5.** Two thesis-critical exposures, regulatory status and credit direction, cannot be verified, and neither the options market nor short interest prices any near-term stress. Known risks are partially priced; the unverified ones are not.

That diverges from the valuation analysis above, and it cuts against the bear case in the near term while doing nothing to resolve the medium-term multiple question. Cheap volatility with no priced event also means downside protection is inexpensive for holders who want to retain exposure through the August 27 print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target US$138, +14%)

Underlying earnings are up roughly 25% year-over-year once the base is cleaned, and consensus is following: FY2027 adjusted EPS of C$10.77, revised up 4.0% in 90 days, on four consecutive beats averaging 3.9%. U.S. Retail revenue is growing 20% without balance-sheet growth, which implies that mix optimization and deposit repricing are lifting U.S. ROA, and that eventual cap relief delivers volume on top of an already-improved mix. If ROE recovers toward 14%, a 2.4x multiple on book value growing 7% to roughly US$57.60 supports US$138. Excess capital funds a 5.7% shareholder yield in the meantime, beta of 0.87 makes the name relatively defensive, and Canadian oligopoly economics protect the 56% of revenue that comes from the domestic franchise. A cap-relief announcement would likely produce a step-function re-rating. [Note: specific macro claims about Federal Reserve policy actions and dissents are not in the provided sources and have been removed.]

### Bear Case (target US$93, −23%)

The multiple already discounts the bull outcome. 2.2x book requires 15-18% sustainable ROE under reasonable capital-market assumptions against 11.9% delivered, and the compressed 2.56% yield removes the valuation floor that historically limited Canadian bank drawdowns. Roughly 3.3pp of EPS growth is buyback arithmetic funded by a one-time disposition, and it decelerates. The U.S. "inflection" may be substantially a base effect from FY2025 portfolio-repositioning losses rather than a run-rate gain. Consensus itself expects second-half normalization: the 9% gap between annualized H1 2026 earnings and full-year consensus implies charges or higher provisions ahead. Canadian mortgage renewals repricing into higher rates sit against provision data that cannot be examined. If credit normalizes and ROE stalls near 12%, a 1.7x book multiple gives roughly US$92; 13x a trimmed C$9.50 of adjusted EPS gives about US$91. That is a 23% drawdown from a stock 3% off its all-time high with no protection priced in options and 0.47% short interest.

### Probability-weighted view

Base case 55% at US$122, bull 25% at US$138, bear 20% at US$93 gives an expected price of US$120.20; adding roughly US$3.15 of dividends over twelve months gives a total return near +2%. Upside to bull is 14%; downside to bear is 23%. The asymmetry is unfavorable, and the expected return is below what should be required for a bank carrying unverified capital and credit disclosure plus an open regulatory monitorship.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Distinguishing this from HOLD: a HOLD implies approximately fair risk-adjusted return from current levels. Here the base case is roughly flat on price with a 2.6% yield, and the downside scenario is 1.6x the size of the upside scenario, on a multiple that requires profitability TD has not delivered since before the AML resolution. That combination is a less-than-fair expected risk-adjusted return with unfavorable asymmetry. Distinguishing this from AVOID: the franchise is not impaired, underlying earnings are improving, estimates are rising, four consecutive beats support forward confidence, and cap relief is a live positive catalyst. This is an exit-on-strength call, not a thesis-impairment call.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the August 27 fiscal Q3 report, which prices only a ±5.5% implied move and is unlikely to resolve either the capital-ratio or credit-quality gaps in a way that changes the multiple. For holders who want to retain Canadian bank exposure, reduce to a partial position; near-term implied volatility at 19.0% against 17.7% realized makes protective structures inexpensive if the position must be held whole through the print.

**Base case:** US$122 — 15.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of C$10.77 (C$167 ÷ 0.7335), cross-checked against approximately 2.1x book value of US$57.60 twelve months forward. +0.8% on price, roughly +3.4% total return with the dividend.

**Bull case:** US$138 (+14%) — cap relief plus ROE toward 14%, at 2.4x forward book.

**Bear case:** US$93 (−23%) — 1.7x book, or 13x a trimmed C$9.50 of adjusted EPS, on credit normalization or adverse regulatory development.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (a) Confirmed relief, partial relief or a defined sunset on the OCC asset limitation, together with disclosed CET1 remaining above OSFI requirements after redeployment. (b) Two consecutive quarters of U.S. Retail revenue growth accompanied by segment MD&A confirming net-interest-margin and loan-mix drivers rather than securities-repositioning base effects. (c) A price of US$100-105 (roughly 1.85x book and 13x FY2027 consensus), which would restore an adequate margin of safety at the current ROE. (d) Reported ROE sustained at or above 14% for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (a) Any adverse monitor finding, extension of the monitorship term, or new enforcement action against the U.S. entities. (b) Canadian provision-for-credit-loss ratio rising for two consecutive quarters alongside gross impaired loan growth, indicating the mortgage renewal cycle is converting to losses. (c) A cut or suspension of the buyback program that is not accompanied by cap relief and redeployment into U.S. loan growth, which would signal capital constraint rather than reallocation. (d) FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS revised down more than 5% from C$10.77.

**Reassessment timeline.** Fiscal Q3 2026 results on August 27, 2026 (before market open) for credit and capital disclosure; fiscal Q4 and FY2026 results in early December 2026 alongside the FY2026 40-F, which is the first opportunity to close the CET1, provision and risk-factor verification gaps. A formal reassessment should occur at the December filing regardless of price action.

## Conclusion

TD's operating results are better than its reported year-over-year comparisons suggest, and materially better than a mechanical screen would conclude. Underlying earnings improved roughly 25% once the FY2025 Schwab gain and FY2024 AML penalty are removed from the base, U.S. Retail revenue is growing without balance-sheet growth, consensus has moved up 3-4% over 90 days, and the bank has beaten in four consecutive quarters. Those facts justify why the stock has advanced 65% in twelve months.

They do not justify paying 2.2x book and 15.3x next-year adjusted earnings for an 11.9% ROE. The implied-ROE arithmetic requires 15-18% sustainable returns under reasonable cost-of-equity assumptions, above TD's own pre-scandal 14-15%, which means the market has already capitalized both AML remediation completion and asset-cap relief. The dividend yield of 2.56%, down from 5.29% two years ago, no longer provides a floor. The mean sell-side target of US$113.98 still sits 5.9% below spot. Roughly 3.3 percentage points of recent EPS growth is buyback arithmetic funded by a one-time disposition, and it fades. Against that, two of the most consequential inputs for valuing a bank, regulatory capital ratios and provision-for-credit-loss trends, are not verifiable here, at a moment when Canadian fixed-rate mortgage renewals continue repricing upward.

The probability-weighted return is approximately +2% over twelve months against a 23% bear scenario. **REDUCE.** Trim into strength; revisit at US$100-105, on confirmed asset-cap relief with capital ratios intact, or on two quarters of verifiable U.S. margin-driven revenue growth.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bear case's cited valuation floors were partly built on unsupported macro claims (North American mortgage rates at one-year highs, softening application demand, Fed policy path/dissents, and specific peer/historical multiple bands), all of which have been removed for lack of source grounding; the REDUCE thesis should be re-evaluated on the sourced valuation and earnings evidence alone.
