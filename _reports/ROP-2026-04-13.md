## Executive Summary

Roper Technologies trades at $343.15, down 37.6% from its 52-week high of $584 and roughly 35% below the analyst consensus median target of $456. The decline reflects three consecutive quarterly EPS misses (albeit by trivial margins of -0.1% to -0.4%), decelerating organic growth (Q4 2025 organic growth of 4% versus 5.4% for the full year), a revenue miss in Q4, and broader rotation out of quality compounders. A company generating ~$2.5B in free cash flow with 69% gross margins and a 28% operating margin now trades at ~16x adjusted FCF, the cheapest valuation in Roper's history as a software-focused platform.

The critical signal is management behavior. Roper initiated its first-ever share repurchase program ($3B) in October 2025 and has already deployed $1.81B by February 20, 2026, accelerating purchases into the price weakness at an average of ~$353/share. CEO Neil Hunn bought $4.5M in open-market shares at ~$452 in November 2025, and Director Thomas Joyce added $502K at ~$358 in February. This cluster buying from multiple insiders using personal capital is the strongest form of insider conviction signal.

The bear case centers on organic growth saturation in niche verticals, $21.3B in goodwill (107% of equity), and $9.4B in debt requiring continuous refinancing. These are legitimate structural risks. But the current valuation — 24x forward earnings versus a 38x peer median, a 37% discount — substantially overcompensates for mid-single-digit organic growth deceleration in a business with 69% gross margins and near-100% FCF conversion.

## Company Overview

Roper Technologies operates as a serial acquirer of vertical software and technology-enabled products businesses across ~25+ defensible niche markets. Following the November 2022 divestiture of its industrial businesses to CD&R (retaining a ~44% minority stake in the resulting "Indicor" entity), Roper transformed into a predominantly software-focused platform.

**Segment composition (FY2025 revenue of $7.90B):**

- **Application Software (56.7%, $4.48B):** Aderant (legal practice management), Deltek (government contracting ERP), Vertafore (P&C insurance), Strata (healthcare analytics), Procare (early childhood education), CentralReach (ABA therapy), PowerPlan (utility asset management), Frontline (K-12 education). Organic growth +5.4%.
- **Network Software (20.3%, $1.60B):** DAT (freight matching network), ConstructConnect (pre-construction data), iPipeline (life insurance distribution), Foundry (VFX/3D). Organic growth +4.1%.
- **Technology Enabled Products (23.0%, $1.82B):** Verathon (airway management medical devices), Neptune (water meters/AMI), Northern Digital (precision measurement), CIVCO (ultrasound accessories). Organic growth +6.5%.

The business model is structurally asset-light: CapEx plus capitalized software totaled $104M in FY2025, or 1.3% of revenue. Roper deploys virtually all free cash flow into acquisitions — $8.96B over three years — and recently added share repurchases as a capital return mechanism.

Interest expense has nearly doubled in two years ($164.7M → $325.0M), and refinancing in a higher-rate environment will sustain this pressure.

Goodwill of $21.3B represents 62% of total assets and 107% of equity — the inevitable consequence of serial acquisition at premium multiples. An impairment would devastate book value, though Roper's strong cash generation makes an impairment trigger (declining segment profitability) unlikely in the near term.

## Growth Analysis

**FY2026 guidance:** Revenue growth of ~8%, organic growth of +5-6%, adjusted EPS of $21.30-$21.55. Q1 2026 EPS guidance of $4.95-$5.00.

Against this guidance, consensus estimates sit at $21.50 for FY2026 and $23.38 for FY2027, implying ~9% EPS growth in the out-year. Estimate revisions have been modestly negative over 90 days (-3.8% for the current quarter, -0.8% for the current year) but have stabilized over 30 days (+0.1% to +0.2%). The revision trajectory has bottomed.

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Organic growth sustainability at 5-6%.** Roper's 25+ niche verticals provide diversification — weakness in government contracting (Deltek) or media (Foundry) is offset by strength in healthcare (CentralReach, Strata), P&C insurance (Vertafore), and freight (DAT). The backlog of $3.42B (+10.3% YoY) and remaining performance obligations of $5.20B (+9.5%) support the guide.

2. **Acquisition-driven compounding.** With $2.64B of revolver capacity remaining and ~$2.5B in annual FCF generation, Roper has substantial firepower for continued deal-making. The company has deployed $8.96B on acquisitions over three years (CentralReach ~$1.85B, Procare ~$1.86B, Transact ~$1.6B, Subsplash ~$800M). The pipeline matters more than any individual deal.

3. **AI monetization.** Roper is embedding AI capabilities across Aderant, CentralReach, Deltek, Vertafore, DAT, ConstructConnect, iPipeline, Foundry, and Subsplash. In niche verticals with domain-specific data advantages, AI features create pricing power and switching costs. This is the most speculative growth vector but could accelerate organic growth toward the higher end of the 5-6% range.

**Concerns:** Q4 organic growth decelerated to 4%. The Mar Vista sell thesis — that many of Roper's niche markets are approaching saturation — deserves weight. If organic growth falls to 3-4% sustainably, the compounding math weakens materially. The earnings surprise record is poor: 3 misses in 4 quarters, albeit by trivial amounts (-0.1% to -0.4%).

## Valuation Assessment

At $343.15, Roper trades at:

- **24.2x trailing P/E** (on reported EPS of $14.20 — note this understates adjusted earnings)
- **16.0x FY2026E adjusted earnings** ($21.50 consensus)
- **14.7x FY2027E** ($23.38)
- **~14.0x FY2025 adjusted FCF** ($2.47B / $36.6B market cap)
- **Peer median P/E: 38.1x** — ROP trades at a **37% discount**

This is the widest peer discount in Roper's history as a software-focused entity. Teledyne (TDY) at 27.4x, Keysight (KEYS) at 38.1x, Trimble (TRMB) at 44.5x, and Zebra (ZBRA) at 29.7x all trade at meaningful premiums despite comparable or lower margin profiles.

The discount is partially justified: Roper's organic growth of 5% lags pure-play software peers, and the acquisition-driven model introduces integration and goodwill risk. But a 37% discount implies the market is pricing in either material organic growth deceleration (sub-3%) or acquisition impairment — neither of which is supported by current operational data.

On a DCF basis, using 9% WACC, $2.5B starting FCF, 7% growth for 5 years (blending organic + acquisition), and a 3% terminal growth rate, fair value approximates $420-440/share. On a multiple basis, even a conservative 20x FY2026E EPS of $21.50 yields $430.

The analyst consensus reflects this gap: median target of $456, mean of $461, with a range of $365-$550. At $343.15, the stock trades 6% below the lowest analyst target.

## Competitive Landscape

Roper's competitive position derives from its portfolio of niche-dominant vertical software businesses. The key structural advantages:

1. **Vertical integration and switching costs.** Products like Aderant (legal), Deltek (government contracting ERP), and Vertafore (P&C insurance distribution) are deeply embedded in customer workflows. Migration costs are high relative to subscription prices, producing retention rates that management does not explicitly disclose but that 69% gross margins and 28% operating margins implicitly confirm.

2. **Decentralized operating model.** Roper provides capital, strategic direction, and shared services while leaving operational decisions to business unit leaders. This preserves entrepreneurial culture in acquired businesses and reduces corporate overhead. The trade-off is limited cross-selling and potentially slower AI integration across the portfolio.

3. **No single customer concentration.** No customer represents ≥10% of revenue. The 25+ niche markets provide diversification that limits cyclical exposure.

4. **Serial acquirer track record.** Since 2001, Roper has deployed >$20B in acquisitions. The model's success depends on valuation discipline and integration execution — historically strong, with the recent $9B acceleration warranting monitoring.

The primary competitive risk is AI disruption. Roper's niche businesses may face commoditization if horizontal AI platforms (from companies such as OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, or Anthropic) can replicate domain-specific capabilities. The 10-K explicitly flags this risk. Roper's defense is domain data moats — freight data at DAT, legal workflow data at Aderant, construction bid data at ConstructConnect — but the durability of these moats against foundation model capabilities is genuinely uncertain over a 3-5 year horizon.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Acquisition execution and goodwill impairment (HIGH).** The 10-K notes "recently acquired reporting units generally represent a higher inherent risk of impairment." Any material write-down would destroy book value. Strong segment-level profitability and FCF generation mitigate the risk, but the magnitude of goodwill concentration is the single largest structural risk.

**2. Organic growth deceleration (MEDIUM-HIGH).** If deceleration continues to 3% or below, the compounding thesis breaks. Government contracting (Deltek) faces budget uncertainty. Media/entertainment (Foundry) faces structural headwinds. The 5-6% FY2026 organic guide must be monitored quarterly.

**3. Debt and refinancing risk (MEDIUM).** The April 1, 2026 8-K (material agreement covering credit facility entry/termination) likely represents a refinancing event. At current leverage, the debt is serviceable but constrains financial flexibility during downturns.

**4. AI disruption (MEDIUM).** Horizontal AI platforms could commoditize vertical software capabilities over time. Roper's domain data moats provide near-term protection, but the 3-5 year trajectory is uncertain. The company is investing proactively but lacks the R&D scale of platform competitors.

**5. Cloud/third-party platform dependency (LOW-MEDIUM).** Concentration on cloud infrastructure providers such as AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud, as well as reliance on AI model providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic, exposes Roper to pricing changes, outages, and terms-of-service modifications across its entire software portfolio.

**6. Macro sensitivity (LOW-MEDIUM).** Roper's niche positioning and recurring revenue base provide recession resilience — but the Technology Enabled Products segment (23% of revenue) has physical manufacturing exposure to tariffs and supply chain disruptions. International revenue at 13% limits direct FX risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($430-$450, +25-31%)

Organic growth stabilizes at 5-6% as guided, validating the backlog growth signal. The buyback program proves well-timed as the stock re-rates toward the peer median. Continued acquisitions at historical return profiles add 6-8% inorganic growth. AI embedding across the portfolio creates pricing power and accelerates organic growth toward the upper end of management's range. Refinancing proceeds smoothly at manageable rates. At 20x FY2026E EPS of $21.50, the stock reaches $430.

### Bear Case ($280-$300, -13% to -18%)

Organic growth decelerates to 3% as niche market saturation materializes. One or more recent acquisitions underperform, triggering goodwill impairment. Refinancing at higher rates further compresses earnings growth. Broader recession reduces enterprise software spending. Multiple compresses to 14x FY2026E, yielding ~$300. AI disruption begins eroding competitive positions in the most vulnerable verticals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $420 — 19.5x FY2026E EPS of $21.50. This represents a modest re-rating from the current 16x but remains well below the 38x peer median, reflecting the organic growth discount. Math: 19.5x × $21.50 = $420.

**Bull case target:** $450 — 21x FY2026E EPS, reflecting sustained organic growth acceleration and successful acquisition integration.

**Bear case target:** $290 — 14x FY2026E EPS if organic growth drops below 3% and goodwill impairment materializes.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: Q1 2026 earnings (late April/early May — organic growth rate is the critical metric), Q2 2026 earnings (July), and any new acquisition announcements.

**Upside/downside from current ($343):** Base case +22%, bull case +31%, bear case -15%.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **Two consecutive quarters of organic growth below 4%.** If Q1 and Q2 2026 both print below 4% organic, the growth saturation thesis gains confirmation and the multiple re-rating thesis is impaired.
2. **Goodwill impairment charge of any size.** An impairment would signal acquisition mispricing and undermine the core capital allocation thesis.
3. **Net leverage exceeding 3.5x debt/EBITDA.** Would indicate the balance sheet is stretching beyond the comfort zone for a serial acquirer in a higher-rate environment.

## Conclusion

Roper Technologies is a high-quality compounder experiencing its first meaningful valuation reset since the 2022 strategic transformation. The 37% peer discount, the 6.8% FCF yield, and aggressive insider buying collectively indicate the stock has overshot to the downside.

The risks are real: $21.3B in goodwill, organic growth deceleration, and AI disruption uncertainty. But at 16x forward earnings for a business generating $2.5B in annual FCF with 69% gross margins and ~100% FCF conversion, the margin of safety is substantial. The prior score trajectory showed declining scores from 7.1 to 6.7, reflecting legitimate deterioration in growth and risk metrics. The current assessment incorporates that deterioration — organic growth and financial risk scores are lower than historical — but finds that the 37% price decline has more than compensated, improving the valuation score from prior assessments.

**Recommendation: BUY** at $343.15, with a base case target of $420 representing 22% upside over 12-18 months.
