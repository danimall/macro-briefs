## Executive Summary

National Presto Industries is a defense contractor with a consumer appliance legacy that the market is still learning to re-classify. The defense segment now generates 81% of revenue through subsidiary AMTEC Corporation, which holds the sole prime contractor position for the U.S. Army's 40mm Ammunition System — a monopoly it has maintained across four successive five-year contracts since 2005. The defense backlog reached $1.75B at year-end 2025, up from $564M just two years earlier, providing 3-4 years of revenue visibility at current run rates.

The investment tension is straightforward: the backlog is exceptional, but the current price already reflects considerable recognition of that fact. The stock has rallied 66% over the past year from $85 to $142, compressing the backlog discount that previously existed. At ~30x trailing earnings on a year when EPS fell 20% (from $5.81 to $4.63) due to tariff impacts on Housewares and working capital demands, the multiple assumes aggressive conversion of backlog into earnings. Meanwhile, cash has been depleted from $88M to $3M, FCF is negative, the special dividend was eliminated, and the Housewares segment's gross margin collapsed from 25% to 8% under Chinese tariffs. The defense growth story is real but the stock has priced in much of the next two years of backlog execution, leaving limited margin of safety at $140+.

## Company Overview

NPK operates across three segments of wildly different quality: Defense (81% of 2025 revenue, $406M), Housewares (19%, $96M), and Safety (<1%, $2M). The company is headquartered in Eau Claire, Wisconsin with approximately 1,200 employees.

The defense segment manufactures 40mm ammunition, medium-caliber cartridge cases (20mm-50mm), precision mechanical and electro-mechanical assemblies, detonators, and other military energetic devices across facilities in Janesville, WI; Antigo, WI; East Camden, AR; Clear Lake, SD; and Marshall, TX. AMTEC's sole-source position on the 40mm system is the company's defining asset — it is the only prime contractor for a critical munitions capability with no second source. The M918E2 TP-DNT IDIQ contract awarded in May 2024 ($818.9M ceiling) adds a meaningful new product line in day/night/thermal training ammunition.

The Housewares segment designs and distributes Presto-branded kitchen electrics sourced primarily from Chinese manufacturers, sold through U.S. and Canadian retailers. The Safety segment (Rely Innovations) sells smoke/CO alarms and generates negligible revenue with negative gross margins.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue

Total revenue grew 30% to $504M in 2025, driven entirely by the defense segment's 43% growth to $406M. Housewares revenue declined 7% to $96M. Over three years, consolidated revenue has grown 56%, with defense revenue roughly doubling from an estimated $196M in 2022.

The defense backlog of $1.75B, expected to ship over 18-42 months, provides forward revenue visibility that is unusual for a company of this size. At the 2025 defense run rate of $406M, the backlog represents approximately 4.3 years of defense revenue. Even with continued acceleration, there is multi-year coverage.

### Profitability

Despite 30% topline growth, net income fell 20% from $41M to $33M ($4.63 EPS vs. $5.81). Several factors explain the disconnect:

- **Housewares gross margin collapsed from 25% to 8%**, reducing segment gross profit by $18M (from $25.5M to $7.6M). Chinese tariffs effective after January 31, 2025 are the primary driver, with LIFO accounting accelerating the impact.
- **A $2.7M vendor bankruptcy write-off** in the Housewares segment.
- **Defense gross margin compressed from 21% to 19%** despite 43% volume growth, reflecting fixed-price contract dynamics and material cost pressure.
- **Interest income fell $3M** as the investment securities portfolio was liquidated to fund inventory.
- **Interest expense rose $830K** from first-ever revolving credit draws.

Consolidated gross margin fell from 20.2% to 16.0%. Operating margin declined from 11.6% to 8.0%.

### Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

The balance sheet is the most important area for near-term risk assessment. Cash has been consumed from $88M (2023) to $3M (2025). The investment securities portfolio is depleted. The company drew $23.6M on its revolving credit facility for the first time. Inventories have doubled from $152M (2022) to $307M, and receivables have grown from $48M to $85M. Purchase obligations stand at $603M.

Operating cash flow was negative $9.1M in 2025, following negative $53.4M in 2024. Free cash flow was negative $36.2M after $27M in capex (up from $7.5M in 2024). The company is in a classic cash-consuming growth phase where the P&L shows profit but working capital absorption creates negative free cash flow. Given the contracted backlog this is not necessarily alarming, but it constrains capital returns and introduces execution risk.

The current ratio of 4.2x and debt/equity of 0.06x remain healthy in absolute terms, but the trajectory — cash declining, borrowings appearing, special dividend eliminated — signals a company whose financial flexibility has narrowed substantially.

### Capital Allocation

NPK maintained its regular $1.00/share annual dividend (~0.7% yield at $140) but eliminated the special dividend that had been $3.00-$3.50/share in prior years. The special dividend elimination is directly attributable to the cash demands of the defense ramp. Share count has been stable at ~7.14M shares with no buyback activity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Backlog Conversion.** The $1.75B defense backlog, shipping over 18-42 months, implies defense revenue of $400-500M annually for the next several years, potentially rising as production capacity expands. The 4th Five-Year 40mm System Contract has one remaining award year (FY26), and the M918E2 TP-DNT contract has an $818.9M ceiling with only ~$256M awarded to date.

**2. Additional Contract Awards.** Sustained global munitions demand from the Ukraine conflict, NATO rearmament, broader Middle East instability, and U.S. munitions stockpile replenishment creates a secular tailwind for ammunition producers. NPK's sole-source position means U.S. Army demand for 40mm systems flows exclusively through AMTEC.

**3. Margin Normalization.** If Housewares margins stabilize (even at the depressed 8% level) and defense margins hold at 19%, overall profitability should improve as defense becomes an even larger share of the mix. Further upside exists if the company exits Housewares entirely, eliminating the tariff drag and simplifying the investment thesis.

Offsetting these: the defense segment's 19% gross margin constrains earnings leverage relative to revenue growth. At $500M+ defense revenue and 19% gross margin, defense gross profit would be ~$95M. Adding $7-8M from an impaired Housewares segment and deducting $40M in SG&A implies operating income of ~$60-65M, or roughly $50M net income ($7.00 EPS). This represents meaningful upside from the current $4.63 but requires 12-24 months of continued execution and stable margins.

## Valuation Assessment

At $140, NPK trades at approximately 30.2x trailing earnings ($4.63 EPS). On a forward basis, using an estimated $6.00-7.00 FY2026E EPS (reflecting defense revenue growth to ~$500M, stable defense margins, and continued Housewares headwinds), the stock trades at 20-23x forward earnings.

Against peers, the A&D peer median P/E is 29.4x, which places NPK's trailing multiple in line and its forward multiple slightly below. However, several considerations complicate the comparison:

- **Quality premium deserved:** NPK's sole-source position and $1.75B backlog justify a premium to peers without equivalent contract visibility.
- **Growth premium partially deserved:** Defense revenue growth of 43% in 2025 is well above A&D peer norms.
- **Discount factors:** $1B market cap with no analyst coverage, no earnings calls, limited float, negative FCF, and tariff-impaired consumer segment all warrant some discount.

The backlog-to-market-cap ratio of 1.75x is compelling on a multi-year DCF basis. Applying a 15-19% gross margin to the $1.75B backlog implies $263-332M of future gross profit. Discounted at 10% over 3 years, this is worth roughly $220-280M in present value against a market cap of $1B. The defense backlog alone does not justify the current price; it requires the assumption of continued contract awards beyond the existing backlog.

The stock has rallied 66% from its May 2025 lows around $85, which has compressed the valuation discount that previously existed. At $85-95, the backlog discount was pronounced; at $140+, much of the known backlog is priced.

## Competitive Landscape

### Defense (Strong and Improving)

AMTEC's position as the sole prime contractor for the 40mm Ammunition System is a moat of exceptional durability. Switching costs for the Army are prohibitive — the specialized manufacturing, testing, and certification requirements for energetic devices create barriers that cannot be overcome quickly by potential competitors. The company has held this position through four successive contract competitions since 2005.

The M918E2 TP-DNT contract expands AMTEC into adjacent munitions categories, broadening the defense revenue base beyond the 40mm system. The current geopolitical environment creates demand urgency that favors incumbents with existing capacity and clearances.

### Housewares (Deteriorating)

The Housewares segment competes in a highly competitive, price-sensitive market against larger players (Hamilton Beach, SharkNinja, Instant Brands) and growing private-label competition. The Presto brand has limited pricing power. Chinese tariffs have structurally impaired the segment's economics — gross margins of 8% are likely unsustainable. The segment's strategic value is questionable; it may be worth more to the company as a divestiture than as a going concern.

### Information Asymmetry Advantage

NPK does not hold earnings calls and has no sell-side analyst coverage. This creates an information advantage for investors who read SEC filings directly. The stock's gradual re-rating from ~$85 to $140+ over the past year suggests the market is slowly recognizing the defense transformation, but the absence of coverage means repricing happens gradually rather than in a single step-change.

## Risk Assessment

### Near-Term Risks

**1. Working Capital Squeeze (HIGH).** If defense deliveries are delayed or contract modifications reduce near-term shipments, the company lacks liquidity buffer. Purchase obligations of $603M create additional commitment.

**2. Tariff Escalation (HIGH).** Further tariff increases on Chinese goods — which the current policy environment suggests are likely — could push the Housewares segment into outright losses. Each 10 percentage points of tariff increase could reduce consolidated operating income by $5-10M.

**3. Fixed-Price Contract Margin Risk (MODERATE).** Escalation clauses are limited to steel, aluminum, and zinc. Rising labor costs (employee count grew 7% to 1,200) and energy costs create ongoing margin pressure that cannot be fully passed through.

**4. FY26 Award Year Uncertainty (MODERATE).** The final award year of the 40mm System Contract is FY26, which has not yet been funded. While rebid risk is minimal given the sole-source position, the size and timing of the award affects near-term backlog growth.

### Structural Risks

**5. Defense Budget Dependency.** All defense revenue is U.S. government sourced. Budget sequestration, administration priority shifts, or conflict de-escalation (however unlikely currently) would directly reduce orders.

**6. Cybersecurity Incident.** A system outage from a cybersecurity event on March 1, 2025 was disclosed via 8-K. For a defense contractor handling sensitive ordnance production, this is concerning. The scope and remediation costs were not disclosed in available filings.

**7. Concentration Risk.** The company's entire competitive advantage depends on maintaining the 40mm sole-source position. Loss of this position in a future rebid — while unlikely — would be catastrophic.

### Insider Activity

Insider activity is modestly negative. CFO David Peuse sold 500 shares on March 17, 2026, leaving him with only 308 shares — an exceptionally small holding for a CFO. Director Randy Lieble sold 2,417 shares ($348K) on March 11, 2026. These are small transactions in absolute terms but directionally relevant given the stock's proximity to 52-week highs. CEO Maryjo Cohen holds 231,079 shares (~$32M at $140), representing 3.2% of shares outstanding — meaningful alignment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$170-180, 20-28% upside)

Defense revenue accelerates to $500M+ in FY2026 with margins stabilizing at 19-20%. Continued contract awards extend the backlog beyond $2B. Housewares is divested or tariff relief emerges, removing the earnings drag. The market re-rates NPK as a pure defense play at 22-25x forward earnings on $7.00+ EPS. Geopolitical tailwinds sustain multi-year munitions demand.

### Bear Case (~$95-105, 25-33% downside)

Defense margins compress further under fixed-price contract pressure and rising input costs. Tariffs persist, pushing Housewares into losses. Working capital demands require additional borrowing, further eroding financial flexibility. The backlog converts at lower margins than expected. The stock de-rates to 15-16x forward earnings as growth investors realize NPK cannot generate cash in the near term despite revenue growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **Price pullback to $110-120**, which would represent 17-19x estimated FY2026E EPS of $6.50 and restore meaningful upside to the base case.
2. **Announced Housewares divestiture**, which would simplify the story, generate cash, and likely trigger a positive re-rating as a pure defense play.
3. **Positive FCF inflection** in FY2026 — evidence that the working capital investment is peaking and backlog conversion is generating cash rather than consuming it.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **Defense gross margin falls below 17%**, indicating that fixed-price contract economics are structurally impaired at higher volumes.
2. **Backlog declines sequentially**, signaling contract award deceleration or demand softening.
3. **Revolving credit draw exceeds $75M**, indicating the cash squeeze is deepening beyond manageable levels.

### Reassessment Timeline

Next quarterly results (expected June 2026). FY26 award year announcement for the 40mm System Contract. Any announcement regarding Housewares strategic alternatives.

## Conclusion

National Presto Industries owns a genuinely rare asset: a sole-source defense contract with a $1.75B backlog in a market demanding accelerated munitions production. The competitive position is excellent and the multi-year revenue visibility is among the best in small-cap defense. CEO alignment at 3.2% ownership with $754K total compensation is strong.

The problem is price, not quality. At $140, the stock has rallied 66% in a year and trades at 30x trailing earnings that were depressed by tariff impacts and working capital demands. On forward estimates, the multiple is more reasonable (20-23x), but this requires assuming continued defense execution, stable margins, and no further deterioration in Housewares — assumptions that are reasonable but not sufficiently discounted for the risks. Cash at $3M, negative FCF, first-ever borrowings, and eliminated special dividends are real constraints that the 30x trailing multiple does not reflect.

The optimal entry point is $110-120, where the backlog discount re-emerges and the risk/reward favors the long side. At current levels, the position merits holding for investors already exposed, with the understanding that the next 12 months of earnings will demonstrate whether the backlog converts into cash earnings or continues absorbing working capital. HOLD.
