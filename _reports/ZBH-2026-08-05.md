## Executive Summary

Zimmer Biomet reported a Q2 2026 result that looks like a beat-and-raise on the headline and like margin erosion underneath. Revenue of $2,177.0M grew 4.8% reported and 4.0% organic constant currency, ahead of consensus; GAAP diluted EPS rose 33.8% to $1.03; FY2026 guidance was raised on both revenue (organic cc 1.0–3.0% to 2.25–3.25%) and adjusted EPS ($8.40–8.55 to $8.47–8.59); and the buyback expectation went to "up to $1 billion." Beneath that, adjusted operating profit fell 3.2% to $559.7M, adjusted operating margin contracted 210bp to 25.7%, and adjusted net earnings fell 2.8% to $399.6M. Adjusted EPS was flat at $2.07 because diluted shares fell 2.8% (198.3M to 192.8M). The two moved by almost exactly the same amount. On 4.8% revenue growth, the company delivered roughly 800bp of negative operating leverage.

The cause is identifiable and disclosed. Zimmer is converting substantial portions of its U.S. sales force from independent agents to employees while displacing distributors in several international markets. Adjusted SG&A grew 8.4% against 4.8% revenue growth, costing 135bp of margin; adjusted gross margin contributed another 120bp of decline; a 44bp benefit from lower R&D dollars (H1 adjusted R&D $215.3M to $208.2M) partly offset. Management frames this as transitional investment ahead of ~$175M of gross annual savings by end-2027. The company's track record argues for skepticism: five restructuring programs since 2019, roughly $832M of cumulative charges against ~$720–745M of claimed recurring annual savings, and SG&A/sales that has gone up rather than down (38.2% FY2024, 39.6% FY2025, 41.3% GAAP in Q2 2026).

The valuation is genuinely low: 11.7x the $8.53 guidance midpoint, ~10.1x EV/estimated adjusted EBITDA, ~6.1% FCF yield on the company's own free-cash-flow definition. It is also explainable: ~2–3% organic growth, a knee franchise (38% of sales) growing 0.1% organically, negative tangible book equity of roughly $1.75B, capital returns that consume essentially all free cash flow, an interim CFO in place since late April, and a five-year total-shareholder-return index of 62.66 against a peer index of 126.87. Base case of ~$105 implies about 5% upside over twelve months against ~17% downside to $83. That skew supports holding existing positions and waiting, not buying. The score trajectory (6.0 → 5.4 → 4.7 → 4.5 → 5.0) has been declining for eighteen months; this quarter neither confirms a durable reversal nor establishes fresh deterioration severe enough to return to AVOID, so the rating stays HOLD with the honest label of uncertain.

## Company Overview

Zimmer Biomet is a pure-play musculoskeletal device maker: knee and hip reconstruction implants, a sports medicine/extremities/trauma/CMFT bundle marketed as S.E.T., bone cement and surgical products, and the ZBEdge digital suite anchored by the ROSA surgical robot. FY2025 revenue of $8,231.5M split Knees $3,322.3M (40.4%), Hips $2,093.5M (25.4%), S.E.T. $2,150.2M (26.1%), and Technology & Data/Bone Cement/Surgical $665.6M (8.1%). U.S. was 57.9% of sales. Roughly 17,000 employees, ~2,000 in R&D, operations in 25-plus countries.

Three structural features of the model matter for how the financials read. First, approximately 85% of FY2025 net sales were consignment sales — Zimmer retains title to implants sitting in hospitals until a procedure occurs, which is why inventories were $2,270.3M at 6/30/2026 and why "additions to instruments" ($162.6M in H1 2026) is a larger capital line than other PP&E ($90.5M). Second, there is no order book: the 10-K states the company "generally do[es] not have firm orders," so there is no backlog, deferred revenue or RPO to underwrite forward revenue. Third, customers are extremely fragmented (no customer above 2% of sales) but surgeon relationships are concentrated in the independent sales agents the company is now converting to employees. The revenue base is therefore only as durable as day-of-procedure coverage, which is exactly what the current transformation touches.

Named competitors are Johnson & Johnson MedTech (DePuy Synthes), Stryker and Smith & Nephew.

FY2025 revenue grew 7.2% while GAAP operating profit fell 14.6% and net income fell 22.0%. Gross margin fell 220bp (63.8% to 61.6%), with management attributing the decline to inventory charges (−190bp), U.S. tariffs (−40bp), acquisition inventory step-up (−40bp), intangible amortization (−40bp) and FX (−20bp), against a +100bp volume/mix benefit and zero price impact. The −190bp reflects roughly $170M of excess-and-obsolete inventory and instrument write-downs taken in December 2025 upon deciding to discontinue certain product lines by 2032. Segment profit margins compressed in all three regions simultaneously (Americas 53.7% to 51.4%, EMEA 35.1% to 32.5%, Asia Pacific 38.7% to 35.5%), which points to a corporate rather than a geographic cause.

The margin bridge reconciles cleanly: adjusted gross margin −120bp (71.1% vs 72.3%), adjusted SG&A −135bp (40.56% vs 39.21% of sales), adjusted R&D +44bp (4.81% vs 5.25%), summing to −211bp. Two conclusions follow. SG&A is the problem, growing at roughly twice the rate of revenue as employee compensation and infrastructure replace variable third-party commissions. And R&D dollars are declining in absolute terms during a period management describes as an innovation cycle — part of which is legitimate (EU MDR initial-compliance costs of $8.7M ended after 2025, and Paragon 28 integration likely removed duplicate programs), but the direction is still down.

H1 2026: sales $4,263.7M (+7.0% reported, +3.4% organic cc); adjusted operating profit $1,130.2M (+4.7%); adjusted net earnings $809.0M (+4.8%); adjusted diluted EPS $4.16 vs $3.88 (+7.2%). Of that 7.2%, roughly 4.75 points came from earnings and 2.4 points from the 199.0M-to-194.3M reduction in diluted shares. About one-third of H1 adjusted EPS growth was share-count driven; in Q2 alone, all of it was.

One item to isolate: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA did not authorize the tariffs at issue led Zimmer to recognize recovery of ~$77M of paid tariffs, of which ~$30M reduced cost of products sold in Q1 2026 (the remainder reducing inventory by $39M and PP&E by $8M). That credit does not appear among the excluded items in the Q2 reconciliation categories, implying it flowed into adjusted results and contributed roughly $0.13 to Q1 adjusted EPS of $2.09. If so, underlying Q1 growth was closer to 8% than 15.5%. This classification is an inference from the reconciliation structure rather than a verified fact, and the $47M sitting in inventory and PP&E will release into COGS and depreciation as a diminishing tailwind. The 10-Q also notes new tariffs have been initiated under alternative legal authorities, so the underlying cost headwind may return while the receivable is being collected.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $807.2M against net earnings of $437.9M, a 1.84x conversion supported by $540.8M of D&A, with a $246.5M working-capital drag (accounts payable/accrued swung $131.4M unfavorably year over year). Free cash flow on the company's own definition — OCF less additions to instruments less additions to other PP&E — was $554.1M in H1 2026 versus $526.1M, up 5.3%.

Standardized data feeds understate the capital intensity here. An XBRL capex figure of ~$224.5M for FY2025 against $1,697.1M of OCF implies ~$1,473M of FCF and a 17.9% margin, but that appears to capture only "additions to other property, plant and equipment" and to exclude instruments, which is the larger line. Using the company's definition, FY2025 FCF was closer to ~$1.17B, or about 14% of sales — a ~6.1% yield on the current market cap rather than ~7.7%. Consignment instrument sets are economically mandatory for this model, not discretionary. Separately, contingent-consideration payments ($69.2M in H1 2026) are classified in financing activities, so earn-out settlements do not reduce reported FCF even on the company definition.

At 6/30/2026: total assets $22,797.8M; cash $410.0M; goodwill $9,919.5M; net intangibles $4,461.6M; total debt $7,479.0M; net debt $7,069.0M; equity $12,632.5M. Goodwill plus intangibles exceeds book equity by roughly $1.75B, so tangible book equity is negative and provides no downside floor. Estimated net debt to adjusted EBITDA is about 2.7x (adjusted EBITDA ~$2,608M, derived from ~26.5% adjusted operating margin plus non-intangible D&A) against a 4.5x credit-agreement covenant, with compliance confirmed in the Q1 10-Q. Headroom is comfortable; the estimate is mine and covenant-defined EBITDA likely permits further add-backs.

The near-term item is the current portion of long-term debt rising from $587.1M to $1,201.5M against $410.0M of cash — €-denominated notes due 12/13/2026 (~$576M) plus $600.0M of 4.700% notes due 2/19/2027 rolling into the current bucket. This is a reclassification of existing balances rather than new borrowing, and a $1.5B revolver runs to June 2030. Refinancing $1.2B of 2026–27 paper is a manageable near-term task rather than a solvency question, though the specific terms of any refinancing and the resulting change in interest expense are not disclosed in the sources and are [unverified].

A recalculated Altman Z-Score of approximately 2.46 puts the company in the grey zone, but the model penalizes exactly this balance sheet — a large goodwill base depressing asset turnover and GAAP EBIT suppressed by $667M of annual intangible amortization plus discrete charges. Read it as evidence that the equity has limited asset protection and is sensitive to earnings power, not as a distress flag.

**Financial health: 5.4.** Real cash generation and covenant headroom, offset by negative tangible book, a $1.2B near-term maturity stack against $410M of cash, distributions (~$1.19B including dividends) that consume essentially all FCF so leverage does not decline, and a new $300M investment-fund commitment callable over four years that sits outside both capex and the buyback authorization.

## Valuation Assessment

At $99.54:

- Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus ($9.00): **11.1x**
- Trailing GAAP P/E ($3.55): **28.0x**
- EV/estimated FY2025 adjusted EBITDA (~$2,608M): **~10.1x**
- FCF yield on company-defined FCF (~$1.17B): **~6.1%** on equity, ~4.5% on EV
- Dividend: $0.96 annualized, **0.97% yield**

A note on data consistency: the provided profile lists forward and trailing P/E as identical at 24.84x, which implies EPS of $3.99 and matches neither the $3.55 GAAP figure nor the $8.53 adjusted guidance midpoint. I have used filing- and guidance-derived multiples throughout.

The ~16x gap between trailing GAAP and forward adjusted is almost entirely $667M/year of intangible amortization (worth $1.32 of H1 2026 adjusted EPS alone, annualizing to roughly $2.60–2.70) plus perpetual restructuring and acquisition charges. Excluding amortization from a 2015 Biomet transaction is defensible. Excluding restructuring for the seventh consecutive year is less so — five programs since 2019 with ~$832M of charges function as a recurring cost of operating this business.

Peer comparison is awkward because the supplied peer multiples are trailing GAAP and the group is heterogeneous: STE 26.1x, HOLX 31.3x, PEN 73.4x, RVTY 48.6x, BAX negative, median 48.6x. On trailing GAAP, ZBH at 28.0x sits in line with STE and HOLX. On forward adjusted earnings, ZBH at 11.7x is at a large discount to any large-cap medtech comparable. The discount is the market pricing ~2–3% organic growth, declining adjusted profit dollars, negative tangible book, and capital returns that fully absorb free cash flow, rather than pricing the adjusted EPS line.

Where would a mispricing argument come from? The 2025 restructuring plan targets ~$175M of gross annual pretax savings against a 2024 baseline by end-2027. If the SG&A build is genuinely front-loaded and 200bp of adjusted operating margin returns on an ~$8.7B revenue base, that is roughly $175M pretax, about $0.74 per share after tax, taking adjusted EPS toward $10 and supporting $130–140 at a 13–14x multiple. UBS's two-notch upgrade to Buy from Sell on 7/28/2026 is consistent with that thesis, though the underlying note is unread and its target unknown. Against that: the company has spent $832M on restructuring across seven years while SG&A/sales rose, so the burden of proof sits with management.

Analyst positioning does not support treating current targets as floors. Mean target is $99.38 and median $95.00, both at or below the current price, with a high of $130 and a low of $83. Recommendations are 3 strong buy, 6 buy, 18 hold, 0 sell — a distribution consistent with a value name lacking a catalyst rather than an inflection stock where estimates are rising and targets lag. The "targets are floors" pattern requires rising estimates; estimates here are flat.

**Valuation: 5.4.** Cheap in absolute and relative terms, with identifiable structural reasons that justify most of the discount. There is asymmetry if margins recover, but at $99.54 the forward multiple has already expanded from the late-May low near $79–82, and base-case upside is thin.

## Competitive Landscape

Large-joint reconstruction is a four-player oligopoly — Zimmer, DePuy Synthes, Stryker, Smith & Nephew — that has historically supported 71–72% adjusted gross margins and low pricing volatility. Zimmer's FY2025 disclosure of zero price impact confirms pricing discipline largely held through 2025. The −0.4% in Q1 2026 and explicit guidance for price declines suggest that equilibrium is loosening.

The question that determines terminal value is robotics-mediated share in knees. ROSA is the capital placement; the implant is the recurring pull-through. Q2 shows placements selling (Technology & Data +21.5%) and implants stalling (knees +0.1%). Three readings compete. Share loss to competing robotic platforms would justify the multiple. A timing lag — capital placements preceding implant pull-through by several quarters — would make the 21.5% a forward indicator. Self-inflicted disruption from the channel overhaul plus China VBP and Italy's payback law would make it temporary.

One data point discriminates partially. U.S. hips grew 5.9% organically while U.S. knees grew 1.4% — through the same sales channel, under the same distributor conversion, to a largely overlapping surgeon base. If channel disruption were the dominant cause, both categories should suffer similarly. The 450bp intra-channel spread is more consistent with a knee-specific competitive or product-cycle issue. Alternative explanations I cannot rule out: the U.S. knee market may be growing more slowly than hips industry-wide; the 2024 Americas ERP disruption created uneven comparison bases by category; and partial-knee or robotic mix shifts can move average selling prices independent of unit share. Competitor Q2 2026 knee growth figures were not obtainable, so share loss remains a hypothesis rather than an established fact.

On the international side, knees declined 1.5% organically, with disclosed causes including China volume-based procurement, Italy's payback law and "buy local" initiatives. The 10-K also notes a shift to "platform distributors" operating sub-distributor networks in several countries, with the company expecting "revenue performance to be inconsistent."

The moat itself remains intact in the ways that are measurable: a ~99-year brand, the largest knee franchise by revenue among the four majors, three-year GPO preferred-source contracts (which the 10-K candidly notes do not obligate purchases), and switching friction from surgeon training and instrument installed base. What has changed is trajectory in the largest franchise.

**Competitive position: 4.5.** Deteriorating in knees, improving in enabling technology and hips, with pricing turning modestly negative and the largest revenue pool flat organically.

## Risk Assessment

**U.S. sales-force conversion (highest-magnitude execution risk).** The FY2025 10-K added a risk factor on transforming the U.S. and international go-to-market model, and Item 1 states the company "recently commenced a multi-year initiative to convert substantial portions of our U.S. sales force from their status as independent sales agents to becoming our employees." In orthopedics the agent typically owns the surgeon relationship; the filing acknowledges risk of losing high-performing distributors to competitors and of coverage gaps. With no backlog and no firm orders, revenue durability equals day-of-procedure coverage. Financial consequences are already visible in SG&A and in H2 guidance.

**Independent-contractor reclassification.** The 10-K retains a risk factor on additional tax liability from recategorizing agents to employees. No accrual or quantification is disclosed and no authority is asserted to have made a claim, but the interaction between a voluntary conversion and historical classification exposure is a specific, non-generic risk.

**China distributor claims.** The Q1 10-Q states that displaced or impacted distributors in China have raised formal and informal legal claims, that possible loss beyond amounts accrued cannot be estimated, and that the matter could have a material adverse impact on results in China. Unquantified.

**Paragon 28 goodwill cushion.** Following the Q1 2026 reorganization into five reporting units, the Global Foot and Ankle unit — whose cash flows and assets are "practically all" Paragon 28 — showed fair value exceeding carrying value by only ~5% at the 1/31/2026 test date, versus >20% for the three other tested units. Paragon 28 carries $619.6M of goodwill on $1,276.5M of consideration, with purchase accounting still preliminary at 3/31/2026, and its contribution to S.E.T. growth is decelerating (10.5 points of FY2025 S.E.T. growth to 2.8 points in Q2 2026 as it laps). Cumulative historical goodwill impairments already total $1,334.5M. An impairment would be non-cash: it would hit GAAP EPS, book equity, tangible book and Altman Z inputs, but not adjusted EPS, EBITDA, operating cash flow, FCF or covenant compliance.

**Governance and leadership.** An Item 5.02 8-K was filed 4/28/2026; the Q1 10-Q filed 5/1/2026 is signed by Paul Stellato as Interim CFO and Chief Accounting Officer; the 8/5/2026 release names a new Asia Pacific president but no CFO. The reason for and identity of the departing officer are unverified and no cause should be inferred. What matters regardless is that the finance function must simultaneously quantify the conversion's P&L path, defend a 5%-cushion goodwill balance, refinance ~$1.2B, manage a $77M tariff receivable and execute a $1B buyback — through an interim officer for at least three months. Beyond that, the Americas/Global Businesses, Asia Pacific and Strategy seats are all new since 2025, and the CEO added the Chairman title in May 2025, reducing board independence in oversight of the transformation.

**Other disclosed items.** IRS proposed adjustments for 2013–2015 and 2016–2019, both disputed; $85.9M remaining TCJA transition tax with 2026 the final payment year; ZimVie spinoff tax-free status a disclosed risk; litigation accruals of $133.1M with a $12.3M product-liability charge in Q2 spanning multiple districts and countries; EU MDR legacy-device recertification due December 2027/2028; recorded contingent consideration of $233.4M against a $0–$720M maximum range through 2031 (Monogram's ~$570M maximum CVR is contingent on development, regulatory and revenue milestones and should not be treated as a liability); and the 2024 Americas ERP disruption as precedent for self-inflicted operational damage. GLP-1 effects on joint-replacement demand are not addressed in any ZBH disclosure I could locate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.4% against 33.2% one-year historical volatility is fair — the options market prices no unusual event risk into the next month, and IV sits at the 46th percentile within the current chain. IV fell 3.1pp (37.5% to 34.4%) while the price rose 8.9% since mid-July, the normal pattern of an earnings event resolving. Term structure is flat (34.4% near, 36.0% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated in a near-term catalyst.

Two details bear mention. Put/call open interest rose from 0.23 to 0.65 over the same window, meaning downside protection was accumulated across the print rather than unwound after it. And skew inverts across tenors: +4.8% (elevated put demand) at the August expiry but negative at three and six months. Read together with 8.2% short interest and five days to cover, positioning is cautious near-term and unremarkable further out. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental read; nothing corroborates a re-rating either.

**Risk factors: 4.4.** Several material, unquantified items (China claims, contractor reclassification, Paragon 28 cushion) sit alongside an interim CFO and a near-term maturity stack. The discount and the short interest suggest some of this is priced, but not all of it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $130 (+31%)

The SG&A build is front-loaded transition cost, not permanent structure. The 2025 restructuring plan delivers ~$175M of gross annual savings against the 2024 baseline by end-2027; adjusted operating margin recovers 200bp toward 27.5%; knees stabilize as ROSA placements convert to implant pull-through (the 21.5% Technology & Data growth being a leading indicator); the ROSA Shoulder launch and Monogram's 2027 autonomous system extend the robotics franchise. Adjusted EPS reaches roughly $9.75–10.00 in FY2028 with the $1B annual buyback retiring ~5% of shares per year. A 13–14x multiple — still a discount to any medtech peer — produces $130–140. The setup requires no market re-rating, only margin normalization plus a knee franchise that stops declining, and the two-notch UBS upgrade indicates at least one sell-side desk has made that call.

### Base case — $105 (+5.5%)

Organic growth settles at 2–3%, adjusted operating margin recovers partially (roughly half the 210bp Q2 decline) as conversion costs anniversary, and adjusted EPS reaches ~$9.00 in FY2027 in line with consensus, with about half the growth from share retirement. The multiple stays at 11.5–12x because organic growth does not justify more. $9.00 × 11.7x = $105, plus ~1% dividend. This is roughly the analyst mean of $99.38 plus a modest re-rating.

### Bear case — $83 (−17%)

Knee softness proves competitive rather than transitional; the intra-channel spread between U.S. hips (+5.9%) and U.S. knees (+1.4%) is the tell. H2 organic growth lands at the low end near 1.1%, pricing deteriorates beyond −0.4%, SG&A stays elevated as the employee model proves structurally more expensive at Zimmer's scale, and adjusted EPS growth beyond FY2026 becomes entirely buyback-driven — mathematically limited by an FCF base already fully committed to distributions. A Paragon 28 goodwill impairment and unquantified China distributor liabilities add headline damage without cash cost. At 9.5x $8.75 the stock is $83, matching the analyst low and sitting above the $79.12 fifty-two-week low.

Base-case upside of roughly 5% against 17% downside is an unfavorable skew for a HOLD-to-BUY decision. The bull case is credible but requires trusting a margin-recovery promise from a management team that has issued four prior versions of it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October/early November 2026) are the first clean test of the ~2.1% implied H2 organic growth and of whether adjusted operating margin stops contracting. The FY2027 guide, likely with Q4 results in February 2027, is the decisive test of the $175M savings target. Two interim checkpoints: naming of a permanent CFO, and the Q4 2026 goodwill test that will include the previously untested Americas CMFT unit.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Adjusted operating margin expands year over year in any quarter** — specifically Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin above the prior-year comparable, demonstrating the SG&A build has peaked. This is the single most important number.
2. **Knees organic constant-currency growth above 2.0% for two consecutive quarters**, with U.S. knees above 3.0%, closing the spread against hips and resolving the share-loss hypothesis in Zimmer's favor.
3. **Price near $85** with fundamentals intact, which would restore a ~10x forward multiple and roughly 24% upside to the $105 base case against a defended $79 floor.

A permanent CFO appointment with credible medtech background, plus adjusted profit dollars growing rather than flat, would add conviction but is not sufficient alone.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $8.40**, or organic cc guidance cut below the current 2.25% floor — either would indicate the channel conversion is destroying revenue faster than modeled.
2. **Knees organic growth turning negative** in the U.S. specifically, which would convert the share-loss hypothesis into an established fact and reset terminal-value assumptions.
3. **A quantified China distributor liability, or a contractor-reclassification tax accrual, above $150M** — approximately 12% of annual free cash flow and enough to constrain the buyback that is currently holding adjusted EPS flat.
4. **Any reduction in the $1B FY2026 buyback expectation**, since per-share growth currently depends on it.

**Position guidance:** Existing holders should hold and wait for the Q3 print rather than adding here. New capital should wait for either the margin inflection in item 1 above or a price in the mid-$80s. A goodwill impairment on Paragon 28 should not by itself trigger a sale — it is non-cash and affects no covenant, cash-flow or adjusted metric.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 4.8, essentially flat against the 5.0 of mid-July despite a 9% higher price, because the positives and negatives in the Q2 print roughly cancel. Revenue beat, guidance was raised on both lines, the buyback expectation went to $1B, ROSA Shoulder cleared FDA, H1 free cash flow rose 5.3%, and one sell-side sell rating was removed. Against that, adjusted operating profit fell 3.2% on 4.8% revenue growth, adjusted operating margin fell 210bp, adjusted earnings dollars declined 2.8% with buybacks doing all the per-share work, the largest franchise grew 0.1% organically, the raised guidance still implies deceleration to ~2.1% organic growth in H2, and finance remains under an interim officer through a channel overhaul, a $1.2B refinancing and a $1B repurchase program.

The eighteen-month score decline (6.0 to 4.8) has been driven by competitive position and management quality, and this quarter does not reverse either. Competitive position stays at 4.5 because knees are flat while robotics grows, with the 450bp U.S. hips-versus-knees spread inside the same disrupted channel unresolved. Management quality stays at 4.3 on the interim CFO, the absence of any open-market insider purchase across a five-month Form 4 window in which the company itself repurchased at $93–99, $832M of restructuring charges without visible margin payoff, a compensation framework whose company-selected measure is consolidated constant-currency revenue (which on its face includes acquired revenue, though plan mechanics were not read), and a five-year TSR index of 62.66 against a peer index of 126.87.

The valuation is the reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. At 11.7x guided adjusted EPS, ~10.1x EV/adjusted EBITDA and a ~6.1% free-cash-flow yield with 2.7x net leverage against a 4.5x covenant, downside is bounded by cash generation and by a buyback retiring ~5% of shares annually. It is not a BUY because the discount is explainable rather than mispriced: consensus estimates have not moved in 90 days, the stock trades at the $99.38 mean target and above the $95 median, and base-case upside of ~5% does not compensate for 17% downside if the knee franchise proves to be losing share. The specific, falsifiable thing to wait for is a quarter in which adjusted operating margin expands year over year. Until that appears, the cheapness describes the business rather than an opportunity.
