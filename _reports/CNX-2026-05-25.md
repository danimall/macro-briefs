## Executive Summary

CNX Resources is a low-cost Appalachian natural gas producer trading at 4.9x trailing earnings and approximately 10% free cash flow yield — among the cheapest valuations in the E&P space. The company's aggressive buyback program ($528M in 2025, $2.4B remaining authorization) and operational efficiency (390 employees, $0.15/Mcfe lifting costs, owned midstream infrastructure) create a compelling long-term value compounding story.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the convergence of three near-term headwinds: (1) current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates are declining sharply (-19% to -22% over 30 days), reflecting lower spot gas prices relative to the period when Q1 2026 benefited from winter demand; (2) short interest at 19.3% with 9.4 days to cover represents substantial institutional bearish positioning; and (3) CNX's 2026 hedge book locks in only $2.74/Mcf on ~449 Bcf, meaning if Hormuz de-escalation materializes and energy broadly sells off, gas prices could compress toward hedge levels, eliminating the premium currently supporting estimates. The combination of falling near-term estimates, elevated short interest, and a put-heavy options market (2.75x P/C ratio) suggests patient capital should wait for a stabilization signal before committing.

The medium-term thesis remains constructive: Utica development optionality, data center demand growth in Appalachia, improving 2027-2028 hedge prices ($3.25-3.28/Mcf), and continued share count reduction at attractive prices all support appreciation over 12-18 months.

## Company Overview

CNX Resources is a vertically-integrated natural gas company operating exclusively in the Appalachian Basin. The company develops Marcellus Shale, Utica Shale, and Coalbed Methane (CBM) properties across Pennsylvania, Ohio, and West Virginia. Proved reserves total 9.7 Tcfe (99% operated, 89.5% natural gas). Production in 2025 reached 629 Bcfe, with 94% from shale and 6% from CBM.

The company operates ~2,600 miles of gathering pipelines through its midstream subsidiary, providing cost advantages and operational control. CNX employs only 390 people — an exceptionally lean structure for a $5B market cap company, reflecting operational automation and outsourcing of drilling services.

## Growth Analysis

CNX's growth profile is moderate. FY2026 guidance of 605-620 Bcfe represents a slight decline from 629 Bcfe in 2025, reflecting natural declines and the absence of an inorganic volume step-up like the Apex acquisition provided.

**Growth Catalysts:**
1. **Utica Shale development:** 612,000 net acres with early-stage results (3 wells TIL Q1 2026). Fuller data late 2026/early 2027. Represents decades of additional inventory if economics prove out.
2. **Data center demand:** Management confirmed RFPs from 9-gigawatt power complexes in Appalachia. Industry estimates suggest 10+ Bcf/day of incremental demand by decade-end.
3. **Per-share growth:** At ~$550M annual FCF and $35/share, CNX retires ~11% of shares annually, driving EPS growth even on flat production.

**Constraints:**
- Heavy hedging at $2.74/Mcf for 2026 prevents participation in any gas price upside
- CBM segment declining 3-4% annually with negative margins
- Full-year 2026 EPS consensus at $3.02 implies only ~2.5x run-rate Q1, suggesting analysts expect lower H2 earnings
- Near-term quarterly estimates declining: current quarter -18.6% (30d), next quarter -21.9% (30d)

## Risk Assessment

**1. Natural Gas Price Risk (HIGH IMPACT, MODERATE PROBABILITY)**
Henry Hub at $3.00-3.50 supports current estimates. A sustained move below $2.50 would compress margins meaningfully despite hedging. The 2026 hedge book at $2.74/Mcf provides a floor but at historically low levels.

**2. Short Interest (ELEVATED — 19.3%)**
With 9.4 days to cover, this could reflect: (a) expectations of gas price decline, (b) concerns about dilution from convertible notes (now resolved), or (c) views that the buyback-only model doesn't create sufficient shareholder value. Short interest at this level creates squeeze potential but also validates that informed investors see specific downside catalysts.

**3. Declining Near-Term Estimates**
Current quarter estimates fell 18.6% in 30 days; next quarter fell 21.9%. Full-year estimates are stable (+2.4% 30d), suggesting this is a timing issue rather than fundamental deterioration.

**4. Regulatory Risk**
Cecil Township setback ordinance (2,500 ft from homes, 5,000 ft from schools) could restrict development if replicated statewide. Four active climate change lawsuits create tail liability.

**5. CBM Segment Losses**
Operating at negative $0.45/Mcf margins. Environmental attribute revenue ($78M) partially offsets but this is a structural drag.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call volume ratio of 2.75x confirms institutional bearish positioning consistent with the short interest data. The 3-month tenor shows 2.00x P/C ratio with 11% implied move, suggesting the market is pricing a meaningful chance of further downside. The implied move to October (19.9%) brackets a scenario where gas prices weaken into summer shoulder season. This options-implied bearishness diverges from the fundamental valuation case — the market is paying for downside protection on a stock trading at 4.9x earnings. This divergence typically resolves either when the bears are right about a specific catalyst (gas price collapse, estimate cuts accelerating), or the protection expires worthless and the stock grinds higher on buyback support.

## Conclusion

CNX Resources is a high-quality capital compounder trading at a substantial discount to both peers and its own asset value. The 4.9x P/E, 10% FCF yield, and $2.4B remaining buyback authorization create a base case where per-share value accretes at 10-15% annually even with flat gas prices.

The specific impediment to upgrading to BUY is the active deterioration in near-term consensus combined with elevated short interest and put buying — informed market participants are expressing a negative near-term view that has not yet fully played out. Buying into falling estimates with elevated short interest has historically been a timing error even when the ultimate fundamental thesis proves correct. The stock merits monitoring for the stabilization signals identified above, at which point the valuation asymmetry would strongly favor an upgrade.
