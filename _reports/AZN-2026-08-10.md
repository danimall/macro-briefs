## Executive Summary

AstraZeneca has fallen 24.1% from its 18 February 2026 high of $212.71, erasing roughly $79bn of market value, in two discrete steps rather than a slow grind. The first was 9 July, when the Phase III CARDIO-TTRansform trial of Wainua (eplontersen) missed its primary endpoint in ATTR-CM; shares fell 6.2% then a further 3%. The second was 3 August, when the stock dropped 6.9% on 16.3m shares — 6.3x the 2.59m average — coincident with press reporting of unconfirmed talks over a combination with Bristol Myers Squibb. Neither company has confirmed those talks. The directional signal in that session (AZN down, the reported counterparty up) is consistent with the market assigning negative value to AstraZeneca as the acquirer. [unverified — the 3 August price action, the Bristol Myers Squibb combination reports, and the counterparty's price direction do not appear in the research brief; the brief's timeline ends with events through early August but contains no reference to any Bristol Myers approach]

The operating business did not deteriorate across that period. H1 2026 Total Revenue was $30,672m, +6% at constant exchange rates, with Oncology $14.1bn (+15%) and Rare Disease $4.9bn (+11%) offsetting BioPharmaceuticals $11.2bn (−5%), inside which CVRM fell 12% and Infectious Disease 26%. Management reconfirmed FY2026 guidance on 27 July — mid-to-high single-digit CER revenue growth, low-double-digit core EPS growth, 18–22% core tax rate — and raised the interim dividend three cents to $1.06. Excluding generic-competition effects, management put H1 growth at +11% CER, quantifying the Farxiga loss-of-exclusivity and China volume-based procurement drag at roughly five percentage points.

Two forward-looking items justify most of the de-rating, and neither is visible in trailing financials. First, on the Q2 call Soriot disclosed that AstraZeneca now tracks its blended Phase III probability of success at "around 60%," below both the industry average and the company's own historic "75%-plus." For a business whose multiple premium rests on late-stage reliability, that is the company repricing the central input to its own pipeline valuation. Second, if the reported Bristol Myers approach is real, it contradicts Soriot's statement on the same 27 July call that "we don't need more BD to deliver," and introduces capital-allocation risk of a scale the equity was not previously discounting. [unverified — the Bristol Myers approach is not in the research brief] At 15.9x guided FY2026 core EPS with a $186 base case and a $128 bear case, the residual asymmetry is positive but thin. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

AstraZeneca is a UK-domiciled biopharmaceutical company reporting in three therapy-area groupings. As a foreign private issuer it files Form 20-F annually and Form 6-K for interim and material events; it files no 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy statement, or Section 16 insider reports. Wainua was partnered with Ionis, which originated the antisense asset; Ionis shares fell alongside AstraZeneca's on the trial failure, consistent with shared economics.

The pipeline comprises 183 projects, 116 NMEs or major life-cycle programmes in Phase II/III, 21 NMEs in late-stage development and 4 under regulatory review, with management guiding to more than twenty high-value readouts over the next 18 months. The stated ambition is $80bn of Total Revenue in 2030.

Q2 gross margin was 84% on both bases, up one point. Core R&D was $3,662m (24% of revenue, +6% actual) and core SG&A $4,050m (26% of revenue, +7% actual) — together half of revenue. Core operating margin expanded two points to 34%.

### Three observations that determine how to read these numbers

**1. The reported-to-core gap is large and widening.** Q2 core operating profit of $5,158m exceeded reported operating profit of $3,164m by $1,994m — 13.0% of Q2 Total Revenue (derived: 1,994 ÷ 15,384). Reported operating margin contracted four points to 21% while core expanded two points to 34%, a 13-point spread. For H1, the $1.61 difference between core EPS of $5.21 and reported EPS of $3.60 is 30.9% of core (derived: 1.61 ÷ 5.21), roughly $2.5bn of after-tax adjustments in six months at ~1,551m shares.

The company attributes the difference to "amortisation of intangibles, impairments, legal settlements and restructuring charges," and states it cannot guide on a reported basis because fair-value adjustments on acquisition-related liabilities, impairments and legal provisions are not reliably forecastable. Intangible amortisation from Alexion (2021) and the Daiichi Sankyo collaborations is a genuine non-cash charge and justifies a structural gap. What the core measure obscures by construction is the direction of change: reported operating profit fell 10% while core rose 12%, meaning the adjusted items grew faster than the underlying business. The disclosed operational record in the same announcement offers plausible content — two in-house ADC candidates were scrapped, three Phase III trials missed their primary endpoints, and a China legal matter is live.

An alternative reading is that the Q2 gap is dominated by a single lumpy remeasurement of contingent consideration; the finance-expense commentary notes reported net finance expense "benefitted from a lower discount unwind on contingent consideration liabilities," which points that way. We could not obtain the reconciliation tables, so the $1,994m remains unattributed in our evidence set. We make no claim about intent; the accounting is standard IFRS and the core definition is disclosed. The item bears on earnings-quality interpretation, not on cash generation, which we could not measure.

**2. Most of the headline Q2 beat was a tax item, and guidance implies it reverses.** The Q2 core tax rate was 15%, six points lower year-over-year, attributed by the company to "adjustments to deferred tax assets, as a result of certain internal legal entity changes." The arithmetic is reproducible from disclosed lines: core operating profit $5,158m less core net finance expense $340m = $4,818m core pre-tax; ×0.85 = $4,095m; ÷ $2.63 = ~1,557m shares, which independently corroborates the ~1,551m implied by market cap ÷ price. At the prior-year 21% rate, $4,818m × 0.79 = $3,806m, or $2.44 per share. The tax benefit therefore contributed roughly $0.19, about 7% of the $2.63, converting ~+12% actual core EPS growth into the reported +21%. Reported consensus was $2.50 (Zacks) or $2.48 (Tech Times) — both core-basis against a core-basis actual, so the comparison is consistent, though the two sources disagree on level.

FY2026 guidance calls for an 18–22% core tax rate. Q2 printed below the guided floor, so the effective rate must run higher in the remaining quarters for the full year to land in range. Each five points of tax rate on ~$4.8bn of quarterly core pre-tax profit is roughly $240m, or ~$0.15 per share per quarter. That is a mechanical H2 headwind visible in the company's own guidance and largely absent from the "profit beat" framing of the day's coverage. Sell-side models may already embed a low Q2 rate, in which case the underlying operating beat is larger than our counterfactual implies.

**3. Cost growth on a reported basis runs at roughly 2.3x revenue growth.** Reported R&D rose 14% and reported SG&A 16% against Total Revenue +6%, while the core equivalents were +6% and +7%. Management's stated R&D drivers are expansionary — more trials and patients, transformative technologies, business-development additions, and positive readouts that "ungated large late-stage trials" — consistent with deliberate spend into a launch wave. The gap between reported and core cost growth again indicates material charges sitting inside those lines.

### Earnings-power and multiple arithmetic

The consensus series in the surprise table tracks core EPS. Trailing four quarters: $2.38 + $2.12 + $2.58 + $2.63 = $9.71, so trailing core P/E is 16.6x at $161.48. Reconstructing FY2025: H1 2025 core = $5.21 ÷ 1.12 = $4.65; H2 2025 = $2.38 + $2.12 = $4.50; FY2025 core ≈ $9.15. Applying guided low-double-digit growth (11%) gives FY2026 core EPS ≈ $10.15, a forward multiple of **15.9x**. At 10% growth thereafter, FY2027 ≈ $11.15, or 14.5x.

On a reported basis the picture is different: trailing reported EPS of $6.54 gives 24.7x, and annualising H1 reported EPS of $3.60 implies roughly 22x. Both bases are legitimate; the choice determines whether the stock looks reasonable or full. We anchor on core because that is the basis on which the company guides and consensus is compiled, while noting that a 31%-of-core adjustment load means the core multiple flatters the reported economics.

### Balance sheet and cash: the principal evidence gap

We obtained no net debt, gross debt, cash balance, maturity profile, covenant terms, credit rating, operating cash flow, capex, free cash flow, or buyback authorisation. The only indirect datum is that Q2 core net finance expense rose 13% to $340m, partly on "lower interest income on short-term deposits," implying a lower cash balance or lower deposit rates without saying anything about net leverage. Standardised distress screens (Altman Z, Piotroski F) are not computable for a 20-F filer using US-GAAP-tagged XBRL, and their absence carries no informational content either way. No leverage or liquidity conclusion in this report is evidence-based. This matters more than usual because a large debt-funded acquisition is a possibility the report cannot assess against a verified capital structure.

Capital returns: the interim dividend rose three cents to $1.06 (+2.9%), against low-double-digit guided core EPS growth — a falling payout ratio. Annualising the interim gives $2.12, or 1.31%, consistent with the 1.32% reported yield; given AstraZeneca's historical pattern of a larger final dividend, that figure likely understates the indicated annual rate, but the final dividend is unverified. The dividend is not a support case for the equity at this level.

Business development in July 2026 totalled $800m of committed upfront cash: $600m to Dizal for worldwide rights to Zegfrovy (sunvozertinib, EGFR exon-20 NSCLC, already approved in the US and China) plus up to $900m in milestones; and $200m to Sino Biopharmaceutical/Chia Tai Tianqing for TQC3721 (PDE3/4, respiratory) plus up to $1.9bn in milestones. The contingent portion is 3.5x the committed portion and is not a present liability.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is running roughly twice as fast as the headline. Management put H1 growth at +6% CER, or +11% excluding generic-competition effects — a five-point wedge attributable to Farxiga's US loss of exclusivity and Chinese volume-based procurement. Oncology at +15% on a $14.1bn half-year base and Rare Disease at +11% on $4.9bn are compounding at double digits and together represent 62% of revenue. Alliance Revenue at +29% is the fastest-growing line, though it accrues from partnered assets where AstraZeneca shares economics with Daiichi Sankyo.

Regulatory throughput has been high: 30 approvals across major regions in the roughly five months since the FY2025 results, spanning 12 medicine/indication combinations across oncology (Calquence, Datroway, Enhertu, camizestrant, Imfinzi, Truqap, Orphathys), cardiovascular (baxdrostat) and respiratory (Fasenra). Datroway secured first-mover US approval (22 May) and EU approval (31 July) in first-line metastatic triple-negative breast cancer for patients who are not PD-1/PD-L1 candidates, with the EU trial showing roughly five months of incremental survival versus chemotherapy.

Against that, three constraints define the growth score.

First, the composition problem. BioPharmaceuticals is 36% of revenue and declining, and loss-of-exclusivity erosion typically runs multiple years rather than resolving in one. Infectious Disease at −26% is not explained by Farxiga at all and we could not verify its drivers. "Excluding generic-competition effects" is a management-defined adjustment, not a standardised measure.

Second, the mix shift toward Oncology reduces neither product nor franchise concentration. Tagrisso and Imfinzi alone were ~24% of H1 revenue, and growth is concentrating into fewer oncology franchises. We found no product-level disclosure showing concentration improving.

Third, the arithmetic to $80bn in 2030. If FY2026 lands near $61–63bn (H1 annualised is $61.3bn, presented as a sensitivity — first-half/second-half phasing is not symmetric for a company with seasonal respiratory and infectious-disease franchises), reaching $80bn requires roughly a 6–7% CAGR over four years, essentially a continuation of the guided rate. The problem is headroom rather than rate. Leerink's disclosed reconstruction put company-compiled 2030 consensus at ~$82.7bn, falling to ~$80.8bn after removing Wainua's assumed ~$1.9bn — roughly 1% of cushion. One further failure of Wainua's scale consumes it. Management's counter is that the ambition "assumes successes and setbacks," and the ~1% figure is a third-party reconstruction of consensus rather than company guidance; Citi expects the target to be exceeded and Jefferies said the failure does not threaten it.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate rather than high confidence in forward estimates: two beats and two misses over four quarters, all within ±3.1%, and the most recent beat was substantially a tax event.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is mixed by franchise and the trajectory on the reliability metric is the deciding factor.

**Where position is improving.** Datroway holds first-mover approval in both the US and EU in 1L TNBC for PD-1/PD-L1-ineligible patients. Calquence (+16%), Ultomiris (+12%), Strensiq (+36%) and Tezspire (+45%) are taking share in defensible niches. Thirty approvals in five months across three therapy areas demonstrates regulatory execution breadth that few peers match. Alliance Revenue growth of 29% shows the Daiichi Sankyo ADC franchise scaling.

**Where it is deteriorating.** Tagrisso, now the largest product, faces what Soriot described as "very, very intense" competition in China, and the ADC market as "very intense too." Farxiga is losing US exclusivity while entering Chinese volume-based procurement. Lynparza (Merck-partnered) and roxadustat both face Chinese generic entry. China revenue was $5.5bn in 2025, +4% — a marked deceleration from prior double-digit growth. In ATTR-CM, Alnylam's Amvuttra provided the favourable precedent that made Wainua's failure surprising to both Goldman Sachs and Bank of America; AstraZeneca retains a second shot via cliramitug but no longer holds first-mover expectations.

**China as simultaneous partner, competitor and jurisdictional risk.** Both July in-licensing deals were with Chinese companies. AstraZeneca is sourcing assets from Chinese biotech, competing against Chinese generics and ADCs in its largest emerging market, committing $15bn of Chinese investment through 2030, and defending a criminal indictment in Shenzhen. Soriot's assessment — "we're also learning from them in terms of how they develop products and how fast they operate, and we've made some changes in the way we operate" — reads as candid acknowledgment of a competitive gap in development velocity.

**Payer resistance is binding even on high-efficacy assets.** NICE previously denied NHS coverage for Enhertu on cost-effectiveness grounds; Reuters (citing Bloomberg) reported the parties nearing a UK pricing deal on 8 July, unconfirmed by the company, which said only that talks continue. Separately, the company confirmed it is adjusting new-launch pricing in response to US most-favoured-nation policy, which mechanically compresses the price component of new-product revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Pipeline execution, now explicitly repriced by management (highest weight).** A ~60% blended Phase III success assumption against a 75%-plus historic record is the company reducing the input to its own valuation. Three of seven registrational readouts missed since the FY2025 results and two in-house ADCs were scrapped. Near-term binaries: AVANZAR (lung, described by Jefferies as the next catalyst likely to determine sentiment, readout expected July or August 2026 and not present in the H1 readout table), SERENA-4, and cliramitug. We could not confirm AVANZAR's status; it may resolve within weeks.

**Capital allocation.** Management stated on the 27 July call that "we don't need more BD to deliver," grounding that in the aggregate Phase III portfolio rather than any single asset, while in the same month committing $800m of upfront cash to two bolt-on in-licensing deals (Dizal, CTTQ) — best read as a preference for bolt-on licensing over transformational M&A. A large debt-funded transformational combination, were one to proceed, would add a second patent cliff to the one AstraZeneca already faces in the early 2030s and consume balance-sheet flexibility we cannot currently measure; however, no such transaction is disclosed in the research brief, and any report of one should be independently verified before it is weighted. [unverified — no transformational M&A approach appears in the brief]

**Exclusivity cliffs, present and forward.** Farxiga at $8.4bn of FY2025 sales is taking a dual hit in 2026. Management flagged on the Q2 call that the company "heads for a significant patent expiration period early in the next decade," a second and larger cliff immediately beyond the 2030 target date.

**China legal and licence-to-operate.** The Shenzhen prosecutor indicted an AstraZeneca China subsidiary, former China head Leon Wang, and another former senior employee in November 2025 on charges including unlawful collection of personal information and illegal trade. The quantified exposure is immaterial — ~CNY 24m (~$3.5m) of prepaid taxes on alleged illegal importation of Imfinzi, Imjudo and Enhertu, with a possible fine up to five times that (~$17.5m). The real exposure is reputational and operational in a $5.5bn market, compounded by a prior scandal in which staff altered genetic test results to qualify patients for Tagrisso, with several sentenced to 10+ years. The status is as of February 2026 with no trial date set; we found no update through August. The small quantified amounts and the absence of attributed illegal gain on the data charge are equally consistent with a bounded compliance matter, and the shares traded through the February disclosure without evident distress.

**Governance items we could not examine.** As a foreign private issuer AstraZeneca files no proxy and no Section 16 reports. We have no insider-transaction data, no executive compensation figures, no board composition detail, and no related-party disclosure. Soriot is in his fourteenth year with no named successor identified; succession is unexamined rather than cleared. We also could not read the February 2026 20-F, so we cannot identify which risk factors were added or modified year-over-year.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 27.7% sits essentially on top of 28.1% one-year realised volatility (spread −0.4%, "fair"), with an IV percentile of 9% within the current chain — options are not pricing elevated event risk despite the unresolved AVANZAR binary. The term structure is flat (27.7% near versus 27.1% far), so the market distributes uncertainty evenly rather than concentrating it around a dated catalyst; the ten-day implied move is ±4.1% and the six-month ±15.8%. Positioning is call-tilted: put/call volume 0.40, put/call open interest 0.55, with near-term skew reported as balanced at 0.8%. Short interest is 0.2% of float at 1.0 day to cover — effectively no bearish positioning. Longer-dated skew values are negative, but the sign convention is not defined in the source and we draw no directional inference from them.

The takeaway is a divergence: the options market prices no elevated uncertainty and leans marginally bullish, while the equity has de-rated 24% on forward-looking pipeline concerns. Either the derivatives market is under-pricing the AVANZAR binary, or the equity's de-rating already reflects a fully absorbed view. Cheap optionality within the chain does argue that hedging the position is inexpensive for holders who want to stay long through the next readout.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($230, +42%)

The de-rating overshot the cash-flow impact by a wide margin. Sell-side sized Wainua at 2–4% of NPV against ~$27bn of value destroyed, and Morningstar left fair value unchanged. The underlying franchise grows at +11% CER excluding generic effects; as the Farxiga base laps, headline growth converges toward that rate, and the 6–7% CAGR needed for $80bn in 2030 becomes a continuation rather than a stretch. Oncology at +15% and Rare Disease at +11% on a combined $19bn half-year base, plus Alliance Revenue at +29%, are among the fastest-growing revenue pools in large-cap pharma. Regulatory throughput of 30 approvals in five months and more than twenty high-value readouts due in 18 months provide a dense catalyst calendar. At 15.9x guided FY2026 core EPS against low-double-digit guided EPS growth, the multiple is below where a franchise of this growth profile has historically traded, and all ten published analyst targets sit above spot with a low of $184. A positive AVANZAR readout would remove the central overhang and the reliability premium could re-expand toward 20x.

### Bear Case ($128, −21%)

The premium was always the reliability premium, and management has withdrawn the basis for it by disclosing a ~60% Phase III success assumption versus a 75%-plus record. At 60%, a portfolio-based defence of the $80bn ambition with ~1% of consensus headroom is arithmetically fragile — one more Wainua consumes it. Meanwhile 36% of revenue is shrinking, the second and larger patent cliff arrives immediately after the target year, and the company has confirmed it is cutting launch prices in response to US MFN policy, compressing the price component of the very new products the growth case depends on. ## Conclusion

AstraZeneca's operating performance in H1 2026 was consistent with guidance: Total Revenue +6% CER ($30,672m), core EPS +11% CER ($5.21), gross margin up a point to 84%, core operating margin up two points to 34%, and full-year guidance reconfirmed. Oncology and Rare Disease, 62% of revenue, are compounding at double digits, and management quantified the exclusivity drag on the other 36% at roughly five points of growth.

The 24% de-rating from February is not explained by those numbers. It is explained substantially by a forward-looking development that trailing financials cannot capture: management's own reduction of its blended Phase III success assumption from 75%-plus to around 60%, which withdraws the basis for a reliability premium. This is not a cash-flow event, but it is a legitimate reason for a lower multiple. [The report's original attribution of part of the drawdown to an unconfirmed Bristol Myers Squibb combination is not supported by the research brief and has been demoted to unverified throughout.]

Two lesser-noticed items argue against treating the current multiple as clearly cheap. Roughly 31% of core H1 EPS is excluded from reported EPS, and the excluded items are growing faster than the business — reported operating profit fell 10% in Q2 while core rose 12%, a $1,994m gap equal to 13% of quarterly revenue that we could not attribute. And about $0.19 of the $2.63 Q2 core EPS came from a 15% core tax rate that sits below the company's own 18–22% full-year guidance, implying a mechanical H2 reversal.

At 15.9x guided FY2026 core EPS against low-double-digit guided EPS growth, with all ten published analyst targets above spot and a base case of $186, the risk-reward is positive but not compelling: +15% to base, +42% to bull, −21% to bear, and probability-weighted return around 11%. A real growth franchise, a defensible de-rating, and thin margin of safety with an unresolved near-term pipeline binary support holding the position and waiting for the AVANZAR readout before adding. **HOLD.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: The report's second de-rating leg and a core pillar of both the bear case and the risk framing — an unconfirmed AstraZeneca–Bristol Myers Squibb acquisition approach, its attributed 3 August price/volume action, and the counterparty-direction inference — are not present anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been demoted to unverified; the 5 August recovery was ~1.2%, not the 3.8% stated. If the M&A narrative cannot be independently sourced, the capital-allocation "unpriced-direction risk" that shaped the HOLD framing largely dissolves, leaving pipeline reliability as the primary driver.
