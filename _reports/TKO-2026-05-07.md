## Executive Summary

TKO Group Holdings is a premium sports entertainment company formed from the merger of UFC and WWE, with the subsequent addition of IMG, On Location, and PBR. The combined entity controls two of the most defensible live sports content franchises globally, monetized through a portfolio of long-term media rights deals (Netflix, Paramount, ESPN), live events, partnerships, and hospitality. Revenue reached $4.74B in 2025 and guidance calls for $5.68-5.78B in 2026 (~20% growth). Adjusted EBITDA margins expanded from 22% to 34% in two years as media rights step-ups flowed through.

The bull case is straightforward: at ~7x guided 2026 EBITDA, TKO trades at a discount to premium sports content peers on a growth-adjusted basis, with clear visibility from contracted media rights, a $3B buyback program retiring ~16% of shares, and a dividend yield near 1.7%. The competitive moat is deep — UFC faces no credible challenger in MMA, and WWE's Netflix migration positions it as a primary live content pillar for streaming.

The bear case centers on execution deterioration: three consecutive quarterly EPS misses, FY2026 EPS estimates cut by 29% in 90 days, FIFA World Cup 2026 hospitality headwinds (hotel bookings 80% below expectations in host cities), 12.9% short interest, zero insider buying, and CEO compensation at $67M+ for a $14B company. These factors collectively suggest the earnings trajectory is less reliable than the revenue growth headline implies, and the market is right to demand a discount until visibility improves.

## Company Overview

TKO Group Holdings was created in September 2023 through the combination of UFC and WWE, both controlled by Endeavor Group. In February 2025, TKO acquired IMG (sports marketing/media), On Location (premium event hospitality), and Professional Bull Riders from Endeavor for $3.25B in OpCo units. The company is controlled by Silver Lake through its ownership of Endeavor (~63% voting interest).

**Segment structure:**
- **UFC** ($1.50B revenue, 57% EBITDA margin): Dominant MMA promotion with exclusive fighter contracts, global media distribution, and premium live events.
- **WWE** ($1.71B revenue, 52% EBITDA margin): Professional wrestling with the Netflix deal (10-year, reportedly ~$500M/year), USA Network SmackDown, ESPN premium events, and The CW for NXT.
- **IMG** ($1.37B revenue, 12% EBITDA margin): Sports marketing, media rights management, production, and On Location hospitality for NFL, Olympics, FIFA World Cup.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth** has been exceptional: $1.67B (2023) → $2.80B (2024) → $4.74B (2025) → guided $5.68-5.78B (2026). Q1 2026 delivered $1.60B (+26% YoY). However, the quality of earnings growth is mixed — the revenue step-ups are largely contracted (media rights), but profitability execution has disappointed.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA expanded from $1.08B (22% margin) in 2024 to $1.59B (33% margin) in 2025. Q1 2026 produced $550M at a 34% margin. Free cash flow was $1.16B in 2025 (24.5% FCF margin) on minimal capital expenditure ($127M). The core UFC/WWE businesses are cash machines.

**Balance sheet:** $3.7B term loan debt (SOFR + 200bps, maturing 2031), net debt ~$2.9B. Net Debt/EBITDA at ~1.8x is manageable for the business quality. The $11.7B in goodwill/intangibles (76% of assets) is structural for an IP-driven acquisition vehicle, not indicative of cash flow risk. Current ratio at 1.26 is adequate.

**Capital return:** $2.0B authorized buyback (expanded by $1B in Q1 2026 to ~$3B total), $867M deployed through 2025. Dividend doubled to $0.76/quarter (~$600M annualized from OpCo). The company upsized its term loan by $1B in September 2025 specifically to fund an $800M ASR — effectively leveraging the balance sheet for repurchases.

**The earnings miss problem:** Q3 2025 missed by -36%, Q4 2025 missed by -126% (generating a GAAP loss), and Q1 2026 missed by -7.7%. Only Q2 2025 beat estimates (+10.5%). This pattern of consistent misses despite strong revenue growth suggests either (a) IMG/On Location cost overruns, (b) integration expenses, (c) interest cost underestimation by the Street, or (d) equity comp dilution effects. Whatever the cause, it has produced severe estimate revisions.

## Growth Analysis

**Revenue visibility is high.** The Netflix deal (10-year, ~$5B total), Paramount partnership, and ESPN arrangement provide contracted revenue through the back half of the decade. UFC and WWE media rights collectively represent ~60% of each segment's revenue, providing a stable base.

**Growth levers:**
1. Netflix international expansion of WWE content (early innings of global rollout)
2. UFC event cadence increases (moving from ~40 events/year toward potential expansion)
3. On Location FIFA World Cup 2026 hospitality (H2 2026 catalyst)
4. Partnership/sponsorship escalation as combined company attracts larger brands
5. Consumer products and licensing (gaming, merchandise)
6. Potential UFC media rights renewal cycle (likely at significant premium)

**Growth concerns:**
- FY2026 EPS estimates cut 29% in 90 days ($5.52 → $3.92)
- Next-year EPS estimates cut 14% ($6.34 → $5.48)
- IMG margins remain volatile (12% vs. -2% a year prior; event-dependent)
- On Location requires successful execution of mega-events to justify acquisition cost
- Revenue guidance maintained at $5.68-5.78B suggests top-line is fine; the margin/EPS compression is the issue

## Valuation Assessment

At $186.22 (current price at data capture) / ~$190.47 (latest close), TKO trades at:
- **84x trailing P/E** (distorted by GAAP losses in Q4 2025)
- **~48x forward P/E on $3.92 FY2026E** (post-revision)
- **~34x forward P/E on $5.48 FY2027E**
- **~8.8x EV/EBITDA on 2025 actual** ($1.59B)
- **~7x EV/EBITDA on 2026 guided EBITDA** (assuming ~$2.1-2.2B based on margin expansion + revenue growth)

EV/EBITDA is the appropriate valuation lens for this business. At 7x forward EBITDA, TKO trades below Live Nation (LYV trades at similar multiples despite negative P/E), and at a meaningful discount to the implied value of comparable sports media assets. Recent NFL media deals, Premier League rights sales, and Formula 1's growth trajectory all suggest premium sports IP commands 10-14x EBITDA in M&A.

The discount exists because: (1) earnings are unreliable (3 consecutive misses), (2) the IMG segment's contribution is lumpy and uncertain, (3) Endeavor control creates governance overhang, and (4) the high trailing P/E scares away quantitative screens.

Analyst consensus target is $234.50 (median), representing ~23% upside from current levels. The target range of $185-$275 is wide, reflecting fundamental uncertainty about earnings trajectory.

**Valuation verdict:** The underlying assets are worth materially more than current market cap if execution normalizes. But at 48x current-year forward EPS with negative revision momentum, the P/E lens offers no margin of safety. The EV/EBITDA discount is real but requires faith that EBITDA margins continue expanding — a faith undermined by recent misses.

## Competitive Landscape

**UFC dominance is unassailable.** The organization has exclusive contracts with the world's top MMA fighters, a 30+ year brand, and distribution infrastructure that no competitor can replicate. PFL (which absorbed Bellator) and ONE Championship operate at a fraction of UFC's economic scale. The fighter exclusivity model means UFC captures the highest-profile content by default.

**WWE has no credible rival.** AEW generated initial excitement but has struggled with ratings erosion, backstage management issues, and an uncertain media rights situation. WWE's Netflix migration gives it global scale that no competitor can access. The 10-year deal length provides structural insulation.

**IMG occupies a unique niche.** The combination of media rights management, event production, and On Location hospitality creates a full-service platform for major event organizers. The FIFA World Cup 2026 partnership is the crown jewel demonstration of this capability.

**Moat trajectory is stable-to-expanding.** Media rights escalation, Netflix global content distribution, and the addition of On Location's experiential hospitality all strengthen the competitive position. No structural threats are emerging.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Earnings Execution (HIGH).** Three consecutive misses with declining magnitude (-126%, -36%, -7.7%) may suggest improvement, but estimate revisions remain deeply negative. Until TKO produces 2-3 consecutive beats, the multiple will remain compressed.

**2. FIFA World Cup 2026 Hospitality Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** Hotel bookings 80% below expectations in host cities and lower foreign fan attendance directly threaten On Location's economics. If the World Cup underperforms, IMG margins could compress in H2 2026, repeating the Paris Olympics pattern of high revenue but negative/thin margins.

**3. Netflix Opt-Out (LOW PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT).** The 5-year opt-out clause in the Netflix deal creates a tail risk. Netflix has shown willingness to exit content deals (live sports experimentation has been mixed). If exercised (~2030), WWE would need to renegotiate its most important contract.

**4. Controlled Company Governance.** Silver Lake/Endeavor's 63% voting control means minority shareholders have limited recourse on compensation, related-party transactions, or strategic direction. The competitive opportunity waiver in the charter is a structural disadvantage.

**5. CEO Compensation.** Ari Emanuel's $67M total comp ($102M actually paid) for a $14B market cap company consumes a disproportionate share of economic value. This has drawn media attention and creates institutional investor reluctance.

**6. Short Interest.** At 12.9% of float with 6.2 days to cover, the short interest is elevated. This signals institutional skepticism about the earnings trajectory and could create volatility around future earnings reports (squeeze potential in either direction).

## Conclusion

TKO owns two of the most competitively advantaged live sports content franchises in the world, generating $1.6B+ EBITDA with 20%+ revenue growth and contracted media rights visibility through the end of the decade. The EV/EBITDA valuation of ~7x forward is attractive for the asset quality. However, the recommendation remains HOLD rather than BUY for a specific, falsifiable reason: **the current negative estimate revision velocity (-29% FY2026 EPS in 90 days) combined with three consecutive quarterly EPS misses indicates the market does not yet understand TKO's true earnings power, and neither does the buy-side consensus.** Until the company demonstrates earnings execution reliability — specifically, two consecutive beats against now-lowered expectations — the multiple compression is rational, not mispriced. The 48x forward P/E on revised estimates offers insufficient margin of safety if the IMG segment disappoints again during FIFA World Cup 2026 (a scenario supported by below-expectations booking data). The thesis is positive on the asset quality and growth trajectory, but the entry point requires either a lower price (~$165-175 for adequate margin of safety at current estimates) or stabilized earnings momentum (2 beats). At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric.
