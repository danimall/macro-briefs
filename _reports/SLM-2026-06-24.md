**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SLM Corporation (Sallie Mae) is the dominant U.S. private student lender, holding roughly $20.3B in net Private Education Loans and originating ~$7.4B in 2025. The investment case rests on a single, large multi-year catalyst: H.R.1, the federal student-loan reform enacted July 4, 2025 and effective for new borrowers July 1, 2026, which eliminates Grad PLUS loans and caps Parent PLUS and graduate borrowing. This legislation removes federal credit from precisely the high-balance, high-quality graduate and professional segment where SLM is strongest. Management has translated that conviction into hard numbers, raising 2026 origination growth guidance to 12-14% from ~6% delivered in 2025, and lifting full-year 2026 EPS guidance to $3.10-$3.20 after a Q1 2026 beat (EPS $1.54 vs. $1.40 prior year, ~36% above consensus).

The complications are equally concrete. Consensus near-term EPS estimates are falling — the current-quarter estimate dropped 26% over 90 days and 4.1% over 30 days, and the next-quarter estimate fell 22.4% over 90 days — even as full-year and next-year estimates edged higher. This split reflects a model that is increasingly back-end loaded and dependent on the timing of loan sales. Loan-sale gains reached a record $369M in 2025 (~37% of pre-tax income), and Q1 2026 non-interest income of $185M included $146M of such gains. The business is transforming from a balance-sheet lender into a hybrid originate-to-distribute platform built around an unnamed private-credit strategic partner. That pivot boosts ROE and funds an aggressive buyback (share count down ~24% in three years), but it makes earnings more cyclical and more sensitive to capital-markets conditions than a hold-to-maturity lender.

The stock trades at $23.24, down ~27% over the trailing year and roughly a third below its 52-week high of $34.97, against the recovery in fundamentals. We rate SLM a HOLD with a constructive bias. The H.R.1 demand thesis and the cheap absolute multiple (~6.5x trailing, ~7.4x the $3.15 current-year consensus) argue for ownership, but the falling near-term estimate trajectory, the gain-on-sale earnings dependence, and the unresolved credit question around the 2026 modified-loan cohort prevent us from upgrading to a high-conviction BUY at this point in the cycle. The prior research trajectory moved from 5.3 (HOLD) to 7.2 (BUY); the new data — particularly the deteriorating near-term estimate revisions and the credit-normalization watch items — argues for moderating that enthusiasm rather than extending it.

## Company Overview

Following the 2014 spin-off of its loan-servicing business (now Navient), SLM is a focused monoline consumer bank organized around Private Education Loans originated, held, and serviced through Sallie Mae Bank, a Utah-chartered industrial bank regulated by the FDIC, UDFI, and CFPB. The company serves more families than any other private student lender and maintains on-campus relationships with 2,100+ institutions, the largest such relationship-management team in the industry.

The flagship product is the Smart Option Student Loan, supplemented by six graduate-specific products (Law, MBA, Medical, Dental, Health Professions, Graduate) and newer niche products including bar-study, residency/relocation, and a 2025-launched Airline Career Loan. Credit quality is high: 2025 originations carried an average FICO of 755 and were 92.8% cosigned, up from 87.5% in 2023, indicating tightening underwriting. The portfolio is concentrated — roughly 71% of total assets (83% ex-cash) are Private Education Loans, and only ~16% of 2025 originations were for-profit-school loans.

Funding comes from ~$21.1B in deposits (42% brokered, 58% retail/core including 529/HSA omnibus accounts), supplemented by ~$4.9B in Private Education Loan ABS (24% of the portfolio) and modest unsecured debt. Named competitors include Citizens Financial, PNC, SoFi, and College Ave.

## Financial Analysis

SLM's reported earnings have been strong and improving. Net income grew 23% to $745M in 2025 (diluted EPS $3.46) from $608M in 2024, and Q1 2026 produced $308M of net income. The drivers were modest net interest margin expansion (5.24% in 2025, 5.29% in Q1 2026) as funding costs fell with declining SOFR, lower provisions ($333M in 2025 versus $409M in 2024, and a net release of $11M in Q1 2026), and record loan-sale gains on ~$4.95B of loans sold. The efficiency ratio improved to 33.2% in 2025 and 30.6% in Q1 2026, best-in-class for a consumer lender.

Two structural features of the earnings stream warrant emphasis. First, reported operating cash flow was negative $399M in 2025. This is normal for a growing balance-sheet lender — originations consume cash that loan sales and securitizations refinance — and is not a distress signal. Second, the composition of income is shifting. As the strategic-partnership model scales (Q1 2026 loan sales of $3.33B versus $2.0B a year earlier), reported EPS becomes more sensitive to sale timing and premiums. The 10-K states that results "may be significantly affected by whether we choose to sell loans." This is the central earnings-quality issue: a future ABS or loan-sale market dislocation would hit SLM harder than it would a pure hold-to-maturity lender.

Capital return has been aggressive. Diluted shares fell from 261.5M in 2022 to 210.9M in 2025 and ~198M in Q1 2026, a ~24% reduction in three years, funded in part by $700M of Bank dividends upstreamed to the holding company in 2025. A new $500M repurchase program was authorized in January 2026, with $259M already deployed in Q1 2026 including a $200M accelerated share repurchase. Bank capital remains solid — CET1 of 11.1% and total capital of 12.4%, both well above well-capitalized thresholds. Consolidated equity slipped slightly to $2.45B in 2025 despite the net income, a direct consequence of the buyback intensity.

Standard distress screens (Altman Z-Score 0.45, partial Piotroski) are not meaningful for a deposit-funded lender; they penalize the inherent leverage (D/E 4.35) and thin working-capital structure of every bank. The relevant lenses — regulatory capital and credit quality — read as strong and normalizing-but-watchable, respectively.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the most important dimension here, and the picture is genuinely two-sided. The bull anchor is H.R.1. By eliminating Grad PLUS, capping Parent PLUS at $20K/year ($65K lifetime), and capping graduate borrowing at $20.5K/year ($100K lifetime; $50K/year for professional degrees), the law removes federal credit from SLM's highest-quality, highest-balance segment. Management's response is unambiguous: it expanded graduate options for medical and dental students in March 2026 and launched the Airline Career Loan to fill the post-H.R.1 professional gap. The mechanism is direct: federal borrowing caps push graduate and parent demand toward private lenders, and SLM's 2,100-school network and underwriting/servicing infrastructure position it to capture a disproportionate share.

The countervailing signal is the estimate-revision trend. While current-year consensus rose 14.8% over 90 days (to $3.15) and next-year rose 6.7% (to $3.39), the near-term quarters are falling sharply: current-quarter estimate down 26% over 90 days to $0.47, next-quarter down 22.4% to $0.38. This is not the clean "+30% across the board" inflection signal that would justify a high-conviction growth call. It reflects a model that is back-end loaded — origination-expansion expense (2026 non-interest expense guided to $750-$780M, rising) is being incurred ahead of the H.R.1 demand that materializes from July 2026 onward, and gain-on-sale timing is lumpy. The four-quarter beat record (35.6%, 19.7% in the two most recent reported quarters) supports confidence in management's ability to deliver against the full-year guide, but the falling near-term cadence tempers it.

The structural backdrop also constrains the thesis: the private education loan market is small (~$11.5B of AY 2024-2025 originations, ~2.6% of total higher-ed spending) and enrollment is flat. Growth must therefore come from share gains, graduate/professional expansion, and the H.R.1-driven federal-to-private demand shift — the last being the variable that matters most and that remains prospective rather than realized.

## Valuation Assessment

SLM trades at ~6.5x trailing EPS of $3.46 and ~7.4x the $3.15 current-year consensus. Against the Consumer Finance peer set (median P/E 12.0), this is a roughly 40-45% discount. The peer table shows wide dispersion — ENVA at 15.4x, EZPW at 16.6x, PRG at 12.0x, BFH at 8.4x, ECPG at 6.3x — reflecting heterogeneous business models. SLM's cleanest comparison is BFH (8.4x), another consumer-credit franchise, against which SLM still screens cheap.

The valuation question is whether the discount reflects a specific mispricing or a structural impairment. There is a defensible mispricing argument: a franchise with a 33% efficiency ratio, 11.9% consolidated ROE, solid capital, and a credible multi-year demand catalyst should not trade at a 40% discount to its sector median. Analyst targets (low $23.00, mean $28.82, median $29.00, high $35.00) imply meaningful upside to the mean. But the discount is not unexplained. The market is pricing the rising dependence on cyclical gain-on-sale earnings, the falling near-term estimate trajectory, the credit-normalization uncertainty, and the H.R.1-driven competitive entry the company itself flags. Cheapness on a stock with a deteriorating near-term estimate path is the kind of value-trap signal that warrants caution; the absolute low multiple alone is not an entry thesis.

We assign a 6.0 — above neutral because the absolute discount is real and there is an identified catalyst, but short of a high score because the near-term estimate trend is negative and a portion of the earnings base is cyclical and timing-dependent. On normalized 2026 earnings of $3.10-$3.20 (management guide) at the peer-median 12x, fair value would sit near $37-$38; we view a base case of $30-$31 (~10x normalized EPS, reflecting the gain-on-sale discount the market reasonably applies) as more realistic.

## Competitive Landscape

SLM's moats are brand, the 2,100-institution relationship network (the largest in the industry), underwriting and servicing scale, and access to deposit and ABS funding. The competitive trajectory is mildly improving on the demand side — H.R.1 expands the private addressable market, particularly in graduate and professional lending — but the 10-K is explicit that the same legislation plus CFPB deregulation will draw competitors (SoFi, College Ave, Citizens, PNC, and new FinTechs) into the newly-vacated graduate space. The net effect on share and pricing is genuinely ambiguous.

The shift to 92.8% cosigned originations and a 755 average FICO signals disciplined underwriting into the expansion, which is a positive trajectory indicator. The strategic-partnership originate-to-distribute model also strengthens the franchise's capital efficiency, allowing SLM to grow originations without proportionate balance-sheet expansion. On balance, the competitive position is solid and the trajectory is modestly favorable, but the coming competitive entry into SLM's core graduate segment prevents a high-conviction score. We assign a 6.8 — leadership intact, demand tailwind real, but pricing pressure from new entrants is a credible offsetting force.

## Risk Assessment

The principal idiosyncratic risks are concentrated and partly unpriced:

**Credit normalization and the modified-loan cohort.** Delinquencies (30+ days, % in repayment) rose to 4.00% at YE2025 from 3.68% a year earlier, and to 3.98% in Q1 2026 versus 3.58% in Q1 2025. Management attributes part of this to restricted modification eligibility and the held-for-sale denominator shift, but discloses that the first wave of loans modified under late-2023 expanded loss-mitigation programs will exit modification in 2026, with performance "unknown but will become clearer throughout 2026." This is the single most important idiosyncratic credit uncertainty. A material adverse credit outcome would require allowance build — the 10-K's severe downside scenario implies a ~$298M (+19.6%) allowance increase, a meaningful earnings hit.

**Earnings dependence on gain-on-sale economics.** With loan-sale gains approaching 37% of pre-tax income and the strategic-partnership model concentrating execution and counterparty risk in a limited number of (unnamed) partners, earnings are more cyclical than the headline ROE suggests. A capital-markets dislocation would compress this revenue stream sharply.

**H.R.1 competitive entry.** The catalyst is double-edged. The same federal void that expands SLM's market invites new competition that could pressure pricing and share.

**Regulatory regime change.** CFPB defunding under H.R.1 and the November 2025 transfer of enforcement toward the DOJ reduce near-term compliance burden but inject uncertainty and lower barriers for competitors. The 10-K newly cites the GENIUS Act stablecoin framework as a deposit-funding threat alongside FinTech competition; 42% brokered-deposit reliance is rate-sensitive.

**Prepayment/consolidation.** Falling rates drove 2025 consolidations to 10.6% of repayment-status loans (from 8.7%), accelerating prepayments and pressuring the portfolio.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and provides no confirmation of the elevated short interest. Near-term ATM IV of 37.6% sits fractionally below 1-year historical volatility of 39.3% (a -1.8% spread, "fair"), with IV in only the 9th percentile of its current chain — uncertainty is not being priced as elevated. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.22, and the OI ratio rose modestly from 0.12 to 0.19 as the stock recovered 7.3%. The term structure is flat. The divergence is that 13.5% of float is short (11.2 days to cover, 22.2M shares) while the options market shows no defensive put demand — bearish institutional positioning that listed-options sentiment does not corroborate. A positive H.R.1 data point or a clean credit read on the 2026 modified-loan cohort could create squeeze pressure given the days-to-cover.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** H.R.1 triggers a sustained federal-to-private demand shift into SLM's highest-quality graduate and professional segments, validating the raised 12-14% origination guidance and supporting the $3.10-$3.20 2026 EPS guide. The capital-light originate-to-distribute model expands ROE and funds continued ~5-7% annual share-count reduction. Credit normalizes without a negative surprise from the modified-loan cohort. The stock re-rates toward the peer-median multiple. At 10-11x normalized 2026 EPS of ~$3.20, the stock reaches $32-$35, near the high analyst target.

**Bear Case.** Near-term estimate cuts prove to be the leading edge of a slowdown rather than expense-timing noise; the 2026 modified-loan cohort performs poorly, forcing allowance build; gain-on-sale economics compress in a capital-markets wobble; and H.R.1 invites enough competition to pressure pricing and share before the demand fully materializes. EPS misses the guide and the multiple stays compressed at ~6-7x on lower earnings, pushing the stock toward $17-$18 — roughly the 52-week low and the level at which the CEO's shares were tax-withheld in March 2026.

**Base Case.** H.R.1 delivers real but gradual origination growth; credit normalizes within management's guided range; earnings come in near the lower-to-middle of the guide; and the stock partially closes the peer discount to ~$30-$31. This implies modest appreciation from $23.24 plus the ~2.3% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth-oriented HOLD** with a 12-month horizon tied to the H.R.1 demand ramp (effective July 1, 2026) and the resolution of the modified-loan credit question through 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Near-term EPS estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter estimates ceasing to fall), confirming the demand ramp rather than expense drag.
- Q2/Q3 2026 results show origination growth tracking toward the 12-14% guide AND the modified-loan cohort exiting modification performs in line with or better than management expectations (delinquencies stabilizing below ~4%).
- Open-market insider buying or a further compression of the multiple to ~6x forward on confirmed earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The 2026 modified-loan cohort deteriorates, forcing a material allowance build (provision guidance raised above the $345-$385M net charge-off range) or delinquencies breaching ~4.5%.
- Full-year 2026 EPS guidance cut below the $3.10 floor, or a quarterly miss after the four-quarter beat streak.
- Evidence that H.R.1-driven competition is compressing origination spreads or pricing in the core graduate segment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July) is the natural checkpoint — it will provide the first post-H.R.1-effective-date origination data and an early read on the modified-loan cohort.

## Conclusion

SLM is a financially sound, well-capitalized, best-in-class-efficiency private student lender with a powerful but still-prospective multi-year demand catalyst in H.R.1. Management has put conviction behind that catalyst by raising origination guidance and reinforcing it with product expansion into the graduate and professional segments the law most directly affects. The stock is cheap in absolute terms and against its sector, and the ~27% drawdown from the trailing-year peak reflects sentiment that has lagged the recovery in reported fundamentals.

We stop short of a BUY for three reasons. First, near-term consensus estimates are falling, which is the opposite of the clean estimate-inflection signal that would justify high conviction, even as full-year numbers edge higher. Second, the earnings base is increasingly dependent on cyclical, timing-sensitive gain-on-sale economics, which deserves a structural discount and which the market is reasonably applying. Third, the 2026 modified-loan cohort is a genuine, unresolved credit unknown against rising delinquencies. The prior report's upgrade to BUY (7.2 overall) appears to have extrapolated the H.R.1 enthusiasm; the updated data — particularly the deteriorating near-term revision trend and the credit watch items — supports a more measured stance. **HOLD**, with a constructive bias and a clear path to upgrade once the demand ramp and the credit cohort resolve favorably.
