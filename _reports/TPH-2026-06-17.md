**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tri Pointe Homes no longer exists as a public equity. On February 13, 2026, the company signed an all-cash merger agreement with Sumitomo Forestry Co., Ltd. (TSE: 1911) at $47.00 per share, a roughly 29% premium to the prior close, and the transaction completed on May 14, 2026. TPH became a wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Forestry America, ceased trading on the NYSE, was removed from the S&P SmallCap 600 (replaced by Bright Horizons), and was deregistered with the SEC. The ticker no longer resolves to a CIK because the reporting entity has been extinguished.

The financial data accompanying this analysis ($4.0B market cap, 1.16 beta, 8.3% short interest, falling estimate revisions) describes the pre-close merger-arbitrage period, not a going concern. From the February announcement through the May close, TPH traded as an arbitrage instrument: the relevant return was the spread between the market price and the $47.00 cash consideration, conditioned on deal completion. That spread compressed to essentially zero, the price flatlined at ~$46.95 from mid-February onward (with the characteristic low-volume "pin" to the deal price), and holders received cash at close. There is no equity left to own.

We rate TPH AVOID, but the rating is mechanical rather than a thesis about a deteriorating business. The investable opportunity, the arb spread, has closed. A new buyer at the current quoted price would be paying $46.95 for a security worth $47.00 in cash that has already been distributed, or for shares that no longer trade. The underlying business was a well-capitalized, premium-positioned homebuilder caught in a cyclical, affordability-driven downturn; management realized a healthy premium on a trough earnings base.

## Company Overview

Founded in April 2009 near the trough of the housing crash, Tri Pointe grew from a Southern California fee-builder into a regionally focused national homebuilder operating in 17 markets across 12 states plus D.C. (per the FY2025 10-K filed February 26, 2026). The company designed, built, and sold single-family attached and detached homes spanning entry-level, move-up, luxury, and active-adult buyers, with base prices from roughly $190K to $3.2M and an average selling price near $680,000, premium-positioned versus the volume builders.

The homebuilding business ran through three geographic segments: West (AZ, CA, NV, WA; 2,506 deliveries in 2025), Central (CO, TX, UT), and East (D.C., FL, MD, NC, SC, VA). A fourth reportable segment, Tri Pointe Solutions, comprised mortgage (Tri Pointe Connect), title/escrow (Tri Pointe Assurance), and P&C insurance agency (Tri Pointe Advantage) operations. Tri Pointe Connect was converted from a joint venture to a wholly owned lender effective February 1, 2024, and originations more than doubled in 2025 (2,795 loans / $1.49B principal vs. 1,262 / $634M in 2024) as the company captured more of its buyers' financing.

The land strategy emphasized capital efficiency: 32,219 lots owned or controlled at year-end 2025 (50% owned / 50% controlled), with heavy use of option contracts and land-banking ($209.6M of non-refundable deposits controlling about $2.1B of land). Sumitomo specifically cited TPH's California regulatory expertise, premium brand, and financial discipline as strategic attractions.

The standalone FY2025 results show a cyclical downturn in progress, the context that motivated management to sell.

Revenue fell about 23% year-over-year, entirely volume-driven (ASP held flat near $680K). Deliveries declined 23%, net new orders fell 24% (4,292 vs. 5,657), and monthly absorption dropped 23% (2.4 vs. 3.1 per community). Net income nearly halved (−47%), with diluted EPS falling from $4.83 to $2.72 (a decline of about 44%). Gross margin compressed from 23.3% to 21.0%, hit by $31.1M of inventory impairments (about 90 bps) plus higher incentives, while SG&A delevered to 12.6% of home sales revenue from 10.8% on lower volume. Backlog collapsed roughly 42–43% to 862 units / $670M from 1,517 units / $1.16B, pointing to weak 2026 deliveries. Operating cash flow fell $535M to $161M, reflecting lower income plus $258M more cash tied up in inventory.

The balance sheet was strong and a key reason a buyer paid up. Altman Z-Score 3.03 (safe zone); debt-to-equity 0.50; homebuilding debt-to-capital 25.0%; net homebuilding debt-to-net-capital just 3.5%. Cash of $982.8M against $1.8B total liquidity, with wide covenant cushions (tangible net worth $3.15B vs. $2.34B required; interest coverage 6.4x vs. 1.5x minimum). Stockholders' equity stood at $3.32B against $3.31B retained earnings. The company repurchased 8.55M shares (about 8.6% of float) at an average $32.42 ($277M) in 2025, well below the eventual $47 deal price.

The Q1 2026 results, the last reported as a public company, continued the compression: GAAP EPS of roughly $0.08 against ~$0.17 consensus and revenue of $507.9M. The earnings surprise record over the trailing four quarters was one beat and three misses, and consensus estimates were falling sharply (current-year EPS revised down 26.5% over 30 days and 33.9% over 90 days). None of this matters going forward, because the residual reporting reflects a business now operating inside Sumitomo, not a public equity.

## Growth Analysis

There is no forward growth to underwrite for public-market investors. The equity has been extinguished. The standalone trajectory entering the merger was negative across every leading indicator: orders, backlog, absorption, and consensus EPS all declining sharply. The 2026 delivery base was set to be materially lower than 2025 given the backlog collapse.

The growth case that remains is Sumitomo's, not the public shareholder's. The parent intends to fold TPH's roughly 6,000 annual deliveries into a five-builder U.S. platform delivering around 15,000 units a year across 18 states, comparable to the fifth-largest U.S. builder (per Sumitomo's own combined-group framing), advancing its "Mission TREEING 2030" target of 23,000 U.S. homes a year by 2030. That is a private-company integration plan; it confers nothing on a public investor because no public investor remains.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is fully resolved. The price was fixed at $47.00 per share in cash, and the deal closed. The stock traded at ~$46.95 through the arb window, a few cents under the consideration reflecting time value and a small residual close-risk discount that has now disappeared. Sole analyst coverage (Evercore ISI) set its price target at exactly $47.00 with a Hold rating, mirroring the deal terms.

The $47.00 takeout valued TPH at roughly 1.25x year-end book equity of about $37.6 per share and at a price-to-trailing-EPS near 17x on depressed FY2025 EPS, a premium on a cyclically trough earnings base. It sat roughly 29% above the pre-deal close and about 45% above the 2025 average buyback price near $32. For a soft homebuilder facing a sharp backlog decline into 2026, the board secured a defensible value realization.

For a prospective buyer today, there is no asymmetric upside. Paying ~$46.95 for $47.00 of realized cash offers a few cents of theoretical spread that has already been distributed; in practice the security is gone. This is why the valuation score is minimal: not because the business is impaired, but because price relative to the only achievable outcome (cash already received) leaves nothing to capture. The stale "$4.0B market cap, 20x forward P/E" framing in the data feed describes a company that no longer trades.

## Competitive Landscape

As a standalone entity, TPH competed against larger, better-capitalized national builders (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup) that carry lower costs of capital, a structural disadvantage the 10-K flagged explicitly and a logical motivation to join a deep-pocketed parent. TPH differentiated on premium brand positioning (average selling price ~$680K vs. the volume builders' lower price points), design (the LivingSmart platform), and California regulatory expertise that Sumitomo prized.

The broader industry was in an affordability-constrained, demand-soft cycle through 2025: elevated mortgage rates, cautious consumer sentiment, rising resale and new-home inventory (notably Texas), and heavy incentive use pressuring margins across the group. TPH's order, absorption, and margin declines were consistent with industry-wide conditions rather than company-specific share loss. Competitive trajectory is moot post-close: TPH's brand and roughly 160 communities are being retained as a Sumitomo subsidiary, with the strategy now set by the parent.

## Risk Assessment

The operative risks shifted entirely to merger-related items, all of which are now resolved. Closing conditions (majority shareholder vote, HSR antitrust clearance, no injunction, no material adverse effect) were satisfied, and the deal closed within roughly three months for a cross-border acquirer of a California-heavy builder. There was no financing condition. Routine plaintiff-firm "fair price" investigations (the Ademi Firm and others) were announced but did not impede the close. The $82.336M termination fee never triggered.

The underlying business risks (housing cyclicality and rate sensitivity, affordability constraints, California concentration including a 10-year strict-liability construction-defect tail, wildfire/water/earthquake exposure, the January 2025 Southern California wildfires, AB 32/solar-mandate regulatory load, tariff/input-cost inflation, subcontractor labor availability, and assumed WRECO (Weyerhaeuser, 2014) construction-defect liabilities) are now Sumitomo's to manage, not a public investor's.

The score trajectory captures the mechanical risk collapse: Risk Factors fell from 4.5 to 2.0 as the equity moved from a live, cyclically pressured business to a closed transaction. The low score reflects that there is no recoverable equity upside, not an elevated chance of capital loss; a buyer at the quoted price simply has nothing left to gain.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** None remaining for a public investor. The bull case played out in full: the board secured $47.00 cash on a trough earnings base, and shareholders were paid. Anyone who owned TPH into the February announcement and held captured that premium. There is no forward bull case because there is no forward equity.

**Bear Case:** Also moot. The only residual risk during the arb window was deal break (no-MAE, antitrust, vote), which did not materialize. For a hypothetical buyer at the current quote, the bear case is simply that the cash has already been distributed and the shares no longer trade: total opportunity cost of capital with no offsetting return.

TPH was a merger-arb instrument from February to May 2026 and is now a completed cash event at $47.00/share. The standalone fundamentals were deteriorating, which is precisely why management sold into a strong premium. That was a sensible outcome for shareholders. It is not an opportunity for new capital.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Timing window:** Not applicable. The arb window is closed and the security has been deregistered.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** The reported short interest with 2.1 days to cover was merger-arb positioning (shorting the modest spread above $47), not a fundamental short. It carried no squeeze characteristics and is now irrelevant.

**Borrow and options signal:** No borrow market exists for a delisted security. Options pricing carries no signal now that the deal has closed.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Undefined. A short cannot be established or carried on an extinguished equity, and the consideration was fixed at $47.00 cash regardless of any view.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** There is no catalyst, no timing trigger, and no tradable security.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This recommendation is terminal rather than time-bounded, because the security has been extinguished.

**Re-entry criteria:** None. TPH common stock no longer exists. There is no path for a public-equity position in Tri Pointe Homes; the company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Forestry America. The only way to gain exposure to the underlying business is through Sumitomo Forestry (TSE: 1911), a separate analysis with entirely different drivers (Japanese forestry/wood-products conglomerate, multi-builder U.S. platform, cross-border currency and governance factors).

**Timeline:** Permanent. There are no headwinds to wait out, and no equity to re-enter.

For practical purposes: any holder of record as of the close received $47.00 per share in cash. No further action or monitoring of TPH is warranted.

## Conclusion

TPH is a closed transaction, not an investable equity. Sumitomo Forestry acquired Tri Pointe Homes for $47.00 per share in cash, the deal completed on May 14, 2026, and the stock was delisted and deregistered. The market data in this feed reflects the pre-close arbitrage period and is stale as a forward indicator. The standalone business was a conservatively levered, premium-positioned homebuilder whose earnings were cyclically depressed by affordability-driven demand softness, the setup in which a deep-pocketed strategic buyer can pay a healthy premium on trough earnings. Shareholders realized that premium.

We rate TPH **AVOID**. The rating is mechanical: the merger-arb spread has collapsed into the cash consideration, the cash has been distributed, and there is no remaining upside to capture and no tradable security. New capital should look elsewhere. The score trajectory (4.0 → 3.9, AVOID → AVOID) and the collapse in the Risk Factors and Valuation dimensions both reflect this resolution rather than any new deterioration in the underlying business.
