**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Electric Power is one of the largest regulated electric utilities in the United States, serving roughly 5.6 million customers across 11 states and operating one of the nation's largest transmission networks (~40,000 miles). The investment case rests on a single, well-supported structural fact: AEP's service territories (Ohio/Columbus, ERCOT/Texas, and the PJM Mid-Atlantic) sit at the center of U.S. data-center development, and the company has converted that geography into 63 GW of contracted 2030 load (~90% data centers, up from 56 GW the prior quarter), a $78B five-year capital plan (raised 8% in a single quarter), and a 7-9% long-term EPS growth target. The contracted-load build is well-corroborated in the company's disclosures and confirms the demand is real and arriving.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and measurable. Despite the load-growth narrative, consensus EPS estimates have not moved: current-year sits at $6.36 (unchanged over 90 days, -0.1%) and next-year at $6.86 (flat). The growth thesis is being financed near current valuation: AEP priced a $2.6B common equity offering at $127.00/share in May 2026, diluting the rate-base growth at the per-share line. A regulated utility funding a capital plan that vastly exceeds operating cash flow with debt and equity, into a higher-for-longer rate path, faces a rising cost of both capital sources. The structural demand is confirmed; the conversion of rate-base growth into rising forward EPS is not yet visible in consensus.

AEP trades at ~18.6x next-year EPS versus a 21.1x electric-utility peer median, a discount partly justified by higher leverage (D/E ~2.6x) and the dilution load. The stock offers a credible mid-to-high-single-digit total-return profile (EPS growth plus a ~3.0% dividend) with downside protection from the regulated franchise, but lacks the rising-estimate momentum that would justify an above-peer rating today. The two-sided case clears at a 6.0 overall, consistent with a confident growth-oriented hold.

## Company Overview

AEP operates through state-regulated vertically integrated and transmission-and-distribution subsidiaries: AEP Ohio (OPCo), AEP Texas, Appalachian Power (APCo), Indiana Michigan Power (I&M), Public Service Company of Oklahoma (PSO), and Southwestern Electric Power (SWEPCo). Its most strategically valuable asset is the FERC-regulated transmission franchise held through AEP Transmission (AEPTCo), which earns attractive formula-based returns and is the primary vehicle for monetizing the data-center load wave.

The business model is a regulated monopoly: earnings grow by investing capital into rate base at an authorized return. The differentiator versus peers is location: AEP's franchises sit directly in the path of AI/data-center electricity demand, which is reactivating utility load growth after roughly two decades of flat demand. The thesis is a utility-side bet on AI load growth: rate-base expansion funded by hyperscaler demand, earning regulated returns.

The C-suite was substantially rebuilt in 2024-2025. Bill Fehrman (ex-Berkshire Hathaway Energy) is now Chairman, President & CEO; Trevor Mihalik (ex-Sempra) is CFO. A new "President, Nuclear Development" role (Alicia Knapp) signals intent to pursue SMRs/new nuclear to serve always-on data-center load.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was approximately $21.9B, up roughly 11% YoY, strong for a regulated utility and reflecting rate increases and load growth. Net income rose to roughly $3.7B from $2.98B in 2024, $2.21B in 2023, and $2.31B in 2022, a clear upward trajectory. Operating EPS (the company-selected measure) rose to $5.97 in 2025 from $5.62 in 2024 and $5.25 in 2023, steady 6-7% growth. Operating margin runs ~24%.

The quarterly earnings record is solid: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 EPS of $1.64 beating the $1.58 estimate (+3.5%) and the trailing four-quarter pattern showing no deterioration in delivery against consensus.

The balance sheet carries D/E of ~2.6x and a current ratio below 1. For a regulated utility funding long-lived rate-base assets with long-term debt, this is normal and authorized by regulators. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.9 nominally flags "distress" but is structurally inapplicable here: the model penalizes the exact capital structure regulators sanction and that recoverable, stable cash flows support. The real risks are regulatory and financing-related, not solvency.

The crux is cash flow and capital allocation. AEP is in a heavy-investment phase: the five-year capital plan was raised to $78B (an 8% increase from the $72B plan released three months earlier). Capex vastly exceeds operating cash flow, so the gap is funded by debt and equity. This is the structurally-negative-FCF profile that makes the financing path the dominant variable. AEP has used capital recycling to soften the equity load, including a ~$2.82B sale of a minority stake in its Ohio/Indiana-Michigan transmission holdings, but still priced $2.6B of fresh common equity at $127.00 in May 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The demand signal is corroborated by AEP's own contracted-load figures, with the 2030 build reaching 63 GW after signing an additional 7 GW in Q1 2026. The investment thesis is a utility-side bet on broad-based AI/data-center electricity demand, which industry estimates put at ~$700B+ of industry-wide Big Tech AI capex in 2026, driving load growth after two decades of flat demand. This breadth argues the demand is structural rather than a single-company story, which raises the durability of AEP's growth thesis relative to a stand-alone narrative.

The 7-9% EPS growth target through 2030, funded by the capital plan, is credible if execution and rate recovery hold. The discrete catalyst worth monitoring is the rolling approval of large-load tariffs (minimum-take/credit-support provisions), which protect ratepayers and shareholders and de-risk the load monetization on a per-docket basis.

The constraint on the growth score is that none of this is yet showing up in consensus forward earnings, which are flat across the revision window. This is the key distinction from a genuine earnings-inflection stock. AEP is a steady compounder whose rate-base growth is being partially offset at the per-share line by equity dilution. The strong inflection-stock signals (estimate revisions >30%, treating analyst targets as floors) do not apply because the estimates are not rising. Growth is real and durable, but it is mid-to-high-single-digit per-share growth, not an accelerating re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

AEP holds the most favorable structural position in the regulated-utility space. Its transmission and generation franchises sit directly in the path of the AI/data-center demand surge across Ohio, ERCOT/Texas, and PJM Mid-Atlantic. The competitive moat is the rate-base franchise itself, a regulated monopoly earning authorized returns on invested capital, and the trajectory is improving because load growth after two decades of stagnation expands the addressable rate base.

The binding constraint is interconnection. PJM queues and generation shortfalls are the bottleneck throttling AEP's biggest opportunity, which is why new CEO Fehrman has publicly criticized PJM and placed AEP's membership "under review," even floating exit from regional grids. FERC and federal officials are simultaneously pressuring grids to accelerate AI data-center connections. The outcome of PJM's structure is partly outside AEP's control and is both the most differentiated risk and a potential catalyst: faster interconnection reform would accelerate load monetization. The competitive position score of 7.0 reflects a widening structural tailwind, tempered by the regulatory friction on cost recovery and the politically charged cost-allocation fight as residential bills rise.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution and recovery risk.** The capital plan must be deployed on time and on budget and recovered through state and FERC rate cases. Cost overruns or unfavorable regulatory outcomes directly compress returns.

**Equity dilution / financing risk.** The May 2026 equity offering was priced essentially at market, financing growth near current valuation. Future plan increases likely imply more equity. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, both interest expense and the cost of equity rise, and the negative-FCF model is most exposed to this dynamic of any utility sub-type.

**Data-center concentration.** ~90% of the contracted 2030 load is data centers. If the AI capex cycle slows or large-load customers fail to ramp, AEP risks stranded or under-recovered investment. The large-load tariffs (minimum-take/credit-support provisions) are the mitigant and the key regulatory battleground.

**PJM standoff.** A brand-new outsider CEO publicly threatening to exit the nation's largest RTO is unusual and signals genuine frustration that interconnection delays are throttling growth, with an uncertain resolution.

**Rate affordability / political backlash.** Surging power bills are a growing political flashpoint; regulators may resist passing data-center infrastructure costs to residential ratepayers.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that several of these are live, partly binary, and not fully priced, particularly the dilution cadence and the PJM structural uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($173):** Large-load take-or-pay tariffs are approved across the remaining jurisdictions, de-risking the contracted pipeline. PJM interconnection reform accelerates, allowing AEP to deploy the capital plan and convert it to rate base faster than modeled. Consensus EPS estimates begin rising as load monetizes, and the stock re-rates toward the upper end of the peer range on ~20x a rising next-year EPS. SMR optionality adds a long-dated leg. Total return reaches the high teens to low twenties annually.

**Bear case ($108):** Rate cases deliver below-requested ROEs, affordability backlash slows cost recovery, and the dilution cadence accelerates as the plan grows again. A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs against the negative-FCF profile, compressing per-share growth below the 7% target. The PJM standoff delays load monetization. The multiple compresses toward the low-leverage discount the balance sheet warrants, implying a high-teens decline.

**Base case ($148):** AEP executes roughly to its 7-9% EPS target with continued dilution trimming per-share growth toward the lower end. The stock earns a modest re-rating toward ~19-20x next-year EPS as tariff approvals accumulate, delivering ~16% price upside plus the ~3.0% dividend over 12-18 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-18 month growth-oriented hold tied to large-load tariff approvals and the conversion of contracted load into rate base.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Consensus EPS estimates begin rising (current-year or next-year revisions turning positive by >2% over a 90-day window), confirming the load growth is reaching the per-share line.
- Approval of additional large-load take-or-pay tariffs across the remaining pending jurisdictions, materially de-risking the contracted pipeline.
- Stabilization or reduction of the equity-issuance cadence (no incremental dilutive raise beyond the $2.6B already priced) while the capital plan proceeds.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- An adverse rate-case outcome materially below requested ROE, or a regulatory ruling that blocks data-center cost recovery from large-load customers.
- A guidance cut to the 7-9% EPS growth target or a withdrawal of the $6.15-6.45 2026 operating-EPS guide.
- A new equity raise materially larger than telegraphed, signaling the plan can no longer be funded without accelerating per-share dilution.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the rolling large-load tariff docket decisions are the natural checkpoints; monitor estimate revisions at each print.

## Conclusion

AEP is the cleanest regulated-utility expression of the data-center load surge, with 63 GW of contracted 2030 load, a $78B rate-base plan, and a credible 7-9% EPS growth target, all at a ~18.6x next-year multiple that sits below the 21.1x peer median. The franchise provides genuine downside protection and a ~3.0% dividend, and the structural tailwind is broad-based rather than speculative.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth rationale. The specific, falsifiable concern preventing a BUY: consensus EPS estimates are flat-to-negative over 90 days despite the load narrative, the May equity raise dilutes the rate-base growth at the per-share line, and a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of a structurally-negative-FCF financing model. The rate-base growth is real but is not yet converting into rising forward EPS, and analyst sentiment is degrading. When estimate revisions turn positive and the remaining large-load tariffs clear, the setup converts to a BUY; until then, the risk/reward is attractive but not asymmetric, and the low-beta profile caps relative upside.
