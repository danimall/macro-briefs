**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Leidos is one of the largest pure-play federal IT and mission-services primes, generating $17.17B in FY2025 revenue with ~86-87% derived from the U.S. government and ~49% from the Department of War and Intelligence Community. The business produces genuinely high returns on capital (ROE 29.7%, operating margin 12.3%, FCF margin ~9.5%) and converted FY2025 operating cash flow of $1.75B into ~$1.63B of free cash flow against just $125M of capex. On trailing EPS of $11.14 and a $112 share price, the stock trades at ~10x earnings and ~9x forward consensus of $12.35 — a discount to the diversified-support-services peer median P/E of 21.7x and to the closest comparables (CACI 21.7x, SAIC 12.9x, KBR 10.4x).

The stock has fallen ~46% from its November 2025 high of $205.77 and ~24% since early May 2026, and the decline reflects fundamentals rather than a price-versus-fundamentals dislocation. Q1 FY2026 showed operating income down 4.2% to $508M and operating margin compressing 100bps to 11.5%, with Homeland operating income down 46% and Defense down 16% on write-downs and integration costs. Two sell-side firms downgraded within days of each other in June 2026 (Jefferies to Hold, cutting its target from $185 to $140; BofA to Neutral) on margin and healthcare-franchise concerns. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than falling off a cliff, but they are not rising either: the current-year consensus has barely moved (+0.3% over 90 days) while next-year has drifted down 0.7%.

We rate LDOS a HOLD. The cash-flow-and-cheapness case is real, and at ~9x forward earnings the downside is bounded by a durable FCF engine and a $49.0B backlog. But the bull case requires either margin stabilization or resolution of the policy overhang, and neither has arrived. The Warfighter executive order poses a specific, underpriced threat to the buyback-and-dividend model that has driven per-share growth, goodwill now exceeds equity after the $2.4B Entrust deal, and a DOJ antitrust grand-jury subpoena remains open and unquantified. This is a quality business with deteriorating incremental margins and a structural policy question, a setup that warrants patience rather than conviction in either direction.

## Company Overview

Leidos (Reston, VA; ~50,000 employees) is a federal technology and services contractor. Its competitive position rests on scale, incumbency on large IDIQ contract vehicles, ~53% of its workforce holding security clearances, and mission-specific franchises in managed health services, air-traffic systems, cyber, and hypersonics. It competes against Booz Allen, CACI, SAIC, GDIT, Peraton, Accenture Federal, Amentum, and the defense primes (LMT, NOC, RTX, LHX).

Effective the first day of FY2026, Leidos realigned from its four legacy segments (National Security & Digital, Health & Civil, Commercial & International, Defense Systems) into four new ones: Intelligence & Digital, Health, Homeland, and Defense. Under the old FY2025 structure, the segment economics revealed where value concentrates: Health & Civil produced ~$5.07B in revenue at a 23.7% operating margin, roughly triple the profitability of the rest of the portfolio, anchored by VA disability and behavioral exams, FAA air-traffic systems, and the DoD/VA electronic health record. National Security & Digital contributed ~$7.6B at a 10.0% margin, while Commercial & International and Defense Systems each ran ~$2.2B with margins recovering from ~4.6% toward ~7.2%.

The high-margin health franchise is both the profit engine and, per BofA's June downgrade, the source of near-term "healthcare headwinds," a dependency that makes any deceleration in that segment disproportionately consequential to the consolidated margin.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings trajectory.** Revenue grew from $14.4B (FY22) to $15.4B (FY23), $16.66B (FY24), and $17.17B (FY25), roughly a 6% CAGR. The FY23 net income dip to $199M was driven by a $596M non-cash goodwill impairment at the Security Enterprise Solutions (SES) airport/border-detection unit; excluding it, underlying earnings power was intact. FY25 net income was $1.45B with EPS up 15%, and FY25 operating income rose 15% to $2.11B as margin expanded 110bps to 12.3% on program wins and efficiency.

**Cash generation.** The FCF story is the core of the bull thesis. FY25 operating cash flow was $1.75B against only $125M capex, yielding ~$1.63B FCF, an FCF yield of ~11.6% at the current $14.1B market cap. Capital was returned aggressively: $400M open-market buybacks plus a $500M accelerated share repurchase plus $211M in dividends, taking share count from ~131M to ~126M.

**The balance-sheet step-up.** To fund the Entrust acquisition ($2.4B cash, closed March 27, 2026), Leidos issued $600M of 4.10% notes due 2029 and $800M of 5.00% notes due 2036, and drew $300M of commercial paper. Post-deal (April 3, 2026), total debt rose to $6.3B, goodwill jumped from $6.34B to $8.09B (~53% of assets), and cash fell to $457M. The leverage covenant permits up to 4.5x net-debt/EBITDA for four quarters post-acquisition (versus a normal 3.75x cap), and management confirmed compliance. Altman Z of 2.82 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for an asset-light, goodwill-heavy services firm rather than a distress signal. This is not a balance-sheet-risk story, but leverage has stepped up meaningfully and the cushion is thinner than it was a year ago.

**Q1 FY2026.** Revenue rose 3.7% to $4.40B, but operating income fell 4.2% to $508M and operating margin compressed 100bps to 11.5%. Diluted EPS was $2.56 versus $2.77. The drivers: $35M of Entrust acquisition/integration costs, a $23M UK pension buy-out settlement loss, and a net unfavorable $9M in contract estimate changes versus a favorable $30M swing in the prior-year quarter, a ~$39M year-over-year deterioration. Homeland operating income dropped 46% and Defense fell 16% on write-downs and integration costs. Some of this is one-time (integration, pension), but the negative EAC swing on a book where 43% of revenue is firm-fixed-price is the item that matters most; it speaks to incremental margin quality rather than headline growth.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line story is stable rather than accelerating. FY2026 revenue guidance is ~$18.2B (roughly in line with consensus), which implies ~6% growth including the Entrust contribution. Backlog stands at $49.0B with $9.7B funded, solid coverage of forward revenue, though the funded ratio is modest, which is normal for federal services subject to annual appropriations.

The forward growth strategy is visible in capital allocation. Entrust pushes Leidos into commercial energy-infrastructure engineering (grid modernization, utility program management), diversifying away from pure federal exposure as part of the "NorthStar 2030" plan. Kudu Dynamics (closed May 2025, $293M) adds AI-enabled cyber to Intelligence & Digital. The SES contribution into a JV with Analogic/Altaris (Leidos retaining a 41.5% minority stake, targeted to close 2H FY2026) sheds the chronically underperforming detection-hardware unit. This is rational portfolio transformation: buying growth and margin (energy, AI cyber) while jettisoning the commoditized problem child.

Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call. Current-year consensus is essentially flat (+0.1% over 30 days), next-quarter estimates are flat, and there is no evidence of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection. The company has beaten in each of the last three quarters (5.9%, 3.8%, 10.3%), so consensus is not aggressive, but the growth thesis here is durable-defensive compounding of ~6% top-line with per-share growth historically amplified by buybacks, a lever now under regulatory question (see Risk Assessment). We score growth prospects a 5.5, down from the 7.5 level of early 2026, reflecting the margin compression and the loss of the buyback amplifier as a reliable driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At $112, LDOS trades at ~9x forward consensus EPS and ~10x trailing. The federal-services peer set is heterogeneous: CACI at 21.7x, SAIC at 12.9x, KBR at 10.4x, RBA at 49.3x, and Amentum at 36.8x, with a median of 21.7x. Leidos sits at the low end alongside KBR and below SAIC despite superior margins and returns on capital (ROE 29.7% versus the peer group). On absolute FCF, the ~11.6% FCF yield is compelling for a defensive, non-cyclical franchise.

The valuation question is whether the discount reflects mispricing or structural impairment. Our read is that it is a mix, which is why we score valuation a 6.0 rather than higher. The cheapness is real, and analyst price targets (a median of $152 and a mean of $151 against a $112 spot) imply meaningful upside if the business simply holds. But under the guardrails for cheap federal-services names, we treat a low multiple as a warning to look for structural impairment rather than an automatic buy. There are three specific reasons the discount may be partly justified: (1) margin compression that persists if the negative EAC swing recurs on the FFP book; (2) the Warfighter EO threatening the buyback engine that has historically converted flat-to-modest EPS into per-share growth; and (3) goodwill now exceeding equity, raising impairment risk if the energy or health forecasts soften. The stock is cheap on cash flow, but the market is pricing a real deterioration in incremental economics and a real question mark over capital return.

The analyst targets span an unusually wide $110 to $225, reflecting genuine disagreement about whether the healthcare and policy headwinds are transitory or structural. We do not treat the $152 median as a floor here, because the estimate-revision backdrop is flat rather than rising and the multiple is depressed for identifiable reasons, not simply lagging an accelerating fundamental. Fair value on ~$12.35 forward EPS at a 12-13x multiple (a discount to the higher-quality peer CACI, a premium to distressed KBR) supports a base case around $150, which is where consensus sits.

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos operates in a defensive, non-cyclical niche, but the FY2026 policy backdrop is unusually disruptive. The moat (scale, clearances, incumbency) remains intact, and the high-margin health franchise (VA exams, FAA air traffic, DoD/VA EHR) is difficult to displace. But three forces cut against the competitive trajectory. First, the FAR overhaul (EO 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement") could invite commercial and non-traditional entrants into defense IT, pressuring win rates over time. Second, DOGE-style efficiency pressure, continuing resolutions, and an actual DHS shutdown (February 13 to April 30, 2026) inject demand-timing uncertainty directly into the newly named Homeland segment. Third, the Warfighter EO's contracting provisions raise the cost of being classified an "underperformer."

The company's response is coherent (pivoting toward commercial energy via Entrust and AI cyber via Kudu while shedding SES) but execution and integration risk is the swing factor. Entrust was priced at a heavy goodwill and intangible multiple ($1.75B goodwill plus $564M intangibles on $637M net identifiable assets), so the deal's success depends on unproven synergies. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating on the balance of these forces; we score it 5.5, down from 7.5 earlier in the year, primarily on the policy-driven win-rate and margin uncertainty rather than on any confirmed share loss.

## Risk Assessment

**Warfighter EO — the underpriced structural risk.** Future DoW contracts, including renewals, are expected to carry provisions that restrict stock buybacks and dividends during any period the Secretary of War deems the company "underperforming," restrict executive comp tied to delivery/production/domestic investment, and require a board-approved remediation plan within 15 days of an underperformance determination. This directly threatens the capital-return model that has amplified per-share growth. A standardized screen would miss it entirely; it is the single most consequential idiosyncratic risk in the file.

**Margin/EAC risk.** 43% of revenue is firm-fixed-price, and Q1 FY26 already showed a $39M year-over-year negative swing in contract estimate changes. Multi-year FFP work carries structural inflation exposure. If EAC deterioration recurs, the market's healthcare/margin thesis is validated.

**Goodwill concentration.** At ~53% of assets (~$8.1B versus ~$5B equity) after Entrust, goodwill-impairment risk is elevated if energy or health forecasts soften. SES already impaired $596M in FY23, which is precisely why management is contributing it into a JV.

**DOJ antitrust grand-jury subpoena (August 2022).** An ongoing criminal investigation into three Intelligence Group procurements in 2021-2022 remains unresolved and unquantified, a low-probability, high-severity tail specific to Leidos.

**Other.** CMMC certification phasing (Nov 2025-Nov 2028) gates DoW eligibility; government-shutdown risk has already materialized once this year; and detection-system product-liability tail risk persists in the residual SES exposure.

We score risk factors 4.5: the presence of a specific, dated policy threat to capital return, an open criminal subpoena, and goodwill exceeding equity are the kind of concentrated, partly-unpriced catalysts that warrant a below-neutral score. Some of this drawdown has already priced these concerns, which is why the score is not lower.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 44.8% sits rich to the 31.6% one-year historical vol (a 13.2-point spread) and at the 74th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (44.8% near-term versus 39.2% at six months). This is the market pricing imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst and the unresolved policy overhang. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.5% near-term, widening only modestly to -3.2% at six months) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.25, so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the fundamental caution and the 13.2% price decline since the prior snapshot. The implied ±10.3% move on the nearest expiry reflects genuine uncertainty about whether margins stabilize. For a holder, the rich IV argues against buying protection at these levels and is more favorable for premium sellers than buyers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150-180).** Leidos is a defensive FCF machine at ~9x forward earnings and an ~11.6% FCF yield, cheaper than higher-quality peers despite best-in-class returns on capital. Q1 margin compression is largely one-time (Entrust integration, UK pension settlement) and reverses through the year; the backlog underwrites ~6% growth; SES divestiture and the Entrust/Kudu pivot improve mix and diversify away from federal risk; and analyst targets (median $152) imply 35%+ upside if the business simply holds. At $150 (12-13x forward EPS), the stock re-rates toward its own history and peer quality.

**Bear case ($90-100).** The negative EAC swing on the FFP book is a leading indicator of persistent incremental-margin deterioration, and the health franchise faces genuine, not transitory, headwinds. The Warfighter EO restricts the buyback engine that drove per-share growth, removing the multiple's key support. Goodwill exceeding equity sets up an impairment if energy or health forecasts soften, and the DOJ subpoena remains an open tail. In this case EPS stalls near $12, the multiple stays compressed at 8x on policy overhang, and the stock drifts toward $95-100, ~10-15% below current levels.

The base case sits near $150: consensus holds, margins stabilize but do not re-expand meaningfully, and the policy overhang lingers without a resolving event.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected early August 2026), which will test whether the Q1 EAC deterioration recurs and whether Entrust integration costs are receding. The SES/Analogic JV close (2H FY2026) is a secondary catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin recovers back toward 12.5% in Q2/Q3 FY2026, with no further negative contract-estimate swings, confirming Q1 was integration- and pension-driven.
- Positive estimate-revision inflection — current-year consensus rising >3% over a 90-day window, indicating the healthcare-headwind narrative is resolving.
- Explicit clarity that the Warfighter EO will not materially constrain Leidos's buyback/dividend program (e.g., no "underperformance" determination on major renewals).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of negative EAC swings or margin compression below ~11%, signaling structural rather than transitory deterioration.
- Any DoW "underperformance" determination or contract language that restricts capital return.
- A goodwill-impairment charge on the energy or health assets, or a material adverse development in the DOJ antitrust investigation.

## Conclusion

LDOS presents a genuine tension between a cheap, cash-generative federal-services franchise and a set of specific, partly-unpriced headwinds. At ~9x forward earnings, an ~11.6% FCF yield, and a ~46% drawdown from the November high, the downside is bounded and the risk/reward has improved with the price. But the drawdown reflects fundamentals rather than a simple price-versus-fundamentals dislocation: Q1 operating margin compressed 100bps, two analysts downgraded on margin and healthcare concerns within days of each other, estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, and the Warfighter EO poses a real threat to the capital-return model that historically converted modest revenue growth into per-share value.

The equity lessons that inform this call cut in offsetting directions. Cheapness alone is treated as a warning rather than a signal in this environment, and Industrials BUYs carry negative sector alpha by default, both arguing against upgrading on the discount alone. There is no +30% estimate-revision inflection to override that caution, and the stock lacks the confirmed catalyst that would justify a BUY. At the same time, the balance sheet is sound, the FCF engine is intact, and the price already reflects meaningful skepticism, which argues against an AVOID.

We rate LDOS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the evidence does not yet support a confident call in either direction. The path to a BUY runs through margin stabilization in the next one to two quarters and clarity on the policy overhang; the path to an AVOID runs through recurring EAC deterioration or an adverse Warfighter/DOJ development. Investors already owning the stock are being paid an ~11.6% FCF yield to wait for that resolution; new capital should await the Q2 print before committing.
