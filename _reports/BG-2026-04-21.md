## Executive Summary

Bunge Global SA completed its transformational acquisition of Viterra in July 2025, creating a combined entity with $70.3B in revenue, $44.5B in total assets, and a leading position in global oilseed processing and grain merchandising. The deal was intended to deliver scale, geographic diversification, and synergies that management projects will support mid-cycle EPS of ~$13 and a growth path to $15+ by 2030. At $4.91 in actual FY2025 EPS, the current profitability run rate is 62% below that baseline target.

The financial reality post-Viterra is concerning. Total debt surged 125% to $14.1B, free cash flow turned negative (-$879M), gross margins compressed to 4.8% (from 8.1% two years prior), and the Grain Merchandising reporting unit sits just 9% above its impairment threshold. Interest expense of $628M now exceeds net income in some quarters. Credit agencies upgraded the combined entity on scale and diversification, but the underlying earnings power has not yet justified the capital deployed. Spruce Point Capital's March 2026 short report — characterizing Bunge as a "non-economic roll-up" with a $1.6B cumulative capital deficit — raises legitimate questions about whether buybacks and dividends have been funded by balance sheet deterioration rather than organic cash generation.

The bull case rests on Viterra synergies materializing ($200-300M targeted), biofuels demand growth via the Bunge-Chevron JV, and a cyclical recovery in processing margins from depressed 2025 levels. These are plausible but unconfirmed. The bear case — that Bunge overpaid for a commodity asset at cycle peak, now carries excessive leverage into a trade-war-disrupted agricultural market, and faces concentrated shareholder overhang from Glencore/CPP — is at least equally supported by the data. With identifiable structural deterioration in margins, cash flow, and leverage, the risk-reward profile is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Bunge Global SA, incorporated in Switzerland and headquartered in Chesterfield, Missouri, is one of the world's largest agribusiness companies. Founded in 1818, the company connects agricultural producers to end consumers across food, feed, and fuel supply chains in over 50 countries with approximately 34,000 employees.

Post-Viterra, the company operates through four reportable segments: Soybean Processing & Refining ($36.3B revenue, $1.225B EBIT), Softseed Processing & Refining ($11.3B revenue, $521M EBIT), Other Oilseeds Processing & Refining ($4.6B revenue, $118M EBIT), and Grain Merchandising & Milling ($18.1B revenue, $465M EBIT). Bunge ranks as the leading global oilseed processor by capacity, a top-three global grain merchandiser by volume, and the leading packaged plant-based oils seller worldwide.

The Viterra acquisition, completed July 2, 2025, for approximately $5.3B in stock and $1.9B in cash, fundamentally reshaped the company. Former Viterra shareholders — Glencore (~17%), CPP Investments (~14%), and BC Investment Management (~3%) — now collectively hold approximately 34% of outstanding shares with board nomination rights.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $70.3B grew 32% YoY, driven almost entirely by the Viterra consolidation in H2 2025. The revenue growth masks severe profitability erosion:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Trend |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Revenue | $59.5B | $53.1B | $70.3B | Viterra-driven |
| Gross Margin | 8.1% | 6.4% | 4.8% | ▼▼ |
| Net Income | $2.24B | $1.14B | $816M | ▼▼ |
| Diluted EPS | $13.66 | $7.99 | $4.91 | ▼▼ |
| ROE | ~20%+ | ~8% | 5.3% | ▼▼ |

Three consecutive years of margin compression reflect both post-Ukraine commodity margin normalization and the dilutive impact of Viterra's lower-margin grain merchandising operations. Net margin of 1.2% and ROE of 5.3% are well below cost-of-equity for a company carrying this level of leverage.

At the segment level, Soybean Processing recovered 40% to $1.225B EBIT, while Softseed declined 21%, Other Oilseeds fell 45%, and Grain Merchandising's 14% improvement included a $155M corn milling divestiture gain. Excluding that gain, underlying Grain Merchandising performance deteriorated. Corporate costs widened to a $796M loss from $367M, reflecting acquisition-related charges and a $118M pension settlement loss.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

The balance sheet transformation post-Viterra is the central financial concern:

- Total debt: $14.1B (up 125% from $6.2B)
- Debt-to-equity: 2.8x
- Cash: $1.1B (down from $3.3B)
- Short-term debt + current maturities: $5.2B (up from $1.5B)
- Goodwill: $3.14B (up from $453M)
- Altman Z-Score: 2.56 (grey zone — moderate distress risk)

The $5.2B in near-term maturities creates refinancing pressure, though $9.1B of committed undrawn revolving facilities provides a liquidity cushion. Interest expense of $628M (+33% YoY) now represents 77% of net income — a dangerous level for a cyclical commodity business. Credit agencies upgraded Bunge (S&P to A-, Moody's Baa1), which provides market access but does not change the fundamental leverage equation.

The Grain Merchandising reporting unit's fair value sits just 9% above its carrying value. Global Cotton at 15% excess is also tight. Any combination of lower commodity margins, trade disruption, or integration difficulties could trigger write-downs against the $3.14B goodwill balance.

### Cash Flow: The Critical Weakness

FY2025 free cash flow was negative: $844M operating cash flow minus $1.72B capex equals -$879M.

- Operating cash flow declined 56% from $1.9B to $844M, driven by lower earnings and Viterra working capital absorption.
- Capex guidance for 2026 remains elevated at $1.5-1.7B.
- Despite negative FCF, Bunge returned $1.05B to shareholders via $551M in buybacks and $502M in dividends.
- This is the core of Spruce Point's critique: if FCF is negative and the company is simultaneously returning $1B+ to shareholders, the capital return is being funded by debt, not earnings. The $3B new buyback authorization compounds this concern.

For the shareholder return commitment (minimum 50% of discretionary cash flow) to be self-funding, Bunge needs OCF above approximately $3.4B (to cover $1.7B capex + $1.7B in returns). Current OCF of $844M is less than a quarter of that requirement.

## Growth Analysis

### The $13 Mid-Cycle EPS Bridge

Management's March 2026 Investor Day framed the investment case around a $13 mid-cycle EPS baseline with a growth path to $15+ by 2030. From the current $4.91, this requires:

1. **Viterra synergies ($200-300M targeted):** Integration is in early stages with costs still accruing. A dedicated Chief Transformation Officer (Pierre Mauger) has been appointed. Synergy realization typically takes 2-3 years post-close.

2. **Commodity cycle recovery:** The 2023 results ($13.66 EPS) were driven by Ukraine-invasion-induced margin expansion that represented a multi-decade outlier for the industry. Whether $13 represents "mid-cycle" or "favorable cycle" is debatable — it has been achieved in exactly one of the last three fiscal years.

3. **Biofuels demand growth:** The Bunge-Chevron JV targets renewable diesel feedstock. JPMorgan's upgrade cited favorable EPA renewable volume obligations. However, biofuels policy is subject to political change, and the 10-K risk factors specifically flag "significant decrease in demand for renewable diesel" as a risk to the JV.

4. **Share count reduction:** The $3B buyback program would retire approximately 30-35M shares at current prices (~$90), reducing the denominator by roughly 18-20%. This alone could lift EPS by $1.00-1.50 from a $4.91 base — if funded sustainably. At negative FCF, sustainability is questionable.

5. **Interest expense reduction:** As Viterra debt is refinanced or repaid, interest expense could decline $100-200M. However, this requires FCF generation to fund repayment, creating a circular dependency.

The bridge from $4.91 to $13 requires multiple simultaneous tailwinds — synergy execution, commodity recovery, biofuels policy support, and share reduction funded by improved cash flow. Any single leg failing makes the $13 target aspirational rather than achievable within the stated timeframe.

### Strategic Divestitures

Bunge has been actively optimizing its portfolio: North American corn milling ($470M, $155M gain), BP Bunge Bioenergia 50% ($195M gain), Spanish subsidiary BISA 40% ($206M), and European margarines/spreads (pending). These divestitures generate proceeds and high-return-on-capital exits, but they also reduce future earning power and make the $13 EPS target harder to reach organically.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $90 per share, Bunge trades at:
- ~18x trailing P/E on $4.91 EPS — expensive for commodity processing
- ~6.9x the $13 "mid-cycle" management target — cheap if achievable
- Approximately 0.07x price-to-sales — characteristic of razor-thin margin commodity businesses
- EV/EBITDA likely in the 8-10x range given ~$2.3B segment EBIT + depreciation

The valuation is the most favorable dimension. Analyst targets from JPMorgan ($134), BofA ($130), and Barclays ($145) imply 44-61% upside, suggesting the sell-side sees the $13 EPS thesis as credible. However, sell-side consensus often anchors to management guidance at Investor Days.

Spruce Point's counter-argument — that capital returns have been funded by balance sheet deterioration and the company has generated a cumulative $1.6B capital deficit — suggests the stock may be cheaper than peers because it deserves to be. If the $13 mid-cycle EPS proves to be peak-cycle rather than mid-cycle, the trailing multiple is less compelling.

Against peers: ADM trades at similar depressed multiples reflecting its own accounting/trading issues. Wilmar trades at lower multiples reflecting Asia discount. The private competitors (Cargill, Louis Dreyfus) are not directly comparable. Within the publicly traded ag-processing peer set, Bunge's multiple is roughly in line — suggesting the market prices the entire sector at normalized-cycle earnings rather than current depressed levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Bunge's competitive position is its strongest dimension. Post-Viterra, the company holds:
- Leading global oilseed processing capacity
- Top-three global grain merchandising position
- 47% of soybean processing capacity in South America (the world's largest soy-producing region)
- 53% of softseed processing in Europe
- Global infrastructure spanning production, logistics, and distribution in 50+ countries

The competitive moat in agribusiness derives from physical infrastructure (crushing plants, port terminals, storage, and logistics networks) that takes years and billions to replicate. Bunge's integrated origin-to-destination model provides informational advantages in commodity flows and logistics optimization that pure trading houses lack.

However, the moat protects market position, not margins. Commodity processing is structurally low-margin (~5% gross, ~1% net). Scale provides cost advantages but does not generate pricing power. The competitive position ensures survival and relevance through commodity cycles but does not guarantee attractive returns on the capital deployed.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

**1. Negative Free Cash Flow Persistence (HIGH).** If Viterra integration delays or commodity margins remain compressed, FCF could remain negative through 2026. Continued capital returns funded by debt would further impair the balance sheet and eventually force a dividend cut or buyback suspension.

**2. Leverage in a Rising Rate/Disrupted Trade Environment (HIGH).** The $14.1B debt load with $5.2B near-term maturities coincides with tariff-driven agricultural trade disruption and elevated geopolitical risk. US-China trade tensions directly impact agricultural commodity flows, Bunge's largest product category. Retaliatory tariffs on US agricultural products would shift trade patterns, potentially stranding some of Bunge's newly expanded capacity.

**3. Goodwill Impairment Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** The 9% excess fair value in Grain Merchandising means impairment is one bad quarter away. A $500M-1B write-down would further depress reported earnings and book value, though it would be non-cash.

**4. Concentrated Shareholder Overhang (MODERATE-HIGH).** Glencore, CPP Investments, and BCIM holding 34% creates selling pressure risk. These are financial investors who acquired Bunge shares through the Viterra deal, not long-term strategic holders. Any of the three could begin systematic selling once lockup restrictions expire, creating sustained downward pressure on the stock.

**5. Biofuels Policy Reversal (MODERATE).** The Bunge-Chevron JV's value proposition depends on EPA renewable volume obligations and European biofuel mandates remaining supportive. Policy changes under any administration could reduce feedstock demand.

**6. Spruce Point Short Thesis (MODERATE).** While activist short reports are inherently biased, the specific claims about capital deficit and non-economic roll-up activity warrant monitoring through future cash flow statements. If FY2026 FCF remains negative while capital returns continue, the thesis gains credibility.

### Geopolitical and Macro Considerations

Elevated oil prices (above $95) increase input costs for transportation and processing across the global supply chain. Iran-related disruption to global energy infrastructure increases fertilizer costs — a significant share of global nitrogen production depends on natural gas supply routes vulnerable to geopolitical disruption — which directly affects farmer economics and planting decisions. While Bunge's fertilizer distribution business in South America benefits from higher fertilizer prices at the revenue line, farmer distress from input cost inflation can reduce planted acreage and crop quality, reducing available supply for processing operations.

Trade policy uncertainty is particularly acute. Bunge's model depends on free-flowing agricultural trade across borders. Tariff escalation between the US and China disrupts soybean trade patterns (China was historically the largest buyer of US soybeans), and broader protectionism could shift commodity flows in ways that strand processing capacity in the wrong geographies.

- Commodity processing margins recover from cyclical trough toward 2023-level spreads
- Biofuels demand growth (EPA RVO, European mandates) provides secular volume tailwind
- $3B buyback program reduces share count by ~20%, amplifying EPS recovery
- Credit upgrades (A-) reduce borrowing costs as acquisition debt is refinanced

- $13 "mid-cycle" EPS proves to be favorable-cycle EPS; normalized baseline is $7-9

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most identifiable near-term catalyst is FY2026 Q1 and Q2 earnings reports, which will be the first full comparison periods showing Viterra contribution on an apples-to-apples basis. If reported margins continue to compress and FCF remains negative while capital returns continue, the Spruce Point thesis gains credibility and institutional holders re-evaluate. Additionally, any indication that Glencore or CPP Investments are filing to sell portions of their combined ~31% stake would be a powerful negative catalyst.

**Timing window:** 3-9 months. Q1 2026 earnings (likely May-June 2026) and Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) are the key data points. If both show continued negative FCF and margin compression, the short thesis is validated. Lockup expirations for Viterra shareholders (if any remain) would provide additional timing precision.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** No current short interest data is available from the provided dataset. Spruce Point's public "Strong Sell" recommendation suggests informed bearish positioning exists but may not yet be widely crowded. At ~$90 per share with ~165M shares outstanding (~$15B market cap), the stock is liquid enough to support meaningful short positions without squeeze dynamics dominating.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data provided. The sell-side is overwhelmingly bullish (JPM, BofA, Barclays all with Buy ratings and $130-145 targets), which means the consensus view is long — creating an asymmetric short setup if the bull thesis fails to materialize. Bearish positioning is likely uncrowded.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $55-65 represents ~30-39% downside from $90. Upside risk to $110-115 (partial recovery toward analyst targets) represents ~22-28% squeeze risk. This produces approximately a 1.3-1.5:1 risk/reward ratio on the short — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The risk/reward ratio does not meet the 2:1 threshold. While the fundamental deterioration is real, Bunge retains leading competitive infrastructure and the commodity cycle could turn in its favor. The appropriate posture is to avoid owning the stock rather than actively betting against it. If FY2026 Q1/Q2 confirm continued negative FCF and goodwill impairment materializes, the short thesis should be re-evaluated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** The following conditions would warrant upgrading to HOLD or BUY:
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow (OCF exceeding capex), demonstrating the combined platform can self-fund operations and capital returns
2. Gross margin recovery above 6.0% on a trailing twelve-month basis, indicating processing margin normalization
3. Resolution of concentrated shareholder overhang — evidence that Glencore/CPP have reduced holdings to below 10% each without severe price impact, or have publicly committed to long-term holds
4. Goodwill impairment risk reduction — Grain Merchandising excess fair value above 20% (vs. current 9%)

**Timeline:** These headwinds are structural, not transient. Integration execution typically requires 24-36 months post-close (through mid-2027 to early 2028). Commodity cycle recovery timing is inherently unpredictable. Shareholder overhang could persist for 12-24 months. Expect the AVOID stance to remain in force through at least H1 2027, with the earliest realistic upgrade point at FY2026 full-year results (Q1 2027).

## Conclusion

Bunge Global completed one of the largest agribusiness acquisitions in history at a challenging point in the commodity cycle. The combined entity has unmatched global scale and infrastructure, but the financial consequences of the deal — $14.1B in debt, negative free cash flow, 4.8% gross margins, and a 39% EPS decline — dominate the near-to-medium term outlook. Management's $13 mid-cycle EPS target requires simultaneous execution on synergies, commodity recovery, biofuels policy, and balance sheet repair, each of which carries independent uncertainty.

The competitive position is strong and durable. The valuation at current depressed earnings is optically full; at mid-cycle earnings it would be cheap. Whether mid-cycle earnings are achievable from the current financial starting point is uncertain, and the evidence suggests meaningful execution risk. Negative FCF, a credible short seller report, 34% shareholder overhang from deal-related investors, and goodwill impairment risk in Grain Merchandising all argue for patience. At $90, the stock prices in meaningful recovery without adequately compensating for the probability that recovery takes longer or delivers less than management projects. AVOID.
