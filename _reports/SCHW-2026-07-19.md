**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab has completed the mechanical reversal of the exact problem that halved its stock in 2023. The defining feature of the 2022–2023 crisis was "cash sorting": rising rates pushed clients to move low-yield sweep cash into money funds and Treasuries, forcing Schwab to plug the funding gap with expensive Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB) advances and to hold an underwater securities book that suppressed regulatory capital. As of Q1 2026, average FHLB borrowings fell to $1.3B (from $10.7B a year earlier) and stood at $0 outstanding at quarter-end, the net interest margin expanded 35bp to 2.88%, and the self-imposed AOCI-inclusive Tier 1 leverage ratio recovered to 6.8%, back inside management's 6.75–7.00% target. Q1'26 net revenue rose 16% to $6.48B and net income rose 30% to $2.48B, with diluted EPS up 38%.

The market is re-rating the business, but consensus estimates continue to lag the recovery. Current-year EPS estimates have risen 5.6% over 90 days and next-year estimates 8.3%, with revisions accelerating (the 30-day next-year revision of +3.8% exceeds the 90-day pace). At $101.56, the stock trades at roughly 13.5x the next-year consensus EPS of $7.52 and ~16.1x current-year, a discount to the peer median P/E of 20 despite Schwab delivering 23% ROE and 40% ROTCE. Analyst mean and median targets ($120.74 and $122) sit 19–20% above the current price, and the CEO bought ~$1.85M of stock on the open market at ~$84 in mid-2026, roughly 17% below today's level.

We rate SCHW a BUY. The thesis is a re-accelerating grower with the funding overhang largely cleared, trading below its peer multiple on rising estimates. The primary risks are genuine and unresolved: a still-underwater securities book (AOCI -$10.8B, HTM unrealized losses $8.6B), a margin-loan surge (+52% YoY) that is pro-cyclical and would contract in a market drawdown, and order-flow revenue that is now larger than commissions and exposed to any SEC rulemaking on payment for order flow. The options market is pricing near-term stress (44.1% ATM IV vs 25.5% realized, backwardated term structure) into the upcoming earnings print, which argues for sizing positions with patience.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is a savings-and-loan holding company running one of the largest wealth-management and brokerage franchises in the U.S. The model is a hybrid of discount broker, bank, and asset manager, monetizing the same client dollar through net interest income, bank deposit fees, asset-management fees, and trading revenue simultaneously. Two reportable segments:

- **Investor Services** — 78% of Q1'26 net revenue ($5.08B). Retail brokerage, advisory, banking/trust, and workplace/retirement services.
- **Advisor Services** — 22% ($1.41B). Custody, trading, banking, and support for independent RIAs. Net revenue grew 22% YoY, faster than the retail side, in the franchise Schwab dominates.

Scale is the moat. As of Q1 2026, Schwab held $11.77 trillion in client assets across 39.1 million active brokerage accounts, with $6.04T receiving ongoing advisory services. Management sizes the U.S. investable-wealth TAM above $85 trillion, framing Schwab's ~14% share as having a long runway. The 2020 TD Ameritrade acquisition is fully integrated, and TD Bank exited its remaining equity stake in February 2025.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 results quantify a genuine recovery rather than a beat driven by one-time items:

- Total net revenue $6.48B, +16% YoY
- Net income $2.48B, +30%; diluted EPS $1.37, +38% (adjusted $1.43)
- Pre-tax margin 49.2% (from 43.8%); ROE 23%, ROTCE 40%
- NIM 2.88%, up from 2.53%

The mechanism behind the margin expansion is specific and checkable. First, the wholesale funding wind-down: average FHLB borrowings dropped to $1.3B, $0 at quarter-end versus $1.85B at YE'25, cutting the average rate paid on total funding from 0.99% to 0.75%. Replacing expensive advances with cheaper client deposits is the single largest NIM lever. Second, asset-mix upgrade: margin loans reached $126.7B (+52% YoY, roughly $21.3B tied to RIA long/short strategies), and bank loans rose to $60.9B (+29%), shifting the earning-asset base toward higher yields as low-coupon legacy securities amortize. Third, engagement: daily average trades hit 9.9M (+34%), trading revenue rose 20% to $1.09B (order-flow revenue +26%), and asset-management/administration fees rose 15% to $1.76B.

The balance-sheet overhang is real but improving. Total assets are $493B. HTM securities carry $8.6B of unrealized losses and AOCI sits at -$10.8B, better than -$11.0B at YE'25 as rates ease and securities roll off. Schwab is a Category III bank and excludes most AOCI from regulatory capital; CSC CET1 is 26.3%, and the AOCI-inclusive adjusted Tier 1 leverage ratio of 6.8% is back in the target band. The Altman-style distress screens that flag deposit-funded institutions are not meaningful here; the correct lens is regulatory capital (well-capitalized on every measure), NIM, and funding mix, all of which are strong and improving.

Capital return has resumed forcefully: $2.4B repurchased in Q1'26 ($12.1B remaining on a $20B authorization), the dividend raised 19% to $0.32/quarter (a ~1.25% yield), and operating cash flow of $7.3B in the quarter. The earnings surprise record is consistent (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, with the miss a modest -2.3% in Q4'25), supporting confidence in the rising consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. The first is NIM normalization, largely realized in reported numbers but with runway as the securities book continues to roll into higher-yielding assets and the last of the wholesale funding is retired. The second is organic asset gathering: Schwab reported record May 2026 core net new assets of $49.9B (+43% YoY), which flows directly into fee-bearing balances and future net interest income. The third is optionality from two new franchises — spot bitcoin/ether (announced April 2026, custody at Charles Schwab Premier Bank with Paxos as sub-custodian, plus 24/7 crypto futures live June 2, 2026) and the $636M Forge Global acquisition (closed March 2026) building a private-markets on-ramp.

The estimate-revision picture is the strongest forward signal in the file. Current-year EPS estimates rose 5.6% over 90 days to $6.31, and next-year rose 8.3% to $7.52, with 30-day revisions (+2.5% CY, +3.8% NY) running faster than the 90-day pace. This falls short of the >30% velocity that flags a trough recovery, so we do not treat it as a maximum-conviction signal, but for a mature financial with 23% ROE it represents durable, broad-based upward momentum across all four forecast periods and 20 covering analysts.

The score trajectory shows Growth Prospects declining from 8.0 to 7.0 across prior reads. Our 7.5 reflects a balance: the funding recovery is now largely in the base rather than incremental, which tempers the headline growth rate, but net-new-asset momentum and the rising estimate track argue against a further markdown. Crypto and Forge are option value, not current earnings (Forge lost $6M in its stub period), so they support the durability of asset gathering without yet contributing to the P&L.

## Valuation Assessment

At $101.56 the stock trades at ~13.5x next-year consensus EPS ($7.52) and ~16.1x current-year ($6.31), against a peer median P/E of 20. The relevant peer set is heterogeneous: HOOD at 44.8x and IBKR at 38.1x are trading/crypto-native growth names, RJF at 14.2x and GS at 19.0x are more traditional. Schwab's blend of a bank balance sheet, the dominant RIA custody platform, and a proprietary fund complex sits between these cohorts, and its 23% ROE / 40% ROTCE quality argues for at least a peer-median multiple. Applying 20x to the next-year estimate yields ~$150; a more conservative 18x yields ~$135, aligning with the high analyst target of $137.

The estimate-revision guardrails apply cleanly here. Estimates are rising and accelerating, the forward P/E is ~13.5x (well under the 30x threshold), and the stock trades below the consensus target, the conditions under which analyst targets function as lagging floors rather than ceilings. This is not a premium-multiple name where rising estimates are already priced. Our valuation lessons caution against treating a low multiple as a buy signal in the absence of a catalyst; here the catalyst is specific and mechanical: the funding-cost reversal that is still flowing through NIM, plus the estimate momentum that consensus is only partway through incorporating. We score Valuation 7.0: cheaper than peers on quality-adjusted forward earnings, with an identifiable reason the market is under-crediting the recovery, but not a deep-value dislocation.

Base case: 18x on next-year EPS = ~$135, roughly 33% upside over a 12-month horizon. This sits between the bear and bull cases below.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab's structural edge is the multiple monetization of a single client dollar, combined with the largest RIA custody platform (Advisor Services), a massive low-cost deposit base, and an in-house fund/ETF complex. Competitors attack pieces of this: Fidelity and Vanguard on scale asset gathering, Morgan Stanley/E*Trade on advice-plus-wealth, and Interactive Brokers, Robinhood, and Coinbase on trading and crypto. The crypto and private-markets moves are defensive-offensive: neutralizing the differentiation that Robinhood/Coinbase built with younger and crypto-holding clients and giving Schwab clients a private-market on-ramp without asset leakage off-platform.

Advisor Services' 22% net revenue growth is direct evidence of share gains in the highest-value custody segment. The score-trajectory data shows Competitive Position marked down from 8.5 to 7.0 in prior reads, a reasonable correction given that Schwab is defending against crypto-native and private-market entrants rather than extending an uncontested lead. We hold at 7.5: the RIA-custody dominance and deposit-base scale remain intact and improving, but the crypto/private-market entry is a response to competitive pressure, and execution in businesses Schwab has historically avoided is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cash sorting / deposit sensitivity (the defining risk).** Earnings remain highly geared to client cash allocation. Client cash is only 9.9% of assets (down from 10.6% a year ago), total bank deposits fell slightly QoQ to $253B, and in a renewed rising- or volatile-rate regime clients would again shift sweep cash out, shrinking the low-cost base. The recovery is real; the structural sensitivity is permanent.

2. **Interest-rate risk / AOCI.** A large fixed-rate, long-duration book (HTM alone $132B, heavily weighted beyond 10 years) sits at a loss; a spike in long rates would deepen the AOCI hole and pressure the adjusted capital ratio. Conversely, a gradual 100bp decline in rates would cut net interest revenue ~3.7%; the rate exposure cuts both ways.

3. **Margin-loan pro-cyclicality.** The margin-loan book, including $21.3B in RIA long/short strategies, is a large driver of NIR growth and among the first revenue lines to contract in a sustained equity drawdown or deleveraging event. Watch this metric as a leading indicator.

4. **Order-flow / PFOF regulatory risk.** Order-flow revenue now exceeds commissions. Any SEC rulemaking on payment for order flow would hit a meaningful profit stream. The *Corrente* antitrust class action settled on non-monetary terms in November 2025, but objectors have appealed to the Fifth Circuit.

5. **TD IDA constraint.** Uninvested client cash swept off-balance-sheet to TD Bank must be kept between $60B and $90B (currently $73.6B and declining as Schwab pulls deposits onto its own balance sheet). Falling below $60B triggers a nonperformance payment, a managed but real cap on how fast Schwab can juice NIM.

6. **New-product execution.** Crypto and private markets carry operational, custody, and reputational risk in areas Schwab has historically avoided.

Because several of these are specific, quantified, and partly binary (PFOF rulemaking, the IDA floor, a rate-driven AOCI deepening), we score Risk Factors 6.0 rather than higher: the business has genuine idiosyncratic exposures that are not fully priced, though none is imminent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 44.1% versus 25.5% one-year realized vol, rich, at the 81st percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (44.1% near-term vs 27.5% six-month), which prices imminent stress around the upcoming earnings print. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.8%), and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.42 to 0.42 since mid-June even as the price rose 11.6%, indicating hedges being lifted into strength rather than accumulated. One flagged unusual print, 869 contracts of the deep out-of-the-money $75 July put (4.7x OI), is a small-notional tail hedge, not a directional statement. The market expects a large move on earnings (~±4.8% implied), which argues for scaling entry and preserving dry powder for a post-print IV crush rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$150–160):** NIM continues to normalize as the last legacy securities roll into higher-yielding assets and the final wholesale funding is retired; net new assets sustain a 40%+ pace; crypto and Forge slow attrition of younger and HNW clients and open new fee pools. Next-year EPS reaches or exceeds $7.52 and the market awards a peer-plus multiple of ~20x, plus continued buybacks ($12.1B remaining) shrinking the share count. ~$150 at 20x is the anchor, with $160 achievable on multiple expansion toward the growth-name cohort.

**Base case (~$135):** 18x on next-year EPS. NIM holds near current levels, asset gathering stays strong, and consensus catches up to the rising estimates. ~33% upside over 12 months.

**Bear case (~$78–82):** A renewed long-rate spike deepens the AOCI hole and re-pressures the adjusted capital ratio; a market drawdown deleverages the $126.7B margin book and cuts trading/order-flow revenue; and an SEC PFOF ruling impairs a now-material profit stream. Multiple compresses to ~13x on a cut estimate near $6.00, implying ~-20 to -23% from current levels, near the 52-week low of $83.96 and just below the low analyst target of $86.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$135** (18x next-year consensus EPS of $7.52), ~33% above the current $101.56.
- **Bull case target: ~$150–160** (20x on $7.52 plus multiple expansion), ~48–58% upside.
- **Bear case target: ~$78–82** (~13x on a cut ~$6.00 estimate), ~-20 to -23% downside.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, keyed to the upcoming earnings print, subsequent quarterly NIM and net-new-asset disclosures, and the trajectory of AOCI as rates move.
- **Upside/downside from current:** +33% to base, +48–58% to bull, -20–23% to bear — a risk/reward skewed favorably to the long side (roughly 1.5–2x reward vs risk at the base target).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM contracts sequentially for two consecutive quarters (indicating renewed cash sorting or an adverse funding shift rather than continued normalization).
2. Margin loans decline more than 15% in a quarter alongside a broad equity drawdown, signaling the pro-cyclical NIR driver reversing.
3. An SEC final rule materially restricting payment for order flow, or the IDA balance approaching the $60B floor and triggering nonperformance economics.
4. Consensus next-year EPS revisions turn negative for two consecutive months, reversing the current momentum.

## Conclusion

Schwab is a re-accelerating grower whose defining crisis has been mechanically resolved: the direct reversal of the problem that halved the stock in 2023. Consensus estimates are rising and accelerating, yet the stock trades below the peer median while earning 23% ROE. The CEO's open-market purchase and record May net new assets corroborate the recovery. Against this sits a still-underwater securities book, a pro-cyclical margin-loan surge, and PFOF regulatory exposure, real risks that justify disciplined sizing and a patient entry given the elevated near-term implied volatility. On balance, the risk/reward favors the long side, and we rate SCHW a BUY with a 12-month base-case target of ~$135. Given the sector-level caution that Financials BUYs default to negative alpha, the distinct company catalyst here — the funding-cost reversal still flowing through NIM plus a below-peer multiple on rising estimates — is the specific reason we lean long rather than treating this as sector beta.
