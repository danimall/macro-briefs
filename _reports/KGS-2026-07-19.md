**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest U.S. operator of large-horsepower contract natural gas compression, running a 4.46 million-horsepower fleet at 98.0% utilization under multi-year, take-or-pay-style contracts with investment-grade upstream and midstream customers. The core business is infrastructure-as-a-service: Kodiak owns the compression units, rents them under 1-7 year primary terms with annual inflation escalators, and books high, stable adjusted gross margins (70.6% in Contract Services in Q1 2026). Revenue grew from $850M in 2023 to $1,308M in 2025, and adjusted EBITDA reached $715M at a 54.7% margin. This is a durable, contracted cash-flow business levered to the secular demand for gas compression in the Permian and Eagle Ford, where ~83% of assets are deployed.

The story that has driven the stock from ~$32 a year ago to ~$65 today is the deliberate pivot into distributed power generation. The April 2026 close of the Distributed Power Solutions (DPS) acquisition (~$587M cash + 2.4M shares) created a new Power Infrastructure segment aimed at behind-the-meter and microgrid power for data centers. The July 2026 Baker Hughes turbine supply agreement (capable of up to ~1 GW by 2030) and management's 300-500 MW/year power growth target give the pivot a credible, marquee-backed roadmap. This is a logical adjacency built on Kodiak's existing gas-engine and field-service competencies, and it repositions a compression yield/growth story as a data-center-power infrastructure play.

The problem is the price and the near-term numbers. At ~$65 the stock trades at roughly 87x trailing earnings and ~25x this year's consensus EPS, while every forward estimate line has been revised down over the past 30 and 90 days (current-year -5.3%, next-year -9.8%). The balance sheet carries $2.79B of debt against a market cap of ~$6.6B, leaving thin margin for a simultaneous dividend, buyback, growth-capex, and a debt-and-equity-financed power build. Two genuine tail risks — a self-reported Mexico FCPA/OFAC/sanctions matter involving cartel-linked payments and a residual $66M Texas sales-tax accrual — sit beneath the surface. We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold): the power optionality is worth owning, but the entry is not compelling here and near-term earnings momentum is negative.

## Company Overview

Kodiak provides contract compression: the mechanical equipment that pressurizes natural gas so it can flow from wellhead through gathering, processing, and transportation systems. Roughly 80% of the fleet is large horsepower (>1,000 HP per unit), which commands longer contracts, higher renewal rates, and better margins than small-HP equipment. The company IPO'd in July 2023 and was transformed in scale by the 2024 CSI Compressco acquisition.

The business has three defining structural features. First, high fleet utilization and a $1.6B contracted Contract Services backlog (~$689M recognizable in the remainder of 2026) provide revenue visibility unusual for the energy-services sector. Second, geographic concentration in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale, both long-life, low-cost basins tied to Gulf Coast LNG export growth. Third, a blue-chip customer base — the four largest customers are ~31-32% of revenue, all investment-grade S&P 500 companies, with one customer above 10% of revenue.

The DPS acquisition, rebranded Kodiak Power Solutions, forms a new Power Infrastructure segment (with the legacy Contract Services segment renamed Compression Infrastructure). This pushes Kodiak into turnkey behind-the-meter distributed power generation for data centers, microgrids, and manufacturing — a direct play on AI/data-center power demand. Post-close, Kodiak committed to ~$244.8M of additional power-gen equipment purchases over roughly four years, and the Baker Hughes agreement provides the turbine supply relationship to scale toward the up-to-1-GW-by-2030 objective.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been strong but is largely CSI-driven: $850M (2023) → $1,159M (2024) → $1,308M (2025), the last step +12.8% YoY. Q1 2026 revenue was $345.8M. Contract Services adjusted gross margin expanded to 68.4% in 2025 and 70.6% in Q1 2026, reflecting both pricing power and scale. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $190M at a 55.0% margin.

GAAP earnings are modest relative to the enterprise because of the capital structure. FY2025 net income was $80.5M against $276M of D&A and $198M of interest expense; adjusted net income was $139.4M ($1.54 adjusted diluted EPS). Q1 2026 GAAP net income fell to $17.9M, but that reflects a one-time $36.5M loss on debt extinguishment (the call premium on redeeming the 7.25% 2029 notes); adjusted diluted EPS was $0.59 versus $0.35 a year earlier.

Cash flow is the metric that matters for a capital-intensive lessor. FY2025 operating cash flow was $599.7M and FCF was $284M (22% margin), with discretionary cash flow of $461.7M, up from $373M. Capex is bifurcated between growth capex ($199.5M in 2025) and predictable maintenance capex (~$76M), with maintenance modest relative to the asset base. The dividend is $0.49/quarter (~$43M/quarter, raised from $0.41 in early 2025), an annualized run-rate of ~$172M. The buyback authorization is $100M with $31.7M remaining.

The defining financial risk is leverage. Long-term debt was $2.79B at 3/31/26, D/E ~2.6x, current ratio 0.84. An Altman Z-Score of 1.17 flags the "distress zone," but this is a false positive for an asset-heavy infrastructure lessor with contracted revenue, 98% utilization, IG counterparties, and full covenant compliance (max leverage 5.25x, min interest coverage 2.5x; Piotroski 4/5). The Z-Score measures structural leverage, not imminent bankruptcy. Management has been actively managing the liability stack: it issued $770M 2033 notes (6.50%) and $630M 2035 notes (6.75%) in September 2025, then $1.0B 2031 notes at 5.875% in Q1 2026, using proceeds to redeem the 7.25% 2029 notes. Net effect is a lower coupon, extended maturities, and no near-term maturity wall (nothing due until the 2030 ABL / 2031 notes). The $2.0B ABL is only $450M drawn (~$1.5B available), with liquidity of ~$1.6B.

Capital allocation is stretched. Kodiak is simultaneously carrying the debt load, paying the dividend, buying back stock, funding growth capex, and now funding a debt-and-equity-financed power expansion. Guided discretionary cash flow of $520-570M can cover this in a supportive commodity and demand environment, but the balance sheet leaves limited margin for error if energy demand softens, DPS integration disappoints, or rates stay higher for longer (the variable-rate ABL is only partially hedged via a $325M swap).

## Growth Analysis

There are two growth engines. The core compression business rides secular tailwinds: unconventional production requires more compression than conventional; multi-well pads and gas-lift raise HP intensity; and Gulf Coast LNG export expansion pulls Permian/Eagle Ford volumes higher. This underpins durable but relatively mature mid-single-to-low-double-digit growth, most of the recent step-change attributable to CSI.

The higher-growth vector is Power Infrastructure. Management guides 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $820-860M and discretionary cash flow of $520-570M, now reporting in three segments, with a long-term target of 300-500 MW/year of power growth through 2030. The DPS acquisition, the turbine commitments, and the Baker Hughes supply relationship are recent, consistent, and reinforcing signals that the pivot is accelerating. Data-center power demand and the scarcity of dispatchable, behind-the-meter gas generation given grid interconnection queues underpin the opportunity. Kodiak's ability to leverage existing gas-engine and field-service competencies plus IG customer relationships gives it a credible entry point.

The countervailing signal is the estimate trend, which is negative in the near term. Current-quarter EPS has been revised to $0.71 (from $0.75 30 days ago, $0.73 90 days ago); next-quarter to $0.63 (-8.7% over 90 days); current-year to $2.56 (-5.5% over 90 days); and next-year to $2.98 (-9.8% over 90 days). This is not the positive revision velocity that would flag a strong-momentum buy. The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, including a -29.6% miss in Q3 2025 and a -19.2% miss in Q4 2025, before a +10.2% beat in Q1 2026. The DPS/power ramp is a 2027+ EBITDA story; the 2026 numbers reflect integration cost, financing drag, and softer near-term compression pricing assumptions. Growth prospects are genuinely good on a multi-year view, but the momentum indicator that predicts near-term outperformance is pointing down, which caps the score.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing metrics KGS looks expensive: ~87x trailing P/E on $0.89 GAAP EPS, and a 2.8% dividend yield. Adjusting for the capital structure and using cash-based metrics is more appropriate for a levered lessor. On adjusted diluted EPS of $1.54 (2025) the multiple is ~43x; on current-year consensus of $2.56 it is ~26x, and on next-year $2.98 it is ~22x. Against a peer median P/E of ~30.8x in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, KGS is not obviously cheap or expensive on forward earnings, though its closest structural comp — Archrock (AROC), the other large-HP compression operator — trades at 19.6x. On that comparison KGS carries a premium that is only justified if the power segment delivers.

The valuation debate hinges on whether the power pivot is being underpriced or overpriced. Analyst price targets (low $72, mean $83.9, median $83.5, high $93) sit above the current ~$65, implying ~10-43% upside to the range. But those targets are not a floor: forward estimates are falling, not rising, and the multiple already embeds a power-optionality premium versus AROC. Under the framework where cheapness is only rewarded with a catalyst — and where cheap-looking multiples in energy have systematically been value traps — KGS does not screen as a mispriced bargain. It screens as a fairly-to-fully-valued name whose upside depends on a 2027+ execution story rather than a current-price discount.

The stock's ~106% twelve-month run is not by itself a reason to mark it down; that appreciation tracked genuine fundamental improvement (CSI scale, margin expansion, the power pivot, the EQT exit). The issue is that at ~$65 the price already reflects a good deal of the power optimism while near-term earnings visibility has deteriorated. We assign a valuation score below neutral: not a value trap, but no asymmetric upside at today's price given falling estimates and a premium to the cleanest compression comp.

## Competitive Landscape

Kodiak's competitive position in core compression is strong and stable-to-improving. Large-HP compression carries high barriers — capital intensity, the cost and difficulty of relocating units, and the need for 95-98% mechanical-availability guarantees. Kodiak's scale, Permian density, standardized fleet, and utilization are genuine cost and reliability advantages. The industry is fragmented regionally, but the large-HP niche consolidates around a few operators (Kodiak, Archrock, USA Compression, the absorbed CSI). The moat here is real, though the compression market is mature.

The power pivot is where competitive trajectory becomes more uncertain. Kodiak is entering distributed power generation from a position of adjacency, not incumbency. It competes for behind-the-meter and microgrid business against equipment OEMs, independent power developers, and utilities. The Baker Hughes supply relationship secures turbine access in a tight equipment market, and Kodiak's existing gas-engine field-service organization and IG customer relationships are transferable. But this is a new business line with execution and integration risk that the company itself flagged as a new 10-K risk factor. The competitive-position trajectory therefore splits: strengthening in the sense of entering a high-growth adjacency with structural tailwinds, but with the durability of that advantage unproven. The prior report's step-down in competitive position (7.0 → 6.5) captured this reasonable caution, and we hold at 6.5 — the compression moat is durable, but the power moat is aspirational until it converts to contracted MW and EBITDA.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by leverage, concentration, and two legacy legal/tax tails.

**Leverage and rates.** A partially-hedged variable-rate ABL and covenants restricting dividends, buybacks, additional debt, and asset sales. The company is FCF-positive and covenant-compliant, but a higher-for-longer rate path raises the financing cost of the power build and compresses the margin for error. The Q1 2026 ABL Fifth Amendment temporarily relaxed the leverage-ratio calculation to accommodate DPS financing, a sign that the balance sheet is being managed close to its constraints.

**Concentration.** ~83% Permian/Eagle Ford; top-4 customers ~31%; one above 10%. A regional disruption or loss of a key IG customer would be outsized. ~9% of revenue-generating HP is month-to-month (30-90 day cancellable), and customer vertical integration (upstream/midstream owning their own compression) is a latent threat.

**Mexico FCPA/OFAC/sanctions matter.** In Q1 2025 Kodiak discovered that, prior to the CSI acquisition, its Mexican business made payments to persons associated with a designated Foreign Terrorist Organization / Specially Designated Global Terrorist (cartel-linked), apparently to protect employees and secure site access. Kodiak self-reported to the DOJ, OFAC, and SEC and sold its Mexico operations on 9/30/2025 (a $33.3M loss). Amounts were deemed immaterial and the company self-reported and exited, but criminal/civil penalties remain possible. This is a genuine, if bounded, tail risk and a reputational overhang not obviously priced.

**Texas sales-tax audit.** The accrued liability was $102.3M at YE2025, reduced to $66.0M at 3/31/26 after a partial settlement (a $28M interest/penalty charge was taken in 2025). Materially reduced but not fully resolved.

**DPS integration and execution.** A new risk factor in the FY2025 10-K: failure to integrate DPS, retain customers/employees, achieve synergies, and the assumption of unknown liabilities. This is the central execution bet.

These risks are specific and not fully reflected in a consensus that carries no Hold or Sell ratings among 14 analysts. The score reflects the presence of two open legal/tax matters, high leverage into a rate headwind, and integration risk on the growth thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Kodiak is a durable, contracted-cash-flow compression leader (98% utilization, IG customers, $1.6B backlog) that has pivoted into the most demanded infrastructure adjacency in the market: distributed power for data centers. The DPS acquisition, turbine commitments, and the Baker Hughes relationship convert an aspirational thesis into a bankable roadmap toward 300-500 MW/year of power growth. As the Power Infrastructure segment scales in 2027-2028, EBITDA re-rates through the $820-860M 2026 guide toward a materially higher run-rate, and the multiple expands as the market reprices KGS from a Permian compression lessor toward a power-infrastructure platform. The EQT sponsor's full exit removes an overhang and improves float. Bull case ~$93 (the analyst high), roughly +43% from current.

**Bear case.** The power pivot is a debt-and-equity-financed bet layered on an already-levered balance sheet into a potentially higher-for-longer rate environment. Near-term estimates are falling across every period (-5% to -10% over 90 days), the surprise record is mixed (two of last four quarters missed by 19-30%), and integration/execution risk on a brand-new business line is real. If data-center power demand disappoints or DPS synergies lag, the market compresses the multiple back toward compression-peer levels (AROC at ~19.6x), and the Mexico FCPA/OFAC matter or residual Texas tax exposure produces a negative surprise. Bear case ~$50, roughly -23% from current, roughly the level where the power premium is stripped out and the stock trades as a levered compression name.

The distribution is roughly two-sided from here, with genuine multi-year upside offset by a demanding entry price, negative near-term earnings momentum, and identifiable tail risks. That combination is a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a positive 12-month-plus growth story anchored on the power pivot, but the risk/reward at ~$65 is not compelling enough to add.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The first Power Infrastructure segment print (expected Q2 2026 reporting) demonstrates contracted MW converting to EBITDA at margins consistent with the compression base, and management raises the 300-500 MW/year trajectory or the 2026 EBITDA guide.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year revisions inflect from negative to +5% or better over a 90-day window), confirming the near-term earnings trough has passed.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores asymmetric upside to the analyst target range on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A materialized penalty or adverse development in the Mexico FCPA/OFAC/sanctions matter, or a Texas sales-tax settlement above the $66M accrual.
- Net leverage rising toward the 5.25x covenant, a dividend cut, or DPS integration cost overruns that push discretionary cash flow below the $520M guide floor.
- Compression fleet utilization falling below ~95% or the backlog contracting, signaling core-business erosion beneath the power narrative.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release and the debut Power Infrastructure segment disclosure, which the options market is pricing as a significant binary event. That print will confirm or challenge whether the power thesis is converting to reported numbers.

## Conclusion

Kodiak is a well-run, scale-advantaged compression leader executing a credible pivot into distributed power generation, backed by the DPS acquisition, turbine commitments, and the Baker Hughes supply relationship. The multi-year growth optionality is real, the contracted core is durable, and the EQT exit is a structural positive. But at ~$65 the stock has re-rated ~106% over twelve months, trades at a premium to the cleanest compression comp, carries its debt load into a potentially higher-for-longer rate path, and is seeing consensus EPS cut 5-10% across every forward period. Two open legal/tax tails (Mexico FCPA/OFAC, Texas sales tax) round out a risk profile that is not fully reflected in a rating slate with no holds or sells among 14 analysts.

We maintain HOLD, consistent with the prior four reports: the reason to keep holding rather than trimming is the power-segment optionality, and the reason not to add is the demanding entry and negative near-term earnings momentum. Own it for the 2027+ power ramp, and let the first segment print and a better price do the work before increasing exposure.
