**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ProAssurance is not trading as an operating insurer. On March 19, 2025 it agreed to be acquired by The Doctors Company (TDC), the largest physician-owned medical malpractice insurer in the United States, in an all-cash merger at approximately $25.00 per share (~$1.3B equity value). Shareholders approved the deal on June 24, 2025, the FTC granted early HSR termination on July 2, 2025, and as of the April 8, 2026 10-K/A, regulatory approvals had been secured in Alabama, D.C., Illinois, Missouri, Texas and Vermont plus Lloyd's of London and the Cayman Islands. Only California (TDC's home state) and Pennsylvania insurance regulators remain outstanding, and management continues to target closing by June 30, 2026.

At $24.65, the stock prices a gross spread of roughly $0.35, or ~1.4% to the deal price. The entire investment question is binary: does the merger close at ~$25.00 within weeks, or does it break? Standalone fundamentals are weak and exist only as the downside scenario. ProAssurance returned to GAAP profitability ($53M in 2024, $51M in 2025) after a $38.6M loss in 2023, and its Specialty P&C segment posted its first underwriting profit since 2017. But the non-GAAP combined ratio remains above 100% (104.2% in 2025), 2025 operating cash flow was negative (–$25.6M), and the premium base is shrinking. The board's decision to sell near book value (~$26/share) reflects a chronic standalone underperformer: 2023–25 book-value CAGR of just 1.19% and TSR that lagged the P&C index, causing performance-share grants to pay zero.

The appropriate framing is a near-closing arbitrage with asymmetric payoff: capped upside of ~1.4% against a break-risk drop of $5 or more toward an unaffected ~$16-20 range. We rate the stock HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale; there is no 12-month appreciation thesis here. The only reason to own the shares is to capture the residual closing spread, which is a tactical decision rather than a fundamental one. We would not initiate a new position at current levels for fundamental reasons; existing holders weighing the ~1.4% spread against break risk should size accordingly.

## Company Overview

ProAssurance is a specialty property & casualty insurer headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama, focused on healthcare-related liability. Its operations comprise:

- **Specialty Property & Casualty** — primarily Medical Professional Liability (MPL) insurance (medical malpractice for physicians, hospitals and healthcare groups). This is the core franchise, expanded materially via the 2021 acquisition of NORCAL.
- **Workers' Compensation Insurance** — through Eastern Alliance Insurance Group.
- **Segregated Portfolio Cell Reinsurance** and a **Lloyd's of London Syndicate** operation (via PRA Corporate Capital Ltd.), with Cayman-domiciled entities Inova Re and Eastern Re.

ProAssurance is a sub-scale specialist (roughly $5.4B total assets, ~$1.1B revenue, ~$1.3B market cap) operating in a hard, competitive MPL market pressured by social inflation, rising claim severity, and large jury verdicts. The acquirer, The Doctors Company, is a reciprocal inter-insurance exchange with the scale and non-public capital structure better suited to absorbing long-tail volatility. The transaction is a consolidation of a sub-scale public competitor into a larger mutual.

## Financial Analysis

The standalone financials describe a multi-year turnaround that culminated in a sale rather than a re-rating.

| Long-term debt ($M) | 427 | 427 | 425 | 420 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | — | (25.6) |

Key observations:

- **Return to profitability** — after the 2023 GAAP loss driven by adverse reserve development and social inflation in MPL, net income recovered to ~$53M (2024) and ~$51M (2025). Non-GAAP operating income improved more sharply, from $50.2M ($0.98/sh) in 2024 to $83.9M ($1.62/sh) in 2025.
- **Underwriting still unprofitable but improving** — the non-GAAP combined ratio improved to 104.2% in 2025 from 109.0% in 2024. A ratio above 100% means premiums do not cover claims and expenses; the business relies on investment income (net investment income ratio ~16.8%) to reach overall profitability. This dependence is typical for long-tail MPL but underscores the fragility of the standalone earnings stream.
- **Specialty P&C reached its first underwriting profit since 2017** — a genuine operational milestone, though it arrived alongside the sale.
- **Negative 2025 operating cash flow (–$25.6M)** is a flag — claims payouts exceeded premium inflows in a shrinking-premium environment (revenue down ~4.5% YoY), compounded by merger costs. For a run-off-tilted specialty insurer this matters as a downside marker but is secondary to the deal.
- **Modest leverage** — $420M debt against $1.35B equity (~31% D/E). Book value is approximately $26/share (1,349M / 51.6M shares), so the ~$25.00 deal price is roughly book value.

The financial health score of 5.0 reflects an adequately capitalized but operationally challenged insurer: positive GAAP earnings carried by investment income, an underwriting line that still loses money, and negative operating cash flow. The balance sheet is sound enough to make a deal break survivable, but not strong enough to make the standalone equity attractive.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis. Revenue fell 4.5% in 2025 to $1,098M as the premium base shrank in a competitive MPL market. The improvement in non-GAAP operating income was driven by favorable prior-year reserve development and investment income, not by top-line expansion.

The estimate revisions warrant comment because they appear superficially positive. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $1.22 from $1.08 thirty days ago (+13.0%) and next-year to $1.25 from $1.11 (+12.3%). Under normal circumstances, +13% current-year revisions with stable forward estimates would be a constructive forward signal. Here they are largely noise; they reflect a thin analyst base (3-4 estimates) marking to recent results, and they are irrelevant to a stock whose price is anchored to a fixed cash deal value. The earnings surprise record reinforces the low signal value: quarterly results have whipsawed (+220.9%, –43.9%, +145.2%, –3.8%), driven by reserve development timing rather than a durable earnings trajectory.

Growth prospects score 4.5: a shrinking premium base, no expansion strategy as a standalone (the strategy is to be acquired), and earnings volatility tied to reserve development rather than underwriting momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

ProAssurance competes in U.S. Medical Professional Liability, a hard, consolidating market pressured by social inflation, rising claim severity, and large jury verdicts—the same forces that drove the 2023 loss and years of sub-100% underwriting. As a sub-scale public player, PRA lacks the diversification and capital flexibility of larger mutuals. The FTC's early HSR termination signals the market remains fragmented enough that combining two MPL specialists raised no antitrust concern.

The company concluded it could no longer compete effectively as a standalone, hence the sale to a larger, scale-advantaged acquirer. Competitive position scores 4.5: a sub-scale franchise in a structurally difficult market, with the strategic answer being absorption rather than independent improvement.

## Risk Assessment

The relevant risks are deal-completion risks, not operating risks:

1. **Regulatory non-approval or delay (primary risk).** California and Pennsylvania insurance department approvals remain outstanding. California's DOI is slow and can impose conditions. A rejection or onerous condition is the principal break scenario.
2. **Timing slippage past the June 30, 2026 target.** Erodes the annualized return and could trigger outside-date renegotiation.
3. **Standalone downside if the deal breaks.** The unaffected price is well below the current level, plausibly $16-20, implying $5+ of downside against ~$0.35 of upside.
4. **MPL industry headwinds.** Social inflation and claims severity persist; these are why a sub-scale player sold.

Risk factors score 5.5. The known risks (CA/PA approval, timing) are partially priced into the ~1.4% spread, and most jurisdictions plus the FTC have already cleared. The asymmetry is unfavorable: small capped upside against meaningful break-risk downside that is not fully reflected in a price sitting so close to the deal value.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is internally inconsistent and should be read with skepticism. Near-term ATM IV is reported at 79.7% against 1-year historical volatility of just 6.1%, the latter consistent with a stock pinned near a fixed deal price for a year. The delta snapshot shows an implausible jump to 1563.9% IV, almost certainly a thin-options data artifact rather than a real signal. The reported 64.8% put/call skew and steep backwardation (near-term vol far exceeding long-term) are the one coherent read: the market is pricing event risk concentrated in the next several weeks, consistent with the closing target and the binary nature of a deal-break. We treat the absolute IV figures as non-informative and the term structure as a confirmation that the catalyst window is imminent.

Separately, short interest is elevated at 24.1% of float with 9.0 days to cover. In a merger-arb context, high short interest is common—it can reflect arbitrage hedging or positioning for a break—and is not a clean directional signal here. Magnetar Financial's Schedule 13D filed shortly after the announcement confirms arbitrage capital crowded into the name, consistent with the tight spread.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$25.00, +1.4%)
The merger closes at ~$25.00 by June 30, 2026 or shortly after. California and Pennsylvania approve without material conditions, as the other six states plus the FTC, Lloyd's and Cayman regulators already have. Holders capture the ~$0.35 spread plus any accrued value. Management's December 2025 pre-payment of ~80% of NEO cash bonuses specifically to manage Section 280G parachute taxes is a strong tell that insiders expect closing imminently.

### Bear Case (~$16-20, –19% to –35%)
California or Pennsylvania rejects the deal or imposes conditions TDC will not accept, or the transaction slips materially past the outside date and is renegotiated lower or terminated. The stock reverts toward an unaffected standalone level reflecting a >100% combined ratio, negative operating cash flow, a shrinking book, and a chronic ROE problem. The downside of $5+ dwarfs the ~$0.35 of upside.

The base case is that the deal closes; the regulatory progress, shareholder approval, FTC clearance, and management's 280G behavior all point that way. But the risk/reward of owning the shares at $24.65 is unattractive on a standalone basis: a high-probability ~1.4% gain against a low-but-real probability of a 20%+ loss.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale; there is no 12-month appreciation thesis. The only reason to own the stock is to capture the residual deal spread, a tactical arbitrage decision rather than a fundamental one. We do not recommend initiating a new fundamental position.

**Upgrade triggers (to a tactical arb BUY):**
- California AND Pennsylvania insurance approvals confirmed, removing the last material conditions and making closing near-certain, at which point the ~1.4% spread becomes a near-riskless short-duration return.
- A widening of the spread (price falling toward $24.00 or below) without any deterioration in deal probability, improving the risk/reward of the arb.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any indication that California or Pennsylvania will reject the deal or impose conditions TDC will not accept.
- A material slip past the outside date with signals of renegotiation or termination.
- An 8-K disclosing a closing-condition failure or a downward price revision.

**Reassessment timeline:** Near-term and event-driven. The June 30, 2026 closing target is the binding date. The May 5 and June 2, 2026 Item 8.01 8-Ks may contain regulatory-status updates and should be reviewed; their full text was not retrievable in the source materials, so their contents are unconfirmed. Any subsequent 8-K on CA/PA approval or deal status is the trigger to act.

## Conclusion

ProAssurance is a near-closing cash merger-arbitrage position, not a standalone equity. At $24.65 the stock offers ~1.4% upside to a ~$25.00 deal price targeted to close by June 30, 2026, with only California and Pennsylvania insurance approvals outstanding after the FTC, six states, Lloyd's and Cayman regulators have cleared and shareholders have approved. The asymmetry is unfavorable for a new fundamental buyer: capped upside against $5+ of break-risk downside toward an unattractive standalone value characterized by a >100% combined ratio, negative operating cash flow, and a shrinking premium base.

We rate PRA **HOLD**. Existing holders may reasonably retain shares to capture the closing spread, treating CA/PA approval as the decisive trigger. New money should not enter on fundamentals; the standalone business does not justify the price, and the residual arb return is a tactical, not an investment, proposition. The overall score of 5.05 reflects a fairly-priced deal-arb situation with no fundamental edge and meaningful binary risk.
