## Executive Summary

Under Armour's fiscal 2026 (ended March 31, 2026) closed with revenue of $4,966.4M, down 3.8%, a GAAP operating loss of $163.1M, and a net loss of $495.6M. The headline loss is dominated by a $294.8M non-cash valuation allowance against U.S. deferred tax assets, which makes GAAP earnings a poor read on the business. The company's own preferred metric is more informative and considerably worse: adjusted operating income, disclosed across six years in the July 2026 proxy, fell from $350.8M in FY2024 to $212.3M in FY2025 to **$100.3M in FY2026** — a 71% decline in two years, and a 2.0% adjusted operating margin on $5.0B of revenue. Operating cash flow was negative in both FY2025 (−$59.3M) and FY2026 (−$75.1M); free cash flow was −$228.0M and −$162.2M, with the year-over-year "improvement" produced by cutting capex from $168.7M to $87.1M (1.75% of sales) rather than by better operations.

The valuation question turns on a share-class subtlety. UA is the **non-voting Class C** line. Standardized data pulls report a $1.35B market cap, which is Class C alone (202.6M shares × $6.61 = $1,339M). Enterprise-wide financials must be divided by all three classes: 188.8M Class A + 34.5M Class B + 202.6M Class C = **425.9M shares**, or **$2,815M of equity value** at $6.61. Adding pro-forma net debt of roughly $291M ($400M of 7.25% 2030 notes plus $200M drawn revolver, less $309.2M cash, after the defeased 2026 notes settled in June) gives an enterprise value near **$3,106M**. That is 0.63x FY2026 revenue — genuinely low for an athletic brand — but **14.8x** our EBITDA proxy of $209.9M (adjusted operating income of $100.3M plus $109.6M of depreciation). The equity is therefore an option on margin recovery, not a discount to asset value, and the option is not cheap.

Our base case lands below the current price. Restoring FY2024's 6.15% adjusted operating margin on ~$4.9B of revenue would produce roughly $300M of adjusted operating income and ~$410M of EBITDA; at 8-9x that supports $6.80-7.80 per share after the pending ~$90M insurance repayment. Full recovery to the last good year, in other words, is roughly where the stock already trades. Meanwhile FY2027 is guided to another revenue decline, interest expense steps to roughly $42M from $30.3M, and only $44.3M of a $305M restructuring add-back remains. This is the third consecutive AVOID, and the stock has risen 12.6% since the last one — we address that directly below, but the arithmetic has moved against the buyer, not for him.

## Company Overview

Under Armour designs and markets athletic apparel, footwear and accessories, sourcing entirely from third parties. The FY2026 10-K discloses that ten manufacturers produced approximately 69% of apparel and accessories and seven produced substantially all footwear, with **no long-term contracts with suppliers or manufacturers**. The company simultaneously carries $1,214.6M of product purchase obligations, all due within one year — 24.5% of FY2026 revenue — against a wholesale channel (57.0% of revenue) that also operates without long-term customer contracts. UA bears inventory commitment risk on both sides.

## Financial Analysis

**The four-year arc.** Revenue is down 15.9% from the FY2023 peak of $5,903.6M. Gross margin peaked at 47.9% in FY2025 and gave back 244bps to 45.5% in FY2026. Total equity fell from $2,153.3M (FY2024) to $1,890.3M (FY2025) to **$1,414.4M** (FY2026), the last step driven mainly by the tax valuation allowance.

**The FY2026 loss decomposed:** operating loss −$163.1M; net interest expense −$30.3M (versus −$6.1M in FY2025, a five-fold increase); other expense −$7.3M; pretax loss −$200.7M; income tax expense **+$294.8M**, an effective rate of negative 146.9%; net loss −$495.6M. The tax charge is non-cash, does not extinguish the underlying NOLs, and can be released if sustained U.S. profitability returns. It is nonetheless an audited statement that management could not assert U.S. profits were more likely than not to materialize soon enough. The alternative reading is that ASC 740 becomes largely mechanical once a three-year cumulative loss position exists, so the charge may not represent a discrete downgrade to management's internal forecast.

**Cash flow is the binding fact.** Two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow at a $5B-revenue company is not a seasonality artifact — FY2026 OCF deteriorated $15.8M year over year while capex fell $81.6M. Capex at 1.75% of revenue is difficult to reconcile with a strategy premised on product innovation and premium positioning.

**Q4 FY2026, derived by subtracting the Q3 10-Q from the 10-K:**

| Q4 (Jan–Mar) $000 | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | 1,171,161 | 1,180,583 | −0.8% |
| Gross margin | **42.01%** | 46.65% | **−464bps** |
| Operating loss | (33,701) | (72,077) | improved $38.4M |
| Operating cash flow | (332,167) | (202,199) | worse by $130.0M |

The revenue decline nearly stopped (−0.8% versus −5.2% in Q3), but gross margin printed the cycle low. The FY2026 quarterly margin path ran +70bps → guided −340/−360bps → −310bps → −464bps. Q3's disclosed walk attributed −200bps to tariffs and **−140bps to pricing and promotional activity in North America**. The most likely synthesis is that revenue stabilization was purchased with price. The offsetting consideration: Q4 is UA's seasonally weakest, most outlet-weighted quarter, and tariffs (~155bps of the full-year margin decline, roughly $77M) are exogenous, so the underlying tariff-neutral compression is materially smaller. The Q4 cash outflow is also partly mechanical — accounts payable fell from $664.5M at December 31 to $420.1M at March 31 — though a $130M year-over-year worsening on lower revenue is not fully explained by seasonality. We have deliberately not annualized any single quarter of cash flow; the sign flips by period (Q1 FY2026 OCF +$48.9M, 9M +$257.1M, full year −$75.1M).

**Capital structure and the cost-of-capital reset.** UA defeased its $600M 3.25% notes due June 2026 by depositing $601.2M into trust in August 2025, and issued $400M of 7.25% notes due July 2030 in June 2025. It carried $200M on a $1.1B revolver at a weighted-average 4.83%, with $45.5M of letters of credit, leaving roughly **$854.5M undrawn** subject to covenants. The revolver matures June 2030 with a springing maturity 91 days earlier if the 2030 notes are unrefinanced, and is secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets. Adjusting the reported 1.62 current ratio for the offsetting defeased note and restricted investment gives **1.97**. Liquidity is better than headline metrics suggest.

The refinancing swapped 3.25% money for 7.25% money. Pro-forma FY2027 interest: $29.0M on the notes, ~$9.7M on the revolver, ~$3M of fees and amortization, near **$42M** — against $209.9M of estimated EBITDA.

**Covenant sensitivity.** The agreement requires EBITDA/interest ≥3.50x and total debt/EBITDA ≤3.25x, with defeased debt and interest excluded, and UA affirms compliance at March 31, 2026. Using our $209.9M EBITDA proxy against $600M of pro-forma debt gives 2.86x leverage; the minimum EBITDA to satisfy the leverage test at $600M of debt is $184.6M, a cushion of roughly 12%. Interest coverage at 5.0x is not the binding test. Two material caveats: we have not read the credit agreement's EBITDA definition, which very likely permits add-backs we have not counted (stock-based compensation of roughly $45M alone would take leverage to ~2.35x), and if the revolver is repaid to zero the minimum EBITDA threshold falls to $123.1M. **The revolver balance each quarter is the highest-value single monitorable in this name.** The direction of travel matters regardless: only $44.3M of the $305M restructuring add-back remains, the plan completes by December 2026, and interest steps up — so FY2027 shows less add-back and more expense against the same tests.

Altman Z of 2.02 places UA in the grey zone, driven by negative EBIT/total assets offsetting adequate working capital and asset turnover.

## Growth Analysis

There is no consolidated growth. Revenue declined 3.8% in FY2026 and management guided FY2027 to a further decline — the disclosure that produced a >20% single-day fall on May 12, 2026. We could not obtain the quantified guidance ranges from primary sources; three independent secondary reports (May 12, 2026) establish the direction and that it was below expectations, and we treat the specific figures as unverified.

Within the mix, three lines are growing: EMEA (+8.6%, and +9.6% in Q1 and +6.0% in Q3, so the growth is consistent rather than a single quarter), Latin America (+8.7%), and licensing (+13.5%, high-margin and capital-light). EMEA's margin now exceeds North America's, which is a genuine change in the geographic profit mix and the most credible element of a constructive case.

The offsetting lines are larger and worse. North America, 57.6% of revenue, fell 7.9% with ~480bps of margin compression. Footwear fell 10.8% (−14.3% in Q1, −12.0% in Q3), and the Curry Brand — UA's only credible basketball franchise — was separated, with a $69.7M non-cash contract termination charge plus inventory reserves. eCommerce fell 12% in Q1 FY2026 while owned stores grew 1%; declining direct online demand alongside stable physical retail points to weakening brand pull rather than channel shift.

The estimate-revision signal that would support a growth call is absent: no consensus estimate revisions or price targets are available in the data set, the trailing four-quarter record is two beats and two misses, and the most recent quarter missed (−$0.03 versus −$0.02 expected). Adjusted operating income falling 52.8% in a single year is the growth datum that matters.

## Valuation Assessment

Correcting the share-class error is the necessary first step. All-class equity value at $6.61 is $2,815M; enterprise value is approximately $3,106M; adding $698.8M of operating lease liabilities gives a lease-inclusive figure near $3,805M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026 revenue | 0.63x |
| EV / FY2026 EBITDA proxy ($209.9M) | 14.8x |
| Trailing / forward P/E | negative |
| Price / book (all-class equity vs $1,414M) | ~2.0x |
| Dividend yield | 0% |

Peer comparison is of limited use because the medians (CRI 16.7x, GIII 12.5x, CPRI 32.1x) are P/Es on profitable or differently-profitable businesses, while UA and OXM print negative earnings. UAA, the voting class, trades at a modest premium and is 26% below its own 52-week high versus 16% for UA — consistent with the disclosed Class C discount.

The cheapness is entirely on sales. On earnings power the multiple is full, and this is the pattern that most often marks a value trap: a low revenue multiple attached to declining revenue and negative free cash flow. Testing the recovery case explicitly:

- **Full recovery to FY2024 profitability**: 6.15% adjusted operating margin on ~$4.9B = ~$300M adjusted operating income, ~$410M EBITDA. At 8-9x EV/EBITDA less pro-forma net debt of ~$381M (including the ~$90M insurance repayment) = **$6.83-7.79 per share**. The last good year, fully re-achieved, gets you roughly to today's price.
- **Base case (partial recovery, FY2027 revenue down modestly)**: EBITDA near $260M at 11x = $2,860M EV less $381M net debt = **$5.82**, which we round to **$5.75**.
- **Bear case (further margin pressure, covenant question becomes live)**: EBITDA ~$175M at 9x less ~$420M net debt = **$2.71**; we set bear at **$3.50**, near the $3.95 fifty-two-week low, on the view that the market has historically paid ~9-10x trough EBITDA rather than less.
- **Bull case (tariff refund recovered, restructuring charges off, EMEA momentum broadens)**: EBITDA ~$340M at 12x less ~$340M net debt = **$8.78**.

Against $6.61, base is −13%, bull is +33%, bear is −47%. The distribution is negatively skewed, and importantly the current price already embeds a substantial part of the recovery. That is the reason the valuation score is 3.5 rather than 5-6 despite 0.63x sales.

Two genuine optionalities argue against scoring valuation lower. First, the tariff litigation sequence disclosed in the 10-K — Supreme Court invalidation of certain IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026, subsequent tariffs ruled illegal by the Court of International Trade on May 7, 2026 but left in effect pending appeal, and an April 2026 refund process UA is pursuing — creates a filing-supported, unquantified one-time recovery that a screen would miss entirely. At roughly $77M of annual tariff cost, a two-year recovery would be ~$0.36 per all-class share pre-tax. Second, EMEA's margin now exceeds North America's; if the mix shift continues, consolidated margin can improve without North America recovering. Neither is underwritable today.

## Competitive Landscape

The FY2026 gross-margin walk is the cleanest competitive evidence: −190bps supply chain (of which −155bps tariffs), **−70bps pricing**, −45bps channel/regional mix, against +45bps FX and +20bps product mix. Management's stated strategy is to raise average selling prices through premium product and a better full-price value proposition. The realized data shows price contributing negatively, and Q3's walk was more explicit at −140bps from North American pricing and promotion. A brand with pricing power converts an industry-wide cost shock into price; UA absorbed it and discounted into it. The tariff shock applied to all importers, so the differentiating variable is the ability to pass it through.

The Curry separation removes UA's basketball anchor. Press reporting (CNBC, WWD, Retail Dive, USA Today, June 1-3, 2026, several citing ESPN) describes a ten-year Curry partnership with Li-Ning including plans for Curry Brand stores in the U.S. and China. UA's filings describe a contract termination and the associated $69.7M charge; they do not establish trademark ownership, consideration in either direction, or residual rights, and we treat those as unverified. What is confirmed from filings is that footwear was already contracting double digits and Asia-Pacific was down 4.8% before this. Li-Ning is the endorsing counterparty to Curry; UA is neither party to nor beneficiary of that arrangement.

UA's own proxy quantifies relative performance: cumulative indexed TSR of $34.40 per $100 invested versus a selected apparel peer group at $52.13 — UA 34% below its own chosen peer set. That is a company disclosure, which makes it difficult to attribute to index selection.

Where the trajectory is favorable: EMEA has grown through the entire downturn with expanding margin, licensing grew 13.5%, and the door count is stable. Where it is unfavorable: the largest region, the weakest category, the direct online channel, and realized pricing all moved the wrong way simultaneously. Trajectory, which matters more than level, is negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant mechanics into FY2027.** Discussed above. Our sensitivity puts the binding leverage threshold near $185M of EBITDA while the revolver stays drawn at $200M, roughly 12% below the FY2026 proxy, at the same time as add-backs expire and interest rises. This is the mechanism by which a revenue miss becomes a balance-sheet event. It is a sensitivity, not a contradiction of the company's affirmed compliance, and unread EBITDA definitions could make it materially conservative.

**A $90M+ cash claim that has been expensed but not paid.** The Fourth Circuit reversed on January 20, 2026, requiring UA to repay approximately $90M of insurance proceeds related to the Consolidated Securities Action; carriers are expected to seek prejudgment interest. UA accrued $98.5M. Against $309.2M of cash of which **$277.6M sits in foreign subsidiaries** — implying roughly $31.6M of domestic cash — the payment will likely be funded from the revolver or repatriation, which loops back to covenant headroom. Mitigant: UA repatriated a $250M dividend in Q2 FY2026 and de-designated ~$650M of foreign earnings, so capacity exists.

**Restructuring that has doubled and slipped twice.** Announced May 2024 at $140-160M, raised to $255M on November 13, 2025 (+$95M including the Curry separation), and to ~$305M on May 11, 2026 (+$50M), with completion pushed to December 31, 2026. $260.7M recorded through March 2026, of which $152.6M of FY2026's $171.5M was in North America. Repeated upsizing indicates the original diagnosis understated the problem.

**Structural risks.** Sourcing concentration without long-term contracts; wholesale at 57% of revenue without long-term customer contracts against $1.21B of one-year purchase commitments; dual-class control with Class C non-voting.

**Control history and quality of earnings.** Material weaknesses existed at both March 31, 2024 and March 31, 2025, and disclosure controls were not effective at December 31, 2025 (balance sheet reconciliations). Management concluded ICFR was effective at March 31, 2026 — real remediation, but after three consecutive deficient assessment dates. Two residual items warrant monitoring rather than dismissal: a ~$4.0M EMEA bill-and-hold revenue arrangement (0.3% of quarterly revenue, immaterial to results, but located in the segment carrying the growth narrative at a company with prior allegations of quarter-to-quarter sales pull-forward), and a supply-chain finance program that grew from $143.8M at March 2025 to $265.3M at December 2025. Total accounts payable at March 2026 ($420.1M) was roughly flat versus March 2025 ($429.9M), which argues against a large sustained payables extension, but the March 2026 supply-chain-finance balance was not disclosed in the materials reviewed, so the full-year change is unresolved.

**Macro overlay.** Tariff costs remain in COGS pending appeal, applying directly to the North American wholesale channel that is already the weakest part of the business. Broader U.S. discretionary-spending conditions bear on FY2027, though this report does not source a specific macro figure.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 76.4% is elevated in absolute terms and consistent with an imminent Q1 FY2027 report (the prior-year Q1 release was August 8, 2025), implying a large expected move. The prior snapshot's 1,536.8% ATM IV reading is not credible and we disregard it, so the "decline" in IV should not be read as complacency. Short interest of 28.39M shares equals roughly 14% of Class C shares outstanding with 7.0 days to cover — enough crowding to generate violent upside on any positive surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($8.78, +33%).** FY2026 was the trough. Restructuring completes in December 2026, removing $171.5M of annual charges and $44.3M of remaining spend. Tariffs are refunded in part following the CIT ruling and the April 2026 refund process, releasing an unquantified one-time benefit and removing a 155bps annual margin drag. EMEA (+8.6%, 16.2% margin) and Latin America (+8.7%) continue compounding while North America stabilizes at a smaller, higher-price base, and licensing (+13.5%) grows as a high-margin annuity. Adjusted operating margin recovers toward the FY2024 6.15%, the U.S. deferred tax valuation allowance reverses, and 14% short interest on the Class C float amplifies the re-rating. Watsa's Fairfax-affiliated vehicle, a ~10.4% holder, added 1.18M Class A shares at an average $4.98 in the days after the May print.

**Bear case ($3.50, −47%).** FY2027 revenue declines as guided while gross margin stays near the 42-45% range, holding adjusted operating income near or below $100M. Interest expense rises to ~$42M, restructuring add-backs expire, and the ~$90M insurance repayment plus prejudgment interest draws further on the revolver, pushing measured leverage toward the 3.25x test. Operating cash flow stays negative for a third year, forcing continued sub-2%-of-sales capex in a business whose stated strategy requires product investment. Footwear keeps contracting with the Curry platform now competing against UA in both the U.S. and China, and North American wholesale absorbs a weakening 2027 U.S. discretionary consumer. The Class C non-voting discount widens.

**Which side the evidence supports.** The company's own adjusted operating income series, the two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow, and management's own FY2027 revenue-decline guidance are filing-grade facts pointing one way. The bull case rests on a margin recovery that has not started, a tariff refund of unknown size, and one growing region that is 24% of revenue. At $6.61 the price already discounts the recovery scenario in full; the buyer is paying for the bull case and taking the bear case risk.

**On the trajectory.** This is the third consecutive AVOID, and the stock is up 12.6% since the June 19 review at $5.87 and ~67% off the November low of $3.95. No new financial disclosure has arrived in that window — the most recent hard financials remain FY2026 year-end. The move is multiple expansion on trough EBITDA (from ~9.4x at the low to ~14.8x today) plus, plausibly, short covering in a name with 14% of the Class C float short and 7 days to cover. We are lowering the valuation score from 4.0 to 3.5 for exactly that reason: the same fundamentals at a 12.6% higher price is a worse risk/reward, not a better one. We would be wrong to maintain AVOID if the imminent Q1 FY2027 print shows gross margin recovering above 46% with positive operating cash flow — that would be the first evidence the trough thesis has substance.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The nearest-dated event is the Q1 FY2027 earnings release, expected within days (the prior-year Q1 report was August 8, 2025). A second, slower catalyst is the resolution of the Fourth Circuit rehearing petition and the associated ~$90M cash payment plus prejudgment interest, which would draw the revolver and raise measured leverage. A third is the December 2026 restructuring completion, after which FY2027 covenant EBITDA carries fewer add-backs against ~$42M of interest.

**Timing window.** Days for the earnings event; two to three quarters for the cash-claim and covenant sequence. The covenant thesis needs a revenue or margin miss to activate and is not self-executing.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 28,389,460 shares short against roughly 202.6M Class C shares outstanding is approximately 14% of the class, with 7.0 days to cover. That is crowded enough, and slow enough to exit, to make a positive surprise painful. The stock has already demonstrated 20%+ single-day moves in both directions in the past twelve months.

**Borrow and options signal.** ATM IV of 76.4% makes protection expensive, and put/call open interest of 0.06 shows no corroborating bearish positioning in the listed market. Options are not confirming the fundamental thesis, which is a warning for shorts rather than support.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $3.50 (−47%) requires the FY2027 revenue decline plus continued margin pressure to be confirmed over multiple quarters. Near-term downside on a single weak print is more likely $5.00-5.50 (−17% to −24%). Upside risk on a margin beat plus a squeeze is $8.00-8.78 (+21% to +33%). Against the imminent binary, near-term reward:risk is roughly 1:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental case is sound but the mechanics are hostile: a binary event within days, 76% implied volatility, 14% of the class short with 7 days to cover, and no options confirmation. The covenant catalyst is real but conditional and too distant to carry. Avoid owning; do not short here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (any two of the following):**

1. **Cash generation turns.** Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow on a trailing-twelve-month basis, with capex restored above 2.5% of sales. FY2026 delivered −$75.1M of OCF and 1.75% capex; the current configuration is not fundable indefinitely.
2. **Gross margin inflects.** Consolidated gross margin above 46.5% with a positive pricing contribution in the disclosed margin walk, which would validate the premiumization strategy rather than contradict it. Q4 FY2026 printed 42.01% with −140bps of North American pricing in Q3.
3. **Adjusted operating income re-accelerates.** The proxy's company-selected metric back above $175M, which would move the leverage covenant threshold from binding to comfortable and support a base case above the current price.
4. **Revolver repaid to zero.** This alone drops the minimum covenant EBITDA from ~$185M to ~$123M and largely defuses the balance-sheet risk. Monitor quarterly.
5. **Price.** A retest of $4.50-5.00 — the level at which the ~10.4% outside holder added in May and where the base case offers double-digit upside rather than a 13% shortfall.

**Downgrade to a more negative stance if:** FY2027 guidance is reduced again; the revolver balance rises above $350M; gross margin prints below 43% for a full year; or a new material weakness reappears after the FY2026 remediation.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — tariffs pending appeal, a guided FY2027 revenue decline, restructuring running to December 2026, and interest stepping to ~$42M — persist through at least FY2027 (ending March 2027). The natural reassessment point is the Q1 FY2027 report now due, followed by the Q2 report in November 2026 which will show the first quarter with materially reduced restructuring add-backs.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Under Armour is a margin-recovery option on a shrinking brand, and the option is priced at fair-to-full value. Adjusted operating income of $100.3M is down 71% in two years on the company's own definition; operating cash flow has been negative for two consecutive years; free cash flow improved only because capex was cut to 1.75% of sales; and FY2027 is guided to another revenue decline. The financing has been secured — 2026 notes defeased, no maturity until 2030, roughly $854M of undrawn revolver — but at a cost that raises interest expense to roughly $42M just as the restructuring add-backs supporting covenant headroom expire.

Corrected for the share-class arithmetic, the equity is not the $1.35B a standard screen reports; it is roughly $2.82B, and the enterprise trades at 14.8x trailing EBITDA. Restoring the last good year's margin in full supports $6.80-7.80, essentially the current price. Our base case of $5.75 sits 13% below it, with a $3.50 bear case against an $8.78 bull case.

There are genuine positives: EMEA growing 8.6% with margin now above North America's, licensing up 13.5%, internal-control remediation complete, an unquantified tariff refund in process, and a long-standing ~10% holder averaging in near $5. None of them is large enough to offset a $2.9B North American business losing 480bps of margin, footwear down 10.8% with its anchor franchise now competing against it, and negative realized pricing at a company whose stated strategy is to raise prices. We would revisit at $4.50-5.00, or earlier on evidence that operating cash flow and gross margin have turned.
