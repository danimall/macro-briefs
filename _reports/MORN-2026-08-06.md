## Executive Summary

Morningstar delivered its strongest operating quarter in years on July 29: Q2 2026 revenue of $663.2M (+9.6%), operating income of $160.6M (+28.4%), adjusted operating margin of 26.5% (+2.8pp), diluted EPS of $2.83 (+35.4%), and free cash flow of $122.5M (+96.3%). The stock has gained 15.5% since July 24 and 40.5% from its June 25 low of $141.84. Consensus estimates moved up 3.5% for FY2026 and 5.3% for FY2027 over 90 days, and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters.

The composition of that result is the reason we are downgrading from BUY, where we have been for three consecutive reports, to HOLD. License-based revenue is 68.6% of the quarter ($455.1M of $663.2M) and grew 3.1% organically; reported license growth of 6.2% was roughly doubled by the CRSP acquisition and currency. Morningstar Direct licenses were "relatively flat" and PitchBook licensed user counts were "relatively flat," language that recurs from the FY2025 10-K, so essentially all growth in the two segments representing 61% of FY2025 revenue came from price and per-account expansion, not seats. Adjusted margins in both of those segments *declined* year over year (Direct Platform 45.2% from 46.0%; PitchBook 30.3% from 31.7%, with 1H adjusted operating income down 0.5%). Consolidated margin expansion is being carried by Morningstar Credit, which grew 23.3% organically on US structured-finance issuance and is 94% transaction-based, and by reduced corporate cost drag.

At $199.33 the shares trade at 16.2x FY2026 consensus of $12.32 and 14.3x FY2027 of $13.94, roughly 12x trailing EV/EBITDA, and a ~6.4% free cash flow yield on our normalized FCF sensitivity. That is cheap against the historical multiple range for index and data franchises and modestly above a de-rated peer median (FDS 15.2x, MKTX 13.8x, both roughly 42-43% below their 52-week highs). It is not cheap enough to carry a BUY when our base case is $230 (+15.4%) against a bear case of $155 (-22.2%). The asymmetry that existed at $153 in June has been consumed by the rally, and the re-rating catalyst — evidence that either core license growth reaccelerates or that CRSP's $3.3 trillion of linked assets can be repriced — remains unverified.

## Company Overview

Morningstar monetizes a proprietary database on managed investments, public equities, private capital markets, and fixed income through three revenue types: license-based ($1,719.2M in FY2025, 70.3% of revenue), asset-based ($343.0M, 14.0%), and transaction-based ($383.3M, 15.7%). Five reportable segments — Direct Platform ($830.6M FY2025), PitchBook ($671.8M), Credit ($354.4M), Wealth ($251.4M), Retirement ($137.6M) — plus Sustainalytics ($112.0M) and Indexes ($87.7M) inside Corporate and All Other. Geography splits US 72% / International 28%.

Two structural features distinguish Morningstar from comparable data companies. First, it issues **no financial guidance** and states in its 10-K that management "generally doesn't take individual meetings with investors and analysts," adding that "given limited analyst coverage of our stock, our stock price may not now, or in the future, reflect the intrinsic value of our business." Only three analysts contribute to the FY2027 consensus, which materially reduces the informational weight we assign to the $220-$260 target range. Second, the company holds its own corporate investment portfolio in products replicating Morningstar model portfolios and strategies, including ETFs where Morningstar is the index provider.

The defining strategic event of the period is the February 2, 2026 acquisition of the Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP) from the University of Chicago for $363.0M cash ($359.6M net of cash acquired), funded on a delayed-draw term facility. Assets linked to Morningstar Indexes rose to $3.6 trillion from $221.0 billion, driven by $3.3 trillion of CRSP-linked assets, roughly a 16-fold expansion of the indexed-asset base. Purchase accounting recorded $233.0M of intangibles and $139.9M of goodwill, both amortizable for tax over ~15 years, with the allocation explicitly preliminary and revisable within one year.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year operating leverage is the standout fact.** Revenue grew from $1,870.6M (FY2022) to $2,445.5M (FY2025), a 9.3% CAGR, while operating margin expanded from 9.0% to 21.5%, approximately 12.5 points. Cost of revenue fell as a share of revenue from 41.7% to 39.0% over that span. FY2025 net income implies roughly $8.93 in GAAP diluted EPS on 42.2M average diluted shares.

**Q2 2026 decomposition.** The 25.8-point gap between revenue growth (+9.6%) and EPS growth (+35.4%) breaks into: operating income +28.4% (of which reported margin benefited from $6.8M of non-recurring other operating income versus $0.8M prior year; adjusted margin still expanded 2.8pp), diluted share count -10.4% (42.5M to 38.1M), partially offset by interest expense of $19.1M versus $10.3M and a tax rate of 26.2% versus 22.8%. Net income rose 21.1%; the remaining ~14 points of EPS growth are share-count arithmetic.

Cost discipline within the quarter is genuine: cost of revenue fell from 38.1% to 36.1% of revenue, sales and marketing from 19.7% to 17.7%. General and administrative *rose* from 13.5% to 14.9%, driven by $12.6M of higher compensation, chiefly stock-based comp attributed to performance versus targets. Total SBC was $27.3M versus $18.4M, with the G&A component rising from $7.1M to $15.8M.

**The organic growth ladder is the decision-relevant series:**

| Period | Total organic | License-based organic |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | +8.0% | +5.6% |
| 1H 2026 | +7.2% | +2.8% |
| Q2 2026 | +6.8% | +3.1% |

Q2 improved marginally on Q1 (implied ~2.5%), so the sequential trend is not deteriorating further, but the step down from FY2025's 5.6% is large and has now persisted for two quarters. Segment organic detail for Q2: Direct Platform +4.8%, PitchBook +4.9% (with disclosed "softness in venture capital and elevated churn in the corporate client segment"), Credit +23.3%, Retirement +17.0%, Wealth -4.6%, Sustainalytics -7.7%, Indexes +13.0% organic against +85.3% reported.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $589.7M, capex $147.1M, FCF $442.6M (18.1% of revenue), down 1.4% year over year despite operating income up 8.6%, on higher cash taxes ($171.8M versus $115.6M) and higher bonus payments. 1H 2026: OCF $247.2M against net income of $214.9M (1.15x conversion), capex $71.1M, FCF $176.1M. Q2 FCF of $122.5M should not be annualized: Morningstar pays its annual bonus in Q1 (~$165.6M for the 2025 program), and accrued compensation fell from $294.2M at year-end to $181.3M at June 30. A defensible normalized frame is FY2025 actual $442.6M plus the ~$55M 1H improvement, or roughly **$480-500M for FY2026**, which we treat as a sensitivity rather than a forecast because it does not fully absorb the interest step-up from CRSP financing.

**Balance sheet — real leverage increase, misleading screens.** Total debt rose from $1,072.6M (12/31/2025) to **$1,713.3M** (6/30/2026): 2025 Term Facility $744.1M, revolver $620.0M, 2030 Notes $349.2M at 2.32%. Remaining revolver availability is **$130.0M of a $750.0M facility**. Covenant leverage was 1.3x at year-end and 1.9x at June 30, in compliance both dates. A 100bp SOFR move changes interest expense by $13.7M annualized, and nearly all incremental debt is floating.

Screening outputs flagging distress (Altman Z 1.46, current ratio 0.99, D/E 1.94) should be discounted but not dismissed. The Z-score input of 0.001 for EBIT/assets is inconsistent with audited figures — FY2025 EBIT/assets was 14.7% ($526.6M/$3,590.2M) — and correcting that input alone adds roughly 0.48 to the score given the 3.3x weight. The current ratio is structurally depressed by $628.4M of current deferred revenue, a performance obligation satisfied with content and staff already on payroll; excluding it, current assets exceed remaining current liabilities comfortably (ratio 1.06 as reported at June 30). D/E of 1.94 reflects treasury stock growing to $2,189.1M and equity falling to $1,023.8M while retained earnings *rose* to $2,383.3M. What is real: debt up roughly 60% in six months, revolver nearly drawn, and reduced capacity to fund another acquisition and $400M+ annual buybacks simultaneously. Goodwill and intangibles are 58.7% of total assets, and 85% of the $523.8M cash and investments sits outside the US.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025: 3,276,578 shares repurchased for $787.0M at an average of ~$240.2, approximately 21% above the current price, so that tranche is underwater on a mark-to-market basis. 1H 2026: 2,291,256 shares for $400.0M at ~$174.6 average, a materially better use of capital. Shares outstanding fell from 42,869,380 (12/31/2024) to 37,513,530 (6/30/2026), a 12.5% reduction in 18 months. $700M of the $1.0B three-year authorization was consumed in eight months, leaving $300M. 1H 2026 buybacks were **2.27x** 1H FCF; combined with the $359.6M CRSP outlay, that is the mechanical source of the debt increase.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has Morningstar earning $12.32 in FY2026 and $13.94 in FY2027 (+13.1%), on a non-GAAP basis consistent with the reported surprise history (Q2 2026 actual $3.10 versus GAAP diluted $2.83). Revisions are positive but modest: +3.3% and +3.6% over 30 days for the current and next year, +3.5% and +5.3% over 90 days. This does not approach the magnitude that signals an underestimated fundamental change; it is consensus catching up to a company that has beaten four straight quarters by 3.3% to 17.2%.

The growth architecture has three parts, and they have different durability.

**Contractual core (~69% of revenue, growing ~3% organically).** Flat seat counts at both Direct and PitchBook mean growth depends on annual price and use-case expansion. That is a legitimate growth mode for embedded workflow tools — deferred revenue of $647.5M and one-to-three-year license terms create real switching friction — but it is a thinner runway than seat growth, and the company disclosed specific soft spots this quarter: exchange market data softness at Morningstar Data, venture capital softness and corporate-client churn at PitchBook. Whether 3% is a floor or a waypoint is the single most important unresolved question in the name.

**Cyclical engine (Credit, 15.8% of Q2 revenue, growing 23-30%).** Credit revenue grew 21.7% in FY2025 and 30.3% in 1H 2026, with adjusted margin at 38.7% versus 32.8%. Credit's adjusted operating income growth of $39.2M in FY2025 essentially equaled consolidated operating income growth of $41.8M. The driver is "robust issuance" and "particular strength in US structured finance ratings." Near term this is a tailwind, but it is issuance-dependent revenue with limited cost flexibility in a downturn, because surveillance obligations on outstanding ratings continue regardless. A structured-finance issuance contraction removes most of the recent consolidated margin story, and the 10-K says so.

**Optionality (CRSP/Indexes).** CRSP contributed roughly $14.5M in Q2 (approximately $58M annualized) against $3.3 trillion of linked assets, an extraordinarily low monetization rate that is plausibly the entire investment case. If Morningstar can reprice or cross-sell into that base, the return on $363M is attractive, and the ~15-year tax amortization of $373M of goodwill and intangibles adds real cash value. The counterargument is that the low rate is the equilibrium: index licensees at that scale are very large passive sponsors with credible in-housing alternatives and substantial negotiating leverage. Morningstar's own 10-K now lists "failing to achieve the anticipated benefits of the CRSP acquisition" as its **first** forward-looking risk. We have no visibility into CRSP's licensees, contract terms, or renewal dates, so we assign this option no value in the base case.

Two smaller items deserve mention. Wealth's reported -6.2% conceals a genuine turnaround: excluding the Morningstar Office sunset, Q2 organic revenue would have risen 5.3%, and adjusted operating income swung from -$9.3M (FY2024) to +$13.5M in 1H 2026 alone. Sustainalytics is shrinking (-11.0% organic in 1H) against EU ESG-ratings-provider rules taking effect in 2026 and US political pressure on ESG mandates.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | At $199.325 |
|---|---|
| TTM adjusted diluted EPS (derived, $2.55+$2.71+$3.18+$3.10) | $11.54 → **17.3x** |
| FY2026 consensus EPS $12.32 | **16.2x** |
| FY2027 consensus EPS $13.94 | **14.3x** |
| Market cap | $7,477M |
| Net debt (6/30/26: $1,713.3M debt less $523.8M cash/investments) | $1,189M |
| Enterprise value | $8,666M |
| FY2025 EBITDA (op. income $526.6M + D&A $189.9M) | $716.5M → **12.1x trailing** |
| FCF yield on normalized $480M | **6.4%** on equity, 5.5% on EV |
| Peer median P/E (FDS 15.2, VIRT 9.2, MKTX 13.8, DFIN 29.3, CBOE 25.3) | 15.2 |

The bull argument on valuation is straightforward: roughly 12x trailing EV/EBITDA and 14.3x out-year earnings for a business with 27% adjusted operating margins, 30.6% ROE, and a share count shrinking 12.5% per 18 months is a wide discount to the multiples index and data franchises have historically commanded. The bear counterargument is equally straightforward: those historical multiples were predicated on durable 8-10% organic subscription growth with unquestioned pricing power. At 3.1% license organic growth, the historical anchor is the wrong one, and the correct comparison set is the currently de-rated cohort — FactSet at 15.2x and 43% below its high, MarketAxess at 13.8x and 42% below its high. Morningstar's premium to that median is defensible on growth and margin differential but is not evidence of mispricing.

The earnings growth carrying the forward multiple down is partly manufactured. Of Q2's 35.4% EPS growth, roughly 14 points came from share count. That is real value creation when shares are bought below intrinsic value — the 1H 2026 average of $174.6 looks good — but it is funded at 2.3x FCF with debt, and $300M of authorization remains. The FY2025 tranche at ~$240 argues against treating management's intrinsic-value estimate as a reliable timing signal.

**Scenario framework:**

- **Base $230** — 16.5x FY2027 consensus $13.94. Assumes license organic holds 3-4%, Credit issuance normalizes toward mid-teens growth rather than contracting, CRSP contributes revenue but no repricing, buyback continues at a reduced pace as leverage constrains. +15.4% from $199.33.
- **Bull $275** — 19x on FY2027 EPS of ~$14.50. Requires license organic reaccelerating above 5% for two quarters (price actions landing, exchange-data and PitchBook softness resolving) plus disclosed evidence of CRSP monetization. +38.0%.
- **Bear $155** — 12.5x on a cut FY2027 estimate near $12.40. Requires US structured-finance issuance contracting, taking Credit revenue and its 38.7% margin down with it, license organic drifting to ~2%, and the multiple compressing to the FactSet/MarketAxess trough range. -22.2%, roughly 9% above the June 25 low of $141.84.

Reward-to-risk to the base case is roughly 0.7:1, and to the bull case roughly 1.7:1 — insufficient for new capital. At $165-172 the base-case upside would be 34-39% against approximately -6% to -10% of incremental bear-case downside, which would restore a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Morningstar competes on several fronts at once: institutional data and analytics, private-capital-markets intelligence, credit ratings, index provision, ESG ratings, and asset/retirement management. The filings do not name competitors but define the pressures precisely.

Trajectory is the concern, and it is negative in the largest segments. Flat license counts at Direct and PitchBook, license organic growth halving, and adjusted margin compression in both (-0.8pp and -1.4pp respectively) are consistent with a market where the buyer base is consolidating and rationalizing seats. The 10-K lists "vendor consolidation and clients' strategic decisions to replace our products and services with in-house products and services" as a standalone forward-looking risk.

The company does not dispute the AI-substitution question. The FY2025 10-K states that "market sentiment regarding the impact of AI on software and data company growth prospects has driven meaningful recent sector-wide stock price declines, including Morningstar's," adding "despite strong operating performance," and warns about free or low-cost AI tools scraping data "including our own content." The Q2 2026 10-Q *expanded* this risk factor — one of only a handful flagged as changed — adding risk where data is "delivered to end users through third-party AI tools or other intermediaries," and that third-party AI systems "may also ingest, train on, reproduce, summarize, simulate or otherwise use our proprietary data, research, ratings, methodologies, content or other intellectual property without permission in ways that are difficult to detect or enforce against." Management is simultaneously spending on AI, with $6.1M of incremental cost-of-revenue in the quarter for AI-related computing and software.

The segments most exposed to substitution are those selling descriptive data and research summaries — precisely the segments with flat seats and decelerating organic growth. The segments least exposed are those where the output is a regulated, licensed, or legally required artifact: NRSRO credit ratings, index licensing under the EU Benchmarks Regulation, and ERISA-fiduciary managed accounts. Morningstar's capital allocation over 18 months has moved toward that regulated side — $363M into CRSP, DealX into ratings-adjacent data, Lumonic into private-credit monitoring. Whether that is deliberate hedging or an attractively priced asset from a motivated non-commercial seller with post-hoc strategic framing, both readings fit the evidence, and the direction is favorable either way.

One additional structural exposure: the SEC has sought comment on whether index providers, model-portfolio providers, and pricing services should be regulated as investment advisers. That would land directly on the newly enlarged Indexes business and on Wealth.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit cyclicality is the largest unpriced risk.** Credit is 94% transaction-based and has supplied effectively all of the recent consolidated operating income growth. A US structured-finance issuance downturn — triggered by spread widening, a policy-rate increase, or a credit repricing event — would reverse that with limited cost offset. The market appears to be capitalizing this revenue at the same multiple as the subscription base.

**Leverage into a higher-rate scenario.** Debt at $1,713.3M, revolver availability at $130.0M, nearly all floating, $13.7M of annualized interest sensitivity per 100bp, interest expense already up 86% year over year in the quarter. Covenant leverage of 1.9x against ~$480M of FCF is comfortable, but flexibility has narrowed materially.

**Unquantified contingencies.** Third-party data-vendor agreements grant extensive audit rights that "have in the past and in the future may be triggered," with the company stating it "generally [is] not able to reasonably estimate a possible loss." One current ERISA class action is referenced without sizing. No accruals disclosed.

**Goodwill and intangibles at 58.7% of assets**, with the CRSP allocation preliminary and revisable within one year. The 10-K names "a significant and sustained decline in stock price and market capitalization" as an impairment trigger; at $7.48B market cap against $1,023.8M book equity there is no immediate trigger.

**Governance and independence.** Mansueto held ~37.5% at year-end 2025, giving him substantial influence over shareholder matters and creating the risk the 10-K names: a rapid stake reduction could pressure the price. The filings are candid about a structure in which Morningstar rates clients' funds, runs its own funds and advisory business — "both the referee and the player in the same industry" — and operates an issuer-pay ratings model. For a company whose stated moat is trust, this is structural. Separately, director Steven Kaplan is a University of Chicago Booth faculty member and CRSP was acquired from the University of Chicago; we found no related-party disclosure in the sections reviewed and flag this as an open question rather than a finding.

**Customer concentration is undisclosed.** Neither filing reviewed provides customer-concentration data. The shifting segment mix establishes nothing about customer diversification.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($275, +38%).** A 27%-adjusted-margin data and index franchise trading at 14.3x out-year earnings and ~12x trailing EV/EBITDA is mispriced if the AI-substitution fear proves to be sentiment rather than substance. Management said as much in its own risk factors. The evidence for that reading: adjusted operating margin expanded 2.8pp in the quarter, FCF nearly doubled, estimates are rising, deferred revenue grew, four consecutive beats, and license organic growth improved sequentially from Q1 to Q2. On top of that sits CRSP — $3.3 trillion of newly acquired linked assets generating roughly $58M of revenue, acquired from a non-commercial seller, with 15-year tax amortization on $373M of purchase accounting. Modest repricing or data cross-selling would be highly accretive against a $363M purchase price. A share count shrinking 12.5% per 18 months compounds any of these outcomes into EPS. The regulated and licensed portions of the revenue base — NRSRO ratings, benchmark-governed index licensing, ERISA managed accounts — are structurally insulated from LLM substitution in ways the market has not distinguished from the descriptive-data business.

**Bear case ($155, -22%).** The recurring 69% of revenue is growing at 3% with flat seats, and the company's own expanded Q2 disclosure describes third-party AI systems ingesting its proprietary data, research, ratings and methodologies "in ways that are difficult to detect or enforce against." If that is the mechanism behind flat seats rather than ordinary cyclicality, price-led growth in a shrinking-seat market has a short runway. The reported earnings improvement rests on a structured-finance issuance cycle running at an unusually strong level, a business with limited downside cost flexibility, capitalized at the same multiple as the subscription base. The company has levered up roughly 60% in six months to fund buybacks at 2.3x FCF and a $363M acquisition, leaving $130M of revolver availability and floating-rate exposure. Sustainalytics is shrinking 11% organically into new EU rules. The SEC has asked whether index providers should be regulated as investment advisers, which would land on the segment Morningstar just enlarged 16-fold. High fixed costs — the 10-K states "a decline in our revenue may lead to a proportionally larger drop in operating income" — make any revenue reversal disproportionately damaging.

**Our resolution.** Both cases are live and neither is resolvable from current disclosure. What tips the call is price. At $153 in June the bull case was being offered at a discount deep enough that the unresolved questions were adequately compensated. At $199.33 base-case upside of 15% against 22% of bear-case downside is not adequate compensation, and the specific catalyst required for the bull case — evidence of CRSP monetization or license reacceleration — will not arrive before the Q3 print at the earliest.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026. The monthly Reg FD 8-K disclosures (filed roughly monthly, e.g. 6/25/2026, 5/22/2026) carry AUMA and flow data and are the highest-frequency check on the asset-based segments in the interim.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **License-based organic growth at or above 5.0% for two consecutive quarters** — this is the single fact that would move the core franchise from a 3% grower to a mid-single-digit grower and justify a 18-19x out-year multiple.
2. **Price at or below $172** — restores base-case upside to approximately 34% against roughly -10% incremental bear-case downside.
3. **Disclosed evidence of CRSP repricing or cross-sell** — Indexes organic growth above 20% with disclosed segment margin, or management quantifying incremental revenue per dollar of linked assets. This converts an unvalued option into a base-case input.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Morningstar Credit organic revenue growth below +5% for two consecutive quarters**, indicating the structured-finance issuance cycle has turned. Credit has supplied effectively all recent consolidated operating income growth; its reversal would take consensus estimates down 10-15%.
2. **License-based organic growth below 2.0%**, which would confirm price-led growth has exhausted itself against flat seats.
3. **Covenant leverage above 2.5x with revolver availability below $75M**, or any goodwill impairment charge against CRSP.

**What we are explicitly not treating as a downgrade trigger:** continued Mansueto selling under the 10b5-1 plan adopted June 8, 2026 (up to 200,000 shares, expiring 4/30/2027). This is programmatic diversification by a founder with an outsized single-stock concentration, and it co-occurs with rising consensus estimates and record margins. We weight it as low-signal. The CFO's open-market purchase of 1,000 shares at $186.59 on March 4, 2026 is a small but genuine positive that partially offsets it.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 5.9, down from 6.4, with the recommendation moving from BUY after three consecutive BUY calls. The decline is concentrated in valuation and growth, and it reflects entry arithmetic plus two confirmed facts from the Q2 filing rather than a broken thesis.

What improved: Q2 operating performance across every reported line, free cash flow conversion, Wealth's underlying turnaround to an 11.4% adjusted margin, estimate revisions of +3.5% and +5.3% over 90 days, and a fourth consecutive beat. What deteriorated or was newly confirmed: total debt at $1,713.3M with $130M of revolver availability and near-total floating-rate exposure; adjusted margin compression in both of the two largest segments; license-based organic growth at 3.1% in 69% of revenue with flat seat counts; and an expanded AI risk factor that escalates the disclosure from demand risk to unenforceable IP leakage. What did not change and is now the dominant consideration: the price is 40.5% above the June low and 15.5% above the level at our last review, which has consumed the margin of safety without resolving either open question.

For existing holders, the position remains worth owning. Roughly 12x trailing EV/EBITDA and a 6.4% free cash flow yield on a business retiring 12.5% of its shares per 18 months delivers a mid-teens annualized total return before any multiple recovery, and CRSP is a genuine unvalued option. For new capital, wait for either license organic growth to clear 5% or a price near $172. The stock does not need to be sold; it does need to be cheaper before it is bought.
