## Executive Summary

Arlo has completed the hardest part of a hardware-to-subscription transition. Subscription and service revenue reached $183.1M in the first half of FY2026 (+24.6%), annual recurring revenue is $365.0M, paid accounts are 6.303M, and service gross margin was 82.4% for the half. The company produced its first full-year GAAP operating income in FY2025 ($6.1M) and first net income ($14.9M), carries no funded debt, holds $141.1M of cash and Treasuries, and has an undrawn $45.0M revolver. That is a materially better business than the one that lost $56.6M in FY2022.

The problem is what the reported profitability is made of. Trailing-twelve-month GAAP net income of approximately $30.5M — the basis for the quoted 54.9x P/E — contains a $6.4M gain on a related-party investment sale, an $8.0M IEEPA tariff refund recorded as a reduction of cost of revenue, and a $4.1M gain on early lease termination. Those three items are $18.5M, or 61% of trailing GAAP net income, and none recurs. Underlying trailing GAAP earnings power is closer to $12M, or roughly $0.11 per diluted share. At the other extreme, management's FY2026 non-GAAP guidance of $0.90–$1.00 excludes stock compensation running at roughly $83M annualized, a figure that at $41.4M in the first half exceeded first-half operating cash flow of $39.3M. True per-share economics sit between those poles and are closer to break-even than to $0.95.

Two structural facts govern the valuation. First, hardware is still sold at a loss: Q2 product gross profit was $(0.2)M on $62.9M of product revenue, but backing out the $8.0M refund puts it at roughly $(8.2)M, or about (13.1)% margin, essentially unchanged from (15.4)% a year earlier. Second, customer concentration is worsening while the contractual floor is gone. One customer (circumstantially Verisure) was 40% of first-half revenue versus 35% a year earlier and 60% of net accounts receivable, under an exclusive European agreement extended to November 2029 with no minimum purchase obligations. That same channel swung EMEA revenue −24.2% in FY2025 and +41.7% in the first half of FY2026. Management's FY2026 revenue guide of $580–600M implies second-half revenue of roughly $274–294M against $280.8M a year earlier, approximately flat, which reads as an acknowledgment that the first-half surge was order-timing recovery rather than a new run rate. We rate ARLO **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

Arlo designs app-controlled smart security devices (Wi-Fi and LTE cameras, video doorbells, floodlight cameras, and a multi-sensor home security system) and monetizes the installed base through cloud subscriptions: Arlo Secure, Arlo Total Security (hardware-inclusive with professional monitoring), Arlo Safe, and Arlo SmartCloud, a B2B computer-vision platform. Cumulative devices shipped reached ~45.6M and registered accounts ~13.6M as of 2026-06-28.

Manufacturing is fully outsourced to Asian ODMs (Foxconn, Tonly, Alpha Networks, Pegatron, Chicony) with production concentrated in Vietnam, Thailand and Indonesia. The cloud platform runs on AWS, including an Irish region for EU data. Amazon therefore occupies three simultaneous roles (infrastructure supplier, major retail channel, and owner of two direct competitors, Blink and Ring), a dependency the company discloses as a specific risk. Distribution spans retail (Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, Costco, Home Depot), carriers (AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon, Telstra, Vodafone), security providers (Verisure), and arlo.com. Headcount was ~376 at year-end, of which 186 in R&D. One reportable segment.

Two acquisitions closed in the first half of FY2026 and shifted the strategic perimeter: Canary Connect (asset purchase, 2026-03-11, $36.0M cash plus $12.9M fixed deferred consideration, $27.9M goodwill) consolidating subscribers in the core category, and Aloe Care Health (merger, 2026-04-16, $15.0M cash plus up to $25.0M earnout carried at $5.4M fair value, $9.0M goodwill) extending the platform into AI-based medical alert and fall-prevention services for aging-in-place care. Goodwill rose from $11.0M to $47.9M and net acquired intangibles to $25.7M, against $158.5M of stockholders' equity.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory ($000s, per SEC XBRL and filings):**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 490,414 | 491,176 | 510,886 | 529,297 |
| Gross margin | 27.7% | 34.1% | 36.7% | 44.0% |
| Operating income | (56,879) | (24,903) | (34,892) | 6,071 |
| Net income | (56,626) | (22,036) | (30,504) | 14,926 |
| Cash | 84,024 | 56,522 | 82,032 | 146,440 |

FY2025 revenue grew only 3.6%, but composition changed decisively: subscriptions +30.2% to $316.4M (59.8% of revenue) while products fell 20.5% to $212.9M. The segment gross profit split is the central fact about this P&L: subscriptions earned $264.0M on $316.4M (83.5% margin); products lost $31.2M on $212.9M ((14.6)% margin, down from (0.3)% in FY2024). Management attributes the deterioration to deliberate ASP cuts and promotion "to stimulate household acquisition and subscriber growth," plus tariffs, quantifying the tariff contribution at $13.5M, or 6.3 points of the 14.3-point decline. The residual ~8 points is pricing, promotion and air freight. Hardware is functionally a customer-acquisition cost booked in cost of revenue rather than in sales and marketing, which makes consolidated gross margin partly a mix artifact: total margin can rise while hardware economics worsen.

**First half FY2026** ($000s): revenue $306,319 (+23.3%), gross profit $147,884 (48.3% margin, +370bp), operating income $10,014 versus $482, net income $17,905 versus $2,289. Q2 alone: revenue $155.9M (+20.5%), gross margin 48.2%, operating income $2.5M, net income $3.0M, diluted EPS $0.03.

Three adjustments materially change how that half reads.

1. **Tariff refund.** Following the 2026-02-20 Supreme Court ruling voiding certain IEEPA tariffs and the 2026-03-04 CIT refund order, Arlo recognized $8.0M in Q2 as a reduction of cost of revenue under an ASC 450 gain-contingency model, plus $0.2M interest, with a further $6.3M received after quarter-end. Total windfall ~$14.3M, roughly reversing one year of incremental tariff cost, and finite. The $8.0M alone is 5.1 points of Q2's 48.2% consolidated gross margin.
2. **Product margin unfixed.** Ex-refund, Q2 product gross profit was approximately $(8.2)M on $62.9M of revenue, ~(13.1)% margin, versus (15.4)% in the prior-year quarter. Improvement of about two points, not fifteen.
3. **Cash conversion stalled.** Operating cash flow was $39.3M versus $39.7M a year earlier despite net income rising from $2.3M to $17.9M. Receivables consumed $23.9M (versus $4.2M) and grew 60.4% to $63.6M against 23.3% revenue growth; inventories consumed $4.1M versus providing $9.8M. Deferred revenue contributed +$13.2M. The benign reading is a late-quarter shipment into a partner on longer-than-U.S. terms, which reverses on collection; the alternative is a structurally longer cash cycle as partner-channel mix grows. With 60% of receivables in one counterparty, the credit concentration is not academic either way.

**Balance sheet (2026-06-28):** cash $101.4M plus short-term Treasuries $39.7M = $141.1M; restricted cash $1.9M; total assets $374.6M; total liabilities $216.1M; equity $158.5M; **no funded debt drawn**, revolver undrawn, covenants (fixed charge coverage ≥1.50x, net leverage ≤3.00x) in compliance. The "debt/equity 1.43" figure circulating in screening data is total liabilities divided by equity and should not be read as leverage; the liabilities are payables, sales-incentive and return reserves, and deferred revenue. Similarly, FY2025's $5,452K is *interest income, net*, an inflow.

**The share-count problem.** Shares outstanding went 100.9M (2024-12-31) → 105.0M (2025-12-31) → 107.6M (2026-06-28), a 6.6% increase over eighteen months during which the company spent $75.4M on repurchases ($45.5M at ~$13.79 average in FY2025; $29.9M at ~$13.00 in 1H26). First-half gross issuance under stock plans was 4.802M shares against 2.273M repurchased. Net dilution is running near 4.5–5% annually with buybacks already applied. Unrecognized compensation cost on unvested RSUs and PSUs is $92.9M over a 2.3-year weighted average, and an evergreen provision added 4,200,189 shares on 2026-01-23. First-half SBC of $41.4M (13.5% of revenue) exceeded first-half operating cash flow of $39.3M. That is the cleanest single test of whether the reported "free cash flow" accrues to shareholders: at present it substantially does not.

Reported FY2025 FCF of $66.9M (12.6% of revenue) also benefited from a $17.7M receivables release; the first half of FY2026 shows the mirror image. Neither figure is a clean forward run rate.

## Growth Analysis

The durable engine is subscriptions. Paid accounts moved 4.599M → 5.687M → 6.303M (FY2024 → FY2025 → 1H26) and ARR $257.3M → $330.5M → $365.0M. Conversion of registered to paid accounts improved from 42.5% to 46.8% in FY2025 and held at 46.5%. FY2025 subscription cost of revenue actually *fell* 4.2% while paid accounts grew 23.7%, producing real platform leverage and the reason service margin reached 83.5%.

That leverage reversed in the most recent half. Subscription cost of revenue rose 31.7% (43.7% in Q2) against subscription revenue growth of 24.6% (19.0% in Q2), pushing service gross margin from 83.3% to 82.4% for the half and from 84.3% to 81.1% in Q2. Part of the compression is acquisition accounting: amortization in service cost of revenue rose from $0.6M to $2.5M, plus $1.2M of acquired-intangible amortization; and part is stated platform reinvestment. Q2's 320bp decline is the first meaningful break in the cloud-efficiency story and warrants monitoring.

Product revenue swung from −20.5% (FY2025) to +21.4% (1H26). The 10-Q attributes this primarily to higher shipments to the largest EMEA customer, plus lower sales incentives and returns. Country disclosure makes the driver unambiguous: Spain $77.6M (from $56.8M) and Sweden $43.5M (from $22.5M), with EMEA up 41.7% to $122.1M. The FY2025 EMEA decline and the 1H26 surge are, on the evidence, the same counterparty's order timing reversing.

**Guidance arithmetic matters here.** Externally reported FY2026 guidance is $580–600M revenue and $0.90–$1.00 non-GAAP EPS, with Q3 guided to ~$145M. First-half actual of $306.3M plus $145M is $451.3M, implying Q4 of roughly $129–149M, flat to down sequentially, and implying second-half revenue of ~$274–294M against $280.8M a year earlier. So a company that grew 23.3% in the first half is guiding the full year to +9.6% to +13.4%. Some of that is conservatism: Arlo has beaten consensus four consecutive quarters, by 38.4%, 42.6%, 31.3% and 4.4%. But the deceleration is consistent with the tariff refund not recurring and with the EMEA comparison turning hostile, and it should not be dismissed.

Estimate revisions corroborate the near-term momentum without reaching inflection velocity: current-year consensus $0.94 (+13.9% over 90 days, +12.1% over 30), next year $1.04 (+8.6%/+5.9%), seven analysts. Rising, but well below the +30% threshold that marks a genuine earnings inflection, and on a non-GAAP basis.

Optionality not yet in the numbers: the June 2025 ADT partnership generated zero FY2025 revenue and is unquantified in FY2026 (ADT is also listed as a competitor); Aloe Care moves Arlo toward caregiver- and potentially payor-funded monitoring, where churn is structurally lower; and municipalities increasingly requiring video verification to reduce false-alarm dispatches supports Arlo Total Security. None of these is yet a revenue line worth underwriting.

## Valuation Assessment

The earnings basis has to be stated explicitly because the three available figures differ by an order of magnitude:

- **Trailing GAAP:** TTM net income ≈ $30.5M, ≈ $0.28 diluted → **54.9x**. Strip the $6.4M investment gain, $8.0M tariff refund and $4.1M lease gain and underlying TTM earnings are ~$12M, ~$0.11 per share → **~138x**.
- **FY2026 non-GAAP guidance/consensus:** $0.94 consensus → **16.2x**; FY2027 $1.04 → **14.6x**. This excludes ~$83M annualized SBC, which is real dilution.
- **Cash:** FY2025 FCF $66.9M → 4.0% yield on market cap, ~4.4% on EV of roughly $1.53B (market cap less $141.1M liquid, plus $12.9M deferred consideration and $5.4M earnout). Charge SBC and the yield approaches zero.

Peer comparison is of limited use: Communications Equipment medians are distorted (HLIT 191x, EXTR 258x, DGII 60x, NTCT 32x, IDCC 26x; median 60.4x). Against DGII and NTCT, the closest in size and business character, 16x forward non-GAAP is not demanding. Against IDCC's 26x on a licensing model, it is not obviously cheap either.

The framework that travels best for Arlo is EV/ARR. At $1.53B EV against $365.0M ARR the stock trades at **4.2x ARR** for a business growing ARR in the low-20s with 82–84% service gross margin. For a pure software franchise that would be inexpensive. For a consumer subscription funded by selling hardware at a ~13% gross loss, with churn, CAC and LTV entirely undisclosed, and with 40% of revenue routed through a single exclusive counterparty carrying no purchase minimum, 4.2x is approximately fair.

A steady-state check: apply FY2026 guided revenue of ~$590M with subscriptions near $375M at 83% margin ($311M of service gross profit) and hardware losing ~$25–30M, against annualized opex of roughly $276M (from the first-half run rate); operating income lands near $10M, which is what the company is in fact printing. The model only produces meaningful profit if opex growth decelerates sharply relative to subscription gross profit, or if hardware losses narrow structurally. Neither has yet happened absent the customs refund.

**Base case $16.00** — 15.4x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.04, cross-checked against 4.3x EV/ARR on ARR of roughly $400M by year-end plus $123M of net cash after deferred consideration and earnout. That is 5.1% above the $15.23 basis. **Bull case $22.00** (+44%): subscription growth holds above 20%, hardware reaches breakeven on ocean freight and ASP stabilization, ADT contributes, and the multiple re-rates toward 19–20x a $1.15 FY2027 figure; the analyst mean of $21.00 sits inside this. **Bear case $10.50** (−31%): the EMEA channel normalizes down as it did in FY2025, service margin keeps compressing, the refund is absent, and FY2027 non-GAAP falls to ~$0.80 at an 13x multiple; the 52-week low of $11.05 is the reference.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives approximately +6% expected return over twelve months against roughly 50% implied volatility. That is a fair-return profile, not an asymmetric one.

Analyst targets of $18–25 (mean $21) sit 18–64% above spot with all seven analysts at Buy or Strong Buy. Those targets are built on the same non-GAAP basis that excludes $83M of stock compensation; they are corroboration of sell-side sentiment rather than independent evidence of mispricing, and at a 16x headline multiple they are not the "targets are floors" case that applies to a clean inflection story with compressed multiples and 30%+ revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

Arlo claims a leading position in the U.S. consumer network-connected camera market. It competes against Amazon (Blink, Ring) and Google (Nest), for whom cameras are an ecosystem hook rather than a profit center; against professional monitoring incumbents ADT and SimpliSafe; and against a value tier (TP-Link, Eufy, Wyze) that the 10-K concedes prices "significantly below our product costs." The company's own stated advantages are brand, U.S. camera share, the cloud/AI platform, direct user relationships and supplier relationships; cost and scale are conceded.

The defensible asset is the installed-base-to-subscription funnel and the platform beneath it: 300 million events per day through the object-detection stack, 146 issued U.S. patents, and service margins in the low-80s that were achieved while absolute subscription cost of revenue fell in FY2025. Trajectory, which is what matters, is mixed. Positive: paid accounts +37% year over year, ARR +24%, conversion rate holding near 47%, and two acquisitions that add subscribers and extend into a lower-churn adjacency. Negative: hardware gross margin at (13)% underlying is direct evidence of eroding pricing power against value entrants; service gross margin reversed 320bp in Q2; and dependency on the exclusive European partner deepened rather than diversified.

Geographic mix is not diversification. Americas share moved 52.1% → 64.2% → 56.5% across FY2024, FY2025 and 1H26, oscillating with one partner's order timing, while single-customer revenue concentration went 32% → 40% and receivable concentration reached 60%. Mix tells you where product shipped; it says nothing about counterparty dependence, which is unambiguously worse.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Customer concentration without a floor (dominant).** One customer was 40% of first-half revenue and 60% of net receivables. Verisure's original $500M five-year minimum purchase commitment was fulfilled in 2024; on 2024-04-25 Verisure extended the agreement five years to November 2029 **with no minimum purchase obligations**, and a performance obligation does not arise until Arlo accepts a purchase order. The 10-K states plainly that volumes fell after minimums lapsed. Verisure also holds exclusive European retail and direct distribution rights, so Arlo cannot route around the relationship. FY2025's −24.2% EMEA decline is the realized downside case. Disclosed Verisure backlog was $40.0M at quarter-end (from $46.3M), converting over six months, which is near-term continuity rather than a multi-year commitment. The identification of "one customer" as Verisure is strong circumstantial inference from the exclusivity, the Spain/Sweden concentration and the separate backlog disclosure, not a filing fact.

**2. Structurally negative hardware margin.** (14.6)% in FY2025 and roughly (13)% ex-refund in Q2 FY2026. The model works only if subscription lifetime value exceeds the hardware subsidy plus support cost. Arlo discloses no churn, gross-versus-net adds, CAC or LTV, so the central economic question cannot be tested from filings.

**3. Non-recurring flatter.** ~$14.3M of IEEPA refunds across Q2 and Q3 FY2026 substantially reverse one year of incremental tariff cost and will not repeat; ongoing non-IEEPA duties persist. Combined with the $6.4M investment gain and prior-year $4.1M lease gain, 61% of trailing GAAP net income is non-recurring.

**4. Deferred tax valuation allowance release.** Full allowance retained against federal NOLs ~$83.8M, state NOLs ~$79.5M and R&D credits of ~$14.7M/$13.8M, with both filings stating a "reasonable possibility" of release "within the next few quarters." Release would produce a large one-time non-cash GAAP benefit and then lift the go-forward tax rate from today's 4.5%. Screens keyed on GAAP EPS or ROE will misread both the release quarter and the quarters after.

**5. Goodwill and integration.** Goodwill tripled to $47.9M plus $25.7M of net intangibles against $158.5M of equity, with no post-acquisition annual impairment test yet performed, while the 10-Q reports "no significant changes" to risk factors despite entry into healthcare-adjacent regulation.

**6. Incentive design.** PSU performance conditions are exclusively subscription metrics — ARR, cumulative paid accounts, subscription gross profit, subscription gross margin — with no consolidated profitability, free-cash-flow or total-return component. February 2026 grants totaled 1,643,000 PSUs to three officers (CEO 1,080,000; CFO/COO 390,000; GC 173,000). This geometry rewards exactly the trade-off being made and is silent on the hardware loss. The counterargument is that subscription-only metrics are conventional mid-transition and that dollar-denominated subscription gross profit imposes some discipline; management has in fact delivered GAAP operating income under the scheme. Noted as risk, not as an assertion of intent.

**7. Regulatory.** Person-detection features expose Arlo to Illinois BIPA, CCPA, GDPR (up to €20M or 4% of revenue) and the EU AI Act (up to €35M or 7%), with EU data resident in Ireland and EMEA at ~40% of first-half revenue. The 10-K discloses prior inquiries into data privacy practices.

**8. Supply chain.** No long-term ODM contracts; sole-source lenses, sensors and custom batteries; epidemic-failure clauses in certain customer contracts can trigger replacement, credits, liquidated damages and reimbursement of freight and truck-roll costs.

**9. Unresolved items.** Shareholder activism is disclosed in the past tense ("has diverted the attention of management") with no identifiable activist, timing or resolution; the substance of the 2026-04-07 bylaw amendment (Item 5.03) is unread; FY2026 guidance and the ~298,000 Q2 paid-account additions are externally reported and unconfirmed against a company filing. These should not be treated as established either way.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is non-bearish. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.3% sits essentially on top of 50.9% one-year realized (spread −0.7%, 17th percentile within the chain), so the market is not pricing elevated event risk despite the print two days before the observation date. Skew is call-heavy across the curve (−17.6% at one month, −23.3% at six months), put/call volume ratio is 0.07 and open-interest ratio 0.01, indicating essentially no put protection outstanding. Term structure is flat (50.3% near versus 49.3% far). The implied ±7.9% move on the nearest expiry is modest for a 50%-vol name.

This cuts against the 13.8% short interest with 9.4 days to cover. Options traders are positioned for upside while a meaningful institutional short base persists, a configuration that makes a squeeze on any Q3 beat plausible and argues against an outright bearish stance, but that also means there is no cheap protection available if the second-half revenue guide proves accurate rather than sandbagged. For a HOLD, the practical read is that near-term optionality is fairly priced in both directions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($22.00, +44%).** ARR compounds above 20% as Canary consolidates subscribers and Aloe Care adds a lower-churn, caregiver-funded revenue stream that is less discretionary than security cameras. Hardware losses narrow structurally as the air-to-ocean freight shift persists, warranty and inventory reserves stay lower, and ASP promotion moderates once the paid-account base is large enough to self-fund. ADT begins contributing in FY2027. The four-quarter beat record (31–43% surprises on three of four) proves guidance is sandbagged and Q3/Q4 land above the implied $129–149M. Estimates continue rising from $1.04; the valuation-allowance release and a 19–20x multiple on $1.15 of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS produce $22. Sell-side targets of $18–25 and 13.8% short interest supply the mechanical fuel.

**Bear case ($10.50, −31%).** The EMEA partner's first-half order surge reverses exactly as FY2025 demonstrated it can, taking 40% of revenue and 60% of receivables with it; guidance implying flat second-half revenue is the warning, not conservatism. The $14.3M customs windfall is absent from FY2027 entirely, exposing (13)% hardware margin. Service gross margin continues the 320bp Q2 reversal as platform reinvestment and acquired-intangible amortization build. Opex growth (1H26 annualizes near $276M against $232.8M of FY2025 gross profit) outruns subscription gross profit. Net dilution near 5% annually continues despite buybacks, and the buyback authorization has $20.1M left. FY2027 non-GAAP falls toward $0.80 at 13x. The 52-week low of $11.05 is only 27% below spot.

The bull case requires either that hardware economics repair without the refund, or that subscriber LTV justifies the subsidy, and Arlo discloses no churn, CAC or LTV data with which to verify the second. That absence is the reason confidence cannot be higher.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). Existing holders can maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $15.23.

- **Base case: $16.00** (+5.1%) — 15.4x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.04, cross-checked at ~4.3x EV/ARR on year-end ARR near $400M plus $123M net cash after deferred consideration and earnout.
- **Bull case: $22.00** (+44%) — 19x $1.15 FY2027 non-GAAP EPS on hardware breakeven and sustained 20%+ ARR growth.
- **Bear case: $10.50** (−31%) — 13x $0.80 FY2027 non-GAAP EPS on EMEA order reversal and continued service-margin compression.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 FY2026 print (early November 2026, which should contain the residual ~$6.3M refund and the first clean read on EMEA order flow) and the Q4/FY2026 print (late February 2027).
- **Probability-weighted expected return:** approximately +6% at 50/25/25 weighting — fair, not asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Product gross margin above (5)% for two consecutive quarters **excluding** tariff refunds, evidencing durable hardware repair rather than a customs windfall.
2. Q3 or Q4 revenue above the implied $129–149M quarterly guide **with** disclosed single-customer concentration falling below 35%, or first quantified ADT contribution — either of which would break the concentration dependency.
3. Any disclosure of churn, gross-versus-net paid adds, or subscriber payback period that validates the negative-hardware-margin acquisition model, combined with a price near $12.50–13.00 (where the company itself repurchased).

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Single-customer revenue concentration exceeding 45%, or receivable concentration exceeding 65%, or a sequential EMEA revenue decline greater than 20%.
2. Service gross margin below 80% for two consecutive quarters, extending the Q2 reversal beyond acquisition amortization.
3. Operating cash flow below net income for a second consecutive half, or accounts receivable growing more than 25 points faster than revenue again, signalling structural rather than timing-driven cash conversion deterioration.
4. Net share count rising more than 4% year over year with the $20.1M remaining authorization exhausted and unrenewed.

**Reassessment point:** the Q3 FY2026 report, which should isolate the residual refund, the first full quarter of Aloe Care, and whether the guided second-half deceleration is real.

## Conclusion

Arlo's score trajectory (6.5 in May, 5.8 in June, 5.5 now) has decelerated but not stabilized, and the decline is locatable rather than sentimental. Growth improved — first-half revenue +23.3%, ARR +$34.5M to $365.0M, current-year estimates +13.9% over 90 days, four straight beats. Valuation compressed against that improvement, because the price rose 16.6% since the prior review while roughly half of the Q2 margin repair was an $8.0M non-recurring customs refund and 61% of trailing GAAP net income is non-recurring. Risk deteriorated on the mechanical facts: single-customer revenue concentration from 35% to 40% and receivable concentration to 60%, under a contract with no purchase floor for three more years. Competitive position weakened at the margin on the first 320bp reversal in service gross margin.

The balance sheet is genuinely sound — no funded debt, $141.1M liquid, undrawn revolver, covenant compliance — so solvency is not the question. The question is whether shareholders receive the cash. First-half stock compensation of $41.4M exceeded first-half operating cash flow of $39.3M, and share count rose 6.6% over eighteen months despite $75.4M of well-timed repurchases at $13.00–13.79 averages. Until either hardware margin repairs without customs assistance or the company discloses subscriber economics that justify the subsidy, 4.2x EV/ARR and 16x a non-GAAP figure that excludes $83M of dilution is a fair price rather than a cheap one.

HOLD at $15.23. The $12.50–13.00 area, where the company itself was buying and where the bear case is substantially discounted, is where the risk/reward becomes more favorable.
