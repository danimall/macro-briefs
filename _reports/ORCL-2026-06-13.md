**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Oracle has reinvented itself over eighteen months from a steady, high-margin database and applications franchise into the most financially aggressive of the AI-infrastructure builders. Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) grew 93% in Q4 FY26 to roughly $5.79B, remaining performance obligations (RPO) ballooned to about $638B, and the company is committing roughly $70B of FY27 capex against a balance sheet that already carries $134.6B of debt and $261B of off-balance-sheet data-center lease commitments. The legacy software-support annuity (~$15B/year, near-100% renewal) is the cash engine, but it is no longer large enough to fund the build, which is why trailing-four-quarter free cash flow is negative roughly $25B and management plans to raise about $40B of debt and equity in FY27.

The central problem with the equity here is an internal inconsistency between the company's own guidance and the consensus numbers that anchor the bull case. Management guided FY27 total revenue to about $67.4B. Implied full-year FY26 revenue is roughly $67.35B (nine-month $48.17B plus Q4 $19.18B). That is essentially flat reported revenue growth, not the high-teens trajectory the stock has been priced on, and it sits alongside a guided gross-margin step-down as low-margin GPU-as-a-service displaces 70%-plus software margins in the revenue mix. Yet consensus FY27 EPS sits at $10.87, implying roughly 35% growth off the $8.05 current-year number. Flat revenue plus margin compression and rising interest expense is difficult to reconcile with 35% EPS growth. The forward multiple that makes Oracle look reasonable — roughly 17x on $10.87 — likely rests on estimates that contradict management's own revenue guide.

We rate ORCL AVOID. The business is a genuine beneficiary of AI-infrastructure demand, but the equity is a binary, leveraged, equity-junior bet on a small number of AI customers paying multi-year contracts, layered on negative free cash flow, a fresh securities class action naming the entire senior leadership, and a leadership team comprising two unproven co-CEOs and a roughly two-month-old CFO. The September Q1 FY27 print carries meaningful negative estimate-revision risk that consensus has not absorbed. We would re-engage only on a valuation reset toward the $130–150 range or on hard evidence that backlog is converting to cash without further dilution.

## Company Overview

Oracle operates three segments: Cloud and Software (~86–88% of revenue), Hardware (~4–5%), and Services (~9%). The historical moat is the Oracle Database, the most-deployed enterprise database, supplemented by Fusion ERP/HCM/SCM, NetSuite, Oracle Health (Cerner), middleware, and Java. The software-support base renews at near-100% and is recognized ratably, producing roughly $15B/year of high-margin, predictable cash flow.

The defining transformation is OCI, Oracle's push to become a fifth U.S. hyperscaler with a particular emphasis on AI training and inference capacity. Cloud services rose from 32% of revenue in FY23 to 43% in FY25 to roughly 52% by Q3 FY26. The strategy leverages Oracle's captive installed base (a migration funnel from on-premise database/ERP to OCI), its multicloud database offering (Autonomous Database running on AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud), and a claimed ability to stand up raw GPU capacity faster and cheaper than rivals. Oracle reports 97.5% GPU fleet utilization and a deep waitlist.

The flagship customer relationship is OpenAI, served through the Stargate / Abilene, Texas project. Oracle is the infrastructure landlord, not the model owner. Five major data-center sites totaling about 5.5 GW are under development (Abilene TX at 42% capacity, Shackleford TX, Dona Ana NM, Saline MI, Port Washington WI). Oracle lit 1.2 GW in FY26 and added 1 GW in Q1 FY27.

## Financial Analysis

**Headline growth is strong, driven entirely by OCI.** Q4 FY26 revenue was $19.18B (+20.6%); Q3 was $17.19B (+22%); nine-month FY26 revenue was $48.17B (+16%). OCI grew 84% in Q3 and 93% in Q4, with GPU/CPU rentals up roughly 2.2x in Q4. Cloud Applications grew a steadier ~12–13%, and software support was roughly flat. Software license revenue is in slow decline (-11% year-to-date).

**Operating margin is holding at the line, but earnings quality and trajectory are deteriorating.** Q3 operating margin was 32% and net income $3.72B (+27%), but Q4 net margin compressed to about 22.4% as buildout costs began to bite. Nine-month net income of $12.78B is flattered by a one-time $2.7B pre-tax gain on the sale of Ampere to SoftBank in November 2025 and was further distorted by a $958M tax charge tied to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Stripping the Ampere gain, underlying earnings quality is materially lower than the headline. Management has guided a gross-margin step-down for FY27 because GPU-as-a-service carries gross margins well below the legacy software business.

**The balance sheet is the central concern.** Total debt rose to roughly $134.6B at 2/28/26 from $92.6B at 5/31/25, with $43B of senior notes issued in nine months at coupons ranging 4.45%–6.85% (the long maturities at 6.7%–6.85%). Interest expense is running about $1.2B/quarter and rising. Stockholders' equity is only about $39B and carries an accumulated deficit of -$7.1B (a legacy of years of buybacks). Property, plant and equipment nearly doubled to $83.6B from $43.5B in nine months. The reported Altman Z-Score of roughly 0.87 places the company in the statistical distress zone, an unusual reading for an investment-grade software company and a direct artifact of the debt-funded build.

**Free cash flow has gone deeply negative.** Capex exploded to $39.2B in nine-month FY26 (from $12.1B prior year); full-year FY26 capex was about $55.7B (beating its own guide by $5.7B); FY27 capex is guided to about $70B. Operating cash flow of roughly $23.5B TTM is now dwarfed by capex, producing negative free cash flow of approximately $25B on a trailing basis, versus +$5.8B a year earlier. Beyond the balance-sheet debt sit $261B of additional data-center lease commitments (15–19 year terms commencing FY26–FY28) plus $11B of unconditional purchase obligations, largely for power. These off-balance-sheet obligations understate the true forward commitment.

**Funding the gap requires continuous capital-market access.** Oracle issued a $5B mandatory convertible preferred (6.50% Series D, February 2026), established a $20B ATM equity program (unused as of Q3), expanded commercial paper to $10B, and added a $10B revolver. The common dividend was raised to $0.50/quarter, but buybacks have been essentially halted ($95M in nine months) as the cash is needed elsewhere.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth story requires careful separation of trailing momentum from forward guidance, because they diverge sharply.

**Trailing:** OCI is genuinely inflecting — 93% growth in Q4, GPU rentals up 2.2x, total revenue up 20.6%. RPO grew from $130B (Feb 2025) to $552.6B (Q3 FY26) to about $638B (Q4 FY26). This is one of the largest backlog accumulations in enterprise software history.

**Forward:** CFO Hilary Maxson stated that only 12% of the ~$638B RPO (~$76.6B) will be recognized in FY27, with 34% (~$219.6B) over FY28–29. The bull thesis is entirely a backlog-conversion-and-timing story: revenue is back-end loaded into FY28 and beyond as data centers come online and customers ramp.

This creates a problem for the consensus EPS path. Flat reported revenue combined with a guided gross-margin step-down, rising interest expense on a growing debt load, and increasing depreciation as $83.6B of PP&E (and growing) is placed in service is very difficult to reconcile with 35% EPS growth. Estimate revisions have been mildly positive (+1.5% on FY27 over 30 days, +1.4% over 90 days), but those revisions predate full digestion of the FY27 revenue guide and margin commentary. The September Q1 FY27 print is the test: if reported revenue growth decelerates toward the guided flat trajectory while margins step down, consensus FY27 EPS faces downside revision that the current price has not absorbed.

The estimate-revision velocity here does not meet the threshold (>30% over 90 days on an inflection name at a reasonable multiple) that would flag a strong forward signal. Revisions are modest, and the revenue guide actively contradicts the EPS growth embedded in consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing P/E, ORCL screens at 31.6x — below the application-software peer median of 89.4x and well below the most direct AI-software comp, Palantir at 147x. On the consensus FY27 EPS, the stock trades at roughly 17x, which superficially looks inexpensive for a "hyperscaler."

The headline cheapness does not survive scrutiny on two fronts.

**First, the EV/EBITDA picture is far less attractive than the P/E.** With net debt running near $103B (total debt ~$134.6B less ~$31.9B cash) and a $529B equity value, enterprise value is roughly $632B before counting the off-balance-sheet lease commitments. Adding the leases, the true economic enterprise value approaches $893B against an EBITDA base that, while growing, is increasingly a low-margin, capital-intensive infrastructure stream rather than high-margin software. The leverage that the P/E ignores is precisely what the EV multiple captures, and it is what differentiates Oracle from cash-rich Microsoft, Amazon, and Google.

**Second, the 17x forward multiple rests on an EPS number that contradicts management's revenue guide.** If FY27 revenue is flat at ~$67.4B with a gross-margin step-down, the $10.87 EPS estimate is likely too high. Re-run the math on flat revenue and compressed margins and the forward multiple is meaningfully higher than 17x, potentially into the mid-20s or above on a more realistic EPS. The valuation conclusion that makes Oracle "cheap for the growth" depends on a consensus number that the company's own guidance undercuts.

Peer comparison cuts both ways but does not rescue the bull case. Oracle is cheaper than the peer median, but those peers (Palantir, Synopsys, Cadence) are asset-light software businesses with no comparable leverage or single-customer concentration. The correct comps for the OCI business are the hyperscalers, and against them Oracle is the most leveraged, the most concentrated, and the only one with negative free cash flow. A discount is warranted; the question is whether the current discount is large enough, and on a corrected (flat-revenue, margin-compressed) earnings base, it is not.

The stock has corrected roughly 47% from its $345.72 high to $184. We do not treat prior appreciation as a valuation input, and a decline alone is not a thesis. But this is a >30% drawdown, and the burden is on identifying what the market is wrong about. Our read is that the sellers are pricing the gap between a spectacular backlog and a flat near-term revenue guide funded by negative free cash flow and dilution. That is a rational repricing, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Oracle's competitive position in AI infrastructure is improving in the narrow sense that it is winning large GPU contracts in a supply-constrained market. The bullish industry read — that GPU scarcity means any capacity OpenAI cannot absorb gets re-rented at little incremental cost — has merit while demand outstrips supply. Oracle's genuine differentiators are durable: the multicloud Autonomous Database strategy (running on rival clouds), Exadata engineered systems, and a captive enterprise migration base that is higher-margin and stickier than commodity GPU rental.

The bearish competitive read is structural. Oracle is competing for commodity GPU capacity against AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud, all of which have vastly larger internal cash-flow cushions, lower funding costs, and more diversified customer bases. Oracle is taking on the most single-customer concentration (OpenAI via Stargate) and is the only one of the group funding the build with negative free cash flow and new debt at 6%-plus. In a downturn or a GPU-pricing reset, Oracle has the least financial flexibility. Its competitive moat in databases and applications remains intact and valuable; its competitive position in commodity AI infrastructure is a higher-beta, lower-margin extension of a strong franchise into a capital-intensive arena where balance-sheet strength is the deciding variable, and there Oracle ranks last.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Backlog convertibility and customer concentration — the entire thesis.** RPO is driven by "certain significant cloud contracts," with OpenAI the dominant counterparty. Only ~12% converts in the next 12 months. The bull case depends on OpenAI and a handful of AI customers paying multi-year contracts. In April 2026, reports that OpenAI missed internal revenue/user targets triggered an AI selloff and pushed Oracle's 5-year CDS to two-week highs. This is single-customer credit risk presented as backlog.

2. **Margin compression.** GPU/AI cloud gross margins are structurally far below legacy software margins. The risk is that Oracle trades a high-margin software franchise for a low-margin, capital-intensive infrastructure business at scale.

3. **Leverage, liquidity, and negative FCF.** Funding ~$70B/year capex against negative FCF requires continuous debt and equity access. Rising new-issuance rates (6%+ on long bonds) and the planned $40B FY27 raise create refinancing and dilution risk. The off-balance-sheet leases magnify the obligation.

4. **Securities class action (new, February 2026).** A putative class action filed in the District of Delaware (2/3/2026) names Oracle, Chairman/CTO Ellison, both co-CEOs, two other executives, and a director, alleging false or misleading statements about the OCI business — filed the same quarter as the company's largest-ever capital raises.

5. **Dilution.** The $5B mandatory convertible preferred converts to common by January 2029, the $20B ATM is available, and equity-funded FY27 raises will dilute common holders. Common ranks junior to the new preferred on dividends and liquidation.

6. **Execution and supply.** Reliance on Nvidia GPUs, third-party hardware, and power availability. The CFO hire from Schneider Electric underscores that electricity, not just chips, is now a binding constraint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced and is not confirming an imminent collapse. Near-term ATM IV is 56.7% against 1-year historical volatility of 61.7%, so implied vol is slightly cheap (-5.0 points). Put/call skew is near-zero (-0.1%), and the term structure is flat (near 56.7% ≈ far 56.0%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term event. The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.35 from 0.79 over the past week as the stock fell 13.8%, and ATM IV collapsed from 145.9% to 56.7% as the Q4 earnings event passed. There is modest unusual put activity (June-26 $165 puts at 9.4x OI, $175 puts at 4.1x), consistent with hedging into the post-earnings drift, but the longer-dated -4.4% six-month put skew is moderate rather than extreme. The binary earnings catalyst has resolved, and the market is not pricing acute near-term downside. For a short thesis, this absence of confirmation is a caution flag, not an endorsement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** GPU demand outstrips global supply; the backlog is real and contracted; Oracle's utilization and waitlist mean even an OpenAI shortfall can be re-rented. As the 5.5 GW of capacity comes online in FY28–29, revenue inflects sharply (34% of RPO converting in FY28–29), operating leverage returns, and the leverage normalizes against a much larger cash-generating base. The captive database/ERP migration funnel and multicloud Autonomous Database provide a higher-margin growth layer beneath the commodity GPU business. On this path, the stock re-rates toward the $245 consensus median and beyond, with the high analyst target at $400.

**Bear case.** FY27 revenue is flat per management's own guide while gross margins step down and interest and depreciation rise, so FY27 EPS comes in materially below the $10.87 consensus. The September Q1 FY27 print triggers negative estimate revisions. Backlog conversion proves slower or lower-margin than hoped; OpenAI credit concerns resurface; the $40B FY27 raise dilutes common and/or pressures the credit. Free cash flow stays negative through FY27. The stock de-rates toward the $155 low analyst target as the market marks down the gap between backlog optics and cash reality. On a flat-revenue, margin-compressed earnings base, the "17x forward" support disappears.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The most identifiable dated catalyst is the Q1 FY27 earnings report in September 2026, where reported revenue growth is likely to decelerate toward the guided flat full-year trajectory while gross margins step down. If consensus FY27 EPS proves stale relative to flat revenue and margin compression, the print could drive negative estimate revisions that the current price has not absorbed. This is a real, dated, asymmetric setup and strengthens the AVOID.

**Timing window.** The catalyst resolves in September 2026, roughly three months out — within a reasonable short horizon, though not imminent.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is only 2.0% of float with 1.6 days to cover. The short is uncrowded, which means little squeeze risk but also means sophisticated investors have not built a conviction short here, consistent with the binary, leveraged nature of the name where downside is large but so is the tail upside on backlog conversion.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming the bearish thesis. Skew is balanced, IV is slightly cheap versus realized vol, and the term structure is flat. The post-earnings IV collapse indicates the market views the immediate binary as resolved. This is the principal argument against an active short: the options market is neutral, not bearish, despite the weak forward setup.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** If the September print confirms the flat-revenue, margin-step-down trajectory and EPS estimates are cut, downside is toward the $155 low analyst target (roughly -16% from $184). Upside risk is substantial: a strong utilization update, a reassuring OpenAI ramp datapoint, accelerated backlog conversion commentary, or a broad AI-infrastructure rally could push the stock back toward $245 (+33%) or beyond. The reward:risk on an active short is therefore worse than 1:1 against the position, well below the 2:1 threshold required.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The forward earnings setup is genuinely weak and the September catalyst is real, which is why we rate the equity AVOID rather than HOLD. But the mechanics do not support an active short: the asymmetry is unfavorable (large squeeze/re-rate upside on any positive backlog datapoint versus moderate downside), the options market is not confirming, and the dominant catalyst — backlog conversion — could break either direction. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short. The corrected revenue picture tips the fundamental calculus more bearish, but it does not flip the short mechanics, because the tail upside on the binary remains too large relative to the contained downside to the low target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria.** We would reconsider a constructive stance on any of the following:
- A valuation reset toward the $130–150 range (roughly 14x–18x on a more conservative, revenue-guide-consistent FY27 EPS in the $8.50–9.00 area), restoring genuine margin of safety against the leverage and concentration.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow, or a credible, dated path to FCF breakeven that does not depend on further equity issuance.
- Hard evidence of backlog converting to cash — accelerating reported revenue toward the high-teens with the gross-margin step-down stabilizing rather than deepening — that reconciles the consensus EPS with management's revenue guide.
- Resolution or material de-risking of the OpenAI single-customer concentration (contract diversification, demonstrated payment capacity, or backlog spread across multiple credit-worthy counterparties).

**Timeline.** We expect the funding/margin/concentration overhangs to persist through at least FY27 (ending May 2027), given the ~$70B capex program, ~$40B planned raise, and back-end-loaded FY28–29 revenue recognition. The September 2026 Q1 FY27 print is the next natural reassessment point and the most likely near-term source of negative estimate revisions.

**What would make us more negative.** A guidance cut on FY27 revenue or capex efficiency; a credit-rating outlook change; renewed OpenAI funding/payment concerns; or a dilutive equity raise priced at a discount.

## Conclusion

Oracle has built a genuine position in AI infrastructure and an extraordinary backlog, but it has done so by levering its balance sheet into the statistical distress zone, turning free cash flow deeply negative, and concentrating its forward revenue on a handful of AI customers led by OpenAI. The bull case is entirely a backlog-conversion-and-timing story whose payoff is back-loaded into FY28–29. The near-term reality, per management's own guidance, is flat FY27 revenue with a gross-margin step-down, which sits awkwardly against the $10.87 consensus EPS implying ~35% growth and the "17x forward" valuation that depends on it. When the leases and net debt are counted, the headline cheapness on P/E dissolves into a full economic enterprise value near $893B on an increasingly low-margin earnings stream.

The score trajectory has correctly tracked deterioration in Financial Health (6.0 to 3.5), Management Quality (7.0 to 4.0, reflecting the unproven co-CEO duo, recently hired CFO, halted buybacks, and the securities class action), and Risk Factors (5.5 to 3.0). The new data — flat FY27 revenue guide, FY27 capex to $70B, the $40B planned raise, and the 8% post-earnings decline — confirms rather than reverses that trend. We rate ORCL **AVOID**. The stock is not an active short given uncrowded short interest, neutral options positioning, and large tail upside on any positive backlog datapoint, but it is a name to stay away from until valuation resets or backlog demonstrably converts to cash without further dilution.
