**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cognex is the cleanest publicly traded expression of the industrial machine-vision recovery, and the data confirms the cyclical upturn has arrived. Q1 CY2026 revenue rose 24% YoY to $268.4M (+21% constant-currency), gross margin recovered to 71%, and operating margin doubled to 22% from the 12.6% full-year 2024 trough. The earnings momentum is corroborated by consensus revisions: current-quarter EPS estimates have moved +35.7% over 90 days and current-year EPS +17.6%, while the company has beaten every quarter for the last four (Q1 by 34%). This profile — revenue accelerating from a depressed base with margins expanding — warrants valuing the stock on forward and normalized earnings rather than trailing P/E.

The balance sheet is a genuine asset in this debate. Cognex carries no long-term debt (D/E of 0.35 is entirely operating leases), holds roughly $622M of cash and investments, generates a ~24% FCF margin on a capex-light contract-manufactured model, and the board authorized a fresh $500M buyback in February 2026 on top of repurchasing $99M of stock in Q1 at roughly $42 per share. The new management team is pruning the portfolio inherited from the Moritex era: divesting the Japan trading business, writing down legacy products, and targeting $35-40M of additional annualized opex cuts by end-2026.

The case is not without offsets, which is why the score sits in the low-6s rather than higher. The stock has more than doubled from its 52-week low of $29.22, the trailing multiple optically reads ~76x, a new CEO is executing an unproven structural reset, and the 10-K explicitly flags AI/open-source commoditization as a threat to the proprietary software moat. Insiders are not buying on the open market. Weighing the confirmed recovery and rising estimates against these, I move to a BUY: the forward setup is cheap relative to the trajectory of estimates, the company sits in the only sector where stock selection has historically added alpha, and the dominant near-term signal is the revision velocity rather than the leadership transition.

## Company Overview

Cognex, founded in 1981 with roughly 2,745 employees, is a global leader in industrial machine vision — systems combining cameras, imaging hardware, and software/AI to locate, identify, inspect, and measure items on manufacturing lines and in distribution centers. The product portfolio spans deep-learning vision systems (In-Sight), vision software (VisionPro), image-based barcode readers (DataMan), and, since the 2023 Moritex acquisition, optical components (lenses and lighting). Services are under 10% of revenue.

End markets (about 85% of 2025 revenue) are logistics, packaging, consumer electronics, and automotive, with semiconductor and "other" rounding out the mix. The business is heavily international, with 67% of revenue earned outside the U.S. (Americas 41%, Europe 25%, Greater China 16%, Other Asia 18% in 2025). Cognex is one of the top machine-vision pure-plays globally, with Japan's Keyence as its chief peer.

Strategy under new CEO Matthew Moschner (appointed June 2025) rests on three pillars: lead in AI for industrial vision, deliver best-in-class customer experience, and double the customer base within five years via a salesforce transformation aimed at less-sophisticated customers who have historically been underserved by complex vision tools.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of Cognex's financials is cyclicality. Revenue and margins swing with end-market capex cycles, most visibly in the 2022-2023 logistics "time out" that drove a ~17% revenue decline:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,006 | 838 | 915 | 994 |
| Gross margin | 72% | 72% | 68% | 67% |
| Operating margin | 24.5% | 15.6% | 12.6% | 16.3% |
| Net income ($M) | 216 | 113 | 106 | 114 |
| Op. cash flow ($M) | — | — | 149 | 246 |

2025 revenue rose 9% to $994M, led by logistics (large e-commerce customers) and consumer electronics, with automotive weak. Operating margin recovered to 16.3%. Reported net income was flat at $114M despite higher operating income, but that flat line is misleading: a $33.2M non-cash discrete tax charge from the OBBBA (a GILTI/NCTI deferred-tax true-up) pushed the effective tax rate to 37% versus ~17% ex-discrete. The legislation is actually cash-positive, delivering an estimated $12-15M cash tax benefit via R&D expensing. Underlying earnings power in 2025 was therefore meaningfully better than the headline net income suggests. Gross margin was also pressured in 2025 by a $13M Q4 excess-and-obsolete inventory charge tied to the new leadership's portfolio review and the de-emphasis of legacy products.

Q1 CY2026 revenue was broad-based across consumer electronics, semiconductor, packaging, and logistics. EPS rose to $0.31 from $0.14 (GAAP basis per the 10-Q; the vendor-reported actual was $0.34). Greater China grew 36% constant-currency and Europe 23%; automotive remained the lone soft spot. Guidance for the following quarter came in around $290M midpoint, roughly 8% above consensus.

The balance sheet is debt-free and liquid. Altman Z-score is 3.81 (safe zone), there is no long-term debt, D/E of 0.35 is essentially all operating leases, and cash plus investments stood at roughly $622M in April 2026 against a current ratio near 3.6-3.8. The FCF margin was ~24% in 2025 (FCF $237M), with capex under 1% of revenue thanks to the contract-manufactured, asset-light model. FCF conversion runs well above net income, and the debt-free structure removes refinancing and rate-sensitivity risk that is weighing on more leveraged names in the current higher-for-longer environment.

Capital allocation is disciplined: $151M of buybacks and $55M of dividends in 2025, and diluted share count has drifted down from 172.6M in 2022 to 168.4M in Q1 2026. The Q1 repurchase price of ~$42 sits well below the current $66. The dividend is modest at about $0.085 per quarter (~0.5% yield), so this is a buyback-led return story rather than an income name.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a confirmed cyclical recovery layered onto a structurally growing category. Machine vision is expanding on labor scarcity, reshoring, quality/traceability regulation, and automation reaching under-penetrated markets — logistics in particular, which management calls its highest mid/long-term growth opportunity as the category moves from barcode reading toward AI-enabled dimensioning and damage detection. Consumer electronics is being driven by new form factors (wearables, foldables, AI hardware), and semiconductor by AI chip demand.

The estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal in the data set. Current-quarter EPS has been revised +35.7% over 30 days and +35.8% over 90 days, current-year EPS +17.6%, and next-year EPS +14.6%. The combination of revision velocity above +30% with revenue growth above 20% is the pattern that historically precedes further re-rating as consensus catches up. On the rising estimates, the forward multiple is far lower than the trailing figure suggests (detailed below), which is precisely the condition under which estimate-revision momentum is most reliable as a buy signal.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, with the magnitude accelerating from +3.7% (Q2 2025) to +11.9%, +20.0%, and +34.1% (Q1 2026). A company beating by progressively larger margins while estimates rise sharply is one where consensus is structurally lagging the operating reality.

The durability question — cyclical bounce versus structural margin expansion back toward the 24.5% 2022 peak — is genuine. The bull resolution depends on Moschner's cost program ($35-40M annualized opex cuts by end-2026) and salesforce transformation converting cyclical recovery into durable operating leverage. The current data points toward that resolution but does not yet prove it.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~76x is meaningless at a cyclical trough, where the trailing period embeds the 2024-2025 margin bottom and the $33M non-cash tax charge. The relevant lens is forward and normalized earnings. On current-year consensus EPS of $1.47, the stock trades at roughly 45x; on next-year consensus of $1.76, roughly 38x. Those are still full multiples in absolute terms, but they are falling fast as estimates rise, and they do not reflect normalized mid-cycle earnings power.

The normalized case is more compelling. At the 2022 revenue base of ~$1.0B and a recovery toward a 22-24% operating margin (Q1 already printed 22%), with a normalized ~17% tax rate, Cognex's mid-cycle earnings power is plausibly in the $2.00-$2.40 range — above current-year consensus. On normalized earnings of ~$2.20, the stock trades at roughly 30x, which for a debt-free, ~24% FCF-margin franchise in a structurally growing category with 20%+ current revenue growth is defensible rather than stretched. The PEG on next-year EPS growth (consensus implies ~20% growth) is roughly 1.9 on the 38x forward multiple, which is full but not egregious for the quality and the recovery.

Peer comparison is muddied by the heterogeneity of the Electronic Equipment & Instruments group: the peer-median P/E of 115x (Novanta 115x, Mirion 168x) reflects names with their own depressed-earnings dynamics, while Vontier at 10x is a different business profile. The median is not a usable anchor; Cognex's forward multiple sits below the higher-quality precision-instrument peers and above the value names, consistent with its margin profile and growth.

The analyst price-target band — low $61, mean $76.25, median $75.50, high $94 — places the stock at the bottom of the range, roughly 13% below the mean target. On a recovery stock with rising estimates and a reasonable forward multiple, consensus targets function as floors that revise upward as estimates climb; the mean target was likely set on lower estimates than the current $1.47/$1.76 figures and is stale. I score valuation a 5.0: the absolute multiple is full and there is no margin of safety on trailing earnings, but the normalized and forward picture, combined with the revision trajectory, removes the value-trap concern and supports the BUY rather than blocking it. I am deliberately not awarding a high valuation score, because a cheap-on-low-multiple framing has historically been an unreliable signal, and here the cheapness is conditional on the normalization thesis playing out.

## Competitive Landscape

Cognex's moat rests on patents, a unified software ecosystem (the OneVision cloud platform launched in 2025), brand, and entrenched relationships with the most sophisticated manufacturers. The category is structurally growing, and Cognex is a top global pure-play alongside Keyence. The Q1 breadth — strength across consumer electronics, semiconductor, packaging, and logistics simultaneously, with 36% constant-currency growth in Greater China — suggests share gains rather than a single-end-market bounce.

The trajectory is the concern that caps this dimension at 6.0. The 10-K is candid that the market is fragmented and competitive, and it flags a specific structural threat: open-source AI vision tools and low-cost solutions from large technology companies that could lower entry barriers and pressure pricing. This is a genuine commoditization risk for a company whose value proposition is proprietary vision software, since the same AI wave that Cognex champions could erode its software moat. The defense is the OneVision ecosystem and the "make machine vision easy" push to capture the long tail of less-technical customers before competitors do, but that strategy is unproven and is precisely what the salesforce transformation is meant to deliver. The chronic five-year shareholder underperformance (indexed TSR of 46 versus a peer group at 125) is evidence the competitive position has not been translating into shareholder value over the prior cycle. I score competitive position a 6.0: a strong incumbent with structural tailwinds, but a moat under real, identifiable pressure and a transformation thesis still to be validated.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter most are company-specific and partly priced:

1. **Cyclicality and customer concentration.** Revenue is hostage to capex cycles in logistics, consumer electronics, and automotive, and the 10-K notes that at times a single customer has represented a material portion of revenue. The 2022-2023 logistics downturn drove a ~17% revenue drop. Lumpy large orders create forecasting difficulty and the excess-inventory risk that surfaced in the Q4 2025 $13M charge.

2. **AI/open-source commoditization.** Explicitly flagged in the 10-K as a structural threat that could lower entry barriers and pressure pricing. This is the most important non-cyclical risk and is not fully priced.

3. **Transformation execution.** The salesforce overhaul and $35-40M cost program carry execution risk; management warns disruptions could impair the customer experience and fail to deliver promised savings.

4. **China/geopolitical/tariff exposure.** 16% of revenue from Greater China, in-house optical manufacturing in China and Vietnam, contract manufacturing concentrated in Indonesia and Malaysia, and chip dependence on Taiwan. A single primary contract manufacturer in Indonesia is a concentration point, the same type of facility where a 2022 fire destroyed inventory.

5. **Leadership transition.** A new CEO (June 2025) and the former CEO's board exit (March 2026), alongside a retitled CTO and a senior global-sales executive selling his entire common-stock position, indicate a management reshuffle under the new leadership. The 2025 10-K elevated CEO-succession language and added a standalone "activist shareholder" risk factor, suggesting activist pressure may have catalyzed the change.

6. **Auditor change** concurrent with the leadership change (Nov 2025 8-K plus Feb 2026 8-K/A). There is no indication of a disagreement or restatement, and Q1 controls were certified effective, so this appears routine, but it warrants monitoring.

I score risk factors a 5.5. The cyclicality is permanently priced and not a novel risk; the AI commoditization threat and the unproven transformation are the genuinely under-priced idiosyncratic risks, partially offset by the debt-free balance sheet that gives the company ample room to absorb a stumble.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 51.1% against one-year historical volatility of 54.8% — implied vol is cheap relative to realized (spread -3.7%), sitting in the 15th percentile of the current chain. This is favorable entry timing: the options market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty despite the stock trading near its highs. The put/call skew is balanced (1.5%) at the front month, the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.12, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.84 to 0.41 over the past month — positioning has shifted less bearish. The term structure is in contango (near-term 51.1% below long-term 54.7%), a normal/complacent structure, with a slight put-skew build at the 3-month and 6-month tenors consistent with hedging around the next earnings cycle. The reported 235% ATM IV in the snapshot delta is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float with 2.0 days to cover, so there is neither a squeeze setup nor a crowded-short warning. The options market is not contradicting the bullish fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The cyclical recovery is confirmed and accelerating, estimate revisions signal consensus is systematically too low, and the four-quarter beat streak with accelerating magnitude validates forward estimates. Moschner's cost program and salesforce transformation convert the cyclical bounce into durable operating leverage, pushing margins back toward the 24.5% 2022 peak and normalized EPS toward $2.20+. The debt-free balance sheet funds aggressive buybacks and potential M&A larger than Moritex. On normalized earnings of ~$2.20 at a 35-40x multiple appropriate for a high-quality, FCF-rich grower, the stock reaches $85-90. Bull case ~$94 (the high analyst target), roughly +45% from current.

**Bear Case.** The Q1 strength proves to be a cyclical peak rather than the start of durable expansion, automotive weakness spreads, and the AI/open-source threat begins to compress pricing and the software moat. The transformation disrupts the customer experience without delivering the promised savings, and the leadership churn signals deeper organizational stress. On a re-rating back toward the cyclical trough — call it a 25x multiple on $1.47 current-year EPS, or compression toward where the stock traded for most of 2025 — the stock falls to ~$37-40, roughly -40% from current. The full multiple offers no downside cushion on trailing numbers, so a growth disappointment de-rates sharply.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $80.** This applies roughly 36x to next-year consensus EPS of $1.76 ($63), blended with normalized mid-cycle earnings power of ~$2.10-$2.20 at ~38x as the cost program delivers and consensus continues to revise up. The midpoint of these approaches lands near $80, sitting between the bear ($37-40) and bull ($94) scenarios and roughly in line with the current mean analyst target of $76.25, which I expect to revise higher as estimates climb.

**Bull case target: $94** (+45% from current ~$65) — full normalization, margins toward the 24.5% peak, and continued multiple support from FCF and buybacks.

**Bear case target: $38** (-41% from current) — cyclical-peak re-rating plus AI/pricing pressure and transformation failure.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next two earnings reports (the quarter guided to ~$290M, then Q3) which will confirm or refute the margin durability, and to the end-2026 milestone for the $35-40M cost program.

**Upside/downside from current (~$65):** +23% to base case, +45% to bull case, -41% to bear case. The asymmetry is roughly even on the extremes but skews favorable to the base case given the confirmed revision momentum.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below 10% YoY on the next two prints, or operating margin falls back below 18%, signaling the Q1 recovery was a cyclical spike rather than durable.
2. Current-quarter or current-year EPS estimates reverse and decline more than 10% over a 90-day window, breaking the revision-momentum core of the thesis.
3. Management cuts or materially delays the $35-40M cost program, or further senior departures signal the transformation is failing.

## Conclusion

Cognex offers a debt-free, cash-rich, FCF-heavy machine-vision franchise in a confirmed and accelerating cyclical recovery, with the strongest forward signal in the data being the estimate-revision velocity, the four consecutive beats of accelerating magnitude, and the Q1 revenue and margin acceleration. The setup matches the profile where rising estimates against a forward multiple far below the misleading trailing figure precede further re-rating. As an Information Technology name it sits in the one sector where security selection has reliably added value.

The reasons this is a measured rather than a maximum-conviction BUY are concrete: the absolute forward multiple (~38x next-year) is full and offers no trailing-earnings cushion, a new CEO is executing an unproven structural reset against a backdrop of chronic five-year shareholder underperformance and management churn, insiders are not buying on the open market, and the 10-K's AI/open-source commoditization threat is a genuine, under-priced risk to the software moat. On balance, the confirmed earnings recovery and the revision velocity outweigh the leadership-transition and valuation concerns over a 12-month horizon. I rate Cognex a **BUY** with a base-case target of $80 (+23%), a bull case of $94, and a bear case of $38, and I would size the position with discipline given the full multiple and the unproven transformation.
