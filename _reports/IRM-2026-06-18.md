**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Iron Mountain has converted itself from a slow-decline physical-records REIT into a double-digit grower, and the latest results confirm the transition is accelerating. Q1 2026 revenue grew 21.6% (17.2% organic), Adjusted EBITDA rose 22.1% to $707.9M, and Normalized FFO grew 29% to $294.5M. The engine is the data center segment, where revenue jumped 47.1% to $254.7M at a 52.1% EBITDA margin against 488 MW leased (97% utilized) and an 852 MW pipeline under construction. The legacy Records & Information Management (RIM) franchise, ~77% of revenue, continues to deliver mid-single-digit organic growth through pricing rather than volume, with a 44.7% segment margin. The completion of Project Matterhorn at the end of 2025 removes roughly $196M of annual transformation expense, a mechanical tailwind to 2026 reported FFO independent of underlying operations.

The constraint on the equity story is financing, not demand. Iron Mountain carries ~$16.9B of debt, negative stockholders' equity of -$1.22B, and generates deeply negative free cash flow because ~$2.2B per year of growth capex plus a ~$920M dividend exceed its $1.34B of operating cash flow. The gap is plugged with new debt issuance. The Altman Z-Score of 0.38 overstates the danger; it is an artifact of REIT distribution economics and real-estate depreciation, not impending insolvency, and the relevant covenant metric (net lease-adjusted leverage of 4.8x against a 7.0x cap, 2.5x fixed-charge coverage) shows comfortable headroom. The model only works if data center returns and continued RIM pricing power outrun a 5.6% weighted-average cost of debt in an environment where the year-end rate path is two-sided and the long end faces structural upward pressure.

We rate IRM a HOLD (growth hold). The fundamental transition is genuine and the data center thesis sits squarely within a confirmed AI-infrastructure demand wave, which supports the growth and competitive scores. The stock has already re-rated to ~53x forward Normalized FFO and trades at $125 versus a $140 median analyst target, leaving limited asymmetry after a 22% twelve-month gain. With valuation full, free cash flow negative, and the funding model dependent on the rate environment cooperating, the risk/reward is balanced. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$100s or evidence that data center stabilized NOI is covering the incremental cost of capital.

## Company Overview

Iron Mountain is a global information-management REIT founded in 1951, serving over 240,000 customers across 61 countries, including roughly 95% of the Fortune 1000, with no single customer exceeding 3% of revenue. The business reports in three units:

- **Global RIM (Records & Information Management):** ~77% of revenue ($5.29B FY25). Physical records storage (740M+ cubic feet), data management, secure shredding, and digital solutions (DXP 2.0, launched October 2025). Highly recurring with strong retention and a 44.7% segment EBITDA margin. Organic growth of +5.7% is driven by pricing ("revenue management"), with stored volumes "relatively stable."
- **Global Data Center:** The growth engine — $803M FY25 revenue, 51.8% EBITDA margin (up 620bps), 488 MW leased at 97% utilization, 852 MW under construction/development, 1,340 MW total potential capacity, 31 centers across 21 markets, a 10.3-year weighted-average lease expiration, and five of the largest hyperscalers as customers.
- **Corporate & Other (ALM + Fine Arts):** Asset Lifecycle Management (IT decommissioning, data erasure, resale) is growing rapidly through tuck-in acquisitions, with downstream component sales concentrated in China.

The competitive position rests on an entrenched records franchise with high switching costs, a trusted chain-of-custody brand, global scale, and a substantial cross-sell opportunity (only ~5% of customers buy from more than one unit). The data center arm differentiates on compliance/security pedigree and 100% clean-energy matching ("Green Power Pass") and benefits from cross-sell into the enterprise base.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is accelerating across both windows.** FY25 revenue was $6.90B (+12.2%, organic +10.2%), with storage rental of $4.05B (+10.1%) and service revenue of $2.85B (+15.5%). Q1 2026 data center storage rental was up 46%.

**Profitability is expanding on a normalized basis.** Adjusted EBITDA was $2.57B FY25 (+15.1%, 37.3% margin), with the Q1 2026 figure at a 36.6% margin. Normalized FFO was $1.08B FY25. Adjusted EPS was $2.12 FY25 (vs. $1.77) and $0.60 in Q1 2026 (vs. $0.43).

**GAAP earnings are a poor gauge here.** Reported net income was only $152.3M FY25 (down from $183.7M), squeezed by $829M of interest expense and a $105.6M FX loss on intercompany balances. Q1 2026 net income of $149.0M was up roughly 8x year-over-year (vs. $16.2M), but that comparison is flattered by a $24.5M FX gain and the absence of Matterhorn costs ($54.7M in Q1 2025). The trailing GAAP EPS of $0.49 produces a misleading 136x P/E. The relevant metrics are Adjusted EBITDA, FFO, and AFFO. On consensus, current-year EPS is $2.43 and next-year $2.67, putting the stock at roughly 52x and 47x forward earnings — still elevated, but far from the trailing figure.

**The balance sheet is the central debate.** Cash is $158.5M (year-end FY25), the revolver was drawn to $1.285B (up from $16.2B at year-end), and stockholders' equity is negative and widening. The covenant lens is more relevant than the Z-Score: net lease-adjusted leverage of 4.8–4.9x against a 7.0x maximum, fixed-charge coverage of 2.5x against a 1.5x minimum, and no rating-based covenant triggers. As a REIT, Iron Mountain distributes most cash flow rather than retaining equity, and depreciation of long-lived real estate exceeds economic decline, which explains the current ratio of 0.74. The weighted-average interest rate is 5.6%, with ~$1.0B of floating-rate exposure hedged via swaps.

**Free cash flow is the real tension.** Operating cash flow is healthy and growing ($1.34B FY25; $338.6M Q1 2026), but capex of ~$2.27B FY25 ($2.07B growth, $1.75B data center) plus dividends of $919.4M substantially exceed it. The gap is funded by debt issuance: €1.2B of 4.75% Euro Notes due 2034 (Sept 2025), a Term Loan B upsize (Nov 2025), and a $433M Virginia 3 data center mortgage refinance (Jan 2026). The dividend was raised to $0.864/quarter (~$3.46 annualized, ~2.7% yield) in November 2025. The payout is covered against Normalized FFO but not against GAAP net income — typical for a REIT, but a reminder that distributions and the buildout both lean on external capital.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs, each with different durability.

**Data center is the highest-conviction driver and the cleanest expression of a confirmed structural demand wave.** The 852 MW construction pipeline against 488 MW operating represents a near-tripling of capacity if leased, and with five major hyperscalers as customers and a 10.3-year WALE, the revenue is contracted and long-dated. This sits within a broad AI-infrastructure capex theme where hyperscaler power and capacity demand has been validated. The durability of data center demand is higher than a single-company story would suggest, which supports a higher growth score than a standalone records business would earn.

**RIM provides a durable, pricing-led base.** Organic growth comes almost entirely from revenue management, not volume, with stored cubic feet "relatively stable." This is sustainable as long as customers tolerate price increases on increasingly dormant archives, which they historically have, given the cost and risk of de-conversion. The risk is a long-term volume inflection as records become purely archival; that is the structural bear case, but it is a slow-burn rather than a near-term threat.

**Matterhorn completion is an underappreciated 2026 tailwind.** The annual transformation cost rolls off, flattering reported FFO and margin growth independent of operations. Investors should distinguish this one-time mechanical benefit from underlying operating leverage.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive over 90 days (current-year EPS +5.1%, next-year +4.4%) but flat over the last 30 days. The revision velocity does not reach the +30% threshold that would mark a strong, underappreciated transition; consensus has largely caught up. The earnings track record is solid (three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a 15.3% beat last quarter), supporting confidence in forward estimates without signaling acceleration the market is missing.

## Valuation Assessment

On any conventional measure, Iron Mountain is not cheap, and the metrics that flatter it (forward FFO) are still rich.

- **Trailing P/E of 136x** is meaningless given the GAAP distortion from FX and interest; ignore it.
- **Forward P/E of ~52x** (on $2.43 FY26 EPS) and **~47x** (on $2.67 FY27) remain premium multiples.
- **Forward Normalized FFO** is the cleanest REIT metric and trades well above the typical specialized-REIT range.
- **Dividend yield of 2.7%** is below the peer median of 5.8% and well below GLPI (6.8%) and EPR (6.3%), reflecting the market's pricing of IRM as a growth REIT rather than an income vehicle.

The peer set (GLPI 15.1x P/E, LAMR 28.1x, OUT 29.0x, EPR 18.3x, FCPT 22.4x; median 22.4x) is heterogeneous and not a clean comp — none of these peers carry a fast-growing data center arm. The appropriate comparison for the data center segment is the hyperscale-REIT cohort (Equinix, Digital Realty), which trades at elevated multiples on the same AI-capex re-rating. On that basis, a sum-of-the-parts argument supports a higher multiple than the legacy records peers, and this is the strongest part of the bull valuation case.

The asymmetry is limited. The stock trades at $125 versus a $140 median and $131.55 mean analyst target — roughly 5–12% to the median/mean and ~19% to the $149 high, against a $44 low that reflects a bearish leverage view. With the multiple already reflecting double-digit growth and the funding model running negative free cash flow into a higher-for-longer rate environment, the valuation does not offer the kind of clear mispricing that would justify a high score. A sector theme can be correct (AI data center demand is real) while an individual stock at ~50x forward earnings is fully priced for the growth it delivers. We do not treat the analyst target as a floor at this multiple; at ~50x forward earnings the targets and the price reflect the same growth optimism, vulnerable to any disappointment. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a full but not absurd multiple with the catalyst (data center ramp) largely already in the price.

## Competitive Landscape

Iron Mountain operates in two distinct competitive contexts.

In **records management**, it is the clear global leader with no comparably scaled rival, competing against fragmented regional players and customers' in-house/cloud alternatives. Switching costs are high (physical de-conversion is expensive and risky), retention is strong, and pricing power is demonstrated. This is a defensive, slow-decline market where Iron Mountain's position is improving in revenue terms even as physical volumes plateau.

In **data centers**, it is a newer entrant competing against far larger specialists (Equinix, Digital Realty) for power, land, connectivity, and hyperscaler contracts. Its edge is compliance/security pedigree, sustainability positioning, and cross-sell into a 240,000-customer enterprise base. The hyperscaler AI-capex wave is a powerful demand tailwind, but it also intensifies capital competition for power and land, and Iron Mountain's data center returns at scale are unproven relative to incumbents. The trajectory is improving — share is being gained in a structurally growing market — which is what the competitive score rewards, but the level of moat in data centers is lower than in records.

The cross-sell opportunity is a credible, underexploited lever. The ALM business adds a third leg with secular tailwinds (data sanitization, circular-economy IT) but carries client and China-geographic concentration that caps its quality.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Leverage and financing dependence.** The most important risk: a model requiring continuous external capital to fund both the REIT distribution and the data center buildout. A higher-for-longer rate path raises refinancing cost and could force a slower buildout or pressure the dividend-growth commitment. This is partly priced (the 2.7% yield and the $44 bear-case analyst target reflect leverage concern), but it is the dominant swing factor.
2. **Secular decline in physical-records activity.** Stored records are becoming more archival, pressuring retrieval/rotation service revenue against largely fixed costs. Volumes are stable for now; the long-term thesis depends on sustained pricing power.
3. **Data center execution.** Capex committed ahead of revenue, exposed to construction cost inflation, power availability, and the need to lease the 852 MW pipeline. Returns at scale are unproven versus specialists.
4. **ALM concentration.** Limited client base and China-concentrated component sales create trade-policy and macro exposure.
5. **FX volatility.** A large share of revenue is non-USD; intercompany balances generate large, lumpy GAAP swings (the $105.6M FY25 loss, the $24.5M Q1 2026 gain).
6. **AI is double-edged** — a data center demand tailwind, but also a regulatory/liability and competitive risk for digital solutions.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contrarian warning. Near-term ATM IV of 32.0% is essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 31.9% (a 0.1% spread — fairly priced, 23rd percentile within the current chain). Put/call skew is balanced at 2.4%, the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.24, and the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.08, indicating no meaningful hedging demand or bearish positioning ahead of a catalyst. The term structure is in normal contango (32.0% near-term vs. 36.9% six-month), consistent with a low-stress setup. The reported jump in one snapshot's ATM IV to 278% is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. The implied ±3.4% near-term move is modest. The options market is not pricing the leverage concern that the fundamental analysis flags, a mild divergence: sentiment is sanguine while the financing model carries real tail risk if credit conditions tighten.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($149+)

The data center arm scales from 488 MW to 1,000+ MW over the next several years at 50%+ EBITDA margins, contracted to hyperscalers on 10-year leases, while RIM sustains mid-single-digit pricing-led growth and Matterhorn cost roll-off lifts margins. Normalized FFO compounds at low-double-digit rates, the dividend grows in step, and the market continues to value IRM on the data center REIT cohort's elevated multiple rather than the legacy records peer median. An easing of the rate environment lowers the cost of the debt-funded buildout, widening returns. The stock reaches the $149 analyst high and beyond as FFO compounds into the multiple.

### Bear Case ($90–105)

A higher-for-longer rate path raises refinancing cost on the debt book and forces a choice between slowing the buildout (cutting growth) and stretching the balance sheet further. Negative free cash flow persists; the market re-rates the stock toward the income-REIT yield framework, demanding a higher dividend yield that implies a lower price. RIM volumes inflect down as archives go fully dormant, and data center returns at scale disappoint relative to specialists. The $44 low analyst target reflects the extreme version of this — a leverage-driven derating — but a more probable bear outcome is a compression toward $90–105 as the growth multiple deflates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The fundamental transition is genuine and the data center thesis is durable, but the stock offers limited asymmetry after a strong run, and the negative-FCF/debt-funded model caps the risk/reward in the current rate regime.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$100s (roughly 15%+ from current levels) that restores asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals — a lower price on intact data center leasing and RIM pricing is a stronger buy case.
- Evidence that data center stabilized NOI is covering the incremental cost of capital — specifically, leasing of a meaningful share of the 852 MW pipeline at contracted rates that demonstrate returns above the 5.6% debt cost.
- A sustained easing of the long-end rate path that lowers refinancing cost on the buildout.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net lease-adjusted leverage rising toward 6.0x or fixed-charge coverage falling toward 2.0x, signaling the funding model is straining.
- A cut or pause to the dividend-growth commitment, which would confirm capital constraint.
- RIM organic storage growth turning negative (a volume inflection, not just slowing pricing), which would undermine the durable base.
- A data center leasing slowdown or a write-down on pipeline capacity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July), with attention to data center leasing progress, leverage, and FFO guidance. The rate path and the broader credit environment are the key macro inputs to monitor between reports.

## Conclusion

Iron Mountain has earned its re-rating. The transition from a slow-decline records business to a double-digit grower led by a 50%+-margin data center arm is real, confirmed by the data center revenue growth, FFO growth, and a long-dated contracted hyperscaler backlog. The Matterhorn cost roll-off provides a clean 2026 tailwind, and the Altman Z-Score distress signal is a false positive driven by REIT economics rather than insolvency risk.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the market already knows most of this. At ~52x forward earnings, a 2.7% yield below the peer median, and a price below the median analyst target, the growth is priced and the asymmetry is thin. The genuine constraint — negative free cash flow funded by a debt book that must be serviced and refinanced at 5.6% into a higher-for-longer rate environment — is the swing factor that the sanguine options market is not pricing. The thesis works if data center returns and RIM pricing outrun the cost of capital; it breaks if rates stay elevated and the buildout's funding strains the balance sheet. We would buy the data center growth story at a better entry (low-$100s) or on confirmation that pipeline leasing is covering the incremental cost of capital. At today's price, the risk/reward is balanced.
