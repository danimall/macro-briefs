## Executive Summary

New Jersey Resources is a diversified energy services holding company whose earnings are anchored by its regulated gas distribution utility (NJNG), which serves ~592,500 customers across central and southern New Jersey and contributed 68% of Q1 FY2026 net income. Supplemental earnings come from commercial solar development (CEV), natural gas marketing/trading (ES), and gas storage/transportation infrastructure (S&T). FY2025 was the company's strongest year on record ($336M net income, $3.33 EPS), driven by a $157M base rate increase approved in November 2024. Q1 FY2026 underlying performance was strong despite a headline 6.7% net income decline attributable to the prior-year $54.9M one-time gain from the residential solar portfolio sale.

The stock has rallied 29% from its November 2025 low of $43.46 to $56.23, now sitting 2.5% below its 52-week high of $57.70. At 17.3x trailing P/E, NJR trades at a 16% discount to the gas utility peer median of 20.5x. Estimate revisions are positive: current-year EPS has been revised up 7.7% over 90 days to $3.37. However, the OBBBA acceleration of ITC phase-outs creates a medium-term headwind for CEV's growth trajectory, options markets are pricing anomalous near-term stress (49.5% implied volatility vs. 16.8% historical), and the company's leverage has risen meaningfully ($3.8B total debt, D/E ~1.4x) during an aggressive capital spending cycle. The 3.4% dividend yield and regulated earnings base support holding for income, but the risk/reward at current levels does not justify a new position.

## Company Overview

NJR operates through four segments. **NJNG** (69% of assets) is the regulated backbone — a BPU-approved cost-of-service gas utility with a 9.6% authorized ROE and 7.08% weighted average cost of capital. **Clean Energy Ventures** (17% of assets) owns 489 MW of commercial solar across the Northeast, generating revenue through PPAs, renewable energy certificates, and federal ITCs. **Energy Services** (2% of assets but 17% of Q1 earnings) is a wholesale/retail natural gas trading operation that captures price differentials across geographies and time periods. **Storage & Transportation** (13% of assets) includes the 32.2M Dth Leaf River salt dome storage facility, the Adelphia Gateway interstate pipeline in southeastern Pennsylvania, and a 50% interest in Steckman Ridge storage.

The company operates on a September 30 fiscal year. Earnings are heavily seasonal, with Q1 (Oct–Dec) and Q2 (Jan–Mar) generating the majority of annual income due to winter heating demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Trajectory:** Operating income grew at an ~8% CAGR from $406M (FY2022) to $509M (FY2025). Net income reached a record $336M in FY2025, up 16% YoY, primarily driven by the November 2024 base rate increase. Q1 FY2026 net income was $122.5M ($1.21 diluted EPS), down from $131.3M ($1.31 EPS) in the prior year, but the entire decline is attributable to the non-recurring $54.9M residential solar portfolio sale gain in Q1 FY2025. Stripping that out, NJNG net income grew 25% to $83.8M, ES more than doubled to $20.6M from a colder-than-normal December (9.7% colder than 30-year average), and S&T improved 30% to $7.4M following the Adelphia rate case settlement.

**Capital Structure & Leverage:** Total debt stands at approximately $3.8B ($3.3B long-term, ~$0.5B short-term borrowings on revolving facilities). Stockholders' equity is $2.4B, producing a D/E ratio of ~1.6x. Long-term debt has grown 32% over four years ($2.5B to $3.3B). The company maintains essentially zero cash ($591K at September 30, 2025), relying entirely on $825M in revolving credit facilities ($575M NJR + $250M NJNG). NJNG carries A1/A+ credit ratings from Moody's and Fitch on senior secured obligations, reflecting the strong regulated cash flow profile. NJR at the parent level is not rated.

**Capital Spending:** FY2025 CapEx was $660M. FY2026 guidance is $685–$820M (NJNG: $430–$480M, CEV: $210–$290M, S&T: $45–$60M). This is a substantial capital cycle for a company of this size, funded through a combination of internally generated cash, long-term debt issuance, sale-leaseback monetization of solar assets ($486M outstanding), and the dividend reinvestment plan (~$15M/year).

**Dividend:** $1.92 annualized ($0.48/quarter), up 6.7% from the prior year. At the current price, the yield is 3.4%, slightly above the peer median of 3.1%. The company has a 30+ year track record of consecutive dividend increases. Current-year EPS consensus of $3.37 implies a payout ratio of ~57%, which is well-covered for a utility.

**Earnings Beat Record:** Three beats in the last four quarters, with the only miss a modest $0.02 shortfall in Q4 FY2025 (a seasonally weak quarter where expectations are near zero). The Q1 FY2026 beat of $0.13 (+12.5%) and Q2 FY2025 beat of $0.13 (+185% on a near-zero base) suggest the company is conservatively guiding.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is well-supported by three factors: (1) the full-year annualization of the November 2024 base rate increase flowing through NJNG, (2) customer growth of 1.1% annually in the service territory, and (3) regulatory approvals for incremental revenue recovery ($3.3M IIP filing, $13.3M SAVEGREEN, $38.1M BGSS self-implementation). Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 7.7% over 90 days (from $3.13 to $3.37), and the current-quarter estimate has risen 13.6% in 90 days ($1.67 to $1.90). This is healthy, accelerating estimate momentum.

Medium-term growth faces a structural challenge: the OBBBA acceleration of the ITC phase-out. Solar projects must be placed in service by December 31, 2027, or begin construction before July 4, 2026, to qualify for ITCs. CEV's business model has depended on ITCs as a core revenue and financing mechanism. The accelerating sale-leaseback pace ($23.2M in Q1 FY2026, up 84% YoY) signals the company is racing to monetize existing projects before the window closes. Post-2027, CEV may shift from a growth contributor to a harvesting/maintenance operation. CEV contributed only 8% of Q1 FY2026 net income (excluding the prior-year one-time gain), so the earnings impact of this transition is manageable but reduces the diversification narrative.

Next-year EPS estimates are essentially flat at $3.44 (vs. $3.37 current year), with negligible 90-day revision (-0.3%). The market is pricing low-single-digit growth beyond the current rate case tailwind, which is appropriate for a mature gas utility.

NJNG's withdrawal of its IIP extension request, with plans to instead file a comprehensive base rate case, is the next major regulatory catalyst. If filed in FY2026 with resolution in FY2027, this could provide a step-up in authorized revenues, though timing, magnitude, and outcome are uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $56.23, NJR trades at 16.9x trailing EPS ($3.33) and 16.7x forward FY2026 consensus ($3.37). The gas utility peer group trades at a median 20.5x P/E, placing NJR at a discount of approximately 18%. This discount is partially explained by NJR's higher leverage, the non-recurrence of CEV's solar portfolio gain, and the OBBBA overhang on the clean energy segment. UGI trades at a steeper discount (13.7x) due to its international exposure and commodity sensitivity, while pure-play regulated peers OGS (20.5x) and SR (20.4x) command fuller multiples.

The analyst consensus median target is $60.50, with a range of $51.00–$63.00. At $56.23, this implies 7.6% upside to the median and 12.0% upside to the high target. Including the 3.4% dividend yield, total return potential to the median target is approximately 11% over 12 months.

The valuation discount to peers has compressed substantially since November 2025. The stock has already captured the re-rating from the Q1 FY2026 earnings beat and positive estimate revisions. Closing the remaining gap to peer multiples would require either a successful base rate case filing or a credible post-ITC growth strategy for CEV. Neither is in hand.

## Competitive Landscape

NJR's competitive position is stable with modest positive trajectory. NJNG's regulated franchise in central/southern New Jersey benefits from consistent population growth and constructive regulatory relationships with the BPU. Customer growth of 1.1% annually is above-average for a mature gas utility. The November 2024 rate case outcome was favorable, and subsequent regulatory approvals (IIP, SAVEGREEN, BGSS) demonstrate ongoing BPU support.

The Energy Services segment provides differentiation that most gas utility peers lack. ES acts as a natural countercyclical earnings buffer: colder, more volatile winters that pressure NJNG's margin recovery mechanism simultaneously benefit ES's trading margins. The Q1 FY2026 result (ES net income doubled to $20.6M) illustrates this dynamic.

Storage and transportation assets (Leaf River, Adelphia, Steckman Ridge) provide steady contracted cash flows with organic expansion potential. The Adelphia rate case settlement positions that asset for improved returns.

The primary competitive vulnerability is structural: New Jersey's 2024 Energy Master Plan and potential future electrification mandates could restrict natural gas hookups over the long term. This is a sector-wide risk, not NJR-specific, but it constrains the terminal growth assumption for NJNG.

## Risk Assessment

**ITC Phase-Out (Medium-term, High Impact):** CEV must navigate strict construction commencement deadlines (July 4, 2026) and placed-in-service requirements (December 31, 2027). Failure to meet these deadlines would strand ITC value on projects in development. The company states OBBBA had no material impact as of December 31, 2025, but explicitly warns about future impacts. CEV's relatively small earnings contribution (8% of Q1 FY2026) limits the financial impact, but the narrative value of the clean energy segment as a growth driver would diminish.

**Leverage & Interest Rate Risk:** The company operates at the edge of its liquidity structure, relying on revolving credit facilities for day-to-day cash management. The weighted average rate on NJR's revolver was 4.99% at December 31, 2025. Annual interest expense of ~$129M consumes a meaningful share of operating income.

**Capital Spending Execution:** The $685–$820M FY2026 CapEx program must be funded through a combination of debt, operating cash flow, and asset monetization during a period of rising costs and potentially tighter credit conditions. Significant project delays or cost overruns could strain the already-thin liquidity buffer.

**Weather Dependence:** A mild winter could materially reduce both NJNG throughput and ES trading margins. The CIP mechanism mitigates but does not eliminate NJNG weather risk, and ES has no comparable hedge.

**Environmental Liabilities:** $144–$200M estimated future expenditures for MGP remediation. Recoverable through the RAC mechanism, but subject to regulatory disallowance risk.

**Electrification Policy Risk:** Long-term structural risk that NJ climate policy could mandate electrification, restricting gas hookups. This would cap NJNG's customer growth trajectory over a 10–20 year horizon.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing anomalous near-term stress: 1-month ATM implied volatility of 49.5% against 16.8% historical volatility, a 32.7 percentage point spread. The term structure is in steep backwardation (49.5% at 1-month collapsing to 24.5% at 3-month and 27.1% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term event. With Q2 FY2026 earnings approaching (the company reports on a September fiscal year, so Q2 covers Jan–Mar and would typically report in late April/early May), this elevated near-term IV likely reflects earnings uncertainty or an idiosyncratic event. The implied move of ±10.2% over the nearest expiry (17 days) is extreme for a stock with 16.8% annualized historical volatility. If the stress is event-related and resolves benignly, IV crush could provide a favorable entry point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($63, +12%):** The upcoming base rate case filing produces another favorable outcome. Customer growth sustains at 1.1%+. CEV successfully navigates the ITC transition by beginning construction on remaining projects before July 2026, monetizing ITCs through accelerated sale-leasebacks. ES delivers above-plan results from continued weather volatility. The multiple re-rates toward the peer median of 20.5x on improved earnings visibility. At 19x $3.37 FY2026 EPS, the stock reaches ~$64.

**Bear Case ($47, -16%):** The ITC phase-out forces CEV to curtail growth spending, removing the clean energy growth narrative. A mild winter in Q2–Q3 FY2026 depresses NJNG and ES earnings. Rising interest rates increase refinancing costs on the debt load. The BPU becomes less accommodative in the next rate case. Electrification policy concerns weigh on the multiple. At 14x $3.37 EPS, the stock retraces to ~$47.

**Base Case ($59, +5%):** Regulated earnings continue to grow at low-single-digit rates from the annualizing rate increase and customer growth. CEV transitions to harvest mode post-2027 without major disruption. The company files a new base rate case in late FY2026 or early FY2027. The stock trades at 17.5x $3.37 FY2026 EPS, reaching ~$59. Including the 3.4% yield, total return of ~9% over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to $49–50 (14.5–14.8x forward EPS), restoring meaningful discount to peers with yield support above 3.8%
- Successful filing and favorable preliminary outcome on the next base rate case
- Benign resolution of the anomalous near-term options stress (IV normalization as a potential entry signal)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- BPU signals less constructive posture on rate recovery or issues adverse ruling on pending filings
- Leverage ratio (debt/total capital) exceeds 65% or credit rating downgrade at the NJNG level
- CEV write-downs on stranded solar projects unable to qualify for ITCs under OBBBA timelines

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 FY2026 earnings (expected late April/early May 2026). This will capture the full January–March heating season and provide the first full quarter with the IIP and SAVEGREEN increases in effect. The anomalous near-term options pricing should also resolve by then.

## Conclusion

NJR is a fundamentally sound gas utility with a strong regulated earnings base, constructive regulatory relationships, and a 30+ year dividend growth track record. The FY2025 base rate increase provided a meaningful earnings step-up, and near-term estimate momentum is positive. At 16.9x trailing earnings with a 3.4% yield, it trades at a justifiable discount to pure-play gas utility peers given its leverage profile and the OBBBA overhang on its clean energy segment.

The 29% rally from November 2025 lows has already captured much of the re-rating from the rate case benefits and positive Q1 FY2026 results. Remaining upside to the consensus median target ($60.50) of 7.6% plus the 3.4% yield provides adequate but unremarkable total return potential. The steep options backwardation pricing near-term stress introduces additional uncertainty that fundamentals alone do not fully explain. For existing holders, the income profile and regulated earnings stability justify maintaining the position. For new capital, the risk/reward at current levels favors waiting for a pullback to the $49–50 range or clarity on the upcoming base rate case filing.
