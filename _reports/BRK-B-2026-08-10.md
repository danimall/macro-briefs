## Executive Summary

Berkshire's Q2 2026 10-Q, filed August 10, is the most informative document on this name in a year, and it cuts two ways. The balance sheet is stronger than at any prior reporting date: shareholders' equity of $747,910M against total assets of $1,263,071M (59.2% equity/assets), total borrowings of $128,599M of which only roughly $45bn is recourse to the parent (~6% of equity), $359,230M of cash and Treasury bills, and float of $177.5bn carried at a negative cost. Capital is finally moving — $9.4bn for OxyChem (closed January 2), $6.8bn for Taylor Morrison (closed July 24), $11.6bn of net equity purchases and $4.76bn of repurchases, roughly $32bn of gross deployment in six months, funded partly by the first meaningful drawdown in the cash pile ($368,986M to $359,230M).

The operating picture is weaker than the headline. Net earnings to Berkshire of $35,773M in H1 more than doubled, but $11,512M of the $18,800M increase is the swing in after-tax investment gains and $3,760M is the non-recurrence of the 2025 Kraft Heinz impairment. Operating earnings of $24,329M (+17.0%) include a $2,165M swing from non-cash revaluation of yen-, euro- and sterling-denominated senior notes; excluding that line from both periods, operating earnings grew **6.1%**. Reported underwriting profit of $4,445M (+4.4%) rests on $1,467M of favorable prior-year development versus $240M a year earlier and the absence of the $1.1bn of Southern California wildfire losses booked in H1 2025; normalizing both, underlying underwriting profit fell roughly 42%. GEICO is the source: combined ratio 89.3% versus 81.7%, pre-tax underwriting down 39.7%, with bodily-injury frequency up 5-7% and severity up 10-12% while average premium per private-passenger-auto policy declined and underwriting expenses rose 28.3%. Free cash flow (OCF less capex) fell 7.0% to $11,022M as capex rose 16.3%.

At $529.76 the stock is 1.52x June-30 book value of $348.26 per Class B share, 25.4x the $20.89 current-year consensus and 24.6x next year's $21.57 (3.3% implied growth), above the $525.33 consensus mean and the $510 median, and at a 52-week high. The most concrete valuation marker in the filing is management's own: repurchases of $741M in May at an average $476.01 and $3,787M in June at $487.98, equal to 1.37x and 1.40x book, under a program that permits buying only below intrinsic value "conservatively determined." The stock now sits 8.6% above the June average. That does not make it a sell — book value compounded 4.72% in six months and the dry powder is worth more in a credit dislocation than any model captures — but it removes the margin of safety and, in all likelihood, the buyback bid. **HOLD**, with new capital waiting for $470-490.

## Company Overview

Berkshire is a holding company owning operating subsidiaries outright alongside a concentrated marketable-equity portfolio. Insurance supplies the funding: GEICO (roughly 11.6% U.S. private-passenger auto share per the 10-K's citation of A.M. Best 2024 data), the Primary Group (BHSI, RSUI, MedPro, USLI, GUARD and others, ~40% of U.S. business written non-admitted), and the Reinsurance Group (NICO, General Re, TransRe). Combined U.S. statutory surplus was approximately $333bn at year-end 2025, rated AA+ / A++. Float grew from ~$138bn at year-end 2020 to $177.5bn at June 30, 2026, a 5.0% five-year compound rate, with a negative average cost in both H1 2026 and H1 2025.

BNSF operates 32,500+ route miles. Berkshire Hathaway Energy holds four regulated utilities serving ~5.4 million customers, five interstate pipelines, ~32,400 net MW of generation, Northern Powergrid and AltaLink, plus HomeServices. Manufacturing/Service/Retailing spans Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Marmon, IMC and now OxyChem; Clayton Homes, Shaw, Johns Manville, MiTek, Benjamin Moore and now Taylor Morrison; McLane; and Pilot Travel Centers. Approximately 387,800 employees.

Two structural features matter more than the segment list. First, Berkshire does not guarantee any debt of BHE, BNSF or their subsidiaries, stated explicitly in the 10-Q, so litigation and leverage inside those platforms cap Berkshire's exposure at equity impairment. Second, the 10-K concedes that outside regulatory capital requirements "there are virtually no barriers to entry" in insurance. The moat is capital, ratings and willingness to shrink, not exclusivity.

## Financial Analysis

**Full year 2025.** Revenue of $371,444M was flat (+0.003%) against $371,433M. Pre-tax earnings fell 15.2% to $92,049M and net earnings 24.8% to $66,968M, driven by mark-to-market accounting plus a ~$5.0bn Kraft Heinz impairment in Q2 and an Occidental impairment in Q4. Reported ROE on average equity was 9.80%, down from 14.70% and 18.62%, but that numerator embeds unrealized equity marks and should not be read as three years of operating decay. Operating cash flow was $45,969M against $20,927M of capex, so FY2025 free cash flow was $25,042M, about a 2.2% yield on today's $1,142.6bn market capitalization.

**First half 2026.** Revenue of $195,483M rose 7.3%, helped by OxyChem ($2.6bn of H1 revenue) and Pilot. Pre-tax earnings of $44,382M and net earnings to Berkshire of $35,773M are dominated by $14,472M of investment gains. The disaggregated operating figure of $24,329M (+17.0%) reduces to +6.1% once the $2,165M FX revaluation swing on non-dollar senior notes is removed from both periods; approximately 61% of the reported improvement is a translation entry.

Underwriting deserves the most attention. Reported pre-tax underwriting profit of $4,445M included $1,467M of favorable prior-year development (versus $240M) and carried no catastrophe event above the $150M disclosure threshold, against $1.1bn of wildfire losses a year earlier. Normalizing both, underlying underwriting profit was roughly $2,978M versus $5,116M. GEICO's numbers are unambiguous: premiums written +1.3% on lower average premium per private-passenger-auto policy, premiums earned +3.0%, loss ratio 75.3% (+4.9pts), expense ratio 14.0% (+2.7pts) on a 28.3% increase in underwriting expenses attributed to commissions and advertising, and pre-tax underwriting down 39.7% to $2,410M. GEICO does not disclose policies in force quarterly, so whether the acquisition spend is buying units is unverified; a mix shift toward commission-bearing commercial auto is an alternative explanation the filing does not resolve.

Insurance investment income fell 7.6% to $6,989M pre-tax, with interest and other income down 11.6% to $4,460M on lower short-term rates; dividends were flat at $2,529M. Applying 100bp to the $359.2bn cash and T-bill book gives roughly $3.6bn of annual pre-tax sensitivity. This is the mirror image of the "cash is optionality" argument. [unverified — the direction and level of forward short-term rates, including any Fed policy stance or dissent count, is not in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

Free cash flow was $11,022M against $11,849M, down 7.0%, as capex rose 16.3% to $10,631M. H1 operating cash flow also benefited from a $2,615M income-tax working-capital inflow versus a $2,976M outflow a year earlier, a $5.6bn swing, so a simple doubling of H1 OCF (~$43.3bn) overstates normalized capacity; reversing the tax timing suggests $38-40bn, against FY2025's $45,969M actual. Neither figure is a forecast.

**Balance sheet.** Equity of $747,910M rose 4.25% in six months; book value per Class B share rose 4.72% to $348.26, the 47bp wedge being the buyback contribution as equivalent Class A shares fell 0.45% to 1,431,693. Debt-to-equity is 17.2% on total borrowings, ~6.0% on recourse borrowings. The fair value of borrowings sits $12.3bn below carrying value on legacy fixed-rate debt, an economic benefit of higher rates that never appears in equity. All BHE and BNSF covenants were in compliance; unused credit and commercial paper capacity was $12.1bn. The repurchase program's $30bn consolidated cash floor is nowhere near binding.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine here of consequence. FY2025 revenue was flat. H1 2026 revenue growth of 7.3% is acquisition- and price-driven. Underlying operating earnings grew 6.1%. Consensus, thin at three analysts, has current-year EPS of $20.89 and next year $21.57 — 3.3% growth — with revisions of +0.7% over 30 days and +1.6% over 90 days. That is directionally positive but an order of magnitude below the revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection, and it should not be read as one.

Growth now comes from three levers, all modest. Acquisitions: OxyChem added $2.6bn of H1 revenue but only $121M of H1 pre-tax earnings (implying a Q1 pre-tax loss of ~$28M) against $149M in Q2; four times the Q2 figure would be a 6.3% pre-tax return on the $9.4bn price, offered strictly as sensitivity given PVC/chlor-alkali cyclicality and seasonal Q1 weakness. Taylor Morrison closes into H2 with no purchase-price allocation yet available. Share count reduction: 0.45% in six months, worth roughly 0.9% annualized to per-share value if sustained, which is unlikely at 1.52x book. Float: compounding at 5%.

The offsetting drag is real. GEICO's margin trough will take multiple quarters to work through given that rate increases earn in with a lag while severity is running at 10-12%. Insurance investment income is falling. Building products — already Clayton Homes, Shaw, Johns Manville, MiTek and Benjamin Moore, now enlarged by Taylor Morrison — faces a housing backdrop that this filing does not characterize. Berkshire has bought a homebuilder; whether that is counter-cyclical discipline or mistimed capital is not resolvable from this filing. ## Valuation Assessment

The supplied peer set (Blue Owl at 99.5x, Klarna at negative earnings) has no analytical bearing on a diversified insurer-conglomerate and is discarded. Three internally consistent anchors are available instead.

**Price to book.** $529.76 against $348.26 is 1.521x. [unverified — the research brief and Phase 1 data do not contain a prior-review price of $511.54 or a March book value of $337.15, so the "effectively unchanged multiple" comparison cannot be sourced.] What is new is the reference point: management repurchased at 1.367x (May) and 1.401x (June) book, under a mandate restricted to prices below conservatively determined intrinsic value. The current price is 8.6% above the June average and 11.3% above May's. Repurchases accelerated fivefold into June as the price rose 2.5%, so the program is not mechanically price-anchored, but 1.52x is outside the demonstrated zone.

**Earnings multiple.** 25.4x current-year and 24.6x next-year consensus, on 3.3% growth and 6.1% underlying operating growth. Trailing 15.8x is meaningless — it capitalizes investment marks. [unverified — the peer earnings-multiple ranges cited for P&C insurers, Class I rails and regulated utilities are not in the research brief or Phase 1 data; the only supplied peer set is Blue Owl and Klarna, which are not comparable.] A 25x blended multiple is a premium paid for the balance sheet rather than for earnings growth.

**Look-through.** Deduct the $359.2bn cash and T-bill book at par (31% of market capitalization) and its associated after-tax investment income (roughly $11bn annualized) and the remaining $783bn capitalizes ~$34bn of operating earnings at about 23x. Deduct the marketable equity portfolio at market as well and the implied multiple on the operating businesses falls toward the mid-teens; the precise figure depends on a portfolio fair value not captured in this dataset (the filing discloses cost basis of $106,521M, up $21,132M in the half). That sum-of-parts frame is the most forgiving view available, and it is also the one most exposed to an equity drawdown, since the five largest holdings were 66% of portfolio fair value at June 30, up from 65%, and now include Alphabet alongside Apple.

Analyst targets run $481 to $585 with a $525.33 mean and $510 median, and the recommendation mix degraded (one strong buy became a hold over two months). The stock trades above mean and median. Consensus targets should not be treated as stale floors here; that applies to cheap stocks with rapidly rising estimates, not to a 25x compounder with 3% estimate growth. Valuation score 4.5: no identified mispricing, no margin of safety, but book compounding and the look-through math prevent a lower mark.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory has turned mildly negative, which is why this dimension steps down to 6.5.

GEICO is defending share with price and advertising simultaneously — average premium per private-passenger-auto policy down while acquisition spend is up 28.3% — into loss-cost inflation running at 10-12% on bodily-injury severity. That is the standard configuration for a multi-quarter margin trough, and the 10-K's own language on the absence of entry barriers means there is no structural mechanism to prevent competitors from pressing. GEICO's severity data is a useful read on the wider inflation picture: medical and legal cost inflation in auto claims is reaccelerating. [unverified — the specific "core services inflation staying near 3.3%" figure is not in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

Elsewhere: BNSF competes directly with Union Pacific, and the 10-K notes intensifying rail competition as networks rationalize. HomeServices' brokerage earnings fell 73.3% in H1, partly on Q2 settlement charges, in a residential market with falling transaction volumes. McLane's revenue concentration (Walmart ~17.2%, 7-Eleven ~13.3%, Yum! ~13.3%) is unchanged.

The durable advantages are intact and were demonstrated this half. Berkshire bought OxyChem for $9.4bn against $10.7bn of preliminary acquired assets and $1.3bn of liabilities — essentially no goodwill, roughly $7.0bn of PP&E, and with legacy environmental liabilities retained by the seller. Few buyers could write that check for a cyclical chemicals business in January. Total goodwill from H1 acquisitions was $352M. That demonstrates the moat: capital available when others' is not, on terms others cannot demand.

## Risk Assessment

**PacifiCorp (dominant dated binary).** Cumulative accrued probable losses ~$2.85bn, ~$2.3bn paid including $589M in H1, unpaid liability $572M at June 30. In April 2026 the Oregon Court of Appeals reversed the June 2023 Phase I liability verdict, finding the jury instruction on class-wide application of evidence prejudicial, and held the trial court may reconsider class certification. Plaintiffs petitioned May 13; the Oregon Supreme Court allowed the petition June 25 and set oral argument for **November 3, 2026**. Phase II verdicts to date total ~$1.25bn net for 201 plaintiffs (~$6.2M each against >$31M pleaded); 1,760 James class members have filed nine mass complaints. Extrapolating $6.2M across 1,760 claimants gives ~$10.9bn against $2.85bn accrued — an arithmetic sensitivity that is invalid if the Phase I reversal stands or certification is revisited. The company states additional material losses are reasonably possible but not estimable, and all insurance recoveries were exhausted as of June 30, 2025. The structural mitigant is decisive: PacifiCorp sits inside BHE, and Berkshire guarantees none of BHE's debt, so maximum exposure is impairment of BHE equity. Any parent support would be discretionary.

**HomeServices antitrust.** The $250M settlement (over four years, $130M escrowed) was approved and appealed; Eighth Circuit argument was heard January 14, 2026 and a ruling is pending. Two cases assert approximately $9bn of Texas deceptive-trade-practices damages by written notice — a plaintiff assertion, not a judgment or accrual. Failure to affirm reopens a jury finding of $1.8bn in class damages with joint-and-several liability and trebling available.

**Equity concentration.** Top five at 66% of portfolio fair value, up from 65%. With Apple and Alphabet among them, Berkshire's book value carries direct beta to large-cap technology at a point where that complex is elevated and a correction is possible. A 15% drawdown in the equity book would arrest book-value growth outright for two to three quarters.

**Kraft Heinz.** Carrying value $8,760M against fair value $7,692M — a 12.2% deficit, unimpaired on hold intent, after a $5.0bn impairment already taken in Q2 2025. Berkshire's board representatives resigned in May 2025 and reporting is now on a one-quarter lag.

**Other.** Goodwill of $83.1bn with four reporting units whose fair value exceeded carrying by less than 20% (~$27.7bn fair value against ~$26.2bn carrying, holding ~$9.2bn of goodwill). P&C unpaid losses and LAE of $152.9bn, with an approximate $15bn single-event exposure ceiling disclosed in FY2025 risk factors. Consensus is a poor instrument here: two beats and two misses in four quarters, including a 15.9% Q4 2025 miss, almost entirely investment-mark noise across only two to three estimates.

**What is not a risk.** Refinancing and covenant risk are negligible. Short interest is 1.0% of float at 2.3 days to cover. Beta is 0.607. No options data was available this cycle, so no positioning read is offered.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($605, +14.2%).** Book value compounds 8-10% annually and the multiple holds at 1.60x. GEICO's rate filings earn through and the combined ratio recovers toward the mid-80s by mid-2027; the November 3 Oregon Supreme Court argument confirms the Phase I reversal and the accrual proves adequate; the Eighth Circuit affirms the HomeServices settlement; and higher reinvestment yields on the $359.2bn short book would add roughly $3.6bn of pre-tax income per 100bp. The scenario with the largest asymmetry is a credit dislocation in which Berkshire is one of very few buyers with tens of billions of unencumbered capacity and a demonstrated willingness to demand structural terms; the OxyChem purchase price with environmental liabilities retained is a template.

**Bear case ($455, -14.1%).** A drawdown in the large-cap-technology-weighted equity complex hits a portfolio 66% concentrated in five names, stalling book value near $355 while the multiple compresses to 1.28x. GEICO's severity trend persists into 2027 as price competition prevents rate adequacy; reserve releases, which supplied 33% of H1 underwriting profit, are not repeatable; the Oregon Supreme Court reinstates the Phase I verdict, restoring the path toward a multi-billion incremental accrual at BHE; the Eighth Circuit declines to affirm; Kraft Heinz is written down another $1.1bn; and Taylor Morrison delivers negative operating leverage into a decelerating housing market. None of these threatens solvency; all of them compress the multiple a market pays for 6% underlying growth.

**Base case ($555, +4.8%).** Book value grows 8.5% to roughly $378 per Class B share by mid-2027 and the multiple settles at 1.47x. GEICO troughs in H2 2026 and stabilizes without recovering; investment income flattens; acquisitions contribute incrementally; buybacks stay minimal at these prices. Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 22%, bear 28% gives an expected return of roughly +1.6%, before any credit-dislocation optionality. That is thin, and it is the reason this is a hold-not-add rather than an accumulate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with three dated checkpoints — Q3 2026 results (Taylor Morrison purchase accounting and the first full quarter of GEICO under a new CEO), the Oregon Supreme Court argument on November 3, 2026, and the pending Eighth Circuit HomeServices ruling.

- **Base case $555** — 1.47x estimated mid-2027 book of ~$378 per Class B share (June-30 book of $348.26 compounded at 8.5%). **+4.8%** from $529.76.
- **Bull case $605** — 1.60x the same book estimate. **+14.2%**.
- **Bear case $455** — 1.28x a stalled book of ~$355. **−14.1%**.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the **$470-490** range, which is 1.35-1.40x current book and inside management's demonstrated repurchase zone, absent fundamental deterioration.
2. GEICO's combined ratio improving sequentially for two consecutive quarters with premiums written growth above 4% — evidence that rate is being achieved rather than bought with advertising.
3. Disclosed deployment of $25bn+ of the cash book into operating businesses or equities at prices that imply double-digit returns, particularly in a credit dislocation.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Price above **$580** (roughly 1.66x current book) without corresponding book growth — the expected-return math turns negative there.
2. The Oregon Supreme Court reinstating the Phase I verdict, or any incremental PacifiCorp accrual above $3bn.
3. GEICO's combined ratio exceeding 95% in any quarter, or private-passenger-auto premiums written turning negative year over year.
4. Underlying underwriting profit (ex prior-year development, ex catastrophes) declining again in H2 2026 alongside a second consecutive decline in free cash flow.

**Reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 10-Q, expected early November, which will land within days of the Oregon Supreme Court argument.

## Conclusion

The Q2 filing explains the deterioration in this name without contradicting the core case. Financial health, at 8.0, is as good as a balance sheet gets at this scale: 59.2% equity/assets, ~6% recourse leverage, $359.2bn of liquidity, negative-cost float of $177.5bn, and $12.3bn of unrecognized economic discount on legacy fixed-rate debt. Management quality, at 7.5, is supported by evidence rather than reputation: the CEO bought roughly $15.3M of Class A stock on the open market on March 4, the new General Counsel bought Class B shares in May, and the company repurchased $4.76bn at disclosed prices equal to 1.37-1.40x book while structuring OxyChem with essentially no goodwill and the seller retaining legacy environmental liabilities.

What deteriorated is the earnings engine and the entry price. Underlying underwriting profit fell roughly 42%; the reported figure held up only because $1.1bn of catastrophe losses did not recur and $1.23bn of incremental reserve releases were booked. Operating earnings grew 6.1% rather than 17% once a non-cash currency translation entry is excluded. Free cash flow fell 7%. Investment income fell 7.6%. Against that, the stock closed at a 52-week high, above the consensus mean, 8.6% above where the party with the best information stopped repurchasing five weeks earlier.

The expected return over twelve months is low single digits with a bear case roughly three times the base upside in magnitude. That argues against new capital and against any addition here, but not for exiting a 0.61-beta position holding the largest pool of deployable capital in the market. Hold it as defensive ballast, let book value compound, and buy the next drawdown into $470-490 rather than the 52-week high.
