## Executive Summary

Booz Allen's July 24 fiscal first-quarter report was the first constructive data point in a year of deterioration. Revenue fell 4.2% to $2.80B, but adjusted EBITDA rose 7.4% to $334M with margin expanding 130 basis points to 11.9%, adjusted diluted EPS rose 22.3% to $1.81 (a beat against non-GAAP consensus of roughly 20%), free cash flow rose 172% to $261M, and quarterly book-to-bill came in at 1.5x against the 0.9x disclosed on the prior quarter's call. Funded backlog — the near-term convertible component — grew 15.3% year over year to $4,664M. National Security revenue returned to growth at +1.35%. Management reaffirmed FY2027 guidance of $11.2-11.7B revenue, $1.24-1.29B adjusted EBITDA, $6.00-6.35 adjusted diluted EPS, and $825-925M free cash flow. The stock has risen roughly 14% since mid-July and about 25% from the June 25 low of $59.71.

Three things prevent that from becoming a buy case. First, the composition of the guided revenue recovery is unresolved. Q1 organic revenue was -4.2%; reaching even the zero-growth low end of guidance requires a substantial acceleration, and two acquisitions (Defy, closed April 6 for $235M; Ultra Mission Solutions, $720M, expected to close in the current quarter) are layering in. Neither target's revenue or margin is disclosed. Twelve times adjusted earnings on acquisition-supported flat revenue is a different proposition than twelve times on an organic recovery, and the difference is not currently verifiable. Second, leverage moved the wrong way. The $1.2B senior notes issuance priced July 28 (a $700M 5.375% 2030 tranche and a $500M 5.900% 2034 tranche, closing August 4) takes pro forma net leverage from 2.7x to roughly 3.29x pre-synergy and adds about $31.6M of pre-tax annual interest, roughly $0.21 per share after tax, that Ultra's earnings must recover. Third, the equity's clearest valuation support — a 3.17% dividend and a $634M remaining repurchase authorization — is the direct target of a January 7, 2026 executive order on defense-contractor distributions, a March 2026 Senate bill that would prohibit them absent waiver, and a Senate Armed Services Committee FY2027 NDAA provision conditioning distributions on a "qualified defense investment plan."

The score has declined from 6.4 in March to 5.2 in July, driven by growth, valuation and competitive-position downgrades. The Q1 print arrests that decline rather than reversing it: operating execution improved, but the price rose while capturing it, leverage rose, and the two structural questions (organic growth, distribution policy) are unchanged. Overall score moves to 5.3. HOLD, classified as uncertain — the dividend would ordinarily support an income classification, and it is the specific item under legislative threat.

## Company Overview

The FY2026 10-K opens by describing Booz Allen as "an advanced technology company building products and solutions for government and business." That is a deliberate change from a consulting-firm self-description, and the filing supports it with named proprietary offerings: Vellox (an AI-native cyber suite), Sit(x)R, EdgeExtend, and a Modular Detachment Kit built across three flagship engineering facilities and more than 20 manufacturing centers. The company claims to be the federal government's largest AI provider with approximately 400 active AI projects.

Scale: approximately 31,500 employees at March 31, 2026 (28,800 customer staff), down to roughly 30,900 at June 30, 2026; 77% hold security clearances. Revenue splits into National Security (Defense plus Intelligence) at 72% of Q1 FY2027 revenue and Civil & Commercial at 28%. The Department of Veterans Affairs was the largest single customer at 10% of FY2026 revenue, and it sits inside the contracting Civil portfolio.

The contract architecture is distinctive. FY2026 work ran through 5,026 contracts and task orders, with approximately 84% of revenue from 2,426 active task orders under indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity vehicles. The largest IDIQ vehicle was ~17% of revenue, the largest single task order ~4%, the largest definite contract ~1%. Prime share was 94%. Granularity means no single loss is existential; the offset, in the 10-K's own language, is that IDIQ vehicles carry "no guarantee of work," so an IDIQ-heavy book can stall in a procurement slowdown without anything being lost. The company runs a single P&L and reports one segment, so there is no segment margin disclosure to isolate where profit is being created.

Relationship framing matters for accuracy: AWS is described as a hyperscaler partner (Booz Allen consumes cloud infrastructure), a16z as a venture counterparty in which Booz Allen is a limited partner, and Shield AI as an emerging-defense-technology collaborator. None is described in the filing as a customer.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue contraction is real on both gross and net bases.** FY2026 revenue of $11,217M fell 6.4% from $11,980M, breaking a streak that ran +15.2% in FY2024 and +12.4% in FY2025. Revenue excluding billable expenses fell 5.3% to $7,767M from $8,200M, so this is not a pass-through artifact. Management attributes the decline to a slowed federal procurement environment including the December-quarter shutdown, headcount reduction, lower billable expenses, the absence of a prior-year $122M favorable claimed-costs revenue item, and a "substantial reduction in the ordinary end of government fiscal year funding surge."

Q1 FY2027 revenue of $2,800M was down 4.2%. The composition is the important detail: National Security $2,028M (+1.35%), Civil & Commercial $772M (-16.4%). The entire consolidated shortfall originates in Civil.

**Margins: the GAAP collapse overstates the underlying deterioration.** FY2026 operating income of $1,033M on 9.21% margin compares with $1,370M on 11.44% — a 223 basis point decline. Adjusted EBITDA margin on total revenue was essentially flat at 10.96% versus 10.98%, because FY2025 GAAP operating income included a $113M favorable claimed-costs change and $115M of insurance recoveries that adjusted EBITDA excluded. The honest reading sits between the two measures: adjusted EBITDA also excludes $33M of FY2026 severance, restructuring, divestiture and acquisition costs, which flatters the current year. Underlying margin was roughly flat to modestly down in FY2026, and genuinely improved in Q1 FY2027 (11.93% adjusted EBITDA margin, up 130bp).

**FY2026 net income is tax-flattered by roughly $1.55 per share, and this materially changes the trailing multiple.** FY2026 pretax income was $862M against an $11M tax provision — a 1.3% effective rate, versus 23.3% in FY2025. Management attributes it to an income-tax-reserve reduction following completion of an IRS examination, a higher-than-estimated R&D credit, and additional FDII deductions. Applying FY2025's rate to FY2026 pretax income implies a ~$201M provision, so the actual result carried roughly a $190M one-time benefit, about $1.55 per share on ~121-122M average diluted shares. Reported FY2026 GAAP EPS of approximately $6.98-7.03 corresponds to a tax-normalized figure nearer $5.40-5.45. The quoted trailing P/E of 11.3x on $6.90 of EPS therefore understates the trailing multiple; on normalized earnings it is closer to 13.7x. FY2027 guidance assumes a 20-23% adjusted effective rate, which is the appropriate go-forward assumption.

The same distortion runs the other way in the quarter. Q1 FY2026 carried a *negative* 25.5% effective rate on an $89M unrecognized-tax-position release; Q1 FY2027 was a normal 21.1%. GAAP diluted EPS consequently fell 24.5% to $1.63 while operating income rose 9% and adjusted EPS rose 22.3%. Screens keyed to GAAP EPS growth will misread both periods in opposite directions.

**Cash flow is the strongest element.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $1,041M against $90M of capex, giving $951M of free cash flow — an 8.5% FCF margin and high conversion against both reported and normalized net income. Q1 FY2027 FCF of $261M should not be annualized: the quarter benefited from a net $16M income tax *receipt* versus a $24M payment a year earlier, capex of $20M against $220M full-year guidance (including $105M for the new Reston headquarters), and a $257M receivables build. Guidance midpoint of $875M implies roughly $205M per quarter for the remaining three quarters, below what Q1 delivered.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $540M, total debt $3,936M, net debt $3,396M against $1,252M LTM adjusted EBITDA for 2.71x net leverage; $1,500M revolver undrawn, ~$2.0B liquidity. Book equity of $1,202M against $2,528M goodwill and $597M intangibles leaves tangible book equity at roughly negative $1,923M. The reported 5.4x debt-to-equity and grey-zone Altman Z-Score are mechanical artifacts of 50.2M treasury shares repurchased over the company's history against $3,644M of retained earnings, in a business with $171M of PP&E and one dominant creditworthy counterparty. The informative coverage measures are operating income to net interest of 5.6x and adjusted EBITDA to net interest of 6.7x. The real constraint is that $2,528M of goodwill (33% of assets, 2.1x book equity) makes any impairment arithmetically severe against the equity base, and goodwill impairment is an enumerated risk factor.

Pro forma for the notes issuance and the Ultra close: total debt roughly $4,422M, cash roughly $306M before subsequent free cash flow and dividends, net debt ~$4,116M, and net leverage of approximately 3.29x pre-synergy and before any Ultra EBITDA. Deleveraging capacity is adequate — $875M guided FCF less roughly $281M of dividends leaves about $594M of discretionary cash annually before buybacks — but the covenant threshold in the credit agreement was not verifiable, so headroom at 3.3x cannot be quantified.

## Growth Analysis

The forward case rests on three verifiable items and one that is not.

**Verifiable: bookings have inflected.** Quarterly book-to-bill of 1.5x against 0.9x the prior quarter, LTM 1.1x, and funded backlog of $4,664M versus $4,047M (+15.3%). On a trailing-twelve-month basis, funded backlog additions were $11.7B versus $11.5B, so the bookings flow has turned modestly positive even though FY2026 fiscal-year additions fell to $11.1B from $12.2B. A 15% increase in funded backlog against a -4.2% revenue quarter is the signature of bookings troughing ahead of revenue.

**Verifiable: total backlog growth is thin and low-quality.** Total backlog of $39,483M grew only 3.2%, and priced options — contract options the government need not exercise — are 62% of it. Unfunded backlog fell 2.1%. Remaining performance obligations were $11.1B at June 30, roughly 28% of the headline backlog figure, with ~65% expected to convert within twelve months. Deferred revenue is negligible ($27M current contract liabilities); this company bills as it performs, so unbilled receivables of $1,702M current plus $149M non-current are the meaningful contract asset.

**Verifiable: the mix is moving toward the funded part of the federal budget.** National Security rose to 72% of revenue from 68%, and both acquisitions push further that way (Defy in cyber, Ultra in defense technology: mission-critical software, encryption, edge compute). This establishes better exposure to the growing half of federal spending. It does not establish diversification — substantially all revenue remains U.S. government, the top IDIQ vehicle is still ~17%, and VA was 10% of FY2026.

**Not verifiable: the organic content of guidance.** Guidance implies 0% to +4% revenue growth against a Q1 result of -4.2%. Defy contributes from April 6 and Ultra from the current quarter. Neither target's revenue is disclosed. The purchase accounting for Defy is suggestive: $235M paid for $129M goodwill, $86M intangibles and only $20M of net identifiable assets, arrived at by netting ~$310M of total receivables against ~$292M of payables and accrued liabilities. A business carrying receivables at 1.3x its purchase price looks more like a value-added reseller than a high-margin software franchise. If that is right, Defy adds revenue at low margin — which would explain why FY2027 adjusted EBITDA margin guidance of "~11%" is flat against FY2026's 10.96% despite Q1 delivering 11.93%. This is a hypothesis built on provisional purchase accounting, not a disclosed fact, and it is the single most decision-relevant unresolved item in the file.

**Estimate revisions cut both ways and the split is informative.** Current-year consensus rose 2.1% over 30 days and 1.6% over 90 days to $6.38, reflecting the Q1 beat. Next-year consensus fell 2.4% and 3.8% to $6.71. Current- and next-quarter estimates fell 2.6% and 3.7%. So analysts are marking up FY2027 on cost execution while marking down FY2028 and shifting earnings later in the current year — consistent with the incremental $0.21 of interest expense plus doubt about organic revenue recovery and back-half margin mix. Implied EPS growth from $6.38 to $6.71 is 5.2%, giving a PEG of roughly 2.3 at 12.0x. The revision picture is mixed at best, with the out-year clearly negative.

Two further items to track: the re-compete win rate fell to 89% from 92% while the new-business win rate improved to 57% from 56%. On a book where 84% of revenue flows through IDIQ task orders, retention economics dominate, so a three-point re-compete decline is a larger headwind than a one-point new-business gain. Offsetting slightly, management notes a decline in bid protests against Booz Allen's new awards, which shortens time to revenue on wins.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.42 against guidance:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Adjusted P/E (FY2027 guidance midpoint $6.175) | 12.1x (range 11.7x–12.4x) |
| Adjusted P/E on consensus $6.38 | 11.7x |
| FCF yield on market cap ($875M midpoint) | 9.8% |
| FCF yield on enterprise value ($12,347M) | 7.1% |
| Dividend yield ($0.59 × 4 = $2.36) | 3.17% |
| Dividend as % of guided FCF | ~32% |
| Trailing P/E on tax-normalized FY2026 EPS (~$5.42) | ~13.7x |
| Implied EPS growth FY2027→FY2028 (consensus) | +5.2% |

The provided peer set — FCN 18.4x, TRU 18.3x, EXPO 26.5x, LZ 97.6x, EFX 28.1x, median 26.5x — is a data-provider grouping by market-cap proximity within "Research & Consulting Services" and contains no federal services companies. Comparing BAH's 12x to a 26.5x median produces a spurious discount. The relevant comparison is the sector's own de-rating, and the proxy's peer-group TSR index fell from 198 in FY2025 to 92 in FY2026, a ~54% decline comparable to BAH's own fall from 232 (FY2024) to 106. On that indexed measure BAH ended FY2026 slightly *above* its peer group. Multiple compression here is a federal-services event, not company-specific failure, which cuts against a mispricing thesis as much as for it, because it means BAH is priced roughly where its comparables are.

So the valuation question is whether 12.0x and a 9.8% FCF yield adequately compensate for a business with declining organic revenue, rising leverage, and legislative risk to its distribution capacity. Three considerations:

**Against a higher score.** Absolute cheapness in federal services has repeatedly been a value trap through this procurement cycle; BAH itself has fallen from $114.48 to $59.71 and back to $74.42 while looking "cheap" at every point. The 9.8% FCF yield is arithmetically attractive, but its transmission to shareholders runs through dividends and buybacks, and both are the explicit subject of the January 7, 2026 executive order and the proposed Warfighter in Defense Contracting Act. A cash yield that cannot be distributed is worth less than one that can. Consensus of $6.38 already sits $0.03 *above* the top of the guided range, so an in-line adjusted result would print as a miss against consensus; the beat cushion that produced consecutive double-digit surprises has narrowed. Analyst distribution is unusually negative for a "cheap" stock: 3 buy, 8 hold, 1 sell, 2 strong sell, with a median target of $79 against a $140 high that is a clear outlier.

**For a higher score.** Free cash flow conversion is genuinely high and largely uncontested by accounting distortion (the tax flattering hits net income, not operating cash flow). At 12x with a 3.2% yield, an operating trough that has already been confirmed in bookings does not require much to produce a re-rate toward 14x. A $180M long-term tax receivable pending Joint Committee on Taxation review is a real potential cash inflow of unspecified timing.

**Net.** Score 5.7 — cheap enough that upside exists if bookings convert, not cheap enough given the leverage step-up, the unresolved organic question, and the distribution risk. The price appreciation since mid-July has compressed the adjusted multiple opportunity; the improvement in evidence and the deterioration in entry price partially offset.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's competitor list has broadened to include large enterprise software companies, global technology providers, defense primes, systems integrators, small businesses, and technology startups. The reason is disclosed in the company's own risk factors: the December 18, 2025 NDAA exempts "nontraditional contractors" from certain compliance requirements, and Booz Allen writes that removing barriers to new entrants "could reduce the likelihood of direct awards to companies like Booz Allen and effect our competitive position." A company conceding a structural competitive headwind in its own Item 1A deserves to be taken at its word.

The April 30, 2026 executive order pushing agencies toward firm-fixed-price awards, and directing them to modify, restructure or renegotiate their top-ten highest-dollar non-fixed-price contracts, applies directly to the 59% of FY2026 revenue that was cost-reimbursable. Booz Allen frames fixed-price as margin-accretive, and Q1 FY2027 showed fixed-price share jumping 300 basis points to 21% from 18%. Fixed-price work transfers performance risk to the contractor; on a productized offering that is favorable, on labor-based delivery it is not, and the single-segment reporting structure means outside observers cannot see which is happening.

The strategic response is coherent: two product acquisitions in five months totaling $955M, named proprietary offerings, ~$500M of deployed-plus-committed venture capital exposure through two related-party funds, and a workforce shifting from consultants to technologists. Whether it works is unproven, and it is being executed by a leadership team in which the CFO, General Counsel, and President/COO roles all changed hands within roughly twelve months.

Trajectory assessment: deteriorating on incumbency and re-compete economics, stabilizing on mix and bookings. Score 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Distribution restriction is the first-order, company-specific risk.** Three disclosed items: the January 7, 2026 executive order "Prioritizing the Warfighter in Defense Contracting," directing identification of underperforming defense contractors that have repurchased shares or made distributions, with consequences for those distributions and executive compensation; the March 2026 Senate bill that would prohibit dividends, buybacks, and executive compensation above thresholds absent waiver; and the SASC FY2027 NDAA provision conditioning distributions on a "qualified defense investment plan." Booz Allen ties its dividend risk factor directly to these. There is no evidence Booz Allen has been designated, and all three remain proposals or directives to identify. The plausible offset is that a company spending $220M of capex including $105M on a new headquarters, running 20-plus manufacturing centers, and buying two product businesses may screen well under an investment-plan framework. The NDAA conference process resolves in the autumn.

**Buyback behavior.** Q1 FY2027 repurchases were $72M total, $50M under the program, versus $181M a year earlier. Monthly detail: April 306,260 shares at ~$82, May 455,965 at ~$76, June 3,542 shares with zero under the program — the sharpest deceleration at the lowest prices of the quarter. FY2026 repurchases of 5.6M shares for $561M averaged ~$100.18, roughly 26% underwater at $74.42. A special Board committee has been appointed to evaluate market conditions and initiate repurchases. The filings state no reason for the June pause; the mechanically plausible explanations are cash preservation ahead of the $720M Ultra close, blackout around the June 19 signing and the late-July notes offering, or covenant headroom management. I decline to infer motive, but the outcome is that the buyback lever is currently near-idle at the cheapest prices in three years.

**Investigations and audit exposure.** The 10-Q discloses awareness of pending audits and investigations involving labor time reporting, procurement integrity, and classified information access, with no material amounts accrued at either fiscal year end. Those categories map directly to False Claims Act exposure, where relators can receive up to 30% of recoveries. Separately, the provision for claimed costs stood at $248M with fiscal years after 2011 still open to DCAA audit; movements in this provision have swung reported results in both directions.

**Transaction and leverage mechanics.** The $500M 5.900% 2034 tranche is subject to special mandatory redemption if Ultra does not close by the later of December 19, 2026 or five business days after any agreed extension, which caps the downside if the deal fails but creates a defined event. Pro forma leverage of ~3.3x sits against a quarterly-tested net leverage covenant whose threshold is not disclosed. Incremental interest of ~$31.6M pre-tax must be earned back by an acquisition whose EBITDA is undisclosed.

**Concentration and operations.** VA at 10% of FY2026 revenue sits inside the -16.4% Civil portfolio. The top IDIQ vehicle is ~17% of revenue. The 10-K flags security clearance processing delays and cites "recent examples of large numbers of security clearances being revoked by executive orders"; with 77% of the workforce cleared, clearance policy is an operating input. The filing also warns of experienced and potentially continuing delays in government invoice payment, which bears directly on the working capital that produced Q1's cash flow strength.

**Management alignment.** The proxy shows CEO compensation of $13.80M granted against $3.27M "actually paid," with a FY2025 actually-paid figure of negative $2.72M — evidence that equity awards are genuinely at risk and have been marked down with the stock. Against that, no open-market insider purchases appear in the Form 4 record from roughly December 2025 through August 2026, at prices well below FY2024 levels; the record shows grants, tax-withholding dispositions, one 350-share sale, and a CEO gift of 74,014 shares. Insiders at cleared government contractors operate under restricted windows and hold large legacy positions (the CEO holds ~719,000 shares directly), so absence of buying is weak evidence. Combined with the buyback at a ~$100 average now 26% underwater, nearly $1B deployed into targets with undisclosed economics, and roughly $500M of deployed-plus-committed venture exposure equal to 42% of book equity growing from $178M to $253M in a single quarter, the recent capital allocation record does not support a high management score.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing resolved event risk rather than stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 41.5% against 41.1% trailing realized — fair, and in the 9th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at the front, term structure is in contango (41.5% near versus 45.5% at six months), and the near-term implied move is ±6.8%. Since the July 19 snapshot, ATM IV collapsed 11.9 points from 53.4% while the price rose 14%, and the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.95 to 0.73 — the earnings binary cleared favorably and hedging demand eased. Near-term put/call volume of 2.55 on the August 21 tenor is noisy on a 14-day expiry. The six-month tenor carries a -2.5% skew reading and a 2.67 put/call volume ratio, which is where the FY2027 NDAA process and the Ultra close both fall; I would characterize that as longer-dated positioning activity rather than a directional signal, given the sign convention is not defined. Short interest of 8.6% of float with 3.4 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded. Nothing in the options structure contradicts a HOLD; nothing in it corroborates a re-rating either.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $105 (+41%)

The bookings inflection converts. LTM book-to-bill holds above 1.1x and funded backlog growth above 10%, Civil & Commercial stabilizes as FY2027 appropriations normalize and the September funding surge partially returns, and National Security accelerates beyond +1.35% on Defy and Ultra content. Cost actions already delivered 130 basis points of margin expansion in Q1; the FY2027 EBITDA guide of ~11% proves conservative and the company exits the year nearer 12%. Ultra closes on schedule and contributes enough EBITDA to absorb the $0.21 of incremental interest and hold pro forma leverage near 3.0x, with deleveraging from ~$594M of post-dividend discretionary cash. The distribution-restriction legislation fails or Booz Allen qualifies under an investment-plan framework, the special Board committee restarts repurchases against the $634M authorization at prices well below the $100 FY2026 average, and the $180M tax receivable clears Joint Committee review. FY2028 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $6.80 and the multiple recovers to 15.5x as the federal-services group re-rates. Timeframe 18 months.

### Bear Case — $58 (-22%)

Organic revenue stays negative through FY2027 and the guided 0-4% growth proves entirely acquisition-derived, which the market discovers as the Defy and Ultra contributions become quantifiable in the Q2 and Q3 reports. Civil keeps contracting toward -20% as VA and DHS work is re-scoped, the re-compete win rate falls further below 89% on an 84% IDIQ base, and the April 2026 fixed-price executive order forces renegotiation of large cost-reimbursable programs at lower margin. Consolidated EBITDA margin dilutes toward the low end of the ~11% guide as low-margin acquired revenue mixes in. The FY2027 NDAA carries the distribution provision, the dividend is capped or suspended and the buyback stays idle, removing the yield and the share-shrinkage that supports the multiple. Leverage sits above 3.3x with the FY2028 interest drag intact, and adjusted EPS settles near $5.80 against a 9.5x multiple. A goodwill impairment against $2,528M of goodwill and $1,202M of book equity, or a quantified FCA settlement from the labor-time-reporting investigations, would push below the June low of $59.71.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).**

**Base case target: $82 (+10.2% from $74.42).** Arithmetic: 13.3x the FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $6.175. This assumes revenue lands near the low-to-middle of the $11.2-11.7B guide with a meaningful acquisition contribution, adjusted EBITDA margin holds at ~11%, free cash flow lands near the $875M midpoint, the dividend is maintained, and the multiple expands roughly one turn as the bookings trough is confirmed in a second consecutive quarter. Sits close to the $79 consensus median and below the $85.58 mean.

**Bull case target: $105 (+41%).** 15.5x roughly $6.80 of FY2028 adjusted EPS.

**Bear case target: $58 (-22%).** 9.5x roughly $5.80 of adjusted EPS with the distribution mechanism impaired.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on three dated events — the fiscal Q2 report (late October 2026, first quarter with Ultra consolidated and the first read on organic-versus-acquired revenue), the Ultra closing deadline of December 19, 2026 (special mandatory redemption on the 2034 notes if missed), and FY2027 NDAA enactment in the autumn.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. Quantified organic revenue disclosure showing organic decline narrowing to better than -2% year over year, or explicit management quantification of the Defy/Ultra contribution consistent with positive organic growth in H2 FY2027.
2. LTM book-to-bill sustained at 1.1x or better with funded backlog growth above 10% for a second consecutive quarter, plus Civil & Commercial decline narrowing to single digits.
3. Resolution of the distribution question in Booz Allen's favor — FY2027 NDAA enacted without a distribution-restriction provision applicable to the company, accompanied by resumption of program repurchases at a run rate above $100M per quarter. A price near $62-65 with these fundamentals intact would also restore adequate asymmetry.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. FY2027 adjusted EPS or FCF guidance cut, or adjusted EBITDA margin guidance reduced below 11%.
2. Enacted legislation or a designation that restricts Booz Allen's dividend or buyback, or a dividend cut.
3. Civil & Commercial revenue decline worsening beyond -20% year over year, or a re-compete win rate below 85% in the FY2027 10-K.
4. Pro forma net leverage above 3.6x, or a quantified accrual arising from the labor-time-reporting / procurement-integrity investigations.

**Reassessment point:** the fiscal Q2 FY2027 report in late October 2026, which should carry the first consolidated Ultra period, an updated backlog series, and enough disclosure to resolve the organic growth question.

## Conclusion

The Q1 FY2027 print delivered the kind of evidence that would justify moving off HOLD: margin expansion, a bookings inflection from 0.9x to 1.5x, funded backlog up 15.3%, National Security back to growth, and reaffirmed guidance. It arrived alongside three developments that pull the other way: a price move that took the adjusted multiple higher, a $1.2B debt issuance that raises pro forma leverage to ~3.3x and adds $0.21 per share of annual interest, and an unresolved question about whether the guided revenue recovery is organic at all. Layered on top is a legislative and executive-order process specifically aimed at the dividend and buyback that carry most of this equity's valuation support, resolving in the autumn.

The 35% drawdown from the 52-week high is largely explained rather than mispriced: revenue went from +12% to -6%, GAAP earnings power was flattered by roughly $1.55 per share of one-time tax benefit, and the company itself concedes in its risk factors that procurement reform reduces the likelihood of direct awards to incumbents like it. The market may be underweighting the bookings turn and the cost execution. The bulls are underweighting that the cash yield's transmission to shareholders is under direct policy threat, and that 12x on acquisition-supported flat revenue is not the same asset as 12x on an organic recovery.

Score 5.3, up marginally from 5.2, reflecting confirmed operating improvement offset by a higher entry price and higher leverage. Existing holders should collect the dividend and wait for the October report. New capital should wait for either the organic-growth disclosure or a price in the low $60s.
