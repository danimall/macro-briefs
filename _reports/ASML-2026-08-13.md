## Executive Summary

ASML has delivered one of the largest intra-year guidance raises we can identify at this revenue scale. FY2026 net sales guidance moved from €34–39bn (Jan 28) to €36–40bn (Apr 15) to €43–45bn (Jul 15), a 20.5% increase in the midpoint inside two quarters, with the gross-margin midpoint lifted ~300bp from 52% to 55%. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS estimates rose 18.6% in 30 days and 21.1% in 90 days across 33 analysts, and next-year estimates rose 25.7% in 90 days to $51.42. Q2 2026 beat its own guidance on both revenue (€9,326m vs €8.4–9.0bn guided) and gross margin (54.0% vs 51–52%), driven primarily by installed-base management (IBM) revenue, which now runs at 29.0% of total sales versus 23.0% in FY2024.

Two features of this raise deserve more weight than the headline. First, the entire acceleration is loaded into H2 2026. H1 2026 revenue of €18,093m grew 17.2% year over year; the FY guide implies H2 of €24,907–26,907m against H2 2025's €17,234m, or +44.5% to +56.1%. Netting the Q3 guide of €11.0–12.0bn leaves an implied Q4 of €12,907–15,907m against Q4 2025's €9,718m — a quarter that must be 33% to 64% larger than any quarter ASML has ever reported. Second, the margin expansion has not happened. H1 2026 gross margin of 53.5% was ~30bp *below* H1 2025's derived 53.8%, and trailing-twelve-month gross margin of 52.7% is flat to FY2025's 52.8%. Hitting the FY guide requires H2 gross margin of 54.4–57.7%, a ~250bp sequential step. Q3 is guided to 55–57%, so the trajectory is management-endorsed, but consensus is now anchored on margins the company has never printed.

Compounding the execution question, quarterly net bookings (€13,158m in Q4 2025, of which €7.4bn EUV) and backlog (€38,797m) were reported with full definitional footnotes in the January release and appear in neither the Q1 nor the Q2 2026 release. In their place: "our order intake remained extremely strong in the first half of the year." Whatever the reporting rationale — bookings are genuinely lumpy and management has long argued they are a poor demand indicator — the practical consequence is that the strongest quantitative support for the forward thesis is no longer falsifiable from primary disclosure. We rate the shares HOLD. The franchise is intact and the growth is real; at ~41x our FY2026E EPS with roughly +10% to base case against -33% to bear, the entry is not compelling.

## Company Overview

ASML supplies photolithography systems — the tools that print circuit patterns onto silicon — plus metrology and inspection equipment, software, and an expanding services and upgrade business. It is the sole commercial supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) and High-NA EUV systems, a position built over roughly two decades and approximately $10bn of R&D and customer co-investment (Intel, TSMC and Samsung participated as co-investors and buyers, not as developers of the product; the technology and installed base belong to ASML).

FY2025 scale: net sales €32,667m (+15.6%), gross margin 52.8%, income from operations €11.3bn, net income €9,609m, basic EPS €24.73, R&D €4.7bn. 535 systems shipped (48 EUV, 279 DUV, 208 metrology and inspection). Regional mix: Asia €28.1bn (86%), United States €4.1bn (12.5%), EMEA €0.5bn (1.5%). Headcount above 44,500 across 60+ locations with 5,100 suppliers.

The mix shift toward installed-base management is the most durable positive development. IBM revenue moved from €6,494m (23.0% of sales) in FY2024 to €8,193m (25.1%) in FY2025 to €5,250m (29.0%) in H1 2026, the latter up 28.1% year over year against a derived H1 2025 base of €4,097m. This is annuity-like revenue with structurally higher margins. It is not customer diversification — upgrades are sold to the same installed base owned by the same handful of leading-edge logic and memory makers, and ASML's disclosed largest-customer percentage was not obtainable for this review. Treat concentration as unquantified rather than improved.

Gross margin has improved sequentially for three quarters (51.6% → 52.2% → 53.0% → 54.0%), which is the strongest evidence the H2 step is achievable. The offsetting fact is that year-over-year the H1 comparison was flat, because H1 2025 was already running near 53.8%.

### Balance sheet and cash conversion

The most notable development in H1 2026 is a ~€6.4bn adverse working-capital swing. Cash and short-term investments fell from €13,322m at Dec 31, 2025 to €7,582m at Jun 28, 2026 (both issuer-reported), with accounts receivable rising €4,230m (€3,023m → €7,253m) and current unearned revenue falling €2,148m (€16,006m → €13,858m) per third-party aggregated statements. Net cash fell from €8,931m to €3,862m. TTM operating cash flow of €11,487m against FY2025's €13,827m implies H1 2026 OCF ran roughly €2.3bn below H1 2025, despite H1 net income of €5,675m being 24% higher.

Mechanically, this is what a shipment acceleration looks like: receivables build ahead of collections, and previously-collected customer prepayments are recognised into revenue as systems ship and are accepted. Two mitigations apply. The December 2025 cash balance was itself a spike (€13.3bn versus €5.1bn one quarter earlier), so part of the decline is normalisation, and TTM OCF of €11.5bn comfortably funds the €2.2bn per half-year buyback pace plus roughly €3bn of annual dividends. Still, net cash of €3.9bn is thinner than the headline balance sheet suggests, and the €13,858m unearned-revenue balance is a delivery obligation, not backlog — the two are separately defined and must not be substituted.

Leverage and returns are unambiguous strengths: total debt €3,720m against €25,995m equity (14.3% D/E), net cash positive, current ratio 1.30x (3.42x excluding customer prepayments, which is the more informative read for this model), TTM ROE ~42%, TTM ROA ~19%. Derived FY2025 free cash flow was €12,253m on the narrow definition (37.5% of sales) or €11,116m including intangibles purchases; TTM figures are €10,041m and €9,893m respectively. Solvency is not a live question.

Two reconciliation flags. Third-party aggregated net income (FY2025 €10,213m; FY2024 €8,349m) does not tie to ASML's US GAAP figures (€9,609m; €7,572m), most plausibly because the aggregator standardises to IFRS statutory statements — ASML itself identifies capitalised development costs, equity-investment valuation and income-tax accounting as the recurring differences. Financing lines and cash balances do reconcile exactly. Separately, the market-data feed reports forward P/E and trailing P/E as identical at 59.05, which against TTM EPS of $29.03 implies a price of ~$1,714 — a stale computation that should not be used.

### Capital allocation

FY2025 returned €8.5bn (€5.9bn buybacks, €2.6bn dividends), roughly 76% of narrow FCF. The FY2025 dividend was €7.50/share, +17%; the first 2026 interim of €1.88 paid Aug 5 is a 17.5% step up from the €1.60 cadence. The 2022–2025 buyback programme completed in December 2025 having repurchased €7.6bn of an "up to €12bn" authorisation — 63% execution. A replacement "up to €12bn" authorisation runs to Dec 31, 2028, with €1.1bn executed in each of Q1 and Q2 2026 (€4.4bn annualised, which would fully consume it in three years).

Share count has fallen from 402.6m (2021) to 385.4m (2025), only 4.3% over four years despite €5.9bn of repurchases in FY2025 alone, because buybacks have been executed at sharply rising prices. Repurchasing at ~58–65x trailing earnings converts euros into materially fewer shares than the 2022–2023 vintage. Under Dutch law, buyback and dividend capacity depends on distributable reserves and annually-renewed AGM authority — a structural constraint US investors tend to underweight.

## Growth Analysis

The demand driver is AI-related capacity expansion at leading-edge logic and memory. CEO Christophe Fouquet: "Ongoing AI-related investments and continued progress in AI technologies are driving demand for advanced Logic and Memory chips… Our customers, in turn, continue to accelerate their capacity expansion plans. This is translating into customer commitments across our product portfolio." TSMC independently raised 2026 revenue and capex outlooks.

Two observations sharpen the growth read. First, the contracting chain is two layers deep: ASML's visibility rests on commitments its customers have from *their* customers. That is real visibility, but derivative — a hyperscaler capex reset transmits to ASML with a lag and through an intermediary who owns the cancellation decision. Second, the H1 2026 regional ranking (Korea #1, Taiwan #2, China #3, US #4) marks a shift from the Taiwan/logic-dominated pattern toward memory. Memory capex is historically the more volatile leg of the cycle.

The clearest forward quantification management has given is capacity, not orders: plans to add 30% to 2026 low-NA EUV capacity of ~65 units for 2027 (→ ~85) and 30% to 2026 DUV immersion capacity of ~130 for 2027 (→ ~170), with a further 30% in each "under investigation" for 2028. This converts ASML from a rationing bottleneck into a volume grower and is now the best available forward indicator with bookings unpublished. It also raises the operating-leverage stakes in both directions: capacity added must be filled, and ASML's own FY2023 downcycle saw operating cash flow fall 30.7% and free cash flow fall 46.3%.

Estimate revisions are the strongest quantitative confirmation available. Current-quarter consensus rose 24.2% in 30 days and 27.5% in 90; next-quarter rose 34.9% and 35.7%; current-year rose 18.6% and 21.1% to $38.03; next-year rose 16.8% and 25.7% to $51.42. Revisions of this magnitude on a 33–34 analyst panel are a genuine positive signal. Two qualifications: the revisions are consensus catching up to guidance already issued on July 15 rather than new information, and the signal is materially weaker at 36–46x forward than it would be at sub-30x, because a premium multiple already discounts continued beats. The earnings-surprise record is mixed — two beats (Q1 +7.1%, Q2 +9.4%) after two misses (Q3 2025 -1.4%, Q4 2025 -4.8%).

Two features define the valuation problem. First, the re-rating has outrun the earnings. FY2025 EPS grew 28.5% while the shares roughly tripled from the August 2025 low of $716. Second, at this multiple the equity is a long-duration asset whose value is dominated by 2028–2035 assumptions, which is why headline risk about the moat produces outsized single-day moves: -6.9% on June 23 alongside Dutch lobbying reports, and up to -8% intraday on July 27 on a single-source report about a Chinese immersion DUV tool. Neither move followed a financial result.

The multiple has not deteriorated since our prior report. On Jul 30 the shares closed at $1,651 with consensus current-year EPS somewhere between the $32.06 30-day-ago print and today's $38.03; today's $1,868 against $38.03 is 49.1x. Estimates have risen roughly in line with the price, so the forward multiple is approximately unchanged. What has changed is the remaining distance to any defensible target and the proximity to two dated binaries.

Peer context within semicap: ASML has no true comparable, since no peer holds sole-source position in a critical process step. Against the broader AI-infrastructure cohort, 36x FY2027 consensus for a business generating 42% ROE with net cash is defensible — conditional on the H2 2026 delivery. Analyst targets bracket an unusually wide range: low $892, mean $2,169, median $2,318, high $2,864. That dispersion (the low is 52% below spot) is itself a statement about how binary the China policy outcome is perceived to be. Recommendation distribution is 6 strong buy / 33 buy / 4 hold / 1 sell, with one hold added in the last month.

We score valuation 4.5. The multiple is not absurd for the franchise, but it prices successful execution of a step-function quarter and a margin level never achieved, and offers no cushion against either binary.

## Competitive Landscape

ASML's position in EUV and High-NA is monopolistic and, on the evidence available, stable. FY2025 delivered the first full-specification TWINSCAN EXE:5200B (High-NA) to a customer and a 37% throughput improvement on the NXE:3800E, with revenue recognised on two High-NA systems in Q4 2025. A report that Intel Foundry shipped the first high-volume logic product manufactured using High-NA appeared in one lower-tier outlet and is not corroborated by ASML's Q2 release or by wire coverage; treat as unconfirmed.

The China domestic-tool question is the live competitive debate. The Information reported on July 27 that an unnamed Chinese company has begun manufacturing an immersion DUV system, with deliveries expected to SMIC and CXMT in 2026, targeting 5 units in 2026 and ~20 in 2027. Analyst rebuttals are specific and testable rather than dismissive: yield parity, not tool existence, is the commercial bar (imported DUV at SMIC already yields far below TSMC); scaling serial production of the machine itself is the most underestimated constraint (5 units against ASML's ~130 DUV immersion units of 2026 capacity, rising 30% for 2027); supporting a global fleet is a separate business from delivering a handful of tools; and much of the addressed revenue is revenue ASML already lost to export controls, so a locally-built tool ASML cannot legally supply does not subtract from a sold-out order book. EUV is regarded as a materially harder problem — light source and optics complexity place it out of reach on any near horizon per the analysts cited.

Our reading: the substantive risk is not a 2026–2027 revenue hit. It is a structural cap on long-run China TAM, which becomes the floor case if the MATCH Act closes DUV sales regardless, plus a multiple risk from moat headlines on a stock at ~58x trailing earnings. Bernstein expects Chinese semiconductor equipment spend to grow ~10% annually for the next 24 months — the market grows while ASML's share of it faces policy and substitution pressure.

We hold competitive position at 8.0. Level is extraordinary and the value-per-system trajectory is improving through High-NA and upgrades; the offset is a long-dated geographic TAM cap and no quantified customer-concentration disclosure.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Export controls and the MATCH Act (dominant discrete risk).** China was €2.9bn / ~16% of H1 2026 revenue and 14% of Q2 net system sales (~€924m), yet the CFO guided ~20% of net sales for full-year 2026 — implying China revenue *increases* in H2. Deriving from that percentage: ~€8.8bn of FY2026 China revenue less €2.9bn in H1 implies roughly €5.9bn in H2, about 23% of the implied H2 total. This is our derivation from a reported CFO percentage, not a company disclosure. The MATCH Act, tabled in April 2026, could bar Chinese purchases of DUV. Notably, the April release explicitly stated that "the bandwidth in our 2026 guidance accommodates potential outcomes of ongoing discussions around export controls"; that hedge does not appear in the July release, where the band narrowed from €4bn to €2bn. Mitigants: legislation tabled is not legislation passed; Dutch ministerial lobbying against forced alignment is active; previously-licensed systems could be grandfathered.

**2. Back-end-loaded execution.** Implied Q4 2026 of €12.9–15.9bn against a €9.7bn record, plus a 250bp H1→H2 gross-margin step. ASML's own forward-looking-statements list enumerates the failure modes: production capacity, supply-chain capacity, parts availability, and "the number and timing of systems ordered, shipped and recognized in revenue." With 5,100 suppliers and field acceptance required for recognition, one quarter of slippage across the December year-end boundary misses the full-year range.

**3. Withdrawn bookings and backlog disclosure.** The primary quantitative demand evidence disappeared from quarterly reporting exactly as the demand narrative became the entire investment case. We do not infer intent — removing lumpy bookings is a defensible reporting choice, and backlog may now be annual-only in the 20-F — and we could not verify whether a policy change was announced. The consequence stands: "order intake extremely strong" is currently unfalsifiable from primary disclosure.

**4. Capacity committed ahead of orders on hand.** ~30% unit capacity additions in both low-NA EUV and DUV immersion for 2027 are fixed cost and working capital built against customer *plans*. FY2023 is the downside template.

**5. Working-capital and cash-conversion deterioration.** Net cash halved to €3.9bn in six months against €2.2bn per half-year of buybacks and ~€3bn annual dividends. Funded from ongoing OCF, but the buffer is thinner.

**6. Information gaps.** As a foreign private issuer, ASML files a 20-F and 6-Ks, no Form 4s and no DEF 14A. The FY2025 20-F risk factors (filed Feb 25, 2026) could not be retrieved for this review, so Item 3.D risks and legal proceedings are unassessed. There is no US insider-transaction signal in either direction and no structured compensation or say-on-pay dataset. This is a genuine asymmetry versus US-domiciled peers.

**7. Unverified items.** ASML reportedly led Mistral AI's 2025 funding round; long-term investments rose €1,656m in FY2025, directionally consistent, but the amount, ownership and accounting treatment were not verified from any filing, and attribution is inference. Reported Samsung talks to invest in Mistral are single-source and lower-tier.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reads "backwardation — market pricing imminent stress," but the structure is more mundane on inspection. The 58.0% near-term ATM IV sits on a one-day expiry (Aug 14) with a ±2.5% implied move, which is largely same-day pinning mechanics. The 1-month (45.9%), 3-month (45.1%) and 6-month (47.8%) tenors sit at or barely above 1-year realized volatility of 45.9% — the market is pricing normal ASML volatility, not a discrete event. The 6-month implied move of 23.7% is consistent with that vol level, not with elevated crash pricing.

More informative is the change since July 30: ATM IV fell from 73.0% to 58.0% while the price rose 13.1%, and put/call open interest collapsed from 1.15 to 0.53. Positioning has moved from defensive to constructive during the rally, removing the hedged cushion that existed at the prior report. Volume put/call is 0.50 and OI put/call 0.53 — call-tilted. The data source labels the 1-day skew of 3.9% as elevated put demand; longer tenors print negative values whose sign convention we do not assume a direction from. Short interest is negligible at 0.3% of float and 0.5 days to cover, so there is neither crowded-short fuel nor informed bearish positioning.

Net read: the options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis, and near-dated vol is no longer cheap enough to make hedging attractive relative to owning less stock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $2,650 (+41.8%)

FY2026 lands at or above the €45bn / 56% top of guidance, validating both the Q4 step-function and the margin plan. Q4 order intake and the 2027 capacity plan carry into a FY2027 revenue figure above €52bn, driving EPS toward $58–60 as operating leverage on the ~30% capacity addition compounds with a rising IBM mix (upgrades carry structurally higher margins and are now 29% of sales). MATCH Act dies in committee or carves out allied DUV; China revenue holds near 20% of sales. High-NA moves into high-volume manufacturing at multiple customers, extending the sole-source runway into the 2030s. At 45x on $58–60 the shares reach $2,600–2,700, near the analyst high of $2,864.

### Bear Case — $1,250 (-33.1%)

Q4 2026 slips across the year-end boundary on a supply-chain or field-acceptance timing issue — an outcome the arithmetic makes plausible given the quarter must be 33–64% above any prior record — and FY2026 lands at €39–41bn with gross margin near 54% rather than 56%. Alternatively the MATCH Act passes mid-H2, removing the derived ~€5.9bn of H2 China revenue on which the narrowed €2bn guidance band depends. Either path resets FY2027 EPS toward $42–45 and, more damagingly, breaks the guidance-credibility premium that has driven the multiple higher. At 28–30x on $42–45 the shares trade $1,200–1,350. The analyst low of $892 corresponds to a harder landing in which the AI capex cycle itself decelerates and ASML carries the newly added 2027 capacity through a FY2023-style downcycle (OCF -30.7%, FCF -46.3%).

### Base Case — $2,050 (+9.7%)

FY2026 lands inside the €43–45bn range with gross margin at the low-to-mid part of 54–56%, requiring a Q4 near the bottom of the implied €12.9–15.9bn band. FY2027 delivers consensus EPS of ~$51.42 as capacity additions convert, with continued upward drift in estimates offset by a modest multiple compression as growth normalises from step-function to high-20s. MATCH Act resolution is partial — restrictions tighten without a full DUV prohibition — and China settles nearer 15% of sales than 20%. 40x on $51.42 gives $2,057, marginally below the analyst mean of $2,169.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: expected value ~$2,000, or +7.1%. At a more cautious 50/20/30 weighting the expected value falls to ~$1,930, or +3.3%. Neither clears a BUY bar against 45%+ realized volatility and two dated binaries.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should maintain exposure; the growth thesis is intact and the sole-source franchise is not replicable on any relevant horizon. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $1,868.21.

**Base case $2,050 (+9.7%) | Bull $2,650 (+41.8%) | Bear $1,250 (-33.1%)**
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (mid-October 2026) and the Q4/FY2026 print (late January 2027).

**Accumulation zone: $1,500–1,600** (-14.3% to -19.7% from the current basis), which corresponds to 29–31x FY2027 consensus of $51.42. That level would restore a margin of safety consistent with the binary risk profile. Note the shares traded $1,550–1,655 as recently as July 27–31 on the China DUV headline, so this zone is reachable on news flow rather than requiring fundamental deterioration.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 2026 revenue ≥€11.5bn with gross margin ≥55%, *and* reinstatement of a quantified order metric (bookings, backlog, or a stated committed-capacity figure) that makes the demand claim verifiable.
2. MATCH Act resolution favourable to ASML — bill dies in committee, or an allied-supplier DUV carve-out is enacted — removing the ~€5.9bn derived H2 China dependency as a binary.
3. Price into $1,500–1,600 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $50.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. FY2026 guidance cut, or narrowed to a range whose midpoint falls below €43bn.
2. MATCH Act passage with a DUV-to-China prohibition, without grandfathering of licensed systems.
3. Q3 gross margin below 54%, which would make the FY guide arithmetically unreachable given H1's 53.5%.
4. Net cash falling below ~€2bn with receivables continuing to grow faster than revenue for a second consecutive quarter, indicating the ramp is consuming more working capital than the shipment schedule justifies.

**Next reassessment:** Q3 2026 results, mid-October 2026. That print is the first genuine test of the H2 step — it must deliver both the €11.0–12.0bn revenue guide and the 55–57% gross margin, and it will indicate whether Q4 needs to be €12.9bn or €15.9bn.

## Conclusion

ASML's franchise quality is not in question, and the growth is documented rather than hoped for: two guidance raises totalling a 20.5% lift to the FY2026 revenue midpoint, consensus EPS up 21–26% over 90 days, IBM revenue up 28% year over year and now 29% of sales, and a ~30% unit capacity expansion committed for 2027. Financial health is strong on any conventional measure — net cash, 14% debt/equity, ~42% ROE, TTM free cash flow near €10bn.

The rating rests on three specific things. The FY2026 guide requires a Q4 that is 33–64% larger than any quarter in company history and an H2 gross margin roughly 250bp above H1, neither of which has printed. The quarterly bookings and backlog figures that would let an outside investor verify the underlying demand were withdrawn from reporting two quarters ago. And the FY2026 guide embeds a China acceleration to ~20% of sales at the same time US legislation that could close DUV sales into China is pending, with the guidance band narrowed and the prior export-control hedge language removed. At ~41x our FY2026E EPS, 36x FY2027 consensus and a 1.6% free-cash-flow yield, the price compensates for none of that.

Own it if you own it. Add on the next moat or policy headline that takes the shares into the $1,500–1,600 zone, or on a Q3 print that delivers the guided margin step and restores a verifiable order metric.
