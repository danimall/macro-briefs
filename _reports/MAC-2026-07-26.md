**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Macerich Company owns interests in roughly 39 predominantly Class A regional retail centers totaling ~41 million square feet, concentrated in affluent, supply-constrained markets (California 24.4% of NOI, Arizona 21.3%, New York 16.7%). The differentiator is portfolio productivity: trailing tenant sales of $899/sq ft portfolio-wide and $941 for the "Go-Forward" set rank among the best in the mall-REIT peer group. The offset is a balance sheet carrying ~$6.45B of pro-rata debt at 7.76x net debt/EBITDA, well above the ~5-6x Class-A peer norm, on a short 3.2-year weighted-average maturity ladder that forces near-constant refinancing into an elevated-rate environment.

The investment case is a timing arbitrage. GAAP is structurally negative (2025 net loss to the company of –$197M; trailing EPS –$0.78) because heavy real-estate depreciation and periodic impairments swamp cash generation. The operating reality is better: FFO as adjusted grew 8.7% to $397M in 2025, Q1 2026 FFO of $0.34/share beat consensus, and leasing spreads have been positive for 17 consecutive quarters. The crux is a ~$116M signed-not-open (SNO) rent pipeline that is contractually signed but not yet in the P&L, which management expects to drive "meaningfully accelerating" NOI growth in 2027-2028 as those tenants open and begin paying rent.

The problem for a new buyer is entry price. The stock has run ~46% over the past year to ~$25.83, closing most of the discount that made the turnaround attractive. It now trades well above one credible NAV estimate (~$15.42) and near the $26.19 consensus mean target, with a modest 2.7% yield versus a 4.0% Retail-REIT peer median. Operating momentum and lender confidence are real, but so is elevated leverage, ongoing equity dilution that caps per-share FFO upside, and a valuation that no longer offers the asymmetry it did nine months ago. We rate MAC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Macerich is a self-managed REIT that owns, operates, leases, and redevelops high-quality regional retail centers through The Macerich Partnership, L.P. Trophy assets include Tysons Corner Center (VA), Queens Center (NY), Scottsdale Fashion Square (AZ), Broadway Plaza (Walnut Creek, CA), Kings Plaza (Brooklyn), Fashion Outlets of Chicago, and Washington Square (OR). Twelve centers sit in unconsolidated joint ventures (many 50/50 with institutional partners), so headline consolidated financials understate the economic footprint; management reports pro-rata figures to bridge the gap, and those are the correct basis for analysis.

The company launched its "Path Forward Plan" in Q2 2024 under CEO Jack Hsieh. The plan has four operating levers: raise permanent physical occupancy toward 88-89%, drive leasing volume and releasing spreads, prune non-core assets (including strategic non-recourse mortgage defaults on impaired properties), and grind down leverage. The strategic edge is irreplaceable real estate in wealthy trade areas where retailers compete for space; the structural liability is leverage inherited from the 2015-era peak and the retail-apocalypse decade.

## Financial Analysis

The standardized data feed distorts Macerich's top line (a "revenue" figure of $63M is an artifact); actual leasing revenue runs ~$950M+ with total revenues near $1.0B. For a REIT with this depreciation load, FFO and NOI are the correct operating metrics, not GAAP net income.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FFO as adjusted | $413M | $365M | $397M (+8.7%) | $92.4M ($0.34/sh) |
| GAAP net loss (to Co.) | –$274M | –$194M | –$197M | –$36.4M |
| Go-Forward NOI (ex-term.) | — | $716.7M | $729.8M (+1.8%) | +1.2% y/y |
| Portfolio occupancy | — | 94.1% | 94.0% | 93.4% |
| Sales/sq ft (<10k) | — | $837 | $881 | $899 |
| Leasing spread (TTM) | — | — | +6.7% | — |

Leasing is the operational bright spot. Macerich signed 1,199 leases / 7.1M sq ft in 2025 (+85% sq ft year over year), including 470k+ sq ft of experiential/dining/new-to-portfolio uses. Operating cash flow was $322M in 2025 (up $38M), and management expects to cover operating expenses, capex, leasing costs, and the dividend from cash flow in 2026, a meaningful self-sufficiency statement.

The balance sheet is the dominant variable. Pro-rata debt carries a 5.27% weighted-average rate, ~91% fixed, on a 3.23-year weighted-average maturity. Interest expense rose $63.6M in 2025, and management warns of higher interest on refinancings as below-market legacy loans reset. The reported Altman Z-score of 0.67 ("distress zone") and D/E of 2.39 flag leverage as the key risk but should be read with a REIT caveat: Altman is calibrated to industrials, and real-estate depreciation depresses book equity in a way that overstates distress. The non-recourse structure of the property debt is a genuine, underappreciated downside buffer: Macerich can surgically hand back impaired assets (Santa Monica Place, Twenty Ninth Street) without endangering the corporate entity.

Liquidity improved materially in early 2026. The company upsized its revolver to $900M, extended it to 2030, and cut pricing to 180-220 bps over SOFR from 200-250, a lender vote of confidence, with ~$780M total liquidity and ~$650M revolver availability as of the May filing. This de-risks the near term even as the short maturity ladder keeps refinancing constant (Fashion Outlets of Niagara matures Oct'26, Fresno Fashion Fair Nov'26).

The financial-health score of 4.5 reflects the tension: genuine cash-flow self-sufficiency and a covered dividend against elevated leverage, a short maturity ladder, and persistent GAAP losses. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator in our framework, and here the elevated-leverage profile keeps it below neutral.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth engine is the SNO rent pipeline: leases signed 2024-2028 that generate incremental rent as tenants open, above prior uses. All 30 anchor-repositioning deals are committed. This is the fuel for management's back-half FFO acceleration: they target ≥3% Go-Forward NOI growth in 2026 with growth "accelerating meaningfully in 2027 and 2028," alongside physical permanent occupancy of 88-89%.

Estimate revisions corroborate the operating momentum. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +31% over 90 days, next-quarter +52%, and next-year estimates flipped from ~$0.00 to +$0.11. The base is a low, near-breakeven GAAP figure, so the percentage moves overstate the fundamental change; the more useful reading is that consensus is drifting toward positive per-share earnings for the first time in the cycle.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, the plan's headline goal is deleveraging, yet Macerich just deployed ~$562M on two debt/cash-funded acquisitions (Crabtree Mall, $290M, Jun 2025; Annapolis Mall, ~$272M including a $12M Sears parcel, Apr 2026), modestly re-levering while claiming to deleverage. The bull read is that these are accretive to 2028 FFO targets and that EBITDA growth handles the deleveraging; the bear read is that they lean on dilutive equity and new debt. Second, equity dilution is structural: a $454M offering (Nov 2024 at $19.75), plus $54M of ATM issuance in 2025 and $86M in Q1'26, pushed share count from ~215M in 2022 to ~272M common+units. Absolute FFO can grow while per-share FFO upside is capped. Growth prospects score 6.0: a real, contractually-anchored acceleration, but one gated by execution timing (openings can slip on construction delays or tenant bankruptcies) and diluted per share.

## Valuation Assessment

Retail-REIT peers trade at a P/E median of 22.5 with a 4.0% yield (KRG 21.8x/4.0%, NNN 22.4x/5.2%, BRX 22.5x/3.9%, ADC 40.4x/4.2%, PECO 45.3x/3.1%). GAAP P/E is meaningless for Macerich given negative earnings, so the relevant frames are FFO multiple, NAV, and yield.

On FFO, at ~$1.36 adjusted run-rate the stock trades around 19x FFO, not obviously cheap for a REIT at this leverage. On yield, the 2.7% payout sits well below the peer median, reflecting a deliberately low dividend held down to fund deleveraging and capex; income buyers are under-served relative to peers. On NAV, the debate is live and matters: one analyst pegs NAV near $15.42, roughly 40% below the current price, while BofA upgraded to Buy in September 2025 on the growth/deleveraging path. The gap reflects how much credit one gives the SNO pipeline and the 2028 targets.

The critical fact for a valuation call is that the discount has largely closed. The stock now trades near the $26.50 median consensus target, inside a $19-$30 range. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors, but here the multiple is not cheap relative to the leverage-adjusted growth, and the stock is already at consensus rather than below it. Cheapness, in our experience, is a weak buy signal in isolation and often a value-trap flag; in MAC's case the fundamentals are improving but the price has moved ahead of them. The valuation score is 4.5: the asymmetry that existed in the high-teens has compressed, and the current price embeds substantial credit for a 2027-2028 execution story that has not yet shown up in reported per-share results.

## Competitive Landscape

Macerich's competitive position is improving, and this is the dimension where the trajectory is clearest. It competes with Simon Property Group (larger, lower-levered), Brookfield-owned malls, Tanger, and private owners. Its edge is sales productivity per square foot ($899-$941) that rivals the best Class-A peers; 17 consecutive quarters of positive releasing spreads (+6.7% TTM) confirm real pricing power at its specific centers. Tenant bankruptcies are running below the decade average, Class-A occupancy and rents are firming, and limited new retail supply supports pricing in premium trade areas, a materially more favorable industry backdrop than the 2018-2021 mall bear case.

The relative weakness remains balance-sheet leverage well above Simon's, which constrains Macerich's ability to play offense on the same terms. Fresh swing factors include tariffs (tenant margins on imported goods, construction-cost inflation on the redevelopment pipeline) and secular e-commerce pressure, partly offset by the experiential/dining pivot. A productive Class-A portfolio gaining leasing momentum with firming spreads warrants a 6.5, consistent with the prior report's upgrade.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing** is the number-one risk. At 7.76x net debt/EBITDA with a 3.2-year average maturity, Macerich perpetually refinances individual mortgages into higher rates; interest expense rose $63.6M in 2025 and management flags further increases on refis.

**Strategic mortgage defaults** are explicitly part of the plan, an unusual feature. Santa Monica Place ($300M non-recourse, defaulted Apr 2024, in receivership), Twenty Ninth Street JV ($76.5M at share, default Feb 2026, in negotiations), and Country Club Plaza (debt forgiven 2024) signal which assets management views as impaired. Because the debt is non-recourse, downside is contained to individual assets, which is a structural buffer, but the pattern flags asset-level stress.

**Tenant bankruptcies** ran to 8 in 2025 (Forever 21, Claire's, Express, ~873k sq ft, ~$12.9M annual rent at share), with Saks Global filing in early 2026; co-tenancy clauses could cascade, though management says the impact is not significant. **Geographic concentration** in CA/NY/AZ carries state-specific tax, regulatory, and natural-disaster exposure (CA earthquake insurance carries a $100M aggregate limit with 5% deductibles). **Execution risk** on the ~$459-505M redevelopment pipeline is real, particularly the mixed-use/residential FlatIron Crossing bet where management has "less experience." **Dilution** remains an ongoing overhang on per-share metrics.

The idiosyncratic risk cluster (leverage, short maturity ladder, ongoing asset-level defaults, and dilution) is only partly priced, warranting a risk score of 4.5. The non-recourse structure and covered dividend prevent a lower mark.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not confirm the elevated 11.1% short interest. Near-term ATM IV is 39.4% versus 30.7% one-year historical vol, modestly rich, pricing an ±8.5% implied move, but the put/call volume ratio is 0.06 and OI ratio 0.01, both extremely call-tilted. ATM IV has collapsed from 141.4% in mid-June to 39.4%, indicating a resolved event (the June price spike/reversal). The 3-month tenor shows a negative –16.3% put skew, suggesting some hedging demand at a specific expiry, but the overall picture is neutral-to-constructive rather than fearful. The 5.1 days-to-cover short interest creates modest squeeze potential but is not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$30):** The SNO pipeline converts on schedule, driving Go-Forward NOI growth from ~3% in 2026 to a meaningful acceleration in 2027-2028. Occupancy climbs to 88-89% permanent, releasing spreads stay positive, EBITDA growth pulls net debt/EBITDA toward 6.5-7x, and the market re-rates the stock closer to a productive Class-A peer multiple as the balance-sheet overhang eases. BofA's Buy thesis plays out and the stock reaches the $30 high target.

**Bear Case (~$19):** SNO tenant openings slip on construction delays or tenant bankruptcies, delaying the FFO acceleration. Refinancing into higher rates erodes the per-share benefit of NOI growth, additional strategic defaults surface, and the market anchors toward the ~$15.42 NAV estimate. The stock retraces toward the $19 low analyst target (~26% downside from current) as the leverage story reasserts itself.

**Base Case (~$26):** Leasing momentum and lender confidence hold, the dividend stays covered, and deleveraging grinds forward, but the ~46% run has already priced most of the near-term progress. The stock trades roughly flat-to-modestly-higher near consensus as investors wait for the 2027-2028 acceleration to appear in reported per-share results.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as **uncertain**: the operating turnaround is genuine but the entry price has closed the asymmetry, and the 2027-2028 thesis is not yet confirmed in reported per-share earnings.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the high-teens to low-$20s that restores a discount to the growth/deleveraging path (a re-entry closer to the $19.75 Nov-2024 offering level would materially improve risk/reward).
- Net debt/EBITDA declining below ~7.0x over the next 2-4 quarters without further dilutive equity issuance, confirming the deleveraging scorecard.
- Evidence that SNO tenants are opening on schedule and converting to paid rent, showing up in accelerating Go-Forward NOI toward the 2027-2028 targets.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net debt/EBITDA rising above ~8.0x, or a materially dilutive equity raise to fund acquisitions rather than EBITDA growth doing the deleveraging.
- SNO pipeline openings slipping materially (construction delays or a wave of tenant bankruptcies impairing signed leases).
- A failed or punitively-priced refinancing on a major near-term maturity (Fashion Outlets of Niagara Oct'26, Fresno Fashion Fair Nov'26), or a cascade of co-tenancy-driven closures.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the net-debt/EBITDA trajectory over the following four quarters are the real scorecard. Watch the Twenty Ninth Street default resolution and the H2 2026 refinancing cadence as near-term checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Macerich is a high-quality-asset, high-leverage turnaround executing credibly against its Path Forward Plan. Leasing spreads, sales productivity, a covered dividend funded from cash flow, and a cheaper/upsized revolver all confirm the operating story is real, and the SNO pipeline gives the 2027-2028 FFO acceleration a contractual anchor. The competitive position is improving and the industry backdrop is more favorable than at any point since the mall bear market.

The reason we stop short of BUY is entry price, not thesis. The stock has appreciated ~46% over the past year to ~$25.83, trading near consensus targets, well above credible NAV estimates, and at ~19x FFO that is not cheap for a REIT at this leverage. The residual asymmetry sits between a ~$30 bull case (~16% upside) and a ~$19 bear case (~26% downside), with the leverage, short maturity ladder, and ongoing dilution keeping the downside tail live. We recommend HOLD, with a path to upgrade on a pullback into the low-$20s or confirmed deleveraging below 7.0x, and to downgrade if leverage rises or the SNO conversion slips.
