## Executive Summary

FTI Consulting is a high-quality expert consulting franchise trading at $184.23, near its 52-week high of $187.85. The company delivered record Q4 2025 revenue of $990.7M (+10.7% YoY) and beat earnings estimates in all four quarters of FY2025 by an average of ~21.7%. Three of five segments — Corporate Finance, FLC, and Strategic Communications — performed exceptionally well, generating $135M of incremental EBITDA. However, a critical disruption at Compass Lexecon (Economic Consulting) destroyed ~$84M of EBITDA, and operating cash flow collapsed 61.5% to $152M as the firm issued $255M in forgivable retention loans.

The balance sheet shifted from net cash to $365M in revolver borrowings, funding $858.6M in share repurchases (15% of float) while generating only $93.6M in free cash flow. Management guided FY2026 revenue of $3.94B–$4.10B (+6.1% midpoint) and EPS of $8.90–$9.50, explicitly stating they are "not yet back to forecasting double-digit EPS growth." With the stock at ~22x trailing/forward earnings, an interim CFO, Economic Consulting EBITDA expected to trough in Q1 2026, and options markets pricing elevated near-term stress (48.3% IV vs. 24.6% HV), the stock prices a recovery that has yet to materialize in the numbers.

## Company Overview

FTI Consulting operates five segments serving crisis, litigation, and transformation situations globally. Corporate Finance (41% of revenue) leads in restructuring and turnaround advisory. FLC (20%) covers investigations, cybersecurity, and dispute advisory. Economic Consulting (19%) houses subsidiary Compass Lexecon, providing expert economic testimony for antitrust and competition matters. Technology (10%) delivers e-discovery and AI-powered legal tools. Strategic Communications (10%) handles crisis and financial communications.

The firm employs 8,118 people (6,421 billable) across 104+ offices in 30+ countries. Revenue splits 63% U.S. / 37% international. No single client exceeds 10% of revenue. FTI advises 95 of the Fortune 100 and is referred by 99 of the top 100 global law firms.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth Trajectory

Revenue growth decelerated sharply from +15.2% (FY2023) to +6.0% (FY2024) to +2.4% (FY2025, $3.79B). The FY2025 headline masks a divergent segment story:

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | YoY Growth | Adj. EBITDA Margin |
|---------|---------------|------------|-------------------|
| Corporate Finance | $1.55B | +11.5% | 20.3% |
| FLC | $764M | +10.8% | 17.7% |
| Strategic Comms | $378M | +12.6% | 17.8% |
| Economic Consulting | $723M | -16.5% | 3.5% |
| Technology | $374M | -10.5% | 12.1% |

The three growth segments collectively generated +28–56% EBITDA growth. Economic Consulting's EBITDA collapse from $109.5M to $25.1M (margin compressed to 3.5%) offset most of this gain. Q4 2025 showed initial recovery breadth: Technology's H2 EBITDA was +69% vs. H1, and total company revenue hit a record.

### Profitability

Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.8% to $463.6M (12.2% margin, +130bps). GAAP net income declined 3.3% to $270.9M, but EPS rose 5.5% to $8.24 due to an ~8.3% reduction in diluted shares. The effective tax rate spiked from 20.2% to 27.0%, creating a meaningful EPS headwind that should partially reverse in FY2026 (guided 22–24%).

### Cash Flow — The Most Concerning Signal

Operating cash flow declined 61.5% to $152.1M. Free cash flow was $93.6M (2.5% margin vs. 9.7% in FY2024). The primary driver was $255M in net forgivable loan issuances ($162M in Q1 alone) to retain talent during the Compass Lexecon crisis. The firm funded $858.6M in buybacks while generating $93.6M in FCF, drawing $365M on its revolver.

FCF conversion (FCF/Net Income) fell from ~129% to ~35%. This warrants monitoring: if forgivable loan issuances remain elevated in FY2026 (management plans continued senior hiring plus increased junior hiring), FCF may not recover proportionally with earnings.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shifted from zero long-term debt to $365M outstanding on a $900M revolving facility ($535M remaining capacity). Net debt is ~$100M (~0.22x EBITDA). Altman Z-Score of 2.93 is in the grey zone but reflects the asset-light services model rather than genuine distress. Current ratio declined from 1.95x to 1.56x. Goodwill is $1.24B (~72% of equity), typical for a professional services acquirer.

The trajectory from net cash to leveraged is manageable but reduces strategic flexibility for acquisitions and limits buyback capacity without further debt.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance implies ~6% revenue growth (midpoint $4.02B) and ~12% EPS growth (midpoint $9.20). The recovery thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Compass Lexecon stabilization.** Management expects Economic Consulting EBITDA to trough in Q1 2026 and stop being a drag by H2 2026. They have been actively hiring academic economists and antitrust experts, but explicitly acknowledged the revenue ramp is "slow" because new academic hires "are not aggressive business developers."

2. **Tax rate normalization.** The FY2025 effective rate of 27% was elevated by ~5pp due to unfavorable share-based compensation tax benefits. The guided FY2026 rate of 22–24% provides a mechanical EPS tailwind.

3. **Continued strength in Corporate Finance, FLC, and Strategic Communications.** These segments collectively grew 11–13% in FY2025 and appear well-positioned for continued demand from restructuring activity, investigations, and crisis communications.

Consensus estimates project $9.28 for FY2026 and $11.07 for FY2027, implying a return to double-digit EPS growth in FY2027. Estimate revisions are stable — no meaningful upward or downward momentum over 30 days, though current-quarter estimates drifted -5.2% over 90 days, consistent with the expected Q1 2026 trough.

The 4-quarter earnings surprise track record (avg ~21.7%, 4/4 beats) provides confidence that management guidance tends to be conservative. However, FY2026 guidance explicitly flagged that the firm is "not yet back to forecasting double-digit EPS growth," which is unusually cautious for a management team with a strong beat history.

## Valuation Assessment

At $184.23, FCN trades at approximately:
- 22.4x trailing EPS ($8.24)
- 19.8x FY2026E consensus ($9.28)
- 16.6x FY2027E consensus ($11.07)

Against the peer median P/E of 32.9x, FCN appears inexpensive. However, the peer set is heterogeneous: BAH at 12.3x reflects government consulting headwinds, while LZ at 79.2x is a growth-stage company. The more relevant comparison is Exponent (EXPO) at 32.9x, which operates a similar high-margin expert consulting model but with no debt, higher margins, and less volatile segment dynamics.

FCN's discount to EXPO is partially justified by: (1) the Compass Lexecon disruption, (2) the shift from net cash to net debt, (3) FCF conversion collapse, and (4) the CFO vacancy. A 20x multiple on $9.28 FY2026E yields ~$186, approximately equal to the current price.

The stock trades at 98% of its 52-week high ($187.85), leaving limited upside to the analyst consensus high target of $188. The mean analyst target is $176.50, below the current price. With only 3 analysts covering the stock (1 Buy, 2 Hold), sparse coverage limits price discovery efficiency but also reduces the risk of a sell-side downgrade wave.

Buyback support provides a floor: at current prices, the remaining cash position and revolving capacity would support additional repurchases, and management has demonstrated willingness to buy aggressively during perceived dislocations.

## Competitive Landscape

FTI's competitive position is strongest in restructuring (Corporate Finance) and litigation consulting (FLC), where it holds #1 or #2 positions in more global markets than any competitor. The firm was named "Global Turnaround Consulting Firm of the Year" for the 10th time. This franchise is durable: restructuring advisory relationships are built on trust and track record, with high switching costs.

Economic Consulting's competitive position deteriorated in 2025 due to the Compass Lexecon talent departures. The antitrust economics expert market is concentrated — a small number of senior economists generate disproportionate revenue through expert testimony — and losing key individuals directly impairs the business. Management's response (aggressive hiring, retention loans) is appropriate but takes 12–18 months to translate to revenue.

Technology faces competitive pressure from larger IT firms and rapid AI evolution. The IQ.AI platform is differentiated, but e-discovery and legal technology are becoming increasingly commoditized.

The natural hedge across the five segments — restructuring thrives in distress while M&A-related consulting thrives in growth — provides cycle diversification. FY2025 demonstrated this: Corp Finance's restructuring practice benefited from Spirit Airlines, Sunnova Energy, and Wolfspeed engagements while Econ Consulting and Tech suffered from lower M&A activity.

## Risk Assessment

### Compass Lexecon Talent Disruption (Critical)
The departure of senior economists from Compass Lexecon is not fully resolved. New academic hires have slow revenue ramps and the U.S. antitrust practice remains weak. If the recovery takes longer than expected, FY2026 guidance is at risk.

### Cash Flow Quality Deterioration
Forgivable loan issuances are both necessary (talent retention) and costly. If the FY2025 run rate continues, the firm's ability to fund buybacks from operations — rather than debt — is impaired.

### CFO Vacancy
An interim CFO during a period of increased leverage, aggressive buybacks, and a complex segment recovery adds execution risk. No timeline for a permanent appointment has been disclosed.

### AI Disruption
Management frames AI as net positive (new dispute categories, investigation complexity). The risk factors acknowledge that "some non-expert services and tasks currently performed by our professionals have been and will continue to be replaced by automation." Higher-value expert testimony and advisory work has a longer runway before AI displacement, but lower-leverage Technology segment tasks are more vulnerable.

### M&A Activity Sensitivity
Two of five segments (Economic Consulting and Technology) depend materially on M&A-related antitrust enforcement (HSR Act second requests). Regulatory regime changes and M&A cycle downturns create revenue volatility outside management's control.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 48.3% is nearly double the 1-year historical volatility of 24.6%. The term structure is in backwardation (48.3% near-term vs. 31.7% at 6-month), which typically signals imminent stress expectations. Put/call skew at 9.0% shows elevated put demand. With Q1 2026 expected to be the Compass Lexecon EBITDA trough and the next earnings report likely in late April/May, the options market appears to be hedging a potential earnings disappointment. This diverges from the stock's proximity to 52-week highs and warrants caution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$210–$220, 14–19% upside)
- Compass Lexecon recovers faster than guided; H2 2026 EBITDA approaches $60–$70M (vs. $25M in FY2025)
- Tax rate normalizes to 22%, adding ~$0.40 EPS mechanically
- Corporate Finance and FLC maintain double-digit growth on continued restructuring demand
- FY2026 EPS reaches $9.50+ (top of guidance), FY2027 consensus of $11+ is achieved
- Multiple re-rates to 20–22x forward on improved growth visibility → $220–$244 on FY2027E
- Buyback support continues to compress the share count 4–6% annually

### Bear Case (~$145–$155, 16–21% downside)
- Compass Lexecon recovery stalls; additional talent departures or slower-than-expected revenue ramp from new hires
- Restructuring cycle peaks as M&A activity and corporate distress normalize
- Forgivable loans remain elevated, suppressing FCF and requiring additional debt
- AI displacement accelerates in Technology segment, compressing margins
- Stock de-rates to 16–17x on earnings stagnation → $148–$158 on FY2026E

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April/May 2026):** Economic Consulting segment EBITDA stabilizes above $10M quarterly, confirming management's trough call. Meaningful positive surprise would be $15M+.
2. **Permanent CFO appointment** with a credible track record.
3. **FCF conversion recovers above 70%** for any trailing 2-quarter period, indicating forgivable loan issuance normalization.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Additional senior talent departures** from Compass Lexecon or other segments, indicating the retention crisis is spreading.
2. **FY2026 guidance cut** below $8.50 EPS, indicating the recovery thesis has failed.
3. **Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 1.5x** without a corresponding strategic acquisition, indicating buybacks are further leveraging the balance sheet during a period of FCF weakness.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint is Q1 2026 earnings (late April/May 2026). The Compass Lexecon EBITDA trough confirmation, permanent CFO appointment status, and initial FY2026 cash flow data will all be visible at that point.

## Conclusion

FTI Consulting is a premier expert consulting franchise with durable competitive advantages in restructuring and litigation consulting. The Compass Lexecon disruption is likely temporary, and management's aggressive buyback at an average cost of $163 per share may ultimately prove well-timed. However, at $184 — near the 52-week high and above the analyst consensus mean of $176.50 — the stock prices in a recovery not yet confirmed in the financials. The appropriate posture is to wait for Q1 2026 earnings to confirm the Compass Lexecon trough before committing capital. HOLD.
