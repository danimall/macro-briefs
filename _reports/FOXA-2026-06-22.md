**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fox Corporation operates a deliberately narrow, high-margin media franchise built around the two content categories least vulnerable to cord-cutting: live opinion-driven news (FOX News, the #1 cable network in primetime for ten consecutive years) and live sports (NFL, World Cup, college football, the rotating Super Bowl). The business generated $16.3B of revenue in FY25 (+17% YoY), $3.62B of Adjusted EBITDA, and ~$3.0B of free cash flow at an 18.4% margin. Management has returned that cash aggressively, buying back roughly 36% of the share count since the 2019 21CF/Disney spin, with a $12B authorization ($3.5B remaining as of the Q3 10-Q). At a ~$9.8B market cap on ~$3.0B of annual FCF, the headline cash-return math is compelling.

The complicating factor is a sharp recent drawdown. FOXA fell from a January high of $76.39 to $52.23, a 32% decline, with most of the damage concentrated in mid-June 2026 on extraordinary volume (33.6M shares June 15 vs. a 3.9M average). The reported financials are noisy in ways that flatter the bears: a negative GAAP "operating margin" and grey-zone Altman Z-Score are presentation artifacts (D&A and a $350M restructuring line sit below operating income), and the GAAP net-income drop is driven almost entirely by a -$776M nine-month mark-to-market loss on the Flutter equity stake, not operations. Strip those out and operating Adjusted EBITDA grew ~11% in the March quarter to a record fiscal-Q3 $954M.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the Smartmatic defamation suit. FOX faces a $2.7B claim with no reserve booked, summary judgment argued December 2025, and trial expected later in 2026. The company settled the comparable Dominion matter for ~$800M in 2023. The operational picture points up across nearly every line, but an unreserved multibillion-dollar litigation tail heading to trial is a genuine binary that the cheap multiple is partly compensating for. Until that resolves or the price discounts a materially worse outcome, the asymmetry is balanced.

## Company Overview

Fox is a U.S.-focused news, sports, and entertainment company with a June 30 fiscal year. It reports two segments plus corporate:

- **Cable Network Programming (~44% of revenue):** FOX News, FOX Business, FS1, FS2, Big Ten Network (61%-owned), FOX Deportes. This is the cash engine, with Cable segment EBITDA of $3.03B in FY25 at roughly a 44% margin. FOX News finished FY25 as the #1 cable network overall in primetime and total day for the tenth straight year.
- **Television (~56% of revenue):** The FOX broadcast network (NFL, MLB, college football, NASCAR, the rotating Super Bowl, the 2026 FIFA Men's World Cup), 29 owned-and-operated stations covering 14 of the top-15 DMAs, and Tubi, the leading ad-supported streaming service (~2.2% of all US TV viewing, ~11B hours streamed, ~100M MAUs).
- **Corporate & Other:** Houses FOX One (the DTC streaming service launched August 2025), Credible (66%-owned consumer finance marketplace), and the FOX Studio Lot.

Revenue splits roughly 47% affiliate/distribution, 42% advertising, 11% other. The strategic thesis is to own the content with the least substitutability rather than chase scripted-streaming scale. Two off-balance-sheet assets carry optionality: a tax step-up asset from the 2019 spin generating cash-tax benefits for years, and a 2.5% Flutter stake plus a call option to acquire 18.6% of FanDuel at a ~$4.5B strike (expires December 2030).

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (audited):** Revenue $16.3B (+17%), boosted by Super Bowl LIX and 2024 election political advertising. Net income attributable to Fox $2.26B (+51%); Adjusted EBITDA $3.62B (+26%); operating cash flow $3.32B; FCF $2.99B. Balance sheet is sound: current ratio 2.91, cash $5.4B against $6.6B long-term debt (modest net leverage), investment-grade at Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB, both stable.

**FY2026 nine months (ended 3/31/26):** Revenue $12.91B (-1% YoY), a planned down-year that laps the absent Super Bowl (~$800M+ gross ad revenue) and 2024 election cycle. Ex-Super Bowl, advertising grew double digits per management. Q3 FY26 revenue was $4.0B with Adjusted EBITDA +11% to a record fiscal-Q3 $954M and Adjusted EPS $1.32 (+20%). Reported GAAP net income fell to $166M from $346M, almost entirely on the -$496M quarterly (-$776M YTD) Flutter mark-to-market; the Flutter carrying value dropped from $1.25B to $458M over nine months. This is non-operating noise that should be normalized.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces the case that operations are healthy: four consecutive beats, by 33.9%, 61.7%, 37.4%, and 25.8%. That magnitude of beat consistency suggests consensus has been chronically conservative on FOX's appointment-content monetization.

Capital allocation dominates the cash story. Fox repurchased $1.9B of stock in the first nine months of FY26, including a $1.5B accelerated repurchase at ~$59–67/share, and raised the authorization to $12B in August 2025. Cash declined from $5.4B to $3.6B largely to fund buybacks. The semi-annual dividend was raised to $0.28/share, well-covered at a ~1.1% yield. Buybacks mechanically lift the Murdoch family trust's voting percentage, though that is capped by the September 2025 stockholders agreement.

The financial-health score of 7.0 reflects genuine cash generation, investment-grade ratings, and a clean balance sheet, balanced against the cash drawdown to fund buybacks and the single unreserved litigation contingency that no leverage ratio captures.

## Growth Analysis

FOX's reported growth is distorted by its biennial cadence: Super Bowl and election years inflate even-numbered fiscal periods, leaving the off-years optically flat. On a normalized basis the underlying trends are constructive but not high-growth:

- **2026 upfront** closed first in the industry with high-single-digit volume growth and market-leading pricing; FOX News CPMs up >45%, 500+ new advertising clients added over two years.
- **FIFA Men's World Cup (Summer 2026):** 104 matches, the most ever on US broadcast TV, spanning fiscal Q4'26 and Q1'27. Management guides it EBITDA-accretive company-wide and a customer-acquisition catalyst for FOX One and Tubi.
- **Midterm political advertising:** management cites third-party estimates of an ~$11B political ad market, a midterm record, with FOX positioned via stations in battleground states.
- **FOX One** (launched August 2025) is exceeding expectations, with churn "much lower than expected" and over 50% of usage being news, pointing to durable, low-churn subscribers rather than sports-event transients. Launch costs (~$155M YTD) depressed corporate EBITDA near-term.
- **Tubi** reached breakeven-or-better for three consecutive quarters, revenue +23%, view time +19%.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not the >30% jump that signals a strong buy: current-year EPS revised +6.0% over 90 days to $4.92, next-year +9.9% to $5.67, with near-term quarters roughly flat. The pattern reads as stable-to-improving rather than accelerating. The structural headwind remains MVPD subscriber erosion of ~6.5% per year (FOX News fell from 67M to 61M subs YoY), which affiliate-rate increases must outrun. Growth prospects score 5.8: a solid, cash-rich franchise with real near-term catalysts but a low secular ceiling on its core distribution base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.23, FOXA trades at roughly 10.6x current-year consensus EPS of $4.92 and ~9.2x next-year $5.67, with a ~1.1% dividend yield. Against the broadcasting peer set, the picture is mixed: the peer median P/E is 16.3 (FOX Class B 16.0, TGNA 16.3, NXST 36.6, WBD negative). FOXA's Class A shares trade at a discount to its own Class B (which carries the votes), reflecting the near-total absence of voting rights on this security plus the recent drawdown.

The cheapness is real on a FCF basis: ~$3.0B of annual FCF against a ~$9.8B market cap is a ~31% FCF yield on the trailing peak year, or comfortably double-digit on a normalized off-year. The aggressive buyback compounds per-share value at these levels. But I am deliberately not rewarding the low multiple as a clean buy signal. Cheapness without a catalyst has been a poor predictor, and here the discount is doing real work: it partly prices the unreserved Smartmatic tail. A 10x multiple on a high-FCF media franchise is not anomalously cheap once a multibillion-dollar contingent liability with a 2026 trial date is factored in.

Analyst targets span $54 (low) to $97 (high), mean $71.40, implying substantial upside if litigation resolves favorably. I treat that mean cautiously: it likely embeds little probability weight on an adverse Smartmatic verdict, so it functions more as a "litigation-clears" scenario than a base case. The valuation score of 6.3 reflects asymmetric upside if the binary breaks right, tempered by the recognition that the multiple is compensating for a real tail rather than mispricing a clean business.

## Competitive Landscape

Fox competes against far larger, vertically integrated peers: Disney/ESPN, Comcast/NBCU, Warner Bros. Discovery, Paramount, and the streaming giants. Its strategy of owning the most non-substitutable live content is a defensible niche, since news and sports are the genres that retain linear-TV pricing power and migrate intact into skinny bundles and DTC. The discontinuation of the Venu Sports JV with Disney/WBD removed a shared-streaming venture and pushed FOX toward its own FOX One platform, now the primary distribution hedge against MVPD erosion.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. FOX News retains the #1 position and is raising CPMs, evidence of widening pricing power in its core. Tubi is gaining viewing share and turning profitable. Against that, the cable subscriber base is eroding, and sports-rights inflation is structural: ~$34B of programming and firm commitments, with the NFL deal (through ~2033, but with a one-time NFL termination right after the 2029 season) the largest single dependency. Management confirmed on the Q3 call that there are no substantive NFL renewal discussions yet despite press reports of tension over streaming migration; this is a multi-year watch item, not a near-term threat. The ~$22B Roku distribution deal (June 2026; single-source press report, terms to be confirmed against the 8-K exhibit) extends owned distribution for Tubi and FOX One. FOX is gaining share in its chosen niches while its legacy distribution base shrinks, a stable-to-modestly-improving position that earns a 6.2.

## Risk Assessment

**Smartmatic ($2.7B defamation claim) — the dominant tail risk.** Summary judgment was argued December 2, 2025; trial expected later in 2026 at the earliest. FOX has booked no reserve, stating a loss is neither probable nor reasonably estimable, while explicitly warning an adverse outcome could be materially adverse. The Dominion precedent (~$800M settlement, April 2023) frames the order of magnitude. Related Delaware derivative suits survived a motion to dismiss. This single contingency is the reason the risk score is 4.2: a genuine binary with multibillion-dollar downside that the balance sheet does not absorb cleanly given the cash drawdown to fund buybacks.

**Secular MVPD decline** (~6.5%/year) requires affiliate rate increases to outrun subscriber losses; FOX One is the strategic hedge but is not yet at scale.

**Sports-rights cost inflation and the NFL termination right** after 2029 introduce a discrete renegotiation risk in the medium term.

**FCC/regulatory exposure:** a $70M license impairment in FY25, licenses in some markets exceeding fair value by <10%, and a license-renewal cycle beginning June 2028. The national ownership cap is under review, which cuts both ways (could enable station M&A).

**Governance:** the dual-class structure leaves Class A holders with almost no voting rights; the family controls 43.9% of Class B votes. The September 2025 LGC Holdco trust restructuring resolved the long-running succession dispute by consolidating control under Lachlan Murdoch, a genuine de-risking, but the related-party conflict via Lachlan's simultaneous News Corp chairmanship persists.

### Options Market Signal

The options market reads as elevated-but-balanced uncertainty rather than directional panic. Near-term ATM IV of 42.3% is rich versus 34.2% one-year historical vol (an 8.1% spread), pricing a ±8.7% implied move on the nearest expiry, but the IV percentile within the current chain is only 22% and the term structure is flat (near 42.3% ≈ far 42.6%). Put/call skew is roughly neutral (+0.5% near-term, drifting to -2.9% at six months), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.24 is notably low, not the heavy put demand one would expect if the market were pricing an imminent adverse litigation outcome. The 16.1% short interest with 9.2 days to cover is meaningful institutional bearishness and creates modest squeeze potential on any favorable resolution. The combination of rich IV but balanced skew, low put volume, and elevated SI suggests the market is pricing event uncertainty (Smartmatic, World Cup execution) symmetrically rather than betting on a directional collapse. The June volume spike coincided with the price break and likely reflected the same litigation/macro repricing rather than new fundamental information.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$72–85+):** Smartmatic settles for a manageable sum or FOX prevails, removing the overhang and triggering a re-rate toward the peer-median multiple and the analyst mean. World Cup and midterm advertising drive a strong FY26 second half; FOX One scales with low churn; Tubi compounds profitability; the ~$3.5B remaining buyback authorization retires more stock at depressed prices, compounding per-share FCF. The FanDuel option moves into the money. On normalized ~$5.67 next-year EPS at a 14–15x multiple, $80–85 is reachable; the $97 analyst high requires both litigation clearance and full re-rating.

**Bear case (~$38–43):** An adverse Smartmatic verdict in the Dominion range or worse forces a multibillion-dollar charge against a balance sheet thinned by buybacks, alongside reputational and advertiser fallout. Cable subscriber erosion accelerates, FOX One stalls, and the NFL renegotiation turns hostile. The stock de-rates further toward distressed-media multiples. A bear outcome implies roughly 18–27% downside from current levels.

**Base case (~$58–62):** Operations deliver as guided through the World Cup and midterms, GAAP optics normalize as the Flutter mark stabilizes, and Smartmatic remains an open but unresolved overhang that keeps the multiple compressed. The buyback provides a per-share tailwind. A modest re-rate toward ~11–11.5x next-year EPS yields $58–62, roughly 11–19% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a balanced-asymmetry situation, not a confident growth or income hold. The franchise is cheap and well-run, but the dominant variable is a binary litigation event whose outcome I cannot handicap with conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Smartmatic resolution at a manageable cost: a settlement materially below the ~$800M Dominion figure, a favorable summary-judgment ruling, or a dismissal that removes the overhang.
- Price compression toward the low-$40s with fundamentals intact, which would price in a worse-than-Dominion outcome and restore clear asymmetry even on a bear litigation scenario.
- FOX One subscriber disclosure confirming scaled, low-churn additions that materially offset the cable erosion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse summary-judgment ruling or trial trajectory pointing toward a Dominion-or-larger liability with no reserve.
- A guidance cut or evidence that ex-Super-Bowl advertising growth has stalled, or accelerating MVPD subscriber losses without offsetting FOX One growth.
- A hostile NFL renewal posture or invocation of the post-2029 termination right.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoints are the FY26 Q4 print (covering the World Cup ramp, expected August 2026), midterm political-ad realization through late 2026, and any Smartmatic summary-judgment ruling or trial scheduling news, which can arrive at any point.

## Conclusion

Fox is a high-quality, cash-generative media franchise trading at a low-double-digit multiple on rising estimates, with a management team returning capital aggressively and a clear strategic hedge (FOX One, Tubi) against the secular decline in its legacy distribution base. The operational evidence (record fiscal-Q3 Adjusted EBITDA, four consecutive double-digit earnings beats, a strong upfront, World Cup and midterm catalysts) is uniformly constructive, and the GAAP optics that have spooked screening models (negative operating margin, grey-zone Z-score, falling net income) are presentation and mark-to-market artifacts rather than operating deterioration.

The reason to hold rather than buy is concentrated in one line item that does not appear on the balance sheet: an unreserved $2.7B Smartmatic claim heading toward a 2026 trial. The 32% drawdown and the ~10x multiple are partly compensation for that tail, not pure mispricing. If the litigation clears favorably, the analyst mean and the bull case toward $80+ are credible, and the buyback compounds the upside. If it breaks the wrong way, the downside to the high-$30s is real against a cash position thinned by repurchases. With the options market pricing the event symmetrically and the fundamentals pointing up, the prudent posture is to hold the cheap optionality while watching the litigation docket, and to upgrade if Smartmatic resolves or the price falls far enough to price in the worst case.
