**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest publicly traded crude and product tanker operators, with a 67-vessel fleet deployed roughly 82% into the spot market through six commercial pools. The investment case is straightforward to state and difficult to underwrite: the company is generating the strongest cash flow in its history on a tanker rate super-cycle that management itself attributes to geopolitics — Strait of Hormuz disruption, Russia/Ukraine ton-mile dislocation, Red Sea reroutings, and sanctions-driven fleet bifurcation. Q1 2026 produced adjusted EPS of $3.90 (GAAP $5.75) versus $1.00 a year earlier, with VLCC spot rates at $86,693/day against $33,531 in Q1 2025. The balance sheet is best-in-class for the sector: net loan-to-value below 7%, an Altman Z of 3.79, ~$918M of liquidity, and 31+ unencumbered vessels.

The earnings power is inseparable from the conflict driving it. The same rate spike that produced record results is the direct mechanical consequence of the Hormuz disruption premium that defines the current oil-shipping regime. A genuine, verified reopening of the strait compresses the disruption premium that INSW is monetizing; a grinding conflict sustains it; an escalation widens it further. The stock is a leveraged, two-sided play on a binary the broader market is actively reassessing. INSW has risen 115% over twelve months but has rolled over ~12% from its 52-week high of $92.66 reached in early May.

We rate INSW a HOLD. The fundamentals are excellent and the franchise is high-quality, but at 7.4x trailing earnings on peak-cycle EPS, with a ~17% global orderbook building, insiders selling into strength, and a $200M ATM program announced near cycle highs, the risk/reward is symmetric rather than asymmetric. The estimate revisions are extraordinary (+40% to current-year consensus over 90 days) but they are revisions to a cyclical peak that the company's own filings warn will normalize. This is a name to own through the cycle for the capital-return profile, not to add to at these levels.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates exclusively in the International Flag trade through two segments. Crude Tankers (52% of 2025 TCE revenue) comprises VLCCs, Suezmaxes, and Aframaxes, plus an asset-light U.S. Gulf lightering business. Product Carriers (48%) covers MRs, LR1s, and one LR2 carrying refined products. At Q1 2026 the fleet totaled 67 vessels (59 owned, 8 chartered-in, ~7.86M dwt including three LR1 newbuilds), down from 70 at year-end 2025 as the company sold older tonnage. Weighted-average fleet age is ~10.9 years.

The operating model relies on commercial pools — Tankers International, Maersk Suezmax, Panamax International, Clean Products Tankers Alliance, Norden, and Aframax International — which provide scale, utilization, and leverage to rate spikes. INSW outsources technical and commercial management to third parties, a hybrid structure that flexes with the cycle. In January 2026 the company bought CMB.Tech's 50% stake in the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool, becoming 100% owner and subsequently launching a new Suezmax pool. This vertical integration into pool management differentiates INSW from pure vessel-owner peers.

The company redomiciled to Bermuda in late 2025, addressing OECD Pillar Two global-minimum-tax exposure while preserving the international-shipping-income exclusion. It is incorporated under structures (formerly Marshall Islands) that carry weaker shareholder protections than U.S.-domiciled peers.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year revenue trend looks like decline but is cyclical normalization. Revenue fell from $1.07B (2023) to $952M (2024) to $843M (2025); net income from $556M to $417M to $309M. These are post-2022 rates reverting toward mid-cycle, not franchise deterioration. The 2025 operating margin held at 41%, net margin 36.7%, ROE 15.3%, ROA 11.6% — figures most industrials would envy.

Q1 2026 reversed the trend violently. Net income reached $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS) versus $50M ($1.00) a year earlier; adjusted net income $194M ($3.90); adjusted EBITDA $244M versus $91M. Shipping revenue rose to $325M from $183M. Fleet-wide spot TCE: VLCC $86,693, Suezmax $68,027, Aframax $51,379, LR1 $70,664, MR $37,224. This is the strongest underlying cash generation in company history, and it is mechanically a function of the rate environment.

The balance sheet is the clearest strength in the story. Altman Z of 3.79 sits in the safe zone; D/E is 0.32; current ratio 3.71. Net debt/total cap fell to 16.5% at year-end 2025 (from 22.2%), and net loan-to-value dropped below 7% by Q1 2026. Liquidity stood at ~$918M (Q1'26): ~$377M cash and short-term investments plus a $541M undrawn revolver. Over 31 vessels are unencumbered. Operating cash flow of $380M in 2025 exceeded net income of $309M, signaling high earnings quality.

Capital allocation has been disciplined and counter-cyclical. The company retired Ocean Yield lease financing by buying six VLCCs for $257.8M (eliminating ~$22M/year of mandatory amortization), funded with a $250M 7.125% 2030 unsecured bond. An ECA facility (DNB/K-Sure, SOFR+125bps) funds six LR1 newbuilds (~$359M program, two remaining to deliver by Q3 2026). In Q1 2026 the company sold seven of its oldest vessels (avg 17 years) for ~$216M, booking $88M of gains.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is cyclical, not secular, and that distinction governs the score. Current-year consensus EPS has risen +40.1% over 90 days to $13.90, current-quarter +352.6% to $5.68, and the company has beaten in four consecutive quarters (Q1'26 by 47.6%). Under normal circumstances, revisions of this velocity combined with revenue acceleration would be a strong forward signal.

The complication is what is being revised. These are upward revisions to a peak-cycle number that the company's own 10-K explicitly warns will normalize. The structure of the consensus is telling: current year at $13.90 but next year at only $6.42 — analysts are modeling a ~54% decline from the 2026 peak as the disruption premium fades. The market is not extrapolating the spike; it is pricing a roll-down. The growth thesis therefore is not "earnings compound from here" but "how long does the elevated rate environment persist before reverting." That is a duration-of-conflict question, not a business-quality question.

On the structural side, there are real tailwinds independent of the spike: limited shipyard capacity (yards booked with LNG, container, and bulk orders) has constrained tanker newbuilds and lifted secondhand values, supporting INSW's asset base and sale-and-purchase strategy. The Tankers International pool ownership deepens the commercial moat. But the swelling orderbook — now ~17% of the world fleet at year-end 2025, up from 14% in 2024 and 7% in 2023, with the crude orderbook up sharply (VLCC +19M dwt YoY) — is the medium-term counterweight that pressures rates precisely when demand normalizes.

Growth Prospects score: 5.0. The numbers are spectacular and the revisions enormous, but they describe a cyclical peak with a consensus-modeled decline next year, not durable forward growth.

## Valuation Assessment

INSW trades at ~7.4x trailing/forward earnings on $6.23 EPS, with a ~$4.0B market cap. Against the peer median P/E of 25.0 in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, this looks like a deep discount. It is not, and the reason is the denominator. The 7.4x multiple is computed on trailing earnings that include the rate spike. On consensus next-year EPS of $6.42 — analysts' estimate of normalized post-peak earnings — the stock trades at ~12.7x, which is the more honest cycle-adjusted multiple. That figure is roughly fair for a high-quality but deeply cyclical spot-exposed tanker operator, not cheap.

The peer comparison is also less clean than it appears. KNTK, AM, DTM, and VNOM are predominantly fee-based midstream names with contracted cash flows — businesses that deserve higher multiples than a spot-exposed tanker precisely because their earnings are more stable. The closest true comp is LPG (Dorian) at 9.2x, another spot-exposed shipping name, which confirms that the market structurally assigns single-digit trailing multiples to peak-cycle shipping earnings because it expects mean reversion. INSW's 7.4x is consistent with that pattern, not an anomaly to be arbitraged.

Analyst targets bracket the uncertainty: low $56, mean $91.17, median $91.50, high $120. The current price of ~$81 sits ~11% below the mean target. We do not treat that gap as a floor here, because the guardrail applies: this is a peak-cycle commodity name where both the price and the targets reflect the same rate-environment optimism, not independent confirmation of mispricing. The low target of $56 (a ~31% decline) is the more instructive number — it shows what the rate-normalization scenario implies.

The special-dividend-inflated yield is not a valuation anchor; the bulk of capital return is the discretionary special dividend ($4.43 of the $4.55 Q1 payout), which is itself a function of peak earnings.

Valuation score: 5.5. Fairly valued on normalized earnings, cheap only on a trailing number that the company says will not repeat.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker industry is fragmented, capital-intensive, and intensely competitive; no single owner sets rates. INSW competes with independent owners, oil majors, and state fleets. Within that structure, INSW is positioned at the high end: a diversified balanced fleet across all major asset classes, modern scrubber-fitted and dual-fuel tonnage, deep pool relationships, and a balance sheet that allows counter-cyclical vessel purchases when distressed owners are forced to sell.

The Tankers International acquisition is a real strategic step — owning the commercial manager of the VLCC pool and extending it to Suezmax gives INSW a structural commercial-management edge over pure vessel owners. The company's indexed cumulative TSR of 470.75 versus a peer group at 196.0 documents sustained outperformance.

The current super-cycle is geopolitically manufactured: Russia/Ukraine sanctions lengthened crude and product ton-miles; Red Sea/Houthi attacks force Cape of Good Hope reroutings; Venezuela and Iran sanctions reshuffle the clean-versus-sanctioned fleet; and the Strait of Hormuz situation is the most acute. These are not durable competitive advantages — they are rents that accrue to all tanker owners and reverse when the disruptions resolve. INSW captures more of them than peers because of fleet quality and pool scale, but the rents themselves are exogenous.

Competitive Position score: 6.5. Improving trajectory via vertical integration and best-in-class balance sheet, but the moat is operational and financial rather than insulating against the commodity cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality and spot exposure (dominant risk).** Roughly 82% of TCE revenue is spot. The CEO and the 10-K explicitly warn that elevated rates are propped up by geopolitics and that normalization could cause rates to decline significantly. Q1 2026's record is not a run-rate, and the consensus already models a ~54% EPS decline into 2027.

**The Hormuz binary cuts both ways.** A verified, sustained reopening of the strait compresses the disruption premium INSW is monetizing — the same mechanism that drove the rally would unwind it. A genuine resolution is the most direct downside catalyst for INSW specifically.

**Orderbook overhang.** The ~17% world-fleet orderbook pressures rates when demand normalizes.

**Vessel value / impairment.** Carrying values are cycle-sensitive; nine MRs carried ~$43.3M above appraised market value at year-end 2025 with no impairment taken. Loan covenants tie to 135% vessel-collateral value.

**U.S.–China port fees.** INSW owns 13 China-built vessels. Tonnage-based port fees introduced October 2025 were suspended for one year from November 10, 2025 — re-escalation is a live risk.

**Capital-allocation tension.** The $200M ATM equity program announced alongside the record special dividend on May 20 (the stock fell ~5.8%) is internally contradictory — issuing equity near cycle highs while paying record dividends. The benign read is opportunistic growth capital and flexibility; the cautious read is that management views shares as fairly valued at $82–89 and is willing to dilute at these levels. (This development is confirmed only via a single Tier-2 source; details are unverified.)

**Tax structure.** Reliance on the U.S. Section 883 exemption and the Bermuda international-shipping-income exclusion depends on substance and ownership tests; failure would tax U.S.-source shipping income.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, acute uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 70.0% against 36.9% one-year historical vol — a 33-point premium, IV percentile at 86%. The term structure is in steep backwardation (70.0% near-term versus 53.9% at six months), the textbook signature of event-hedging around a known binary rather than a structural regime shift. The nearest expiry implies a ±10.2% move. Put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.8% near-term but flips slightly negative at three months, and the put/call OI ratio sits at a benign 0.42 — net positioning is not aggressively bearish. The reading is consistent with the fundamental view: the market expects a large move tied to the Hormuz/rate binary and is hedging it, without a directional consensus. For a long holder this is a moment to discount the front-end vol spike rather than read it as a sell signal; for a prospective buyer it argues against adding ahead of an unhedged binary.

Risk Factors score: 4.5. The Hormuz binary, the orderbook overhang, and the ATM/dilution tension are specific, near-term, and not fully resolved by the strong balance sheet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The rate environment persists longer than consensus models. The Hormuz disruption grinds on, Red Sea reroutings continue, and the sanctions-driven fleet bifurcation keeps ton-miles elevated. The orderbook is partially absorbed by IMO decarbonization scrapping of older tonnage and limited yard slots. INSW compounds the 85%-of-adjusted-net-income payout policy, returning multiple dollars of special dividends per quarter, while its sub-7% net LTV lets it buy distressed tonnage if rates dip. Tankers International pool ownership expands and adds fee income. In this scenario, FY26 EPS lands near or above the $13.90 consensus, the special dividends continue, and the stock re-rates toward the $120 high target as the cycle proves more durable than feared. Bull case: ~$110.

### Bear Case

The Hormuz situation resolves through a verified reopening, the disruption premium compresses, and spot TCE rates fall back toward mid-cycle. The orderbook delivers into a normalizing demand environment, pressuring rates further. FY27 EPS reverts toward or below the $6.42 consensus; the special dividend shrinks with adjusted net income; the $200M ATM dilution proves to have been struck near the top. On ~$6.40 normalized EPS at ~8.5x (the bear-case shipping multiple), the stock trades to the low-$50s, in line with the $56 analyst low. Bear case: ~$55, roughly 32% downside from current.

The base case is somewhere between: a gradual rate roll-down over 2026–2027 as some disruptions persist and others resolve, with INSW's payout policy and asset quality cushioning the equity. Base case: ~$80–85, essentially flat to current, with the special-dividend stream providing the bulk of total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A sustained, physically verified extension of the disruption regime that pushes FY27 consensus EPS materially above the current $6.42, while the stock remains below ~10x that figure.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s on no fundamental deterioration, compressing the normalized forward P/E below ~8x and restoring asymmetric upside.
- Insider open-market buying or cancellation of the ATM program, signaling management views shares as undervalued.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed, sustained Hormuz reopening that begins compressing VLCC spot TCE back toward the $40k range, with FY27 estimates rolling over below $6.00.
- Material impairment recognition on the MR fleet or covenant pressure from falling vessel-collateral values.
- Drawdown of the $200M ATM at depressed prices, signaling distress rather than opportunism.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) for confirmation of whether Q1's rate spike sustained or rolled over, and ongoing monitoring of the Hormuz situation. The thesis is event-driven, not calendar-driven; the binary resolves on the conflict, not the quarter.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a high-quality, well-managed, fortress-balance-sheet tanker operator printing record earnings on a geopolitically-driven super-cycle. Everything good about the company is real: the sub-7% net LTV, the ~$918M liquidity, the institutionalized capital-return policy, the Tankers International vertical integration, the standout long-run TSR. None of it changes the central fact that Q1 2026's adjusted EPS is a peak the company's own filings warn will normalize, and that the rate environment producing it is tied directly to the Strait of Hormuz disruption — a binary that cuts both ways.

At ~7.4x trailing but ~12.7x normalized forward earnings, with a ~17% orderbook building, insiders selling into the spike rather than buying, and a $200M ATM struck near cycle highs, the stock is fairly valued, not mispriced. The price decline from ~$92 to ~$81 reflects a genuine reassessment of the rate-environment durability, not a mispricing to exploit. We rate INSW a HOLD — own it through the cycle for the capital-return profile and asset quality, but do not add at these levels into an unhedged binary.
