## Executive Summary

Chubb delivered a high-quality second quarter on every metric that management is compensated on, and a slightly deteriorating one on the metric that determines the next three years of earnings. Core operating income rose 14.6% to $2,840M ($7.26 per share, +18.2%), beating the $6.95 consensus by 4.4% and extending the beat streak to four quarters. The P&C combined ratio improved to 83.8% from 85.6%, tangible book value per share rose 17.1% year over year to $131.93, and adjusted net investment income set a record at $1.88B. GAAP net income fell 3.8% to $2,854M, which reconciles almost entirely to private-equity mark-to-market inside equity in earnings of partially-owned entities collapsing from $540M to $6M against an exceptional prior-year comparison, rather than to any underwriting or investment-income weakness.

The underlying picture is flatter than the headline. The current-accident-year combined ratio excluding catastrophes was 82.2% against 82.3%, and management says so in the MD&A. The entire 1.8-point headline improvement came from a lower catastrophe load (4.0 points versus 5.5) and marginally better prior-period development. Within that flat underlying number, the two segments most exposed to the property pricing cycle went backwards — North America Commercial P&C by 0.7 points and Global Reinsurance by 3.4 points — because Chubb chose to walk away from 30.1% of its Major Accounts property and short-tail book. Greenberg's own characterization on July 21 is the most decision-relevant disclosure of the quarter: soft conditions persist in large-account and E&S property, and "soft market conditions are spreading to certain areas of casualty while financial lines also remain soft." Price is falling into a casualty loss trend that is still rising, and Chubb's own long-tail prior-period development was net adverse by $146M in the first half, with corporate run-off adverse development accelerating 126% year over year on molestation claims.

That combination supports a HOLD rather than a BUY at this price. The franchise deserves its premium; the question is how much. At 12.7x FY2026 consensus and 2.67x tangible book, roughly 7% separates the stock from a defensible base case of $378, against 15% downside to $300 in a reserve-true-up or hard-cat scenario. Scores have declined from 7.5 in May to 5.97 now, driven almost entirely by valuation compression of the opportunity rather than by business deterioration; nothing in the Q2 filing reverses that trend, and the casualty commentary mildly reinforces it.

## Company Overview

Chubb Limited is a Swiss-domiciled global insurer and reinsurer operating in 54 countries, with $281.3B of total assets and $75.4B of Chubb shareholders' equity at June 30, 2026. Q2 net premiums written of $14,705M and segment income of $4,043M split across six reporting units:

| Segment | Q2'26 NPW | Combined ratio | Segment income |
|---|---|---|---|
| NA Commercial P&C | $5,594M | 85.4% | $1,727M |
| NA Personal P&C | $2,054M | 67.3% | $728M |
| NA Agricultural | $776M | 89.7% | $79M |
| Overseas General | $3,990M | 82.2% | $995M |
| Global Reinsurance | $354M | 76.1% | $182M |
| Life Insurance | $1,937M | n/a | $332M |

Four distinct earnings engines matter. North America commercial (large-account/specialty $3,257M plus middle-market/small commercial $2,337M) is the largest underwriting profit pool and the one under pricing pressure. North America high-net-worth personal lines is the most differentiated franchise and the largest single concentration of catastrophe risk. Overseas General spreads across EMEA (41% of segment NPW), Asia (37%) and Latin America (21%). Life Insurance includes majority-owned Huatai in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Korea, plus Chubb Benefits worksite products in North America.

Beneath the underwriting sits a $172.6B investment portfolio at average A/A credit quality, 2.29x shareholders' equity. That leverage converts modest reinvestment-yield gains into material earnings: GAAP net investment income of $1,760M in Q2 (+12.3%) and $3,469M in the first half (+10.9%). For 2026, investment income growth is doing more for consolidated earnings than underwriting margin is.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and the GAAP/core bridge.** Q2 net premiums earned rose 5.8% to $13,889M against NPW growth of 3.6% (2.0% constant dollars), so earned premium is still catching up to prior-period writings. Core operating income of $2,840M and core operating EPS of $7.26 compare with GAAP net income of $2,854M and diluted EPS of $7.30. The bridge to the 3.8% GAAP decline runs through "Other (income) expense," which fell from $655M of income to $196M, within which equity in net income of partially-owned entities dropped from $657M to $124M. The 10-Q discloses private-equity marks inside that line of $540M in Q2 2025 versus $6M in Q2 2026, and total private-equity marks excluded from net investment income of $512M versus $99M. The comparison, not the operation, produced the decline.

This mechanism cuts both ways and deserves standing attention. Chubb carries $17,385M of private equities at net asset value as a practical expedient, plus a single unconsolidated third-party-managed fund at $5.3B where carrying value approximates maximum risk of loss, and $7,125M of maximum future funding commitments that do not sit on the balance sheet. Marks in this book are lumpy and not independently observable. A quarter of negative alternatives marks would compress GAAP net income the same way the absence of marks did this quarter.

**Margins.** The consolidated CAY ex-cat combined ratio of 82.2% (versus 82.3%) is flat, with divergence beneath it:

| CAY ex-cat combined ratio | Q2'26 | Q2'25 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| NA Commercial P&C | 81.8% | 81.1% | +0.7 worse |
| NA Personal P&C | 69.9% | 72.2% | −2.3 better |
| NA Agricultural | 87.6% | 88.8% | −1.2 better |
| Overseas General | 85.2% | 85.4% | −0.2 better |
| Global Reinsurance | 76.9% | 73.5% | +3.4 worse |

The mechanism is explicit in the MD&A: reduced property exposure changed the business mix in NA Commercial, and Global Reinsurance lost catastrophe-exposed property premium while carrying higher underlying loss expectations on property. High-margin property premium subsidized those ratios; removing it leaves a worse mix. Separately, the policy acquisition cost ratio rose to 19.1% from 18.5%, and in four of five P&C segments, because growth is shifting toward consumer and international lines that carry structurally higher acquisition costs (Overseas General 26.6%, Global Reinsurance 32.7%).

**Reserves.** Net unpaid losses of $71,216M against $75,372M of equity is 0.94x reserve leverage. First-half prior-period development of $569M favorable decomposes into $715M favorable short-tail, $146M **adverse** long-tail, and $173M **adverse** corporate run-off. The long-tail pattern is workers' compensation favorable offsetting commercial general liability and general casualty adverse, in both 2026 and the 2025 comparatives. Corporate run-off adverse development of $158M in Q2 alone versus $70M a year earlier (+126%), attributed primarily to molestation-related claims, is the fastest-moving negative in the filing.

The bull reading is defensible: workers' compensation redundancy has been genuine and durable industry-wide for a decade, and the individual casualty movements are small against a $71.2B net reserve base. The bear reading is equally supported by the same disclosure: releases are being generated in the least persistent lines (property, auto physical damage, surety) while the most persistent liabilities consume them. Chubb discloses both prominently, and no inference about management intent is warranted.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** First-half operating cash flow of $7,677M against $5,117M a year earlier is genuine strength, comfortably exceeding first-half net income of $5,229M, which means earnings are cash-backed. It should not be annualized to $15.4B: the half benefited from unusually low paid losses following the 2025 wildfire event, a $2,122M unearned-premium build and $1,832M of future policy benefits (both timing/growth items), and second-half carries hurricane and crop seasonality. A normalized $12–13B is the defensible base.

Financial debt of $18,115M plus $427M of hybrids gives 19.1% debt-to-adjusted-capitalization and 24.6% debt-to-equity, conservative for the rating. The 2026 issuance program was deliberately multi-currency (CHF200M, $1.0B of 5.30% 2036s, CNH4.0B and CAD800M), with the CNH, CHF and CAD instruments designated as non-derivative net investment hedges of foreign subsidiary equity. Full-year interest expense is guided to roughly $808M. Liquidity includes $3.0B of undrawn revolver to December 2030 and $2.0B of unused commercial paper capacity, with all covenants met.

AOCI of −$5,923M (from −$4,975M at year end) comprises −$3,282M of net unrealized investment depreciation and −$3,015M of cumulative translation adjustment. Gross unrealized losses on available-for-sale fixed maturities of $4,543M against $1,081M of appreciation, with $3,046M in positions held over twelve months, are economically limited against long-duration liabilities but do mean reported book value understates hold-to-maturity value.

**A note on screening artifacts.** An Altman Z-Score below the distress threshold appears in standard screens for Chubb. Altman's model depends on working capital to assets and sales to assets, neither of which has meaning for a company holding roughly $200B of policyholder and loss-reserve liabilities on $281.3B of assets with $30.6B of half-year revenue. The relevant solvency evidence is 19.1% debt-to-adjusted-capitalization, a modeled 1-in-250 worldwide catastrophe PML of 12.0% of equity, and all covenants met. Cash-flow-versus-accrual and leverage-direction components of composite screens remain informative and read favorably here.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth understates and overstates in different places. Consolidated P&C premiums grew 3%, or 6.3% excluding large-account and E&S property. North America grew roughly 0.5% (commercial −2.3%, or +4.1% ex major and specialty property), while personal lines and agriculture each grew 6%. Overseas General grew 10.2% (Latin America +15.6%, Asia +12%, Europe +5.1%), international life premiums and deposits +14.4%, Chubb Benefits worksite +23.4%, personal auto +10.5%, homeowners +6.8%. Middle-market P&C grew 12.0% while middle-market financial lines declined 2.8%.

The growth mix is therefore migrating toward consumer, international and life, and away from the US large-account commercial business where pricing is deteriorating fastest. That is a favorable rotation for revenue durability, though it carries the higher acquisition-cost drag noted above, and it does not reduce concentration in Chubb's two largest single exposures: a legacy US long-tail casualty reserve book at 0.94x equity, and catastrophe PML anchored in US hurricane and California earthquake.

Investment income is the other engine, growing 12.3% in Q2 on a portfolio up 9% year over year. This is the sector's shock absorber in 2026, and it is rate-dependent in both directions. A rate-cutting cycle would remove that offset at the same time pricing softens, which is the principal path to a multi-year earnings plateau.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus rose 2.2% over 90 days to $27.69, current quarter +0.8% to $6.33, but next quarter −0.1% and next year −0.4% to $29.11. The pattern is a modest mark-to-market of realized 2026 beats without any upgrade to 2027 earnings power. FY2026 consensus implies 11.8% core operating EPS growth over FY2025's ~$24.76; FY2027 implies 5.1%. Consensus for the current quarter ($6.33) sits 15% below the Q3 2025 actual of $7.49, so a normalized catastrophe load is already embedded. This is a stable, mid-single-digit compounder with a favorable near-term earnings mix, not a name with high revision velocity.

Roughly 3.6 points of the 18.2% core operating EPS growth came from a 3.1% reduction in weighted diluted shares (403.85M to 391.25M). Underlying core operating income growth was 14.6%. Both are good; the share-count contribution is a lever with declining accretion at 2.67x tangible book.

## Valuation Assessment

At $352.54:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $27.69 | **12.7x** |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $29.11 | **12.1x** |
| P/E on trailing four-quarter core operating EPS ($29.09) | 12.1x |
| Price / book ($195.45) | **1.80x** |
| Price / tangible book ($131.93) | **2.67x** |
| Annualized 1H ROE | 13.9% |
| Dividend yield ($4.08) | 1.16% |
| Total shareholder yield (dividend + ~$4B buyback pace) | ~4.1% |

A data conflict warrants flagging: the profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 12.47 alongside EPS of $25.68, which do not reconcile ($352.54 / $25.68 = 13.7x). The 12.47 figure corresponds to trailing four-quarter core operating EPS of $29.09, which is the more internally consistent basis and matches the earnings-surprise table. Consensus forward multiples above are computed directly from the estimate table.

**Peer context.** Peer median P/E is 9.0x (PGR 10.3, TRV 9.0, ALL 4.8, HIG 9.0, ACGL 7.0) and peer median yield 1.6% against Chubb's 1.16%. Chubb trades at roughly a 40% P/E premium to the median and above every listed comparable. Part of that premium is justified: Chubb's diversification across four earnings engines, its A/A portfolio at 2.29x equity, 13.9% ROE, and demonstrated willingness to shrink rather than chase price genuinely differ from a US-concentrated commercial carrier. But peer multiples are computed on trailing GAAP bases distorted by realized gains (ALL at 4.8x is not a like-for-like comparison), so the cleaner test is absolute.

On an absolute basis, the compounding math is the whole case. Management guides, conditioned on catastrophes and FX, to double-digit tangible book value growth. Applying 12% to $131.93 gives mid-2027 tangible book of roughly $148. At the current 2.67x multiple that is $395; at a more conservative 2.55x it is $377. Cross-checking on earnings, 13.0x FY2027 consensus of $29.11 is $378. **Base case $378, +7.2% from spot.**

Analyst consensus corroborates rather than drives: mean $365.26, median $369.00, range $306–$425, with a rating distribution of 2 strong buy, 7 buy, 13 hold, 3 strong sell and a stable trend. The mean sits 3.6% above spot. Given the stock's 12.1x forward multiple, analyst targets here are not obviously stale in the way they can be on inflecting names with rising estimates: next-year estimates have drifted slightly *lower*, which removes the mechanism by which targets get chased upward.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a fairly-priced quality compounder rather than a mispricing. There is no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion, the absolute yield support is thin at 1.16%, and cycle-adjusted earnings power is close to trailing rather than materially above it: 2026 earnings are being helped by a light catastrophe half and record investment income, both of which are more likely to normalize than to improve.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is running a bifurcated cycle and Chubb sits across the divide.

Property is softening from the top down, and Chubb's filings are the clearest available evidence. Major Accounts property and other short-tail NPW fell 30.1% in the quarter and 26.9% year to date; that decline alone reduced NA Commercial growth by roughly 6.4 points and consolidated commercial growth by 4.5 points. Global Reinsurance NPW fell 6.7% on increased client retentions, lower underlying rates and less favorable terms. The Baldwin Group reported at end-July that commercial property continues to become more competitive while casualty pressures persist.

Casualty is the reverse problem, and Greenberg's statement that softening is now spreading into casualty and that financial lines remain soft is the late-cycle configuration that historically precedes reserve pain: price declining into a loss trend that is still rising. Chubb's own general liability and general casualty development is adverse in both 2026 and 2025 comparatives.

Where Chubb is structurally advantaged is that its fastest-growing lines are the least exposed to the softening: high-net-worth personal (+6.0% NPW with the underlying ratio improving 2.3 points), Overseas General consumer (+12.1%), Latin America (+15.6%), Asia (+12%), international life traditional regular premium (+12.4%), and worksite benefits (+23.4%). Few competitors serve the affluent US personal segment at Chubb's scale, and the pricing evidence supports genuine power there.

The competitive-position trajectory is therefore mixed rather than improving. Chubb is deliberately ceding share in its most profitable historical line and accepting a measurable mix penalty in reported ratios, while gaining share in consumer and international lines that carry higher acquisition costs and lower underwriting margins. Incentive design supports the choice — performance shares vest on relative combined ratio and tangible book value growth rather than premium growth — but the shareholder outcome depends on Greenberg's forecast that "the growth penalty we are paying in property will dissipate going forward," which is a management projection, not a fact.

## Risk Assessment

**Long-tail casualty reserve adequacy (highest consequence).** Net reserves of $71.2B against $75.4B of equity. A 5% adverse movement is roughly $3.6B, about $9.22 per share of book value on shares outstanding and, more importantly, a likely trigger for multiple compression toward peer levels. A 10% movement consumes roughly 9.4% of book. Social inflation, nuclear verdicts, litigation funding and reviver statutes are all disclosed drivers.

**Molestation and A&E run-off, accelerating.** $173M adverse in 1H 2026 versus $83M in 1H 2025. Active subsidiaries have ceded roughly $1.9B to Century Indemnity, the run-off entity, and a Century rehabilitation would expose those recoverables.

**Catastrophe concentration.** Modeled net PMLs at June 30: worldwide 1-in-100 aggregate $5,650M (7.5% of equity), 1-in-250 $9,052M (12.0%), US hurricane 1-in-100 $3,766M (5.0%), California earthquake 1-in-250 $2,082M (2.8%). The April 2026–March 2027 program retains the first $1.75B per US occurrence. The 2025 California wildfires cost $1.47B and pushed NA Personal's 1H 2025 combined ratio to 115.1%.

**China concentration, larger than segment reporting implies.** China corporate bonds of $8,192M are the largest single-country corporate holding, roughly 3x the UK's $2,776M, plus PRC government securities $2,570M and Province of Hunan $569M, exceeding $11.3B of direct fixed-income exposure. Add consolidated Huatai (noncontrolling interests $5,473M) and CNH-denominated funding of roughly $1.9B designated as net investment hedges. The structure is prudently currency-matched and the positions are largely investment grade with no evidence of credit stress; the aggregate is nonetheless a genuine single-jurisdiction exposure that no segment disclosure surfaces.

**Alternatives marks and unfunded commitments.** This is the channel through which broader alternatives stress would reach Chubb's reported GAAP earnings.

**Taxes.** Effective rate 20.5% in Q2 and 21.0% in 1H versus 19.3% prior year, rising structurally on the Bermuda corporate income tax, OECD Pillar Two and the Swiss income inclusion rule, with a $285M valuation allowance against tax benefits on certain investment unrealized losses.

**Succession.** Greenberg has led the company since 2004 and executive-officer retention is a disclosed risk factor. An 8-K dated November 4, 2025 carried Items 5.02 and 7.01 whose contents could not be retrieved; 2026 leadership announcements at Chubb Tempest Re and Westchester were divisional. Group CEO succession remains unresolved and unverified.

**Governance.** Cumulative TSR crossed below Chubb's own selected peer group in 2024 (191.46 vs 193.19) and the gap widened in 2025 (219.13 vs 228.54), while CEO total compensation rose 43% from 2021 to 2025 and compensation actually paid reached $58.47M. The gap is small in cumulative terms and 2025 relative TSR was mechanically depressed by an exogenous wildfire event, but the direction is a legitimate shareholder question.

**Swiss structure.** Dividends require annual shareholder approval in Swiss francs and are paid from capital contribution reserves whose capacity the company estimates lasts to roughly 2032–2036; share cancellations require capital-band procedures. These are second-order frictions a US-domiciled peer does not face.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 21.1% against 19.3% one-year realized is fairly priced (1.8-point spread, 12th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is flat (21.1% near, 20.0% at six months), so the market prices no concentrated event and distributes uncertainty evenly. The nearest expiry implies a ±3.3% move. Put/call skew is characterized as balanced at the front. Open-interest put/call of 0.34 is call-tilted and unchanged across the most recent snapshot, while the put/call volume ratio of 2.55 is driven by the six-month tenor's 29.00 reading, which on 345 total contracts across six expirations most likely reflects one sizeable dated put position rather than a broad hedging shift. Short interest of 1.1% of float and 2.4 days to cover leaves no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The options market prices no cheap optionality, no catalyst, and no positioning that challenges the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($415, +17.7%).** Property rates firm in 2027 as the growth penalty laps, and Chubb re-enters large-account and E&S property with retained pricing discipline, restoring the high-margin mix that cost NA Commercial 0.7 points and Global Reinsurance 3.4 points of CAY ex-cat ratio. Investment income continues compounding double digits as the portfolio rolls into higher reinvestment yields, adding earnings independent of underwriting. Workers' compensation redundancy proves deep enough to fund casualty true-ups for several more years. A light second-half catastrophe season delivers FY2026 core operating EPS toward $29–30 against the $27.69 consensus, the $7.5B authorization retires 3%+ of shares annually, and tangible book compounds at 12–14%. At 2.8x mid-2027 tangible book of roughly $148, the stock reaches $415.

**Bear case ($300, −14.9%).** Casualty softening deepens per management's own warning while general liability loss trend continues rising, producing a visible reserve strengthening in 2027–2028; a $3–5B charge takes 4–7% off book value and compresses the multiple toward the 9.0x peer median. Property stays soft, so the "penalty dissipates" forecast does not arrive and top-line growth stagnates at 2–4% for multiple years while the acquisition ratio keeps rising on consumer mix. A major US hurricane consumes the $1.75B retention. Private-equity marks turn negative, reversing the GAAP tailwind that flattered 2026. Rate cuts remove the investment-income offset. At 2.0x tangible book of $148, $296.

**Which is more likely.** The base case sits closer to the bull mechanics on earnings and closer to the bear on multiple. Chubb probably does compound tangible book at 11–13% and does out-earn consensus in a normal catastrophe year. But at 12.1x forward and 2.67x tangible book, the multiple already pays for that, and the marginal buyer is asked to accept a casualty-reserve tail and a $1.75B hurricane retention for roughly 7% of upside. The asymmetry is not compelling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $378** (13.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $29.11 = $378; cross-check 2.55x estimated mid-2027 tangible book of ~$148 = $377). **+7.2% from $352.54.**
**Bull case: $415** (2.8x mid-2027 TBVPS ~$148). **+17.7%.**
**Bear case: $300** (2.0x TBVPS ~$148, or peer-median multiple applied post-reserve-charge). **−14.9%.**
**Timeframe: 12 months**, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 2026 report (late October, first hurricane-season read against a $6.33 consensus) and the FY2026 10-K in late February 2027, which will disclose full-year long-tail and molestation development.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price below $315** (roughly 10.6% below spot, 2.39x current tangible book, ~10.8x FY2027 consensus) with fundamentals intact — that restores a ~20% path to base case and is the cleanest trigger.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of non-adverse long-tail prior-period development** with corporate run-off adverse development below $75M per quarter, which would remove the central bear mechanism.
3. **Evidence that property pricing has bottomed** — Major Accounts property NPW declining less than 10% year over year, or Greenberg reversing the "soft conditions spreading to casualty" language, alongside NA Commercial CAY ex-cat combined ratio improving year over year.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A net adverse prior-period development quarter** at the consolidated level, or corporate run-off adverse development exceeding $250M in a single quarter.
2. **Casualty CAY ex-cat combined ratio deterioration exceeding 2 points year over year** in NA Commercial, indicating loss-trend recognition rather than mix.
3. **Group CEO succession announced without an internal successor of Keogh's standing**, or a resolution of the unretrieved November 2025 8-K that implies disorderly transition.
4. **Negative private-equity marks exceeding $300M in a quarter** combined with an increase in maximum future funding commitments above $8B, which would convert the alternatives book from an earnings tailwind into a liquidity and earnings-quality problem.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings in late October 2026 is the next natural checkpoint.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Chubb is one of the better-run large-cap financials in the market: a 83.8% combined ratio, 13.9% ROE, 17.1% tangible book growth, cash-backed earnings with $7,677M of first-half operating cash flow against $5,229M of net income, 19.1% debt-to-adjusted-capitalization, and a management team demonstrably willing to shed 30% of a property book rather than underwrite at a loss, with an incentive plan built to reward exactly that. The score decline from 7.5 in May to 5.97 now is a valuation and cycle-position story rather than a business-quality story, and the Q2 filing confirms the business rather than reversing it.

What it does not offer at $352.54 is asymmetry. Twelve times forward earnings and 2.67x tangible book on a franchise growing net premiums 2% in constant dollars, with the underlying combined ratio flat, casualty pricing softening into a rising loss trend by management's own account, corporate run-off adverse development up 126%, and a $1.75B per-occurrence hurricane retention still ahead in the season, produces roughly 7% to a base case against 15% downside. Existing holders should own it as a defensive anchor and collect the ~4% shareholder yield plus mid-single-digit compounding. New capital is better committed in the low $300s, or on the specific evidence that long-tail development has stabilized and the property cycle has turned.
