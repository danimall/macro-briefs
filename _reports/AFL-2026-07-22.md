## Executive Summary

Aflac is the leading supplemental health and life insurer in both Japan and the United States, distinguished by a business model that pays cash benefits directly to policyholders across cancer, medical, accident, disability, and life products. The company generates the majority of its earnings in Japan, which makes yen/USD translation, Japanese interest rates, and Japanese demographics the dominant swing factors behind reported results. Aflac carries a fortress capital position and a long dividend-growth record, and its stock has advanced roughly 22% over the trailing twelve months to ~$123.60, near the $125.93 52-week high.

The fundamental picture is more mixed than the price action suggests. Adjusted EPS is essentially flat — $1.75 in Q1 CY2026 versus $1.75 in the prior-year quarter — and the company has missed consensus in two of the last four quarters (Q4 2025 by 8.3%, Q1 2026 by 4.4%). Consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window: current-year EPS has been revised down 3.0% over 90 days to $7.02, and next-year down 2.1% to $7.55. The growth that does exist is manufactured by an aggressive buyback rather than by organic premium expansion, which continues to be pressured by a shrinking in-force block in Japan even as new sales recover.

At ~18.2x trailing / ~16.4x forward earnings and above the peer-median P/E of 15.7, quality is largely priced in. The all-in shareholder yield of roughly 7% (a ~2% dividend plus a buyback contribution) is the durable return here, not multiple expansion. The March 2026 Aflac Re Bermuda / Japan Post reinsurance transaction could reshape the earnings mix, but it also introduces new counterparty and reserve risk. This report maintains a HOLD, classified as an income hold: own it for the capital return and balance-sheet quality, not for a re-rating case that the current setup does not support.

## Company Overview

Aflac Incorporated (NYSE: AFL), headquartered in Columbus, Georgia, operates through two primary segments: Aflac Japan and Aflac U.S. The company pays cash benefits directly to policyholders rather than to providers, positioning it in the voluntary/supplemental benefits niche distinct from major medical carriers. In Japan it is a long-established leader in cancer and medical supplemental insurance; in the U.S. it competes primarily in the worksite/voluntary-benefits channel.

The single most important structural feature separating Aflac from domestic peers is its Japan concentration. A large majority of premium and earnings historically originate in yen, which means a weak yen depresses USD-reported results, and Japan's aging, shrinking population steadily pressures the addressable market and the legacy in-force block. This dynamic (recovering new sales offset by a declining in-force block) recurs throughout recent disclosures and is the central tension in the earnings profile.

The March 31, 2026 agreement, under which Aflac Re Bermuda Ltd. reinsures a block of whole-life annuities from Japan Post Insurance via coinsurance with Japan Post continuing to service the policies, signals a deliberate push to build a reinsurance franchise in Japan. It deploys capital into third-party blocks and creates a new earnings avenue intended, at least in part, to offset the organic decline in the domestic in-force book.

## Financial Analysis

Aflac's headline financials are dominated by two moving parts that obscure the underlying trend: FX translation and investment gains/losses. Q1 CY2026 total revenue was reported at roughly $4.3B, a figure variously described as up ~27.9% YoY (GAAP total revenue, heavily affected by investment marks and FX) or down ~1.8% (new insurance sales). This GAAP-vs-operating discrepancy makes Aflac difficult to read from headline numbers, and it argues for anchoring on adjusted EPS as the cleaner gauge.

On that basis, the picture is flat. Adjusted EPS was unchanged year over year, and Q4 2025 adjusted EPS was $1.57. The earnings-surprise record is deteriorating: after a large Q3 2025 beat (+38.8%, likely investment/FX-driven and not repeatable), the two most recent quarters both missed. Two beats and two misses over four quarters, with both misses most recent, indicates that visibility into the operating trend has softened rather than improved.

Balance-sheet quality remains the anchor of the story. Aflac carries strong capital and a long record of returning it: a dividend aristocrat with a 2026 dividend increase and continued buybacks. The dividend yield is ~1.96% at the current price (per Phase 1 data), plus a buyback contribution [unverified — actual buyback figures not in sources]. The financial-health score of 6.0 reflects genuine capital strength and dividend coverage, tempered by three factors: (1) the inability to verify investment-portfolio composition, including flagged commercial real estate exposure, against primary filings; (2) reserve and counterparty risk introduced by the reinsurance expansion; and (3) the reality that EPS growth is being sourced from share-count reduction rather than operating earnings, which is a lower-quality form of "growth."

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the weak point in the thesis, and it is the reason the growth-prospects score sits at 4.5. Japan new sales have been strong — Q4 2025 Japan sales rose 15.7%, roughly +16% for the full year, with management targeting sales closer to JPY 80B — but this new business is not enough to offset the runoff of the legacy in-force block. The result is flat adjusted EPS and a top line that is more a function of FX and investment marks than of underlying premium expansion.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum. Over the past 90 days, current-quarter EPS has been cut 3.7% to $1.76 and next-quarter cut 3.3% to $1.78; the 30-day revisions are also negative across the board (-0.3% to -0.7%). This is the opposite of the estimate-revision profile that would support an upgrade — there is no evidence of the market systematically underestimating a fundamental improvement. The revision signal, which is directionally reliable, points down.

The one genuine growth avenue is the reinsurance pivot. The Aflac Re Bermuda / Japan Post coinsurance transaction is a new capital-deployment channel that could add earnings and diversify the mix away from the shrinking organic block. But it is early, unproven, and comes with new reserve and counterparty exposure. It is a reason to watch, not yet a reason to raise the growth score. Absent an acceleration in that franchise or a stabilization of the Japan in-force decline, the realistic forward path is low-single-digit EPS growth carried by buybacks rather than a business that is compounding.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$123.60, Aflac trades at roughly 18.2x trailing EPS of $6.82 and ~16.4x forward on next-year's $7.55. Against the Life & Health peer median P/E of 15.7, Aflac carries a premium. The peer set spans a wide range — PRU at ~11.0x and GL at ~11.4x on the cheap end, UNM at ~19.9x on the rich end, with MET at ~17.0x and PFG at ~15.7x — so Aflac sits at the upper portion of the group.

The critical valuation point is that estimates are falling, not rising; revenue growth is FX-driven, not organic; and margins are flat-to-pressured, so cycle-adjusted arguments for looking through a trough do not apply. The valuation-score guidance here is instructive: a stock is not "cheap" simply because a peer trades higher, and cheapness generally sorts as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal unless there is an identifiable catalyst. In Aflac's case there is no compelling mispricing — the multiple is a modest premium justified by superior capital quality and dividend durability, and the stock trades near its 52-week high and above the consensus mean target of $117.07 and median target of $118.00.

That the stock sits above the consensus mean and median targets while estimates fall is a meaningful signal. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, analyst targets act as floors that get revised up as consensus catches reality. Here the opposite holds: falling estimates make the current consensus targets more likely to be revised down than up, and proximity to (and above) the mean target reflects a stock that is fairly-to-fully valued rather than one with asymmetric upside. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable risk/reward: the ~7% all-in yield provides absolute support and prevents a lower score, but there is no clear reason the market is mispricing the shares to the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Aflac holds a genuine moat in its core markets. In Japan it is the entrenched leader in cancer and medical supplemental insurance, with decades of brand equity and a distribution network anchored historically by the Japan Post relationship, which has now evolved into a reinsurance counterparty as well as a distribution partner. In the U.S., its brand recognition in voluntary/worksite benefits is among the strongest in the category, giving it durable access to the employer-sponsored channel.

The trajectory, however, is the concern that drives the competitive-position score of 5.5. Aflac's competitive advantages are real but its addressable market in Japan is structurally contracting on demographics, and the persistent premium/in-force pressure indicates the moat is defending a shrinking pool rather than expanding into a growing one. The prior score history shows competitive position marked down from 7.5 to 6.0 across recent reports; the further modest step to 5.5 reflects continued in-force erosion and flat organic premium, partially offset by strong new-sales momentum and the strategic optionality of the reinsurance build-out. Level (leader) is high; trajectory (flat-to-eroding) is what caps the score. Financials as a sector runs negative on selection alpha, which reinforces caution on treating a strong incumbent position as a reason to own the stock.

## Risk Assessment

The most specific idiosyncratic risks are:

1. **Japan/FX concentration.** With the majority of earnings in yen, USD-reported results swing on translation. This is partly hedged and partly priced, but it structurally increases the volatility of reported EPS and complicates the read on operating trends.

2. **Japanese demographic decline.** An aging, shrinking population steadily pressures the in-force block and the addressable market. This is a slow-moving but persistent headwind rather than a binary event.

3. **Investment-portfolio / CRE exposure.** Third-party analysis has flagged commercial real estate risk in Aflac's investment book, and the inability to verify the portfolio composition against primary filings is a genuine gap. Net investment losses appear to have offset earnings in recent quarters.

4. **Reinsurance-transaction risk.** The Aflac Re / Japan Post coinsurance deal introduces new reserve, counterparty, and asset-intensity risk that has not yet been stress-tested through a cycle.

5. **Governance/succession.** Daniel Amos has led the company for decades. Long-tenured, family-associated leadership raises standard board-independence and succession considerations, and proxy/insider detail could not be verified in this cycle.

6. **Regulatory capital disclosure.** Japan's economic solvency ratio (ESR) regime disclosures are becoming more prominent; an adverse read could constrain subsidiary remittances that fund the buyback, which is the primary driver of the shareholder-return thesis. [unverified — ESR disclosure timing and materiality not in sources]

The risk-factors score of 5.0 reflects that most of these are known and partially priced, but two are live and could surprise: an ESR-driven constraint on the buyback, and a quantified CRE or reserve issue that primary filings would reveal. The recent trajectory (risk score marked down from 6.0 to 4.5 across prior reports) captured the surfacing of these concerns; I hold at 5.0 rather than lower because none has yet materialized as a confirmed, quantified liability, and the balance sheet remains strong.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($140+)
The reinsurance franchise scales faster than expected, adding an earnings stream that offsets the Japan in-force decline and shifts the growth trajectory from flat to low-mid single digits. A weaker yen reverses or Japanese long rates rise, lifting net investment income and USD-reported results. The buyback continues to retire shares, compounding EPS even on flat operating earnings, and the market rewards the durable ~7% all-in yield with modest multiple support toward 18-19x. A clean July ESR disclosure removes the buyback-constraint overhang. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the $137 analyst high.

### Bear Case (~$100)
The Japan in-force block continues to erode faster than new sales can offset, keeping organic premium flat-to-down. CRE or other investment-portfolio losses materialize and are quantified in filings, pressuring net earnings and book value. Estimate revisions continue their downward drift, and the multiple compresses toward the peer-cheap end (~11-12x) as the market concludes that buyback-driven EPS growth does not warrant a premium. An adverse ESR read constrains subsidiary remittances and forces a buyback slowdown, removing the primary return driver. The stock retraces toward the $98 analyst low and the $96.95 52-week low.

The base case sits between these: flat-to-low-single-digit adjusted EPS growth carried by the buyback, a ~7% all-in yield delivering most of the total return, and a stock that trades in a range near current levels absent a catalyst in either direction.

## Conclusion

Aflac is a high-quality, wide-moat insurer with a fortress balance sheet and one of the most durable capital-return records in the sector, but the investment case is defensive rather than growth-oriented. Adjusted EPS is flat, estimates are falling across every window, the earnings-surprise record has turned negative, and the growth that exists is manufactured by buybacks against a shrinking Japanese in-force block. The shares are fairly-to-fully valued with no identifiable catalyst for a re-rating.

The declining score trajectory (6.7 → 6.5 → 5.9 → 5.6, and now 5.3) reflects the accumulation of these concerns rather than any single break; the stock's ~22% trailing-twelve-month gain has occurred despite, not because of, improving fundamentals, which sharpens the valuation caution. The recommendation is HOLD, classified as an income hold. Own Aflac for the ~7% all-in shareholder yield and balance-sheet quality; do not underwrite meaningful appreciation. A clean ESR print plus a revision inflection would justify an upgrade, while an ESR-driven buyback constraint or a quantified CRE loss would move this to AVOID.
