## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is a pure-play ethanol producer operating through majority-owned subsidiaries (One Earth Energy at 76.1% ownership, NuGen Energy at 99.7%) and a 10.3% equity stake in Big River Resources. The company shipped approximately 722 million gallons of ethanol through its consolidated entities in the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026, generating $83 million in net income attributable to REX shareholders. The balance sheet is exceptional: $376 million in cash and short-term investments against essentially zero debt ($3.2M in lease obligations), placing approximately 23% of the company's $1.6 billion market cap in liquid assets.

The investment case centers on three vectors: (1) the 45Z clean fuel production tax credit, extended through 2029 under OBBBA, which turned REX's effective tax rate negative in FY2025 and creates substantial per-gallon economics for low-carbon-intensity ethanol; (2) the One Earth expansion from 150M to 200M gallons per year with integrated carbon sequestration; and (3) aggressive share repurchases funded from internal cash. The stock has appreciated 133% over the past year, moving from ~$21 to ~$49, driven by earnings recovery and 45Z credit recognition. At ~19.5x forward earnings, the valuation reflects substantial improvement from prior levels but is not egregiously expensive given the growth optionality and cash cushion. However, the concentration of risks — commodity crush spreads, regulatory dependency, tariff exposure, carbon sequestration permitting, and an $82.7M IRS audit — warrants a hold rather than a fresh entry at current prices.

## Company Overview

REX completed its transformation from a consumer electronics retailer into an ethanol investor beginning in 2006. Today, the company operates as a holding company with controlling interests in two ethanol production facilities and a minority stake in a third. The business is straightforward: buy corn, convert it to ethanol (plus co-products like dried distillers grains and corn oil), and sell the output. Profitability is determined primarily by the "crush spread" — the difference between ethanol selling prices and corn input costs — which REX can only hedge four months forward.

The company completed a 2-for-1 stock split on September 15, 2025. All per-share figures in this report are split-adjusted.

**Key Facilities:**
- One Earth Energy (Gibson City, IL): 150M gallons/year, expanding to 175M then 200M. REX ownership: 76.1%
- NuGen Energy (Marion, SD): REX ownership: 99.7%
- Big River Resources (multiple sites): REX ownership: 10.3% (equity method)

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

FY2025 (ended January 31, 2026) revenue was $650M, essentially flat versus $642M in FY2024 but well below the $833M in FY2023 when ethanol prices were elevated. Despite modest revenue, profitability improved significantly:

- Net income to REX: $83.0M (up 43% from $58M in FY2024)
- EPS (split-adjusted): ~$2.50
- Gross margin: 14.4% (vs. 14.2% prior year, 11.8% in FY2023)

The earnings improvement is largely attributable to $28.1M in 45Z clean fuel production tax credits, which drove the effective tax rate to negative 7.3%. Normalizing to a 22% tax rate would reduce net income to approximately $59-60M, implying the tax credit contributed roughly $23-24M in incremental earnings.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is among the strongest in any publicly traded energy company at this market cap:

- Cash + short-term investments: $375.8M
- Total debt: $3.2M (essentially zero)
- Current ratio: 5.9x
- Stockholders' equity: $610.7M
- Enterprise value (market cap minus net cash): ~$1.25B

### Cash Flow

- Operating cash flow: $117.8M (up 84% from $64.2M in FY2024)
- Capital expenditures: $68.4M (One Earth expansion + CI reduction)
- Free cash flow: $49.4M
- Share repurchases: $32.9M (1.65M shares at ~$19.93 split-adjusted)

Management has guided FY2026 capex of $70-80M with total project spending of $220-230M for the One Earth expansion and carbon sequestration. All growth investment is internally funded.

### Most Recent Quarter (Q3 FY2025, ended October 31, 2025)

Revenue: $175.6M (+0.4% YoY). Net income to REX: $23.4M (-4.4% YoY). The nine-month period showed 17% YoY earnings decline before a strong Q4 recovery drove the full-year result to $83M.

## Growth Analysis

Growth potential derives from three specific channels:

**1. Volume Expansion:** One Earth is expanding from 150M to 175M gallons (construction permit received), with a further expansion to 200M gallons planned. At REX's 76.1% ownership, this adds approximately 38M incremental gallons — a ~13% production increase on their effective ownership base of ~294M gallons.

**2. 45Z Tax Credit Optimization:** The 45Z credit rewards lower carbon intensity (CI) scores. REX is investing in CI reduction at One Earth, and if carbon sequestration comes online (EPA Class VI permit expected Q3 2026), the CI score could drop substantially, increasing per-gallon credits from approximately $0.10-$0.20 to potentially $0.50-$1.00/gallon. The math: 200M gallons × $0.50 credit × 76.1% ownership = $76M in annual credits — though this is the bull case dependent on successful permitting.

**3. Carbon Sequestration (45Q):** If the on-site sequestration at One Earth receives its Class VI permit and becomes operational, the $85/ton credit (45Q) provides an alternative revenue stream. Companies must choose between 45Q and 45Z in any given year, optimizing for maximum value.

**Constraints:** Revenue growth is fundamentally limited by ethanol pricing and production capacity in an industry with structural overcapacity (18.5B gallons capacity vs. 15.0B mandated). Organic growth beyond the One Earth expansion requires additional acquisitions. The EV adoption trend poses a secular headwind to gasoline and ethanol demand, though the timeline is long (2035+).

## Valuation Assessment

At $49.16, REX trades at:
- Market cap: $1.62B
- Enterprise value (net of cash): ~$1.25B
- Trailing P/E: ~19.7x (on $2.50 EPS)
- Forward P/E: ~19.5x
- EV/Net Income (FY2025): ~15x

Relative to peers in the refining/marketing space (median P/E 10.2x, median dividend yield 2.6%), REX trades at roughly double the multiple with zero dividend yield. The premium reflects: (1) the fortress balance sheet (23% of market cap in cash), (2) 45Z growth optionality, and (3) the share count reduction from buybacks.

Adjusting for normalized earnings power without the 45Z windfall ($59-60M), the EV/E multiple rises to ~21x. This is expensive for a cyclical commodity business. The premium is justified only if 45Z credits are recurring (they are, through 2029) and grow (they could, with carbon sequestration).

On a growth-adjusted basis: if FY2027 earnings reach $100-110M (expansion + higher credits + buyback accretion), the forward EV/E on that target is 11-12x, which is more reasonable. But this requires execution on expansion, permitting success, and stable crush spreads.

The 133% appreciation over 12 months reflects the market re-rating for 45Z economics. At current levels, much of the near-term upside has been captured. The remaining upside is contingent on carbon sequestration success, which carries binary risk.

## Competitive Landscape

REX competes with ADM, Green Plains, ALTO, and numerous smaller regional ethanol producers. The industry operates at structural overcapacity, which limits pricing power. REX's competitive advantages are:

**Strengths:**
- Financial flexibility is unmatched among mid-cap ethanol producers (zero debt, massive cash reserves vs. Green Plains at ~$1.4B debt)
- CI reduction investments position the company for maximum 45Z value
- On-site carbon sequestration (if successful) eliminates pipeline dependency

**Weaknesses:**
- Production scale is modest (~294M effective gallons vs. ADM's ~1.8B+)
- No dividend policy in an industry where peers yield 2.6%
- NuGen's carbon capture reliant on Summit Carbon Solutions pipeline, which faces severe legal setbacks (North Dakota permits voided, South Dakota eminent domain ban)

The 45Z credit system creates a new competitive dynamic where CI score determines relative profitability. REX's investments in CI reduction are forward-looking, but competitors (particularly larger, better-resourced players) are making similar investments.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

**1. Commodity Price Volatility:** Crush spreads drive profitability and are essentially unhedgeable beyond four months. A corn price spike or ethanol price collapse could reduce quarterly earnings by 50%+ with limited visibility.

**2. Carbon Sequestration Permitting ($58.9M at risk):** $58.4M already invested in the One Earth carbon sequestration project. EPA Class VI permit expected Q3 2026. Illinois moratorium on CO2 pipeline construction certificates runs until July 1, 2026. If the project fails, the investment faces potential write-off AND the company's CI score remains higher than projected, reducing 45Z credit value.

**3. IRS Audit (~$82.7M exposure):** $58.2M in refined coal Section 45 tax credits and $24.5M in R&E tax credits under IRS scrutiny, with the IRS proposing to deny them. At $82.7M total, this represents approximately one full year of net income. The company states it will "vigorously defend" but the outcome is uncertain.

**4. Tariff & Trade Risk:** 36% of U.S. distillers grains exports go to Mexico; 36% of U.S. ethanol exports go to Canada. Escalating trade tensions with these partners could materially reduce demand and depress domestic prices.

**5. Regulatory Dependency:** The entire ethanol industry depends on the RFS mandate. EPA small refinery exemptions (63 granted in August 2025 alone) and the eventual phase-out of gasoline demand via EV adoption are structural headwinds.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$60-65, +22-33%)
- One Earth expansion reaches 200M gallons by late 2027
- Carbon sequestration receives Class VI permit in Q3 2026, reducing CI score and boosting 45Z credits to $50-80M annually
- Record corn harvest (USDA projects 16.75B bushels, +12%) keeps input costs low
- Continued buybacks at 3-5% annual share count reduction
- FY2027 EPS of $3.50-$4.00 at 16-17x = $56-68

### Bear Case (~$33-37, -25-33%)
- Crush spreads compress to FY2024 levels due to structural overcapacity
- Carbon sequestration permit delayed or denied; $58.9M impairment
- IRS prevails on audit, requiring ~$82.7M payment (though this would likely be spread over time)
- Canada/Mexico tariff retaliation reduces export demand
- EPS falls to $1.80-$2.00; market applies peer multiple of 10-12x to EV → stock at $33-37

### Base Case (~$50-55, flat to +12%)
- Expansion proceeds on schedule; carbon sequestration receives permit with delay
- Crush spreads remain near FY2025 levels
- 45Z credits of $30-35M annually
- IRS audit settles at partial recovery
- FY2027 EPS of $2.80-$3.10 at 17-18x = $48-56

2. Carbon sequestration permit denied or delayed beyond 2027
3. Stock exceeds $55 without corresponding earnings acceleration (P/E expanding beyond 22x on current earnings)
4. IRS rules against REX on full $82.7M with no appeal path

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late June/early July 2026 for Q1 FY2026). EPA Class VI permit decision expected Q3 2026 (September-October timeframe). These two events should clarify the primary bull/bear scenarios within 4-5 months.

## Conclusion

REX American Resources is a well-managed ethanol company with an exceptional balance sheet and legitimate growth optionality through 45Z tax credits and capacity expansion. At ~19.5x forward earnings, the valuation is reasonable but not cheap for a cyclical commodity business, and the remaining upside depends heavily on carbon sequestration success (binary event) and sustained favorable crush spreads (inherently unpredictable).

The fortress balance sheet provides meaningful downside protection — the company can weather any cyclical downturn and fund all growth internally. However, the IRS audit exposure, tariff risk to export markets, and execution risk on the carbon sequestration project warrant patience rather than chase at these levels. HOLD with a positive outlook contingent on carbon sequestration permitting and continued strong crush spreads. A pullback to the $38-42 range would create a more compelling entry point for the growth thesis.
