## Executive Summary

Global Payments completed its transformational Worldpay acquisition in January 2026 and divested Issuer Solutions to FIS, emerging as the largest pure-play merchant acquirer in the United States with ~$4 trillion in payment volume across 6+ million merchant locations. At $68, the stock trades at approximately 4.9x forward adjusted earnings — a historically extreme discount to its 5-year average of ~15x and well below the peer median P/E of 26.2x. The company guides for ~5% adjusted net revenue growth, 13–15% adjusted EPS growth, and $4B+ in adjusted FCF by 2027, with a $7.5B shareholder return commitment (approximately 40% of current market cap).

The investment case hinges on whether the Worldpay integration delivers $600M+ in targeted cost savings while stabilizing revenue growth against persistent competitive pressure from Stripe, Adyen, and Block. Three misses in the last four quarters, negative near-term estimate revisions (-3.6% for the current quarter over 30 days), and $19.5B+ in post-acquisition debt create material uncertainty. A prior BUY recommendation at higher prices has lost 5.1%, and the fundamental picture has not improved sufficiently to warrant a repeat conviction call. The stock is cheap, but cheapness alone is not a catalyst, and the integration execution timeline stretches through 2027.

## Company Overview

Global Payments is a payments technology company headquartered in Atlanta, operating in 37 countries with approximately 26,000 employees. Following the January 2026 Worldpay acquisition and simultaneous Issuer Solutions divestiture, GPN operates a single Merchant Solutions segment organized into three pillars: Point-of-Sale & Software Solutions (cloud-based POS under the "Genius" brand and vertical software), Integrated & Embedded Solutions (payments embedded in third-party business software), and Core Payments Solutions (direct sales, ISO, and bank referral channels). Revenue is predominantly priced as a percentage of transaction value or per-transaction fee, providing natural operating leverage as volumes grow.

The Worldpay deal added massive scale: GPN now processes approximately 94 billion transactions annually and serves merchants in 175+ countries. The transaction was financed with ~$6.2B in cash plus 43.3M GPN shares issued to GTCR, which now holds ~15% of outstanding common stock. GPN received $7.7B in cash from FIS for the Issuer Solutions segment.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

FY2025 reported revenue of $7.71B represents the Merchant Solutions continuing operations basis after removing Issuer Solutions. On a like-for-like basis, Merchant Solutions revenue was essentially flat (-0.4%), but organic growth excluding divestitures (AdvancedMD, Payroll Solutions) was approximately 7%. This gap between headline and organic figures creates analytical noise. The 7% organic figure is more representative but still trails the double-digit growth rates of technology-native competitors.

Integrated & Embedded Solutions, the strongest pillar, grew 6.6%. Point-of-Sale & Software declined 12.6% on a reported basis (impacted by divestitures). Core Payments declined 1.7% as GPN exited wholesale business and certain Asia Pacific markets.

### Profitability

Merchant Solutions segment operating margin expanded 210 basis points to 35.5% in FY2025, a genuinely positive signal of operational improvement. However, consolidated operating margin compressed to 22.8% from 25.5% due to $737.5M in acquisition and transformation expenses and a 43.4% increase in corporate costs. Adjusted EPS grew to $12.22 from $11.55, maintaining a steady compounding trajectory ($8.16 in 2021 → $12.22 in 2025).

Management's FY2026 guidance calls for 150 basis points of adjusted operating margin expansion and $650M+ in annual run-rate operating income benefit from the transformation program by H1 2027.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow of $2.04B in FY2025 (26.5% FCF margin) is strong for any company, excellent for one mid-transformation. Operating cash flow was $2.66B. Management targets $4B+ adjusted FCF by 2027 and $5B by 2028. If these figures materialize, the current $18.7B market cap implies the stock trades at approximately 4.7x 2027E FCF and 3.7x 2028E FCF.

The $7.5B capital return commitment through end-2027 is credible given FCF trajectory. A $550M ASR was already launched in February 2026. Prior ASRs executed at $84.61 and $102.07, meaning current prices offer superior buyback economics.

### Balance Sheet — Elevated Risk

Long-term debt stood at $19.5B as of FY2025 end, with $981M in truly available cash (excluding $6.2B reserved for the Worldpay close and settlement balances). The debt stack includes $16.4B in senior unsecured notes, $2.0B in 1.5% convertible notes due 2031, $1.5B in 1.0% convertibles due 2029, and $1.5B drawn on a $7.25B revolver. Interest expense was $650M in FY2025 and will likely rise in FY2026.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 places GPN technically in the distress zone, though this is largely an artifact of payments industry balance sheet structure (massive goodwill, low asset turnover). Investment-grade credit ratings and $2B+ FCF mitigate near-term solvency risk. The net leverage target of 3.0x by end-2027 provides a measurable deleveraging timeline.

Both convertible note tranches ($157 and $139 conversion prices) are deeply out of the money at $68, meaning no near-term dilution but also no early redemption option. This is inert debt at low coupon rates.

### Earnings Quality

The 1-beat-in-4-quarters track record is concerning. Three consecutive quarters of modest misses (-2.0%, -0.5%, -0.3%) followed by a marginal beat (+0.4%) suggests management's visibility into its own performance is limited during this transformation period. This pattern undermines confidence in a company asking investors to trust a multi-year integration thesis.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions — Negative Momentum

Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 3.6% over 30 days and 4.4% over 90 days ($2.99 → $2.86). Current-year estimates are down modestly (-0.5% over 30 days), though next-year estimates show a +2.8% 90-day revision, suggesting some analyst willingness to look through near-term noise. The overall picture is negative near-term momentum that has not yet stabilized.

### Organic Growth Drivers

The Worldpay combination creates several growth vectors: (1) unified Genius POS platform across a dramatically larger merchant base, (2) cross-selling Worldpay's e-commerce capabilities to GPN's traditional merchant base and vice versa, (3) 500 salespeople targeted by mid-2026 (up from a 200-person expansion), and (4) membership in the European Payments Initiative for Wero instant payments.

Management's ~5% adjusted net revenue growth guidance for FY2026 is plausible given the combined scale, but it lags the industry leaders. The secular challenge remains: Stripe, Adyen, and Block are growing 20-40% annually while GPN is growing mid-single digits. Scale advantages in merchant acquiring are real (network effects, distribution breadth), but technology velocity matters in a market where merchants increasingly demand API-first, developer-friendly integrations.

### Synergy and Cost Reduction Path

The $600M+ cost savings target over three years is the quantifiable growth lever. If achieved, this represents approximately $2.20 per share in incremental EPS (at 275M diluted shares, 20% tax rate). Combined with the $7.5B buyback (potentially retiring 30-40% of outstanding shares at current prices), the mechanical EPS growth from cost savings and capital return alone could support 15-20% annual EPS growth through 2028 even with minimal revenue acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

### Absolute Valuation

At $68 and ~4.9x forward adjusted EPS of $13.78, GPN is trading at a level that implies either (a) secular decline, (b) integration failure, or (c) significant probability of both. The 5-year average forward P/E is approximately 15x. Even applying a 50% discount to account for integration risk and competitive concerns would yield a P/E of 7.5x, or approximately $103 per share.

On a FCF basis: $2.04B current FCF on an $18.7B market cap = 10.9% FCF yield. If FCF reaches $4B by 2027 (management target) and the share count is reduced by 15-20% through buybacks, FCF per share could approach $18-20, making today's price roughly 3.5x 2027 FCF.

### Relative Valuation

The peer median P/E of 26.2x makes GPN look absurdly cheap at 4.9x. Even FISV at 10.6x (the cheapest comparable) trades at more than double GPN's multiple. The wide discount reflects three factors: integration uncertainty, competitive share loss concerns, and the 3-miss earnings track record. The discount is partially justified but appears overdone on a probability-weighted basis.

### Floor Valuation

GTCR's 15% stake, Elliott Management's board involvement, and David Einhorn's re-entry at ~$78 create a soft valuation floor. CNBC reporting on LBO potential is relevant: at $68 with $5B in projected 2028 FCF, the private equity math is straightforward. GTCR's existing stake and board representation would facilitate a take-private.

## Competitive Landscape

GPN is now the largest merchant acquirer in the US by volume, competing with Fiserv, FIS, and Chase Paymentech among incumbents, and Stripe, Adyen, Block, Toast, and Shopify among technology-native disruptors. Incumbents have distribution scale and existing merchant relationships; disruptors have superior technology, developer ecosystems, and growth rates.

The Worldpay acquisition addresses the scale dimension but does not directly solve the technology velocity gap. The Genius POS consolidation and sales force expansion are incremental improvements rather than structural repositioning. GPN's vertical software strategy (restaurant, retail, education, real estate) provides some differentiation through bundled solutions, but individual verticals face their own specialized competitors (Toast in restaurants, Shopify in e-commerce).

The emerging risk of agentic AI commerce — where AI agents transact on behalf of consumers and may bypass traditional payment rails — is flagged in GPN's own 10-K risk factors. This is a long-tail risk but directionally relevant over a 3-5 year horizon.

## Risk Assessment

### Integration Execution (Critical)

The simultaneous Worldpay integration and Issuer Solutions separation is the dominant near-term risk. Management is running two complex programs in parallel across 37 countries and 26,000 employees. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges heightened complexity. Historical base rates for large-scale payments M&A integration are mixed (FIS/Worldpay in 2019 is the directly relevant cautionary precedent). The $600M cost savings target requires flawless execution across technology platforms, customer migrations, and organizational restructuring.

### Debt Burden

In a higher-for-longer rate environment with SOFR at 3.64% and rate hike probability rising, $650M+ annual interest expense is a meaningful drag. The 3.75x leverage covenant adds constraint. Any revenue shortfall or integration delay that impairs FCF could force difficult choices between deleveraging, buybacks, and investment.

### Secular Share Loss

Net revenue growth of ~2% in 2025 (reported basis) vs. 6% prior year reflects ongoing share loss to technology-native competitors. The Worldpay combination adds scale but does not guarantee a reversal of this trend. If the combined entity's organic growth fails to accelerate above mid-single digits, the valuation re-rating thesis fails regardless of cost savings.

### Credit Loss Exposure

The allowance for credit losses more than doubled to $50.2M from $24M, driven partly by Worldpay's high-risk merchant portfolio. This is a new and under-appreciated risk factor post-acquisition. In a consumer downturn, merchant failure rates rise, and GPN absorbs settlement losses.

### Consumer Spending Sensitivity

With consumer confidence at record lows (Michigan 47.6) and consumer spending showing five consecutive data points of weakness, GPN's transaction volume faces headwinds. Payment volume is directly tied to consumer spending. A recession scenario would compress both volumes and merchant survival rates.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 46.4% is modestly rich versus 42.3% 1-year historical volatility (spread of 4.1%), and the 31st percentile IV rank suggests the market is not pricing extreme stress despite the stock's decline. The elevated put/call skew of 6.9% in near-term options and 1.23 OI put/call ratio indicate persistent hedging demand. The 6-month put/call volume ratio of 2.50 is elevated, suggesting institutional positioning for further downside over that horizon. The term structure is in contango (near-term 46.4% < long-term 48.7%), consistent with sustained uncertainty rather than an imminent catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95–110, 40–62% upside)

Worldpay integration executes on schedule, delivering $600M+ in cost savings by H1 2027. Organic revenue growth re-accelerates to 6-8% as the Genius POS platform gains traction and Worldpay's e-commerce capabilities cross-sell effectively. Adjusted FCF reaches $4B+ by 2027. The $7.5B buyback reduces share count by 25-30% at depressed prices. Multiple re-rates from ~5x to 8-10x forward earnings as execution proves out. Elliott's board oversight and GTCR's 15% stake maintain strategic discipline. Einhorn's re-entry at ~$78 validates the value thesis.

### Bear Case ($45–55, 19–34% downside)

Integration stumbles: customer attrition, technology platform conflicts, or delayed synergy realization. Consumer recession compresses transaction volumes by 5-10%, impacting revenue while fixed costs from the integration persist. Credit losses from Worldpay's high-risk merchant portfolio escalate in a downturn. Organic growth stagnates at 2-3%, confirming secular share loss to Stripe/Adyen. Rating agencies downgrade debt, raising borrowing costs. The stock trades to 4x a revised-down EPS of $12, implying $48. Goodwill impairment risk from Worldpay overpayment becomes a write-down catalyst.

### What Changed Since the Prior BUY at Higher Prices

The prior BUY (issued February 2026 at approximately $72) has lost 5.1%. What has worsened: (1) Current-quarter estimates have been cut 4.4% over 90 days, confirming negative earnings momentum. (2) The stock has broken through the February lows, establishing a new 52-week low of $62.45. (3) The macro environment has deteriorated substantially — record-low consumer sentiment, US naval blockade of Iranian ports driving oil above $102, and rising credit cascade probability all weigh on a consumer-spending-sensitive business. (4) The 3-miss-in-4-quarters pattern has not been broken by subsequent data. What has not changed: the FCF thesis, the buyback commitment, the valuation discount, and the Elliott/GTCR governance alignment remain intact. The bull case is directionally the same but requires a longer timeline given the worsened macro backdrop.

The prior BUY was premature. The stock needed either (a) stabilization of near-term estimates, (b) a first quarter of Worldpay-combined results demonstrating execution, or (c) a macro environment not actively deteriorating. None of these conditions have been met.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **First post-Worldpay quarterly results (expected May/June 2026) showing combined organic revenue growth ≥5% and adjusted operating margin expansion ≥100bps.** This would confirm early integration traction and break the miss pattern.
2. **Current-quarter estimate revisions stabilizing or turning positive** (30-day revision trend moving from -3.6% to flat or positive).
3. **Consumer confidence or spending data showing stabilization**, removing the macro headwind to transaction volumes.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **First post-Worldpay results showing organic revenue growth ≤2% or margin compression**, indicating integration is not delivering.
2. **Credit rating downgrade** by any major agency.
3. **Management reduces FY2026 guidance** (currently ~5% net revenue growth, 13-15% EPS growth).

### Reassessment Timeline

Next natural reassessment: Q1 FY2026 earnings report (expected May 2026), which will be the first quarter reflecting Worldpay-combined operations.

## Conclusion

GPN presents a genuine value proposition at 4.9x forward adjusted earnings; the market is pricing in significant probability of integration failure and sustained competitive decline. The FCF generation, buyback commitment, and activist/PE governance alignment provide real downside support. However, the miss pattern, negative near-term estimate revisions, elevated debt, and a deteriorating consumer macro environment prevent conviction on timing. The prior BUY lost money because it was issued before the thesis had confirmatory evidence; repeating that call now, with a worse macro backdrop, would compound the mistake.

The correct posture is HOLD with close monitoring of the first Worldpay-combined quarterly results. If those results demonstrate execution (5%+ organic revenue growth, margin expansion, EPS beat), the valuation gap becomes actionable. If they disappoint, the stock likely tests the mid-$50s and the thesis requires fundamental reassessment.
