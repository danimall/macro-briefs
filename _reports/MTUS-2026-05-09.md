## Executive Summary

Metallus Inc is a specialty steel manufacturer at a cyclical trough with multiple catalysts that could drive meaningful earnings recovery over the next 12-18 months. The company's order book is up 40%+ year-over-year, government-funded defense capacity is ramping through H2 2026, Section 232 tariffs provide structural protection, and pension obligations are declining. At zero debt with $157M cash (20% of market cap), the balance sheet supports the turnaround thesis.

The critical challenge is timing. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative (-30.9% for FY2026 over 90 days), indicating analysts see the recovery trajectory more slowly than the Q1 2026 beat might suggest. The company earned essentially zero in 2025, and while Q1 2026 showed inflection ($24.6M adjusted EBITDA vs. $17.7M a year earlier), the annualized run-rate of ~$100M EBITDA still places this at approximately 6x EV/EBITDA — reasonable for a steel company but not screaming cheap given the macro backdrop. Peace deal dynamics in oil markets and potential recession represent demand risk for industrial and automotive end markets that constitute 76% of revenue.

The stock has appreciated 41% over the past year from its $11 low, largely driven by tariff optimism and the defense contract narrative. At $18.45, it trades near the middle of its 52-week range ($11-$21.73). The setup favors holding through the H2 2026 ramp-up period rather than initiating at current levels, with clear upgrade triggers tied to EBITDA acceleration and volume shipments.

## Company Overview

Metallus Inc (formerly TimkenSteel) manufactures specialty alloy, carbon, and micro-alloy steel using electric arc furnace technology from ~100% recycled scrap. Based entirely in Canton, Ohio, the company operates three primary steel facilities with ~1.2M tons annual melt capacity and ~0.9M tons shipment capacity, plus two downstream precision component plants.

Revenue breaks down as: Automotive 41% ($472M), Industrial 35% ($408M), Aerospace & Defense 14% ($161M), Energy 9% ($99M). The company produces 500+ steel grades for demanding applications including transmissions, artillery bodies, rocket motors, and drill pipe.

The 2024-2025 period represented a cyclical trough: revenue dropped from $1.36B (2023) to $1.08B (2024) before recovering slightly to $1.16B (2025), while adjusted EBITDA collapsed from $200M to ~$76M. The company is now in early-stage recovery with lead times extending to late Q3 2026 and a $99.75M government-funded defense capacity expansion reaching operational status.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**

- FCF: -$93M (2025) — misleading given one-time pension spike and government-reimbursed capex

- Shares outstanding reduced 26% since 2022 via buybacks at average $14.53-$15.50

**Key Concern:** Gross margins compressed to 8.2% from 13.7% in 2023. At current SG&A of ~$94M, the company needs approximately $95M in gross profit just to reach operating breakeven. The operating leverage cuts both ways — small volume/price improvements generate outsized profit gains, but the margin of safety is thin.

## Valuation Assessment

Traditional metrics are difficult to apply: trailing P/E of 264x reflects near-zero trailing earnings at the cyclical trough. Forward P/E is similarly distorted.

**Enterprise Value Framework:**
- Market cap: $768M
- Less cash: $157M
- Enterprise value: ~$611M
- 2025 adjusted EBITDA: $76M → EV/EBITDA: 8.0x
- Q1 2026 annualized EBITDA (~$100M): EV/EBITDA: 6.1x
- Normalized EBITDA ($130-150M — below 2023 peak): EV/EBITDA: 4.1-4.7x

**Peer Context:** The peer set is messy (SXC, AMR, CLF all negative earnings; WS at 17.5x; HCC at 33.5x). Among profitable steel peers, Metallus at 6x current-run-rate EBITDA appears reasonable — neither cheap enough to be an obvious mispricing nor expensive enough to warrant concern, given the recovery thesis.

**Price vs. Analyst Targets:** Consensus mean $22.50 (22% upside), range $21-$24. With only 2 analysts covering, these targets are low-confidence but directionally consistent with the recovery thesis.

**Buyback Signal:** Management has been aggressive buyers at $14.53-$15.50 average. At $18.45, the stock trades 19-27% above these repurchase levels, suggesting management may slow buyback pace unless they believe normalized value is substantially higher.

Cash represents 20% of market cap, providing meaningful downside support. The stock would need to fall to ~$4/share before the balance sheet itself becomes a concern.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Advantages:**
- Section 232 tariffs (50%) create a structural moat against foreign SBQ steel imports (Gerdau, Tenaris, Vallourec, ArcelorMittal)
- "Melted and poured" requirements for defense applications limit supply to domestic-only producers
- Technical expertise (50% of sales team has engineering backgrounds) creates switching costs in high-performance applications
- 500+ steel grade portfolio with application-specific engineering support

**Competitive Trajectory — Improving:**
- A&D growing from 8% (2023) → 14% (2025) → targeting 20%+ of sales — higher-margin, stickier business
- Government-funded capacity expansion (effectively free) improves cost position vs. competitors who must self-fund
- Tariff expansion to derivative products (April 2026) further insulates supply chain domestically

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Single geographic location (Canton, Ohio) — competitors with multiple plants have resilience advantage
- Customer concentration (top 10 = 50% of sales) creates single-point-of-failure risk
- Smaller scale vs. Nucor, SDI, CLF limits pricing power in commodity-oriented products

## Risk Assessment

**1. Steel Cycle / Recession Risk (HIGH):** With 76% of revenue tied to industrial and automotive end markets, demand destruction is the primary threat. The 2024-2025 experience shows how quickly profitability evaporates when volumes decline 15-20%.

**2. Tariff Reversal Risk (MODERATE):** Section 232 tariffs are an executive order instrument. While unlikely under the current administration, a policy shift would immediately expose domestic producers to foreign competition, particularly from Gerdau (Brazil) and Asian mills.

**3. Pension Volatility (MODERATE):** $91.7M underfunding with annual remeasurement swings ($6-40M). The pension-to-401(k) election window (through May 30, 2026) could structurally reduce this risk, but participation rates are unknown.

**4. New Electricity Contract (LOW-MODERATE):** Favorable long-term contract expired May 2025. New structure (70% fixed, 30% spot) creates permanent cost headwind and margin sensitivity to energy prices.

**5. Concentration Risk (MODERATE):** All production in one county, one customer >10% of sales, automotive as 41% of revenue.

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) is the natural test. By then, the bloom reheat furnace should be at full operational capacity, pricing actions should be more fully reflected, and the cycle direction should be clearer.

## Conclusion

Metallus presents a legitimate cyclical recovery thesis with multiple specific catalysts (government-funded capacity, defense ramp, tariff protection, pension reduction) and a clean balance sheet. The Q1 2026 beat confirms initial inflection. However, the stock has already appreciated 41% from its low, estimate revisions remain uniformly negative, and the macro environment creates meaningful headwinds to the very end markets that must recover for the thesis to work.

At $18.45, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock is correctly positioned between the $11 trough and $21.73 peak. The path to $22-24 requires multiple quarters of executed improvement in an uncertain macro backdrop. For investors already holding, the catalysts justify patience. For new positions, waiting for either (a) a pullback below $16 or (b) confirmation of EBITDA acceleration in Q2 2026 offers better entry points with clearer asymmetry.
