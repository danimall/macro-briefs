## Executive Summary

Assurant's August 4 second-quarter report was the strongest print of the current cycle. GAAP net income rose 27% to $298.6M ($5.95 diluted), adjusted EPS of $6.41 beat the $5.23 consensus by 22.5%, and adjusted EPS excluding catastrophes rose 19% to $6.60. Both segments contributed: Global Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA +21% to $244.4M, Global Housing +28% to $274.8M (+18% ex-catastrophe). Management raised the 2026 outlook for the second time this year and now expects buybacks toward the upper end of the $300–350M range, with $544M of authorization remaining at July 31. The shares rose 6.3% on August 5 to a close of $301.57, within 1% of the 52-week high of $303.94.

Two things in the guidance deserve attention because they pull in opposite directions. First, the guide is conservative by construction: it assumes zero favorable prior-year reserve development in H2 2026 against $41.9M realized in H1 and $113.1M in FY2025, a $71M year-over-year headwind that management quantifies explicitly. At the guided "mid single digits" growth off the $22.81 FY2025 ex-catastrophe base, the implied H2 2026 result is roughly $11.04 versus $11.48 in H2 2025 — a 3.8% decline embedded in the guide. If H2 non-catastrophe frequency merely normalizes and any PYD emerges, reported ex-cat EPS beats. Second, the same quarter that produced the beat leaned on drivers management labelled as non-repeatable: Housing's gain came substantially from "lower than typical claims frequency" plus lower catastrophe reinsurance costs, and $10M of Connected Living's improvement was disclosed as non-run-rate.

The valuation asymmetry that supported a BUY eighteen months ago has largely been harvested. At $295.24 the stock trades at 13.4x the FY2026 quarterly-build consensus (~$22.00), 13.1x the FY2027 consensus of $22.56, 12.5x trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS of $23.68, 17.4x trailing GAAP EPS of $16.93, and ~2.4x June 30 book value of ~$122/share. A year ago at $188 the multiple was closer to 9x. Price is at the consensus median target of $295 against a mean of $293, a high of $310 and a low of $274, with six covering analysts all at buy or strong buy — target headroom is effectively exhausted until estimates are revised. HOLD, classified as a growth hold: base case $320 (+8.4%), bull $365 (+24%), bear $235 (-20%).

## Company Overview

Assurant operates a business-to-business-to-consumer protection and lifecycle-services model, embedding products inside the distribution channels of large corporate clients rather than selling directly to retail consumers. The client set is mobile carriers and MSOs, electronics and appliance retailers, auto dealer groups, banks and card issuers, mortgage servicers and property managers.

**Global Lifestyle** generated FY2025 net earned premiums, fees and other income of $9,582.5M. Connected Living ($5,378.7M) covers mobile device protection and lifecycle services — extended service contracts, trade-in and upgrade programs, repair, refurbishment and disposition across roughly 1,150 repair and partner locations — plus extended service contracts on consumer electronics and appliances and a growing financial-services block. Global Automotive ($4,203.8M) sells vehicle service contracts, GAP and ancillary coverage through dealer channels, and provides lender risk management for commercial equipment.

**Global Housing** generated $2,768.8M. Homeowners ($2,192.4M) is dominated by lender-placed (force-placed) coverage, where Assurant tracks insurance status directly inside mortgage servicer platforms and automatically issues coverage when a borrower's voluntary policy lapses. Renters and Other ($576.4M) includes renters insurance through property managers and fee income as the second-largest NFIP administrator, which is 100% reinsured to the federal government and carries no retained underwriting risk.

**Corporate and Other** houses holding-company costs and the home-warranty build-out; its adjusted EBITDA loss was $(123.8)M in FY2025 and is guided to approximately $(145)M in 2026.

The structural advantage sits in the integration: Assurant underwrites, administers and physically repairs or refurbishes, and its tracking systems are wired into client platforms. Most lender-placed agreements and many Lifestyle agreements are exclusive with three-to-five-year terms. The counter-reading, which the 10-K itself concedes, is that these are outsourced functions where the client owns the end customer and can insource — Item 1A states clients "may disintermediate us by developing internal capabilities... which has occurred from time to time."

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 total revenues rose 7.9% to $12,814.3M, with net earned premiums +7.0% to $10,482.9M and fees and other income +14.5% to $1,875.9M. Pre-tax income rose 17.3% to $1,087.4M and net income 14.8% to $872.7M, with the effective tax rate up to 19.7% from 18.0%. Adjusted EPS excluding catastrophes was $22.81 versus GAAP diluted EPS of $16.93 — a $5.88 (26%) gap composed of reportable catastrophes, purchased-intangible amortization and realized investment losses.

The five-year adjusted ex-catastrophe EPS series ($12.28 in 2021 to $22.81 in 2025) compounds at 16.7%, but three components of that record are not fully repeatable: favorable prior-year development, a ~2.5% annual reduction in share count, and Housing's placement-rate tailwind.

**Segment trajectories diverge.** Global Housing adjusted EBITDA went $574.2M (2023) → $671.2M (2024) → $858.7M (2025); excluding catastrophes, $916.4M → $1,057.5M in 2025, +15.4%. Global Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA was nearly flat over the same window ($792.3M → $773.4M → $801.3M) while segment revenue grew 11.9%, compressing the implied EBITDA margin from 9.3% to 8.4% as trade-in and reverse-logistics cost of sales grew 17% in FY2025 and 43% in Q1 2026 Connected Living. Global Automotive revenue was $4,184.6M → $4,159.4M → $4,203.8M, +0.5% cumulative over two years, with FY2025 earnings improvement sourced from loss experience and investment income rather than volume.

Q1 and Q2 2026 broke the Lifestyle margin pattern: segment adjusted EBITDA rose 20% then 21% against revenue growth well below that. Adjusting for the disclosed $10M non-run-rate item, Q2 Connected Living earnings still grew 22%. Two quarters is not a trend, and management's "low double digits" 2026 segment guide implies the margin gains persist but do not accelerate.

**Balance sheet and liquidity are a genuine strength.** Debt was $2,208.1M carrying at June 30, 2026, giving debt-to-total-capital of 26.6% (2,208.1 / (2,208.1 + 6,097.9)). The maturity ladder has no wall: 4.90% $300M due 2028 is the next maturity, after an August 2025 $300M issue at 5.55% funded redemption of the $175M 6.10% notes. A $500M five-year unsecured revolver entered June 2025 was undrawn through 2025, and the commercial paper program is unused. FY2025 interest coverage was 10.9x ((1,087.4 + 109.7) / 109.7). Holding-company liquidity was $911M at June 30 against a self-imposed $225M minimum, with $925.1M upstreamed from subsidiaries in FY2025 and roughly $791.9M of ordinary dividend capacity available in 2026 without regulatory approval. A.M. Best rates most domestic insurance subsidiaries A+; holding-company senior debt is BBB (S&P) / Baa2 (Moody's), both stable.

FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,833.9M versus $1,332.7M, but that figure should not be annualized as free cash flow: it includes cash received on assumption of a new Connected Living block, lower mobile inventory outflows, and a $145M swing in cash taxes paid ($271.6M vs $126.6M). No standalone capex line was available; guided 2026 depreciation of ~$180M is the only proxy.

Two balance-sheet qualifications matter. Goodwill of $2,646.3M equals 45% of total equity, with $1,521.9M assigned to Global Automotive — the segment with flat revenue for three years. A quantitative test at October 1, 2025 found no impairment. And total liabilities to equity of 4.92x is a function of $21.1B of unearned premiums and reserves, which is the shape of the business rather than a leverage signal; the meaningful solvency evidence is the ratings, coverage and holdco liquidity above.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance for 2026 is mid-single-digit growth in both adjusted EBITDA ex-catastrophe (off $1,734M) and adjusted EPS ex-catastrophe (off $22.81), or approximately 10% excluding the $71M of lower favorable Housing PYD. Segment guidance: Lifestyle low double digits, Housing ex-cat "grow modestly," Corporate and Other loss ~$(145)M.

Estimate revisions are supportive but not an inflection: current-year consensus is $21.09, up 2.7% over 90 days; next year $22.56, up 1.8%; current quarter $5.19, up 0.5% over 30 days. (The annual figures are internally inconsistent with the quarterly build — Q1 actual $5.93 plus Q2 actual $6.41 plus Q3E $5.19 plus Q4E $4.47 sums to ~$22.00 — so I use the quarterly build for FY2026 arithmetic and note the discrepancy.) The revision magnitude is nowhere near the +30% threshold that flags a genuine earnings inflection; this is a stable mid-single-digit compounder with a beat-prone guide, not a re-rating candidate on revision velocity.

The beat record is the strongest forward-confidence input: four consecutive beats at +32.7%, +0.9%, +11.3% and +22.5%. Combined with the zero-PYD H2 assumption, that argues FY2026 lands above the guide. If H2 2026 ex-cat EPS merely matches H2 2025's $11.48, the full year prints $24.39 (+6.9%); at 5% H2 growth, $24.96 (+9.4%).

Sources of durable growth: renters (a new book acquired in 2025, digital attachment investment), specialty Housing products, dealer-channel expansion into heavy equipment and leased portfolios, financial services within Connected Living (10.5% → 12.6% of segment revenue), and ~2.5% annual share-count reduction. Sources of doubt: Automotive volume has not grown in three years; domestic extended service contracts are declining (implied electronics/appliance ESC revenue fell roughly 5% from 2023 to 2025 while mobile grew ~45%, taking mobile from 44.8% to 53.0% of Connected Living); and the home-warranty adjacency is being funded out of reported earnings with no disclosed revenue, subscriber or margin data — a ~$21M incremental annual drag against an unverifiable payoff.

## Competitive Landscape

No single competitor overlaps all of Assurant's lines. That diversifies competitive exposure and eliminates a natural valuation comparable, which may partly explain the persistent relative discount.

In mobile, Assurant's ownership of the full device lifecycle — protection, trade-in, repair, refurbishment, resale — is difficult to replicate and is expanding with the used-device market. The economics are thinner than the revenue suggests: trade-in volume drives cost of sales up 17–43%, and a meaningful revenue driver is carrier promotional calendars that Assurant does not control. The Q2 evidence that Lifestyle EBITDA can grow at 21% on 9% revenue growth is the single most encouraging competitive datapoint in this cycle, and it is why I mark competitive position up modestly from the prior review.

In automotive, flat revenue for three years against active channel expansion suggests initiatives are offsetting attrition rather than exceeding it. That is where the largest goodwill block sits.

In housing, Assurant is capturing flow created by other carriers' withdrawal, and simultaneously paying less for catastrophe reinsurance. Both blades of that scissors are cyclical. The tracking infrastructure inside servicer platforms is the actual moat, and it is the thing regulators have addressed before: the 2017 multistate Regulatory Settlement Agreement requires more frequent lender-placed rate filings, and Item 1A warns regulators "may cause additional changes to the structure of the lender-placed insurance industry, including the arrangements under which we track coverage."

## Risk Assessment

**Client concentration with disclosed disintermediation history.** Item 1A states both segments receive a substantial portion of revenue from a few clients, that clients have insourced "from time to time," and that concentration weakens bargaining power on renewal, potentially requiring up-front concessions. Assurant discloses no client names or revenue percentages, so the exposure cannot be sized from the filings. Meanwhile the product mix is shifting toward mobile carriers, the channel management itself identifies as the dependency.

**Lender-placed cyclicality and adverse selection are the same phenomenon.** Elevated placement rates raise revenue while automatically loading catastrophe-exposed geographies Assurant cannot decline. If admitted carriers re-enter California and Texas as rate adequacy is restored, the volume tailwind unwinds with no operational failure. Management's guide of "grow modestly" for Housing ex-cat implicitly leans this way.

**Reserve development normalization is quantified and near-term.** H1 2026 favorable PYD was $41.9M versus $63.4M; the guide assumes zero in H2. Favorable development has run at least three consecutive years, and redundancy once released cannot be released again.

**Catastrophe exposure into H2.** The U.S. program provides $1.76B in excess of a $160.0M per-occurrence retention (approximately $1.98B in excess of retention for Florida including FHCF), with one automatic reinstatement on layers 1–6. Reportable catastrophes were $198.2M in FY2025 and $186.8M in H1 2025 alone. Guidance is stated ex-catastrophe, so a heavy hurricane season would not breach the guide but would compress GAAP earnings and, at a 52-week high, sentiment.

**Goodwill at 45% of equity**, concentrated in the flat-revenue Automotive unit, with management compensated on measures that exclude purchased-intangible amortization and realized investment results.

**Operational control risk.** Item 1A twice concedes reliance on "significant manual processes" pending completion of a global financial system implementation. ICFR was certified effective at December 31, 2025.

Offsetting: no maturity until March 2028, undrawn $500M revolver, $911M holdco liquidity, 10.9x interest coverage, improved unrealized bond position ($(55.7)M from $(349.7)M), and the March 2026 disposal of the runoff subsidiary carrying a $472.0M John Hancock recoverable against a $632.0M 30-year gross reserve — a long-tail contingency retired for an $11.2M accounting loss, and confirmed complete (held-for-sale balances at zero on the June 30 balance sheet).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $365 (+24%).** The Lifestyle margin inflection is real and holds: two quarters of 20%+ segment EBITDA growth on single-digit revenue growth become four, validated by the "low double digits" 2026 guide, and the market stops discounting Lifestyle revenue as low-margin volume. Housing's placement-rate elevation proves structural rather than cyclical because climate-driven loss costs permanently reduce voluntary appetite in wildfire and convective-storm zones. H2 2026 PYD emerges anywhere near H1's pace, so reported ex-cat EPS exceeds the mid-single-digit guide and consensus revises FY2027 well above $22.56. The Miami sale closes, adding roughly $80M pre-tax. Buybacks at the upper end of $300–350M plus ~2.5% annual share shrink compound the per-share result. FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$25.50 at 14.5x = $365.

**Base case — $320 (+8.4%).** FY2026 lands modestly above the guide at $24.3–24.5 ex-catastrophe on a normal cat load; FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~$24.00 as Lifestyle grows low double digits, Housing grows modestly ex-PYD, Automotive stays flat and Corporate drag widens to $(145)M. The multiple holds near 13.5x, which is where the market has settled after this year's re-rating. 13.5x × $24.00 = $324, rounded to $320. Total return with the 1.19% dividend is roughly 10%.

**Bear case — $235 (-20%).** An above-average Atlantic season pushes reportable catastrophes past $300M, compressing GAAP EPS; H2 PYD is genuinely zero; non-catastrophe frequency normalizes from "lower than typical"; voluntary carriers begin re-entering California and Texas, and placement rates roll over. Lifestyle margin gains stall as trade-in mix reasserts, and a major carrier renegotiates on renewal with up-front concessions. FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$20.00 at 11.5x — the low end of the multiple range the market applied for five years — equals $230.

The distribution is modestly positive but not wide enough to justify new capital at 13.4x on earnings running above normalized, in a sector where selection has historically added little above index beta.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report (early November) is the decisive checkpoint. It carries the Atlantic hurricane season outcome, the first test of the zero-PYD H2 assumption, and the third data point on Lifestyle margin durability. The Q4 report in February 2026 brings 2027 guidance and the annual goodwill test.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Price reset to $250–260** with fundamentals intact — that is ~11.5x FY2027 consensus and restores 20%+ upside to the base case.
2. **Global Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA growth above 15% in Q3 and Q4 with no non-run-rate items disclosed**, confirming that trade-in mix no longer dilutes segment margin. Combined with Housing ex-cat EBITDA growth ex-PYD above 8%, that would justify a 15x multiple and a base case above $360.
3. **Disclosure that materially reduces the concentration verification gap** — a named multi-year carrier renewal with disclosed economics, or customer-concentration percentages in the FY2026 10-K showing no client above ~10% of revenue.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Reportable catastrophes above $350M in H2 2026**, or any single event breaching the $160M retention with reinstatement drawn, which would reset the earnings base and the reinsurance cost line simultaneously.
2. **Housing placement-rate reversal evidence** — Homeowners net earned premium declining year over year for two consecutive quarters, or management commentary confirming voluntary carrier re-entry in California/Texas.
3. **Loss or material renegotiation of a mobile carrier or large servicer relationship**, or Global Automotive goodwill impairment against the $1,521.9M balance.
4. **Adjusted EPS ex-cat guidance cut below the current mid-single-digit range**, which would break the ten-year profitable-growth record management is explicitly managing to.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this review was 7.0 → 7.0 → 6.9 → 6.4 → 6.0, driven by valuation compression as the price rose, Lifestyle margin erosion, and insider selling with no offsetting purchases. Q2 2026 partially reverses one of those three: Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA grew 21% on 9% revenue growth, the second consecutive quarter of margin expansion, which raises competitive position modestly. Management quality improves slightly on capital-allocation evidence — buybacks were deliberately throttled from $125M in Q1 (average $224.73) to $75M in Q2 and $30M in July as the price rose, which is the discipline you want, and the dividend at $3.52 annualized is covered at 15.4% of adjusted ex-cat EPS. The five open-market insider sales totalling ~$9.6M between March and June, headlined by the CFO reducing 40,063 shares to 18,552, all followed the March 16 vesting event and were executed at $210–$263 versus $295 today; that timing argues against much information content. Financial health remains the strongest dimension: 26.6% debt-to-capital, no maturity until 2028, 10.9x coverage, $911M holdco liquidity and a retired long-tail LTC contingency.

Valuation is where the case stalls. At $295.24 the stock is 13.4x FY2026 and 13.1x FY2027 consensus, 2.4x book, at the consensus median target, 2.8% below its 52-week high, and up 57% in twelve months on a multiple that expanded from roughly 9x. The earnings supporting that multiple include favorable prior-year development that is being deliberately zeroed out, non-catastrophe claim frequency management calls lower than typical, softening reinsurance costs, and lender-placed placement rates elevated by other carriers' retreat. Those are all real earnings; they are not normalized earnings. Overall score 6.1, up marginally on the Lifestyle inflection. HOLD as a growth hold — the compounding is intact and the guide is beatable, but the entry price no longer offers the asymmetry required to add.
