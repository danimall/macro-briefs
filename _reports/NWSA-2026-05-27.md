**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

News Corp is a diversified media and information services company that has undergone material structural improvement over the past 18 months, divesting Foxtel, reducing debt by $920M, accumulating $2.4B in cash (as of FY2025 year-end), and demonstrating accelerating reported revenue growth from 2% (FY2025) to 9% reported (Q3 FY2026, of which approximately 5 percentage points was attributable to favorable FX). The stock trades at $26.41, implying a market cap of approximately $14.6B based on ~554M diluted shares outstanding. News Corp's 61.4% stake in ASX-listed REA Group is worth an estimated US$8.5–12B depending on REA's market capitalization and prevailing AUD/USD exchange rates, representing a substantial portion of News Corp's equity value and suggesting the market assigns a meaningful discount to the remaining businesses: Dow Jones ($588M EBITDA), HarperCollins ($296M EBITDA), Move, and News Media.

The company has delivered strong recent results, with Q3 FY2026 revenue of $2.185B beating analyst expectations. Revenue growth is positive across the three highest-quality segments (Dow Jones +8%, Digital Real Estate +17%, Book Publishing +8% in Q3 FY2026 on a reported basis), though on a constant-currency basis, growth is more moderate (Dow Jones +6%, Digital Real Estate +8%, Book Publishing +4%, News Media -2%). Management has authorized approximately $1.3B in buybacks (roughly 9% of current market cap) and has the cash to execute. AI content licensing deals with OpenAI and Meta are generating revenue now, with additional agreements in negotiation.

The primary risks preventing the market from closing this discount are governance-related (Murdoch family dual-class control prevents takeout), AI disruption uncertainty (could devalue traditional content), and U.S. housing market weakness (affecting Move/Realtor.com). These are real but may be priced too aggressively at current levels.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates five segments after the April 2025 Foxtel divestiture:

- **Dow Jones (28% of revenue, $2.33B):** The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, and professional information services including Risk & Compliance (+15% growth in FY2025), Dow Jones Energy (+11% in FY2025), and Factiva. Digital revenues represent 82% of segment total. Management has stated a pathway to $1B segment EBITDA within 5 years (from $588M), though the company notes this is not a profit forecast.
- **Digital Real Estate Services (21%, $1.80B):** 61.4% stake in REA Group (Australia's dominant property platform) and 80% of Move (Realtor.com). REA commands 4x traffic vs. its nearest competitor in Australia.
- **Book Publishing (25%, $2.15B):** HarperCollins, the world's #2 consumer publisher. Backlist titles contribute 64% of consumer revenues, providing stability.
- **News Media (26%, $2.17B):** News Corp Australia, News UK (The Sun, The Times), New York Post. Secular decline in print offset by digital subscription growth.
- **Other:** Corporate overhead ($223M negative EBITDA).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Acceleration:** Continuing operations revenue grew 2% in FY2025. In Q3 FY2026, reported revenue grew 9% YoY to $2.185B; however, on a constant-currency basis, revenue growth was approximately 4%, with a favorable $88M / 5 percentage-point FX impact. The acceleration on a reported basis is broad-based, though on an adjusted basis News Media declined 2%.

**Profitability Inflection:** Total Segment EBITDA grew 14% to $1.415B in FY2025. Q3 FY2026 Segment EBITDA grew 18% to $343M. Net income from continuing operations rose 71% to $648M in FY2025. FCF was $571M (6.8% margin), which understates underlying cash generation due to $120M in restructuring charges.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:** Post-Foxtel sale, FY2025 year-end cash stood at $2.4B against $1.94B long-term debt. By March 31, 2026, cash had declined to $2.171B and borrowings were $1.988B, leaving a narrow net cash position of approximately $183M. Approximately $280M of consolidated cash is held by REA Group and is not readily accessible to News Corp unless REA declares a dividend. Current ratio: 1.84x.

**Capital Return:** Approximately $1.3B in total buyback authorization as of July 2025. By March 31, 2026, the company had already spent $456M on share repurchases in the first nine months of FY2026, reducing remaining authorization. At current FCF generation ($571M annually) plus the existing cash buffer, the company has meaningful capacity for continued buybacks.

**EPS Trajectory:** FY2025 diluted EPS from continuing operations was $0.84 (total diluted EPS including the Foxtel gain was $2.07). Forward estimates of $1.08 (FY2026E) and $1.29 (FY2027E) represent 29% and 19% growth respectively from continuing operations, implying a forward P/E of 24.5x on FY2026E and 20.5x on FY2027E.

## Growth Analysis

Growth drivers are specific and measurable:

1. **Dow Jones Professional Information ($903M in FY2025, with key subcomponents growing 11-15%):** Total professional information revenue grew 7% in FY2025, led by Risk & Compliance (+15%) and Dow Jones Energy (+11%). In Q3 FY2026, professional information accelerated to +11% growth, with Risk & Compliance hitting $100M quarterly revenue (+19% YoY). This is enterprise SaaS-like recurring revenue with high retention rates.

2. **REA Group (+12% in FY2025, +17% reported Q3 FY2026):** Dominant Australian property platform with pricing power. Growth driven by depth product penetration and yield management on listings. Australia's housing market is healthier than the U.S.

3. **AI Content Licensing:** Active deals with OpenAI and Meta generating revenue now. Additional agreements in negotiation. CEO Thomson has positioned News Corp as one of the most aggressive negotiators in the space. Management has indicated expectations of receiving funds from the Anthropic settlement with authors/publishers, though specific financial terms have not been disclosed.

4. **U.S. Housing Recovery (potential catalyst, not priced):** Move/Realtor.com lead volumes declined 9% in FY2025, depressed by elevated mortgage rates. Any normalization in rates or transaction volumes provides meaningful optionality. This is currently a headwind, which is precisely why it represents potential upside.

5. **HarperCollins Digital:** Audiobook streaming partnerships (Spotify) and digital revenues growing within the backlist-heavy portfolio.

**Growth risk:** Next-quarter estimate revisions are negative (-10.7% over 30 days). This appears tied to News Media seasonality and currency headwinds rather than structural deterioration. Full-year FY2027 estimates are rising (+3.8% over 90 days).

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation thesis centers on a conglomerate discount that appears excessive given the quality of the underlying businesses:

**Sum-of-Parts (Illustrative):**
- REA Group 61.4% stake: approximately US$8.5–12B (based on REA's ASX market cap, which has fluctuated between A$21B–$31B in recent months, converted at AUD/USD ~0.65)
- Dow Jones: $588M EBITDA × 15x (conservative for professional information services) = $8.8B
- HarperCollins: $296M EBITDA × 8x = $2.4B
- Move: Depressed but $500M–$1B on a recovery scenario
- News Media: $153M EBITDA × 5x = $765M
- Net cash: ~$183M (as of March 2026)
- **Illustrative Sum: ~$21–25B** vs. ~$14.6B market cap

This implies a conglomerate discount of approximately 35–42%. The discount reflects the governance overhang (dual-class structure), the complexity of the portfolio, and market skepticism about the lower-quality News Media segment.

**Traditional Multiples:**
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 10x on FY2025 segment EBITDA of $1.415B (using ~$14.6B equity value, $2.0B debt, less $2.2B cash = ~$14.4B EV)
- Forward P/E: 24.5x FY2026E, 20.5x FY2027E — in-line with peer median of 24.0x
- P/FCF: approximately 25.6x on FY2025 FCF of $571M

**Why the discount may narrow:** The market prices News Corp as a declining media conglomerate rather than a portfolio increasingly dominated by high-recurring-revenue information services (Dow Jones professional) and digital platform economics (REA Group). The Foxtel divestiture and accelerating reported growth in Q3 FY2026 are data points the market hasn't fully re-rated. The buyback creates a mechanical catalyst for per-share value accrual.

## Competitive Landscape

**Improving trajectory in highest-quality businesses:**

- Dow Jones Risk & Compliance growing 15-19% competes with a fragmented market (no single dominant player). The product benefits from increasing global regulatory complexity, a secular tailwind.
- REA Group's 4x traffic advantage in Australia is a network-effects moat. Competitors (Domain, Facebook Marketplace) have failed to meaningfully erode share over the past decade.
- HarperCollins benefits from scale advantages in an industry where distribution (physical and digital) favors large publishers. Backlist catalog provides high-margin recurring revenue.

**Deteriorating in lower-quality businesses:**

- News Media faces structural print decline and algorithm-driven traffic loss. Google's algorithm changes have materially reduced traffic to The Sun's digital properties.
- Move/Realtor.com has lost market share to Zillow over the past 5 years. The NAR settlement creates additional uncertainty.

Net competitive position score reflects the tension: the growing businesses (60%+ of EBITDA) have strong moats, while the shrinking businesses (News Media) face secular headwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Governance/Control Premium Impossibility (Primary Discount Driver):** Murdoch family holds ~40.6% of voting stock through dual-class structure. No takeover is possible without family consent. Lachlan Murdoch serves as both News Corp Chair and Fox Corp CEO, an acknowledged conflict. This caps upside because the most efficient path to closing the SOTP discount (sale of the company, spin-offs) requires family initiative.

**2. AI Disruption:** Generative AI could reduce traffic to owned properties via summarization. While News Corp is monetizing AI through licensing today, there's no guarantee renewal rates hold, or that AI companies won't develop alternative content sourcing methods. The legal landscape is genuinely uncertain.

**3. U.S. Housing Market:** Move remains hampered by elevated rates. If the current rate environment persists for 2+ years, Move's competitive position may erode further relative to better-capitalized Zillow.

**4. Currency Risk:** ~50% of revenues in AUD/GBP. A significant USD strengthening would compress reported growth, as demonstrated by the 5 percentage-point FX benefit in Q3 FY2026 (which could reverse).

**5. Estimate Momentum Concern:** Near-term EPS estimates declining (-10.7% for next quarter). While this appears seasonal/currency-driven, negative estimate momentum can suppress the stock near-term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-38, +25-44%):** Buyback execution reduces share count meaningfully over 2-3 years, driving per-share value higher. Dow Jones professional services grow toward $1B EBITDA target on 11-15% CAGR. U.S. housing market recovers, reaccelerating Move. AI licensing revenue scales with additional partnerships. Market partially closes conglomerate discount as portfolio quality improves. REA Group continues compounding at 12-17%.

**Bear Case ($22-24, -8-17%):** AI disruption accelerates, reducing traffic to owned properties and undermining content licensing pricing power. U.S. housing remains depressed indefinitely. Print decline accelerates faster than digital transition. Murdoch family makes value-destructive capital allocation decisions (overpaying for M&A, reinstating Fox/News Corp merger). Rising rates crush REA Group multiple. News Media restructuring charges continue without meaningful improvement.

**Base Case ($30-33, +14-25%):** Company executes buyback at moderate pace, Dow Jones professional grows 12%+ annually, REA Group compounds, AI licensing provides incremental revenue. Conglomerate discount narrows modestly as portfolio quality becomes more visible. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.25-1.30.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $31.50** — 20.5x FY2027E EPS of $1.29 + modest conglomerate discount narrowing. Alternatively: 10x FY2027E EBITDA (~$1.6B) = ~$14.0B EV → ~$29/share after adjusting for net debt position.

**Bull case target: $35** — 25x FY2027E EPS (reflecting recognition of information services quality) or narrowing of conglomerate discount to ~30%.

**Bear case target: $22** — Return to February 2026 lows on macro selloff + AI disruption narrative. 16x trough EPS estimate.

**Upside/downside from current ($26.41):** Base +19%, Bull +33%, Bear -17%. Risk/reward: ~2.5:1 on base case.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key dates: Q4 FY2026 earnings (August 2026), buyback pace disclosure, AI licensing deal announcements, Fed rate decisions affecting housing outlook.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Dow Jones professional information revenue growth declines below 8% for two consecutive quarters (would signal competitive displacement)
2. REA Group stake value declines below US$7B (AUD depreciation + property market collapse)
3. Management abandons or significantly reduces buyback authorization without accretive M&A to explain reallocation

## Conclusion

News Corp at $26.41 represents a portfolio of improving businesses trading at a conglomerate discount that appears excessive given the accelerating transformation toward high-quality information services and digital platform economics. Specific catalysts for repricing include the active buyback program, accelerating reported revenue growth, and AI licensing monetization.

The governance constraint (dual-class structure preventing takeout) is the specific, concrete reason this discount persists. It is real and will likely prevent full SOTP realization. However, the buyback provides a mechanism for value accrual independent of market re-rating, and the business trajectory is clearly improving. At 20.5x FY2027E earnings with improving growth and meaningful buyback capacity, the risk/reward skews positive for patient investors willing to accept the governance overhang.

Recommendation: **BUY** at current levels with position sizing of 1.5-2% reflecting the governance overhang and estimate momentum concern.
