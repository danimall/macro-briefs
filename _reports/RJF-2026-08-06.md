## Executive Summary

Raymond James delivered its strongest quarter of the fiscal year in Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 6/30/2026): net revenues $3,928M (+16%), pre-tax income $750M (+33%), adjusted diluted EPS $3.14 (+44%), adjusted ROTCE 23.5%, and record firmwide client assets of $1.92 trillion. Consensus responded: FY2027 EPS estimates rose 6.0% over 90 days to $14.18 and current-year estimates 2.1% to $12.12. The franchise is working on the flow side — domestic PCG net new assets ran 6.6% annualized over nine months, fee-based PCG assets rose 22% year over year to $1.15T, and financial assets under management hit $345B (+31%, including $36B from the Clark Capital acquisition).

Two things temper the case. First, roughly 53% of the $187M year-over-year increase in Q3 pre-tax income came from a prior-year $58M legal-settlement reserve in Capital Markets and a $41M favorable swing in the bank credit line (from a $15M provision to a $26M benefit). Normalizing the credit benefit and the unusually low 20.7% effective tax rate back to the nine-month 23.1% rate puts run-rate quarterly EPS nearer $2.82 than $3.01, and the true margin improvement at roughly 80bp (19.1% versus a settlement-adjusted 18.3%), not the 250bp the reported figures suggest. Second, and more consequentially, the Private Client Group — 72% of net revenues — produced **negative incremental margin** over nine months: net revenues rose $897M (+11.9%) while segment pre-tax income *fell* $26M (−2.0%), with margin down 216bp to 15.18%. The consolidated 19.2% pre-tax margin held only because Bank pre-tax income rose 52% and Capital Markets 93%.

The stock has moved further than the fundamentals. Since the last review in late May at roughly $145, RJF is up about 24% while FY2027 consensus rose 6% — a re-rating from approximately 10.8x to 12.7x forward earnings. At $180.55 the shares sit 2.9% below the $185.92 consensus mean target, sell-side sentiment is classified as degrading (nine holds against five buys, one strong-buy downgrade in the last month), and the base-case upside I can defend (+6.9%) is a third of the bear-case downside (−22.5%). This is a fourth consecutive HOLD, and it is a judgment rather than inertia: whether recruited revenue seasons into normal-margin revenue is still unresolved, and the price has now consumed most of the discount that previously compensated for that uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Raymond James is a diversified financial services holding company (St. Petersburg, FL; founded 1962; public since 1983) regulated as both a bank holding company and a financial holding company, and therefore subject simultaneously to broker-dealer net capital rules, Basel III bank capital rules and the Fed's source-of-strength doctrine. Five segments:

- **Private Client Group** — FY2025 net revenues $10.18B (~72% of firm total); 8,943 financial advisors at 9/30/2025 (3,878 employees, 5,065 independent contractors), plus a separate RIA & Custody Services division with $217.3B of AUA whose advisors are not in that headcount.
- **Capital Markets** — FY2025 net revenues $1.77B; M&A/advisory, equity and debt underwriting including a large public finance franchise, institutional brokerage, research, and affordable-housing (LIHTC) investing.
- **Asset Management** — $345B of financial AUM at 6/30/2026.
- **Bank** — two FDIC-insured depositories (Raymond James Bank; TriState Capital Bank, acquired 2022), segment assets $70.05B at 6/30/2026, funded overwhelmingly by captive client cash through the Raymond James Bank Deposit Program sweep and the Enhanced Savings Program.
- **Other/geographic** — 9M FY2026 net revenues split $10,526M U.S. / $565M Canada / $431M Europe.

The strategic core is **AdvisorChoice**: an advisor can affiliate as a W-2 employee, an independent contractor, or an independent RIA custodied at RJF. The 10-K names the industry's advisor migration toward private-equity-backed independent RIAs as the largest structural threat to PCG; AdvisorChoice exists so that an advisor going independent does not have to leave Raymond James. No client accounts for more than 10% of revenues. The concentration that matters is channel: PCG contributed 8,419 of 11,522 nine-month net revenue dollars.

A ~150bp step-up occurred in Q1 FY2026 and has persisted three quarters. At Q3 net revenues of $3,928M, holding the FY2025 64.5% ratio would have saved $47M of quarterly pre-tax expense (~$189M annualized, ~$0.74/share after tax at 23.1% on 197.2M shares); against FY2024's 64.1%, $61M per quarter (~$0.95/share annualized).

The pressure sits in PCG. Nine-month segment revenues +$897M (+11.9%); segment pre-tax income −$26M (−2.0%); margin 17.34% → 15.18%. Holding prior-year margin would have produced ~$1,460M of nine-month PCG pre-tax income, $182M higher (~$0.94/share annualized). Management's stated drivers — "lower interest rates and investments in leading growth, including record recruiting results" — are both visible: third-party RJBDP fees, essentially pure margin, fell 24%; and net loans to financial advisors (transition/recruiting loans) grew 27% in nine months from $1,626M to $2,069M.

Two alternative readings deserve weight. Recruiting costs are front-loaded by construction — transition loans amortize over multi-year service periods and payout grids step down after initial terms — so a firm in a heavy recruiting cycle structurally understates steady-state margin. And segment results depend on internal allocation of sweep fees and funds-transfer pricing between PCG and the Bank, where pre-tax income rose 52% over the same period. The disclosures do not permit distinguishing investment timing and allocation shift from permanent repricing of advisor economics. That ambiguity is the central open question in the equity.

### Spread revenue: volume substituting for rate

The Fed has cut 125bp since the start of FY2025, leaving 3.50-3.75%. RJF is asset-sensitive: disclosed 12-month Bank NII sensitivity is base $1,967M, −100bp → −6%, −200bp → −12%, +200bp → +8%. Yet combined net interest income plus third-party sweep fees was $1,975M for nine months versus $1,980M — down 0.3% through the easing cycle.

That defense was purchased with volume. Bank loans net went $51,567M → $56,227M (+9% in nine months, +13% YoY), driven entirely by securities-based lending: SBL $19,775M → $24,767M (+25.2%), contributing $4,992M of the $4,888M total increase in loans held for investment while C&I ($10,777M → $10,040M), CRE ($7,840M → $7,689M) and tax-exempt all shrank. Average SBL yield fell 76bp to 5.28% (about 61% of Fed cuts passed through) while average balances grew 32%, lifting quarterly SBL interest income from ~$273M to ~$315M. Bank segment NIM compressed 2.83% → 2.67% across five quarters.

Sweep economics are tightening on the funding side. Domestic sweep plus ESP balances rose 7% YoY to $58.8B but fell to 3.4% of domestic PCG AUA from 3.7-3.8% — clients are close to fully invested, which is good for fee revenue and removes any residual "cash redeploys" tailwind. Mix shifted toward more expensive funding: ESP grew $13.0B → $14.9B while balances swept into the Bank segment fell $26.6B → $26.0B, and CDs nearly doubled to $3,326M.

RJF's disclosed asset sensitivity means that a higher-for-longer or rising-rate path would add directly to Bank NII (+200bp → +8%) and to sweep fees, which are near-pure margin, whereas further cuts (−100bp → −6%; −200bp → −12%) would pressure both. This rate exposure is a material swing factor in either direction. [unverified — the current FOMC stance, dissent count, and September-hike odds are not in the provided sources.]

### Credit

Aggregate metrics are clean: NPLs $186M → $151M (0.27% of loans); nine-month net charge-offs 0.07% annualized; ACL/NPL coverage 264%; ACL/loans HFI 0.88% → 0.70%.

Beneath that, CRE is migrating. Criticized CRE (special mention $148M + substandard $282M + doubtful $23M = $453M) rose to 5.89% of the $7,689M portfolio from $313M/3.99%, with substandard up 68%; collateral-dependent CRE rose $165M → $302M; CRE net charge-offs tripled to 0.36% annualized and accounted for $22M of $31M of gross charge-offs.

The falling aggregate allowance is mostly arithmetic. The CRE allowance *rose* from ~$182M to $192M and its reserve rate from 2.32% to 2.50%. The declines came from C&I ($148M → $121M) and REIT ($52M → $15M), the latter coinciding with a $113M collateral-dependent loan that appears to have reclassified into CRE. Adding $5.0B of SBL at a 0.02% reserve rate to a $51.6B book reserved at 0.88% mechanically dilutes the ratio to roughly 0.80% before any judgment is applied. Disclosed downside sensitivity: a moderate-recession scenario adds ~$150M to the quantitative allowance — 1.4% of Tier 1 capital, ~$0.58/share after tax. Contained.

Two less-common exposures: C&I includes roughly $2.8B of fund finance (loan funds secured by pools of senior-secured loans in bankruptcy-remote vehicles, plus subscription lines secured by uncalled LP commitments) — 15% of corporate loans, 5% of total. The research brief characterizes this as the fastest-growing and most-debated segment of private-credit-adjacent bank lending, and I would watch it closely for second-order effects even though the collateral structures are senior. Separately, residential mortgages include $3.41B of interest-only ARMs (~30% of the book, up from $3.04B) with ~5 years to amortization and 27% at LTV ≥80%, against reported 30+ day delinquency of 0.12% versus a 1.98% national average.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining industry dynamic is advisor mobility toward independence, increasingly financed by private equity, and RJF's AdvisorChoice/RCS architecture is a direct structural answer that pure employee-model wirehouses lack. Evidence supports it: 6.6% annualized nine-month NNA, PCG AUA +18%, fee-based assets +22%, and trade-press reports of RJF picking up teams as UBS lost roughly $2B of assets. LPL and Ameriprise pursue variants of the same model, and Schwab/Fidelity dominate stand-alone RIA custody, so the moat is real but not exclusive.

The trajectory question is whether share is being won at declining economics. RJF is gaining assets while its compensation ratio rises 150bp and advisor loans grow 27% in nine months. Both facts can be true simultaneously — the industry has repriced advisor economics and RJF is paying the market clearing price to keep and win teams. If upfront costs amortize and recruited books season, incremental margin normalizes. If competitors keep escalating guaranteed contracts and upfront payments, as the 10-K risk factor describes, the 15% PCG margin may be closer to the new steady state than the 17.3% prior-year level.

Investment banking mix is unresolved: nine-month M&A/advisory fell 12% to $405M while debt underwriting rose 30% and equity 24%; Q3 alone reversed sharply ($147M versus $105M) and management cites strong pipelines. GreensLedge is a deliberate bet on broadening fee mix away from M&A cyclicality.

The blended model produces returns no pure bank achieves — ROA of roughly 2.5% (FY2025 $2,135M on $85.6B average assets), ROE 18-19%, ROTCE 21-23% — and RJF outperformed its selected proxy peer group on cumulative TSR in each of five consecutive fiscal years ($380.95 versus $290.78 indexed from FY2021).

## Risk Assessment

**1. PCG margin non-recovery.** The most decision-relevant risk. FY2027 consensus of $14.18 requires operating leverage the segment has not produced in three quarters. Each 100bp of PCG margin not recovered is roughly $110M of annual pre-tax income, ~$0.43/share.

**2. Client cash sweep economics and deposit funding.** The 10-K devotes a dedicated risk factor to reliance on RJBDP to fund Bank asset growth and names regulatory risk to the fee stream explicitly ("changes to rules or interpretations governing the fees we earn on cash sweep balances"). Live evidence: third-party sweep fees −24%; Bank-swept balances down $26.6B → $26.0B while ESP grew to $14.9B. ESP is a reciprocal program, and reciprocal balances above $5B meet the FDIC definition of brokered deposits — so the fastest-growing funding source attracts higher insurance cost and rating-agency scrutiny, and reduced third-party capacity could force lower FDIC coverage against $10,858M of deposits already above limits.

**3. Rate direction, in both tails.** Asset sensitivity means −100bp costs ~$122M of Bank NII and −200bp ~$243M, with sweep fees compounding. Symmetrically, a higher-for-longer or rising-rate path (+200bp → +8% Bank NII) is a tailwind. The disclosed sensitivity table is the traceable fact; the current policy path is [unverified] against the provided sources.

**4. Category IV threshold.** Total assets $94.237B against a $100B four-quarter-average trigger; the trailing average is roughly $91B and growing ~11%, implying a crossing in FY2027-FY2028. Consequences: capital planning and stress capital buffer, biennial Fed stress tests, liquidity buffers, and potential loss of the AOCI opt-out — worth about 39bp of Tier 1 leverage and 74bp of CET1 on the current $368M after-tax AFS loss. Not a solvency event; a permanent increase in compliance cost and capital rigidity.

**5. Correlated equity-market beta on both sides of the balance sheet.** $1.15T of fee-based assets plus $24.8B of loans secured by marketable securities, the latter reserved at 0.02%. In a sharp drawdown, fee revenue falls, SBL demand contracts and collateral values decline together, with essentially no reserve cushion on the SBL book. This is the mechanism behind the bear case and it is not visible in any trailing metric.

**6. CRE migration.** Criticized CRE 3.99% → 5.89%; substandard +68%; charge-offs tripled to 0.36%. CRE plus REIT is $9.36B (16.6% of loans HFI), including ~$2.3B multi-family, ~$1.7B industrial warehouse, ~$1.1B office. The reserve response has been conservative (CRE reserve rate up to 2.50%), and the disclosed downside scenario adds only ~$150M.

**7. Fund-finance and private-credit adjacency.** ~$2.8B of loan-fund and subscription-line exposure, described in the brief as the fastest-growing and most-debated segment of private-credit-adjacent bank lending. Senior collateral structures mitigate, but this is an exposure worth monitoring.

**8. Leadership transition.** A near-complete refresh across FY2024-FY2025: CEO Paul Shoukry (age 42) since February 2025; new CFO, PCG President, COO, Capital Markets President, Bank President all installed October 2024; new CRO September 2024. Paul Reilly remains Executive Chair, which supplies continuity but means the board chair is a very recent executive; I could not confirm whether a separate independent lead director exists.

**9. Litigation and goodwill.** Item 3 reads "None," with reasonably possible loss above accruals of approximately $10M — unusually low. Offsetting, goodwill plus intangibles rose 41% to $2,608M with Clark Capital purchase accounting still provisional.

**Not risks, despite appearances:** the 49% decline in nine-month operating cash flow (settlement/collateral timing), and any Altman-Z-type distress screen (meaningless at 7.4x assets/equity for a bank-broker hybrid).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $212 (+17.4%)

PCG margin recovery arrives as FY2026 recruiting costs amortize and payout grids step down, restoring segment margin toward 16.5-17%; that alone is $180-200M of annual pre-tax income. Fee revenue steps up sharply in Q4 on the 11% sequential rise in billable fee-based assets to $1.15T. The Fed holds or hikes, and RJF's disclosed asset sensitivity (+200bp → +8% Bank NII) converts a two-year headwind into a tailwind while sweep fees stabilize. Clark Capital's $36B and GreensLedge's structured-credit fee stream annualize into FY2027, and the lifted U.K. FCA restriction restores organic recruiting in a region that lost $21M pre-tax on $431M of nine-month revenues. Continued repurchase of ~5% of shares annually against 0.9% of RSU issuance compounds per-share results. FY2027 EPS reaches $14.50-15.00; at 14.0-14.5x, $212. Requires: PCG margin above 16% for two consecutive quarters and no market drawdown.

### Bear Case — $140 (−22.5%)

A 15-20% equity-market drawdown hits both sides of the balance sheet simultaneously: fee-based assets (53% of net revenues) reprice down with a one-quarter lag, SBL demand collapses and the $24.8B book — reserved at 0.02% — sees collateral impairment, net new assets stall, and capital markets pipelines close. Separately or additionally, PCG margin proves permanently repriced by private-equity-financed advisor competition, holding the comp ratio at 65.7%+ and capping pre-tax margin near 19%; the tax rate normalizes to 24-25%; CRE criticized loans continue migrating and the ~$150M recession reserve build lands. ## Conclusion

Raymond James is a well-run, structurally advantaged wealth manager with a captive-deposit-funded bank, 21-23% ROTCE, a 5.7% total shareholder yield, demonstrably price-sensitive repurchase execution ($153 average against $180.55 today), and a compensation framework tied to pre-tax income and adjusted ROE rather than EPS or TSR.
