## Executive Summary

BorgWarner is a $14.3B-revenue global propulsion supplier that has spent the last two years doing three things simultaneously: writing off approximately $1.41B of electrification assets, expanding margins on a flat revenue base, and shrinking its share count by roughly 12–13%. All three are now visible in the numbers. Adjusted operating margin went 9.5% (FY2023) → 10.1% (FY2024) → 10.7% (FY2025) → ~10.9% in 1H26, free cash flow rose from $565M to $1,179M, and diluted weighted-average shares fell from 218.2M in Q2 2025 to 206.3M in Q2 2026. The Q2 2026 print (adjusted EPS $1.42 against a $1.29 consensus, gross margin 19.8% versus 17.6%, FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $5.05–$5.30) was the strongest quarter in the recent record and the fourth consecutive beat.

The problem is the entry, not the business. At $68.66 the stock trades at 13.3x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $5.175 and 11.6x the FY2027 consensus of $5.94, against a peer median trailing P/E of 14.2 (Autoliv 14.2x, Dorman 18.0x, Gentex 12.6x, Lear 11.1x). That is parity-to-slight-discount rather than the deep-value multiple BWA carried when it traded at $40.50 last October. The re-rating happened on February 11, 2026, when the shares jumped 22% in one session on the TurboCell/Endeavour industrial power-generation supply agreement, and continued to a $78.82 high on June 3. Enterprise value of roughly $15.8B against ~$2.1–2.2B of adjusted EBITDA is 7.2–7.5x, at the upper end of the auto-supplier range.

Three specific items keep us at HOLD rather than upgrading on the strength of the print. First, the guidance raise embeds a second-half step-down: $5.05–$5.30 against 1H26 adjusted EPS of ~$2.66 implies 2H26 of $2.39–$2.64 versus roughly $2.58 in 2H25, and consensus next-quarter EPS was cut 4.3% over 30 days consistent with that. Second, warranty adjustments of prior estimates jumped to approximately $31M in 1H26 from ~$3M in 1H25, the diagnostic line for a field issue on already-sold product, and at that run rate roughly 40bps of margin, most of a year's expansion target. Third, top-two customer concentration is worsening, not improving: Volkswagen was ~13% of FY2025 sales (from ~10%) and Ford ~12%, for ~25% combined. We raise the overall score to 5.5 from 5.0, reversing the recent downtrend, but hold the rating.

## Company Overview

BorgWarner supplies propulsion systems primarily to light-vehicle OEMs, with secondary exposure to commercial vehicles, off-highway equipment, tier-one systems suppliers and a small aftermarket. The July 2023 spin-off of PHINIA (Fuel Systems and Aftermarket) left a smaller, more concentrated company. Four reportable segments produced the following FY2025 economics:

| Segment | FY2025 sales | Segment adj. OI | Margin | FY2024 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drivetrain & Morse Systems | $5,635M | $1,041M | 18.5% | 18.2% |
| Turbos & Thermal Technologies | $5,769M | $879M | 15.2% | 14.9% |
| PowerDrive Systems | $2,322M | $(83)M | (3.6)% | (7.6)% |
| Battery & Charging Systems | $590M | $(39)M | (6.6)% | (6.5)% |
| Corporate | — | $(261)M | | |
| **Company adjusted OI** | **$14,316M** | **$1,537M** | **10.7%** | **10.1%** |

The economic structure is unusually stark. The two "Foundational" segments earned $1,920M of adjusted operating income; the two electrification segments consumed $122M and corporate absorbed $261M. Foundational revenue (internal combustion plus the ICE-adjacent content in hybrids) was $11,746M and has been flat for three years ($12,161M in 2023). eProducts grew to $2,570M from $2,037M but remains loss-making. Light-vehicle turbochargers alone were approximately 21% of consolidated sales, the only product line above 10%.

Geographically, 84% of FY2025 sales were produced outside the United States; China was $2,968M (~20.7%) and Mexico $1,741M (~12.2%). U.S. pre-tax results were a loss in each of the last three years — $(177)M, $(303)M, $(316)M — against non-U.S. pre-tax income of $701M, $842M and $1,307M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat and has been for three years.** $14,198M (2023) → $14,086M (2024) → $14,316M (2025), and 1H26 of $7,181M was up 0.4% against $7,153M. Q2 2026 sales of $3,648M were up roughly 0.3%. Any earnings growth here must come from margin, mix or share count, and it does.

**Margin expansion is real and mechanically identifiable.** Management attributes the FY2026 improvement to roughly 60bps from foundational-business performance, ~20bps from exiting the loss-making charging business, and ~20bps from cost control. The evidence supports this: PowerDrive Systems' adjusted loss narrowed by $61M year-over-year in FY2025 (margin −7.6% → −3.6%), and Q2 2026 gross margin of 19.8% versus 17.6% is a 220bp step-up. One caution: FY2025 full-year gross margin of 18.68% was slightly *below* FY2024's 18.80%, so the gross-margin momentum is a recent, two-quarter phenomenon rather than a three-year trend.

**GAAP earnings are heavily distorted and the distortion is directional.** FY2025 GAAP operating income was $536M (3.74% margin) against adjusted $1,537M. The bridge is dominated by $624M of impairments (of which $423M was Battery & Charging Systems goodwill), $101M of restructuring, $90M of accelerated depreciation, $66M of intangible amortization, a $40M net PHINIA legal settlement charge and $32M of charging-exit costs. GAAP diluted EPS of $1.28 with a 36.1% effective tax rate (inflated by $126M of non-deductible goodwill impairment tax effect) is why the profile-level trailing P/E field reads in the 30s. Trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS is $5.25 ($1.24 + $1.35 + $1.24 + $1.42), which is the decision-relevant figure. FY2026 GAAP operating margin is guided to ~9.6–9.8%, converging toward adjusted as the impairments annualize out.

**Cash flow quality is genuinely good, and the mechanism checks out.**

| ($M) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 1,397 | 1,382 | 1,648 |
| Capex incl. tooling | (832) | (671) | (469) |
| **Free cash flow** | **565** | **711** | **1,179** |
| Capex % of sales | 5.9% | 4.8% | 3.3% |
| D&A | 582 | 673 | 719 |

Operating cash flow rose $266M while net earnings from continuing operations fell from $428M to $335M. The reconciliation is working-capital normalization (a net use of ~$34M in 2025 versus ~$208M in 2024, a ~$174M swing) plus $49M higher depreciation and $21M lower pension contributions — not receivables factoring or payables stretching. Accounts payable were actually a $207M *use* of cash. FY2025 FCF of $1,179M equals an 8.4% yield on the $14.0B market capitalization.

The one place to discount the cash-flow figure is capex, at $469M or 65% of D&A and 3.3% of sales. That is the low end for a global manufacturer, and the company is now adding an industrial power-generation product line that will need capacity. Normalizing capex to 4.5% of sales (~$645M) reduces FCF to roughly $1.0B and the yield to ~7.2%. Still attractive; not 8.4%.

**Balance sheet is conservatively capitalized and the debt cost is a structural advantage.** Cash $2,313M against $3,899M of total debt at year-end 2025, net debt ~$1,586M. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.1–2.19B puts net leverage at 0.72–0.76x and gross at ~1.8x. Interest coverage is ~15.5x on $99M of interest expense, reflecting a weighted-average borrowing rate of 2.5% including swaps. The $2B revolver (September 2028) and $2B commercial paper program are undrawn, with a single debt-to-EBITDA covenant in compliance. Senior notes carry a fair value $175M *below* carrying value, the mark-to-market benefit of low coupons.

The maturity ladder has one event: $1,106M of 2.65% notes due 2027. At a 5.0% refinancing rate that is roughly $26M of incremental pre-tax interest, about $0.10 of after-tax EPS, partly offsettable with the $2.3B cash balance. Meaningful, not thesis-changing.

**Return on capital, with the honest caveat.** Adjusted operating income of $1,537M taxed at ~24% gives NOPAT of ~$1,168M against invested capital of ~$7.2B (debt $3,899M + equity $5,614M − cash $2,313M), or ~16.2% ROIC. Adding back the $1.0B of goodwill impaired in 2024–25 raises invested capital to ~$8.2B and lowers ROIC to ~14.2%. The through-cycle figure sits between these two, and the higher number exists partly because the denominator was written down.

**Noncontrolling interests leak a material share of earnings.** Of $335M of FY2025 net earnings from continuing operations, $58M (17.3%) accrued to noncontrolling interests, with $49M paid to them in dividends. Combined with $35M of equity-affiliate income, roughly $93M of the earnings picture sits in partially-owned or non-consolidated entities. Any EV/EBITDA comparison that ignores the NCI claim overstates value to BWA holders.

## Growth Analysis

There are four growth vectors, of unequal quality.

**1. Share count (the largest and most reliable).** Basic weighted shares went 232.8M (2023) → 223.5M (2024) → 214.2M (2025); Q2 2026 diluted was 206.3M against 218.2M, a 5.5% year-over-year reduction. Roughly $650M was repurchased in the four quarters through 6/30/26, and a new $1B authorization in July 2026 leaves ~$1.35B available — 9.6% of the current market capitalization. At an 8% FCF yield and 0.72x net leverage, this is repeatable. It is also arithmetic on a flat revenue base, and investors paying 13.3x for "EPS growth" should size that correctly: adjusted operating margin improved ~65bps FY2024→FY2025 and ~79bps 1H25→1H26, while share count fell ~5%. Both levers are roughly comparable in EPS contribution.

**2. PowerDrive Systems to breakeven.** Sales grew 21.7% to $2,322M in FY2025 with the loss narrowing from $(144)M to $(83)M. Closing the remaining ~$83M gap would add roughly $0.30–$0.40 to EPS at current share counts. This is a specific, identifiable lever that does not depend on the data-center story. It also sits primarily in Asia ($1,298M of segment sales), where Chinese OEM price pressure is most acute, so the path is not assured.

**3. Industrial power generation.** On February 11, 2026, BorgWarner announced a Master Supply Agreement with TurboCell, a subsidiary of data-center infrastructure developer Endeavour. The structure matters: BorgWarner manufactures and sells modular turbine generator systems, TurboCell purchases and pays, and Endeavour owns TurboCell and the hyperscaler relationships. BorgWarner does not operate data centers and is not a party to the end-customer contracts. The Q2 10-Q discloses expected power-generation industrial solutions sales of approximately $300M in 2027 with production beginning that year; management referenced roughly 2 GW of associated capacity and confirmed no change-of-control trigger in the agreement. Battery energy storage systems and microgrid inverters are in quoting and sampling with no announced awards.

Two calibrations. $300M on a $14.3B base is ~2% of revenue, but at industrial-power margins it is worth more than 2% of earnings, and the auto-supplier multiple prices none of it. Against that, the figure is a management expectation, not contracted backlog — the company's own revenue-recognition policy notes that long-term supply arrangements are not ASC 606 contracts until volumes are contractually known, and disclosed contract liabilities are trivially small ($1M current, $1M non-current). This is the correct place to look for the next re-rating trigger, and also the place where a slip would hurt most, since the entire new growth vector runs through one commercial counterparty's capital plans.

**4. Underlying volume — flat at best.** Global light-vehicle production is flat, North American EV adoption is delayed, and the demand environment is soft. Auto suppliers are geared to vehicle build schedules, and management's own assumption of ~15% second-half decremental margins signals they are planning for softness.

Consensus reflects this mix: FY2026 $5.22 (+0.6% over 30 days), FY2027 $5.94 (+1.6%), against current-quarter and next-quarter estimates cut 2.7% and 4.3% over 30 days. Full-year estimates drifting up while near quarters drift down is the signature of a guidance raise with a conservative second-half embedded. There is no >30% estimate-revision inflection here; this is a stable-estimate, margin-and-buyback story.

Peer dispersion is wide and partly artificial: Aptiv's 46.4x is GAAP-distorted like BorgWarner's own reported trailing figure, while Lear at 11.1x and Gentex at 12.6x are clean. On a like-for-like forward basis BWA at 13.2–13.3x sits at roughly peer parity, with better leverage (0.72x net), better FCF yield and a lower dividend payout, offset by lower yield support and the same flat-volume backdrop.

The case that the market is mispricing this is narrower than it was six months ago. It rests on: (a) the industrial power-generation vector receiving no multiple credit, (b) $1.35B of remaining repurchase authorization at an 8% FCF yield mechanically compounding EPS ~4–5% annually, and (c) PowerDrive's $83M loss representing recoverable earnings. Against that: revenue has not grown in three years, the multiple is no longer a discount, and the analyst mean target of $79.67 implies 16% upside with a low target of $62 — 9.7% *below* the current price. Fifteen covering analysts show 3 strong buys, 7 buys, 5 holds, with one strong sell removed and one hold added in the last month; sentiment is stable, consistent with a fairly-priced situation rather than a mispricing.

We are deliberately skeptical of the "cheap auto supplier" framing. Flat revenue, worsening customer concentration and a 21%-of-sales exposure to an ICE-adjacent product line are exactly the profile that keeps multiples compressed for years. Power-generation revenue converting from expectation to disclosed backlog is what would break that pattern, and it is not yet in hand.

**Scenario framework (all from the $68.66 basis):**

- **Base $77 (+12.1%):** 13.4x on FY2027 EPS of $5.75, slightly below the $5.94 consensus to allow for the 2027 interest repricing (~$0.10) and residual warranty drag. Implies buyback continues at ~4–5% annually and power generation contributes on schedule.
- **Bull $95 (+38.4%):** 15.5x on $6.15, requiring PowerDrive at or near breakeven, power-generation revenue confirmed above $300M with a second named counterparty or disclosed backlog, warranty true-ups reverting to normal, and stable light-vehicle volumes. Aligns with the $95 high target.
- **Bear $53 (−22.8%):** 10.5x on $5.05, requiring a light-vehicle production decline, recurring $30M-plus half-yearly warranty adjustments, and power generation slipping to 2028. The October 2025 low of $40.50 marks where a genuine recession would take it.

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly +10% price return plus a 1% dividend, or ~11% total. For a cyclical with peer beta around 1.37, required return is comparable. That is a fair-value outcome and the reason the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

Three dynamics define the position, and the trajectory is genuinely mixed rather than clearly improving or deteriorating.

**Foundational products are holding pricing and expanding margin.** Drivetrain & Morse at 18.5% and Turbos & Thermal at 15.2% adjusted margins, both up year-over-year across three years of flat-to-declining volumes, is evidence of real pricing and cost discipline. Turbochargers benefit from hybrid programs, which use them — the 21% concentration is currently a tailwind and becomes a headwind only if BEV adoption re-accelerates. Management has repeatedly failed to forecast the powertrain mix path (the 2024 *and* 2025 impairments), so we assign no directional confidence here beyond the current hybrid-favorable mix.

**Battery systems is being commoditized and BorgWarner is retreating deliberately.** The FY2025 goodwill impairment rationale, repeated in the auditor's critical audit matter, names "dual sourcing actions by certain customers," "pricing pressures from competitors," and "electric vehicle adoption delays in North America." That is a disclosure that the customer relationship structure weakened, not merely that volumes slipped. The response — exit charging (February 2025, ~$7M proceeds, $22M loss), consolidate the North American battery footprint, let revenue fall from $729M to $590M to a ~$400M annualized 1H26 pace — looks like deliberate pruning rather than uncontrolled share loss, because segment losses *narrowed* (1H26 margin ~−2.0% versus ~−11.0% in 1H25) even as revenue collapsed 35%.

**Power generation is a credibility test.** The incumbents in gensets and prime power — Caterpillar, Cummins, Generac, GE Vernova — have distribution, service networks and installed bases BorgWarner does not. The counter-argument is that BorgWarner is not entering that channel; it is a component and systems supplier to a developer that has already secured end demand, structurally similar to its tier-one OEM model. That framing is credible and it is also what makes the revenue single-counterparty dependent.

**Customer concentration is the clearest negative trajectory.** VW at ~13% (up 3 points in two years) and Ford at ~12% (down from 14%), for ~25% combined. A shifting eProducts/Foundational mix is not customer diversification: the eProducts revenue share rose from 14.3% to 17.9% over three years while the largest single customer's share increased. Those measure different things, and only the second speaks to concentration risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Warranty is the emergent item.** FY2025 was orderly — accrual $215M → $254M, $108M of current-period provisions, only $1M of adjustments to prior estimates, and a $21M insurance recovery on a resolved historical matter. The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses approximately $31M of prior-estimate adjustments in 1H26 against ~$3M in 1H25, payments roughly doubling, an accrual near $269M, and a ~$27M cost-recovery receivable. Adjustments to *prior* estimates indicate a field issue on already-sold product rather than warranty scaling with volume. At $30M per half-year this consumes roughly 40bps of operating margin, most of a year's expansion target. One half is not a trend, a discrete campaign can be non-recurring, and the recovery receivable implies a supplier or insurer bears part of it, but this is the single most important item to check in the Q3 filing.

**Residual impairment exposure is quantified and has no cushion.** Note 12 states the B&CS carrying value "now approximates its fair value," using a 14% WACC. Remaining B&CS goodwill was $146M at 12/31/25 and ~$141M at 6/30/26, against a segment whose 1H26 revenue fell ~35% to $202M. A further charge up to ~$141M is plausible. It would be non-cash and irrelevant to covenant compliance at 0.72x net leverage, but it would again distort GAAP results.

**Tax and geography.** All pre-tax profit is earned outside the U.S. FY2025 cash taxes were $372M, of which $371M was foreign and $0 was U.S. federal; China alone was $105M. The company carries $360M of valuation allowances against $982M of gross deferred tax assets and has concluded net DTAs in seven jurisdictions including the U.S. are more-likely-than-not unrealizable. The elevated ~24% cash tax rate is structural, and U.S. tariff and R&D spend lands where it generates little tax benefit. China at ~20.7% of sales also carries the U.S.-China policy tail.

**Tariffs — cost incurred, refund optionality unmodelled.** FY2025 tariff costs of ~$108M with ~$80M recovered from customers netted to a ~$28M drag. Following the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 invalidation of the IEEPA tariffs, BorgWarner is submitting CBP refund claims but has recognized no receivable, and the filings do not address whether recovered amounts must be passed back to customers. We assign zero to this.

**Pension settlement.** The U.S. plan termination (effective October 31, 2025, up to eighteen months) carries an expected non-cash settlement loss of $70–80M in 2026, reconciling to $86M of net actuarial loss in AOCI. Cash impact is minimal; GAAP EPS impact is not.

**Restructuring is open-ended.** FY2025 restructuring of $101M included $62M of individually approved actions outside named plans, and the 10-K states future actions "could result in significant restructuring expense."

**Management and governance.** Insider activity in the reviewed window shows open-market selling and no purchases, including a CEO sale of ~29,000 shares at ~$67.31 in May 2026 and multiple VP-level sales across a $52–$73 range. The 10b5-1 status is unverified, and the stock had roughly doubled off its low, so this is best read as an absence of buying conviction rather than a bearish signal. Offsetting that: the Q1 2025 performance-share redesign replaced eProduct Revenue and eProduct Revenue Mix metrics with Relative Revenue Growth and Cumulative Adjusted EPS (2025–2027), alongside adjusted operating margin and free cash flow. Given that the prior metric set coincided with $1.4B of write-downs, that realignment is a positive, with the honest caveat that Cumulative Adjusted EPS is advanced by repurchasing stock as well as by operating improvement. At an 8% FCF yield, buying back stock is a defensible use of capital; the metric design and the capital action are aligned. The dividend was raised 55% to $0.17/quarter in Q3 2025, a ~13% payout of guided EPS. Rajesh Kalathur (formerly Deere's senior finance leadership) joined the board on July 31, 2026, coherent with the industrial pivot. Unvested equity is ~4.1M shares at target (~2.0% of shares outstanding), rising to ~6.0M at maximum performance payout, which is immaterial against the pace of repurchase.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.0% sits below one-year realized volatility of 38.6% and at the 2nd percentile of the current chain, having collapsed from 53.1% on July 23 as the Q2 event resolved. Term structure is flat (35.0% near versus 36.7% at six months), the nearest-expiry skew is balanced at −1.9%, and put/call open interest is 0.01 with a put/call volume ratio of 0.21 — essentially no institutional put protection outstanding. Longer tenors show skew readings of −6.0% (1m), −4.3% (3m) and −7.3% (6m); we report these without asserting a direction given the undefined sign convention. Implied moves are ±8.1% at one month and ±18.2% at six months. Short interest is 5.0% of float with 3.9 days to cover — uncrowded on both sides.

Options are priced for a quiet, resolved situation with cheap optionality, which corroborates the fundamental read of a fairly-valued name rather than a contested one. For an owner, this makes covered-call or collar structures inexpensive to consider; it does not constitute independent confirmation of upside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$95, +38%)

Foundational margins keep grinding higher (Drivetrain 18.5%, Turbos 15.2%, both rising), PowerDrive closes its remaining $83M loss and adds $0.30–$0.40 to EPS, and the TurboCell program converts the ~$300M 2027 expectation into disclosed backlog with a second counterparty award from the BESS or microgrid-inverter pipeline. Warranty true-ups prove to be a single discrete campaign. The company deploys the remaining $1.35B of authorization at an 8% FCF yield, shrinking shares another 9% and taking FY2027 EPS toward $6.15. The market re-rates BWA off the auto-supplier multiple toward an industrial-power comparable at 15.5x, on the argument that a growing, higher-margin, non-automotive revenue stream deserves different treatment.

### Bear Case (~$53, −23%)

Light-vehicle production declines, and OEM schedules cut into a supplier with 25% top-two customer concentration. Management's own ~15% second-half decremental margin assumption becomes the actual outcome rather than conservatism, and 2H26 adjusted EPS of $2.39–$2.64 versus $2.66 in 1H26 marks the turn rather than the trough. Warranty prior-estimate adjustments recur at $30M-plus per half, consuming 40bps of margin. VW's rising share becomes a negotiating problem rather than a growth story, with dual-sourcing already disclosed in battery systems. The remaining $141M of B&CS goodwill is written off. Power generation slips to 2028 because Endeavour's capital plans change. The $1.1B 2027 maturity reprices from 2.65% to above 5%. The multiple compresses to 10.5x on $5.05, and the stock retraces most of the February–June re-rating.

### What Would Make This a BUY

A disclosed, contracted power-generation backlog materially above $300M, or a second named industrial counterparty, would justify a different multiple framework. Absent that, a price in the high $50s (roughly 11x FY2027 consensus, ~10% FCF yield on normalized capex) would restore the margin of safety that 13.3x does not provide.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Horizon: 12 months, with the Q3 2026 report (late October) as the first checkpoint.**

- **Base case target: $77 (+12.1% from $68.66).** 13.4x on FY2027 EPS of $5.75 — set below the $5.94 consensus to absorb ~$0.10 of 2027 interest repricing and residual warranty drag. Plus ~1% dividend yield.
- **Bull case target: $95 (+38.4%).** 15.5x on $6.15, consistent with the $95 analyst high.
- **Bear case target: $53 (−22.8%).** 10.5x on $5.05. The $40.50 52-week low is the recession floor.
- **Probability-weighted (50/25/25): ~+11% total return**, approximately the cost of equity for a ~1.35-beta cyclical. Fair, not compelling.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A price of $58–$60 (≈11x FY2027 consensus, ~10% normalized FCF yield) without fundamental deterioration.
2. Power generation converting from expectation to disclosure: a contracted backlog figure, a second named counterparty, or a 2027 revenue expectation raised above $300M.
3. PowerDrive Systems posting positive segment adjusted operating income for two consecutive quarters, confirming the ~$0.30–$0.40 EPS lever.
4. Warranty adjustments of prior estimates reverting below $10M in 2H26 while adjusted operating margin holds above 10.7%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Warranty prior-estimate adjustments of $30M or more again in 2H26, or the recovery receivable being written off.
2. A cut to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance below $5.05, or 2H26 adjusted EPS tracking below $2.30.
3. Top-two customer concentration exceeding 28%, or disclosure of a lost VW or Ford program of scale.
4. A pause or material slowdown in repurchase activity, which would remove roughly half the EPS growth engine.
5. Evidence that Endeavour/TurboCell order timing has shifted, pushing power-generation revenue to 2028.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October, with the 10-Q warranty roll-forward and any updated power-generation disclosure as the two items that carry the most information. Interim checkpoints: monthly North American light-vehicle production data and any 8-K disclosing a power-generation award.

## Conclusion

BorgWarner has done the operational work. Margin expansion is real and traceable to identifiable sources, the balance sheet has absorbed $1.41B of electrification write-downs and now shows 0.72x net leverage at a 2.5% average borrowing cost, free cash flow of $1,179M supports an 8.4% yield, and the buyback is shrinking the share count roughly 5% annually with $1.35B of authorization remaining. Four consecutive earnings beats and a raised FY2026 guidance range of $5.05–$5.30 mean the Q2 print reversed, rather than continued, the recent deterioration in our assessment; we raise the overall score to 5.5 from 5.0, driven by financial health (7.0) and confirmed by the gross-margin step-up.

The rating stays HOLD because the price now reflects that work.
