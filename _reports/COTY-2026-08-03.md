## Executive Summary

Coty has had a materially better three months on the financing side and a materially worse two years on the operating side, and the second fact still dominates. Since our June 18 review the company signed the Gucci Beauty transition agreement with Kering (July 7), receiving $250M in cash at signing against approximately $400M of total consideration, of which $370M is non-contingent and roughly $340M net of an estimated $30M cash tax. Coty will operate Gucci Beauty through at least June 30, 2027, about a year ahead of the original term, and all pending Gucci-related litigation was mutually resolved. Combined with $792.2M of gross debt retired in the nine months to March 31 (funded principally by the $750M Wella disposal), the near-term maturity wall is gone: the next real maturity is the 2027 Euro Notes at $573.5M in May 2027, against roughly $1.5B of undrawn revolver capacity and the Gucci cash already in hand.

That is where the good news stops. Nine-month FY2026 net revenue of $4,537.4M was down only 2%, but the disclosed decomposition is volume -6% and FX +4%; in Q3 alone volume fell 8% against +6% FX. Gross margin compressed 200bp to 63.5% for the nine months and 230bp to 61.8% in Q3, with 70bp of the Q3 decline from excess-and-obsolescence provisions — inventory built for demand that did not arrive. Adjusted EBITDA fell 21% to $753.3M (16.6% margin from 20.6%), and 38% in Q3 to $127.0M. Consumer Beauty segment adjusted EBITDA was negative $23.6M in Q3 and only 4.0% of revenue for the nine months, down from 11.0%. Prestige — 60% of company revenue and the entire profit engine — posted Q3 price/mix of -3% on increased trade incentives, which is the segment defending share with discounts.

The decisive arithmetic is what the Gucci proceeds do to credit metrics. Barclays, via Reuters (July 20), estimates the license exit removes roughly $115M of annual adjusted EBITDA, about 15% of profits, landing primarily in FY2028. Against an $800M forward EBITDA base and net debt of $2,959M, pre-deal reported net leverage is roughly 3.70x. Applying $340M of net proceeds to debt while removing $115M of EBITDA gives $2,619M / $685M = **3.82x** — leverage rises, because the cash reduces net debt by 11.5% while the EBITDA loss is 14.4%. The deal was a good price for the twelve-or-so months of license life Coty surrendered; it does not repair the FY2028 balance sheet. With the stock up 40% from its June 29 low of $1.96 and trading above the $2.55 median analyst target, we maintain AVOID for a fourth consecutive quarter, with the overall score edging up to 3.1 from 3.0 to reflect genuine financing improvement.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1904 and dual-listed on the NYSE and Euronext Paris, Coty operates in approximately 123 countries with roughly 11,636 full-time employees. Two segments:

**Prestige** (9M FY2026 revenue $3,034.0M, 67% of total) carries the company. Licensed fragrance is the economic core: approximately 60% of FY2025 net revenues were prestige fragrance, of which roughly 91% came from the top seven brands — Gucci, Hugo Boss, Calvin Klein, Burberry, Chloé, Marc Jacobs, Davidoff among them. Coty held 22 brand licenses at June 30, 2025 with average duration of about 24 years; 48% of FY2025 revenue came from owned or effectively perpetual licenses and 37% from exclusive licenses with 7-25 years remaining.

**Consumer Beauty** ($1,503.4M, 33%) is mass-market: CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, Max Factor, Bourjois, a distinct Brazilian portfolio, and licensed adidas, David Beckham, Nautica and others. It is the subject of a strategic review that, per the Q3 10-Q, has produced no finalized changes to strategy, capital allocation or operational priorities.

Two structural features deserve credit. Retailer concentration is unusually low for consumer staples — no retailer exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue, with Walmart and A.S. Watson each at roughly 4%. And Coty manufactures approximately 81% of its products in-house, which supports the licensing pitch to brand owners and captures margin. The corollary is fixed-cost deleverage when volumes fall, which is the mechanism now visible in the 100bp adverse manufacturing/material cost contribution to Q3 gross margin.

JAB Beauty B.V. is deemed beneficial owner of approximately 54% of Class A stock. Coty is a controlled company. Markus Strobel has served as both Executive Chairman and Interim CEO since January 1, 2026, following Sue Nabi's departure effective December 31, 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended June 30, 2025):** Revenue $5,892.9M, -3.7%. Operating income $241.1M, down 56% from $546.7M. Net loss attributable to Coty $(367.9)M against +$89.4M prior year. Adjusted EBITDA $1,081.7M, essentially flat versus the FY2024 peak of $1,091.1M. The gap between a flat adjusted number and a $368M GAAP loss is impairments, restructuring, license amortization, Wella marks and derivative losses. Coty recorded a ~$23M tax provision on a pre-tax loss.

**Nine months FY2026:** the deterioration migrated into the adjusted numbers.

| | 9M FY2026 | 9M FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | $4,537.4M | $4,640.5M | -2% |
| Gross margin | 63.5% | 65.5% | -200bp |
| Operating income | $(38.8)M | $225.6M | swing |
| Asset impairments | $362.8M | $212.8M | +$150.0M |
| Net loss attributable | $(463.8)M | $(299.1)M | worse |
| Adjusted operating income | $587.2M (12.9%) | $785.2M (16.9%) | -25% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $753.3M (16.6%) | $955.0M (20.6%) | -21% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $0.23 | $0.27 | -15% |

The single most important interpretive point: **any screen reading "-2% revenue" understates the demand problem by four to six points.** Volume declined 6% for the nine months and 8% in Q3. FX added 4-6 points. Some of the volume decline is deliberate — the 10-Q cites reduced distribution in certain channels for Calvin Klein, which can be margin-accretive — but Consumer Beauty's decomposition (color cosmetics -$39.9M, mass fragrance -$33.2M including a license expiry, mass body care -$13.0M on competitive pricing in Brazilian deodorants) reads as lost share, not managed retreat.

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the story, with one large asterisk.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $421.8M, up from $409.4M, against a $463.8M net loss, because the loss is dominated by non-cash items: D&A $354.1M, impairments $362.8M, equity-investment losses $194.0M, forward-contract losses $124.4M, and a $(156.3)M deferred tax benefit. Free cash flow (OCF less $146.2M capex) was $275.6M.

The asterisk is $208.7M of cash paid during the nine months on forward share-repurchase contracts, classified in financing. Netting that against FCF leaves roughly **$66.9M** of discretionary cash before debt principal — which is why the Wella sale, not operations, funded the $792.2M of debt retirement. Cash interest paid was $149.3M; cash taxes $71.1M. Do not annualize any of this: December is the seasonal collection peak, and net factored receivables *declined* $22.2M over the period (a use of cash, not a source).

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: cash $257.1M, total debt $3,216.2M, **net debt $2,959.1M** (corroborating Reuters' "around $2.9 billion"). Current ratio 0.82. Goodwill $3,810.0M and other intangibles $2,860.6M against total Coty stockholders' equity of $3,091.4M — **tangible book equity is negative by roughly $3.4B.** The Altman Z-Score computes to 1.19, inside the distress zone, and given negative working capital and negative tangible equity that is directionally correct. (The Piotroski F-Score of 4 carries a "positive net income" input contradicting the reported loss and is unusable here.)

Debt reduction was real: the 2026 Dollar and Euro Notes and 2028 Euro Notes were retired, Term A and Term B are fully repaid, and $900M of 5.600% 2031 Notes were issued in October 2025. Post-refinancing maturities are $573.5M in FY2027, zero in FY2028, $699.6M in FY2029 and $1,650.0M thereafter. Revolver capacity is the $1,670.0M facility plus a €300M tranche, with pro forma drawings near $494M after the April 15 repayment of the residual €250M notes.

**The covenant is the binding constraint to watch.** The credit agreement requires Total Net Leverage ≤ 4.00:1.00 for test periods from March 31, 2026 through July 11, 2028; Coty states it was compliant at March 31. On a reported basis, FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $832-880M (nine months of $753.3M plus a Q4 in the $79-127M range depending on whether Q3's 38% decline persists or moderates) against $2,959M of net debt implies **3.4x-3.6x**. The credit-agreement definition permits add-backs for cost savings, unusual items and future unrealized synergies, so covenant headroom is wider than the reported calculation. Headroom exists; the trajectory is the risk.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at present, and the forward path is negative before it is positive.

**Near term (FY2026 Q4, reported ~late August):** management guided reported net revenue to decline mid-single-digit percent, with gross margin pressured by lower sales, net tariff impact of roughly $30M in FY2026, and elevated excess-and-obsolescence provisions. Middle East disruption was guided at a 2-3% sales impact for the quarter against a region sized at mid-single-digit percent of FY2025 revenue.

**FY2027:** Gucci remains in the P&L through June 30, 2027, so the license loss does not bite. Offsets are the $80M of fiscal-2026 cost savings annualizing, restructuring under the April 2025 Fixed Cost Reduction Plan (~$80M total cash cost, roughly half in FY2027), and the end of the forward-repurchase cash drain after settlement in December 2026 / January 2027. Against that: continued volume erosion, a fading FX tailwind, and $42.3M of FY2027 severance cash outflows. A plausible FY2027 adjusted EBITDA range is $740-820M.

**FY2028:** the Gucci hole opens. Barclays sizes it at roughly $115M of annual adjusted EBITDA, about 15% of profits; the implied base (~$767M) is consistent with a forward rather than FY2026 denominator. Coty grew Gucci Beauty revenue more than 60% since 2019, which is precisely why the loss is expensive. Replacement licenses — Etro is confirmed in the FY2025 10-K; Swarovski and Marni are reported by Reuters but do not appear in the 10-K brand table we reviewed — are unlikely to be of Gucci's scale within two years.

**Consensus is falling and has been consistently wrong.** Current-year EPS estimates were cut 25.5% over 90 days to $0.23; next year is down 6.4% to $0.34. That $0.34 implies roughly 48% adjusted EPS growth in a year when EBITDA is at best flat, achievable only through lower interest expense and cost savings, and we would discount it. The company has missed consensus in all four reported quarters, including a $(0.03) print against a $(0.00) estimate for the March quarter and a 25.5% miss in December.

Growth Prospects: **2.5** — unchanged from June. No new operating data has arrived since May 5, but the Gucci agreement formalizes a 15% EBITDA loss that was previously a 2028 abstraction.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value:** $2,465M equity + $2,959M net debt + $142.4M Series B preferred + $85.7M redeemable non-controlling interests ≈ **$5,652M**. Against FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $832-880M that is **6.4x-6.8x**. On the consensus $0.34 for next year, the equity trades at 8.2x; on the $0.23 current-year figure, 12.2x. Trailing GAAP EPS is negative $(0.44), rendering P/E meaningless.

Peer context is of limited use given the dispersion: ELF at 137.9x, EL negative, KVUE 21.5x, PG 21.7x, median 21.7x with a 2.9% median yield. Coty pays no dividend (suspended since April 2020, and management has tied restoration to approaching 2.0x net leverage versus ~3.5x today) and has no positive earnings to multiply. The only comparison that transfers is EV/EBITDA, where roughly 6.5x is a discount to the large-cap staples complex — appropriately so for a business with mid-single-digit volume declines, ~3.5x leverage, negative tangible equity, and a known 15% EBITDA loss in two years.

**Scenario build (all from the $2.80 basis):**

*Base case $2.60.* FY2027 adjusted EBITDA $780M at 6.5x EV/EBITDA = $5,070M EV. Net debt at FY2027 year-end ~$2,550M after $340M of Gucci net proceeds and modest FCF net of restructuring cash. Less $228M of preferred and NCI: equity $2,292M / 880.5M shares = **$2.60, or -7% from spot.** A pro-forma FY2028 cross-check gives the same answer: $700M EBITDA at 7.0x less $2.4B net debt and $228M = $2.58.

*Bull case $4.10.* Volume stabilizes, Coty.Curated delivers SKU rationalization benefits, Consumer Beauty is divested at or above book with proceeds to debt, and FY2028 EBITDA holds at $800M on 7.5x: $6.0B EV less $2.2B net debt and $228M = $3,572M, **$4.06/share, +45%.**

*Bear case $1.40.* Volume decline persists near -8%, FY2027 EBITDA falls to $680M, a ratings action reinstates covenants and re-encumbers collateral, the Prestige goodwill test fails, and talc settlements begin to be quantified. 6.0x on $680M = $4,080M EV less $2,700M net debt and $228M = $1,152M, **$1.31/share, -53%.** Round to $1.40, consistent with the $1.50 low analyst target and the $1.82 52-week low.

Probability-weighting at 25/45/30 gives roughly $2.62 — below the current price. The equity is 44% of enterprise value, so a 10% EBITDA miss moves EV by ~$560M and the equity by ~23%. That leverage is the reason a cheap-looking multiple does not translate into asymmetric upside.

The incremental value created by the Gucci deal is worth quantifying against the rally. Coty received roughly $340M net of tax for surrendering perhaps six to twelve months of license life (call it $60-115M of forgone EBITDA, or $55-105M in present value) plus resolution of the litigation. That is a good trade — three to six times the value given up — and adds roughly $0.26-$0.32 per share of intrinsic value. The stock has risen $0.79 from its June 29 low. The re-rating has more than captured the value created.

Valuation: **3.2**. Not stretched, not attractive; a low multiple attached to a declining, levered earnings stream with base case at or below spot.

## Competitive Landscape

Coty's competitive trajectory is deteriorating in both segments, and the Gucci outcome crystallized it. Kering agreed in October 2025 to sell Kering Beauté to L'Oréal for approximately €4bn, with L'Oréal obtaining a 50-year exclusive Gucci Beauty license; L'Oréal's CEO has since described a first launch "as early as 2028" and multibillion-euro ambitions. Coty was never a party — it is the incumbent licensee being displaced by a better-capitalized competitor, on an asset it grew 60% since 2019.

Within Prestige, the Q3 price/mix of -3% attributed to increased trade incentives on prestige fragrances is the most important competitive datapoint in the filing. Coty's prestige fragrance franchise has been the argument for owning the stock; discounting to hold volume, while volume still falls 8%, suggests the category's pricing power is not Coty's to keep. Prestige segment adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 25.5% to 22.8%.

In Consumer Beauty the position is worse than deteriorating — it is being written off in real time. Q3 impairments of $362.8M included $237.1M of Consumer Beauty goodwill and $125.7M of indefinite-lived trademarks (CoverGirl $50.6M, Sally Hansen $48.5M, Max Factor $22.5M, Bourjois $4.1M), following $212.8M of similar charges a year earlier. Post-impairment, the Consumer Beauty reporting unit has **0% excess of fair value over carrying value**, with a 25bp reduction in growth assumptions triggering a further $35M. Bourjois carries a 21.2% discount rate — management's own assessment of that asset's risk. The company is attempting to sell or fix these brands at the point of minimum earnings, and Reuters notes valuations "are tight while inflation and interest rates are high."

The offsets are real but secondary: no retailer above 10% of revenue is a genuine structural advantage over peers dependent on one or two mass accounts; 81% in-house manufacturing supports the licensor pitch; mass skincare grew $8.6M in the nine months. Competitive Position: **3.5**, down from 4.0 — the Gucci displacement is now definitive and dated, and Prestige has begun buying volume with incentives.

## Risk Assessment

**Prestige goodwill cushion of 3.7%.** The Q3 10-Q discloses that the Prestige reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only 3.7% on $3,216.5M of net goodwill, with test assumptions of revenue growth up to 6.0% and an 11.75% discount rate. Break-even sensitivities: a 160bp reduction in annual revenue growth or a 75bp increase in the discount rate. Nine-month Prestige revenue was -1%. The FY2026 annual test (May 1 test date) will be disclosed in the 10-K expected in late August. A write-down is non-cash and does not affect the EBITDA-based covenant, but with negative tangible equity of $3.4B it invites rating-agency and lender scrutiny rather than being ignored as accounting noise.

**Ratings contingency.** Covenant suspension and collateral release on the Senior Secured Notes were triggered in September 2024 by investment-grade ratings from two of three agencies, "subject to reinstatement if those ratings agencies withdraw their investment grade rating." Pricing steps up if leverage reaches 4.75x or ratings fall below BB+/Ba1. Reuters (July 20) references "heightened scrutiny from rating agencies wary that the Gucci licence exit will temporarily compress margins." We could not verify current agency ratings; this is a live conditional risk, not a resolved one.

**Talc litigation, explicitly escalating.** The 10-Q language is unusually direct: settlement values and other costs "have increased substantially and are expected to increase in the future," with the range of reasonably possible loss in excess of accruals not reasonably estimable. An unquantifiable, worsening mass tort on a balance sheet with negative tangible equity carries more weight than the same exposure at a peer with $10B of tangible book.

**Brazilian tax assessments totaling approximately $409M** (Goiás ICMS ~$159.8M; three IPI assessments ~$199.9M; Minas Gerais ICMS ~$49.5M), with R$1,094.8M (~$208.4M) of surety bonds and R$227.3M (~$43.3M) of cash deposits posted and no loss recognized. Separately, gross unrecognized tax benefits of $230.5M.

**Securities and derivative litigation.** *Srinivasan v. Coty* (S.D.N.Y., filed March 23, 2026) alleges November 2025 FY2026 guidance statements were materially false; three derivative/proxy actions followed between April 3 and April 14, including §14(a) claims regarding the September 2025 proxy.

**Forward share-repurchase contracts.** Coty holds $490.0M combined notional from December 2022 and November 2023, paying interest at ~7% plus cash Hedge Valuation Adjustments when the share price falls. Identified HVA payments total roughly $385.5M — about 79% of notional — with no shares acquired, since the share count was fixed at inception. The contracts settle December 2026 (net cash) or January 2027 (physical). The residual liability and underlying share count are not disclosed; the below-expectation implied interest ($14.3M for nine months versus ~$25.7M on full notional) suggests the effective notional has already been reduced. The losses are largely sunk; the drain terminates within six months, which is a genuine forward positive.

**FX reversal.** Four to six points of translation tailwind are masking volume declines. If the dollar strengthens, reported revenue deteriorates independent of operations.

**Governance.** Executive Chairman and Interim CEO are the same person during a strategic review and a large disposition, removing independent board oversight of the CEO. JAB's 54% stake insulates Coty from consolidation — Reuters' phrasing — which cuts both ways: no takeover premium, but also a genuine take-private tail at a depressed price. An October 1, 2025 secondment arrangement with a JAB affiliate for "executive management services" ($0.7M in nine months) overlaps the CEO transition; the secondee is not named and no source connects it to Strobel, so this remains an open question rather than a finding.

Risk Factors: **2.8.**

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 89.9% is essentially unchanged over the observation window and is very high in absolute terms, consistent with a market pricing wide binary outcomes into the FY2026 annual report and the pending Consumer Beauty review. Open-interest put/call of 0.37 is call-tilted, which at a stock with 13.4% short interest and 2.9 days to cover is consistent with speculative upside positioning and/or overwriting rather than institutional hedging demand. The practical read: option premium is expensive on both sides, which raises the cost of expressing either a bearish or a bullish view through options, and the absence of concentrated put demand is a mild warning to anyone considering a short. It does not contradict the fundamental view; it does argue against paying for downside protection here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($4.10, +45%).** The Gucci deal demonstrated that management can monetize a deteriorating asset at a good price — roughly three to six times the EBITDA surrendered, plus litigation resolution. The forward-repurchase drain ($208.7M in nine months) ends by January 2027, restoring meaningful cash. The $80M fiscal-2026 savings program annualizes and Coty.Curated adds more. Consumer Beauty is either divested for cash applied to debt or stabilized around a narrower SKU set anchored on Lash Blast, Simply Ageless and Rimmel; mass skincare already grew $8.6M. Retailer concentration is minimal, prestige fragrance remains a structurally growing global category, and 81% in-house manufacturing captures margin as volumes recover. Insiders bought — the Consumer Beauty president 83,000 shares at $2.41, the CFO 5,000 at $2.65 — with no open-market sales, and the interim CEO's six million options require the stock to reach $5.56 (50% vesting) and $9.00 (100%). At roughly 6.5x EV/EBITDA with ~$1.5B of undrawn revolver and no maturity until May 2027, a stabilized Coty is worth substantially more than $2.80.

**Bear case ($1.40, -53%).** Volume is down 8% and Prestige is discounting to defend it; the FX tailwind that halved the reported decline will fade. Adjusted EBITDA fell 21% for the nine months and 38% in Q3, and the Q4 guide is for further revenue decline with pressured gross margin. Consumer Beauty is already loss-making at the segment level on an adjusted basis and its reporting unit has zero valuation cushion. Prestige goodwill has a 3.7% cushion against assumptions of 6% growth in a shrinking segment. The Gucci proceeds do not fix credit: $340M against $2,959M of net debt is an 11.5% reduction, while $115M against ~$800M of EBITDA is a 14.4% reduction, so leverage moves from ~3.70x to ~3.82x against a 4.00x covenant. A downgrade reinstates covenants and re-encumbers collateral. Talc costs are rising and unquantifiable, $409M of Brazilian assessments are unreserved, four consecutive earnings misses and a 25.5% ninety-day cut to current-year estimates mean consensus visibility is poor, and there is no permanent CEO or approved strategy. Equity is 44% of enterprise value, so modest EBITDA disappointment produces outsized equity loss.

**Our view.** The bull case requires volume inflection that no disclosed datapoint yet supports, and the bear case requires only continuation. The market is not overshooting: an 80% decline since early 2024 reflects a business whose adjusted EBITDA peaked at $1,091M in FY2024 and is heading toward roughly $700M in FY2028, carried on $2.9B of net debt with negative tangible equity. We can identify what the sellers are pricing, and we largely agree with it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (any two would move us to HOLD; all three to BUY):**

1. **Volume stabilization.** Two consecutive quarters of ex-FX volume decline of 2% or better in Prestige, with price/mix back to zero or positive (versus Q3 FY2026 volume -8%, Prestige price/mix -3%). This is the single most important number and appears in the volume/price/FX decomposition each quarter.
2. **Leverage trajectory.** Reported-basis net debt/adjusted EBITDA below 3.0x on a stabilized EBITDA base of at least $800M — which requires either a Consumer Beauty divestiture generating $1.0B or more of proceeds applied to debt, or EBITDA stabilization plus two years of FCF. Watch the FY2027 leverage disclosure against the 4.00x covenant that runs through July 11, 2028.
3. **Price.** Below $2.00 with management guiding FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $800M or better. At $1.90 the base case of $2.60 offers 37% upside against a $1.40 bear, which inverts the current asymmetry.

**Additional upgrade triggers:** appointment of a permanent CEO with a board that separates the chair role; a Prestige goodwill test that passes the FY2026 annual review with a widened cushion; confirmation of the Swarovski and Marni licenses with disclosed scale; quantification of talc exposure within an accrued range.

**Timeline for headwinds:** through FY2028 (fiscal year ending June 2028), which is the first full year without Gucci and the year Reuters and Barclays identify as the trough. The nearest decision point is the FY2026 Form 10-K and Q4 results, expected in late August 2026 (FY2025 was filed August 21, 2025). That filing should disclose the May 1 annual goodwill test outcome, full-year adjusted EBITDA against our $832-880M sensitivity, initial FY2027 guidance, and any update on the Consumer Beauty review.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The FY2026 Form 10-K and Q4 results, expected in late August 2026. Management has already guided Q4 reported revenue to decline mid-single digits with gross margin pressured by lower sales, net tariffs and elevated excess-and-obsolescence, so the earnings direction is pre-announced. The genuinely new information will be the FY2026 annual goodwill test against a 3.7% Prestige cushion and initial FY2027 guidance. A Prestige impairment plus FY2027 EBITDA guidance below $760M would be the bear trigger.

**Timing window.** Roughly three to four weeks — short enough that carry is manageable. The secondary window is the Consumer Beauty review outcome, which has no disclosed timeline.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 13.4% of float short, 50.6M shares, 2.9 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme; the low days-to-cover means the position can exit without a squeeze, which cuts both ways — it also means a positive surprise clears quickly.

**Borrow and options signal.** ATM IV of 89.9% makes put protection expensive, and open-interest put/call of 0.37 is call-tilted. The options market is not confirming the bearish thesis; if anything, positioning skews toward speculative upside. On a $2.80 stock with 13.4% short interest, borrow is likely a meaningful drag. This combination argues against paying for the trade.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $1.40-1.80 (-36% to -50%). Upside risks are material and specific: a Consumer Beauty divestiture announcement, a JAB take-private or tender at a premium given 54% control and an 80% price decline, further asset monetizations of the Gucci type, and the receipt of up to $150M of additional Kering cash (of which $120M is non-contingent) by September 30, 2027. A realistic squeeze target is $3.60-4.10. Reward:risk of roughly $1.10 against $0.80 is **1.4:1** — below our 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental case is sound, but the mechanics are not: sub-2:1 reward-to-risk, expensive borrow and option premium, call-tilted positioning, and a controlled-company take-private tail at a depressed price. This is a stock to not own rather than to short.

## Conclusion

**AVOID. Overall score 3.1** (from 3.0 in June).

The score improved marginally for a specific, verifiable reason: financing risk fell. The Gucci agreement delivered $250M of cash at signing against roughly $340M of net-of-tax non-contingent proceeds, resolved all Gucci-related litigation, and gave the company a defined operating horizon through June 30, 2027. Combined with $792.2M of debt retired, the elimination of Term A and Term B, and roughly $1.5B of undrawn revolver against no maturity until May 2027, Coty is no longer a near-term liquidity story. Management executed that transaction well — receiving three to six times the value of the license life it surrendered.

The recommendation does not change because the operating business is deteriorating faster than the balance sheet is healing, and the arithmetic of the Gucci exit does not repair the credit. Nine-month volume fell 6% and Q3 volume 8%, with reported revenue down only 2% because currency added four to six points. Adjusted EBITDA fell 21%, and 38% in the March quarter.
