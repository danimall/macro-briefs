**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Progress Software is a leveraged software roll-up: it buys mature, cash-generative enterprise infrastructure assets, integrates them onto a lean platform, and runs them for margin and recurring revenue rather than organic growth. The model works on cash economics — FY2025 free cash flow was $229M on a 23.5% margin against roughly $70M of interest expense — but the reported GAAP picture is muddied by ~$145M/year of acquired-intangible amortization and ~$78M of stock-based compensation, which compress GAAP net income to $73M against a management-defined non-GAAP operating income of $384.8M. The central tension is that the deal-driven growth engine now competes with debt paydown for the same free cash flow, and organic ARR is growing only ~2% with net revenue retention of ~99–100%.

The stock has fallen roughly 48% from its 52-week high of $65.50 to about $33.84, and trades at ~17x trailing earnings and near the low end of its systems-software peer set. That decline is driven less by fundamentals than by repricing — revenue rose 30% (largely from the ShareFile acquisition), the company beat consensus in three of the last four quarters, and consensus EPS estimates have drifted higher (current-year FY26 EPS revised +5.2% over 90 days to $5.98). What has repriced is the market's willingness to pay for a flat-organic, highly-levered roll-up in a higher-for-longer rate environment, compounded by an unresolved MOVEit litigation tail and a balance sheet where total debt (~$1.29B) exceeds equity (~$478M) and market cap ($1.4B) sits above goodwill alone ($1.31B).

We rate PRGS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case — deleveraging drives a multiple re-rating on a genuinely cheap FCF stream — is coherent and the estimate-revision trend is a mild positive. But the thesis lacks a dated catalyst, the April 2026 refinancing swapped 1.0% convertible debt for 5.37% floating revolver debt (raising cash interest cost), the flagship AI acquisition (Nuclia) had its earn-out written to zero, and insiders have been net sellers with no open-market buying into the decline. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the prior three AVOID calls no longer fully capture the value now embedded at $34, but the case for owning it is not yet strong enough for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Progress operates a "total growth strategy" centered on acquiring established software businesses and consolidating them onto a shared operating platform. It reports as a single segment and positions itself around "responsible AI-powered applications," though the underlying portfolio is predominantly legacy enterprise infrastructure. Fiscal year ends November 30.

The acquisition cadence defines the business: Ipswitch (2019), Chef (2020), Kemp (2021), MarkLogic (2023), ShareFile (October 2024, ~$875M), and Nuclia (June 2025, ~$21M, agentic RAG/AI). The product portfolio spans OpenEdge (the legacy application-development cash cow), ShareFile (SaaS document collaboration, now the second revenue pillar), MOVEit (managed file transfer), Chef (DevSecOps), MarkLogic, Kemp LoadMaster, Flowmon, WhatsUp Gold, Sitefinity, DataDirect, and AI-oriented Semaphore/Nuclia/Corticon.

OpenEdge plus ShareFile together account for slightly more than half of total revenue. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, roughly half of revenue flows through indirect channels (ISVs, OEMs, VARs), and ~36% of revenue is international (29% EMEA). The company employs 2,801 people, with heavy low-cost development presence in Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, and India. The moat rests on embedded, sticky infrastructure with high switching costs — particularly OpenEdge, which is embedded via ISVs and OEMs — and recurring maintenance/subscription streams. Management concedes it is not a technology leader, stating there is no dominant vendor and that competitors have greater resources. The closest strategic analog is Constellation Software or Roper's buy-and-hold software model, executed here with more leverage and less scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $602M → FY2023 $694M → FY2024 $753M → FY2025 $978M (+30% YoY). The FY2025 jump is almost entirely inorganic; ShareFile contributed $261.6M in FY2025 against only a $21.1M one-month stub in FY2024. Organic dynamics are flat — ARR grew ~2% YoY to $852M at FY2025-end (rising to $868M at Q2 FY2026), and net revenue retention is ~99–100%, down from a prior 100–102%.

**Revenue mix shift:** SaaS revenue surged from $44.6M (6% of total) to $287.9M (29%) on ShareFile; maintenance was flat near $410M; license revenue declined 5% to $238M. Q2 FY2026 showed a license rebound (+36% YoY on DataDirect/Chef/MarkLogic timing), while maintenance (-2%) and services (-6%) continue to erode.

**Profitability and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap:** GAAP operating margin was 15.7%, net margin 7.5%, net income $73.1M in FY2025. Adding back ~$145M of acquired-intangible amortization and ~$78M of stock-based comp lifts the cash picture materially; management's non-GAAP operating income was $384.8M and its pay-for-performance net income figure $251.9M. The gap is standard for an intangible-heavy roll-up, but it means trailing GAAP P/E understates the cash earnings multiple, and the trailing 17.3x is misleading in both directions — it flatters the multiple relative to cash flow while overstating GAAP earnings quality.

**Cash flow — the genuine strength:** Operating cash flow was $235M in FY2025, free cash flow $229M (23.5% margin), with negligible capex ($5.7M). H1 FY2026 OCF rose to $177.5M from $98.9M on strong collections. This FCF services the debt and funds buybacks and M&A.

**Balance sheet — the genuine concern:** Altman Z-Score of 0.79 sits in the "distress" zone, but this is largely a structural artifact of the leveraged, goodwill-heavy model rather than operational distress. Equity is thin ($478M) because ~$1.9B of goodwill plus intangibles sit against ~$1.29B of debt, and working capital is negative because ~$425M of deferred revenue (a favorable non-cash liability) sits in current liabilities. The current ratio of 0.49 is normal for SaaS; the D/E of 5.14 reflects real leverage. Total debt at Q2 FY2026 was ~$1.29B — $450M of 3.5% convertible notes due 2030 plus $850M drawn on the $1.5B revolver at 5.37%. The $360M 1.0% 2026 convertible notes were redeemed in April 2026 (funded by drawing the revolver), swapping cheap converts for higher-cost floating debt. FY2025 interest expense was $70.9M, up 121% year-over-year.

**Capital allocation:** The dividend was suspended at the ShareFile close (October 2024) to prioritize debt paydown and M&A. Buybacks continue ($105M in FY2025, $55M in H1 FY2026, $147.5M remaining authorization), modestly reducing share count from 43.0M to 41.3M.

We score Financial Health 4.5. The FCF generation is a genuine strength that supports the debt (interest coverage of ~3.2x on FCF), but the leverage, the floating-rate reset, the thin equity, and the impairment overhang keep this below the neutral line. Financial health is most reliable as a downside filter, and the leverage and refinancing structure are exactly the features that flag risk here.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is bifurcated. Headline revenue growth of 30% is entirely acquisition-driven; strip out ShareFile and the organic business is essentially flat, evidenced by ~2% ARR growth and NRR of ~99–100% (declining from prior periods). The model requires continuous accretive M&A to grow, and leverage now constrains that M&A capacity, creating a self-reinforcing bind: the deal engine competes with debt paydown for the same cash, and management's dividend suspension is an implicit acknowledgment of that constraint.

The forward earnings picture is more constructive than the organic-growth picture. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up +5.2% over 90 days to $5.98 (and next-year to $6.06, +6.0%), and current-quarter estimates are up +8.3%. The company beat consensus in three of the last four quarters (by 6.6%, 12.8%, and 13.4%, with one in-line print). This reflects margin realization as ShareFile integration matures and restructuring costs roll off, plus the OBBBA R&E expensing tailwind reducing cash taxes from FY2026. Q2 FY2026 revenue was $253.5M (+7% YoY) with $0.50 diluted EPS (up from $0.39); H1 EPS was $1.03 versus $0.63.

The estimate-revision trend is positive but modest — well below the +30%/90-day threshold that would flag a genuine earnings acceleration, and it is driven by margin/tax mechanics rather than accelerating demand. The Nuclia earn-out written to zero indicates the flagship AI acquisition is underdelivering against FY2026 revenue targets, tempering the AI-transformation narrative. We score Growth Prospects 4.5. Weighting growth_prospects heavily as the primary winner-selection dimension, the near-flat organic base and M&A-dependent model justify a below-neutral score despite the favorable near-term EPS revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$33.84, PRGS trades at 17.3x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.66 and roughly 5.7x current-year consensus EPS of $5.98 — the latter is the figure that matters, since consensus EPS reflects the non-GAAP/cash-adjusted framework the company and Street use. On that basis the stock is optically cheap: ~5.7x forward EPS, and a FCF yield above 16% on $229M FCF against a $1.4B market cap.

Against peers, the systems-software median P/E is 53.5 (a set skewed by loss-makers and high-multiple names like CVLT at 80.3x, ATEN at 53.5x, and NABL at negative). PATH at 17.6x and QLYS at 19.9x are the more comparable profitable references. PRGS's trailing 17.3x sits below those, and its forward multiple is dramatically lower. The discount reflects flat organic growth, elevated leverage, the MOVEit tail, and a suspended dividend, but the magnitude looks large relative to a 23.5% FCF margin and rising estimates.

The proxy's own pay-versus-performance disclosure quantifies the de-rating: cumulative TSR of 104.6 against a peer-group TSR of 272.7 over the measurement window, even as net income and non-GAAP operating income rose. The market has systematically penalized the growth quality and balance-sheet risk. The bull case for the valuation score rests on deleveraging driving multiple re-rating; if the revolver draws down and the leverage narrative improves, a re-rating from ~5.7x to even 8–9x forward FCF/EPS is plausible.

Cheapness is not itself a buy signal here. The valuation dimension has been reliably anti-predictive — cheap names have underperformed, and the effect is strengthening. PRGS is the archetypal candidate for a value trap: cheap for structural reasons (leverage, flat organic growth, litigation) with no dated catalyst to unlock the value. The deleveraging thesis is real but requires patience and is exposed to the floating-rate reset working against it. We score Valuation 5.5 — the cheapness is genuine and there is an identifiable (if uncatalyzed) re-rating path, but we deliberately withhold a higher score given the absence of a near-term trigger and the anti-predictive pattern of low-multiple names.

## Competitive Landscape

Progress competes in fragmented, mature niches — application-development platforms, managed file transfer, DevOps, data connectivity, network monitoring, and digital experience platforms — against both proprietary and open-source vendors. It is a consolidator rather than an innovator. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating: OpenEdge carries secular-decline risk as a legacy platform, maintenance and services revenue are eroding (-2% and -6% in Q2), and NRR has slipped toward ~99–100%. The moat (high switching costs on embedded infrastructure) is durable but not widening.

The AI rebranding is marketing-forward with limited demonstrated revenue contribution — the Nuclia earn-out written to zero is direct evidence the AI pivot is not yet gaining commercial traction. The peer-group TSR gap confirms the market rewards higher-growth systems-software names and is discounting PRGS's flat organic profile.

We score Competitive Position 4.5, in line with the recent trajectory (down from 5.5). The trajectory matters more than the level here: flat-to-eroding organic dynamics offset by acquisition-driven scale do not constitute an improving competitive position. This is a company holding sticky but slowly-declining niches, not gaining share.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing/rate risk (most material):** With $850M now on a floating-rate revolver at 5.37%, the credit facility carries financial covenants; a breach would accelerate the debt. The 2030 converts and revolver create a 2030 maturity wall. The entire thesis hinges on FCF-funded deleveraging, and the April 2026 refinancing worsened the interest profile even as total debt fell.

**MOVEit cyber litigation (unquantified tail):** The 2023 MOVEit zero-day breach spawned multidistrict litigation in the District of Massachusetts. As of Q2 FY2026, the court dismissed (in whole or part) 23 of 33 bellwether claims, but fact discovery runs to September 29, 2026 and class-certification briefing begins August 2026 — not expected to conclude within 12 months. The company has recorded no loss contingency (unable to estimate) and has only ~$1.1M of cyber insurance remaining, down from $15M. A material adverse settlement or judgment is possible and unreserved. (The data exfiltration occurred in customer-controlled environments, not Progress's own systems, which bears on liability scope.)

**Anemic organic growth:** The 2% ARR growth and ~99–100% NRR mean the model requires continuous accretive M&A to grow, but leverage now constrains that capacity.

**Product concentration:** More than 50% of revenue from OpenEdge (legacy, secular-decline risk) plus ShareFile.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment:** $1.31B goodwill plus $515M intangibles, with market cap modestly above goodwill alone — a metric management flags as an impairment trigger if it were to fall below carrying values on a sustained basis.

**Convertible dilution dynamics:** Management notes the converts may encourage short-selling; anti-dilutive share count rose to ~4.3M from ~0.8M as the stock fell.

We score Risk Factors 4.0. The MOVEit tail is genuinely unreserved and asymmetric, the leverage is real and constraining, and the impairment trigger is live. These are specific, idiosyncratic risks not fully priced. Because risk_factors has become mildly anti-predictive, we temper how much weight we place on this score for winner-selection, but the concentration of specific downside catalysts justifies a below-neutral reading.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 90.7% against 51.6% one-year historical volatility — rich by a 39.1-point spread, in the 87th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (90.7% near-term versus 62.5% at six months), which the market uses to price an imminent catalyst or event. The nearest expiry implies a ±15.9% move. Put/call skew is balanced (0.9%), and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.38 and 0.31), so the elevated vol is not accompanied by directional put demand — the market expects a large move but is not clearly positioned for a downside break. Short interest is 13.2% of float (6.3 days to cover), confirming institutional bearish positioning but not at squeeze extremes. The rich near-term IV suggests an event (likely the next earnings print or MOVEit litigation milestone) is being priced; for a fundamental holder, this argues against paying up for the stock ahead of a binary and favors patience.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (target ~$50–$83):** The stock is deeply discounted on a 23.5% FCF-margin business trading at ~5.7x forward EPS and a >16% FCF yield. As the $850M revolver draws down and leverage falls, the multiple re-rates toward profitable-peer levels (PATH/QLYS at 17–20x GAAP). Estimate revisions are already turning up (+5–6% over 90 days on FY26/FY27), ShareFile integration is maturing into margin, restructuring costs are rolling off, and the OBBBA R&E expensing benefit lowers cash taxes. If deleveraging proceeds and the MOVEit litigation resolves without a material charge, a re-rating to 8–10x forward EPS supports the $45.50 median and $50.83 mean analyst targets, with the $83 high target reflecting a full re-rating plus resumed accretive M&A. On stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, analyst targets tend to lag, and the current forward multiple sits well below the growth-adjusted range.

**Bear Case (target ~$24–$26):** The floating-rate revolver at 5.37% keeps interest expense elevated, absorbing the FCF that was supposed to fund deleveraging and M&A, so the growth engine stalls. Organic ARR stays near 2%, NRR keeps slipping, and the AI pivot (Nuclia earn-out to zero) fails to add growth. A material adverse MOVEit class-certification outcome forces a large, currently-unbookable charge against ~$1.1M of remaining insurance. A sustained market-cap decline below goodwill triggers an impairment. The stock retests its 52-week low of $23.82. Insider net selling with no open-market buying is consistent with this scenario.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The thesis is genuinely two-sided: a cheap, cash-generative asset with an identifiable re-rating path, offset by real leverage, a floating-rate reset working against deleveraging, an unreserved litigation tail, and no dated catalyst. The prior three AVOID calls captured the de-rating well, but at ~$34 — down ~48% from the high — the embedded value now offsets much of the downside, so a blanket AVOID no longer fits. The case for a BUY is not yet made because the deleveraging-to-re-rating narrative requires a catalyst that does not exist on the calendar and is exposed to rate risk.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of revolver paydown reducing total debt below ~$1.1B, with interest expense stabilizing or falling, demonstrating the deleveraging thesis is executing.
- A favorable MOVEit class-certification outcome (denial of certification or dismissal of remaining bellwether claims) that removes the unreserved tail.
- Organic ARR growth re-accelerating above ~4% with NRR stabilizing above 100%, or resumption of accretive M&A funded without incremental leverage.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material MOVEit settlement/judgment or a class-certification ruling against the company that forces an unbookable charge.
- A goodwill impairment charge, or market cap sustained meaningfully below goodwill.
- Covenant pressure on the revolver, or estimate revisions turning negative alongside a Q3/Q4 FY2026 miss.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings print (late September 2026), which coincides with the MOVEit fact-discovery close (September 29, 2026) and class-certification briefing window — the cluster of catalysts the options market is pricing through its backwardated term structure.

## Conclusion

Progress Software is a cash-rich, growth-poor, highly-levered software roll-up whose stock has been correctly de-rated for its balance sheet and flat organic profile but is now cheap enough that the risk/reward has moved toward neutral. The ~$229M FCF stream comfortably covers interest and supports the deleveraging thesis; the ~5.7x forward EPS multiple and >16% FCF yield offer real value if leverage falls and the MOVEit tail resolves benignly. Against that, the April 2026 refinancing raised cash interest costs, organic growth is near zero, the AI pivot is underdelivering, insiders are selling into the decline, and the litigation tail is unreserved with insurance nearly exhausted.

We recommend HOLD (uncertain). The overall score of 4.6 reflects a business with a durable cash engine but structurally constrained growth and specific, uncatalyzed downside risks. We would turn constructive on evidence of actual deleveraging plus a favorable MOVEit resolution, and would move to AVOID on an impairment, an adverse litigation outcome, or negative estimate momentum.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: market cap (~$1.37B) is above goodwill (~$1.31B), not below it, weakening the "market prices in impairment" framing used in the risk and bear-case sections; total debt is ~$1.29B (not $1.4B), modestly reducing the leverage severity cited throughout.
