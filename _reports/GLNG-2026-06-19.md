**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Golar LNG has completed a multi-year transformation into a pure-play floating LNG (FLNG) infrastructure provider, shedding its legacy shipping, carrier, and FSRU businesses to become what management describes as the only proven independent FLNG-as-a-service operator. The investment case is a backlog-realization story rather than a current-earnings story: against roughly $265M of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA, the company has secured approximately $14B of 20-year contracted EBITDA backlog through two Argentine contracts (Hilli/SESA at $285M/yr and MKII/SESA at $400M/yr) plus the BP-anchored Gimi project. If the two Argentine units come online on schedule in 2027-2028, contracted fixed EBITDA roughly triples. The market capitalization of ~$5.1B is being underwritten substantially on revenue streams that have not yet started.

The forward earnings trajectory supports the thesis: Q1 2026 net income to Golar reached $83.6M (versus $8.2M a year earlier), revenue grew 120% to $137.6M, and adjusted EBITDA rose 158% to $105.6M on the first full quarter of Gimi contribution. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 58.5% over 90 days and next-year estimates up 33.8%. The active strategic review run by Goldman Sachs International, which contemplates a sale, merger, or asset divestiture, is a live M&A catalyst that explains part of the stock's strength and Deutsche Bank's price target raise to $65.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the concentration of risk. Both 20-year growth contracts depend on SESA, a newly formed Argentine entity, and on the construction and financing of a ~500km dedicated pipeline from Vaca Muerta to the Gulf of San Matías that does not yet have EPC and financing fully arranged. The thesis layers Argentine sovereign, currency, regulatory, and infrastructure-execution risk over a 20-year horizon onto an otherwise attractive niche business. The stock has retraced ~14% from its $57.79 trailing-52-week high to ~$50 but remains up ~17% over the trailing year and trades at ~36x trailing earnings; at this level the backlog optionality is substantially priced, the merchant commodity exposure cuts both ways, and there is no asymmetric entry. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$40s, on concrete progress de-risking the Argentine pipeline, or on a defined strategic-review outcome.

## Company Overview

Golar LNG is a Bermuda-incorporated foreign private issuer (NASDAQ: GLNG) that files Form 20-F annually and Form 6-K for interim and material events. Over an 80-year corporate history it pioneered maritime LNG infrastructure and, through 2024-2025, completed a strategic pivot away from LNG carriers and FSRUs into a focused FLNG-as-a-service model. FLNG-as-a-service lets oil majors and national oil companies monetize stranded or competitively-priced gas reserves with lower capital intensity and faster speed-to-market than land-based liquefaction.

The asset base comprises:
- **FLNG Hilli Episeyo** — ~1.4 MTPA, operating offshore Cameroon under a liquefaction tolling agreement, with 152 cargoes offloaded and over 10M tonnes cumulative. The Cameroon contract ends Q3 2026; the vessel then goes to Seatrium (Singapore) for a ~$350M upgrade and life-extension before beginning a 20-year contract with SESA in Argentina in H2 2027.
- **FLNG Gimi** — 2.7 MTPA nameplate, 70% Golar-owned (Seatrium owns 30%). Achieved commercial operations June 2025 on a 20-year lease-and-operate agreement at the BP-operated Greater Tortue Ahmeyim project offshore Mauritania/Senegal, and is overproducing (Q1 2026 invoiced day rate 19% above contract).
- **MKII FLNG** — 3.5 MTPA conversion under construction at CIMC Raffles (Yantai, China), a ~$2.2B project with $1.2B spent (entirely equity-funded), targeting Q4 2027 completion and H2 2028 deployment to SESA in Argentina on a 20-year contract.
- **Fourth FLNG** — under active consideration, with long-lead items reserved in May 2026 and intent to order within 2026.

Total liquefaction capacity is 5.1 MTPA on the water plus 3.5 MTPA under construction. Management is chaired by Tor Olav Trøim, with Karl Fredrik Staubo as CEO and Eduardo Maranhão as CFO. This is a founder/sponsor-influenced board with deep Norwegian maritime-finance roots, reflected in the company's dual SEC and Oslo (Norwegian Securities Trading Act) disclosure regime.

## Financial Analysis

Reported GAAP financials require careful interpretation because of three accounting features that distort standard screens. First, Golar consolidates a lessor variable-interest entity for the Hilli sale-and-leaseback, which makes the XBRL "total debt" line ($301M for 2025) misleadingly small; the economically relevant figure is the ~$2.7B contractual debt non-GAAP measure, with net debt around $1.5-1.7B. Second, FLNG Gimi is accounted for as a sales-type lease, which eliminates its principal amortization from GAAP revenue and understates economic activity. Third, embedded commodity derivatives in the Hilli tolling agreement (TTF gas and Brent oil linkages) produce large non-cash mark-to-market swings — a $21M unrealized loss in Q4 2025 followed by a $34M unrealized gain in Q1 2026 — making GAAP net income lumpy. The cleaner lenses are adjusted EBITDA, contractual debt, and total Golar cash.

On those metrics, FY2025 revenue was $393.5M (+51% YoY), net income to Golar $65.7M (+29%), and adjusted EBITDA $264.6M (+10%). The Q1 2026 results reflect the first full quarter of Gimi contribution plus a favorable commodity mark.

Liquidity is solid. Total Golar cash was ~$1.2B at year-end 2025 and ~$1.0B at Q1 2026. Recent financings include $500M of 7.50% senior unsecured notes due 2030 (October 2025) and a new $1.2B Gimi bank facility (November 2025, SOFR+2.50%, 7-year tenor, 16-year amortization) that released roughly $400M of equity to Golar's 70% share. The MKII conversion has been 100% equity-funded so far ($1.2B), which creates substantial liquidity-release optionality once that unit is financed against its 20-year contract. The 7.50% coupon on the new notes reflects a rising cost of capital worth monitoring given the higher-for-longer rate path.

The earnings surprise record is constructive: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, including a 24.5% beat in Q1 2026 ($0.53 actual versus $0.42 estimate) and a 29.6% beat in Q3 2025. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly at the margin: a $0.25/quarter dividend (~2.0% yield) plus a $150M buyback program, of which 1.1M shares were repurchased in Q4 2025 at ~$37.76 (well below the current price), with $109M remaining.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on backlog conversion. The backlog breaks down as $285M/yr fixed adjusted EBITDA from Hilli/SESA ($5.7B), $400M/yr from MKII/SESA ($8B), and ~$3B of net earnings backlog from Gimi (Golar's 70% share). The fourth FLNG, if ordered within 2026, would extend this trajectory.

Estimate revisions reinforce the trajectory. Current-year EPS consensus moved from $0.64 ninety days ago to $1.01 today, and next-year from $0.80 to $1.07. These are large upward revisions on a stock with revenue growth well above 40% in the most recent quarter, the kind of combination that typically precedes further re-rating as consensus catches up to contracted ramp. We weight this signal positively, but with two qualifiers. The near-quarter estimate ($0.39, revised down 12.7% over 30 days) and the next-quarter estimate (now -$0.04) reflect the Hilli earnings gap between the Cameroon contract end (Q3 2026) and the Argentine commercial operations date (H2 2027), during which the vessel earns nothing and incurs its $350M repositioning cost. The annual estimates capture the structural ramp; the quarterly path is bumpy.

Demand-side validation has improved. SESA signed an 8-year LNG sale agreement with Germany's state-owned SEFE (up to 2 MTPA from 2027), making SEFE — already the Hilli offtaker since 2022 — Argentina's first long-term LNG customer and de-risking the downstream offtake. Management cites Middle East escalation, energy-security demand, and AI/data-center power growth as accelerating commercial discussions, consistent with the broader confirmation that gas-fired generation demand for LNG infrastructure is real and broad-based. The structural niche is attractive: FLNG monetizes stranded gas at lower capex/ton than onshore, faster, and with shorter shipping routes, exactly the outsourced-capital-intensity pitch NOCs and majors want in a supply-diversification environment.

## Valuation Assessment

GLNG trades at ~36x trailing earnings on $0.60 trailing EPS, which is uninformative at this point in the asset cycle given rising estimates. On forward consensus of $1.01 for the current year, the multiple is ~49x, and on next year's $1.07 it is ~46x — still elevated because GAAP EPS remains depressed during the Hilli gap and continues to be distorted by commodity marks. The more relevant frame is EV-to-contracted-EBITDA. At a ~$5.1B market cap plus ~$2.7B contractual debt less ~$1.0B cash, enterprise value is roughly $6.8B against current EBITDA of ~$265M (~26x); against fully-ramped contracted EBITDA approaching $700M+ once both Argentine units run, the multiple falls toward the high single digits. The valuation question is therefore entirely about the probability and timing of backlog conversion.

The peer table is of limited help because the listed Energy comparables (Magnolia at 15.8x, PBF at 10.8x, CNX at 4.4x, peer median 10.8x) are upstream E&P and refining names with fundamentally different cash-flow profiles — none is a long-dated, contracted floating-infrastructure business. A toll-and-lease infrastructure model with a 20-year backlog warrants a premium to commodity producers, but the size of that premium depends on confidence in Argentine execution.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $44.50, median $64.00, high $70.00, mean $60.28, with the consensus mix at 7 buys and 2 holds and sentiment improving. Deutsche Bank's PT sits at $65, while Pareto downgraded to Hold on valuation after the stock's run. At ~$50, the stock sits below the median target but above the low. We are cautious about treating the consensus median as a floor here: at ~46-49x forward GAAP EPS the multiple is not cheap on reported earnings, the backlog optionality requiring Argentine infrastructure to be built is already substantially in the price, and the merchant commodity exposure adds two-sided variance the targets may not fully reflect. We see fair value in a range that overlaps current levels rather than implying clear asymmetric upside, which supports a HOLD. We would treat the low-$40s as the level where the backlog is no longer fully priced and the risk/reward turns favorable.

## Competitive Landscape

Golar's competitive position is its strongest dimension. It is the only company with a multi-unit operating track record of converting LNG carriers into floating liquefaction vessels and running them at high uptime, with Hilli's 152 cargoes and Gimi overproducing at 19% above its contractual day rate as evidence. The primary reference competitor in floating liquefaction is Eni, but Eni's units are captive oil-major assets rather than independent service offerings, leaving Golar effectively alone in the independent FLNG-as-a-service category. The in-house technical platform delivering competitive capex/ton and operational reliability is a genuine moat in a niche with high barriers to entry.

The structural tailwind is energy security and supply diversification driving NOCs and majors to outsource liquefaction capital intensity. The structural headwind, acknowledged by management, is a multi-year U.S.-led LNG supply wave that could soften tolling rates and LNG prices, pressuring both new-contract economics and Golar's merchant exposure. Golar's counter — that AI/data-center power demand, demand elasticity, and an oil-price floor will absorb the supply — is plausible but unproven. The competitive trajectory is improving: the company has consolidated into a focused, high-uptime operator with a unique platform and is the natural consolidator/partner of choice in its niche, which is also what makes it a credible M&A target.

## Risk Assessment

**Argentina country and counterparty concentration (dominant risk).** Both 20-year growth contracts depend on SESA, owned by Pan American Energy (30%), YPF (25%), Pampa Energía (20%), Harbour Energy (15%), and Golar (10%). The economics require: (a) SESA building a ~500km dedicated pipeline from Vaca Muerta to the Gulf of San Matías, where the pipe supplier (Welspun) is selected but EPC and financing are still being arranged; (b) Argentine political, currency, and regulatory stability over 20 years; and (c) SESA meeting its feed-gas obligations. Failure of pipeline construction or financing would directly impair the backlog. The corporate counterparties are solid, but the weak link is Argentine sovereign and infrastructure execution over two decades. This is the central reason for the depressed risk score.

**Commodity-price exposure (two-sided).** Golar deliberately re-introduced merchant exposure on top of fixed tolling. The combination of Hilli (~$30M/yr) and MKII (~$40M/yr) merchant upside plus the 10% SESA equity totals up to ~$100M of annual upside per $1/MMBtu above $8 FOB, but ~$28M of downside per $1 below SESA's cash break-even. This is currently a tailwind with LNG prices well above $8, but it is a structural risk in an oversupply scenario and is a meaningful departure from a pure toll-taker profile.

**Execution and the Hilli gap.** The MKII conversion in China and the Hilli ~$350M upgrade and relocation to Singapore both carry shipyard schedule and cost risk. Hilli earns nothing between Cameroon contract end (Q3 2026) and Argentine COD (H2 2027) while incurring the repositioning budget ($50M spent as of Q1 2026), which is why near-quarter estimates are weak.

**Financing dependence.** Growth is financing-dependent: MKII still requires asset-level financing (being arranged with a commercial bank and export credit agencies), and the 7.50% unsecured note coupon signals a rising cost of capital in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Strategic-review uncertainty.** The Q1 2026 filing added a dedicated risk factor on the review, flagging the possibility of no transaction, operational disruption, management distraction, and counterparty/financing uncertainty during the process. The review is both the primary near-term catalyst and a source of overhang.

**Disclosure gap.** As a foreign private issuer exempt from Section 16, Golar provides no U.S. insider-transaction visibility, removing a normally useful signal. The related-party web (Trøim/Seatrium/Stolt-Nielsen, with Seatrium both the Gimi 30% co-owner and the Hilli upgrade yard) warrants attention, though it is not a confirmed red flag.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-complacent and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 35.0% versus 29.2% one-year historical volatility (modestly rich, +5.8pp), with an IV percentile of only 27% within the current chain and a balanced put/call skew of 0.3%. The term structure is in contango (near-term 35.0% below long-term 39.5%), a normal structure that prices rising uncertainty further out, consistent with the Hilli gap and Argentine ramp risk crystallizing in 2027. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.6%. ATM IV has fallen from 40.9% to 35.0% over the past two weeks as the price drifted down ~4.4%, suggesting resolved near-term event risk rather than building stress. The headline put/call volume ratio of 10.55 is noisy against a put/call OI ratio of just 0.03, so the OI structure (very call-heavy) is the cleaner read and is not bearish. Short interest is moderate at 7.2% of float with 3.5 days to cover, leaving limited squeeze dynamics in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The two Argentine units come online on schedule, tripling contracted fixed EBITDA toward $700M+ and re-rating the stock as a long-dated infrastructure compounder. The strategic review yields a sale or merger at a premium that crystallizes the backlog value the market is discounting. Merchant commodity exposure stays a tailwind in a tight LNG market, adding up to ~$100M/yr per $1 above $8/MMBtu. A fourth FLNG order extends the growth runway. In this scenario the stock approaches the high analyst target of ~$70 (+41% from ~$50).

**Bear Case.** The Argentine pipeline financing or construction stalls, SESA fails to meet feed-gas or pipeline commitments, or Argentine sovereign/currency instability impairs the 20-year economics, directly cutting the backlog the market cap is built on. A U.S.-led LNG supply wave softens tolling rates and flips merchant exposure to a headwind (~$28M/yr per $1 below break-even). The Hilli gap and the $350M repositioning weigh on 2026-2027 earnings, and the strategic review concludes without a transaction. In this scenario the stock falls toward the low analyst target of ~$44.50 (-11%) or below if Argentine execution genuinely cracks, with a plausible bear trough in the high-$30s where the stock traded in late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Concrete de-risking of the Argentine pipeline — EPC contract awarded with committed financing for the Vaca Muerta-to-San Matías line, or MKII asset-level financing closed on favorable terms.
- A defined, value-accretive strategic-review outcome (sale/merger at a premium, or a structure that crystallizes backlog value).
- A pullback toward the low-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, where the backlog optionality is no longer fully priced and EV/contracted-EBITDA offers clearer asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material slippage or financing failure on the SESA pipeline, or any signal that SESA cannot meet its feed-gas/commercial commitments.
- A sustained LNG price decline below SESA's cash break-even that turns merchant exposure into a recurring drag.
- MKII or Hilli conversion schedule/cost overruns that push the 2027-2028 ramp meaningfully later, or a leverage deterioration as financing is layered on.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 results (and management commentary on the strategic review and fourth-FLNG order), and any 6-K disclosure of pipeline EPC/financing milestones or a strategic-review resolution. The core 2027-2028 ramp is the multi-year horizon over which the thesis is ultimately proven.

## Conclusion

Golar is a unique, well-positioned FLNG-as-a-service operator with a genuine moat, an inflecting earnings profile, and a contracted 20-year backlog that would roughly triple current EBITDA on conversion. The competitive position and growth durability are strong, and the Goldman-led strategic review is a live catalyst that could crystallize value over the next 6-12 months. These factors keep us constructive on the business.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the entire upside hinges on Argentine sovereign, currency, and pipeline-construction execution over a 20-year horizon — risk that is concentrated, hard to handicap, and not yet de-risked at the infrastructure level. At ~$50 the stock has retraced from its highs but still trades at a rich GAAP multiple with the backlog optionality substantially priced, and the newly-introduced merchant commodity exposure adds two-sided variance. We see no asymmetric entry at current levels. We would move to BUY on concrete Argentine pipeline financing/construction progress, a defined strategic-review outcome, or a pullback toward the low-$40s, and would move to AVOID on evidence the Argentine infrastructure or financing is cracking. This is a growth-realization hold predicated on a specific 2027-2028 catalyst path, not an income or value call.
