## Executive Summary

Donaldson Company is a well-established global leader in filtration technology with approximately $3.7B in annual revenue, a dominant aftermarket replacement business generating ~$1.8B in recurring-type revenue, and a strong balance sheet that has supported aggressive capital returns. The company's competitive positioning remains solid, anchored by its OEM relationships (including a 39-year Caterpillar JV), 3,100+ patents, and global manufacturing scale across 77 facilities.

However, the investment case faces meaningful near-term uncertainty. H1 FY2026 data reveals underlying volume declines for the first time in this cycle — organic revenue growth was entirely pricing/FX driven. Industrial Solutions segment margins have collapsed 400+ basis points over two years due to manufacturing transition disruptions. The pending $820M Facet acquisition will approximately double leverage at a time when execution is already strained. The most recent quarter delivered a 7.5% EPS miss, breaking what had been a consistent beat/inline track record. With the stock trading at ~27x trailing earnings (a premium multiple for a low-single-digit grower), there is insufficient margin of safety to warrant a BUY, but the aftermarket franchise's durability and the potential for margin recovery prevent a bearish conclusion.

## Company Overview

Donaldson (founded 1915, headquartered in Minneapolis) operates through three segments: Mobile Solutions (62% of sales), Industrial Solutions (30%), and Life Sciences (8%). The business model's core strength is its aftermarket exposure — 79% of the Mobile Solutions segment ($1.82B) consists of replacement filters sold through independent distributors and OEM dealer networks. This creates a stable, recurring revenue base tied to the installed equipment population rather than new equipment sales cycles.

Geographic diversification is meaningful: 44% U.S./Canada, 28% EMEA, 17% APAC, 11% LATAM. The company employs approximately 15,000 people across 150+ locations globally.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue reached $3.69B (+2.9% YoY), with H1 FY2026 at $1.83B (+3.5% YoY). However, the quality of growth has deteriorated significantly. In H1 FY2026, volume actually declined $10M — all reported growth was from pricing ($32M) and favorable FX ($40M). This is the first period of negative organic volume in the current cycle and represents a structural concern if pricing power fades.

**Margins:** Gross margins have compressed sequentially — 35.5% (FY2024) → 34.8% (FY2025) → 34.3% (H1 FY2026). The Industrial Solutions segment has experienced the worst deterioration, with pre-tax margins falling from 18.6% (FY2024) to 12.2% (H1 FY2026), attributed to operational inefficiencies during a manufacturing relocation for Power Generation products.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $419M (down from $493M in FY2024), with FCF of $340M (9.2% margin). The net cash cycle has deteriorated from 76 days to 95 days over 18 months, suggesting working capital management is slipping. H1 FY2026 OCF was $158M vs. $163M prior year.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt increased 40% in 18 months to $674M (Jan 2026) from $483M (Jul 2024). Debt-to-equity sits at 1.05x. The Altman Z-Score of 3.33 indicates no near-term solvency risk. With the $820M Facet acquisition pending (expected mid-2026), pro-forma leverage could approach 2.0-2.5x EBITDA. The company proactively upsized its revolver to $600M and added a $200M term loan in anticipation.

**Capital Returns:** Management returned $463M in FY2025 ($332M buybacks + $132M dividends, exceeding $340M FCF), reducing shares outstanding by ~5% over two years. This aggressive buyback program, while accretive, was partially debt-funded and precedes the company's largest-ever acquisition — a capital allocation sequence that introduces timing risk.

## Growth Analysis

Donaldson's organic growth trajectory is low-single-digit, driven primarily by pricing rather than volume. Estimate revisions reflect this: current-year EPS consensus has declined 1.7% over 90 days (from $4.05 to $3.98), and next-year estimates are down 1.4% (from $4.43 to $4.37). There is no meaningful positive revision momentum.

Growth catalysts exist but carry execution uncertainty:
- **Facet acquisition** ($820M): Adds Aerospace & Defense and Power Generation filtration capabilities. Both end markets have secular tailwinds (defense spending, data center power demand). But integration during an already-strained manufacturing transition adds operational risk.
- **Life Sciences recovery**: The segment turned profitable ($14.7M in H1 FY2026 vs. losses prior year), driven by Disk Drive and Food & Beverage demand. However, the bioprocessing thesis — which drove $100M+ in acquisitions — has largely failed (reflected in $62M of impairments).
- **Medica S.p.A.** (49% stake with option on remaining 51%): Medical/water membrane filtration represents a long-term option but is not yet material.

Against these catalysts: On-Road truck production remains weak, agriculture markets are challenged, and the company's backlog has declined from $575M to $552M year-over-year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.94, DCI trades at:
- 25.8x trailing P/E (on $3.05 GAAP EPS, which includes impairment; ~23x adjusted)
- 20.6x forward P/E on current-year consensus of $3.98
- 18.8x forward P/E on next-year consensus of $4.37
- ~28x FCF ($340M FY2025 FCF / $9.5B market cap)
- ~2.6x EV/Sales

Versus peers: the sector median P/E is 31.3x (driven by higher-growth names like SPXC at 38x and Crane at 31x). DCI's 26x multiple is below-median, reflecting its lower growth rate. Flowserve (FLS) at 24.2x with a higher yield (1.4% vs. DCI's 1.5%) is the closest comparable.

The valuation is neither cheap nor expensive for what the business delivers. At low-single-digit growth with modestly declining estimates, 20-21x forward earnings is a reasonable multiple for a quality industrial franchise. The current price offers approximately 17% upside to consensus mean target of $96.40, but this target implicitly assumes successful Facet integration and margin recovery — outcomes that are uncertain.

The stock sits 27% below its February 2026 high of $112.84, reflecting the post-earnings selloff and broader industrial weakness. The decline appears fundamentally justified given the volume deterioration and margin compression, though the magnitude may slightly overstate the impairment to the franchise's long-term earning power.

## Competitive Landscape

Donaldson's competitive position is solid and stable, supported by:
- **Aftermarket lock-in**: Once OEMs specify Donaldson filters for their equipment, the aftermarket replacement stream is largely captive. The 39-year Caterpillar JV exemplifies this dynamic.
- **Technology leadership**: 3,100+ patents, proprietary media technologies (PowerCore, Ultra-Web, Synteq XP) that enable performance claims supporting premium pricing.
- **Global scale**: 77 manufacturing/distribution centers enable local delivery and customization that smaller competitors cannot match.
- **Regulatory tailwinds**: Stricter emissions and air quality standards globally increase filtration content per application.

Competitive threats are gradual rather than acute. In Mobile Solutions, regional aftermarket players compete on price but lack the technology portfolio. In Life Sciences bioprocessing, DCI competes against Danaher/Cytiva and Sartorius — entrenched players with far greater scale, which partly explains the impairment charges on acquired assets. The company appears to have acknowledged this mismatch by writing down Univercells and Solaris.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term risks (12-month horizon):**
1. **Facet integration execution** during an already-strained manufacturing transition. If Industrial Solutions margins continue their decline while absorbing the acquisition, EPS could undershoot consensus by 5-10%.
2. **Volume deterioration acceleration.** If macro conditions worsen, the pricing-only growth model collapses. The backlog decline from $575M to $552M supports this concern.
3. **Leverage spike.** Pro-forma debt/EBITDA of 2.0-2.5x post-Facet is manageable but reduces financial flexibility.
4. **Tariff uncertainty.** While DCI estimates only ~$35M annual impact, the IEEPA Supreme Court ruling creates legal ambiguity around future trade policy that complicates procurement planning.
5. **Earnings miss pattern.** If the next quarter also misses, the stock will face further multiple compression.

**Structural risks:**
- Bioprocessing strategy has largely failed; remaining optionality value is limited
- CEO Carpenter (age 66, in role since 2015) approaching potential succession; COO appointment in Aug 2025 signals transition planning
- Rising input costs with uncertain pass-through ability

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($96-100):** Facet acquisition closes successfully mid-2026, Industrial Solutions margin recovery materializes in H2 FY2026 as manufacturing transition completes, and aftermarket demand remains stable. Forward EPS of ~$4.40 (next year) at a 22-23x multiple yields $97-101 per share. Additional upside from tariff recovery (potential refunds of previously paid tariffs under IEEPA ruling) and Life Sciences acceleration.

**Bear Case ($65-70):** Macro conditions deteriorate into recession, volumes decline further, pricing power fades, Facet integration creates additional margin headwinds, and leverage concerns weigh on the multiple. Adjusted EPS of $3.50-3.70 at an 18-19x multiple yields $63-70 per share. This scenario also carries risk of dividend growth deceleration or buyback suspension to manage post-acquisition leverage.

**Base Case ($88-92):** Successful but slow Facet integration, gradual Industrial Solutions margin recovery (takes 12-18 months rather than 6), low-single-digit organic growth maintained by aftermarket resilience. Current-year EPS of ~$4.00 at 22x = ~$88 per share.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Industrial Solutions pre-tax margins recover above 15% for two consecutive quarters (from 12.2% currently)
- Stock declines to $70-72 range (~18x forward EPS), providing sufficient margin of safety
- Facet acquisition closes and first post-close quarter shows accretive contribution without margin dilution

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of negative organic revenue growth (not just volume — total organic)
- Post-Facet leverage exceeds 3.0x EBITDA with no clear deleveraging timeline
- Another EPS miss of >5% combined with guidance cut

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late May/early June 2026 for Q3 FY2026), plus Facet closing announcement (expected mid-2026). These two events will determine whether the margin recovery and acquisition thesis are tracking.

## Conclusion

Donaldson is a quality industrial franchise trading at a fair multiple for its growth profile. The aftermarket business provides durable cash generation, and the company's global scale and technology leadership support long-term competitive positioning. However, the near-term picture is clouded by declining volumes, material margin compression in the Industrial Solutions segment, a recent earnings miss, and the execution risk of integrating an $820M acquisition while leverage approximately doubles. Consensus estimates are flat-to-declining, and there is no specific non-consensus catalyst that would justify a BUY at current levels. The analyst community's majority HOLD rating (5 of 8 analysts) reflects this balanced positioning accurately.

HOLD with a reassessment following Q3 FY2026 earnings and Facet closing.
