## Executive Summary

Visa's operating results through the June 2026 quarter are strong on any absolute measure. Nine-month net revenue of $33,764M grew 15%, non-GAAP diluted EPS of $9.79 grew 15%, value-added services revenue reached $3.8B in Q3 (+33%) and 32.7% of net revenue versus 27.3% for FY2025, and free cash flow conversion ran at 107.6% of net income in FY2025 on capex of only $1,482M against $40.0B of net revenue. The cash side of the interchange litigation that has weighed on the stock for a decade is largely discharged: roughly $3.0B was paid over nine months, litigation escrow fell to $888M from $2,990M, accrued litigation fell to $1,274M from $3,033M, and the U.S. covered accrual now stands at $822M. The related securities class action was dismissed without leave to amend on June 29, 2026.

The problem is price against a catalyst set that is two-sided at best. At $366.13 the stock is 2.1% below its 52-week high of $373.97, 27.7x current-year consensus of $13.21 and 24.5x next-year consensus of $14.95 — a PEG of roughly 1.9 on the 13.2% EPS growth those two estimates imply. Estimate revisions are positive but small (+1.0% over 90 days for both fiscal years), nowhere near the magnitude that signals an unrecognized inflection. Revenue growth decelerated from approximately 17% in Q2 FY2026 to 14% in Q3, and Q4 EPS growth was guided to the low end of mid-teens. Visa also trades at a premium to Mastercard on both trailing and forward multiples, a reversal of the historical relationship, while Mastercard sits 11% below its own 52-week high.

Three specific unquantified liabilities sit on top of a full multiple. The DOJ's monopolization suit over U.S. debit network services survived a motion to dismiss on June 23, 2025, seeks injunctive relief that the retrospective litigation-responsibility plan does not cover, and carries no accrual or estimable range. The Potayto-Potahto class action filed April 21, 2026 attacks the forward-looking release in the settlement whose final-approval motion was filed July 15, 2026 — and the same judge rejected a prior version of that settlement in June 2024. A February 18, 2026 UK Competition Appeal Tribunal decision found that, outside certain merchant categories, interchange was not passed on by merchants, removing Visa's principal quantum defense in Europe; Visa has sought permission to appeal, and the European preferred-stock loss absorber has shrunk to roughly $1.27B against 100-plus outstanding merchant claims and a hard June 2028 expiry. HOLD, with a base case of $395 (+7.9%) against a bear case of $299 (-18.3%).

## Company Overview

Visa operates a payments network. It does not issue cards, extend credit, set consumer rates, or bear credit risk. Interchange reimbursement fees are paid by acquirers to issuers; merchant discount rates are set by acquirers. Visa's fees are charged to issuers and acquirers and, per the FY2025 10-K, are set independently of interchange. This structural fact governs how to read the 2026 settlement headlines and is developed below.

FY2025 scale: 329 billion payments and cash transactions carried the Visa brand, of which 258 billion (78.4%) were processed on VisaNet, with the remaining 21.6% authorized and cleared on other networks under U.S. debit routing rules and comparable regimes abroad. Total payments and cash volume was $17 trillion across nearly 5 billion credentials, approximately 14,500 financial-institution clients, and 175 million merchant locations. Visa Token Service has provisioned 16 billion tokens. Tap-to-pay is 79% of global face-to-face transactions.

Per the Nilson Report figures reproduced in the 10-K, calendar 2024 payments volume was $13,433B for Visa versus $8,014B for Mastercard, $1,750B for American Express, $319B for JCB and $253B for Diners/Discover — 62.6% of the Visa-plus-Mastercard total and 56.5% of the five networks combined. Visa's card share of the duopoly is 60.4% and its transaction share is 60.4% (311B versus Mastercard's 204B), so Visa's volume share exceeds its transaction share, reflecting a higher average ticket.

The business is organized around Consumer Payments, Commercial & Money Movement Solutions (Visa Direct processed 12.5 billion transactions for 650-plus partners in FY2025), and Value-Added Services (issuing solutions including DPS and Pismo, acceptance via Cybersource and Authorize.net, risk and security including Featurespace, and advisory including Tink). Management's addressable-market figures — $40T+ consumer spend, ~$200T of commercial flows sourced to 2022 studies, a $520B VAS opportunity — are company estimates and should be treated as framing of optionality rather than verified sizing.

Client incentives grew 14.4% against 12.2% gross revenue growth and rose from 27.36% of gross revenue in FY2023 to 27.70% and then 28.25% in FY2025 — roughly 45bp per year of erosion in what Visa retains per dollar of gross fee. This is the cleanest quantitative evidence in the filings that issuer bargaining power is improving at the margin. The counterargument is legitimate: upfront incentive payments are capitalized and amortized, so a heavy renewal year distorts a single period, and incentives may be buying volume that lifts gross revenue later. Three consecutive years in the same direction weakens that alternative without eliminating it.

### Margin

Reported FY2025 operating margin fell to 60.0% from 65.7%, but the litigation provision was $2,562M versus $462M. Excluding provisions, operating income grew 10.4% to $26,556M and margin declined about 60bp to 66.4% on personnel expense +11.1%, G&A +20.5% and D&A +18.0%. The provision has appeared in each of the last three years at $927M / $462M / $2,562M, so treating it as non-operating is a convention rather than an economic fact — it is a recurring cost of the business model at varying magnitude.

Net income was $20,058M (+1.6%); diluted class A GAAP EPS $10.20 (+4.8%, outpacing net income because as-converted share count fell 2.7%). Management's non-GAAP EPS was $11.47, +14%. The effective tax rate was 17%, aided by a $263M discrete benefit and a Singapore incentive worth $453M ($0.23/share) that expires September 30, 2028 — a small, dateable headwind.

Shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow in each of the last two years, funded in part by €3.5 billion ($3,924M) of senior notes issued in May 2025. In the nine months to June 2026, buybacks were $16.5B, cash fell to $12.4B from $17.2B, total equity fell to $35.2B from $37.9B, and the commercial paper program was increased from $3.0B to $7.0B in July 2026 with $1.5B outstanding at June 30 versus zero a year earlier. A new $20B authorization was added in April 2026, leaving $28.4B available — approximately 4.0% of as-converted equity value.

None of this constitutes stress. Net debt at FY2025-end was roughly $5.2B against an operating-income-plus-D&A proxy of $25.2B, all covenants were met, and the $7.0B revolver was undrawn. But the direction is a deliberately levered buyback at a price near all-time highs, and the CP expansion coincides with peak daily settlement exposure rising to $168.6B (average $99.5B) against collateral of $9.5B. The revolver exists explicitly to protect settlement integrity, which is at least as plausible an explanation for the CP capacity as any funding need.

Two screening artifacts deserve dismissal and one deserves attention. Goodwill and intangibles of $47,525M (47.7% of assets, overwhelmingly Visa Europe) leave tangible book equity at approximately negative $9.6B — economically irrelevant for an asset-light network but mechanically punitive in book-value models. The current ratio's fall from 1.283 to 1.078 is driven by $5,569M of notes reclassifying to current maturities and by $3,625M of customer collateral that is exactly offset by a restricted asset; excluding both sides, the ratio is essentially unchanged. What does survive is the two-year pattern of distributions above free cash flow.

A valuation subtlety matters here. The quoted $623.2B market cap divided by $366.13 implies 1,702M shares, approximately Visa's class A count alone. As-converted class A was 1,930M at September 2025 and roughly 1,880M at June 2026, giving equity value of approximately $687B — about 10% above the quoted figure. Any EV/EBITDA or price/sales screen built on the quoted cap makes Visa look 10% cheaper than it is. Per-share P/E is unaffected, since diluted EPS already uses the as-converted denominator.

## Growth Analysis

Q3 FY2026 net revenue was $11,633M, +14%, with nine-month revenue +15%. Non-GAAP EPS was $3.32, +11% for the quarter and +15% for nine months. Against the surprise history, this was a 0.8% beat on the $3.29 consensus in the data provided (the research brief cites a lower ~$3.23 consensus, which would make it a larger beat). The four-quarter record is two beats and two misses — for a company that historically beat consistently, that is a modest deterioration in forecast visibility, and it argues for taking consensus at face value rather than assuming a beat cushion.

Three growth vectors are working. Value-added services grew 33% in Q3 and 32% over nine months, versus 24% in FY2025 — genuine acceleration, and now nearly a third of net revenue. Commercial and money-movement flows are compounding off Visa Direct's 12.5 billion FY2025 transactions. International transaction revenue, at $14,166M or 35.4% of FY2025 net revenue, is the highest-yield line and benefits from cross-border travel normalization and premium credential growth.

Three qualifications matter as much. First, deceleration: growth moved from roughly 17% in Q2 FY2026 to 14% in Q3, and Q4 EPS growth was guided (externally reported, non-GAAP) to the low end of mid-teens. FIFA World Cup 2026 marketing spend is concentrated in the June and September 2026 quarters, which pressures Q4 margins. Second, the incentive drag compounds against the top line. Third, remaining performance obligations of $5.6B should not be read as backlog — Visa elected the optional exemption not to disclose RPO for payments network services, so the figure captures primarily VAS contracts against a roughly $45B annualized revenue run rate. It is a partial visibility metric.

Estimate revisions are constructive but unremarkable: current year $13.21 (+1.0% over 90 days), next year $14.95 (+1.0%), current quarter marginally negative at -0.3%. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a market systematically underestimating an inflection. It is a well-modeled compounder delivering close to plan.

Arithmetic on the full year: nine-month net revenue of $33,764M plus a Q4 in line with Q3 implies roughly $45.4B, about +13.5% for FY2026. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $13.21 implies Q4 of approximately $3.42 against the $3.44 current-quarter estimate — internally consistent.

## Valuation Assessment

Start by reconciling the conflicting inputs. The profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 31.17, which cannot both hold: $366.13 ÷ trailing GAAP EPS of $10.20 is 35.9x, while $366.13 ÷ trailing non-GAAP EPS of $11.47 is 31.9x. The 31.17 figure approximates the trailing non-GAAP basis. On the consensus estimates provided:

- FY2026E $13.21 → **27.7x**
- FY2027E $14.95 → **24.5x**
- Implied FY26→FY27 EPS growth 13.2% → **PEG ≈ 1.86**
- Dividend $2.68 annualized → **0.73% yield**
- Buyback capacity $28.4B ≈ 4.0% of as-converted equity value

Peer context requires care. The stated peer median P/E of 21.0 is distorted by PayPal at 7.7x and Fiserv at 9.0x, both structurally impaired (Fiserv trades 70% below its 52-week high). The only true comparable is Mastercard at 28.1x trailing, 11% below its own 52-week high. On a forward basis Visa's 27.7x sits at or slightly above Mastercard's — a reversal of the historical relationship in which Mastercard's faster growth commanded the premium. Visa is 2.1% from its high; Mastercard is 11% from its. Whatever the relative merits, Visa is not the cheaper way to own the duopoly today.

A 25-28x forward multiple on a 13-15% grower is a long-duration cash-flow claim, and discount-rate pressure is the mechanism most likely to cap the multiple even if earnings deliver. [unverified — no macro rate data in sources]

The counterweight is that consensus targets sit well above spot: mean $413.45, median $420, high $450, low $330. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates; at 27.7x forward with +1% revisions, that condition is not met here. The targets are more plausibly read as the same 26-28x multiple applied to the same estimates, offering no independent information.

Setting the base case: 26.4x FY2027 consensus of $14.95 gives **$395**, +7.9% from $366.13. That multiple assumes the settlement receives final approval without a materially adverse revision, the DOJ matter does not produce a remedy within the window, and revenue growth settles in the low teens. The bull case applies 30x to $14.95 for **$449** (+22.6%), requiring VAS growth to hold above 30%, incentives to stabilize as a share of gross revenue, and the DOJ matter to resolve on terms that do not touch debit routing economics. The bear case applies 20x to $14.95 for **$299** (-18.3%) — a de-rating consistent with an adverse DOJ remedy, a settlement release unwound by the Potayto-Potahto action, or a step-down in premium-card economics from honor-all-cards relaxation.

Base-case upside of 7.9% against bear-case downside of 18.3% is not an asymmetry that justifies new capital. A price near $320-330 (21.4-22.1x FY2027) would restore it.

## Competitive Landscape

The duopoly is stable and neither party has incentive to compete on price. Visa's moat combines 4.8 billion credentials, 175 million merchant locations, 16 billion provisioned tokens, and regulatory embeddedness as a designated payment system in multiple jurisdictions — which raises entry barriers while inviting intervention.

The competitive threat is volume that never touches card rails. The 10-K names real-time payment networks in 80-plus countries with government sponsorship (FedNow, PIX, UPI), and states directly that cross-border RTP linkages such as PayNow-UPI "will compete with our cross-border business" — the 35.4% of net revenue that carries Visa's highest yield. Stablecoins appear as both opportunity and competition, with the 10-K noting industry experimentation in B2B blockchain payments that "can operate globally 24/7." Digital wallets can be funded from bank accounts rather than cards. European processors may bypass VisaNet under the IFR.

Visa's response is a two-track hedge: capture A2A flows through card-enabled rails (Visa Pay, Visa A2A in the UK, Tink) and sell infrastructure to the rails themselves (Visa Protect for A2A Payments, described as its first fraud solution built for real-time non-card payments). The 10-K states that RTP networks "can also be customers for value-added services." VAS growth of 32% over nine months versus 24% in FY2025 is the best available evidence the hedge is gaining traction, though Visa does not disclose how much VAS revenue derives from non-Visa-network volume, so the hedge's independence from the core cannot be verified.

Stablecoin exposure is positional rather than material: settlement volume ran at a $2.5B annualized rate as of September 2025, roughly 0.015% of Visa's $17 trillion. The asymmetry is that stablecoins' clearest cost advantage lies in cross-border and B2B corridors, exactly where Visa's yield is highest.

Agentic commerce cuts favorably. Visa Intelligent Commerce, the Trusted Agent Protocol, an MCP server exposing Visa APIs, and Payment Passkey position tokenization and dispute resolution — incumbent strengths — as the trust layer for AI-initiated purchases. This is the most credible upside optionality in the filings and, unlike the TAM estimates, is grounded in shipped product.

On trajectory, which matters more than level: the incentive line is deteriorating about 45bp per year, only 78.4% of branded transactions are processed on VisaNet, and one client has been 11% of net revenue in each of the last three fiscal years — mix diversification in VAS and geography does not reduce that dependence. Against that, VAS is accelerating and the agentic-commerce position is real. The net read is a dominant franchise with mildly eroding economics and a credible but unverified hedge.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ monopolization suit (most consequential, unquantified).** Filed September 24, 2024 in S.D.N.Y., alleging monopolization of general-purpose debit and card-not-present debit network services through agreements with merchants and acquirers. The motion to dismiss was denied June 23, 2025. The relief sought is injunctive — conduct or structural remedies affecting the agreements that keep debit volume on VisaNet — and the 10-K states plainly that the litigation-responsibility plan addresses monetary liability but that judgments requiring changes to business practices, rules or contractual commitments "could adversely affect the Company's financial results." No accrual, no estimable range. The transmission channel is data processing revenue, the fastest-growing core line at $19,993M and +12.9% in FY2025. Consolidated U.S. Debit Class Actions had motions granted in part and denied in part on October 29, 2025, with amended complaints filed February 27, 2026. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q reports no development on the DOJ matter, which is consistent with no reportable development rather than resolution; no trial date is confirmed.

**Settlement durability and rule risk.** Preliminary approval of the amended injunctive-relief class settlement was granted June 9, 2026; the final-approval motion was filed July 15, 2026. Headline values reported externally range from $38B (Reuters) to $200B (Bloomberg) against a rejected $30B prior proposal — a spread wide enough to render secondary coverage unreliable on magnitude. Those figures estimate merchant savings from future interchange reductions, funded principally by issuers, not cash from Visa. Visa's genuine exposure is the reported relaxation of the honor-all-cards rule: if merchants can decline specific high-interchange categories, the premium/affluent strategy the 10-K identifies as generating above-average revenue per cardholder in cross-border, credit and e-commerce is directly impaired. Lower interchange also reduces issuer rewards funding, which over time increases issuer leverage on the incentive line. The Potayto-Potahto class action filed April 21, 2026 attacks the forward-looking release, and Judge Cogan rejected the prior injunctive-relief settlement in June 2024, so final approval should be treated as probable rather than assured. The settlement terms themselves were not read for this analysis.

**Europe.** The February 18, 2026 CAT decision finding interchange was not passed on by merchants outside certain categories removes the principal quantum defense; Visa has sought permission to appeal. The Court of Appeal granted permission on March 17, 2026 to appeal the June 25, 2025 CAT finding on rate restrictions. A December 19, 2024 Court of Appeal ruling limiting damages to six years before filing caps the long tail. Over 1,150 European merchant claims have been filed since 2013, 950-plus settled, 100-plus outstanding, with new Dutch (damages from 1992), Swiss, Israeli and German actions live and the company anticipating more. The offsetting mechanism — reductions in the series B and C preferred conversion rate, which shift losses to former member banks via share-count reduction rather than to class A earnings — has thinned to approximately $1.27B of available recovery from $2,234M a year earlier, mostly because of the scheduled $1.4B Ninth Anniversary Release in August 2025. Coverage is capped at 70% for certain inter-regional claims, excludes competition-law fines, and the preferred becomes fully convertible on the twelfth anniversary of the Visa Europe closing in June 2028.

**Other.** An EC preliminary investigation into acquirer fees opened August 30, 2024 (no accrual). A new Brazilian requirement extends Visa's settlement guarantees to sellers, increasing exposure without offsetting revenue — a template worth watching if other jurisdictions follow. Prisma/Newpay ($1.5B, February 2026) remains subject to Argentine competition review and Prosa has been pending approximately two years. The May 2026 class B exchange converted roughly 92M as-converted class A equivalent into class C, which carries no transfer restrictions — about 4.9% of the as-converted base becoming saleable, though the larger May 2024 exchange produced no evident lasting price impact.

**Partial de-risking to acknowledge.** The related securities class action was dismissed without leave to amend on June 29, 2026, which resolves the disclosure-fraud overhang and, because the three derivative actions were stayed pending it, likely those as well. Cash litigation exposure is down sharply, with settlements covering merchants representing approximately 95% of opt-out payment card sales volume.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is neutral and offers no confirmation in either direction. Near-term ATM IV of 24.6% against 21.8% one-year historical volatility is a 2.8-point spread — fair, not rich. The 40th-percentile IV reading within the current chain and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.83 with balanced 2.0% skew indicate no accumulation of downside protection. The headline backwardation (24.6% near versus 21.8% long-dated) is an artifact of a three-day expiry: the one-month tenor prices 21.6% and the three-month 20.5%, both at or below realized volatility, so the term structure prices no imminent stress once same-day mechanics are stripped out. IV fell 1.8 points intraday on a 1.0% price decline, consistent with post-earnings decay rather than a developing catalyst. Short interest of 1.4% of float and 2.4 days to cover confirms no bearish crowding. The single unusual print — 518 contracts of the September 4 $275 put at 4.3x open interest, 24.8% out of the money — is a small, cheap tail hedge and does not carry thesis weight. The three-month put/call volume ratio of 2.82 alongside a six-month ratio of 0.24 suggests some near-dated hedging around the final-approval and Q4 print window, but the volumes involved (881 contracts across 11 expirations) are too thin to interpret.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($449, +22.6%).** VAS sustains 30%-plus growth and passes 35% of net revenue, making the A2A hedge visibly self-funding. The interchange settlement receives final approval with honor-all-cards mechanics narrower than feared, removing a decade-old overhang and allowing the multiple to expand toward 30x. The DOJ matter settles on conduct terms that leave debit routing economics intact. Client incentives stabilize near 28% of gross revenue as the FY2025-26 renewal cycle passes. The $563M severance action resets the cost base, restoring operating leverage against low-teens revenue growth, and $28.4B of buyback capacity retires roughly 4% of the as-converted base annually. Agentic commerce converts tokenization and dispute infrastructure into a new fee pool. On 30x FY2027 consensus of $14.95, $449.

**Bear case ($299, -18.3%).** The DOJ obtains a remedy loosening merchant and acquirer agreements, and data processing revenue growth — currently the fastest core line at +12.9% — steps down toward mid-single digits. Honor-all-cards relaxation causes large merchants to decline premium categories, compressing the affluent/cross-border yield engine that carries 35.4% of net revenue. The Potayto-Potahto action or objector appeals delay or unwind the forward-looking release, restarting the merchant claims cycle. European losses exhaust the roughly $1.27B preferred cushion, after which interchange judgments hit the income statement directly, and the mechanism expires in June 2028 regardless. Incentives continue eroding 45bp per year while RTP substitution accelerates in cross-border corridors. On 20x FY2027 consensus of $14.95, $299.

**Base case ($395, +7.9%).** Revenue growth settles at 12-14%, EPS compounds 13-15%, VAS decelerates from 32% toward the mid-20s, incentives keep drifting up modestly, the settlement obtains final approval on roughly the reported terms with a modest drag on premium credential growth appearing in FY2028 rather than FY2027, and the DOJ matter remains unresolved through the window. 26.4x FY2027 consensus of $14.95.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth thesis intact, entry unattractive). Reassessment: Q4 FY2026 print, late October 2026.**

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted from 7.2 in May to 6.1 now. The current data confirms most of that drift rather than reversing it: the incentive ratio has deteriorated for three straight years, there are still no open-market insider purchases, the DOJ matter remains unquantified, and the February 2026 UK pass-on ruling went against Visa. Two items argue the other way — the securities class action dismissal and the near-complete discharge of the interchange cash liability — and they are why the score has stabilized rather than continued falling at the prior pace. What has not changed is that the stock sits 2.1% below its high at 24.5x next-year earnings for 13% growth, which is the reason the recommendation stays HOLD rather than becoming a BUY.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price at or below $320-330 (21.4-22.1x FY2027 consensus of $14.95) with FY2027 estimates holding at or above $14.80, restoring base-case upside above 20%.
2. Final approval of the injunctive-relief settlement entered with the forward-looking release intact and the Potayto-Potahto challenge dismissed or resolved, plus disclosure of honor-all-cards mechanics that leave premium category acceptance materially unchanged.
3. Client incentives flat or lower as a share of gross revenue for two consecutive quarters, confirming the take-rate erosion has stopped, alongside VAS growth sustained above 30%.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A DOJ remedy — by ruling or consent — that alters merchant or acquirer agreements governing debit routing, or the disclosure of an accrual or estimable range for that matter.
2. Net revenue growth decelerating below 10% year over year for two consecutive quarters, or FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $14.00.
3. Client incentives exceeding 29.5% of gross revenue, or evidence that a major issuer renewal was won at materially worse economics.
4. Final approval of the settlement denied or vacated on appeal, restarting the merchant litigation cycle with the European preferred cushion below $1.0B.

**What to check at the October print:** Q4 net revenue growth against the 14% Q3 rate and the guided low-end-of-mid-teens EPS growth; the FY2025 full-year incentive ratio; VAS as a share of net revenue against Q3's 32.7%; commercial paper outstanding against the expanded $7.0B program; disclosure of Prisma/Newpay and Prosa closing status; any update on the DOJ docket; and initial FY2027 guidance against the $14.95 consensus.

## Conclusion

Visa is a high-quality asset at a price that offers no margin of safety. The franchise delivers 15% revenue growth, 66% underlying operating margins, 107% free cash flow conversion and strong returns on equity with $5.2B of net debt, and the value-added services segment is accelerating into the structural threat rather than retreating from it. Those attributes justify a premium multiple. They do not justify paying 27.7x this year and 24.5x next year, 2% below the 52-week high and at a premium to Mastercard, when the identified catalysts over the next twelve months are an unquantified DOJ injunctive-relief case, a settlement release under active legal attack, an adverse European pass-on ruling under appeal, and a loss-absorption mechanism that expires in June 2028.

Base-case upside of 7.9% against bear-case downside of 18.3% argues for holding existing positions through the compounding and waiting on new capital. The stock becomes interesting again in the low $320s, or on confirmation that the settlement release survives intact and the incentive line has stopped eroding. Overall score 6.1; HOLD.
