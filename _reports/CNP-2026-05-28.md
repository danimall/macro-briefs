**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

CenterPoint Energy is a regulated utility executing an aggressive capital deployment strategy, pivoting from gas distribution toward electric transmission and distribution in Houston — one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the U.S. The company's load growth story is genuinely differentiated: 12.2 GW of firmly committed large industrial load (primarily data centers), a 50% projected peak load increase by 2029, and customer growth of ~2% annually in its Houston Electric franchise. Management has delivered consistent 8-9% non-GAAP EPS growth and is executing a smart capital recycling strategy (selling gas LDCs at premium valuations to fund electric capex).

However, the stock now trades at 26.4x trailing earnings — a 29% premium to the multi-utility peer median of 20.5x — while carrying structural negative free cash flow of -$2.4B annually, a Moody's negative outlook on the parent entity, and a $65.5B 10-year capital plan requiring $4B+ in equity issuance and continuous debt market access. The growth thesis is real, but the current valuation already prices in successful execution with limited margin for error.

## Company Overview

CenterPoint Energy serves over 7 million metered customers across four states through two primary segments: Electric (Houston Electric T&D within ERCOT; Indiana electric T&D and generation within MISO) and Natural Gas (distribution in Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio, and Texas). The company is actively reshaping its portfolio — selling the Ohio gas LDC for $2.62B and having already divested Louisiana/Mississippi gas operations — to concentrate capital in its high-growth Houston Electric franchise.

Houston Electric serves approximately 3.0 million metered customers in the nation's 4th-largest city, benefiting from diversified economic drivers: energy/petrochemicals, healthcare, logistics, advanced manufacturing, and an accelerating data center buildout. The company's 10-year capital plan totals $65.5B (2026-2035), with $22.4B allocated to electric assets in 2026-2030 alone.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
Non-GAAP EPS has grown from $1.38 (2022) to $1.76 (2025), representing consistent 8-9% annual growth. Q1 2026 delivered $0.56 non-GAAP EPS (+5.7% YoY). Full-year 2026 guidance is $1.89-$1.91, implying 7.4-8.5% growth. At the current price of $42.75, trailing P/E is 26.4x against $1.62 in reported EPS (2024 non-GAAP basis) or approximately 22.5x on 2026 non-GAAP guidance midpoint of $1.90.

**Cash Flow Reality:**
Operating cash flow was $2.49B in 2025 against $4.9B in capital expenditure, producing negative free cash flow of -$2.4B. This deficit is projected to widen to approximately -$4.0B in 2026 as capex ramps to $6.7-6.8B. The structural FCF deficit will persist through at least 2030, requiring continuous external financing.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt stands at approximately $22-23B (excluding securitization bonds), with debt-to-equity of 4.17x. While high leverage is standard for regulated utilities, Moody's has placed a negative outlook on both the parent (Baa2) and Houston Electric (A2), citing execution risk on the capital plan and leverage trajectory. FFO/Debt sits at 12.2-12.5%, needing improvement toward the 14-15% target.

**Financing Strategy:**
Management is funding the gap through: (1) asset recycling — the Ohio sale nets ~$2.4B; (2) ~$4B of common equity through 2035 (including a new $1B ATM program launched May 2026); (3) convertible notes ($1B due 2028); and (4) junior subordinated notes ($700M). The multi-channel approach works in accommodating capital markets but creates vulnerability if any single channel tightens.

**Earnings Surprises:**
The four-quarter track record is mediocre: 1 beat (Q3 2025, +12.6%), 2 in-line results, and 1 miss (Q2 2025, -5.7%). This pattern suggests limited earnings visibility relative to consensus — management isn't consistently sandbagging expectations.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Flat. Current-year EPS estimates unchanged at $1.91 over 30 and 90 days. Next-quarter estimates show upward revision (+4.0% over 30 days, +5.2% over 90 days). This stability is typical for regulated utilities but provides no positive catalyst signal.

## Growth Analysis

CenterPoint's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Houston Load Growth:** The acceleration from 7.5 GW to 12.2 GW of firmly committed large industrial load in a single quarter (Q4 2025 to Q1 2026) is remarkable among U.S. utilities. Management projects peak load growth of 50% by year-end 2029 and near-doubling by mid-2030s. If realized, this supports the 7-9% EPS growth target through 2035.

**2. Capital Recycling:** Selling low-growth gas LDCs at premium valuations (Ohio at ~26x earnings, ~1.9x rate base) to fund electric growth at regulated returns is value-accretive. The spread between exit multiples on gas and deployment returns on electric investments creates embedded value.

**3. Rate Base Growth:** The $65.5B capital plan implies rate base roughly doubling over 10 years, with an additional $10B+ of incremental opportunities identified. Under cost-of-service regulation, rate base growth translates directly to earnings growth (assuming constructive regulatory outcomes).

**Key Growth Risk:** The data center demand thesis has significant uncertainty. Technology companies could delay, cancel, or relocate projects. The 12.2 GW of "firmly committed" load is defined as having signed study commitments, site control, and materials/labor to execute, but this is not the same as fully contracted energy delivery. A slowdown in AI capex — which could result from a semiconductor correction, recession, or technology maturation — would directly impair CNP's capital deployment justification.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.75, CNP trades at:
- 26.4x trailing P/E (GAAP EPS $1.62)
- ~22.5x 2026E non-GAAP EPS ($1.90 midpoint)
- ~20.6x 2027E non-GAAP EPS ($2.08 consensus)
- Dividend yield: 2.1% (vs. peer median 2.7%)

**Peer Context:**
The multi-utility peer median P/E is 20.5x (DTE 23.9x, AEE 20.0x, CMS 20.5x, NI 23.5x, PCG 12.8x). CNP's 26.4x trailing multiple represents a 29% premium to peers. Even on 2026 non-GAAP estimates, the 22.5x multiple exceeds all peers except DTE (23.9x) and NI (23.5x), while CNP carries higher growth but also higher leverage and financing risk.

**What justifies the premium?**
CNP's 7-9% EPS growth target is genuinely above the utility sector average of 5-7%. The Houston Electric franchise has unique load growth characteristics. A 2-3x P/E premium relative to slower-growing peers (AEE, CMS) is reasonable for a utility growing 2-3pp faster.

**What's already priced in?**
At 22.5x on 2026 estimates, the market is assigning a substantial growth premium. For the stock to appreciate materially from here, either: (a) EPS growth must accelerate beyond the 7-9% guidance (unlikely given the regulated nature of earnings), or (b) the multiple must expand further — which requires evidence that the growth is even more durable than currently expected, or a significant decline in interest rates.

The 2.1% dividend yield provides minimal income support relative to risk-free alternatives. The total return proposition (2.1% dividend + 7-9% EPS growth = 9-11% theoretical annual return) is reasonable but relies heavily on multiple maintenance at current elevated levels.

**Assessment:** The stock is fairly valued to slightly overvalued relative to the growth it delivers. The premium is justified by superior growth, but the premium itself creates asymmetric risk — any execution stumble or data center demand disappointment would compress the multiple toward peer averages (20-22x), implying 8-12% downside from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Houston Electric occupies one of the strongest franchise positions in the U.S. utility sector. Key competitive advantages:

**Territorial monopoly in a high-growth market:** Houston's diversified economy (energy, healthcare, logistics, manufacturing) provides multiple demand drivers beyond any single sector. Residential meter growth of ~2% annually is roughly 2x the national utility average.

**Data center positioning:** Texas's deregulated retail energy market (ERCOT) combined with Houston's existing transmission infrastructure, land availability, and proximity to Gulf Coast generation creates a favorable environment for large industrial loads.

**Regulatory position improving:** ~85% of rate base has been through rate cases since 2023. New Texas legislation (SB 1963, HB 4384) provides additional cost recovery mechanisms. Large industrial load sharing fixed costs lowers bills for residential customers, providing political cover for continued capital deployment.

**Competitive threats are limited** given the regulated monopoly structure. The primary competitive risk is that data center operators choose other ERCOT regions (DFW, West Texas) or other states entirely, reducing CNP's load growth below projections.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Financing Risk (HIGH):** $6.7B+ annual capex in an elevated interest rate environment creates meaningful cost-of-capital pressure. A Moody's downgrade from Baa2 to Baa3 would increase borrowing costs across the debt stack. The current FFO/Debt of 12.2-12.5% has limited buffer above downgrade thresholds.

2. **Data Center Load Execution (HIGH):** "Firmly committed" does not equal "energized." The 12.2 GW pipeline could face delays, cancellations, or scope reductions. An AI spending slowdown or semiconductor correction could pause data center construction decisions. If even 30% of committed load slips by 2+ years, the capital plan needs revision.

3. **Regulatory/Affordability (MEDIUM):** Indiana already cancelled ~$1B in renewable projects due to affordability concerns. Texas HB 1002 imposes affordability metrics with financial penalties. While Houston Electric's delivery charges have grown below inflation (1.1% CAGR vs. 2.8% CPI), political pressure could intensify if total customer bills rise materially.

4. **Storm Exposure (MEDIUM):** Gulf Coast location creates recurring hurricane risk. Hurricane Beryl (2024) cost recovery (~$800M) remains partially unresolved. The TEEEF controversy damaged regulatory goodwill.

5. **Interest Rate Sensitivity (MEDIUM-HIGH):** CNP's 2.1% dividend yield offers minimal carry vs. risk-free alternatives. A significant move higher in long-term rates would likely compress utility P/E multiples by 1-2 turns.

### Options Market Signal

Options term structure shows **backwardation** — near-term IV (20.7%) exceeds longer-term IV (18.6%), pricing imminent stress or uncertainty. Near-term put/call volume ratio is extremely elevated at 14.0x for the June expiry, suggesting concentrated hedging activity around the current price level. However, OI P/C ratio at 0.13 indicates structurally call-dominated positioning (long-term holders). The 5.0pp spread between IV (20.7%) and historical vol (15.7%) means options are relatively rich, pricing in elevated uncertainty. This divergence warrants monitoring — while not alarming for a utility, it suggests institutional hedging of near-term downside around current levels.

Short interest at 7.2% of float with 7.0 days to cover is moderately elevated for a utility (typical range 2-5%), suggesting some informed capital is positioned for downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($47-50, 10-17% upside):**
- Data center load growth accelerates further; 12.2 GW proves to be a floor, not a ceiling
- Successful Ohio sale completion (Q4 2026) provides capital flexibility and demonstrates portfolio optimization
- Interest rates decline, reducing cost of capital and expanding utility multiples
- EPS growth at high end of 7-9% range; 2027 estimates revised upward toward $2.15+
- Multiple maintained at 22-24x as growth utility premium solidifies

**Bear Case ($36-38, 11-16% downside):**
- Data center buildout slows amid semiconductor correction or AI spending pullback; committed load delays 2+ years
- Moody's downgrades parent to Baa3; financing costs rise, equity issuance becomes dilutive at lower prices
- Long-term Treasury rates move materially higher; utility sector P/E compresses 2-3 turns
- Regulatory pushback on customer affordability limits rate case outcomes in Indiana and Texas
- Multiple compresses toward peer average (20-21x on 2026E) = $38-40 fair value

## Conclusion

CenterPoint Energy has a genuine growth advantage within the utility sector — the Houston franchise, data center demand pipeline, and capital recycling strategy are all legitimate differentiators. However, at 26.4x trailing earnings (22.5x 2026E), the stock already prices in successful execution of a $65.5B capital plan that requires continuous market access in an elevated rate environment. The Moody's negative outlook, structural -$2.4B annual FCF, and mediocre earnings beat record suggest limited near-term catalyst potential.

The risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. Downside to peer-average valuation (20.5x) is $38-40, while upside to analyst consensus mean of $46 represents approximately 8% appreciation plus a 2.1% dividend. CNP is a growth-oriented HOLD — worth maintaining for investors who believe in the Houston data center thesis and can tolerate the financing risk, but not offering sufficient asymmetry for new capital deployment at current prices.
