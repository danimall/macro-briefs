**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

CenterPoint Energy is a regulated utility with an unusually compelling demand growth story: 12.2 GW of firmly committed industrial load at Houston Electric, with 8 GW of data center capacity expected energized by 2029 and 3.5 GW already under construction. This is one of the most concrete AI/data center beneficiary stories in the regulated utility universe, and the market is pricing it accordingly — CNP trades at 26.4x trailing earnings versus a multi-utility peer median of 20.5x.

The offsetting reality is that this growth requires a $65.5 billion, 10-year capital plan funded largely by external financing. With debt-to-equity already at 4.17x, free cash flow at -$2.4 billion annually, Moody's on negative outlook, and the 30-year Treasury yield at [unverified] levels, the cost of executing this plan is rising materially. Annual interest expense at Houston Electric alone grew 47% over the past two years. The company's growth trajectory is real but its financing vulnerability is acute and worsening in the current rate environment.

The stock has appreciated 14% over the past year to $42.78, approaching the 52-week high of $44.47. At ~22.5x forward FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $1.90, the premium over peers reflects the load growth thesis but offers limited cushion against capital plan execution missteps, regulatory pushback on the 765-kV transmission project, or sustained elevated borrowing costs.

## Company Overview

CenterPoint Energy serves over 7 million metered customers across Texas, Indiana, Minnesota, and Ohio through two segments: Electric (transmission and distribution in the Houston/ERCOT region and Indiana/MISO) and Natural Gas (distribution across four states). The company is headquartered in Houston and is the only investor-owned electric and gas utility based in Texas.

The strategic narrative has shifted materially over the past two years. CenterPoint is actively shedding slower-growth natural gas distribution assets (Louisiana/Mississippi sold March 2025; Ohio sale for $2.62B expected Q4 2026) to concentrate capital in its highest-growth service territory — Houston Electric — where data center and industrial load growth is driving a forecasted near-doubling of peak demand by the mid-2030s.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew from $8.64B (2024) to $9.36B (2025). Operating income expanded to $2.11B (22.5% margin). Non-GAAP EPS has compounded at ~9% annually: $1.38 (2022) → $1.50 (2023) → $1.62 (2024) → $1.76 (2025). Management guides $1.89-$1.91 for FY2026 (midpoint $1.90, 8% growth). Q1 2026 delivered $0.56 non-GAAP EPS versus $0.53 prior year.

**Balance Sheet Leverage:** Total debt of ~$21.8B against $11.2B of stockholders' equity produces a 4.17x debt-to-equity ratio — elevated even by utility standards. Cash on hand is negligible at $38M. The current ratio of 0.91 indicates short-term liquidity tightness. Moody's Baa2 rating carries a negative outlook specifically tied to the leverage trajectory required by the capital plan.

**Free Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $2.49B against capex of $4.87B produces free cash flow of -$2.38B. This deficit is structural and worsening — 2026 planned capex rises to $6.7B, creating an ~$4-5B annual external financing need. The company issued $3.7B in new debt in 2025, $1.0B in convertible notes (adding dilution optionality), and utilizes an ATM equity program.

**Interest Expense Growth:** Houston Electric's interest burden jumped from $303M (2023) to $445M (2025), a 47% increase in two years. With ~$4-5B in new annual borrowing needed, this trajectory accelerates further in 2026-2027.

**Capital Recycling:** The Ohio LDC sale for $2.62B provides meaningful one-time deleveraging capacity, but at 40% of a single year's capex, it buys time rather than resolves the structural funding gap.

## Growth Analysis

**The Data Center Load Growth Story:** Houston Electric has 12.2 GW of firmly committed industrial load, expects to energize 8 GW of data center capacity by 2029, with 3.5 GW already under construction. Management forecasts peak demand growing to over 30 GW by 2029 (a nearly 50% increase), nearly doubling by mid-2030s versus 2024. This is among the most aggressive load growth profiles in the regulated utility sector.

**Visibility is High but Not Certain:** "Firmly committed" involves signed agreements and interconnection studies, but doesn't mean "energized." The 10-K explicitly acknowledges "significant uncertainty" and the possibility that large load customers may "delay or cancel their planned projects." If data center investment cycles slow (a plausible scenario if AI monetization disappoints), some portion of this committed load may not materialize on schedule.

**Rate Base Growth:** Management targets 10%+ rate base growth annually, funded by the $65.5B ten-year plan. If executed, this mechanical growth in the utility rate base should support the 8% non-GAAP EPS CAGR target through at least 2030.

**Customer Growth:** Houston Electric adds ~2% customers annually through organic population growth, providing a durable base growth rate independent of the data center thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

CenterPoint's competitive position is strong and improving along the dimension that matters most: service territory demand growth. Few regulated utilities globally have a comparable pipeline of committed large industrial loads. The Houston metropolitan area's combination of abundant land, competitive energy costs, natural gas generation access, and grid interconnection points makes it structurally attractive for hyperscale data center development.

The company's regulatory toolkit is favorable: Texas offers multiple interim recovery mechanisms (DCRF, TCOS, GRIP) that reduce traditional regulatory lag. ~85% of projected capital is expected to flow through trackers or forward test year rate cases.

However, the competitive moat faces an emerging challenge: the 765-kV transmission project required to serve this load growth is encountering political opposition from Texas lawmakers and rural communities. If this project is delayed or scaled back, the physical ability to deliver power to committed loads could become the binding constraint, regardless of demand.

## Risk Assessment

**Capital Plan Execution (Primary Risk):** At elevated Treasury yields, each $1B borrowed adds substantial annual interest expense. Over the 10-year plan, total incremental interest burden could compress the net earnings growth achievable per dollar of rate base deployed.

**765-kV Transmission Project Political Risk:** Texas lawmakers are actively questioning this project's rationale and cost-effectiveness. Rural opposition and environmental concerns create permitting uncertainty. A material delay or cancellation would undermine the physical infrastructure required to deliver power to committed data center loads.

**Regulatory Affordability Constraint:** Indiana Electric already cancelled ~$1B in renewable projects due to customer affordability concerns. Houston Electric's last rate case resulted in a revenue reduction. As capital spending accelerates and rates remain elevated, customer bills rise, increasing the probability of regulatory pushback that limits earned ROE.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With 4.17x D/E and deeply negative FCF requiring continuous refinancing, CNP is among the most rate-sensitive utilities. Each 25bp rise in long-term rates increases annual financing costs by ~$10-12M on new issuance alone, with cumulative impact over the multi-year plan.

**Load Growth Materialization Risk:** Data center commitments can be deferred. If AI investment cycles moderate or hyperscalers choose alternative locations, some portion of the 12.2 GW pipeline may not energize on schedule. Infrastructure built for deferred load creates stranded cost risk.

**Hurricane Exposure:** Houston Electric lacks insurance on its T&D system. Hurricane Beryl (2024) caused massive damage and ongoing litigation. Recovery relies on securitization bonds and rate proceedings — functional but not instantaneous.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($47-50):** Data center load materializes on schedule, with energizations validating the 8 GW by 2029 target. Ohio LDC sale closes at $2.62B, providing deleveraging. 765-kV transmission gains regulatory approval. Rate base compounds at 10%+, supporting 8% EPS growth through 2030. Market re-rates toward 25-26x forward on confirmed premium growth. Potential rate cuts (if inflation moderates) reduce financing costs and compress credit spreads.

**Bear Case ($36-39):** Interest rates remain elevated or rise further, compressing utility valuations sector-wide. Data center load commitments are deferred 12-24 months. 765-kV project faces material delays. Moody's downgrades to Baa3, increasing borrowing costs. Capital plan requires dilutive equity issuance beyond current ATM program. Stock de-rates toward peer median of 20.5x on execution skepticism.

**Base Case ($44-46):** Growth materializes largely on schedule with minor delays. Financing costs remain manageable but elevated. Ohio sale closes. Premium to peers narrows slightly as market digests rising interest burden. Total return of dividend (2.1%) plus modest capital appreciation approximates 5-8% annually.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back below $39 (20.5x forward) on broad utility sector weakness without fundamental deterioration in load commitments
- 765-kV transmission project receives clear regulatory approval, removing the primary political risk
- Evidence of 2+ GW data center energizations ahead of schedule

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Moody's downgrade to Baa3 or S&P downgrade below BBB, signaling capital markets access deterioration
- Cancellation or material downscaling of the 765-kV transmission project
- Evidence of >3 GW data center load deferrals or cancellations from committed customers
- Dilutive equity issuance exceeding $2B beyond planned ATM (signaling plan unaffordability)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (likely July 2026); Ohio LDC sale closing (Q4 2026); 765-kV regulatory developments (ongoing through 2026-2027).

## Conclusion

CenterPoint Energy offers one of the regulated utility sector's most tangible growth stories, anchored by real data center commitments and Houston's structural attractiveness as a load growth market. The management team has executed well historically (201.5% 5-year TSR vs. 130.1% for peers), and the 8% non-GAAP EPS growth target is underpinned by identifiable rate base expansion.

However, at 22.5x forward earnings — a 10% premium to peers — the stock prices in substantial execution success while offering limited margin of safety against the significant risks: a leveraged balance sheet requiring flawless capital markets access in a high-rate environment, political opposition to the key transmission project, and the binary nature of data center load materialization. With flat estimate revisions and no near-term catalyst to drive re-rating, the risk-reward is balanced at current levels.

Growth Prospects improved from 6.5 to 7.5 over prior reports as load commitments became more concrete, while Risk Factors declined from 6.0 to 5.0 as the financing challenge intensified. These offsetting forces produce a stock that rewards patience over immediate action. HOLD with a growth thesis — accumulate on weakness below $39 or upgrade upon 765-kV regulatory clarity.
