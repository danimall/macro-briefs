**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Henry Schein is the largest global distributor of dental and medical products and services, serving over one million customers across 34 countries. The investment case has shifted from a slow-growth, perennially under-earning distribution story to a transformation story catalyzed by KKR, which took two board seats in early 2025 after the stock's five-year total shareholder return (116) lagged its peer group (144). The defining developments since then — a $250M KKR equity investment matched by a $250M buyback, a standstill cap raised from 14.9% to 19.9%, a 35-year CEO handing off to an equity-incentivized successor, a $125M operating-income improvement program, and a board overhaul — point to a multi-year value-creation engagement rather than a quick activist exit.

The fundamentals underneath the transformation are stable but modest. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 6.3% to $3,368M, though only 2.5% was internal local-currency growth, with FX contributing 3.1 points. Gross margin expanded slightly to 31.8% from 31.6%, and the mix continues shifting toward higher-margin Specialty Products (55.3% gross) and Technology (68.6% gross), which both grew faster than core distribution. Adjusted EPS of $1.32 beat consensus, but net income attributable to HSIC fell to $107M from $110M; reported EPS rose only because the share count dropped roughly 8% year-over-year on aggressive, partly debt-funded buybacks. The balance sheet sits in the Altman grey zone (Z-score 2.21), total debt rose $301M in a single quarter to $3,408M, and ~$903M of redeemable minority interests carry put rights that represent a recurring cash overhang.

We rate HSIC a HOLD with a growth lean. At ~$76.53, the stock trades at roughly 14.3x the $5.34 current-year consensus EPS and ~23x trailing — a discount to large medical distributor peers (COR 20.6x, MCK 19.3x) on a P/E basis but appropriately so given thinner margins, higher leverage, and a lower-quality earnings stream. The KKR/Lowery plan could re-rate the multiple if cost-out and mix-shift execution lift returns on capital, and a KKR-designated director's open-market purchase at $69.19 is a tangible alignment signal. The upside to the $87-88 analyst consensus depends on execution that is not yet proven in the numbers, and the downside is bounded by leverage and working-capital volatility. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Henry Schein operates through three reportable segments. Global Distribution & Value-Added Services (~84% of sales) distributes national-brand and corporate-brand dental and medical consumables and equipment, plus financial services, education, and consulting; it subdivides into Global Dental (~52% of total) and Global Medical (~32%), and carries a low ~25.8% gross margin. Global Specialty Products (~12% of sales) is company-manufactured dental implants, biomaterials, endodontics, orthodontics, and orthopedics, built largely through acquisition and earning a 55.3% gross margin. Global Technology (~5% of sales) is the Henry Schein One practice-management software joint venture plus e-services, the highest-margin (68.6%) and fastest-growing (6.9% internal growth) segment.

The competitive moat rests on scale, SKU breadth, distribution-center density, and a unified "One Schein" go-to-market that bundles supplies, equipment, specialty products, and software. Structural tailwinds include aging demographics (the 45+ population is projected to grow ~10% by 2036), migration of care to office and alternate-care sites, and the consolidation of customers into DSOs and GPOs that favor large broad-line distributors. The strategic response to thin distribution margins is a deliberate mix shift toward manufactured Specialty Products and software, both of which grew faster than the core distribution business in Q1.

The transformation overlay is the most important element of the current story. KKR is capped at 19.9% ownership, holds two board seats (Max Lin and William "Dan" Daniel), exercised an Extension Election in December 2025 keeping those designees through the 2027 AGM, and added equity-swap exposure of ~918,723 shares in March 2026. Frederick M. Lowery became CEO on March 2, 2026, succeeding Stanley Bergman after ~35 years; Bergman remains Chairman through the 2026 annual meeting, then becomes Chairman Emeritus, with a new independent chairman to follow.

## Financial Analysis

By segment, Q1 Dental grew 9.0% (FX-aided), Specialty 8.1%, Technology 7.0%, and Medical only 1.7% on a weak respiratory-test season. Operating income rose to $182M from $175M, though the prior-year quarter benefited from a $20M cyber-insurance recovery that understates the underlying operating comparison.

GAAP diluted EPS was $0.92, down from a year earlier. The gap between rising adjusted EPS and falling net income is explained by the buyback: share count fell from 124.2M to 114.4M. This is a genuine per-share tailwind but it is financial engineering layered on a flat-to-down operating profit base, and investors should separate the two.

The cash-flow and balance-sheet picture warrants attention. Operating cash flow was negative $97M in Q1 versus positive $37M a year earlier, driven by a $223M working-capital drain in payables and accrued expenses. Q1 is historically the seasonal low point of the working-capital cycle, so this is not by itself alarming, but it was funded by a $283M draw on credit lines. Bank credit lines jumped to $1,046M from $764M, while cash sits at only $138M. The company repurchased $126M of stock in Q1 with $655M remaining authorized. Funding buybacks and NCI buyouts through debt while operating cash flow is seasonally negative pushed leverage up in one quarter.

Longer-term financial-health metrics from the FY2025 10-K: D/E ~1.98, ROE 12.9%, ROA 3.7%, operating margin 5.0%, net margin 3.2%, and full-year FCF ~$573M (4.3% margin). The Altman Z-score sits in the grey zone — not a distress signal for an asset-heavy, thin-margin distributor, but a reminder that the model has limited cushion. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$5.3B represent roughly 47% of total assets, reflecting the acquisition-driven build of the Specialty segment. Interest expense has climbed to ~$39M/quarter as the company refinances older 2.35-3.5% notes into newer paper (the latest February 2026 private placement at 5.40%), a structural drag that will persist if rates stay elevated.

A specific structural overhang: redeemable noncontrolling interests of ~$903M, including Internet Brands' 33.6% stake in Henry Schein One, carry put rights that can force HSIC to buy them out at fair value. The January 2025 MOU pushed the Internet Brands put to January 2029 and extended HSIC's call to 2032, buying time, but $32M of NCI buyouts already flowed through Q1, and HSIC pays Internet Brands ~$31M/year in IP royalties.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying organic growth is mid-single-digit at best. The 2.5% internal local-currency growth in Q1 is the cleanest read on the business; the headline rate flatters that with FX and acquisitions. Dental is described by management as stable with HSIC taking share, and stable procedure volumes plus cloud practice-management adoption support that. Medical is the soft spot, held to 1.7% on the weak respiratory-test season.

The growth lever that matters most is mix. Specialty (manufactured, 55.3% gross) and Technology (software, 68.6% gross) are both growing faster than the ~26%-margin distribution core, and every point of mix shift toward those segments lifts blended margins. Layered on top is the $125M operating-income improvement target for 2026, reaffirmed at Q1, plus a 2024 restructuring program running through 2027 (now winding down — $12M of charges in Q1 versus $25M a year earlier). If the cost-out lands and mix continues shifting, operating margin can move off the 5.0% floor without requiring heroic revenue acceleration.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not the +30% inflection that would flag a strong buy signal. Current-year consensus is $5.34, revised up 0.4-0.6% over 30 and 90 days; next-year is $5.91, essentially flat over 90 days. The next-quarter estimate was cut 3.9% over 30 days and 5.1% over 90 days, reflecting the medical softness and the company's decision to hold rather than raise its 2026 outlook at Q1. The earnings track record is solid (3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters; the miss was Q2 FY2025 at -9.5%), which supports reasonable confidence in the current-year number but does not indicate accelerating momentum. Forward EPS growth of roughly 10-11% (from $5.34 to $5.91) is decent for a distributor and is partly buyback-driven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.53 the stock trades at ~14.3x current-year consensus EPS and ~13.0x next-year $5.91, against ~23x trailing on $3.27. The forward multiple is below the medical distributor peer median (P/E 20.6, with CAH 30.1x, COR 20.6x, MCK 19.3x), but the discount is largely warranted: HSIC's net margin (3.2%) and ROA (3.7%) are thinner, its leverage (D/E ~1.98, grey-zone Z-score) is higher than the strongest peers, and a meaningful share of recent per-share growth is buyback-driven rather than operating. A lower multiple on lower-quality, more-levered earnings is not a mispricing on its own.

The case for some upside rests on the transformation, not the static multiple. If the $125M cost-out program and the mix shift toward Specialty/Technology lift operating margin and ROIC, the stock can re-rate toward the peer median, and consensus targets (mean $87.21, median $88.00, range $64-100) imply ~14% upside to the mean. Those targets are a reasonable anchor rather than a floor here: HSIC is a stable-growth distributor with positive-but-modest revisions, not a high-momentum name where consensus systematically lags. The KKR/Lowery plan is the variable that could push earnings power above the trailing trend; until the cost-out shows up in margins, the appropriate stance is to value the business on demonstrated economics, which support a multiple close to where it trades.

Downside support comes from the absolute valuation: at ~14x forward earnings for a defensive, scale-advantaged distributor with recurring demand, the multiple has limited room to compress further without a fundamental deterioration. The bear risk is more about earnings (a working-capital or medical-demand stumble, or interest expense eating the cost-out gains) than about multiple de-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

HSIC competes with Patterson Companies in dental, Cardinal Health and McKesson in medical, and increasingly with third-party online commerce sites that pressure consumable margins. The industry consolidates on both sides. Distributor consolidation favors scale players like HSIC, but customer consolidation into DSOs and GPOs shifts volume toward lower-margin centralized purchasing, a structural margin-mix headwind that grows as the customer base concentrates.

HSIC's strategic answer of migrating mix toward manufactured Specialty Products and proprietary software is the right defense against the commoditization of distribution, and both segments are executing (Specialty +8.1%, Technology +7.0% internal). The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving: the company is taking share in U.S. dental and building a higher-margin product layer, but the core distribution business faces persistent pricing pressure from online channels and lower-margin large-account customers. The prior-report downgrade of Competitive Position from 7.0 to 6.0 captured this dynamic; we hold it at 6.0, reflecting genuine progress on mix against genuine pressure on the core.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage in a thin-margin model.** D/E ~1.98 and the grey-zone Z-score leave limited cushion. Rising refinancing costs (newer notes at 5.40% vs. older 2.35-3.5%) and interest expense of ~$39M/quarter are a structural drag. Debt-funded buybacks juice EPS but add leverage in a company with little room to absorb a demand or working-capital shock.

**Working-capital and cash-flow volatility.** Negative operating cash flow in Q1, funded by credit-line draws, is partly seasonal but warrants monitoring given the simultaneous buyback and NCI cash outflows.

**Redeemable NCI overhang (~$903M).** Put rights, including Internet Brands' Henry Schein One stake, represent a recurring future cash use that competes with buybacks, M&A, and deleveraging.

**Execution and governance.** A 35-year CEO transition, a board overhaul, and a $125M cost target all running simultaneously create execution risk. The KKR partnership is constructive but is a named risk factor, and the relationship could shift if results disappoint.

**External demand and policy.** OBBBA Medicaid cuts (signed July 2025) may pressure utilization; tariffs dented U.S. dental-equipment sales in mid-2025 and remain a volatile variable; the October 2023 cyberattack remains a named risk given e-commerce dependence; and the EU regulatory load (MDR, AI Act, EHDS, CSRD/CSDDD) adds compliance cost.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute stress despite a technically backwarded term structure. Near-term ATM IV of 29.9% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 27.3% (fair, +2.6pp spread) and at only the 25th percentile within the current chain. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call OI ratio of 0.04 (down from 0.13 since early May), put/call volume ratio of 0.12, and a -5.3% put/call skew. The implied move on the nearest June 18 expiry is ±5.4%. The backwardation reflects the proximity of catalysts and the company's transformation cadence rather than a directional bearish bet, and the call-tilted positioning is mildly consistent with the bullish lean from the director's open-market purchase. There is no put-skew divergence to flag against our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The KKR/Lowery value-creation plan re-rates a perennially under-earning distributor. The $125M operating-income program lands, restructuring winds down, and the mix shift toward Specialty and Technology lifts blended margins off the 5.0% operating floor. Continued ~8% annual buyback shrinkage compounds per-share earnings. Dental share gains persist and medical normalizes after the weak respiratory season. The forward multiple re-rates toward the peer median, and EPS reaches the $5.91 next-year consensus and beyond. The stock works toward the $88-100 upper analyst range, roughly +15% to +30%. The Dan Daniel open-market purchase signals the partner-aligned director sees value near recent lows.

**Bear case.** Organic growth stays stuck at ~2.5%, the cost-out is partly offset by rising interest expense and the "value-creation consulting" run-rate ($13M in Q1, up from $8M), and the NCI puts plus debt-funded buybacks keep leverage elevated. A working-capital or medical-demand stumble, or a macro consumer-confidence hit to discretionary dental procedures, pressures earnings. Net income continues to drift while EPS is propped only by buybacks. The transformation disappoints, KKR's patience is tested, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the analyst range near $64, roughly -16%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 FY2026 earnings (early August 2026), with the 2026 AGM and new-chairman appointment as a secondary governance milestone. The transformation thesis plays out over 12-24 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin expands toward 6%+ with visible contribution from the $125M program, and consensus EPS revisions turn positive across both current and next year (currently the next-quarter estimate is being cut).
- Internal local-currency revenue growth accelerates above ~4% with medical-segment recovery, confirming the share-gain story without FX support.
- Operating cash flow normalizes positive through H1 and total debt stabilizes or declines, removing the leverage overhang while buybacks continue.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Internal organic growth decelerates below ~1% or the company cuts its 2026 outlook on broad demand weakness rather than isolated respiratory softness.
- Leverage rises further (total debt toward $3.8B+) while operating cash flow stays negative beyond the seasonal Q1, signaling buybacks are being financed past prudent limits.
- The KKR partnership turns adversarial, or the new-CEO transition shows execution stumbles (margin program slips, restructuring charges re-accelerate).

## Conclusion

Henry Schein is a defensive, scale-advantaged health care distributor in the middle of a credible KKR-catalyzed transformation: an equity-aligned new CEO, a board overhaul, a $125M cost-out program, a mix shift toward higher-margin Specialty and Technology, and a partner building toward a 19.9% stake whose designated director bought stock in the open market. The fundamentals are stable-to-improving but unspectacular — 2.5% internal growth, slight margin expansion, and EPS growth that leans heavily on debt-funded buybacks while net income drifts. The balance sheet sits in the grey zone with ~$3.4B of debt and ~$900M of put-able minority interests.

At ~14x forward earnings, the valuation is reasonable but not cheap enough to be asymmetric given the leverage and the quality of the earnings stream. The re-rating case is genuine and supported by tangible alignment signals, but it requires execution that has not yet shown up in margins or net income, and consensus revisions are modestly positive rather than inflecting. With ~14% upside to the consensus mean target against a ~16% downside in the bear case, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate HSIC a HOLD with a growth lean, and would upgrade on confirmed margin expansion from the value-creation program or accelerating organic growth, and downgrade on deteriorating cash flow or a demand-driven guidance cut.
