**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Viavi Solutions has undergone a fundamental transformation over the past four quarters. A company that generated flat-to-declining revenue for three years ($1,292M FY22 → $1,000M FY24 trough) is now growing revenue in the low-40s percent range, driven by AI/data-center fiber and datacom buildouts, rising aerospace and defense demand, and two bolt-on acquisitions (Inertial Labs and the Spirent high-speed-ethernet assets bought from Keysight). Q3 FY26 revenue was $406.8M, up 42.8% YoY, with the NSE segment up 54.4% and its operating margin expanding from 10.4% to 17.2% on operating leverage. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 25.7% for the current quarter and 27.6% for next year over the trailing 90 days — the magnitude that typically precedes continued re-rating. Four straight earnings beats reinforce the momentum.

The complications are equally concrete. The transformation was funded with debt: total borrowings roughly doubled to ~$1.08B (a $600M Term Loan B at ~6.17%, $400M 2029 notes, $250M 2031 converts), interest expense nearly doubled to $14.3M in Q3, and the 9-month GAAP result was a net loss of −$63.1M driven by a $46.2M debt-extinguishment loss, $24.3M of earnout remeasurement, and heavy acquisition amortization that persists at $60–78M/yr through FY2030. The 2031 convertibles (conversion price $13.79) are deeply in-the-money and actively dilutive; diluted share count already reached 249.5M. Essentially the entire C-suite and board sold into the 2026 rally with no offsetting open-market purchases; the CEO alone sold ~$20.2M across February and May 2026, the bulk at $51–55, cutting his stake to ~1.19M shares.

At ~$39.52, the stock is down roughly 35% from its 52-week high of $60.43, yet trades at ~42x current-year non-GAAP EPS ($0.94) and sits below the analyst target range of $60–70. The operating momentum is real and the estimate-revision signal is strong, but the valuation is not cheap on any honest basis (GAAP is negative; the "true" earnings power sits between GAAP and non-GAAP given the recurring adjustments), leverage constrains flexibility, and insider distribution signals management viewed the low-to-mid $50s as full value. We rate VIAV a HOLD (growth_hold), with the August 5 FY26 print the near-term catalyst to reassess.

## Company Overview

VIAVI is a global leader in network test, monitoring, and assurance, and in optical technologies, operating two segments:

**Network and Service Enablement (NSE)** — roughly 72–79% of revenue. Test, monitoring, assurance, and security solutions spanning telecom, datacom, data centers, cloud, wireless, satellite, public safety, and military/critical infrastructure, plus resilient positioning/navigation/timing (PNT) via the Inertial Labs acquisition. Effective March 2025, the former Network Enablement and Service Enablement units were merged into a single segment. NSE is the cyclical, capex-sensitive part of the business, but is currently the growth engine on AI/data-center demand.

**Optical Security and Performance Products (OSP)** — roughly 21–28% of revenue but the profit engine, running ~35–36% operating margin versus NSE's mid-teens. OSP makes optical coatings for anti-counterfeiting (banknote security pigments sold worldwide through a strategic alliance with SICPA), 3D sensing for consumer electronics, and government/aerospace/automotive/industrial applications. The banknote anti-counterfeiting franchise is a durable, high-margin near-monopoly.

The strategy is straightforward: defend the OSP profit core, invest into the secular AI/data-center and defense/PNT tailwinds within NSE, and extend into adjacencies via M&A. The two 2025–2026 acquisitions execute that plan directly. Fiscal year ends in late June; FY26 (ended June 27, 2026) results are due August 5, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (fiscal year, June-end):** $1,292M (FY22) → $1,106M (FY23) → $1,000M (FY24, trough, net loss −$25.8M) → $1,084M (FY25, +8.4%, net income +$34.8M) → sharply accelerating in FY26. Nine-month FY26 revenue reached $1,075.2M, up 35.4% YoY, already nearly matching full-year FY25.

**Segment detail (Q3 FY26):** OSP steady at ~35% margin. Spirent HSE/CE contributed $54.3M and Inertial Labs $22.6M in the quarter. Consolidated gross margin held near 57%, with NSE gross margin rising to 65.3%. This reflects genuine operating leverage, not just acquired revenue.

**The GAAP versus non-GAAP gap is wide and matters.** The GAAP net loss was driven by non-operating items: the debt-extinguishment loss, the Inertial Labs contingent-consideration remeasurement, and $47.6M of acquisition amortization. Q3 non-GAAP EPS was $0.27 (+80% YoY) on a 21.0% non-GAAP operating margin. The business is meaningfully more profitable on an operating basis than GAAP shows, but the adjustments are large and recurring, and earnout remeasurement is inherently volatile. The honest earnings power sits between the two figures; leaning entirely on non-GAAP overstates it, and leaning entirely on GAAP understates it.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $89.8M and FCF $62M. Nine-month FY26 OCF fell to $47.2M on a working-capital build (AR +$62.5M, inventory +$37.3M) supporting the growth ramp. Cash stood at $499M as of March 2026 against total debt of ~$1.08B (up from ~$625M pre-Spirent). The FY25 10-K carried an Altman Z-Score of 1.75 (nominally distress zone), but this is a leverage/low-EBIT artifact compounded by a −$69.7B accumulated deficit that is a legacy JDS Uniphase optical-bubble writedown — not operational and not a solvency signal. The company is covenant-compliant, generating FCF, and prepaying its term loan ($150M already retired). Leverage is real and constrains flexibility; bankruptcy risk is low.

**Earnings quality track record.** Four consecutive beats: Q3 FY26 +14.5%, Q2 +15.6%, Q1 +11.3%, Q4 FY25 +10.7%. The consistency of high-single-to-mid-teens beats supports confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is well-supported and broad-based rather than single-source. Management repeatedly cites two structural tailwinds: AI-driven data-center fiber/datacom buildouts and rising aerospace/defense spending, with anti-counterfeiting and 3D sensing recovering in OSP. Confirmation across the broader infrastructure layer — hyperscaler capex, data-center power and networking demand, and defense budget expansion visible across multiple companies — raises the durability of VIAVI's demand story relative to a single-company narrative.

The estimate-revision signal is strong: current-quarter EPS revised +25.7% over 90 days ($0.24 → $0.30), next-quarter +24.0%, and next-year +27.6% ($1.01 → $1.29). Revenue growth exceeding 35% combined with 25%+ estimate revisions is the pattern that historically precedes continued outperformance as consensus catches up to reality. I weight this signal materially positive. The critical qualifier: it applies most cleanly to genuinely cheap stocks, and VIAV trades at ~42x current-year non-GAAP EPS — reasonable relative to its growth (PEG on next-year $1.29 and ~30% growth is under 1.5) but not so cheap that the signal alone justifies a BUY when GAAP is negative.

The two acquisitions add structural growth beyond the organic ramp. The Spirent high-speed-ethernet assets, bought from Keysight for $399.3M because regulators forced Keysight to divest them as a condition of its own Spirent acquisition, are directly complementary to VIAVI's ethernet test platform. Inertial Labs adds resilient PNT into the defense/aerospace demand cycle. The main headwind is legacy wireless/cable spend from network equipment makers and service providers, which remains soft and caps the low end of NSE growth.

## Valuation Assessment

VIAVI's headline multiples are distorted by the GAAP loss (trailing and forward P/E print as negative). The relevant metric is forward non-GAAP EPS: at ~$39.52 on current-year non-GAAP consensus of $0.94, the stock trades at ~42x, and ~31x next-year consensus of $1.29. Against ~30% earnings growth, the PEG is roughly 1.0–1.4 — not stretched for the growth being delivered, but not a bargain either.

Peer comparison is muddy because the Communications Equipment cohort is heterogeneous and several peers also carry distorted multiples (VSAT at −287x, EXTR at 258.5x, peer median P/E 60.4x). On a like-for-like forward basis, VIAV screens roughly in line with the higher-quality peers (IDCC 26.3x, NTCT 32.4x) adjusted for its faster growth. The stock does not screen as clearly cheap or clearly expensive against the group.

The valuation case rests on three points. First, analyst targets ($60 low, $70 high, $65 median) sit 52–77% above the current price. On a stock with rising estimates trading around 30x next-year earnings, targets typically function as floors rather than ceilings; the caveat is that those targets were set when the stock was in the $50s, and the insider selling clustered at $51–55 signals that management viewed that zone as full-to-rich value. That tension caps my confidence in treating the $60–70 range as a hard floor.

Second, the drawdown from the 52-week high. Under our framework, a price decline on intact fundamentals improves risk/reward. Fundamentals are indeed intact (estimate revisions positive, four beats, backlog momentum), which argues the drawdown is a better entry than the peak. But at a >30% drawdown the burden shifts to identifying what the market is repricing: a combination of general AI/tech-hardware de-rating fears (the semiconductor/AI-capex-deceleration debate visible in adjacent names), rising-rate pressure on a leveraged balance sheet, active convertible dilution, and the insider distribution. These are real concerns, not just volatility.

Third, the cycle-adjusted argument: trailing GAAP is not meaningful at this juncture given the deal artifacts, and the underlying operating profit power ($0.94–1.29 forward EPS) is well above what the negative GAAP print suggests. This supports a valuation score above the "value trap" floor. Net, I score valuation at 5.0 — the growth justifies the multiple, but there is no identifiable, catalyst-driven mispricing large enough to warrant a high score, and cheapness historically sorts the wrong way in this regime.

## Competitive Landscape

VIAVI's competitive position is stable-to-improving, with the trajectory more favorable than a year ago. In NSE, the company competes against Keysight (from which it just bought complementary assets), EXFO, and various Asia-based test vendors, in a market subject to telecom capex cyclicality, customer consolidation, Asian commoditization, and a secular shift toward virtualized/software-defined networks that could erode legacy hardware demand. Against those headwinds, NSE is currently gaining share of a growing pie — the revenue growth and margin expansion reflect both the AI/data-center tailwind and the Spirent/Inertial Labs additions filling out the ethernet-test and PNT portfolios.

In OSP, the banknote anti-counterfeiting franchise via the SICPA alliance is a durable quasi-monopoly with ~35% margins and limited competitive threat; 3D sensing adds optionality tied to consumer-electronics cycles. This segment anchors the profit base and diversifies away from NSE cyclicality.

The competitive trajectory improvement is real but bounded. The Spirent acquisition is a genuine share gain in high-speed ethernet test, positioning VIAVI directly in the AI-networking test market. Integration success across two back-to-back deals plus a ~5% workforce restructuring is the key FY26–27 execution watch item; the moat widens if integration delivers, and narrows if it stalls or if virtualization accelerates faster than the hardware-test franchise can adapt.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and financing drag.** The $600M Term Loan B carries SOFR+2.50% (6.17% current). In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the debt load is a persistent earnings and flexibility constraint, though not a solvency threat given $499M cash and active prepayment.

**Active convertible dilution.** The 2031 converts are deeply in-the-money after the surge and were reclassified short-term. Share count already rose from 223M to 234M via note settlements. This is a live, ongoing dilution headwind to per-share metrics.

**Earnout volatility.** The Inertial Labs contingent consideration (up to $175M) has whipsawed the P&L and cash flows — fair value swung from $139M to $68.2M, and a $73.5M payment cleared in Q3. This is a recurring, hard-to-model swing factor. Historical note: the 2022 Jackson Labs earnout expired unmet, a reminder acquired-growth targets don't always materialize.

**Customer concentration and industry consolidation** in NSE, plus Asia-based commoditization and the virtualization/software-defined-networking secular threat.

**Tariffs/geopolitics.** China is ~16–20% of revenue with Taiwan supply-chain dependence. The 2025 IEEPA tariffs were struck down and replaced by temporary 2026 tariffs — a modified risk factor in the Q3 10-Q.

**Integration and leadership transition.** Two back-to-back acquisitions plus restructuring, alongside multiple Item 5.02 leadership-change 8-Ks in 2025–2026.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 113.9% versus 66.6% historical volatility — rich by 47 points and in the 94th percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (113.9% near-term versus 96.6% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst (the August 5 earnings print). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±24.9%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−2.2%) and the put/call volume ratio (0.58) and OI ratio (0.11) are not bearishly tilted, so the market is pricing a large move but not a directional collapse. ATM IV has collapsed from 218.7% to 113.9% since the mid-June snapshot as the price fell 18%, meaning some prior stress has been discounted. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that entering ahead of an earnings binary priced for a ~25% move argues for patience — waiting for the post-print IV crush would offer a cleaner entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60–70).** The AI/data-center and defense/PNT tailwinds are durable and broad-based, NSE continues compounding 30%+ with expanding margins, Spirent and Inertial Labs integrate cleanly and prove accretive, OSP anchors a stable high-margin profit base, and consensus estimates keep rising. As the company deleverages (prepaying the term loan) and GAAP earnings normalize once deal artifacts roll off, the market re-rates toward analyst targets. Non-GAAP EPS scaling toward $1.30–1.50 at a ~40–45x multiple supports the $60–70 range.

**Bear Case (~$26–30).** AI-capex deceleration fears prove justified, NSE growth reverts to its historically cyclical pattern, integration stumbles or the earnout swings against the company, rising-rate pressure compounds the leveraged balance sheet, and convertible dilution grinds per-share metrics lower. The insider distribution at $51–55 marks the top, and the stock de-rates toward ~25x a stalled ~$1.00 non-GAAP EPS. A ~25% miss-driven move on a weak August print would put the low-$30s in play quickly.

The base case sits between these. The operating momentum and estimate-revision signal are strong enough to keep this a growth hold, but leverage, dilution, recurring GAAP drag, and unanimous insider selling into the rally prevent the asymmetric upside a BUY requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 6–12 month horizon anchored to the August 5, 2026 FY26 print and subsequent quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26/Q4 results confirm sustained NSE growth above 30% YoY with non-GAAP operating margin holding at/above 21%, and management guides FY27 revenue growth above 15% organic.
- A pullback into the low-$30s (below ~28x next-year non-GAAP EPS) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring genuine asymmetry.
- Evidence of net debt reduction and a narrowing GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap as deal artifacts roll off, plus any open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q4 revenue growth decelerates below ~15% YoY or NSE margins compress, signaling the cycle is rolling over.
- An integration writedown, a large adverse earnout remeasurement, or a covenant/leverage warning.
- Estimate revisions turn negative alongside a guidance cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the August 5, 2026 FY26 earnings release, with the options market pricing a ~25% move around it. Reassess promptly after the print and IV crush.

## Conclusion

VIAVI has transitioned from a stagnant test-and-measurement vendor into a data-center and aerospace-defense growth story, with 42.8% Q3 revenue growth, NSE margins expanding several hundred basis points, four consecutive beats, and 25%+ estimate revisions that historically precede continued re-rating. The OSP anti-counterfeiting franchise anchors a durable, high-margin profit base, and the Spirent/Inertial Labs acquisitions extend the company directly into AI-networking test and resilient PNT.

The offsets keep this from being a BUY. Leverage roughly doubled and constrains flexibility; the 9-month GAAP loss reflects recurring, not one-time, adjustments; convertible dilution is active; the earnout is a volatile P&L swing factor; and the entire C-suite and board sold into the rally at $51–55 with no open-market buys, signaling management saw the mid-$50s as full value. At ~$39.52 on ~42x current-year non-GAAP EPS, the stock is neither cheap nor stretched, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate VIAV a HOLD, favoring patience through the August 5 earnings binary; a cleaner BUY setup would emerge either on a low-$30s pullback that restores asymmetry, or on a print that confirms the growth durability that leverage and dilution currently offset.
