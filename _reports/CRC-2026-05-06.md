## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation has completed a rapid consolidation of California's onshore conventional oil production through three acquisitions (Aera in July 2024, Berry in December 2025), emerging as the dominant producer in a structurally supply-short market. Q1 2026 production reached 154 MBoe/d (81% oil), with full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDAX guided at $970–$1,070M. The stock trades at approximately 11.3x forward earnings on FY2026E consensus of $5.53 — a multiple that appears compressed relative to the earnings revision velocity (+210% over 90 days) and the company's unique competitive position.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a dominant position in California onshore production with Brent-linked premium pricing, (2) acquisition-driven production growth from 86 MBoe/d (2023) to a targeted 175 MBoe/d exit rate in 2026, and (3) optionality on Carbon TerraVault CCS operations approaching first injection. Against this, the primary risks are binary regulatory exposure in California, commodity price sensitivity, and a pattern of insider selling at current levels. At 11-13x forward earnings with consensus targets clustered at $72-$88, the risk/reward remains favorable for a 12-month holding period.

## Company Overview

CRC is an independent E&P company operating predominantly in California's San Joaquin, Los Angeles, Sacramento, and Ventura basins, with recent expansion into Utah's Uinta Basin via the Berry merger. The company was spun off from Occidental Petroleum in 2014, restructured through Chapter 11 in 2020, and has since executed an aggressive roll-up strategy. It now controls proved reserves of 654 MMBoe and generates total operating revenues of approximately $3.7B annually (of which $2.9B represents oil, natural gas, and NGL sales).

The business is organized into three segments: (1) Oil & Natural Gas (core E&P), (2) Carbon Management (Carbon TerraVault CCS JV with Brookfield), and (3) Electricity Generation (Elk Hills cogeneration plant selling resource adequacy contracts). The E&P segment represents approximately 79–81% of total operating revenue and essentially all operating income.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Growth:** Oil, natural gas, and NGL sales expanded from $2.16B (2023) to $2.91B (2025), driven almost entirely by production acquisitions. Total operating revenues in 2025 were $3.67B, including derivative gains, purchased-commodity marketing revenue, electricity revenue, and other items. Net income has declined from $564M to $363M over this period due to integration costs, higher DD&A charges on acquired assets, and lower commodity realizations. However, adjusted metrics tell a cleaner story: Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDAX of $304M annualizes to $1,216M before accounting for the production ramp and synergy realization still ahead.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $865M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was depressed at $99M due to working capital timing (derivative settlements, Berry integration). Free cash flow before working capital was $116M in Q1, implying an annualized run rate of $460-$500M on current production — this understates normalized FCF because the 7-rig program's production additions accrue through the year.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1,310M against trailing EBITDAX of approximately $1,200M implies net leverage of approximately 1.1x. Liquidity stands at $1,276M. The recent debt optimization (swapping 8.25% 2029 notes for 7.0% 2034 notes) extends maturities and reduces interest expense by ~$4.4M annually. As of March 31, 2026, the current ratio was approximately 0.55 (current assets of $788M vs. current liabilities of $1,441M), reflecting normal E&P working capital dynamics and Berry integration timing rather than liquidity stress, given the $1,276M in total liquidity available.

**Asset Retirement Obligations:** The $1,033M ARO represents a significant long-tail liability. A 1% increase in the inflation discount rate assumption adds $94M. This is partially offset by the cash flow generated from the wells themselves before abandonment — but it constrains terminal valuation.

**Shareholder Returns:** $1.62/share annual dividend (2.3% yield at current price) plus $600M remaining buyback authorization through 2027. Total returns since 2021 of approximately $1.57B (including $1.17B in repurchases and $403M in dividends) demonstrate management's commitment to capital return.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth from 86 MBoe/d to 154 MBoe/d (79% increase) over 2023-Q1 2026 is entirely acquisition-driven. Organic decline is 4-7 MBoe/d annually. The critical near-term growth driver is the ramp from 4 to 7 rigs in H2 2026, targeting a 175 MBoe/d exit rate — representing roughly 14% intra-year growth on an entry-to-exit basis.

The estimate revision data is unambiguous: current-quarter EPS estimates rose from $0.24 to $1.53 over 90 days (+526%), and full-year FY2026E went from $1.78 to $5.53 (+210%). These revisions reflect the market's adjustment to the transformed production profile post-Berry integration and the elevated oil price assumptions in guidance. Even next-year estimates ($4.87) imply significant earnings power at lower commodity prices.

The Carbon TerraVault business offers pure optionality. With first injection imminent at Elk Hills, a successful CCS demonstration could attract market attention and re-rate the stock. However, the segment has generated losses of $86M (2025) and $94M (2024) with zero revenue to date, meaning this is a call option, not a present-value contributor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.55 (the profile price; recent trading ~$70), CRC trades at approximately 11.3x FY2026E earnings ($5.53) and roughly 15.1x trailing EPS ($4.15). Against the peer median P/E of 17.5x, CRC trades at a significant discount on a forward basis. This discount partially reflects the regulatory overhang that has historically suppressed valuation.

The magnitude of estimate revisions combined with the compressed forward multiple creates the profile where the market is systematically underpricing fundamental improvement. Consensus price targets range from $72 to $88, with a median of $84 — implying 20-34% upside from the profile price. At the recent trading price of $70.13, upside to median target narrows to ~20%.

Critical valuation caveat: CRC's official March 2026 guidance was built on an assumed Brent price of $65.57/Bbl — roughly in line with spot at the time. CRC's Q1 2026 10-Q noted that the Brent forward curve for the 12 months following March 31, 2026 was $83.66. If realized prices average $70-75 through the full year, EBITDAX should track near the guided range. A sustained decline below $65 Brent would compress EBITDAX meaningfully below the low end of guidance, potentially reducing EPS to $3.00–$3.50. The forward multiple on scenario-adjusted earnings would be closer to 18-20x, roughly in line with or above peers. The BUY case therefore depends on oil prices remaining at or above the $65-70 Brent level through H2 2026.

Given the current geopolitical environment (Iran peace deal framework creating binary oil price risk), the asymmetry favors longs: deal failure → oil >$100 (well above guidance assumptions) vs. deal success → oil $85-95 (supportive of results tracking above guided midpoint). Only a deep collapse to $55-60 Brent would materially impair the thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC's competitive position is exceptional within its operating geography. Through the Aera and Berry mergers, CRC has consolidated the large majority of conventional California onshore production into a single operator. This creates:

**Dominant incumbent position:** California refineries source crude from a limited number of in-state producers; in-state demand exceeds production by approximately 70%. CRC's crude trades at a consistent premium to WTI ($66.52/bbl realized vs. $64.81 WTI in 2025).

**Operating efficiency:** Shared infrastructure, surface facilities, and workforce across adjacent leases create meaningful cost synergies. The Berry synergy target was raised 12% to $90-100M annually, suggesting integration is running ahead of plan.

**Barriers to entry:** Regulatory permitting complexity in California deters new entrants. The SB 237 resolution benefits CRC disproportionately as the incumbent with existing well pads, surface rights, and permit applications in process.

**CCS first-mover advantage:** CRC's control of depleted reservoirs with known geology and existing surface infrastructure provides a competitive moat for CCS development that would take competitors years and billions to replicate.

The counter-argument is that dominance in a politically hostile state eventually attracts more regulatory attention. California's history of aggressive climate legislation suggests this risk is persistent and real.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory risk (HIGH):** Operations are concentrated 85%+ in California. While SB 237 resolved the immediate permitting crisis, future legislative sessions could impose new restrictions. Local government setback rules, environmental litigation, and potential production moratoriums remain plausible tail risks. This is the principal reason the stock trades at a discount to peers.

**Commodity price sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH):** At 81% oil weighting, CRC's breakeven economics on existing production are approximately $25-30/bbl fully loaded, providing substantial downside protection on an operating basis. Current spot near $65-70 Brent is roughly in line with the $65.57 assumption underlying guidance.

**Integration execution (MODERATE):** Three major acquisitions in 18 months. Berry brings Utah operations under different regulatory frameworks (federal/tribal lands, NEPA). Cultural and systems integration risk is real but partially mitigated by the raised synergy guidance.

**Resource adequacy revenue decline (LOW-MODERATE):** The $125-$135M headwind from lower RA contract prices is explicitly guided and presumably embedded in consensus. But if grid dynamics continue to deteriorate (more RA-eligible capacity), this revenue line could compress further.

**Insider selling pattern (LOW-MODERATE):** CPPIB sold $214M at $61.10 in March. Multiple executives sold in early March at $62-66. While likely portfolio rebalancing and tax-driven respectively (CPPIB still holds ~8% of company), the absence of any open-market insider purchases at these levels suggests informed parties don't see dramatic near-term mispricing.

## Conclusion

CRC merits a BUY recommendation at current levels, with position sizing considerations given binary oil price risk and the stock's 100%+ appreciation over the past 12 months. Production nearly doubled in two years, estimate revisions are among the most extreme in E&P (+210% over 90 days), and the competitive position as California's dominant onshore producer is durable in the medium term.

The prior score trajectory (6.1 → 6.5 → current 6.87) reflects genuine fundamental improvement through successful acquisition integration, permitting resolution, and earnings revision acceleration. The key risk is that the stock now trades within 10% of its 52-week high while consensus EPS estimates may embed oil price assumptions above current spot. This creates asymmetric earnings delivery risk — beats are partially expected, while a sustained oil price downturn could trigger a meaningful de-rating.

Appropriate sizing is 1.5-2.0% given the concentration risk (single state, single commodity) and binary geopolitical overlay on oil pricing. The 11/12 analyst consensus at Buy/Strong Buy with a $72-$88 target range provides institutional support for the thesis.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The official 2026 adjusted EBITDAX guidance is $970–$1,070M (not $1.4–$1.5B as initially reported), and the Brent price assumption underlying guidance is $65.57/Bbl (not $90.58). These corrections materially reduce the margin of safety in the earnings-power narrative and forward valuation argument supporting the BUY recommendation. The consensus EPS of $5.53 used in the valuation may not be fully reconcilable with official company guidance at stated commodity assumptions.
