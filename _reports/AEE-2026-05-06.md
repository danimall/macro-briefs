## Executive Summary

Ameren is a regulated utility with a compelling long-term growth story anchored by a $30.5–$33.1 billion capital plan (2026–2030) and 2.2 GW of executed data center service agreements. Its 10.6% rate base CAGR through 2030 is among the highest in the regulated utility peer set, supported by the 2025 PPRA legislation in Missouri and multi-year rate plans in Illinois. Q1 2026 EPS of $1.28 (+20% YoY) confirmed execution momentum, and the company reaffirmed $5.25–$5.45 FY2026 guidance.

The investment challenge is that this growth story is priced at fair value, not at a discount. At 20.9x forward earnings, AEE trades at the peer median P/E despite (a) needing to execute a capex program that will dilute shareholders by 14–15% over five years, (b) facing Illinois ROE appeals with uncertain outcomes, and (c) carrying earnings sharing mechanisms that cap upside from data center load. The options market is pricing near-term stress — 26.5% implied volatility vs. 15.3% realized, in backwardation — suggesting a catalyst the equity hasn't yet discounted.

The stock warrants holding for investors who own it given the durable 6–8% EPS growth trajectory, but the specific conditions for upgrading to BUY require either a 7–10% price correction or resolution of the Illinois regulatory appeals in Ameren's favor.

## Company Overview

Ameren operates as a regulated utility holding company serving 2.5 million electric and 900,000+ natural gas customers across Missouri and Illinois. Revenue is generated through four segments: Ameren Missouri (vertically integrated generation/transmission/distribution, ~52% of electric revenues), Ameren Illinois Electric Distribution (~26%), Ameren Illinois Natural Gas (~11%), and Ameren Transmission (~6%, FERC-regulated formula rates).

The company's strategic direction is defined by three vectors: (1) generation transition from coal to renewables, natural gas, and nuclear; (2) massive transmission expansion through MISO long-range projects ($3.1B assigned, with additional competitive bid opportunities); and (3) data center load growth that creates demand for incremental generation capacity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue reached $8.80B (+15.4% YoY), driven by the April 2025 MoPSC rate order (+$355M) and MISO capacity price increases (22x jump to $667/MW-day). Adjusted EPS grew 8.6% to $5.03, with GAAP EPS of $5.35 including a $0.32/share one-time tax benefit from NOL carryforward revaluation.

**Q1 2026:** EPS of $1.28 vs. $1.07 YoY (+20%), beating estimates by 7.3%.

**Cash Flow Quality:** Operating cash flow of $3.35B in 2025 (+21% YoY) exceeded net income of $1.49B (OCF/NI ratio of 2.3x). However, free cash flow is deeply negative at -$775M given $4.13B in capex. FCF will remain negative throughout the investment cycle — this is structural and expected for a utility in aggressive rate base growth mode.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 3.62x and $18.2B in long-term debt are appropriate for a regulated utility (where allowed returns are earned on the rate base financed largely with debt). Credit ratings are solid investment-grade: Baa1/BBB+ at the holdco level, A2/A for Ameren Missouri secured obligations. The $3.2B credit facility (extended to December 2030) provides adequate liquidity.

**Interest Expense Trajectory:** Interest costs grew $113M YoY in 2025 (to $776M), and parent company losses widened to $145M from $83M — reflecting the financing cost of the capital plan. With $30.5–$33.1B in planned capex requiring ongoing debt issuance, interest expense will continue rising and represents a headwind to earnings growth if long-term rates remain elevated above 5%.

**Dilution:** Shares outstanding grew from 258.4M (2022) to 270.5M (2025), and the company plans $4B in additional equity issuances (2026–2030). On a $31B market cap, this implies ~14–15% additional dilution over the plan period. The per-share growth rate is lower than the rate base growth rate because of this dilution.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth:** From $29.8B (year-end 2025) at a projected 10.6% CAGR through 2030, representing one of the most aggressive capital plans in the regulated sector. Key investment categories include:
- Renewable generation (solar, battery) and gas peaking plants
- MISO long-range transmission projects ($1.8B Tranche 1, $1.3B Tranche 2)
- Grid modernization and distribution reliability
- Nuclear life extension and potential new nuclear (1,500 MW by 2040)

**Data Center Tailwind:** 2.2 GW in executed Electric Service Agreements with 12-year terms, 80% minimum demand charges, and exit fees. An additional 3.4 GW in total construction agreements signed with developers. This represents genuine incremental load growth — a rarity for U.S. utilities — but comes with an earnings sharing mechanism at 9.74% ROE that caps upside (65% of excess returned to customers).

**EPS Growth:** Company guides 6–8% adjusted EPS CAGR (2026–2030) off a $5.35 midpoint base. FY2026 consensus is $5.37 (current year) and $5.84 (next year), implying ~9% growth. The 6–8% range accounts for dilution, regulatory uncertainty, and execution variability.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year estimates are stable (+0.1% over 90 days). Next-year estimates have moved modestly higher (+0.9% over 90 days). No meaningful directional shift — consensus is not accelerating or deteriorating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $111.64, AEE trades at 20.9x forward earnings ($5.35 FY2025 EPS). This is precisely at the peer median of 20.9x (DTE), above CMS (20.6x), and well below CNP (26.3x) and NI (24.7x).

Whether AEE's premium growth rate (10.6% rate base CAGR vs. typical peer 7–8%) justifies a premium multiple depends on whether execution risk and dilution offset this advantage. At current levels, the market has priced the growth relatively fairly:

- **Consensus mean target:** $120.60 (8% upside)
- **Consensus median:** $122.00 (9% upside)
- **P/E at consensus FY2027E ($5.84):** 19.1x — compression toward peer average
- **Dividend yield:** 2.7% — below DTE (3.1%), above PCG (1.2%), in line with NI (2.5%)

The multiple doesn't offer a margin of safety. PCG at 12.7x offers a discount but reflects specific risk. CMS at 20.6x with lower growth arguably represents worse value. AEE at 20.9x with 10.6% rate base growth is reasonable but not cheap — the stock trades within 3% of its 52-week high.

**Why the market is not clearly wrong:** The earnings sharing mechanism on data center load caps upside surprise potential. The $4B in planned equity dilutes future EPS growth below rate base growth. The Illinois ROE of 8.72% is below cost of equity. These factors collectively prevent a material "expectations gap" — the consensus already reflects the high-growth plan tempered by these constraints.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameren occupies a structurally advantaged position among regulated utilities:

1. **Regulatory framework superiority:** PISA (Missouri), PPRA (extending PISA to 2035 with CWIP treatment for generation), MYRP with revenue balancing (Illinois), and FERC formula rates (transmission) collectively minimize regulatory lag across all segments.

2. **Data center positioning:** Missouri's land availability, reliable grid, and competitive rates position Ameren ahead of capacity-constrained peers for data center demand.

3. **Transmission growth optionality:** MISO's long-range transmission planning process assigns projects based on incumbent position and competitive bidding. Ameren's $3.1B in assigned projects plus $4.4B in remaining competitive opportunities provide visible multi-year growth.

4. **Rate competitiveness:** Ameren Missouri's residential rates sit below the national average, providing headroom for increases to fund the capital plan without triggering political backlash.

The PPRA legislation and data center ESAs represent durable structural gains realized in 2025–2026.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Appeals (High Impact):** Ameren Illinois has filed multiple appeals of ICC orders challenging the 8.72% allowed ROE — well below the 10.48% FERC transmission ROE and below cost-of-equity estimates. Outcomes are binary: success would add meaningful EPS upside (50–75bp ROE improvement translates to ~$0.10–$0.15/share annually); failure would confirm a structural earnings drag on the Illinois electric segment.

**Execution on $30.5–$33.1B Plan:** The largest risk in absolute dollar terms. Supply chain disruption, tariff impacts on solar panels and equipment, labor shortages, and construction delays could compress returns or push timelines. The company explicitly acknowledges tariff risk in filings.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With $18.2B in long-term debt and plans for continued issuance, every 50bp increase in long-term rates costs ~$90M annually in incremental interest. At 30Y Treasury above 5% (current environment), refinancing maturing debt and funding new projects becomes more expensive, directly compressing the spread between allowed ROE and actual cost of capital.

**Dilution:** $4B in planned equity issuance is required to fund the plan. This mechanically limits per-share earnings growth below rate base growth. Consensus already reflects this, but it constrains upside surprise potential.

**Data Center Realization Risk:** The company explicitly warns that "future demand from data centers may not be realized at the current projected pace." A slowdown in AI infrastructure buildout or customer-specific decisions to delay or cancel facilities would reduce load forecasts underpinning generation investment plans.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing material near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.5% is 11.2pp above the 15.3% one-year historical volatility. The term structure is in backwardation (1-week: 26.5%, 3-month: 22.2%, 6-month: 18.1%), which typically signals the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or stress event. Put skew at 10.5% for the nearest expiry indicates elevated demand for downside protection.

Given that Q1 2026 earnings already reported (May 5) and the next earnings date is approximately August 2026, this vol pattern likely reflects one or more of: (a) regulatory ruling expectations on the Illinois appeals, (b) rate environment sensitivity as 30Y yields remain above 5%, or (c) sector-wide utility derating risk from rising real rates. The low P/C OI ratio of 0.20 suggests limited outstanding put open interest in absolute terms — the elevated IV may reflect thin liquidity rather than massive institutional hedging.

This divergence between equity price (near 52-week high) and options pricing (elevated downside concern) supports maintaining HOLD rather than upgrading to BUY.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($122–$136, +9% to +22%):**
- Illinois regulatory appeals succeed, raising allowed ROE by 50–100bp → $0.10–$0.25/share annual earnings uplift
- Data center load materializes faster than plan → higher load growth reduces dilution per unit of rate base
- Interest rates moderate → lower financing costs improve earned ROE spreads
- MISO transmission competitive bids awarded → extends growth runway beyond current plan
- Multiple re-rates toward CNP/NI (24–26x) on premium growth recognition

**Bear Case ($95–$105, -6% to -15%):**
- Illinois appeals fail, confirming 8.72% ROE as permanent
- Interest rates rise further, compressing earned-allowed ROE spread below 100bp
- Capex plan faces material delays/cost overruns from tariffs or supply chain
- Data center customers delay or reduce demand commitments
- FERC eliminates 50bp RTO incentive adder (-$19M/year)
- Market de-rates utilities broadly as 30Y yields sustain above 5%

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock corrects to $100–$103 range (18.5–19.0x forward P/E) without fundamental deterioration — creates 15%+ upside to consensus target
2. Illinois appellate court rules favorably on ROE appeal — quantifiable EPS uplift of $0.10–$0.25/share that isn't priced in
3. Next rate case filing (Missouri or Illinois) signals ROE improvement to >10% — would confirm regulatory direction

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Illinois appeals result in adverse ruling confirming or lowering the 8.72% ROE permanently
2. Data center ESA customers exercise material cancellation provisions (reducing committed load below 1.5 GW)
3. Interest expense growth exceeds 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating capital plan funding costs are outpacing earned returns

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural assessment point is Q2 2026 earnings (~August 2026), which will provide updated capex execution data, financing costs, and regulatory calendar clarity. Illinois appellate rulings could arrive at any time — no statutory deadline.

## Conclusion

Ameren is a well-positioned regulated utility executing on a credible, premium-growth capital plan. The 10.6% rate base CAGR, data center load agreements, and constructive regulatory framework differentiate it from slower-growth peers. Q1 2026 results confirmed execution momentum.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the absence of valuation asymmetry at current levels. At 20.9x forward earnings — peer-median multiple despite carrying above-average execution risk, planned dilution, and an unresolved Illinois ROE dispute — the stock offers 8–9% upside to consensus targets with identifiable 6–15% downside scenarios. The options market's backwardated term structure and elevated IV reinforce that near-term risk is not fully resolved. Insider sales by the CEO and a Group President in recent months (at $111–$113, approximately current levels) further suggest management does not view the stock as undervalued from this base.

The correct posture is holding for the 6–8% EPS CAGR plus 2.7% dividend yield (total return ~9–11% annually), with readiness to upgrade on a price correction to the $100–$103 range or a favorable Illinois regulatory outcome.
