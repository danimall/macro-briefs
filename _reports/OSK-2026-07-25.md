**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Oshkosh is a diversified specialty-vehicle manufacturer with leading positions in three cyclical end markets: aerial work platforms and telehandlers (Access, ~43% of sales), fire apparatus and airport ground-support (Vocational, ~36%), and defense tactical vehicles plus the USPS Next Generation Delivery Vehicle program (Transport, ~20%). The business carries genuine franchise moats: Pierce is #1 in North American fire apparatus, JLG leads aerial work platforms, and Oshkosh Defense holds several sole-source DoD platforms, supported by an investment-grade balance sheet (BBB/Baa3, debt/cap 19.5%) and a 12-year record of double-digit dividend increases.

The near-term problem is earnings, not solvency. Q1 2026 was a sharp miss: EPS fell to $0.68 from $1.72 a year earlier (-60%), operating margin compressed to 3.5% from 7.6% on flat revenue, and gross margin dropped 380bps to 13.5% on adverse mix, tariff-driven material inflation, and higher manufacturing overhead. Access operating income fell 66% as the segment works through a cyclical downturn (backlog -30% in 2025, tariff cost of roughly $23M in the quarter), while Vocational stumbled on fire-apparatus delivery shortfalls and softer refuse volume. Management characterized conditions as "more challenging and dynamic than anticipated" and now expects only about 30% of full-year earnings in the first half, yet reaffirmed a FY26 GAAP EPS of roughly $10.90. That is an aggressive, back-half-loaded plan resting on Access price/cost recovery, fire-apparatus throughput normalization, and a follow-on NGDV order producing a favorable catch-up adjustment.

We rate OSK a HOLD (uncertain). At $155.14 the stock trades at ~14x current-year consensus of $10.90 and ~11x next-year $13.95, a discount to the peer-median P/E of ~20.9x that is partly justified by the earnings risk embedded in the guide and by broadly falling estimate revisions. There is no clearly identifiable catalyst to force a directional call before the H2 execution story resolves. The scoring trajectory has been steadily declining (6.3 in April to 5.3 in June), and the Q1 data confirm rather than reverse that deterioration in growth and near-term earnings quality. We continue to hold.

## Company Overview

Oshkosh generated roughly $10.42B of revenue in 2025 across three segments:

- **Access (43%, $4.49B)** — JLG aerial work platforms and telehandlers, SkyTrak, Jerr-Dan towing/recovery. Sold primarily to equipment-rental companies and contractors. The most cyclical segment, tied to non-residential construction. Market leader against Terex/Genie, Skyjack/Linamar, Haulotte, and Chinese entrants XCMG and Dingli.
- **Vocational (36%, $3.73B)** — Pierce fire apparatus (#1 in North America), Oshkosh AeroTech airport ground-support (acquired 2023 for $805M), ARFF vehicles, McNeilus refuse trucks, IMT cranes. Highest-margin, longest-cycle segment, with a $6.6B backlog and demonstrated pricing power in fire apparatus.
- **Transport (20%, $2.10B)** — Oshkosh Defense tactical wheeled vehicles (HEMTT, FHTV, FMTV, JLTV/L-ATV, frequently sole-source to DoD) plus the USPS NGDV postal delivery vehicle program, which grew 352% in 2025.

About 20% of consolidated sales are to the U.S. government. The diversification across construction, defense, municipal, and aviation end markets is the core structural advantage, but each of those markets is itself cyclical, and 2025-2026 has seen the largest segment (Access) turn down at the same time the newest program (NGDV) is ramping with unproven economics.

Oshkosh rode a strong post-COVID cyclical recovery from 2022 to 2024, then turned down in 2025: revenue -2.9%, operating income -7%. The driver was a 13% contraction in Access (Caterpillar telehandler agreement expired, softer construction, sales discounts up $118M), partly offset by Vocational (+12.6% revenue, margin +270bps to 14.7%) and the NGDV ramp (+$365M).

The balance sheet is solid. Total debt of $1.1B against debt/cap of 19.5%, current ratio 1.94, and investment-grade ratings across all three agencies. FY25 operating cash flow of $783M benefited from customer advances and roughly $90M of OBBBA tax deferral. ROE was 14.3%, ROA 6.4%. The Altman Z of 2.54 sits in the grey zone, which reflects the manufacturing-heavy, asset-intensive structure rather than any distress signal. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: $278M of buybacks in 2025, share count reduced from roughly 64.6M to 62.5M, and a 12th consecutive year of double-digit dividend increases (now $0.57/quarter, ~1.5% yield).

The Q1 2026 segment detail: Access operating income -66% to $34.7M (3.7% margin, down from 10.8%); Vocational operating income -28% to $84.7M on fire-apparatus delivery shortfalls and refuse volume down $55M; Transport improved modestly to $4.2M as NGDV ramped (+$163M) and JLTV wound down.

The earnings surprise record confirms deteriorating visibility: after beats of +14.6% (Jun'25) and +3.1% (Sep'25), the company missed by -3.2% in Dec'25 and by a substantial -27.8% in Mar'26. A streak of beats turning into a large miss signals the guidance framework has lost precision at the exact moment management is asking investors to underwrite a back-half inflection.

A March 2026 refinancing established a new $1.6B five-year revolver maturing March 2031 (leverage covenant max 3.75x, well inside current ~2.2x D/E); the $500M term loan due March 2027 is now classified current, a manageable near-term refinancing item rather than a cliff.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is currently negative in aggregate. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised down 19.5% over 90 days (to $2.61 from $3.24) and 2.1% over 30 days; current-year estimates are down 2.8% over 90 days to $10.90, and next-year down 2.0% to $13.95. The one bright spot is next-quarter, up 3.8% over 90 days, consistent with the back-half-loaded plan. The revision trend is broadly falling: negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the estimate-revision signal that would support a more constructive stance.

The forward story has three moving parts:

1. **Access** must inflect on price/cost recovery. This is the largest segment and the most operating-leveraged to the current downturn. The structural loss of Caterpillar telehandler volume is a headwind that price recovery alone will not offset, and Chinese importers (XCMG, Dingli) are pressuring pricing.

2. **Vocational** is the most durable growth driver, on a $6.6B backlog and robust municipal fire-apparatus demand with pricing power. The Q1 stumble was throughput-related (weather, travel disruption, manufacturing pace) rather than demand-driven, which is more recoverable, but throughput recovery is an execution question.

3. **NGDV** is genuinely growing but carries an $813M deferred-cost balance and a roughly $125M "underwater" position where deferred costs exceed future profits on existing orders. Only 51,500 of a potential 165,000 vehicles have been ordered. FY26 guidance explicitly assumes a follow-on order with a favorable catch-up adjustment. If USPS under-orders or shifts mix, this becomes an impairment risk rather than a growth tailwind.

Net, the durable component is Vocational fire-apparatus demand and the multi-year defense modernization cycle. The rest of the FY26 growth thesis is a bet that several favorable outcomes converge in the second half. That is not a high-durability growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the peer-median P/E of 20.9x (Construction Machinery & Heavy Transportation Equipment: TEX 43.3x, FSS 25.1x, GATX 18.5x, RUSHA 20.9x, TRN 10.8x), Oshkosh's ~14x current-year and ~11x next-year multiples look cheap on the surface.

The discount is not obviously a mispricing. It reflects three things the market is pricing correctly: (a) the FY26 EPS number is at real risk given the Q1 miss and the back-half-loaded plan, so the "14x" may be built on an estimate that proves too high; (b) estimate revisions are falling, not rising; and (c) the earnings base is cyclically elevated by defense/NGDV timing and prone to lumpy percentage-of-completion catch-up adjustments (negative $37.9M in 2025, negative $46.9M in 2024). A "cheap" multiple on a number that may not hold is not a compelling entry.

Applying our valuation discipline: this is a downgrade-cycle name with falling estimates, so we do not treat consensus targets as floors and give cheapness limited credit. Analyst targets span $138 low to $197 high (mean $163, median $168), placing the stock roughly 5-8% below consensus mean/median, which is modest and consistent with a fairly valued position rather than a clear opportunity.

Our fair-value framing: on a base-case normalized EPS closer to $10.50-11.00 (reflecting execution risk on the guide) at a mid-cycle 14-15x multiple appropriate for a cyclical industrial in a downturn, base value sits around $155-165, essentially where the stock trades. Upside to a $185-195 bull case requires the H2 inflection and NGDV follow-on to materialize; downside to $120-130 (near the 52-week low of $116.77) opens up if the guide is cut. That symmetry supports a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Oshkosh holds strong-to-leading positions in purpose-built niches where scale, engineering, vertical integration, and dealer/service networks create defensible moats. Pierce leads North American fire apparatus; JLG leads aerial work platforms; Oshkosh Defense holds sole-source positions on multiple tactical-vehicle platforms.

The competitive trajectory is mixed and tilting slightly negative in the near term. In Access, Chinese importers are pricing below market and the loss of the Caterpillar telehandler relationship removed structural volume; the largest, historically most profitable segment is losing ground on both volume and pricing. In defense, a January 2026 executive order prioritizing "the warfighter" (speed, affordability, domestic sourcing) plus increasing government demands for design rights (FMTV A2) raise recompete risk on platforms that have been reliably sole-source. Offsetting these, Vocational's fire-apparatus franchise remains a genuine share-and-pricing strength with a large backlog, and the defense modernization cycle is a structural tailwind.

The five-year cumulative TSR of 186 slightly trailed the peer group's 192.4, consistent with a stable-to-modestly-eroding competitive position rather than clear share gains. Per our sector calibration, Industrials BUYs carry negative default alpha, so we require a distinct company catalyst before rewarding competitive positioning here, and the current evidence points to trajectory stabilization at best, not expansion.

## Risk Assessment

The most important idiosyncratic risks, ranked by potential to move the stock in ways not fully priced:

1. **FY26 guidance risk.** The reaffirmed ~$10.90 EPS requires a strong second-half inflection dependent on Access price/cost recovery, fire-apparatus throughput normalization, and a favorable NGDV follow-on order. A cut is the largest near-term downside catalyst.

2. **Tariffs.** Tariff cost is guided to roughly $200M in 2026 (~$1.90+/share gross), up from $35M in 2025. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized produced a $19.7M refund receivable ($13.5M in Q1 operating income), but expanded Section 232 tariffs offset the benefit, leaving the net 2026 impact roughly unchanged. The situation is fluid in both directions.

3. **NGDV impairment risk.** The ~$125M underwater deferred-cost position and USPS's ability to terminate for convenience or under-order create asymmetric downside if the follow-on order does not materialize on favorable terms.

4. **Transport earnings quality.** Percentage-of-completion accounting has produced negative cumulative catch-up adjustments two years running ($37.9M in 2025, $46.9M in 2024): lumpy, estimate-driven earnings.

5. **Defense EO capital-return constraint.** The 10-K flags that the January 2026 executive order could create disincentives limiting Oshkosh's ability to repurchase stock or pay dividends, an underappreciated governance/capital-allocation risk for a company built partly on a 12-year dividend-growth record.

6. **Customer/dealer concentration.** Access's ten largest debtors are ~27% of consolidated receivables; $580M of customer-indebtedness guarantees (max exposure $100.7M).

The concentration of binary/near-binary items (guidance credibility, tariff resolution, NGDV order, defense recompete) argues for a below-average risk score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185-195).** Vocational fire-apparatus throughput normalizes and its backlog converts at expanding margins; Access price actions recover the tariff/cost drag as the construction cycle stabilizes; the USPS places a follow-on NGDV order producing a favorable catch-up adjustment; and the FY26 guide of ~$10.90 GAAP / ~$11.50 adjusted proves achievable, setting up the $13.95 next-year consensus. In that scenario, a re-rating toward the peer-median multiple on a credible ~$13-14 forward number supports $185-195, with the median analyst target of $168 revised higher.

**Bear Case ($120-130).** The Q1 miss extends into H2, the guide is cut mid-year, Access remains in a deeper downturn than management expects (Chinese pricing, structural Caterpillar-volume loss), the NGDV follow-on order slips or arrives on worse terms triggering a deferred-cost write-down, and tariffs surprise to the upside. EPS lands closer to $9-9.50 and the multiple compresses on cyclical fear, dragging the stock toward its 52-week low.

**Base Case ($155-165).** The company muddles through a mediocre year: partial H2 recovery, EPS in the $10.00-10.75 range (a modest miss versus the $10.90 guide), Vocational carrying the load while Access stays weak. The stock trades roughly flat at a fair 14-15x normalized cyclical earnings, with the dividend providing a small floor.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided, which supports the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (roughly early August), which will provide the first read on whether the back-half inflection is materializing. The FY26 full-year print and any interim NGDV order news are the subsequent triggers.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 results confirm the H2 inflection — sequential Access margin recovery back above ~7% and reaffirmed (or raised) FY26 guidance with visible fire-apparatus throughput improvement.
- A confirmed USPS follow-on NGDV order on economics that eliminate the underwater deferred-cost position and turn the catch-up adjustment favorable.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus stops falling and rises) alongside a pullback that widens the discount to peers — e.g., the stock below ~$135 on unchanged or improving fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A mid-year FY26 guidance cut, particularly one implying full-year EPS below ~$9.50.
- A material NGDV deferred-cost impairment charge, or USPS reducing/canceling planned order volume.
- Access backlog and margin deteriorating further in Q2 (segment margin below ~4% again), signaling the downturn is structural rather than cyclical.
- Activation of the defense EO capital-return constraint (a dividend cut or buyback suspension).

## Conclusion

Oshkosh is a well-run, investment-grade industrial with authentic franchise moats and a strong capital-return history, currently in an earnings air-pocket. The Q1 2026 miss, the ~$200M tariff drag, the Access downturn, and the heavily back-half-loaded FY26 guide combine to make the near-term earnings path uncertain, and consensus estimates are falling across most windows. The ~14x current-year multiple is cheap versus peers but that discount is largely earned by the risk embedded in a guidance number that may not hold. The scoring trajectory has declined steadily since April, and the new data confirm that trend rather than reverse it.

We maintain a HOLD (uncertain). The growth momentum is negative, the guide is precarious, and there is no insider conviction buying, which rules out a compelling long; the solid balance sheet, real franchise value, and possibility of a genuine H2 recovery rule out a clear short. The risk/reward is balanced around the current price. We would turn constructive on confirmation of the H2 inflection (particularly a favorable NGDV follow-on order and Access margin recovery) or on a meaningful pullback that widens the peer discount, and negative on a guidance cut or a deferred-cost impairment. The Q2 print is the key event to watch.
