**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SLB is the largest oilfield-services company in the world, operating in more than 100 countries with roughly 82% of revenue generated outside the United States. The investment question at $56 — near the 52-week high of $58.82 and up ~57% from the June 2025 low — is whether the franchise quality and an asserted 2027-2028 upstream recovery justify holding through a soft, oversupplied services cycle that is currently compressing organic revenue and margins.

The case for patience is straightforward: SLB is the scale leader with an unmatched international footprint, a deepening Digital/AI moat (28% pretax margin, +121% Data Center Solutions growth), and a disciplined capital-return program exceeding $4 billion annually with a 2.1% yield. The case for caution is equally concrete: organic revenue fell ~6% in FY25 and ~7% in Q1'26, net income dropped 24% to $3.37 billion, free cash flow turned negative (-$23 million) in Q1'26, and the all-stock ChampionX acquisition diluted share count ~5%, pushing EPS down from $0.58 to $0.50 year-over-year. The recovery narrative rests on a 2027-2028 timeline that remains forecast rather than fact.

The market has already seen the bad news. Q1'26 results, the negative FCF print, and the Middle East demobilization in Qatar and Iraq were all public by late April 2026, and the stock held the high-$50s through May and June rather than de-rating. Estimate revisions, which fell sharply on a 90-day basis (-9% current year), have nearly stopped on a 30-day basis (-0.4% current year, +0.3% next year). With 25 of 29 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy, a $62-63 mean/median target implying ~10% upside, and a forward multiple (~16.8x next-year EPS) that is mid-range for oilfield services rather than stretched, the asymmetry is balanced. We rate SLB HOLD.

## Company Overview

Following its FY2025 reporting change, SLB operates five divisions:

- **Production Systems** ($13.3B, 37% of revenue) — completions, artificial lift, surface systems, OneSubsea, and now ChampionX production chemicals. The strategic growth engine and the company's pivot toward the less-cyclical production-and-recovery market.
- **Well Construction** ($11.9B, 33%) — drilling-related and the most cyclically exposed division; revenue fell 11% YoY in FY25.
- **Reservoir Performance** ($6.8B, 19%) — evaluation, stimulation, intervention.
- **Digital** ($2.7B, 7%) — newly broken out as a standalone segment; the highest-margin business at 28% pretax margin, anchored by the Lumi data/AI platform.
- **All Other** — Asset Performance Solutions, Data Center Solutions (modular data-center manufacturing sold to hyperscaler customers), and the SLB Capturi carbon-capture JV.

The strategic thesis is a deliberate de-emphasis of pure drilling exposure in favor of production/recovery (via ChampionX) and high-margin digital. The legal entity is now SLB Limited, Curaçao-domiciled, with debt guaranteed via Schlumberger Investment S.A.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $35.7B FY25, down 2% YoY from $36.3B. Stripping out the ChampionX contribution (+$1.5B), organic revenue declined ~6%, a genuine cyclical contraction driven by international weakness in Saudi Arabia, Mexico, and offshore Sub-Saharan Africa.

**Profitability:** Net income fell to $3.37B, down 24% from $4.46B, absorbing roughly $1.1B of pretax charges: workforce reductions, ChampionX merger/integration costs, a $210M goodwill impairment on the SLB Capturi JV, and inventory purchase-accounting amortization. Net margin was 9.7%, ROE 13.2%, ROA 6.3%.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow held steady at $6.5B; free cash flow was $4.1B by management's definition. Capital discipline is intact: capex of ~$1.7B, total capital investments of $2.4B, guided to ~$2.5B for 2026.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets $54.9B; equity $26.1B; net debt $7.4B (flat YoY). Current ratio 1.33, D/E ~1.06, appropriate for an asset-heavy services business. Goodwill plus intangibles now total $21.8B, roughly 40% of assets, elevated by ChampionX and the prior Aker subsea deal. The SLB Capturi impairment demonstrates this exposure is not theoretical. Altman Z of 1.99 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for the sector and not a distress signal given $6.5B OCF and ~$5B of undrawn credit facilities.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue $8.7B (+3% YoY with ChampionX, -7% organic). The standout negative was free cash flow of -$23M (vs +$103M in Q1'25), with net debt rising to $8.2B on seasonal working-capital build plus conflict disruption. EPS fell to $0.50 from $0.58. Share count rose to ~1.5B from 1.42B as a result of the all-stock ChampionX deal, which is dilutive to per-share metrics. Backlog was $5.9B.

The negative-FCF quarter deserves context rather than alarm: full-year FCF has held near $4B for three consecutive years, and Q1 is seasonally the weakest. But it is a reminder that the buyback pace ($2.4B in FY25) is not always self-funding in soft quarters.

**Capital returns:** ~$4.0B returned in FY25 ($1.6B dividends, $2.4B buybacks); the dividend was raised 3.5% to $0.295/quarter in January 2026. Management has committed to >$4B of returns in 2026, with $6.3B of the $10B buyback authorization used through Q1'26.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative on an organic basis, with the most cyclical division (Well Construction) seeing Q1'26 pretax margin compress 463bps YoY to 15%. Headline Q1'26 revenue "growth" of +3% is entirely ChampionX; the underlying business contracted.

Estimate revisions tell a stabilizing story. The 90-day cuts were steep — current-year EPS down 9% to $2.60, current-quarter down 20.7% — but the 30-day data shows the bleeding has nearly stopped: current-year -0.4%, next-quarter -1.0%, and next-year +0.3% to $3.34. Consensus has largely caught down to the soft cycle, which removes "continued cuts" as a high-probability forward driver. The EPS trajectory from $2.60 (FY26) to $3.34 (FY27) implies the consensus already embeds the recovery the company forecasts.

The growth optionality sits in three places. First, **Digital** at 28% pretax margins growing into a structurally higher-margin mix. Second, **Data Center Solutions**, which management calls its "fastest growing business for years to come" (+121% FY25, +45% YoY Q1'26), monetizing modular manufacturing and power/cooling engineering for the AI buildout — though at only ~$460M FY25 revenue it is optionality, not yet a needle-mover. Third, the **2027-2028 upstream recovery thesis**: SLB argues the Middle East conflict accelerates supply/demand rebalancing, that post-conflict oil prices settle above pre-conflict levels, and that deepwater (Brazil, Guyana) is "accelerating regardless of the conflict." This is the hinge: credible given SLB's footprint, but forecast rather than booked.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation analysis requires correcting the peer set. The named comparables — WMB (midstream, 31.4x), VLO (18.6x), MPC (17.0x), PSX (17.6x), all refiners, and EOG (13.5x, E&P) — contain no oilfield-services company. Refiners and midstream operators structurally trade at low-to-mid-teens multiples because they are commodity-margin, lower-growth businesses; comparing SLB to them produces an artificial "premium" that says nothing about relative value within oilfield services.

Measured against true oilfield-services peers, SLB at ~24x current-year and ~16.8x next-year EPS sits at the upper end of the sub-industry range but is broadly justified by its larger scale, broader international footprint, and richer Digital mix. This is full valuation, not a distressed premium, for the sub-industry leader. [Halliburton and Baker Hughes specific forward multiples — unverified: not in provided sources]

Cross-checking against fundamentals: at $56 on $3.34 FY27 consensus, the forward multiple is ~16.8x for a business consensus expects to grow EPS ~28% from FY26 to FY27, implying a PEG well below 1.0 if that recovery materializes, but the recovery is exactly what is uncertain. On trailing/current-year earnings ($2.35-2.60), the ~22-24x multiple is full for a business with organic revenue contracting. The 2.1% dividend yield, below the 2.8% peer median but covered by $4B+ of FCF, provides modest downside support.

Analyst targets ($62.36 mean / $63 median, $43-71 range) imply ~10% upside to the mean. Given the stock trades below 30x forward and revisions have stabilized, these targets are not stale optimism, but neither do they describe an asymmetric setup: ~10% upside against a downside that the Hormuz tail could reopen is balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

SLB's competitive position is the strongest element of the thesis. It is the largest oilfield-services company globally, with the deepest international footprint, particularly in the Middle East, technology leadership through its "fit-for-basin" approach, and a widening digital/AI moat. The Digital division's 28% pretax margin and the standalone Data Center Solutions business represent genuine differentiation.

The NVIDIA partnership (expanded March 2026) develops AI infrastructure and models for the energy industry; it is a collaboration, with the energy-AI products belonging to SLB, distinct from SLB's separate Data Center Solutions manufacturing business that supplies hyperscaler customers. Both reinforce the diversification-away-from-drilling narrative.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in mix terms even as the cycle pressures volumes. The pivot toward production/recovery via ChampionX structurally reduces drilling-cycle dependence, and the international footprint that is the company's biggest near-term exposure (via Middle East demobilization) is also its biggest leverage to a regional recovery. We score competitive position above sector neutral but cap it because the same scale that confers advantage also concentrates conflict and country risk.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Middle East conflict (dominant near-term risk).** SLB demobilized operations in multiple countries to protect personnel. The Middle East is ~70% of the $2.7B Middle East & Asia quarterly revenue, with the impact concentrated in Well Construction and Reservoir Performance. The 10-Q Risk Factors state "no material changes," but the MD&A discusses the conflict extensively, making it a live operational risk not yet codified as a standalone factor.

2. **Commodity cyclicality.** Demand tracks customer E&P capex; an extended downturn would pressure Well Construction most.

3. **Integration/goodwill risk.** $21.8B of goodwill+intangibles (~40% of assets); the $210M SLB Capturi impairment is a cautionary precedent if ChampionX synergies fall short.

4. **Pemex credit risk.** SLB has written ~$0.3B of credit default swaps tied to its Mexican customer's borrowings used to pay SLB's own receivables, an unusual structure signaling collection stress with a key national oil company. Russia remains ~4% of revenue with ~$0.7B net assets on the books.

5. **Energy transition / capital access.** Risk that funding shifts away from fossil-fuel-linked companies and that SLB mis-times the transition.

6. **FX exposure.** 82% non-US revenue, hedged via cross-currency swaps.

The two-sided nature of the Middle East exposure is central. A verified, sustained reopening of regional operations would relieve the demobilization drag and let suspended operations resume, but it would also cap the oil-price tailwind the recovery thesis depends on. A prolonged conflict supports prices but extends the operational disruption. Neither outcome is clearly favorable to the share price at current levels, which is the core reason for a HOLD rather than a directional call.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 86.3% against 33.6% one-year historical volatility — extremely rich (52.7pp spread), at the 86th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in steep backwardation (86.3% near-term vs 39.1% at six months). This is event-hedging, consistent with a known binary (a regional catalyst or earnings event) rather than a directional informed bet. The put/call skew is balanced at 0.9% near-term and only modestly negative further out (-2.6% at three months, -3.3% at six months), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.37 and OI ratio of 0.42 are call-tilted, not bearish. The options market is pricing imminent uncertainty without a directional bias to the downside, which corroborates the two-sided HOLD framing. The IV spike since late May (40.5% → 66.0% ATM) reflects the near-term binary repricing, not a fundamental deterioration signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($66-71):** The 2027-2028 upstream recovery materializes. A post-conflict oil-price level above pre-conflict, combined with deepwater acceleration in Brazil and Guyana and strategic-reserve replenishment, drives a broad-based upstream spending cycle. SLB's international scale captures disproportionate share, organic growth turns positive, ChampionX synergies land, Digital and Data Center Solutions compound at 28%+ margins, and FY27 EPS reaches or exceeds $3.34. At 21x that figure, the stock approaches $70. Capital returns continue above $4B.

**Bear Case ($43-46):** The conflict drags on or de-escalates in a way that caps oil prices without restoring activity. Organic revenue keeps contracting, Well Construction margins stay compressed, the 2027-2028 recovery slips, and a goodwill impairment on ChampionX surfaces. Net debt rises further on weak FCF quarters, the buyback throttles back, and the multiple compresses. EPS settles near $2.40-2.60, and at 17-18x the stock retraces to the low-$40s, the analyst-low target.

**Base Case ($60-62):** Estimates have stabilized, the soft cycle is largely priced, and the stock grinds modestly higher toward consensus as the 2027 recovery comes into view but before it is confirmed. EPS recovers toward $3.00-3.34 over 18 months; a ~19-20x multiple on improving earnings supports ~$60-62, broadly in line with the analyst mean and ~7-10% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for Q2 FCF normalization and Middle East operational commentary; the regional conflict situation on any verified de-escalation signal; and the FY27 capex/recovery framing on the year-end call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth turns positive YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the recovery thesis is real rather than forecast.
- Q2/Q3 free cash flow returns to a normalized ~$1B+ quarterly run-rate with net debt declining, removing the buyback-funding concern.
- Forward EPS revisions inflect positive (30-day and 90-day both above +5%) alongside a pullback toward the low-$50s that restores an asymmetric entry, particularly on a verified de-escalation that resolves the demobilization without collapsing oil.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A ChampionX or further SLB Capturi-style goodwill impairment, signaling the acquisition-driven balance-sheet expansion is not earning its cost.
- Two consecutive quarters of negative or near-zero FCF forcing a buyback suspension or net-debt breach above ~$9B.
- Current-year and next-year EPS revisions resume falling more than 5% over 90 days, indicating the recovery thesis is unwinding rather than stabilizing.

## Conclusion

SLB is the highest-quality franchise in oilfield services, and the prior AVOID framing rested on a flawed comparison to refiners and midstream operators that structurally trade at lower multiples than services companies. Corrected against true oilfield-services peers, the ~16.8x next-year multiple is reasonable for the scale leader. The bear catalyst — Q1'26 organic decline, negative FCF, and Middle East demobilization — was fully public by late April and the stock held the high-$50s rather than de-rating, which means the bearish case has had its chance and largely failed to fire. Estimate cuts have stabilized, capital returns exceed $4B with a 2.1% yield floor, and 25 of 29 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy.

We do not rate this a BUY. Organic revenue is still contracting, the recovery rests on a 2027-2028 timeline that is forecast rather than booked, ChampionX dilution is masking the per-share story, and the Middle East exposure is genuinely two-sided: a de-escalation relieves operations but caps oil prices, while a grind supports prices but extends the disruption. With ~7-10% upside to consensus against a low-$40s downside in the bear case, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate SLB **HOLD** and would revisit on either a confirmed positive organic-growth inflection (upgrade) or a goodwill impairment and FCF deterioration that forces a buyback pause (downgrade).
