**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hamilton Lane is a pure-play private-markets investment-solutions firm that earns the bulk of its revenue from recurring management and advisory fees on roughly $142B of discretionary AUM, supplemented by fixed-fee advisory on a further $905B of assets under advisement. FY2026 (year ended 3/31/2026) was an operationally strong year: fee-related earnings (FRE) grew 25% to $344.5M at a ~50% margin, management and advisory fees grew 14% to $584.2M, fee-earning AUM grew 13%, and the firm raised its dividend 11% and expanded its buyback authorization to $100M. Despite this, the stock has fallen roughly 40% from its 52-week high of ~$161 (reached in August 2025) to ~$82, while the broad market rose. The decline reflects a sector-wide de-rating of listed private-markets managers on rate fears and concerns about valuation marks, private-credit health, and liquidity mismatches in evergreen vehicles, rather than company-specific fundamental deterioration.

The central investment question is whether the ~40% drawdown prices a genuine impairment of the franchise or an overshoot driven by sentiment toward the alternatives complex. The evidence favors overshoot on the durable part of the business. The recurring management-fee base, tied to committed capital and accounting for 75%+ of revenue over the past five years, is structurally insulated from the redemption risk the bears cite. The headline "revenue miss" optics in Q4 (-2% total revenue) were entirely a function of a 45% drop in lumpy incentive fees; recurring management fees grew 21% in the same quarter. The most direct insider signal in the file is a broad open-market buying cluster: Co-Chairmen Hartley Rogers (~$13M) and Mario Giannini, both Co-CEOs, the COO, and a director, purchasing from $107 down to $76, which contradicts the bearish tape.

We rate HLNE a BUY at ~$82 with disciplined sizing. At ~13.9x trailing/forward EPS the stock trades at roughly a 58% discount to the asset-manager peer median of 33.1x, with a PEG below 1.0 against a multi-year FRE compounding rate near 20%, and every covering analyst's target sits above the current price (mean ~$136, median ~$141, low ~$102). The principal qualifier is a real higher-for-longer rate headwind that compresses both the growth velocity of fundraising and the equity multiple investors will pay; the score reflects that the asymmetry is favorable but not riskless, with evergreen redemption behavior the key thing to monitor.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1991 and headquartered in Conshohocken, PA, Hamilton Lane is one of the largest dedicated private-markets investment-solutions firms globally, with ~785 employees across North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific, and the Middle East. It operates as a single reportable segment and oversees ~$1.05 trillion in total assets, split between $141.8B in discretionary AUM (fee-earning) and $905.3B in non-discretionary assets under advisement (largely fixed-fee advisory).

The firm sells access across the private-markets spectrum (private equity, private credit, infrastructure, secondaries, co-investments, impact) through three channels:
- **Specialized funds** — ~$40.6B fee-earning AUM, ~64% of management fees — including traditional drawdown funds and a fast-growing evergreen/perpetual line marketed to private wealth.
- **Customized separate accounts** — ~$40.9B fee-earning AUM, ~24% of management fees — large institutional mandates, often bundled with reporting technology.
- **Advisory and reporting/data** — including the Cobalt analytics platform — ~12% of fees combined.

Its competitive position rests on a three-decade track record, a data moat (visibility into 120+ funds and 3,000+ underlying assets through its advisory and Cobalt footprint), deep GP relationships that provide access to capacity-constrained deals, and a diversified fee base (no client over 2% of fees; top-10 ~11%). The model is asset-light: Hamilton Lane is an intermediary that earns fees on others' capital rather than a balance-sheet investor at scale.

The corporate structure is an Up-C with dual-class voting. HLI's only material asset is its ~77% interest in the operating entity HLA, and insiders/stockholders control ~73-74% of voting power, making HLNE a "controlled company" exempt from certain Nasdaq governance standards.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2026 results show a high-margin, growing recurring franchise partly obscured by a lumpy incentive-fee line.

**Revenue.** Total revenue of $759.0M grew 6% YoY, but this understates the core engine. Management and advisory fees grew 14% to $584.2M, with specialized-fund fees up 19% to $374.4M (driven by evergreen funds) and reporting/data fees up 22% to $35.8M. Incentive fees fell 12% to $174.8M from $199.1M, the volatile component that masked recurring-fee strength. Over five years, recurring management and advisory fees have averaged more than 75% of total revenue.

**Profitability.** FRE, the cleanest read on the core franchise, grew 25% to $344.5M with the margin rising to ~50% from 48%. Adjusted EBITDA grew 11% to $407.6M. Net income attributable to HLI rose 15% to $249.2M; GAAP diluted EPS was $5.92 and non-GAAP EPS $5.90 (+17%). The divergence between FRE growth (+25%) and headline revenue growth (+6%) is the key analytical point: the durable, fee-based earnings stream accelerated even as the lumpy carry line declined. Long-run CAGRs (FY21→FY26) are strong: management fees ~15%, total revenue ~17%, FRE ~20%, net income ~21%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow rose to $424.9M from $300.8M, against minimal capex of $5.8M — high-quality cash conversion well above net income. Cash stood at $361.0M (ex-consolidated funds) against modest debt of $278.4M (term loan, multi-draw facility, and $100M 5.28% senior notes due 2029). The leverage covenant of ≤3.5x leaves ample headroom. The investment balance jumped to $1.30B from $761M, but ~$526M of that is consolidated third-party fund capital (offset by $44.2M of non-controlling interest in net income and corresponding investing-cash-flow swings); core fund co-investments are ~$568M. The reported Altman Z-Score distress flag is a false positive for an asset-light manager: it penalizes low asset turnover and the Up-C structure, while real leverage is minimal and ROE exceeds 40%.

**Hidden asset.** Unrealized (allocated) carried interest was $1.5B as of 3/31/2026, up 23% YoY, spread across 124 vehicles — a large off-balance-sheet reservoir of future incentive income whose only uncertainty is realization timing. Evergreen-fund carry rose 52% to $239.5M and Secondary Fund VI carry rose 83% to $138.4M.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 11% to a $2.40 annualized target (current yield ~2.9%), the buyback authorization was increased to $100M, and ~$20M was repurchased in Q4 FY26 at ~$100/share. A structural cash leak exists in the $235.4M Tax Receivable Agreement payable to pre-IPO owners (85% of realized tax benefits), which also carries change-of-control acceleration risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis centers on the recurring fee engine and, within it, evergreen funds. Management cited more than $265M of evergreen net inflows in April alone, signaling continued acceleration of the highest-value channel into FY27. Evergreen funds drove ~$72M of incremental management fees, ~$6.6B of contributions, a 67% jump in evergreen incentive fees to $116M, and a 52% rise in evergreen allocated carry in FY2026.

The structural tailwind is the "democratization" of private markets — channeling alternatives into private wealth and, following the 2025 executive order opening defined-contribution plans, into 401(k)s via evergreen vehicles. This is a multi-year demand driver with broad industry confirmation (Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, KKR, Blue Owl all pursuing the same channel). For Hamilton Lane, the advisor-plus-data model is a differentiated way to participate, because the $905B of advised assets and the Cobalt platform feed deal flow and benchmarking into its discretionary products.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, including +37.0% in the September 2025 quarter and +27.4% in the June 2025 quarter, with the most recent quarter (March 2026) a more modest +2.2% beat. Estimate revisions are roughly stable rather than rising: current-year consensus is $6.10 (+1.6% over 30 days, -4.8% over 90 days) and next-year $7.40 (-0.6% over 30 days, -5.1% over 90 days). The 90-day softening reflects the rate environment's drag on fundraising velocity and incentive-fee timing, not a break in the recurring-fee trend. The growth thesis is durable but moderating in velocity — strong enough to support a high score, short of the +30% revision-acceleration profile that would mark a sharp acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$82, HLNE trades at ~13.9x trailing P/E (and effectively the same on forward, given $6.10 current-year consensus). Against the Asset Management & Custody Banks peer median P/E of 33.1 (CNS 25.2, AAMI 33.1, WT 40.7, FHI 11.2; StepStone negative on a one-off basis), this is roughly a 58% discount. On a growth-adjusted basis, a ~14x multiple against a ~20% FRE CAGR and high-teens EPS growth produces a PEG below 1.0.

The valuation gap is real but partly justified, which is why the score is strong rather than maximal. Three factors warrant some discount to peers: (1) the higher-for-longer rate path compresses the multiple investors assign to fee-stream growth across the alternatives complex; (2) incentive-fee lumpiness produces quarterly headline volatility that the market penalizes; and (3) the controlled-company governance and TRA cash leakage reduce the per-share value capture for public holders. None of these is new, and all were present when the stock traded at $161, so they do not explain the magnitude of the de-rating on their own.

Analyst targets here function as floors rather than ceilings, given the stock is cheap relative to its growth: the low target of $102 implies ~24% upside, the mean ~$136 implies ~65%, and the high ~$176 implies ~114%. The analyst recommendation distribution remains constructive (2 strong buy, 4 buy, 1 hold), though it has softened modestly (one strong-buy moved to hold over three months), consistent with the rate-driven sector caution rather than a company-specific downgrade. A dividend yield of ~2.9%, covered comfortably by FRE, provides absolute-yield support beneath the valuation.

The valuation score reflects a genuine, identifiable mispricing — a recurring franchise compounding FRE in the mid-20s%, available at a P/E roughly 58% below peers — tempered by the recognition that being cheap alone is not a thesis, and that the rate headwind is a real reason the multiple may stay compressed for some time.

## Competitive Landscape

Hamilton Lane competes against both larger alternative managers (Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, KKR, Blue Owl) and other allocators and advisors (StepStone, GCM Grosvenor). Its differentiation is the combination of advisory scale and proprietary data: by advising on $905B of non-discretionary assets and running the Cobalt platform, it sees deal flow and benchmarking unavailable to most pure-product competitors, reinforcing GP access and informing its discretionary products. The fee base is diversified, with no client over 2% of fees.

The competitive trajectory is favorable on the metrics that matter: the firm is gaining share in the fastest-growing channel (evergreen), and specialized-fund fees rose 19%. The principal competitive risk is that the same wealth and retirement channel is being chased by far larger balance sheets, which could compress economics over time and raise distribution costs. For now, Hamilton Lane's data-and-advisory moat and three-decade track record position it as a differentiated participant rather than a price-taker, supporting a positive but not dominant competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Evergreen concentration and redemption risk (central).** Evergreens are now the primary growth engine but, unlike locked-up drawdown funds, carry investor redemption/repurchase rights. A liquidity event, gating, or NAV error in daily/interval-priced vehicles holding illiquid assets could trigger outflows and reputational damage. This is the single most important risk to monitor. The mitigant is that this risk attaches to roughly a quarter of revenue (evergreen/incentive-linked), while the 75%+ committed-capital management-fee base is structurally insulated.

**Incentive-fee lumpiness.** Realization timing is unpredictable, producing recurring "headline miss" noise that the market periodically penalizes.

**Non-contracted fees.** Customized separate-account and advisory fees are not long-term contracted and can be terminated on 30-90 days' notice.

**Valuation lag and marks.** Illiquid private assets are valued on a ~3-month lag and are model-dependent, a focal point of the broader private-markets skepticism narrative.

**Governance.** The Up-C dual-class structure gives insiders ~73-74% voting control and "controlled company" exemptions; the TRA siphons 85% of realized tax benefits to pre-IPO owners and carries change-of-control acceleration risk. Public Class A holders have limited influence.

**Macro/rate sensitivity.** Higher-for-longer rates slow fundraising velocity, pressure private-asset valuations, and compress the equity multiple — the proximate driver of the ~40% drawdown.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning leans constructive rather than fearful. Near-term ATM IV is 53.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 39.7% (rich by ~13.7 points), but the put/call skew is -8.6% (call-heavy) at the 1-month tenor and flattens to roughly neutral further out, with a flat term structure. ATM IV has fallen sharply from 66.8% to 53.4% over the past month even as the price declined ~5%, indicating resolving rather than building uncertainty. The put/call volume ratio of 2.83 looks bearish in isolation but sits against a call-heavy skew, suggesting hedging/income flows rather than directional bearishness. Short interest is elevated at 11.8% of float with 4.4 days to cover — a genuine bearish institutional tilt and a modest squeeze cushion, but not extreme. The net read: the options market is pricing a wide implied move (±12.2% near-term) consistent with a contested, sentiment-driven name, without the put-heavy skew that would signal informed positioning for a sharp decline.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($141-176)

The recurring fee franchise compounds FRE in the high-teens-to-20s%, evergreen net inflows continue accelerating (April +$265M is the leading indicator), incentive fees normalize upward from a depressed FY26 base, and the $1.5B unrealized carry pool begins to realize. As rate fears ease, the alternatives complex re-rates and HLNE's multiple normalizes toward the peer cohort. Applying ~20-22x to forward EPS approaching $7.40 supports $150-176; the high analyst target of $176 implies ~114% upside. Insider conviction (open-market buying from $107 to $76) proves prescient.

### Bear Case ($62)

The private-markets skepticism narrative proves substantially correct: evergreen redemption gates spread to Hamilton Lane's vehicles, NAV marks come down as private-credit and private-equity valuations reset, fundraising velocity stalls under sustained high rates, and incentive fees stay depressed for multiple years. The multiple compresses further toward the low end of asset managers (~10x) on a flat-to-down EPS, taking the stock toward $62, roughly 25% below current levels. The controlled-company structure and TRA leakage cap the value public holders capture in any partial recovery.

The asymmetry favors the bull case: from $82, the bear case implies ~25% downside while the base and bull cases imply 48% and 72-114% upside, against a recurring franchise whose core fee base is insulated from the most-cited risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $122** (~48% upside). This applies ~16.5x to a normalized forward EPS of ~$7.40 — a multiple that closes roughly half the gap to the peer median while still discounting for rate sensitivity, lumpy incentive fees, and governance. It sits between the bear ($62) and bull ($141-176) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $141-176** (~72% to ~114% upside) — multiple re-rating toward the peer cohort on continued FRE compounding and evergreen acceleration.

**Bear case target: $62** (~25% downside) — evergreen gating, NAV resets, and multiple compression to ~10x on stalled growth.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to FY27 quarterly prints (each tests evergreen net flows and FRE) and the trajectory of the rate path. The first one or two quarters confirming continued evergreen inflows and FRE growth would be the catalyst for re-rating.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Evergreen net flows turn negative for two consecutive quarters, or any of Hamilton Lane's evergreen vehicles imposes redemption gating.
2. FRE growth decelerates below mid-single digits YoY, signaling the recurring engine has stalled rather than merely moderated.
3. A material downward revision to private-asset NAV marks that flows through to management-fee-bearing AUM, or current-year EPS consensus falling below ~$5.50.

## Conclusion

Hamilton Lane offers a recurring, high-margin private-markets fee franchise at ~13.9x earnings, roughly a 58% discount to the asset-manager peer median, after a ~40% drawdown driven by sector-wide sentiment rather than company-specific deterioration. The Q4 "revenue miss" was an artifact of lumpy incentive fees, not the recurring engine, which accelerated. The most direct signal in the file is a broad, repeated open-market insider-buying cluster across the senior team and a director, from $107 down to $76, which aligns insiders with public holders.

The qualifications are genuine: a higher-for-longer rate path compresses both fundraising velocity and the multiple, evergreen redemption risk is real and attaches to the fastest-growing part of the business, and the controlled-company structure with TRA leakage limits per-share value capture. These warrant disciplined position sizing rather than a maximum-conviction stance. On balance, the mispricing of the insulated, committed-capital fee base against a deratable but durable franchise, combined with insider conviction and an analyst target floor ~24% above the current price, supports a **BUY** at ~$82 with a base-case target of $122 over 12-18 months.
