**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kohl's is a U.S.-only mid-tier department store operating 1,151 stores plus an e-commerce channel, selling moderately priced apparel, footwear, home goods, and beauty (via its Sephora partnership) to an inflation-sensitive moderate-income customer. The investment debate is whether a multi-year revenue decline — $18.1B (FY2022) to $15.5B (FY2025) — is bottoming, or whether the recent stabilization is a pause in a structural unwind. The Q1 FY2026 print (net sales −1.7%, comps −1.1%) decelerated sharply from the FY2025 pace (−4.0% sales, −3.1% comps), gross margin expanded 34bps, the revolver was paid to zero, cash rose to $674M, and Moody's revised its outlook to positive. The company has now beaten EPS estimates four quarters running, and consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 3.9% over 90 days. The business is repairing, not collapsing.

That fundamental improvement is what changes our call. We are downgrading from AVOID to HOLD. Prior reports flagged structural impairment as the basis for AVOID, but a structural-impairment thesis requires deteriorating fundamentals, and the current data shows decelerating sales declines, expanding gross margin, falling leverage, and rising consensus estimates. The stock has also already rallied 107% off its 52-week low of $7.93 to $17.25, so the "decline is not yet priced in" argument no longer holds empirically: much of the recovery has occurred, and at ~$17.77 the stock trades essentially at the $18.08 consensus mean target. An AVOID that the stock has already outrun, and that we cannot defend as a short (25.4% short interest, crowded), is functionally a HOLD.

We do not, however, see a BUY. The secular headwind is real: traffic and transactions are still falling, the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide ($1.00–$1.60) has a midpoint below FY2025's $1.62, true economic leverage is high once $5B+ of lease and financing obligations are counted, credit ratings are junk, and there is no open-market insider buying. The stock is a small, ~$2.0B, sentiment-driven instrument where short-squeeze mechanics dominate price action both ways. The balanced fact pattern — genuine secular/leverage risk offset by measurable operational and balance-sheet improvement and rising estimates — supports an uncertain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kohl's operates a single reportable segment: an omnichannel broadline retail business across its store base and Kohls.com (digital is roughly 26–29% of sales). Its differentiation rests on three assets. First, the **Sephora at Kohl's** partnership (855 full-size shops plus 294 small-format locations in over 1,100 stores), which drives the only growing category — "Accessories incl. Sephora" rose 2.0% in FY2025 — and brings younger, higher-frequency traffic; Kohl's owns the inventory, is the principal on sales, and splits operating profit 50/50 with Sephora (Sephora's share runs through COGS). Second, proprietary/private brands, which are gross-margin accretive and gained penetration in Q1. Third, the Kohl's Card credit/loyalty ecosystem, where accounts are owned by Capital One and Kohl's shares net risk-adjusted revenue (a declining "Other revenue" line).

The turnaround strategy has three pillars: a curated/balanced assortment, reestablishing value/quality leadership, and a frictionless omnichannel experience. The customer base — moderate-income and discretionary-spending-sensitive — is the same cohort showing stress in the broader macro data, making Kohl's more exposed to a U.S. consumer slowdown than geographically diversified peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Sales have fallen every year for four years: $18.1B → $17.5B → $16.2B → $15.5B (FY2025). The FY2025 decline was driven almost entirely by a ~4% drop in transaction volume. Declines were broad-based (Women's −5.7%, Footwear −6.9%, Children's −6.5%), with only Accessories/Sephora growing. The single most important fundamental data point is that this decline is decelerating, with digital growing 4% in Q1 FY2026.

**Profitability is thin and partly cosmetic.** FY2025 GAAP operating income was $624M (4.0% margin) and net income $272M ($2.38 EPS), but this was flattered by a one-time $129M credit-card interchange legal settlement gain. On an adjusted basis, operating income was essentially flat ($510M vs $509M) and adjusted EPS was $1.62 vs $1.50. The genuine bright spot is gross margin, which improved on tighter inventory (down 7%), fewer clearance markdowns, and moderating shrink. Net margin was only 1.8%; ROE 6.7%.

**Cash flow is strong on the surface but partly one-time.** FY2025 operating cash flow jumped to $1.38B (from $648M) and FCF to ~$1.0B, but this was inflated by the $129M settlement and a $200M inventory drawdown; adjusted FCF was ~$935M. Q1 FY2026 reverted to a seasonal cash use of $74M and a net loss of $0.13. The earnings surprise record is consistently positive — four straight beats, including a 32.3% beat on the most recent quarterly loss (−$0.13 vs −$0.19 estimate) and a 24.0% beat in the prior quarter — which lends credibility to the rising consensus estimates.

**Leverage is the central risk.** Altman Z-Score sits at 2.03 (grey zone), and debt/equity is 3.3x. Absolute bond debt is manageable (~$1.4B of long-term notes), but the company carries ~$4.7B of operating and finance lease liabilities plus ~$453M of financing obligations from failed sale-leasebacks at a punitive 16% implied rate. True economic leverage is far higher than the headline debt figure. Credit ratings are junk and were downgraded through 2025 (Moody's B2, S&P B+, Fitch BB−), a coupon step-up has already added 175bps to the 2031 notes, and the company issued $360M of 10.0% senior secured notes due 2030 (secured by 11 distribution/fulfillment centers) — expensive, secured borrowing that signals constrained access to unsecured markets.

**Balance-sheet repair is genuine and is the other side of the ledger.** The revolver was paid down from $545M YoY, $353M of 4.25% notes were repaid at maturity, the company executed opportunistic open-market debt buybacks at discounts ($87M in Q4 FY25, $50M in Q1 FY26), the current ratio improved to 1.46, and Moody's revised its outlook to positive in Q1 FY2026. This is the empirical evidence against the structural-impairment thesis: a company in genuine deterioration does not de-lever, accumulate cash, and earn a positive ratings outlook simultaneously.

**FY2026 guidance** calls for net sales −2% to flat, adjusted operating margin 2.8–3.4%, and adjusted EPS of $1.00–$1.60. The midpoint ($1.30) is below FY2025, so this is stabilization, not yet an earnings recovery. Capex was cut to $350–400M, and the dividend was held at $0.125/quarter — a 75% cut from the prior $0.50/quarter rate, reflecting a leverage-first capital priority.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic top-line growth story here, and we do not score one as present. The realistic best case is that the decline flattens and that gross-margin discipline plus modest mix improvement (Sephora, proprietary brands) drive operating-margin stabilization. The decelerating comp trajectory (−4% FY2025 → −1.1% Q1 FY2026) is the strongest evidence the bleeding is slowing.

The estimate-revision picture supports a modest positive lean rather than a strong one. Current-year EPS consensus has risen to $1.41 (+3.9% over 90 days), and next-year to $1.49 (+2.2%). These are positive but well short of the +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine acceleration — and they are accompanied by a next-quarter revision of −4.8%, so the momentum is uneven. We weight growth prospects as the single most reliable dimension for sorting outcomes, and here the signal is mildly positive at best: revenue is still contracting in absolute terms, and the FY2026 EPS guide midpoint is below FY2025. The two unrecognized optionality items — the ~$140M filed tariff refund claim (gain-contingency, not in the financials) and a potential further deceleration toward flat comps — are real but legally and operationally uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.77, Kohl's trades at roughly 7.3x trailing EPS of $2.38 and ~13.7x the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide midpoint. The trailing multiple is misleadingly low because trailing EPS is inflated by the one-time settlement; on adjusted/forward earnings the multiple is closer to a fair 11–14x. Against the broadline-retail peer median P/E of 25.3x, Kohl's appears cheap, but that comparison is not clean: the peer set includes structurally healthier or growth-oriented names (Amazon 28.9x, eBay 25.3x, Etsy 28.0x). The relevant comparable is Macy's at 10.3x — another secularly challenged mid-tier department store — and on that basis Kohl's is roughly fairly valued, not cheap.

We treat the apparent cheapness with deliberate skepticism. A low multiple on a business with declining revenue, junk credit, and heavy lease leverage is a value-trap signature unless paired with an independent catalyst. The catalysts here are partial: the tariff refund optionality and continued de-leveraging support the floor, but neither is a near-term re-rating trigger, and the dividend (~2.9% yield) was cut and is not a safe income anchor. The stock now sits essentially at the $18.08 consensus mean target. We do not penalize a stock for prior appreciation, but the analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply cleanly here because there is no rapidly accelerating growth to drag targets upward — estimate revisions are modest, not the +30% that pulls consensus higher within weeks. The risk/reward is roughly balanced: a base case near $15 (−13%), a bull case near $28 (+58%) on a squeeze plus realized turnaround, and a bear case near $9 (−49%) on consumer-cliff acceleration. That symmetry is consistent with a HOLD, not a BUY or an AVOID.

We score valuation a 5.0 — a low multiple offset by genuine structural/leverage concerns and the absence of a sharp re-rating catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Kohl's occupies the most structurally challenged corner of retail, the mid-tier department store, squeezed on three sides: off-price (TJX, Ross, Burlington) winning on treasure-hunt value; mass merchants (Walmart, Target, Amazon) winning on price and convenience; and specialty/DTC brands winning on assortment and brand. The four-year revenue decline and the broad-based category losses confirm continued share erosion. The 1,151 large-format stores carry heavy fixed lease costs in an era of falling traffic, and the all-U.S. footprint concentrates exposure to a domestic consumer slowdown. The cumulative TSR gap — 53.6 vs the peer group's 164.1 — quantifies how much worse Kohl's has navigated the post-pandemic period than its competitors.

Against that, the competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: gross margin is expanding on inventory and markdown discipline, proprietary-brand penetration rose in Q1, Sephora drives the only growing category, and management has stated no further mass store closures are planned for 2026 — a vote of confidence in the remaining fleet. The trajectory matters more than the level for this dimension, and the trajectory is stabilizing from a weak base rather than deteriorating. We score competitive position 3.5: structurally challenged with deteriorating multi-year share, partly offset by recent operational traction.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Secular decline of the mid-tier department store.** The dominant risk: persistent traffic and transaction losses to off-price, mass, and specialty channels. Structural, not cyclical. Q1's deceleration is encouraging but one quarter does not reverse a four-year trend.
2. **Leverage and credit quality.** Junk ratings, expensive 10% secured notes, a coupon step-up already triggered, and $5B+ of lease/financing obligations that dwarf the ~$1.4B bond figure. Further S&P downgrades would raise borrowing costs; the Moody's positive outlook is the offsetting signal.
3. **Tariff exposure.** Kohl's paid ~$190M in IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2025–Feb 2026). A Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down IEEPA tariffs; Kohl's filed ~$140M in Phase 1 refund claims but recognizes no gain yet (gain-contingency accounting), and the administration replaced them with Section 122 tariffs that are themselves under legal challenge. A fluid, hard-to-model swing factor — potential upside on refunds, ongoing downside on replacement tariffs.
4. **Credit-card program sensitivity.** "Other revenue" is declining (lower late fees/finance charges, regulatory risk from CFPB late-fee rules, BNPL substitution), and its profitability is tied to the same stressed moderate-income consumer.
5. **Consumer cliff.** The macro backdrop disproportionately hits Kohl's core moderate-income customer. A material consumer slowdown would pressure comps and earnings.
6. **Governance.** A "for cause" CEO termination over undisclosed conflicts within four months, on top of serial CEO turnover (fourth transition in a few years), is a genuine red flag that drew public analyst criticism. The new permanent CEO (Bender), the credible COO hire (Elliott Rodgers, ex–Foot Locker/Ulta, effective Sept 2026), and CFO continuity (Jill Timm) are stabilizing, but the bar for board trust is low.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, two-sided uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 72.2% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 82.4% (a −10.1% spread), so IV is relatively cheap, but the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 72.2% > long-term 68.7%), signaling the market expects near-term stress. Put/call skew is elevated at +4.3% on the front week (some downside hedging demand), though the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.33 and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.77 to 0.47 as the stock rose 36% since the prior snapshot — net positioning has shifted less bearish. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.2%. Read together: the market expects volatility but no longer leans decisively bearish, consistent with our balanced HOLD.

### Short Interest

Short interest is 25.4% of float, with 4.5 days to cover. This is the defining mechanical feature of the stock: the May 2026 ~15–19% one-day pop on a "less-bad" print was explicitly attributed to short covering. High short interest with a small float makes the equity prone to violent squeezes, which caps the case for shorting and adds upside tail risk to any bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$28, +58%).** The Q1 deceleration extends toward flat comps; gross-margin discipline and Sephora/proprietary mix lift adjusted operating margin into the upper half of the 2.8–3.4% guide; de-leveraging continues and S&P/Fitch follow Moody's toward a positive outlook; the ~$140M tariff refund is realized as a one-time cash and earnings tailwind; and a "less-bad" print triggers another short squeeze against the 25%+ short interest. In this scenario adjusted EPS recovers toward $1.60+, the multiple holds at ~17x, and the stock re-rates to the high $20s.

**Bear case (~$9, −49%).** A consumer slowdown hits the moderate-income core; comps re-accelerate to the downside; Section 122 replacement tariffs raise costs without refund offset; a further credit downgrade lifts borrowing costs against the heavy lease load; and the squeeze unwinds. Adjusted EPS falls below the $1.00 low end of guidance, the multiple compresses, and the stock retraces toward its low.

**Base case (~$15, −13%).** Sales decline flattens but does not reverse; adjusted EPS lands near the guide midpoint; leverage improves slowly; tariff refund timing remains uncertain; and the stock drifts back toward the Macy's-comparable ~11x, roughly $15, after the squeeze-driven move fades.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). The fundamentals are stabilizing, not deteriorating, which removes the basis for AVOID, but the secular and leverage risks plus the absence of insider buying cap the case at HOLD rather than BUY. The stock trades at consensus after its rally, with roughly symmetric risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY2026 comps turn positive or flat (vs −1.1% in Q1), confirming the deceleration is durable rather than a one-quarter artifact.
- Adjusted EPS guidance raised above FY2025's $1.62, signaling a genuine earnings recovery rather than stabilization.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by C-suite executives (currently absent — the strongest missing bullish signal), or a credit-ratings upgrade (not just outlook) from S&P or Fitch.
- The ~$140M tariff refund recognized as realized cash/earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps re-accelerate to −4% or worse, confirming a renewed consumer-driven decline.
- A further credit downgrade or evidence of constrained funding access (another high-coupon secured issuance).
- Adjusted operating margin guidance cut below 2.8%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected late August 2026) is the natural reassessment point, with the September 2026 COO transition and any S&P/Fitch ratings action as secondary catalysts.

## Conclusion

Kohl's is a structurally challenged mid-tier department store whose multi-year decline is decelerating, with measurable balance-sheet repair and four consecutive earnings beats driving consensus estimates modestly higher. That improving fundamental trajectory removes the basis for a structural-impairment AVOID, which requires deteriorating fundamentals. We are downgrading to HOLD.

We stop short of a BUY because revenue is still contracting in absolute terms, the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide midpoint sits below FY2025, true economic leverage is high once $5B+ of lease and financing obligations are counted, governance has a genuine recent red flag, there is no open-market insider buying, and the stock has already rallied to trade essentially at the $18.08 consensus mean. The equity is a small, sentiment-driven, 25%-shorted instrument where mechanics dominate price action both ways, which also makes it un-shortable at acceptable risk. With a base case near −13%, a bull case near +58%, and a bear case near −49%, the risk/reward is balanced. We recommend HOLD for investors already in the name rather than initiating a position, and the next earnings report will determine whether the stabilization is re-rated upward or whether the secular decline reasserts.
