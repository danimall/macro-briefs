**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ADT is the largest US residential and small-business security monitoring company, serving roughly 6.1 million subscribers with a subscription-like model: heavy upfront acquisition cost, then predictable recurring monthly revenue (RMR) of ~$359M/month, which supplies about 85% of total revenue. Following the 2023 commercial divestiture and 2024 solar exit, the business is now a single reportable segment and throws off substantial cash — Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $638M against $1.279B of revenue and $674M of adjusted EBITDA (~53% margin). FY2025 delivered record net income of $596M on $5.13B of revenue (+4.7%).

The equity is down about 18% since our prior review and sits near the low end of its 52-week range ($6.24–$8.94), roughly 23% below the high. Part of that decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration rather than pure sentiment: gross revenue attrition rose to 13.1% (from 12.6% a year prior) driven by non-payment disconnects, and the Q1 provision for credit losses jumped to $69M from $50M with the retail-installment allowance nearly doubling since December. At roughly $40M of annualized RMR per 100bps of attrition, this is the metric that governs the thesis, and it is moving the wrong way. This is a consumer-stress signal inside a recurring-revenue base normally treated as defensive.

Against that, the setup has three supports worth underwriting: (1) the stock is cheap on an absolute and relative basis (~7.6x forward EPS vs. a peer-median P/E of ~19.5, 3.3% dividend); (2) Apollo fully exited in May 2026 (~102M shares at $7.25, zero shares after), removing years of secondary-offering overhang; and (3) a cluster of open-market insider purchases by the CFO, a director, the CBO, the COO and the CMO occurred immediately after Apollo's cleanup at ~$6.75–$6.91. We rate ADT a HOLD. The durable return is the ~3.3% dividend plus buyback accretion on a shrinking share count; a re-rating requires attrition and credit metrics to stabilize, which the current consumer environment does not yet support.

## Company Overview

ADT provides professionally-monitored security, interactive/smart-home services, and DIY/self-setup offerings to residential and small-business customers across the United States. The economics resemble a subscription business: management notes revenue break-even on a new subscriber is roughly two years, amortized against a 15-year assumed customer life. The moat rests on the ADT brand (carried as a $1.33B indefinite-lived trade name), national scale, a 6M+ installed base, an authorized-dealer origination network, and a deep partnership with Google covering product technology and cloud.

The reported revenue "step-down" from ~$6.4B to ~$5.0B that a standardized data pull would flag is an artifact of the divestiture and solar exit, both now in discontinued operations. This was deliberate refocusing, not organic decline. Strategically, ADT is pushing the next-generation ADT+ platform and expanding outright-sales (equipment sold to the customer rather than company-owned), now ~30% of new subscribers. The February 2026 $164M cash acquisition of Origin Wireless adds patented RF-based presence/ambient-sensing technology intended to improve alarm verification. The capital structure is a legacy of Apollo's 2016 LBO — highly leveraged by design.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is strong and improving. FY2025 net income was up from $501M in 2024 and $463M in 2023. Q1 2026 net income of $168.4M rose 20% YoY, diluted continuing EPS reached $0.20 (from $0.16), and adjusted EPS was $0.23. Q1 operating income of $325M reflects 25%+ operating margins, and interest expense fell to $98M from $121M on lower rates and reduced swap losses. Adjusted EBITDA at ~53% margin confirms the underlying unit economics remain intact.

Cash flow is the standout. Q1 2026 operating cash flow supports a business that generates roughly $1.7B of annual operating cash, which makes the leverage serviceable. Total debt of ~$7.67B against equity of $3.8B produces an Altman Z-Score in the "distress" zone (~1.1), but that reading is misleading: it reflects the LBO capital structure and an intangible-heavy balance sheet (~$9.8B intangibles + $5.0B goodwill), not operating distress. The company is covenant-compliant, with $800M revolver availability and $117M of receivables-facility capacity. The $2.78B accumulated deficit (improving from $2.91B) is likewise an LBO artifact.

Maturities are well-laddered: the $75M 2026 notes were redeemed at maturity in April 2026 with cash, and the next wall is $1.0B of 3.375% notes due August 2027. A refinancing wave (Term Loan A due 2030, Term Loan B-2 due 2032 at SOFR+1.75%, 5.875% notes due 2033) has termed out the stack and lowered the cost of debt, with ~$6.85B notional of swaps hedging the variable exposure.

The blemish is credit quality inside the subscriber base. The allowance for credit losses rose to $69.9M from $63.8M, and the retail-installment-contract allowance nearly doubled ($37M → $53M since December). This tracks the non-payment-driven attrition and is the clearest earnings-quality warning in the file. Capital allocation has pivoted from deleveraging toward shareholder returns: a new $1.5B three-year buyback (~$230M executed by April 2026, at $6.57 in Q1 and $6.92 in April) and a $0.055/quarter dividend. Common share count fell from 765M to 750M in Q1 alone.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is anemic. Q1 revenue rose just 0.9% YoY, and RMR was flat as the October 2025 multifamily divestiture offset price increases. Consensus EPS estimates are essentially unchanged — current year revised +2.0% over 90 days, next year +0.9%, current quarter -3.4% — with no meaningful directional momentum. The four-quarter beat streak (10.2%, 2.4%, 6.4%, 13.9%) is real but modest, and driven substantially by lower interest expense and share-count reduction rather than top-line acceleration.

The EPS growth that exists is per-share arithmetic: buybacks shrinking the denominator plus a falling cost of debt, not subscriber or revenue expansion. This does not clear the bar for a growth thesis. The ADT+ / outright-sales transition adds a further complication: it mechanically shifts revenue from smooth recurring recognition toward point-in-time installation revenue, muddying GAAP comparisons. RMR, attrition, and adjusted EBITDA are the cleaner lenses, and on those the business is holding roughly flat while attrition creeps higher. The Origin acquisition is a capability play, not expected to be material to 2026 revenue. We do not model a re-acceleration; the growth trajectory continuing its decline from prior reviews (5.5 → 4.8) is consistent with what the filings show.

## Valuation Assessment

ADT trades at ~7.6x current-year consensus EPS ($0.91) and ~9.5x trailing, against a Specialized Consumer Services peer-median P/E of ~19.5. On yield it offers 3.3% versus a peer median of ~2.0%, and the FCF yield is high given ~$1.7B of annual operating cash on a ~$4.5B market cap. On the surface this is inexpensive.

The caution is that cheapness here carries value-trap characteristics rather than a clear mispricing. The discount to peers is explained by real factors: ~$7.7B of debt, flat organic growth, rising attrition, and deteriorating consumer credit. A low multiple on a leveraged, low-growth business with worsening non-payment trends is not automatically a buy signal — it can persist for years. The nearest comparable, H&R Block, trades at 6.4x and is also a leveraged, low-growth consumer-services name; the market is pricing this cohort on capital-return yield, not growth.

What limits the downside and prevents a lower valuation score: the Apollo overhang is gone (a structural positive for float and independence), the $1.5B buyback is retiring shares below peer TSR and is accretive if fundamentals hold, and insiders bought at these levels. The analyst target range ($7.00 low, $9.00 high, $8.50 median) sits above the current $6.90, but on a stock at the low end of its multiple with flat estimates we treat those targets as a range, not a floor: the estimate-revision picture is flat, so there is no re-rating engine pulling price toward them. Valuation is supportive but not asymmetric; the buyback and yield anchor the floor while flat growth and credit deterioration cap the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

ADT leads a fragmenting market against DIY/tech entrants (Ring/Amazon, Google Nest, SimpliSafe, Wyze) that pressure pricing and push self-setup, telecom/cable bundlers, and regional dealers. Its defenses are brand, professional monitoring, national scale, and the Google partnership. ADT+, DIY offerings, and the Origin deal are direct responses to the tech-driven shift.

The trajectory matters more than the level. Rising gross attrition — even accounting for the non-payment component — signals competitive and consumer pressure on the installed base, and the DIY funnel that expands new-subscriber counts does so at lower RMR per subscriber. Pricing power partially offsets this (average price increases held RMR flat despite the multifamily sale), but a market leader with rising churn is a deteriorating competitive position, consistent with the decline in this dimension from prior reviews (6.0 → 5.0). The Google relationship cuts both ways: it supplies product and cloud technology but embeds a dependency, including a $200M cloud purchase commitment through 2030 with a 30% cancellation penalty, and Google-affiliated interests hold the Class B shares.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is consumer credit deterioration inside a base normally treated as defensive. Attrition on non-payment disconnects, provisions up 38% QoQ, and the RIC allowance nearly doubling are early cracks. At ~$40M of RMR per 100bps of attrition, sustained deterioration would erode the recurring-revenue foundation the entire thesis rests on. This is not fully priced and is the reason we do not view the price decline as pure sentiment.

Secondary risks: leverage and refinancing (~$7.7B debt leaves little cushion and makes the equity rate-sensitive, though the next material maturity is $1.0B in August 2027 and the stack is termed out); the ADT+/outright-sales transition complicating comparisons and revenue timing; Google dependency; and a restatement history (2022 10-K and two 2022/2023 10-Qs restated in July 2023) alongside an ongoing CRM/ERP cloud migration that management expects to materially affect internal controls over financial reporting during 2026. Governance watch-items include the combined CEO/Chairman/President role (DeVries, $14.9M FY2025 comp) and the dual-class structure, with cumulative TSR (115.3) trailing the peer group (142.3).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.3% is rich versus 27.9% one-year historical vol (86th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.3% near vs 34.3% at six months), indicating the market is pricing elevated event risk around the next earnings print. The signal is not directionally bearish: the put/call skew is call-heavy (-13.4%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.28. The prior snapshot's 313.9% IV reading collapsed to 42.3%, which appears to have been a stale/thin-options artifact now normalized. The net read: elevated event risk into earnings, but positioning is not tilted toward downside protection, consistent with the insider buying rather than contradicting it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$8.50–$9.00):** Attrition and credit provisions prove to be a cyclical, non-payment blip that stabilizes as the consumer environment normalizes; RMR resumes low-single-digit growth as price increases stick and ADT+/DIY funnel expansion offsets churn; the buyback compounds EPS on a shrinking share count; and the removal of the Apollo overhang plus the insider-buying signal drive a partial re-rating toward the peer-median multiple and analyst targets. This is roughly the analyst high target and represents ~25–30% upside plus the dividend.

**Bear case (~$5.50–$5.75):** The non-payment attrition accelerates as consumer stress deepens, credit provisions keep rising, and RMR turns negative — each 100bps of attrition removing ~$40M of annualized recurring revenue. Flat organic growth combined with the debt load leaves the equity exposed if FCF softens or refinancing conditions tighten into the 2027 maturity, and the model-transition noise obscures the deterioration until it is well underway. This is roughly 17–20% downside.

**Base case (~$7.20):** Attrition drifts modestly higher but the recurring base holds, EPS grinds up ~5–7% on buybacks and lower interest cost, and the stock re-rates only slightly off a depressed multiple as the market waits for churn to confirm direction. This sits between the scenarios and implies ~4% upside plus the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q2 and Q3 2026 prints where attrition, RMR, and credit-provision trends will confirm or refute the consumer-stress read.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross revenue attrition stabilizes or improves below 13.0% for two consecutive quarters, with the credit-loss provision reverting toward the ~$50M run-rate.
- RMR returns to sequential growth (ex-divestiture), confirming pricing power is outrunning churn.
- Continued open-market insider accumulation alongside buyback execution below $7.00, with net debt trending down.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Attrition breaks above 14% or credit provisions continue climbing, signaling the recurring base is eroding rather than stabilizing.
- FCF conversion deteriorates such that the buyback and dividend are no longer comfortably covered while leverage rises.
- A dividend cut, covenant pressure, or an adverse development in the ongoing ICFR/ERP-migration transition.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled earnings; monitor the August 2027 note maturity approach and any refinancing terms in the interim.

## Conclusion

ADT is a cash-rich, leveraged, low-growth home-security monitor trading cheaply on earnings and yield, with a genuinely strong management signal — a clean sponsor exit paired with a cluster of open-market insider purchases near the lows — and an accretive buyback. Those supports argue against selling. Working the other way are flat organic growth and, more consequentially, rising non-payment attrition and jumping credit provisions that expose the recurring-revenue base to the consumer-stress cycle. The two-sided setup, with an income-oriented return profile and a re-rating gated on churn and credit stabilizing, supports a HOLD. We would need to see attrition and provisions turn before treating the valuation discount as an opportunity rather than a fair reflection of the risk; conversely, a break higher in attrition or a coverage problem on the dividend would move us to AVOID.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (income hold). Overall score 5.4/10.
