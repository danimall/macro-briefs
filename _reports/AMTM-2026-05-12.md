## Executive Summary

Amentum Holdings is a $14.4B-revenue government services company formed in September 2024 through a Reverse Morris Trust transaction combining legacy Amentum with Jacobs Solutions' Critical Mission Solutions business. At $24.25/share, the stock trades at approximately 8.7x EV/Adjusted EBITDA — a 25-50% discount to peers like SAIC (10x), Leidos (14x), and Booz Allen Hamilton (18x). The discount reflects legitimate near-term concerns: 3.4x net leverage, a PE-consortium overhang distributing shares to LPs, GAAP net income of only $66M on $14.4B of revenue, and DOGE-related headline risk.

The investment case rests on three mechanisms for value creation: (1) amortization roll-off that will mechanically grow reported EPS as the $479M annual amortization charge declines, narrowing the gap between $516M FCF and $66M net income; (2) debt reduction at $700M+/year pace, which should bring leverage below 2.5x within two years; and (3) the September 2026 expiration of RMT restrictions, which unlocks buybacks, strategic M&A, and positions the company as a potential acquisition target. However, the timeline on these catalysts extends 6-18 months, the government budget environment introduces binary headline risks, and the PE overhang creates persistent selling pressure on any rally.

The stock is appropriately held here rather than bought. The valuation discount is real but explained by identifiable structural overhang rather than a market error. A BUY upgrade requires visible evidence of overhang resolution, continued backlog growth, and confirmation that DOGE impacts remain immaterial.

## Company Overview

Amentum operates through two segments:

**Digital Solutions (DS) — $5.5B revenue:** Intelligence analytics, space systems, cybersecurity, and federal IT modernization. Serves primarily the Intelligence Community and DOD. Adjusted EBITDA margin approximately 7.9%.

**Global Engineering Solutions (GES) — $8.9B revenue:** Environmental remediation, nuclear power solutions, platform engineering, sustainment, and supply chain management. Key clients include DOE, UK MOD, UK NDA, and Australian DOD. Adjusted EBITDA margin approximately 7.5%.

The company employs ~50,000 people across 70+ countries with an asset-light model (only $114M in PP&E). Approximately 81% of revenue derives from the U.S. federal government, with the DOD/Intelligence Community representing 54% of Q1 FY2026 revenue. Total backlog stands at $47.2B (3.3x annual revenue), providing substantial revenue visibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
FY2025 revenue reached $14.4B, reflecting the full combined entity. Q1 FY2026 revenue of $3.24B declined 5.2% YoY, but the company attributes this to quantifiable discrete factors: a $150M government shutdown impact, JV accounting transitions, and $110M from divestitures. The Digital Solutions segment grew 4% organically. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $3.48B was flat YoY, with adjusted EPS of $0.60 beating consensus of $0.58.

GAAP operating margin was 3.3% in FY2025 ($480M), with net margin of only 0.4% ($66M net income). These thin GAAP margins are depressed by $479M in intangible amortization from the CMS acquisition — a non-cash charge that declines annually. Adjusted EBITDA of $1,104M represents a 7.7% margin.

**Cash Flow:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $543M with capex of only $27M, yielding FCF of $516M (3.6% FCF margin on revenue, but a 7.8x multiple of net income). This demonstrates the asset-light nature of the business and the non-economic nature of amortization charges. At the current $5.9B market cap, the stock trades at approximately 11.4x FCF.

Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $136M due to seasonality (extra pay cycle) and shutdown timing effects — consistent with the lumpy cash flow patterns typical of government services companies.

**Balance Sheet and Leverage:**
Total debt of $4.0B ($2.99B Term Loan at SOFR + spread, $1.0B Senior Notes at 7.25% fixed) against $247M cash yields net debt of approximately $3.75B. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA stands at 3.4x — elevated but declining rapidly. Management made $722M in voluntary principal payments in FY2025. Available revolver capacity is $768M, and $1.6B in variable rate debt is hedged via interest rate swaps through January 2027.

Goodwill of $5.7B represents 49.8% of total assets. With market capitalization at $5.9B and total equity at $4.6B, there is limited cushion before impairment testing becomes a concern.

**Earnings Beat Pattern:**
Four consecutive beats with a median surprise of 2.5%. Beats are modest in magnitude (2-4%), consistent with a government services company where contract revenues are predictable but cost management drives modest outperformance.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth going forward is constrained. FY2026 guidance of ~$14B implies flat to slight contraction. The sources of future growth include:

1. **Backlog conversion:** $47.2B in backlog (up from $45.0B at the transaction close) provides 3+ years of revenue visibility. Funded backlog of $6.9B represents approximately two quarters of revenue.

2. **Defense budget tailwinds:** The proposed GFY2026 defense budget of $1.01T (+13%) and the "One Big, Beautiful Bill Act" (increased defense, NASA, and energy spending) align with Amentum's core markets.

3. **International expansion:** AUKUS nuclear submarine program, UK nuclear decommissioning, and Indo-Pacific defense modernization represent multi-decade programs.

4. **Margin expansion:** SG&A declined from 3.8% to 3.6% of revenue in Q1 FY2026 as integration synergies emerge. Fixed-price contract proportion rose to 29% (from 24%), which carries both margin opportunity and execution risk.

5. **Amortization roll-off:** The $479M annual amortization charge will decline over a 3-12 year schedule, mechanically growing EPS without operational improvement.

Estimate revisions are essentially flat (current year +0.3% over 90 days, next year +0.2%). Consensus projects $2.41 EPS for FY2026 and $2.75 for FY2027 — implying 14% EPS growth driven primarily by debt paydown (lower interest expense) and amortization reduction rather than revenue growth. At $2.41 FY2026 EPS, the stock trades at 10.1x forward earnings.

The discount to peers is substantial but partially explained by: (a) PE overhang creating persistent supply, (b) 3.4x leverage vs. 2-3x for mature peers, (c) limited public trading history (18 months), (d) GAAP profitability screens poorly due to amortization.

As leverage declines toward 2.5x (likely by mid-2027) and amortization mechanically grows EPS, the discount should narrow. Analyst consensus target of $35 (median) implies 44% upside; the low target of $29 implies 20% upside even in a pessimistic scenario.

At 10.1x FY2026 earnings for a company with $47.2B in backlog, 81% government revenue (stable), and $516M FCF, the stock screens cheap on an absolute basis. Whether the structural overhang prevents realization of value within a reasonable timeframe is the central uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Amentum occupies a strong competitive position. Security clearances for 50,000 employees create extremely high barriers to entry. The combined nuclear/environmental expertise (legacy Amentum/AECOM) plus digital/intelligence capabilities (Jacobs CMS) create a unique breadth of offerings that few competitors can match at scale.

The competitive trajectory is improving. The $425M Cal Fire contract (April 2026) demonstrates successful diversification beyond federal markets. Backlog growth from $45.0B to $47.2B during a period of government shutdowns and budget uncertainty indicates continued win rates on recompetes and new program captures.

Key competitive advantages: incumbency on critical long-duration programs (nuclear weapons complex, intelligence systems), scale enabling bids on the largest contracts, and geographic reach across 70+ countries. The merger created a company with capability breadth previously only available from larger primes like Lockheed or Northrop, but without the hardware/manufacturing cost structure.

## Risk Assessment

**Government Concentration (HIGH):** 81% revenue from U.S. federal government. Two shutdowns already impacted FY2026 by $150M+. DOGE headline risk is real, though management states impacts have been "not material to date." A sustained CR or further shutdown activity could suppress quarterly results.

**Leverage (MODERATE-HIGH):** 3.4x net debt/EBITDA with $353M annual interest expense consumes significant cash flow. The 7.25% Senior Notes are expensive. However, $1.6B in swaps through January 2027 provides near-term protection, and the $722M paydown pace suggests rapid deleveraging.

**PE Overhang (MODERATE):** The Amentum Equityholder consortium distributes shares to LPs, creating persistent selling pressure. Board nomination rights (up to 5 of 13 directors at >25.1% ownership) add governance overhang. The timeline for resolution is uncertain.

**Goodwill Impairment (MODERATE):** $5.7B in goodwill on a $5.9B market cap. A $186M impairment was taken in FY2023. Further stock price deterioration could trigger testing and a non-cash writedown.

**Fixed-Price Contract Shift (MODERATE):** Increase from 24% to 29% fixed-price introduces execution risk. Net favorable estimate adjustments of $23M in Q1 suggest current outperformance, but cost overruns on large programs could reverse this.

**RMT Restriction (LOW — time-limited):** Until September 2026, the company cannot pursue buybacks, major asset sales (>30%), or become a takeover target. This restriction expires in approximately 4 months and represents a potential catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35-42, +44% to +73%)

The bull case relies on multiple re-rating as structural overhang resolves: (1) PE overhang diminishes as distributions complete and float normalizes; (2) leverage reaches 2.5x by mid-2027, approaching investment-grade metrics; (3) RMT restriction expires September 2026, enabling buyback authorization; (4) amortization roll-off grows reported EPS from $0.27 trailing toward $2.75+ consensus for FY2027; (5) defense budget expansion drives mid-single-digit organic revenue growth; (6) potential acquisition target premium once RMT restrictions lift. At 12-14x forward EPS of $2.75, the stock reaches $33-39.

### Bear Case ($19-22, -9% to -22%)

The bear case centers on: (1) DOGE initiatives accelerate beyond "not material" and reduce contract awards or trigger program cancellations; (2) continued government shutdowns/CRs suppress revenue below $13.5B; (3) PE overhang persists and selling pressure accelerates; (4) rate environment prevents further leverage reduction (hedges expire Jan 2027, exposing $1.4B to higher floating rates); (5) fixed-price contract losses on large programs; (6) goodwill impairment forces writedown that further suppresses sentiment. At 8x reduced EPS of $2.20, the stock reaches $17.60.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. PE overhang resolution — visible reduction in Amentum Equityholder stake below 15% of float or cessation of LP distributions
2. Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue growth >3% with maintained or expanding EBITDA margins
3. Stock pullback to $20-21 (creating >15x FY2027 P/FCF yield) while fundamentals remain stable

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. DOGE impacts quantified at >$500M annual revenue reduction or specific contract cancellations affecting >5% of backlog
2. Leverage increases above 4.0x due to operating deterioration (not voluntary events)
3. Goodwill impairment charge exceeding $1B signaling permanent value destruction

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely May/June 2026 for Q3 FY2026), September 2026 RMT restriction expiration, and any 8-K disclosures regarding DOGE contract impacts.

## Conclusion

Amentum Holdings presents a genuine valuation discount with identifiable catalysts for resolution, but the catalysts are 6-18 months out, and the near-term environment (PE overhang, DOGE headline risk, government budget uncertainty, elevated leverage) prevents confident assignment of a BUY recommendation today.

The stock is worth owning at these levels for patient investors willing to wait for multiple expansion as leverage declines and amortization rolls off. It is not yet a BUY because: (a) the Industrials sector requires elevated scrutiny given historically poor BUY outcomes; (b) revenue growth is flat, not accelerating; (c) estimate revisions are stagnant; and (d) the PE overhang creates supply dynamics that can suppress price regardless of fundamentals.

The optimal entry point for a BUY is either a pullback to $20-21 (where P/FCF reaches compelling levels) or evidence that the September 2026 catalyst will unlock capital returns. The current risk/reward is modestly positive (44% upside to consensus target vs. 15-20% downside to bear case) but does not meet the threshold for an Industrials BUY given the quality and timing uncertainties.
