## Executive Summary

ACADIA delivered its best operating quarter in two years and is nonetheless a difficult stock to underwrite at $27.15. Q2 2026 revenue was $308.0M (+16.4% YoY), DAYBUE net sales grew 29.9% to $124.8M — an acceleration from +12.3% for full-year 2025 to +19.6% in Q1 to +29.9% in Q2 — and operating margin recovered to 12.3% from a negative 1.7% in Q1. Diluted EPS of $0.18 beat the single-analyst $0.06 estimate. Cash and investments rose to $956.5M with no borrowings on the balance sheet. Externally reported FY2026 guidance of $1.22-1.28B in revenue and $480-510M for DAYBUE implies H2 revenue of roughly $674M, up 17% half-over-half.

The valuation arithmetic is where the case breaks down. The screened trailing P/E of 12.4x is built on FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.30, of which the large majority is a $252.1M net income tax benefit from releasing the deferred-tax valuation allowance after ACADIA achieved cumulative three-year profitability. Pre-tax FY2025 income was $138.9M; taxed at the 30.4% effective rate the company actually recorded in H1 2026, normalized EPS was approximately $0.57. Q2 2026's actual $0.18 at a 32.2% effective rate corroborates that level. Against a derived FY2026 GAAP EPS of roughly $0.45-0.60 (H1 actual was $0.20), the stock trades at 45-60x current-year earnings and 31.9x the 13-analyst FY2027 consensus of $0.85 — a consensus that has fallen 10.3% over 90 days and 3.2% in the last 30 days. Enterprise value of $3.72B is about 3.0x FY2026 revenue.

That multiple is a bet on remlifanserin. The RADIANT Phase 2 readout in Alzheimer's disease psychosis is guided for September-October 2026, inside a seamless Phase 2→Phase 3 design where Phase 3 enrollment has already begun. The stock enters that event marginally above the top of its 2026 closing range ($20.32-$27.51 through July 28), with near-term implied volatility at 54.5% against 37.3% realized and put skew widening from 3.2% at one month to 8.3% at three months and 12.7% at six months; the market is pricing the binary with a downside tilt at exactly the tenors that bracket it. Layered on top: a securities class action with no accrual heading into an August 17 mandatory settlement conference and September 2 final pretrial conference, a Federal Circuit appeal on the pimavanserin estate with no oral argument scheduled, and a separate Zydus trial on November 2. We see a genuine business improving and a price that already discounts the improvement plus a coin flip. HOLD.

## Company Overview

ACADIA is a single-segment commercial-stage CNS and rare-disease biopharmaceutical company (798 employees at 12/31/25, San Diego headquarters, Princeton NJ operations, Swiss European entity). It owns no manufacturing; all API and drug product is outsourced. Two marketed franchises:

**NUPLAZID (pimavanserin)** — a 5-HT2A inverse agonist, the only FDA-approved therapy for hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson's disease psychosis, approved April 2016. Worldwide rights owned outright with no royalty burden. Net sales: $549.2M (2023) → $609.4M (2024) → $680.1M (2025) → $350.1M in H1 2026. Carries a boxed warning. Company estimates ~130,000 U.S. Parkinson's patients treated annually with an atypical antipsychotic; penetration is stated inconsistently in the FY2025 10-K at "approximately 20%" in the Business section and "approximate 25%" in the Strategy discussion.

**DAYBUE / DAYBUE STIX (trofinetide)** — the only approved treatment for Rett syndrome (FDA March 2023; Canada October 2024; Israel December 2025). Net sales: $177.2M (2023) → $348.4M (2024) → $391.4M (2025) → $226.0M in H1 2026. STIX, a dye- and preservative-free oral-solution powder, was approved December 2025 with broad availability early in Q2 2026. ACADIA licenses trofinetide from Neuren: North American royalties tier 10% (≤$250M) to 15% (>$750M) with up to $455M of remaining NA milestones; ex-North American royalties are mid-teens to low-twenties percent, with up to $426.3M of further ex-NA milestones and $831.3M tied to ercanetide (NNZ-2591).

**Pipeline.** Remlifanserin (formerly ACP-204, in-house discovered, composition-of-matter protection to 2038) is the lead asset: RADIANT Phase 2 in ADP (~318 patients, 30mg and 60mg vs placebo, SAPS-H+D at week 6), enrollment complete, Phase 3 screening begun, topline September-October 2026, FDA Fast Track granted July 2026. ILLUMERA Phase 2 in Lewy body dementia psychosis started September 2025. ACP-211 (MDD) topline Q2-Q3 2027; ACP-711 Phase 2 start slipped from 2026 to 2027; ACP-271 in first-in-human.

The track record on shots-on-goal is poor: pimavanserin failed ADVANCE-2 in schizophrenia negative symptoms (March 2024) and Phase 2 in pediatric autism irritability (October 2024); ACP-101 failed Phase 3 COMPASS PWS (September 2025) after roughly $91M of cumulative external spend and was terminated; two of three Stoke collaboration programs were discontinued in May 2025 from a $60M upfront paid in 2022.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and the shape of growth

| $M | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 | Q1'26 (derived) | Q2'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NUPLAZID | 549.2 | 609.4 | 680.1 | 350.1 | 166.9 | 183.1 |
| DAYBUE | 177.2 | 348.4 | 391.4 | 226.0 | 101.1 | 124.8 |
| **Total** | **726.4** | **957.8** | **1,071.5** | **576.0** | **268.1** | **308.0** |
| YoY | +40.4% | +31.8% | +11.9% | +13.2% | +9.7% | +16.4% |

The simple screen reading — deceleration from 32% to 12% — misses a reacceleration. DAYBUE moved from +19.6% in Q1 to +29.9% in Q2, which management attributes to both unit growth and higher average net selling price. The most plausible driver is the STIX launch, though the filings do not decompose it; favorable gross-to-net timing is an additive or alternative explanation, and the company explicitly warns that quarterly allowances fluctuate with its share of the Medicare Part D coverage gap. NUPLAZID grew only 8.7% in Q2 and 6.7% in H1 against a stated $1.0B-by-2028 ambition; from an implied ~$755M in FY2026 that would require a ~15% CAGR for two years, which current run-rates do not support. Treat the 2028 target as aspirational.

### Margins: correct the 2024 comparison, then look at Q1 2026

FY2025 operating income of $104.8M versus FY2024's $230.8M looks like collapse but is mostly a comparison artifact: FY2024 included the $146.5M net gain on the Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher sale. Excluding it, FY2024 operating income was approximately $84.4M, so FY2025 grew underlying operating income roughly 24% with margin improving from ~8.8% to 9.8%.

The real compression is in 2026 and it is concentrated in one quarter. H1 2026 operating income was $33.2M (5.8% margin) versus $51.7M (10.2%) in H1 2025. Derived Q1 2026 operating income was **negative $4.6M** on $268.1M of revenue, against +$19.3M in Q1 2025. Q2 2026 margin of 12.3% was essentially flat against Q2 2025's 12.2%. The driver is SG&A: H1 2026 SG&A of $331.3M rose 27.5% against revenue up 13.2%, moving from 51.1% to 57.5% of revenue, with derived Q1 SG&A of $171.0M — higher in absolute dollars than Q2's $160.3M and 63.8% of Q1 revenue. Management cites expansion of both the DAYBUE and NUPLAZID field teams. R&D was nearly flat (+1.4% to $158.4M) as ACP-101 wound down and remlifanserin scaled (+51.7% to $54.0M).

Two readings compete. Transitional: front-loaded launch and pre-launch spend ahead of STIX and EU, with Q2's recovery confirming it. Structural: growth in mature indications now requires permanently higher promotional intensity, in which case FY2026 margin settles nearer H1's 5.8% than Q2's 12.3% and our EPS scenario is too high. H2 SG&A is the single most informative line item in the next print. The proxy's sole company-selected performance measure is Total Revenue, with no profitability or capital-efficiency counterweight, a compensation design consistent with what happened to SG&A.

### The FY2025 net income figure is not earnings power

FY2025 net income was $391.0M on pre-tax income of $138.9M. The gap is a $252.1M net income tax benefit from releasing the valuation allowance, recognizing $249.9M of deferred tax assets as realizable. Every FY2025 net-income-derived ratio inherits the distortion: reported net margin of 36.5%, ROA of ~25%, and ROE of ~31.9% are not repeatable. The earnings-surprise history makes the same point — the December 2025 quarter shows $1.60 actual against a $0.14 estimate, a 1,044% "beat" that is an accounting event, not operating outperformance. Normalized FY2025 EPS is approximately $0.57.

The DTA is nonetheless a real economic asset: the balance was $240.7M at 6/30/26, and ACADIA will pay materially less cash tax than 30% for years while consuming NOLs, with OBBBA's restoration of domestic R&E expensing adding further cash-tax benefit. The correct framing is that GAAP net income overstated FY2025 earnings power while GAAP tax expense understates near-term cash flow.

### Balance sheet: clean, and mislabeled by standard screens

At 6/30/26: total assets $1,742.9M, total liabilities $436.3M, equity $1,306.6M, cash and investment securities $956.5M (up from $819.7M at 12/31/25), current ratio 3.39. **There are no borrowings** — only payables, accruals, and $73.9M of operating lease liabilities (6.1-year weighted term, 5.1% discount rate). Any "debt-to-equity of 0.27" figure is total liabilities over equity (0.334 at 6/30/26), not leverage. Similarly, the FY2025 "$31.7M interest expense" tag is interest *income*: the 10-Q reports interest income, net of $8.0M in Q2 and $16.0M in H1, a ~$32M annual run-rate on the portfolio. ACADIA is a net creditor.

Net cash is $5.56 per share, 20.5% of the market capitalization — a genuine floor component in any bear case.

### Cash flow quality

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $134.8M versus $84.3M in H1 2025, and the cash bridge reconciles to the $136.8M increase in cash and investments. Composition matters: net income $35.1M plus ~$47.0M of non-cash add-backs (stock comp $31.6M, intangible amortization $5.4M, deferred taxes $9.7M, depreciation $0.7M) equals $82.1M, with a net working-capital inflow of $52.7M on top. Within that, accounts payable rose $33.7M (from an unusually low $10.9M base) and accrued liabilities rose $46.1M including accrued sales allowances from $140.9M to $187.4M — together roughly 59% of reported OCF, partly offset by a $29.2M receivables build.

**Do not annualize H1 OCF.** Excluding the payables-and-accrual build, H1 cash generation was closer to $55M, implying a normalized annual pace of $110-130M, consistent with FY2025's actual $109.8M. The more forgiving read is also defensible: the allowance rollforward shows $123.0M of prior-period rebates already paid in cash during H1, so the reserve build reflects growing gross sales and IRA accrual rather than deferral of a known invoice. The sustainable figure sits between. H1 capex was $10.3M (versus $0.3M a year earlier) as facilities including a new 12-year Princeton lease came on.

Gross-to-net deductions are substantial: the H1 provision on current-period sales of $180.5M against $576.0M of net revenue implies gross sales near $756.5M and a ~23.9% deduction rate.

### Dilution

Shares rose from 170.31M to 172.13M in six months (+1.07%) on option/RSU exercises and ESPP, with $14.7M of financing inflows. A further 13.97M potentially dilutive shares were excluded as anti-dilutive in H1 (down from 18.71M) — roughly 8.1% of current shares, or ~7.5% ownership dilution if all became dilutive; these are not part of current diluted capitalization. Stock compensation is running at $31.6M in H1 (5.5% of revenue, +23% YoY).

**Financial health: 7.0.** Debt-free with 20.5% of market cap in net cash, a 3.39 current ratio, positive free cash flow, and a real deferred-tax asset. Marked down from higher for the working-capital-heavy cash flow composition, ~24% gross-to-net exposure with a demonstrated estimation error, and an unreserved securities-litigation liability.

## Growth Analysis

Three things support the growth case. First, DAYBUE's sequential acceleration is filing-verified, with FY2026 guidance implying ~$269M in H2, up 19% half-over-half from H1's $226.0M. Second, the EU pathway advanced: a negative CHMP trend vote in January 2026 and negative opinion (March per the 10-Q) reversed to a positive opinion on re-examination in June 2026 recommending approval of DAYBU, an outcome unusual enough that a competitor publicly cited it as precedent. Third, remlifanserin addresses a wholly unserved indication: roughly 800,000-850,000 U.S. patients treated for Alzheimer's disease psychosis, about two-thirds on off-label antipsychotics, with no approved therapy.

Four things constrain it. NUPLAZID, at 63% of revenue, is growing 6.7% in H1 against an aspirational $1.0B 2028 target. The EU opportunity is smaller and thinner than the 13% one-day stock move implied: the recommended indication is age five and older versus two and older in the U.S., ex-NA royalties to Neuren are mid-teens to low-twenties percent versus 10-15% in NA, pricing must be negotiated country by country under the new joint HTA regime, the European Commission decision was still outstanding at the August filing date, and substantially all revenue remains North American. Aggregate contingent milestone obligations rose to $3.6B at 6/30/26 — not a solvency issue, but a structural cap on the economics of any success case, particularly ex-NA trofinetide and ercanetide.

Consensus earnings estimates are falling while revenue accelerates. FY2027 consensus (13 analysts) fell from $0.95 to $0.85 over 90 days and $0.88 to $0.85 over 30 days; the current-year figure fell from $0.49 to $0.39 over 90 days, though on a single analyst. Rising revenue with falling EPS estimates is the SG&A question restated in consensus form. This is the opposite of the positive-revision pattern that would justify paying a premium multiple, and it is why we hold growth prospects at **5.8** rather than restoring it toward the 7.2 recorded in March.

## Valuation Assessment

Start by discarding the headline. At $27.15 the quoted 12.4x trailing P/E is an artifact of the tax-benefit-inflated $2.30 FY2025 EPS. On defensible bases:

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Market cap | 172.13M × $27.15 | $4.674B |
| Enterprise value | $4.674B − $956.5M | $3.718B |
| EV / FY2026 revenue | $3.718B / $1.25B midpoint | 3.0x |
| P/E on normalized FY2025 | $27.15 / ~$0.57 | ~48x |
| P/E on derived FY2026 | $27.15 / $0.45-0.60 | 45-60x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus (13 analysts) | $27.15 / $0.85 | 31.9x |
| Ex-cash P/E on FY2027, ex-interest income | ($27.15 − $5.56) / ~$0.72 | ~30x |

The FY2026 derivation: taking the $1.25B revenue midpoint, H2 revenue of ~$674M at Q2's 12.3% operating margin yields ~$83M, giving ~$116M full-year operating income; adding ~$32M interest income and taxing at 30% gives ~$104M net income, roughly $0.60 diluted. The 1-analyst current-year consensus of $0.39 implies H2 EPS of $0.19 against H1's $0.20 actual — effectively assuming H2 margin gives back Q2's recovery. The range $0.45-0.60 spans both readings.

Peer context is of limited use. The stated Pharmaceuticals peer median P/E of 38.4 is drawn from a set including PTCT at negative 32.3x and APLS at 38.4x — companies at different points of profitability with heterogeneous multiples. What we can say is that ACADIA's ~30x ex-cash FY2027 multiple is not a discount to comparable de-risked commercial biopharma, and it applies to an earnings stream where 63% of revenue sits behind a litigated 2030 composition-of-matter expiry.

The bull argument on valuation is that 3.0x EV/sales for a debt-free company growing mid-teens with two labeled monopolies is not demanding, and that consensus targets (mean $33, median $35, high $45, low $17) sit well above spot with 16 of 21 analysts at buy or strong buy. We are not persuaded to treat those targets as a floor here. Analyst targets act as floors on names where estimates are *rising* and the multiple is modest; ACADIA has falling EPS estimates and a 45-60x current-year multiple, so target and price both reflect the same optionality on the same unresolved readout rather than independent confirmation.

What would make this cheap is remlifanserin working. What makes it expensive is that the shares sit marginally above their 2026 closing high entering the readout, so a substantial share of a favorable outcome appears pre-paid while an unfavorable outcome removes the entire pipeline premium.

**Valuation: 5.0.** No identified mispricing in the buyer's favor; not obviously stretched either, given the net-cash cushion and 3.0x EV/sales floor on a growing commercial base.

## Competitive Landscape

Neither marketed product faces an approved direct competitor, which is a genuine but narrow moat: the barrier is label exclusivity, not demonstrated superiority over an approved alternative.

**PDP.** NUPLAZID competes against off-label generic antipsychotics — quetiapine, clozapine, risperidone, aripiprazole, olanzapine — which are cheap and entrenched. Disadvantages are the boxed warning, a several-week onset that the 10-K acknowledges may cause discontinuation before benefit, and under-diagnosis (patients may not report symptoms; neurologists may not screen). At 20-25% penetration of ~130,000 treated patients, headroom exists on paper; the delivered growth rate of 8.7% in Q2 is the relevant evidence.

**Rett syndrome.** DAYBUE has no approved competitor anywhere today, but the pipeline against it is credible and near-term. UCB has announced plans to start a Phase 3 of fenfluramine in Rett during 2026 — a large-cap sponsor with established CNS commercial infrastructure. Taysha (TSHA-102) and Neurogene (NGN-401) both have AAV gene therapies in pivotal trials; a one-time gene therapy, if approved, would displace rather than compete with chronic oral dosing. Unravel Bio is in early-stage work with vorinostat. The 10-Q also flags DAYBUE's own persistence problem unusually explicitly: sub-recommended titration or short duration may cause physicians or patients to "develop negative beliefs about the effectiveness of DAYBUE products." No quantitative persistence or patients-on-therapy disclosure is available, which is the most important missing operating metric in the franchise.

**ADP.** The precedent that matters is ACADIA's own 2021 sNDA for pimavanserin in dementia-related psychosis, which FDA did not approve and which is the subject of the pending securities class action. Remlifanserin shares the 5-HT2A mechanism — a rationale supported by the PDP approval, but also correlated risk with a program FDA previously found insufficient in a dementia population.

**Policy.** ACADIA expects to qualify for the IRA small-biotech exception, exempting NUPLAZID from selection until 2027 with the earliest negotiated price in 2029, and to qualify as a specified small manufacturer for the 2029-2030 discount phase-in. That is a favorable position versus large-cap peers, but it converges with the 2030 composition-of-matter expiry: negotiated pricing and patent expiry arrive in the same window.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Trajectory is mixed: DAYBUE is gaining on STIX and an EU label, NUPLAZID is growing single digits against generics, and the medium-term Rett threat set is unusually well-capitalized.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are stacked and, unusually, several of them resolve within 90 days.

**1. RADIANT Phase 2, September-October 2026.** A binary inside the next two months on the asset that justifies most of the premium over commercial-franchise-plus-cash value. The seamless design means Phase 3 enrollment and spend are already committed (remlifanserin external spend: $85.6M FY2025, $54.0M H1 2026). Fast Track is procedural, not evidence of efficacy. The 10-K's own language notes RADIANT "is subject to certain defined enrollment criteria that caused enrollment to take longer than expected," and the topline window already slipped from "August-October" to "September-October."

**2. NUPLAZID exclusivity is a litigation outcome, not a date.** Composition-of-matter expires 2030 with PTE; polymorph and method-of-use patents expire 2026-2028; later patents run to 2037-2038. Settled entry: Zydus 10mg tablet September 2036, Hetero and Zydus 34mg capsule February 2038 — each "subject to certain triggers for earlier launch," which typically accelerate settled entrants if another generic enters first. ACADIA won Pimavanserin I at the Federal Circuit (June 9, 2025) and Pimavanserin II at district court (June 9, 2025), but MSN and Aurobindo appealed on June 16, 2025, briefing closed December 19, 2025, and **no oral argument had been scheduled as of the August 2026 10-Q**. A separate suit over a proposed Zydus 34mg *tablet* goes to trial November 2, 2026, with ACADIA also alleging breach of the 2023 settlement. Winning preserves the status quo; losing could pull entry forward by years on 63% of revenue and likely cascades through the settlement triggers. Because argument is uncalendared, an adverse ruling can arrive with little warning.

**3. Securities class action with zero reserve, calendar now active.** *City of Birmingham* (S.D. Cal., filed 2021, class certified March 2024) alleges the 2021 dementia-related-psychosis sNDA contained statistical and design deficiencies unlikely to be approved. Discovery closed September 2025, pretrial motions heard April 2026, disclosures exchanged July 10, 2026, with a mandatory settlement conference August 17, 2026 and final pretrial conference September 2, 2026. ACADIA has accrued nothing beyond attorneys' fees, stating it cannot estimate possible loss. The opt-out action (*Alger Dynamic Opportunities Fund*) had its stay lifted July 8, 2026 — a second, uncapped exposure just reactivated. The derivative case settled in principle with $1.5M of fees paid by the carrier.

**4. MFN pricing conflicts directly with ex-U.S. expansion.** The Q2 10-Q materially expanded the healthcare-reform risk factor, adding MFN agreements with several manufacturers, a proposed "Great Healthcare Plan," MAHA Commission DTC-advertising enforcement, and FDA approval of Colorado's Section 804 Canadian importation program on June 15, 2026. The company's own language: if MFN applies to products it commercializes outside the U.S., "our U.S. pricing would have to be reduced to the lowest price paid... In such event, we may choose to forgo the ex-U.S. market to preserve more favorable U.S. pricing." The EU approval that lifted the stock 13% could, under MFN, impair pricing on the far larger U.S. base. Whether MFN reaches ACADIA is unresolved, but it is disclosed as a strategic tension rather than boilerplate.

**5. Gross-to-net estimation quality.** December 2025 brought first CMS Part D inflation-cap rebate invoices for October 2022-September 2024 periods, higher than expected because government and customer data understated NUPLAZID's actual Medicare volume mix. Total unfavorable change ~$20.1M, of which $11.8M related to periods through December 31, 2024. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective and reported no material weakness, and this was genuinely first-cycle data with no baseline — but a core revenue-recognition input for the largest product was misestimated for two years in a business where deductions run ~24% of gross. Next invoice cycle expected June 2027.

**6. Concentration and single-source supply.** Four U.S. customers were approximately 79% of NUPLAZID revenue and 44% of total product revenue at 12/31/25; DAYBUE is sold in the U.S. through a single wholesale distributor. The Q2 10-Q does not update these percentages, so concentration should be assumed unchanged — DAYBUE's growing mix share does not diversify the customer base when DAYBUE itself flows through one counterparty. Manufacturing is fully outsourced with acknowledged single points of failure: Siegfried AG for NUPLAZID API and Patheon for 10mg drug product, with the 10-Q stating ACADIA has "not entered into any agreements with any alternate suppliers" for either. DAYBUE API takes approximately two years to produce. All sites are in Canada, the EU and Switzerland, exposing the company to the pending BIS Section 232 investigation, with tariff costs difficult to pass through before calendar 2027 given 12-18 month formulary contracting.

**7. Baker Bros. supply overhang.** The Baker Entities held ~43.06M shares (~25%) per a May 29, 2026 Form 4, with two affiliated directors. In February 2026 the expired 2016 registration rights agreement was replaced with a 2026 agreement covering all securities now held or later acquired, effective up to 10 years, obliging ACADIA to facilitate underwritten offerings and block trades. Registration rights are standard anchor-investor consideration and renewal does not signal intent to sell, but the issuer carries the cost and process of facilitating its largest holder's exits for a decade, and the company lists it as a risk factor.

**8. EU exclusivity reform.** Political agreement on the EU Pharma Package (December 11, 2025) would cut baseline market protection by a year, restructure orphan incentives, and expand the Bolar exemption to permit reimbursement preparation and tender participation while patents remain in force. Subject to formal approval.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing the readout with a downside tilt and has repriced sharply in the last three weeks. ATM IV rose from 37.3% to 54.5% between July 19 and August 5 while the stock rose 6.1% — rising vol on rising price is characteristic of catalyst anticipation, not complacency. Near-term IV of 54.5% against 37.3% one-year realized is rich (17.3pp spread), with a ±9.6% implied move on the August 21 expiry. The term structure is in contango and skew builds with tenor: 3.2% put skew at one month, 8.3% at three months (September 18, with a ±19.6% implied move), 12.7% at six months. That pattern maps onto the September-October RADIANT window and the November 2 Zydus trial, and it indicates the market is paying up for downside protection across the catalyst path rather than for upside participation. Put/call open interest of 0.20 is call-heavy in absolute terms, so positioning is not uniformly bearish — but 10.2% short interest at 5.9 days to cover means a positive readout has squeeze fuel, which is part of why we do not recommend shorting.

For a fundamental holder the practical implication is that the binary is expensive to insure and expensive to speculate on. Neither buying stock nor buying vol offers an attractive entry here.

**Risk factors: 3.5.** A binary Phase 2 within 60 days at a 2026 price high, an unreserved class action in the pretrial calendar, an uncalendared Federal Circuit appeal on 63% of revenue with settlement-trigger cascade risk, unresolved MFN tension with the growth plan, unchanged four-customer concentration, and no alternate supplier for the largest product's API.

## Management Quality

The forward-looking indicators are weak.

**Insider activity.** Across roughly 25 Form 4 filings from March to July 2026 there were **no open-market purchases** by any officer or director. The pattern is uniformly RSU conversion followed by same- or next-day sale: the CEO converted 23,509 RSUs on March 24 and sold 11,641 shares at $21.47 the next day, holding 14,803 shares directly afterward; the CFO sold on four dates March-May; the EVP of R&D converted 32,272 RSUs July 29 and sold 16,558 on July 30 at ~$26.15; the principal accounting officer sold 11,421 shares at $26.08 on June 26 — the day of the positive CHMP news. Two new 10b5-1 plans were adopted (CFO May 28 for up to 105,328 shares; CCO June 1 for up to 88,771 shares), both commencing November 6, 2026. We would not read motive into 10b5-1 adoption — a November 6 start is the natural first open window after a September-October data event and the Q3 release, and cooling-off periods exist for exactly this purpose. The factual statement is that two officers have pre-committed to potentially selling up to ~194,000 combined shares in the window immediately after the company's largest binary catalyst, and that no one is buying.

**Alignment and record.** The DEF 14A discloses cumulative TSR below the peer index in all five disclosed years, ending FY2025 at an indexed 49.96 versus 119.92 — a shortfall of roughly 58% of peer value.
