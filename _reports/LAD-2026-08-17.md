## Executive Summary

Lithia reported Q2 2026 on July 29 and the stock traded to an all-time high of $439.49 the same session before settling 14.1% lower at $377.55. The release led with a 17% increase in GAAP diluted EPS to $11.54, a 9% increase in adjusted diluted EPS to $10.03, and a 23% dividend increase. Underneath those figures, net income attributable to Lithia rose 1.5% to $260.0M. The entire GAAP EPS increase came from a 13.1% reduction in weighted diluted shares (22.5M versus 25.9M). Same-store revenue fell 1.6%, same-store gross profit fell 2.7%, same-store new units fell 2.2%, same-store used retail units fell 6.1%, and same-store new vehicle gross profit per unit fell 11.1% to $2,718.

The most consequential number in the filings is one management discloses but does not headline: on the same-store adjusted basis that isolates underlying cost leverage, SG&A as a percentage of gross profit worsened 140bps in Q2 to 68.6% and 280bps in H1 to 70.1%. Lithia's 10-K states plainly that the company has no cost advantage in purchasing new vehicles from manufacturers. The scale thesis therefore rests on SG&A leverage, centralized administration and cost of capital — and the metric that measures the first of those is moving the wrong way for a second consecutive period.

Set against that, the captive lender is a real and improving business. Driveway Finance Corporation went from a $45.9M loss in FY2023 to $8.4M in FY2024, $74.6M in FY2025, and $57.8M in H1 2026 alone, with Q2 income up 81.6% to $36.5M. Penetration reached 17.5% of US retail units from 14.8%, against a stated long-term target above 20%. Term ABS coupons fell from 5.58%–7.37% on 2023 vintages to 3.75%–4.42% on the February 2026 issuance. At 10.4x current-year consensus EPS of $36.33 and 9.0x next-year $42.16, with $620.5M of buyback authorization equal to 7.5% of market capitalization, the equity is not expensively priced. It is also not cheap against the direct franchised-dealer comparables — AutoNation at 8.9x and Asbury at 7.8x — which means the low absolute multiple is a sector characteristic rather than a Lithia-specific discount. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Lithia & Driveway operated 467 locations representing 59 brands across the US, UK and Canada at June 30, 2026, up from 455 locations and 54 brands at year-end 2025. H1 2026 revenue split geographically as US $14,583.7M (76.5%), UK $3,819.7M (20.0%) and Canada $659.3M (3.5%).

Two reported segments. Vehicle Operations ($19,062.7M of H1 revenue) covers new and used retail, F&I commissions and aftersales. Financing Operations covers DFC, which is structurally three different businesses under one heading: a pure captive lender in the US originating only from Lithia stores and Driveway; a lender in Canada originating from both Lithia stores and third-party dealerships; and a fleet funding and management division in the UK.

The profit pool is more concentrated than the revenue mix suggests. Aftersales generated $1,247.8M of H1 gross profit — 42.7% of the $2,919.1M total — on 11.1% of revenue, at a 59.1% gross margin that expanded 160bps. Roughly 25% of aftersales revenue is manufacturer warranty or manufacturer-sponsored maintenance; customer-pay was 56.7% of same-store aftersales revenue in Q2. F&I carries 100% gross margin and produced $726.1M in H1.

Two equity-method holdings differentiate Lithia from pure franchised-dealer peers. Pinewood Technologies Group PLC (31.95% voting interest, carried at fair value, $129.5M at 6/30/26 versus $177.2M at 12/31/25) is a UK-listed dealer management system vendor whose software Lithia is rolling out across its own network through 2028. Lithia is simultaneously a minority shareholder in Pinewood and a licensee of Pinewood's software; quarterly fair value changes flow through Other income. Wheels, Inc., a fleet management provider, is held at 26.5% through Lithia Marubeni Mobility Holdings at a $217.0M book value, and paid a $9.8M dividend in H1.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue growth is acquisition-only

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 28,187.8 | 31,042.3 | 36,188.2 | 37,634.9 |
| Gross margin | 18.28% | 16.84% | 15.37% | 15.23% |
| Operating margin | 6.89% | 5.45% | 4.35% | 4.24% |

FY2025 revenue grew 4.0% alongside 17 store acquisitions. H1 2026 revenue grew 1.6% to $19,062.7M while same-store revenue fell 1.5%. Reported used retail units fell 2.7% in Q2 *including* acquisitions; used revenue rose only because same-store used ASPs increased 4.2%. H1 acquisitions consumed $221.7M of cash and contributed $103.1M of revenue and $5.0M of operating income post-close — expected for partial-period ownership, but no in-period offset to organic decline.

### Margin: GPU normalization with no disclosed floor

Same-store new GPU of $2,718 (-11.1%) follows FY2025's $2,904 (-8.2%). New vehicle gross margin compressed 80bps in Q2. Total same-store vehicle gross profit per unit fell 2.4% to $4,119. Offsets were used GPU +6.3% to $2,019, aftersales margin +120bps same-store, and flat F&I per unit at $1,811 — though same-store F&I revenue fell 5.2% on volume. Neither the filings nor the disclosed commentary establishes where normalization ends; H1 new vehicle revenue of $9,208.6M means each 100bps of new gross margin is roughly $92M of gross profit.

Consolidated operating margin was 4.6% in Q2 (4.5% adjusted, flat YoY) but 4.1% in H1 versus 4.4%.

### Earnings quality: two legitimate figures diverge

| | Q2'26 | Q2'25 | H1'26 | H1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income to LAD ($M) | 260.0 | 256.1 | 360.4 | 465.6 |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $11.54 | $9.87 | $15.68 | $17.80 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $10.03 | $9.20 | $17.32 | $17.12 |
| Weighted diluted shares (M) | 22.5 | 25.9 | 23.0 | 26.2 |

H1 GAAP net income fell 22.6% while adjusted EPS rose 1.2%. The gap is dominated by the equity-method line swinging from +$30.9M to -$38.9M, attributed to Pinewood fair value changes and FX remeasurement. The non-GAAP treatment is symmetric — Lithia excluded a $26.7M Pinewood *gain* in H1 2025 and a $45.2M *loss* in H1 2026 — which is a point in the company's favor on disclosure discipline.

One basis note on the earnings-surprise record: the reported Q2 "surprise" of 30.1% compares GAAP $11.54 against a consensus of $8.87. On matching bases the adjusted result was $10.03 versus $8.87, or +13.1%. Still a clear beat, roughly half the headline magnitude.

### Cash flow: the headline overstates generation by ~$1.14B

Reported H1 operating cash flow was **negative $174.1M** versus +$331.4M a year earlier, driven by a $433.8M inventory build and $534.5M of finance-receivable growth. Lithia's "adjusted net cash provided by operating activities" — which adds back non-trade floor plan borrowings and removes finance-receivable growth — was $1,747.8M versus $576.7M, apparently more than tripling.

The footnote to that table discloses that the figure includes $1,138.3M from converting inventory-secured revolvers to floor plan facilities. Stripping it: $1,747.8M − $1,138.3M = $609.5M versus $576.7M, **+5.7%**. Corroborating that this is mechanical: Q2 floor plan interest rose $14.7M (+26.7%) while other interest fell $15.6M (-43.2%); floor plan debt rose from $5,008.9M to $6,387.4M while revolving lines were net repaid $500.0M.

The commercial rationale is documented and credible — the February 27, 2026 credit amendment cut the used-vehicle floor plan margin from 1.40% to 1.20%, eliminated a 0.10% credit spread adjustment, and converted facilities to VIN-specific structures. Moving borrowings to a cheaper facility is straightforward interest optimization, and Lithia disclosed the cash-flow effect prominently. The point is how the number should be read, not what motivated it. FY2025's adjusted OCF of $1,291.3M (against reported $356.7M) is the better run-rate anchor; neither H1 figure should be annualized given genuine seasonality (North America weakest in Q1; UK strongest in Q1 and Q3 on registration-plate practice).

### Balance sheet and liquidity

At June 30: total assets $26,245.8M, liabilities $19,818.4M, equity $6,427.4M. Total debt $15,912.7M, of which inventory-related $6,390.9M and financing-operations-related $4,200.4M. Company-defined net debt $5,143.6M, or 0.80x equity. Fixed-rate notes: $400M 4.625% due 2027, $800M 3.875% due 2029, $600M 5.500% due 2030, $550M 4.375% due 2031, plus $1,106.7M of mortgages, finance leases and other. 63% of total debt was variable-rate at year-end 2025 — a direct exposure to the higher-for-longer rate path.

Two liquidity facts matter more than the leverage ratio. Total available funds fell 16.9% to $1,267.5M, with cash of $110.3M and available credit down $269.0M to $1,090.2M. And DFC's warehouse facilities are drawn $1,459.0M with only **$32.2M remaining** — DFC's growth is entirely contingent on uninterrupted term ABS access, a dependency the 10-K acknowledges could force a pause in origination if alternative financing were unavailable. On the company's own preferred measure, debt-to-total-capital excluding floor plan and non-recourse debt rose from 48.4% to 52.5%.

Recomputing Altman Z on FY2025 10-K inputs and the current market cap gives 2.39 — grey zone, and materially above what an operating-income-tagged screen would return, because Lithia's separate "Financing operations income" line ($74.6M) is easily mistaken for consolidated operating income ($1,594.7M). Altman Z is in any case poorly calibrated for franchised auto retail, where floor plan debt is short-dated, inventory-collateralized and self-liquidating, and partly supplied by manufacturer-affiliated lenders.

### Capital allocation

Cumulative repurchases total 13,092,455 shares at an average $231.39 (~$3.03B). H1 2026: 1,795,491 shares at $279.07 average, $534.0M spent — 2.4x the $221.7M on acquisitions. Shares outstanding fell to 22.0M from 23.5M in six months (-6.4%). $620.5M authorization remains after a $500M addition on May 26, 2026, equal to 7.5% of market capitalization.

Management's stated logic is explicit: with acquisition pricing elevated, repurchases may be the more attractive use of funds. Given a disclosed acquisition framework of 3x–6x intangibles-to-EBITDA and the stock at ~11x earnings, that judgment is internally coherent. The blended $231.39 cost basis against $377.55 represents a +63% return on repurchased capital. Two qualifications: the average spans several years and says nothing about the timing of the 2026 tranche at $279.07; and the buybacks were funded during a period of negative reported operating cash flow and a 16.9% liquidity decline, which is balance-sheet capacity deployment rather than free-cash-flow return.

Dilution is genuinely absent. The diluted/basic gap is 0.0–0.1M shares and the 10-Q describes antidilutive effects as immaterial. Reported diluted shares are, for practical purposes, the fully diluted capitalization.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has current-year adjusted EPS at $36.33 (+5.0% in 30 days, +5.6% in 90 days) and next year at $42.16 (+3.3%/+3.9%). These are positive revisions but well below the magnitude that signals a genuine earnings inflection.

The achievability check matters. H1 adjusted EPS was $17.32 versus $17.12, +1.2%. Reaching $36.33 requires H2 of $19.01 versus $16.34, +16.3% — which looks demanding until decomposed. At H1 weighted shares of 23.0M and 22.0M outstanding at quarter-end, H2 average shares near 21.7M against roughly 24.5M a year earlier delivers most of the required growth on approximately 3% net income growth. DFC alone, growing from $36.5M in Q2 toward the 20%+ penetration target, plausibly supplies that. Current-year consensus is credible.

FY2027's $42.16 is harder. From $36.33 that is +16.1%; another ~6% share-count reduction supplies roughly 6pp, leaving ~9% net income growth to be found from DFC ramp and GPU stabilization. My base case uses $39.00, roughly half that operating contribution.

The organic engine is contracting. Same-store units are down across new and used, GPU is normalizing with no disclosed floor, and the acquisition pipeline is being deliberately de-emphasized on price. The UK agency-model transition mechanically reduces reported revenue, SG&A and floor plan interest with an uncertain net profit effect — and Lithia acquired Preston, Chester & Derby Group 1 Jaguar Land Rover in May 2026, adding exposure to a brand on the announced-agency list. Three of six H1 acquisitions were UK.

Durable growth vectors: DFC penetration 17.5% → 20%+ on a growing receivable base with a documented 200-250bps funding cost tailwind; aftersales gross profit growing with 160bps of margin expansion, framed by management as counter-cyclical; and share-count reduction with 7.5% of market cap authorized. Structural headwind: electrification compresses the aftersales and extended-warranty profit pool that currently supplies 42.7% of gross profit, anchored to California's 2035 mandate and the UK's 2030/2035 rules.

## Competitive Landscape

Lithia characterizes automotive retail as massive and unconsolidated, and simultaneously as mature with minimal expected growth in new unit sales. Both are true, and they jointly define the model: flat addressable volume, so growth requires consolidation, share gain and adjacent services.

The critical structural limitation is disclosed in the 10-K: Lithia has no cost advantage in purchasing new vehicles from manufacturers. Scale benefits must come from SG&A leverage, centralized administration, inventory breadth, captive finance economics and cost of capital. The same-store adjusted SG&A/gross profit deterioration of 140bps in Q2 and 280bps in H1 is therefore a direct challenge to the scale thesis. Some allowance is warranted — recently acquired stores sit outside the same-store base while their integration costs sit inside consolidated SG&A, and newly acquired UK stores likely carry higher cost ratios pending integration — but this is the second consecutive period of deterioration and the metric to monitor.

Where competitive position is improving: DFC penetration from 14.8% to 17.5% captures F&I and interest-margin economics previously ceded to third-party lenders, with credit metrics improving on every disclosed measure (annualized net losses 0.9% versus 1.4%, past-due 3.3% versus 4.6%, recovery 50.1% versus 47.8%, weighted average FICO 748). Aftersales margin expanded 160bps. The Pinewood DMS rollout reduces dependency on CDK, whose June 2024 cybersecurity incident measurably dented same-store sales.

Threats named in the filings, in order of specificity: Scout Motors secured a Colorado dealer license in 2025 permitting direct-to-consumer sales to October 2026, following Tesla and Rivian precedents; Chinese and Indian manufacturers entering the US and selecting distribution partners, where new-brand success is additive to Lithia only if Lithia holds the franchise; e-commerce-only competitors; non-ownership consumption models; and F&I competition from financial institutions. Five manufacturers — Honda, Toyota, Ford, BMW and Stellantis — represent 25% of sales, and four of five carry substantial import exposure, making differential tariff incidence a live but unquantified consideration. In the UK, franchise-protection law does not exist; Lithia's rights there are contractual only, on 20% of revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**UK motor finance redress — unquantified and unaccrued.** The FCA published its motor finance redress scheme on March 30, 2026, covering agreements from April 2007 to November 2024, with an average payout near £829 and industry cost estimated at £9.1bn. Parts of the scheme have been suspended pending legal challenge. Lithia's FY2025 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, predates publication and describes the matter as still on appeal; the Q2 10-Q did not refresh Item 1A. Lithia discloses no accrual and no quantified exposure.

The counterparty analysis materially reframes this. The scheme obliges **lenders** to compensate consumers, and every named participant in external reporting — Lloyds, Santander, FirstRand/Aldermore — is a lender. Lithia's UK dealerships acted as brokers receiving commissions *from* lenders. Direct exposure would arise only through liability for its own regulated broking conduct or lender attempts to recover commissions from brokers, which is precisely the contingency Lithia's own risk factor contemplates. The larger and already-observable effect is indirect: the 10-K notes lenders pausing underwriting caused significant UK market disruption and reduced sales volume, and that UK dealers must now explicitly disclose commissions to customers. Lower go-forward commission rates on a fifth of revenue is a margin issue independent of any retrospective liability. An investor cannot verify Lithia's conclusion from the filings, because Lithia has never stated it.

**DFC credit and residual structure.** Current loss performance is strong, but it is being earned in a benign used-vehicle-value environment on a portfolio originated at 95.6% weighted average front-end LTV over 72-month weighted average terms, with a 2.9% allowance ($153.4M against $5,407.5M) and 50.1% average recovery on repossession. High LTV plus six-year amortization means an extended period of borrower negative equity. Loss rates also decline mechanically as a fast-growing portfolio's average age falls — the 2026 origination cohort is already $1,497.7M of the $5,179.7M auto loan book and is largely unseasoned. Add the $32.2M of remaining warehouse capacity and DFC is a levered growth engine dependent on continuous ABS access.

**Rate and macro exposure.** 63% variable-rate debt, floor plan rates of 4.78%–4.88% and revolver at 4.93%. DFC contract rates fell from 8.7% to 8.0% following Federal Reserve rate cuts; a reversal compresses both affordability and floor plan cost. Same-store used ASPs +4.2% against same-store used units -6.1% is consistent with affordability constraint persisting.

**Governance and alignment.** The proxy discloses cumulative TSR below the company's selected peer group in all five reported years (2025: 117.15 versus 146.89; five-year 17.2% versus 46.9%), while CEO compensation actually paid rose to $22,817,359 in FY2025 from $20,673,914, in a year the TSR index fell. The company-selected performance measure is Revenue — a weak alignment metric at an acquisition-driven consolidator, since revenue is purchasable regardless of return on capital. Mitigating: the measure set also includes EPS and TSR, comp actually paid is mark-to-market volatile in both directions, and the relative gap narrowed from -41.9 to -29.7 index points. Four Item 5.02 filings in roughly twelve months are unresolved (underlying documents inaccessible), and NEO average total compensation fell 42.6% to $2,612,837, consistent with — but not proof of — NEO population change.

**Insider activity carries no signal here.** Across Form 4 filings from August 2025 through August 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director. Activity consists of January 2026 annual grants and Code F tax-withholding dispositions, plus trivially small director sales (75–297 shares, pattern-consistent with automatic plans). This is not bearish — withholding and small automatic sales carry no informational content, and executives receive large annual RSU grants. It does mean the "insiders are buying" support that often accompanies value cases is absent. The buyback is management's expressed confidence, not personal purchases.

**Key-man risk, contractually embedded.** Bryan DeBoer is identified in most store franchise agreements as the individual on whose resources and expertise manufacturers may rely when awarding or approving franchise transfers. The credit agreement's 35% change-of-control trigger carves out Lithia Holdings Company, Sidney DeBoer and Bryan DeBoer.

**Other:** subjective acceleration clauses permitting lenders to accelerate on deeming themselves insecure or on a material adverse change; cross-default provisions; the June 2026 presentation change combining used wholesale into used retail and fleet into new retail, which reduces line-item comparability against the FY2025 10-K.

The chain prices a near-term event rather than a directional regime change. Near-term ATM IV of 41.2% against 37.1% one-year realized is rich by 4.0 points, at the 69th percentile within the chain, with the term structure in backwardation (41.2% near versus 34.9% at six months). Put/call open interest is 1.23 and the three-day expiry shows a 3.2% put/call skew the data source characterizes as elevated put demand; the one-month tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 2.14 against an implied move of 8.3%. It also argues against an outright bearish stance at this price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($480, +27.1%).** DFC reaches its 20%+ penetration target on a growing receivable base while ABS coupons stay 200-250bps below 2023 vintages, taking Financing Operations income from an ~$115M annualized run rate toward $150M+. New GPU normalization completes in 2027 and same-store gross profit inflects positive. Same-store SG&A/gross profit deterioration proves to be an integration artifact of the six H1 acquisitions and reverses as UK stores are absorbed. The remaining $620.5M authorization plus continued deployment retires another 10-12% of shares, delivering consensus $42.16 in FY2027 against which 11.4x is not demanding for a business with 42.7% of gross profit in counter-cyclical aftersales. UK redress liability lands where the counterparty analysis suggests — on lenders, not brokers — and the risk factor is quietly retired. A 15.4% short base amplifies the move.

**Bear case ($285, -24.5%).** GPU normalization continues past 2027 with no floor, taking same-store gross profit down another 3-5% annually while same-store SG&A/gross profit stays above 70%. Operating margin compresses from 4.1% toward 3.5%, and net income falls even as share count shrinks, so EPS stalls near $34-36. UK commission rates reset lower on 20% of revenue and lenders pursue broker recovery, forcing a first-time accrual. DFC's 95.6% LTV / 72-month book seasons into a used-value decline, pushing net losses from 0.9% toward the 2.9% allowance and requiring a reserve build, while $32.2M of remaining warehouse capacity constrains the response. Liquidity, already down 16.9% with $110.3M of cash, forces the buyback to slow, removing the sole source of EPS growth. The multiple converges to AutoNation's 8.9x and Asbury's 7.8x.

**Base case ($410, +8.6%).** Consensus current-year $36.33 is achieved on roughly flat H2 net income plus buyback. FY2027 lands near $39.00 as DFC adds $30-40M of pre-tax income and buyback retires another 6% of shares, partly offset by continued GPU decline and no same-store cost leverage improvement. The multiple holds near 10.5x on the strength of the captive-finance contribution and the shrinking share count.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment point:** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026. The three specific disclosures to check are (a) same-store adjusted SG&A/gross profit — the direction of this single line determines whether the scale thesis is intact; (b) same-store new GPU sequential trend against Q2's $2,718; and (c) DFC penetration against the 17.5% Q2 level and warehouse availability against the $32.2M remaining.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Same-store adjusted SG&A/gross profit improving year over year for one quarter, confirming the H1 deterioration was integration-driven rather than structural.
2. Same-store new GPU flat-to-up sequentially, establishing a normalization floor, alongside same-store gross profit returning to growth.
3. A price near $310-320 — roughly 8.0x FY2027 consensus $42.16, at which the premium to AutoNation and Asbury is largely eliminated and the base case offers ~28% upside.
4. DFC penetration reaching 19%+ with warehouse capacity restored above $300M and continued net loss rates below 1.2%.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of the following:**
1. A first-time accrual or quantified disclosure of UK motor finance commission exposure, or a lender recovery claim against Lithia's UK broking entities.
2. Total available funds falling below $1.0B, or the buyback pace decelerating materially below the H1 run rate of $534M per half-year while acquisitions continue.
3. DFC annualized net credit losses exceeding 1.5%, or an allowance build above 3.5% of ending managed receivables.
4. Same-store gross profit declining more than 5% year over year in any quarter, or new GPU falling below $2,400.
5. Current-year consensus revised down more than 5% over any 90-day window.

**Position guidance for existing owners:** hold. The 10.4x forward multiple, 7.5%-of-cap remaining authorization, and documented DFC earnings ramp are sufficient reason not to exit at 14% below the July high. **For new capital:** no urgent entry at $377.55. The stock is 57.5% above its March low and the base case offers under 9% upside against a 24.5% bear. The staged entry range is $310-320.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report — 6.0 in March, 5.4 in April, 5.5 in May and June, 5.1 in July — has been declining, and the Q2 print does not reverse it. It divides cleanly. Driveway Finance is a documented, mechanism-backed inflection: three years of improving income, a 200-250bps funding-cost tailwind visible in ABS coupons, penetration two-thirds of the way to its target, and credit metrics improving on every disclosed measure. That leg justifies part of Lithia's premium to AutoNation and Asbury and supports current-year consensus.

The offsetting evidence is that the core retail business is contracting organically while the metric that measures Lithia's only stated source of scale advantage deteriorates.
