## Executive Summary

Comcast trades at $27.94, representing approximately 5.2x trailing EPS of $5.39 and 7.8x forward consensus of $3.56 for FY2026. The trailing multiple is misleading: FY2025 earnings were inflated by a $9.4B pre-tax gain from the Hulu interest sale, making the forward multiple the more relevant valuation anchor. Even adjusted, the stock is cheap relative to Communication Services peers and to its own history. The 4.7% dividend yield, 18 consecutive years of dividend growth, and $21.9B in annual FCF provide a meaningful income floor.

The core business is deteriorating. Domestic broadband subscribers fell 711,000 in 2025, accelerating from -411,000 in 2024. Analyst estimates have been cut across all periods: current-year EPS down 8.8% over 90 days, next-year down 5.0%. The Versant spinoff (completed January 2, 2026) shed declining linear cable networks but also removed revenue and EBITDA that won't be replaced in the near term. Peacock continues to lose ~$1.1B annually. The broadband subscriber trajectory — the single most important variable for Comcast's equity value — is worsening, not stabilizing.

The investment case requires conviction that Comcast can stabilize broadband penetration through DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades, wireless bundling, and StreamSaver aggregation, while Epic Universe and theme park expansion provide a growth offset. These are plausible paths, but none has yet produced evidence of subscriber stabilization. At 7.8x forward earnings with a 4.7% yield, the stock is defensible as an income position, but the deteriorating fundamentals prevent a BUY recommendation until subscriber trends stabilize.

## Company Overview

Comcast is the largest US cable operator by subscribers, with 31.3 million domestic broadband customers across a 65-million homes-passed footprint. Following the January 2, 2026 Versant Media Group spinoff, the company operates primarily through Connectivity & Platforms (Xfinity broadband, wireless, video; Sky UK/Italy) and Content & Experiences (NBC broadcast, Telemundo, Peacock streaming, Universal Studios, Universal theme parks).

The company generated $123.7B in revenue in FY2025 (flat YoY) and $33.6B in operating cash flow. It employs a dual-class share structure: Chairman and Co-CEO Brian Roberts controls 100% of Class B stock, giving him non-dilutable 33⅓% voting power plus veto authority over mergers and governance changes. Michael Cavanagh serves as Co-CEO, and a new head of Connectivity & Platforms (Steve Croney) was appointed in late 2025, signaling management urgency about broadband.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Flat at $123.7B in both FY2024 and FY2025. The core Residential Connectivity & Platforms segment declined 1.2% ($71.6B to $70.7B), with video revenue down 5.1% and advertising down 9.2%. Domestic wireless (+16.3%) and international connectivity (+10.2%) partially offset these declines.

**Earnings:** Reported EPS of $5.39 was inflated by the $9.4B Hulu gain. Adjusted EBITDA declined 1.8% ($38.1B to $37.4B), and Residential Connectivity EBITDA margin compressed 50bps to 37.7%. Operating income excluding the Hulu gain fell 11.3%. The underlying earnings power is declining, and consensus reflects this: FY2026 EPS estimate of $3.56 is 34% below the reported $5.39, and FY2027 at $3.85 still below the pre-Hulu normalized level.

**Free Cash Flow:** $21.9B in FCF (17.7% margin) is exceptional, though it benefited from a dramatic reduction in cash taxes ($7.1B to $0.8B) due to capital loss carrybacks and accelerated depreciation provisions. Normalized FCF is lower but still substantial — probably in the $16-18B range, or roughly $4.50/share, covering the $1.32 dividend 3.4x.

**Balance Sheet:** $98.9B in total debt, 2.81x D/E. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 is technically in the distress zone but is a mechanical artifact of heavy goodwill ($84B combined goodwill/intangibles) and low asset turnover. The weighted-average debt maturity of ~15 years, 95% fixed at 3.8% average coupon, and $11.8B in undrawn revolving credit provide substantial cushion. Current ratio of 0.88 is below 1.0, a persistent working capital concern.

**Capital Return:** $6.8B in buybacks (5.1% share count reduction) and $4.9B in dividends in FY2025, totaling $11.7B or 53% of FCF. $8.9B remains on the current $15B buyback authorization. At $27.94, buybacks are accretive and aggressive repurchasing at these levels could meaningfully support EPS even as the top line contracts.

**Earnings Quality:** 3 beats in 4 quarters, with a Q3 2025 miss of -0.9%. The beat pattern suggests modest conservatism in guidance, but the Q3 miss coincided with accelerating broadband losses, which warrants caution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated: structural decline in legacy segments versus emerging growth in wireless, streaming, and theme parks.

**Declining:**
- Broadband subscribers: -711K in 2025, accelerating from -411K in 2024. Wells Fargo projects cable companies could lose 1 million residential broadband subs in 2026. Penetration fell from 49.8% to 47.6% of homes passed.
- Video subscribers: -1.3M in 2025 to 11.3M. Irreversible secular decline.
- Video revenue: -5.1% YoY. Advertising revenue: -9.2%.

**Growing:**
- Wireless: +16.3% revenue, +1.5M net lines to 9.3M. Capital-light MVNO model on Verizon's network. Bundling with broadband is the primary retention strategy.
- Theme parks: $9.8B revenue (+14.2%), with Q4 posting $2.9B (+22%) and first-ever quarterly EBITDA above $1B. Epic Universe in Orlando opened May 2025 and is still ramping below full capacity. FY2026 will be its first full year. UK park planned for 2031.
- International connectivity: +10.2% (Sky UK/Italy broadband expansion).
- Peacock: Revenue grew to $5.4B (from $3.2B in 2024), paid subs at 44M. Still losing $1.1B annually. Domestic-only strategy limits total addressable market.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current-year EPS consensus has fallen from $3.90 to $3.56 over 90 days (-8.8%). Next-year has fallen from $4.05 to $3.85 (-5.0%). Current-quarter estimates dropped 12.5% in 90 days. This is broad, sustained negative revision momentum across all periods, which typically leads prices rather than follows them.

The critical question is whether wireless cross-selling and DOCSIS 4.0 can slow or reverse broadband losses. DOCSIS 4.0 now covers "millions" of homes (up from ~1M in 2024), enabling multi-gigabit symmetrical speeds over existing coax — a meaningful competitive response to fiber at lower capex intensity. The StreamSaver bundles (aggregating Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, Max, Peacock, Apple TV+ at up to 45% savings) position Xfinity as a streaming super-aggregator, potentially improving broadband stickiness. These strategies are directionally correct but have not yet produced a subscriber stabilization.

## Competitive Landscape

Comcast faces structurally intensifying competition in its core broadband business from three vectors:

1. **Fiber overbuilders:** AT&T, Verizon Fios, and smaller providers are aggressively deploying fiber within Comcast's footprint. Fiber offers superior upload speeds and lower latency. Comcast's DOCSIS 4.0 response narrows the performance gap at lower capital cost, but "near-fiber" may not be sufficient for customers who can get actual fiber.

2. **Fixed wireless access (FWA):** T-Mobile and Verizon offer 5G home internet at $25-50/month, significantly undercutting cable broadband pricing. FWA captured the majority of recent broadband net additions industry-wide. While throughput limitations prevent FWA from serving all use cases, it is effective enough for many households.

3. **Government-subsidized builds:** The $42.5B BEAD program will fund new broadband infrastructure, some of which will overlap Comcast's footprint, creating new competitors in previously uncontested markets.

In theme parks, Epic Universe positions Universal Orlando as a multi-day destination competing directly with Disney World. The early results are strong, and the park is still below capacity. In streaming, Peacock's domestic-only 44M subscriber base is dwarfed by Netflix and Disney+, but the super-aggregator strategy (StreamSaver bundles) is strategically differentiated — Comcast is positioning as the platform through which consumers access multiple services, rather than competing directly in the content arms race.

Comcast retains meaningful scale advantages: 65M homes passed, the largest cable footprint, business services at 55.9% EBITDA margins, and a fully integrated content/distribution ecosystem. These advantages are real but eroding as competition intensifies.

## Risk Assessment

**Broadband subscriber erosion (HIGH):** The most important risk. Each lost subscriber reduces recurring high-margin revenue. If the rate of decline accelerates despite DOCSIS 4.0 and wireless bundling, the equity's value proposition breaks down. Wells Fargo's projection of -1M industry-wide cable broadband subs in 2026 suggests continued pressure.

**Estimate deterioration (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Negative revision momentum of the current magnitude typically persists for 1-2 additional quarters before stabilizing. No evidence yet of a trough in estimates.

**Leverage (MEDIUM):** $98.9B in debt at 2.81x D/E is manageable given FCF generation and favorable debt structure, but limits strategic flexibility. A recession-driven decline in advertising and theme park attendance would compress EBITDA while debt obligations remain fixed.

**Peacock losses (MEDIUM):** $1.1B annual loss is improving but persistent. If subscriber growth stalls at 44M, the path to profitability extends, absorbing FCF that could otherwise go to debt reduction or buybacks.

**Dual-class control (MEDIUM):** Brian Roberts' non-dilutable 33⅓% voting control plus veto power removes shareholder influence over strategic direction. The Versant spinoff was Roberts' call; investors had no recourse to oppose it.

**Consumer macro weakness (MEDIUM):** Record-low consumer sentiment (47.6) and rising energy costs pressure discretionary spending on broadband upgrades, theme parks, and streaming subscriptions. Theme park revenue is particularly exposed — a recession could reverse the Epic Universe-driven growth trajectory.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($36-38, +29-36%)
- DOCSIS 4.0 deployment accelerates, narrowing broadband loss rate to <200K/year by H2 2026
- Wireless reaches 12M+ lines, meaningfully contributing to bundled ARPU and retention
- Epic Universe reaches full capacity, driving theme park EBITDA above $5B annually
- Peacock reaches breakeven by late 2027
- $8.9B in remaining buyback authorization reduces share count another 8-10% at current prices
- Multiple re-rates to 10-11x forward earnings as the market credits stabilization: 10.5x × $3.56 = $37.40

### Bear Case ($20-22, -21-28%)
- Broadband losses accelerate to 1M+ in 2026, validating secular decline thesis
- FWA and fiber competition forces broadband price cuts, compressing margins further
- Recession reduces theme park attendance and advertising revenue simultaneously
- Peacock losses persist or worsen as content costs rise
- Leverage becomes constraining if EBITDA declines 10%+
- Multiple compresses to 6x forward: 6x × $3.56 = $21.36

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Broadband net subscriber losses narrow to <100K in any single quarter, demonstrating stabilization
2. Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive months
3. Price falls to $24 or below (implying ~6.7x FY2026 EPS), providing sufficient margin of safety to absorb continued deterioration

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Broadband net losses exceed 300K in any single quarter (annualized pace of 1.2M+)
2. Peacock losses widen beyond $1.5B annually
3. Dividend is cut or buyback program is suspended

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late April / early May 2026). Q1 2026 broadband subscriber data will be the most important single data point. A second reassessment following Q2 results when Epic Universe's first full-year contribution becomes clearer.

## Conclusion

Comcast is a high-quality cash flow generator trading at a historically cheap valuation, with a 4.7% dividend yield and aggressive buyback program providing meaningful total return. The Versant spinoff simplified the business, and Epic Universe is a genuine growth asset. However, the core broadband business is in accelerating decline, estimates are falling across all periods, and management's competitive responses (DOCSIS 4.0, wireless bundling, StreamSaver) have not yet produced subscriber stabilization.

The prior trajectory from BUY to HOLD reflected deteriorating competitive dynamics, and that deterioration has continued. Competitive Position, Management Quality, and Risk Factors scores remain at their lower levels because the underlying conditions that drove the downgrades have not reversed.

At current valuation, the stock is defensible as an income position. FCF generation provides a substantial floor, and aggressive buybacks at these levels are accretive. But until broadband subscriber trends stabilize, the stock remains a HOLD. The risk/reward is reasonable, but the downside scenario (value trap in a secularly declining business) is plausible enough to prevent higher conviction.
