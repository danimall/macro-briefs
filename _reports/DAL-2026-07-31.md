## Executive Summary

Delta absorbed a roughly 78% year-over-year increase in jet fuel prices (Q2 2026 adjusted fuel $3.93/gallon versus $2.21) and reaffirmed the full-year adjusted EPS guidance it set in January, $6.50-7.50, without cutting it. Q2 adjusted revenue rose 14% to $17.67B on approximately 1% capacity growth, with total unit revenue up 12.4% and corporate sales up 20%. Management passed through roughly 60% of the incremental fuel cost in Q2 and guided Q3 to close to 100%, which is why Q3 operating margin is guided to 11-13% against 8.8% delivered in Q2. Consensus has followed: the current-quarter estimate moved from $1.72 to $2.23 over thirty days, and FY2027 sits at $8.79.

The mechanics behind the recovery are real and partly structural. Spirit Airlines ceased operations on May 2, 2026, removing a large block of domestic ULCC seats. Engine availability, not airframe output, is the binding constraint on industry capacity growth, which limits how quickly competitors can respond to high fares. Delta is deliberately shrinking main cabin capacity (down 2-3% in Q2) while holding premium capacity roughly flat, and premium revenue exceeded main cabin revenue for the first time. American Express remuneration is guided to approximately $9B in 2026, up about 10%. Delta TechOps is guided to roughly $1.2B of revenue, up about 50%. The Trainer refinery flipped from roughly break-even to $351M of segment operating income in a single quarter as crack spreads widened.

Where we step back from the prior BUY is on the near-term setup rather than the business. The Q3 guide rests on an all-in fuel assumption of approximately $3.15/gallon, a 20% sequential decline from Q2's realized $3.93, and it was set on July 10. Crude subsequently gained roughly 20% over the month of July with multiple active disruption zones in the Middle East, and management itself described crack spreads as potentially "sticky." The refinery, which is the offset that made the reaffirmed guidance possible, was running at 75% of throughput after a June 25 fire with no disclosed restart date. Current-quarter consensus of $2.23 now sits essentially at the guide midpoint of $2.25, so there is no cushion in expectations. Against a base case of $105 (+18%) and a bear case of $68 (-23%), the asymmetry that justified a BUY at $70 in May has compressed to roughly one-for-one at $88.59, seven percent below the 52-week high. We hold the position and would add on weakness rather than at current levels.

## Company Overview

Delta operates one of three U.S. global network franchises, hubbed in Atlanta with Detroit, Minneapolis-St. Paul, Salt Lake City, New York, Boston, Los Angeles and Seattle. What separates it from a generic airline income statement is the share of revenue that does not originate as a main-cabin ticket. Management characterizes "diverse revenue streams" as 61% of Q2 revenue, up two points year over year, with premium and loyalty revenue each up nearly 20%, cargo up 39%, and MRO up 30%.

Four components matter for the investment case:

**Loyalty and co-brand.** American Express purchases SkyMiles miles, brand and marketing services, and cardholder benefits. FY2025 remuneration was approximately $8.2B, up about 11%; 2026 is guided to approximately $9B, with card spend growing double digits for seven consecutive quarters and a stated long-term $10B target. Amex is a paying commercial counterparty, not a passenger customer.

**Premium segmentation.** Delta has extended a basic/classic/extra fare architecture into Delta Comfort and is rolling it across all premium cabins. Premium capacity has not grown for several years and management says it will not grow next year either, which concentrates the pricing benefit.

**Non-passenger businesses.** Monroe Energy operates the Trainer, Pennsylvania refinery (approximately 208,000 b/d). Delta TechOps is a third-party MRO with a stated ambition to more than double revenue over several years at mid-teens margins.

**Foreign carrier stakes and joint ventures.** Minority positions in Aeroméxico, LATAM, Korean Air/Hanjin, Air France-KLM, China Eastern, Virgin Atlantic, and roughly 12.7% of WestJet. These generate substantial non-cash mark-to-market volatility in GAAP results.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline.** Revenue approximately $63.4B, operating income approximately $5.82B (9.2% margin), net income approximately $5.0B (7.9% margin), ROE 24.0%, asset turnover 0.78x, current ratio 0.40x.

An important accounting distinction: FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of roughly $7.65 sits well above FY2025 adjusted EPS, which the "+20% growth" framing around the $6.50-7.50 2026 guide implies at roughly $5.83 at the midpoint. The gap of approximately $1.80 per share is attributable primarily to gains on equity investments and refinery/hedge accounting items. The provided data field showing trailing and forward P/E at an identical 14.70x is inconsistent with the stated $7.66 EPS ($88.59/$7.66 = 11.6x) and appears to reference a different earnings basis. We use our own computed multiples throughout.

**Q2 2026.**

| | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| GAAP revenue | $19.757B | ~$16.6B |
| Adjusted revenue | $17.67B | — |
| GAAP operating income | $1.864B | ~$2.10B |
| GAAP net income | $1.604B | ~$2.13B |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $2.44 | $3.27 |
| Adjusted EPS | $1.56 | — |

The $1.56 adjusted result beat non-GAAP consensus of $1.48 (LSEG); adjusted revenue of $17.67B beat $17.53B. The GAAP $2.44 should not be described as a beat because no clean GAAP consensus exists. The $0.88 gap between GAAP and adjusted is principally a ~$349M pretax mark-to-market gain on equity investments plus roughly $300M of hedge and refinery adjustments.

**GAAP earnings are close to uninformative quarter to quarter.** H1 2026 net income of $1.315B against Q2's $1.604B implies a GAAP net loss of approximately $289M in Q1 2026, despite positive Q1 operating income of roughly $501M. The Q1 loss was driven substantially by approximately $551M of mark-to-market losses on the equity portfolio. These items are non-cash and do not touch operating cash flow, free cash flow or debt capacity, but any GAAP-multiple exercise on DAL is unreliable.

**Fuel is the dominant variable.** Q2 fuel expense was approximately $4.4B, up nearly $2B year over year, rising to roughly 21% of H1 operating expense from 17%. Full-year 2026 fuel cost is guided up nearly $4B. Delta carries no meaningful hedge book. Implied consumption of roughly 1.12B gallons per quarter (4.3-4.5B annually) means each $0.10/gallon move is approximately $430-450M pretax annually, or about $0.50 per diluted share after tax at a 24% rate, and roughly $0.125 per share per quarter. This is a static sensitivity that ignores the refinery offset and revenue recapture.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $4.0B, reinvestment $2.6B, free cash flow $1.4B. FY2026 FCF is guided to $3-4B, which on a $57.9B market capitalization is a 5.2-6.9% yield (midpoint approximately 6.0%). We do not annualize the H1 figure: air traffic liability builds reverse through the summer flying season, so H1 FCF is seasonally depressed and the company's own full-year range is the better anchor, implying H2 FCF of $1.6-2.6B.

Adjusted net debt was $13.6B at June 30, down from year-end; balance-sheet total debt approximately $12.9B against equity of approximately $21.8B. Management targets 2.0x gross leverage by year-end 2026 and 1.0x long term, with investment-grade ratings from all three agencies. Current maturities of debt and finance leases rose to roughly $3.4B, including a $1.25B term loan due December 2026.

Two balance-sheet caveats deserve honesty. The Altman Z-Score of 1.28 computed on the FY2025 filing sits nominally in the distress zone, but the driver is negative working capital of -0.205 of assets, which reflects air traffic liability and approximately $9.6B of loyalty deferred revenue sitting in current liabilities and settled by delivering future service rather than cash. The same artifact produces the 0.40 current ratio. The screen also reported equity-to-assets and equity-to-liabilities as identical values (0.256), which is arithmetically impossible and understates the Z-Score; treat it as directionally low and approximate. Against that, the real cash-settling claims are the $3.4B of current maturities and roughly $27.6B of aircraft purchase commitments, and Delta remains the least levered U.S. network carrier.

**Capital allocation is sending two signals.** The quarterly dividend was raised 15% to $0.2150 (declared June 18, 2026), a 0.97% yield at $88.59. A $1.0B repurchase authorization approved in June 2025 had produced zero repurchases as of June 30, 2026. Aircraft purchase commitments rose from approximately $15.4B at December 31, 2025 to approximately $27.6B at June 30, 2026, a roughly $12B increase in six months reflecting 787-10, Airbus widebody and A321neo option exercises. The deleveraging-first sequencing is defensible for a business that just held guidance through a fuel shock. The unused buyback is also consistent with management not viewing the shares as compellingly cheap, and the commitment increase creates a competing claim on the same cash flow. We cannot distinguish between those readings from the filings and do not infer intent.

Residual dilution risk is low: the remaining CARES Act payroll support warrants were exercised in Q1 2026 with none outstanding, so the ~658M diluted share count reflects essentially the full current capitalization.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is price-led, not volume-led, which is the correct way to underwrite it. Q2 capacity grew roughly 1%; Q3 is guided to +1% and Q4 to +2-3%, led by international. Revenue growth of 14% adjusted came from a 12.4% increase in unit revenue.

The composition is favorable. Main cabin unit revenue grew mid-teens in the month of June even as main cabin capacity was cut 2-3%. The commercial team noted the TRASM exit rate was higher than the entry rate because fuel-recapture pricing began in March when April was already about 70% booked, which mechanically pushed the benefit into Q3. Corporate sales rose 20%, with Los Angeles and Boston nearer 30%. Amex remuneration grows roughly 10% independent of seat-mile economics. TechOps at approximately $1.2B and up 50% is small but high-margin and compounding.

**Estimate revisions.** Current quarter $2.23 (+29.9% 30-day, +37.4% 90-day), next quarter $2.22 (+24.6%/+30.5%), current year $6.63 (+20.2%/+21.6%), next year $8.79 (+12.0%/+10.4%). The 90-day quarterly revision exceeds the +30% threshold that historically flags earnings inflection, and forward P/E is well under 30x, so the signal formally applies. Two qualifications keep us from treating it as decisive. First, revenue growth of 14-19% is below the 30% threshold that accompanies the strongest version of this pattern. Second, the quarterly revision is largely mechanical: analysts moved from $1.72 to $2.23 after the July 10 guide of $2.00-2.50, so the revision reflects management's assumption set rather than independent new information. Consensus is now anchored to the guide midpoint of $2.25 with no cushion.

At the annual level the picture is more favorable: consensus $6.63 sits below the $7.00 guide midpoint, leaving room to beat if H2 delivers. Bridging the arithmetic, H1 adjusted EPS was $2.20 ($0.64 in Q1, $1.56 in Q2). Reaching $6.63 requires $4.43 in H2; with Q3 at the $2.25 midpoint, Q4 must deliver roughly $2.18 versus $1.55 in Q4 2025, up 41%. Reaching the $7.00 guide midpoint requires Q4 of roughly $2.55, up 65%. That is a demanding second half even before considering fuel.

The four-quarter beat record (4.4%, 10.7%, 0.5%, 10.5%) supports some confidence in delivery, though the pattern is uneven and the smallest beat came in the December quarter.

Longer term, management reiterates a mid-teens operating margin and ROIC framework against 9.2% FY2025 operating margin and 11% current ROIC, and Bastian sized the main-cabin margin recovery to 2019-type levels as worth two to three points of total margin. Those are targets, not commitments.

## Valuation Assessment

At $88.59:

| Basis | EPS | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 GAAP (trailing) | $7.66 | 11.6x |
| FY2026 guide midpoint (adjusted) | $7.00 | 12.7x |
| FY2026 consensus (adjusted) | $6.63 | 13.4x |
| FY2027 consensus (adjusted) | $8.79 | 10.1x |
| FY2026 FCF ($3-4B) | — | 5.2-6.9% yield |

Peer medians are of limited use here. The provided peer P/E median of 29.0x is distorted by AAL at 47.9x and ALK at 75.6x, both reflecting depressed earnings rather than premium quality. The one clean comparable is UAL at 10.0x, against which Delta trades at a premium of roughly 16% on trailing earnings. That premium is defensible on balance-sheet quality (investment grade at all three agencies versus UAL's high-yield profile), the Amex annuity, the refinery hedge, and higher premium-cabin mix. It is not a large enough gap to argue mispricing on its own.

The bull valuation argument is that 10.1x FY2027 consensus for a business with investment-grade credit, 61% of revenue outside conventional main-cabin sales, a growing contractual cash stream from a single investment-grade counterparty, and a 6% FCF yield is too cheap if the pricing environment persists. The bear argument is that the FY2027 number embeds sustained fuel-recapture pricing, and the multiple has already expanded materially this year as the market moved from pricing DAL as a distressed cyclical to pricing the structural improvement.

Two observations temper the "cheap" read. First, the earnings base itself is fuel-inflated: high crack spreads simultaneously raise fares and generate refinery profit, and both reverse together if cracks normalize faster than fares reset. Second, the multiple re-rating has already happened. The stock rose 65% over twelve months and sits within 7% of its 52-week high; prior appreciation is not a valuation input, but the gap to the $105 consensus median has narrowed to 19% while the downside on a fuel-driven Q3 miss is comparable in magnitude.

Analyst positioning: mean target $105.52, median $105.00, high $125.00, low $50.00, with 24 buy/strong-buy ratings against one strong-sell. Wells Fargo raised to $105 from $75 on June 30. On a stock at 10-13x forward earnings with rising estimates, consensus targets generally lag rather than cap, so we treat $105 as a reasonable base rather than a ceiling. We score valuation at 5.9 — modestly constructive, not a source of edge, because the discount to intrinsic value is contingent on an earnings base that is itself cycle-dependent.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is genuinely improving, which is why this dimension carries our highest score.

**Supply is contracting into resilient demand.** Spirit's liquidation removed a substantial block of domestic ULCC seats. Engine availability, which Bastian identified as more binding than airframe output, constrains everyone's growth; he was explicit that new-technology engine durability "isn't anywhere close to the pre-existing technologies."

**The low-cost cost advantage has eroded.** Nine or ten years ago ULCCs had the best margins and used fuel hedges to take share; no carrier now runs a meaningful hedge book, and labor, airport, technology and aircraft costs have risen for everyone. Management estimates the low end of the market needs another 5% of fare increase just to break even at current fuel. That is a self-serving estimate about competitors and should be treated as a hypothesis, but it is directionally consistent with Spirit's failure.

**Delta is repricing the weakest part of its own business.** Cutting main cabin capacity while holding premium flat produced mid-teens main-cabin unit revenue growth in June and pushed premium revenue above main cabin revenue for the first time. This alters the product being sold rather than simply raising the price of the old one, which makes it more durable than a fuel-driven fare spike.

**Structural offsets Delta owns and peers do not.** Monroe Energy generated $351M of segment operating income in Q2 versus roughly -$10M a year earlier, contributing an 11-cent gross benefit per gallon net of a 5-cent outage impact. The Amex contract is loosely correlated with seat-mile economics. TechOps at 50% growth monetizes maintenance capability as a third-party business.

**What could reverse it.** Capacity discipline is easier to maintain when engines are scarce. The 737 MAX 10 arrives in Delta colors in 2027, and eventual engine supply normalization allows competitors to add seats. There are unconfirmed reports that Spirit may return. And CASM-ex rose 6.8% in Q2 against a "low single-digit" long-term framework, partly a denominator problem created by Delta's own capacity cuts but also reflecting a 4% May 2026 pay increase and approximately $500M of H1 profit sharing. Management guides modest CASM-ex improvement in Q3 with more in Q4, and the path back depends on up-gauging with delivery-dependent aircraft.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Q3 fuel assumption is the most immediate exposure.** The Q3 guide assumes all-in fuel including a 5-cent refinery benefit. Applying our sensitivity, realized Q3 fuel of $3.50/gallon rather than $3.15 costs roughly $392M pretax in the quarter, or about $0.44 per share, which would take the guide midpoint of $2.25 toward $1.81 against consensus of $2.23. Partial offset: higher cracks lift refinery income and higher fuel supports the fare environment with a one-to-two booking-cycle lag, and Q3 is guided at close to 100% pass-through. The counterargument is that Q2's realized price embedded a peak-spike month and forward curves in early July may have shown backwardation.

**2. Refinery single-point-of-failure, currently realized.** The June 25 fire left Trainer at 75% of throughput as of July 10, with an unquantified "tail of outage" into Q3. The asset that is Delta's best fuel hedge in a high-crack regime is simultaneously an industrial facility that can fail precisely when it is most valuable. Delta has already haircut the assumed benefit to 5 cents in Q3 from 11 cents gross in Q2.

**3. Aeroméxico antitrust immunity is a live binary.** DOT ordered termination of the JV's antitrust immunity in September 2025; Delta obtained a stay in November 2025 pending Eleventh Circuit review, and oral argument occurred June 23, 2026. Reporting on June 18, 2026 indicated DOT is not backing down despite a new U.S.-Mexico bilateral agreement. No ruling has been located, and Delta has not quantified the JV's revenue contribution, so the exposure cannot be sized. An adverse outcome would unwind revenue sharing across a substantial transborder network and could pressure the carrying value of the Aeroméxico stake. A procedural remand preserving the JV is also plausible.

**4. Counterparty concentration is rising while management markets diversification.** The "61% diverse revenue streams" figure measures mix away from main-cabin tickets and establishes something real. It does not establish reduced counterparty concentration. Approximately $9B of 2026 Amex remuneration on $66-68B of expected revenue is roughly 13-14% of total revenue from one contract, up in both absolute and relative terms, with a stated $10B target implying further increase. That concentration is in a long-dated, mutually beneficial agreement with an investment-grade institution, which is materially better than concentration in a volatile end customer, but it ties Delta's economics increasingly to Amex card economics, U.S. consumer credit conditions, and eventual renewal terms. Separately, the loyalty deferred revenue is a liability representing service owed, not forward revenue visibility.

**5. Consumer cycle.** Airlines carry among the highest consumer-discretionary betas in the market. Corporate sales up 20% and the premium mix shift are real offsets; the main cabin repricing is the exposed leg. [unverified — the research brief contains no macro data on mortgage rates or household sentiment surveys to support a specific weaker-H2 discretionary claim.]

**6. Non-fuel cost inflation.** CASM-ex +6.8% against a low-single-digit framework. Part is the capacity denominator, part is crew and revenue-related cost. If capacity discipline persists into 2027, the unit cost path depends on delivery-dependent up-gauging.

**7. GAAP volatility from the equity portfolio.** The stakes swung from roughly -$551M in Q1 to +$349M in Q2. Non-cash and non-operating, but it makes GAAP-based screening and quarterly comparison unreliable.

**8. Leverage structure.** Not a solvency concern given investment-grade ratings and $13.6B adjusted net debt, but $3.4B of current maturities including a $1.25B December 2026 term loan, plus a $12B six-month increase in aircraft commitments, compete with the stated path to 2.0x by year-end.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 80.5% is an artifact of a one-day expiry and should be discounted; the same applies to the reported ATM IV jump from 46.1% to 104.7% in the delta snapshot. The informative tenors are 1-month at 37.6%, 3-month at 34.1% and 6-month at 38.7% against one-year historical volatility of 39.8%, so options are priced at or slightly below realized volatility rather than rich, and the "backwardation / imminent stress" label follows from the one-day expiry rather than from positioning. The 3-month implied move of ±9.6% brackets the mid-October Q3 print, and the 6-month implied move of ±20.5% is wide enough to encompass both our base and bear scenarios.

Flow and open interest disagree. Put/call volume of 0.17 is heavily call-tilted, and the only unusual print is a call purchase (6,034 contracts of the August 7 $93 strike against 302 open interest, 20x). Open interest, however, moved from a put/call ratio of 0.72 to 1.01, so held positioning has become more balanced or defensive even as near-term flow leans bullish. The data source labels the near-term 3.3% skew as elevated put demand; longer-dated skew readings are reported without a defined sign convention and we draw no directional inference from them. Short interest is 3.9% of float with 2.9 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

Net read: the options market is pricing a meaningful event around the Q3 print without a strong directional lean, which is consistent with our view that consensus at the guide midpoint leaves no cushion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $120-125

Fuel stabilizes or eases into H2 while fares hold, delivering the guided 11-13% Q3 operating margin and double-digit H2 margins. FY2026 lands at or above the $7.00 guide midpoint and FY2027 consensus of $8.79 proves conservative as the full-year benefit of ~100% pass-through, restored refinery throughput, and international network additions (Hong Kong, Melbourne, LAX-Shanghai, Riyadh, Tel Aviv) compounds. Spirit's capacity does not return, engine scarcity persists, and industry supply stays constrained through 2027. CASM-ex decelerates toward low single digits as up-gauging begins with MAX 10 deliveries. Gross leverage reaches 2.0x, the $1B authorization is activated, and the multiple expands toward 14x FY2027 earnings: $8.79 × 14 = $123. This is consistent with the $125 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $68

Crack spreads stay elevated through Q3 and realized fuel comes in at $3.50-3.70/gallon against the $3.15 assumption, with the refinery offset impaired by a longer-than-expected Trainer outage. Q3 adjusted EPS prints $1.70-1.85 against $2.23 consensus and the stock derates from a near-52-week-high starting point. The H2 consumer deteriorates, main cabin RASM rolls over, and the 41-65% Q4 earnings growth embedded in FY2026 estimates does not materialize. CASM-ex stays near 7% as capacity discipline holds. FY2027 resets to roughly $6.50 and the multiple compresses to 10.5x: approximately $68, or -23% from $88.59. An adverse Eleventh Circuit ruling on the Aeroméxico JV would be additive to the downside.

### Base Case — $105

Q3 comes in within the guided range but toward the lower half as fuel runs modestly above assumption, offset by the revenue exit rate and higher refinery contribution from wider cracks. FY2026 lands at $6.60-7.00, FY2027 at $8.50-8.80. The stock holds a 12x multiple on FY2027 earnings ($8.79 × 12 = $105.5) or equivalently 15x FY2026 guide midpoint ($7.00 × 15 = $105). This matches the $105 analyst median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** We see a positive twelve-month path with identifiable catalysts and would own the position through the cycle. We would not add at $88.59.

**Base case target: $105 (+18.5% from $88.59).** 12x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $8.79, cross-checked at 15x the FY2026 guide midpoint of $7.00.

**Bull case target: $123-125 (+39-41%).** 14x FY2027 consensus of $8.79.

**Bear case target: $68 (-23%).** 10.5x a reset FY2027 of approximately $6.50.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print in mid-October 2026. The 3-month options tenor implies ±9.6%, which brackets that event.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 realized all-in fuel at or below $3.25/gallon with operating margin at 11% or better, confirming the pass-through mechanism at a verified fuel level rather than an assumed one.
2. Trainer refinery restored to full throughput with a disclosed completion date, restoring the 10-11 cent per gallon hedge benefit.
3. Activation of the $1B repurchase authorization or clustered open-market insider purchases, which would resolve the current tension between the deleveraging narrative and the absence of any signal that management views the shares as cheap.
4. Price back to the low $70s on macro rather than company-specific news, which would restore roughly 45% upside to the $105 base case against a bear case near current levels.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Q3 realized fuel above $3.50/gallon with operating margin below 10%, or any cut to the FY2026 $6.50-7.50 range.
2. An Eleventh Circuit ruling upholding DOT's termination of the Aeroméxico JV antitrust immunity without a preserving remand.
3. Domestic main cabin unit revenue turning negative year over year, which would indicate the fare recapture is reversing faster than fuel.
4. CASM-ex above 6% again in Q3 without a corresponding capacity explanation, indicating structural rather than denominator-driven cost inflation.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in mid-October 2026, with an interim check on Trainer restart disclosure and the jet crack spread through August-September.

## Conclusion

Delta's score trajectory has improved from 5.4 in April to 6.6 in July, and the Q2 print justified that improvement: reaffirming full-year guidance through a nearly $4B fuel headwind is a demonstration of pricing power that few airlines could produce. We are moving to 6.1 and to HOLD from BUY, and the reason is specific rather than a reaction to the share price.

The Q3 guide that consensus has now fully adopted ($2.23 against a $2.00-2.50 range) rests on the fuel assumption set on July 10, and crude gained roughly 20% over the month of July. Our sensitivity analysis puts each dime of miss at about $0.125 per share in the quarter, so a $3.50 realization would cost roughly $0.44 and take the result below the bottom of the guided range. The refinery that would normally cushion a high-crack quarter was at 75% throughput with no announced restart date. That is an identifiable, near-term, asymmetric risk to a stock trading 7% below its 52-week high with 24 of 25 analysts positive.

Against that, the structural case is intact and improving: Spirit's capacity is gone, engine scarcity constrains the industry's ability to respond to high fares, Amex remuneration compounds at roughly 10% toward a $10B target, TechOps is growing 50%, premium revenue has passed main cabin revenue, and the balance sheet is heading toward 2.0x gross leverage with investment-grade ratings at all three agencies. At 10.1x FY2027 consensus and a 6% free cash flow yield, the stock is not expensive relative to that franchise. The dimension scores reflect a genuinely improving competitive position (6.8) and solid financial health for an airline (6.3), held back by the concentration of near-term binary risks (5.2) and a capital-allocation record that raises the dividend and expands aircraft commitments by $12B in six months while leaving a $1B buyback authorization untouched and showing no insider open-market purchases (5.8).

Base case $105 (+18.5%) against bear case $68 (-23%) is roughly symmetric. Own the position, do not add at $88.59, and revisit on the October print or on a pullback toward the low $70s.
