**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

CSG Systems International was acquired by NEC Corporation (via its subsidiary Netcracker Technology) at $80.70 per share in an all-cash transaction that closed on May 14, 2026. The stock currently trades at $80.69, reflecting the completed merger price less fractional rounding. Shares are in the process of being delisted from NASDAQ, and all public shareholders will receive $80.70 per share in cash.

There is no remaining equity investment thesis for CSGS. The transaction received stockholder approval on January 30, 2026, CFIUS clearance on May 12, 2026, and officially closed two days later. Form 4 filings from May 14, 2026 confirm dispositions at $80.70 for all insiders, including CEO Brian Shepherd ($46.4M) and CFO Hai Tran ($10.5M). This report serves as documentation of the completed acquisition and the characteristics of the business NEC purchased.

The pre-acquisition business was a well-run SaaS platform generating $1.2B in annual revenue with 11-12% FCF margins, dominant positions in North American cable/broadband billing (Charter ~19%, Comcast ~17% of revenue), and $2.3B in remaining performance obligations providing multi-year visibility. NEC paid approximately 15.6x trailing FCF and 1.8x revenue — a reasonable premium for a strategically valuable asset in the telecom BSS/OSS consolidation wave.

## Company Overview

CSG Systems provided customer engagement, revenue management, digital monetization, and payment solutions primarily to communications service providers. Founded over 40 years ago, the company evolved from a traditional billing vendor into a SaaS platform processing transactions across broadband, cable, satellite, telecommunications, and increasingly non-communications verticals (financial services, healthcare, retail, government).

**Revenue composition (Q1 2026):**
- SaaS and related solutions: 91% ($287M)
- Software & services: 5% ($15M)
- Maintenance: 4% ($12M)

**Geographic mix:** Americas 86%, EMEA 9%, Asia Pacific 5%

**Vertical diversification:** Broadband/Cable/Satellite 48%, Telecommunications 17%, Other 35% (growing)

The NEC/Netcracker acquisition creates a combined entity targeting the "telco-to-techco" transformation, combining CSG's North American cable/broadband billing strength with Netcracker's global telecom OSS/BSS and AI-driven automation capabilities.

## Growth Analysis

CSG's organic growth had moderated to low-single-digits (2-3% annually in 2024-2025), constrained by the mature North American cable/broadband market. The "Other" vertical was the fastest-growing segment, reaching 35% of revenue (up from 33% a year prior), demonstrating successful diversification beyond core communications.

$2.3B in remaining performance obligations (65% expected by end of 2028) provided strong revenue visibility. Q1 2026's 4.8% growth represented an acceleration from FY2025's 2.2%, though this may have included some contract timing effects.

The strategic rationale for NEC's acquisition centered on combining platforms to serve global telecom operators' digital transformation needs — a market where neither company alone had complete coverage. CSG brought customer intimacy in North American cable; Netcracker brought global scale and next-generation architecture.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $80.70 acquisition price, NEC paid:
- ~15.6x trailing FCF ($141M)
- ~1.8x trailing revenue ($1.22B)
- ~36x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.25 adjusted for merger costs)
- ~15.8x forward consensus EPS ($5.11 FY2026 estimate)

Relative to peers at the time of the announcement (October 2025), CSG's acquisition multiple of ~16x forward earnings represented a modest premium to the peer median P/E of 16.4x but well below Exlservice (18.8x). The 18% premium to the pre-announcement trading price (~$68.75 on Oct 28) was reasonable for a strategic transaction eliminating a competitor while adding complementary capabilities.

Given that the merger has closed and shares trade at the deal price, valuation analysis is academic. The stock will be delisted.

## Competitive Landscape

CSG competed in the telecom BSS market against Amdocs (larger, direct competitor), Ericsson (acquired BSS assets), Oracle Communications, and Netcracker itself (now parent company). CSG's competitive advantages included:

- Deep incumbency in North American cable billing (Charter, Comcast relationships spanning decades)
- High switching costs (mission-critical billing systems)
- Platform stickiness evidenced by $2.3B backlog
- 5-year TSR of 110% vs. peer group's 43.3%, evidence of sustained operational execution

The customer concentration risk was material: Charter (~19%) and Comcast (~17%) together represented ~36% of revenue. Post-acquisition under NEC, this concentration persists as a business risk but is diversified within NEC's broader revenue base.
