## Executive Summary

People Incorporated (formerly IAC) has completed a strategic identity change that most screens have not caught up with. What remains after the Angi spin-off, the Care.com sale and the wind-down of the Search segment is a controlled holding company with two assets that matter: the former Dotdash Meredith publishing business, now named People Inc., and 66.8 million shares of MGM Resorts (roughly 26.1% of MGM). Everything else (Turo, The Daily Beast, Vivian Health, real estate, domains, limited-partner fund interests) is explicitly for sale. Chairman Barry Diller said as much on the August 4 call: the general acquisition business "was not going to produce results as it had for the past couple of decades."

The valuation arithmetic is the reason to look. Using the last verified full share count of 74.9 million (69.1M common plus 5.8M Class B, March 23, 2026), equity value is approximately $3.04bn. Add operating-company net debt of roughly $1.1bn, subtract the MGM stake at the company's own $48.30 bid ($3.23bn), subtract ~$800M of parent cash and the ~$189M of signed LP-stake proceeds, and the implied enterprise value of the publishing business plus all remaining non-core assets is approximately **negative $0.1bn** against 2026 operating-company adjusted EBITDA guidance of $325-355M. Marking MGM conservatively at $38 (roughly a pre-bid level implied by a normal takeover premium), the implied opco enterprise value is still only about $0.6bn, or 1.8x guided EBITDA. The discount is real under conservative marks, not an artifact of using the bid price.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a purchase is the structure sitting on top of the arithmetic. The $48.30 proposal for the 189 million MGM shares PPLI does not own requires approximately **$9.1bn of cash** against ~$800M of parent cash and a $3bn equity market value, with no committed financing disclosed anywhere in the filings or reporting reviewed. MGM's special committee is reported to view the price as inadequate, which means the two live paths are PPLI paying more (worse for PPLI's balance sheet) or the deal failing (MGM marks lower). Diller controls ~46.2% of the vote, so minority holders cannot force monetization and cannot block the transaction. Underneath, core sessions at the publishing business fell 22% year over year, offset almost entirely by rate and by new revenue lines, an offset that has worked for eleven consecutive quarters and has an arithmetic ceiling. Management said resolution on MGM was likely "certainly within the next 60 days" from August 4; the options market is pricing that event.

## Company Overview

Two entities share a name, and the distinction is material. **People Incorporated** is the SEC registrant (ticker PPLI, formerly IAC Inc.). **People Inc.** is the operating subsidiary (formerly Dotdash Meredith). A corporate consolidation of the two layers is underway through Q1 2027. Guidance of $325-355M adjusted EBITDA refers to the subsidiary; consolidated guidance is **$255-290M**. Conflating the two overstates consolidated earnings power by roughly 25%.

The publishing portfolio includes People, Southern Living, Better Homes and Gardens, InStyle, Travel + Leisure and MyRecipes, with more than 10 million print subscribers and 4.5 million registered MyRecipes users. Management now splits digital revenue into session-based (just under 60%: display, programmatic, affiliate commerce) and non-session-based (40%+: AI content licensing, Apple News, social video, events, subscriptions, the Decipher ad-data product). The stated plan is ~20% annual growth in non-session revenue against roughly flat session revenue, producing mid-to-high-single-digit digital growth.

On the licensing side, People Inc. is the **licensor and payment recipient**; OpenAI, Meta and Microsoft are paying licensees, and Netflix licenses a curated short-form video library. Google is a non-paying user of the content and the defendant in ad-tech litigation People has brought, an adversarial relationship rather than a commercial one.

## Financial Analysis

**The revenue decline is portfolio surgery.** FY2025 consolidated revenue was approximately $2.39bn against $3.81bn in FY2024, down ~37%. FY2025 contains one quarter of Angi (deconsolidated March 31, 2025) versus four in FY2024; the Search segment is winding down following Google's December 10, 2025 non-renewal notice; Care.com was sold for ~$300.2M net proceeds. Any model anchored to trailing consolidated revenue is measuring a restructuring. The correct organic reference is the operating subsidiary.

**Q2 2026 operating results** (management-reported, corroborated in the 10-Q):

| Metric | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|
| Digital revenue | +6% YoY (11th consecutive quarter of growth) |
| Digital adjusted EBITDA | +18%; margin 26% vs 23% |
| Non-session revenue (~42% of digital) | +16% |
| Session-based revenue (~58%) | –1% |
| **Core sessions** | **–22%** |
| Licensing | +23% |
| Performance marketing | +13% (guided to moderate in 2H) |
| Print revenue | –18% |
| Google share of total traffic | 21%, from "roughly two thirds" historically |

Two reproducible checks. The disclosed mix is internally consistent: (0.42 × 16%) + (0.58 × –1%) = +6.1%, against reported +6%. And implied monetization per session, indexing session revenue at 0.99 against sessions at 0.78, is 1.27, roughly +27%. That figure is an upper bound rather than a measured ad rate, because session-based revenue includes affiliate commerce with its own volume dynamics, and because the sessions being lost may be disproportionately low-value long-tail search arrivals. Under that alternative reading, much of the gap is mix, which self-exhausts once the low-value tail is gone. Management did not disclose the value distribution of lost sessions.

**Balance sheet.** At December 31, 2025: total assets ~$7.13bn, stockholders' equity ~$4.73bn (goodwill $1.79bn, 38% of equity), cash ~$960M (of which ~$284M at the opco), long-term debt ~$1.40bn. FY2025 operating loss ~$(97)M, net loss ~$(104)M. From the Q2 call: parent cash ~$800M, operating-company LTM free cash flow $179M, net leverage targeted below 3.0x by year-end 2026. At the ~$340M EBITDA midpoint that implies opco net debt under ~$1.0bn versus ~$1.12bn at YE2025, achievable from operating cash flow without asset sales. The definition of "net leverage" (whether parent cash is netted, whether the new or old EBITDA definition applies, whether it maps to a credit-agreement covenant) is not verified; the parent-cash question alone spans roughly 2.4 turns. The debt maturity ladder, fixed/floating mix and covenant headroom were not retrieved, a material gap for a company contemplating a large levered acquisition.

**Reported EPS is not usable.** The Q2 2026 print showed –$6.68 against a –$0.45 estimate, following –$0.94 in Q1 and +$1.68 in Q4 2025. The four quarters sum to –$6.21, while the profile field reports trailing EPS of –$1.49 and a positive 7.14x trailing P/E identical to the forward figure. These are data artifacts driven by divestiture losses, wind-down charges and mark-to-market on the MGM position. The current-year consensus EPS of $8.68, revised from $1.62 thirty days ago (+434%), cannot be read as operating earnings power; it almost certainly incorporates one-time items. Next-year consensus of $2.64 ($40.53 = 15.3x) is the cleaner reference, and even that is contaminated for a holding company that marks a $3bn equity stake through earnings. **The valuation case here has to be built on assets and adjusted EBITDA, not on P/E.**

**The adjusted-EBITDA definition changed in the same quarter guidance was "reaffirmed."** Operating-company guidance moved from $310-340M to $325-355M, with the $15M uplift entirely the add-back of forecasted Google litigation expense. The new definition also excludes lease impairments and buyouts, gains and losses on certain asset sales, non-recurring restructuring and M&A costs. Management's rationale (that three divestitures and a corporate consolidation made the old series unrepresentative, and that prosecuting a claim is an asset-generating legal spend rather than an operating cost) is credible on its face, and a restated quarterly bridge was provided. I do not infer motive from the accounting choice. The procedural consequence is that new-definition results must not be compared to old-definition history or to consensus built on the old basis.

**The cost-out is dated and specific.** Corporate expense: Q3 in line with Q2, Q4 below $20M, Q1 2027 lower again, run-rate target $45M annual against $80M guided for 2026, with total-company stock-based compensation targeted at $30M and Q2 2027 the first "fully clean" quarter. That is roughly **$35M of annualized recapture**, ~13% of the $272M consolidated guidance midpoint. One disclosed caveat most models will miss: the $45M target assumes no reallocation of executive compensation from subsidiary to parent, which management said is likely to occur and would flatter subsidiary EBITDA without changing consolidated economics. The $325-355M and $45M figures are therefore not independently stable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question splits by layer.

At the **digital publishing layer**, growth is real and repeatable: +6% revenue with +18% EBITDA and 300bp of margin expansion, driven by licensing (+23%), non-session lines (+16%) and rate. Product velocity is visible: Southern Living Insiders launched in July, a subscription app in August, a "People Premium" bundle planned for October, 47 original social series, the Hot Luck festival acquired with multi-city 2027 expansion, plus the Netflix short-form license. The 10-million-plus print subscriber base is the acquisition funnel for those subscription products.

At the **enterprise layer**, the company is shrinking. Print (–18%) is secular and, by management's own design, has been the offset to parent corporate overhead ("as it has by design for the last several years"), with Q3 print EBITDA explicitly not covering overhead. That creates a race between print decay and the $80M-to-$45M cost reduction. Consolidated 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $255-290M is not a growth number; it is a rebased one. The $189M LP-stake sale is a flow, not an earnings stream, and it consumes a finite tax asset; management was candid that nothing of that scale remains.

The ceiling on the current model is arithmetic. Revenue per session cannot rise 27% indefinitely against a 22% volume decline. If non-session initiatives scale on schedule, the business compounds in the mid-to-high single digits; if they slip a year, the volume decay dominates quickly. Eleven consecutive quarters of digital growth is meaningful evidence that the offset works, and it is the strongest single fact in the operating file. It is not evidence that it works for another eleven.

Advertising cyclicality is a nearer-term drag with a specific edge: management rated the ad market "6 out of 10" in both Q1 and Q2, with weakness concentrated in **food, beverage and CPG**, disproportionately relevant to a portfolio anchored by Southern Living, Better Homes and Gardens and MyRecipes.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer multiples are of limited use. The Interactive Media & Services comparables show a median P/E of 21.6 with dispersion from –2.4x (ZoomInfo) to 173.8x (TripAdvisor), and PPLI's GAAP earnings are dominated by non-operating items. The relevant framework is sum of the parts and the implied residual value of the operating business.

**Implied opco value, reproducible from stated inputs** (74.9M shares at $40.53 = $3.036bn equity):

| Step | $bn |
|---|---|
| Equity value | 3.036 |
| + Operating-company net debt (~$1.40bn less ~$284M opco cash) | 1.116 |
| – MGM stake, 66.8M shares at $48.30 bid | (3.227) |
| – Parent cash | (0.800) |
| – Signed LP proceeds (Q3 close, substantially tax-sheltered) | (0.190) |
| **= Implied EV of People Inc. plus all non-core assets** | **(0.065)** |

Against $325-355M of guided subsidiary adjusted EBITDA, that is zero or less. The obvious objection is that MGM should be marked to market rather than to PPLI's own premium bid. Testing that: at an MGM mark of $38 (approximately a pre-bid level implied by a conventional takeover premium to the $48.30 offer), the implied opco EV becomes $0.62bn, or **1.8x** guided EBITDA. For the implied opco value to reach a defensible 5x (~$1.7bn on the $340M midpoint), MGM would have to trade near $22. The discount survives conservative marking. **I do not have a current MGM quote, so the precise mark and therefore the precise discount are unverified.**

**Indicative gross NAV per share** (74.9M shares; my calculation, not a company or consensus figure): MGM at $48.30 = $43.08; parent cash $10.68; LP proceeds $2.54; opco equity at 7x EBITDA ($2.38bn EV less $1.1bn net debt) = $17.09. Total ≈ **$73.40**, before any value for Turo, The Daily Beast, Vivian, real estate, domains, or the Google ad-tech claim. At a $44 MGM mark the total is ~$69.60; the opco multiple assumption alone swings the figure roughly $12/share between 5x and 9x.

Three deductions matter and are frequently omitted:

1. **Embedded tax.** The capital-loss carryforward is "more than $250M" and is already earmarked against the LP gain. If the original MGM cost basis is on the order of $1bn (unconfirmed and flagged), the embedded gain at $48.30 approaches $2.2bn and monetization tax leakage would be roughly $450-500M, about $6/share. This does not bite if PPLI buys more rather than sells, which is management's stated intent.
2. **Structural holding-company discount.** Diller controls ~46.2% of the vote (insiders ~53.1%), capped at 48.5% by agreement. Minority holders cannot compel monetization. A persistent discount is the rational response to a control block that has stated it intends to *increase* concentration in a single illiquid position.
3. **Negative optionality on the bid itself.** A closed $9.1bn cash acquisition converts PPLI from a discounted holding company into a highly levered gaming operator. That is a different security with a different multiple, and the sign of the change for minority equity is not knowable without seeing the financing.

Applying a 30% discount to a tax-adjusted NAV of roughly $65 produces a base value near **$46**, about 13% above the current price. Consensus targets sit materially higher (low $48, mean $57.30, high $70, with 7 of 11 analysts at buy or strong buy). Those targets are supportive but are unlikely to be modeling a levered buyout scenario, and at a company where GAAP earnings are unusable, target dispersion of $48-70 is itself an indication of how much rests on unresolved variables.

## Competitive Landscape

The company provides one of the better-quantified public case studies of generative AI search reshaping premium publishing, and of one adaptation path.

The damage is measured: core sessions –22%, Google from roughly two-thirds of traffic to 21%. The offset has three legs. **Rate** — Diller: "There's no tricks in that. We're not overloading pages with ads. It's pure rate." **Licensing** — +23%, enforced technically through Cloudflare-based crawler blocking that converts free scraping into paid negotiation, with Vogel distinguishing "the all-you-can-eat deal of Meta or OpenAI, and then there's the a la carte deal of Microsoft" (implying the Microsoft arrangement is usage-based and more scalable, the others fixed-fee with capped upside but better visibility). **Format migration** — Apple News, Netflix short-form, social series, events, subscriptions.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed. Unit economics are improving (digital EBITDA margin 23% → 26%) and brand-safe inventory is repricing upward as low-quality content proliferates. Volume is collapsing. The single highest-leverage uncertainty is legal: the licensing line depends on the enforceability of crawler blocking and on the unresolved question of whether AI training on copyrighted content is fair use. Google remains a non-paying user, and blocking Google's AI crawler today may mean losing search indexing as well. Regulatory separation of those crawlers would hand People real negotiating leverage; a broad fair-use finding would pressure the fastest-growing revenue line at renewal.

On the MGM side, the sector's M&A activity cuts both ways. A competing bidder would raise the mark on PPLI's stake while defeating the acquisition, and sector heat may push MGM's trading price above $48.30, simultaneously increasing NAV and reducing the probability of the deal closing at the proposed price. PPLI's ownership percentage has also been rising partly because MGM's own buybacks shrink the denominator, meaning the stake can grow without PPLI deploying capital.

## Risk Assessment

**1. MGM financing and outcome (dominant).** Approximately $9.1bn of cash required (189M shares not owned × $48.30) against ~$800M of parent cash, roughly 3x PPLI's own equity value, on top of MGM's debt (the difference between the ~$18bn enterprise and ~$12.4bn equity framings implies roughly $5.6bn of net debt attributed to MGM in press coverage). No committed financing was found in any source. The special committee is reported to view the price as inadequate. Gaming regulators in Nevada, Massachusetts, Michigan, Maryland, New Jersey and Macau would need suitability findings on the acquirer. Diller is conflicted as an MGM director and has committed to recusal. Diller's own hedge ("whether it works or not is speculative," and "we're going to increase our ownership in MGM either in one swoop or slowly") is consistent either with a serious undisclosed financing package (normal at the non-binding stage) or with a creeping-accumulation strategy in which the bid sets a floor. I cannot distinguish between these on the available evidence.

**2. Traffic decay.** –22% core sessions, with 21% residual Google dependency that management has said it would consider eliminating deliberately in exchange for licensing leverage. That would be a voluntary revenue amputation with high variance.

**3. Deliberately increasing concentration.** Post-disposals, PPLI becomes a two-asset company: a declining-traffic publisher and a single Las Vegas-centric gaming equity. Revenue-mix diversification inside the publishing business is improving; enterprise-level asset concentration is worsening. These are different measures and should not be conflated.

**4. Print as a load-bearing offset.** Print EBITDA covers parent corporate overhead by design and is declining 18% annually toward what Vogel expects to become "relatively immaterial." That makes the $45M corporate run-rate target more load-bearing than it appears.

**5. Governance.** A controlled company with a chairman-driven bid, 16 months of operating without a CEO, and a full parent C-suite turnover (Halpin and Handler to consultancies, Vogel to CEO, Quinn to CFO) executed on August 5, mid-negotiation. Promoting the operators who generate essentially all the cash flow is coherent and cost-reducing; it also removes the parent-level M&A and legal infrastructure while a $9bn bid and a material litigation claim are live. The five-year pay-versus-performance record is hard to explain away: indexed TSR of ~$37.67 versus ~$263.67 for the peer group from December 31, 2020, a gap of over 200 percentage points. Care.com was sold at a large capital loss, evidence of historical overpayment.

**6. Unquantified asset.** The Google ad-tech claim, with People as plaintiff, ~$15M of 2026 expense, expected resolution in 2027, and management calling potential settlement "material" without a figure. Treat as an option with unspecified strike and expiry; assign no value.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 64.2% against 32.3% one-year realized, in the 84th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in steep backwardation to 35.1% at six months and an implied move of ±14.5% on the August 21 tenor. That is the market pricing a dated, imminent binary, consistent with Diller's "next 60 days" comment on MGM. Put/call open interest of 3.53 shows substantial downside protection outstanding, while the 3-month skew is characterized by the data as call-heavy: event positioning on both sides rather than a directional consensus. Short interest of 16.2% of float at 9.2 days to cover is elevated; a portion may reflect hedged merger-arbitrage structures rather than outright bearishness (inference, not established), but at 9.2 days to cover a deal-terminated-plus-aggressive-buyback outcome carries genuine squeeze potential. Options are not cheap here: a long position taken now pays a substantial volatility premium for event exposure, and there is no case for using derivatives to express this thesis at 64% implied against 32% realized.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $62 (+53%)

MGM resolves within the stated window in a way that validates the mark: either a definitive agreement at or above $48.30 with disclosed financing, or termination followed by continued accumulation while MGM trades near the bid on standalone merit. The LP sale closes in Q3, adding ~$189M of substantially untaxed cash to an ~$800M parent balance (a 24% liquidity increase from one transaction). The corporate cost glide path lands on schedule (under $20M in Q4, $45M run-rate, a clean Q2 2027), recapturing ~$35M of EBITDA. Digital growth holds mid-single-digit with margins above 26% as licensing, subscriptions and events scale, and regulators require Google to separate AI and search crawlers, converting the largest non-paying user into a payer. Buybacks continue at the pace implied by the share count falling from 76.9M to 74.9M between February 2 and March 23, 2026, compounding per-share NAV. The Google ad-tech claim settles materially in 2027. NAV discount narrows from ~40% toward 10%.

### Bear Case — $30 (–26%)

The special committee's reported view prevails and PPLI either walks away or raises the price. If it walks, MGM marks back toward pre-bid levels and the largest NAV component falls ~20%. If it raises and closes, PPLI takes on multiples of its own equity value in acquisition debt, and the equity reprices as a levered gaming holdco rather than a discounted asset play. Concurrently, session decline outpaces rate: the mix-shift explanation for +27% implied monetization proves correct, the low-value session tail exhausts, and session revenue turns negative mid-single-digit while print EBITDA falls below corporate overhead before the cost-out lands. A broad fair-use ruling pressures licensing at renewal. Applying 4x to opco EBITDA, an MGM mark of $38, and a 35% holdco discount to a tax-adjusted NAV yields roughly $30.

### Base Case — $46 (+13%)

MGM resolution is inconclusive or ends in slow accumulation; MGM marks between $40 and $48; the LP sale closes; cost-out lands roughly on schedule with modest slippage; digital growth decelerates to low-single-digit as performance marketing moderates against a tough 2H comp; the holdco discount narrows only modestly because the control structure and increasing concentration justify part of it. Tax-adjusted NAV of ~$65 at a 30% discount.

Judgment-weighted (45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear) gives an expected value near $45, roughly +11% against $40.53. That is a positive expected return with wide dispersion and a binary inside two months whose outcome I cannot handicap and, given the information cutoff, may already have occurred.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders have adequate compensation for the wait; new capital has no urgent entry signal ahead of the MGM resolution, and the options market is charging a 32-point volatility premium to express the event.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. MGM proposal formally withdrawn or terminated **and** PPLI announces an expanded repurchase authorization or discloses continued open-market MGM accumulation funded from cash flow. That removes the levered-buyout tail while preserving the NAV discount.
2. A definitive MGM agreement accompanied by **disclosed committed financing** in which PPLI's pro-forma consolidated net leverage is under 4.0x and no meaningful equity issuance at or below NAV is required.
3. Q4 2026 corporate expense printing below $20M with Q1 2027 lower, confirming the $45M run-rate, **and** digital revenue growth of 5%+ with session-based revenue no worse than –3%.
4. Price near $33-34 (roughly a 50% discount to a conservatively marked, tax-adjusted NAV) with the operating trajectory intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A closed MGM transaction requiring PPLI equity issuance at or below the prevailing price, or pro-forma consolidated leverage above 5x.
2. Session-based revenue declining more than 5% year over year in any quarter, or digital revenue growth falling below 2%, the point at which rate compensation demonstrably stops covering volume decay.
3. Print EBITDA failing to cover full-year parent corporate overhead as guided, combined with corporate expense above $20M in Q4 2026.
4. An adverse fair-use ruling or a material AI licensing counterparty non-renewal, given licensing is the fastest-growing line.
5. Any goodwill impairment against the $1.79bn balance (38% of book equity), which would confirm publishing-asset value erosion rather than portfolio surgery.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate on any 8-K regarding MGM (Diller's window runs to approximately October 3, 2026); Q3 2026 results in early November, which should confirm the LP-sale close, the Q4 corporate cost step-down, 2H performance-marketing moderation, and the first full quarter under the Vogel/Quinn parent management team.

**Verification gaps to close before sizing any position:** the current MGM share price and therefore the mark-to-market stake value; PPLI's MGM cost basis and embedded tax; the definition of the "under 3x net leverage" target; the opco debt maturity ladder and covenant package; and the current share count, which buybacks have likely reduced below the 74.9M used here (which would raise per-share NAV).

## Conclusion

The core valuation observation holds under conservative assumptions: at $40.53, after crediting the MGM stake at a pre-bid mark of $38 rather than the company's own $48.30 bid, the market is valuing People Inc. plus Turo, The Daily Beast, Vivian Health, corporate real estate, internet domains and a live antitrust claim against Google at roughly 1.8x guided operating-company EBITDA. Eleven consecutive quarters of digital revenue growth with margins expanding from 23% to 26%, $179M of LTM operating-company free cash flow, a signed and substantially tax-free $189M cash inflow, and a dated $35M cost-out program are not the profile of a business worth nothing.

Three things prevent that from being a purchase recommendation. First, the largest asset is marked by a bid the target's committee reportedly considers too low, so both live paths (pay more, or walk away) reduce the mark or the balance sheet. Second, the acquisition as proposed requires roughly three times PPLI's own equity value in cash with no disclosed financing, which makes the equity's forward risk profile unknowable rather than merely uncertain. Third, the discount is partly structural: a 46.2% controlling vote, a stated intent to concentrate further into a single illiquid position, a five-year TSR of –62% against a peer group up ~164%, and a full parent C-suite turnover executed mid-negotiation. The market is discounting a control structure that has historically destroyed value and is now attempting its largest transaction ever with its dealmaking apparatus dismantled.

Own it if you already do. Wait for the MGM resolution, or for a price in the low $30s, before adding.
