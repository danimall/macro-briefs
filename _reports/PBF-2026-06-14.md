**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PBF Energy is one of the largest independent merchant refiners in the United States, with six refineries totaling roughly 1,000,000 bpd of throughput and a weighted-average Nelson Complexity of ~12.8. The investment case turns on three two-sided moving parts: a structural improvement in West Coast supply economics, a leveraged and cash-consuming balance sheet, and an open-ended regulatory liability at its Martinez refinery. The stock has nearly doubled off its June 2025 low of $20.37 and now trades at $41.89, having pulled back ~20% from its March 2026 peak of $52.18.

The most important analytical catch is the gap between PBF's headline Q1'26 GAAP profit of $1.65 per share and its adjusted loss of $0.88. The GAAP figure was inflated by roughly $390M of pre-tax special items: a $313.0M non-cash LCM inventory reserve reversal driven by rising crude prices, plus a $106.5M insurance gain on the Martinez fire. Strip those out and PBF still lost money in a seasonally weak quarter despite materially wider crack spreads, and that adjusted $0.88 loss missed the consensus estimate of a $0.71 loss by roughly 23%. The headline number overstates underlying earnings power.

Against that, the forward picture is improving in a way that a single-quarter geopolitical premium does not explain. Next-year EPS estimates have been revised +83.4% over 30 days and +178.5% over 90 days; current-year estimates are up +33.0% and +173.2% over the same windows. A forward-year revision of this magnitude reflects the market re-rating PBF's normalized earnings power, consistent with the documented closures of Phillips 66's Los Angeles refinery and Valero's Benicia refinery (drawn from secondary news sources; magnitude/timing to be confirmed), which tighten West Coast supply and benefit PBF's 323 kbpd of California capacity (Torrance + Martinez) on a multi-year horizon. With the stock already below the consensus mean target of $44.08 and trading at the peer-median ~10x, much of the bear case is in the price. The balance of evidence supports HOLD.

## Company Overview

PBF operates as a pure-play, unbranded merchant refiner with no retail or marketing buffer. Its earnings are a direct function of crack spreads and crude differentials. The six-refinery footprint spans four PADD regions:

| Refinery | Region (PADD) | Capacity (bpd) | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delaware City, DE | East Coast (1) | 180,000 | 13.6 |
| Paulsboro, NJ | East Coast (1) | 155,000 | 9.1 |
| Toledo, OH | Mid-Continent (2) | 180,000 | 11.0 |
| Chalmette, LA | Gulf Coast (3) | 185,000 | 13.0 |
| Torrance, CA | West Coast (5) | 166,000 | 13.8 |
| Martinez, CA | West Coast (5) | 157,000 | 16.1 |

The business reports in two segments: Refining (essentially all third-party revenue) and Logistics (PBFX terminals, pipelines, and storage, mostly intercompany and eliminated in consolidation). PBF also holds a 50% interest in St. Bernard Renewables, a renewable diesel facility co-located at Chalmette in a JV with Eni's Enilive subsidiary.

The corporate structure is an Up-C: PBF Energy Inc. owns ~99.3% of PBF Energy Company LLC, with a Tax Receivable Agreement obligating PBF to pay 85% of certain realized tax benefits to legacy unitholders ($168.2M liability outstanding; $130.8M was paid in January 2025 for the 2023 tax year). The high facility complexity and heavy/sour-crude capability are genuine advantages; the absence of integration and the concentration of two refineries in California are the structural offsets.

## Financial Analysis

Q1'26 showed a sharp YoY swing on the headline:

- Revenue: $7.90B vs. $7.07B in Q1'25 (+11.7%)
- Net income attributable to PBF: $198.3M ($1.65 diluted) vs. a $(401.8)M loss ($(3.53)) in Q1'25
- Gross refining margin: $1,036.9M ($13.65/bbl) vs. $391.7M ($5.96/bbl)
- EBITDA: $459.4M vs. $(339.6)M

The quality of that profit is weak. Removing the special items (net of ~$11.5M Martinez fire expense and $9.4M RBI execution cost), the adjusted fully-converted net loss was $(102.4)M, or $(0.88)/share. PBF was still loss-making at the operating level in Q1 despite crack spreads that were dramatically higher YoY (ANS West Coast 4-3-1 at $36.65 vs. $23.09).

Operating metrics improved underneath: production of 841 kbpd (vs. 733), and refining opex of $8.70/bbl (down from $10.74) as Martinez fire costs rolled off.

The balance sheet is the clearest source of concern. Total debt rose to $2,852M (carrying) from $2,202M at YE'25, with the revolver drawn to $750M from $100M in a single quarter. Net debt of $2,260M pushed net debt/cap to 29% from 23% (36% excluding special items). The debt stack includes $801.6M of 6.00% notes ('28), $800M of 9.875% notes ('30), and $500M of 7.875% notes ('30); the 9.875% coupon signals the market prices PBF credit as sub-investment-grade. Liquidity is ~$2.4B and covenants are in compliance.

Cash generation is the binding issue. Operating cash flow was $(323.7)M in Q1'26 (an improvement from $(661.4)M in Q1'25 but still negative), driven by a working-capital build (receivables +$770M). Investing used $401.8M (capex $349M + $137M turnarounds). The funding gap was bridged by the revolver draw. Full-year 2026 capex guidance is $885–925M ex-Martinez rebuild. The dividend of $0.275/quarter (~$32M) was maintained, and no buybacks were executed in Q1 despite ~$732M of remaining authorization, a sensible prioritization of balance-sheet preservation.

## Growth Analysis

The forward-estimate trajectory is the strongest piece of the bull case and deserves explicit weighting:

|--------|-------------|----------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $4.22 | +54.6% | +155.7% |
| Next Quarter | $3.54 | +0.2% | +290.9% |
| Current Year | $7.88 | +33.0% | +173.2% |
| Next Year | $6.28 | +83.4% | +178.5% |

The next-year revision cannot be a one-quarter Hormuz premium. Geopolitical crack-spread spikes show up in current-quarter and current-year estimates, which are indeed rising, but the magnitude of the *forward-year* revisions points to the market re-rating PBF's normalized earnings power. The most plausible structural driver is West Coast supply tightening as the Phillips 66 LA and Valero Benicia capacity exits, leaving surviving California refiners facing tighter regional supply and wider crack spreads. This is a multi-year dynamic, not a transient one.

The earnings-surprise record tempers the enthusiasm but does not invalidate it. Over the last four quarters PBF beat in three (including a +607.8% surprise in Q4'25 and +22.6% in Q3'25) and missed once (-23.2% in Q1'26). The single recent miss is not evidence of structurally negative earnings power; it reflects a seasonally weak quarter where the adjusted print was a narrowing loss. The track record argues PBF can earn meaningfully when crack spreads cooperate.

The offset is RFS/RIN compliance cost, which more than doubled YoY to $278M in Q1'26 (from $120M) after the March 27, 2026 EPA RFS finalization lifted 2026/2027 volumes and RIN prices (effective basket price $8.76 vs. $4.75). The renewable-credit liability rose to $653.5M. This is a structural, growing margin drag that partially offsets the West Coast tailwind, and it is why the growth case is balanced rather than one-sided.

On normalized refining margins above the current trough, PBF's earnings power supports the present multiple. The stock is also not cheap enough to be a clear long given the cash-flow and litigation risks. The valuation is balanced — a 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

PBF's structural position is improving at the margin. The defining dynamic for the next several years is shrinking PADD 5 (West Coast) capacity. California's regulatory regime — Cap-and-Invest extended to 2045, LCFS targeting 90% carbon-intensity reduction by 2045, EV mandates (ACCII/ACF), and crude-production restrictions — is driving competitors out. The Phillips 66 (LA) and Valero (Benicia) closures remove substantial regional supply just as PBF's Martinez refinery returns to full operation. With Torrance and Martinez (among the highest-complexity assets in the fleet at 13.8 and 16.1), PBF becomes one of the last large independent merchant refiners with significant California capacity.

The same regulatory regime that thins competition raises PBF's own cost and long-run demand risk. PBF is making a bet that near- and medium-term supply tightness outweighs long-term decarbonization headwinds. That bet is contingent on Martinez running reliably post-fire, which feeds directly into the risk assessment below. The competitive trajectory is positive but not free of the regulatory tail that created it.

## Risk Assessment

**Martinez fire aftermath and regulatory overhang.** The February 1, 2025 fire shut Martinez (157 kbpd) for most of 2025; full restart, including the FCC, was completed in early May 2026. Cumulative insurance recoveries reached $1.0B against a $30M deductible, largely covering rebuild costs. The open-ended exposure is the investigation slate: CalOSHA, Bay Area Air District (22 NOVs), Contra Costa County, DOJ, the US Attorney's Office, and EPA, plus an unresolved 2022 catalyst-release investigation and class-action/mass-tort suits in discovery through 2028. Penalties and potential operational/permit restrictions are unquantified and could impair the very asset anchoring the West Coast thesis. This is the most material company-specific risk and is not fully priceable.

**Leverage and liquidity in a downcycle.** A sustained margin trough combined with ~$900M of 2026 capex and a maintained dividend would pressure the balance sheet. The 9.875% note coupon reflects the market's credit view. A May 28, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03) discloses a new material financing obligation post-dating the Q1 10-Q, which warrants follow-up and could further raise the leverage profile.

**RFS/RIN cost escalation.** A structural and growing margin drag.

**California decarbonization and trade-policy risk.** Two of six refineries face the most hostile US refining jurisdiction. New Q1'26 10-Q risk factors include US tariffs on imported feedstocks (referencing the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling on emergency tariff authority) and updated geopolitical (Iran/Israel/US) language.

**Commodity volatility.** As an unbranded merchant refiner, PBF is fully exposed to crack spreads and differentials, with LIFO/LCM accounting producing large non-cash swings ($313M reversal this quarter following a charge last quarter).

**Ownership overhang.** Carlos Slim's Control Empresarial de Capitales has sold over 2.5 million shares between late March and early June 2026 (down to ~18.1M shares held) in a steady, price-insensitive manner. This is a portfolio decision rather than an operational signal, but it is a persistent technical supply pressure on the stock.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not corroborate an acute bearish view. Near-term ATM IV of 62.7% is roughly fair against 64.4% one-year historical vol (spread -1.7%), and sits at just the 18th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is balanced at 0.9%, the put/call volume ratio is 0.05, and the term structure is in contango (62.7% near vs. 65.0% long-dated), a normal, non-stressed configuration. ATM IV has fallen 9.3pp since early June (from 71.9%), and the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 0.55 to 0.06. The one flagged unusual trade was a $44 July call (4.0x vol/OI, +5% out of the money), which leans bullish rather than bearish. The 15.1% short interest (4.2 days to cover) reflects institutional caution, but the options market is not pricing imminent downside, consistent with the view that much of the bear case is already in the equity price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

West Coast supply tightening from the Phillips 66 LA and Valero Benicia closures structurally widens California crack spreads, and PBF's 323 kbpd at Torrance and Martinez captures the benefit on a multi-year horizon. The next-year EPS revisions confirm the market is re-rating normalized earnings power beyond any transient geopolitical premium. Martinez runs reliably, the RBI cost program delivers, and refining margins normalize above trough. On normalized earnings, the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $62 (~48% upside). The 2.67% dividend pays the wait.

### Bear Case

The Q1 GAAP profit was an accounting artifact (special items masking an $0.88 adjusted loss). Crack spreads normalize lower as the Hormuz premium reverses, RFS/RIN costs keep climbing ($278M and rising), and the revolver-funded cash burn forces a choice between capex, the dividend, and deleveraging. A Martinez DOJ/EPA penalty or operational restriction impairs the anchor asset. The stock retests the low analyst target of $30 (~28% downside).

The two cases roughly cancel over a 12-month horizon, which is the basis for the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, expected late July/early August 2026), plus a near-term read on the May 28, 2026 financing 8-K to confirm the post-quarter leverage picture.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted (ex-special-items) quarterly profitability confirmed for at least one quarter, demonstrating the West Coast tailwind is showing up in operating results rather than just estimates.
- Operating cash flow turns positive and the revolver draw is paid down materially below $750M, removing the funding-gap overhang.
- Favorable or bounded resolution of the Martinez DOJ/EPA/CalOSHA investigations, capping the open-ended regulatory tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Crack spreads roll over and forward EPS estimates begin falling, reversing the revision trend.
- A material Martinez penalty or imposed operational/permit restriction that impairs throughput.
- Continued negative operating cash flow forcing further revolver draws toward covenant limits, or a dividend cut.

## Conclusion

PBF is a leveraged merchant-refiner turnaround where the evidence is two-sided. The bear case is well-documented and correct on its facts: the Q1 profit was flattered by special items, the adjusted result was a loss that missed consensus, the revolver draw spiked, operating cash flow was negative, and the Martinez regulatory slate is open-ended. The bull case is equally substantiated and not reducible to a transient geopolitical premium: a structural West Coast supply tightening benefits PBF's highest-complexity California assets, and forward-year EPS revisions reflect a re-rating of normalized earnings power. At $41.89, below the consensus mean and at the peer-median ~10x, much of the bear case is already in the price, and the 2.67% yield provides modest support.

The negatives and positives roughly offset over the next twelve months. The recommendation is **HOLD**, with the next earnings report and the resolution path on Martinez and leverage as the key reassessment points.
