**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

O-I Glass is one of the world's largest glass-container manufacturers, operating 64 plants across 18 countries. The equity has fallen 38% over the past year to roughly $8.88, near its 52-week low of $7.75, and the business now presents as a distressed-industrial turnaround: three consecutive years of GAAP net losses (-$103M, -$106M, -$129M in 2023-2025), a debt-to-equity ratio of 6.4x, and an Altman Z-Score of 0.91 that sits deep in the statistical distress zone. The proximate trigger for the decline was the April 29, 2026 Q1 print: adjusted EPS of $0.05 versus $0.40 a year earlier (a -71.8% miss versus the $0.18 consensus), Europe segment operating profit collapsing to $0 from $68M, and a full-year adjusted EPS guidance cut to $1.00-$1.50.

The central analytical question is whether that deterioration is asymmetrically negative from here or already discounted. Our conclusion is that it is largely discounted. The guidance cut, the European energy step-up (~$150M reset as favorable gas contracts expired), and the volume weakness are all disclosed and reflected in a stock that dropped from ~$16 in February to ~$8.88 today. Against that, the offsets are real and specific: Fit to Win cost savings delivered ~$240M in 2025, ahead of plan against a ≥$750M cumulative target; segment operating profit rose 13% to $846M in 2025 despite falling GAAP earnings; March 2026 volumes improved to -2% YoY from -8% for the quarter; roughly 75-80% of 2026 European gas needs are hedged; and five insiders, including the CFO, the General Counsel, and a director, bought stock on the open market in May 2026 at $8.50-$9.30, right at the current price.

Because the equity is so debt-dominated (market cap ~$1.4B against ~$5.0B of debt, EV ~$6.1B), small changes in operating profit translate into outsized moves in the thin equity slice. That cuts both ways. We are moving from AVOID to HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the priced-in negative and the genuine recovery optionality make the risk/reward two-sided, and the distressed balance sheet leaves too little margin for error to justify a BUY. This report reconciles that judgment against our three prior AVOID calls.

## Company Overview

O-I Glass manufactures glass bottles and jars for the beverage and food industries across two reportable segments: Americas (~$3.64B net sales in 2025, 57% of segment total, covering North America, Mexico, Brazil, and South America) and Europe (~$2.69B, 43%, principally France and Italy). Non-U.S. operations account for roughly 73% of sales, with France, Italy, and Mexico each individually exceeding 10% of consolidated revenue. The company holds 50% JV interests in Italy and Mexico.

The end-market mix is a structural headwind. Alcoholic beverages (beer, wine, and spirits) represent roughly 60% of Q1 2026 volume, food 23%, and non-alcoholic beverages 16%. Alcohol consumption is in secular decline across developed markets, and glass faces ongoing substrate substitution to aluminum cans, PET, cartons, and pouches. Management's strategic response is a distressed-industrial playbook: close redundant furnaces and plants, cut SG&A through the Fit to Win program, deliberately exit unprofitable volume, and reposition toward higher-value categories (premium spirits, food, non-alcoholic, ready-to-drink). That repositioning intentionally sacrifices reported tonnage in the near term.

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe a highly leveraged, cyclically pressured business in the middle of a large restructuring.

**Revenue is declining:** $6.86B (2022) → $7.11B (2023) → $6.53B (2024) → $6.43B (2025). Q1 2026 sales were $1.54B, down 2% YoY, with shipments down 8-9% in tons, partly a tough comparison against pre-tariff buying in Q1 2025, partly genuine weakness in beer/wine/spirits.

**GAAP profitability has deteriorated sharply from the 2022 peak.** Operating income fell from $805M (2022) to $67M (2023) to $38M (2024) to negative in 2025. Net income swung from +$584M (2022) to losses of -$103M, -$106M, and -$129M across 2023-2025. Q1 2026 posted a net loss of -$73M (-$0.48/share). The GAAP losses are dominated by non-operating items: ~$443M of restructuring and impairment charges in 2025 (plus $206M in 2024) and $341M of annual interest expense. On management's segment operating profit basis, 2025 was up 13% to $846M, driven by Fit to Win cost savings. The gap between GAAP losses and rising segment profit is the crux: the business is operationally viable but its earnings power is being consumed by leverage and restructuring cost.

**The balance sheet is the binding constraint.** Total debt is ~$5.0B (long-term $4.8B) against equity of $1.29B, producing D/E of 6.4x. The Altman Z-Score of 0.91 places the company in the distress zone, with negative EBIT/assets and equity/assets of only 15.6%. Cash fell from $759M (Dec 2025) to $317M (Mar 2026) on a seasonal working-capital swing, partially offset by heavy receivables factoring ($438M of receivables sold, a quality-of-earnings item that flatters reported leverage and working capital). A $1.24B undrawn revolver provides the liquidity backstop.

**The refinancing profile has improved.** In September 2025, O-I refinanced into a new $2.7B secured facility (Term Loan A due 2030, Term Loan B due 2032, $1.25B revolver). The senior note ladder runs 6.625% '27, 6.25% '28, 5.25% '29, 4.75% '30, 7.25% '31, 7.375% '32, with roughly 30% floating-rate and a 5.36% weighted average. The single financial maintenance covenant, Secured Leverage Ratio ≤ 2.50x, was in compliance as of Q1 2026. The senior notes disclosed as trading below par are the longer-dated tranches (4.75% '30 at ~93, 7.25% '31 at ~96, 7.375% '32 at ~94), which signals credit caution on the back end; the brief does not provide price levels for the front-end notes.

**Cash flow is thin after debt service.** Operating cash flow was $600M in 2025 (up from $489M), guided to ~$500-600M for 2026 against ~$450M capex plus ~$150M restructuring payments, leaving near-zero true free cash flow before any energy or volume disappointment. Capital allocation is prioritized to debt service and capex, with only a modest $40M/year anti-dilutive buyback and no common dividend. Goodwill remains $1.49B (16% of assets); the Europe reporting unit exceeded its carrying value by only 21% in the October 2025 test, leaving a thin impairment cushion.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is negative and will likely remain so through mid-2026. Revenue has declined for two straight years, and Q1 2026 tonnage fell 8-9%. The secular decline in alcohol consumption and continued substrate substitution cap the addressable base in O-I's largest category.

Two forward signals argue for a stabilization path rather than continued decay. First, the volume trajectory improved sequentially within Q1, and management guides to flat volume in Q2 and low-to-mid-single-digit growth in H2 2026, supported by new business wins concentrated in the Americas. The longer-term target is 1-2% profitable growth from 2027. Second, Fit to Win is running ahead of plan, and those savings are what drove segment operating profit up 13% in a year when revenue fell.

The 2026 profit bridge is dominated by a European energy headwind. Favorable gas contracts expired at end-2025, embedding ~$150M of higher European energy costs into 2026, with a potential additional $75-100M if Middle East conflict drives further inflation. Energy is 10-20% of manufacturing cost. The full-year EPS guidance cut reflects the race between self-help cost savings and external energy and demand pressure. R&D spending is also falling ($42M in 2025 versus $80M in 2024) following the MAGMA halt, which trims the innovation pipeline.

Net, the growth outlook is low but not collapsing: a stabilization-then-modest-recovery path that depends on Europe energy not worsening and Fit to Win continuing to over-deliver.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing earnings the stock is meaningless to value (negative EPS). The relevant frame is management's FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $1.00-$1.50; at $8.88, that implies a forward multiple of roughly 6-9x, well below the peer median P/E of 19.9x (Greif 21.5x, Silgan 15.1x, AptarGroup 19.9x, Ball 16.4x).

That discount is not a free lunch. All four peers are profitable, less levered, and pay dividends; O-I carries a distress-zone balance sheet, no dividend, and negative GAAP earnings. A compressed multiple that reflects genuine leverage and cyclical risk is often a value trap, not a mispricing, and we treat optical cheapness here as a warning rather than a buy signal in its own right.

The case for the discount being partly earned by recovery optionality rests on the enterprise-value math. With market cap ~$1.4B against ~$5.0B of debt, the equity is a thin residual claim on a ~$6.1B enterprise. If Europe segment profit recovers even partially in H2 2026 as guided, and Fit to Win savings continue compounding, the incremental operating profit accrues disproportionately to the equity because the debt base is fixed. A move in segment operating profit of $100-150M, plausible given Fit to Win's run rate versus the $150M energy headwind, can shift the equity value materially given the leverage. The same mechanism works in reverse: a further European energy spike above the derivative price cap, or renewed volume weakness, drives the equity lower.

We score valuation at 5.0: the forward multiple is optically cheap with real recovery optionality, but the balance-sheet distress and the anti-predictive history of "cheap" leveraged industrials keep it from a higher score. There is no clean analyst consensus target in the dataset (targets are null); we do not manufacture one.

## Competitive Landscape

O-I is one of the world's largest glass-container producers, competing against Ardagh, Verallia, and Vitro, and, more consequentially, against alternative packaging. The industry faces excess capacity in parts of Europe, intense price competition, and a consolidating customer base (large beverage companies) that increases pricing pressure on suppliers. These are structural, not cyclical, pressures.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-deteriorating. The MAGMA halt in Q2 2025 (a $117M charge) is a meaningful strategic retreat: MAGMA had been positioned as O-I's next-generation modular melting and decarbonization differentiator, and abandoning it in favor of near-term deleveraging cedes a potential cost-curve and ESG advantage even as it is rational balance-sheet management. The "shrink to strength" strategy (closing redundant furnaces, exiting unprofitable volume, and repositioning toward premium spirits, food, and non-alcoholic categories) is the correct playbook for a distressed operator but does not widen the moat; it defends margin against a shrinking base. We score competitive position 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that specifically matter for O-I, beyond generic sector cyclicality:

1. **European energy cost step-up, the dominant near-term earnings risk.** The reset is already in the P&L, with a potential additional $75-100M if Middle East conflict escalates. The gas hedge and the new energy-derivative contract (with an indemnification structure carrying a price trigger and price cap, newly disclosed in the Q1 10-Q) limit but do not eliminate the exposure; a spike above the cap would leave the company fully exposed.

2. **Leverage and refinancing risk.** $5.0B of debt against a shrinking, low-margin, cyclical earnings base, with an Altman Z of 0.91 and springing-maturity provisions tying term-loan maturities to the note ladder. The single covenant (Secured Leverage ≤ 2.50x) is currently met, so no covenant or liquidity trigger is imminent, but the margin for error is thin.

3. **Secular and cyclical demand decline** in beer/wine/spirits, compounded by ongoing destocking and substrate substitution.

4. **Fit to Win execution risk**, heightened by governance turnover (below) and by European plant/furnace closures facing workers'-council and labor constraints (72% of U.S./Canada and 83% of South America/Mexico workforces are unionized).

5. **Goodwill impairment risk** — a further profit shortfall could trigger a non-cash write-down given the thin Europe cushion.

6. **FX translation** — 73% of sales are non-USD; 2025 FX was a tailwind that may reverse in 2026.

7. **Legal/regulatory** — a French antitrust investigation (no charges yet, potential material fines) and rising EU ETS costs (~$28M in 2025, free-allowance phase-out 2026-2034).

Much of the near-term risk is now priced after the -38% decline. The incremental risks not fully reflected are an energy escalation above the derivative cap and a Europe goodwill impairment. We score risk factors 3.5, up modestly from prior reports to reflect that the headline deterioration is now in the price.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility is 54.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 50.7%, slightly rich but not extreme, at only the 5th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.2% at the front, though -6.6% at the 3-month tenor), and the term structure is in contango (54.3% near versus 60.0% at six months), a normal, non-stressed shape. IV rose 9 points (45.3% → 54.3%) over the past three weeks as the price slipped 5.9%, indicating the market is pricing modestly higher uncertainty but not acute crash risk. The implied one-month move is ±12.7%. The collapse in put/call OI ratio (0.80 → 0.07) does not reflect a bearish tilt; combined with balanced skew, the options market is not confirming an urgent downside catalyst, consistent with our view that the negative is largely discounted.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The equity is a leveraged option on a turnaround that is running ahead of plan. Fit to Win is over-delivering, segment operating profit rose despite falling revenue, and volumes are improving sequentially. With European gas largely hedged and the guidance cut already digested, H2 2026 could show Europe stabilizing while Fit to Win compounds. Because the ~$6.1B enterprise is dominated by ~$5.0B of debt, a $100-150M swing in operating profit produces an outsized move in the ~$1.4B equity. The five-insider open-market buying cluster in May 2026 (the CFO, General Counsel, an SVP, and a director, all at $8.50-$9.30) is a genuine vote of confidence at the current price. A $1.24B undrawn revolver argues against imminent distress. Recovery toward the low-to-mid teens is plausible if Europe profit rebuilds. Bull case: ~$14-15 (+55-70%).

**Bear case.** The balance sheet leaves no margin for error. If the European energy headwind worsens above the derivative price cap, or if the additional $75-100M Middle East scenario materializes, or if volume weakness persists, segment profit cannot simultaneously cover $341M of interest, $450M of capex, and $150M of restructuring, and the thin equity compresses. A Europe goodwill impairment or covenant pressure would accelerate the decline. Continued alcohol-category shrinkage and substrate substitution cap the recovery even if energy normalizes. Bear case: ~$5-6 (-33% to -44%), with a tail below the $7.75 low only if a covenant or liquidity event emerges (not our base case given current compliance and revolver capacity).

**Reconciling the two-sided setup.** The prior three reports carried an AVOID. On re-examination, the AVOID reasoning was internally undermined: the bearish catalysts (guidance cut, Q1 miss, Europe zero) are already reflected in the -38% decline and the position near the 52-week low, and there is no dated forward catalyst that asymmetrically drives the stock lower; Q2 is guided flat, H2 to growth, and the next print could as easily be a beat if Europe stabilizes. To sustain an AVOID we would need to quantify downside below the $7.75 low tied to a specific covenant or liquidity trigger; the secured leverage covenant is in compliance at a 2.50x threshold, and the revolver is undrawn at $1.24B, so no such trigger is imminent. When a bearish thesis is already in the price, insiders are buying at the current level, and the self-help program is over-delivering, the honest classification is a two-sided HOLD, not an AVOID. We also weigh that Materials-sector selection has been a persistent source of negative relative returns, which keeps us from a BUY on optical cheapness alone, and that financial health, the single most reliable discriminator in our framework, remains genuinely weak here (Z 0.91, D/E 6.4x). The result is a low-conviction, uncertain HOLD.

**On management and governance (reconciling Management Quality).** The insider buying cluster is a strong forward-looking positive. It is partly offset by governance turnover (multiple Item 5.02 officer/director filings across November 2025, March 2026, and July 2026 during a critical restructuring) and by CEO compensation of $11.5M reported / $18.1M "actually paid" in a year with a $129M net loss (TSR-linked, and O-I's TSR of 123.96 did beat its peer group's 87.49, which partly explains the equity outcome). The CEO (Hardie) and CFO (Haudrich) remain in place and are among the buyers. Balancing the genuine open-market purchases against real turnover during a self-help turnaround, we set Management Quality at 5.0.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: a two-sided, leveraged-turnaround equity where we lack conviction for either a BUY or an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Europe segment operating profit recovers to positive and sustains for two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026), demonstrating the energy headwind is being absorbed rather than compounding.
- Fit to Win cumulative savings track ahead of the ≥$750M target with FY2026 adjusted EPS delivered at or above the midpoint of guidance, and free cash flow after debt service and restructuring turns durably positive.
- Volume returns to the guided flat-to-growth path in H2 2026 with continued Americas new-business wins.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A European energy escalation above the derivative price cap, or realization of the additional $75-100M cost scenario, that forces a further guidance cut below $1.00 adjusted EPS.
- A Europe goodwill impairment or a move toward the 2.50x secured leverage covenant that raises refinancing/covenant risk.
- Volume decline re-accelerates (worse than -5% YoY) and segment operating profit falls below the level needed to cover interest plus maintenance capex.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July / early August) is the key checkpoint; Europe segment profit versus the energy step-up is the single most important monitorable. Monitoring the longer-dated bond prices as a real-time credit gauge is warranted between prints.

## Conclusion

O-I Glass is a highly leveraged glass-container manufacturer whose equity now functions as a call option on the Fit to Win cost turnaround succeeding against a European energy headwind that has already zeroed out Europe segment profit. The fundamental deterioration is real (three years of GAAP losses, D/E of 6.4x, an Altman Z of 0.91, a -71.8% Q1 miss, and a guidance cut) but it is largely reflected in the 38% decline to near the 52-week low. Against that priced-in negative sit genuine offsets: Fit to Win running ahead of plan, segment operating profit up 13%, sequential volume improvement, European gas largely hedged, and a five-insider open-market buying cluster including the CFO at the current price. With no imminent covenant or liquidity trigger and a risk/reward that is genuinely two-sided from $8.88, the appropriate call is HOLD (uncertain), an upgrade from the prior AVOID that reconciles the earlier reasoning's internal tension. The distressed balance sheet and negative Materials-sector selection history keep us from a BUY; the priced-in negatives and real recovery optionality keep us from an AVOID. Europe segment profit at the next print is the decisive variable.
