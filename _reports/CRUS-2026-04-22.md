## Executive Summary

Cirrus Logic is a fabless semiconductor company specializing in low-power, high-precision mixed-signal processing, primarily for Apple's smartphone ecosystem. Apple accounts for 89% of FY2025 revenue (rising to 94% in the December 2025 quarter), making CRUS functionally a single-customer business. This concentration simultaneously constitutes the company's greatest vulnerability and its deepest competitive moat.

The financials are excellent. CRUS carries zero debt against $1.08B in liquid assets, generates $400-500M in annual operating cash flow on sub-$25M capex, and is retiring shares at ~4.5% annually. A structural tax rate reduction (from ~25.5% to ~16.1% effective rate following the OBBBA legislation) adds $40-50M annually to net income. Gross margins have expanded from 50.4% (FY2023) to 53.1% (Q3 FY2026), and operating margins reached 26.3% in the most recent quarter. Consensus FY2027 EPS estimates have risen 13.5% over the past 90 days to $9.06, and the company has beaten estimates in all four reported quarters by an average of 27.7%.

At $169.80, CRUS trades at approximately 18.7x forward consensus EPS and roughly 20x trailing FCF. This is in line with analog/mixed-signal semiconductor peers (Skyworks 22.6x, Qorvo 22.5x) but carries a higher growth trajectory and superior balance sheet. The March 2026 announcement that Apple included CRUS in its American Manufacturing Program — a public endorsement naming Cirrus as a key development partner for semiconductor technologies at GlobalFoundries in New York — materially reduces the near-term probability of Apple designing out CRUS content, which is the single biggest risk investors price into the stock.

## Company Overview

Cirrus Logic, headquartered in Austin, Texas, designs and sells mixed-signal integrated circuits through two product lines: Audio (60% of FY2025 revenue, $1.14B) and High-Performance Mixed-Signal (HPMS, 40%, $759M). Audio products include amplifiers, codecs, smart codecs, ADCs, DACs, and DSPs used in smartphones, laptops, tablets, wearables, and automotive. HPMS products include camera controllers (autofocus/OIS), haptic/sensing solutions, and battery/power management ICs.

The company employs 1,660 people, 71% in R&D, and holds approximately 4,130 patents. Manufacturing is fully outsourced (fabless model) to TSMC in Taiwan and GlobalFoundries in Singapore and Germany, with assembly/test at ASE, Amkor, and others. The fabless model enables the capital-light profile that produces >95% FCF conversion on net income.

CRUS's strategic priority is increasing content per device within Apple's ecosystem (camera controllers, haptics, power ICs — all growing faster than audio) while expanding into adjacent markets, primarily laptops (most immediate), automotive, and industrial. The company secured its first high-volume mainstream PC codec design win in Q3 FY2026, and multiple OEMs showcased PCs with CRUS amplifiers, codecs, and haptic drivers at CES in January 2026. A new voice interface component targeting AI-enabled PCs is currently sampling.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue was $1.896B (+6.0% YoY). The first nine months of FY2026 produced $1.549B (+5.2% YoY). Growth is modest at the top line because CRUS operates in a maturing smartphone market where unit growth is minimal. Revenue growth comes from content expansion — more chips per device — offset by ongoing ASP declines driven by Apple's negotiating leverage. HPMS grew 10% YoY in the first nine months of FY2026 versus 2% for Audio, confirming the content-expansion thesis is working.

**Profitability:** Gross margins expanded from 50.4% (FY2023) to 52.5% (FY2025) to 53.1% (Q3 FY2026), driven by higher HPMS mix and latest-generation product ramps. Operating margins reached 26.3% in Q3 FY2026, up from 21.6% for full-year FY2025, reflecting operating leverage as R&D spending has been held roughly flat ($435M FY2025 vs. $458M FY2023) while revenue grows. The OBBBA tax reform reduced the effective tax rate from ~25.5% to ~16.1%, producing a Q3 FY2026 net margin of 24.2%.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF was $422M (22.2% FCF margin) on just $23M capex. Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow of $499M already exceeds FY2025's full-year $444M. FCF conversion on net income consistently runs above 100%, reflecting high earnings quality. An additional $110M in working capital was released as inventory declined from $299M to $189M, unwinding the GlobalFoundries wafer commitment.

**Balance Sheet:** Zero debt ($300M revolver undrawn, maturing July 2026). Cash and marketable securities totaled $1.08B as of December 2025. Current ratio: 7.7x. Altman Z-Score: 3.61 (safe zone). This is among the cleanest balance sheets in the mid-cap semiconductor space.

**Capital Allocation:** $261M in buybacks in FY2025, $210M in the first nine months of FY2026. Diluted shares declined from 55.2M to 52.7M (4.5% reduction). $344M remaining on a $500M authorization. No dividend. R&D investment runs at 21-23% of revenue, appropriate for a technology company at this scale.

**Earnings Momentum:** Four consecutive earnings beats averaging 27.7% above consensus. Current-quarter EPS estimates revised up 44.7% over 90 days (from $1.22 to $1.76). Full-year FY2027 estimates up 13.5% over 90 days to $9.06. This trajectory reflects the market gradually pricing in the tax tailwind and margin expansion.

## Growth Analysis

CRUS's growth story has three components, each operating on a different timeline:

**Near-term (FY2026-2027): Content expansion within Apple.** HPMS revenue is growing at roughly 2x the rate of Audio. Each new iPhone generation incorporates more CRUS silicon — camera controllers, haptic drivers, power management ICs — on top of the established audio codec and amplifier content. The Apple American Manufacturing Program inclusion (March 2026) signals that Apple is expanding, not contracting, the CRUS content roadmap.

**Medium-term (FY2027-2028): Laptop and AI PC penetration.** The first mainstream PC codec design win (Q3 FY2026) and CES product showcases represent the beginning of a potentially meaningful revenue stream. Voice interfaces, enhanced audio processing, and haptic feedback for AI-enabled laptops require exactly the mixed-signal processing CRUS provides. TAM contribution is modest initially (likely $50-100M incremental revenue within 2-3 years) but represents genuine diversification away from Apple.

**Long-term (FY2028+): Automotive haptics and industrial.** New automotive haptic component family announced Q3 FY2026. Revenue contribution is years away, but automotive design cycles are long and sticky once won.

The constraint on growth is the underlying smartphone market. Global smartphone shipments are growing at low single digits, and replacement cycles are lengthening. CRUS must outgrow unit volumes through content — a strategy that has worked historically but requires continuous innovation and Apple's willingness to adopt new CRUS components rather than in-sourcing them.

FY2027 consensus revenue growth is implied at mid-to-high single digits, supported by continued HPMS expansion and the tax tailwind flowing through to EPS.

Against FY2028 consensus of $9.08, the forward multiple is essentially unchanged at 18.7x, implying the market expects flat growth beyond FY2027 — a conservative assumption given the laptop and HPMS expansion trajectory.

CRUS's 18.7x multiple represents a ~17% discount to these peers despite superior margin trajectory, zero debt, and stronger estimate momentum. The discount reflects Apple concentration risk, which is rational but may be excessive given the American Manufacturing Program endorsement.

**FCF yield:** At ~$500M+ annualized FCF and $8.7B market cap, the FCF yield is approximately 5.7%. For a technology company with expanding margins, zero debt, and 4.5% annual share count reduction, this is attractive.

The 52-week high of $173 is only 2% above the current price. The consensus mean target of $160 is below the current price, though this likely reflects stale estimates that haven't fully incorporated the tax benefit and margin expansion.

If the laptop diversification thesis gains traction and reduces the Apple concentration discount, a move toward 22-24x forward earnings is plausible over 12-18 months.

## Competitive Landscape

Cirrus Logic occupies a specialized niche in mixed-signal processing where no single competitor spans all product lines. In audio, competitors include AKM Semiconductor, Realtek, and Qualcomm (integrating audio into application processors). In HPMS, competition comes from Analog Devices, Texas Instruments, Skyworks, and STMicroelectronics.

The competitive moat is narrow but deep within Apple's ecosystem. CRUS components are custom-designed for Apple's system architecture, involve joint development with Apple engineering teams, and are protected by exclusivity periods. Switching costs for Apple are high — qualifying new suppliers for mixed-signal components involves multi-year design cycles and extensive testing. The 4,130-patent portfolio provides additional protection.

The primary long-term competitive threat is Apple's in-house silicon team designing out CRUS functionality. Apple has demonstrated the capability and willingness to bring chip design in-house (M-series processors replacing Intel, modem development replacing Qualcomm). However, mixed-signal analog design is a fundamentally different discipline from digital logic — it requires specialized expertise and process technology that is difficult to replicate, even for Apple. The American Manufacturing Program inclusion argues against near-term in-sourcing.

In the laptop market, CRUS faces incumbent competitors (Realtek dominates PC audio codecs) but brings a differentiated offering in high-performance audio, haptics, and voice processing that aligns with the AI PC upgrade cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Apple concentration (Critical).** At 89-94% of revenue, any material reduction in Apple content — whether through in-sourcing, competitive displacement, or lower iPhone volumes — would be devastating. The March 2026 Manufacturing Program announcement mitigates near-term design-out risk, but the structural vulnerability persists. This risk cannot be hedged within a CRUS position.

**IRS transfer pricing dispute ($232M exposure).** The IRS has asserted $168.3M in additional tax plus $63.7M in penalties for FY2017-2019 transfer pricing between U.S. and U.K. affiliates. If resolved against CRUS, the $232M exposure (excluding interest) represents approximately 27% of current liquid assets. The company states it "vigorously disputes" the claims. Resolution timeline is uncertain.

**TSMC/Taiwan supply chain risk.** CRUS relies on TSMC for critical wafer fabrication. Any disruption to TSMC operations — whether from China-Taiwan tensions, natural disaster, or trade restrictions — would halt production. The GlobalFoundries partnership provides partial diversification, but TSMC remains irreplaceable for certain process nodes.

**ASP erosion.** Every quarterly filing references "declines in ASPs due to previously anticipated pricing reductions." Apple's negotiating leverage ensures persistent pricing pressure. CRUS must run faster (more content, new products) to offset this structural headwind.

**UK R&D tax credit dependence.** CRUS receives a £43M annual R&D expenditure credit from the UK. If this program were modified or rescinded, R&D expenses would increase materially and profitability would decline.

**Tariff risk.** 99% of sales are international, with China the largest ship-to destination (61% of Q3 FY2026 sales, $357M). U.S.-China trade tensions could disrupt the supply chain or customer relationships.

## Conclusion

Cirrus Logic presents a compelling risk/reward at 18.7x forward earnings for a debt-free, FCF-generative semiconductor business with expanding margins, a structural tax tailwind, and positive estimate momentum. The Apple concentration risk is real — at 89-94% of revenue, CRUS is functionally an Apple supplier with a public market wrapper. The March 2026 American Manufacturing Program inclusion is the strongest public signal in years that Apple is investing in the CRUS relationship rather than diversifying away from it. The 17% P/E discount to analog semiconductor peers overstates the near-term in-sourcing risk.

The stock has rallied significantly from its April 2025 lows, and the consensus mean price target of $160 sits below the current price, which warrants caution on entry timing. However, consensus targets appear stale relative to the estimate revision trajectory. BUY with a 12-18 month horizon, targeting $200 base case. Position sizing should reflect the binary nature of the Apple concentration risk — this is a 2-3% position, not a core holding.
