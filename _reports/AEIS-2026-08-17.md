## Executive Summary

Advanced Energy has delivered the operating inflection it has been promising since its December 2024 investor day, and Q2 2026 removed most of the ambiguity. Revenue of $574.1M rose 30.0% year over year with all four end markets growing; non-GAAP EPS of $2.74 beat the $2.24 consensus by 22.1% — the fourth consecutive beat; non-GAAP operating margin reached 21.9%, up from 10.2% in FY2024. My calculation of H1 2026 incremental non-GAAP operating margin — the change in non-GAAP operating income of $104.0M divided by the change in revenue of $239.0M — is 43.5%. That figure, not the headline growth rate, is the engine of the equity story: a company that converts 43 cents of every incremental revenue dollar to operating profit re-rates quickly when volume arrives.

Volume is arriving. Management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to low-to-mid-30% from low-to-mid-20%, guided Q3 to $640M ±$20M (+11.5% sequentially) with non-GAAP EPS of $3.00 ±$0.25, and told investors to expect record revenue in both Q3 and Q4, second-half semiconductor revenue up nearly 50% year over year, and full-year data center growth of at least 50%. Consensus has moved hard in response: FY2026 EPS of $11.24 is up 20.6% in 90 days and FY2027 EPS of $15.19 is up 29.2%. At the $330.26 close that is 29.4x current-year and 21.7x next-year earnings against 35% expected EPS growth — a PEG near 0.6. The lowest of twelve published price targets ($370) sits 12.0% above spot.

Three concrete offsets keep this at ACCUMULATE rather than BUY. First, customer concentration worsened in each of the last two years to three customers at 23%, 19% and 12% of FY2025 revenue (54% total, 56% of receivables), and management has confirmed the data center business rests on four hyperscale accounts — end-market diversification has improved while customer diversification has deteriorated. Second, H1 2026 free cash flow was negative $6.1M (operating cash flow of $80.0M less $86.1M of capex) with inventory up 30.9% in six months to $538.1M, or 145 days, in precisely the account Ernst & Young designated its critical audit matter. Third, the Singapore tax holiday worth $31.0M, or $0.82 per diluted share, in FY2025 expires June 30, 2027, and the benefit is scaling with foreign profit. At 21.7x FY2027 there is room for all three, but the downside scenario is deep enough (-38%) that staged entry is the appropriate expression.

## Company Overview

Advanced Energy designs and manufactures precision power conversion, measurement and control products — equipment that transforms raw utility or facility power into the highly controllable, repeatable power that complex process tools and high-density computing racks require. Incorporated in Colorado in 1981 and reincorporated in Delaware in 1995, it is headquartered in Denver and employs approximately 13,000 people. It reports as a single segment with four disclosed end markets.

| End market | Q2 2026 | % of rev | Q2 2025 | YoY | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor Equipment | $278.3M | 48.5% | $209.5M | +32.8% | $839.9M | $792.5M |
| Data Center Computing | $191.5M | 33.4% | $141.6M | +35.2% | $587.3M | $284.2M |
| Industrial & Medical | $80.0M | 13.9% | $68.6M | +16.6% | $282.3M | $316.2M |
| Telecom & Networking | $24.3M | 4.2% | $21.8M | +11.5% | $89.3M | $89.1M |
| **Total** | **$574.1M** | | **$441.5M** | **+30.0%** | **$1,798.8M** | **$1,482.0M** |

Semiconductor Equipment is the franchise: plasma power for dry etch, strip and deposition, plus high-voltage power, system power, remote plasma sources and thermal/sensing instrumentation. The 10-K is unusually specific about the company's own position — it claims leading share in conductor etch and deposition and explicitly identifies dielectric etch as "a targeted application with lower market share." That asymmetry frames the growth thesis, because a low-share position in a large adjacent application does not require the end market to expand.

Data Center Computing supplies power shelves and conversion modules designed into cloud service providers' custom racks. Advanced Energy is a component supplier here; the racks and systems belong to the hyperscalers and their ODM partners. Management has described the account strategy as deliberately selective because those customers require substantial engineering support.

Manufacturing runs from large factories in the Philippines, Malaysia and Mexico, with the Zhongshan, China plant having ceased manufacturing in Q2 2025 and a new Thailand facility scheduled for first revenue in Q4 2026. R&D was $232.4M in FY2025, 12.9% of revenue, within a historical 12.2%–14.3% band. Named competitors are COMET, Daihen, MKS Instruments and TRUMPF Hüttinger in semiconductor; Delta Electronics, Flex and Lite-On in data center; and a fragmented set including TDK-Lambda, XP Power and MEAN WELL in industrial/medical.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin trajectory.** The sequence is the most important series in the file:

| Period | Revenue | GAAP GM | Non-GAAP GM | Non-GAAP op margin | Non-GAAP EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $1,482.0M | 35.7% | 36.3% | 10.2% | $3.71 |
| FY2025 | $1,798.8M | 37.7% | 38.7% | 15.8% | $6.41 |
| H1 2026 | $1,085.1M | 40.3% | 41.0% | 20.6% | $4.79 |
| Q2 2026 | $574.1M | 41.1% | 41.9% | 21.9% | $2.74 |

One adjustment is required and management supplied it unprompted: roughly 120bps of the Q2 gross margin came from non-recurring tariff refunds, so the underlying non-GAAP gross margin was 40.7%. Guidance of 41–41.5% for Q3 excluding refunds and roughly 42% for Q4 means the underlying trend still improves after stripping the one-timer. The CFO also quantified the leverage slope — approximately 40bps of gross margin per incremental $50M of quarterly revenue — which is a usable modeling input and reconciles with the 43.5% incremental operating margin I computed.

**Cash flow is the clear weakness.** H1 2026 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $80.0M against $86.1M of cash capex, producing free cash flow of negative $6.1M versus positive $33.7M in H1 2025 — while income from continuing operations was $121.8M. The reconciliation is working capital and capex, not accrual quality: non-cash add-backs totaled roughly $107.3M (D&A $32.3M, stock compensation $39.5M, the $31.8M induced-conversion loss, and smaller items), implying pre-working-capital operating cash of about $229.1M, against which working capital consumed $149.1M — inventories $(128.3)M, receivables $(79.9)M, partially offset by payables of +$92.5M.

Inventory is the item to watch. It rose from $411.2M at year-end to $538.1M at June 30, up 30.9% in six months, with parts and raw materials alone up 35.0% to $423.5M. On Q2-annualized cost of revenue that is 145 days and 2.51 turns, up from roughly 134 days at year-end on a trailing basis. Management frames this as deliberate — strategic component buying and elevated factory staffing to avoid constraining customer ramps in a market where AI-related component lead times are extending. Receivables discipline is intact at 64 days DSO, improved from 66. But Ernst & Young named inventory valuation the critical audit matter on a smaller ($411.2M) balance, so this is the account where a demand push-out would land first.

Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow, built by summing H2 2025 and H1 2026, is $239.0M of operating cash flow less $151.5M of capex, or $87.5M — a 0.66% yield on the $13,227M market cap at $330.26. I would not annualize H1's negative print: capex is front-loaded into Thailand and a new ERP, and inventory building for a ramp is a balance-sheet event. As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast, if working capital merely stopped growing in H2 while the pre-working-capital operating cash run rate held near $229M per half, H2 free cash flow would be roughly $145M. The less charitable normalized view is that a business growing 30%+ with 145 days of inventory structurally consumes cash until growth decelerates.

**Balance sheet, rebuilt in Q2.** Cash is $1,396.5M against debt principal of $1,286.7M — net cash of $109.8M, down from $216.2M at year-end because the refinancing consumed roughly $106M net. The current ratio recovered to 3.79x from 1.59x, the latter an artifact of $567.5M of convertible notes sitting in current liabilities at 12/31/2025 after the stock exceeded 130% of the $137.46 conversion price. Any screen-based distress reading built on year-end data (an Altman Z near 2.6) reflects that reclassification, not the current structure. The $600M revolver remains fully undrawn and has never been drawn. Gross debt to my estimate of TTM non-GAAP EBITDA (~$429M) is about 3.0x; net leverage is negative.

The May–June financing deserves credit. Advanced Energy issued $1.15B of 0% convertible notes due 2031 at a $508.78 conversion price (54.1% above the current close), netting $1,128.1M, and paid $69.0M for a capped call struck to $678.38. My derived all-in cost — $1,059.1M net raised against $1,150M repayable in five years — is approximately 1.66% per annum. Concurrently it induced conversion of $438.3M of the 2.5% 2028 notes for $442.4M cash plus roughly 2.0M shares (a $31.8M P&L charge, non-deductible, which pushed Q2's effective tax rate to 18.2%) and recovered $44.6M unwinding the associated hedges and warrants. The remaining $136.7M of 2028 notes was called for September 23, 2026, with cash for principal and shares for the excess: at $330.26 that is roughly $331.8M of conversion value, $195.1M of excess, and approximately 0.59M shares — 1.5% of current shares — plus a $136.7M cash outflow.

Dilution should be kept in tiers. Reported Q2 diluted shares were 42.3M GAAP and 41.0M non-GAAP against 40,050,442 shares outstanding; shares rose 5.8% in six months, mostly from the induced conversion. The 3,390,430-share cap on the 2031 notes is a maximum contingent figure equal to 8.5% of current shares (7.8% post-issuance ownership dilution), requiring the stock above $508.78, requiring the company to elect share settlement of the excess, and economically offset by the capped call up to $678.38. Separately, the equity plan pool rose from 1.4M to 3.7M shares after the May 8 annual meeting.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on four legs with differing verification quality.

**Near-term semiconductor volume (highest confidence).** Q2 semiconductor revenue grew 32.8% and management guided second-half semiconductor revenue up nearly 50% year over year. Management was explicit that most of this comes from existing products, with new platforms contributing more meaningfully in 2027 — which makes the near-term number less dependent on qualification timing.

**Data center (high confidence, structurally lower margin).** DCC grew 35.2% in Q2 and is guided to at least 50% for the year, having already gone from 19.2% of FY2024 revenue to 33.4% of Q2 2026. The FY2025 risk factors state that DCC "generally has lower margins than our other markets," so mix is a real drag that semiconductor mix, new products and roughly 140bps of manufacturing consolidation savings have so far more than offset.

**Industrial & Medical recovery (moderate confidence).** I&M fell from $474.4M in FY2023 to $282.3M in FY2025, a 40% two-year decline, and grew 16.6% in Q2. Management reported bookings over the past three quarters at nearly double the prior two-year rate. That is an externally reported management statement, not filing text, but the segment's depressed base makes recovery arithmetic favorable.

**2027–2028 optionality (lowest confidence, and the market is paying for it).** Dielectric etch share gains, eVerest/eVoS platform acceptance, "second-wave" data center customers, and the 800V rack architecture with initial production revenue in late 2027 and a meaningful ramp in 2028. Management says it is running ahead of the December 2024 investor day plan. None of this is in the guidance horizon, and management dated the 800V ramp beyond FY2027.

Consensus reflects the first three legs and some of the fourth: FY2026 EPS $11.24 (+20.6% in 90 days), FY2027 $15.19 (+29.2%). Applying the raised revenue growth guidance to FY2025's $1,798.8M gives $2,338M–$2,428M for FY2026, midpoint roughly $2,390M. My bottom-up FY2026 check — H1 actual $4.79 plus the $3.00 Q3 guide plus roughly $3.25 in Q4 — lands near $11.00, so consensus is modestly above my straight-line build, which the beat pattern (22.1%, 3.5%, 7.8%, 16.1% over four quarters) makes defensible. Capacity is not the constraint: management stated the factory network could support roughly $5B of revenue when fully built out against a ~$2.4B FY2026 base, though that is a capability statement I could not locate in a filing.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is a design-win moat. Requalifying a plasma generator on an etch chamber is expensive and slow, which is why the 10-K's competitive factors lead with product performance and adjacent-product compatibility before price. The trajectory is improving: non-GAAP gross margin ex-refund of 40.7% against 36.3% in FY2024 is evidence of mix and pricing gains, not just volume absorption.

In semiconductor, MKS Instruments is the most direct and best-resourced comparable in RF and plasma power. The company's own asymmetry — leading share in conductor etch and deposition, low share in dielectric etch — is the highest-quality growth available to it, because share capture in a large adjacent application does not depend on WFE expansion. The structural argument that plasma power grows faster than WFE (more plasma steps, more complex power content per step) is credible and has worked for other subsystem suppliers. The counterargument is that the dielectric-etch incumbent has every incentive to defend, and no independent third-party share data is available to verify the company's characterizations.

In data center, competitors Delta Electronics, Flex and Lite-On are all far larger, with Delta in particular vertically integrated and scale-advantaged. Advanced Energy's stated approach is deliberately narrow — high-end, high-power, differentiated applications where power density, efficiency and time-to-production matter. Serving four hyperscale customers is a choice with a cost: lower gross margins and severe concentration. The 800V transition is the strategic prize because it raises content per rack, but initial production revenue is dated to late 2027.

Two structural negatives deserve weight. China export controls have already produced a disclosed, permanent loss of position: the FY2025 10-K states that since the October 2022 rules, Chinese customers "replaced us at least in part with competitors [that] operate outside the scope of U.S. export rules," and Asia-based competitors benefit from local government funding and purchasing preferences. And the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative indexed TSR trailing the peer group in every year of the five-year window, including 2025 (220.15 versus 236.57). FY2025's +82% index gain was catch-up within a re-rating cohort rather than outperformance of it.

Geographic ship-to mix shifted sharply — US down in absolute dollars to $132.3M (23.0% of Q2 revenue) from $141.7M, Mexico roughly tripled to $124.1M, Malaysia more than doubled to $66.5M. Revenue is attributed by customer ship-to location, so the most plausible reading is shipments of DCC power shelves to ODM rack-assembly sites. That is an inference; the filings neither name the customers nor explain the shift.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, worsening.** FY2025: three customers at 23%, 19% and 12% of revenue (54%) and 26%, 20% and 10% of receivables (56%). FY2024: two at 26% and 11% (37% of revenue). FY2023: one at 22%. Concentration increased in each of the last two years. End-market diversification improved simultaneously, and both facts are consistent — DCC growth came from a small number of very large accounts, so growing a "new" end market concentrated the customer base. With no long-term purchase commitments, just-in-time hubs, and an obligation to hold minimum finished-goods inventory for certain customers, a single program push-out is highly consequential. The Q2 10-Q does not update concentration; the most recent hard data is 12/31/2025.

**Inventory and the critical audit matter.** $538.1M, 145 days, 2.51 turns, with parts and raw materials up 35% in six months — in the exact account Ernst & Young flagged as requiring the most judgment. Management asserts limited obsolescence risk. The benign reading requires the inventory to convert; the alternative is that some of the build reflects buying ahead of component price increases rather than confirmed demand.

**Tax cliff, quantified.** The Singapore holiday expires June 30, 2027 and was worth $31.0M / $0.82 per diluted share in FY2025 (versus $12.4M / $0.33 in FY2024), conditional on employment and investment thresholds. GAAP effective tax rate was 11.5% in FY2025 and 10.8% in H1 2026 against a 21% US statutory rate, structurally because domestic pretax losses of $(54.2)M, $(43.2)M and $(17.5)M in 2025/2024/2023 offset foreign pretax income of $222.9M, $95.6M and $140.0M. The benefit scales with foreign profit, so on the current base the annualized amount is materially larger than $31M. Any FY2028 bridge must model a rate step-up.

**Non-recurring items in the reported margin.** Roughly 120bps of Q2 gross margin was tariff refunds. Management disclosed and guided ex-refund, which is good practice, but a reader taking 41.1% GAAP gross margin at face value overstates the run rate. A separately reported "management targets gross margins above 43%" is unverified in any filing and inconsistent with the 41–42% guidance; it should not be treated as guidance.

**Cyclicality and macro.** Roughly 48% of revenue is tied to WFE and 33% to hyperscaler capex. Semiconductor equipment positioning is crowded after a large year-to-date sector advance, and a multi-week correction in the group would hit this name harder than the index given a beta above the peer median [unverified — actual: N/A]. The ~$5B nameplate capacity that produces 43% incremental margins on the way up produces the same leverage in reverse.

**Other disclosed items.** ERP implementation risk including possible write-off of capitalized costs; most manufacturing sites under operating leases; four overlapping restructuring plans costing $53.7M cumulatively with $10.8M accrued; unfunded pension obligations of $(51.6)M; legacy solar-inverter warranties running 1 to 20 years in discontinued operations; counterparty default risk on the unsecured hedges and capped call. One disclosure quality flag: Note 6 of the Q2 10-Q states combined convertible fair value of $3.4 billion against $1,286.7M of principal, which does not reconcile and appears to be an error; it affects no statement, covenant or multiple, but should be treated as unreliable rather than as evidence about the notes.

**Management alignment.** Insider activity is sales only across the reviewed window — seven dispositions between $310 and $381, including the CFO's 4,314 shares at $315.99 (leaving 22,217), with no open-market purchases and a wave of new 10b5-1 plans including the CEO's for up to 50,000 shares. These are overwhelmingly pre-scheduled, and the 10-Q supplies a mechanical driver: net payments related to stock-based awards jumped to $(39.1)M in H1 2026 from $(8.0)M as market-based awards settled above target at elevated prices. Set against rising estimates and record results, this is diversification, not a fundamental signal. The more substantive governance items are the buyback suspension (zero repurchases in Q2, $0.3M in H1, $166.6M authorized, stated as mechanically tied to the 2028 redemption notice) while holding $1.40B of cash, the equity pool increase from 1.4M to 3.7M shares, and CEO compensation of $11.19M (up 27.7%, $31.56M actually paid) against five-year TSR that trails the peer group.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($490, +48.4%).** The FY2027 consensus of $15.19 proves conservative because the beat pattern persists and the 2027 product cycle (eVerest/eVoS, dielectric-etch share, second-wave data center customers) contributes on schedule. Revenue approaches $3.0B, non-GAAP operating margin reaches the mid-20s on 40%+ incremental margins, FY2027 EPS lands near $16.50, and the multiple holds at roughly 30x as the market extends credit for 800V content growth in 2028 and the ~$5B capacity runway. Working capital normalizes in H2 2026, free cash flow inflects positively, and the $1.40B cash balance funds buybacks once the 2028 redemption clears in late September. The tax step-up is largely offset by OBBBA and Pillar II mechanics.

**Bear case ($205, -37.9%).** AI capex digests. One of the four hyperscale DCC accounts pushes a program, and because three customers are 54% of revenue there is no offset. Semiconductor orders pause as trailing-edge weakness persists and China share losses compound. The 145-day inventory becomes a write-down in the account the auditor flagged, the 43.5% incremental margin runs in reverse, and the Singapore expiry lands on a decelerating base. FY2027 EPS falls to roughly $10.50 — below current FY2026 consensus — and the multiple compresses to 19.5x. The 52-week low of $143.38 is a reminder of how far this stock travels when the cycle turns; net cash and the undrawn revolver make solvency a non-issue, which is why the bear case is a de-rating rather than a distress scenario.

**Base case ($395, +19.6%).** 26.0x FY2027 consensus of $15.19. This assumes guidance is delivered, the H2 semiconductor and DCC ramps land roughly on plan, non-GAAP gross margin follows the guided path to about 42% in Q4, working capital stops consuming cash by Q4, and the multiple holds near the low end of the semicap peer range to account for concentration and the pending tax normalization. Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear, expected return is approximately +12%.

Add the balance either on a pullback into $285–300 — approximately 25x FY2026 consensus, a level the stock traded at as recently as late July with materially lower estimates — or on the Q3 print (expected early November) confirming positive free cash flow. ## Conclusion

The score trajectory (5.5 → 6.1 → 6.3 → 6.4 → 6.3) and the prior HOLD both hinged on entry timing into the August 3 print rather than on any doubt about the operating inflection. ACCUMULATE. Own it in tranches, with the second tranche contingent on either a better price ($285–300) or the Q3 print showing the inventory build converting to cash.
