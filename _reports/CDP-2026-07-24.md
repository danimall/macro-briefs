**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

COPT Defense Properties is a self-managed office REIT with a niche that separates it from other names in a bruised subsector: it owns and develops properties adjacent to or inside U.S. Government defense installations, where security requirements mandate on-site work and physical proximity to mission-critical government functions. The Defense/IT portfolio accounts for 90.3% of annualized rental revenue (ARR) and has held year-end occupancy at or above 93% for nine consecutive years. The business rides two favorable secular themes, rising U.S. defense spending and Northern Virginia data-center demand, that are countercyclical to the remote-work and demand-decline forces that have gutted traditional office real estate. The company's total shareholder return of 132 versus a peer-group 81 over the measured window quantifies the difference.

The fundamentals are steady and high-quality. FY2025 diluted FFO per share (as adjusted) grew 5.8% to $2.72; Q1 2026 FFO grew 6.2% to $0.69 with occupancy improving to 94.4% total and 95.6% in the Defense/IT segment, and tenant retention at 90.8%. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: weighted-average cost of debt is roughly 3.4% on largely fixed-rate unsecured notes, there are no significant maturities until 2028, and the FFO payout ratio sits near 44.6%, leaving retained cash to self-fund development. The Altman Z "distress" screen and the tiny XBRL "revenue" figure are model artifacts and should be disregarded; actual FY2025 real estate revenue was $721.8M.

The issue is price. The stock has re-rated approximately 34% over twelve months to $37.48, near its 52-week high of $38.06, and now trades about 1% above the mean analyst target of $37.00 and about 3% above the median of $36.50. At ~13.8x forward FFO, the dividend yield has compressed to 3.4%, well below the Office REIT peer median of 5.6%. The quality is now substantially recognized by the market. Estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative (current-year EPS -2.8% over 90 days), and the defining near-term event, the 2026 lease-expiration wall, where 19.3% of ARR (2.9M sq ft, 83% of it USG leases) rolls, partly delayed by the 43-day 2025 federal shutdown, is a swing factor that limits the case for chasing the stock here. We rate CDP HOLD (growth_hold), reflecting a durable mid-single-digit FFO compounder whose re-rating is largely complete.

## Company Overview

COPT owns, operates, and develops a portfolio concentrated in Defense/IT locations. As of March 31, 2026 it held 201 Defense/IT operating properties (23.2M sq ft), 6 non-core "Other" office properties (2.0M sq ft), roughly 980 acres of controlled developable land (~10.8M sq ft of future capacity), and seven properties under development (~1.0M sq ft). The Defense/IT portfolio represents 92.1% of square footage; the "Other" legacy office segment (9.7% of ARR, only ~76–80% occupied) is explicitly non-strategic and slated for disposition.

Sub-segment ARR concentrations: Fort Meade/BW Corridor (44.5%, the crown jewel, serving NSA/Cyber Command), NoVA Defense/IT (13.7%), Lackland AFB/San Antonio (10.1%), Redstone Arsenal/Huntsville (8.8%), Data Center Shells (8.4% and rising), and Navy Support (4.8%).

The competitive moat is structural rather than reputational. Tenants often cannot relocate away from the demand drivers; security build-outs (SCIFs, ATFP hardening, access control) are heavily tenant-funded, creating high switching costs; more than one-third of COPT's ~430 employees carry government clearances; and mission requirements force on-site work, insulating the portfolio from the remote-work trend. COPT typically owns most of the properties in specific defense-oriented business parks, and its ~1,000 acres of adjacent land bank create a supply barrier competitors cannot easily replicate.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 net income was $159.5M (+10.8% from $143.9M), diluted EPS $1.34 vs $1.23. The metric that matters for a REIT, diluted FFO per share as adjusted, grew 5.8% to $2.72 from $2.57. NOI from real estate operations rose 6.4% to $445.6M, driven by +$14.9M of Same-Property NOI and +$11M from development and acquisitions. Total ARR grew to $728.1M from $686.8M in 2024 and $646.7M in 2023, a steady mid-single-digit progression. Year-end occupancy was 94.0% total, 95.5% Defense/IT.

Q1 2026 momentum was solid: real estate revenue $194.6M (+9.6%), net income $40.1M (+10.8%), diluted FFO/share $0.69 vs $0.65 (+6.2%). Operating cash flow rose 34% to $96.4M.

The capital structure is the standout strength. Total debt is roughly $2.55B after the March 2026 repayment of the $400M 2.25% Notes; debt-to-equity around 2.06x is normal for a REIT. The weighted-average interest rate is approximately 3.4%, locked in on fixed-rate unsecured notes (coupons of 2.00%, 2.75%, 2.90%, 4.50%, 5.25%) with a well-laddered schedule and no significant maturities until $345M in 2028, the 2026 bond having been pre-funded a full six months early via an October 2025 issuance of $400M 4.50% Notes due 2030. Only ~5% of debt is property-encumbered, and variable-rate exposure is minimal after the February 2026 swap expiries. The ~44.6% FFO payout ratio leaves ample retained cash to fund the equity portion of development internally. All three agencies rate the company investment-grade with stable outlooks; the company has revolver capacity plus a development facility and an untapped $300M ATM program that provide liquidity flexibility. In a higher-for-longer rate environment that directly compresses financing-heavy, negative-FCF regulated utilities and REITs, COPT's near-term refinancing insulation is a material advantage.

Two data cautions: the XBRL "revenue" field of $42M captured only ancillary construction/service revenue, not the $763.9M total (real estate revenue $721.8M), and the Altman Z-Score of 0.76 is a false distress signal from applying a manufacturing model to a leveraged, asset-heavy REIT. Both should be disregarded.

## Growth Analysis

External growth is development-led and self-funded. In 2025 COPT delivered four new pre-leased developments plus a $40M NoVA acquisition; in April 2026 it added ~17 acres in Chantilly, VA (two fully-leased operating properties) for ~$43M, deepening its position in the supply-constrained Northern Virginia market where it is the dominant defense-adjacent landlord. Data-center shells are a rising growth and capital-recycling engine, with a track record of monetizing shells through JVs at attractive margins.

The demand backdrop is favorable. Management cites the FY2026 defense appropriations increase (approved February 2026) and additional funds in the July 2025 legislation as demand drivers for priority missions (ISR, cyber, missile defense, space, naval/UAV) that its tenants support, consistent with the broadly confirmed U.S. multi-front defense demand structure. The Northern Virginia data-center market, the world's largest, is being reinforced by the AI-capex cycle, and COPT's shell development participates as a picks-and-shovels beneficiary.

That said, growth here is measured mid-single digits, not accelerating. FFO/share growth of 5.8% (FY25) and 6.2% (Q1 26) is durable but does not clear the +30% estimate-revision or +40% revenue-growth inflection thresholds that would warrant cycle-adjusted re-rating treatment. Consensus estimate revisions have in fact drifted modestly negative: current-year EPS -2.8% and next-quarter -2.9% over 90 days, with 30-day trends flat. The earnings track record is in-line rather than accelerating: one beat, two in-line, one miss over the last four quarters, with surprises of ±2%. The trajectory supports steady compounding, not an upside inflection, which is why we hold Growth Prospects at 6.0, consistent with the prior read.

## Valuation Assessment

CDP trades at $37.48, near its 52-week high of $38.06 and up ~34% over twelve months. On trailing/forward EPS of ~$1.34/$1.33 the P/E is ~28x, but the relevant REIT metric is FFO: at ~$2.72 FY25 FFO (and next-year consensus implying modestly higher), the stock sits around 13.5–13.8x forward FFO. Against the Office REIT peer set the picture is mixed: several peers (CUZ, SLG, DEI) carry negative or distorted GAAP P/Es reflecting genuine office-sector stress, and the peer median P/E of 36.2x is not a clean comparable. The more telling relative metric is yield: CDP's 3.4% dividend yield is well below the peer median of 5.6% (KRC 5.6%, HIW 6.6%, DEI 6.1%, SLG 4.8%, CUZ 4.4%). The market is correctly awarding COPT a quality premium for its defense-driven demand insulation, but that premium is now substantial.

The valuation discipline here matters. The prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down; the re-rating was earned through FFO growth and occupancy gains. But the current price already reflects the quality story: it stands ~1% above the mean analyst target of $37.00 and ~3% above the median of $36.50, with the high target at $42.00 and low at $34.00. On a name growing FFO at ~6% with stable-to-slightly-negative estimate revisions and a compressed yield, the analyst-targets-as-floors principle does not apply: this is a fully-valued quality compounder where consensus targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects that the price offers limited asymmetric upside from here, not a value-trap dynamic. There is no identifiable catalyst for near-term multiple expansion, and the yield support that anchors many REITs is diluted at 3.4%.

## Competitive Landscape

COPT's competitive position is strong and stable, arguably the best in the office subsector on a trajectory basis. Its structural advantages, incumbency in specific defense business parks, tenant-funded security infrastructure that raises switching costs, a credentialed workforce, and a ~1,000-acre adjacent land bank, are difficult for competitors to replicate. Nine consecutive years of ≥93% Defense/IT occupancy and 90.8% Q1 retention evidence the durability. The company sidesteps the two forces crushing traditional office (remote work, secular demand decline) because security mandates on-site work.

Competition exists for land/acquisitions (from better-capitalized REITs and private equity) and for cleared talent, and the data-center shell growth engine depends on continued development allocations from a single anchor cloud customer. The prior report's competitive-position score stepped down from 8.0 to 7.0 over the trajectory; we place it at 7.5, reflecting the stable moat while acknowledging that the incremental growth vectors (data-center shells, NoVA land) carry concentration and allocation dependency rather than a broadening of the franchise. The competitive edge is defensible but not visibly widening.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is government/tenant concentration. The U.S. Government is the single largest tenant at 35.4% of ARR; the top three tenants total 51.1% and the top ten 64.4%. Most USG leases are one-year terms with renewal options and can be terminated if Congress fails to fund. The most important near-term watch item is the 2026 lease-expiration wall: 2.9M sq ft (19.3% of ARR) expires this year, and 83.3% of those expirations are USG leases. The 2025 federal shutdown already delayed some renewal leasing (though rent continued and leases stayed in effect). Management expects "virtually all" to renew, and 90.8% Q1 retention supports that, but this is a genuine binary the market is watching into 2H 2026. This is the single largest source of downside surprise not fully de-risked in the current price.

Secondary risks: the data-center shell portfolio (33 shells, 6.3M sq ft, 11.3% of ARR) is leased to a single unnamed "Fortune 100 Company" cloud-computing customer, a concentration and a growth-allocation dependency. The legacy "Other" office segment is difficult to sell in a weak office capital market and carries impairment risk until disposed. Maryland climate legislation could impose future energy-performance penalties and non-recoverable capex on roughly half the portfolio. The Mid-Atlantic geographic concentration exposes the company to regional federal-spending and workforce cuts, though the defense niche has insulated it to date.

Risk factors score 5.5: the concentration risks are real and specific, and the 2026 renewal wall is a dated, material event, but most of these are known and largely priced, with the balance sheet providing a strong downside buffer.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 40.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 18.5%, rich by 22 points, with the term structure in backwardation (40.4% near-term vs 31.7% at six months), consistent with the upcoming earnings print and the 2026 renewal newsflow. Put/call skew of 11.4% at the front month indicates elevated near-term put demand, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.4%. IV has compressed sharply from 64.8% (early June) to 40.4% as the stock rose 18.6%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.33 to 0.14, so hedging has come off as the stock advanced. The elevated near-term put skew is a modest divergence against a constructive fundamental view, and it argues against adding at the high end of the range immediately ahead of the earnings/renewal catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42–45).** The 2026 lease wall renews smoothly at 90%+ retention, occupancy pushes toward 96% Defense/IT, defense appropriations continue to expand tenant demand, and data-center shell monetizations add capital-recycling gains. FFO/share compounds toward ~$2.90+ in FY26 and continues mid-single-digit growth, the market extends the quality premium, and the yield stays compressed as the multiple holds ~14.5–15x forward FFO. This roughly matches the analyst high target of $42.

**Bear Case ($29–31).** A prolonged federal shutdown or defense-budget disruption delays or shrinks the 2026 renewals, the Fortune 100 data-center customer curtails future development allocations, the "Other" segment takes an impairment, and a higher-for-longer rate environment compresses REIT multiples across the board. The yield re-rates toward the peer median (5%+), implying a price in the low-$30s to high-$20s. This corresponds to roughly the analyst low target of $34 in a milder version and lower if renewals disappoint materially, a ~17–23% drawdown from current levels.

**Base Case ($37–39).** Renewals proceed largely as management expects, FFO grows ~5–6%, the dividend is comfortably covered and grows modestly, and the stock trades in line with slowly rising FFO at a stable premium multiple. Total return is driven by the ~3.4% yield plus mid-single-digit FFO growth, net of a multiple that has little room to expand from here. This is where the balance of evidence points.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth_hold)**. This is a durable mid-single-digit FFO compounder held for its combination of defense-driven demand insulation and best-in-class balance sheet, but the re-rating that drove the prior BUY calls is largely complete, and the current price sits at/above consensus targets with limited near-term upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed pullback toward the low-$30s (a yield closer to 4%+ and forward FFO multiple compressing toward ~12x), which would restore asymmetric upside.
- 2026 lease-wall renewals confirmed at ≥90% retention through 2H 2026 alongside a return to positive estimate-revision velocity, which would validate durable acceleration.
- A material new data-center shell allocation or JV monetization that broadens the growth engine beyond a single anchor customer.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A prolonged federal shutdown or defense-budget contraction that visibly disrupts the 2026 renewals (retention falling below ~85%).
- Loss or material reduction of the Fortune 100 data-center customer relationship.
- A meaningful impairment on the "Other" segment or a dividend-coverage deterioration (FFO payout rising materially above the current ~45%).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026), with the 2026 lease-renewal cadence the primary data point to monitor through the second half of the year. The near-term options-implied ±9.4% move flags the upcoming print as a volatility event.

## Conclusion

COPT Defense Properties is a differentiated business structured as a REIT: a defense-infrastructure landlord whose demand is driven by the U.S. defense budget far more than the commercial real estate cycle. The fundamentals are healthy and the balance sheet is among the best in the subsector: ~3.4% cost of debt, no maturities until 2028, a ~44.6% FFO payout, and investment-grade ratings across all three agencies. The trajectory data shows the score stepping down (6.5 → 6.9 → 6.2) and the recommendation moving from BUY to HOLD as the ~34% re-rating pushed the stock near its 52-week high and above consensus targets. New data confirms rather than reverses that shift: fundamentals remain solid, but the price now fully reflects the quality, the yield has compressed to 3.4% versus a peer median of 5.6%, estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative, and the 2026 lease-expiration wall is an unresolved near-term swing factor. We rate this a stock to hold for steady FFO compounding and to add on a pullback toward the low-$30s rather than chase at $37.48. We maintain HOLD.
