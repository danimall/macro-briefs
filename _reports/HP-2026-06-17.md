**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Helmerich & Payne has spent the last 18 months remaking itself from the dominant U.S. land driller into a globally diversified drilling-solutions company, financed by the ~$2.0 billion all-cash acquisition of KCA Deutag (closed January 16, 2025). The transformation has produced a balance sheet roughly four times more levered than before and a swing from a $344M net profit in FY2024 to a $164M net loss in FY2025, extended by a further $155M loss in the first half of FY2026. Most of the loss is non-cash — $194M of impairments in FY25 (including a $192M goodwill writedown of the acquired units taken within roughly six months of closing) and another $129M of fleet-scrap impairments in H1 FY26 — but the underlying operating picture is mixed: the North America cash engine is contracting while the new International Solutions segment is losing roughly $100M a quarter, largely on suspended Saudi Aramco rigs.

The shares have nonetheless more than doubled off their June 2025 low of $15.08, reaching a 52-week high of $41.82 in late April–May 2026 before settling near $36.36. The stock made those fresh highs *before* the May 6 Q2 print, which missed adjusted-EPS consensus by an enormous margin (-$0.38 vs -$0.06); the stock fell from $39.83 to $36.90 the following session. Forward estimate revisions corroborate a recovery read: next-year consensus EPS has risen +36.2% over 90 days to $1.38, and next-quarter estimates are up +29.9%. Analyst positioning is net-bullish (mean target $41.2, +15% from current; seven buy/strong-buy ratings versus one sell; sentiment classified as improving).

The evidence here is genuinely two-sided, and neither an outright negative nor a positive call is supportable. The deal-thesis risk, contract-quality downgrade, and a striking cluster of executive departures during the loss period are real and unresolved. But the company is deleveraging rather than distressing: the acquisition term loan was fully retired in April 2026, the $950M revolver is undrawn, operating cash flow remains solidly positive at $219M for H1 FY26, and capex is being cut. The terminal-decline case required to make this uninvestable is not established; the recovery case is not yet proven. We rate HP a HOLD with an uncertain rationale and set the reassessment point at the early-August Q3 print.

## Company Overview

H&P is a Tulsa-based drilling-solutions and technology company, historically the leading U.S. land driller through its FlexRig fleet and an integrated model that bundles proprietary rig hardware, automation software, and digital services. The KCA Deutag acquisition reorganized the company into three reporting segments plus "Other":

- **North America Solutions** (203 available rigs at 3/31/26): the legacy core, concentrated in the Permian/Texas onshore market. Still the profit engine, contributing roughly $148M of segment operating income in H1 FY26.
- **International Solutions** (130 rigs): Middle East (Saudi Arabia, Oman), Latin America (Argentina, Colombia), Europe, Africa, Australia. Currently loss-making at roughly -$155M segment operating loss for H1 FY26.
- **Offshore Solutions** (4 platform rigs): primarily asset-light management/O&M contracts where the customer owns the rig — North Sea, Norwegian Sea, Caspian, Gulf of America. Profitable and growing, up about 45% in segment operating income H1 FY26.
- **Other / BENTEC**: manufacturing/engineering and captive insurance subsidiaries.

Total fleet stands at 337 rigs, down from 367 at fiscal year-end after a deliberate fleet-rationalization scrapping program. The KCAD deal lifted international revenue mix from roughly 28% to roughly 41% of the total. The strategic logic is to diversify away from a maturing, capital-disciplined U.S. shale market toward longer-cycle, national-oil-company-driven international and offshore work.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $3.68B, up 34% from $2.75B, driven entirely by roughly $1.0B of acquired KCA Deutag revenue (258 days of consolidation). Organic North America revenue actually declined 3.4%. The company posted a FY2025 net loss of -$163.7M (-$1.66/share) versus +$344M (+$3.43) the prior year. The loss extended into FY26: H1 net loss of -$155.3M (-$1.57/share), with Q2 revenue of $932M (-8.2% YoY) and an adjusted loss of -$0.38 against a -$0.06 consensus.

**Drivers of the losses.** The bulk is non-cash. FY25 included $194M of impairments — mostly the $192M goodwill writedown of the International Solutions and BENTEC units in Q3 FY25, explicitly triggered by the sustained decline in H&P's own share price and market capitalization — plus $54.7M of acquisition transaction costs, interest expense rising to $108M (from $29M), and roughly $22M of investment losses. H1 FY26 added $129M of impairments from scrapping 30 North America rigs, 3 offshore rigs, and writing international rigs down to scrap value, plus a $23.3M single-rig writedown.

**Cash flow quality.** The distinction between accounting losses and cash matters here. Operating cash flow was $543M in FY25 and $219M in H1 FY26, consistently exceeding net income because the losses are largely non-cash impairments. FCF was a thin +$117M in FY25; FY26 capex is guided down to $280–320M from $426M, which should support free cash flow. The company also monetized non-core assets, selling the Utica Square shopping center for $129M after quarter-end.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt jumped to roughly $2.06B at 9/30/25 from $545M in FY23 — the cost of an all-cash deal. The debt is well-laddered senior notes ($350M due 2027, $350M 2029, $550M 2031 at 2.90%, $550M 2034 at 5.50%) plus Oman secured facilities. The $400M acquisition term loan was paid down aggressively and fully retired in April 2026. D/E is about 2.37 and the current ratio is 1.8. The Altman Z-Score of 1.51 nominally places H&P in the "distress zone," but that figure is distorted by impairment-driven near-zero EBIT, not a liquidity problem — the company remains covenant-compliant with $950M undrawn on its revolver and positive operating cash flow. This is a leverage-and-earnings-quality issue, not an imminent solvency one.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was cut from $1.68/share (FY24) to $1.00/share (FY25) — the supplemental dividend was suspended to fund the deal, leaving a $0.25 quarterly base. Buybacks are paused. The current yield is about 2.7%.

Financial Health scores a 4.5: the leverage and the swing to a GAAP loss are real and the dividend was cut, but the resilient operating cash flow, fully-retired term loan, undrawn revolver, and disciplined capex reduction keep this from being a distress score.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated and at a cyclical trough. North America — the profit engine — is shrinking: revenue days, active rig count, and pricing all declined YoY, and management is scrapping ~30 NA rigs in a move that reads as structural rather than purely cyclical capacity reduction. U.S. E&P consolidation has concentrated buying power among large operators that extract pricing concessions, a headwind that is visible directly in H&P's declining domestic revenue.

Against that, the forward estimate revisions are the most important growth signal in the file and cut clearly positive. Next-year consensus EPS has risen to $1.38, up +20.4% over 30 days and +36.2% over 90 days. Next-quarter EPS is up +14.5% over 30 days and +29.9% over 90 days. On a cyclical-trough driller, out-year revisions of this magnitude are a recovery signal — the Street is marking up earnings power as Saudi rig resumptions, fleet right-sizing savings ($50M of targeted G&A), and integration synergies flow through. Current-year estimates have been cut hard (the -119% revision to a -$0.06 loss reflects the Q2 miss and impairments), so the recovery is back-end loaded into FY27.

I weight the positive out-year revisions, but I do not treat them as a clean BUY signal for two reasons. First, the company has missed three of its last four quarters by wide margins (Q2 by -1676%, Q1 by -246%, Q4 by -104%), which undercuts confidence that consensus is anchored to anything firm. Second, the recovery depends on Saudi rig resumptions and KCAD integration delivering — neither is proven, and the contracts underpinning the international rebound are structurally weak (see Risk Assessment). Growth Prospects scores a 5.0: real recovery optionality, real execution uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-9.6x) because the company is loss-making, so headline multiples are uninformative. The relevant frame is normalized/forward earnings power. On the rising next-year consensus of $1.38, the stock trades at roughly 26x — not cheap for a cyclical driller, and a level that already embeds a meaningful recovery. The peer set is heterogeneous: Patterson-UTI shows a negative P/E (also loss-making), Valaris trades at 6.3x, and Noble at 32.3x, giving a peer median of 32.3x that is not particularly useful given the dispersion and differing business mixes.

The asymmetry here is modest. The analyst mean target of $41.2 implies +15% upside; the high target of $47 implies +29%; the low of $30 implies -17%. That is roughly balanced-to-slightly-positive reward:risk, consistent with a stock that has already re-rated more than 2x off its low and now sits near the middle of the analyst target range. A June 2026 Seeking Alpha piece argued the valuation had become "rich" — a defensible view given that 26x forward earnings on a contracting North America base and an unproven international turnaround leaves little margin for further disappointment.

The prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark valuation down — the move off the low was driven by oil-price support and the recovery thesis, not multiple-only speculation. But at 26x forward earnings the stock no longer offers a compelling discount to its own improving estimates, and the recovery it requires is not yet in the numbers. Valuation scores a 5.0: neither a clear mispricing to exploit nor a stretched multiple to fade.

## Competitive Landscape

H&P remains the leader in high-spec AC land rigs, where demand is structurally favored as drilling bifurcates toward technology-enabled, automated rigs and away from the oversupplied low-spec end. That moat in the legacy North America business is intact but is being squeezed by a maturing, fiscally-disciplined shale market and by operator consolidation that compresses dayrates.

The KCAD acquisition was meant to diversify the company toward longer-cycle international and offshore work. The strategic rationale is sound, but execution to date has been poor: the acquired units impaired within six months, International Solutions is losing roughly $100M a quarter, and the acquired contract book is structurally weaker than H&P's legacy daywork contracts. The Offshore segment — primarily asset-light O&M — is the genuine bright spot, growing operating income about 45% and winning a 5-year bp Azerbaijan O&M renewal. Competitive Position scores a 5.0: a strong legacy franchise, but the trajectory is mixed, with the new international platform unproven and the core domestic market contracting.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Deal-thesis and integration risk.** Writing off goodwill within ~6 months of closing — explicitly because H&P's own market cap fell — is a strong signal the market is skeptical the deal created the value paid. A material weakness in internal control over financial reporting tied to KCAD integration was disclosed (reported remediated by Q2 FY26).

2. **Saudi Arabia contract quality.** Saudi Aramco suspended 27 legacy KCA Deutag rigs through 9/30/25, with only 7 resumption notices received. Many acquired KCAD contracts allow suspension or early termination with no standby or termination payment — materially worse than H&P's legacy book. This is the single biggest swing factor behind the International segment loss, and it makes the $8.3B headline backlog (only ~13% realizable in the rest of FY26) less firm than it appears.

3. **North America structural softness.** The cash engine is shrinking on lower revenue days, active rigs, and pricing, with ~30 rigs being scrapped.

4. **Leverage and refinancing.** Interest expense is now a structural ~$100M+ annual headwind. The 2.90% 2031 notes will eventually refinance at materially higher rates, and a credit-rating downgrade would compound that.

5. **Governance and leadership turnover.** Both the CEO (Lindsay, transition agreement 2/26/26) and CFO (Vann, transition agreement 3/12/26) signed retirement agreements during the loss period, alongside a cluster of other officer departures from September 2025 through March 2026. The new CEO (Adams) is an internal promotion. This cadence of executive change during a downturn warrants close monitoring.

6. **Geopolitical/FX exposure.** Greatly expanded into the Middle East, Africa, Europe, and Latin America. Argentina currency controls, a long-running Venezuela expropriation claim, and Strait of Hormuz transit risk all increased post-deal. Oil-price tailwinds from Middle East tension cut both ways for a driller with significant regional exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The current options snapshot shows near-term ATM IV of 91.3% (and a delta snapshot reading 109%) against 1-year historical volatility of 43.6%, with the term structure in steep backwardation. This is an artifact: the 91.3% reading comes from a 1-day-to-expiry contract (expiry 2026-06-18 in a 2026-06-17 snapshot), where near-zero-DTE gamma mechanically inflates implied vol. There is no scheduled catalyst inside that window — the next earnings report is in early August. The 1-month and 3-month tenors price IV at a far more normal 45–47%, and the put/call skew is balanced (-0.2%), with a low put/call volume ratio of 0.42 and OI ratio of 0.28. The options market is not pricing imminent, event-driven downside; the spike is a snapshot-timing effect that should not be read as a directional signal.

Short interest is elevated at 11.7% of float with 6.8 days to cover, indicating genuine institutional skepticism, but not at squeeze-risk levels.

Risk Factors scores a 4.5: the contract-quality downgrade, governance turnover, and deal-impairment red flags are specific and not yet resolved, though the deleveraging balance sheet and the fact that the market has already absorbed the Q2 miss temper the downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** H&P sits at a cyclical earnings trough with rising out-year estimates. Saudi rig resumptions accelerate (targeting ~23 operating rigs there by mid-2026), fleet right-sizing delivers G&A savings, and integration synergies flow through. The non-cash impairments wash out of GAAP earnings, operating cash flow stays robust, the deleveraging continues, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $47 (+29%) as the international segment turns profitable. Offshore O&M growth and the asset-light model provide a steady, under-appreciated earnings stream.

**Bear case.** The KCAD deal proves to have been overpriced; further impairments follow; Saudi suspensions persist and the no-termination-payment contracts deliver continued international losses; North America keeps contracting structurally; and an oil-price reversal removes the cyclical tailwind. Earnings recovery slips into FY27 and beyond, and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $30 (-17%) or lower.

The base case sits between these: the company muddles through a slow recovery, deleveraging steadily while the international turnaround proves slower than consensus hopes. That maps to roughly the current price-to-$41 range, which is why the reward:risk is close to balanced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the evidence is genuinely two-sided and neither a positive nor a negative call is supportable on current data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 FY26 (early August) shows International Solutions narrowing its operating loss materially, with Saudi rig count progressing toward the ~23 target and resumption notices accelerating.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive earnings surprises, restoring confidence that consensus is anchored — the current 3-of-4-miss record is a real impediment.
- Forward next-year EPS sustains or extends above $1.40 while the stock trades below ~22x that figure (i.e., a pullback toward the low-$30s that restores asymmetry on improving estimates).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further goodwill or fleet impairment, signaling the KCAD deal continues to destroy value.
- Saudi contract terminations (not just suspensions) under the no-payment provisions, or a broadening of international losses.
- A credit-rating downgrade or deterioration in operating cash flow below the level needed to cover capex and the dividend.
- Evidence the North America contraction is accelerating structurally rather than cyclically.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY26 earnings report in early August is the decision point. That print should clarify whether the Saudi resumptions and synergy targets are materializing, and whether the out-year estimate revisions are validated by operating progress or unwound.

## Conclusion

Helmerich & Payne is a cyclical-trough land driller that made a large, debt-funded bet on international diversification which has not yet paid off — the acquired units impaired almost immediately, the international segment is losing money on suspended Saudi rigs with weak contract terms, the domestic cash engine is contracting, and both the CEO and CFO retired during the loss period with no insider buying to signal conviction. Those are real, specific concerns that prevent a positive rating.

But the offsetting evidence is equally specific and prevents a negative one. The balance sheet is actively deleveraging — the acquisition term loan was fully retired, the revolver is undrawn, operating cash flow remains solidly positive, and capex is being cut. Forward estimates are rising sharply, the Street is net-bullish with a mean target above the current price, and the market reaction to the Q2 miss was a single-session decline rather than a sustained collapse. The options "imminent stress" reading is a 1-day-to-expiry snapshot artifact, not a catalyst signal, and there is no scheduled event before the August earnings report. The terminal-decline thesis that would justify an active negative call is not established, and the recovery thesis is not yet proven.

We rate HP a **HOLD (uncertain)**. The right posture is to wait for the Q3 print to adjudicate between the deleveraging-plus-recovery read and the deal-impairment read, rather than to commit capital to either side of a balanced setup.
