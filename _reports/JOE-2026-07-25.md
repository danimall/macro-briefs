**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The St. Joe Company is a Northwest Florida land-monetization and development platform that converts a large, low-cost-basis coastal land bank into homesites, hotels, clubs, commercial leasing, and multi-family communities. The defining strategic development of the past several years is the deliberate shift away from lumpy, one-time land sales toward recurring revenue: hospitality (Watersound Club memberships, 12 hotels, golf, marinas) and commercial leasing now supply a growing, higher-durability earnings base. That pivot is working. Q1 2026 hospitality revenue hit a record $44.7M (+12.9% YoY) with gross margin expanding from 18.2% to 24.4%, and total Q1 revenue reached $99.0M, the highest first quarter in the company's history excluding the 2014 timberland sale.

The investment case is two-sided. On the positive side, the land is carried at historical cost net of depletion (~$1.0B) and almost certainly worth materially more than book; the debt is structured better than almost any real estate peer (weighted-average rate 4.7%, 82.7% fixed/swapped, ~19.7-year average remaining maturity); operating cash flow is robust ($42.2M in Q1'26); and net in-migration into the Florida Panhandle continues to override national housing headwinds. On the negative side, the stock trades at roughly 32x trailing earnings, quarterly earnings are volatile and JV-dependent (Q1'26 net income fell to $13.9M from $17.5M purely on a swing in Latitude Margaritaville home closings, 83 vs. 192), and the largest shareholder, Bruce Berkowitz's Fairholme, has been selling continuously for over a year, creating a persistent technical supply that caps upside.

We rate JOE a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.05, consistent with the improving trajectory across recent reviews (6.2 → 6.4 → 6.05). The fundamental engine is intact and compounding. What is missing is a valuation entry point or a near-term catalyst that would justify a BUY, and the risk profile is defined by concentration and a known seller rather than by fundamental deterioration.

## Company Overview

St. Joe owns and develops a contiguous land position concentrated entirely in Northwest Florida (roughly 87% in Bay, Gulf, and Walton counties, and about 90% within 15 miles of the Gulf coast). Much of this land was acquired decades ago as timberland at very low cost basis, and the business model is to entitle and develop that land into higher-value uses while retaining an operating interest in what gets built.

The company reports three segments:

- **Residential (28.6% of Q1'26 revenue):** Master-planned communities and homesite sales, primarily to ~18 homebuilder relationships plus limited retail. Includes the flagship 55+ Latitude Margaritaville Watersound JV (50/50 with Minto; ~3,700 homes planned, 2,273 sold to date). Homesite gross margins run ~45–46%.
- **Hospitality (49.5% of Q1'26 revenue):** The recurring-revenue growth engine: Watersound Club (3,647 members), 12 hotels (~1,298 rooms), golf, beach clubs, marinas, and F&B. Record Q1 revenue and material margin expansion.
- **Commercial (17.6% of Q1'26 revenue):** ~1.2M sq ft of leased space (96% leased), 1,064 multi-family units (95% leased), self-storage, timber, and land sales, plus an FSU/TMH medical campus development.

The competitive structure is unusual: rather than competing head-to-head with national homebuilders, St. Joe sells them land, capturing appreciation plus a residual percentage of completed home value above thresholds. This is lower-risk and higher-margin than vertical homebuilding, and it lets the company participate in the housing cycle without carrying construction and warranty risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet (as of 3/31/26).** Total assets $1.52B, total equity $774M, net debt ~$380M. Cash of $136M plus a $200M time deposit held in an SPE that offsets the $180M Timber Note obligation. Investment in real estate carried at ~$1.0B on a historical-cost, net-of-depletion basis.

The debt structure is the standout financial strength and is materially better than nearly any real estate peer in the current environment. Weighted-average effective rate of 4.7%, 82.7% fixed or swapped, and an average remaining maturity of roughly 19.7 years, much of it long-dated, HUD-insured multi-family financing at 2.7–5.9%. Near-term maturities are modest ($64M in 2027, $37M in 2028). This insulates St. Joe from the refinancing pressure squeezing most real estate companies as rates stay higher-for-longer: the company simply does not have a wall to refinance at today's rates. The Altman Z-Score sits in the grey zone (~1.9), but that reflects the asset-heavy, land-carrying balance sheet the model penalizes, not genuine distress. Given a 4.7% blended cost of debt over a ~20-year average life against $774M of equity and a conservatively-carried land bank, the "distress" reading is a scoring artifact.

**Revenue and earnings trend.** On the proxy's company-selected total revenue measure: $267M (2021) → $252M (2022) → $389M (2023) → $403M (2024) → $513M (2025). Net income $74.5M (2021) → $115.6M (2025), a record. (Note: consolidated GAAP revenue in the filings differs from the proxy's company-selected measure, which appears to gross up JV and other items; directionally the growth trend is strong and accelerating either way.)

**Q1 2026 results.** Operating income $18.2M (+7.7%). Equity income from unconsolidated JVs dropped from $10.2M to $3.5M, driving the net income decline. Core consolidated operations improved, and operating cash flow was robust at $42.2M (up from $29.0M). This is the recurring pattern investors must internalize: headline net income can decline while the underlying wholly-owned business strengthens, because JV home-closing cadence is timing-driven and disconnected from operational health.

**Capital allocation.** Quarterly dividend raised to $0.16 (from $0.14); $54.9M remaining buyback authorization; ROI-gated development spending with Q1'26 capex of $20.7M. The dividend yields only ~1.0%, so this is not an income name; the yield is well-covered but incidental to the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

The forward pipeline supports continued top-line growth. Homesites under contract surged to 3,204 as of 3/31/26 from 952 a year earlier. Roughly 1,326 of those are in the long-dated, variable-revenue Pigeon Creek project, so the cleaner read is the ~1,231 homesites under contract excluding Pigeon Creek/SouthWood, representing ~$119.9M of future revenue, a healthy visible pipeline against a company generating roughly $400–500M in annual revenue.

The more durable growth story is the recurring-revenue mix shift. Watersound Club membership grew +149 YoY to 3,647, and commercial leasing is 96% leased with multi-family 95% leased. As memberships, hotel rooms, and leased square footage scale, the earnings base becomes less dependent on lumpy homesite sales and JV closings, which should over time compress the quarter-to-quarter volatility that currently characterizes reported results.

The demand driver is net in-migration into Northwest Florida, which management cites repeatedly as the offset to national affordability, rate, and insurance headwinds. This micro-market has bucked the national housing slowdown via cash buyers and quality-of-life appeal. The growth is genuine, but it is single-region and single-demographic-trend dependent, which is why we score Growth Prospects a solid but not exceptional 6.2: the momentum is real, the durability is good, but the concentration limits the ceiling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.12, JOE trades at ~32x trailing earnings (EPS $1.99), against a $3.57B market cap. On its face this is a full multiple, but trailing P/E is a poor primary metric for St. Joe because a substantial fraction of intrinsic value sits in land carried at historical cost that generates no current earnings, and because JV timing distorts reported net income.

The peer table is of limited use here. The listed Real Estate comparables (HIW at 36.2x, NSA at 60.7x, SLG at -20.4x, CURB at 99.7x, APLE at 22.3x; peer median 60.7x P/E, 5.0% yield) are traditional REITs with fundamentally different economics: externally-driven cap-rate valuation, high payout ratios, and no comparable embedded land-appreciation optionality. On a headline P/E basis JOE is cheaper than the peer median, but that comparison is not meaningful because the businesses are not comparable. St. Joe is effectively a land-bank compounder with an operating overlay, not a yield vehicle.

The genuine bull argument on valuation is asset value: the 23,653-homesite entitled pipeline, 1.2M+ sq ft of commercial, extensive undeveloped entitlements, and timberland acquired decades ago all sit at conservative historical cost. The market value of the land bank very plausibly exceeds book by a wide margin, which is the core NAV thesis. However, this value is illiquid, unrealized, and monetized over years; it does not translate into a near-term re-rating catalyst, and the market has long known about it.

We score Valuation 4.8. The multiple is not egregious given the asset backing and the recurring-revenue trajectory, but there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to close a NAV gap, and the Berkowitz supply overhang argues the market will keep the multiple range-bound. Consistent with the broader pattern that a merely "reasonable" multiple on a slow-monetization asset story is not, by itself, a reason to expect outperformance, we do not treat cheapness relative to unrealizable NAV as a buy signal. This is a fair-value situation, not an asymmetric-upside one.

## Competitive Landscape

St. Joe has no direct comparable. It is the dominant private landowner and developer in a specific, fast-growing coastal region, with an irreplaceable, contiguous coastal land position that cannot be replicated at anything close to its cost basis. The moat is structural: scarce coastal land, entitlements accumulated over decades, and a self-funding development model that captures land appreciation plus homesite residuals rather than bearing homebuilding risk directly.

The competitive trajectory is improving-to-stable rather than deteriorating. The deliberate build-out of recurring hospitality and leasing revenue is widening the moat by adding durable, sticky cash flows (memberships, leases) on top of the land bank. Membership growth, 96% commercial occupancy, and margin expansion all point to a strengthening operating franchise. We score Competitive Position 7.5, a slight step down from the prior 8.0: the moat remains genuinely wide, but the entire franchise's fortunes are tied to one region's in-migration trend, which is a structural ceiling on how high the competitive-position score can go, and the score should reflect trajectory and durability rather than a maximum reading for an inherently concentrated bet.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic concentration.** 100% of assets are in Northwest Florida. Hurricane and severe-weather exposure is acute; a major storm could materially alter demand, pricing, and property values. The company self-insures certain property and timber risk, which amplifies event risk.

**Single-driver demand dependence.** The entire thesis rests on continued net migration into the Florida Panhandle. A reversal, driven by insurance-cost inflation, a national recession, or a Florida-specific shock, would hit residential and hospitality simultaneously.

**Lumpy, JV-driven earnings.** Homebuilder purchases are sporadic; hospitality is seasonal (Q2/Q3-heavy). The Latitude Margaritaville JV drove the entire Q1'26 net income decline via a swing in home-closing cadence. Investors must track JV closing pace as a distinct quarterly variable disconnected from wholly-owned business health.

**JV guarantee exposure.** Maximum exposure to loss on unconsolidated JVs was $127.4M (carrying values plus guarantees), including Watersound Fountains ($41M), Pier Park TPS, and the Latitude completion guaranty.

**2029 cash event.** The $45.6M deferred tax liability from the 2014 timberland sale comes due in 2029, alongside the Timber Note maturity and Senior Notes repayment. This is fully disclosed and largely funded by the $200M time deposit (which covers the $180M notes, leaving ~$20M toward the tax bill), so it is manageable but a discrete future outflow to model.

**The Berkowitz/Fairholme overhang, the dominant non-fundamental factor.** Bruce Berkowitz, via Fairholme, is the ~10%+ largest shareholder and has been selling near-continuously from August 2025 through June 2026: over 1.5 million shares at prices from ~$50 up to ~$73 and back to ~$65, with holdings declining from ~16.5M to ~15.1M shares. JOE still represents ~80% of Fairholme's $1.51B portfolio, so the selling reads as concentration-reduction / risk management (possibly redemption-driven) rather than a fundamental view on intrinsic value. It is a persistent supply of stock that likely caps upside until it clears, and it is the single most important swing factor for near-term price performance.

We score Risk Factors 5.3. The debt structure genuinely de-risks the balance sheet, but the combination of extreme geographic/weather concentration, single-driver demand dependence, JV earnings volatility, and a large, persistent seller keeps this below a neutral reading.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.2% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 30.3% (a 10.9-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.2% near-term vs. 30.8% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the upcoming quarterly print given the JV-timing volatility that has whipsawed reported earnings. ATM IV rose from 32.2% to 41.2% since late May while the price fell modestly (~2.7%), consistent with hedging into a known event rather than directional conviction. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4% near-term), and the 3m/6m tenors show negative skew (-5.2% / -7.3%), i.e., relatively more call demand further out, not the pattern of aggressive downside positioning. Short interest is low at 3.5% of float (2.8 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The net read: the options market is pricing near-term earnings uncertainty, not a structural bearish thesis, which is congruent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The land bank is worth well above the ~$1.0B carried cost; the 23,653-homesite pipeline plus 1.2M+ sq ft of commercial and extensive entitlements represent years of monetization runway. The recurring-revenue pivot is compounding: hospitality margins expanded, memberships are growing, and leasing occupancy is in the mid-90s, reducing dependence on lumpy land sales and building a more durable earnings base. The debt structure (4.7% blended, ~19.7-year average life, 82.7% fixed) is a genuine, underappreciated moat in a higher-for-longer rate world. In-migration into Northwest Florida continues to override national housing weakness. If the Berkowitz supply clears and the JV closing cadence normalizes, earnings visibility improves and the stock can re-rate toward NAV. Bull target ~$73 (roughly the 52-week high area, ~18% above current), reflecting NAV-gap closure and overhang relief.

**Bear case.** A major hurricane, a reversal in Florida in-migration, or a broader consumer/housing downturn hits residential and hospitality simultaneously in a 100%-concentrated portfolio. JV timing continues to make reported earnings volatile and hard to underwrite. The Berkowitz selling persists and caps the multiple. At ~32x trailing earnings against slow, unrealizable NAV monetization, there is limited valuation support if growth decelerates. Bear target ~$46 (the 52-week low area, ~26% below current).

**Base case.** The recurring-revenue engine keeps compounding, homesite pipeline conversion continues, JV closings normalize over the next few quarters, and the stock trades in a range as the Berkowitz overhang and full multiple offset the fundamental progress. Base case ~$65 (~5% above current, roughly book NAV plus modest operating growth, no re-rating). This sits between the bear (~$46) and bull (~$73) scenarios and reflects a fairly-valued compounder without a near-term catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a multi-year hold thesis, reassessed quarterly given the JV-driven earnings volatility.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation that Fairholme has materially slowed or ceased selling (Form 4 evidence of stabilized or rising holdings), removing the primary technical overhang.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $50s, which would restore asymmetric upside against a conservatively-carried land bank and a growing recurring-revenue base.
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating that recurring hospitality/commercial earnings growth is offsetting JV closing volatility, improving earnings visibility.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A reversal in Northwest Florida in-migration or a material Florida insurance/affordability shock that dents residential absorption and hospitality demand.
- Evidence the land-monetization pipeline is stalling (homesites under contract falling, homebuilder relationships contracting).
- A major uninsured/self-insured storm loss that materially impairs the asset base.
- Berkowitz selling accelerating into an outright exit that signals a fundamental view change rather than diversification.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report, with particular attention to Latitude Margaritaville home-closing cadence, hospitality margin trajectory, and any change in Fairholme's Form 4 activity.

## Conclusion

St. Joe is a high-quality, well-financed compounder with a genuinely irreplaceable asset base and a working strategic pivot toward recurring revenue. The debt structure is best-in-class for the sector, the land bank is conservatively carried and worth more than book, and the Panhandle in-migration tailwind remains intact. Those strengths justify continued ownership. What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the absence of an entry-point discount or a near-term catalyst: the stock trades at a full multiple, quarterly earnings are volatile and JV-dependent, and the persistent Fairholme supply overhang is likely to keep the multiple range-bound until it clears. This is a growth_hold: own it for the multi-year land-monetization and recurring-revenue compounding story, add on a pullback into the low-to-mid $50s or on confirmation the Berkowitz overhang has lifted, and monitor JV closing cadence and in-migration data as the key variables.
