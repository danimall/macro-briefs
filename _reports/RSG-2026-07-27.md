**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Republic Services is the second-largest U.S. environmental (solid waste) services company, running a vertically integrated, route-based, recurring-revenue franchise. Its economic engine is internalization — roughly 68% of collected waste flows into landfills it owns and operates, capturing margin at both the collection and disposal stages. Landfills are the scarce asset in this industry; new permits are effectively unobtainable, which gives incumbents durable local pricing power. That power is on clear display: Q1 2026 core price rose 5.7% against labor and fuel inflation, operating margin held at 20.2%, and operating cash flow grew 20% year over year to $1,227M.

The tension in the story is growth composition. Q1 revenue grew 2.6% and diluted EPS grew 7.6%, but the entire top-line contribution came from price (+3.4% yield) and acquisitions (+1.1%); organic volume was negative (−0.8%), and the Environmental Solutions segment (Group 3) is deteriorating with revenue down 9.8% and EBITDA down 16%. This is a quality compounder manufacturing growth through pricing and roll-up M&A while underlying volumes shrink. The model is sustainable in the near term because waste demand is inelastic and pricing has historically outrun costs, but it leaves less cushion if pricing normalizes into a still-soft volume backdrop.

At $216.91, RSG has declined roughly 11.9% from its 52-week high of $246.25 and trades at approximately 30x forward EPS ($7.29 consensus for the current year). That is a discount to a peer median P/E of 38.8 but full relative to a high-single-digit compounding rate. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, options markets are pricing near-term stress in backwardation, and the West Lake Superfund liability remains an idiosyncratic tail. We rate the stock HOLD (growth_hold): a durable compounder worth owning through the cycle, but without an identifiable near-term catalyst to justify a re-rating from current levels.

## Company Overview

As of March 31, 2026, Republic operated 381 collection operations, 258 transfer stations, 81 recycling centers, 209 active landfills, 24 hazardous-waste TSDFs, 2 polymer centers, and 85 landfill gas-to-energy/renewable projects across the U.S. and Canada, plus post-closure responsibility for 124 closed landfills. The company reports in three segments:

- **Group 1 (Recycling & Waste, Western U.S.):** Q1'26 net revenue $1,928M, EBITDA $669M — the growth engine, with revenue up 7.3% and EBITDA up 12% YoY.
- **Group 2 (Recycling & Waste, SE/Midwest/East/Canada):** $1,780M revenue, $575M EBITDA — roughly flat.
- **Group 3 (Environmental Solutions — hazardous waste, field/industrial services):** $405M revenue, $78M EBITDA — the current drag, revenue −9.8%, EBITDA −16%.

Revenue mix skews toward the highest-margin collection lines: collection ~69% (small-container/commercial at 31.8% is the largest sub-line), landfill ~11%, Environmental Solutions ~10%, transfer ~5%, and recycling/commodities ~5%. The differentiated growth vector is the sustainability/circular-economy platform: renewable natural gas (RNG) projects that monetize landfill gas, Polymer Centers, and the Blue Polymers JV with Ravago, all of which harvest Section 48/IRA tax credits.

Leadership is stable. Jon Vander Ark serves as President and CEO (and the chief operating decision maker), with Brian DelGhiaccio as CFO; both signed the Q1 2026 10-Q. A February 12, 2026 8-K flagged an officer/director change (Item 5.02) that could not be independently confirmed via news, but the CEO and CFO remain in place, suggesting a routine transition rather than a disruptive departure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Q1 2026 revenue was $4,113M, up 2.6% YoY. The decomposition is the central fact of this report: average yield +3.4%, acquisitions +1.1%, fuel fees +0.2%, offset by volume −0.8% and Environmental Solutions −1.3%. Core price rose 5.7% (6.8% of the related business), the clearest evidence of pricing power. Operating income was $830M at a 20.2% margin (versus 20.1% a year earlier); margin stability is a hallmark of this franchise. Diluted EPS was $1.70, up 7.6%, on net income of $525M. Operating cash flow of $1,227M (+20%) reflects excellent cash conversion.

For FY2025, revenue was approximately $16.6B, operating margin roughly 19.9%, and ROE approximately 17.9% — steady mid-single-digit revenue growth translating into high-single/low-double-digit EPS growth.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is $13.99B; net debt/EBITDA of approximately 2.6x sits well under the 3.75x covenant ceiling. Credit ratings are A- (S&P and Fitch) and A3 (Moody's), solidly investment grade. Debt is well-laddered from 2026 to 2056 with several low-coupon legacy notes (1.4–3.4%). Floating-rate debt of $2.8B carries roughly $28M of annual interest sensitivity per 100bp move, manageable against $1.2B of quarterly operating cash flow. A screening Altman Z-Score of ~1.45 flags "distress," but this is a false positive: the model penalizes RSG's high leverage, sub-1.0 current ratio, and asset intensity, none of which reflect genuine distress for a recurring-cash, investment-grade infrastructure business.

**Capital allocation.** In Q1'26, Republic repurchased $292M of stock (1.3M shares at ~$218) and paid $193M in dividends, with $1.4B remaining on the $3B authorization through December 2026. The quarterly dividend was raised 7.8% to $0.625, continuing a long streak of annual increases. Management plans $1B+ of acquisitions in 2026 (tuck-in roll-up strategy), having already deployed $437M in Q1 (Hamm/Cornejo in Kansas; Robinson in Utah closed May 2026). Capex runs roughly $476M/quarter for fleet, landfill development, and digital platforms. Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly, with performance metrics (adjusted free cash flow, ROIC, TSR) aligned to shareholder outcomes.

The effective tax rate fell to 19.9% in Q1'26 from 25.6% a year earlier, driven by $37M of renewable-energy investment tax credits. The RNG/renewable JVs generated a −$52M equity-method loss (up from −$12M), which surfaces as a drag on the income statement but is economically a tax-advantaged reinvestment: the ~$37M of associated Section 48 credits more than offset the accounting loss.

## Growth Analysis

Republic's growth algorithm is currently price plus M&A, with volume subtracting. That composition matters more than the headline. Core price against a soft volume backdrop demonstrates the pricing power the franchise is built on, and tuck-in acquisitions add roughly a point of top-line growth per year at accretive returns. Group 1 (Western U.S.) is compounding, the sustainability platform (RNG, Polymer Centers, Blue Polymers) is a differentiated growth vector, and the earnings surprise record is solid (three beats and one miss over the trailing four quarters, with beats of 5.6% and 7.4% in the two prior periods).

The offsets are real. Negative volume is driven by slowing construction and certain manufacturing end-markets hitting large-container collection, plus municipal contract losses and broker business in residential/small-container. Group 3 (Environmental Solutions) is deteriorating on declining emergency-response activity. Consensus estimates are not confirming any acceleration: current-year EPS of $7.29 has been revised 0.0% over 30 days and up just 0.6% over 90 days; the current quarter is down 0.7% over 30 days. This is broadly flat-to-negative earnings momentum, well short of the kind of sharp upward revision velocity that would signal an acceleration. Next-year EPS of $8.07 implies roughly 11% growth off the current-year base, consistent with the historical high-single/low-double-digit algorithm.

The growth thesis is durable but not accelerating. The near-term construction/industrial volume softness is more a macro signal than a share-loss problem, though the municipal residential losses are company-specific and worth monitoring. This is high-quality, predictable compounding, with the growth rate constrained by negative volume until the industrial cycle turns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $216.91, RSG trades at approximately 30x forward EPS and roughly 27x next-year EPS. That is a discount to the Environmental & Facilities Services peer median P/E of 38.8, with Waste Management at 31.7, Rollins at 43.2, Clean Harbors at 38.8, and Casella at a distorted 817.9. The dividend yield of ~1.15% is below the peer median (0.6% median, though skewed).

The discount to peers is only modestly informative here. WM, the closest comparable, trades at a nearly identical 31.7x with a similar growth profile, so RSG is priced roughly in line with its most direct competitor rather than at a meaningful discount. Against RSG's own high-single/low-double-digit EPS growth, ~30x forward yields a PEG in the vicinity of 2.7–3.0 — full, not cheap. This is not a cyclical trough where trailing multiples understate normalized earnings; margins are stable at ~20% and estimates are flat, so forward P/E is a fair read. The stock is a quality compounder priced for its quality, with limited margin of safety.

The ~11.9% decline from the 52-week high does not, by itself, create a valuation case. The pullback reflects a modest de-rating on flat estimate momentum and soft volume rather than a mispricing the market has overlooked. Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $208, high $270, mean $245.4, median $249.5. The mean target implies ~13% upside, but with estimates flat and no identifiable catalyst, we treat the median as an aspirational figure premised on continued pricing execution rather than an imminent re-rating trigger. We do not treat proximity to a full multiple as a buy signal; at a PEG well above 1.5, the estimate-revision-as-floor logic does not apply. The valuation score reflects a stretched multiple relative to a volume-constrained growth rate.

## Competitive Landscape

Republic operates in a consolidated oligopoly — Waste Management (#1), Republic (#2), Waste Connections, and GFL — protected by structural barriers: landfill permitting scarcity, route-density economics, and capital intensity. The defining industry feature is pricing power that consistently outruns cost inflation, which RSG is demonstrating with +5.7% core price against labor and fuel inflation. Internalization (~68% of collected waste disposed at owned landfills) captures margin at both stages and reinforces the moat.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-softening rather than deteriorating. The negative volume is largely a sector-wide macro phenomenon (construction/industrial slowdown pressuring special-waste and large-container/C&D volumes across all incumbents), not evidence of share loss. The exception is municipal residential contract losses, which are company-specific and bear watching. The sustainability platform (RNG, polymer recycling) is a differentiated growth vector that peers are also pursuing but where RSG has committed capital and JV structures in place. On balance, the competitive position remains strong and durable, with the trajectory flat rather than improving, warranting a downgrade from the prior 8.5 toward 7.5, consistent with the score trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume softness masked by pricing.** The growth algorithm is 100% price plus M&A, 0% volume. If pricing decelerates while volume stays negative, organic growth could stall. This is the dominant, if slow-moving, risk.

**West Lake Landfill Superfund.** Subsidiary Bridgeton Landfill is a Potentially Responsible Party at a Missouri site containing radiologically-impacted material from the Manhattan Project. The EPA's January 2025 Explanation of Significant Differences raised the undiscounted cost estimate to $392M (from $229M), a roughly 71% increase. RSG intends to contest liability for the federally-generated radiological material, but final remedy design and allocation are unresolved. This is a genuine, quantifiable idiosyncratic tail risk that standardized screens would miss.

**Environmental Solutions deterioration.** Group 3 EBITDA is declining on lower emergency-response activity — the one segment moving the wrong way, currently offset by Group 1 pricing.

**Recycled commodity and fuel exposure.** Recycled commodity prices fell to $120/ton from $155/ton; a $10/ton move is roughly $13M of revenue and operating income (unhedged). Diesel at $4.12/gal versus $3.63 adds cost, largely offset by ~$42M of fuel recovery fees.

**Environmental/remediation and labor liabilities.** $2.78B is accrued for landfill capping/closure/post-closure plus remediation, with a reasonably-possible high end ~$276M above recorded. Union labor carries periodic work-stoppage risk and potential multiemployer pension withdrawal liabilities.

Most of these risks are known and priced. The West Lake situation is the clearest binary/tail item that is not fully reflected. The risk score of 5.5 reflects a manageable but non-trivial idiosyncratic overhang, down from the prior 7.5 as the West Lake cost estimate escalation and the volume/Group 3 deterioration have become more salient.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.4% sits above one-year historical volatility of 19.6% (a 6.8% spread — rich), and the term structure is in backwardation (26.4% near-term versus 19.3% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±5.4%. Put/call skew is balanced (−1.6% near-term), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 2.31 to 1.25 since the prior snapshot as the stock recovered 6.1%, a reduction in defensive positioning. Short interest is minimal at 1.8% of float (2.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-bearish signal and no squeeze dynamic. Elevated near-term IV argues for patience on entry, but positioning is not directionally bearish.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Pricing power holds at 5–7% annually with high retention; the industrial/construction cycle turns and volume returns to positive; tuck-in M&A continues at accretive returns with the $1B+ 2026 budget; the RNG/polymer platform scales into a meaningful, tax-advantaged earnings contributor; and the market re-rates the durable compounding toward peer multiples. In that scenario, EPS compounds low-double-digits and the stock approaches the analyst high target of ~$270 (roughly +24%).

**Bear case.** Volume stays negative or worsens as the industrial slowdown persists; pricing decelerates toward cost inflation, compressing the price-cost spread; Environmental Solutions continues to deteriorate; West Lake liability escalates further or an adverse allocation is imposed; and the multiple compresses toward a low-double-digit-growth fair value. In that scenario the stock drifts toward the analyst low target of ~$208 (roughly −4%) or below, into the low $190s.

**Base case.** Pricing plus M&A sustains ~2–4% revenue growth and high-single/low-double-digit EPS growth; volume remains a modest drag until the cycle turns; the multiple holds around 27–30x forward. On $8.07 next-year EPS at ~28x, fair value is approximately $226 (roughly +4%), with the rising dividend adding another point-plus. Total return in the mid-single digits over twelve months, dominated by compounding rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic volume turns positive (a return to flat-to-positive volume alongside sustained 5%+ core price), signaling the growth algorithm is no longer volume-constrained.
- Estimate-revision inflection: current-year or next-year EPS revised up 3%+ over 30 days, confirming accelerating momentum.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $190s (near the 52-week low of $196.41), which would compress forward P/E toward ~24x and restore a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Core price decelerates below ~4% while volume remains negative, indicating the price-cost spread is narrowing.
- A materially adverse West Lake Superfund allocation or a further step-up in the cost estimate.
- Consecutive quarters of Group 3 (Environmental Solutions) deterioration spreading into the core Recycling & Waste EBITDA margin.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (the ±5.4% implied move and IV backwardation flag this as the near-term catalyst), then the FY2026 M&A cadence and 2027 guidance.

## Conclusion

Republic Services is a high-quality, moat-protected compounder with best-in-class pricing power, stable ~20% operating margins, excellent cash conversion, an investment-grade balance sheet, and disciplined, shareholder-friendly capital allocation. Those attributes are real and durable. The constraint is that growth is currently manufactured entirely through pricing and M&A while organic volume shrinks, and the stock trades at ~30x forward EPS, a full multiple relative to a high-single/low-double-digit growth rate, with flat estimate momentum and no identifiable near-term catalyst to force a re-rating. The ~11.9% pullback from the high improves the entry marginally but does not create a compelling asymmetric setup; the peer-relative discount is modest and largely explained by an in-line comparison to Waste Management.

We rate RSG HOLD. Own it for the durable compounding, the rising dividend, and the optionality in the sustainability platform, and add on weakness toward the low-to-mid $190s or on a volume/estimate inflection. The score has drifted lower over the past two quarters (7.1 → 6.4 → 6.0), driven by risk and growth de-rating as the West Lake escalation and volume/Group 3 softness became clearer; today's data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it.
