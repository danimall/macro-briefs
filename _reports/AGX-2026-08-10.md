## Executive Summary

Argan is the closest available pure-play on the shortage of US gas-fired power-plant construction capacity. Its Power segment — Gemma Power Systems — generated 80.1% of FY2026 revenue and carries roughly 4.16 GW of contracted US combined-cycle capacity awarded between December 2024 and October 2025, plus two Irish thermal projects. Consolidated backlog doubled from $1.4B at January 2025 to $2.9B at January 2026, Power gross margin expanded from 16.7% to 22.4%, and Q1 FY2027 revenue grew 50.2% to $290.954M with diluted EPS of $3.24 against a $2.33 consensus. The company carries no funded debt against $973.6M of cash, CDs and Treasuries. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 8.8% (FY2027) and 8.9% (FY2028) over ninety days, and the last four quarters produced beats of 50.9%, 5.1%, 73.7% and 38.9%.

The problem is price and earnings quality. At $592.76 the shares trade at 52.1x trailing-twelve-month EPS of approximately $11.38, 48.4x current-year consensus of $12.24, and 36.5x next-year consensus of $16.25, against a peer median of 25.9x. Enterprise value net of gross cash is roughly $7.34B, or 39.2x TTM EBITDA of $186.9M and 7.0x TTM revenue. Two adjustments matter. First, the effective tax rate of 14.2% (FY2026) and 14.3% (Q1 FY2027) versus 23.2% and 24.3% in the prior comparable periods reflects windfall deductions on appreciated stock compensation; at a 25% rate, TTM EPS is approximately $9.75 rather than $11.38, lifting the trailing multiple to about 60.8x. Second, FY2026 operating cash flow of $414.7M included $214.7M that management itself labels a "temporary increase in contract liabilities" — customer advances on newly mobilized fixed-price projects. The company's own working-capital measure was flat at $421.0M (1/31/26) and $421.4M (4/30/26) despite $113.4M of Q1 operating inflow. Structural free cash flow is better approximated at roughly $159M, a 1.9% yield on market capitalization.

Three further items constrain conviction. Backlog declined from $2.9B to $2.8B in Q1 FY2027 while $291M of revenue was burned; the rounding in the disclosure prevents a precise book-to-bill, but awards were almost certainly below burn. Every insider Form 4 filed between April and July 31, 2026 was a sale, totaling well over $120M, including a 71% reduction by one long-tenured holder at $643–$760, while the company repurchased only $3.0M of stock against $84.7M of remaining authorization and share count rose from 13,950,712 to 14,020,427. The July 30, 2026 session produced a 17.8% single-day advance (from $492.35 to $579.87) that I cannot attribute to any filing I was able to read, alongside an August 4 8-K whose exhibit content was not retrievable. Rating **HOLD**, hold rationale **uncertain**: the demand thesis is confirmed and the balance sheet is genuinely strong, but the base case sits at the current price and the downside asymmetry at 48x forward earnings is material.

## Company Overview

Argan is a Delaware holding company headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, operating three subsidiaries as independent businesses.

**Power (80.1% of FY2026 revenue; $756.5M).** Full-scope EPC, commissioning, maintenance and project development for power generation, executed largely under fixed-price contracts with one-to-four-year durations. Customers are independent power producers, public utilities, equipment OEMs and large industrial power users. International operations in Ireland and the UK serve Irish utilities, plant owners, data-center operators and OEMs, and provide turbine and boiler installation and outage services.

**Industrial (17.7%; $167.6M).** On-site industrial construction and field services concentrated in the Southeast, plus a 90,000-plus square-foot pipe-and-vessel fabrication complex near Greenville, North Carolina. In November 2025 the segment won a contract to fabricate approximately 2,000 horizontal pressure vessels for thermal-energy-storage and chilled-water buffer cooling systems at a customer's data centers, prompting a land purchase in Farmville, North Carolina for a second fabrication facility due in Q3 FY2027. Argan is the fabricator and supplier here; it does not own or operate data centers.

**Teledata (2.2%; $20.6M).** Utility and technology wiring in the Mid-Atlantic, largely under task orders against master agreements.

Headcount was 1,409 at January 31, 2026. PP&E net of $18.3M against $1,286.4M of total assets confirms an asset-light, craft-labor and subcontractor-leveraged model.

The current project slate: 1.4 GW Ward County, Texas (FNTP Oct 2025, CY2029); 860 MW ERCOT (FNTP Oct 2025, CY2028); 1.2 GW Sandow Lakes, Texas (NTP Apr 2025, CY2028); 700 MW US combined-cycle (FNTP Dec 2024, CY2028); 170 MW County Meath, Ireland (Jul 2025, CY2028); ~300 MW Tarbert biofuel, County Kerry (Jan 2025, late CY2027); 405 MW Illinois solar (FNTP Aug 2024, FY2027). The 950 MW Trumbull Energy Center reached final completion in Q1 FY2027 after achieving substantial completion ahead of schedule.

## Financial Analysis

| FY2023 | $455.0M | 19.0% | $41.7M | $33.1M | — |
| FY2024 | $573.3M | 14.1% | $36.5M | $32.4M | — |

FY2026 revenue grew only 8.1% after FY2025's 52.5%. The earnings gain was margin and tax: 440bp of gross margin expansion, all of it in Power (16.7% → 22.4%), while Industrial declined to 12.1% and Teledata to 19.5%. Management attributes the Power gain to project and contract-type mix, improved international profitability, and cost reduction on Trumbull as it completed ahead of schedule.

Q1 FY2027 reversed the volume deceleration: revenue $290.954M (+50.2%), with Power +41.4%, Industrial +99.8% and Teledata +45.2%. Gross margin 21.0% (Power 23.6%), SG&A down to 5.4% of revenue from 6.5%, delivering real operating leverage. Operating income $45.395M (+86.5%), other income $8.374M, net income $46.063M (+104.3%), EPS $3.24, EBITDA $54.403M.

Derived TTM (FY2026 + Q1 FY2027 − Q1 FY2026): revenue $1,041.9M; net income $161.3M; EBITDA $186.9M; operating income $155.8M; pretax income $184.5M; other income $28.7M (15.6% of pretax); diluted EPS approximately $11.38 (sum-of-quarters approximation, not a company-reported figure).

**Cash-flow quality is the single most important analytical adjustment.** FY2026 operating cash flow of $414.7M on $137.8M of net income and $3.9M of capex produced a headline 43.5% free-cash-flow margin. Management's reconciliation attributes $157.3M to net income adjusted for non-cash items and $214.7M to a "temporary increase in contract liabilities… primarily due to the net effect of the early phase of construction activities on certain projects." Contract liabilities stood at $565.8M at April 30, 2026 — 44.0% of total assets and 70% of total liabilities. In Q1, $222.6M of revenue was recognized out of opening contract liabilities against $51.8M of additions. The clean confirmation is management's own liquidity metric: net liquidity (working capital) was $421.0M at January 31 and $421.4M at April 30, essentially unchanged despite $113.4M of quarterly operating inflow. A reasonable structural free-cash-flow estimate is TTM net income of $161.3M plus roughly $2.5M of D&A less roughly $5M of capex, or about $159M — 15% of revenue, not 43%. On $8.31B of market capitalization that is a 1.9% yield.

The float is not fictional. Argan earns roughly a 3.4% pretax yield on average cash-plus-investments of about $840M, producing TTM other income of $28.7M, or 15.6% of pretax profit. That income is recurring while the pipeline refills. It reverses when awards stop, which is the same event that ends the growth thesis.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $1,286.4M, liabilities $812.9M, equity $473.5M at April 30, 2026. **No funded debt**, no borrowings under the $35M Bank of America facility (SOFR+1.85%, $30M accordion, plus a $25M European letter-of-credit companion facility) at either January 31 or April 30, and a single $0.5M letter of credit outstanding. The facility's only financial covenant is positive rolling-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA; the company was compliant. The facility expires May 31, 2027; refinancing risk on an undrawn facility for a profitable, debt-free borrower is low. Most company assets are pledged to secure it.

Investments of $617.7M comprise $172.8M of Bank of America CDs and $444.9M of AFS Treasuries, $308.8M of which matures in three to five years. Gross unrealized losses swung to $2.1M from $0.5M, driving a $2.7M other-comprehensive loss in Q1. Roughly 11% of cash and investments sit in Irish and UK subsidiaries.

Screening metrics require correction. The Altman Z-score of 2.53 and a "debt-to-equity of 1.57" are artifacts: the latter is total-liabilities-to-equity, and 70% of those liabilities are customer advances. Z-score inputs of equity/assets 0.39 and equity/liabilities 0.638 are depressed by the same float that inflates the asset base. Applied to a debt-free contractor holding $974M of cash and Treasuries, the model understates solvency. Its residual signal is legitimate in a different form: contract liabilities are an obligation to perform, equity/assets slipped from 39.0% to 36.8% in one quarter, and unsatisfied bonded performance obligations doubled from roughly $0.5B to roughly $1.0B between January 31 and April 30 as the large gas projects mobilized. Solvency risk is very low while single-project execution risk is the real tail, and a meaningful share of the $974M is working collateral for bonding and parent guarantees rather than freely deployable capital.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026: $24.3M of dividends, $9.9M of buybacks, $8.4M net for share-award settlements, $11.5M final contribution to a solar tax-credit entity, $3.9M capex, $171.7M net into Treasuries. Q1 FY2027: $7.0M of dividends, $3.0M of buybacks (6,450 shares at an average $458.21), and $23.7M net for share-based award settlements. The board raised the repurchase authorization by $50M on April 8 to $200M aggregate, leaving $84.7M available. Share count rose to 14,020,427 from 13,950,712. The quarterly dividend went from $0.375 to $0.500 effective October 2025; $2.00 annualized is a 0.34% yield at $592.76 and roughly 18% of TTM EPS. Buying at $458 rather than at $700 is defensible discipline; spending nearly eight times as much on employee award settlements as on repurchases, and letting the share count rise, is not.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is confirmed rather than speculative. Argan's own MD&A cites US electricity demand at its highest level in roughly two decades, driven by data centers supporting AI, electrification and reshoring, against an aging and retiring generation fleet. The mechanism reaching Argan runs: load growth → new gas-fired dispatchable capacity approved and financed → notice to proceed → EPC revenue.

Near-term visibility is solid. RUPO of $2.8B at April 30 with management estimating approximately 42% recognized over the next twelve months implies about $1.18B, or 13% above TTM revenue of $1,041.9M, with substantially all the remainder inside 12–24 months. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.24 and FY2028 of $16.25 imply 32.8% earnings growth into next year. Estimate revisions of +8.8% and +8.9% over ninety days are positive but well below the +30% threshold that marks a true inflection; 30-day revisions are flat, meaning consensus has already absorbed the Q1 beat.

The Industrial pressure-vessel contract is a genuine new vector: segment backlog went from $53.2M (1/31/25) to $253.0M (1/31/26) to $225.5M (4/30/26), and Q1 Industrial revenue nearly doubled. Industrial gross margin at 11.8% is roughly half Power's, so the mix shift is mildly dilutive to consolidated margin even as it diversifies revenue.

**The offsetting fact is the Q1 award pause.** Consolidated backlog fell from $2.9B to $2.8B while $291M of revenue was recognized. Because both figures are disclosed rounded to $0.1B, implied gross new awards could range from roughly $91M to $291M — a book-to-bill of approximately 0.31x to 1.00x. The only defensible conclusion is directional: Argan booked less new work than it burned. That is weak evidence in isolation, since nearly all of FY2026's $1.5B net backlog addition arrived in about two months of October 2025 and this business books three or four contracts a year. It is not weak evidence at 48x forward earnings, where the multiple capitalizes continued award momentum. Argan also cannot accelerate revenue faster than turbines are delivered or interconnection agreements executed; three of the five gating constraints management identifies (equipment supply, interconnection, financing) are outside its control and cap its own throughput as much as they protect its pricing.

Growth-adjusted, the picture is less extreme but not cheap. Current-year P/E of 48.4x against 32.8% forward EPS growth gives a PEG of about 1.48; on next-year earnings the multiple is 36.5x, or a PEG of roughly 1.11 if one extrapolates a further year of similar growth. That is defensible for a debt-free business earning 29.8% ROE in a supply-constrained end market. It is not a mispricing.

Two adjustments argue the effective multiple is higher than it looks. The 14% effective tax rate is a function of share-price appreciation between grant and vest on stock compensation; it is real cash tax savings but self-limiting, and consensus estimates almost certainly embed its continuation. At 25%, FY2028 EPS of $16.25 becomes roughly $14.00, and the forward multiple becomes 42.3x. Separately, Power gross margin of 22.4%–23.6% is unusually high for lump-sum turnkey EPC. Margins at that level typically reflect contingency release and favorable cumulative catch-up adjustments as projects mature; as the newly mobilized 2028–2029 projects come to dominate the mix, early-phase conservatism should mechanically pull blended margin toward the mid-teens before recovering late in those contracts. I cannot distinguish this from structurally better contracted pricing on the 2025-vintage awards, because Argan does not disclose contracted margin by vintage.

Scenario framework:

- **Base $590 (−0.5%):** 36.3x FY2028 consensus $16.25. Assumes awards resume at a pace that holds backlog near $3B, Power margin drifts toward 19–20% on mix, tax rate stays below 20%, and the multiple compresses modestly as growth normalizes.
- **Bull $790 (+33.3%):** FY2028 EPS beats to roughly $19.00 (consistent with the 39%–74% beat pattern) at 41.6x. Requires at least one new large FNTP, durable Power margin above 22%, and the pressure-vessel franchise scaling. Brackets the $805.75 52-week high and sits below the $860 analyst high.
- **Bear $395 (−33.4%):** 28.2x on tax-normalized FY2028 EPS of about $14.00, with Power margin reverting to the mid-teens as new-vintage projects dominate, no incremental large award through FY2028, and multiple compression toward the peer cohort. An adverse UK ruling or a single material cost overrun accelerates this.

Probability-weighting 25/50/25 gives an expected return of roughly zero. Analyst mean of $679.8 sits 14.7% above spot and median $700 sits 18.1% above, but with only five covering analysts (1 strong buy, 1 buy, 3 hold) and a 48x forward multiple, those targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantages evident in the filings are specific. Argan states that for domestic gas-fired combined-cycle EPC, "the number of capable competitors has declined over the past decade as several major firms have exited the market, been acquired, or moved away from fixed-price contracts," and that developers "rely on a relatively small group of contractors with demonstrated experience." Argan's willingness to take fixed-price risk when peers have retreated is a real differentiator, and the 950 MW Trumbull project completing ahead of schedule is objective evidence of execution capability on the exact scope that matters. The debt-free, $974M-liquid balance sheet supporting roughly $1.0B of bonded performance obligations and parent guarantees is itself a barrier: a thinly capitalized contractor cannot bond a 1.4 GW project. The in-house pipe and pressure-vessel fabrication capability is now winning data-center thermal-storage work directly, extending the franchise into an adjacent, faster-cycling revenue stream.

The limits are equally clear and management concedes them. Growing demand "may attract new entrants or prompt former competitors to re-engage," and remaining participants "continue to be highly capable." Competition in renewables EPC is "more diverse," consistent with Industrial at 11.8% and Teledata at 11.0% Q1 gross margin. The margin premium belongs to large-scale thermal EPC, not to Argan as a whole. International operations produced the company's one significant loss of recent years — approximately $13.4M of cumulative contract losses on a terminated UK project — so the FY2026 commentary about "significant improvement in profitability" internationally describes recovery from a low base in Ireland rather than a proven multi-year record.

Customer concentration has not improved. Top-three customers were 50%, 51% and 50% of revenue in FY2026, FY2025 and FY2024 respectively. Q1 FY2027's top three at 15%/13%/12% (40%) is better than the prior-year quarter's 55%, and Industrial rose from 15.1% to 20.0% of quarterly revenue, but that is a segment-mix and project-timing fact, not customer diversification. Receivable concentration at April 30 was 19%/16%/13%/11%/11% across five customers; contract-asset concentration was 22%/22%/12%. In an EPC business where a single project runs $500M–$1B, structural concentration is normal, and the counterparties are utilities and well-capitalized IPPs with progress billing and lien rights. It remains a real exposure.

The transmission channel from any AI-capex slowdown is spelled out in Argan's own risk factors: spark spread and capacity-market economics. Declining wholesale-to-fuel margins, lower capacity prices, or capacity-market rule changes "could reduce the expected returns on new power generation projects and discourage or delay project development." That would show up in Argan's award cadence before it shows up in backlog, and in backlog well before it shows up in revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed-price EPC estimate reversal.** Revenue is recognized cost-to-cost with estimates re-forecast monthly; changes flow through as cumulative catch-up adjustments that "could be material." The same mechanism that released contingency on Trumbull reverses on a project that goes wrong. With four large gas projects scheduled for CY2028–CY2029 completion, this is the dominant single-name risk and it is not captured by any leverage or liquidity ratio.

**Liquidated damages are not initially reserved.** Argan discloses that at contract outset "the potential amounts of liquidated damages typically are not subtracted from the transaction price"; they are recognized only when a significant revenue reversal becomes probable. Schedule slippage therefore hits revenue directly and without warning from prior-period accruals.

**UK litigation and unapproved variations.** Billable amounts on the terminated overseas project were $25.1M at April 30, including an expected refund of a $9.9M letter-of-credit draw Argan contends was improper. The UK subsidiary sued EP NI Energy Limited and EP UK Investment Limited in the English High Court in March 2025; the owner has counterclaimed. Separately, $15.0M of contract variations pending customer approval (up from $11.4M) relate primarily to an overseas project. Roughly $40M of gross recognized value depends on litigation and counterparty approvals — up to about 25% of one year's earnings in a bad outcome, against $473.5M of equity. The $13.7M of foreign NOL deferred tax assets remain fully valuation-allowanced, which is management's own signal on UK earning power. Resolution timing in English commercial courts is measured in years.

**Backlog is terminable for convenience.** Customers hold unilateral termination rights subject to compensation for completed work and demobilization costs. Management notes no customer has ever terminated a material contract for convenience, and RUPO of $2.8B matches reported backlog, but the disclosure framework carries that judgment.

**Tax normalization.** A move to a 25% effective rate reduces TTM EPS by approximately 14%. EPS-linked performance RSUs (the CEO held 28,839 after an April 8, 2026 grant) mean reported EPS achievement is partly a function of the share price through the windfall-deduction channel. This is a mechanical feature of the tax and accounting rules rather than evidence of intent, and the compensation metric set otherwise is well constructed (EBITDA, EBITDA as a percent of revenue, SG&A as a percent of revenue, ROE, backlog). The practical implication is that pretax income growth (+44.3% in FY2026) is the cleaner read than net income growth (+61.2%).

**Fraud exposure.** Argan was the target of a criminal scheme in FY2024 resulting in fraudulently induced outbound wire transfers, and its own risk factor ties the exposure to its large cash balances. With $974M on hand this remains live rather than theoretical, though controls were concluded effective as of April 30, 2026 with no material changes during the quarter.

**Tariffs.** Both the 10-K and 10-Q flag steel and aluminum tariffs as cost and lead-time risks; on fixed-price work, unrecovered input inflation lands directly in gross margin.

**Verification gaps that limit confidence.** The August 4, 2026 8-K (Items 8.01 and 9.01, with an EX-99.1 exhibit) could not be read. The July 30 session saw a 17.8% single-day advance from $492.35 to $579.87 that I cannot attribute to any filing available to me, following a slide from a $790 close in late June. Fiscal Q2 ended July 31, 2026 and has not yet been reported. A meaningful positive information event has plausibly occurred that would weaken the backlog-pause observation; I cannot confirm it. This uncertainty is a direct input into the hold rationale.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($790, +33.3%)

The gas-fired capacity shortage is a multi-year structural condition, not a cycle. Argan holds roughly 4.16 GW of contracted US thermal capacity plus two Irish projects, and the competitive set for lump-sum-turnkey combined-cycle work has genuinely thinned. Power margins at 22–24% reflect scarcity pricing on 2025-vintage awards rather than contingency release, and hold. Award cadence resumes — historically arriving in bursts — pushing backlog above $3.2B and extending visibility into FY2030. The pressure-vessel data-center franchise scales from the Farmville facility, adding a shorter-cycle, repeatable revenue stream at improving Industrial margins. Four consecutive beats averaging well above 30% continue; FY2028 EPS reaches $19.00 against $16.25 consensus. The debt-free balance sheet earns 3.4% on $974M of float while requiring no external capital in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and the $84.7M of remaining repurchase authorization is deployed on weakness as it was at $458. At 41.6x FY2028 the shares reach $790.

### Bear Case ($395, −33.4%)

The Q1 backlog decline is the leading edge rather than noise. Spark spreads and capacity-market resets, or a slowdown in the AI-capex chain that is the proximate driver of new load, delay notices to proceed; awards stay below burn for several quarters and backlog falls through $2.5B. Power gross margin reverts to the mid-teens as the four newly mobilized projects dominate the mix with early-phase conservatism — a directional near-certainty, uncertain only in magnitude. The effective tax rate normalizes toward 25% as the share price stops compounding, removing 14% from EPS. Contract liabilities shift from a source to a use of cash as the projects progress, exposing the 1.9% structural free-cash-flow yield beneath the 43.5% headline. The UK litigation resolves adversely, writing down part of $40M of recognized value. The multiple compresses toward the 25.9x peer median on tax-normalized FY2028 EPS of $14.00, taking the shares to roughly $395.

### Management Quality

Operating execution has been strong: Trumbull early, SG&A down 110bp year over year, incident rates below the industry average every year for five years, segment succession planned rather than reactive, and a legal hire specifically to reduce commercial risk accepted in customer contracts. Grant-date compensation is disciplined — CEO total compensation rose only from $1.877M (FY2022) to $2.668M (FY2026) while TSR indexed from 88 to 886.

Forward-looking alignment indicators are weaker, and that is what the score reflects. Across every Form 4 from April through July 31, 2026 there was no open-market purchase by any insider. William F. Griffin Jr. sold 100,000 shares for approximately $69.2M over ten days in June at $643.46, $725.85 and $760.43, cutting reported holdings roughly 71%. CEO Watson sold about 31,183 shares for roughly $19.5M while retaining about 40,005 (worth roughly $23.7M); he is not exiting. Gemma's CEO sold 13,568 shares for about $8.4M; directors Leimkuhler, Getsinger, Flanders, Sweeney, Jeffrey and Larroque all sold; the CFO's direct holding is approximately 1,479 shares. Most sales followed a substantial April 16–17 vesting event, so a large share is mechanical diversification after a roughly 20x three-year move, and single-stock risk reduction by long-tenured holders carries limited forward information. The pattern that does carry information is the combination: clustered selling by more than three insiders within thirty days, one holding reduced by 71%, zero offsetting purchases across a $579–$760 price band, only $3.0M of company repurchases against $84.7M authorized, $23.7M spent settling employee awards, and a rising share count. Rising estimates and record results mute the signal but do not erase a one-directional pattern this broad.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD. Hold rationale: uncertain.** Existing owners have a strong business with confirmed end-market demand and no balance-sheet risk; prospective buyers have no margin of safety at 48.4x current-year earnings and face roughly symmetric ±33% scenario outcomes.

- **Base case: $590** (36.3x FY2028 consensus $16.25) — −0.5% from $592.76
- **Bull case: $790** (41.6x FY2028 EPS of $19.00) — +33.3%
- **Bear case: $395** (28.2x tax-normalized FY2028 EPS of $14.00) — −33.4%
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q2 FY2027 report (expected early September 2026), the Q3 report, and any Item 8.01 award announcements.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A new full notice to proceed on a project of $500M or greater, taking backlog above $3.2B and restoring book-to-bill above 1.0x — the single most important variable, since the multiple capitalizes award momentum.
2. Power gross margin sustained at 20% or better for two consecutive quarters *while* the four 2028–2029 projects are in early phase, which would evidence structurally higher contracted pricing rather than contingency release.
3. Price toward $460–$490 (roughly 28–30x FY2028 consensus) with backlog and estimates intact, which would restore a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Power gross margin below 16% in any quarter combined with no incremental large award — the combination that validates the bear mechanism.

## Conclusion

Argan is a debt-free, asset-light contractor with demonstrated large-scale combined-cycle execution capability, positioned against a confirmed multi-year shortage of dispatchable US generation capacity, with $2.8B of backlog covering 2.7x trailing revenue and four consecutive substantial earnings beats. The business quality is real, and the financial structure — zero funded debt, $974M of cash and Treasuries, a facility with a single EBITDA covenant and no drawings — is close to the best in its industry.

The price already reflects most of that. At $592.76 the shares carry 48.4x current-year and 36.5x next-year consensus against a 25.9x peer median, with reported earnings flattered roughly 14% by a share-price-dependent tax rate and reported free cash flow flattered by $214.7M of customer-advance float that management itself calls temporary. Structural free cash flow of approximately $159M is a 1.9% yield. Power's 22–24% gross margin is unusually high for fixed-price EPC and faces a mechanical mix headwind as four newly mobilized projects dominate revenue. The most recent quarter showed backlog burning faster than awards were booked; insiders sold well over $120M with no offsetting purchase while the company repurchased $3.0M and share count rose. Weighed against a base case sitting at the current price and roughly symmetric ±33% scenarios, the appropriate action is to hold what you own, not to add. A resumption of large-project awards, or a price in the high $400s on unchanged fundamentals, would change that.
