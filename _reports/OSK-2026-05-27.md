**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Oshkosh Corporation sits at an uncomfortable juncture: optically cheap at 11.3x guided FY2026 EPS ($11.50) with a massive $14.5B backlog, but facing a Q1 earnings collapse (-28% miss), $200M in tariff costs, and Access segment margins that fell from 15.6% in FY2024 to 3.7% in Q1 2026. The stock has declined 28% from its February 2026 high of $180.49 to $129.39, reflecting legitimate fundamental deterioration rather than mere sentiment.

The bull case requires believing management's heavily back-end loaded guidance — roughly 70% of full-year earnings expected in H2 — which depends on Access margin recovery, fire apparatus production throughput, and a USPS follow-on delivery order that triggers a favorable catch-up adjustment on $813M in deferred contract costs. The bear case centers on continued tariff pressure, Chinese competitive encroachment in Access equipment, and the possibility that the follow-on NGDV order is delayed or reduced amid DOGE-era government cost-cutting.

With consensus estimates falling (current year -3.0% over 90 days, current quarter -17.8% over 30 days), two consecutive earnings misses, and the stock trading below the lowest analyst price target ($138), the risk/reward is balanced but not compelling enough for a BUY given execution uncertainty. The Vocational segment's $6.6B backlog and 16%+ target margins provide a genuine floor, but insufficient to offset the magnitude of risks in the other two segments.

## Company Overview

Oshkosh is a diversified industrial technology company operating across three segments: Access (43% of FY2025 revenue — JLG aerial work platforms, telehandlers), Vocational (36% — Pierce fire apparatus, McNeilus refuse vehicles, AeroTech airport equipment), and Transport (20% — DoD tactical vehicles and USPS Next Generation Delivery Vehicles). The company employs ~18,400 people across 37 manufacturing facilities and generates approximately 80% of revenue from non-government customers.

The business model spans highly cyclical end markets (Access equipment tied to non-residential construction), stable municipal demand (Vocational), and government contracts with long production timelines (Transport). This diversification historically smoothed earnings, but FY2025-2026 represents a period where all three segments face simultaneous headwinds: Access is cycling down, JLTV production ended, and NGDV remains unprofitable on existing orders.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Revenue peaked at $10.73B in FY2024, declined 2.9% to $10.42B in FY2025, and Q1 2026 was flat at $2.32B. The Access segment contracted 13% in FY2025 and showed continued pressure in Q1 2026. Transport revenue grew 10.8% in Q1 on NGDV ramp ($216.6M vs. $50.3M prior year), but at near-zero margins.

**Margin Compression Is Severe:** Consolidated operating margins declined from 9.4% (FY2024) to 9.0% (FY2025) to 3.5% in Q1 2026. Access margins collapsed from 15.6% → 11.2% → 3.7% over the same period. The Q1 deterioration reflects tariff costs (+250 bps headwind), adverse mix (270 bps), and competitive discounting. Management guides 9.7% Access margins for full-year 2026, implying H2 recovery to 12-13%.

**Balance Sheet Adequate But Not Conservative:** Debt-to-capitalization of 20.4% with ~$1.15B total debt against $1.8B available liquidity. Investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa3/BBB) maintained. The March 2026 refinancing of the revolver to $1.6B at improved terms confirms market access. However, $813M in deferred NGDV contract costs represents a concentrated asset requiring future orders to generate returns.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF of $618M (5.9% margin on revenue) was solid. FY2026 guidance of $550-650M operating cash flow is lower, reflecting NGDV investment. At current market cap ($8.1B), this implies a 6.8-8.0% OCF yield — attractive if sustainable.

**Capital Returns:** $278M in buybacks (FY2025), $47M in Q1 2026. Shares outstanding declining ~2.7% annually. 12th consecutive year of double-digit dividend growth. The $2.28 annualized dividend (1.8% yield) is well-covered by current earnings.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth visibility is poor. Estimate revisions tell the story: current quarter EPS cut 17.8% in 30 days, current year estimates down 3.0% over 90 days. This negative revision momentum contrasts with the FY2027 consensus of $14.05 (28% growth over FY2026), which requires successful execution of all the assumptions management has outlined.

**Positive growth vectors:**
- NGDV production ramp: revenue grew 330% YoY in Q1 2026 (from $50.3M to $216.6M). Potential for 165,000 total vehicles over 10 years (51,500 ordered to date).
- Vocational backlog of $6.6B provides 4+ years of revenue visibility at improving margins (guided to 16.1% in FY2026).
- International expansion in Access markets and defense tactical vehicles.
- Electrification initiatives (battery-electric JLG platforms, electric refuse vehicles).

**Negative growth vectors:**
- Access backlog declined 30.4% YoY. Caterpillar telehandler agreement expiration removes ~$659M in prior revenue.
- JLTV production completed — a revenue gap until next-generation platforms mature.
- Chinese competitors (XCMG, Dingli) gaining share in global aerial work platforms.
- Tariffs create $200M drag in FY2026, equivalent to ~$3/share pre-tax.

Growth in FY2026 is effectively flat revenue with all EPS growth dependent on margin recovery and share count reduction. FY2027 growth requires NGDV profitability inflection, Access recovery, and continued Vocational execution.

## Valuation Assessment

At $129.39, OSK trades at:
- 11.3x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS ($11.50)
- 12.9x trailing EPS ($10.02)
- 9.2x FY2027 consensus EPS ($14.05)
- ~7.6% FCF yield on FY2025 FCF
- ~14.4x forward P/E per the financial data

Against peer medians (P/E 20.8x), OSK appears cheap. But the discount reflects concrete risks: two consecutive earnings misses, negative estimate revisions, $200M tariff headwind, and cyclical downturn in the largest segment. Trinity Industries at 10.3x and GATX at 18.2x bracket the range for heavy transport/industrial names.

The stock currently trades 6.2% below the lowest analyst target ($138), suggesting the Street views current prices as incorporating excessive pessimism. The consensus target of $164.79 (median $168) implies 27-30% upside. However, these targets were likely set before the Q1 miss was fully digested — some may be stale.

The valuation is reasonable if you accept FY2026 guidance, but the track record of two consecutive misses and heavily back-loaded guidance creates a credibility gap. A 12-13x multiple on a more conservative $10-11 EPS (reflecting further tariff/volume pressure) yields $120-143, broadly consistent with the current trading range.

## Competitive Landscape

**Access:** JLG is the global #1 or #2 player in aerial work platforms (alongside Genie/Terex). The competitive position is structurally challenged by Chinese manufacturers who can undercut on price in markets without U.S.-style safety regulatory barriers. North American market share is defensible near-term due to dealer networks and after-market service, but long-term margin pressure is structural as Chinese platforms improve quality. The Caterpillar telehandler agreement loss ($659M revenue) is a specific competitive setback.

**Vocational:** Pierce holds #1 position in North American fire apparatus with genuine competitive advantages: custom manufacturing expertise, premium brand recognition, extensive dealer/service network, and multi-year municipal budgeting cycles that create switching costs. The $6.6B backlog demonstrates this moat. McNeilus in refuse collection holds strong market position. AeroTech benefits from post-COVID airport infrastructure catch-up.

**Transport:** NGDV contract is a near-monopoly for conventional postal vehicles, but Rivian competes for the electric portion. Defense tactical vehicles face increasing competition (AM General, Rheinmetall, GM Defense) and government pressure to retain IP rights (as evidenced by the JLTV IP license requirement). The competitive environment in defense is tightening.

The prior score trajectory showed Competitive Position declining from 7.0 to 6.5. This further decline to 6.0 reflects the Q1 margin collapse in Access (demonstrating inability to pass tariff costs), continued Chinese competitive pressure, and the confirmation that the Caterpillar relationship loss was not replaced.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Impact ($200M / ~$3/share):** The single largest quantifiable near-term risk. Access segment sources globally and cannot fully pass through costs in a competitive market. The IEEPA Supreme Court ruling provided partial relief ($19.7M), but expanded Section 232 tariffs more than offset.

**NGDV Deferred Cost Asset ($813M):** The highest-consequence binary risk. The asset exceeds future profits on existing orders by $125M. Full-year guidance explicitly assumes a follow-on order. If USPS delays or reduces orders (budget pressure, DOGE initiatives, political changes), impairment risk is material — potentially $100-200M write-down.

**Access Cyclical Downturn:** Non-residential construction spending is uncertain. Rental companies (key customers) are moderating fleet expansion. The segment's 10 largest debtors represent 27% of consolidated receivables, and Oshkosh guarantees $580M in customer indebtedness.

**Back-End Loaded Guidance:** ~70% of FY2026 earnings in H2 creates binary quarterly outcomes. A Q2 miss would effectively eliminate the possibility of achieving guidance, likely triggering another significant downward revision.

**Goodwill Risk:** $1.44B in goodwill, ~78% concentrated in JLG/Access. At 11.5-15% discount rates, sustained margin weakness in Access could trigger impairment testing concerns.

### Options Market Signal

The options market presents a mixed but cautiously bearish picture. Near-term IV at 46.1% (vs. 38.6% historical) reflects pricing of continued uncertainty, appropriate given the recent miss. The term structure is in backwardation (46.1% near-term > 40.9% long-term), indicating the market prices imminent stress resolving over time rather than building. Put/call OI ratio at 1.30 is elevated from the prior 0.40 reading, reflecting institutional protection demand post-earnings. Call-heavy skew (-4.8%) on volume suggests some contrarian buying, but the magnitude is modest. The 5.5% short interest with 5.3 days to cover is moderate — enough to indicate conviction sellers but not a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($155-175, +20-35%)
- H2 2026 delivers as guided: Access margins recover to 12-13%, Vocational hits 16%+ margins
- USPS orders additional NGDV tranche, triggering favorable catch-up adjustment ($100M+ in cumulative profit recognition)
- Tariff resolution or pass-through stabilization restores normalized margins
- FY2027 EPS of $14+ materializes, and the stock re-rates to 12-13x = $168-182
- Buyback program (7.6M shares authorized, ~12% of outstanding) provides floor support

### Bear Case ($95-110, -15-27%)
- Q2 misses again, management cuts full-year guidance to $9-10 EPS
- NGDV follow-on order delayed beyond FY2026, triggering partial impairment of deferred costs
- Access margins remain compressed (5-7%) as tariffs persist and Chinese competition intensifies
- Non-residential construction weakens further, Access backlog declines another 20-30%
- Stock re-rates to 10-11x lower EPS ($9-10) = $90-110

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 FY2026 earnings (expected early August) confirms H2 ramp trajectory — Access margins above 8% and consolidated operating income on pace for full-year guidance
2. USPS follow-on NGDV order announced (removes the deferred cost risk and confirms multi-year revenue stream)
3. Stock declines below $110 without fundamental deterioration beyond what is currently known (would create ~10% FCF yield)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 EPS miss combined with guidance cut — would signal management credibility failure and confirm cyclical bottom not yet reached
2. NGDV order cancellation or indefinite delay announced by USPS
3. Access margins remain below 5% for two consecutive quarters (would suggest structural, not cyclical, impairment)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 FY2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) is the critical data point. Management's ability to show sequential margin improvement will determine whether the 70% H2 earnings weighting is achievable.

## Conclusion

Oshkosh is a quality industrial company experiencing a difficult transition year. The Vocational segment is genuinely strong, the backlog provides multi-year visibility, and the valuation at 11.3x guided EPS is undemanding. However, the Q1 miss was severe (-28%), the guidance is heavily back-loaded, tariff headwinds are quantifiably large ($200M), and the NGDV deferred cost asset creates concentrated binary risk.

The prior score trajectory (6.3 → 6.1, BUY → HOLD) captured the beginning of this deterioration. The current analysis confirms the HOLD is appropriate: the stock is not expensive enough to avoid (7.6% FCF yield, below all analyst targets), but the near-term execution risks are too concentrated for conviction on the buy side. Two consecutive misses, negative estimate revisions across all horizons, and a management team that needs to deliver H2 results materially above H1 create a credibility gap that requires proof.

The prudent approach is to wait for Q2 confirmation before re-engaging. If margin recovery materializes, the stock likely re-rates toward $150-170. If it doesn't, the bear case of $95-110 is realistic. At current prices, you are being compensated for risk but not for uncertainty.
