**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

City Holding Company is the holding company for City National Bank of West Virginia, a 96-branch community bank spanning West Virginia, Kentucky, Virginia, and southeastern Ohio with $6.76 billion in assets. Among community banks its size, City is a genuine outlier on quality: 2025 ROAA of 1.97% and ROATCE of 21.2% placed it in the 100th and 95th percentiles of its 20-bank regional peer group, and its 48% efficiency ratio and low-cost retail deposit base (only ~15% uninsured) drive a near-4% net interest margin. Credit is pristine, capital ratios are among the highest in the group (CET1 ~16.9%), and the company returns capital aggressively through a growing dividend and a fresh ~7% buyback authorization.

The problem for the equity is that the operating trajectory has flattened while the multiple has expanded. Reported net interest income peaked at $61.3M in Q3 2025 and has since declined, primarily because $150M of receive-fixed interest-rate swaps matured in October and November 2025. Quarterly EPS ran to a $2.41 peak in Q3 2025 and has since settled around $2.18–$2.20, with buybacks (share count down from 14.65M to 14.11M year over year) doing meaningful lifting. Loans are flat, net new checking-account growth has decelerated to +0.2% YTD from +1.3% in 2025, and the markets City serves are slow-growth, aging, and energy-adjacent.

At $134.85, near the 52-week high of $136.17, the stock trades at roughly 14.9x forward EPS and about 3.0x tangible book value. That is a well-earned premium for a bank generating ~2% ROA, but it sits well above the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.9x and leaves little room for error if margins compress in a cutting cycle or if credit normalizes off exceptionally low levels. This is a high-quality compounder whose return is now dominated by its ~2.6% dividend yield plus buyback, not by re-rating potential. We rate CHCO HOLD as an income-oriented position.

## Company Overview

City Holding operates a single Community Banking segment through City National Bank of West Virginia, headquartered in Charleston. The 96-branch network is concentrated in West Virginia (58 branches), with meaningful footprints in Kentucky (22), Virginia (13), and Ohio (3). The franchise is retail- and consumer-oriented, serving rural and small-community markets alongside mid-size cities including Charleston, Huntington, Martinsburg, Ashland, Lexington, and Winchester. The bank offers deposits, lending, and wealth/trust services.

The competitive core is a premium, low-cost, retail-funded deposit base. Checking and savings accounts fund roughly 60% of assets, only about 15% of deposits are uninsured, and only ~15% of time deposits exceed $250K. This funding profile is the source of the bank's sector-leading efficiency and margin. Loans total $4.50 billion against $5.34 billion of deposits, a conservative 84% loan-to-deposit ratio.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is elite for the peer set.** FY2025 delivered net income of $130.5M, ROAA of 1.97%, ROATCE of 21.2%, an efficiency ratio of 48.2%, and NIM of 3.94%. Q1 2026 produced net income of $31.7M and diluted EPS of $2.20 (up from $2.06 in Q1 2025), with ROA of 1.92%, ROATCE of 19.3%, efficiency of 48.9%, and NIM improving to 3.97%.

**Earnings have plateaued off a Q3-2025 peak, and the reason matters.** Quarterly net income ran Q1'25 $30.3M → Q2 $33.4M → Q3 $35.2M (peak, $2.41 EPS) → Q4 $31.6M → Q1'26 $31.7M. The swap roll-off plus lower reinvestment yields on loans and securities compresses NII, and share buybacks are supporting EPS while operating income is flat. Forward EPS growth therefore depends on loan growth (currently flat) and margin defense, neither of which is trending upward.

**Balance sheet is conservative.** Gross loans of $4.50B were essentially flat sequentially (−0.3% QoQ), with C&I down $12.4M. Deposits grew 0.8% QoQ. The $1.47B AFS securities book carries roughly $109M of gross unrealized losses, producing an AOCI drag of −$81.3M on equity. These are predominantly agency MBS that management does not intend to sell and against which no credit-loss allowance is warranted. It is a rate-driven mark rather than a credit issue, but it depresses tangible book and would matter only in a liquidity-stressed scenario, which is remote given $1.8B of untapped borrowing capacity and $762M of unpledged securities.

**Credit quality is pristine.** NPAs to loans plus OREO stand at 0.27% (improved from 0.32%), ACL/loans is 0.44%, and annualized net charge-offs are 0.07%. The only notable individual credit is a movie-theater C&I loan on nonaccrual since Q3 2024, which took an $0.85M charge-off in Q1'26, leaving ~$5.0M outstanding. The low absolute ACL/loans ratio (0.44%) is a function of the clean book but also means the reserve cushion is thin relative to the CRE concentration if the cycle turns.

**Capital and returns.** Holdco CET1 of 16.9%, Total RBC of 17.3%, and TCE/TA of 9.65% place City among the best-capitalized banks in its group. The dividend is $0.87/quarter ($3.48 annualized, ~39.5% payout, up from $0.79 in early 2025). A new 1,000,000-share (~7%) buyback was authorized in March 2026, with 262K shares repurchased in Q1'26 at ~$117.79. Book value per share is $56.29; tangible book value per share is $45.14.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the story. C&I and consumer balances are declining modestly, and the markets City serves — Appalachian West Virginia and adjacent rural regions — have slow-growth, aging demographics and legacy energy exposure, which structurally caps organic expansion.

Consensus estimates are edging higher but modestly: current-year EPS has been revised to $9.05 from $8.97 ninety days ago (+0.9%), and next-year EPS to $9.50 from $9.45 ninety days ago (+0.5%). These are stable, low-single-digit revision trends that fall well short of signaling a structural earnings acceleration. The earnings-surprise record is solid but decelerating: beats of 14.7% and 9.6% in mid-2025 gave way to a 5.5% miss in Q4'25 and a razor-thin 0.5% beat in Q1'26, consistent with the swap-driven NII plateau.

The realistic growth engine over the next 12–24 months is a combination of continued buybacks reducing share count and modest margin defense, supplemented by potential M&A. City has an acquisitive history (Citizens Commerce Bancshares in 2023) and the capital to be a consolidator in WV/KY/VA. A well-priced acquisition would be the most credible path to reaccelerating earnings, but none is announced. Growth Prospects score of 4.5 reflects flat loans, slow-growth markets, and only modest estimate revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $134.85, CHCO trades at approximately 14.9x forward EPS (consensus $9.05 current year, $9.50 next year) and roughly 2.4x book / ~3.0x tangible book value. Against the Regional Banks peer median P/E of 12.9x, City carries a ~15% premium, and its multiple sits well above cheaper comparables like OFG (9.8x) and CASH (9.9x).

That premium is defensible on fundamentals — a bank earning ~2% ROA and ~20% ROATCE with a 48% efficiency ratio deserves to trade above the group. The question for the valuation score is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside, and the answer is no. The multiple is full, the stock sits at the top of its 52-week range ($113.21–$136.17), earnings are flat off a hedge-supported peak, and estimate revisions are running at less than 1%, short of the velocity that would justify looking through a full multiple. Cheapness elsewhere in the sector has historically been a value trap, and City is a high-quality name priced for its quality.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly — low $125, mean $131.40, median $132.00, high $135.00 — with the stock already trading above the mean and at the high. On a full-multiple, low-growth bank, these targets are appropriately clustered around fair value. The dividend yield plus buyback provides absolute-return support, which is the primary reason to own the shares, but it does not create a mispricing. Valuation score of 4.5: fairly-to-fully valued, no identifiable catalyst for re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

City is a clear outperformer within its 20-bank regional peer group ($3–10B assets, including names like Community Trust, Stock Yards, German American, Lakeland, Peoples, S&T, and Tompkins). Its 100th-percentile ROAA and 95th-percentile ROATCE rankings reflect a durable structural advantage rooted in deposit-funding cost and expense discipline, not a cyclical fluke. The retail deposit franchise is difficult to replicate and provides genuine moat characteristics in a rising-cost-of-funds environment.

The trajectory of that competitive position is stable rather than improving. Market share is not visibly eroding, and the deposit franchise remains intact, but the bank is not gaining ground either: loans are flat, DDA account growth has decelerated, and the addressable markets are slow-growth. City also faces the same structural headwind as all community banks from non-bank and fintech lenders, an explicitly cited risk. Competitive Position score of 6.0 reflects a strong, defensible franchise on a flat trajectory rather than a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest-rate and margin sensitivity.** The maturity of the swaps already pressured NII in H2 2025. The bank's own model shows a −300bp rate shock reducing NII by ~12.4%; the balance sheet is modestly liability-sensitive to the downside in a cutting cycle. Margin is the single largest earnings driver, and the current macro environment leaves the direction two-sided.

**CRE concentration.** Commercial real estate is $1.87B (~42% of loans), including hotels ($396M, 8.8% of loans, with a ~$22M substandard bucket) and non-residential lessors. Credit is clean today, but hospitality and CRE are the areas most likely to normalize first if the commercial environment weakens. The thin 0.44% ACL/loans ratio provides limited cushion against a CRE downturn.

**Geographic and demographic concentration** in Appalachian markets tied to slow growth, aging demographics, and legacy energy exposure limits both loan growth and diversification.

**AOCI drag** suppresses tangible equity; a higher-for-longer scenario extends the recovery timeline, though it is not a solvency or liquidity concern.

**Governance and succession.** The CEO (63) has led since 2005 and retains a legacy change-in-control package with a 147.5% excise-tax gross-up, a governance feature most peers have eliminated. EVP DeRito (76) retired at end-2025, the board is aging (several directors 69–76), and a May 2026 8-K referencing a "Bumgarner Appointment" (the 61-year-old CFO), which the underlying research could not independently confirm as to its exact nature (promotion vs. role change), points to an internal management change that bears watching. These are manageable rather than acute risks.

These risks are largely priced into a stable, low-multiple-premium bank stock, which supports a middle-of-the-road Risk Factors score of 6.0. There is no binary regulatory or litigation catalyst, but the CRE concentration and margin sensitivity are real, unpriced-at-the-margin exposures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.3% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 19.7%, and the term structure is in backwardation (33.3% near-term vs 20.3% at six months), signaling the market is pricing near-term stress. The put/call skew of 16.4% indicates elevated put demand. ATM IV has risen sharply from 24.4% to 39.0% since late May even as the stock climbed 8.1%, a combination that suggests hedging into the run-up rather than complacency. For a HOLD, this argues against chasing the stock at the 52-week high and supports patience for a pullback.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** City continues to compound tangible book value at ~20% ROATCE, defends its margin through disciplined deposit pricing as the curve normalizes, and deploys its ~17% CET1 into an accretive WV/KY/VA acquisition that reaccelerates earnings. Continued buybacks shrink the share count, the dividend keeps growing off a conservative ~40% payout, and the premium multiple holds or expands modestly. In this scenario the stock reaches roughly $150 (16x $9.50 next-year EPS), an ~11% gain plus dividend.

**Bear Case.** Rate cuts compress NIM faster than deposit costs can reprice down (the −300bp/−12.4% NII sensitivity), loan growth stays flat, CRE/hotel credit normalizes off pristine levels and forces reserve build, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~13x. On flat EPS of ~$8.90 at 13x, the stock falls to roughly $116, about −14% from current levels, with the dividend cushioning total return.

**Base Case.** Earnings hold roughly flat to low-single-digit growth as buybacks offset a modestly compressing margin, credit stays clean, and the multiple holds near current levels. The stock delivers its dividend plus buyback yield with limited price appreciation, landing around $132–$138 over the next twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July), which will show whether NIM has stabilized post-swap-maturity and whether loan growth has resumed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$120s, restoring a dividend-plus-buyback yield with a margin of safety and a P/E closer to ~13x on forward EPS.
- Evidence of reaccelerating loan growth (>3% annualized) or NIM stabilizing/expanding above 4.0% for two consecutive quarters, breaking the post-swap plateau.
- An accretive, sensibly-priced acquisition that adds a durable earnings-growth vector.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM compression accelerating (below ~3.7%) in a cutting cycle without offsetting deposit repricing.
- Material CRE/hotel credit deterioration — a rise in NPAs above ~0.60% or a reserve build that meaningfully lifts ACL/loans.
- A dilutive or richly-priced acquisition (>2x tangible book on the target with weak deposit quality).

## Conclusion

City Holding is one of the highest-quality community banks in the country, with elite returns, a low-cost retail deposit franchise, pristine credit, and a fortress capital position. None of that is in dispute. The issue is price and trajectory: the stock sits at its 52-week high, trades at a full ~15x forward EPS and ~3.0x tangible book, and its earnings have plateaued off a hedge-supported Q3-2025 peak while loans are flat and estimate revisions are running below 1%. The premium is earned, but it is fully reflected, and the near-term options market is pricing stress into the next print.

We rate CHCO **HOLD** with an income-hold rationale. The durable return here is the ~2.6% dividend plus buyback yield from a bank that will keep compounding tangible book: a reasonable core holding for income-oriented investors, but not a source of asymmetric appreciation at today's price. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$120s or on clear evidence that margin and loan growth have reaccelerated.
