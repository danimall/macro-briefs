**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Qorvo ceased to be a standalone equity investment on October 27, 2025, when it signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Skyworks Solutions (SWKS). Under the terms, each QRVO share converts into 0.960 Skyworks shares plus $32.50 in cash, with QRVO holders owning roughly 37% of the combined company. Both companies' shareholders approved the transaction on February 11, 2026. As a result, the stock trades primarily as a function of the deal spread and the probability of close, not on standalone multiples or DCF. Any valuation framework must weight P(close) × implied deal value against P(break) × standalone value.

At $87.57, QRVO sits below the implied deal value (0.960 × SWKS price + $32.50), and the gap reflects two things: the time value to close and the antitrust break risk. The FTC issued a Second Request on February 5, 2026, extending the HSR waiting period, and substantive scrutiny is expected for a transaction that consolidates the #1 and #3 players in premium RF front-end modules. The merger agreement's regulatory-commitment structure is weak: Skyworks' divestiture obligation is capped at product lines representing under $100M in annual revenue, with an explicit carve-out for any remedy "material to the combined company." If the FTC demands material divestitures, Skyworks is not obligated to accept them. The break fee payable to Qorvo on an antitrust failure is only $100M, modest against the deal size.

The offsetting comfort is that the underlying business has genuinely improved. FY2026 gross margin expanded 460 bps to 45.9%, operating income rose to $411M from $95M, EPS jumped to $3.62 from $0.58, and free cash flow reached $680M (an 18.5% FCF margin). This gives the "deal breaks" scenario a firmer floor than most merger targets carry, though ~50% revenue concentration in Apple caps the standalone upside. We rate QRVO HOLD (uncertain): a hold-to-catalyst position where the dominant variable is a binary regulatory outcome that neither we nor management control.

## Company Overview

Qorvo is a global supplier of RF, analog/mixed-signal, and power semiconductor solutions, organized into three reportable segments (FY2026 revenue $3.68B):

- **Advanced Cellular Group (ACG)** — $2.55B (69% of revenue). RF front-end modules for smartphones, wearables, laptops, and tablets. This is the profit engine, with operating margin of 26.2% in FY26 (up from 23.1%), heavily tied to Apple and Samsung.
- **High Performance Analog (HPA)** — $706M (19%). RF, analog mixed-signal, and power management for defense/aerospace, infrastructure (base stations, DOCSIS 4.0), and industrial markets. Revenue grew 10.7% and operating margin rose to 26.8% from 17.1%, the cleanest growth story.
- **Connectivity and Sensors Group (CSG)** — $422M (11%). UWB, Matter, BLE, Zigbee, Thread, Wi-Fi, and cellular IoT. Operating loss of $42M (narrowing from -$56M); this segment triggered a $36.5M goodwill and $45.9M intangible impairment in FY26.

Qorvo, Skyworks, and Broadcom form the dominant triopoly in premium-smartphone RF front-end modules, with Murata a significant player in filters. The merger's strategic rationale is to consolidate two of these three players into a stronger combined entity capable of countering chipset vendors (Qualcomm, MediaTek) that increasingly integrate RF into their reference designs, plus pricing pressure in mass-market Android.

## Financial Analysis

FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) is a margin-driven turnaround executed by deliberately shrinking the low-margin business:

- **Revenue $3,678.5M**, down 1.1% YoY, reflecting a planned exit from low-margin mass-market Android.
- **Gross margin 45.9%** vs. 41.3% prior year, a +460 bps improvement driven by the Android exit and favorable HPA mix.
- **Operating income $411.4M** (11.2% margin) vs. $95.5M (2.6%), aided by lower impairments ($82M vs. $193M).
- **Diluted EPS $3.62** vs. $0.58.
- **Operating cash flow $808.6M** (up from $622M); **FCF $679.6M** (18.5% margin).
- Q3 FY26 (Dec 27, 2025) accelerated: revenue +8.4% YoY, gross margin 46.7%, operating margin 19.4%, EPS $1.75.

The earnings-surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats, including a 37.3% beat in the March 2026 quarter and 49.2% in June 2025. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-quarter EPS revised +8.0% over 30 days, next-quarter +3.9%, and current-year +1.8% over 90 days. These revisions confirm the operational turnaround, though in a merger context their significance is muted; the stock does not re-rate on standalone earnings momentum while it trades as a deal.

The balance sheet is solid for a semiconductor business. Cash was $1.22B at FY26 year-end, but roughly $1.0–1.13B sits offshore ($808–950M in Singapore), and repatriation would trigger taxes, relevant to any standalone capital-return math. Debt is $1.55B in senior notes (4.375% due 2029, 3.375% due 2031), D/E 0.74, current ratio 3.24, no revolver borrowings, in compliance with all covenants. The Altman Z-Score of 2.81 sits in the grey zone, weighed down by $2.35B of goodwill (~40% of assets). Qorvo repurchased $536.7M of stock in FY26, suspended buybacks on deal signing, then resumed within merger-agreement limits, with ~$416M remaining authorized. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is a secondary consideration given the pending acquisition, but it matters for the deal-break scenario. The top line contracted 1.1% in FY26 by design: management is trading revenue for margin, exiting mass-market Android to concentrate on higher-value content. The strategy is working on margins but shrinks the base and increases dependence on Apple, which reached ~50% of revenue in FY26 (up from 47%), with Samsung at ~10% (top two = 59%).

The genuine growth vector is HPA: defense/aerospace, DOCSIS 4.0 cable infrastructure, base stations, and industrial. This diversification reduces smartphone cyclicality and forms the core of the long-term bull case, but for the combined company post-merger, not for standalone QRVO. CSG remains a value-destroyer and a likely candidate for rationalization in a combined entity.

The estimate-revision picture is positive but modest: current-year consensus of $6.90 EPS is up 1.8% over 90 days, next-year $7.83 down 1.5%. These do not clear the threshold of a re-rating catalyst, and in any case the merger caps standalone re-rating. We score growth prospects at 5.0: a business improving on margins and defense mix, but shrinking on the top line with heavy customer concentration and a growth thesis that mostly accrues to the acquirer.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer table underscores how meaningless standalone multiples are here. The "peer median" P/E of 494.4 is distorted by loss-making comps (MXL at -53.2, SLAB at -142.8, ALGM at -595.7) and a single high-multiple outlier (CBRS at 494.4). QRVO's trailing/forward P/E of ~24x on $3.62 EPS is unexceptional but essentially irrelevant, because the stock does not price on its own earnings; it prices on the deal.

The relevant valuation exercise is: P(close) × implied deal value + P(break) × standalone value. The deal value is 0.960 × SWKS price + $32.50. At $87.57, QRVO trades below the current implied deal value, and the discount compensates holders for (a) time to close (an outside date of April 27, 2027, extendable to October 2027, though a BNP Paribas note flags a possible end-2026 close) and (b) the antitrust break risk. Because the stock component is a fixed exchange ratio and unhedged, QRVO holders bear Skyworks' share-price risk between now and close.

On a standalone basis, if the deal breaks, we estimate fair value in the $70–80 range: a modest premium to the pre-deal trading range on the strength of the FY26 margin turnaround, discounted for the loss of the acquisition premium and the Apple concentration. The analyst target range ($66 low, $120 high, $91 mean) brackets both the break-scenario floor and the deal-close ceiling. We assign a valuation score of 5.5, reflecting a modestly positive spread to implied deal value offset by meaningful break risk and the fact that a "cheap" appearance here is a function of deal probability, not intrinsic mispricing. The equity-lessons caution against rewarding cheapness without an independent catalyst applies squarely: the only catalyst is regulatory clearance, and that is binary.

## Competitive Landscape

Qorvo occupies a strong position in a consolidated oligopoly. Qorvo's crown-jewel ACG segment carries a 26.2% operating margin and premium content at Apple and Samsung.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The deliberate exit from mass-market Android improves margins and defends the premium position. But the central competitive threat is real: chipset vendors like Qualcomm and MediaTek increasingly integrate their own RF into reference designs, structurally squeezing standalone RF vendors. The merger with Skyworks is itself an acknowledgment that scale is needed to defend against this. HPA's defense and infrastructure diversification lowers reliance on smartphone cycles.

Level is strong; trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding for standalone Qorvo, which is precisely why a sale to Skyworks emerged. We score competitive position at 5.5: a leader in a defensible triopoly, but facing integration pressure from chipset vendors and dependent on a single customer for half its revenue.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is now dominated by deal-specific, binary considerations:

1. **Antitrust is the crux.** The FTC Second Request confirms substantive scrutiny. The merger agreement's weak remedy structure — a divestiture cap of under $100M in revenue and a carve-out for anything "material to the combined company" — means Skyworks can walk if the FTC demands more, paying Qorvo only $100M. This asymmetry ($100M antitrust break fee vs. $298.7M superior-proposal fee) is the single most important risk.

2. **Fixed exchange ratio.** The 0.960 stock component is unhedged; QRVO's value floats with SWKS.

3. **No-shop lock-up.** Because shareholders already approved, Qorvo cannot pursue a superior proposal; there is no upside from a competing bid.

4. **Standalone business risks (relevant only if the deal breaks):** extreme customer concentration (Apple ~50%, top two 59%), CSG operating losses and impairments, ~13% China/tariff exposure with power-management demand already hit, and SAW filter manufacturing transition from North Carolina to Texas ($15M cost in FY26).

Because the dominant risk is a binary regulatory outcome with an asymmetric break structure that the market is only partially pricing, we score risk factors at 4.5. This reflects a specific, dated, asymmetric catalyst rather than a generic sector adjustment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.7% is rich against 1-year historical volatility of 35.8% (a 14.9-point spread), consistent with a market pricing elevated event uncertainty around the regulatory timeline. The term structure is roughly flat (50.7% near vs. 49.8% far), with the 3-month tenor bulging to 57.1%, concentrating uncertainty around the mid-2026 window when a regulatory resolution could land. Put/call skew is balanced (2.8% near-term), and put/call volume and OI ratios are extremely low (0.03 and 0.02), indicating no aggressive downside hedging despite the deal-break risk. The implied ±8.3% near-term move is modest for a binary event, suggesting the market currently assigns high probability to the deal proceeding. IV has fallen from 54.6% to 50.7% since late May even as the price fell 19.1%, consistent with a spread that has widened on price rather than a spike in perceived deal-break risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The deal closes, potentially ahead of schedule. QRVO holders receive 0.960 SWKS shares + $32.50 cash, and the spread to current price converges to zero and then tracks Skyworks' stock. If Skyworks re-rates on merger synergies (consolidating two of three RF players, rationalizing CSG, defending premium content), the combined-company value exceeds the current implied deal value. The FY26 turnaround supports the combined entity's earnings power. Bull target ~$110–120, tracking Skyworks appreciation plus full spread capture.

### Bear Case
The FTC demands material divestitures beyond the <$100M cap, Skyworks exercises its right to walk, and Qorvo collects only a $100M break fee. The stock reverts to standalone value, which we estimate at $70–80 given the margin improvement offset by loss of the premium and Apple concentration. Alternatively, the deal closes but Skyworks' stock falls (fixed-ratio risk), diluting realized value. Bear target ~$70, roughly -20% from current.

### Base Case
The deal closes on approximately the current timeline, with modest divestitures within the agreed cap. QRVO holders realize implied deal value converging over 6–12 months. Base target ~$95, tracking implied deal value with spread compression, roughly +8% from current, though the realized value depends on Skyworks' share price at close.

The Starboard fingerprints reinforce the deal-completion thesis: board seat (Aug 2025) → merger (Oct 2025) → Voting and Support Agreement with Skyworks, an activist-driven sale. Director Peter Feld's ~1.9M-share sale (~$192M) on June 2, 2026 at $100–103, and the CEO's ~58,000-share sale at ~$100 on June 1, are best read as monetizing into strength while the spread was narrow rather than a bet against the deal, though a $190M+ activist trim while retaining ~5.6M shares reduces insider skin in the game near current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a hold-to-catalyst position. The reassessment points are the regulatory milestones, not earnings.

**Upgrade triggers (to a spread-capture BUY):**
- FTC clearance or a consent decree with divestitures within the agreed cap, removing the primary break risk.
- Confirmed guidance (from the companies, not third-tier notes) of an accelerated close, compressing the arb timeline and improving the annualized return on the spread.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FTC litigation to block, or a demand for divestitures materially exceeding the <$100M cap that Skyworks declines, signaling likely deal termination and reversion toward $70–80.
- A sharp decline in SWKS shares that erodes implied deal value below standalone value, making the fixed-ratio risk unfavorable.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next 6–12 months, tied to the HSR/FTC resolution. The outside date is April 27, 2027 (extendable to October 27, 2027); the practical window for resolution is the second half of 2026.

## Conclusion

QRVO is a merger-arbitrage position where the dominant variable is a binary FTC outcome that the merger agreement's weak remedy structure leaves genuinely two-sided. At $87.57, holders capture a modest positive spread to implied deal value while bearing antitrust break risk (with only a $100M cushion), fixed-ratio exposure to Skyworks, and — if the deal breaks — reversion to a standalone value we estimate at $70–80. The underlying business has improved materially, which provides a firmer-than-usual downside floor for a target and de-risks the break scenario, though ~50% Apple concentration caps standalone upside.

The score has improved from 4.5 to 5.5 over the past several months, driven by the FY26 financial turnaround (financial health rose to reflect the strengthened balance sheet and cash generation) and confirmation that the deal is progressing (the June 2026 Skyworks debt-exchange offer is a concrete integration step). Our current read confirms that improving trajectory on the fundamentals while holding the recommendation at HOLD, because the recommendation is now governed by a regulatory outcome rather than by business performance. We classify this as an uncertain hold: we lack sufficient conviction for a spread-capture BUY (the antitrust remedy cap is a real flag) or an AVOID (the business floor is solid and options positioning implies high deal-completion odds). Investors comfortable with event-driven risk can hold for spread convergence; those without a view on the FTC outcome should stand aside until the regulatory path clarifies.
