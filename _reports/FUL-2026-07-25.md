**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H.B. Fuller is the world's largest pure-play adhesives company, a 138-year-old Minnesota-based formulator selling into hygiene, packaging, medical, electronics, transportation, clean energy, and construction end-markets across 34 countries. The investment story today rests on two competing forces. On one side, the company is executing a deliberate rotation of capital toward its highest-margin, highest-growth franchises (medical adhesives and engineering adhesives), and this mix-shift is beginning to show in the numbers, with the Hygiene, Health & Consumable segment posting a 230 bps EBITDA-margin jump to 17.9% in Q2 FY2026 and consensus EPS estimates rising 3-5% across all windows over the past 90 days. On the other side, organic growth is being carried entirely by price and currency: H1 FY2026 organic revenue fell 1.7% with unit volumes down 3.5%. The market's negative reaction to a Q2 beat (the stock fell roughly 5%) reflects a correct focus on the quality and durability of that growth.

The defining recent event is the June 25, 2026 announcement of an all-cash offer for Advanced Medical Solutions Group plc (LSE:AMS), a UK surgical-adhesives and wound-closure company, at ~£715M ($942M) enterprise value. This roughly doubles down on a medical-adhesives strategy already begun with the 2025 GEM/Medifill acquisitions (~$197M). The deal is financed via bridge facilities of up to $3.0B, on top of pre-existing debt of ~$2.1B. Strategically the logic is sound: medical adhesives carry higher margins and lower cyclicality than commoditized construction lines. Financially it front-loads integration and leverage risk at a moment when the base business is not growing in units.

The stock at $55.70 trades at roughly 11.4x the FY2026 consensus of $4.87 and ~10.5x the FY2027 estimate of $5.32, a meaningful discount to the 21.3x specialty-chemicals peer median. That discount is not obviously a mispricing. It compensates for declining volumes, rising pro-forma leverage, and the absence of an independent near-term catalyst to close the gap. We rate FUL a HOLD. The score has drifted modestly higher over the past several months (5.4 → 5.6), and this report holds that trajectory rather than extending it, given that a fourth consecutive HOLD warrants a higher bar for change and none of the underlying tensions (volumes, leverage, integration) have resolved.

## Company Overview

H.B. Fuller (founded 1887, incorporated in Minnesota 1915) is a global manufacturer of adhesives, sealants, and specialty chemicals. The business is structurally diversified: no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and products embed into customer manufacturing processes (diapers, food and beverage packaging, medical devices, electronics, solar, roofing), which creates switching costs and recurring demand. Roughly 56% of revenue (~$2.0B) is generated outside the United States.

The company reorganized into three reportable segments in FY2025:

- **Hygiene, Health & Consumable Adhesives (HHC)** — ~45% of revenue; packaging, nonwovens/hygiene, health and beauty, and the growing Medical Adhesives Technologies franchise. EBITDA margin ~16-18%, expanding.
- **Engineering Adhesives (EA)** — ~30% of revenue; highest-margin segment at ~21-22% EBITDA. Transportation, electronics, clean energy, aerospace/defense.
- **Building Adhesive Solutions (BAS)** — ~25% of revenue; ~15% EBITDA margin. Roofing, infrastructure, insulating glass, woodworking. Formed in FY2025 by combining construction businesses; the North America Flooring line was divested (the source of the grout litigation discussed below).

Raw materials account for roughly 75% of cost of goods sold, mostly petroleum and natural-gas derivatives, so gross margins are highly sensitive to input costs and pricing discipline. CEO Celeste Mastin has led since December 2022; CFO John Corkrean has held the seat since 2016.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended November 29, 2025).** Revenue was $3,473.6M, down 2.7% YoY and flat organically (+0.8% price, -0.8% volume, -2.1% M&A/divestiture, -0.6% FX). Gross margin expanded 130 bps to 31.1% on pricing, lower raw-material costs, and restructuring benefits. Net income rose 16.7% to $152.0M; diluted EPS was $2.75 versus $2.30. Adjusted EBITDA was $620.7M (17.9% margin, +4.5%). Operating cash flow declined to $263.5M from $302.4M, and free cash flow fell to $121.2M from $163.2M, pressured by $51.9M of working-capital use and the litigation accrual.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended May 30, 2026).** Revenue was $950.3M, up 5.8% (+2.6% organic, +3.1% FX), with growth driven by +3.0% pricing against -0.4% volume. Diluted EPS was $1.23 versus $0.76 a year earlier; adjusted EBITDA of $181.0M grew 9.3% at a 19.1% margin. The HHC margin jump is the clearest evidence the medical-adhesives mix-shift is reaching the P&L. BAS was strong (+6.2% organic); EA was soft (-1.0% organic). The result beat the ~$926M consensus.

The reason for the stock's reaction is the volume story. Over the six-month period, volumes fell 3.5%. Growth is being manufactured by price and currency, not units. This is the central tension in the fundamentals: reported growth looks acceptable, underlying demand does not. The mechanism matters: price-led growth in a specialty-chemical business with 75% raw-material COGS can persist while input costs are stable, but it is not durable if end-market demand keeps contracting, because pricing power erodes when customers face volume pressure of their own.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was ~$2,072M at Q2 FY2026 against $114M cash and $2,082M equity, putting total debt-to-capital near 50%. The debt stack includes Term Loan A ($431M, maturing February 2028), Term Loan B ($979M, February 2030), $300M of 4.0% notes (February 2027), and $300M of 4.25% notes (October 2028), plus a ~$700M revolver. Annual interest expense runs ~$133M, and a 1-point move in floating rates equates to ~$6.6M (partly hedged via $800M of fixed swaps).

The Altman Z-Score of 1.7 nominally flags "distress," but this is a false positive. It is driven by acquisition-funded leverage plus a near-zero GAAP EBIT/assets ratio distorted by heavy intangible amortization (~$178M D&A) and the one-time $34.8M FY2025 litigation charge. The covenant picture is comfortable: secured leverage of 2.3x against a 4.5x maximum, interest coverage of 5.2x against a 2.0x minimum. This is a leveraged but stable cash generator, not a bankruptcy candidate. Goodwill ($1.69B) plus intangibles ($0.77B) represent ~48% of assets, reflecting the acquisition-heavy model; the 2025 impairment test showed 33-80% headroom.

The forward leverage question is the one that matters. The AMS acquisition, bridge-financed via facilities of up to $3.0B, will push pro-forma leverage up materially from the current 2.3x secured. Management guides FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $650-675M. On ~$715M of incremental enterprise value, pro-forma net-debt-to-EBITDA plausibly moves toward the mid-3x range before synergies and any equity contribution, still inside covenants but leaving less cushion and pushing more of the free cash flow toward interest at a higher-for-longer rate path.

**Financial health score: 5.5.** The company generates reliable EBITDA and free cash flow and is comfortably covenant-compliant, which supports a middling score. But the deteriorating volume trend, the step-up in leverage for AMS, and the fact that H1 FCF was only $12.8M (seasonally low, with capex stepped up to $104M from $64.5M) keep this from rising. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator in our framework, and FUL sits in the ambiguous middle: not distressed, not fortress.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is a mix-shift story, not a volume story. Engineering Adhesives already earns ~22% EBITDA margins versus ~15-16% for the other segments, and management is explicitly rotating capital toward EA and medical adhesives while divesting commoditized lines (NA Flooring). The Q2 HHC margin jump is early proof the strategy works at the segment level.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-quarter consensus rose 4.8%, next-quarter 4.0%, current-year 3.2%, and next-year 3.6% over the past 90 days, and the revisions over the past 30 days match those 90-day moves. This is genuine positive earnings momentum, but it falls well short of the +30% revision velocity that signals a true inflection. It is a company beating modestly and guiding up modestly, not one at a cyclical trough with earnings tripling.

The durability question is whether volumes inflect. The adhesives industry is mature, fragmented, and cyclical, tied to industrial production, consumer staples (defensive), durable goods, and construction (cyclical). FUL is offsetting weak demand with price and portfolio mix. If end-markets recover, the mix-shift compounds on a growing volume base and the growth thesis strengthens materially. If volumes keep contracting, pricing power fades and the reported-growth cushion thins.

The AMS acquisition is the larger growth lever. AMS brings surgical adhesives, wound closure, and dressings — higher-margin, higher-growth, less-cyclical medical revenue. If integrated on plan, it structurally lifts consolidated margins and reduces the cyclicality that currently caps the multiple. That is a real prize, but it is a CY2027 story at the earliest given the end-of-CY2026 expected close, and it carries integration risk on top of the ongoing Project ONE ERP rollout ($300-320M total, still deploying into Brazil/Asia).

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.** Positive estimate revisions and a credible mix-shift support the score; declining unit volumes and the fact that the transformational growth from AMS is more than a year out, and contingent on close and integration, cap it. Our framework treats growth as a directional winner-selector only alongside financial health, and here the growth signal is real but thin.

## Valuation Assessment

At $55.70, FUL trades at ~11.4x FY2026 consensus EPS and ~10.5x FY2027. Against the specialty-chemicals peer median P/E of 21.3x, this is roughly a 45-50% discount. The peer set is heterogeneous — Quaker Houghton at 581x (distorted trough earnings), Avient at 21.3x, Ingevity negative, Minerals Technologies at 14.9x, Innospec at 18.8x — so the median overstates the cleanest comparable. The more relevant comps (MTX at 14.9x, IOSP at 18.8x) still sit above FUL.

The instinct is to treat this discount as a buying opportunity, but our framework is deliberately skeptical of cheapness as a standalone signal: low multiples in this environment have more often marked value traps than mispricings, and the burden is on identifying why the market is wrong. Here the discount is largely explained rather than mispriced: declining organic volumes, rising pro-forma leverage, and a large acquisition that adds risk before it adds earnings. The 1.7% dividend yield provides some absolute support (the company has a ~7.7% five-year dividend growth rate and Dividend King ambitions), but buybacks will likely slow while AMS is digested, removing a per-share tailwind.

The stock has declined ~19% from its February 2026 high of $68.63 and sits near the middle of its 52-week range ($48.71-$68.63). That drawdown coincided with the post-Q2 volume reaction and the deal announcement, so it reflects genuine new information (leverage step-up, volume softness), not indiscriminate selling. The analyst consensus target range of $67-80 (mean ~$73, median $75) implies 20%+ upside, and sentiment is improving (the ratings mix shifted toward Buy over the past three months). But those targets substantially pre-date full digestion of the leverage-financed AMS deal and are more likely to compress toward spot than to pull the price up, given the absence of a near-term catalyst.

**Valuation score: 5.8.** The forward multiple is genuinely low in absolute terms and relative to cleaner peers, and the estimate-revision trend is positive, which argues against a pure value-trap read. But without an independent catalyst to close the discount — and with leverage and volumes as active overhangs — this is not asymmetric enough to warrant a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

FUL's competitive position is stable-to-improving, which is the more favorable end of the trajectory framework. The company competes on global reach, portfolio breadth, and technical service against large multinationals (Henkel, Arkema/Bostik) and regional specialists. Its scale advantage is real for multinational customers requiring consistent global supply, and the embedded, spec-in nature of adhesives creates switching costs.

The trajectory is improving at the margin because the deliberate mix-shift is upgrading the revenue base. Divesting NA Flooring, acquiring GEM/Medifill, and now pursuing AMS all move the portfolio toward higher-margin, higher-growth, less-cyclical medical and engineering adhesives. The HHC margin jump and the 22% EA margins are evidence this is working. Against this, the volume decline suggests share is not being gained in the core cyclical lines: the improvement is coming from mix, not from taking volume from competitors, which is likely an industry-wide demand phenomenon rather than a FUL-specific weakness.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** The moat is durable and the portfolio trajectory is genuinely improving through capital rotation. We temper the score because the recent competitive-position signal has been mildly anti-predictive in our framework and because the improvement is mix-driven rather than volume-share-driven.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume/demand softness** is the most important near-term risk. Organic growth is price- and FX-driven. A weak durable-goods and construction cycle would pressure this further, and pricing power erodes when customers face their own volume declines.

**Leverage step-up for AMS** is the dominant new risk. The bridge (364-day maturity) must be termed out with permanent debt or notes, and the cost and covenant headroom of that refinancing is a key item to monitor.

**Raw-material cost sensitivity** is structural: 75% of COGS, with a 1% change equating to ~$12.6M net income / ~$0.23 EPS. In an inflationary spike, pricing lags and margins compress, given the petroleum/natural-gas feedstock base.

**FX exposure** — 56% of revenue offshore, with volatile Turkish lira, Egyptian pound, Brazilian real, and Chinese renminbi exposures. FX was a -$20.1M revenue headwind in FY2025 and a tailwind in H1'26; it cuts both ways.

**Litigation** — the Rouse grout settlement (up to $75M, $34.8M accrued, preliminary court approval June 10, 2026) stems from a divested business, with insurance recovery being pursued. Legacy asbestos is largely indemnified/insured. Emerging PFAS regulatory exposure is a longer-tail watch item. None is thesis-changing today.

**Execution risk** on the Project ONE ERP rollout layered on top of the AMS integration is a genuine concentration of management bandwidth.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm and does not confirm a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 24.8% sits well below the 33.7% one-year historical volatility (a -9.0% spread, cheap), in the 5th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure (near-term 24.8% below long-term 34.0%), a normal, complacent structure. ATM IV collapsed from 38.3% to 24.8% as the price fell 13.1% since late June, indicating the market has processed the Q2 print and deal announcement and is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-7.5% near-term), not defensive. This complacency is a double-edged read: cheap IV would favor entry timing if we had a directional conviction, but it also signals no informed positioning ahead of a near-term move. Short interest is modest at 6.1% of float (2.3 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

**Risk factors score: 4.8.** The combination of specific, near-term downside catalysts — volume deterioration, a leverage-financed acquisition requiring a term-out, contained-but-live litigation, and raw-material/FX sensitivity — outweighs the calm options positioning. These are idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in a low-multiple, calm-IV stock. This is the trajectory dimension that improved from 4.5 to 5.0 in the prior report; the AMS leverage overhang argues against extending that improvement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($67-80).** Unit volumes inflect positively in H2 FY2026 / FY2027 as industrial and construction end-markets recover, allowing the mix-shift to compound on a growing base. The AMS acquisition closes on plan by end-CY2026 and integrates cleanly, structurally lifting consolidated margins toward the high-teens/low-20s and reducing cyclicality. EBITDA reaches the upper end of the $650-675M FY2026 guide and grows from there. Estimate revisions continue their positive trend, and consensus targets are realized as the market re-rates a lower-cyclicality medical-adhesives platform toward peer multiples. At 15x the FY2027 estimate of $5.32, the stock is ~$80.

**Bear case ($44-48).** Volumes keep contracting (worse than -3.5%), pricing power fades as customers push back, and organic revenue turns decisively negative. The AMS bridge is termed out at higher rates than expected, pushing pro-forma leverage toward the top of the comfortable range and pressuring free cash flow. Integration stumbles, and the medical-adhesives margin promise is delayed. Raw-material costs spike, compressing gross margin before pricing catches up. The stock revisits its 52-week low of $48.71 or below, toward $44-48, roughly 20% downside.

**Base case ($60-64).** Volumes stabilize but do not meaningfully recover; price and mix carry low-single-digit reported growth. AMS closes and begins to contribute, but the leverage overhang caps the multiple for several quarters. FY2026 EBITDA lands mid-guide (~$660M), FY2027 EPS approaches the $5.32 consensus. The stock re-rates modestly from 11x toward ~12x forward as the term-out completes and covenant headroom becomes visible, reaching ~$62-64. This is ~11-15% upside to the midpoint over 12-18 months, roughly matched by the ~20% downside to the bear case: a symmetric-to-slightly-favorable but not compelling risk/reward.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the analysis deliberately holds rather than extends the modest upward score drift, because none of the core tensions (volumes, leverage, integration) has resolved and the bar for a fourth unchanged call is higher.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Organic volume growth turns positive on the Q3 or Q4 FY2026 print (from the current -3.5% H1 trend), confirming the mix-shift is compounding on a stable base rather than masking demand erosion.
2. The AMS bridge is termed out with permanent financing at a defined cost that keeps pro-forma net leverage below ~3.5x with visible covenant headroom, and the deal receives regulatory and AMS shareholder approval on schedule.
3. Estimate revisions accelerate materially (approaching +15-30% over 90 days) alongside continued margin expansion — the signal that would flag a genuine inflection rather than modest beats.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic volumes deteriorate below -5% and pricing turns negative, signaling lost pricing power in a contracting demand environment.
2. The AMS financing terms out at materially higher cost, pushing pro-forma leverage toward covenant limits, or the deal breaks and management is left with an integration-less balance-sheet cost.
3. A guidance cut to the $650-675M FY2026 EBITDA range, or a raw-material-driven gross-margin compression that outpaces pricing.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (expected late September 2026), which will show whether volumes are stabilizing and provide an update on AMS timing and financing. The AMS shareholder vote and regulatory milestones through late CY2026 are the larger event path.

## Conclusion

H.B. Fuller is a well-run, cash-generative global adhesives leader executing a credible transformation toward higher-margin medical and engineering adhesives. The strategic direction is sound, the moat is durable, estimate revisions are positive, and the forward multiple of ~11.4x is genuinely low against peers. But the discount is largely explained rather than mispriced: unit volumes are declining, the AMS acquisition adds material leverage that must be termed out before it adds earnings, and there is no independent catalyst to close the valuation gap in the next two quarters. The calm options positioning and modest short interest confirm the market sees a range-bound stock rather than an imminent move in either direction.

The risk/reward is symmetric-to-slightly-favorable — roughly 11-15% upside to a $62-64 base case against ~20% downside to the bear case — which is not enough asymmetry to warrant a BUY on a name where financial health sits in the ambiguous middle and idiosyncratic risks (volumes, leverage, integration) are live. We rate FUL a HOLD, classified uncertain, and would revisit on a positive volume inflection or clarity on the AMS term-out. An income-oriented holder can reasonably hold for the ~1.7% dividend and its growth history, but the current setup does not offer the conviction for a stronger call.
