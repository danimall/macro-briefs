**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Oceaneering International is the scale leader in work-class remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), holding roughly 60% market share of ROV services on contracted floating drilling rigs, with a diversified portfolio spanning offshore energy services through defense and space engineering. The company has executed a multi-year operating improvement: revenue grew from $2.07B (2022) to $2.78B (2025), and operating margin expanded from 5.4% to 10.9% over the same period. The balance sheet is clean, with $689M cash at year-end 2025 against a single $500M note due 2028, an undrawn revolver, and an Altman Z-Score of 4.33 (safe zone).

The investment question turns on what the stock actually earns. The headline trailing P/E of 11.1x rests on 2025 diluted EPS of $3.49 and $354M net income, but that figure is materially overstated by a one-time $154M valuation-allowance release that converted a $77M 2024 tax expense into a $68M 2025 tax benefit. Pre-tax income of ~$422M and Adjusted EBITDA of ~$401M are the cleaner gauges, and 2026 carries a full cash-tax burden guided to $95-105M. On consensus 2026 EPS of $1.89, the stock at $37.44 trades at roughly 20x forward earnings, a fair-to-full multiple set against energy leading indicators that are rolling over.

Floating-rig count under contract fell 6.2% in 2025 to ~137, ROV utilization slipped from 67% to 61% in Q1 2026, and management guides Offshore Projects Group to shrink in 2026 while expecting energy results "weighted to the second half." The bull case leans on a second-half-2026 offshore recovery plus accelerating defense/space momentum (ADTech unfunded backlog more than doubled to $658M). Both are plausible but unproven. After a strong twelve-month run to near the 52-week high, with no open-market insider buying and the options market pricing a ±9.3% near-term move with elevated put skew, the setup is balanced. We rate OII HOLD.

## Company Overview

Oceaneering operates five segments across two businesses. The Energy business (~83% of 2025 revenue, $2.32B) comprises:

- **Subsea Robotics** ($855M revenue, 31% of total, ~30% operating margin) — the largest contributor to Energy operating income. The company owns the world's largest work-class ROV fleet (250 vehicles) and holds an estimated ~60% share of ROV services on contracted floating drilling rigs.
- **Manufactured Products** ($569M, 20%; margin rose from 8% to 13%) — subsea umbilicals and control hardware, plus a non-energy mobile-robotics/theme-park line.
- **Offshore Projects Group (OPG)** ($616M, 22%) — vessel-based subsea installation, intervention, IMR, diving, and decommissioning, principally in the U.S. Gulf and West Africa.
- **Integrity Management & Digital Solutions (IMDS)** ($284M, 10%) — asset integrity, inspection, NDT, and software.

The **Aerospace & Defense Technologies (ADTech)** segment (~17%, $460M) provides engineering and manufacturing for the U.S. Navy, NASA, and space contractors, and management has designated it the primary 2026 growth engine.

Roughly 55% of revenue is foreign (Africa, UK, Norway, Brazil, Asia/Australia). The U.S. Government is the single largest customer at ~12% of consolidated revenue. The competitive moat rests on the scale and breadth of the ROV fleet, dominant drill-support share, Jones Act-compliant owned vessels in the U.S. Gulf, and a cross-cycle ability to redeploy harsh-environment robotics competencies between energy and defense end markets.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year financial trajectory is strong. Operating income climbed from $111M (2022) to $181M (2023) to $246M (2024) to $305M (2025), a steady margin expansion driven by Subsea Robotics pricing discipline and umbilical-margin recovery. Free cash flow reached $208M in 2025 (a 7.5% FCF margin), with capex held at a disciplined ~$111M, mostly ROV fleet refresh (16 conventional ROVs retired and replaced).

The single most important normalization point is the tax distortion: the $154M valuation-allowance release (primarily $140M U.S. federal) produced a $68M tax benefit instead of a normalized expense. With 2026 cash taxes guided to $95-105M, the run-rate after-tax earnings power is far below the headline. Anyone anchoring to the trailing P/E is mismeasuring this stock by roughly half.

The balance sheet supports the franchise. Cash fell to $607M in Q1 2026, against $500M of 6.0% Senior Notes due February 2028 as the only debt, plus a fully undrawn $215M revolver. The reported debt/equity of 2.49 reflects historically depressed book equity rather than financial stress; equity rebuilt from $520M to $1.07B over three years, and the company sits comfortably within its 3.25x net-leverage covenant. Current ratio is 1.99.

Q1 2026 was a soft, seasonally weak quarter as expected: revenue $692M (+3% YoY, a beat), but operating income fell 21% to $58M and diluted EPS came in at $0.36 versus $0.49 a year earlier. A $59M working-capital outflow is the normal Q1 pattern (Q1 2025 was -$81M). OPG operating income fell from $35.7M to $18.3M (margin 22%→14%), and IMDS swung to a small operating loss (-$1.0M) on lower Africa/Australia activity tied to Middle East uncertainty. Offsetting this, Q1 order intake was ~$1B, one of the strongest quarters since 2020, and management reaffirmed full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $390-440M.

The earnings-surprise record is encouraging: four consecutive beats, including +64% in Q3 2025 and +35.6% in Q4 2025, with a more modest +11.6% in Q1 2026. The deceleration in the beat magnitude is consistent with the offshore plateau now visible in utilization data.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are bifurcated and, on balance, decelerating in the near term. The energy leading indicators that drive the core ROV economics are rolling over, ROV utilization declined to 65% over the year, and management anticipates a globally oversupplied oil market through early 2026. OPG is the one segment guided to shrink in 2026 on lower U.S. Gulf and West Africa activity, and that decline is already visible in the Q1 print.

The offsetting growth story is the defense/space pivot. ADTech is named the primary 2026 growth engine, with unfunded backlog up from $279M. Subsea tree installations are projected to rise to 370 in 2026 from 343, a constructive medium-term demand signal for Manufactured Products despite a soft current order book (book-to-bill of 0.84, with backlog down 15% to $511M). Total company backlog rose to $2.70B from $2.44B, led by Subsea Robotics ($1.04B) and IMDS ($517M).

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and does not provide a clear directional signal. Current-year EPS estimates have edged up (+2.6% over 30 days, +5.6% over 90 days), but next-year estimates have fallen (-1.6% over 30 days, -8.2% over 90 days), and next-quarter estimates dropped 7.3% over 30 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a clean earnings inflection; it reflects a business whose near-term trajectory has flattened while the longer-dated outlook softens. We weight this as neutral-to-slightly-negative for growth.

## Valuation Assessment

On the correct earnings base, OII is not cheap. On 2027 consensus of $2.08 it trades at ~18x. These multiples sit against low-single-digit revenue growth, a guided decline in one segment, and an offshore upcycle that has plateaued. The trailing 11.1x P/E is an artifact of the one-time tax release and should be disregarded.

Peer comparison is heterogeneous and provides limited anchoring. Within oil & gas equipment & services, P/E multiples range from TDW at 12.3x and AROC at 18.2x to LBRT at 32.2x and WHD at 54.5x (with AESI showing negative earnings). The peer median of 32.2x reflects names with different growth profiles and cyclical positions; OII's ~20x forward multiple is reasonable for a higher-quality, lower-leverage franchise with a defense ballast, but does not screen as a discount once the tax distortion is removed.

Analyst price targets cluster tightly around the current price: mean $35.25, median $35.50, high $39.00, low $31.00, implying the stock trades modestly above the consensus mean. The Seeking Alpha DCF cited fair value near $37.50 with a one-year target of $41-43. At the current price, the stock sits within the fair-value band, with limited demonstrable mispricing. The valuation score is held at 5.0: the franchise quality and balance sheet justify a respectable multiple, but there is no specific, identifiable reason the market is wrong here. The cycle-adjusted earnings power is roughly in line with where the price already sits.

## Competitive Landscape

Oceaneering's competitive position is stable-to-strong, anchored by a defensible niche. With its drill-support ROV share and the largest work-class fleet, the company benefits from equipment-availability advantages, deployment reach, and Jones Act-compliant owned vessels that create a structural barrier in the U.S. Gulf. Umbilical manufacturing is consolidating; competitors have cut capacity into rising demand, which has improved Manufactured Products pricing.

The competitive threats are real but secondary. In low-spec ROV work, smaller players can undercut on price, capping pricing power at the bottom end. Survey and IMR work faces competition from Fugro, DOF, and Saipem-affiliated operators. The diversification into defense/space lets the company monetize core robotics IP across cycles, but it also carries a specific geopolitical cost (discussed under Risk). The trajectory is one of maintained share and improving margins in the core franchise, with a credible expansion into adjacent high-margin engineering markets. We score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Offshore cyclicality with a softening leading indicator.** The central swing factor is offshore E&P spending. The declining rig count, falling utilization, and a guided second-half-weighted recovery create real timing risk. If the oversupplied oil market persists past early 2026, the second-half recovery the guidance relies on may slip.

**OPG decline.** The one segment management expects to shrink; the Q1 deterioration confirms the trajectory.

**China geopolitical overhang.** This is the most unusual and escalating risk. In December 2024 China sanctioned the parent company and certain executives over U.S.-Taiwan arms sales; in September 2025 China placed Oceaneering on its "Unreliable Entity List," generally prohibiting China-related import/export and new investment. Management expects no material impact, but the overhang is worsening and is tied to the defense work that is supposed to drive growth.

**U.S. Government funding dependence.** ADTech, the growth engine, relies on defense/NASA appropriations exposed to shutdowns, continuing resolutions, and debt-ceiling dynamics.

**Middle East exposure** already pushed IMDS to a Q1 operating loss and is flagged in guidance.

**Smaller items:** an ADTech contract dispute settled for $6.8M, a Ghana VAT reserve, a $13M theme-park inventory write-down in the non-energy mobility line (a recurring drag), and an ERP implementation underway (execution/cost watch item). The 2028 note maturity is the key capital-markets event, though refinancing risk is low given the cash position.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 53.7% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 41.1% (a 12.6pp spread), with an implied move of ±9.3% on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew of 6.8% indicates elevated put demand, and the term structure is flat (53.7% near, 55.4% far), suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst. The very low put/call volume (0.01) and OI ratios (0.09) indicate the options market is not heavily positioned bearishly in volume terms, but the IV richness and put skew are consistent with a market that sees two-sided risk after a strong run. This reinforces a balanced posture rather than a directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The offshore upcycle resumes in the second half of 2026, ROV utilization rebounds, and subsea tree installations rising to 370 lift Manufactured Products. ADTech delivers on its more-than-doubled unfunded backlog, providing oil-price-independent growth. Adjusted EBITDA lands at the top of the guided range, the company opportunistically refinances or tenders the 2028 notes, and buybacks accelerate from the $40M 2025 pace. On a reaccelerating EBITDA and re-rating toward defense-services multiples, the stock reaches the $41-43 range, roughly 10-15% above current.

### Bear Case
The oversupplied oil market persists, the second-half recovery stalls, and ROV utilization continues to drift below 61%. OPG declines more than guided, IMDS remains pressured by Middle East activity, and the China Unreliable Entity List restrictions begin to impede defense work. Normalized 2026 EPS comes in below $1.89, and the market de-rates the energy beta toward TDW's ~12x, taking the stock toward $25-27, roughly 28-33% below current.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced: ~10-15% to the bull case against ~28-33% to the bear case, with the most-likely outcome clustering near the current price as guidance is met but the cycle stays flat.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality and balance sheet are real, but at ~20x normalized 2026 earnings near the 52-week high, with decelerating energy indicators and no open-market insider buying, the risk/reward does not support a BUY, and the business is too sound and cash-generative to AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ROV utilization stabilizes and reverses above 65% in Q2/Q3 2026, confirming the second-half recovery, alongside Adjusted EBITDA tracking toward the top of its range.
- ADTech funded backlog converts at a pace that pushes consolidated 2027 EPS estimates back above $2.20 (reversing the current -8.2% 90-day next-year revision).
- A pullback toward $30-32 (the lower analyst target band and roughly 16-17x normalized 2026 EPS), which would restore valuation asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ROV utilization falls below 58% with floating-rig count declining further, signaling the offshore plateau has become a downturn.
- China Unreliable Entity List restrictions begin materially impeding ADTech revenue or supply chain.
- Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the $390M floor, or OPG decline exceeds management's framing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) is the key checkpoint; it will show whether the guidance's second-half recovery is materializing in utilization and OPG trends.

## Conclusion

Oceaneering is a well-run, scale-advantaged ROV franchise with a clean balance sheet, improving margins, four consecutive earnings beats, and a credible diversification into defense and space that reduces pure oil-price beta. Those are the reasons the stock has performed strongly over twelve months. The reasons to wait are equally concrete: the headline 11x P/E is a tax-driven illusion, the true forward multiple is ~20x, the energy leading indicators are softening, the recovery is back-half-loaded and unproven, and the stock sits above its consensus mean target with no insider conviction signal. The score trajectory captures this tension: financial health has improved while valuation and growth have both deteriorated, leaving the overall picture roughly balanced. We maintain HOLD and would look to add only on a pullback toward the low-$30s or on confirmed second-half utilization recovery.
