## Executive Summary

Harmony Biosciences reported the strongest quarter in its history on August 4, 2026: net product revenue of $261.3M, up 30.3% year over year and 21.3% sequentially, with diluted EPS of $1.28 against a $1.02 consensus (a 25.6% beat). Average patients on therapy reached approximately 8,950, up 450 sequentially. Consensus EPS estimates have moved sharply higher — current-year up 16.1% in 30 days and next-year up 11.1% — and the company holds $962.5M of cash and investments against $155M of term debt, giving net cash of $807.5M, or $13.87 per share and 35.8% of the current market capitalization. On trailing-twelve-month diluted EPS of $3.08 (Q3'25 $0.87 + Q4'25 $0.38 + Q1'26 $0.55 + Q2'26 $1.28), the stock trades at 12.6x, and enterprise value is 1.42x the midpoint of FY2026 revenue guidance.

Three facts explain why that multiple has not compressed the other way for five years. First, WAKIX is 100% of revenue and Harmony's own August 4 earnings release states that its IP estate "supports WAKIX exclusivity to March 2030 (inclusive of 6-months of pediatric exclusivity)" — six months earlier than most of its settlement licenses imply, and 3.6 years from today. Second, the economics are being taken by licensors: FY2025 cost of product sold was $198.3M, of which $188.5M was Bioprojet royalties, leaving roughly 1.1% of revenue as actual manufacturing cost. That royalty burden rose from 19.2% of net revenue in FY2023 to 21.7% in FY2025 and is now being added to by new Novitium and MSN licenses; COGS ran 22.6% of revenue in H1 2026 versus 18.2% in H1 2025. Third, FDA approved Takeda's ORZEYFUL (oveporexton) for adults with narcolepsy type 1 in early August 2026 — the first therapy addressing the underlying orexin deficiency — while Harmony's own orexin-2 agonist, BP-205, only reported Phase 1 single-ascending-dose data on August 4 and does not begin Phase 2 until mid-2027.

The near-term fulcrum is legal, not commercial. Harmony has settled with six of seven ANDA filers; the seventh, AET Pharma (with Alfred E. Tiefenbacher and Sandoz as marketing partner), went to bench trial in February 2026, and the Delaware court called for closing arguments on October 22, 2026. A Harmony win preserves the March 2030 status quo already embedded in the plan. A loss on both the '197 polymorph and '947 method-of-use patents could permit generic entry years early and, through acceleration language in the settlements, invite the six settled filers behind it. Defendants also filed antitrust counterclaims on June 22, 2026, amended July 21, with no disclosed reserve and a $20.0M judgment cross-default in the credit agreement. Weighting the outcomes gives an expected return in the low single digits from $38.735, with a bear case near $22. That is a HOLD, and honestly an uncertain one.

## Company Overview

Harmony operates one reportable segment (rare neurological diseases) and generates all revenue in the United States. WAKIX (pitolisant) is a first-in-class histamine-3 receptor antagonist/inverse agonist approved for excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy (2019), cataplexy in adults (2020), EDS in pediatric patients 6+ (2024), and cataplexy in pediatric patients 6+ (February 2026). Its principal commercial differentiator is that it is not a DEA-scheduled controlled substance and carries no REMS obligation, unlike the oxybates that anchor the competing standard of care. Management notes that only about 4,000 of the roughly 9,000 US HCPs treating narcolepsy are enrolled in the oxybate REMS, leaving a large prescriber base reachable without that friction.

Harmony does not own the underlying intellectual property. Pitolisant is licensed from Bioprojet under a 2017 agreement (US rights) and a 2022 agreement (next-generation GR and HD formulations, US and Latin America). Two structural constraints matter: Harmony must obtain Bioprojet's consent before commencing any pitolisant clinical trial, and it is contractually barred from developing, marketing, or filing an NDA for any product that would compete with pitolisant in the field. The company is locked into the pitolisant franchise for sleep/wake, which conditions how its pipeline choices should be read.

Distribution runs through exactly three specialty pharmacies (CVS Caremark 38%, Accredo 36%, PANTHERx 26% of FY2025 gross product revenue). These are channel intermediaries, not sources of end demand, so the exposure is contracting and credit concentration rather than customer demand concentration. Manufacturing is fully outsourced and largely sole-sourced (Interor, Corden Pharma Chenôve, Patheon UK). Headcount was 293 at year-end 2025.

The pipeline is entirely pre-revenue: pitolisant GR (NDA accepted July 2026, PDUFA April 1, 2027), pitolisant HD (Phase 3 topline 2027, PDUFA 2028), pitolisant in Prader-Willi syndrome (TEMPO topline mid-2027, which also completes the second and final FDA-agreed requirement for pediatric exclusivity), EPX-100 in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut (topline 1H 2027), and BP-205 (Phase 1). ZYN002 in Fragile X was phased out, accounting for an $11.3M six-month R&D reduction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong but guidance implies sharp deceleration.** FY2023 $582.0M, FY2024 $714.7M (+22.8%), FY2025 $868.5M (+21.5%), H1 2026 $476.7M (+23.7%). Q2 2026 was $261.3M, driven by units +26.5% and a roughly 7% January price increase, partially offset by about 4.0 percentage points of higher rebates and gross-to-net.

The reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $1.00-1.04B implies H2 2026 revenue of $523M-$563M against H2 2025 of $483.2M — growth of +8.2% to +16.5%, midpoint +12.4%, versus +23.7% in H1. Two readings are plausible and I cannot adjudicate between them: ordinary conservatism after a quarter that partly rebounded from Q1 seasonal market-access friction (Q1 2026 revenue of $215.4M grew only 16.6%), or an anticipated slowdown. The ORZEYFUL approval is a third candidate, but guidance was set before the approval was public and Harmony has published no commentary on it.

**Unit economics are extraordinary and the licensor captures most of the spread.** Q2 revenue of $261.3M across ~8,950 average patients implies roughly $29,200 per patient per quarter, or about $117,000 annualized. Actual product cost is roughly 1.1% of revenue. The gross margin story is entirely a royalty story, and the royalty is going the wrong way:

| Period | COGS % of revenue |
|---|---|
| FY2023 | 20.8% |
| FY2024 | 21.9% |
| FY2025 | 22.8% |
| H1 2025 | 18.2% |
| H1 2026 | 22.6% |
| Q2 2026 | 24.2% |

The Bioprojet royalty appears tiered and ascending with cumulative annual sales, so first-half rates understate full-year levels and quarter-versus-quarter comparisons overstate the incremental effect of new licenses. Layered on top: the January 2026 Novitium license ($15M upfront, low single-digit royalties on current *and future* pitolisant products) and the February 2026 MSN license ($17M upfront, $25M contingent on USPTO approval of pending patents, plus additional low single-digit royalties). Scaling FY2025's 22.8% by the H1'26/H1'25 ratio points to FY2026 COGS in the mid-to-high 20s percent — a further 300-500bp of gross margin erosion. That is a derived estimate contingent on the tiering pattern repeating; the company guides only to revenue.

**Reported margins in both Q2 quarters are distorted.** Q2 2026 operating income of $89.3M (34.2% margin) versus $48.2M (24.0%) in Q2 2025 flatters the trend: prior-year Q2 carried a $15.0M CiRC upfront in R&D and an ANDA settlement charge in G&A, while current-year Q2 carried neither. The cleaner comparison is H1 operating margin of 26.6% ($126.6M on $476.7M) versus 27.1% in H1 2025 — and H1 2026 absorbed roughly $32M of Novitium and MSN upfronts. Net margin has compressed at the annual level: 22.1% (FY2023) → 20.4% (FY2024) → 18.3% (FY2025).

**Cash conversion weakened.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $120.2M versus $113.3M in H1 2025 (+6.1%) while net income rose 26.5% to $107.9M. Accrued expenses fell $21.9M (consistent with paying out settlement amounts accrued at year-end — accrued legal and professional fees were $30.0M at 12/31/2025 against $3.0M a year earlier) and receivables rose $14.2M. Receivables of $111.0M against Q2 revenue of $261.3M imply roughly 39 days sales outstanding, unremarkable for specialty distribution. I am not annualizing H1 or Q2 cash flow: H1 contained one-time settlement payouts and $32M of licensing upfronts, while H2 carries GR pre-launch spending, three Phase 3 programs, and BP-205 Phase 1b.

**Balance sheet is the strongest element of the file.** At 6/30/2026: cash and equivalents $549.8M, short-term investments $118.0M, long-term investments $294.7M, total $962.5M; term debt principal $155.0M; stockholders' equity $999.2M. Net cash $807.5M ($13.87/share). Current ratio 3.48x; debt/equity 0.155x; FY2025 interest coverage roughly 14x. Debt is a JPMorgan-led Term Loan A maturing July 2028, amortizing $5M quarterly with a $115M balloon at SOFR + 3.50-4.00%. Two blemishes: the 10-Q discloses a non-financial covenant event of default (a subsidiary guarantor joinder delay) dating to July 2025 and not waived until May 4, 2026 — ten months of unremediated non-compliance, with no cash consequence but a control observation; and the credit agreement contains a cross-default at judgments above $20.0M, which is live given the antitrust counterclaims.

One mechanical tailwind: the capitalized Bioprojet license ($215M gross) amortizes at $23.845M annually and is fully amortized around 2029, lifting reported earnings by roughly $24M pretax per year thereafter with no change in cash economics.

**Earnings quality on estimates.** The four-quarter surprise record is 2 beats, 2 misses, and the misses were large (-58.4% in Q4 2025, -22.3% in Q1 2026) before the +25.6% Q2 beat. Only two analysts contribute to consensus. Note also a basis question: current-year consensus of $5.22 is difficult to reconcile with H1 GAAP EPS of $1.84 and guidance-implied H2 revenue of ~$543M, and is most plausibly a non-GAAP figure adding back amortization and stock compensation. On GAAP, adding back the ~$32M of H1 IPR&D charges and holding a 28% adjusted net margin through H2 produces FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS in the $4.30-4.50 range. I use the GAAP figure for multiples below and flag the basis explicitly rather than treating $5.22 as comparable.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is durable and visible. Penetration is roughly 11% of the roughly 80,000 diagnosed US narcolepsy patients management cites, with the second-highest quarterly patient add in company history in Q2. The sales force, remote sales, field reimbursement, and patient-outreach expansions were fully hired, trained, and deployed within Q2, and a new online portal and revised reimbursement-support process were launched to shorten time to therapy. Formulary access exceeds 80% of insured lives. Estimate revisions are unambiguously positive across every window shown.

The problem is duration, not rate. Growth compounds against a fixed end date. Management's own public exclusivity date is March 2030, and the binding constraint is the earliest licensed entrant (Hikma, March 2030) rather than the July 2030 dates in the Novugen, Annora, Lupin, and Novitium settlements. Most settlements contain "or earlier under certain circumstances" acceleration language, meaning one early entrant likely triggers a cascade. Pediatric exclusivity is also not yet banked — it requires satisfactory completion of the TEMPO Prader-Willi study, with topline not until mid-2027.

The pipeline is Harmony's answer, and it is a real one on paper. Pitolisant GR (PDUFA April 1, 2027, launch targeted 1H 2027) addresses GI symptoms affecting 80-90% of narcolepsy patients and permits initiation at a therapeutic dose without titration; the GR and HD applications, if granted, would run to October 2044, and the licensed amorphous pitolisant patent runs to 2042. If Harmony converts the majority of its patient base onto a formulation with post-2030 protection before generic pitolisant arrives, the cliff is deferred rather than crossed. That is the highest-value lever in the story and it is not yet demonstrated.

The rest of the pipeline is 2028 and beyond: HD (fatigue in narcolepsy, sleep inertia in idiopathic hypersomnia), TEMPO in Prader-Willi, EPX-100 in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut with a rare pediatric disease designation that now carries a restored priority review voucher program authorized through September 30, 2029. BP-205 has encouraging Phase 1 pharmacokinetics (30-75 minute Tmax, ~25-hour half-life supporting once-daily dosing, no serious or severe treatment-emergent adverse events) but no patient efficacy data and no Phase 2 start until mid-2027.

Pipeline breadth is not revenue diversification. Revenue mix remains 100% WAKIX and channel concentration remains three specialty pharmacies at 100% of gross revenue; nothing changes either measure before 2027 at the earliest.

The peer set supplied (VCEL 91.5x, ADMA 12.2x, SRPT 23.5x, plus two loss-makers) is heterogeneous and low-information: none share HRMY's profile of a single product with a dated 2030 exclusivity end. The relevant comparison is not the biotech median but the multiple pharma markets assign to a patent-cliff annuity, which is typically 6-9x forward earnings. HRMY at ~8.8x forward GAAP earnings is inside that band, not below it.

The most useful framing is a cash-accumulation build rather than a multiple. From today to March 2030 is 3.6 years. Taking H2 2026 net income of roughly $130M, FY2027 $180M, FY2028 $185M, FY2029 $170M (declining net margin as the royalty stack builds and GR/HD launch spending arrives), plus a partial Q1 2030, cumulative retained earnings are on the order of $700-800M, or $12-14 per share. Added to today's $13.87 of net cash, that is $26-28 per share of assets accumulated by the cliff. The residual — GR/HD franchise value beyond 2030, EPX-100, BP-205, PRV optionality, and whatever business development the $807M buys — must be worth $11-13 per share to justify today's price. That is a demanding but not unreasonable requirement, and it is roughly what a rational discount rate on this asset produces. My base case is $42, close to spot.

Consensus mean is $46.10 and median $47.50, with a $30 low and $70 high, and one strong-sell rating in a ten-analyst set. The dispersion is the point: this is not a stock where consensus targets act as a floor, because the range reflects genuinely divergent litigation and post-2030 assumptions rather than incremental estimate lag.

I score valuation 5.4. The cheapness on near-term earnings is real, but the discount has an identifiable, dated cause, and the 15% move over the past three weeks has closed most of the gap to my base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Three layers of competition, and the trajectory in each is unfavorable or newly worse.

**Branded incumbents.** Oxybates (Jazz's Xyrem/Xywav, Avadel's Lumryz), stimulants, modafinil/armodafinil, and solriamfetol. WAKIX's advantages — non-scheduled status, no REMS, a label spanning NT1 and NT2 plus pediatrics 6+ — are durable while exclusivity lasts. Its disclosed disadvantages are notable: Harmony's own risk factors state WAKIX did not demonstrate non-inferiority to modafinil, and clinical response can take several weeks.

**Generic pitolisant, no later than March 2030.** Six of seven ANDA filers hold dated licenses. This is a known, dated constraint.

**The orexin agonist class, now commercial.** FDA approved Takeda's ORZEYFUL (oveporexton) for adults with narcolepsy type 1 around August 5, 2026, described across trade press as the first therapy addressing the underlying orexin deficiency rather than symptoms. This is externally reported and unconfirmed in any Harmony filing — the FY2025 10-K does not name oveporexton and the Q2 10-Q states no material risk-factor changes as of June 30, 2026.

Scope matters. The approval covers adult NT1 only, which is the cataplexy population. WAKIX retains adult NT2, pediatrics, and prospectively idiopathic hypersomnia via pitolisant HD. Harmony does not disclose its NT1/NT2 patient split, so the exposed share of ~8,950 patients is unknown — this is the single most important missing input in the file. An offsetting possibility is that a mechanistically targeted therapy improves diagnosis rates and pulls untreated patients into care, expanding the treated market; NT1 launches also take years to build share.

The strategic read-through is harder to offset. Harmony cannot, under the Bioprojet agreements, develop or file an NDA for any product competing with pitolisant in the field, and needs Bioprojet's consent to run any pitolisant trial. Its orexin entrant is sublicensed (discovered by Teijin, partnered with Bioprojet) and enters Phase 2 in mid-2027, roughly five years behind an approved competitor. Combined with a royalty stack approaching the mid-20s percent of net sales and rising, the trajectory of the competitive position is deteriorating even as unit volumes grow. I score this dimension 4.3.

## Risk Assessment

**AET/Sandoz litigation (the dominant binary).** The Delaware bench trial concluded February 17-19, 2026 on the '197 polymorph and '947 method-of-use patents; post-trial briefing ran through May 21, 2026; the court called for closing arguments on October 22, 2026. Harmony also filed a separate April 2026 suit against AET, Tiefenbacher, and Sandoz on the acquired '920 amorphous pitolisant patent, which provides a second line of defense. A win preserves the March 2030 plan. A loss on both patents could permit generic launch upon ANDA approval and trigger acceleration in the six settled licenses. With roughly $1B of revenue at near-100% product gross margin before royalties, an early cascade would be severe. Defendants' antitrust counterclaims (filed June 22, 2026, amended July 21) carry no disclosed reserve and interact with a $20.0M judgment cross-default in the term loan.

**Unquantified ORZEYFUL erosion.** No company assessment exists, no NT1/NT2 split is disclosed, and no pricing or payer detail is available. Any erosion estimate is speculative.

**Contingent pediatric exclusivity.** Six months of the March 2030 date depends on satisfactory completion of TEMPO, with topline mid-2027.

**Concentration.** 100% of revenue from one product; 100% of gross revenue through three specialty pharmacies; sole-sourced API and finished product. Balance-sheet inventory of $6.4M against disclosed supply coverage into Q4 2028 is most plausibly explained by near-zero unit manufacturing cost plus supplier-held material, but that reconciliation is inferred rather than confirmed.

**Governance and finance-function stability.** Two CFOs departed within three months — Sandip Kapadia (April 14, 2026) and Glenn Reicin (July 16, 2026, roughly twelve weeks into the role, forfeiting a 164,309-share option grant). An interim principal financial officer signed the Q2 10-Q. The company stated Reicin's departure was not due to any disagreement, and there is no evidence of accounting irregularity: Deloitte issued clean opinions on both the FY2025 financials and internal control over financial reporting, with no non-reliance filing. Separately, the CiRC agreement (June 2025) paid $15M upfront plus option fees for discovery-stage cell therapy assets to an entity controlled by Paragon Biosciences, whose president and owner was Harmony's board Chairman; I could not verify the independent-approval process.

**Alignment.** The sole disclosed performance measure in the proxy is Net Product Revenue — the metric least sensitive to the royalty stacking compressing margins and to returns on the roughly $150M of in-licensing upfronts since 2023. CEO FY2025 total compensation was $7.90M. Five-year TSR index was 104 versus a peer index of 120 while revenue nearly tripled and net income rose from $34.6M to $158.7M.

**Insider and capital-allocation signals.** No open-market insider purchases by any officer or director from May 2025 through May 2026, across a price range from the high $20s to low $40s. The CEO exercised and sold 25,933 shares at $40.11 in December 2025; the former CFO sold to zero common holdings by January 2026, roughly three months before separation. $150M remains under a repurchase authorization with zero shares repurchased in 2024, 2025, or H1 2026 while net cash built to $807.5M and roughly $47M went to former patent adversaries in early 2026. Management framed capital priorities toward business development targeting revenue-generating assets for 2028-2032. That is a coherent answer to the cliff and simultaneously a statement that management does not view its own equity at ~9x forward earnings as the most attractive use of cash.

I score risk factors 3.8. Some of this is priced — the 12.6x multiple exists precisely because the cliff is known. The litigation binary, the unmodeled competitive entry, and the absence of a permanent CFO through both are not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 66.0% sits 21.2 points above one-year realized volatility of 44.7%, and the nearest 13-day expiry prices a ±14.7% move with no scheduled earnings event — the market is paying for headline risk around the ORZEYFUL launch and litigation calendar. Term structure is in contango, with six-month IV at 70.7% above the near-term 66.0%, and the six-month put/call skew of -6.4% is more negative than the balanced -1.8% at three months; the longer tenors span both the October 22 closing arguments and the Q4 BP-205 MAD readout. Put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.92 to 2.34 between July 20 and August 7 even as the price rose 15%, indicating protection accumulated into strength rather than sold. Short interest is 11.8% of float at 6.4 days to cover. ATM IV also fell 8.9 points over that window, so the volatility bid is concentrated in structure rather than level.

The read is consistent with the fundamental view: the market treats this as a two-sided binary with a wide distribution, not a directional derating. Option premium is expensive, which argues against expressing a view by buying calls and in favor of patience or covered-call overwriting for existing holders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($58, +50%).** Harmony prevails against AET/Sandoz in Delaware, confirming exclusivity to March 2030 and defusing the acceleration clauses. Pitolisant GR is approved on the April 1, 2027 PDUFA date and converts a majority of the patient base onto a formulation with patent protection potentially into 2044, materially deferring the cliff rather than crossing it. TEMPO completes, securing pediatric exclusivity. Pitolisant HD adds idiopathic hypersomnia and differentiated fatigue/sleep-inertia labeling in 2028. ORZEYFUL expands the diagnosed and treated narcolepsy market more than it cannibalizes WAKIX's NT2 and pediatric base. BP-205 advances into Phase 2 with clean MAD data, and the $807M of net cash buys a revenue-generating asset for the 2028-2032 window. Post-2030 visibility improves, the market grants 9-10x on rising earnings, and $58 becomes reachable within 18-24 months.

**Bear case ($22, -43%).** AET/Sandoz prevails on the '197 and '947 patents, generic pitolisant enters on ANDA approval well before 2030, and acceleration clauses bring the six settled filers in behind it. Alternatively, ORZEYFUL takes NT1 share faster than the sell-side assumes, H2 2026 lands at the low end of guidance (+8.2%), and FY2027 guidance disappoints. The royalty stack pushes FY2026 COGS into the high 20s percent, compressing net margin further while GR pre-launch and three Phase 3 programs consume operating leverage. The antitrust counterclaims produce a judgment above $20.0M, triggering the credit-agreement cross-default. Value compresses toward net cash of $13.87 plus a few years of residual earnings.

**Base case ($42, +8.4%).** Harmony wins or partially wins the patent case, WAKIX runs to March 2030, GR launches in 1H 2027 and converts a meaningful but not dominant share of patients, ORZEYFUL takes a portion of adult NT1 over two to three years, and the company accumulates $12-14 per share of retained earnings on top of today's $13.87 of net cash. The market continues to apply a cliff discount, and the stock compounds roughly in line with cash accumulation less terminal-value erosion.

Probability-weighting these outcomes at roughly 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear produces an expected return in the low single digits from $38.735. The asymmetry is unfavorable in magnitude (+8% to base against -43% to bear) but is offset in expectation by the size of the bull case. That combination — modest positive expected value with a wide, event-driven distribution — is the definition of a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). Existing holders can maintain exposure; new capital has no entry signal at $38.735 and should wait for the October 22 litigation resolution or a lower price.

**Base case: $42** — 3.6 years of exclusivity generating $12-14/share of cumulative retained earnings on top of $13.87/share net cash, plus $11-13/share of residual GR/HD, EPX-100, BP-205 and business-development value, discounted for the cliff. +8.4% from spot.

**Bull case: $58** — approximately 9x a normalized FY2028 GAAP earnings base of roughly $6.00-6.50 per share under a successful GR conversion, litigation win, and secured pediatric exclusivity. +49.7%.

**Bear case: $22** — net cash of $13.87 plus roughly two years of residual earnings under early generic entry or a material guidance cut. -43.2%.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on four dated events: Q3 2026 results (the first clean read on ORZEYFUL's effect on patient adds and the first test of the guidance-implied +12.4% H2), closing arguments on October 22, 2026 with a decision thereafter, BP-205 MAD data in Q4 2026, and the pitolisant GR PDUFA on April 1, 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A favorable ruling in the AET litigation on the '197 and/or '947 patents that leaves the March 2030 date intact, with no adverse antitrust finding. This removes the largest term in the bear case and would justify a higher terminal multiple.
2. Q3 2026 revenue at or above $270M with patient adds of 350+ and reaffirmed or raised FY2026 guidance — evidence that ORZEYFUL is not immediately eroding the base and that H2 conservatism was ordinary.
3. A permanent CFO appointment combined with the initiation of repurchases under the remaining $150M authorization, or a business-development transaction that adds 2028-2032 revenue at a defensible price.
4. Price below $30 with fundamentals intact, which would restore roughly 40% upside to base case against the same bear.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. An adverse ruling on both litigated patents, or any acceleration of a settled filer's entry date. This is the single condition that would move me to AVOID.
2. FY2026 revenue guidance cut, or Q3 revenue below $250M with sequential patient adds under 200 — evidence of NT1 erosion arriving faster than modeled.
3. FY2026 cost of product sold above 27% of revenue, confirming that royalty stacking is compressing net margin toward the mid-teens.
4. A disclosed antitrust reserve or judgment exposure approaching $20.0M, which would engage the credit-agreement cross-default.
5. Failure to complete TEMPO on schedule, putting six months of pediatric exclusivity at risk.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings (early November 2026), by which point the October 22 closing arguments will have occurred and the first post-ORZEYFUL patient-add data will be available.

## Conclusion

Harmony is a well-run commercial pharmaceutical business with a genuinely differentiated product, roughly 11% penetration of its addressable population, a fortress balance sheet holding 36% of market capitalization in net cash, and a trailing multiple of 12.6x that would be arresting in almost any other context. The Q2 print was strong on every operating measure and consensus estimates have moved up sharply.

The multiple is low for identifiable reasons that the Q2 print did not change. Exclusivity ends in March 2030 by management's own disclosure — 3.6 years away. All revenue comes from one product whose core IP Harmony licenses rather than owns, and whose licensors capture a rising share of net sales that reached 24.2% of revenue in Q2. A mechanistically superior competitor entered the cataplexy segment days after the quarter closed, and Harmony's own answer in that class is entering Phase 2 in mid-2027. The finance function has had no permanent leader since April. And the outcome that most determines the next 12 months of returns is a judicial decision expected after October 22, 2026, on which I have no informational edge.

Weighting those outcomes gives low-single-digit expected return with a distribution running from roughly $22 to $58. The overall score of 5.1 continues a gradual decline from 6.3 in March, driven by the same two dimensions as before — a deteriorating competitive trajectory and management-alignment concerns — partly offset this cycle by a real earnings beat and rising estimates.
