## Executive Summary

Tidewater owns and operates 204 offshore support vessels across five geographic segments and more than 30 countries. The investment question is narrow and answerable from the filings: is the offshore vessel upcycle still delivering pricing, and is the equity priced for what the fleet actually earns? On the first question, the Q2 2026 10-Q (filed August 3) says no, at least for now. H1 2026 revenue fell 1% to $668.5M while operating income fell 22% to $121.5M, because average day rates were down 0.5% year over year ($22,614 vs. $22,730) while vessel operating cost per active day rose 9.1% ($9,535 vs. $8,738). The spread between day rate and cost per active day — the company's core unit economic — expanded 8.5% in FY2025 to $13,571 and has since contracted to $13,079 in H1 2026 and $13,052 in Q2. Utilization improved from 75.1% to 78.5% and did not offset the cost pressure.

On the second question, the headline multiple is misleading. Reported FY2025 net income of $333.5M exceeded pretax income of $220.2M because the tax line was a $113.2M benefit, driven by a Q4 2025 internal "Vessel Realignment" that consolidated much of the fleet into a single U.S. entity plus a valuation allowance release. The $195.8M deferred tax benefit in the cash flow statement is non-cash. Normalizing FY2025 pretax income at a 30% rate produces roughly $3.05 of EPS versus the ~$6.60 reported, and the H1 2026 effective tax rate of 68.9% ($59,965K on $87,023K pretax) confirms this was not a durable rate reduction. Against normalized earnings power of roughly $3.00-3.50 per share, the stock trades at 21-25x, not the 12x the trailing multiple implies.

What genuinely supports the equity is the balance sheet and the asset. Cash of $613.5M ($616.1M including restricted) against $666.7M of face debt leaves net debt of $50.6M — 0.09x FY2025 EBITDA of $544.9M against a 3.0x revolver covenant ceiling — with the $250M revolver entirely undrawn and nothing maturing before the 9.125% notes in July 2030, which trade at 107.2. The Wilson Sons Ultratug acquisition (22 Brazilian PSVs, $500M enterprise value) has cleared CADE and all lender change-of-control waivers and is expected to close around September 1, adding inorganic scale and Brazilian local-content incumbency. But at $18.3M of enterprise value per owned vessel, the market is already paying between the $16.1M per PSV Tidewater paid for Solstad in July 2023 and the $22.7M per PSV it is paying for Wilson. Base case upside of roughly 7% against bear case downside near 30% does not justify new capital. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Incorporated in 1956, Delaware-domiciled and headquartered in Houston, Tidewater charters offshore support vessels to the offshore energy industry. As of December 31, 2025 it owned 208 vessels and employed approximately 7,300; as of June 30, 2026 the owned fleet was 204 vessels with an average age of 13.6 years. FY2025 vessel revenue split 72.3% platform supply vessels (139 hulls), 24.2% anchor handling towing supply (52 hulls) and 3.5% other.

Revenue comes from time charters ranging from months to several years, plus a smaller spot book. Base day rates are generally fixed; customers bear fuel while a vessel is on hire, Tidewater bears it off-hire and in drydock. Five segments — Americas, Asia Pacific, Middle East, Europe/Mediterranean, West Africa — each run by a managing director reporting to the CEO.

Two structural features matter. First, only 9 of 208 vessels were U.S.-flagged at year-end 2025, so Jones Act protection covers a small slice of the fleet while imposing a real constraint: a dual stock certificate system and a charter cap of 24% non-U.S.-citizen ownership. Second, the fourth competitive factor the 10-K names — "the legal ability to provide the service in a given region" — is the durable one. Local content, local flagging and local ownership rules protect incumbency where Tidewater has it and block entry where it does not, which is the strategic logic for buying a Brazilian operator rather than sailing vessels into Brazil.

Customer concentration is real: top five at 29.8% of FY2025 revenue, top ten at 47.9%. Eni was the only ≥10% customer in the last three years (10.3% in 2023, 12.3% in 2024, below 10% in 2025). The 10-K does not disclose prior-year top-five or top-ten cohort figures, so Eni falling below the threshold is a fact about one relationship and not evidence that aggregate concentration improved.

The 2022-2024 tripling was substantially the Solstad acquisition (closed July 5, 2023; 37 PSVs for ~$594.2M). That engine has stalled: FY2025 revenue +0.5%, H1 2026 revenue -1%. Operating income has fallen for two consecutive periods, -9.2% in FY2025 and -22% in H1 2026.

The cost detail from the 10-Q: repair and maintenance +17% ($53.8M), fuel/lube/supplies +27% ($37.5M), insurance +20%, crew +4% (crew is the largest single line at $204.1M, 59% of vessel operating cost). Management attributes the increases to higher repair days including "several high-cost repairs," higher mobilization activity, war-risk crew bonuses in the Middle East, and higher fuel prices. Some of that is identifiably non-recurring — approximately $6.0M of Iran-conflict crew, insurance and fuel costs from March 2026 alone — and mobilization spend can precede revenue. That is the honest counterweight to the margin deterioration, and it is why I treat the H1 spread compression as a warning rather than a settled trend.

One additional dynamic deserves flagging: revenue per average vessel has risen (FY2024 $6.20M, FY2025 $6.35M, H1 2026 annualized $6.49M) while the average fleet shrank from 217 to 213 to 206. Tidewater has held revenue flat by divesting and stacking low-earning tonnage. That is competent asset management and it is finite — it makes the flat headline look better than the underlying market.

West Africa is roughly 40% of segment operating profit on 27% of the fleet and is the segment deteriorating fastest — H1 day rates down 12.5% to $20,741, revenue down 14%. The offsetting positive is that West Africa utilization jumped from 63.2% to 78.4% as idle and drydock days fell and average stacked vessels dropped from five to two. Better asset use, materially worse pricing.

Capital is poorly matched to returns. Asia Pacific earned $1.59M of operating profit per vessel per half on 18 vessels; the Middle East earned $0.28M per vessel on 45 — a 5.6x gap, with average day rates of $37,237 versus $14,428. Some of that is asset mix (the Middle East fleet skews to smaller AHTS and shallow-water tonnage that cannot simply be redeployed to Australia) and local content rules, so it is not straightforwardly an allocation error. It does frame where self-help upside would come from, and the filings show redeployment already occurring — six vessels transferred into Europe/Mediterranean year over year.

### Cash flow: the quality question

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 282.5 | 379.1 | 154.1 | 86.2 |
| Capex (PP&E) | — | 25.8 | 15.5 | 29.8 |
| Cash drydock & survey | 133.3 | 98.6 | 67.1 | 59.7 |
| Derived FCF (OCF − capex) | — | 353.3 | 138.6 | 56.4 |

FY2025 GAAP free cash flow of $353.3M (26.1% of revenue) was excellent. H1 2026 FCF of $56.4M is down 59%, with the margin falling from 20.5% to 8.4%.

Three technical points. First, recertification drydock costs are capitalized and amortized over 30 months while the cash outflow runs through operating activities. FY2025 D&A of $262.3M included $111.2M of drydock amortization while $98.6M of drydock cash left through OCF. Any EBITDA multiple for Tidewater overstates cash generation unless drydock spend is deducted. Second, the 10-Q discloses a 2025 correction of foreign-exchange effects on cash previously embedded in operating cash flow; management assessed it immaterial and revised rather than restated, reducing H1 2025 OCF by $17.3M to $154.1M. No effect on net income or total cash, but anyone comparing to previously published figures is working off a superseded base. Third, I am not annualizing H1 2026 cash flow as a forecast. H1 carried $41.5M of working-capital drag (receivables −$21.2M, accrued expenses −$19.5M, payables −$3.7M), elevated Wilson deal expenses in G&A, and lumpy drydock timing. As a labeled sensitivity only, 2× H1 FCF is $112.8M (3.1% yield at $74.12); a normalized construction using H1 operating income plus the implied ~$8M of one-time deal costs and a seasonally stronger Q3 North Sea lands nearer $180-230M before any Wilson contribution.

### Balance sheet: the clear strength

At June 30, 2026: cash $613.5M ($616.1M with restricted); $650.0M of 9.125% senior notes due July 15, 2030; $16.7M of vessel facility agreements (fixed 2.7%-6.3%, no financial covenants); total face debt $666.7M; net debt approximately $50.6M. Total equity $1,388.3M, working capital $697.3M, current ratio ~3.6x. The $250M DNB-agented revolver (matures April 15, 2030) was entirely undrawn at the August 3 filing date.

A screened debt-to-equity of 1.75x does not reconcile to the filed balance sheet. Total liabilities of $978.0M ÷ equity of $1,388.3M = 0.70x; interest-bearing debt ÷ equity = 0.48x. Net debt / FY2025 EBITDA is 0.09x against a 3.0x covenant ceiling. A grey-zone Altman Z-Score near 2.4 reflects low asset turnover (0.57x) and a legacy accumulated deficit from the 2017 restructuring rather than distress. Any bear case built on balance-sheet fragility is unsupported by the filings, and the notes trading at 107.2 ($696.7M fair value on $650M face) says the credit market agrees.

The refinancing was expensive. On July 7, 2025 Tidewater issued the $650M notes and redeemed the term loan, the 10.375% 2028 notes and the 8.50% November 2026 notes, paying $19.5M of premiums, writing off ~$7.5M of unamortized costs and booking a $27.1M extinguishment loss. What it bought: an unsecured structure with all prior collateral released, a clean 2030 wall, and the undrawn revolver. Forward cash interest is roughly $60M annually, and the notes are not callable at a fixed price until July 15, 2027 (make-whole before that), so there is no cheap exit from the 9.125% coupon in the near term. Interest coverage on H1 operating income is 3.65x.

Pro forma for Wilson (~$269M net cash out, ~$231M debt assumed), cash falls to roughly $347M, debt rises to roughly $898M, net debt to roughly $551M — about 1.0x FY2025 EBITDA before counting any Wilson contribution, still far inside the covenant.

Dilution is immaterial. Total shares including all in-the-money instruments were 50,373,971 at June 30, 2026 versus 49,754,957 common — full conversion would issue 619,014 shares, 1.24% of current shares outstanding, diluting existing holders to 98.8% of prior ownership. The legacy 2017 restructuring and GulfMark warrant overhang is nearly exhausted.

**Financial health: 6.5.** Near-unlevered net position, ample liquidity, no maturity wall, no covenant risk. Offset by a 59% collapse in H1 free cash flow, an expensive coupon locked to mid-2027, drydock cash that makes EBITDA flatter cash generation, and a non-GAAP FCF definition used in compensation and investor materials ($426.0M for FY2025) that does not reconcile to the $353.3M GAAP calculation.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth has stopped. FY2025 revenue +0.5%, H1 2026 −1%, and the day-rate line is flat to down. The growth that exists is inorganic: Wilson adds 22 Brazilian PSVs from an expected September 1 close, all local regulatory approvals including CADE received and all lender change-of-control waivers obtained, with remaining conditions limited to documentation and amendments to the targets' credit facilities.

The FY2026 guidance bridge is a genuine concern. Guidance of $1.43-1.48B revenue at 49-51% gross margin was reaffirmed circa mid-June assuming the Wilson close. H1 actual revenue was $668.5M, implying H2 of $761.5-811.5M. Holding Q2 flat gives $684.6M of organic H2, leaving $76.9-126.9M from four months of Wilson plus organic growth — $19.2-31.7M per month against roughly 669 vessel-days per month for 22 PSVs, or roughly $28,700-47,400 per vessel-day at full utilization. The low end is plausible for modern Brazilian PSVs; the high end looks demanding. Critically, the guidance was set when the expected close was late Q2, implying six to seven months of contribution rather than four. Whether guidance was rebased on the August 3 call is unverified. Treat the top half of the range as difficult on the later close date and the bottom half as achievable.

Estimate revisions are mixed and thin. The current-quarter estimate is up 15.2% over 30 days and 17.8% over 90 days, but rests on a single analyst. The current-year figure has been cut 11.6% over 90 days (to $3.52) even as it rose 4.6% in the last 30. Next-year consensus of $6.26 (five analysts) is up 4.5% over 90 days and unchanged over 30. This does not meet the threshold of a strong revision signal, and the $6.26 requires scrutiny: H1 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.56, and normalizing H1 pretax income of $87.0M at 30% gives roughly $1.21 for the half, or $2.42 annualized. Getting to $6.26 requires pretax income roughly doubling from an annualized H1 base, which needs day-rate recovery, a substantially lower effective tax rate, and material Wilson accretion simultaneously.

The delivery record argues for discounting that number. Four consecutive quarters of misses: −9.0% (Jun-26), −82.4% (Mar-26), −53.1% (Dec-25), −15.4% (Sep-25). A company that has missed four straight times, twice by more than half, does not earn the benefit of the doubt on a consensus figure implying substantial earnings growth.

The longer-cycle case is intact but distant. Tidewater's own 10-Q outlook maintains "a positive outlook for a sustained upcycle." A constructive external holder (Black Bear Value Partners, Q2 2026) places the offshore capex recovery "over the next one to two years" while conceding "the near-term outlook has become somewhat less certain." The supply argument — essentially no OSV newbuild orderbook after a decade of capital starvation — is inferable from filing language and from the escalation in per-vessel transaction prices between Solstad and Wilson, but I did not verify global orderbook data independently.

**Growth prospects: 4.8.** Flat revenue, declining operating income, cost inflation running ahead of price, and a guidance range that may need rebasing. Wilson provides a dated inorganic step-up with regulatory conditions cleared, which is why this is not lower.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with what the trailing multiple actually measures. Reported EPS of $6.64 gives a 12.0x trailing P/E, and against a peer median P/E of 38.2 that looks like a deep discount. It is not. The FY2025 tax benefit of $113.2M made net income exceed pretax income by 51%, and the H1 2026 effective rate of 68.9% establishes that the rate reduction was not durable. Normalizing FY2025 pretax income at 30% gives roughly $3.05 of EPS — 24.3x at $74.12. Normalized 2026 earnings power, derived from H1 pretax income with the ~$8M of deal costs and ~$6M of Iran-related costs added back, plus partial-year Wilson, lands in a $3.00-3.50 range, or 21-25x. That is the honest earnings-based reading, and it is not cheap. The closest offshore-services comparable in the peer table, Oceaneering at 11.8x, trades at half that on a reported basis; the 38.2x peer median is dragged by Helix at 100.5x and Cactus at 55.0x and is too heterogeneous to be decisive.

Enterprise-value metrics look better. EV of $3,738.5M against FY2025 EBITDA of $544.9M is 6.86x; deducting the $98.6M of drydock cash that EBITDA ignores gives 8.38x on $446.3M of cash EBITDA. Pro forma for Wilson, EV rises to roughly $4,239M, and if the 22 modern Brazilian PSVs contribute EBITDA, the pro-forma multiple could improve, but I could not verify Wilson's EBITDA from any source, so I cannot state a pro-forma multiple. Reasonable for an asset-heavy cyclical with a clean balance sheet, and not a mispricing.

The asset check is the most useful discipline here. EV per owned vessel is $3,738.5M ÷ 204 = $18.3M. Tidewater paid $16.1M per PSV for Solstad in July 2023 and is paying $22.7M per PSV for Wilson in 2026 — 41% higher per unit three years later. The market is therefore already capitalizing the fleet between Tidewater's last two private-market transaction prices, with no discount applied for a 13.6-year average age, for 52 AHTS hulls, or for the 17 small crew and utility vessels. Net PP&E per vessel is $5.09M, so the gap between book and market is enormous — but the market has already closed most of it. Book value per share of approximately $28.00 puts the stock at 2.65x book.

Two items add modest value not in the base case. The Venezuela judgment against PDVSA, perfected via writ of attachment on PDV Holding shares, stood at approximately $85.7M including interest and $4.4M of reimbursable Special Master fees at June 30, 2026 — roughly $1.72 per share pre-tax, 2.3% of the price — with a Third Circuit hearing set for October 21, 2026. Nothing is recorded; the company calls collection "highly uncertain" with OFAC approval and competing claims outstanding. This should not be capitalized into a base case. Second, the unused $500M repurchase authorization is real optionality if management deploys it at lower prices, as its 2025 behavior suggests it would.

Analyst targets: mean $86.57 (+16.8%), median $90.00 (+21.4%), low $54.00 (−27.1%), high $117.00. At 21-25x normalized earnings the stock is not in the category where consensus targets function as stale floors — the multiple is not compressed relative to growth, and growth is currently negative at the operating-income line.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Neither stretched nor cheap. EV/EBITDA under 7x and near-private-market fleet value cap the downside; 21-25x normalized earnings, a tax-flattered headline multiple, and 2.65x book cap the upside. The prior report's 4.5 understated the improvement from a 20% decline off the April high with the balance sheet intact and Wilson conditions cleared; the earnings-quality problem prevents anything higher.

## Competitive Landscape

Tidewater claims "the broadest geographic operating footprint in the offshore vessel industry" and cites fleet size, age, diversity, geographic distribution, economies of scale and regional experience. The durable element is regulatory: local construction, flagging, ownership, employment and procurement requirements protect incumbents and block entrants. Buying Wilson buys the Brazilian flag, the crews, the Petrobras relationships and the local entity — faster and more defensible than redeploying vessels.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is flat to modestly negative. Day rates are flat overall and down 12.5% in the segment producing 40% of profit. Average fleet count has fallen from 217 to 206. Middle East demand has softened because the Iran conflict "has limited some customers' ability to operate at full capacity in the segment." Hedge fund ownership thinned from 36 portfolios at end-Q4 2025 to 31 at end-Q1 2026 per third-party tracking.

Against that, utilization improved 340bp to 78.5%, stacked vessels in West Africa fell from five to two, and the escalation in per-vessel transaction prices between 2023 and 2026 is consistent with scarce tonnage. Seasonality also matters for reading quarters: North Sea utilization is depressed November-March, the U.S. Gulf is affected June-November, and India/Southeast Asia November-April. Q1 2026 Europe/Mediterranean weakness ($8.0M of vessel operating profit versus $26.8M in Q2) was largely seasonal.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** A defensible franchise with a genuine regulatory moat, but pricing power has stopped expanding and the profit engine is deflating. Wilson strengthens the moat in one attractive jurisdiction.

## Risk Assessment

**Day-rate deflation against cost inflation.** The live risk. Day rates −0.5% year over year, cost per active day +9.1%, West Africa rates −12.5%. The FY2025 10-K outlook warns that "sustained oil prices in the low $60s per barrel may delay some drilling projects initially expected to commence in 2026," and lists industry overcapacity first among industry risks.

**Iran conflict and the Strait of Hormuz.** The 10-Q discloses that on February 28, 2026 the U.S. and Israel initiated military conflict with Iran, causing partial closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with Iran targeting assets in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Dubai, Kuwait and Bahrain. A ceasefire was negotiated but "the conflict has continued intermittently and expanded in the region." The UAE ended OPEC membership effective May 1, 2026. Quantified Middle East cost impact from March 2026: +$4.2M crew wages and travel, +$0.8M insurance, +$1.0M fuel. The net direction is ambiguous — higher oil prices should eventually support offshore capex, while immediate costs, insurance availability and customer disruption are negatives. External commentary attributes part of the Q2 share decline to the June ceasefire deflating an energy-security premium.

**IMO 2027 measures — the most under-appreciated item.** The 10-K states that a range of measures planned for 2027 implementation, including fuel standards and market-based measures, "may individually and collectively have a material impact on our profitability." That is unusually direct language, and it lands on a 13.6-year-old average fleet against a separate risk factor citing "changing customer demands for vessel specifications, which may make some of our older vessels technologically obsolete." No quantification is provided, which is precisely why this is unlikely to be in consensus models.

**Tax volatility.** Pillar Two accruals of ~$9.4M in H1 2026, $18.1M of U.S. tax on Net CFC Tested Income and Subpart F, deemed-profit regimes taxing gross revenue, and a Q1 2026 internal vessel sale that triggered $2.9M of Pillar Two top-up tax on a gain eliminated in consolidation. Effective rate 68.9% in H1. A $338.1M valuation allowance remains against $515.7M of gross deferred tax assets.

**Wilson integration and closing.** Timing already slipped from "late in the second quarter" to "around September 1," with remaining conditions including amendments to the targets' credit facilities. Integrating a 22-vessel Brazilian operation is a new-jurisdiction, new-flag-state undertaking. I could not verify Wilson's EBITDA, revenue or contracted backlog from any source, so accretion is unknown, and the $22.7M per vessel paid is 41% above the Solstad per-unit price.

**Trapped and non-USD cash.** Approximately 15% of cash held in foreign subsidiaries was awaiting USD conversion at June 30, 2026 (24% at December 31, 2025 — improving). H1 2026 FX losses of $4.1M compare to a $19.3M gain in H1 2025, a $23.4M swing across CFA/XOF francs, Norwegian kroner, Brazilian real, Angolan kwanza, sterling and euro. Euro forwards ($1.9M asset, $2.3M of H1 gains, not designated as hedges) are a partial mitigant against exposures that are largely to euro-pegged African currencies rather than the euro itself.

**Concentration and political risk.** Top-ten customers at 47.9% of revenue with no meaningful switching costs; the 10-K states the loss of any significant customer could have a material adverse effect. Operations in 30+ countries under local content, currency and ownership requirements, with direct historical experience of tail risk in PDVSA's 2009 Venezuelan seizure. U.K. and Norwegian union coverage plus industry-wide multi-employer defined benefit participation carries withdrawal-liability exposure.

**Unverified filings.** A July 6, 2026 8-K (Item 8.01) could not be retrieved; its contents are unknown but material by classification. Item 5.02 filings dated March 19 and June 18, 2026 plus MD&A reference to "charges associated with a transition and separation agreement" indicate at least one senior departure whose identity is unconfirmed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 66.2% sits 14.0 points above 52.2% one-year realized volatility and in the 92nd percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (66.2% at 16 days versus 46.4% at 163 days) and a ±12.0% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Put/call open interest is 1.20. Notably, since the July 19 snapshot ATM IV rose from 51.1% to 66.2% while the price fell 5.7%, and put/call open interest moved from 0.01 to 1.20 — a large structural change in positioning. The Q2 print has already occurred (August 3), so the near-term premium is being paid for something else: the Wilson close around September 1, ongoing Hormuz-linked oil volatility, and potentially the October 21 Third Circuit hearing. The 1-month put/call skew is characterized as balanced, so the structure prices magnitude rather than a clear directional view. Combined with 15.9% short interest and 7.0 days to cover, positioning is two-sided: enough short base to fuel a squeeze on any positive Wilson or day-rate surprise, and enough protection demand to indicate institutions are paying up for near-term downside coverage. This is consistent with a HOLD and inconsistent with either a confident long or a confident short.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** Multiple live items that are unquantified in models — IMO 2027 fuel standards, tax rate volatility, Wilson accretion, a possible guidance rebase — against a balance sheet that removes solvency risk and a Venezuela judgment worth roughly $1.72 per share of free optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $100 (+34.9%)

Offshore capex recovers on schedule as mature onshore plays moderate, and the OSV fleet cannot be replicated: no meaningful newbuild orderbook, per-vessel prices up 41% in three years. Day rates resume expanding in 2027 while the H1 2026 cost spike proves largely transitory — the ~$6.0M of Iran-related costs, the identified "high-cost repairs," and elevated mobilization spend reverse, and mobilization converts into revenue. Wilson closes September 1 and proves accretive, adding modern Brazilian PSVs at scarcity-value pricing in a local-content-protected market with Petrobras demand. West Africa's utilization gain (63.2% → 78.4%) eventually shows up in pricing as idle tonnage clears. The effective tax rate normalizes toward the 25-30% range. Management deploys the untouched $500M authorization — 13.6% of the market cap — at prices closer to the 2025 average of $39.30 than to today's. The Third Circuit affirms the Venezuela waterfall and $85.7M arrives. At 8.0x pro-forma EBITDA of $700M less net debt of $551M, the equity is worth roughly $101 per share. A 15.9% short base amplifies any of these.

### Bear Case — $52 (−29.8%)

Day-rate deflation broadens from West Africa to the rest of the fleet as low-$60s oil delays 2026-2027 projects, while cost inflation persists because crew, insurance and repair costs are structural rather than cyclical. The spread per active day continues compressing from $13,052 and the divest-and-stack lever that has held revenue flat exhausts itself. FY2026 guidance is rebased downward as the four-month Wilson contribution cannot fill the $76.9-126.9M H2 gap. Wilson integration in a new jurisdiction consumes management attention and costs more than modeled, with $231M of assumed debt and net debt rising to roughly 1.0x EBITDA before any contribution, and no cheap refinancing of the 9.125% coupon before July 2027. IMO 2027 fuel standards and market-based measures impose either capital spend on a 13.6-year fleet or accelerated retirement of older tonnage. The effective tax rate stays in the 50-69% range on jurisdictional mix. Investors reprice from the tax-flattered 12x headline toward 21-25x normalized earnings and conclude the multiple is full. At 5.5x EBITDA of $560M less $551M net debt, equity is worth roughly $51. The $54 analyst low target sits right there.

### Score Trajectory

Scores have declined for five consecutive reports, from 6.1 in May to 5.1 in July, with valuation −2.5, growth −2.0 and competitive position −2.0 over that span. ## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which the spread per active day expands sequentially from the Q2 2026 level of $13,052 — day rates rising while cost per active day is flat or falling. This is the single metric that determines whether the cycle is still working.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance rebased below $1.40B, or gross margin guidance cut below 49%, indicating the day-rate environment is deteriorating faster than the cost base can adjust.
2. West Africa day-rate deflation spreading — a second segment printing a double-digit year-over-year day-rate decline, or consolidated average day rates falling below $22,000.
3. Wilson closing conditions failing (credit facility amendments not obtained) or a post-close disclosure that the acquired operation is loss-making or materially below the implied ~$28,700 per vessel-day required at the low end of guidance.
4. Any quantification of IMO 2027 compliance capital spend exceeding $150M, or a fleet impairment charge.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Tidewater is a well-financed operator of a scarce asset base whose unit economics have stopped improving. The balance sheet is not in question: $616.1M of cash against $666.7M of face debt, net debt of $50.6M at 0.09x EBITDA, an undrawn $250M revolver, nothing due before July 2030, and notes trading at 107.2. That removes the solvency leg of any bear case and is why this is not an AVOID.

What prevents a BUY is the combination of three things. The operating spread per active day has inflected from +8.5% in FY2025 to −6.5% in H1 2026, driven by 9.1% cost inflation against flat day rates and 12.5% rate deflation in the segment producing 40% of segment profit. The headline 12x P/E is an artifact of a non-repeatable $113.2M deferred tax benefit; on normalized earnings of $3.00-3.50 the stock trades at 21-25x, with the H1 effective rate of 68.9% confirming the tax relief was one-time. And at $18.3M of enterprise value per owned vessel, the market is already paying between Tidewater's own 2023 Solstad price of $16.1M and its 2026 Wilson price of $22.7M, so there is no asset-value discount to fund a margin of safety on a 13.6-year-old fleet facing 2027 fuel standards that management itself says "may have a material impact on our profitability."

Four consecutive earnings misses, two of them exceeding 50%, argue for discounting the $6.26 next-year consensus that would make the stock look cheap. Near-term implied volatility 14 points above realized in backwardation, 15.9% short interest with 7.0 days to cover, and an unused $500M repurchase authorization together describe a genuinely two-sided setup rather than a mispricing in either direction. Existing holders have asset support and a strong balance sheet to wait on; new capital should wait for either evidence that the spread per active day is expanding again or a price near $58-62, where enterprise value per vessel would approach the 2023 transaction price and the base case would offer 25%+ upside.
