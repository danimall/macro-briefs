## Executive Summary

Bank of Montreal has produced a real, measurable improvement in returns. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) delivered adjusted diluted EPS of C$3.67 against C$2.62 a year earlier, with reported ROE of 13.0% and adjusted ROE of 13.5% versus 9.4%/9.8%. Decomposing that improvement matters: implied average common equity was C$78.5B in Q2 FY2026 against C$79.3B in Q2 FY2025, down roughly 1.1%, meaning approximately 97% of the 3.6pp ROE gain came from higher earnings rather than a shrinking denominator. The bank has also beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters by 5.4% to 8.5%. Management's stated ambition, laid out at a March 26, 2026 investor day, is ROE above 15% by 2028.

The problem is price. Over the 21 months from October 31, 2024 to August 10, 2026, book value per share rose from C$104.40 to approximately C$112, about 7%, while the price-to-book multiple expanded from 1.22x to roughly 2.28x — an 87% multiple expansion. At C$254.99 the stock trades at 18.7x trailing adjusted EPS of C$13.65, 17.6x the FY2026 consensus of C$14.51 and 15.6x the FY2027 consensus of C$16.30, against a Canadian big-six history closer to 10–12x forward earnings. On a single-stage justified price-to-book framework, the current 13.5% quarterly adjusted-ROE run rate, a 9.5% cost of equity and 4% growth support roughly 1.73x book, or about C$194 — near the C$193.47 average price at which BMO itself repurchased 6.0M shares during Q2 FY2026. Reaching 2.3x requires both full delivery of a sustainable ROE above 15% and a cost of equity below roughly 9%.

The sell side agrees the stock is ahead of itself. Consensus is Hold across 14 analysts, and every reported target sits below the current quote: the USD target range is $143.11 to $180.03 with a mean of $163.16, against a spot price of $183.03. That is unusual — analyst targets typically lag genuinely inflecting stocks — and here estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting (+2.0% for the current year and +1.0% for next year over 90 days), which removes the "consensus is stale" defense. Two earnings-quality issues sharpen the case for trimming: performing-loan provisions were C$5M in Q2 FY2026, C$5M in Q4 FY2025 and C$24M in Q3 FY2025, effectively nil for three of the last four disclosed quarters, so the headline PCL decline reflects the end of overlay building rather than lower realized loss content (impaired PCL was 0.46% of average net loans in FY2025 versus 0.47% in FY2024); and loans grew 0.03% in FY2025 while deposits fell 0.6%, so revenue growth of 10.6% came from margin, fees and C$467M of FX translation, not volume.

We rate BMO **REDUCE**. Base case C$235 (US$168, −7.9%), bull C$285 (US$204, +11.6%), bear C$196 (US$140, −23.2%). The business is sound; the entry point is not.

## Company Overview

BMO is one of Canada's Big Six banks and, in its own May 2026 language, "the eighth largest bank in North America by assets." At October 31, 2025 it held C$1.4768 trillion of assets, C$682.9B of gross loans and acceptances, C$976.2B of deposits, C$864.9B of assets under administration and C$506.7B of assets under management.

Effective Q4 FY2025 the bank reorganized into four reportable segments, moving the US wealth business into US Personal & Commercial to create a unified US Banking segment, with priors restated. The resulting FY2025 disclosure is the clearest statement of the investment case:

| Segment | FY2025 net income | Avg. allocated common equity | Adjusted ROE | FY2024 adj. ROE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canadian P&C | C$3,295M | C$16,744M (21.4%) | 19.8% | 21.5% |
| US Banking | C$2,810M | C$37,075M (47.5%) | 8.1% | 6.4% |
| Wealth Management | C$1,381M | C$3,028M (3.9%) | 45.6% | 36.5% |
| Capital Markets | C$1,977M | C$13,786M (17.6%) | 14.2% | 11.4% |
| Corporate Services | C$(738)M | C$7,493M (9.6%) | n/a | n/a |
| **Total** | **C$8,725M** | **C$78,126M** | **11.3%** | **9.8%** |

US Banking consumes 47.5% of average allocated common equity at an 8.1% adjusted return; Wealth Management consumes 3.9% at 45.6%. The gap between group adjusted ROE of 11.3% and a mid-teens peer-quality return sits almost entirely in the US franchise built out through the Bank of the West acquisition.

One measurement caveat: BMO raised its segment capital allocation rate to 12.0% of risk-weighted assets in FY2025 from 11.5% in FY2024, mechanically depressing every segment's FY2025 ROE. Roughly 0.9pp of Canadian P&C's 1.7pp apparent decline is attributable to that denominator change (21.5% × [1 − 1/1.0435]), so the Canadian franchise deteriorated less than the headline suggests.

Note a reporting-regime issue that affects how any screen treats this name. BMO files on Form 40-F under the Multijurisdictional Disclosure System and furnishes interim results on Form 6-K; it does not file 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A or US-GAAP XBRL company facts, and its insiders report on Canada's SEDI system rather than Section 16. An "insufficient data" or "no insider filings" output for BMO is a filing-regime artifact and should be scored neither positively nor negatively. Bank solvency here is governed by OSFI capital and liquidity ratios, which BMO does disclose. Our analysis rests on BMO's own results releases; we did not read the 40-F, the audited FY2025 statements or the annual MD&A risk discussion, which is a genuine limitation on the risk-factor section below.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus (in CAD, on an adjusted basis, matching the four-quarter surprise history where actuals of C$3.67 and C$3.48 correspond exactly to reported Q2 and Q1 FY2026 adjusted EPS) is C$14.51 for FY2026 and C$16.30 for FY2027 — implying 19% and 12% growth off FY2025 adjusted EPS of C$12.16. Revisions are stable: +0.1% over 30 days and +2.0% over 90 days for the current year, +1.0% over 90 days for next year. This is not the estimate-revision inflection pattern (>30% over 90 days) that reliably precedes further re-rating; it is a mature franchise where consensus has crept modestly higher.

A first-principles check on FY2026: H1 actual adjusted EPS is C$7.15. Annualizing Q2's C$3.67 gives C$14.68, but that overstates a normalized figure. Performing-loan provisions of C$5M compare to an FY2025 average of roughly C$117M per quarter; normalizing costs about C$112M pre-tax, roughly C$0.12 per quarter or C$0.48 annually at the 24.4% effective tax rate. Q2 Capital Markets net income rose 47% year over year and Insurance net income rose 47% "primarily due to favourable market movements," neither of which is a run rate. Expense seasonality is real — Q4 FY2025 non-interest expense of C$5,556M was 8.8% above Q3's C$5,105M. A defensible normalized FY2026 adjusted EPS is C$14.0–14.5, placing the C$14.51 consensus at the top of that range rather than conservatively below it. The four-quarter beat streak (average 7.2%) argues management guides carefully; the normalization argues H2 is harder.

Where does growth come from over the next two years? Not from the balance sheet. Loans were flat in FY2025, deposits fell, and the bank is shrinking assets deliberately: 138 US branches are being divested (C$102M goodwill write-down in Q4 FY2025 plus C$26M pre-tax in Q2 FY2026), a transportation and vendor finance book reported externally at approximately C$14.5B (2.12% of gross loans) is being sold to Stonepeak, and two significant risk transfers on roughly $5B of corporate loans were completed in late July 2026. Management's Q2 loan-growth language is precise — "solid **sequential** commercial banking loan growth in both Canada and the United States" — and US Banking revenue growth came from non-interest revenue and NIM "partially offset by lower balances."

Growth is therefore a return-on-capital story: raise US Banking ROE from 8.1%, grow Wealth Management (Burgundy consolidated, Euroz Hartleys' Australian capital markets business announced), and keep the efficiency ratio improving (57.6% TTM versus 58.2% FY2025). The arithmetic of the 15% target is demanding. Closing the group gap from 11.3% to 15.0% on the FY2025 average equity base requires roughly C$2,891M of additional net income available to common (0.037 × 78,126). Sourced entirely from US Banking, its ROE would need to rise from 8.1% to about 15.9% — approximately a doubling. Progress is real (6.4% → 8.1% in FY2025, with US Banking adjusted net income up 30% in USD terms in Q2 FY2026), and divesting sub-hurdle books mechanically helps the ratio, but the path is multi-year.

**Growth prospects: 5.8.** Solid EPS trajectory with a credible ROE improvement plan, undermined by zero volume growth, deliberate balance-sheet shrinkage, roughly 3pp of buyback contribution to EPS, and stable rather than accelerating estimates.

Two independent frameworks give the same answer.

**Price-to-book against realized returns.** A single-stage justified P/B = (ROE − g) ÷ (CoE − g). At the current 13.5% quarterly adjusted-ROE run rate, a 9.5% cost of equity and 4% growth: (0.135 − 0.04) ÷ 0.055 = 1.73x, or approximately C$194. At the 15% 2028 target: 2.0x at a 9.5% CoE, 2.2x at 9.0%, 2.4x at 8.5%. The current 2.28x therefore requires simultaneous delivery of a sustainable ROE above 15% and a cost of equity below roughly 9%. Both may happen. Neither is a margin of safety. The alternative explanation we cannot rule out is a genuine structural decline in the required return on large Canadian bank equity.

**Earnings power.** Normalized adjusted EPS at 13.5% ROE on ~C$112 of book is approximately C$15.1; at 15% ROE, C$16.8. Against C$254.99 that is 16.9x and 15.2x. Canadian big-six banks have historically traded nearer 10–12x forward earnings. Even granting a re-rating of the group to 13–14x on structurally better returns, the current multiple embeds full execution.

**Sell side.** Consensus is Hold across 14 analysts. USD targets: low $143.11, mean $163.16, median $166.34, high $180.03 — the *high* target is 1.6% below spot. Post-Q2 revisions were uniformly upward and uniformly still below the price (Scotiabank to C$239, TD C$239, CIBC C$244, Desjardins C$230, BofA C$230). Rating distribution has hardened slightly bearish over three months: strong-buy/buy 5, hold 6, sell/strong-sell 3, versus 4/7/3 three months ago. On an inflection stock with rapidly rising estimates, treating targets as stale floors is often correct. Here estimates are flat and the multiple is at a decade-plus high, so the targets are better read as an independent constraint.

**What the market is paying for.** From October 31, 2024 (C$126.88 on C$104.40 book, 1.22x) to October 31, 2025 (C$174.23 on C$111.57, 1.56x) to August 10, 2026 (C$254.99 on ~C$112, 2.28x): book value per share up about 7%, multiple up about 87%. The +64% one-year move is not a fundamental problem in itself — but it was delivered by multiple expansion.

**Valuation: 3.0.** No identifiable mispricing in the bank's favor; the multiple prices full delivery of a 2028 target with no cushion, and a reversion to the ROE-justified 1.75x book costs roughly 24% with no change in earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

BMO operates in a concentrated Canadian oligopoly and as a mid-tier super-regional in the United States. Trajectory by franchise:

**Canadian P&C** is the profit engine at 19.8% adjusted ROE on 21.4% of allocated equity, but FY2025 net income fell 4.7% (roughly half of which is the capital-allocation methodology change) before recovering to +15% in Q2 FY2026 on 5% revenue growth from NIM, card and mutual-fund distribution fees, "partially offset by lower deposit fee revenue." Declining deposit fees in a Canadian rate-cutting environment is a sector headwind, not a BMO-specific defect.

**US Banking** is being optimized for return rather than scale — the correct response to an 8.1% ROE, and the single clearest evidence of management discipline in the file. Selling 138 branches, exiting transportation/vendor finance, and executing SRTs all raise segment ROE by removing sub-hurdle assets. The cost is that BMO's US growth optionality shrinks, and the Stonepeak structure leaves BMO holding roughly 19.9% of the divested entity, so it is a partial rather than clean exit.

**Wealth Management** at 45.6% adjusted ROE on 3.9% of equity is the highest-quality piece of the bank and the one being expanded (Burgundy consolidated with contingent consideration outstanding; Euroz Hartleys announced). If BMO can shift the capital mix toward this segment, the 15% group target becomes materially easier.

**Capital Markets** improved to 14.2% adjusted ROE from 11.4%, with Q2 net income up 47% year over year — a markets-cycle-dependent result, not a run rate, and a hire from UBS to lead US M&A signals intent to build rather than harvest.

The bank's own award citations (Best Commercial Bank in Canada, Best Bank in North America) are issuer press releases referencing third-party awards and carry no evidentiary weight on moat durability.

**Competitive position: 6.0.** Structurally protected Canadian oligopoly position, a genuinely excellent and expanding wealth franchise, and a US franchise whose returns are improving from a low base — but flat loans, declining deposit fees, and a shrinking asset base mean share is not being gained.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The performing-loan allowance has no remaining cushion.** Performing provisions were C$5M in Q2 FY2026, C$5M in Q4 FY2025 and C$24M in Q3 FY2025, against C$470M for FY2025 as a whole. Meanwhile impaired PCL of C$734M in Q2 FY2026 was only 4% below the prior year's C$765M, and the impaired PCL ratio was 0.46% in FY2025 versus 0.47% in FY2024. BMO attributes the near-nil performing provision to "the net impact of model changes, largely offset by portfolio credit migration and lower portfolio balances." The reasonable interpretation is that the headline 30% PCL decline reflects the cessation of overlay building rather than lower realized loss content, so a credit turn would push provisions up from a base with nothing to release. The alternative — that improved macro scenarios and smaller portfolio balances genuinely justify no build — cannot be excluded without the allowance disclosures in the Q2 Report to Shareholders. We infer no management motive from the provisioning outcome; the point is that the P&L cushion is gone either way.

**2. Canadian consumer and housing leverage.** BMO names this explicitly in its own forward-looking risk enumeration, and Canadian Press coverage of the Q2 release reported the bank staying "vigilant" amid "signs of pressure building on consumers" (headline and snippet only; the full article and transcript were not retrievable). A Canadian retail credit normalization would hit the highest-ROE, most heavily weighted domestic segment.

**3. Valuation de-rating requires no operating deterioration.** A move to 1.75x book — the level the current 13.5% ROE run rate supports at a 9.5% cost of equity — is roughly C$194, about 24% below spot, at unchanged earnings.

**4. Q3 FY2026 print mechanics (August 25).** The Stonepeak transaction met held-for-sale criteria in Q3, triggering an expected charge of approximately C$1.1B pre-tax / C$0.9B after-tax, primarily goodwill in Corporate Services, treated as an adjusting item. That is roughly C$1.29 per share of GAAP drag (C$0.9B ÷ 700.4M shares). Reported EPS will look materially weaker than adjusted, and any comparison to "consensus" must be like-for-like; mixing bases produces a spurious ~C$1.29 miss. Closing remains subject to regulatory approvals in Q4 FY2026.

**5. Potential G-SIB designation.** BMO explicitly lists the possibility of being designated a global systemically important bank, with consequences for funding costs and capital requirements. For a bank now describing itself as eighth-largest in North America, this is specific and rarely modelled.

**6. Tariffs and trade policy.** BMO's risk language names tariffs, countermeasures and mitigation policies. The franchise is concentrated in Canadian commercial lending and US Midwest commercial banking, both trade-exposed, and the transportation finance book being sold is directly levered to freight volumes.

**7. FX cuts both ways, including for USD-based holders.** FX added C$93M of net income and C$0.13 of EPS in FY2025; it reversed in Q2 FY2026, reducing US Banking net income 5%, revenue 4% and expenses 3%. FX also moves CET1 through source-currency RWA and is cited in every recent quarter's CET1 decline. For a US-dollar investor, part of the +61% NYSE gain over the past year is CAD appreciation, which is reversible independent of the bank's performance.

**8. Simultaneous portfolio reshaping.** Two divestitures and two acquisitions are in flight alongside a reorganized US segment and a new internal AI institute. Execution risk is elevated relative to a steady-state year.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** The specific, not-yet-priced items — a zero-cushion allowance into a softening Canadian consumer, 24% de-rating risk at unchanged earnings, and G-SIB optionality — outweigh the genuine strengths of deposit funding and regulatory capital headroom.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 19.9% against one-year historical volatility of 19.9% — precisely fair, with an IV percentile of 18% within the current chain. Skew is balanced (0.1%), and the term structure is in contango: 19.9% at the August 21 tenor, stepping up to 25.0% for September 18 (38 days) and settling at 22.4% for November 20. The September tenor is the one spanning the August 25 Q3 print, and its ±6.3% implied move versus ±3.0% for the pre-earnings expiry is consistent with event pricing around that report — plausibly reflecting the GAAP/adjusted confusion the C$1.29 charge will create rather than a directional view.

Positioning is mixed and not decisive. The put/call volume ratio of 5.33 is elevated but is single-session flow on a modestly liquid US line; open interest is call-tilted at 0.52. Short interest of 4.04M shares is about 0.58% of the 700.4M share count, with 7.6 days to cover — an uncrowded position, meaning few sophisticated investors have pressed a bearish view. Read together: the options market prices no dislocation and provides no confirmation of the de-rating thesis. Cheap options are not available as an expression either way; a holder wanting to protect gains is buying protection at fair rather than depressed levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to C$285 / US$204, +11.6%)

The ROE improvement is real and earnings-driven: 97% of the Q2 ROE gain came from higher net income, not capital shrinkage. US Banking adjusted ROE rose from 6.4% to 8.1% in FY2025 and net income grew 30% in USD terms in Q2 FY2026, and every divestiture (138 branches, C$14.5B transportation/vendor book, ~$5B of SRTs) mechanically lifts the ratio by removing sub-hurdle assets. Wealth Management earns 45.6% on 3.9% of equity and is being expanded. The efficiency ratio has improved to 57.6% TTM from 58.2%, with adjusted operating leverage of +4.3% in FY2025. Four consecutive beats averaging 7.2% suggest consensus of C$14.51 for FY2026 and C$16.30 for FY2027 is beatable. If the bank tracks to 15% ROE by 2028 and the market's required return on Canadian bank equity is genuinely lower than the historical 9.5%, 2.4x book on ~C$120 of FY2027 book value supports approximately C$288, and 17.5x FY2027 consensus supports C$285. Dividend growth continues at roughly 5% annually on a 50% adjusted payout.

### Bear Case (to C$196 / US$140, −23.2%)

The multiple is doing the work the fundamentals have not. Book value per share grew 7% over 21 months while the P/B multiple expanded 87%. The performing allowance is exhausted after three of four quarters at effectively zero build, while impaired losses have barely improved (0.46% versus 0.47%), so the credit line item can only get worse from here — and BMO's largest, highest-return franchise is levered to a Canadian consumer that the bank itself describes as showing pressure. Loans grew 0.03% in FY2025 and deposits fell; the balance sheet is shrinking by roughly 2.1% of loans from the Stonepeak sale alone. CET1 has fallen 50bp over five quarters while the bank repurchases stock at 2.3x book — a materially worse use of capital than the C$193.47 average paid in Q2. If ROE plateaus near the current 13.5% and the cost of equity stays at 9.5%, justified P/B is 1.73x, or C$194. Twelve of fourteen analysts see the stock as fairly or over-valued and the highest published target is below spot.

### Weighing them

Assign 40% to base (−7.9% price, −5.2% total return with the 2.68% yield), 25% to bull (+11.6% price, +14.3% total) and 35% to bear (−23.2% price, −20.5% total). Probability-weighted total return is approximately −6%. The asymmetry is the decisive point: roughly 12 points of upside against 23 points of downside, with the downside requiring no operating deterioration at all. That is the profile of a well-run bank at the wrong price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Twelve-month horizon.

**Base case: C$235 / US$168 (−7.9% from US$183.03).** Derivation: 14.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of C$16.30, equivalent to approximately 2.0x an estimated FY2027 book value of ~C$118 at a 14% ROE. This sits close to the yfinance USD mean of $163.16.

**Bull case: C$285 / US$204 (+11.6%).** 17.5x FY2027 consensus, or 2.4x ~C$120 book — requires visible progress toward 15% ROE and a sustained sub-9% cost of equity.

**Bear case: C$196 / US$140 (−23.2%).** 1.75x book at the current 13.5% ROE run rate and a 9.5% cost of equity, equivalently ~12x FY2027 consensus.

## Conclusion

BMO's operating improvement is genuine and well-evidenced: adjusted ROE of 13.5% in Q2 FY2026 against 9.8% a year earlier, 97% of it earnings-driven, four consecutive beats, an efficiency ratio improving to 57.6%, and a disciplined program of selling sub-hurdle assets to lift returns on the 47.5% of equity trapped in an 8.1%-ROE US franchise. Management has committed publicly to ROE above 15% by 2028, and the wealth franchise gives them a credible mix-shift lever.

The share price has already paid for that outcome and then some. At 2.28x book on a 12.5% trailing ROE, 18.7x trailing adjusted earnings and 15.6x FY2027 consensus, the multiple requires both full delivery of a 15%-plus return and a cost of equity below 9%. Book value per share grew 7% over 21 months while the multiple expanded 87%. The performing-loan allowance has been drawn to zero for three of the last four quarters while impaired losses have barely improved, so the provision line offers no further help and considerable potential harm if Canadian consumer credit softens as the bank itself hints. CET1 has slipped 50bp over five quarters while the company repurchases stock at prices roughly 32% above where it last bought. Consensus is Hold and the highest published target is below spot.

Probability-weighted total return over twelve months is approximately −6%, with roughly 12 points of upside against 23 points of downside that requires no earnings deterioration to materialize. **REDUCE.** Revisit near C$205 / US$147, or on two consecutive quarters of adjusted ROE at or above 14.5% with US Banking above 10%.
