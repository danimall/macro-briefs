**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Core Scientific is midway through a business transformation from Bitcoin self-mining to high-density colocation (HDC) for AI and cloud workloads. The pivot is showing hard evidence of working. Colocation revenue grew from $10.6M in Q2 2025 to $136.7M in Q2 2026, carrying a 59% segment gross margin, and consolidated gross margin lifted from 6% to 43% over the same window. Adjusted EBITDA turned to +$41.1M in Q2 2026 and first-half operating cash flow swung positive to +$231M. The strategic asset underneath this is scarce: energized, permitted power sites under long-term utility contracts, which are the binding constraint on AI data-center buildouts given multi-year interconnection queues. That scarcity is why CoreWeave offered ~$9B for the company in July 2025, and why shareholders rejected it in October 2025 — a decision the subsequent rally and the AMD deal have so far vindicated.

The counterweight is a balance sheet and accounting profile that make this a difficult company to underwrite with conviction. Long-term debt jumped from $1.06B at year-end 2025 to $4.30B by Q2 2026, funding roughly $1.19B of first-half capex against deeply negative free cash flow. The GAAP picture is genuinely distorted: the $1.16B Q2 net loss is roughly 90% a non-cash warrant/CVR remeasurement that mechanically grows as the stock rises, and the –$2.42B stockholders' deficit is largely a warrant-liability artifact rather than economic insolvency (liquidity is $1.82B). Reported numbers materially understate the operating business. Two items resist that benign reading: a March 2, 2026 Item 4.02 non-reliance restatement, and an auditor transition (KPMG/Marcum) in the same twelve-month window. For a company whose entire financial statements hinge on complex, judgment-heavy fair-value accounting, an unresolved restatement of unknown scope is a real accounting-quality flag.

The result is a two-sided setup that does not clear the bar for a BUY. The operating inflection, the power-secured land moat, and the AMD validation are real. So are the leverage, the funding dependence through the 2025–2027 build phase, the 2027 revenue-timing gap on the anchor contract, the restatement overhang, and 22.6% short interest with backwardated options pricing near-term stress. We rate CORZ **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: the evidence supports neither confident accumulation nor a short. We lay out the specific conditions that would move us either way.

## Company Overview

Core Scientific is a Delaware-incorporated operator (HQ Dover, DE; operations centered in Miami, FL) of large-scale, purpose-built data centers. The company went public via SPAC in January 2022, filed Chapter 11 in December 2022 during the crypto downturn, and emerged from bankruptcy on January 23, 2024 with a restructured capital stack of secured convertible notes, secured notes, contingent value rights, and warrants (several tranches of which now trade separately as CORZW and CORZZ). That emergence structure is the source of the warrant-liability accounting that dominates the reported income statement.

The business now runs three segments. Colocation (HDC) is the growth engine, leasing purpose-built data-center capacity to AI and cloud customers at high margin. Digital-asset self-mining is legacy and now loss-making (Q2 2026 segment gross margin –56%), being wound down or repurposed to colocation. Digital-asset hosted mining is a small (~37% margin) legacy line. The physical footprint spans Texas (4 sites), Georgia (2), Alabama, Kentucky, North Carolina, North Dakota, and Oklahoma, anchored by long-term utility power contracts (TVA, Duke Energy, municipal utilities). Control of energized, permitted power capacity is the core competitive asset and the reason the company became an acquisition target.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue-mix inflection is the entire story, and it requires reading past the full-year headline. Total revenue fell from $510.7M (2024) to $319.0M (2025) because mining revenue collapsed faster than colocation ramped. The quarterly trajectory shows the pivot taking hold:

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colocation revenue | $10.6M | $77.5M | $136.7M |
| Total revenue | $78.6M | $115.2M | $164.2M |
| Consolidated gross margin | 6% | 26% | 43% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $28.5M | $8.9M | $41.1M |
| Net loss | $(936.8)M | $(347.2)M | $(1,155.3)M |

Colocation is an attractive business at 59% segment gross margin, running at roughly $635M annualized revenue on 437 MW of billing capacity by mid-July. Management cites ~1.1 GW leased and >$24B of "potential contracted revenue," but this figure is company-defined and unaudited: direction of travel, not a booked result.

The GAAP net loss is dominated by non-cash items, not operating deterioration. The Q2 2026 net loss was driven by a $1.05B non-cash change in the fair value of warrants and CVRs. Because these instruments are classified as liabilities and marked to market, a rising stock price mechanically produces a larger GAAP loss. The stockholders' deficit at June 30, 2026 (widened from –$962.7M at year-end 2025) reflects $1.98B of combined warrant liabilities plus an accumulated deficit of –$5.65B, most of it accounting rather than cash economics. Liquidity is healthy: $1.77B cash plus digital assets, $1.82B total, with restricted cash of ~$782M (likely project/debt escrows).

The funding model is where the picture is genuinely stressed. Long-term debt rose from $1.06B to $4.30B, with $4.275B of new debt raised in H1 2026 (and $1.0B paid down) to fund the buildout. First-half operating cash flow turned positive at +$231M, but capex was ~$1.19B ($954M PP&E + $232M land/development). Free cash flow is deeply negative, and the model depends on continued access to debt markets to complete builds ahead of contracted revenue that does not begin flowing meaningfully until 2027. This is the central financial tension: operations are inflecting, but the company is not self-funding.

The earnings-surprise record underscores how noisy the reported numbers are. Over the last four quarters, CORZ posted a +587.8% EPS surprise (Q4 2025) and a –1,380.4% surprise (Q1 2026), both artifacts of mark-to-market swings rather than operating volatility of that magnitude. This makes consensus EPS a nearly useless anchor for the stock.

We score Financial Health a 4.0. The operating cash-flow inflection and $1.8B liquidity argue against distress, and the negative equity is largely an accounting artifact. But the 4x debt increase in one half-year, deep negative FCF, external-funding dependence, and an unresolved restatement together warrant a below-average score. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator we track, and this profile does not clear the bar for a neutral rating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth setup is the strongest part of the thesis. Colocation revenue is compounding sequentially (+77% Q1→Q2 2026), consolidated gross margin is expanding, and adjusted EBITDA has inflected positive. This sits directly inside a structural demand theme with broad confirmation across the AI infrastructure complex: hyperscaler capex, power-generation demand, and data-center electrical and structural buildout are all confirming the load surge even where the chip layer has shown isolated softness. Power-secured land is the scarce input, and Core Scientific controls it at scale.

The AMD partnership announced July 28, 2026 is the anchor. It is a commercial arrangement potentially up to 2.5 GW: 15-year leases (with 3× five-year renewals) on ~529–530 MW of critical IT capacity, of which AMD directly takes 377 MW and ~152 MW goes to unnamed neocloud operators under AMD credit backstop. AMD received a warrant for up to 30M CORZ shares at $23.47, vesting 12,222 shares per MW brought online (~6.5M vested at signing). The structure mirrors the Nvidia–IREN warrant-for-capacity model, aligning the chipmaker's economics with the landlord hosting its silicon. AMD stock fell >5% on the news while CORZ rose ~10%, indicating the market read the deal as more favorable to Core Scientific.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, the flagship AMD revenue does not begin until 2027, and the full 2.5 GW headline depends on AMD electing to call up to 1,925 MW of uncommitted additional capacity through December 2028. Between now and 2027 the company spends ~$1B+/quarter of debt-funded capex against back-loaded revenue. Second, the estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than the clean +30% inflection signal that would justify a stronger call: current-year EPS estimates improved +10.3% over 30 days but next-year estimates fell –5.8% (30d) and –29.4% (90d), and the estimates themselves are compromised by warrant-driven distortion. The revenue growth is real and broad-based; the earnings-visibility signal is not yet aligned.

We score Growth Prospects a 7.5. The revenue trajectory, margin expansion, and AMD validation are high-conviction; the 2027 timing gap and mixed estimate revisions keep it from higher.

## Valuation Assessment

CORZ cannot be valued on trailing or forward P/E: the reported earnings are dominated by non-cash warrant remeasurement, and the forward P/E of –6.2 is meaningless. The company must be valued on EBITDA power, contracted capacity, and the option value of the land/power portfolio.

On the operating base, Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $41.1M annualizes to ~$164M, but this understates the run-rate given the sequential ramp: by mid-July, ~$635M annualized colocation revenue at 59% margin implies a colocation gross-profit run-rate approaching $375M before overhead and legacy mining drag. Against a ~$6.65B market cap and ~$10.9B enterprise value (adding ~$4.3B net debt), the stock trades at a demanding multiple on current EBITDA but a more reasonable one on the contracted-capacity trajectory into 2027, when AMD revenue begins. The peer cohort (IREN, Hut 8, and other crypto-miner-to-AI-neocloud names) has been richly re-rated on the same power-scarcity thesis, so pre-pivot mining multiples are the wrong benchmark.

The analyst target range is wide and informative: low $25, median $31.50, high $55, mean $33.17, all above the current $20.74. On a stock with genuine forward optionality, targets this far above spot would ordinarily read as a floor. Two things cut against treating them that way here. The sentiment trend on those targets is flagged as degrading, and the stock has fallen ~32% from its June high of $30.46 to $20.74, a decline steep enough that the burden of proof shifts to the bull. The market is pricing something: most plausibly the restatement overhang, the 2027 revenue-timing gap, and funding-condition sensitivity. Sophisticated holders are selling, and the specific thing they may be discounting is the combination of accounting uncertainty and the multi-year gap between capex outlay and anchor-contract revenue.

We score Valuation a 5.0. There is credible upside on the contracted-capacity thesis if execution holds, but no clean margin of safety, the earnings base is unmeasurable on standard metrics, and the discount to targets is offset by identifiable overhangs rather than pure mispricing. Cheapness on distorted earnings is not a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Core Scientific's moat is control of energized, permitted power capacity under long-term utility contracts, the scarcest input in AI infrastructure. Interconnection queues run multiple years, so incumbent secured sites cannot be replicated on a competitive timeline. This is the asset that drew the CoreWeave bid and made shareholders confident enough to reject it.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. Being selected as a primary AMD landlord — with AMD willing to backstop tenant credit for 152 MW — validates the site portfolio against a field that includes IREN, Hut 8, and Nscale. The warrant-for-capacity model (Nvidia/IREN, now AMD/CORZ) is emerging as a sector template that ties chipmakers' upside to infrastructure providers, and Core Scientific is now on the favorable side of one of the largest such arrangements. AMD is aggressively seeding an ecosystem to challenge Nvidia (committing ~2 GW of accelerators to OpenAI, acquiring Silo AI), and its choice of Core Scientific as anchor real estate is a meaningful competitive signal.

The offsetting concern is customer concentration in a small number of credit-sensitive neocloud tenants. Core Scientific still hosts CoreWeave GPUs under a separate multi-hundred-MW hosting agreement despite the failed merger, and AMD's credit backstop is an implicit acknowledgment that a slice of demand comes from sub-investment-grade counterparties. Concentration risk is embedded inside a headline growth number.

We score Competitive Position a 7.0: improving trajectory, genuine power/land scarcity moat, and AMD validation, tempered by tenant concentration and credit-quality exposure.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where conviction breaks down, and it is the primary reason for the HOLD rather than a BUY.

**Restatement and accounting quality (highest priority).** The March 2, 2026 8-K carried Item 4.02 — non-reliance on previously issued financial statements — meaning management/auditors concluded prior financials could no longer be relied upon. The exact scope is unconfirmed but most plausibly relates to the complex warrant/CVR fair-value accounting given the magnitude of those swings. Compounding this is an auditor transition (both KPMG and Marcum consents appear in the FY2025 10-K, indicating a change year). A restatement plus an auditor change in the same ~12-month window, at a company whose entire reported financials hinge on judgment-heavy fair-value accounting following a recent bankruptcy emergence, is a genuine yellow flag. Whether a material weakness in internal controls was disclosed is a critical open diligence item.

**Leverage and funding dependence.** Debt quadrupled in one half-year, and the buildout requires ongoing capital-markets access. Any tightening in financing conditions or a shift in AI-capex sentiment directly threatens completion of builds. This ties the equity to the broader AI-financing channel, where GPU-collateralized and data-center debt supply is expanding rapidly and demand-side questions are unresolved.

**Execution and revenue-timing gap.** Anchor AMD revenue does not start until 2027, and the 2.5 GW headline depends on AMD electing uncommitted capacity. The company spends ~$1B+/quarter through the gap.

**Legacy mining drag and Bitcoin exposure.** Self-mining runs at negative gross margin, and digital assets on the balance sheet add earnings volatility unrelated to the colocation thesis.

We score Risk Factors a 3.5: the restatement, leverage, and 2027 timing gap are specific, material, and not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 109.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 65.2% — rich by 44 points, in the 88th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (109.3% near-term vs 83.0% long-term), which typically signals an imminent catalyst or event. Put/call skew is elevated at 8.2% in the front week (though it flips negative in the 1m and 3m tenors), and put/call open interest has jumped from 0.08 to 1.11 since early June alongside the price decline. Short interest is 22.6% of float with 5.3 days to cover — high enough to create squeeze risk on any positive surprise, but also reflecting substantial informed bearish positioning. The heavy short-dated call volume ($20 and $21 strikes expiring within days, 64x and 8x volume/OI) suggests speculative positioning into the earnings window rather than a directional conviction signal. The net read: the market expects a large move and is leaning defensive, consistent with our two-sided fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($40–55).** The colocation pivot continues compounding, AMD calls incremental capacity toward the 2.5 GW headline, the restatement resolves as a benign warrant-accounting correction with no material weakness, financing conditions stay open through the build phase, and 2027 anchor revenue begins on schedule. On successful conversion of contracted capacity and the sector's power-scarcity re-rating, the analyst high target of $55 and mean of ~$33 become reachable as the market values EBITDA power rather than distorted GAAP.

**Bear Case ($10–14).** AI-capex sentiment softens or credit conditions tighten, closing the financing window on which the buildout depends; the restatement proves broader than warrant accounting and surfaces a material weakness; neocloud tenant credit deteriorates (the AMD backstop being the tell); and the 2027 revenue ramp slips. On a leveraged, capex-front-loaded, pre-anchor-revenue business, any of these compresses the equity sharply. High short interest amplifies downside on a confidence break.

The two cases are wide and roughly balanced in probability given the unresolved accounting item and the 2027 timing gap. That symmetry, not a lack of upside, drives the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 earnings report (colocation ramp, EBITDA trajectory, any restatement/material-weakness disclosure), any 8-K/10-Q clarifying the scope of the March 2026 non-reliance, and financing-agreement disclosures on the debt stack (covenants, maturity schedule).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Resolution of the March 2026 restatement with confirmed limited scope (warrant/CVR accounting only) and no material weakness in internal controls, or a clean auditor sign-off on restated financials.
- Two consecutive quarters of sequential colocation revenue and adjusted-EBITDA growth with positive or self-funding operating cash flow trending toward FCF neutrality, plus visible progress on 2027 AMD revenue commencement (energized MW milestones).
- Estimate revisions turning cleanly positive on a normalized (ex-warrant) basis, or the stock derating toward the low-to-mid teens while the contracted backlog holds, improving risk/reward at a lower entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Restatement scope broadening beyond warrant accounting, disclosure of a material weakness, or a further delay in restated financials.
- Deterioration in financing access (failed/repriced debt raise, covenant pressure) or a material AI-capex-sentiment reversal that threatens the buildout.
- Neocloud tenant credit stress surfacing (AMD-backstopped or CoreWeave-related), or a 2027 AMD revenue-commencement slip.

## Conclusion

Core Scientific is a legitimate operating turnaround wrapped in a difficult balance sheet and accounting profile. The colocation pivot is working, and the power-secured land moat, confirmed by the AMD partnership and the shareholder rejection of the CoreWeave bid, is real and scarce. Those are the ingredients of a compelling AI-infrastructure long.

They are offset by a debt stack built in one half-year, deep negative free cash flow dependent on continued financing access, anchor revenue that does not begin until 2027, and an unresolved March 2026 non-reliance restatement layered on an auditor transition, at a company whose reported financials are inseparable from complex fair-value accounting. The drawdown from the June high, short interest, backwardated IV, and degrading analyst-target sentiment all point to a market pricing these overhangs rather than simply being volatile.

We rate CORZ **HOLD (uncertain)**. The operating inflection is too real to short into, and the accounting and funding risks are too material and too unresolved to accumulate on. The path to a BUY is specific and monitorable: restatement clarity with no material weakness, sustained self-funding EBITDA growth, and visible 2027 revenue progress. Until those clear, the risk/reward is balanced and the position is watch-and-verify.
