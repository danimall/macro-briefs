## Executive Summary

Hims & Hers is a direct-to-consumer telehealth platform whose 2024–2025 hypergrowth was built on compounded GLP-1 weight-loss medications, a revenue stream that was legally permissible only during an FDA-declared semaglutide shortage. With the shortage resolved and federal regulators reining in compounding, the company has been forced into a margin-dilutive pivot toward branded and generic GLP-1 supply. Q1 2026 showed the cost of that pivot directly: revenue grew only 4% YoY to $608.1M (a miss versus ~$617M consensus), US revenue declined 8% to $529.9M, gross margin fell from 73% to 65%, and the company swung to a $92.1M net loss from a $49.5M profit a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $44.3M (7% margin) from $91.1M (16%).

The central question is whether this transition rebuilds a more durable, branded-and-generic business or permanently impairs the economics that made HIMS attractive. Management's "raise revenue, cut EBITDA" guidance is the tell: FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $2.8–3.0B while Adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut to $275–350M, with the CFO conceding profits return only in 2027. This is a deliberate trade of margin for volume and regulatory legitimacy. The bear case is that the company is buying revenue at the expense of its best economics; the bull case is that branded supply (now enabled by the March 2026 Novo Nordisk settlement) plus ex-US generic optionality (Canada generic semaglutide via Apotex, May 2026) and the pending Eucalyptus acquisition rebuild a defensible multi-specialty platform.

We move our recommendation to HOLD (from a prior AVOID). The fundamental deterioration is real and not merely valuation compression, which keeps the stock well short of a BUY. But the bear thesis is largely already in a price that has fallen 62% from its $70.43 high, the two binary overhangs that crushed the stock have passed their acute phase, there is no dated catalyst to drive shares materially lower, and 32.6% short interest with 2.8 days to cover plus bullish near-term call positioning make the remaining downside difficult to realize. That combination of genuine impairment, no near-term downside trigger, resolving overhangs, and a measurable-but-unproven 2027 inflection describes an uncertain HOLD rather than an actionable short or an avoid.

## Company Overview

HIMS operates a subscription-based telehealth model: customers obtain online medical consultations and receive prescription and non-prescription products shipped on a recurring auto-billing basis. The platform spans sexual health (the original ED/hair-loss franchise), dermatology, mental health, and weight loss (GLP-1s), with weight loss having become the dominant growth driver in 2024–2025.

Structurally, the business carries ~2.6 million subscribers (Q1 2026, +9% YoY), a predominantly recurring revenue base, and a vertically integrated "closed-loop" infrastructure of owned fulfillment, affiliated pharmacies, and proprietary technology and diagnostics. Capex spiked to roughly $226M in 2025 from near-zero historically, and PP&E plus internal-use software now sit at ~$334M on the balance sheet. International expansion has been rapid: "Rest of World" revenue rose from $7.3M to $78.2M YoY in Q1 2026 (+969%), and the company launched generic semaglutide in Canada in May 2026 after Novo's Canadian patent expiry.

The competitive differentiation is brand, scale, and vertical integration in an inherently low-barrier DTC category. Marketing spend of $222M (~37% of Q1 revenue) drives subscriber acquisition; the moat is the combination of that marketing flywheel with owned pharmacy and fulfillment, plus a multi-specialty platform that can cross-sell beyond weight loss.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (annual).** $272M (2021) → $527M (2022) → $872M (2023) → $1.48B (2024) → $2.35B (2025, +59%). The 2024–2025 acceleration was overwhelmingly compounded GLP-1.

**Q1 2026.**
- Revenue $608.1M, +4% YoY, a sharp deceleration from ~60%+ and a miss versus consensus.
- US revenue declined 8% YoY to $529.9M; consolidated growth was carried entirely by international off a small base.
- Gross margin fell to 65% from 73% a year earlier (adjusted GM ~70%), reflecting the shift from high-margin compounded product to lower-margin branded GLP-1.
- Net loss of $92.1M versus $49.5M net income a year prior, a ~$142M swing. GAAP EPS −$0.40 against a consensus profit.
- Adjusted EBITDA $44.3M (7% margin) from $91.1M (16%).
- Monthly revenue per average subscriber fell 6% to ~$80.

**Quality-of-earnings flags.** The loss included ~$33.5M of restructuring/write-downs (compounded inventory), $15M legal settlement costs, $13.4M acquisition costs, and ~$27M of non-cash fair-value charges on equity securities and earn-out liabilities. Management revised its Adjusted EBITDA definition in Q1 2026 to add back the pivot-related restructuring — a change that warrants skepticism given that executive compensation is tied to Adjusted EBITDA. Operating cash flow was positive at $89.4M and FCF $53.0M, but the operating figure was flattered by a $167.6M increase in accounts payable against a $116.2M jump in receivables. Those working-capital swings reflect a business mid-reconfiguration (inventory and supplier terms shifting with the GLP-1 transition) and bear watching for sustainability; underlying cash conversion is weaker than the headline.

**Balance sheet.** Cash plus short-term AFS investments of ~$751M ($222M cash + $529M investments). Against this sits $974M of convertible senior notes, the source of the elevated reported leverage (~3x D/E), but the cash coupon is minimal — cash interest paid was just $1.05M in the quarter. Net cash is roughly neutral to modestly negative depending on conversion treatment, so the balance sheet is not stressed and there is no near-term refinancing cliff. Total equity fell to $446M from $541M at year-end 2025 on the Q1 loss; accumulated deficit grew to $206M; goodwill plus intangibles climbed to ~$604M on an active acquisition spree ($137.9M spent in Q1 alone).

**Guidance (excludes pending Eucalyptus).** FY2026 revenue raised to $2.8–3.0B (+19–28%), Adjusted EBITDA cut to $275–350M (10–12% margin), with profits guided to return in 2027. Long-term targets of $6.5B revenue / $1.3B Adjusted EBITDA by 2030 are aggressive and entirely dependent on the GLP-1 transition succeeding.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided, which is why a single headline number misleads in either direction. Consolidated +4% growth masks an 8% decline in the US core, the engine that built the company, offset by international growth off a tiny base. That is not a stable mix.

Against that, three forward signals are real. First, raised revenue guidance indicates management expects branded/generic volume to more than replace the compounded revenue lost to the regulatory crackdown. Second, the ex-US generic strategy is a genuine new vector: as Novo's patents lapse outside the US, HIMS can sell generic semaglutide (Canada via Apotex) at higher margins than branded resale, partially re-mixing the gross-margin profile upward over time. Third, the pending Eucalyptus acquisition adds international telehealth scale (excluded from current guidance), and the multi-specialty platform retains cross-sell potential beyond weight loss.

The hard constraint is estimate momentum. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from +$0.56 (90 days ago) to −$0.08, a −114% revision, and the current quarter from +$0.11 to −$0.04. The next-year estimate has firmed modestly (+5.1% over 30 days to ~$0.67), but that rests on only two analysts and assumes the 2027 profitability recovery management has promised but not demonstrated. The strong estimate-revision BUY signal — rising estimates plus accelerating revenue — is absent here; near-term revisions are sharply negative even as revenue guidance rose, precisely because the volume is being bought at the expense of margin. Growth durability is therefore unproven, not absent.

## Valuation Assessment

HIMS is loss-making this year on a GAAP basis (trailing/forward P/E negative), so trailing multiples are uninformative. On forward metrics, the stock trades at roughly 2.0x EV/sales against guided revenue growth — not expensive for a grower if margins normalize, and not cheap if the 65% gross margin proves structural. On the fragile ~$0.67 FY2027 EPS estimate, the stock sits near 40x, which is rich for a recovery that has not yet begun.

Peer context is heterogeneous and only partly applicable. Health Care Services comparables (CHE at 23x, SHC 40x, VCYT 44x, CON 20x; peer median P/E ~40x) are mostly profitable businesses, so a direct multiple comparison overstates how clean the read-across is. The cleaner observation is that HIMS trades essentially at the analyst mean target ($26.60) with a range of $21 (low) to $35 (high) and a $25 median — the sell-side itself is split and prices the stock as a coin flip on the transition.

The valuation neither offers clear asymmetric upside nor screams overvaluation. It embeds the binary directly: if margins stabilize and the US core returns to growth, the 2.0x EV/sales is too low for the growth rate; if the 65% gross margin is permanent and US revenue keeps shrinking, the forward earnings power that justifies any premium evaporates. We do not see a specific, identifiable mispricing in either direction, which is the basis for a middling valuation score rather than a high or low one. The bear case is substantially in the price already; a 62% decline from the high is not a stock that has yet to reckon with the regulatory and margin problems.

## Competitive Landscape

The DTC GLP-1 telehealth space is in regulatory transition. The FDA's resolution of the semaglutide shortage ended the compounding gray market that fueled the entire sub-sector's 2024–2025 boom; the legal basis for the compounded revenue HIMS relied on is eroding. HIMS now competes more directly within branded-drug economics controlled by Eli Lilly (tirzepatide/Zepbound) and Novo Nordisk (semaglutide/Wegovy) — manufacturers that hold pricing power and have demonstrated willingness to terminate or bypass telehealth partners. Novo terminated its 2025 partnership with HIMS over "deceptive marketing" allegations and filed a patent suit before the March 2026 settlement; that history defines the dependency.

HIMS's differentiation versus pure resellers is real but compressing: 2.6M subscribers, brand, owned fulfillment, and a multi-specialty platform are advantages, but the US core is shrinking and gross margin is contracting 800bps, both signs of a position under pressure rather than strengthening. The international/generic strategy is the one genuinely improving competitive vector — selling generic semaglutide as ex-US patents lapse gives HIMS a margin and timing edge that pure-branded resellers lack. On balance the trajectory is deteriorating in the core market and improving at the margins internationally, which is why competitive position scores below the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**1. GLP-1 regulatory and business-model impairment (dominant risk).** The compounded semaglutide stream that drove 2024–2025 is structurally impaired. This single dynamic explains the US revenue decline, the margin collapse, and the inventory write-downs. Much of this is now in the price, but the durability of the replacement (branded/generic) economics is unproven.

**2. Novo Nordisk dependency.** HIMS's branded GLP-1 economics depend on terms with a powerful supplier that has already walked away once. The March 2026 settlement reopened the relationship, but the dependency cuts both ways and is not fully resolvable.

**3. Margin structure.** Branded GLP-1 gross margins are far below compounded. The 73%→65% drop may be structural unless international generics and the legacy specialty franchises re-mix upward.

**4. Earnings quality and governance.** The Q1 Adjusted EBITDA redefinition (adding back restructuring) occurred in the same quarter the business deteriorated and is tied to compensation. The shift from quarterly to annual shareholder letters reduces disclosure granularity. Working-capital-flattered operating cash flow needs monitoring.

**5. Subscriber economics.** Subscribers grew only 9% while revenue per subscriber fell 6%. If GLP-1 was the acquisition hook, churn risk rises as the offering becomes more expensive and the value proposition narrows.

**6. Integration risk.** Aggressive M&A plus the pending Eucalyptus deal introduces integration and goodwill-impairment risk, particularly given the earn-out fair-value volatility already hitting the P&L.

The front end of the options market is positioned bullishly into a name where 32.6% of the float is short, which is the setup for squeeze risk rather than an easy short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$35, +30%)
The Novo settlement is the "turning point" JPMorgan flagged: branded supply legitimizes the platform, international generic semaglutide (Canada and beyond) re-mixes margins upward, Eucalyptus adds scale, and FY2026 revenue proves volume more than replaces the lost compounded stream. Profits return in 2027 as the transition normalizes, the 65% gross margin troughs and recovers toward 70%+ on generic mix, and the US core stabilizes. At 2.0x EV/sales on a re-accelerating, profitable platform, the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. The director's open-market buy at ~$24 — his first since 2021, made after the print — signals insider conviction at current levels.

### Bear Case (~$18–21, −22% to −33%)
The 65% gross margin is permanent, the US core keeps shrinking, branded GLP-1 is a low-margin commodity HIMS resells at the pleasure of Lilly and Novo, and the "raise revenue, cut EBITDA" guidance is a confession that the company is buying low-quality revenue to mask the loss of its best economics. Subscriber churn accelerates as the value proposition narrows, the 2027 profitability recovery slips, the redefined Adjusted EBITDA flatters a deteriorating reality, and integration of an M&A spree produces goodwill impairments. The stock drifts toward the analyst low and below as the loss-making year plays out.

The bear case targets roughly −22% to −33% downside, which is symmetric with the +30% bull upside, and with no dated catalyst to force the bearish outcome, against 32.6% short interest and bullish front-end positioning that make squeezes easy. That symmetry is why the position is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the first natural checkpoint, with the Eucalyptus close (mid-2026) and the FY2026 print as subsequent markers. The 2027 profitability claim is the ultimate test.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands sequentially (≥66% for two consecutive quarters) and US revenue returns to positive YoY growth, confirming the branded/generic transition is rebuilding rather than impairing the business.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive and the FY2027 estimate firms above ~$0.75 with broader analyst coverage, signaling the inflection is being confirmed rather than promised.
- Adjusted EBITDA delivers within the $275–350M guide without further definitional changes, restoring confidence in the non-GAAP framing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin compresses further (below ~62%) or US revenue decline accelerates beyond −8%, indicating structural rather than transitional impairment.
- FY2026 revenue guidance is cut, subscriber count declines sequentially, or a goodwill/intangible impairment is taken — any of which would falsify the volume-replaces-margin thesis.
- A renewed regulatory action or a deterioration in the Novo relationship re-introduces a binary downside catalyst that is not currently priced.

## Conclusion

HIMS is a high-variance turnaround in the middle of a forced, margin-dilutive transition. The fundamental deterioration is genuine: an 8% US decline, an 800bp gross-margin compression, a $142M swing to a net loss, and −114% current-year estimate revisions are operating reality, not artifacts of valuation. That keeps the stock far from a BUY: the positive estimate-revision signal is absent, near-term earnings momentum is negative, and the 2027 recovery is promised rather than demonstrated.

It is equally not an AVOID at this price. The stock has already absorbed a 62% decline from its high while recovering off its February trough (~$13.74 52-week low); the two binary overhangs that crushed it — the FDA compounding crackdown and the Novo litigation — have moved past their acute phase with the March 2026 settlement; there is no dated catalyst to drive shares materially lower; and 32.6% short interest with 2.8 days to cover, demonstrated squeeze behavior, cheap implied volatility, and bullish front-end call positioning make the identified downside (−22% to −33%, symmetric with the bull case) difficult to monetize and risky to short. A director's first open-market purchase since 2021, made after the miss at ~$24, is the strongest single insider signal in the dataset and points the other way. Issuing an AVOID here would re-state a view already in the price on a name where the mechanics favor the bulls in the near term.

The honest classification is an uncertain HOLD. The setup is binary, the evidence is genuinely two-sided, and the next two to three quarters of margin and US-revenue data will determine whether this is a platform rebuilding on more defensible economics or a business that traded away its best margins for legitimacy. We will reassess at Q2 earnings against the specific triggers above.

**Recommendation: HOLD.**
