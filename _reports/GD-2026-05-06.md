## Executive Summary

General Dynamics is a $93.6B diversified aerospace and defense company with franchise positions in nuclear submarines (Electric Boat), combat vehicles (Abrams, Stryker), business aviation (Gulfstream), and government IT services (GDIT). The company's total backlog reached $118B in FY2025, up 30% year-over-year, with a defense book-to-bill ratio of 1.6x. As of Q1 2026, backlog has grown further to $130.8B with total estimated contract value of $188.4B.

The stock trades at approximately 21x forward earnings (based on consensus FY2026E of ~$16.63), representing a discount to the broader aerospace & defense peer group, though the magnitude varies depending on the comparable set. Among the most relevant defense primes (LMT, NOC, LHX), GD trades modestly below the group median. This discount persists despite: (a) double-digit revenue growth (10.1% in FY2025), (b) improving ROIC (14.2%, up from 12.3% in 2023), (c) the Q1 2026 EPS beat ($4.10 vs. approximately $3.70 consensus, a beat of roughly 11%), and (d) multi-decade visibility on the Columbia-class submarine program ($125B+ program of record). The primary near-term risk is geopolitical sentiment around potential U.S.-Iran diplomatic developments, which temporarily depresses defense sector sentiment, but GD's demand drivers — submarine construction, European rearmament, and Gulfstream demand — are structurally insulated from any single conflict resolution.

The declining score trajectory (7.3 → 7.1 → 7.0) reflects deteriorating risk perception tied to government efficiency initiatives and tariff headwinds, not fundamental deterioration. Revenue growth remains above 10%, estimates are rising, the backlog provides multi-year visibility, and the valuation discount to peers has widened rather than narrowed.

## Company Overview

General Dynamics operates four segments: **Aerospace** (25% of revenue; Gulfstream jets, Jet Aviation services), **Marine Systems** (32%; Columbia-class and Virginia-class submarines, surface combatants), **Combat Systems** (17%; Abrams, Stryker, international vehicles, munitions), and **Technologies** (26%; GDIT IT services, Mission Systems C5ISR). The company derives 68% of revenue from the U.S. government, 15% from U.S. commercial customers, and 17% from international sources.

The competitive structure is unusual: GD holds effective monopoly or duopoly positions in several of its largest programs. Electric Boat is one of only two submarine yards in the United States. GD is sole-source on Abrams tanks and Stryker vehicles. These positions create multi-decade revenue certainty that is rare in any industry.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $39.4B (2022) → $42.3B (2023) → $47.7B (2024) → $52.6B (2025). The 10.1% growth in FY2025 was driven by Aerospace (+16.5%) and Marine Systems (+16.6%). Management guided FY2026 to approximately $54.5-55B, implying 3-4% growth — a deceleration from recent rates but still solid given the backlog provides visibility well beyond a single year.

**Margins:** Consolidated operating margin of 10.2% with significant segment variation (Combat Systems 14.4%, Aerospace 13.3%, Technologies 9.5%, Marine Systems 7.0%). Marine margins expanded 50bps in FY2025 as supply chain pressures eased, with guidance for further improvement to 7.3% in FY2026. Aerospace margins face a 30bps tariff headwind that management expects to intensify.

**Cash generation:** FCF of $3.96B in FY2025, representing 94% conversion of net income. Operating cash flow of $5.1B comfortably services the $8.0B total debt load, $1.6B annual dividend, and $1.16B capex. The debt/equity ratio is approximately 0.31x (total debt of ~$8.0B against shareholders' equity of ~$25.6B), reflecting a conservatively leveraged balance sheet. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 0.9x.

**Capital allocation:** The 28th consecutive annual dividend increase demonstrates commitment to shareholder returns. Share repurchases were reduced to $637M in FY2025 (from $1.5B in FY2024), primarily to cover dilution from stock vesting/exercises. This coincided with a 30% increase in capex to $1.16B, suggesting management is prioritizing shipyard expansion investment during a period of unprecedented backlog growth.

## Growth Analysis

The central growth thesis rests on the FY2025 backlog of $118B — equivalent to 2.2 years of current revenue — which grew further to $130.8B as of Q1 2026. Total estimated contract value reached $188.4B as of Q1 2026. The 1.6x defense book-to-bill in FY2025 indicates the backlog is growing faster than it's being consumed.

**Submarine programs:** The Columbia-class ($125B+ program of record) is the Navy's highest priority acquisition. First delivery tracks to 2028. Combined Virginia/Columbia awards of $20.1B in FY2025 alone. The Navy is investing approximately $900M in automated manufacturing capabilities to support the submarine industrial base, signaling multi-decade commitment to expanding production capacity.

**Combat Systems:** Backlog increased 60% YoY to $27.2B, driven by European rearmament demand. International wheeled/tracked vehicle awards totaled $9.2B in FY2025. NATO spending commitments and European defense autonomy initiatives are structural demand drivers independent of any single conflict.

**Aerospace:** Gulfstream delivered 158 aircraft in FY2025 (+16.2% YoY) with 1.2x book-to-bill despite the growth. The completion of the new aircraft family (G300 through G800) positions GD as the technology leader in large-cabin business jets. G700 deliveries are ramping, G800 entered service, and G300/G400 are in development.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen from $16.28 to $16.63 over 90 days (+2.2%). Management raised FY2026 EPS guidance to $16.45–$16.55 following the Q1 2026 beat, up from prior guidance of $16.10–$16.20. Sell-side consensus of approximately $16.63 sits modestly above the upper end of management guidance. While not at the +30% threshold that constitutes a strong BUY signal, the direction is positive and accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

GD trades at approximately 21x forward FY2026 consensus EPS of ~$16.63, and approximately 19.3x FY2027 consensus of $18.13. Among the most comparable defense primes, forward P/E multiples range from approximately 20x (LMT) to 27-30x (LHX), with NOC in the mid-20s. GD trades near the lower end of this range. Higher-growth or more commercially-exposed aerospace names (such as HWM) trade at significantly higher multiples but are less directly comparable.

The GD discount relative to the defense group requires explanation: historically, GD has traded at a premium to NOC and at parity with LMT given superior margin trajectory and Gulfstream's commercial exposure. The current ~21x vs. LMT's approximately 20x represents rough parity, while the discount to NOC and LHX is more notable.

**Why the market may be wrong:** The ~21x multiple appears to embed concern about (a) GDIT exposure to government efficiency cuts, and (b) near-term growth deceleration from 10% to 3-4% as guided. However, the 3-4% guidance is conservative given the 30% backlog increase, and GDIT represents only 26% of revenue (and a shrinking share as Marine/Combat grow faster). The market is pricing the weakest segment's risk across the entire enterprise.

**Valuation math:** At 23x FY2026E EPS of $16.63 = $382 (10% upside). At 22x FY2027E of $18.13 = $399 (14% upside). Analyst consensus median target of $390 implies similar logic. The high target of $444 requires ~24.5x FY2027E — achievable if the peer discount narrows.

**FCF yield:** $3.96B FCF / $93.6B market cap = 4.2%. Combined with the 1.8% dividend yield and modest buyback activity, total shareholder yield is approximately 2.5-3.0%.

## Competitive Landscape

GD's competitive position is among the strongest in any industrial company:

1. **Submarine monopoly/duopoly:** Only GD and HII can build nuclear submarines. Columbia-class is GD-led. The Navy has no alternative supplier and is investing in GD's industrial base to increase capacity.

2. **Sole-source combat vehicles:** Abrams and Stryker have no domestic competitors. International expansion (60% backlog growth) demonstrates the platforms' appeal beyond captured U.S. demand.

3. **Gulfstream brand franchise:** The premium large-cabin business jet brand globally with 3,000+ aircraft in service. The all-new family (G300-G800) provides a full decade of production lifecycle ahead.

4. **GDIT scale:** While competitively contested, GDIT's existing contract base and clearance-holding workforce create switching costs in classified programs.

The primary competitive risk is in Technologies, where government IT spending is being rationalized and commercial technology companies are increasingly competing for government contracts.

## Risk Assessment

**Government budget concentration (68% of revenue):** The most material structural risk. A continuing resolution, government shutdown, or material defense budget cut would impact near-term revenue recognition. However, the Columbia-class program has the highest possible priority rating and is effectively untouchable. Combat Systems benefits from international diversification (60% backlog growth from non-U.S. sources).

**Technologies segment margin pressure:** Guided down 30bps to 9.2% for FY2026. Government efficiency initiatives have resulted in contract modifications and staff reductions. This is the segment most vulnerable to political risk, though management characterizes the impact as "limited" going forward.

**Tariff escalation:** Approximately $39M impact in FY2025 (30bps on Aerospace margin) with "higher impact expected in 2026." At 30bps of Aerospace margin, this is manageable at current levels but could compound if trade policy deteriorates further. The Israel-based mid-cabin supplier situation adds uncertainty.

**Fixed-price contract execution:** 51% of government revenue is fixed-price. Marine Systems experienced supply chain cost overruns in FY2024 that compressed margins. While margins recovered in FY2025, the long-duration nature of submarine programs creates persistent execution risk.

**Geopolitical sentiment risk:** Potential U.S.-Iran diplomatic developments could temporarily suppress defense sector sentiment. However, GD's demand drivers are predominantly structural (submarine modernization, European rearmament, Gulfstream business cycle) rather than conflict-driven. A diplomatic resolution does not reduce the Columbia-class program of record or European defense spending commitments. As of early May 2026, reports indicate no firm deadline has been set for any agreement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400-444, 15-27% upside):** Defense spending increases in FY27 budget, European orders accelerate, submarine margins expand toward 8%+ as supply chain normalizes, Gulfstream deliveries exceed guidance, GDIT stabilizes. Multiple re-rates toward higher-multiple defense peers (24-25x forward). Timeline: 12-18 months.

**Bear Case ($290-313, 10-17% downside):** Diplomatic breakthrough confirmed, defense sentiment collapses, government efficiency cuts deepen GDIT contracts materially, tariffs escalate to 50bps+ Aerospace headwind, Gulfstream demand slows on macro weakness. Multiple compresses to NOC levels (17-18x). Timeline: 6-12 months.

**Base Case ($385-395, 10-13% upside):** Backlog converts at guided rates, Marine margins expand to 7.3%, FY2026 EPS of $16.45-16.65, multiple holds at 23x. Dividend provides 1.8% yield during hold period. Total return of 12-15% over 12 months.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $53B (would indicate backlog conversion issues)
2. Marine Systems margin decline below 6.5% in any quarter (would signal supply chain regression)
3. Defense book-to-bill falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters (would indicate demand deceleration)

## Conclusion

General Dynamics at ~21x forward earnings represents a franchise-quality defense company trading at a discount to its peer group. The $130.8B backlog (as of Q1 2026), 1.6x defense book-to-bill, submarine monopoly position, and Q1 2026 EPS beat support a BUY recommendation with a 12-month base case target of $390 (12% upside plus 1.8% dividend). Geopolitical sentiment creates near-term headwind but does not structurally impair GD's demand drivers. The declining score trajectory (7.3 → 7.0) reflects risk perception around GDIT and tariffs — real but quantifiable concerns that do not warrant the peer discount. The specific risk preventing a higher-conviction BUY is the ~21x multiple's limited room for compression if defense sentiment deteriorates further and the 3-4% revenue growth guidance represents a legitimate deceleration from 10%+ rates.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The peer valuation discount is narrower than originally presented when using the most comparable defense primes (LMT ~20x, NOC ~27x, LHX ~28-30x); the valuation upside case relies more on multiple expansion toward mid-peer-group levels rather than correction of a "substantial" discount.
