**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pentair is a pure-play water solutions manufacturer (Pool, Flow, Water Solutions) that has executed one of the cleaner industrial margin-expansion programs of the cycle. Operating margin rose from 14.4% in 2022 to 20.5% in 2025, gross margin reached 40.5%, and net income grew from $481M to $654M while revenue stayed essentially flat at ~$4.1B. The mechanism is deliberate: pricing (+4.0% in 2025), sourcing and operations efficiency, and an "80/20" portfolio rationalization that sheds low-margin revenue. The trade-off is that volume has declined every year for three-plus years (-2.1% in 2025, -2.3% in 2024, -4.2% in Q1 2026), so the entire growth algorithm rests on price and cost-out rather than demand.

The market has reset the multiple accordingly. Shares are down roughly 30% from early-2026 levels and fell about 12% in the week of the Q1 2026 report despite a beat (adjusted EPS $1.22 vs $1.17 consensus) and a reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guide of $4.83–$4.93 (+23–25% YoY). Analysts cut targets into the print (TD Cowen to $75, BofA to $80, Barclays to $92). The selloff reflects concern over the durability of price-led growth and the tariff overhang, not the headline numbers. At ~14.5x the FY2026 adjusted midpoint of $4.88, PNR now trades at less than half the peer median P/E of 31.2x, with a 17.9% FCF margin, ~114% FCF conversion, and a de-levering balance sheet.

We rate PNR a HOLD with a constructive lean. The valuation reset has materially improved the risk/reward versus the $100+ levels of last summer, and management's aggressive buyback at the lows ($200M repurchased in Q1 2026 alone versus $225M for all of 2025) signals genuine conviction. Three things keep us from a BUY: near-term EPS estimates are drifting lower, single-customer concentration in the highest-margin segment is rising (15% → 18% of total sales), and the growth story requires a volume inflection that has not yet appeared. We want evidence that volumes are bottoming before underwriting the re-rate.

## Company Overview

Pentair is an Irish-incorporated, UK-tax-resident manufacturer with roughly 9,000 employees and ~$4.18B in FY2025 revenue, structured around three segments (FY2025 basis):

- **Pool (~38% of sales)** — residential and commercial pool equipment (pumps, filters, heaters, lights, automation, cleaners) under Pentair Pool, Kreepy Krauly, Pleatco, and Sta-Rite. FY2025 segment margin of 33.8% makes this the most profitable business and the primary earnings driver.
- **Flow (~37%)** — pumps and fluid-treatment systems for residential, commercial/infrastructure (fire suppression, flood control, wastewater), and industrial markets. FY2025 margin 23.3%.
- **Water Solutions (~25%)** — commercial and residential water treatment, filtration, softening, and Manitowoc/Everpure commercial ice machines. FY2025 margin 23.9%.

Effective January 1, 2026, Pentair moved its residential and irrigation flow business from Flow into Water Solutions. Under the new structure, the mix is Water Solutions ~38%, Pool ~37%, Flow ~25%. The competitive moat is brand plus distribution plus installed base, particularly in pool (where it competes with Hayward and Fluidra) and water treatment, rather than proprietary technology.

CEO John Stauch remains in place. The company completed an orderly CFO succession, with long-tenured Bob Fishman transitioning out and Nick Brazis stepping in as EVP & CFO, communicated on the Q4 2025 call and reflected in the Q1 2026 10-Q signature. The cluster of officer/director change 8-Ks across 2025–2026 reflects planned transitions rather than a destabilizing exodus.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature is high-quality earnings against a flat top line. Revenue progressed $4,122M (2022) → $4,105M (2023) → $4,083M (2024) → $4,176M (2025), a 2.3% gain in 2025 driven by +4.0% price and bolt-on M&A against -2.1% volume. The same pattern held in Q1 2026: revenue $1.037B (+2.6%), with price +5.3% and volume -4.2%.

Margins drive the story. Operating margin climbed 14.4% (2022) → 18.0% (2023) → 19.7% (2024) → 20.5% (2025); gross margin reached 40.5% versus 33.1% in 2022. Net income grew to $654M in 2025, with ROE of 16.9% and ROA of 9.5%. Cash generation is strong and clean: FY2025 operating cash flow of $815M and FCF of $746M, a 17.9% FCF margin and roughly 114% of net income, consistent with management's ~100% conversion target.

The balance sheet is solid and de-levering. Total debt fell to $1.65B at year-end 2025 from $2.3B in 2022, net interest expense dropped 22% in 2025, current ratio is 1.61, and D/E is 0.78. The Altman Z of 2.68 sits in the grey zone, but that reflects goodwill and intangibles at 67% of assets ($4.6B), not liquidity or earnings stress. Debt rose to $1.94B at Q1-end on seasonal revolver draws for early-buy programs, a normal Q1 working-capital build.

One quality-of-earnings flag deserves attention: adjusted EPS runs roughly 20–25% above GAAP. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.22 compares with GAAP continuing-ops EPS of $0.98, bridged by intangible amortization, restructuring, and transformation costs. FY2025 add-backs included ~$41M transformation, ~$31M restructuring, and ~$48M impairments (a $30.9M Water Solutions intangible write-off plus $17.1M other). Transformation costs have recurred every year since 2023 and are added back annually. The guided EPS growth is on the adjusted base, so the gap warrants ongoing scrutiny, though FCF conversion above 100% partially validates the earnings quality.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and opportunistic. A new $1.0B buyback was authorized in December 2025; the company repurchased $200M in Q1 2026 alone against ~$800M remaining, an aggressive pace that signals management views the stock as cheap. The dividend was raised 8% to $0.27/quarter (the company cites a 50-year dividend record), and share count is declining (165.5M → 161.6M). The current yield of ~1.5% is modest, in line with the peer median of 1.4%, so the income case is secondary to the total-return case.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is bifurcated. On the earnings line, the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (by 0.8% to 3.9%). On the demand line, the underlying volume trend has been negative for more than three years, backlog fell 7.1% in 2025 (Pool backlog -33%), and the housing- and pool-construction-sensitive end markets remain soft.

Estimate revisions corroborate the caution rather than the optimism. Current-quarter consensus has been trimmed -1.3% over both 30 and 90 days, and next-quarter estimates are down -2.2% to -2.3%. Full-year estimates are roughly flat (+0.2%). This revision velocity does not signal a genuine earnings inflection; it is a stable-to-softening near-term picture where the company is defending guidance through pricing and cost-out while volumes stay weak.

The durability question is whether price can continue to carry the algorithm. Pricing has been the dominant growth lever for three years, and at some point pricing normalizes. If that happens before volume recovers, the margin/EPS engine stalls. The bull's offset is the pool aftermarket and replacement cycle, which is structurally more resilient than new-build and represents a large attachment base. Over a full cycle, replacement demand in an aging installed base should provide a floor, and any housing/pool-construction recovery would convert the company's expanded margin structure into outsized EPS leverage. We do not yet see that turn in the data, which holds the Growth score at the lower end.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.80, PNR trades at ~14.5x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $4.88 and ~17.8x trailing earnings of $3.93. Against the same-sector peer median P/E of 31.2x (SWK 32.6x, IEX 31.2x, NDSN 30.7x, FTV 34.3x, SNA 19.2x), the stock sits at less than half the group multiple. Even adjusting for Pentair's negative-volume profile and price-led growth, this is a wide discount for a business generating 20.5% operating margins, 16.9% ROE, and a 17.9% FCF margin.

The valuation case is genuinely two-sided. The bear reads the discount as deserved: a company whose entire growth is price plus cost-out, with negative volumes, rising customer concentration, and a 20–25% gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings should not trade at peer multiples. The bull reads it as an over-correction: the stock fell ~12% on a quarter where it beat and reaffirmed +23–25% EPS growth, the de-rate took it to ~14.5x forward versus a 5-year average of ~17.5x on operating cash flow, and the company is buying back stock aggressively at these levels.

Our view is that fair value sits in the middle. We are not willing to underwrite a full re-rate toward peer multiples without a volume inflection, but the absolute valuation and FCF support limit downside. On a normalized basis, a 16–18x multiple on $4.88 of FY2026 adjusted EPS implies roughly $78–$88 of fair value, modestly above the current price. The analyst target range of $75 (low) to $125 (high), with a mean of ~$101 and median of ~$104.5, brackets a wide outcome distribution; the recent target cuts pulled the low end down to current levels, which indicates the marginal seller has largely repriced. We score Valuation moderately positive: there is a real discount, but it is partly justified by the volume problem, so this is not a deep-value asymmetric setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Pentair's moat is brand, distribution, and installed base rather than technology. In pool, it competes with Hayward and Fluidra in a market where the aftermarket and replacement business carries high margins and high attachment rates, which should prove resilient across a cycle even with weak new-build. In water treatment and flow, the company acknowledges competitors with "substantially greater resources" and low-cost regional players, which caps pricing power in those verticals.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The "80/20" strategy deliberately sheds low-margin revenue, which mechanically depresses reported volumes and sales while lifting margins, a defensible but optically unhelpful trade-off that the market is currently penalizing. Bolt-on M&A has been disciplined and conservatively financed: Hydra-Stop ($292M, Sept 2025, specialty insertion valves into Flow) and G&F Manufacturing ($116M, Dec 2024, pool heat pumps into Pool). These extend the niche infrastructure-repair and pool-equipment franchises without straining the balance sheet.

The most material competitive risk is the rising single-customer concentration in Pool: one customer (almost certainly a large pool distributor) reached ~18% of consolidated net sales in 2025, up from 15% in 2024, concentrated in the highest-margin segment. Destocking, order-pattern shifts, or any channel disruption at this customer would hit the earnings engine directly. We hold Competitive Position at neutral-to-slightly-positive: the franchise is strong, but share trajectory is being clouded by the deliberate volume shedding and the concentration is moving the wrong way.

## Risk Assessment

The risk factors were unchanged in the Q1 2026 10-Q versus the 10-K. The most company-specific:

1. **Customer concentration (rising):** One Pool customer at ~18% of total sales, concentrated in the highest-margin business. A genuine downside catalyst if it reverses.
2. **Volume stagnation / pricing exhaustion:** Three-plus years of negative volume; the EPS engine depends on continued price realization and cost-out.
3. **Tariff exposure with a live legal twist:** Pentair sources steel, stainless, resins, and motors/drives globally (30% of sales ex-US). The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs created two-sided uncertainty: a reimbursement process for previously-paid tariffs opened April 20, 2026 (a possible unquantified cash tailwind), offset by uncertainty over what replacement tariffs follow. USMCA is also up for review in 2026.
4. **Goodwill/intangibles at 67% of assets ($4.6B):** A demand downturn could trigger impairment; $48M was already taken in 2025.
5. **Pillar Two minimum tax:** Structurally pressures the historically low 14.1% effective tax rate that underpins the Irish/UK structure.
6. **Multi-year ERP implementation:** Internal-control and execution risk flagged.
7. **Legacy asbestos:** ~795 claims pending (mostly discontinued ops), with eroding insurance coverage. Manageable but persistent.

On balance, the idiosyncratic risk profile is mid-range. The volume problem and customer concentration are real and not fully priced; the tariff situation is two-sided and could surprise either way; the goodwill and tax risks are lower-probability tail concerns. This keeps the Risk score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is more sanguine than the price action. Near-term ATM IV is 34.6% against 27.5% one-year historical volatility, modestly rich (a 7.1pp spread), but at only the 8th percentile within the current chain, so implied vol is low relative to where it has recently traded. The put/call skew is -3.3% (call-heavy) and the term structure is flat (near 34.6% ≈ far 35.2%), indicating uncertainty is distributed evenly across tenors rather than concentrated in a near-term binary. The P/C open-interest ratio fell from 2.90 to 1.57 alongside the 11.4% price decline since early May, meaning hedging demand eased as the stock fell. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.3%. Short interest is uncrowded at 5.0% of float with 3.4 days to cover. Net read: the options market is not pricing acute downside, and the call-heavy skew is mildly consistent with the constructive lean.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The selloff over-corrected a beat-and-reaffirm quarter. At ~14.5x forward adjusted EPS against a peer median above 30x, PNR is structurally cheap for a 20.5%-operating-margin, ~18% FCF-margin franchise with a de-levering balance sheet. The pool aftermarket/replacement base provides demand resilience, and any housing or pool-construction recovery converts the expanded margin structure into outsized EPS leverage. Management is buying back stock aggressively at the lows ($200M in Q1 alone), the tariff-refund process opened in April 2026 is an unquantified cash optionality, and the company has beaten consensus four straight quarters. A re-rate toward 17–18x on $4.88 FY2026 EPS plus continued buyback shrinkage puts the stock in the low-to-mid $80s. Bull case ~$95–$105 if volumes inflect and the multiple normalizes toward the peer group.

### Bear Case
Three years of negative volume is the demand signal, and price-led growth is finite. As pricing normalizes and the easy cost-out is harvested, the EPS engine stalls against a backlog that is already shrinking (-7.1%, Pool -33%). The 15% → 18% customer concentration in the Pool segment is a fragility, and a destocking event there would hit margins hardest. The 20–25% adjusted-vs-GAAP gap, fed by recurring "transformation" add-backs, flatters the growth rate. Replacement tariffs post-SCOTUS could raise input costs without a matching refund. Falling near-term estimates (current quarter -1.3%, next quarter -2.2%) point the wrong way. Bear case ~$60–$65 (roughly -8% to -15%) on a multiple of ~13x on a flat-to-down adjusted EPS as the volume problem persists into a soft macro.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-oriented (constructive) lean over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Volume turns positive (or clearly bottoms toward flat) in any of the next two quarterly reports, confirming the demand inflection the EPS story needs.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current/next-quarter consensus stops falling and trends up), removing the negative near-term momentum.
- Backlog stabilizes or grows, particularly in Pool, reversing the -33% Pool decline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The ~18% Pool customer reduces orders or destocks materially, with a visible hit to Pool segment margin (33.8% trending toward the low-30s or below).
- Price realization decelerates below ~2% while volumes remain negative, signaling the pricing lever is exhausted before volumes recover.
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut below the $4.83 low end, or a goodwill impairment is taken against the $4.6B intangible base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint, specifically the volume bridge and any commentary on the ~18% customer and tariff refunds.

## Conclusion

Pentair is a high-quality water/pool franchise that has manufactured impressive EPS growth from a flat top line through pricing and self-help. The market has stopped paying a quality premium for that, de-rating the stock ~30% on the legitimate concern that price-led growth without volume is not durable, compounded by rising customer concentration, a widening adjusted-vs-GAAP gap, and tariff uncertainty. At ~14.5x forward adjusted EPS, less than half the peer median, with strong free cash flow, a de-levering balance sheet, and management buying back stock aggressively at the lows, the downside is meaningfully more contained than it was at $100+.

We are not yet prepared to call the bottom of the volume problem, and the negative near-term estimate revisions argue against chasing the re-rate today. The call is a HOLD with a constructive lean: own it if you already do, accumulate on further weakness toward the low-$60s where the FCF yield and buyback support become compelling, but wait for evidence that volumes are bottoming before underwriting a move back toward the peer multiple. The score has drifted lower over the past several months (6.7 → 6.4), and current data confirms the deceleration in growth and the analyst caution rather than reversing it; what has improved is the valuation, which now offsets enough of that to hold the overall assessment roughly stable.
