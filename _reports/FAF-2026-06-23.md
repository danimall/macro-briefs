**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First American Financial is one of two dominant U.S. title insurance underwriters, a consolidated duopoly with Fidelity National Financial that splits the bulk of a high-barrier, regulatorily-licensed market. The business is deeply cyclical, with revenue tied to real estate transaction volume and mortgage rates. After three years of declining net income (from $1,241M in the 2021 housing-boom peak to a $131M trough in 2024), earnings rebounded sharply to $622M in 2025, and Q1 2026 extended the recovery with revenue up 16.2% and net income attributable up 71% year-over-year.

The recovery is genuine but uneven. Growth is being driven by commercial direct premiums (+47.6% in Q1 2026) and refinance (+76.5%), the more rate-sensitive and, in the case of refinance, lower-margin channels. The higher-margin residential purchase market is still contracting (premiums −3.5%, orders per day −4.1%). This makes the earnings quality of the current upcycle more rate-dependent than the headline numbers suggest. A durable, high-margin recovery requires the purchase market to inflect, which has not yet happened. The mechanism matters: purchase volume depends on existing-home turnover, which is sensitive to the relationship between current mortgage rates and the lower rates on outstanding mortgages, so the purchase inflection is hostage to a meaningful decline in long rates.

At ~10x forward EPS of $6.87 against a 12.6x peer median, FAF trades at a discount that reflects cyclical fragility rather than structural impairment. The combination of a covered 3.2% dividend, a $300M buyback authorization that is barely 11% used, a fortress investment portfolio, and a four-quarter streak of double-digit earnings beats supports a BUY. The score has drifted lower across the last three reports (6.8 → 6.9 → 6.7), and the price is roughly flat to slightly higher over that window. We maintain BUY but acknowledge the bar for a fourth consecutive unchanged call is higher: the thesis now leans more heavily on the purchase-market inflection and capital-return optionality than on the already-priced refinance rebound.

## Company Overview

First American operates through three reportable segments:

- **Title Insurance & Services (~94% of revenue):** Issues title policies on residential and commercial property; provides escrow/closing, 1031 exchange facilitation, title-plant data, valuation, default services, warehouse lending (FirstFunding), mortgage subservicing, and banking/trust through First American Trust, FSB. Operates in 49 states plus DC and internationally.
- **Home Warranty (~6% of revenue):** Residential service contracts on HVAC and appliances in 36 states plus DC. Renewal-driven, high-margin (23.5% pretax in Q1 2026), and less volume-sensitive.
- **Corporate:** Houses the venture investment portfolio and financing facilities.

The competitive advantage is structural. Title insurance is an oligopoly with high barriers: proprietary title plants (FAF carries $687M in title-plant and index assets accumulated over decades and effectively irreproducible), state licensing, scale, and financial-strength ratings that lenders and GSEs require. FAF is the purer-play title bet relative to FNF, which carries an F&G annuity/life diversification engine. That makes FAF more directly leveraged to a housing and refinance recovery, and more exposed to a stall.

## Financial Analysis

**Net income to the Company by year:**
- 2021: $1,241M (boom peak)
- 2022: $263M
- 2023: $217M
- 2024: $131M (trough — purchase and refi both depressed)
- 2025: $622M (rebound; revenue ~$7.45B vs ~$6.13B in 2024)

**Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025:**
- Revenue: $1,838M vs $1,582M (+16.2%)
- Net income attributable: $125.1M ($1.21 diluted EPS) vs $74.2M ($0.71), +71%
- Title segment pretax margin: 9.6% vs 7.2%
- Mix: commercial direct premiums +47.6%, refinance +76.5%, residential purchase −3.5%; refinance orders/day +48.1%, purchase orders/day −4.1%; domestic average revenue per order $4,229 (+12.9%, helped by commercial mix)

The earnings-surprise record is consistent and large: Q1 2026 beat by 25.6%, Q4 2025 by 37.4%, Q3 2025 by 15.9%, Q2 2025 by 11.2% — four straight beats. This pattern raises confidence in forward estimates and suggests consensus is still catching up to the operating leverage in a recovering volume environment.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**
- Total equity ~$5.5B; goodwill $1.82B
- Investment portfolio: $9.0B in debt securities, 98% investment grade, 72% government-backed/AAA
- Debt-to-cap 32.2% (21.9% excluding warehouse secured financings) — conservative for the business model
- AOCI loss of $322.5M (up from $256.7M), reflecting $309M gross unrealized bond losses from higher rates. This is a non-credit, mark-to-market drag that pulls to par as bonds mature, so reported book value understates economic value on a hold-to-maturity basis.
- Loss provision rate steady at 3.0% of premiums (3.75% current-year ultimate, offset by 0.75% favorable prior-year reserve releases — reserves remain redundant)
- Capital return: $0.55/quarter dividend (~3.2% yield), $266.5M remaining on a $300M buyback; Q1 2026 returned ~$90M ($56M dividends + $34M buybacks)

FAF also administers more than $21B in off-balance-sheet escrow, trust, 1031, and subservicing deposits, much of it at its own FSB. This is both a hidden earnings asset (net investment income +12%, partly from these balances and warehouse lending) and a hidden risk: the deposits shrink when transaction volumes fall, and there is contingent liability for misapplied funds.

The financial-health profile scores well as a downside filter: high-quality investment portfolio, conservative leverage, redundant reserves, and well-covered capital return. The standard Altman Z-Score is uninformative here — the model is not calibrated for insurers whose balance sheets are dominated by investment portfolios and deposits.

## Growth Analysis

The growth trajectory is a cyclical recovery off a depressed base, not a secular growth story. The MBA forecast cited by management projects total residential originations up 43.5% year-over-year in Q1 2026 (purchase +22.1%, refinance +95.5%), a constructive backdrop if rates cooperate. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year consensus EPS moved to $6.87 from $6.48 ninety days ago (+6.0%), and next year to $7.26 from $7.02 (+3.4%). These are healthy but not the +30% velocity that signals a sharp inflection; the recovery is gradual and partly priced.

The growth quality question is the lopsidedness. Refinance and commercial are leading, while the higher-margin residential purchase channel is still contracting. Refinance is the most rate-sensitive and lowest-margin of the three; it can reverse quickly if long rates back up. The mechanism for a durable upcycle runs through purchase volume: lower long-term rates → lower mortgage rates → improved affordability and reduced rate-lock-in → higher existing-home turnover → higher-margin purchase title premiums. Until that chain engages, the recovery's earnings power is more fragile than the 71% EPS jump implies.

Home Warranty provides a stable, renewal-driven, high-margin (23.5% pretax) counterweight that is less volume-sensitive, and ancillary fee businesses (subservicing, warehouse lending) cushion the cyclicality. The growth prospects score of 6.5 reflects a real recovery with credible estimate momentum, tempered by the dependence on a purchase-market inflection that has not yet materialized.

## Valuation Assessment

FAF trades at ~10.1x forward EPS of $6.87 and ~9.6x next-year EPS of $7.26, against a Property & Casualty peer median of 12.6x. The peer set is heterogeneous — Mercury at 6.5x and Hanover at 9.9x sit below FAF, while Kinsale (13.7x), Selective (12.6x), and RLI (12.6x) sit above — but these are mostly specialty and standard P&C insurers, not title underwriters, so the comparison is imperfect. The cleaner comparison is to FAF's own normalized earnings power: at trough (2024), net income was $131M; in a normalized mid-cycle environment with a recovered purchase market, EPS power is plausibly above the current $6.87 forward figure given the operating leverage demonstrated by the four consecutive double-digit beats.

The discount to peers reflects cyclical fragility and the narrow, rate-dependent nature of the current recovery, not structural impairment. The lessons of this cycle caution against treating a low multiple as a buy signal in isolation — cheapness has been anti-predictive, and a 10x multiple needs an independent catalyst. Here the catalysts are concrete: continued positive estimate revisions, the four-quarter beat streak that consensus keeps underestimating, capital-return optionality from a barely-used $300M buyback, and the purchase-market inflection if rates ease. The AOCI hole ($322.5M) depresses reported book and modestly understates economic value, providing additional downside cushion on a P/B basis.

Analyst targets sit well above spot: low $80, mean $86.2, median $85, high $95, against a current price of ~$68.28. Sentiment has degraded slightly (one strong-buy moved to hold over three months, leaving 2 strong-buy / 3 buy / 0 hold currently). Given FAF is not at a premium multiple (10x forward, well below 30x), the gap to consensus targets is meaningful — roughly 26% to the mean — and on a recovering-earnings stock those targets are more likely to be revised up than to prove a ceiling. We score valuation 7.0: the asymmetry favors upside, anchored by a specific catalyst path rather than cheapness alone.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. title underwriting market is a rational duopoly. FAF and FNF dominate, with Old Republic and Stewart as smaller players. Pricing is consolidated and disciplined, and the proprietary title-plant moat ($687M in plant/index assets) is effectively irreproducible. FAF differentiates through aggressive digitization and AI in title production (driving higher software amortization from recently deployed digital settlement products) and through ancillary fee businesses that dampen volume sensitivity.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly widening. Title segment margins are recovering (9.6% from 7.2%), and commercial market share gains are evident in the +47.6% premium growth. But two factors restrain the competitive-position score. First, the company's own TSR (142) has lagged its peer group (211) over the measurement period, consistent with the cyclical trough. Second, and more important, the GSE title-waiver pilots and attorney-opinion-letter alternatives represent a structural long-term overhang for the entire industry. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have experimented with title insurance waivers and AOLs on certain refinances, which could disintermediate traditional title insurance over time. Adoption has so far been limited and largely confined to refinances, but any restructuring of the GSEs — now a live policy topic — is a genuine wildcard for the moat. The prior report trimmed competitive position from 7.5 to 7.0; we hold at 6.5, reflecting the disintermediation tail rather than any near-term share loss.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cyclical/interest-rate dependence (primary risk).** A substantial majority of revenue depends on real estate and mortgage activity. The current recovery is narrow — led by refi and commercial — while the larger purchase market is still contracting. A renewed rate spike or recession would directly hit revenue and could raise claims. The macro backdrop for the purchase-market inflection the bull case requires remains dependent on the trajectory of long-term mortgage rates through H2 2026; a sustained move higher in rates would pressure both purchase volume and the rate-sensitive refinance channel currently carrying the recovery.

2. **December 2023 cybersecurity incident.** Explicitly cited in the 10-K as a realized risk. Title companies hold massive escrow/trust balances ($10.5B escrow, $5.8B trust, $2.6B 1031, $2.5B subservicing at quarter-end), making them prime wire-fraud and ransomware targets. Management acknowledges unremediated vulnerabilities "will always exist." This is a tail litigation/regulatory risk that is partly priced but not quantifiable.

3. **GSE / title-waiver disruption.** The existential industry threat. Limited adoption to date, but a structural overhang on the entire group.

4. **Rate regulation and agent liability.** Title rates require state approval; FAF cannot reprice quickly in a rapidly declining market. Independent agents retained 79.3% of agency premiums, and regulators increasingly hold underwriters responsible for agent misconduct and defalcation.

5. **Investment/venture portfolio volatility.** The ~$258M venture book and equity securities can produce lumpy gains/losses; rising rates created the AOCI bond loss.

6. **Leadership transition.** A complete top-of-house turnover — former CFO Mark Seaton to CEO, Matthew Wajner to CFO — was executed quietly through Item 5.02 8-K filings. Internal, finance-led succession suggests continuity, but a new CEO and CFO simultaneously warrants monitoring of the first full strategic articulation.

Most of these risks are sector-standard and partly priced. The idiosyncratic, less-priced items are the cyber tail and the GSE-waiver overhang. The risk-factor score of 5.5 reflects the genuine rate-dependence of the current recovery plus these two unquantifiable tails, against a fortress balance sheet that caps downside.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive-to-neutral. Near-term ATM IV of 29.6% sits roughly in line with one-year historical volatility of 28.1% (spread +1.5%, fair), and IV percentile within the current chain is low at 9% — the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call skew is call-heavy at −13.8%, and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.64 (though the put/call volume ratio of 1.33 is slightly elevated). The term structure is in contango (29.6% near vs 33.9% long), a normal/complacent shape. ATM IV has eased from 29.7% to 27.8% over the last month as the price rose 2.7%. Net read: the options market is not flagging a near-term catalyst or stress, and the absence of expensive downside protection is consistent with the fundamental view that risk/reward favors the long side at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($85–95):** The residential purchase market inflects as long rates ease, joining the refinance and commercial recovery already underway and driving the higher-margin channel that a durable upcycle requires. Title segment margins continue expanding past 10%, the four-quarter beat streak persists, and FY26 EPS lands above the $6.87 consensus. The new management team leans into the barely-used $300M buyback at a sub-11x multiple. The stock re-rates toward the peer median on normalized earnings power, reaching the analyst target range of $80–95.

**Bear case (~$53–55):** A renewed rate spike or recession stalls the recovery. The narrow, refi-led rebound reverses as refinance volume — the most rate-sensitive channel — collapses, while the purchase market stays locked. Margins compress back toward trough levels, the AOCI loss widens further, and the stock retests its 52-week low near $53. A GSE-waiver acceleration or a cyber/defalcation event adds an idiosyncratic leg down.

The probability-weighted setup favors the upside: the balance sheet caps the bear case, the dividend is covered through the cycle, and the catalysts for the bull case (estimate revisions, buyback, purchase inflection) are concrete and partly underway.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $80.** This reflects ~11.6x the FY26 consensus EPS of $6.87 — a move from the current ~10.1x toward the lower end of the peer multiple range, justified by the demonstrated four-quarter beat record and continued margin recovery. This sits between the bear and bull cases. Upside to base case: ~17% from $68.28.

**Bull case target: $90.** ~12.4x next-year EPS of $7.26 if the purchase market inflects and management accelerates buybacks. Upside: ~32%.

**Bear case target: $54.** A retest of the 52-week low if the recovery stalls on a rate spike. Downside: ~21%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two to three earnings reports (the company reports quarterly; last earnings April 22, 2026) and the trajectory of mortgage rates through H2 2026.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Residential purchase premiums decline by more than 10% year-over-year in any quarter (signals the purchase inflection is failing rather than approaching)
- Title segment pretax margin compresses back below 7% (reverses the recovery)
- Long-term mortgage rates rise sustainably, collapsing the refinance channel that is currently carrying the recovery
- A dividend cut or suspension of the buyback authorization

## Conclusion

First American is a duopoly title insurer in the early innings of a cyclical earnings recovery, trading at ~10x forward EPS against a 12.6x peer median with rising estimates, a four-quarter double-digit beat streak, a covered 3.2% dividend, and a barely-touched $300M buyback. The recovery is real but currently narrow and rate-dependent, led by refinance and commercial while the higher-margin purchase market lags. The bull case requires that purchase inflection; the bear case is a rate spike that stalls it. With analyst targets ~26% above spot, a fortress balance sheet that caps downside, and benign options positioning, the risk/reward favors accumulation.

We maintain **BUY** with an overall score of 6.4, modestly below the prior 6.7. The reduction reflects the narrowing-but-improving earnings quality, the GSE-waiver and cyber tails, the quiet complete leadership turnover, and the rate-dependence of the purchase-market inflection — not any deterioration in the price or the fundamentals, which remain on a recovery track. This is the fourth consecutive BUY; the thesis now rests more squarely on the purchase inflection and capital-return optionality than on the already-priced refinance rebound, and we would reassess if the purchase channel fails to stabilize over the next two prints.
