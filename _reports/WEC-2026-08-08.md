## Executive Summary

WEC Energy Group is one of the better-positioned Midwest regulated utilities in the AI load cycle, and it has converted that position into unusually favorable regulatory terms. The PSCW's Very Large Customer and Bespoke Resources tariffs, final written order issued May 21, 2026, allow a negotiated 10.48%–10.98% ROE on a 57.0% common equity ratio, fixed for the contract term, excluded from general rate cases and from the earnings sharing mechanisms that apply to the rest of WE's book. Against WE's 9.80% authorized ROE on a 53.0% equity layer, that is roughly 93 basis points of additional return on a 400 basis point thicker equity slice, with the upside non-refundable. WEC has already begun booking a "bespoke resources current return" line ($15.1M in 1H 2026), meaning cash return during construction rather than at completion. On the strength of that structure alone, WEC deserves a premium to the average regulated utility.

The problem is the counterparty and the funding. The contracting electric customer under those tariffs is Oracle America Cloud Services LLC, whose parent S&P downgraded to BBB− on July 9, 2026 and whose CDS-implied risk has been quoted near an 18-year high. The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses a peak collateral requirement of approximately $7 billion — roughly 20% of WEC's market capitalization and about 19% of the entire $37.5B 2026–2030 capital plan — and states that management "fully expects" OACS and its parent to provide it. Oracle has sued the PSCW in Ozaukee County Circuit Court to void the credit-rating and collateral rules; WE itself petitioned for rehearing to loosen those provisions on June 10, 2026 and was denied by operation of law. No 8-K through August 3, 2026 discloses how much collateral is actually posted. Capex steps from $4,398.1M (2025 actual) to $5,018.1M (2026E) to $6,949.5M (2027E), which on our sensitivity implies external funding near $4.5B in 2027 versus roughly $2.16B in 2025. The broader interest-rate backdrop (30-year Treasury level, Fed meeting/dissent count) is not documented in the provided sources and has been removed. [unverified]

At $107.63 the stock trades at 19.3x current-year consensus of $5.59 and 17.9x next-year $6.01, versus a peer median P/E of 17.4, and yields 3.54% versus a 4.0% peer median. That is a premium multiple and a below-peer yield attached to a company with more idiosyncratic binary risk than its peer set. Our probability-weighted 12-month total return is roughly 6%, close to a utility cost of equity and below it once the unresolved collateral question is weighted. HOLD: the 3.54% dividend at a 68.5% payout on 2026 guidance is covered and the rate-base compounding is real, but there is no entry asymmetry ahead of the Q4 2026 rate orders.

## Company Overview

WEC is a Milwaukee-based regulated holding company operating six reportable segments across four states. The Wisconsin segment (WE, WPS, WG, UMERC) is the earnings core, serving 1,696,600 electric and 1,545,000 gas customers, and generated $4,992.8M of electric revenue in 2025 — 90.0% of consolidated electric revenue. Illinois (Peoples Gas, North Shore Gas) is pure gas distribution: 1,064,000 customers, $1,683.6M of 2025 revenue. Other States (Minnesota, Michigan gas) adds 444,100 customers and $531.8M.

Two non-rate-base structures drive returns above what an "allowed ROE" screen implies. The Electric Transmission segment holds roughly a 60% equity interest in American Transmission Company; ATC contributes no consolidated revenue but delivered $86.1M of net income in 1H 2026, capital-light and FERC-regulated. Non-Utility Energy Infrastructure holds We Power (~2,500 MW leased to WE at a grandfathered 12.7% ROE on a 53–55% assumed equity ratio under Wisconsin's Leased Generation Law, with leases the PSCW may not modify except as the lease provides), Bluewater gas storage, and WECI's interests in 12 utility-scale wind and solar facilities with 10–22 year offtake contracts.

Authorized returns: WE/WPS/WG at 9.80% on 53.0% equity with earnings sharing mechanisms; UMERC 9.86%/50.0%; PGL and NSG 9.38% (weakest in the portfolio); MERC 9.65%/53.0%; MGU 9.86%/50.0%. WEC owns 8,375 MW of generation; 2025 supply mix was 30.5% coal, 32.8% gas, 8.4% owned renewables, 19.8% purchased nuclear.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Revenue rose 14.0% to $9,800.1M from $8,599.9M, substantially reflecting 2025 rate orders, colder weather and higher pass-through gas costs — the last of which inflates revenue without margin. GAAP diluted EPS fell to $4.81 from $4.83 even as net income to common reached $1,557.5M, because of a $205.0M pre-tax Q4 charge on the Illinois Attorney General settlements over PGL's QIP and uncollectible-expense riders (approximately $130M impairment, $25M revenue reduction, and a separate $50.0M UEA revenue reduction, with refunds flowing to customers 2026–2028) plus share growth.

The GAAP-to-adjusted bridge reconciles cleanly, which matters for earnings quality. Adjusted EPS of $5.27 less GAAP $4.81 is $0.46; implied diluted shares of $1,557.5M ÷ $4.81 = 323.8M; $0.46 × 323.8M = $148.9M after tax. The $205.0M pre-tax charge at a ~27% combined rate is $149.7M. The two agree within 0.5%, so the entire 2025 adjustment is one identified regulatory settlement rather than a stack of recurring "one-timers."

**1H 2026.** Revenue $5,496.3M (+6.5%); diluted EPS $3.36 versus $3.02 (+11.3%); net income to common $1,103.6M. Q2 revenue $2,062.1M (+2.6%), diluted EPS $0.91 versus $0.76. The Q2 EPS beat consensus by 10.5% per the surprise table; the consensus figure is a non-GAAP estimate and Q2 2026 appears to contain no non-recurring items, so the comparison is close to like-for-like. Reuters attributed the gain to higher commercial and industrial sales including data centers. Revenue came in below consensus and the reaffirmed full-year range was below some analyst forecasts; the stock fell from $113.61 on July 28 to $109.42 by July 31 and $107.63 by August 7.

**Earnings composition.** 1H 2026 net income to common: Utility $863.5M (78.2%), ATC $86.1M (7.8%), Non-Utility Energy Infrastructure $239.3M (21.7%), Corporate −$85.3M. The non-utility segment produced 21.7% of consolidated net income on $183.0M of external revenue (3.3% of total), reporting $181.2M pre-tax income and a $61.0M tax **benefit** — after-tax income exceeded pre-tax by 32%. Consolidated effective tax rate was 6.7% in 1H 2026 against FY2026 guidance of 5.5%–6.5%, with $168.5M of PTCs recognized. WEC has sold substantially all 2023–2025 WECI PTCs to third parties under IRA transferability with a significant portion of 2026 credits contracted, so these are cash benefits rather than deferred-tax bookkeeping. Two offsets to the concentration concern: wind output is seasonally weighted to Q1/Q4, flattering the 1H share; and the underlying facilities carry 10–22 year offtake contracts, so PTC expiry would compress the tax line rather than eliminate segment cash flow.

**Returns.** FY2025 net income to common ÷ year-end common equity of $13,613.6M = 11.44%; adding back the ~$149M after-tax Illinois charge, 12.54%. That sits 250–330bp above the 9.38%–9.86% authorized subsidiary ROEs, and it does so with $3,052.8M of goodwill in the equity base mechanically depressing the ratio. The gap is structural — We Power's 12.7% lease ROE, ATC's FERC returns, WECI's tax credits — not regulatory over-earning. It is also finite: We Power leases began 2005–2011 with 25–30 year terms, rolling off between roughly 2030 and 2041.

**Cash flow and the funding gap.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,379.4M against $4,398.1M of capex, so free cash flow was −$1,018.7M; dividends of roughly $1,145M put external funding near $2,164M. The capital plan is $37.5B for 2026–2030 ($33.4B utility, $4.1B ATC share), with utility capex of $5,018.1M (2026), $6,949.5M (2027) and $6,965.6M (2028). A 2027 sensitivity — capex $6,949.5M plus ~$215M ATC plus ~$1,320M dividends, against OCF compounding at 8%/yr to ~$3,940M — implies roughly $4,545M of external funding in a single year, about 13% of current market capitalization. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast; the OCF assumption is highly sensitive to the Q4 2026 rate orders. Do not annualize 1H 2026 OCF of $2,210.7M: the 10-K states that gas cash collections concentrate January–June and that late-summer/fall requires short-term borrowing, and 1H benefited from a ~$479.5M receivable drawdown.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $52,750.4M at 6/30/26. Total debt $21,942.2M against $13,613.6M common equity at 12/31/25 — debt/total capitalization 61.7% actual, 59.8% adjusted for rating-agency equity credit on junior subordinated notes; 61.4%/59.6% at 6/30/26. Authorized equity layers are 50–53%, so consolidated leverage runs above the regulated capital structures, as is normal at the holding company. Current liabilities exceed current assets by $2,426.6M, and $11.7B of minimum purchase obligations (coal, gas, transport, PPAs) sit off the income statement. Moody's revised WPS's outlook to negative in November 2025.

Composite distress screens are not decision-useful here: a rate-regulated utility structurally runs negative working capital, low asset turnover (~0.19x) and low retained-earnings-to-assets. The informative leverage signals are the 61.4% debt/cap versus 50–53% authorized layers and the WPS outlook revision.

**Dilution.** At 6/30/26 there were ~325.85M shares (cross-check: $35.07B ÷ $107.63 = 326.1M). A $3.0B equity distribution program was established in October 2025; 6,392,021 shares sat under unsettled forward sale contracts with expected proceeds of ~$725.6M — 1.96% of existing shares, or 1.92% post-issuance ownership dilution if physically settled. Separately, $2,625M of convertible notes ($862.5M 2027, $900.0M 2028, $862.5M 2029) had **not** met conversion conditions at 6/30/26; only ~2.4M shares were in Q2 diluted EPS. Two tranches trade well above par (121% and 124% of par), so conversion triggers are approaching without having been reached. Conversion prices were not verifiable; maximum contingent dilution belongs in scenario work, not the headline share count.

**Customer stress.** The Wisconsin segment's credit loss provision was $99.3M in 1H 2026 versus $37.8M in 1H 2025; $51.1M was deferred for future recovery (versus $13.0M), so net bad-debt expense roughly doubled to $48.2M from $24.8M. Receivables more than 90 days past due rose to $134.0M from $89.8M at 12/31/25. Wisconsin riders mitigate most earnings impact and a June-versus-December comparison inside a heating cycle is not clean, but a gross provision that doubles on a like-for-like half-year basis is a real household-stress signal arriving exactly as WEC files consecutive rate increases.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on rate base, not volumes, and WEC states as much. The FY2025 10-K forecasts 2026 retail electric sales volumes +1.6% but adds: "excluding the very large data center customers, we currently forecast sales volumes to be relatively flat for 2026," with retail gas volumes decreasing slightly. Customer counts grew about 0.8%. The company is "engaged in discussions with a small number of customers" for large-scale data centers; on the Q2 call management referenced at least two additional operators at scales far smaller than Oracle or Microsoft.

The pipeline is substantial where it exists: Microsoft investment in southern Wisconsin exceeding $20B; up to 2.6 GW of load growth in the Milwaukee–Chicago corridor through 2030; Vantage's Port Washington campus at 1.3 GW through 2030 with potential incremental 2.2 GW for 3.5 GW total, targeted to come online as soon as late 2027. Generation additions approved or pending include 1,228 MW of gas approved plus 1,660 MW pending, 955 MW solar and 411 MW storage approved plus 1,333 MW solar and 212 MW storage pending, and 160 MW of wind. OCPP Units 7 & 8 retirement was pushed to 2027, with roughly $604.6M of net book value to retire.

Near-term earnings growth is mid-single-digit. FY2026 guidance of $5.51–$5.61 (externally reported and reaffirmed on the July 29 call; not verified against an 8-K exhibit) against FY2025 adjusted EPS of $5.27 implies +4.6% to +6.5%, midpoint +5.5%. Consensus is $5.59 for 2026 and $6.01 for 2027, implying 7.5% growth. The composition is where the caution sits: 1H 2026 actual EPS of $3.36 against the guidance midpoint implies 2H 2026 of $2.15–$2.25 versus roughly $2.25 of 2H 2025 adjusted EPS — flat to down about 4%, after an 11.3% first-half gain. Utilities routinely guide the back half conservatively with two rate decisions pending, and a Q4 order could land outside the guidance basis; this is a hypothesis, not confirmed deceleration. But it is corroborated by estimate revisions: the next-quarter consensus was cut 7.4% in 30 days and 10.2% in 90 days, while current-year and next-year estimates were unchanged. That pattern — near-term quarter marked down, annuals static — reads as timing rather than impairment, and it is the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that would justify paying up.

The decisive growth catalysts are dated. Rate cases filed April 1, 2026 with decisions expected Q4 2026: WE electric requesting +$175.8M (4.7%) for 2027 and +$179.5M (4.5%) for 2028 at a 9.9% ROE; PGL requesting +$144.0M (14.6%) at a 10.10% ROE. Together roughly $320M of annualized Wisconsin revenue plus $144M in Illinois is at stake — meaningful against ~$1.56B of net income.

## Competitive Landscape

Franchise monopolies do not compete for retail customers. WEC's real contests are for regulatory capital allocation across four commissions plus FERC, and now for hyperscale load against neighboring utilities.

On the second front WEC has executed well. The Bespoke Resources tariff assigns specific generation to specific very large customers, a cleaner allocation than the arrangements Wisconsin regulators criticized in the Alliant–Meta transaction. In a jurisdiction where the commission has signaled it dislikes opacity, structural transparency is a competitive asset. WEC also owns roughly 60% of ATC, giving it transmission optionality in a corridor where interconnection is the binding constraint — though even there the PSCW recently sent an ATC data-center connection project back for rework.

The trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving. Positive: an approved, premium-return tariff; a 2.6 GW corridor pipeline; Microsoft signed under the tariff at a separate Mount Pleasant campus; current-return recognition during construction. Negative: direct customer concentration is worsening, not improving. A revenue mix shifting toward large commercial and industrial load does not constitute diversification when the incremental load is attributable to a handful of named counterparties.

The Illinois gas business is the structural offset. PGL must retire all cast and ductile iron pipe under 36 inches by January 1, 2035 under a February 2025 ICC order, with civil penalties for non-compliance and an ICC-hired safety monitor, ramping to roughly $500M/year by 2028 — spent at the portfolio's lowest allowed ROE (9.38%), with prudency reviewed only retrospectively, in a city pursuing building electrification, under a commission that has already extracted a $205.0M settlement. Capital must be deployed regardless of the terminal value of the asset. That business competes for the same balance sheet as the Wisconsin opportunity.

One credibility note: the stated goal is coal as backup only by 2030 and eliminated by 2032, yet the disclosed supply mix rises 29.0% (2023) → 29.4% (2024) → 30.5% (2025) → 30.7% (2026E). The reconciling evidence is real — contracted coal tonnage falls from 4,542k tons (2026) to 1,125k (2027) to zero (2028), and OCPP 7&8 plus Weston 3 retire by 2031 — but near-term utilization is rising to serve growing load while replacement capacity is under construction, and the OCPP retirement already slipped from 2026 to 2027.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Single-counterparty credit concentration (the defining risk).** The Q2 10-Q names OACS as the very large customer under the VLC and Bespoke tariffs and discloses that, following the parent's downgrade, contracts require additional collateral scaling with cumulative project spend. Reuters has described Oracle as on the cusp of junk. Management stated on the call that collateral adequate to current exposure is already in hand and that "I don't think the collateral will be an issue long term," and the ~$7B figure is a peak, not a present obligation. But no filing or 8-K through August 3, 2026 confirms the amount posted. Get the roles right: Vantage is the Port Washington developer, OACS is WE's contracting electric customer and the collateral obligor, OpenAI is a reported co-developer and not a WEC contracting party, and WE owns the assets and bears stranded-cost exposure if OACS fails to perform.

**2. The regulatory-protection paradox.** WE petitioned for rehearing on June 10, 2026 to loosen the collateral provisions, arguing the rules would "limit the ability of numerous investment-grade companies to invest in Wisconsin"; the petition was deemed denied. Oracle then sued the PSCW in Ozaukee County Circuit Court to set aside the credit-rating requirements. WE is an interested party but did not join as plaintiff. If the court strikes the provisions, WEC gains a commercially easier path to signing hyperscalers and loses the ratepayer backstop that insulates its VLC-directed investment from becoming a political liability. Management's stated rationale — Wisconsin's competitiveness for future customers — is coherent, and the CEO has said ratings agencies reacted positively to the threshold. We are not inferring motive from procedural posture; both readings fit the record, and the outcome and timing of the litigation are unresolved.

**3. Illinois asymmetry.** Lowest allowed ROE (9.38%), a $205.0M settlement with refunds running 2026–2028, a mandated ~$500M/year pipe program to 2035, a pending 14.6% PGL rate request in Chicago in a politically exposed setting, and a "Future of Gas" proceeding threatening terminal value.

**4. Tax-credit policy cliff.** With roughly 21.7% of 1H 2026 net income traceable to the tax-credit-advantaged segment and a 5.5%–6.5% guided effective tax rate, OBBBA's July 4, 2026 construction-start requirement and the June 2026 court decision vacating beginning-of-construction safe harbor guidance are first-order, not footnotes. Mitigant: 10–22 year offtake contracts mean PTC expiry compresses the tax line rather than the revenue line.

**5. Funding and rate sensitivity.** External funding near $4.5B in 2027 on our sensitivity, a $3.0B ATM with ~1.96% of shares already under forwards, converts approaching but not at their triggers, 61.4% debt/cap, and a WPS negative outlook — all into a rising long-end yield environment [macro level unverified].

**6. Wisconsin politics.** Data centers are a live issue in the gubernatorial race, and the PSCW recently sent an ATC data-center connection project back for rework. WEC's plan requires sustained approvals in an election year. (A referenced Marquette poll on voter attitudes was not independently verified.)

**7. Affordability.** Gross Wisconsin credit-loss provision up 163% year over year on a like-for-like half-year, 90-plus-day receivables up 49% since December, arriving as WEC seeks consecutive increases.

**8. Governance.** Combined chair/CEO; CEO compensation of $12,188,492 for FY2025, up 27.6% over two years; five-year cumulative TSR of $135.40 per $100 against a self-selected peer group at $149.01 — roughly 13.6 points of underperformance while pay rose. Adjusted EPS is the company-selected incentive metric, so the Illinois charge sat outside the headline measure even as GAAP EPS declined 0.4% (Net Income is also a listed measure, so the charge was not necessarily costless). No open-market insider purchases were found; February 2026 activity was exercise-and-sell against options struck at $58.305 and $68.175 that are approaching ten-year expiry — routine, not directional, though those sales cleared at $110.70–$116.50 versus $107.63 today.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($126, +17.3%).** Q4 2026 orders land near the ask — roughly $320M of annualized Wisconsin revenue at or near a 9.9% ROE and meaningful Illinois relief — validating the $37.5B plan and lifting 2027 EPS toward or above the $6.01 consensus. OACS collateral is confirmed posted, or the Ozaukee County case resolves without gutting the tariff's protections, removing the single largest overhang. Port Washington energizes on schedule in late 2027, and one or two additional operators sign under the Bespoke tariff, converting a single-name dependency into a portfolio. 21x $6.01 = $126, plus a 3.5% dividend for total return near 21%.

**Bear case ($92, −14.5%).** Oracle's credit deteriorates further, collateral becomes contested in fact rather than only in law, or the campus is delayed or scaled back — putting WE's VLC-directed spend in the position of needing recovery from other ratepayers in an election year. Illinois delivers another adverse outcome on the 14.6% request while the mandated $500M/year pipe program continues at 9.38%. The OBBBA construction-start rules and the vacated safe harbor compress the tax-credit stream that carries a fifth of earnings. The 2027 funding requirement is met with equity at a de-rated price. 15.5x on $5.95 = $92.

**Base case ($113, +5.0%).** Rate orders arrive with the usual haircut — call it 70–85% of the ask — supporting mid-single-digit EPS growth toward $6.01 in 2027. Collateral proves adequate to actual exposure, the litigation drags, and no additional large VLC signs in the next twelve months. WEC holds a modest premium to the 17.4x peer median at 18.8x on $6.01 = $113. With a 3.54% dividend, total return roughly 8.5%.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear gives about 2.5% of price return plus 3.5% of dividend, or roughly 6% total — at or slightly below a reasonable utility cost of equity, with the largest single risk unresolved and unverifiable from filings. That is HOLD territory, not REDUCE (the dividend is covered, the businesses are regulated with cost recovery, and the tariff structure is genuinely valuable) and not ACCUMULATE (there is no margin of safety at 19.3x with a below-peer yield).

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have declined 6.9 → 6.5 → 6.2 → 5.5 across four reads, with the same HOLD each time. The further decline is not a reaction to the 5.4% price drop since July 28; it rests on identifiable new facts since June 18: Oracle's downgrade to BBB− on July 9 and near-junk CDS pricing, the July 11 denial of WE's rehearing petition followed by Oracle's suit against the regulator, the Wisconsin gross credit-loss provision doubling in the Q2 10-Q, a Q2 revenue miss with full-year guidance below some forecasts, and next-quarter estimates cut 7.4%/10.2%. Working the other direction — and the reason this is not a downgrade to REDUCE — the May 21 final VLC order confirmed a return structure superior to anything in the peer set, and the Q2 EPS beat with an 11.3% first-half gain shows the base business delivering. Financial health, management quality and risk factors continue to carry the decline; competitive position holds, because the tariff design is a real advantage even as counterparty concentration worsens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: 6–9 months, anchored on the Q4 2026 rate orders.**

- **Base case target: $113** — 18.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.01, an ~8% premium to the 17.4x peer median for the VLC return structure and ATC. Upside from $107.63: **+5.0%**; with the $3.81 dividend, total return **~8.5%**.
- **Bull case target: $126** — 21x $6.01. Upside **+17.3%**.
- **Bear case target: $92** — 15.5x $5.95. Downside **−14.5%**.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Filed confirmation — 8-K, 10-Q or transcript — of the amount of collateral posted by OACS, at a level covering then-current exposure, **or** a court ruling that preserves the tariff's credit-support provisions substantially intact.
2. Q4 2026 rate orders granting at least ~80% of the WE electric request at a 9.7%+ ROE, with Illinois avoiding a punitive outcome, and 2027 consensus holding at or above $6.01.
3. A price near **$95–98** on unchanged fundamentals — 16x $6.01 with a 3.9%+ yield at peer-median terms — which would restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Oracle downgraded to speculative grade, or any disclosure that OACS has failed to post required collateral, or a Port Washington delay/descope announcement.

## Conclusion

WEC has done the difficult regulatory work well. Securing a tariff at 10.48%–10.98% ROE on a 57% equity layer, fixed for term and excluded from earnings sharing, is a better outcome than most utilities have obtained from the AI load cycle, and the current-return mechanism during construction improves cash timing. Consolidated ROE of 11.44% GAAP and 12.54% adjusted, against authorized subsidiary ROEs of 9.38%–9.86%, reflects structures — the We Power leases, ATC, WECI's transferable tax credits — that are hard to replicate and, in the leases' case, protected from regulatory modification.

That quality is priced. At 19.3x current-year and 17.9x next-year consensus with a 3.54% yield, WEC carries a premium multiple and a below-peer yield while entering the steepest part of a capital cycle that on our sensitivity requires roughly $4.5B of external funding in 2027 alone. And the single largest incremental growth commitment rests on an electric customer whose parent is rated BBB−, secured by a collateral package with a ~$7B peak requirement whose legal validity is being challenged by the obligor and whose posted amount cannot be verified from any filing. That combination — premium valuation, doubling funding need, unverified counterparty protection, and an Illinois gas business that must spend ~$500M/year at the lowest allowed ROE in the portfolio — is why we score the risk dimension at 4.8 and the valuation at 4.8.

Existing owners are compensated with a covered 3.54% dividend at a 68.5% payout while the Q4 2026 rate orders and the Oracle collateral question resolve. New capital gains nothing by acting before those resolve; $95–98 would restore the asymmetry, as would filed confirmation that the collateral is posted alongside a constructive Wisconsin order. **HOLD.**
