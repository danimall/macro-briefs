## Executive Summary

RGA's Q2 2026 result, furnished on Form 8-K on August 6, was the strongest print in the company's history on its own reported basis: adjusted operating income of $586M ($8.89 per diluted share) against $315M ($4.72) a year earlier, GAAP net income of $462M ($7.01), trailing-twelve-month adjusted operating ROE of 17.4% (18.4% excluding notable items), and book value per share excluding AOCI of $173.77, up 11.5% year over year. There were no notable items in Q2 2026, Q2 2025, H1 2026 or H1 2025, so the improvement is not an assumption-review artifact. All eight geographic sub-segments improved year over year, and the quarterly dividend was raised 5.4% to $0.98.

The share price has not moved with the earnings. RGA closed at $241.79 on July 17 and trades at $243.005 now, so the fourth consecutive beat and a 6.8% ninety-day upward revision to current-year consensus (to $28.26) have arrived as multiple compression rather than price appreciation. At $243.005 the stock trades at 8.60x current-year consensus, 8.29x next-year consensus of $29.30, 1.159x reported book value of $209.73, and 1.398x book excluding AOCI. That is a cheaper entry than the prior review at roughly 9.5x, on better numbers.

Two things keep this at HOLD rather than a purchase recommendation. First, the entire valuation argument depends on which book-value denominator is economically correct. On the ex-AOCI basis the returns are high-teens and 1.40x book is inexpensive; on reported equity the trailing ROE is 11.5% and 1.16x book is close to fair. The gap is the +$8.68 billion discount-rate credit sitting in AOCI, which is an accounting offset to a $8.48 billion gross unrealized loss on available-for-sale bonds and reverses if rates fall. Second, RGA completes its annual assumption review in Q3 every year; the 2025 review produced a $222 million unfavorable UK mortality-improvement charge that pushed EMEA Traditional to a full-year operating loss of $106 million. The 2026 review is entirely ahead of the current price, and the driver cited last year was a slow-moving trend assumption rather than a one-off. At 0.8% below the 52-week high, with consensus implying only ~3.7% EPS growth next year, the margin of safety does not compensate for a precedented, dated, unhedged event.

## Company Overview

RGA is a Missouri-domiciled life and health reinsurance holding company, one of a handful of global players in that market. As of June 30, 2026 it reported approximately $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force and $167.1 billion of total assets. It reports nine segments: four geographies (U.S. & Latin America, Canada, EMEA, Asia Pacific), each split into Traditional and Financial Solutions, plus Corporate and Other.

Traditional reinsurance assumes mortality and morbidity risk from primary insurers in exchange for premium — individual and group life, disability, long-term care, critical illness. Individual life treaties run for the remaining lifetime of the insured, with premium earned over 10–30 years. Financial Solutions earns investment spread and fees rather than biometric margin: longevity reinsurance, asset-intensive reinsurance (annuities, COLI, indexed and variable life, stable value), pension risk transfer, fee-based synthetic GICs ($16.9 billion notional at 3/31/26), financial reinsurance and capital solutions, and $5.8 billion of Funding Agreement Backed Notes.

The business mix has shifted materially. In H1 2026, Financial Solutions generated $746M of the $1,372M segment pre-tax total, or 54%. Total cash and invested assets were $142.0 billion at 3/31/26, of which $107.3 billion was AFS fixed maturities, $11.3 billion mortgage loans, $8.4 billion funds withheld at interest, and $4.1 billion limited partnerships and real estate joint ventures.

Two quarters is not enough to distinguish normalization from favorable variance.

### Leverage, liquidity and the screening artifacts

Automated screens return an Altman Z-Score of 0.33 and debt/equity of 10.63 for RGA. Neither is usable. Altman's largest-weighted input is working capital to total assets, and an insurance balance sheet is unclassified — there is no current/non-current split. The market-equity-to-liabilities term is distorted because $150.7 billion of RGA's $164.1 billion of liabilities are policyholder reserves and deposit-type contracts matched against dedicated assets, not callable debt. Sales-to-assets is structurally ~0.16 for any balance-sheet-intensive insurer. Four of five inputs are undefined for this business model.

The leverage metrics that matter: long-term debt of $6,105M at 3/31/26 (up from $5,710M), total borrowings $6.2B, weighted-average interest rate 5.40%. Debt to debt-plus-reported-equity is 31.5%; on ex-AOCI equity of $10,980M it is 35.7%. The disclosed revolver covenant caps consolidated indebtedness at 35% of indebtedness plus "adjusted RGA Inc.'s shareholders' equity," with a $5.8 billion minimum consolidated net worth. RGA states it was in compliance with all covenants at 3/31/26. My two calculations bracket the 35% limit, which means the covenant's definitions differ from balance-sheet captions — most plausibly by excluding subordinated debentures from indebtedness. This is a definitional gap, not evidence of a covenant problem, but it does mean headroom for further debt-funded block growth cannot be reproduced from public disclosure.

Liquidity is ample: $5.4 billion of cash plus short-term investments, an undrawn $850 million revolver, $302 million of additional FHLB capacity, and a $200 million committed contingent-capital facility.

### Investment portfolio and credit

Average investment yield excluding spread-related business was 5.33% in Q2 2026 versus 5.31%, and 4.93% in Q1 versus 4.64%. Stripping variable investment income, the Q1 underlying yield was 4.85% versus 4.90% — flat to slightly down. The reported improvement came from variable investment income swinging from negative $6M to positive $26M. Credit costs are rising off a low base: Q1 2026 allowance and impairment charges of $43M versus $8M, the fixed-maturity credit allowance up from $210M to $232M, B-rated holdings from 0.6% to 1.2% of fair value, and "in or near default" positions from $42M to $124M amortized cost. RGA also took $106M of realized capital losses in Q1 repositioning the portfolio for yield.

Two portfolio concentrations warrant naming. Japanese government securities rose from $6,516M to $9,873M amortized cost in a single quarter (+51.5%), reaching 7.3% of fixed maturities, against fair value of $7,817M — a $2,056 million gross unrealized loss, roughly 21% below cost. These are long-duration governments purchased to match a 12-year weighted-average Japanese liability duration accreting at 2.5%, so the mark reflects asset/liability matching in a rising-JGB-yield environment rather than credit deterioration. What cannot be verified from public disclosure is hedge effectiveness on a position growing this fast; a mismatch would surface in earnings, not only OCI. Given continued Japanese rate pressure, this is the portfolio line to monitor. Second, mortgage loans of $11.3 billion are 95.6% commercial and include $1,720M of office (15.0%); $335M (3.1%) carries debt-service coverage below 1.00x against a $115M total allowance, and the watch list grew from $125M to $143M.

### The AOCI question

RGA's AOCI is positive $2,314 million at 3/31/26, so book value excluding AOCI ($173.77) is *lower* than reported book ($209.73) — the reverse of most insurers' recent experience. The composition:

| Component (net of tax) | 12/31/25 | 3/31/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Net unrealized investment losses | ($4,807M) | ($6,466M) |
| Effect of updating discount rates on future policy benefits | $7,372M | $8,680M |
| FX translation | $121M | $101M |
| Pension / MRB credit risk | ($4M) | ($1M) |
| **Total** | **$2,682M** | **$2,314M** |

Gross unrealized losses on AFS fixed maturities rose $1,526M in the quarter to $8,483M as rates rose, while the LDTI liability-discount-rate remeasurement moved $1,664M pre-tax in RGA's favor. Net AOCI moved only −$368M. The two are economically linked — the same rate move marking down long assets marks down long liabilities. The book is 94.3% investment grade, amortized cost is $114,976M against $107,328M fair value, and $7,324M of gross unrealized loss sits in the twelve-months-or-greater bucket across 7,588 securities (up from 5,968), with no allowance because RGA neither intends to sell nor judges collection improbable.

The counterargument is that the offset is an accounting relationship, not a cash-flow guarantee: the discount-rate OCI benefit reverses if rates fall, whereas realized credit losses on the asset side do not reverse. That asymmetry is why the valuation debate has no clean resolution.

### Cash flow

Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $2,873 million versus $1,429 million. This should not be annualized. Insurance operating cash flow is dominated by block-transaction timing (the Equitable block began contributing mid-2025), Q1 income taxes paid were $37 million against a $110 million provision, and RGA reported a $4,619 million non-cash transfer of invested assets in the quarter. Four times the quarter implies ~$11.5 billion; anchoring on the prior-year quarter implies ~$5.7 billion. The width of that range is the finding — operating cash flow is not a usable valuation input for this business.

## Growth Analysis

Balance-sheet growth is the dominant fact. Total assets grew $37.9 billion (+31.9%) in 2025 and a further $10.5 billion in H1 2026 to $167.1 billion, up 25.2% from $133.5 billion at June 2025. Three identifiable drivers:

**The Equitable transaction** (executed July 31, 2025). RGA's U.S. Financial Solutions segment assumed a 75% quota share of Equitable Holdings' subsidiaries' in-force individual life liabilities on coinsurance and modified coinsurance bases, with total liabilities of approximately $12 billion. A further $16.1 billion of separate account liabilities are reinsured on a modco basis and offset on RGA's balance sheet — the ceding company retains the separate account assets and RGA earns a fee on policyholder account value without direct investment-performance exposure. Equitable's subsidiaries are the ceding companies; RGA is the assuming reinsurer and does not hold the policyholder relationships. Q1 2026 contribution was $39 million to pre-tax income.

**Organic asset-intensive growth.** U.S. Financial Solutions asset-intensive invested assets grew from $25.8 billion (3/31/25) to $37.2 billion (3/31/26), driven by $13.6 billion of new transactions less $0.8 billion of runoff and $1.5 billion moved out via external retrocession. Asia Pacific Financial Solutions' liability for future policy benefits at original discount rate grew 49% to $21.4B; EMEA's from $7.1B to $10.6B.

**Deposits.** Net deposits on investment-type policies were $1,578 million in Q1 2026 alone. Policyholder account balances rose from $24.8 billion (3/31/25) to $38.7 billion (3/31/26). Note that deposit and new-transaction figures are period flows while invested-asset and account-balance figures are ending balances; they are not additive.

Asia Pacific is the most striking engine: FY2025 adjusted operating income of $759M, up $222M, and H1 2026 Traditional plus Financial Solutions of $439M versus $346M. Policyholder account balances there roughly doubled from $4.6B to $9.1B.

The constraint on the growth score is that balance-sheet growth is not translating into comparable per-share earnings growth in consensus. Current-year consensus is $28.26 and next-year $29.30 — implied growth of 3.7%. Ninety-day revisions were +6.8% for the current year but only +1.0% for next year, and thirty-day revisions were +4.3% and +0.6%. Four consecutive beats averaging about +23% surprise (35.1%, 14.2%, 33.5%, 9.0%) suggest consensus is too low, but the sell-side is modelling a plateau in per-share earnings power as spread-based business dilutes ROE mix and additional capital is deployed. The estimate-revision magnitude here (+6.8% current-year, ninety days) is positive but well short of the threshold that signals a genuine earnings inflection, and the base of the improvement is a depressed prior-year comparison rather than accelerating volume.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining industry dynamic is the migration of life-insurance balance sheets to reinsurers, and it is visible directly in RGA's numbers. Total assets grew 32% in 2025 and 25% year over year, almost entirely through assumed blocks and asset-intensive/longevity transactions, while U.S. Traditional net premiums shrank. Primary insurers are shedding capital-intensive in-force liabilities; reinsurers with capital, ratings and asset-management capability are the buyers. The Equitable transaction is the clearest instance.

This changes who RGA competes with. Asset-intensive and PRT blocks are contested by private-capital-backed platforms — Athene/Apollo, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Sixth Street — that often carry lower cost of capital via permanent third-party capital. RGA's response is Ruby Re, a Missouri-domiciled sidecar launched in Q4 2023 that RGA sponsors but does not invest in or consolidate, and to which it retrocedes U.S. asset-intensive business, holding a $4.1 billion recoverable from it. That lets RGA write more business than its own capital would support, with third-party investors bearing the strain while RGA retains origination and management economics.

Three structural advantages appear in the filings. First, genuine geographic earnings diversification: H1 2026 adjusted operating income before taxes was U.S. & LatAm $575M, Asia Pacific $439M, EMEA $354M, Canada $104M — no region dominant. Second, a mortality/longevity internal hedge. FY2025 demonstrated it literally: the adverse UK mortality-improvement update cost EMEA Traditional $222 million while EMEA Financial Solutions recognized roughly $24 million of favorable longevity experience. The hedge is real and not size-matched. Third, access to multi-billion-dollar block transactions, a capital-and-ratings-dependent moat rather than a technological one.

Capacity discipline supports the competitive read: RGA raised per-life retention from $8 million to $30 million effective January 1, 2025, accepting a $136 million future-policy-benefit remeasurement loss in Q3 2024 to do so. That is a statement that retrocession pricing was unattractive relative to retaining mortality risk, and it reduces future ceding costs at the expense of higher per-claim volatility.

Against this: cumulative TSR (2021 = 100) shows RGA at 201.29 in 2024, overtaking a peer group at 189.87, then falling back below in 2025 (195.32 vs 201.00). Trajectory is improving on operating metrics and roughly flat on relative market performance. Competitive position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Q3 assumption review — the nearest dated risk.** RGA's stated policy is to complete its annual assumption review in the third quarter. The 2025 review produced a $222 million unfavorable UK mortality charge, pushing EMEA Traditional to a full-year adjusted operating loss of $106 million and its loss ratio to 101% from 93%. H1 2026 contained zero notable items and no cash-flow assumption changes, so the full 2026 outcome is ahead of the current price. Because the cited driver was deteriorating mortality *improvement* — a trend assumption — a repeat true-up is plausible. Partially offsetting: a large reset in one year often moves the assumption to a more conservative basis, and the same trend benefits the longevity and PRT books.

**Counterparty concentration is flat to modestly worse, not improving.** No client exceeded 10% of gross premiums and other revenues in Q1 2026 or Q1 2025. But 90% or more of the $3.1 billion of U.S. Financial Solutions funds withheld at interest is associated with two clients (versus $3.2 billion with two clients a year earlier), the majority of the $3.7 billion policy loan balance sits with one client, and excluding Ruby Re three reinsurers account for ~33% of ceded receivables. Claims recoverable more than 90 days past due rose from $6M to $16M. The revenue-mix shift toward Financial Solutions has not diversified these named exposures.

**Ruby Re.** A $4.1 billion recoverable from an unconsolidated vehicle whose capitalization, investor base and financial condition are not disclosed in the filings reviewed. Certain agreements are modco or funds-withheld, so RGA retains legal ownership of the withheld assets while economic benefits pass to Ruby Re. The obligation runs to RGA; the exposure is Ruby Re's ability to pay.

**Crediting-rate floor asymmetry.** Of the $26,299M U.S. Financial Solutions policyholder account balance, $22,063M (84%) is already at its guaranteed minimum crediting rate, and $14,214M carries minimums of 4.00% or greater. RGA cannot reduce credited rates on this block, so a falling-rate environment compresses spread directly into earnings. This is dormant under the current higher-for-longer policy setting but becomes live the moment the rate path turns.

**Ratings-trigger cliff.** For the majority of RGA's derivative agreements, a termination event occurs if senior long-term debt ratings fall below BBB+/Baa1 or financial strength ratings below A−/A3, across $53.2 billion of notional. This makes capital management structurally ratings-constrained and helps explain the modest buyback.

**Off-balance-sheet commitments.** Maximum potential obligations for statutory reserve support (Regulation XXX/A-XXX funding plus one real-estate lease commitment) aggregate roughly $29.6 billion, laddered from 2034 to 2049. RGA judges the triggering events remote and these are fee-earning contingent commitments rather than funded debt — maximum exposure, not expected loss. Separately, unfunded investment commitments total $2,247M (LPs/JVs), $2,702M (bank loans and private placements), $344M (mortgage loans) and $124M (lifetime mortgages), callable at counterparty request.

**Tax.** The Q1 2026 effective rate rose to 24.9% from 22.2% on foreign income taxed above the U.S. rate and an increased valuation allowance on foreign tax credits; Q2's GAAP rate of 23.4% exceeded management's own 22–23% expectation. With operations across Bermuda, Ireland, the UK, Canada, Japan, Korea and Australia, the OECD global minimum tax regime is a structural overhang.

**Verification gaps.** No Q2 2026 Form 10-Q is yet available, so balance-sheet detail (AOCI composition, unrealized-loss aging, funds-withheld concentration, mortgage credit metrics) is current only through March 31, 2026. No earnings call transcript was retrievable, so management's commentary on the Q3 assumption review is not in evidence. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (June 22 and July 9, 2026) remain unidentified, though CEO and CFO signature blocks are unchanged between the May 8 10-Q and the August release.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $295 (+21.4%)

Claims experience continues to normalize toward pricing assumptions, extending the trend management dates to 2023, and the Q3 assumption review passes without a material charge or with a favorable net offset from the longevity book. Ex-notables operating ROE holds in the 17–18% range, ex-AOCI book value compounds toward $191–195, and the market begins capitalizing spread-and-fee earnings from assumed blocks nearer to 1.55x ex-AOCI book. Consensus, which has been beaten four consecutive times by an average of about 23%, migrates from $29.30 toward $32, and 9.5–10x on that gives $300. Asia Pacific continues compounding at 40%-plus liability growth, the block pipeline stays priced at high-teens returns, and Ruby Re capacity lets RGA write beyond its own capital base. The $400M remaining on the repurchase authorization is deployed at a discount to intrinsic value.

### Bear case — $205 (−15.6%)

The Q3 assumption review produces another UK or U.S. mortality true-up of $200–350 million, confirming that 2025 was a trend reset rather than a one-off and reopening the question of reserve adequacy across a $72.3 billion future-policy-benefit balance. Simultaneously, credit costs continue the trajectory from $8M to $43M per quarter, the Japanese government bond position's mark deepens as BOJ policy tightens, and the market marks RGA on reported equity rather than ex-AOCI — where trailing ROE is 11.5% and the current 1.16x multiple already fully reflects it. U.S. Traditional premium erosion continues, revealing that the segment is losing share rather than repricing. A rate-cutting cycle compresses spread on the $22.1 billion of policyholder balances already at guaranteed minimum crediting rates. Multiple compresses to 1.15x on a book that grows less than 5%.

### What would resolve the debate

Two disclosures. First, the Q2 2026 10-Q, which will show whether the AFS unrealized loss and discount-rate credit continued to offset, whether funds-withheld concentration moved, and whether credit allowances kept building. Second, the Q3 release, which carries the assumption review. Both land inside the next quarter.
