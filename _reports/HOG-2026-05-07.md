## Executive Summary

Harley-Davidson is in the early stages of a turnaround under a new CEO who assumed the role in October 2025 and announced the "Back to the Bricks" strategic plan on May 5, 2026. The stock trades at $23.84, roughly in line with the $2.6B market cap vs. $3.14B in stockholders' equity and $3.09B in cash. This below-book-value situation looks superficially attractive, but the underlying motorcycle business (HDMC) generated an operating loss in 2025, and 2026 guidance calls for only breakeven (-$40M to +$10M). Revenue has fallen 27% from 2022 peak levels. The HDFS financial services arm was restructured via a $5B+ asset sale to KKR/PIMCO, creating a one-time balance sheet improvement but permanently reducing the segment's earnings contribution from ~$250M to ~$50-60M annually.

The Q1 2026 retail inflection (+16% U.S., +8% global) is encouraging, but it follows a cumulative 17% decline and reflects extremely easy comparisons plus dealer inventory destocking. The Sprint (late 2026) and Sportster (2027) represent the first credible product strategy to address the demographic cliff, but revenue contribution is 12-18 months away. Tariffs ($45M in Q1 alone, $75-105M for the full year), the weakening consumer, and a 3-of-4-quarter EPS miss streak create a challenging fundamental backdrop. The 19% short interest, elevated put/call OI ratio (1.45), and ATM IV at 59.7% vs. 39.9% HV confirm that the market is pricing meaningful downside risk.

The turnaround is unproven and the earnings trajectory remains negative for the next 2-3 quarters. There is no identified catalyst in the next 12 months that generates positive earnings momentum — the Sprint doesn't ship until late 2026 at earliest, HDMC breakeven guidance implies continued margin pressure, and tariff uncertainty persists. AVOID.

## Company Overview

Harley-Davidson operates three segments: HDMC (core motorcycle manufacturing and merchandising), HDFS (financial services, now capital-light after the KKR/PIMCO transaction), and LiveWire (electric motorcycles — a cash-burning subsidiary producing 653 units in 2025 and losing ~$75M annually).

The company holds 34.5% of the U.S. 601+cc motorcycle market and 3.4% in Europe, both declining. It manufactures primarily in the U.S. (York, PA and Wisconsin plants) with a Thailand facility for Asian distribution. Average selling price is ~$21K+, positioning the brand as premium/aspirational but creating affordability challenges in a high-rate environment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Decline:** Revenue fell from $4.93B (2022) to $3.60B (2025) — a 27% decline driven entirely by volume compression. Wholesale shipments dropped from 180K (2023) to 124K (2025), a 31% cumulative decline. Q1 2026 continued the trend at -12% YoY ($1.17B).

**Margins Collapsed:** HDMC operating margin deteriorated from 13.6% (2023) to -0.8% (2025). The fixed-cost manufacturing base in the U.S. creates severe negative operating leverage on volume declines. Every 10K units of lost production disproportionately impacts profitability.

**Earnings Profile:**
- FY2025 EPS: $2.78 (inflated by $191M one-time HDFS reserve release)
- Q4 2025 EPS: -$2.44 (massive miss, -131.7% surprise)
- Q1 2026 EPS: $0.22 (miss vs. $0.27 estimate)
- FY2026E consensus: $0.34; FY2027E: $1.72

The forward P/E of ~12.3x (profile data) appears to use a trailing figure. On consensus FY2026 EPS of $0.34, the actual forward P/E is ~70x. On FY2027 estimates of $1.72, it's ~14x — still requiring meaningful execution on the turnaround plan.

**Balance Sheet Improvement:** The HDFS transaction reduced debt from $6.96B to $2.97B. Cash rose to $3.09B. Total liquidity stands at $4.3B. The balance sheet is no longer a concern — this company is not going bankrupt. But cash on the balance sheet doesn't create earnings growth absent productive deployment.

**Cash Flow:** FCF was $415M in 2025 (11.5% margin), declining from $1.06B operating cash flow in 2024 due to lower volumes. Share repurchases are paused pending strategic clarity.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term (2026):** Consensus expects essentially breakeven earnings ($0.34 EPS). Revenue guidance implies continued mid-single-digit wholesale declines partially offset by pricing. The Q1 retail inflection (+16% U.S.) is the single most bullish data point, but:
- It compares to a -13% decline in 2025 and -4% in 2024
- It may reflect pent-up demand from destocking rather than sustainable growth
- Wholesale (which drives manufacturing profitability) didn't inflect — shipments were still down

**Medium-Term (2027+):** The strategic plan targets >$350M HDMC EBITDA by 2027 (vs. approximately breakeven in 2025-2026). This requires:
- Mid-single-digit retail unit growth CAGR
- Gross margin improvement to 25-30% (from ~25% currently)
- OpEx below 20% of sales
- Sprint launch success (late 2026)
- Sportster launch success (2027)

These are plausible targets if executed well, but the track record is poor: Harley has announced multiple strategic pivots over the past decade (Rewire, Hardwire) without arresting the fundamental volume decline.

**Estimate Revisions — Negative:** Current quarter estimates fell 21.6% over 90 days. Next quarter fell 37.8%. FY2026 fell 83.1% over 90 days. The 30-day uptick in annual estimates (+23.3% for FY2026, +11.6% for FY2027) appears to reflect the Back to the Bricks announcement and mild stabilization of the outlook, but from drastically reduced levels.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.84, HOG trades at:
- ~0.83x tangible book value ($2.6B market cap / $3.14B equity)
- ~70x FY2026 consensus EPS ($0.34)
- ~14x FY2027 consensus EPS ($1.72)
- ~6.3x trailing FCF ($415M)
- P/E peer median: 22.3x

The below-book valuation superficially suggests cheapness, but the motorcycle business itself is barely profitable. Cash on the balance sheet essentially equals the market cap, meaning the market assigns minimal value to the going-concern motorcycle operations. This is defensible given that HDMC lost money in 2025 and is guided for breakeven in 2026.

The FY2027 P/E of 14x would be attractive if the $1.72 EPS materializes. That requires $350M+ HDMC EBITDA (the strategic plan target) plus continued HDFS contribution. Execution risk is substantial. Analyst consensus mean target of $23.95 (essentially flat) confirms limited near-term upside expectation.

The valuation is not expensive on an asset basis, but it's not clearly mispriced either — the market is correctly pricing a turnaround that has yet to prove itself.

## Competitive Landscape

Harley-Davidson's competitive position is deteriorating:
- U.S. market share lost 2.8 percentage points in one year (37.3% → 34.5%)
- European share declined 1.6 points
- The overall heavyweight motorcycle market is contracting (U.S. -6.1%, Europe -16.4% in 2025)
- Japanese competitors (Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki) benefit from a weak yen
- Indian Motorcycle (Polaris) competes directly in the core heavyweight touring/cruiser segments

**Remaining Strengths:**
- Iconic brand heritage (123 years)
- Exclusive dealer network (multi-brand dealers can't sell Harley, creating loyalty)
- High-margin Parts & Accessories ($614M) and Apparel ($216M) businesses
- Used bike ecosystem creates recurring touchpoints

**Structural Weakness:** The average Harley buyer is aging, and the company has failed to bring in younger riders at accessible price points. The Sprint and Sportster address this but represent a pivot that may dilute the premium brand positioning.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure (Severe):** $45M in Q1 2026 alone, with $75-105M expected for the full year. Steel/aluminum tariffs, Thailand-origin tariffs for Asian distribution, and potential EU rebalancing duties (31% if the suspension expires) create multi-vector cost pressure. The IEEPA constitutional challenge adds policy uncertainty. At breakeven operating income levels, $75-105M in tariffs is the difference between profit and loss.

**Consumer Macro (Elevated):** Harley sells $21K+ discretionary luxury goods into a consumer environment with high-rate headwinds. Motorcycle purchases are among the most deferrable consumer expenditures.

**Execution Risk (High):** New CEO (7 months in), new strategy (announced May 5, 2026), paused buybacks, ongoing layoffs, multiple executive departures. The Back to the Bricks plan has specific targets but zero execution history. Every prior Harley strategic plan failed to arrest volume decline.

**Short Interest (19%):** Institutional bearish positioning is elevated. Days to cover at 4.5 suggests modest but not extreme squeeze risk.

**LiveWire Cash Burn:** ~$70-80M/year with minimal revenue. Management has capped investment but hasn't shut it down.

**Recall Risk:** 88,000 unit recall announced May 2026 (crankcase pressure issue) adds warranty expense.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing significant near-term uncertainty:
- ATM IV of 59.7% vs. 39.9% HV represents a 19.8pp premium — the market expects large moves beyond recent realized volatility
- IV rose 11.4pp (48.3% → 59.7%) over the past month while the stock rose 12.8% — rising vol alongside rising price is unusual and suggests informed positioning for reversal risk
- Put/Call OI ratio of 1.45 (up from 0.96 a month ago) confirms elevated put demand
- Term structure in backwardation (near 59.7% > far 46.6%) indicates the market prices imminent event risk
- 1-month put skew of 7.7% is elevated

Institutional derivatives positioning is distinctly bearish and does not confirm the post-earnings bounce as sustainable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$28-32, +17-34%):** Q1 retail inflection proves durable. Sprint launches on time in late 2026 and expands the addressable market. HDMC achieves $350M+ EBITDA by 2027 as guided. Tariff regime moderates (IEEPA ruling or policy change). Share repurchases resume. Stock re-rates to 16-18x FY2027 EPS of $1.72 = $28-31.

**Bear Case (~$14-17, -29-41%):** Retail inflection was temporary/comparison-driven. Consumer recession materializes. Tariffs persist at full rate ($105M+). Sprint launch delayed or underwhelms. Volume continues declining. HDMC remains unprofitable. Stock trades to 10x trough EPS or further below book on continued cash burn. The $12 analyst low target implies this scenario.

**Base Case (~$22-25, -7% to +5%):** Muddle-through. Retail stabilizes but doesn't accelerate. HDMC achieves breakeven but not meaningful profitability in 2026. Investors wait for 2027 product cadence. Stock remains range-bound as turnaround remains a "show me" story.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Consumer earnings weakness confirming discretionary pullback; Q2 2026 earnings (est. ~August) showing continued HDMC operating losses and tariff drag; potential EU rebalancing tariff reinstatement.

**Timing window:** 2-4 months. The Sprint launch in late 2026 provides a potential positive catalyst that makes longer-duration shorts riskier. The optimal window is now through Q2 earnings (August).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 19% SI with 4.5 days to cover is elevated but below the 25% crowded threshold. Institutional ownership is high. Squeeze risk is moderate — the May 5 earnings day spike (+8.2% intraday before fading) demonstrates the stock can have violent short-covering episodes without fundamentally changing the thesis.

**Borrow and options signal:** The P/C OI ratio of 1.45, backwardation in term structure, and elevated put skew confirm the bearish thesis. IV at 59.7% means put protection is expensive, but the 6-month tenor at 46.6% is more reasonable for swing trades.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $17 (28% downside) vs. bull case requiring execution beyond 2026 ($28-32). From $23.84, downside to $17 = 28.7%, upside risk to $28 = 17.4%. Risk/reward on a short: 1.6:1 — below the 2:1 threshold for active recommendation.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is correct (structural decline, consumer macro headwind, tariff drag) but the risk/reward is insufficient due to (1) below-book valuation providing a floor, (2) $3.1B in cash creating M&A/buyback optionality, (3) 19% SI creating squeeze episodes, and (4) the Back to the Bricks catalyst in late 2026. Avoid owning but don't actively short.

**Timeline:** Current headwinds (tariffs, volume uncertainty, unproven strategy) are expected to persist through at least Q3 2026. The earliest point at which the bull case becomes testable is late 2026 / early 2027 when Sprint ships and FY2027 guidance is issued.

## Conclusion

Harley-Davidson presents classic value trap risk. The below-book valuation and CEO insider buying create optical appeal, but the underlying motorcycle business lost money in 2025, is guided for breakeven in 2026, and faces structural volume decline, tariff headwinds, and consumer macro weakness. The Back to the Bricks strategy is plausible but requires 12-18 months to generate revenue contribution from new products. Analyst estimates have been slashed (83% reduction in FY2026 over 90 days), 3 of the last 4 quarters missed, and the options market is pricing elevated stress.

The score trajectory (4.0 → 4.3 → 4.6) reflects genuine improvement in financial health (balance sheet deleveraging) and reduced near-term bankruptcy risk. But improving from "distressed" to "stable but unprofitable" does not constitute a buy case. AVOID until turnaround execution becomes visible in financial results.
