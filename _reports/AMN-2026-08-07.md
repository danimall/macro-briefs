## Executive Summary

AMN Healthcare's reported first-half 2026 results are close to unusable as a valuation anchor. H1 revenue of $2,051.6M was up 52% year over year, and H1 diluted EPS was $2.11 against a loss of $(3.06) a year earlier. Both figures rest almost entirely on one line: labor-disruption (strike) staffing revenue of $747.2M in H1 2026 versus $54.4M in H1 2025. Because Q2 strike revenue was $25.3M, Q1 carried approximately $722.0M — roughly half of a normal full year of the entire nurse and allied segment, delivered in ninety days. On the August 6 call, the CFO said Q3 guidance embeds only "around $7 million or $8 million" of strike revenue. The sequence is therefore $722M → $25M → $7-8M, and consolidated revenue will fall from roughly $1,378M in Q1 to a guided $640-655M in Q3, a ~53% sequential decline that is mix, not deterioration.

Strip the windfall out and something more interesting appears. Q2 revenue excluding labor disruption was approximately $648.0M against $642.4M, up 0.9% — the first positive underlying comparison in years. Travel nurse revenue grew 9.6%, allied 7.9%, international nurse 23%, and average travelers on assignment rose 6% after falling 14% for full-year 2025. Q3 revenue guidance of $647.5M at the midpoint came in roughly 4% above consensus with almost no strike contribution, and management targets 9-11% nurse and allied growth, which would be the strongest in four years. The windfall's cash also transformed the balance sheet: cash of $361.8M versus $34.0M at year-end, revolver undrawn versus $25.0M, equity $741.3M, and no maturity before the $350M 4.000% notes due April 2029.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than an upgrade to ACCUMULATE. First, valuation is genuinely ambiguous: at $30.80 the enterprise trades near 6.3x normalized EBITDA — reasonable for staffing — but near 30x the $1.04 FY2027 consensus GAAP EPS, and next-year estimates have been cut 4.4% in thirty days even as near-term quarters were raised sharply. Second, the two segments that were supposed to justify a higher multiple are the two still shrinking at the profit line: physician and leadership operating income fell 22% in H1 and technology and workforce fell 29%, with technology gross margin sliding from 58.9% (FY2024) to 52.7% (FY2025) to 48.6% in Q2 2026. Third, roughly $237.2M of the $361.8M cash balance is client deposits against four unreconciled strike events that management expects to settle in Q3 and Q4 with either refunds or incremental invoices — direction unknown and unbounded in the disclosure. The prior AVOID at ~$35 has been earned back to HOLD by a 12% lower price and a better operating print, not by resolution of the core uncertainty.

## Company Overview

AMN is a U.S. healthcare workforce solutions company (Dallas-headquartered) operating three segments. Hospital systems and provider organizations are the paying counterparties; AMN employs or contracts the clinicians.

- **Nurse and allied solutions** — $1,647.3M, 60% of FY2025 revenue. Travel nursing, international nurse staffing and permanent placement, rapid-response/crisis nursing, labor-disruption staffing, local/per-diem, allied health, and revenue cycle solutions.
- **Physician and leadership solutions** — $696.4M, 26%. Locum tenens (CRNAs the largest specialty), interim leadership, executive and academic search, physician permanent placement.
- **Technology and workforce solutions** — $386.7M, 14%. Language services (400+ health systems, 2,700+ hospitals), vendor management systems (ShiftWise Flex, Medefis, b4health), workforce optimization, and RPO.

Approximately 48% of FY2025 revenue flowed through managed services programs, representing about $1.8B of client spend under management, with a further ~$1.4B of vendor-neutral VMS spend, for roughly $3.3B total. Under MSP arrangements AMN books revenue gross when it supplies its own clinicians and net as an agent when it subcontracts associate vendors — a mix effect that can move reported revenue without proportionate gross-profit dollars. In H1 2026 MSP arrangements rose to 65% of consolidated revenue from 46%.

Client concentration is severe and named: Kaiser Foundation Hospitals and affiliates were approximately 22% of FY2025 consolidated revenue and 32% of nurse and allied segment revenue, or roughly $601M. No other client exceeded 5%.

## Financial Analysis

### The contraction that preceded the inflection

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,243.2 | 3,789.3 | 2,983.8 | 2,730.4 |
| Gross margin | 32.7% | 33.0% | 30.8% | 28.3% |
| Operating income | 647.1 | 338.4 | (102.7) | (55.5) |
| Net income | 444 | 211 | (147) | (96) |
| Adjusted EBITDA (co.) | 847 | 583 | 347 | 243 |

Revenue fell 47.9% from FY2022 to FY2025 and adjusted EBITDA fell 71%. FY2025's operating loss was dominated by non-cash items — a $109.5M goodwill impairment in physician and leadership and an $18.3M long-lived asset impairment, against a $39.1M gain on the Smart Square divestiture. Cumulative accumulated goodwill impairment stood at $546.9M at 6/30/2026, split $277.7M nurse and allied and $269.2M physician and leadership. The technology and workforce unit has never been written down and carries $371.6M of goodwill.

FY2025 margin compression was broad: nurse and allied 23.0% (from 24.5%), physician and leadership 27.6% (from 29.7%), technology and workforce 52.7% (from 58.9%). The last of these matters most, because that segment was the stated path to recurring, less cyclical revenue. Management attributed the decline to language-services pricing pressure from increased competition, adverse mix from shrinking VMS, and the Smart Square sale.

### H1 2026: what changed and what did not

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 673.2 | 658.2 | 2,051.6 | 1,347.7 |
| Gross margin | 30.6% | 29.8% | 28.0% | 29.3% |
| Operating income | 26.9 | (123.7) | 144.1 | (111.2) |
| Net income | 21.2 | (116.2) | 83.3 | (117.3) |

Segment operating income less corporate overhead — the cleanest available EBITDA proxy — was $70.9M in Q2 2026 versus $52.8M, and $233.6M in H1 versus $114.5M. Q1 alone therefore contributed roughly $162.7M, against a rebuilt FY2025 full-year figure of about $220.4M. (AMN's own FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was $243M; the ~$23M definitional gap is unreconciled, so treat all EBITDA multiples here with roughly ±10% definitional uncertainty.)

Two quality caveats on Q2. Nurse and allied gross margin jumped to 28.4% from 23.9%, and management attributed the improvement to reserve releases and billing true-ups from prior-period strike events, quantified at +$10.0M of revenue. That is a prior-period estimate revision landing in current-quarter margin. The CFO separately said underlying segment gross margins showed no significant change. A cleaner Q2 run-rate is therefore roughly $60.9M of segment operating income less corporate, or ~$244M annualized.

Two segments are still going backwards at the profit line: physician and leadership operating income fell to $21.9M from $27.9M in H1 (-22%), and technology and workforce to $49.9M from $70.5M (-29%). Within technology, VMS revenue fell 20.3% in Q2 after -31% in FY2025 (partly outright client losses), and language services fell 8.1% on lower pricing. Locum tenens revenue fell 8.3% on 8% fewer days filled.

### Cash flow and balance sheet — improved, and partly borrowed

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $372.5M versus $171.2M. The composition is critical: the 10-Q attributes the increase to +$95.5M of net income excluding non-cash items, +$91.2M from client deposits and related reserves, +$37.9M of payables timing, and +$33.2M of accrued compensation tied to labor-disruption services. The cash flow statement shows a +$143.2M change in other liabilities. Netting that out, H1 FCF normalizes to roughly $213.5M ($372.5M − $15.8M capex − $143.2M other-liabilities build). Neither figure should be annualized.

Balance sheet at 6/30/2026 versus 12/31/2025: cash $361.8M (from $34.0M); revolver $0 drawn (from $25.0M) with $430.0M available on a $450.0M facility; notes payable net $742.9M (face $750M — $350M 4.000% due 2029, $400M 6.500% due 2031); equity $741.3M (from $642.1M); current ratio 1.13 (from 0.94). **Client deposits and reserves rose to $237.2M from $97.3M.**

Net debt at face is $388.2M. On TTM segment-operating-income-less-corporate of roughly $339.5M that is ~1.1x, against a covenant ceiling of 5.25x. But if the entire deposit balance were refunded rather than earned, adjusted net debt would be closer to $625.4M and TTM EBITDA materially lower. The covenant history reinforces caution: the credit agreement was amended twice in thirteen months, the October 2025 Fifth Amendment reset net leverage to ≤5.25x through March 2027, extended maturity to October 2030, and **cut revolver commitments from $750M to $450M**. AMN also paid up roughly 190bp on the refinanced tranche (retiring $500M of 4.625% 2027 notes with $400M of 6.500% 2031 notes), pushing the maturity wall out four years while raising annual notes coupon cost from ~$37.1M to ~$40.0M on $100M less principal.

Capital allocation is the weakest strand. Against $361.8M of cash and $224.4M of remaining buyback authorization, AMN repurchased 85,487 shares at an average $26.33 (~$2.3M), all in May 2026. Capex fell from $103.7M (2023) to $80.9M (2024) to $35.6M (2025), with H1 2026 at $15.8M — a 66% two-year reduction at a company whose strategy and risk factors both center on technology investment. FY2025's headline 18.4% trailing FCF yield was substantially manufactured by that capex cut plus a $71.6M receivables release driven by declining revenue. Neither source recurs in a growth scenario.

## Growth Analysis

The honest decomposition is that AMN has one genuine growth engine, one episodic windfall business, and two shrinking franchises.

**Genuine growth.** Core nurse staffing volumes have turned. Average travelers on assignment rose 6% year over year in Q2 2026 against a 14% decline for full-year 2025. Travel nurse revenue $228.0M (+9.6%), allied $157.2M (+7.9%), international nurse $39.9M (+23%) after nine consecutive quarters of decline. Q3 guidance of $640-655M with only $7-8M of strike revenue, and a 9-11% nurse and allied growth target, is the strongest forward signal in this file. It arrived roughly 4% above consensus.

**Episodic.** Labor-disruption staffing is real and differentiated. The Event Management System platform, expanded in 2025 to orchestrate external suppliers alongside AMN clinicians, produced $722M in a single quarter. Industry reporting counted 76,000 healthcare workers involved in 2026's five largest strikes, including Kaiser Permanente, New York City hospitals, and Mass General Brigham — where more than 4,000 Brigham and Women's nurses plus ~450 home-health clinicians struck on July 8, 2026, followed by a four-day lockout. Management said client requests for labor-disruption support increased in 2025 and expects the trend to continue. But the revenue is unforecastable, and the Q3 guide of $7-8M sits oddly against a July event of that scale, which either means AMN was not a material supplier, or guidance excludes revenue it cannot yet estimate.

**Shrinking.** Locum tenens days filled -8%, interim leadership -19% in FY2025 on client insourcing and deferred hiring, VMS -20% on utilization and client losses, language services -8% on price. The technology and workforce segment declined 12% year over year in FY2025 and a further 15% year over year in Q2 2026 with gross margin down 1,020bp from FY2024.

**Estimate revisions cut both ways, and the split is the tell.** Current-quarter consensus rose 46.7% in thirty days and 35.7% in ninety; next-quarter rose 28.6%. Current-year rose 4.1%. But **next-year consensus fell 4.4% in thirty days and 2.8% in ninety, to $1.04**. Near-term revisions are being driven by strike true-ups and the Q3 beat-and-raise; the durable year is being marked down. This is not the inflection pattern where rising estimates and cheap forward multiples reinforce each other — it is a windfall being recognized while normalized earnings power is trimmed.

Earnings surprises confirm low model visibility rather than sustained beat capacity: Q2 non-GAAP $0.77 versus $0.19 (+304%), Q1 $2.10 versus $1.63 (+29%), Q4 2025 $0.22 versus $0.28 (-21.5%), Q3 2025 $0.39 versus $0.20 (+93%). A 4x consensus beat is evidence that sell-side models cannot anticipate reserve releases, in either direction.

## Valuation Assessment

All arithmetic uses the frozen $30.80 basis. (The extended-hours print of $32.80 and the $32.90 quote embedded in the profile snapshot are diagnostics; the $32.90 figure implies a $1,276M cap versus $1,193M at $30.80.)

- Shares outstanding 6/30/2026: 38.716M; Q2 diluted weighted average 39.732M.
- Market cap: 38.716M × $30.80 = **$1,192.5M**.
- Net debt (face): $750.0M − $361.8M = **$388.2M**. EV = **$1,580.7M**.
- Deposit-adjusted net debt: $625.4M → EV **$1,817.9M**.

**On EBITDA, AMN looks reasonable.** Normalized EBITDA of ~$244-260M (Q2's $70.9M less the ~$10M reserve release, annualized, cross-checked against Q3 guidance at a ~10% margin) implies **6.1-6.5x EV/EBITDA**, or 7.0-7.5x on deposit-adjusted net debt. Staffing comparables historically transact and trade in the 7-10x range; Cross Country Healthcare was acquired at the trough of the cycle. Trailing EV/segment-operating-income-less-corporate of 4.7x flatters materially because of Q1.

**On earnings, AMN looks expensive.** FY2027 consensus GAAP EPS of $1.04 puts the stock at **29.6x**, against a peer median P/E of 17.2x (ADUS 17.2x, MD 11.7x, PGNY 33.6x, with SDGR and NEO loss-making). My own bridge lands near consensus: normalized EBITDA $250M less D&A of roughly $155-165M (heavy acquisition-intangible amortization) less ~$33M net interest, taxed at 26%, gives GAAP EPS near $1.05-1.15. FY2026 consensus of $2.77 is 11.1x, but that figure is strike-inflated and internally inconsistent with H1 non-GAAP EPS of $2.87 already booked — a basis mismatch worth noting rather than resolving.

The gap between 6.3x EBITDA and 30x earnings is the whole valuation question. It is created by $750M of debt and large non-cash intangible amortization. Normalized free cash flow — EBITDA ~$250M less normalized capex (I assume $50-60M, meaningfully above the depressed $35.6M), less $33M interest, less ~$25M cash taxes, less working-capital consumption in a growth year — lands near $110-130M, a **9-11% FCF yield**. That is supportive but not compelling for a business where two of three segments are contracting and one revenue line is unforecastable.

Analyst consensus is clustered at fair value: mean target $32.43, median $32.00, high $40, low $26, with a 1/2/5/0/1 distribution across strong buy through strong sell. On the stated framework, analyst targets act as floors only for cheap, accelerating names; here the out-year estimate is falling, so the targets are better read as a genuine fair-value consensus.

Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument — the stock's 79% twelve-month gain and 107% rise from the January low of $14.87 reflect real fundamental improvement (volume turn, balance sheet repair, strike windfall). But at 30x normalized GAAP earnings and ~6.3x EBITDA with falling out-year estimates, there is no identifiable mispricing in either direction large enough to demand action. Valuation scores 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors include Aya Healthcare, CHG, Cross Country, HealthTrust, Ingenovis, Jackson Healthcare, Medical Solutions, Amergis, LanguageLine, and — most importantly — hospital systems' own internal travel agencies and float pools.

**Where the moat is real.** Mobilizing thousands of credentialed replacement clinicians into a struck facility within days requires credentialing infrastructure, travel and housing logistics, supplier orchestration, and working capital to prefund clinician pay. AMN built a dedicated platform for it and expanded it in 2025. The $722M single quarter is the evidence. MSP scale ($1.8B of spend under management, 48% of revenue) and the AMN Passport app (340,000+ registered users) also carry switching cost.

**Where it is eroding.** Precisely in the segment AMN designated as its strategic future. VMS is losing clients outright. Language services is losing price to competition. The segment's gross margin has fallen 1,020bp in six quarters and its operating income is down 29% year over year. AMN sold Smart Square. Meanwhile the FY2025 10-K's lead risk factor is clients improving their own recruiting through analytics, automation, machine learning and internal travel programs — and MD&A confirms it is already happening in interim leadership and search. The secular demand statistics AMN cites (86,000 physician shortfall by 2036, 4.5M global nursing gap by 2030) coexisted with a 48% revenue collapse, because the binding constraint is hospital willingness to pay premium contingent-labor rates, not clinician scarcity.

Net trajectory: improving in commoditized staffing, deteriorating in the higher-margin technology franchise. Competitive position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Kaiser concentration (22% of FY2025 revenue, 32% of nurse and allied).** The 10-K attaches material-adverse-effect language. The H1 2026 mix shift moved nurse and allied to 76% of consolidated revenue from 59% and MSP to 65% from 46%, which *increases* exposure to the segment where Kaiser dominates. There is no FY2026 concentration disclosure. Industry reporting names Kaiser among 2026's largest strikes and AMN provides Kaiser clinical managed services, but AMN does not identify strike clients — any attribution of the windfall to Kaiser is inference, not fact.

**Unreconciled strike revenue.** Five events completed, one settled, four in reconciliation. Revenue reflects management estimates of volume, hours, rates and reimbursable costs; on completion, actual amounts reconcile against deposits received and "may result in a refund from contract liabilities or an incremental invoice." Reconciliation packages were delivered in Q2; settlement is expected in Q3 and Q4. Client deposits stand at $237.2M. Q2's +$10.0M favorable true-up demonstrates these estimates move. The disclosure is more forthcoming than required and there is no indication of aggressive accounting — ASC 606 requires exactly this treatment, and prefunding is the normal commercial structure for emergency strike staffing. The problem is forecastability, for management as much as for investors.

**Technology segment goodwill.** $371.6M with zero accumulated impairment, in the fastest-declining unit with the highest disclosed sensitivity (a 50bp WACC change moves excess fair value over carrying value ~23%; a 50bp revenue-growth change moves it ~8%). Physician and leadership shows a 1% growth sensitivity after two write-downs totaling $208.7M — minimal headroom against a unit whose H1 operating income fell 22%.

**Litigation and insurance.** AMN self-insures via a captive but is uninsured for wage-and-hour and competition/antitrust claims, with California and federal wage-and-hour actions identified as the most significant accrued matters. Professional liability expense escalated $5.5M (2023) → $7.8M (2024) → $18.9M (2025), including a $10.5M unfavorable actuarial adjustment; the accrual is $53.9M.

**Other specific exposures.** Locum tenens independent-contractor classification (with corporate-practice-of-medicine prohibitions in some states) against $130.8M of Q2 revenue; visa retrogression on international nursing and a second-order immigration-enforcement channel into language services demand; ACA/Medicaid policy erosion, which MD&A confirms already caused clients to adopt a conservative stance in Q2 2025.

**Screening artifacts.** Two mechanical traps: the SEC XBRL "net income" field for AMN returns pre-tax income (FY2025 $(101.1)M versus the actual $(95.7)M net loss), and the Altman Z of 1.89 with a 0.94 current ratio is computed off the 12/31/2025 balance sheet, two quarters stale to the downside. Recomputed correctly, FY2025 ROE was −14.2% and ROA −4.2%. The Q2 liquidity improvement is real but partly funded by the $237.2M deposit liability, so the current-ratio improvement is less robust than it appears.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress and it does not corroborate a constructive view. Near-term ATM IV is 99.1% against 63.5% one-year realized (35.7pp rich, 82nd percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (99.1% at 13 days versus 62.6% at six months), and the nearest expiry implies a ±16.1% move. Put/call skew of 22.3% is flagged as elevated put demand at the front tenor, while the three- and six-month tenors read −10.4% and −2.3%. Since the July 20 snapshot, ATM IV rose 26.2pp and the put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.29 to 1.18 as the stock fell 12%. Short interest is 11.1% of float at 3.5 days to cover.

Front-month vol staying near 99% *after* the August 6 print is the notable part. The most plausible explanation is positioning around the Q3/Q4 reconciliation settlements and the $237.2M deposit balance, which is precisely the unquantifiable exposure identified above. Elevated near-term vol also means the market is paying for optionality here — writing covered calls against existing positions is better compensated than usual, and buying premium is expensive.

Risk factors score 3.5: multiple specific, partly binary exposures, at least two of which (reconciliation direction, technology goodwill) are not plausibly priced into a 6x EBITDA multiple.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $45 (+46%)

The core nurse business inflection is real and early: travelers on assignment +6%, travel nurse +9.6%, international +23%, and Q3 nurse and allied guided to 9-11% growth after three years of contraction. Bill rates have stabilized and hospital contingent-labor budgets are normalizing. The labor-disruption franchise proves recurrent rather than one-off — 76,000 workers were involved in 2026's five largest strikes, management says client requests are rising, and the EMS platform is hard to replicate, so periodic $200-700M revenue events recur every few years and each one recapitalizes the balance sheet. Reconciliations settle favorably, as Q2's +$10.0M true-up hints. Technology and workforce stabilizes as VMS churn annualizes and language pricing finds a floor. Normalized EBITDA reaches $290-300M; at 7.5x, EV of ~$2,175M less ~$370M net debt gives ~$1,805M of equity, and AMN deploys the $224.4M remaining authorization to shrink the share count. $45 sits above the $40 street high because the street does not model repeat windfalls or aggressive repurchase.

### Bear Case — $18 (−42%)

Reconciliation of the four unsettled events produces net refunds, reversing revenue and margin recognized in H1 and draining cash toward the deposit liability. Q3 and Q4 make the underlying earnings power visible at ~$0.25-0.27 per quarter, and the market re-anchors on ~$1.00-1.10 of GAAP EPS rather than the $2.11 H1 print. Technology and workforce keeps declining mid-teens with margin compression, triggering the first impairment of its $371.6M goodwill. Kaiser repricing or partial loss — 22% of revenue, more concentrated after the mix shift — becomes a live issue. Client insourcing and AI-enabled self-service continue to erode interim leadership, search, and locum tenens. Normalized EBITDA falls toward $210M; at 5.5x with deposit-adjusted net debt near $480M, equity is worth ~$675M, or ~$17. The stock traded at $14.87 in January 2026, so this range is not hypothetical.

### Base Case — $32 (+4%)

Nurse and allied grows high single digits through 2027, offset by continued declines in locum tenens, VMS, and language services; consolidated FY2027 revenue lands near $2.6-2.7B with adjusted EBITDA of ~$255M. Reconciliations settle roughly neutral. At 6.5x, EV of $1,658M less $388M net debt gives $1,270M of equity on ~39.7M diluted shares, or $32.00 — which happens to match the analyst median exactly. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected value near $31.75, roughly +3% from $30.80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Expected return is approximately fair with unusually wide two-sided dispersion, and the single largest swing factor — the direction of four strike reconciliations — is unknowable to management as well as to investors until Q3 and Q4 settlements. Existing holders have adequate compensation to wait; new capital has no urgent entry signal and better entry arithmetic below $26.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 and Q4 reconciliation settlements complete at or above recognized amounts, with the client-deposit balance falling toward zero without a revenue reversal. This alone converts $237.2M of ambiguous liquidity into unencumbered cash and would justify the 6.3x EBITDA multiple.
2. Two consecutive quarters of nurse and allied revenue growth at or above 9% *with* segment gross margin at or above 24% excluding prior-period true-ups — i.e., growth on unit economics rather than reserve releases.
3. Technology and workforce revenue decline moderating to low single digits with gross margin stabilizing at or above 48%, which would remove the most likely location of the next goodwill write-down.
4. Meaningful buyback execution — $75M+ deployed against the $224.4M authorization — or clustered open-market insider purchases, neither of which is present today.
5. A price near $26 (the street low) on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock at ~5.4x normalized EBITDA and ~25x FY2027 consensus with the bear case only ~30% below.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any disclosed net refund on the strike reconciliations, or a Q3/Q4 revenue reversal attributable to prior-period true-ups.
2. Impairment of any portion of the $371.6M technology and workforce goodwill, or a further 200bp+ decline in that segment's gross margin.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $0.90 (currently $1.04, down 4.4% in thirty days).
4. Any disclosure indicating change in the Kaiser relationship — contract renegotiation, scope reduction, or a shift toward subcontracted (net-revenue) fill.
5. Renewed revolver drawing or a third credit-agreement amendment.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report (early November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint. It should reveal (a) actual revenue against the $640-655M guide with minimal strike contribution, (b) the first tranche of reconciliation settlements, (c) whether nurse and allied hit the 9-11% growth target, and (d) whether the deposit balance is unwinding. A second checkpoint is the Q4/FY2026 report in February 2027, which brings the annual goodwill test results and initial FY2027 guidance.

## Conclusion

AMN at $30.80 is a business whose reported numbers and underlying economics have temporarily diverged by a factor of two. The strike windfall was real, generated real cash, and repaired a balance sheet that had absorbed a 48% revenue collapse and two covenant amendments in thirteen months. The core nurse staffing business is genuinely growing again for the first time since 2022, and Q3 guidance above consensus with almost no strike revenue is the cleanest evidence available that the underlying turn is not an accounting artifact.

What prevents a constructive rating is that the valuation supports neither conviction. At 6.3x normalized EBITDA and a 9-11% normalized FCF yield, AMN is not expensive.
