## Executive Summary

National Presto Industries has transformed from a diversified housewares/defense manufacturer into a defense-first company, with the Defense segment now representing 81% of revenue (up from 71% in 2024). The company's position as sole prime contractor for the U.S. Army's 40mm ammunition system has generated a $1.75 billion defense backlog, exceeding the company's $1.03 billion market cap by 70%. Revenue grew 30% in FY2025, driven by 43% growth in Defense, and the multi-year demand tailwind from NATO restocking, Ukraine assistance, and U.S. military readiness spending is structural.

The problem is execution and near-term financial strain. Despite 30% revenue growth, consolidated gross margins compressed from 20.2% to 16.0%, net income fell 20%, and operating cash flow was negative for the second consecutive year (-$9.1M). Cash reserves have collapsed from $88M to $3.3M over two years as inventory ballooned to $307M. The housewares segment saw gross margins crater from 25% to 8% under tariff pressure. At ~31x trailing earnings, the stock has already rallied 70% over the past year and prices in a substantial portion of the backlog conversion thesis. The risk-reward at current levels is balanced, warranting a HOLD until cash flow inflects positive or a pullback creates a more favorable entry point.

## Company Overview

National Presto Industries operates three segments from its Eau Claire, Wisconsin headquarters with approximately 1,200 employees:

**Defense (~81% of revenue, $406M):** Through subsidiaries AMTEC Corporation, Spectra Technologies, Amron, Tech Ord, and Woodlawn Manufacturing, NPK manufactures 40mm ammunition, precision mechanical/electro-mechanical assemblies, medium caliber cartridge cases, detonators, and related military ordnance. AMTEC has been the sole prime contractor for the U.S. Army's 40mm ammunition system through four successive five-year contracts dating back to 2005. Manufacturing spans five facilities across Wisconsin, Arkansas, South Dakota, and Texas.

**Housewares (~19% of revenue, $96M):** Designs and distributes small kitchen appliances under the Presto® brand — pressure cookers, griddles, deep fryers, and related products. Products are primarily sourced from China and sold through U.S. retailers.

**Safety (<0.4% of revenue, $2M):** A startup segment offering smoke/CO alarms and PFAS-Free Foam fire extinguishers. Operates at a loss and has been narrowed through divestitures of Rusoh (2023) and OneEvent's refrigeration business (2025).

### Profitability — Volume Without Margin

The central tension in NPK's financials is the disconnect between revenue growth and profitability:

- Consolidated gross margin fell from 20.2% to 16.0% despite 30% revenue growth
- Gross profit grew only 2% ($78.6M to $80.3M) on $116M of incremental revenue
- Operating income declined 11% to $40.2M; net income fell 20% to $33.1M
- Defense gross margins declined modestly from 21% to 19%
- Housewares gross margins collapsed from 25% to 8%, driven by tariffs on Chinese imports and $1.3M in distribution center relocation costs

The Housewares margin collapse is not a one-quarter issue. Tariffs on Chinese goods took effect in early 2025, and the majority of Housewares products are sourced from China with no disclosed alternative supply chain. The 10-K explicitly states that increased tariffs "would have a material adverse effect on the Company's business." This has already materialized.

Two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow despite profitable operations is a structural concern. The root cause is a $117M inventory build over two years, driven by the defense production ramp. CapEx tripled to $27M for capacity expansion. The company drew $23.6M on its revolving credit facility for the first time, incurring $832K in interest expense (versus $2K in 2024).

Management's thesis is that this is transient: as the $1.75B backlog converts to deliveries over the next 18-42 months, working capital should reverse and cash generation should resume. This is plausible but unproven. The company eliminated its extra dividend in 2025 (paid $1.00/share regular only, versus $4.50 total in 2024), confirming that management recognizes the cash constraint.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet remains sound in absolute terms: stockholders' equity of $395M, current ratio of 4.2x, and debt/equity of 0.27. However, the trajectory is deteriorating rapidly. Inventory at $307M represents 61% of total assets and 76% of current assets. Purchase obligations of approximately $603M for defense materials represent additional committed cash outflows. Whether the $23.6M revolver draw and remaining liquidity are sufficient to fund the continued defense ramp without requiring additional financing is the key balance sheet question.

## Growth Analysis

NPK's growth story rests on converting its $1.75B defense backlog into revenue and, critically, into cash. The backlog grew 61% YoY from $1.09B, driven by continued awards under the FY22-26 40mm system contract (awards to date ~$965M against a $1.413B ceiling, with one award year remaining) and the M918E2 TP-DNT IDIQ contract ($256M awarded against an $818.9M ceiling). A third delivery order of $119.8M was announced in March 2026.

The defense demand tailwind is structural. U.S. military readiness spending, NATO restocking, and Ukraine security assistance create multi-year visibility. The 40mm ammunition system is a consumable product — rounds are expended in training and combat, creating recurring demand qualitatively different from one-time equipment procurement.

At current Defense run rates (~$406M/year) and assuming the backlog converts over 3-4 years with continued new awards, Defense revenue could grow to $500-600M annually. The critical unknown is margin recovery: if Defense gross margins stabilize at 19% and improve toward 21-22% with scale, consolidated profitability could improve materially even if Housewares remains impaired.

Housewares offers no growth catalyst. The segment is shrinking, tariff-impaired, and operating in a competitive, commoditized market where NPK has lost its largest customer above the 10% threshold. The Safety segment is negligible and management has acknowledged it may not ultimately succeed.

Earnings per share trajectory is highly sensitive to margin assumptions. At $600M in revenue with a blended 17% gross margin and current SG&A levels, EPS could reach $6-7. At 20% gross margin, EPS could approach $9-10. The wide range reflects genuine uncertainty about whether scale will restore margins or whether fixed-price contract dynamics and tariff headwinds persist.

## Valuation Assessment

NPK trades at approximately 31x trailing EPS of $4.63 on a market cap of $1.03B. For a company with declining earnings (-20% YoY), negative free cash flow, and a 0.7% dividend yield, this multiple is elevated.

The bull case for valuation rests on forward earnings power. If Defense revenue reaches $550M at 20% gross margins, with Housewares stabilizing at $90M and 12% margins, consolidated gross profit could reach $121M. After SG&A of ~$40M, operating income would be ~$81M, and net income could approach $62M, or roughly $8.65/share. At 20x forward earnings — reasonable for a defense contractor with sole-source positioning — that implies a $173 fair value, 21% above current levels.

The bear case: current margins persist (16% gross), tariffs worsen, and Defense execution lags. At $504M revenue and 16% gross margin, the company is generating $33M in net income ($4.63 EPS). At 20x earnings, that's $93 — 35% below current price.

The market appears to be pricing a scenario between base and bull, which is reasonable given the backlog visibility but leaves limited margin of safety. At 31x trailing earnings with negative FCF, the stock is priced for execution that has yet to be demonstrated.

Comparable defense contractors trade at wide ranges: large-caps (LMT, RTX, NOC) at 17-25x forward earnings with proven cash generation. Small defense pure-plays with sole-source positions and large backlogs relative to market cap can trade at premiums, but 31x on declining earnings with negative cash flow is stretched. The backlog provides the premium justification; the cash flow strain limits how much premium is warranted.

## Competitive Landscape

NPK's Defense segment occupies one of the most defensible competitive positions available to a sub-$2B market cap company. As sole prime contractor for the U.S. Army's 40mm ammunition system across four consecutive five-year contracts since 2005, AMTEC has deep institutional relationships, specialized manufacturing capabilities across five facilities, and regulatory/security clearances that create exceptionally high barriers to entry.

The 40mm system is a workhorse platform used across NATO for training and combat. Demand is structural and recurring. The escalation in contract ceilings from ~$540M (FY17-21) to $1.413B (FY22-26) reflects both price increases and genuine demand growth. The M918E2 IDIQ contract ($818.9M ceiling) represents an additional growth vector.

The Housewares segment is competitively weak and deteriorating. The 10-K notes industry consolidation favoring larger players. Presto® competes against Instant Brands, Hamilton Beach, and numerous Asian manufacturers. With products sourced from China, tariff exposure is a permanent competitive disadvantage versus domestically manufactured alternatives or competitors with diversified supply chains. Amazon falling below the 10% customer threshold and a vendor bankruptcy highlight structural fragility.

On a consolidated basis, the competitive trajectory is positive: the growing, defensible Defense segment is overwhelming the shrinking, vulnerable Housewares segment.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Cash Flow Conversion (HIGH)
If Defense delivery timelines extend or production costs increase under fixed-price contracts, the cash crunch detailed in the Financial Analysis section could intensify, potentially requiring additional debt or dilutive financing beyond the current $23.6M revolver draw.

### 2. Tariff Impact on Housewares (HIGH — ALREADY REALIZED)
With no disclosed supply chain diversification away from China and no long-term retailer agreements, this segment could approach break-even or loss territory. Under LIFO accounting, tariff costs are expensed immediately.

### 3. Fixed-Price Contract Risk (MODERATE)
Defense contracts are almost entirely fixed-price with limited escalation clauses (only steel, aluminum, zinc). In the current inflationary environment — particularly for energy, labor, and specialty materials — margin erosion on the backlog could be substantial if costs exceed estimates.

### 4. Government Concentration (MODERATE)
Virtually all Defense revenue comes from the DOD. Contracts can be terminated for convenience at any time. While the dual-theater environment and global ammunition demand reduce the near-term probability of contract cancellation, any shift in government procurement priorities would be material.

### 5. Cybersecurity Incident (MODERATE — MONITORING)
The March 2025 cybersecurity incident at a defense ordnance manufacturer is potentially serious. CMMC requirements are tightening, and a material breach could affect future contract eligibility. Remediation costs and operational impacts were not fully disclosed.

### 6. Executive Change (UNKNOWN)
The March 4, 2026 8-K filing disclosed a departure or election of directors/officers. The specific individual and role were not identified in available data. If this relates to CEO MaryJo Cohen (who owns 3.2% of the company), it would be a significant negative.

### 7. Insider Selling
CFO David Peuse sold 500 shares (more than half his position) at $140 in March 2026. Director Randy Lieble sold 2,417 shares (~32% of his holdings) at $144. No insider buying was observed. The sales are modest in dollar terms but notable given the small executive ownership stakes outside the CEO.

### 8. Minimal Analyst Coverage
Only one analyst rating was identified (Hold at $141). Micro-cap status and limited institutional coverage create information asymmetry, which cuts both ways — mispricing is possible, but liquidity and institutional support during drawdowns may be limited.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$170-180, +19-26%)
Defense revenue accelerates to $550-600M as the backlog converts. Gross margins recover to 20%+ with scale benefits. Working capital reverses as inventory converts to deliveries, generating substantial free cash flow. Extra dividend is reinstated. The market re-rates NPK as a defense pure-play at 22-25x normalized earnings of $7-8/share. Tariff relief or supply chain diversification stabilizes Housewares. New contract awards extend the backlog beyond current visibility.

### Bear Case (~$90-95, -33-37%)
Defense margins continue compressing under fixed-price contracts in an inflationary environment. The cash flow crunch intensifies, requiring additional debt or dilutive financing. Tariffs persist, driving Housewares to breakeven. The cybersecurity incident triggers regulatory consequences affecting contract eligibility. Backlog conversion takes longer than expected, with delivery delays eroding margin assumptions. At 15x normalized earnings of $6/share, the stock trades to $90.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**

2. Stock pulls back to $115-120 (approximately 20-22x estimated FY2027 EPS of $5.50-6.00), creating a more favorable risk-reward
3. Defense gross margins stabilize at or above 20% for two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Operating cash flow remains negative through Q2 FY2026, indicating the defense ramp is consuming more cash than expected
2. Additional short-term borrowing exceeds $50M, signaling liquidity strain
3. Loss of the sole-source 40mm contract or failure to win the next FY27+ contract cycle

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report (expected June 2026). Focus on: (a) operating cash flow sign, (b) inventory trajectory, (c) Defense gross margin, (d) clarification on the March 2026 executive change 8-K.

## Conclusion

National Presto Industries has a compelling long-term transformation underway: a sole-source defense contractor with structural demand tailwinds from global military spending and a management team with meaningful insider ownership and lean compensation. The defense positioning is genuinely enviable for a company of this size.

The difficulty is timing and price. At $143 and ~31x trailing earnings, the stock has already appreciated 70% in 12 months and prices in a significant portion of the backlog conversion thesis. Near-term risks from cash flow strain, the tariff-impaired Housewares segment, the cybersecurity incident, and unidentified executive changes are meaningful. Available earnings surprise data is limited and dated, providing minimal forward visibility, and the lack of analyst coverage compounds the uncertainty.

The appropriate stance is HOLD. The defense thesis has multi-year duration and the backlog provides genuine growth visibility, but the stock needs either a price correction or demonstrated cash flow inflection to justify adding risk at current levels. Monitor quarterly cash flow, defense margins, and the executive change disclosure as the primary decision inputs.
