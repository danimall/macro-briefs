**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MGIC Investment Corporation is one of the six legacy U.S. private mortgage insurers, providing default protection on low-down-payment residential mortgages, almost entirely GSE-eligible business. The investment case is a capital-return compounder: net income has been stable in the $700–865M range since 2021 (FY2025 $738M), but per-share book value and earnings are rising because management has retired roughly 30% of the share count since 2022, from ~305.8M shares to 213.2M as of 3/31/2026. A fresh $750M repurchase authorization (April 2026, ~13% of market cap) and a $400M subsidiary-to-holding-company dividend confirm the program continues. The balance sheet is over-capitalized: PMIERs Available Assets of $5.8B against a $2.9B minimum requirement, a ~100% excess cushion, and risk-to-capital of 9.6:1 versus a 25:1 regulatory ceiling.

The fundamental signal to weigh against this is the inflecting loss cycle. The Q1 2026 loss ratio jumped to 14.1% from 3.9% a year earlier, a 247% rise in losses incurred to $33.2M. Two forces drove it: more current-year new-delinquency notices, and a shrinking favorable prior-year reserve-development tailwind (down to $(31.2)M from $(49.9)M). The era of near-zero net losses, driven by home-price appreciation letting delinquent borrowers cure via sale, is moderating, and management is explicitly guiding losses higher. The absolute loss level is still low and the capital cushion is enormous, but the direction is unfavorable and reinsurance ceding (new books cede ~40% vs. ~15% on legacy books) is structurally compressing net premium yield (31.1 bps vs. 33.0 bps YoY).

We rate MTG a HOLD on an income/capital-return basis. The stock at $26.49 trades at roughly 1.12x book value (BVPS ~$23.6) and ~8.4x trailing EPS, a discount to peer P/E on a normalizing-but-still-low loss backdrop. The all-in shareholder yield (dividend plus buyback) is the durable return driver. What is missing is a re-rating catalyst: the Street is neutral (RBC Sector Perform, Zacks Hold), estimate revisions are flat, and the loss cycle is turning the wrong way. This is a quality franchise fully returning its earnings stream to shareholders, without asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

MGIC Investment Corporation operates through its principal subsidiary, Mortgage Guaranty Insurance Corporation, one of the largest U.S. private mortgage insurers. The product insures lenders against borrower default on loans with loan-to-value ratios above 80%, predominantly mortgages sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The company runs as a single reportable segment (Mortgage Insurance), with two subsidiaries (MAC and MIC) participating in GSE Credit Risk Transfer programs (~$478M risk in force).

Scale metrics as of 3/31/2026: Primary Insurance in Force of $302.7B (up 3% YoY), Primary Risk in Force of $81.2B, and Q1 2026 New Insurance Written of $14.4B (up 41% YoY). The NIW surge was driven largely by a higher refinance share (20.6% of NIW versus 5.6% a year earlier) as rates fluctuated, plus a stronger competitive position. Annual persistency was 84.0%.

MGIC competes against five other PMI players (Essent, Radian, Arch MI, Enact, National MI) and against government alternatives (FHA, VA) and GSE-level credit risk transfer. Its competitive advantages are financial-strength ratings (Moody's A2 stable, S&P A- positive, A.M. Best A stable), scale, a sophisticated reinsurance program reducing required capital, and a large seasoned book with improved post-2009 risk characteristics. Competition is conducted primarily through proprietary "black box" pricing engines, so the contest is about risk-adjusted pricing and capital efficiency rather than brand.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** Q1 2026 net income was $165.3M ($0.76 diluted EPS) versus $185.5M ($0.75) in Q1 2025. EPS rose modestly despite lower net income because of the buyback. The annual net income series is stable: $635M (2021), $865M (2022), $713M (2023), $763M (2024), $738M (2025). This is a business with durable ~$700–800M earnings power, not a growth engine.

**The loss cycle.** Losses incurred net rose 247% to $33.2M, reflecting both higher current-year new-notice losses ($64.5M) and a shrinking favorable prior-year development credit ($(31.2)M vs. $(49.9)M). The loss-reserving model is delinquency-triggered rather than ultimate-loss-based, so reserves are only booked when a loan goes delinquent; a downturn can hit earnings sharply and suddenly. Sensitivity disclosures: a 1-point change in claim rate moves reserves ±$20M; a $1,000 change in severity ±$7M. The benign environment that suppressed losses — home-price appreciation enabling cure-via-sale — is moderating, and management says it expects net losses and LAE paid to increase. The absolute level remains low, but the trajectory is negative.

**Net premium yield.** The driver of the compression to 31.1 bps is higher ceded premiums under expanded reinsurance, not a deterioration in the in-force portfolio yield itself (~38 bps). This trades current premium income for capital efficiency and downside protection.

**Balance sheet.** Shareholders' equity is $5.04B; the investment portfolio is $5.7B, high quality (82% AAA/AA/A, 4.2-year duration, 4.0% pre-tax yield). The PMIERs cushion gives a ~$2.9B excess; risk-to-capital is 9.6:1 versus the 25:1 regulatory maximum; policyholder position is $3.8B above the required MPP. Leverage is modest: a single $650M 5.25% senior note due August 2028, in covenant compliance. The $(167.5)M AOCI position reflects unrealized bond losses from higher rates; it is non-cash and the securities are held-to-recovery capable.

**Capital return.** This is the franchise. The share count fell from ~305.8M (2022) to 213.2M (3/31/2026), roughly 30%. Q1 2026 alone retired 7.2M shares for $192.6M; FY2025 retired ~30.1M shares for $782M. The April 2026 Board authorization adds $750M (on top of $232.6M remaining), and the $400M subsidiary-to-holdco dividend in April 2026 refilled holdco liquidity (~$0.7B cash/investments at quarter-end). The quarterly dividend is $0.15/share (~2.3% yield). Combining dividend and buyback, MGIC is returning effectively its entire earnings stream-plus to shareholders, executed at roughly 1.1x book — accretive to BVPS as long as the price stays near or below book.

The score trajectory shows Financial Health rising from 6.5 to 8.5 across prior reports. The capital strength fully justifies a high score, but we trim modestly to 8.0: the loss-cycle inflection and the reinsurance-dependent nature of the reported capital strength (reinsurance provides $3.1B of PMIERs credit) argue against pushing higher.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is structurally limited. Revenue was $297.1M in Q1 2026, down 3% YoY, missing expectations on lower net premiums earned (more ceding) and the sharp rise in losses incurred. Insurance in Force grew only 3% YoY. The 41% NIW jump is real but quality-of-source matters: it was driven heavily by a temporary refinance surge, which is rate-path-dependent and not a durable demand expansion. Management characterizes the underlying market as "subdued mortgage insurance market growth."

Consensus estimates are flat. Current-year EPS sits at $3.06 (vs. $3.05 ninety days ago, +0.3%); next-year at $3.30 (down 0.5% over 90 days). There is no estimate-revision momentum — the +30% revision signal that would flag an inflection is entirely absent. The earnings beat/miss record over four quarters is mixed (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss), with the two beats concentrated in mid-2025 and the most recent quarters in-line-to-slightly-below.

The only mechanism by which earnings-per-share grow is the buyback. With net income flat at ~$738M and the share count falling ~7% annually, EPS can grind up mid-single-digits even with flat-to-declining absolute profit. That engine is reliable as long as capital remains excess and the loss cycle does not consume the buyback. Growth Prospects score 5.0 — the most reliable dimension for sorting winners, and here it is genuinely middling: per-share economics compound, but absolute earnings do not grow and the loss cycle is a headwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.49, MTG trades at ~8.4x trailing EPS of $3.14 and ~1.12x book (BVPS ~$23.6). The forward P/E on the $3.06 current-year estimate is ~8.7x; on the $3.30 next-year estimate ~8.0x.

Against the reinsurance peer set, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 6.9x (SiriusPoint 6.0x, RenaissanceRe 4.9x, Everest 6.9x, RGA 11.2x). MTG at ~8.4x sits above the median, but the peer comparison is imperfect — those names are property-cat and life reinsurers with different earnings volatility and cycle dynamics. Within the mortgage-insurance cohort specifically (Essent, Radian, Enact, National MI), MGIC's ~1.1x book and high-single-digit P/E are broadly in line, and the company itself flags trading at a discount to its closest peers. The dividend yield of 2.3% is above the peer median of 1.8%.

The case for the stock being cheap rests on the all-in shareholder yield: a ~2.3% dividend plus a buyback running at ~13% of market cap in fresh authorization, retiring ~7% of shares annually near book value. The case against treating cheapness as a buy signal is that low multiples in this group reflect the absence of growth and the embedded cyclicality of the loss reserve, and a compressed multiple without a catalyst tends to stay compressed. The valuation discount has persisted because the Street is neutral (RBC Sector Perform with ~6.5% implied upside, Zacks Hold), estimate revisions are flat, and the loss cycle is normalizing higher.

We score Valuation 5.8. The yield support and discount-to-book provide a floor, but there is no identifiable catalyst that converts cheapness into appreciation, and the loss-cycle inflection works against multiple expansion. Treating the low multiple as an automatic positive would ignore that cheap multiples in flat-growth financials have historically sorted the wrong way.

## Competitive Landscape

MGIC sits in a mature six-player oligopoly competing on risk-based pricing and capital efficiency, against the perennial backdrop of FHA/VA government substitutes. Its position is stable rather than improving: scale, top-tier ratings, and a sophisticated reinsurance program are genuine advantages, but they are matched by Essent, Arch, and the other established players using the same proprietary pricing engines and the same QSR/ILN capital structures. The Q1 NIW market-share gain is encouraging but largely refi-driven and therefore cyclical.

Two structural dynamics bear watching. First, reinsurance is doing progressively more of the capital lifting, and now provides $3.1B of PMIERs credit. This is sensible capital management but makes reported strength partly contingent on reinsurer availability, pricing, and continued GSE recognition of the capital credit. Second, the favorable-loss tailwind is fading industry-wide, not just at MGIC, so the entire group faces the same normalization. The research flagged an unconfirmed "new market entrant" thread that could not be verified; if a seventh player enters, it would pressure pricing discipline, the swing factor for industry profitability.

Competitive Position scores 5.5 — stable, with no evidence of share erosion but no widening moat either. The trajectory matters more than the level here, and the trajectory is flat.

## Risk Assessment

**Loss-cycle inflection.** The delinquency-triggered reserving model means a housing downturn hits earnings suddenly. Q1's jump in losses incurred is the early sign the favorable-development tailwind is fading. This is the most important near-term fundamental risk, and it is only partially priced.

**GSE dependence and reform.** Substantially all NIW is GSE-eligible. The GSEs remain in FHFA conservatorship, and any privatization, reform, or PMIERs amendment could materially alter capital requirements or PMI demand. This is a binary, unpredictable tail that has hung over the sector for years; it is not imminent but it is unquantifiable.

**Reinsurance dependence.** With $3.1B of PMIERs credit from reinsurance and new books ceding ~40%, a rise in reinsurance pricing, a fall in availability, or a GSE/state cut to the capital credit would degrade capital efficiency and returns.

**Management transition and insider selling.** Four Item 5.02 officer/director-change 8-Ks in roughly 14 months is an unusually high cadence suggesting board/management refresh or succession activity, and the specific filings could not be retrieved. Separately, three senior executives (CFO Colson 60,000 shares; GC Maggio 80,937 shares; President/COO Miosi up to 305,084 shares) adopted 10b5-1 selling plans in early 2026. These are pre-arranged and likely benign given a strong stock run, but the cluster of departure filings alongside senior-executive selling plans warrants attention. We treat the selling as low-signal — it co-occurs with stable estimates and record capital strength, not deteriorating fundamentals — but the unverified 8-K contents are a genuine open thread.

**Concentration.** PMI is a lender-driven channel; loss of a large lender relationship or a rating downgrade would hurt NIW.

Risk Factors score 5.5 — specific, identifiable risks (loss inflection, GSE tail, the management-transition/insider cluster) sit alongside a fortress balance sheet that caps the downside. The net is mildly cautious.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress despite a constructive headline tone. Near-term ATM IV of 29.8% is rich versus 24.0% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (29.8% near-term vs. 25.8% six-month), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst, most plausibly the next earnings print. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.5%), and the put/call OI ratio of 3.91 is elevated but falling (from 4.80), suggesting some bearish positioning is unwinding. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.8%. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.4% of float, 3.7 days to cover. The signal is consistent with a market braced for the loss cycle to show up in the next report rather than a directional conviction bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** MGIC is a fortress-balance-sheet capital-return machine. With excess PMIERs capital and a fresh buyback authorization already funded at the holdco, the company retires ~7% of shares annually near book value, compounding BVPS and EPS even as absolute net income stays flat. The loss level remains low in absolute terms, the book is well-seasoned with improved post-2009 risk characteristics, and the reinsurance program protects the downside. At ~1.1x book and ~8.4x earnings with the dividend plus buyback, the all-in yield is attractive for a high-quality financial. If the loss cycle normalizes gently and the Street warms to the capital-return story, the stock re-rates toward $32–34.

**Bear case.** The loss cycle inflects harder than expected as HPA-enabled cures dry up, the delinquency-triggered reserve model produces a sharp earnings hit, and reinsurance economics tighten, compressing both net premium yield and capital efficiency. Net income falls below the ~$700M run-rate, the buyback slows, and the per-share compounding engine stalls. GSE reform headlines reintroduce the existential tail. The stock drifts to ~$22–23 (roughly book value), a ~13–17% decline from current levels.

The base case sits between: flat-to-modestly-declining net income, continued aggressive buybacks delivering mid-single-digit EPS growth, gradual loss normalization, and a stock that grinds with the buyback toward $28–29, close to the consensus mean target of $28.67.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income/capital-return basis. The recommendation has now been HOLD across three consecutive reports, and the bar for changing it is appropriately high; the current data confirms the prior stance — strengthening capital return offset by an inflecting loss cycle and absent growth catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint, particularly for the loss-ratio trajectory the options market is bracing for. The October/December 8-K cadence and any GSE-reform developments are secondary monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Loss ratio stabilizes or improves (Q2/Q3 loss ratio holding below ~12% with new-notice losses flattening), removing the central fundamental overhang.
- Stock pulls back toward or below book value (~$23–24) while the buyback continues, restoring an asymmetric entry at a clear discount.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year EPS revised up >5%) confirming earnings power is holding above the ~$3.05 base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss ratio accelerates above ~20% with rising new-delinquency notices, signaling the cycle is turning faster than the capital cushion can absorb in earnings terms.
- A material GSE-reform or PMIERs amendment that raises capital requirements or threatens PMI demand.
- The buyback is curtailed or suspended (the core thesis driver), or holdco liquidity is no longer being refilled via subsidiary dividends.
- The unverified management-transition 8-Ks reveal an adverse governance or succession development.

## Conclusion

MGIC is a well-run, over-capitalized private mortgage insurer whose value compounds through one of the more disciplined buyback programs in financials — ~30% of shares retired since 2022, executed near book value, funded by an excess-capital position that dwarfs regulatory minimums. For an investor seeking a high-quality financial with a ~2.3% dividend, a large ongoing buyback, and a fortress balance sheet, MTG delivers. What it does not offer today is asymmetric upside: absolute earnings are flat, the loss cycle is inflecting higher off historic lows, net premium yield is compressing under heavier reinsurance ceding, and the Street is neutral with flat estimates and no near-term catalyst. The ~1.1x book, ~8.4x earnings valuation is fair rather than cheap given the normalizing loss backdrop and the GSE-reform tail.

We rate MTG a HOLD (income/capital-return basis), overall score 6.1, consistent with the improving trajectory of the franchise's capital strength but tempered by the loss-cycle turn and the lack of a re-rating catalyst. Hold for the yield and the per-share compounding; revisit on a pullback toward book value or on evidence the loss cycle has stabilized.
