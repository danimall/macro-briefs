**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enviri Corporation is a different company than its trailing financial statements suggest. On June 1, 2026 the firm closed a restructuring: Veolia acquired the Clean Earth regulated-waste segment for ~$3.0 billion, ~$1.1 billion of debt was repaid, ~$1.3 billion of cash went to former shareholders (a $14.5–$16.5 per-share distribution), and the remaining Harsco Environmental (HE) and Harsco Rail businesses were spun into the entity that trades today as NVRI. The reported trailing figures (~$2.2B revenue, ~$1.5B debt, chronic net losses, a -9.4x P/E) describe the pre-spin, three-segment, leverage-impaired company that no longer exists. The relevant entity is a ~$1.3B-revenue, two-segment industrial with ~$140M of pro forma adjusted EBITDA and a balance sheet whose net-leverage covenant reset from 5.50x to 3.00x.

The investment case is a margin self-help story layered on a deleveraging event. HE, a market-leading, contract-based provider of on-site environmental services to global steel and aluminum producers, is guided to $170–180M of adjusted EBITDA and grew revenue ~6% in Q1 2026. Harsco Rail is the drag: it is guided to a $19–26M EBITDA loss for 2026 and burned $18M of cash in Q1, almost entirely on three problematic European fixed-price (engineer-to-order) contracts. Moving Rail toward breakeven would raise consolidated EBITDA roughly 15% with no revenue growth, and management has made "de-risking" Rail its top priority now that the cleaner balance sheet gives it the flexibility to absorb a charge or exit a contract.

We rate NVRI a HOLD. The forward thesis is genuine: earnings inflection through Rail stabilization, ~6% HE growth, fresh clustered insider buying (the CEO put ~$1.0M of personal capital into open-market stock at $19.43, the CFO and a director also bought), and a single analyst target at $26 implying ~18% upside. The central catalyst is binary and unresolved: management has told Network Rail it may need to "substantially revise the contract's economic terms or find a mutually acceptable exit," warning that exit could be "materially unfavorable." At ~7x EV/EBITDA on Rail-depressed earnings, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling, and the stock sits near its post-spin high.

## Company Overview

Today's Enviri operates two segments:

- **Harsco Environmental (HE)** — on-site environmental services to the steel and metals industry across ~30 countries: resource recovery, recycling of waste/byproducts, materials handling, aluminum dross processing, and "ecoproducts" (road surfacing, metallurgical additives, agriculture/turf, cement additives). The model is recurring, contract-based, and tied to customer steel/aluminum production volumes. Roughly $1.0B of revenue and the company's profit engine.

- **Harsco Rail** — highly engineered railway track-maintenance equipment, aftermarket parts, and diagnostic/contracting services for major railways and transit systems (Network Rail UK, Deutsche Bahn, SBB Switzerland, North American railroads). Roughly $0.28B of revenue. The aftermarket business (~40% of Rail revenue at ~2x OEM margins) is profitable; the OEM equipment cycle is weak and the European ETO contracts are loss-making.

The Clean Earth divestiture to Veolia validated the value of the regulated-waste asset, funded the deleveraging, and returned the bulk of the cash to former holders. Leadership turned over cleanly at close: 12-year Chairman/CEO Nicholas Grasberger and CFO Thomas Vadaketh departed; President/COO Russell Hochman became CEO and Peter Minan (a returning executive familiar with both HE and Rail) became CFO.

## Financial Analysis

The pre-spin consolidated statements are not a useful guide to the go-forward business and should be set aside for valuation purposes. For context only: FY2025 old-Enviri revenue was $2.24B with a net loss of $167.6M; long-term debt was $1.53B against just $255M of equity; operating income was near breakeven ($4.2M) while interest expense alone ran ~$111M/year. That entity was consuming nearly all operating earnings to service leverage. Q1 2026 consolidated results (pre-close) showed operating income collapsing to $0.8M from $29.3M, driven by $12.5M of Clean Earth sale costs, the non-recurrence of a $10.4M favorable Rail forward-loss adjustment from Q1 2025, and weaker Clean Earth volumes.

The relevant carve-out / pro forma picture:

- **Q1 2026 combined revenue $324.0M** — HE $256.7M (+6% YoY), Rail $67.3M.
- **Q1 2026 combined operating loss –$9.1M** (vs. +$13.9M prior year), inflated by one-time deal/restructuring costs and full corporate cost allocation.
- **FY2026 guidance: ~$140M pro forma combined adjusted EBITDA** at midpoint — HE $170–180M less a Rail EBITDA loss of $(19)–$(26)M. Modest positive free cash flow expected for the year.
- Q1 adjusted EPS was **$0.10 against –$0.30 consensus** (a clear beat); adjusted FCF was –$6M, seasonally weak.

The balance-sheet transformation is the core financial-health change. Pre-close net leverage was 4.98x against an amended 5.50x covenant ceiling, uncomfortably tight. The ~$1.1B debt repayment (full revolver, part of the term loan, full redemption of the 5.75% Senior Notes, AR facility terminated) resets the leverage covenant to 3.00x, and management states compliance with $152M available on the revolver. Quarterly interest burden, ~$28M on the old $1.5B of debt, falls sharply. The residual risk is Rail's cash burn (–$18M in Q1) and a $56.7M forward-loss reserve on the ETO contracts.

One data-quality flag: during FY2025 management identified errors in the UK defined-benefit pension actuarial estimates and revised prior-period financials (deemed immaterial cumulatively, no cash-flow effect). It warrants monitoring, partly because it hints at a possible future pension buy-out transaction that could remove a liability but consume cash.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is modest; the growth thesis rests on earnings inflection rather than top line. HE grew ~6% in Q1 and benefits from a sticky, contract-based model tied to global steel/aluminum output. The EU steel trade measures — reduced import quotas and higher above-quota tariffs proposed October 2025, implementation around July 2026 — could support European steel utilization and, in turn, HE service volumes. This is a swing factor, not a guaranteed tailwind.

The larger earnings lever is Rail. The segment's 2026 loss is concentrated in the European ETO contracts. If management stabilizes Rail toward breakeven — by de-risking or exiting the worst contracts, cutting Rail SG&A and inventory, and tilting mix toward the higher-margin aftermarket — consolidated EBITDA could rise ~$22M, roughly 15%, off the ~$140M base with no revenue growth. Management frames the timeline as "demonstrable progress in 2026" with implementation programs landing in 2027.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and reflects an entity in transition: +133.8% (Q1 2026, the post-restructuring quarter), +26.5% (Q4 2025), but two prior misses (-135.3% and -74.9%). The recent two-quarter improvement is encouraging but the sample is short and noisy. We weight the Rail order book caution heavily: management acknowledged the 2026 OEM equipment order book is "a good bit behind" historical norms, primarily in North America, which caps near-term Rail revenue recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

With ~28.1M shares at $22.01, the equity is worth ~$611M. Adding back roughly ~$430M of residual debt (net of available revolver capacity) puts enterprise value near ~$1.0B, or about **7x the ~$140M pro forma EBITDA**. That is a fair multiple for a low-growth, two-segment industrial with one profitable, cyclical core (HE) and one cash-burning problem segment (Rail).

The trailing P/E of -9.4x is meaningless and reflects the defunct consolidated entity. Peer multiples (Tennant 51.7x, Proto Labs 73.5x, Enerpac 22.1x, Kennametal 19.4x; median 51.7x) are not directly comparable given NVRI's depressed near-term earnings and the Rail loss, but they indicate the sector trades well above NVRI on conventional metrics, appropriate given NVRI's lower quality and growth.

The valuation case is conditional, not a screening bargain. The EBITDA base is suppressed by ~$22M of Rail losses; on normalized earnings power (Rail at breakeven, ~$170M EBITDA), the same EV would represent ~6x, which would be cheap for HE's quality. The catalyst that closes that gap is identifiable (Rail de-risking) but unresolved and partly binary. We score valuation moderate: there is a specific reason the market may be under-pricing the normalized earnings power, but a fair 7x today with genuine Rail tail risk does not offer deep asymmetry. The single analyst target of $26 (~18% above current) is a reasonable floor on the bull path but reflects the same normalization assumption.

## Competitive Landscape

HE holds a market-leading position as a global on-site environmental services provider to steel and metals producers, operating under long-term contracts in ~30 countries. The model is sticky and recurring, with revenue keyed to customer production volumes. Its competitive trajectory is stable to modestly improving (+6% Q1 growth, potential EU steel-policy support), and the Clean Earth sale to Veolia implicitly validated the broader asset quality of the franchise.

Rail competes in a niche of highly engineered track-maintenance equipment for major railways. The aftermarket franchise is durable and profitable, but the OEM equipment cycle is weak and the three European fixed-price ETO contracts have repeatedly required loss provisions. The segment's competitive position is mixed: a defensible aftermarket wrapped around a structurally unprofitable, behind-schedule OEM/ETO book. On balance the consolidated competitive trajectory is stable, anchored by HE and dragged by Rail.

## Risk Assessment

- **Harsco Rail ETO contracts (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Three European fixed-price contracts — Network Rail (68% complete), Deutsche Bahn (56%), SBB (91%) — carry the forward-loss reserve. Management has explicitly warned it may need to "substantially revise the contract's economic terms or find a mutually acceptable exit" on Network Rail, and that exit could be "materially unfavorable." This is a binary, near-term overhang that could produce a one-time charge while ultimately removing a cash drain. Resolution is both the key risk and the key catalyst.
- **Legal/environmental contingencies carried into New Enviri.** A Brazil/CSN slag matter now includes a criminal proceeding (initiated September 2025) seeking ~$82M jointly from CSN and Enviri; the company disputes liability. The Bahrain salt-cake matter carries a $27.0M reserve with uncertain recovery. Newtown Creek Superfund (one of 30+ PRPs; EPA Record of Decision not before 2028) and ~17,000 mostly inactive, insured asbestos actions round out the tail. Total environmental liabilities attributable to New Enviri are ~$28M.
- **Cyclicality and trade policy.** HE volumes track global steel/aluminum production. EU steel protection measures cut both ways — potentially supportive of European output but uncertain in timing and effect.
- **Standalone-company execution.** New Enviri must replace shared corporate functions, may face higher standalone costs, and is providing transition services to Veolia.
- **Pension control item.** The FY2025 UK pension actuarial restatement is a data-quality flag and a hint of a possible future buy-out that could consume cash.
- **Price-series distortion.** The trailing "+161%" price move is not apples-to-apples — the special distribution means pre- and post-June-1 prices reflect different entities. Do not read the run-up as either momentum confirmation or an over-extension signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 37.1% against trailing one-year realized of 43.8% — options screen cheap (IV percentile 13% within the chain), suggesting the market is not pricing an imminent shock. Put/call skew is -14.6% (call-heavy), and the term structure is in contango (1-month 37.1% < 6-month 47.8%), a complacent/normal configuration. The longer-dated vol step-up to ~48% by October is consistent with the market reserving uncertainty for the Rail-contract resolution timeline. Short interest is effectively nil (reported shares short of 1, a data artifact indicating no meaningful bearish positioning). The options posture is mildly constructive and not contradicting the fundamental view, though the call-skew complacency means downside from a Rail charge may be under-hedged.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26+).** Management executes the self-help program: Rail moves toward breakeven through aftermarket mix shift, SG&A and inventory cuts, and exit/renegotiation of the worst European ETO contracts. HE compounds ~mid-single digits, aided by EU steel-protection measures lifting European utilization. Consolidated EBITDA migrates toward $160–170M, and a clean, deleveraged balance sheet with positive FCF supports a re-rating to ~8x EV/EBITDA. Fresh clustered insider buying at $18–19.43 signals management sees that path. The single sell-side target of $26 is the anchor.

**Bear Case (~$15, the 52-week low).** Network Rail exit triggers a material unfavorable charge; the other ETO contracts slip further on inflation, supply-chain, and homologation issues; the weak OEM order book caps Rail revenue. Steel cyclicality or a softer European output picture pressures HE. Standalone costs run higher than planned. EBITDA stalls near or below $130M and the stock de-rates back toward the mid-teens, roughly 30% downside.

**Base Case (~$23).** HE delivers ~$175M EBITDA; Rail loss narrows modestly but is not fully resolved within 2026; consolidated EBITDA lands near guidance (~$140–145M). The stock holds in the low-$20s as the market waits for Rail clarity, with modest upside on incremental self-help progress. At ~7x EV/EBITDA the shares are fairly valued pending catalyst resolution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is earnings inflection — Rail de-risking lifting consolidated EBITDA ~15% on a flat top line, plus ~6% HE growth and a potential EU steel-policy tailwind — supported by clustered fresh insider conviction and a $26 analyst target. It is not yet a BUY because the central catalyst is binary and unresolved, the order book is weak, and the shares already trade at a fair ~7x EV/EBITDA near the post-spin high.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two quarterly prints (Q2 2026 in early August, Q3 2026 in early November), which should reveal whether the Rail "demonstrable progress in 2026" claim is materializing, plus the July 2026 EU steel-measure implementation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A definitive resolution of the Network Rail contract (exit or renegotiation) with a quantified, contained charge, accompanied by Rail tracking toward a smaller-than-guided 2026 EBITDA loss (better than $(19)M).
- Two consecutive quarters of HE EBITDA at or above the $170–180M run-rate with positive consolidated FCF, confirming the deleveraged model self-funds.
- A pullback toward the insiders' $18–19 entry zone, which would restore meaningful asymmetry at ~6x normalized EBITDA.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Network Rail or Deutsche Bahn charge materially larger than the $56.7M forward-loss reserve, or a covenant-pressuring cash drain that pushes net leverage back toward the 3.00x ceiling.
- HE EBITDA guidance cut below ~$165M, or evidence European steel volumes are contracting rather than benefiting from trade measures.
- Adverse escalation in the Brazil/CSN criminal proceeding (~$82M claim) or a material adverse pension/buy-out cash requirement.

## Conclusion

NVRI is best understood as a post-spin special situation: a newly deleveraged, two-segment industrial with ~$140M pro forma EBITDA, a 3.00x leverage covenant after ~$1.1B of debt repayment, and an unusually strong fresh insider-buying signal. The forward case is credible: stabilizing the loss-making Rail franchise toward breakeven would lift consolidated EBITDA ~15% without revenue growth, and HE is a sticky, market-leading, modestly growing core. The value-creation lever and the principal risk are the same thing: the European ETO contracts, where a Network Rail exit could be "materially unfavorable" and the resolution timeline runs through year-end. At ~7x EV/EBITDA on Rail-depressed earnings, with the stock near its post-spin high, the asymmetry is balanced. We hold for the earnings-inflection thesis while waiting for Rail clarity, and would turn more constructive on a contained Network Rail resolution or a pullback toward where insiders bought.
