## Executive Summary

UnitedHealth Group is in the early stages of recovering from the most severe operational deterioration in its history. The company's medical care ratio spiked from 83.2% in FY2023 to 89.1% in FY2025, cutting operating income by 41% and net income by 46%. Q1 2026 provided the first concrete evidence of stabilization: MCR improved to 83.9%, adjusted EPS of $7.23 beat consensus by 9%, and full-year guidance was raised to >$18.25.

The stock has recovered 46% from its $234.60 low (August 2025) to $394.65, leaving it 35% below all-time highs and trading at 21.6x forward adjusted earnings. This multiple sits below UNH's 5-year historical average of ~23-25x but assumes a clean recovery path that faces material obstacles: Medicare Advantage membership is contracting, the DOJ criminal investigation remains unresolved, Optum Health's value-based care model posted losses, and all three rating agencies maintain negative outlooks.

Berkshire Hathaway's complete exit from its 5 million share position in Q1 2026 — after accumulating near $271 and selling near $394 — provides a useful external signal that the valuation recovery from crisis levels has largely occurred. What remains is a multi-year earnings normalization story with meaningful binary risks, not an asymmetric opportunity.

## Company Overview

UnitedHealth Group operates as the largest U.S. health care company by revenue ($447.6B in FY2025) through two platforms:

**UnitedHealthcare** provides health insurance to ~49.8 million members across commercial (29.7M), Medicare Advantage (8.4M), Medicaid (7.4M), and Medicare Supplement (4.3M) segments.

**Optum** ($270.6B revenue) operates three segments: Optum Health (value-based care delivery, ~95M people served), Optum Insight (health technology/analytics), and Optum Rx (pharmacy benefits, 1.66B adjusted scripts annually).

The vertical integration between insurance and care delivery was designed to create cost advantages through better data, aligned incentives, and reduced administrative friction. The 2024-2025 period tested this thesis severely, as elevated medical costs hit UNH on both the insurance side (paying claims) and the care delivery side (absorbing risk in value-based contracts).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Top-line growth remained strong at 12% in FY2025 ($447.6B), driven by Medicare Part D IRA impacts, Optum Rx volume, and commercial enrollment. Q1 2026 revenue of $111.7B beat estimates by $2B.

**Profitability — The Core Problem:**
- Operating margin: 8.7% (2023) → 8.1% (2024) → 4.2% (2025)
- Net income: $22.4B → $14.4B → $12.1B
- EPS: $23.86 → $15.51 → $13.23
- Medical care ratio: 83.2% → 85.5% → 89.1%

The MCR deterioration alone accounts for roughly $26B in incremental medical costs versus the FY2023 baseline (600bps × ~$430B in medical-exposed revenue). Q4 2025 was particularly severe: $2.5B restructuring charges, $799M in additional Change Healthcare reserves, and net income of just $10M.

**Q1 2026 Recovery Metrics:**
- MCR: 83.9% (improved 90bps YoY from 84.8% in Q1 2025)
- Adjusted EPS: $7.23 (beat $6.64 consensus)
- Full-year guidance raised to >$18.25 adjusted EPS

**Balance Sheet:**
- Long-term debt: $72.3B (D/E 0.67 — manageable for a health insurer)
- Goodwill: $110.5B (36% of $309.6B total assets)
- Cash: $24.4B (only $1.1B unrestricted)
- Current ratio: 0.79 (typical for insurers given medical claims payable)

**Cash Flow:**
- OCF: $19.7B (down from $24.2B in 2024 and $29.1B in 2023)
- Capital returns: $13.4B ($5.5B buybacks + $7.9B dividends)
- FCF yield at current market cap: approximately 4.5%

**Credit:** All three agencies maintain negative outlooks (Moody's A2, S&P A+, Fitch A). The negative outlook reflects uncertainty about the duration of margin pressure, not imminent downgrade risk.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is a recovery story, not an expansion story.** Consensus estimates project:
- FY2026E: $18.36 EPS (+39% YoY off a depressed base)
- FY2027E: $20.76 EPS (+13%)

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year up 2.9% over 90 days, next year up 3.7%. These are constructive but nowhere near the >30% revision velocity that would signal a strong buy signal.

**Structural Headwinds to Growth:**
1. Medicare Advantage membership contracting (down 965K in Q1 2026). CMS rate notices for 2027 described as "far below" forward medical cost trend. UNH is making county-by-county withdrawal decisions.
2. Medicaid membership declining due to eligibility redeterminations and state exits.
3. Optum Health's value-based care model posted a $(278M) operating loss in FY2025 with loss contract reserves established for 2026.

**Growth Catalysts:**
1. MCR normalization toward 84-85% as pricing catches up to utilization (requires 12-18 months of clean data)
2. AI investment ($1.5B) targeting operational efficiency with "conservative" 2:1 ROI projected within 12-18 months
3. Commercial enrollment continues growing
4. Optum Rx script volumes expanding

The growth path back to $25+ EPS (pre-crisis levels) requires sustained MCR improvement over 2-3 years, stable Medicare Advantage membership, and resolution of Optum Health losses. This is plausible but not certain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $394.65:
- Trailing P/E: 29.8x (on depressed $13.23 EPS — not meaningful)
- Forward P/E on FY2026 guidance: 21.6x ($18.25+ adjusted EPS)
- Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus: 19.0x ($20.76)
- FCF yield: ~4.5%
- Dividend yield: 2.2%

**Peer comparison:**
- ELV (Elevance): 16.8x P/E with more stable earnings trajectory
- HUM (Humana): 32.7x — also recovering from MA cost pressures
- Peer median: 33.8x (skewed by Molina at 51x and Humana at 33x)

UNH at 21.6x forward is below its 5-year average (~23-25x) and below peer median. The discount reflects legitimate uncertainties: DOJ investigation, leadership questions, structural MA pressure. Whether the discount sufficiently compensates for these risks determines the investment decision.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $389, median $400. The stock at $395 is essentially at consensus — limited upside from the Street's collective estimate. High target $444 implies 12.5% upside; low target $287 implies 27% downside.

The valuation is neither compellingly cheap nor expensive. At 21.6x forward with 13% projected earnings growth and material binary risks, the risk/reward is approximately balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

UNH's competitive position remains the strongest in managed care by a significant margin. Revenue of $448B is roughly 4x Elevance ($87B market cap) and 10x Humana ($37B market cap). The integrated model — combining insurance data with care delivery and pharmacy operations — creates cost and data advantages no competitor can replicate at similar scale.

However, the trajectory showed cracks in 2025:
- Company TSR underperformed peer group by 46pp over the proxy measurement period
- Optum Health's loss challenged the vertical integration thesis
- Medicare Advantage market position is shrinking rather than expanding

The competitive moat remains wide in absolute terms. UNH processes more data, serves more members, and operates more care delivery sites than any competitor. But the value-based care model that was supposed to demonstrate the superiority of integration instead demonstrated its vulnerability to adverse selection and cost trend miscalculation.

## Risk Assessment

**1. DOJ Criminal Investigation (Unquantifiable, Binary):** An ongoing criminal probe into alleged Medicare fraud creates tail risk that is extremely difficult to price. Criminal findings could result in monetary penalties, exclusion from government programs, or forced structural changes. The probability of severe outcomes is low but non-zero; the magnitude is potentially catastrophic.

**2. Medicare Advantage Structural Funding Gap:** CMS rate notices have been "well below" forward medical cost trends for multiple years. The 2027 Advanced Notice is described as "far below" trend. UNH can withdraw from unprofitable counties but cannot control the rate-setting process. This is a multi-year headwind with no clear resolution mechanism absent legislative change.

**3. Goodwill Impairment ($110.5B):** Optum Health posted a loss in FY2025 after years of goodwill-building acquisitions. Management stated all units passed October 2025 impairment tests, but sustained underperformance could trigger charges. A 10% impairment of total goodwill = ~$11B charge = approximately one year of net income.

**4. Medical Cost Trend Recurrence:** A 1% change in medical costs payable equals ~$300M in net earnings impact. Favorable prior-period development collapsed from $840M (2023) to $140M (2025), indicating reserves are no longer conservatively set. If utilization trends worsen again in H2 2026, the MCR improvement could reverse.

**5. Leadership Instability:** Three CEO-level changes in under 12 months. Hemsley's return provides stability but he brings potential conflicts of interest (personal healthcare investments reported by the Wall Street Journal in February 2026, triggering a Scott+Scott attorney investigation filed March 2026). Four new executives hired at $115M total compensation in 2025-2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($440-470, +12-19%):**
- MCR continues normalizing toward 84-85% through 2026-2027
- AI investments deliver measurable operational savings (projected 2:1 ROI)
- DOJ investigation resolved with manageable fine/settlement
- Medicare Advantage rates eventually catch up to cost trends
- EPS recovers to $22-25 range by FY2028, multiple re-rates to 20-22x
- Goldman's "profit inflection" thesis plays out as pricing resets

**Bear Case ($270-300, -24-31%):**
- DOJ investigation results in material penalties or structural remedies
- MCR improvement stalls as utilization remains elevated
- Medicare Advantage membership contraction accelerates
- Goodwill impairment charges triggered at Optum Health
- Market assigns 15-16x multiple reflecting structural growth impairment
- Medical cost inflation in a stagflationary macro environment pushes MCR back above 85%

**Base Case ($380-420):** Gradual earnings recovery to ~$20 EPS by FY2027, multiple holds at 20-21x as DOJ overhang prevents full re-rating. Stock oscillates in current range until key risks resolve.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

1. DOJ investigation resolved with quantified, manageable penalty (<$5B)
2. Q2 2026 MCR at or below 84.0%, confirming sustained improvement
3. Stock pullback to $340 or below (15% discount to current) on non-fundamental selling, creating 18x forward P/E entry point

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DOJ investigation escalates to indictment or program exclusion
2. MCR reverses above 86% in any reported quarter
3. Full-year guidance cut below $17 EPS
4. Goodwill impairment charge exceeding $5B

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected July 2026) is the next natural evaluation point. Three key data points: MCR trajectory, Medicare Advantage membership update, and any DOJ-related disclosures.

## Conclusion

UnitedHealth Group is recovering from an extraordinary convergence of operational, leadership, and legal crises. Q1 2026 provided the first evidence that the worst is behind: MCR improved, guidance was raised, and the new management team is executing on cost reduction and portfolio rationalization.

At $394.65 and 21.6x forward earnings, the stock prices a recovery that proceeds smoothly over 18-24 months. Several binary risks — particularly the DOJ investigation and structural Medicare Advantage funding shortfalls — could derail this recovery at any point. Berkshire's complete exit after capturing the initial recovery move is instructive: the risk/reward has normalized from asymmetrically favorable (at $271) to approximately balanced (at $395).

The appropriate positioning is to hold existing positions without adding. The stock offers adequate earnings recovery potential and a 2.2% dividend yield, but lacks the specific non-consensus catalyst and margin of safety required for new capital deployment at this level. The next 1-2 quarters of MCR data and any DOJ developments will determine whether UNH deserves upgrading.
