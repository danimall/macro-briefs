**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kraft Heinz is a $30bn packaged-food company caught between two credible narratives: a chronic secular decline that the market has priced with a distressed multiple and a 6.2% dividend yield, and a fresh, externally-led turnaround under new CEO Steve Cahillane (former Kellanova/Kellogg CEO, who took over in January 2026) who has redirected roughly $600M from a shelved spin-off into reinvestment. The stock trades at $25.67, down 10.8% over the trailing year and near the middle of its $21.04–$29.19 52-week range. The headline GAAP loss (−$4.93 diluted EPS) is almost entirely non-cash: $9.3B of goodwill and intangible impairments in 2025 masks a business that still generated $3.66B of free cash flow and $2.60 in adjusted EPS.

The bull case is straightforward: adjusted earnings power near $2.60, FCF that comfortably covers a ~$1.9B dividend, a deeply discounted multiple relative to normalized earnings, and a credible new leader buying shares. The bear case is equally concrete: North America organic sales fell 4.7% in FY2025 on a 5-point volume decline, adjusted operating income fell 11.5%, the Kraft brand carries less than a 2% fair-value cushion against an $8.5B carrying value (guaranteeing further impairment absent a price recovery), and Berkshire's registered ~325M-share (27.5%) stake sits as a structural overhang on the float.

Our recommendation is HOLD, classified as an income hold. The dividend is well-covered today, the new-CEO reinvestment thesis shows early signs of traction (portfolio share "gaining or holding" improved from 21% to a claimed 58% exit rate), and the valuation is not stretched. But there is no clean appreciation catalyst: the spin-off that could have crystallized sum-of-the-parts value is paused, the impairment overhang is recurring, and underlying (adjusted) profitability is still eroding. Own it for the yield and the option on a turnaround, not for a re-rating.

## Company Overview

Kraft Heinz was formed by the 2015 merger of Kraft and Heinz, orchestrated by Berkshire Hathaway and 3G Capital. It is one of the largest packaged-food companies globally, marketing across eight consumer platforms (Taste Elevation, Easy Ready Meals, Substantial Snacking, Desserts, Hydration, Cheese, Coffee, Meats) and reporting three geographies: North America (~75% of sales, $18.6B), International Developed Markets ($3.5B), and Emerging Markets ($2.8B). Marquee brands include Heinz, Kraft, Oscar Mayer, Philadelphia, Velveeta, Lunchables, Capri-Sun, Kool-Aid, Jell-O, Maxwell House, Ore-Ida, and Classico.

The company's competitive advantage rests on brand equity and retail scale. The structural problem, visible throughout the filings, is that this portfolio skews toward the most pressured center-of-store categories (processed meats, boxed mac & cheese, powdered/sugary beverages, coffee) where private label is gaining share and where health and "ultra-processed food" scrutiny is intensifying. The relative strength is the global Heinz condiments/sauces franchise (Condiments, Sauces & Spreads is the largest reportable category at ~$2.7B/quarter and one of the few growing) and the Emerging Markets segment, which posted +4.6% organic growth in FY2025, the only growth engine in the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in secular decline.** Sales fell from $26.6B (2023) to $25.8B (2024) to $24.9B (2025, −3.5%). FY2025 organic sales declined 3.4%, driven by a 4.1-point volume/mix drop only partly offset by +0.7 points of price. North America was the epicenter: organic −4.7% with volume down 5.0 points across cold cuts, coffee, condiments, bacon, frozen snacks, and desserts. Q1-2026 showed marginal stabilization: reported sales +0.8% but organic still −0.4% with volume/mix −1.2 points. The trajectory is flattening, not reversing.

**The 2025 GAAP loss is impairment-driven, not operational.** KHC reported a −$5.85B net loss (diluted EPS −$4.93) against +$2.74B in 2024. The swing is almost entirely $9.3B of non-cash impairments ($6.7B goodwill + $2.6B intangibles), following $3.7B in 2024. Excluding these, adjusted operating income was $4.75B (−11.5%) and adjusted EPS $2.60 (−15%). The business remains solidly profitable, but margins are compressing: commodity and manufacturing inflation (coffee, meats, eggs) is outpacing the efficiency program, and price actions are costing share.

**Cash flow is the anchor.** Operating cash flow was $4.46B and FCF $3.66B (~14.7% margin) in 2025; Q1-2026 OCF rose to roughly $1.0B. This funds the $1.9B annual dividend ($0.40/quarter, ~6.2% yield at $25.67) with meaningful cover (~1.9x). Capex is modest at ~$800–950M/year. FCF conversion above 100% of adjusted net income signals high-quality cash earnings even as reported results are noisy.

**The balance sheet issue is asset quality, not liquidity.** Total debt is ~$21.2B against $41.7B of equity (D/E ~0.96); no revolver is drawn, and ~$1.9B of notes due June 2026 will be repaid from marketable securities. The critical fact: goodwill ($22.2B) plus intangibles ($37.5B) equals $59.7B, roughly 73% of total assets. Tangible equity is deeply negative. The Altman Z-Score of 1.47 ("distress zone") is misleading; it reflects impairment-driven negative EBIT, not liquidity stress, and the company generates robust cash and holds investment-grade ratings. The recurring impairment risk, however, is genuine.

**Quality-of-earnings caveat.** Q1-2026's reported EPS of $0.67 was flattered by a $178M unrealized commodity-hedge gain, a $45M pension-settlement benefit, and nonrecurring procurement recoveries. Adjusted EPS actually fell 6.5% to $0.58 and adjusted operating income fell 11.8%. The four-quarter beat streak (14.2%, 8.6%, 4.4%, 7.5%) is real but occurs against estimates that reflect a declining base.

On our framework, financial health earns a middling 5.0: strong and stable FCF generation and manageable, investment-grade leverage on one side; a balance sheet that is ~73% intangibles with a near-certain further writedown, ratings under negative watch, and a defensively-loosened equity covenant (lowered from $35B to $25B in July 2025) on the other.

## Growth Analysis

There is little organic growth here to underwrite. Revenue has declined three consecutive years, North America volumes are shrinking mid-single-digits, and the only growth engine (Emerging Markets at ~11% of sales) is too small to offset. The company's own reinvestment program (~$600M redirected into pricing/value, innovation, marketing, and headcount) is a defensive move to slow volume erosion, not a growth accelerant. Management cites portfolio share "gaining or holding" improving from 21% (FY2025) to 35% (Q1-2026) to a claimed 58% (March 2026 exit rate), which is encouraging directionally but unverified in reported volumes.

Estimate revisions are effectively flat: current-year consensus of $2.06 has risen +1.1% over 90 days, while next-year $2.10 has been cut 1.9% and next-quarter $0.47 cut 3.6%. This is a business roughly holding a declining line, not the +30% velocity that would signal a fundamental turn. Structural demand headwinds compound the picture: GLP-1/weight-loss drug adoption, SNAP benefit cuts under the OBBBA already reducing low-income demand, and the company's own commitment to remove FD&C colors by end-2027 (a cost and reformulation burden). Growth prospects score 3.5.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP, KHC is unvaluable (negative earnings from impairments). The relevant metrics are forward and normalized. At $25.67 against consensus current-year EPS of $2.06, the forward P/E is ~12.5x; on adjusted FY2025 EPS of $2.60 it is ~9.9x. Both sit well below the packaged-foods peer median P/E of 28.8x, though the peer set is bimodal: Hershey (33.7x), Tyson (44.0x), and Hormel (28.8x) at the high end; General Mills (8.3x) and McCormick (8.1x) at the low end. KHC trades in the cheap cohort alongside General Mills, and for similar reasons: secular volume pressure and no growth to justify a premium.

The 6.2% dividend yield is the most tangible valuation support, and it exceeds every peer except General Mills (7.2%). Consensus analyst targets bracket the stock tightly (mean $24.21, median $24.00, low $19.00, high $40.00), leaving essentially no upside to the mean and modest downside to the low. Analyst positioning is bearish-to-neutral: one buy, fourteen holds, four strong sells, with no recent upgrades.

Discipline matters here. A low multiple on a structurally challenged consumer staple is a value-trap signal more often than a bargain: cheapness in this sector has repeatedly sorted the wrong way, and KHC's own five-year TSR ($86.97 indexed vs. a peer $201.42) is the cautionary evidence. The multiple is cheap because the earnings base is eroding and the balance sheet carries $37.2B of brands/units with ≤20% impairment cushion. The bull needs the reinvestment program to stabilize volumes before the multiple can re-rate; absent that, "cheap" simply persists. We score valuation 4.5: the yield and normalized-earnings discount provide a floor, but there is no identified catalyst to close the gap to intrinsic value, and the cheapness reflects genuine structural impairment risk rather than mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

KHC competes against branded peers (Mondelez, General Mills, Nestlé, Unilever, Conagra) and, increasingly, private label, which is winning share as consumers trade down. The sector-wide problem (volume deflation, GLP-1 demand headwinds, health scrutiny) is real, but KHC's underperformance is company-specific and severe: five-year TSR is less than half the peer index. Its portfolio concentration in the most-pressured center-store categories is a structural disadvantage relative to peers with healthier snacking or global-brand mixes.

The competitive-position trajectory has deteriorated (this dimension declined from 5.5 to 3.5 across our prior reports), and the new data does not reverse that. North America volume down 5 points in FY2025 and still negative in Q1-2026 confirms ongoing share loss in the largest segment. The counterweight is the Heinz condiments/sauces franchise (globally strong, growing) and Emerging Markets (+4.6% organic). Cahillane's playbook (reinvest behind mega-brands like Heinz, Kraft Mac & Cheese, and Capri-Sun, and make them relevant to shifting preferences) is credible but unproven in this portfolio. The claimed share-gain improvement is not yet confirmation. Competitive position scores 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Recurring impairment overhang (highest conviction).** After 2025's writedowns, reporting units and brands with ≤20% fair-value cushion still carry $37.2B. The Kraft brand alone (<2% cushion, $8.5B carrying value) and five reporting units with <5% cushion ($21.9B goodwill) are near-certain candidates for further writedowns if the depressed market cap persists or forecasts are cut. The 10-Q explicitly flags "heightened risk for impairments." Cumulative goodwill impairment has reached $20.2B; the 2015 merger premium is being systematically written off.

**Berkshire exit overhang.** On January 20, 2026, KHC filed a prospectus supplement registering 325.4M Berkshire shares for resale. Berkshire took a $3.76B writedown on the stake in Q2 2025, and new Berkshire CEO Greg Abel has been exiting positions. Any sale would pressure the float and, via lower market cap, feed the impairment risk. This is a structural cap on near-term price action regardless of fundamentals.

**IRS transfer-pricing dispute.** NOPAs for 2018–2022 propose ~$1.22B in additional tax plus ~$425M in penalties (excluding interest), with no reserve taken. An adverse outcome would be a cash and earnings surprise.

**Rating and covenant pressure.** S&P BBB (negative), Fitch BBB (negative), Moody's Baa2 under review for downgrade. The July 2025 covenant amendment lowered the minimum-equity threshold to $25B, a defensive move signaling management anticipated further impairment-driven equity erosion.

**Dividend durability.** The payout is well-covered by FCF today, but management explicitly reserves the right to cut, ratings are under pressure, and the reinvestment program plus a potential IRS cash outflow could squeeze the payout. A cut would remove the primary support for the stock, the single most important downside trigger for an income holder.

**Legacy governance litigation.** A Delaware derivative suit alleging 3G-era insider trading and misleading impairment disclosures was revived by the Delaware Supreme Court in June 2025 and consolidated in December 2025.

Given the concentration of specific, partly-unpriced risks (near-certain further impairment, a registered 27.5% seller, an unreserved ~$1.6B tax exposure), risk factors score 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 27.8% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 26.1% (spread +1.7%, IV percentile 4%), and the put/call skew is balanced at −2.3%. IV has collapsed from 43.4% to 27.8% since mid-June while the stock rose 11.5%, consistent with resolved event uncertainty rather than a building catalyst. The 3-month tenor shows a pronounced +10.2% put skew and the 6-month implied move is ±15%: the market is pricing more downside protection demand into the fall, plausibly around further impairment risk or a Berkshire share sale. Short interest of 10.8% of float (6.9 days to cover) is elevated but not crowded, consistent with institutional skepticism rather than a squeeze setup. The falling P/C OI ratio (0.65 → 0.39) suggests some bearish positioning has unwound into the rally.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$34–40).** The reinvestment program stabilizes and then re-accelerates volumes; adjusted EPS holds near $2.60 and grows low-single-digits; the multiple re-rates toward the mid-teens as the market gains confidence in the turnaround. The yield is defended, the dividend attracts income buyers, and Berkshire's overhang clears in an orderly fashion. Cahillane's credibility proves warranted; the Heinz franchise and Emerging Markets carry the portfolio. At 14–15x $2.60 plus dividends, the stock reaches the mid-to-high $30s, approaching the $40 street high.

**Bear Case (~$19).** Volume erosion continues, the Kraft brand and other low-cushion units take further multibillion-dollar writedowns, and the depressed price triggers the equity covenant conversation. The IRS dispute resolves adversely (~$1.6B cash). Ratings are downgraded, raising the cost of the debt stack. Berkshire sells into a weak tape, flooding the float. Under margin and cash pressure, the dividend is cut, removing the stock's primary support. The multiple compresses to General Mills-like levels on a still-declining base, taking the stock to the analyst-low $19, roughly 26% downside.

The probability distribution is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-negative: the yield and normalized-earnings floor cap the downside somewhat, but the impairment and Berkshire overhangs cap the upside and there is no near-term appreciation catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (income hold) with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored on quarterly volume trends and the dividend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- North America organic sales turn positive on a volume basis (not price-led) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the reinvestment program is re-accelerating the core.
- Berkshire's registered stake is placed in an orderly secondary or otherwise resolved, removing the float overhang.
- A clean impairment cycle (a quarter or annual test with no further material writedown to the Kraft brand or low-cushion units) combined with positive estimate-revision velocity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or explicit signal of one, which removes the primary thesis support.
- A ratings downgrade to below investment grade, or a further covenant amendment signaling equity stress.
- An adverse IRS resolution requiring a ~$1.6B cash outflow, or accelerating volume declines (North America organic worse than −5% again).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2-2026, expected early August) for volume trends and dividend commentary; the annual goodwill impairment test (typically Q4) for the Kraft-brand cushion; and any Berkshire Form 4 activity.

## Conclusion

Kraft Heinz is a deep-value, high-yield turnaround with a credible new CEO, offset by secular volume decline, a chronic and recurring impairment overhang, a registered ~27.5% Berkshire selling overhang, ratings pressure, and an unresolved ~$1.6B IRS exposure. The GAAP loss is optical; the real debate is whether reinvestment can re-accelerate volume before the dividend or credit rating come under strain. The stock's ~10% year-to-date pullback reflects genuine structural questions, not just volatility; at this magnitude the burden of proof sits with the bull, and the evidence of a fundamental turn (flat estimate revisions, still-negative volume) is not yet sufficient.

The trajectory has been noisy (HOLD → AVOID → AVOID → HOLD; overall scores 4.6 → 3.8 → 3.9 → 4.6), with competitive position deteriorating and management quality improving on the Cahillane transition. The current data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: the business is stabilizing at the margin, leadership is materially stronger, but the core is still shrinking and the balance sheet risk is unresolved. We rate KHC a HOLD on the well-covered 6.2% yield and the option value on a credible turnaround, with no expectation of near-term appreciation. Income investors can own it; growth-oriented capital has better places to be, and Consumer Staples selection here adds little beyond beta.
