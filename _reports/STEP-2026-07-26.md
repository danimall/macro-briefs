**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StepStone is a global private-markets solutions provider that builds customized portfolios across private equity, infrastructure, private debt, and real estate, operating as an allocator, advisor, and data provider rather than a traditional buyout GP. The operating business is compounding at a rate that few asset managers can match: management and advisory fees grew 21% in FY26 to $926.5M, fee-related earnings (FRE) rose 14% to $354.4M, and the durable recurring core has grown at roughly a 27% CAGR since FY21. Forward visibility is unusually strong: $40.1B of undeployed fee-earning capital (up 63% YoY) that mechanically converts to management fees as it activates, plus a $2.0B accrued carried-interest backlog. The stock trades at roughly 17x forward adjusted net income after a ~45% drawdown from $76 to $42.61.

The complication is that the headline financials look like distress and are not. GAAP net loss attributable to StepStone was -$535.8M (-$6.78/share) in FY26, and stockholders' equity is negative -$413.6M. Both are artifacts of the Up-C structure and, more specifically, $1,742M of GAAP equity-based compensation (~$1,723M non-cash), driven largely by the mark-to-market on a profits interest owned by the StepStone Private Wealth (SPW) management team. This liability inflates precisely because the private-wealth franchise is succeeding. This is a cash-generative, low-leverage (net debt ~$58M against ~$354M FRE) asset-light manager, not a solvency risk.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is a genuinely binary overhang. The SPW profits-interest holders acquired a put right that becomes exercisable June 30, 2026, with an estimated settlement range of $564.4M to $2,257.6M in cash (StepStone may elect up to 75% in partnership units). Running in parallel are the SRA/SRE/SPD asset-class-entity buy-ins, a decade-long series of annual ~5% exchanges consuming cash and issuing equity. These two programs plus a new dividend and buyback compete for the same capital. The cheap forward multiple is real, but whether it converts into per-share value or leaks away through dilution and cash drain depends on how these settlements are funded, which the company has not yet disclosed. The company-selected performance metric of FRE, insider buying at the lows, and record Q4 operating results are constructive; the estimate-revision trend is negative, and the June 30 put window and August 6 earnings are the near-term arbiters.

## Company Overview

StepStone occupies a distinct niche in private markets. Rather than raising a flagship buyout fund and directing capital, it acts as a "picker of pickers," constructing bespoke private-markets programs for institutions and, increasingly, private-wealth clients. It deploys across all four private-markets asset classes and all three access modes: primaries (fund commitments), secondaries, and co-investments. The proprietary SPI platform (Research, Reporting, Pacing) tracks tens of thousands of underlying funds and GPs, and this data scale is the core of its edge in manager selection and secondaries/co-investment pricing.

Scale as of March 31, 2026 was approximately $885B of total capital responsibility: AUM of $233.3B (full-discretion; +23% YoY), AUA of $651.8B (advisory/non-discretionary, mostly fixed-fee; +25%), and fee-earning AUM of $144.0B (+19%). The revenue mix in FY26 ($931.6M fee revenue) was roughly 59% focused commingled funds, 32% SMAs, 8% advisory/data, and 1% fund reimbursements. The weighted-average management fee rate is ~0.64% (PE ~0.74%).

The fastest-growing vector is the private-wealth distribution engine (SPW), which is central to both the growth thesis and the risk case. Fiscal year ends March 31; all FY26 figures are for the year ended March 31, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The right way to read this business is through its non-GAAP disclosures, because the GAAP presentation is dominated by non-cash structural items. On the operating measures the trend is clearly positive:

- Management and advisory fees: $585M (FY24) → $767M (FY25) → $926.5M (FY26), +21% YoY.
- FRE: $189.8M → $312.2M → $354.4M, +14% YoY.
- FRE margin: 41% (FY25) → 38% (FY26), with Q4 recovering to 40%. The dip reflects headcount and private-wealth infrastructure investment; this bears monitoring but is a growth-investment decision, not competitive erosion.
- Adjusted net income: $244.1M → $264.6M, +8%; ANI/share $2.05 → $2.16, +5%.
- Adjusted revenues: $969.7M → $1,320.2M, +36%.
- Gross realized performance fees: $199.2M → $388.5M, +95%.

Performance fees are lumpy but inflected up in FY26: incentive fees jumped to $220.1M (from $32.3M), concentrated in FQ3 (~$208M) on strong CY2025 venture/growth marks. The accrued carried-interest backlog stands at $2,036.9M (+36% YoY), an off-balance-sheet store of unrealized value that converts to cash over time but is marked on underlying-manager NAVs reported on a 3-month lag, making it sensitive to private-market repricing.

The GAAP loss requires explicit deconstruction. Net loss attributable to StepStone was -$535.8M and stockholders' equity is -$413.6M. The dominant driver is $1,742M of GAAP equity-based compensation in FY26, of which ~$1,723M is non-cash: IPO-legacy awards, subsidiary profits interests, and especially the mark-to-market on the SPW profits interest. Strip these out and the business earned $264.6M ANI and $354.4M FRE, both up YoY. The Altman Z-Score and negative equity flags that a mechanical screen would surface are not indicative of bankruptcy risk here.

The balance sheet supports that reading. Cash is ~$213M; investments in StepStone Funds are ~$2.29B (including $2.04B accrued carry and $752.8M legacy Greenspring). Debt is modest at ~$270.6M: a $300M SOFR-based revolver (~5.8%, maturing May 2029) drawn plus $175M of 5.52% senior notes (maturing Oct 2029), in compliance with net-leverage and minimum-FEAUM covenants. Netting the ~$213M cash against ~$270.6M debt implies net debt of only ~$58M. The $931.2M of "debt of Consolidated Funds" on the balance sheet is non-recourse to StepStone.

Earnings quality against consensus is solid but softening at the margin. The company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (FQ4 +9.6%, FQ3 +7.8%, FQ2 +9.1%) with a single miss (FQ1 FY26 -8.0%). Estimate revisions, however, are broadly negative: current-quarter EPS revised -5.0% over 30 days and -7.7% over 90 days; next-quarter -6.2%/-9.1%; current-year -2.6%/-3.0%; next-year -0.5%/-2.1%. The near-term revision deceleration is the clearest fundamental caution in the data set, and it argues against reading the price decline as pure sentiment.

Capital allocation is maturing. FY26 dividends totaled $1.48/share ($0.28 quarterly + a $0.40 supplemental), and in May 2026 the board declared a $0.55 supplemental plus $0.28 regular (paid 6/30/26). A first-ever $100M buyback was authorized in March 2026. The current dividend yield is ~2.7%. These signal confidence, but they compete for cash against two multi-year structural obligations discussed under Risk Assessment.

## Growth Analysis

The forward visibility is the strongest part of the story. The undeployed fee-earning capital represents committed capital that will generate management fees as it activates, providing a mechanical tailwind to FRE independent of new fundraising. Combined with the carry backlog and the ~27% recurring management-fee CAGR since FY21, the base case for continued double-digit FRE growth is well-supported. Management called FY26 Q4 the "best quarter ever" for FRE ($105.3M), fundraising, and FEAUM contributions, and signaled continued FY27 FRE growth.

Two secular tailwinds underpin durability. First, the allocation shift to private markets continues, and StepStone's breadth across four asset classes and three access modes lets it capture flows wherever they land. Second, private-wealth "retailization" is the primary growth vector, and the DOL's March 30, 2026 proposed 401(k) private-markets safe harbor could expand the addressable market for private assets in defined-contribution plans. StepStone via SPW is positioned to benefit, though it will compete against products purpose-built for the DC channel.

The growth signal here is genuine but does not meet the highest bar. Revenue and FRE growth are strong, but the estimate-revision velocity is negative rather than the +30%/90-day pattern that would flag an accelerating earnings surprise. This is a durable double-digit compounder with strong visibility. Weighted against the sector tailwind confirmation and the UFEC runway, Growth Prospects earns a 6.8.

## Valuation Assessment

On the operating numbers StepStone is genuinely inexpensive. At $42.61 on FY26 ANI/share of $2.16, the trailing multiple is ~19.7x; on the current-year consensus of $2.43 it is ~17.5x; and on next-year $3.30 it is ~12.9x. Third-party analysis pegs it at ~17.6x FY27E EPS. The trailing GAAP P/E of -6.3x is meaningless given the non-cash comp distortion.

Peer context is mixed and instructive. The Asset Management & Custody Banks peer set shows wide dispersion: HLNE at 13.3x, APAM at 8.9x, CNS at 25.2x, AAMI at 33.1x, WT at 40.7x, with a peer-median P/E of 25.2x. StepStone at ~12.9x next-year sits below the median and roughly in line with HLNE, the other high-quality private-markets allocator, which trades at a ~50% discount to its 52-week high. HLNE is the most relevant comparison: a similar forward multiple for a similar recurring-fee franchise. Both are being repriced on rate fears and redemption-gate risk that attaches to the alternatives complex broadly, plus, in StepStone's case, the SPW overhang.

The valuation is not a clean buy signal because the discount reflects the genuine, unresolved binary of the SPW put settlement, negative near-term estimate revisions, and the dilution overhang from the parallel buy-in programs. Cheapness alone is not a catalyst, and a compressed multiple frequently reflects a real overhang rather than a mispricing. Analyst targets (low $50, mean $66.5, high $92) sit well above the current price, but with estimates falling I treat the low end as the more reliable anchor and the mean/high as contingent on the overhang resolving favorably. The upside is real and identifiable, but it is gated by an event whose outcome is not yet visible. Valuation scores a 6.0: cheap enough to matter, not cheap enough to override the overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

StepStone's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter. AUM +23%, AUA +25%, FEAUM +19%, and UFEC +63% all indicate share gains and a widening pipeline. The moat rests on proprietary data scale (the SPI platform), blended access across all four private-markets asset classes and all three modes, sticky long-duration institutional capital, and a fast-scaling private-wealth distribution engine. It competes with dedicated allocators (Hamilton Lane, GCM Grosvenor) and with the private-wealth distribution efforts of mega-managers (Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares). Against the mega-managers, StepStone's advantage is architecture-agnostic breadth: it is not selling a single house product but constructing multi-manager portfolios, which is structurally attractive to wealth platforms wanting open architecture.

The FRE margin dip from 41% to 38% is the one competitive-position caution, but the Q4 recovery to 40% and the fact that it stems from growth investment (headcount, private-wealth infrastructure) rather than fee compression argues it is not moat erosion. The diversification across infrastructure, private debt, and real estate, all growing FEAUM double digits, also dilutes the venture/AI-valuation concentration risk that has driven part of the recent sell-off; only ~11% of AUM is in software per third-party analysis. Competitive Position earns a 6.8: a challenger gaining share with a widening data moat and structural tailwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**SPW put/call liability — the dominant overhang.** SPW management received profits interests in November 2022 with a put right exercisable starting June 30, 2026 (StepStone's call right begins after 6/30/2027). StepStone may elect up to 75% in partnership units. This is a potentially multi-billion-dollar cash-and/or-dilution event that may require raising debt or equity. It is also the driver of the GAAP losses. The liability grows as the wealth franchise succeeds (SPW-attributable net income to profits-interest holders was $136.2M in FY26). How StepStone funds the settlement — cash versus units — will determine near-term dilution and leverage, and this is the single most important swing factor for the equity.

**Asset Class Entity buy-ins (SRA, SRE, SPD).** Under 2/7/2024 agreements, StepStone is buying 100% of its real assets, real estate, and private-debt subsidiaries over ~10 annual ~5% exchanges paid in cash plus Class A/D units. This is an ongoing multi-year cash-and-dilution program running in parallel with the SPW put, and accretion is uncertain because it is priced on future adjusted net income and trading multiples.

**Performance-fee/carry valuation sensitivity.** Carry is marked on underlying-manager NAVs reported on a 3-month lag. If private-market marks deflate, a live risk given the alternatives-complex stress and any AI-capex repricing, both revenue and the carry backlog compress.

**Client termination/concentration.** SMAs (32% of fees) are terminable on 30–90 days' notice; commingled-fund GPs are removable by majority vote; state-pension RFP contracts require periodic renewal.

**Governance transition.** StepStone lost "controlled company" status on 9/18/2025 (Class B super-voting sunset). It must reach Nasdaq compliance (majority-independent board, independent comp and nominating committees) by 9/18/2026 and is currently non-compliant. Principal holders are exempted from anti-takeover provisions, an entrenchment feature.

**Regulatory.** SEC scrutiny of private-credit and retailization, plus expanding private-wealth channel compliance obligations (Reg S-P, AML, DORA, AIFMD, MiFID II).

Because the SPW put is a dated, binary, potentially multi-billion-dollar event that the market is only now focusing on (the Hunterbrook short report of ~7/2/26 repackaged already-disclosed 10-K figures), and because a second cash-consuming buy-in program runs alongside it, idiosyncratic risk is elevated relative to the current valuation. Risk Factors scores a 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 70.5% versus 46.1% historical volatility, rich at the 65th percentile within the chain, with a put/call skew of 5.4% (elevated put demand) and a term structure in backwardation (70.5% 1m vs. 64.8% 6m). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±16.8%. IV rose 4.5pp since the prior snapshot even as the stock fell 5.7%, consistent with the market pricing an imminent binary tied to the June 30 put window and the August 6 earnings print. Short interest is elevated at 10.0% of float (4.2 days to cover), reflecting institutional bearishness but not a crowded squeeze setup. The positioning confirms the fundamental read: the market is anxious about a specific, dated event rather than a slow bleed, and IV should compress once the SPW funding path clarifies.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** StepStone is a structurally advantaged private-markets allocator compounding management fees ~27% and FRE ~14%+, with the strongest forward visibility in its peer set: UFEC mechanically converting to fees, plus the carry backlog. The GAAP losses and negative equity are accounting noise; the real business is asset-light, low-leverage, and cash-generative. At ~13x next-year ANI it is priced as if the SPW overhang is a solvency threat, which it is not. If the put is funded predominantly in units (up to 75% allowed) or in staged cash the company can cover from FRE and the revolver, the overhang resolves without a dilutive equity raise, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median (25.2x) and the analyst mean target of $66.5, roughly 56% upside. Insider buying at the lows (McCabe's ~$5M open-market purchase at ~$42 in June, a director's ~$566K buy at ~$44 in February) signals that those with the most information view the sell-off as overdone.

**Bear case.** The SPW put settles near the high end ($2.26B) and StepStone is forced to fund it with a mix of cash draw and dilutive equity or debt at a time when the alternatives complex is under redemption-gate and rate stress. The parallel SRA/SRE/SPD buy-ins compound the dilution, private-market marks deflate on AI-capex repricing (compressing the carry backlog and incentive fees), and estimate revisions, already negative, continue to slide. In that scenario per-share value leaks away despite the strong operating numbers, and the stock re-tests the $38.85 low or lower. Co-COO Fernandez's full liquidation of his Class A position into weakness (~$14M+, some sold below $45) is the offsetting insider tell.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain — the operating business is attractive and the valuation is cheap, but the binary SPW settlement gates conviction in either direction).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Clarity that the SPW put will be funded predominantly in units or in staged cash serviceable from FRE/revolver, without a dilutive equity raise — most likely disclosed around or after the 6/30/26 put window or on the 8/6/26 call.
2. Estimate-revision momentum turning positive (current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions inflecting from negative to flat/positive over a 30-day window).
3. A pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s low ($38.85) on unchanged fundamentals, widening the discount to forward ANI and improving the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Confirmation that the SPW put settles near the high end ($1.5B+) and requires a materially dilutive equity raise.
2. A private-market valuation reset that compresses the carry backlog and forces incentive-fee guidance lower, combined with continued FEAUM/UFEC deceleration.
3. Evidence of fee-related margin compression persisting below the mid-30s%, indicating the private-wealth investment is not scaling to operating leverage.

**Reassessment timeline.** The 6/30/26 SPW put window and the Q1 FY27 earnings release on August 6, 2026 are the near-term arbiters. This is a 6–12 month view: the thesis resolves largely on how the put is funded and on the FY27 FRE trajectory. For reference against the analyst range, a favorable resolution supports the mean target of $66.5; a base case of continued double-digit FRE growth with a still-unresolved overhang keeps the stock range-bound near the low target of $50; the bear case is a re-test of the $38.85 low.

## Conclusion

StepStone is a high-quality, fast-growing private-markets franchise trading at a genuine discount to its recurring-earnings power, and the mechanical screening flags (negative equity, GAAP loss, negative trailing P/E) are structural artifacts rather than signs of distress. The operating business — 27% management-fee CAGR, undeployed fee-earning capital, the carry backlog, modest leverage, and rising shareholder returns — has the profile of a durable compounder. The reason to hold rather than buy is that the cheap forward multiple is genuinely contingent: the SPW put opening June 30, 2026 (a $564M–$2.26B settlement) and the parallel SRA/SRE/SPD buy-ins are real cash-and-dilution programs whose funding will determine whether the discount converts to per-share value or dissipates. Insider buying at the lows and record Q4 operating results argue the sell-off is overdone; negative estimate revisions, the governance transition, and the SPW overhang argue for patience. The August 6 print and the funding path for the put right will resolve the call. HOLD, with a clear upgrade path if the settlement is funded without a dilutive equity raise and revisions inflect.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: net debt is only ~$58M (cash ~$213M vs. debt ~$270.6M), not ~$270M — the balance sheet is even stronger than the report implied, which modestly strengthens the case against treating the SPW overhang as a solvency risk.
