**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Inspire Medical Systems is a single-product medical device company built around the only FDA-approved hypoglossal nerve stimulation therapy for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). The investment debate is no longer about a premium-growth franchise. Revenue growth decelerated from +53% (2023) to +28.5% (2024) to +13.6% (2025), and in Q1 2026 grew just 1.6% year-over-year. Management responded by cutting FY2026 guidance to $825M–$875M, which implies a revenue *decline* of roughly 4% to 10% versus 2025, the first contraction in the company's public history. The stock has fallen roughly 72% from its 52-week high of $147.03 to ~$41.

Three forces converged. First, the company's own next-generation Inspire V launch outran the reimbursement infrastructure, creating CPT coding confusion (competing claims around codes 64568, 64582 with a potential −52 reduced-services modifier, and an April 2026 AHA recommendation to use unlisted code 64999) that management says has hurt revenue and will continue to do so through 2026. Second, CMS's Project WISeR prior-authorization pilot in six states began delaying traditional Medicare cases in mid-January. Combined, management quantified the drag at $120M–$150M for the year. Third, the Q1 10-Q escalated its GLP-1 language: management now concedes revenue "is being adversely impacted by the increasing prevalence and adoption of GLP-1s" as patients defer implants to trial tirzepatide first, a retreat from the prior framing that GLP-1s expand the eligible pool.

The bull case rests on the coding issue being a self-correcting, mechanically fixable air pocket once a dedicated CPT code is issued. The problem is that there is no assurance on timing (BofA flags resolution possibly not until 2028), the GLP-1 threat is structural, forward EPS estimates have been cut ~55% over 90 days, and a cluster of government and securities litigation over the Inspire V launch period sits unaccrued. The headline 9.3x trailing P/E capitalizes a one-time tax benefit, not durable earnings. We rate INSP AVOID.

## Company Overview

Inspire sells an implantable, closed-loop hypoglossal nerve stimulation device positioned as a second-line therapy for patients with moderate-to-severe OSA who cannot tolerate or benefit from CPAP. The product is FDA-, EU MDR-, and Japan PMDA-approved, and is the only neurostimulation therapy in its category. It is contraindicated for complete concentric collapse of the airway.

The business model is hospital- and ASC-driven: those facilities are the purchasing customers, with demand generated through a large direct sales force (954 sales/marketing/reimbursement staff across 284 U.S. territories at year-end 2025) targeting ENT surgeons and sleep centers, plus heavy direct-to-consumer advertising. Revenue is roughly 95.6% U.S. and 4.4% international. Coverage exceeds 300M U.S. lives across all major national commercial insurers and all seven Medicare Administrative Contractors. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

The competitive moat consists of first-mover status in upper-airway stimulation, strong clinical data (STAR trial, ADHERE registry), 119 U.S. patents, and a high PMA regulatory barrier. That moat protects the device category but does not protect against the two forces now compressing demand: reimbursement-coding mechanics and pharmaceutical substitution.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is genuinely strong and is not the source of risk here. Altman Z-Score of 6.76 places the company deep in the safe zone, the current ratio is ~6.0, debt/equity is 0.16 with no meaningful debt, and cash plus investments stood at roughly $370M at March 31, 2026. There is no solvency or refinancing risk.

The problem is the income statement and its trajectory:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 407.9 | 624.8 | 802.8 | 912.0 | 204.6 |
| YoY growth | — | +53% | +28.5% | +13.6% | +1.6% |
| Gross margin | 83.8% | 84.5% | 84.7% | 85.4% | 86.5% |
| Operating income ($M) | (47.6) | (40.3) | 36.1 | 51.0 | (1.1) |
| Net income ($M) | (44.9) | (21.2) | 53.5 | 145.4 | (11.3) |

Two distortions deserve emphasis. First, 2025's $145.4M net income is roughly 60% a one-time, non-cash $88.8M deferred-tax benefit from releasing the valuation allowance after three years of cumulative taxable income. Underlying operating income was only $51.0M (5.6% operating margin), and SG&A grew 17.9% in 2025, faster than revenue's 13.6%, driven by sales headcount and a $33M jump in advertising. Strip out the tax benefit and underlying profitability is thin.

Second, the tax reversal is now a headwind. Having released the valuation allowance, INSP books tax expense, and stock-comp shortfalls (awards vesting below grant-date value as the share price fell) drove a Q1 2026 effective rate of 571%: a $13.7M tax expense on $2.4M pretax income, producing an $11.3M GAAP net loss despite positive operating cash flow. Expect noisy reported earnings going forward.

Free cash flow was $78.5M in 2025 (8.6% margin), with operating cash flow of $117M, so the company is cash-generative. But a heavy inventory build (from ~$80M to ~$145M) to support the Inspire V launch ties up working capital and raises obsolescence risk on legacy Inspire IV, which already took a $2.1M write-down in 2025. The reported 4.89 trailing EPS that produces a 9.3x P/E is an artifact of the tax benefit, not a representation of forward earnings power. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $0.91 (down from $2.02 ninety days ago), placing the stock at roughly 45x forward earnings on a depressed base.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has broken in a specific, observable way. The deceleration sequence (+53% → +28.5% → +13.6% → +1.6%) is monotone and now negative on a guided basis. This is the defining fact of the investment.

The estimate revisions are severe and confirm the deterioration rather than reflecting transient noise. Over 90 days, current-quarter consensus moved from +$0.27 to −$0.25 (a −190.3% swing), next-quarter from $0.51 to $0.14 (−72.6%), current-year from $2.02 to $0.91 (−55.0%), and next-year from $2.69 to $1.30 (−51.8%). When estimate revisions of this magnitude run *downward*, the appropriate read is that consensus is catching down to a deteriorating reality. This is the inverse of the positive-revision signal that supports buying; it confirms the bear case.

Three drivers explain the collapse:

1. **Inspire V coding (resolvable but uncertain).** The August 2024-approved, May 2025-launched Inspire V created multi-stakeholder confusion over how to bill the procedure, including a potential −52 modifier cutting the professional fee 10–50% and an AHA push toward unlisted code 64999 that slows claims. A reduced professional fee could discourage surgeons from performing the procedure. Management is seeking a dedicated CPT code with "no guarantee as to timing or outcome." BofA flags this may not resolve until 2028.

2. **Project WISeR (transient policy headwind).** CMS's prior-authorization pilot in six states began delaying traditional Medicare cases in mid-January 2026.

3. **GLP-1 substitution (structural).** Lilly's Zepbound was FDA-approved for OSA in obese patients in December 2024, with SURMOUNT-OSA showing a 50.7% AHI reduction and 43% disease resolution at the top dose. Apnimed's oral drug is in Phase 3. Management's escalation from "GLP-1s expand the pool" to "GLP-1s are adversely impacting our revenue" is the most important secular tell in the filings.

The first two are at least theoretically self-limiting. The third is not, and it directly caps the second-line surgical TAM. Growth prospects score 3.5.

## Valuation Assessment

On a trailing basis INSP looks cheap at 9.3x, but that multiple capitalizes tax-benefit-inflated earnings and is not a meaningful anchor. The peer set illustrates how unusable trailing P/E is across this group: TNDM at −11.3x, ENOV at −1.1x, AHCO at −17.1x, CNMD at 19.4x, AORT at 85.5x (peer median 85.5x). Most peers are at or near breakeven, so multiples are not comparable.

The honest valuation lens is forward earnings on a depressed and falling base. At $0.91 current-year consensus, the stock trades near 45x forward earnings; at $1.30 next-year consensus, roughly 32x. Those are not value multiples for a business guided to *declining* revenue with an open structural threat to its end market. The analyst price target range is $39 (low) to $65 (high) with a median of $53, and the sell-side has been cutting (Truist to ~$64 in April, BofA downgrading in May). With the stock at $41 and the low target at $39, the consensus floor offers little protection.

A cheap multiple on impaired or shrinking fundamentals is a value-trap signature, not an opportunity. The decisive question — whether the 2026 decline is a temporary, self-correcting coding air pocket or a structural shift where GLP-1s permanently cap the addressable market — is not yet answerable from the filings. Until a dedicated CPT code is issued and GLP-1 demand erosion is bounded, paying for a recovery that may not arrive is speculative. We score valuation 4.5: there is genuine downside support from the cash balance and absolute price, but no identifiable catalyst that makes the current price asymmetrically attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Inspire retains a defensible device-category position. The PTAB denied all three of Nyxoah's IPR petitions on April 14, 2026, a procedural win that protects its asserted patents. Its PMA regulatory barrier is high relative to 510(k) pathways, and its clinical dataset is the deepest in upper-airway stimulation.

The competitive pressure is coming from outside the device category. Nyxoah (open-loop bilateral stimulation) and LivaNova compete on the device front and are in mutual patent litigation, but the more significant threat is pharmaceutical: GLP-1s and emerging oral agents (Apnimed Phase 3) attack the demand funnel directly, diverting patients before they reach a surgical decision. Inspire's moat is real within its category, but the category itself is being squeezed by substitution and by payer cost-containment (WISeR prior-auth, GPO pricing pressure, OBBBA Medicaid cuts). Trajectory matters more than level here: a category leader whose end market is being structurally narrowed scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack is unusually dense and largely unaccrued:

- **DOJ False Claims Act / Anti-Kickback investigation.** A Civil Investigative Demand was received on January 17, 2025 from the U.S. Attorney (D. Minn.) on marketing, promotion, and reimbursement practices. Outcome and timing are unknown.
- **Qui tam whistleblower suit** alleging improper Medicare reimbursement certification. DOJ declined to intervene in January 2026, but the relator is proceeding.
- **Securities class action** (Indiana PRS, after City of Pontiac was voluntarily dismissed) and **two derivative suits** (Korte, Davis), all alleging misleading statements about the Inspire V launch between August 6, 2024 and August 4, 2025. No loss has been accrued, so any settlement is incremental downside.
- **Reimbursement coding** could remain unresolved into 2028 per BofA, prolonging the revenue drag.
- **Single-product, elective-procedure concentration** subject to surgeon capacity constraints (repeatedly cited as a growth limiter), seasonality, and payer policy shifts.

The litigation cluster all points at the same Inspire V launch period, the same window in which DOJ is investigating reimbursement practices. That convergence raises the probability that legal/regulatory overhang persists beyond the operational issues. Risk factors score 3.5: these are specific, catalyst-bearing, unaccrued, and not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 113.0% versus 77.2% one-year historical volatility (rich by 35.7 points, 92nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (113.0% near-term vs 72.1% at six months), indicating the market expects a near-term catalyst. ATM IV jumped from 61.8% to 113.0% over the past three weeks while the price fell 4.8%, and the put/call OI ratio rose to 1.26. Put/call skew is elevated at 37.4%. This confirms the cautious fundamental view: positioning is defensive and the market anticipates further volatility. Short interest of 16.8% of float (2.6 days to cover) signals meaningful institutional bearish positioning, though the modest days-to-cover limits squeeze fuel.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The 2026 revenue decline is a temporary, self-inflicted air pocket. Inspire has navigated CPT code changes before; a dedicated code is mechanically achievable and would restore claims processing and surgeon economics. WISeR is a pilot that will run its course. GLP-1s, over a multi-year horizon, lower BMI into Inspire's indication and increase OSA diagnoses, expanding rather than shrinking the funnel. The balance sheet ($370M cash, no debt, 85%+ gross margin, FCF-positive) gives the company ample runway to wait out the disruption, and at $41 the stock already discounts the bad news. If revenue re-accelerates to double digits in 2027 and the tax noise normalizes, normalized EPS power could approach the prior $2.50–$3.00 range, supporting a $60–$65 re-rating (toward the high analyst target).

**Bear Case.** The decline is structural. GLP-1s permanently divert second-line surgical candidates, capping the TAM at exactly the moment the coding mess discourages surgeon participation, and the dedicated CPT code does not arrive until 2028. SG&A continues to grow faster than a shrinking top line, compressing the already-thin ~5-6% operating margin into sustained operating losses. The DOJ Anti-Kickback investigation and the securities/derivative litigation cluster produce a settlement. Forward estimates, already cut 55% in 90 days, get cut again. On $1.00–$1.30 of normalized EPS and a de-rated 20–25x multiple appropriate for a no-growth/declining medtech name, the stock is worth $25–$33, roughly 20-40% below current levels. The low analyst target of $39 sits just below the current price.

The weight of evidence — monotone deceleration into a guided decline, severe downward estimate revisions, escalating GLP-1 language from management itself, an unaccrued litigation cluster, and a "cheap" multiple resting on a one-time tax benefit — tilts toward the bear interpretation. The bull case requires a specific, undated catalyst (a new CPT code) plus a benign read on a structural threat that management has stopped dismissing.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalysts are the next earnings report (likely early August 2026), where any further guidance cut or quantification of the GLP-1 drag would pressure the stock, and developments on the dedicated CPT code (no defined timeline). The DOJ investigation and securities litigation are open-ended with no fixed resolution date. The absence of a single dated, high-probability catalyst weakens the short case.

**Timing window.** The operational drag is explicitly guided to persist through 2026, and BofA's reimbursement-resolution view extends to 2028. That is a long carry window for a thesis whose central downside (further estimate cuts) is gradual rather than a single cliff event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 16.8% of float with 2.6 days to cover. That is elevated but not extreme; the low days-to-cover means a positive surprise (a CPT code announcement, a litigation dismissal, an in-line print) could trigger a sharp covering rally. The February 26, 2026 single-day move (intraday high $86 from a $59 base) demonstrates this stock's capacity for violent short-covering spikes.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm the bearish thesis (113% near-term IV, 37.4% put skew, backwardation, P/C OI 1.26), but rich near-term IV means options-based downside expression carries high premium cost, and the elevated IV reflects that much bad news is already priced. Borrow is presumably tight given 16.8% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $25–$33 (roughly −20% to −40%); upside risk on a coding/litigation resolution or short squeeze is plausibly +40% or more toward the $60s. That asymmetry is unfavorable — below 2:1 against the short — given the squeeze profile and the amount of bad news already in the price.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis is bearish, but there is no specific dated catalyst, the carry window is long (through 2026, possibly to 2028), 16.8% SI with a demonstrated history of squeeze spikes creates meaningful upside risk, and much of the downside is already discounted after a ~72% decline. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. **A dedicated CPT code is issued** for the Inspire V procedure (or clear, durable resolution of the 64999/64582/−52-modifier confusion), evidenced by reaccelerating sequential revenue and stabilizing surgeon economics.
2. **GLP-1 impact is bounded** — management reverts to net-neutral or net-positive framing on GLP-1s, supported by stabilizing implant volumes, demonstrating the substitution effect is not permanently capping the TAM.
3. **Forward estimate revisions turn positive** for two consecutive quarters, with current-year EPS consensus stabilizing or rising rather than the −55% 90-day slide seen now.
4. **Litigation overhang clears** — favorable resolution or dismissal of the DOJ Anti-Kickback investigation and securities class actions without material settlement.

**Timeline.** Headwinds are guided to persist through 2026, with reimbursement resolution potentially extending to 2028. We would not revisit a constructive stance until at least two quarters of evidence (likely the Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 prints) show the coding air pocket reversing and GLP-1 erosion stabilizing.

## Conclusion

Inspire Medical Systems pairs a fortress balance sheet with a franchise in the middle of a sharp, partly self-inflicted growth slowdown. The headline trailing P/E is a value trap built on a one-time tax benefit; on forward earnings the stock trades at a multiple that does not support a business guided to decline. The coding crisis is theoretically resolvable but undated, the WISeR pilot is transient, and the GLP-1 threat is structural and now acknowledged by management. Layered on top is a litigation cluster — a DOJ Anti-Kickback CID, a live qui tam suit, and securities/derivative actions — all unaccrued and tied to the same Inspire V launch window.

The trajectory of our own assessment (HOLD → AVOID → AVOID, with growth prospects cut from 5.0 to 3.5) reflects deteriorating fundamentals, not merely a falling stock price. The price decline is justified by genuine deterioration: a guided revenue contraction, severe negative estimate revisions, and escalating substitution risk. There is no specific catalyst that makes the current price asymmetrically attractive, and the bull case depends on an undated coding fix plus a benign view of a structural threat. We maintain AVOID and do not regard the stock as an active short given the absence of a dated catalyst and meaningful squeeze risk.
