## Executive Summary

Glaukos is in the early innings of commercializing iDose TR, a category-creating intracameral drug delivery system for glaucoma differentiated by its up to three-year duration and FDA-approved re-administration in patients who maintain a healthy cornea. Q1 2026 revenue of $150.6M (+41% YoY) exceeded expectations, driven by iDose TR generating ~$54M in the quarter alone (annualizing >$200M). Management raised full-year guidance to $620-635M. The company's non-GAAP operating loss narrowed to just ($10.5M) on 84% gross margins, suggesting non-GAAP breakeven may be achievable within the next few quarters if current trends persist.

The investment case rests on iDose TR's ability to convert a $6B glaucoma drop market plagued by 40-60% patient non-compliance into a recurring procedural revenue stream. The January 2026 FDA approval for unlimited re-administration in patients with healthy corneas converts iDose TR from a one-time procedure into a recurring model, with each qualifying patient eligible for re-administration approximately every three years. With Epioxa (first FDA-approved epi-on CXL therapy) in controlled commercial launch, Glaukos has two differentiated products scaling concurrently, though the Photrexa-to-Epioxa transition may create transient revenue headwinds.

At $120.23, the stock trades at approximately 11.4x guided FY2026 revenue — expensive by traditional medtech standards but potentially justified given 40%+ growth, 84% gross margins, and a clear profitability inflection. Analyst consensus targets range from $136 to $170, and the unanimous buy consensus (13/13) reflects confidence in the iDose TR ramp. The primary risks are reimbursement complexity across Medicare Administrative Contractors, extended payment terms inflating near-term revenue ($109M in A/R with a materially increased allowance for credit losses), and the inherent execution challenges of scaling a category-creating product in U.S. ophthalmology.

## Company Overview

Glaukos Corporation develops and commercializes ophthalmic pharmaceutical and device therapies across three categories: glaucoma (83% of FY2025 revenue), corneal disorders (17%), and retinal disease (pipeline stage). Founded in 1998 and headquartered in San Clemente, California, the company pioneered micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) with the iStent in 2012 and is now scaling its drug delivery platform.

**Revenue Composition (FY2025: $507M):**
- U.S. Glaucoma: $298.6M (+50% YoY) — driven by iDose TR ramp
- International Glaucoma: $122.5M (+18% YoY)
- Corneal Health: $86.4M (+8% YoY)

The company employs approximately 1,094 people and manufactures primarily in the U.S. (San Clemente, CA and Burlington, MA), though it contracts with third-party manufacturers in the U.S. and Germany for certain drug formulations. U.S.-based manufacturing of core products provides meaningful, though not complete, insulation from tariff disruptions.

## Growth Analysis

**iDose TR — The Core Growth Engine:**

iDose TR addresses a fundamental structural problem: glaucoma patients must take daily eye drops indefinitely, and 40-60% fail to comply. iDose TR delivers travoprost continuously for up to 3 years via an implanted device. Three critical milestones have de-risked the growth trajectory:

1. **Unlimited re-administration approval (January 2026):** FDA approved re-administration in patients who maintain a healthy cornea, converting a one-time procedure into a recurring revenue stream approximately every three years per qualifying patient — though actual utilization cadence will depend on physician practice patterns, reimbursement dynamics, and real-world outcomes.
2. **Reimbursement buildout progressing:** As of March 31, 2026, five of seven MACs had formally published iDose TR professional fees, and management stated that reimbursement has become more timely and consistent across all MACs, supporting increased utilization.
3. **Clinical data supporting adoption:** Phase 3 showed sustained IOP reduction with a favorable safety profile.

At $54M in Q1 2026 revenue (annualizing >$200M), iDose TR is scaling faster than most medtech product launches. The addressable market in the U.S. alone includes ~3M glaucoma patients on drops.

**Epioxa — Second Growth Vector:**

Epioxa received FDA approval in October 2025 as the first FDA-approved, incision-free topical drug therapy that does not require removal of the corneal epithelium for keratoconus treatment. The company initiated a controlled commercial launch in March 2026 with approximately 65% U.S. population coverage via deployed O2N systems. The new J-code (J2789) becomes effective July 1, 2026, which should meaningfully accelerate reimbursement access. Management has noted potential transient headwinds from the Photrexa-to-Epioxa transition during 2026, and Q1 2026 corneal health revenue was $21.3M.

**Pipeline Optionality:**

The pipeline includes iDose TREX/TRIO (next-gen glaucoma), PRESERFLO MicroShunt (Phase 3), iStent infinite (label expansion for mild-moderate glaucoma), iLution blepharitis (Phase 2), and GLK-401 retinal (first-in-human complete). Over $800M invested in R&D since 2018 across 5 platforms.

**Estimate Revisions:**

Next-year (FY2027) EPS estimates have been revised upward +21.3% over 90 days, from $0.40 to $0.48. Current-year estimates improved +9.9%. Consensus still projects only modest profitability in FY2027, leaving room for further upside if iDose TR continues its current trajectory.

The stock is not cheap on any near-term metric. ## Competitive Landscape

**Glaucoma — Strengthening Position:**

iDose TR occupies a differentiated position as a long-duration, refillable intracameral drug delivery system with FDA-approved re-administration. Key competitive dynamics:

- **AbbVie's Durysta:** An FDA-approved intracameral bimatoprost implant for glaucoma that is biodegradable with more limited retreatment options. iDose TR is differentiated by its up to three-year duration and FDA-approved unlimited re-administration in qualifying patients, creating a durable competitive advantage in the sustained-release glaucoma implant category.
- **Alcon's Hydrus Microstent:** MIGS device that competes more directly with iStent. The Hydrus royalty agreement with Glaukos expired April 2025, removing a passive income stream.
- **Sight Sciences OMNI:** Canaloplasty/trabeculotomy device with a different mechanism. Faces its own reimbursement challenges.

The iStent franchise (non-iDose products) experienced "single-digit decline" in U.S. sales in FY2025 due to LCD coverage restrictions and CMS fee changes. This is an acknowledged headwind that iDose TR's growth is more than offsetting.

**Corneal Health — First-Mover Advantage:**

Glaukos has the only FDA-approved CXL therapy for keratoconus in the U.S. Epioxa's epi-on approach eliminates epithelium removal, reducing procedure time, pain, and recovery. Competition exists from compounding pharmacies and international providers, but regulatory barriers are substantial in the U.S. market.

**Competitive Trajectory:** Improving. The company is transitioning from a device company competing on procedural volumes (iStent) to a pharmaceutical/device hybrid with differentiated products (iDose TR, Epioxa) that face limited direct competition. This shift strengthens margins and defensibility.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement Complexity (HIGH):**
Coverage inconsistency remains the single most significant near-term risk to the growth trajectory. Further LCD restrictions (as seen with MIGS combo procedures in late 2024) could disrupt ordering patterns among both Medicare FFS and non-Medicare FFS patients.

**Extended Payment Terms / Revenue Quality (MODERATE):**
A/R growth from $61M to $109M in FY2025 (nearly 80% increase vs. 32% revenue growth) signals that extended terms are contributing to top-line outperformance. The allowance for credit losses increased from $1.1M to $8.4M, indicating management is recognizing elevated credit risk. If collection issues emerge or terms must be extended further to maintain growth, the revenue quality narrative weakens. Management uses an expected-loss methodology and will continue reassessing credit losses.

**Path to Profitability Not Guaranteed (MODERATE):**
While the trajectory is favorable, achieving non-GAAP breakeven within 2-3 quarters is an analyst estimate, not company guidance. The company has $80M+ in upcoming capex (Huntsville facility), plans continued heavy R&D investment ($151M in FY2025), and carries $63M in annual SBC dilution. Any slowdown in iDose TR adoption extends the profitability timeline. GAAP net loss actually widened slightly in Q1 2026 to ($19.8M) from ($18.1M) in Q1 2025.

**Manufacturing Concentration (LOW-MODERATE):**
Single-site manufacturing in San Clemente, CA creates natural disaster risk. The Huntsville facility will provide geographic diversification when operational.

**Tariff Insulation (POSITIVE):**
U.S.-based manufacturing of core products provides meaningful protection from the current tariff environment, though the company contracts with third-party manufacturers (including in Germany) for certain drug formulations and acknowledges tariffs could have some impact on the business or customers.

## Conclusion

Glaukos is a BUY at $120.23 based on: (1) a differentiated product (iDose TR) scaling at 40%+ growth with visible profitability inflection, (2) a second product launch (Epioxa) providing diversified growth potential, (3) 84% gross margins enabling rapid operating leverage, and (4) analyst consensus unanimously bullish with a $136-170 target range above the current price.

The valuation is demanding at 11.4x FY2026 revenue and requires continued execution on iDose TR commercialization. The options market signals elevated uncertainty around sustainability. However, Q1 2026 results demonstrate the thesis is progressing ahead of plan — a significant EPS beat (non-GAAP loss approximately 40% narrower than consensus), guidance raise, and accelerating revenue growth.

The specific reason the market may be underpricing this stock: consensus FY2027 EPS of $0.48 likely underestimates the operating leverage potential of an 84% gross margin business growing at 25%+. If revenue reaches $800M with even 18% non-GAAP operating margin, that's $2.45/share in non-GAAP EPS — implying the stock trades at ~49x a reasonable FY2027 non-GAAP EPS estimate that consensus has not yet fully modeled. For a differentiated high-growth medtech with limited direct competition and potentially recurring revenue characteristics, that multiple is defensible.

Risk is concentrated in reimbursement execution and the sustainability of extended payment terms. Position sizing should reflect this binary quality — the thesis works or it doesn't, and mid-course correction opportunities are limited given the stock's volatility.
