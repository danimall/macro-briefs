**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FTAI Aviation is an independent aircraft-engine maintenance and leasing platform built around three high-volume narrowbody engine families (CFM56-5B, CFM56-7B, V2500) that power the Boeing 737NG and Airbus A320ceo fleets. The investment story has two halves that pull in opposite directions. The operating business is excellent: Aerospace Products revenue grew 104% YoY to $743.8M in Q1 2026, total revenue rose 65% to $830.7M, and the company has lifted its share of the CFM56 aftermarket from roughly 5% to roughly 12% in two years against a structurally supply-constrained MRO market. The balance sheet and earnings-quality profile are the problem: $3.5B of unsecured debt sits against just $432M of equity, GAAP operating cash flow was negative $160M in Q1, reported earnings lean on non-recurring Russia insurance recoveries and related-party Partnership gains, and the company has missed consensus EPS in three of the last four quarters.

Layered on top is a governance and credibility cluster that deserves elevated scrutiny: a January 2025 short-seller campaign attacking the revenue model (which the Audit Committee investigated and dismissed as "without merit"), a June 2025 auditor change, and a March 2026 CFO replacement on which the stock fell. None of these individually proves anything. Together, they raise the bar an analyst should require before treating reported numbers at face value, particularly given a revenue model in which engines migrate between leasing equipment and inventory and asset-sale proceeds land in investing rather than operating cash flow.

At ~$247, the stock trades at roughly 49x trailing EPS of $4.60, well below the analyst target range of $319–$408. The disconnect between a euphoric sell-side target distribution and a 10.6% short float, three consecutive misses, and broadly falling near-term estimates captures the central tension: a real growth franchise on a fragile capital structure with unresolved earnings-quality questions. We rate FTAI a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting genuine two-sided risk rather than asymmetry favoring a long position.

## Company Overview

FTAI operates two reportable segments. **Aerospace Products** is the growth engine, built on a proprietary Maintenance, Repair & Exchange (MRE) model that rebuilds engines and modules at lower cost than OEM shop visits, then sells or exchanges them, supplemented by PMA (Parts Manufacturer Approval) parts developed through joint ventures. This segment now represents roughly 90% of consolidated revenue and runs MRE facilities in Montreal, Miami, Lisbon, and Orange (CA), plus 50% JV stakes in QuickTurn Europe (Rome) and Prime Engine Accessories (Bristol).

**Aviation Leasing** owns and manages 230 aviation assets (29 aircraft, 201 engines). This segment is shrinking on-balance-sheet by design as FTAI transfers assets into its Strategic Capital Initiative (SCI). The 2025 Partnership raised $2.0B of third-party equity, with FTAI acting as both Servicer and a 19% limited partner, a structure that lets FTAI scale aircraft acquisition using outside capital while earning servicing fees and profit participation. Leasing revenue fell to $86.9M from $137.0M as Seed Assets were sold into the Partnership; this is intentional asset-light pivoting, not deterioration.

The company is Cayman Islands-domiciled and NYC-headquartered. In May 2024, it internalized management by terminating its external agreement with Fortress affiliate FIG LLC, removing the 1.5% management fee and incentive allocations, a structural positive that eliminated a related-party drag. A newer optionality, FTAI Power, converts retired CFM56 engines into aeroderivative power turbines; a March 2026 JV with Jereh Group targets 100 units in 2027. The "AI data center power" framing around this vertical comes from investor commentary, not FTAI's own disclosures, and the unit and margin economics are third-party estimates.

## Financial Analysis

Growth is the clear strength. Q1 2026 total revenue was $830.7M versus $502.1M (up 65%), net income $137.9M versus $102.4M, and diluted EPS $1.29 versus $0.87. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA reached $325.6M (up 21%), with FY2025 company-selected Adjusted EBITDA of $1.19B, up from $862M in FY2024. The mix shift toward Aerospace Products carries strong segment economics and is the engine of the entire thesis.

The cash-flow presentation requires care. The negative $160M GAAP operating cash flow (versus -$26M in Q1 2025) would flag a naive screen as distress. It is not. The negative figure reflects a deliberate inventory build to $1.36B (from $1.19B) to feed the growing MRE business, combined with FTAI's non-standard classification under which engine teardown/rebuild working-capital flows run through operating activities while asset-sale proceeds land in investing activities (+$317M in Q1; $409.6M of total asset-sale proceeds). Management's preferred liquidity metric (operating CF plus finance-lease principal plus maintenance reserves) was +$152.6M. The presentation materially understates true cash generation, but it also means the headline operating cash flow line is not a usable quality check, which raises the importance of scrutinizing earnings quality through other channels.

The balance sheet is the central risk. FTAI carries $3.5B of unsecured Senior Notes ($1.0B at 5.5% due 2028, $497M at 7.88% due 2030, $700M at 7.0% due 2031, $800M at 7.0% due 2032, $497M at 5.88% due 2033) against just $431.7M of equity and $412M of cash. This thin-equity, debt-funded asset structure drives the distressed Altman Z-Score signal, a function of capital structure, not operating weakness. Maturities are well-laddered with nothing due within 12 months and only $228.8M of interest due in the next year, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. On April 24, 2026 it upsized its revolver to $2.025B and entered an additional material financing on April 30, providing substantial dry powder. The model nonetheless depends on continuous access to capital markets and asset-sale liquidity to fund both inventory growth and dividends. Several notes carry coupons above 7%, and operating cash flow has historically run below distributions, meaning the raised dividend ($0.45/quarter, up 50%, ~$165M/yr ordinary plus preferred) is effectively funded by asset sales and financing, sustainable only while the growth model performs.

Two earnings-quality flags warrant attention. First, FTAI continues to book sizable Russia insurance recoveries on stranded assets ($44.6M in Q1 2026, $30.1M in Q1 2025) as "Other income." These are non-recurring and flatter reported earnings. Second, gains on sales into the related-party 2025 Partnership (FTAI is both seller and 19% owner/Servicer) involve intercompany profit eliminations of roughly $10M/quarter and inherent valuation subjectivity. Neither is improper, but both reduce the cleanliness of headline net income.

## Growth Analysis

The structural tailwind is real and broad-based. OEM new-engine output shortfalls at CFM and Pratt, combined with elongated MRO turnaround times, keep 737NG and A320ceo fleets flying longer than retirement schedules implied, sustaining demand for FTAI's lower-cost MRE proposition. FTAI operates at roughly 55% of capacity per third-party investor estimates and targets 25% long-term share. The runway to fill existing capacity alone supports continued high growth without new facility investment.

Two optionalities sit on top of the base business. PMA adoption can materially lift EBITDA-per-module as more proprietary parts displace OEM parts in each rebuild. FTAI Power could become a meaningful incremental EBITDA line by 2027 if the Jereh JV scales to its 100-unit target, though this is early-stage, unproven at scale, and the more aggressive EBITDA estimates circulating are third-party rather than company-guided.

The estimate revision picture is mixed in a way that matters. Next-year consensus EPS has risen sharply, +46.5% over 90 days to $11.54 (per 2 covering analysts in the provided data), reflecting genuine confidence in the multi-year ramp. But near-term estimates are falling: current quarter -3.6% and current year -3.0% over 30 days, against three consecutive EPS misses (-15.2%, -16.0%, -10.8% in the last three reported quarters, after an 18.7% beat in the quarter ended June 2025). The pattern shows a business whose top line is compounding rapidly while quarterly EPS delivery has repeatedly fallen short of consensus, likely a function of inventory-build timing, margin concessions to land anchor customers, and the lumpiness of asset-sale gains. Strong forward revenue growth is not in doubt; consistent quarterly earnings conversion is.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$247 against trailing EPS of $4.60, FTAI trades at roughly 49x trailing earnings. On forward consensus, the picture compresses considerably: current-year EPS of $7.37 implies ~34x, and next-year EPS of $11.54 implies ~21x. The forward multiples are not extreme for a business growing revenue 65% YoY with a credible path to 25% aftermarket share, and the sharp upward revision to next-year estimates argues for evaluating on forward rather than trailing numbers. On a PEG basis against the multi-year growth trajectory, the stock is not obviously expensive.

The complication is that the forward estimates rest on a thin equity base, a non-standard cash presentation, and earnings flattered by non-recurring items. The market is pricing in a lot of growth on a highly-levered platform, which makes the valuation sensitive to any accounting, customer-concentration, or capital-markets shock. The analyst target distribution (low $319, mean $350.6, high $408) sits 29% to 65% above the current price, with 10 buy/strong-buy ratings and zero holds. That is an unusually one-sided sell-side stance against a 10.6% short float and a track record of three consecutive misses. When the most informed long-side analysts and a meaningful short base disagree this starkly, the outcome is genuinely two-sided rather than asymmetrically favorable.

The 434% cumulative TSR versus 182% for the peer group means the market has already rewarded the model handsomely. This raises the bar for continued outperformance and increases downside sensitivity to disappointment. We assign a below-average valuation score: the forward multiple is defensible, but the combination of leverage, earnings-quality flags, and the gap between sell-side targets and short positioning prevents this from being a clean mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

FTAI's competitive position is improving, which supports an above-average score on trajectory. The company is taking share in a structurally supply-constrained market. Its edge is a vertically integrated, modular CFM56-specialized model combined with PMA parts that improve unit economics as adoption grows, plus an asset-light SCI structure that lets it scale aircraft acquisition using third-party capital.

The competitive set is formidable: engine OEMs (CFM/GE/Safran, RTX/Pratt), independent MROs, and parts distributors, several with greater scale and lower cost of capital. FTAI's lower cost of capital is precisely what its thin-equity, high-coupon balance sheet does not provide, a structural disadvantage against OEM-backed competitors over a full cycle. The single-platform concentration in CFM56/V2500 is both the source of the share gains and the long-term vulnerability: eventual fleet retirement or an accelerated transition to LEAP/GTF engines would erode the aftermarket and the value of FTAI's inventory. The supply constraint that extends 737NG/A320ceo economic life is real today, but it is a multi-year window, not permanent.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the weakest dimension and the primary reason this is not a BUY.

**Leverage and thin equity.** The $3.5B/$432M debt-to-equity structure makes the business structurally dependent on continuous capital-markets and asset-sale access to fund inventory and dividends. A credit-market freeze or a decline in appraised asset values would constrain the model directly.

**Earnings-quality and credibility cluster.** The January 2025 short-seller reports challenged revenue recognition and the characterization of engine/module sales. The Audit Committee investigation concluded the allegations were without merit, but the surrounding facts (a complex revenue model, a non-standard cash-flow presentation, the June 2025 auditor change, the March 2026 CFO replacement, and earnings flattered by Russia insurance recoveries and related-party Partnership gains) collectively mean related-party transaction integrity and earnings quality deserve elevated scrutiny. The company states ICFR is effective with no material weaknesses.

**Customer concentration.** In Q1 2026, three customers were 28%, 17%, and 10% of Aerospace Products revenue, and one customer was 21% of total receivables. Concentration is rising as FTAI lands anchor airline customers, having "conceded some margin to secure a partnership with a major U.S. airline."

**SCI execution and conflicts.** FTAI is both Servicer and 19% LP of the 2025 Partnership, which is described as the primary buyer of all future on-lease 737NG/A320ceo aircraft. Expanded risk disclosures cover allocation conflicts, valuation subjectivity, litigation risk as Servicer, and Investment Company Act / Investment Advisers Act registration risk.

**Structural and tax risk.** Cayman incorporation limits shareholder remedies; the company was a PFIC in 2022–2023; and it is exposed to BEPS Pillar Two and Bermuda's new 15% tax, plus reliance on the Section 883 aircraft-income exemption.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated stress. Near-term ATM IV is 93.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 61.1% (a rich 32.1-point spread, 82nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (93.2% near-term versus 70.4% long-term). The near-week put/call volume ratio is elevated at 2.33 with a -26.4% call-heavy skew, while the 1-month tenor flips to a 7.33 put/call volume ratio: the market is hedging a near-term binary while still carrying meaningful call interest. The 4.4 days to cover on the short interest confirms institutional bearish positioning. The composite signal is consistent with a stock where high volatility and genuine two-sided disagreement are being priced, reinforcing the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

FTAI is the lowest-cost, fastest-scaling independent in a structurally supply-constrained CFM56/V2500 aftermarket, with a clear runway from ~12% to 25% share and only ~55% capacity utilization today. PMA adoption lifts EBITDA-per-module, the SCI structure scales the aircraft book using third-party capital, and FTAI Power adds a genuine incremental EBITDA optionality by 2027. The +46.5% revision to next-year EPS puts the forward multiple at ~21x for a business compounding revenue at 65%. If the growth model keeps performing and the accounting questions stay resolved, the stock re-rates toward the $319–$408 analyst range. Bull case ~$360.

### Bear Case

The thin equity base makes the model fragile to any capital-markets disruption. Three consecutive EPS misses, falling near-term estimates, earnings flattered by Russia insurance recoveries and related-party Partnership gains, a non-standard cash presentation that hides operating cash flow, an auditor change, and a CFO replacement together constitute a credibility overhang that could de-rate the multiple sharply on any negative surprise. A large director sale (~$61.5M by Tuchman) and no open-market insider buying offer no offsetting conviction signal. Customer concentration is rising as margin is conceded to anchor clients. Bear case ~$160 (roughly -35% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting genuine two-sided risk rather than a confident directional view.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the Q2 2026 report, expected late July/early August 2026), which will test whether quarterly EPS conversion finally meets the rising forward estimates and whether the SCI II launch (expected Q3 2026) proceeds.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of EPS meeting or beating consensus, breaking the three-quarter miss streak, with growth driven by core MRE economics rather than Russia insurance recoveries or Partnership gains.
- Concrete evidence the new auditor and CFO produce clean filings with no restatement or disagreement disclosures, retiring the credibility overhang.
- A successful SCI II raise and clear improvement in the equity base or leverage ratio that reduces capital-markets dependency.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Any restatement, material-weakness disclosure, or auditor/CFO disagreement surfacing in subsequent filings.
- Loss of a top-three Aerospace Products customer or a customer-concentration-driven revenue shortfall.
- A credit-market disruption or covenant pressure that constrains inventory funding or forces a dividend cut.
- Falling next-year estimates (reversal of the +46.5% revision) signaling the multi-year ramp is stalling.

## Conclusion

FTAI is a real, high-quality growth franchise gaining share in a structurally attractive aftermarket, and the forward valuation is defensible at ~21x next-year earnings for a business compounding revenue at 65%. That case is offset by a genuinely fragile capital structure, an unresolved earnings-quality and governance cluster, three consecutive EPS misses against falling near-term estimates, and a 10.6% short float that signals informed disagreement with the one-sided sell-side target range. The operating business deserves a high growth and competitive score; the balance sheet, management/governance turnover, and risk profile pull the overall assessment down to a balanced, two-sided situation. We rate FTAI a HOLD. The asymmetry favoring a long position is not present at the current price given the leverage and credibility questions. Existing holders with conviction in the model can retain; new capital should wait for either a cleaner earnings-quality picture or a more favorable entry.
