## Executive Summary

Gilead reported its Q2 2026 result on August 4 and absorbed the ~$11.5 billion acquired-IPR&D charge management had flagged in the Q1 10-Q. Reported EPS of -$6.75 came in 8.5% better than the -$7.38 consensus, the fourth consecutive quarterly beat. The stock rose 3.1% on the print and gave it all back the following session, closing $131.76 before slipping to $130.155. That two-sided reaction captures the situation: the accounting noise is understood and priced, and the real debate is about what Gilead earns on the other side of a government pricing reset that lands in 2028.

The forward arithmetic looks cheap. FY2027 consensus EPS of $9.62 is unchanged over 90 days and puts the stock at **13.5x**. Underlying FY2026 earnings power — consensus of -$0.75 plus the ~$9.17/share IPR&D charge — reconciles to roughly **$8.65**, close to the $8.65 figure consensus carried 90 days ago before the deals were quantified. The 108.7% "cut" to the current-year estimate is entirely the charge; operational expectations are flat to modestly higher, with next-quarter estimates revised **+7.0%** over 30 days. Biotech peers trade at a median trailing 24.6x (Amgen 24.6x, Vertex 26.4x). Gilead is at 19.2x trailing and 13.5x forward, with a 2.5% dividend yield covered at 42% of free cash flow.

The reason for the discount is specific and quantifiable in direction if not magnitude. Biktarvy generated $14,334M in FY2025 — **49.6% of product sales** — and was selected in January 2026 for government price determination effective 2028, with management stating the negotiated price will be "substantially lower." Gross-to-net deductions reached $19,953M in FY2025, **41% of gross product sales**, up 250 basis points, meaning Gilead now keeps 59 cents of every gross dollar and each additional point of erosion costs roughly $490M of net revenue. The December 2025 agreement with Washington bought a conditional three-year Section 232 tariff exclusion in exchange for MFN pricing in Medicaid on selected existing products and **MFN pricing on all future launched products**, which by construction applies to BIC/LEN, anito-cel and the Tubulis and Ouro assets Gilead just spent $12 billion to acquire. The migration vehicle carries the pricing concession with it. That is why 13.5x is not obviously a bargain.

Base case $144 (15x FY2027 consensus), bull $172, bear $100. Base-plus-dividend total return of roughly 13% against 23% downside is a hold, warranted on the evidence.

## Company Overview

Gilead is a single-reporting-segment biopharmaceutical company operating in more than 35 countries, with FY2025 total revenue of $29,443M and product sales of $28,915M. The revenue architecture is heavily weighted to one franchise and, within it, one molecule:

| Franchise | FY2025 sales | % of product sales | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| HIV | $20,752M | 71.8% | +6% |
| Liver Disease | $3,217M | 11.1% | +6% |
| Oncology (Cell Therapy + Trodelvy) | $3,236M | 11.2% | −2% |
| Veklury | $911M | 3.2% | −49% |
| Other | $799M | 2.8% | −10% |

Biktarvy at $14,334M is 48.7% of total revenue. Descovy added $2,758M (+31%). Approximately 90% of U.S. gross product sales flow through three wholesalers — Cardinal Health, Cencora and McKesson — which also represent roughly 60% of the accounts receivable balance at March 31, 2026.

Q1 2026 (the last quarter with published detail): revenue $6,960M (+4%), HIV $5,030M (+10%), Biktarvy $3,361M (+7%), Descovy $807M (+38%), Yeztugo $166M from nil, Trodelvy $402M (+37%), Livdelzi $133M from $40M, Cell Therapy $407M (−12%), Veklury $144M (−52%). Product gross margin improved to **79.2%** from 76.7% on the expiry of a royalty obligation and mix.

The defining recent corporate action is roughly $12 billion of upfront cash deployed across three single-asset acquisitions in a single quarter: Arcellx (closed April 2026, ~$7.1B for shares not already owned, plus a $5/share CVR contingent on ≥$6.0B cumulative anito-cel net sales through YE2029), Tubulis (~$3.2B upfront, up to $1.9B milestones), and Ouro Medicines (~$1.675B upfront, with Galapagos funding 50% and receiving 20–23% royalties on net sales). All three are accounted for as asset acquisitions because the lead asset represents substantially all of the fair value acquired, so the entire purchase price is expensed immediately. That mechanic is not a discretionary choice; it produces the FY2026 GAAP loss and leaves no new balance-sheet intangible to impair later.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality is the first thing to establish, because both the FY2025 and FY2026 headline numbers are misleading in opposite directions.**

FY2025 net income of $8,510M and diluted EPS of $6.78 compare against $480M and $0.38 in FY2024. Revenue grew 2%. The $8.03B swing came from: a $3.8B CymaBay acquired-IPR&D charge that did not repeat; IPR&D impairments of $590M in 2025 versus $4,180M in 2024 (Trodelvy/NSCLC); higher unrealized equity-security gains; and lower SG&A. It also came from tax: the FY2025 effective rate was **13.1%** versus 30.5% in FY2024, including a discrete ~$450M benefit from an October 2025 settlement with a tax authority. Q1 2026's effective rate was 21.7%. Normalizing FY2025 pre-tax income of $9,796M at 21.7% gives net income of roughly $7,670M and EPS of about **$6.11**, which puts the trailing multiple at 21.3x rather than 19.2x.

The profile reports a data inconsistency: trailing EPS of $6.78 alongside a trailing P/E of 17.92, which implies EPS of $7.27. Neither reconciles cleanly. Using the filed FY2025 diluted EPS of $6.78 against $130.155 gives 19.2x, and that is the figure I will use.

Q1 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.61 versus $1.04 also flatters. A $142M non-cash net gain from equity securities replaced a $426M loss — a $568M pre-tax swing inside a $931M pre-tax income increase. Excluding equity marks, pre-tax income rose roughly $363M. SG&A rose 15% to $1,451M, with selling and marketing up 19% to $898M on Yeztugo launch spend, plus $63M of G&A from a donation of equity securities to the officer-linked Gilead Foundation.

**Cash generation is the reliable series.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $10,019M against $563M of capex, giving free cash flow of $9,456M — a 32.1% FCF margin. OCF fell 7% year over year on inventory build and higher tax payments, including the final $1.3B TCJA transition-tax installment. That installment is now complete, a forward tailwind of roughly $1.3B per year to cash conversion. Q1 2026 OCF of $2,544M was up 45%, but Q1 is structurally distorted at Gilead — the filings state that strong second-half wholesaler purchasing typically produces first-quarter inventory drawdown — and the improvement was attributed to timing on tax and inventory. As a labeled sensitivity only, 4 × $2,544M = $10.2B, close to FY2025's actual. A normalized starting point is FY2025 FCF of $9.46B, plus roughly $1.3B of completed transition tax, less incremental cash interest on new debt.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt, net fell from $24,937M at year-end 2025 to $22,174M at March 31, 2026 after repaying $2.75B of March 2026 notes. Cash was $7,628M plus $997M of marketable debt securities, down from $3,044M — the portfolio was deliberately liquidated, with the remainder sold in April 2026, to fund the acquisitions. Gilead stockholders' equity was $23,515M, so total debt/equity is **0.94x** and total liabilities/equity 1.60x at year-end. A screened figure of 2.6x is not reproducible from the filed balance sheet on either definition.

Forward leverage is the real question. In April 2026 Gilead entered a one-year $4.7B senior unsecured term loan facility, drawing $1.1B, with the undrawn $3.6B committed to the Tubulis and Ouro closings. An 8-K filed May 20, 2026 carries the same item combination (1.01/2.03/8.01/9.01) as Gilead's prior senior-note issuances, so a term-out is the likely subject, but the filing was not retrievable and the amount, coupon and maturity are unverified. Building from March 31 balances plus $1.1B drawn, ~$7.1B of Arcellx cash, ~$4.0B for Tubulis and Gilead's ~$0.84B half of Ouro, net debt plausibly reaches the high-$20s billions by end-2026 before remaining-year cash generation. Against ~$9–10B of normalized annual FCF that is roughly 3x — serviceable, but a step-change from the historical 1.3–1.5x posture.

Two cost-of-capital observations. First, the legacy note stack is a genuine economic asset: senior notes with a carrying value of $21,080M had a fair value of $19,178M at March 31, reflecting coupons like the 1.20% 2027s, 1.65% 2030s and 2.80% 2050s issued into the 2020 rate environment. Second, replacing them is expensive in the current regime. New term-loan borrowing at SOFR plus margin implies something in the 4.5–5.5% range against 1.2–2.8% legacy coupons, and Gilead simultaneously gave up interest income on roughly $8.6B of liquidated cash and securities. The combined pre-tax drag is plausibly $500–700M annually, or $0.31–0.44 per share after tax — roughly 3–5% of FY2027 consensus, which consensus should already carry but which does not reverse.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.96 ("grey zone") is an artifact of asset turnover (0.499) and equity/liabilities (0.385), both mechanical consequences of carrying $16.4B of net intangibles and $8.3B of goodwill. A company producing $9.5B of FCF at 79% gross margin is not a solvency question. Prospectively, the leverage step-up deserves monitoring; retrospectively the Z-Score is not a distress signal.

**Score: Financial Health 6.5.** Excellent cash generation and gross margin, appropriate leverage for the business model, a covered and growing dividend — offset by the deployment of roughly 1.25 years of free cash flow in one quarter, full liquidation of the securities buffer, and an unverified new financing.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying growth rate is modest: revenue +2% in FY2025 and +4% in Q1 2026. Beneath that, the mix is bifurcating.

**Growing:** HIV +10% in Q1, driven by demand and realized price. Descovy +38%. Yeztugo (twice-yearly injectable lenacapavir PrEP, the first and only such option in the U.S., with a strong CDC guideline recommendation) delivered $166M in its first full quarter. Trodelvy +37%. Livdelzi/Lyvdelzi from seladelpar went from $40M to $133M year over year, the clearest evidence that the $3.8B CymaBay purchase is producing revenue.

**Shrinking:** Veklury −52%, structurally. Cell Therapy −12% in Q1 after −7% in FY2025, with the same explanation each period ("ongoing competitive headwinds"); Yescarta −14% in Q1 2026, Tecartus −15% in FY2025. Management's own 2026 outlook guides Cell Therapy lower again.

**The composition question inside HIV matters.** Biktarvy's growth is largely internal: Genvoya −28% and Odefsey −21% in Q1 2026, with the 10-K attributing Biktarvy growth to "patients switching from Genvoya and other Gilead HIV products." That is a defensive characteristic — physicians switch within the portfolio rather than out of it — but it also means the franchise is running out of internal share to harvest. Yeztugo and BIC/LEN are therefore the actual growth vectors, not adjacencies.

**The dated catalyst calendar over the next twelve months:**
- **BIC/LEN** (bictegravir 75mg + lenacapavir 50mg oral single tablet, for virologically suppressed adults): NDA accepted under priority review, **PDUFA August 27, 2026**. ARTISTRY-1/-2 met primary endpoints, showing non-inferiority to baseline multi-tablet regimens and to Biktarvy at Week 48. This is the mechanism by which the Biktarvy base migrates onto a molecule with 2040s-era exclusivity.
- **Anito-cel** (Arcellx) BLA in 4L+ relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma: **PDUFA December 23, 2026**.
- Two FDA decisions expected in 2026 on Trodelvy in first-line breast cancer.

Consensus FY2027 EPS of $9.62 against underlying FY2026 of roughly $8.65 implies about **11% earnings growth**, on flat-to-modest revenue growth plus margin and mix. The forward PEG works out to roughly 1.2. That describes a mid-single-digit-revenue franchise with operating leverage and a policy headwind arriving in 2028, not a growth company.

The Biktarvy loss-of-exclusivity extension deserves credit as a growth-preserving action: settlements with Lupin, Cipla and Laurus Labs set earliest U.S. generic entry at **April 1, 2036**, more than two years later than the prior December 2033 projection. It is a floor with acceleration provisions, not a guarantee, and the 10-K warns exclusivity "may be earlier than expected." A new Paragraph IV challenge from Cipla on Descovy via a 505(b)(2) NDA (January 2026, with Gilead suing in Delaware in February) is worth watching given Descovy is the fastest-growing legacy asset.

**Score: Growth Prospects 5.5.** Real launch traction on Yeztugo, Livdelzi and Trodelvy against a 2–4% consolidated revenue rate, a structurally shrinking cell therapy business, and a 2028 pricing reset on half the revenue base.

The Genmab and Alnylam figures are artifacts of one-time items and pre-scale earnings respectively; the useful comparison is Amgen — a similarly mature, dividend-paying large-cap with its own patent and policy exposure — at 24.6x trailing versus Gilead's 19.2x trailing and 13.5x forward.

Analyst targets: low $120, high $180, mean $157.15, median $160. The mean implies +20.7% from $130.155. Ratings are 6 strong buy / 16 buy / 7 hold / 0 sell, unchanged for three months.

**Does the discount represent a mispricing?** Partly. The specific, dated catalyst is the August 27 BIC/LEN decision, which, if approved, converts a 2036 exclusivity floor into a 2040s runway on the franchise that produces 72% of product sales. Markets typically pay up for that duration extension, and the stock is not pricing it — the September options tenor implies an 8.6% move at 29.9% IV against 27.0% realized, only a modest premium, consistent with approval being broadly expected after positive Phase 3 data.

But two structural facts blunt the mispricing argument, and both come from the filings. First, the December 2025 agreement with the administration commits Gilead to MFN pricing "in the U.S. market for future launched products." BIC/LEN is a future launched product. The vehicle intended to carry the franchise into the 2040s therefore launches into a contractually lower price regime, and the filings offer no quantification. Second, gross-to-net is already 41% of gross sales and moving in one direction: Medicare Part D redesign has taken 250bps; Biktarvy enters price determination for 2028; the Florida ADAP removed Biktarvy from its formulary in March 2026 with additional restrictions on Descovy access; enhanced ACA subsidies expired at end-2025, already reducing exchange enrollment; and OBBB Medicaid work requirements arrive in 2027. Each incremental point of gross-to-net is worth about $490M of net revenue.

So the cheapness has an identifiable structural cause rather than being an oversight. A 15x multiple on FY2027 consensus of $9.62 gives **$144**, roughly 10.6% above the current basis, plus a 2.5% yield. That is my base case, and it embeds a mild re-rating from 13.5x on BIC/LEN approval offset by the 2028 pricing discount. Reaching the $157 consensus mean requires 16.3x, which is achievable only if the 2028 reset proves milder than the language implies or if anito-cel and Trodelvy both deliver.

**Score: Valuation 6.0.** Genuinely inexpensive on forward earnings with a dated catalyst and yield support, but the discount is compensation for a real and unquantified pricing reset on half the revenue base, not an error.

## Competitive Landscape

**HIV: dominant, with the competitive frontier moved to long-acting.** Gilead's position is defended by switching gravity within its own portfolio and by the April 2036 Biktarvy settlement date. Yeztugo is described in the filings as the first and only twice-yearly PrEP option in the U.S., with a strong CDC recommendation and EC authorization for the same molecule as Yeytuo. First-full-quarter sales of $166M against a 19% increase in selling and marketing expense shows the launch is working and what it costs. The FDA clinical hold on GS-1720/GS-4182 — the weekly-oral candidates, including the WONDERS-1 and WONDERS-2 trials — is a setback for the post-Biktarvy oral franchise distinct from the progressing BIC/LEN program.

**Cell therapy: losing a category Gilead helped create.** Three consecutive periods of decline with an unchanged explanation, against industry conditions the filings identify: the January 2024 FDA class boxed warning on secondary T-cell malignancies across all approved CAR-Ts, and inadequate hospital reimbursement under DRG 018. REMS requirements were lifted in June 2025, a modest offset. A $3,881M net intangible for axicabtagene ciloleucel sits against a business declining 14% year over year, though management identified no impairment indicators in Q1 2026.

**Oncology: bought rather than built.** The internal record is poor — EVOKE-01 failed in January 2024 producing $4.2B of NSCLC impairments; ASCENT-07 missed its PFS primary endpoint in November 2025 in 1L HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer; STAR-221 and EDGE-Gastric with Arcus were discontinued at interim; bulevirtide was impaired $590M across two 2025 quarters. The response has been external: Arcellx, Tubulis ADCs, Kymera CDK2 degraders, and in vivo CAR programs via Interius (~$350M) and Shenzhen Pregene. The in vivo direction is strategically coherent and simultaneously erodes the value of Kite's ex vivo manufacturing and Kite Konnect logistics. Gilead is funding the technology that would obsolete its own installed base, which is the correct decision and a real write-down risk.

**Inflammation: a third leg built with shared risk.** Livdelzi's 3.3x growth, the LEO Pharma oral STAT6 partnership ($250M upfront), and the Ouro/Galapagos structure (Galapagos funds 50% of upfront and milestones, absorbs Ouro's operating assets and pre-registrational development costs, and receives 20–23% royalties; Gilead retains commercialization rights) all show capital efficiency where the company can get it. In the Ouro case, Gilead owns commercialization rights encumbered by a 20–23% royalty, not the assets outright.

Trajectory summary: HIV stable to improving on Yeztugo and prospectively BIC/LEN; oncology deteriorating organically with an expensive external fix whose economics are unproven. **Score: Competitive Position 6.0**, consistent with the prior reading rather than a further decline — the LOE extension and Yeztugo traction are genuine offsets to cell therapy erosion.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Biktarvy concentration into a 2028 price reset.** At 49.6% of product sales, Biktarvy is the single largest identifiable forward earnings risk. Management states the negotiated price will be "substantially lower" and may raise Medicaid rebates and reduce 340B ceiling prices. A wording discrepancy is unresolved: the FY2025 10-K describes Medicare negotiation effective 2028, while the Q1 2026 10-Q describes Maximum Fair Price determination under **Medicaid**. The 2028 date and the direction are consistent across filings; the program is not. Neither Gilead nor sell-side has quantified the magnitude.

**2. Tariffs.** The December 2025 agreement excludes Gilead branded products from Section 232 tariffs for three years, conditional on further U.S. manufacturing investment. In April 2026 a Proclamation imposed 100% tariffs on patented pharmaceuticals, expected effective in 2H 2026, and the 10-Q cautions there is no assurance the exemption persists. The $32B U.S. investment pledge through 2030 is now partly a compliance obligation rather than discretionary capex, reducing flexibility if returns disappoint.

**3. Unreserved litigation.** Roughly 23,000 active TDF product-liability plaintiffs with a federal bellwether trial set for **March 2027**; Aetna's opt-out trial **January 2027**; a Texas TMFPA qui tam trial **December 2026**; the HIV antitrust appeal argued October 2025 with Phase II stayed. Gilead states it has **no material accruals** for these matters, so any adverse outcome lands in P&L unreserved.

**4. Intangible impairment exposure.** Trodelvy net finite-lived intangible $7,296M, Yescarta $3,881M, indefinite-lived IPR&D $2,300M, plus **$638M of pre-commercial Trodelvy inventory for which the manufacturing process is not yet FDA-approved.** Roughly $13.5B is exposed to two 2026 first-line breast cancer decisions and to a declining CAR-T category. Trodelvy growing 37% with positive ASCENT-03/-04 readouts supports the carrying value in the base case; impairment would be non-cash and would not affect FCF, covenants or the dividend, but would signal real value destruction.

**5. Anito-cel's implied hurdle.** The $5/share CVR pays only on ≥$6.0B cumulative global net sales through YE2029. From a late-2026/2027 launch that implies roughly $2B per year at run-rate, against Gilead's *entire* current Cell Therapy franchise at about $1.8B annually and shrinking. The structure indicates the sellers negotiated for upside the buyer would not pay for outright. Consideration is cash — roughly 67.8M shares at $5 ≈ $339M if triggered — with **no share issuance in any of the three deals**; diluted shares were 1,254M in Q1 2026 and existing holders suffer no ownership dilution from the M&A.

**6. Leverage step-up into a higher-for-longer rate environment.** Net debt plausibly reaching the high-$20s billions by end-2026 against ~$9–10B FCF, with the securities buffer gone and refinancing costs several hundred basis points above the legacy stack.

**Score: Risk Factors 5.0.** A dense calendar of dated binaries, a policy reset of unknown magnitude on half of revenue, and material litigation carried without accrual.

## Management Quality

The trajectory in this dimension has been the sharpest deterioration in prior assessments (7.5 to 5.5), and the current evidence does not reverse it.

**Insider activity is one-directional.** Form 4 filings through August 3, 2026 show sales and tax-withholding dispositions only, with no open-market C-suite purchases. O'Day sold across April 28, June 1, July 1 and August 3 at $126–$133, ending at 592,133 shares. Dickinson (CFO) sold 3,000 shares monthly on the 15th at $140.96, $132.27, $125.40 and $129.93. Mercier exercised 25,000 options at a $66.64 strike on May 15 and sold ~28,000 shares the same day. On February 25, 2026 O'Day adopted a 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 747,975 shares through May 29, 2027 — more than his entire reported holding, so it contemplates future vesting.

The regularity is the signature of pre-committed diversification, and insider selling under a pre-set plan is weak evidence. Two things still matter: the plans were adopted in late February when the stock averaged $148.51 and are executing into $126–$133 without pause, and there is no offsetting open-market buying anywhere in the C-suite through the drawdown from the February high (the February repurchase-table average of $148.51 to the current $130.155 is roughly a 12% decline). Estimate revisions are flat rather than rising sharply, so the usual counterweight — insider selling contradicted by rapidly rising consensus — is absent here.

**Buyback discipline weakened as the stock cheapened.** Q1 2026 repurchases of $419M at an average $136.54 (January $125.79, February $148.51, March $145.81) versus $730M at $102.46 in Q1 2025, with $6,383M of authorization remaining. The stated aim is to "opportunistically reduce share count," and diluted shares went from 1,262M in 2022 to 1,254M in Q1 2026 — a 0.6% reduction over four years, essentially only offsetting equity compensation. The straightforward explanation is cash marshalling for the April acquisitions, which is defensible for one quarter, but the "opportunistic" language is not being borne out at scale.

**Compensation lacks a capital-efficiency test.** CEO total compensation was $28.44M in FY2025, up 20%; compensation actually paid $63.26M, up from $44.79M, which is mark-to-market inflation in a strong TSR year. Performance measures are Net Product Sales, Relative TSR and Adjusted Operating Income, with Adjusted EPS Growth added for 2026 grants. Using Net Product Sales is defensible for a company with deteriorating gross-to-net because it captures net pricing. The gap is that none of the measures tests returns on capital — precisely the discipline one would want at a company deploying $12B of upfront cash on pipeline assets in a single quarter, where acquired IPR&D charges are typically excluded from "adjusted" measures. Pay does track outcomes (CAP of $15.48M in 2023 when TSR was flat, indexed 5-year TSR of 255 versus a peer group at 125), and the combined Chairman/CEO structure concentrates authority.

**Credit where due:** the Lupin/Cipla/Laurus settlements extending Biktarvy exclusivity by more than two years were a high-value defensive action; the Galapagos 50/50 structure, the Arcellx CVR and the LEO partnership all show genuine risk-sharing; and the dividend has grown to $0.82 quarterly at a 42% FCF payout.

**Score: Management Quality 5.5**, held rather than lowered further.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $172 (+32%)

BIC/LEN is approved on August 27 and Gilead executes the same internal-switching playbook that built Biktarvy, migrating a $14B+ base onto a molecule with 2040s exclusivity and removing the central bear argument (the 2036 cliff) for a decade. Yeztugo compounds from $166M quarterly toward a franchise scale that lifts the HIV growth rate, aided by the CDC recommendation and the PEPFAR partnership targeting up to two million people over three years. Anito-cel is approved December 23 and, in multiple myeloma rather than lymphoma, arrests the cell therapy decline. The two Trodelvy first-line breast cancer decisions land positively, validating the $7.3B intangible and clearing the $638M pre-commercial inventory. The 2028 Biktarvy price determination proves milder than the risk-factor language implies. Free cash flow normalizes toward $10.5–11B with the transition tax gone, the term loan is refinanced, and the buyback resumes at scale against $6.4B of remaining authorization. At 17.9x FY2027 consensus — the analyst high case — that is $172.

### Bear Case — $100 (−23%)

The 2028 government price on Biktarvy proves severe, and because the December 2025 agreement prices *future launched products* at MFN levels, BIC/LEN provides duration without price protection. Gross-to-net moves past 43–44% as state ADAPs follow Florida in delisting Biktarvy, ACA enrollment continues falling, and Medicaid work requirements bite from 2027 — each point costing ~$490M of net revenue. The 100% pharmaceutical tariff takes effect in 2H 2026 and the conditional exemption does not hold. Cell therapy keeps declining and the $3.88B Yescarta intangible is impaired; anito-cel fails to clear its own $6.0B CVR hurdle, validating that the $7.1B was paid into a shrinking category. ## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The holding case rests on a dated catalyst sequence and a forward multiple 45% below the peer median, not on yield alone. ## Conclusion

Gilead is a high-quality cash generator — $9.46B of FY2025 free cash flow at a 32.1% margin, 79.2% product gross margin, a 2.5% dividend covered at 42% of FCF, and 0.94x debt to equity — trading at 13.5x FY2027 consensus against a peer median near 24.6x. Four consecutive earnings beats, including the August 4 print where the guided $11.5B charge came in 8.5% better than feared, and a three-week-away PDUFA on the most consequential item in the pipeline argue against dismissing the discount.

The discount nonetheless has a specific cause. Half of product sales sit in one molecule entering government price determination in 2028, gross-to-net has already reached 41% of gross sales and is being pressured from Medicare redesign, Medicaid MFN, 340B, state ADAP delistings and ACA enrollment simultaneously, and the December 2025 federal agreement prices every future launch — BIC/LEN included — at MFN levels. Management has committed roughly 1.25 years of free cash flow to three single-asset acquisitions in a quarter, in oncology, where the internal record includes EVOKE-01, ASCENT-07, the Arcus TIGIT discontinuations and two bulevirtide impairments, and where the compensation plan contains no returns-on-capital test. Insiders have sold continuously through the drawdown without a single offsetting purchase, and the buyback slowed as the stock cheapened.

Own it for the catalyst sequence and the yield; do not add here. The base case of $144 plus dividends is a ~13% total return against a $100 bear case, and the August 27 decision plus the first clean post-charge quarter should resolve enough of the pricing question to justify a firmer call in either direction.

**Overall Score: 5.8 / 10 — HOLD.**
