**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Hayward Holdings is the #2 North American pool equipment manufacturer with approximately 33% market share in what is effectively a three-player oligopoly. The company's business model is structurally attractive: 85% of North American residential revenue is aftermarket (non-discretionary repair/replacement), generating consistent 20%+ FCF margins and 48% gross margins. At $14.17 per share, HAYW trades at ~19x forward earnings and offers a 7.3% FCF yield on market cap.

The challenge is that growth is being manufactured through aggressive pricing rather than organic demand recovery. FY2025 delivered 6.7% revenue growth entirely from price (5.4%) and acquisitions (1.1%), with zero volume contribution. Q1 2026 showed pricing acceleration to 10.3% in North America — a direct tariff pass-through — with only 1.4% volume growth. In a macro environment where consumer spending is weakening and real wages are negative, the sustainability of double-digit price increases on residential pool equipment is uncertain.

The balance sheet carries $955M in term loan debt maturing May 2028, requiring refinancing in approximately 24 months. At current effective rates of ~6%, interest expense is manageable relative to cash flow, but the refinancing event introduces rate sensitivity. Combined with extreme customer concentration (Pool Corporation = 33% of revenue, 46% of receivables), the risk profile warrants patience until volume recovery becomes visible.

## Company Overview

Hayward Holdings designs and manufactures pool equipment, outdoor living products, and industrial flow control products. Founded in 1925, the company went public in 2021 following a period of private equity ownership, which explains the significant goodwill ($1.95B, 62% of total assets) and leverage on the balance sheet.

**Revenue Segments:**
- North America (~82-85%): Residential and commercial pool equipment + flow control
- Europe & Rest of World (~15-18%): Pool equipment in Europe, Middle East, Australia, Latin America

**Business Model Attributes:**
- Products include pumps, filters, heaters, sanitizers, LED lighting, IoT-enabled automation
- Replacement cycle of 8-11 years over a pool's 30+ year life creates recurring demand
- Non-discretionary products (chlorination, filtration, pumps) comprise ~51% of sales
- Distributor model (~80% through distribution) with Pool Corporation as dominant channel partner
- Low capital intensity (CapEx ~2.6% of revenue)
- 7 manufacturing facilities across US, Spain, and China

Margins are strong for an industrial equipment manufacturer, reflecting oligopolistic pricing power and operational efficiency. The Q1 2026 gross margin compression (46.5% vs 48.0%) warrants monitoring — if tariff costs cannot be fully passed through, gross margin degradation will be the leading indicator.

At 2.4x leverage, this is manageable assuming credit markets remain accessible, but the cost of new debt will likely be similar to or higher than the current ~6% effective rate given the macro environment.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth (FY2026-2027)
Consensus estimates: FY2026 EPS $0.86, FY2027 EPS $0.96. This implies ~26% EPS growth FY2026 (vs. FY2025 EPS of $0.68) and ~12% FY2027.

**Drivers:**
- Continued pricing pass-through (tariff-related)
- Modest volume recovery as aging installed base requires replacement
- Margin expansion from operating leverage
- Share repurchases ($440M remaining authorization)

**Constraints:**
- New pool construction suppressed by high mortgage rates
- Consumer spending weakening
- Price elasticity risk — 10%+ annual price increases on a $500-2,000 pump or heater may begin deferring purchases
- Volume was zero in FY2025 and only +1.4% in Q1 2026

### Long-Term Growth
The structural story — growing installed base of 25M pools, mandatory equipment replacement cycles, upgrade opportunities to energy-efficient/IoT products — supports a low-single-digit volume growth rate (2-4%) under normal conditions. Adding pricing power (2-3% in a normalized environment) suggests a sustainable mid-single-digit revenue growth business with operating leverage delivering high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS growth.

The disconnect is timing. Current macro headwinds (rates, consumer weakness, tariff uncertainty) are suppressing volume growth. The thesis requires patience until these headwinds abate.

### Estimate Revisions
Revisions are modestly positive: FY2026 EPS up +1.5% over 30 days and +1.4% over 90 days; FY2027 up +1.9% over 30 days. These are mild positive revisions — directionally supportive and not deteriorating, but not the magnitude (+30%+) that signals a strong acceleration.

## Competitive Landscape

The North American pool equipment market is a stable oligopoly: Pentair (#1), Hayward (#2, ~33%), and Fluidra form the dominant triad. Barriers to entry are substantial:

1. **Installed base lock-in**: Once a builder installs Hayward equipment, replacement parts and upgrades are sourced from the same ecosystem for 30+ years
2. **Channel relationships**: Distributor partnerships built over decades are difficult to replicate
3. **Regulatory moats**: DOE energy efficiency standards favor established players with engineering capabilities
4. **Product breadth**: Full-system solutions reduce builder switching incentives
5. **Brand + loyalty programs**: Totally Hayward program creates channel stickiness

**Vulnerabilities:**
- R&D spend at 2.4-2.6% of revenue is relatively low — if AI/IoT competitors emerge from adjacent markets, Hayward's technology cadence may lag
- Pool Corporation's bargaining power as a 33% customer is significant
- Chinese manufacturers attempting to enter at lower price points (acknowledged in 10-K)
- Counterfeit products noted as risk factor

The competitive position is stable but not improving. Market share has been relatively constant. The company is defending position through pricing and loyalty programs rather than gaining share through innovation or channel expansion.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Not Fully Priced)

1. **Consumer Demand Cliff (H2 2026):** Pool equipment — even aftermarket — can be deferred 6-12 months in stressed environments (e.g., running a pool pump past its optimal replacement date). The 10.3% Q1 pricing increase may accelerate deferrals.

2. **Tariff Escalation Risk:** Current pricing already embeds significant tariff pass-through. If tariffs escalate further or if the Supreme Court ruling is superseded, Hayward faces a choice: absorb costs (margin compression) or raise prices further (volume destruction). The company sources from China and uses steel/aluminum.

3. **Pool Corporation Concentration:** A single customer representing 33% of revenue and 46% of receivables is a structural risk that the market partially prices but perhaps not fully. POOL's own inventory decisions drove the 2023 revenue collapse (-24.5%).

4. **Refinancing Event (May 2028):** In a rising-rate environment, the $955M maturity is approaching the 18-month window where credit markets begin pricing the refinancing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV (42.5%) sits rich to historical volatility (30.7%) by 11.8 percentage points, with the term structure in backwardation (42.5% near vs. 31.9% far). This pattern typically indicates the market is pricing a specific near-term event or uncertainty that dissipates over time — likely related to the earnings beat/miss cadence and tariff policy evolution. The backwardation is not extreme but does suggest elevated uncertainty relative to the stock's typical volatility. Short interest at 5.5% is modest and not indicative of crowded positioning in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

- Pricing power sustains gross margins even as tariffs create input cost pressure
- Share buybacks ($440M authorization) provide EPS tailwind at current depressed prices

- Tariff escalation compresses margins if price increases face resistance
- Refinancing in 2027-2028 at higher rates increases interest expense by $15-25M annually
- EPS compressed to $0.70-0.75; 16x multiple = $11-12

## Conclusion

Hayward Holdings is a fundamentally sound business in an attractive industry structure, trading at a reasonable but not compelling valuation. The 7.3% FCF yield and ~16.5x forward P/E provide downside support, while the oligopolistic market position and 85% recurring aftermarket revenue create a durable earnings floor. The stock offers 20%+ upside to consensus targets if growth normalizes.

However, the growth trajectory is entirely price-dependent — a condition that cannot persist indefinitely in a weakening consumer environment without volume participation. The refinancing overhang, customer concentration, and evolving tariff regime create multiple paths to earnings disappointment. With analyst sentiment degrading (recent downgrades, one new Strong Sell) and volume recovery still unproven, the risk/reward does not justify adding exposure today. The appropriate posture is patient monitoring through the peak pool season (Q2-Q3 2026) to determine whether organic demand confirms the recovery thesis.
