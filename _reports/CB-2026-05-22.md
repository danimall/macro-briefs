## Executive Summary

Chubb Limited is the world's largest publicly traded P&C insurer by market capitalization ($127.7B), generating $54.8B in net premiums written across 54 countries with a P&C combined ratio of 85.7% in 2025. The company delivered record net income of $10.3B and record investment income of $6.5B in FY2025, with the current accident year combined ratio (excluding catastrophes) improving 120 basis points over two years to 81.9%.

At $329.25, the stock trades at approximately 12.2x forward EPS ($27.09 FY2026E) and 11.3x FY2027E ($29.23), a premium to the peer median of 9.1x that is warranted by Chubb's superior underwriting profitability, geographic breadth, and investment income trajectory. Consensus estimates are rising modestly (+1.1% over 90 days for FY2026, +1.2% for FY2027), and the investment income tailwind from reinvesting $12.8B annual operating cash flow at 5.0% market yields provides an earnings growth mechanism independent of pricing cycles.

The primary risks are catastrophe exposure (the California wildfires cost $1.47B in 2025), long-tail casualty reserve uncertainty amplified by social inflation, and the permanent step-up in effective tax rate from Bermuda's new corporate income tax. These are genuine and material, but largely priced given the stock's 5% discount to its 52-week high and the market's awareness of cat volatility.

## Company Overview

Chubb operates six business segments: North America Commercial P&C (38% of net premiums earned), Overseas General Insurance (27%), Life Insurance (14%), North America Personal P&C (13%), North America Agricultural Insurance (5%), and Global Reinsurance (3%). The company is one of a very limited number of global insurers that can serve multinational corporations through locally admitted paper in 54 countries simultaneously.

Key structural advantages include controlled distribution channels in Asia (captive agency, telemarketing), a $168.8B investment portfolio averaging A/A credit quality, and an underwriting culture built over two decades under CEO Evan Greenberg that consistently produces combined ratios well below industry averages.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**
- Personal auto insurance: +19.6% (pricing adequacy recovery)
- Life Insurance: +15.1% (+17.3% constant currency), driven by North Asia
- Chubb Benefits (worksite): +32.1%
- Overseas General Asia: +10.7%
- Overall constant-currency NPW growth: 7.0%

**Geographic Strategy:**
Asia now represents 36% of Overseas General premiums (up from 33% in 2023). Huatai consolidation in China (87.2% owned), Liberty Mutual Thailand acquisition, and acceleration in life insurance sales across North Asia represent a deliberate shift toward higher-growth markets. 40% of Chubb's workforce is in Asia.

**Investment Income Tailwind:**
The reinvestment dynamic provides an estimated 2-3% annual earnings growth independent of underwriting results. The 50bp gap between book yield (4.5%) and market yield on fixed maturities (5.0%) will narrow gradually as securities mature and are reinvested at prevailing rates. The pace of this rollover depends on contractual maturities and prepayment patterns across the portfolio; Chubb's available-for-sale fixed maturities include approximately $4.7B due within one year, $35.6B due in one to five years, and $30.7B in mortgage-backed securities with varying expected maturities. This embedded reinvestment tailwind supports continued investment income growth even if rates decline modestly.

**Growth Constraints:**
- Commercial property rates are declining (Large Risk and E&S brokerage property); management noted property and financial lines market conditions are soft or softening in Q1 2026
- Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax permanently reduced after-tax growth rate relative to the pre-2025 regime
- Consensus estimates rising at modest +1.1-1.2% pace over 90 days — positive but not accelerating

## Competitive Landscape

1. **Commercial lines:** Rate adequacy achieved across most casualty lines, with Chubb's scale enabling selective growth while maintaining underwriting discipline. The FY2025 CAY combined ratio improvement from 83.1% to 81.9% demonstrates pricing exceeding loss cost trends. However, Q1 2026 showed some line-specific softening: management noted property and financial lines conditions are soft or softening, and Chubb reduced exposure in Major Accounts and E&S shared/layered property. North America Commercial CAY ex-cat combined ratio was 81.8% in Q1 2026 vs. 81.3% in Q1 2025 — modestly worse sequentially, though still excellent.

2. **Personal lines (HNW):** The personal auto recovery (+19.6% growth) reflects years of accumulated rate adequacy now flowing through. The underlying personal lines CAY combined ratio improved from 78.5% to 72.3% ex-cats.

3. **Asia:** Few Western insurers have Chubb's distribution footprint in Asia. Huatai provides a platform in China that competitors cannot easily replicate; life insurance growth of 17.3% (constant currency) in North Asia leverages this structural advantage.

4. **Claims service:** Consistently cited as industry-leading, creating retention advantages and enabling premium pricing particularly in complex commercial accounts.

5. **Technology investment:** Chubb Marketplace digital platform and AI adoption improving efficiency, though not yet a quantified competitive moat.

The trajectory is broadly positive: geographic mix shifting toward higher-growth Asia, underlying loss ratios improving, and investment income growing. Selective softening in property and financial lines warrants monitoring but has not yet impaired overall growth or profitability.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Exposure (Material, Partially Priced):**
The 1-in-250 worldwide aggregate PML is $9.3B (12.6% of equity). California wildfires cost $1.47B in 2025. Climate change may increase frequency/severity. Chubb manages exposure through reinsurance ($1.75B U.S. retention) and portfolio management (reducing wildfire-prone areas), but this is an inherently volatile risk that can materially impact any single quarter.

**Long-Tail Casualty Reserves & Social Inflation:**
76% of loss reserves are casualty-related. Workers' compensation tail sensitivity alone swings reserves by ±$1.1B. Social inflation (higher jury verdicts, litigation funding) is an industry-wide issue affecting excess/umbrella lines. While Chubb demonstrated favorable prior period development of $1.13B in 2025, this could reverse if social inflation accelerates beyond pricing assumptions.

**Bermuda Tax / OECD Uncertainty:**
Effective tax rate rose from 5.4% (2023) to 18.6% (2025). The $1.14B deferred tax asset recognized in 2023 could be limited post-2026 per OECD Administrative Guidance issued January 2025. This risk depends on whether and how jurisdictions incorporate the guidance into law, timing of DTA reversals, and evolving tax mechanics. If the DTA reverses under unfavorable conditions, it would reduce reported equity and could impact 2027+ earnings.

**Key-Man Risk:**
CEO Evan Greenberg (71) has led Chubb since 2004. His underwriting philosophy permeates the organization, and his departure would create meaningful uncertainty around strategy continuity, particularly regarding the Asia expansion and underwriting discipline culture.

**China/Huatai Concentration:**
$8.5B in Chinese corporate bonds + $2.0B in Chinese government bonds + Huatai P&C operations in a competitive market dominated by state-owned insurers + geopolitical risk = meaningful concentration that could produce a writedown in an adverse scenario.

## Conclusion

Chubb warrants a BUY at $329.25. The stock trades at approximately 12.2x forward earnings, a premium to peers justified by structurally superior underwriting profitability, unmatched global diversification, and a $6.5B investment income engine with embedded growth. Estimates are rising, the most recent quarter delivered a clean operating beat, and the reinvestment of $12.8B annual operating cash flow at current market yields provides a growth mechanism partially independent of insurance pricing cycles.

The risks — catastrophe exposure, casualty reserve uncertainty, property/financial lines softening, and Bermuda tax headwinds — are genuine but largely priced at the current 5% discount to 52-week highs and balanced options positioning. With a 12-month base case of $365 (11% upside) and a risk/reward of 1.7:1, this is a well-compensated position in a high-quality compounder benefiting from structurally higher interest rates.

Position sizing: 2-3% given Financials sector concentration and catastrophe tail risk.
