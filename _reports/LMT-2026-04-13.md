## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest defense contractor, trading at $619 with a $143B market cap. The stock has rallied ~34% from its April 2025 lows near $410, driven by Middle East conflict escalation, a massive Pentagon FY2027 budget request ($2.2T proposed, 85 F-35s vs. 47 prior year), and a $4.7B PAC-3 MSE contract award. The backlog stands at $193.6B (+10% YoY), representing approximately 2.6 years of revenue at current run rates, with 37% expected to convert in the next 12 months.

At ~19.3x forward earnings on $31.95 consensus FY2027 EPS, LMT trades at a 46% discount to its A&D peer median P/E of 35.7x. This discount is partially warranted: classified program losses have consumed over $3B in the past two years, pension volatility creates significant GAAP noise, and Executive Order 14372 introduces a new risk to LMT's capital return framework (buybacks, dividends, executive comp). However, 4/4 quarterly earnings beats in FY2025 (average surprise +15.7%), accelerating revenue growth, $6.9B in free cash flow, and the most favorable defense procurement environment since the Reagan era collectively argue that the discount is excessive. The current estimate revision trajectory is stable to slightly positive (+1.9% on current-year EPS over 90 days), consistent with a business where fundamental demand vectors are strengthening even as program execution creates periodic margin headwinds.

The prior score trajectory shows a decline from 7.3 to 7.1, driven by Management Quality and Risk Factor downgrades related to the abrupt CFO transition and the emergence of EO 14372. The current analysis confirms those risks remain active. The CFO transition appears to have stabilized (Evan Scott is a 26-year veteran), but EO 14372 is now being codified legislatively by a bipartisan Hawley-Warren bill, making the capital return risk more concrete than it was in March. These risks prevent a maximum-conviction rating but do not invalidate the core thesis of a dominant franchise trading at a deep discount to peers during a structural demand upcycle.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin operates four segments:

- **Aeronautics** (40% of FY2025 revenue, $30.3B): Dominated by the F-35 Lightning II, which alone constitutes 27% of total company sales ($20.3B). Also includes C-130J, F-16, and classified programs.
- **Missiles and Fire Control** (19%, $14.5B): PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, PrSM, Javelin, and HIMARS-related systems. The fastest-growing segment operationally.
- **Rotary and Mission Systems** (23%, $17.3B): Black Hawk/Seahawk helicopters, Aegis Combat System, radar, and cyber solutions.
- **Space** (17%, $13.0B): Satellites, Trident II D5 Fleet Ballistic Missile, Orion spacecraft, and classified space systems.

The U.S. Government accounts for 72% of revenue (63% DoD, 9% other agencies), with international customers providing 28% (77% Foreign Military Sales through the U.S. Government, 23% Direct Commercial Sales). The customer base is extremely concentrated but uniquely creditworthy.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

FY2025 revenue of $75.0B grew 5.6% YoY, accelerating from 5.1% in FY2024 and 2.4% in FY2023. Management guided FY2026 to approximately $78.75B at midpoint, implying ~5% continued growth and 1.2% above consensus at the time of guidance. Key growth drivers:

- F-35 Lot 18/19 definitization (296 aircraft, September 2025)
- Munitions production ramp: JASSM/LRASM and PrSM line expansions
- PAC-3 MSE: $4.7B Army contract awarded April 2026
- International demand: FY2027 Pentagon budget requests 85 F-35s, nearly double the prior 47

Quarterly revenue progression in FY2025: $17.3B → $18.2B → $19.2B → $20.3B, with Q4 growing 9.1% YoY. This acceleration suggests the backlog is converting at an increasing rate.

### Profitability

GAAP net income declined for the second consecutive year ($6.9B → $5.3B → $5.0B), but this overstates the operational deterioration. Operating income recovered to $7.7B from $7.0B in 2024, when it was depressed by a $1.4B reach-forward loss on a classified MFC program. The primary GAAP drag is pension-related: non-service FAS pension expense swung from +$62M income in 2024 to -$874M expense in 2025, including a $479M noncash settlement charge from transferring $943M of pension obligations to insurance companies.

Segment-level margins tell the more important story:

| Segment | FY2025 Margin | FY2024 Margin | Change |
|---------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Aeronautics | 6.9% | 8.8% | -190bps |
| MFC | 13.8% | 3.3% | +1,050bps |
| RMS | 7.6% | 11.1% | -350bps |
| Space | 10.3% | 9.8% | +50bps |

MFC's recovery from 3.3% to 13.8% reflects the non-recurrence of the $1.4B classified loss that cratered 2024. Aeronautics margin compression reflects a new $950M reach-forward loss on a different classified program in Q2 2025, while RMS absorbed $665M in losses on CMHP and TUHP from sanctions-related disruptions to Turkish export permits.

### Free Cash Flow

FCF of $6.9B (9.2% margin) in FY2025 improved $1.6B YoY, driven by $8.6B cash from operations against $1.65B capex. FCF conversion significantly exceeds net income (FCF/NI of ~1.38x), indicating high-quality cash earnings despite the GAAP noise from pension and classified program charges. This conversion ratio is the strongest evidence that the GAAP income decline is misleading.

### Balance Sheet

Debt-to-equity of 7.9x is extreme in absolute terms but structurally characteristic of defense primes that lever against government-guaranteed cash flows. Total debt of $21.7B against $6.7B of equity, with $4.1B cash (up from $2.5B). The Altman Z-score of 1.53 (technically distress zone) is a false signal: the formula penalizes leverage without accounting for the unique creditworthiness of a company whose receivables are backed by the U.S. Treasury. LMT issued $2.0B in new senior unsecured notes in 2025, suggesting capital markets access remains fully intact.

The current ratio of 1.09 is adequate but thin. Management quantified 2025 tariff impact on cash flows at $485M, with "a substantial portion" expected to be recoverable through exclusions and cost recovery in pricing.

### Earnings Surprise Track Record

Four consecutive beats in FY2025 with an average surprise of +15.7%. The largest beat was Q4 at +29.0% ($7.43 actual vs. $5.76 estimate), suggesting consensus systematically underestimates the company's non-cash charge profile. This pattern increases confidence in forward estimates, particularly the FY2026 consensus of $29.93 and FY2027 of $31.95.

## Growth Analysis

Three structural growth vectors are converging:

**1. Procurement Upcycle.** The FY2027 Pentagon budget proposes a $2.2T top-line with 85 F-35s (up from 47), $2.9B for the AIM-260 missile, and accelerated Precision Strike Missile production. LMT has dominant positions across all of these line items. The FY2026 NDAA authorized $901B for defense, $8B above the President's request, indicating bipartisan support for elevated spending.

**2. International Demand.** Global defense budgets are increasing across NATO, the Middle East, and the Indo-Pacific. The F-35 now has commitments from 7 partner countries and 12 FMS customers. International revenue was 28% of FY2025, with FMS providing a multiplier on domestic procurement decisions (allies purchase the same platforms the U.S. buys).

**3. Munitions Replenishment.** Active conflict in the Middle East has created urgent demand for PAC-3, THAAD interceptors, JASSM/LRASM cruise missiles, and PrSM. The $4.7B PAC-3 MSE contract (April 2026) and the Precision Strike Missile framework agreement (March 2026) directly address this vector. MFC revenue is likely to accelerate from its FY2025 base of $14.5B.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS has risen +1.9% over 90 days to $29.93, with next-year at $31.95 (+2.2% over 90 days). These are modest positive revisions, and the beat/miss track record suggests upside risk to these figures.

**Backlog:** $193.6B (+10% YoY from $176.0B) provides approximately 2.6 years of revenue visibility. The 10% backlog growth rate outpaces the ~5% revenue growth rate, implying an extending revenue runway.

The forward P/E of 19.3x ($619 / $31.95 FY2027 consensus) is the most relevant metric. At a 46% discount to the A&D peer median, LMT is priced as though its growth prospects are substantially worse than peers, which the data contradicts. Boeing (87.5x) and Howmet (55.3x) inflate the peer median, but even against the more comparable Northrop Grumman (19.6x) and General Dynamics (21.8x), LMT trades at a discount despite having the largest backlog, the strongest franchise program (F-35), and the best FCF generation.

The discount is partially explained by: (1) classified program loss opacity, (2) pension volatility, (3) EO 14372 risk to capital returns, and (4) the 5-year TSR underperformance vs. peers (156.4% vs. 230.5%). The degree of discount appears excessive given the FCF trajectory, backlog growth, and demand environment.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $668, median $665, range $517–$770. At $619, the stock is 7.4% below the median target and 24.4% below the high target.

A re-rating to 21x FY2027 EPS ($31.95) yields ~$671. At 23x (still below the peer median of 35.7x), the stock reaches ~$735. The earnings beat pattern (+15.7% average surprise) suggests consensus estimates may be conservative, providing additional upside optionality.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT occupies a near-unassailable position in several defense verticals:

- **5th-Gen Tactical Aircraft:** Sole-source F-35 producer. Boeing's F/A-18 is a generation behind. No competing 5th-gen production program exists in the West. The F-35's installed base (1,293 delivered, 368 in backlog) creates decades of sustainment revenue.
- **Air & Missile Defense:** Duopoly with RTX (Raytheon) across PAC-3/THAAD. The current Middle East conflict validates the criticality of these systems.
- **Tactical Missiles:** JASSM/LRASM production is ramping aggressively. PrSM is replacing ATACMS as the Army's precision strike weapon. Both are LMT programs with limited competitive alternatives.
- **Strategic Deterrence:** Sole Trident II D5 provider, the backbone of the U.S. nuclear submarine-launched ballistic missile capability.

Anduril Industries and Kratos Defense present emerging competitive risks in autonomous systems and rapid prototyping. The Trump Administration's acquisition reform EOs favor speed and OTA contracts, potentially advantaging smaller, more agile competitors. However, these risks are concentrated at the program margins rather than in LMT's core franchise platforms, which have multi-decade production and sustainment timelines.

## Risk Assessment

### Classified Program Losses (HIGH)

The single most material risk. Cumulative reach-forward losses across classified programs exceed $3B over FY2024-2025. The Aeronautics classified program ($950M in FY2025 on top of $555M in FY2024) carries explicit management warning that "additional losses in future periods" may be required. The classified designation prevents any external assessment of the remaining risk envelope. A single-quarter loss comparable to the $1.4B MFC event in 2024 would reduce FY2026 EPS by approximately $5.50–6.00, converting what appears to be a well-supported earnings trajectory into a miss.

### Executive Order 14372 (MEDIUM-HIGH, RISING)

The "Prioritizing the Warfighter" EO targets underperforming contractors with restrictions on buybacks, dividends, and executive compensation. The bipartisan Hawley-Warren bill is now codifying this legislatively, making it more durable than a standalone executive order. LMT has multiple programs that could be classified as "underperforming" (classified losses, CMHP delays, F-35 sustainment cost issues). If applied to LMT, restrictions on the $3B+ annual capital return program would remove a core pillar of the equity thesis. DFARS implementation expected in 2026.

### F-35 Concentration (MEDIUM)

At 27% of total revenue, any disruption to the F-35 program — congressional cuts, sustainment cost challenges, technology refresh delays — would have outsized impact. The near-doubling of the procurement request (85 vs. 47) mitigates this risk in the near term but does not eliminate the structural concentration.

### Pension Volatility (MEDIUM)

FAS pension expense swung by $936M between FY2024 and FY2025. The $479M settlement charge in December 2025 will likely recur as LMT continues de-risking. These are noncash items that do not affect FCF, but they create GAAP earnings volatility that may compress the P/E multiple.

### Rare Earth Supply Chain (LOW-MEDIUM)

China's export controls on rare earth minerals affect critical defense inputs. LMT acknowledges potential adverse impacts and is developing mitigation plans, but the timeline and cost of alternatives remain uncertain.

### Tariff Exposure (LOW)

Management quantified the tariff impact at $485M on cash flows, with recovery expected through exclusions and cost adjustments. The long-term impact is expected to be immaterial given the cost-plus nature of many contracts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$700–735)

The defense procurement super-cycle is real and accelerating. The FY2027 budget with 85 F-35s, accelerated munitions production, and urgent Middle East replenishment demand validates the structural thesis. LMT re-rates from 19.3x to 22–23x forward earnings as classified program losses stabilize, pension de-risking completes, and EO 14372 implementation proves narrower than feared. $31.95 FY2027 EPS is beaten by the historical 15.7% average surprise, delivering ~$37 actual EPS. At 22x × $34 (conservative beat): ~$748. Backlog growth exceeding revenue growth sustains the multi-year revenue acceleration.

### Bear Case (~$500–530)

Another major classified program loss ($1B+) in FY2026 compresses margins and destroys earnings confidence. EO 14372 is implemented broadly, restricting LMT's $3B+ annual capital return program, forcing a re-rating to utility-like multiples. F-35 sustainment costs trigger congressional scrutiny, slowing procurement. Pension charges continue to distort GAAP results. The stock re-rates to 16x × $29.93 = ~$479, or with further estimate cuts, $500–530.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $700** — 21.5x FY2027 EPS of $31.95 plus modest upside from the earnings beat pattern. This represents a partial closure of the peer discount from 46% to approximately 40%, justified by improving demand fundamentals and stabilizing segment margins.

**Bull case target: $735** — 23x FY2027E, consistent with earning above consensus and gradual peer re-rating as EO 14372 proves manageable.

**Bear case target: $515** — 16.5x FY2027E ($31.95), reflecting another major classified loss and/or capital return restrictions. This is 17% below the current price.

**Upside/downside from current ($619):** Base case +13.1%, bull case +18.7%, bear case -16.8%. Risk/reward of approximately 1:1 to base, improving to 1.1:1 at bull.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with key catalysts:
- Q1 FY2026 earnings (anticipated late April 2026) — first look at FY2026 execution and margin trajectory
- EO 14372 DFARS implementation (expected mid-2026) — clarifies scope and applicability
- FY2027 budget authorization (late 2026) — confirms procurement volumes

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. A single-quarter classified program reach-forward loss exceeding $800M, which would indicate the loss problem is worsening rather than stabilizing
2. EO 14372 implementation restricting LMT-specific buybacks or dividends, confirmed by DoD notification
3. F-35 procurement request in the FY2028 budget declining below 70 units (reversing the demand upcycle thesis)

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin trades at a significant discount to A&D peers despite having the largest backlog in the industry, the strongest franchise program, and the most consistent FCF generation during the most favorable demand environment in decades. The 19.3x forward multiple implies a growth and risk profile meaningfully worse than what the data supports.

The risks are real: classified program losses are opaque and recurring, EO 14372 could restructure the capital return framework, and pension volatility distorts GAAP results. These justify a discount to peers but not the current 46% discount.

The rating is BUY with a $700 base case target (13% upside). Position sizing should reflect the classified program loss tail risk — full position is appropriate but maximum conviction is not warranted until EO 14372 implementation scope is clarified and at least two quarters of FY2026 earnings confirm that classified losses have stabilized.
