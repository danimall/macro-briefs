**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ormat is the world's leading vertically integrated geothermal company, controlling the full value chain from resource exploration and proprietary Organic Rankine Cycle equipment manufacturing through plant ownership and operation. The core asset is a fleet of 35 geothermal complexes (1,340 MW) that run at an ~84% capacity factor versus 20-30% for wind and solar, delivering firm, dispatchable, 24/7 baseload renewable power under long-term PPAs (94% fixed-price, ~14-year weighted-average remaining term). That firm-power profile is now scarce: surging hyperscaler electricity demand is repricing PPAs, with Ormat signing new/renewal contracts above $100/MWh versus $60-80/MWh over the prior five years. The Google (up to 150 MW via NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff) and Switch (13 MW, 20-year) agreements are the demand thesis materializing.

The tension is between a real structural tailwind and a set of financial realities that the market has been repricing since June. The stock has fallen from a $146 high (June 3) to $99, a ~32% drawdown, despite a strong Q1 2026 (operating income +57.6% YoY, Adjusted EBITDA +29.7% YoY). At $99 the shares trade at ~49x trailing EPS of $2.02 and ~44x current-year consensus of $2.26, a substantial premium to a utility peer median around 22x. Reported net income has been flat for three years ($124.4M → $123.7M → $123.9M) even as revenue grew from $734M to $989.5M, because 2025 saw sharp gross-margin compression (Electricity margin fell 610bps to 28.5%) and because roughly half of net income is monetized tax benefits ($66.7M in 2025) rather than pure operating profit. Free cash flow was -$284.7M on $619.8M of growth capex.

We rate ORA a HOLD with a growth bias. The competitive moat and the data-center firm-power narrative are genuine and improving, and the Q1 operating rebound suggests the 2025 margin pressure was a capex-timing/curtailment issue rather than structural erosion. But the valuation is stretched on trailing and current-year earnings, near-quarter estimates were cut 32% over 90 days, FCF is deeply negative, and insiders sold heavily 25-30% above today's price. We would not deploy fresh capital at this level into a Utilities-sector name where selection alpha has historically been weak. We would move to BUY on confirmation of a durable margin recovery and a wider margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Ormat operates three segments:

- **Electricity (70.1% of FY2025 revenue, $693.9M):** 35 plants/complexes, 1,340 MW capacity, geothermal 81.3% of capacity. Sold under long-term, mostly fixed-price PPAs. This is the annuity core, and its earnings quality is highest, though it is where 2025 margin compression concentrated. Electricity revenue declined 1.2% in 2025 on US curtailments and Puna wellfield issues.
- **Product (21.9%, $216.7M):** Design, manufacture, and sale of OEC/binary units plus EPC services to third parties, 95% international (heavily New Zealand). Lumpy percentage-of-completion revenue, ~$352M backlog. Grew +55% in 2025 but at compressed margins amid intensifying competition (Turboden/Mitsubishi, Baker Hughes, Exergy, Kaishan, Fuji).
- **Energy Storage (8.0%, $79M):** 19 US grid-scale BESS projects (415 MW/1,038 MWh), mostly merchant revenue transitioning toward tolling. Grew +109% but remains a small, capital-intensive, competitive business with merchant price volatility.

Geographically, international plants (Kenya, Guatemala, Honduras, Guadeloupe, Indonesia) generate outsized profitability, roughly 39% of gross profit and 49% of net income on ~20% of total revenue, because they are newer and lower-cost. That concentration cuts both ways: it drives returns and it is the source of the highest payment/political risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is real; headline earnings are flat.** Revenue moved $734M (2022) → $829M → $880M → $989.5M (2025, +12.5%), driven by Product and Storage. Net income to stockholders was essentially unchanged at $123.9M (diluted EPS $2.02, versus $2.08 in 2023). The disconnect is margin.

**Margin compression is the key 2025 story.** Total gross margin fell to 27.6% from 31.0% (2024) and 31.8% (2023). Electricity gross margin dropped 610bps to 28.5% from 34.6%, driven by ~$20M higher depreciation (from the capex surge, front-loaded before new-capacity revenue ramps), ~$8.3M higher property tax, and ~$18.6M of US curtailment revenue loss (McGinness, Mammoth, Tungsten, Dixie Valley). The critical read for scoring: is this structural or timing? The Q1 2026 print argues heavily for timing/ramp, consistent with new capacity coming online at the higher PPA prices now available.

**The balance sheet is utility-appropriate, not distressed.** Total long-term debt ~$2.66B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.14 ("distress zone") is a false alarm; it applies a manufacturing model to a capital-intensive utility. Covenant metrics are comfortable: net-debt/EBITDA 4.36x versus a 6x covenant, equity/assets 42.9% versus a 25% floor, 84.3% fixed-rate debt. Cash $147.4M plus $388.9M undrawn credit. The 2026 funding need (~$675M capex + $303.7M debt repayment) is met through operating cash flow, project/tax-equity financing, and new debt. The one refinancing item to watch is the $476.4M convertible notes due July 2027.

**Free cash flow is deeply negative by design.** Operating cash flow was $335.1M (down from $410.9M) against $619.8M of capex, producing FCF of -$284.7M. This is deliberate growth investment, not distress, but in a higher-for-longer rate environment it means continuous reliance on capital markets and rising financing cost on the negative-FCF model, a structural headwind shared across capital-intensive renewable developers.

**Earnings quality carries a policy dependence.** "Income attributable to sale of tax benefits" was $66.7M in 2025 against $105.7M pretax income and $123.9M net income; a large share of profitability is monetized PTCs/ITCs. This stream is durable through 2033 under OBBBA (which preserved 100% geothermal/storage credits), but it is policy-dependent and a sensitivity a casual screen misses. It also tempers how much weight to place on the reported EPS multiple.

The earnings surprise record is strong (4 consecutive beats, including a 40.3% beat in Q1 2026), which supports confidence in the current-year figure. But the estimate-revision picture is mixed and important: current-quarter consensus was cut 32.2% over 90 days (to $0.25) and next-quarter 36.0% (to $0.23), while current-year ($2.26) and next-year ($2.52) held roughly flat. The near-term cuts reflect the lumpy, seasonally weak Q2/Q3 quarters (Product timing, curtailment seasonality); the full-year stability is the more relevant signal. This is a business with a strong Q1 and soft near-term quarters, not the +30% revision velocity that flags a clean inflection buy.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs, with varying durability.

**1. Data-center firm-power demand (highest conviction).** Hyperscaler load growth is repricing firm, dispatchable power, and geothermal's 84% capacity factor is precisely the attribute intermittent renewables lack. The Google PPA (deliveries 2028-2030, subject to Nevada PUC approval in H2 2026) and Switch (Q1 2030 start) are concrete evidence. Rising PPA pricing above $100/MWh is the margin catalyst that would reverse the 2025 Electricity compression as legacy contracts roll and new capacity comes online. This aligns with the broader confirmation of data-center load growth now visible across regulated-utility hard data and power-generation names.

**2. Capacity expansion.** New COD at Ijen (Indonesia), Lower Rio and Arrowleaf storage, plus M&A (Blue Mountain 20 MW $88.7M; Hoku solar+storage 30 MW Hawaii $80.5M). The capex surge that pressured 2025 margins should convert to revenue and EBITDA over 2026-2028.

**3. Next-generation geothermal (EGS) optionality (free call option).** Partnerships with SLB/Schlumberger and a $25M investment in Sage Geosystems, plus the New Ventures portfolio (LAVA, TerraWave, RepAir, Novocycle), position Ormat to co-opt rather than be disrupted by venture-backed EGS entrants using oil-and-gas drilling techniques. This expands the addressable resource beyond natural hydrothermal but remains commercially unproven.

The offsets: Electricity revenue declined in 2025 on curtailments and wellfield cooling; Product and Storage carry lower and more volatile margins; and reservoir decline is a structural governor on the core annuity. Net-net, growth prospects are solid and improving but not the clean high-velocity inflection that would justify a premium multiple standalone.

## Valuation Assessment

At $99, ORA trades at a premium of roughly 2x to the utility peer median P/E of ~22.4x (POR 22.4, NJR 16.1, BKH 18.5, SWX 26.7, TXNM 42.1). The dividend yield of ~0.5% is far below the peer median 3.4%, reflecting the ~20% payout / reinvestment-first policy, so there is little absolute yield support.

The case for a premium: Ormat is a growth developer with a differentiated firm-power product, mid-single-digit-to-double-digit revenue growth, and PPA repricing tailwinds that regulated gas/electric peers do not have. On next-year consensus of $2.52 the multiple compresses toward ~39x, still rich. Even adjusting for the policy-driven tax-benefit component and normalizing toward the Q1 operating trajectory, the valuation prices in substantial future margin recovery and capacity conversion. The trailing multiple is not meaningfully distorted by a cyclical trough; this is a slow-compounding asset base, not a semiconductor-style inflection, so the cycle-adjustment logic that would rescue a 49x trailing multiple does not fully apply here.

The peer set and analyst targets frame the range. Consensus targets run $115 low / $132.5 median / $152 high, all above the current $99, and the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading (targets have not yet caught the recent decline, and one strong-sell rating persists). On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, we would treat those targets as floors. Here, with a >40x multiple and near-term estimate cuts, the targets are better read as optimistic anchors than as guaranteed floors; the guardrail against extrapolating consensus on premium-multiple names applies.

We also weight the sector-selection evidence: Utilities BUYs have historically produced negative alpha, and a compressed-looking valuation ("cheap" screens) has been anti-predictive. ORA is not cheap on any conventional metric, so there is no value-trap risk here, but the premium requires the growth and margin recovery to deliver, and the current price does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a high valuation score. We score valuation 5.0: fair-to-slightly-rich, neither a clear bargain nor an obvious overvaluation, with the recent de-rating having removed the most acute excess.

## Competitive Landscape

Ormat's moat is vertical integration and firm baseload economics. It manufactures its own OEC/binary units (182 patents), giving cost, quality, and timing control that pure-play developers lack. The 84% capacity factor and dispatchable profile are structurally advantaged versus intermittent renewables precisely as grid operators and data centers pay up for firm power. The trajectory is improving: PPA pricing is re-rating upward, and Ormat is winning the marquee data-center-linked contracts.

Competitive pressures are real but manageable. In Product, intensifying competition (Turboden/Mitsubishi, Baker Hughes, Exergy, Kaishan, Fuji) has already compressed operating margins, a segment-level negative. In Storage, Ormat is a small player in a capital-driven, merchant-exposed market. In next-gen geothermal, venture-backed EGS entrants are a potential disruptor, which Ormat is addressing through co-investment (SLB, Sage) rather than head-on competition. On balance, the core Electricity moat is widening while the ancillary segments face competitive headwinds, a net improving-to-stable competitive position, scored 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Geological/wellfield risk (the defining operational risk).** Multiple plants show resource cooling: McGinness Hills (~5°F/yr decline, a top-2 revenue plant), Don A. Campbell (3-4°F/yr), Brawley ($7.2M impairment in 2025), Puna (temporary reduction), Sarulla (ongoing remediation). Reservoir decline is unpredictable and directly threatens the annuity's output and margins.

**Customer/project concentration and emerging-market payment risk.** SCPPA 17.8%, NV Energy 13.8%, KPLC (Kenya) 11.9% of revenue; top two plants (McGinness + Olkaria III) 20.7%. KPLC is $29.5M overdue and simultaneously seeking more favorable PPA rates, in the business's most profitable region (~49% of net income comes from international ops). ENEE (Honduras) $20.3M overdue. Deterioration here would hit disproportionately.

**Israel concentration.** Senior management and the main Product manufacturing base sit ~26 miles from Gaza; Middle East conflict directly threatens the Product segment and supply chain.

**Policy dependence.** OBBBA added FEOC restrictions on Chinese content (a storage supply-chain headwind, partly mitigated by pre-2026 battery safe-harboring). A new risk factor: BLM halting solar-PV permits on federal lands where Ormat has applied.

**Financing/refinancing.** Negative FCF requires continuous capital-market access; the $476.4M convertible notes due July 2027 are the near-term refinancing item, and higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the growth model.

**Regulatory approval binaries.** The Google PPA is contingent on Nevada PUC approval in H2 2026, a near-term catalyst that is not yet certain.

Because concentration (KPLC, top plants), wellfield decline, and policy/tax dependence are specific, quantified, and partly reversing (KPLC rate negotiation, resource cooling), and because the valuation prices in a clean recovery that is not guaranteed, we score risk factors 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV rose to 47.8% from 38.1% over the past month while the stock fell 20.1%, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 47.8% > 6-month 37.8%), signaling an imminent event: the upcoming earnings print and the Nevada PUC approval window. IV is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 32.4% (65th percentile within the chain), so options are expensive; the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.3%. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (-0.4%) but steepens to -4.5% at six months, indicating growing demand for downside protection into H2 2026, consistent with the regulatory-approval and refinancing overhangs. The P/C OI ratio jumping to 23.90 is dominated by a few far-dated open-interest lines and should be read as noise rather than a directional signal. Net read: the market is bracing for a volatile earnings/regulatory window, which reinforces patience over fresh deployment.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-152).** The Q1 operating rebound proves the 2025 margin compression was capex-timing and curtailment, not structural erosion. New capacity comes online at PPA prices above $100/MWh, Electricity margins re-rate back toward 34%, and the data-center firm-power thesis compounds through the Google and Switch deals plus follow-on hyperscaler contracts. Nevada PUC approves the Google tariff in H2 2026. EGS optionality (SLB/Sage) opens a much larger addressable market. On a re-rated ~$3.00+ earnings power over 2027-2028 and continued firm-power scarcity premium, the shares reclaim the low-$130s to low-$150s.

**Bear Case ($75-85).** Wellfield cooling accelerates at McGinness and other top plants, structurally capping Electricity output and margins. KPLC forces rate concessions and stretches receivables in the most profitable region. Product margins keep compressing on competition. The negative-FCF model meets a higher-for-longer rate path and a tougher 2027 convertible refinancing. Estimate cuts broaden from near-term quarters into the full year, and the ~44x current-year multiple compresses toward the peer median as the growth premium unwinds, taking the stock into the high-$70s to mid-$80s (near the 52-week low of $84).

The insider signal cuts toward caution: the CFO and multiple directors sold heavily at $122-138 in May 2026 (one director exiting entirely), 25-30% above today's price, with no offsetting open-market buying. These were largely option/RSU-driven and mechanically routine, but there was no dip-buying at $99 into a strong operating trajectory.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings print and the H2 2026 Nevada PUC ruling on the Google tariff.**

This is a growth-hold: we see a credible 12-month appreciation path driven by margin recovery and firm-power PPA repricing, but the current price, near-term estimate cuts, negative FCF, and event-driven options stress do not offer the asymmetric entry that would justify a BUY at $99.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A second consecutive quarter confirming Electricity gross margin recovery back above ~32%, demonstrating the 2025 compression was timing not structural.
- Nevada PUC approval of the Google/NV Energy Clean Transition Tariff, converting the 150 MW into bankable revenue.
- A pullback toward the mid-to-high $80s (near the 52-week low) that compresses the current-year multiple below ~35x and widens the margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of accelerating wellfield decline at a top-2 revenue plant (McGinness/Olkaria) that materially reduces output guidance.
- KPLC rate concessions or a further material step-up in overdue international receivables that impairs the most profitable segment.
- Full-year consensus EPS cut materially (current year breaking below ~$2.00) rather than just the near-term quarters, signaling the softness is structural.
- A failed or materially dilutive resolution of the July 2027 convertible refinancing.

## Conclusion

Ormat is an advantaged geothermal leader with a firm-power product that is scarce and repricing higher as data-center demand accelerates. The Q1 2026 operating rebound is encouraging evidence that the 2025 margin compression was a capex-timing and curtailment problem rather than structural erosion, and the Google/Switch contracts make the demand thesis concrete. Against that, the equity trades at a large premium to the utility peer median, with flat headline net income, roughly half of profit from policy-driven tax-benefit monetization, deeply negative FCF, near-term estimate cuts, insider selling well above the current price, and a regulatory-approval binary in H2 2026.

The ~32% decline from the June high has removed the most acute valuation excess and improved the risk/reward, but not to the point of an asymmetric buy in a sector where selection has historically added little alpha. We rate ORA a HOLD with a growth bias, and we would upgrade to BUY on a confirmed margin-recovery print, Nevada PUC approval, or a further pullback toward the mid-to-high $80s.
