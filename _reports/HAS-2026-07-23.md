**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hasbro has become a trading-card franchise with a legacy toy business attached. FY25 headline financials show a net loss of $322.4M (−$2.30 diluted EPS), but that number is entirely a $1,021.9M non-cash goodwill impairment in the Consumer Products segment. Strip that out and the business grew revenue +13.7% to $4,701.3M, generated $893.2M of operating cash flow and roughly $830M of free cash flow (~18% FCF margin), and posted a record year at Wizards of the Coast (WotC): $2,186.9M revenue (+44.7%) at a 46.0% operating margin, driven by MAGIC: THE GATHERING and its "Universes Beyond" crossover sets. Q1 2026 confirmed the trend (revenue +12.7%, 27.0% operating margin, Magic +35.6%), and the July 21 Q2 beat-and-raise (Magic +32%, full-year outlook raised) pushed the stock up nearly 15% off recent lows.

Everything good about Hasbro is concentrated in one franchise. WotC is now ~46% of revenue and the overwhelming majority of operating profit, while Consumer Products remains loss-making at the operating line. That concentration is compounded by a live securities class action (West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund, SDNY) alleging misleading statements about Magic's growth and "segmentation" strategy during 2021-2023, litigation that targets the exact narrative the bull case rests on. A late-March 2026 cyber breach adds near-term legal overhang and a Q2 revenue timing drag in the toy segment.

Current-year consensus EPS has risen +8.0% over 90 days to $6.17 and the company has beaten every quarter over the last year (by 11.3%, 28.3%, 56.5%, 1.9%). At ~$89 the stock trades at roughly 14.4x current-year EPS and ~13.7x next-year ($6.48), a slight discount to the toy/leisure peer median of 15.5x but cheap for a business with a 46%-margin, moaty franchise generating high-teens FCF margins. The setup favors a constructive HOLD with a positive 12-month growth thesis, held back from BUY by concentration, litigation, and governance flags rather than by fundamentals or valuation.

## Company Overview

Hasbro operates three segments with sharply divergent economics:

- **Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming (WotC):** The crown jewel. FY25 revenue $2,186.9M (+44.7%) at a 46.0% operating margin ($1,006.8M operating profit). Anchored by Magic: The Gathering, which added $641.5M of revenue YoY, driven by Universes Beyond crossover sets — the Final Fantasy set was the best-selling Magic set of all time by revenue, alongside Avatar and Marvel's Spider-Man. Also includes Dungeons & Dragons and high-margin digital licensing (Monopoly Go! via Scopely contributed $168M in FY25; Baldur's Gate 3 via Larian).
- **Consumer Products (traditional toys):** FY25 revenue $2,437.6M (−4.2%), an operating loss of $942.6M after the $1,021.9M goodwill impairment. Brands include Nerf, Play-Doh, Transformers, Peppa Pig, Beyblade, and licensed Marvel/Star Wars.
- **Entertainment:** De-emphasized post-eOne divestiture; ~$77M revenue, roughly breakeven.

Management's "Playing to Win" strategy pivots toward higher-margin games, IP, and licensing, "ages up" toward 13+ collectors, expands digital, and leans on partner co-investment. The "Operational Excellence" cost program has delivered ~$800M of a ~$1.0B gross savings target. The competitive advantage is a differentiated games franchise (Magic/D&D) with a 30+ year moat, an engaged collector base, and demonstrated pricing power.

## Financial Analysis

The GAAP headline is misleading in both directions. The FY25 net loss is driven entirely by the non-cash impairment; the underlying business is strengthening.

**Cash generation is robust and belies the GAAP loss.** FY25 operating cash flow was $893.2M (up from $847.4M) and free cash flow was approximately $830M against a $13.0B market cap, a ~6.4% FCF yield. Q1 2026 delivered operating profit of $270.3M (27.0% margin), with WotC up 25.9% at a 51.2% segment margin. The July Q2 beat-and-raise reiterated FY26 guidance of +3-5% constant-currency revenue, adjusted operating margin of 24-25%, and adjusted EBITDA of $1.40-1.45B.

**The scary balance-sheet screens are impairment artifacts, not liquidity distress.** The Altman Z of 1.22 ("distress") and D/E of 8.82 are distorted by impairment-depleted book equity, which fell to $565.5M at YE25 from $1,185M and has since recovered to $674.2M at Q1 2026 as earnings rebuild retained earnings. Against ~$3.6B of fixed-rate debt, Hasbro holds $857M cash plus $498M short-term investments and a fully undrawn $1.1B revolver (amended February 2026, extended to 2031). It is in compliance with covenants (net leverage ≤3.75x, interest coverage ≥3.0x). The $497M 3.55% notes due November 2026 are already pre-funded via a March 2026 $400M 4.65% 2031 note issuance plus Treasury investments.

The thin equity base is a genuine constraint: any further impairment cuts directly into an already-depleted equity cushion. The Family Brands reporting unit's fair value exceeds its carrying value by only ~15% ($325.2M goodwill), so a modest additional charge is plausible if entertainment/CP forecasts weaken. Financial health therefore scores below the level a $830M-FCF business would otherwise merit, because the capital structure has less margin for error than the cash flows suggest.

**Capital allocation is mixed.** The dividend runs ~$0.70/quarter (~$392M/yr), and a new $1.0B buyback was authorized in February 2026 with only $7.7M used in Q1. Capex is guided to ~$250M in 2026, reflecting rising software-development spend for internally developed AAA titles (Exodus, Warlock: D&D, both slated for 2027), which carries write-off risk given $416M of unamortized capitalized software.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests almost entirely on Magic and digital licensing. WotC grew 44.7% in FY25 and 25.9% in Q1 2026, with Magic up 35.6% in Q1 and +32% in Q2. The Universes Beyond model — licensing external IP (Final Fantasy, Marvel, Avatar, TMNT, Street Fighter) into Magic sets — has meaningfully expanded the addressable base and set-buying cadence.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum without reaching the extreme velocity that would flag a clear inflection buy. Current-year EPS consensus rose +8.0% over 90 days to $6.17 (and +3.1% over 30 days), while next-year rose +3.6% to $6.48. The four-quarter beat record is strong, giving high confidence in forward estimates. This is durable positive earnings momentum rather than a trough-to-recovery inflection — WotC is already at peak margins, so the growth question is durability, not recovery.

New licensing wins extend the pipeline: Hasbro was named global primary toy licensee for Harry Potter (from 2027, via Warner Bros. Discovery/HBO), plus collaborations with Amazon MGM (Voltron) and Legendary (Street Fighter), and the LUMEE advertising JV with Animaj. These are capital-light licensing deals consistent with the strategy. A potential IEEPA-tariff refund tailwind — following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling against those tariffs — is an unquantified 2026 upside that Hasbro is still evaluating (FY25 absorbed $44.9M of tariff costs, Q1 2026 $8.3M).

The growth I am scoring is real but narrow. Consumer Products remains a structural drag: FY25 revenue fell 4.2% and the segment lost money at the operating line even before the impairment. Growth prospects are therefore constructive but not top-tier, because the consolidated growth rate (+3-5% constant currency guided for FY26) blends a strong franchise against a shrinking one.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89, HAS trades at approximately 14.4x current-year EPS ($6.17) and ~13.7x next-year ($6.48). Against the Leisure Products peer median P/E of 15.5x, Hasbro is at a slight discount despite owning the highest-quality asset in the group (a 46%-margin recurring-card franchise). Peer comparables are heterogeneous and mostly lower-quality: Mattel at 9.3x (a structurally challenged toy pure-play, −35% off its 52-week high), Thor at 15.5x, and several names (Brunswick, Polaris) showing negative trailing P/E on cyclical losses.

Trailing P/E is distorted by the impairment-driven loss and should be disregarded. On forward and normalized earnings, the stock is reasonable-to-cheap: a business generating ~$830M FCF with a moaty core franchise, growing consolidated revenue mid-single-digits and WotC at 25%+, should not trade at a discount to a peer group full of cyclical, lower-margin toy and powersports makers. The 3.15% dividend yield (above the 2.2% peer median) provides absolute yield support.

I resist over-rewarding the low multiple. Prevailing evidence shows that "cheap" screens have been reliably anti-predictive in this environment, and cheapness alone is not a buy signal. The valuation score is a 6.2 rather than a 7+ because the discount is partly justified: thin equity, extreme single-franchise concentration, and a securities suit attacking the growth narrative are real reasons the market applies a haircut. Analyst targets (mean $107.69, median $110.00, high $120.00, low $85.00) sit meaningfully above spot, but at a stock trading around 14x forward with positive revisions, those targets are more likely floors than ceilings if Magic sustains; consensus tends to catch up 2-4 weeks after beats. My base case leans on that dynamic without treating it as guaranteed given the concentration risk.

Consumer Discretionary as a sector has historically produced negative alpha, which is a reason to demand company-specific outperformance rather than lean on sector beta. Hasbro's WotC franchise economics are distinct enough from cyclical discretionary demand — collector/specialty behavior closer to a specialty-consumer model — to warrant the constructive lean, but not to override the sector caution entirely.

## Competitive Landscape

Hasbro's competitive position is bifurcated. In games, Magic and Dungeons & Dragons are effectively unassailable franchises with collector economics and pricing power; there is no credible direct competitor to Magic in physical trading-card gaming at scale, and the 30+ year installed base and secondary-market ecosystem create a durable moat. The Universes Beyond strategy has, so far, widened rather than diluted that moat by expanding the buyer pool.

In traditional toys, the position is deteriorating: Consumer Products revenue is shrinking and losing money, and Hasbro competes with Mattel and with digital entertainment for consumer time and attention. Customer concentration is a structural feature: top-5 customers are ~35% of revenue, with Amazon (11%) and Walmart (9%) alone at 20%.

The key competitive risk to the crown jewel is self-inflicted: franchise fatigue and oversaturation. The 10-K explicitly flags that "frequent product releases, cross-brand collaborations… may contribute to franchise fatigue or oversaturation." Universes Beyond sets carry royalty obligations (royalties rose to 7.8% of revenue), so growth from licensed crossovers is both margin-dilutive at the edge and dependent on continued release cadence. Over-monetization is the central long-term threat to Magic. On balance, the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in games and deteriorating in toys, netting to a slightly above-neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** The bull case is the concentration risk. WotC is ~46% of revenue and the vast majority of operating profit; any adverse trend in Magic disproportionately impacts results. This is the single dominant idiosyncratic risk.

**Securities litigation.** West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Hasbro (SDNY) alleges false/misleading statements about Magic's growth and "segmentation" strategy from September 2021 to October 2023. A related derivative action (Sbriglio) is stayed pending outcome. This directly attacks the crown-jewel narrative; an adverse development could damage both financials and credibility.

**Cyber breach.** A late-March 2026 unauthorized network access event took systems offline and delayed the Q1 10-Q filing. Management says it is contained and Magic shipments were unaffected, but Consumer Products saw order/shipping/invoicing delays that shifted some Q2 revenue into H2 2026. A class action (Sheila Standing v. Hasbro, D.R.I.) has been filed. Financially likely manageable (cyber-insurance claims in process), but it adds legal overhang.

**Impairment tail.** Remaining goodwill is $1,256.7M; the Family Brands unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only ~15%, leaving a modest additional-impairment risk that would further erode thin equity.

**Governance / pay-for-performance.** CEO "compensation actually paid" spiked to $35.3M in FY2025 — a net-loss year — while cumulative TSR (108) trailed the peer group (151). The mark-to-market mechanics explain the number, but peak pay in a loss year is a genuine flag, and it is the primary reason management quality scores below 5.

**Digital execution.** Rising capitalized software spend ($416M unamortized) for internally developed AAA titles (2027 launches) creates write-off risk if they disappoint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming an imminent negative catalyst. Near-term ATM IV is 34.7% versus 29.6% historical vol (modestly rich, 5.0pp spread), IV percentile is a middling 32%, and put/call skew is balanced at −1.5% with a put/call OI ratio of 0.93. The term structure is in normal contango (34.7% near vs 38.1% at six months), signaling complacency rather than event stress. ATM IV rose from 22.1% to 34.7% over the prior six weeks, but that tracks the run into and through the July earnings beat rather than defensive positioning. The absence of elevated put demand is consistent with the constructive fundamental read, though the 18% six-month implied move reflects genuine uncertainty about franchise durability further out.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$115-120):** Magic sustains 20%+ growth as Universes Beyond continues to expand the collector base; WotC margins hold near 50%; the Operational Excellence program completes its final ~$200M of savings; a tariff refund materializes; and the securities suit resolves without material damage. Consolidated FCF pushes toward $900M+, the buyback accelerates, and the multiple re-rates toward the low-to-mid teens on next-year EPS approaching $7. This aligns with the analyst high target of $120.

**Bear case (~$70, roughly −21% from ~$89):** Magic shows franchise fatigue as oversaturation and royalty drag bite; the securities class action produces an adverse ruling or settlement that reprices the growth narrative; a further Family Brands impairment erodes the thin equity base; the cyber-driven Q2 revenue slippage does not fully recover; and Consumer Products continues to bleed. The multiple compresses toward Mattel-like levels and the stock retests the 52-week low near $69.50.

**Base case (~$100):** Magic normalizes to still-strong high-teens/20% growth, FY26 guidance is met near the high end, current-year EPS lands around $6.20-6.30, and the stock re-rates modestly toward 15-16x forward as consensus targets pull the price up. This sits between the analyst mean ($107.69) and current price, reflecting a discount for unresolved litigation and concentration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$100** (12 months). Approximately 15.5x FY26 EPS of ~$6.45 (splitting current-year $6.17 and next-year $6.48, adjusted for continued modest upward revisions). Upside of ~12% from ~$89.

**Bull case target: ~$118** (+33%). Low-to-mid teens multiple on next-year EPS pushing toward $7 with margin durability and litigation clearance.

**Bear case target: ~$70** (−21%). Multiple compression toward peer-cyclical levels on a Magic deceleration or adverse legal outcome.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next 2-3 quarterly prints (next earnings following the July 21 Q2 release) and the progression of the West Palm Beach securities litigation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Magic revenue growth sustains above 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters with WotC margin holding ≥48%, demonstrating the concentration is durable strength rather than fragility.
- Favorable dismissal or immaterial settlement of the West Palm Beach securities class action, removing the narrative-risk overhang.
- Consumer Products stabilizes to flat-or-positive revenue growth, reducing the impairment tail and broadening the earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Magic revenue decelerates below +10% YoY or WotC margin compresses below ~42%, signaling franchise fatigue.
- Adverse development in the securities litigation (class certification with damaging disclosures, or a large accrual).
- A further material goodwill impairment (Family Brands) that pushes book equity back below ~$500M.
- FY26 guidance cut on tariff, cyber, or demand deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, plus any 8-K on the securities suit or cyber-breach litigation.

## Conclusion

Hasbro is a fundamentally improving, high-FCF franchise whose GAAP loss and distress-screen optics mask genuine strength. The Q2 beat-and-raise, +8% current-year estimate revisions, four consecutive beats, ~$830M FCF, and a 46%+ WotC operating margin all support a constructive stance, and at ~14x forward the valuation is reasonable-to-cheap for the quality on offer. The stock's price decline earlier in 2026 was not accompanied by fundamental deterioration; the underlying business improved while the price fell, which improved the risk/reward, a dynamic the recent 15% recovery has begun to correct.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and not price-related. Extreme Magic concentration, a securities class action attacking the exact growth narrative, a live cyber breach with litigation follow-through, a thin equity base with a residual impairment tail, and the optics of $35.3M CEO pay in a loss year each impose a real discount. The score trajectory has declined from 6.4 to the mid-5s over several reports, driven by governance and concentration concerns; the new Q2 data confirms the improving-fundamentals half of the story while leaving those risk overhangs intact. I rate HAS a constructive growth-oriented HOLD, with a clear path to BUY if Magic durability is demonstrated for two more quarters and the securities litigation clears.
