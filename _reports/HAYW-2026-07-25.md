**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hayward is a leading designer and manufacturer of residential and commercial pool equipment, holding an estimated ~33% share of the North American residential pool market and generating roughly 85% of its North American sales from aftermarket (repair/replace/upgrade) demand tied to an 8–11 year equipment replacement cycle. The business converts revenue into cash efficiently (FY25 FCF $227M, 20.3% FCF margin), earns high segment margins (FY25 NAM adjusted segment income margin 32.4%), and carries a moat built on brand, entrenched distributor/builder relationships, and a large installed base. On quality of franchise, HAYW is above average for its sector.

The tension in the story is that the top-line recovery is almost entirely price, not volume. FY2025 revenue grew 6.7%, but volume was flat (0.0%) and the balance came from +5.4% price plus +1.1% M&A. Q1 FY2026 repeated the pattern (+11.5% sales, of which +8.6% price and only +1.0% volume). Pricing has flattered margins, but it raises two questions the market is now weighing: whether pricing power holds if tariff-driven increases reverse, and whether flat units reflect a category-wide demand problem that no single company can grow through. Pentair's mid-July 2026 guidance cut, 52-week low, and CFO departure, attributed to weak pool demand, high rates, and continued distributor destocking, confirm the category volume problem while handing Hayward a share-gain opportunity.

We rate HAYW a HOLD. The franchise is genuinely good and the balance sheet is manageable, but consensus EPS estimates are drifting lower across every near-term window (current quarter -6.4% over 90 days), the stock trades at ~19.8x forward EPS with no growth catalyst beyond price, and the risk stack (POOL concentration, 2028 refinancing, an ambiguous tariff-refund dynamic, and an escalating litigation cluster with insider-trading allegations against sitting executives) keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. This is our third consecutive HOLD, and nothing in the new data warrants a directional upgrade.

## Company Overview

Hayward operates as a "specialty water management" company across two segments:

- **North America (NAM):** ~85% of sales (~82% in Q1'26), higher-margin (FY25 gross margin 49.9%), including flow-control industrial thermoplastic valves. Products span pumps, filters, heaters, automatic cleaners, salt chlorinators/sanitizers, LED lighting, and IoT controls (OmniLogic).
- **Europe & Rest of World (E&RW):** ~15% of sales, lower-margin (FY25 gross margin 36.7%) but recovering from Middle East and Ukraine-Russia demand weakness.

Manufacturing spans seven plants (North Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee, Rhode Island, two in Spain, one in China). The moat rests on brand strength, distributor/servicer relationships, an installed base that drives non-discretionary replacement demand, and an IP portfolio of ~221 U.S. and 242 foreign patents. Chief competitors are Pentair and Fluidra (Zodiac/Jandy). Distribution is dominated by Pool Corporation, which is both Hayward's largest customer and a competitor's distributor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** 2022 $1,314M (post-COVID peak) → 2023 $992M (-24%, distributor destocking collapse) → 2024 $1,052M (+6%) → 2025 $1,122M (+6.7%). The 2022→2023 crash reflected pandemic-era over-ordering unwinding, the same episode that underlies the outstanding securities litigation.

**Margin execution is the bright spot.** FY25 gross margin reached 48.0% (+170 bps YoY), operating margin 20.8% (+90 bps), net margin 13.5%. Adjusted EBITDA was $299.3M (26.7% margin), and net income rose 27.7% to $151.6M. Q1 FY2026 continued the trend: operating income +27%, net income +63% to $23.4M, adjusted EBITDA +14.8%. The catch is that margin gains have been driven substantially by price, which lifts profitability disproportionately when volumes are flat but leaves the business exposed if pricing reverses.

**Cash generation is high-quality.** FY25 operating cash flow was $256M with capex light at ~$29M (2.6% of sales), producing $227M of free cash flow. Year-end cash stood at $329.6M. The Q1'26 headline operating cash flow of -$150.6M looks alarming but is an artifact of (a) the normal seasonal Early-Buy accounts-receivable build and (b) a deliberate decision not to factor $100M of receivables this year (vs. $100M sold in Q1'25). AR ballooned to $430.9M from $280M; this is a working-capital timing effect, not a cash-generation deterioration.

**Balance sheet:** Current ratio 2.94, D/E ~0.98. Debt is a $955M First Lien Term Loan due May 2028 at an effective ~6.0% rate (5.97% in Q1'26), with $700M of notional interest-rate swaps hedging variable exposure. The ABL is undrawn ($124.9M availability at year-end). Interest expense is falling (FY25 $50.3M vs. $62.2M FY24) on rate cuts and deleveraging. The main quantitative caution is that goodwill plus intangibles total $1,954M, ~62% of total assets, a large impairment overhang (no impairment recorded to date, qualitative testing only). Altman Z of 2.16 sits in the grey zone, depressed by the debt load and intangible-heavy asset base rather than by operating distress; coverage and profitability are healthy.

**Earnings quality track record:** 3 beats and 1 in-line over the last four quarters, with beats in the 14–15% range on the smaller seasonal quarters. That supports confidence in the current estimate base, but the more important signal is the revision direction, which is negative.

## Growth Analysis

The central issue is that reported growth is not organic unit growth. FY2025 volume was 0.0% and Q1'26 volume was just +1.0%; price carried the rest. This is consistent with a category still normalizing from the pandemic boom-bust and now facing a macro headwind — elevated interest rates, weak housing turnover, and cautious consumer spending on outdoor projects — that suppresses new-pool construction and big-ticket upgrades. Pentair's July 2026 guidance cut is direct evidence that the volume problem is industry-wide, not company-specific.

Hayward's ~85% aftermarket mix is a relative defensive advantage: repair-and-replace demand is far less discretionary than new construction, which cushions the downside. The E&RW recovery has also been running better than headline optics suggest. But the growth engine for the next 12 months is limited to (a) continued price realization (which may fade if tariffs are refunded/removed), (b) modest volume recovery if rates ease, and (c) incremental share gains from a stumbling Pentair.

Consensus estimates are not corroborating an acceleration. EPS revisions are negative across every near-term window: current quarter $0.24 (-5.7% 30d, -6.4% 90d), next quarter $0.15 (-3.6% 30d), current year $0.84 (-1.9% 30d), with next year $0.94 essentially flat (+0.5% 90d). This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would signal a recovery worth chasing. We weight the falling revisions as a meaningful drag on the Growth Prospects score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $14.43, HAYW trades at ~19.8x trailing/forward EPS ($0.68 TTM; $0.84 current-year consensus implies ~17.2x on the year). Against the stated peer set (Sensata 154x, Enersys 29x, Acuity 21x, Powell 57x, Mercury negative; peer median 56.7x), Hayward looks cheap, but the peer group is a poor comparable: those names are diversified electrical/electronic component makers with different cyclicality and growth profiles, and Sensata/Powell/Mercury multiples are distorted by depressed or negative earnings. The more relevant benchmark is the pool-equipment oligopoly (Pentair, Fluidra) and Hayward's own history; on that basis ~18–20x forward is a full, not cheap, multiple for a business with flat volumes and falling estimates.

Our calibration treats a "cheap" screen with skepticism, and this case reinforces why: the apparent peer discount is not an independent mispricing signal, it reflects a heterogeneous sector. There is no earnings-inflection catalyst justifying a cycle-adjusted premium; this is a price-led recovery with 0% volume growth, not a trough-earnings situation with tripling EPS. The valuation is therefore roughly fair. Analyst targets (low $15.00, high $19.50, mean $17.21, median $17.00) sit above the current price, implying ~19% upside to the mean, but the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading and estimates are falling, so we do not treat those targets as reliable floors on a stock without positive revision momentum.

We score Valuation 5.5. The absolute multiple is undemanding for the franchise quality, but there is no identified catalyst to close a gap, and falling estimates argue against paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

Hayward operates in a three-player oligopoly (Hayward, Pentair, Fluidra) selling through a concentrated channel dominated by Pool Corporation. The moat is real: ~33% NAM share, a large installed base driving recurring aftermarket demand, entrenched distributor/builder/servicer relationships, and a differentiated IoT/controls portfolio. Margin trajectory (gross margin +170 bps in FY25) is consistent with pricing power and share stability rather than erosion.

The competitive trajectory is modestly improving on a relative basis. Pentair's simultaneous guidance cut, CFO departure, and 52-week low create a genuine share-gain window, and Stifel has flagged Hayward as a likely beneficiary. Hayward's margin execution and E&RW recovery have outpaced peers. Against that, two structural cautions: the FY2025 10-K materially expanded language around AI as both a competitive/operational and cybersecurity risk, and added detail on "competitor-customers" (i.e., Pool Corp) potentially leveraging the commercial relationship to support competing products. The distribution channel that Hayward depends on also carries its rivals' equipment, which caps how much share the company can capture without channel cooperation. On balance, competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving; we score it 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Customer concentration (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Pool Corporation is ~33% of FY25 net sales and ~46% of accounts receivable; a second customer is 12% of sales/14% of AR. Distributor destocking decisions — largely outside Hayward's control — already caused one revenue collapse (2022→2023) and remain the key swing factor. This concentration is not fully priced into a ~20x multiple.

2. **Tariffs and an ambiguous refund dynamic.** Hayward sources parts from China and consumes steel/aluminum/copper/titanium/ruthenium. The Feb 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down certain IEEPA tariffs opened the possibility of refunds, but Hayward disclosed that refunds may have to be shared back with customers who received price concessions, so a "refund" headline may not translate to retained profit. The administration has announced replacement tariffs under other authorities, leaving net exposure unresolved. A prior-year duty-drawback charge already had to be refunded to CBP.

3. **Refinancing.** The $955M term loan matures May 2028, a refinancing event within a reasonable investment horizon; variable-rate risk is partially hedged.

4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** At 62% of total assets, a demand-driven earnings shortfall could trigger an impairment; only qualitative testing has been performed.

5. **Litigation cluster.** The securities class action (2021–2022 period) is settled in principle, with preliminary approval Feb 28, 2026 and a final hearing July 28, 2026; $10M is already funded by insurers. Four derivative suits (Heicklen, Hertzog, Tackett, Aitken) name officers/directors and PE sponsors MSD and CCMP, several alleging insider trading during the class period. The direct cost appears contained (insurer-funded), but the reputational tail is live and awkwardly timed against the CEO's active selling program.

6. **Latent dilution.** 503.7M shares issuable on potential conversions/exchanges vs. 217M outstanding — a large latent supply consideration tied to the legacy PE capital structure.

The mitigants (defensive aftermarket mix, strong FCF, undrawn ABL, healthy coverage) keep this from being a distressed profile, but the concentration and unresolved binary items (tariff outcome, refinancing, litigation) justify a Risk Factors score of 5.0, consistent with the prior report's decline.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 72.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 32.8% — rich by 39.6 points and in the 81st percentile of the current chain. IV has surged from 42.5% to 72.4% since late May while the price barely moved (+1.8%), and the term structure is in backwardation (72.4% near-term vs. 45.8% at 6 months). This is the market pricing imminent, event-driven stress, most plausibly around the July 28 litigation final hearing and the upcoming earnings print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±16.1%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at the 1-month tenor (-1.8%) but turns notably put-heavy at 3 months (+20.7% skew), indicating hedging demand into the fall. Short interest is modest at 5.8% of float (3.6 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short setup. The options market corroborates elevated near-term uncertainty rather than a directional bear thesis, and the rich IV argues against paying up for the stock ahead of these events.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($18–20)

Hayward is a durable, wide-moat franchise with 85% aftermarket revenue that compounds through the replacement cycle regardless of new-construction weakness. Margins are expanding, FCF conversion is high (~20% margin), and the $440M remaining buyback authorization retires shares at reasonable prices. Pentair's stumble opens a real share-gain window, and if rates ease, pent-up upgrade/remodel demand converts flat volumes into low-to-mid single-digit unit growth on top of continued price. A tariff refund, if retained, would be pure upside. At ~18–20x on recovering EPS, the stock re-rates toward the $19.50 high analyst target.

### Bear Case ($11–12)

The recovery is pure price and pricing power fades — either because tariff-driven increases reverse (with refunds partly owed back to customers) or because a soft consumer resists further increases. Volume stays flat-to-negative as high rates continue to suppress new pools and big-ticket upgrades, and POOL destocking recurs, compressing the ~33% of sales that flow through one distributor. Falling estimates continue, the multiple compresses toward the low end of its history, and a goodwill impairment or an adverse litigation surprise adds a discrete leg down. The stock revisits the low-$12s (the 52-week low is $12.93).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality and balance sheet support ownership, but flat volume, falling estimates, a full multiple, and unresolved binary risks leave the 12-month risk/reward balanced. We lack the evidence for a confident directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Volume growth turns positive and durable (>3% organic units for two consecutive quarters), confirming the recovery is more than price.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (30-day and 90-day both rising), reversing the current negative momentum.
- A clean tariff-refund outcome that Hayward retains, or confirmed share gains from Pentair showing up in NAM revenue, alongside a pullback toward the low-$13s.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- POOL destocking recurs or POOL reduces its purchasing commitment (any commentary of a distributor inventory reset).
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or an adverse/expanded litigation development beyond the insurer-funded settlement.
- Pricing turns negative in NAM (price contribution goes to zero or below) with volume still flat, signaling margin compression ahead.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the July 28, 2026 litigation final hearing and the next quarterly earnings release, plus any clarity on the June 23 financing action and the 2028 term-loan refinancing path. Reassess at Q2 FY2026 results.

## Conclusion

Hayward is a high-quality, cash-generative pool-equipment leader executing well on margins during a weak-volume, price-led recovery. The defensive aftermarket mix, ~33% NAM share, and 20% FCF margin justify a solid franchise valuation. But the growth is being carried by price rather than units, consensus estimates are falling across every near-term window, and the ~20x forward multiple offers no asymmetry without a catalyst. The Pentair-stumble share-gain opportunity is real but does not solve the category-wide volume problem, and the risk stack — POOL concentration, an ambiguous tariff-refund dynamic, a 2028 refinancing, 62%-of-assets intangibles, and an active litigation cluster with insider-trading allegations against sitting executives — keeps the setup balanced. Rich near-term implied volatility signals the market expects event-driven turbulence rather than a directional break. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.65, essentially unchanged from the prior report, and would look to turn constructive on either a durable volume recovery with rising estimates or a pullback toward the low-$13s.
