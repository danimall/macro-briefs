**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Woodmark is no longer an investable public equity. The company completed an all-stock merger into MasterBrand, Inc. (NYSE: MBC) on May 28, 2026, became a wholly owned MasterBrand subsidiary, and was delisted from Nasdaq. The consideration was a fixed 5.150 MBC shares per AMWD share, leaving pre-merger MasterBrand holders with approximately 63% of the combined company and former AMWD holders with approximately 37%. The final AMWD trade was $48.09 on May 27, 2026, which implies a contemporaneous MBC reference price of $9.34 (48.09 ÷ 5.150). The authoritative pricing snapshot for this report returns no valid price, currency, or market date for AMWD, consistent with a deregistering security rather than a data outage. No scenario, margin-of-safety, or target arithmetic is possible or meaningful on this ticker, and none is presented.

The condition of the business at the point of sale matters more than the ticker. American Woodmark was sold into an accelerating decline. FY2025 (fiscal year ended April 30, 2025) net sales fell 7.5% to $1,709.6M and management's own adjusted EPS fell 19.8% to $6.90 while GAAP diluted EPS fell only 9.1% to $6.50, the gap almost entirely explained by the roll-off of RSI customer-relationship amortization ($30.4M in FY2024 to zero in FY2025), lower incentive accruals, and a 5.91% reduction in weighted diluted shares from $96.7M of buybacks that exceeded company-defined free cash flow of $65.7M. Operating cash flow fell 53% to $108.4M. In FY2026 the deterioration steepened: nine-month net sales of $1,122.0M were 14.3% below the prior-year period against guidance of roughly flat, and the final reported quarter (ended January 31, 2026) showed revenue down 18.4%, gross margin of 11.64% versus 20.4% in FY2024, a $33.9M operating loss, negative $13.1M operating cash flow, and cash down to $28.3M.

The rating is AVOID at 3.4, a reversal of the June HOLD (4.9) and a break in the improving score trajectory. The downgrade is driven primarily by the confirmation that no AMWD common equity exists to own, and secondarily by the FY2026 operating record, which was not fully visible in earlier reads. There is no price at all, layered on a terminal quarter that inverted cash generation. Former holders now own MBC and should evaluate that position on MasterBrand's own disclosures, including whether the $767.6M of AMWD goodwill survived acquisition accounting and what the combined leverage path looks like against MasterBrand's stated target of net leverage below 2x by end-2028.

## Company Overview

American Woodmark manufactured and distributed kitchen, bath, and office cabinetry, home organization products, and hardware for the U.S. repair-and-remodel (R&R) and new-construction markets. As of April 30, 2025 it operated 17 manufacturing facilities across nine U.S. states plus Tijuana and Monterrey, Mexico (four Mexican plants), eight turnkey-installation service centers, and one standalone distribution center, employing more than 7,800 people with roughly 228 unionized (Anaheim, California only). The company self-described a "number two or three" U.S. position with approximately 11% market share.

The channel structure was three-legged in name and two-legged in economics. FY2025 net sales split builders 43.5% (down 5.1%), home centers approximately 40.7% (down 9.3%), and independent dealers/distributors 15.8% (down 8.9%). Home Depot and Lowe's combined represented 40.8% of net sales, effectively the entire home center channel. The 10-K is explicit that these relationships operate on purchase orders without long-term contracts, that customers "have in the past discontinued, and may in the future choose to discontinue, purchasing some or all of our products with little or no notice," and that they impose logistics performance fines.

Product architecture split made-to-order (higher-grade, factory-direct) from stock (limited SKUs, cash-and-carry through home centers), with home organization exclusively stock. Brands included Waypoint Living Spaces and the newer 1951 Cabinetry family targeting affordable price points. Management's strategic framework (Growth, Digital Transformation, Platform Design) centered on channel expansion, a multi-year cloud ERP migration, and complexity reduction.

Neither the merger 8-Ks nor the S-4 were available, so the review's mechanics, including any collar, ratio adjustment, or walk-away right, remain unverified. Absent a collar, AMWD holders bore MBC's share-price path from August 2025 through close rather than escaping the sector downturn.

Revenue fell 17.3% from the FY2023 peak, with −10.6% in FY2024 and −7.5% in FY2025.

**FY2025 earnings quality was materially weaker than the GAAP line suggested.** GAAP diluted EPS of $6.50 was down 9.1%. Three cushions account for most of the apparent resilience: intangible amortization of RSI customer relationships went from $45.7M (FY2023) to $30.4M (FY2024) to zero (FY2025), the single largest driver of the 39.1% decline in G&A ($124.0M to $75.5M); $13.4M of that G&A relief was lower incentive and profit-sharing cost, which is a consequence of missing plan rather than structural efficiency; and weighted diluted shares fell 5.91% (16,260,222 to 15,299,261). Management's own normalized measures (adjusted EPS down 19.8%, from $8.60 to $6.90, and adjusted EBITDA down 17.5%, from $252.8M to $208.6M, margin 13.7% to 12.2%) are the correct read of run-rate earnings power. The GAAP presentation understated the deterioration by roughly eleven percentage points of EPS decline.

**Cash conversion broke before earnings did.** Operating cash flow fell 53% while net income fell 14.4%. The 10-K attributes the gap to inventory ($51.2M outflow), accounts payable ($18.8M), accrued compensation ($21.0M), prepaid/other ($12.0M), and accrued marketing ($8.1M), partly offset by $27.9M lower income tax payments. Two items are diagnostic: inventory rose from $159.1M to $178.1M while sales fell 7.5% (days extending into a declining top line), and the accrued-compensation outflow settled FY2024 bonuses in the same year the P&L benefited from a lower FY2025 accrual, flattering income and penalizing cash simultaneously.

**Capital allocation ran ahead of cash generation.** Company-defined free cash flow (operating cash flow less capex including promotional displays) was $65.7M against $96.7M of repurchases. With $5.3M of net debt repayment, cash fell $39.2M to $48.2M. The Board added a second $125M authorization on November 20, 2024, leaving $117.8M available at year-end, and the FY2026 outlook stated an intent to be "opportunistic" on buybacks and to "deprioritize debt repayments." Two-year repurchases totaled $184.4M against a business shrinking at a double-digit rate.

### Balance sheet at the last audited date

Total long-term debt including current maturities was $373.5M, or 28.5% of total capital. The October 10, 2024 amended credit agreement provided a $500M revolver and $200M term loan maturing October 2029, with approximately $314.2M available at April 30, 2025 and covenants of minimum 2.00x interest coverage and maximum 4.00x total net leverage. Using disclosed figures, net funded debt of $325.3M against $208.6M adjusted EBITDA implies roughly 1.56x net leverage, and interest coverage was approximately 20x. Two commonly circulated screening figures are misleading and should be corrected: a reported "debt-to-equity of 1.71" is in fact the equity multiplier (total assets $1,570.6M ÷ equity $916.0M = 1.715); actual liabilities/equity was 0.715 and funded debt/equity 0.408. Similarly, a reported Altman Z of 2.69 rests on a mis-specified equity-to-liabilities input; the correct ratio is 1.399 and the Z''-variant recomputes to 4.01, above the 2.60 safe threshold. Third-party "total debt" of $509.9M reflects lease liabilities included in the aggregator definition and excluded from the 10-K's long-term debt caption; the roughly $136M gap is definitional, not a discrepancy.

None of this survived FY2026 intact. On the same lease assumption, funded debt at January 31, 2026 was approximately $348M, or roughly $320M net of $28.3M cash. Against an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $95–110M (a sensitivity built on assumed D&A phasing, assumed stock compensation, and an assumed fourth quarter, since no FY2026 10-K was filed), net funded leverage lands near 2.9–3.2x against a 4.00x maximum. Headroom narrowed by roughly half in nine months while remaining inside the covenant on these assumptions.

## Growth Analysis

There is no forward growth case for the AMWD instrument. For the underlying franchise, the trajectory through the last reported quarter was uniformly negative and accelerating: revenue −7.5% in FY2025, then −12.2%, −12.8% and −18.4% sequentially through FY2026, with gross margin compressing 880 basis points from FY2024's 20.4% to 11.64%. Every channel declined in FY2025, with home centers weakest at −9.3%.

The demand mechanics are specific rather than generic cyclicality. The 10-K's own market data show single-family starts up 2.0% and completions up 1.2% in FY2025 with mortgage rates 41 basis points lower, yet builder-channel revenue fell 5.1%. The pain concentrated in R&R, where existing home sales at thirty-year lows suppress remodel spend, and in mix, with consumers "prioritizing smaller sized projects and more value-based product offerings." Layered on that is a structural pricing asymmetry management disclosed plainly: a six-to-nine-month lag before pricing actions show results, no long-term fixed supply agreements, no raw-material hedging, and customer pressure to cut prices when input costs fall. That structure loses margin as costs rise and gives it back as they fall, which is the mechanical explanation for the margin path.

The one identifiable growth vector, Section 232 duties of 25% on imported kitchen cabinets and vanities effective approximately October 14, 2025, did not appear in the numbers. Revenue decline accelerated from −12.8% to −18.4% in the first full post-tariff quarter and gross margin fell from 15.2% to 11.6%. The demand contraction plausibly swamped any protective effect, and the company's own pricing lag implies benefits would take multiple quarters, so the effects are confounded and cannot be separated from available data. The scheduled step to 50% was delayed from January 1, 2026 to January 1, 2027, meaning the largest potential protective effect had not arrived when AMWD stopped reporting. The merger parties stated synergy and accretion expectations assume existing tariffs with no future tariff changes, so no tariff upside was underwritten in the deal case.

For orientation on the residual MBC exposure: MasterBrand's Q2 2026 net sales were $815.2M, including $125.5M from American Woodmark from the close date, with legacy MasterBrand at approximately $690M, down about 5%. The $125.5M covers roughly four to five weeks in what was seasonally AMWD's strongest period (the 10-K notes higher sales in fiscal Q1 and Q4, i.e., May–July and February–April) and should not be annualized. Combined-entity second-half 2026 revenue guidance is $2.05–2.11B.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger combined the largest and the #2/#3 North American residential cabinet manufacturers into an entity with roughly $4.0–4.3B of annualized revenue (legacy MBC approximately $2.8B annualized from $690M in Q2 2026, plus AMWD approximately $1.3–1.5B, a derived figure). The $90M synergy target is roughly 2.1% of combined revenue, a modest and credible cost objective for a roll-up with overlapping plants, distribution networks, and SKU sets.

The strategic logic is defensible on the evidence. Both parties were shrinking, both carried excess capacity commissioned into a downturn, and both depended on the same two retail counterparties. In an industry the 10-K describes as containing "several thousand local, regional, and national manufacturers," where AMWD noted that "our largest remodeling customers and competitors continued to utilize sales promotions," combining fixed costs is more attractive than competing on promotional intensity. Industry commentary notes the transaction further concentrates capacity among fewer national suppliers, which modestly shifts procurement leverage relative to builders.

American Woodmark's own competitive position was deteriorating on trajectory even where the level looked adequate. An 11% share and a #2/#3 position with 20-plus-year relationships with top-10 customers is a real franchise; a 17.3% revenue decline from peak, 880 basis points of gross margin erosion, and an operating loss in the final reported quarter describe erosion in absolute terms. Whether share was lost to imports, to MasterBrand, or to no one (a shrinking category) cannot be resolved from available data; the two-customer channel makes shelf-space decisions, not aggregate share, the operative variable, and those decisions are not disclosed. The Mexico footprint, presented in the 10-K as a low-cost alternative to Asian manufacturing, became a two-sided exposure under Section 232: the treatment of Mexican-origin cabinets and components under USMCA content tests or carve-outs is not established by any source reviewed, and it is the single largest unresolved input into whether the tariff regime was net favorable.

## Risk Assessment

**No investable instrument (dominant).** AMWD common stock does not trade. Any order entered against the ticker either fails or fills against a residual, illiquid, deregistering line. This is an availability constraint, not a priced risk.

**Unresolved goodwill and terminal-quarter accounting.** Goodwill of $767.6M equaled 48.9% of FY2025 total assets and had been unchanged since FY2022. Management concluded no impairment through April 30, 2025, a conclusion that predates a revenue decline to $324M quarterly and an 11.6% gross margin. The composition of the $33.9M Q3 operating loss is unverified, and the inference that charges were deductible (and therefore probably merger, severance, or long-lived-asset items rather than goodwill) should not be treated as established.

**Two-customer concentration transferred to MBC.** Home Depot and Lowe's at 40.8% of AMWD sales, on purchase orders with no long-term contracts, now sits alongside MasterBrand's own retail dependence. Post-merger, the combined entity's negotiating position with those two counterparties is the central open question for the residual equity.

**Liquidity path.** Cash fell from $87.4M (April 2024) to $48.2M (April 2025) to $28.3M (January 2026), with the final reported quarter burning $21.0M of free cash flow. Debt including leases declined each quarter of FY2026 ($509.9M to $483.7M), so the cash decline reflects operating burn plus scheduled term-loan amortization rather than incremental borrowing.

**Integration and systems.** The ERP program was scheduled to convert Lincolnton and Hamlet, North Carolina, the largest East Coast stock facilities, during FY2026, the same year volumes fell and a merger was pending. Whether the conversions proceeded, and whether they contributed to the Q3 margin, is unverified; fixed-cost absorption failure at a $324M quarterly run rate is a sufficient explanation on its own.

**Data provenance.** All FY2026 quarterly figures are third-party aggregations; the FY2026 fourth quarter and full year were never publicly reported; the exchange ratio and ownership split rest on external reporting corroborated by internally consistent derived share counts; insider Form 4 activity around the announcement and close was not retrieved, so no conclusion is drawn on insider behavior.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (applies to the residual MBC position, not to AMWD).** The combination creates a roughly $4.0–4.3B-revenue national cabinet maker with $90M of targeted run-rate synergies (about 2.1% of combined revenue) at the trough of a cabinet cycle driven by thirty-year lows in existing home sales. Section 232 duties of 25% on imported cabinets and vanities, stepping to 50% on January 1, 2027, structurally favor a manufacturer with 17 North American plants over Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, and Malaysian competitors, an effect the merger case explicitly did not underwrite, making it optionality rather than a base-case assumption. If existing home turnover normalizes, the operating leverage on a combined fixed-cost base that has been cut twice is substantial: AMWD alone ran 20.4% gross margin in FY2024 versus 11.64% in its last reported quarter. MasterBrand targets net leverage below 2x by end-2028.

**Bear case.** Both merging parties were shrinking when they combined (legacy MBC −5%, AMWD −14.3% over nine months), so the deal removes a competitor without adding demand. The combined entity inherits AMWD's disclosed structural pricing asymmetry (a six-to-nine-month price-action lag, no raw-material hedging, no long-term supply contracts, and customer pressure to cut price when inputs fall) into a channel where two retailers set terms on purchase orders. Section 232 raises input and cross-border component costs against uncertain treatment of Mexican-origin goods, and the one post-tariff quarter of AMWD data showed accelerating revenue decline and roughly 350 basis points of further margin compression. AMWD's $767.6M goodwill and the composition of its final-quarter charges remain unresolved, so the acquired asset base's carrying value is an open question. Integration of a business that was losing money and burning cash at the point of acquisition consumes management attention that neither party has spare.

For the AMWD ticker specifically, neither case is actionable. The security does not exist.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria for AMWD**: There are none. American Woodmark will not re-list. The ticker should be removed from the active coverage universe once deregistration is confirmed, and no capital should be allocated to it under any scenario.

**For holders who received MBC shares (approximately 5.150 per AMWD share)**: The position is now a MasterBrand investment and requires evaluation on MasterBrand's disclosures. The specific items to establish before sizing that exposure:

1. **Purchase accounting on AMWD.** What was the acquired goodwill and intangible balance, and did MasterBrand write down any portion of AMWD's $767.6M pre-merger goodwill at or after close? This determines whether the acquired asset base was carried at a defensible value.
2. **Combined leverage against the stated path.** MasterBrand targets net leverage below 2x by end-2028. Track quarterly net debt against combined EBITDA, and specifically whether AMWD's cash burn (negative $21.0M free cash flow in its last reported quarter) has reversed inside the combined entity.
3. **Synergy realization versus the $90M three-year target.** Look for dollar-quantified progress by quarter rather than reaffirmation language, and for the associated cash restructuring cost.
4. **Retail channel concentration disclosure.** Combined Home Depot and Lowe's exposure, and any change in shelf-space or program terms following consolidation of the #1 and #2/#3 suppliers.
5. **Section 232 economics with the January 1, 2027 step to 50%.** Whether management begins quantifying a net benefit, and specifically how Mexican-origin cabinets and components are treated.

**Timeline**: The next natural decision point for the residual exposure is MasterBrand's Q3 2026 report, which will be the first full quarter including American Woodmark and the first period in which combined margins, working capital, and synergy capture can be measured against the $2.05–2.11B second-half revenue guidance. The tariff step on January 1, 2027 is the next external catalyst.

## Conclusion

**AVOID, overall score 3.4.** The recommendation rests first on availability: American Woodmark ceased to be a listed equity on May 28, 2026, the pricing snapshot returns no valid price or market date, analyst coverage has terminated, and no valuation or scenario arithmetic can be constructed. This reverses the June HOLD (4.9) and breaks the improving score trajectory, and the reversal is appropriate: the earlier readings were assessing a business that had, by the time of the third report, already been absorbed into MasterBrand.

The second leg is the terminal operating record, which is worse than the FY2025 GAAP presentation implied. Adjusted EPS fell 19.8% and adjusted EBITDA 17.5% in FY2025 while GAAP EPS fell only 9.1%, cushioned by an intangible amortization roll-off, lower incentive accruals, and buybacks that consumed roughly 1.5x free cash flow. FY2026 then missed roughly flat revenue guidance by 12–14 percentage points over nine months and ended its last reported quarter with an 11.64% gross margin, a $33.9M operating loss, negative $21.0M free cash flow, and $28.3M of cash. Whether the $767.6M goodwill survived that deterioration is unresolved and now sits inside MasterBrand's accounts.

Former holders own approximately 37% of a combined company with a plausible cost-synergy case and tariff optionality that was deliberately not underwritten in the deal economics. That exposure may be worth holding, but it is an MBC decision requiring MBC diligence (leverage path, purchase accounting, retail concentration, and synergy capture) that this report does not supply. On the AMWD ticker itself, no position, long or short, is available or advisable.
