## Executive Summary

Toast has finished the transition from a cash-burning growth story to a profitable one. Revenue compounded from $2,731M in FY2022 to $6,153M in FY2025; net income went from –$275M to +$342M; Adjusted EBITDA from –$115M to +$633M; free cash flow to $608M. H1 2026 extended the trend: revenue $3,538M (+22.6%), operating income $262M (+113%), net income $280M (+106%). Q2 2026 delivered 175bp of gross margin expansion and 281bp of operating margin expansion on 23.1% revenue growth, with operating expenses up only 17% and stock-based compensation *falling* in absolute dollars ($55M vs $60M). That last item is rare at this growth rate and deserves credit. The balance sheet carries zero drawn debt against $1,713M of unencumbered cash and securities plus an undrawn $347M revolver.

The problem is price and an unpriced regulatory binary. The supplied market capitalization of $18.3B reflects only the 512M Class A shares and omits 65M economically identical Class B shares; on all 577M shares outstanding the equity is ~$20.5B and enterprise value ~$18.8B. That is 23.5x the midpoint of guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, 32.6x trailing free cash flow (a 2.8% FCF yield), 43x trailing GAAP EPS, and ~56x earnings normalized to a 25% tax rate — Toast paid a 1.2% effective rate in FY2025 against a fully-reserved $436M deferred tax asset. Against consensus mean $38.12 and median $39.00, the stock has already recovered roughly 55-60% from its mid-May low of $22.26 and sits 24% below the August 2025 high of $46.81. The margin of safety that existed at $23 does not exist at $35.50.

The item that caps conviction rather than the multiple is Note 11 of the Q2 2026 10-Q. Toast received an FTC Civil Investigative Demand in June 2025 under the FTC Act, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act; in June 2026 FTC staff delivered a **draft complaint and a proposed settlement order**. Toast "strongly disagrees," is "prepared to litigate," says resolution "could have a materially adverse impact," and carries no accrual and no estimable range. Those three statutes point at subscription enrollment, auto-renewal and cancellation mechanics plus financial-data safeguarding — the machinery through which Toast attaches multiple products, and payments in particular, to ~180,000 small-business locations. There was no 8-K (none is required for a CID) and no press coverage across multiple searches. The options market is pricing near-term volatility *below* realized and skewing toward calls, which is consistent with the market not having registered the disclosure. We rate the stock HOLD: own it if you do, but the entry math does not support new capital here.

## Company Overview

Toast sells an integrated restaurant operating system (cloud POS software, payment processing, hardware, payroll, working-capital lending, and insurance brokerage) to small and mid-sized food service operators, and increasingly to food-and-beverage retail. As of June 30, 2026 it served approximately 180,000 Locations, up 22% year over year, and processed $215B of gross payment volume on a trailing-twelve-month basis. The company estimates it reaches roughly 20% of U.S. restaurant locations.

FY2025 revenue broke down as subscription services $936M at a 71.8% gross margin, financial technology solutions $5,037M at 22.8%, and hardware plus professional services $180M at **negative 122%** — a $220M gross loss. FinTech revenue is reported gross of interchange and network fees, which is why consolidated gross margin looks like a hardware manufacturer's 25.9% while the underlying software economics are strong. Hardware is deliberately sold below cost as a customer-acquisition subsidy, recovered through payments take rate and rising software ARPU. The FY2025 risk factors state that "except for a small subset of our customers, businesses are unable to subscribe to our platform without also subscribing to our payment services." Payments attach is effectively mandatory, which is both the engine of the model and the reason the FTC's statutory focus deserves attention.

Two structural facts carry forward. Toast does not own its lending: Toast Capital loans are originated by a single third-party Utah-chartered industrial bank, with Toast marketing and servicing them. Toast's platform runs on AWS as sole cloud provider. Neither is unusual, but both concentrate dependency.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating leverage is genuine, and partly mechanical.** Q2 2026 gross margin of 27.04% versus 25.29% and operating margin of 7.97% versus 5.16% are real. But GAAP recurring gross profit (subscription + FinTech) grew 30.9% while the non-GAAP version — adding back SBC and depreciation allocated to those cost lines — grew 28.2%. The difference comes from D&A inside subscription and FinTech COGS falling from $29M to $13M in H1 as capitalized-software cohorts fully amortized. Subscription cost of revenue actually *declined* in H1 (to $124M from $130M), lifting subscription gross margin 760bp to 77.8%. Capitalized software gross assets rose from $150M to $202M during 2025, so this tailwind reverses. Underlying leverage is roughly 28%, not 31%.

**Take rate is expanding, modestly.** Net take rate (FinTech revenue less FinTech cost, over GPV) improved from 0.569% in Q2 2025 to 0.591% in Q2 2026, about 2.2bp. A broader monetization rate — recurring GAAP gross profit over GPV — moved from 0.896% to 0.964%, roughly 6.8bp. Toast is capturing more per dollar of volume.

**Per-location economics are flat, and that is the central operating question.** GPV per Location was $337K in Q2 2026 versus $338K in Q2 2025. ARR per Location was $13,383 versus $13,071, up 2.4%. Against 22% Location growth, 22% GPV growth and 25% ARR growth, roughly 22 points of the growth came from unit expansion and 2-3 points from deepening. Three explanations cannot be separated with disclosed data: enterprise "Non-Toast Processing Locations" sit in the Location denominator and contribute zero GPV; new international and retail locations may carry lower average tickets; or restaurant same-store sales are genuinely soft. ARR per Location also excludes Toast Capital fees by definition, so it understates monetization. Flat GPV per Location should not be read as a clean consumer-spending indicator, but it does mean the compounding engine is distribution, and distribution has a domestic ceiling at ~20% penetration.

**Cash flow moved the wrong way in H1 2026 for identifiable reasons.** Operating cash flow was $276M versus $302M and free cash flow $245M versus $277M, despite net income doubling. Inventories consumed $103M of cash versus contributing $15M a year earlier — a $118M swing — with inventory rising from $114M to $217M including a newly broken-out $58M raw materials line. Non-cancellable hardware purchase commitments jumped to $194M from $106M, all due within twelve months. The FY2025 10-K names the AI-driven memory chip shortage as a hardware sourcing risk; the build is consistent with precautionary sourcing ahead of higher component costs, which is prudent but points to 2027 hardware COGS pressure on a segment already at a $140M H1 gross loss (versus $101M). Separately, capitalized sales commissions grew from $220M to $267M, with $93M capitalized against $46M amortized — a $47M cash outflow equal to about 18% of H1 operating income that GAAP operating expense does not carry. Neither item is an accounting concern; together they mean GAAP operating income is currently running ahead of cash generation, reversing FY2025 when FCF of $608M exceeded net income of $342M.

**The balance sheet is stronger than screens suggest.** Total liabilities of $1,136M against $2,045M equity produce a headline debt-to-equity of roughly 0.5x, but $638M of those liabilities — 56% — are payment-settlement pass-throughs ($422M accrued transaction-based costs and a $216M customer funds obligation matched by an equal cash asset). Drawn debt is zero. The revolver was undrawn with $3M of letters of credit and full covenant compliance at June 30. There is no maturity wall and no refinancing exposure to current long-end pressure.

**Taxes are the least-appreciated line.** FY2025 tax expense was $4M on $346M of pretax income, a 1.2% rate, because of a full $436M valuation allowance. Federal NOLs are $928M ($843M indefinite-lived); state NOLs $873M, of which $718M begins expiring in 2026. Both the 10-K and the Q2 10-Q say a portion or all of the allowance could be released within twelve months. A release produces a large one-time non-cash tax benefit that inflates GAAP EPS in that quarter and then raises the ongoing GAAP rate — an accounting recognition event, not an economic improvement. Normalizing TTM pretax income of $498M at 25% gives $374M of net income against $486M reported, meaning headline TTM earnings are roughly 30% above a tax-normalized figure. With federal NOLs of $928M against a pretax run-rate approaching $500-600M and an 80% taxable-income limitation, cash taxes should begin biting in 2027-2028. That is a dateable FCF headwind, not a hypothetical.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is decelerating on a predictable curve: 41.5% (FY2023), 28.3% (FY2024), 24.0% (FY2025), 22.6% (H1 2026). Location growth of 22% with a record 9,500 net adds in Q2 (versus 7,000 in Q1) is the strongest single evidence that share gains continue. ARR reached $2,409M, up 25%.

Management raised FY2026 recurring gross profit growth guidance from 21-23% (May 8) to 23-25% (August 4). Mapping guided "RGP" to non-GAAP subscription plus FinTech gross profit — verified by reconciling the CEO's "27%" Q1 statement to $529M versus $415M — the FY2026 range implies $2,321-2,359M against FY2025's $1,887M. H1 actual was $1,124M, so implied H2 is $1,197-1,235M against H2 2025 of $1,008M: 18.8-22.5% growth versus 27.9% in H1. That is a sensitivity derived from a guidance range, not a forecast, and the guide was *raised*, which tilts toward embedded conservatism. But it does mean the second half is not guided to sustain the first half's pace.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats of 20.8%, 23.2%, 26.2% and 24.9%. Estimate revisions are positive but modest — current year $1.38 (+2.6% over 30 days, +5.5% over 90 days), next year $1.74 (+2.5%, +2.9%). This is steady upward drift, not the 30%+ velocity that signals systematic underestimation. The supplied consensus contains an internal inconsistency: H1 GAAP EPS actual of $0.46 plus quarterly estimates of $0.37 and $0.34 sums to $1.17, not the $1.38 current-year figure, so the annual estimates likely sit on a different basis or embed a valuation-allowance release. We therefore use a range rather than a point when applying them.

Growth durability rests on new addressable markets. The Q2 10-Q rewrote the self-description from a restaurant platform to "a global technology platform built for restaurant **and retail** businesses… integrat[ing] software, **agentic AI**, payments, financial technology solutions, and hardware." Retail, international and hospitality are the answer to domestic saturation. None has disclosed unit economics yet, and the flat GPV per Location suggests new-vintage locations may monetize below the installed base initially.

## Competitive Landscape

Toast competes against Block's Square for Restaurants, Fiserv's Clover, Shift4, SpotOn, Lightspeed, PAR Technology, Olo, and legacy incumbents including Oracle MICROS and TouchBistro. The market is fragmented, and 22% Location growth with record net adds is credible evidence of continued share capture.

The defensible ground is the depth of the operating stack — payroll, inventory, capital, insurance (licensed in 50 states plus D.C.), 200+ technology partners, 67 granted or allowed U.S. patents — combined with field-sales density in an SMB market where local presence matters. What the filings do not support is proprietary payments infrastructure or proprietary lending capability.

The Google Maps agentic-ordering integration announced around August 6 illustrates the limits of that differentiation. Google owns the discovery surface; Toast merchants can be surfaced there and orders route to the merchant's Toast POS. Block's Square announced the identical integration on the same day, with menus and hours syncing from the Square Dashboard at no additional fee. The surface owner has every incentive to integrate breadth, which makes discovery-layer AI partnerships table stakes. The stock rose 6.7% on this and the Q2 print combined.

Two competitive pressure points show in the numbers. The hardware subsidy is deepening: gross loss of $181M (FY23), $170M (FY24), $220M (FY25), and $140M in H1 2026 versus $101M — from 12.0% to 12.7% of recurring gross profit. Moving upmarket dilutes the model: the 10-K discloses that select enterprise customers can be "Non-Toast Processing Locations" using Toast POS without Toast payments, that some enterprise arrangements carry no minimum location commitment, and that enterprise and mid-market price concessions are a risk. Enterprise wins trade unit count for take rate and contractual certainty.

## Risk Assessment

**FTC enforcement (dominant, unpriced).** A draft complaint accompanied by a proposed order is a late procedural posture: staff has formed a theory and priced a remedy. ROSCA governs negative-option marketing, auto-renewal and cancellation mechanics; FTC Act §5 covers deceptive practices; GLBA covers safeguarding customer financial data. The plausible injunctive component — mandated changes to enrollment, disclosure, renewal or cancellation across ~180,000 SMB locations — could matter more than any fine, because Toast's economics depend on multi-product attach and on the near-mandatory payments bundle. The counter-case is real: most draft complaints resolve in negotiated orders with modest monetary components and process remediation, and Toast raises a threshold question about whether the cited statutes apply to its business model. We cannot size this. The market has been given no notice of it outside Note 11.

**Toast Capital credit exposure, growing ~4x faster than volume.** The contingent liability for expected credit losses went from $29M (12/31/24) to $46M (12/31/25) to $53M (6/30/26) — up 83% in eighteen months against 22% GPV growth. The non-contingent stand-ready liability is $21M; restricted cash collateral with the bank partner $73M. Guarantee credit-loss expense was $18M in Q2 versus $11M (+64%) and $35M in H1 versus $27M. Total credit loss expense in the cash flow statement was $54M in H1 versus $40M. Ernst & Young designated the valuation of this contingent liability the **sole Critical Audit Matter** in the FY2025 audit. Toast must purchase ineligible loans, capped at 15% of original principal of loans made in the applicable quarter. Toast has newly begun purchasing *performing* loans and holding them for investment — $80M purchased, $30M repaid, $46M net at June 30 from $0 at year-end. That changes the risk type from guaranteeing defaults to owning credit, and the portfolio is entirely unseasoned. Two readings cannot be separated without origination volumes: either SMB borrower credit is deteriorating, or Toast is scaling originations faster than payments at a stable loss rate. The move into performing paper suggests the latter is at least partly true. The 10-K also flags "true lender" and state usury challenge risk with no formal proceedings to date.

**SMB and consumer cyclicality.** Toast's customers are small restaurants with structurally high failure rates. Flat GPV per Location is at minimum consistent with soft same-store sales. The Q2 10-Q newly names tariffs as an uncertainty for consumer demand and restaurant operations.

**Hardware cost inflation.** The 10-K names AI-driven memory shortage as a sourcing risk. Toast's hardware segment already runs at a deeply negative gross margin; the $58M new raw-materials line and $194M of twelve-month purchase commitments indicate management is pre-buying, which defers but does not eliminate the pressure. A one-time ~$10M tariff refund flattered Q2 COGS.

**Governance and dilution.** Class B carries ten votes and holds 55.9% of voting power on 11.3% of the economics, with an automatic sunset on September 24, 2028. Direction is favorable — organic conversions took Class B from 81M (12/31/24) to 65M (6/30/26) and voting power from 62.3% to 55.9% — though Class A-only repurchases mechanically push the other way (~1.2pp per $1B repurchased). Outstanding awards of 39M shares represent 6.3% ownership dilution if all vest; $417M of unrecognized compensation cost remains over ~three years. The 119M plan-authorized shares are future capacity, not current dilution. The 10-K notes previously identified material weaknesses; E&Y attested to effective controls as of December 31, 2025, and disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026.

**Concentration that is not a risk:** no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.8% sits five points *below* one-year realized volatility of 46.8%, the term structure is in contango (44.4% at six months), put/call volume is 0.44 and open interest 0.46 — call-tilted — and near-term skew is balanced. Short interest is 8.0% of float at 2.8 days to cover: not crowded. The one anomaly is 806 contracts of August 14 $33 puts against 102 open interest (7.9x), a small position just below spot. The composite read is a market pricing no near-term binary. Set against the unaccrued, late-stage FTC matter with an unquantifiable injunctive component, that is a divergence: near-term optionality is cheap relative to a known, dated, unresolved regulatory process. The six-month implied move of ±22.2% does price a wide twelve-month distribution, which is appropriate. For holders unwilling to trim, longer-dated protection is inexpensive relative to realized volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $47 (+32%).** Location adds hold above 9,000 per quarter as retail, international and hospitality open genuine new TAM; the FY2026 RGP guide proves conservative as it did at Q1; take rate continues expanding 2-3bp annually; the FTC matter resolves in a negotiated order with a modest fine and process remediation that does not disturb subscription attach; the inventory build unwinds in H2 and FY2026 FCF exceeds $650M; a valuation-allowance release plus continued 20%+ EPS beats pull consensus toward $2.00+ for FY2027. At 24x FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$1.05B plus $2B net cash, equity is ~$27B, or $47 per share. Analyst high target is $45.

**Base case — $38.50 (+8.5%).** Revenue growth decelerates to ~20% in FY2027; RGP growth lands near the middle of guidance with the H2 step-down partly realized; net take rate grinds higher; hardware losses stabilize as memory pre-buys absorb cost; the FTC settles with a fine in the tens of millions and disclosure/cancellation-process changes that trim but do not break attach; cash taxes begin in 2027, compressing FCF growth below EBITDA growth. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA ~$1.0B at 20x EV/EBITDA plus net cash gives $37.6-38.5. This coincides with the consensus median of $39.00.

**Bear case — $25 (–30%).** The FTC settlement order requires material changes to enrollment, renewal or cancellation practices, or challenges the mandatory payments bundle, pressuring ARR retention and new-location attach; Location adds decelerate below 6,000 per quarter as U.S. penetration saturates and new TAMs monetize below the installed base; SMB credit deteriorates and the Toast Capital contingent liability plus the unseasoned held-for-investment book produce a step-change in credit expense; hardware COGS inflation from memory pricing deepens the subsidy; growth settles in the mid-teens and the multiple compresses to 15x on roughly $850M of EBITDA, giving ~$14.5B equity or $25. Analyst low target is $25.00. The five-year TSR record demonstrates this multiple compression is not hypothetical for this stock.

Probability-weighting roughly 55% base, 25% bull, 20% bear gives an expected return near +5%. That is approximately fair for a name with 47% realized volatility and an unquantifiable regulatory binary, which is where HOLD belongs rather than ACCUMULATE or REDUCE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026) should reveal whether H2 RGP growth lands at the guided 19-22% or above, whether the H1 inventory build converts back to cash, and whether any FTC development has been reported. Any 8-K or 10-Q update on the FTC matter is an immediate reassessment trigger regardless of date.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **FTC resolution disclosed with a quantified, non-structural remedy** — a monetary settlement without injunctive terms that alter subscription enrollment, renewal, cancellation or the payments bundle. This alone would justify moving to ACCUMULATE at current levels.
2. **Price at or below $29** with fundamentals intact (Location adds ≥8,000/quarter, RGP growth ≥22%), which restores a base-case return above 30%.
3. **Evidence of per-location monetization inflecting** — ARR per Location growth above 6% year over year, or GPV per Location returning to growth for two consecutive quarters, which would demonstrate the model compounds on more than distribution.
4. **H2 2026 free cash flow above $350M**, confirming the inventory build was timing rather than a structural COGS shift, alongside a resumed and expanded buyback authorization.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **An FTC settlement order or complaint containing injunctive requirements** that change enrollment, renewal, cancellation or bundling practices — regardless of the monetary amount. This would put the ARR durability assumption in the base case at risk.
2. **Net Location adds below 6,000 in any quarter**, or Location growth decelerating below 15% year over year, which would break the unit-expansion engine that supplies ~22 of Toast's ~24 points of growth.
3. **Toast Capital credit deterioration** — the contingent liability exceeding $75M, guarantee credit-loss expense growth above 80% year over year, or a disclosed allowance on the held-for-investment loan book above 3% of principal.
4. **Recurring gross profit growth guidance cut below 21%**, or two consecutive quarters of recurring gross profit growth below 20%.
5. **Hardware gross loss exceeding 15% of recurring gross profit** on a trailing basis, indicating memory-cost inflation is outrunning pricing.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. Given six-month implied volatility of 44.4% priced roughly in line with realized, and an unresolved regulatory process, purchasing longer-dated downside protection is reasonable for holders who cannot tolerate the bear case.

## Conclusion

Toast is a better business than it was two years ago and its financial position is genuinely strong: zero drawn debt, $1.71B of liquidity, four straight 20%+ earnings beats, operating expenses growing 6 points slower than revenue, stock compensation declining in absolute dollars, and a management team that repurchased 19M shares at an average of $25.58 during the H1 drawdown and stopped buying in June as the price recovered — roughly $190M of mark-to-market value created versus repurchasing today. That capital-allocation record is the strongest single piece of shareholder-alignment evidence in the file, and it is only partly offset by zero open-market insider purchases in sixteen months, compensation metrics tied to non-GAAP measures that exclude the largest non-cash cost, and a five-year TSR that trails the company's own peer group by roughly 152 points.

What we cannot do is underwrite the price. At $35.4951 the corrected enterprise value of $18.8B is 23.5x guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, 32.7x trailing free cash flow, and ~56x tax-normalized earnings, after a 55-60% rally off the May low. Base case is $38.50, roughly 8.5% above spot and in line with the consensus median. Against that sits an FTC draft complaint and proposed settlement order carrying no accrual, no estimable range, no external coverage, and a statutory focus on the exact subscription mechanics that make the model work — plus a lending contingent liability growing four times faster than payment volume and flagged by the auditor as the sole Critical Audit Matter, and a new unseasoned book of purchased performing loans. Growth is decelerating on plan, guidance embeds an H2 step-down, and cash taxes arrive in 2027-2028.

**HOLD.** Existing owners should stay, monitor the FTC docket, and treat cheap longer-dated protection as sensible. New capital should wait for either a clean regulatory resolution or a price near $29, where the base case restores the asymmetry that a $23 entry offered three months ago.
