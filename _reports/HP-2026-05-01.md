## Executive Summary

Helmerich & Payne is the largest U.S. land drilling company, operating 367 rigs globally following its January 2025 acquisition of KCA Deutag for approximately $2.0 billion. The deal was intended to diversify HP into international and offshore markets, but execution has been severely challenged: 27 Saudi rigs were suspended, the company recorded a $192M goodwill impairment within five months of closing, and net income swung to a $164M loss in FY2025. Long-term debt increased from $545M to over $2B, interest expense rose 4x to $108M annually, and both the CEO and CFO have been replaced during the most critical phase of integration.

The stock has rallied 113% from its May 2025 low of $14.65 to $40.38, now trading within 2% of its 52-week high and at a level that implies substantial recovery in international operations and sustained commodity strength. Consensus analyst targets center around $38, below the current price. With a trailing P/E that is meaningfully negative, 3 of 4 recent quarterly earnings misses, 11.2% short interest, and the options market in backwardation pricing near-term stress, the risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Helmerich & Payne, founded in 1920 and headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma, operates through three segments:

**North America Solutions (NAS):** 223 available rigs, 144 active at FY2025 end. HP commands roughly 37% market share in the Permian Basin with its proprietary FlexRig® platform, and 50% of active rigs operate under performance-based contracts. NAS generated $2.36B in FY2025 revenue, down 3.4% YoY as legacy E&P capital discipline reduced drilling activity.

**International Solutions:** 137 available rigs, 61 active at FY2025 end. This segment was dramatically expanded by the KCA Deutag acquisition, with operations in Saudi Arabia (71 rigs, 27 suspended), Argentina, Colombia, Europe, Africa, and Australia. This segment posted a $292M operating loss in FY2025.

**Offshore Solutions:** 7 platform rigs (3 active), primarily asset-light management contracts in the North Sea, Angola, Azerbaijan, and Canada — inherited from KCA Deutag.

The company also operates BENTEC™, a manufacturing/engineering unit from KCA Deutag. Total contract backlog stands at $7.0B, up from $1.5B pre-acquisition, though the quality of that backlog is a central concern discussed below.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $2.05B | $2.86B | $2.75B | $3.68B |
| Operating Income | $45M | $562M | $452M | $3.3M |
| Net Income | $7M | $434M | $344M | -$164M |
| EPS (diluted) | $0.07 | $4.22 | $3.43 | -$1.66 |

Revenue grew 34% to $3.68B in FY2025, but virtually all of that growth came from ~258 days of consolidated KCA Deutag operations. The legacy North America business contracted. Operating income collapsed from $452M to $3.3M, burdened by $192M in goodwill impairment, $55M in acquisition costs, and $108M in interest expense (up from $29M).

### Balance Sheet Stress

Long-term debt increased from $545M (FY2023) to $2.057B (FY2025). The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 0.20x to 2.37x. Net debt stands at approximately $1.86B. The debt structure includes:

- $350M at 4.65% due 2027
- $350M at 4.85% due 2029
- $550M at 2.90% due 2031
- $550M at 5.50% due 2034
- ~$190M term loan (partially repaid, demonstrating deleveraging intent)
- $83M Oman facility loans

The company maintains $1.14B in total liquidity ($197M cash + $950M undrawn revolver), which provides adequate near-term cushion. However, annual interest expense of $108M+ consumes a meaningful share of the $543M in operating cash flow and $117M in free cash flow generated in FY2025.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow declined from $685M (FY2024) to $543M (FY2025). FCF was $117M after $426M in capex. Management guided FY2026 capex at $280-320M, which should improve FCF generation. The base dividend was cut from $1.68/share (FY2024) to $1.00/share, and the supplemental dividend was suspended entirely. Share buybacks have effectively ceased. These are rational capital allocation decisions given the leverage, but they represent material deterioration in shareholder returns.

### Recent Quarterly Performance

Q1 FY2026 (December 2025 quarter) delivered revenue of $1.02B (+50.2% YoY) but a loss of $0.15/share versus consensus of $0.10 profit. The prior four quarters show a troubling pattern: 3 misses and 1 in-line, including a -245.6% miss in Q1 FY2026 and a -95% miss in Q2 FY2025.

### Estimate Revisions

Current quarter (Q2 FY2026) consensus has deteriorated from $0.08 profit 90 days ago to a $0.04 loss currently. Current-year (FY2026) estimates have fallen 63% over 90 days from $0.46 to $0.17. Next-year (FY2027) estimates rose 29% over 90 days to $1.07, reflecting expectations that Saudi rig resumptions and synergy realization will eventually flow through. This creates a "valley earnings" setup where the central question is whether the recovery materializes as priced.

## Growth Analysis

HP's growth thesis rests on three pillars, each carrying meaningful execution risk:

**1. Saudi Arabia Rig Resumptions:** Of 27 suspended rigs, 7 received resumption notices post-FYE, expected to restart in H1 calendar 2026, bringing Saudi operating rigs to 24. If successful, this closes the largest drag on international profitability. However, the remaining 20+ suspended rigs represent dead capital, and the timeline for further resumptions is uncertain. Saudi Aramco's drilling activity is a function of OPEC+ production targets and national oil policy — neither of which HP controls.

**2. Synergy Realization:** Management doubled its synergy target from $25M to $50M+, including workforce reductions. This is credible but partially offset by integration friction. The simultaneous CEO and CFO transitions add execution risk to this process.

**3. North America Stabilization:** NAS rig count needs to stabilize. Active rigs fell from 151 to 144, backlog declined from $700M to $500M, and Q4 direct margin guidance was $230-250M versus $266M actual. With U.S. E&P capital discipline intact and OPEC+ potentially adding supply, NAS stabilization is not assured.

At $40.38, the stock is priced at roughly 38x FY2027E EPS of $1.07 — an aggressive multiple for a cyclical, leveraged drilling company with 3 of 4 recent quarterly misses. If FY2027 EPS arrives closer to $1.50 on full Saudi recovery plus synergies, the multiple compresses to 27x — still not cheap for this sector. The growth case exists but is priced optimistically.

## Competitive Landscape

In North America, HP retains genuine advantages: largest fleet, dominant Permian share (~37%), proprietary FlexRig® technology, and 50% performance-contract penetration. These structural moats have been built over decades and are defensible.

Internationally, the position is far more precarious. The KCA Deutag acquisition provides scale, but:

- Contracts lack early termination fee protections, meaning the $5.7B international/offshore backlog is structurally less firm than legacy HP contracts
- Saudi Aramco suspensions demonstrated the fragility of NOC-dependent revenue
- Competition from local and regional players (ADES in Saudi Arabia, local operators in Latin America) is intensifying
- The offshore management contracts are asset-light and recurring, but represent a small portion of overall economics

R&D spending declined from $41M to $34M as integration cost-cutting took priority, which could erode the technology differentiation advantage over time.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating in the near term: NAS is contracting, international operations are loss-making, and management bandwidth is consumed by integration rather than innovation.

## Risk Assessment

### Idiosyncratic Risks Not Fully Priced

**Saudi Rig Resumptions (HIGH):** The $479M of backlog from suspended contracts could evaporate without early termination payments given the KCA Deutag contract structure. If further suspensions occur or resumptions are delayed beyond H2 2026, the recovery thesis breaks.

**Simultaneous CEO/CFO Turnover (HIGH):** Trey Adams replaced John Lindsay as CEO on March 4, 2026. CFO Kevin Vann retires June 30, 2026, replaced by Todd Scruggs on July 1. Replacing both C-suite principals during a $2B acquisition integration is a material execution risk. Neither successor has a track record at HP that investors can evaluate.

**Balance Sheet Fragility (MODERATE-HIGH):** At 2.37x D/E, the company has limited margin for error in a cyclical downturn. The target of 1x net-debt-to-EBITDA by 2026 requires EBITDA expansion that is not yet visible in reported results.

**Further Goodwill Impairment (MODERATE):** Offshore Solutions fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~20% at last test. If oil prices weaken or offshore contracts are not renewed, additional impairment is possible on the remaining $183M goodwill and $486M in intangible assets.

**Oil Price Sensitivity (MODERATE):** Management explicitly flags that tariff announcements and OPEC+ supply increases could lead to reduced activity in fiscal year 2026. U.S. E&P capital discipline means rig count response to higher prices has been minimal. HP benefits only if customers choose to drill, and the evidence suggests they are choosing not to increase activity.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 54.9% is 10.6 percentage points rich to 1-year historical volatility of 44.4%, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (54.9% near-term vs. 48.5% at 6 months), consistent with near-term event risk pricing. The P/C OI ratio of 0.11 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting speculative positioning on continued upside — which diverges from the fundamental picture. The prior snapshot showed IV at 355.7% (likely a data anomaly or event-driven spike), but the normalization to 54.9% alongside a 21.8% price increase suggests the acute stress period has passed while residual uncertainty remains.

Short interest at 11.2% of float with 6 days to cover is elevated. Institutional bearish positioning is meaningful but not at levels that create obvious squeeze risk.

Any Saudi Aramco announcement on further rig suspensions or OPEC+ supply increases would directly impair the thesis.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** at this entry. The fundamental thesis is bearish, but the stock is near its 52-week high with speculative call-heavy positioning, making squeeze risk in the immediate term non-trivial. The appropriate posture is to avoid owning the stock. If the stock rallies above $42-43 on no new fundamental developments, the short setup would improve.

## Conclusion

Helmerich & Payne is a high-quality North American driller that made a transformational acquisition at an unfortunate time. The KCA Deutag deal increased debt nearly fourfold, introduced structurally weaker contract terms, and coincided with Saudi rig suspensions that drove the International segment to a $292M operating loss. The company has now replaced both its CEO and CFO during the most complex integration phase.

At $40.38, the stock trades above the consensus mean target of $38.40 and prices in a recovery that has not yet materialized in reported results. FY2026E EPS of $0.17 and current-quarter estimates of -$0.04 do not support the current valuation.

The turnaround could eventually work. Saudi rig resumptions, synergy capture, and lower capex could produce a credible $1.00-1.50 EPS path by FY2027-2028. But that outcome is not sufficiently probable at current prices to justify ownership. The risk/reward is asymmetrically skewed to the downside. AVOID.
