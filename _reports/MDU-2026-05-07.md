## Executive Summary

MDU Resources has completed a multi-year transformation from a construction conglomerate into a pure-play regulated utility, spinning off Knife River (2023) and Everus (2024). The remaining entity — electric, natural gas distribution, and pipeline operations serving 1.2M+ customers across 8 states — generated $0.93 EPS in 2025, with guidance of $0.93-$1.00 for 2026 and a long-term 6-8% EPS growth target.

The central investment question is the Bakken East Pipeline, a $2.7-3.2B project (59-70% of current market cap) that attracted 1.4 Bcf/day of shipper interest in its binding open season. If financed and built, it would more than double pipeline segment rate base and could drive substantial earnings growth. However, the stock at $22.50 trades at 24x trailing earnings — a 28% premium to the peer median P/E of 18.8x — suggesting the market already assigns meaningful probability to Bakken East success. With a September 2025 covenant breach, negative free cash flow, ongoing equity dilution, and a Q1 2026 earnings miss, the risk-reward is balanced at current levels.

The 12-month thesis rests on progress toward a final investment decision on Bakken East and continued rate base growth through pending rate cases in 6+ states. These catalysts are concrete and dated, but the premium valuation limits upside if execution merely meets expectations rather than exceeds them.

## Company Overview

MDU Resources is a Bismarck, North Dakota-headquartered regulated energy delivery company with three operating segments:

**Electric Utility:** Serves ~146,550 customers across 185 communities in North Dakota, Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming. Rate base of $1.8B. Generation mix includes coal, natural gas peakers, and growing renewables (Badger Wind Farm 49% interest acquired December 2025; ~40% of owned generation from renewable sources).

**Natural Gas Distribution:** Serves ~1.08M customers across 343 communities in 8 states via Montana-Dakota, Cascade, and Intermountain subsidiaries. Rate base of $2.2B. Approximately 22,000 miles of distribution and 540 miles of transmission lines.

**Pipeline (WBI Energy):** 3,800+ miles of natural gas transmission and storage, 193 Bcf of working gas storage capacity. Strategically positioned near four producing basins including the Bakken. Net plant investment of $1.0B.

The company employs 2,096 people and serves territories experiencing above-average population and economic growth.

## Growth Analysis

MDU's growth thesis has two distinct components:

**Organic Rate Base Growth (Base Case):**
- $3.1B capex plan over 2026-2030 drives mid-single-digit rate base growth
- Active rate cases in Montana ($10.4M), Wyoming ($5.8M), Idaho ($13.0M), Washington ($10.8M Year 2), Oregon ($16.4M pending), plus North Dakota and Minnesota cases anticipated later in 2026
- Data center load: Applied Digital's Ellendale, ND facility at 180 MW current, 350 MW approved (530 MW total potential), plus 50 MW site near Leola, SD
- This alone supports the 6-8% EPS growth target

**Bakken East Pipeline (Optionality):**
- $2.7-3.2B investment; 1.4 Bcf/day shipper interest in binding open season
- ~40% signed under precedent agreements; remainder in active negotiation
- North Dakota committed $50M/year for 10 years (political support)
- Phase 1 targeted November 2029; Phase 2 November 2030
- No final investment decision yet; financing structure unknown (balance sheet vs. partnerships vs. other)
- If fully built on MDU's balance sheet, could more than double pipeline rate base
- Realistic probability: partnership structure likely given MDU's capital constraints

**Growth Headwinds:**
- Washington building electrification codes threaten gas distribution growth (35% of gas distribution retail revenue at risk)
- Coyote Station (coal) faces regulatory uncertainty — EPA mercury/GHG regulations, regional haze
- Q1 2026 miss (-7.2%) suggests execution isn't flawless
- Earnings track record: 3 misses in last 4 quarters

MDU trades at a 28% premium to peers on P/E and offers 130bps less yield. The premium reflects growth optionality (Bakken East, data center demand, above-average customer growth), but this optionality is already being capitalized by the market.

**Forward valuation:** At $0.97 consensus FY2026 EPS, the forward P/E is 23.2x. At 6-8% growth, FY2027 EPS of ~$1.06 produces 21.2x forward. For this to be attractive, you need to believe Bakken East delivers incremental EPS above the base $1.06 estimate — but that project's earnings contribution won't appear until 2030+ at the earliest.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean $22.14, median $22.00, range $20-$24. At $22.50, the stock trades above the consensus median with only 7% upside to the high target. J.P. Morgan initiated Neutral with a $22 target in April 2026, explicitly noting "premium valuation offsets pipeline upside."

**Dividend:** $0.56 annualized ($0.14/quarter) = 2.5% yield. Below-peer yield means the stock must deliver growth to justify holding. 60% payout ratio on FY2025 EPS is sustainable but leaves limited buffer given negative FCF.

The market is pricing MDU as if Bakken East is at least partially proceeding. If the project is delayed, scaled down, or cancelled, de-rating toward peer average (18.8x on $1.00 EPS = $18.80) is the risk. That represents 16% downside.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Service territories in above-average growth regions (Bakken, Pacific Northwest)
- Multi-state regulatory diversification across 8 jurisdictions reduces single-state risk
- Strategic pipeline positioning near four producing basins with storage assets
- MISO membership provides wholesale market access and transmission planning
- Data center demand creates incremental load growth unavailable to most small-cap utilities

**Weaknesses:**
- Small scale ($4.6B market cap) limits capital access relative to larger utilities
- Heavy dependence on weather (Q1 2026 miss attributed to warm weather)
- Coal plant joint ownership constrains retirement timing decisions
- Customer concentration: Applied Digital could represent 20%+ of electric load at full buildout

Competitive position is stable to improving. Customer growth at 3x national average and Bakken East positioning provide differentiated growth vectors. However, the company lacks the balance sheet depth of larger peers to fund ambitious capital programs without dilution.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Bakken East Financing/Execution (High Impact, Medium Probability):** At $2.7-3.2B for a $4.6B market cap company already at 2.75x D/E with negative FCF, the financing challenge is acute. Continued equity dilution, partnership dilution of economics, or project delays/cancellation are all realistic outcomes.

2. **Covenant Compliance (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** The September 2025 breach demonstrates operational proximity to financial limits. Additional capex stress could trigger further covenant pressure, constraining growth spending.

3. **Regulatory Lag (Medium Impact, High Probability):** Operating across 8 states with varying rate case timelines in a rising-cost environment means earned returns will intermittently compress.

4. **Washington Building Electrification (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** Court ruled Initiative 2066 unconstitutional; pending state Supreme Court review. 35% of gas distribution retail revenue is at stake over the medium-to-long term.

5. **Data Center Customer Concentration (Medium Impact, Low Probability):** Applied Digital's 530 MW potential represents significant concentration relative to MDU's generation portfolio. Credit quality and demand certainty of that counterparty matter.

6. **Equity Dilution (Low-Medium Impact, High Probability):** ~8% dilution over recent quarters with $70-95M more planned in 2026. Ongoing equity needs to fund growth compress per-share returns.

## Conclusion

MDU Resources is a well-positioned small-cap utility with legitimate above-average growth drivers: 1.5% customer growth, data center demand, active rate cases in 6+ states, and the Bakken East pipeline option. Management has executed well on the spinoff transformation (225% TSR vs. 163% peer group), and director insider buying at $20.85 is a modestly positive signal.

However, at 24x trailing earnings with a 2.5% yield, a 3-of-4 quarterly miss track record, a recent covenant breach, and ongoing equity dilution, the market is already capitalizing significant growth optionality. The stock sits at the analyst consensus price target with only $1.50 (7%) upside to the high target. Bakken East could drive re-rating above $24, but final investment decision is 6-12+ months away, and the financing structure will likely dilute per-share economics.

HOLD for investors already positioned who want exposure to the Bakken East option and above-average utility growth. The specific 12-month catalyst set — final investment decision, rate case outcomes, data center load confirmation — provides concrete reasons to continue holding. A pullback to $19-20 would create a more attractive entry point for new money.
