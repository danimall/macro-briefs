## Executive Summary

Sealed Air Corporation completed its acquisition by private equity firm Clayton, Dubilier & Rice (CD&R) on April 9, 2026, at $42.15 per share in an all-cash transaction. The company has been delisted from the NYSE. There is no investable security remaining for public market participants.

The acquisition concluded a multi-year period of shareholder value destruction (5-year TSR of -7% vs. peer group +42%), chronic CEO turnover (two changes in roughly 18 months), and structural revenue decline in the Protective segment. The $42.15 price represented a 41% premium to the unaffected stock price and valued the enterprise at approximately $10.3 billion. All regulatory approvals were obtained, and shareholders overwhelmingly approved the transaction on February 25, 2026.

For any investors holding residual positions or encountering SEE in legacy portfolios, the recommendation is to ensure all shares have been properly tendered and cash proceeds received.

## Company Overview

Sealed Air was a global packaging solutions provider operating through two segments: Food (67% of 2025 revenue, $3.6B) and Protective (33%, $1.8B). The Food segment, anchored by the CRYOVAC brand, served industrial food processors with barrier bags, pouches, films, and automation equipment. The Protective segment provided inflatable packaging, shrink films, and bagging systems under BUBBLE WRAP and AUTOBAG brands to e-commerce, consumer goods, and industrial markets.

The company operated in 46 countries with 16,100 employees, generating approximately 49% of sales outside the United States. Headquarters were in Charlotte, North Carolina.

## Financial Analysis

At the time of acquisition, Sealed Air's financial profile was characterized by:

**Revenue:** Three consecutive years of decline — $5,642M (2022) → $5,489M (2023) → $5,393M (2024) → $5,360M (2025). The Protective segment drove most of this weakness, falling 10% cumulatively over 2023-2025 due to customer churn in fulfillment and North American industrial weakness.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA grew modestly to $1,134M in 2025 (21.2% margin) as CTO2Grow cost savings ($160M annualized target achieved) offset volume declines. Adjusted EPS reached $3.34 in FY2025.

**Balance Sheet:** Extremely leveraged with $4.01B total debt, D/E of 4.67x, and an Altman Z-Score of 1.45 (distress zone). The company carried sub-investment-grade credit ratings (Ba1/BB+). Working capital was negative at -$182M due to $625M in near-term maturities reclassified as current.

**Cash Flow:** Free cash flow of $459M in 2025 (8.6% FCF margin) was directed entirely toward debt reduction. No share repurchases occurred in 2024-2025.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects were structurally limited at the time of acquisition:
- Revenue declined for three consecutive years
- The Protective segment faced secular headwinds from e-commerce packaging alternatives
- Food segment growth was constrained by formula pricing tied to raw material deflation
- Organic volume growth in Food turned negative in 2025 after briefly recovering in 2024

CD&R's acquisition thesis presumably centered on operational improvement and potential Protective segment restructuring under private ownership, neither of which benefits public shareholders.

## Valuation Assessment

The $42.15 acquisition price valued Sealed Air at approximately:
- 9.1x EV/EBITDA (on $1,134M Adjusted EBITDA)
- 12.6x trailing adjusted EPS ($3.34)
- 11.5x forward P/E (on analyst estimates of ~$3.67 for 2026)

These multiples were reasonable for a leveraged packaging company with declining revenue but strong cash flow generation. Analyst targets at the time ranged from $42.00 to $52.00, with the floor essentially set at the deal price.

## Competitive Landscape

Sealed Air maintained strong positioning in food packaging through CRYOVAC's technological leadership in barrier films and automation. However, the Protective segment faced intensifying competition from alternative materials, customer-developed solutions (particularly among large e-commerce players), and pricing pressure. The competitive position was stable in Food but eroding in Protective.
