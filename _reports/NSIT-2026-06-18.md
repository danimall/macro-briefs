**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Insight Enterprises is a solutions integrator transitioning from low-margin IT product distribution toward higher-margin services, cloud, cybersecurity, and AI integration. The headline financials look weak: net sales down from $10.4B (2022) to $8.2B (2025), GAAP EPS down to $4.86 from $6.55, long-term debt up from $292M to $1.47B, and the stock fell from a 52-week high of $148.58 to a March 2026 trough of $63.62. That decline reflects a real deterioration in 2025 GAAP optics. The market, and my prior assessment of this name, errs in reading those optics as the operating reality.

The reported revenue decline is overwhelmingly an accounting artifact: a deliberate principal-to-agent revenue recognition shift (software/cloud now booked net rather than gross) plus the on-prem-to-cloud migration booked net in services. Gross profit held roughly flat near $1.76B across 2024 and 2025 even as revenue fell, with gross margin expanding to a record 21.4% in 2025 and reaching 21.7% in Q1 2026 (+240bps YoY). The 2025 GAAP earnings weakness absorbed roughly $100M of charges: $37.1M severance/restructuring, $25.3M earnout revaluation, $25.1M warrant-settlement revaluation, $12.6M real-estate impairment, most of which do not recur. Adjusted earnings from operations were essentially flat at $504.0M. The market transacts on the adjusted base: consensus current-year EPS is $11.43, management guides $11.00–$11.50, and the trailing four quarters of adjusted EPS sum to roughly $10.72.

On the market-relevant denominator, NSIT trades at roughly 9.2x consensus current-year adjusted EPS and 8.7x next-year, with estimates rising +8.3% (current year) and +7.2% (next year) over 90 days, a +15.6% Q1 beat, and a JPMorgan upgrade on May 27. Q1 2026 confirmed an operational turn: adjusted EFO +27%, Cloud GP +35%, North America adjusted EFO margin to 7.3%. The combination of broad positive revision velocity plus a single-digit forward multiple meets the inflection-BUY bar. The recommendation is BUY.

## Company Overview

Insight Enterprises (founded 1988, IPO 1995, headquartered in Chandler, AZ) is a Fortune 500 solutions integrator operating in 21 countries with 14,505 teammates. It resells and integrates IT hardware, software, cloud, and services across three geographic segments: North America (81% of 2025 sales), EMEA (16%), and APAC (3%).

The business model that a standard data pull misses is the divergence between revenue mix and gross-profit mix. In 2025, product (hardware plus software) represented 79% of net sales but only 41% of gross profit, while services represented 21% of sales but 59% of gross profit. The strategic thesis — becoming an "AI-first solutions integrator" — is to grow the services/cloud mix to expand margins, organized around five pillars: Hybrid Multicloud, Cybersecurity, Data & AI, Digital Workplace & Devices (including Device-as-a-Service), and Intelligent Applications.

It competes on two fronts: against value-added resellers (CDW, SHI, World Wide Technology, Presidio, Computacenter) and against systems integrators and consultants (Accenture, Capgemini, Atos, TCS, Infosys, HCL). Its key suppliers (Microsoft, Cisco, Dell, HP, Adobe) also sell direct, which is a structural disintermediation risk discussed below. Addressable market is roughly $1.3T; Insight's $8.2B is under 1% share, leaving ample runway in a fragmented, consolidating industry.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue decline is mix, not demand.** Net sales fell from $10.4B (2022) to $9.2B (2023) to $8.7B (2024) to $8.2B (2025), a 5% YoY drop in 2025. The decline is driven by the principal-to-agent shift (software/cloud booked net as agent) and on-prem-to-cloud migration (cloud recorded net in services). In Q1 2026, agent/net revenue was $208.7M and growing at roughly 10% of sales. Scoring this company on the top line alone produces a materially wrong conclusion.

**Gross profit is the real metric and it is at record levels.** Gross profit was roughly flat at ~$1.76B across 2024 and 2025 despite the revenue drop, with gross margin expanding +110bps to a record 21.4% in 2025. Q1 2026: Cloud GP +35%, Core Services GP +19%. The mix shift toward services is converting flat gross profit into expanding margin and operating leverage.

**The GAAP-to-adjusted gap, properly decomposed.** This is the crux of the valuation case, and where my prior analysis erred. 2025 GAAP net income fell to $157.3M ($4.86 EPS) from $249.7M ($6.55). Of the charges absorbed, severance/restructuring, the warrant-settlement revaluation, and the real-estate impairment ($74.8M combined) are genuinely non-recurring; only the earnout revaluation ($25.3M) arguably recurs, and even that tails off as the SADA, Infocenter, Inspire11, and Sekuro earnouts mature over 2026–2027. A higher 30.3% tax rate also weighed on the figure. Treating the entire ~$5.89 GAAP-to-adjusted gap as recurring — which is what anchoring valuation to $4.86 does — overweights one-time items. Adjusted EFO held essentially flat at $504.0M (6.1% of sales vs. 5.8%), confirming underlying operations held up.

**Q1 2026 inflection.** GAAP EPS $0.97 (vs. $0.22), EFO +19%, adjusted EFO +27% to $141.1M (6.6% of sales), North America adjusted EFO margin to 7.3%. The quarter beat consensus by 15.6% ($2.88 vs. $2.49). The trailing four quarters of adjusted EPS ($2.88, $2.96, $2.43, $2.45) sum to ~$10.72 — the base the market and consensus actually use, not $4.86.

**Balance sheet — the area requiring genuine scrutiny.** Long-term debt rose from $292M (2022) to $1,361M (2025) and $1,469M at Q1 2026, to fund the $333M convertible-note repayment, $222M warrant settlements, and acquisitions. The stack includes $493M of 6.625% Senior Notes due 2032 plus ~$976M drawn on a $2.0B ABL (maturing 2030). Interest expense rose 46% in 2025 and 51% in Q1 2026, a real headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment given the ABL is largely variable-rate.

The Altman Z of 1.58 ("distress" zone) is a false alarm. It reflects the structural geometry of a thin-margin distributor — $5.5B+ trade receivables, $4.3B+ payables, low equity-to-assets — not insolvency risk. The reported D/E of 4.51 similarly overstates leverage because much of the liability base is trade payables, and the ABL is asset-backed by receivables. The company generates positive FCF (~$279M in 2025 on minimal ~$25M capex), is covenant-compliant, and has $1.0B+ of undrawn ABL capacity. Operating cash flow fell to $303.8M from $632.8M in 2024, but this partly reflects the "inverted cash cycle": growing hardware sales consume cash because Insight pays partners before clients pay it. The genuine concern is the rise in interest-bearing debt and surging interest expense, which I weight in financial health and risk scoring, not bankruptcy.

**Capital allocation.** $151.1M of buybacks in 2025 (after $200M in 2024), $75M more in Q1 2026 at an average $83.37, with a new $299M authorization (Dec 2025), ~$224M remaining. No dividend. Acquisitions are the primary use of cash.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is margin-led, not revenue-led, and it is working. Gross profit growth, not net sales, is the right frame. Management's FY2026 guidance is adjusted diluted EPS of $11.00–$11.50 with a stated bias to the high end, roughly 5% growth over 2025's $10.75 adjusted, gross profit up low-single-digits, and gross margin ~21.5%.

The estimate revision picture is the strongest forward signal here. Consensus current-year EPS rose to $11.43 from $10.55 ninety days ago (+8.3%); next-year to $12.06 from $11.25 (+7.2%); next-quarter +7.0%. These are broad-based, accelerating revisions across every horizon, consistent with the Q1 beat and the operating leverage emerging from the services pivot. Combined with the confirmed Q1 inflection and a forward P/E in the single digits, this is the inflection-BUY profile: rising estimates plus reasonable forward valuation. The +8% current-year revision does not reach the +30% threshold seen in deep-trough recoveries, but it does not need to — that threshold is the trigger for stocks whose forward multiples are otherwise hard to justify. At ~9x forward, the bar for a re-rating is far lower; even modest continued revision momentum drives multiple expansion as consensus catches up.

Secular drivers support durability. The AI PC / Copilot+ refresh cycle and enterprise AI/cloud demand are tailwinds to the device and services mix. Two acquisitions in October 2025 — Inspire11 (AI/data, ~$210M) and Sekuro (APAC cybersecurity, ~$79M) — push the AI/security mix and should contribute to 2026 services growth. The offsetting headwind is the flagged 2026 DRAM/NAND shortage, which raises hardware costs but can also lift hardware revenue dollars and tends to be margin-neutral-to-positive for a reseller passing through pricing.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question turns entirely on the denominator, and the correct denominator is the adjusted earnings base the market transacts on. At $105.10, NSIT trades at ~9.2x consensus current-year adjusted EPS of $11.43 and ~8.7x next-year $12.06. Even at the low end of management guidance ($11.00), the forward multiple is ~9.6x. This is a steep discount to the company's own history and to where a margin-expanding services integrator should trade.

Peer context reinforces the discount. Technology Distributor peer medians sit at 17.8x P/E (PLUS 17.8x, CNXN 21.0x, SCSC 15.0x, AVT 34.6x, ARW 15.9x). On a forward basis, NSIT at ~9x is roughly half the peer-median multiple, and unlike the pure box-movers, NSIT is actively expanding gross margin and shifting toward stickier services revenue. The market is applying a distributor-trough multiple to a business whose gross-profit mix increasingly resembles a services company.

The bear's counter is that the reported P/E of 18.7x (on $4.86 GAAP) makes the stock merely fairly valued. That framing fails because it capitalizes ~$75M of genuinely one-time 2025 charges as if permanent. Strip the non-recurring severance, warrant-settlement, and real-estate items and the earnings base is roughly double the GAAP figure, which is what the adjusted EFO of $504.0M and the trailing ~$10.72 of adjusted EPS show. Analyst consensus (mean target $103, median $105, high $120) clusters near the current price, but on a name with rising estimates and a single-digit forward multiple, consensus targets lag; JPMorgan's May 27 upgrade from Underweight to Neutral is the first evidence of that lag closing.

Applying a still-conservative 11x to the midpoint of guidance ($11.25) yields ~$124; 12x on next-year $12.06 yields ~$145. These are not heroic multiples for a margin-expanding business; they simply move NSIT toward the lower end of its peer group. I score valuation 7.2 — the market is mispricing the stock by anchoring to a distorted GAAP figure, with an identifiable catalyst (continued adjusted EPS delivery, JPM-style re-ratings) to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Insight occupies a defensible but contested position. Its scale ($8.2B sales, <1% of a $1.3T market), multi-vendor breadth, and deepening services capability differentiate it from pure resellers, and the services/cloud pivot moves it up the value chain toward stickier, higher-margin, recurring revenue (Device-as-a-Service, managed cloud, cybersecurity).

The trajectory is improving on the metric that matters: gross margin, expanding +110bps in 2025 and +240bps in Q1 2026, with Cloud GP +35% and Core Services GP +19%. That reflects share-of-wallet gains in the high-value layers of the IT stack rather than box-moving volume. The structural risk is twofold: its key suppliers (Microsoft, Cisco, Dell) sell direct and can disintermediate resellers, and the industry is intensely price-competitive and consolidating. The Microsoft relationship is both moat and dependency — 32% of purchases, ~17% of net sales — tying NSIT's fortunes to Microsoft's Copilot/cloud momentum and to Microsoft's unilateral incentive-program decisions, which materially hurt 2025 results. On balance the competitive trajectory is positive but not dominant; I score it 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Partner incentive dependence** is the most company-specific risk. Partner funding (rebates, MDF) from Microsoft, TD Synnex, Google, Cisco, and Ingram Micro is concentrated — Microsoft alone is 32% of purchases / ~17% of sales; top-5 partners are 63% of purchases. Cloud incentive changes already hurt 2025, and these programs change frequently and unilaterally.

**Leverage and rate exposure.** $1.5B of mostly variable-rate debt with rising interest expense. Rates expected higher-for-longer through 2026. The acquisition strategy explicitly adds debt. This is the genuine financial constraint and the main reason financial health scores below 6.

**DRAM/NAND shortage (new for 2026).** Flagged in the 10-K and Q1 10-Q as causing lower supply and upward hardware pricing — a margin and demand wildcard, though pass-through pricing limits the downside.

**CEO transition.** Jack Azagury became President & CEO effective April 13, 2026, inheriting an unfinished transformation. Joyce Mullen (CEO since January 2022) departed to a consulting role. Execution risk is real and was freshly added to the risk language; the thesis is partly binary on Azagury continuing the margin-mix momentum.

**Earnout overhang.** Large contingent earnouts (SADA up to $120M, Infocenter up to $53M, Inspire11 $66M, Sekuro AUD 122.5M) are non-deductible and create GAAP earnings noise and tax-rate volatility into 2026–2027.

**Public sector** (~12% of Q1 sales) carries shutdown, budget, and termination-for-convenience risk.

I score risk factors 5.5 — the leverage/rate and partner-concentration risks are material and partly idiosyncratic, but they are the standard profile of this name, largely known, and substantially compensated by a single-digit forward multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 46.2% is essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 46.6% (spread −0.4%, IV percentile 10% within the chain) — the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is −5.2% (call-heavy) and put/call OI is an unusually low 0.02, with no put-heavy hedging into earnings. The term structure is flat. The implied one-month move of ~6.9% is moderate. (The options-delta snapshot showing ATM IV jumping to 343% is a corrupted thin-options reading and is treated as non-informative.) Options sentiment is mildly supportive of the long thesis: positioning is call-leaning and IV offers no warning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The services/cloud mix shift continues to expand gross margin and deliver operating leverage; adjusted EPS lands at or above the high end of the $11.00–$11.50 guide and grows toward $12+ in 2027. Estimates keep rising, JPM-style upgrades multiply, and the forward multiple re-rates from ~9x toward the low teens. At 12x next-year $12.06 the stock reaches ~$145 (+38%). AI PC refresh and enterprise AI demand provide a volume tailwind; Inspire11 and Sekuro accrete to the high-margin mix. Continued buybacks (~$224M authorized) shrink the share count.

**Bear Case.** Microsoft or other partners cut incentive programs again, compressing the very services margins the thesis depends on. The DRAM/NAND shortage suppresses hardware demand without offsetting price pass-through. Higher-for-longer rates keep interest expense climbing against a $1.5B variable-rate balance, eroding net income even as gross profit holds. The new CEO stumbles on execution of an unfinished pivot. In that scenario the stock retests the $75 area (analyst low, near the 2026 lows), roughly −29% from current.

The asymmetry favors the bull case at ~9x forward. The bear scenario is largely a return to the recent trough that the inflection has already begun to reverse; the bull scenario is a partial closure of a peer-multiple discount that is hard to justify on a margin-expanding business.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $124** — 11x the guidance midpoint of $11.25 adjusted EPS, a multiple still well below the 17.8x peer median, reflecting partial closure of the discount as estimate revisions and re-ratings continue. Upside +18% from $105.10.

**Bull case target: $145** — 12x next-year consensus $12.06. Upside +38%.

**Bear case target: $75** — the analyst low, near the 2026 trough; partner-incentive cuts plus rate drag compress adjusted EPS and the re-rating reverses. Downside −29%.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings prints (the next confirmation points for the margin-mix thesis) and full-year guidance delivery.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Gross margin declines below ~21.0% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the services-mix expansion has stalled or partner incentives have been cut materially.
2. Management cuts FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance below the $11.00 floor, or current-year consensus revisions turn negative.
3. North America adjusted EFO margin falls back below ~6.0%, reversing the Q1 operating-leverage signal.

## Conclusion

The market and my prior assessment misread Insight Enterprises by anchoring to a GAAP earnings figure distorted by roughly $75M of one-time 2025 charges. On the earnings base the company actually delivers and the market actually transacts on — ~$11.43 consensus current-year adjusted EPS, guided to $11.00–$11.50 — NSIT trades at ~9x forward, roughly half its peer-group multiple, while Q1 2026 confirmed a genuine operating turn and estimates are rising. Sell-side sentiment is turning (JPM upgrade), the Q1 beat validates conservatism in the guide, and options positioning is call-leaning with no bearish hedging.

The genuine risks — partner-incentive dependence, $1.5B of mostly variable-rate debt with rising interest expense, a new CEO, and earnout noise — are real and warrant the below-6 risk and financial-health scores. But they are the standard, largely-known profile of this name, and a single-digit forward multiple more than compensates for them. The evidence pattern matches the inflection-BUY archetype: confirmed operational turn, broad positive revision velocity, and a forward valuation far below any reasonableness threshold. Recommendation: **BUY**, with a 12-month base-case target of $124 (+18%) and a bull case of $145 (+38%).
