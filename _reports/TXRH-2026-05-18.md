## Executive Summary

Texas Roadhouse is the best-performing casual dining chain in the United States, generating $8.7M average unit volumes (roughly double the casual dining norm) on the back of a differentiated value strategy, managing-partner incentive model, and genuine traffic growth in an industry where most competitors are losing guests. Q1 2026 results confirmed margin recovery: +7.1% comparable sales (+4.5% traffic), restaurant margins of 16.3% (vs. 15.5% FY2025), and lowered commodity inflation guidance. The stock rallied 12% on the earnings print and now trades at $177.25, or approximately 29x trailing and 28x forward EPS.

The fundamental quality is unambiguous. The question is whether 28x forward earnings adequately compensates for the risk that (a) beef and diesel costs exceed management's 6-7% guidance, (b) consumer spending compresses traffic in H2 2026, and (c) three consecutive quarterly earnings misses prior to Q1 2026 indicate visibility limitations. The stock currently trades 11% below its 52-week high and 8% above the analyst consensus low target. I maintain HOLD with a growth thesis — the business deserves ownership at the right price, but the specific macro configuration facing a discretionary steakhouse at 28x earnings requires confirmation that Q1's traffic strength persists through the inflation pipeline before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Texas Roadhouse operates 822 restaurants across three brands: the flagship Texas Roadhouse (744 locations, 648 company-owned), Bubba's 33 (56 company-owned sports restaurants), and Jaggers (16 fast-casual units in early growth). The company generated $5.88B in FY2025 revenue and $1.63B in Q1 2026 alone (+12.8% YoY).

The business model is distinguished by: (1) in-house meat cutting at each location, (2) made-from-scratch food preparation with no microwave or deep-fry reliance, (3) a managing-partner profit-sharing model that aligns restaurant-level operators with corporate results, and (4) a deliberate under-pricing strategy where menu increases remain below commodity and labor inflation to protect guest traffic and long-term brand value.

The company is systematically consolidating its domestic franchise system (56 → 31 franchisees in ~18 months), investing $72M in Q1 2026 alone to buy back stores. This increases control over brand execution and captures full unit economics but front-loads capex and goodwill.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $4.01B (2022) → $4.63B (2023) → $5.37B (2024) → $5.88B (2025) → $6.5B+ annualized (Q1 2026). Growth has been consistently double-digit, driven by a combination of new unit openings (~35/year) and mid-to-high single-digit comp growth.

**Margins — the critical variable:** Restaurant-level margin compressed from 17.1% (2024) to 15.5% (2025) as the company absorbed 6.1% commodity and 3.7% labor inflation with only 3.1% in menu price increases. Q1 2026 margins recovered to 16.3%, suggesting the trough was FY2025. Management lowered 2026 commodity inflation guidance to 6-7%. If beef stabilizes, margins have room to expand toward 17% by Q4 2026.

**Cash generation:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $730M; free cash flow was $342M (5.8% FCF yield on revenue; approximately 2.9% FCF yield on current market cap at ~$11.7B). Q1 2026 OCF was $259M — strong seasonal cash generation.

**Balance sheet:** Effectively debt-free in funded terms. Only $50M drawn on a $450M revolver (expandable to $700M, maturing 2030). Cash on hand of $215M. Debt-to-equity of 1.42x is driven entirely by operating leases — the company has no meaningful credit risk.

**Capital allocation (2025):** $388M capex (new stores), $108M franchise acquisitions, $180M dividends ($3.00/share annualized, 1.7% yield), $150M buybacks. Total shareholder returns of $330M. The 10% dividend raise and $500M buyback authorization reflect confidence.

**Key ratios:** ROA 11.7%, ROE 28.4%, effective tax rate 13.8-15% (FICA tip credit advantage). Working capital is negative — normal and healthy for a cash-generative restaurant with no accounts receivable.

## Growth Analysis

**Comparable sales momentum:** The +7.1% comp in Q1 2026 (4.5% traffic + 2.6% check) is exceptional. First five weeks of Q2 showed +6.5% — momentum persists. In an industry where most casual dining chains are experiencing flat-to-negative traffic, TXRH's consistent positive guest counts represent structural market share gains.

**Unit growth:** ~35 company openings targeted for 2026, representing ~5% annual unit growth. With 22 restaurants under construction, the pipeline is visible. The white-space analysis suggests room for 1,000+ total Texas Roadhouse units domestically (currently ~744), implying a decade of 3-5% unit growth remaining.

**Franchise consolidation:** Acquiring remaining 31 domestic franchises captures incremental margin (full restaurant economics vs. royalty). At $72M/quarter pace, consolidation completes within 12-18 months.

**Bubba's 33 concern:** Only 0.9% comp in Q1 2026 vs. 7.5% for the flagship. At 56 units (~8% of the company portfolio), this is manageable but warrants monitoring. If Bubba's 33 fails to accelerate, capital reallocation toward additional Texas Roadhouse units would be value-accretive.

**Growth estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates are $6.41 (up +5.0% in 30 days for the current quarter, with full-year up +2.0% in 30 days but down 2.8% in 90 days). Next-year estimates are $7.65 (down 5.6% in 90 days). The 90-day negative revisions reflect the three consecutive quarterly misses prior to Q1 2026. The 30-day positive revisions reflect Q1's beat. This creates a mixed signal — revision momentum does not justify a valuation premium.

## Valuation Assessment

At $177.25, TXRH trades at approximately 29x trailing EPS ($6.10) and 28x consensus FY2026 EPS ($6.41). On FY2027 estimates of $7.65, the stock trades at 23x forward.

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 30.8x, anchored by CAVA (143x) and BROS (80x) — high-growth concepts. Excluding these, Domino's at 17.9x and Brinker at 13.2x represent more mature casual dining. TXRH's 28x premium over traditional casual dining is justified by its traffic growth, unit economics, and execution consistency. But the stock is not cheap relative to its own growth rate: with EPS expected to grow ~19% from $6.41 to $7.65, the PEG ratio is approximately 1.5x on current-year basis — reasonable but not compelling.

**DCF framework:** $342M FCF growing at 12-15% for 5 years, then 5% terminal, discounted at 9% WACC suggests fair value of $175-195 per share. The current price sits within this range.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean price target of $195.80 and median of $192.00, with a range of $165-$234. The current price at $177.25 sits 10% below the mean target, suggesting moderate upside if consensus is correct.

**Why not BUY despite a 6.9 score:** The specific concern is the macro environment's interaction with TXRH's business model. Elevated energy prices flow through to (a) beef costs (feedlot energy, transportation), (b) diesel/logistics costs, and (c) consumer gasoline expense reducing discretionary spending on dining out. Rising input costs create a dual squeeze: input costs rise faster than menu pricing while traffic becomes vulnerable to consumer trade-down. TXRH's Q1 beat occurred with January-March data; the macro stress may intensify. The 6-7% commodity guidance may prove optimistic if diesel/energy costs accelerate further. At 28x earnings with three of four prior quarters missed, the risk-reward at current price does not offer the asymmetric upside required for BUY conviction.

**Specific falsifiable threshold:** If Q2 2026 earnings (likely August) show comp traffic above +3% AND restaurant margin above 16.0% despite macro stress, the thesis upgrades to BUY because it demonstrates resilience to the current environment.

## Competitive Landscape

Texas Roadhouse occupies a uniquely defensible position in casual dining. Key competitive advantages:

1. **In-house meat cutting** — no other major chain replicates this at scale. It ensures freshness, reduces waste through precise portioning, and differentiates the product.

2. **Managing-partner model** — restaurant-level GMs earn profit-sharing tied to their specific location's P&L. This creates entrepreneurial operators within a corporate framework and reduces turnover.

3. **Value positioning** — by pricing below inflation, TXRH maintains relative value vs. fast-casual alternatives. As consumers trade down from fine dining, TXRH captures them. As competitors raise prices aggressively, TXRH's traffic gap widens.

4. **No national advertising** — the company spends on localized marketing, reducing SG&A while building community-level loyalty. This is a structural cost advantage.

The primary competitive risk is fast-casual encroachment from concepts like Cava, Chipotle, and Sweetgreen that offer quality at lower price points. However, TXRH's full-service dining experience — including the bread, the atmosphere, and the value-for-money steak — creates differentiation that fast-casual cannot replicate.

## Risk Assessment

**Beef cost inflation (HIGH):** Approximately 50% of food costs are beef, sourced from four major suppliers. A further escalation beyond 6-7% guidance (via tariffs on Canadian beef, energy-driven diesel costs, or drought) could push margins below 15% and force either aggressive pricing (risking traffic) or continued margin absorption.

**Consumer spending vulnerability (MODERATE-HIGH):** With discretionary spending under pressure from elevated gas prices and inflation, casual dining spend is at risk. TXRH's value positioning provides some insulation, but a ~$50 average dinner for two still requires discretionary budget. Q1 traffic was strong, but the macro environment may deteriorate further.

**Earnings miss pattern (MODERATE):** Three consecutive misses before Q1's narrow 1% beat reduces confidence in management's ability to forecast near-term profitability. The margin compression from beef inflation is inherently difficult to predict quarter-to-quarter.

**GLP-1 drugs (LOW-MODERATE):** Newly disclosed risk factor. Population-level reduction in caloric intake could gradually reduce restaurant visit frequency. Hard to quantify near-term but relevant for long-term terminal value assumptions.

**Tariff exposure (MODERATE):** Canadian beef imports could be affected by trade policy changes. No quantification available, but management has flagged it in forward-looking statements.

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Comp traffic turns negative in any quarter
2. Restaurant margin falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters
3. Management abandons value-pricing strategy in favor of aggressive pricing (would signal desperation over discipline)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026). Upcoming consumer earnings this week provide directional consumer data but TXRH's next company-specific catalyst is the Q2 report.

## Conclusion

Texas Roadhouse is a fundamentally excellent business — the best-in-class casual dining operator with proven traffic growth, disciplined capital allocation, and a defensible competitive model. The Q1 2026 beat confirmed margin recovery has begun, and the franchise consolidation strategy positions the company for long-term earnings compounding. However, at 28x forward earnings in an environment where input costs are elevated and real consumer spending is under pressure, the risk-reward requires further confirmation before committing. The specific risk that beef cost inflation re-accelerates or that consumer traffic declines as macro stress intensifies is not yet resolved. I maintain HOLD with explicit upgrade criteria tied to Q2 2026 traffic and margin data.
