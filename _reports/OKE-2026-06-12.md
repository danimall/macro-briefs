**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ONEOK is one of North America's largest integrated midstream operators, running roughly 60,000 miles of pipeline across four fee-based segments (natural gas gathering & processing, natural gas liquids, natural gas pipelines, and refined products & crude). Over three years it transformed from an NGL-centric operator into a diversified gas/NGL/crude/refined-products platform through a debt- and equity-funded acquisition roll-up: Magellan (2023), EnLink (controlling 2024, full 2025), Medallion (2024), and a series of Permian and Gulf Coast bolt-ons. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $5.24B (2023) to $8.02B (2025), and the company carries investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB/BBB) and a ~4.7% dividend yield covered roughly 1.4x by free cash flow.

The investment case is governed by two facts. First, the recent growth is overwhelmingly acquisition-driven; the swing variables are synergy realization and deleveraging from ~4.2x net debt/EBITDA toward the covenant step-down to 5.0x and lower. Second, the headline Q1 2026 strength (Adjusted EBITDA $1,997M, +12.5% YoY) leaned heavily on transient items — roughly $70M of favorable Waha-to-Katy gas basis differentials plus a $19M Winter Storm Fern benefit in the Natural Gas Pipelines segment — which are optimization/marketing earnings, not durable fee income. The supposedly defensive G&P segment saw EBITDA fall $24M on weaker NGL prices, showing the "90% fee-based" label understates commodity and differential sensitivity.

We maintain a HOLD. At $89-90 the stock trades at ~15.9x trailing EPS and ~15.6x forward FY26 consensus of $5.71, a meaningful discount to midstream peers (TRGP 27.5x, KMI 21.1x, WMB 31.4x). That discount is partly earned — higher leverage, integration risk, and lower earnings quality than the fee-based framing implies — and partly an opportunity if deleveraging and the LNG/NGL-export/data-center demand thesis play out. The yield and structural demand exposure justify holding for income; with the stock back near the upper third of its 52-week range and below the ~$95 consensus mean, we do not see asymmetric upside to chase here.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates four segments:

1. **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing (G&P)** — ~22,600 miles of gathering pipe and processing plants in the Williston/Bakken, Powder River, Anadarko/SCOOP/STACK, Barnett, and Permian basins. Plants ran ~78% utilized in 2025. This segment retains NGL price exposure through fee-with-percentage-of-proceeds contracts (management hedges ~75% of equity volumes).
2. **Natural Gas Liquids (NGL)** — the franchise core: ~10,100 miles of gathering and 4,800 miles of distribution pipe, 1.2 MMBbl/d fractionation capacity running ~94% utilized, and 40 MMBbl of storage connecting supply basins to Conway, KS and Mont Belvieu, TX.
3. **Natural Gas Pipelines** — ~8,300 miles, ~91-93% subscribed under long-term firm contracts, 74 Bcf storage, and ownership of the Sabine Pipeline / Henry Hub (the NYMEX gas pricing point). Lowest volumetric risk of the four.
4. **Refined Products & Crude** — the legacy Magellan system, one of the longest US refined-products common-carrier networks (9,800 miles across 15 states), plus crude transport, gathering, 100 MMBbl storage, and two marine terminals.

Management states ~90% of earnings are fee-based, largely under long-term contracts with minimum-volume commitments and investment-grade counterparties. The competitive edge stems from scale, multi-basin integration, and ownership of strategic chokepoints — Mont Belvieu fractionation, Henry Hub, Cushing storage — that let ONEOK monetize location and product differentials that pure-play competitors cannot. The company is headquartered in Tulsa, OK with roughly 6,300 employees.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory (FY):**
- Revenue $17.7B (2023) → $21.7B (2024) → $33.6B (2025). Most of the 2025 jump is commodity-sales gross-up that offsets in cost of sales; Adjusted EBITDA is the cleaner operating gauge.
- Adjusted EBITDA $5.24B → $6.78B → $8.02B (+18% in 2025), driven by a full year of EnLink/Medallion.
- Net income attributable to OKE $2.66B → $3.04B → $3.39B. Diluted EPS $5.48 → $5.17 → $5.42 — earnings growth diluted by a share count that rose from ~484M to ~625M from stock-funded M&A.
- Operating cash flow $4.42B → $4.89B → $5.60B; 2025 capex $3.15B.

**Q1 2026:** Net income to OKE $774M (+22% YoY), diluted EPS $1.23 vs $1.04. Segment detail matters here. Natural Gas Pipelines EBITDA jumped to $339M (+$127M), with $70M from favorable Waha-to-Katy gas basis differentials and $19M from Winter Storm Fern — neither recurring. NGL added $71M. G&P fell $24M on lower realized NGL/gas prices. The company took a $60M noncash impairment on its 50% Powder Springs JV in Refined Products. The reported result was essentially in-line with the $1.32 estimate (+0.3% surprise), consistent with a four-quarter track record of small beats and misses (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss; largest surprise +1.9%). This is a low-variance reporter, not a serial beater.

**Balance sheet and leverage:**
- Total debt ~$34.0B at YE2025 (long-term ~$31B); equity $22.5B; D/E ~2.96x.
- Credit-agreement net debt/EBITDA was 4.2x at Q1'26 against a 5.5x covenant that steps to 5.0x after Q2'26 — deleveraging is on track but leverage is genuinely elevated.
- Persistent working-capital deficit ($1.9B at YE2025, $2.3B at Q1'26) from current maturities plus commercial paper; current ratio 0.71. Management characterizes this as normal for the model.
- Investment grade across all three agencies (Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB / Fitch BBB, all Stable). Liquidity backstops include a $3.5B revolver to 2030, a $3.5B CP program ($1.6B drawn at Q1'26), an unused $1.0B ATM, and a new $1.2B 364-day term loan established in April 2026.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.15 nominally lands in "distress" territory, but this is a known artifact of applying a manufacturing-oriented model to a capital-intensive, asset-heavy, ex-MLP midstream operator. With $5.6B+ of operating cash flow, investment-grade ratings, and ~1.4x dividend coverage, bankruptcy risk is not the issue. The real watch-item is the leverage and refinancing cost the score is flagging — ONEOK issued 2055 notes at 6.25% in 2025, so each refinancing cycle at current rates raises the interest burden on a $34B stack.

**Capital allocation:** The dividend rose to $1.07/quarter ($4.28 annualized) in 2026 from $4.12 in 2025, a ~4.7% yield. The buyback authorization is barely used relative to deleveraging and growth capex priorities (2026 capex guided $2.7-3.2B). This is a reasonable ordering given the leverage, but it means per-share growth depends on organic EBITDA and synergy capture rather than shrinking the share count.

## Growth Analysis

ONEOK's near-term growth is dominated by the full-year inclusion of EnLink and Medallion rather than organic same-store expansion. Estimate revisions are mildly positive: the current quarter has moved +3.6% over 90 days and current-year FY26 EPS is up +1.3% to $5.71, with FY27 at $6.15 (+0.8% over 90 days). These are constructive but well short of the thresholds (>30% revisions, >40% revenue growth) that would warrant a growth-led re-rating. This is a steady-compounder revision profile.

The organic growth pipeline is credible and tied to durable demand themes:
- **LNG feedgas and NGL exports** — the Texas City LPG export terminal JV with MPLX (50%, $700M, early 2028) directly targets growing seaborne NGL demand; ownership of Mont Belvieu fractionation feeds the same chain.
- **Permian takeaway** — the Bighorn plant ($365M, mid-2027) and Eiger Express gas pipeline (25.5%, $350M, Permian-to-Katy, mid-2028) address the egress bottleneck for growing Permian gas and NGL volumes.
- **Data-center / AI power demand** — incremental natural-gas pipeline and storage value as power demand from data centers rises. Management cites "growing demand from data centers," and sell-side coverage corroborates the theme.
- **Refined products** — the Greater Denver expansion ($480M, mid-2026, serving the DIA airport) and Medford fractionator rebuild ($485M).

The demand themes are real and broad-based, but OKE's translation of them into per-share value runs through a heavily levered balance sheet and a share count enlarged by acquisitions. The 10-K itself warns that accretive-looking deals "may nevertheless reduce cash from operations on a per-share basis." The growth thesis is durable at the asset level; the equity-holder return depends on synergy execution and disciplined financing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $89-90, OKE trades at ~15.9x trailing EPS of $5.42 and ~15.6x forward FY26 consensus of $5.71. Against the peer set, this is a clear discount:

| KMI (Kinder Morgan) | 21.1 | 3.7% |

| **OKE** | **~15.6x fwd** | **~4.7%** |

The discount is partly justified and partly opportunity. Justifications: ONEOK carries higher leverage, more integration risk from the recent roll-up, and an earnings base with greater commodity/differential sensitivity than the headline "90% fee-based" framing implies. WMB and TRGP also carry richer growth or asset-mix premiums (WMB's pure gas-transmission profile, TRGP's NGL-export leverage).

The opportunity case: a fully-deleveraged ONEOK growing organic EBITDA mid-single-digits with these demand tailwinds should not trade at this wide a discount to the peer median. If net leverage falls toward 3.5-4.0x and synergies prove durable, a re-rating toward 18-19x forward is reasonable, implying ~$103-108. That is roughly where the analyst high target of $113 and the mean of ~$95 bracket the upside.

On the income side, the ~4.7% yield, raised to $4.28 annualized and covered ~1.4x by FCF, provides a valuation floor. The yield is well above the peer median (2.9%) and supports the absolute valuation case for income-oriented holders. We score valuation moderately: the discount is real but is at least half-explained by the leverage and earnings-quality differential, so this is not a clean mispricing. The stock at ~$90 sits below the ~$95 consensus mean and $95 median, so it is not stretched, but the asymmetry is not compelling near current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK competes with Energy Transfer, Williams, Enterprise Products, Kinder Morgan, MPLX, and Targa for basin supply and downstream connectivity. The structural advantage is integration: owning fractionation (Mont Belvieu), storage, hub assets (Henry Hub via Sabine, Cushing), and one of the longest refined-products systems lets ONEOK capture differentials others cannot. The Q1'26 Waha-to-Katy basis capture, while volatile, is a direct expression of this — the company monetized a regional gas-price spread because it owns the connective infrastructure.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the asset level: the Magellan/EnLink/Medallion deals widened the moat from NGL-centric to a multi-product, multi-basin platform with optionality across the value chain. Long-term firm contracts (up to 23 years; $6.97B of unsatisfied performance obligations) and ~91-93% subscribed gas pipelines provide stable underpinning. The offsetting concern is execution: managing a rapidly expanded enterprise with embedded JV dependencies (Northern Border, Overland Pass, BridgeTex, Matterhorn, Roadrunner) over which OKE has limited unilateral control. The Powder Springs impairment is a small reminder of JV downside. The position is widening, but the realized advantage now depends on integration discipline rather than asset positioning alone.

## Risk Assessment

- **Integration and leverage from the roll-up.** This is the central risk. ONEOK must realize EnLink/Medallion/Magellan synergies while deleveraging $34B of debt. The 10-K explicitly warns deals may reduce per-share cash from operations. Slippage on either front pressures the equity.
- **Earnings-quality drift.** Q1'26's pipeline upside was largely transient basis differentials and a winter-storm benefit. Extrapolating that level overstates run-rate fee income. The optimization/marketing component is becoming more material and is inherently volatile.
- **Commodity/volumetric exposure in G&P.** Despite the fee-based label, G&P EBITDA fell on lower NGL/gas prices in Q1'26 — the leading edge of this risk. Throughput also depends on third-party drilling in the Williston, Mid-Con, and Permian.
- **Interest-rate/refinancing.** A $34B debt stack with current maturities and new issuance at 5-6%+ raises interest cost on each refinancing cycle; the negative working-capital position keeps the company reliant on revolver/CP access.
- **Regulatory.** FERC rate risk on interstate pipelines and shifting GHG/methane rules (net near-term effect is lower compliance cost given the OBBBA methane-fee suspension and EPA rollbacks, but with heavy litigation uncertainty).
- **Governance loose threads.** Two Item-5.02 8-Ks (Jan and Mar 2026) and the OWM JV put-right (a redeemable noncontrolling interest) warrant monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and confirms a low-conviction, balanced setup. Near-term ATM IV of 27.7% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 26.5% (1.1% spread), with IV in only the 2nd percentile of its own chain — the market is pricing complacency, not stress. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.2% near-term, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.36 and OI ratio of 0.60 lean slightly call-heavy. The term structure is flat (27.7% near vs 29.1% far). IV has compressed from 30.9% to 27.7% over the past two weeks as the price rose 2.6%, indicating resolved near-term uncertainty rather than building event risk. There is no options-implied bearish divergence against a constructive fundamental read, and no cheap-vol entry signal either. Short interest is modest at 4.4% of float (5.7 days to cover), consistent with no informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($103-113):** Deleveraging proceeds on schedule toward 3.5-4.0x net debt/EBITDA, synergies from EnLink/Medallion prove durable, and the LNG-export, NGL-export, and data-center demand themes lift gas-pipeline and fractionation value. The growth-capex slate (Bighorn, Texas City LPG, Eiger Express, Greater Denver) converts to incremental fee EBITDA. The peer-relative discount closes toward 18-19x forward EPS, and the growing dividend attracts income rotation. At ~$6.15 FY27 EPS and an 18x multiple, the stock reaches ~$110, near the $113 analyst high.

**Bear Case ($72-78):** Synergies disappoint or integration costs run high, leverage deleverages slowly while refinancing costs climb, and the optimization/marketing earnings that flattered recent quarters reverse, exposing softer underlying fee growth. G&P remains pressured by weak NGL prices. The discount widens or earnings disappoint, taking the stock back toward the low-$70s. At ~$5.50 normalized EPS and a 13.5x trough multiple, the downside is roughly $74, about -17% from current.

The asymmetry near $90 is roughly balanced: ~+15-25% to base/bull, ~-17% to bear, with the ~4.7% yield cushioning the wait. That balance, combined with four consecutive HOLD ratings and a gently declining score trajectory, supports continuing to hold rather than upgrading.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold)** — held primarily for the covered ~4.7% yield and structural gas/NGL demand exposure, with a constructive but not asymmetric 12-month appreciation profile.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, expected late July 2026), which carries the covenant step-down to 5.0x and the first clean read on whether Q1's transient pipeline upside repeats or fades.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net debt/EBITDA falls below ~3.8x while the dividend coverage holds above 1.3x, demonstrating deleveraging without distribution strain.
- A pullback toward the low-$80s (closer to the $84 analyst low / 14x forward) on intact fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside to the ~$95+ consensus and the demand-driven re-rating case.
- Evidence of durable, organic fee EBITDA growth (not basis-differential or storm-driven) of mid-single-digits in two consecutive quarters, confirming synergy realization.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net debt/EBITDA rising above ~4.6x or a credit-rating outlook revision to negative.
- Two consecutive quarters of declining fee-based segment EBITDA combined with growing reliance on optimization/marketing earnings to hit guidance.
- A dividend-coverage breach below ~1.1x or any signal of distribution risk.

## Conclusion

ONEOK is a scaled, investment-grade midstream operator that has transformed itself into a diversified gas/NGL/crude/refined-products platform, now positioned to benefit from LNG feedgas, NGL exports, and data-center-driven gas demand. The dividend and the peer-relative valuation discount are the supporting pillars. The offsetting realities are elevated leverage, an earnings base whose recent strength leaned on transient gas-basis differentials and a winter storm, and a "90% fee-based" framing that understates commodity sensitivity, as the G&P decline showed. Estimate revisions are mildly positive but not at re-rating magnitude, the company is a low-variance earnings reporter rather than a serial beater, and there is no insider conviction buying.

At ~$90, against a ~$95 consensus mean and within the upper third of the 52-week range, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We maintain HOLD. Income holders should retain the position for the covered yield and demand tailwinds; new capital seeking a better entry should wait for a pullback toward the low-$80s or confirmation that deleveraging and organic fee growth are tracking ahead of the optimization-earnings tailwind. The four-report HOLD streak and gently declining score reflect a steady, fairly-valued compounder.
