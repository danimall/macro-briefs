**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Genworth is a financial holding company that is best understood as a leveraged, deeply discounted claim on its ~81% stake in Enact Holdings (a leading U.S. private mortgage insurer), wrapped around a $44.1B runoff long-term-care (LTC) liability, with two embedded call options — a nascent CareScout growth business and a potential ~$750M litigation recovery from the AXA/Santander payment-protection-insurance case. At ~$9.92 the stock trades at roughly 0.4x book value ($22.88 at 3/31/26) and below independent estimates of the Enact stake alone (~$4.7B versus GNW's ~$3.8B market cap), implying the market assigns negative value to everything outside Enact. That discount is the central bull case; the LTC reserve tail and single-point dependence on Enact's dividend capacity are the central bear case.

The equity thesis rests on a buyback flywheel: Enact returns ~$500M in 2026, of which GNW's 81% share is ~$405M, which management redeploys buying back stock at ~0.4x book. Share count has fallen from 468.8M (2023 average) to 385.2M (3/31/26), about 18% in under three years, and the per-share book value and per-share Enact exposure compound as long as the discount persists. The mechanism is genuinely accretive. The problem is that the same cash flow that funds buybacks is also the only thing standing between the holding company and its obligations, since Genworth receives no dividends from the walled-off legacy insurance subsidiaries and has no holdco revolver.

We rate GNW a HOLD. The valuation is cheap on a sum-of-the-parts basis and the capital-return program is real, but the stock has already run 35% off its 52-week low, the last two quarters missed consensus badly (-41% and -92%), and the LTC liability is structurally set to generate more remeasurement volatility over the next decade, not less. The risk/reward is roughly balanced. We would turn more constructive on a pullback toward book-value-implied floors or on resolution of the AXA appeal in Genworth's favor.

## Company Overview

Genworth Holdings operates through three distinct pieces, and the structure matters more than any single financial metric:

1. **Enact Holdings (~81% owned; publicly traded as ACT)** — the profit engine and effectively the only source of holding-company cash. Primary insurance in-force of $273B, weighted-average FICO of 746, and 96% of risk in 2018-and-later vintages.

2. **Closed Block (runoff)** — legacy LTC, life, and annuity policies no longer sold, managed on a standalone basis. Genworth does not expect to receive dividends from these subsidiaries. The block is dominated by a $44.1B LTC future-policy-benefit liability recorded at a locked-in discount rate.

3. **Corporate & Other / CareScout** — the growth pivot. CareScout Services (fee-based aging-care network, senior-living placement, assessments) plus CareScout Insurance, which re-entered the individual LTC market in October 2025 with a "Care Assurance" product after roughly a decade's absence.

Holding-company liquidity depends entirely on Enact's ability to pay dividends and repurchase stock. The Closed Block is ring-fenced and self-funding through reserves and continued in-force rate actions. Management underscored this in Q1 2026 by changing its headline metric to "adjusted operating income excluding Closed Block," a deliberate signal that it wants the market to value Enact plus CareScout and treat the LTC runoff as a self-funding legacy.

## Financial Analysis

**Consolidated FY2025:** Revenue of $7,301M (flat YoY). Net income to common of $223M, down 25% from $299M in 2024. Adjusted operating income of $144M, down 47% from $273M. Diluted EPS of $0.54.

The segment breakdown reveals the tension:
- **Enact: +$558M** adjusted operating income (down slightly from $585M on lower reserve releases and higher new delinquencies)
- **Closed Block: –$317M** (worsened from –$214M): LTC –$326M, Life –$66M, Annuities +$75M
- **Corporate/Other: –$97M** (CareScout investment drag)

Enact earns roughly $558M of segment profit per year, but the Closed Block bleeds over $300M and Corporate loses another ~$100M, compressing consolidated adjusted operating income to $144M. LTC assumption updates and liability remeasurements periodically consume Enact's earnings at the consolidated line. Q4 2025 was the clearest illustration — net income of just $2M and a GAAP loss from continuing operations, driven by a $143M Closed Block liability remeasurement loss and a $47M unfavorable LTC assumption update. Q1 2026 recovered to $47M net income to common.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets of $88.1B; total equity of $9.77B; Genworth stockholders' equity of $8.75B (Enact minority interest of $1.02B). Investments of ~$61.2B, 75% fixed maturity and 97% investment grade — conservatively managed. Book value per share was $22.33 at 12/31/25 and $22.88 at 3/31/26 (ex-AOCI ~$25.71).

**Capital allocation.** This is where the bull case operates. Weighted shares fell from 468.8M (2023 average) to 385.2M at 3/31/26 (share count was 391.8M at 12/31/25), roughly 7% per year. In 2025 GNW repurchased $245M of stock at ~$7.99 average; a $350M authorization from September 2025 had ~$222M remaining as of February 2026. The funding comes from Enact: GNW received $407M in 2025 ($309M buybacks + $98M dividends) and expects ~$405M in 2026, backed by a fresh $500M Enact buyback authorization dated February 3, 2026. Holdco cash was $234M (including $127M held for obligations); Genworth Holdings debt is $783M with no maturities until June 2034. The clean near-term maturity profile removes the refinancing-cliff risk that would otherwise dominate a discounted holding-company thesis.

The earnings-surprise record tempers confidence in near-term consensus: 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters missing by 41% (Q1 2026, $0.12 vs. $0.20) and 92% (Q4 2025, $0.01 vs. $0.06). The Q3 2025 "beat" of 455% was a low-base artifact. GAAP results swing on LTC remeasurement rather than operational deterioration, which is why consensus visibility is poor.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated revenue was flat in FY2025 and the business is not a growth story in any conventional sense. The Closed Block is in permanent runoff by design. Enact's growth is constrained by elevated interest rates: persistency is high (~82%) as existing borrowers stay put, which supports in-force but suppresses new insurance written. Enact's loss ratio has already begun normalizing higher (from 4% in 2024 to 11% in 2025) as reserve releases shrink and delinquencies rise to 2.62%, which caps its near-term earnings trajectory.

The forward growth optionality lies in two places. First, CareScout — the individual LTC re-entry — is a contrarian bet that a disciplined, network-supported, better-priced product can succeed where legacy LTC failed. It is loss-making today and needs financial-strength ratings and regulatory approvals to scale. The payoff, if it materializes, is years out. Second, the AXA/Santander litigation recovery of ~$750M (roughly 20% of the market cap) would provide a one-time capital injection that management has said would fund CareScout, buybacks, and debt paydown. Neither is a recurring-earnings growth engine.

The per-share growth mechanism is buyback-driven, not organic: shrinking the share count ~7% per year at 0.4x book compounds per-share book value even with flat consolidated earnings. That is a legitimate lever, but it is arithmetic on a static-to-declining earnings base, not fundamental growth. Growth Prospects earns a 4.5, consistent with the declining trajectory in prior reviews. The estimate-revision signal offers no support here — near-term consensus has been repeatedly cut and the two most recent quarters missed materially.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation case is the strongest single element of the thesis and the reason the stock is not an AVOID. At ~$9.92 GNW trades at ~0.4x book value and ~0.39x book ex-AOCI. The ~81% Enact stake is independently estimated at ~$4.7B versus GNW's ~$3.8B total market cap, meaning the market assigns negative aggregate value to the Closed Block, CareScout, holdco cash, and the litigation option combined. That is a specific, identifiable mispricing rather than a generic "low P/E."

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The trailing P/E of ~19x looks expensive versus the multi-line insurance peer median of 12.6x (HMN 12.2x, AFG 12.6x, AIZ 13.3x, LNC 4.3x), but the trailing P/E is nearly meaningless here because GNW's earnings are suppressed by Closed Block remeasurement losses that are largely non-cash and self-funding. The relevant metric is price-to-book and sum-of-the-parts, on which GNW is the cheapest name in the group by a wide margin. LNC trades at 21% below its 52-week high and KMPR at 62% below theirs, so the sector carries embedded skepticism, but none approaches GNW's 0.4x book discount.

We do not treat the discount as an automatic buy signal. A persistent sub-book multiple that has not closed despite years of buybacks is partly a value-trap warning: the market is pricing genuine uncertainty about whether LTC surprises will ultimately consume the Enact cash flow the bull case depends on. The catalyst for a re-rating exists (buyback accretion, the AXA appeal, CareScout traction) but each is either slow-compounding or binary. We assign Valuation a 6.5 — cheap with an identifiable mispricing, but discounted for the reasons the discount persists and for the ~35% run off the low that has already narrowed some of the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

**Enact** operates in an oligopolistic U.S. private mortgage-insurance market alongside MGIC, Radian, Essent, Arch, and National MI, and against government competition (FHA/VA) and GSE credit-risk-transfer programs. Returns are driven by pricing discipline, reinsurance sophistication, and vintage quality. Enact's book is high-quality (FICO 746, 96% post-2018 vintage) and its capital position is strong (PMIERs sufficiency 162%, or $1,919M above requirement; risk-to-capital 10.1:1 against a 25:1 maximum). The main competitive vulnerability is customer concentration — the largest customer is 22% of new insurance written and the top five are 33%. Enact's position is stable-to-solid but not improving; the loss ratio is normalizing higher off a cyclical low.

**LTC/CareScout.** Most carriers have exited the LTC market, and CareScout's re-entry is a contrarian move to build a disciplined, network-supported franchise. It is too early to judge share dynamics; the business is sub-scale and loss-making. The competitive position of the legacy Closed Block is irrelevant — it is in runoff and its "competitiveness" is entirely a function of continued regulatory approval of premium increases (cumulative NPV benefit of $34.5B achieved against a $39.5B target, with ~$5.0B remaining and one state in active litigation over refused increases).

On balance the competitive trajectory is neither clearly improving nor deteriorating — Enact is a stable share-holder normalizing off cyclical lows, CareScout is an unproven challenger, and the legacy block is a managed wind-down. Competitive Position earns a 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are substantial and several are not fully priced:

1. **Single-point dependence on Enact dividends.** GNW has no holdco revolver. If Enact's board curtails dividends in a housing downturn — the loss ratio has already risen from 4% to 11% — the buyback engine and CareScout funding stop, and the entire per-share compounding thesis breaks. This is the dominant risk.

2. **LTC reserve adequacy and volatility.** The $44.1B LTC liability is the elephant. A 5% increase in future claim costs equals a ~$1,640M pre-tax hit. Roughly half of the LTC cohorts are "capped" at a 100% net premium ratio (unprofitable pre-2003 blocks). Claims are set to rise, not fall: the 592,000 insureds in the Choice I/II blocks (average attained age 75-78) do not reach peak claim years (85+) for over a decade, meaning remeasurement volatility and rate-action dependence will persist for years. This is the structural reason the book-value discount does not close.

3. **In-force rate-action execution.** Sustainability of the legacy subs depends on continued regulatory approval of premium increases. Genworth is already in litigation with one state, and NY (GLICNY) required a $79M statutory reserve addition in 2024.

4. **Mortgage-cycle risk at Enact.** A recession or home-price decline would raise claims. Regulatory overhangs include GSE reform, PMIERs changes, and potential Basel III Endgame effects on MI demand.

5. **CareScout execution.** A new line of business that may consume capital longer than expected, requires ratings and approvals to scale, and could divert management attention.

Consolidated RBC of ~300% (down from 306%) and low legacy-sub financial-strength ratings (which limit reinsurance availability and force excess collateral posting) round out the picture. The combination of a large, quantified LTC tail, single-point cash dependence, and two consecutive material earnings misses supports a Risk Factors score of 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 27.4% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 23.9%, the term structure is in backwardation (27.4% near-term vs. 25.3% at six months), and near-term put/call skew is elevated at 8.2% — the market is paying up for downside protection into the next few weeks. Put/call volume and open-interest ratios are near zero, indicating this is a lightly-traded options complex rather than a heavily-hedged one, so the skew signal is thin. Short interest is low at 3.4% of float (2.6 days to cover), which means neither a crowded short nor meaningful squeeze fuel. The market anticipates a catalyst — most likely the AXA appeal (July 21-23 hearing) and the next earnings print — but positioning is too light to draw strong conclusions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$13-15):** The Enact dividend holds, the ~$405M/year of holdco cash keeps retiring stock at ~0.4x book, and per-share book value compounds toward the mid-$20s. The AXA/Santander appeal resolves in Genworth's favor, delivering a ~$750M recovery that funds accelerated buybacks and CareScout. The market narrows the sum-of-the-parts discount as Closed Block remeasurement volatility stabilizes, re-rating the stock toward 0.55-0.65x book. CareScout demonstrates early traction, adding an option value the market currently prices at zero.

**Bear Case (~$7):** A housing downturn raises Enact's loss ratio and pressures its dividend, cutting off holdco cash. An adverse LTC assumption update triggers a large remeasurement loss and forces additional statutory reserves. The AXA appeal fails. CareScout consumes capital without scaling. The discount widens back toward the low end as the market re-prices the LTC tail. Downside to ~$7 is roughly 29% from current levels, near the 52-week low of $7.32.

**Base Case (~$11):** The buyback flywheel continues, per-share book value grinds higher, and the discount narrows modestly as Enact cash flow proves durable, but the LTC tail keeps a lid on the multiple. The AXA outcome is treated as a call option, not underwritten. Modest upside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are the July 21-23, 2026 AXA/Santander Court of Appeal hearing and the subsequent earnings release. The buyback thesis plays out over a 12-18 month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AXA appeal resolves in Genworth's favor, confirming a ~$750M recovery pathway
- A pullback toward the high-$7s/low-$8s that restores a wider margin of safety on the ~0.4x book discount
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-positive LTC assumption experience (no adverse remeasurement), demonstrating the tail is better-behaved than feared, alongside a confirmed Enact dividend/buyback cadence

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any material cut to Enact's dividend or buyback authorization, which severs the holdco cash flow
- A large adverse LTC remeasurement (>$200M) or a rate-action denial that materially raises required reserves
- Consolidated RBC falling below ~275% or CareScout losses accelerating without a scaling path

## Conclusion

GNW is a genuinely cheap, structurally complex holding vehicle. The ~0.4x book valuation and the sum-of-the-parts dislocation (Enact stake worth more than the whole company) are real, and the sub-book buyback flywheel is a legitimate per-share compounding mechanism funded by ~$405M/year of Enact cash. Those are the reasons the stock is not an AVOID. But the same cash flow the buyback depends on is the company's single point of failure, the $44.1B LTC liability is set to generate more volatility over the next decade rather than less, and the last two quarters missed consensus by wide margins. The AXA recovery and CareScout are options, not underwritten value.

After a 35% run off the low, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate GNW a HOLD (uncertain), and would move to BUY on a favorable AXA outcome, a wider entry discount in the high-$7s/low-$8s, or demonstrated LTC-tail stability. The single analyst covering the name carries a $12.00 target; we regard that as a plausible bull-case anchor rather than a base case, given the reserve tail and dividend-dependence risks that a discounted holding-company structure carries.
