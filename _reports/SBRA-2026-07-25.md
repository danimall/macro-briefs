**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sabra Health Care REIT is a mid-cap ($5.6B) healthcare REIT that owns and finances skilled nursing facilities, managed senior housing communities, and specialty healthcare real estate across the U.S. and Canada. The investable story over the past year has been a deliberate strategic pivot: converting triple-net leased facilities into directly-operated managed senior housing (SHOP) and acquiring managed communities, trading the stability of a net-lease landlord for operating upside and direct exposure to occupancy and labor costs. Resident fees grew 51% year-over-year in Q1 2026 while rental revenue was flat-to-down, confirming that headline revenue growth is a mix shift rather than same-store rent acceleration.

The near-term catalyst that re-rated the stock is the Avamere re-tenanting announced July 21, 2026. Avamere's founder is exiting the skilled nursing business, and Sabra is transferring all 26 SNFs to new operators (22 to Cascadia Healthcare, 4 to an existing national tenant) at combined annualized cash rent of ~$53M, roughly 30% above the ~$41M trailing rent from Avamere. Coupled with >$9M of additional run-rate NOI from smaller re-tenanting initiatives, this drove a raise to FY2026 Normalized FFO guidance of $1.53–$1.55 and Normalized AFFO of $1.59–$1.61, and a ~10% single-day stock move. This is competent, operator-initiated asset management that kept the real estate while upgrading operator quality.

The problem for new capital is that the market has already paid for it. After a 22% run, SBRA trades at $22.36, near its 52-week high of $22.55, at roughly 14x Normalized AFFO with a dividend yield of ~5.4%, sitting essentially at the consensus mean target of $22.71. The estimate-revision picture is negative (current-year EPS revised down 4.3% over 90 days), the quarterly beat/miss record has deteriorated (three misses in the last four quarters), and the SNF reimbursement/regulatory overhang that differentiates SBRA from cleaner senior-housing and medical-office peers remains. This is an income hold: the covered ~5.4% dividend is the durable return, with limited appreciation upside from here absent further accretive re-tenanting.

## Company Overview

Sabra has operated as a REIT since 2011 and is self-managed and self-administered with only 58 corporate employees. As of Q1 2026 the portfolio comprised 361 properties held for investment, 13 loans receivable, 5 preferred equity investments, and 2 unconsolidated joint ventures. By book value:

- **Skilled Nursing / Transitional Care (SNF): 208 properties, ~47% of book value** — the historical core, leased triple-net.
- **Senior Housing – Managed (SHOP): 90 communities, ~34% of book value** — operated by third-party managers with Sabra taking direct operating exposure (RIDEA structure).
- **Senior Housing – Leased: 32 properties; Behavioral Health: 16; Specialty Hospitals/Other: 15.**

The business runs on two engines. The first is triple-net leases (271 facilities, ~7-year weighted-average remaining term, CPI escalators capped 1%–5%) where tenants bear operating costs. The second is the growing Managed segment, where Sabra collects resident fees and absorbs operating costs and upside. The underlying investment thesis is demographic: the U.S. 75+ population is projected to grow ~10.1% cumulatively through 2035 against constrained SNF and senior housing supply. No single tenant represents ≥10% of revenue, and the portfolio is geographically diversified (Texas is the largest state at ~16% of beds). CEO and Chair Richard Matros, with 40+ years in healthcare real estate, is the central figure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is real but concentrated in the mix shift.** Total revenue grew from ~$449M (FY2023) to ~$503M (FY2024) to ~$575M (FY2025), and Q1 2026 revenue was $221.8M versus $183.5M a year earlier (+21%). The engine is resident fees and services in the Managed segment: $236M (2023) → $285M (2024) → $357M (2025), with Q1 2026 at $116.7M versus $77.4M. This reflects both acquisitions and conversions of triple-net leases into managed communities. Rental revenue, by contrast, is flat-to-declining ($374M in 2025 versus $381M in 2024; Q1 2026 $95.1M versus $96.0M) as leases transition out. Reported top-line growth overstates organic economic improvement because a chunk of it is Sabra taking gross resident fees onto its books in exchange for absorbing the corresponding operating costs.

**Earnings and REIT metrics.** Net income recovered from –$78M (FY2022) to $14M (FY2023) to $127M (FY2024) to $156M (FY2025), driven by fewer impairments and better operations. For a REIT, FFO/AFFO are the operative metrics: FY2025 FFO was $360.9M ($1.48/share) and AFFO $361.6M ($1.47/share), up from AFFO of $1.43 in 2024 and $1.33 in 2023. Q1 2026 AFFO was $0.39/share versus $0.37. FY2025 FFO/AFFO benefited from a $17.2M non-cash gain on terminated interest-rate swaps, which is appropriately backed out of AFFO.

**Cash flow and dividend coverage are solid.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $348.6M comfortably covered the $289.5M common dividend; Q1 2026 OCF of $98.4M covered the $75.7M dividend. The dividend has been held flat at $0.30/quarter ($1.20 annualized, ~5.4% yield), producing an AFFO payout ratio near 82% — well-covered, with room for a future raise that management has chosen to defer in favor of retaining capital for de-levering and growth.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$2.69B (Q1 2026): $1.25B senior notes (2027/2029/2031 maturities), ~$1.04B term loans, $355M on the revolver, and $43M secured. Debt/equity is ~0.95x with equity-to-assets ~51%. The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 flags "distress" but is a false signal — it is driven by the low revenue/assets ratio (0.06) inherent to an asset-heavy REIT, and coverage, ~$1.2B liquidity, covenant compliance, and OCF-to-dividend coverage all indicate a financially sound company. Management proactively de-risked near-term maturities: in July 2025 it redeemed the $500M 5.125% 2026 notes with a new $500M term loan (2030 maturity, swapped to ~4.64%). Interest expense declined YoY (FY2025 $112.5M versus $115.3M) despite higher rates. The next meaningful maturities are the $100M 2027 notes and the revolver (Jan 2027, with two 6-month extensions).

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth path rests on three levers. First, the Avamere re-tenanting: the ~30% rent uplift, with all 26 SNFs transferring to new operators by year-end 2026. Second, smaller re-tenanting, rent-reset, and lease-amendment initiatives adding >$9M/year of run-rate cash NOI. Third, continued acquisition of managed senior housing communities funded through the ATM (Q1 2026 acquisitions of 3 SHOP communities plus 1 SNF for $96.1M), plus capital recycling (FY2025 sold 15 facilities for ~$88.5M; post-Q1 sold 3 held-for-sale SNFs for ~$79.4M gross).

The raised FY2026 guidance builds on the initial February 2026 guidance that implied ~4.9% NFFO / ~5.4% NAFFO growth. This is mid-single-digit per-share growth — respectable for a healthcare REIT but not enough to warrant a growth re-rating.

The estimate-revision data cuts against near-term momentum. Current-quarter EPS is unchanged over 30 days but down 4.8% over 90 days; next-quarter down 2.0%/30d and 7.4%/90d; current-year down 0.8%/30d and 4.3%/90d. Only next-year estimates ticked up modestly (+2.2%/30d), likely reflecting the Avamere accretion feeding forward-year models. The beat/miss record has also weakened: after a +51% beat in Q2 2025, the last three quarters missed (–51.7%, –37.6%, –5.3%), which tempers confidence in the visibility of headline EPS (though FFO/AFFO, less impacted by non-cash items, held up better). The mix shift itself introduces earnings volatility: managed opex rose 22% in FY2025 and 45% in Q1 2026 (largely acquisition-driven), and the SHOP model gives Sabra no triple-net buffer against labor inflation or occupancy softness.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.36 SBRA trades at ~14x the midpoint of FY2026 Normalized AFFO guidance (~$1.60) and offers a ~5.4% dividend. Against the Health Care REIT peer set, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 23.4x and median yield 6.1%. SBRA's headline trailing P/E of ~35x is not the right lens for a REIT (GAAP net income is depressed by non-cash D&A and lumpy impairments); on AFFO it is more reasonably valued. But relative to the peers with positive earnings — CTRE at 23.4x, LTC at 14.5x with a 6.1% yield, UHT at 31.4x with 7.4% — SBRA's ~5.4% yield sits below the median, and it trades essentially at its 52-week high while several peers trade at meaningful discounts to their highs (MPT –25%, CTRE –16%, LTC –11%).

The stock is at roughly the consensus mean target of $22.71 (median $23.00, range $19.00–$25.00). Absent a new accretive catalyst, analyst targets here are a reasonable ceiling proxy rather than a floor: this is a mid-single-digit grower at a fair-to-full AFFO multiple, not a cheap stock where consensus lags. Citi's June 23 downgrade to Neutral (while upgrading American Healthcare REIT to Buy) was a relative-value rotation call rather than a fundamental red flag, but it captures the point: on a risk-adjusted basis, cleaner senior-housing/medical-office peers without SNF reimbursement overhang look relatively more attractive at current prices.

Cheapness is not the setup here, and where a low multiple exists (trailing GAAP) it reflects the REIT accounting artifact rather than mispriced value. The valuation score is below neutral because the stock has already captured the Avamere accretion, sits at the top of its range near consensus, and faces negative near-term estimate revisions; the asymmetry favors downside from a disappointment more than upside from re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Sabra competes against far larger healthcare REITs — Welltower, Ventas, Omega, American Healthcare REIT — that enjoy lower costs of capital. Sabra's niche is being a flexible capital source to mid-size and underserved operators in secondary markets, using loans and preferred equity with purchase options as an acquisition pipeline. Industry tailwinds (aging demographics, constrained supply) are favorable, but the SNF sub-sector carries reimbursement and regulatory overhang that pure senior-housing and medical-office peers avoid.

The competitive-position trajectory is modestly improving, which is why this dimension has ticked up in prior reviews. The Avamere transition is the evidence: an operator-initiated exit that Sabra converted into a rent uplift by lining up a stronger operator (Cascadia, whose leadership has prior experience with Avamere facilities). The deliberate rotation toward managed senior housing also reduces SNF concentration over time (SNF revenue fell from 35.6% in FY2025 to ~31% in Q1 2026), improving the portfolio's structural profile even as it raises operating-cost exposure. The trade-off is that the SHOP pivot pushes Sabra toward being a senior-housing operator hybrid, competing more directly on operating execution against larger, better-capitalized platforms.

## Risk Assessment

1. **SNF reimbursement and regulatory exposure.** ~31% of Q1 2026 revenue is SNF-tied. Medicare SNF rate updates are decelerating: +4.2% FY2025, +3.2% FY2026, and a proposed +2.4% FY2027. Medicaid is the larger swing factor given state budget pressures. The repeal of the CMS federal Minimum Staffing Standards (effective 2/2/2026) is a positive for operators.
2. **Legislative risk to the REIT-owns-healthcare model.** The 10-K flags proposed legislation ranging from additional oversight to potential prohibitions on investors acquiring or increasing control in nursing homes — a genuine structural risk specific to SNF-heavy REITs.
3. **Operating exposure via the Managed segment.** As SHOP grows, Sabra directly absorbs labor cost inflation, occupancy volatility, and management-fee risk with no triple-net buffer.
4. **Tenant/operator transition risk.** Re-tenanting causes non-cash rent write-offs and temporary rent gaps ($8.7M of non-cash write-offs in FY2025). The Avamere transition, while accretive, must execute cleanly through year-end 2026.
5. **Equity-dilution funding model.** Growth is ATM-funded: 20.5M forward shares at ~$19.17 average plus 3.2M at $18.10, and share count rose from ~233M (2024) to ~252M (Q1 2026). The model works only while the stock trades above issuance/NAV; a decline makes growth more dilutive.
6. **Rate/refinancing risk.** Variable-rate revolver at ~4.68% and swapped term loans; a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the ATM-funded growth model. Manageable given laddered maturities and current liquidity.

Item 1A risk factors were unchanged in the Q1 2026 10-Q versus the FY2025 10-K — no new or modified risks flagged. The risk score sits at neutral: the risks are largely known and partially priced, but the SNF regulatory overhang and the SHOP operating-cost exposure are genuine idiosyncratic factors that cleaner peers avoid, and the negative estimate-revision trend suggests some of the near-term downside is not fully discounted at a 52-week-high price.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 25.7% versus 1-year historical volatility of 23.5% — fair, with a modest 2.2% spread and IV in the 26th percentile of its chain. The term structure is in contango (25.7% near-term versus 27.9% long-term), a normal/complacent structure. The one flag is a 7.0% put/call skew at the near-term tenor, indicating elevated put demand into the August expiry, consistent with the 13.7% short interest (7.2 days to cover). Put/call volume and OI ratios are near zero, so this is not aggressive directional positioning. IV falling from 30.4% to 25.7% as the stock rose 15.8% since the prior snapshot reflects resolved uncertainty around the Avamere/guidance catalyst. The options market is not pricing an imminent large move, but the modest put skew plus meaningful short interest signals some institutional caution at these levels, consistent with an income hold rather than a chase.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($25–$26).** The Avamere re-tenanting executes cleanly, Cascadia stabilizes the 22 facilities, and the additional run-rate NOI flows through as guided, delivering Normalized AFFO at the top of guidance with continued mid-single-digit growth into FY2027. The SHOP mix shift adds occupancy-driven upside as senior housing demand strengthens with demographics, SNF concentration keeps falling, and the market rewards the improving portfolio quality with a slightly higher AFFO multiple. A future dividend raise (payout at ~82% leaves room) provides an additional catalyst. This gets the stock toward the high end of the analyst range (high target $25.00) with the ~5.4% yield on top.

**Bear Case ($18–$19).** Medicaid pressure or adverse SNF-ownership legislation weighs on the ~31% SNF book; SHOP labor inflation and occupancy softness compress managed-segment margins with no triple-net buffer; the Avamere transition incurs larger-than-expected transition costs or rent gaps; and estimate revisions continue drifting lower, prompting further target cuts (à la Citi's downgrade). With the stock ATM-funded, a decline makes the growth engine more dilutive. This retraces toward the 52-week low and the low analyst target of $19.00, an ~15% drawdown, cushioned by the covered dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The durable return here is the ~5.4% covered dividend backed by demographic tailwinds and competent asset management, not near-term appreciation. After a 22% run to the top of its 52-week range at ~14x Normalized AFFO and roughly the consensus target, the appreciation case is largely spent, and negative estimate revisions plus the SNF regulatory overhang argue against chasing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$19s/high-$18s, where the ~5.4%+ yield and Normalized AFFO multiple offer a more attractive entry and re-open appreciation potential.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (current-year and next-quarter consensus stabilizing or rising over consecutive periods) as Avamere accretion flows through.
- Evidence of accelerating same-store SHOP occupancy/NOI growth confirming the mix shift is compounding rather than just adding gross revenue.
- A dividend raise signaling management confidence in AFFO durability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse SNF-ownership legislation advancing, or a material Medicaid rate cut in a large state.
- Avamere/Cascadia transition slippage — rent gaps or non-cash write-offs materially above the guided accretion.
- AFFO payout ratio deteriorating above ~90% or OCF failing to cover the dividend.
- The stock breaking issuance/NAV support (below ~$18–$19) while management continues ATM issuance, turning the growth model dilutive.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026) for confirmation of the Avamere transition timeline and guidance, and year-end 2026 as the transition completes and FY2027 guidance is set.

## Conclusion

Sabra is a soundly financed mid-cap healthcare REIT executing a credible strategic pivot, and the Avamere re-tenanting is a genuine, well-managed catalyst that deserves the re-rating it produced. The dividend is well-covered at ~82% of AFFO, leverage is appropriate for the model, and liability management has been competent. But the investment case for new money is asymmetric to the downside at $22.36: the stock sits near its 52-week high and its consensus target, at a fair-to-full ~14x Normalized AFFO with a below-peer-median yield, while quarterly estimate revisions drift lower and the SNF reimbursement/regulatory overhang persists. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.2 → 5.5) and three consecutive HOLDs reflect a stock that has oscillated around fair value, and the current price captures the good news. Hold for the ~5.4% yield; add on a pullback toward the high-$18s/low-$19s or on confirmation that the mix shift is delivering compounding same-store NOI growth rather than just gross-revenue mix.
