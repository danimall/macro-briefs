**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AAON manufactures premium, highly-configurable commercial/industrial HVAC equipment and, increasingly, custom cooling systems for hyperscale and AI data centers through its BASX segment. The investment story has two layers. The legacy commercial-HVAC franchise is a mature, rate-sensitive, cyclical business where AAON is a niche premium player taking replacement-market share against much larger rivals (Trane, Carrier, Lennox, Daikin, JCI). The growth engine is BASX data-center cooling, where put-in-place data-center construction rose roughly 240% from 2022 to 2025 and the liquid-cooling transition for >100kW AI racks is a structural tailwind AAON addresses with new proprietary Coolant Distribution Units and free-cooling chillers.

The numbers confirm the transition is live. FY2025 revenue grew 20% to $1.44B, but net income fell 36% to $108M as a stacked set of largely one-time headwinds (an ERP go-live at Longview and Memphis that choked coil production, the mandatory R-454B refrigerant switch, and Memphis ramp overhead) compressed operating margin from 17.4% to 10.1%. Q1 2026 showed the recovery: revenue +54% to $497M, operating margin recovering to 11.5%, operating cash flow turning positive, and total backlog reaching $2.13B (BASX backlog $1.62B, +160% YoY). Consensus estimates have moved with the fundamentals — current-year EPS revised +13.6% and next-year +14.8% over 90 days.

The tension is valuation and cash quality. At $107.68 the stock trades near 48x consensus FY26 EPS of $2.25 and roughly 32x FY27 of $3.40, a premium to the Building Products peer median P/E of ~22.7x. The business consumed roughly $190M of free cash flow in FY2025 (capex plus a large working-capital build) funded by a revolver that grew from $200M to a $425M draw. The growth is real and the estimate momentum is a genuine positive signal, but the price already reflects much of it, and the model carries hyperscaler-scale order volatility, customer concentration, and upfront working-capital funding risk. We rate AAON a HOLD (growth_hold), constructive on the transformation but disciplined on entry.

## Company Overview

AAON operates three reporting segments under a "mass semi-customization" model — build-to-order manufacturing using flexible CAM systems:

- **AAON Oklahoma** (Tulsa, Memphis, Parkville): the legacy franchise — rooftop units, air handlers, heat pumps, controls, and aftermarket parts. Roughly 50–56% of revenue.
- **AAON Coil Products** (Longview, TX): coils plus semi-custom units, increasingly producing BASX-branded data-center gear.
- **BASX** (Redmond, OR; acquired 2021): custom cooling for hyperscale/AI data centers — CRAH units, fan coil walls, evaporative coolers, new-in-2025 Coolant Distribution Units for liquid cooling, and a water-free Free Cooling Chiller, plus cleanroom systems.

The company's competitive model is differentiated in two ways. It sells through independent manufacturers' representatives rather than a captive channel like its larger competitors, and it competes on total cost of ownership and technical engineering rather than price. Its Tulsa NAIC lab has testing capabilities management describes as globally unique. End customers for BASX include Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, QTS, and Applied Digital — AAON builds the cooling equipment for their facilities.

A leadership transition completed over the past year: Matthew J. Tobolski, a BASX/technology-oriented executive, is now CEO; prior CEO Gary D. Fields moved to the board; and founder Norman Asbjornson (in his 90s) remains a director and by far the largest holder at ~11.6M shares.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 income statement reads poorly in isolation but the drivers are transitional. The four identifiable causes of the margin compression:

1. **ERP disruption** — go-lives at Longview (April 1) and Memphis (November 1) choked coil production, cascading into Tulsa rooftop output and crushing AAON Oklahoma gross margin from 37.3% to 29.0%.
2. **Refrigerant transition** — the mandatory EPA switch to low-GWP R-454B on January 1, 2025 created supply-chain constraints.
3. **Memphis ramp overhead** (~$16M in 2025) sits in the AAON Oklahoma segment while its output books to BASX at cost, depressing legacy-segment optics.
4. **Legacy demand softness** — AAON-branded sales fell 8.3% as nonresidential construction contracted; interest expense surged to $17.7M (from $2.9M) on the revolver draw.

Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery: diluted EPS $0.48 (+37%) and operating cash flow of +$34M versus –$9M a year earlier. Tulsa production is recovering and ERP disruption is receding.

The central financial watch item is cash flow. FY2025 operating cash flow was roughly $0.5M and free cash flow was approximately –$190M ($191M capex plus intangibles against near-zero OCF). This is growth-driven rather than distress: capex funded Memphis/Longview/Redmond capacity while OCF was consumed by a working-capital build (AR +$167M, inventories +$74M, contract assets +$112M) that is structural to BASX data-center jobs, where AAON funds working capital upfront. Revolver debt rose to $398M at YE25 and $425M by Q1'26.

Leverage remains conservative at 1.71x against a 3.0x covenant cap, current ratio 2.6x, and roughly $174M of revolver availability at Q1'26 (down from $201M) on a facility recently expanded to $600M via accordion. The Altman Z of 2.59 sits in the grey zone purely because of the debt-funded working-capital swing, not underlying weakness. FCF conversion below 100% is expected in a scaling capital-hungry model, but it warrants monitoring: the AR balance now exceeds $290M and the key question is whether contract assets convert to cash on schedule. Capital allocation shows discipline appropriate to the phase — $190M of capex planned again for 2026, buybacks paused in Q1'26 (zero open-market repurchases), and a 25% dividend raise to $0.40 annualized (~0.4% yield).

Financial health scores a 5.5. The conservative reported leverage and strong backlog support the balance sheet, but the negative free cash flow, declining revolver availability, and working-capital intensity temper the score. This aligns with the recent trajectory (6.5 → 5.5) — the decline reflects the genuine cash-quality tension in the model, not a scoring artifact.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest dimension in this story and the one where the evidence is cleanest. BASX-branded product sales rose 143% in 2025 to $549M. Total backlog rose 111% YoY to $1.83B at YE2025 and added another $300M by Q1'26. This is a business being re-rated as an AI-infrastructure derivative, and the demand signal is corroborated by the broader data-center capex environment: the liquid-cooling transition for >100kW racks and rising put-in-place data-center construction confirm a structural, multi-year demand shift at the infrastructure layer.

Estimate revisions reinforce this, with next-quarter EPS up +10.7% over the same 90-day window. The revision magnitude falls just short of the +30% threshold that constitutes a standalone strong-buy signal, and the current-year figure has flattened slightly over the most recent 30 days (–0.3%), so the momentum is positive but not accelerating. Combined with revenue growth well above 40% in Q1'26, the direction is clearly favorable.

Two qualifications keep this from a 9. First, the backlog is not guaranteed revenue — the 10-K explicitly notes data-center orders carry "more risk and we often see shifts in timing, cancellations and re-issuances," unlike historically firm commercial orders. Second, the legacy segment provides no growth cushion; management sees "stabilization" but "no clear reacceleration" in nonresidential construction. The earnings-surprise record is also two-sided (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters, including a –34.8% Q2'25 miss and a –14.8% Q4'25 miss before the +62.3% Q1'26 beat), reflecting the lumpiness the company itself warns about. Growth Prospects scores 8.0, consistent with the improving trajectory (7.0 → 8.5) but shaded down modestly to reflect order-timing risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $107.68, AAON carries a market cap of ~$8.4B. Against the Building Products peer median P/E of 22.7x (SSD 22.7x, BLDR 29.8x, WTS 29.4x, AOS 15.6x, AWI 22.0x), AAON commands a substantial premium.

The cycle-adjustment argument partially supports the premium. Trailing EPS of $1.29 reflected the ERP/refrigerant/Memphis trough, so trailing P/E of ~76x overstates the true multiple; the recovery is real and estimates are rising. On a PEG basis, using the ~51% EPS growth implied between FY26 and FY27 consensus, the FY27 multiple of 32x gives a PEG below 1.0, which is reasonable for a genuine growth acceleration. Analyst price targets (mean $151, median $150, range $150–154) sit well above the current price, but because forward P/E remains above 30x and the multiple already prices in continued beats, those targets are not automatic floors — they reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the stock.

Even on the more generous FY27 basis, the stock is not cheap, and the free-cash-flow profile means the "earnings" being valued do not yet convert to cash. A pure-play comparison is instructive: KeyBanc's July 2026 initiation rated AAON Sector Weight while rating pure-play data-center-cooling peer Vertiv Overweight, implicitly flagging that AAON carries legacy-HVAC drag and is more fully valued. The stock has pulled back ~28% from its May high near $150 to ~$108, which improves the risk/reward, but the drawdown reflects legitimate concern about order lumpiness and cash conversion, not just noise. Cheapness is not the argument here, and the premium is only partially justified by growth. Valuation scores 5.0 — balanced, with the premium multiple offset by genuine estimate momentum, and no clear asymmetric upside at the current level.

## Competitive Landscape

AAON's competitive position is genuinely differentiating and, in the data-center segment, improving. In legacy HVAC it is a premium niche player taking replacement-market share against far larger, better-resourced competitors — a stable-to-slightly-improving position but structurally constrained by scale. In data-center cooling, BASX is a credible engineered-to-order competitor to Vertiv, STULZ, Munters, Modine, and Johnson Controls (Silent-Aire), with new CDU and free-cooling chiller platforms directly addressing the liquid-cooling transition.

The trajectory favors AAON in the growth segment: the 160% YoY BASX backlog growth indicates share capture in an expanding market. The moat there is technical engineering and customization capability rather than scale, which cuts both ways against well-capitalized rivals like Vertiv. The strategic execution question is whether AAON can scale BASX capacity (Memphis, Longview, Redmond) fast enough to convert its backlog without margin dilution — Q1'26 already showed a BASX margin drag from outsourcing. On balance the competitive position is improving in the segment that matters most, supporting a score of 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully reflected in the current premium multiple:

1. **Data-center customer and order concentration (central risk).** Three customers each exceeded 10% of revenue in 2025 (up from two in 2024), and three exceeded 10% of AR. A single large customer's deferral could produce a material quarterly air-pocket against the backlog.
2. **Working-capital intensity.** Growth consumes cash upfront through contract assets and inventory. A demand pause after heavy capex and inventory commitments (some under non-cancellable contracts) could strand capital, as management explicitly warns.
3. **Legacy cyclicality.** Nonresidential construction remains soft and rate-sensitive; the higher-for-longer rate environment is a headwind to the legacy franchise and, indirectly, to the revolver-funded working-capital model via interest expense.
4. **Execution/ERP.** The ERP rollout is not fully complete company-wide and remains a named risk. Single-location concentration adds tail risk (Tulsa in tornado alley, Redmond in wildfire zone).
5. **Regulatory/refrigerant patchwork.** State-level low-GWP mandates (NY 2034, with CA/WA expected to follow) will require costly R&D and a patchwork of state SKUs.
6. **Leadership transition.** A fresh CEO and unproven top-team dynamic during the company's most important strategic pivot.

Risk Factors scores 4.5 — the concentration, cash-conversion, and order-volatility risks are specific and material, and the premium valuation leaves limited cushion if any binary plays out unfavorably.

Near-term ATM IV of 97.2% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 60.8% (a 36.4pp spread) and at the 97th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 97.2% > 6-month 79.1%) — a market pricing imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming earnings event. The put/call volume ratio of 9.50 looks alarming but the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.37 and put/call skew is roughly balanced at –2.0%, so the elevated volume ratio reads as short-dated hedging or event positioning rather than a structural bearish tilt. The implied move of ±21.4% into the nearest expiry signals the market expects a large reaction to the next print, arguing for patience on entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($150–170).** BASX backlog converts to revenue on schedule with recovering margins as ERP disruption fully clears and Memphis/Longview capacity scales. The AI data-center cooling tailwind proves durable and multi-year, driving revenue growth above 40% and EPS toward and beyond the $3.40 FY27 consensus. Estimate revisions continue upward as consensus catches the recovery, and the stock re-rates on a combination of higher earnings and sustained premium multiple. Working capital normalizes as contract assets convert, turning FCF positive and removing the cash-quality overhang. At 40–45x FY27 EPS of $3.40+, the stock reaches the analyst target zone.

**Bear Case ($75–85).** Data-center order lumpiness produces a quarterly air-pocket — a large customer defers or cancels, backlog conversion slows, and the market punishes the elevated multiple hard. Working capital continues to consume cash, revolver availability tightens below comfortable levels, and interest expense compounds. Legacy HVAC stays soft in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Margins fail to recover to prior peak as BASX outsourcing dilutes profitability. On a compressed ~30x multiple against disappointed FY27 estimates, the stock retraces toward the low-to-mid $80s, roughly 20–30% below current levels.

The base case sits between: continued backlog conversion, gradual margin recovery, and EPS around $3.40 in FY27, but a multiple that normalizes somewhat from today's premium as the market demands cash-flow proof. That produces a fair value near current levels, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale — the 12-month thesis is positive but the price already discounts much of it, and the entry point is unattractive relative to the risk.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$80s to high-$80s, where FY27 forward P/E compresses toward the mid-20s and the risk/reward turns clearly asymmetric.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow with backlog conversion visible in reported revenue, demonstrating that the working-capital-intensive model can self-fund.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions re-accelerating above +30% over 90 days alongside sustained backlog growth, which would elevate the standalone growth signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material data-center order cancellation or backlog reduction, or a customer deferral that cuts a quarter's revenue guidance materially.
- Revolver availability falling below ~$100M without a corresponding OCF turn, signaling the model is outrunning its financing.
- Operating margin failing to hold above ~10% over the next two quarters, indicating BASX margin dilution is structural rather than transitional.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report is the natural checkpoint given the ±21.4% implied move — it will test both backlog-to-cash conversion and margin recovery. Reassess promptly after the print.

## Conclusion

AAON is a business-model transformation into AI data-center cooling wrapped around a cyclical legacy HVAC franchise. The growth evidence is strong and corroborated: BASX backlog +160% YoY to $1.62B, total backlog $2.13B, Q1'26 revenue +54%, and positive estimate revisions of +13.6% current-year and +14.8% next-year. The 2025 earnings decline was largely self-inflicted and transitional — ERP go-lives, the refrigerant switch, and Memphis ramp overhead — and Q1'26 confirms the recovery is underway.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are equally concrete. The stock trades at ~48x FY26 and ~32x FY27 consensus EPS, a premium to peers that leaves limited margin for error against a model that burned ~$190M in FCF, carries hyperscaler-scale order volatility and customer concentration, and depends on backlog converting to cash on schedule. Insider activity is cautionary-neutral — clustered director and executive selling in the $134–143 range, no open-market buying, and a paused buyback. The recent ~28% drawdown from the May high has improved the entry but not enough to make the risk/reward asymmetric.

We rate AAON a **HOLD**. The transformation is real and the company deserves ownership at a better price or after backlog-to-cash conversion is proven. At current levels, the price and the cash-quality tension keep us on the sidelines pending the next earnings print. Overall score: 5.9, consistent with the flat HOLD trajectory across three prior reports.
