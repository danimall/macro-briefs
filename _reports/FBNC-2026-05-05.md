## Executive Summary

First Bancorp is a $12.7 billion-asset community bank holding company operating 113 branches across North and South Carolina, now emerging from a multi-year earnings trough caused by a deliberate $110M securities portfolio restructuring. The repositioning — selling ~$1B of sub-2% yielding bonds and reinvesting at 4%+ — depressed reported EPS to $1.84 (2024) and $2.68 (2025), but Q1 2026 annualizes at $4.52 and consensus estimates approximately $4.35 for full-year 2026 (per SNL consensus as of April 2026). The stock trades at approximately 13.3x forward earnings on an accelerating EPS trajectory with the securities restructuring now fully reflected in run-rate results.

The investment case rests on three factors: (1) the securities restructuring is complete and its full NII benefit is still seasoning into results; (2) the Carolinas — among the fastest-growing U.S. states by population and business formation — provide a structural organic growth tailwind; and (3) at $57.87, the stock offers potential upside if earnings delivery exceeds current consensus with downside supported by tangible book value of $29.01 per share (1.94x P/TBV). The primary risk is CRE concentration (87% of loans secured by real estate) in an environment where 30-year Treasury yields have breached 5% and commercial real estate refinancing stress is elevated nationally.

## Company Overview

First Bancorp (FBNC) is the fourth-largest NC-headquartered commercial bank, operating through its subsidiary First Bank. The bank was organized in 1934 and began operations in 1935 as Bank of Montgomery; the holding company was incorporated in 1983. FBNC has grown to $12.7B in assets through a combination of organic growth and strategic acquisitions (Select Bancorp in 2021, GrandSouth Bancorp in 2023).

**Business Model:** Traditional community banking — net interest income represents the dominant revenue stream, supplemented by service charges, insurance commissions, wealth management fees, and SBA lending. The loan portfolio is diversified: 33% non-owner occupied CRE, 20% residential 1-4 family, 15% owner-occupied CRE, 12% C&I, 9% construction, 6% multifamily. The deposit base is high-quality: 32% noninterest-bearing, negligible brokered deposits ($4.9M), ~40% uninsured.

**Geographic Footprint:** 100 NC branches spanning Charlotte, Raleigh/Triangle, Greensboro/Triad, Asheville, and Wilmington; 13 SC branches in Greenville, Columbia, and Charleston. No single county exceeds 10% of total loans, providing meaningful geographic diversification within the Carolinas.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power Inflection:**

The reported 2024-2025 EPS trajectory is misleading. The company realized $109.6M in aggregate securities losses ($38M in 2024, $71.6M in 2025) to restructure $1B+ of the investment portfolio from sub-2% yields to 4%+ yields. Adjusted for these one-time items, 2025 EPS was $4.01 (per company-reported non-GAAP figures). Q1 2026's $1.13 EPS confirms the run-rate has stabilized at this higher level, with Q1 2026 ROTCE of 16.05% and ROCE of 11.22% demonstrating strong underlying profitability.

**NIM Expansion:** Net interest margin recovered from a 2.89% trough to 3.40% (full-year 2025) and expanded further to 3.67%-3.68% in Q1 2026, driven by: (a) deposit cost repricing downward (from 1.76% peak to 1.32%), (b) securities restructuring adding $20-25M in annual pretax NII, and (c) loan repricing at higher rates with an increased variable-rate mix (29% vs. 23% prior year).

**Capital Position:** CET1 of 14.10% is 710bps above the regulatory minimum. TCE ratio improved from 8.22% to 9.61% year-over-year as unrealized securities losses narrowed from $368M to $194M. Tangible book value per share was $29.01 at March 31, 2026.

**Expense Discipline:** Noninterest expenses grew 1.6% in 2025 while net interest income grew 19.9%. This operating leverage reflects a well-run community bank in an improving rate environment.

**Credit Quality:** NPAs/total assets at 0.30% (0.32% at Q1 2026). Net charge-offs at 0.10% of average loans (0.06% annualized in Q1 2026). Allowance for credit losses covers nonaccrual loans 3.4x. The Hurricane Helene reserve of $13M (taken Q3 2024) has been almost entirely released ($11.1M reversed in 2025), confirming initial provisioning was conservative.

**Capital Return:** The $40M buyback authorization remains largely untapped, providing potential shareholder return support.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth:** 7.8% loan growth in 2025 ($8.1B to $8.7B), driven by the Carolinas' above-average population growth and business formation rates. A new loan participation initiative targeting larger commercial borrowers indicates management sees room to move upmarket without sacrificing credit discipline. However, the company notes that competition for high-quality loans remains strong and loan rates remain under competitive pressure.

**Earnings Growth:** SNL consensus estimates FY2026 EPS of approximately $4.35 (as of April 2026), representing significant growth versus reported 2025 EPS of $2.68. The 2025-to-2026 step-change reflects the full-year benefit of completed securities restructuring, while further out, growth reflects organic loan growth, continued deposit repricing, and modest operating leverage.

**Q1 2026 Momentum:** Q1 2026 results ($46.7M net income, $1.13 EPS, 16.05% ROTCE) suggest the bank may be tracking ahead of the $4.35 consensus. If maintained, annualized Q1 results would imply approximately $4.52 EPS for 2026, representing meaningful upside to current estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $57.87, FBNC trades at:
- Approximately 13.3x forward P/E (FY2026E consensus of ~$4.35 per SNL)
- ~1.94x tangible book value ($29.01 per share at Q1 2026)
- 21.6x trailing P/E (on depressed 2025 reported earnings)

According to the company's own peer valuation data (April 2026 investor presentation), FBNC trades at 13.3x 2026 consensus EPS versus a peer median of 12.3x, and 1.94x P/TBV versus a peer median of 1.82x. The modest premium to peers reflects FBNC's strong NIM trajectory, above-average credit quality, and attractive Carolinas market positioning.

**Why the valuation premium may be justified:** The trailing P/E of ~21.6x appears expensive, which may deter screens and algorithmic buyers that look at backward-facing multiples. The $109.6M in securities losses over 2024-2025 created a trough in reported earnings that masks the true earnings power now visible in Q1 2026 results. Forward-looking investors who understand the restructuring see a bank with strong momentum; backward-looking screens see elevated trailing multiples.

**Upside potential:** If FBNC delivers EPS closer to the Q1 annualized run-rate of $4.52 (or higher), the effective forward multiple would compress to approximately 12.8x, bringing it closer to the peer median. The stock's upside case depends on earnings delivery above current consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Advantages:**
- Diversified 113-branch network in two of America's fastest-growing states
- High-quality deposit franchise (32% NIB, negligible brokered) provides low-cost funding
- Scale benefits from crossing $10B threshold now absorbed (Durbin Amendment impact behind them)
- Local decision-making vs. national bank bureaucracy attracts commercial clients

**Trajectory Assessment:** FBNC's competitive position is stable. Loan growth of 7.8% in 2025 reflects strong origination activity, though the company acknowledges competitive pressures on loan pricing. The expansion into SC via GrandSouth (now fully integrated) opens a new growth vector. The bank doesn't have a clear structural moat distinguishing it from other well-run Carolinas community banks — it is a high-quality operator in a good market, which is defensible but not irreplaceable.

**Risks to Position:** National banks and super-regionals (Truist, Regions) compete on pricing and product breadth. Fintech lenders erode the small-loan and consumer segments. The bank's competitive differentiation relies on relationship quality and local knowledge, which is durable but hard to scale beyond a certain geographic radius.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary Risk):** 87% of loans are secured by real estate. Non-owner occupied CRE is 33% of total loans ($2.8B). With 30-year Treasury yields breaching 5%, CRE refinancing risk is elevated. While S&P Global reported the industrywide CRE delinquency rate at 1.53% in Q4 2025 (suggesting stabilization), refinancing pressures from higher long-term rates remain a concern. FBNC's mitigants: (a) geographic diversification (no single county >10%), (b) granular loan sizes (office avg. $1.4M), (c) strong underwriting (credit metrics suggest discipline), (d) Carolinas CRE markets are relatively stronger than national averages due to population growth. A national CRE correction would not spare even well-positioned Carolinas banks entirely.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** The bank is moderately asset-sensitive (+4.6% NII in +200bp). With the Fed constrained and unable to cut, the elevated rate environment is net positive for NIM in the near term. However, if rates rise further (30Y already >5%), borrower stress could offset NIM benefits through higher provisions.

**Goodwill ($478.8M):** Represents 29% of equity. A sustained decline in stock price below book value (~$41/share) could trigger impairment testing. At current prices ($57.87), there is meaningful buffer.

**Valuation Premium to Peers:** At 13.3x versus a 12.3x peer median and 1.94x P/TBV versus 1.82x peer median, FBNC carries a modest premium that requires continued execution to sustain. A miss on earnings expectations or credit deterioration could lead to a sharper de-rating than peers.

**Short Interest:** 5.4% of float with 8.2 days to cover is moderate. The 8.2 days-to-cover figure suggests the short position would be difficult to exit quickly if sentiment shifted, which provides some squeeze potential.

**Macro/Geopolitical:** As a rate-sensitive bank with CRE exposure, FBNC is directly impacted by the 30Y >5% environment. The stagflationary macro backdrop affects FBNC less directly than credit-intensive consumer lenders — the bank's borrowers are commercial entities and residential property owners in growing markets, not leveraged consumers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($64-68, +11-18%):**
- NIM continues expanding as remaining fixed-rate loans reprice higher and deposit costs stabilize
- FY2026 EPS delivery of $4.50+ (above $4.35 consensus) given Q1 annualized run-rate of $4.52
- Carolinas organic growth continues at 7-9% annually
- Multiple maintained or modestly expanded as earnings momentum continues
- $40M buyback is activated at current valuations

**Bear Case ($44-48, -17% to -24%):**
- CRE stress materializes in the Carolinas as 30Y >5% impairs refinancing
- NIM compression if rate cuts eventually arrive (asset sensitivity works in reverse)
- Loan growth stalls as economic uncertainty dampens borrower demand
- Goodwill impairment test triggered if stock falls below tangible book
- Regional banks de-rate broadly in a credit cycle
- Stock reverts toward 10x the lower end of the EPS range

## Conclusion

First Bancorp offers a straightforward value proposition: a well-run community bank in one of America's best banking markets, with earnings power that has been obscured by a one-time portfolio restructuring and is now normalizing into reported results. At approximately 13.3x forward earnings — a modest premium to peers justified by superior NIM trajectory and credit quality — the stock offers upside if the bank continues to deliver above-consensus results as Q1 2026 suggests. The primary risk is CRE concentration in a rising long-rate environment, mitigated by geographic diversification, granular loan sizes, and the fundamental growth dynamics of the Carolinas. BUY at current levels with a base case target of $63, predicated on continued earnings execution above current consensus.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected forward P/E (13.3x vs. originally stated 11.4x), lower consensus EPS ($4.35 vs. $4.69), and company data showing FBNC trades at a premium to peers (not a discount) materially reduce the valuation-based upside case and narrow the risk/reward ratio from the original thesis.
