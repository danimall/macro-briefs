## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe Holdings Ltd. is a Bermuda-domiciled global reinsurer generating exceptional returns through a three-pillar model: underwriting income, fee income from managed third-party capital, and investment income from a $36B portfolio. The company produced $56.03 in diluted EPS for 2025, representing a 25.9% ROE, while absorbing $786M net from the California Wildfires — one of the largest single-event losses in insurance history. Q1 2026 continued the strong trajectory with a 73% combined ratio, operating EPS of $13.75 (GAAP diluted EPS of $6.57), and 21.8% annualized operating ROE.

At $303.19, the stock trades at approximately 5.4x 2025 earnings, 1.23x book value ($247/share), and at a discount to reinsurance-sector peers. The company has reduced its share count by 16.6% over two years ($1.6B repurchased in 2025 alone), amplifying per-share value creation. The primary risks are casualty reserve deterioration (2017-2019 accident years showing 16-18% adverse development), reinsurance cycle softening as capacity returns, and the structural impact of Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax.

The score trajectory shows consistent BUY ratings at 7.4-7.5 over the past three months. Valuation has compressed from 8.5 to 7.5 as the stock appreciated 22% over the trailing year, while financial health improved to 8.5 reflecting the Q1 2026 results. I maintain the BUY recommendation. The combination of elite capital allocation, proven catastrophe resilience, and a valuation still below peers creates asymmetric upside with a defined margin of safety.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe was founded in 1993 and has evolved from a pure-play property catastrophe reinsurer into a diversified reinsurance platform following the transformational Validus acquisition (closed November 2023), which significantly expanded assets and scale. The company operates through two segments:

**Property Segment** ($4.9B gross premiums written, 2025): Catastrophe and other property reinsurance. Highly profitable in benign loss years — 61.4% combined ratio in 2025 despite $1.2B in large loss events. This is the earnings engine.

**Casualty and Specialty Segment** ($6.8B gross premiums written, 2025): General casualty, professional liability, credit/mortgage, and specialty lines. Longer-tail business with a 104.4% combined ratio in 2025 — slightly unprofitable on underwriting but valuable for generating investment float.

The Capital Partners platform manages third-party capital through joint ventures (DaVinci, Fontana, Medici, Vermeer) and managed funds, generating $329M in fee income for 2025. This creates a fee-based revenue stream with lower capital consumption.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at RNR is properly measured by book value per share accumulation plus dividends, the primary compensation metric for management. This grew 27% in 2025, 19.4% in 2024, and 59.3% in 2023.

**Growth headwinds are emerging:**
1. **Cycle maturation.** Management explicitly states "recent favorable reinsurance market results have increased competition and supply of reinsurance capacity, which has put some pressure on rates and margins." Casualty gross premiums declined 1.6% in 2025 (professional liability -11.2%, general casualty -5.9%).
2. **Bermuda CIT.** The new 15% corporate income tax increased income tax expense by $363.7M year over year to $396.3M in 2025, driven primarily by profitability across operating jurisdictions including Bermuda's new CIT. The 2025 effective tax rate was 9.9%, representing a permanent structural headwind to after-tax earnings relative to pre-2025 levels.
3. **Scale limitations.** At $11.7B in gross premiums, further growth requires either market share gains in a softening market or new product lines.

**Growth offsets:**
1. **Capital Partners expansion.** The March 2025 Medici UCITS launch ($341.5M) opens European distribution channels. Fee income surged 209% YoY in Q1 2026.
2. **Share buybacks.** The $1.6B repurchased in 2025 demonstrates management's commitment to per-share value creation, though the future pace will depend on capital needs, catastrophe losses, regulatory constraints, and management's stated preference to deploy capital into profitable underwriting opportunities before returning excess.
3. **Investment portfolio yield.** The 4.8% yield on $36B generates $1.7B+ annually — the current rate environment benefits the float model.

## Valuation Assessment

At $303.19, RNR trades at:
- 5.4x 2025 trailing earnings ($56.03 EPS)
- 1.23x book value ($247/share)
- A discount to selected peer P/E multiples

The peer comparison requires nuance: commonly cited peers such as Reinsurance Group of America (life reinsurance), Everest Group (diversified reinsurance), MGIC/Radian (mortgage insurance), and SiriusPoint (specialty) represent a cross-sector set rather than pure property-catastrophe reinsurance comparables. RNR's property catastrophe concentration introduces earnings volatility that warrants some discount to less volatile peers.

However, the magnitude of any discount appears excessive given: (a) 25.9% ROE in 2025 vs. peer averages of 12-15%, (b) 16.6% share count reduction over two years, (c) $329M in fee income creating a more stable revenue layer, and (d) proven catastrophe resilience (absorbed California Wildfires and still generated $56 EPS).

A fair multiple for a 20-25% operating ROE reinsurer with aggressive buybacks and a fee income layer is 8-9x normalized earnings. At normalized earnings of $43-47 and 8.5x P/E, fair value approximates $350-400. At book value growth of 15-20% annually, book value reaches $285-295 by year-end 2026, supporting $350+ at 1.2x P/BV.

## Competitive Landscape

RNR competes against Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, Everest Re, Arch Capital (ACGL), and Berkshire Hathaway's reinsurance operations. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Proprietary catastrophe modeling (RenaissanceRe Risk Sciences).** The company claims its models outperform commercially available tools for climate change-adjusted loss estimation. This enables better risk selection, particularly in tail events.

2. **Capital Partners platform.** Few global reinsurers can match the combination of proprietary modeling, underwriting expertise, and institutional fund management. This creates a capital-light revenue stream that most peers cannot replicate at similar scale.

3. **Financial strength ratings.** A+ from AM Best, S&P, and Fitch; A1 from Moody's — with positive outlooks from S&P and from AM Best on long-term issuer credit ratings (AM Best financial strength rating outlooks are stable) — essential for writing large catastrophe covers.

4. **Post-Validus scale.** The 2023 acquisition created a larger leading global reinsurance platform with diversification across property, casualty, and specialty lines.

**Competitive concerns:** Broker concentration (81.3% through three brokers) creates counterparty dependency. Management acknowledges increased competition and capacity return but states they "prioritize margin over growth," which history suggests is the correct discipline at this cycle stage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Catastrophe Event Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** A single large hurricane or earthquake could produce $1-2B+ in net losses. The California Wildfires cost $786M net in Q1 2025. This risk is structural and well-understood by the market.

**2. Casualty Reserve Deterioration (HIGH — and possibly underappreciated).** 2017-2019 accident year reserves have deteriorated 16-18% from original estimates. Social inflation (nuclear verdicts, litigation funding) is accelerating. Management's sensitivity analysis: a 10% increase in expected loss ratios plus slower reporting would require $2.7B in additional reserves, representing 23.4% of shareholders' equity. This is the primary bear case.

**3. Cycle Softening (MODERATE).** Rates are declining from peak levels. The property catastrophe market remains adequately priced per management, but the casualty market is showing competitive pressure. If combined ratios drift above 95% sustained, the ROE profile degrades significantly.

**4. Bermuda CIT + OECD Risk (MODERATE).** The 15% tax is already embedded in current results. The incremental risk is OECD Article 9.1 guidance limiting a $640M deferred tax asset — this could reduce future tax benefits.

**5. Third-Party Capital Redemptions.** Large cat losses could trigger investor withdrawals from Capital Partners vehicles, reducing fee income and forcing RNR to absorb more risk on its balance sheet.
