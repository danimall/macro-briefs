## Executive Summary

Fabrinet is executing better than at any point in its history and its stock is 39.0% below the $748.89 high set on May 14, 2026. Nine-month FY2026 revenue reached $3,325.3M, up 32.5%, with Q3 accelerating to $1,214.3M (+39.3%); operating margin expanded to 9.9% from 9.0% a year earlier; the balance sheet carries $945.2M of cash and investments against zero interest-bearing debt; and return on invested capital, derived from FY2026E EBIT of roughly $456M against roughly $1.36B of invested capital, runs near 31%. Consensus EPS estimates have moved up: next-year consensus rose 3.7% over 90 days to $17.32. At $456.94 the shares trade at 26.4x that FY2027 number against 25.4% expected earnings growth ($17.32 vs $13.81), a PEG near 1.0, and every published analyst target sits above spot (low $635, mean $732, high $850).

A drawdown of this magnitude on improving fundamentals requires a specific explanation of what sellers are pricing, and there is one. Fabrinet captures a manufacturing-services margin on the AI optical buildout, not an IP margin: gross margin is 11.9% and has eroded roughly 80bp since FY2023, the FY2025 10-K states that process improvements developed on a customer's product are immediately assigned to that customer, and the company holds only its own process know-how plus a small number of PRC optics patents. Underneath the headline growth, the legacy datacom franchise contracted 7.7% like-for-like over nine months and NVIDIA's revenue share fell from 35.1% to 27.6% of a growing base, meaning NVIDIA dollars declined roughly 6.7% in FY2025. Customer concentration worsened materially: four customers each exceeded 10% of nine-month revenue, aggregating 58.1%, versus two customers at 47.5% a year earlier. Free cash flow fell to $41.2M in nine months from $207.3M in all of FY2025 as a 70-day cash cycle absorbed 32-39% growth alongside a capex step-up. And the Chonburi corporate income tax exemption expires in June 2026, at fiscal year-end, which puts a quantifiable effective-tax-rate step-up directly into the FY2027 guidance management gives on August 17.

We keep FN at HOLD with an overall score of 6.1, down from 6.5. The decline sits in competitive position and management quality, not in valuation; valuation is unchanged because the lower price offsets that nothing has broken in the earnings stream. The upgrade path is clear and dated: an August 17 print that reaffirms 30%+ growth into FY2027 while disclosing a manageable tax rate and a reverting inventory build would move this to BUY. Buying two weeks ahead of that print, with near-term implied volatility at 111.7% versus 71.5% realized and a ±19.8% implied move priced into the August 21 expiry, means paying for a coin flip.

## Company Overview

Fabrinet is a Cayman Islands–incorporated contract manufacturer providing advanced optical packaging and precision optical, electro-mechanical and electronic manufacturing services to OEMs of complex products. It builds ROADMs, optical amplifiers, modulators, tunable lasers, transceivers, transponders and active optical cables; industrial lasers for semiconductor processing, biotech and metrology; and automotive and medical sensors. It vertically integrates into customized optics and glass (crystal growth of YVO4, Nd:YVO4, Cr:YAG, BBO, plus precision glass drawing, coating, lapping and polishing) used internally and sold merchant.

The footprint is heavily concentrated: approximately 3.3M of 3.7M total square feet sits in Thailand, along with $475.0M of $529.4M in long-lived assets and 16,220 of 16,457 employees. Secondary sites include Fuzhou (PRC), Mountain Lakes NJ, Fabrinet West (Silicon Valley NPI) and Fabrinet Israel (NPI). Revenue per employee ran approximately $208K in FY2025.

The stated moat is breadth of optical process technology combined with the "factory-within-a-factory" model, which physically segregates customer programs (some customers require on-site anonymity, others biometric access) so that an OEM can outsource without exposing its designs to a manufacturer that competes with it. The 10-K identifies the historical barrier to optical outsourcing as the shortage of third-party partners with both optical process capability and robust IP protection. The moat is trust plus process depth plus a low-cost Thai labor base.

One disclosure discipline point matters for any bull case: the 10-K states that customers provide roughly 12-month forecasts but purchase via individual POs with short lead times subject to revision or cancellation, and that "we do not believe that backlog is a reliable indicator of our future revenues." There is no backlog or RPO metric. Forward visibility here is management commentary and capacity, not contracted obligation.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded from $1,879.4M in FY2021 to $3,419.3M in FY2025 (+18.6% in FY2025), then accelerated: nine-month FY2026 revenue of $3,325.3M is up 32.5%, and Q3 FY2026 of $1,214.3M is up 39.3%. Q4 guidance of $1.25-1.29B implies FY2026 revenue near $4.60B, roughly +34%.

Margin structure, from the filings:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 | Q3 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 12.7% | 12.4% | 12.1% | 12.0% | 11.9% |
| Operating margin | 9.5% | 9.6% | 9.5% | 9.9% | 9.9% |
| Net margin | 9.4% | 10.3% | 9.7% | 10.0% | 10.3% |
| Effective tax rate | 4.7% | 4.9% | 6.4% | 6.1% | 6.7% |

Gross margin has eroded about 80bp since FY2023 while operating margin expanded about 40bp. The bridge is SG&A leverage: Q3 SG&A of $24.3M equals 2.0% of revenue against 2.6% in FY2025 and roughly 2.9% in FY2023. That is genuine scale leverage on a fixed corporate cost base, and it is also a finite lever. Manufacturing economics are not improving; corporate overhead absorption is.

Two contributors to earnings sit below the operating line. FY2025 interest income of $40.2M equaled 11.3% of pretax income. In Q3 FY2026, pretax income of $134.2M exceeded operating income of $120.0M by $14.2M, comprising $7.4M of interest income and a $7.0M FX gain. Excluding the FX gain alone, Q3 GAAP diluted EPS would have been roughly $3.27 rather than $3.45. FX moves both directions and flows straight through: Q2 FY2026 carried a $3.2M FX loss and Q1 FY2025 a $7.1M loss. Interest income is also declining sequentially as cash is absorbed into working capital and capex.

Cash conversion is the material change in FY2026:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $413.1M | $328.4M | $201.8M |
| Net income | $296.2M | $332.5M | $333.8M |
| OCF ÷ net income | 1.39x | 0.99x | 0.60x |
| Cash capex | — | $121.1M | $160.6M |
| FCF | — | $207.3M | $41.2M |

Nine-month operating cash flow fell 26% year over year even as nine-month net income rose $88M. The drivers, from the cash flow statement: inventories −$297.4M, receivables −$150.1M, other assets −$77.5M, partly funded by payables +$225.7M and other payables +$91.2M.

The efficiency ratios argue this is growth absorption rather than discipline breaking down. Using quarterly cost of revenue as the denominator so balances and flows match: inventory days moved from 66.2 at fiscal year-end to 74.5 at Q3; DSO improved from 75.9 to 68.1; DPO was flat at 72.7 to 73.1. Net cash conversion cycle: 69.5 days now versus 69.4 days at year-end. Applying an unchanged ~70-day cycle to a 32-39% growth rate mechanically consumes cash. Raw materials up 65.7% and WIP up 44.1% since year-end is also consistent with management's stated laser, memory and ASIC shortages and with pre-positioning for the Building 10 ramp.

What remains true regardless of the explanation: reported EPS growth is outrunning cash generation, FY2026 FCF will land far below FY2025's $207.3M, and at $456.94 the equity is capitalized at well over 100x FY2026 free cash flow. An inventory build can also reflect demand pull-forward or slower program ramps; the mitigant is contractual, in that customers are generally obligated to purchase excess and obsolete inventory, and the FY2025 sensitivity disclosure indicates a 10% change in the E&O estimate would move net income by only about $0.6M, implying retained exposure is small. Whether Q4 inventory days revert is the check.

Balance sheet at March 27, 2026: cash $356.6M plus short-term investments $588.7M equals $945.2M against zero interest-bearing debt. Total assets $3,509.0M; net PP&E $525.0M (from $380.6M); total liabilities $1,204.3M against equity $2,304.7M for 0.52x total-liabilities-to-equity; current ratio approximately 2.5x. The only facility is an undrawn Bank of Ayudhya line, reduced from $55M to $30M in the December 2024 quarter, maturing 2039. Altman Z of 4.65 on FY2025 data sits well above the 2.99 threshold. Covenant and refinancing risk are not live issues.

Two forward commitments deserve naming: $2.74B of purchase obligations to third-party suppliers and $164.7M of capital expenditure commitments. The purchase obligations are supplier commitments in the normal course, not customer backlog and not debt. Read against cancellable customer POs, they define the core asymmetry of the model: Fabrinet commits to suppliers on a longer horizon than customers commit to Fabrinet.

Financial health scores 7.0. The balance sheet is genuinely strong and there is no refinancing or covenant exposure, but the FCF shortfall, the 0.60x OCF/net-income ratio and the reliance on interest and FX for roughly a tenth of pretax income keep it below the 8s.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of growth changed materially in FY2026, and the change is more informative than the headline. Nine-month FY2026 versus FY2025 by end market: Telecom $1,179.3M (+51.9%), Datacom $811.7M (−7.7%), Datacenter interconnect $477.2M (+73.4%), high-performance computing $207.7M (no prior-year amount), Automotive $354.4M (+5.3%, but Q3 alone −10.8%), Industrial laser $125.3M (+10.5%), Others $169.7M (+31.4%).

The DCI and HPC lines are new disclosure categories, so the obvious question is whether the Datacom decline is an artifact of a carve-out. A reconciliation of implied Q4 FY2025 revenue across the restated categories lands within $0.2M of the actual $909.7M, which indicates DCI was carved out of Telecom rather than Datacom. On that basis the 7.7% Datacom contraction is a genuine like-for-like decline, HPC appears to be new revenue, and Telecom's +51.9% is measured on a properly restated base. This reconciliation relies partly on nine-month non-optical splits derived from disclosed growth rates rather than read directly, so confidence is moderate rather than certain.

Combined with NVIDIA falling from 35.1% to 27.6% of revenue and Israel bill-to revenue falling 10.9% over nine months, the picture is that the legacy AI-datacom franchise is flat to shrinking and all FY2026 growth is coming from telecom systems, datacenter interconnect, brand-new HPC, and direct hyperscaler programs. Management offered a partially benign counter on the Q3 call, saying datacom would have set a record "by a wide margin" but for laser, memory and ASIC shortages, i.e. supply constraint rather than share loss. Both can be partially true.

The forward capacity story is concrete. Building 10 in Chonburi is approximately 2.0M square feet at roughly $132.5M of shell cost, adding what management describes as roughly $3.0B of revenue capacity. That claim cross-checks: current 2.9M square feet of manufacturing space supports about $4.6B annualized, or roughly $1,586 per square foot, and 2.0M new square feet at that density implies about $3.17B. Management's "six months of operating profit pays for the entire 2 million square feet" framing is conservative on the shell but excludes equipment and tooling, which is the larger and recurring spend. The disclosure tension: the FY2025 10-K said FY2026 capex would decrease from FY2025's $121.1M, and nine-month FY2026 cash capex was already $160.6M with $164.7M of commitments outstanding. The most probable reading is a mid-year upward revision as demand and program awards firmed.

The other forward item is the direct-to-hyperscaler channel. Management stated on the Q3 call that two 800G datacom transceiver programs are shipping directly to a hyperscale customer with initial ramps in Q4. Separately, the March 2025 Amazon warrant (up to 381,922 shares at $208.48, vesting on payments made by or on behalf of Amazon under a commercial agreement) has vested from 38,192 shares at fiscal year-end to 70,662 at Q3, an additional 8.5% of the total in nine months. Amazon is the customer and Fabrinet the supplier here; the equity was issued as inducement and is recognized as a reduction of revenue ($2.0M in Q3, $3.2M in nine months), which mechanically suppresses reported gross margin. The inference that Amazon is the unnamed hyperscaler is plausible but unproven, and the vesting thresholds are undisclosed so the 8.5% figure cannot be converted into a revenue estimate.

Estimate revisions are supportive but modest, not explosive: current quarter +0.8% over 90 days, current year +1.4% to $13.81, next year +3.7% to $17.32. This is not the +30% ninety-day revision velocity that marks a violent acceleration; it is a steady grind upward from nine analysts. The earnings track record is four quarters with three beats, but the beats are small (+1.5%, +0.5%, +0.5%) after one 2.4% miss, which describes tight guidance management rather than systematic conservatism.

Growth prospects score 7.0. Revenue growth is high and capacity is being added at high returns, but the durable, disclosed portion of the franchise is contracting while the growth sits in new, unnamed, uncontracted programs with no backlog metric to verify them.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $456.94 basis, market capitalization is $16.37B; net of cash and investments, enterprise value is approximately $15.43B.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E revenue (~$4.60B) | 3.35x |
| P/E on FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($13.81 consensus) | 33.1x |
| P/E on FY2026E GAAP EPS (~$12.75 derived) | 35.8x |
| P/E on FY2027E consensus EPS ($17.32) | 26.4x |
| PEG (FY2027 growth of 25.4%) | 1.04 |
| EV / FY2026E EBIT (~$456M) | 33.8x |
| P / FY2025 FCF ($207.3M) | 79x (FY2026 will be materially worse) |
| ROIC (derived, at 6.5% ETR) | ~31% |

Peer comparables in electronic manufacturing services show a median P/E of 94.6 (SANM 52.3, TTMI 101.8, PLXS 42.1, IPGP 163.6, BHE 94.6). Those multiples are inflated by depressed or trough earnings across the group and are not a usable anchor; the more relevant comparison is that Fabrinet is growing revenue nearly twice as fast as Coherent (39.3% versus 20.5%) while trading at a fraction of the multiple the design-owning layer commands, and Fabrinet's YTD 2026 stock performance has trailed Coherent by roughly 71 percentage points.

Two things pull in opposite directions. In favor: 26.4x forward earnings with a PEG near 1.0 and 31% ROIC on a debt-free balance sheet is not a stretched multiple, and every published target (low $635, mean $732, median $750, high $850) sits 39% to 86% above spot. Where a stock with rising estimates trades that far below the lowest published target, the most likely explanations are either genuine mispricing or stale sell-side models that have not been recalibrated since the May peak. Against: the 31% ROIC depends heavily on a 6.5% effective tax rate (at 20% it would be roughly 27%), the FCF-based multiple is poor and worsening, and gross margin of 11.9% leaves no cushion for baht appreciation or mix.

The tax rate is the quantifiable swing factor and it is dated. On derived FY2026E pretax income of roughly $483M and 36.3M diluted shares: at the current ~6.5% ETR, EPS lands near $12.4-12.8 GAAP; at 15% it is roughly $11.31 (−9%); at a full 20% Thai statutory rate it is roughly $10.65 (−14%). A jump straight to 20% is not the base case: Building 9 remains exempt through 2031, Building 10 is under construction, and Fabrinet has a long record of securing BOI promotions on new investment. But the realized ETR has already stair-stepped from 4.7% in FY2023 to 6.7% in Q3 FY2026, and any FY2027 model carrying a flat 6% rate is too optimistic. That risk is the most plausible reason FY2027 consensus of $17.32 could prove high.

Valuation scores 6.2, unchanged. The lower price has improved absolute risk/reward, and I am not treating cheapness on its own as a buy signal; the specific reason to think the market may be wrong is that 26.4x forward earnings with 25% growth, 31% ROIC and no debt is a multiple appropriate to a decelerating business, and Fabrinet is accelerating. The specific reason to withhold conviction is that the earnings on which that multiple is computed carry an undisclosed ETR assumption and are not yet converting to cash.

## Competitive Landscape

Fabrinet occupies the packaging and assembly layer of the optical chain. Named competitors split into Western/global EMS providers (Benchmark, Celestica, Jabil, Sanmina, Venture) and vertically integrated Chinese transceiver manufacturers: InnoLight Technology (Suzhou) in the FY2025 10-K, with **Eoptolink Technology newly added in the Q3 FY2026 10-Q**. In customized optics it competes with CASTECH, Excelitas and Photop, the latter a Coherent subsidiary, meaning a customer's affiliate competes with Fabrinet's merchant optics business. The 10-K also names customers' internal manufacturing capability as a competitor, which is the honest framing of insourcing risk.

The Eoptolink addition matters because InnoLight and Eoptolink sell finished 800G and 1.6T modules directly to hyperscalers, the exact lane Fabrinet is entering with its two direct 800G programs. Moving from OEM subcontractor to direct hyperscaler supplier raises revenue potential and shortens the value chain, and it places Fabrinet against competitors who own their optics IP and have their own low-cost base. The filing does not say whether the addition reflects a new competitive encounter or merely updated market-share data.

The structural constraint on the moat is value capture. Gross margin is 11.9%; process improvements developed on a customer's product are assigned to that customer; the company has no meaningful product patent estate. The durable part of the thesis is the industry backdrop the 10-K describes (OEMs shedding internal capacity to concentrate on R&D and go-to-market) plus the trust and segregation model that is hard to replicate quickly. Industrial laser and sensor OEMs remain underpenetrated for outsourcing, which is real optionality. Automotive is currently a drag, down 10.8% in Q3.

Competitive position scores 5.8, down from prior reports. The direction is mixed rather than clearly improving: share is being gained in telecom systems, DCI and direct hyperscaler work, while the largest customer's dollars declined, legacy datacom contracted like-for-like, gross margin eroded 80bp over three years, and a new vertically integrated competitor entered the disclosed list in the lane Fabrinet is expanding into.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, worsening on both measures.** The number of top-tier accounts doubled (broadening) and the aggregate share held by them rose 10.6 percentage points (worsening). End-market mix is diversifying while customer concentration deteriorates; the first does not offset the second. Three customers each exceeded 10% of trade receivables at both balance-sheet dates. The two new ≥10% customers are unnamed, since names are disclosed only annually.

**Tax holiday cliff, June 2026.** The Chonburi exemption expires at FY2026 year-end; Pinehurst's 50% exemption already expired June 2025; Building 9 runs to 2031 but is capped at the actual investment amount. Thai statutory is 20%. OECD Pillar Two, with additional side-by-side guidance issued January 5, 2026, is flagged as a potential further increase. This is the clearest dated, quantifiable risk to FY2027 EPS, and it will be addressed, or conspicuously not addressed, in the August 17 guidance.

**Thailand single-country concentration.** Baht appreciation is a structural gross-margin headwind: THB forward notional rose to $253.0M from $165.0M, derivative liabilities are $10.8M, and AOCI now expects roughly $8.9M of losses to reclassify into earnings within twelve months, reversing an expected $1.5M gain a year earlier. The USD depreciated approximately 9.7% against the baht over the two years to March 2026.

**No firm order book against $2.74B of supplier obligations.** No long-term supply agreements, cancellable POs, backlog explicitly disclaimed. The contractual mitigant on excess inventory is meaningful but does not cover volume risk.

**Sole-source suppliers and active component shortages.** Laser, memory and ASIC shortages are currently suppressing datacom shipments per management. Qualifying alternate suppliers has historically required customer requalification and delayed revenue.

**Newly added filing risks.** A "U.S.-Iran war" was added to the list of armed conflicts that could disrupt operations, relevant given Fabrinet Israel and material Israel-billed revenue. Customer consolidation (Nokia/Infinera, Lumentum/NeoPhotonics, Coherent/II-VI, Cisco/Acacia) is flagged as a volume and pricing risk, and customers' end customers include Huawei and ZTE, creating indirect export-control exposure. The February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling creates unquantified tariff-refund optionality that has not been recorded as a receivable; if tariffs were passed through to customers, any refund would largely be owed onward. Do not size it.

**Governance.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed October 16 and 17, 2025 whose contents could not be retrieved; circumstantial evidence (a first equity grant to Caroline Dowling on October 13, 2025) points to a board addition rather than an executive departure, and Grady and Sverha were still filing as CEO and CFO thereafter. This remains a verification gap. The March 2026 agreement to acquire 16.0% of Raytek Semiconductor for NT$1.02B (~$32.4M) is financially immaterial at 0.2% of market cap, was not described in an 8-K, and the target's business could not be verified.

**Management alignment.** Between August 2025 and May 2026 the CEO reduced holdings 60%, the CFO 73%, the President/COO 50% and the EVP of Sales 50%, with four directors also selling and zero open-market purchases in the 25 most recent transactions. The most recent director sale was at $711.91 on May 22, 2026. The clustering immediately after the August 18-19, 2025 earnings and 10-K is textbook post-window plan execution, holdings arose largely from equity awards, and TSR compounded from $100 to $498 over five fiscal years against a peer index at $119; diversification is a sufficient explanation and I do not infer a negative view of intrinsic value. What the record establishes is that the entire senior operating team cut economic exposure by half or more within four months. Alongside that, the disclosed incentive measures are revenue, non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP operating margin and non-GAAP EPS, with no return-on-capital, free-cash-flow or cash-conversion metric; in a year when capex ran 33% above the prior full year in nine months and FCF fell from $207.3M to $41.2M, that is a real if second-order misalignment. The buyback was effectively paused ($5.2M in nine months versus $103.5M a year earlier, with $169.0M authorized), which is fully explained by capital-allocation arithmetic rather than signalling.

Risk factors score 4.5. The dated tax cliff, worsening concentration, a binary print two weeks out and single-country operational concentration are material and only partly priced. The score is marginally above the prior report because a 39% drawdown has absorbed more of this than it had, and because there is no leverage, covenant or refinancing channel through which any of it becomes existential.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $680 (+49%)

Revenue compounds from a $4.60B FY2026 base toward $6.0B+ in FY2027 as Building 10 phases in, DCI grows off a +73.4% nine-month base, HPC scales from $207.7M, and the two direct 800G hyperscaler programs ramp beyond initial volumes, moving Fabrinet from OEM subcontractor toward direct supplier with structurally larger program sizes. Component shortages ease and datacom returns to growth, revealing the FY2026 decline as supply-constrained rather than share loss. SG&A leverage holds near 2.0% of revenue and operating margin drifts above 10%. New BOI promotions on Building 10 hold the effective tax rate in the high single digits, protecting FY2027 consensus of $17.32 and supporting FY2028 EPS above $21. Inventory days revert in Q4 and free cash flow re-converges toward net income as growth moderates from 39% toward the mid-20s. On $21-22 of FY2028 earnings at 31x, a multiple the market awarded this business repeatedly in FY2026, the shares reach $650-680, roughly the level implied by the lowest published sell-side target.

### Bear Case — $315 (−31%)

FY2027 guidance on August 17 embeds an effective tax rate stepping toward 15%, cutting EPS roughly 9% against consensus and forcing sell-side models built on a 6% rate to reset. Datacom's like-for-like contraction proves competitive rather than supply-driven as InnoLight and Eoptolink win the direct-to-hyperscaler share Fabrinet is chasing, and one of the four ≥10% customers reduces volumes with the short-lead-time, cancellable PO structure providing no protection. Baht appreciation pushes gross margin below 11.5% and the $8.9M of AOCI losses scheduled to reclassify within twelve months lands in cost of revenue. Inventory days do not revert; FY2027 capex on Building 10 equipment keeps free cash flow near or below $100M, and the market applies a cash-based rather than earnings-based multiple to an 11.9%-gross-margin assembler with no product IP. On $15-16 of FY2027 EPS at 20-21x, the shares trade near $315, still 16% above the $272.49 fifty-two-week low.

The bear case does not require the sector thesis to fail. AI optical demand can be entirely real while an assembler earning 11.9% gross margin, assigning process IP to its customers and holding no contracted backlog captures too little of it to sustain a 30x+ multiple. That is the most coherent explanation for a 39% drawdown occurring alongside a 39% revenue growth quarter, and it is why the burden of proof sits with the bulls here rather than with the sellers.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Base case $520, roughly 30x FY2027 consensus EPS of $17.32 or 25x an FY2028 estimate near $20.80 — **+13.8% from the $456.94 basis**. Bull case $680 (+48.8%). Bear case $315 (−31.0%). Timeframe: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint on **August 17, 2026** (FY2026 Q4 and initial FY2027 guidance).

This base case sits below the lowest published analyst target of $635. I regard the sell-side range ($635-850, mean $732) as materially stale (set when the shares traded $600-750) and as embedding a flat single-digit effective tax rate through FY2027. If the August 17 print resolves the tax question favorably and reaffirms 30%-plus growth, those targets become achievable rather than aspirational and the base case moves toward $600.

**Upgrade to BUY on any two of the following:**
1. FY2027 guidance implying revenue growth of 25%+ **and** a disclosed or derivable effective tax rate of 10% or below, confirming new BOI promotions offset the Chonburi expiry.
2. Q4 datacom returning to year-over-year growth, confirming the 7.7% nine-month decline was supply-constrained rather than share loss, with management quantifying the shortage impact.
3. Q4 inventory days reverting toward 66-68 (from 74.5) with operating cash flow exceeding net income for the quarter, evidencing the working-capital build was a growth artifact.
4. A price near $380-400 on unchanged FY2027 estimates, which would put the shares below 23x forward earnings with 25% growth.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. FY2027 guidance implying an effective tax rate at or above 15% without an offsetting revenue or margin surprise — roughly a 9% EPS reset against consensus.
2. Loss or material reduction of any ≥10% customer, or aggregate ≥10%-customer share rising above 62% of revenue.
3. Gross margin falling below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters, indicating baht and mix pressure is outrunning SG&A leverage.
