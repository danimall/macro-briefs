## Executive Summary

Dollar General has recovered roughly half of the profitability it lost between FY2022 and FY2024, and the mechanism is narrower than the headline recovery suggests. Operating margin fell 364 basis points from 8.80% (FY2022) to 4.22% (FY2024) before rebounding to 5.16% in FY2025 and 5.92% in Q1 FY2026. Decomposing the peak-to-FY2025 decline: gross margin accounts for 57bp, SG&A for 306bp. All of the FY2025 recovery came from gross margin (+107bp on lower shrink, higher markups, lower damages), while SG&A worsened another 13bp. Q1 FY2026 repeated the pattern: gross margin +65bp, SG&A +25bp worse. Shrink in cost of goods fell to 1.42% of sales in Q1 from 1.69%, which is genuine, disclosed and probably has further to run, but it is a finite pool. The structural gap versus prior earnings power is a cost-structure gap that management's own disclosures show widening.

The growth algorithm has also changed. Net new stores fell from +882 (FY2023) to +299 (FY2025), a 1.45% unit growth rate, with closures tripling to 290 and new-store capex down 48% to $203.5M. Capital moved into remodels — 4,301 completed in FY2025 versus 1,706 in FY2024, with ~4,270 planned for FY2026 — and total capex is guided up to $1.4–1.5B from $1,241M. Management's stated reason is that construction, occupancy and interest costs have degraded new-store returns. That is a rational reallocation, but it converts DG from a square-footage compounder into a same-store productivity story whose payback is undisclosed: sales per square foot moved only $263 → $271 over five quarters, and proxy-disclosed Adjusted EBIT of $2,486.7M remains 31% below the FY2022 peak of $3,590.5M.

At 16.6x FY2026 consensus EPS of $7.38 and 15.3x FY2027's $8.03, DG carries a 25% discount to the 22.2x peer median and a 21% discount to Dollar Tree at 21.0x. Most of that discount is earned. We hold: the base case of $132 offers +7.6% plus a 1.9% dividend against a $95 bear case (-22.6%), and the largest swing factors — SNAP contribution to revenue, the range of loss in the securities litigation, and remodel-cohort economics — are not disclosed anywhere in the filings. Existing holders are paid to wait for the late-August Q2 print, which the options market prices at a ±9.5% move. New capital has no urgent entry signal at this price.

## Company Overview

Dollar General operated 21,055 stores in 48 states and Mexico as of May 1, 2026, up from 20,893 at fiscal year-end. Stores average roughly 7,500 square feet of selling space with deliberately limited SKUs per category and price points typically $10 or less. The moat is geographic rather than price-based: approximately 80% of stores sit in towns of 20,000 or fewer people, and roughly 75% of the U.S. population lives within five miles of a store. Management identifies the target customer as "low and fixed income households often underserved by other retailers (including grocers)," and claims a price advantage over most food and drug retailers while conceding prices are merely "competitive with even the largest discount retailers."

FY2025 revenue mix: consumables 82.0%, seasonal 10.1%, home 5.2%, apparel 2.7%. Management states plainly that consumables carry the lowest gross margin. One reporting segment, with net income as the segment profit measure. Named competitors are Walmart, Family Dollar and Dollar Tree.

Two adjacent concepts remain subordinate. pOpshelf held flat at 180 standalone stores through Q1 FY2026, with new expansion paused beginning in 2025 following $214.2M of impairment charges (majority pOpshelf-related) from the Q4 FY2024 portfolio review. Mexico added 8 stores in FY2025 and 5 in Q1 FY2026, with operations described as immaterial.

Supplier concentration, not customer concentration, is the relevant exposure: the two largest suppliers represented approximately 11% and 8% of purchases in FY2025, with direct imports at roughly 4% of purchases at cost. Tariff exposure therefore arrives mostly indirectly through domestic vendors who import.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin trajectory (FY percentages calculated from reported revenue, gross profit, SG&A and operating income):**

| Fiscal year ended | Net sales | Gross margin | SG&A % | Operating margin | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feb 3, 2023 | $37,844.9M | 31.23% | 22.44% | 8.80% | — |
| Feb 2, 2024 | $38,691.6M | 30.29% | 23.97% | 6.32% | $7.55 |
| Jan 31, 2025 | $40,612.3M | 29.59% | 25.37% | 4.22% | $5.11 |
| Jan 30, 2026 | $42,724.4M | 30.66% | 25.50% | 5.16% | $6.85 |
| Q1 FY2026 (May 1, 2026) | $10,787.0M | 31.62% | 25.70% | 5.92% | $2.00 |

The named FY2025 SG&A drivers were incentive compensation and repairs/maintenance. The Q1 FY2026 drivers were depreciation and amortization, utilities, and property taxes — two of the three are not cyclical. D&A has risen from 1.92% of sales (FY2022) to 2.45% (FY2025) to 2.51% (Q1 FY2026), and the remodel program is the proximate cause. Management frames Project Elevate as "potentially mitigating future repairs and maintenance expense," which converts operating expense into capex and subsequent depreciation. The P&L effect of that swap is ambiguous; the near-term cash effect is negative given capex guided up 13–21%.

**Sales quality.** FY2025 same-store sales were +3.0% (traffic +1.6%, ticket +1.4%, items per transaction flat) versus +1.4% in FY2024. Q1 FY2026 decelerated to +2.0% (traffic +1.4%, ticket +0.5%, items per transaction *declining*). Traffic-led growth is the higher-quality form and is consistent with trade-down. But basket depth has gone from rising to flat to falling across three reporting periods, with ticket growth now coming almost entirely from higher average retail prices. An alternative reading is that delivery and smaller fill-in trips mechanically reduce items per basket while adding trips; the filings do not disaggregate enough to distinguish these.

On mix, precision matters. Management notes non-consumables have outgrown consumables in same-store sales for five consecutive quarters. That is a growth-rate statement. Actual consumables mix moved from 82.76% to 82.44% year-over-year in Q1 (32bp) and from 82.17% to 82.04% on a full-year basis (13bp). Directionally favorable for margin, not mix transformation.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,634.5M against $1,241.2M of capex for $2,393.3M of free cash flow — an 8.8% trailing yield on the current market capitalization. That flatters the run rate. The MD&A itemizes roughly $813M of working-capital and timing support inside the $638.4M year-over-year OCF increase: income taxes +$199.2M, accrued expenses +$250.0M (incentive compensation accrual), payables +$185.3M, inventories +$178.5M. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act separately reduced 2025 cash taxes significantly with no effective-rate impact. Two indications the inventory tailwind is fading: per-store inventory fell 7.0% year-over-year at January 30, 2026 but only 1.6% at May 1, 2026, and Q1 total inventories rose 5%.

As a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast: if half of the identified timing items ($400M) does not recur and capex runs at the guided $1.45B midpoint, FCF is approximately $1,784M, a 6.6% yield. Holding OCF flat and only stepping capex up gives $2,184M, an 8.1% yield. A 6.5–8.0% normalized range is defensible. Q1 FY2026 cash flow, which is not annualizable given fourth-quarter seasonal concentration, was OCF of $716.2M (down $131.0M despite net income rising $52.2M) as inventories consumed $308.1M versus releasing $124.8M a year earlier; Q1 FCF was $364.6M versus $556.3M.

**Balance sheet and credit.** At May 1, 2026: total assets $31,699.7M, liabilities $22,856.4M, equity $8,843.3M, cash $1,353.1M, current ratio 1.17. Funded obligations of $4,576.4M against FY2025 EBITDA of roughly $3,250M (operating profit $2,203.7M plus D&A $1,046.3M) is 1.41x. Operating lease liabilities are $11,222.0M ($13,671.3M undiscounted, roughly $2.0B due within a year), which puts lease-adjusted leverage near 3.0x EBITDAR. EV/EBITDA is 9.3x ex-leases and 7.9x EV/EBITDAR lease-adjusted.

Note a definitional trap: screens reporting debt-to-equity of 2.64 are computing total liabilities over equity, which includes capitalized operating leases, payables and deferred taxes. Funded-debt-to-equity is 0.52x. Both measure something real; conflating them misstates the risk.

Deleveraging is documented. FY2025 repayments totaled $1.7B across three redemptions ($500M of 4.15% notes, $600M of 3.875% notes, $550M of 4.625% notes). Net interest expense fell from $326.8M (FY2023) to $230.6M (FY2025) and to $47.2M in Q1 from $64.6M. Remaining senior notes total $4.5B with no maturity before May 2028; long-term debt due within one year is $14.4M. Liquidity comprises $1.35B cash and an undrawn $2.375B revolver maturing September 2029 (management cites $2.18B of incremental usable liquidity given commercial-paper backstop intent).

The most important credit disclosure is historical but instructive. Moody's downgraded DG to Baa3 in 2025 — one notch above sub-investment grade — while moving to a Stable outlook; S&P holds BBB/Stable; commercial paper is P-3/A-2, the lowest investment-grade CP tier, which narrows the buyer base. On March 11, 2025 DG amended its revolver to raise the maximum leverage ratio and lower the minimum fixed-charge coverage ratio through January 30, 2026, and was restricted from repurchasing stock during that Covenant Relief Period. DG was compliant at both January 30 and May 1, 2026, and the relief period has lapsed. Proactive covenant amendment is a common and inexpensive precaution for an investment-grade issuer entering an uncertain year, so this does not establish that a breach was imminent. What it does establish is that the buyback restriction has ended while $1.38B of authorization sits unused since 2022.

Reconstructing the full Piotroski F-Score from two years of filings yields 7 of 9 (misses: current ratio 1.135 vs 1.189, and share count rising to 220.09M from 219.88M basic because equity compensation vests while buybacks remain suspended). Altman Z, recomputed with market rather than book equity in the 0.6× term, is roughly 2.6–2.8 rather than the 2.26 commonly screened — genuinely in the grey zone, consistent with Baa3/BBB, and well above the 1.81 distress threshold. Operating lease assets are 35.8% of the asset base, which mechanically depresses every ratio in the specification for any lease-heavy retailer.

## Growth Analysis

The unit growth engine has stalled by design:

| Fiscal year | Opened | Closed | Net | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | 987 | 105 | +882 | 19,986 |
| FY2024 | 725 | 117 | +608 | 20,594 |
| FY2025 | 589 | 290 | +299 | 20,893 |
| FY2026 plan | ~450 U.S. + ~10 Mexico | — | — | — |

Net unit growth of 1.45% cannot support the mid-to-high single-digit revenue growth investors historically underwrote. FY2025 revenue grew 5.2% and Q1 FY2026 grew 3.4%, increasingly dependent on comps that decelerated to +2.0%.

Consensus captures this: FY2026 EPS of $7.38 (+7.7% over FY2025's $6.85) and FY2027 at $8.03 (+8.8%). Both are margin-recovery-driven rather than volume-driven. Estimate revisions are stable — +1.5% over 90 days for the current year, +1.0% for next year, +0.2–0.5% over 30 days. This is not an estimate-revision inflection; there is no +30%/90-day signal here and no basis for treating consensus targets as stale floors.

The earnings surprise record shows four consecutive beats, but with decaying magnitude: +15.2%, +31.4%, +13.7%, +3.0%. The most recent quarter beat by roughly six cents on a $1.94 estimate ($2.00 actual), and the stock fell 3.3% on the June 2 print despite it. That reaction, combined with the March 12 gap down (roughly -6.1% on the FY2025 full-year release), indicates the market is discounting guidance and cost commentary rather than reported EPS.

Two identifiable EPS headwinds are already in motion. The Work Opportunity Tax Credit expired for employees hired after December 31, 2025, and DG's 10-K warns of "a significant negative impact on our future earnings per share" absent reauthorization. Q1's effective rate rose 150bp to 24.9%, attributed primarily to expired federal credits, and the drag builds through the year as hiring cycles through the new rule. If the effective rate normalizes from FY2025's 23.0% toward roughly 26% on approximately $2.0B of pretax income, the annual cost is around $60M, or $0.27 per share — roughly 4% of FY2025 EPS. That estimate is sensitive to the normalized-rate assumption and reverses entirely on Congressional reauthorization, which has happened repeatedly in the past. Separately, DG disclosed "significantly higher fuel costs in the first quarter of 2026" and expects the trend "to continue for an uncertain duration." DG's private fleet and freight costs face a live headwind that also compresses the customer's discretionary budget, which disproportionately hits the higher-margin non-consumable categories the remodel program is designed to grow.

A cheap multiple attached to a business whose unit growth has collapsed and whose SG&A ratio has risen every year is the classic value-trap configuration, and we score valuation accordingly rather than rewarding the low multiple on its own.

What partially offsets that: the normalized FCF yield of 6.5–8.0% is real and comfortably funds the dividend at 21.7% of reported FY2025 FCF. And there is one specific, management-signposted catalyst. Management states it withheld repurchases "to preserve our investment grade credit rating and maintain financial flexibility" and "anticipate[s] resuming share repurchases at the appropriate time." An authorization is not a commitment, has no expiration, and has now gone unused for four years; we do not capitalize it in the base case.

## Competitive Landscape

The proximity moat is intact at the level and flat-to-eroding at the margin.

**Zoning is now a structural constraint.** Item 1A specifically cites "moratoria on small box discount retail development such as those passed by certain local governments in areas where we operate or seek to operate." Combined with elevated construction and financing costs, this explains the decline from 987 openings (FY2023) to a planned ~450 (FY2026). This is not cyclical.

**The e-commerce gap is acknowledged rather than closed.** DG states it "currently do[es] not offer traditional online shopping to a significant degree and ha[s] seen a greater willingness of our customers to adopt online shopping generally." The response is delivery of store inventory via third parties plus myDG Delivery in "the majority" of stores, which management says is becoming a meaningful comp contributor, and the DG Media Network, a retail-media platform in which brand partners — simultaneously DG's suppliers — pay for audience access. That is a defensible answer for a rural, low-ticket, high-frequency basket, and it does not close the assortment gap versus Walmart or Amazon for stock-up trips. DG also concedes competitors "have greater financial, distribution, marketing and other resources… and may be able to secure better arrangements with suppliers."

**The customer cuts both ways.** Filings state customers "continue to feel constrained" by rent, healthcare, energy and food-at-home costs, and that DG's core customers "are often among the first to be affected by negative or uncertain economic conditions and among the last to feel the effects of improving" ones. Management flags that food-at-home inflation "moderately accelerated in 2025." A trade-down tailwind to traffic and a pressure on basket depth can operate at the same time. Traffic +1.4% and items per transaction declining in Q1 is exactly what that combination produces. DG does not disclose income-cohort data, so the net effect cannot be verified.

## Risk Assessment

**Securities litigation ruling is a live, near-dated binary.** *Washtenaw County Employees' Retirement System v. Dollar General* (M.D. Tenn.) alleges misrepresentation of the impact of store labor, inventory and pricing practices over a class period of May 28, 2020 to August 28, 2024. Dismissal was granted without prejudice on June 23, 2025; plaintiffs won leave to amend on March 24, 2026; a third consolidated amended complaint was filed; defendants moved to dismiss on April 21, 2026; briefing completed May 29, 2026. Three sets of derivative actions are stayed pending that ruling. DG states it cannot estimate a range of loss and that an adverse outcome "could have a material adverse effect on the Company's consolidated financial statements as a whole." The ruling is a catalyst in either direction with no disclosed magnitude.

**ERP conversion overlaps the margin-recovery mechanism.** The 10-Q discloses a phased ERP replacement of financial and HR systems across 2026–2027, with the consolidated financial reporting platform expected to go live in Q2 FY2026 — the quarter now closing — followed by HR/payroll, procurement and merchandise payables. Item 1A separately flags modernization of the legacy inventory replenishment system and a new point-of-sale system. Replacing reporting, replenishment and POS at 21,000 stores concurrently is meaningful execution risk for a company whose entire earnings recovery rests on inventory and shrink discipline.

**SNAP exposure is newly specific and entirely unquantified.** FY2025 risk factors escalate from generic language to naming "changes to the work requirement minimum standards for qualification enacted in 2025, and beginning in January 2026, certain states' exclusion of historically covered product categories." With 82% of sales in consumables and a stated core customer of low and fixed-income households, this is plausibly material. DG does not disclose SNAP as a percentage of sales anywhere in its filings. We flag this as a genuine gap rather than assuming it is small.

**WOTC expiration** as quantified above: approximately $60M / $0.27 per share annually, already visible in Q1's 150bp effective-rate increase.

**Credit constraint.** Baa3 is one notch above sub-investment grade; P-3 commercial paper narrows the funding base. Neither is a solvency question given $14.4M of long-term debt due within a year against $1.35B cash and $2.18B of usable revolver, but the rating is a binding constraint on buyback willingness.

**Tariffs.** DG states that on February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs and that DG has submitted refund claims to CBP with uncertain timing and amount. We report this as DG characterized it. With roughly 4% direct imports, the direct refund is likely modest against $42.7B of revenue; the larger exposures are indirect pass-through from importing domestic vendors and the 10-K's warning about "potential… replacement tariffs under other statutes."

**Supply chain finance** is disclosed at $406.1M confirmed obligations at May 1, 2026 (up from $385.2M) inside $4,341.3M of payables, roughly 9%. Modest, disclosed, growing slowly, and a source of working-capital support that would reverse if curtailed. Nothing in the disclosure suggests distress.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 46.2% sits 9.8 points above one-year realized volatility of 36.4% — rich. The term structure is flat overall (46.2% near versus 45.1% far), but the one-month tenor expiring August 28 prints 58.9% IV with a 9.5% implied move, which locates the event risk squarely on the late-August Q2 FY2026 report. Put/call skew is call-heavy at -8.6%, and open-interest put/call fell from 1.03 to 0.87 since July 22 while the price rose 2.1%. Positioning is not bearish; the market is pricing a large two-sided earnings reaction rather than directional downside. Short interest at 3.9% of float with 3.1 days to cover shows no crowded bearish view either. The practical implication is timing: initiating ahead of a print carrying a ±9.5% implied move with volatility rich to realized is paying for uncertainty rather than being compensated for it. Existing holders bear that move; new capital can wait for the vol crush and the guidance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $160 (+30.4%)

Shrink normalization has further to run — 1.42% of sales in Q1 versus 1.69% a year earlier, still above pre-disruption levels — and gross margin recovery continues at 40–60bp per year. The 4,300-per-year remodel cadence produces measurable comp lift, non-consumables (five consecutive quarters of relative outperformance) build enough mix to matter, and sales leverage begins compressing the SG&A ratio for the first time since FY2022. With the covenant relief period lapsed and $1.65B of notes retired, management executes the $1.38B authorization, retiring 5.1% of shares and adding roughly 5% to per-share earnings. WOTC is reauthorized, reversing $0.27 of drag. Stagflationary pressure on the low-income consumer drives continued trade-down traffic and higher-income penetration. FY2027 EPS reaches $8.75 and the multiple closes the gap to Dollar Tree at 18–20x.

### Bear Case → $95 (-22.6%)

Gross-margin recovery decelerates as the shrink pool is exhausted while SG&A inflation continues: D&A is now 2.51% of sales and rising by construction of the remodel program, utilities and property taxes are structural, and fuel costs are explicitly guided to continue. SNAP work requirements and January 2026 state product-category exclusions reduce trips or basket size on an 82%-consumables base by an amount nobody can model because DG does not disclose it. Items per transaction continues declining, and ticket growth built on price rather than units proves fragile. The Q2 ERP financial-reporting go-live disrupts the inventory and replenishment discipline the margin recovery depends on. An adverse litigation ruling revives the derivative actions. The buyback stays parked to protect a Baa3 rating. FY2027 EPS lands near $6.90 and the multiple compresses to 13–14x on a business with 1.45% unit growth and no cost leverage.

### What Would Resolve It

The undisclosed items decide the outcome: SNAP's contribution to revenue, remodel-cohort sales lift, and the litigation loss range. None is available in the filings. The verifiable evidence — four consecutive beats with decaying magnitude, stable but unexciting estimate revisions, a documented gross-margin recovery, a documented SG&A deterioration, and a documented unit-growth collapse — supports a fair-value conclusion rather than a directional one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (fifth consecutive; overall score 5.35, marginally below the prior 5.5). The score decline is located in risk factors, not in price. Fundamentals are broadly as described in prior work; the differences this cycle are the imminent ERP financial-reporting go-live, the fully briefed litigation motion now awaiting ruling, and the newly specific SNAP language. Estimates have risen slightly over 90 days while the stock sits 22.5% below its February high of $158.23; that is improving risk/reward at the margin, and it is not enough to change the rating because the March 12 and June 2 selloffs were guidance-and-cost reactions rather than indiscriminate de-rating.

**Reference values from the $122.685 basis:** base $132 (+7.6%, ~9.5% with the dividend), bull $160 (+30.4%), bear $95 (-22.6%). Probability-weighted expected total return approximately 7.6% over twelve months.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **A share repurchase actually executed** — disclosure in a 10-Q or 8-K of repurchases against the $1.38B authorization. Even $500M deployed would signal the Baa3 constraint has loosened and would validate the capital-return leg of the bull case.
2. **SG&A ratio flat or improving year-over-year for two consecutive quarters** while gross margin holds. This is the single measurable condition that would convert the remodel program from a D&A drag into an earnings engine. Q2 and Q3 FY2026 are the tests.
3. **Price below $105** with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $8.00, which would put the stock under 13.1x forward earnings and take the normalized FCF yield above 8%.
4. Dismissal of the third amended securities complaint with prejudice, removing the litigation binary and the stayed derivative actions with it.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Same-store sales below +1.0%** or a further decline in items per transaction alongside decelerating traffic — the signal that trade-down traffic is no longer offsetting basket compression.
2. **Gross margin flat or down year-over-year** in any quarter, which would exhaust the only leg of the current recovery while SG&A continues rising.
3. **A material weakness or restatement associated with the ERP conversion**, or any disclosure of inventory/replenishment disruption at the store level.
4. FY2026 EPS guidance cut, or FY2027 consensus revised below $7.25 (a >9% cut from the current $8.03).
5. Denial of the motion to dismiss with a quantified reserve established.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2026 report in late August 2026 is the next decision point — it covers the ERP financial-reporting go-live quarter and will show whether the SG&A pattern broke or repeated. The litigation ruling could arrive at any time given briefing completed May 29, 2026. A secondary checkpoint is the Q3 report (late November/early December), by which point the WOTC drag will be fully in the run rate and any buyback restart should be visible.

## Conclusion

Dollar General is a cash-generative, defensively positioned retailer trading at 16.6x forward earnings with 1.41x funded leverage, no debt maturity before May 2028, and a 6.5–8.0% normalized free cash flow yield. Those attributes are real, and they justify continuing to own the position rather than exiting it.

They do not justify adding. The recovery narrative rests entirely on gross margin, where shrink improvement from 1.69% to 1.42% of sales is genuine but finite, while the structural profitability gap versus FY2022 is a 306-basis-point SG&A gap that has widened in every reporting period including the most recent one. Net unit growth of 1.45% has replaced a 4.6% growth algorithm, and the remodel program that replaced it capitalizes spending into rising depreciation without disclosed cohort economics.
