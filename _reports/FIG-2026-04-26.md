## Executive Summary

Figma is a $7.7B market cap collaborative design platform that IPO'd on August 1, 2025 at $33/share, briefly traded above $140, and has since declined ~88% from its post-IPO high to $17.49. The company crossed $1 billion in annual revenue in FY2025 growing 41% YoY with $237M in adjusted free cash flow. The balance sheet holds $1.58B in cash and marketable securities with zero debt.

The problem is structural. Three AI-native design tools launched within weeks of each other in March-April 2026 — Google Stitch, Anthropic Claude Design, and Canva AI 2.0 — directly threatening the UI/UX design workflow that underpins Figma's entire revenue base. Figma's per-seat pricing model is particularly exposed: AI tools reduce the number of designers needed per project, and tech layoffs have already compressed seat counts at enterprise customers. Meanwhile, the company is distributing equity at an extraordinary rate — $1.15B in SBC against $1.06B in revenue in FY2025, with 222M additional shares from lock-up releases still pending through mid-2026. CEO Dylan Field sold $7.7M in stock in February 2026. The 26.5% short interest signals that institutional investors have already identified these dynamics.

At 7.3x trailing revenue and ~32x adjusted FCF, the stock is not cheap enough to compensate for the combination of AI disruption risk, dilution overhang, and lock-up supply. The consensus analyst target of $40.25 implies 130% upside, but that consensus was formed before the April 2026 AI competitive shock. Until there is evidence that Figma can successfully transition its pricing model and demonstrate that AI tools are additive rather than cannibalistic to its seat economics, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Figma operates a browser-based collaborative design platform used by approximately 13 million users and ~95% of Fortune 500 companies. The company holds an estimated 80-90% market share in UI/UX design tools, a position it built by disrupting Adobe's desktop-centric Creative Cloud suite. Revenue is generated through seat-based SaaS subscriptions across Starter (free), Professional, Organization, and Enterprise tiers.

The product portfolio expanded significantly in 2025 with four new launches: Figma Sites (design-to-web publishing), Figma Make (AI-powered prompt-to-prototype), Figma Buzz (marketing asset creation), and Figma Draw (vector editing). Three acquisitions in 2025 totaling $153.5M focused on AI capabilities. The company operates as a single reportable segment under CEO/co-founder Dylan Field, who controls 73.3% of voting power through a dual-class share structure.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Figma's revenue trajectory is strong: $1.056B in FY2025, up ~41% YoY, with Q4 2025 revenue of $303.8M (+40.1%). Key business metrics support sustained expansion — net dollar retention of 131%, paid customers over $100K ARR growing 44% YoY to 1,262, and remaining performance obligations of $517.2M. International revenue grew 45% and represents a majority of total revenue.

### Profitability

GAAP financials are severely distorted by stock-based compensation. The $976M one-time IPO RSU release charge in Q3 2025 pushed FY2025 GAAP operating loss to ($1.29B). Stripping out SBC, the non-GAAP picture is more constructive: 11% non-GAAP operating margin for the first nine months of 2025, with Q3 at 12%. However, this represents meaningful compression from the 24% non-GAAP operating margin in Q3 2024. The compression is driven by AI infrastructure costs (hosting/compute increasing $17.6M in Q3 alone) and payroll tax increases tied to the IPO.

Underlying non-GAAP gross margin of ~82-83% remains healthy for an enterprise SaaS company, though the GAAP gross margin of 69% in Q3 (impacted by $43M SBC allocated to COGS) signals that the true cost structure is evolving as AI workloads increase.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Free cash flow generation is genuinely impressive: $204.2M through nine months of 2025 (27% margin), with full-year adjusted FCF of ~$237M. The balance sheet is fortress-quality: $1.58B in cash and securities, zero debt, and a $500M undrawn revolver maturing June 2030. The only notable liability is $474M in deferred revenue, which is a positive indicator of forward revenue visibility.

Two unconventional treasury allocations: $30M in USDC stablecoins and $96.4M in Bitcoin ETF holdings. These introduce asset volatility into what should be a conservative cash management framework and suggest idiosyncratic management judgment.

### The Dilution Problem

FY2025 SBC of $1.15B represents 109% of revenue — the company is distributing more equity than it generates in sales. While the $976M IPO charge is one-time, ongoing SBC will remain elevated: 90.5M RSUs outstanding, 21.3M stock options, 74.2M shares available for future grants, and CEO awards totaling 51.5M shares of Class B stock.

The diluted share count expanded from ~215M (December 2024) to ~493M (September 2025) due to the IPO conversion. An additional 222M shares from extended lock-up holders are being released in four tranches through mid-2026:
- 38.9M shares released November 7, 2025
- 44.4M shares released after FY2025 earnings
- 61.1M shares released after Q1 2026 earnings
- 77.7M shares released after Q2 2026 earnings (or by August 31, 2026)

This staggered supply schedule creates persistent selling pressure through mid-2026.

## Growth Analysis

### Bull Case for Growth

Revenue growth acceleration from 38% (Q3) to 40% (Q4) suggests the product expansion strategy is gaining traction. The Enterprise tier ($100K+ ARR) growing at 44% YoY demonstrates upmarket momentum. A 131% net dollar retention rate means existing customers are expanding usage meaningfully. The $517M in remaining performance obligations provides forward revenue visibility.

The four new products launched in 2025 (Sites, Make, Buzz, Draw) expand Figma's TAM from the ~$20B collaborative design market into adjacent categories: web publishing, marketing content creation, and AI-assisted prototyping. If even one of these products achieves meaningful adoption, it provides a growth vector independent of the core design tool.

### Bear Case for Growth

The seat-based pricing model faces a structural headwind from AI productivity gains. If AI tools allow one designer to do the work of three, enterprise customers need fewer Figma seats. The March 2025 pricing change — requiring administrator approval for seat upgrades — explicitly acknowledges this risk and trades organic viral expansion for administrative control. The 10-Q states plainly: "we may need to make further changes to our billing models" in response to AI dynamics.

Estimate revisions tell a cautionary story. Next-quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 20% over 90 days. Current-year EPS of $0.23 is down 2.4% from 90 days ago. These are modest negative revisions, not dramatic, but they do not support the prior trajectory of improving growth scores.

The consensus estimates of $0.23 current-year and $0.29 next-year EPS imply approximately $100-130M in non-GAAP net income against a $7.7B market cap — roughly 50-60x non-GAAP earnings. This pricing requires sustained 35%+ revenue growth for several years with margin expansion, which the AI competitive threat directly jeopardizes.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.49, Figma trades at approximately:
- 7.3x trailing revenue ($1.06B)
- ~32x trailing adjusted FCF ($237M)
- ~50-60x forward non-GAAP earnings (EPS $0.23-0.29)
- ~88% below its 52-week high of $142.92

The consensus analyst target of $40.25 (median $38.50) implies 130% upside, with a range of $30-$60. These targets were largely set before the April 2026 AI competitive shock and likely require revision.

Adobe trades at roughly 5x revenue with approximately 10% growth. Figma's 7.3x multiple with 40% growth looks reasonable on a growth-adjusted basis — the implied Price/Earnings-Growth ratio is attractive. However, this comparison ignores three critical factors: (1) Figma's SBC runs at 109% of revenue vs. Adobe's ~15%, meaning the real economic cost to shareholders is dramatically higher; (2) Figma faces existential AI competition that Adobe largely does not (Adobe's revenue base is more diversified across video, publishing, and creative tools); (3) the lock-up share schedule creates mechanical selling pressure through mid-2026 that suppresses price regardless of fundamentals.

The 32x FCF multiple would be defensible for a high-growth SaaS company with a clear moat. The central question is whether Figma's moat — network effects from collaboration and the installed base of enterprise design systems — is durable against AI-native alternatives. If it is, the stock is genuinely cheap. If it isn't, the FCF will compress as the company must invest aggressively to defend its position, and 32x on a declining FCF trajectory is expensive.

## Competitive Landscape

Figma's competitive position is deteriorating on multiple fronts simultaneously:

**Google Stitch** (launched March 2026): A free AI-native design canvas backed by Google's AI infrastructure and distribution. The 12% stock decline on launch day reflects the market's assessment that Google has the resources to commoditize basic design functionality. Free pricing undercuts Figma's Professional tier directly.

**Anthropic Claude Design** (launched April 17, 2026): A prompt-to-prototype tool that achieved #1 on Hacker News. The 8.7% single-day stock decline indicates the market views this as a legitimate competitive threat. Current consensus holds that Claude Design targets amateur/casual users, but the trajectory of AI capability improvement means this distinction could narrow within 12-18 months.

**Canva AI 2.0** (launched April 16, 2026): Canva has 265M MAUs and is repositioning as "an AI platform with design tools." This targets the prosumer/marketing professional segment, which overlaps with Figma's newer products (Buzz, Slides).

The common thread: all three competitors are AI-native, and two are free or nearly free. Figma is responding by embedding AI throughout its platform (Figma Make, AI acquisitions), but it is playing defense on AI while simultaneously trying to expand its product surface area.

Figma's durable advantage is its enterprise installed base — the millions of design files, component libraries, and collaborative workflows embedded in Fortune 500 organizations. Switching costs for large design teams are material. The 131% net dollar retention rate confirms that existing customers are not defecting. But the threat is not immediate defection — it is gradual seat count compression as AI reduces headcount requirements, plus competitive encroachment from below as AI tools make basic design accessible to non-designers.

## Risk Assessment

### AI Disruption (Severity: High, Probability: Elevated)
Figma's 10-Q explicitly warns that AI could "reduce the difficulty and cost for competitors to build competitive products" and "decrease the number of designers/developers using Figma's platform." The seat-based model amplifies this: fewer designers needed → fewer seats purchased → revenue compression.

### Lock-Up Supply Overhang (Severity: High, Probability: Near-Certain)
The next release of 61.1M shares occurs after Q1 2026 earnings. At current daily volume of ~12M shares, each tranche represents 3-5 days of trading volume, creating mechanical selling pressure irrespective of fundamentals.

### Dilution from SBC (Severity: High, Ongoing)
The 5% annual evergreen provision on equity plans enables perpetual dilution. Even normalized ongoing SBC (ex-IPO charge) will likely run at 40-60% of revenue for the next several years.

### Dual-Class Voting Structure (Severity: Moderate)
CEO Dylan Field's 73.3% voting control means minority shareholders have essentially no governance influence. The unconventional treasury allocations (Bitcoin ETF, USDC) illustrate the risk of unchecked management judgment.

### OFAC Compliance Investigation (Severity: Low-Moderate)
Voluntary self-disclosure to OFAC regarding potential sanctions violations from users in sanctioned countries. Outcome uncertain but potentially material if penalties are assessed.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 96.2% is modestly rich to the 89.3% historical volatility — a 6.9pp spread indicating the market is pricing slightly elevated uncertainty. The flat term structure (96.2% near-term vs. 94.9% far-term) suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed across timeframes rather than concentrated around a specific event. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (0.3%), and the put/call OI ratio of 1.00 shows no strong directional bias.

Unusual activity shows concentrated call buying at $20-21 strikes expiring May 2026 (1,884 contracts at $20 for May 1, 850 contracts at $21 for May 15) alongside put buying at $15 for May 8 (747 contracts). This suggests speculative positioning for a near-term bounce alongside downside hedging. The 26.5% short interest with 3.6 days to cover creates meaningful squeeze potential if any positive catalyst materializes, but also reflects strong institutional conviction in the bearish thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($30-35, 70-100% upside)
Figma's enterprise installed base proves stickier than the market expects. AI tools are additive (expanding Figma's TAM by making design accessible to more users) rather than cannibalistic. The company successfully transitions toward consumption-based or platform-fee pricing that captures value from AI-assisted workflows. Revenue growth sustains above 30% while non-GAAP margins expand toward 20%+ as IPO-related costs normalize. The $1.58B cash balance provides ample runway to invest in AI capabilities. At 10x forward revenue on $1.5B FY2026E revenue, the stock reaches $34. If AI fears prove overblown, re-rating to 15x revenue produces $51.

### Bear Case ($8-12, 30-55% downside)
AI design tools mature faster than expected, compressing the professional UI/UX design market. Enterprise customers reduce seat counts by 20-30% over 18 months as AI productivity gains take hold. Revenue growth decelerates to 15-20% by mid-2027. Lock-up share releases and insider selling create persistent supply pressure. SBC continues diluting shareholders at extreme rates. At 5x forward revenue on decelerating $1.2B revenue, the stock trades to $14. Deeper multiple compression to 3-4x (reflecting commoditized growth software) produces $8-10.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The next lock-up release of 61.1M shares follows Q1 2026 earnings — approximately 14% of outstanding shares entering the float in a compressed timeframe. Secondary catalyst: Q1 2026 earnings itself, the first quarter to fully reflect the AI competitive shock from Google Stitch (March) and Claude Design/Canva AI 2.0 (April). If management guides conservatively or reports decelerating new customer acquisition, the stock has substantial downside.

**Timing window:** 4-8 weeks. Q1 2026 earnings (likely late May or early June based on the Feb 18 Q4 report timing) provides the binary catalyst. Lock-up release follows shortly after.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest at 26.5% of float with 3.6 days to cover is elevated and creates meaningful squeeze risk, above the 25% threshold where squeeze dynamics become a genuine concern. Any positive surprise — a major enterprise deal announcement, better-than-expected earnings, or AI partnership — could trigger a violent short squeeze. The concentrated call buying at $20-21 strikes suggests positioning for exactly this scenario.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV at 96.2% (rich to HV) increases the cost of put-based short expression. The flat term structure means no particular tenor offers a discount. The balanced put/call skew (0.3%) suggests the short thesis is not yet crowded in the options market despite high share-level short interest — a divergence worth monitoring.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base case short target: $12-14 on earnings disappointment plus lock-up supply. Upside risk: $25-28 on squeeze or positive earnings surprise. From $17.49: downside of $3.50-5.50 (20-31%) vs. upside risk of $7.50-10.50 (43-60%). The reward:risk ratio is approximately 0.5:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** Despite genuine structural headwinds, the 26.5% short interest creates prohibitive squeeze risk, and the reward:risk ratio is below 2:1. The thesis is "bad business trajectory" at already-depressed levels (88% below 52-week high), without an imminent catalyst offering asymmetric downside. The stock is down 88% from peak — much of the bearish thesis is already priced. Avoiding ownership is the appropriate expression of the view, not active shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Figma becomes investable if the following conditions are met:
1. **Lock-up overhang clears:** All four lock-up tranches released (estimated completion August 2026). Until then, mechanical supply pressure makes long positioning premature.
2. **AI competitive response demonstrated:** Evidence that Figma Make or other AI features are driving net-new user acquisition or preventing seat count compression. Specific metric: net dollar retention rate sustaining above 120% for two consecutive quarters after Q1 2026.
3. **Pricing model transition:** Management announces or implements consumption-based pricing or AI-specific pricing tier that decouples revenue from headcount. This removes the structural seat-compression risk.
4. **SBC normalization:** Quarterly SBC below 30% of revenue for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating management is reducing dilution.

Any three of four conditions met would warrant upgrading to HOLD. All four would warrant BUY consideration, with valuation-dependent entry between $15-20 (5-7x forward revenue).

**Timeline:** Headwinds are expected to persist for 6-12 months. The lock-up schedule creates supply pressure through August 2026. The AI competitive threat is structural and will take 2-3 quarters of earnings data to assess the actual revenue impact. Earliest plausible re-entry: Q3 2026 earnings (likely October 2026) if lock-ups are cleared and AI metrics are constructive.

## Conclusion

Figma is a high-quality software business facing a genuine structural challenge at an inopportune time. The 41% revenue growth, $237M in FCF, $1.58B fortress balance sheet, and 80-90% market share in UI/UX design are all real. Under normal circumstances, a stock down 88% from its high with these fundamentals would be worth deep investigation.

Three well-resourced competitors launched AI-native design tools in a four-week window, directly threatening the design workflow that generates 100% of Figma's revenue. The per-seat pricing model amplifies this threat by linking revenue to headcount at a time when AI is reducing headcount requirements. SBC at 109% of revenue means shareholders are paying for growth with dilution rather than cash. The CEO sold $7.7M in stock in February 2026. And the lock-up schedule creates mechanical selling pressure through August 2026.

The prior score trajectory showed improvement (5.9 → 6.7), but that trajectory was established before the April 2026 AI competitive shock. The competitive position and risk profile have deteriorated materially since the March analysis. The identifiable catalysts over the next 6-12 months are predominantly negative (lock-up releases, AI competition intensifying, potential earnings deceleration). The fortress balance sheet prevents this from being a solvency story, but it does not prevent sustained underperformance from dilution and competitive pressure. AVOID, with re-entry conditioned on lock-up clearance, pricing model transition, and demonstrated AI competitive resilience.
