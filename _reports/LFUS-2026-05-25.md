## Executive Summary

Littelfuse is a market-leading electronic components manufacturer experiencing a cyclical recovery. After a 15% revenue decline through the 2023-2024 semiconductor inventory correction, the company is now posting 18.5% YoY revenue growth (Q1 2026), expanding operating margins (15.4% vs. 12.7% prior year), and beating EPS estimates by 14-20% for four consecutive quarters. The $1.07B backlog (+61% YoY) and secular demand from data center power, grid infrastructure, and energy storage systems provide strong forward visibility.

The stock has appreciated 121% from its 52-week low of $201.19, largely reflecting this recovery. At $460, LFUS trades at 31.2x forward FY26 consensus EPS of $14.76. While estimates are rising (+14.4% over 90 days for FY26), the current multiple exceeds the company's historical average and sits above a PEG-justified level for mid-teens growth. The specific risk preventing an upgrade to BUY is threefold: (1) 24% of revenue ($573M) derives from China during an active tariff escalation environment with reciprocal trade restrictions, (2) a product recall with "reasonably possible" loss of undetermined magnitude remains unresolved, and (3) new management (both CEO and CFO joined 2025) remains strategically unproven despite strong early operational execution. These are falsifiable — resolution of any two would warrant a BUY upgrade.

## Company Overview

Littelfuse designs and manufactures electronic components serving three segments: Electronics (56% of revenue, $1.35B), Transportation (28%, $676M), and Industrial (15%, $364M). The company holds the leading position in circuit protection — fuses, TVS diodes, varistors, and PTC devices — serving over 100,000 end customers through direct relationships and distribution partners (Arrow 9.5% of revenue). With ~17,000 employees across 20+ countries and ~65% international revenue, LFUS has a genuinely global manufacturing and distribution footprint.

The company's products are mission-critical components embedded early in customer design processes, creating high switching costs and long product lifecycles. Its competitive advantage stems from breadth of portfolio (enabling one-stop-shop procurement), deep engineering relationships, and the regulatory/qualification barriers in circuit protection standards.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Recovery:** FY2025 revenue of $2,386M represented +8.9% growth after two years of decline. Q1 2026 accelerated to $657M (+18.5% YoY), with organic growth contributing 9% and the Basler acquisition adding the remainder. All three segments grew: Electronics +18.1%, Transportation +5.3%, Industrial +45.3%.

**Margin Expansion:** Gross margin expanded to 38.7% in Q1 2026 (+130bps YoY) on volume leverage. Adjusted operating margin reached 15.4% vs. 12.7% prior year, demonstrating operating leverage on the recovery. The fixed-cost manufacturing base generates significant incremental margins on volume growth.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $434M (18.2% of revenue) and free cash flow of $366M (15.3% FCF margin) demonstrate exceptional cash conversion for an industrial manufacturer. Capital expenditures remain modest at $68M (2.8% of revenue), reflecting a largely maintained manufacturing footprint.

**Balance Sheet:** Conservative leverage with $632M total debt (reduced from $803M at year-end 2025) against $482M cash. The March 2026 refinancing expanded the revolving facility to $800M with 2031 maturity, providing $599M in available capacity. Total liquidity of ~$1.08B provides ample headroom for both M&A and shareholder returns.

**GAAP vs. Adjusted:** The trailing P/E of -285x reflects the $301.2M non-cash goodwill impairment in Q4 2025 related to the Dortmund Fab acquisition. This is a one-time item. On an adjusted basis, FY2025 EPS was approximately $10.80, and the Q1 2026 run-rate suggests $12-13 annualized.

## Growth Analysis

**Estimate Trajectory:** Consensus FY26 EPS of $14.76 has risen +14.4% over 90 days. FY27 estimates of $17.27 imply 17% growth. Current quarter (Q2 2026) consensus stands at $3.78, up 13.3% over 90 days. The revision velocity is positive and accelerating, though below the 30% threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal per inflection stock criteria.

**Secular Drivers:**
- *Data Centers:* Power density increases drive demand for higher-rated circuit protection, power semiconductors (SiC MOSFETs, IGBTs), and industrial fuses. This is the single largest growth vector.
- *Grid Infrastructure:* Utility modernization, renewable integration, and transmission upgrades require Littelfuse's industrial protection portfolio. The $350M Basler acquisition directly addresses this.
- *Energy Storage:* Battery energy storage systems require specialized high-voltage fusing and semiconductor protection.
- *Vehicle Electrification:* Higher-voltage EV architectures need more sophisticated protection, though this end market has softened.

**Backlog:** The 61% surge to $1.07B at year-end 2025 (substantially all scheduled for 2026 delivery) provides unusual forward visibility for a component manufacturer. However, some portion likely reflects tariff-driven pull-forward ordering, which could create a demand air pocket in late 2026.

**Guidance:** Management guided Q2 2026 revenue of $690-710M, representing ~21-25% YoY growth and sequential acceleration from Q1's $657M.

## Competitive Landscape

Littelfuse maintains the broadest circuit protection portfolio in the industry, which constitutes a genuine competitive moat. The company's engineering relationships create design-in barriers that typically last 5-10 years per product cycle. In its core fuse/TVS/varistor markets, LFUS commands premium pricing and maintains 38%+ gross margins through a cycle.

The weaker competitive position is in power semiconductors, where Infineon, ON Semi, and STMicro have meaningfully greater scale. The $301M impairment reflects this reality — the Dortmund Fab acquisition attempted to build semiconductor manufacturing scale that the company may not naturally possess.

The Basler acquisition ($350M) strengthens the industrial controls portfolio for grid/utility applications — a market where Littelfuse's existing protection products create natural cross-selling opportunities. Early integration results (Industrial +45.3% in Q1) are encouraging.

## Risk Assessment

**1. China Revenue Concentration (24%, ~$573M):** This is the most quantifiable near-term risk. Reciprocal tariff escalation could simultaneously raise Littelfuse's input costs and reduce demand from Chinese customers. The 10-K explicitly calls out this dual-risk. Even a 10% demand reduction from China would reduce revenue by ~$57M and EPS by ~$1.50 (10% of consensus).

**2. Tariff-Driven Backlog Front-Loading:** The 61% backlog build coincides with tariff implementations. If 15-25% of the backlog represents pull-forward rather than structural demand, the Q3-Q4 2026 and 2027 revenue trajectory could disappoint.

**3. Product Recall Liability:** A customer has notified Littelfuse of a product recall potentially attributable to certain fuses. The company states a loss is "reasonably possible" but has no accrual and cannot determine the range. Product liability claims in mission-critical components can be substantial.

**4. Dortmund Fab Rationalization:** Ongoing restructuring ($5M in Q1 2026 alone) indicates the power semiconductor business is still being right-sized. Further impairments are possible if projected volumes don't materialize.

**5. New Management Execution:** Both CEO and CFO joined in 2025. While Q1 2026 results were strong, the strategic direction (to be detailed at May 14 Investor Day) is still unproven. The prior management team significantly underperformed peers (5-year TSR of 104% vs. 237% for the peer group).

## Conclusion

Littelfuse is executing well through an earnings recovery with strong secular tailwinds. The 121% appreciation from the 52-week low has largely discounted the near-term recovery trajectory. At 31.2x FY26 consensus with PEG of 1.8x, the stock is approximately fairly valued for its growth rate, with upside contingent on estimate revisions accelerating beyond current pace or risk resolution (China/recall/Dortmund). The 24% China revenue concentration creates binary earnings risk that could compress the multiple 20-25% in an adverse tariff scenario, and the product recall liability remains unquantified. These risks are concrete and time-bound — Q2/Q3 results will either confirm continued momentum or reveal the front-loading thesis. Maintain HOLD with a positive growth bias.
