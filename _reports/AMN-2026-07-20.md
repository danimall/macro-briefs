**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMN Healthcare is the largest U.S. healthcare workforce and staffing firm, and its stock has rallied 72% off its January 2026 low to $35.00, largely on a single quarter of extraordinary results. Q1 2026 revenue doubled to $1,378M with $62.2M net income ($1.60 diluted EPS) and $562M of operating cash flow. The problem is that $722.0M of that revenue came from healthcare labor-disruption (strike) staffing, versus $38.6M in the prior-year quarter. Stripping out the strike windfall, the underlying business is still contracting: travel nurse demand is down year over year, locum days filled fell 9%, VMS revenue fell 18%, and language services fell 8% on pricing. The market is pricing a fundamental turn that the operating data does not support.

The multi-year picture is a violent post-COVID normalization. Revenue fell from $5,243M in 2022 to $2,730M in 2025 (a ~48% peak-to-trough decline), gross margin compressed from 33.0% (2023) to 28.3% (2025), and the company posted GAAP net losses in both 2024 (–$173M) and 2025 (–$101M), driven mostly by goodwill and intangible impairments totaling over $127M in 2025 alone. Cash generation remains intact ($234M FCF in 2025), so this is a re-basing and margin story rather than a solvency crisis, but the balance sheet has softened: total debt of ~$767M, D/E of 2.26x, a sub-1 current ratio, an Altman Z-Score of 1.89 (grey zone), and a coupon that stepped up from 4.625% to 6.5% on the 2031 refinancing.

At $35.00, AMN trades above the $29 consensus median target and near the $38 high, on trailing losses and forward estimates that themselves embed the assumption that strike revenue partly persists. The negative-EPS headline is not the point; normalized (ex-strike) earnings power is materially below what the current price implies, and there is no identifiable catalyst to close the gap in the near term. We rate the stock AVOID at current levels. We do not recommend shorting: the strike-driven earnings stream is genuinely unpredictable, and a new wave of labor disruptions could drive another blowout quarter.

## Company Overview

AMN operates three reportable segments. **Nurse & Allied Solutions** (~60% of FY2025 revenue) covers travel nurse, allied, international nurse, rapid-response staffing, and, increasingly central to the current story, labor-disruption (strike) staffing; roughly 71% of segment revenue runs through Managed Services Programs. **Physician & Leadership Solutions** (~26%) covers locum tenens (CRNAs the largest specialty), interim leadership, executive search, and physician placement. **Technology & Workforce Solutions** (~14%) covers language interpretation, VMS SaaS platforms (ShiftWise Flex, Medefis, b4health), workforce optimization, and RPO.

The company positions itself as a "total talent solutions" partner, and its moat rests on national scale, MSP/VMS platform stickiness, a large credentialed clinician network, and a demonstrated ability to mobilize thousands of clinicians rapidly during labor disruptions. MSP penetration was ~48% of consolidated revenue in FY2025, spiking to 74% in Q1 2026 as the strike surge flowed through managed programs. The core business remains cyclical contingent-labor supply, currently in a multi-year downcycle following the COVID bill-rate and volume bubble.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (annual):**
- 2022: $5,243M revenue / $607M net income / $647M operating income (COVID peak)
- 2023: $3,789M / $284M net income
- 2024: $2,984M / –$173M net loss (incl. $222M goodwill impairment)
- 2025: $2,730M (–8% YoY) / –$101M net loss (incl. $109.5M goodwill + $18.3M intangible impairment, partly offset by $39.1M Smart Square gain)

Gross margin compressed from 33.0% (2023) to 30.8% (2024) to 28.3% (2025) on clinician pay-package compression and pricing pressure, most acutely in language services. FY2025 operating margin was –2.0%, but the reported losses are overwhelmingly non-cash impairments. Cash flow quality held up: FY2025 operating cash flow was $269M and FCF $234M (8.6% FCF margin). DSO improved from 70 to 47 days as revenue shrank. The equity story is about revenue re-basing and margin, not immediate cash burn.

**Q1 2026 — a spike, not an inflection.** The doubling of revenue is driven by a wave of simultaneous large-scale healthcare labor strikes. This carries reconciliation risk: the company held $513M of client deposits/reserves as contract liabilities at quarter-end, with four completed labor-disruption events still being reconciled with customers into Q2–Q3 2026. Much of the $560.7M cash balance reflects these strike-related deposits that will be paid out or reconciled, not durable free cash.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** The ~$767M of total debt comprises $350M of 2029 Notes at 4.0% plus $400M of new 2031 Notes at 6.5%, with the revolver undrawn at 3/31/26. The current ratio is 0.94. The Piotroski F-Score is weak (2 of signals). Management loosened the credit facility's Net Leverage covenant to ≤5.25x through Q1 2027 (Fifth Amendment, October 2025), signaling that they anticipated covenant pressure as normalized EBITDA falls. Goodwill remains large at $756M after $547M of cumulative impairments, with the physician reporting unit's fair value repeatedly coming in near carrying value.

**Capital allocation.** The October 2025 refinancing replaced $500M of 4.625% 2027 Notes with $400M of 6.5% 2031 Notes, extended the revolver to 2030, and cut its size from $750M to $450M, structurally raising interest cost while addressing a maturity. No buybacks in Q1 2026 despite $226.7M of remaining authorization, which is appropriately conservative given the leverage and the uncertain durability of the earnings base.

We score Financial Health 4.5. Cash generation and covenant compliance are currently adequate, and the losses are impairment-driven rather than operational, which supports the score above a distressed level. But the grey-zone Z-score, sub-1 current ratio, rising interest cost, and the fact that a large chunk of headline cash is offsetting strike deposits keep this well below a healthy reading.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data superficially resembles a positive-momentum signal — current-quarter EPS was revised +165.7% over 90 days and current-year EPS +30.8% — but this is a mechanical artifact of the strike windfall being folded into consensus, not organic acceleration. The revealing figure is the next-year estimate, which was revised *down* 4.5% over 90 days to $1.09, versus the current-year $2.66. Consensus itself expects a ~59% EPS decline into next year as strike revenue normalizes. The market is modeling the current surge as transitory.

Underneath the strike layer, every core line is soft or declining. Structural demand erosion is the concern: hospital clients are insourcing, building internal float pools, deferring hiring, and adopting AI/analytics to reduce contingent-labor reliance. The 10-K flags AI explicitly as both a client tool that reduces demand for AMN and an internal opportunity.

The one genuine bright spot is labor-disruption staffing itself. Management notes clients increasingly request strike-contingency support and expects the trend to continue. This is a higher-margin, moat-like capability competitors struggle to match, but the revenue is inherently lumpy and unpredictable, impossible to underwrite as a growth base. We do not credit the +30% current-year revision as the strong inflection BUY signal the pattern usually flags, because it fails the guardrail: it reflects one-time event revenue rather than a trough recovering from depressed levels, and the next-year revision is negative. Growth Prospects score 4.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.00 with 38.8M shares, AMN's market cap is ~$1.4B. The stock trades on negative trailing EPS (–$2.48), which makes the trailing P/E meaningless. The relevant question is normalized earnings power. Consensus current-year EPS is inflated by strike revenue; next-year consensus of $1.09 is a cleaner (still generous) proxy for a normalizing base, which puts the stock at roughly 32x forward-year earnings — an expensive multiple for a business with declining underlying revenue, compressing margins, and 2.26x leverage.

Peer medians in Health Care Services sit around 17.2x P/E (MD 11.7x, ADUS 17.2x). Applying even a peer-median multiple to $1.09 of normalized EPS yields ~$19, close to where the stock traded before the strike surprise. The current price embeds either a permanent step-up in high-margin strike revenue or a demand recovery, neither of which the operating data supports.

Analyst targets reinforce the caution. The mean target is $29.86, with a $22 low and a $38 high, all below the current price. When a stock trades above consensus on strike-inflated numbers, the analyst targets are a ceiling reflecting the same optimism, not a floor. This is a case where a "cheap"-looking forward metric is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal: the compression toward normalized earnings has not happened yet, and cheapness (to inflated numbers) reliably sorts the wrong way. Valuation scores 3.5. The stock is priced for a recovery that requires either recurring strikes or a demand turn that the segment data contradicts.

## Competitive Landscape

AMN is the scale leader in a structurally challenged industry. The U.S. healthcare staffing market is deflating from the COVID bill-rate bubble, with hospital cost pressure, aggressive insourcing, wage/rate normalization, and GPO/competing-MSP intermediation all squeezing pricing. The five-year relative TSR gap is stark: AMN's cumulative TSR indexed to $100 at end-2020 fell to 23 by 2025, versus a peer group at 113 — roughly a 77% shareholder loss while peers were roughly flat. That underperformance reflects AMN's heavier travel-nurse mix and Kaiser concentration, and it shows the market has penalized AMN's specific de-rating beyond the sector average.

The trajectory is negative on the recurring lines: VMS is losing clients, language services is losing on price, and locum volume is contracting. The offsetting positive is genuine: AMN's ability to mobilize thousands of clinicians for strikes is a differentiated, high-margin capability that competitors cannot easily replicate, and management sees strike-contingency demand rising structurally. That is a real moat feature, but it does not stabilize the core cyclical business. Competitive Position scores 4.5 — scale leader, but with eroding share and pricing across most recurring segments, partly offset by the strike-response differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**Reliance on non-recurring strike revenue.** The entire Q1 2026 recovery is strike staffing, explicitly described as unpredictable and event-driven. When strikes resolve, this revenue evaporates. Extrapolating the Q1 run-rate would badly overstate earnings power.

**Kaiser concentration.** Kaiser Foundation Hospitals and affiliates were ~22% of consolidated revenue in 2025 — unusual concentration for a company this size. Some Kaiser revenue in Q1 2026 likely reflects the very labor-disruption events driving the spike, adding to the transience of the surge. Any reduction would be materially adverse.

**Structural demand erosion.** Insourcing, internal float pools, and client adoption of AI/analytics all reduce contingent-labor reliance. This is the underappreciated long-term risk.

**Leverage and refinancing cost.** The coupon step-up and the covenant loosening signal management proactively addressing tightness. As strike EBITDA rolls off, covenant headroom compresses.

**Goodwill impairment risk persists.** With the physician unit's fair value repeatedly near carrying value, a 50bp WACC change swings potential impairment ~9–14%.

**Reconciliation risk.** The $513M of client deposits sit against four unreconciled strike events flowing into Q2–Q3 2026.

**Governance.** Recurring Item 5.02 executive/director departures (multiple filings across 2023–2026) during a downturn, and no meaningful open-market insider buying at trough prices in late 2025/early 2026, temper the recovery narrative.

Risk Factors score 3.5, reflecting concentrated, partly binary risks (Kaiser, strike-revenue reversal, reconciliation, impairment) that the current elevated price does not adequately discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.9% against 64.0% one-year historical vol (rich by 8.9 points, 84th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation: front-month IV (72.9%) exceeds the 6-month (65.7%), which typically indicates the market is pricing imminent event risk. Put/call skew is elevated at 9.7% (heavy put demand) in the front month, with a ±17.0% implied move on the nearest expiry. Options positioning is consistent with the fundamental caution here: the market is paying up for downside protection into the next earnings print, when the strike windfall may fail to repeat. The rich IV and backwardation argue against buying the stock into a potential volatility event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38–45).** Labor-disruption staffing proves to be a structurally recurring, high-margin revenue stream rather than a one-off, as more healthcare workers unionize and strike activity persists. Underlying nurse/locum demand troughs in 2026 and re-bases upward as post-COVID normalization completes. Kaiser remains a stable partner. Cash generation funds deleveraging, restoring covenant headroom and re-rating the multiple. In this scenario the $2.66 current-year EPS proves more durable than consensus fears and the stock holds a premium multiple.

**Bear Case ($19–22).** Strikes resolve, the quarterly windfall evaporates, and the market re-anchors to normalized earnings near $1.09. Underlying demand keeps eroding on insourcing and AI-driven client efficiency. Kaiser concentration bites. Goodwill takes another impairment as the physician unit's fair value slips below carrying value. Applying a peer-median ~17x multiple to normalized EPS puts the stock near $19, roughly 45% below the current price and back toward pre-surge levels.

The base case sits closer to the bear: normalized EPS of roughly $1.10–1.30 at a 15–17x multiple implies a fair value of $18–22. The current $35 price requires the bull case to materialize, and the segment data actively contradicts it.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest bearish catalyst is the reversal of strike revenue as current labor disputes resolve, which would show up in the next one to two earnings prints (the last earnings date was 2026-05-07; the next report is likely early August 2026). The Q2/Q3 reconciliation of the client-deposit balance could also produce negative surprises. None of these is a hard-dated, guaranteed-negative event — strike revenue is unpredictable in both directions.

**Timing window.** A meaningful de-rating would require the market to see a strike-free quarter, which may not arrive on a predictable schedule. This is the core problem for a short: the same unpredictability that makes the current run unsustainable means new strikes could produce another blowout quarter at any time, extending the pain for a short position.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 9.9% of float with 3.1 days to cover — moderate and uncrowded enough to leave room, but the low days-to-cover means a positive strike-driven surprise could squeeze quickly.

**Borrow and options signal.** With the options market already pricing a downside event, puts are expensive and the easy asymmetry is gone. Buying protection here means paying up.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside target is roughly $19–22 (–37% to –45%). Upside risk from a new strike wave or an earnings beat is substantial and open-ended, given the demonstrated ±17% implied move and the $38–45 bull case. Reward:risk is therefore worse than 2:1 once event uncertainty and rich hedging cost are factored in.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis (overpriced on non-recurring earnings) is sound, but the catalyst is undated and two-sided: a genuinely unpredictable strike stream can produce upside surprises at any time. Avoid ownership; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit AMN as a BUY candidate if: (1) the stock compresses toward $19–22, aligning price with normalized (ex-strike) earnings power at a peer-appropriate 15–17x multiple; (2) underlying (ex-strike) segment revenue — nurse, locum, VMS, language — stabilizes or turns positive for two consecutive quarters, confirming a genuine demand trough; or (3) management demonstrates that labor-disruption revenue is recurring at a scale sufficient to underwrite a durable earnings base, evidenced by multi-quarter contracted strike-contingency revenue rather than event-driven spikes. Clustered open-market insider buying at current or lower prices would also be a meaningful positive signal, given its current absence.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — post-COVID demand normalization, insourcing, and pricing pressure — are structural and likely persist through at least 2026 and into 2027. The strike windfall could distort reported results for one to two more quarters, delaying the market's re-anchoring to normalized numbers. We expect a clearer read on normalized earnings power by the second half of 2026.

**Downgrade to active caution.** A goodwill impairment on the physician reporting unit, evidence of Kaiser reducing its relationship, or a covenant-headroom scare as strike EBITDA rolls off would each deepen the bear case.

## Conclusion

AMN's headline recovery rests almost entirely on one-time labor-disruption staffing, while every recurring segment — travel nurse, locum, VMS, language services — is still declining. The stock's rally has carried it above the consensus median target on numbers that consensus itself expects to fall ~59% into next year. At roughly 32x a generous normalized EPS estimate, against a peer median near 17x, a declining core business, 2.26x leverage, a grey-zone Altman Z, 22% Kaiser concentration, and recurring executive turnover, the risk/reward is skewed to the downside. Cash generation and covenant compliance keep this out of distressed territory, but the equity is priced for a recovery the operating data does not support.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The fair-value range is $18–22 versus the current $35.00, roughly 40% downside to base case. We do not recommend an active short: the unpredictable strike-revenue stream creates genuine two-sided event risk and squeeze potential, and rich options pricing has removed the easy asymmetry. Wait for the price to re-anchor to normalized earnings, or for durable evidence that either the core demand has troughed or strike revenue is recurring at scale.
