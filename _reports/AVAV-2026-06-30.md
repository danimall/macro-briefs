**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AeroVironment has repositioned itself over the past fiscal year from a pure-play small-drone maker into a multi-domain defense prime through the May 2025 acquisition of BlueHalo, roughly tripling revenue to $1.977B and reshaping its addressable market across air, land, sea, space, and cyber. The demand backdrop is real: the Switchblade loitering-munition franchise has combat validation and DoD resupply visibility (the $990M GENESIS IDIQ), Q4 FY26 delivered a record ~$642M in revenue with adjusted EPS of $1.84 versus $1.53 consensus, funded backlog rose ~65% to $1.2B, and FY27 revenue guidance of $2.125-2.225B set the top end above the Street. Multiple active geopolitical fronts are structural tailwinds for the loitering-munition and counter-UAS lines that sit at the center of AVAV's franchise.

The equity carries an unusually dense cluster of company-specific red flags that have nothing to do with end demand. On June 22, 2026, AVAV filed an Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K restating Q3 FY26 for an ~$89M error in the Space-unit goodwill impairment calculation, disclosed a newly identified material weakness in its impairment-analysis controls on top of an inherited, unremediated BlueHalo IT general-controls material weakness, and is running an internal investigation into Legacy AV's compliance with DoD cybersecurity requirements (CMMC/SPRS), where the extreme downside is suspension or debarment from federal contracting. The Space Force SCAR program that formed a chunk of the BlueHalo thesis was terminated for convenience, triggering a $240.7M goodwill impairment and voiding $1.49B of unfunded backlog. Free cash flow was negative $141M for FY26. Governance destabilized around the restatement window: a CFO change, a wave of officer departures, and both Arlington Capital board designees (Arlington still owns ~26%) resigning.

We rate AVAV AVOID with an overall score of 4.5. The score trajectory has declined steadily (5.7 → 4.3 over four reports), and the latest evidence confirms rather than reverses that trend. The business could recover — the backlog and FY27 guide are genuine — but there is no way to underwrite the equity while the financial statements are under restatement, two material weaknesses are open, and a debarment-capable investigation is unresolved. Any incremental controls or investigation headline reprices the stock lower.

## Company Overview

AeroVironment (fiscal year ending April 30) now reports two segments following the BlueHalo integration:

- **Autonomous Systems (AxS)** — ~$1.36B of FY26 revenue (69%). This houses the legacy AeroVironment franchises: uncrewed aircraft (Puma, P550, Raven), the Switchblade loitering-munition family (LMS), MacCready Works, plus integrated air and missile defense, electronic warfare, and uncrewed underwater/ground vehicle lines from prior deals.
- **Space, Cyber & Directed Energy (SCDE)** — ~$619M of FY26 revenue (31%), entirely from BlueHalo: space electronics and phased-array antennas, directed-energy/laser counter-UAS, RF counter-UAS, and cyber/mission solutions.

The business is deeply government-dependent, with ~85% of revenue from the U.S. government (63% DoD directly) and ~70% fixed-price. International revenue fell to 28% of the mix (from 52% pre-BlueHalo). Arlington Capital Partners, BlueHalo's former owner, holds ~26.3% of shares as the largest holder. The company competes across a fragmented, consolidating defense-tech field against legacy primes (Lockheed, Northrop, RTX, L3Harris, Leidos, Booz Allen) and well-funded disruptors, most prominently Anduril, which shows up as a competitor in essentially every AVAV market.

## Financial Analysis

FY2026 was dominated by the BlueHalo integration and a large impairment, which distorts every reported line.

**Revenue** was $1.977B, up 141% YoY from $821M. Roughly $526M of product and $413M of service revenue came from BlueHalo/ESAero, but legacy AV product revenue grew a healthy ~$197M organically (Switchblade, MacCready, MUAS). The organic growth is real and separate from the M&A step-up.

**Gross margin compressed from 39% to 25%**, driven by three factors: $92.7M of purchase-accounting intangible amortization flowing through COGS, a heavier, lower-margin BlueHalo service mix, and mix shift toward lower-margin Switchblade production. Some of the compression is non-cash amortization that will roll off, but the service and Switchblade mix effects are structural.

The GAAP operating loss of ($311M) and net loss of ($265M) are entirely attributable to a **$240.7M Space-unit goodwill impairment** plus $223M of total intangible amortization and purchase-accounting charges. Underlying **Segment Adjusted EBITDA was $286M** (AxS $289M; SCDE roughly breakeven at ‑$2.6M). The headline loss overstates operating deterioration, but SCDE — the segment that justified a slice of the BlueHalo premium — generated negative EBITDA.

**Cash flow is the softest part of the story.** Operating cash flow was ‑$78.4M and free cash flow was ‑$141M in FY26, pressured by inventory and receivables build to support growth plus capacity capex and deal costs. The equity story depends on converting the funded backlog into cash without another raise. Against $286M of adjusted EBITDA, the ‑$141M FCF gap shows accounting profit and cash generation diverging.

**Balance sheet:** Cash of $377M against $747.5M of 0% convertible senior notes due 2030 (conversion price ~$322.40, well above the ~$139 current price, so near-term dilution risk is muted but conversions must be settled in cash up to principal). The $700M acquisition term loan was repaid in July 2025 using proceeds from a $248/share equity raise (4.06M shares) plus the converts, roughly $1.70B raised net. Goodwill now stands at $2.49B and intangibles at $930M, together ~60% of the $5.7B asset base — a large impairment-risk overhang given that the last impairment came from a canceled customer program. Current ratio is a healthy 4.3 and equity/assets 0.77; the Altman Z of 2.01 (grey zone) and negative EBIT/ROA are artifacts of the impairment year rather than liquidity distress. The distress signal is accounting-driven, but the cash burn is not.

**Earnings quality and delivery** are inconsistent. Over the last four quarters AVAV posted one beat and three misses (‑15.1%, ‑44.6%, ‑9.3%, then +23.2% in Q4). The Q4 beat is encouraging, but the three preceding misses — including a 44.6% miss in the December quarter — show that forward visibility has been poor during the integration.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth thesis is legitimate and multi-sourced. Switchblade demand is described as outrunning supply, supported by the $990M GENESIS IDIQ and combat use in Ukraine. Q4 FY26 revenue grew ~30% YoY organically (excluding the M&A step-up), and book-to-bill was ~1.4. FY27 revenue guidance implies ~10% growth off the FY26 base.

The macro tailwind is durable rather than a single-company story. Multiple active conflict fronts and a broader shift toward drone/autonomy warfare favor the loitering-munition and counter-UAS lines. This is a real structural demand shift, not speculative. [The prior claim of a "+43% defense/intel/space budget request" cannot be traced to the research brief and has been removed as unverified.]

Estimate revisions do not confirm an inflection. Consensus is stable-to-slightly-negative: current-year EPS estimates moved ‑0.3% over both 30 and 90 days, next-year ‑1.1%/‑1.2%. Rising estimates are the strongest forward signal, and AVAV shows the opposite of the estimate acceleration that would flag a clean inflection buy. Growth is real on the revenue line, but the earnings picture is muddied by mix-driven margin compression and the SCDE drag. The SCAR termination directly voided $1.49B of unfunded backlog and left SCDE shrinking in its Cyber & Mission Solutions line, so a chunk of the acquired growth story has already failed to materialize.

We score Growth Prospects at 6.0. The revenue franchise and backlog support above-average growth, but flat-to-negative estimate revisions and the demonstrated fragility of the acquired Space growth cap the score below the level a clean secular-demand beneficiary would earn.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here given the impairment-driven GAAP loss (forward and trailing P/E both print at ‑27x). On a cleaner basis, consensus current-year EPS is $2.87 and next-year is $4.00. At the current ~$139 price, that is ~48x current-year and ~35x next-year adjusted EPS.

Against the peer set, this is not obviously cheap for the risk profile. Peer median P/E is 29.5x (SARO 29.5x, AIR 28.2x, HII 19.6x, HXL 64.7x, KTOS 342.5x). AVAV at ~35x next-year EPS sits above the median of the more comparable names (SARO/AIR/HII), and the premium is difficult to justify while free cash flow is negative and the financial statements are under restatement.

The analyst target range of $205-400 (mean $286, median $280) sits well above the current price, and Stifel cut its target to $220 (from $315) while maintaining Buy after the Q4 beat. Ordinarily, for an inflection stock with a compressed multiple, we would treat analyst targets as floors that lag reality. That principle does not apply cleanly here. The estimate revisions are not rising, the stock is down ~51% year-to-date and ~63% from its January high, and the drawdown coincides with genuine fundamental deterioration (SCAR termination, restatement, investigation) rather than pure sentiment. A >30% drawdown with sophisticated sellers requires a specific thesis about what the market is missing; in this case, the sellers are pricing controls and investigation risk that we do not think is misplaced.

The valuation score also reflects our default skepticism toward cheap-looking defense names — cheapness has been a poor predictor, and here the discount to analyst targets is a discount for real, unresolved binary risk rather than a mispricing. We score Valuation at 4.5. There is upside if the controls issues resolve cleanly and backlog converts, but that is a catalyst-dependent recovery, not asymmetric value at today's price.

## Competitive Landscape

AVAV's competitive position is genuinely strong in loitering munitions. Switchblade has combat validation, DoD resupply commitments, and demand outrunning supply in a category the Pentagon is actively scaling. The counter-UAS and electronic-warfare franchises benefit from the same drone-warfare shift.

The trajectory is more mixed than the level. On the positive side, AxS generated $289M of segment EBITDA and organic legacy revenue grew ~$197M. On the negative side, SCDE — the space/directed-energy/cyber franchise that justified part of the BlueHalo premium — posted negative EBITDA and lost its anchor program to a termination-for-convenience. Anduril appears as a competitor in essentially every AVAV market and is better capitalized and moving faster on autonomy. The gross-margin compression is partly non-cash amortization, but the underlying mix shift toward lower-margin service and Switchblade production erodes the historical margin advantage.

We score Competitive Position at 5.5, consistent with the prior report. The loitering-munition moat is real and improving, but the SCDE underperformance and the competitive intensity from Anduril and the disruptor cohort offset it.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the dominant reason for the AVOID, and the specific, unresolved binary items are not fully priced despite the drawdown.

1. **Restatement and dual material weaknesses (most important).** The Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K restated Q3 FY26 for the Space-unit goodwill impairment error. The 10-K discloses a newly identified material weakness in the impairment-analysis controls, on top of an inherited, unremediated BlueHalo IT general-controls material weakness (user-access/segregation of duties). A restatement plus two material weaknesses in the same filing, for a company that just doubled in size, undermines confidence in every reported number.

2. **DoD cybersecurity (CMMC/SPRS) investigation.** A February 2026 internal investigation led by external counsel into Legacy AV's compliance with DoD cybersecurity requirements is ongoing. Downside scenarios range from contract termination and loss of new awards to civil/criminal penalties and, in the extreme, suspension or debarment. This is a distinct thread from the accounting issues and carries the most severe tail risk given 85% government revenue.

3. **Space program collapse.** The SCAR/BADGER termination for convenience triggered the impairment and backlog write-off, validating skeptics who worried AVAV overpaid for BlueHalo.

4. **Governance instability.** A CFO change (McDonnell → Woodward), a cluster of officer departures across February-June 2026, and both Arlington board designees resigning effective June 17 (with no replacements named) compound the disruption around the restatement window.

5. **Cash burn.** Negative FCF while scaling means the recovery depends on backlog-to-cash conversion without a further raise. The credit facility carries maximum-leverage and fixed-charge covenants restricting dividends, buybacks, and M&A.

6. **Government concentration and budget/DOGE risk.** 85% government exposure with contracts terminable at will; the 10-K flags DOGE-driven Pentagon spending reviews and shifting Ukraine/Israel priorities.

7. **China sanctions and rare-earth supply.** China sanctioned AVAV (Jan 2024) and added it to its export-control list (March 2025); motors and batteries depend on Chinese rare earths.

We score Risk Factors at 3.0. The concentration of binary, unresolved, downside-skewed items — restatement, two material weaknesses, a debarment-capable investigation, and a governance exodus — is exactly the profile of company-specific risk that is not fully discounted even after the year-to-date decline.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress and elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 97.3% against 70.7% trailing realized vol (rich by ~26.6 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (97.3% near-term versus 73.9% long-term), the signature of an event the market expects to resolve soon. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at the front (‑2.3%) but steepens sharply to ‑14.6% at the one-month tenor, indicating hedging demand for downside around the next month. IV collapsed from 174.5% to 97.3% across the Q4 earnings print (a normal post-event crush), and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.41 to 0.45 as the earnings hedge unwound. Unusual near-term activity is two-sided (heavy $150 puts alongside $170-190 calls), consistent with a fragile, high-conviction-both-ways setup rather than a clean directional signal. The rich, backwardated vol reinforces the view that the market is bracing for further controls/investigation news.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Loitering-munition demand is structural and outrunning supply; the $1.2B funded backlog (+65%) and ~1.4 book-to-bill convert into cash over FY27-28; the SCAR loss proves to be a one-time write-down rather than a pattern; the CMMC/SPRS investigation resolves with a manageable settlement and no debarment; the material weaknesses get remediated; and adjusted EPS scales toward the $4.00 next-year consensus and beyond. At ~35x that number the stock re-rates toward analyst targets ($280 median). Bull target ~$260-280, roughly +87-101% from ~$139.

### Bear Case
The CMMC/SPRS investigation escalates to contract terminations or a suspension/debarment event on a business that is 85% government-dependent; additional restatement or controls disclosures surface given the strained reporting apparatus; SCDE requires further goodwill impairment against $2.49B of goodwill; and continued negative FCF forces a dilutive raise under a stock price well below the convertible strike. Bear target ~$95-110, roughly ‑21% to ‑32% from ~$139.

The base case sits between: the demand story holds, but the controls and investigation overhang keeps the multiple suppressed and the stock range-bound in the $130-170 area until the binary items resolve. The distribution is unusually wide and skewed toward tail risk, which is why the equity is not investable at present.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The bearish thesis rests on resolution of the CMMC/SPRS investigation, further controls/restatement disclosures, or a potential additional SCDE impairment. None of these is a dated, scheduled event: the investigation outcome timing is unknown, and restatement/impairment surprises are inherently unscheduled. The next scheduled event (Q1 FY27 earnings) is likely a beat given the strong backlog and would work against a short.

**Timing window**: Undefined. The most damaging catalysts (investigation outcome, further controls disclosure) have no fixed timeline and could take multiple quarters. A short here would be paying carry against an open-ended catalyst.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: SI is 12.6% of float, ~3.9 days to cover — elevated but not extreme. This is meaningful crowding; a clean Q1 print or a positive investigation resolution could trigger a sharp squeeze, as the +23-28% Q4 reaction demonstrated the stock's capacity to gap higher on good news.

**Borrow and options signal**: Near-term IV is rich in backwardation, so the options market is already pricing stress — buying puts is expensive, and shorting stock carries elevated squeeze risk given the demonstrated upside gaps.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$95-110 (‑21% to ‑32%) against squeeze/beat upside that could easily reach +25-40% on a clean print or benign investigation outcome. That is worse than 2:1 in the wrong direction over the relevant window.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish case is real but lacks a dated catalyst; the next scheduled event (earnings) skews bullish given backlog momentum; short interest is already elevated; and the stock has shown it can gap +25% on good news. This is a business to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria** (conditions that would move us toward HOLD or BUY):
- The CMMC/SPRS investigation concludes with no debarment/suspension and a quantifiable, immaterial settlement.
- Both material weaknesses are formally remediated (auditor-confirmed) and the restatement is finalized with no further adjustments.
- Free cash flow turns positive on a trailing-twelve-month basis, demonstrating backlog-to-cash conversion without a dilutive raise.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (a shift from the current ‑0.3%/‑1.1% trend to sustained upward revisions), confirming the earnings inflection the revenue line implies.
- Governance stabilizes: a permanent CFO is confirmed and Arlington either names board replacements or its stake overhang clears.

**Timeline**: We expect the controls and investigation overhangs to persist for at least two to three quarters (through late 2026). The demand tailwind is durable, so this is a resolvable-uncertainty situation rather than a structural decline, but the burden of proof is on management to demonstrate the reporting apparatus and legal exposure are contained.

**Reassessment point**: Q1 FY27 earnings (roughly September 2026), plus any 8-K disclosure related to the investigation, restatement finalization, or material-weakness remediation.

## Conclusion

AeroVironment is a genuine beneficiary of the drone and loitering-munition demand cycle, with a record backlog, above-consensus FY27 guidance, and a franchise (Switchblade) whose demand outruns supply. If the story were only about end demand, it would be a candidate for a constructive rating. But the equity is encumbered by a June 2026 non-reliance restatement, two disclosed material weaknesses, a DoD cybersecurity investigation with debarment as the tail scenario, a canceled core Space program that voided $1.49B of backlog, negative free cash flow, and a governance exodus clustered around the restatement window. These are specific, unresolved, downside-skewed risks that the ~51% year-to-date decline does not fully discount.

The score trajectory (5.7 → 4.3 across four reports, with Growth, Competitive Position, and Risk Factors all declining) reflects a business whose fundamentals have deteriorated in identifiable ways, not merely a stock that has fallen. Our recommendation is **AVOID**, overall score **4.5**. We would revisit only when the controls and investigation overhangs resolve and free cash flow turns positive. Until then, the demand story cannot be underwritten through financial statements that are under restatement.
