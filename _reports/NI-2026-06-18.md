**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NiSource is a fully regulated multi-utility holding company serving roughly 3.3 million gas and 500,000 electric customers across six states, organized into Columbia (gas distribution) and NIPSCO (gas and electric in northern Indiana). The conventional regulated business (monopoly franchises, defined ROEs on rate base, and an unusually deep set of cost-recovery trackers) produces stable, low-beta earnings. What distinguishes NI from gas-distribution-only peers is the data-center generation strategy run through its GenCo subsidiary, which has converted northern Indiana's emergence as a hyperscaler hub into binding long-term contracts with Amazon Data Services (now expanded to 2,800 MW by end-2032) and an Alphabet subsidiary (300 MW by 2030, 15-year term). This is a real multi-year earnings growth driver, partly de-risked by parent-backed credit support and a Blackstone co-investment that funds the build while preserving NI's investment-grade rating.

The financial profile is a heavy-investment story rather than a free-cash-flow story. Q1 2026 EPS rose 6% to $1.06 on 8.2% revenue growth, but capex was $805M in the quarter alone against a 2026 plan of $5.2–5.6B and a cumulative 2026–2030 program north of $20B including GenCo. Funding that program requires sustained debt issuance and ATM equity, and interest expense already jumped 44% YoY to $191.6M. The model works as long as rate cases land, IURC approves the special contracts, and the IG rating holds; each of those is a live variable with a dated resolution over the next two quarters.

At $46.77, NI trades at roughly 23x forward EPS of $2.06, a premium to the multi-utility peer median near 20x, with a 2.5% dividend yield below the ~3.1% peer median. Estimates have been broadly flat-to-down (current-quarter consensus -6.3% over 90 days) and the company has missed in three of the last four quarters, which tempers the case for paying up. The data-center optionality is already partly embedded in the multiple, so the asymmetry ahead of the Q2 (Amazon) and Q3 (Alphabet) IURC orders is balanced rather than skewed. We rate NI a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale, looking for clean regulatory approvals and a wider valuation discount to upgrade.

## Company Overview

NiSource operates two reportable segments. Columbia Operations provides regulated natural gas distribution to roughly 2.44 million customers in Ohio, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Kentucky, and Maryland; it generated $472.3M of operating income in Q1 2026. NIPSCO (Northern Indiana Public Service Company) provides regulated gas and electric service to roughly 497,000 electric and 879,000 gas customers in northern Indiana, contributing $348.5M of Q1 operating income. Substantially all current operating income flows through traditional rate-regulated mechanisms: gas-cost and fuel pass-throughs plus a defined ROE on rate base.

The strategic differentiator is GenCo (NIPSCO Generation LLC), a non-utility subsidiary building generation to serve hyperscale data-center load in northern Indiana. The buildout includes a new combined-cycle gas plant, 400 MW + 100 MW of battery storage, and a "Pool Resource Asset" portfolio (~340 MW of owned/contracted generation plus up to ~175 MW of seasonal market purchases). The aggregate cost of the contract and pool assets to serve current data-center customers is estimated at $9.25–9.75B. NiSource owns and operates the generation and bears construction and financing risk, while the hyperscalers provide parent-backed credit support and pay capacity charges. These contracts are pending IURC and, in part, FERC approval.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 total operating revenue was $2,363.1M, up 8.2% from $2,183.2M, with operating income up 7.9% to $819.2M. Net income available to common rose to $510.7M from $474.8M, and diluted EPS increased 6% to $1.06 from $1.00. Growth was driven by rate-case increases and regulatory capital recovery (NIPSCO electric Step 2 rates effective March 2026 and Columbia of Pennsylvania rates effective January 2026), partly offset by higher D&A, O&M, and interest expense, which rose 44% YoY to $191.6M from $132.8M.

The balance sheet carries $15.46B of long-term debt against $11.93B of total equity (including $2.27B of Blackstone-related noncontrolling interest). Debt-to-cap is 51.5% against a 70% covenant limit, comfortable for a regulated utility. The commercial-paper program was raised to $2.5B in March 2026, with $1.29B outstanding at 4.12% and net available liquidity of $1.63B. Credit ratings are investment grade at BBB+/Baa2/BBB, all Stable; maintaining IG is explicitly central to the financing plan, since a downgrade would trigger roughly $158.5M of collateral calls and raise financing costs across the program.

Cash flow reflects the investment intensity. Q1 operating cash flow fell to $442.3M from $686.4M, driven by working-capital swings in exchange gas and payables rather than earnings deterioration. Against $805.2M of Q1 capex, the company is structurally free-cash-flow negative and funds the gap with debt and ATM equity. Forward equity sales were struck at $41.84 (October 2025) and $45.63 (February 2026), with roughly $1.25B remaining on a $1.5B ATM program; management is pre-funding equity needs near current price levels, which signals discipline but also confirms ongoing dilution.

Earnings quality and visibility warrant a caution flag: NI has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 -2.3%, Q4 2025 -1.3%, Q3 2025 -6.8%), beating only narrowly in Q2 2025. These are small misses consistent with a regulated utility, not a sign of operational distress, but they argue against treating forward estimates as conservative.

## Growth Analysis

The base regulated business supports mid-single-digit EPS growth through rate-base expansion funded by a heavy capex program: $3.7–5.1B annually in base capex. NiSource's reliance on trackers (TDSIC, CEP, IRP, SAVE, SMRP, FMCA, GCA, FAC) reduces regulatory lag and supports recovery of that investment with less rate-case timing risk than peers carry.

The incremental growth lever is GenCo. The Amazon contract (expanded from 2,400 MW) carries a fixed capacity charge from 2027–2031 regardless of termination, and the Alphabet subsidiary contract begins service summer 2026. GenCo also committed roughly $658.7M under two energy-storage agreements (one converting to the 167 MW Tipton BESS in 2028) plus a subsequent ~$1.1B capacity agreement spanning 2028–2040. Management says it continues negotiating with additional counterparties, so contracted load could expand further.

The growth quality is mixed. On the positive side, the offtakers are among the most creditworthy counterparties available, demand is real and broad-based (consistent with the wider confirmation of data-center load growth visible across regulated utilities, power generation, and electrical equipment), and contract terms include termination payments designed to recover NiSource's investment plus parent support. On the negative side, near-term consensus EPS revisions have been flat to down (current-quarter estimates fell 6.3% over 90 days and the current-year figure of $2.06 is essentially unchanged), so the growth narrative has not yet shown up as upward revision velocity. There is no large upward estimate-revision signal here; growth is durable but gradual, and the GenCo earnings contribution is back-end-loaded as construction (on-site work starts mid-2026) and IURC approvals progress. Roughly $49M per quarter of net income now leaks to noncontrolling interest under the Blackstone structure, which moderates per-share growth relative to headline GenCo economics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.77 against $2.06 of current-year consensus EPS, NI trades at roughly 22.7x forward earnings (24.0x on the trailing $1.95). The multi-utility peer set (CMS 20.1x, CNP 26.1x, AEE 19.5x, DTE 24.0x, PNW 19.3x) has a median of 20.1x. NI sits at a premium to that median, and its dividend yield is below the peer median. The premium is defensible only on the data-center growth optionality; absent that, the regulated base would warrant a multiple closer to the peer median.

The cycle-adjusted-valuation lens does not rescue the case here. This is not a trough-earnings setup: EPS is growing mid-single-digits, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and there is no margin-expansion-plus-revenue-acceleration combination that would make trailing multiples misleading. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle does not apply either, because growth is not accelerating into rising estimates. The consensus mean target is $51.36 (median $52, range $43–56), implying roughly 10% upside to the mean — respectable but not asymmetric for a stock that has already moved 19% over the trailing year and sits near its 52-week high of $48.98.

The market is paying a premium for a data-center story that is partly real and partly still contingent on IURC approvals. A low yield and an above-peer multiple on a capital-intensive, dilution-funded, FCF-negative balance sheet in a higher-for-longer rate environment does not offer a margin of safety. The valuation is full rather than stretched, which supports a HOLD but not a BUY. We would find the stock genuinely attractive in the low-$40s, where the forward multiple compresses toward the peer median and the data-center optionality becomes free.

## Competitive Landscape

The core moat is the standard regulated-utility franchise (monopoly territories and regulated returns) augmented by an unusually favorable geographic position. Northern Indiana has become a hyperscaler magnet thanks to proximity to Chicago, available land, and MISO interconnection, and NiSource has been a first mover in converting that demand into binding contracts with Amazon and Alphabet. That distinguishes NI from gas-distribution-only peers and is a competitive advantage the broader market is partly, but not fully, recognizing.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: load growth is structurally underpinned, the tracker-heavy regulatory model limits lag, and EPA's February 2026 rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding plus proposed repeal of power-plant GHG standards could save roughly $675M of contemplated compliance cost. Offsetting this, the coal-to-gas-and-renewables transition has hit reliability friction: NIPSCO's R.M. Schahfer coal facility, slated for end-2025 retirement, is operating under successive DOE Section 202(c) emergency orders (currently through June 21, 2026), with $65.9M deferred to regulatory assets in Q1 and the recovery mechanism still being litigated at FERC/MISO. State decarbonization pressure (Maryland building electrification rules) threatens gas-distribution growth in part of the footprint. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-improving, which supports a score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by the GenCo strategy, which is why management added an entire "Data Center Operations and Strategy Risk" category to the FY2025 10-K. The most material idiosyncratic risks:

1. **Regulatory approval (binary, near-dated).** The Amazon special contract, the GenCo PPA, and the Alphabet contract all require IURC (and some FERC) approval. A settlement with the OUCC and NIPSCO Industrial Group was reached in February 2026, but the Citizens Action Coalition is actively opposing it. IURC orders are expected Q2 2026 (Amazon) and Q3 2026 (Alphabet). Adverse or delayed rulings would directly impair the growth premium embedded in the multiple.

2. **Execution and construction risk on the new generation buildout.** On-site construction begins mid-2026; cost overruns, supply-chain delays, equipment tariffs, and labor productivity are all flagged, with tariff-driven inflation explicitly called out.

3. **Customer concentration.** The Amazon and Alphabet contracts anchor the GenCo economics; termination or capacity reduction is a named risk, mitigated but not eliminated by termination payments and parent credit support.

4. **Financing / IG rating.** The plan requires significant additional financing; a downgrade triggers the collateral calls noted above and raises costs across the program. With interest expense already up sharply YoY, financing cost is the key swing variable, and the higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to a FCF-negative, dilution-funded utility.

5. **Operational and regulatory overhangs.** An IURC investigation into NIPSCO gas-meter accuracy (hearing July 2026), March 2026 storm damage to the Dunn's Bridge I solar facility (expected mostly insured), environmental CCR/MGP remediation liabilities (~$81M), and a brief NIPSCO union lockout in April 2026 (resolved) add discrete items to monitor.

The Q1 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K. The risk score sits at the low end of neutral because the regulatory approvals are genuine binary events with limited offsetting asymmetric upside already in the price.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 21.9% versus 18.0% one-year historical vol — modestly rich but at only the 23rd percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.9%, the put/call volume ratio is negligible at 0.05, and the term structure is flat (21.9% near vs. 20.9% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed with no concentrated hedging into the Q2 IURC catalyst. The 6-month skew turns slightly negative (-2.4%), consistent with modest call interest rather than downside fear. The options market is not pricing the regulatory catalysts as a high-variance event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** IURC approves the Amazon and Alphabet contracts cleanly in Q2–Q3 2026, the company announces additional hyperscaler offtake, GenCo construction proceeds on budget, and the Blackstone structure plus forward equity sales fund the build without IG impairment or excessive dilution. EPS compounds in the high-single digits as GenCo earnings ramp from 2027, and the multiple holds its premium. The stock reaches the consensus high of $56, roughly 20% above current levels.

**Bear case.** Regulatory approval is delayed or conditioned, consumer-advocate opposition extracts concessions that erode contract economics, construction costs inflate on tariffs and labor, and rising interest expense plus continued dilution compress per-share growth. Estimate revisions, already flat-to-negative, turn lower, and the premium-to-peer multiple compresses toward the 20x median. At 20x on $2.06, the stock trades to roughly $41, about 12% below current levels, with the dividend providing some support.

The base case sits between these: gradual GenCo ramp, mostly favorable but not frictionless regulatory outcomes, mid-single-digit EPS growth, and a multiple that holds near current levels. That yields a base-case target around $51–52, in line with consensus, for roughly 10% total return including the dividend over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a visible multi-year EPS-and-dividend-growth path anchored by data-center load, but the valuation premium and binary near-term regulatory events cap the asymmetry, so the stock is worth owning for the growth trajectory rather than as a fresh entry at this price.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 IURC order on the Amazon contract is the next natural reassessment point, followed by the Q3 2026 Alphabet order and the July 2026 gas-meter-accuracy hearing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clean IURC approval of both the Amazon and Alphabet contracts without economics-eroding conditions, confirming the GenCo earnings ramp.
- A pullback to the low-$40s, compressing the forward multiple toward the peer median so the data-center optionality is no longer fully priced.
- A turn in consensus revisions to positive (current-year EPS revised up >2%) as GenCo contributions enter estimates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- IURC rejection or material adverse conditioning of the special contracts, or termination/reduction by Amazon or Alphabet.
- Loss of investment-grade rating or credit-watch placement, triggering collateral calls and raising financing costs.
- Material GenCo construction cost overruns or schedule slippage that push the earnings ramp beyond 2027–2028, combined with a dividend-coverage strain.

## Conclusion

NiSource offers a defensible regulated utility core with a genuine, partly de-risked data-center growth engine that distinguishes it from gas-distribution peers. The Amazon and Alphabet offtake, the tracker-heavy recovery model, and the Blackstone co-investment structure support mid-single-digit-plus EPS growth and continued dividend increases (raised to $0.60/quarter).

What holds us back from a BUY is price. At a premium to the peer multiple and below the peer yield, NI runs FCF-negative on a $5B+ annual capex program funded by debt and dilution into a higher-for-longer rate environment, with three of the last four quarters missing consensus and revisions flat-to-negative. The data-center optionality is already partly in the multiple, and the binary IURC approvals are unresolved. The risk/reward is balanced rather than skewed. We rate NI a HOLD, with a clear path to upgrade on clean regulatory approvals or a re-rating toward the peer multiple in the low-$40s.
