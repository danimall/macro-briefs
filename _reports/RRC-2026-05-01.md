## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas and NGL producer trading at ~11.5x trailing earnings with one of the strongest balance sheet improvement trajectories in the E&P sector — net debt reduced from $2.74B in 2021 to $834M at Q1 2026. The Q1 2026 earnings beat of 19% ($1.52 vs. $1.28 consensus) was driven by record NGL premiums ($4.41/bbl above Mont Belvieu), natural gas realizations at a decade-high premium to NYMEX, and continued cost discipline at $2.07/mcfe total cash unit costs. Estimate revisions are strongly positive: FY2026 EPS revised up 25% over 90 days to $4.28, with FY2027 at $4.63.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) structural demand growth from U.S. LNG exports and emerging data center power requirements creating a higher floor for domestic natural gas prices; (2) a differentiated NGL marketing strategy generating real alpha through international export channels; and (3) a balance sheet that now supports aggressive capital returns ($1.5B buyback authorization remaining). The primary risks are 100% concentration in Pennsylvania's Marcellus Shale and $5.9B in transportation commitments that represent a massive fixed-cost structure. At ~10x forward earnings with proved reserve PV-10 ($11.6B) exceeding market cap ($10.1B), the stock offers reasonable upside with identifiable catalysts over the next 12 months.

## Company Overview

Range Resources Corporation is an independent natural gas, NGL, and oil producer operating exclusively in the Marcellus Shale of southwestern Pennsylvania. The company pioneered the development of this basin and remains one of the lowest-cost operators in North American gas production.

**Production mix:** ~69% natural gas, ~30% NGLs, ~1% oil. FY2025 production averaged 2.24 Bcfe/day, with management targeting 2.35–2.40 Bcfe/day in 2026 and a longer-term goal of 2.6 Bcfe/day by ~2028, representing approximately 20% cumulative growth over three years.

**Revenue composition (FY2025):** Natural gas $1.73B (61%), NGLs $979M (35%), Oil $106M (4%). An additional ~$173M comes from brokered gas marketing, which operates at a net loss.

The company's competitive moat derives from its position in the lowest-cost gas basin, decades of drilling inventory at current pace, and a diversified marketing portfolio that exports approximately 80% of propane and butane volumes to premium international markets.

Cash unit costs remained flat at $2.07/mcfe in Q1 2026 despite production growth.

### Balance Sheet Transformation

The deleveraging trajectory is the single most compelling financial element:

- **2021:** Net debt $2.74B
- **YE 2024:** Net debt $1.83B
- **YE 2025:** Net debt $1.22B
- **Q1 2026:** Net debt $834M (~0.5x EBITDA)

In January 2026, RRC fully redeemed $600M of 8.25% senior notes due 2029, eliminating its highest-cost debt tranche. Remaining debt consists of $500M in 4.75% senior notes plus $334M drawn on the credit facility ($3.0B borrowing base, $2.0B committed). Interest expense dropped 36% YoY in Q1 2026 to $0.09/mcfe.

The current ratio of 0.67 appears concerning in isolation but is structural for E&P companies with revolving credit facilities. Liquidity of ~$1.1B (undrawn revolver + cash) provides ample coverage. The retained earnings deficit narrowed from ($2.46B) in 2022 to ($909M) at YE 2025.

Cash taxes remain minimal through 2027 due to NOL carryforwards, adding approximately $100-200M in annual free cash flow relative to a fully-taxed position.

### Capital Allocation

FY2025 capital spending was $674M, well within the $650-700M budget. Free cash flow of approximately $500M was allocated to:

- Share repurchases: $231M (6.4M shares — 3.5x the $65M in 2024)
- Dividends: $86M ($0.36/share, up 12.5% YoY)
- Debt repayment: $609M of senior note retirement

Total shareholder returns (buybacks plus dividends) were $317M in FY2025. The 2026 capital budget is $650-700M with production ramping in H2 as new processing capacity comes online. Maintenance capex is estimated at $570-600M to hold production flat at 2.6 Bcfe/day once the growth target is achieved. The remaining share repurchase authorization of $1.5B represents approximately 15% of market cap.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Growth

The three-year growth plan targets approximately 20% cumulative production growth from ~2.2 Bcfe/day to ~2.6 Bcfe/day by ~2028. This is modest by historical E&P standards but appropriate for a company prioritizing capital efficiency and returns. The 2026 target of 2.35-2.40 Bcfe/day represents 5-7% YoY growth.

Growth capital is front-loaded, with a second completion crew added at mid-year 2026. Once the 2.6 Bcfe/day target is reached, the company can sustain that production level with $570-600M annual capex, generating substantial free cash flow at current strip prices.

### Demand Catalysts

**LNG Exports:** New export facilities commissioned in 2025 were the primary driver of higher domestic gas prices. Additional capacity coming online through 2027 creates incremental demand pull for Appalachian gas.

**Data Centers:** Management disclosed active dialogue on over a dozen potential data center/power generation direct-supply projects. A 75 Mmcf/day commitment has been secured. At full utilization, this represents ~3% of current production — small individually but with potential to become material as the pipeline develops. This is early-stage but real.

**NGL Export Demand:** Over half of exported propane/butane is on medium-term contracts linked to European (ARA) and Asian (FEI) indices, providing structural demand support independent of domestic pricing.

## Competitive Landscape

RRC's competitive position centers on cost leadership. Total cash unit costs of $2.07/mcfe in Q1 2026 make it one of the cheapest gas producers in North America. The Marcellus Shale's geological superiority (high EUR per well, low decline rates, proximity to East Coast demand centers) provides structural cost advantages that competitors in less favorable basins cannot easily replicate.

The NGL marketing strategy is an underappreciated competitive advantage. By accessing premium international markets through Gulf Coast export facilities, RRC generated a record $4.41/bbl premium above Mont Belvieu in Q1 2026, which most Appalachian peers cannot replicate due to infrastructure limitations and marketing contracts. Management raised NGL premium guidance to $1.25-$2.50/bbl for FY2026, translating to approximately $160M in incremental annual cash flow.

Environmental leadership (net zero Scope 1 & 2 GHG emissions, MiQ "A" certification) may become increasingly valuable as European and Asian LNG buyers impose carbon intensity requirements on sourcing. This is speculative but directionally positive.

The primary competitive weakness is basin concentration. Competitors like EQT and CNX also operate primarily in Appalachia, while larger diversified E&Ps have more flexible balance sheets and broader hedging capabilities.

## Risk Assessment

### Single-Basin Regulatory Risk (High Impact, Moderate Probability)

100% of proved reserves sit in Pennsylvania's Marcellus Shale. Active threats include: Cecil Township's ordinance increasing setback distances from 500 to 2,500 feet (adopted November 2024), a statewide citizen petition to expand setback distances accepted for review by the PA Environmental Quality Board in December 2025, and the persistent risk of a Pennsylvania severance tax. Any of these could impair production capacity or economics. A hypothetical statewide hydraulic fracturing ban would be existential, though probability remains very low (<5%).

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (High Impact, Moderate Probability)

Revenue swung from $5.3B (2022) to $2.3B (2024) purely on commodity prices. At the current ~$5/mcf NYMEX level, earnings power is robust. A return to $2.50/mcf — which occurred as recently as 2024 — would compress EPS to approximately $1.00-1.50, implying the stock could trade at $15-20 in a sustained low-price environment. The $5.9B transportation commitment base means fixed costs cannot be reduced proportionally.

### Transportation Commitment Overhang ($5.9B)

This is the most material structural risk. $5.86B in transportation, gathering, and processing commitments — nearly 60% of market cap — extend over a decade ($2.16B due after 2030). These are "ship-or-pay" obligations: if production is curtailed for any reason, cash outflows continue. This creates significant downside asymmetry in adverse scenarios.

### Third-Party Infrastructure Dependency

The 10-K explicitly warns that disruption of certain third-party gas processing facilities could require curtailment of "a significant amount" of production. Business interruption insurance is limited for this risk. This is a low-probability, high-impact scenario.

### Management Retention Risk

The 10-K notes that none of the senior management team has employment agreements, creating retention risk — a factor underscored by the recent departure of VP Kavanaugh.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 36.0% sits 3.5pp above 1-year historical volatility of 32.5%, modestly rich. The term structure is in backwardation (36.0% near-term vs. 32.8% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a near-term catalyst or uncertainty, likely related to commodity price volatility. Put/call volume ratio of 0.40 and OI ratio of 0.44 indicate call-heavy positioning, consistent with recent price strength. The balanced put/call skew (-1.8%) suggests no significant directional fear. The options market is not contradicting a constructive fundamental view.

The IV compression from the prior snapshot (243.9% → 36.0%) reflects resolution of the earnings event on April 21.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-59; +29-38%)

Natural gas prices sustain above $4.50/mcf through 2027 on LNG export demand and data center power requirements. NGL premiums remain at or above the $1.25-$2.50 guidance range. Production ramp to 2.6 Bcfe/day proceeds on schedule. Net debt falls below $500M by YE 2026, enabling acceleration of buybacks against the $1.5B authorization. Data center supply contracts materialize at scale (several hundred Mmcf/day within 24 months). Multiple expands toward the peer median of 14-16x forward earnings as the market recognizes the balance sheet transformation and differentiated NGL strategy. Math: 14x $4.28 CY2026E = $59.90.

### Bear Case ($28-32; -25-34%)

Natural gas prices retreat to $2.50-3.00/mcf on warmer winters, slowing LNG demand, or economic contraction. NGL premiums normalize as international benchmarks converge with Mont Belvieu. Pennsylvania enacts meaningful setback legislation or a severance tax, impairing production economics. The $5.9B transportation commitment base creates cash flow compression at lower production volumes. Commodity price-driven EPS compression to $1.50-2.00 at trough pricing. Math: 10x $2.00 trough = $20; realistic floor with support from PV-10 asset value ~$28-32.

## Conclusion

Range Resources has executed a compelling balance sheet transformation while positioning itself as a low-cost beneficiary of structural gas demand growth from LNG exports and data centers. At ~10x forward earnings with an improving estimate trajectory and PV-10 reserve value exceeding market cap, the stock offers reasonable upside with identified catalysts.

The recommendation is BUY with 2-3% position sizing, reflecting the commodity sensitivity and single-basin concentration risk that temper conviction. The previous score trajectory (6.2 → 6.4, with an upgrade to BUY in April) is confirmed by the Q1 earnings beat and continued estimate revisions. The Growth Prospects score improves from 6.0 to 6.5, reflecting the data center demand channel and stronger-than-expected production ramp economics, while Management Quality is moderated slightly from 7.0 to 6.5 given broad insider selling (CFO, CEO, VP Kavanaugh departing) with zero open-market purchases — a pattern that does not reinforce the bullish thesis. The overall score improves marginally to 6.73, consistent with the improving trajectory.

Short interest at 8.6% with 4.3 days to cover is manageable and does not present material squeeze risk. The consensus analyst median target of $46 (8% above current) appears conservative relative to fundamentals; the $59 high-end target is achievable in the bull scenario.
