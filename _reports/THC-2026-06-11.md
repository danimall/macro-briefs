**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tenet Healthcare runs two businesses with diverging fortunes. The ambulatory surgery platform (USPI), with 541 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals across 37 states, is the highest-quality for-profit ASC operator at scale, carrying a 31.7% segment operating margin and growing same-facility net revenue per case 5.6% on rising acuity. The legacy hospital segment is showing the first footprint of a policy-driven coverage-loss cycle: Q1 2026 ED visits fell 2.7%, surgeries fell 0.4%, charity/uninsured admissions rose 10.1%, and payer mix turned less favorable. The structural mix-shift toward outpatient is durable; the hospital headwind is just beginning.

The headline financials are doubly distorted. FY2024 operating income was inflated by a ~$2.9B divestiture gain, and Q1 2026 included a $413M one-time Conifer/CommonSpirit contract-termination payment. Strip both out and underlying adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat year-over-year ($1,162M vs. $1,163M in Q1). The reported diluted EPS of $8.01 (vs. $4.27) and the 9% year-over-year reduction in share count show that much of the EPS-growth story is buyback engineering layered on flat operating cash generation. That is sustainable while free cash flow and balance-sheet capacity hold, but it is not the same as organic earnings growth.

The stock has fallen from ~$238 in March to ~$165, a roughly 30% drawdown that reflects a policy-risk derating rather than a fundamentals collapse. At ~8.6x trailing EPS the multiple is undemanding, and the USPI franchise deserves a premium. But the EPTC subsidy expiration is already live and pressuring volumes, the most consequential OBBBA Medicaid provisions hit in 2027, management explicitly "cannot estimate" the OBBBA impact, and estimate revisions are mixed-to-modestly-negative on a near-term basis. Combined with a cluster of senior-executive departures and heavy insider selling near the highs, this is a balanced setup. We rate THC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Tenet is a Dallas-based diversified healthcare services company structured around two segments:

1. **Ambulatory Care (USPI)** — As of Q1 2026, interests in 541 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals across 37 states (415 consolidated), run on a JV model with physicians and not-for-profit systems. Core service lines are orthopedics, total joint, spine/MSK, GI, pain management, ENT, ophthalmology, and urology. This is the highest-margin, fastest-growing part of the company and the core of the bull thesis.

2. **Hospital Operations** — 50 acute-care/specialty hospitals across 8 states (concentrated in TX, FL, CA, MI), plus 132 outpatient facilities and an employed-physician network. This segment also houses Conifer Health Solutions, a revenue-cycle management (RCM) business.

The strategic arc of recent years has been deliberate: divest low-margin inpatient hospitals (including five Alabama hospitals and others in 2024) and reinvest in USPI. The economic logic is sound: outpatient surgery carries far higher margins than inpatient care, and the secular migration of procedures from hospital to ASC settings, driven by surgical and anesthesia advances, is a multi-year tailwind. Tenet is the best-positioned for-profit operator to capture it.

## Financial Analysis

**Underlying profitability is steady; headline comparisons are misleading.**

- **FY2025:** Net income to shareholders ~$2.37B (per proxy); operating income $3.508B; interest expense $821M, or 23% of operating income. The FY2024-to-FY2025 "decline" in operating income is an artifact of the FY2024 divestiture gain. Underlying continuing operations improved.

- **Q1 2026:** Net operating revenues $5.368B (+2.8% YoY); operating income $1.296B; net income to shareholders $702M; diluted EPS $8.01 vs. $4.27. Adjusted EPS of $4.82 beat the $4.18 consensus by 14.1%. The print included a $413M one-time "revenue from contract termination" from the Conifer/CommonSpirit Omnibus Agreement.

- **Segment divergence:** Ambulatory Care revenue +10.6% (acquisitions plus net-revenue-per-case growth and higher acuity), 31.7% operating margin. Hospital Operations revenue +0.5% with less favorable payer mix. The volume softness on the hospital side is the early signal worth monitoring.

- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow $1.641B in Q1 (vs. $815M), but ~$540M was the Conifer cash receipt. FY2026 adjusted FCF guidance (after NCI distributions) is $1.6B–$1.83B; capex $700–800M. A meaningful slice of consolidated cash leaks out as distributions to noncontrolling interests (~$197M in Q1 alone), the structural cost of the JV model; this is real cash that does not accrue to Tenet shareholders.

- **Balance sheet:** Total long-term debt $13.2B; D/E ~4.9x; Altman Z-Score in the distress zone (~1.6). This reads worse than it is. The thin equity is largely a function of aggressive buybacks (treasury stock $5.26B), and the leverage is well-managed: 100% fixed-rate debt except an undrawn $1.9B revolver, staggered maturities 2027–2033, effective rate ~5.7%, $2.97B cash. The nearest sizable wall is $1.5B of 5.125% notes due 2027, followed by ~$2.35B in 2028. Near-term solvency is not the concern; refinancing cost in a higher-rate environment and policy-driven EBITDA erosion are.

- **Capital allocation:** Buybacks are doing heavy lifting: $318M in Q1 (avg ~$236/share, above the current $165), $1.17B remaining on a $3B authorization. Diluted share count fell from ~95.0M to ~86.8M YoY, a major EPS tailwind. Recent repurchases were executed near the highs, a capital-allocation demerit. No dividend.

The earnings-surprise record is clean: three consecutive beats of 10.9% to 14.8%. That supports confidence in management's ability to hit adjusted numbers, but the beats are on adjusted figures that exclude the one-time items doing the optical work.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture splits cleanly. USPI is genuinely growing: +10.6% revenue, +5.3% same-facility systemwide net revenue, +5.6% net revenue per case. This is the durable engine, and the secular ASC migration thesis is corroborated across the for-profit hospital complex.

The hospital segment is at best flat and at risk of contraction. The combination of declining ED visits and surgeries against rising charity/uninsured admissions is precisely the footprint you would expect from the Enhanced Premium Tax Credit (EPTC) expiration, which took effect at the end of 2025. Uninsured net patient revenue dropped to 0.3% of mix from 1.0%, consistent with patients losing marketplace coverage and either delaying care or shifting to uncompensated channels.

Estimate revisions reflect this tension. Current-year EPS estimates rose +4.9% over 90 days (helped by the Q1 beat and buyback math) but have stalled over the trailing 30 days (+0.1%), and next-year estimates have ticked down (−0.5% over 30 days). The current-quarter estimate is down 1.3% over 90 days. This is a flattening rather than the +30% revision velocity that would signal a clean upturn, consistent with a company whose underlying EBITDA is stable while a policy headwind builds. We do not treat the estimate picture as a BUY signal here.

The Conifer/CommonSpirit transaction trades future revenue for cash today. Tenet receives $1.9B over three years and full ownership of Conifer, but forfeits its single largest RCM client on a contract that ran to 2032. Conifer's contracted performance obligations fall from $1.82B in 2026 to ~$91M/year thereafter, a forward revenue cliff for that business that the segment will have to backfill.

## Valuation Assessment

THC trades at ~8.6x trailing EPS of $15.49 and ~8.6x forward (the figures converge given flat underlying EBITDA). Against the Health Care Facilities peer median P/E of 17.8x, the discount is wide. But the peer set is heterogeneous and not a clean read: EHC at 17.8x and ENSG at 27.7x are post-acute/skilled-nursing models; UHS at 6.1x is the closest acute-care comp and trades even cheaper than THC, at a 42% discount to its 52-week high. The acute-hospital subgroup carries a structural discount precisely because of the policy and leverage risks Tenet shares.

The right way to frame the multiple: a low P/E on a hospital operator facing a known, dated reimbursement headwind (EPTC live now, OBBBA Medicaid cuts in 2027) is not automatically cheap; it can be a value trap if EBITDA erodes. The mitigant is that a large share of Tenet's segment value now sits in USPI, which deserves a materially higher multiple than the hospital business and is growing double-digits. A sum-of-the-parts view supports a valuation above the blended multiple. Consensus targets ($210 low, $245 median, $288 high) imply roughly 27% to 75% upside from $165, but those targets were largely set before the full weight of the policy derating and the soft Q1 volumes were digested, and the stock has fallen below even the low target.

Applying a normalized ~$17.81 current-year adjusted EPS at a 10–11x multiple (a modest premium to UHS reflecting the USPI mix) supports roughly $178–196 of fair value, above the current $165 but well short of consensus. The valuation is reasonable, not compelling. We score it 6.5: there is a credible case the market is over-discounting the USPI franchise, but the offsetting risk that hospital EBITDA contracts into 2027 prevents a high-conviction valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

USPI is the genuine differentiator and a defensible moat. As the largest for-profit ASC platform at scale, with a JV model that aligns physician partners and embeds switching costs, it is structurally positioned to capture the secular shift of surgical volume to lower-cost outpatient settings. The net-revenue-per-case growth on higher acuity shows pricing and case-mix power.

The hospital segment's competitive position is weaker and the headwinds are industry-wide. Soft inpatient/ED volumes, payer-mix deterioration, the EPTC expiration, and OBBBA Medicaid risk hit HCA, CHS, and UHS alike. Tenet's smaller, divested footprint (8 states, urban/suburban) reduces but does not eliminate exposure. Managed-care concentration is a specific vulnerability: ~70% ($9.7B) of hospital net patient revenue is managed care, the top 10 payers are 69% of that, and 67% of hospital receivables are managed care. Payer consolidation and vertical integration (insurers buying providers, narrow networks) tilt bargaining power toward payers, and Tenet flags rising denials.

In RCM, losing CommonSpirit underscores client-concentration risk and a competitive position under pressure against in-house provider teams and outsourcers. The competitive trajectory is improving in ambulatory and deteriorating in hospitals/RCM, netting to slightly above average given USPI's quality.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by government reimbursement policy, and several of the binary events are dated and not fully priced.

1. **EPTC expiration (live now).** Enhanced ACA marketplace subsidies expired at year-end 2025. The 10-K ties this to higher premiums, lower enrollment, and a shift toward uninsured/government coverage in 2026. Q1 already showed the footprint. This is an active, not hypothetical, headwind.

2. **OBBBA (enacted July 2025).** The most consequential Medicaid provisions (work requirements, caps on state-directed payments, limits on provider taxes, stricter eligibility) take effect in 2027. CBO projects millions losing coverage by 2034. Management states it "cannot estimate" the impact. This is the central overhang and the most likely driver of the derating.

3. **Medicaid DSH cuts** scheduled to begin October 1, 2027, separate from OBBBA. Medicaid supplemental/DSH payments were $304M in Q1 alone, material and at policy risk.

4. **Leverage.** Well-laddered and 100% fixed-rate, so this is a managed risk rather than an imminent one, but it limits flexibility if EBITDA erodes and refinancing in 2027–2028 occurs at higher rates.

5. **Labor.** ~20% of hospital employees unionized; California healthcare minimum-wage increases phasing in through 2028.

6. **Conifer revenue cliff.** A forward revenue headwind in RCM as performance obligations roll off.

The combination of a live coverage-loss cycle and a 2027 policy cliff that management itself cannot quantify justifies a low risk score (4.5). These are specific, dated, asymmetric downside catalysts, not generic sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. ATM IV rose from 40.4% to 54.0% over the past month as the stock fell 15.3%, and the put/call OI ratio flipped from 0.53 to 1.19. Near-term IV (54.0%) sits rich versus 1-year historical volatility (38.2%), and the term structure is in backwardation (54.0% near-term vs. 46.5% at six months), indicating the market expects imminent volatility resolving over the next several weeks. The 6-month put skew of −2.6% shows modest demand for downside protection over the policy-uncertainty horizon. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.3%. This configuration is consistent with a market that has de-rated the stock on policy fears and is hedging continued downside, not one pricing a clean recovery. It corroborates the cautious stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
USPI is a structurally advantaged, double-digit-growth ASC platform that the market is under-valuing inside a blended ~8.6x multiple. Underlying EBITDA is stable, not declining; the $413M Conifer item and the FY2024 divestiture gain are obscuring a steady engine. Buybacks below the Q1 average accrete EPS, and $1.17B of authorization remains. If the EPTC/OBBBA impact proves manageable, as Tenet's geographic mix and managed-care exposure may allow, sentiment recovers and a re-rating toward consensus territory is plausible. Bull case: ~$240–245 (+45–48%).

### Bear Case
The EPTC expiration is the leading edge of a multi-year coverage-loss cycle that intensifies when OBBBA Medicaid cuts and DSH reductions land in 2027. Hospital volumes and payer mix deteriorate further; managed-care denials rise; DSH/supplemental payments shrink. Underlying EBITDA contracts, exposing the EPS-growth story as buyback engineering that cannot continue indefinitely against falling cash flow and 2027–2028 refinancing at higher rates. The Conifer revenue cliff bites. A hospital operator with eroding EBITDA and ~4.9x leverage re-rates lower; the stock retests $145–150. Bear case: ~$147 (−11%).

### Net Assessment
The base case sits in between: USPI growth roughly offsets hospital softness, underlying EBITDA stays broadly flat, buybacks support EPS, and the policy impact phases in gradually rather than as a cliff. Fair value around $178–196, modestly above the current price. The upside to the bull case (~45%) is wider than the downside to the bear case (~11%), but the bear case carries genuine probability because the catalysts are dated and management cannot quantify the largest one. That asymmetry is favorable but not decisive, and it depends on policy outcomes outside the company's control. The balanced risk/reward and unquantifiable lead catalyst support a hold rather than a high-conviction buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected late July/early August), with the FY2026 guidance trajectory and hospital volume/payer-mix trends as the key reads. The 2027 policy cliff (OBBBA Medicaid provisions, DSH cuts) is the longer-horizon swing factor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Hospital-segment volumes stabilize for two consecutive quarters (ED visits and surgeries flat-to-positive YoY) and payer mix stops deteriorating, demonstrating the EPTC impact is contained.
- USPI same-facility net-revenue-per-case growth holds ≥5% while consolidated adjusted EBITDA inflects upward (not just buyback-driven EPS), with current-year estimate revisions turning positive again (>+5% over 90 days on an organic basis).
- The stock compresses further toward $145–150 on policy sentiment without a corresponding cut to adjusted EBITDA guidance, widening the risk/reward materially.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Management cuts FY2026 adjusted EBITDA or FCF guidance, confirming organic deterioration beneath the optical items.
- Hospital uninsured/charity admissions accelerate beyond the Q1 pace and managed-care denial rates rise materially, signaling the coverage-loss cycle is steepening ahead of the 2027 cliff.
- Leverage flexibility tightens (revolver drawn, refinancing at materially higher rates, or buyback authorization paused), or further senior-executive departures signal internal concern.

## Conclusion

Tenet owns a genuinely valuable asset in USPI and trades at an undemanding headline multiple, and the reported numbers understate the stability of its underlying earnings power. Those are real positives. Against them sits a live, dated, multi-year reimbursement headwind that management itself cannot quantify, hospital-side volume and payer-mix softness that is already visible, an EPS-growth narrative leaning heavily on buybacks atop flat operating EBITDA, a forward RCM revenue cliff, and a yellow flag from insider behavior: heavy C-suite selling near the highs and a cluster of senior departures, with no offsetting open-market buying.

The price decline is partly a healthy improvement in entry multiple, but it is not purely sentiment; the Q1 volume and payer-mix prints are early confirmation that the policy headwind is operating as the risk factors warned, and estimate revisions have flattened-to-negative on a near-term basis. A >30% drawdown on a stock facing a quantified-to-unquantifiable policy cliff suggests the sellers are not simply wrong. We rate the recommendation HOLD: the risk/reward is balanced, the largest catalyst is unknowable in magnitude, and the prudent posture is to wait for evidence that hospital trends stabilize or for a further price decline that more fully compensates for the 2027 policy risk. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
