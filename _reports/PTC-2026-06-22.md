**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PTC is a Boston-based industrial software company built around the "digital thread" — connecting CAD design data through PLM, ALM, and service workflows for roughly 30,000 discrete-manufacturing customers. The business is ~95% recurring, generated FY25 free cash flow of ~$857M at a ~31% margin (best-in-class for the software space), and grew organic annual recurring revenue (ARR) roughly 10% to $2.48B. The March 2026 divestiture of the ThingWorx/Kepware IoT businesses to TPG (~$531M plus up to $125M contingent, ~$463M pre-tax gain) narrowed the portfolio onto the high-moat PLM and CAD franchises and funded an aggressive buyback that retired ~3% of shares in two quarters.

The investment question is what to make of a ~48% decline from the $219.69 July 2025 high to $114.58, including a sharp ~14% two-day drop on June 11-12. Most of this is the unwind of an overextended summer 2025 run plus the mechanical loss of IoT revenue from the headline base, not fundamental deterioration. Headline ARR fell to +3% in Q2 FY26, but that is a divestiture artifact — ex-IoT organic ARR grew ~11% (CAD +~10%, PLM +~11%). The company beat consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters by 21-51%, and current-year and next-year EPS estimates are up 5.3% and 4.6% over 90 days. At ~14x forward EPS of ~$8.07, PTC trades at roughly a 65% discount to the application-software peer median P/E of ~40.8x, despite carrying a higher FCF margin than most of that cohort.

We rate PTC a BUY with disciplined sizing. The bull case rests on the gap between an unchanged-to-improving fundamental profile and a multiple compressed to a level that prices structural impairment. The constraints are real: discrete-manufacturing/tariff cyclicality is the dominant demand risk, ARR growth has settled into high single digits rather than the SaaS-premium double digits, management turnover (new CEO FY24, new CFO FY26) raises execution risk, and there is no obvious near-term catalyst to force the re-rating. We do not treat this as a maximum-conviction name, but the risk/reward at this price is favorably skewed and consistent with the only sector — Information Technology — where stock selection has reliably added alpha.

## Company Overview

Following the IoT divestiture, PTC operates as a single reportable segment concentrated in two product groups:

- **PLM / digital thread (~60% of software revenue):** Windchill (PLM), Codebeamer and pure::variants (ALM / systems engineering), Arena (cloud-native PLM), ServiceMax (service lifecycle management), and Servigistics (service-parts optimization).
- **CAD:** Creo (on-premise and Creo+ SaaS) and Onshape (cloud-native CAD).

PTC sells primarily through a direct sales force (~75% of bookings) with a partner/SI ecosystem. Core verticals are discrete manufacturing — industrials, federal/aerospace and defense, automotive, electronics and high-tech, and medical devices. The competitive set is Dassault Systèmes and Siemens Digital Industries (PLM/CAD), Autodesk (CAD), IBM/Jama/Siemens (ALM), and Oracle/SAP/IFS/Salesforce (SLM). PTC's differentiation is the integration of CAD design data through PLM, ALM, and service workflows, plus cloud-native assets (Onshape, Arena) that are organically SaaS rather than retrofitted on-premise products.

The IoT exit is a deliberate narrowing. ThingWorx and Kepware lacked the scale to compete against hyperscaler IoT platforms, and selling them concentrates capital on franchises where PTC has durable share — PLM leadership through Windchill and a differentiated ALM position through Codebeamer as automotive and medtech move toward software-defined products.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the ASC 606 distortion.** GAAP revenue moved FY22 $1,933M → FY23 $2,097M → FY24 $2,298M → FY25 $2,739M (+19%), with Q2 FY26 at $774M (+22% GAAP, +15% constant currency). These headline figures overstate underlying momentum. Under ASC 606, on-premise subscription license revenue is recognized upfront at contract start, so reported growth is inflated in periods with a higher volume of longer-duration contract starts. The cleaner metric is ARR. Any model anchored to GAAP revenue will overstate the growth rate; ARR ex-divestiture is the figure to underwrite, and it sits in the high single to low double digits.

**Profitability and cash flow.** FY25 GAAP operating income was ~$982M (~36% margin); non-GAAP operating margin ~47.5%. Net income nearly doubled to $734M from $376M, with GAAP EPS $6.08 and non-GAAP $7.94. The center of the thesis is cash: FY25 FCF of ~$857M, +16% YoY, ~31% FCF margin. Management flagged a material positive cash-tax benefit in FY26-27 from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which directly supports the buyback and deleveraging program. FCF is the company's own primary performance metric, and the conversion of net income to cash is high-quality.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was reduced to ~$1.20B as of November 2025 from ~$1.75B: $500M 4.000% senior notes due Feb 2028, ~$469M amortizing term loan, and ~$301M revolver draw maturing Jan 2028, with ~$949M of revolver capacity unused. The balance sheet is goodwill-heavy — ~$3.49B goodwill plus ~$0.8B intangibles, roughly two-thirds of total assets, the legacy of the ServiceMax, Codebeamer, Arena, and Onshape acquisitions. This is why the Altman Z-Score sits in the grey zone (~2.5) despite strong cash generation. The grey-zone reading is a structural artifact of an acquisitive software roll-up, not a distress signal; the real watch item is impairment risk, not solvency. Net leverage is modest against annual FCF.

**Capital allocation.** PTC repurchased ~$300M of stock in FY25 and ramped to ~$626M in Q2 FY26 alone ($375M accelerated share repurchase plus $250M open market), retiring shares from ~119.5M at FY25-end to ~115.5M. A $2B authorization runs Oct 2024-Sep 2027, with a fresh $2B in place for Oct 2026-Sep 2028. Deleveraging and buybacks are proceeding in parallel, funded by divestiture proceeds and the cash-tax tailwind. There is no dividend.

**Earnings surprise track record.** PTC beat consensus in each of the last four quarters: +34.3% (Jun-25), +51.3% (Sep-25), +21.6% (Dec-25), and +26.4% (Mar-26). A consistent beat pattern of this magnitude raises confidence that current consensus is conservative, though the ASC 606 lumpiness that drives some of these beats also makes any single quarter hard to forecast.

## Growth Analysis

Underwrite this on organic ARR, which is running ~8-11%. FY26 guidance is ~8-8.5% organic ARR growth. That is a durable, high-margin compounding rate but below the double-digit pace that earns a SaaS-premium multiple, which is the central reason PTC's TSR has lagged its software peer set even as fundamentals held.

The growth drivers are: the secular digitalization of manufacturing (digital thread, model-based systems engineering, AI-assisted design); SaaS migration through Windchill+, Creo+, Onshape, and Arena, which carries both upside (net-revenue expansion) and execution risk; and the strength in Codebeamer ALM as automotive and medtech adopt software-defined product development. The Q2 FY26 PLM and CAD prints indicate broad-based rather than single-product momentum.

Estimate revisions are stable to modestly positive: current-year EPS up 5.3% and next-year up 4.6% over 90 days, against essentially flat 30-day moves. The near-quarter estimates softened (current quarter -3.3%, next quarter -10.5% over 90 days), which reflects the lost IoT revenue rolling out of the base rather than organic deceleration. This is a steady compounder with rising full-year estimates and a depressed multiple, not the +30%-in-90-days revision that would flag a strong standalone BUY signal. The growth score reflects a high-quality but mid-single-to-low-double-digit organic rate, marked down modestly from prior reads to acknowledge that ARR has settled below the SaaS premium tier.

## Valuation Assessment

The application-software peer median P/E is ~40.8x (Tyler 40.8x, Dynatrace 74.8x, Guidewire 63.8x, DocuSign 28.9x, Bentley 35.0x). PTC sits at roughly a 65% discount to that median while generating a FCF margin that exceeds most of the cohort. Even granting that PTC's organic ARR growth is slower than several of those names, a 65% multiple discount is far wider than the growth differential justifies.

The discount is partly explained by structure: GAAP optics that confuse the unwary, the goodwill-heavy balance sheet, manufacturing-cycle exposure that pure-SaaS peers lack, and the absence of a dividend. None of these constitute structural impairment of the franchise, which is the bar for treating a compressed multiple as a value trap rather than a mispricing. The ARR and FCF data show a healthy, growing business; the multiple prices something closer to terminal stagnation.

On absolute terms, ~$857M FCF against a ~$13.2B market cap is a ~6.5% FCF yield, rising with the cash-tax tailwind and the share-count reduction. Analyst price targets — mean $180.1, median $180.0, low $130, high $230 — cluster well above the current price, with even the lowest target implying ~13% upside and the mean ~57%. Rosenblatt's $190 target (Buy) was cut from $220 only mechanically to reflect the smaller post-IoT revenue base, not a thesis downgrade. We treat consensus as a reasonable floor here, given the stock trades at ~14x forward with rising full-year estimates, well below the 50x threshold where analyst targets become consensus optimism rather than independent confirmation.

We score valuation 7.0. The discount is genuine and the catalysts (continued buybacks, ARR proof points, eventual recognition that organic growth is intact) are identifiable, though no single near-term trigger forces the re-rating. We deliberately temper the score below an 8 because a "cheap" multiple is not a buy signal on its own; the case rests on the franchise quality and FCF, with cheapness as a tailwind rather than the thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

PTC competes in a consolidating PLM/CAD oligopoly. Dassault Systèmes and Siemens are larger and more diversified, and Autodesk is the scaled CAD competitor. PTC's defensible positions are PLM leadership through Windchill, the only major vendor with organically cloud-native CAD/PLM (Onshape, Arena) rather than retrofitted on-premise products, and a differentiated ALM franchise (Codebeamer) levered to software-defined products in automotive and medtech.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving within the core: the ex-divestiture ARR prints indicate PTC is holding or gaining share in its chosen verticals. The IoT exit removes a sub-scale business that was losing to hyperscaler platforms, which improves the average competitive quality of the remaining portfolio even as it shrinks the headline. The principal competitive risk is SaaS transition execution — Windchill+ and Creo+ adoption is unproven, and Autodesk, Dassault, and Siemens are pressing their own cloud offerings. AI-assisted design is an emerging vector that could either reinforce the digital-thread moat (more data flowing through PTC's integrated stack) or commoditize parts of the CAD/PLM workflow. We score competitive position 6.5 — a strong franchise with a stable-to-improving trajectory in its core, held below 7 by the unproven SaaS migration and the larger, better-resourced primary competitors.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Discrete-manufacturing cyclicality and tariffs.** The dominant demand risk. The 10-K explicitly names recently imposed and threatened tariffs, high rates, and FX as factors that could lead customers to delay or forgo purchases. PTC's end-market concentration in industrials, automotive, and electronics ties it directly to the manufacturing capex cycle.
2. **ASC 606 lumpiness.** Management states it may be unable to confirm expectations until a quarter closes. This makes GAAP results hard to forecast and prone to optical misses, which contributed to the ~6% post-earnings decline on the last report.
3. **SaaS transition execution.** Windchill+ and Creo+ adoption is unproven against intensifying competitive SaaS pressure.
4. **Goodwill impairment.** The ~$3.5B of goodwill means underperformance of past acquisitions could force a non-cash write-down. Not a solvency issue, but an earnings-headline risk.
5. **Management turnover.** New CEO (FY24, Neil Barua, former ServiceMax CEO), new CFO (FY26, Jennifer DiRico replacing long-tenured Kristian Talvitie), and a go-to-market reorg. Strategically coherent but raises execution-continuity risk.
6. **FX and geopolitical.** Significant non-USD revenue and explicit exposure to Israel operations.
7. **AI integration.** Both a competitive threat and a product-execution/liability risk, newly emphasized in the 10-K.

Most of these are partly priced after a ~48% drawdown. The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the manufacturing-cycle exposure and the management turnover are genuine, not-fully-priced idiosyncratic risks, balanced against a franchise with strong cash flow and a balance sheet that is goodwill-heavy but not distressed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 41.0% sits above 1-year HV of 35.7% (a 5.3pp rich spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.0% near-term vs 37.9% at six months), which the market typically uses to price an imminent event. The put/call OI ratio jumped to 1.33 from 0.59 as the stock fell ~19.5% since the prior snapshot, indicating hedging demand built into the decline. Skew is balanced (1.1% near-term), so this reads as event-hedging and post-drop caution rather than a directional conviction that the stock breaks lower. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.3%. For a buyer, the rich near-term IV argues against paying up for upside calls; accumulating the equity into the elevated-vol window is the cleaner expression.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$180-190, +57% to +66%):** Organic ARR continues compounding at ~8-11%, the market recognizes that headline weakness was a divestiture/ASC 606 artifact rather than fundamental deterioration, and the multiple re-rates toward a discounted-but-rational software level (~22-24x forward EPS of ~$8.50-9.00). The cash-tax tailwind and accelerated buyback compound per-share value. This is the consensus mean/median target zone and the Rosenblatt $190.

**Base case (~$150, +31%):** PTC re-rates partway, to ~18x forward EPS of ~$8.30, as continued ARR proof points and buyback support pull the multiple off the trough without a full recovery to the peer cohort. FCF and estimate revisions stay positive; the manufacturing cycle neither collapses nor inflects sharply.

**Bear case (~$100, -13%):** A manufacturing/tariff-driven capex slowdown pushes organic ARR growth below ~6%, SaaS-transition execution disappoints, or a goodwill impairment dents headline earnings. The multiple stays compressed at ~12x and forward EPS estimates roll over toward $8.00. Even here, the ~31% FCF margin and ~6.5%+ FCF yield limit the downside; the cash generation sets the floor.

The asymmetry favors the long. Downside to the bear case is ~13%, upside to base ~31% and to bull ~57-66%, anchored by a franchise that generates ~$857M of cash and is retiring shares aggressively at a depressed price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $150 (+31% from $114.58).** Math: ~18x forward EPS of ~$8.30, a partial re-rate from the current ~14x trough toward a discounted-but-rational software multiple, supported by ~8-9% organic ARR growth and ~$900M+ FCF.

**Bull case target: $180-190 (+57% to +66%).** ~22-24x forward EPS of ~$8.50-9.00, matching the consensus mean/median and the Rosenblatt Buy target.

**Bear case target: $100 (-13%).** ~12x forward EPS of ~$8.00 on a manufacturing-cycle slowdown, cushioned by the FCF floor.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** The re-rating depends on two or three quarters of clean ARR prints that strip out the IoT noise, plus continued buyback execution. The next earnings report is the first natural checkpoint for whether organic ARR holds in the ~8-11% range without the divestiture distortion.

**Upside/downside from current:** +31% to base, +57-66% to bull, -13% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Organic (ex-divestiture) ARR growth decelerating below ~6% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the manufacturing cycle is biting harder than expected.
- A goodwill impairment charge, or current-year EPS estimates revised down more than 5% over 90 days, reversing the current positive revision trend.
- Buyback pace materially slowing while leverage rises, which would undercut the per-share compounding that supports the base case.

## Conclusion

PTC is a high-quality industrial-software cash generator trading at ~14x forward earnings after a ~48% drawdown that conflates a clean portfolio refocus and ASC 606 reporting optics with fundamental deterioration the data does not support. Organic ARR is compounding at ~8-11%, FCF margins are ~31%, the company beat consensus in each of the last four quarters, and full-year estimates are rising. The ~65% discount to the application-software peer median is wider than the growth differential warrants, and analyst targets sit well above the current price.

The case is not without friction: manufacturing-cycle and tariff exposure is the dominant demand risk, ARR growth sits below the SaaS premium tier, management turnover raises execution risk, and no single near-term catalyst forces the re-rating. We rate PTC a **BUY with disciplined sizing**, consistent with the favorable risk/reward (roughly 31% upside to base versus 13% downside to bear) and with stock selection in Information Technology being the most reliable source of alpha. Accumulate on weakness; the elevated near-term implied volatility argues for buying the equity rather than paying up for call optionality.
