**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avis Budget Group is a highly leveraged vehicle-rental operator whose equity has become a special situation rather than a fundamentals-driven security. The company carries roughly $24.4 billion of debt against deeply negative stockholders' equity of −$3.4 billion (Q1 2026), an Altman Z-Score of 0.05 that places it firmly in distress territory, and a string of net losses: −$1.82 billion attributable in 2024 and −$889 million in 2025, with a further −$283 million (−$8.01/share) in seasonally weak Q1 2026. Liquidity is thin at roughly $915 million against the multi-billion debt stack.

The defining feature of the current price is a short squeeze, not the income statement. In April 2026 CAR surged from under $100 to an intraday record near $847 before collapsing roughly 48% in a single session and continuing down to the current ~$186. SRS Investment Management (the controlling shareholder, now supplying both the CEO and Executive Chairman) and Pentwater Capital (a 10% holder) together accumulated more than 100% economic interest through shares plus swaps and options, locking up an estimated 82% of the float. Short interest remains at 58.5% of float. The "market cap" of $6.6 billion therefore reflects a tiny, manipulated tradable float colliding with a crowded short, not a price at which the entire equity could clear. Pentwater itself sold millions of shares into the spike at $250–$702, monetizing the squeeze rather than endorsing the fundamentals.

We maintain AVOID, consistent with the prior three reports (4.0 → 3.5 → 3.1), and our overall score declines again to 2.7. There are genuine operational green shoots — revenue per day turned positive (+3% in Q1 2026), International segment Adjusted EBITDA rose 80% to $290 million in 2025, and the fleet is shrinking with discipline — but these do not offset negative book equity, refinancing dependence on the ABS market, recurring discretionary impairments that corrupt earnings quality, and a share price whose primary risk is violent mean-reversion. The stock is uninvestable on fundamentals and dangerous to trade on either side.

## Company Overview

Avis Budget operates three global mobility brands (Avis, Budget, and Zipcar) across approximately 180 countries, with an average fleet of roughly 684,000 vehicles in 2025 (declining to about 620,000 in Q1 2026 as management shrinks the fleet). The business reports in two segments: Americas (~76% of revenue, $8.9 billion in 2025) and International (~$2.75 billion). By brand, Q1 2026 revenue split roughly Avis 56%, Budget 38%, other (including Zipcar) 6%.

The single most important structural feature is that approximately 84% of the fleet is "risk" (non-program) vehicles: Avis bears residual-value risk on disposal rather than relying on manufacturer repurchase guarantees. This makes earnings acutely sensitive to used-car prices and to management's discretionary decisions on fleet aging and rotation. The extraordinary 2021–2023 used-car windfall (net income of +$2.76 billion in 2022 and +$1.63 billion in 2023) masked this fragility; its normalization is what produced the subsequent losses.

A leadership transition completed in mid-2025: longtime CEO Joseph Ferraro moved to a Board Advisor role effective June 30, 2025, with Brian J. Choi installed as CEO and Jagdeep Pahwa as Executive Chairman. Both are affiliated with SRS Investment Management, which operates under a Cooperation Agreement and holds a position large enough to approach the change-of-control thresholds embedded in the senior notes. The controlling owner now runs the company.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue is flat to declining: $11.99 billion (2022) → $12.01 billion (2023) → $11.79 billion (2024) → $11.65 billion (2025), a drift of roughly −1% per year. Q1 2026 revenue grew 4% YoY to $2.53 billion on +3% revenue per day, the clearest sign of a pricing recovery, though volume was down 1%.

The earnings collapse is a depreciation and impairment story rather than a demand collapse. Vehicle depreciation and lease charges rose from $828 million (2022) to $1.74 billion (2023) to roughly $3.0 billion in both 2024 and 2025 as abnormal used-car gains reversed. On top of that, impairments hit $2.5 billion in 2024 (including ~$2.3 billion from accelerating fleet rotation) and $518 million in 2025 (a write-down of U.S. EV rental vehicles tied to the elimination of EV tax credits under the OBBBA legislation). Two consecutive years of large impairments driven by management's own fleet decisions signal both the volatility and the discretion embedded in reported results.

Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes the recurring impairments, was $628 million in 2024 and $748 million in 2025. But Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was a −$113 million loss, worse than a year prior, reflecting higher operating, SG&A, and vehicle interest costs. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter (Q3 is the summer-travel peak), so a single weak quarter is not decisive, but the year-over-year deterioration at the EBITDA line is not the trajectory of a clean turnaround.

The balance sheet is the core problem. Stockholders' equity is deeply negative: −$3.13 billion at year-end 2025 and −$3.40 billion in Q1 2026. Part of this is structural (roughly $10.75 billion of treasury stock from years of aggressive buybacks under an $8.1 billion authorization), but accumulated losses have eroded retained earnings to $856 million in Q1 2026 from $3.85 billion in 2023. Total debt is approximately $24.4 billion (Q1 2026): ~$6.0 billion corporate plus ~$18.4 billion vehicle-program asset-backed debt through the AESOP/Avis Budget Rental Car Funding structures, with roughly $5.9 billion unhedged floating-rate. Corporate senior notes carry coupons of 5.75%–8.375%, confirming the sub-investment-grade credit profile. The Altman Z-Score of 0.05, Piotroski score of 2/5, and current ratio of 0.72 all point to distress.

Operating cash flow is robust (~$3.3 billion in 2025), but the business consumes enormous capital reinvesting in fleet, and financing is a continuous treadmill of ABS issuance and repayment. Liquidity of roughly $915 million ($528 million cash plus $387 million revolver) is thin against the debt stack, with most vehicle debt maturing within five years.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here in the conventional sense. Top-line revenue has declined for three consecutive years, and the Q1 2026 +4% print is early evidence of a pricing recovery rather than a structural acceleration. Revenue per day at +3% is the most encouraging operational data point, and management's fleet discipline (shrinking from ~684,000 to ~620,000 vehicles) should support utilization and per-unit economics if it holds.

Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum. Current-year EPS consensus has been cut from $9.66 (90 days ago) to $3.42 (a −64.6% revision) and the current quarter from $2.97 to $1.81 (−39.0%). The lone bright spot is the +9.3% 30-day revision to next-year EPS ($7.68 → $8.40), but next-year estimates were still cut −30.5% over 90 days. The earnings track record is poor: three misses in the last four quarters, including a −665.9% miss in Q4 2025 and a −13.7% miss in Q1 2026. This is the opposite of the profile (rising estimates plus accelerating revenue) that would warrant rewarding a depressed multiple.

The relative bright spot is International, where 2025 Adjusted EBITDA rose 80% to $290 million on better revenue per day and lower per-unit fleet costs, while Americas EBITDA was roughly flat. International is the smaller segment, so this does not move the consolidated picture decisively, but it is the cleanest evidence of operational improvement in the portfolio.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional valuation is nearly meaningless here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (−9.95) because the company is loss-making. Book value is negative, so price-to-book is not usable. The peer set (A.O. Smith, Armstrong World, JBT Marel, Federal Signal) trades at a median P/E of 25.1 with no leverage comparable to CAR's; these are not appropriate comparables for a negative-equity company with $24 billion of debt.

The more honest framing is that the equity is a thin slice of value sitting on top of an enormous, senior debt stack. With −$3.4 billion of book equity and a 0.05 Altman Z, the intrinsic equity value on a fundamental basis is plausibly far below the current $6.6 billion market cap. The market cap exists because SRS and Pentwater have locked up roughly 82% of the float, leaving a tiny tradable supply against a 58.5%-of-float short position, a recipe for price dislocation in both directions rather than a reflection of clearing value.

Analyst price targets reinforce the disconnect: the mean target is $132 and median $140, both well below the current $186, with a low of $85 and a high of $160; the entire target range sits below the current price. This is the rare case where consensus targets function as a ceiling, not a floor, because the price is squeeze-inflated rather than fundamentally supported. Barclays downgraded to Underweight in April 2026 with an implied downside of roughly 83% from then-elevated levels. We do not treat the cheap-looking forward EPS as a buy signal: at a distressed, negative-equity company with falling estimates and recurring impairments, a low multiple is a value trap. We score valuation 2.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. rental-car market is a tight oligopoly (Avis, Hertz, and Enterprise) where pricing discipline depends on industry fleet supply. The two public players (Avis and Hertz) are simultaneously working through post-pandemic fleet-cost normalization and EV missteps; Hertz's stock moved sympathetically with Avis on the Q1 print, underscoring shared industry headwinds. The competitive position is best described as stable-to-eroding: the brands retain global recognition and an entrenched airport-counter presence, but the residual-value exposure (84% risk vehicles), high leverage, and EV write-downs reflect operational missteps rather than a widening moat.

Longer-term structural threats cited in the 10-K include ride-hailing, car-sharing, and technology entrants. The International segment's EBITDA growth in 2025 is the clearest evidence of competitive execution, but it is the smaller business. On balance the franchise is durable enough to avoid a low competitive score, but there is no evidence of share gains or moat widening that would justify a high one. We score competitive position 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is among the most acute in the industrials universe, and much of it is not adequately priced because the share price is divorced from fundamentals.

1. **Capital structure and refinancing risk (most material).** Heavy dependence on the asset-backed securities market to fund fleet means any ABS disruption, rating downgrade, or decline in fleet collateral value could force early repayment or higher collateral postings. The high coupons on the senior notes show the credit market already prices elevated risk.

2. **Residual-value and fleet-cost risk.** With risk vehicles dominating the fleet, falling used-car prices flow directly to the P&L. Management has twice taken large impairments by accelerating fleet rotation, demonstrating both the volatility and the discretion embedded in reported earnings.

3. **EV and policy risk.** The OBBBA's elimination of EV tax credits diminished EV residual values and directly drove the 2025 EV impairment.

4. **Controlling-shareholder dynamics.** If SRS exceeds 50% ownership, it could trigger a change-of-control put on the senior notes (101% offer), a debt-acceleration risk tied directly to ownership concentration. Minority shareholders are along for the ride on SRS's strategy.

5. **Squeeze mean-reversion.** The stock already fell ~48% intraday from its April peak and ~12% on the Q1 print. With short interest at 58.5% and a locked-up float, both melt-ups and collapses are possible on thin volume.

6. **Earnings quality.** The novel Interpace Ventures VIE (created to monetize tax credits, using HLBV accounting, with $965 million of alternative ABS funding) adds opacity to an already noisy GAAP picture.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress and an elevated tail. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 69.2% against one-year historical volatility of 112.0%; IV looks "cheap" only relative to the company's own extraordinary realized volatility, not in absolute terms. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 69.2% > long-term 62.0%), consistent with the market pricing near-dated catalyst risk. Put/call skew is elevated at 7.6% and the put/call volume ratio is 2.30, both reflecting strong demand for downside protection that aligns with our bearish fundamental view. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.0%. The sharp collapse in ATM IV from 157% to 69.2% over the prior month reflects the unwind of the squeeze frenzy, not resolution of fundamental risk. The options signal corroborates AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The bull case is operational and narrow: revenue per day is recovering, International EBITDA grew in 2025, fleet discipline is improving utilization, the worst impairments may be behind the company, and a sophisticated activist owner (SRS) now controls operations and capital allocation. If used-car prices stabilize and pricing power holds into the seasonally strong Q3, consolidated Adjusted EBITDA could recover toward or above the 2025 level of $748 million, and the equity, as a thin, levered slice of a recovering enterprise, could re-rate sharply. The ATM equity program, if executed at elevated prices, would be value-accretive deleveraging given negative book equity. In a benign macro and a squeeze that refuses to fully unwind, the stock could trade meaningfully higher than fundamentals justify.

### Bear Case
The bear case is structural and far more probable. Negative equity, $24 billion of debt, thin liquidity, and recurring discretionary impairments leave little margin for error. Estimates are falling, the company missed three of the last four quarters, and Q1 Adjusted EBITDA deteriorated year over year. The price is squeeze-driven: Pentwater has already sold into the spike, short interest remains elevated, and analyst targets sit entirely below the current price. Once the float dynamics normalize or a credit/used-car shock hits, the equity could revert toward the $85–$140 analyst range or lower, a 25–55% decline from the current $186. A change-of-control debt trigger or an ABS-market disruption would be more severe.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The clearest dated catalysts are the next earnings report (following the April 29 print, the next quarterly report falls in late July/early August 2026, covering the seasonally important Q2) and any further dilution from the March 2026 ATM equity program. Estimate revisions are negative and the company has missed three of the last four quarters, so a continued miss or a soft Q3 guide could pressure the stock. The fundamental thesis, reversion toward the $85–$140 analyst range, is sound.

**Timing window**: A fundamental catalyst (earnings) is six-plus weeks out; the ATM overhang is open-ended. There is no single dated event with high certainty of resolution in the immediate term.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: This is the decisive factor. Short interest is 58.5% of float with 3.7 days to cover, and SRS/Pentwater have locked up roughly 82% of the float. The stock already demonstrated a 500–700% squeeze in April 2026. Shorting into a 58.5% short-interest, locked-float meme situation carries extreme squeeze risk that dwarfs the fundamental downside available.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options confirm the bearish lean (7.6% put skew, 2.30 put/call volume ratio, backwardated term structure), but borrow on a name this heavily shorted is expensive and recall risk is high. Carrying costs would be punishing.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Fundamentals support a target of $85–$140 (roughly 25–55% downside). But the upside risk from a renewed squeeze is unbounded in the short term; the stock printed $847 within the last 90 days. The reward:risk is unfavorable for an active short despite a correct thesis.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT**. The bearish fundamental thesis is correct, but 58.5% short interest, a locked-up float controlled by SRS/Pentwater, and a demonstrated 500–700% squeeze in the same quarter make this uninvestable on the short side. Avoid the equity entirely rather than attempt to short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. We do not see a fundamental basis for owning the equity at any point in the foreseeable future given negative book equity and the capital-structure risk.

**Re-entry criteria**: We would reconsider only on a combination of (1) restoration of positive stockholders' equity or a credible, executed deleveraging path that materially reduces the debt load; (2) two consecutive quarters of positive consolidated Adjusted EBITDA with positive estimate revisions; (3) normalization of the float and short interest (SI below ~15% of float) such that the price reflects fundamentals rather than squeeze mechanics; and (4) resolution of the SRS change-of-control debt-trigger ambiguity. A clean used-car price environment and demonstrated discipline against further fleet impairments would also be required.

**Timeline**: The capital-structure headwinds are structural and likely to persist for multiple years; the float/squeeze distortion could resolve in a matter of weeks to a few quarters, but resolution typically means a sharp price decline rather than an improved entry. We expect the equity to remain uninvestable on fundamentals through at least the next 12–18 months.

## Conclusion

Avis Budget is a distressed, negative-equity, loss-making rental operator carrying roughly $24 billion of debt and an Altman Z-Score of 0.05, whose current ~$186 share price is the unstable residue of an April 2026 short squeeze that ran the stock to ~$847 before it collapsed sharply. The operational improvements (revenue per day +3%, International EBITDA up 80%, fleet discipline) are real but immaterial against the capital-structure risk, falling estimates, recurring discretionary impairments, and a price disconnected from intrinsic value. Analyst targets sit entirely below the current price, and the options market is pricing imminent stress.

We maintain **AVOID** and lower our overall score to 2.7, continuing the declining trajectory (4.0 → 3.5 → 3.1 → 2.7). The fundamental case is straightforwardly bearish, but the 58.5% short interest and locked-up float make this a crowded short rather than an actionable one. The right action is to stay away from the equity on both sides.
