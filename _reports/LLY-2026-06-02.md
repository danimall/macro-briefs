**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is producing the most extreme revenue growth in large-cap pharmaceuticals: FY2025 revenue rose 45% to $65.2B, Q1 2026 accelerated to +56% YoY, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $82-85B (roughly 26-30% growth). Net income doubled to $20.6B in 2025; Q1'26 net income was up 168% YoY. The engine is tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), now reportedly the world's best-selling drug, which represented 56% of FY2025 revenue and 65% of Q1'26 revenue. Operating cash flow doubled to $16.8B in 2025. This is volume-driven growth (+50% in 2025, +65% in Q1'26) achieved while realized prices fell (−6% in 2025, −13% in Q1'26), a trade-off central to the forward thesis.

The valuation case has improved materially. At $1,082, the stock trades around 30x current-year consensus EPS ($36.18) and roughly 24x next-year ($44.47), against EPS estimates that have been revised up +5.7% (current year) and +5.6% (next year) over 30 days and continue rising. For a company compounding earnings at 25%+ with 83% gross margins and ~78% ROE, this multiple is below where consensus targets sit (median $1,251, mean $1,215) and well within the range justified by growth-adjusted peer comparison. The estimate-revision velocity combined with revenue acceleration typically precedes further re-rating as consensus catches up.

The reasons this is a disciplined BUY rather than a maximum-conviction one are concentration and two unresolved competitive/regulatory debates. Sixty-five percent of revenue in a single molecule means any safety signal, demand shock, or pricing action on tirzepatide would be destabilizing. Novo Nordisk has first-mover advantage in oral GLP-1, and Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) launch is off to a sluggish start, the first clear instance of Lilly trailing in a launch race. The November 2025 US government pricing deal plus accelerating IRA selections create genuine uncertainty about whether volume offsets per-unit price concessions. These debates are specific rather than boilerplate, which caps the risk score and warrants position sizing well short of maximum.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a 150-year-old Indianapolis-based pharmaceutical company operating as a single human-pharmaceuticals segment across ~90 countries with roughly 50,000 employees (12,000 in R&D). It runs four therapeutic franchises (Cardiometabolic Health, Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience), but the story is overwhelmingly cardiometabolic. Six products each exceed $3B in revenue (Mounjaro, Zepbound, Verzenio, Trulicity, Taltz, Jardiance), collectively 82% of revenue, and the incretin (GLP-1/GIP) franchise dominates.

The competitive position rests on best-in-class efficacy in the highest-growth category in pharmaceutical history, a deep incretin pipeline spanning oral and injectable formats and multiple mechanisms, large-scale manufacturing capital deployment as a barrier to entry, and a direct-to-patient platform (LillyDirect). Tirzepatide compound patents extend to 2036 (US), 2037 (EU), and 2040 (Japan), giving a long runway before generic erosion of the core franchise.

Profitability is world-class: gross margin ~83%, net margin ~32%, ROE ~78%. Operating cash flow reached $5.3B in Q1'26 (versus $1.7B prior-year).

The free cash flow picture requires context. FCF was only $9.0B in 2025 (a 13.8% margin) because capex surged from $5.1B (2024) to $7.8B (2025), with management guiding to "meaningfully higher" near-term spend as it builds plants across North Carolina, Wisconsin, Indiana, Virginia, Texas, Alabama, Pennsylvania, Ireland, Germany, and the Netherlands. Total debt rose to $43.4B (Q1'26) from $33.6B (2024) to fund this expansion alongside dividends and buybacks. The dividend was raised to $6.92/year for 2026 (a thin ~0.6% yield); $4.1B was repurchased in 2025 plus $2.3B in Q1'26, leaving $8.6B on a $15B authorization.

A note on screening artifacts: any model flagging Lilly on an Altman Z-Score of ~1.25 ("distress zone") would draw the wrong conclusion. That reading reflects thin equity-to-assets (23.6%), a function of decades of aggressive capital return depressing retained earnings, and a GAAP operating margin distorted by large non-deductible acquired IPR&D charges ($2.9B in 2025) run through the P&L. With 83% gross margins, $16.8B of operating cash flow, and a top-tier credit profile, bankruptcy risk is effectively nil. The cash generation supports a 7.5 financial-health score; the offset is the rising leverage and capex-driven FCF compression in a market still wrestling with elevated rates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has multiple confirming legs. The incretin market is the dominant structural growth story in pharma, effectively a Lilly–Novo duopoly. Mounjaro generated $8.66B in Q1'26 alone (+125% YoY), Zepbound $4.16B (+80%). China revenue grew 54% as Mounjaro entered the National Reimbursed Drug List, illustrating the volume-for-price playbook.

Estimate revisions confirm the growth rather than merely lagging it. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $36.18 from $34.24 thirty days ago (+5.7%) and from $34.66 ninety days ago. Next-year EPS rose to $44.47 from $42.09 (+5.6% over 30 days, +6.0% over 90 days). Current-quarter estimates jumped +9.0% in 30 days. Estimates rising broadly across all tenors while revenue grows 56% typically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to the fundamental run-rate. With earnings effectively doubling at ~24x forward, rather than a premium-multiple growth stock pricing perfection, the revision signal carries weight here.

The pipeline gives the growth durability beyond a single-product view. Retatrutide's TRIUMPH-1 Phase 3 (reported May 21, 2026) produced ~28.3% average weight loss (70.3 lbs) at the top dose over 80 weeks, with ~45% of patients achieving ≥30% loss, approaching bariatric-surgery efficacy. This positions Lilly to lead the next generation of obesity therapy in severe (Class III) obesity. Beyond that: eloralintide, lepodisiran, muvalaplin, orforglipron line extensions (OSA, hypertension, PAD), insulin efsitora, plus oncology (imlunestrant, olomorasib) and neuroscience (Kisunla in Alzheimer's). With tirzepatide leading injectable efficacy today and retatrutide extending it tomorrow, the franchise can defend its lead even if it cedes some oral share.

The structural offset is price. Realized prices fell 6% in 2025 and 13% in Q1'26. Management is explicitly trading price for volume and access, formalized by the November 2025 government deal and China NRDL inclusion. The central forward debate is whether expanded Medicare/Medicaid/cash-pay volume more than offsets per-unit concessions. Management itself flags this as uncertain in the 10-K, which is the right reason to keep the growth score at 8.5 rather than 9+.

## Valuation Assessment

At $1,082, Lilly trades at roughly 30x current-year consensus EPS and ~24x next-year, with a PEG below 1.0 against 25%+ earnings growth. Peer medians sit at 20.0x P/E (JNJ 26.1x, MRK 33.2x, PFE 20.0x, BMY 16.0x), but those peers face patent cliffs and low-single-digit-to-flat growth. Lilly's premium to the median is modest relative to the growth differential; MRK trades at 33x on far slower growth, which underscores that the market is not over-paying for Lilly's expansion.

The estimate-revision dynamic matters for how to read consensus targets. With the stock at a ~24x next-year multiple and a PEG under 1, analyst targets (median $1,251, mean $1,215, high $1,500, low $850) function as floors that tend to migrate higher as estimates rise, and they have been rising for four consecutive periods. The current price sits ~2% above the analyst current-price anchor ($1,061) and ~13% below the median target. The multiple is grounded in delivered, accelerating earnings rather than speculative growth extrapolation.

The valuation score is 6.5 rather than higher because two specific factors temper the asymmetry. First, the price/volume trade introduces a genuine wedge: if pricing concessions outrun volume, the 24x forward multiple compresses on lower realized estimates. Second, the stock has been volatile (52-week range $623.78 to $1,149.10) and now sits near the high after a +45% move off lows; while prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, the proximity to all-time highs means less margin of safety if either the oral launch or pricing debate breaks against the bull case. The math supports a base-case fair value around $1,250 (roughly 28x the rising next-year estimate, justified by 25%+ growth and best-in-class margins), but the path is conditioned on resolving the two debates.

## Competitive Landscape

Lilly leads the incretin duopoly on injectable efficacy: tirzepatide outperforms semaglutide, and retatrutide is poised to extend that lead into severe obesity. Compound patents to 2036+ protect the core franchise. Manufacturing scale (the multi-plant build-out plus ~$10B in take-or-pay supply commitments) is simultaneously a moat and a risk: it is hard for new entrants to replicate, but it embeds a large bet on sustained hyper-demand.

The competitive frontier is shifting to oral GLP-1s, which expand the addressable market because they are manufacturing-light and patient-friendly. Novo got first-mover advantage with its oral Wegovy, launching roughly three months ahead of Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron), which received FDA approval April 1, 2026. Early scripts show a sluggish start (3,707 in week one, 7,335 by week four), trailing Novo. This is the first clear instance of Lilly following rather than leading in a launch race, and because oral is the format that unlocks the mass market, the early-share data is the single most important near-term metric to monitor. It cuts against the "uncontested dominance" embedded in bull-case targets. Jefferies still views a "blockbuster first year" as doable, but the data is a caution flag.

The competitive-position score of 7.5 reflects a genuine divergence the trajectory data flagged: best-in-class injectable position and pipeline depth (supporting a high level), offset by the first real evidence of trajectory deterioration in oral, where Lilly is the follower. The prior 9.0 score assumed uncontested dominance; the Foundayo data justifies the step-down to 7.5, not lower, because retatrutide and the injectable lead remain intact.

## Risk Assessment

**Product concentration** is the dominant risk. With 56-65% of revenue in one molecule, any safety signal, demand shock, supply disruption, or pricing action on tirzepatide would be materially destabilizing. The 10-K explicitly flags that incretin volume fluctuations from channel dynamics or demand "can have a disproportionate impact."

**Pricing — IRA and the November 2025 government deal.** Jardiance was hit with a 66% Medicare discount effective 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio were selected (Jan 2026) for 2028 pricing, and Lilly expects additional significant products to be selected, accelerating erosion ahead of patent expiry. The voluntary US government agreement (finalized Q1 2026) lowers Medicaid and certain prices, commits to "more balanced pricing across developed nations," and created a Medicare GLP-1 "Bridge Program" (July 2026–Dec 2027). In exchange, Lilly secured a 3-year grace period from Section 232 pharma tariffs conditional on US manufacturing investment it was already making, an optionality that partially de-risks a macro overhang. Net financial impact is genuinely uncertain; the 10-K warns expanded-access revenue "may be insufficient to offset pricing concessions."

**Oral competition** (above) is a NEW risk where Lilly is, for the first time, behind.

**PBM access volatility.** CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound from preferred status (July 2025), agreed to restore it (Oct 2026) and add Foundayo (June 2026), a reminder of how rapidly formulary decisions swing volumes.

**Litigation.** Insulin pricing MDL; 340B disputes; Mounjaro/Trulicity/Zepbound product-liability MDLs (GI injuries and NAION) in E.D. Pennsylvania; an adverse Average Manufacturer Price qui tam verdict (Seventh Circuit affirmed, SCOTUS petition filed); Emgality patent reversal (Teva, Federal Circuit, April 2026). None deemed material to financial position, but the product-liability MDLs bear watching given franchise concentration.

**Manufacturing/demand bet.** The aggressive capex and ~$10B take-or-pay commitments assume sustained demand. If oral GLP-1s commoditize the market or demand disappoints, the 10-K warns of stranded capacity and contractual payment obligations.

These risks are specific and partly unpriced (the pricing-offset uncertainty and the oral-share question in particular), which sets the risk-factor score at 5.5, below the midpoint and the right place for a hyper-concentrated franchise facing a tightening regulatory and competitive environment.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,500):** Volume more than offsets pricing concessions as Medicare/Medicaid/cash-pay access expands; tirzepatide sustains hyper-growth; retatrutide gains approval and opens severe-obesity TAM; Foundayo recovers to a competitive oral share; FY2026 lands at the top of the $82-85B guide with continued estimate beats. At ~33x a next-year estimate revised toward $46-47, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case ($760-820, roughly −25% to −30%):** Pricing concessions outrun volume, compressing realized estimates; Foundayo continues to trail Novo and oral commoditizes the market faster than expected; a tirzepatide safety signal or demand shock emerges; the multiple de-rates toward 18-19x on a stagnating estimate. The concentrated franchise amplifies any single negative datapoint.

**Base case ($1,250, +15%):** Volume-for-price roughly nets neutral-to-positive; tirzepatide growth decelerates but stays robust; retatrutide progresses; Foundayo stabilizes at a respectable second-place share. ~28x the rising next-year estimate, validated by 25%+ growth and best-in-class margins. The market is currently underpricing the durability of the estimate-revision cycle, which is the specific mispricing the BUY rests on.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,250** (28x next-year consensus EPS of ~$44.47, justified by 25%+ growth, 83% gross margins, and PEG <1). Upside ~15% from $1,082.

**Bull case target: $1,500** (+39%) — high end of analyst range on top-of-guide delivery plus retatrutide and oral recovery.

**Bear case target: $760-820** (−25% to −30%) — pricing concessions outrun volume and/or oral share erodes, de-rating to 18-19x on a falling estimate.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to: FY2026 quarterly prints validating the $82-85B guide, ongoing Foundayo weekly-script trajectory, retatrutide regulatory progress, and implementation detail on the government pricing deal and Bridge Program (July 2026).

**Upside/downside from current ($1,082):** +15% to base, +39% to bull, −25% to −30% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters where net realized-price declines accelerate beyond −15% without commensurate volume offset, signaling the volume-for-price trade is failing.
2. Foundayo scripts failing to inflect upward through Q3 2026 (no recovery toward Novo's oral trajectory) combined with a Novo oral-share lead widening, confirming structural loss of the oral market.
3. A material safety signal or FDA action on tirzepatide, or a downward FY2026 guidance revision below $82B.

A move to AVOID would require trigger 1 or 3; deterioration on trigger 2 alone would move this to a growth HOLD pending resolution.

## Conclusion

Lilly offers the rare combination of an inflection-grade growth profile (revenue +56%, EPS roughly doubling, estimates rising +5-9%) at a multiple (~24x next-year forward, PEG <1) that does not yet price in the continued beats the revision cycle implies. The estimate-revision velocity plus revenue acceleration is the strongest forward signal in the data, and on a sub-30x next-year multiple it favors further re-rating. The trajectory of prior BUYs (six reports, scores 6.8 to 7.4) has been correct; the new data (Q1'26 results, raised guidance, retatrutide TRIUMPH-1) confirms rather than reverses the thesis, while the Foundayo slow start and the pricing-offset uncertainty justify the step-downs in competitive position (9.0→7.5) and management/risk scoring that the trajectory shows.

The recommendation is **BUY**, with disciplined sizing (suggested 2-3% position, scaled in rather than chased given fair IV and proximity to highs). The two genuine debates, volume-for-price and oral-GLP-1 share, are the items to monitor, and the bear case is real at −25% to −30% given franchise concentration. The asymmetry favors ownership: +15% to base, +39% to bull, against a bear case that requires a specific, monitorable failure rather than a general de-rating. The concentration and competitive risks are specific and not fully priced, which is why the risk score is held appropriately low.
