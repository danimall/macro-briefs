**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is producing growth rates that almost no other company at its scale can match. FY2025 revenue rose 45% to $65.2B, net income grew 95% to $20.6B, and Q1 2026 accelerated further — revenue up 56% to $19.8B and net income up 168% to $7.4B. The engine is the tirzepatide franchise (Mounjaro and Zepbound), which reached 65% of revenue in Q1'26. Consensus EPS estimates are rising across all horizons (current year +5.7% over 30 days to $36.18; next year +5.6% to $44.47), and the company has beaten estimates in each of the last four quarters by an average of roughly 19%. These markers — accelerating revenue, rising estimates, a clean beat streak — historically precede continued upward re-rating.

The valuation question is whether ~38x trailing earnings is justified. On forward estimates it is not as stretched as the headline implies: at $1,082 the stock trades around 30x the current-year consensus of $36.18 and roughly 24x the next-year $44.47, against 20%+ near-term earnings growth. That puts the PEG near 1.2, reasonable for a franchise leader with 83% gross margins and 78% ROE. The bear case is concentration and price: two products generate 65% of revenue, and price is now a persistent and accelerating drag (-6% in FY2025, -13% consolidated and -25% ex-U.S. in Q1'26) as the IRA, the November 2025 voluntary government pricing agreement, and PBM formulary decisions reshape the economics toward volume over price.

We maintain a BUY, lowering our overall score modestly to 7.2 to reflect that the risk profile has shifted toward a volume/access story whose key swing factor — the Foundayo oral GLP-1 launch — is trailing Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy on early script data. The thesis depends on volume continuing to swamp price concessions and on Foundayo scaling, particularly ex-U.S. The reward/risk remains favorable at current levels, but conviction is bounded by concentration and pricing dynamics that are no longer hypothetical.

## Company Overview

Lilly operates as a single reportable segment focused on discovery, development, manufacture, and sale of innovative medicines. The business is now overwhelmingly defined by its incretin/cardiometabolic franchise. In FY2025, Mounjaro ($22.97B, type 2 diabetes) plus Zepbound ($13.54B, obesity) accounted for 56% of total revenue. Six products each cleared $3B in FY2025 — Mounjaro, Zepbound, Verzenio, Trulicity, Taltz, Jardiance — together making up 82% of revenue.

Beyond cardiometabolic, Lilly carries an oncology portfolio (Verzenio, Jaypirca, Inluriyo), immunology (Taltz, Omvoh, Ebglyss), and neuroscience (Kisunla/donanemab in Alzheimer's). The competitive backbone is tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism, which delivers best-in-class efficacy, a manufacturing scale-up funded by aggressive capex, a deep late-stage pipeline (orforglipron, retatrutide, eloralintide, lepodisiran), and the LillyDirect direct-to-patient channel that captures cash-pay demand. Lilly's tirzepatide brand holds roughly 54.8% U.S. GLP-1 share, up from 47% a year earlier, versus Novo Nordisk's semaglutide at ~37%.

## Financial Analysis

The growth trajectory is exceptional and accelerating:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 28.5 | 34.1 | 45.0 | 65.2 (+45%) | 19.8 (+56%) |
| Net income ($B) | 6.2 | 5.2 | 10.6 | 20.6 (+95%) | 7.4 (+168%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | 11.71 | 22.95 | 8.26 (+170%) |

The composition matters. FY2025 growth was 50% volume against -6% price; Q1'26 was even more volume-weighted with -13% consolidated price (-25% ex-U.S. as Mounjaro entered China's NRDL). Lilly is deliberately trading price for volume to expand access. Gross margin held near 83% in FY2025, exceptional even for branded pharma, driven by favorable mix and improved production cost, partly offset by lower realized prices. R&D ran $13.3B (20% of revenue), up 21%.

Cash generation has scaled with the business. FY2025 operating cash flow nearly doubled to $16.8B; free cash flow was $9.0B after $7.8B of capex tied to the manufacturing buildout. Q1'26 operating cash flow was $5.3B. ROE was 77.8%, ROA 18.4%, net margin 31.7%, current ratio 1.58.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress" zone) is a false signal here. It is driven by high debt-to-equity (4.24x) and a low equity-to-assets ratio, both artifacts of debt-funded capacity expansion plus aggressive buybacks and dividends depleting book equity. A company generating $16.8B in operating cash flow at 83% gross margin and 78% ROE is not financially distressed. Total debt rose to $43.4B in Q1'26 from $33.6B at end-2024, funding manufacturing, while equity grew to $31.2B as retained earnings build. The real balance-sheet question is execution on demand, not solvency: strand the capex against demand that fails to materialize and the leverage becomes a problem, but that is a demand risk, not a covenant risk.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and disciplined. The dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized for 2026, a ~0.6% yield at current price). A $15B buyback authorized December 2024 had $8.6B remaining as of Q1'26 ($4.1B repurchased in 2025, $2.3B in Q1'26). Capex is set to rise meaningfully, with contractual manufacturing commitments that could require up to ~$10B over roughly eight years if specified volumes are not purchased. The tax rate is creeping up (16.5% in 2024 to 19.8% in 2025) on jurisdictional mix, OBBBA changes, and non-deductible IPR&D charges of $3B+/year.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision picture is the strongest forward signal here. Consensus current-quarter EPS rose 9.0% over 30 days to $8.81. These are not the explosive 30%+ revisions that flag a trough-earnings inflection, but they are broad, consistent, and rising across all four horizons, which is more durable than a single-quarter spike. Combined with revenue growth above 50% and a four-quarter beat streak (27.0%, 11.9%, 22.0%, 14.5% surprises), the forward setup favors continued upward revisions as analysts chase reported results.

Two structural drivers underpin the growth case. First, the incretin market remains vastly underpenetrated relative to the addressable obese and diabetic population, and the shift from injectables to oral GLP-1s could expand the market further by lowering cost-of-goods and broadening patient willingness. Second, the late-stage pipeline is genuinely deep: orforglipron (Foundayo, approved April 2026), retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3 endpoints met), eloralintide, lepodisiran/muvalaplin for Lp(a)/CV, efsitora (weekly insulin, submitted), oncology ADCs, and Kisunla in Alzheimer's. This breadth converts a two-product story into a multi-decade franchise.

The growth offset is price. Price erosion is accelerating, and the November 2025 government agreement plus IRA price-setting (Jardiance in 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio selected January 2026 for 2028) will compound it. The thesis requires volume growth to keep overwhelming price. So far it does, comfortably, but as easy year-over-year comparisons fade and pricing concessions accumulate, the spread between volume and price growth bears close monitoring.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing earnings are the wrong lens given the trajectory; the forward multiples are ~30x current-year and ~24x next-year consensus. With near-term earnings growth above 20% and a four-quarter beat history that argues consensus is conservative, the PEG sits near 1.2 — reasonable, not cheap, for a franchise leader with structural tailwinds.

Peer context illustrates the premium and the justification. The pharmaceutical peer median P/E is 20.0 (JNJ 26.1, MRK 33.2, PFE 20.0, BMY 16.0). Lilly trades well above the group, but those peers are growing low-single-digits to mid-single-digits and several face their own patent cliffs and pipeline gaps. Lilly is compounding revenue at 45-56% with 83% gross margins. A premium of roughly 1.5x the group on a forward basis is defensible for a substantial growth differential. The relevant comparison is the small set of franchise compounders growing this fast, where Lilly screens favorably.

Analyst targets bracket the call: mean $1,215, median $1,251, high $1,500, low $850. The median sits ~16% above the current price. On a stock with rising estimates trading below 30x forward earnings, consensus targets tend to lag and revise upward as quarters print; we treat them as a floor rather than a ceiling here. We anchor a 12-month base case to ~28x our next-year EPS estimate of ~$44.50, which implies roughly $1,245, in line with the median target and ~15% above current price.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity/incretin market is a two-horse race between Lilly and Novo Nordisk, now transitioning from injectables to oral GLP-1s. Lilly leads injectables decisively: Zepbound holds ~59.5% obesity share, and tirzepatide ~54.8% of total GLP-1 share and gaining, with share gains of roughly 8 points year-over-year.

The oral transition is where the competitive question sharpens. Novo's oral Wegovy reached the market with a roughly three-month head start and already accounts for ~31% of total Wegovy scripts, validating strong patient preference for a pill. Lilly's Foundayo carries a structural edge — no food or water restrictions versus oral Wegovy's fasting requirement, manufacturing scale, lower COGS as a non-peptide pill, and broad ex-U.S. distribution. But early script data is lagging Novo materially: Foundayo ran ~11,700-16,000 weekly scripts in week 7 (late May 2026) versus Novo's ~67,000 in its seventh week and ~146,000/week currently.

Two caveats temper the early-launch concern. IQVIA data likely undercounts ~80% of Foundayo's channel mix (45% LillyDirect, 35% telehealth); Lilly cited ~20,000 patients by late April against a much lower IQVIA tally. And reimbursement was the gating factor, with CVS Caremark coverage coming online around June 2026 — a concrete tailwind. Jefferies frames a "blockbuster first year doable" ($1.6B 2026 versus ~$1.2B consensus) with peak estimates near $30B and the largest opportunity ex-U.S. The competitive race is early and gated by brand awareness and reimbursement rather than efficacy, which favors the player with the better-profiled molecule and deeper distribution.

The industry backdrop is pricing pressure across the board — IRA Medicare price-setting, MFN/voluntary government agreements, drug-affordability boards, 340B expansion, and PBM formulary power. The entire sector is repricing toward higher-volume/lower-price economics, and Lilly is leaning into that shift rather than resisting it.

## Risk Assessment

**Incretin concentration.** Two products generate 65% of revenue. Any safety signal, demand shock, supply disruption, pricing concession, or superior competitor entrant on tirzepatide would be financially disproportionate. The 10-K explicitly flags that incretin volume fluctuations can have a disproportionate impact on results. This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk and the primary reason conviction is bounded.

**Government pricing — structurally new and expanding.** The November 2025 voluntary agreement with the U.S. government (finalized Q1 2026) commits Lilly to lower Medicaid and certain drug prices and to launch new medicines with a "more balanced pricing approach across developed nations." The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program runs July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027, and management states uptake is "unknown" and may not offset pricing concessions. IRA price-setting is escalating, with management expecting more products selected in future years. This converts Lilly from a pure pricing-power story into a volume/access bet, a meaningful change in the thesis.

**Payer power.** CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound as a preferred obesity medicine on some plans in July 2025, a concrete demonstration that formulary decisions can move the franchise.

**Patent cliff.** Trulicity loses key patent and data protection "in the next few years"; a December 2025 EU agreement-in-principle would reduce maximum data-protection periods.

**Foundayo execution and the FDA safety request.** The FDA asked Lilly to collect additional long-term safety data post-approval — manageable but worth monitoring, as it could shape labeling and physician confidence. The slow early launch versus Novo, even if overstated by IQVIA's channel blind spots, is a near-term stock sensitivity.

**Litigation.** Insulin pricing MDL; Mounjaro/Trulicity/Zepbound product-liability MDLs (GI and NAION optic-nerve claims) in E.D. Pa.; 340B litigation (Supreme Court petition March 2026); the April 2026 Federal Circuit reversal in the Emgality/Teva patent case (charge taken); and an affirmed AMP qui tam verdict. Lilly is predominantly self-insured for product liability.

**Manufacturing bet.** The capex scale-up assumes demand materializes; overestimation would strand capital and trigger contractual payment obligations (up to ~$10B). China supply-chain dependency adds tariff and BIOSECURE Act exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 41.8% versus 38.0% one-year historical volatility (rich by 3.7pp) at the 81st percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (41.8% near-term versus 34.7% at six months), pricing imminent event risk consistent with sensitivity to weekly Foundayo script prints and the CVS Caremark coverage onset. Skew is call-heavy (-7.3% put/call skew, put/call volume 0.64, OI 0.63), indicating positioning tilts bullish rather than defensive. The recent shift (P/C OI 0.49 to 0.63 as price fell 4.1%) shows modest hedging picking up, but the overall structure supports rather than contradicts the constructive fundamental view. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing or using volatility to structure positions, rather than against the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,500, +39%).** Volume growth continues to swamp price erosion; Foundayo scales faster than IQVIA implies as CVS coverage and ex-U.S. distribution ramp, validating the ~$30B peak estimate; the deeper pipeline (retatrutide, oral franchise breadth) extends the franchise well beyond tirzepatide; and consensus EPS continues to revise upward toward and beyond the $44+ next-year level. At ~33-34x a rising next-year estimate the stock reaches the Street high target.

**Bear Case (~$850, -21%).** Price erosion accelerates faster than volume can offset as easy comparisons fade and IRA/MFN concessions compound; Foundayo's launch confirms Novo's durable oral lead; a safety signal or label restriction on tirzepatide or orforglipron impairs the concentrated franchise; and the multiple compresses toward the high-teens peer range on a maturing growth profile. This maps to the analyst low and roughly 19x next-year EPS.

**Base Case (~$1,245, +15%).** Volume continues to dominate, Foundayo grows into a multi-billion-dollar product over 2026-2027 without matching the bull peak, estimates drift up, and the multiple holds near 28x next-year EPS as the volume/access story is validated quarter by quarter.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$1,245** — 28x our ~$44.50 next-year EPS estimate, in line with the median analyst target of $1,251.
**Bull case target: ~$1,500** — Street high, ~33-34x a rising next-year estimate.
**Bear case target: ~$850** — analyst low, ~19x next-year EPS on multiple compression toward the peer range.

**Upside/downside from current ($1,082):** +15% to base, +39% to bull, -21% to bear.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with key checkpoints at quarterly earnings (next print roughly late July 2026), the CVS Caremark coverage ramp from June 2026, and the Medicare Bridge Program window beginning July 1, 2026.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. If Foundayo weekly scripts fail to accelerate after CVS coverage onset (no meaningful step-up by the Q3 2026 print), undermining the oral-franchise leg of the thesis.
2. If consolidated price erosion accelerates beyond -15% while volume growth decelerates below ~35%, narrowing the spread that drives revenue growth.
3. If a material safety signal, label restriction, or adverse MDL development emerges on tirzepatide or orforglipron, given the 65% revenue concentration.
4. If current-year or next-year consensus EPS revisions turn negative on two consecutive monthly readings.

## Conclusion

Lilly offers a rare combination at mega-cap scale: 45-56% revenue growth, rising consensus estimates across all horizons, a clean four-quarter beat streak, and an 83% gross-margin franchise with 78% ROE. At ~24x next-year and ~30x current-year consensus EPS against 20%+ near-term earnings growth, the valuation is reasonable rather than stretched for the quality and durability on offer, and analyst targets (median $1,251) sit above current price with room to revise higher as quarters print.

The risk profile has shifted: the November 2025 government pricing agreement and accelerating IRA/MFN concessions convert this from a pricing-power story into a volume/access bet, and the Foundayo oral launch is trailing Novo on early (likely mismeasured) script data. These factors cap conviction and warrant the modest score reduction to 7.2 from prior readings. The volume-for-price strategy is working, the pipeline is deep enough to extend the franchise beyond tirzepatide, and the reward/risk at current levels remains favorable: roughly +15% to base and +39% to bull against -21% to bear. We maintain a **BUY** with disciplined sizing appropriate to the concentration and pricing risks, and we will reassess on the next earnings print and the Foundayo coverage ramp.
