## Executive Summary

Waystar is a healthcare payments technology company processing 7.5 billion transactions annually (~$2.4 trillion in gross claims), serving over 30,000 clients including 16 of the 20 U.S. News Best Hospitals. The business has undergone a meaningful financial transformation since its June 2024 IPO: net income swung from a $19M loss (FY2024) to $112M profit (FY2025), free cash flow nearly doubled to $283M, and Q1 2026 showed 22% revenue growth acceleration. Despite this, the stock has experienced significant volatility since its IPO at $21.50, trading well below its 52-week high.

The valuation compression is attributable to multiple factors: (1) PE sponsor secondary offerings totaling 55.4M shares in 2025, (2) broader macro uncertainty around healthcare spending, and (3) sentiment headwinds common to recently-public, acquisition-driven companies. Based on the most recent available market data, Waystar trades at a discount to where its growth profile and margin structure would typically place it within healthcare IT. Analyst consensus remains constructive, with available estimates suggesting a median target around $35-37.

Key risks include the heavy goodwill concentration (~91% of assets), variable-rate debt exposure ($1.48B at SOFR+200bps, partially hedged), potential commoditization of RCM functions by large tech AI entrants, and the fading tailwind from Change Healthcare's 2024 cyberattack. These are real but quantifiable, and at recent price levels, the risk/reward appears favorable toward the long side.

## Company Overview

Waystar provides a cloud-native, AI-powered platform that automates healthcare revenue cycle management across the entire payment lifecycle, from patient eligibility verification and prior authorization through claims submission, denial management, and patient payment collection. The platform's architecture is multi-tenant and microservices-based (>99.9% uptime), processing ~$2.4 trillion in annual gross claims volume.

The revenue model is highly recurring: over 99% comes from subscription or volume-based fees on 2-3 year contracts with automatic renewal and price escalators. Revenue splits approximately 51% subscription / 49% volume-based. Client concentration is low — top 10 clients represent only 10.8% of revenue. The company serves roughly one-third hospitals/health systems and two-thirds ambulatory/alternate sites of care.

Waystar's AI capabilities (branded "AltitudeAI") incorporate machine learning, large language models (including Google Cloud's Gemini), generative AI, and emerging agentic AI to automate workflows, reduce denials, and improve collections. The company has 500+ channel partner integrations with EHR/PM systems.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has accelerated.** FY2025 revenue was $1.099B (+16.5% YoY), driven by subscription revenue growth of 21.9%. Q1 2026 reported $313.9M (+22% YoY), reflecting both Iodine acquisition contribution and continued growth from existing and acquired clients. The company does not separately disclose the organic/inorganic breakdown for Q1 2026, though the FY2025 10-K noted Iodine contributed approximately $31M in post-acquisition revenue during FY2025. Full-year 2026 guidance of $1.274B-$1.294B implies 16-18% growth, which appears conservative given Q1's 22% pace.

**Profitability transformation is complete.** Operating margins expanded from 13.1% (FY2024) to 22.7% (FY2025). Adjusted EBITDA margins reached 42.0%, up 140bps. Net income swung from a ($19M) loss to $112M profit, driven by operating leverage and a $69M reduction in interest expense following IPO-funded debt paydown of ~$1B.

**Free cash flow quality is strong.** FCF of $283M (25.8% margin) represents nearly 2x the prior year level, reflecting low capex intensity (2.4% of revenue) and favorable working capital dynamics. FCF substantially exceeds GAAP net income due to non-cash D&A, indicating high earnings quality.

**Balance sheet carries acquisition-driven leverage.** Net debt/Adj. EBITDA stands at ~3.2x following the $1.26B Iodine acquisition (closed October 2025). The $1.48B first-lien term loan matures October 2029 at SOFR+200bps. The company maintains interest-rate hedges on portions of its floating-rate debt, including swap agreements covering $275M through March 2027. With $500M undrawn revolver capacity and cash on hand, liquidity is adequate. The goodwill/intangible concentration at approximately 90-92% of total assets is characteristic of acquisition-driven SaaS but represents impairment risk if integration fails.

**Net revenue retention was 112.0% for FY2025** (up from 110.1% in FY2024), confirming successful cross-sell/upsell execution and client stickiness. However, the most recent LTM NRR as of Q1 2026 was 110.5% (down from 113.5% for the twelve months ended March 31, 2025), suggesting some normalization in the expansion rate that warrants monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

Waystar's growth is driven by three vectors:

**1. Cross-sell/upsell to existing clients.** Management estimates they can approximately double revenue from the existing client base alone. With 1,391 clients at >$100K (up from 1,203), the company is actively moving clients up the revenue tier. NRR of 110-112% quantifies this at the portfolio level.

**2. New client acquisition.** Waystar serves ~1M of 7.5M addressable providers. The 85% competitive win rate and 74 NPS score support sustained new client growth. The Change Healthcare disruption brought 30,000+ providers to the platform via 48-hour emergency implementations.

**3. Iodine acquisition expands TAM into clinical AI.** The $1.26B Iodine acquisition (AI-powered clinical intelligence for documentation, utilization management, revenue leakage) expands beyond traditional RCM into clinical workflow automation. According to the acquisition press release, this expands Waystar's TAM by more than 15% beyond the current $20B addressable market. Management has also referenced the broader ~$100B RCM labor pool as a longer-term automation opportunity, though this represents a directional aspiration rather than a near-term software TAM.

**Risks to growth:** The Change Healthcare tailwind is quantifiably fading ($34M FY2024 → $11M FY2025). First renewal cycles for disruption-acquired clients occur in 2026-2027. Large tech AI entrants could commoditize basic RCM functions, though Waystar's proprietary data asset (7.5B annual transactions) and deep payer integrations create meaningful barriers.

The addressable market of $20B growing at ~5% CAGR, combined with Waystar's ~4-8% share, provides years of growth runway even at modest share gain assumptions.

Waystar's valuation depends heavily on the reference share price, which has been volatile in 2026. Available market data from permitted sources shows prices ranging from approximately $26-30 in January-March 2026, with a 52-week range of approximately $21-$48. Using approximately 192M shares outstanding and $1.48B net debt (per the Q1 2026 10-Q):

At a share price in the mid-to-high $20s range:
- **EV/Revenue (FY2026E):** approximately 5.0-5.5x on guided revenue of ~$1.28B
- **Forward P/E:** approximately 16-19x on non-GAAP basis (based on available Yahoo Finance data showing forward P/E of ~18.5x as of January 2026)
- **EV/Adj. EBITDA (FY2025):** approximately 13-14x

For a healthcare SaaS company growing 16-22% with 42%+ adjusted EBITDA margins, 25%+ FCF margins, and 110-112% NRR, these multiples represent a discount to high-growth healthcare IT peers, though less extreme than would be implied by trough pricing.

The stock has declined meaningfully from its 52-week high while fundamentals improved across every dimension. This dislocation reflects: (a) PE sponsor selling pressure from three 2025 secondaries, (b) broader small/mid-cap healthcare weakness, and (c) the macro overhang from potential government healthcare spending cuts.

Analyst consensus is constructive. Available data suggests a median target around $35-37, with a range of approximately $27 to $45. The majority of covering analysts maintain Buy or equivalent ratings.

## Competitive Landscape

Waystar operates in a fragmented $20B market with significant competitive advantages:

**Structural moats:** (1) Network effects from 7.5B annual transactions feeding AI models that improve with scale, (2) switching costs from deep integrations with 500+ EHR/PM partners and multi-year contracts, (3) proprietary data asset covering ~60% of US patients, (4) end-to-end platform breadth vs. competitors' point solutions.

**Competitive position is improving.** NRR expanded from 110.1% to 112.0% in FY2025 (though moderating to 110.5% LTM as of Q1 2026). Win rates remain at 85%. The Change Healthcare disruption provided generational market share gains. The Iodine acquisition extended capabilities into clinical AI, creating a more comprehensive offering than any single competitor.

**Primary threats:** (1) Large tech companies (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) deploying AI that could commoditize basic RCM functions — Waystar's response is to move up-stack toward autonomous revenue cycle and clinical intelligence. (2) EHR vendors (Epic, Oracle Health) integrating RCM natively — though this has been a threat for decades without materializing at scale due to the complexity of payer integrations. (3) UnitedHealth/Change Healthcare rebuilding post-cyberattack — this is the most proximate competitive risk for client retention.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Goodwill impairment risk (Moderate-High).** At approximately 90-92% of assets, any sustained stock price depression or integration failure triggers write-down risk. The single-reporting-unit structure means impairment hits the entire entity.

**2. Variable-rate debt exposure (Moderate).** $1.48B at SOFR+200bps, partially hedged through interest-rate swap agreements. The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses that a hypothetical 100bps change in interest rates would have affected quarterly interest expense by approximately $3.7M (annualizing to ~$15M), though this impact is partially mitigated by existing hedges covering portions of the debt through 2027.

**3. AI commoditization threat (Medium-term, High impact).** The 10-K explicitly identifies large tech companies with greater resources deploying AI tools that could commoditize core RCM functions. Waystar's proprietary data and integration depth provide 2-3 years of defensibility, but this is the key existential question for the decade ahead.

**4. PE sponsor overhang (Fading).** Three 2025 secondaries totaling 55.4M shares. EQT, CPPIB, Bain, and Advent retain significant positions. Additional secondaries are possible but the most aggressive selling appears to have occurred.

**5. Healthcare policy risk (Background).** Government cost-cutting, potential efficiency efforts, Medicaid changes, and evolving AI regulation create uncertainty around healthcare IT spending.

**6. Change Healthcare client retention (2026-2027).** The 30,000+ emergency-acquired clients enter their first renewal window in 2026-2027. Retention rates for these "crisis" clients may differ from organic acquisition.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35-40, +30% to +50% from recent levels)

If: (a) PE sponsor selling pressure clears (3 secondaries already completed), (b) Iodine integration demonstrates cross-sell traction in H2 2026 results, (c) the AI/automation narrative strengthens with product launches, then the multiple re-rates toward peer median levels. The consensus target range supports this scenario. The key catalyst is demonstration of sustained organic growth alongside Iodine integration benefits.

### Bear Case ($18-22, -15% to -30% from recent levels)

If: (a) Change Healthcare-acquired clients churn at renewal (2026-2027), reducing organic growth to single digits, (b) large tech AI entrants begin winning RCM deals from hospitals, compressing Waystar's win rate below 70%, (c) healthcare spending cuts under government efficiency programs reduce provider IT budgets, and (d) the Iodine integration underperforms, requiring goodwill impairment. Under this scenario, revenue growth decelerates to 8-10%, the market applies a compressed multiple reflecting lower growth SaaS, and the stock trades toward the low end of its historical range.

## Conclusion

Waystar represents a compelling risk/reward setup: a profitable, high-growth healthcare SaaS company with 42% EBITDA margins that has experienced meaningful valuation compression despite materially improved fundamentals. With 22% Q1 revenue growth, $283M in FCF, 110-112% NRR, and a $20B+ addressable market at low single-digit penetration, the stock appears undervalued relative to its growth profile and quality characteristics.

The risks — goodwill concentration, AI competitive threat, and variable-rate debt — are real but manageable. A director purchased shares at approximately $26.25 in March 2026 (~$189K open-market buy). Analyst consensus targets imply meaningful upside. At current forward multiples with 16-22% growth, the fears around PE selling, AI disruption, and healthcare policy uncertainty appear disproportionate to the valuation discount.
