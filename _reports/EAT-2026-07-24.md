**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brinker International executed a substantial operational turnaround in casual dining over FY2024-FY2025. Chili's, which generates roughly 90% of revenue and is overwhelmingly company-operated domestically, delivered +25.3% company comps in FY2025 with +16.0% coming from traffic, a rare result in a segment that has shown almost no structural traffic growth for a decade. Sales leverage on fixed labor and occupancy costs drove operating margin from 5.2% to 9.5%, lifting operating income from $230M to $512M and rebuilding stockholders' equity from negative $268M (FY2022) to positive $371M. The balance sheet is now clean: a fully undrawn $1.0B revolver, only $350M of 8.25% notes due 2031 (callable July 2026 and under active review for early redemption), and negative working capital that reflects the normal cash-tender restaurant model rather than distress.

The turnaround is now colliding with the law of large numbers. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q (quarter ended March 25, 2026) shows Chili's comps decelerated to +4.0%, with traffic turning negative at -1.2% and all growth becoming price-led (+4.6% menu price). Maggiano's, roughly 7-8% of sales, is in outright decline: Q3 comps -4.6% on -10.4% traffic, with nine-month segment operating income collapsing from $46.7M to $15.5M. Commodity costs (meat, seafood, poultry) turned unfavorable in Q3, and the sales-leverage tailwind that produced the FY2025 margin gains is fading as comps normalize.

At ~$185, EAT trades near a 52-week high (196.39) and roughly 84% above its November 2025 trough of $100.30, at about 18x forward EPS. That multiple prices in continued execution against very tough prior-year comparisons. The valuation, elevated short interest (17.4% of float), near-term implied volatility of 60.6% in backwardation, and cautious insider behavior (director selling, a fresh CEO 10b5-1 sale plan, no open-market buying) collectively describe a two-sided, uncertain setup rather than a clean buy or sell. We rate EAT a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Brinker owns, operates, and franchises two casual-dining brands. **Chili's Grill & Bar** is the flagship, contributing ~90% of revenue, with average unit volumes of $4.5M in FY2025 and only 8.2% of U.S. units franchised, so Brinker captures full restaurant-level economics and full operating leverage on comp swings. International Chili's is 99% franchised, providing a small asset-light royalty stream (franchisee sales +16.7% in nine-month FY2026, with ~24-28 international openings planned). **Maggiano's Little Italy** is a small, higher-check (~$39/guest) polished-casual brand of roughly 51 units concentrated in malls and banquet/special-occasion occasions.

As of March 25, 2026, the system spanned 1,632 restaurants (1,162 company-owned, 470 franchised) across the U.S., 28 countries, and two territories. The turnaround engine under CEO Kevin Hochman (ex-KFC US president) centered on menu simplification around five "core equities" (burgers, fajitas, Chicken Crispers, margaritas, and the viral Triple Dipper), the "3 for Me" value platform starting at $10.99, heavy streaming/social advertising, and improved staffing and hospitality. The strategy delivered traffic, not just price, in FY2025, the distinguishing feature of the recovery.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trajectory documents the scale of the recovery:

| Revenue ($M) | 3,896 | 4,133 | 4,415 | 5,384 (+22%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 160 | 144 | 230 | 512 |
| Operating margin | 4.1% | 3.5% | 5.2% | 9.5% |
| Net income ($M) | 115 | 91 | 155 | 383+ |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 422 | 679 |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | — | 414 |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | (268) | (144) | 39 | 371 |

FY2025 margin expansion came almost entirely from sales leverage on fixed costs: restaurant labor improved 1.4 points and restaurant expenses 2.8 points as a percentage of sales, with food/beverage costs flat at 25.3%. This is high-quality margin gain when comps are running +25%, but it is the same mechanism that reverses when comps normalize. Q3 FY2026 already showed food and beverage costs turning unfavorable (+0.6 point on commodity inflation) and restaurant expenses running unfavorable.

The balance-sheet repair is the most durable element of the story and is under-appreciated relative to the headline distress flags. Equity reached +$406M in Q3 FY2026. Brinker repaid the $350M 5.00% notes in October 2024 and now carries only the 8.25% notes. Management is actively assessing early redemption of the 8.25% notes (callable from July 15, 2026), which would trim an interest-expense run-rate already down from $65M (FY2024) to roughly $42M annualized. The Altman Z of 2.71 (grey zone) and current ratio of 0.31 look alarming in isolation but are structurally normal for a cash-tender restaurant with heavy operating leases: customers pay before payables come due, so negative working capital is a feature rather than a warning. Much of the optical 7.2x D/E reflects ~$1.3B of capitalized operating leases.

Capital allocation runs entirely through buybacks (no dividend). Brinker repurchased $343M (2.5M shares) in the first 39 weeks of FY2026 following a $400M authorization increase in August 2025, with ~$207M remaining as of Q3. Share count fell from ~44.5M to ~42.9M. Capex of ~$265M (FY2025) funds reimages, ~6-7 new company Chili's per year, and Maggiano's reimages. The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: three beats and one in-line print over the last four quarters, with beats of 7.8% and 8.2% in the two prior quarters, though the March 2026 quarter came in essentially at consensus (+0.1%), a possible early sign that visibility is tightening as comps normalize.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question is binary and time-sensitive: can comps re-accelerate off a brutal prior-year base, or are the easy gains behind? The Q3 FY2026 data argues the latter is the base case for the coming quarters. The composition deteriorated more than the headline: traffic turned negative while price carried the entire gain. Nine-month Chili's comps of +10.6% versus a +4.0% Q3 confirm the deceleration is intra-year and accelerating. Price-led growth with negative traffic is a lower-quality mix and more vulnerable to a value-focused consumer trading down or to competitors copying the "3 for Me" playbook, a risk the company explicitly added to its Q3 forward-looking language ("competitors employing our same strategies or discounting").

Maggiano's is a genuine drag rather than noise. Nine-month revenue is down 9.8%, and the two-thirds YoY collapse in segment operating income prompted a formal "Back to Maggiano's" rescue plan and the substantial elimination of banquet service charges in Q1 FY2026. It is small enough not to sink the company but represents both a profit headwind and a management-attention distraction.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but no longer suggest re-acceleration: current-quarter EPS revised +1.5% over 90 days, next-quarter +1.7%, current year +0.7%, next year +0.9%. This is stabilization, not acceleration, and well short of the revision velocity that would flag a strong buy signal. Consensus embeds roughly $10.76 for the current year and $12.45 for next year, implying continued mid-single-digit EPS growth largely carried by buybacks, price, and modest unit growth rather than a return to the FY2025 pace. The growth prospects score of 5.0 reflects a company that has completed its recovery and is now normalizing, with the primary forward lever being capital return rather than organic momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$185, EAT trades at ~18x forward EPS. Against restaurant peers this looks cheap on a headline basis: peer median P/E is 38.3x (Dutch Bros 102.6x, Cava 169.7x, Wingstop 38.3x, Cheesecake Factory 22.0x, Domino's 18.0x). But the peer set is a growth cohort with mid-teens-to-30%+ unit or comp growth, whereas EAT is a mature casual-dining operator whose comp growth has normalized to low-single-digits with negative traffic. The relevant comparison is Domino's at 18.0x, a similarly mature, high-return, buyback-driven franchise, and against that anchor EAT is fairly valued, not cheap. Cheesecake Factory at 22.0x is the closest direct casual-dining comp and trades at a premium, but with a dividend and less exposure to a single decelerating value platform.

The consensus target framework: current mean $191.19, median $190.00, range $139.00 to $224.00. The stock sits essentially at the mean, with the low target ($139) implying ~25% downside if comps disappoint and the high ($224) implying ~21% upside if momentum re-accelerates, a roughly symmetric spread that mirrors the fundamental two-sidedness. The estimate-revision signal is too muted (+0.7% to +1.7%) and the forward multiple too close to fair value to treat consensus targets as stale floors; this is not the cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates setup where targets lag reality.

Cheapness on a trailing basis is not a reason to reward this stock. The compression that would make it "cheap" versus the growth cohort reflects the market's correct recognition that its growth is normalizing, so the discount is earned rather than a mispricing. We would need a specific catalyst (comp re-acceleration, note redemption materially lowering the cost structure, or a Maggiano's turnaround) to justify a higher valuation score, and none is confirmed. We assign valuation a 4.5, treating the apparent discount to the growth-heavy peer set as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and noting the stock's run from the November trough has already captured the balance-sheet-repair re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Chili's won share in a stagnant segment by pairing a nationally-scaled value message with better execution and menu discipline (avoiding the constant LTO churn that plagues peers). That is a real competitive achievement and the FY2025 traffic gains are evidence of a widened relative position versus Applebee's, Outback/Bloomin', and Darden's brands. The question is durability. The "3 for Me" platform is inherently low-margin and replicable; the company itself now flags competitor discounting as a forward risk. Traffic turning negative in Q3 suggests the relative share gains have plateaued and competitors are responding. International franchising provides a small, growing asset-light royalty stream but is not material to the near-term thesis.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-softening rather than clearly improving. The moat of scale advertising plus operational execution is real but not structurally defensible against value-matching competitors, and Maggiano's decline signals the portfolio is not uniformly strengthening. This supports a competitive position score of 5.5: a market leader that gained share and is now defending it, with the trajectory flattening.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant, time-sensitive risk is decelerating momentum. Chili's traffic has turned negative, growth is purely price-led, and the sales-leverage that produced FY2025 margins reverses mechanically as comps normalize. Commodity inflation turned unfavorable in Q3, and the forward-looking section newly emphasizes tariffs and competitor discounting. Against a backdrop of caution for the mid-market consumer, a value-led concept that has lost traffic momentum is exposed to further softening.

Geographic concentration adds tail risk: 18.9% of company units in Texas, 11.8% in Florida, 9.2% in California, exposing Brinker to state wage regulation (California) and weather/hurricane disruption. Brand concentration is high given Chili's dominance of revenue; any food-safety or reputation event is not diversified. Maggiano's remains a profit drag with an unproven turnaround plan.

Governance and insider signals are mildly cautious. CEO Hochman's FY2025 "compensation actually paid" of $122.3M (versus $30.5M granted total comp) reflects extraordinary alignment with a 750.8 five-year TSR versus a 182.1 peer group, but the absolute magnitude could become a say-on-pay lightning rod if performance slows. Director Frances Allen sold ~$0.75M in May-June 2026 at $135-143, and Hochman adopted a new 10b5-1 plan (up to 120,000 shares through March 2027) on March 4, 2026, with no offsetting open-market buying even at prices well below today's ~$185. A steady cadence of executive-change 8-Ks (including a March 2, 2026 officer change) bears monitoring for team stability post-turnaround. Insider selling here is best read as de-risking after a large run rather than a strong bearish signal, but the absence of any buying is consistent with a "fully valued" internal view.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 60.6% sits rich against 47.4% one-year historical volatility (13.2-point spread) and in the 89th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (60.6% near-term versus 49.3% at six months); the market expects an imminent event, most likely the next earnings print. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±13.2%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%) and put/call volume (0.40) and OI (0.44) ratios are call-tilted, so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite 17.4% short interest and 5.8 days to cover. IV has fallen from 69.7% to 60.6% over the past five weeks as the stock rallied 18.6%, suggesting some resolution of prior uncertainty, but the elevated absolute level and backwardation confirm a binary setup into the next report. The combination of high short interest and neutral-to-bullish options positioning is a modest warning for shorts (squeeze potential) and a caution for longs (event risk).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$224):** The note redemption in July 2026 lowers the cost structure, buybacks continue to shrink the share count, "3 for Me" holds value share, Maggiano's stabilizes off the "Back to Maggiano's" plan, and Chili's comps re-accelerate as new menu and marketing initiatives lap the toughest comparisons by late FY2026. On ~$12.45+ next-year EPS at a modest re-rating toward 18x, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range as the market treats the turnaround as durable rather than cyclical.

**Bear Case (~$139):** Traffic continues negative, price-led comps stall against a spending-constrained consumer, competitors match the value platform, commodity inflation compresses margins, and the sales-leverage reversal accelerates as comps fall toward flat. Earnings estimates roll over, the multiple compresses toward the low end of the casual-dining range, and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target (~-25%), giving back a meaningful share of the 2025-2026 gains.

**Base Case (~$190):** Mid-single-digit comp growth largely from price, modest margin give-back offset by interest savings and buybacks, EPS growing toward consensus $12.45 next year. The stock trades roughly in line with the current price and consensus mean, delivering low-single-digit returns as capital return offsets decelerating organic momentum.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — the growth trajectory is normalizing and the setup into the next print is genuinely two-sided, without sufficient evidence for a confident directional call).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Chili's comp traffic returns to positive (>0%) for a full quarter, confirming the value platform is still winning share rather than pricing to hold sales.
- Confirmed early redemption of the 8.25% notes at attractive terms plus continued buyback execution, lowering the run-rate cost structure and improving per-share economics.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +5% over 90 days, or a pullback toward the mid-$150s that restores asymmetric upside to consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Chili's comps decelerate below +2% or turn negative, with traffic remaining negative and margins contracting more than 100bps YoY.
- Maggiano's decline deepens (segment operating income continuing toward breakeven) without stabilization from the turnaround plan.
- A guidance cut or a first earnings miss, breaking the beat streak and confirming tightening visibility.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings report is the natural reassessment point and the event the options market is pricing. Monitor the note-redemption decision (callable July 15, 2026) as a discrete capital-structure catalyst.

## Conclusion

Brinker's turnaround was real, and the balance-sheet transformation from negative equity to a clean, largely deleveraged structure is a durable improvement that lowers the company's risk profile versus its headline distress metrics. But the operational story has moved from recovery to normalization: Chili's traffic has turned negative, growth is now price-only, Maggiano's is shrinking, and the sales-leverage tailwind that drove FY2025 margins is reversing. At ~$185, near a 52-week high and up ~84% from the November trough, the stock trades at ~18x forward earnings that already price continued execution against difficult comparisons. Estimate revisions are stabilizing rather than accelerating, insiders are de-risking, and the options market is pricing an imminent binary event. The risk/reward, roughly symmetric between a $139 bear target and a $224 bull target around a ~$190 base, does not justify chasing the stock here, nor does the durability of the franchise and the ongoing buyback support a bearish stance. We rate EAT a HOLD and would want either confirmation that comp traffic can re-accelerate or a pullback toward the mid-$150s before turning constructive.
