**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avista is a $3.3B regulated electric and natural gas utility serving the Inland Pacific Northwest, with a hydro-heavy generation fleet and a constructive multi-state regulatory framework (Washington, Idaho, Oregon, Montana, Alaska, plus FERC). The core thesis is income, not growth. The stock yields ~4.8% on an annualized ~$1.97 dividend, carries a 0.23 beta, and benefits from decoupling and power-cost adjustment mechanisms that immunize most of its margin from weather and volume swings. Net income has risen from $147M in 2021 to $192M in 2025, and Q1 2026 net income rose 16% YoY to $92M.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the per-share growth story is weak and the one differentiated growth catalyst has stalled. Avista funds the equity portion of an escalating capex plan ($615M in 2026 stepping to $800M by 2028) through continuous at-the-market share issuance (~$90M planned in 2026), so rate-base growth leaks into dilution. Diluted share count rose from ~80.0M to ~82.6M year-over-year. Management's 2026 EPS guidance of $2.52–$2.72 sits below the $2.76 reported for 2025, reflecting the Colstrip exit reset and rate-relief timing. The 500 MW data-center opportunity that some investors treated as a growth premium was paused on June 12, 2026 after the Spokane City Council proposed a one-year data-center moratorium, and that capacity was never in the base capex plan to begin with.

We rate Avista HOLD with an income rationale. The dividend is covered and the regulated cash flows are durable, which supports owning the shares for yield and low correlation. At ~14.1x forward earnings against a ~4.8% yield with ~1% dividend growth and EPS guided flat-to-down, there is no asymmetric appreciation case. The September 2026 Washington multi-year rate plan hearing is the next fundamental catalyst, and a constructive outcome there is the most realistic path to an upgrade.

## Company Overview

Avista operates two reportable segments. **Avista Utilities** is the core operating division and contributes well over 90% of earnings, providing electric distribution and transmission plus natural gas distribution across eastern Washington, northern Idaho, and parts of Oregon. It serves approximately 429,000 electric and 386,000 gas customers across roughly 34,000 square miles. **Alaska Electric Light & Power (AEL&P)** is a small wholly-owned subsidiary serving ~17,600 customers in Juneau on an islanded, non-interconnected hydro system, contributing roughly $4M of quarterly net income. A small non-regulated arm (Avista Capital) holds ~$149M of venture and other equity investments.

The generation mix is hydro-heavy, a structural advantage as the region decarbonizes and as the Washington Climate Commitment Act and Oregon Climate Protection Plan tighten. Washington is the dominant regulatory jurisdiction and operates under a multi-year rate plan framework. Cost-recovery mechanisms (decoupling, the Washington ERM and Idaho PCA power-cost adjustments, PGAs for gas, and dedicated wildfire and insurance balancing accounts) are the source of the company's earnings stability and its low beta.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability has trended steadily upward. Full-year net income progressed from $147M (2021) to $155M (2022), $171M (2023), $180M (2024), and $192M (2025). The company-selected EPS measure rose from $2.10 in 2021 to $2.76 in 2025. Q1 2026 net income was $92M (EPS $1.11) versus $79M (EPS $0.98) in Q1 2025, an increase driven by general-rate-case margin and net investment gains in the non-regulated "Other" segment.

Revenue optics are noisy and not the right metric. Q1 2026 total revenue fell to $570M from $617M, but this reflects pass-through dynamics (lower wholesale prices, lower PGA/CCA rates, Colstrip line-item shifts). The relevant figure, utility margin, was essentially flat at $349M versus $347M, with gas margin up $5M offsetting electric margin down $3M. Weather was mild (heating degree days at 90% of normal, use-per-customer down 10–15%), yet margin held because decoupling deferrals (+$14M electric, +$17M gas) neutralized the volume loss. This validates the low-volatility earnings model.

Capital intensity is the defining feature of the balance sheet. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $179M against $150M of capex (up from $103M a year earlier). Base capex is guided to escalate: $615M (2026), $635M (2027), $800M (2028), $680M (2029), $710M (2030). This requires continuous external financing. The capital structure is 54.3% debt / 45.7% equity, total debt ~$3.3B against ~$2.78B equity, comfortably inside the 65% debt-to-cap covenant. Liquidity was $156M available under credit facilities at quarter-end, adequate but not generous against the capex ramp, with $385M of short-term borrowings outstanding.

The low Altman Z-Score and sub-1.0 current ratio that screening tools flag are normal for a regulated utility that finances long-lived rate-base assets with long-term debt and rolling short-term paper. Covenant compliance and investment-grade ratings are intact, so this is not a distress signal. The genuine financial-health constraint is dilution: Avista funds the equity portion of capex via ATM issuance (~$90M planned in 2026 alongside $230M of new long-term debt), which is a persistent drag on per-share growth. We score financial health at 5.5: resilient and covenant-compliant, but capital-inefficient on a per-share basis given the dilutive funding model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case, which drives the 5.0 score. Rate-base growth from the capex program is real, but it converts to per-share earnings only after netting dilution and rate lag. Management guides 2026 EPS below the prior-year figure. Estimate revisions tell the same story: the current-year consensus of $2.60 is down 0.8% over 90 days and next year's $2.79 is down 2.3%, with no movement in the last 30 days. The near-term quarterly revisions look large in percentage terms (current quarter +28.6% and next quarter +42.8% over 90 days), but these reflect normal seasonal re-estimation of small quarterly figures ($0.20 → $0.26 and $0.26 → $0.37), not an earnings inflection. There is no +30% full-year revision signal here.

The differentiated growth catalyst was the 500 MW data-center MOU targeted for late May 2026. That load would have been enormous relative to a system whose entire retail base peaks around 1.5–2 GW. On June 11–12, 2026, the Spokane City Council proposed a one-year data-center moratorium and Avista paused negotiations, citing the need for broader policy and community alignment. Two points matter for scoring. First, Avista is the electricity provider and evaluator, not the developer, so its role in the project is contingent on multiple parties and approvals. Second, the capex guidance explicitly excludes any large-load integration capex, so the data center was never in the base plan; it was upside optionality that is now clouded. The pause does not impair the base plan, but it removes the growth premium some investors had ascribed.

Avista sits within the AI/data-center electricity-demand theme that has re-rated the utility group, but as a small, hydro-constrained utility now facing local political resistance, its ability to capture that demand is limited. The peer-lagging three-year relative TSR (121 versus a peer group at 145) confirms the market has not viewed Avista as a growth name within utilities.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.40, Avista trades at ~14.1x forward earnings and ~15.7x trailing. Against the utility peer set provided (MGEE 19.7x, AWR 22.4x, CPK 19.5x, OTTR 13.3x, CWT 22.7x, median 19.7x), Avista screens cheap on headline P/E. But the discount is earned, not a mispricing. Avista yields ~4.8% versus a peer-median 2.6%, which is the market's compensation for a heavier dilutive capex funding model, a flat-to-down EPS guide, and peer-lagging TSR. The lone peer trading near Avista's multiple (Otter Tail at 13.3x) carries its own idiosyncratic earnings concentration.

A low P/E alone does not make the valuation score high, and I am explicitly skeptical of treating cheapness as a buy signal here because there is no independent appreciation catalyst to close the gap. The forward multiple sits below the growth rate would-be supporters might point to, but Avista's per-share growth is low single digit at best after dilution, so a sub-15x multiple is appropriate rather than anomalous. The value is in the yield.

Analyst targets frame a range of $37 (low) to $50 (high) with a $40.50 median and $41.67 mean; current price sits near the low end of that band, consistent with cautious sentiment. Barclays cut its target to $39 (Hold) in June 2026, reversing a $42 hike made two weeks earlier specifically on the data-center pause. These targets do not function as floors here because Avista is not an inflection stock with rising estimates; the targets and the price both reflect the same modest-growth, yield-driven thesis. We score valuation 5.5: fairly valued for what it is, with the yield providing downside support but no asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, Avista's moat is its regulatory framework and rate base rather than any product or pricing advantage. The trajectory is roughly stable: constructive Idaho (Aug 2025, ~9.5–9.6% ROE) and Oregon (May 2025) rate-case settlements, decoupling that protects margin, and the Colstrip coal exit completed January 1, 2026 in an earnings-neutral way that removes a long-term stranded-asset and carbon-compliance liability. The hydro-heavy fleet is a durable advantage in a decarbonizing region.

Offsetting these are friction points. The WUTC declined to modify the ERM baseline even as Avista forecasts power-supply costs running above base rates, meaning Avista absorbs some power-cost risk between cases and is recovering $65M of 2025 ERM deferrals over 12 months starting July 2026. Authorized ROEs of ~9.5–9.8% are modest, and the pending 2026 Washington MYRP request to move ROE toward 10.2%–10.5% faces evidentiary hearings in September 2026 with an uncertain outcome. The peer-lagging relative TSR signals the market views Avista as a laggard within its group. We score competitive position 5.5: a stable regulated franchise with no improving trajectory that would warrant a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Wildfire liability** is the single most important tail risk. As a Western utility with overhead lines in forested, windy terrain, Avista faces catastrophic ignition exposure. The Q1 10-Q flags the Orofino, Idaho fire (Aug 2023), where investigators concluded an electrical fault was the cause and Avista has a distribution line near the ignition point; the company reports no evidence of negligence and only a $0.2M suppression-cost claim so far. Dedicated wildfire-resiliency regulatory assets (~$27M) and a balancing account exist, but a major loss-of-life event of the PG&E/PacifiCorp type could overwhelm them. This is a low-probability, high-severity exposure that is only partially priced.

**Data-center policy risk** is the live, near-term issue: the moratorium proposal and negotiation pause add political and timing uncertainty to what had been the growth narrative. **Regulatory lag and ROE adequacy** remain ongoing drags given the ERM friction. **Power-supply and hydro dependence** matter with snowpack currently below normal, though the ERM passes most through with a sharing band and lag. **Climate regulation** is escalating: Washington CCA obligations have grown to $153M and Oregon's CPP is in litigation with Avista a party. **Capital-market sensitivity** is structural: the escalating capex plan makes Avista dependent on continued debt and equity access, and higher rates raise both financing cost and dilution.

The Q1 2026 YoY EPS beat was partly driven by net investment gains in the non-regulated Avista Capital portfolio (~$149M of hard-to-value Level-3 investments), which inject earnings volatility unusual for a defensive utility and warrant monitoring. We score risk factors 5.0: the wildfire tail and the freshly clouded data-center catalyst are specific, material, and not fully reflected in a ~14x multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$46–50):** The September 2026 Washington MYRP settles constructively at or near the requested 10.2%–10.5% ROE, ERM under-recovery is resolved, and the data-center moratorium proves temporary or is scoped narrowly, allowing the 500 MW load (and associated incremental rate base) to advance. Rate-base growth then translates more cleanly into EPS, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median ~19.7x, and the yield is supplemented by capital appreciation. This is the analyst high target of $50.

**Bear case (~$33–35):** A major wildfire ignition event creates liability that overwhelms the balancing-account mechanisms; or the Washington rate case settles below request and ERM friction persists; or rising rates raise financing costs and force more dilutive equity issuance, compressing per-share growth further. EPS stays flat-to-down, the data center is permanently shelved, and the stock drifts toward the 52-week low of $35.50 or below. This represents roughly 11–16% downside from current levels.

**Base case (~$40–42):** Avista delivers EPS in its $2.52–$2.72 guide, the dividend grows ~1%, the rate case settles at a modestly improved but unspectacular ROE, and the data center remains optionality without near-term resolution. The stock tracks the yield plus low-single-digit total return, oscillating within the analyst $37–$41.67 mean band.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD held primarily for income. The reassessment timeline is anchored to the September 2026 Washington MYRP evidentiary hearings and the subsequent settlement order, with quarterly earnings (next print in early August 2026) as interim checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Constructive Washington MYRP settlement at or above ~10.0% ROE with resolution of the ERM under-recovery, materially improving the per-share earnings conversion of the capex program.
- Spokane data-center moratorium lifted or narrowly scoped such that the 500 MW load advances toward a binding agreement, adding rate base above the base plan.
- Full-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive (>+5% over 90 days) on a sustained basis, signaling that rate relief is outrunning dilution.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material wildfire-ignition event creating liability that exceeds balancing-account capacity.
- Washington rate case settling well below request with persistent ERM under-recovery, leaving EPS structurally flat while dilution continues.
- A dividend coverage strain or guidance cut, or evidence that ATM issuance is accelerating beyond the ~$90M/year pace to fund the capex ramp.

## Conclusion

Avista is a well-run, defensive, hydro-advantaged regulated utility with durable, decoupling-protected cash flows and a covered dividend. Net income is rising steadily and the regulatory framework is constructive across five states. The reasons to own it are income and low correlation, not appreciation. Per-share growth is capped by a dilutive ATM-funded capex cycle, 2026 EPS is guided below 2025, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and the one differentiated growth catalyst, the 500 MW data center, has been paused on local political opposition and was never in the base capex plan. Against ~14x forward earnings, the valuation is fair rather than cheap once the earned yield premium is accounted for.

We rate Avista **HOLD** on an income rationale. The dividend and stable cash flows justify continued ownership for yield-oriented investors, but there is no asymmetric upside case at current levels and no near-term catalyst likely to drive multiple expansion before the September 2026 rate-case outcome. New capital lacks a compelling entry point; existing holders are reasonably compensated to wait for the rate case.
