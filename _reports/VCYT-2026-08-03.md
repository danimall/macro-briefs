## Executive Summary

Veracyte reported a strong second quarter on July 30, 2026: revenue $150.3M (+15%), gross margin 72.2% versus 69.0%, GAAP operating income of $23.0M against a $5.3M loss, adjusted EBITDA of $44.0M (29.2% margin), and raised full-year revenue guidance to $590-596M from $582-592M. The stock rose to $59.78 that day and then fell 22.5% on July 31 to $46.32 on 6.0M shares against a 1.04M average. Nothing in the 10-Q filed that morning, in the guidance raise, or in any disclosure I can identify accounts for a move of that size. That gap is the single most important caveat in this report, and it argues against deploying new capital regardless of how the fundamentals read.

What I can identify is a specific, dated, quantifiable structural event that the market has legitimate reason to price. Veracyte's PAMA data-reporting window for its three principal Medicare codes — CPT 81546 (Afirma), 81542 (Decipher Prostate), and 0016U (Decipher Bladder) — ran May 1 through July 31, 2026, based on private-payer rates collected in the first half of 2025, with revised Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule rates expected to take effect January 1, 2027 for three years. Afirma has been reimbursed at $3,600 since 2018 and Decipher Prostate at $3,873 since 2021, both set by gapfill/crosswalk rather than by private-payer medians. Medicare fee-for-service is 34% of testing revenue, roughly $197M on the FY2026 guide. A 10% rate reduction removes ~$19.7M of revenue and, at ~90% incremental gross margin against largely fixed operating expense, ~13% of guided adjusted EBITDA. A 20% reduction removes ~$39M and ~26% of EBITDA, with zero volume decline. Consensus corroborates that this is the live question: FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $1.93 against FY2026's $1.92 implies no earnings growth next year at a company growing testing revenue 17-18% this year.

The business itself is in good shape. Debt-free with $485.2M of cash and short-term investments, a 9.16x current ratio, FY2025 free cash flow of $126.6M (24.5% of revenue), reagent and supply costs down 10% in Q2 on 14% more tests, and a durable position in prostate genomics where Decipher is the only gene-expression test in the 2026 NCCN Advanced Prognostic Tools table. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name, and the bar for repeating it should be high. I am repeating it because the price decline has improved entry economics into a business whose 2027 earnings base is genuinely unresolved, and because two of the weaker dimensions, capital allocation and idiosyncratic risk, have not improved.

## Company Overview

Veracyte operates a single-segment cancer diagnostics business from South San Francisco, with labs in South San Francisco, San Diego, and a cytopathology facility in Austin, plus MRD development in Haifa, Israel. Two delivery models exist: centralized US laboratory developed tests (LDTs) run in CLIA labs, and ex-US in vitro diagnostic kits, currently limited to Prosigna in breast cancer.

Financially the company is two tests. Q2 2026 revenue split: Decipher Prostate $91.9M (+20%), Afirma $51.2M (+18%), Prosigna product revenue $3.9M. Biopharmaceutical and other revenue fell 86% in the first half to $1.1M following the wind-down of the French subsidiary. Approximately 97% of revenue is US centralized-lab testing in two indications.

Two structural dependencies deserve naming. Thyroid Cytopathology Partners, an independent Texas professional association Veracyte does not own, performs the cytopathology that underpins Afirma under a per-aspiration fee arrangement ($1.683M of cost in Q2). The nCounter platform on which Prosigna runs is licensed from Bruker Spatial Biology following Bruker's acquisition of NanoString assets, so Veracyte is a licensee of a platform it does not control. Separately, under a 2023 agreement Veracyte intends to offer decentralized ex-US IVDs on Illumina's NextSeq 550Dx as Illumina's platform customer. Several sample-collection kit and reagent components come from single-source suppliers.

Two new products launched in Q2 2026: Prosigna as a US LDT, and the TrueMRD Monitoring Test for muscle-invasive bladder cancer, which secured Medicare coverage in the quarter (the first coverage for Veracyte's whole-genome-sequencing MRD platform). Neither is included in FY2026 guidance.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** Revenue compounded from $296.5M (FY2022) to $517.1M (FY2025), with first-half 2026 at $289.4M (+18.3%). Gross margin moved from 66.9% (FY2024) to 70.1% (FY2025) to 72.5% in 1H26. FY2025 operating income rose $41.6M to $57.8M.

That FY2025 operating improvement contains two large non-operational items that nearly cancel: a $20.5M asset impairment (versus $3.4M) against a $15.3M credit from contingent-consideration revaluation inside G&A (versus a $2.2M charge). Net effect approximately +$0.3M. The bridge is therefore substantively operational: gross profit rose roughly $64M against roughly $22.5M of opex growth, implying incremental gross margin near 90% on FY2025's $71.4M of revenue growth. That contribution economics is the central financial fact about this company and it is what makes both the upside and the PAMA downside so leveraged.

**Q2 2026 quality adjustments.** Three items should be removed before extrapolating headline results.

*Prior-period collection true-ups.* The 10-Q discloses $4.5M in Q2 2026 and $8.7M in 1H26 from cash collections exceeding prior estimated variable consideration, including successful reimbursement appeals, versus $1.8M and $2.2M in the prior-year periods. The incremental benefit is $2.7M in the quarter and $6.5M in the half. Excluding it, Q2 testing revenue growth is 16.9% rather than 19.1%, and 1H26 is 19.5% rather than 22.3%. These are legitimate ASC 606 revisions, not aggressive estimates, but they are not repeatable volume.

*Near-zero tax rate.* The effective rate was 3.9% in Q2 and 4.1% in 1H26, against 30.1% in 1H25, because of a full valuation allowance against $561.9M of mostly indefinite-lived federal NOLs. At a 21% statutory rate, 1H26 diluted EPS would be approximately $0.55 rather than the $0.66 reported, so roughly 17% of reported GAAP EPS is attributable to the tax rate. Cash taxes paid were only $1.5M, so the benefit is real cash today, but GAAP EPS faces a normalization headwind, and management flags that a partial valuation-allowance release within twelve months would create a one-time non-cash benefit followed by a higher reported rate.

*Escrow settlement.* Other income in 1H26 includes $4.179M from settlement of escrow claims, all in Q1, equal to 7.4% of 1H26 pretax income. Counterparty and underlying acquisition are undisclosed.

**The R&D line is misleading.** Q2 R&D rose 81% to $29.4M and 1H26 rose 66%. The MD&A discloses that $6.5M of the quarterly increase ($12.5M for the half) is reassignment of R&D-related software expense previously in G&A, with G&A falling $4.9M in the quarter and $15.0M in the half correspondingly. Direct research and development expense, the line closest to project spend, rose only 15% ($3.785M versus $3.292M). Total opex is unaffected. A screen reading "R&D up 81%" as pipeline acceleration or "G&A down 23%" as cost discipline would misread both. Gross margin expanded to 72.2% while absorbing newly assigned customer-service costs into cost of revenue, which makes the margin gain harder-won than it appears.

**Balance sheet.** No debt. Total liabilities of $98.0M at June 30, 2026 comprise operating lease liabilities $40.1M, accrued liabilities $47.7M, payables $8.0M, contingent consideration $0.9M, deferred taxes $0.6M, deferred revenue $0.6M. No revolver, notes, or covenants. Cash and equivalents $299.5M plus short-term investments $185.7M equals $485.2M, up $72.3M in the half. Current ratio 9.16x. Equity $1,388.4M against total assets $1,486.4M, of which goodwill is $767.2M (52%) and intangibles $82.7M.

Two balance-sheet details worth flagging. The lease portfolio's future minimum payments of $71.1M discount to $40.1M at a disclosed 11.4% weighted-average rate (high, reflecting unrated, no-debt status) with terms to March 2040. Cash paid for lease liabilities was only $0.296M in Q2 against $1.767M of straight-line expense, consistent with an abatement/tenant-improvement period, so cash lease outflows will rise. Accounts receivable grew 21% to $54.2M against 18% half-year revenue growth. AR at roughly 33 days of Q2 revenue is unremarkable in absolute terms, but the direction is mildly negative, and UnitedHealthcare represents 13% of revenue against 17% of receivables (up from 14% at year-end) while Medicare is 33% of revenue against 19% of receivables.

**Cash flow.** 1H26 operating cash flow $81.0M less $5.8M capex equals $75.2M free cash flow. FY2025: $136.3M less $9.7M equals $126.6M (24.5% of revenue). Two approaches to FY2026 converge near $145-150M: doubling the half (which overstates, since it includes the $4.2M escrow item and near-zero cash taxes and ignores disclosed seasonality) and growing FY2025 FCF at the ~15% revenue guide. The important qualifier is that approximately $54M annualized is funded by non-cash stock compensation (1H26 SBC $26.8M, 9.3% of revenue), so cash-on-cash economics are materially weaker than the headline.

Prior scores moved financial health from 5.5 to 8.0. The underlying facts support high single digits — debt-free, nine-times current coverage, positive and growing FCF — with modest deductions for SBC intensity, goodwill at half of assets tested as a single reporting unit, and rising cash lease outflows. I score 7.8.

## Growth Analysis

Testing revenue guidance of $576-582M implies 17-18% growth, and revenue guidance was raised twice. Underneath, the composition is decelerating and the guide says so.

**Guidance embeds a second-half step-down.** 1H26 testing revenue was $280.8M. At the $579M midpoint, implied 2H26 testing revenue is $298.2M against 2H25's $263.6M — 13.1% growth, versus 22.3% actual in 1H26. On total revenue, implied 2H26 of $303.6M against 2H25's $272.5M is +11.4%, versus +18.3% in the first half. Part of that is mechanical (the $6.5M of incremental collection true-ups will not repeat at the same magnitude, and comparisons stiffen) and part may be conservatism, but an investor underwriting "durable double-digit growth" should note that management's own guide steps down to low teens.

**Volume versus price.** Q2 diagnostic volume grew 14% while testing revenue grew 19%. Of the ~5-point gap, ~2.2 points is the true-up delta, leaving ~2.8 points of genuine ASP improvement. This is a change in character: in FY2025, volume grew 19% against 18% testing revenue growth, implying flat-to-slightly-negative ASP. The most plausible explanation for the 2026 ASP inflection is the expanded MolDX coverage decisions — the July 2024 thyroid LCD and the September/December 2024 metastatic Decipher LCD retroactive to October 18, 2024 — converting previously denied claims into paid claims, which would also explain elevated appeal recoveries. That mechanism is not a permanent tailwind; once the denial backlog is worked, ASP growth reverts to rate and mix.

**Franchise divergence.** Decipher volume grew 27% in FY2025 and 17% in Q2 2026, with Q2 revenue +20% to $91.9M. Afirma grew 11% and 10% respectively, on a Medicare rate frozen since 2018. Prostate is carrying the company; thyroid is maturing.

**New products carry the incremental story and are excluded from guidance.** TrueMRD and Prosigna LDT revenue is outside the guide. That framing is conservative, and any traction is upside, but it also means there is no management-sanctioned number for the two products that constitute the multi-year growth narrative.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus rose 16.5% over 90 days and next year 9.3%. Both are below the threshold I would treat as a strong inflection signal, and the composition is backward-looking: current-quarter estimates are down 2.6% over 90 days and next-quarter down 3.1%, while the annual figures rose because realized Q1 and Q2 beats replaced estimates. Forward quarters are being trimmed. The high-conviction combination of >30% revisions with accelerating revenue is not present here.

The four-quarter beat streak (+28% to +133% surprises) also warrants discounting. The surprise series is struck on GAAP actuals against estimates that appear not to anticipate a ~4% effective tax rate; a consensus modeling a normal rate will systematically under-forecast GAAP EPS at this company. The non-GAAP progression — Q2 $0.54 against $0.44 a year earlier — is the cleaner read and is good but not extraordinary.

Growth prospects: 6.0, down modestly from 6.3, on confirmed second-half deceleration inside the raised guide and negative forward-quarter revisions.

A note on basis: the annual consensus figures of $1.92 and $1.93 reconcile with the quarterly $0.42 and $0.45 estimates on a non-GAAP basis; the quarterly surprise table appears struck on GAAP actuals. I use non-GAAP for forward multiples and flag the inconsistency rather than mixing bases.

**PEG cannot be constructed favorably.** Forward earnings-to-growth requires forward earnings growth, and consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.93 against FY2026's $1.92 is +0.5%. At 24x forward with no consensus earnings growth next year, the multiple is not cheap on the metric that matters. The bull rebuttal is that consensus is simply embedding a conservative PAMA assumption; if rates hold or a statutory cap limits the reduction, FY2027 earnings power is materially higher. That is the actual debate, and it is a reimbursement-policy question, not a valuation question.

**Peer context is weak.** The supplied comparables — PRVA at 137.8x, CON 19.9x, CRVL 28.4x, PGNY 33.6x, RDNT negative — are health care services, not molecular diagnostics, and the 33.6x median is not a usable anchor for a genomics testing business with 72% gross margins and Medicare rate exposure. Dispersion across the set is extreme, which tells you the group is heterogeneous rather than that VCYT is cheap at 24x.

**Analyst targets are stale and should not be treated as confirmation.** Mean $58.60, median $61.50, low $45.00, high $70.00 against a "current" reference of $44.85. Targets act as floors on inflection names that look cheap against rising estimates; here the out-year consensus is flat and the multiple is 24x, so the mean target and the pre-decline price likely reflect the same set of assumptions rather than independent verification.

**The five-year record frames the multiple question.** Proxy pay-versus-performance discloses cumulative TSR of $86.02 per $100 invested at December 31, 2020 against $124.75 for the compensation peer group — roughly -14% versus +25% — while revenue rose from about $117M to $517M. The entire gap is multiple compression from a 2020 diagnostics-sector valuation peak. At 5.5x EV/revenue, the multiple is no longer obviously demanding. It is also not obviously cheap once you net out $54M of SBC from EBITDA.

Valuation: 6.0. The July 31 decline improved the entry meaningfully, but a 24x multiple against flat consensus out-year earnings and a January 2027 rate reset does not constitute identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Four arenas with materially different positions.

**Prostate (Decipher) — strongest.** Sole gene-expression test in the 2026 NCCN Advanced Prognostic Tools table, coverage for more than 215M enrollees, more than 115 peer-reviewed studies, and newly covered metastatic/castration-resistant indications. Guideline inclusion gates both adoption and coverage in this industry, so this is a genuine asset. The named emerging threat is ArteraAI, applying AI to digital pathology rather than genomics — architecturally different and potentially cheaper if it achieves guideline and coverage parity. That is the competitive development to monitor most closely for the core franchise.

**Thyroid (Afirma) — co-leader under pressure.** Volume growth of 10-11% is roughly seven points below Decipher, the Medicare rate is frozen at $3,600 since 2018, and Veracyte is now plaintiff in five-patent litigation against Sonic Healthcare USA over ThyroSeq v3 in the Eastern District of Texas, seeking treble damages and an injunction. The motion to dismiss was denied January 20, 2026, the Markman hearing was held July 15, 2026, and a jury trial is scheduled for Q1 2027. The USPTO granted ex parte reexamination of one asserted patent on July 21, 2026, an adverse procedural step on one of five. Litigation costs were $1.7M in 1H26. I cannot distinguish whether the suit reflects competitive pressure or routine enforcement of valuable IP; slower Afirma growth may simply reflect a more penetrated market against roughly 650,000 annual US FNA biopsies.

**MRD (TrueMRD) — largest TAM, weakest position.** The 10-K names Natera as primary competition in MIBC. Externally reported mid-2026 developments — none of which appear in Veracyte's filings and none of which I could verify against primary sources — indicate Natera secured NCCN bladder guideline inclusion for tumor-informed ctDNA-MRD (per Natera's own June 23, 2026 release, with NCCN's actual language unverified and possibly technology-agnostic), FDA companion-diagnostic authorization of Signatera with atezolizumab in MIBC (per a secondary interview, not an FDA document), EU IVDR Class C certification, and Japanese approval in colorectal cancer. If accurate, Veracyte chose as its first MRD indication the one where its named competitor holds a companion-diagnostic position that gates drug eligibility — a structurally stronger place in the treatment algorithm than a coverage decision. Veracyte's differentiation, whole-genome sequencing requiring under one tube of blood with two-week turnaround versus bespoke tumor-informed panels, is a real technical distinction of unproven commercial value.

**Breast (Prosigna) — challenger with no US traction.** The 10-K concedes Exact Sciences' Oncotype DX commands a substantial majority of the market. Ex-US Prosigna revenue has been static near $7.5M per half. The US LDT launch is a share-taking attempt against an entrenched standard of care.

Sector-wide, the 10-Q MD&A states plainly that payers are deploying prior authorization, reduced payer-portion reimbursement, and laboratory benefit managers to reduce utilization. Competitive position: 6.0 — improving in prostate, stable-to-pressured in thyroid, weak in the two new arenas.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PAMA repricing effective January 1, 2027 — the dominant quantifiable risk.** Two unresolved items materially bound the sensitivity described above: whether a statutory year-over-year reduction cap applies in the 2027-2029 cycle (historically PAMA has phased reductions), and whether any Veracyte code carries Advanced Diagnostic Laboratory Test status with different rate-setting. I could not verify either. The scenarios are unbounded illustrations; the realistic downside is likely narrower, and the direction of change is not yet known. The 10-Q does not confirm submission occurred.

**2. Payer concentration is worsening, not improving.** FY2025: Medicare 33% of revenue (from 31% in each of 2024 and 2023) and UnitedHealthcare 14% (from 13% and 11%) — 47% combined, up from 44% and 42%. Q2 2026: 33% and 13%. The revenue mix shift away from biopharma does not constitute diversification; direct payer concentration has increased for three consecutive years.

**3. The unexplained July 31 decline.** A 22.5% single-day fall on 5.75x average volume, the day after a beat and a guidance raise, is not explained by anything in the filings or news I can access. Candidate explanations include digestion of the implied second-half deceleration, a reimbursement development, a competitor announcement, or a sector de-rating. I will not assign a cause I cannot verify, and I treat the gap as a reason to require more evidence before adding capital rather than as a discount to exploit.

**4. Tax normalization.** Roughly 17% of 1H26 GAAP EPS comes from the 4.1% effective rate. Any valuation-allowance release creates a one-time benefit followed by a permanently higher reported rate.

**5. Capital allocation and disclosure structure.** The 2021 HalioDx acquisition was wound down through French judicial restructuring, with deconsolidation on August 1, 2025 and roughly $39M of FY2025 cost ($20.5M impairment, $6.7M deconsolidation loss, $11.9M of incremental professional fees). Goodwill remains $767.2M with no impairment ever recorded, because Veracyte reports one segment and one reporting unit, commingling Decipher, HalioDx, and C2i goodwill. Near-term impairment risk is remote given $3.7B market capitalization against $1.39B book equity, but investors cannot assess the return on any individual acquisition. Separately, C2i contingent-consideration milestone probability fell from a 34% weighted average at year-end to 16% at June 30, with the remaining liability down to $0.9M and now attributed entirely to the nCounter license — an accounting credit generated by lowering internal forecasts for acquired programs, though TrueMRD did launch and secure coverage in the same quarter, which cuts against reading it as program failure.

**6. Operational dependencies.** TCP for Afirma cytopathology (not owned), the Bruker-controlled nCounter platform under license, single-source kit and reagent suppliers, and TrueMRD development concentrated in Haifa, Israel with the 10-Q retaining explicit Middle East conflict language.

**7. Regulatory pathway.** The FDA's LDT final rule was vacated March 31, 2025 with no appeal, preserving the pathway on which nearly all US revenue depends. VALID Act proposals remain pending; a legislative revival would impose substantial cost on the LDT model. Prosigna is mid-transition to IVDR Class C.

**8. Two-sided patent catalyst.** A Q1 2027 jury verdict with an injunction against ThyroSeq would be materially positive for Afirma (roughly $200M annualized on Q2's $51.2M). Trial dates slip, outcomes are uncertain, and one asserted patent is under reexamination. Largely unmodeled in either direction.

Risk factors: 4.2. This is a genuinely high-idiosyncratic-risk profile with a dated binary inside six months.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $63 (+36%)

PAMA rates hold, or a statutory cap limits the reduction to a low single-digit revenue impact, removing the overhang that is suppressing the FY2027 multiple. Testing revenue compounds mid-to-high teens on Decipher's NCCN position and the metastatic indication ramp; ASP improvement persists longer than expected as expanded MolDX coverage converts a multi-quarter denial backlog. TrueMRD and Prosigna LDT begin contributing revenue that is entirely absent from guidance, and the Q1 2027 jury trial produces a favorable verdict or settlement that stabilizes Afirma. With ~90% incremental gross margin, revenue upside converts to EBITDA at high rates; FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$2.30 and the stock re-rates to 28x. Net cash of $485.2M and no debt mean none of this requires financing.

### Bear Case — $32 (-31%)

The PAMA reset cuts Medicare FFS rates 20%, removing ~$39M of revenue and ~26% of adjusted EBITDA from January 1, 2027 with no volume decline. Tax normalization compounds it, taking FY2027 non-GAAP EPS to roughly $1.45. Afirma growth continues decelerating from 10% as ThyroSeq competes and the patent case fails or is delayed; TrueMRD fails to gain traction against a competitor holding a companion-diagnostic position in the same indication; Prosigna gains no US share against Oncotype DX. Second-half 2026 growth comes in at the low end of the implied 11-13%, confirming that 2025-2026 ASP gains were a one-off coverage-expansion effect. At 20x on $1.45, supported by roughly $6/share of net cash, the stock trades to $32, still well above the $22.92 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fifth consecutive). Rationale: uncertain.**

The trajectory has been 5.8 → 6.8 → 6.6 → 6.5 → 6.2, now 6.0. The decline is not a response to the share price — a lower price on intact fundamentals improves risk/reward and I have credited that in the valuation score. It reflects two specific things: guidance itself now embeds a nine-point second-half deceleration in testing revenue growth, and the PAMA determination has moved from a distant disclosure to an event whose data window has closed with preliminary rates expected within months. Financial health remains the strongest dimension; management quality and risk are the weakest and neither improved this cycle.

**Base case: $51 (+10.1% from $46.32).** 26x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of approximately $1.95, assuming a PAMA reduction in the high single digits on Medicare FFS partially offset by mid-teens volume growth and modest new-product contribution. Cross-check on enterprise value: 5.5x on FY2027 revenue near $650M gives $3.58B, plus net cash building toward $560M, equals roughly $50-51 per share on ~82M diluted shares.

**Bull case: $63 (+36%).** 28x on $2.30.
**Bear case: $32 (-31%).** 20x on $1.45, with net cash providing support.

**Timeframe: 6-9 months**, governed by the CMS preliminary CLFS determination for the 2027-2029 cycle (historically published in autumn, with final rates late in the year), the Q3 2026 print, and secondarily the Q1 2027 jury trial.

**Upgrade to BUY requires any two of:**
1. CMS preliminary 2027 rates for CPT 81546, 81542, and 0016U showing a combined weighted reduction of 5% or less to Veracyte's Medicare FFS revenue base, or confirmation that a statutory cap or ADLT status bounds the reduction.
2. Q3 2026 testing revenue growth of 15% or better *excluding* prior-period collection true-ups, demonstrating the implied 13% second-half guide was conservatism rather than a genuine step-down.
3. Disclosed TrueMRD and/or Prosigna LDT revenue at a run rate above $15M annualized, converting excluded-from-guidance optionality into a modelled contribution.
4. A price near $38-40 without fundamental deterioration, which would put the shares near 20x forward on flat consensus and roughly 4.5x EV/revenue — a level where the PAMA bear case is substantially pre-paid.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. CMS preliminary rates imply a reduction of 15% or more to Medicare FFS revenue with no offsetting cap, taking roughly 20%+ of adjusted EBITDA out of the 2027 base.
2. Decipher volume growth decelerates below 12% in any quarter, which would indicate the core franchise's guideline advantage is eroding (ArteraAI or otherwise) rather than merely maturing.
3. Testing revenue growth ex-true-ups falls below 12% in Q3 or Q4 2026, or FY2027 revenue guidance is set below 8% growth.
4. Any goodwill impairment against the $767.2M single reporting unit, or a further material acquisition unwind.

**Reassessment point: the Q3 2026 report, and the CMS preliminary determination file, whichever lands first.**

## Conclusion

Veracyte is a well-run, debt-free diagnostics company with the strongest position in prostate genomic risk stratification, 72% gross margins, roughly 90% incremental gross margin on new volume, $485.2M of net cash, and free cash flow near 24% of revenue. Four quarters of beats, two guidance raises, and reagent cost per test falling while volumes grow 14% all point to genuine operating improvement. The July 31 decline of 22.5% has taken the multiple to 24x forward non-GAAP earnings and 5.5x EV/revenue, which is not demanding for a business of this quality.

Three things keep me at HOLD rather than moving to BUY. First, the Medicare rate reset effective January 1, 2027 is dated, quantifiable, and unhedged: at ~90% incremental margin, a 10-20% cut to rates on 34% of testing revenue removes 13-26% of adjusted EBITDA with no volume decline, and I could not verify whether a statutory cap bounds it. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.93 against FY2026's $1.92 shows the sell side reaching the same conclusion. Second, the raised guidance itself implies second-half testing growth of 13% against 22% delivered in the first half, and forward-quarter estimates are being trimmed even as annual figures rise on banked beats, so the estimate-revision signal is mechanical, not forward-looking. Third, a 22.5% one-day decline the day after a beat and a raise represents information I do not have; the appropriate response is to require evidence, not to assume the sellers were wrong.
