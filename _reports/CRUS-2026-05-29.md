**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cirrus Logic is a fabless designer of low-power, high-precision audio and mixed-signal semiconductors that occupies a defensible niche as a custom co-development partner to Apple. FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) was a record year: revenue of $2.0B (+5% YoY), gross margin of 52.8%, operating income of $460M, net income of $414M (+25%), and free cash flow of $637M at a ~32% FCF margin. The balance sheet holds roughly $1.1B in cash and securities, no funded debt, an Altman Z-Score of 3.72, and a current ratio above 7x. The company has retired ~8% of its diluted share count over three years through consistent buybacks funded entirely from cash flow. On the financial-health axis, this is among the cleanest large-cap semiconductor names available.

The investment case is constrained by what sits beneath those numbers. Apple was 91% of FY26 revenue and reached 94% in Q3 FY26 — concentration that has risen every year for three consecutive years despite a multi-year diversification message. Two of the larger contributors to FY26's earnings strength are non-recurring or non-operational: the effective tax rate fell ~9 points (from 25.5% to 16.6%) on the OBBBA R&D-capitalization repeal, a durable level-shift but not a repeatable growth engine, and operating cash flow jumped ~$200M partly on a ~$103M favorable working-capital swing as prepaid GlobalFoundries wafers unwound. Both tailwinds fade in FY27. Layered on top are structural ASP step-downs to Apple (explicitly cited in the Q3 and 9-month results), the GlobalFoundries capacity agreement expiring at end of calendar 2026, ~$232M of unaccrued IRS transfer-pricing exposure, and the disclosed risk that Apple has the resources to internally develop or re-architect away the functions Cirrus supplies.

The stock has risen 73.6% over the trailing twelve months and trades at ~22x trailing/forward earnings, a discount to the ~28.7x peer median, though that median is distorted by several peers carrying negative or non-meaningful P/Es. Estimate revisions are mixed: the current quarter has been marked up +22% over 90 days, but next-quarter (-4.7% over 30 days) and current-year (-2.1% over 30 days) revisions are negative. We rate CRUS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The optionality in HPMS content growth (FaceID smart power IC, battery/power ICs, PC and automotive haptics) is real and worth owning through, but it is not yet large enough relative to the Apple base, and the price already reflects the favorable FY26 print without discounting the concentration tail.

## Company Overview

Cirrus Logic designs mixed-signal and audio semiconductors from its Austin, TX headquarters (~1,668 employees). It operates as a single reportable segment but discloses two product lines:

- **Audio** — $1.16B in FY26 (58% of revenue, +2% YoY). Smart codecs and boosted amplifiers; the company positions itself as the smartphone-audio leader.
- **High-Performance Mixed-Signal (HPMS)** — $837M in FY26 (42% of revenue, +10% YoY). Camera controllers, power and battery ICs, haptics. This is the designated growth engine.

The strategy has three explicit prongs: defend smartphone audio leadership, grow HPMS content per smartphone, and expand into PCs, automotive, industrial, professional audio, and imaging. The moat is deep custom-silicon engineering co-developed with its anchor customer — parts that are "often specific to our customer's system architecture and frequently cannot be sold to other customers." That same feature is the constraint: high switching costs and deep design-in, but no diversification and captive pricing. Named competitors include Qualcomm, Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, Synaptics, Goodix, Realtek, STMicroelectronics, Skyworks, and AKM. Roughly 99% of sales are shipped internationally to contract manufacturers (primarily China, India, Vietnam) even though the end customer is U.S.-based.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is excellent on every conventional measure of resilience and capital efficiency.

| Metric (FY, $M) | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,898 | 1,789 | 1,896 | **1,997** |
| Gross margin | 50.4% | 51.2% | 52.5% | **52.8%** |
| Operating income | 249 | 343 | 410 | **460** |
| Net income | 177 | 275 | 332 | **414** |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | 444 | **651** |
| FCF | — | — | — | **637 (31.9% margin)** |
| Diluted shares | 57.2 | 56.0 | 55.2 | **52.8** |

**Quality of earnings.** Operating cash flow ($651M) exceeded net income ($414M), and capex is trivial (~$14M) under the fabless model, producing a ~32% FCF margin. FCF conversion above 100% of net income signals high-quality earnings with no accrual flattery.

**Decomposing the FY26 earnings growth.** Net income grew 25% on just 5% revenue growth. Two of the three drivers are not operating leverage:
- The effective tax rate fell from 25.5% (FY25) to 16.6% (FY26) — Q3 FY26 was just 13.6% vs. 24.5% a year prior — because the OBBBA (enacted July 4, 2025) repealed the TCJA requirement to capitalize U.S. R&D, removing a large GILTI penalty. This is real and recurring, but it is a one-time level-shift; FY27 will not get a repeat ~9-point boost.
- Operating cash flow rose ~$200M partly on a ~$103M favorable working-capital swing (inventory fell from $299M at March 2025 to $189M at December 2025 as prepaid GlobalFoundries wafers wound down). This tailwind reverses as the prepayment fully amortizes (down to ~$33M) and inventory normalizes.
- The genuine operating contributor is the 30bp of gross-margin expansion and the ongoing share-count reduction.

**Balance sheet.** ~$800M cash plus ~$304M marketable securities, $2.13B equity, $436M goodwill (stable, no impairments), and essentially no interest-bearing debt — the $300M revolver was undrawn at year-end and interest expense is just commitment fees of ~$0.9M/year. The screening D/E of 1.17 reflects a total-liabilities/equity framing rather than funded leverage; effective financial leverage is nil. The current ratio of 7.4x and Z-Score of 3.72 place the company firmly in the safe zone.

**Capital allocation.** ~$280M of buybacks in FY26 (vs. ~$261M in FY25), retiring ~2M shares in the first nine months at an average of ~$107 — well below the current ~$174 price, which validates the discipline. A $500M authorization from March 2025 had ~$344M remaining at December 2025. No dividend, no acquisitions of note. Diluted shares are down ~8% over three years, a meaningful lever on EPS.

One note for FY27 modeling: the May 6, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01 and 2.03, filed alongside Q4 results) most plausibly represents a new or refinanced credit facility (the existing revolver matures July 8, 2026) and/or a new wafer-supply arrangement, but the document text was not retrievable. The specifics should be confirmed before relying on them.

The current-quarter mark-up of +22% is isolated to one quarter while next-quarter and current-year revisions are negative, and next-year is essentially flat. This is the seasonal pattern of a smartphone-content supplier rather than a broad-based earnings acceleration. A revision-velocity signal that would favor an aggressive call requires that revisions be rising across the curve alongside accelerating revenue growth; here, revenue grew 5% in FY26 and the full-year and out-year EPS estimates are flat-to-down. The strong current-quarter print and the weaker out-quarters net to muted forward earnings momentum.

The structural growth thesis runs through HPMS, which grew 10% in FY26 versus 2% for Audio. Management cites PC share gains "across all PC segments," AI-PC design wins, a new automotive haptic family, and a new general-market audio family sampling. The new FaceID smart power IC co-developed with Apple is Cirrus's first product in this category and a content-expansion within the existing customer. These are real, but small relative to a $1.16B Audio base that is itself dependent on the same customer. The smartphone market is maturing with lengthening replacement cycles (management's own characterization), so the growth lever is content per device, not unit growth.

CRUS trades at ~22x trailing earnings on $7.85 EPS (and ~20x forward on the $8.90 current-year consensus), against a current price of ~$174 and market cap of ~$8.6B. The peer set:

The peer median of 28.7x is set by Qorvo alone, since four of the five comps carry negative or non-meaningful P/Es (loss-making or near-breakeven). On that basis CRUS at 22x looks cheap, but the comparison is unreliable when most of the cohort isn't generating GAAP earnings. The cleaner read is that CRUS is a profitable, high-margin, debt-free outlier in a peer group that is largely struggling, which partly justifies the premium it carries to the loss-makers and the discount it carries to a quality compounder with diversified end markets.

The case for a higher valuation score would rest on the FaceID/HPMS content story re-rating the multiple. We are not comfortable underwriting that today for three reasons. First, the FY26 earnings base is flattered by a non-recurring tax level-shift and a one-time working-capital cash surge, so the trailing EPS overstates the durable earnings power. Second, forward estimates are flat-to-down outside the current quarter, so there is no rising-estimate tailwind to compress the multiple. Third, the single-customer concentration and structural ASP step-downs cap the terminal multiple Cirrus can sustain relative to a diversified analog peer like Analog Devices or Texas Instruments. The stock's 73.6% twelve-month appreciation reflects genuine fundamental improvement and is not itself a reason to mark down the valuation, but at 22x on a partly-inflated base with a concentration overhang, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

Analyst targets span $140 (low) to $200 (high), with a mean of $181.4 and median of $197.0. At ~$174 the stock sits between mean and median, implying ~4-13% upside to consensus and ~20% downside to the low. That is not the limited-downside/substantial-upside skew we require for a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

Cirrus is the leader in smartphone audio and a deeply embedded custom-silicon partner. The moat is structural: parts are co-developed with the anchor customer, frequently cannot be sold to others, and carry high switching costs once designed in. That position is stable-to-improving on the HPMS content axis, with share wins in PCs, the new automotive haptics, and the FaceID smart power IC expanding dollar content within Apple.

The competitive risk is not catalog competitors like Synaptics or Goodix taking share; it is the anchor customer itself. The 10-K explicitly states Apple "ha[s] sufficient resources to internally develop technology solutions and semiconductor components that could replace" Cirrus parts and could "transition to different audio and system architectures" or integrate the functionality into other components. Apple's documented in-housing of modems and other silicon makes this a credible, if not imminent, tail risk. Customers can stop designing in Cirrus parts "with limited notice and little or no penalty," with no minimum-purchase commitments. The Apple American Manufacturing Program addition (Reuters, March 26, 2026) — Cirrus working with GlobalFoundries at the Malta, NY fab — is a constructive signal of continued partnership depth and supply-chain geographic diversification, but it is Apple naming Cirrus as a supplier, not a structural change to the dependency.

The competitive-position trajectory improved modestly in prior reviews (from 6.5 to 7.0) on the HPMS momentum. We hold the score slightly lower at 6.0 because the rising Apple concentration (87% → 89% → 91% → 94% in Q3) cuts against the diversification thesis the higher score implied; the moat is real but it is narrowing, not widening, in customer-mix terms.

## Risk Assessment

This is fundamentally a single-customer story, and the dependence is intensifying:

- **Apple concentration** with top-10 customers at 96-97%. Among the most concentrated revenue bases of any large-cap semiconductor company.
- **Self-design / re-architecture risk** disclosed explicitly in the 10-K (above).
- **Structural ASP erosion** — both Q3 and 9-month results cite "previously anticipated pricing reductions," with commitments "not to exceed certain pricing" on some products, capping pass-through of cost inflation.
- **GlobalFoundries capacity agreement expiry** at end of calendar 2026. The 2021 agreement was amended in February 2025 to *reduce* committed wafer quantities — a signal that original demand assumptions overshot. What replaces it for 2027+ is unresolved; the Malta, NY tie-up appears to be the path but terms are not yet visible in filings.
- **Supply concentration** — the substantial majority of wafers are made by TSMC in Taiwan, a direct China-Taiwan geopolitical exposure.
- **IRS transfer-pricing dispute** — FY2017-2019 returns under exam, with a Revenue Agent's Report asserting ~$168.3M additional tax plus ~$63.7M penalties (ex-interest), ~$232M total, unaccrued beyond existing reserves. Low probability but non-trivial and material to a future period if adverse.
- **Earnings-quality fade** — the FY27 comparison loses the ~9-point tax tailwind and the working-capital cash surge.

We score Risk Factors at 4.5 because the concentration and ASP risks are specific, structural, and only partially priced. A diversified analog peer with the same margins and balance sheet would trade well above 22x; the discount Cirrus carries is the market's partial pricing of these risks, but the rising concentration and the unaccrued IRS exposure are not fully reflected.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** HPMS content growth outruns Apple's ASP step-downs. The FaceID smart power IC, battery/power ICs, PC share gains across all segments, and automotive haptics compound into a genuine diversification away from smartphone-audio dependence. Margins hold at 52-53%, buybacks continue retiring ~4% of shares annually below $175, and the multiple re-rates toward a diversified-analog peer level as the customer mix broadens. FY27 EPS approaches $9.40 and the stock reaches the $200 analyst high.

**Bear case.** Apple accelerates insourcing or re-architects audio/power functions; ASP step-downs outpace cost reductions and compress gross margin below 51%; the GlobalFoundries expiry produces a worse-cost foundry arrangement; the IRS dispute resolves adversely; and the FY27 earnings comparison disappoints as the tax and working-capital tailwinds reverse. The multiple compresses toward the analyst low of $140 (-20% from current).

The base case sits in between: HPMS grows mid-to-high single digits, Audio stays roughly flat, the tax and working-capital tailwinds fade, and EPS settles near the $8.90-9.40 consensus range with the multiple holding around 20-22x.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY27 guidance (issued with the next quarter's results) shows HPMS revenue growth re-accelerating above 15% YoY *and* full-year EPS estimates revising up across the curve (not just the current quarter), with the stock holding below ~20x forward.
- Apple revenue concentration declines meaningfully (below ~88%) on genuine non-Apple design-win ramps, reducing the concentration discount.
- The post-2026 GlobalFoundries/Malta arrangement is disclosed on terms that protect gross margin at or above 52%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Apple guides to or executes architecture changes / insourcing that materially reduce Cirrus content (any disclosed design-out or socket loss).
- Gross margin compresses below 51% on ASP step-downs not offset by cost reductions for two consecutive quarters.
- The IRS dispute resolves adversely with a material incremental accrual, or the GlobalFoundries replacement arrangement raises unit costs.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 earnings release (expected early August 2026), which should carry FY27 guidance and clarify the foundry path. The expiry of the GlobalFoundries agreement at end of calendar 2026 is the second hard checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Cirrus Logic is a high-quality, debt-free, cash-generative semiconductor leader with best-in-class ~32% FCF margins and disciplined buybacks executed below current prices. On financial health alone it deserves a high score. The constraint is structural: ~91-94% of revenue runs through a single customer that has the resources and stated optionality to design Cirrus out, ASP step-downs are contractually embedded, and a meaningful portion of FY26's headline earnings growth came from a non-recurring tax level-shift and a one-time working-capital cash swing that fade in FY27. Forward estimates are flat-to-down outside the current quarter, and at ~22x on a partly-inflated earnings base the stock prices in the HPMS content story without compensating for the concentration tail.

We rate CRUS a **HOLD** (growth-hold). The diversification optionality is real and worth owning through, but it is not yet large enough to re-rate the multiple, and the risk/reward at the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric. The call is to hold for the HPMS content trajectory while monitoring FY27 guidance, the post-2026 foundry arrangement, and Apple's concentration trend. The validation concern that a score above the threshold should map to a BUY is addressed by the specific, falsifiable constraints above: the absence of broad-based positive estimate revisions, the fade of the tax tailwind and working-capital benefit in FY27, and the rising single-customer concentration that the current 22x multiple does not adequately discount. These are concrete, monitorable conditions, and an upgrade to BUY is contingent on the upgrade triggers listed above.
