**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CME Group operates the world's largest derivatives exchange and one of its leading central-counterparty clearing franchises, running a vertically integrated toll-booth model that captures a fee on every trade it executes and clears. The fundamentals are accelerating: 2025 revenue grew 6% to $6.52B, net income grew 15% to $4.07B, and Q1 2026 revenue jumped 14% YoY to $1.88B on a record 36.2M average daily contracts (+22%), driven by Middle East geopolitical volatility across metals, energy, and interest-rate complexes. Operating margin expanded to 69.7% in Q1 2026, and free cash flow generation is elite (~$4.19B in 2025, ~64% FCF margin, on de minimis ~$84M capex). This is a high-quality, capital-light compounder with an AA-/Aa3 balance sheet and only ~$3.4B of true corporate debt.

The stock has fallen roughly 24% from its March 2026 high near $329 to ~$249 despite improving operating results. The decline reflects a competitive/regulatory narrative rather than fundamentals. The CFTC's decision to permit Coinbase and Kalshi to list 24/7 perpetual futures — under what CME argues is a lighter regulatory regime — prompted CME to sue the CFTC and its chairman in June 2026. FMX Futures Exchange (Lutnick/BGC-backed) is attacking CME's crown-jewel interest-rate/Treasury franchise, and large clearing members continue to press for converting clearing houses to utility/not-for-profit structures. These are the reasons the multiple compressed, and they are not resolvable in the near term.

We rate CME a HOLD (growth-lean) with an overall score of 6.2, extending an unbroken HOLD sequence. The bull case — that the moat holds, the litigation/lobbying succeeds, and the ~20x forward multiple at a discount to the 26x peer median re-rates — is credible. But three things keep us from a BUY: consensus estimates are falling across every window (current-quarter EPS revised -6.5% over 90 days), the ~$5B+ investment-income lever is a headwind as the Fed cuts, and insiders sold ~$12M into the May peak with no dip-buying since. The setup is constructive but two-sided; we would accumulate toward the low-$230s where the risk/reward turns clearly asymmetric.

## Company Overview

CME Group operates four designated contract markets — CME, CBOT, NYMEX, and COMEX — offering benchmark futures and options across interest rates (SOFR, U.S. Treasuries, Fed Funds), equity indices (E-mini S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Russell 2000), FX, agriculture, energy (WTI, natural gas), and metals (gold, copper, silver), plus a growing crypto suite. The company both executes trades (93% electronically via CME Globex) and clears/guarantees them through its own clearing house, standing as counterparty to every trade. This vertical integration is the core moat: it generated roughly $72B of average daily margin savings in 2025 through cross-margining with FICC/OCC, which locks in institutional customers. Deep liquidity, open interest, and product breadth create self-reinforcing network effects that new entrants struggle to replicate.

Revenue splits into clearing and transaction fees (~81%, $5.28B in 2025), market data and information services (~12%, $803M, +13% YoY), and other access/connectivity/collateral services (~7%, $436M). Complementary businesses include BrokerTec (cash Treasuries/repo), EBS (spot FX), and the CME Term SOFR benchmark — the only ARRC-endorsed forward-looking SOFR rate. CME reports as a single segment.

CEO/Chairman Terrence Duffy has led since 2016 and been on the board since 1995. President & CFO Lynne Fitzpatrick expanded her role in November 2024 to also oversee HR and transformation. The governance structure is idiosyncratic: Class B shareholders (CME members) elect six directors regardless of economic stake and hold protected trading-fee and open-outcry rights, and roughly 85% of contract volume comes from members who pay lower fees. Member interests can diverge from common shareholders, which structurally constrains management's pricing latitude.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year record is one of steady growth at elite margins. Revenue rose from $5.02B (2022) to $5.58B (2023), $6.13B (2024), and $6.52B (2025, +6%). Net income climbed from $2.69B to $3.23B, $3.53B, and $4.07B (2025, +15%). Diluted EPS reached $11.16 in 2025 (+15%). Q1 2026 accelerated: net income $1.15B (+21%), diluted EPS $3.18 (+21%).

Volume is the driver. 2025 set a record 28.1M ADV (+6%). June 2026 posted a record 30.6M monthly ADV (+19%), led by metals (+130% in Q1), energy (+37%), and interest rates (+24%). A fee increase effective February 1, 2025 lifted the rate per contract, though this must be read against a mix shift: Q1 2026 average rate per contract fell 5% to $0.652 as lower-fee micro contracts grew rapidly in equities, energy, and metals. Volume growth more than offset the compression, but the structural drift toward retail/micro products is a real headwind to per-contract economics.

Cash generation is exceptional, on minimal capex (~$85M guided for 2026). Q2 2026 (reported July 22) delivered record Q2 revenue above $1.7B, adjusted EPS of ~$2.99 (a modest beat), and ~69% operating margin on second-highest quarterly volume ever — with a slight revenue miss versus consensus that produced a mixed analyst reaction.

A critical balance-sheet correction: standardized screening tools flag CME with an Altman Z-Score of 0.44 ("distress zone") and D/E of 5.91. These are false signals. Roughly $165B of the balance sheet consists of performance bonds and guaranty-fund contributions appearing as both current assets and current liabilities — pass-through customer clearing collateral (of which $144.3B sits in CME's Federal Reserve account), not operating leverage. Actual corporate debt is only $3.4B against $4.07B annual net income and ~$28.7B equity. CME is rated AA-/Aa3, stable. True leverage is very low.

One under-appreciated earnings lever: investment income on reinvested clearing collateral was $5.25B gross in 2025 (up from $3.94B). The bulk (~$4.84B) passes back to clearing firms, but CME keeps the spread. This is a material tailwind in a high-rate environment and a headwind as the Fed cuts — a rate sensitivity distinct from the pure volume story.

Capital return is generous and disciplined. The dividend policy targets 50-60% of prior-year cash earnings via a $1.30/quarter regular dividend plus a large annual variable special dividend ($6.15/share declared February 2026; total Q1 2026 dividends ~$7.45/share, ~$2.7B). The $3.0B buyback authorization had $2.2B remaining after Q1 2026, with $536M repurchased in Q1 at ~$291.58 — above the current price, which flatters the average cost but shows management's willingness to buy at higher levels.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is genuinely strong on the top line but softening on estimates. Q1's revenue and volume growth, plus June's record monthly volume, reflect an elevated-volatility regime driven by geopolitical stress. Volume-and-volatility-driven revenue is cyclically high right now, which raises the bar for the durability of the growth trend into 2027 if volatility normalizes.

Consensus estimate revisions are the key caution. Current-quarter EPS has been cut from $3.11 to $2.91 over 90 days (-6.5%), with the current-year estimate down 1.7% to $12.16 and next-year down 0.9% to $12.83. The 30-day trend remains negative (-2.5% current quarter). This is the signature of a company whose fundamentals are running strong on the print but whose forward estimates are being trimmed — likely reflecting rate-per-contract compression, Fed-cut headwinds to investment income, and analyst caution on the competitive overhang. It is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would flag an inflection buy; here estimates are drifting the wrong way.

Structural growth optionality is real but unproven. Two offense moves matter: CMESC (CME Securities Clearing), which received SEC approval in December 2025, positions CME for the SEC-mandated U.S. Treasury clearing (December 31, 2026) and repo clearing (June 30, 2027) transitions — a meaningful TAM expansion, though it means competing against entrenched FICC/DTCC and management concedes securities clearing "is new to our business." Treasury Link (Q4 2026 launch) connects the ~$1T cash Treasury market with Treasury futures for single-trade spread execution, reinforcing the interest-rate moat precisely where FMX is attacking. The FanDuel prediction-markets JV (launched December 2025) and 24/7 crypto trading extend the retail footprint but carry unsettled regulatory status. Market data revenue growing 13% is a quieter, higher-quality recurring stream.

Growth is best characterized as durable at the franchise level and cyclically strong at the moment, but with forward estimates being trimmed and the growth optionality (securities clearing, prediction markets) still requiring execution against incumbents. We score growth 6.0 — directionally positive, but the falling revisions temper it, and growth momentum is not a full-strength standalone signal in the current regime.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$249 on 2025 EPS of $11.16, CME trades at ~22x trailing and ~20x forward (on ~$12.16 current-year consensus). Against the Financial Exchanges & Data peer set, this is a discount: peer median P/E is 26.2x, with ICE at 20.2x, NDAQ at 26.1x, SPGI at 26.2x, MCO at 31.7x, and MSCI at 34.0x. CME's dividend yield (~2.2% regular, materially higher including the special) exceeds the 1.3% peer median. On quality-adjusted metrics — 62.5% net margin, ~64% FCF margin, AA- balance sheet, benchmark-owning network moat — a discount to the peer median is not obviously warranted by business quality.

The discount is warranted by the competitive/regulatory overhang and by the earnings-composition risk. The investment-income lever is rate-sensitive and faces compression as the Fed cuts; a portion of CME's earnings power is not purely franchise-driven volume. Rate-per-contract compression from micro-contract mix shift is a structural drag on the fee line. And falling estimates mean the forward multiple is being computed on a moving denominator that is drifting lower.

We do not treat the ~24% decline from the March high as evidence of overvaluation — price appreciation and depreciation are not valuation inputs. But this is a >30% drawdown from the high (peak ~$329 to a June low of $218), which raises the burden of proof for the bull. The specific thing the sellers may be right about: if regulatory arbitrage allows lightly regulated 24/7 perpetual-futures venues to erode the value of CME's regulated, vertically integrated model, the multiple compression is justified rather than a mispricing. The thing the sellers may be missing: fundamentals are accelerating, the litigation/lobbying response signals CME will fight hard, and CMESC/Treasury Link deepen the moat. Both readings are defensible, which is why this is a HOLD.

Analyst targets bracket the range: low $226, mean $280, median $285, high $324, with sentiment "improving." Because CME is not at a premium multiple (it is below peers) and has positive raw growth, the consensus median ~$285 is a reasonable base-case anchor, implying ~14% upside. We assign valuation a 6.5 — cheap versus peers with a defined discount cause, but not a screaming mispricing given the falling estimates and the genuine overhang. A low multiple relative to peers is not by itself a buy signal; it requires a catalyst to close, and the catalyst here (moat holds / litigation succeeds) is binary and undated.

## Competitive Landscape

CME's moat rests on unmatched liquidity and open interest, cross-margining capital efficiencies, the vertically integrated clearing model, and ownership of benchmarks (SOFR, S&P 500 via the S&P DJI JV). The core franchise remains dominant. But the competitive picture is shifting on three fronts, and the trajectory has turned from clearly improving to contested — which is why we mark down competitive position to 5.5 (from a peak of 9.0 in earlier reports).

The first and largest front is the CFTC permission for Coinbase and Kalshi to list perpetual futures — 24/7 contracts that compete directly with CME's crypto suite and potentially broader derivatives, under a regime CME views as regulatory arbitrage. This is the single biggest sentiment overhang and the primary explanation for the de-rating. The litigation response signals CME sees this as an existential threat to the "level playing field" that historically protected regulated incumbents, not a niche crypto concern.

A second front is FMX, which is explicitly targeting CME's interest-rate/Treasury complex — its most profitable and defensible franchise — with a rival clearing arrangement. FMX is attacking from a small base against CME's open-interest network effects, but a well-capitalized entrant aimed at the crown jewel is a step-change from the prior competitive backdrop. CME's Treasury Link and CMESC securities clearing are the countermoves.

A third front is the pressure from CME's largest clearing members, who have long argued that clearing houses should be operated as utilities/not-for-profit and have sought rules to let participants move open positions to firm-owned clearing houses. If adopted, this would attack the core vertical model directly.

The moat is intact today — network effects in liquidity and margin efficiency are hard to dislodge, and no competitor has meaningfully taken share in the flagship complexes. But the regulatory barrier that protected incumbents is being contested on multiple fronts simultaneously, which shifts the competitive trajectory from improving to defensively stable-at-risk.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully priced are meaningful, which is why we score risk factors 5.5.

The dominant risk is the perpetual-futures/CFTC dynamic. It is now partly priced, but the outcome is binary and undated: a favorable regulatory or judicial resolution re-rates the stock; an adverse one validates the compression. The FMX challenge and the utility-clearing agitation compound this.

Investment-income sensitivity is under-appreciated relative to the volume story. The Fed cut multiple times in 2025, and sustained further cuts would compress the investment-income lever and the spread CME retains. Rate-per-contract compression from micro-contract mix shift is a persistent structural drag on fee economics.

Volume dependence on volatility is the cyclical risk: revenue is transaction-fee based against a largely fixed cost base, so a sustained low-volatility regime would pressure both revenue and margins. Current volumes are cyclically elevated on geopolitical stress; a de-escalation that calms markets would remove a tailwind even as it helps the broader tape.

Operational concentration is a live risk: on November 27, 2025, CME's largest data center (CyrusOne) suffered a critical cooling failure from human error, forcing a full market halt and a late open. Management called the impact "not material," but it underscores third-party single-point-of-failure risk during the multi-year Google Cloud migration, which also carries elevated dual-running costs.

Legal and governance: the long-running Class B shareholder class action went to a jury that returned a unanimous verdict for CME on all counts (July 25, 2025), with post-trial motions and likely appeal pending — no accrual required. The combined Chairman/CEO role and the Class B member governance structure are structural idiosyncrasies that constrain management's pricing latitude. A June 17, 2026 8-K flags a director/officer departure/election worth monitoring for succession context.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 42.0% against 23.7% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 70th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (42.0% near vs 27.6% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent move to resolve. Put/call skew is call-heavy (-3.5%) and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.49 to 0.40 as the price rose ~3.8% into the July 22 print, so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the overhang. The implied ±1.7% near-term move is modest. Net read: the market is pricing event risk (earnings and regulatory headlines) with elevated near-term vol, but positioning is balanced rather than skewed bearish — consistent with a two-sided setup, and the rich near-term IV favors patience on entry until the vol crush post-event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
CME is a dominant, capital-light, network-moated toll-booth with 62.5% net margins and ~64% FCF conversion, trading at ~20x forward — a discount to the 26x peer median that its quality does not justify. Fundamentals are accelerating (Q1 revenue +14%, EPS +21%, record volumes), and the ~24% de-rating is a competitive-narrative overhang, not a fundamentals story. If the moat holds — network effects in liquidity and margin efficiency have historically defeated challengers — and if CME's litigation/lobbying constrains the regulatory-arbitrage threat, the multiple re-rates toward the peer group. CMESC securities clearing (tapping the SEC's 2026-27 Treasury/repo clearing mandate) and Treasury Link add genuine TAM optionality that deepens the interest-rate franchise exactly where FMX attacks. Generous capital return (special dividend + buyback) supports the stock; management bought back $536M in Q1 at ~$291.58, above today's price. Bull case: mid-$300s, consistent with peer-multiple re-rating on ~$13 forward EPS.

### Bear Case
The regulatory barrier that protected incumbents is genuinely eroding. If 24/7 perpetual-futures venues (Coinbase/Kalshi) capture derivatives volume under lighter regulation, and FMX takes share in the rate-futures crown jewel, the vertical model's premium multiple compresses further. Estimates are already falling (-6.5% current-quarter over 90 days). The investment-income lever reverses as the Fed cuts. Volume is cyclically elevated on geopolitical volatility that a de-escalation would remove. Micro-contract mix shift structurally compresses rate per contract. Insiders sold ~$12M into the May peak with no dip-buying. Bear case: low $200s to $226 (the analyst low), roughly -10% from current, if the competitive fears prove justified and the volatility tailwind fades.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-lean).** The thesis is a positive 12-month growth story (accelerating fundamentals, cheap versus peers, moat intact) held back by an undated binary regulatory fork, falling estimates, and rate-sensitivity headwinds. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the score has drifted from 7.5 (May) to 6.2 as competitive position, management quality, and risk factors were all marked down on the perpetual-futures/FMX overhang and insider selling. New data (Q2 record volumes, but a slight revenue miss and continued negative revisions) confirms rather than reverses the drift — fundamentals strong, forward visibility clouded.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable resolution or clear de-escalation of the CFTC perpetual-futures dispute (court ruling in CME's favor, CFTC narrowing the permission, or evidence perpetual-futures venues are not taking meaningful derivatives share from CME).
- Estimate-revision inflection: current-year and next-year consensus turning positive over a 30-day window, reversing the current -1.7%/-0.9% trend.
- A pullback toward the low-$230s (below the peer-discounted forward multiple) with fundamentals intact, where risk/reward turns clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of actual volume/open-interest share loss in the interest-rate/Treasury complex to FMX, or in crypto/broader derivatives to Coinbase/Kalshi.
- Adverse regulatory action mandating utility/not-for-profit clearing structure or portability of open positions to member clearing houses.
- ADV reverting below prior-year levels in a normalized-volatility regime combined with accelerating rate-per-contract compression, pressuring both revenue and margins.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 2026, late October) for volume durability and rate-per-contract trend; ongoing monitoring of the CME v. CFTC litigation and any CFTC rulemaking; the December 31, 2026 Treasury-clearing mandate go-live and CMESC traction versus FICC. The Fed's rate path is a continuous input given the investment-income sensitivity.

## Conclusion

CME is a high-quality franchise delivering accelerating operating results — Q1 2026 revenue +14%, EPS +21%, record volumes, ~70% operating margins, ~64% FCF conversion, and an AA- balance sheet whose apparent distress metrics are a screening artifact of pass-through clearing collateral. At ~20x forward it is cheaper than a peer group trading at 26x median, and the ~24% de-rating from the March high reflects a competitive/regulatory narrative rather than fundamental deterioration. That combination would ordinarily point to a BUY.

What holds us at HOLD is the confluence of a genuine, undated, binary overhang (CFTC-permitted perpetual futures, the CME v. CFTC lawsuit, and FMX's assault on the rate-futures crown jewel), falling forward estimates (-6.5% current-quarter over 90 days), a large investment-income lever that compresses as the Fed cuts, cyclically elevated volatility-driven volumes, and insider selling of ~$12M into the peak with no subsequent dip-buying. The cheap-versus-peers valuation is a discount with an identifiable cause, and closing it requires the moat to hold and the litigation to break CME's way — outcomes we cannot yet handicap with confidence. We would move constructive toward a BUY on an estimate-revision inflection, a favorable regulatory resolution, or accumulation into the low-$230s. Until then, this is a growth-lean HOLD with a base-case anchor near the ~$285 consensus median (~14% upside) balanced against a low-$200s bear case if the competitive fears prove justified.
