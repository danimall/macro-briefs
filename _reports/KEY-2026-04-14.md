## Executive Summary

KeyCorp has executed the most consequential balance sheet repositioning among super-regional banks in recent years. The company sold approximately $10 billion in low-yielding mortgage-backed securities in H2 2024, absorbed a $1.8 billion loss, and reinvested proceeds into higher-yielding assets, funded in part by Bank of Nova Scotia's $2.8 billion strategic investment for a 14.9% equity stake. Net interest income rose 23% YoY to $4.67 billion in 2025, NIM expanded 53 basis points to 2.69%, and net income swung from -$161 million to +$1.83 billion.

The transformation is real but incomplete. Management's 2026 guidance calls for NII growth of 8-10%, an exit NIM of 3.00-3.05% by Q4, and revenue of approximately $8 billion. The medium-term target of 15%+ ROTCE by end-2027 implies 30-40% net income growth from 2025 levels. At ~12.7x forward earnings with a 3.8% dividend yield, the valuation reflects skepticism about this trajectory that appears unwarranted given the mechanical nature of the NIM expansion (fixed-rate securities repricing, consumer loan runoff, C&I growth). The primary risks are CRE credit deterioration (charge-offs doubled YoY in multifamily), uninsured deposit concentration at 45%, and the governance implications of Scotiabank potentially increasing its stake to 19.99%.

## Company Overview

KeyCorp operates as a super-regional bank holding company with $184.4 billion in total consolidated assets, headquartered in Cleveland, Ohio. KeyBank National Association, its principal subsidiary, serves customers through 940 full-service branches and 1,120 ATMs across 15 states, with approximately 17,900 employees.

The company is organized into two business segments:

**Consumer Bank** ($527M net income in 2025, +110% YoY): Retail banking, wealth management ($70 billion AUM, a record), mortgage, home equity, credit cards, and treasury services for individuals and small businesses.

**Commercial Bank** ($1.45B net income in 2025, +33% YoY): Middle market, large corporate, and institutional banking, including KeyBanc Capital Markets (KBCM), a nationally significant investment banking platform providing underwriting, syndicated finance, M&A advisory, and trading. KEY is also the #1 or near-#1 commercial mortgage loan servicer in the US, administering $566.6 billion in CRE loans.

The KBCM platform and CRE servicing franchise differentiate KEY from most super-regional peers. Investment banking and debt placement fees totaled $780 million in 2025 (+13.5% YoY), providing meaningful revenue diversification beyond spread income.

## Financial Analysis

### The 2024-2025 Transformation

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | Change |
|--------|------|------|--------|
| Net Interest Income (TE) | $4,671M | $3,810M | +23% |
| Net Interest Margin | 2.69% | 2.16% | +53 bps |
| Adjusted Revenue (TE) | $7,513M | $6,455M | +16% |
| Net Income | $1,829M | -$161M | Turnaround |
| ROTCE | 11.85% | -3.01% | Turnaround |
| CET1 Ratio | 11.78% | 11.92% | Well-capitalized |
| Tangible BV/Share | $13.77 | $11.70 | +18% |
| NCOs/Avg Loans | 0.41% | 0.41% | Stable |

Three interconnected actions drove the recovery: (1) the $10 billion securities portfolio repositioning, which lifted AFS portfolio yield from 2.71% to 3.73%; (2) the Scotiabank capital infusion that funded the repositioning without diluting capital ratios; and (3) intentional balance sheet mix shift — running off ~$2.4 billion in low-yielding consumer loans while growing C&I loans $4.8 billion (+9%).

### Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The loan-to-deposit ratio of 72.5% sits well below management's 80% target, providing significant capacity for loan growth without deposit pressure. Total deposits of $148.7 billion are funding $107.6 billion in loans. Interest-bearing deposit costs declined to 2.41% from 2.82%, reflecting easing deposit competition. Wholesale funding dropped to $11 billion from $14.2 billion.

Available contingent liquidity of $97 billion provides a substantial buffer. The parent company holds $4.9 billion in cash and short-term investments, sufficient for 24+ months of obligations.

### Capital Return

Management authorized a $1 billion share repurchase program in March 2025 and repurchased 11.1 million shares in Q4 2025. The quarterly dividend of $0.205/share ($0.82 annualized) yields approximately 3.8% at current prices.

### Credit Quality

Headline net charge-offs remain stable at 41 basis points of average loans. However, the composition is shifting. CRE mortgage charge-offs jumped from 0.27% to 0.65%, driven by multifamily and senior housing stress. Total CRE charge-offs reached $87 million, up from $38 million. Nonperforming assets improved 19% to $627 million, and ALLL coverage of nonperforming loans rose to 232% from 186%.

The provision for credit losses increased to $471 million from $335 million, with management citing "elevated economic uncertainty and loan growth." Under a downside economic scenario, management estimates the ALLL build would be approximately 1.7x for commercial and 1.6x for consumer portfolios, implying potential for $700 million or more in additional provisioning under severe stress.

## Growth Analysis

### NIM Expansion Trajectory

The core growth driver is mechanical NIM expansion as the repositioned securities portfolio and loan book reprice:

- 2024: 2.16%
- 2025: 2.69%
- Q4 2026 exit rate guidance: 3.00-3.05%
- End-2027 target: 3.25%+

Three quantifiable factors drive this: (a) AFS securities reinvesting at 3.67%+ weighted average yield versus legacy yields below 2%, with an 8-year weighted average maturity creating a multi-year repricing tailwind; (b) continued runoff of low-yielding consumer loans being replaced by higher-yielding C&I originations; (c) deposit costs declining as rate competition eases.

### 2026 Guidance

- Revenue (TE): approximately $8 billion (+7% YoY)
- NII (TE): up 8-10%
- Adjusted noninterest expense: up 3-4% (positive operating leverage continuing)
- Average loans: up 1-2% (commercial up approximately 5%)
- NCOs: 40-45 basis points

### Fee Income Diversification

Investment banking and debt placement fees ($780 million, +13.5%), wealth management AUM at a record $70 billion, and the CRE servicing franchise ($567 billion administered) provide non-interest revenue diversification that most super-regional peers cannot match. However, KBCM revenues are cyclical and tied to capital markets activity, which faces headwinds from current geopolitical and macro uncertainty.

### Data Center CRE: Emerging Growth Vertical

KEY's data center CRE exposure grew from $152 million (December 2024) to $695 million (December 2025), a 4.6x increase. This positions the bank to capture AI infrastructure financing demand, leveraging its existing CRE underwriting and servicing capabilities.

### Medium-Term Target Assessment

Management's 15%+ ROTCE target by end-2027 requires approximately 30-40% net income growth from 2025's $1.83 billion base. This is ambitious given the macro backdrop. Achieving it requires: continued NIM expansion to 3.25%+, fee revenue growth, disciplined expense management, and credit losses remaining contained. I assign roughly 55-60% probability to achieving the target within the stated timeframe, with the most likely shortfall cause being higher-than-expected credit costs from CRE or macro deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $16.50 per share, KEY trades at:

- ~12.7x forward earnings
- ~1.2x tangible book value ($13.77 TBV/share)
- 3.8% dividend yield

These multiples are reasonable for a super-regional bank in the middle of a NIM recovery cycle. The 12.7x forward P/E is roughly in line with the super-regional peer median but represents a discount to higher-quality names like USB, PNC, and MTB. Given NIM expanding 50+ bps over the next two years and ROTCE improving from 11.85% toward 15%, the current multiple undervalues the forward earnings power.

A simple framework: if KEY achieves $1.60 in EPS for FY2026 (consistent with the ~$8 billion revenue guidance and current cost/credit structure) and the market applies a 14-15x multiple reflecting improving profitability, the stock would be worth $22-24. At the 2027 ROTCE target of 15%+, earnings power would approach $2.00+/share, supporting $26-30 at peer-average multiples.

Tangible book value of $13.77 provides a reasonable downside floor. The stock currently trades at 1.2x TBV; even in a stress scenario where TBV declines 10%, the stock at 1.0x TBV would imply ~$12.40, roughly 25% downside from current levels.

Morgan Stanley's March 2026 price target raise to $26 (from $24, Equal Weight) provides one external reference point.

## Competitive Landscape

KEY occupies a distinctive niche among super-regionals. The KBCM investment banking platform generates fee revenue that approaches the scale of much larger institutions relative to its asset base. The $567 billion CRE servicing/administration franchise is a genuine competitive moat — building these relationships and systems takes years, and the recurring fee stream provides counter-cyclical support (loan workouts during stress actually increase servicing activity).

However, the bank lacks the deposit franchise scale of PNC ($556B assets), USB ($679B), or Truist ($526B). At $184 billion in assets, KEY is a mid-tier Category IV bank that faces structural disadvantages in funding costs and regulatory efficiency relative to both larger and smaller competitors. The 45% uninsured deposit ratio is a vulnerability that larger money-center banks and smaller community banks with higher insured ratios do not share.

The Scotiabank relationship creates a unique competitive dynamic. Cross-border banking collaboration (US-Canada), potential referral of Scotiabank's corporate clients to KBCM, and access to Canadian capital markets capabilities represent strategic upside. The two Scotiabank-designated board directors create governance risk but also bring international banking perspective.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Credit Deterioration (HIGH)

CRE loans total $16.6 billion (15.6% of total loans), with multifamily comprising 45.8% of the CRE book ($7.6 billion including $2.15 billion in construction). Multifamily oversupply in certain urban markets is widely acknowledged by management and industry analysts. If multifamily or office stress accelerates in a recessionary environment, provisioning could consume a significant portion of earnings. Under management's own downside scenario, the commercial ALLL increase of ~1.7x implies $700M+ in additional reserves.

### Uninsured Deposit Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH)

At 45% of total deposits uninsured ($66.2 billion), KEY carries above-average deposit flight risk. While $12 billion is collateralized by government-backed securities and available contingent liquidity is $97 billion, the 2023 SVB/FRC experience demonstrated that uninsured deposit runs can overwhelm even well-capitalized institutions. Any crisis of confidence affecting super-regionals would hit KEY harder than peers with lower uninsured ratios.

### Scotiabank Governance Risk (MODERATE)

Scotiabank's potential increase to 19.99% would make it the dominant shareholder without triggering bank holding company controls. The 10-K explicitly warns that Scotiabank's interests "may conflict with the interests of our other shareholders" and that its presence "may have the effect of deterring takeovers." Minority shareholders would have limited recourse if Scotiabank's strategic priorities diverge from value maximization.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)

KEY shifted to modestly asset-sensitive positioning in 2025, with NII declining 0.35% in a -200bp scenario and rising 0.41% in +200bp. The 67% variable-rate loan book (up from 63%) means aggressive Fed cuts would compress NII. However, with the Fed at 3.64% and inflation running above target, aggressive cuts are unlikely in 2026. A 5% change in deposit beta assumptions swings simulation results by approximately 97 basis points, highlighting model sensitivity.

### Macro/Tariff Risk (MODERATE-HIGH)

The 10-K explicitly cites tariffs and trade policy as risks to borrower creditworthiness and asset prices. Elevated energy costs and record-low consumer sentiment make recession within the next 12 months a material probability. A recession would pressure both credit quality and fee revenue (particularly KBCM capital markets fees).

### Management Transition Risk (LOW-MODERATE)

Three officer departures/transitions since January 2025, including the CRO role. The new CRO (Mohit Ramani) brings large-bank experience, but turnover in risk management leadership during elevated economic uncertainty warrants attention.

### Options Market Signal

No current options data was provided. Given KEY's beta of 1.09 and elevated super-regional bank implied volatility relative to money-center peers in the current geopolitical environment, investors should expect elevated options premium on KEY. The lack of specific options data limits assessment of informed positioning ahead of the Q1 2026 earnings report (consensus $0.41 EPS, +24% YoY).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($24-26, +45-58% upside)

NIM expansion continues mechanically toward 3.00% by Q4 2026 and 3.25%+ by end-2027. C&I loan growth of 5%+ drives revenue above guidance. Credit losses remain contained at 40-45 bps as multifamily stress proves manageable. KBCM benefits from elevated capital markets volatility. Scotiabank collaboration generates incremental revenue. ROTCE reaches 15% by end-2027. Share buybacks reduce count meaningfully. The market re-rates KEY from 12.7x to 14-15x forward earnings on improved profitability trajectory. FY2027 EPS power of $2.00+ at 13-14x = $26-28.

### Bear Case ($12-14, -15 to -27% downside)

Recession materializes in H1 2026, driven by energy costs and consumer weakness. CRE losses accelerate beyond management's downside scenario, requiring $1 billion+ in provisioning. Capital markets fees contract as deal activity freezes. Uninsured deposits face pressure during a broader confidence shock. NIM expansion stalls as the Fed eventually cuts rates to combat recession. ROTCE stagnates at 10-11%. Scotiabank's increased stake creates governance overhang. Stock re-rates toward 1.0x TBV (~$12-14).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $22-24** (14-15x FY2026E EPS of ~$1.55-1.60). This represents 33-45% upside from ~$16.50, driven by continued NIM expansion, modest loan growth, and multiple expansion as the market recognizes the durability of the earnings recovery.

**Bull case target: $26** (13x FY2027E EPS of ~$2.00). 58% upside. Requires execution against the 15% ROTCE target and benign credit environment.

**Bear case target: $12-14** (1.0x stressed TBV). 15-27% downside. Requires recession + CRE credit blow-up + deposit pressure.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (consensus $0.41, +24% YoY), NIM trajectory confirmation through quarterly reports, and resolution of Scotiabank stake increase.

**Upside/downside from current (~$16.50):** Base case +33-45%, bull case +58%, bear case -15% to -27%. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 2:1 on the base case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM fails to reach 2.85% by mid-2026 (below the trajectory toward 3.00-3.05% Q4 exit), indicating the securities repositioning benefit is stalling
2. Net charge-offs exceed 60 bps for two consecutive quarters, indicating credit deterioration beyond management's guidance range
3. Uninsured deposits decline more than 5% in a single quarter, indicating depositor confidence erosion

## Conclusion

KeyCorp has completed the painful phase of its balance sheet transformation. The $1.8 billion securities loss in 2024, funded by Scotiabank's capital, is now generating measurable and ongoing NIM expansion. The trajectory from 2.16% NIM to 2.69% to 3.00%+ is mechanically driven by fixed-rate asset repricing and is unlikely to reverse absent aggressive Fed easing (which is itself unlikely given the current inflation trajectory).

At 12.7x forward earnings with a 3.8% dividend yield, the stock offers asymmetric risk/reward among super-regionals. The primary risks (CRE credit stress, uninsured deposit concentration, Scotiabank governance) are real but manageable for a well-capitalized institution with $97 billion contingent liquidity, 11.78% CET1, and 232% NPL coverage. The combination of NIM expansion, capital return, and potential multiple re-rating supports a BUY recommendation with a 12-18 month base case target of $22-24.
