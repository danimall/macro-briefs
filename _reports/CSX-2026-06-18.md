**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CSX is one of two dominant Eastern U.S. Class I freight railroads, operating ~20,000 route miles across a region housing roughly two-thirds of the U.S. population. The investment story has two competing threads. The first is a credible operational turnaround under new CEO Steve Angel, the former Linde chief installed in September 2025, whose first full quarter as operator produced a 560bps year-over-year operating-margin expansion, a 6% reduction in operating expenses, and EPS up 26% to $0.43. The second is a structural overhang: the pending $85B Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger would create the first U.S. transcontinental railroad and reshape the competitive environment CSX has operated in for decades. The stock is effectively a derivative play on a Surface Transportation Board ruling now pushed into 2027.

FY2025 was a trough year: revenue fell 3% to $14.09B, operating income fell 14%, EPS fell 14% to $1.54, and operating margin compressed to 32.1% from a 2022 peak of 40.6%. The drivers were a mix of secular (coal revenue -15%) and transitory (Hurricane Helene rebuild ~$520M cumulative, Howard Street tunnel disruption ~$53M, CEO transition costs, casualty charges). Q1 2026 is the first hard evidence that the new management playbook reverses the margin erosion rather than merely the cyclical trough.

The problem is price. CSX has rallied ~42% from its $31.80 52-week low to ~$45.57, leaving it at ~24x forward earnings against rising-but-modest estimate revisions (+3.6% on current-year EPS over 90 days) and a high-single-digit/low-double-digit EPS growth profile. The self-help thesis is no longer a secret, and the strategic uncertainty around UP-NS is a genuine binary that the operational story cannot offset. This is a hold for the earnings-recovery trajectory and the demonstrated insider conviction, not a fresh-entry buy.

## Company Overview

CSX runs a quasi-monopolistic Eastern rail franchise connecting every major metropolitan area in the eastern United States, plus Ontario and Quebec, linking ~250 short-line railroads and 70+ ports. The right-of-way is impossible to replicate, which is the source of the durable pricing power and high incremental margins characteristic of Class I rails.

FY2025 revenue of $14.1B breaks down as follows:
- **Merchandise (~62%, $8.8B):** Chemicals ($2.8B, the largest single category), agriculture/food, automotive, minerals, forest products, metals, fertilizers. Highest-margin, most stable book with pricing power (revenue/unit +1% YoY).
- **Intermodal (~15%, $2.1B):** The growth engine — volume +4% in 2025, +6% in Q1 2026, aided by international port volume and domestic wins.
- **Coal (~13%, $1.9B):** The structural decliner — revenue down 15% in 2025 on weak export met/thermal coal and lower benchmark rates; revenue/unit -13%.
- **Trucking (~6%, $0.8B):** Quality Carriers, being wound down (see Risk Assessment).
- **Other (~4%):** demurrage and ancillary.

The coal-to-intermodal mix shift is structurally favorable: as the most volatile, commodity-price-sensitive revenue stream shrinks and intermodal grows (further enabled by the completed Howard Street tunnel double-stack clearance in Baltimore), the revenue base becomes more stable over time, though intermodal carries lower revenue per unit.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trend documents a clear earnings decline that bottomed in 2025:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $14.85B | $14.66B | $14.54B | $14.09B |
| Operating income | $6.02B | $5.56B | $5.25B | $4.52B |
| Operating margin | 40.6% | 37.9% | 36.1% | 32.1% |
| Net income | $4.17B | $3.72B | $3.47B | $2.89B |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $1.79 | $1.54 |

Operating margin compressed ~850bps over three years. The deterioration reflected both the coal collapse and operational underperformance that the prior management team failed to arrest, which is the context for Angel's appointment.

**Q1 2026 reverses the trend.** Operating income +20% to $1.25B, net earnings +25% to $807M, EPS +26% to $0.43, revenue +2%, volume +3%, with operating margin up ~560bps on a 6% opex reduction. Management raised the 2026 revenue-growth outlook to mid-single digits. The earnings surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in four quarters, with the March 2026 quarter beating by 9.2% (the December 2025 miss of -6.1% was the trough quarter). Consensus has responded — current-year EPS estimates are up 3.6% over 90 days to $1.91, next-year up 4.0% to $2.17.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $4.6B (down from $5.2B); FCF before dividends $1.79B (down from $2.78B). The FY2025 FCF decline was partly one-time: $429M of previously-postponed taxes paid with no offsetting deferral, plus storm-rebuild capex and a $96M locomotive prepayment. Leverage is high but normal for a railroad: long-term debt $18.2B, D/E ~2.3x, total equity ~$13.1B. Ratings are stable investment-grade (Fitch A-, Moody's A3, S&P BBB+).

The working-capital deficit of $583M (current ratio 0.81) and the resulting Altman Z-Score of 1.33 ("distress") are model artifacts, not signals of fragility. Negative working capital is standard for capital-intensive rails; CSX carries a $1.2B undrawn revolver plus a $1.0B CP program. ROE of 22%, ROA of 6.6%, and a 32% operating margin describe a healthy, highly profitable business. Capital allocation remains disciplined: $1.4B buybacks plus $972M dividends in 2025, a 21st consecutive annual dividend increase (+8%), and a steady share-count reduction from 2.14B (2022) to 1.87B (2025), a ~12% cut that supports EPS. 2026 capex is guided below $2.4B.

The financial-health score of 6.5 reflects strong cash generation and an appropriate (if leveraged) capital structure offset by the recent FCF compression and the cyclical sensitivity of the revenue base.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is recovery-driven, not secular acceleration. The forward thesis rests on three legs: (1) operational self-help under Angel, which directly converts to margin and EPS through opex discipline regardless of revenue, (2) intermodal volume growth structurally aided by the Howard Street tunnel completion, and (3) the natural tailwind of coal becoming a smaller share of the mix, reducing earnings volatility.

The estimate-revision data confirms positive but modest momentum: current and next quarter both revised up. This is meaningful directional confirmation but well short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a sharp inflection deserving an aggressive growth premium. CSX is recovering, not re-accelerating into a new growth regime. The implied EPS path — roughly $1.91 (2026) to $2.17 (2027), ~14% growth — combines margin recovery with continued buybacks rather than topline expansion.

The growth-prospects score of 6.5 credits the demonstrated margin recovery and the favorable mix shift while recognizing that the topline growth ceiling is structurally modest for a mature Class I rail in a region with declining coal demand.

The peer set is heterogeneous and a poor direct comparator: FDX/UPS are parcel/logistics with structurally different economics, and MMM/JCI/WM span industrials and waste. The more relevant comparison is the Class I rail cohort, where CSX's ~24x forward multiple sits roughly in line with the group and reflects a normal rail premium for the franchise quality.

The case against paying up here is straightforward. The stock has re-rated ~42% off its low, and the analyst mean target of $46.25 and median of $47.75 sit essentially at the current price, with the high at $53 and the low at $30. On a stock with modest single-digit revenue growth and rising-but-unspectacular estimate revisions (not the >30% velocity that would justify treating targets as stale floors), proximity to consensus is informative: the re-rating has substantially captured the self-help thesis. The earnings-recovery story is now well understood. The dividend yield of ~1.2% provides little valuation support.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a fairly-valued stock: the multiple is defensible for the franchise quality and recovery trajectory, but there is no identifiable mispricing to exploit, and a "cheap-looking" P/E relative to a non-comparable peer median is not a reason to reward valuation. Asymmetry to the upside has largely been realized.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining industry event is the **Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern $85B merger**, agreed in summer 2025, which would create a ~50,000-mile, 43-state transcontinental system. As of mid-2026 the STB accepted the application but paused review on May 28, 2026, demanding more information, with a decision expected in 2027. Opposition is building: Canadian Pacific Kansas City urged scrutiny, and rail unions oppose it.

The mechanism that matters for CSX: if UP-NS is approved, a competitor could offer seamless coast-to-coast single-line service, a structural disadvantage in long-haul intermodal. CSX would likely need a Western partner (BNSF being the natural counterparty), setting up a defensive second mega-merger. This BNSF inference is strategic logic, not a disclosed transaction. If the deal is rejected or heavily conditioned, the status quo is preserved and the overhang clears.

The competitive-position trajectory has weakened relative to the prior assessment, which is why this score has come down from 7.5 to the current 5.5. The standalone moat is intact: the right-of-way is irreplaceable and pricing power persists, but the prospective competitive environment is deteriorating in a way the market cannot yet price because the regulatory outcome is unknown. The proxy quantifies the recent underperformance: 5-year TSR of 128 materially lags the peer group's 190, and Economic Profit (CSX's chosen returns-above-cost-of-capital metric) fell from $2.34B (2024) to $1.62B (2025). CSX is the strategic outlier in the consolidation endgame.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Industry consolidation / strategic isolation (the central risk).** The UP-NS outcome is the single most important variable for the stock over the next 1–2 years and is genuinely binary. The STB pause is a near-term reprieve, not a resolution.
2. **Coal secular decline.** Both export (benchmark-driven) and domestic (gas substitution, emissions regulation) coal are structurally pressured.
3. **Hazmat / catastrophic accident liability.** The common-carrier mandate forces hazmat transport regardless of risk; a major derailment (the Norfolk Southern East Palestine precedent) could create large liabilities. Personal-injury reserves are $154M, environmental reserves $156M across ~220 sites.
4. **Severe weather.** Hurricane Helene's destruction of the Blue Ridge subdivision required substantial rebuild capex; the 10-K expects weather frequency/severity to rise.
5. **Cybersecurity.** Designated critical infrastructure, explicitly flagging nation-state threat actors.
6. **Labor.** ~16,900 of ~23,000 employees unionized under the Railway Labor Act; new agreements ratified by ~75% of the workforce, remainder in negotiation.
7. **Macro/trade.** Tariffs and trade-policy shifts directly affect automotive production, intermodal port flows, and export coal.

The risk-factors score of 5.0 reflects the unpriced binary regulatory outcome and the persistent hazmat/weather tail, partially offset by the fact that coal decline and sector cyclicality are well understood and already priced.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options picture is dominated by an apparent data artifact: 1-week ATM IV reads 122.9% against 1-year historical vol of 22.3%, a 100-point spread that does not correspond to any known imminent catalyst (the last earnings were April 22; the next report is not until late July). The 1-month tenor (24.9% IV) and 6-month tenor (25.0%) are far more credible and sit only modestly above historical vol, indicating the term structure beyond the noisy front week is unremarkable. Skew is mildly call-heavy (-8.6% at 1-month, P/C OI ratio 0.39), and short interest is low at 2.1% of float (3 days to cover): there is no organized bearish positioning. The single unusual print, 2,360 contracts on the $48 call expiring June 26 (10.5x OI), is consistent with mild bullish near-term positioning rather than a hedging signal. The options market is not flagging stress despite the corrupted front-week IV, and positioning leans marginally constructive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Angel's operational playbook, proven over years at Linde, continues to compound margin gains through 2026 and 2027. Opex discipline drives operating ratio improvement that the Street under-models; intermodal growth (helped by Howard Street) offsets coal decline; buybacks shrink the share count further. The STB rejects or heavily conditions UP-NS, removing the strategic overhang. EPS reaches roughly the high-$2 range in 2027, and the stock re-rates toward the $53 high target.

**Bear case.** UP-NS is approved, CSX faces a transcontinental competitor in long-haul intermodal, and the market discounts a defensive, value-uncertain BNSF combination. Coal volumes deteriorate faster than expected, a major derailment generates large liabilities, or weather-driven capex recurs. The self-help margin gains prove partly cyclical (volume-driven) rather than structural, and the re-rated multiple compresses. The stock retraces toward the $30 low target.

The base case sits between: the turnaround is real and continues at a decelerating pace, the regulatory outcome remains an unresolved overhang into 2027, and the stock trades in a range as the operational improvement is offset by strategic uncertainty.

The CEO's ~$1M open-market purchase of 25,000 shares at $40.27 in March 2026 — code "P", near the lows — is the strongest discrete insider signal and the primary reason the management-quality score is 7.5. It is a genuine conviction trade by an operator with a demonstrable margin-improvement record, distinct from routine grants. The CFO's June option-exercise-and-sell transactions (struck ~$22–23) are routine monetization, not bearish. The presence of Mantle Ridge's Paul Hilal on the board signals continued activist alignment with performance. Compensation is anchored to Economic Profit, a returns-above-cost-of-capital metric well aligned with shareholders.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-recovery rationale; the position is worth holding for the earnings trajectory but does not offer asymmetric entry at current prices.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s (within ~5–8% of the CEO's $40.27 buy level) that restores valuation cushion without fundamental deterioration.
- Two consecutive quarters confirming the margin recovery is structural — operating ratio improvement sustained on flat-to-modest volume, with opex reductions holding.
- STB rejection or heavy conditioning of UP-NS, which removes the strategic overhang and would justify a higher competitive-position score.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- STB approval of UP-NS on terms favorable to the combined entity, with no credible CSX defensive path, indicating durable competitive disadvantage in long-haul intermodal.
- Reversal of the operational improvement — operating ratio backsliding, a guidance cut, or estimate revisions turning negative.
- A material catastrophic-accident liability event or a faster-than-modeled coal collapse.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the late-July 2026 Q2 report (test of margin durability and full-year guidance), with the dominant variable being the STB's UP-NS decision now expected in 2027. The score trajectory (6.1 → 6.0, HOLD → HOLD) reflects a stable-to-modestly-declining view; the current analysis confirms the hold and explicitly raises the bar for any future BUY given that the re-rating has captured much of the self-help upside.

## Conclusion

CSX is a high-quality franchise executing a credible CEO-led turnaround, validated by Q1 2026's margin expansion, the opex cut, and a meaningful open-market CEO purchase near the lows. The operational story is real, but the stock has already re-rated ~42% off its low to ~24x forward earnings, sits essentially at the consensus mean target, and carries a genuine binary strategic overhang from the UP-NS merger that the standalone self-help thesis cannot resolve and that the market cannot yet price. The recovery thesis is well understood and largely captured. Hold for the earnings trajectory and the management alignment; wait for either a better entry price or resolution of the regulatory uncertainty before adding.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth_hold). Overall score 5.95.
