# Investment Research Report: NewMarket Corporation (NEU)

**Price basis:** $850.16 (official close; the $854.33 last trade is a +0.49% extended-hours diagnostic and is not used for valuation arithmetic). Shares outstanding 9,196,406 (10-Q cover, 6/30/2026). Market capitalization $7,818M. Information cutoff 2026-08-03.

---

## Executive Summary

NewMarket reported Q2 2026 EPS of $14.54, up 22.8% year over year, and the stock gapped higher on July 30 to an all-time high of $876.33 before settling near $850. Decomposing the quarter changes the read. Petroleum additives shipment volume fell 3.8% in the quarter and 5.3% in the half; the entire revenue and margin gain came from selling price and mix (+$29.6M), which both the 10-Q MD&A and the press release attribute specifically to surcharges implemented in response to Middle East supply-chain cost inflation. A 320bp lower effective tax rate (21.5% vs. 24.7%) contributed roughly $0.60 of the $2.70 EPS increase, and consolidated R&D fell $3.5M in the half while petroleum additives H1 gross profit actually declined $0.4M. On a rolling four-quarter basis, management discloses petroleum additives segment margin of 20.7% and describes it as "within our historical range," not above it.

The balance sheet and the capital-return machine are the strongest parts of the story and are not in question. Credit-agreement leverage was 1.20x against a 3.75x covenant, company-calculated net debt/EBITDA 1.0x, $589M of the $900M revolver undrawn to January 2029, current ratio 2.25. FY2025 free cash flow of $491.4M (operating cash flow $569M less $77.6M capex) is a 6.3% yield on the current market capitalization, and the dividend consumed only 22% of it. Share count fell 3.4% in 18 months. CEO total compensation of $3.69M against $418.7M of net income, with incentive metrics tied to absolute GAAP operating profit, net income and EPS, is unusually shareholder-friendly for a $7.8B company.

The problem is price against what is knowable. Consolidated revenue has been flat at $2.7–2.8B for four years; management's own Item 1 states that drain-interval extension has "generally offset" demand growth from higher vehicle population and miles driven. The specialty materials segment, the source of the re-rating, produced operating profit of $12.4M in Q1 2026 (down 46.4% year over year on sales up 8.2%) and $22.3M in Q2, and H1 segment operating profit grew only 3.1% on 30.9% sales growth. The company itself repurchased 204,927 shares in H1 at an average of $622.95, bought 1,384 shares in April at $622.40, and bought none in May or June. The only insider open-market purchase in the window was a director at $619.21. Observable corporate and insider buying clusters roughly 27% below the current price. That is the clearest fact available on how the people with the most information view $850.

## Company Overview

NewMarket is a Richmond, Virginia holding company with two reportable segments. **Petroleum additives** (Afton Chemical) was 93.0% of FY2025 net sales at $2,534M — highly formulated lubricant and fuel additive packages sold to integrated oil companies and independent blenders, with no direct sales to end users or OEMs. Roughly 61% of consolidated FY2025 sales were outside the United States (North America ~40% of segment sales, EMEAI ~30%, Asia Pacific ~20%). **Specialty materials** was $182M (6.7%) and comprises AMPAC (acquired January 2024, $681M net of cash; ammonium, sodium and potassium perchlorates for solid rocket motors) and Calca Solutions (acquired October 2025, $218M; Ultra Pure and high-purity hydrazine for satellite propulsion). Ethyl Corporation, NewMarket Development and NewMarket Services sit in "all other" ($9M).

Management states it has committed "approximately $1 billion" to specialty materials since 2024 — reconcilable as ~$915M of purchase consideration plus an up-to-$100M project to add an ammonium perchlorate line increasing AP capacity by more than 50%, expected online toward the end of 2026. Language on the project timing is unchanged between the February 10-K and the July 10-Q, so there is no disclosed slip.

The 2022–2024 earnings improvement was entirely margin: gross margin expanded 860bp as raw-material costs deflated after the 2022 spike while selling prices held. **H1 2026:** net sales $1,416.8M (+1.2%), operating profit $306.9M (+1.3%), net income $251.8M (+6.2%), EPS $27.14 (+8.1%). Applying H1 2025's 23.9% effective rate to H1 2026 pretax income of $322.0M yields net income of $245.0M, so the lower rate supplied roughly $6.8M, or $0.73 per share — about 36% of the $2.03 EPS increase. Petroleum additives H1 operating profit rose $2.4M while segment gross profit fell $0.4M; the 10-Q attributes the difference to lower research, development and testing cost.

Four observed quarters span 21.4% to 43.2% margin. Management warns prospectively in both the 10-K and 10-Q of "substantial variation in quarterly results" for this segment and specifically flags government shutdown impact. Extrapolating Q2's 33.3% is extrapolating the second-best of four quarters. Purchase-accounting mechanics contributed: total amortization rose from $12.7M to $18.6M H1/H1 on Calca intangibles, and segment D&A from $17.7M to $24.2M. On a pre-D&A basis, segment EBITDA margin still fell from 53.7% to 47.1%, so incremental margin on the acquired revenue was thin.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $286.2M against $281.4M. Accounts payable rose $103.3M (to $341.6M) while receivables rose $58.6M and inventories $21.3M, holding reported net working-capital drag to −$3.6M. Management cites increased purchasing, higher raw material costs and normal invoice timing; the disclosed supplier finance program had immaterial confirmed invoices outstanding at both dates. Stripping the payables build would put H1 operating cash flow near $183M. Neither figure is a forecast, but the H1 number should not be doubled. Capex was $51.7M against FY guidance narrowed from $100–150M to $100–130M, so H2 capex steps up materially.

Debt at 6/30/2026 was $854.8M: 2.70% notes due 2031 ($395.8M net), revolver $309M (4.8% average in H1), and $150M of 3.78% private placements amortizing $50M each January through 2029. All fixed except the revolver. The $250M AMPAC term loan was retired in full during 2025. Net debt fell from $805.8M to $761.2M in six months while $182.0M was returned to shareholders.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.85 that appears on screens is a formula artifact: substituting the actual market value of equity for book equity in the fourth term ($7,818M ÷ $1,714.2M total liabilities = 4.56) lifts the score to roughly 4.9, well inside the safe zone. The revenue/assets term fell from 1.17x in FY2023 to 0.78x in FY2025 because acquisitions added ~$1.2B to the denominator. Distress risk is not a live consideration.

**Financial health: 7.4.** Genuine strength on leverage, liquidity, covenant headroom and cash conversion. Two deductions: H1 operating cash flow was payables-assisted, and $605.4M of prepaid pension cost equals 32.9% of book equity while contributing roughly $29.4M of non-service pension and OPEB income in H1 — about 9.1% of pretax income — on an 8.0% assumed return for equity-heavy U.S. plans. That is standard ASC 715 accounting, but it is non-operating, market-dependent earnings that flatter reported ROE of 23.5%.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated growth is modest and its composition is unfavorable. Petroleum additive shipment volume fell 4.9% in FY2025, roughly 7% in Q1 2026, 3.8% in Q2 2026 and 5.3% in H1 2026. Management attributes the decline to both market softness and a deliberate exit from low-margin business without quantifying either — the single most important undisclosed split in the file, because voluntary pruning and involuntary share loss look identical in the data and have opposite implications. Management's Outlook expects market softness to persist through 2026.

Growth therefore has to come from three places. First, price: Q2's +$29.6M of price/mix, driven by surcharges tied to a named exogenous cost event, is reversible by construction if Middle East logistics and raw-material costs normalize. The counter-case is real — NEU retained price when costs fell in 2023–24, producing 860bp of gross-margin expansion — but the surcharge is not sized in any disclosure, so neither outcome can be quantified.

Second, specialty materials. Sales grew 30.9% H1/H1 to $125.3M, but operating profit grew 3.1%. The dated catalyst is the AP line adding more than 50% capacity toward the end of 2026; specialty materials additions to long-lived assets were $33.4M in H1 2026 versus $5.0M in H1 2025, corroborating the ramp, and the Q2 press release broadened the expansion language to include high-purity hydrazine. The economics so far are unimpressive: annualizing H1 segment operating profit of $34.8M gives ~$70M on ~$1.0B committed, a 6.9% pretax / ~5.4% after-tax return, or ~10.5% pretax adding back ~$37M of annual purchase-accounting amortization. Both are below consolidated FY2025 ROA of 12.0%. The segment carries no disclosed backlog, remaining performance obligations or contract-award value; the $20M "Backlog" line in the Calca allocation is a purchase-accounting intangible with a five-year life, not revenue visibility.

Third, share count. Repurchases plus dividends have added roughly 2% per year to per-share growth, with $301.78M of the $500M authorization remaining through 12/31/2027.

Against these, purchase-accounting amortization is a locked-in GAAP drag: $37.3M in 2027 declining only to $32.9M by 2031, or $4.06 per share pretax and about $3.17 after tax — roughly 6.8% of trailing EPS — held flat for five years.

**Growth prospects: 5.2.** Flat four-year revenue, two years of volume decline, price-driven and partly reversible near-term growth, one dated capacity catalyst with unverified end-market tightness, and modest per-share accretion from buybacks.

## Valuation Assessment

At $850.16: market capitalization $7,818M; enterprise value $7,818M + $854.8M debt − $93.6M cash = $8,580M. Trailing EPS of $46.48 (Q3'25 $10.67 + Q4'25 $8.65 + Q1'26 $12.62 + Q2'26 $14.54) gives **18.3x**, matching the reported trailing multiple. Company-disclosed rolling four-quarter EBITDA of $733.8M gives **EV/EBITDA 11.7x**. Dividend of $12.00 annualized is a **1.41% yield**. FCF yield is 6.3% on FY2025 actuals and 5.6–6.0% on a FY2026 sensitivity of $440–470M, contingent on the payables question.

Peer context is heterogeneous and the median is not usable: WLK at negative earnings, ESI at 69.9x, BCPC at 33.9x, EMN at 21.2x, AXTA at 19.1x. The two cleanest comparables — Eastman and Axalta — trade at 19–21x with weaker balance sheets and more cyclical earnings. NEU at 18.3x is roughly in line to modestly cheap against those on headline multiples, and it deserves a quality premium on leverage (1.0x net debt/EBITDA), returns and dividend coverage.

Three adjustments erode that comfort. Trailing EPS embeds roughly 9% of pretax income from non-operating pension credits that a purchaser cannot distribute and that carry a disclosed sensitivity of −$10M of 2026 income per 100bp cut to expected return assumptions. It embeds a 21.8% H1 effective rate; normalizing toward 24.5% costs roughly $1.30 of EPS. And it embeds a surcharge of undisclosed magnitude within the $29.6M Q2 price/mix.

The 2025 return was multiple, not earnings: the proxy shows FY2025 TSR indexed at 191.93 against a peer group at 109.07, with TSR rising about 32 points while net income and operating profit both fell. The stock has since added a further 24% and sits 3.0% below the all-time high, 46.6% above the March low of $580.03. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but the absence of underlying earnings growth behind that appreciation is.

**Base case $870.** FY2027 EPS of roughly $50 — surcharge partially retained, specialty materials capacity contributing from 2027, volumes flat, tax normalizing toward 24%, 2% share shrink — at 17.5x. That is 2.3% above spot.
**Bull case $1,020.** Surcharges retained after costs normalize, AP and hydrazine capacity filled at Q2-type margins, volumes stabilize, and the market capitalizes specialty materials on defense-adjacent multiples: EPS ~$51 at 20x. +20%.
**Bear case $640.** Surcharge reverses with the cost event, volume declines persist near −5%, specialty materials reverts toward Q1-type quarters, tax normalizes, pension return assumption is cut: EPS $38–40 at 16x. −24.7%. This clusters near the $623 buyback average and above the $580 52-week low; the February 12 print produced a −14.4% single-day move on Q4 EPS of $8.65, establishing that the market will reprice this name hard on a weak quarter.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Fairly priced, not stretched, with poor asymmetry — roughly 2% to base against 25% to bear. A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong; here the multiple is defensible and the identifiable mispricing risk runs the other way.

## Competitive Landscape

Petroleum additives is a four-player oligopoly — Afton, Lubrizol (Berkshire Hathaway), Infineum (ExxonMobil/Shell JV) and Chevron Oronite — gated by formulation know-how and OEM/industry specification approval rather than capital intensity. NEU holds ~1,400 issued or pending patents, employs ~450 people in R&D and testing, and spent 5.2% of segment sales on R&D in FY2025. The evidence the moat is real: 19–22.5% segment operating margins sustained through a raw-material shock and two years of volume decline, and the ability to implement cost-recovery surcharges within a quarter of the cost event. The evidence the moat does not confer growth: flat revenue for four years and management's own statement that drain-interval extension offsets vehicle-population and miles-driven growth. All three principal competitors are subsidiaries or JVs of far larger parents with no standalone public reporting, which means NEU is the participant most visibly accountable for quarterly margin in a soft-volume market.

Specialty materials is a qualification moat rather than a technology moat. Management states it believes it is a leading global ammonium perchlorate producer and the leading U.S. producer of Ultra Pure and high-purity hydrazine, and that foreign AP producers are largely not qualified for the U.S. programs that constitute most domestic demand. These are company statements in a 10-K and were not independently corroborated; I cannot verify whether an industry-wide AP supply constraint exists or how NEU's capacity compares to competitors'. The +50% expansion is circumstantial evidence that management sees demand exceeding capacity.

Two points temper the market's apparent enthusiasm. Raw materials for the segment are described as available from multiple sources, a favorable contrast with petroleum additives' single-sourced inputs, but production is single-site (Cedar City, Utah for perchlorates, one Louisiana facility for hydrazine, with $29.4M of indefinite-lived water rights on the balance sheet signalling site dependence). And in both the 10-K and 10-Q Outlook, immediately after describing the ~$1B commitment, management writes that "our primary focus in the acquisition area remains on the petroleum additives industry" because that industry "will provide the greatest opportunity for solid returns." Management is signalling that the defense/space diversification is opportunistic, which matters if the market is capitalizing NEU as an emerging defense-materials company.

**Competitive position: 5.9.** Pricing power confirmed; volume trajectory negative for a second year with the pruning-versus-share-loss split undisclosed. Trajectory is mixed rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Surcharge reversibility (highest un-priced item).** Q2's price/mix is explicitly tied to Middle East supply-chain cost inflation. Magnitude is undisclosed, so the earnings at risk cannot be sized. At 3.0% below the all-time high the market is not pricing reversal. If Middle East logistics normalize, both the cost and the price should come out, and the timing may not match.

**U.S. government appropriations concentration.** A risk factor added with the 2024–25 acquisitions covers annual appropriations, incremental program funding, termination for convenience (in which case "the full profit anticipated under a given contract is unlikely to be realized"), and program curtailment. Management flags government shutdown impact in both the 10-K and 10-Q Outlook. This attaches to the segment holding $799M of the company's $923M of goodwill and intangibles.

**Volume erosion.** Second consecutive year, with management expecting softness through 2026 and no disclosure separating deliberate pruning from share loss.

**Pension dependency.** The $605.4M prepaid pension cost is 32.9% of book equity; 8.0% U.S. and 7.8% U.K. assumed returns on equity-heavy portfolios (U.K. actual 53% equities against a 40% target). Disclosed sensitivity: −100bp on expected return removes ~$8M U.S. plus ~$2M U.K. of 2026 income. A formal triennial U.K. funding valuation is due in 2026 and could change contribution requirements.

**Single-site concentration**, acute in specialty materials precisely because competing AP producers are (per management) largely unqualified — the barrier that constitutes the moat also means a site disruption has no easy substitute.

**Electrification.** The risk factor names electric-vehicle acceptance as potentially rendering products obsolete, and notes replacement products "may have lower margins." Engine oil additives are ~70% of lubricant additive market volume by management's estimate. This is a decade-scale rather than a 12-month risk.

**Environmental and impairment.** Accrued remediation was $12M at 6/30/2026, but recurring environmental operating and clean-up spend runs $37–44M annually, primarily in cost of goods sold. Calca's purchase-price allocation remains preliminary through 10/1/2026, with intangibles and goodwill each already adjusted $3M in H1.

Set against these: no covenant risk, wide liquidity, incidental legal proceedings, and a distributable cash-generation base that comfortably covers the dividend at 22% of free cash flow.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 29.2% sits 4.4 points below one-year realized volatility of 33.6%, at the 30th percentile within the chain, with a flat term structure (27.5% at six months). Near-term put/call skew of 5.8% indicates elevated put demand at the August 21 tenor; that does not persist into the September (1.2%) or December tenors. The nearest expiry implies ±5.3%, but no earnings fall before it — the December tenor at ±14.8% spans the late-October print. NEU's two most recent earnings gaps were −14.4% (February 12) and +10.1% (July 30). Longer-dated volatility at 27.5% looks reasonable against that realized gap history rather than rich.

Short interest of 9.1% of float, 671,475 shares and 6.0 days to cover on a 9.2M-share count with 113,000 average daily volume is mechanically significant: any positive surprise in a name this thin creates squeeze pressure, which argues against expressing caution through a short position. The overall read — cheap near-term volatility, modest near-term put demand that does not extend, no directional conviction in longer tenors — is consistent with hedging the post-earnings gap rather than positioning for a decline.

**Risk factors: 5.4.** Balance-sheet risk is minimal, but three identifiable, unquantified reversal channels (surcharge, tax rate, specialty materials quarterly margin) sit inside trailing earnings the market is capitalizing at an all-time-high price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,020 (+20%)

Surcharges prove sticky as they did in 2023–24, when NEU retained price after raw-material costs deflated and expanded gross margin 860bp over two years. The AP line comes online on schedule at the end of 2026 at Q2-type margins, taking specialty materials revenue toward $330M+ in 2027 and adding roughly $2 per share after tax; hydrazine capacity expansion follows. Petroleum additives volume stabilizes as the low-margin pruning laps, letting 20.7% rolling segment margin translate into growth rather than defense. Share count continues shrinking 2%+ annually against a remaining $301.78M authorization. The market continues to capitalize the defense/space franchise on qualification-barrier logic rather than a 6.7%-of-revenue segment's current economics. EPS of ~$51 at 20x.

### Bear Case — $640 (−24.7%)

Middle East logistics costs normalize and surcharge revenue exits with them; the undisclosed magnitude means the market cannot pre-position for it. Volume declines continue near −5%, confirming share loss rather than pruning. Specialty materials delivers another Q1-2026-type quarter (21.4% margin, operating profit −46% year over year) and the market discovers the segment's quarterly variance as it did on February 12 when a $8.65 Q4 EPS print produced a −14.4% single-day move. Tax normalizes toward 24.5%, removing ~$1.30 of EPS. Equity-market weakness cuts the 8% pension return assumption, removing another ~$10M of pretax income and pressuring the $605.4M surplus that supports 33% of book equity. The ~$1B specialty materials investment continues earning ~5.4% after tax. EPS of $38–40 at 16x.

The bear case is not a solvency case — 1.0x net leverage and $589M of undrawn revolver make that clear. It is a multiple-and-earnings-quality case, and the price provides no cushion against it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price toward **$680–700**, roughly 15x normalized EPS of ~$46 and near the $623 level at which the company and its only insider buyer transacted. That would restore 20%+ upside to base case against a bounded bear.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of flat-or-better petroleum additive shipment volume** alongside retained pricing, which would resolve the pruning-versus-share-loss question in NEU's favor and convert a 20.7% margin from defensive to accretive.
3. **AP line online by year-end 2026 with specialty materials revenue run-rating above $90M per quarter at ≥28% segment margin**, validating the ~$1B at a return at or above consolidated ROA of 12.0%.
4. **Resumption of buybacks in size at prices near spot** — the pause above ~$623 is the most informative capital-allocation datum available.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Shipment declines widening beyond −6% without offsetting price, indicating structural share loss.
2. Sequential reversal of price/mix without corresponding raw-material relief, confirming surcharge give-back at negative margin.
3. Specialty materials segment operating margin below 20% for two consecutive quarters after the capacity addition, which would mark the ~$1B as value-destructive against a 12% ROA hurdle.
4. Any goodwill or intangible impairment against the $799M in specialty materials, or a government shutdown/appropriations disruption to AP or hydrazine programs.
5. A cut to the 8.0% U.S. pension return assumption, which would remove ~$8M of 2026 income and reprice the earnings-quality discussion.

**Reassessment timeline:** the **Q3 2026 print in late October** is the decisive checkpoint — the first quarter that can show whether the surcharge and the 33.3% specialty materials margin persist, and whether H2 operating cash flow holds after the $103.3M H1 payables build. Secondary: **AP line commissioning at year-end 2026**, and the **U.K. triennial pension valuation** due during 2026.

## Conclusion

This is a HOLD on NEU, and the score sits at 5.96 while the business itself has not deteriorated. The driver is composition. Management quality has improved on demonstrated evidence — $3.69M CEO compensation against $418.7M of net income, absolute GAAP incentive metrics, a 9.1% dividend increase covered at 22% of free cash flow, a 3.4% share-count reduction, the AMPAC term loan retired within two years, and a repurchase program that bought aggressively at $623 and stopped as the shares advanced. Financial health remains strong at 1.0x net leverage with 3x covenant headroom. What has compressed is valuation and competitive trajectory: the stock is 3.0% below its all-time high at 18.3x trailing earnings that contain a surcharge of undisclosed size, a 320bp tax tailwind, a $3.5M R&D reduction and roughly 9% of pretax income from non-operating pension credits, on a business whose core-segment volumes have declined for two consecutive years and whose consolidated revenue has been flat for four.

Base case $870 against bull $1,020 and bear $640 is 2% upside versus 25% downside. That is not a short — 9.1% short interest with 6 days to cover on a 9.2M-share float, a fortress balance sheet and a dated capacity catalyst make bearish expression expensive and mechanically dangerous. It is a name to own only at a price where the reversal channels are already discounted. The decisive variables — surcharge magnitude, the pruning-versus-share-loss split, specialty materials order timing, and the returns on the ~$1B committed — are not disclosed anywhere in the filings, and the October print is the first place they can be observed. Hold existing positions; new capital should wait for $680–700 or for volume stabilization with retained pricing.
