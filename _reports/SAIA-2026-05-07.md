## Executive Summary

Saia is in the trough of a multi-year J-curve investment cycle. The company spent $1.6B+ in 2024-2025 transforming from a regional LTL carrier into a national 213-terminal network, absorbing former Yellow Corporation assets. This investment produced a 410bps operating ratio deterioration in 2025 (from 85.0% to 89.1%) and a 27% decline in operating income. Q1 2026 showed the first green shoots: record revenue of $806M, positive shipment growth, and a slight EPS beat. Management guided 400-450bps sequential OR improvement for Q2 2026.

The stock has rallied ~78% from its June 2025 lows near $248 to $449 today, now trading at 47x trailing EPS of $9.52 — but trailing EPS represents trough earnings. On FY2027 consensus of ~$14.14, the forward P/E compresses to ~32x. The investment thesis hinges on whether margin recovery materializes at a rate sufficient to justify current multiples. With positive estimate revisions (+9.1% current quarter, +5.1% full year over 30 days), the trajectory is favorable but the magnitude of revisions does not yet signal an explosive earnings recovery that would justify aggressive positioning at these levels.

The structural thesis remains intact: post-Yellow capacity tightness supports pricing, the non-union cost structure provides operating leverage, and terminal maturation should drive density-led margin improvement over 12-24 months. However, macro headwinds (tariffs reducing freight demand, potential recession) and execution risk on 28+ new terminals keep the risk/reward balanced rather than compelling at current prices.

## Company Overview

Saia, Inc. operates as a national less-than-truckload carrier across all 48 contiguous US states, generating approximately 97% of revenue from LTL services. The company handles ~35,000 shipments daily through 213 terminals using ~7,700 tractors and ~26,500 trailers, staffed by ~14,500 non-union employees.

The January 2024 acquisition of 17 terminals (plus leases on 11 more) from Yellow Corporation's bankruptcy estate — combined with organic openings — expanded the network by 22% in two years. This converted Saia from the #7 LTL carrier with primarily regional/super-regional coverage into a true national competitor positioned to capture share from larger incumbents.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Q1 2026 revenue hit a record $806M (+2.4% YoY), marking the first positive growth reading after a flat 2025 ($3.23B, +0.8% YoY). LTL revenue per shipment grew 1.2% to $352 in 2025, with the October 2025 GRI of 5.9% and January 2026 contractual renewals averaging 6.6% supporting continued yield improvement.

**Profitability (the central issue):** Operating ratio deteriorated from 85.0% (2024) to 89.1% (2025), peaking at 91.9% in Q4 2025. The primary cost drivers: salaries/wages/benefits up $91.9M (+6.2%), depreciation up $38.5M (+18.3%), and insurance claims up $15.1M. The Q1 2026 OR of approximately 93.8% (implied from EPS/revenue) reflects typical seasonality, but management's Q2 guide would bring it toward ~89-90% — still well above the 85% achieved in 2024 and far from Old Dominion's low-70s benchmark.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow remained healthy at $595M (2025) despite margin pressure. Free cash flow collapsed to $27M on $568M capex. With 2026 capex guided at $350-400M and OCF expected around $600-650M, FCF should normalize to $200-250M.

**Balance sheet:** Net debt of $163M is highly manageable against $595M OCF. The December 2024 credit facility amendment provides $600M availability (expandable to $900M) through 2029, eliminating refinancing risk. Current ratio of 1.64x and D/E of 1.35x reflect appropriate leverage for a capital-intensive transportation company in expansion mode.

**Return on capital:** ROE declined from ~23% (2022-2023) to 9.9% (2025). This must recover toward 15%+ for the valuation to be sustainable — terminal maturation and the guided Q2 2026 OR improvement are the mechanisms by which this should occur.

## Growth Analysis

Saia's path to earnings growth is primarily through operating leverage as existing terminals gain density:

1. **Terminal maturation:** 28+ terminals opened in 2024-2025 are in early stages. New terminals typically take 3-5 years to reach full density. Each terminal generating incremental shipments improves fixed-cost absorption.

2. **Pricing power:** The post-Yellow capacity reduction (~10% of national LTL capacity removed) supports industry pricing discipline. Saia's GRI and contractual renewals demonstrate retained pricing power even in soft demand.

3. **Volume inflection:** Q1 2026 showed +1% shipment growth — the first positive reading after flat-to-declining trends. If the freight market recovers in H2 2026, Saia's expanded network captures disproportionate volume.

4. **Estimate revisions:** Current quarter EPS revised up 9.1% over 30 days (from $2.99 to $3.26). Full-year FY2026 at $11.16 (up 5.1%) and FY2027 at $14.14 (up 3.5%). These are positive but moderate — they do not reach the 30%+ threshold that signals an explosive recovery.

**Consensus earnings trajectory:** $9.52 (2025) → $11.16 (2026E) → $14.14 (2027E), implying 17% and 27% growth respectively. This represents a recovery back toward prior peak earnings ($13.40-$13.51) by 2027, with the terminal expansion providing a runway beyond that level.

## Competitive Landscape

**Improving competitive position (7/10).** Saia's strategic positioning has strengthened materially:

1. **Network completeness:** The expansion from ~175 to 213 terminals achieves a critical mass that allows Saia to compete for national accounts that previously defaulted to ODFL, FedEx Freight, or XPO. This is a permanent structural upgrade.

2. **Post-Yellow capacity void:** Yellow's removal of ~10% of national LTL capacity created a lasting supply-demand imbalance that benefits all remaining carriers. Saia captured physical assets (terminals, some customer relationships) that directly translated into network density.

3. **Non-union cost advantage:** Approximately 15-20% structural cost advantage vs. unionized competitors (ABF/ARCB). This provides pricing flexibility to capture share while maintaining margins once terminals mature.

4. **Service quality:** 21% reduction in preventable accidents and 10% decline in lost-time injuries in 2025 demonstrates improving service quality — critical for customer retention in LTL where reliability justifies premium pricing.

5. **Vulnerability:** Saia remains significantly below Old Dominion in terms of operating efficiency (89% OR vs. ODFL's ~72-73%). Until Saia demonstrates sustained OR improvement toward 82-84%, it cannot command ODFL-like multiples. The gap, however, represents the upside opportunity.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Macro/demand (HIGH):** Tariff-driven freight demand weakness and potential economic slowdown directly threaten the timing of terminal maturation. Every quarter of delayed density improvement compounds the cost of the expansion investment.

2. **Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** 28+ new terminals simultaneously in early-stage ramp is unprecedented for Saia. Integration complexity, local competitive dynamics, and the challenge of hiring/training for entirely new markets create execution risk that isn't fully controllable.

3. **Insurance/claims volatility (MODERATE):** The $4.7M Q4 2025 hit from prior-year case development demonstrates how self-insured claims can disrupt quarterly results. With $125M accrued and industry "nuclear verdict" trends, this remains an unpredictable margin headwind.

4. **Unionization (LOW probability, HIGH impact):** Expansion into former Yellow markets increases organizing exposure. The 10-K explicitly notes that acquiring former Yellow Corporation terminals "could increase our overall risk of unionization." Unionization would fundamentally impair the cost structure that underpins the entire investment thesis.

5. **Tariff-specific (MODERATE):** Unlike pure domestic freight, Saia transports imported goods from ports to distribution centers. Sustained tariff escalation structurally reduces freight volumes in specific lanes.

## Conclusion

Saia is a high-quality franchise executing a transformative but risky network expansion. The structural thesis — post-Yellow capacity tightness, non-union cost advantage, terminal maturation driving operating leverage — is sound and represents a clear path to earnings recovery. Q1 2026's record revenue and positive shipment growth provide early confirmation. However, at 47x trailing trough EPS and ~32x FY2027 consensus, the stock prices in substantial recovery that remains 12-18 months from full realization.

The 12-month growth thesis is specific and testable: management has guided 400-450bps sequential OR improvement for Q2 2026, estimate revisions are trending positively, and capex normalization will restore FCF. This is a stock worth holding through the proving period rather than chasing at all-time highs or selling into improving fundamentals. The risk/reward improves meaningfully at $360-380 (a pullback to 25-27x FY2027E), where the margin of safety would justify a BUY upgrade.

HOLD with a growth thesis: the J-curve recovery is underway, and Q2 2026 will confirm whether the slope matches management's guidance. Current positioning is reasonable for existing holders; new capital should wait for better entry or confirmed acceleration.
