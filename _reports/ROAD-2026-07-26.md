**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Construction Partners is a Sunbelt civil-infrastructure roll-up built around hot-mix asphalt (HMA) paving, aggregates, and liquid-asphalt distribution. Since its 2007 formation by SunTx Capital Partners, the company has acquired and integrated 54+ local platforms, and it is now in the most aggressive phase of that strategy. FY2025 revenue jumped 54% to $2.81B (roughly $835M of that from acquisitions, $153M organic), gross margin expanded to 15.6%, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $3.59–$3.65B with adjusted EBITDA of $552–$564M. The operating story is working: same-market organic growth of 7–11%, backlog growing to $3.1B, and a credible path toward a ~17% adjusted-EBITDA-margin "ROAD 2030" target driven by better plant utilization and route density.

The problem is the balance sheet the growth has been built on. Long-term debt tripled from $487M (FY24) to $1.57B (FY25) and reached $1.76B by Q2 FY26; goodwill quadrupled to $1.10B (roughly 32% of assets). Net leverage sits at 3.23x against a covenant that steps down to 4.25x through Sept 2026, 4.00x in Dec 2026, and 3.75x by Sept 2027. Roughly 100% of the debt is floating-rate SOFR with only $300M hedged, so every 100bps of rate movement swings ±$17.6M of pre-tax income against ~$102M of net income. Management continues to fund tuck-ins on the revolver (drawn from $190M to $345M in two quarters) even as covenants ratchet down. This is a leveraged bet that rates stay contained and integration stays clean, embedded in a business that is at cyclical and seasonal peaks.

At ~$103, ROAD trades at ~56x trailing EPS ($1.84), ~35x current-year consensus ($2.97), and ~28x next-year ($3.75). That is neither a distressed multiple nor a bargain for a business carrying this much financial and integration risk with estimate revisions now turning negative across the front two windows. The stock has fallen from a $151 high (its 52-week high, reached intraday May 8) back toward its 52-week low, but the fundamentals do not point to either a clean buy or a clean short. We rate ROAD a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, pending clarity on de-leveraging trajectory and the terms of two unexplained June 2026 debt agreements.

## Company Overview

Construction Partners builds and maintains transportation infrastructure (highways, roads, bridges, airports) plus commercial and residential site work across eight Sunbelt states: Alabama, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Texas. The business is vertically integrated around asphalt: HMA manufacturing (internal and third-party sales), paving and roadway base construction, site development, aggregates mining, and liquid-asphalt-cement distribution through an owned terminal.

Revenue is roughly 65% publicly funded (federal, state, local), with state DOTs alone representing 43.4% of FY2025 revenue and no single DOT above 10%. In H1 FY2026 the public mix rose to ~67–69%, with Florida DOT the only customer above 10% (~12%). The competitive dynamic is intensely local: because transporting HMA is expensive, bid success depends on plant proximity to job sites. That is why the roll-up model creates value: acquiring local asphalt plants builds geographic density and route economics that translate into higher plant utilization and margin.

The company is a Nasdaq "controlled company." SunTx controls roughly 61% of voting power through Class B shares carrying 10 votes each, while owning a far smaller economic stake. ROAD pays no dividend.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** The multi-year trajectory is steep: $1.30B (FY22) → $1.56B (FY23) → $1.82B (FY24) → $2.81B (FY25, +54.2%). H1 FY2026 revenue was $1.58B (+39.3% YoY). Gross margin has climbed steadily from 10.7% (FY22) to 14.2% (FY24) to 15.6% (FY25), with H1 FY26 at 14.0% (H1 is seasonally weaker). Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 12.1% (FY24) to 15.1% (FY25); the FY26 guide implies ~15.3%.

**Earnings quality.** GAAP net margin is thin at 3.6% because heavy D&A ($148M), interest expense ($90M), and acquisition costs ($26M) absorb operating income. Net income rose from $68.9M (FY24) to $101.8M (FY25); adjusted net income was $122.0M. Trailing EPS of $1.84 reflects both the thin GAAP margin and share dilution. This is a high-D&A, capital-intensive business, so adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow are the more relevant performance gauges — operating cash flow was $291M in FY25, exceeding net income, with FCF of $153M (5.5% margin). The Piotroski profile is solid on the operating dimensions.

**The balance sheet is the central issue.** Debt/equity is 2.55x and the Altman Z-Score is 1.86, in the grey zone — that reflects leverage rather than operating distress, but it is a real flag. Interest coverage of 5.34x is comfortable today, and the company is in compliance. The mechanism to watch: the covenant tightens by 50bps over the next 18 months while management keeps borrowing on the revolver to fund tuck-ins. If demand softens or a margin miss compresses EBITDA, the shrinking headroom becomes binding faster than a static leverage figure suggests.

**Rate exposure is underappreciated.** The $300M hedge is a swap maturing June 2027, and the weighted-average rate is already ~5.90%. Interest expense rose 374% YoY in FY25. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a direct and material headwind, and the swap expiry in mid-2027 removes even the partial hedge just as the tightest covenant test arrives.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is favorable. IIJA and IRA infrastructure funding support Sunbelt transportation spending, and Sunbelt population and economic growth drive private site work. Backlog grew from $3.0B (Sept 2025) to $3.1B (Mar 2026), of which ~$2.6B is contracted and ~$1.5B is expected to convert during the balance of FY2026. Management cited exceeding profitability expectations and growing backlog when raising guidance, though it also credited favorable weather for the Q2 beat — a factor that cuts both ways.

The growth is two-engined: an organic core running 7–11% same-market growth on top of an M&A machine. FY2026 acquisitions already include VMC Houston HMA plants ($108M), P&S Paving in Florida ($145M including $51M stock), GMJ Paving in Houston ($38M), and Four Star Paving in Nashville ($58M). Houston/Texas and Florida are the clear expansion priorities. Management's attribution of margin gains to "efficient utilization of plants and equipment" is a credible synergy signal, not just accounting mix.

The forward earnings picture is less clean than the revenue narrative. Current-quarter consensus was cut 7.2% over 30 days and 5.9% over 90 days; current-year estimates fell 2.4% over 30 days (though still +4.9% over 90 days); next-year estimates fell 2.1% over 30 days (+3.1% over 90 days). The 30-day trend across every window is negative — momentum rolling over even as the top-line story remains intact. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (two beats, two misses in the last four quarters), which tempers confidence in the front-quarter consensus. Revenue durability is high, but earnings visibility is deteriorating at the margin, and much of the growth is inorganic and debt-funded rather than self-financing.

## Valuation Assessment

Because trailing earnings are depressed by D&A and interest rather than a cyclical trough, the cycle-adjusted-valuation carve-out does not straightforwardly apply — this is not a company whose trailing multiple is meaningless because earnings are about to triple. The forward multiples are the right anchor, and ~28x next-year for a business with mid-single-digit net margin, 3.23x net leverage, and negative front-quarter revisions is full rather than cheap.

Peer comparison is unhelpful here. The listed engineering-and-construction comparables (LGN at a distressed 4,212x, IESC at 39x, FER at 47.5x) are heterogeneous businesses with different capital structures and end markets; the "peer median P/E of 47.5" is not a clean benchmark for a leveraged asphalt roll-up. On its own merits, ROAD's valuation prices in continued flawless integration and margin expansion toward the 17% target, with no discount for the leverage and rate risk it carries.

Analyst targets (low $130, mean/median $145, high $165) sit 26–60% above the current price. On a stock trading below 30x forward with rising estimates, these would ordinarily function as floors. Here estimates are falling on the front two quarters, the sentiment trend is degrading, and the stock has fallen roughly 32% from its $151 high. The targets look stale relative to the deteriorating revision trend, so we treat them as an upside reference rather than a reliable floor. The valuation dimension does not offer asymmetric upside at the current price: the multiple is not stretched enough to short, but not compressed enough to reward given the balance-sheet risk.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is a genuine strength and the best part of the story. The asphalt paving industry is highly fragmented and structurally local; HMA transport costs make plant proximity decisive in bidding. ROAD's roll-up creates real, defensible geographic density: acquiring local platforms improves route economics and plant utilization, which shows up directly in the gross-margin climb. The relationship with Vulcan Materials is instructive: ROAD bought Houston HMA assets from Vulcan, an acquirer-of-assets dynamic rather than a head-to-head loss.

The moat is improving on trajectory — share and density are being added in Texas and Florida, margins are expanding, and the backlog is growing. Competitive risk is company-specific execution (integrating five-plus deals per year without degrading bid discipline or key-employee retention) rather than structural displacement. The Sunbelt concentration is both a tailwind (fast-growing region) and a concentration risk (hurricane exposure, regional economic sensitivity). On balance, the competitive trajectory is positive, which supports a competitive-position score above the sector's generally weak selection profile.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and covenant step-downs.** The defining risk. Net leverage against a covenant tightening to 3.75x by Sept 2027, funded increasingly on the revolver, leaves limited headroom if EBITDA disappoints. Interest coverage is comfortable now but the trajectory of the covenant and the debt both point the wrong way.

**Floating-rate exposure.** The hedge rolls off precisely when the tightest test arrives, leaving the floating book fully exposed as a direct earnings drag in the higher-for-longer environment.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1.10B of goodwill (~32% of assets), much of it from premium-multiple acquisitions whose pre-acquisition financials were often not independently audited. Management flags impairment risk explicitly if conditions worsen. A non-cash charge would not breach the leverage covenant directly but would dent reported earnings and sentiment.

**Integration at scale.** Five deals in FY25 (~$1.5B) plus five more into FY26 strains management, systems, and retention.

**Government funding dependence.** 65% publicly funded; exposure to Highway Trust Fund solvency, IIJA appropriation timing, and state budget cycles. Contracts are cancellable and carry debarment risk.

**Commodity and geopolitical input costs.** Liquid asphalt, diesel, natural gas. Public contracts carry price-escalation clauses (a mitigant); backlog and private work are less protected. A new Q2 FY26 risk factor added Iran/Strait of Hormuz exposure to crude and asphalt supply, relevant given the current energy-market tail risk.

**Governance.** SunTx's ~61% voting control via 10:1 Class B shares, controlled-company exemptions from independent-board/comp requirements, a classified board, supermajority provisions, and recurring related-party fees ($1.8M/yr management fee, ~$2.3M H1 FY26 family-owned subcontracting, an "Island Pond" access agreement) leave Class A holders as minority passengers. This is a structural discount factor and mutes takeover-premium optionality.

**Unresolved June 2026 debt 8-Ks.** Two consecutive Item 1.01/2.03 filings indicate new material debt agreements whose terms were not retrievable. Given the leverage narrative, the rate, maturity, and any covenant relief are highly relevant and should be reviewed before adding.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 64.0% is rich versus 48.8% one-year historical volatility (a 15.3pp spread, 88th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (64.0% near-term vs 53.0% at six months). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±14.0%. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1%) and put/call volume is modest (0.52), so the market is pricing elevated uncertainty rather than a directional collapse. This does not confirm a bearish directional thesis, but it does argue against paying up for entry ahead of the next print. Short interest is modest at 5.6% of float (3.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short and there is no meaningful squeeze setup either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The roll-up is compounding on genuine operating synergies. Organic growth of 7–11% plus disciplined tuck-ins drives revenue to guidance and beyond, adjusted EBITDA margin climbs toward the 17% "ROAD 2030" target, and management de-levers toward the 3.75x covenant as acquisition cadence slows and integrated plants ramp. IIJA/IRA funding and Sunbelt growth sustain demand. If rates ease, the floating-rate drag reverses into a tailwind. On $3.75+ next-year EPS at a ~40x multiple (justified by continued mid-teens growth and margin expansion), the stock re-rates toward the $150 area and analyst high targets of $165 become reachable. Bull case: ~$155–165.

**Bear case.** Rates stay high or rise, the hedge rolls off in June 2027, and floating-rate interest compresses earnings just as the covenant tightens to 3.75x. A demand softening (Highway Trust Fund appropriation delay, state budget pressure) or a margin miss pushes leverage toward the ceiling, forcing a slowdown in M&A that removes the growth engine. Goodwill impairment on premium-priced deals dents reported earnings and sentiment. The multiple compresses to ~20x on flat-to-lower earnings. Bear case: ~$75–85, roughly 18–27% downside from current.

**Base case.** Revenue guidance is met, margins grind higher slowly, and the company neither breaches covenants nor materially de-levers — it borrows to acquire while integrated plants improve utilization. Earnings land near consensus, the negative revision momentum stabilizes, and the multiple stays in the ~28–32x forward range. The stock trades in a range around $105–120 as the market waits for de-leveraging evidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the operating business is good, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced given the leverage, rate sensitivity, full forward multiple, and deteriorating front-quarter estimates. We do not have sufficient conviction for either a buy or a short.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next fiscal Q3 print (August) is the natural checkpoint: watch for revenue delivery against the raised guide, adjusted EBITDA margin progression, the net-leverage trajectory, and any commentary on the June 2026 debt agreements. The FY-end print in November and the covenant step-down to 4.00x in December 2026 are the next structural mileposts.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Net leverage declining meaningfully below 3.0x on organic EBITDA growth (not just acquisition-EBITDA add), demonstrating a real de-leveraging path ahead of the 3.75x Sept-2027 test.
- Estimate revisions turning positive again across the front two quarters, combined with confirmation the June 2026 debt agreements extended maturities or provided covenant relief on favorable terms.
- A pullback that compresses the forward multiple below ~22x next-year EPS while backlog and margin trajectory remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Net leverage rising toward 3.75x or covenant headroom shrinking materially, especially if driven by a margin miss rather than a scheduled acquisition step-up.
- A goodwill impairment charge or evidence that acquired plants are underperforming underwriting assumptions.
- A guidance cut, a second consecutive earnings miss, or evidence that rate expense is materially outrunning EBITDA growth.

## Conclusion

Construction Partners is a well-run, genuinely synergistic Sunbelt asphalt roll-up in the most leveraged phase of its strategy. The operating metrics are strong: 54% revenue growth, expanding gross margins, growing backlog, raised guidance, and a defensible local-density moat. But the company has traded balance-sheet safety for scale: nearly all-floating debt, 3.23x net leverage into tightening covenants, $1.10B of goodwill, and a swap that expires just as the toughest covenant test arrives. At ~28x next-year and ~35x current-year earnings with front-quarter estimates falling and near-term options pricing imminent stress, the valuation offers no cushion for the financial risk. The stock's drawdown from its high reflects the market repricing that leverage-plus-rate risk, not an obvious mispricing to exploit.

We rate ROAD a HOLD. The path to a BUY runs through demonstrated de-leveraging and stabilizing estimates; the path to an AVOID runs through covenant pressure, a margin miss, or impairment. Until the August print clarifies the leverage trajectory and the June 2026 financing terms are known, the risk/reward does not justify a stronger call in either direction.
