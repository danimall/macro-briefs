**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is the dominant force in the largest pharmaceutical growth market in a generation. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) drove total revenue up 45% to $65.2B in FY2025 and accelerated to +56% YoY in Q1 2026, with the franchise now representing 65% of revenue. Net income grew 168% in the quarter to $7.4B and the company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters by 12-27%. Management raised the FY2026 revenue outlook to roughly $85B and controls about 60% of the global GLP-1 market while Novo Nordisk posts declining sales. The pipeline (orforglipron, the first oral GLP-1 with no food/water restriction, FDA-approved for obesity in April 2026; retatrutide, ~19% weight loss in Phase 3; eloralintide; and lepodisiran) is the deepest in cardiometabolic medicine.

The central tension is that all of this growth is volume-driven while per-unit economics are eroding. Realized prices fell 6% in FY2025 (U.S. -10%) and 13% in Q1 2026; the +50% to +65% volume growth more than offset that, but the offset is the entire bull case and is not guaranteed to persist. In November 2025 Lilly reached voluntary pricing agreements with the U.S. government: lower Medicaid prices, a Medicare "GLP-1 Bridge Program" (July 2026–December 2027), and a commitment toward international reference pricing. Management itself warns that expanded-access revenue "may be insufficient to offset pricing concessions." Trulicity and Verzenio were selected for IRA Medicare price-setting in 2028, and tirzepatide's eventual selection is the long-term overhang.

At ~$1,099 the stock trades at ~48x trailing and ~30x forward FY2026 EPS ($36.21 consensus). That is a premium, but consensus estimates are rising (+4.4% on the current year over 90 days, +5.5% on next year), the franchise is the highest-quality in pharma, and the price is up roughly 40% from ~$785 in June 2025 on the back of 50%+ revenue growth, meaning the multiple has compressed as earnings caught up. We rate LLY a BUY with disciplined sizing. The growth durability is real and broad-based; the pricing/policy risk and ~65% single-franchise concentration are why this is not a maximum-conviction call.

## Company Overview

Lilly operates as a single reportable segment spanning discovery, development, manufacturing, and marketing of pharmaceuticals worldwide. The commercial profile has been reshaped by the incretin/GLP-1 class:

- **Cardiometabolic franchise (the engine).** Tirzepatide — Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity) — was 56% of total revenue in FY2025. FY2025 splits: Mounjaro $22.97B (+99% YoY), Zepbound $13.54B (+175% YoY).
- **Secondary portfolio.** Verzenio (oncology, $5.7B FY25), Jardiance (Boehringer Ingelheim collaboration), Trulicity (declining -16% in Q1 as it cannibalizes to tirzepatide and faces patent loss), Taltz (immunology), Kisunla (donanemab, Alzheimer's), Omvoh, Jaypirca, and Inluriyo.
- **Competitive structure.** Lilly and Novo Nordisk together control >90% of the global GLP-1 market; CNBC and Fierce Pharma place Lilly at ~60% and widening its lead.

## Financial Analysis

The growth profile is exceptional for a company of this scale:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $34.1B | $45.0B | $65.2B (+45%) | $19.8B (+56% YoY) |
| Net income | $5.2B | $10.6B | $20.6B (+95%) | $7.4B (+168% YoY) |
| Diluted EPS | $5.80 | $11.71 | $22.95 | $8.26 |
| Gross margin | ~79% | 81.3% | 83.0% | 81.9% |

**Margin and mix.** Gross margin expanded to 83.0% in FY25 on favorable product mix and lower production cost, despite the realized price decline. Q1 2026 GM eased to 81.9% on the steeper Q1 price decline. The margin direction is the variable to watch: if the price/volume tradeoff turns unfavorable, gross margin is the first place it shows up.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $16.8B in FY2025 (from $8.8B). FCF was $9.0B, depressed relative to net income because capex rose 53% to $7.8B (from $5.1B) for manufacturing expansion. This is growth capex, a deliberate bet on durable incretin demand, supported by up to ~$10B of take-or-pay supply commitments. FCF conversion below net income here reflects investment intensity, not earnings quality issues.

**Balance sheet.** A screening Altman Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress zone") is a false signal. It is distorted by high D/E (~4.24x) and thin equity-to-assets (23.6%) because Lilly funds growth with debt and returns capital aggressively. Against $20.6B net income, 77.8% ROE, 18.4% ROA, a 1.58 current ratio, and a $10B commercial paper backstop, solvency risk is negligible. Total debt rose to ~$43.4B (Q1 2026). The leverage is intentional and appropriate for a business compounding earnings at this rate; it is not a stress point, but it does mean rising rates raise the marginal cost of the capex build.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend raised to $6.92 annualized for 2026 (yield ~0.6%, immaterial as a return source). A $15B buyback (authorized Dec 2024) has $8.6B remaining; $2.3B was repurchased in Q1 2026 at ~$987/share. Reinvestment in capacity and business development takes priority over outsized buybacks, which is the correct ordering given the growth runway.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has high durability because it rests on multiple confirming signals rather than a single product:

1. **Volume, not price, is driving everything.** FY2025 volume +50%, Q1 2026 +65%, more than offsetting double-digit price declines. The obesity TAM is large enough that, if access keeps expanding, volume can continue absorbing price concessions for several years.
2. **Estimate revisions are positive and rising.** Current-year consensus EPS rose +4.4% over 90 days to $36.21; next year +5.5% to $44.46. The current quarter was revised +8.2% over 90 days. These are not the +30% revisions that flag a trough recovery, but for a $981B company they represent meaningful, consistent upward momentum.
3. **Beat track record is consistent.** Four consecutive beats (12-27%), with the most recent quarter (March 2026) beating by 27%. This supports confidence in the rising consensus and suggests management guidance remains conservative.
4. **Pipeline breadth.** Orforglipron (Foundayo) met a Phase 3 type-2-diabetes endpoint in June 2026 and carries an FDA Commissioner's National Priority Voucher. The oral format solves the injectable supply bottleneck and is ideal for the Medicare population opening under the Bridge Program. Retatrutide (GGG triple agonist) hit all Phase 3 obesity endpoints. Behind these sit eloralintide, lepodisiran and muvalaplin (Lp(a)), MASH, OSA programs, plus oncology assets (olomorasib, sofetabart, both Breakthrough Therapy).
5. **Competitor weakness.** While Lilly grew +55.5% in Q1, Novo posted a ~4% constant-currency sales decline and is cutting ~9,000 jobs. The duopoly is tilting decisively toward Lilly.

The one yellow flag: an early Spherix read (May 2026) suggests Foundayo's initial launch uptake trails Novo's oral Wegovy. This is single-source and preliminary, but it contradicts the consensus "Lilly dominance" narrative on the most important new product and warrants monitoring at the next quarterly print.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,099, LLY trades at ~30x forward FY2026 consensus, falling to ~24.7x on FY2027 ($44.46). Against the Pharmaceuticals peer median P/E of 20.0 (JNJ 27.6, MRK 33.8, PFE 20.0, BMY 15.9), Lilly carries a clear premium, but the peer set is growing low-single-digits or shrinking, while Lilly is compounding revenue at 45-56% and EPS faster than that. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~30x forward for >25% near-term EPS growth (PEG roughly 1.0-1.2) is not stretched for the highest-quality franchise in the sector.

The most important valuation observation is that the price re-rated downward as earnings grew 50%+: it sits well off the $1,182 high and within the $623-$1,182 range it has traded over the period. A stock that delivers 50% earnings growth while its multiple compresses is getting cheaper relative to its own earnings power.

That said, this does not warrant a high valuation score. The premium multiple already embeds continued hypergrowth, so the asymmetry is not strongly favorable: beats are expected (limited re-rating upside), while a pricing/policy disappointment or safety signal would compress the multiple sharply. Consensus targets cluster at $1,219 mean / $1,251 median (modest 11-14% upside), with a $1,500 high and $850 low. With the multiple already pricing the growth, those targets are closer to fair estimates of a balanced setup than stale floors. We score valuation 5.5: fair value with modest upside, neither a bargain nor stretched.

## Competitive Landscape

Lilly's competitive position is strong and improving, which is the trajectory that matters most. The GLP-1 market is a genuine duopoly (>90% combined share), and the balance has shifted toward Lilly: Q1 2026 growth of +55.5% against Novo's decline, tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism widely viewed as efficacy-superior to semaglutide, and a pipeline (retatrutide, orforglipron) that extends the lead into the next product cycles.

Three battlegrounds define the next two years: (1) the oral GLP-1 market, where Foundayo competes with Novo's oral semaglutide, critical for Medicare seniors and for scaling supply, since pills are far easier to manufacture than injectables; (2) Medicare access opening under the Bridge Program from July 2026; (3) next-generation efficacy (retatrutide vs. Novo's CagriSema). Roche and others are emerging but distant.

The competitive score is moderated from the maximum by two factors. First, the franchise concentration cuts both ways: dominance in one class is fragile if that class faces a safety or coverage shock. Second, the early Foundayo uptake data, if confirmed, would suggest Novo retains more oral-market staying power than the bull case assumes. The trajectory is clearly positive; the concentration is why we score it 7.8 rather than 9.

## Risk Assessment

The risk factors are unusually concentrated for a company this size, and several are specific catalysts not fully priced:

1. **Product concentration.** Two products are 65% of revenue; six are 82%. Any safety signal, supply disruption, or demand shock would be acutely material. There is active product-liability MDL litigation (GI injuries, NAION) plus class actions in Israel and Canada.
2. **Pricing/policy — the dominant variable.** The U.S. government agreements are double-edged: they de-risk political headline overhang and unlock Medicare obesity access, but management explicitly warns expanded-access revenue may not offset the pricing concessions. This is the single most important number to model for 2026-2028.
3. **IRA cadence accelerating.** Jardiance faces government-set Medicare prices in 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio were selected (Jan 2026) for 2028.
4. **Payer access friction.** CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound as a preferred obesity drug on some plans (July 2025); a survey suggests ~10% of employers may drop GLP-1 coverage in 2027.
5. **IP and supply.** Trulicity loses protection in the next few years; the Emgality patent case was reversed against Lilly (April 2026). Heavy China-supplier dependency for intermediates, currently tariff-exempt but at policy risk.
6. **Capex asymmetry.** The 53% capex increase with ~$10B take-or-pay commitments is a strong conviction signal, but it strands capacity and triggers contractual payments if demand or coverage disappoints.

These are real, specific, and partly unpriced (particularly the magnitude of the price/access tradeoff), which is why risk scores 5.0: sector-average risk would warrant higher, but the concentration plus the pricing-agreement uncertainty are idiosyncratic catalysts for material downside.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-favorable. Near-term ATM IV is 28.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 38.1%; implied vol is cheap (-9.7pp spread), which makes options-based entry hedging inexpensive and suggests the market is not pricing an imminent shock. The put/call skew is balanced (+1.3% near term), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.66 leans bullish. The term structure is in normal contango (28.3% near vs 33.9% six-month), with longer-dated skew turning more negative (-3.9% at six months), consistent with the market pricing the July 2026 Bridge Program start and later pricing decisions as the genuine event risk. ATM IV collapsed 11 points (39.3% to 28.3%) over the past few days as a near-term catalyst passed without incident. Short interest is just 1.1% of float (2.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic either way. The cheap near-term vol modestly favors current entry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($1,400-$1,500)
Obesity volume is so large that price concessions are easily absorbed; the Medicare Bridge Program and international access expand the TAM faster than per-unit prices fall. Orforglipron scales rapidly on its manufacturing advantage and oral convenience, retatrutide establishes a next-gen efficacy lead, and Novo continues to fade. FY2027 EPS of $44+ compounds toward $55+ by 2028, and the stock holds a ~30x multiple, supporting $1,500+. Estimate revisions continue rising and the four-quarter beat streak extends.

### Bear Case ($850-$900)
The government pricing agreements and accelerating IRA selection compress per-unit economics faster than volume compensates; gross margin rolls over from 83%. Foundayo's launch uptake confirms it trails Novo's oral entrant, undercutting the dominance narrative. Employer GLP-1 coverage pullback in 2027 and PBM exclusions soften the commercial book. A safety signal in the MDL litigation or a demand disappointment strands the capex build and take-or-pay commitments. The multiple compresses toward the peer set as growth decelerates, taking the stock to the $850 low analyst target (-23%).

The base case is that volume continues to outrun price erosion through 2027, the pipeline executes, and EPS growth supports a gradually moderating but still-premium multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,280** — ~29x the next-year consensus EPS, conservative against the current 30x forward, reflecting modest multiple compression as the law of large numbers slows growth. This sits between the bear ($875) and bull ($1,500) cases and implies ~16% upside from $1,099.

**Bull case target: $1,500** (+36%) — sustained 30x on accelerating FY2027/2028 earnings power as access expands and the pipeline delivers.

**Bear case target: $875** (-20%) — multiple compression toward the peer set as pricing erosion outpaces volume and growth decelerates.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** Key catalysts: Q2 2026 earnings (orforglipron launch trajectory, gross-margin direction, FY26 guidance update), the July 1, 2026 Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program launch, retatrutide/orforglipron data and regulatory milestones, and the price/volume disclosure each quarter.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Gross margin declines below 80% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the price/volume tradeoff has turned unfavorable.
- Volume growth decelerates below ~25% YoY while price declines accelerate, breaking the volume-offset mechanism.
- Confirmed orforglipron launch underperformance versus Novo's oral entrant in a second independent data read, or a material safety signal in the incretin MDL litigation.

## Conclusion

Lilly is the best-positioned franchise in pharmaceuticals, delivering 50%+ revenue growth and 168% net-income growth on the back of the largest therapeutic market in a generation, with a pipeline that extends the lead. The growth is broad-based, the estimate revisions are positive, and the multiple has compressed as earnings caught up to the price. The reasons this is a disciplined BUY rather than a maximum-conviction one are specific and unresolved: ~65% revenue concentration in a single franchise, double-digit realized price declines that management warns may not be offset by expanded access, accelerating IRA exposure, and a capex build that creates downside asymmetry if demand disappoints. At ~30x forward earnings the market is pricing continued hypergrowth, which limits the multiple-expansion upside while leaving sharp downside on any disappointment.

We recommend a BUY with disciplined sizing. The franchise quality and growth durability justify ownership; the concentration and pricing dynamics argue against concentrating the position. Accumulate on weakness toward the low-$900s where the risk/reward improves materially; trim into strength above $1,400 where the bull case is largely priced.

**Recommendation: BUY (disciplined sizing) | Overall Score: 6.9/10**
