## Executive Summary

Apogee Enterprises is a $700M market cap building products company experiencing simultaneous cyclical and operational deterioration. Revenue peaked in FY2023 at $1.44B and has declined for two consecutive years, with organic revenue falling 2.7% YTD in FY2026 across its three core architectural segments (91% of revenue). Margins are compressing sharply: gross margin fell from 28.1% to 22.9% and net margin from 6.2% to 3.6% (annualized 9M FY2026). Adjusted diluted EPS through nine months is $2.55 versus $4.08 in the comparable period, a 37% decline.

Both CEO Ty Silberhorn (October 31, 2025, unexplained separation) and CFO Matthew Osberg (January 7, 2026, resignation) departed within 10 weeks of each other, during an active $28-29M restructuring. Board Chair Donald Nolan has assumed interim CEO duties. No open-market insider purchases have occurred. A Pomerantz securities investigation is underway. The current macro regime of stagflation with rising rates, $116 oil, and elevated construction financing costs adds to the cyclical headwinds on non-residential construction demand.

Three analysts cover the stock, all rating it Buy with targets of $40-$46 (median $44). At $32.46, the stock trades 26-42% below these targets. The analyst consensus appears stale relative to the magnitude of the Q3 FY2026 earnings miss, the management departures, and the macro deterioration since those targets were set.

## Company Overview

Apogee operates four segments serving non-residential construction and specialty coatings markets:

- **Architectural Metals (39% of FY2025 sales):** Aluminum window, curtainwall, storefront, and entrance systems (Tubelite, EFCO, Linetec, Alumicor brands). The largest segment, contracting from $650M peak (FY2023) to ~$525M annualized run-rate.
- **Architectural Services (31%):** Harmon brand — integrated project management and field installation for building façades under fixed-price contracts. $774.7M backlog provides ~2 years of visibility.
- **Architectural Glass (21%):** Viracon brand — high-performance insulating, laminated, and monolithic glass. Historically the highest-margin segment, now experiencing the steepest margin decline.
- **Performance Surfaces (9%, growing to ~14%):** Coated materials for framing, museums, graphic arts, industrial flooring (Tru Vue, ChromaLuxe, ResinDEK). Expanded via the $241M UW Solutions acquisition (closed November 2024).

Approximately 91% of revenue is U.S.-derived, with 6% from Canada and the remainder from Brazil and other international markets.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

| Fiscal Year | Revenue ($M) | YoY Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $1,314 | +6.7% |
| FY2023 | $1,441 | +9.6% |
| FY2024 | $1,417 | -1.6% |
| FY2025 | $1,361 | -3.9% |
| 9M FY2026 | $1,053 | +3.8% reported / -2.7% organic |

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years. The 9M FY2026 reported growth of 3.8% is entirely attributable to the UW Solutions acquisition ($74.8M contribution); organic revenue declined 2.7%. Architectural Glass revenue declined 12.4% organically in the nine-month period. Architectural Metals is contracting at a rate that implies roughly $525M annualized versus its $650M FY2023 peak, a 19% decline from peak.

### Profitability Compression

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 Ann. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross Margin | 25.9% | 26.4% | 22.9% |
| Operating Margin | 9.4% | 8.7% | 5.6% |
| Net Margin | 7.0% | 6.2% | 3.6% |
| Adj. EBITDA Margin | 13.1% | 14.2% | 11.9% |
| Diluted EPS | $4.51 | $3.89 | $1.74 (9M) |
| Adj. Diluted EPS | $4.77 | $4.97 | $2.55 (9M) |

Gross margin has compressed 350bps from FY2025 on a 9-month basis, driven by lower pricing, higher aluminum costs, restructuring charges ($23.6M through 9M), and tariff impacts on Canadian cross-border activity. The segment-level picture is uniformly negative:

- **Architectural Glass** adj. EBITDA margin: 23.3% → 16.9% (-640bps). This was Apogee's highest-quality segment.
- **Architectural Metals:** 15.4% → 11.9% (-350bps)
- **Architectural Services:** 7.9% → 6.8% (-110bps)
- **Performance Surfaces:** 24.3% → 21.7% (-260bps, diluted by UW Solutions acquisition)

Every segment margin is declining. There is no offset.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt stands at approximately $214M ($255M total debt minus $41M cash), with net leverage of ~1.3x trailing adjusted EBITDA. Available revolver capacity of $407M provides adequate liquidity. Goodwill and intangibles total $350M (31% of total assets), largely from the UW Solutions acquisition. The balance sheet is adequate but not a source of differentiation.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow fell to $66.6M in 9M FY2026 versus $95.1M in the comparable period, a 30% decline driven by lower earnings, restructuring payments, and the $24.7M arbitration settlement (sealant product defect). Capex was $18.3M, yielding FCF of approximately $48M on a 9-month basis. No share repurchases occurred in FY2026, versus $15M in the prior year. Management is conserving capital.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The last four quarters show 2 beats and 2 misses: Q3 FY2026 missed by 1.8% (reported $1.02 vs. $1.04 estimate), and Q4 FY2025 missed by 0.6%. Q2 FY2026 and Q1 FY2026 beat by 17.7% and 14.3% respectively. The progression from meaningful beats to marginal misses suggests deteriorating visibility and/or accelerating margin pressure that management's guidance did not fully capture.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth prospects are poor across the core business. Three of four segments are declining organically, and the sole growth contributor (Performance Surfaces) is growing only through acquisition. Whether the non-residential construction cycle is mid-downturn or approaching a trough is unclear from available data.

**Negative signals:**
- Architectural Glass revenue declining 12.4% organically suggests demand is still falling
- Office construction starts are structurally impaired by remote work trends (explicitly cited as a risk factor in the 10-K)
- Higher interest rates increase construction financing costs, suppressing project starts
- The current stagflation regime (oil at $116, core PCE at 3.1%, rising rate hike probability) is hostile to construction activity
- Aluminium at 4-year peaks and Gulf smelter destruction add direct input cost pressure to Architectural Metals

**Positive signals:**
- Architectural Services backlog at $774.7M provides ~2 years of revenue visibility
- Building energy-efficiency regulations create secular demand for high-performance glass and curtainwall systems
- Project Fortify Phase 2 targets $25-26M in annualized savings (expected completion Q4 FY2026)
- Performance Surfaces diversification into more stable end markets (distribution centers, manufacturing)

The restructuring savings, if fully realized, would restore approximately 180bps of margin on current revenue. This only partially offsets the cyclical compression. In a worsening macro environment, savings can be consumed by further volume/price declines.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.46, APOG trades at:
- **19.6x trailing EPS** ($3.89 FY2025)
- **~13.8x annualized 9M FY2026 adjusted EPS** ($2.55 × 4/3 ≈ $3.40, giving ~9.5x; or using forward P/E of 19.6x if the market is looking through the trough)
- **~35% below 52-week high** of $49.99
- **Market cap:** $703M
- **Dividend yield:** 3.3%

Peer comparison is complicated by the heterogeneous building products group. The peer median P/E of 49.6x is skewed by AMWD and MBC (both >49x with depressed earnings). IIIN at 13.4x and ROCK at 15.2x are more relevant comparables. Against these, APOG's 19.6x trailing P/E is at a premium despite declining margins and a management vacuum. The three covering analysts target $40-$46, but these targets likely predate the full extent of the macro deterioration (oil at $116, rate hike probability at 35-40%) and may not fully account for the dual C-suite departure.

The 3.3% dividend yield provides some floor, but the payout ratio is rising as earnings decline ($16.6M dividends on $37.4M net income through 9 months = 44% payout). If earnings continue to deteriorate, dividend sustainability comes under scrutiny.

## Competitive Landscape

Apogee operates in a fragmented market with many regional competitors and no dominant national player. Its competitive advantages include: (1) one of the few firms with national installation capability and performance bond capacity ($1.2B outstanding), (2) vertical integration across design-fabrication-installation, (3) proprietary Viracon glass coatings, and (4) established architect/owner relationships.

These advantages are real but cyclical. They provide more value in up-markets when projects are complex and specification-driven. In down-markets, price competition intensifies and differentiation matters less. The 640bps margin collapse in Architectural Glass (Viracon) suggests that even the most differentiated segment is under significant competitive or demand pressure.

The Performance Surfaces diversification is strategically sound but early. UW Solutions generated a $1.8M net loss over its first nine months within Apogee, and margin dilution is evident. The thesis that specialty coatings represent a higher-margin, less cyclical growth platform remains unproven at scale.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks (High Probability, High Impact)

1. **Management Vacuum:** CEO and CFO departures within 10 weeks during active restructuring. No disclosed reason for CEO separation. Pomerantz securities investigation underway. Interim CEO (board chair Nolan) is a placeholder, not a permanent solution. Interim CFO appointment compounds uncertainty. This is the single largest risk: restructuring execution, strategic direction, and capital allocation decisions are all in flux.

2. **Cyclical Downturn Deepening:** Non-residential construction is rate-sensitive. With the Fed signaling potential rate hikes (35-40% model probability), 30-year Treasury approaching 5%, and stagflation conditions, construction activity is likely to decline further. Three of four segments are already declining organically.

3. **Margin Deterioration Accelerating:** Every segment's margin is compressing. Aluminum costs are rising (4-year peak on LME due to Gulf smelter destruction at Alba and Emirates Global Aluminium). Tariffs on Canadian operations are an incremental headwind. Project Fortify savings may be offset or exceeded by continued volume/price decline.

### Moderate Risks

4. **Canada Tariff Escalation:** ~$85M in cross-border Canadian revenue at risk from U.S.-Canada trade tensions. Already impacting Architectural Services margins.

5. **UW Solutions Integration:** $241M acquisition generating net losses in initial integration period. $118M in segment goodwill could face impairment if performance doesn't improve.

6. **Sealant Product Litigation:** $24.7M arbitration already paid. Additional claims possible.

7. **IRS Examination:** FY2023 returns under examination since September 2025. Uncertain outcome.

### Short Interest

Short interest at 4.8% of float with 5.2 days to cover is moderately elevated but not extreme. The days-to-cover metric suggests limited liquidity for short covering, which could amplify moves in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Stock has declined 35% from 52-week high, creating potential entry if cyclical trough is near
- $774.7M backlog provides 2+ years of revenue visibility in Services
- Project Fortify Phase 2 delivers $25-26M in annualized savings, restoring margins in FY2027
- Performance Surfaces diversification proves out, with UW Solutions reaching 20%+ EBITDA margins
- New permanent CEO and CFO provide fresh strategic direction and restore investor confidence
- 3.3% dividend yield provides downside support
- Analyst consensus of $43 median target implies 33% upside

### Bear Case
- Non-residential construction cycle has further to fall in a stagflation/rising-rate environment
- Aluminum cost inflation (Gulf smelter destruction) accelerates Architectural Metals margin compression
- CEO/CFO departures signal deeper operational or governance issues not yet disclosed
- Pomerantz investigation reveals material issues
- UW Solutions continues to underperform, leading to goodwill impairment
- Restructuring savings are consumed by volume decline, providing no net margin improvement
- Earnings continue declining, pushing the P/E higher and potentially threatening the dividend
- Current analyst targets are stale and will be revised lower

### Assessment

The bear case is more probable in the current environment. The dual management departure during an active restructuring is a significant governance red flag. Organic revenue is declining across three of four segments. Margins are compressing at every level. The macro regime (stagflation, rising rates, $116 oil, aluminium at 4-year peaks) is directionally hostile to both non-residential construction demand and building products input costs. No open-market insider purchases suggest that anyone with inside knowledge views the stock as cheap.

The 19.6x trailing P/E on declining earnings is not obviously cheap when comparable peers IIIN and ROCK trade at 13-15x. The 3.3% yield is supportive but not sufficient to justify holding through continued fundamental deterioration.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: AVOID**

Apogee faces cyclical headwinds (non-residential construction downturn), operational deterioration (margins compressing across all four segments), input cost inflation (aluminium at multi-year highs), and a management vacuum (CEO + CFO departed within 10 weeks). Restructuring savings from Project Fortify may provide partial offset, but execution risk is elevated without permanent leadership. The current macro environment of rising rates, stagflation, and elevated commodity costs makes a near-term cyclical recovery unlikely. Analyst targets of $40-$46 appear to reflect conditions that have since deteriorated. The stock's 35% decline from its 52-week high is warranted by fundamentals, and further downside risk persists until: (1) a permanent CEO and CFO are installed, (2) organic revenue declines stabilize, and (3) there is visibility on margin recovery beyond restructuring savings. Until those conditions are met, the risk/reward is unfavorable.
