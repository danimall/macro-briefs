## Executive Summary

Lennar's operating model in this cycle is explicit and internally consistent: price to market, hold volume, and drive down construction cost. The filings confirm it has been executed. Deliveries rose 3% to 82,583 in FY2025 into a falling market, active communities expanded 18% over two years to 1,683, and construction costs are cited as the offsetting favorable variance in every segment discussion. The offset has been insufficient in five of five reporting periods examined. Home-sales gross margin fell from 22.3% (FY2024) to 17.7% (FY2025) to 15.4% (1H FY2026), while SG&A deleveraged from 7.3% to 9.2% of home-sales revenue. Diluted EPS went from $14.31 to $7.98, with FY2026 consensus at $5.54. Combining management's own Q3 FY2026 guidance (approximately 16% gross margin, 8.8–9.0% SG&A) implies a homebuilding operating margin on home sales of roughly 7.0–7.2%, against 14.9% in FY2024.

The less-discussed problem is cash. FY2025 operating cash flow was $216.8M on $34.19B of revenue, a 0.6% conversion rate, and free cash flow after $188.6M of capex was $28.2M. In 1H FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $717.9M. Over the same two periods Lennar returned $2.31B and $984M to shareholders, funded by a $1.7B delayed-draw term loan, $700M of new notes, and a homebuilding cash drawdown from $4.66B (11/30/24) to $1.82B (5/31/26). Net homebuilding debt to total capital moved from −9.4% to +9.4% in eighteen months, a $4.6B swing. The disclosed mechanism is the land-light transition: deposits and pre-acquisition costs on real estate grew to $7.06B, and Note 9 of the Q2 10-Q confirms that option maintenance fees paid to land banks are capitalized into land basis and released through cost of homes sold on takedown. That is the accounting channel connecting the strategy to management's repeated attribution of margin decline to "higher land costs."

At $88.18 the stock is 38.9% below its 52-week high of $144.24, which prompts the question of whether the selling is overdone. On the reconciled share count of 240.9M (see Financial Analysis), book value per share is $89.75, so the stock trades at 0.98x book. Using a residual-income identity with a 10% cost of equity and 3% terminal growth, 0.98x book implies a sustainable ROE near 10%. Lennar's FY2026 consensus earnings of $5.54 on ~$21.5B of equity produce a 6.1% ROE. The market is therefore not pricing distress at these levels; it is pricing full normalization. Against that, near-term consensus is falling hard (current-quarter estimate cut 25.3% in thirty days), the stock sits above the $86.23 consensus mean target, sell-side distribution is 8 of 18 at sell or strong sell, mortgage rates are in the mid-to-upper 6% range per management's own MD&A, and no officer or director has bought shares in the open market at approximately book value. Expected return on 30/45/25 bear/base/bull weights is roughly zero with a −26% bear leg. That combination supports REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Lennar is one of the two largest U.S. homebuilders by volume, delivering 82,583 homes in fiscal 2025 (November year-end) at a $391,000 average sales price. Homebuilding contributed $32.27B of $34.19B FY2025 revenue across four segments: East (346 active communities at 5/31/26), Central (462), South Central (433), and West (441), plus urban divisions and a ~40% interest in FivePoint Holdings carried at $602.8M.

Financial Services generated $1.20B of FY2025 revenue and $609.9M of segment operating earnings from mortgage origination, title, and insurance. It is fully captive: 100% of FY2025 residential originations were to buyers of homes Lennar built, with an 83% capture rate in Q2 FY2026. Multifamily ($681M revenue, $75M FY2025 operating loss) is being wound down — LMV I has sold 36 of 38 projects and a majority interest in Quarterra was sold to TPG Real Estate in December 2025. Lennar Other holds retained Rialto fund interests ($130.3M) and LENX technology positions, including $89.6M of Level 1 marketable equities at 5/31/26, down from $232.4M at 11/30/25.

The strategic direction is stated plainly: move toward a "pure play, asset-light, new home manufacturing company." The February 2025 Millrose spin-off contributed $5.6B of land (~87,000 homesites) and $1.0B of cash, reducing equity by $4.84B. Owned homesites collapsed from 85,428 (18% of total) at 11/30/24 to 11,075 (2%) at 5/31/26; years of owned supply fell from 1.1 to 0.1. Total controlled-plus-owned homesites were roughly flat at 495,466. The land pipeline did not shrink; title and carry moved to third parties.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Revenue $33.67B (FY22) → $34.23B → $35.44B → $34.19B (FY25). Pre-tax income $6.01B → $5.20B → $5.18B → $2.81B. Net income $4.61B → $3.94B → $3.93B → $2.08B. FY2025 net income fell 47% on a 3.5% revenue decline, which isolates margin, not demand, as the driver.

**Margin detail.** Incentives per home rose from $48,800 (10.3% of revenue) in FY2024 to $62,700 (13.8%) in FY2025, then eased to $55,200 (12.9%) in Q2 FY2026. Regional Q2 FY2026 gross margins: West 12.6% (from 16.7%) on $2.76B of home-sales revenue, the largest segment carrying the worst margin; Central 15.4% (from 18.7%); South Central ~17.3% with the heaviest incentive intensity at 18.8% of revenue in 1H; East improved to 19.1% from 18.9% as incentives there fell to $57,500 from $74,800.

**Q2 FY2026 specifics.** Total revenue $7.94B (−5.2% y/y); home-sales revenue $7.60B (−2.5%); deliveries 20,519 (+1.9%) at a $371,000 ASP (−4.6%); GAAP net earnings $304.8M or $1.24 diluted, versus $477.4M and $1.81. Management's adjusted figure excluding $23.3M of pre-tax technology marks was $1.31. Consensus of $1.25 was on a basis closest to GAAP and the print was a 1.1% miss; three of the last four quarters have missed, including a 13.7% miss in the December quarter. Financial Services operating earnings fell 36% to $100.2M on "lower profit per locked loan"; origination volume was flat ($5.07B vs $4.88B), so this is per-unit margin compression, removing the one segment that cushioned FY2025.

**Cash conversion is the central financial fact.** FY2025 OCF $216.8M against $2.078B of net income; 1H FY2026 OCF −$717.9M against $541.5M of net earnings. Deposits and pre-acquisition costs on real estate rose $1.5B in FY2025 ("primarily as a result of option contracts with Millrose") and a further $555M in 1H FY2026, "which included option maintenance fees paid to land banks," reaching $7.06B. That balance equals 33% of stockholders' equity and earns nothing.

Two readings are available and neither is fully resolvable from the disclosures. The transitional reading: deposits build as owned land converts to optioned land, the mix has now stabilized at 98% controlled, the 1H FY2026 outflow of $555M is smaller than 1H FY2025's $782M, and Lennar's first half is seasonally cash-consumptive given back-half-weighted deliveries. The structural reading: capitalized option carry is a permanent addition to land basis that flows through cost of homes sold rather than through interest expense, so it depresses gross margin indefinitely rather than reversing with the cycle. As an illustrative sensitivity, not a disclosed figure: if land is roughly 20% of a $375,000 ASP and a land bank earns a mid-teens return on land held approximately two years, embedded carry would be on the order of $10,000–15,000 per home, or 250–400bp of gross margin. The filings disclose no option fee rate, no takedown schedule, and no minimum-purchase obligation, so this cannot be verified, and management's competing attribution ("lower revenue per square foot and higher land costs year over year") places peak-basis 2021–2023 land and a 12% ASP decline ahead of carry cost in the ordering. Both mechanisms are plausibly operating.

**Balance sheet.** Homebuilding debt $4.047B at 5/31/26 against $1.816B of homebuilding cash; net homebuilding debt/total capital 9.4%, up from −9.4% at 11/30/24. Absolute leverage is conservative and covenant headroom is wide: tangible net worth $15.96B versus a $10.0B minimum, leverage ratio 14.7% versus a 60% maximum, liquidity test 30.0x versus 1.00x required. Maturities are manageable: $350.4M and $699.1M in 2027, $694.8M in 2030, and the $1.70B floating-rate term loan due May 2028 (extendable to 2029) at ~4.7%; the $400M 5.25% notes due June 2026 were redeemed after quarter-end. Homebuilding interest incurred rose to $111.5M in 1H FY2026 from $73.3M, on average debt of $4.11B versus $2.53B.

Off-balance-sheet exposure is substantial: $2.07B performance letters of credit, $0.80B financial letters of credit, $5.71B of surety bonds standing behind $3.28B of anticipated site-improvement costs, $6.98B of non-refundable option deposits, and $1.49B of consolidated inventory not owned against $1.31B of non-recourse liabilities.

**Share count reconciliation.** The supplied market capitalization of $18.56B implies ~210.5M shares. The Q2 10-Q reconciles to 263.09M Class A plus 36.60M Class B issued, less 58.79M treasury, or 240.90M outstanding, corroborated by weighted-average basic and diluted shares of 240.78M (Lennar reports basic and diluted EPS as identical under the two-class method, with no meaningful option or convertible overhang). Market capitalization at $88.18 is therefore ~$21.24B, and all multiples below use that basis. Using $18.6B would overstate cheapness by roughly 12%.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative on every measure that matters.

**Volume guidance was cut.** FY2026 deliveries guided "approximately 85,000" in the January 10-K became 82,000–83,000 in the June 10-Q, "reflecting current pressures on interest rates and continued macro uncertainty." FY2025 actual was 82,583, so the revised guide is flat to marginally lower. For a company whose thesis is to defend volume with price, a volume cut delivered alongside 15.4% gross margins indicates price concession is no longer clearing the market at the prior pace.

**Orders and backlog are mixed.** Q2 orders 21,749, −3.8% y/y; 1H orders 40,264 versus 40,956, −1.7%; cancellations steady at 14% across all four comparison periods. Backlog at 5/31/26 was 16,818 units and $6.61B — units +8.2% y/y, dollars +1.9%, backlog ASP −5.8% to $393,000. Backlog is genuine near-term visibility (performance obligations "generally satisfied in less than one year") and carries cancellation rights; it is not multi-year coverage.

**Estimates are falling, not rising.** Current-quarter consensus fell 25.3% in thirty days to $1.30; next quarter −6.2% to $2.03; current year −8.7% over ninety days to $5.54; next year −14.6% to $6.48. Eight to eleven analysts contribute, so this is not a thin-coverage artifact. FY2026 consensus implies roughly a 31% y/y EPS decline; FY2027 implies a 17% recovery that depends entirely on margin normalization.

**The one constructive datapoint.** Q2 marked the first meaningful decline in incentives on deliveries after three years of increases — $55,200 per home versus $59,500 a year earlier — which management describes as possibly "a leading indicator of margin recovery," immediately hedged with "it is too early to declare a sustained trend." Q3 gross margin is guided to approximately 16%, 40bp above Q2's 15.6%. Management expects "sequential margin improvement to continue as the year progresses." That is a real signal worth monitoring, but 40bp per quarter against 670bp of cumulative compression is a multi-year path, and it has not yet appeared in consensus, which is moving the other way.

**Medium-term optionality.** Community count is up 18% over two years, giving Lennar more selling locations than at any prior point; the Rausch Coleman structure ($254M for the homebuilding operations while Millrose took the land) demonstrates a repeatable, low-capital route to consolidating regional builders. Both are real assets in a demand recovery. Neither is a 2026–2027 earnings driver.

There is no growth premium to justify that positioning: FY2026 earnings are contracting roughly 31%.

The book-value support requires care. Price/book decomposes cleanly: with a 10% cost of equity and 3% terminal growth, fair P/B equals (ROE − g)/(r − g). FY2026 consensus earnings of $5.54 on ~238M average shares produce ~$1.32B of net income against ~$21.5B of equity, a 6.1% ROE, which supports roughly 0.44x book. A 10% ROE supports 1.0x. FY2024's ~14.5% ROE would support ~1.64x, or roughly $147. At 0.98x book the market is already capitalizing a full return to double-digit ROE. That is the specific reason the stock is not cheap: the apparent discount to book prices normalization that is not yet in evidence, and the land-light structure's central unresolved question is whether normalized ROE is the double-digit levels of prior cycles or something structurally lower once third-party land carry is permanently embedded in cost of sales.

Tangible book of $74.67 (after $3.632B of goodwill) is a more relevant floor for a builder whose owned inventory has shrunk to $10.9B. At 1.18x tangible book with expanding impairment indicators, the downside cushion is thinner than the headline P/B suggests.

The stock trades above the $86.23 consensus mean and $85.00 median target. Analyst targets function as floors only for inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples; here estimates are falling 8.7% to 14.6% over ninety days, so the targets are more likely to be cut than raised.

A normalized scenario worth carrying: 85,000 deliveries at $375,000 ASP, 17% gross margin, 8.5% SG&A produces roughly $2.7B of homebuilding operating profit on home sales; adding ~$450M of Financial Services and subtracting the ~$175M per quarter of other drag observed in Q2 gives ~$2.5B pre-tax, ~$1.86B net, or roughly $7.90 per share on 235M shares. At 13x that is $103. That scenario requires both incentive normalization and volume recovery to 85,000, and it is the honest bull arithmetic.

## Competitive Landscape

Lennar's competitive trajectory is genuinely two-sided, which is why the score sits below neutral rather than at either extreme.

**Improving.** Scale is intact and share was gained: deliveries up 3% in FY2025 into a contracting market, selling locations up 18% over two years, construction costs down (they are the cited favorable offset in every segment discussion), cycle times at "a record low," and the captive mortgage operation capturing 83–84% of Lennar's own buyers. The land-light structure permits capital-light M&A — the Rausch Coleman transaction added roughly 4,500 annual deliveries and expanded South Central communities from 285 to 417 for $254M of Lennar cash, because Millrose funded the land.

**Deteriorating.** The evidence standard that matters most for competitive trajectory, gross margin versus peers, points the wrong way. Lennar's 15.4% 1H margin sits well below the volume-builder cohort, and the West segment, the largest by revenue, is at 12.6% with California-specific homeowner-insurance availability problems disclosed following the January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires. Share was purchased with price: ASP fell from $423,000 (FY2024) to $371,000 (Q2 FY2026), a 12% decline in eighteen months, and the volume acquired through Rausch carries the lowest ASP ($232,000–$238,000) and the heaviest incentive load (18.8% of revenue) in the portfolio, diluting mix.

**The unproven experiment.** Management's stated payoff for land-light is "a meaningfully lower cost of capital" and the promise that "as margins begin to grow, returns on capital and equity will grow faster." The mechanical prediction is margin compression paired with higher asset turn and higher ROE. Eighteen months in, the compression has arrived (22.3% → 15.4%) and the return expansion has not (ROE 9.6% in FY2025 versus ~14.5% in FY2024, tracking toward ~6% in FY2026). The alternative reading — that the return benefit is back-end loaded and the observation window is contaminated by peak-basis land and a 12% ASP decline — is fully consistent with the same data. This is unresolved, and it is the single most important thing to monitor.

**Risk transformation, not elimination.** A land-light balance sheet caps inventory impairment (only $10.9B of owned inventory and $801M of land under development remain) but substitutes deposit-forfeiture risk: $6.98B of non-refundable deposits plus $411M of letters of credit posted in lieu of cash. FY2025 already saw Lennar walk away from 15,500 controlled homesites, versus 6,300 in FY2024, writing off $23.1M of deposits.

## Risk Assessment

**Land-bank counterparty concentration (most underappreciated).** The 10-K states the majority of land-banking arrangements are concentrated in a limited number of land banks including Millrose; the Q2 10-Q quantifies $2.6B of deposits and pre-acquisition costs with three land banks, of which $1.2B relates to Millrose. Lennar discloses that land banks may pledge optioned inventory as collateral and that on a land-bank bankruptcy "creditors may reject our purchase options." It also discloses that Millrose lacks capacity to fund all of Lennar's land needs and limits acquisitions to land Lennar expects to use within five years. Lennar's mitigation, that other land banks "could be substituted," is an assertion, not an audited conclusion. Millrose's own leverage and funding capacity were not examined for this report and are a direct extension of Lennar's risk.

**Impairment pipeline widening.** Communities reviewed for impairment indicators rose from 102 to 151 y/y (6.3% to 9.0% of active communities) while communities actually written down rose only 14 to 18 and valuation adjustments $17.9M to $36.2M. Total valuation adjustments plus option write-offs were $108.3M in 1H FY2026. Absolute amounts remain small (0.26% of 1H home-sale revenue), and the review population may reflect conservative monitoring rather than impending charges, but the flagged count is the forward indicator, and the West is where it would concentrate.

**Rate path.** Management's own MD&A notes mortgage rates "stubbornly elevated in the mid-to-upper 6% range," headline CPI at 4.2% y/y in May driven by energy tied to supply disruptions from the Iran conflict, and "the Federal Reserve remains on hold... near-term rate relief appears unlikely." Incentive relief reverses quickly if rates rise from here.

**Financial Services has no diversification.** With 100% of FY2025 originations to Lennar's own buyers, this segment amplifies rather than offsets the delivery cycle, and it is already down 36%. Separately, $3.0B of warehouse lines require renewal or replacement during FY2026, and commitments have already shrunk to $2.775B from $3.575B.

**Governance.** Stuart Miller holds Executive Chairman, CEO and President titles and is the Chief Operating Decision Maker for segment reporting, with ~42% of combined voting power. Four Item 5.02 8-Ks in eighteen months (June 8, 2026; November 14, 2025; August 4, 2025; January 15, 2025) were not read for this report; if any involves a CFO or COO-level change it would be material to the assessment. The recurring "non-deductible executive compensation" tax-rate item indicates compensation above the Section 162(m) threshold for multiple executives; the proxy was not reviewed, so pay-versus-performance alignment is not established.

**Other.** Tariffs on lumber, steel, aluminum, solar panels and washing machines are disclosed as already having raised construction costs. Subcontractor insurance unavailability has forced Lennar to waive customary insurance requirements and raise self-insured retentions. Level 1 technology marks have swung P&L by −$91.9M to +$130.2M in recent periods; these are non-cash and do not affect covenants or operating cash flow, but they add noise to net-income-based ratios.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.3% sits 5.9 points below 37.1% one-year realized volatility, in the first percentile of the current chain, with a balanced −0.9% skew and a ±3.4% implied move on the August 14 expiry. Term structure is in contango: 31.3% near versus 42.0% at the September 18 tenor (11.2% implied move, spanning the Q3 print and the FOMC) and 41.8% at six months. Since the July 21 snapshot, ATM IV collapsed from 44.0% to 31.3% while the stock rose 7.7%, and put/call open interest fell from 2.68 to 1.51 — protection was unwound into the rally. The one dissonant reading is the August 21 tenor, carrying a +5.2% put skew and a 2.05 put/call volume ratio.

The market prices essentially no near-term event risk while pricing considerably more over the three-to-six-month window that contains the September earnings report and the rate decision. That is a complacent near-dated structure in a name whose current-quarter consensus was cut 25.3% over the same thirty days. Cheap near-term volatility argues for expressing caution through options rather than at spot for holders who want to retain exposure. Short interest at 0.3% of float and 0.2 days to cover means the bearish case carries no squeeze risk and is not crowded; the negative view is expressed through the sell-side ratings distribution (8 of 18 at sell or strong sell) rather than through borrow.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+34%)

Incentives peaked in FY2025 and the Q2 decline to $55,200 per home is the first turn in three years, with East already at $57,500 versus $74,800. Construction costs are falling and cycle times are at record lows, so any revenue-per-square-foot stabilization converts directly to margin. Gross margin steps from 15.6% to ~16% in Q3 and continues sequentially; community count is up 18%, giving Lennar more selling locations than ever when demand returns. If the long end moves lower and mortgage rates move toward 6%, affordability improves at the exact price points where Lennar's incentive load is heaviest. Federal attention to housing affordability, which management flags in both filings, is unpriced optionality. Land-light then delivers as designed: 98% of homesites controlled rather than owned, minimal capital tied up in dirt, and margin recovery flowing to a smaller equity base. Normalized 85,000 deliveries at 17% gross margin and 8.5% SG&A supports roughly $7.90 of EPS; 14x, or 1.3x book, gets to $110–120. Deposit balances stabilize, takedowns release cash, and FY2027 free cash flow inflects sharply positive.

### Bear Case — $65 (−26%)

Roughly 250–400bp of the 670bp margin compression is structural, being the capitalized cost of renting land from third-party capital that flows permanently through cost of homes sold, so normalized gross margin is 18–19% rather than 22%. Normalized ROE settles at 9–11% rather than double digits at the higher end, and 0.98x book is then the ceiling rather than the floor. Mortgage rates stay near 7%; incentives resume climbing; the volume guidance cut from 85,000 to 82,000–83,000 proves to be the first of several. The impairment pipeline converts — 151 flagged communities becomes materially more than 18 written down, concentrated in the West at 12.6% margin — and tangible book of $74.67 erodes. Financial Services continues to compress per-loan profit with no diversification offset. Operating cash flow stays negative, the remaining $1.0B buyback authorization is exhausted, and net homebuilding debt/capital passes 15% while the term loan reprices. At 12x a $5.40 FY2027 outcome, or 0.72x current book, the stock is $65, precisely the low analyst target.

### What the 38.9% drawdown from the 52-week high is discounting

This is a stock falling on impaired fundamentals, not on intact ones. Diluted EPS has gone from $14.31 to $7.98 to a $5.54 consensus, gross margin has lost 670bp, and free cash flow has gone from $2.4B of operating cash flow in FY2024 to negative $718M in 1H FY2026. The drawdown is proportionate to the earnings decline. The relevant question is not whether sellers are wrong about the past but whether $88.18 correctly prices the future, and on the residual-income arithmetic the price already embeds a return to roughly 10% ROE from a current 6%. There is no identified mispricing to exploit on the long side.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Gross margin printing at or above 16.5% in Q3 FY2026 with SG&A at or below 8.8%, confirming the sequential path rather than merely meeting the ~16% guide.
2. Operating cash flow positive for the fiscal third quarter with the deposits and pre-acquisition costs balance flat or declining from $7.06B, evidencing that the deposit build is transitional.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 delivery guidance cut a second time, below 82,000.
2. Communities written down exceeding 35 in a single half-year, or valuation adjustments plus option write-offs exceeding $250M in a half, indicating the 151 flagged communities are converting.
3. Any disclosed disruption in a land-bank relationship, a Millrose funding constraint, or failure to renew the $3.0B of FY2026 warehouse commitments.
4. Net homebuilding debt/total capital exceeding 20% while operating cash flow remains negative.
5. An Item 5.02 filing disclosing a CFO or COO departure.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 FY2026 report, expected mid-September 2026, is the decisive checkpoint: it tests the ~16% gross margin guide, the sequential-improvement claim, whether incentives declined for a second consecutive quarter, and whether third-quarter operating cash flow turns positive with back-half-weighted deliveries. The September FOMC decision is the second input. Reassess no later than the first week of October 2026.

## Conclusion

Lennar has executed the strategy it described. Volume held, community count grew 18%, construction costs and cycle times fell, and the balance sheet carries only 15.8% homebuilding debt to total capital with covenant headroom several times over. None of that is in dispute. The problem is what the model has produced so far: 670bp of gross margin surrendered, SG&A deleveraging to 9.2%, diluted EPS down from $14.31 to a $5.54 consensus, $217M of operating cash flow on $34.19B of FY2025 revenue followed by negative $718M in the first half of FY2026, and $3.3B of shareholder returns across those two periods funded by a $1.7B term loan and a homebuilding cash drawdown from $4.66B to $1.82B.

At $88.18 the stock offers a 2.27% yield and 0.98x stated book, which reads as support until the return arithmetic is applied: 0.98x book capitalizes a sustainable ROE near 10%, against 6.1% on FY2026 consensus.
