## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest independent natural gas producer in the United States, formed through the October 2024 merger of Chesapeake Energy and Southwestern Energy. The company produces approximately 7.2 Bcfe/day across three core basins (Haynesville, Northeast Appalachia, Southwest Appalachia) and holds 25,880 Bcfe in proved reserves with a PV-10 of $19.4 billion. It was added to the S&P 500 in March 2025 and carries investment-grade ratings from all three agencies.

The stock trades at approximately 11.0x forward earnings (FY2026E EPS of $9.10), a discount for an S&P 500 constituent with structural demand tailwinds from LNG export capacity growth, AI-driven power demand, and industrial reshoring. Q1 2026 results were strong ($3.83 adjusted EPS, beating consensus), and management reaffirmed full-year production guidance of ~7.5 Bcfe/d. The company has $5.7 billion in liquidity (as of March 31, 2026), no revolver borrowings, and over 60% of 2026 gas volumes hedged with floor protection.

The two material negatives are management instability (CEO departed February 2026, permanent replacement still being sought, CFO replaced April 2026) and natural gas price sensitivity in the near-term. Current quarter estimates have been revised down 13-22% as Henry Hub prices moderated from winter storm peaks. However, the structural demand thesis (LNG export capacity growth, data center power requirements) is intact, and the valuation provides adequate margin of safety against commodity cyclicality.

## Company Overview

Expand Energy operates across three distinct natural gas basins:

- **Haynesville** (Louisiana/Texas): ~3,000 MMcf/day, 42% of production. Strategically proximate to Gulf Coast LNG terminals. Connected via the company's 35% interest in the NG3 pipeline (in service October 2025).
- **Northeast Appalachia** (Pennsylvania, Marcellus): ~2,624 MMcf/day, 37% of production. Periodically benefits from premium pricing near Northeast demand centers, though realized prices vary by season.
- **Southwest Appalachia** (West Virginia/Ohio, Marcellus/Utica): ~1,559 MMcfe/day, 21% of production. Includes oil and NGL exposure.

The company operates ~99% of current daily production, holds working interests in approximately 6,600 gross wells across 4.2 million gross acres, and owns vertically integrated oilfield services (acquired via the SWN merger). Production is approximately 92% natural gas by volume.

The strategic evolution toward a broader gas supply and marketing role is evidenced by the Delfin LNG offtake partnership, the hiring of Dan Turco (from ExxonMobil's global LNG trading desk), and the NG3 pipeline investment providing direct gathering-to-terminal connectivity. This strategy remains in early stages and is subject to significant commercial and regulatory execution risk.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Headwinds:** Current quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 13.2% over 30 days and 22.1% over 90 days, reflecting Henry Hub price normalization after the Q1 2026 winter storm spike. This is a commodity reversion effect and does not reflect operational deterioration. Full-year 2026 estimates remain relatively stable ($9.10, down only 0.7% in 30 days) and are up 4.0% over 90 days.

**Structural Demand Drivers:**
1. **LNG Export Capacity:** U.S. capacity expected to grow significantly over the coming decade, with the company's proxy statement noting LNG feed-gas requirements could exceed 36 Bcf/d by 2040 as export capacity more than doubles. EXE's Haynesville assets sit on top of the feedstock supply for Gulf Coast terminals.
2. **AI/Data Center Power:** Management explicitly identifies this as a structural demand catalyst. Natural gas is the marginal fuel for incremental power generation in most U.S. markets.
3. **Industrial Reshoring:** Domestic manufacturing expansion drives incremental baseload gas demand.
4. **Weather Volatility:** Extreme weather creates episodic price spikes that benefit hedged producers with upside participation (EXE's costless collar structure).

**Production Growth:** 2026 guidance calls for $2.75-$2.95B in capex with 205-235 gross well completions across 11-12 rigs. The massive PUD reserve upgrade (3,842 Bcfe to 7,304 Bcfe) provides deep inventory runway.

**Next-Year Consensus:** $9.77 EPS for FY2027, revised up 4.5% over 30 days. Analysts see improving pricing and/or production growth beyond the near-term storm-related normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market Position:** EXE is the largest independent U.S. natural gas producer. Its closest pure-play competitor is EQT Corp (which acquired Equitrans Midstream and is moving toward integrated midstream). Diversified majors (ExxonMobil, Chevron) participate in the broader market but lack EXE's pure natural gas focus and basin-specific scale.

**Competitive Advantages:**
- **Scale economies:** G&A of $0.07/Mcfe is among the lowest in the industry. Purchasing power on services, marketing leverage with LNG buyers.
- **Basin diversification:** Haynesville provides LNG export proximity; Appalachia provides Northeast demand-center access. Natural hedge against basin-specific disruptions.
- **Investment grade access:** Lower cost of capital vs. sub-IG peers (APA at 10.1x P/E but higher leverage and geopolitical risk).
- **Vertical integration:** Owned drilling rigs and services reduce well-cost inflation sensitivity.
- **LNG value chain ambitions:** The Delfin LNG partnership, NG3 pipeline, and Turco hire point toward participation in the global LNG value chain. If executed successfully, this could move EXE beyond purely domestic commodity price-taking, though this outcome remains subject to significant execution, regulatory, and capital risk.

**Competitive Trajectory:** Improving. The merger doubled scale, the NG3 pipeline added infrastructure value, the Delfin partnership advances the LNG strategy, and unit costs are declining (DD&A $1.13/Mcfe vs. $1.26 prior year, Haynesville breakevens reduced 15%).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Management Instability (HIGH):**
The CEO departed abruptly in February 2026 (suspicious timing relative to 10-K filing). The CFO was replaced in April 2026 (after an August 2025 departure of the predecessor under reportedly unclear circumstances). A permanent CEO has not been named. During a complex three-basin integration, this level of C-suite turnover creates execution risk and strategic uncertainty.

Mitigants: Interim CEO Wichterich has board-level experience and prior Interim CEO tenure. His open-market purchase at $107-108 signals confidence. Board and operational teams appear stable. Investment grade ratings maintained.

**2. Natural Gas Price Sensitivity (HIGH):**
At 92% gas production, EXE is heavily exposed to Henry Hub. The EIA's April 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook forecasts $3.67/MMBtu for 2026 and $3.59/MMBtu for 2027, materially below the elevated winter storm prices seen in Q1 2026. Gas prices can be extremely volatile, and a sustained move below $3.00 would significantly compress margins.

Mitigants: Over 60% of 2026 volumes hedged with floor protection via costless collars. Breakevens continue declining. LNG demand growth provides structural price support over time.

**3. ~$9.2B Midstream Commitments:**
Take-or-pay GP&T obligations (approximately $9.2B undiscounted as of March 31, 2026) create fixed cost exposure regardless of production levels. GP&T costs rose from $0.75/Mcfe in 2024 to $0.91/Mcfe in 2025, and further increased to $1.03/Mcfe in Q1 2026, driven by increased volumes, annual fee escalations, and NG3 going into service. This ongoing cost inflation remains a material risk to netbacks.

**4. LNG Strategy Execution Risk:**
The bull thesis requires continued U.S. LNG export growth. Regulatory reversal, terminal construction delays, or global demand shifts could impair the thesis. Expand disclosed in its Q1 2026 release that previously announced SPAs with Delfin and Gunvor were terminated, and a new SPA to purchase approximately 1.15 million tonnes per year of LNG from Delfin FLNG 1 was executed in April 2026, subject to FID, with a targeted start in 2031. The LNG strategy is real but remains in early stages with significant commercial and timeline uncertainty.

**5. Integration Execution:**
Three distinct basins with different geological and market characteristics. Full synergy capture not yet complete.

## Conclusion

Expand Energy offers compelling risk/reward at 11.0x forward earnings for the largest independent U.S. natural gas producer. The structural demand thesis (LNG exports, AI power, reshoring) provides multi-year visibility beyond near-term commodity noise. The investment-grade balance sheet, $5.7B liquidity, 60%+ hedged volumes, and declining unit costs create a resilient financial profile. The primary concerns — management instability and commodity sensitivity — are genuine but appear priced into the discount vs. peer multiples.

The prior upgrade to BUY remains warranted: the valuation cushion is sufficient, the structural demand drivers are strengthening, and insider buying confirms management confidence. The management instability represents a legitimate discount factor but does not merit downgrading an otherwise well-positioned asset. The stock should re-rate toward $118-125 as the CEO vacancy resolves and LNG demand growth materializes in operational results.

**Recommendation: BUY** — 2-3% position sizing. Commodity binary risk warrants standard rather than concentrated positioning.
