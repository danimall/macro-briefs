## Executive Summary

Central Pacific Financial Corp. is a $912M market cap Hawaii-based bank holding company operating 27 branches through its subsidiary, Central Pacific Bank. The company delivered strong FY2025 results (EPS $2.86, +45% net income growth, 13.6% ROE) driven by NIM expansion from 3.01% to 3.45%, and Q1 2026 continued the trend with a 4.4% earnings beat, NIM at 3.53%, and resumed loan growth. At $34.95, the stock trades at ~11.6x trailing and ~10.7x forward FY2026 consensus ($3.27), with a 3.3% dividend yield.

The fundamental profile is solid: CET1 at 12.7%, NPAs at 0.19% of assets, deposit costs declining (0.90%), and management guiding 4-6% NII growth in 2026. The deposit franchise (53% with 10+ year tenure, 91.7% core, no brokered deposits) is a genuine competitive asset. Total capital return (dividends + buybacks) should approach 6-7% of market cap in 2026.

The stock does not meet the threshold for a BUY recommendation due to three specific concerns: (1) the stock has appreciated 30% over the past year and now trades within 2% of its 52-week high ($35.45), meaning the valuation discount versus peers has substantially compressed; (2) estimate revisions are turning negative — next year EPS estimates have declined 1.8% over 90 days, and current quarter estimates fell 2.7% in the past 30 days; and (3) the earnings beat/miss record is inconsistent (2-for-4), reducing confidence that the company will outperform consensus going forward. The income return profile (3.3% yield + buyback support) makes this a reasonable hold for income-oriented portfolios.

## Company Overview

Central Pacific Financial Corp. operates as the 4th largest depository institution in Hawaii by deposit market share. Founded in 1954 by Japanese-American WWII veterans, the bank provides commercial banking, residential mortgage, consumer lending, and wealth management services primarily across the Hawaiian islands, with selective mainland diversification.

Key operational metrics:
- Total assets: $7.41 billion
- 27 branches across 4 Hawaiian islands + 55 ATMs
- 763 employees (average tenure 9 years)
- Single reportable segment
- 17-time Hawaii SBA Lender of the Year

The business model centers on relationship-based banking in an oligopolistic market. Hawaii's concentrated banking structure (4 major players controlling the vast majority of deposits) creates barriers to entry and supports pricing power, particularly on the deposit side.

## Growth Analysis

CPF's growth profile is moderate and appropriate for a community bank:

**NIM-driven NII growth (4-6% guided for 2026):** The positive repricing dynamics are quantifiable and have high visibility — CD maturities, securities cash flows, and new loan yields are known. This provides reasonable confidence in near-term earnings trajectory.

**Loan growth resumption:** After relatively flat 2025, Q1 2026 showed $31M in net loan growth. Mainland expansion (loans grew from $711M to $821M in one year) provides a diversification vector, though mainland construction loans ($60M from near-zero) introduce unfamiliar credit risk.

**Expense discipline:** Management guides 2.5-3.5% expense growth, well below NII growth, meaning continued efficiency improvement.

**Forward consensus:** $3.27 EPS for FY2026 (+14% YoY) and $3.57 for FY2027 (+9%). The deceleration from FY2025's 45% growth to mid-teens is expected as NIM normalization runs its course.

**Limiting factors:** Hawaii's GDP growth typically tracks 1-3% real. UHERO projects tourism softness in 2026 (-1.3% visitor arrivals). The bank has limited ability to grow significantly faster than its primary market economy without taking outsized credit risk.

CPF trades at a 17-24% discount to peer median P/E while generating above-peer ROE. At face value, this suggests undervaluation. However, several factors explain and partially justify the discount:

1. **Geographic concentration:** Single-state exposure to Hawaii (tourism-dependent, natural disaster risk, extreme real estate valuations) warrants a diversification discount
2. **Smaller scale:** $7.4B assets limits product capabilities versus larger regional peers
3. **52-week high proximity:** At $34.95 vs. a 52-week high of $35.45, the stock is 98.6% of its 52-week peak. The discount to peers has already compressed materially over the past year

Analyst consensus target: $39.00 (median), implying ~12% upside. Range: $37-$41. The consensus target at $39 represents approximately 11.9x FY2026 EPS ($3.27), still modestly below peer multiples.

The valuation is reasonable but no longer cheap in absolute terms. A year ago, CPF was arguably mispriced; the market has largely corrected this. Further re-rating requires either sustained estimate revisions upward (which are currently moving the other direction) or a catalyst like M&A.

## Competitive Landscape

CPF operates in a 4-player oligopoly in Hawaii banking:
1. First Hawaiian Bank (FHB) — $23B+ assets
2. Bank of Hawaii (BOH) — ~$23B assets
3. American Savings Bank — subsidiary of Hawaiian Electric Industries
4. Central Pacific Bank — $7.4B assets

**Competitive advantages:**
- Deep relationship banking with 53% of deposits having 10+ year tenure
- 91.7% core deposits with no brokered funding — lowest-cost funding structure
- 17-time SBA Lender of the Year — dominant small business franchise
- Japanese/Korean financial institution deposit partnerships

**Competitive trajectory:** Stable. CPF is not gaining or losing meaningful market share. The Hawaiian banking market grows slowly but steadily. Fintech disruption is acknowledged but limited in Hawaii's relationship-driven culture. The FDIC's relaxation of merger review rules (returning to 1998 guidelines) and CPF's $3.1M "strategic opportunity" expense in 2024 suggest potential future M&A activity, though no concrete near-term catalyst exists.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Geographic concentration (HIGH):** 84% of loans in Hawaii, ~80% collateralized by real estate. Median Oahu home: $1.14M. A Hawaii-specific recession (tourism collapse, military base closure, natural disaster) would impact CPF disproportionately versus diversified peers. This is the structural discount driver.

2. **Classified loan deterioration (MODERATE):** The jump from $24.3M to $67.6M in classified loans (3.17% to 8.56% of Tier 1 + ACL) warrants monitoring. Management attributes this to one CRE relationship. If additional downgrades emerge, the market will penalize the stock.

3. **NIM plateau risk (MODERATE):** NIM at 3.53% is near the high end of the cycle for a bank this size. Management's guidance of 3.50-3.55% confirms limited further expansion. If rate dynamics shift (rapid cuts, or hikes that raise deposit costs), the NIM tailwind reverses.

4. **Mainland expansion execution (MODERATE):** Growing from $711M to $821M in mainland loans (+15% in one year), with construction loans surging from near-zero to $60M, introduces credit risk in markets where CPF lacks the deep relationship knowledge that protects its Hawaiian book.

5. **Tariff/tourism headwinds (LOW-MODERATE):** UHERO projects -1.3% visitor arrivals and -2.4% visitor spending in 2026. Current global trade uncertainty and potential recession would hit Hawaii's tourism-dependent economy.

6. **Insurance cost spiral (LOW-MODERATE):** Post-Maui fire insurance premium increases could pressure residential real estate values and borrower affordability.

**Risk magnitude assessment:** The geographic concentration risk is structural and permanent — it is the reason CPF trades at a discount to peers and is largely priced. The classified loan jump is the most actionable near-term risk; a second quarter of deterioration would signal systemic credit issues rather than a one-off.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-41, +9-17% upside):**
- NIM sustains at 3.50-3.55% through 2026, NII grows 5-6%
- Loan growth accelerates to mid-single digits with stable credit
- M&A activity (either as acquirer of a smaller franchise or as a target — CPF's 10-K language + $3.1M "strategic opportunity" expense suggest awareness)
- Proposed Basel capital rule changes release 50-100 bps of CET1, enabling additional buybacks
- Multiple re-rates to 12-13x forward earnings (still below peer median)
- Total return: ~12% price appreciation + 3.3% dividend = ~15%

**Bear Case ($28-30, -14-20% downside):**
- Hawaii tourism declines more than projected; residential real estate values correct 10-15%
- Classified loans continue rising; additional CRE downgrades emerge
- NIM compresses if deposit competition intensifies or if rates rise forcing higher funding costs
- Mainland expansion credit losses surface (construction, auto portfolios)
- Multiple compresses to 9x on deteriorating fundamentals
- Stock revisits August 2025 lows ($25.62) only in severe Hawaii-specific stress scenario

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $30-31 (forward P/E ~9.5x) on market-wide or sector-wide selling unrelated to CPF fundamentals — this would recreate the valuation gap
2. Next-year estimate revisions inflect positively (+3%+ over 30 days) indicating earnings momentum re-acceleration
3. Concrete M&A announcement (either as acquirer or target) with accretive financial terms

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Classified loans rise above $100M or NPAs exceed 0.50% of assets — signals systemic Hawaii CRE credit deterioration
2. NIM compresses below 3.30% for two consecutive quarters
3. Management cuts dividend or suspends buyback program

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late July 2026 for Q2 results). Key metrics to monitor: NIM trajectory, classified loan trends, deposit cost trends, loan growth sustainability.

## Conclusion

CPF is a well-run Hawaii bank with strong capital ratios, improving profitability, and an attractive dividend yield. The business is operating well and management has executed on NIM expansion, expense control, and capital return. The fundamental profile at ~11x forward earnings and 3.3% yield with ~6% total capital return is reasonable for an income-oriented holding.

The recommendation remains HOLD rather than BUY for three concrete, falsifiable reasons:

1. **Estimate momentum is negative:** FY2027 EPS consensus has declined from $3.63 to $3.57 over 90 days (-1.8%), and current quarter estimates fell from $0.81 to $0.78 in the past 30 days (-2.7%). This is inconsistent with the positive estimate revision profile required for high-conviction BUY calls in Financials.

2. **Inconsistent earnings execution:** A 2-for-4 beat/miss record (with misses in Q2 and Q3 2025 of -5.2% and -2.2% respectively) reduces confidence that consensus estimates represent a beatable bar. The Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 beats recovered credibility, but the pattern is not yet sufficiently established.

3. **Valuation discount compression:** The stock has appreciated 30% over the trailing year and trades within 2% of its 52-week high. The P/E discount to peers has already meaningfully compressed. The remaining gap (~17-24% discount to peer median 14x) reflects structural Hawaii concentration risk that is unlikely to close absent an M&A catalyst. Analyst consensus implies only 12% upside to $39.

The stock is appropriate as an income-generating position (3.3% yield + buyback support) with optionality on M&A or further NIM expansion. It becomes a BUY on a pullback to ~$30-31 or on evidence that estimate revisions are re-accelerating.
