**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Asbury Automotive Group is one of the largest U.S. franchised automotive retailers, running 202 franchises across 158 locations in 14 states as of Q1 2026, alongside its in-house finance-and-insurance product platform, Total Care Auto (TCA). The investment question is whether the current ~8x trailing P/E is a genuine discount or a fair reflection of an operating base that is decelerating while headline earnings are propped up by one-time gains. The business generates roughly $589M of free cash flow and buys back stock aggressively.

The evidence points to the latter, at least in the near term. Reported net income rose 42% in Q1 2026 to $187.8M, but that was driven entirely by a $125.8M pre-tax divestiture gain. Underlying income from operations fell 17%, and SG&A as a percentage of gross profit deteriorated to 70.2% from 63.0% a year earlier — a ~700bp swing that traces directly to the cost strain of integrating the $1.76B Herb Chambers acquisition, migrating from CDK to the Tekion dealer management system (only ~74 of 158 stores migrated by Q1'26), and rolling out TCA across the portfolio simultaneously. New-vehicle gross profit per unit (GPU), the dominant earnings swing factor, has fallen from $3,697 in 2024 to $3,432 in 2025 to $3,271 in Q1'26, with same-store new GPU down ~10% year over year. The post-COVID margin windfall is unwinding at an uncertain pace.

Against this, the bull case rests on three points: management is putting real cash to work buying its own stock (CEO Hult ~$1M open-market at ~$205, plus director purchases at $182–202), which reads as conviction that the market is over-discounting the normalization; the parts-and-service annuity (47.9% of gross profit) is growing on a record 12.8-year average vehicle age; and TCA's revenue-recognition timing is currently depressing reported F&I even as it builds long-term deferred revenue and profitability. None of it is a dated catalyst, and consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.25, consistent with the declining trajectory of prior reports (5.8 → 5.2 → 5.25). The risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Asbury operates a franchised auto retail model built on four revenue streams with sharply different economics:

- **New vehicles** — 52.8% of revenue but only ~20% of gross profit (thin margin, most exposed to GPU normalization).
- **Used vehicles** — ~29% of revenue, ~8% of gross profit; used GPU improved ~10% to $1,672 in 2025 on disciplined sourcing.
- **Parts & Service (P&S)** — 13.9% of revenue but 47.9% of gross profit; the highest-margin, most stable, counter-cyclical line.
- **Finance & Insurance (F&I)** — 4.3% of revenue, 23.4% of gross profit; the segment where TCA vertical integration provides a structural edge over peers.

The company reports two segments, Dealerships and TCA, the latter acquired via the 2021 Larry H. Miller transaction. OEM concentration is meaningful but diversified: Toyota/Lexus at 30% of new-vehicle revenue, Ford/Lincoln 14%, Honda/Acura 10%, Stellantis 8%, GM 7%, Mercedes 7%, Hyundai 6%. Competitive advantages include national vendor scale, a shared-service back office, brand and geographic diversification, state franchise laws, and the TCA F&I engine that most peers lack in-house.

A CEO transition appears to have occurred in Q2 2026. Form 4 filings show Daniel Clara titled Chief Operating Officer on a 3/5/2026 equity grant and Chief Executive Officer on a 5/4/2026 grant, while David Hult signed the Q1 10-Q as CEO on 5/1/2026. This aligns with a cluster of 8-K Item 5.02 officer-change filings in late 2025 and early 2026. This succession is inferred from Form 4 officer titles and the timing of 8-K filings; the specific succession 8-K text was not directly retrievable and should be confirmed. A leadership change during a triple operational transformation amplifies execution risk.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $14.1B (2023) to $17.2B (2024) to $18.0B (2025), but the growth is acquisition-driven (Koons in December 2023, Herb Chambers in July 2025), not organic. Same-store trends are soft.

The core narrative is post-COVID margin normalization. Operating income margin compressed from a 2022 peak of 8.7% ($1.27B) to 4.8% ($861M) in 2025. Net income moved $997M (2022) → $602M (2023) → $430M (2024) → $492M (2025), with the 2025 uptick flattered by an $80.2M divestiture gain. Both the FY2025 net income growth (+14%) and Q1'26 (+42%) are heavily inflated by one-time dealership divestiture gains. Normalizing those out, the operating business is decelerating.

Q1 2026 crystallized the concern. Revenue was $4.11B (-1% YoY), gross profit was roughly flat, but income from operations fell 17%. SAAR fell to 15.6M (Q1'25 had tariff-driven pull-forward), and severe weather hurt volumes.

Offsets exist. P&S gross profit rose ~9% in 2025 on the record vehicle age, driving more customer-pay and warranty work. Used-vehicle GPU improved. But these partial offsets do not reverse the front-end weakness.

On the balance sheet: total debt $3.59B plus floor-plan $2.03B; net leverage 3.2x, at the high end of the 2.5–3.5x target and up from 2.9x pre-Herb Chambers. Altman Z of 2.45 sits in the grey zone, D/E ~3.0, current ratio 0.95 — leveraged, but typical for auto retail given floor-plan financing. FCF was $589M in 2025 (OCF $775M) against disciplined capex of ~$186M, guiding ~$250M for 2026. About $1.97B of debt is variable-rate; a 100bp move is roughly $20–26M of annual interest impact, partly hedged via five interest-rate swaps. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this variable exposure is a modest but real drag.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly on paper: share count fell from 22.3M (2022) to 18.6M (April 2026), the buyback authorization was raised to $500M on 2/25/2026, and $147M was repurchased in Q1'26 with $453M remaining. No dividend. The tension is that leverage sits at the top of range while the company is buying back stock into a decelerating operating base — a bet that the margin normalization is over-discounted rather than a structural earnings reset.

## Growth Analysis

There is no clean organic growth story here at present. Reported revenue growth is acquisition-fueled, same-store metrics are soft, and the dominant earnings driver — new-vehicle GPU — is reverting toward pre-COVID levels with an uncertain trajectory. Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration: current-quarter consensus fell 5.1% over 90 days, current-year fell 2.4% to $25.75, and even next-year came down 0.6% to $29.40. This is broad-based negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the estimate-revision signal that would support a more aggressive stance.

The earnings surprise track record reinforces the caution: after beating by 7.2% (Q2'25) and 4.1% (Q3'25), the company missed by 0.9% (Q4'25) and 5.3% (Q1'26). A recent miss following a beat streak signals deteriorating visibility, consistent with GPU normalizing and integration costs biting.

The structural growth levers that could re-accelerate the story are real but slow and partly non-cyclical: (1) full TCA rollout across the portfolio, including Herb Chambers, targeted for 2026, which should lift long-run F&I profitability — currently masked because TCA revenue is recognized over the contract life versus immediate recognition for third-party F&I, so reported same-store F&I is being depressed by a timing effect while deferred revenue builds; (2) the P&S annuity riding the vehicle-age tailwind; and (3) accretion from Herb Chambers, which contributed $566M of revenue and $14.7M of net income in Q1'26.

Given falling estimates, an acquisition-dependent top line, and a normalizing margin base, growth prospects score 4.3. The score is not lower because the P&S and TCA components provide genuine, if modest, forward optionality that is being obscured by near-term optics.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$222 the stock trades at roughly 8x trailing EPS of $25.13 and ~8.6x the forward-year consensus of $25.75. That is a deep discount to the peer median P/E of 26.7, but the peer set is heterogeneous and misleading here. The most relevant comparable, Group 1 Automotive (GPI), the closest franchised-dealer analog, trades at 12.5x, also at a ~33% discount to its 52-week high. The higher-multiple names (VVV, AAP, PHIN, SAH) are parts distributors and service businesses with different economics. Against GPI, Asbury trades at roughly 0.71x the comparable multiple.

The discount to GPI is partly justified: Asbury carries higher leverage (3.2x net at the top of range), is deeper into a triple integration that is inflating SG&A, has falling estimate revisions, an ongoing FTC enforcement matter, a CEO transition, and headline earnings distorted by divestiture gains. A low P/E on a decelerating operating base with these overhangs can be a fair price for elevated uncertainty rather than a bargain.

The valuation dimension deserves particular skepticism given the historical tendency for "cheap" auto-retail names to sort the wrong way. The trailing multiple looks compelling, but the earnings it is applied to are inflated by one-time gains, and the operating earnings power is declining. Normalizing out ~$125.8M of Q1 divestiture gains materially lowers the true run-rate. The case for meaningful re-rating requires a specific catalyst — SG&A leverage recovering as Tekion migration completes and TCA rollout finishes, or GPU stabilizing above bear expectations. Neither is dated or confirmed.

We score valuation 5.5, down from the 7.0 assigned earlier in the trajectory. The multiple is genuinely low, and the FCF yield (roughly 14% on 2025 FCF against the ~$4.1B market cap) provides real downside support and buyback firepower. But the absence of an identifiable catalyst, the earnings-quality distortion, and the leverage caution keep this from being a high-conviction cheap-stock call. Analyst targets bracket the current price ($212 low, $223 median, $234.70 mean, $312 high), which for a decelerating name with falling estimates should be read as fair-value confirmation rather than as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Asbury competes with public consolidators (AutoNation, Penske, Lithia, Group 1, Sonic), independent used retailers (CarMax, Carvana), and independent service shops. The industry backdrop is the unwinding of the extraordinary 2021–2022 new-vehicle margin windfall as inventory normalizes; SAAR is running ~15.6–16.2M. Affordability pressure — high prices plus elevated financing rates — is pushing consumers toward used vehicles and lengthening ownership to a record 12.8-year average age, a durable tailwind for the P&S annuity that partially offsets front-end weakness.

Franchise laws remain a protective moat, though EV direct-to-consumer carve-outs are a slow-moving structural threat to the model over the long term. Asbury's clearest differentiator versus peers is the vertically integrated TCA F&I platform, which should lift F&I profitability once fully rolled out across the enlarged footprint.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving or deteriorating. The scale advantages are real, and the active portfolio pruning (24 franchises divested in 2025 for $566.5M, 14 more in Q1'26 for $361.5M) is reshaping the footprint toward higher-quality assets. But the 5-year TSR of 160 (2020=100) materially lags the peer group's 228 — Asbury has underperformed peer auto retailers even as executive pay rose. That relative underperformance is the honest measure of competitive execution over the cycle, and it caps the competitive-position score at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced center on execution and one enforcement matter:

1. **New-vehicle GPU normalization** — the dominant near-term earnings headwind. Margins are reverting toward pre-COVID levels; the endpoint and pace are uncertain, and the market may be under- or over-discounting the terminal GPU.

2. **FTC enforcement action** — on 8/16/2024 the FTC filed an administrative enforcement action alleging FTC Act Section 5 and ECOA violations tied to F&I add-on sales at three David McDavid dealerships. Asbury counter-sued the FTC (ND Texas, 10/4/2024) on constitutional grounds. Both are pending with no loss estimate. This strikes at a high-margin profit center (F&I/TCA) and carries industry-wide practice risk. It is unquantified and therefore not fully priced.

3. **Triple integration strain** — simultaneously integrating Herb Chambers, rolling out TCA, and migrating from CDK to Tekion is the single biggest controllable risk. The Q1 SG&A spike and the 2024 material weakness (ICFR deemed not effective at a third-party DMS vendor supporting acquired Koons stores, remediated during 2025) both trace to integration/systems strain. The remediated material weakness warrants monitoring during ongoing integration.

4. **Leverage at the top of range (3.2x) with a 0.95 current ratio** — combined with heavy buybacks, this leaves less cushion if margin normalization overshoots or a consumer recession arrives. The world-model backdrop of a plausible H2 consumer cliff (housing wealth effect, mid-market spending deceleration) is directly relevant to a discretionary big-ticket retailer.

5. **Tariffs** — 25% auto/parts tariffs under Section 232/301 persist (the February 2026 SCOTUS ruling struck down IEEPA-based tariffs, with refunds processing), pressuring vehicle prices and affordability.

6. **CEO transition** during the triple transformation amplifies execution risk.

7. **Structural** — OEM dependence, recurring franchise-rights impairments ($141M in 2025, $149.5M in 2024, $117.2M in 2023), and the long-term EV direct-sales threat to the franchise model.

We score risk factors 5.0. The FTC matter and the integration/leverage combination are genuine, partly unpriced downside vectors, but the FCF generation and buyback support provide asymmetric cushion on the low end.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 44.0% is rich against 32.1% one-year historical vol (an ~11.9pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.0% near-term vs 37.5% at six months), which typically signals the market pricing imminent event stress — likely the next earnings print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.1%. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (-0.4% near-term), though it steepens to -5.2% at six months, indicating some demand for longer-dated downside protection. ATM IV has collapsed from 62.3% to 44.0% since mid-June as the stock rose 15%, so a meaningful chunk of prior event stress has already unwound. Short interest at 10.9% of float (4.8 days to cover) reflects modest institutional bearish positioning, consistent with the deceleration thesis but not at squeeze-risk levels. The options picture confirms the fundamental read: elevated near-term uncertainty around the operating trajectory, no strong directional conviction either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The market is over-discounting a normalization that is largely behind us. New-vehicle GPU is reverting to a new steady state, but the counter-cyclical P&S annuity is growing on a record vehicle age, used GPU is improving, and TCA full rollout in 2026 lifts long-run F&I profitability that reported numbers currently understate on timing. SG&A leverage recovers as Tekion migration completes and integration costs roll off, restoring the ~63% SG&A/gross-profit ratio. Management's open-market buying signals conviction that ~$180–205 was a floor. On ~$26–29 of forward EPS and a re-rating toward GPI's 12.5x, the stock reaches the high-$200s to low-$300s. Bull target ~$300, aligned with the top-end analyst target of $312.

### Bear Case

GPU normalization overshoots to the downside, a consumer cliff cuts big-ticket demand, and SG&A leverage fails to recover as the triple integration drags into 2027. Divestiture gains stop flattering headline earnings, exposing an operating base earning materially below the ~$25 trailing EPS. Leverage at 3.2x with a 0.95 current ratio and $1.97B of variable-rate debt amplifies the earnings hit in a higher-for-longer environment. The FTC matter escalates into a quantified liability and pressures industry F&I practices. On normalized earnings of ~$20 and a multiple that stays at ~8–9x, the stock revisits the 52-week low near $172, roughly 23% below current levels. Bear target ~$172, near the analyst low of $212 discounted for a fundamental miss.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is balanced with no dated catalyst that tilts the risk/reward decisively.

**Reassessment timeline**: Next quarterly earnings (roughly late July 2026) is the key checkpoint, with the SG&A/gross-profit ratio and same-store new-vehicle GPU as the two decisive KPIs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SG&A as a percentage of gross profit recovers by 300bp+ toward the low-60s in the next two prints, evidencing that integration/DMS costs are rolling off and operating leverage is returning.
- Same-store new-vehicle GPU stabilizes (sequential flat-to-up), signaling the normalization has found its floor above bear expectations.
- A positive turn in estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising rather than falling), combined with continued open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continued SG&A deterioration or a further same-store GPU leg down of >10%, indicating structural rather than transitional margin erosion.
- FTC matter escalates to a quantified material liability or a broader industry F&I-practice ruling.
- Net leverage rises above the 3.5x target ceiling while the company continues buybacks, signaling capital-allocation stress.
- Evidence of a consumer demand cliff (SAAR falling meaningfully below 15M with volume declines flowing through same-store units).

**Base case**: ~$225, roughly flat from current levels, reflecting ~8.5–9x forward EPS of ~$25.75 with modest multiple support offset by falling estimates and integration overhang. Upside to the bull case (~$300) is ~35%; downside to the bear case (~$172) is ~23%.

## Conclusion

Asbury is a scale-advantaged, cash-generative franchised auto retailer trading at a genuine discount to its closest peer, with management demonstrating alignment through open-market purchases. The offsetting reality is that headline earnings are inflated by divestiture gains, the operating business is decelerating (Q1'26 operating income -17%, SG&A/gross-profit +700bp), estimates are falling across every window, leverage sits at the top of range, and a triple integration plus a CEO transition raises execution risk. The low P/E is real but is applied to a distorted, declining earnings base, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating.

The score trajectory has declined steadily (5.8 → 5.2 → 5.25), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend; this is the latest in a series of HOLDs, and the operating deceleration justifies not upgrading. We hold at an overall 5.25. The stock is worth owning for patient investors who believe the margin normalization is over-discounted, but the honest classification is uncertain: the near-term operating direction is down, the valuation support is real, and the two roughly cancel. We would turn constructive on evidence of SG&A leverage recovery and GPU stabilization, and turn cautious on continued cost deterioration or an FTC escalation.
