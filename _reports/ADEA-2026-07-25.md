**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Adeia is a pure-play intellectual-property licensing platform that monetizes a portfolio of roughly 13,750 media and semiconductor patents against the world's largest media, consumer-electronics, and semiconductor companies. The business is asset-light in the extreme (about 150 employees, capex near $1.8M a year, and operating margins around 40%), with revenue arriving as per-subscriber, per-unit, and fixed license fees, punctuated by lumpy non-recurring settlements when litigation resolves. The sector tag ("IT Consulting & Other Services") is misleading; this is a royalty business that should be valued as one: high-margin, cash-generative, and structurally lumpy because settlements drive the top-line swings.

The central tension is a mix shift. FY2025 revenue grew 18% to $443.4M and Q1 2026 revenue grew 19.5% to $104.8M, but that headline growth masks two opposing currents. Recurring revenue, the annuity base tied to Pay-TV subscribers, fell 21.4% YoY in Q1 2026 as cord-cutting shrinks MVPD bases and anchor licensees (DISH, DIRECTV) go unlicensed and litigate. Offsetting that, semiconductor revenue jumped from roughly $3.7M to $33.5M in a single quarter on new licenses signed with AMD, Microsoft, and another chipmaker. The AMD settlement in March 2026 (WDTX cases dismissed with prejudice, ITC case terminated) is the single most important development: it validates the hybrid-bonding/advanced-packaging patent thesis against a marquee AI-chip customer, and it is the growth story that a static data pull would miss.

We rate ADEA a HOLD. The stock has re-rated from roughly $13 in mid-2025 to a $34.34 52-week high before pulling back to ~$26, and near-term estimate revisions are negative (current quarter -13.1% over 30 days). The valuation is neither cheap nor stretched: ~24.7x trailing earnings on lumpy, litigation-dependent profits, above the depressed peer median but justified by superior margins and the semiconductor optionality. The semiconductor growth is real and de-risking, but the recurring Pay-TV base is eroding while its largest customers turn adversarial, and 55.7% of FY2025 revenue came from five customers. This is a two-sided setup pending the August 3 Q2 print.

## Company Overview

Adeia is the successor to the Rovi/TiVo/Xperi lineage, retained as "Adeia Inc." when Xperi Holding spun off its product business (Xperi Inc.) in a tax-free separation on October 1, 2022. It operates one reportable segment (IP Licensing) and monetizes patents whose last expiry runs to March 2045. About 80% of the portfolio is internally developed.

Revenue splits into two verticals. **Media** (the larger base) licenses MVPDs and vMVPDs (Comcast/Sky, Charter, Cox, DISH, Verizon, YouTube TV), OTT/streaming platforms (Amazon, Disney+, Hulu, Peacock, Starz, DAZN), and CE makers (Samsung, LG, Roku, TCL, Canon). **Semiconductor** (the growth engine) licenses hybrid bonding / Direct Bond Interconnect (DBI), advanced packaging, and emerging technologies such as RapidCool direct-to-chip liquid cooling, to Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix, Sony, Kioxia, SanDisk, UMC, and now AMD.

The moat is patent exclusivity: competitors cannot license the same inventions, so the operative risk is not competition but renewal, enforcement, and validity. Adeia's business model is a continuous, coordinated global enforcement campaign in which settlements are the revenue events and new lawsuits both signal future revenue and disrupt the recurring base as customers go unlicensed.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a record year.** Revenue reached $443.4M, up 18% from $376.0M. Recurring revenue was $351.3M (+3%), while non-recurring revenue surged 166% to $92.0M, driven by the Q4 2025 Disney settlement and long-term license (partly offset against the prior-year Amazon deal recognized in Q4 2024). Operating income was $175.0M at a 39.5% margin; net income was $111.1M at a 25.1% margin, producing ROE of 23.1%. Operating cash flow was $158.1M (down 26% from $212.5M in 2024 on working-capital timing rather than operating deterioration), and free cash flow was $156.3M, a 35.2% FCF margin. On the ~$2.87B market cap, that is roughly a 5.4% FCF yield.

The balance sheet carries cash and securities of $136.7M against Term Loan B of $426.7M (reduced by $60.4M in principal payments during the year). Current ratio was 3.81 and debt/equity 1.16. The Altman Z-score of 2.4 sits in the grey zone, driven by leverage rather than operating weakness; interest coverage and cash generation are healthy. Two Term Loan B repricings in 2025 (down to SOFR+2.50%) cut interest expense 23% YoY.

**Q1 2026 crystallized the mix shift.** Revenue was $104.8M (+19.5% YoY) with net income of $22.8M and diluted EPS of $0.20. Recurring revenue fell to $66.3M on Pay-TV royalty declines, while non-recurring revenue rose roughly 10x to $38.5M. Semiconductor revenue jumped to $33.5M from $3.7M on the new AMD, Microsoft, and additional semiconductor licenses. Debt was reduced further to $398.6M (~7.3% floating rate), and cash plus securities stood at $115.8M. Remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue) were $437.2M, giving multi-year visibility ($85M remaining 2026, $100M 2027, $90M 2028).

Capital allocation prioritizes deleveraging ahead of the Term Loan B maturity in June 2028 (~$356M balloon). The dividend is $0.05/quarter (~$22M/yr, held flat since 2020), the yield a token ~0.7%. Buybacks run ~$20M/yr with $150M of authorization remaining, though the average repurchase price rose from $13.55 in 2025 to $22.45 in Q1 2026: management is buying at roughly double last year's price as the stock re-rated, which is less efficient capital deployment than the prior year.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: 2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 beat by 29.6%, Q3 2025 missed by 23.5%). The variance reflects the settlement-driven lumpiness inherent to the model, which argues for lower confidence in any single-quarter estimate.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth thesis hinges on a single question: can rising semiconductor licensing (plus OTT penetration) outrun declining Pay-TV recurring revenue? Q1 2026 offered the first clear datapoint that it can at the headline level (semi added roughly $30M while recurring fell about $18M), but the recurring decline is structural, not transient.

The semiconductor vertical is the durable positive. As Moore's Law slows, 2.5D/3D heterogeneous integration and advanced packaging are becoming central to AI compute, which is exactly where Adeia's foundational hybrid-bonding/DBI patents sit. The AMD settlement is proof that leading AI-chip players need this IP and will pay after litigation. Semi went from ~4% of Q1'25 revenue to ~32% of Q1'26 revenue. This aligns with the broad, multi-source confirmation of AI-infrastructure demand at the packaging and compute layers, which raises the durability of this leg relative to a single-company story.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and, on balance, cautionary for the near term. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 13.1% over 30 days and next-quarter estimates cut 13.9% over 90 days, signaling that consensus is pulling in near-term expectations, likely reflecting the recurring Pay-TV erosion and the timing uncertainty of new settlements. Against that, current-year estimates are roughly flat (+0.5% over 90 days) and next-year estimates edged up (+2.8% over 90 days). This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a high-conviction inflection buy; it is a business where the growth engine (semi) is real but the annuity base (media) is leaking, and consensus is uncertain about the net.

The $437.2M RPO provides a floor of visibility, but a meaningful portion of forward growth still depends on converting active disputes (DISH, DIRECTV, Fubo, Bell Canada/Telus) into signed licenses, an inherently uncertain, non-scheduled process.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$26, ADEA trades at ~24.7x trailing earnings and roughly 18x the $1.42 current-year consensus EPS / ~16.8x the $1.55 next-year estimate. On free cash flow, the ~5.4% FCF yield is reasonable for a high-margin licensor with modest but real net growth.

Peer comparison requires care because the sector tag is wrong. Nominal IT-services peers (KD 13.3x, TDC 7.5x, EPAM 13.3x, VYX 23.8x, DXC 88.2x on distressed earnings; median 13.3x) are not clean comparables; Adeia's 40% operating margins, asset-light model, and 35% FCF margin justify a premium to services businesses. A patent-licensing peer set is the more appropriate frame, and against that the multiple is mid-range rather than stretched. [unverified — no licensing-peer multiples in the provided data]

The valuation is two-sided. The bull anchor: analyst targets range from $30 low to $43 high (mean $37, median $37.5), all above the current price, implying ~15%–65% upside if the semiconductor licensing pipeline continues to convert. The bear anchor: earnings are lumpy and litigation-dependent, near-term estimates are being cut, and the recurring base — the part of the business that deserves the highest multiple because it is the annuity — is shrinking 21% YoY. A "cheap" screen would be a value-trap warning here rather than a buy signal; the multiple is supported by quality and the semi catalyst, not by an obvious mispricing. We do not treat the analyst targets as firm floors because the stock is not screening cheap relative to a clear, accelerating estimate ramp; the near-term revisions are negative, which weakens the case that consensus targets are stale to the upside.

Net, valuation is fair rather than compelling: enough upside if settlements keep landing to avoid an AVOID, not enough asymmetric mispricing to justify a BUY at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Adeia does not compete for licensees in the traditional sense; patent exclusivity means its "competition" is really the willingness of large tech, media, and semiconductor companies to pay versus litigate. Two secular forces define the trajectory.

The headwind is Pay-TV/MVPD subscriber erosion, which structurally shrinks the recurring media base, compounded by cash-strapped and consolidating Pay-TV players (DISH/EchoStar, DIRECTV) increasingly resisting and litigating rather than renewing. DISH became unlicensed on April 1, 2026; DIRECTV filed a declaratory-judgment action in December 2025. These are the highest-value legacy relationships turning adversarial.

The tailwind is the semiconductor/AI-packaging portfolio, where the competitive position is improving. RapidCool adds thermal-management optionality as liquid cooling scales in data centers. This is a structurally advantaged position in a growing end market.

The competitive-position trajectory has weakened in our sequencing (from 7.5 to 5.8 across prior reports), and the current data supports a stable-but-mixed read rather than a clear improvement: the semi vertical is strengthening while the media annuity deteriorates and its anchor customers litigate. A market leader losing its recurring base offsets a challenger gaining share in semiconductors, netting to roughly neutral trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** Five customers accounted for 55.7% of FY2025 revenue and one customer represented 68% of accounts receivable. Loss of or dispute with any anchor is material and not fully priced.

**Pay-TV secular decline plus licensee disputes.** The recurring base is eroding as cord-cutting shrinks MVPD subscribers, and key Pay-TV customers are going unlicensed and litigating. This is the single most under-appreciated negative because headline growth masks the recurring decline.

**Litigation-dependent revenue.** Revenue lumpiness is structural; large non-recurring payments come from settlements. Litigation expense was $24.7M in 2025 (+81% YoY). Patents can be invalidated via inter partes review, which historically carries a high invalidation rate; both Disney and AMD filed IPRs before settling. The Fubo suit (July 1, 2026) pressured the stock, and the market reacts negatively to new suits and positively to settlements.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** ~$399M of variable-rate debt means a 1% rate move is roughly $4.2M of interest. If rates stay elevated, refinancing the June 2028 balloon is a live consideration, though deleveraging is on track.

**Portfolio replenishment and MFN clauses.** Patents expire and must be continually reinvented or acquired; fully paid-up semiconductor licenses can convert to royalty-free. Most-favored-nation clauses in some contracts limit pricing flexibility.

**Governance.** Two Item 5.02 (officer/director change) 8-K filings in January and May 2026 warrant monitoring; the underlying detail was not fully disclosed. The IRS closed the 2022 spin-off audit with no change, removing one tail risk.

Netting these, the risk score is below neutral: concentration plus adversarial anchor customers plus binary litigation outcomes are specific, unpriced downside catalysts, only partly offset by the de-risked spin-off tax audit and the visible RPO.

Near-term ATM IV is 99.0% versus 63.0% one-year historical volatility (richly bid, at the 100th percentile of the current chain) with a backwardated term structure (99.0% near vs 75.0% at six months) that signals the market expects a near-term event, almost certainly the August 3 Q2 print. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The semiconductor/AI-packaging vertical is inflecting durably. Marquee AI-chip players will license Adeia's hybrid-bonding IP, and the pipeline (Microsoft, RPX, and active disputes with DISH, DIRECTV, Fubo) converts into new licenses that more than offset Pay-TV decline. Semi revenue continues scaling toward a larger share of the mix, the recurring base stabilizes as OTT penetration deepens, deleveraging continues toward the 2028 maturity, and the multiple re-rates toward the licensing-peer cohort. Analyst targets of $37–43 imply 42%–65% upside. Bull target: $40.

**Bear Case.** Recurring revenue continues to erode faster than semi can backfill as DISH, DIRECTV, and Fubo remain unlicensed and litigation drags. Non-recurring settlements prove lumpy and fail to arrive on schedule, near-term estimate cuts extend, and a patent-invalidation or adverse ruling impairs a key portfolio. The 55.7% customer concentration bites, and the stock derates toward the low end of its range. Bear target: $18, roughly 31% below the current price.

**Base Case.** Semi growth roughly offsets recurring decline, headline revenue grows modestly, earnings stay lumpy but trend up on the RPO and the AMD-validated pipeline, and the multiple holds. Base target: $30, applying ~19x the $1.55 next-year EPS estimate plus modest settlement optionality, consistent with the low end of the analyst range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 3, 2026 Q2 print is the immediate catalyst. It will show whether semiconductor revenue holds above the ~$30M Q1 level, whether the recurring decline stabilizes or accelerates, and whether new settlements (DISH, DIRECTV, Fubo, Bell Canada/Telus) are landing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Semiconductor revenue sustains or grows above the Q1'26 ~$33.5M level for two consecutive quarters, confirming the vertical is a durable engine rather than a one-time AMD catch-up.
- Recurring revenue decline moderates to low-single-digit YoY (from -21%), or a DISH/DIRECTV settlement re-licenses an anchor customer.
- Near-term estimate revisions turn positive, or the stock pulls back toward the low-$20s while the semi pipeline remains intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material patent invalidation (IPR ruling) or adverse court decision impairing a core portfolio.
- Recurring revenue decline accelerates beyond -21% while semi growth stalls, indicating the mix shift is failing to offset the annuity erosion.
- Loss of a top-five customer without replacement, or a Term Loan B refinancing complication as the 2028 maturity approaches.

Given the rich near-term IV and backwardation, patient investors should wait for the post-earnings volatility crush before adjusting exposure.

## Conclusion

Adeia is a high-quality, cash-generative licensing platform navigating a genuine transition: a strengthening semiconductor/AI-packaging franchise validated by the AMD settlement, set against a shrinking Pay-TV recurring base whose anchor customers are turning adversarial. The FY2025 record year and the Q1 2026 mix shift show the semi engine can offset media decline at the headline level, but the recurring erosion, customer concentration, and litigation-driven lumpiness keep the risk profile elevated and the earnings path uncertain.

At ~$26, having round-tripped from a $34.34 high, the valuation is fair rather than compelling: ~17x next-year EPS on a business with 35% FCF margins, but with negative near-term estimate revisions and no obvious mispricing to exploit. The score trajectory has declined (6.6 → 6.3 → 5.7), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that easing: the semi catalyst is real, but so is the recurring deterioration and the near-term estimate cutting. We maintain HOLD; the semiconductor thesis needs one or two more quarters of confirmation, and the recurring base needs to stabilize, before the risk/reward justifies a stronger call. The August 3 print is the decisive near-term test.
