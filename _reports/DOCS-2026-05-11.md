## Executive Summary

Doximity is the dominant professional network for U.S. physicians, with more than 85% penetration of U.S. physicians across all specialties and 90%+ of graduating medical students. The company generates 90% gross margins, 55% EBITDA margins, and $267M in annual free cash flow — exceptional unit economics enabled by a software-only model with minimal capex requirements. The stock has declined 66% from its 52-week high of $76.51 to $25.98, driven by three converging headwinds: pharma customer budget delays related to White House MFN pricing agreements, a CFO departure during medical leave, and market concern that AI tools could disintermediate Doximity's pharma marketing platform.

At approximately $4.8B market cap with ~$735M in net cash, the stock trades at approximately 11.4x EV/Adjusted EBITDA and ~18x FCF — valuations that reflect meaningful skepticism about growth durability for a business of this quality. Management has stated that January 2026 bookings were the strongest since IPO, net revenue retention remains 112%+ (117% for top-20 customers), and the company's AI clinical products (300,000+ unique prescribers) represent an entirely new revenue stream not yet included in guidance. The 4% Q4 growth rate may reflect budget timing rather than structural demand destruction, and management expects to exit CY2026 as a double-digit grower.

The combination of fortress balance sheet, monopolistic physician network, aggressive buybacks at depressed prices ($197M in Q3 alone, $500M new authorization), and an AI monetization catalyst expected in 2H 2026 creates attractive upside from current levels. The CFO vacancy and 11.5% short interest introduce execution uncertainty, but the valuation compensates for these risks.

## Company Overview

Doximity operates the largest digital platform for U.S. medical professionals, with over two million registered members as of FY2025, including more than 85% of U.S. physicians. The platform provides clinical workflow tools (telehealth via "Dialer," HIPAA-compliant fax, secure messaging, AI writing assistant, scheduling) and monetizes physician engagement primarily through pharmaceutical marketing solutions and health system hiring/enterprise products.

Revenue composition is heavily weighted toward Marketing Solutions, where pharmaceutical manufacturers pay subscription fees to deliver targeted content to specific physician audiences. The top 116 customers (spending >$500K annually) account for 84% of total revenue. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but the concentration among top-20 pharma companies creates meaningful budget-cycle dependency.

The dual-class share structure gives insiders approximately 76-80% voting control through Class B shares (10 votes per share), effectively preventing hostile takeovers or activist campaigns.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Headwinds (Q4 FY2026: 4% guided growth):**
The sharp deceleration to 4% growth in Q4 coincides with pharma budget timing disruption. Multiple major pharma companies signed MFN pricing agreements with the White House (14 agreements by December 19, 2025, growing to 17 by early 2026), creating budget uncertainty and delayed deal signings. Management has stated that January bookings were "the best since going public," though this claim from earnings call commentary could not be independently verified in SEC filings.

**Medium-Term Growth Vectors:**
1. **Pharma budget release** — Budget uncertainty may be temporary; pharmaceutical marketing spend is relatively inelastic to macroeconomic cycles. Management expects double-digit growth by CY2026 exit.
2. **AI monetization** — 300,000+ prescribers using AI products with zero revenue contribution in current guidance. Commercial launch expected later in 2026. The "search" TAM (55% of digital healthcare marketing spend per management) represents an entirely new market Doximity has never participated in.
3. **Multi-module bundling (DocDynamic)** — Management has indicated this is driving increasing share of bookings, reflecting successful cross-selling of multiple products to customers.
4. **International expansion** — Currently U.S.-only, though not emphasized as near-term priority.

The organic ceiling for Marketing Solutions depends on the overall digital healthcare advertising market growth rate. Doximity has consistently taken share given its monopolistic physician audience, but sustained double-digit growth requires either (a) continued share gains, (b) AI monetization, or (c) new product penetration.

## Competitive Landscape

**Monopolistic Physician Network:** With 85%+ of U.S. physicians as registered members, Doximity possesses the largest verified physician audience available for commercial targeting. This creates a two-sided network effect: pharma companies must use Doximity to reach physicians at scale, and physicians remain because peers are there and clinical tools are embedded in daily workflow.

**Marketing Solutions:** The primary competitor (Medscape/WebMD) lacks the identity-verified, engagement-rich data Doximity possesses. A median 10:1 ROI documented across 965 studies provides a quantifiable value proposition that generic digital advertising cannot match.

**Telehealth:** Management has indicated Doximity's telehealth product is highly ranked among health system decision-makers, with significant daily visit volumes demonstrating infrastructure resilience and deep clinical embedding.

**AI/Clinical Decision Support:** Competes with established players like UpToDate (Wolters Kluwer) and emerging AI-native tools. Doximity is differentiating via physician expert reviewers validating AI responses, integrated drug reference, and full PDF access to medical journals. Management has reported favorable results in head-to-head comparisons with competing products.

The AI investment deepens physician workflow embedding, multi-module bundling increases switching costs, and the network effect strengthens as more clinical tools aggregate on a single platform.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Pharma Budget Concentration (HIGH):** Top-20 pharma companies drive a disproportionate share of revenue. MFN pricing deals, tariff uncertainty, and potential DTC advertising restrictions create recurring budget-cycle volatility. One structural pharma advertising downturn could meaningfully impair revenue.

2. **Growth Deceleration to Structural Low-Single-Digits (MEDIUM-HIGH):** If the digital healthcare ad market is growing in the mid-single-digits and AI monetization fails to materialize on schedule, Doximity's growth could settle at 5-8% permanently. The exit to "double-digit growth" by late CY2026 is management's stated expectation without guarantee. The Q4 deceleration may reflect timing disruption, but the causal link to MFN budget delays specifically (versus broader demand normalization) is not conclusively established in public filings.

3. **CFO Vacancy (MEDIUM):** Anna Bryson's resignation in April 2026 leaves a leadership gap during strategic transition. Interim arrangements with a board member stepping in are functional but not permanent. Prolonged vacancy could signal organizational issues.

4. **AI Monetization Execution Risk (MEDIUM):** The company is investing in AI infrastructure (non-GAAP gross margin declining from 93% to 91%) with zero current revenue contribution. If AI monetization falls short, the investment becomes a pure cost drag without growth offset.

5. **Dual-Class Structure (LOW-MEDIUM):** ~76-80% voting control by insiders prevents market-based governance mechanisms. While this means management could theoretically pursue a take-private, it also reduces governance optionality for minority shareholders who have limited influence over strategic direction.

6. **Short Interest (MEDIUM):** 11.5% of float short with 5.3 days to cover indicates sustained institutional bearish positioning. This reflects genuine skepticism about the growth trajectory.

### Options Market Signal

## Conclusion

Doximity presents attractive risk/reward at current levels. The 66% drawdown has created a valuation supported by: (a) ~$735M net cash (~15% of market cap), (b) $270M annual FCF with 47% margins, (c) aggressive buybacks at depressed prices, and (d) a monopolistic physician network with 85%+ penetration that cannot be replicated.

The near-term uncertainty is real — pharma budget timing, CFO vacancy, and AI disruption perception are legitimate concerns. But the market is pricing significant permanent impairment into a business that generated $267M of free cash flow last year, has 90% gross margins, negligible debt, and management-reported January bookings strength. The "going private" question from Goldman's analyst on the earnings call reflects how cheap this has become relative to the quality of the underlying business.

At ~11.4x EV/EBITDA for a 90% gross margin, monopolistic-position software business with AI optionality, the asymmetry favors buyers with a 12-month horizon. The valuation represents a meaningful discount to intrinsic value if growth reaccelerates toward double-digits. Position sizing should reflect the elevated near-term volatility and the binary nature of upcoming catalysts (Q4 earnings, AI launch timing, permanent CFO appointment).

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% portfolio allocation. The combination of fortress balance sheet, attractive valuation relative to quality, and identifiable re-acceleration catalysts within 6-12 months meets the threshold for conviction purchase despite near-term uncertainty.
