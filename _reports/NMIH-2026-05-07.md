## Executive Summary

NMI Holdings is the youngest and smallest of six U.S. private mortgage insurers by primary insurance-in-force, operating a toll-booth business on high-LTV residential lending with only 225 employees managing a $222 billion insurance-in-force portfolio. The company generates 55% net margins, 58% FCF margins, and 15% ROE while trading at 7.5x forward earnings and 1.10x book value — a persistent valuation discount driven by cyclical concerns about credit normalization and existential GSE reform risk.

The core investment case rests on three pillars: (1) book value compounding at 17% CAGR with aggressive buybacks reducing share count 3% annually, (2) massive excess capital ($1.44B above PMIERs requirements as of year-end 2025) providing both growth optionality and stress cushion, and (3) a valuation supported by tangible book value per share approaching the current stock price. The primary risks — claims severity normalization (55% → 76% over three years) and the 2022 vintage's elevated loss ratio (17.0% ITD as of Q1 2026) — represent genuine headwinds but are quantifiable and manageable given the capital buffer. At 7.5x forward earnings with consensus targets ranging $42-49, the risk/reward skews favorably for a 12-month holding period.

## Company Overview

NMI Holdings provides private mortgage insurance through its subsidiary National Mortgage Insurance Corporation, protecting lenders and investors against default-related losses on residential mortgages with loan-to-value ratios exceeding 80%. The company's existence is mandated by GSE charter requirements that high-LTV loans carry credit enhancement — making NMIH a quasi-regulatory beneficiary.

Key operating characteristics: 684,058 policies in force, $59.3B primary risk-in-force, 2,193 master policy relationships, 93% monthly premium policies (recurring revenue), weighted average FICO >760 for 50% of the portfolio, and 98% fixed-rate mortgages insured. The company's proprietary Rate GPS pricing platform enables granular, loan-level risk pricing, though risk-based pricing approaches are now broadly utilized across the private MI industry.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 8-10% is driven primarily by: (1) portfolio growth as new insurance written (NIW) exceeds runoff, (2) premium rate sustainability at current levels, and (3) modest improvement in persistency. Management guided for 2026 market volume "similar to 2025," implying NIW in the $45-50B range.

Growth constraints are real but quantifiable. Origination volumes remain depressed by affordability (mortgage rates ~6.5%), limiting NIW expansion. Persistency at 82-84% benefits from rate lock-in (most insured borrowers hold rates below current market levels), but a meaningful rate decline would trigger refinancing waves and accelerate portfolio runoff. The longer-term persistency trend has been declining: 86.1% in 2023, 84.6% in 2024, 83.4% in 2025, and 82.2% at March 31, 2026.

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story: FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.14 is up 0.7% over 90 days but essentially flat over 30 days (+0.1%). FY2027 estimates of $5.47 have declined 1.1% over 90 days. This is a steady compounder with modest positive drift in near-term expectations and slight deterioration in outer-year estimates.

The annual EPS growth trajectory from $4.92 (FY2025) to $5.14 (FY2026E) to $5.47 (FY2027E) implies 4.5% and 6.4% growth, respectively. Combined with ~3% annual share count reduction from buybacks, per-share economics improve 7-9% annually without margin expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

NMIH is the smallest by primary insurance-in-force among the six private mortgage insurers competing for an estimated ~$300B annual NIW market, with approximately 15-16% share based on aggregate peer NIW figures. Its competitive position is stable rather than rapidly expanding: master policy count grew from 1,974 to 2,193 over two years, indicating steady relationship additions but not a step-change in market penetration.

**Advantages:** Rate GPS proprietary pricing enables granular risk selection, evidenced by portfolio quality (50% FICO >760). The company emphasizes individual loan-level underwriting and validation as a differentiator. The young portfolio (entirely post-2013) avoids legacy credit issues that previously plagued competitors.

**Vulnerabilities:** Scale disadvantage relative to ESNT and ACT in terms of reinsurance purchasing power and customer diversification. The company's 225-employee model is efficient but creates key-person and operational concentration risks. FHA premium reductions could divert borrowers from private MI to government programs. Basel IV proposals, if implemented unfavorably, could reduce bank demand for MI capital relief. Competitors including Radian also employ sophisticated model-based pricing, loan-level analytics, and AI/ML-driven risk management — NMIH's operational advantages, while real, exist within a competitive landscape that has broadly adopted similar approaches.

## Risk Assessment

**Claims Severity Trajectory (PRIMARY NEAR-TERM RISK):**
Claims severity increased from 55% (FY2023) to 61% (FY2024) to 76% (FY2025). This is the single most important operating metric to monitor. Management attributes the rise to newer vintage defaults with less accumulated home equity. If home prices flatten or decline, severity could approach historical industry norms of 80-100%. The precise sensitivity to further severity increases is difficult to isolate from disclosed data given the multiple factors affecting claims expense (new defaults, case reserve aging, prior-year development, cure activity, and reinsurance), but directionally, continued severity escalation would meaningfully increase incurred losses.

**2022 Vintage Concentration:**
The 2022 book year shows a 17.0% inception-to-date incurred loss ratio as of Q1 2026 (up from 16.6% at year-end 2025) versus 1.3-3.3% for prior vintages. With $40.1B still in force (approximately 18% of total IIF) and a 1.9% current default rate (vs. portfolio average 1.17%), this vintage represents outsized tail risk. Written during peak affordability stress at elevated rates, these borrowers have the least home equity and highest payment burden.

**GSE Reform / Existential Risk:**
NMIH's entire business exists because of GSE charter requirements mandating credit enhancement on high-LTV loans. The FHFA director is now removable at presidential discretion, and active conversations about GSE exit from conservatorship are underway. While MI industry representatives describe Washington discussions as "constructive," any restructuring that eliminated MI requirements would be terminal for the business model. The company itself warns in its risk factors that changes reducing or discontinuing the use of private MI by the GSEs could materially affect the business.

**Rate Sensitivity (Two-Directional):**
Rates falling below ~6% would trigger refinancing waves, accelerating portfolio runoff and reducing persistency from current levels. Rates rising further would suppress origination volumes and NIW. The current 6.5% rate environment is near-optimal for NMIH — high enough to maintain persistency, low enough to permit reasonable origination volumes.

**Recent Earnings Trajectory:**
Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.28 represented a modest beat versus consensus estimates. The pattern of declining net income despite record revenue suggests credit costs are normalizing and margin pressure from claims is partially offsetting top-line growth, warranting modest conservatism in forward assumptions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-52, +26-36%):**
- Housing supply deficit sustains home prices, keeping severity from further escalation
- Persistency remains elevated (>82%) through 2026-2027 as rate lock-in persists
- Buybacks continue at $100M+/year pace, reducing shares 3%+ annually
- Book value reaches $38-40 within 12 months, supporting P/B re-rating toward peer median of 1.3x
- MI industry recognized as permanently essential infrastructure; P/E re-rates from 7.5x to 9-10x

**Bear Case ($30-32, -17-21%):**
- Recession triggers unemployment spike → default rate doubles from 1.2% to 2.5%+
- Home price decline of 10-15% → severity exceeds 100% on newer vintages
- GSE reform eliminates or reduces MI requirements
- Loss ratio expands from 9.6% to 25-30%, cutting net margins to 35-40%
- Stock trades to 0.9x book value ($31) reflecting peak-cycle concerns

## Conclusion

NMIH represents a high-quality, capital-light financial franchise trading at a material discount to both intrinsic value and peer multiples. The 7.5x forward P/E for a 15% ROE business growing book value per share at a double-digit CAGR is compelling, with downside supported by the proximity of tangible book value to the current stock price. The stock's recent 8% decline from its late-April highs on credit normalization concerns creates a reasonable entry point.

The primary risk is credit deterioration in the 2022 vintage and rising claims severity. These are real and quantifiable headwinds, but the $1.44B excess capital buffer and sub-30% combined ratio provide substantial margin of safety. If credit normalizes gradually (base case), the buyback-driven compounding delivers mid-teens total returns; if credit deteriorates sharply (bear case), book value provides a valuation anchor approximately 10% below the current price — though mortgage insurers can trade below book value during periods of acute credit stress.

The rating is BUY with 2-3% position sizing, appropriate for a high-quality financial compounder with identifiable but manageable cyclical risks.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The book-value floor argument is weaker than originally presented: GAAP BVPS growth has been closer to $0.60-$0.90/quarter (not $1.50), and book value is not a hard downside floor for a cyclical credit insurer. The recommendation remains valid on earnings-multiple and capital-return grounds but should not be primarily anchored to book-value convergence.
