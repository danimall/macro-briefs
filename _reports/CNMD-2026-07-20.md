**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CONMED is a mid-cap surgical device company undergoing a self-help operational turnaround whose equity has become a leveraged bet on two variables: whether the manufacturing-optimization and portfolio-pruning program continues to convert into margin recovery, and whether management refinances an $800M convertible note maturing June 15, 2027 without materially impairing per-share earnings power. The stock has fallen ~62% on a total-return basis versus a peer group that roughly tripled over the same period, and now trades at ~24x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.51, a figure that badly misstates underlying economics.

The headline GAAP earnings collapse in 2025 (net income $132M → $47M, net margin 10.1% → 3.4%) is almost entirely optical. Roughly $64M of the operating-income decline is a non-cash swing in contingent-consideration fair value ($23M expense in 2025 versus a $41M gain in 2024), layered on ~$12.2M of former-CEO advisory payments, ~$12.9M of consulting fees, and a ~$22.2M inventory/tooling/patent write-off from product-line rationalization. Strip these out and revenue grew 5.2% and the company's own adjusted diluted EPS rose to a record $4.59 (from $4.17 in 2024). Operating cash flow was $170.7M with FCF near $151M (~11% margin). The business itself is not deteriorating; the reported numbers were run through a kitchen-sink year.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the convertible note. At a conversion price of ~$145.33 against a ~$43 stock, the $800M convert cannot convert to equity and must be refinanced in cash. The current blended debt cost near 2.6–2.8% is a direct function of that cheap coupon; refinancing $800M at prevailing rates of ~5%+ adds materially to interest expense and pressures the EPS recovery the turnaround is supposed to deliver. A minimum-liquidity covenant activates 91 days before maturity. Management is buying back stock aggressively and has suspended the dividend, consuming liquidity ahead of the maturity, which signals conviction in undervaluation but reduces the cushion. Until the refinancing is done on visible terms, the equity carries a binary overhang that neither the operational improvement nor the depressed multiple offsets.

## Company Overview

CONMED (HQ Largo, FL; ~3,900 employees; manufacturing in the U.S. and Mexico) designs and sells devices and equipment for surgical procedures, reported as a single operating segment with two product lines:

- **General Surgery (~58% of sales, ~$800M)** — the growth engine, anchored by AirSeal (a valveless insufflation system for minimally invasive and robotic surgery and a genuine point of differentiation), Buffalo Filter smoke evacuation, electrosurgical energy devices, and endomechanical instruments. Competes against Medtronic, J&J/Ethicon, Stryker, Olympus, and Applied Medical.
- **Orthopedic Surgery (~42% of sales, ~$575M)** — sports medicine (BioBrace, Y-Knot), lower-extremity implants (CoLink), Hall powered instruments, and MTF allograft service fees. Competes against Smith & Nephew, Arthrex, Stryker, J&J/DePuy, Zimmer Biomet, Globus, and Treace.

The business model is high quality at the unit level: ~85–86% of revenue is recurring single-use product, with capital equipment (~14%) pulling through consumable sales. International is ~44% of revenue. No customer exceeds 10% of sales. This is a durable razor/razor-blade franchise; the equity's problems sit in the capital structure and in execution risk, not in the underlying asset.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** is steady mid-single-digit: $1,045M (2022) → $1,245M (2023) → $1,307M (2024) → $1,375M (2025), +5.2% in 2025 (+5.1% constant currency), led by AirSeal, BioBrace, specimen bags, and biliary.

**The 2025 GAAP earnings decline is largely non-recurring.** Operating income fell from $200M to $103M and net income from $132M to $47M, driven by: (1) the ~$64M unfavorable swing in contingent-consideration fair value (Biorez/In2Bones earnouts — non-cash, non-tax-deductible); (2) ~$12.2M cash-plus-stock advisory payments to the former CEO; (3) ~$12.9M consulting and operational-optimization fees; and (4) a ~$22.2M inventory/tooling/patent write-off from product-line rationalization. The effective tax rate jumped to 33.8% from 18.8% partly because the contingent-consideration charge and former-CEO comp are non-deductible.

**Cash generation is healthy and higher-quality than GAAP suggests.** OCF of $170.7M (versus $167.0M in 2024) exceeds net income, on modest ~$20M capex.

**Q1 2026 shows the turnaround taking hold.** Revenue of $317M was down 1.3% reported, but the decline is entirely the deliberate gastroenterology exit (gastro fell to $9.5M from $25.0M YoY); orthopedics grew +6.8% on BioBrace. Gross margin expanded 260 bps to 57.9%, operating margin rose to 8.0% from 5.0%, and net income more than doubled to $13.8M ($0.45 diluted versus $0.19). The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term estimates: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters (most recent quarter +6.5%, prior quarter +5.9%).

**The balance sheet is the constraint.** Total debt is ~$840M (44% of total capital); D/E ~1.25x. Goodwill ($807M) plus other intangibles ($582M) total ~$1.39B, roughly 60% of $2.33B total assets, a consequence of an acquisition-led history. Current ratio is strong at 2.14, but the Altman Z of 2.02 sits in the grey zone, dragged by leverage and thin EBIT/assets. That is not a distress signal given the recurring-revenue base and comfortable liquidity, but it flags the sensitivity of the equity to the refinancing outcome.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is intact and defined: mid-single-digit revenue growth carried by AirSeal, BioBrace, and Buffalo Filter, with orthopedics reaccelerating once the gastro drag is isolated. R&D stepped up to 5.2% of sales in Q1 2026 from a lean ~4% historically, reframed by management as investment into key growth drivers.

The near-term earnings improvement is real but its durability depends on the interest-expense outcome. The margin recovery is the strongest evidence of the self-help program working, and it flows through to the record adjusted EPS. This is not the estimate-revision-led setup that warrants a strong forward tilt: consensus estimates are broadly falling, with current-year EPS revised -0.3% over 90 days and next-year EPS cut -4.2% over 90 days. Falling forward estimates against a genuine operational improvement is what to expect when the market is pricing higher refinancing costs into future EPS. The growth thesis is a margin-and-mix story with a financing offset, not an accelerating-revenue story.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP P/E of ~24x on $1.51 EPS is not a usable metric here, because the trailing period was a kitchen-sink year. On the company's adjusted diluted EPS of $4.59 (2025) and consensus of $4.34 (current year) and $4.72 (next year), the stock at ~$43 trades at roughly 9–10x forward adjusted earnings. That is a low absolute multiple for a franchise generating ~11% FCF margins with an 85%+ recurring base.

The peer table is nearly uninformative for anchoring: same-sector comparables show a median P/E of 85.5 distorted by negative and outlier readings (IART -2.7, ENOV -1.1, STAA -68.3, AORT 85.5), reflecting a heterogeneous group of impaired and loss-making names rather than a clean cohort. The more relevant benchmark is CONMED's own history and the ~62% relative TSR shortfall versus peers (indexed TSR $37.86 versus peer group $126.87 at year-end 2025).

The valuation is genuinely low, but cheapness is not a sufficient buy signal on its own. A compressed multiple that reflects a real, unresolved structural overhang — here, an $800M cash refinancing at higher rates — is a value trap until the catalyst clears. The specific reason the market is discounting the stock is identifiable and rational: refinancing ~$800M from a ~2.25% coupon to ~5%+ could add ~$20–22M of pre-tax annual interest expense, roughly $0.50–0.60 per share of after-tax EPS drag against a ~$4.50 adjusted base. That is a ~12–13% haircut to earnings power, and it is why the low multiple is not obviously mispriced. Analyst price targets (low $36, high $40, mean $39, median $39.5) sit below the current price, signaling the same caution. The asymmetry turns clearly favorable once refinancing terms are known and the drag is bounded. At that point, a re-rating from ~9–10x toward the low-to-mid teens on a stabilized ~$4.00–4.50 EPS base is plausible. Today the risk/reward is roughly balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

CONMED is a smaller, focused player among far larger competitors (Medtronic, J&J, Stryker, Smith & Nephew, Zimmer Biomet, Olympus, Boston Scientific). Its edge is niche differentiation rather than scale: AirSeal in insufflation, Buffalo Filter in smoke evacuation (the broadest portfolio in that category), and BioBrace in soft-tissue augmentation. The recurring single-use base gives real customer stickiness.

Trajectory is mixed. Orthopedics reacceleration and Buffalo Filter's category leadership are positives, and the deliberate gastro exit sharpens the mix toward higher-growth, higher-margin lines. Against that, the 10-K explicitly flags competitors' greater R&D resources and the specific risk of a replacement for AirSeal, CONMED's most differentiated asset. R&D at ~4–5% of sales is below larger peers, a long-term competitiveness question. Industry headwinds (GPO/IDN purchasing leverage, Medicare reimbursement cuts, China volume-based procurement, Italy's device payback law, EU MDR costs) apply across the group and are largely priced. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving, dependent on defending AirSeal and continuing to scale BioBrace.

## Risk Assessment

**The $800M 2.250% convertible notes due June 15, 2027 are the dominant risk and now sit within ~1 year of maturity.** They are deeply out of the money and must be refinanced in cash. Management states it intends to secure incremental financing but explicitly warns there is no assurance of acceptable terms. A minimum-liquidity covenant (≥$75M plus early-maturing principal once >$200M) activates 91 days before maturity. Refinancing at market rates would raise interest expense and pressure EPS, the primary risk to the equity.

**Leadership instability.** A dense cluster of Item 5.02 filings from September 2024 through June 2026 accompanied the CEO transition to Patrick J. Beyer and an unstable CFO seat. This was resolved with John E. Gallagher joining as EVP & CFO (Form 4, 7/15/2026), but simultaneous top-two-seat turnover during a leverage-heavy refinancing year is a governance concern.

**Portfolio and operational execution.** The gastro exit (terminating the W.L. Gore VIABIL distribution deal and selling remaining assets in Q1–April 2026) carries stranded-cost and lost-earnings risk. The manufacturing-optimization program is early; one strong quarter is encouraging but not yet proof.

**Other risks:** ~31% of revenue depends on third-party ethylene-oxide sterilization (facility-closure/regulatory exposure); MTF allograft and distribution-rights dependence; and goodwill/intangible impairment risk given ~60% of assets are intangible against a depressed share price (management concluded no impairment in Q4 2025, but the margin narrows if the stock stays low).

**Cleared overhangs and possible offsets:** The DOJ closed its royalty-payment investigation on 9/5/2025 without prosecution, removing a material overhang. CONMED is pursuing IEEPA tariff-refund claims following a Supreme Court ruling — unbooked, uncertain-timing upside. June 2026 8-Ks (Items 1.01/2.03) reference material agreements and new financial obligations whose bodies could not be retrieved; these plausibly relate to refinancing steps and warrant close follow-up.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 51.2% is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 44.1%, and the term structure is in backwardation (51.2% near-term versus 42.7% at six months), consistent with a specific upcoming event: the July 29, 2026 Q2 print and, over a longer arc, refinancing developments. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±12.2%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.8% near-term), and the six-month tenor shows a positive skew (8.2%), suggesting the market is not aggressively pricing downside further out. ATM IV has fallen sharply from 73.3% in early May to 51.2% as the stock recovered ~19% into mid-July, indicating some resolution of near-term uncertainty, though IV remains elevated. Short interest of 10.0% of float with 4.0 days to cover is moderate — enough to matter but not a crowded short. The signal is consistent with the fundamental read: a specific, dated event risk, not a directional collapse thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$52–58).** The refinancing gets done on manageable terms (or the June 2026 8-Ks already reflect a credit-facility path that caps the drag), removing the fulcrum overhang. Margin recovery continues — gross margin holds near or above 58% and operating margin trends toward low-double-digits — validating the self-help program. BioBrace and AirSeal sustain mid-single-digit-plus revenue growth, orthopedics mix improves post-gastro-exit, and the stock re-rates toward the low-to-mid teens on a stabilized ~$4.00–4.50 adjusted EPS base. Aggressive buybacks at depressed prices amplify per-share recovery. IEEPA tariff refunds and the cleared DOJ matter provide additional tailwind.

**Bear Case (~$28–32).** Refinancing $800M at ~5%+ adds ~$20M+ of annual interest expense, cutting ~$0.50–0.60 from EPS and stalling the recovery just as the operational improvement was gaining traction. Buybacks ahead of the maturity prove to have consumed liquidity that the market later demands back, forcing tighter refinancing terms or additional issuance. A goodwill/intangible impairment is triggered if the stock stays depressed through the annual test. Execution on the manufacturing program falters and margins give back some of the Q1 gain.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The operational improvement is real and the valuation is genuinely low, but the equity's outcome is gated by a binary refinancing event whose terms are not yet visible, and forward estimates are falling. This is a hold pending catalyst resolution rather than a confident income hold (the dividend is suspended) or a confident growth hold (the financing overhang caps upside).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Refinancing of the $800M 2027 convert completed or clearly de-risked, with pro-forma interest expense that limits the EPS drag to under ~$0.40/share, or confirmation that the June 2026 8-K financing arrangements bound the maturity risk.
- Q2 2026 (reported July 29, 2026) confirming gross margin holds ≥57.5% and operating margin ≥8%, with orthopedics growth sustained mid-single-digit-plus and current-year adjusted EPS guidance reaffirmed at ~$4.30+.
- Estimate-revision trend turning positive for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Management guidance or filings indicating refinancing on onerous terms (rate materially above ~6%, or a covenant-heavy structure), or an inability to secure financing as the 91-day liquidity-covenant window approaches in early 2027.
- Gross margin reversing back toward ~55% or operating margin falling below 6%, signaling the self-help program stalled.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a resumption of leadership turnover.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the July 29, 2026 Q2 print for margin durability, followed by any refinancing disclosure through late 2026. The convert-maturity clock (June 15, 2027) sets the hard deadline by which the fulcrum must resolve.

## Conclusion

CONMED is a decent franchise with a good recurring-revenue base and a real, early-stage operational turnaround, priced at a low single-digit-to-low-double-digit forward multiple after severe relative underperformance. The improvement in Q1 2026 margins and the record adjusted EPS demonstrate the self-help program is working, and the DOJ clearance and potential tariff refunds are under-appreciated positives. The trajectory of the scores — financial health and risk factors both improving over prior reports — reflects the same recognition that the 2025 GAAP collapse was optical, not fundamental.

The equity remains a leveraged bet on refinancing an $800M convert that must be repaid in cash at rates well above its 2.25% coupon, an event that could remove ~12–13% of earnings power and that the market is rationally discounting (analyst targets sit below the current price; near-term IV is elevated in backwardation). Cheapness alone does not resolve this; it is the kind of compressed multiple that stays compressed until a structural overhang clears. Until refinancing terms are visible and the margin recovery is confirmed durable, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD, with a clear path to upgrade once the refinancing is de-risked and Q2 confirms the operational trend.
