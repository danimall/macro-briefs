**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AECOM is the world's largest infrastructure consulting and engineering design firm, ranked #1 globally by ENR in water, transportation design, facilities design, and environmental engineering. Over the past several years the firm exited substantially all at-risk self-perform construction and repositioned as an asset-light, fee-based professional-services business with ~51,000 billable employees and no single client above 10% of revenue. FY25 was a strong year operationally: net income attributable to AECOM rose 39.6% to $561.8M on 80bps of gross-margin expansion, free cash flow reached $684.9M, and total backlog hit a record $39.7B. The stock has fallen roughly 38% from its November 2025 high of $135.52 to ~$69, with multiple distinct down-legs around the November and May earnings dates.

The drawdown reflects the market pricing one specific concern. First-half FY26 operating cash flow cratered to $74.0M from $341.7M a year earlier, days sales outstanding rose to 83 from 74, and unbilled claims in contract assets nearly doubled to ~$680M. AECOM is recognizing record GAAP profit but not collecting it, which management attributes to a "longer-than-anticipated claims resolution process." This matters more than usual because the balance sheet is thin by design: stockholders' equity is only $2.49B against $2.74B of debt, retained earnings are negative, and the firm carries a 4.00x leverage covenant. The free-cash-flow engine that funds an aggressive buyback program depends directly on converting those claims to cash.

Against that, the most senior insiders are buying. The CEO, CFO, and President all made open-market purchases at ~$70-71 in May-June 2026, after routine tax-driven sales at ~$97 in December 2025. That is a high-conviction signal that management views the selloff as overdone, and the stock trades at ~11.5x forward EPS versus a peer median near 28x. We resolve the tension as a HOLD with a growth bias rather than a BUY: the appreciation case is real but contingent on a cash-conversion outcome that will not be confirmed for one to two quarters, and the leverage profile leaves little margin if it disappoints.

## Company Overview

AECOM operates three reportable segments. Americas (~78% of revenue, $12.5B in FY25) provides planning, design, and program/construction management to public and private clients across the US, Canada, and Latin America. International (~22%, $3.6B) covers EMEA, India, and Asia-Pacific and carries a higher gross margin (9.2% vs Americas 7.0%). AECOM Capital is a small real-estate co-investment arm now largely transitioned to a third-party platform.

End markets are transportation, water, environment/energy, and facilities. Demand is tied to large public infrastructure programs: the US IIJA and the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the UK's £725B 10-Year Infrastructure Strategy, and Canada's Major Projects Office, supplemented by emergent drivers in defense, data centers/electrification, and water remediation (PFAS). Governments are roughly 50% of revenue, but US federal is only ~7%, which limits direct exposure to the federal procurement reform, shutdown, and budget-efficiency pressures currently reshaping pure federal-services franchises.

The business model is deliberately capital-light. Income comes from billing employee time on client projects rather than taking construction balance-sheet risk. The self-perform at-risk construction operations have been in discontinued operations since FY2020, though certain legacy claims and indemnity tails remain.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue was essentially flat at $16.14B in FY25 versus $16.11B in FY24 (+0.2%), but the headline understates the underlying trend because ~53% of revenue is low-margin pass-through subcontractor cost. Pass-through declined from 56% to 53%, a favorable mix shift, and net service revenue grew across most end markets. The real story is margin: gross margin rose to 7.5% from 6.7%, operating margin to 6.4% from 5.1%, and net income attributable to AECOM jumped 39.6% to $561.8M. The multi-year net-income path is $311M (FY22) → $55M (FY23, depressed by a $279M JV impairment) → $402M (FY24) → $562M (FY25). Company-adjusted EPS rose $3.40 → $3.71 → $4.52 → $5.26. Q2 FY26 (quarter ended 4/3/2026) extended the trend: revenue +0.8% to $3.80B, net income attributable to AECOM +25.5% to $179.9M, continuing-ops diluted EPS $1.42 vs $1.16, Americas gross margin to 7.7%.

**Cash flow — the crux.** FY25 operating cash flow was $821.6M and free cash flow $684.9M (4.2% margin), with OCF exceeding net income, a marker of high earnings quality. That broke down in the first half of FY26: operating cash flow fell to $74.0M from $341.7M, a ~78% decline, driven by a $282M working-capital drain. DSO rose to 83 days from 74, and unbilled claims in contract assets ballooned to ~$680M from ~$400M. The mechanism is straightforward: AECOM recognizes revenue on cost-to-cost estimates that include variable consideration (claims, award fees, unpriced change orders); when collection on those items slows, GAAP profit and cash diverge. Until the claims resolve, the company is booking earnings it is not banking.

**Balance sheet.** Equity is light by design. Stockholders' equity is $2.49B against $12.2B of assets, and retained earnings are negative (-$1.48B at Q2 FY26) as a result of years of buybacks rather than operating losses; goodwill ($3.7B) exceeds equity. D/E is 3.81 and the Altman Z-score of 2.12 sits in the grey zone, again a function of thin equity and leverage rather than operating distress. ROE is 25.5% and ROA 5.2%. Total debt is ~$2.74B and well-laddered: in July 2025 AECOM refinanced its 5.125% 2027 notes with $1.2B of 6.000% Senior Notes due 2033, lifting interest expense (1H FY26 interest $95.8M vs $85.2M), and in March 2026 it extended its revolver/Term A maturity to 2031. The 4.00x leverage covenant is a live constraint; the firm is currently in compliance, but a prolonged cash-conversion shortfall combined with $441.9M of 1H FY26 buybacks narrows the cushion.

**Earnings surprise record.** AECOM has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (Q2 FY26 +1.5%, Q1 FY26 +9.6%, prior quarters +0.7% and +5.1%). The beats lend confidence to the forward earnings line; they do not address the cash-conversion question, which is a balance-sheet and timing issue rather than a P&L issue.

## Growth Analysis

Earnings growth is genuine but margin-led rather than revenue-led. Reported revenue is flat, and Q2 grew only 0.8%, so the FY25 and Q2 net-income gains come from gross-margin expansion (80bps+ per year), the offshoring "enterprise capability centers," continuous improvement, and a richer mix of advisory work. Management has raised its segment-adjusted operating-margin target to 20%+ exiting FY2028, citing operating leverage from proprietary "AECOM AI" tooling and higher-margin Advisory services. The two FY25 acquisitions ($375.9M total, ~$203M of digital-asset intangibles now amortizing) are consistent with that investment direction.

Backlog supports durability: it rose 6.1% in FY25, with remaining unsatisfied performance obligations of $20.1B at Q2 FY26, about 60% expected to convert within twelve months. Named program pipelines in defense, energy/data centers, and water (including PFAS remediation) provide multi-year visibility, and the breadth of government funding across the US, UK, and Canada reduces reliance on any single appropriations cycle.

The growth thesis has two limits. First, with revenue flat, the path to the 20% margin target relies on continued mix shift and AI-driven efficiency rather than top-line acceleration; margin expansion has a ceiling that revenue growth does not. Second, the estimate-revision picture is only modestly constructive: current-year consensus is flat over 90 days at $5.99, next-year up 1.4% to $6.72, and the current quarter has actually been revised down 2.6%. This is stability rather than rapidly rising consensus. The growth signal is durable but moderate.

## Valuation Assessment

On the surface, ACM is cheap. At ~$69 the stock trades at ~14.5x trailing EPS of $4.79 and roughly 11.5x the $5.99 current-year consensus, against a Construction & Engineering peer median P/E of 28.4 (ECG 32.9, FLR 22.8, MYRG 46.8, ACA 28.4, TTEK 16.6). The forward multiple is below the company's expected EPS growth rate, implying a PEG well under 1.0, and the 1.74% dividend (recently raised to $0.31/quarter) provides modest yield support. Analyst price targets span $90 low to $145 high, with a $105 mean and $102 median, all well above the current price; the analyst panel remains constructive (2 strong buy, 9 buy, 2 hold).

The discount is not purely an error. The market is applying it for an identifiable reason: the cash-conversion shortfall, the thin equity base, and the governance friction from the construction-management strategic-review reversal (the company announced a potential sale in November 2025, then disclosed in May 2026 that it would retain the business). Targets have also been cut into the decline — Barclays moved to Equal Weight with a $135→$100 target in December 2025. So while the headline multiple is low, part of the gap reflects a justified penalty for cash-conversion and leverage risk rather than pure mispricing.

We treat cheapness here as a conditional rather than a standalone buy signal. The asymmetric upside is real if claims convert to cash and the FCF engine restarts at the FY25 run-rate against an $8.8B market cap (an ~8% FCF yield). The risk is that the discount persists or widens if cash conversion does not normalize, in which case the leverage profile becomes the dominant variable and the "cheap" multiple is a trap. The valuation score reflects genuine upside optionality discounted for an unresolved, falsifiable risk.

## Competitive Landscape

AECOM is the scale leader in a fragmented A&E and consulting market that includes Jacobs, WSP, Stantec, Arcadis, and Tetra Tech. Its differentiation is the combination of global network, technical depth, and the ability to pursue mega-programs (high-speed rail, large water systems, megacity development) that smaller firms cannot staff. The competitive trajectory is improving on the evidence that matters: market-leadership rankings are intact, gross margin is expanding (a sign of pricing and mix power), backlog is at a record, and the pivot toward higher-margin Advisory and proprietary AI tooling leverages the firm's design-data libraries in a way peers cannot easily replicate.

The moat is talent-and-tooling rather than capital. Barriers to entry are low, and the firm's edge depends on retaining technical staff and building proprietary AI — which is why the ~$130M of unrecognized stock compensation and the $203M of digital-asset investment are strategically central. This is a durable but not impregnable position; the structural infrastructure tailwinds (aging assets, electrification, water resilience, defense) favor scale incumbents, and AECOM is positioned to capture more than its share.

## Risk Assessment

**Cash conversion / claims collection (elevated, the dominant near-term risk).** If claims convert over the next one to two quarters, the FCF and buyback story restarts; if they do not, leverage and capital-return capacity become constrained. This is binary-ish and not fully resolvable from current data.

**Leverage and thin equity.** Equity against $2.74B debt plus $904M letters of credit and $6.1B surety bonds leaves a small cushion. The 4.00x leverage covenant is a real constraint, and interest expense is rising on the 6% 2033 notes.

**Tax leak.** AECOM is under IRS examination of FY2017-2020 returns over tax credits; it booked a $47M reserve in FY25 and an additional $34.4M in Q2 FY26 for uncertain positions. This is a recurring, low-visibility drag that could crystallize as a one-time settlement charge.

**Legacy construction tails.** Despite exiting the business, the firm retains exposure: a $61.8M Q1 FY26 non-cash loss on the DOE New York demolition project claim, a $53M FY25 Montana refinery loss under appeal, divested-business surety guarantees, and a guaranty on a civil-infrastructure buyer's debt extended to March 2028.

**Government and contract risk.** ~50% government revenue carries appropriations, shutdown, termination-for-convenience, and audit risk, though the modest ~7% US federal share limits direct procurement-reform exposure. About 25% of revenue is fixed-price, placing cost-overrun risk on AECOM.

**Governance / communication.** The construction-management strategic-review reversal is a self-inflicted blemish that likely contributed to the loss of investor confidence, even if the underlying decision to retain a cash-generative, synergistic unit is defensible.

### Options Market Signal

The clean options snapshot shows near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.8% against one-year historical volatility of 33.1% — a 2.7-point spread, with IV in only the 12th percentile of its recent chain, so the market is not pricing an imminent shock. Term structure is in contango (near-term 35.8% below longer-dated 39.7%), a complacent configuration. The cautionary note is positioning: the put/call open-interest ratio of 1.82 and put/call volume ratio of 1.19 show a persistent put bias, consistent with hedging or skepticism following the drawdown. (A reported jump in one ATM IV reading to ~810% is a thin-options data artifact and carries no information.) Net read: options are not signaling a near-term catalyst, but positioning leans defensive, aligned with a HOLD rather than a high-conviction long.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$105-130)
A #1 global franchise with record backlog, 80bps+ annual gross-margin expansion, and a credible path to 20%+ segment operating margin by FY2028 is being valued at ~11.5x forward earnings versus a 28x peer median. The cash-flow shortfall is a timing problem tied to claims resolution; as those claims convert, FCF normalizes toward the FY25 level (~8% yield) and funds continued buybacks below intrinsic value. Three of the most senior insiders bought open-market stock at ~$70-71, the strongest available conviction signal, and analyst targets (mean $105, high $145) imply a re-rating well above the current price. Re-rating toward even a 14-16x forward multiple on a recovering cash story gets the stock back into the $90-105 range, with the bull case toward $130 if margin targets are met and cash conversion fully restores.

### Bear Case (~$55-60)
Claims do not convert on schedule, OCF stays depressed, and the divergence between GAAP earnings and cash becomes a credibility problem against a 3.8x-levered, negative-retained-earnings balance sheet. Continued $400M+ buyback pace into weak cash flow pressures the leverage covenant. The IRS reserve grows, legacy tails produce additional charges, and revenue stays flat so the entire equity story rests on margin gains with a ceiling. In that scenario the multiple stays compressed or de-rates further toward 9-10x forward, taking the stock to the mid-to-high $50s, ~15-20% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-biased).** This is a downgrade from the prior BUY. The franchise quality, backlog, margin trajectory, deep peer discount, and insider buying support an appreciation thesis, but the unresolved cash-conversion question against a thin, levered balance sheet keeps the risk/reward short of a buy. The score path (6.2 → 6.2 → 6.9, BUY) is reset to a HOLD because the most recent data point — the confirmed 1H FY26 cash-flow collapse — is genuine fundamental information, not merely price action, and because the prior BUY at ~$71 has not worked while that concern remains open.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY26, expected August 2026). The fiscal-year-end FY26 print (November 2026) is the fuller test of cash conversion.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating cash flow recovers toward historical conversion (OCF approaching or exceeding net income on a trailing-twelve-month basis) and DSO declines back toward the low-70s.
- Unbilled claims in contract assets decline materially, evidencing collection.
- Continued backlog growth (>$40B) alongside maintained or raised margin guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of sub-$150M operating cash flow with DSO above 85, signaling structural rather than timing-driven deterioration.
- Leverage approaching the 4.00x covenant, or buybacks continuing at a pace that pressures it.
- A material adverse IRS settlement or a new legacy-construction charge above ~$50M.

## Conclusion

AECOM is a high-quality, market-leading infrastructure design franchise posting record earnings and record backlog, trading at a substantial discount to peers, with senior insiders buying their own stock into the decline. Those are the ingredients of a buy. The reason it stops short is concrete and measurable: the firm is recognizing strong profit it is not yet collecting, and its capital-light, thinly-equitized, 3.8x-levered balance sheet leaves little room if claims fail to convert. The dividend yield is too low to anchor an income case, so the hold rests on a growth thesis whose central catalyst — cash conversion — is genuinely unresolved over the next one to two quarters. We rate ACM a HOLD with a growth bias, with a clear path to upgrade should the August and November prints show claims turning into cash and DSO normalizing.
