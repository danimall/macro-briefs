## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is the world's largest publicly traded life and health reinsurer, with $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force, $156.6 billion in total assets, and operations spanning the U.S., Asia Pacific, EMEA, and Canada. The company generated $23.7 billion in revenue in 2025, up 7.2% YoY, with adjusted operating income before tax of $1.97 billion (+12.3%). GAAP net income surged 65% to $1.18 billion, though the increase was partly amplified by depressed 2024 results (portfolio repositioning losses, assumption review charges).

At ~11.6x forward earnings, RGA trades at a modest premium to its reinsurance peer median of 9.0x. This premium is warranted: RGA offers a 13.3% three-year revenue CAGR, a 15.7% adjusted operating ROE, and a unique competitive position as the only pure-play life reinsurer of this scale. The $32 billion Equitable Holdings transaction, closed in July 2025, substantially expands the company's asset-intensive business and deepens its relationship with a major U.S. life insurer.

Key risks include a $222 million EMEA assumption loss tied to deteriorating U.K. mortality trends, Moody's negative outlook on the A1 rating, rapid balance sheet expansion (assets nearly doubled in three years), and credit quality deterioration ($181 million in losses/impairments, up from $76 million). These risks are manageable given the conservative pure financial debt leverage (0.42x D/E) and 94.3% investment-grade portfolio. The current rate environment — with SOFR at 3.64% and no cuts on the horizon — provides a structural tailwind to investment income that extends for as long as the hawkish regime persists.

## Company Overview

RGA was formed in 1992 and is headquartered in St. Louis, Missouri, with approximately 4,300 employees. It operates through two primary business lines across four geographic segments:

**Traditional Reinsurance (~75% of net premiums):** Individual and group life/health, disability, critical illness, and long-term care reinsurance. Premiums are earned over 10-30+ years on in-force blocks.

**Financial Solutions (~25% of net premiums, rapidly growing):** Asset-intensive reinsurance (annuities, COLI/BOLI), longevity reinsurance, pension risk transfer, capital solutions, stable value products, and a Funding Agreement Backed Note program.

**Geographic breakdown (2025 adjusted operating income before tax):**
- U.S. & Latin America: $869M (45%)
- Asia Pacific: $759M (39%)
- EMEA: $363M (19%)
- Canada: $194M (10%)
- Corporate & Other: -$218M

The client base is diversified: top five clients represent ~21% of gross premiums, with no single client exceeding 10%. New business production surged 45% in 2025 to $733.5 billion face amount, driven overwhelmingly by the Equitable deal but also strong organic growth in EMEA Traditional ($171.8B vs. $119.5B).

The gross debt-to-equity ratio of 10.6x is misleading — this is the structural leverage of an insurance balance sheet, where $143 billion in liabilities consists primarily of future policy benefit reserves and interest-sensitive contract liabilities with predictable, long-duration cash flows. Pure financial debt leverage stands at 0.42x ($5.7B debt / $13.5B equity), conservative for the industry. Covenants require consolidated indebtedness below 35% of total capitalization.

The fixed maturity portfolio is 94.3% investment grade. However, credit losses and impairments more than doubled to $181 million in 2025 from $76 million in 2024. Level 3 (illiquid) assets rose to 13.5% of fixed maturity securities from 11.2%, reflecting the push into private placements for yield. Gross unrealized losses of $7.0 billion on fixed maturities (up from $6.4 billion) are a function of the rate environment and will reverse if rates decline — but rates are going higher, not lower, in the current regime.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

1. **Rising cession rates in North America.** Post-LDTI and Bermuda regulatory changes are increasing the proportion of life insurance risk that primary insurers cede to reinsurers. This is a structural demand driver.

2. **Demographic tailwinds.** Aging populations globally increase demand for annuities, pension risk transfer, longevity products, and long-term care reinsurance. RGA's dual capability in biometric risk and financial solutions positions it for both sides of the longevity trade.

3. **Asia Pacific expansion.** Asia Pacific generated $759 million in adjusted operating income (39% of total), growing rapidly as markets in Japan, Australia, India, and Southeast Asia deepen their insurance penetration. The invested asset base supporting Asia Pacific grew from $22.2 billion to $29.4 billion in 2025.

4. **Investment income leverage.** Higher-for-longer rates compound through the entire $100+ billion investment portfolio. At current rates, the reinvestment yield on maturing bonds exceeds the portfolio yield, creating a multi-year tailwind.

### Equitable Holdings Deal

The $32 billion Equitable transaction (75% quota share of in-force individual life liabilities) closed July 31, 2025, and contributed $68 million to pre-tax income in the partial year. The U.S. Financial Solutions invested asset base grew by $14.5 billion. This deal shifts RGA's revenue mix further toward asset-intensive business and concentrates the counterparty relationship with Equitable. The full-year earnings contribution in 2026 will be the first true test of this deal's return profile.

## Valuation Assessment

RGA trades at approximately 11.6x both trailing and forward earnings, reflecting the trailing $17.69 EPS and forward consensus of ~$17.82 (current-year $26.11 implies roughly that on a trailing basis). Against the reinsurance peer median of 9.0x (Everest Group 9.0x, RenRe 5.0x, MGIC 9.3x, SiriusPoint 6.0x), RGA carries a 29% premium.

**The premium is justified by three factors:**

1. **Growth trajectory.** RGA's 13.3% revenue CAGR and 12.3% operating income growth exceed what Everest, MGIC, or SiriusPoint deliver. RenRe at 5.0x has a superior ROE but is a pure property cat reinsurer with fundamentally different risk characteristics.

2. **Scale and diversification.** RGA is the only pure-play life reinsurer of this size, with global geographic reach and dual traditional/financial solutions capabilities. This provides earnings stability that property-focused peers lack.

3. **Investment income compounding.** The rate environment provides a structural earnings floor. RGA's $100+ billion invested asset base generates investment income that is largely independent of underwriting results.

**Price-to-book:** At $206 per share and $13.5 billion in equity (roughly $206 per share on 65.5 million shares), the stock trades at approximately 1.0x book value. This is inexpensive for a company generating 15.7% adjusted operating ROE. However, book value includes significant accumulated OCI drag from $7.0 billion in unrealized fixed maturity losses.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median is $261, with a range of $215 to $273. At $206, the stock sits 21% below the median target, providing meaningful upside even to the low target ($215, +4.4%).

Applying 12x to FY2026 consensus EPS of $26.11 yields a fair value of ~$313. However, given the risks (U.K. mortality, credit quality, Equitable integration), a 10.5-11x multiple is more appropriate, yielding a range of $274-287 per share. My base case target is $255, which implies ~11x FY2026 EPS adjusted for the assumption review risk.

## Competitive Landscape

RGA occupies a unique competitive position as the world's largest and only publicly traded pure-play life reinsurer. Its principal competitors are:

- **Swiss Re, Munich Re, Hannover Re:** Larger overall but diversified across P&C; none is a pure life reinsurance play.
- **SCOR:** French-listed, smaller scale, similar biometric expertise.
- **Apollo/Athene, KKR, Brookfield:** PE-backed competitors focused on asset accumulation. They compete aggressively on price for large in-force blocks but lack RGA's actuarial depth and client relationships built over decades.

RGA's moat rests on three elements: (1) proprietary mortality/morbidity data across billions of covered lives, creating an information advantage in underwriting complex risks; (2) client relationships that are structurally sticky because ceding companies cannot easily transfer reinsurance treaties; and (3) regulatory expertise across multiple jurisdictions (LDTI, Solvency II, PBR) that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

The competitive threat from PE-backed firms is real but concentrated in asset-intensive blocks where capital cost is the primary variable. In traditional biometric reinsurance, RGA's competitive position is stronger and less contested.

## Risk Assessment

### U.K. Mortality Assumption Loss ($222M) — HIGH

The $222 million pre-tax loss in EMEA Traditional reflects deterioration in mortality improvements and ongoing challenges within the U.K. healthcare system, specifically documented degradation of NHS outcomes. For a reinsurer with $1.1 trillion of EMEA life reinsurance in force, continued deterioration in U.K. mortality could produce recurring assumption charges. The EMEA Traditional segment ran a 101% loss ratio in 2025 — above breakeven.

### Rapid Balance Sheet Expansion / Equitable Integration — MEDIUM-HIGH

The Equitable deal added ~$32 billion in notional liabilities. The partial-year contribution of $68 million against this balance sheet expansion is thin. Full-year results in 2026 will be the first real test. Funds withheld at interest of $8.1 billion, with ~90% of the U.S. segregated portfolio concentrated in two clients, creates counterparty concentration risk.

### Moody's Negative Outlook — MEDIUM

A downgrade from A1 to A2 would not breach derivative termination thresholds or recapture triggers (which are set at much lower levels), but it signals concern about leverage trajectory and could incrementally increase cost of capital.

### Credit Quality Deterioration — MEDIUM

Credit losses/impairments doubled to $181 million. Level 3 assets rose to 13.5% of fixed maturities. In a credit cascade environment (probability 58-68% based on current macro conditions), RGA's push into private placements and alternatives creates mark-to-market exposure. The 94.3% investment-grade portfolio provides a buffer, but the direction is negative.

### Management Turnover — LOW-MEDIUM

At least seven executive departure/election 8-K filings between 2024-2026 suggest meaningful leadership transition. The CFO role and multiple EVP positions have changed hands. CEO Cheng's compensation alignment (ROE-based, embedded value targets) is appropriate, but the pace of turnover warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 26.8% is marginally above 24.4% historical volatility — the spread of 2.3 percentage points is reasonable and does not signal significant mispricing. The 4.7% put/call skew (1-month tenor) indicates modestly elevated put demand, consistent with macro uncertainty rather than stock-specific bearishness. The flat term structure (near 26.8%, far 27.0%) suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific catalyst. IV percentile at 10% indicates options are cheap relative to their own recent range, which is favorable for entry timing.

Short interest is minimal at 1.8% of float with 3.2 days to cover — no squeeze risk, no informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($255-273)

The Equitable deal delivers above expectations in 2026, generating $150-200 million in full-year pre-tax income. Investment yields continue rising as the higher-for-longer rate regime extends through 2026-2027, adding $100-200 million annually to investment income. Asia Pacific continues strong organic growth. U.K. mortality losses stabilize as the assumption update captures the worst of the NHS deterioration. At 10-11x a rising FY2027 EPS of $28.88, the stock reaches $289-318.

### Bear Case ($170-180)

U.K. mortality continues deteriorating, producing another $150-200 million assumption loss in 2026. Credit cascade materializes, triggering mark-to-market losses on the $11.1 billion mortgage loan book and private placements. Moody's downgrades to A2, increasing funding costs. The Equitable block underperforms expectations. Insurance rate softening (10-20% at Jan 2026 renewals, observed across the sector) compresses pricing power in traditional business. At 8x reduced FY2026 EPS of ~$22, the stock trades to $176.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Second consecutive U.K. mortality assumption loss exceeding $150 million in the 2026 annual review (would indicate structural, worsening problem rather than one-time reset).
2. Moody's downgrade to A2 combined with spread widening on RGA's outstanding debt above 200bp over Treasuries.
3. Equitable block full-year 2026 contribution below $80 million pre-tax (would suggest deal economics are weaker than modeled).

## Conclusion

RGA offers a differentiated exposure to the life reinsurance industry at a reasonable 11.6x forward multiple, with 24% upside to the base case target. The Equitable deal expands the asset base and extends the growth trajectory, though it introduces concentration and integration risk.

The U.K. mortality assumption loss is a specific, emerging risk that requires monitoring through the 2026 review cycle. Credit quality deterioration and management turnover add incremental concerns. These risks are quantifiable, and at current prices the market offers the stock at a valuation that provides adequate compensation for bearing them.

The 1.8% dividend yield and $500 million buyback authorization provide capital return support. Options pricing is near historical lows, creating a favorable entry point from a volatility perspective. The 3-beat, 1-miss earnings record with the most recent quarter beating by 33.5% supports confidence in forward estimates.

Rating: **BUY** at $206 with a $255 base case target over 12-18 months.
