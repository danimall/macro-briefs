**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG is a pure-play owner-operator of modern Very Large Gas Carriers (VLGCs), with roughly 99% of revenue flowing through the Helios Pool commercial joint venture and thus almost entirely levered to spot VLGC freight rates. FY2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) was a strong rebound year: revenue rose 36% to $481.5M, net income more than doubled to $193.7M, and the time-charter-equivalent (TCE) rate climbed to $52,238/day from $39,778 the prior year. Q4 was exceptional (revenue $153.3M, +102% YoY; net income $81.0M against $8.1M a year earlier; TCE of $63,615/day), but the driver was a geopolitical anomaly rather than durable structural demand. The Strait of Hormuz disruption from the Iran conflict plus the Saudi Aramco Juaymah NGL terminal outage cut Middle East LPG exports from roughly 4 MMT in January to 1.3 MMT in March, forced vessel re-routing that lengthened ton-miles, and drove the Baltic LPG Index to about $95 during calendar Q1 2026.

The scoring problem is straightforward: the ~9.8x trailing P/E looks cheap, but it capitalizes peak-cycle earnings powered partly by a reversible geopolitical shock. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 81% over 90 days to $6.22, yet next-year EPS is set at $3.44 — the sell side itself models a roughly 45% earnings decline. That forward step-down, combined with a newbuild orderbook equal to 27.5% of the existing fleet (much of it dual-fuel VLAC tonnage that can also carry LPG), is the core reason cheapness here is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. Offsetting this: a conservatively leveraged balance sheet (D/E 0.64, $327M cash), embedded vessel value roughly $600M above carrying value validated by the *Cobra* sale at $81.9M, and a credible CEO open-market purchase of 15,000 shares at $27.30 near the January cyclical trough.

We rate LPG a HOLD. The setup is two-sided on a spot-rate business whose upside driver (Hormuz/Panama disruption, tight U.S. export logistics) is the same thing that would reverse violently on de-escalation. The prior report trajectory (BUY in April, three subsequent HOLDs, overall drifting from 6.8 to 5.7) reflects the same tension, and current data confirms it: financial health has strengthened while valuation and growth prospects have weakened as the market prices in peak-cycle earnings.

## Company Overview

Dorian LPG is a Marshall Islands corporation headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, operating a fleet of 27 VLGCs as of mid-May 2026: 21 owned/bareboat vessels plus 6 time-chartered-in. The owned fleet is modern (built mostly 2014–2016, plus a 2023 dual-fuel vessel and the 2026-built *Areion*), with nearly all vessels of fuel-efficient "ECO" design and 17 scrubber-equipped. Six vessels are dual-fuel LPG-burning and several are ammonia-capable, positioning the company for a potential future ammonia-fuel/cargo transition.

The commercial model runs through the Helios Pool, a 50/50 joint venture with MOL Energia (a Mitsui O.S.K. Lines subsidiary). Vessels earn a pro-rata share of pool profits, delivering scale and utilization benefits but tethering earnings almost entirely to spot VLGC freight rates benchmarked to the Baltic LPG Index (Ras Tanura–Chiba route). This is a commodity price-taker with heavy operating leverage: low marginal cost per voyage means each incremental dollar of TCE flows disproportionately to the bottom line, which is why net income more than doubled on a 31% rise in TCE.

The core trade lanes move propane and butane on long-haul routes from the U.S. Gulf and the Middle East Gulf to Asia and Europe. Dorian sits among the larger independent VLGC owners (top-10 owners hold ~41% of the global fleet), competing with BW LPG, NYK Line, Petredec, and Avance Gas.

## Financial Analysis

The financials show the boom-and-partial-retrace pattern typical of shipping.

| Metric (FY, $M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 560.7 | 353.3 | 481.5 |
| Net income | 307.4 | 90.2 | 193.7 |
| Adj. EBITDA | 417.4 | 206.0 | 305.1 |
| TCE rate ($/day) | 62,129 | 39,778 | 52,238 |
| Diluted EPS | ~7.4 | 2.14 | 4.54 |

FY2026 margins were high (roughly 40% net margin and 63% Adjusted EBITDA margin), reflecting the asset-heavy, low-marginal-cost model at healthy rates. Cost discipline was evident: daily vessel opex fell to $10,557 from $11,143 (Q4 opex just $9,780/day), and interest expense declined to $29.2M from $35.8M on debt paydown and lower SOFR.

Cash generation was solid. Operating cash flow was approximately $210M, capex roughly $94M (largely the *Areion* delivery), and free cash flow about $116M. The balance sheet is conservative for a shipping company: total debt of $565.8M (long-term debt net of fees ~$460M), equity of $1.14B, current ratio ~2.67, and D/E of 0.64. Roughly 85% of debt is hedged or fixed. Altman Z sits around 2.6 (grey zone, normal for capital-intensive shipping), and Piotroski signals were strong.

The most important balance-sheet item is off it: aggregate vessel market value was reported roughly $600M above carrying value, and the May 2026 sale of the 2015/2016-built *Cobra* at $81.9M net validates that ships can be monetized well above book. That embedded NAV is a genuine downside cushion and argues against scoring financial health lower despite the cyclical earnings.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and warrants weighting: over the last four quarters, two beats and two misses, including a 61.4% miss in the June 2025 quarter and a 27.5% beat in the March 2026 quarter. Quarter-to-quarter earnings visibility is genuinely poor because rates swing violently, which reduces confidence in extrapolating any single strong print.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic "growth" thesis here in the conventional sense: Dorian's earnings trajectory is a function of the VLGC rate cycle, not a compounding revenue base. Fleet capacity is roughly flat (the *Areion* delivery offset by the *Cobra* sale), so top-line and earnings movement is almost entirely rate-driven.

The estimate revision picture requires careful reading. Current-year EPS has been revised up 130.3% over 90 days (current quarter) and 81.0% over 90 days (current year, to $6.22). Under the framework that treats large positive revisions on cheap inflection stocks as a strong buy signal, this would qualify. But the signal is undercut by two facts. First, the revision reflects a geopolitical rate spike (Hormuz, Aramco outage) that management itself frames cautiously, not a structural demand inflection. Second, the sell side sets next-year EPS at $3.44 — a roughly 45% decline from the $6.22 current-year figure — which means the same analysts revising near-term estimates up are simultaneously modeling a sharp normalization. The 30-day revision on the current year is slightly negative (-1.1%), consistent with rates having peaked. When the forward curve of consensus estimates itself bends down that hard, treating trailing or current-year cheapness as a re-rating catalyst is the wrong read.

The structural demand pillars are real but qualified. U.S. LPG export growth is genuine (exports rose to ~6.3 MMT/month in early 2026), and BW LPG independently cited a shortage of available vessels on the U.S.–Far East trade, corroborating the tight market. Against that, PDH margins turned negative (roughly -$155/MT) and naphtha/propane cracking margins deteriorated in Q1 2026, a demand-side warning that petrochemical LPG buying, a key structural pillar, can soften if margins stay negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $44.39 the stock trades at 9.8x trailing EPS of $4.54 and about 7.1x current-year consensus of $6.22. Against the peer group in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation (median P/E 24.9, median yield 4.2%), Dorian looks deeply discounted. But that peer set is mostly regulated/contracted midstream (KNTK, AM, DTM) rather than spot-exposed tankers; the truer comparable is INSW at 7.5x, another spot-levered marine name trading at a similar cyclical-peak discount. Tanker and gas-carrier equities habitually trade at single-digit multiples of peak earnings because the market refuses to capitalize the top of the cycle at normal multiples; the low P/E discounts the coming normalization rather than signaling a mispricing.

The relevant question is what mid-cycle earnings power looks like. FY2025, a weaker year, produced $2.14 EPS at a $39,778 TCE. Consensus next-year EPS of $3.44 implies a TCE somewhere in the mid-$40,000s. On $3.44, the stock trades at 12.9x — no longer cheap for a business with no unit growth, a 27.5%-of-fleet orderbook overhang, and reversible geopolitical tailwinds. The bull counterargument is the embedded NAV: vessels ~$600M above book against a $1.9B market cap provides a hard-asset floor and optionality on further sales at premium prices. That NAV support is the single best reason not to score valuation lower, and it caps downside more effectively than the earnings multiple suggests.

Analyst price targets (low $45, mean/median $51, high $55) sit modestly above the current $44.39. Because forward estimates bend down and the multiple is discounting peak earnings, those targets are not floors that will be revised higher; they reflect the same recognition that current earnings are near the top of the range. We do not treat proximity to targets as either a ceiling or a re-rating trigger; the more relevant anchor is normalized earnings power, which does not support meaningful upside from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian's competitive position is stable rather than improving. Its advantages are structural to owning a young, fuel-efficient, scrubber-fitted fleet: at ~10.5 years the owned fleet is younger than the ~11.8-year global average, giving a modest efficiency and marketability edge, and its scrubber-equipped vessels capture the fuel-spread benefit when spreads are wide. The Helios Pool provides scale and utilization that a standalone owner of comparable size could not match.

None of this is share-gaining or moat-widening in a way the market has failed to price. The competitive dynamic that matters most is the orderbook: roughly 116 VLGCs/VLACs (~10.4M cbm, 27.5% of the fleet) scheduled for delivery through 2029, a large fraction of it ammonia-capable VLAC tonnage. If ammonia demand disappoints, those vessels default into the LPG trade and compress rates for everyone, including Dorian's efficiency-advantaged fleet. Dorian's dual-fuel and ammonia-capable ships plus the April 2026 appointment of ABS Chairman/CEO Christopher Wiernicki to the board position it for an energy-transition scenario, but the same VLAC newbuild wave that creates that optionality is the primary oversupply threat. The competitive trajectory is defensible but flat, with the sector's supply picture the dominant swing factor.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are specific and material, and several are not fully reflected at a peak-earnings price:

1. **~99% revenue concentration in the spot-exposed Helios Pool.** Earnings are almost fully levered to VLGC spot rates, which ranged $39,800–$63,600/day across just eight quarters. A downcycle halves net income quickly, as FY2025 demonstrated.

2. **Orderbook overhang (27.5% of fleet).** The single biggest structural risk. Whether the newbuild wave is absorbed by growing LPG/ammonia trade or becomes a glut will define rates into 2027–2029.

3. **Geopolitics cut both ways.** The Q1 2026 strength came from events that inflate ton-miles and rates short-term but represent a demand shock if prolonged, and that reverse rapidly on de-escalation. A verified Strait of Hormuz reopening would compress the very ton-mile premium driving current earnings. The upside catalyst and the downside catalyst are the same variable.

4. **Trade and regulatory frictions.** China tariffs on U.S. propane/butane, USTR Section 301 port fees on Chinese-built vessels (suspended ~1 year as of November 2025), and the U.S. Maritime Action Plan (February 2026) contemplating fees on non-U.S.-built ships. The U.S. Gulf–Asia route is central to Dorian's economics.

5. **Panama Canal transit constraints**, a recurring bottleneck reshaping routing and economics.

6. **Impairment sensitivity.** One vessel showed an impairment indicator; a ~$10.5M charge would trigger at roughly -25% of the 10-year average TCE. Manageable, but indicative of how carrying values track rates.

7. **Tax structure.** Reliance on the Section 883 exemption and PFIC considerations, structural for foreign-domiciled shippers.

The offsetting factor is the balance sheet: low leverage, cash on hand, hedged debt, and embedded NAV give Dorian the capacity to ride out a downcycle that would impair over-levered peers. The risk score is below neutral (specific, partly-unpriced downside catalysts at a peak-earnings price) but not deeply distressed (solvency is not in question).

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates a cautious read. Near-term ATM IV of 44.3% sits roughly fair to 1-year historical volatility of 42.6% (1.6% spread), so the market is not pricing a directional surprise into premium. The term-structure backwardation (near-term 44.3% vs. long-term 40.1%) signals the market expects imminent stress or a near-term catalyst, consistent with sensitivity to Hormuz-related headlines and the upcoming quarterly print. Near-term put/call skew of 5.8% reflects elevated put demand, a modest hedging tilt against downside. Short interest is moderate at 7.3% of float (2.9 days to cover), neither a squeeze setup nor evidence of a heavy informed short. The options market is pricing two-sided, event-driven risk rather than a clean directional view, reinforcing the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Structural U.S. LPG export growth plus persistent ton-mile lengthening from Panama constraints and Middle East disruption sustains VLGC rates above mid-cycle for longer than consensus models. The tight vessel supply on the U.S.–Far East trade (corroborated by BW LPG) holds, the VLAC orderbook is absorbed by genuine ammonia demand rather than defaulting into LPG, and Dorian continues to monetize NAV at premium prices while returning capital via escalating irregular dividends ($2.45/share in FY26, $1.00 declared in May 2026). In this scenario, earnings hold near $5–6, the embedded NAV re-rates into the equity, and the stock reaches the low-to-mid $50s (bull ~$54).

**Bear Case.** A verified Hormuz reopening and Panama normalization compress ton-miles and rates back toward the FY2025 $39,800/day level or below. The orderbook delivers into a softening market, ammonia demand disappoints and VLACs flood the LPG trade, and negative PDH/cracking margins suppress petrochemical LPG buying. Next-year EPS of $3.44 proves optimistic; earnings revert toward $2–2.50, the discretionary dividend is cut sharply, and the stock derates to the high-$20s to low-$30s (bear ~$29, roughly -35% from current). The NAV floor cushions but does not prevent this.

The probability-weighted outcome sits near current levels because the primary upside driver is a reversible geopolitical anomaly, and the sell side's own forward estimates already model normalization.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The classification is `uncertain` — the setup is genuinely two-sided on a spot-rate business, and the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that VLGC rate strength is structural rather than geopolitical — Baltic LPG Index holding above ~$70 for two-plus quarters *after* any Hormuz/Panama normalization, confirming the tight-supply thesis independent of disruption.
- Consensus next-year EPS revised up above $4.50 (versus current $3.44), signaling the sell side sees durable mid-cycle earnings rather than a peak-and-fade.
- A meaningful pullback toward the low-$30s that widens the discount to NAV and restores asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed, sustained Strait of Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of uninterrupted commercial transit) that reverses the ton-mile premium, coupled with the Baltic Index falling below ~$50.
- Orderbook deliveries accelerating into a softening rate environment with VLAC tonnage visibly entering the LPG spot trade.
- A dividend cut signaling management's own read that the cycle has turned, or a vessel impairment charge indicating carrying values are catching down to rates.

**Reassessment timeline:** The June-quarter print (expected roughly August 2026) is the natural checkpoint; it will show whether the ~$63,600/day Q4 TCE was a one-quarter anomaly or an early-cycle run-rate, and how management frames the geopolitical setup and dividend cadence.

## Conclusion

Dorian LPG is a well-run, conservatively financed pure-play VLGC owner that delivered a strong FY2026 rebound. The problem for the equity is that the strength is concentrated in a Q4 driven by a reversible geopolitical shock, and the trailing multiple capitalizes earnings near the top of the cycle. The sell side's own forward estimates — an 81% current-year revision up alongside a ~45% next-year step-down — capture the tension: cheap on today's earnings, fairly-to-fully valued on normalized earnings, with the orderbook as the structural overhang. The CEO's January open-market buy at $27.30 and the embedded NAV are credible supports that cap downside and argue against a bearish call. Weighing the reversible upside driver against the hard-asset floor, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD, with the next earnings print as the decision point.
