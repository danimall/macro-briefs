## Executive Summary

Meta's advertising business is delivering close to the best results in its history. Q2 2026 revenue of $60,801M grew 28% year over year (27% in constant currency), with ad impressions up 14% and average price per ad up 12% at the same time, a combination that indicates advertiser demand is absorbing incremental supply without yield erosion. Average revenue per person reached $16.86, up 24%. Underlying operating margin, adjusting for $2,400M of legal charges and $1,180M of May-2026 severance, was 36.8%. The reported GAAP EPS "miss" of $6.18 against a $7.36 estimate is almost entirely those two discrete items: adding them back at the guided 16% tax rate gives roughly $7.35, essentially the consensus figure. The operating business did not miss.

What has changed is the capital structure supporting it. Free cash flow for the first half fell 30% to $13,170M while operating cash flow rose 29% to $64,088M, because capital expenditure went from $29,479M to $49,113M. FY2026 capex guidance was raised to $130-145B. Contractual commitments plus signed-but-not-commenced leases grew from roughly $235B at year-end 2025 to approximately $696B including $68B of July-2026 subsequent-event leases, about 49% of the corrected $1.502T equity value, on lease terms of 18 to 30 years against hardware Meta depreciates over 5.5 years. Buybacks went to zero in the first half from $22,921M a year earlier with $25.03B of authorization untouched; net new debt of $24,910M was raised instead; share count rose from 2,530M to 2,548M; unrecognized RSU cost of $79,790M implies roughly $26.6B of annual stock compensation against $20,430M in FY2025. Tens of billions of exposure sit in unconsolidated ventures where Meta holds 20% and guarantees residual values with a disclosed maximum exposure to loss of $46,030M on Louisiana alone.

At $556.71, Meta trades at 18.8x normalized trailing earnings of roughly $29.61 and 16.3x FY2027 consensus of $34.10, versus Alphabet at 27.2x trailing. The discount is real and the ad franchise justifies some of it being closed. But the earnings on which that multiple is computed do not yet carry depreciation on the $80,345M of construction in progress, cash taxes are temporarily suppressed ($1,999M paid in 1H 2026 versus $5,544M), accrued-but-unpaid capex in payables nearly doubled to $19,502M, and four trials capable of producing adverse verdicts fall between August 12, 2026 and February 2027. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name, and the score continues to drift lower, from 6.8 in April to 5.5 now. The decline this cycle is driven by the commitment and off-balance-sheet disclosures in the Q2 10-Q and the dated litigation calendar, not by the share price, which is unchanged from the prior review's basis.

## Company Overview

Meta reports two segments. Family of Apps — Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, Threads and Meta AI — produced $60,370M of Q2 2026 revenue, 99.3% of the total, of which $59,363M was advertising. "Other revenue" within FoA (WhatsApp paid messaging and subscriptions) was $1,007M, up 73% off a small base. Reality Labs produced $431M, up 16%, with growth from AI glasses partially offset by lower Quest sales. The FY2025 10-K guides 2026 Reality Labs operating expense to roughly 70% wearables and 30% VR/Horizon, a documented reallocation away from the metaverse.

Family daily active people averaged 3.60B in June 2026 versus 3.48B a year earlier, +3%. The arithmetic of growth is therefore 1.03 × 1.24 ≈ 1.27: roughly seven-eighths of revenue growth came from monetizing existing users harder and one-eighth from user growth. Advertising was 97.6% of Q2 revenue. The fast-growing non-advertising lines are real but together represent 2.4% of revenue; they do not yet reduce dependence on the ad auction.

Geographically, Q2 revenue by customer address was US & Canada $23,863M, Asia-Pacific $16,073M, Europe $14,009M and Rest of World $6,856M. Impression growth was strongest in Asia-Pacific, the lowest-monetizing region, which the 10-Q identifies as a partial offset to price-per-ad gains.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Total costs and expenses rose 55% to $42,026M against 28% revenue growth. R&D rose 67% to $21,656M; G&A rose 111% to $5,609M including the $2,400M legal charge. Operating income fell 8% to $18,775M and operating margin compressed from 43.0% to 30.9%; the Family of Apps segment margin fell from 52.6% to 38.8%. Adjusted for the legal charge and severance, the margin is 36.8%, still down roughly 620 basis points year over year. The compression is not primarily discrete.

The structural drivers are identifiable from the filing: depreciation and amortization of $6,003M in the quarter versus $4,283M (+40%), with servers-and-network depreciation of $9,013M in the half versus $5,738M (+57%); stock-based compensation of $7,601M in the quarter, of which $6,760M sits in R&D; newly disclosed third-party AI token costs in R&D and marketing; elevated server component prices; and AI compensation packages. Meta extended server useful life to 5.5 years effective January 1, 2025; depreciation is accelerating despite a favorable life assumption, and that lever cannot be pulled again.

**Two tax discretes make GAAP earnings unusable without adjustment.** FY2025 net income fell to $60,458M from $62,360M even as operating income rose to $83.3B, because of a $15,930M discrete non-cash charge on OBBBA enactment including a $14,030M valuation allowance. Q1 2026 then recorded an $8,030M discrete CAMT benefit, producing a negative 5% effective tax rate for the half. Q2 normalized to 16%; management guides 15-17% for the remainder of 2026. Trailing-twelve-month GAAP EPS is $26.55; normalizing for both discretes gives roughly $29.61. Any screen keyed to reported net income, ROA or accruals will misread Meta in both directions right now; leverage, liquidity, margin and turnover components remain informative.

**Cash flow is the substantive change.** First-half operating cash flow of $64,088M grew 29%; free cash flow (Meta's definition, net of finance lease principal) fell 30% to $13,170M from $18,883M. FCF margin went from 21.0% to 11.2%. Two disclosures indicate the reported cash capex understates spending already incurred and that operating cash flow is temporarily flattered:

- PP&E sitting in accounts payable and accrued liabilities was $19,502M at 6/30/26 versus $10,618M a year earlier. This is a balance rather than a flow, so it does not by itself establish a rate of increase, but a near-doubling means a large volume of incurred capital spending has yet to pass through the cash statement.
- Cash income taxes paid net were $1,999M in the half versus $5,544M, against a guided 15-17% book rate. OBBBA immediate expensing of domestic R&D and capital investment is suppressing cash taxes; that reverses.

A sensitivity, not a forecast: if FY2026 operating cash flow lands at $145-150B (+25-30% on FY2025's $115.8B), FY2026 free cash flow spans roughly $0B to $20B across the $130-145B capex range, versus $43,590M in FY2025. On $1.502T of equity value that is a 0-1.3% free cash flow yield against 2.90% for FY2025. Working-capital timing, the accrual catch-up and cash-tax normalization all skew this to the low end.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable securities total $90,260M against long-term debt of $83,664M — net cash of roughly $6.6B, from a company that carried $10B of total debt in 2022. Current ratio 2.23x (from 2.60x), debt/equity 0.32x. The debt is five tranches of senior unsecured notes with no financial covenants; the maturity ladder is remote ($0 remaining 2026, $2.75B 2027, $73.75B thereafter out to 2066), with coupons on the newest tranches reaching 6.45%. The notes trade at a fair value of $79.75B against $84.0B face, roughly 5% below par, consistent with rate and spread moves rather than credit concern given interest coverage. Net interest is now approximately a wash: Q2 interest income $859M versus expense $783M, up 225%. Construction in progress rose from $50,521M to $80,345M in six months. Separately, $10,800M of money market funds were reclassified to restricted cash under escrow requirements in multi-year infrastructure purchase agreements, released only in 2028-2030; total restricted cash equivalents are $13,550M and are unavailable for distribution.

**A correction to screen data.** The supplied market capitalization of $1.301T implies approximately 2,205M shares — the Class A count alone. Total shares outstanding at 6/30/26 were 2,548M (2,206M Class A plus 342M Class B). At $556.71 equity value is $1.418T and enterprise value approximately $1.412T. Per-share multiples are unaffected, but any EV- or FCF-yield work built on $1.30T understates the denominator by roughly 9%.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is high, decelerating, and increasingly dependent on monetization intensity. First-half revenue was $117,111M, +30%. Q3 guidance of $60-63B implies roughly 17-23% growth against an estimated Q3 2025 base, a step down from 28%. Management cited international ad-market macro uncertainty and potential FX headwinds, relevant because FX contributed $685M in Q2 and $2,430M in the half, and constant-currency growth was 27% rather than 28%.

Consensus estimates are moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS was cut 3.5% in 30 days and 3.3% in 90; current-year from $32.34 to $31.96; next year from $34.64 to $34.10. The 30-day cuts exceed the 90-day cuts across all four periods, meaning the revision pace has accelerated downward since the print. This is the inverse of the estimate-revision pattern that supports adding to a position: revenue is compounding while the earnings translation is being marked down.

Three growth vectors deserve separate treatment. First, ad monetization: impressions +14% and pricing +12% together is the strongest evidence in the filing, with online commerce the largest vertical contributor. Reels continues to monetize below Feed and Stories and the 10-Q expects that to persist, so engagement mix is a structural yield headwind. Second, non-advertising: FoA Other revenue +73% to $1,007M and Reality Labs +16% to $431M are growing fast off bases too small to matter. Reality Labs lost $8,647M in the half against $833M of revenue, roughly flat versus $8,739M, with FY2026 loss guided "similar to 2025"; the loss has stopped growing but has not shrunk. Third, new markets: the Muse Spark and Muse Image proprietary models and the "Meta Compute" cloud offering have no disclosed revenue line in the Q2 10-Q, and Meta enters AI coding and compute rental without incumbency in either.

The counterweight to the growth case is the depreciation already contracted for. Using an assumed 60/40 server/building mix on the $80,345M CIP balance at 5.5-year and 25-year lives gives roughly $10B of incremental annualized depreciation once placed in service — about $3.92 per share pretax, $3.30 after tax at 16%, or nearly 10% of FY2027 consensus EPS. That estimate depends on mix and in-service timing that Meta does not disclose and should be treated as a sensitivity. The direction is not in question.

## Competitive Landscape

The core auction business is strengthening. Simultaneous impression and price growth, 24% ARPP growth, and online commerce leading vertical contribution indicate the ranking and recommendation stack is improving relative to alternatives. TikTok is named in the filing as reducing engagement particularly among younger users, but is not visible as a revenue drag.

Three competitive positions are deteriorating, and they are what pull this dimension down from where it stood in April.

Compute input pricing is the first. Three separate disclosures converge: newly identified third-party AI token costs in R&D and marketing; most of the $349,310M of non-cancelable commitments relating to third-party cloud and technical infrastructure; and the 10-K's concession that "a substantial portion of our technical infrastructure is also provided by third parties." Meta buys compute at market prices while simultaneously building it at escalating cost. A reported ~$35.2B capacity contract with CoreWeave — under which Meta is the customer — is secondary-source and unconfirmed by filings, as is the reported no-resale clause.

The WhatsApp API remedy is the second. In June 2026 the European Commission imposed an interim measure requiring WhatsApp to offer Business API access free of charge to general-purpose AI providers, following a December 2025 investigation. Meta intends to appeal; similar investigations are open in Africa, Brazil and Turkey. The mechanism is direct: Meta's most defensible distribution asset is opened to competing AI assistants at the moment Meta is spending $130-145B annually to build its own. This is a structural remedy rather than a fine, and it is not obviously reflected in estimates.

Model strategy discontinuity is the third. The FY2025 10-K frames Llama and open-sourcing as core. Since then Meta launched Muse Spark and Muse Image as proprietary models with published API pricing characterized by its AI chief as aggressive versus Anthropic and OpenAI. Entering paid frontier-model APIs and AI coding places Meta against incumbents in markets where it has no distribution advantage and where price competition is already visible.

The reported July 2, 2026 town hall remark that AI agent development "hasn't really accelerated in the way that we expected" comes from a leaked recording and is not company-confirmed. If accurate it is highly consequential given who said it; it should not be treated as guidance.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation, with dated binary events.** The Q2 10-Q states that maximum aggregate damages sought across proceedings "could amount to an aggregate of up to hundreds of billions of dollars and, as a result, could be material to the financial condition of the company." Q2 G&A already carried $2,400M of legal charges. The calendar: Tennessee AG trial began July 20, 2026; the MDL state-AG trial is set for **August 12, 2026** (four states plus a federal disgorgement claim asserted by all 29 MDL state AGs); the New Mexico privacy trial is set for **September 8, 2026** with penalties sought up to $62.85B; two further user bellwethers October 28, 2026; school-district bellwether February 2027. Verdicts already rendered are far smaller than the asks — $6M compensatory with 70% allocated to Meta in the March 2026 JCCP bellwether, $375M civil penalty in New Mexico — and both are on appeal. Over 200,000 individual mass-arbitration demands have been filed since November 2024. The relevant risk is not the headline ask but that an August or September verdict forces a step-change in accruals within weeks.

**Tax.** A September 2025 Statutory Notice of Deficiency asserts $15.89B of additional tax for 2017-2019 plus interest and penalties, primarily transfer pricing; Meta petitioned the Tax Court in December 2025. Gross unrecognized tax benefits rose to $18,740M from $16,450M, plus $2,970M of accrued interest and penalties. The 2011-2013 notice remains pending, and a May 2025 Tax Court opinion valued transferred IP $1.48B above Meta's figure. Meta states its accrual is adequate; transfer-pricing notices routinely settle well below asserted amounts.

**Off-balance-sheet structures.** The Louisiana Venture: Meta holds 20%, funds its pro rata share of roughly $27B of development cost, leases from the venture beginning 2029 (~$12.31B initial commitment), provides residual value guarantees with a declining threshold of approximately $28B, and provides construction and property management services. Meta concluded it is not the primary beneficiary and does not consolidate. Disclosed maximum exposure to loss is $46,030M, with $6,410M across other unconsolidated VIEs. El Paso replicates the template — 20% interest, ~$2.3B of contributed assets, a ~$1B one-time distribution to Meta, RVGs to approximately $13B, and $1,270M of goodwill reclassified to held-for-sale. I draw no conclusion about motive; third-party capital and specialist developers plausibly buy speed at 5GW scale, and the non-consolidation conclusion appears well documented. The economic effect is that reported net cash of $6.6B and 0.32x debt/equity understate committed and contingent claims by roughly $59B of disclosed maximum VIE exposure.

**Commitments and duration mismatch.** Leases signed but not commenced went from $103,770M to $278,990M; non-cancelable commitments from $131,050M to $349,310M; plus roughly $68B of July 2026 leases commencing 2027-2028 with 18-20 year terms. The near-term binding tranches are $53,520M due in 2026 and $81,650M in 2027, affordable against expected operating cash flow. The longer issue is committing to 18-30 year leases at what may be a cyclical peak in construction and financing cost against hardware on a 5.5-year depreciation schedule. The WSJ reported on July 28, 2026 that a Meta data-center project priced a $12.5B offering at a higher rate than a comparable prior-year deal; I could not read the full article and venture-level debt terms are not in the filings.

**Regulatory.** The FTC's structural case seeking Instagram and WhatsApp divestiture was decided in Meta's favor on November 18, 2025, but the FTC appealed on January 20, 2026. The FTC consent-order administrative proceeding seeks to prohibit use of minors' data commercially, impose board-composition changes, and limit product launches, with a status update due by the earlier of 30 days after decision or September 8, 2026. The DMA "subscription for no ads" decision, the €798M Marketplace tying fine, DSA minors findings from April and July 2026, and the stayed €1.2B Irish DPC fine remain live. Europe is 23% of revenue.

**Governance.** Class B carries ten votes; 342M Class B shares command approximately 60.8% of voting power. There is no shareholder mechanism to alter the capex program.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($790, +41.9%).** The ad engine is compounding at 25%+ with 37% underlying margins and simultaneous volume and price growth, which is what a widening moat looks like in an auction business. At 16.3x FY2027 consensus the stock carries a 40% discount to Alphabet on a franchise with comparable durability. Capex has an identifiable ceiling: if 2027 spend is flat at $145B while operating cash flow compounds 20%+, free cash flow mechanically recovers past $50B and the $25.03B buyback authorization is available immediately. Reality Labs losses have stopped growing and mix is shifting to glasses, which sell. The litigation asks are orders of magnitude above the two verdicts actually rendered, and the FTC structural case has already been won at trial. The market cap that most screens display is 9% too low, so the stock is even more discounted than it appears on EV-based measures.

**Bear case ($430, −22.8%).** Free cash flow does not recover, because three effects compound: the $19,502M of accrued capex in payables converts to cash, cash taxes normalize from $1,999M per half toward the 15-17% book rate, and roughly $10B of annual depreciation from CIP begins hitting the P&L while 2027 capex steps up again to service the ~$696B of commitments. Estimates continue to be cut, so the 16x multiple compresses on a falling number. An adverse August or September verdict forces accruals materially above the $2,400M already booked. The EC's free WhatsApp API remedy erodes the distribution advantage. Revenue growth decelerates toward the guided 17-23% and then lower as monetization intensity meets advertiser ROI limits — with DAP growing only 3%, there is no user-growth cushion. Buybacks stay suspended, dilution continues at 0.7% per half, and the equity re-rates to a capital-intensive multiple.

**Where I stand.** Both cases turn on a single unobservable — the return on compute — and no filing contains the evidence. The base case assumes the ad engine holds at high-teens growth, roughly two-thirds of the depreciation drag lands in FY2027 earnings, and litigation resolves within a few billion dollars of current accruals: 19.4x on $32 of FY2027 EPS, or $620. That is +11.4% from the authoritative close and +5.1% from the last trade, against −22.8% in the bear case. The asymmetry is not attractive enough to add capital ahead of an August 12 trial, and the business is too strong and too cheap on earnings to sell.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** three dated checkpoints inside ten weeks — the MDL state-AG trial beginning August 12, 2026; the FTC consent-order status update due by September 8, 2026; and the New Mexico privacy trial beginning September 8, 2026. The Q3 print in late October 2026 is the first opportunity to see whether cash capex converges to the accrual pace and whether any buyback resumes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Resumption of repurchases at scale.** Any 10-Q or 8-K disclosure of $8B+ of quarterly repurchases from the intact $25.03B authorization would demonstrate that management sees the capex program as self-funding, and would arrest the dilution running at 0.7% per half.
2. **Free cash flow inflection with capex discipline.** Q3 2026 free cash flow above $10B for the quarter combined with FY2027 capex guidance at or below $145B. This is the single cleanest confirmation that the spend has a ceiling.
3. **Litigation resolution inside accruals.** Defense verdicts or settlements in the August and September trials that do not require accruals materially above the $2,400M booked in Q2, ideally alongside a global framework covering the 200,000+ arbitration claimants.
4. **Price.** Below roughly $480 — approximately 14x FY2027 consensus and 8% under the 52-week low — the base case alone would carry 29% upside and the depreciation drag would be substantially discounted.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **FY2027 capex guided above $160B**, or FY2026 free cash flow reported below zero. Either would confirm the commitment schedule is driving spend rather than the reverse.
2. **An adverse verdict requiring accruals above roughly $10B**, or any injunctive remedy in the FTC consent-order proceeding that constrains product launches or board composition.
3. **Ad revenue growth decelerating below 15% year over year** for two consecutive quarters, or price per ad turning negative. With DAP growing 3%, monetization intensity is the entire growth engine; its deceleration removes the offset to the depreciation step-up.
4. **Continued estimate cuts exceeding 5% over 90 days on FY2027**, which would confirm that the 16x forward multiple is on a number still in decline.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD and the overall score moves from 5.8 to 5.5. The reduction is not a response to the share price, which is unchanged from the prior review's basis of $556.71. It reflects the substance of the Q2 10-Q: commitments and signed-but-not-commenced leases grew roughly threefold in six months to about $696B; disclosed maximum exposure to loss across unconsolidated ventures reached $46,030M on Louisiana alone plus $6,410M elsewhere, with a further ~$13B of El Paso residual value guarantees pending; and Meta itself now states that litigation damages sought "could be material to the financial condition of the company," with four adverse-verdict-capable trials between August 2026 and February 2027.

Set against that, the operating business is performing about as well as it ever has. Twenty-eight percent revenue growth with impressions up 14% and pricing up 12% at once, 36.8% underlying operating margin, 24% ARPP growth, and a stock at 18.8x normalized trailing and 16.3x FY2027 consensus versus Alphabet at 27.2x. The GAAP miss was discrete charges, not operations.

The two facts are reconcilable. Meta has converted a business that returned $22.9B of buybacks in one half into one that raised $24.9B of debt in the next, and it has pre-committed capital claims equal to roughly half its equity value against hardware it depreciates over 5.5 years. Whether that produces returns is the only question that matters for the next twelve months, and it cannot be answered from the disclosures available. Owners should hold and monitor the four triggers above. New capital should wait for either the free-cash-flow and buyback confirmation, or a price closer to $480, recognizing that at the extended-hours level near $590, the base case offers only about 5% and the bear case about 27% of downside.
