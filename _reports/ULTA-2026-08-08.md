## Executive Summary

Ulta Beauty spent FY2024 and FY2025 growing revenue while shrinking operating profit. Net sales rose 10.6% cumulatively from $11,207.3M (FY2023) to $12,392.8M (FY2025), yet operating income fell 8.6% from $1,678.0M to $1,533.0M. Gross margin was flat at 39.1% across the period; the entire gap sits in SG&A, which deleveraged 256 basis points to 26.6% of sales on store payroll, restored incentive compensation, corporate overhead tied to an ERP replacement, and international build-out. Diluted EPS of $25.64 in FY2025 remains 1.5% below FY2023's $26.03 even after buybacks retired 9.3% of the share count. This margin compression drove the stock from $714.97 in February 2026 to $443.60 on June 30, and the score on this name has drifted from 6.8 in February to 5.9 in June.

The Q1 FY2026 print, filed June 2, is the first genuine counter-evidence. Net sales grew 11.1% to $3,163.9M on comps of +5.3% (ticket +3.7%, transactions +1.6%), gross margin expanded 100bp to 40.1%, and operating margin held at 14.2% versus 14.1% despite another 80bp of SG&A deleverage. Diluted EPS of $7.74 beat the $7.00 consensus by 10.6%. Decomposing the $1.04 year-over-year increase: at the prior-year diluted count of 45,508k shares EPS would have been $7.48, so the buyback contributed roughly $0.26 (25%); the effective tax rate fell 70bp to 23.9% on purchased transferable federal tax credits, worth roughly $0.07 (7%); the residual $0.71 (68%) is operating. Net income itself grew 11.6%. That is materially higher quality than FY2025, where net income fell 4.0% and effectively all EPS growth came from the share count. Management raised FY2026 EPS guidance to $28.36–$28.80 from an initial $28.05–$28.55 (externally reported; the figures reconcile exactly to the $25.64 base at the stated 10.6%–12.3% growth rates).

The problem is price. The shares have run 25.3% from the June 30 close of $450.98 to $565.15, which puts them at 19.6x the $28.78 current-year consensus, 19.8x the $28.58 guidance midpoint, and 17.7x the $31.87 next-year consensus. A $615 base case (19.3x next-year consensus) offers 8.8% upside; a resumption of SG&A deleverage combined with consumer softening supports a $445 bear case, 21.3% below spot. Two specific items argue against paying up here. First, consensus raised the full-year number by only $0.20 (from $28.58 to $28.78 over 90 days) despite a $0.742 Q1 beat, with the next-quarter estimate cut 4.1% — roughly $0.54 of the beat was reallocated out of the second half rather than added to the year. Second, the Target shop-in-shop wind-down completes in August 2026 and Ulta has never disclosed its dollar contribution; the revenue is bounded at under 1% of sales by inference, but royalty income carries near-full incremental margin, so $60–100M would equal 4–6.5% of FY2025 operating income. Fifth consecutive HOLD, with the rating now resting on earnings growth rather than any margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Ulta operates the largest specialty beauty platform in the United States, built to consolidate three historically separate channels (prestige, mass, and professional hair care) into one box carrying roughly 30,000 SKUs with a salon in nearly every location. At January 31, 2026 the company operated 1,591 stores (1,505 U.S. Ulta, 86 Space NK in the U.K./Ireland), rising to 1,608 by May 2, 2026. Unit growth has re-accelerated: 33 openings in FY2023, 66 in FY2024, 67 in FY2025, and 19 U.S. openings in Q1 FY2026 versus 6 in the prior-year quarter. All-in new-store investment averaged approximately $2.4M in FY2025.

Category mix in Q1 FY2026: cosmetics 40%, skincare/wellness 24%, haircare 18%, fragrance 12%, services 4%, other 2%. Fragrance is the only category gaining mix share; skincare gave back a point. Services have been stable at 4% for years, a differentiator but not a growth engine.

International arrived in 2025 through three distinct structures that should not be conflated. Space NK is a wholly owned, consolidated subsidiary acquired July 10, 2025 for $399.2M of net assets ($386.8M cash outflow net of cash acquired), producing $213.8M of goodwill and a $201.6M indefinite-lived trade name. Mexico is an equity-method joint venture that Ulta does not consolidate; its share of losses was $3.857M in FY2025 and $1.579M in Q1 FY2026 versus $0.628M a year earlier, so the loss run-rate is widening. The Middle East is a franchise arrangement generating royalties. The 10-K states international activities "are not significant to our financial results."

Strategy is framed in the 10-K as "Ulta Beauty Unleashed": grow the core, scale new accretive businesses (retail media via UB Media, and a third-party marketplace), and streamline the cost structure. Ulta reports one segment and discloses no revenue or margin for media or marketplace, so those initiatives cannot be sized from filings.

Space NK's consolidation mechanically dilutes the margin line, since a sub-$500M luxury U.K. retailer almost certainly runs below a $12B U.S. franchise. But mix cannot explain the pattern: FY2024 deleveraged 82bp with no Space NK in the base, and Q1 FY2026 deleveraged another 80bp. Segment expense disclosure identifies the mechanism directly — associate expenses of $1,714.7M in FY2025, up roughly 17.5% year over year against 9.7% sales growth. This is structural cost growth with an acquisition overlay, not an integration artifact.

Earnings quality supports taking the compression at face value. Operating cash flow of $1,502.8M exceeded net income by $349.3M in FY2025, and the 10-K reports no impairment charges on long-lived assets, goodwill, or intangibles in any of FY2023–FY2025. Ulta publishes no non-GAAP EPS, so there is no adjustment gap to litigate. The 256bp of deleverage is cash cost.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 OCF $1,502.8M less capex $434.8M gives FCF of $1,068.0M (8.6% FCF margin), versus $964.1M in FY2024 and $1,041.0M in FY2023. FY2026 capex is guided "not greater than $450 million." At $565.15, the FCF yield on market cap is 4.4%.

**Balance sheet:** At May 2, 2026, total assets $6,895.2M, liabilities $4,314.3M, equity $2,581.0M. Cash $166.3M plus $55.0M short-term investments, down from $424.2M of cash at year-end. Inventories $2,386.4M, up 12.5% year over year against 11.1% sales growth — acceptable, not pristine. Current ratio 1.31 versus 1.41 at January 31.

Debt is small but newly present. The $1.0B U.S. ABL revolver (matures March 13, 2029) is secured by substantially all company assets with a springing 1.0x fixed-charge covenant; outstanding was $0 at both year-ends and $102.0M at May 2, 2026 at a 7.25% weighted average rate. Space NK carries a £40.0M NatWest facility with subsidiary-level maintenance covenants (interest coverage ≥4.0x, leverage ≤2.0x), drawn $42.9M. Total borrowings $144.9M. Undiscounted operating lease obligations of $2,634.7M are the real fixed-obligation stack, against $2,119.8M capitalized on balance sheet. A derived Altman Z of approximately 6.5 confirms solvency is not the question.

Q1 FCF of $203.6M ($261.9M OCF less $58.3M capex) funded $545.3M of cash repurchases, with the gap covered by a $257.9M cash drawdown, $83.3M of net borrowing, and $15.0M of maturing investments. Q1 is seasonally Ulta's weakest cash quarter — working capital peaks August through November for the holiday build and Q4 is the harvest — so 2.7x FCF is a timing artifact rather than a run-rate. Normalized, FY2025 cash repurchases of $901.4M were 84% of FY2025 FCF. For the first time in the periods presented, Ulta is repurchasing stock while carrying borrowings on a facility whose covenants explicitly limit share repurchases.

**Returns:** ROE of 43.6% on average equity of $2,645.9M is flattered by buybacks charged against retained earnings ($555.0M in Q1 alone). ROA of roughly 17–18% on average assets is the cleaner read, and it is declining.

The supplied profile lists both trailing and forward P/E at 20.21 against an EPS field of $25.64, which implies $27.96 of earnings. The 20.21 figure is a forward calculation. Derived from filings, trailing P/E on $25.64 is 22.1x.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are verifiable and one is not.

**Comps.** +5.4% in FY2025 and +5.3% in Q1 FY2026, with the Q1 mix skewed to ticket (+3.7%) over transactions (+1.6%). Ticket-led comps in a category management itself describes as facing "persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures" are lower quality than traffic-led comps, and the 1.6% transaction growth is the number to watch as the consumer backdrop tightens.

**Units.** The doubling of the opening cadence from 33 (FY2023) to 66–67 (FY2024–25), with a 19-versus-6 Q1 acceleration, supplies non-comp revenue for the next several years at roughly $2.4M of investment per store. This is the most mechanical and reliable growth source in the model.

**Adjacencies.** UB Media and the marketplace are the credible high-margin offsets to a maturing store base, and they are also the least verifiable claims in the story. Ulta reports one segment and discloses nothing about either. Any margin-recovery model that leans on retail media is leaning on an undisclosed number.

**International.** Space NK contributed to the 11.1% Q1 growth rate, probably 2–3 points given comps of +5.3% plus new U.S. stores, though Ulta provided no pro forma disclosure. Mexico is losing money at a widening rate. The offset to the growth optionality is the $201.6M indefinite-lived trade name, which is non-amortizing and tested annually against projected Space NK revenues, so an international shortfall arrives as a discrete write-down.

**Consensus and the revision pattern.** Current-year consensus is $28.78 (20 analysts), next-year $31.87, implying 12.2% and 10.7% growth. Full-year revisions are modestly positive (+0.7% and +1.0% over 90 days), which does not clear the +30% threshold that would signal a genuine turn. The internal composition matters more: the current quarter was cut 1.9% and the next quarter 4.1% over 90 days, while the full year rose only $0.20 despite a $0.742 Q1 beat. Roughly $0.54 of that beat has been reallocated out of the second half. The next-quarter estimate covers the period in which the Target wind-down lands. Consensus at $28.78 also sits $0.20 above the $28.58 guidance midpoint, which is a modest setup risk rather than a comfort.

The beat/miss record is respectable but not uniform: +11.8%, +8.7%, −2.1%, +10.6% over the last four quarters. Three of four beats, with the miss in the seasonally largest (January) quarter.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat sources the filings actually support are scale in a fragmented category (which buys exclusive and pre-launch allocations), the prestige-plus-mass-plus-professional assortment spread that no single competitor replicates, off-mall real-estate density, and the loyalty program, which Item 1 characterizes as contributing roughly 95% of net sales from a member base in the mid-40-millions.

Against that, Item 1A concedes two things unusual for a category leader. The market has "few barriers to entry," and "many of our competitors are, and many of our potential competitors may be, larger and have greater financial, marketing, and other resources" — an explicit acknowledgment of Amazon, Walmart, Target, and LVMH-owned Sephora. The e-commerce risk factor is candid about cannibalization ("may do so in part by attracting existing guests, rather than new guests... thereby reducing the financial performance of our stores") and about vendors building competing direct channels.

That last point connects to the structural vulnerability. Top-ten brand partners supplied approximately 51% of net sales in FY2025 and 54% in FY2024, with no long-term supply agreements and an explicit warning that partners "could in the future decide to scale back or end their partnerships with us." The 3-point decline is not evidence of diversification — FY2025 includes roughly two quarters of Space NK revenue with a different brand mix in the denominator, so the ratio can fall while U.S. vendor dependence is unchanged. A changing revenue mix and a diversified supply base are different things, and only the former is established.

The Target relationship requires precision about roles. Target owned and operated the shop-in-shop space; Ulta licensed brand and assortment expertise and received royalty and commission revenue. Target was a commercial licensee, not a merchandise customer. On the August 2026 termination, Ulta loses a high-margin royalty stream, loses a loyalty-program acquisition funnel, and faces a former partner reportedly building an in-house beauty concept in those doors. BofA's July 29 note frames the exit as a growth opportunity on the theory that displaced guests migrate to Ulta's own channels. Both readings are plausible; neither is quantifiable from disclosure.

Item 1A now names "advances relating to AI and agentic commerce" as a competitive threat. For a retailer whose moat rests on a first-party loyalty data asset and physical convenience, AI-mediated purchasing is a channel-disintermediation risk that is not visible in any current financial metric.

Net trajectory: Ulta is still gaining modest share in a growing category, but the moat is being defended with cost (ERP, media, marketplace, payroll) rather than compounding on its own, and Q1's transaction growth of 1.6% is thin evidence of share capture. Stable-to-slightly-eroding.

## Risk Assessment

**Target wind-down (imminent, unquantified).** Item 1A contains no Target-specific risk factor, only generic trademark-licensing language, which suggests management judges the exposure immaterial. The "Other" revenue bucket that contains royalties and commissions totals 2% of net sales in both Q1 FY2026 and Q1 FY2025. No dollar figure has ever been disclosed; treat any specific number as unestablished.

**Supplier concentration.** 51% of net sales from ten brand partners, no long-term agreements, ongoing vendor consolidation in the industry. The single most important structural risk in the filings.

**Shrink normalization exhaustion.** Lower shrink has been cited as a gross margin driver in three consecutive periods. It has a floor and should not be extrapolated.

**Management continuity.** Nine Item 5.02 8-K filings between January 2025 and July 2026. Verified: CEO transition to Kecia Steelman (January 2025); CFO Paula Oyibo separation with Controller Christopher Lialios interim for roughly six months until Christopher DelOrefice's October 8, 2025 offer letter; a new Executive Severance Plan around December 18, 2025; and Kelly Garcia, an Ulta director since 2022 who signed the FY2025 10-K as a director, appointed CTO effective August 31, 2026. Three C-suite seats turning over in eighteen months, during the Target wind-down and international build-out, makes Item 1A's retention risk factor live. The director-to-CTO move has a benign reading (a known technologist appointed as AI risk rises) and an unfavorable one (reduced board independence, or difficulty recruiting externally).

**Buyback covenant ceiling.** The ABL is secured by substantially all assets and its covenants limit the ability to repurchase stock. Currently non-binding, but Ulta is now drawing on the facility while repurchasing, at a 7.25% cost of funds. Net interest swung from +$3.5M to +$0.7M year over year.

**Space NK impairment.** $201.6M indefinite-lived trade name plus $224.6M of goodwill, tested annually against projected Space NK revenues. An international disappointment surfaces as a discrete write-down.

**Distribution centralization and ERP.** Four DCs plus three fulfillment nodes with no direct-to-store vendor deliveries; management itself flags outsized single-node exposure. The recent ERP replacement carries both execution risk and persistent cost.

**Macro.** The 10-K and 10-Q state that "persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures have impacted consumer spending habits broadly" and that Ulta expects those pressures to continue in 2026. Beauty has been more resilient than many discretionary categories, but the consumer backdrop remains a live risk.

Litigation is not a factor: management states proceedings individually or in aggregate will not have a material adverse effect.

For an existing holder, buying September downside is inexpensive relative to realized volatility. Short interest of 5.7% of float and 3.4 days to cover means the tape is uncrowded on the short side, so there is no squeeze mechanic supporting the recent move.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($715, +26.5%)

The FY2024–25 margin compression was an investment cycle rather than a permanent reset, and Q1 FY2026 is the turn. Gross margin expanded 100bp to 40.1%, operating margin held despite continued SG&A deleverage, and 68% of the EPS increase was operating. Associate-expense growth of 17.5% against sales growth of 9.7% cannot persist indefinitely; as ERP, marketplace and international spending annualizes, sales growth of 8–10% against SG&A growth of 5–6% restores 100–150bp of operating margin over two years. Unit growth has doubled to a 66–67 store cadence with $2.4M investment per box, supplying reliable non-comp revenue. UB Media and the marketplace add high-margin revenue the market cannot yet see because Ulta reports one segment. The Target exit frees management attention and brand-partner allocation while displaced guests migrate into Ulta's own loyalty funnel. Capital allocation is genuinely accretive — $458.20 average repurchase cost versus $565.15 today, with $1.25B of authorization remaining equal to 5.1% of market cap, and negligible equity dilution (Q1 dilutive effect was 183 thousand shares, 0.4%). At 22x roughly $32.50 of FY2027 earnings, that is $715.

### Bear Case ($445, −21.3%)

Q1 is Ulta's smallest quarter and the 10-Q explicitly warns it is not indicative of the year. Two of the three margin drivers — lower shrink and a discretionary purchase of transferable federal tax credits worth roughly $0.07 per share — are normalization or one-off items. Consensus behavior corroborates the caution: analysts raised the full year by only $0.20 against a $0.742 beat and cut the next quarter 4.1%, reallocating roughly $0.54 out of the second half into which the Target wind-down lands. Structural cost growth persists: SG&A deleveraged 82bp in FY2024 without Space NK in the base and another 80bp in Q1 FY2026. Ulta concedes few barriers to entry, larger-resourced competitors, and that 51% of sales come from ten vendors with no long-term contracts and growing incentive to sell direct. Agentic commerce threatens the loyalty-plus-convenience moat directly. Three C-suite changes in eighteen months raise execution risk. On $27.50 of EPS at 16x, the stock returns to $445.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The thesis for holding is earnings growth, not multiple expansion or margin of safety.

- **Base case: $615** — 19.3x next-year consensus EPS of $31.87, in line with Ulta's typical non-crisis forward multiple and embedding no re-rating for a one-quarter margin recovery. **+8.8% from $565.15.**
- **Bull case: $715** — 22x approximately $32.50, requiring confirmed operating leverage across two more quarters. **+26.5%.**
- **Bear case: $445** — 16x approximately $27.50, just above the $443.60 52-week low. **−21.3%.**
- **Probability-weighted return (50/25/25): approximately +6%** over twelve months.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q2 FY2026 report (early September, ±11.8% implied) and the Q3 report that will contain the first full quarter without Target royalties.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which SG&A as a percentage of sales declines year over year, with gross margin at or above 39.5%. This is the single measurement that converts the margin-trough hypothesis into a thesis.
2. A price near $490–505 with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $31.50, which would put the shares below 16x next-year earnings and restore roughly 22% upside to base. That combination would warrant BUY.
3. Explicit quantification of the Target contribution alongside a reaffirmed or raised FY2026 guidance range, removing the largest unmeasured variable.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Comparable-sales transaction growth turning negative, or comps decelerating below +2.0%, indicating the ticket-led comp was pricing rather than demand.
2. FY2026 guidance cut below the current $28.36 low end, or full-year consensus falling below $28.00.
3. Sustained debt-funded repurchases — revolver borrowings above roughly $300M against continued buybacks — which would move the covenant restriction on repurchases from theoretical toward binding.
4. A Space NK trade-name or goodwill impairment, which would confirm the international thesis is not converting.

**Reassessment date:** the Q2 FY2026 earnings release in early September 2026.

Existing holders who want to keep exposure through the Target transition can hedge the September event at a below-realized cost. That is a position-management note, not a change to the rating.

## Conclusion

Ulta is a high-return, clean-accounting retailer whose operating profit fell 8.6% while revenue grew 10.6% over two years, and whose most recent quarter is the first credible evidence that the cost cycle has crested. The Q1 FY2026 decomposition matters: 68% operating, 25% buyback, 7% tax, with net income up 11.6% after a year in which it fell 4.0%. Financial health is strong: $1,068M of FY2025 free cash flow, $349M of positive accruals, no impairments in three years, a derived Altman Z near 6.5, and $145M of borrowings against $2.58B of equity.

What keeps this a hold at $565.15 is the combination of a compressed reward and two unresolved variables. The 25.3% recovery from the June 30 low leaves 8.8% to a $615 base against 21.3% downside if SG&A deleverage resumes. Consensus itself is signaling caution by moving $0.54 of the Q1 beat out of the second half. The Target wind-down completes within weeks and has never been quantified, and three C-suite seats have turned over in eighteen months. Competitive position and management quality are the dimensions that drove the score from 6.8 in February to 5.9 in June, and neither has produced the evidence needed to restore them: the vendor-concentration ratio improved only because the denominator changed, and there has been no open-market insider buying through a 21% drawdown from the high.

The read is a business that has probably passed its margin trough, trading at a price that already reflects that possibility. Own it if you own it; new capital should wait for either two quarters of confirmed SG&A leverage or a re-entry near $490–505.
