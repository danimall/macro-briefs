## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation is one of the largest private landowners in Texas, holding approximately 882,000 surface acres and 224,000 net royalty acres (NRA) concentrated in the Permian Basin. TPL earns oil and gas royalties requiring zero capital expenditure, collects water services revenue from operators drilling on its land, and grants easements across its surface acreage. The result is an asset-light operation generating 86% EBITDA margins with 114 employees and $798M in FY2025 revenue.

FY2025 revenue grew 13.1% despite a 15% decline in average WTI prices, as royalty production volumes surged 29% to 34.6 MBoe/d. Free cash flow reached $498M on minimal capex. The balance sheet carries effectively zero net debt (Altman Z-score 8.46) with a $500M undrawn revolver for acquisition flexibility. The $451M royalty acquisition in November 2025 — the largest in company history — will be accretive to 2026 volumes, and the Bolt Data & Energy investment ($50M) positions TPL at the intersection of energy infrastructure and AI/data center buildout.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. At ~59x trailing earnings and ~42x EBITDA, TPL trades at roughly 5x its E&P peer median P/E. This premium reflects the asset-light royalty model, irreplaceable land position, and data center optionality, but it also embeds optimistic assumptions about commodity prices, volume growth persistence, and speculative data center monetization. Oil markets face meaningful downside risk from a deteriorating global demand outlook and potential OPEC+ supply additions. The risk/reward at current levels skews to the downside unless the data center narrative converts to contractual revenue within the next 12 months.

## Company Overview

TPL traces its origins to 1888, organized as a business trust to manage lands formerly belonging to the Texas and Pacific Railway Company. The company reorganized as a Delaware C-corporation in January 2021 and was added to the S&P 500 in November 2024, replacing Marathon Oil.

**Revenue composition (FY2025):**
- Oil & Gas Royalties: ~52% ($413M) — perpetual non-participating royalty interests (NPRIs, principally 1/16th and 1/128th) requiring zero capex or opex
- Water Services & Operations: ~37% ($307M) — sourced water for completions ($170M) and produced water royalties ($124M)
- Easements & Surface Income: ~11% ($78M) — pipeline easements, power line easements, commercial leases, caliche/sand sales

The company operates two reportable segments: Land and Resource Management (62% of revenue) and Water Services and Operations (38%). Total employees: 114.

TPL completed a 3-for-1 stock split effective December 22, 2025.

### Speculative Upside: Data Center Optionality

TPL's $50M investment in Bolt Data & Energy positions it for potential data center development on West Texas surface acreage. The thesis: West Texas has abundant land, proximity to power generation (natural gas, solar, wind), and water resources critical for cooling. KeyBanc has raised its target to $639 on this basis.

Current status: Bolt is "currently pursuing commercial partnerships and anchor customers." It has no contracted revenue. TPL may contribute land for additional equity. Reports of potential data center development on TPL land remain unconfirmed. The $45.5M cumulative Transmissive desalination investment is complementary — providing treated water for industrial use — but Phase 2B construction was paused during 2025 and isn't expected to complete until mid-2026.

This optionality is real but unpriced in any rigorous way. The 10-K explicitly warns that Bolt is an early-stage company dependent on venture funding. The stock's move from ~$280 to ~$540 in February-March 2026 appears driven substantially by data center enthusiasm. The subsequent correction to ~$410 suggests the market is partially unwinding the speculative premium.

TPL trades at an extraordinary premium to every E&P comparable. The premium is partially justified: the royalty model has zero capex requirements, 86% EBITDA margins, no operating cost exposure, and no reserve depletion risk (TPL's minerals are perpetual — operators deplete their own reserves while TPL's interest endures). TPL's competitive moat — 882,000 surface acres in the most productive basin on Earth — is irreplicable.

On a forward earnings basis using the FY2026 consensus of $9.56 EPS, TPL trades at ~43x. Using FY2027 consensus of $10.60, it trades at ~39x.

A DCF approach with $500M normalized FCF, 8% WACC, and 3% terminal growth yields approximately $100B enterprise value — well above the current $29B market cap. However, this is mechanically sensitive to discount rate assumptions and requires believing perpetual volume growth. A more conservative scenario using $500M FCF, 10% discount rate, and 2% terminal growth yields roughly $62B — still supportive but with much less margin of safety given the commodity cycle.

The core valuation question: does TPL deserve a "luxury goods" multiple for an energy business? The royalty structure, scale, and emerging data center optionality argue yes. The direct commodity price exposure, dependence on third-party operator drilling decisions, and 8.2% short interest argue the premium is at risk.

At ~43x FY2026E, a compression to 30x (still a massive premium to peers) on current estimates implies ~$287, or ~30% downside. A rerating to 50x on rising estimates ($10.60 FY2027E) implies ~$530, or ~29% upside. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric at current prices, but the downside scenario is more probable given the deteriorating macro environment for oil.

## Competitive Landscape

TPL has **no direct peers**. The 10-K states that the Land and Resource Management segment has "few direct competitors." The closest analogues are smaller mineral rights companies (VNOM, BSM, MNRL), but none approach TPL's surface acreage scale or operational breadth.

Key structural advantages:
1. **Irreplaceable land position:** 882,000 surface acres cannot be replicated. Adjacent surface ownership is fragmented.
2. **Zero-capex royalty income:** Operators bear 100% of drilling costs; TPL collects royalty revenue passively.
3. **Water monopoly dynamics:** TPL's surface footprint gives it structural advantages in water sourcing, disposal, and rights-of-way. Operators cannot access water or dispose of produced water without surface rights.
4. **Easement annuity stream:** 30+ year pipeline and power line easements with CPI escalators create inflation-protected recurring revenue.
5. **S&P 500 inclusion:** Ensures institutional ownership, liquidity, and index fund demand.

Customer concentration (~40% from three operators) is a risk but reflects the structural reality of the Permian — a limited number of major operators control the majority of activity.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Risk (HIGH)
TPL's royalty revenue is directly linked to oil and gas prices. WTI has declined from $76.63 in FY2024 to $65.39 in FY2025. OPEC+ supply additions, potential demand destruction from recession (US consumer sentiment at record lows), and a strong dollar all pressure crude prices. A sustained move below $60 WTI would reduce royalty revenue, slow operator drilling activity, and compress water services demand simultaneously. The current Hormuz disruption and naval blockade support oil above $100 near term, but TPL's volumes would still face headwinds if operators pull rigs in response to geopolitical uncertainty.

### Operator Drilling Activity (MODERATE-HIGH)
Permian horizontal rig counts have already declined from 296 to 257 average weekly rigs in 2025. TPL production grew 29% despite this, aided by longer laterals and efficiency gains, but this offset has limits. If rig counts fall further toward 200, volume growth will decelerate or reverse.

### Valuation Compression Risk (HIGH)
At ~59x trailing earnings, any disappointment in commodity prices, volume growth, or the data center narrative would likely trigger meaningful selling. The February-March 2026 spike to $540 and subsequent correction to ~$410 demonstrates how quickly sentiment can shift. Short interest at 8.2% with 7.7 days to cover is elevated and reflects institutional skepticism about valuation sustainability.

### Transmissive/Bolt Execution Risk (MODERATE)
$95M+ committed to unproven ventures ($45.5M Transmissive, $50M Bolt). Transmissive construction was paused in 2025; Bolt has no customers. Total exposure is manageable relative to TPL's FCF but the data center narrative driving the stock's premium depends on Bolt's success.

### Regulatory Risk (MODERATE)
Texas Railroad Commission's Seismic Response Areas limit saltwater disposal injection. Further restrictions could constrain produced water disposal volumes. However, this also creates opportunity for TPL's desalination technology.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term IV of 55.1% trades rich to 47% historical vol (8pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (55.1% near-term vs. 45.0% at 3 months). ## Earnings Surprise Analysis

The reported earnings surprise data requires careful interpretation. The Q2 and Q3 2025 "misses" of -72.7% and -70.6% appear to be artifacts of the December 2025 3-for-1 stock split — analysts likely had pre-split estimates that weren't adjusted in the dataset. The most recent quarter (Q4 2025) showed a 3.0% beat ($1.80 actual vs. $1.75 estimate), which is modest but positive.

Estimate revisions are meaningfully positive: current-year FY2026 EPS consensus has risen 15.7% in 30 days to $9.56, and FY2027 consensus is up 20.2% over 90 days to $10.60. This accelerating revision trajectory likely reflects the full-year contribution from the November 2025 royalty acquisition and water segment momentum.

### Why Not BUY

Despite the 6.8 overall score reflecting genuine business quality, the specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation is **valuation asymmetry in a deteriorating oil environment.**

**TPL requires sustained WTI above $75 AND visible data center revenue within 12 months to justify its current ~43x forward multiple.** WTI is currently trading in the mid-$60s on a trailing basis and has been declining throughout FY2025. While the Hormuz blockade introduces near-term price support, every prior conflict-driven oil spike during this crisis has reversed within 72 hours to 2 weeks. If WTI averages $65 in FY2026 (consistent with the recent trend), TPL's royalty revenue per Boe compresses further, and the $9.56 FY2026 EPS consensus is at risk of downward revision — despite volume growth. Simultaneously, Bolt has zero customers and zero contracted revenue. A premium multiple dependent on speculative optionality plus direct commodity exposure in a macro environment where recession probability is 45-50% creates asymmetric downside.

If WTI averages above $80 for two consecutive quarters AND Bolt announces a contracted anchor customer, the thesis shifts to BUY. Until those conditions are met, the quality of the underlying business is not sufficient to overcome the valuation risk.

## Conclusion

TPL is a genuinely exceptional business: an irreplaceable Permian Basin landholding generating $498M FCF with 86% EBITDA margins, zero required capex on its core royalty stream, and emerging optionality in data center infrastructure. The 29% production volume growth in FY2025 despite lower oil prices demonstrates the franchise's resilience, and accelerating analyst estimate revisions (+16% in 30 days) confirm positive fundamental momentum.

The constraint is price. At ~43x FY2026 estimated earnings — a 5x premium to the E&P peer median — the stock prices in both premium business quality and speculative data center upside that has not yet materialized. With oil prices under pressure from demand weakness (record-low consumer sentiment, 45-50% recession probability) and the data center narrative resting on an early-stage venture with no customers, the risk/reward at $410 is roughly symmetric. The business quality justifies holding for investors with existing positions, but initiating new positions requires either a valuation correction or fundamental catalysts that convert speculation to contracted reality. HOLD with a positive 12-month growth orientation.
