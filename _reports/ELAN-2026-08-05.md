## Executive Summary

Elanco has spent roughly seven years as a leveraged carve-out with a deteriorating profit base. The August 5 Q2 2026 report is the clearest evidence yet that this has changed. Revenue grew 10% reported and 8% organic constant currency, adjusted EBITDA rose 21% to $288M with margin up 200bp to 21.2%, adjusted EPS of $0.34 beat the $0.27 consensus by 23.8% — the fourth consecutive beat — and management raised FY2026 guidance for the second time this year, to $5,090-5,140M revenue, $1,010-1,035M adjusted EBITDA and $1.10-1.16 adjusted EPS. At the midpoint, FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1,022M would be up 13% and would be the first annual increase since at least 2021, reversing a decline from $1,059M (2021) to $901M (2025). Reported net leverage fell 0.5x in six months to 3.1x on the company's definition (3.39x including the HQ finance lease), a fourth-quarter 2025 rating upgrade cut the coupon on the 2028 notes to 6.40%, and the two largest litigation overhangs — the Zenrelia securities class action and the Bayer-inherited Tevra antitrust verdict — both resolved in Elanco's favor within the last five months.

The problem is what the raised guidance implies about the second half. H1 2026 revenue grew 13% and adjusted EBITDA 21%. Subtracting H1 actuals from the new full-year range leaves H2 revenue of roughly $2,351-2,401M against approximately $2,281M a year earlier (+3% to +5%) and H2 adjusted EBITDA of $388-413M against $387M (+0.3% to +6.7%). Some of that is arithmetic conservatism from a management team that has beaten four straight quarters by an average of about 24%. Some of it is real: H1 carried roughly one point of FX tailwind in Q2 on a revenue base that is 52% non-USD, comparisons harden, and January 2026 brought an at-risk US generic launch against Seresto more than a year before the September 2027 formulation patent expires. Seresto printed $117M of Q2 revenue with no year-over-year comparative disclosed; on the company's own 70%-in-H1 seasonality it is plausibly a $400M+ annual, explicitly higher-margin franchise, and the erosion rate is the single most important undisclosed number in the file.

At 22.7x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint and about 16.0x EV to guided adjusted EBITDA, the market already pays for the inflection continuing. Consensus at $1.07 for the current year sits below the new guidance floor and will mechanically move up, and 16 of 17 covering analysts are at buy or strong buy with a $30.07 mean target. That is corroboration, not independent confirmation. Base case $28.50 (+11.3%), bull $34.00 (+32.8%), bear $19.00 (-25.8%). Balanced asymmetry on a business whose legacy earnings are protected by brand and channel rather than composition-of-matter patents supports a fifth HOLD, classified as a growth hold: the 12-month thesis is positive, the entry price is not.

## Company Overview

Elanco is a pure-play animal health company, independently incorporated in 2018 and separated from Eli Lilly in March 2019, which acquired Bayer Animal Health in August 2020 — the transaction that created most of the $4,845M of goodwill and $3.4B of net intangibles on the balance sheet and most of the leverage. It operates as one reportable segment, selling roughly 200 brands in more than 90 countries through 16 manufacturing sites and about 140 contract manufacturers, with approximately 9,400 full-time employees and a 2,200-person sales force.

FY2025 revenue of $4,715M split Pet Health $2,300M (49%), Farm Animal $2,362M (50%) and contract manufacturing $53M. The United States was 47% of revenue; Brazil, the UK and China are the largest international markets, and 51% of FY2025 revenue (52% in H1 2026) was denominated in non-USD currencies. Concentration is meaningful on both ends: the top five product families — Advantage Family, Seresto, Credelio Family, Rumensin, Maxiban/Monteban — were about 38% of revenue, and an affiliate of Cencora was roughly 12% of revenue with a second distributor at 7%, so about 19% of sales pass through two counterparties.

Two structural features define the equity case. First, a large share of the legacy base has no composition-of-matter protection at all: the 10-K discloses that Advantage Family (about 10% of revenue), Rumensin and Maxiban/Monteban are unpatented, Experior's US active-ingredient patent expired April 2025, and Galliprant's active-ingredient patents have expired in most markets. The base erodes by default, which is why the company must keep launching. Second, the omnichannel distribution model — vet clinics, distributors, big-box retail, specialty pet and e-commerce for products including Advantage and Seresto — is a genuine differentiator against vet-channel-dependent competitors, though management concedes retail-channel margins may be lower.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings are not the operating story, and the reason is specific.** FY2025 produced a $232M net loss on 6% revenue growth with gross margin essentially flat at 55.0% versus 54.9%. The bridge from the income statement: gross profit $2,593M less R&D $368M less selling/marketing/administrative $1,430M less intangible amortization $543M less impairment and restructuring $237M equals $15M of operating income; less $19M other expense and $220M interest gives a $224M pretax loss, and an $8M tax charge yields the $232M net loss. The same year generated $560M of operating cash flow and $284M of free cash flow. Roughly $8.2B of the $13.4B asset base is Bayer-deal goodwill and intangibles amortizing at about $550M annually. Any screen keyed to GAAP net income, ROA or operating margin will misread this business — and the trailing P/E of -52.5 in the supplied data is arithmetically meaningless.

Amortization exclusion is not free, and the FY2025 charge detail shows why: $47M of the $237M was a marketed-product intangible impairment "due to a decline in projected sales of a product group acquired in a past acquisition." Some Bayer intangibles are impairing because the underlying products are worth less. The remaining charges were $155M for the December 2025 restructuring plan ($116M cash severance, $39M non-cash for Monheim, Germany R&D and Kansas City manufacturing) and $16M for two suspended capital projects.

**The five-year profit record is the bear case in one table.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosure gives adjusted EBITDA of $1,059M (2021), $1,017M, $979M, $910M and $901M (2025) — down 14.9% while revenue was flat to up, with implied margin falling from about 22% to 19.1%. Part of the 2024-25 step-down is the July 2024 aqua divestiture. Most is operating: selling/marketing/administrative expense rose from $1,265M to $1,430M (+13%) on 7% revenue growth, as management front-loaded launch spending for Zenrelia, Credelio Quattro and AdTab. Over the same window total shareholder return indexed to 74 against a peer index of 188.

**H1 2026 breaks that trend in the reported numbers.** Revenue $2,739M (+13%), gross margin 57.8% (+40bp), adjusted EBITDA $622M (+21%) at 22.9% margin (+170bp), adjusted EPS $0.75 (+19%), GAAP net income $111M versus $78M. Q2 growth decomposed as price +2%, FX +1%, volume +6%, acquisition +1%. TTM adjusted EBITDA reached $1,008M at June 30.

Two cautions on the H1 numbers. Seasonality is material and disclosed: roughly 70% of Seresto's and 60% of Advantage Family's full-year revenue lands in H1 on Northern Hemisphere flea and tick season, and both are described as higher-margin — so 22.9% H1 margin overstates the full-year run rate, which guidance implies near 20%. Cash flow is similarly back-weighted: H1 operating cash flow was $290M with Q2 alone at $277M, implying roughly $13M in Q1, because working capital absorbed about $239M as receivables built from $873M to $1,077M. Inventory has now risen across three consecutive balance-sheet dates ($1,574M, $1,737M, $1,769M) despite accelerating revenue. That may be deliberate build for Credelio Quattro's international launches and Befrena's ramp; it is the line item I would probe next quarter.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt at June 30 was $3,663M net of issuance costs, plus a $255M HQ finance lease, against $530M of cash. Weighted-average effective rate 5.79%, roughly 80% fixed or swapped, $750M revolver fully undrawn to July 2029, in compliance with all covenants. The October 2025 refinancing retired the $2,102M 2027 term loan into 2029-2032 maturities; nothing material is due before the $750M 2028 notes and a $336M 2028 incremental facility. Management reports net leverage of 3.1x by excluding the finance lease; including it gives $3,414M net debt over $1,008M TTM adjusted EBITDA, or 3.39x. Both belong in the file, and management has said it expects to exercise a $250M HQ purchase option at the end of the lease. The stated target is roughly 3.0x at year-end 2026 and below 3.0x during 2027.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.78 flagging "distress" is mechanically distorted here: EBIT/assets is near zero because of $543M of non-cash amortization, and revenue/assets of 0.35 is depressed by an acquisition-inflated denominator. The credit evidence — covenant compliance, an undrawn revolver, a Q4 2025 rating upgrade that cut the 2028 note coupon to 6.40%, no maturities before 2028, and $560M of annual operating cash flow — is the better guide. Bankruptcy risk over 24 months looks low. Refinancing risk in a severe downside is real but currently well managed.

Two financing items deserve explicit weight. The May 2025 Blackstone monetization of US XDEMVY royalties raised $290M net at a disclosed 18.3% effective rate, and the liability grew from $304M to $315M in H1 2026 because $29M of imputed interest exceeded $18M of royalty revenue recognized — revenue Elanco books but does not receive. Whether the accretion reflects a back-weighted royalty schedule or underperformance versus the deal model cannot be determined from the disclosures. Either way, this is debt-like capital at roughly triple Elanco's marginal borrowing cost. Second, $2,300M notional of interest rate swaps mature in August 2026, replaced by $1,450M of forward-starting swaps — an $850M reduction in hedged notional that increases floating-rate exposure.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three products and a cost program.

**Zenrelia**, an oral JAK inhibitor for canine dermatitis, reached blockbuster status on a July year-to-date basis, is used in roughly 18,000 US clinics, and has gained nine points of US JAK share year over year with sequential gains versus Q1 and claimed 40%+ share in key European markets. Management calls it the single largest contributor to global growth. It achieved that against an FDA-mandated boxed warning that the company's own risk factors say slowed initial US adoption, and the FDA has since concluded certain warning language may be removed.

**Credelio Quattro** has penetrated over 50% of the US clinic base (up about 10 points and 3,000 clinics versus Q1), gained four points of dollar share in US vet-clinic broad-spectrum sales, launched in Australia, Canada and Japan in Q2 with EU and UK submissions pending, and carries a conditional FDA approval for New World screwworm.

**Befrena**, a USDA-approved anti-IL31 monoclonal antibody launched in Q2, has shipped to nearly 1,400 US clinics with demand running "2x above expectations" and capacity as the binding constraint — which retroactively justifies the FY2025 capex step-up to $276M for the Elwood, Kansas biologics facility. The caution is that early launch demand is frequently inflated by clinic stocking, a distortion the 10-K itself repeatedly flags.

Farm animal is a quieter positive: cattle grew 17% reported and 12% organic CC in Q2, the fastest species, aided by Rumensin, poultry vaccines and Experior's October 2024 heifer clearances (heifers are nearly 40% of the US fed-cattle population). Swine declined 4% organic CC. The AHV International acquisition, closed April 30 for $243M total consideration ($76M cash, $100M deferred through 2030, $67M fair value of up to $140M contingent — all cash, no dilution), contributed about one point to Q2 growth and is expected to matter more from 2027. Note 56% of consideration was booked as goodwill.

**Elanco Ascend**, announced with the Q2 release, targets $200-250M of adjusted EBITDA net savings by 2030 — roughly 20-25% of current EBITDA. Mechanics, phasing and one-time costs are not yet in a filing, and it is distinct from the 2025 restructuring plan's approximately $60M of 2027 savings. Treat it as a stated ambition, not a modeled input.

**The deceleration in the guide is the central growth question.** Management raised the year, yet the implied H2 sets revenue growth at roughly 3-5% and adjusted EBITDA growth at 0-7%, against H1's 13% and 21%. Working the EPS path: H1 delivered $0.75, Q3 is guided $0.19-0.22, so implied Q4 is roughly $0.13-0.22 against $0.13 actual in Q4 2025. The full year therefore hinges on the seasonally thinnest quarter. Given four consecutive beats averaging about 24%, conservatism is the likelier explanation. But the alternative — that base erosion from Seresto and the unpatented parasiticide franchise is starting to show as FX support fades — cannot be ruled out from disclosed data. Management's own phrasing, that innovation has "stabilized our base business," implies the base is flat rather than growing.

Guided organic constant-currency growth of 6-7% for FY2026 sits at or modestly above management's stated mid-single-digit industry CAGR, consistent with genuine share capture. Estimate revisions corroborate direction without signalling inflection: current-year consensus +3.1% and next-year +2.5% over 90 days. This is not the >30% revision velocity that marks a mispriced earnings trough.

## Competitive Landscape

Animal health is a consolidated oligopoly. Elanco names Zoetis, Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health and Merck Animal Health as primary competitors, plus generics and nutritional-health producers. At roughly $5.1B of guided 2026 revenue, Elanco is a clear number two or three by scale, and the proxy's peer TSR index of 188 against Elanco's 74 confirms that the sector compounded while Elanco did not. That framing matters: the industry is healthy; Elanco's relative position deteriorated on execution and leverage and may now be recovering.

The decisive front is companion-animal dermatology, a category Zoetis built with Apoquel and Cytopoint. Elanco is attacking both modalities — Zenrelia against the oral JAK and Befrena against the anti-IL31 antibody — and is taking share on company-sourced data (Kynetec for US JAK share; internal estimates for the European claim, as Elanco's own footnote states). It is doing so from a label handicap that has since eased. I did not review Zoetis disclosures, so the mirror image of these share shifts is unverified.

In parasiticides the strategy is a managed franchise migration: an eroding, largely unpatented, retail- and e-commerce-distributed OTC base (Advantage Family, Seresto) into a differentiated prescription vet-channel product (Credelio Quattro, covering fleas, ticks, heartworm, three tapeworm species, and conditionally New World screwworm). The logic is sound; the arithmetic is unforgiving. Credelio Quattro must out-earn the Seresto and Advantage decline, and by management's own description innovation has stabilized rather than grown the base. The channel is also shifting away from veterinary distribution for flea and tick products, where the 10-K concedes retail margins may be lower, while distributor and buying-group consolidation presses price from the other side.

Pricing power is intact in aggregate: +2% price in both Pet Health and Farm Animal in Q2, with guidance for an accelerating price contribution versus 2025.

## Risk Assessment

**Seresto generic erosion (highest-consequence, unquantified).** A competitor began selling a generic Seresto competitor in the US in January 2026, more than a year before the September 2027 formulation patent expires. European formulation patents expired June 2025, with supplementary protection certificates in Spain, Italy and the UK expiring August 2026. Seresto printed $117M in Q2 with no prior-year comparative disclosed; on 70%-in-H1 seasonality it is plausibly a $400M+ annual, higher-margin franchise, meaning gross margin would be hit harder than revenue. Total Pet Health grew 11% organic CC in Q2, so erosion has not prevented segment acceleration — but the magnitude is unknown and should not be assumed benign.

**Patent breadth beyond Seresto.** Advantage Family (about 10% of revenue), Rumensin and Maxiban/Monteban carry no patent protection. Interceptor Plus runs to October 2028.

**Concentration of the growth case.** Zenrelia, Credelio Quattro and Befrena carry the entire 2026 growth story. Zenrelia's boxed-warning history means its safety profile is already an area of regulatory attention.

**FX and macro.** With 52% of revenue non-USD, a stronger dollar would reverse the modest FX tailwind (+1% to revenue in Q2). Brazil is a large market, and emerging-market currency stress is a live risk. Pet health also carries indirect exposure to discretionary consumer spending through the retail and e-commerce channel.

**Rate exposure.** The August 2026 swap maturity cuts hedged notional by $850M, raising floating exposure at the margin. Separately, CHF cross-currency net investment hedges carried roughly $155M of cumulative losses at year-end 2025 with tenors in August and November 2026 and February 2027; CHF 200M of the August maturity was extended to 2031 in July, and derivative liabilities in other current liabilities rose from $111M to $158M.

**Bovaer.** A designated blockbuster whose commercial thesis was undercut by policy rather than product failure: sustainability adoption incentives were frozen or rescinded, adoption has been "tempered," additional studies are underway, and management warns of possible sales at minimal or negative gross margin plus inventory and asset write-offs. The Q2 10-Q repeats this materially unchanged.

**Litigation — improved, not final.** Barpar (Zenrelia securities) was dismissed with prejudice on March 26, 2026 with a Fourth Circuit appeal pending and four derivative suits stayed behind it; Tevra's Bayer-inherited antitrust verdict was affirmed by the Ninth Circuit July 6 with rehearing denied July 31; Safron was closed when the Indiana Supreme Court denied appeal February 26 (cert petition filed May 15); Kraus-Silfen and Spradlin settled in principle for a non-material amount. No material liabilities are booked. Two of the three largest overhangs cleared within five months — a genuine de-risking that the equity may not have fully priced.

**Tail risks.** Shared-class and animal-only antibiotics were 24% of FY2025 revenue, with 89% of the animal-only portion ionophores spared regulation to date; a reclassification would sweep in Rumensin and Maxiban/Monteban. The December 2025 BIOSECURE Act could raise R&D costs with no quantification provided. Pension plans in deficit showed a $154M shortfall. Tariffs are currently immaterial with animal health largely carved out of the April 2026 Section 232 action, but the regime is unstable.

**Governance.** CEO granted compensation rose from $12.06M to $14.93M (+24%) across five years in which the company's own selected performance measure fell 15% and TSR indexed to 74 against peers at 188. Realized pay is genuinely equity-sensitive (−$2.02M actually paid in 2022; $37.81M in 2025 on the share recovery off a depressed base), and metric selection — adjusted EBITDA, Elanco Cash Earnings, adjusted EBITDAR, TSR — is well suited to a leveraged deleveraging story. A 2024 cooperation agreement added two investor-nominated directors; the activist is described in filings only as "an investor." Insider activity across the last 25 Form 4s contains no open-market purchases and no discretionary sales — deferred-stock-unit accruals, director grants and one CFO tax-withholding disposition. That is genuinely uninformative in both directions.

The six-month tenor shows negative skew (−4.3%) and a ±27.3% implied move, which is consistent with the market treating the Seresto and 2027 patent question as the longer-dated uncertainty rather than the near-term print. None of this changes the fundamental read; it does argue against paying up into the post-print move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($34, +32.8%).** Five years of adjusted EBITDA decline ended in 2026, and the H2 guide proves conservative in the pattern of four consecutive beats. Zenrelia, Credelio Quattro and Befrena compound as international launches annualize, Seresto erosion stays contained because the franchise is defended by brand and channel rather than exclusivity, restructuring delivers $60M in 2027 and Elanco Ascend begins to show against its $200-250M-by-2030 target. Capex steps down from $276M to $175-200M, receivables convert in H2, and free cash flow funds net debt reduction through 3.0x during 2027 — the stated unlock for capital allocation flexibility, which in this credit means buybacks or bolt-ons rather than a dividend. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $1,140M and the multiple re-rates modestly toward animal health peers as the GAAP loss converts to GAAP profit and the amortization drag begins to roll off. Cleared litigation removes both a financial and a narrative overhang from the product now driving all the growth.

**Bear case ($19, −25.8%).** The H2 guide is honest, not conservative, and the 21% H1 adjusted EBITDA growth was flattered by seasonality and initial stocking on three launches. Seresto erosion accelerates through the August 2026 European SPC lapse and the September 2027 US expiry, landing on the highest-margin revenue in the portfolio, while Advantage Family, Rumensin and Maxiban remain fully exposed to generics. FX reverses as the dollar firms, the August swap roll raises floating-rate cost, and CHF hedge settlements consume cash. Bovaer requires incremental study spend with no subsidy tailwind and risks inventory write-offs. Working capital fails to release, inventory at $1,769M proves to contain slow-moving legacy stock, and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA stalls near $980M. At 14x EV on that number with net debt near $3.3B the equity is worth about $19, and the 22.7x forward multiple on adjusted earnings that exclude $550M of annual amortization compresses toward the low-to-mid teens.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The operating inflection is confirmed in reported data — that is not in dispute. What is unresolved is durability, and management's own raised guidance is the primary evidence for caution: it implies H2 adjusted EBITDA growth of 0-7% after H1's 21%. Paying 16x EV/EBITDA and 22.7x forward adjusted EPS for a business whose next four quarters are guided to roughly flat second-half profit growth, with an unquantified patent cliff arriving in 2027, gives approximately 11% base-case upside against 26% bear-case downside. The prior four HOLDs were issued at lower conviction on the operating trend; this one is issued at higher conviction on the trend and lower conviction on the entry price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price toward $22-23** with fundamentals intact, which would put the stock near 19-20x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint and roughly 14.5x EV to guided adjusted EBITDA — a level at which the base case offers over 25% upside and the Seresto downside is largely absorbed.
2. **Q3 (late October/early November) or Q4 disclosure that quantifies Seresto's year-over-year trend as a low-single-digit revenue headwind or better**, ideally with a third-party share reference. This is the single largest unknown; resolving it favorably would justify a higher multiple directly.
3. **FY2027 initial guidance of adjusted EBITDA at or above $1,100M with reported net leverage at or below 3.0x**, delivered without relying on FX. That would establish the inflection as a trend rather than a single guided year and confirm the deleveraging unlock.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A cut to FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance below $1,010M**, or Q3 adjusted EPS below the $0.19 guidance floor — either would break the four-quarter beat pattern that underpins confidence in the H2 ramp.
2. **Pet Health organic constant-currency growth decelerating below 4%** for two consecutive quarters, which would indicate Credelio Quattro and Zenrelia are no longer out-earning base erosion.
3. **Reported net leverage rising above 3.4x**, a covenant amendment, or evidence of adverse hedge settlement cash outflows materially exceeding the roughly $155M cumulative loss position.
4. **Reinstatement of the Barpar securities class action by the Fourth Circuit**, which would revive four stayed derivative suits and re-open the Zenrelia credibility question.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 report (late October/early November, first clean read on the guided H2 deceleration and on receivable conversion), the August 2026 swap roll and European Seresto SPC lapse, and FY2027 guidance in February 2027. Twelve-month horizon.

## Conclusion

Elanco has done the operationally difficult thing: it has broken a five-year profit decline while carrying 3.4x leverage, launched three products into categories dominated by a stronger competitor, refinanced its nearest maturity wall five years out, earned a rating upgrade, and cleared two large litigation overhangs. Q2 delivered a fourth consecutive beat and a second guidance raise, and adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1,022M at the FY2026 midpoint would be the first annual increase since at least 2021.
