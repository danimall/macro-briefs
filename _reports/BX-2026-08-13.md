## Executive Summary

Blackstone delivered a strong second quarter and the market has now largely paid for it. Total AUM reached a record $1.35 trillion at June 30, 2026, distributable earnings rose 26% to roughly $2.0 billion ($1.52 per share), fee-related earnings rose 22% to $1.29 per share, and inflows were $70 billion in the quarter and $260 billion over the trailing twelve months — the latter equal to roughly 21% of AUM. GAAP revenues of $5.037 billion were up about 36% year over year, with realized performance allocations of $1.365 billion up roughly 64%. Management and advisory fees, net, grew 11.3% to $2.266 billion. The proximate cause of the stock's earlier drawdown — BCRED, the ~$82 billion non-traded credit fund, breaching its 5% quarterly repurchase cap twice (7.9% of NAV in Q1, 10% in Q2) — appears to be easing, with management characterizing Q3 requests as "down materially" and BREIT simultaneously posting $1.2 billion of Q2 inflows with repurchases down 42% year over year, its best net flow quarter in four years.

The issue is that the recovery has already happened. From the March 12 low of $101.73 the shares are up roughly 44%, and up about 17% in the three weeks since mid-July. At $145.945 the stock trades above both the consensus mean target of $142.38 and the median of $140.00, with the sell-side distribution shifting toward the middle (11 holds versus 9 three months ago, strong buys down from 4 to 3). Estimate revisions do not corroborate the rally: the current-quarter DE estimate was cut 7.8% over 30 days, next-year consensus slipped 0.8%, and full-year 2026 consensus of $6.02 per share sits *below* the $6.15 trailing-four-quarter actual — implying consensus sees roughly flat-to-low-single-digit DE growth this year despite the Q2 print showing 26%. Options positioning confirms the sentiment shift rather than a fundamental one: ATM implied volatility collapsed from 62.8% to 33.9% and the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 1.62 to 0.87 as the price rose.

The unresolved analytical question is the composition of the 22% FRE growth against 11.3% management-fee growth. The residual is most plausibly fee-related performance revenue crystallizing off perpetual-capital vehicles that marked up on data-center appreciation — the CFO stated that QTS was the single driver of appreciation across both the infrastructure and commercial real estate portfolios in the quarter. If that is right, a meaningful slice of the 22% is a function of one theme's marks rather than durable operating leverage, and paying 24x current-year DE for it is not obviously attractive. We hold the position for the 3.5% forward yield and the AUM compounding; we would not add capital here.

## Company Overview

Blackstone is the largest alternative asset manager globally, operating four reportable segments: Real Estate, Private Equity, Credit & Insurance, and Multi-Asset Investing. The economics are fee-and-carry, not balance-sheet: total consolidated assets were $49.9 billion at June 30, 2026 against $1.35 trillion of AUM, a ratio of 3.7%. Recurring revenue comes from management fees on $921.7 billion of fee-earning AUM (FY2025); episodic revenue comes from performance allocations as general partner, of which a substantial share is owed onward to employees as performance compensation.

Two structural features define the current thesis. The first is perpetual capital, $523.6 billion at year-end 2025 and up 18% — vehicles with no fixed term (BREIT, BCRED, BXSL, BIP, insurance mandates, and now BXDC). This converts a fundraise-deploy-harvest cycle into something closer to an annuity, provided redemptions stay within contractual gating limits. It is also the epicenter of the last twelve months of stress.

The second is the digital-infrastructure platform, which management valued at $185 billion at the Q2 call, up from roughly $130 billion at the start of 2026. It originates in the 2021 QTS Realty acquisition and the 2024 AirTrunk purchase. The platform now touches every segment: data centers are 27% of BREIT, they drove appreciation in both infrastructure and CRE in Q2, and nine of the ten best-appreciating investments in the quarter had an AI link. Blackstone also announced a joint venture with Google in May 2026 — a new U.S. entity offering data-center capacity, operations, networking, and Google Cloud's TPUs as compute-as-a-service, with a $5 billion initial Blackstone equity investment. The TPU technology is Google's; Blackstone is the capital and infrastructure partner. Whether the $5 billion is corporate or fund capital is not disclosed in the filings we reviewed, and it matters — the corporate balance sheet holds only about $11.4 billion of accessible liquidity, which makes fund capital the more plausible structure but leaves the firm's own exposure unverified.

A note on share count, because it determines every multiple. The 752.6 million Class A shares outstanding at June 30 multiply to roughly $109.8 billion at $145.945 — but 444.6 million Blackstone Holdings Partnership Units, exchangeable one-for-one, sit behind that as non-controlling interests. Full-economics unit count is approximately 1,197–1,240 million, or $174.7–181.0 billion of equity value. Any model pairing the narrow $110 billion figure with firm-wide DE understates the multiple by roughly 60%. We work in per-share terms throughout to avoid the trap.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** Q2 2026 GAAP revenues were $5.037 billion versus $3.71 billion a year earlier. Net income attributable to Blackstone Inc. was $1.229 billion, diluted EPS $1.54 versus $0.98. For the first half, management fees were $4.415 billion (+12.1%), realized performance allocations $2.468 billion (+77%), attributable net income $1.879 billion, and diluted EPS $2.37. Operating cash flow was $2.93 billion in 1H26 versus $2.00 billion in 1H25 — a 47% increase that corroborates the realization inflection in cash rather than accrual terms.

On the non-GAAP basis the market uses, Q2 DE was $1.52 per share against a $1.39 consensus, a 9.3% beat. The four-quarter record is three beats and one small miss (Q1 2026, $1.36 versus $1.39, -1.9%), with the two prior quarters beating by 10.7% and 20.3%. Trailing four-quarter DE per share is $6.15.

**The FRE composition problem.** FY2025 FRE grew roughly 9%; Q2 2026 FRE grew 22%. Base management fees in the same quarter grew 11.3%. The arithmetic gap has to come from fee-related performance revenues, transaction/advisory fees, or margin expansion. Given the CFO's statement that QTS was the single appreciation driver across two portfolios, the most probable explanation is crystallization of fee-related performance revenue from perpetual vehicles marking up on data-center value. That is legitimate revenue, but it is mark-dependent and therefore less durable than management fees. The alternative — genuine operating leverage plus a step-change in the insurance and credit mandate base — cannot be ruled out from what we reviewed. This is the single most decision-relevant open item, and it is unresolved. The Q3 supplement and segment tables are the resolving documents.

**Balance sheet.** Loans payable were $13.19 billion at June 30 versus $12.45 billion at December 31, with $800 million drawn on the revolver. Against that sit cash and equivalents of $2.5 billion, corporate treasury investments of $0.40 billion, and roughly $7.8 billion of liquid other invested assets — approximately $11.4 billion of accessible resources. Fee-related earnings alone of roughly $5.7 billion annually cover the debt comfortably; there is no refinancing cliff or covenant issue in evidence. Two contractual claims deserve mention: a Tax Receivable Agreement obligation of $2.067 billion (undiscounted, payable to pre-IPO owners and exchanging unitholders) and $5.90 billion of unfunded GP commitments to Blackstone's own funds.

Accrued performance allocations rose to $13.91 billion from $12.98 billion at year-end; net of performance compensation, net accrued performance revenues were $7.468 billion, up 13% year over year. Divided across roughly 1,240 million full-economics units, that is $6.02 per unit — about 4.1% of the share price in embedded but contingent value. Retained earnings of negative $18.6 million reflect an ~85% DE payout policy, not accumulated losses.

**Screening artifacts.** Standard screens misclassify this company badly and should be set aside. The Altman Z-Score of 0.63 zeroes out its largest-weighted term because Blackstone does not present a classified balance sheet, and the model was calibrated on manufacturers with inventory. A reported operating margin of -3.0% alongside a 66.8% net margin cannot both describe the same economics — the GAAP operating line is distorted by performance-allocation compensation and consolidated-fund presentation. The 69.8% ROE pairs consolidated net income including NCI against attributable equity; corrected, attributable ROE is roughly 33–34%. We also note the supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 32.92 against $3.87 EPS, which does not reconcile at the current price ($37.7x); we use per-share DE and filed GAAP EPS instead.

**Financial health score: 7.0.** Asset-light, investment-grade, several times covered on fee earnings alone, and cash conversion improving. The offsets are the ~85% payout leaving thin retained capital, the $2.067 billion TRA claim, $5.90 billion of unfunded commitments, and an unexplained $1.152 billion Level III equity-options liability marked with a 5.6% volatility input — a low assumption for a liability of that size, flagged for follow-up rather than as a finding.

## Growth Analysis

The gross flow data are genuinely impressive and hard to dismiss. $70 billion of Q2 inflows and $260 billion over twelve months against $1.35 trillion of AUM means the firm is replacing more than a fifth of its asset base annually with new commitments. The credit platform (corporate plus real estate) reached $550 billion, up 13%, taking $33 billion of Q2 inflows — nearly half the firm total. Credit & Insurance AUM was $469.3 billion, up 15%. BXMA reached $109 billion, up 21%.

Three specific growth vectors are worth separating:

**Digital infrastructure.** The platform value nearly doubled in six months. BXDC — Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust — completed a $2 billion offering in Q2, creating a permanent-capital buyer for the assets QTS develops. Structurally this is elegant: develop, lease, sell into a vehicle Blackstone also manages, and earn fees twice on the same asset. The $1 trillion addressable market management cites is a projection for a market Schwarzman himself called nascent, and should be treated as such.

**The semi-liquid channel, stabilizing.** BREIT raised $1.2 billion with repurchases down 42%, its best net flows in four years, with data centers now 27% of the portfolio and a 9.4% net return for the largest share class since inception. BCRED still raised roughly $1 billion of new equity in Q2 even while gating — the fund is not experiencing a one-way run. Management says Q3 requests are down materially, though that is an unaudited partial-quarter characterization.

**Policy optionality.** The August 2025 Executive Order on democratizing access to alternatives in defined-contribution plans is the largest addressable-market expansion available to the firm. It is a policy direction, not a realized flow, and no reasonable forecast should capitalize it yet.

Against that: consensus does not share the acceleration view. Current-year DE consensus of $6.02 is *below* the $6.15 trailing figure. The current-quarter estimate was cut 7.8% in 30 days. Incentive fees fell 18.6% year over year. The single-family rental growth channel was closed by statute — the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law July 10, 2026, barring investors with 350 or more single-family homes from further purchases (it caps acquisitions; it does not compel divestiture). The 2027 consensus of $7.49 implies roughly 24% growth over the 2026 consensus of $6.02, which requires realizations to accelerate further into an exit environment that remains dependent on cooperative markets. [unverified — no 10-year, 30-year, or Fed policy figures appear in the sources.]

**Growth prospects score: 6.2.** Strong gross flows and a genuine perpetual-capital franchise, offset by consensus that has stopped rising, a closed SFR channel, declining incentive fees, and an unverified FRE composition.

## Valuation Assessment

Working in per-share terms against the $145.945 basis:

| Metric | Calculation | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing four-quarter DE/share | $145.945 ÷ $6.15 | **23.7x** |
| FY2026 consensus DE/share | $145.945 ÷ $6.02 | **24.2x** |
| FY2027 consensus DE/share | $145.945 ÷ $7.49 | **19.5x** |
| 1H26 GAAP EPS annualized *(sensitivity)* | $145.945 ÷ $4.74 | **~30.8x** |
| Full-economics equity ÷ FY2025 DE | ~$175–181B ÷ $7.11B | **24.6x–25.5x** |
| Forward dividend yield | $1.29 × 4 ÷ $145.945 | **3.54%** |
| Net accrued carry per unit | $7.468B ÷ ~1,240M units | **$6.02 (4.1% of price)** |

The peer table is heterogeneous and needs adjustment. BK (18.4x), STT (16.3x), and AMP (13.3x) are custody and wealth businesses with fundamentally different growth and fee structures — they are not the comparison set. Within alternatives, KKR trades at 32.8x and APO at 45.3x on reported bases distorted by GAAP treatment of insurance and performance items. On a like-for-like DE basis, BX at 24.2x current-year and 19.5x out-year is roughly mid-range for the group and no longer carries the discount it did in March.

The PEG arithmetic is the cleanest frame. On 2027's implied ~24% DE growth, 24.2x current-year DE gives a PEG near 1.0 — reasonable *if* 2027 delivers. But 2026's own implied growth over the trailing base is roughly flat, which puts the current-year multiple at a high ratio to near-term growth. The valuation therefore rests substantially on the 2027 reacceleration, which in turn rests on realizations, which rest on exit markets.

Two further considerations argue against treating the current price as cheap. First, the stock trades above both consensus mean ($142.38) and median ($140.00) targets while estimates drift sideways-to-down — the condition under which analyst targets function as ceilings rather than floors. The pattern where consensus targets lag reality applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and compressed multiples; neither condition holds here. Second, the entire move off the March low occurred without upward estimate revision, meaning it was multiple expansion on sentiment normalization. The mispricing existed at $102–115 in March, when the market was pricing a semi-liquid channel collapse against 11% fee growth. It has been substantially harvested.

**Valuation score: 4.8.** Not stretched — the 3.5% yield, 19.5x out-year, and $6.02 per unit of embedded net carry provide real support. But there is no identifiable mispricing at $145.945, and the risk/reward is skewed: roughly 8% to base case against 23% to bear.

## Competitive Landscape

Blackstone's competitive position is mixed in trajectory rather than uniformly improving, which is what matters for scoring.

Widening: the data-center development franchise is difficult to replicate given land, power, and permitting constraints, and BXDC gives Blackstone a captive permanent-capital exit for developed assets — an advantage over competitors who must sell to third parties (though Blackstone did sell data centers to Digital Realty in Q2, showing it uses both channels). Distribution breadth across institutional, insurance, and private-wealth channels remains the industry's best; $70 billion of quarterly inflows during a period when it was gating a flagship fund is evidence of franchise strength.

Compressing: the FY2025 10-K's fee-pressure language indicates the competitive constraint binds on price rather than access. Gating redemptions twice in consecutive quarters is reputationally costly in a channel where trust is the product, even though gates are contractual and disclosed. Morningstar's data show the semi-liquid market approaching $600 billion, up 41% since end-2024, but with the direction of travel reversing — investors who clamored to enter private credit in late 2024 now want out. The WSJ reported in August that managers compute liquidity metrics inconsistently, which makes cross-manager comparisons unreliable and raises the odds that scrutiny broadens.

A distinction the market conflates: Blackstone's revenue mix is diversifying (management explicitly cited "expanding scope of our activity"), but counterparty concentration is not demonstrably improving. Data centers now drive appreciation across three segments simultaneously, 80% of the global CRE equity platform sits in three asset classes (data centers, logistics, rental housing), and the 10-K discloses that digital-infrastructure demand is concentrated among a small number of large counterparties it does not name. Segment diversification and counterparty diversification are different measures; only the first is improving.

**Competitive position score: 6.6**, essentially holding the prior 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center concentration is the dominant risk and cuts across the whole firm.** A hyperscaler capex pause would compress management fees (lower fee-earning AUM marks), performance allocations (lower carry), and fundraising (weaker track record) at the same time. The usual multi-strategy diversification is reduced when one theme drives appreciation in three segments. We could not verify counterparty names, lease tenors, or tenant credit quality, so the severity is unquantified — long-dated leases to investment-grade tenants would make contracted cash flow far more durable than spot AI sentiment, but that is an assumption, not a finding.

**Semi-liquid redemption risk is improving but unresolved.** BCRED breached its 5% cap at 7.9% and then 10% of NAV. The mechanism that worries us is sequencing: gated funds that must sell assets to meet redemptions can mark down NAV, which reduces fee-earning AUM and can trigger further redemptions. The Q3 10-Q is the resolving document; management's "down materially" is a characterization, not a reported figure.

**Regulatory.** The ROAD to Housing Act caps SFR acquisitions (Senate 85-5, House 358-32). NAIC raised the risk-based capital charge on residual tranches of ABS securitizations from 30% to 45%, making private structured credit more capital-expensive for insurers — a direct threat to the fastest-growing fee pool. AIFMD II leverage limits take effect in 2026; FinCEN's AML rule for advisers in January 2028. State-level proposals to cap public pension allocations to alternatives threaten the institutional base.

**Governance.** Directors are elected by written consent of Blackstone Group Management L.L.C., the sole holder of Series II preferred. The Q2 10-Q states there will be no matter for stockholders to vote on at the September 22, 2026 annual meeting. Joseph Baratta joined the board on August 6, increasing insider representation. This structure has existed since the 2007 IPO and coexisted with strong returns, so it is a persistent discount factor rather than new deterioration — but proxy contests, say-on-pay, and shareholder proposals are unavailable if capital allocation goes wrong.

**Macro.** Higher-for-longer rates would compress alternatives-manager multiples directly and slow the realization cycle that 2027 consensus depends on. The exit-market assumption embedded in $7.49 of 2027 DE is one of the softest inputs in the model. ### Options Market Signal

The options market has moved from defensive to neutral, which removes a supportive contrarian signal rather than providing one. ATM implied volatility fell from 62.8% on July 20 to 33.9% now while the price rose 18.4%, and the put/call open-interest ratio dropped from 1.62 to 0.87. Near-term IV of 33.9% against 36.1% one-year realized is roughly fair (-2.2pp spread), at the 33rd percentile within the current chain. The headline 8.5% put skew belongs to a one-day expiry and reflects same-day mechanics; the 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month skew readings are -1.5%, -2.9%, and -3.3%, and the term structure is flat (33.9% near, 33.0% far), pricing uncertainty evenly rather than clustering around a catalyst. Short interest is 3.5% of float with 5.4 days to cover — uncrowded, meaning informed capital is not positioned for a decline, but also that no squeeze support exists. The composite read: the fear premium that made March attractive has been paid out. Falling IV alongside a sharp rally is consistent with resolved uncertainty shading into complacency.

**Risk factors score: 4.7.** The concentration risk is real and only partially priced, the FRE composition gap is decision-relevant and unresolved, and the governance structure offers no remedy. Offsetting, there is no leverage or refinancing risk, and gating is contractual rather than a breach.

## Management Quality

The score trajectory shows management quality falling from 8.5 to 5.5 across prior reports. Current evidence confirms that level rather than reversing it.

Strategic execution is genuinely good. BXDC creates a captive permanent-capital exit for developed data centers. The May 11 BREDS/Brio initiative to finance construction of 50,000+ *for-sale* homes annually was announced two months before the ROAD to Housing Act became law and converts a regulatory constraint into a lending opportunity in exactly the category the statute favors — well-timed positioning, though the commitment was explicitly unsized. The Google JV positions Blackstone as the capital and infrastructure partner in a compute-as-a-service structure with a credible counterparty. No Item 5.02 executive-departure 8-K has been filed since November 2024.

Capital allocation and insider behavior are less flattering. FY2025 repurchases were roughly $122.6 million; 1H26 repurchases were 0.4 million shares for $48.4 million, with about $1.6 billion remaining authorized — roughly 0.03% of full-economics equity value, while the stock traded as low as $101.73. Schwarzman said on July 23 that "our stock is on sale today." Retaining capacity for $5.90 billion of unfunded GP commitments, the $2.067 billion TRA, and the Google JV equity is a rational reason to preserve liquidity, and repurchasing stock while gating fund redemptions carries optical risk. Either reading leaves a gap between the rhetoric and the action.

Insider transactions were sales only: Chief Legal Officer John Finley sold 45,000 shares at roughly $147 on August 11 (leaving 516,017 held), Vikrant Sawhney sold 30,000 at ~$135.60 and 30,014 at ~$125.80, and Chief Accounting Officer David Payne sold 9,500 at $145.23. Director Ruth Porat made purchases in the $17,000–33,000 range, consistent with dividend reinvestment or deferred-compensation mechanics rather than conviction. Blackstone executives are paid predominantly in equity and carry, so diversification selling is normal, and Schwarzman and Gray do not appear as sellers. But there were no C-suite open-market purchases into the drawdown, and the recent sales came into the recovery from $126 to $147. Because estimates are flat rather than rising, the usual mitigation for insider selling — dominant positive revision momentum — is absent here.

Note also the separate category of Form 4 filings by Blackstone entities as >10% holders of Medline, Legence, Jersey Mike's HoldCo, and Argon Holdco. Those are fund monetizations generating carried interest, not insider sales of BX stock, and corroborate the realization surge.

**Management quality score: 5.4.**

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($184, +26.1%).** 2027 DE consensus of $7.49 is delivered and the market pays 24.6x for it. Requires: the FRE composition question resolving in favor of durable operating leverage; BCRED turning net-positive and BREIT's inflection extending; the data-center platform continuing to compound with lease terms and tenant credit proving durable; and the 401(k) alternatives rule producing actual flows. This also implicitly requires a realization environment that cooperates.

**Base case ($158, +8.3%).** 21.1x the 2027 consensus of $7.49 — a mid-cycle alternatives multiple. Assumes AUM compounds at low-to-mid teens, semi-liquid flows normalize to modestly positive, data-center appreciation moderates from a doubling in six months to something sustainable, and DE grows modestly in 2026 before reaccelerating. Add the 3.54% dividend for a total return near 12% over twelve months.

**Bear case ($112, -23.3%).** 15.0x 2027 DE if that year comes in flat with 2026 rather than up ~24%. Triggered by any of: renewed semi-liquid outflows on NAV markdowns; a hyperscaler capex pause reversing data-center marks and stalling realizations; the 22% FRE growth proving to have been fee-related performance revenue that does not recur. This level is near the March trading range, so it is not a hypothetical floor.

Probability-weighting 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear gives roughly +4.9% price return plus 3.5% yield, or about 8% total — positive but not compelling, and achieved by accepting a 23% downside tail against an 8% base-case gain.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Twelve-month horizon.**

Existing holders should retain exposure for the 3.54% forward yield and the AUM compounding. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. The Q3 2026 supplement discloses that the majority of Q2's 22% FRE growth came from management-fee-line operating leverage or new insurance/credit mandates rather than crystallized fee-related performance revenue, *and* consensus 2026 DE per share is revised above $6.15.
2. BCRED reports Q3 redemption requests below the 5% cap in the Q3 10-Q with positive net flows, confirming the "down materially" characterization with a filed figure.
3. Price returns to $125–132 (roughly 17–18x 2027 consensus DE) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring 20%+ upside to base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A rally through $165–170 (22–23x 2027 consensus) before the FRE composition question resolves — at that level the base case is fully paid and the asymmetry becomes clearly unfavorable.
2. BCRED or BREIT reporting a third consecutive quarter of cap-exceeding redemption requests, or a disclosed NAV markdown in either vehicle.
3. Any hyperscaler capex guidance reduction that reads as broad rather than idiosyncratic, or disclosure indicating short weighted-average lease terms or non-investment-grade tenant concentration in the data-center platform.
4. 2027 consensus DE per share cut below $7.00, which would remove the reacceleration on which the current multiple depends.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release and 10-Q (late October / early November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint. It resolves the FRE composition question, the BCRED Q3 trend, and June-quarter perpetual capital and dry powder — none of which we can verify today.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on Blackstone, and the reasoning has changed even though the rating has not. The prior holds reflected a business performing well into a derating driven by semi-liquid credit fears. That derating has now substantially reversed — roughly 44% off the low, 17% in three weeks — without any upward revision to consensus earnings. The stock trades above both the mean and median sell-side target, the sell-side distribution has shifted toward hold, current-quarter estimates were cut 7.8%, and the options market has unwound its protective positioning. At 24.2x a year consensus sees growing modestly, and 19.5x a year consensus expects to grow ~24%, the valuation depends on the 2027 reacceleration in an exit environment that remains uncertain.

The business is high quality: 33–34% attributable ROE, $260 billion of trailing inflows, $523.6 billion of perpetual capital, an asset-light balance sheet covered several times over by fee earnings, and $6.02 per unit of embedded net accrued carry. The data-center franchise is a genuine and hard-to-replicate advantage. But it is also the concentration risk the 10-K itself names, driving appreciation across three segments at once, and the 22% FRE growth that justifies the current multiple has an unattributed residual we cannot resolve from the filings.

Hold what you own. Add at $125–132, or after the Q3 filing confirms that the fee-related earnings acceleration is structural rather than mark-driven.
