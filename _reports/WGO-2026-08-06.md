## Executive Summary

Winnebago is two businesses moving in opposite directions inside one deteriorating end market. Over the nine months ended May 30, 2026, Motorhome RV revenue rose 16.9% to $933.9M and swung from a $7.0M operating loss to $25.3M of operating income, driven by the Grand Design Lineage motorized launch (Class B revenue up 50.7% to $172.0M). Over the same period Towable RV revenue fell 9.1% to $830.5M, and in the third quarter alone towable revenue fell 26.1% with units down 26.5% and fifth wheels down 41.6%. Motorhome RV is now the larger segment by revenue, reversing the FY2022–FY2024 configuration. Management attributed the towable weakness partly to "targeted pressure from rivals" against Grand Design, historically the highest-margin franchise in the portfolio.

The reason we move from HOLD to AVOID is not the operating result. It is the relationship between price and estimates. On June 25 the company cut FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.65–2.75B from $2.8–3.0B and adjusted EPS to $1.65–2.00 from $2.10–2.80, and reported adjusted EPS of $0.66 against a $0.77 consensus (-13.9%). The stock rose that day, because expectations had already reset (close $30.88 versus prior close $27.27, roughly +13%). It has continued higher: from $27.27 on June 24 to $31.305, roughly +15%. Over the same 90-day window, consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS fell from $2.34 to $1.80 (-22.9%) and FY2027 from $2.99 to $2.39 (-20.2%). The forward multiple therefore expanded from about 13.4x to 17.4x on the current year and from 10.5x to 13.1x on next year — a roughly 30% re-rating into falling numbers. Revisions were still negative in the most recent 30 days (-2.2% current year, -4.7% next year).

The estimates are likely to fall further. Winnebago's FY2026 guidance rests on a CY2026 North American RV wholesale assumption of 290,000–310,000 units, set June 25. Patrick Industries guided 285,000–300,000 on July 30 and LCI Industries cut to 280,000–300,000 on August 5 — both midpoints below Winnebago's. Camping World, the largest U.S. RV retailer, cut its outlook on July 30 citing deteriorating conditions and inventory-clearance margin pressure. Winnebago's own towable dealer field inventory rose 5.5% year over year to 18,721 units while its nine-month towable wholesale deliveries fell 9.9%, which mathematically requires retail registrations to have fallen faster than shipments and implies further wholesale under-shipment in FY2027. The financing cost of a large discretionary purchase remains a headwind to demand. We would revisit in the low-to-mid $20s.

## Company Overview

Winnebago manufactures recreational vehicles and boats sold through more than 760 independent dealer locations in the U.S. and Canada. Nine operating segments are aggregated into three reportable segments: Towable RV (Grand Design and Winnebago-branded travel trailers and fifth wheels, MSRP roughly $20,000–$163,000), Motorhome RV (Winnebago, Newmar, and a Grand Design motorized segment established in Q1 FY2025, MSRP $143,000–$1,717,000), and Marine (Barletta aluminum pontoons, Chris-Craft fiberglass runabouts). Corporate/All Other contains Winnebago Specialty Vehicles and Lithionics, a lithium battery business acquired in April 2023. Headcount was approximately 5,300 at FY2025 year-end, none unionized.

Dealers buy on cash terms and finance inventory through third-party floorplan lenders; Winnebago provides repurchase agreements to those lenders covering up to 18 months for RVs and 24 months for marine. No dealer organization exceeded 10% of net revenues in any of the past three fiscal years. Chassis are sourced from Mercedes-Benz, Stellantis, Freightliner, Ford and Spartan under arrangements with no minimum purchase or minimum supply commitments. Manufacturing is concentrated in northern Iowa, northern Indiana and Gulf Coast Florida. The company is a distant number three in RV volume behind Thor Industries and Berkshire-owned Forest River, and its own 10-K concedes that some competitors are "much larger than us, most notably in the towable RV market."

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year descent frames everything else:

| Fiscal year (Aug FYE) | Revenue | Gross margin | Operating income | Op. margin | Net income | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $4,957.7M | 18.7% | $583.5M | 11.8% | $390.6M | — |
| FY2023 | $3,490.7M | 16.8% | $300.7M | 8.6% | $215.9M | — |
| FY2024 | $2,973.5M | 14.6% | $100.2M | 3.4% | $13.0M | $0.44 |
| FY2025 | $2,798.2M | 13.0% | $57.2M | 2.0% | $25.7M | $0.91 |

Revenue is down 43.6% from the FY2022 peak, gross margin down 570bp, operating income down 90.2%. FY2024's 66% effective tax rate reflected a non-deductible $32.7M debt inducement loss and a non-deductible $30.3M Chris-Craft goodwill impairment.

The nine-month FY2026 consolidated picture looks like recovery — revenue $2,058.8M (+1.9%), operating income $48.6M (+30.9%, margin 2.4% vs 1.8%), net income $24.8M vs $12.0M, adjusted EPS $1.31 vs $0.96. Q3 alone reverses it: revenue $698.7M (-9.9%), operating income $23.0M (-23.9%), adjusted EBITDA $37.8M at a 5.4% margin versus $46.5M, adjusted diluted EPS $0.66 vs $0.81.

The single most creditable operational data point in the file is gross margin stability: 13.6% in Q3 FY2026 versus 13.7% a year earlier, and 13.1% versus 13.2% for nine months, achieved through a 9.9% quarterly revenue decline and acknowledged input-cost inflation. SG&A fell 5.4% in the quarter. This is cost execution, not an accounting artifact. It is also partly mix — Motorhome ASP rose 6.0% over nine months to $216,779 — and mix and "selective price adjustments" are finite levers in an affordability-constrained market.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt is $450.0M: $100.0M of 6.25% Senior Secured Notes due July 2028 (reduced from $300M via a $100M tender in February 2025 and a $100M par redemption in February 2026), and $350.0M of 3.25% unsecured convertibles due January 2030 with an $87.93 conversion price — roughly 181% above spot — and cash-settled principal, further hedged by a call spread. The $350M ABL is undrawn. Nothing is due until FY2028. Interest expense fell to $16.3M for nine months from $19.3M; cash interest paid was $13.3M. Cash was $57.1M at May 30 (the seasonal trough; it was $10.5M at the same point in FY2025 and $174.0M at FY2025 year-end). Current ratio 2.42; shareholders' equity $1,234.4M.

Net leverage checks to roughly 3.08x: net debt $392.9M over TTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$127.7M, consistent with management's stated ~3.0x. Measured at the seasonal cash trough, so fiscal-year-end leverage should be lower.

**Cash flow and the dividend.** Nine-month operating cash flow was +$26.2M against -$52.5M a year earlier, a $78.7M improvement driven mainly by receivables. Capex was $16.8M, so nine-month free cash flow was $9.4M against $30.1M of dividends paid. That shortfall should not be annualized: FY2025 full-year operating cash flow of $128.9M against nine-month -$52.5M implies roughly +$181M in the June–August quarter, and full-year FY2025 free cash flow of $89.5M covered ~$39M of dividends about 2.3x. The cleaner signal is retained earnings, a balance rather than a flow: $1,747.8M (Aug 2023) → $1,723.3M (Aug 2024) → $1,710.0M (Aug 2025) → $1,704.4M (May 2026). Declared dividends have exceeded GAAP net income for three consecutive years and again year to date, reconciling exactly for nine-month FY2026 ($24.8M net income less $30.4M declared). At $1.40 annualized the payout is about 114% of guided reported EPS midpoint and 77% of guided adjusted EPS midpoint. Roughly $22M of annual non-cash intangible amortization sits between reported EPS and cash generation, so a >100% GAAP payout is not itself evidence of unsustainability — but the cushion is narrowing, and the Senior Secured Notes carry occurrence-based restrictions on distributions in the event of a significant EBITDA reduction.

Screening artifacts deserve a caveat. The Altman Z of 3.23 is carried entirely by the balance sheet; EBIT/assets contributes 0.027. Substituting market equity for book equity in the equity/liabilities term ($885M/$929.7M = 0.95 versus 1.32) reduces Z to roughly 3.0. ROA is 1.2% and ROE 2.1% — resilience without capital efficiency.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance implies revenue of $2.65–2.75B against FY2025's $2,798.2M, a decline of 1.7% to 5.3%. Backing out nine-month actuals of $2,058.8M gives implied Q4 revenue of $591–691M against $777.3M a year ago — down 11.1% to 23.9%. Management confirmed Q4 sales would be down sequentially and double digits year over year. Implied Q4 reported EPS is $0.18–$0.53 and implied Q4 adjusted EPS $0.34–$0.69, against nine-month adjusted actuals of $1.31.

Consensus is tracking guidance down rather than through it:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.49 | $0.57 | $0.86 | -42.9% |
| Next quarter | $0.40 | $0.43 | $0.50 | -19.0% |
| Current year | $1.80 | $1.84 | $2.34 | -22.9% |

Estimate revisions of this magnitude are among the more reliable forward indicators available, and here they point in the wrong direction with no sign of stabilization — the 30-day column is still negative across all four periods.

The forward-looking industry data has moved further against the company since guidance was set. Winnebago assumed 290,000–310,000 CY2026 North American wholesale shipments on June 25. Patrick Industries published 285,000–300,000 on July 30 and LCI Industries cut to 280,000–300,000 on August 5. Both companies sell components into OEM order books and therefore have direct visibility. Their fiscal calendars differ from Winnebago's August year-end and methodologies vary, so these are not company-verified figures for Winnebago — but the revision path is one-directional and post-dates the guide.

The internal leading indicator agrees. Towable dealer field inventory rose 5.5% to 18,721 units while nine-month towable wholesale deliveries fell 9.9% and Q3 deliveries fell 26.5%. Field inventory has to be worked down, which means further wholesale under-shipment of retail in FY2027 from an already-reduced base. Motorhome field inventory fell 4.0% and Marine rose 3.5%, so the problem is specific to towables.

Against this: the motorized turnaround is real and product-driven, not purely a low base. Class B revenue grew 50.7% over nine months and Class C and other 21.7%, tied to the Lineage launch. Barletta held 9.3% TTM share of the U.S. aluminum pontoon market through April, the clearest verifiable share-gain claim in the portfolio. New affordability products (Grand Design Transcend Lite at roughly $15,000–$16,000 street, an Arca off-grid truck, a Barletta SANZA entry line) address the actual constraint. MY2027 pricing is planned at low single digits to low double digits. The offset is that some motorhome growth is new-nameplate dealer pipeline fill rather than recurring retail pull, and the available data does not separate the two.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported profile "forward P/E" of 23.49 is identical to the trailing figure and is a data artifact: 23.5x reconciles to TTM reported diluted EPS of roughly $1.36 ($0.91 FY2025 less ~$0.42 of 9M FY2025 plus $0.87 of 9M FY2026), not to a forward estimate.

On forward numbers at $31.305:

- **17.4x** FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.80 (and 25.6x the guided reported EPS midpoint of $1.225)
- **13.1x** FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $2.39, an estimate cut 20.2% in 90 days
- **~10.4x** EV/adjusted EBITDA: EV of ~$1,278M against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $117–125M (nine-month actual $92.4M plus a Q4 scaled from Q4 FY2025's ~$35.3M; the company does not guide to EBITDA)
- **0.72x** book value, but **2.85x tangible book** — goodwill of $484.2M and other intangibles of $440.7M are 45% of total assets, so the sub-book optic is not asset backing
- **4.5% dividend yield** ($1.40 annualized), with the coverage caveats above

A double-digit EV/EBITDA multiple on trough EBITDA and 2.85x tangible book is not distressed pricing. This is the central point: WGO does not currently offer the compensation a cyclical should offer at the bottom of its earnings cycle, and it offers less than it did six weeks ago.

The supplied peer table (Ford, GM, Tesla) is not usable — those are mass-market auto OEMs with different capital intensity, financing arms and demand drivers, and a "peer median P/E" of 366x carries no information. The relevant comparison set is Thor, LCI, Patrick and Camping World, all of which have cut forecasts in the last five weeks.

Cycle-adjusted, the bull arithmetic is available but distant. If the industry stabilizes near 300,000–320,000 units and Winnebago restores revenue to $2.9–3.1B at a 5.5–6.5% operating margin, operating income of $160–200M less ~$18M interest at a 22% tax rate produces roughly $3.90–$5.00 of reported EPS. That path requires both volume recovery and 350–450bp of operating margin expansion from FY2025's 2.0%, and nothing in the current data indicates it begins in FY2027.

Analyst targets: mean $38, median $35, low $30, high $73. The $73 high is a mid-cycle recovery case. We treat the mean as stale rather than as a floor — the "targets lag reality" dynamic applies to stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, and here estimates are falling while the multiple has expanded ~30%. The recommendation mix has already drifted from 6 buy/8 hold three months ago to 5 buy/10 hold.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory, not level, is what matters, and the trajectory is split.

**Deteriorating where it counts most.** Towable RV was the historical profit engine, and Grand Design's fifth-wheel franchise carries $244.7M of goodwill. Q3 towable units fell 26.5% with fifth wheels down 41.6%, and margin compressed to 5.8% from 8.0%. Management identified "targeted pressure from rivals" specifically in towables and fifth wheels. Field inventory rising 5.5% while shipments fall confirms retail is falling faster than wholesale. The plausible alternative reading — that fifth wheels are simply the most rate-sensitive, highest-ticket towable category and this is cycle rather than share — cannot be excluded from the available data, and the two readings have materially different valuation consequences. We weight the risk-adjusted case: management's own language points to competitive pressure, not only cycle.

**Improving in motorized and marine.** The Lineage launch and the FY2025 Winnebago Motorhome restructuring produced a segment swing from a $7.0M nine-month loss to $25.3M of operating income. Barletta's 9.3% pontoon share is a genuine gain.

**Supplier bargaining power is shifting against Winnebago.** Patrick Industries and LCI Industries agreed to an all-stock merger on June 30, 2026 (~52%/48% ownership split), with Patrick targeting $150M of net run-rate cost synergies. Patrick and LCI are suppliers that Winnebago pays; combining the two largest engineered-component vendors reduces supplier competition precisely as Winnebago attempts to push MY2027 price increases and launch cost-sensitive value products. The FY2025 10-K discloses one unnamed supplier at ~14% of consolidated raw material purchases; whether that supplier is Patrick, LCI or a third party is not disclosed and we do not treat the identification as established. The merger was pending, not closed, as of the latest reporting.

The company's newly introduced "retail dollar share" metric exceeds its unit share by construction, because ASPs are higher. It is a fair measure of revenue capture and not evidence of unit-share defense.

## Risk Assessment

**Dealer repurchase contingency.** Total contingent liability on repurchase agreements was $1,777.7M at May 30, 2026, up 8.4% from $1,640.0M at fiscal year-end — roughly 2.0x market capitalization — against a recorded reserve of $1.3M (0.07%), down from $1.6M. No material repurchase activity occurred in either nine-month period, and dispersion across dealers and lenders plus resale value mitigate the exposure. It is nonetheless the largest off-balance-sheet tail, and it is rising while field inventory builds.

**Floorplan lender concentration.** Two institutions held approximately 51% of financed dealer inventory dollars at FY2025 year-end. A retrenchment by either would compress shipments independent of retail demand.

**Chris-Craft trade-name impairment test in Q4 FY2026.** The 10-Q states explicitly that a quantitative assessment will be performed in the fourth quarter and that sustained revenue decline could produce a charge. Chris-Craft goodwill was already fully written off in FY2024. Indefinite-lived trade names total $352.3M. Any charge would be non-cash and would not affect EBITDA, adjusted EPS, cash flow or covenants, but it would reduce book equity and would signal brand deterioration in an asset acquired in 2018.

**ABL maturity July 15, 2027.** Undrawn, but within twelve months and subject to acceleration factors. Availability is a borrowing base of eligible receivables and inventory that shrinks as the business shrinks; the 10-K warns explicitly on this point. Renegotiation in a weak RV market could bring less capacity or tighter terms.

**Distribution restrictions under stress.** The Senior Secured Notes contain occurrence-based limits on distributions and share purchases in the event of a significant EBITDA reduction or significant ABL borrowing — a contractual, not discretionary, constraint on the dividend in a downside scenario, with $100M outstanding to July 2028.

**Warranty drift.** The nine-month provision rose to $71.5M from $69.6M while shipments declined, and claims paid fell to $72.1M from $76.5M. Provision per unit is rising, consistent with FY2025 MD&A commentary on "higher warranty experience."

**Unquantified potential positive.** The 10-Q discloses that on February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not authorize the imposition of tariffs, and that the company will assess an impending refund of IEEPA-associated tariffs in future periods. The impact "cannot be reasonably estimated." We model no benefit, but it is a real upside asymmetry for shorts.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Six Item 5.02 officer/director filings since August 2024 is an unusually high cadence. The content of the May 1, 2026 8-K (Items 5.02 and 7.01) is unverified; a same-dated initial Form 4 for a 1,553-share grant is consistent with a new director appointment but we do not characterize it either way. CEO grant-date total compensation rose 24.7% to a five-year high of $9.00M in FY2025, the year TSR fell to 67.5 against a 93.5 peer index; the mechanical explanation (more shares granted at a lower grant price plus option fair-value effects) is plausible but we could not verify target-setting intent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 48.0% sits fractionally below one-year realized volatility of 50.3% (spread -2.4%, characterized as fair) at the 21st percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure to 51.9% at six months. The nearest expiry prices a ±8.3% move; the December tenor prices ±24.2%. Put/call open interest is 0.57 — call-tilted — and the near-term skew of -1.2% is labeled balanced. Short interest is 12.8% of float at 3.0 days to cover.

The read for our AVOID: the options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Positioning is modestly call-tilted, near-term volatility is cheap-to-fair rather than stressed, and the longer-dated contango prices uncertainty out at the December expiry rather than around the October print. Combined with 12.8% short interest and a 4.5% dividend, this is an unattractive structure for expressing the view with a short position. It also flags a genuine risk to our own call: with volatility cheap and positioning light on the put side, a Q4 print that merely meets the low end of guidance could produce a violent squeeze. The October 22, 2025 FY2025 filing day gapped the stock higher (prior close $31.62; open $36.31; close $40.64, roughly +29%), which is the relevant precedent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($42, +34%).** The June guidance cut marks the trough of the wholesale destocking cycle. Field inventory clears through the fall, MY2027 price increases stick, the motorized franchise continues compounding on the Lineage platform and Newmar/Winnebago restructuring, and Barletta keeps gaining pontoon share. Gross margin has already proven defensible at 13.6% through a 10% revenue decline, so a volume recovery drops through at high incremental margin. Deleveraging is done — no maturities before FY2028 — so equity captures the full recovery. An IEEPA tariff refund provides unquantified upside. At $2.75–3.00 of adjusted EPS by FY2028 and 14x, the stock is worth $39–42.

**Bear case ($22, -30%).** The towable share loss is structural rather than cyclical. Field inventory forces continued under-shipment through FY2027 while Camping World and other retailers clear inventory at discounted prices, capping both volume and price. Supplier consolidation absorbs the MY2027 increases, so the pricing lever fails and gross margin finally breaks below 13%. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $2.00, the market pays 11x for a share-losing number three in a shrinking category, and the dividend cushion narrows to the point where the Senior Secured Notes' distribution restrictions become relevant. Equivalently, ~8.7x EV/EBITDA on $110M of EBITDA less $393M of net debt implies roughly $20/share.

**Base case ($29, -7%).** FY2026 lands at or near the low end of guidance. FY2027 guidance, when issued in October, is framed on a 280,000–300,000 unit industry assumption, moving consensus toward $2.10–2.20. The market pays 13–14x, or equivalently 16x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.80. Both routes converge near $29. The 4.5% dividend makes total return roughly flat over twelve months — inadequate compensation for the distribution of outcomes around it.

The decisive evidence is the price/estimate divergence. Since the last review the stock is up approximately 15% while FY2026 and FY2027 consensus fell 22.9% and 20.2% respectively, and the two suppliers with the clearest read on OEM order books have marked the industry below the assumption embedded in Winnebago's own guidance. That combination removes the margin of safety that justified prior HOLDs at $27–28.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q4/FY2026 report, expected in October 2026 (FY2025 results were released October 22, 2025). Three specific vectors: revenue landing at or below the $2.65B low end against implied Q4 revenue of $591–691M; initial FY2027 guidance framed on a 280,000–300,000 unit industry assumption rather than 290,000–310,000; and the disclosed Q4 quantitative Chris-Craft trade-name impairment test, with $352.3M of indefinite-lived trade names on the books.

**Timing window.** Roughly ten weeks. Short enough to be actionable, but the entire thesis resolves in a single print.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 12.8% of float short, 3.0 days to cover, shares short 2.98M. Elevated but not crowded. The squeeze precedent is specific and recent: the FY2025 filing day on October 22, 2025 opened the stock at $36.31 against a $31.62 prior close and it closed at $40.64 — a roughly 29% single-day gain on 3.4M shares. The same setup exists now.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning does not confirm the thesis. Put/call open interest is 0.57 (call-tilted), near-term implied volatility of 48.0% is fractionally below 50.3% realized, and the near-term skew is described as balanced. Carrying cost includes the 4.5% dividend. Nothing in the volatility surface suggests informed bearish positioning ahead of the catalyst.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $22 (-30%). Upside risk to the $38 analyst mean (+21%), with the October 2025 precedent showing a plausible 25%+ single-day gap on a good print, plus unquantified IEEPA refund optionality. Reward:risk is approximately 1:1 before the dividend carry, and worse after.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish case is a fair-to-slightly-rich multiple on falling estimates in a deteriorating end market, which argues for not owning the stock. It is not a solvency or accounting story: gross debt is $450M with nothing due until FY2028, the $350M ABL is undrawn, covenants are in compliance, and convertible dilution requires an $87.93 share price. Add 12.8% short interest, a call-tilted options book, a 4.5% dividend carry, an unquantified tariff refund, and a demonstrated capacity to gap ~29% on a filing-day print, and the mechanics do not support an active short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (specific and monitorable):**

1. **Price.** $23–25, which would put the stock at 10–11x a marked-down FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$2.20 and roughly 8x EV/EBITDA on $115M — a genuine trough multiple rather than a mid-cycle one. This is 20–27% below the current basis.
2. **Estimate stabilization.** Two consecutive months with FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS flat or higher. The current sequence is $2.99 → $2.51 → $2.39, and the 30-day change is still -4.7%. A flattening here is the single cleanest signal that the destocking cycle has been discounted.
3. **Towable field inventory declining year over year** while wholesale deliveries stabilize. The current combination — inventory +5.5%, nine-month deliveries -9.9% — mathematically requires further under-shipment. Reversal would remove the FY2027 overhang.
4. **Industry forecast convergence.** Winnebago's FY2027 guidance issued in October at an industry assumption at or above the prevailing supplier range (currently 280,000–300,000), rather than above it as the current guide sits.

**Conditions that would move us to HOLD sooner:** Q4 FY2026 revenue at or above the $691M implied high end with gross margin held at or above 13.0%, alongside FY2027 guidance built on a conservative industry assumption. Conditions that would confirm the AVOID and push toward the bear case: FY2026 revenue below $2.65B, a Chris-Craft trade-name charge accompanied by further towable share loss, or any indication that the July 2027 ABL renewal is being negotiated at reduced capacity.

**Horizon.** The RV wholesale destocking cycle looks likely to persist through FY2027 given the field-inventory arithmetic. We would expect the earliest credible inflection signal at the October 2026 print and the more probable one at the Q2 FY2027 report in March 2027.

## Conclusion

**AVOID at $31.305.** Winnebago has done the two things a cyclical should do in a downturn: it has taken $187M of gross debt out of the capital structure and pushed all maturities past FY2028, and it has held gross margin at 13.6% through a 9.9% revenue decline while cutting SG&A 5.4%. The motorized franchise has genuinely inflected, with Class B revenue up 50.7% over nine months and the segment swinging from a $7.0M loss to $25.3M of operating income. Solvency risk over the next 24 months looks low.

What the company has not done is stop losing volume in the franchise that historically carried its earnings. Towable units fell 26.5% in the quarter, fifth wheels 41.6%, and dealer field inventory rose 5.5% while shipments fell — which requires retail to have declined faster than wholesale and implies further under-shipment ahead. Guidance was cut twice this year and now rests on an industry assumption that two component suppliers have marked below since it was issued, with the largest RV retailer cutting its own outlook on the same theme.

The stock trades at 17.4x FY2026 adjusted consensus, 13.1x a FY2027 estimate that has fallen 20% in 90 days, roughly 10.4x trough EV/EBITDA and 2.85x tangible book — after a 15% rally from the June low. That combination of expanding multiple and contracting estimates is the specific condition under which cheapness fails to protect capital. The 4.5% dividend is covered on normalized full-year free cash flow but not by GAAP earnings, and it is subject to contractual distribution restrictions in a downside EBITDA scenario. We prefer to wait for the estimate cuts to stop, or for the low-to-mid $20s.
