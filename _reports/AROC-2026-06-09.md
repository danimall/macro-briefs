**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest U.S. provider of contract natural gas compression by total fleet horsepower, operating a 4.8M-HP fleet at 96% average utilization and earning 73% adjusted gross margins in its core Contract Operations segment. The business is volume-levered rather than price-levered: compression is a "must-run" service tied to gas production and transportation throughput, which insulates Archrock from the commodity-price whipsaw that dominates short-cycle oilfield services. With U.S. dry-gas production at record highs and LNG-export capacity expanding ~9-10% per year, the demand backdrop is durable. The stock has risen roughly 34% over the trailing twelve months to $34.71 against a 52-week range of $21.17-$40.12.

The investment case rests on three pillars that the data largely confirms: structural margin expansion (contract-ops margin up to 72-73% from 67% in 2024), a genuine multi-year supply-driven moat (Caterpillar engine lead times approaching ~160 weeks raise the replacement cost of fleet and cap new entrants), and a balance sheet that has been materially de-risked over the past two quarters (2028 Notes redeemed, $800M of 6.0% 2034 Notes issued, revolver drawn just $97M of $1.5B, debt now almost entirely fixed-rate with no near-term maturities until 2032/2034). The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 is a structural artifact of an asset-heavy, leveraged infrastructure model and a legacy accumulated deficit, not evidence of operating distress.

What holds this at HOLD rather than BUY is the absence of asymmetric upside at the current price. The stock trades at ~17-18x current-year consensus EPS of $2.03, current-year estimate revisions have turned modestly negative over the past 30 days (-2.0%), Q1 2026 adjusted EPS missed consensus ($0.42 vs $0.47), and the CFO is departing through an orderly but still-in-progress transition. The fundamental floor is solid, but the multiple already reflects the structural story, and the calibration of recent results argues against treating the consensus price target as a near-certain floor. This is a high-quality cash compounder priced for its quality.

## Company Overview

Archrock operates two segments. **Contract Operations** (~85% of revenue) owns, installs, operates, and maintains a fleet of compression equipment that it rents to natural gas producers and midstream gatherers as a service. This is the high-margin core. **Aftermarket Services** (~15% of revenue) provides maintenance, overhaul, parts, and reconfiguration for customer-owned equipment at lower margins (~24%).

The fleet totaled 4.79M available horsepower and 4.57M operating horsepower at year-end 2025, with spot utilization of 95% — effectively fleet-constrained rather than demand-constrained. Approximately 60% of the fleet serves midstream gathering and 40% serves gas-lift applications for oil enhancement. Because revenue is tied to gas production and transportation volumes rather than the commodity price directly, Archrock's cash flows are structurally more stable than typical oilfield-service peers.

Recent growth has been acquisition-driven. The TOPS acquisition closed August 2024 (~$869M cash) and NGCS closed May 2025 ($349.4M, adding ~326k HP in the Permian). A small Flowco divestiture (~47k HP) was opportunistic. With the acquisition spree digested and the debt wall refinanced, management is pivoting toward harvesting free cash flow and returning capital.

Revenue grew ~76% over three years (2022 to 2025) and net income nearly doubled in 2025. Margin expansion is real and structural, helped by higher rates, the acquired fleet, and a ~$35M sales/use tax audit benefit. The mechanism is durable: a fleet running at 96% utilization with rising rates against a fixed asset base produces operating leverage.

Free cash flow was $120M in 2025 (FCF margin ~8%), held down by heavy growth capex ($348M growth + $111M maintenance). 2026 capex is guided to $400-445M, of which growth capex is $250-275M, and Q1 2026 growth capex moderated sharply to $65M from $139M in Q1 2025 — consistent with the pivot toward FCF harvest. ROE of 21.6% is strong.

The balance sheet has been the most important development. As of March 31, 2026, debt was almost entirely fixed-rate ($2.3B notes), and a 1% rate move affects annual interest by just ~$1M. The 2027 Notes ($300M) were redeemed in November 2025, the 2028 Notes on April 1, 2026, and the 2034 Notes were issued in January 2026. The credit-facility cost fell to 5.1% from 5.8% and covenants were loosened. The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 ("distress zone") reflects D/E ~1.9 and a -$2.26B accumulated deficit from the legacy Exterran spin-off, not operational distress. The Piotroski F-score, 96% utilization, 21.6% ROE, and growing operating cash flow describe a sound business.

The Q1 2026 result deserves context. Revenue was $373.8M (+8% YoY), net income $73.8M, EPS $0.41, and adjusted EPS $0.42. The miss was driven by higher SG&A — a $3.7M acceleration of incentive comp tied to the CFO transition plus higher cash-settled incentive comp from the rising share price. Underlying contract-ops margin expanded to 72% from 70% a year earlier. The miss is low-quality and largely non-operational, but it signals that consensus visibility on quarterly cadence is imperfect, which matters for how much weight to place on forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is the strongest part of the thesis. The EIA projects U.S. dry-gas production at all-time highs through 2026-2027, driven by LNG exports growing ~9-10% per year and pipeline exports to Mexico. Compression is required throughout the gas value chain, so as production volumes grow, compression demand grows. Archrock is a volume-levered, toll-like beneficiary of the U.S. LNG export buildout, with multiple independent confirming signals (record production, contracted LNG offtake, expanding export terminals).

Estimate revisions are mixed and worth parsing precisely. The forward-quarter estimates are rising (current quarter +9.6% over 90 days to $0.50; next quarter +10.1% to $0.53), a genuine positive momentum signal. But current-year EPS has been revised down -2.0% over the past 30 days (to $2.03 from $2.07), and next-year is roughly flat (+0.1% over 90 days to $2.34). The picture is positive at the quarterly level but flattening at the annual level. This does not meet the threshold of an inflection signal — there is no +30% revision velocity here, and the business is not recovering from a trough; it is a high-utilization operator growing in the high single digits.

Growth from here is more measured than the 2024-2025 acquisition surge. Organic growth is constrained by the same Caterpillar lead-time bottleneck that strengthens the competitive position: the company cannot add fleet faster than its suppliers can deliver engines. Guidance for 2026 reaffirms Adjusted EBITDA of $865-915M, implying roughly flat-to-modest growth off the 2025 figure. The growth story is durable but moderating into a return-of-capital phase.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.71, Archrock trades at ~17-18x current-year consensus EPS and ~14.8x next-year EPS of $2.34. EV is elevated by ~$2.4B of debt, putting EV/EBITDA on 2026 guidance (~$890M midpoint) around 8.5x — a reasonable multiple for a high-utilization infrastructure operator but not cheap.

The peer table requires care. Reported peer P/Es are distorted: KGS (the most direct compression peer) shows 86x, NOV 81x, WHD 53x, LBRT 31x, with a peer median of 53.2x. These trailing figures reflect depressed or non-normalized earnings across the equipment/services group rather than a clean read on compression economics. AROC at ~19x trailing is the lowest multiple in the set, which is appropriate given its more stable revenue but also reflects the leverage and the contract-cancellation structure that limit how high the multiple can run. The 2.6% dividend yield (raised to $0.22/quarter from $0.19) sits above the peer median yield of 1.3% and provides support.

The valuation does not present a clear mispricing. The structural story — scarce fleet, supply-driven moat, LNG tailwind — is well understood and reflected in the 34% twelve-month appreciation. The price decline from the May high near $40 to $34.71 (~13%) is a moderate pullback on fundamentals that are intact, which improves the entry point modestly but does not create asymmetry. To justify a high valuation score I would need a specific reason the market is wrong, and the evidence points to a fairly-priced quality compounder rather than a discount. The current-year revision drift argues against treating the consensus mean target of $41.89 as a near-certain floor; it is a credible 12-month objective, not a stale lagging anchor.

Consensus price targets range from $39 low to $46 high (mean $41.89, median $41.00), implying ~12-32% upside. With a bear case in the high-$20s, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric rather than the 2:1-or-better asymmetry that would support a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock is the largest U.S. compression fleet operator. The 10-K describes the market as "highly competitive" with "low barriers to entry," but that boilerplate is currently overridden by the equipment supply bottleneck. Caterpillar engine lead times approaching ~160 weeks (roughly three years) raise the replacement cost and barrier to entry for new fleet, support rate increases on existing fleet, and make Archrock's deployed 4.8M HP a scarce, appreciating asset. The $47M of gains on sale of used equipment in 2025 is direct evidence of this scarcity. This is a real, multi-year competitive advantage, though it is supply-driven and therefore temporary; it persists only as long as the engine bottleneck does. (Note: the ~160-week figure derives from a single secondary source summarizing the earnings call and warrants corroboration against the primary transcript.)

Scale reinforces the position through purchasing power, service-network density, and the ability to absorb acquired fleets (TOPS, NGCS) at favorable economics. Utilization with strong rate trends confirms the company is gaining, not losing, ground in the current environment. The competitive trajectory is improving, which I weight heavily. The main offset is that 60% of fleet revenue depends on midstream gathering volumes that, after initial contract terms of 12-36 months, become cancelable on 30 days' notice — limiting durable pricing power if production volumes ever soften.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and capital-market dependence** is the most material risk. The debt funded the acquisition spree, and the model requires continuous access to debt and equity to fund growth capex. Covenant ceilings (Total Debt/EBITDA ≤5.25x, temporary 5.50x) are currently met with room, and the refinancing has removed near-term maturity pressure, but financial flexibility is the swing factor for dividends and growth.

**Contract structure** is the second risk: agreements run 12-36 months (up to 60 for large HP) and become cancelable on 30 days' notice after the initial term, which typically does not recoup equipment cost. The company has limited ability to raise prices during initial terms, so cost inflation and tariffs are passed through with a lag.

**Customer concentration** — top five customers are 35% of revenue, consistent 2023-2025, including related-party Hilcorp (~$9M Q1 2026, board seat since 2019).

**CFO transition** is the live governance item. Douglas Aron is departing under a Transition and Separation Agreement dated March 25, 2026; he still signed the Q1 10-Q on May 6, indicating an orderly process. A successor has not been independently confirmed, and a separate February 24 Item 5.02 filing suggests additional officer changes. This is a watch-item rather than a red flag, but it temporarily reduces management-continuity visibility.

**Secular energy-transition risk** is long-dated — electrification of compression or reduced gas demand would erode fleet value over a multi-decade horizon, partly hedged through small minority investments in emissions/efficiency technology. The near-term methane/GHG regulatory direction is favorable (EPA OOOOb/OOOOc deadlines pushed to 2027, methane charge delayed to 2034).

### Options Market Signal

The options chain is mildly cautious on timing but not bearish on direction. Near-term ATM IV of 42.3% is rich versus 29.4% one-year historical volatility (a 12.9pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.3% near-term vs 38.7% six-month), indicating the market is pricing some imminent event risk. The put/call OI ratio is 0.52 and skew is -3.6% (call-heavy), with put/call volume at just 0.12 — positioning leans constructive rather than defensive. The OI ratio collapsed from 2.05 to 0.52 in a single session alongside the IV rise, consistent with hedge unwinding and fresh call interest rather than informed downside positioning. Short interest is modest at 5.4% of float (4.1 days to cover), with no squeeze dynamic. The net read: the options market is pricing a near-term catalyst with a slight upward tilt, consistent with a HOLD that has a positive bias rather than a warning against the position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($46):** U.S. gas production and LNG exports continue to expand, keeping the fleet at 95-96% utilization with rising rates. The Caterpillar bottleneck persists, sustaining pricing power and pushing used-equipment gains higher. The capital-allocation pivot delivers rising free cash flow that funds dividend increases and buybacks below intrinsic value (repurchases to date averaged $20.91/share). 2026 EBITDA reaches the top of guidance ($915M), and the stock re-rates modestly to ~9.5x EV/EBITDA. This is roughly +32% from current.

**Bear Case (high-$20s, ~-20%):** A softening in gas-production growth or a midstream-volume slowdown triggers contract cancellations (30-day notice) and rate pressure. Leverage limits flexibility, growth capex compresses returns, and the CFO transition coincides with execution missteps. Multiple compresses toward 14x EPS on a flat earnings outlook. This puts the stock around $28-29.

**Base Case ($41):** Demand stays firm, margins hold near 72-73%, EBITDA lands near the $890M midpoint, and the company executes the harvest-and-return pivot. The stock tracks the consensus median target, supported by the 2.6% yield and modest buyback. This implies ~18% upside over twelve months.

The distribution is positively skewed but not asymmetric enough to clear the bar for a high-conviction long. The base case is constructive, but the downside is a real -20% with identifiable triggers, and the upside requires the supply bottleneck and demand to both persist.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Timeframe: 12 months.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $30-31 (~12-15% lower) on unchanged fundamentals, which would create the 2:1+ asymmetry currently absent.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turning decisively positive (+5%+ over 90 days) alongside reaffirmed or raised 2026 EBITDA guidance, confirming the quarterly momentum is flowing through to the annual outlook.
- A clear free-cash-flow inflection — 2026 FCF tracking materially above $200M as growth capex moderates — confirming the harvest pivot with an accelerated buyback below intrinsic value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Average fleet utilization falling below ~92%, signaling demand softening or contract cancellations.
- Total Debt/EBITDA approaching the 5.25x covenant ceiling, forcing a dividend or capex cut.
- A disorderly CFO transition or further unexplained senior-officer departures, or evidence of accounting/control weakness.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected early August 2026), where the key watch items are SG&A normalization post-CFO-comp acceleration, the order-book commentary, confirmation of the incoming CFO, and any update on Caterpillar lead times.

## Conclusion

Archrock is a high-quality, cash-generative infrastructure business with a genuine multi-year competitive advantage and a durable demand tailwind from U.S. gas production and LNG exports. The balance sheet has been materially de-risked, margins have expanded structurally, and the company is shifting toward returning capital. The score trajectory has been improving (6.6 → 6.8), and the financial-health and competitive-position dimensions have strengthened, which the latest data confirms.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: at ~17-18x current-year EPS having appreciated 34% over twelve months, the structural story is already in the price. The concrete impediments to a BUY are (1) current-year EPS estimates revised down -2.0% over the past 30 days, removing the annual-level positive-revision support that a BUY would require; (2) a Q1 2026 EPS miss ($0.42 vs $0.47) that, while low-quality, signals imperfect quarterly visibility; (3) an in-progress CFO transition with an unconfirmed successor; and (4) a risk/reward distribution that is roughly symmetric (~+18% base case versus ~-20% bear case) rather than the 2:1+ asymmetry a high-conviction long demands. None of these is a thesis-killer, which is why the rating is not AVOID; together they argue for owning the position for its growth-and-yield profile while waiting for either a better entry near $30-31 or a decisive turn in annual estimate revisions before adding. The 2.6% dividend and modest buyback provide downside support in the interim.
