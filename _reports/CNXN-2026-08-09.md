## Executive Summary

Connection printed the best quarter in its history. Q2 2026 net sales of $854.0M grew 12.4%, gross profit of $157.5M was a record, operating margin reached 5.01% versus a 3.5% four-year norm, and diluted EPS of $1.31 beat the two-analyst consensus by 23.5%. Operating income rose 39.2% on 12.4% revenue growth, with SG&A up only 7.1%, which reflects real operating leverage rather than accounting noise.

The problem is what produced it. On the earnings call, CEO McGrath disclosed that endpoint device **units** grew 3% while notebook/mobility/desktop **revenue** grew 19.5%, and CFO Baker confirmed "there was absolutely price inflation." Roughly 16 percentage points of endpoint revenue growth — approximately $60M of the $77.7M endpoint increase, against a $94.3M total consolidated increase — came from average selling prices, driven by the global DRAM/NAND shortage that AI datacenter demand created. Baker separately estimated that pull-forward was "mid-single digits, maybe a little lower" of the quarter, implying underlying growth nearer 7–9% than 12.4%. Servers and storage revenue fell 18.3% and desktops fell 6.5%, confirming a narrow endpoint cycle rather than a broad demand expansion. Management guides Q3 revenue sequentially lower.

For a reseller, memory-cost inflation is mechanically accretive while it passes through: hold gross margin percentage, and higher price levels drop more gross-profit dollars onto a semi-fixed cost base. That is what happened (gross margin +30bps, SG&A/sales −70bps, operating margin +90bps). It is also a price-level effect that reverses, and the market is currently capitalizing it at a premium multiple. At $84.14, CNXN trades 22.3x LTM diluted EPS of ~$3.78 and roughly 20x the $4.17 current-year consensus, against a 17.8x technology-distributor peer median, while the closest public comparable, Insight Enterprises, sits 21% below its own 52-week high at 19.3x. LTM operating cash flow of approximately $42M and free cash flow near $34M convert only about 36% of $95.8M of reported net income, the corporate buyback stopped entirely in Q2 with $81.2M still authorized, and both the CEO and CFO reduced their holdings by 17% and 36% respectively at $83–87 in the days after the print. The probability-weighted expected return from here is modestly negative with materially asymmetric downside. Trim.

## Company Overview

Connection is a US-focused value-added reseller and IT solutions provider based in Merrimack, New Hampshire, offering over 460,000 products from more than 1,600 suppliers, with an ISO 9001:2015 / SOC 2 Type 2 integration and distribution center in Wilmington, Ohio, more than 5,000 technical certifications, and multinational procurement capability through GlobalServe. It manufactures nothing; it resells and integrates.

Three segments, with Q2 2026 detail:

| Segment | Q2 net sales | YoY | Gross margin | Segment op. income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise Solutions | $369.6M | +13.4% | 14.9% (+30bp) | $15.1M |
| Business Solutions (SMB) | $343.9M | +17.3% | 23.0% (−50bp) | $31.7M |
| Public Sector Solutions | $140.5M | flat | 16.5% (+130bp) | −$0.1M |
| HQ/unallocated | — | — | — | −$3.7M |
| **Total** | **$854.0M** | **+12.4%** | **18.4%** | **$43.0M** |

The structural fact segment revenue obscures: Business Solutions is 40% of revenue but 68% of Q2 segment operating income, and was 94% of FY2025 segment operating profit. Investors are buying an SMB reseller with two lower-quality attachments.

A presentation note matters for peer comparison. Software, cloud, and agency transactions are recognized net, so FY2025 gross billings of $4,134.1M produced only $2,872.7M of reported net sales. The 18.4% "gross margin" is partly an artifact of that presentation; on a gross-billings basis FY2025 gross profit was roughly 13.1%.

## Financial Analysis

**Five-year trajectory:**

| Year | Net sales | YoY | Gross margin | Operating margin | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $3,125M | — | 16.8% | — | $89.2M |
| 2023 | $2,851M | −8.8% | 17.9% | — | $83.3M |
| 2024 | $2,802M | −1.7% | 18.6% | 3.47% | $87.1M |
| 2025 | $2,873M | +2.5% | 18.8% | 3.46% | $83.7M |
| H1 2026 | $1,575.9M | +7.9% | 18.4% | 4.01% | $50.4M |
| Q2 2026 | $854.0M | +12.4% | 18.4% | 5.01% | $33.2M |

Through 2025 the company was stagnant: revenue below the 2022 peak, operating margin pinned near 3.5% despite 200bps of gross-margin improvement (largely net-basis mix), and FY2025 net income the lowest of four years at the highest gross margin, because SG&A absorbed the gains and $6.0M of severance hit the P&L. Q2 2026 broke the pattern.

**Balance sheet, genuinely strong.** At 6/30/26: total assets $1,427.6M, total liabilities $475.0M, equity $952.7M, **zero funded debt**. Cash $123.7M plus $217.0M of short-dated US Treasuries equals $340.7M, or $13.50 per share. Current ratio 2.88x. Lease liabilities $7.5M. A screened debt/equity near 0.50 is an artifact — those liabilities are ~80% accounts payable ($377.6M) and accruals; there are no borrowings.

**Cash conversion, the weak link.** LTM operating cash flow reconciles to roughly $42.1M (FY2025 $65.5M, less H1 2025 −$26.1M, plus H1 2026 −$49.5M), against LTM net income of $95.843M. Net of approximately $8M of capex, LTM free cash flow is near $34M — about 36% conversion of reported earnings, and a 1.6% free-cash-flow yield against a 22.3x P/E. FY2025 operating cash flow had already fallen 62% from $173.9M as inventory rose from $95.1M to $143.6M and DSO extended from 72 to 76 days.

H1 2026 absorbed roughly $102.8M of working capital (AR +$80.6M, inventory +$61.5M, AP +$39.3M) against $50.4M of net income plus ~$10.8M of non-cash addbacks. First-half burn is seasonal — H1 2025 was also negative at −$26.1M — so the sign is not new. The magnitude is: Q2 2026 consumed $63.8M where Q2 2025 generated $26.3M, a $90M single-quarter swing, and within Q2 accounts payable *declined* $18.8M while receivables rose $66.9M. The company funded a customer-credit build while paying down vendor credit. Cash conversion cycle widened to 49 days from 44; days inventory 27 from 20.

Baker guided inventory down to "the $150 million range" by year-end from $205.1M, which if delivered releases roughly $55M in H2. That is the single most important near-term item to verify.

**Two liquidity-structure facts.** The $50M receivable-collateralized credit facility expired March 31, 2025 and management chose not to renew or replace it. Contingent liquidity is therefore own cash, $377.6M of vendor trade credit, and supplier-finance programs (obligations $65.7M, up from $58.6M). At current net cash this is not a solvency issue; it is a flexibility constraint if memory scarcity forces a larger inventory commitment while vendors simultaneously tighten terms — a risk the 10-K names explicitly. Separately, the Q2 press release still describes Adjusted EBITDA as the measure used "in certain financial covenants contained in our credit agreement." There is no credit agreement. This is stale boilerplate that reflects loose review of non-GAAP language.

**Earnings composition.** FY2025 pretax income of $113.7M exceeded operating income of $99.3M by exactly the $14.4M of net **interest income** on the Treasury portfolio (LTM interest expense is $82K). LTM interest income of $13.2M is 10.2% of $130.2M LTM pretax income — a rate-sensitive, non-operating stream that is already shrinking (Q2 net interest income $2.5M versus $3.2M). The working-capital build is a double debit: it consumes the cash and removes the income that cash earned, roughly $4M of pretax income, or $0.12 of annual EPS, per $100M redeployed at 4%.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers, all identifiable and all finite.

**1. Memory-driven ASP inflation.** Endpoint units +3% versus endpoint revenue +19.5%; notebooks/mobility moved from 34% to 40% of net sales. The DRAM/NAND shortage was flagged as new in the FY2025 10-K ("business impact is yet to be determined") and elevated into the Q2 10-Q's forward-looking risk list. The 10-Q states both directions plainly: higher inventory costs "may reduce our margins or require us to raise prices," and shortage "could additionally result in a lack of availability of products." Connection is in the favorable pass-through phase. Neither demand destruction from higher prices nor holding high-cost inventory into a price decline has arrived.

**2. Windows 11 / aging-fleet refresh.** Windows 10 end-of-support has passed. This cycle is mature, not nascent.

**3. Pull-forward.** Baker: not 10% of the business, "mid-single digits, maybe a little lower," including a couple of customers representing over $10M each that were "pretty clearly pull-ins." Taken literally, $25–43M of Q2's $854M was borrowed from later periods. Offsetting this, McGrath noted some Enterprise customers delayed on fixed budget cycles.

**Evidence against a pure price bubble.** Servers/storage fell 18.3% while notebooks surged, which is inconsistent with indiscriminate inflation. Software grew 15% (aided by Microsoft's June fiscal year-end) and networking 11.5%. Vertical growth was broad: retail +31%, manufacturing +27%, financial services +23%, healthcare +15%. And Connection passed cost through without margin loss, which is a competence signal.

**Evidence of segment damage.** Public Sector operating income swung from +$2.3M in FY2024 to −$9.4M in FY2025 (derived from disclosed segment gross profit less SG&A, reconciling exactly to reported operating income), consuming nearly all of Business Solutions' $16M improvement, which is why company operating income rose only $2.2M in a year when the profit engine grew 21%. H1 2026 federal government sales fell $48.9M, or 57.4%, attributed to large prior-period orders that did not repeat; Q2 federal was down 24.7%. State/local/education grew 2.0% in H1. Constructively, Public Sector gross margin expanded 130bps in Q2 and segment SG&A fell $2.6M in H1, putting the segment near breakeven in Q2 (−$0.08M versus −$2.13M). A return to +$8–10M of segment income is roughly $0.25–0.28 of EPS and is the cleanest self-help lever available.

**Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call.** Current-year consensus rose 6.4% and next-year 5.4% over 90 days — positive, but an order of magnitude below the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal. Current-quarter and next-quarter estimates ticked *down* 0.9% and 1.4%. Implied forward earnings growth of 7.0% ($4.17 → $4.46) against a ~20x forward multiple gives a PEG near 2.9.

Guidance is qualitative and unambiguous in direction: Q3 revenue sequentially lower, high-single-digit YoY growth, full-year outperformance of the US IT market by 200bps. The last is explicitly relative — management concedes the absolute rate belongs to a cycle it does not control.

Management also asserted record Enterprise backlog and three-year-high Business Solutions backlog. Connection discloses no backlog figure or definition in any filing; contract liabilities of $9.0M are cash received in advance, not backlog. Treat the claim as unverified.

CNXN at 22.3x trailing carries a 25% premium to the median while the most similar business, Insight, has derated. Net cash of $13.50/share justifies part of that premium, but not most of it: strip after-tax interest income of roughly $0.38/share from LTM EPS and the ex-cash multiple is $70.64 / $3.40 = 20.8x, essentially unchanged. The cash is already earning its way into the P/E.

**Annualizing Q2 is not available.** $1.31 × 4 = $5.24 and 16.1x is contradicted by guidance for sequentially lower Q3 revenue, by the Microsoft year-end benefit, and by mid-single-digit pull-forward.

**Base case: $82.** H1 actual EPS of $1.99 plus H2 of roughly $2.00–$2.20 gives ~$4.00–$4.20 for FY2026, consistent with the $4.17 consensus. Apply 19x to a normalized FY2027 figure of ~$4.30 (consensus $4.46 discounted for pull-forward reversal): $82. Cross-check on EV/EBIT: normalized operating income of $105–110M at 13.5–14x, plus $341M of cash, gives $1,758–1,881M, or $69.6–74.5/share — below the earnings-based figure, which tempers any temptation to round the base case up. Base $82 is −2.5% from spot.

**Bull case: $103.** Memory tightness persists through 2027, AI-capable device specifications lift durable product mix rather than transient price, Public Sector returns to +$8–10M of segment income, and services mix continues lifting Enterprise gross margin. FY2027 EPS ~$4.90 at 21x = $103, or +22.4%. The persistence leg depends on the direction and durability of memory pricing, which is issuer-sourced only and not externally verified in this work; the bull case treats continued tightness as an assumption, not an established fact.

**Bear case: $60.** Memory pricing normalizes in 2027 with endpoint units roughly flat; revenue deflates toward $2.9–3.0B and operating margin reverts to the 3.5–4.0% historical band. At $2.9B and 3.75%, operating income is ~$109M; add ~$10M of interest income and tax at 27% for ~$87M net income, or ~$3.45/share. At 16–18x that is $55–62; call it $60, or −28.7%. Net cash of $13.50/share is a partial floor. This scenario requires only that a cyclical price spike mean-revert.

**Probability weighting.** 25% bull, 45% base, 30% bear: (0.25 × +22.4%) + (0.45 × −2.5%) + (0.30 × −28.7%) = +5.6 − 1.1 − 8.6 = **−4.1%**. Even shading the base to flat leaves expected return near −3%.

Two market-based cross-checks. The single published price target is $83, below spot; thin coverage (two analysts) makes this low-information, and I do not treat it as independent evidence, but it is not the setup where consensus targets lag a visible inflection: at a ~2.9 PEG, the target and the price reflect the same cycle assumption. And the corporate buyback, which was aggressive at $60s prices in FY2025 ($76.3M for the year, $60.5M in H1 2025 alone, retiring ~4% of shares), fell to $2.5M in Q1 2026 and **zero** in Q2 with $81.2M authorized.

Valuation score 4.5: not a structurally impaired value trap, but a premium multiple on cycle-peak, price-driven earnings with negative near-term expected return and an identified normalization mechanism.

## Competitive Landscape

Connection is the number-three US public pure-play IT solutions provider behind CDW and Insight, both named in its own 10-K as "the current leaders in the space," at roughly one-seventh of CDW's revenue. Scale matters here for vendor rebate tiers, purchasing terms, and fixed investment in services capability, and it shows in conversion: 3.5% historical operating margin (5.0% at the current peak) sits below CDW's structural level despite a superficially high gross margin.

**Trajectory is mixed.** Positive: SMB gross margin of 23.0% is a genuine relationship moat with fragmented, price-insensitive customers; Enterprise gross margin expanded 30bps on services mix; Public Sector margin repair is underway; the company passed through memory inflation without margin loss, which not all channel participants will manage; Dell named it 2026 North America Channel Services Sales Partner of the Year.

**Negative and structural, per the company's own filings:** supplier concentration of 59% from three sources (TD Synnex 25%, Ingram Micro 21%, Microsoft 13% of purchases; by manufacturer Microsoft 16%, HP 13%, Dell 12%), with "substantially all" vendor arrangements "easily terminable" and no long-term contracts; Apple, Dell, HP, and Lenovo selling direct with stated intent to increase it, having "on multiple occasions, sold or attempted to sell directly to our customers"; Amazon and e-tailers encroaching on commercial accounts; cloud/SaaS/IaaS migration that "may reduce the procurement of products and services from us"; and vendor incentive compression, where the company has *already* "experienced a decrease in the level of vendor consideration available to us from certain manufacturers." That last item is an opaque, vendor-controlled component of gross margin that is renegotiated annually and never quantified.

The AI positioning (TSX services organization, Helix center) is credible as a medium-term margin-mix story, but no services revenue line, services gross margin, or AI-attributable revenue is disclosed, so it cannot be sized. The 10-K's new claim to compete with Palantir and Scale.ai on bespoke AI projects reads as aspirational; nothing in the segment or product-mix disclosure suggests such work is financially material. The immediate AI effect on Connection runs the other way: AI datacenter demand created the memory shortage inflating its cost of goods.

Competitive position score 4.8, a marginal improvement on demonstrated pricing discipline and Public Sector margin repair, against intact disintermediation pressure and a federal franchise down 57.4% in H1.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory reversal with elevated inventory.** Inventory of $205.1M is up 43% from year-end. The 10-K acknowledges the company "may purchase product in advance of customer orders" with "limited return rights." Management says part of the build sits against identified Enterprise customer commitments to secure supply; the split between committed and speculative is not disclosed, so markdown exposure cannot be sized. A sharp memory-price decline hits revenue level, margin, and inventory value simultaneously. This is the dominant single risk and it is currently being scored one-sided.

**Liquidity flexibility.** No committed revolver since March 2025, negative H1 operating cash flow, and dependence on $377.6M of vendor trade credit that the 10-K itself flags as a risk if contracted.

**Public sector / federal.** H1 federal down 57.4%, which the 10-Q attributes primarily to a few large orders in the prior period that did not repeat. The 10-K flags that adverse changes in government spending policies, including budget cuts or temporary shutdowns, could cause Public Sector customers to reduce or delay purchases — a headwind for a reseller whose federal business already halved.

**Governance.** Patricia Gallup controlled approximately 55% at 12/31/25. The 10-K risk factor is unusually direct: she "can prevent a takeover of us by one or more third parties," which "could deprive our stockholders of a control premium," and control "may result in decisions that are not in the best interest of our unaffiliated public stockholders." In an industry the company describes as consolidating, minority holders cannot access consolidation value without her assent.

**Related-party headquarters.** Connection occupies its Merrimack HQ and an adjacent facility leased from an affiliate of common ownership, and both leases **have expired**; occupancy continues month-to-month under prior terms while the company intends to negotiate a long-term HQ lease. Related-party lease cost was $837K in H1 2026 — immaterial to earnings, and stable at $419–420K per quarter for two years, so no evidence of overreach. The unpriced item is the terms of a future long-term lease with the controlling holder's affiliate, negotiated by a board she controls, with no shareholder visibility.

**Coverage thinness.** Two analysts. The "26% above consensus" framing circulating in secondary media rests on a very thin estimate base; separately, a widely circulated transcript reports a $0.27 dividend where the 10-Q and press release both state $0.20, and reports Public Sector gross margin at 15.5% where the filed figure is 16.5%. Primary documents govern: the dividend is $0.80 annualized, a 0.95% yield, roughly 21% of LTM EPS.

**Offsets.** Zero funded debt, $340.7M net cash, 2.88x current ratio, ~$4M annual capex, effective disclosure controls, no litigation of note, goodwill of $73.6M confined to the two profitable reporting units, and a clean 8-K record with no M&A, restatements, or executive departures in the trailing twelve months.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.6% against 25.6% one-year realized is roughly fair (3.0pp spread), skew is balanced at −0.2%, the put/call volume ratio is 0.15, and term structure is in contango (28.6% at one month rising to 32.4% at six months). Short interest is 3.9% of float with 2.4 days to cover. ATM IV rose 4.5pp since early July alongside a 16.2% price gain, so the market has increased its uncertainty estimate rather than settling.

The options market prices no near-term stress and no directional bear conviction, consistent with my view that the normalization risk is under-discounted rather than over-discounted. The rising six-month vol is the one place where a longer-dated cycle concern is faintly visible. This corroborates the REDUCE case in that protection is not expensive; it does not confirm the thesis, and I do not weight it heavily.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($103, +22.4%)
The memory shortage is a multi-quarter phenomenon driven by AI datacenter allocation of DRAM and NAND capacity, so ASP support persists into 2027. A meaningful share of the ASP increase reflects durable mix shift toward higher-specification AI-capable devices rather than pure inflation, making the gross-profit-dollar step-up partly permanent. Connection demonstrated it can pass cost through without margin loss, an execution differentiator against sub-scale channel peers. Public Sector completes its turn from −$9.4M to +$8–10M, worth ~$0.25–0.28 of EPS. Services mix keeps lifting Enterprise gross margin. Inventory unwinds to $150M as guided, releasing ~$55M and restoring interest income. Vertical breadth (retail +31%, manufacturing +27%, financial services +23%) and management's claim of record backlog indicate real underlying demand. FY2027 EPS ~$4.90 at 21x.

### Bear Case ($60, −28.7%)
Endpoint units grew 3%. Everything else was price and timing. When memory pricing normalizes, revenue deflates toward $2.9–3.0B, operating margin reverts to the 3.5–4.0% band that held for four consecutive years, and EPS falls to roughly $3.45, at which 16–18x is $55–62. The Windows 11 refresh is behind, not ahead. Pull-forward of mid-single-digit percentage means Q3 and Q4 face harder comparisons than the reported run rate implies. High-cost inventory of $205.1M meets falling prices. Interest income, 10.2% of pretax profit, keeps shrinking as cash funds working capital, with no revolver as backup. Federal revenue halved. Vendors continue compressing rebates and selling direct. And the CEO and CFO reduced their holdings 17% and 36% at $83–87 while the company itself stopped buying stock it had bought aggressively in the $60s.

### Base Case ($82, −2.5%)
Growth decelerates as guided but does not collapse. FY2026 EPS ~$4.17, FY2027 ~$4.30 after allowing for pull-forward reversal. The multiple settles nearer 19x as the price component of growth becomes visible. Investors earn the 0.95% dividend and little else.

The asymmetry from $84.14 is unfavorable: −28.7% of downside against +22.4% of upside from a base case that is slightly below spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base $82 (−2.5%), bull $103 (+22.4%), bear $60 (−28.7%); probability-weighted expected return approximately −4%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst; the natural window is between now and the Q3 2026 report (late October), because Q3 is guided sequentially lower against pull-forward-inflated comparisons and is the first period where the price-versus-volume question gets a live test. Reduce roughly half of a full position now and retain the remainder into the Q3 print, given the balance sheet floor of $13.50/share in net cash and the possibility that memory tightness extends the cycle.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Q3 shows endpoint **unit** growth accelerating above 6–8%, evidence that volume, not price, is now carrying growth.
2. H2 operating cash flow turns positive with inventory delivered at or below the guided ~$150M, restoring free-cash-flow conversion above 70% of net income.
3. Buyback resumes at scale against the $81.2M authorization, or any open-market insider purchase by McGrath, Baker, or a director.
4. Public Sector posts two consecutive quarters of positive segment operating income.
5. Price below $70 (roughly 16.3x FY2026E) restores an adequate margin of safety on the normalization case.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Gross margin percentage compresses more than 50bps year over year while inventory remains above $180M, the signature of holding high-cost stock into a price decline.
2. Any inventory write-down, or a vendor materially contracting trade credit, given the absence of a revolver.
3. A long-term Merrimack headquarters lease executed with the controlling holder's affiliate on terms materially above the current ~$1.7M annualized run rate, or without disclosed independent review.
4. Federal revenue declines resume in the second half after the H1 −57.4% comparison lapses.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results, late October 2026. Interim checkpoints: any Form 4 activity, and DRAM/NAND spot pricing as an external read on the ASP driver (noting that all memory-shortage evidence available here is issuer-sourced and the direction and durability of memory pricing, the largest single swing factor for 2027 earnings, is not externally verified in this work).

## Conclusion

This is a well-run, debt-free reseller that just delivered its best operating quarter and is being paid for it at 22.3x trailing and roughly 20x forward earnings, a 25% premium to the technology-distributor peer median. The record quarter is real. Its composition is the issue: 3% unit growth against 19.5% endpoint revenue growth, mid-single-digit pull-forward, servers and storage down 18.3%, and management guiding Q3 revenue sequentially lower. Capitalizing memory-shortage ASP inflation at a premium multiple is a bet that a cyclical price spike does not mean-revert, and the filings quantify what reversion costs: roughly $3.45 of normalized EPS, or $55–62 per share.

Three corroborating signals point the same direction. Free cash flow of approximately $34M converts only 36% of $95.8M of reported net income, with no revolver behind it. The buyback that retired 4% of shares at $60s prices in FY2025 stopped completely in Q2 with $81.2M authorized, an outcome consistent with either capital discipline or cash necessity and unfavorable to the bull case under either reading. And both principal officers reduced holdings materially at $83–87 in the week after the print, with no offsetting open-market purchases; the filings establish the transactions, not the motive, but the pattern warrants weight in a period of only 6.4% estimate revisions rather than a genuine inflection.

Scores decline to 5.05 from 5.5, extending the trajectory, with the deterioration located in valuation (price up 16.2% against 6.4% forward-estimate revisions), cash conversion, and the incremental governance and capital-allocation data since July. Existing holders should trim into strength; new capital should wait for $70 or for evidence that unit volume, not price, is carrying the business.
