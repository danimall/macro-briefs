**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Boyd Gaming is a geographically diversified regional casino operator running 27 properties across 11 states, organized into Las Vegas Locals, Downtown Las Vegas, Midwest & South (the largest segment at roughly 52% of revenue), and Online. The headline financials for FY2025 look strong on the surface — net income of $1,843M — but nearly all of that is a one-time $1,748M pre-tax gain from selling Boyd's passive 5% FanDuel stake. Strip that out and the operating business contracted: GAAP operating income fell 19% to $748M, and Adjusted EBITDAR, the appropriate metric for a REIT-leased casino operator, declined to $1,354M from $1,391M in 2024. The trailing P/E of 3.8x, which superficially looks like deep value, is a direct artifact of the FanDuel gain and carries no analytical meaning.

The FanDuel transaction was financially astute in one respect: Boyd cashed out a passive stake at a high price and cut total debt from ~$3.2B to ~$2.06B, but it carried a structural cost. As part of the deal, Boyd terminated certain market-access agreements and re-signed others at lower rates, permanently impairing the highest-margin revenue stream. Online segment EBITDAR collapsed roughly 64% in Q1 2026 ($8.4M vs $23.3M) with margin falling from 59% to 32.7%, and management guides these lower economics to persist. Las Vegas Locals EBITDAR fell 6.2% and Downtown fell 9.7% on destination softness that began in Q3 2025, and both goodwill (~$958M) and intangibles (~$1.4B) in Midwest & South carry an explicit impairment flag after $128M was already written down in 2025.

The score trajectory has declined steadily (6.3 → 6.1 → 5.5) across three prior HOLD calls, and the current read continues that path. The bull case rests on durable Midwest & South cash flow (segment EBITDAR +5.1% in Q1 2026), aggressive per-share accretion via buybacks (~20% of shares retired in ~18 months), and a clean-ish balance sheet post-de-levering. The bear case is that per-share metrics are being manufactured by a shrinking denominator financed partly with re-borrowed money, on a flat-to-declining EBITDAR base, with three distinct earnings drags (Online, Las Vegas, impairment risk) and falling estimate revisions. With the stock near its 52-week high, estimates broadly declining, and net insider selling including an $8.6M CEO sale, we maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Boyd Gaming has operated since 1975 and is headquartered in Las Vegas. Its four segments capture distinct demand drivers:

- **Las Vegas Locals** (8 properties including Gold Coast, Orleans, Sam's Town, Suncoast): serves Las Vegas residents rather than the Strip; currently softening.
- **Downtown Las Vegas** (California, Fremont, Main Street Station): uniquely dependent on the Hawaiian tourist market, which accounts for roughly 57% of occupied rooms.
- **Midwest & South**: the largest segment at ~52% of revenue (~$2.1B), spanning riverboats and land-based casinos across nine states. Currently the strongest performer.
- **Online**: Boyd Interactive (B2B/B2C iGaming) plus market-access fees from third-party operators using Boyd's state licenses. This is the segment structurally impaired by the FanDuel deal.

Boyd also manages Sky River Casino in California for the Wilton Rancheria tribe for roughly $99M/year. The founding Boyd family controls approximately 30% of shares, with Marianne Boyd Johnson as Chairman, a meaningful governance factor. Competitive advantages include scale and geographic diversification across regional markets, the Boyd Rewards cross-property loyalty program, and largely cash-based revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality is lower than headline.** FY2025 revenue of $4,092M (+4.1%) includes roughly $576M of pass-through "online reimbursements" — gaming taxes recorded as equal revenue and expense at zero margin. Core gaming revenue rose only 2.1%.

**The earnings picture, properly adjusted, shows contraction.** Contributing factors to the operating-income and EBITDAR declines were $128.4M of impairment charges (Midwest & South and LV Locals), higher D&A, and erosion of high-margin online market-access fees.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $977M against capex of $588M (elevated by growth projects), for FCF of roughly $388M. Boyd retired its Term A Loan in de-levering with FanDuel proceeds. Simultaneously it repurchased $778M of stock in 2025 (10.1M shares), shrinking the share count from ~93M (2024 average) to ~74.3M by April 2026. The dividend was raised to $0.20/quarter. Q1 2026 added $155M of buybacks, and the board increased authorization by $500M on April 8, 2026 (total $3.3B authorized).

**Balance sheet.** Altman Z of 1.33 and D/E of 2.52 look alarming in isolation but are structural for an asset-heavy, REIT-leased casino operator, not a solvency signal. Boyd is covenant-compliant against a 4.5x maximum net-leverage covenant. The current ratio of 0.54 reflects an intentional negative-working-capital model. In January 2026 Boyd refinanced into a $1.45B revolver plus a $1.2B delayed-draw Term A facility, which carries a springing maturity of September 1, 2027 if the $1B 4.75% notes due December 2027 are not refinanced first.

**Q1 2026 (most timely data).** Revenue $997.4M (+0.6%), operating income −18% to $164M, EPS $1.37, Adjusted EBITDAR $317.4M versus $337.5M a year earlier. The segment detail is the story: Online EBITDAR −64%, LV Locals −6.2%, Downtown −9.7%, offset by Midwest & South +5.1%. This was Boyd's first double miss (EPS $1.60 actual vs $1.75 estimate for the quarter reported April 23, per the surprise record, −8.5%) after a run of beats.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is weak on organic fundamentals and dependent on financial engineering. Revenue growth is flattered by pass-through reimbursements. The per-share earnings story is driven almost entirely by the ~20% reduction in share count, funded partly by re-leveraging (debt rose back toward $2.3B in Q1 2026 as buybacks resumed), a mechanism that only sustains as long as regional cash flow holds.

Estimate revisions confirm negative momentum. Current-year consensus EPS fell to $7.16 from $7.42 ninety days ago (−3.5%), next-year to $7.83 from $8.07 (−2.9%), and current-quarter to $1.89 from $1.94 (−2.5%). The 30-day changes are near-flat to slightly negative. The trend is broadly down, the opposite of the estimate-revision profile that would support an aggressive growth rating.

Growth projects offer modest medium-term upside: the Norfolk, VA resort ($750M, transitional casino opened November 2025, full resort late 2027, developed via a consolidated VIE with the Pamunkey Tribe); Cadence Crossing in Henderson (opened March 2026); and the Par-A-Dice Illinois redevelopment. Portfolio reshaping includes the closure of Sam's Town Tunica and the pending Q3 2026 sale of Sam's Town Shreveport, plus the ~$53M Design Works Studios acquisition to bolster Boyd Interactive. These are incremental, not transformative, and Norfolk's full contribution is a 2027-2028 event. Weighing the segment mix, the structural Online step-down, and falling estimates, growth prospects score below average.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 3.8x is meaningless; it divides price by GAAP EPS distorted upward by the FanDuel gain. The relevant lens is forward EPS and EBITDAR. On current-year consensus EPS of $7.16, the stock at $87.72 trades at ~12.2x; on next-year $7.83, ~11.2x. That is a reasonable, not cheap, multiple for a regional gaming operator with flat-to-declining EBITDAR, fixed REIT rent obligations of ~$114M/year, and three active earnings drags.

The analyst target range runs $84 (low) to $110 (high), mean $94.9, median $92, placing the stock at roughly its median target with modest room to the mean. On stocks with rising estimates, we would treat analyst targets as floors; here estimates are falling, so the targets are not stale in the bullish direction and offer no independent confirmation of undervaluation.

We deliberately avoid rewarding the optically low trailing multiple. Cheapness by itself is not a buy signal, and in this case the "cheapness" is an accounting artifact rather than a genuine discount to intrinsic value. Without positive estimate revisions or an identified catalyst to re-rate the multiple, and with the operating business contracting, the valuation is fair rather than mispriced. Score: neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Boyd competes in fragmented, highly regulated regional markets against Caesars, Penn Entertainment, Churchill Downs, Golden Entertainment, and tribal operators, with secondary competition from lotteries, VGTs, and online gaming. Few new markets means competition intensifies via reinvestment and promotion within existing markets.

The competitive trajectory is mixed to slightly deteriorating. The FanDuel monetization signals that the passive "license landlord" windfall of the early legalization era is normalizing: Boyd cashed out at a high price but surrendered its most attractive online economics. Las Vegas Locals and Downtown are ceding profitability to destination softness. The offset is genuine: Midwest & South, steady slot-driven cash-based play, is carrying the company, absorbing Las Vegas weakness with Midwest strength. That is a stable-to-modestly-eroding competitive position rather than an improving one, which keeps this dimension at neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are more specific than sector truisms:

1. **Online market-access fee erosion (structural, self-inflicted).** Post-FanDuel, these near-pure-margin fees re-signed at lower rates; management guides the dilution to persist with a full year of impact. This is a permanent step-down.
2. **Midwest & South impairment overhang.** Both the 10-K and 10-Q flag that goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles in the segment have fair values that "did not significantly exceed" carrying values. Further deterioration triggers additional non-cash charges. The segment carrying the company's growth is closest to a write-down.
3. **Las Vegas destination/locals softness.** A macro-sensitive consumer segment weakening since Q3 2025. [unverified — brief does not support a broader "consumer-cliff" macro characterization]
4. **2027 refinancing / springing maturity.** The $1B 4.75% notes mature December 2027 with a September 1, 2027 springing maturity trigger. Refinancing will likely be at higher coupons, pressuring interest costs just as FanDuel-driven interest savings fade.
5. **GLPI Master Leases.** ~$114M/year triple-net rent with escalators, extended to April 2031, a large fixed obligation constraining FCF.
6. **Hawaiian market concentration** for Downtown and **Boyd family 30% control**.

Several 2026 Item 5.02 (director/officer change) 8-Ks (February 24, May 8, June 25, and a July 17 amendment) could not be fully verified and warrant follow-up.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 36.1% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 26.2% (a 10-point spread, 71st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (36.1% near-term vs 28.5% at six months); the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely around the next earnings print. The put/call skew is call-heavy (−13.8%) and OI ratio a benign 0.44, so positioning is not bearish, but the elevated near-term IV signals event risk that argues against paying up here. IV rose 4.7 points since early June on a roughly flat price, consistent with rising uncertainty into a catalyst rather than resolved complacency.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-110).** Midwest & South cash flow proves durable and continues offsetting Las Vegas weakness; the buyback-driven share-count reduction compounds per-share earnings; Norfolk ramps toward its full 2027 resort opening; and the de-levered balance sheet supports continued capital return. At ~11x on a stabilizing $7.83+ EPS with sustained buybacks, the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Texas Capital and Benchmark both initiated Buy in mid-2026 on this durable-regional-cash-flow-plus-capital-return framing.

**Bear Case ($72-76, roughly −13% to −18%).** The Online margin step-down proves permanent, Las Vegas softness deepens, and Midwest & South takes a further impairment. EBITDAR stays flat-to-down while buybacks are increasingly debt-financed and the 2027 refinance lifts interest expense. Per-share optics no longer mask a stalling operating business, and the multiple compresses toward the 52-week low near $76.

The reality sits in between. The operating business is contracting modestly, the balance sheet is sound but re-leveraging, capital return is real but financially engineered, and estimates are drifting lower. There is no clean catalyst to break the tie in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call. The operating business is softening, estimates are falling, and insiders are net sellers, which caps the bull case; but Midwest resilience, aggressive buybacks, and a de-levered balance sheet floor the downside. The stock near its high on declining estimates offers balanced risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive quarters (current-year consensus rising above $7.42 rather than falling), signaling the Online/Las Vegas drags have bottomed.
- Las Vegas Locals EBITDAR returns to year-over-year growth AND Midwest & South avoids further impairment on the annual test.
- A meaningful pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s that restores a genuine discount to normalized earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive double miss or Adjusted EBITDAR decline accelerating beyond the Q1 2026 pace.
- A further Midwest & South goodwill/intangible impairment, confirming the segment carrying the company is deteriorating.
- The 2027 notes refinanced at materially higher coupons that visibly compress FCF, or buybacks continuing to be debt-financed into a declining EBITDAR base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the near-term IV backwardation flags this as the key event), and the annual goodwill impairment test. The Q3 2026 close of the Sam's Town Shreveport sale and the pace of Norfolk capex ($300M in 2026) are secondary checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Boyd Gaming is a well-diversified regional operator with a management team that executed a smart FanDuel monetization and returns capital aggressively. But the clean operating trend is negative: Adjusted EBITDAR fell in 2025 and again in Q1 2026, the highest-margin Online segment lost roughly two-thirds of its profitability in a self-inflicted trade-off, Las Vegas is softening, and the Midwest & South segment that is carrying the company sits under an explicit impairment flag. Per-share earnings are being sustained largely by a shrinking share count financed partly with re-borrowed money, and consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window. With the stock near its 52-week high, net insider selling, elevated near-term implied volatility pricing event risk, and no positive catalyst, the risk/reward is balanced. We maintain HOLD and would need either a genuine estimate-revision inflection or a pullback into the mid-$70s to turn more constructive.
