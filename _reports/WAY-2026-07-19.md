**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Waystar is a cloud-based healthcare payments software company that automates the revenue cycle for providers, the process of getting paid by both insurers and patients. The business is high-quality by the standards of enterprise software: over 99% of revenue is recurring, adjusted EBITDA margins run above 43%, cash conversion exceeds net income, and the company processed more than 7.5 billion transactions and $2.4 trillion of gross claims volume in 2025 across roughly 60% of U.S. patients. FY2025 revenue grew 16.5% to $1,099.3M and net income swung from a $19.1M loss to $112.1M of profit. Q1 2026 revenue rose 22.4% to $313.9M with a 43.1% EBITDA margin.

Despite this, the stock trades at $22.27, only marginally above its $21.50 June 2024 IPO price and 46% below its ~$41.47 52-week high. The gap between fundamentals and price is explained less by business weakness than by mechanical share supply: private-equity sponsors (EQT, CPPIB, Bain, and now Advent via the Iodine deal) sold roughly 55 million shares across three secondary offerings in 2025, and Advent's lockup on Iodine consideration shares runs until approximately April 2027. This overhang has repeatedly absorbed demand and pinned the price near the IPO level even as earnings inflected positive.

The central analytical question is whether the underlying organic growth engine justifies buying into that overhang. Headline 22% growth is materially inflated by the Iodine acquisition (100% subscription, closed October 1, 2025) and by a fading tailwind from the February 2024 Change Healthcare cyberattack, which drove 30,000+ providers to Waystar. Net Revenue Retention has already decelerated from 113.5% to 110.5% as that windfall normalizes, and organic volume-based growth was closer to 7% in Q1. Even on a normalized basis, a low-double-digit-growth, 43%-margin, deleveraging recurring-revenue franchise trading at roughly 10-11x forward EBITDA is priced for disappointment that the numbers do not yet show. We rate WAY a BUY with disciplined sizing, recognizing that the re-rating catalyst (overhang clearing) is dated later than most.

## Company Overview

Waystar, headquartered in Lehi, Utah with a major Louisville, Kentucky operating base, sells software that manages healthcare revenue-cycle management (RCM): eligibility verification, claims submission, denial management, remittance, and patient payment collection. It serves approximately 30,000 clients representing over one million distinct providers, including 16 of the top 20 U.S. News Best Hospitals (17 pro forma with Iodine), and touches roughly one in three U.S. hospital discharges.

The revenue model is durable. Revenue splits between subscription revenue (~55% of Q1'26 revenue, driven by provider-count and minimum fees) and volume-based revenue (~44%, transaction and dollar-volume fees), with under 1% one-time implementation or hardware. The business splits into provider solutions (~70% of revenue, high margin with only 6-7% third-party cost) and patient payment solutions (lower margin, ~60% third-party cost). The company operates as a single reportable segment and markets a proprietary AI engine, AltitudeAI, plus Iodine's clinical-documentation-integrity models (IodineIQ).

The competitive structure favors incumbents. Waystar's data network feeds AI models that improve with scale, and its integration into provider billing workflows creates high switching costs. The company has completed ten acquisitions since 2018 and built a land-and-expand model in which client volume growth and cross-sell drive net revenue retention above 100%.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2024-to-FY2025 transition is the profitability inflection that matters. The swing from a net loss to net income was driven by operating leverage on a fixed-cost software base plus a roughly halving of interest expense as IPO proceeds retired debt.

Q1 2026 (ended March 31, 2026) revenue composition matters: subscription revenue rose 37.7% (Iodine is entirely subscription), while organic volume-based revenue grew 7.3%. Net income was $43.3M (13.8% margin) versus $29.3M. Adjusted EBITDA was $135.4M, up from a 42.0% margin while absorbing an acquisition. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.42 versus $0.32. Operating cash flow was $84.9M, up 32% and comfortably above net income, with modest capex/capitalized software of roughly $15M — high-quality software cash conversion.

The balance sheet reflects the private-equity roll-up heritage. Of $5.8B total assets, goodwill is $4.0B and intangibles $1.26B, meaning tangible book value is deeply negative once goodwill is stripped, a normal characteristic for a serial acquirer of software assets rather than a red flag in isolation. Total debt is approximately $1.48B (a $1,377.7M First Lien term loan plus a $100M receivables facility), with net leverage around 3.5x at the Iodine close and actively declining. The company repaid $20M of First Lien in February 2026 and refinanced its receivables facility, cutting the spread from SOFR+1.61% to SOFR+1.10% and extending it to 2029. The First Lien sits at SOFR+2.00% (effective rate 5.84%), with over $550M hedged via swaps at roughly 3.3-3.6% fixed. Cash of $34M plus $125M of investment securities provides adequate liquidity, and the accumulated deficit of $65.5M is shrinking.

The debt maturity profile is manageable: the $1.44B First Lien is due in 2029, making refinancing a 2028-29 story rather than a near-term concern. The main financial-health drags are the floating-rate exposure (a 100bp move is roughly $3.7M/quarter of interest, partly hedged) in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and heavy stock-based compensation ($11.4M/quarter, ~3.6% of revenue) with a ~$215M unvested equity overhang to be expensed over roughly three years.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story requires separating durable organic momentum from one-time and inorganic contributions. Iodine drives the bulk of the subscription line's growth. Underneath sits a fading windfall: the Change Healthcare cyberattack drove providers to Waystar at elevated win rates, contributing roughly $11M of incremental revenue in Q1 2025 alone. As that cohort normalizes, NRR has decelerated (TTM March 2025 to TTM March 2026).

That deceleration is the key metric to watch, but 110.5% NRR remains healthy and consistent with a durable land-and-expand model. Consensus estimates are stable — current-year EPS of $1.64 (revised +0.8% over 90 days) and next-year $1.85 (+0.3%) — with no directional deterioration, which supports the interpretation that the guidance revisions the market scrutinized in mid-2026 reflect conservatism and hard comps rather than demand weakness. The earnings track record is solid: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with a 7.8% beat in Q1 2026.

The forward growth drivers are structural. U.S. administrative healthcare costs run roughly $440B annually, with up to 60M claims denied yearly for administrative errors, the exact problem Waystar and Iodine address. Provider labor cost pressure, the push toward AI-driven automation, and rising claim-denial complexity all favor RCM automation. Iodine expands Waystar's addressable market by more than 15% and deepens penetration in large health systems, with management projecting accretion to revenue growth and non-GAAP EPS in 2027 and over $15M of run-rate synergies within 18-24 months. This does not follow the classic cyclical-inflection template of the world model's high-conviction signals — estimate revisions are flat, not +30% — so the growth case rests on durability of a mid-teens compounder rather than an earnings snap-back. That earns a solid but not exceptional Growth Prospects score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.27 the stock carries a trailing/forward P/E near 33x on GAAP EPS, but GAAP substantially understates cash economics given heavy purchase-accounting amortization from the roll-up. On non-GAAP EPS the picture is materially cheaper: current-year EPS implies roughly 13.6x forward, and next-year roughly 12x. On enterprise value, with a ~$4.3B market cap plus ~$1.32B net debt (total debt less ~$159M cash and investment securities) against forward adjusted EBITDA in the ~$570-590M range, EV/EBITDA sits near 9-10x, a striking discount for a 43%-margin, 99%-recurring software franchise growing double digits.

The peer set is heterogeneous and only partially informative. Health Care Technology comparables show wide dispersion (DOCS at 20.4x, OMCL at 85.9x, HSTM at 39.2x, SOLV at 9.7x, CERT loss-making) with a peer-median P/E of 39.2x. Waystar trades at roughly a third of that GAAP multiple, and on the more relevant recurring-EBITDA basis it sits well below software peers of comparable quality and growth. The valuation case does not rest on cheapness alone; a low multiple by itself is more often a value trap than an opportunity. Here the specific reason the market is mispricing the stock is identifiable and non-fundamental: PE-sponsor secondary supply, which has held the stock near its IPO price despite the earnings inflection. That is a mechanical, self-liquidating pressure, and Advent's lockup expiry gives it a rough clearing date.

Analyst targets bracket meaningful upside: low $25, mean $33.57, median $33, high $44. Given a sub-30x forward-earnings multiple and PEG under 1.5 on the non-GAAP path, these targets function more as floors than ceilings if organic growth holds and the overhang clears. We anchor a base case at ~$30 (roughly 16x next-year non-GAAP EPS, a modest premium to the current depressed multiple and still a discount to peers), with the bull case toward the high-$30s/low-$40s as sponsor supply is absorbed.

## Competitive Landscape

Waystar's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which matters more than absolute level. The most important dynamic is that the Change Healthcare (Optum/UnitedHealth) cyberattack, which crippled the dominant clearinghouse, allowed Waystar to position as the resilient alternative and permanently onboard providers, some in as little as 48 hours. While the associated revenue tailwind is fading, the client relationships and the reputational positioning as the safe incumbent are durable.

The moat rests on three reinforcing assets: a proprietary data network spanning ~60% of U.S. patients that improves AI model accuracy at scale, deep workflow integration that raises switching costs, and platform breadth spanning both provider and patient payment solutions. Competitors include Optum/Change Healthcare, R1 RCM, Availity, and various point solutions, but Waystar's combination of scale, data, and AI differentiation is difficult to replicate. The Iodine acquisition strengthens the position by adding clinical-documentation-integrity capabilities and deepening penetration among large health systems, expanding the platform's cross-sell surface.

The one structural competitive nuance is margin mix. Patient payment solutions carry ~60% third-party cost versus 6-7% for provider solutions, and management expects the patient-payment mix to increase slightly over time, which could exert mild downward pressure on blended gross margin. Iodine's high-margin, 100%-subscription profile offsets this at the EBITDA level.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is moderate, with the idiosyncratic risks concentrated and partially priced.

**Fading Change Healthcare tailwind.** The one-time windfall that inflated 2024-2025 growth and win rates is normalizing, visible in the NRR decline. As comps toughen, organic growth will optically decelerate, and the market may struggle to distinguish genuine growth from the windfall's roll-off. This is a real but understood risk, and 110.5% NRR still signals net expansion.

**PE-sponsor overhang.** This is both the reason the stock is cheap and the reason it may stay cheap. Additional secondaries between now and the April 2027 Iodine-consideration lockup expiry could cap the stock or trigger air pockets. The overhang is self-liquidating, but the timeline is long.

**Iodine integration.** A $1.26B deal (>25% of market cap) with accretion not projected until 2027 carries execution risk, though the company's ten-deal track record is favorable.

**Leverage and rates.** ~$1.4B of floating-rate debt in a higher-for-longer environment is a modest drag, mitigated by hedging (~$550M swapped at ~3.3-3.6%) and no maturity wall before 2029.

**Regulatory/policy.** Revenue is tied to healthcare transaction volumes exposed to Medicaid and reimbursement policy changes; HIPAA and data-privacy compliance are ongoing obligations. The 10-Q notes no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 73.0% against 49.6% one-year historical volatility — rich, in the 80th percentile of the current chain — with a backwardated term structure (73.0% near-term vs 56.0% at six months) that signals the market expects an imminent catalyst, likely the next earnings print. The implied one-month move is ±17.7%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (2.0%) and put/call volume is light at 0.33, so this is elevated-uncertainty positioning rather than a directional bearish bet. The P/C open-interest ratio collapsed from 9.18 to 1.01 over the last month even as the stock rose 18%, consistent with a bearish-positioning unwind. For a fundamental buyer, rich near-term IV argues against chasing and in favor of scaling in or using the elevated premium to sell puts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$38-44).** Sponsor supply is absorbed as the Advent lockup approaches and clears in 2027, removing the mechanical cap. Organic growth stabilizes in the low double digits with NRR holding above 108-110%, Iodine delivers its synergies and turns accretive in 2027, and the market re-rates the recurring-revenue base toward software-peer multiples. EBITDA margins expand past 43% on operating leverage. On ~$2.00+ non-GAAP EPS and a ~18-20x multiple, the stock reaches the high-$30s to low-$40s, converging with the $44 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$16-17).** The Change Healthcare cohort churns faster than expected, dragging NRR below 108% and revealing organic growth in the mid-single digits once Iodine anniversaries. Continued or larger secondary offerings pressure the price, and the market treats the business as a low-growth, levered software asset. The stock retests its ~$17.26 52-week low, roughly 22% downside.

**Base Case (~$30).** Organic growth normalizes to low double digits, Iodine integrates on plan, deleveraging continues, and the overhang gradually clears. A ~16x multiple on ~$1.85 next-year non-GAAP EPS supports ~$30, roughly 35% above the current price.

The setup is asymmetric in the buyer's favor: the downside is bounded by a real, cash-generative, deleveraging franchise, while the upside requires only that a temporary supply pressure clears against unchanged fundamentals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$30**, derived from ~16x next-year non-GAAP EPS of $1.85, a modest premium to today's depressed ~12x forward and still a discount to health-tech software peers. Upside to base case is ~35%.

**Bull case target: ~$40** (~18-20x on $2.00+ EPS as the overhang clears and multiple converges toward peers), roughly +80%.

**Bear case target: ~$17** (retest of the 52-week low on faster churn plus continued sponsor selling), roughly -22%.

**Timeframe: 12-24 months.** The primary re-rating catalyst, Advent's lockup expiry around April 2027, makes the thesis deliberately longer-dated than a typical earnings-cycle trade. Interim catalysts include quarterly NRR/organic-growth prints and evidence of Iodine synergy delivery.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net Revenue Retention falling below 106-108% in consecutive quarters, signaling structural churn rather than windfall normalization.
2. A guidance cut that management attributes to demand weakness rather than comps, or negative consensus EPS revisions exceeding ~5% over 90 days.
3. A new, larger-than-expected secondary offering combined with deteriorating fundamentals — supply pressure alone on intact fundamentals is not an invalidation, but supply plus fundamental cracks would be.

## Conclusion

Waystar is a profitable, cash-generative, moat-protected healthcare software franchise whose share price has been held near its IPO level by mechanical private-equity supply rather than by any deterioration in the business. On the metrics that matter — 43% adjusted EBITDA margins, 99% recurring revenue, positive and growing net income, cash conversion above net income, and active deleveraging — this is a higher-quality business than a ~12x forward-earnings multiple implies. The clearest risks are the fading Change Healthcare tailwind (best tracked through NRR) and continued sponsor selling into the April 2027 lockup expiry, both understood and, in the case of the overhang, self-liquidating.

The score trajectory has improved steadily (5.7 in March to 6.6 now) with three consecutive BUY calls, and the current data confirm rather than reverse that trend: the price has fallen since the prior report while fundamentals held, which improves risk/reward rather than damaging the thesis. We maintain a BUY with disciplined sizing, mindful that the primary catalyst is dated and that rich near-term implied volatility argues for scaling in rather than chasing. A director's open-market purchase at $26.25, above the current price, is a modest but genuine vote of confidence amid otherwise routine insider activity.
