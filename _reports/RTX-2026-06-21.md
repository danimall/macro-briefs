**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX is executing an operational recovery. Revenue grew 9.7% in 2025 to $88.6B, operating margin expanded from 8.1% to 10.5% and reached 11.6% in Q1 2026, and EPS rose from $3.55 (2024) to $4.96 (2025). The backlog stands at a record $271B, roughly three times annual revenue, split $162B commercial and $109B defense, with Raytheon defense bookings up 51% YoY in Q1. The company has beaten consensus EPS in four straight quarters, deleveraged (debt/cap 40%→36%), and earned a Moody's outlook upgrade to Baa1/positive. Jefferies moved to Buy with a $220 target on June 4, and Citi added RTX to a 30-day catalyst watch ahead of Q2.

The business quality and growth durability are not in question. The question is price and the asymmetry of the binary overhangs. At $185.60, RTX trades at ~26.9x current-year consensus EPS of $6.91 and ~24.5x next-year $7.58, a discount to GE Aerospace (41.3x) and Howmet (61.4x) but a premium to the pure defense primes (LMT 26.6x, GD 22.6x). The dividend yield is 1.57%, below the peer median and too thin to anchor a value case. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (+1.5% CY, +0.9% NY over 90 days), so rising consensus is not driving re-rating; the recovery is largely recognized.

We rate RTX a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The specific reason we stop short of BUY: the forward multiple already embeds the margin recovery while the upside catalysts (Golden Dome awards, tariff refunds) are explicitly not in the numbers per management, and a cluster of unresolved binary liabilities — ~$2.8B+ in DCMA Cost Accounting Standards claims now remanded to the appeals court, a freshly-engaged 3-year DPA compliance monitor whose breach triggers prosecution, the live Powder Metal SEC subpoena, and a new executive order that RTX itself flags could limit its distributions and buybacks — sits against only modest upside to the consensus mean of $215.73 (~16%). The risk/reward is roughly symmetric near current levels, not the asymmetric upside a BUY requires.

## Company Overview

RTX is one of the largest aerospace and defense primes globally, formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies (which had absorbed Rockwell Collins) with Raytheon Company. It operates three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** — FY25 revenue $30.2B, 16.3% operating margin. Avionics, interiors, power and electrical, landing and sensor systems, connected aviation. The highest-margin segment, levered to the commercial aftermarket. Boeing and Airbus together are ~16% of segment sales.
- **Pratt & Whitney** — FY25 revenue $32.9B, 7.9% operating margin. The GTF (PW1000G) family on the A320neo/A220/E2 (2,600+ aircraft, 90+ operators) and the F135, sole engine for the F-35. Lowest current margin owing to the Powder Metal Matter and the OEM production ramp, but the highest long-tail aftermarket value — P&W maintenance is roughly 45% of company RPO, realized over up to 20 years.
- **Raytheon** — FY25 revenue $28.0B, 11.5% operating margin. Missiles and integrated air and missile defense: Patriot, AMRAAM, SM-3/SM-6, LTAMDS, NASAMS, SPY-6, Tomahawk, Stinger, Javelin, hypersonics.

About 180,000 employees across ~225 facilities in ~25 countries. The backlog underwrites multi-year revenue visibility, with roughly 25% recognized within twelve months.

## Financial Analysis

The recovery trajectory is broad-based. Revenue moved from $68.9B (2023, depressed by the Powder Metal charge) to $80.7B (2024) to $88.6B (2025), with $8.9B of organic growth spread across all three segments. Q1 2026 continued at +8.7% YoY to $22.1B.

Margin expansion went 5.2% (2023) → 8.1% (2024) → 10.5% (2025) → 11.6% (Q1'26), with every segment improving YoY in the latest quarter. P&W margin reached 8.7% in Q1'26 as the Powder Metal drag fades. Diluted EPS climbed $2.23 → $3.55 → $4.96, with Q1'26 at $1.51 versus $1.14 a year earlier. The Q1 effective tax rate dropped to 14.4% from 17.0% on the FDDEI benefit from the 2025 tax act, a real but partly non-repeatable tailwind.

Cash generation is healthy. Operating cash flow was $10.6B in 2025; free cash flow $7.9B (a 9% margin) after $2.6B capex. Q1'26 OCF rose to $1.86B from $1.31B. The balance sheet improved: total debt down to $37.9B from $41.3B, cash $7.4B, average maturity ~12 years at 4.5%. S&P holds BBB+/stable.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.45, nominally in the "distress" zone, is misleading and should be disregarded. It is depressed by ~$85B of goodwill and intangibles (roughly half of $171B total assets) from the UTC/Raytheon/Rockwell mergers, which deflate the asset-efficiency and equity-to-asset inputs. A company with $10.6B operating cash flow, a recent credit-outlook upgrade, and a $271B backlog is not distressed. One real consequence of the merger accounting is ~$2.0B/year of acquisition-amortization (~$1.15 of 2025 EPS), a genuine non-cash drag on GAAP earnings that explains part of the elevated trailing P/E.

Capital allocation is conservative post-deleveraging. The dividend was raised to $0.68/quarter ($2.72 annualized, 1.57% yield). Buybacks were minimal in 2025 ($50M) after the large 2023 ASR ($12.9B); only ~$0.6B remains on the $11B authorization. This is appropriate balance-sheet repair, but it means shareholders are not receiving meaningful return-of-capital support at current prices.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two structural tailwinds and one optionality layer.

First, the defense up-cycle: Ukraine, the Middle East, and Indo-Pacific demand, an FY26 NDAA near $900B, and Raytheon bookings up 51% YoY in Q1 ($6.6B). The $109B defense backlog converts this demand into recognizable revenue over multiple years.

Second, the commercial aerospace aftermarket recovery drives Collins (the highest-margin segment) and the long-tail P&W maintenance stream. The GTF Advantage certification in 2025 restores some of the franchise dented by Powder Metal.

Third, optionality not in the numbers: the 2025 reconciliation act adds $156.2B for the Department of War through 2029, including $24.4B for Golden Dome missile defense and $25.4B for munitions resiliency. RTX's Patriot/SM-3/LTAMDS/sensor portfolio is well-positioned, but management explicitly cautions that materiality depends on actual awards and timing. This is upside, not base case.

The constraint on the growth score is that estimate revisions are flat — CY consensus moved just +1.5% over 90 days, next-year +0.9%, and next-quarter fell 2.7%. Rapidly rising estimates that pull the multiple up on inflection names are absent here. The four consecutive beats (16.1%, 4.6%, 19.5%, 7.0%) confirm execution but have not produced upward revision velocity. This is a steady mid-to-high-single-digit revenue compounder with margin expansion, not an accelerating inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $185.60, RTX trades at ~34.8x trailing earnings, ~24.5x next-year. Against the peer set:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |

| BA | 88.8 | — |
| GE | 41.3 | 0.6% |
| HWM | 61.4 | 0.2% |
| LMT | 26.6 | 2.6% |
| GD | 22.6 | 1.9% |
| **RTX** | **34.8 (trailing)** | **1.6%** |

On a forward basis RTX (~26.9x) sits below the commercial-aero comps (GE, HWM) and roughly in line with the pure defense primes (LMT, GD), despite a more diversified commercial-plus-defense mix. The trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because ~$1.15 of EPS is non-cash merger amortization; on a cash-EPS basis the gap to the defense primes narrows. The stock is not egregiously expensive.

But it is not cheap either, and the burden for a BUY is showing the market is mispricing it. The forward multiple already embeds the margin recovery to 11.6%; the upside catalysts (Golden Dome awards, ~$0.5B tariff refund) are excluded from guidance per management and are option value, not anchor. The 1.57% yield provides little support. Consensus mean target is $215.73 (median $220), ~16% above spot, with a low of $180 (essentially at the money) and high of $242. The $180 low target — below current price — and the recent appearance of sell/strong-sell ratings (analyst sentiment is flagged "degrading," with sells going 1→2 and strong-sells 0→1) tell you the upside is contested.

This is a full but not stretched multiple where the recovery is recognized and the next leg requires either Golden Dome conversion or a further multiple re-rate toward GE, neither of which is yet evidenced. We score valuation 5.3: priced fairly for the visible fundamentals, with the asymmetry that would justify a BUY dependent on contingent catalysts.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's position is strong and largely stable. Its differentiator versus peers is the balanced commercial (~57% of sales including commercial aero and foreign commercial) and defense mix, a global footprint, and now-lower leverage than the immediate post-merger years. The $271B backlog (up from $218B a year earlier) is the standout competitive asset, providing visibility few peers match.

The competitive trajectory has softened modestly from prior reads (the dimension stepped down from 8.5 to 7.5 over recent reports), reflecting real pressures the filings flag: new defense entrants with different technology and business models, government demands for expanded IP rights, foreign-government sponsorship of competitors, and ~$14B notional in offset/ICIP obligations required to win international defense work. In engines, the GTF efficiency lead (GTF Advantage certified 2025) remains the key asset versus CFM (GE/Safran), though Powder Metal dented the franchise. None of this is share erosion — RTX is gaining defense bookings — so we hold competitive position at 7.5, the highest of the six dimensions and the strongest pillar of the thesis.

## Risk Assessment

RTX carries an unusual density of binary, company-specific overhangs that are not fully priced and that collectively cap the risk score:

1. **DCMA Cost Accounting Standards claims against Pratt** — the most under-discussed risk. Multiple claims: ~$1.7B plus interest (IR&D allocation 2007–2019), $1.1B (2019–2023), plus collaborator-parts claims. RTX believes them without merit, and the ASBCA ruled substantially in P&W's favor on the collaborator-parts methodology in 2021, but the CAFC remanded in December 2025, reopening the question. Aggregate identified exposure (excluding interest and the unquantified collaborator-parts claims) is at least ~$2.8B.

2. **DPA compliance monitor (newly engaged April 2026).** The legacy Raytheon bribery/defective-pricing matters were resolved in 2024 for ~$964M under two DPAs plus an SEC Administrative Order. An independent monitor was engaged in April 2026, starting the 3-year DPA term; a breach could trigger prosecution. A separate ITAR Consent Agreement has $33M due August 2026.

3. **Powder Metal SEC subpoena and securities litigation.** The securities class action was dismissed in September 2025 but is on appeal to the 2nd Circuit; derivative suits remain. The customer-compensation accrual fell to $0.5B (Q1'26) from $1.7B (YE24), and ~$0.7B of 2026 cash outflow remains. The financial tail is shrinking but the SEC and litigation tails are live.

4. **Defense-contractor executive order.** A new EO may limit distributions, buybacks, and executive-comp incentives for contractors deemed to be underperforming or producing too slowly. RTX explicitly flags this as a potential adverse effect on its stock, a novel capital-return risk given the company's reliance on the dividend with buybacks already minimal.

5. **Concentration and geopolitics.** U.S. government = 38% of sales; Airbus = 29% of P&W sales; China sanctions on Raytheon and a Collins JV; Russia counter-sanctions naming RTX executives. These are partly priced sector risks but compound the company-specific list.

On the positive side, the tariff refund (~$0.5B paid in IEEPA tariffs ruled unauthorized, refund process pending, nothing booked) is a hidden call option, and the Powder Metal accrual wind-down de-risks the largest 2023 issue.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral with a mild caution tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 27.7% versus 24.4% one-year historical vol, modestly rich (spread 3.3pp), and at only the 6th percentile within the current chain — uncertainty is not elevated. Put/call skew is +4.0% (some put demand) at the front, but the term structure is flat and skew turns negative at six months (-2.4%), indicating no structural bearish positioning further out. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.67 to 0.92 as the stock fell 3.8% from $193 to $185.60 into the Q2 catalyst window, consistent with hedging into earnings rather than directional bearishness. Implied move at the nearest expiry is ±3.4%. Short interest is negligible at 1.2% of float (2.9 days to cover), so no squeeze dynamics and no informed bearish crowd. The options market is pricing an ordinary earnings event, neither confirming nor challenging the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$220–242)
The defense up-cycle and commercial aftermarket drive sustained high-single-digit revenue growth; margins continue toward 12%+ as Powder Metal fully rolls off and the OEM ramp matures. Golden Dome awards begin converting RTX's missile-defense portfolio into incremental backlog not yet in the numbers. The tariff refund materializes as a one-time tailwind. The multiple re-rates toward GE Aerospace as the commercial mix is recognized. Jefferies' $220 and the $242 high target are reached on a combination of EPS toward $7.58+ and multiple expansion. This delivers ~16–30% upside.

### Bear Case (~$150–160)
The DCMA CAS claims resolve unfavorably on remand, creating a multi-billion-dollar charge. The DPA monitor surfaces a compliance issue, or the SEC Powder Metal matter escalates. The defense-contractor EO constrains capital return. A commercial aftermarket softening slows Collins. The multiple compresses toward the defense primes' lower bound on flat estimate revisions, with the $180 analyst low target and below as the floor. This is ~14–19% downside.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric — ~16% to the consensus mean against ~15% to the low end of the bear case — which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY. The growth and quality are real, but they are priced, and the upside requires contingent catalysts while the downside list is concrete and partly unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), the Citi catalyst-watch event, where beat-and-raise potential and any Golden Dome award commentary will be most informative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$160s/low-$170s (~24x forward or below) that restores asymmetric upside to the $215+ consensus mean.
- Confirmed Golden Dome awards adding quantified backlog, moving the upside from option value into the base case.
- Resolution of the DCMA CAS remand and/or DPA monitor period without material adverse findings, removing the binary tail and justifying a defense-prime-to-GE re-rate.
- A return of upward estimate-revision velocity (CY consensus revisions >+5% over 90 days) signaling the recovery is not yet fully captured.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse DCMA CAS ruling or DPA breach producing a charge above ~$2B, or SEC escalation on Powder Metal.
- Invocation of the defense-contractor EO restricting RTX's distributions or buybacks.
- Margin reversal (operating margin falling back below 10%) or a quarterly miss after the four-beat streak, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- Sustained downward estimate revisions (CY consensus falling >3% over 90 days).

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality aerospace and defense prime delivering a genuine operational recovery, with a record backlog, expanding margins, a deleveraged balance sheet, and a credit-outlook upgrade. The growth thesis is durable and supported by structural defense demand. We rate it HOLD because the entry price at ~26.9x forward earnings and a 1.57% yield already reflects that recovery, the upside catalysts (Golden Dome, tariff refunds) are explicitly contingent and not in guidance, estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating, and a dense set of unresolved binary risks — DCMA CAS claims now remanded, a freshly-engaged DPA monitor, the Powder Metal SEC subpoena, and a capital-return EO RTX itself flags — sits against only ~16% upside to the consensus mean. That is a fair-value setup with roughly symmetric risk/reward, not the asymmetric upside that warrants a BUY. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$160s or on resolution of one or more of the binary overhangs.
