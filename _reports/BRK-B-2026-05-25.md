## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway trades at $486.38, representing approximately 1.45x book value ($717.4B equity / ~2.15B equivalent Class B shares). The company reported Q1 2026 operating earnings growth of 17.7% YoY, resumed share repurchases ($235M) for the first time in six quarters, and sits on approximately $373.5 billion in cash and T-Bills (net of unsettled purchase payables), a substantial portion of its $1.05 trillion market cap held in risk-free assets earning 4%+ yields. Greg Abel's $14.5M open-market purchase on March 4, 2026 is a highly informative data point for this thesis: the new CEO, with full visibility into the company's operations and pipeline, chose to deploy capital through his revocable trust at roughly current prices.

The market may be underappreciating the composition of Berkshire's value. Valuing the operating businesses requires careful separation of investment income generated by cash and securities from earnings attributable to the underlying operating franchises. After stripping out cash/T-bills and the public equity portfolio, and removing the associated investment income those assets generate (~$14B annually), the operating businesses generate approximately $30–31B in annual earnings. The implied multiple on those businesses, while below what comparable industrial franchises trade at independently, is not as extreme as a simple headline calculation would suggest.

The primary headwinds are well-known: succession risk (now partially addressed by 5 months of Abel execution), modest growth, and the challenge of deploying approximately $374B productively. The stock has declined 3.4% over the past 6 months from its $516.85 high, creating a modestly better entry point while fundamentals have improved (Q1 operating earnings beat, buybacks resumed).

## Company Overview

Berkshire Hathaway is a $1.05 trillion diversified holding company headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska. It operates through five major segments:

- **Insurance** (GEICO, BH Primary, BH Reinsurance): $176.9B in float, $9.5B pre-tax underwriting profit (2025), $529B in invested assets
- **BNSF Railroad**: 32,500+ route miles, $5.5B after-tax earnings, 65.5% operating ratio
- **Berkshire Hathaway Energy**: Regulated utilities serving 5.4M customers, $4.0B after-tax earnings
- **Manufacturing, Service & Retailing**: $214B revenues, $17.5B pre-tax earnings spanning aerospace (PCC), chemicals (Lubrizol, OxyChem), housing (Clayton), retail, and consumer products
- **Investment Portfolio**: ~$288B in equities (Q1 2026), ~$373.5B in cash/T-Bills (net, Q1 2026)

The company employs 387,800 people across dozens of subsidiaries. Its economic model centers on generating low-cost insurance float and deploying it into wholly-owned businesses and public equities at attractive returns.

## Growth Analysis

Berkshire's growth profile is modest but improving:

- Operating earnings CAGR (2022-2025): approximately 10-13%, driven significantly by rising insurance investment income alongside operational improvement
- Q1 2026 operating growth: +17.7% headline / +7.2% organic
- BNSF volumes: +0.3% (2025), improving operating efficiency
- GEICO policies-in-force: growing again after years of deliberate contraction
- PCC aerospace: +34.2% pre-tax earnings in 2025 (tied to commercial aviation ramp)
- OxyChem acquisition (Jan 2026): based on Occidental's recent reported results for the chemicals segment (discontinued operations income of $495M pre-tax in 2025 and $285M in 2024), near-term annual earnings contribution may be considerably below peak levels, though management may target operational improvements over time

The growth headwinds are real:
- Revenue flat at $371.4B
- Pilot Travel Centers: -69% earnings YoY in 2025, continued decline Q1 2026
- Insurance cycle softening per Abel's May 2026 commentary
- EPS estimates revised down: -15.4% for current year (90-day), -2.4% for next year
- Only 2 analysts cover the stock with formal estimates, limiting revision data quality

The cash position is both optionality and drag. At current T-Bill rates, it contributes ~$16-17B annually, but deployed into operating businesses earning 15%+ ROIC, the same capital could generate substantially more. The gap between current and potential earnings from that cash is the single largest growth lever; Abel needs one or two large acquisitions to unlock it.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current metrics:**
- Price: $486.38
- Trailing P/E: 14.5x (distorted by investment gains volatility)
- Forward P/E: 22.8x (on formally tracked estimates, which are sparse)
- Price/Book: ~1.45x ($727.2B Q1 2026 equity / ~2.157B Class B equivalent shares = ~$337 book value per share)

**Sum-of-parts analysis:**

A proper sum-of-parts must account for the fact that a significant portion of Berkshire's operating earnings (~$14B from insurance investment income and other investment-related income) is generated by the cash and securities being stripped out. Adjusting for this:

| Component | Value | Per-share equivalent |
|-----------|-------|---------------------|
| Cash/T-Bills (net) | ~$374B | ~$173 |
| Public equities (Q1 2026 gross) | $288B (after-tax: ~$245B) | ~$114 |
| Operating businesses (ex-investment income, ~$30B at 13-15x) | $390-450B | ~$181-209 |
| **Total intrinsic value** | **~$1,009-1,069B** | **~$468-496** |

This suggests the stock is trading approximately in line with or modestly below a reasonable intrinsic value estimate. The valuation is supported by:

1. Substantial cash and liquid assets provide meaningful downside protection, though not all cash should be treated as excess given $176.9B of insurance float obligations and operating liquidity needs.
2. Buybacks resumed at current levels. Abel buying back stock at ~1.4x book signals management views current price as at or below intrinsic value.
3. Abel's revocable trust purchased shares at approximately today's price ($725-733K/share Class A equivalent, ~$487/share Class B).
4. Operating business valuations remain reasonable relative to quality, even at multiples above the unadjusted headline calculation, given the durability and diversification of the earnings streams.

The analyst consensus target mean of $520.33 (7% upside) and high of $570 (17% upside) bracket the sum-of-parts range. The low target of $481 sits just below current price, suggesting limited downside consensus.

Versus peers: the peer comparison data provided (OWL at 85.5x, KLAR at -29.5x) is not relevant. Berkshire's unique combination of insurance, railroad, utilities, and diversified manufacturing/services makes direct peer comparison difficult, as the company itself acknowledges in its filings.

## Competitive Landscape

**Insurance:** Berkshire's competitive moat in insurance is unrivaled at scale. The $333B statutory surplus enables it to write catastrophe risks ($15B single-event maximum) that no other insurer can absorb. GEICO faces competitive pressure from Progressive (which grew policies 11% vs. GEICO's 2% in Q1), but the company is investing in advertising and technology to close the gap.

**Railroad:** BNSF is one of two Class I railroads in the Western U.S. (alongside Union Pacific). Its 65.5% operating ratio ranks 5th of 6 Class I railroads; operational improvement represents a tangible opportunity under Abel's management focus. Volume growth is modest but the duopoly structure provides pricing power.

**Energy:** BHE's regulated model provides stable, predictable returns. Data center power demand is an emerging tailwind (MEC saw 9.6% volume growth in 2025 from data storage customers). The OBBBA phase-out of clean energy credits creates medium-term uncertainty but BHE's diverse generation portfolio limits exposure.

**Manufacturing:** PCC's aerospace earnings (+34.2% in 2025) benefit from the multi-year Boeing/Airbus production ramp. OxyChem adds a top-3 position in North American PVC/chlor-alkali. Lubrizol faces long-term EV headwinds but remains cash-generative for decades.

The Competitive Position score reflects a slight downgrade from prior reports due to GEICO's market share loss to Progressive and the insurance cycle softening that Abel acknowledged.

## Risk Assessment

**Succession (Medium-High):** The most consequential CEO transition in corporate America is 5 months old. Abel has demonstrated competence (strong Q1, resumed buybacks at appropriate levels, open-market purchase) but his capital allocation capability at Berkshire's scale is unproven. The organizational culture appears intact: no executive departures, operating managers unchanged.

**Cash Deployment (Medium):** Finding deals that earn attractive returns is inherently difficult at this scale. Abel's $9.5B OxyChem acquisition was competent but small relative to the cash position. Every quarter without deployment effectively earns only the T-Bill rate on that capital.

**Insurance Cycle (Medium):** Abel explicitly warned of market softening at the May 2026 meeting. If combined ratios deteriorate from current levels, underwriting earnings could decline 30-40% from peak.

**Estimate Revisions (Low-Medium):** Current year EPS estimates down 15.4% over 90 days. Only 2 analysts formally track the stock, and Berkshire's quarterly earnings are notoriously lumpy due to GAAP mark-to-market on the equity portfolio.

**Geopolitical/Macro (Low-Medium):** The 10-K contains newly strengthened geopolitical risk language. Trade tariffs affect building products and manufacturing businesses. The diversification across dozens of subsidiaries and the domestic focus of most operations limits concentration risk.

**Pilot Travel Centers (Low):** Earnings collapsed -69% in 2025 but this is a <5% position within Berkshire's earnings stream. Goodwill cushion is thin (~$1.5B above carrying value on $6.5B goodwill), creating potential impairment risk.

**Options Market Signal:** Short interest at only 1.0% of float and days-to-cover at 2.3 indicate minimal bearish positioning. The low beta (0.622) and relatively tight trading range ($455-$517 over 52 weeks) reflect the market's view of Berkshire as a low-volatility, stable value compounding vehicle. No unusual options activity signals detected.

## Conclusion

Berkshire Hathaway at $486 offers a BUY for investors seeking capital preservation with modest appreciation potential. The operating businesses represent high-quality, diversified franchises trading at reasonable multiples when properly adjusted for investment income associated with the cash and securities portfolio. Abel's open-market purchase at current prices provides a positive insider signal. The substantial cash and liquid asset position provides meaningful downside protection given the diversification of the underlying businesses, though this cash coexists with $176.9B in insurance float obligations and cannot be entirely characterized as excess distributable value. The 0.622 beta provides portfolio protection in a rising-rate/stagflation environment, and the resumed buybacks signal management alignment.

The declining estimate revisions (-15.4% current year) reflect the mechanical reality of investment gains volatility flowing through GAAP earnings, not operational deterioration. The stock offers modestly asymmetric risk/reward: limited downside (asset backing + diversification), with upside catalysts from acquisition deployment, continued operational improvement at BNSF, and gradual succession risk reduction as Abel builds his track record.

This is a risk-adjusted opportunity rather than a high-growth thesis. In a macro environment characterized by rate uncertainty, geopolitical risk, and potential equity market volatility, Berkshire's combination of cash optionality, diversification, and low correlation represents genuine portfolio utility at a reasonable price.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected price/book (1.45x vs. 1.35x), revised SOTP analysis removing double-counted investment income, reduced OxyChem earnings estimate, and corrected FCF conversion ratio collectively narrow the margin of safety from the originally stated 25-30% to a more modest 5-7% base-case upside, which may not fully support the BUY recommendation for all investor profiles.
