## Executive Summary

G-III is a wholesale apparel design and distribution platform mid-way through replacing about a third of its revenue base. Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger licensed product generated roughly $828M, or 28.0%, of FY2026 net sales of $2,957.0M. Management's FY2027 guidance of approximately $2.71B "incorporates the anticipated loss of approximately $470 million" of that revenue, and the 10-Q expiration table implies the residual ~$358M disappears during FY2028. The company has responded by shifting the wholesale mix toward owned brands — proprietary brands were 55.6% of wholesale sales in FY2026 versus 45.1% two years earlier — and that shift is visible in the numbers: Q1 FY2027 wholesale gross margin excluding the tariff catch-up was 43.8% against 40.4% a year earlier, a 340 basis point underlying improvement driven by pricing and mix.

The offset is that cost growth consumed the margin gain. Reported Q1 FY2027 operating profit of $85.2M includes $102.7M of cost-of-goods relief relating to inventory sold in the prior year; stripping that leaves an underlying operating loss of roughly $17.5M against $8.5M of profit a year earlier, corroborated by management's own adjusted EBITDA of $(7.7)M versus $+19.5M. SG&A rose $23.8M on sales that fell $47.6M. Q1 is the seasonally smallest quarter (Q3 and Q4 were about 59.5% of FY2026 sales), so the loss is not diagnostic, but FY2027 guidance itself points down: non-GAAP EPS of $2.15–2.25 versus $2.61 in FY2026, and adjusted EBITDA of $178–182M versus $192.4M.

At $36.72 the stock sits 2.2% below its 52-week high after a 50% twelve-month advance, trading at 16.7x the guided non-GAAP midpoint and 6.5x pre-deal EV/adjusted EBITDA. The 6.5x figure credits $394.2M of cash that is committed to funding roughly $500M of the Marc Jacobs transaction — 32% of market capitalization and 127% of the cash balance — into a structure where the IP-owning joint venture's five-manager board is three WHP Global appointees to two from G-III, and where no target revenue or EBITDA has been disclosed by either LVMH (the seller) or G-III. Layer on an unremediated ICFR material weakness carrying an adverse Ernst & Young opinion, a $139.5M tariff refund receivable equal to about $2.36 per diluted share after tax, and 27.8% short interest with 11.5 days to cover, and the risk stack is dense relative to a base case that sits close to spot. HOLD.

## Company Overview

G-III designs, sources and distributes apparel and accessories across more than 30 owned and licensed brands. It owns no factories, holds no long-term supplier contracts, and sells principally to U.S. department stores and off-price chains. Retail is small and shrinking: 47 company-operated stores at 1/31/26, substantially all outlets, generating $186.0M of sales and a $(5.0)M operating loss (narrowed from $(30.5)M in FY2024).

FY2026 segment detail: Wholesale net sales $2,866.3M at 37.4% gross margin and $113.0M operating profit; Retail $186.0M at 50.1% gross margin and $(5.0)M operating loss; eliminations $(95.3)M; consolidated $2,957.0M at 39.4% gross margin and $108.0M operating profit (3.7% margin). Geographically, the U.S. was $2,284.9M (77.3%) and non-U.S. $672.1M (22.7%).

Owned brands include DKNY, Donna Karan, Karl Lagerfeld, Vilebrequin, G.H. Bass and Wilsons Leather. Licensed-in brands include Levi's, Nautica, Halston, Champion, Converse, BCBG, French Connection (effective February 2026), Starter, and the four major U.S. sports leagues, plus the expiring Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger rights.

## Financial Analysis

### Three-year trend

| Net sales | 3,098.2 | 3,180.8 | 2,957.0 |
| Gross margin | 40.1% | 40.8% | 39.4% |
| SG&A % of sales | 29.8% | 30.5% | 33.1% |
| Asset impairments | 6.8 | 8.2 | 48.6 |
| Operating profit / margin | 283.3 / 9.1% | 293.1 / 9.1% | 108.0 / 3.7% |
| Effective tax rate | 27.4% | 28.4% | 39.1% |
| Net income attributable | 176.2 | 193.6 | 67.4 |
| Diluted EPS | $3.75 | $4.20 | $1.51 |
| Operating cash flow | 587.6 | 316.4 | 299.1 |

The FY2026 earnings decline decomposes into identifiable pieces. Tariffs compressed wholesale gross margin 200bp; on $2,866.3M of wholesale sales that is roughly $57M of gross profit. The Saks Global bankruptcy in January 2026 drove a $40.0M write-off of equity investments (which, being non-deductible, added a $9.3M valuation allowance and pushed the tax rate to 39.1%) plus a $17.5M increase in the doubtful-account allowance; total FY2026 credit-loss provision was $19.8M versus $6.2M. Another $8.6M of impairments covered an equity-method e-commerce retailer, an e-commerce platform write-off and store fixtures. The item least likely to reverse on its own is SG&A deleverage: absolute SG&A grew only $8.7M, but as a percentage of sales it rose 260bp because the revenue base shrank.

Adding back all $48.6M of impairments gives roughly $156.6M of pre-impairment operating profit (5.3% margin) against $301.3M on the same basis in FY2025. Even excluding every impairment, operating profit roughly halved. Management's non-GAAP FY2026 figures were net income of $116.2M, EPS of $2.61 and adjusted EBITDA of $192.4M.

### Balance sheet

This is the strongest part of the story as of the last reported date. Cash rose from $181.4M at 1/31/25 to $406.7M at 1/31/26 and stood at $394.2M at 4/30/26. Total debt was $15.4M at 4/30/26, with zero drawn on the $700M five-year ABL (JPMorgan agent, matures June 2029, SOFR + 1.50–2.00%, availability roughly $425M at 4/30/26 on a borrowing base that fell from ~$510M one quarter earlier). The entire $400M of senior secured notes was voluntarily redeemed at par in August 2024, and net interest and financing charges fell from $39.6M in FY2024 to $0.5M in FY2026. Equity was $1,824.1M at 4/30/26, of which $664.5M is intangibles (including $638.9M of indefinite-lived trademarks, principally DKNY/Donna Karan and Karl Lagerfeld). Off-balance-sheet commitments include $332.4M of gross operating leases and $182.0M of minimum royalty and advertising obligations, $78.4M of which falls in FY2027 regardless of sell-through.

### Cash-flow quality

FY2026 operating cash flow of $299.1M against $67.4M of net income produced $263.9M of free cash flow after $35.2M of capex — a 17.0% yield on market capitalization. Two features argue against treating that as a run rate. First, accounts receivable released $87.7M, which MD&A attributes explicitly to lower fourth-quarter sales; cash generated by a shrinking revenue base is a working-capital liquidation. Second, a supply-chain finance program went from zero confirmed obligations outstanding at the start of FY2026 to $114.7M at 1/31/26 ($782.7M of invoices confirmed, $668.0M paid during the year), and MD&A links the payables increase to that program. The balance had already declined to $95.5M by 4/30/26. Both sources are available cash rather than durable earnings power; structural free cash flow is closer to guided adjusted EBITDA of $178–182M less capex, cash interest and cash taxes.

Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow was $(2.0)M versus $+93.8M a year earlier, because the $139.5M tariff receivable is a P&L and balance-sheet benefit with no cash received.

## Growth Analysis

The decisive derived figure is what guidance implies for the retained portfolio. CK/TH revenue in FY2026 was approximately 28.0% of $2,957.0M, or ~$828M. Guidance absorbs a $470M loss, leaving roughly $358M of CK/TH in FY2027. Total guided sales of $2,710M less that $358M implies a go-forward portfolio of about $2,352M, against $2,129M in FY2026 — organic growth of roughly 10.5%. An independent check corroborates the residual: the 10-Q shows $391.6M of FY2026 sales under licenses expiring 12/31/2026, and eleven of FY2027's twelve months precede that date (11/12 × $391.6M ≈ $359M).

Double-digit growth in the non-CK/TH book is not implausible. Converse and BCBG launched in FY2026 and French Connection ships from Spring 2026, so FY2027 captures full-year contribution from ramping licenses plus one new one. Q1 FY2027 already showed +$42.9M from Donna Karan, BCBG, Converse and French Connection against $86.9M of CK/TH decline. But it is an aggressive assumption for a wholesaler selling into a channel that is contracting: Saks Global filed Chapter 11 in January 2026, Hudson's Bay liquidated in 2025, and Macy's is closing 150 stores through 2028.

The runoff is a two-year problem. The residual ~$358M leaves in FY2028, meaning the go-forward base must grow again just to hold FY2027 revenue. Consensus next-year EPS of $2.47 (three analysts) implies roughly 11% growth over the FY2027 midpoint, which requires either meaningful Marc Jacobs accretion or continued double-digit organic growth in the retained book. Neither is verifiable today: Marc Jacobs standalone revenue and EBITDA are not disclosed by LVMH or G-III, and management's "$1B of Marc Jacobs revenue" is a stated long-term ambition rather than an acquired figure.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus rose 6.4% over 90 days to $2.22 and next-year 7.7% to $2.47. That is genuine positive momentum, and it tracks the guidance midpoint, but it is well short of the magnitude that signals a mispriced earnings inflection. The current-quarter revision of +54.5% (to $0.23 from $0.15) is off a trivially small base.

## Valuation Assessment

Reproducible arithmetic at $36.72:

- **Market capitalization**: 42,190,573 × $36.72 = $1,549M.
- **Pre-deal enterprise value**: $1,549M − $394.2M cash + $15.4M debt = $1,170M. Against guided FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $180M, **6.5x**; against FY2026's $192.4M, 6.1x.
- **Forward P/E**: $36.72 ÷ $2.20 guided non-GAAP midpoint = **16.7x**; on consensus $2.22, 16.5x; on next-year $2.47, 14.9x.
- **Price/book**: $1,549M ÷ $1,824.1M = **0.85x**. Tangible book of about $1,160M equals $27.49 per share, so **1.34x tangible book**.
- **Dividend**: $0.40 annualized = 1.09% yield, roughly $16.9M against guided adjusted EBITDA of $180M — comfortably covered.

The 6.5x EBITDA multiple is the most attractive-looking metric and also the most misleading, because it treats $394.2M of cash as permanent when approximately $500M is committed to the Marc Jacobs joint venture. Working through it post-deal: value the legacy business at 7.0x FY2027 EBITDA ($1,260M), carry the Marc Jacobs investment at cost ($500M), and net debt becomes roughly $121M ($500M outflow less $394M cash plus $15M existing debt). Equity value = $1,260M + $500M − $121M = $1,639M, or $38.85 per share. At 6.5x on the legacy business the same arithmetic produces $36.72 — exactly spot. The market is, in effect, paying 6.5x for the declining legacy book and marking Marc Jacobs at cost. That is a fair rather than a cheap mark for a business whose revenue declines for two more years.

Peer comparison is close to uninformative here. The stated peer median P/E of 32.1 rests on a set in which Under Armour (both share classes) and Oxford Industries carry negative earnings and Capri trades at 32.1x on depressed profits; Carter's at 16.7x is the only clean comparable and sits essentially on top of G-III's 16.7x forward multiple. The apparel-wholesale cohort is heterogeneous and mostly distressed; a discount or premium to that median carries little signal.

The three-analyst target band is $38–40 (mean $39.33), implying 3.5% to 8.9% upside. Analyst targets deserve treatment as floors only when a stock is cheap relative to rising forward earnings. Here forward non-GAAP EPS is guided *down* year over year, so the targets are not independent confirmation of upside — they are a thin consensus roughly in line with the current price.

The cheap-looking metrics (0.85x book, 6.5x EBITDA, 17% trailing FCF yield) all have identifiable qualifications: book value is 36% indefinite-lived trademarks with a demonstrated impairment history (Sonia Rykiel written down $5.9M in FY2024 and fully impaired $7.4M in FY2025); the EBITDA multiple credits soon-to-be-deployed cash; and the FCF yield is flattered by working-capital release from a shrinking base plus a one-time supply-chain-finance payables build. A valuation score of 5.0 reflects a stock priced roughly at fair value with the balance of identifiable catalysts running two ways.

## Competitive Landscape

G-III's structural bet is that retail vendor consolidation favors large multi-brand suppliers. The 10-K's own industry framing — chains closing unprofitable stores, retail consolidation, and retailers wanting fewer vendors — supports the logic: a supplier that can deliver DKNY, Donna Karan, Karl Lagerfeld, Nautica, Champion, Converse, BCBG, French Connection and licensed sports product across price points is harder to cut from a vendor list.

The evidence for improving position is specific. Proprietary brands rose from 45.1% to 55.6% of wholesale sales in two years, owned brands carry structurally higher gross margins, and Q1 FY2027's ex-tariff wholesale gross margin of 43.8% (up 340bp) is the first hard confirmation in reported numbers. License-win velocity is real: Nautica, Halston and Champion in FY2025; Converse and BCBG in FY2026; French Connection in FY2027. Non-U.S. pre-tax income of $58.7M exceeded U.S. pre-tax income of $52.0M for the first time in the three years shown, though that reflects U.S. profit falling 76% far more than international profit growing 15%.

The evidence against is equally specific. The CK/TH loss demonstrates licensor power directly: G-III built roughly a third of its revenue on brands it did not own, and the owner reclaimed them. Item 1A is explicit that licensors control brand positioning and can take categories in-house. Customer concentration has not improved — top three customers were 43.0% of FY2026 sales (Macy's group 20.6%, TJX 11.4%, Ross 11.0%) versus 43.8% in FY2025, and top ten were 67.6%. Receivable concentration deteriorated materially, with two customers at 27.8% and 13.7% of net accounts receivable at 1/31/26 (41.5% in two names) against four customers between 11.5% and 17.0% a year earlier. Brand diversification and customer diversification are separate measures; the first improved, the second did not.

Sourcing is concentrated: 71.6% of FY2026 product came from China, Vietnam and Bangladesh, with two Vietnamese vendors representing 27.0% and 15.6% of inventory purchases. G-III owns no factories, holds no long-term supply contracts, and does not hedge FX (purchase orders are USD-denominated, which transfers currency risk to vendors who may raise dollar prices). MD&A flags Red Sea/Suez disruption and Iran-related developments as affecting inventory timing and shipping costs.

Marc Jacobs improves the brand portfolio's quality but not its control profile as much as headlines suggest. G-III acquires 50% of MJ Topco (which retains the trademark) and separately acquires the operating business, which it runs as a licensee under a license running through December 2041 with ten five-year renewal options covering the U.S., Canada, Mexico and Western Europe. MJ Topco's five-manager board is three WHP Global appointees and two from G-III. Bilateral consent rights over amendments, M&A, dispositions, debt above thresholds, related-party transactions and bankruptcy materially constrain that majority, and a 2041 term with ten renewals is close to perpetual in practice. Still, G-III supplies ~$500M and takes the operating risk while a counterparty controls the licensor's board, and G-III's funding obligation is conditioned on WHP's debt and equity financing closing substantially concurrently. Certain China and Japan assets may be sold to third parties or retained by MJ Topco, in which case G-III would not control the brand in those markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control failure with an adverse audit opinion.** Management concluded disclosure controls were not effective at both 1/31/26 and 4/30/26 because of a material weakness in IT general controls at Karl Lagerfeld Holding B.V. (~9% of FY2026 net sales). Ernst & Young issued an adverse opinion on ICFR effectiveness for FY2026 while issuing an unqualified opinion on the financial statements. Remediation was incomplete as of the Q1 filing. ITGC weaknesses are among the most common and most remediable categories, frequently arising from system migrations (G-III did write off an e-commerce platform in FY2026, consistent with that origin), and no misstatement was identified. The aggravating context is the combination: an unremediated control failure, a judgment-heavy critical audit matter (wholesale markdown allowances, with $154.6M of sales-allowance provisions running through Schedule II), a foreign subsidiary reporting on a calendar-year basis, and a pending ~$500M cross-border joint-venture acquisition to integrate.

**The $139.5M tariff receivable.** Recorded at 4/30/26 for expected IEEPA refunds ($119.7M through COGS plus $19.8M reducing inventory carrying value), judged probable in part because third-party financial institutions have offered to purchase the claim. That is meaningful corroboration of value, and the Supreme Court ruled against the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 with the CIT ordering refunds on 3/4/2026 and CBP opening a claims process on 4/20/2026. The exposure is magnitude and timing: $139.5M is 9.0% of market capitalization and roughly $2.36 per diluted share after tax at a 25% rate. Separately, a new 10% Section 122 global tariff took effect 2/24/2026; the CIT ruled on 5/7/2026 that it exceeded presidential authority, but injunctive relief runs only to plaintiffs and CBP continues to collect pending an appellate decision expected in the fall of 2026. FY2027 guidance assumes rest-of-year tariffs approximate IEEPA-regime rates.

**Litigation running both directions with PVH.** G-III sued PVH and two subsidiaries in June 2025 over the denial of a three-year extension of the CK/TH women's suits licenses and related conduct that it says subjected it to contractual penalties; Calvin Klein, Inc. and Tommy Hilfiger Licensing filed their own complaint against G-III in July 2025. G-III states it cannot estimate any reasonably possible loss or range of loss. No quantification is responsibly possible.

**Customer credit losses already realized.** Saks Global's bankruptcy cost $40.0M of equity write-offs plus a $17.5M allowance addition; Hudson's Bay's liquidation contributed to $8.3M of write-offs. With two customers now representing 41.5% of net receivables, single-name credit risk is elevated in a channel that continues to contract.

**Trademark carrying value.** $638.9M of indefinite-lived trademarks equals 36.3% of 1/31/26 equity, tested by relief-from-royalty. FY2026 testing found fair value substantially exceeded carrying value for the most significant assets, but the company has written these down when forecasts slipped, and higher discount rates are a live input given the current rate path.

**Post-deal leverage is unquantified.** No pro forma balance sheet, no disclosed WHP financing terms, and no borrowing-base projection are available. ABL availability is borrowing-base dependent and fell from ~$510M to ~$425M in one quarter. The revolver rate being paid was 7.25% at 4/30/26.

**Key-person risk.** The 10-K flags dependence on Morris Goldfarb, who held about 4.11M shares (roughly 9.7% of shares outstanding) after the June 2026 vesting.

**Unverified items** that should not be treated as established: Marc Jacobs deal aggregate economics (press reports of ~$925M, ~$850M and ~$1B are mutually inconsistent and unconfirmed in filings); Marc Jacobs standalone financials; the cause of the CK/TH expiration-schedule revision between the 10-K and the 10-Q; and whether any securities class action has actually been filed (a plaintiffs'-firm solicitation dated 4/15/2026 references figures that reconcile to the filings, but Item 3 of the 10-K discloses no such action).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.8% sits close to 33.6% one-year realized — fair pricing with no near-term event premium, and the 9th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango: 35.8% at one month, 47.1% at three months, 40.1% at six months. The three-month tenor (September 18 expiry, ±13.7% implied) spans the Q2 print and carries positive put/call skew of 3.5%, while the one-month tenor is call-heavy at -10.2%. Positioning has flipped hard since the prior snapshot: ATM IV collapsed from 81.4% to 35.8% and put/call open interest fell from 3.48 to 0.87 as the price rose 12.8%. That collapse in put demand alongside 27.8% short interest and 11.5 days to cover is consistent with the twelve-month advance being partly a positioning unwind rather than solely a fundamental re-rating. The reading is not a challenge to the fundamental view; it does mean the recent price strength carries less informational content than a comparable move in a lightly shorted name, and that near-dated options are not currently pricing the September quarter or the fall tariff appellate decision as a high-variance event at the front end.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $46 (25% upside)

The mix shift works. Owned brands keep taking share of wholesale sales past 55.6%, sustaining the 340bp underlying gross-margin gain, while the incentive-compensation and professional-fee elements of the Q1 SG&A increase (compensation +$17.3M, professional fees +$6.4M, of which $3.4M is Marc Jacobs deal cost) do not recur at that pace. The retained portfolio delivers the ~10.5% organic growth embedded in guidance on full-year Converse, BCBG and French Connection contribution, and FY2027 lands at or above the $2.25 top end. The $139.5M tariff claim converts to cash in FY2027 at or near full value, adding roughly $2.36 per share of book value the market currently discounts. Marc Jacobs closes and proves accretive, giving G-III a very long-dated renewable license on a brand in which it also holds half the residual IP value — structurally superior to the third-party license that CK/TH was. At 18x an FY2028 non-GAAP number near $2.55 the stock is $46, and the 27.8% short base provides mechanical fuel on any positive surprise.

### Bear Case — $25 (32% downside)

The FY2028 bridge fails. The residual ~$358M of CK/TH exits, and the retained book cannot grow double-digits two years running into a channel losing Saks, Hudson's Bay and 150 Macy's doors. SG&A stays at 33% of a smaller sales base, compressing adjusted EBITDA below $170M. The Section 122 appeal goes against the taxpayer and/or the IEEPA refund settles materially below $139.5M or arrives late, forcing a reversal that is worth roughly $2.36 per share pre-tax-effect and would eliminate most of guided FY2027 GAAP earnings. The ~$500M Marc Jacobs deployment converts a net-cash balance sheet into modest leverage at 7%+ revolver rates for a business whose economics are undisclosed, and the joint venture requires additional funding. The ICFR weakness persists or widens through an integration year. At 11x a depressed $2.30 the stock is $25, near 0.9x adjusted tangible book.

### Base Case — $38 (3.5% upside)

The legacy business earns $178–182M of adjusted EBITDA and is worth 6.5–7.0x, Marc Jacobs is marked at cost pending disclosure of its economics, and the tariff claim is credited at a partial probability. That produces $37–39, approximately 17x the guided $2.20 and in line with the thin $38–40 analyst band. The asymmetry from here is +3.5% to base, +25% to bull, −32% to bear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The classification is deliberate: the Marc Jacobs economics are unverifiable from primary sources, the tariff receivable outcome is outside management's control, and the FY2028 revenue bridge is unresolved. Existing holders own a business with real margin-mix improvement and, as of the last reported balance sheet, no leverage problem. New capital has little margin of safety 2.2% below a 52-week high.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2027 report in early September 2026 (the September 18 option tenor prices ±13.7%), followed by the Section 122 appellate decision expected in the fall and any 8-K disclosing Marc Jacobs closing and pro forma financials.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would support a BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which the retained (non-CK/TH) portfolio grows at or above 10% year over year *and* SG&A declines as a percentage of sales versus the prior-year quarter. This is the specific combination the FY2027 guidance requires and the Q1 result failed.
2. Disclosure of Marc Jacobs revenue and EBITDA implying a purchase multiple below roughly 10x, together with pro forma post-close net leverage under 1.0x.
3. Cash collection of a substantial majority of the $139.5M tariff receivable, converting an accounting asset into balance-sheet capacity.
4. A price near $28–30 (roughly 13x the guided non-GAAP midpoint, 1.1x tangible book) on unchanged guidance, which would restore the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance cut below $2.00 or adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $170M, indicating the embedded ~10.5% organic growth assumption has failed.
2. Any write-down of the $139.5M tariff receivable, or an adverse final Section 122 outcome combined with continued collection.
3. Marc Jacobs closing on terms requiring incremental funding beyond ~$500M, or post-close net leverage above 2.0x adjusted EBITDA.
4. Expansion of the ICFR material weakness beyond the KLH ITGC scope, or a restatement.
5. Loss or non-renewal of another material license, or a further leg of customer distress at Macy's, TJX or Ross that shows up in the allowance for doubtful accounts.

## Conclusion

The trajectory in prior work on this name has been upward (4.8 to 5.3 to 5.4) and this report interrupts it at 5.0. The reason is not the price advance itself; a higher price on improving fundamentals is a better business, not a worse one. It is that the newest hard data cut the other way while the price rose 12.8%. The June 5 guidance introduced FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $2.15–2.25 against $2.61 delivered in FY2026 and adjusted EBITDA of $178–182M against $192.4M. The Q1 print, stripped of the $102.7M prior-year tariff catch-up, showed an underlying operating loss of roughly $17.5M against $8.5M of profit, with SG&A up $23.8M on sales down $47.6M. Operating cash flow was negative $2.0M. And the balance-sheet strength that carried the financial-health score to 6.5 in the prior review is committed: approximately $500M, or 127% of the 4/30/26 cash balance, funds a joint venture whose target economics are undisclosed and whose IP-holding entity has a WHP-majority board.

What genuinely improved is the underlying margin structure — 43.8% ex-tariff wholesale gross margin versus 40.4%, driven by a proprietary mix that has moved from 45.1% to 55.6% of wholesale sales in two years. That is the plank of the bull case worth owning, and it is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID, alongside a capital-allocation record that repaid $525M of debt, repurchased 5.97M shares at an average $22.78 against today's $36.72, and initiated a covered dividend.

But at 16.7x guided non-GAAP earnings that are declining, 6.5x an EBITDA multiple that credits cash about to be spent, 3.5% above the base case and 3.5–8.9% below a three-analyst target band, the asymmetry does not reward new capital.
