**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AAON has transformed from a niche premium-HVAC manufacturer into a direct beneficiary of the AI/data-center cooling build-out. The BASX segment (custom liquid cooling, coolant distribution units, and air-handling systems for hyperscale data centers) now drives the growth story, with backlog up 107% YoY to $2.13B at Q1 2026 (BASX backlog alone $1.62B, +160% YoY). Management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to 40-45% on May 7, and Q1 2026 revenue grew 54% with operating margin recovering to 11.5% from the FY25 trough. Estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-year consensus EPS rose +13.9% in 30 days and next-year +11.1%. This is a real structural demand shift, corroborated by named customers including Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, QTS, and Applied Digital.

The complication is that the market already understands this. At $133-137, AAON trades at roughly 94x trailing and ~60x forward EPS (current-year consensus $2.26), a substantial premium to its Building Products peer median of 29x. The stock has risen 83% over twelve months and gapped from ~$98 to ~$129 on the Q1 print and guidance raise. The growth is genuine and the moat (the Norman Asbjornson Innovation Center test lab, custom-engineering capability) is differentiated, but the premium multiple now prices continued execution: beats are expected, while a single hyperscaler capex pause, a working-capital squeeze, or a margin disappointment would derate the stock sharply.

Beneath the recovery sit three real tensions. FY25 free cash flow was -$190M as working capital ballooned to fund data-center jobs, pushing the revolver from $76.5M to $425M with only ~$174M of headroom remaining against ~$190M of guided 2026 capex. The center of gravity is shifting toward structurally lower-margin BASX work (~24-27% gross vs. ~37% historically in core commercial), raising a question about steady-state profitability. And customer concentration rose from two to three 10%+ customers, all hyperscalers with cancellable/re-schedulable orders. We rate AAON a HOLD (growth_hold): the business quality and demand durability justify owning it, but the current price offers no margin of safety.

## Company Overview

AAON is a Tulsa, Oklahoma-based engineer and manufacturer of premium, highly-configurable HVAC and thermal-management equipment, organized into three reportable segments:

- **AAON Oklahoma (~56% of FY25 sales):** The legacy core: semi-custom commercial and industrial rooftop units, air handlers, heat pumps, and controls. Sold through an independent manufacturers' representative network, competing on lifecycle value rather than price.
- **BASX (~22%) plus BASX-branded product built at AAON Coil Products (~23%):** The growth engine: custom liquid cooling and air-handling for hyperscale data centers (acquired 2021) and cleanroom systems for pharma/semiconductor. In 2025 BASX launched a proprietary CDU supporting >100kW rack densities and a water-free free-cooling chiller aimed at AI/HPC deployments.

The defining competitive asset is the Norman Asbjornson Innovation Center, a testing lab capable of evaluating up to 300-ton AC and 540-ton chiller systems under full environmental load, capability that wins mission-critical large-tonnage and data-center business. Headcount surged 53% to ~5,897 by February 2026, reflecting the aggressive capacity build for data-center demand. Competitors span comfort cooling (Lennox, Trane, Carrier, Daikin, Johnson Controls) and thermal management (Vertiv, STULZ, Munters, Modine, Silent-Aire/JCI).

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 financials look worst precisely at the moment of greatest momentum, a transition-year trough rather than fundamental deterioration.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 889 | 1,169 | 1,201 | 1,442 (+20%) | 497 (+54% YoY) |
| Gross margin | 26.7% | 34.1% | 33.1% | 26.7% | 25.1% |
| Operating income ($M) | 127 | 227 | 209 | 146 | 57 |
| Operating margin | 14.3% | 19.5% | 17.4% | 10.1% | 11.5% |
| Net income ($M) | 100 | 178 | 169 | 108 | 40 (+36% YoY) |

FY25 net income fell ~36% despite 20% revenue growth, with gross margin compressing ~640bps. The causes were largely transitional: an ERP go-live at Longview, TX (April 2025) that disrupted coil production and starved the Tulsa rooftop plant; the EPA-mandated R-454B refrigerant transition (effective January 2025) that created supply constraints; an 8.3% decline in core AAON-branded sales on soft new-construction demand; and sub-optimal overhead absorption from the Memphis plant ramp (whose costs sit in the AAON Oklahoma segment while revenue flows through BASX, depressing reported AAON Oklahoma margins by ~$16M in FY25).

Q1 2026 confirms the recovery: Tulsa production normalized and operating cash flow turned positive at +$34M. The 62.3% Q1 EPS beat ($0.48 vs. $0.30) confirms the operational recovery, though the four-quarter track record (two beats, two misses, including a -34.8% Q2'25 miss) reflects the lumpiness and transition disruptions of the past year.

The area to watch is cash flow. Operating cash flow collapsed to just $534K (from $193M in 2024) on a working-capital build (receivables +$167M, contract assets +$112M, inventory +$74M) to fund data-center growth, plus ~$205M of capex. This was debt-funded: the revolver went from $76.5M (YE24) to $398M (YE25) to $425M (Q1'26), with capacity expanded to $600M via accordion.

Leverage remains modest at 1.71x against a 3.0x covenant ceiling, and the Altman Z of 2.59 reflects the revolver draw rather than distress. The balance sheet is not stressed today, but the trajectory (revolver up ~5.6x in a year) means continued 40%+ growth will keep cash conversion under pressure and could require an equity raise or slower data-center conversion. Capital allocation has been measured: the dividend was raised to $0.40/yr, $30M of buybacks executed in 2025 (none in Q1'26, preserving cash), and tax structures (New Markets Tax Credits including a new $50.5M Memphis tranche in April 2026, plus OBBBA 100% bonus depreciation generating ~$57M federal NOLs) provide low-cost financing and a cash-tax tailwind.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest dimension here and it is corroborated across multiple data points rather than a single-company story. Data-center put-in-place construction spending rose ~240% from 2022-2025 per management, and AAON's BASX backlog is essentially all AI/data-center liquid cooling. Total backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility, with most firm and deliverable in 12-18 months.

Estimate revisions reinforce the recovery. Next-quarter estimates jumped +10.4% in 30 days, consistent with the guidance raise, and the breadth of the data-center demand signal (multiple hyperscaler customers, capacity additions across Memphis, Longview, Redmond, and Parkville, the 53% headcount surge) argues for durability rather than a one-quarter spike.

The qualifier is that revision velocity, while clearly positive, sits below the +30% threshold that flags a true cheap-inflection BUY, and the stock already trades at a premium multiple. The growth is real, but it is not being delivered at a price that prices in only trough conditions. Management's framing of a "$1B data center opportunity" alongside a light-commercial HVAC recovery suggests a long runway, but the core commercial business has only "stabilized" with "no clear reacceleration," so the near-term growth rests heavily on BASX conversion.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward multiple falls to ~41x the FY27 estimate of $3.29. The Building Products peer median P/E is 29x (ALLE 18.3x, WMS 24.8x, WTS 29.4x, BLDR 29.8x), so AAON commands roughly a 2x premium even on next-year earnings.

We evaluate this on forward and normalized earnings given the clear earnings recovery, rather than the meaningless trailing P/E at a margin trough. On that basis, the stock is not cheap but is defensible only if you underwrite multi-year 30%+ growth holding through FY27 and beyond. Analyst targets (mean $143.5, median $150, range $120-154) sit modestly above the current price, implying ~5-10% upside to the median. We do not treat these as floors here: at ~60x forward, the stock is in premium-multiple territory where targets and price reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation, and the asymmetry shifts toward downside on any disappointment.

The deeper valuation question is the steady-state margin. If BASX's lower gross margin permanently shifts the company's mix below the mid-30s the market historically capitalized, then the appropriate multiple on a larger but lower-margin revenue base is lower than the current premium implies. Management argues volume-driven overhead absorption and price realization will offset mix, but this is unproven. The valuation score is low (4.0) because the multiple prices continued flawless execution with no margin of safety, a value-of-growth that is already largely paid for, not a mispricing we can identify in our favor.

## Competitive Landscape

AAON's competitive position is improving in its highest-growth end market and stable-to-slightly-pressured in its legacy core. In data-center thermal management, AAON/BASX is differentiated as a custom-engineering specialist with deep testing capability, winning mission-critical hyperscaler business against Vertiv (the market leader), STULZ, Munters, and Modine. The NAIC test lab is a genuine moat for large-tonnage, performance-validated systems, and the named hyperscaler customer roster confirms the company is taking real share in liquid cooling and CDUs.

In legacy commercial HVAC, AAON competes against far larger players (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Daikin) and historically gained share in owner-controlled/replacement markets on total cost of ownership. Management notes improved cost competitiveness narrowing the price gap in new construction, but core AAON-branded sales fell 8.3% in FY25, so the trajectory here is, at best, stabilizing.

The net competitive trajectory is positive, driven by structural data-center tailwinds the company is well-positioned to capture. The risk to the moat is that hyperscalers eventually standardize cooling specs or vertically integrate, but for now AAON's custom capability is a defensible niche. We score competitive position 7.0: improving in the segment that matters, with a real but bounded edge.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully reflected in the premium valuation:

1. **Customer concentration is rising at the worst moment.** FY25 had three customers each >10% of revenue (up from two), all data-center hyperscalers whose orders the 10-K explicitly describes as carrying "more risk… shifts in timing, cancellations and re-issuances." A single hyperscaler capex pause could dent the backlog-conversion narrative the stock now depends on.

2. **Working-capital fragility.** The revolver grew to fund the data-center model; with ~$174M of headroom and ~$190M guided capex, a slower-than-hoped cash conversion or continued 40%+ growth could force an equity raise. This is a real near-term constraint, not solvency risk.

3. **Margin mix shift.** As BASX becomes the growth driver, structural gross margin may settle below the ~33% the market was accustomed to. The recovery thesis hinges on overhead absorption and price offsetting mix, unproven at scale.

4. **AI-capex sensitivity.** AAON is increasingly an AI-infrastructure derivative play. Any broad deceleration in hyperscaler data-center spending, a live debate in the market, would hit the backlog directly.

5. **Execution and governance.** The 2025 ERP and refrigerant disruptions demonstrated fragility during transformation. A new CFO mid-scale-up plus three officer-change 8-Ks in under a year is a yellow flag during a critical scaling phase.

6. **Commodities and tariffs.** Copper +7-11%, aluminum +19% in Q1'26; the 6% AAON-branded tariff surcharge only fully covered costs by late 2025. BASX job-pricing has better pass-through.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($170-185).** The AI data-center cooling super-cycle proves durable; BASX converts its backlog on schedule, FY26 revenue grows 40-45% and FY27 sustains 25-30%. Overhead absorption and price realization push gross margin back toward the low-30s, operating margin recovers to mid-teens, and FY27 EPS lands above the $3.29 consensus, perhaps toward $3.75-4.00. Working capital normalizes as growth moderates, FCF turns positive, and the revolver stabilizes. At ~45x a $4.00 FY27 number, the stock reaches the mid-$180s. The company is increasingly valued as a scarce AI-infrastructure derivative with a defensible moat.

**Bear Case ($85-95).** A hyperscaler capex pause or order re-scheduling exposes the lumpiness and concentration of the data-center model, and backlog conversion slips. Margin mix proves structural (gross margin settles in the mid-20s), so even revenue growth fails to translate into the earnings the multiple requires. Working capital forces a dilutive equity raise. Estimates roll over, and a stock at ~60x forward derates toward the peer cohort's 25-30x on FY26 EPS of ~$2.26, implying ~$60-70; even on FY27 at 30x, ~$95. A >30% drawdown from current levels is plausible on any meaningful disappointment given the premium starting point.

The base case sits in between. The growth is real, but the price already discounts much of it, and the binary nature of hyperscaler order timing makes the distribution of outcomes wide. This balance is why we land on HOLD rather than BUY despite the genuine quality of the franchise.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 FY26, early August 2026), which will test whether the margin recovery holds and whether backlog continues to grow.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$90s to low-$100s (~30x FY27 EPS) that restores a margin of safety on unchanged or improving fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin expansion back toward 30%+ confirming the mix-shift concern is being offset, alongside positive free cash flow.
- Backlog continuing to grow with no material hyperscaler order cancellation, plus a stabilizing revolver balance (evidence the working-capital intensity is moderating).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A hyperscaler capex pause or order cancellation/re-scheduling that visibly slows backlog conversion.
- A dilutive equity raise to fund working capital, or revolver headroom approaching exhaustion.
- Gross margin compressing further below 25% with no path back, confirming structurally lower steady-state profitability.
- Estimate revisions turning negative on a guidance cut.

**Score trajectory note:** AAON's overall score has drifted from 5.9 (Mar) to 6.1 (May) and back to 5.9 here, with growth prospects rising to 8.5 and financial health declining to 5.5, a divergence that captures the central tension. The growth thesis has strengthened (guidance raise, backlog +107%, rising estimates), while the cash-flow and balance-sheet picture deteriorated (FCF -$190M, revolver grew ~5.6x). The stock has declined modestly from the post-Q1 highs near $148 to ~$133, which on improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward at the margin, but not enough to overcome a ~60x forward multiple. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the bar for changing the call is a re-rating in either direction (a meaningful pullback or a confirmed margin/cash inflection), not a continuation of the current premium-priced grind.

## Conclusion

AAON is a high-quality manufacturer with a genuine, durable position in the AI/data-center cooling build-out, validated by the backlog, the FY26 revenue guide, named hyperscaler customers, and a differentiated custom-engineering moat. The growth dimension is the strongest in the analysis (8.5), and the competitive trajectory is positive (7.0). What holds us back from a BUY is valuation and risk symmetry: at ~60x forward EPS, roughly double the peer median, the market already capitalizes flawless execution, while the working-capital intensity, the unresolved margin-mix question, and rising hyperscaler concentration create a wide and asymmetric distribution of outcomes. We rate AAON HOLD with a constructive long-term view; the right entry is either a pullback that restores a margin of safety or confirmation that the data-center growth is converting to expanding margins and positive free cash flow.
