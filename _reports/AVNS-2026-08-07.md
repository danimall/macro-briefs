## Executive Summary

Avanos Medical no longer trades. Affiliates of American Industrial Partners ("AIP") acquired the company for **$25.00 per share in cash** under a merger agreement signed April 13, 2026. Stockholders approved the transaction on July 22, 2026 (8-K Item 5.07), all regulatory clearances including HSR were obtained by July 2, and the merger closed **July 27, 2026**. The July 30, 2026 8-K carries Items 2.01 (completion of disposition), 3.01 (delisting notice), 5.01 (change in control) and 3.03 (modification of securityholder rights). Form 4s dated July 27 show every insider position going to zero at $25.00.

The market data accompanying this file — $24.99 last price, $1.17bn market capitalization, 46,847,816 shares — is arithmetically consistent ($24.99 × 46.85M = $1,170.7M) but describes a security whose final trade occurred **July 24, 2026** and whose shares were cancelled three days later. The price history makes the mechanics visible: the stock closed $14.53 on April 13, gapped to $24.63 on April 14 on 23.9M shares against an 859K average, then traded in a $24.46–$25.49 band for fourteen weeks with the drift of a settled arbitrage. Residual short interest of 1.75M shares (4.4% of float, 2.0 days to cover), a single analyst carrying a HOLD with a "target" equal to the last print, one contributing analyst on the estimate file, and an options put/call open-interest ratio moving from 0.00 to 0.16 are all stale artifacts of a closed deal rather than forward signals.

The rating is therefore **AVOID**. There is no security to initiate, no residual spread worth capturing (the $0.01 gap to consideration is +0.04% on a transaction that has already funded), and no forward participation in the assets. This reverses a score trajectory that had improved from 4.4 (AVOID, May) to 5.6 (HOLD, June) to 5.8 (HOLD, July). That improvement was correct and was driven by deal certainty: valuation moved 3.0 → 5.5 and risk factors 4.5 → 7.0 as merger conditions cleared. Deal certainty has now been fully consumed. The remainder of this report documents what the business was worth, why it sold, and what the disclosures imply for the small/mid-cap medtech and Mexico-manufacturing cohort.

## Company Overview

Avanos was a ~$700M-revenue medical technology company headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia, spun out of Kimberly-Clark in 2014. Effective January 1, 2025 it reported two segments plus a residual bucket:

**Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS)** — FY2025 net sales $432.9M (62% of total), segment operating income $82.6M (19.1% margin). Enteral feeding $314.7M (MIC-KEY, Corpak, CORTRAK) and neonate solutions $118.2M (NeoMed, plus Nexus TKO connectors acquired September 2025). The 10-K discloses that MIC-KEY, Corpak and NeoMed each individually exceeded 10% of consolidated net sales in 2023–2025.

**Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R)** — FY2025 net sales $237.8M (34%), segment operating income $9.2M (3.9% margin). Radiofrequency ablation $139.0M (+10.1%; COOLIEF, Trident, ESENTEC) and surgical pain and recovery $98.8M (−8.5%; ON-Q, ambIT, Game Ready).

**Corporate and Other** — $30.5M of residual revenue from exiting lines (IV infusion oncology pumps, hyaluronic acid injectables until the July 2025 divestiture, Game Ready rental until Q4 2025).

Route to market ran heavily through intermediaries: 49% of FY2025 North American net sales and 67% of ex-North America sales moved through distributors or wholesalers, and 29% of global net sales were GPO-contracted on roughly three-year terms. Manufacturing was concentrated in Mexico — two facilities employing 1,324 of 2,287 total employees (57.9% of headcount) under Maquiladora status — plus U.S. and one Canadian site.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: segment growth masked by deliberate wind-down.** Reported net sales were $673.3M (2023), $687.8M (2024), $701.2M (2025) — FY2025 growth of only 1.9%. The two operating segments grew 6.4%; Corporate and Other fell 46.7%. Q1 2026 sharpened the divergence: total revenue $182.2M (+8.8%), segments +14.6%, Corporate and Other −81.4% to $1.9M. Nexus contributed $5.8M of Q1 2026 sales, so organic SNS growth was approximately 16.9% and organic total growth approximately 5.3% ((182.2 − 5.8) / 167.5 − 1).

The split within that was stark. SNS Q1 2026 net sales rose 22.7% on 19.0% volume, with neonate +48.1%. PM&R rose 0.2% and posted a **segment operating loss of $1.8M** against +$0.2M a year earlier, as surgical pain fell 11.0% while RFA grew 8.8%.

**Margins: a headline collapse that decomposes usefully.** Consolidated gross margin fell 56.4% → 55.4% → 50.5% across 2023–2025. Roughly 200bp of the 490bp FY2025 decline sits in Corporate and Other, where $30.5M of revenue carried $44.5M of cost of goods sold — negative $14.0M of gross profit on lines being exited. That drag shrank to −$2.2M in Q1 2026 ($1.9M revenue against $4.1M COGS), so it was self-correcting.

The residual is the more important finding, because it sits in the growth engine. SNS gross margin fell ~370bp (57.9% → 54.2%) while PM&R fell ~140bp. SNS sales grew 9.2% in FY2025 while SNS COGS grew 19.0%, and SNS operating income rose only 2.2% ($80.8M → $82.6M). Q1 2026 repeated the pattern: SNS sales +22.7%, COGS +32.3%, operating income +9.5%, margin 18.6% versus 20.9%. Management attributes the cost pressure to tariffs and to pricing on exited lines. A competing reading is that part of the FY2025 increase reflects the $32.4M restructuring charge, portions of which were allocated to COGS and are non-recurring; but Q1 2026 carried only $1.8M of that charge and still showed ~340bp of SNS gross-margin decline, so restructuring cannot account for the whole effect. The mechanism that best fits the evidence: tariffs on Mexico-manufactured goods and China-sourced components raised landed cost, while three-year GPO pricing terms prevented prompt pass-through.

**Profitability and impairments.** Segment operating income rose $76.5M → $83.5M → $91.8M across 2023–2025. Consolidated GAAP operating income was $4.2M (2023), −$396.2M (2024), −$61.6M (2025); net losses were −$61.8M, −$392.1M, −$72.9M. Two non-cash impairment events drive the gap: Q4 2024 $336.5M of goodwill plus $100.2M of intangibles/asset-group write-downs, and Q2 2025 $77.0M against the PM&R reporting unit, triggered explicitly by a decline in Avanos' own market capitalization at the June 30 interim test. Cumulative impairments of $513.7M exceeded the $394.9M of goodwill remaining at year-end 2025. Goodwill plus intangibles of $512.7M still equalled 47.8% of total assets.

Company-defined adjusted EBITDA (from the proxy and merger-proxy supplement) ran $98.9M (2023) → $107.6M (2024) → **$86.8M (2025)**, a 19.3% one-year decline. That number, more than any other, explains the equity de-rating from roughly $20 to $11–14 over 2025, which then mechanically triggered the impairment test. The definitional caveat: the earlier years in that series likely still reflect Respiratory Health, and the proxy's 2021 figure of $40.0M is inconsistent in scale, so the series should not be treated as a clean continuing-operations trend. Only FY2025 ($86.8M) and LTM-to-3/31/2026 ($82.4M) are independently corroborated.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow $74.7M against $31.6M of capex gives free cash flow of $43.1M, 6.1% of revenue, down from $82.9M and 12.1% in 2024. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was −$12.3M versus +$25.7M a year earlier, dominated by seasonality: accrued salaries fell from $37.0M to $21.7M on the annual bonus payout, part of a $33.7M accrual unwind, and the prior year had benefited from a $25.1M receivable release that did not repeat. Pre-working-capital generation was approximately $19.0M (net income $5.1M + D&A $10.2M + SBC $3.7M). Annualizing the reported figure would imply −$49M, which is a labeled arithmetic exercise and not a credible run rate.

Leverage was minimal. At 12/31/2025: cash $89.8M, term loan $100.8M, revolver undrawn of $375M capacity — net debt ~$11.0M, or 0.13x adjusted EBITDA. At 3/31/2026: cash $65.6M, term loan $98.4M, net debt $32.8M, covenant-compliant. The Credit Agreement matured **June 24, 2027**, with $90.6M due that year; effective rate 5.5% with a $65M pay-fixed swap added during 2025. Equity of $778.2M sat against an accumulated deficit of $779.9M, which matters because the health screen's Altman Z of 3.27 substitutes book equity where the classic model uses retained earnings/total assets, which for Avanos was −0.726. The "safe" conclusion survives on independent evidence (0.13x net leverage, undrawn revolver, covenant compliance), but the Z-score itself should not be cited as a like-for-like output.

**Earnings surprise data requires a basis caveat.** The supplied table shows four consecutive beats, including +54.1% for the March 2026 quarter ($0.22 actual against $0.14 estimate). A secondary summary of the same quarter reports adjusted EPS of $0.22 against a non-GAAP consensus of $0.23 — a small miss. These are different consensus sets on the same actual; the reconcilable facts are that revenue beat, adjusted EPS fell 15.4% year over year, and adjusted operating income declined 5.3% on 8.8% revenue growth. The pattern of the final quarter was volume growth converting poorly to profit.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth question is now unanswerable from public sources, because Avanos deregisters and its performance under AIP ownership is not observable. What the final filings establish:

The growth asset was SNS, compounding mid-teens organically with a 48% neonate quarter and three products individually above 10% of consolidated sales. Its economics were being eroded at the cost line rather than the revenue line. The declining asset was surgical pain and recovery — down 8.1% (2023), 8.5% (2025) and 11.0% (Q1 2026). Management attributes this to volume and to revenue streams it chose not to pursue; the 10-K also names Pacira Pharmaceuticals as a competitor, whose EXPAREL long-acting injectable is a pharmaceutical substitute for continuous-infusion pumps. Both explanations are supported and the filings do not disaggregate deliberate exits from competitive losses. That ambiguity is material to how a buyer would underwrite the franchise.

R&D fell $27.2M (2023) → $26.2M (2024) → **$23.3M (2025)**, 3.3% of sales, with PM&R-specific R&D dropping $9.8M → $7.3M → $5.1M and $1.3M in Q1 2026. The innovation budget for the segment facing a reimbursement threat was cut roughly in half in two years. The benign reading is portfolio rationalization following divestitures plus a preference for acquired capability (Diros, Nexus) over internal development.

The single analyst estimate file — current year $1.00, next year $1.25, current-quarter estimate cut 33.3% over 90 days while next-quarter rose 37.5% — carries one contributor and predates deal closure. It should not be used to infer momentum in either direction.

## Valuation Assessment

**The only valuation that matters is the one that cleared.** Consideration was $25.00 per share in cash against an April 13, 2026 close of $14.53 — a **72.1% premium**. Reconstructing enterprise value from balance-sheet inputs: 46,673,069 shares at 3/31/2026 × $25.00 = $1,166.8M equity (or $1,187.5M on 47.5M diluted weighted-average shares), plus net debt of $32.8M, gives **$1,200M–$1,220M**. AIP announced "approximately $1.272 billion"; the $50–70M difference is most plausibly the cash-out of options, RSUs and PSUs (all converted to cash under the merger agreement) and possibly the $17.3M Nexus earn-out. I have not seen the issuer's bridge, so both figures are carried.

On LTM adjusted EBITDA of $82.4M — derived in the proxy supplement as FY2025 $86.8M less Q1 2025 $21.6M plus Q1 2026 $17.2M — the transaction cleared at **14.6x–15.4x**. On FY2025 actual EBITDA of $86.8M it cleared at 13.8x–14.7x. Pre-announcement, the public market valued the same cash flows at **8.8x** ($14.53 × 47.5M = $690.2M equity + $32.8M net debt = $723.0M EV ÷ $82.4M).

J.P. Morgan's disclosed benchmarks frame how unusual that was. Its public-comparables analysis (Align, Bioventus, CONMED, Enovis, Envista, Haemonetics, ICU Medical, Integra, LivaNova, Varex) spanned 7.0x–12.6x 2026E adjusted EBITDA, median 9.4x, implying **$12.75–$23.50 per share**. Its twelve selected transactions spanned 6.3x–13.4x LTM adjusted EBITDA, median 10.7x, implying **$9.75–$21.75**. The $25.00 price exceeded the top of both market-based ranges. A fairness opinion normally also contains a DCF, which I did not obtain and which typically implies higher values, so "$25.00 exceeded both ranges" is accurate as stated but is not a statement about the full opinion.

What AIP appears to have underwritten is not reported EBITDA. Segment operating income was $91.8M in FY2025 while Corporate and Other consumed $76.4M excluding impairment (−$153.4M + $77.0M); the Q1 2026 corporate drag of $12.4M annualizes near $50M. Remove a large share of stranded corporate cost left behind by the Respiratory Health divestiture, add public-company cost savings and the $15–20M of annualized savings management had already identified from the expanded restructuring plan, and a ~14.8x reported multiple becomes high-single-digits on pro-forma EBITDA. A sponsor with almost no existing leverage to displace (0.13x) and a June 2027 maturity available to refinance into an LBO structure had a clean canvas.

The supplied peer table is of limited use here: QDEL, BLFS, NEOG and XRAY all carry negative P/E, and the "median P/E 38.9" is a single positive observation (HAE). That dispersion is itself the macro point: a cohort of scaled branded medtech names trading at 7–8x forward EBITDA (Enovis 7.0x, Integra 7.2x, Varex 7.5x, CONMED 8.3x in J.P. Morgan's set) is what made Avanos both cheap and buyable.

The valuation score of 2.0 reflects the current instrument: $24.99 against fixed cash consideration of $25.00, on shares already cancelled. There is no asymmetry left to price.

## Competitive Landscape

Avanos described itself accurately: "no single company competes with us across the full breadth of our offerings" — a collection of niche category leaders rather than a platform. SNS competed with Boston Scientific, Cook Medical, Applied Medical Technology and Cardinal Health, holding entrenched standard-of-care positions in enteral and neonatal feeding with above-market growth. PM&R competed with Boston Scientific, Pacira, Stryker, Medtronic, Pajunk, Stratus Medical and Nice Recovery Systems.

Pricing power was structurally capped. With 29% of global net sales GPO-contracted on three-year terms, and the 10-K noting that even where Avanos was sole contracted supplier "members of the GPO are generally free to purchase from other suppliers," cost inflation could not be passed through promptly. That constraint interacted directly with the tariff problem to produce the SNS margin leakage described above.

Customer concentration disclosure shifted sharply. FY2023 named Medline ~15%, McKesson ~13%, Owens & Minor ~6%. FY2024 named McKesson ~18% and Medline ~17% (top two ~35%). FY2025 named **only Medline at ~12%**, implying McKesson fell below the 10% threshold — a swing of roughly $50M+ of annual channel volume. The disappearance of a name from a concentration table does not establish that the customer base broadened; the sub-threshold composition is undisclosed. No filing explains the cause, and it is consistent with the "withdrawal from certain revenue streams that did not meet our return criteria" cited as a 0.7–0.9pt drag on segment growth.

## Risk Assessment

Ranked by materiality as of the final filings:

**1. RFA Medicare coverage — unresolved.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that multiple Medicare Administrative Contractors drafted a proposed coverage policy questioning whether RFA treatment for peripheral nerve pain is reasonable and necessary, with a final determination expected in early 2026. RFA was $139.0M of FY2025 net sales (19.8% of total) and PM&R's only growing line. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no outcome and I could not confirm one from any source. PM&R goodwill was written down $77.0M in Q2 2025 and the segment swung to an operating loss in Q1 2026.

**2. Tariffs and Mexico concentration.** Both the 10-K and 10-Q carry dedicated "Risks Related to Tariffs" MD&A sections and name tariffs as a primary driver of higher cost of products sold in both periods. Two Mexican facilities under Maquiladora status held 57.9% of headcount; components were sourced from China and Mexico; a tariff extension on a Chinese-sourced component expired at end-2025. The FX leg was managed ($43.2M notional of peso hedges; a hypothetical 10% peso appreciation was disclosed as immaterial to FY2025 operating profit); the tariff leg was not.

**3. Impairment reflexivity.** The 10-K states directly that "a decline in our stock price could result in a goodwill impairment charge in the future," and the Q2 2025 test was triggered by exactly that. With $512.7M of goodwill and intangibles remaining (47.8% of assets), the loop between falling equity value and GAAP write-downs was live.

**4. 2027 maturity against declining EBITDA.** $90.6M due in 2027 with quarterly amortization stepping up. Not a distress constraint given cash of $65–90M, positive annual FCF and an undrawn $375M revolver, but a scheduled decision point roughly fourteen months from deal signing.

**5. Resolved items.** The MicroCool surgical gown DPA with DOJ expired July 7, 2024 and the case was dismissed in January 2025. No Kimberly-Clark indemnification costs were reported in Q1 2026 or Q1 2025.

**6. Current instrument risk.** Zero. Shares were cancelled for cash on July 27, 2026. The corollary is zero forward participation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (retrospective, $25.00 realized).** A shareholder who held from the April 13 close of $14.53 through settlement realized 72.1% in fourteen weeks against a five-year TSR index that had fallen to $24.48 per $100 invested while the peer index rose to $130.23. The board obtained a price above the top of both of its banker's market-based valuation ranges, on a 3.21% termination fee ($37.5M ÷ $1,166.8M equity value) that did not deter competing interest, in a process that began as inbound sponsor contact on July 7, 2025 (AIP's Jamie Tam to UBS) and ran nine months with multiple lettered sponsors. The proxy supplement affirmatively discloses that AIP never discussed executive employment before signing. For a company with negative GAAP operating income in two of three years, $513.7M of impairments, halved PM&R R&D and an unresolved Medicare coverage threat on 20% of revenue, that is a good outcome.

**Bear case (prospective, current instrument).** There is nothing to buy. The $0.01 gap between the last print and the consideration is +0.04% on a transaction that has already funded, and the position was cashed out on July 27. Any capital allocated on the basis of the $24.99 quote, the $1.17bn "market cap," the four-quarter beat record, or the single-analyst $1.25 next-year estimate is allocated to a security that does not exist. Downstream data pipelines are the specific hazard: this file contains a stale HOLD rating, stale estimate revisions, stale short interest and stale option chains, all of which would screen as a live small-cap medtech with improving fundamentals.

**Management and governance assessment.** David Pacitti became CEO in April 2025 and received FY2025 total compensation of $9,469,803 (compensation actually paid $7,906,179) against a prior-year PEO total of $4,701,549 — consistent with a new-hire inducement package but not decomposed in the data available. Five-year cumulative TSR of $24.48 per $100 against a peer index of $130.23 is among the starker pay-versus-performance disclosures in small-cap medtech. Capital allocation: a $25M repurchase authorization approved November 1, 2024 saw zero purchases, with capital redirected to debt reduction ($25M revolver + $9.4M term loan in 2025) and M&A (Nexus, $28.0M) — defensible at $11–14 per share, and in any case a live sale process from mid-2025 would ordinarily preclude open-market repurchases.

On insider activity, two items warrant discipline. An SVP sold 15,000 shares in open-market transactions at $11.00–$11.02 on November 18, 2025, approximately the stock's trough and five months before a $25.00 deal; whether a pre-existing 10b5-1 plan governed is not determinable from the available Form 4 data, and no inference about knowledge is supportable. Annual equity grants on March 13, 2026 (Pacitti 101,341 shares plus 239,354 options) fell one month before signing, at a share price near $13.29–$13.79. The timing is consistent with a routine post-10-K annual cycle and equally consistent with grants made during a live process; the proxy supplement's disclosure on executive employment cuts against a captured-management reading. I draw no motive inference. Management quality scores 4.5: the sale outcome was good, the five-year value record was poor, and the alignment evidence is mixed.

The catalysts that would have driven a short — an adverse MAC coverage determination on RFA, a failed 2027 refinancing, continued SNS margin leakage — were extinguished as tradable events when the merger closed on July 27, 2026. **Borrow and options signal**: No borrow exists post-cancellation. Option chains show put/call open interest of 0.16 with no live underlying. Neither supports a position.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Undefined. A short against fixed cash consideration of $25.00 at a price of $24.99 has maximum profit of $0.01 per share, no path to further decline, and unlimited operational risk from delisting mechanics and forced settlement. Reward:risk is effectively zero.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The company was acquired for cash and deregistered. There is no instrument, no borrow, no catalyst and no downside. The AVOID rating reflects the absence of an investable security.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: None exist for AVNS common stock. The equity was cancelled for $25.00 cash on July 27, 2026 and the company has initiated delisting and intends to deregister. Re-entry would require a new registered security — an AIP-sponsored IPO of the platform, a carve-out of SNS or PM&R, or public debt issued by the post-LBO entity. None is announced or observable.

**Coverage action**: Remove AVNS from the active equity universe pending confirmation of Form 25/Form 15 deregistration. Until removal, treat all supplied market data — price, market capitalization, short interest, estimate revisions, options open interest, the single analyst HOLD — as terminal artifacts that must not be fed to screens or scored as live signals.

**Timeline**: Permanent for the common equity. The final observable financials are the Q1 2026 10-Q (filed May 5, 2026) and the FY2025 10-K (filed February 24, 2026).

**Transferable read-across to monitor** (the reason to keep the file rather than discard it):
1. **Sponsor take-privates of sub-$2bn medtech remain an active bid.** Four of J.P. Morgan's twelve selected transactions were sponsor-led, three within 21 months of this deal (ARCHIMED/ZimVie 11.2x, Patient Square/Patterson 12.3x, Carlyle/Vantive 8.8x). Screening criterion that fits Avanos and would fit others: category-leading assets, sub-1.0x net leverage, a large stranded-corporate-cost line following a divestiture, a maturity 12–24 months out, and an equity trading at 7–9x EBITDA against 10x+ precedent medians. Peers in the disclosed comp set at the low end — Enovis 7.0x, Integra 7.2x, Varex 7.5x, CONMED 8.3x 2026E EBITDA — screen on the multiple leg.
2. **Mexico-manufacturing tariff exposure is a live margin variable, distinct from FX.** Avanos hedged the peso effectively (10% appreciation disclosed as immaterial) and still absorbed ~290bp of segment gross-margin compression from tariffs, because GPO three-year pricing blocked pass-through. For any medtech or consumer-health name with Maquiladora manufacturing and GPO-contracted revenue, the pass-through lag rather than the FX exposure is the variable to model.
3. **The RFA peripheral-nerve coverage determination remains unresolved and affects other issuers.** Boston Scientific, Stryker and Medtronic all compete in that category. Confirmation of the MAC outcome is the single highest-value open item carried forward from this file.

## Conclusion

**AVOID — no investable security.** Avanos Medical was acquired by affiliates of American Industrial Partners for $25.00 per share in cash; the merger closed July 27, 2026, and the July 30 8-K confirms disposition, delisting and change of control. The $24.99 quote dated July 24, 2026 is a deal-pegged final trade, and the shares behind it have been cancelled. The $0.01 residual spread offers +0.04% on an already-funded transaction.

The overall score of 3.98 falls from 5.8 because the instrument is gone. Fundamentals did not deteriorate into the close: the business ended with net debt of $32.8M against an undrawn $375M revolver, SNS growing roughly 17% organically, and a takeout at 14.6x–15.4x LTM adjusted EBITDA — above the top of both market-based ranges in its own banker's fairness analysis and above the pre-announcement trading multiple of 8.8x. What the buyer paid for was the removal of approximately $50M of annualized stranded corporate cost against $91.8M of segment operating income, not the reported earnings the public market was discounting.

Two open items are worth carrying forward: the MAC coverage determination on RFA for peripheral nerve pain, which sat on $139.0M (19.8%) of revenue with no confirmed outcome, and the unread February 26, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01 and 8.01) filed two days after the 10-K and during the live sale process, from which no inference should be drawn. Neither is actionable in AVNS. Both are relevant to the medtech names still listed.
