**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMETEK is a diversified manufacturer of electronic instruments and electromechanical devices that operates as a serial-acquirer compounder. The business generates ~26% operating margins, ~20% net margins, and converts roughly 113% of net income to free cash flow on capex of only ~1.8% of sales. After a sluggish 2025 (overall organic growth +2%, EIG organic down 1%), the company posted a sharp cyclical recovery in Q1 2026: organic sales +5%, organic orders +22%, a record $3.87B backlog (book-to-bill comfortably above 1.0), operating margin of 26.7%, and raised full-year guidance. The order surge concentrated in EMG (organic sales +11%, segment margin up 380 bps to 25.7%) points to recovery in automation, semiconductor, and aerospace end markets after a soft patch.

The investment case rests on the order/backlog recovery converting into durable revenue growth while the M&A engine continues to add ~3-4 points of growth per year. The franchise has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (by 2.3% to 6.4%), consensus estimates are stable-to-modestly-rising (+1.1% over 90 days on FY26 and FY27), and the balance sheet is conservatively levered (net debt/EBITDA ~0.8x, FCF $1.67B in 2025). This is a clean expression of the industrial automation/precision-instrumentation cycle turning back up.

The principal constraint is valuation. At $231-237 the stock trades at ~29x FY26 consensus EPS of $8.14 and ~26x FY27 of $8.81, a premium to its own history but a discount to its closest serial-acquirer peers (ROK 47.5x, ETN 38.5x, EMR 32.9x). The premium is defensible given best-in-class margins, FCF quality, and the order recovery, but it leaves limited margin for error if the +22% order figure proves to be a timing artifact rather than a durable recovery. The combination of a confirmed earnings inflection, a four-quarter beat streak, rising backlog, and a multiple that sits below peers adjusted for quality supports a BUY, with entry discipline given the absence of asymmetric upside at current levels.

## Company Overview

AMETEK operates through two segments:

- **Electronic Instruments Group (EIG)** — ~66% of sales ($4.92B in 2025). Advanced analytical, test, and measurement instruments serving process/analytical markets (70% of EIG) and aerospace & power (30%). End markets include life sciences, pharma, semiconductor, power, oil & gas, research/metrology, and commercial/military aerospace. This is the higher-margin segment at ~29-31% operating margins.
- **Electromechanical Group (EMG)** — ~34% of sales ($2.48B). Precision motion control, engineered medical components (single-use surgical, implantables, drug delivery), thermal management, specialty metals, electrical interconnects, and aviation MRO. Automation and engineered solutions are 70% of EMG; aerospace 30%.

The operating framework is the "AMETEK Growth Model": Operational Excellence (lean/Six Sigma margin expansion), Strategic Acquisitions, Global/Market Expansion, and New Product Development. The competitive moat is niche market leadership: highly differentiated, often technologically superior products in fragmented end markets where no single customer is material (the largest five customers are ~4% of EIG sales and ~15% of EMG; no customer exceeds 5%). The model is asset-light and high-FCF. International is ~48% of sales, and ~27% of 2025 sales came from products introduced in the prior three years.

Profitability is best-in-class for industrials: 2025 net margin ~20%, EBITDA $2.30B, ROE 13.9%, ROA 9.2%. Cash flow quality is high — FCF equals ~113% of net income, a function of the asset-light model and modest capex. R&D&E ran ~$383M (5% of sales).

The balance sheet is conservatively levered. Net debt was $1.83B, net debt/capital just 14.7% (13.4% by Q1'26), and total debt $2.28B against $10.6B equity, implying net leverage of roughly 0.8x. The 2025 Altman Z-Score of 2.96 ("grey zone") is an artifact of a goodwill-heavy balance sheet (asset turnover only 0.46), not a distress signal; interest coverage, leverage, and FCF are all healthy. A $2.3B commercial paper program established in January 2025 ($740M outstanding at year-end) funds M&A flexibly.

Capital allocation is M&A-first, supplemented by a rising dividend (raised 11% in February 2025 to $0.31, then another 10% in February 2026 to $0.34) and opportunistic buybacks ($443M in 2025; $807M remaining on a $1.25B authorization). The dividend yield is only ~0.6%, so this is a total-return-via-reinvestment story, not an income name.

The key trend shift: 2025 organic growth was sluggish, but Q1 2026 inflected sharply. EMG was the standout (operating margin up 380 bps to 25.7%). Excluding acquisition dilution, organic Operational-Excellence margin expansion was +70 bps in 2025 and +130 bps in Q1'26; the core franchise keeps improving underneath the M&A noise.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has three layers. First, the cyclical recovery: the +22% organic order figure and the record backlog de-risk near-term revenue and signal that the automation/semiconductor/aerospace cycle is turning back up after the 2025 soft patch. Book-to-bill well above 1.0 typically precedes several quarters of accelerating revenue recognition. Second, the M&A engine: roughly 4 of 2025's 6.6% total growth came from acquisitions. The company has completed ~15 deals since 2021 (~$1.8B annualized sales added) and continues at pace — Kern (Jan 2025), FARO (July 2025), LKC ($209.6M, Jan 2026), and a definitive agreement for First Aviation Services (~$80M sales, April 2026), plus a June 12, 2026 material agreement and new debt obligation that likely represents additional acquisition financing. Third, new-product development: ~27% of 2025 sales came from products launched in the prior three years.

The FARO/Creaform/Virtek/Kern cluster is a deliberate build-out of a 3D metrology / "digital reality" platform, a higher-growth ultra-precision space adjacent to semiconductor and advanced manufacturing. End markets are diversifying toward secular themes — semiconductor, medical, aerospace & defense, automation, metrology, and power/data-center/grid.

Consensus reflects this: FY26 EPS of $8.14 (+27% above 2025's $6.40, though that gap includes the inflection plus M&A) and FY27 of $8.81 (+8%). The four-quarter beat streak supports confidence in those estimates. The 90-day revision trend is modestly positive but not the +30% velocity that flags a deep-trough inflection; AME is a mid-cycle re-acceleration, not a name emerging from a collapse. The principal uncertainty is whether +22% orders is durable demand or partly an order-timing/easy-comparison artifact; I treat it as a genuine positive but would watch Q2-Q3 for normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

AMETEK competes on technology, quality, and service in fragmented niche markets rather than on scale, which underpins durable ~26% margins. The moat is the breadth of small, defensible market-leadership positions where no single customer is material and switching costs in instrumentation/precision components are high. The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: EMG margins expanded 380 bps YoY in Q1'26, the historical EMG-to-EIG margin gap is narrowing, and the digital-reality/metrology platform build-out positions the company in higher-growth adjacencies (semiconductor inspection, advanced manufacturing).

The relevant peer set is other high-quality serial acquirers — Roper, Danaher, Fortive, Dover. Against these, AMETEK's Operational Excellence discipline and the ROTC governance metric (which penalizes overpaying for goodwill) are competitive advantages in the capital-allocation contest that defines this category. The Q1'26 order surge suggests share is at minimum stable and the franchise is participating fully in the cyclical upturn. The score reflects a widening-moat trajectory tempered by the fact that the business is a collection of niches rather than a single dominant platform.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Goodwill/intangible concentration.** Goodwill plus intangibles total ~$11.3B, ~70% of total assets; goodwill plus indefinite-lived intangibles alone are $8.27B (51.5%). The strategy depends on M&A producing returns above price paid; a downturn at a large reporting unit could force a non-cash impairment. No impairment occurred in 2025, but this is the structural vulnerability and the reason the Z-Score reads "grey zone."

2. **Acquisition dependence and integration risk.** The pace is accelerating (4 deals in ~15 months plus a June 2026 financing event), and recent deals are margin-dilutive near-term (60-100 bps drag in 2025, 80-90 bps in Q1'26). Rising competition for assets raises purchase-price risk. Integration execution and price discipline are the variables that matter most.

3. **Cyclicality.** Aerospace/defense, oil & gas, process instrumentation, and power are tied to customer capex and government budgets. The 2025 EIG organic decline of 1% demonstrates the sensitivity; the durability of the order recovery is the open question.

4. **Tariffs and international exposure.** International sales span 22 manufacturing countries. Both the 10-K and Q1'26 10-Q expanded tariff/trade-restriction language. Management says impacts have not been material and is mitigating via pricing and localized production, but the situation is evolving. The Q1'26 10-Q also newly flags Middle East/Iran conflict macro uncertainty (limited direct exposure).

5. **Governance yellow flag.** Five-year TSR indexed to 175 vs. peer-group 190 — AMETEK underperformed its comp peer group even as CEO pay rose ($16.5M total, $21.3M comp-actually-paid in 2025). A recent CFO transition (Dalip Puri) and two Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks (Nov 2025, May 2026) warrant monitoring, though the comp metrics (Adjusted EPS, Relative TSR, ROTC) are well-aligned with the model.

6. **Legacy liabilities.** Asbestos litigation (largely seller-indemnified, no judgments) and PRP status at 13 environmental sites ($40.4M reserve, mostly de minimis). Not currently material.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($280-300+):** The order recovery proves durable as the automation/semiconductor/aerospace cycle re-accelerates, driving organic sales toward high-single-digits through 2026-2027. Margin expansion continues as acquisition dilution rolls off and EMG mix improves. The M&A engine adds 3-4 points of growth annually with disciplined returns, FARO/metrology compounds in higher-growth adjacencies, and the stock holds or modestly re-rates toward the upper end of its serial-acquirer cohort. On FY27 EPS at 32-34x, the stock reaches $280-300.

**Bear Case ($185-200):** The +22% order figure proves to be a timing/easy-comparison artifact, organic growth normalizes back toward the 2025 low-single-digit pace, and the cyclical end markets (oil & gas, process, aerospace capex) soften. Acquisition dilution persists, a reporting-unit downturn raises goodwill-impairment risk, and the forward multiple compresses toward 23-24x. On FY26 EPS at 23x, the stock falls toward $185-190 (roughly -18% to -20% from current).

The risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside only if the order recovery holds. The four-quarter beat streak, rising backlog, and stable-to-positive estimate revisions tilt the probability toward the constructive case, but the full multiple limits the cushion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $262.** Applying ~29.5x to FY27 consensus EPS yields ~$260, and ~32x FY26 EPS yields ~$260; the convergence anchors a $262 base case, consistent with the analyst mean/median of $259-261. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

- **Base case:** $262 (~+13% from $231)
- **Bull case:** $290 (~+25%)
- **Bear case:** $190 (~-18%)
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with Q2'26 and Q3'26 earnings (the order-durability test) and the integration of First Aviation / the June 2026 financed deal as the key catalysts.

**Thesis invalidation triggers (exit or downgrade):**
1. Organic orders turn negative or organic sales growth falls below +2% YoY in either Q2'26 or Q3'26, signaling the order surge was a timing artifact.
2. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge is taken at any reporting unit, confirming the M&A-return concern.
3. Segment operating margins compress more than ~150 bps from acquisition dilution without an offsetting organic recovery, or FY26 guidance is cut.

## Conclusion

AMETEK is a high-quality, asset-light industrial compounder at a fairly-valued price, with a confirmed cyclical recovery that the market has not yet fully extrapolated. The Q1'26 results provide concrete evidence the demand cycle is turning up, while ~26% operating margins, 113% FCF conversion, and ~0.8x net leverage make the franchise resilient. The ~29x forward multiple is the binding constraint — it offers no deep-value discount and limits multiple-expansion upside — but it is defensible against peers when adjusted for AME's superior margins and cash conversion, and it leaves ~13% base-case and ~25% bull-case upside driven by earnings growth.

The overall score of 6.65 sits above the BUY threshold, and the recommendation is **BUY** rather than HOLD for a specific, falsifiable reason: the demand inflection is real and measurable, it is corroborated by raised guidance and a four-quarter beat streak, estimates are stable-to-rising, and the multiple is below the peer cohort — the combination produces positive expected return over 12-18 months that a HOLD would understate. The risk that would invalidate this — order normalization in Q2-Q3 — is concrete and monitorable, and is the reason for entry discipline (accumulate on pullbacks toward the low-$220s) rather than chasing strength. The position should be sized with awareness that industrials carry a structural alpha headwind, so AME is a quality-tilt holding rather than a high-conviction concentrated bet.
