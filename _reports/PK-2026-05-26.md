## Executive Summary

Park Hotels & Resorts is a $2.3B lodging REIT owning 33 premium-branded hotels (~22,000 rooms) concentrated in Hawaii, Florida, New York, and other gateway markets. The company is currently navigating a $1.6B debt maturity wall in 2026 while simultaneously executing a $1B+ renovation cycle that temporarily depresses earnings. The stock trades at $11.47 with an 8.7% dividend yield, which superficially appears attractive.

The fundamental picture is deteriorating. EPS estimates are falling sharply (current year -38% over 90 days, next quarter -86%), the company has missed earnings 3 of the last 4 quarters, short interest sits at 25.2% of float (9 days to cover), and the Altman Z-Score of 1.29 places PK in the distress zone. While management has pre-arranged credit facilities to address the November 2026 HHV mortgage maturity, refinancing $1.6B at higher rates will mechanically increase interest expense and compress FFO further — precisely the dynamic the estimate revisions are capturing.

The renovation thesis (post-completion EBITDA uplift in 2027+) has merit based on early Bonnet Creek results, but it requires navigating 12-18 months of elevated financial risk with negative earnings momentum and macro headwinds from the current stagflationary environment compressing discretionary travel demand.

## Company Overview

Park Hotels & Resorts was spun off from Hilton Worldwide in January 2017 and operates as a pure-play hotel owner with no management operations. The portfolio is dominated by Hilton-branded properties (Hilton Hotels, Waldorf Astoria, Signia, Embassy Suites) across a "Core" portfolio (20 consolidated hotels generating ~90% of EBITDA) and a "Non-Core" portfolio (12 properties slated for disposal).

Key assets include the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort (the single largest asset, collateral for the critical $1.275B mortgage), the Bonnet Creek complex in Orlando (Waldorf Astoria + Signia by Hilton), and the New York Hilton Midtown. Geographic concentration is extreme: Florida and Hawaii represent ~36% of room count and 39% of revenue.

Since the spin-off, PK has disposed of 51 hotels for ~$3B total while reducing share count from 228M to 201M (12% reduction). The company strategically defaulted on $725M of non-recourse debt secured by two San Francisco Hiltons in 2023 — a rational economic decision given San Francisco's post-COVID hotel collapse, but one that eliminated ~$1.5B in book value and damaged credibility with creditors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings are declining on a reported basis:**

- Revenue peaked at $2.698B in 2023 and has fallen to $2.541B in 2025 (-5.8% from peak)
- Q1 2026 revenue of $622M declined 1.3% YoY
- Operating income swung to -$33M in 2025 (from +$391M in 2024) driven by a $318M impairment charge and $56M accelerated depreciation on the Royal Palm renovation
- Adjusted FFO/share declined from $2.06 (2024) to $1.97 (2025), and 2026 guidance is $1.73-$1.89 — a further 4-12% decline

**The balance sheet is the central concern:**

- Total debt: $3.85B at 5.2% weighted average rate (95% fixed)
- Cash: $156M (down from $232M at year-end 2025)
- **$1.6B maturing in 2026:** $1.275B HHV Mortgage (Nov 2026) + $324M other mortgage loans
- Available liquidity: $1B revolver + $800M undrawn Delayed Draw Term Loan + $700M undrawn Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan
- Post-refinancing, no significant maturities until Q4 2028

Management plans to draw on the Delayed Draw ($800M) and Bonnet Creek ($700M, SOFR + 2.25%) facilities to refinance. At current SOFR levels (~5.3%), the blended refinancing rate will likely exceed the existing rate on the HHV mortgage, adding ~$15-25M in incremental annual interest expense. This is the mechanism through which the estimate downgrades are flowing.

**Cash flow adequacy:**

- 2025 OCF: $398M
- 2025 FCF: $102M ($398M - $296M capex)
- At $83M/quarter capex run-rate and the $201M annual dividend obligation, FCF coverage is thin during the renovation-heavy period

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is negative.** Estimate revisions:

| Period | 90-Day Revision |
|--------|----------------|
| Current Quarter | -4.0% |
| Next Quarter | -86.0% |
| Current Year | -38.3% |
| Next Year | +80.4% |

The current/next quarter and current year deterioration reflects: (1) higher refinancing costs, (2) Royal Palm closure impact (one fewer operating hotel), (3) macro uncertainty on travel demand, and (4) slower Non-Core disposition timeline.

The +80.4% next-year revision reflects the market beginning to price in renovation completion uplift — Royal Palm reopening (June 2026), Hawaii tower completions, and full-year contribution from newly renovated assets. This creates a "valley" pattern: earnings trough in 2026 before potential recovery in 2027.

**2026 guidance (raised after Q1 beat):**
- RevPAR growth: 0.5%-2.5%
- Adjusted EBITDA: $587M-$617M (vs. $609M in 2025 — essentially flat at midpoint)
- The Q1 beat was driven by Bonnet Creek post-renovation strength (+6-10pp occupancy) and Hawaii recovery post-strike

**Long-term growth catalysts:**
- $1B+ renovation pipeline completion (2027): early evidence from Bonnet Creek shows 11-15% F&B revenue uplift and meaningful occupancy gains
- Royal Palm reopening coincides with FIFA World Cup 2026 (Miami matches)
- Non-Core dispositions fund buybacks at depressed prices
- Limited new hotel supply pipeline due to high construction costs

Macro headwinds are severe: elevated energy costs (airline fuel = less discretionary travel), potential consumer retrenchment, and international inbound travel declining amid trade tensions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $11.47, PK trades at:
- **8.7% dividend yield** (vs. 3.3% peer median) — this premium reflects elevated risk, not hidden value
- **EV/EBITDA ~10.5x** (on 2026E midpoint $602M) vs. peer range of 9-14x
- **Negative trailing P/E** (-10.6x) due to impairment charges; unhelpful
- **Forward P/E:** Not calculable on GAAP EPS (losses expected); on FFO/share ($1.81 midpoint), the stock trades at 6.3x — cheap for a REIT with luxury/upper-upscale assets

The peer comparison shows PK at a deep discount: peer median P/E is 24.2x with 3.3% yields. PK's 8.7% yield and negative P/E suggest the market is pricing in structural concerns (leverage, execution risk, asset quality questions).

The analyst consensus median target of $11.50 is essentially equal to the current price, suggesting no expected appreciation. The range is wide ($9.00-$19.50), reflecting genuine uncertainty about whether the renovation cycle payoff materializes.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis for further downside is sound (deteriorating estimates, high leverage, macro headwinds), but 25.2% short interest with 9 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk for new short initiation. The trade was correct to enter 6-12 months ago; at current SI levels, the remaining downside is limited relative to mechanical squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not initiate new shorts.

## Competitive Landscape

PK's competitive position is weakening relative to lodging REIT peers:

- **TSR of 84.35 vs. peer group 137.83** — substantial underperformance
- **Highest leverage** in the peer group relative to asset quality
- **Brand dependency on Hilton** without control over loyalty program or management decisions
- **Geographic concentration** (69% in six markets) reduces diversification benefits
- **The San Francisco default** may have permanently impaired capital market access relative to peers who honored all obligations

Positive differentiators:
- Iconic, irreplaceable assets (Hilton Hawaiian Village has no comparable competitor in Waikiki at 2,800+ rooms)
- Luxury/upper-upscale positioning carries higher RevPAR potential
- Active capital recycling from Non-Core to buybacks is shareholder-friendly if executed

PK is operating with higher risk and producing lower returns than comparably-sized peers (DRH, SHO, XHR, PEB).

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks (not priced by the market):**

1. **Refinancing execution risk (HIGH).** While credit facilities are committed, any delay or complication drawing the Delayed Draw or Bonnet Creek facilities would create an acute liquidity crisis. The $156M cash position provides zero margin for error.

2. **Demand deterioration in H2 2026 (MODERATE-HIGH).** Consumer spending buffer is eroding. A recession-driven RevPAR decline of 5-10% would violate growth guidance and compress EBITDA below the range needed to support the current capital structure.

3. **Non-Core disposal failures (MODERATE).** Barclays specifically flagged doubt that the program completes in 2026. Each quarter of delay adds carrying costs without offsetting cash inflows.

4. **Rate environment persistence.** If long-term yields remain elevated, hotel valuations are structurally impaired due to cap rate expansion working against asset values. This limits both refinancing terms and disposal proceeds.

**Options Market Signal:**

The P/C OI ratio surged from 1.05 to 15.69 in one month, indicating massive put accumulation relative to calls. The options and lending markets are expressing high conviction on continued downside. The extreme put/call ratio may mean the downside is already reflected in options pricing, making puts expensive.

**Short interest context:** At 25.2% of float with 9 days to cover, PK is one of the most heavily-shorted REITs. The persistence of this position (not a spike) suggests fundamental concerns rather than speculative positioning, though squeeze risk remains mechanically present.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($15-17):** Renovations complete successfully through H1 2027, generating a 15-20% EBITDA uplift to $700M+. Non-Core sales proceed at reasonable values, deleveraging the balance sheet. RevPAR growth accelerates on World Cup 2026 demand and Hawaii recovery. FFO/share normalizes to $2.30-$2.50, warranting a 6-7x multiple = $14-17.50. The 12% share count reduction amplifies per-share gains. Dividend remains secure at 8.7%. Probability: 25-30%.

**Base Case ($10-12):** Refinancing executes as planned but at higher costs. RevPAR grows at the low end of guidance (0.5-1%). Non-Core sales are slow and at impaired values. Consumer retrenchment limits 2027 recovery magnitude. FFO/share comes in at $1.73-$1.80 (low end of guide). Stock treads water around $11 with the dividend providing total return. Probability: 40-45%.

**Bear Case ($7-9):** Recession materializes in H2 2026, RevPAR declines 3-5%. Refinancing proceeds but at worse terms than modeled. Non-Core sales stall entirely. Dividend cut to preserve liquidity. FFO/share falls to $1.30-$1.50. Stock de-rates to 5x FFO = $6.50-$7.50. Probability: 25-30%.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The primary near-term catalyst is the November 2026 HHV mortgage maturity. While refinancing facilities are committed, the actual execution and final terms will be disclosed in Q3-Q4 2026. Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August) will reveal whether the raised RevPAR guidance holds against weakening consumer trends. If Q2 misses, the stock will likely breach the $9.84 52-week low.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The refinancing resolution (Nov 2026) and Q2/Q3 earnings provide clear checkpoints.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 25.2% SI and 9 days to cover, this is a crowded short. A positive catalyst (better-than-expected Q2, early refinancing at favorable terms, activist involvement, M&A rumor) could trigger a short squeeze of 15-25% in a compressed timeframe. The crowding makes the risk/reward on new short initiation unfavorable despite the fundamental thesis being sound.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $7.50-$9.00, representing 22-35% downside. Upside risk (squeeze to $13-14) represents 13-22% adverse move. Risk/reward is approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 — borderline given the crowding.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** Fundamental thesis for further downside is sound, but at current SI levels the remaining downside is limited relative to mechanical squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not initiate new shorts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view to HOLD or BUY):**
- Successful refinancing of HHV mortgage at ≤5.5% blended rate with no dividend reduction
- Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate revisions (current quarter and next quarter both revised upward)
- Short interest declining below 15%
- RevPAR growth exceeding 3% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating renovation payoff

**Timeline:** These headwinds are expected to persist through at least Q4 2026, with potential resolution in H1 2027 if renovations complete and refinancing executes cleanly.

## Conclusion

Park Hotels & Resorts presents a challenging risk/reward profile at $11.47. The 8.7% dividend yield is compensation for genuine structural risk: $1.6B in 2026 maturities, falling earnings estimates, 25% short interest, and macro headwinds threatening the leisure travel demand that underpins the portfolio. While the long-term renovation thesis has merit (Bonnet Creek early results are encouraging), the path to value realization requires navigating 12-18 months of acute financial risk with negative earnings momentum.

The analyst consensus median target of $11.50 confirms the market sees no near-term appreciation catalyst. With 12 of 18 analysts at HOLD, 2 at Strong Sell, and estimates falling at accelerating rates, the weight of evidence favors avoidance. The dividend yield alone does not compensate for the probability of a dividend cut in a recession scenario or the capital loss risk embedded in the $7.50-$9.00 bear case.

Investors seeking lodging REIT exposure should consider less leveraged alternatives (DRH at 22.9x P/E with 3.3% yield and lower refinancing risk) or wait for PK to resolve its 2026 maturity wall before re-engaging.
