**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Formula One (FWONA) completed a multi-year reorganization on December 15, 2025 that converted it from a tracking-stock complex into a pure-play global motorsports company. It now owns 100% of the commercial rights to Formula 1 (under "100-Year Agreements" running through 2110) and roughly 84% of MotoGP (rights through 2060, acquired July 3, 2025 for ~$3.66B). These are quasi-monopoly assets: ownership of the commercial rights to a global sport, not a team, with extraordinarily long-dated contracts, owned IP, contractual revenue escalators, and high operating leverage. F1 grew revenue 13.5% in 2025 to $3,873M with Adjusted OIBDA up 20% to $946M, and the consolidated business generated $789M of free cash flow on $4,482M of revenue (a 17.6% FCF margin). This is a high-quality, capital-light, contracted-revenue franchise.

The headline financials are a trap for automated screens. Reported revenue fell from $12.5B (2023) to $3.65B (2024) to $4.48B (2025), and total assets dropped from $41B to $15.4B, but this reflects the deconsolidation of SiriusXM (September 2024) and Live Nation/Liberty Live (December 2025), both now discontinued operations, not operating deterioration. The 2024 net loss of -$2.06B was a discontinued-operations artifact. The underlying motorsports business is growing double digits with strong cash conversion. Any model keying off the revenue cliff or the 1.82 Altman Z-Score (skewed by $7.0B goodwill plus $5.1B intangibles) will misjudge the name.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation against the current setup. The stock trades at a premium multiple with consensus current-year EPS cut 31% in 30 days and 61% in 90 days. The Malone control dynamic worsened in 2025 (he resigned from the board and the voting-cap mechanism is no longer expected to operate, so his voting power could drift toward a majority), which entrenches control and reduces change-of-control optionality. The options market is pricing acute near-term stress (one-month IV 53.6% versus 23.4% realized, steep backwardation). The MotoGP monetization thesis is real and is the entire incremental-upside case, but it is unproven, was funded with leverage, and the MotoGP team agreement (IRTA) must be renegotiated by end-2026. We see roughly balanced risk/reward at the current price and rate it a growth-oriented HOLD.

## Company Overview

Liberty Media Corporation, through the Liberty Live Split-Off completed December 15, 2025, became a pure-play motorsports company. The Liberty Formula One stock is no longer a tracking stock; it now represents the entire economic and voting interest in the company. Two operating segments:

**Formula 1 (100%-owned).** Exclusive commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship under the 100-Year Agreements. The 2025 season ran 24 races in 21 countries, with hundreds of millions of viewers across roughly 200 territories. Revenue is split among race promotion (26.7% of F1 revenue), media rights (31.3%), sponsorship (21.7%), and hospitality/freight/F2-F3/F1 Academy. Most contracts are multi-year, paid in advance, with contractual annual escalators (CPI-linked or fixed up to 5%), producing a highly visible recurring base. The 2026 Concorde Agreement locks in all 11 teams (Cadillac joins in 2026) through 2030.

**MotoGP (~84%-owned).** Exclusive commercial rights to the FIM Grand Prix World Championship through December 31, 2060. The 2025 season ran 22 events in 18 countries. The revenue model is similar to F1 but more media-rights-weighted (41.2%). The strategic thesis is to apply the F1 playbook (which grew F1 revenue from $1.83B in 2018 to $3.87B in 2025) to an under-monetized MotoGP that skews Euro/Asia-centric and is earlier in its monetization curve.

Three share series exist: FWONA is Series A (1 vote/share), FWONB is Series B (10 votes, almost entirely held by John Malone), and FWONK is the more liquid Series C (non-voting). Underlying company economics are identical across series; most sell-side coverage references FWONK.

## Financial Analysis

The continuing motorsports operations, not the headline reported totals, are the relevant numbers:

- **F1 revenue:** $3,411M (2024) → $3,873M (2025), +13.5%. F1 operating income $492M → $632M (+28%). F1 Adjusted OIBDA $791M → $946M (+20%).
- **Consolidated 2025:** Revenue $4,482M, operating income $577M, net income $555M, Adjusted OIBDA $1,068M (+38% YoY, aided by ~5.5 months of MotoGP).
- **MotoGP (pro-forma full-year):** Revenue $573M (2025) vs $503M (2024), +13.9% (+8.6% constant currency); Adjusted OIBDA $201M.
- **Cash generation:** Operating cash flow $908M, capex $119M, FCF $789M (17.6% margin). This is a high-conversion, capital-light model.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is roughly $5.1B long-term plus $52M short-term against $7.76B equity and $1.06B cash, for D/E of ~0.9x and a current ratio of 1.46x. The structure matters: corporate-level debt is small (~$499M, mostly 2.25% convertible notes due 2027), with most leverage sitting at the F1 and MotoGP subsidiary level (Senior Loan Facilities). The MotoGP acquisition was funded partly by a $2.5B distribution drawn on F1's facilities, raising consolidated leverage and lifting interest expense to $249M. The 2027 convert maturity and the subsidiary facilities are manageable given the FCF base, but the leverage added to fund MotoGP reduces the margin of safety relative to the pre-acquisition F1-only profile.

**Screening caveat.** The Altman Z of 1.82 (grey zone) and ROA of 3.9% understate quality because the large goodwill/intangible base inflates the asset denominator. For an asset-light rights business, EV/OIBDA and FCF are the informative metrics; Piotroski is 5/5 on available signals (positive net income, ROA, OCF; OCF > NI; current ratio >1).

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Over the last four quarters: Q1 2025 +162.6%, Q2 2025 +128.4%, Q3 2025 -11.0%, Q4 2025 in-line (-0.3%). Q1 2026 then beat handily ($0.03 vs -$0.28 expected per the research; the standardized record shows the FY24 split-affected pattern). The large positive surprises early in 2025 partly reflect reorganization timing and one-time items (the "F1" movie licensing revenue, for example), so they should not be extrapolated as a clean beat streak.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two layers. The first is the contracted, escalating F1 base: multi-year promotion and media-rights contracts with built-in escalators, plus visible sponsorship growth, support mid-to-high single-digit organic growth before any new monetization. The Apple U.S. F1 media-rights deal (five years starting 2026, replacing ESPN's seven-year run) is a meaningful renewal at scale and reflects the broader re-rating of premium live sports rights in a fragmenting streaming market. F1 owns the championship; Apple is a distributor/licensee, so this is a revenue contract for Liberty, not an equity dependency.

The second layer is MotoGP, which is the entire incremental-upside thesis. The same management team that roughly tripled F1 revenue from 2018 to 2025 is applying the playbook to an under-monetized asset with a rights runway to 2060. Liberty is exploring a second U.S. race (Miami described as "logical"). BofA called MotoGP "a major catalyst"; Morgan Stanley named the stock its top media/entertainment pick. The upside is genuine but unproven, and there is no near-term hard data point that quantifies the MotoGP uplift yet.

The cautionary signal is consensus EPS revisions. Current-year EPS has been cut from $3.75 (90 days ago) to $2.13 (30 days ago) to $1.47 now, a 31% cut in 30 days and 61% in 90 days; next-year was cut 17% in 30 days. Some of this reflects post-reorganization re-basing of estimates and integration costs (including the one-time $50M Concorde incentive payments excluded from Adjusted OIBDA), but the direction is unambiguously negative and contradicts a clean growth-acceleration narrative. We weight the falling revisions against the operating-OIBDA growth and conclude the growth thesis is durable but the near-term earnings trajectory is being marked down faster than the operating story justifies, which keeps us from upgrading.

## Valuation Assessment

FWONA trades at $79.73, near the low end of its 52-week range ($73.70–$99.52), at roughly 35x trailing EPS of $2.17. On the standardized data the forward multiple is ~35x; on the more conservative basis the research brief cites (~51x forward earnings per Seeking Alpha), it is richer still. The business is better valued on EV/OIBDA or FCF: against $1,068M consolidated Adjusted OIBDA (with a partial year of MotoGP, so the run-rate is higher), the ~$1.9B equity market cap plus ~$4.1B net-of-cash debt implies a mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit EV/OIBDA depending on how one annualizes MotoGP — reasonable for a quasi-monopoly rights asset, but not cheap given the leverage and the falling EPS estimates.

Analyst price targets cluster well above spot: low $100, high $115, mean $108.67, median $110, with six Buy ratings and no Holds or Sells. On a stock with positive revisions and a sub-30x forward multiple, those targets would function as floors. That is not the situation here. With consensus EPS being cut sharply and a premium multiple, analyst targets and the implied price both embed the same MotoGP re-rating optimism, so the targets are not independent confirmation of upside. The gap between $79.73 and a $108–110 consensus, combined with sharply falling estimates, signals that the sell-side targets are stale on the downside rather than the stock being obviously mispriced cheap.

The bull valuation argument is the sum-of-the-parts simplification: with the tracking-stock complexity (SiriusXM, Braves, Live Nation, QuintEvents) fully unwound, the historical conglomerate discount should collapse, and a clean single-asset-class equity should re-rate. That is plausible and is part of why we hold rather than avoid. But a premium multiple on falling near-term earnings, with the upside resting on unproven MotoGP execution, does not offer asymmetric upside today. We score valuation 5.0 — fair, not a mispricing in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is the strongest dimension. Liberty owns the commercial rights to two global sports, not teams. The rights are extraordinarily long-dated (2110 for F1, 2060 for MotoGP), the IP (footage from 1981/1994 onward, trademarks) is owned outright, and the businesses carry strong pricing power and operating leverage. The structural tailwind is the secular re-rating of premium, can't-time-shift live sports rights in a fragmenting media market, the dynamic that produced the Apple F1 deal. F1's U.S. growth, helped by "Drive to Survive," is the template now being applied to MotoGP.

Competition is for viewership, attendance, and sponsorship/media dollars against all global sports rights (Olympics, FIFA, Champions League, major leagues) and the broader live-entertainment market, not for the franchises themselves. The trajectory is improving share of wallet and widening monetization, particularly in the U.S. The constraints on the moat come from the governance side rather than competition: the FIA and FIM hold consent/approval rights over calendar, regulations, and circuit homologation and prioritize sporting/safety concerns over Liberty's commercial interests, and under the 2026 Concorde Agreement 70% team consent is required for more than 24 races or fewer than 8 Europe/North America races, capping calendar expansion. Net trajectory is positive enough to score 8.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Existential, low-probability:** Termination of the 100-Year Agreements (F1) or the FIM Agreement (MotoGP) would effectively end the respective business. Triggers include unpermitted change of control, material breach, or insolvency. A decline in sport popularity, a credible breakaway series, a scandal, or fatal accidents could impair the franchise. These are tail risks, not base-case, but they are uniquely severe for a rights-only business.

**Near-term and elevated:** MotoGP integration risk is newly flagged in the 2025 10-K (84% stake, preliminary purchase price allocation, unproven monetization). The MotoGP team agreement (IRTA) expires end-2026 and is being renegotiated for 2027–2031 with "no assurance" of favorable terms — a concrete watch item. The 2024/2025 split-offs could trigger large tax liabilities under Section 355(e) if a 50%+ ownership change is deemed part of a plan, and Liberty may forgo attractive M&A to preserve tax-free treatment. Added 2025 risk language on U.S. tariffs and discretionary-spending exposure touches live events and hospitality. Many promotion and media contracts are with governments/sovereign entities, adding counterparty and FX risk (MotoGP is Euro-functional).

**Governance:** As of January 31, 2026, Malone controls ~49% of voting power via ~97% of Series B shares. The Exchange Agreement mechanism that capped his voting power at ~49% is no longer expected to operate, so his voting power could exceed 49% toward a majority. This entrenches control, deters takeovers, and reduces change-of-control optionality, while a change of control could itself jeopardize the 100-Year Agreements. The multi-class structure, staggered board, 66⅔% supermajority requirements, and overlapping directors/officers with other Liberty entities compound the conflict-of-interest profile.

**Insider activity:** The large December 2025 Form 4 cluster is almost entirely mechanical (forced disposal of LLYV-linked securities in the split-off, code "J"), not discretionary selling of F1 stock. The one discretionary signal is Director Chase Carey (former F1 CEO/chairman), who exercised and immediately sold options in December 2025 (~$16M) and May 2026 (~$9M at ~$90/share). Given the deep-in-the-money strikes ($28–$33), this reads as diversification by a departing-era insider rather than a directional bet. There is no meaningful open-market insider buying to offset it.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. One-month ATM IV is 53.6% versus 23.4% one-year realized (a ~30-point premium), with a term structure in steep backwardation (53.6% near-term versus 28.0% at six months) and an implied move of roughly ±6.6% to ±10% into the June 18 expiry. The skew is call-heavy (-12.4%) and put/call OI is just 0.05, so the elevated near-term IV is pricing an event with two-sided or upside-leaning positioning rather than a directional crash bet. Short interest is modest at 5.4% of float (10.1 days to cover). The market expects a near-term catalyst (likely tied to MotoGP/IRTA news flow or estimate resets), the IV is rich, and this is not a clean cheap-entry setup. The richness argues for patience rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** A clean, single-asset-class equity holding quasi-monopoly rights to two global sports with contracted, escalating, paid-in-advance revenue and 17.6% FCF margins. The sum-of-the-parts discount collapses post-reorganization and the stock re-rates. MotoGP monetization, executed by the team that tripled F1 revenue, delivers double-digit revenue and OIBDA growth on a 2060 runway, with a second U.S. race adding optionality. The Apple deal validates premium-rights pricing power. Bull target ~$110–115, in line with the high end of consensus.

**Bear case.** A premium multiple on a business with sharply falling near-term EPS estimates, leverage added to fund an unproven MotoGP acquisition, a worsening Malone control overhang that caps change-of-control value, and a near-term IRTA renegotiation with no assurance of favorable terms. If MotoGP monetization disappoints or integration costs persist, the multiple compresses toward the mid-$60s. Bear target ~$65 (roughly -18% from current).

The MotoGP thesis carries the incremental upside, and it is unproven and leveraged. The base business is excellent and defensible, but the current price already embeds substantial re-rating optimism while estimates are being marked down. That combination yields balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Hold for the multi-year MotoGP monetization and post-reorganization re-rating thesis; the business quality justifies ownership, but the entry is not asymmetric at the current price.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any IRTA renegotiation news flow over the next two to three quarters; the IRTA agreement must be renegotiated by end-2026, which is the key dated watch item.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- MotoGP delivers a quantified monetization data point (a major media-rights renewal at a premium, a confirmed second U.S. race, or constant-currency OIBDA growth accelerating above the F1 base) that converts the unproven thesis into hard evidence.
- IRTA renegotiated on favorable 2027–2031 terms, removing the near-term team-agreement overhang.
- Price pullback toward the mid-$70s (near the 52-week low of $73.70) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside to the $100+ consensus, or stabilization/turn in current-year EPS revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- IRTA renegotiation fails or produces materially worse economics, or any credible threat to the F1/MotoGP commercial-rights agreements (FIA/FIM dispute, change-of-control trigger).
- Continued EPS revision deterioration beyond integration timing (current-year consensus cut a further 20%+ without an offsetting OIBDA explanation), or evidence MotoGP monetization is structurally below the F1 template.
- Malone voting power moving to outright majority in a way that triggers a tax-free-status or change-of-control complication.

## Conclusion

Liberty Formula One is a capital-light business with contracted, escalating revenue from quasi-monopoly rights to two global sports with runways to 2060 and 2110, generating $789M of free cash flow at a 17.6% margin. The reorganization has simplified the equity into a clean motorsports pure-play, and the competitive position is excellent. We are not buyers at $79.73 because the stock carries a premium multiple while current-year EPS estimates have been cut 61% in 90 days, the upside rests entirely on unproven and leveraged MotoGP monetization, the Malone control overhang has worsened, and the options market is pricing acute near-term stress into the June expiry. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate FWONA a HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$70s or on hard confirmation that MotoGP monetization and the IRTA renegotiation are tracking favorably.
