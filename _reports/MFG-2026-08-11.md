## Executive Summary

Mizuho has delivered what a Japanese megabank was supposed to deliver in a rate-normalization cycle. Net income attributable to shareholders for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026 was ¥1,158.0bn (≈$8.29bn), reported as a record +41% year, and the June 2026 quarter grew profit 45% year over year with the annual forecast raised. Consensus EPS estimates have moved up 9.6% in thirty days for the current fiscal year. The ADS has advanced 67.6% over the trailing twelve months, from $6.24 in August 2025 to $10.71, touching a 52-week high of $10.86 on August 5.

The central analytical issue is that the reported valuation screens are wrong by a factor of five, and once corrected the stock is no longer cheap. The supplied profile shows a trailing and forward P/E of 3.11 against EPS of $3.17, but that EPS is a per-common-share figure while the price is per ADS, and one ADS represents approximately one-fifth of a common share (three independent reconciliations of reported net income against the primary-source share count of 2,484,581,000 converge on a 4.8-5.0x ratio, consistent with a pre-2020 2:1 ratio surviving a 1-for-10 reverse split). On the correct per-ADS basis, trailing EPS is roughly $0.63-0.67 and the stock trades at 16-17x trailing, 14.5x the current-year consensus of $0.74 and 12.2x the out-year $0.88. Implied market capitalization is approximately $133bn (2.485bn shares at an implied $53.55 per common share). Against an externally reported 1.3x price-to-book struck in March 2026 at roughly $8 per ADS, the current price implies something closer to 1.7x book on a trailing ROE of roughly 9%. That is a full multiple for a Japanese megabank, not a discounted one.

Three things keep this from being a REDUCE. Estimate revisions are positive and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters by 19-54%. Management raised the annual forecast at the July 30 print, and the ADS rose from $9.81 to $10.35 that day and to a new high a week later. And the share count is genuinely shrinking — from 2,537.1m at the FY2024 cover date to 2,484.6m at the FY2026 cover date, with a monotonic interim reading in between, i.e. ~1.8% annual accretion that was absent in FY2022-FY2024. Three things keep it from being an ACCUMULATE: the price sits above the $10.48 consensus mean target with the high at $12.06; the earnings driver is exogenous BOJ policy that Fitch and Morningstar both expect to moderate from FY2027, with Fitch attributing part of the recent upside to market-related gains and acquisitions; and capital adequacy, asset quality and dividend policy are not verifiable from available disclosure, which caps the confidence any rating here can carry. HOLD, with the honest label that the uncertainty is evidentiary as much as fundamental.

## Company Overview

Mizuho Financial Group is one of three Japanese megabank groups, operating principally through Mizuho Bank (commercial banking) and Mizuho Securities, with a franchise organized around capital markets, advisory, treasury and markets, asset management and institutional services, delivered across Japan, Asia-Pacific, the Americas and EMEA. It is a foreign private issuer: it files a Form 20-F annually (most recently June 26, 2026 for the year ended March 31, 2026) and discloses interim results on Form 6-K (two filed July 30, 2026).

Two structural features of the disclosure regime matter to how this report should be read. First, standard credit screens are unavailable and inapplicable: Altman Z and Piotroski are built on working-capital, current-ratio and asset-turnover inputs that invert or lose meaning for a deposit-funded lender. Their absence is not a distress signal. Second, SEC XBRL company facts for the issuer contain only share-count data; no revenue, income, balance-sheet or cash-flow tags are machine-retrievable, and direct retrieval of the 20-F text was blocked. Consequently the income-statement figures below are externally reported and the per-share arithmetic is derived. Share counts are the one primary-source financial fact available.

Third-party framing places Mizuho as the most domestically rate-levered of the three. Morningstar attributes its earnings driver specifically to net interest margin gains, versus MUFG (Morgan Stanley associate contribution) and SMFG (~35% of loans outside Japan). Management is marketing corporate and investment banking league-table progress: its investor-relations page flagged a July 6, 2026 update to a page titled "Strengthening our competitive edge in CIB business." That indicates where management wants attention directed; it does not establish share gains.

**Earnings quality — the base effect.** The +41% headline is materially flattered by the comparison quarter. If Q4 profit rose 660% to $2.51bn, the year-ago quarter was approximately $0.33bn; working the full year backward from +41% implies a prior-year total of roughly $5.88bn, of which the first three quarters were roughly $5.55bn. On that arithmetic, nine-month-to-nine-month growth was low single digits and essentially the entire annual increase arose in the January-March quarter. Two caveats are material: rounded headline percentages compound error, and the sources may compute on different bases (US GAAP in the 20-F versus Japanese GAAP domestically; yen versus USD-converted). The alternative reading is that the prior-year Q4 contained a one-time charge and the current quarter is the clean number, in which case the run-rate is higher than the nine-month comparison implies. I cannot resolve which, and the distinction matters directly to the forward earnings level. Fitch's independent comment that "recent upside has come from one-off items, including market-related gains and contributions from acquisitions" is consistent with the less flattering reading.

**Quarterly delivery.** Four consecutive beats: 51.5% (Sep-2025 quarter), 19.3% (Dec-2025), 28.4% (Mar-2026) and 54.4% (Jun-2026), on a per-common-share yen basis. The magnitude and consistency argue that sell-side models are systematically behind the NII trajectory, which supports confidence in the direction of near-term estimates even where the individual quarterly figures do not fully reconcile against the annual total (they do not: the four listed quarters sum to more than the ¥466 per share implied by ¥1,158.0bn over ~2.49bn shares, indicating a basis or period-labeling difference I could not close). Treat the beat record as a directional signal on estimate momentum, not as a reconciled earnings series.

The interim reading confirms a progressive decline rather than a single cancellation event. The share-count decline is a fact; the buyback program behind it is an inference: treasury-share cancellation or capital-structure mechanics could produce a similar pattern, and no authorization size, yen amount or duration was obtained.

**What is not established.** CET1 ratio, total assets, loan balances, NPL ratio and coverage, NIM, LCR, deposit mix and betas, cross-shareholding reduction progress, and declared dividend per share are all unknown. The correct conclusion on capital adequacy and asset quality is "not assessed," not "sound." This is the single largest constraint on the Financial Health score, which sits at 5.8 rather than the 7+ that a well-capitalized megabank printing record earnings might otherwise support. The reported trailing dividend yield of 1.32% (≈$0.138 per ADS) appears to reflect an incomplete payment period rather than the full declared annual rate; because I cannot verify the declared dividend or payout ratio, I do not underwrite an income case for this name.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is real, sector-wide, and driven by a variable Mizuho does not control.

All three megabanks printed record annual profits for the year ended March 2026 — MUFG +30% to ¥2.4tn, SMFG +34%, Mizuho +41% — and all three printed 33-48% first-quarter growth in the June 2026 quarter (MUFG +48%, Mizuho +45%, SMFG +33%). Fitch's explanation is generic to the sector: higher yen rates improving lending margins, healthy corporate funding demand, stronger fee income. Reuters attributed MUFG's Q1 to "strong loan demand linked to the end of deflation in Japan." UBS frames this as structural: end of deflation, higher domestic rates, stronger wholesale activity. That breadth raises the durability of the demand thesis relative to a single-company story.

The durability of *Mizuho's* participation is where the disagreement sits. Morningstar expects Mizuho's NIM gains to ease from FY2027 as non-Japan rates resume an easing cycle, and sees MUFG decelerating to ~5% and SMFG to ~9% over comparable horizons. Fitch expects growth to moderate with credit costs and deposit competition ahead, and questions the sustainability of profit growth at current levels. Because Mizuho is the most domestically levered of the three, it has the greatest sensitivity to further BOJ tightening from the 0.75% policy rate verified as of December 2025, and the weakest relative position if global easing dominates. The current BOJ rate is not verified in this cycle; the policy path is the central variable in the thesis and I cannot observe it directly.

Consensus estimates: current-year $0.74 per ADS (+9.6% in 30 days, +6.7% in 90 days), next-year $0.88 (+18.9% over current year). The direction is unambiguous and the 30-day acceleration exceeds the 90-day trend, which is the more informative pattern. Two qualifications: the revision magnitude is well below the threshold at which estimate momentum becomes a standalone buy signal, and the panel is a single analyst — an extremely thin consensus that can move sharply on one model update. The company's own raised annual forecast is the more credible anchor, and its magnitude was not obtainable.

Growth Prospects scores 6.0: confirmed acceleration and rising estimates, discounted for exogeneity, third-party expectations of a FY2027 plateau, and the base-effect and one-off contamination in the trailing number.

## Valuation Assessment

**Correcting the screen.** The supplied 3.11x P/E is an artifact of mixing a per-common-share EPS ($3.169) with a per-ADS price. Any screen that treats $10.71 as a claim on a full common share understates the multiple fivefold. Corrected:

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Implied common-share price | $10.71 × 5 | $53.55 |
| Implied market cap | 2.4846bn × $53.55 | ≈$133bn |
| Trailing P/E | $10.71 ÷ $0.63-0.67 | 16.0-17.0x |
| Current-year P/E | $10.71 ÷ $0.74 | 14.5x |
| Out-year P/E | $10.71 ÷ $0.88 | 12.2x |
| Implied P/B | 1.3x (Mar-2026, ≈$8.06) adjusted for price and modest book accretion | ≈1.7x |

The price-to-book estimate is single-source and unverified, so treat 1.7x as approximate. Its implication is the important part: 1.3x book against a 15x P/E in March implied a trailing ROE near 8.7%, and paying 1.7x book for a 9-10% ROE bank requires the ROE to move toward 11-12% for the multiple to be reasonable. That is what the bull case on further BOJ hikes assumes, and what Morningstar and Fitch question from FY2027. For context, US regional banks earning 11-13% ROE trade in a 1.5-2.0x tangible-book band [unverified — no peer benchmark data available]; MFG is being priced in that band on a lower current return.

**Market-derived checks.** The consensus mean and median target is $10.48, below the $10.71 close; the high is $12.06 (+12.6%) and the low $8.90 (-16.9%). Analyst targets function as floors on names where the stock is cheap relative to rising estimates, and that condition is only partly met here. Forward multiples of 14.5x and 12.2x are not stretched, but the stock has already closed the gap to consensus, the analyst distribution has shifted to one Buy and one Strong Sell over the past two months (from one Buy and one Hold), and the driver is a macro variable rather than a company-specific execution ramp that analysts habitually underestimate. Short interest of 0.1% of float and 2.5 days to cover indicates no one is positioned against this: there is neither a squeeze mechanic to harvest nor an informed bearish cohort to fade.

**Sector-relative caution.** Financials selections have historically underperformed the index on a sector-adjusted basis absent a distinct company catalyst. Mizuho's catalyst of BOJ normalization is the sector's catalyst, shared with MUFG and SMFG, and it is well recognized (Nomura has SMFG and Mizuho as top picks). A rating that requires the trade to keep working for everyone equally is not company-specific alpha.

Valuation scores 5.0: not expensive on forward earnings, not cheap on book against current returns, and offering no margin of safety at a price above the consensus mean.

## Competitive Landscape

Mizuho holds an entrenched position in Japanese corporate banking with a global wholesale footprint. Beyond that, specifics on market share, loan-book composition, deposit-franchise cost advantage, segment profitability and fee mix are unavailable, so the assessment must be trajectory-based on the evidence that exists.

Two developments point in a positive direction. Management is actively marketing CIB league-table progress, which at minimum suggests it believes the trajectory is defensible. And the Rakuten Bank alliance, reportedly entered May 20, 2026, addresses the sector's next likely pressure point directly: Mizuho Bank reportedly takes 23,559,673 Rakuten Bank Class A shares (convertible to common) as part of Rakuten's FinTech reorganization, while Rakuten Bank acquires corporate loans that Mizuho originates. Read against Fitch's warning about deposit competition, this is a coherent structure: Mizuho contracts for access to a low-cost digital retail deposit base by selling it corporate credit, rather than competing for retail deposits through its own branches. It is simultaneously an implicit acknowledgement that Mizuho's own retail funding franchise is not where it would like it to be. All deal terms — consideration, resulting ownership percentage, conditions, accounting treatment — come from a single low-tier secondary source and are unconfirmed against the corresponding 6-K.

Two point in a negative direction. Mizuho is the least internationally diversified of the three on earnings drivers, which is an advantage only while the BOJ is the marginal tightener. And an outside author's characterization of high operating costs and limited commission-revenue growth, while unverified, is not contradicted by anything I could obtain. There is also a genuine tension between the two legs of the bull case: UBS notes that M&A financing, large-corporate lending, overseas loans and structured transactions consume more capital than domestic lending, so a CIB share-gain strategy grows risk-weighted assets and competes with buyback capacity for the same capital. An investor underwriting both CIB expansion and accelerating repurchase may be counting the same capital twice. Without CET1, RWA or management capital targets, this is a hypothesis; strong retained earnings plus cross-shareholding disposals could fund both comfortably.

Competitive Position scores 5.5: stable-to-modestly-improving position, with the improvement asserted by management rather than verified, and the relative positioning among the three weakest on cycle durability.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-path dependence.** The earnings engine is BOJ policy. The verified anchor is 0.75% as of December 2025, a three-decade high. Morningstar expects NIM gains to ease from FY2027; Fitch expects moderation. If the normalization path stalls, the multiple and the earnings both compress, and Mizuho is the most exposed of the three.

**Earnings quality.** Fitch attributes part of the upside to one-off market-related gains and acquisition contributions. The independent Q4 base-effect decomposition points the same way. A normalization that strips ~15% of earnings as non-recurring takes the trailing multiple from ~16x to ~19x on the same price.

**Credit costs and deposit competition.** Reuters framed the megabanks' record results against growing fears of borrower stress in private credit. Fitch flags higher credit costs and deposit competition; the latter is what converts a rate tailwind into a margin squeeze, and Japanese deposit betas through a full tightening cycle are untested.

**Capital and asset quality unverified.** No CET1, no NPL, no coverage, no LCR. This is a genuine risk to the *rating*, not merely to the report: a capital or credit surprise would be unforeseeable from the evidence base available.

**Geopolitical and macro transmission.** Reuters cited Middle East war-driven energy and supply-chain volatility as the backdrop for the June quarter. MUFG's CEO warned of bottom-line impact; SMFG said such risks are partly provisioned; Mizuho said only that it will monitor and "flexibly revise" its outlook, the least specific of the three on provisioning. Separately, Japanese financial conditions are a live stress channel: a steeper long end lifts NII but pressures marks on held bonds and raises the risk of carry-unwind and forced foreign-asset repatriation across the Japanese institutional complex. I treat this as a hypothesis, not a verified exposure, because no securities-portfolio or duration data was obtainable.

**FX for the USD holder.** All ADS returns embed yen/dollar. The USD figures above rest on assumed rates of roughly 139-145 JPY/USD; a 5% move changes reported USD earnings and market cap by roughly the same amount with no change to the business.

**Governance and disclosure.** As a foreign private issuer, Mizuho files no proxy and its insiders are generally outside Section 16, so the absence of insider buying carries no signal here, and no compensation-alignment or related-party conclusion is available. Two unresolved items: a 6-K/A amendment dated May 29, 2026 (purpose unknown) and an "Extraordinary Report" filed July 1, 2026. Mizuho's history of major IT failures and associated regulatory action earlier this decade was not verified in this cycle and is flagged only as a confirmation item; it is nonetheless directly relevant to the reported Rakuten clause under which Mizuho would provide backup cash-disbursement capability to another bank.

**Capital-deployment reaction.** When news broke on May 18 that Mizuho was considering a Rakuten Bank investment, the ADS fell as much as 7.7%. That is direct evidence that shareholders view capital deployed into the Rakuten financial complex as a risk rather than an unambiguous positive.

Risk Factors scores 4.8: the rate-path binary, the earnings-quality question and the unverifiable capital picture are each material, and none is fully priced into a stock trading at a 52-week high above consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $13.20 per ADS (+23.2%)

The BOJ continues tightening from 0.75%, deposit betas stay low through the next 100bp, and Mizuho, the most domestically rate-levered megabank, captures the largest incremental NII. Estimates keep migrating up: the current-year consensus has already moved 9.6% in thirty days, the company raised its own annual forecast in July, and the June quarter beat by 54%. If FY2027 (year ending March 2028) EPS reaches the $0.88 consensus and the market applies 15x — a reasonable multiple for a bank whose ROE is migrating from 9% toward 11-12% — the ADS is worth $13.20. Supporting legs: the ~1.8% annual share-count decline continues or accelerates; the Rakuten alliance delivers cheap deposit funding for originate-and-distribute corporate credit, structurally lowering Mizuho's cost of funds ahead of the sector's deposit-competition phase; CIB league-table progress converts to durable fee income that reduces rate dependence; and Japanese equity strength lifts cross-shareholding marks and disposal gains. Nomura's view that the megabanks look undervalued relative to earnings strength is the sell-side expression of this case.

### Bear Case — $8.15 per ADS (-23.9%)

The rate trade has already been paid for. Fitch is right that recent upside is partly one-off market gains and acquisition contributions, and Morningstar is right that NIM gains ease from FY2027 as non-Japan rates fall. Underlying nine-month growth into March 2026 was low single digits once the washed-out comparison quarter is removed. Deposit competition arrives, betas rise faster than asset yields reprice, and credit costs normalize from an unusually benign base, plausibly with a private-credit or Middle East-linked corporate impairment as the trigger. Meanwhile CIB expansion consumes capital that the market has been assuming funds buybacks, and the Rakuten stake absorbs more. On flat-to-down current-year EPS and an 11x multiple — where Japanese megabanks traded for most of the last decade — the ADS is $8.15, near the $8.90 analyst low and consistent with the March 2026 trading range. A stronger yen would soften the local decline for USD holders; a weaker yen would compound it.

### Base Case — $11.45 per ADS (+6.9%)

Mizuho delivers the current-year consensus of $0.74 and progresses toward $0.88, but the multiple does not expand further because the market has already re-rated the sector and third-party analysts are flagging a FY2027 plateau. $11.45 is 15.5x current-year $0.74 and 13.0x out-year $0.88, the midpoint of where a 10-11% ROE Japanese bank with credible but decelerating growth should clear. Adding the reported 1.3% dividend yield gives roughly 8% total return over twelve months. Probability-weighting the three cases at 50/25/25 gives an expected price return of approximately +3.2%, or about +4.5% including the dividend as reported. That is fair-to-slightly-below-fair for the risk taken, which is what a HOLD means.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no entry signal at $10.71.

**Reassessment timeline.** FY2026 second-quarter results, expected late October/early November 2026 (Mizuho discloses interim results on Form 6-K; the Q1 package was published July 30). Interim checkpoints: each BOJ policy meeting, and any 6-K disclosing the Rakuten transaction terms or a buyback authorization.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. Price at or below $9.25 — approximately 12.5x current-year consensus of $0.74 — with estimates still rising. That level restores roughly 24% upside to the base case and would offset the absence of verified capital data.
2. Verified CET1 at or above 11.5% together with a disclosed buyback authorization of 3% or more of shares outstanding with a stated yen amount, converting the inferred repurchase program into an underwritable capital-return leg.
3. BOJ policy rate at or above 1.00% accompanied by disclosed deposit betas below 30% and a second consecutive upward revision to the company's own annual forecast. This is the specific evidence that the NII tailwind is passing through rather than being competed away.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any one of:**
1. The BOJ signals a pause or reversal, or the megabank complex derates together on a falling domestic yield curve. The single-variable dependence means this alone is sufficient.
2. Second-quarter results show net interest income flat or down sequentially with rising deposit costs, or credit costs rising more than 50% year over year.
3. Disclosure that Rakuten-related or CIB-driven capital deployment materially reduces buyback capacity — specifically, a stated reduction in the repurchase program or a CET1 decline of more than 50bp attributable to RWA growth.
4. A recurrence of material operational or regulatory action of the kind Mizuho experienced earlier this decade, which would reprice both the multiple and the funding franchise.

**Avoid trigger:** confirmation of a capital or asset-quality problem that the unverified disclosure base is currently masking — a CET1 print below 10%, a material adverse-development charge, or a restatement.

## Conclusion

Mizuho is a legitimately improved business trading at a price that reflects it. The BOJ normalization trade has produced record profits across all three megabanks, Mizuho has captured the largest percentage gain of the three, it has beaten consensus four quarters running by 19-54%, it has raised its annual forecast, and it is shrinking its share count for the first time in years. None of that is in dispute.

What is in dispute is whether the earnings level holds and whether the current multiple leaves anything for a new buyer. On the corrected per-ADS basis the stock is 16-17x trailing, 14.5x current-year, and roughly 1.7x book on a 9-10% return on equity — full for a Japanese bank whose principal earnings driver is exogenous monetary policy that two independent rating and research houses expect to plateau from FY2027, and part of whose trailing outperformance Fitch attributes to non-recurring items. The price sits above the $10.48 consensus mean target and 1.4% below its 52-week high, with the analyst distribution having shifted to one Buy and one Strong Sell. Capital adequacy, asset quality and dividend policy could not be verified, which limits how much confidence any rating on this name can carry and argues against paying up.

The probability-weighted expected return of roughly 4.5% including dividends, against 24% bear-case downside, is a fair-value proposition. HOLD. The setup becomes interesting again near $9.25, or on verified capital and buyback disclosure that converts the inferred capital-return story into an underwritable one.

- Rationale: Fair value is 15.5x current-year consensus of $0.74 per ADS and 13.0x the out-year $0.88, the mid-range multiple for a 10-11% ROE Japanese bank with decelerating growth.

**Thesis:** Mizuho is the most domestically rate-levered Japanese megabank and has converted BOJ normalization into a record year, four consecutive beats and a raised FY forecast, with the share count shrinking ~1.8% annually for the first time in years. The problem is price: after a 67.6% twelve-month advance the ADS discounts that delivery, while the earnings driver is exogenous policy that Fitch and Morningstar expect to plateau from FY2027.

**Variant view:** Consensus screens show a 3.1x P/E because a per-common-share EPS is divided into a per-ADS price; the true multiple is roughly 5x higher, so the 'cheap Japanese bank' setup is a data artifact. Separately, the +41% record year is largely a washed-out March-quarter base rather than underlying momentum.

### Catalysts

- **Medium — 3-4 months:** Q2 FY2026 results (late Oct/early Nov) test NII pass-through, deposit costs and a possible second upward revision to the company's own annual forecast.
- **Medium — 3-9 months:** Disclosure of CET1 plus a sized buyback authorization would convert the inferred ~1.8% annual share-count reduction into an underwritable capital-return leg.
- **Medium — 6-12 months:** BOJ policy rate above 1.00% with deposit betas staying low would extend the NII tailwind Mizuho is most levered to among the three megabanks.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **0-12 months:** BOJ signals a pause or reversal, or the megabank complex derates together on a falling domestic yield curve.
- **3-4 months:** Q2 net interest income flat or down sequentially with rising deposit costs, or credit costs up more than 50% year over year.
- **0-12 months:** CET1 printed below 10%, a material adverse-development charge, or a restatement.
