## Executive Summary

Kinetik Holdings is a Delaware Basin-focused integrated midstream operator and the 4th largest natural gas processor in the Permian Basin by capacity (2.4+ Bcf/d). The company delivered record Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $251M and reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of $950M–$1.05B, representing ~7% growth at the midpoint (adjusted for the EPIC crude pipeline sale). At ~$50/share, the stock trades at approximately 7.5x EV/EBITDA — fair-to-modestly-cheap relative to midstream peers (AM at ~10x, DTM at ~12x) — while offering a 6.5% dividend yield with 3-5% annual growth targets.

The investment case hinges on the distinction between GAAP EPS (structurally meaningless for this partnership, swinging from -$0.07 to +$2.16 quarter-to-quarter due to non-cash items and gain/loss recognition) and the cash-flow-based metrics midstream investors use. On EV/EBITDA and dividend yield, KNTK offers reasonable value. However, several real risks prevent a more constructive stance: 3.9x leverage with $1.81B floating-rate exposure, geographic concentration risk in a single basin facing Waha gas price weakness (causing ~220 MMcf/d of production curtailments), a pending Energy Transfer bench trial with uncertain magnitude, and a persistent I Squared Capital selling overhang (~17M units remaining to be liquidated). The 6.5% yield compensates holders during this period of uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Kinetik Holdings Inc. is a C-corp holding company that owns the general partner interest and ~42% limited partner interest in Kinetik Holdings LP. The remaining ~58% is held by noncontrolling unitholders including Blackstone and I Squared Capital.

**Two Segments:**
- **Midstream Logistics** (majority): Gas gathering/processing (4,200+ miles of pipeline, 8 processing complexes), crude oil gathering (280 miles), and produced water disposal (370 miles, 580K bbl/d capacity)
- **Pipeline Transportation**: Equity method investments in PHP (55.5% interest, 2.65 Bcf/d to Gulf Coast) and Shin Oak/Breviloba (33%, 600+ MBbl/d NGL to Mont Belvieu), plus wholly-owned Kinetik NGL and Delaware Link pipelines

The company operates exclusively in the Delaware Basin (western Permian), with nearly 70% of 2026 capex directed toward New Mexico expansion — an early-mover position in Eddy County where production growth potential is substantial.

The forward P/E of ~20x shown in the profile data uses a different EPS basis than the GAAP figures, but even this is structurally inappropriate. Midstream companies are universally valued on EV/EBITDA.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Growth Drivers:**
1. **ECCC Pipeline** (Eddy County to Culberson County, 150 MMcf/d initial capacity) — expected in-service Q2 2026, a near-term catalyst that will capture growing New Mexico production
2. **Kings Landing Processing Complex** — reached full operations Sept 2025 (200 MMcf/d added capacity); acid gas injection project expected year-end 2026
3. **New Mexico Expansion** — 70% of 2026 capex ($315M-$357M) directed to this underdeveloped region with significant acreage dedications from active producers

**Headwinds:**
- Waha gas price weakness causing ~170-220 MMcf/d of production curtailments from upstream customers. The company has no control over producer decisions to curtail.
- The curtailment offset is Gulf Coast gas marketing through PHP ownership — Q1 2026 demonstrated this hedge works (record EBITDA despite curtailments), but the sustainability of marketing gains depends on the Waha-to-Gulf Coast spread remaining wide.
- EBITDA growth guidance of 7% at midpoint is solid but not exceptional for the midstream sector.

**Estimate Revisions (Negative):**
GAAP EPS estimates are falling sharply: -42.5% (90-day) for current quarter, -47.5% for next quarter, -26.1% for FY2026. However, this reflects non-cash items and partnership accounting noise, not fundamental deterioration. The company reaffirmed EBITDA guidance. The disconnect between falling GAAP estimates and stable EBITDA guidance is a structural feature of this entity type.

At 7.5x EV/EBITDA with 7% growth and a 6.5% yield, KNTK is not expensive. The discount to peers is partially justified by: (a) higher leverage (3.9x vs. typical 3.0-3.5x), (b) geographic concentration in a single basin, (c) partnership complexity that suppresses multiple, (d) I Squared overhang. Without these factors, fair value would be 9-10x EV/EBITDA ($58-$65 stock price equivalent). With them, the current 7.5x is fair, offering yield but limited multiple expansion potential while these overhangs persist.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market Position:** 4th largest gas processor in the Delaware Basin/Permian by capacity. Competitors include Targa Resources, Enterprise Products, Energy Transfer, and MPLX.

**Advantages:**
- Integrated multi-service platform (gas + crude + water) creates customer stickiness vs. single-service competitors
- PHP ownership (55.5%) provides direct Gulf Coast pricing access, partially insulating from Waha weakness
- Early-mover position in New Mexico (Eddy County) with dedicated acreage agreements
- $428.9M in contractually committed future revenue

**Challenges:**
- Smaller scale than Targa, Enterprise, or Energy Transfer — less diversification and fewer economies of scale
- TSR underperformance vs. peers (206% vs. 347% peer group) suggests persistent market discount
- Single-basin concentration vs. diversified peers reduces portfolio benefits during regional dislocations
- Producer consolidation (Permian Resources, Diamondback) gives counterparties more bargaining leverage

## Risk Assessment

**1. Waha Gas Price / Curtailment Risk (HIGH):** Currently ~220 MMcf/d of curtailments from weak Waha pricing. While the company demonstrated an ability to offset this in Q1 through Gulf Coast marketing, persistent curtailments could constrain volume growth and delay capex payback periods. ECCC and new takeaway capacity (Matterhorn, Whistler) should eventually alleviate Waha pressure, but timing is uncertain.

**2. I Squared Capital Overhang (MEDIUM-HIGH):** I Squared still holds ~17M units (~10% of fully diluted shares). Their Feb-April 2026 sales totaled $225M+. Based on selling pace ($40-80M/quarter), this overhang could persist for 4-6 more quarters.

**3. Leverage / Interest Rate Exposure (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Each 100bp increase adds ~$18M to annual interest expense. The company hedges $1.0B via swaps, but remaining exposure is material. In a scenario where rates remain elevated or rise further, interest expense consumes a larger share of EBITDA.

**4. Energy Transfer Litigation (MEDIUM):** A bench trial on NGL purchase agreement disputes occurred in March/April 2026. The outcome is pending with uncertain magnitude. Energy Transfer is a major industry counterparty — adverse rulings could impact future operating costs.

**5. Geographic Concentration (MEDIUM):** 100% of assets in the Delaware Basin. Regulatory changes (water disposal restrictions, seismicity rules, emissions regulations) would have undiversifiable impact.

**6. Short Interest (MEDIUM):** At 11.8% of float with 6.2 days to cover, meaningful institutional bearish positioning exists. This likely reflects I Squared overhang concerns, Waha weakness, or fundamental disagreement on leverage sustainability.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-$60, +10-20%):**
- ECCC pipeline comes online Q2 2026, unlocking New Mexico volume growth ahead of schedule
- Waha constraints ease as Matterhorn/Whistler fill and gas-weighted producers reduce curtailments
- 2026 EBITDA hits the high end of guidance ($1.05B), demonstrating leverage to volume growth
- I Squared completes its exit over 2-3 quarters, removing the technical overhang
- Multiple re-rates toward 8.5-9.0x EV/EBITDA as leverage declines toward 3.5x through EBITDA growth
- Energy Transfer litigation resolves favorably or with immaterial impact
- Dividend raised 5% annually, supporting yield floor

**Bear Case ($38-$42, -16-24%):**
- Waha pricing weakness persists or worsens, curtailments expand above 250 MMcf/d
- Producer consolidation reduces drilling activity on Kinetik-dedicated acreage
- Interest rates rise further, adding $25-50M to annual interest burden
- Energy Transfer litigation results in material financial obligation
- I Squared sells remaining units into a weak tape, compressing the stock to 6.5x EV/EBITDA
- Dividend growth slows to 0% or coverage deteriorates, reducing yield premium attractiveness
- Tariff-driven construction cost inflation compresses returns on NM expansion capex

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $42-$44 range (EV/EBITDA compresses to ~6.8x) while 2026 EBITDA guidance is maintained — at that level, dividend yield would exceed 7.5% and valuation would be clearly cheap
2. I Squared Capital completes its exit, removing ~10% of float from potential supply
3. ECCC pipeline in-service and Q3 2026 EBITDA demonstrates meaningful volume uplift beyond $260M/quarter

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. 2026 EBITDA guidance cut below $900M (would imply leverage rising above 4.3x and coverage deteriorating)
2. Energy Transfer litigation results in a judgment exceeding $200M
3. Leverage exceeds 4.5x due to cost overruns or volume shortfall, raising refinancing risk for 2028 maturities

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August) — will provide first full-quarter evidence of ECCC pipeline contribution, updated curtailment levels, and progress on leverage reduction.

## Conclusion

Kinetik Holdings is a competently managed Delaware Basin midstream operator generating record EBITDA, growing volumes, and paying a well-supported 6.5% dividend. At ~7.5x EV/EBITDA, it is fairly valued relative to its risk profile — the discount to midstream peers is earned by higher leverage, single-basin concentration, and the I Squared overhang, but not so severe as to imply distress or fundamental deterioration.

The GAAP EPS volatility and falling estimates are red herrings for this partnership structure. The correct framework — EV/EBITDA on reaffirmed $950M-$1.05B guidance — shows a stock that is neither cheap enough to warrant an aggressive BUY nor overvalued enough to warrant an AVOID. The 6.5% yield provides adequate compensation for holding through the resolution of the overhang and litigation risks. Rating: income hold.
