## Executive Summary

Hanmi Financial is the holding company for Hanmi Bank, an $8.0 billion-asset Korean-American commercial bank headquartered in Los Angeles with 32 branches across nine states. The last two years produced a genuine earnings recovery: net income fell from $101.4M in 2022 to $62.2M in 2024 as deposit costs repriced, then rebounded to $76.1M in 2025 and $46.1M in the first half of 2026 (a $92M annualized pace). The mechanism is unambiguous and entirely liability-side: net interest margin expanded 29 basis points year over year to 3.36% while the loan yield actually *fell* three basis points. Cost of interest-bearing deposits dropped from 3.64% to 3.17%, average borrowings collapsed from $60.1M to $15.3M, and period-end borrowings are now zero after $150M of FHLB advances were retired in Q1 2026. Total interest expense fell 10.7% year over year while interest income rose 2.0%.

That tailwind is close to exhausted. Cost of deposits declined a single basis point sequentially (2.26% → 2.25%). New loan production yields 6.59% against payoffs at 6.39%, a 20 basis point spread on churned volume. Loans held for investment fell 0.2% sequentially, net line utilization on commercial revolvers was *negative* $47.8 million in the quarter, and commercial real estate at 61.4% of loans already exceeds 300% of bank total capital, constraining the ability to grow the largest book without tripping into formal supervisory concentration status. From here, net interest income growth requires balance sheet growth that is not currently happening.

The credit picture is bifurcating in a way the headline metrics conceal. Nonperforming assets improved to 0.12% of assets, the metric management leads with and the metric in the executive compensation scorecard. Total criticized loans rose 144.7% year over year, from $46.6M (0.74% of loans) to $113.9M (1.74%), and 30-89 day delinquencies rose 146.9% sequentially to $32.8M (0.50% of loans). Roughly two-thirds of that increase traces to two identified credits: a $21.2M retail CRE loan that went delinquent in Q2 and a ~$55M hospitality relationship that was upgraded to pass in Q2 2025 and re-downgraded to special mention two quarters later. Sizing the exposure honestly: a 25% severity on both loans costs $0.47 per share and 1.75% of tangible common equity; a harsh 50% severity costs $0.94 per share and 3.5% of TCE, roughly three months of pre-provision net revenue. This is an earnings-risk question, not a solvency question. Whether the 0.74% → 1.74% migration *rate* continues is the open issue, and it is not answerable until Q3 is filed.

At 1.18x tangible book, ~10.1x current-year consensus and a covered 3.5% dividend, the stock is priced roughly fairly for a bank earning 10.5-11.8% on tangible equity with a 61% CRE book. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Hanmi Bank operates 32 full-service branches plus eight non-branch locations (variously described as loan production offices and loan centers across two July 2026 releases, a reclassification rather than a change in scale) in California, Texas, Illinois, Virginia, New Jersey, New York, Colorado, Washington and Georgia. The franchise's origin is Korean-American community banking, and that heritage remains visible in the filings rather than only in marketing. Trade finance and related service charges rose 30.2% year over year in Q2 2026 to $1.902M, the fastest-growing fee line. The FY2025 10-K carries a country-specific risk factor on South Korean tariffs referencing the 15% rate agreed July 31, 2025, a disclosure a generalist regional bank would not need.

The competitive advantage is the classic community-bank kind: relationship deposit gathering within a defined ethnic-commercial niche, producing noninterest-bearing deposits at 30.7% of the funding base (well above regional norms) plus SBA 7(a) origination-and-sale capability and trade finance expertise that generate fee income disproportionate to asset size. These are defensible mainly because larger banks find the segment too small and too relationship-intensive to attack efficiently, not because of structural barriers.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin and revenue.** Q2 2026 net income was $23.505M, diluted EPS $0.79, ROAA 1.20%, ROAE 11.09%, efficiency ratio 54.07% (from 55.74% a year earlier). First-half revenue (net interest income plus fees) was $143.9M against $128.0M, up 12.4%, while noninterest expense rose 8.5% to $77.4M, roughly 3.9 points of positive operating leverage. The two-basis-point sequential NIM decline is explained by FHLB dividend normalization: the FHLB stock yield was 20.56% in Q1 versus 5.36% in Q2 on an unchanged $16.4M balance, a distribution artifact worth about three basis points, not operating deterioration.

**Earnings quality.** Pre-provision net revenue in Q2 was $33.2M ($72.2M revenue less $39.0M expense), roughly $132.7M annualized. Against a $70.5M allowance for credit losses, that is coverage of 1.9x; Hanmi can absorb very large credit surprises out of earnings before touching capital. This is the single most important solvency-relevant figure in the file and the reason the credit migration discussed below should be framed as an earnings issue.

The Q2 result did benefit from an unusually low credit loss expense of $1.186M, versus $2.892M in Q1 and $7.631M in Q2 2025, about 7 basis points annualized on the loan book. Applying a through-cycle provision of 25-30 basis points ($4.1-4.9M quarterly) adds roughly $2.9-3.7M of pre-tax expense per quarter, or $0.29-0.37 per share annually after tax at the 26.5% effective rate. **Normalized EPS is therefore approximately $2.79-2.87 against a $3.16 Q2 annualized run rate**, implying a normalized ROTCE near 10.5% rather than the 11.8% Q2 print. Consensus of $3.18 for the current year and $3.37 for next year implicitly assumes provisions stay near current depressed levels.

Tangible book value per share grew 8.6% year over year while the company paid a 3.5% dividend and repurchased stock. Securities are **entirely available-for-sale** ($896.6M) with no held-to-maturity bucket, so the full mark flows through the disclosed −$46.6M accumulated other comprehensive loss (5.8% of TCE, improved from −$54.5M). There is no hidden HTM mark of the kind that destroyed several banks in 2023. Cash was $331.2M and Fed discount window capacity had been expanded from $27.6M to $424.5M through additional collateral pledging, a deliberate post-2023 preparation that reflects well on treasury management.

Actual revenue was $72.205M, up 10.7%. Anyone running a screen or sentiment model on this name should verify the revenue input directly.

## Growth Analysis

This is the weakest dimension and the primary reason the stock is not a BUY.

**The funding-cost engine has stopped.** Time deposits are $2,654.6M, or 38.2% of total deposits, at an average 3.67% cost versus a 3.17% blended interest-bearing cost, so some repricing benefit remains, but it is a fraction of what has already been harvested. Borrowings are already zero; that lever has been fully pulled.

**Loan growth is absent.** Over the past five quarters net line utilization reads +$12.4M, −$39.5M, −$16.1M, +$4.4M, −$47.8M, negative in three of five and sharply so most recently. Existing commercial borrowers are drawing less. Whether that reflects deleveraging by small and middle-market businesses or borrowers with excess liquidity choosing to self-fund at a 6.6% cost, the effect on Hanmi is the same: headline growth is harder than production figures suggest.

**The two growth engines have offsetting problems.** C&I average balances rose about 30% year over year ($818.5M to $1,065.7M), but the C&I yield fell from 7.45% to 6.57%, largely mechanical since C&I books are floating and reprice with SOFR, so C&I interest income grew only ~15% on 30% balance growth. Equipment finance, the highest-yielding book at 6.80%, is being deliberately run off (down 18.5% year over year, from a ~$595.8M average in 2023 to $362.8M) because it carries the highest loss rate: FY2025 net charge-offs ~1.62% of average balances, Q1 2026 annualized ~2.39%. Running it off is the correct decision and costs the bank yield.

**CRE, the largest book, cannot be the growth vehicle.** My derived estimate, using disclosed capital ratios to back into risk-weighted assets of roughly $6.8B, puts CRE at approximately 470-500% of total capital. That figure is derived, not disclosed; the 10-K states only that the 300% level is exceeded. The practical constraint is real: accelerating CRE growth risks tripping the second leg of the interagency test (50% growth over 36 months) and inviting enhanced supervisory scrutiny. This explains why loans are flat and why growth is being routed through lower-yielding C&I.

**Fee income is volume-constrained.** SBA gain on sale fell to $1.318M in Q2 from $2.160M a year earlier on sale volume of $20.9M versus $32.5M in Q1, even though the premium *improved* to 7.92%. The constraint is origination, not pricing.

**Estimate revisions confirm the picture.** Current-year consensus is essentially flat (+0.5% over 30 days, +0.6% over 90 days) while next-year has been cut 1.6% over 30 days and 1.5% over 90 days. There is no positive revision momentum here, the opposite of the earnings-inflection setup that would justify paying up.

One unverified data point: a Seeking Alpha headline dated 2026-07-22 attributes "low to mid-single-digit 2026 loan growth" and "stable net interest margin" guidance to management. No transcript was accessible and no company-issued numerical guidance was located. Treat it as unconfirmed, though it is directionally consistent with the filings.

Against regional-bank peers of similar size, HAFC screens as the cheapest of the group: TRST 16.1x, SBSI 14.4x, CFFN 13.6x, CPF 12.1x, HAFC 10.8x trailing (EGBN is loss-making at −6.9x and uninformative). Peer median P/E is 14.4x and median yield 3.2%.

I do not think that ~25-30% discount is a mispricing to be arbitraged. It is a reasonable price for the specific risk profile: 61.4% CRE concentration at roughly 470-500% of total capital, criticized loans up 145% year over year, 43.7% uninsured deposits, and a modeled −4.55% net interest income sensitivity to a 100 basis point rate cut. A bank sustaining 12%+ ROTCE can justify 1.4-1.5x tangible book; Hanmi's normalized 10.5% supports something closer to 1.1-1.3x. The stock sits inside that band.

The forward-return arithmetic from here, absent multiple change: 3.5% dividend yield, plus buyback capacity of 1.99 million shares (6.7% of shares outstanding, ~$64M authorized remaining), plus retained-earnings TBV accretion of roughly 4-5% at a 58% total payout. That supports a high-single to low-double-digit total return if the ~11% ROE holds.

Two points on the buyback economics. Execution price has risen from a $23.91 FY2025 average to $30.24 in Q2 2026, or ~1.12x tangible book. At a repurchase earnings yield of ~9.4% the transaction remains EPS-accretive, and the TBV dilution is trivial (160,000 shares × $3.20 premium = $512K, 0.06% of TCE). But repurchasing at 1.12x TBV is materially less valuable than at 0.9x, and the capital-return contribution to per-share value is correspondingly smaller than a year ago.

On the historical record: the five-year TSR index of 297 versus a peer index of 158 (roughly +197% versus +58%) was achieved while net income *fell* 23%, from $98.7M in 2021 to $76.1M in 2025. That outperformance was multiple expansion from a depressed base, and it is not available a second time from 1.18x tangible book. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but the *source* of that appreciation matters for what the next five years can plausibly deliver.

Analyst targets: low $33, mean $34.25, median $34, high $36, from four contributors (one strong buy, three holds, unchanged for three months). The mean implies 7% upside. Two caveats: the coverage is thin, and the most visible bullish action, D.A. Davidson raising its target to $34 from $31.50 around July 21, came from the firm that served as sole placement agent on Hanmi's $55M subordinated note offering nine days later. On a name at ~10x forward with flat-to-falling estimates, I do not treat consensus targets as floors; that principle applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, which this is not.

## Competitive Landscape

Hanmi competes across three tiers: money-center and large regional banks on price and technology; larger Asian-American institutions (East West Bancorp and Cathay General, both several times its size); and smaller Korean-American peers such as Open Bank and PCB Bancorp. Peer-specific metrics could not be independently retrieved, so this framing rests on the 10-K's own characterization and general industry structure.

The relevant trajectory questions are visible in Hanmi's own numbers, and they lean modestly negative:

**Funding mix is drifting the wrong way.** Noninterest-bearing deposits grew $30.0M, or 1.43%, year over year, while total deposits grew 3.4%, so NIB's share of funding *declined* from 31.3% to 30.7%. Management's Q2 commentary highlighted "robust deposit growth" and NIB "increasing 5.2%," which is a factually correct sequential figure that largely recovers a Q4 2025 dip; the year-over-year trend is roughly flat. Growth came disproportionately from the rate-sensitive buckets: time deposits over $250,000 up 5.3%, California state time deposits up 20% to $180M, while money market and savings *fell* 1.5%. Holding 30.7% NIB through intense deposit competition is still a strong absolute result that most regional banks would envy; the criticism is of emphasis, not accuracy.

**Asset mix is shifting toward lower-yielding assets** as equipment finance (6.80% yield) runs off and C&I (6.57% and falling with SOFR) grows.

**Fee franchise is mixed:** trade finance up 30.2%, SBA gain on sale down 39% on volume.

One genuinely encouraging data point on underwriting: the $8.6M office charge-off taken during 2025 came from a *syndicated* office participation rather than a directly originated relationship loan, and the $10.2M collateral-dependent office nonaccrual received a ~$9.7M payment in Q1 2026, essentially resolving it. Total office CRE is ~$503M, or 7.7% of loans, low for a CRE-heavy bank. If relationship underwriting genuinely adds information, this is the pattern one would expect; it is a single observation and should not be generalized.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Criticized-loan migration behind an improving headline.** The central risk.

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| NPAs / assets | 0.33% | 0.16% | **0.12%** |
| 30-89 day delinquencies | $10.9M (0.17%) | $13.3M (0.20%) | **$32.8M (0.50%)** |
| Special mention | $12.7M | $93.7M | $68.2M |
| Classified | $33.9M | $22.7M | **$45.7M** |
| **Total criticized** | **$46.6M (0.74%)** | $116.4M (1.78%) | **$113.9M (1.74%)** |

NPAs are a lagging indicator; risk-grade migration and delinquency are leading indicators, and they moved 145-147% in the wrong direction. NPA/assets is one of three metrics in the executive compensation scorecard (alongside ROAA and efficiency ratio); criticized and classified balances are not. I draw no inference about motive: internal risk grades are management judgments and making them a pay metric would create precisely the wrong incentive, while NPA status is objective and auditable. The practical implication for an investor is that no compensation mechanism will surface criticized-loan drift, so it must be tracked independently.

The counter-case is substantial. Two identified loans account for roughly two-thirds of the increase, so this is not broad portfolio decay. Net charge-offs were 0.08% annualized in Q2. Both loans are real-estate secured. The allowance of $70.5M covers 710% of nonaccruals, 154% of classified loans and 61.9% of all criticized loans. Sizing: 25% severity on the full $76.2M costs $0.47/share and 1.75% of TCE; 50% severity costs $0.94/share and 3.5% of TCE, absorbed by about three months of pre-provision net revenue. On the loans currently identified, this is an earnings event.

**2. Allowance methodology change.** Effective January 1, 2025 Hanmi moved from a probability-of-default/loss-given-default framework to a historical loss-rate approach, applied prospectively as a change in estimate. Within the FY2025 build, the quantitative component contributed ~+$10.2M while the qualitative component released ~−$7.6M, and the allowance ratio fell from 1.12% to 1.07% in a year when charge-offs rose. That combination always warrants examination. The benign explanation is also the expected one: a PD/LGD framework requires large qualitative overlays precisely because it is less anchored to the institution's own loss history, and moving to historical loss rates mechanically shifts reserve into the quantitative bucket. Supporting the benign read, the reserve ratio has been *stable* at 1.07-1.08% across five subsequent quarters and the absolute allowance has grown from $69.8M to $70.5M on flat loans, not the pattern an earnings-management hypothesis would predict. Worth monitoring if criticized loans keep rising while the ratio stays flat.

**3. Rate positioning.** The Q1 2026 10-Q shows net interest income falling 4.55% for an instantaneous −100bp parallel shift and 8.13% for −200bp; measured sensitivity *increased* from the FY2025 10-K's −3.07%. On the Q2 net interest income run rate of $255.4M, −100bp implies roughly −$11.6M pre-tax, −$0.29 per share (~9% of the run rate). The apparent contradiction, models say cuts hurt yet NIM expanded 29bp *as* rates fell, resolves as follows: the realized benefit came from lagged repricing of a $2.65B time-deposit book plus structural moves (retiring $150M of FHLB advances, equipment finance runoff), which are one-time catch-ups rather than a repeatable response function. As the deposit tailwind exhausts, the simulation's directional warning becomes more binding. A higher-for-longer path is itself a stressor for a 61% CRE book at refinancing.

**4. Uninsured deposits.** ~$2.92B, or 43.7% of deposits, with roughly $1.34B in accounts of $5M or more. For a bank serving a concentrated commercial community where information travels quickly, this is the genuine tail risk. Mitigants are real and specific: zero borrowings, $331.2M cash, an all-AFS securities book with no hidden mark, and $424.5M of discount window capacity, over $750M of combined contingent liquidity. Contingent liquidity is only as good as the willingness to use it, and 2023 demonstrated that discount window access carries stigma.

**5. Smaller specific exposures.** Approximately $79M of New York City rent-regulated multifamily (~17% of the multifamily book, 1.2% of loans), with the 10-K adding language referencing the recent NYC mayoral election, small in absolute terms but actively monitored. South Korean tariffs at the 15% rate affect trade finance customers. Hanmi licenses third-party AI/ML models for credit scoring and fraud detection and does not develop models internally, placing model-risk dependency on vendors it does not control; this may also help explain data processing expense rising 19.0% in H1 against a balance sheet up 1.8%. The 10-K discloses that directors on the relevant committee other than one recent appointee lack significant cybersecurity risk management experience.

**6. Liability management.** The $55M subordinated note offering closed July 30 (6.50% fixed for five years, then 3M SOFR + 234bp, 2036 maturity, callable 2031, Tier 2 qualifying) funds redemption of $110M of existing callable notes, a net *reduction* in subordinated debt of roughly 42%, to ~$75.8M pro forma. The pre-tax earnings effect is plausibly within ±$1M annually and immaterial; the coupon on the notes being redeemed is not disclosed in accessible filings, so this remains bounded and directional. Strategically it is competent: locking 6.50% fixed on halved principal at a bank with 10% TCE/TA that no longer needs the Tier 2 cushion, ahead of what an inferred ~3.75-4.25% legacy coupon would have reset to at current short rates. Tier 2 falls by ~$55M, roughly 0.81% of my derived RWA, taking holdco total risk-based capital from 15.29% to ~14.5%, still far above well-capitalized. CET1 and Tier 1 are unaffected. An alternative reading worth holding open: halving Tier 2 also reduces capital available to support future CRE growth, consistent with a management team that does not expect to need it.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is not usable as a signal here. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 117.8% against 31.4% one-year historical volatility, in steep backwardation to 38.3% at six months, would ordinarily indicate the market pricing imminent stress, but the entire chain consists of 21 contracts across four expirations, Hanmi reported on July 21, and the next scheduled catalyst (Q3 earnings) falls well beyond the August 21 expiry that carries the 117.8% reading. A separate snapshot showing 145.5% at an identical unchanged price reinforces the interpretation that these are quote artifacts in a near-nonexistent market rather than informed positioning. The put/call skew of −22.7% is described as call-heavy; I would not draw a directional conclusion from it at this liquidity. Short interest of 4.2% of float with 3.8 days to cover is uncrowded and consistent with no organized bear thesis. Net takeaway: neither confirmation nor challenge to the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $40 (+25%)

A bank generating 11%+ ROTCE with 10.03% TCE/TA, zero borrowings, no held-to-maturity securities mark, a 35% dividend payout and 6.7% of shares authorized for repurchase is not expensively priced at 1.18x tangible book. If the two named problem credits resolve at low severity (both are real-estate secured, and the hospitality relationship was upgraded in 2025 only after a ~$21M principal paydown), criticized loans revert toward 1% of loans, provisions stay near 15-20 basis points, and consensus EPS of $3.37 for next year is achievable. Loan growth resuming to mid-single digits with NIM stable at 3.35%+ would restore revenue momentum, and the remaining time-deposit repricing (average 3.67% versus 3.17% blended) provides a residual funding tailwind. On sustained 12% ROTCE, 1.4x tangible book of roughly $28.20 by mid-2027 supports $39-40.

Separately, an $8 billion Korean-American franchise with 32 Los Angeles-area branches and a 30.7% noninterest-bearing deposit base is a scarce asset in a consolidating regional-bank sector; a takeout at 1.6-1.8x tangible book would be $43-49. The forward-looking-statements language contemplating "strategic transactions" is boilerplate carried verbatim from 2025 and is not evidence of any process; I assign no probability to it and include it only as unpriced optionality.

### Bear Case — $24 (−25%)

The 0.74% → 1.74% criticized migration continues for several more quarters rather than stabilizing. The $32.8M of 30-89 day delinquencies moves into nonaccrual in Q3, provisions normalize to 40-50 basis points ($6.5-8.2M quarterly versus $1.2M in Q2), and EPS falls toward $2.50. The growth problem persists: net line utilization stays negative, CRE cannot grow because of the concentration test, equipment finance runoff continues removing the highest-yielding asset, and the deposit-cost tailwind is fully spent, so pre-provision revenue flattens while provisions rise. At $2.50 of earnings and a 9.5x multiple appropriate to a CRE-concentrated bank with deteriorating credit, the stock is $23.75. That is roughly 0.88x tangible book, which is where the market prices regional banks with unresolved CRE questions. An additional path to the same outcome: a sharp back-up in long rates compresses the all-AFS securities mark and visibly reduces TCE/TA, since there is no held-to-maturity smoothing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Reassess at Q3 2026 earnings, expected late October 2026.**

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 total criticized loans decline below $90M (from $113.9M) *or* the $21.2M retail CRE loan cures and returns to accrual status, confirming the migration was idiosyncratic rather than the leading edge.
2. Two consecutive quarters of loans held for investment growing at an annualized 4%+ with NIM held at 3.35% or better and net line utilization turning positive, evidence that revenue growth can resume without the funding tailwind.
3. Price toward $27-28, approximately 1.0x tangible book and ~9x normalized earnings, which would restore a genuine margin of safety against the credit uncertainty.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Nonperforming loans exceeding 0.40% of loans in Q3 as the $32.8M delinquency bucket migrates to nonaccrual.
2. Credit loss provision above 40 basis points annualized for two consecutive quarters, which would cut EPS toward $2.50 and invalidate consensus.
3. Criticized loans exceeding 2.5% of loans while the allowance ratio remains held at 1.07-1.08%, the combination that would make the FY2025 methodology change a live concern rather than a monitoring item.
4. Noninterest-bearing deposit share falling below 29% of total deposits, indicating the core funding franchise is eroding rather than holding flat.

**What to check first when Q3 is filed:** whether the $32.8M of 30-89 day delinquencies migrated to nonaccrual, the criticized-loan table, and the provision. No Q2 earnings call transcript was accessible from any source, meaning management's own characterization of the $21.2M and ~$55M credits (expected severities, collateral coverage, workout status) is unknown. That is the most consequential information gap in this analysis, and it is precisely the evidence that would separate "two idiosyncratic well-collateralized workouts" from "the leading edge of CRE deterioration."

**Income case for holding:** $1.12 annualized dividend at a 35% payout on run-rate earnings, 3.50% yield, funded by a bank with 10.03% TCE/TA, zero borrowings and pre-provision net revenue at 1.9x its entire loan-loss allowance. Even under the bear-case $2.50 EPS scenario, payout rises only to 45%; the dividend is not at risk from the credit outcomes contemplated here.

## Conclusion

Hanmi has done the right things operationally: retired all wholesale borrowings, halved subordinated debt at a fixed 6.50%, expanded contingent liquidity from $27.6M to $424.5M, kept the securities book entirely available-for-sale, run off its highest-loss asset class, and built tangible book value per share 8.6% year over year while returning 58% of earnings to shareholders. Executive compensation is restrained ($2.56M CEO total, a 3.3x ratio to the NEO average) and the mark-to-market mechanism worked in both directions, falling to $1.38M in 2023 when the stock declined. The balance sheet is the best part of the story and is what keeps a 61% CRE concentration and 43.7% uninsured deposits from being existential.

What is missing is a reason to pay up. The earnings recovery was funding-cost driven and that source is nearly exhausted: one basis point of sequential deposit-cost improvement, a 20 basis point spread between new production and payoffs, loans down 0.2%, revolver utilization down $47.8M, and the largest loan book constrained by a concentration test. Estimates are flat to falling. There were no open-market insider purchases in five months of Form 4 filings near multi-year highs, and two small open-market sales, including the Chief Credit Officer three months after special mention loans tripled; individually these sales are minor and plausibly routine diversification, but in the absence of any offsetting buying and with estimates drifting lower, they add nothing to the bull case. The credit question is genuinely open: a 145% year-over-year increase in criticized loans sitting behind a 0.12% NPA headline is the kind of divergence that resolves one way or the other within two quarters.

At 1.18x tangible book, ~10.1x current-year consensus and 11.3x normalized earnings against a 10.5% normalized return on tangible equity, the stock is priced about right. The 3.5% dividend is well covered, the capital cushion is real, and the forward total return absent multiple change is high-single to low-double-digit. That is a reasonable holding and an insufficient case for new capital ahead of the Q3 credit print. **HOLD**, with a base case of $34 (1.23x projected tangible book, ~11x normalized EPS, +6% price plus 3.5% yield), a bull case of $40 and a bear case of $24.
