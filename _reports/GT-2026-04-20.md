## Executive Summary

Goodyear is a highly leveraged tire manufacturer ($5.8B debt, D/E 4.58x) in the middle of a multi-year transformation that has so far failed to arrest four consecutive years of revenue decline. The company divested approximately $2.2B in assets in 2025 (OTR tires, Dunlop brand, polymer chemicals) and delivered ~$1.5B in annualized cost savings, yet segment operating income still declined 18.8% to $1.057B. The $1.721B net loss was dominated by non-cash charges ($1.4B deferred tax asset valuation allowance, $674M goodwill impairment), but these impairments reflect management's own assessment that future U.S. profitability is unlikely to support book values.

The tariff narrative is the primary bull case: Goodyear sources only ~12% of U.S. sales from tariff-exposed countries versus ~55% for the industry, creating a meaningful cost advantage. However, the $300M annualized tariff cost to Goodyear itself roughly offsets the $300M in incremental Goodyear Forward savings for 2026. Meanwhile, the consumer spending environment is weakening (record-low Michigan sentiment at 47.6, housing activity collapsing), replacement tire volumes fell 6.3% in 2025, and analysts have slashed full-year 2026 EPS estimates by 52.5% over the past 90 days. With $1.17B in 2026 debt maturities, a USW labor contract expiring in July 2026, and interest expense consuming roughly 40% of segment operating income, the margin of safety is inadequate for the risk profile.

## Company Overview

Goodyear operates through three segments: Americas (60% of revenue), EMEA (30%), and Asia Pacific (10%), with 49 manufacturing facilities across 19 countries. The company shipped 158.7 million tire units in 2025, ranking third globally behind Michelin and Bridgestone. The "Goodyear Forward" transformation plan completed in 2025 focused on portfolio optimization (divestitures), margin enhancement ($1.5B savings), and deleveraging ($1.76B in debt reduction in 2025). The company is repositioning toward a premium brand strategy with Cooper as the primary second-tier brand, having sold the Dunlop brand to Sumitomo.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

Revenue declined for the fourth consecutive year: $20.8B (2022) → $20.1B (2023) → $18.9B (2024) → $18.3B (2025), a cumulative 12% decline. Tire unit shipments fell 4.7% in 2025 with replacement volumes down 6.3%. International replacement was particularly weak at -7.4%.

Segment operating income of $1.057B represented a 5.8% margin, down from 6.9% ($1.302B) in 2024. The Americas segment saw SOI decline 21.2% to $735M, overwhelmed by $305M in higher conversion costs, $277M in raw material costs, $131M in tariff/transportation costs, and $92M in lost insurance proceeds. EMEA improved 23.9% to $114M, though at a thin 2.1% margin.

The headline $1.721B net loss includes $1.4B in deferred tax asset valuation allowance (non-cash), $674M goodwill impairment (non-cash), $201M pension settlements, and $194M rationalization charges, partially offset by $816M in divestiture gains. The pretax loss was $133M. EPS was -$5.99.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt of $5.8B against stockholders' equity of $3.2B yields a debt-to-equity ratio of 4.58x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.36 places the company firmly in the distress zone (<1.81). Cash was $0.8B, with $4.4B in unused credit facility capacity (up from $3.6B) after the revolver was extended to May 2030.

Outstanding notes total $4.4B across maturities from 2027-2033, with $1.17B due in 2026. Interest expense is guided at $400-425M for 2026. Off-balance-sheet exposure includes $551M in supplier financing programs and $892M in accounts receivable factoring. A $103M subordinated loan to the TireHub distribution JV adds additional credit risk.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $796M in 2025, but this includes $486M of deferred revenue from divestiture-related transition agreements. Adjusted for this, underlying operating cash flow was approximately $310M. Capital expenditures were $826M (down from $1.188B) and are expected to stay at ~$825M in 2026. Free cash flow was effectively negative before divestiture windfalls. Rationalization payments of ~$225M and working capital improvement of ~$100M are expected in 2026.

### Earnings Track Record

Goodyear missed estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters. The Q4 2025 miss (-20.3%) followed a strong Q3 beat (+69.4%), suggesting the improvement was not durable. The Q2 2025 miss (-345.7%) reflects severe operational difficulties during the transformation.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained by multiple factors:

**Volume trajectory is negative.** Replacement tire volumes declined 6.3% in 2025, driven by lower-tier import competition and weakening consumer spending. Management's own goodwill impairment DCF analysis and the DTA valuation allowance both embed expectations of weak U.S. profitability.

**Estimate revisions are sharply negative.** Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimates have fallen 52.5% over 90 days (from $1.10 to $0.52). Current-quarter estimates collapsed from +$0.15 to -$0.41 over the same period. Next-year estimates are down 26.8%. This is broad-based deterioration, not single-quarter noise.

**The transformation offsets are largely neutral for 2026.** Incremental savings of ~$300M and ~$300M raw material benefit are approximately offset by ~$300M tariff cost and ~$185M divestiture drag. Volume and pricing will determine whether margins actually improve.

**Potential positives:** EV tire market opportunity (~$1.2B U.S. market, targeting 10% share by 2026). Lawton, Oklahoma plant conversion from standard to premium production. Cooper brand repositioning in EMEA to fill the Dunlop gap. These are multi-year initiatives against a current backdrop of declining volumes and consumer weakness.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.06/share, Goodyear has a market cap of approximately $2.0B. The forward P/E based on current-year consensus of $0.52 is approximately 13.6x, but this estimate has been falling rapidly and may not be reliable. The negative trailing P/E (-1.18x) reflects the large GAAP loss. On an EV/EBITDA basis, with $5.8B debt and ~$0.8B cash, the enterprise value is approximately $7.0B against segment operating income of $1.057B, implying ~6.6x SOI.

The peer group is heterogeneous (Six Flags, Penn Entertainment, National Vision), making direct comparison less meaningful. Among actual tire/auto parts peers, the stock trades at a significant discount to Michelin and Bridgestone, which is warranted given Goodyear's leverage, negative earnings trajectory, and lower profitability.

The analyst consensus target is $9.19 (median $9.00), with a range of $7.30-$11.00. The distribution of analyst recommendations (1 Strong Buy, 3 Buy, 5 Hold, 1 Sell) is relatively bearish for sell-side coverage. The low target of $7.30 is only 3.4% above the current price, providing almost no margin for error.

The stock trades at $7.06 versus a 52-week high of $12.03 (41% discount) and near the 52-week low of $6.14 (15% above). The 27.6% decline over the past year reflects the market repricing the turnaround thesis lower.

## Competitive Landscape

Goodyear ranks third globally behind Michelin and Bridgestone but operates with the least competitive cost structure among the three. Management explicitly acknowledges its "manufacturing footprint is less cost-competitive than that of our principal competitors." Plant closures and modernization are underway but create temporary disruption.

The tariff dynamic is the most meaningful competitive development. With only ~12% of U.S. sales from tariff-exposed countries versus ~55% industry average, Goodyear has a structural 10.5% cost advantage over import-dependent competitors under current tariff policy. CEO Stewart described this as a "really favorable, strong position." This advantage could drive market share gains if tariffs persist.

However, lower-tier import competition remains the structural challenge. The Cooper Tire intangibles impairment ($125M in 2024) was specifically driven by lower-tier import pressure. While tariffs may temporarily shield against this, the long-term trend of commoditizing tire markets persists.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Leverage and refinancing risk.** Altman Z of 1.36 (distress zone) with $1.17B in 2026 maturities. Any credit market tightening or operating deterioration could create a liquidity crisis. Credit cascade dynamics currently building in private credit and high-yield markets compound this risk.

2. **Consumer spending deterioration.** Record-low consumer sentiment (47.6), rising inflation (CPI heading above 4%), and higher energy costs directly suppress tire replacement demand. Tires are a semi-discretionary purchase that consumers defer in downturns.

3. **USW labor contract expiring July 2026.** Covers ~4,300 U.S. associates plus ~19,000 international associates. A strike during the critical transformation period could be devastating.

4. **Tariff reversal risk.** The near-term bull case depends on tariff policy persistence. A trade deal or policy reversal eliminates the competitive advantage immediately.

5. **Divestiture execution.** Ongoing transition agreements with Yokohama, Sumitomo, and the chemicals buyer span 5-15 years and create operational complexity. The deferred revenue ($486M recognized in 2025 OCF) is non-recurring.

6. **Off-balance-sheet exposure.** $551M supplier financing + $892M AR factoring + $103M TireHub loan = $1.55B in semi-hidden obligations that could unwind under credit stress.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 66.4% is 42% above the 46.7% historical volatility, pricing in event risk. The term structure is in steep backwardation (66.4% near-term → 52.0% at 6 months), consistent with near-term stress expectations. However, the put/call OI ratio of 0.66 is call-heavy, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.11 suggests minimal current put demand despite elevated IV. This divergence between rich IV and call-heavy positioning suggests speculative call buying (turnaround bet) rather than institutional protection. The 14% implied move over the nearest expiry is large relative to fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Tariffs persist and Goodyear captures significant market share from import-dependent competitors. The 10.5% cost advantage drives volume recovery and margin expansion. Goodyear Forward savings plus raw material tailwinds boost SOI above $1.2B. Debt reduction continues, credit quality improves, and the stock re-rates to $10-11 (analyst high target). EV tire market penetration adds a growth vector. David Tepper's Appaloosa Management position provides institutional validation.

### Bear Case
Consumer spending deterioration accelerates tire replacement deferrals. Volumes decline another 5-7% in 2026. Tariff costs and divestiture drag overwhelm savings. EPS comes in below the already-reduced $0.52 consensus. The 2026 debt maturities are refinanced at higher rates, pushing interest expense above $450M. The USW contract negotiation in July proves contentious. Credit market stress makes refinancing more expensive or restricted. The stock tests or breaks through the $6.14 52-week low, heading toward $4-5 on a potential $2B market cap valued at roughly 4x EV/SOI.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two identifiable catalysts with timelines: (1) Current quarter EPS estimate of -$0.41 (likely Q1 2026 reporting) — a further miss below this already-negative estimate would confirm the downward trajectory; (2) USW labor contract expiration in July 2026 — any indication of contentious negotiations or work stoppage would pressure the stock materially. The 90-day estimate revision trend (-373.1% for current quarter, -52.5% for current year) confirms deteriorating fundamentals ahead.

**Timing window:** Current quarter earnings report (likely May 2026) and USW contract deadline (July 2026) create a 2-4 month window of potential catalysts. Carrying costs are manageable over this period.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 13.8% of float with 3.7 days to cover. This is elevated but below the 20% threshold where squeeze risk becomes dominant. Institutional ownership provides sufficient lending supply. The Appaloosa/Tepper position creates some squeeze risk if the turnaround thesis gains traction, but position sizing can account for this.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV at 66.4% (91st percentile) means put protection is expensive. The call-heavy OI (P/C ratio 0.66) and low put volume ratio (0.11) suggest limited institutional downside positioning — the trade is not crowded via options. Steep backwardation in the vol term structure confirms near-term stress expectations.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $4.50-5.00 (29-36% downside from $7.06). Upside risk to $9.00-9.50 on tariff narrative re-acceleration (27-35% upside). Risk/reward is approximately 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold for active shorting.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** Risk/reward is approximately 1:1 due to tariff optionality and Tepper's institutional backing. The stock is already 41% below its 52-week high, limiting remaining downside relative to squeeze risk. The correct expression is to avoid owning rather than actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** (1) D/E declines below 3.0x through combination of debt paydown and earnings improvement; (2) Two consecutive quarters of positive earnings surprises and replacement tire volume growth; (3) Free cash flow turns sustainably positive (excluding divestiture-related deferred revenue).

**Timeline:** Headwinds are expected to persist through at least Q3 2026. The USW contract resolution (July 2026), CPI trajectory through summer 2026, and the direction of consumer spending will determine whether the fundamental picture improves. Earliest reassessment: post-Q2 2026 earnings (August 2026).

## Conclusion

Goodyear presents a classic value trap profile: low absolute price, an identifiable turnaround narrative (tariffs, cost savings), and an institutional champion (Tepper). Against this, the data shows four years of revenue decline, 3 earnings misses in 4 quarters, sharply falling estimates, extreme leverage (Altman Z 1.36), negative free cash flow, management's own impairments signaling weak U.S. profitability expectations, a deteriorating consumer spending environment, and upcoming labor contract risk. The tariff advantage is real but the company bears $300M in tariff costs itself, and tariff policy can reverse. At $7.06, the stock is priced for the turnaround to work; the balance of evidence suggests it has not yet begun.
