## Executive Summary

Icahn Enterprises is a Delaware master limited partnership whose economics are those of a leveraged holding company, not the "Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing" classification the data feed assigns. Energy contributed $7,188M of $9,658M FY2025 revenue (74.4%) only because IEP consolidates roughly 70% of CVR Energy. The security's value drivers are a proprietary hedge fund, five senior note tranches totaling $4.9B face, a distribution paid mostly in scrip, and a controlling unitholder owning approximately 86% of the units.

Three numbers frame the position. First, equity attributable to IEP fell from $2,466M (12/31/2024) to $1,942M (12/31/2025) to $1,147M (3/31/2026) — a 53.5% decline in five quarters. Second, in Q1 2026 IEP declared $325M of distributions while losing $459M attributable; the reconciliation ($1,942M − $459M − $325M − ~$11M other ≈ $1,147M) confirms the distribution is being paid out of capital. Third, the non-Investment operating segments generated $460M of pre-working-capital operating cash and only $52M of net income attributable to IEP in FY2025, against $504M of consolidated interest expense and holding-company senior-note interest guided at $337M for 2026. The operating businesses do not earn the holding company's interest bill.

Against that, the July 21, 2026 agreement to sell Pep Boys to a subsidiary of Mavis Tire Express Services for approximately $700M cash is the most constructive development in several years. It converts a segment that consumed roughly $151M of cash in FY2025 and $96M in Q1 2026 alone into liquidity equal to about 2.1x the 2026 holding-company interest bill, while IEP retains the previously carved-out real estate plus the AAMCO and Precision Tune franchising businesses. That justifies a marginal uptick in our financial-health assessment from the prior review. It does not change the recommendation. At $7.63 the units trade at roughly 4.2-4.5x GAAP book value per unit and a 51-59% premium to the company's own reported indicative NAV, with the per-unit NAV denominator compounding at approximately 5.5% per quarter through the scrip distribution. This is the fourth consecutive AVOID; the score moves from 2.1 to 2.2 on the Mavis transaction, and the recommendation is unchanged because the premium-to-NAV, negative holding-company cash flow, and 2027 refinancing arithmetic are all intact.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has declined four consecutive years.** $14,101M (2022) → $10,847M (2023) → $10,020M (2024) → $9,658M (2025), a 31.5% three-year contraction. The 2022-23 step-down reflects the Auto Plus deconsolidation and lower refined-product prices; 2024-25 declines are Energy price/volume and store closures. Total assets fell 49.1% over the same three years, from $27,914M to $14,215M. Cash and equivalents fell from $2,603M to $1,450M during 2025.

**Losses have been continuous.** Net loss attributable to IEP: $(183)M, $(684)M, $(445)M, $(299)M for 2022-2025. The FY2025 narrowing is not operating improvement. Real Estate segment income of $256M compares to $223M of gain on the August 2025 property sale plus $47M on the June country club sale — $270M of disposal gains, implying ongoing Real Estate operations were breakeven-to-negative. Energy gross profit of $302M is attributed partly to "favorable RFS impacts" following an August 2025 small-refinery-exemption decision that cut Wynnewood's 2020-2024 obligations by more than 424 million RINs, roughly $488M. We cannot determine how much of that flowed through FY2025 income versus prior-period accrual release, so we do not quantify it. Underlying segment performance deteriorated across the board: Automotive $(16)M → $(130)M, Food Packaging $(6)M → $(66)M, Home Fashion $(8)M → $(14)M, Pharma $+9M → $(4)M.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 consolidated operating cash flow was $(313)M against $341M capex — FCF of $(654)M. The consolidated line is contaminated by Investment-segment trading flows, so the segment decomposition matters more:

- Holding Company operating cash flow: $(310)M in 2025, $(230)M in 2024, $(221)M in 2023. Structural: cash interest of $(335)M against $57M of interest and dividend income (down from $98M) and $(30)M of operating costs.
- Other operating segments: +$114M in 2025, down from $504M and $1,168M. Before working capital these segments generated $460M; a $346M working-capital drag, mostly Energy payables around the Coffeyville turnaround, cut it to $114M.

**Debt and covenants.** Holding-company notes totaled $4,905M face at 12/31/2025, $4,664M net of treasury and discounts: $250M 6.250% 2026 (fully redeemed 2/26/2026), $1,455M 5.250% 2027, $750M 4.375% 2029, $700M 9.750% 2029, $1,000M 10.000% 2029, $750M 9.000% 2030. Recomputing the coupon load net of treasury holdings gives ~$333M, consistent with the company's $337M 2026 guidance. Guided interest steps down to $296M (2027), $260M (2028), $212M (2029), $32M (2030).

Two covenant facts drive the analysis. As of both 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026, "based on covenants in the indentures governing our senior notes, we are not permitted to incur additional indebtedness; however, we are permitted to issue new notes in connection with debt refinancings of existing notes." Since the November 2024 secured offering all IEP notes are secured and therefore excluded from the indenture ratio test. IEP can refinance but cannot lever up, and the ratio test that historically gated distributions has been largely defused by securing everything.

**The 2027 wall is arithmetic, not opinion.** $1,455M of 5.250% notes mature in 2027. IEP's most recent new-money pricing was the August 2025 add-on of $500M to the 10.000% secured notes due 2029. Refinancing the 2027s at 9.75% adds approximately $65M of annual interest ($1,455M × 4.50%); at 10.0%, $69M. Against $52M of net income attributable from the operating segments in FY2025, a single refinancing event more than consumes the entire earnings contribution of every operating business IEP owns. [unverified] The macro backdrop — the level of long-term Treasury yields, the Fed's recent meeting cadence and dissents, and high-yield spread levels — is not documented in the provided sources and has been removed.

**Segment liquidity thinned during 2025.** Non-Investment segment cash fell from $1,197M to $595M: Energy $987M → $511M, Automotive $133M → $14M, Pharma $42M → $26M. Segment third-party debt was $1,952M. Revolver availability across all segments was $302M, of which $296M sat at CVR — subsequently expanded to $550M (extendable to $700M) with maturity pushed from June 2027 to February 2031 in the February 2026 refinancing, which also swapped $600M of 8.50% 2029 notes and $217M of 5.75% 2028 notes for $600M at 7.50% due 2031 and $400M at 7.875% due 2034, at a $28M extinguishment loss. That is genuine subsidiary de-risking at a higher coupon.

## The Distribution

This is what most holders buy and it is the most commonly mis-modeled item in the security.

FY2025 declarations aggregated $2.00 per unit, each holder electing cash or additional units. Aggregate cash to electing holders was $282M, of which $175M went to Mr. Icahn and affiliates. Non-electing holders receive units **by default**.

The April 2026 settlement of the $325M Q1 declaration is the cleanest evidence: **34,841,101 units plus $51 million of cash** — 15.7% cash, 84.3% scrip. Unit count reconciles exactly: 637,209,452 at 3/31/2026 + 34,841,101 = 672,050,553 as of May 6, 2026. Icahn's cash election within that was $25M, down from ~$175M for full-year 2025.

Derived implications:
- Indicated yield: $2.00 / $7.63 = **26.2%**. This is not a 26% cash yield.
- The April issuance equals **+5.47%** of the 637.2M pre-existing units; a cash-electing holder's proportionate ownership fell **5.18%** (34.841/672.051). The larger figure is issuance against the old base; the smaller is ownership transferred.
- **Sensitivity, not forecast:** if the Q1 2026 election mix persisted four quarters, unit count would grow ~23.7% annually and the cash cost would be ~$204M/year. The mix is chosen quarterly and has varied materially — FY2025 actual cash was $282M, well above 4 × $51M.

Issuing units does not create NAV. The cash portion reduces it. A unit-electing holder maintains proportionate ownership of an entity whose attributable equity fell 53.5% in five quarters; a cash-electing holder monetizes at a large premium to management's own NAV estimate. Holding-company financing activities in FY2025 show $(288)M of partnership distributions against $85M of ATM equity issuance, and holding-company operating cash flow of $(310)M — the cash portion is funded by asset monetization and fund redemptions.

The ATM funding channel is deteriorating in price terms. IEP sold 10,067,399 units for $84M gross in 2025 (~$8.34/unit) versus 5,806,986 units for $102M in 2024 (~$17.57/unit) — nearly twice the units for less money. The November 2022 agreement is exhausted; $363M of capacity remains under the August 2024 agreement, with the 10-K warning there is no assurance capital will be available on acceptable terms.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth vector in the portfolio as currently constituted.

Revenue has fallen four straight years and the Pep Boys sale removes another $1.4B of consolidated revenue. Viskase's FY2025 revenue fell 10% to $362M, mostly volume ($37M) with $9M of price erosion, and gross margin compressed from 17% to 10%; management states the benefits of North American facility consolidation will not arrive "until later in 2026, if at all." Pharma lost patent exclusivity — two generics launched in the U.S. during 2025 per settlement agreements — with gross margin falling 48% to 39%; management "anticipate[s]" ex-U.S. launches will "eventually offset the lost revenue," an unquantified forward statement. Real Estate disposals permanently remove revenue: the country club generated ~$33M of FY2024 other revenues and management expects a corresponding annualized reduction.

The Investment segment has produced −16.9% (2023), −3.5% (2024), +0.4% (2025) and −8.2% in Q1 2026. Dollar attribution over three years: long positions $(131)M, short positions $(2,176)M, other +$657M, total $(1,650)M. Positioning at 12/31/2025 was net short 13% notional (long 101%, short 114%). A persistently net-short, index-hedged book during a strong equity tape is structurally penalized; broad market hedges alone lost $227M in 2025 and $261M in 2024, and those are not described as CVR hedges.

The one credible growth option is refining consolidation. The 10-K discloses IEP is "considering, with CVR Energy, potential strategic transactions... including the acquisition of material amounts of refining assets," alongside "strategic options involving CVR Partners," with the filing's own caveat that there is no assurance anything materializes. The constraint is the covenant prohibiting new holding-company debt: any large acquisition must be financed at CVR — which now has $550-700M of ABL capacity and maturities out to 2031 — or with equity issued below stated NAV. Optionality exists; it is not a forecastable earnings stream, and we do not capitalize it.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer table supplied (PARR 6.2x, CVI −72.7x, PBF 10.8x, WKC −3.0x, REX 15.3x; median 10.8x) is not usable. IEP has negative trailing and forward EPS (−$0.52 trailing), and its economics are those of a holding company with a consolidated hedge fund and large public minorities, not a standalone refiner. The relevant framework is price against net asset value.

**GAAP book value.** Attributable equity of $1,147M at 3/31/2026 divided by the 637.209M units outstanding on that date is **$1.80/unit**; against the 672.05M units outstanding as of May 6, 2026 it is **$1.71/unit**. At $7.63 the units trade at **4.24x to 4.46x** GAAP book.

**Indicative NAV.** A secondary source reports IEP's May 6, 2026 release disclosed indicative NAV of approximately $3.4B. We could not verify this against the primary release or an 8-K, and treat it as unconfirmed. Taken at face value: $3.4B / 672.05M units = **$5.06/unit**, implying a **50.8% premium** at $7.63. If the post-June unit count is nearer 709M (estimated from Icahn's 36.46M-unit June receipt scaled by the April mix), per-unit INAV is ~$4.80 and the premium is ~59%. INAV is a management-estimated non-GAAP measure that marks operating subsidiaries roughly $2.25B above GAAP carrying value; the premium is therefore measured against the more generous of the two anchors.

**Market capitalization.** 672,050,553 units × $7.63 = **$5,128M**. Every distribution mechanically raises the denominator against which any NAV is divided.

**What the price implies.** For $7.63 to be fair value on a $3.4B NAV, the buyer is paying roughly $1.7B — about 33% of market capitalization — for the Icahn deal-making option and the disclosed refining-M&A ambition, in a structure where the holding company cannot incur new debt, its operating subsidiaries earn one-tenth of its interest bill, and the per-unit NAV denominator compounds ~5.5% per quarter. Closed-end and holding-company structures with negative internal cash flow, a scrip distribution and an unquantified option value historically trade at discounts to NAV, not premiums above 50%.

The valuation score is low not because the multiple is compressed (it is not — there is no positive earnings multiple to compress) but because the only supportable anchors sit 34-59% below the current price and the denominator is growing. This is the opposite of a value-trap-versus-bargain question; there is no cheapness to be trapped by.

## Competitive Landscape

**Refining (CVR Energy).** Energy gross profit: $1,228M (2023) → $160M (2024) → $302M (2025); gross margin 13% → 2% → 4%. CVR is a small inland mid-continent refiner, structurally more exposed to RIN compliance costs than integrated peers, which is exactly why the small-refinery-exemption regime dominates its earnings. The 10-K notes RIN cost depends on the outcome of numerous lawsuits — a material earnings input under litigation. The renewable diesel unit reverted to hydrocarbon service in December 2025 "considering unfavorable economics," removing a loss-making activity after accelerated depreciation. CVR suspended its dividend in October 2024 and paid none in 2025; IEP received $100M of CVR Energy dividends in 2024 and **$0** in 2025, with only a $3M CVR Partners distribution. CVR Partners paid $77M to non-controlling interests versus $3M to IEP — the cash is largely trapped at the public minority level.

**Automotive services.** Management cites reduced consumer spending on repairs and $33M of higher service labor costs — weak ticket against wage inflation. SG&A rose $41M (10%) while revenue fell 7.6%. Selling to Mavis, a scaled tire-and-service consolidator, is a rational outcome: density and purchasing power accrue to national platforms, which ~800 locations under a diversified holding company could not match.

**Food packaging.** Casings is a consolidated global oligopoly. Viskase's 700bp gross-margin collapse on a 10% revenue decline is what a subscale participant looks like when volumes fall and fixed costs stay.

**Activist investing.** External capital is withdrawing; IEP's share of fund AUM rose from 64% to 75% not because IEP added capital but because others left. Sell-side coverage is essentially absent (one recommendation in the dataset, no published targets), which means the INAV calculation faces little independent scrutiny.

**Post-Pep-Boys concentration worsens.** Removing $1.4B of Automotive revenue raises Energy's share of the remainder. What remains is a small inland refiner plus a distressed casings maker, a shrinking real estate portfolio with $213M of seller-financing exposure, a small home textiles business, a franchising business and a pharma unit facing generic entry. Mix change from subtraction is not diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The equity account is on a trajectory toward exhaustion.** $2,466M → $1,942M → $1,147M across five quarters. Q2 2026 results are not yet public. If Q2 produces even half of Q1's $(459)M loss and another $325M is declared, attributable equity approaches $600-700M against a $5.1B market capitalization.

**2. IEP is short its own largest operating asset, and the accounting is asymmetric.** Of $452M of Q1 2026 Investment Funds short-position losses, $425M were energy-sector losses "related to losses on refining hedges, which represent certain equity and commodity derivative positions intended to serve as economic hedges against the value of CVR Energy," with commodity contracts contributing $(239)M. Because the derivatives are marked through income while consolidated CVR carrying value is not written up as its market value rises, a refining rally produces large visible losses with no offsetting visible gain. CVR separately reported a Q1 2026 gross loss of $115M including $195M of unfavorable derivatives from its own crack hedges. IEP is short refining margins in two places. The practical consequence for the imminent Q2 print is that reported results may look materially worse than the underlying economics if refining margins were strong through the quarter. The counter-reading, which we cannot exclude, is that the funds are simply positioned wrong-way in energy with flattering framing — no notional amounts are disclosed alongside the hedged position's fair value, so hedge effectiveness is unverifiable.

**3. Holding-company liquidity requires continuous monetization.** Holding-company cash fell $839M → $624M during Q1 2026. The quarter reconciles as operating $(54)M + investing $+108M + financing $(271)M; the investing inflow was almost entirely the $240M fund redemption. Absent it, the quarter was roughly $(457)M. An illustrative 2026 requirement — $337M interest + ~$204M cash distributions at the Q1 mix + ~$36M operating costs ≈ $577M, before subsidiary funding (Q1 alone sent $126M to Automotive) — sits above starting cash. The $700M Pep Boys proceeds and the redeemable ~$2.2B fund stake mean this is not a near-term solvency event; it establishes that the structure must sell assets to stand still.

**4. Viskase came within a waiver of default.** The 10-K states plainly that IEP made $30M of private placements in 2025 and $15M in January 2026 and that "if we had not provided additional capital the segment would not have met its debt obligations." On January 23, 2026 Viskase obtained a waiver of any events of default for the period ending December 31, 2025. Segment debt is $142M with $5M of revolver availability. The March 26, 2026 Enzon reverse merger brought $40M of cash into the subsidiary and took IEP's ownership to ~94%.

**5. RFS/RIN exposure swings hundreds of millions in both directions.** The RFS liability rose from $72M at 12/31/2025 to $204M at 3/31/2026 — nearly tripling in one quarter on higher RIN prices — while the EPA has not acted on Wynnewood's July 2025 SRE petition despite a 90-day statutory obligation.

**6. Concentration and pledge risk.** Mr. Icahn pledged $568M of fund interests at 12/31/2025, reduced to $371M at 3/31/2026. This is the same category of exposure that drove the 2023 dislocation. His redemptions ($508M in 2025, $175M in Q1 2026) are at least as plausibly explained by servicing pledged borrowings or funding cash elections as by any view on fund prospects; we draw no inference about returns.

**7. Quality-of-earnings items.** The $223M August 2025 real estate gain — the largest single positive item in FY2025 — was recognized on a sale where IEP received cash plus a $132M seller loan, $81M of preferred equity and a profits interest, with the loan disclosed as collateral-dependent and repayment "expected to be provided substantially through the planned sale of certain properties" by the buyer. Maximum exposure is ~$213M. The buyer is a third-party developer; related-party designation arises from equity-method accounting, not common control. Seller financing is ordinary in commercial real estate; the observation is that the gain is higher-quality-looking than the cash received.

**8. Unresolved SDNY inquiry.** The May 3, 2023 contact from the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York remains disclosed with "no substantive communication since the initial inquiry" — three years without resolution or closure. Status is genuinely unknown.

**9. Governance disclosure is structurally absent.** No DEF 14A exists. As an MLP, IEP holds no annual meeting, unitholders do not elect directors, and there is no say-on-pay or standard proxy disclosure of board composition, related-party transactions or peer TSR. That absence is itself a governance finding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 52.8% against 27.9% one-year historical volatility (24.9pp rich, 90th percentile within the chain) in clear backwardation — 52.8% at 17 days versus 35.2% at 45 days and 29.6% at 136 days — prices imminent event risk, consistent with a Q2 print due within days of the report date. The nearest expiry implies ±12.1%.

The composition is mixed rather than confirmatory of either case. Put/call volume of 0.14 (0.41 in the front month) and a call-heavy skew reading of −13.5% show near-term flow tilted toward calls, while put/call open interest of 1.66 shows accumulated protection. Read alongside 13.7% short interest and 22.6 days to cover, the front-month call activity is at least as consistent with short-side hedging against a squeeze or a Pep Boys-related pop as with directional bullishness. We do not treat the options structure as independent evidence for either the bull or bear case; the operative takeaway is that a large-magnitude, near-dated repricing is being paid for on both sides, and the earnings print is the resolution point.

### Management Quality

Andrew Teno resigned as CEO effective May 6, 2026 — the second CEO change in recent years (Teno succeeded David Willetts in 2023). Ted Papapostolou moved from CFO to CEO at a $3.5M base salary; Robert Flint became CFO at $1.5M. Most revealing: Papapostolou's previously disclosed NAV incentive program was **terminated in full without payment**. The straightforward reading, given GAAP equity down from $2,466M to $1,147M, is that a NAV-linked award was worthless and was replaced by large fixed cash compensation. The alternative — that a CFO-level structure was inappropriate for a CEO role and was superseded by an arrangement we have not read — cannot be excluded; "without payment" does not by itself prove the metric was missed. What is unresolved is whether NAV-linked alignment survives anywhere in the plan.

Capital allocation cuts both ways. In FY2025 IEP repurchased **no units** while selling 10.07M units through the ATM at ~$8.34, below its own stated NAV, and bought back $50M face of 9.000% 2030 notes for $46M. Prioritizing the balance sheet over per-unit value is defensible but dilutive. Against that, the Mavis transaction is a rational, value-realizing disposal at a price equal to ~13.5% of market capitalization for a business burning cash, and the February 2026 CVR refinancing genuinely extended the subsidiary maturity wall. That combination supports a modest improvement from the prior review.

Insider activity requires precise labeling. Icahn's reported holdings rose from ~450.8M units (December 2024) to ~618.4M (June 2026), but these are code-"J" transactions corresponding to in-kind distribution elections — non-cash receipts of newly issued units, not open-market purchases. Treating this as insider buying would be a material analytical error. It conveys that the controlling holder prefers units to cash at these levels, which is mildly informative and nothing more.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $9.50 (+24.5%)

Pep Boys closes on terms as announced, delivering ~$700M cash that is upstreamed to the holding company, covering 2026's ~$577M requirement without further fund redemptions and eliminating a ~$150M annual cash drain. CVR reinstates its dividend, restoring the $100M+ conduit that went to zero in 2025 — the single highest-leverage positive catalyst available. Refining cracks stay elevated through 2026-27 on the constrained-supply backdrop and the SRE regime turns favorable again, lifting CVR earnings and market value while the "refining hedge" losses that flatter nothing reverse. Management announces a refining acquisition financed at CVR, where the ABL now runs to $550-700M with maturities to 2031, producing a step-change in indicative NAV. The 2027 notes are refinanced at a coupon closer to 8% than 10%. On this path the premium to a rising NAV becomes defensible and the units retest the $9.52 52-week high.

### Bear Case — $4.75 (−37.7%)

The units converge toward the company's own indicative NAV of roughly $5.06/unit, and that NAV keeps falling. Q2 and Q3 2026 losses continue at even half the Q1 pace while $325M quarterly distributions are declared, taking attributable equity below $600M. The 2027 refinancing prices near 10%, adding ~$65-69M of permanent annual interest against operating segments that produced $52M of attributable net income. CVR keeps its dividend suspended; the RFS liability keeps building from $204M; Viskase requires another capital injection or trips its amended covenants. The ATM prices progressively lower, the scrip distribution compounds the unit count ~20%+ annually, and the market reprices the Icahn option value toward zero. A $3.0B NAV against 750M units would be $4.00/unit; $4.75 assumes the market still pays a modest premium.

### Base Case — $6.50 (−14.8%)

Pep Boys closes and buys roughly a year of holding-company runway; the acute liquidity question stays dormant. The structural arithmetic does not change: negative holding-company operating cash flow, a distribution funded from capital and paid mostly in scrip, a 5%+ quarterly increase in the unit count, and a 2027 refinancing that consumes more than the operating businesses earn. Per-unit NAV grinds lower and the price follows it partway while retaining some option premium. A cash-electing holder receives approximately $2.00 over twelve months against a ~15% price decline; the total-return calculation must not simply add a 26% yield to a price target, because 84% of the most recent payment was scrip and the cash portion is funded by shrinking the asset base.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q2 2026 print is imminent — the front-month IV structure (52.8% versus 29.6% at six months, ±12.1% implied) is consistent with an early-August release. A repeat of Q1's magnitude would take attributable equity below $700M. Beyond that, the 2027 refinancing is a dated, arithmetic event but is 12-18 months from pricing.

**Timing window.** The near catalyst is days away; the decisive one (2027 refinancing, cumulative NAV erosion) is 12-24 months out. That mismatch is unfavorable for a short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 13.7% of float short, 16.78M shares, **22.6 days to cover**. Because Icahn holds ~86%, the true float is only ~94M units, so shares short represent roughly 18% of tradable supply against average daily volume of 915K. This is an extremely crowded, illiquid short.

**Borrow and carry — the decisive factor.** A short must fund the distribution. At $2.00 per unit annually against $7.63, the carry is **26.2% per year** before borrow fees, and the payment mechanics (unit or cash election, with units the default) complicate the mechanics further. Even a fully correct thesis returning the units to $5.06 (−34%) over twelve months nets roughly 8% before borrow costs. Options positioning does not confirm the bearish case either: front-month put/call volume of 0.14 with a call-heavy skew is inconsistent with fresh bearish accumulation.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear $4.75 (−37.7%) against a squeeze scenario toward the $9.52 high (+24.8%) on a Pep Boys close or a CVR dividend reinstatement. Before carry, that is roughly 1.5:1; after 26% annual distribution carry it is below 1:1.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria — all three would need to be satisfied:**

1. **Price at or below the company's own indicative NAV per unit.** At an unverified ~$3.4B INAV and a ~700M unit count, that is roughly **$4.85/unit or lower** — approximately 36% below the frozen basis. A holding company with negative internal cash flow and a scrip distribution should trade at a discount to NAV, not a premium; we would want a 10-20% discount, implying the low-$4s.
2. **The distribution restructured to a level covered by cash from operations, or eliminated.** The specific, checkable test: a quarterly declaration whose **cash** portion is fully funded by holding-company operating cash flow plus subsidiary dividends received, without fund redemptions or asset-sale proceeds. Concretely, this requires CVR Energy dividend reinstatement at $100M+ annually and holding-company operating cash flow turning positive from $(310)M.
3. **The 2027 maturity refinanced at a coupon below 8%, or repaid from asset sales.** Pricing near 9.75-10% adds $65-69M of permanent annual interest against $52M of attributable operating-segment income. Repayment from disposal proceeds — Pep Boys plus further Real Estate monetization — would be the constructive path.

**Additional upgrade markers we would watch:** two consecutive quarters of positive attributable net income excluding derivative marks and disposal gains; Viskase self-funding its debt service without parent injections; a disclosed refining acquisition with financing terms attached; a new management incentive structure with explicit per-unit NAV linkage; and resolution or formal closure of the SDNY inquiry.

**Timeline for headwinds.** Expect the structure to require asset monetization through at least 2027, when the $1,455M maturity is addressed. The Pep Boys proceeds cover roughly twelve months of holding-company outflow, so the next genuine liquidity decision point is mid-2027 unless CVR's dividend returns first.

**Downgrade markers from here** (would push toward the bear target rather than change the recommendation): Pep Boys termination or a material price adjustment; a further Viskase covenant event; attributable equity below $600M; RFS liability above $300M; or ATM issuance below $7.00 per unit.

**Reassessment point.** The Q2 2026 print, due within days, and the Pep Boys closing.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Overall score 2.2, up marginally from 2.1 on the Mavis transaction, with the fourth consecutive AVOID recommendation.

The Pep Boys sale is real, confirmed by an 8-K, and the best capital-allocation decision this holding company has made in several years — approximately $700M of cash for a business that consumed $151M in FY2025, with the carved-out real estate and the AAMCO/Precision Tune franchises retained. It justifies raising the financial-health assessment from 1.8 to 2.3 and management quality from 2.2 to 2.5. It does not alter the recommendation, because it addresses liquidity timing rather than the structure's arithmetic.

That arithmetic: operating subsidiaries produced $52M of net income attributable to IEP in FY2025 against $504M of consolidated interest expense and $337M of guided 2026 holding-company interest; the holding company's operating cash flow has been negative for three consecutive years ($(221)M, $(230)M, $(310)M); the covenants prohibit incurring new debt; the 2027 refinancing adds $65-69M of permanent annual interest at current market coupons; attributable equity has fallen 53.5% in five quarters; and the 26% headline distribution was 84% scrip in April 2026, compounding the unit count 5.47% in a single quarter against a shrinking asset base. At $7.63 the units trade at 4.2-4.5x GAAP book and a 51-59% premium to the company's own indicative NAV — a premium that must be defended by option value on Icahn's deal-making, in a vehicle that cannot borrow at the parent to execute a deal.

The imminent Q2 print carries a ±12.1% implied move and may look worse than the economics because the funds' refining hedges mark to market while consolidated CVR carrying value does not rise. That asymmetry is a reason to distrust the reported loss as a measure of value destruction. It is not a reason to pay a 50%+ premium to management's own NAV for a structure that must sell assets to service its own interest bill. We would revisit in the low-$4s, or on evidence that the distribution has been reset to a level the operating businesses can fund.
