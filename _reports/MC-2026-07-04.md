**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Moelis & Company is a pure-play independent advisory bank that has ridden the M&A cycle recovery from a 2023 operating loss to a record FY2025: revenue of $1,516.8M (+27% YoY), net income of $259.6M (+71%), and a comp ratio compressed from a crisis-level 84% in 2023 to 67% in 2025. The balance sheet is fortress-grade: zero funded debt, ~$849M in cash and liquid sovereign-debt investments, and a broker-dealer net-capital cushion of $372M against a $250K requirement. Return on equity of ~46% and free cash flow of roughly $540M (35.6% FCF margin) confirm a capital-light franchise that converts earnings to cash efficiently. The February 2026 authorization of a $300M buyback (a 3x step-up from the prior program) plus a 3.8% dividend yield give a total shareholder yield that anchors the investment case.

The complication is momentum. After +40% (2024) and +27% (2025) revenue growth, Q1 2026 revenue grew only +4% YoY. The headline –21% net income decline is a tax artifact (the prior-year quarter carried a $10.7M tax benefit; pretax income actually rose 7%), so the operational picture is better than the EPS print. But consensus estimates have moved the wrong way: current-year EPS has been cut 10.2% over 90 days and current-quarter estimates cut 16.1%, and the Q1 2026 EPS actual missed by 11.5% after three consecutive beats. This deceleration is confirmed in both the reported numbers and the forward estimates, and is not a price-only concern.

At $68.55 the stock trades at ~23x trailing and forward EPS, a premium to the peer median P/E of ~22.0 despite estimates that are flat-to-falling rather than rising. The shares sit roughly 12% below the 52-week high of $78.22 but well above the March 2026 low near $51. The combination of a fortress balance sheet and attractive yield against a decelerating advisory pipeline and a full multiple produces a balanced, income-oriented HOLD. We would upgrade on confirmed revenue re-acceleration with positive estimate revisions, or on a pullback that restores yield support toward the mid-$50s.

## Company Overview

Moelis operates a single business segment, advisory, providing M&A advice, recapitalizations and restructurings, capital markets advisory, private capital advisory (fund placement and GP-led secondaries), and other corporate finance services to corporations, financial sponsors, governments, and sovereign wealth funds. The firm runs 20+ offices globally and employed 1,012 advisory bankers at year-end 2025, growing to 1,027 by Q1 2026. FY2025 revenue came from 363 clients, with 254 paying $1M or more (up from 241 in 2024).

The model is deliberately conflict-free: no lending, no trading, no proprietary balance-sheet activity. This positions the firm as an independent adviser versus bulge-bracket banks that bundle financing to win mandates, but it also removes the ability to cross-subsidize advice with a balance sheet. The countercyclical hedge is real: the M&A franchise rises in expansions while the restructuring and liability-management franchise rises in downturns, giving management a credible claim to operate "through all phases of the business cycle." Roughly 84% of FY2025 revenue was U.S.-sourced, with ~16% international.

Revenue depends on Managing Directors who own the client relationships, which makes talent both the primary asset and the primary risk. Founder Ken Moelis retains ~37% voting control through Class B super-voting shares and Partner Holdings, and co-founder Navid Mahmoodzadegan is now CEO and Chief Operating Decision Maker following an orderly 2025 succession.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 numbers are strong across the board:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $854.7M | $1,194.5M (+40%) | $1,516.8M (+27%) |
| Comp & benefits | $714.7M (84% rev) | $830.2M (69%) | $1,017.1M (67%) |
| Operating income | –$40.4M | $172.9M | $273.9M (+58%) |
| Net income | –$27.5M | $151.5M | $259.6M (+71%) |
| Operating cash flow | $158.5M | $427.5M | $576.3M |

The comp ratio is the single most important lever in this business, and its improvement from 84% to 67% drove the operating-leverage story. Net margin reached 17.1%, ROE ~46%, ROA 14.9%, and FCF margin 35.6%. The balance sheet carries $508.6M cash plus $340M short-term sovereign-debt investments at year-end 2025 with zero interest-bearing debt; the only credit facilities are $50M aggregate and undrawn.

Two commonly cited "warning" signals are false positives here. The Altman Z-score of 2.17 (grey zone) and debt-to-equity of 1.87 reflect operating lease liabilities ($267M) and the tax receivable agreement payable ($301M) rather than real financial leverage. There is no funded debt. The Piotroski partial score of 4/4 (positive net income, ROA, operating cash flow, and OCF above net income) confirms genuine quality. The –$801M retained-earnings deficit is a structural artifact of the Up-C partnership structure and heavy payout, not accumulated losses.

Q1 2026 shows the moderation clearly. Revenue was $319.8M, a "record first quarter" but only +4% YoY, a sharp deceleration from the 2024-2025 pace. Net income of $42.3M was down 21% YoY, but this is a tax optic: the prior-year quarter had a $10.7M tax benefit versus a $3.9M tax expense this quarter (lower excess tax benefits on equity delivered at lower share prices). Pretax income rose 7% ($46.2M vs $43.1M). The comp ratio held at 66%; non-comp expenses rose to 22% of revenue on deal-related, technology, and occupancy costs tied to headcount growth. The Q1 cash draw to $153.7M from $508.6M is the normal seasonal pattern of bonus and partner tax-distribution payments, not a red flag.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and accelerating. The quarterly dividend is $0.65/share ($2.60 annualized, ~3.8% yield). The new $300M buyback (total authorization $301.5M) is a 3x increase over the prior program; $117M was deployed in Q1 2026 with ~$248M remaining. Given ~$72M of quarterly equity-comp expense and ~79M diluted shares, the buyback partially offsets persistent dilution rather than fully retiring the float. Financial Health scores 8.0, the strongest dimension and the most reliable downside filter, supported by zero debt and high cash conversion.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs, and both have weakened at the margin. The cyclical leg, an M&A recovery lifting all advisory revenue, remains intact but is decelerating. Management cites strategic acquirers pursuing larger transformational deals, rising sponsor activity, record GP-led secondaries, and a restructuring/liability-management pipeline as prior solutions run their course. The structural leg, the deliberately built private capital advisory pillar (fund placement, GP-led secondaries) following "substantial investment in 2025," is a higher-recurring-revenue adjacency to the lumpy core.

The forward evidence is mixed-to-cautious. Estimate revisions have moved down, not up: current-year EPS cut 10.2% over 90 days (to $3.09) and current-quarter cut 16.1% (to $0.61); next-year sits at $3.95, cut 5.8%. The 30-day trend has stabilized (roughly flat), which suggests the cuts have moderated, but there is no positive revision velocity here. The earnings surprise record (three beats followed by an 11.5% Q1 2026 miss) argues for lower confidence in forward estimates than the FY2025 beat streak would imply.

A newly emphasized headwind: Q1 2026 MD&A flags that "AI-driven disruption is weighing particularly on M&A sentiment within the software sector." This is nascent and two-sided (AI also drives strategic-repositioning M&A), but it is a specific sector-level dampener worth tracking.

Growth Prospects scores 5.5. Growth is the most reliable dimension for sorting winners, and the honest read here is a decelerating top line with falling-then-stabilizing estimates. That does not clear the bar for a growth-driven upgrade; the estimate revisions are negative over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.55, MC trades at ~23.0x trailing and forward EPS (trailing EPS $3.17; forward P/E 23.0x). Against the Investment Banking & Brokerage peer set, the median P/E is 22.0x:

| Ticker | Name | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|------|-----|-----------|
| BGC | BGC Group | 30.4 | 0.7% |
| PIPR | Piper Sandler | 19.7 | 1.0% |
| PJT | PJT Partners | 22.0 | 0.6% |
| HLI | Houlihan Lokey | 21.8 | 2.1% |
| SNEX | StoneX Group | 23.0 | — |

MC trades at a modest premium to the peer median, which its ~3.8% dividend yield (well above every peer) and fortress balance sheet partly justify. The problem is that the multiple is not accompanied by rising estimates. When a stock trades at a premium multiple with estimates being cut, beats are already priced and misses derate the shares, precisely the asymmetry that played out in the Q1 miss and subsequent price weakness. This is not a cyclical-trough situation where a high trailing multiple masks tripling earnings; earnings are near a cyclical high with forward estimates flat-to-down, so trailing/forward P/E is the right lens and it reads full rather than cheap.

The analyst consensus supports a balanced view: mean target $71.7, median $70.5, high $86.0, low $58.0. The stock sits close to the median target, and with estimates falling rather than rising, these targets function as fair-value anchors rather than stale floors.

Valuation scores 5.5. A "cheap" score is not warranted, and cheapness has been anti-predictive as a signal: a low multiple alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy trigger. Here the multiple is not even cheap; it is a peer-premium name with an income cushion and no independent catalyst to argue mispricing. The yield support prevents a lower score.

## Competitive Landscape

Moelis competes with independents (Evercore, Lazard, PJT, Centerview, Perella Weinberg, Houlihan Lokey) and bulge brackets. Its differentiator is conflict-free independent advice; its structural advantage over pure-M&A boutiques is the restructuring and liability-management franchise, which picks up when M&A slows. The 254 clients paying $1M+ and 363 total clients show a broadening, senior-banker-led relationship base. The 8 new MD hires in Q1 2026 and a deliberately expanded private capital advisory unit are evidence of a firm investing to gain share.

The competitive risks are specific. The 10-K flags erosion of non-compete enforceability (California and other states restricting restrictive covenants) as a growing threat to MD retention, and it explicitly worries about bulge brackets bundling financing to win mandates and a proliferation of new independent entrants driving fee pressure. Talent mobility cuts both ways: Moelis can hire MDs away, but it can also lose teams that take client relationships with them.

Competitive Position scores 6.0, an improving trajectory on client-count growth, MD hires, and the private-capital build, tempered by fee-pressure and non-compete-erosion headwinds. This dimension's recent predictive signal has weakened, so we weight it modestly and confirm the read against growth and financial health rather than leaning on it alone.

## Risk Assessment

**Revenue lumpiness** is the defining risk. Substantially all revenue is transaction-milestone advisory fees with no long-term contracts; timing is outside the firm's control, and one strong period is not predictive. This directly drives stock volatility (implied moves of ±6.7% near-term, ±12.8% at three months).

**Talent and key-person risk.** The firm depends on retaining MDs, and the 10-K explicitly names dependence on Messrs. Mahmoodzadegan and Moelis. The 2025 leadership transition, though orderly and insider-led (co-founder to co-founder), concentrates continuity risk in one executive while Ken Moelis retains ~37% voting control.

**Governance and minority-shareholder rights.** Class B super-voting shares mean public Class A holders cannot force a change of control or extract a takeover premium. The tax receivable agreement ($269–301M payable, 85% of realized tax savings) could accelerate dramatically on a change of control, a de facto poison pill.

**Estimate momentum and the recent miss.** Falling 90-day estimates and the Q1 2026 EPS miss (–11.5%) signal reduced forward visibility.

**AI-driven M&A disruption** in the software sector is a newly flagged, nascent headwind.

**Positive de-risking:** the July 2025 Delaware Chancery dismissal of aiding-and-abetting and unjust-enrichment claims against the Moelis entities (Archer/Atlas Crest SPAC litigation) removed a reputational/legal overhang, and the August 2023 SEC off-channel-messaging matter ($10M) is resolved.

Insider activity is entirely routine: RSU/partnership-unit grants, option/RSU conversions, and tax-withholding dispositions. There are no open-market C-suite purchases (no bullish signal) and no clustered selling (no bearish signal). The one GC sale of 2,765 shares in February 2026 accompanied RSU vesting/tax settlement and is not standalone bearish.

Risk Factors scores 5.5. The volatility and governance risks are largely priced into a cyclical advisory name, but the falling estimates, the AI-M&A software headwind, and the Class B control structure keep this from a higher score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.0% versus 35.0% historical volatility, rich by ~6.1 points, indicating the market prices elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call skew of 6.5% on the front-month shows elevated put demand, though skew flips negative at 3m (–1.1%) and 6m (–2.1%), so the hedging is concentrated near-term rather than a structural bearish tilt. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.06 to 0.92 since the prior snapshot, a meaningful build in put open interest that weighs against any bullish stance. Short interest is moderate at 6.5% of float, 5.2 days to cover, not a squeeze setup. The flat term structure (41.0% near ≈ 42.5% far) indicates uncertainty spread evenly rather than a single dated binary. Net: the options market is mildly cautious near-term, corroborating the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($82–86):** The M&A cycle re-accelerates in H2 2026, restructuring and liability-management work rises as prior solutions run their course, and the comp ratio holds near 66-67%, driving operating leverage on a recovering top line. Private capital advisory adds a higher-recurring revenue stream. The buyback shrinks the float while the dividend supports the floor. Revenue returns to double-digit growth, estimates re-rate upward, and the stock reaches the high analyst target near $86 (roughly +25%).

**Bear Case ($52–55):** Advisory deceleration continues, the AI-driven software-M&A chill broadens, and a soft macro (higher-for-longer rates dampening deal financing) keeps 2026 revenue flat-to-down. Estimates keep falling, the premium multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range, and the stock revisits the March 2026 low near $51 (roughly –20% to –25%). The dividend remains covered by FCF, cushioning but not preventing the derating.

**Base Case ($68–72):** Revenue growth stays low-single-digit to mid-single-digit, the comp ratio holds, and the shareholder yield (dividend plus buyback) roughly offsets a flat multiple. The stock oscillates around current levels, delivering total return close to the ~3.8% dividend plus modest buyback accretion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

The hold rests on the shareholder yield and cyclical optionality rather than on a specific appreciation catalyst, hence the income-hold classification. The dividend is well-covered by ~$540M FCF, and the tripled buyback provides a secondary return stream. But the decelerating pipeline, falling-then-stabilizing estimates, and full peer-premium multiple leave no asymmetric entry today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue re-acceleration (YoY growth back above ~10%) accompanied by positive current-year EPS estimate revisions (reversal of the –10.2% 90-day trend).
- A pullback toward the mid-$50s that restores dividend yield support above ~4.5% while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comp ratio deterioration back above ~72% signaling lost operating leverage.
- A material MD-team departure to a competitor, or evidence the AI-driven software-M&A chill is broadening into other advisory verticals and cutting the pipeline materially.
- Consecutive quarters of double-digit YoY revenue declines with continued estimate cuts.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July/early August), with attention to revenue trajectory, comp ratio, backlog/announced-transaction commentary, and any change in AI-M&A language.

## Conclusion

Moelis is a high-quality, debt-free advisory franchise coming off a record year, with a fortress balance sheet, ~46% ROE, and a total shareholder yield anchored by the dividend and tripled buyback. Those attributes underpin the Financial Health score of 8.0 and justify owning the shares for income and cyclical exposure. The offsetting reality is that revenue growth decelerated to +4% in Q1 2026, consensus estimates have been cut ~10% for the current year, the Q1 EPS actual missed by 11.5% after three beats, and the stock trades at a modest premium to the peer median with no positive estimate momentum to argue mispricing. Near-term options positioning is mildly cautious (rich IV, elevated front-month put demand, rising put OI).

This is a balanced, income-oriented HOLD at $68.55. The dividend and buyback provide downside support and a return floor; the decelerating pipeline and full multiple cap near-term upside. We would turn constructive on confirmed revenue re-acceleration with rising estimates, or on a pullback into the mid-$50s that restores yield-driven asymmetry. Overall score 6.1, consistent with the improving-but-not-yet-BUY trajectory (5.8 → 6.2 → 6.1), where the Financial Health improvement is offset by decelerating growth and a valuation that leaves little margin of safety.
