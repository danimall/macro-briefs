**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Advanced Energy Industries designs and manufactures precision power conversion, measurement, and control products that sit in the picks-and-shovels layer of the AI buildout. The company supplies power to two distinct AI demand vectors: the plasma and high-voltage power inside semiconductor fab equipment that makes AI chips, and the power shelves and rack-level conversion that run AI servers for hyperscalers. Both markets are inflecting at the same time. Data Center Computing revenue rose from $284M in 2024 to $587M in 2025 (+107%), then doubled again year-over-year in Q1 2026 to $194M, moving from roughly 19% to 38% of quarterly revenue in twelve months. Company-wide revenue grew 21.4% in 2025 to $1,799M and 26.3% in Q1 2026 to $511M, while GAAP operating margin expanded from 2.5% in 2024 to 9.3% in 2025 to 13.4% in Q1 2026.

The setup carries the classic markers of a genuine earnings inflection rather than financial engineering. Consensus next-year EPS estimates have risen 14.3% over 90 days to $12.29, current-year estimates are up 8.1% to $9.42, and the company has beaten in each of the last four quarters (by an average of ~10%). The balance sheet has been transformed: the entire $564M term loan was repaid in 2024, and in June 2026 management issued a redemption notice for the remaining $575M of 2.5% convertible notes due 2028, removing the dilution overhang that had mechanically inflated current liabilities and depressed the Altman Z-score. Net of the converts, the company sits net-cash positive with ~$700M in cash against an undrawn $600M revolver.

The stock has appreciated roughly 120% over twelve months and trades at ~65x trailing GAAP EPS. On the metrics that matter for an inflection stock, the picture is less stretched: ~33.4x current-year and ~25.6x next-year consensus EPS at ~$315, well below a peer group where the median P/E sits near 141x. The newest data confirms the trajectory of prior scoring (5.5 → 6.3 over four reports, upgraded to BUY on the last read). We rate AEIS a BUY with disciplined sizing. The real risks — top-three customer concentration near 54%, structurally lower data-center margins, and a negative Q1 operating cash flow on a working-capital build — are specific and monitorable, and argue against a maximum-conviction position rather than against ownership.

## Company Overview

Advanced Energy, founded in 1981 and headquartered in Denver, designs highly engineered precision power products that convert raw utility power into controllable, repeatable power for complex equipment. The company reports as a single segment but sells across four end markets, with FY2025 revenue split as follows:

- **Semiconductor Equipment — $839.9M (46.7%)**: Plasma power, high-voltage power, and adjacent sensing for etch, deposition, ion implant, metrology, and packaging. Strategy is to outgrow wafer fab equipment (WFE) spending through additional plasma process steps and share gains in dielectric etch. Competitors include MKS Instruments, COMET, Daihen, and TRUMPF Hüttinger.
- **Data Center Computing — $587.3M (32.6%)**: Power solutions for AI server racks and power shelves sold to hyperscalers and their partners. Grew +106.7% in 2025. Competitors include Delta Electronics, Flex, and Lite-On.
- **Industrial & Medical — $282.3M (15.7%)**: Precision, low-noise power for medical, life science, test & measurement, robotics, and defense/aerospace. Down 10.7% in 2025 on customer inventory rebalancing, with early signs of recovery.
- **Telecom & Networking — $89.3M (5.0%)**: Application-specific power for wireless and networking OEMs. Roughly flat.

Manufacturing is concentrated in the Philippines, Malaysia, and Mexico, with a new Thailand facility; roughly 70% of revenue comes from customers outside the U.S. The company employs approximately 13,000 people. Steve Kelley is CEO and Paul Oldham is CFO. The competitive moat rests on long-lived design wins (products carry multi-year lifecycles), leadership in plasma power for semiconductors, and a power-density and efficiency edge that positioned the company to capture AI power-shelf design wins.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The trajectory is a cyclical trough-and-recovery overlaid with an AI-driven acceleration: $1,845M (2022) → $1,656M (2023) → $1,482M (2024 trough) → $1,799M (2025, +21.4%) → Q1 2026 $511.0M (+26.3% YoY vs. $404.6M). Data Center Computing is the engine, doubling to $194.2M in Q1 2026 and reaching 38% of quarterly revenue.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded from 35.7% (2024) to 37.7% (2025) to 39.3% in Q1 2026, with non-GAAP gross margin at 40.1%. The 2025 gain of roughly 140 bps came from footprint consolidation (Zhongshan closure, Thailand ramp, site consolidations). GAAP operating margin reached 13.4% in Q1 2026 (non-GAAP operating margin 19.1%). Net income rose from $54.2M (2024) to ~$148–149M (2025), with Q1 2026 net income of $66.8M versus $24.7M a year earlier. GAAP diluted EPS moved from $1.49 (2024) to $3.87 (2025); non-GAAP EPS from $3.71 to $6.41, with Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $2.09 versus $1.23.

Margins are expanding despite the fastest-growing segment (data center) carrying structurally lower margins than the corporate average. That the manufacturing consolidation is outrunning the mix drag is the central operating validation of the bull case.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $234.7M (up from $133M), with FCF of $127.3M after capex rose to $107.4M for capacity and a new ERP. Q1 2026 operating cash flow swung to negative $5.6M on a working-capital build — accounts receivable up $52.4M and inventory up $48.4M to support demand. This is consistent with a company ramping into rising orders, but it merits monitoring: if revenue growth stalls, that working capital reverses and the bears' channel-stuffing concern gains traction. Cash stood at $699.5M in Q1 2026, with a current ratio of 1.59.

Leverage has been transformed. The entire $564M term loan was repaid in 2024, leaving only the $575M 2.5% convertible notes due 2028, now being redeemed. The Altman Z-score of 2.64 ("grey zone") is depressed almost entirely because the converts were reclassified to current liabilities once the stock cleared the conversion trigger, which is a mechanical accounting effect rather than a solvency issue. Net of the converts, the company is net-cash positive.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is modest at $0.10/quarter ($15.6M/yr, ~0.13% yield). Buybacks were $30.4M in 2025 with $166.6M of authorization remaining. R&D held steady at 12–14% of revenue (~$232M in 2025), appropriate for a technology-differentiated power supplier.

The financial-health picture supports a 7.0: strong FCF generation, a net-cash balance sheet, appropriate (now minimal) leverage, and improving operating leverage. The negative Q1 operating cash flow and data-center margin dilution are the offsets that keep it from scoring higher.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest dimension here, and it clears a high evidentiary bar. Three signals reinforce one another:

1. **Revenue acceleration off a trough.** Company revenue grew +21% in 2025 and +26% in Q1 2026, with the data-center vector doubling year-over-year for two consecutive periods.

2. **Estimate revisions of inflection magnitude.** Next-year consensus EPS rose 14.3% over 90 days (with a +4.3% move in just the last 30 days, indicating acceleration) to $12.29. Current-year estimates rose 8.1% to $9.42. This combination of revenue growth above 25% and rising estimates is among the more reliable forward indicators of continued outperformance, and it is present here on a stock that trades near 26x forward — the setup where the signal carries the most weight.

3. **Consistent beats.** Four straight beats (3.5%, 7.8%, 16.1%, 12.5% over the trailing four quarters) raise confidence in the current consensus and suggest management guidance remains conservative.

The durability of the growth thesis is enhanced by the breadth of the underlying demand. The AI infrastructure buildout is being confirmed across multiple layers of AEIS's own end markets — hyperscaler capex feeding data-center power and WFE spending feeding semiconductor power — and AEIS participates through two of those layers simultaneously. WFE recovery is expected to accelerate in H2 2026, adding to the semiconductor vector. The July 2026 launch of 800V DC converters positions AEIS for the high-voltage rack architecture transition that next-generation NVIDIA-class AI infrastructure is driving. Growth Prospects earns an 8.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$315, the trailing GAAP P/E of ~65x looks demanding, but trailing multiples are the wrong lens for a stock whose earnings roughly doubled in the last year and are guided higher. On consensus forward estimates the picture changes materially:

- Current-year EPS $9.42 → ~33.4x on $315 (or ~32.3x on the $303.99 reference price used for consensus targets).
- Next-year EPS $12.29 → ~25.6x on $315 (or ~24.7x on $303.99).

The peer group in Semiconductor Materials & Equipment trades at a median P/E of 141x (FORM 149.7, ONTO 141.2, KLIC 108.0, ACMR 70.0, UCTT negative). AEIS at a fraction of that median while growing revenue at 26% and expanding operating margin sharply year-over-year is not obviously expensive relative to its cohort. The PEG on next-year growth is well below 1.0. Analyst price targets — low $350, median $421, mean $429, high $535 — all sit above the current price, with the low target implying ~11% upside from $315. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a next-year multiple in the mid-20s, consensus targets tend to lag and get revised up as the company continues to beat.

Two counterweights keep this from being a high-conviction valuation call. First, the calibration record is unambiguous that "cheap" screens have been anti-predictive and that a low multiple alone is never a buy signal; the score must rest on why the market is wrong, which here is the specific evidence that estimates are being revised up faster than the price has moved and that peers command far higher multiples. Second, the trailing multiple and the 9.7% post-Q1 drop show how sensitive the stock is to any deceleration; a single quarter of order deferral by a top hyperscaler customer would re-rate the shares quickly. On balance, the forward math and the peer discount support a modestly positive valuation view. We score Valuation 5.5: the mispricing case is real but the margin of safety is thin at current prices, and the stock is well off its lows, making entry timing matter.

## Competitive Landscape

AEIS occupies a defensible niche in a market with no single dominant competitor. In semiconductor plasma and RF power it competes primarily against MKS Instruments (larger and broader), COMET, and TRUMPF Hüttinger; in data-center power against Delta Electronics, Flex, and Lite-On, which are larger, Asia-based scale players that benefit from local incentives and price competition. The competitive trajectory is improving on the strength of design-win momentum: the doubling of data-center revenue reflects AEIS winning production-ready, high-density power-shelf slots ahead of competitors, and the 800V converter launch extends that lead into the next rack generation. Design wins in this business carry multi-year product lifecycles, which builds a recurring revenue base as those platforms ramp.

The offsetting concern is that hyperscaler power is precisely where AEIS faces the largest, most price-competitive rivals and where customers place orders on a purchase-order/JIT basis with no long-term commitments. The moat in semiconductors (deep, sticky design-win relationships) is stronger than in data center, where scale and price matter more. Gross margin holding up through the mix shift is the operational tell that the company is not simply buying share. Competitive Position earns a 6.5: improving trajectory, but with a structurally weaker position in its fastest-growing end market.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are specific and largely idiosyncratic rather than sector truisms:

1. **Customer concentration.** Three customers were 23%, 19%, and 12% of 2025 revenue (~54% combined), up from two at 26%/11% in 2024. There are no long-term purchase commitments; sales are largely purchase-order/JIT. Loss or deferral by any one would be material and is the single largest downside catalyst. This risk is intensifying, not easing, as the data-center mix grows.

2. **Data-center margin dilution.** Management explicitly warns this fast-growing segment carries lower margins. As mix shifts further toward data center, blended gross margin faces structural pressure, partly offsetting cost-reduction gains. The bull case depends on footprint savings continuing to outrun the drag.

3. **Cyclicality and over-build risk.** The company must scale capacity for AI demand without over-building if that demand proves unsustainable, a risk it calls out directly. The negative Q1 operating cash flow on AR and inventory builds is the near-term expression of this.

4. **China/export controls and tariffs.** Since October 2022, U.S. export rules have cost AEIS business to non-U.S. competitors. 2025 tariffs pressured Q1 2026 gross margin, offsetting roughly half the cost savings. The Zhongshan plant closed in Q2 2025.

5. **Execution risk.** A multi-year global ERP rollout (re-staggered in 2025) plus overlapping restructuring plans running through 2027.

6. **Legacy inverter warranty tail** (discontinued 2015 line with warranties up to 20 years) and ~$48.5M unfunded pension, plus meaningful unhedged FX exposure.

The convertible-note redemption removes what a naive data pull would flag as balance-sheet stress; the Q1 2026 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K. Insider activity is routine equity-comp liquidity, not a bearish signal. Net, the risks are real, concentrated, and partly intensifying, keeping Risk Factors at 5.0: the concentration and margin-mix exposures are not fully priced given the stock's sensitivity to any deceleration.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 82.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 57.2% — rich by 25 points, at the 83rd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 82.2% > 6-month 69.7%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±18.3%, consistent with a market bracing for a large reaction to the August 3 Q2 print. Skew is essentially balanced (-1.3% put/call), and the put/call volume ratio (0.56) and OI ratio (0.16) lean bullish rather than bearish, so the elevated vol reflects event risk rather than directional pessimism. ATM IV collapsed from an extreme 388.6% reading in mid-June to 82.2% now; the earlier figure was almost certainly a thin-options artifact, and the current level is the informative one. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues for either scaling in ahead of the print or waiting for the post-earnings vol crush to find a better entry, rather than paying full premium into the event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$425–535).** AI infrastructure demand sustains, data-center revenue continues to compound while footprint consolidation holds or expands blended gross margin above 40%, and the semiconductor recovery accelerates in H2 2026. Next-year EPS proves conservative given four straight beats; on 30–40x that base the stock reaches the $421 median and $535 high analyst targets. The 800V converter positions AEIS as a default supplier into the next rack generation, and the cleaned-up net-cash balance sheet gives management room for buybacks and bolt-on M&A. This case matches the mean analyst target of $429.

**Bear case (~$210–240).** A top hyperscaler customer defers or reallocates orders, exposing the ~54% concentration; the data-center margin drag overwhelms cost savings as mix crosses further past 40% of revenue; the Q1 negative operating cash flow proves to be a demand-pull-forward that reverses; and the broader AI-capex ROI debate hits the whole picks-and-shovels cohort. At ~18–20x a haircut-to-$11–12 EPS, the stock retraces toward the $210–240 area — roughly 25–33% downside from current levels, consistent with the >18% single-expiry implied move that options are pricing.

The base case sits between these: continued but decelerating growth, margins roughly holding, and the multiple settling around 30x current-year / ~22x next-year EPS as consensus catches up.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $390.** ~30x current-year consensus EPS of $9.42, or ~22x next-year $12.29 net of some deceleration and margin drag. This implies ~24% upside from $315 and sits between the bear and bull scenarios.
- **Bull case target: $490.** ~40x current-year / ~32x next-year EPS if AI demand and margin expansion both hold; roughly the midpoint of the median-to-high analyst target range.
- **Bear case target: $225.** ~18x a haircut-to-$12 EPS on order deferral or margin compression; ~29% downside from $315.
- **Timeframe: 12 months.** The near-term catalyst is the August 3, 2026 Q2 print, with WFE recovery data and hyperscaler capex updates through H2 2026 as the durability tests.
- **Upside/downside from current ($315):** +24% to base, +56% to bull, -29% to bear.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:**
  1. Data Center Computing revenue growth decelerating below ~15% YoY in any quarter, or a disclosed loss/deferral by a top-three customer.
  2. Blended gross margin falling below ~37% (cost savings no longer offsetting mix), signaling the margin thesis has broken.
  3. Operating cash flow remaining negative for a second consecutive quarter without a clear working-capital unwind, indicating the Q1 build was demand pull-forward rather than a healthy ramp.

## Conclusion

AEIS combines a cyclical trough recovery with an AI-driven inflection, expressed through two distinct demand vectors that the current forward multiple does not fully capture. The data-center business doubling for two consecutive periods, operating margin expanding sharply in a year, estimate revisions of +14% for next year, four straight beats, and a de-risked net-cash balance sheet form a coherent bull case that the trailing headline multiple obscures. On the metrics appropriate to an inflection stock — ~26x next-year EPS against a peer median near 141x, PEG below 1.0, and analyst targets that all sit above the current price — the market appears to be lagging the earnings reality rather than over-extrapolating it.

The recommendation is BUY, with sizing discipline rather than maximum conviction. The customer concentration, the structurally lower data-center margins, and the negative Q1 operating cash flow are specific, monitorable risks that could reverse the thesis quickly given how sensitive the stock is to deceleration, as the 9.7% post-Q1 drop demonstrated. The rich near-term implied volatility ahead of the August 3 print favors scaling in or waiting for the post-earnings vol crush for a better entry. This confirms the improving score trajectory (5.5 → 6.3) and the prior upgrade to BUY; the convertible redemption, the continued estimate revisions, and the sustained data-center growth reinforce rather than reverse the view.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward valuation is less attractive than stated — next-year P/E is ~25.6x at $315 (not ~18.9x) and current-year ~33.4x (not ~24.6x), which narrows the discount-to-peers argument underpinning the Valuation view.
