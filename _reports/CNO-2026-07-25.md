**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNO Financial Group is a $5.0B holding company for a group of insurance subsidiaries (Bankers Life, Washington National, Colonial Penn) serving middle-income pre-retiree and retired Americans, a demographic niche it has occupied since emerging from the Conseco bankruptcy in 2003. The investment case rests on a distinction that a naïve screen misreads: GAAP EPS fell to $2.30 in 2025 (down ~41% from $3.89), but that decline is almost entirely non-operating. Net operating income rose to $439.2M ($4.40/diluted share), a record, with the GAAP gap explained by a $101.9M Optavise goodwill impairment, $64.0M of adverse embedded-derivative/MRB marks on fixed indexed annuities, $69.0M of realized investment losses, and TechMod modernization expense. On the metric management is measured against, 2025 was the best year in the company's post-reorganization history, and 2026 operating-EPS guidance of $4.25–4.45 implies continued strength.

The forward story has three legs. First, an industry shift: the decade-long move from Medicare Supplement to Medicare Advantage is reversing as MA carriers cut benefits under CMS rate pressure, and CNO captures this doubly, through MedSupp underwriting margin and MA distribution fees. Second, a health-margin engine that is improving (total insurance product margin $1,067.6M, health margin up to 34% of policy income, long-term-care margin jumping to $145.1M on favorable claims). Third, aggressive capital return: share count fell ~8% in 2025 (106.1M to 97.8M) on $319.9M of buybacks, with a remaining buyback authorization of $420.4M plus a new $500M authorization added Feb 2025 funding continued per-share accretion.

What holds this at HOLD rather than BUY is the price. The stock has climbed ~42% over the trailing twelve months to a 52-week high near $54, and at ~21x trailing / ~12x forward operating EPS the multiple has expanded ahead of the earnings; the valuation dimension has been the weakest and most-declining input across our last three reviews. The CNO Bermuda Re affiliated-reinsurance structure (~$10.7B of statutory reserves ceded to a wholly-owned Bermuda entity) sits in the path of the NAIC's newly adopted AG 55 asset-adequacy testing, a regulatory risk that could compete with buybacks for holding-company cash. Combined with the legacy LTC tail and a compressing annuity spread, the setup after the run is roughly balanced. We rate CNO HOLD with a constructive growth bias.

## Company Overview

CNO operates three insurance product lines (annuity, health, life) plus investment and fee-income segments, distributed through two divisions. The Consumer Division sells through ~4,600 exclusive producing agents across ~230 branch offices plus one of the largest direct-to-consumer insurance operations (Colonial Penn's TV/web/call-center model). The Worksite Division uses ~400 exclusive agents selling voluntary benefits at the workplace under the Washington National brand.

2025 premium collections of $4.6B broke down as annuities 42% ($1.94B, dominated by fixed indexed annuities at $1.74B, up 12.8% YoY), health 36% ($1.66B, comprising $745M supplemental health, $627M Medicare supplement, $292M long-term care), and life 21% ($985M). The company is a small player in most of its markets (1.7% Medicare supplement share, ranked #7) but holds the #2 position in individual long-term care new premiums at 25.8% share via Bankers Life. Its competitive advantage is narrow: distribution reach into an underserved middle-income senior demographic and direct-response brand recognition, rather than scale or ratings. Financial-strength ratings are mid-tier and stable (Fitch A, S&P A-, Moody's A3, AM Best A), and management concedes most major competitors are rated higher, a persistent handicap in worksite sales where employers weigh carrier ratings.

A strategic move in late 2025 was the exit of the Optavise worksite fee-services business, which drove the $101.9M impairment. The trade is deliberate: it cuts ~$30M of annual fee revenue but adds ~$20M of annual pre-tax income, exchanging low-quality fee revenue for margin and sharpening the focus on core insurance.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue and earnings trajectory:

| Revenue | $3.58B | $4.15B | $4.45B | $4.49B |
| GAAP Net income | $396.8M | $276.5M | $404.0M | $229.3M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $2.40 | $3.89 | $2.30 |
| Net operating income | — | $356.1M | $429.3M | $439.2M |
| Stockholders' equity | $1.40B | $2.22B | $2.50B | $2.64B |

Operating earnings are the clean read, and they show ~23% two-year growth ($356.1M in 2023 to $439.2M in 2025). The $209.9M net non-operating loss in 2025 comprised the Optavise impairment ($101.9M), the FIA embedded-derivative/MRB swing (-$64.0M), realized investment losses (-$69.0M), TechMod expense (-$20.3M), and a $17.3M loss on the divested worksite fee business. None of these recur at that magnitude, and the derivative marks reverse with rates.

Margins by line tell the operating story. Health is the profit engine and improving: health margin reached $556.6M (34% of policy income vs. 31% in 2023), with long-term-care margin jumping to $145.1M (a 50% margin) from $83.0M in 2023 on favorable claims and short-duration product growth, and supplemental health at $305.4M. Life margin rose to $272.4M, helped by a shift to cheaper digital lead channels (advertising down to $67.7M from $92.5M in 2023). The soft spot is annuities: margin fell to $238.6M from $274.2M as higher-spread legacy policies surrendered, with adjusted FIA margin/liabilities slipping to 1.85% from 2.19%. Operating ROE was ~11.4% in 2025, up from 8.6% in 2023, and management targets a further ~200 bps run-rate improvement through 2027.

The balance sheet is solid despite screening artifacts. Total assets are $38.8B against a ~$30.0B investment portfolio that is 91% fixed maturities plus mortgage loans, 96% investment grade, with only 3.5% below-investment-grade and 3.1% alternatives. The $1.1B AOCI loss (from higher rates marking down the AFS bond book) depresses GAAP book value to $27.92/share versus $39.72 excluding AOCI. Corporate debt fell to $1.34B after repaying 2025 notes, leaving no maturities until the $500M 5.25% notes due 2029; debt/total capital is 26.2% ex-AOCI (within the 25–28% target, well inside the 35% covenant). Statutory RBC is ~380% (within the 360–390% target) and holding-company cash is $351.4M against a $150M minimum. The 13.7x debt/equity and 0.14 Altman Z-Score from standard screens are meaningless for an insurer; the Piotroski partial score of 4/4 (positive net income, ROA, operating cash flow, and OCF exceeding net income) is the relevant health read.

Capital allocation is doing heavy lifting on per-share metrics. Operating cash flow was $675.7M in 2025, holding-company free cash flow $365.5M (2026 guidance $200–250M). The company repurchased 8.1M shares for $319.9M and paid $66.4M in dividends ($0.67/share, quarterly raised to $0.17). The four-quarter earnings record (beats of 11.0%, 20.6%, 39.3%, and 1.5%) supports confidence in forward estimates, though the magnitude of the mid-2025 beats partly reflects the operating-vs-GAAP reconciliation noise rather than clean upside.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a blend of a modest organic top line, a product inflection, and mechanical per-share accretion from buybacks. Headline revenue growth is slow (up just 0.9% in 2025 to $4.49B), so this is not a fast-growing franchise on a consolidated-revenue basis. The growth thesis operates one level down.

The Medicare Supplement reversal is the key forward development. Management explicitly flagged very strong Q4 2025 MedSupp sales "reflecting a growing shift in consumer preferences from Medicare Advantage to Medicare Supplement, reversing a decade-long trend," with most sales carrying January 2026 effective dates so the benefit lands in 2026. CNO is positively exposed on both sides, through underwriting margin on its own MedSupp policies and distribution fees on third-party MA plans, and approved rate actions (~10.2% requested / ~14.5% approved) grade in through Q4 2026. This is an industry tailwind rather than a company-specific hope.

Fixed indexed annuities remain the volume driver, riding retirement-income demand from aging boomers, but the segment's margin faces spread compression as high-spread legacy blocks surrender, and industry FIA competition is intense. The LTC pivot to short-duration products (95% of policies now ≤4-year benefit periods, 99% of 2025 new sales ≤2-year) reduces tail risk while the current claims experience is favorable.

Operating-EPS growth from ~$4.40 (2025) to the guidance midpoint (2026) looks flat, which is a fair caution: the growth is coming through the operating-ROE improvement target and continued share-count reduction rather than a step-change in earnings power. With ~$920M of authorization against a ~$5.0B market cap, buybacks can retire meaningful float, though the Section 382 ownership-change constraint (protecting the ~$711.7M DTA and ~$976.4M of NOLs) caps how aggressively CNO can repurchase. We score growth prospects at 5.8: directionally positive with a specific MedSupp catalyst, but modest in magnitude and partly financially engineered.

## Valuation Assessment

At $53.23, CNO trades at ~21x trailing GAAP EPS, but that trailing figure is depressed by non-operating items, so the more relevant lens is ~12x the 2026 operating-EPS guide midpoint of $4.35. On operating earnings the stock is not expensive in absolute terms, and it sits roughly in line with the Life & Health peer median P/E of 11.7x (PRI 11.7x, GL 11.4x; JXN and BHF carry negative trailing P/Es on their own accounting noise). Book value comparison is complicated by AOCI: 1.9x GAAP book but 1.3x ex-AOCI book, the latter reasonable for an ~11.4% operating-ROE insurer.

The problem is trajectory, not level. The stock has re-rated to a 52-week high, and the multiple has expanded faster than the operating earnings have grown; 2026 guidance at the midpoint is essentially flat versus 2025's operating result. Our valuation score for CNO has declined from 5.8 to 5.2 to 4.8 across the last three reviews because the price kept climbing into a flat-to-modestly-growing operating-EPS outlook. The cheapness that existed at $40–44 has been largely arbitraged away. The dividend yield of ~1.4% offers only thin support relative to the peer median of 0.8% and is not a valuation anchor on its own.

We are deliberately skeptical of treating the ~12x operating multiple as a signal to buy. There is no fresh catalyst that repriced the multiple lower; the multiple expanded into the run, and the operating-EPS growth to justify further re-rating is modest. Applying a valuation score below 5 reflects that the asymmetry has compressed: at ~12x flat-to-slightly-growing operating EPS with a regulatory overhang, the upside case requires the MedSupp tailwind and buyback to compound for multiple years while nothing goes wrong on Bermuda Re or LTC. That is a reasonable base case for holding, but not a mispricing to press. Fair value on ~13x the 2027 operating-EPS trajectory (assuming the 200 bps ROE improvement delivers ~$4.70) lands around $55–61, only modestly above spot.

## Competitive Landscape

CNO's moat is narrow and stable rather than widening. It succeeds by avoiding direct scale competition with giants (Northwestern Mutual, New York Life, UnitedHealthcare, AFLAC, Unum, Globe Life) and focusing on smaller face amounts, guaranteed/simplified-issue products, and worksite voluntary benefits in the middle-income senior segment. Its distribution reach, exclusive agents plus a large direct-response operation, is the core asset, along with the #2 individual LTC position.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the MA-to-MedSupp reversal plays directly to CNO's underwriting-plus-distribution model, and the expanding health margin is evidence of pricing/claims discipline. On the negative side, the ratings gap is a structural handicap that matters most in worksite sales where employers weigh carrier strength, and the annuity block is losing spread as high-margin legacy policies surrender into a competitive FIA market. The Optavise exit removes a subscale fee business but does not add a new competitive edge. Net, the position is stable-to-slightly-improving in health, stable-to-eroding in annuities, and unchanged in life. We score competitive position at 5.5: a durable niche without a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

The most consequential forward risk is the CNO Bermuda Re affiliated-reinsurance structure. CNO has ceded ~$10.7B of statutory reserves (roughly $8.8B of Bankers Life FIA reserves plus ~$1.9B of Washington National supplemental-health reserves, the latter effective October 2025) to its own wholly-owned Bermuda Class C reinsurer for capital efficiency. This sits in the path of the NAIC's newly adopted AG 55 (reinsurance asset-adequacy testing, effective YE2025) and potential BMA rule changes. If regulators or the BMA force additional reserves or recapture, the holding company must inject capital, competing directly with buybacks and dividends for the $200–250M of annual free cash flow. Standard screens miss this risk entirely, and it is the single most important item to monitor.

The legacy LTC tail is a second material risk. As the #2 individual LTC writer, CNO holds guaranteed-renewable blocks it cannot exit without regulatory approval, depending on state-by-state actuarially justified rate increases. A 10% adverse morbidity move would hit pre-tax income by ~$88M. Mitigants are meaningful: the short-duration product pivot, and $2.1B of legacy LTC ceded 100% to Wilton Re. Reinsurance counterparty concentration is a related exposure: $3.68B of receivables, heavily concentrated in Wilton Re ($2.35B, A+) and Jackson National ($827M), with CNO remaining primarily liable on default.

Interest-rate and spread risk is embedded in the model (asset duration 7.5 years vs. liability duration 8.1 years; a 10% rate rise cuts fixed-maturity fair value ~$691.7M), and ~$3.9B of liabilities are surrenderable without penalty. The $711.7M DTA and ~$976.4M NOLs create a Section 382 constraint on buyback pace. New 10-K disclosures added AG 55, VM-22 (principle-based reserving for non-variable annuities, effective 1/1/2026), NAIC CLO/structured-security RBC modeling changes, and expanded AI risk language. A governance monitoring flag: two consecutive Item 5.02 (officer/director) 8-Ks filed in January and February 2026 warrant follow-up for possible management transitions, though the specifics could not be independently confirmed.

Balancing these, the investment portfolio is genuinely high-quality (96% investment grade, modest CRE office at ~11% of the mortgage book, minimal alternatives), capital is comfortable (RBC ~380%, no debt maturities until 2029), and much of the LTC risk is ceded or de-risked. We score risk factors at 5.5: the Bermuda Re/AG 55 overhang and LTC tail are real and not fully priced, offset by strong portfolio quality and capital adequacy.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60–66):** The MA-to-MedSupp reversal proves durable and compounds through 2026–2027 effective dates, health margin continues expanding, and the 200 bps operating-ROE improvement target delivers operating EPS toward ~$4.70 by 2027. Buybacks retire another ~15% of the float over two years, and the multiple holds at ~13x. AG 55 proves manageable without forced recapture, and the LTC block continues its favorable claims trend. This is roughly 13x $4.90–5.00 operating EPS, ~13–24% upside.

**Bear Case ($40–43):** AG 55 or BMA changes force additional reserves at CNO Bermuda Re, requiring holding-company capital injection that curtails buybacks. Annuity spread compression accelerates as more high-spread policies surrender, LTC morbidity turns adverse (the ~$88M pre-tax sensitivity bites), and the MedSupp inflection proves shorter-lived than management projects. The multiple compresses back toward 9–10x operating EPS on regulatory uncertainty. This is ~19–25% downside, roughly the 52-week low area.

The realistic distribution is skewed neither strongly up nor down after the run. The operating fundamentals are sound and the buyback provides a floor on per-share metrics, but the multiple has already expanded and the regulatory overhang caps how much further it can re-rate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings print (the options backwardation is pricing that event), with AG 55 implementation and MedSupp rate-action grading through Q4 2026 as the key intermediate checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s that restores a ~9–10x operating-EPS multiple and re-creates asymmetric upside.
- Clear evidence that AG 55 asset-adequacy testing is manageable without material reserve additions or recapture at CNO Bermuda Re, removing the primary overhang.
- Operating-EPS estimate revisions turning meaningfully positive (guidance raised above the current $4.25–4.45 range) on sustained MedSupp momentum and continued health-margin expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A regulatory ruling (NAIC/BMA) forcing additional Bermuda Re reserves or recapture that compels a holding-company capital injection and curtails the buyback.
- Adverse LTC morbidity development materially exceeding the ~$88M pre-tax sensitivity, or a valuation allowance against the $711.7M DTA.
- Operating ROE reversing below ~9% or annuity spread compression accelerating beyond the current pace, undermining the 2027 ROE-improvement target.

## Conclusion

CNO is a well-run, well-capitalized middle-income senior insurer whose record 2025 operating results were obscured by non-operating GAAP noise that a standard screen would misread as a collapse. The forward case has genuine substance: the Medicare Supplement reversal is an industry tailwind, the health margin is expanding, the portfolio is high-quality, and the buyback meaningfully shrinks the float. The company's score trajectory (6.1 → 6.5 → 6.3) reflects improving financial-health and risk reads offset by a steadily declining valuation input, and the current data confirms that pattern: fundamentals are intact-to-improving, but the price has climbed to a 52-week high while operating EPS is guided roughly flat.

The result is a balanced setup. At ~12x forward operating earnings in line with peers, CNO is neither cheap enough to press nor expensive enough to avoid, and the CNO Bermuda Re / AG 55 regulatory overhang plus the LTC tail cap the upside asymmetry. This is a company worth owning for the operating-EPS compounding, the ~1.4% dividend, and the buyback-driven per-share accretion, but not one where the market is materially mispricing the stock today. We rate CNO HOLD with a constructive growth bias, and would upgrade to BUY on a pullback to the low-to-mid $40s or on regulatory clarity around the Bermuda reinsurance structure.
