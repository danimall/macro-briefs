## Executive Summary

CarMax is the largest used vehicle retailer in the United States with 256 stores, a captive finance arm servicing $16.4B in auto loans, and an omni-channel platform covering ~85% of the U.S. population. The company is in the midst of a severe earnings downturn: GAAP EPS collapsed from $3.21 in FY2025 to $1.68 in FY2026, driven by volume stagnation, margin compression, credit losses, and a $141M Edmunds goodwill impairment. The stock has declined 45% from its 52-week high of $71.99 to $39.60.

The bear case centers on three reinforcing headwinds: (1) management is deliberately cutting prices to drive volume, guiding to $300/unit lower margins in Q1 FY2027; (2) CAF net credit losses have risen to 2.34% with delinquencies accelerating and recovery rates falling; and (3) consensus estimates are being revised downward across every time horizon (FY2027 EPS cut 9%, FY2028 cut 17% in the last 30 days). A new CEO with no auto retail experience adds execution uncertainty. At 23.5x trailing P/E — more than double the peer median of 11.2x — the stock requires a volume-driven earnings recovery that remains unproven.

The bull case requires belief that price cuts generate sufficient volume to leverage the fixed cost base, that CAF's Tier 2 expansion pays off without excessive credit losses, and that $200M in SG&A savings offset margin headwinds. These are plausible outcomes over 18-24 months but carry substantial execution risk under a new management team navigating a hostile consumer environment.

## Company Overview

CarMax operates two segments: CarMax Sales Operations (retail/wholesale vehicle sales, EPPs, and related services generating $25.9B revenue) and CarMax Auto Finance (CAF), which originated $8.0B in loans in FY2026 and financed 42.4% of retail unit sales. The company sold 780,684 used vehicles and 538,203 wholesale units in FY2026. Its competitive advantages include scale (largest single used car retailer with 3.6% market share), a no-haggle pricing model, proprietary data from millions of transactions, and vertical integration across sourcing, reconditioning, financing, and remarketing.

CarMax self-sources ~987K vehicles annually directly from consumers, reducing auction dependency. The Edmunds digital property (acquired 2021 for ~$1.2B) provides automotive shopping content but has been written down entirely, with goodwill impaired by $141.3M in Q4 FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is negative.** Revenue declined from $29.7B (FY2023) to $25.9B (FY2026), a 13% decline over three years. Used unit volumes at 780,684 remain well below the pre-pandemic run rate and declined 2.0% on a comparable store basis in FY2026.

**Profitability is collapsing.** GAAP net income fell 50.6% YoY to $247.3M. Adjusted EPS declined 11% to $2.91. SG&A consumed 87.4% of gross profit for the full year, reaching 101% in Q4 — meaning operations generated no operating profit after selling costs in the final quarter. Gross profit per used unit declined to $2,253, and management guided to a further $300/unit reduction in Q1 FY2027.

**Balance sheet is leveraged.** Total debt-to-equity of 3.48x appears extreme, though $15.8B in non-recourse notes (funding matched auto loan receivables) are legally isolated. Corporate-level debt of ~$1.74B is manageable against $1.78B operating cash flow, but $841M is already drawn on the revolver and buybacks have been paused due to leverage exceeding target ranges. Cash on hand is only $123M.

**CAF credit quality is deteriorating.** Net credit losses rose to 2.34% (from 1.93% two years ago). Past-due accounts reached 5.11%. Recovery rates declined to 45.5%. The provision for loan losses increased 17% YoY to $391.2M. Industry-wide auto loan serious delinquencies are at their highest since 2010 (5.2% per NY Fed). Management is simultaneously expanding into riskier Tier 2/Tier 3 lending — a strategy that increases potential income per customer but amplifies downside risk in a recession.

**Cash flow was artificially boosted.** FY2026 operating cash flow of $1.78B included $909M from selling non-prime loans off-balance sheet. Stripping this out, underlying cash generation is approximately $870M — still adequate but declining with earnings.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is negative.** Consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.32 represents a 20% decline from adjusted FY2026 EPS of $2.91 (which itself declined 11% from FY2025). Estimates are being revised downward: current year EPS cut 9% in 30 days, next year cut 17%.

**The volume recovery thesis is unproven.** Management's strategy of cutting prices to drive volume is economically sound for a high-fixed-cost business, but Q4 FY2026 showed negative operating leverage (SG&A at 101% of gross profit), and comparable store sales declined 2.0% in FY2026 despite an improving supply environment. Market share declined in H2 calendar 2025 from 3.7% to 3.6%.

**SG&A savings provide partial offset.** The $200M annualized savings target (up from $150M) includes a 30% reduction in customer experience center workforce and other restructuring. Even if fully realized by end of FY2027, this provides approximately $1.37/share in pre-tax benefit — insufficient to offset the combined margin compression and volume weakness.

**Tariff dynamics are ambiguous.** The 25% auto import tariff pushes new car prices higher (10.4% YoY per KBB), theoretically driving buyers toward used vehicles. However, it also raises reconditioning parts costs and could constrain future supply of affordable late-model used cars. The net effect is uncertain and does not constitute a reliable growth catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

KMX trades at 23.5x trailing EPS of $1.68 and approximately 13.6x adjusted FY2026 EPS of $2.91. On forward estimates (FY2027E EPS $2.32), the stock trades at approximately 17x. The peer median P/E is 11.2x, with direct competitors LAD at 10.0x, AN at 11.2x, GPI at 13.3x, and ABG at 7.0x.

The premium to peers historically reflected CarMax's scale advantages, captive finance earnings, and perceived growth trajectory. With growth now negative, margins compressing, and CAF quality deteriorating, the premium is difficult to justify. At the peer median of 11.2x on $2.32 FY2027E EPS, fair value would be approximately $26 — 34% below the current price. Even at a generous 15x multiple (reflecting scale premium), fair value is approximately $35.

The analyst consensus median target is $39.00 with a mean of $42.17. Four analysts rate the stock Strong Sell and only two rate it Buy, with 13 at Hold. The wide range ($19 to $99) reflects extreme uncertainty about the trajectory.

The stock's 45% decline from highs does not make it cheap when the earnings power being capitalized has itself declined 50%+. The current P/E remains elevated because trailing earnings include the disastrous Q4 FY2026 (net loss of $120.7M). On any normalized earnings basis, the stock still carries a significant premium to franchised dealer peers.

## Competitive Landscape

CarMax's competitive position is stable but not improving. Market share declined from 3.7% to 3.6% in H2 2025. Key competitive dynamics:

**Carvana** has emerged from a debt restructuring with a leaner cost structure and is gaining digital market share. Carvana's fully online model appeals to younger buyers, and its restoration of operational viability removes what was previously a structural competitive advantage for CarMax (Carvana's near-failure in 2022-23 reduced competitive pressure temporarily).

**Franchised dealers** operate certified pre-owned programs with manufacturer backing, access to warranty revenue, and local customer relationships. They dominate the 1-3 year old vehicle segment.

**Digital platforms** (Cars.com, Facebook Marketplace) facilitate private sales that bypass all retailers. CarMax's no-haggle value proposition is weakened when consumers can easily compare prices online.

CarMax retains meaningful advantages in self-sourcing (987K vehicles purchased from consumers), proprietary data from millions of transactions, and integrated finance. These advantages are durable but not translating into growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit cycle risk is the most dangerous near-term threat.** CAF's $16.4B portfolio is experiencing rising losses at the same time management is expanding into riskier tiers. A 10% worsening in loss assumptions adds ~$52M to the allowance. In a recession scenario with unemployment above 5%, credit losses could reach 3.5-4.0%, implying $150-250M in additional provisions beyond current levels.

**Consumer stress is acute and worsening.** Auto loan rates above 11% APR, total auto loan balances at $1.67 trillion, and elevated vehicle prices constrain the customer base. CarMax's sub-$20K segment — where they're trying to grow — is precisely the demographic most affected by these pressures.

**CEO execution risk is elevated.** Keith Barr brings hospitality industry experience (IHG Hotels) but no auto retail background. The strategic pivot he inherits (price cuts + Tier 2 lending expansion + cost reduction) requires deep operational knowledge of vehicle merchandising, reconditioning economics, and credit risk management. The leadership transition involving three CEOs in approximately 4 months signals organizational instability.

**Leverage constrains optionality.** With buybacks paused and $841M drawn on the revolver, CarMax has limited financial flexibility to weather a prolonged downturn or invest in market share gains.

### Options Market Signal

The OI put/call ratio of 2.09 confirms bearish institutional positioning, consistent with 11.6% short interest. However, the volume P/C ratio of 0.61 and negative put/call skew (-3.7%) suggest near-term call buying activity, possibly positioning for a short squeeze or bounce. The flat term structure (45.8% near vs. 47.3% far) indicates the market sees no resolution of uncertainty in either direction, consistent with a prolonged deterioration rather than a single event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-60, +39-52% upside)
- Price cuts generate 10-15% volume growth in FY2027, leveraging fixed costs
- $200M SG&A savings flow through to earnings by H2 FY2027
- CAF Tier 2 expansion adds $100-120M in annual pre-tax income without excessive losses
- Tariff-driven new car price inflation permanently shifts demand toward used market
- New CEO brings fresh perspective on digital transformation and cost discipline
- Starboard activist involvement accelerates operational improvement

### Bear Case ($22-28, 29-44% downside)
- Price cuts fail to generate proportional volume (consumers are too stretched)
- CAF credit losses reach 3.0-3.5% in a consumer recession, requiring $200M+ incremental provisioning
- Market share continues to erode to Carvana and franchised dealer CPO programs
- EPS declines to $1.50-2.00 range; stock re-rates to 15x = $22-30
- Leverage constraints prevent capital return and limit strategic flexibility
- New CEO pivot takes 18+ months to gain traction; activist pressure creates short-termism

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Quarterly earnings reports (next expected July 2026 for Q1 FY2027) should confirm the guided $300/unit margin compression. If volume doesn't meaningfully accelerate alongside the margin cut, EPS could come in below the already-reduced $0.92 consensus. Consumer earnings in May 2026 (HD, TGT, WMT) may also serve as negative sentiment catalysts for consumer discretionary broadly.

**Timing window:** July 2026 earnings is the primary near-term catalyst. Credit deterioration in CAF is an ongoing monthly data point that manifests in quarterly provision adjustments.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 11.6% of float with 3.8 days to cover, the short is moderately crowded. Not extreme enough to create high squeeze risk, but meaningful enough that any positive surprise (volume beat, take-out speculation) could generate a 15-20% squeeze. Institutional ownership is high.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options are cheap to historical (IV 45.8% vs HV 54.9%), which is unusual for a name under fundamental pressure — may reflect short-sellers using stock rather than options. OI P/C at 2.09 confirms institutional bearish conviction. Borrow cost likely manageable given the large float.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $22-28 implies 29-44% downside from $39.60. Upside risk to $50-55 on any volume inflection or macro improvement (26-39% upside risk). Risk/reward approximately 1.2:1 on the short — below the 2:1 threshold for an active recommendation.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is fundamentally sound (margin compression + credit deterioration + premium valuation vs. peers), but the 1.2:1 risk/reward is insufficient given moderate squeeze risk, Starboard activist involvement creating potential positive optionality, and the possibility that tariff-driven demand shifts could surprise to the upside. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Consider upgrading to HOLD/BUY if:
1. Comparable store used unit sales turn positive for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the price-for-volume strategy is working
2. CAF net credit losses stabilize below 2.5% for two consecutive quarters
3. Stock trades below 12x forward EPS (approximately $28 on current estimates), offering genuine value vs. peers
4. New CEO articulates a credible strategic plan with measurable milestones (expected at next analyst day)

**Timeline:** The fundamental headwinds (margin compression, credit losses, estimate revisions) are likely to persist for at least 2-3 quarters. The earliest credible positive inflection would be Q2-Q3 FY2027 (reported October 2026-January 2027) if Q1 volume responds to price cuts. Until then, the stock remains in estimate-reduction mode with no positive catalyst.

## Conclusion

CarMax is a structurally sound business experiencing a cyclical and operational trough simultaneously. The company's scale, vertical integration, and data advantages are real, but they are not translating into growth or profitability in the current environment. At 23.5x trailing earnings (2x the peer median), the stock carries a premium that requires earnings recovery evidence rather than hope. Consensus estimates are declining across all horizons, CAF credit losses are accelerating, the new CEO is untested in the industry, and consumer affordability remains deeply challenged. The 45% price decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration, not market overreaction. AVOID until volume recovery is demonstrated in reported results and credit loss trends stabilize.
