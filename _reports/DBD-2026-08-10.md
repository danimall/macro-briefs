## Executive Summary

Diebold Nixdorf is a post-Chapter-11 cash-handling infrastructure company whose equity has roughly tripled since emergence in August 2023 (proxy-disclosed cumulative TSR index of 330.04 at 12/31/25 versus a 17-company peer group at 124.03). At $74.31 the stock is 19.3% below its July 27 high of $92.09, most of that decline occurring in a single 17% session on July 29 when Q2 results landed alongside a reaffirmed full-year outlook. The valuation is low on cash metrics: enterprise value of approximately $3,216M against a guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA midpoint of $522.5M is 6.2x, and guided free cash flow of $255-270M against $2,527M of market capitalization is a 10.4% yield. Peer median P/E in electronic equipment sits at 32.5; DBD is 13.8x current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $5.37 and 10.5x next-year consensus of $7.02.

The reason for the discount is visible in the segment detail. Reported revenue growth in both FY2025 (+1.5%) and H1 2026 (+3.8%) was essentially all currency. Backing out the FX percentage points the company itself discloses, total constant-currency revenue was approximately -0.6% in Q2 and -0.4% in H1, with Banking — 69-74% of the business — down roughly 8.4% in Q2 and 7.2% in H1 on a constant-currency basis. Retail grew about 20% constant currency but at roughly 60% of Banking's gross and operating margin, so the mix shift dilutes consolidated gross profit. Total segment operating profit fell 1.3% year over year in Q2 ($150.2M to $148.3M); consolidated operating profit rose only because unallocated corporate charges fell $11.1M. Q2 gross margin of 25.74% becomes 24.35% once the disclosed $13.0M of U.S. tariff recoveries is removed, versus 25.57% a year earlier, an underlying decline of roughly 120bps against a guidance component calling for 25-50bps of expansion.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case and is better than standardized distress screens imply: $950M of 7.75% senior secured notes due March 2030 with nothing maturing before then, a fully undrawn $310M revolver, $282.4M of cash, net debt of $689.3M (about 1.3x guided EBITDA), roughly 5.5x EBITDA-to-cash-interest coverage, and three rating agencies that upgraded or initiated higher within the past twelve months (S&P B+ 9/18/25, Moody's B1 12/16/25, Fitch BB- 4/23/26). Solvency is not the question; earnings durability is. We rate the shares HOLD with a base case of $84 (+13%), a bull case of $100 (+35%) and a bear case of $55 (-26%), giving a probability-weighted expected return in the high single digits — positive, but insufficient margin of safety to justify new capital while the Banking trajectory remains unresolved.

## Company Overview

Diebold Nixdorf designs, manufactures and services the physical and software infrastructure banks and retailers use to move cash and process transactions, operating in more than 100 countries with approximately 20,000 employees. Two segments:

**Banking (73.5% of FY2025 revenue, $2,797.0M):** ATMs, cash recyclers, dispensers and teller automation under the DN Series brand; the Vynamic software portfolio; and installation, maintenance and managed services on a large installed fleet. The stated growth vector is replacing dispense-only ATMs with recyclers (DN600, DN500) and a new multi-vendor service capability in North America that services competitors' hardware under a single contract.

**Retail (26.5%, $1,008.7M):** BEETLE electronic point-of-sale, DN Series EASY ONE self-checkout, EASY MAX kiosks, the Vynamic Retail Platform, and Vynamic Smart Vision, an AI computer-vision product for shrink, produce recognition and age verification. The footprint is historically European (the 10-K cites 21 of the top 25 European retailers), with North America framed as the expansion opportunity.

Services exceeds 56% of revenue, and approximately 60% of revenue is recognized over time. The recurring service base attached to a low-growth hardware franchise is the structural asset here.

Fresh-start accounting from the August 11, 2023 emergence shapes the reported financials. Retained earnings were reset to zero and stood at only $112.4M at 6/30/26 against $3,854.4M of assets. The balance sheet carries $743.2M of stepped-up intangibles generating roughly $100M of annual non-cash amortization, which is the primary driver of the roughly 2x gap between guided adjusted EPS ($5.25-5.75) and trailing GAAP diluted EPS of $2.54. Authorized common stock is only 45,000,000 shares against 37,906,017 issued, which limits headroom for equity-funded transactions without a charter amendment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 net sales were $3,805.7M, +1.5%, with MD&A attributing the increase first to currency. H1 2026 revenue of $1,822.5M was +3.8% reported against a disclosed +4.2 points of FX. The segment split is where the information sits:

| H1 2026 | Revenue | Reported | FX pts | Approx. constant currency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Banking | $1,262.3M | -3.5% | +3.7 | ≈ -7.2% |
| Retail | $560.2M | +25.1% | +5.7 | ≈ +19.4% |
| Total | $1,822.5M | +3.8% | +4.2 | ≈ -0.4% |

Banking fell from 74.2% to 68.6% of Q2 revenue in one year. Management attributes the decline to delivery timing and to specific geographies (Middle East, Africa, Europe in Q2; North America and Europe in H1), and the supporting evidence is real: product backlog rebuilt sequentially from $733.1M at 12/31/25 to approximately $790M at 3/31/26, and Q2 order entry rose 3% year over year. Two quarters is not a trend. Product backlog at 12/31/25 was also down 7.2% from $790.1M a year earlier, no backlog is measured for the service business, and backlog orders are cancellable without penalty, so the metric supports the timing story without settling it.

**Margins.** Reported Q2 gross margin was 25.74% against 25.57%. The 10-Q discloses $10.0M of tariff recoveries in products and $3.0M in services; removing $13.0M gives 24.35%, roughly 120bps lower year over year. The filing does not state whether these recoveries are refunds of previously expensed duties (one-time) or recurring customer surcharges; the cash-flow discussion's reference to "approximately $13.0 U.S. tariff refunds received" tilts toward the former, but the ambiguity is genuine and material because 25-50bps of gross margin expansion is an explicit guidance component.

Service margin has now declined for consecutive periods (-80bps in FY2025 to 24.0%, -50bps in Q2 2026 to 23.6%), attributed to a technician tool platform and a centralized repair center. Product margin gains (+280bps FY2025, +110bps Q2) are attributed to mix and pricing, partially offset by higher memory cost.

**Where the profit growth comes from.** Q2 segment operating profit was $148.3M versus $150.2M, down 1.3% (Banking -7.1% to $116.2M; Retail +27.9% to $32.1M). Consolidated operating profit nonetheless rose to $57.4M from $56.2M because unallocated corporate charges fell to $66.0M from $77.1M. For H1, segment profit rose 2.5% while corporate charges fell $15.3M. The Operational Evolution Program initiated in Q4 2025 carries $105M of total expected cost, of which $70.6M was already charged through 6/30/26 ($43.7M in H1 2026 alone). Overhead removal at a post-bankruptcy company with a bloated cost base is the right action and the savings can be permanent, but the program is two-thirds expensed and cannot substitute for segment growth beyond 2027.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $18.1M from $45.8M; less $13.4M of capex and $12.9M of capitalized software, free cash flow was -$8.2M. Full-year guidance is $255-270M. Before treating that as a guidance failure in progress, note the precedent: H1 2025 free cash flow on the same definition was $18.8M against FY2025 operating cash flow of $300.7M, implying H2 2025 operating cash flow of $254.9M. Extreme second-half concentration is this company's structural pattern, and we are deliberately not run-rating H1.

The composition still warrants attention. H1 sources included a $85.0M accounts-payable build that MD&A attributes to "reduced supplier payments" and $26.8M from receivables; uses included $82.8M of inventory build, $30.1M of indirect taxes, $32.0M of accrued salaries (including $20.6M of severance payouts) and $30.6M of income taxes. Cash interest paid rose to $37.4M from $21.7M. Inventories reached $595.9M, with finished goods up 39% to $214.1M, attributed to memory costs, higher finished goods and seasonality. If that inventory is pre-positioned for H2 deliveries it converts to cash; if the payables stretch unwinds simultaneously, the H2 requirement becomes harder.

**Tax.** FY2025's 19.3% effective rate was flattered by roughly $54M of discrete items (a $21.4M valuation-allowance release in Canada and Mexico, $13.6M of unrecognized tax benefits, and an $18.7M German rate-driven deferred write-down). Q2 2026's rate was 52.9% (H1 51.3%), attributed in Note 8 "primarily due to decreased interest expense deductibility in 2026." That is the main reason H1 GAAP net income was only $21.7M on $90.0M of operating profit. Some of this may be interim estimation noise, but the company's own attribution points to a structural limitation that compresses both GAAP EPS and cash taxes as EBITDA grows.

**Capital structure.** Total debt principal of $971.7M, of which $950.0M is the 7.75% notes due March 2030. Revolver ($310M, matures December 2029) fully undrawn with $285.7M available after letters of credit. The contractual obligations table shows zero debt maturing inside three years. Tangible book equity is negative (goodwill $627.8M plus intangibles $743.2M against book equity of $1,099.9M), which for a services business generating a 10% FCF yield is a fresh-start accounting artifact rather than a solvency signal. The Altman Z of 1.96 is similarly distorted: substituting market equity for book equity in the formula adds roughly 0.31 to the score, and the retained-earnings term reflects the 2023 reset rather than earning power.

## Growth Analysis

DBD has delivered no constant-currency revenue growth for two consecutive years, and the equity's earnings trajectory is driven by three mechanical levers rather than volume.

**Lever one: cost-out.** The $105M OEP plus North Canton subassembly footprint reduction of about 40% and a roughly halved Brazilian manufacturing footprint. This is producing the current profit growth and is finite.

**Lever two: amortization roll-off.** The $104.0M technology know-how tranche amortizes over 3.2 years. As it rolls off, GAAP EPS rises with no operational change, narrowing the GAAP/non-GAAP gap.

**Lever three: share count.** Shares fell from 35,384,690 at 12/31/25 to 34,004,734 at 6/30/26, -3.9% in six months, and roughly 9.6% below FY2024 basic weighted-average shares of 37.6M. Management has stated an intent to return a majority of free cash flow. At the guided FCF level, continued repurchase at current prices supports roughly 8-10% annual per-share accretion independent of operating growth.

Consensus reflects this: current-year EPS of $5.37 rising to $7.02 next year is +31% on essentially flat revenue. Estimate revisions do not confirm an inflection — the current quarter was cut 10.1% (from $1.45 to $1.31) while the next quarter rose 2.2% and next year rose 3.3%. Consensus has moved earnings from Q3 into Q4 and nudged the out-year higher. This is below the 30% ninety-day revision velocity that would constitute a genuine inflection signal, and no such signal is present here.

Retail is the one genuine volume growth engine: approximately 20% constant-currency growth, with third-party summaries of company commentary citing roughly 170% European point-of-sale growth in Q2 and near-70% North American growth off a modest base in Q1, plus named-category wins (a top-10 fuel and convenience chain, a large pharmacy chain, a regional grocer). Vynamic Smart Vision gives the segment an AI-adjacent software story. The constraint is arithmetic: Retail carried a 22.0% gross margin in Q2 versus Banking's 28.5%, so every point of mix shift compresses consolidated gross profit until Retail scales.

The long-duration question is terminal value. The 10-K risk factors name the threat directly — competition from Venmo, Zelle, Apple Pay and digital currencies. Cash-handling volumes will not grow indefinitely, and the answer to that risk is the managed-services and multi-vendor-service pivot, which is not yet visible in Banking's revenue line.

The profile's supplied P/E field of 24.47 cannot be reconciled with $74.31 and $2.54 of trailing EPS; our own arithmetic above is used throughout.

Peer medians in electronic equipment sit at a 32.5 P/E (CXT 22.0, OSIS 27.0, BMI 32.5, NSSC 40.1, MIR 177.7). DBD at 13.8x current-year adjusted EPS trades at roughly 42% of that median. A large part of that discount is deserved — these peers are growing organically, DBD is not; DBD carries 1.3x net leverage and a 51-53% effective tax rate; and DBD's terminal market is cash. The relevant question is whether the discount is too wide.

Three things argue it is somewhat too wide. First, the revenue guide is not demanding: FY2026 midpoint of $3.90B less H1 actual of $1,822.5M implies H2 revenue of about $2,077M against H2 2025's $2,049.4M, or +1.4% growth, with H1 representing 46.7% of the implied year versus 46.1% in FY2025. Second, the cash return is real and mechanical: $57.0M of authorization remained at 6/30/26 (2.3% of current market cap), and the blended cost of ~$243M of cumulative repurchases is approximately $63.8, below today's price. Third, if the Brink's/NCR Atleos transaction value of $6.6bn against roughly $4.4B of Atleos revenue is enterprise value, the implied ~1.5x revenue multiple is roughly double DBD's 0.82x — an illustrative rather than validated comparable, since the figure comes from a single wire report and the businesses' recurring-revenue mixes differ.

Three things argue the discount is warranted. First, the EBITDA denominator is not verified: reconstructing H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA from the segment note yields roughly $205M, while back-solving from disclosed growth percentages yields roughly $220M, so H2 must deliver approximately $290-330M, or 57-60% of the year. Second, roughly $13M of Q2 gross profit and a similar amount of H1 operating cash flow came from tariff recoveries of undisclosed durability. Third, memory cost inflation — quantified by management at $3-5M in Q1 alone — is being capitalized into a $74.9M inventory build now and will flow through COGS over the next two to three quarters, concentrated in the memory-heavy, lower-margin EPOS line.

Consensus targets of $95-100 (mean $98.33) from three analysts sit 28-35% above spot. With coverage this thin, no recorded revisions and a stock that fell 17% in a single session, these targets are corroboration at best and possibly stale. We do not treat them as a floor.

**Our base case is $84**, roughly 12x next-year consensus EPS of $7.02, cross-checked at 6.9x EV/EBITDA on the guidance midpoint and an 8.7% FCF yield. That is +13% from $74.31.

## Competitive Landscape

Named banking-hardware competitors are NCR Atleos, Hyosung TNS, Hitachi Channel Solutions, OKI Electric, GRG Banking, Glory and Triton; banking software competitors include KAL, Fiserv, Auriga and banks' internal teams; retail competitors are NCR Voyix, Toshiba, Fujitsu, Elo Touch and Glory, with Oracle, Aptos and GK Software on the software side.

The defining industry event is Brink's acquisition of NCR Atleos, cleared by India's competition authority on July 22, 2026 and guided by Brink's toward an early-Q1 close. It cuts both ways. DBD's largest banking-hardware competitor will spend 2027 inside an integration, which is favorable timing for DBD's stated North American multi-vendor service push and for banks re-tendering during an ownership change. Against that, the combined entity pairs Brink's cash-in-transit network with Atleos's hardware, software and Allpoint ATM network, a more vertically integrated ATM-as-a-service competitor in precisely the outsourced managed-services category DBD identifies as its growth area.

The read-across data is unflattering. Atleos reported Q2 CY2026 revenue flat year over year at approximately $1.1B while DBD Banking fell roughly 8% in constant currency. Brink's reported +7.1% revenue growth with a fourteenth consecutive quarter of mid-teens-or-better organic growth in its cash-management services lines. That combination weakens a pure "industry softness" explanation for DBD's Banking decline and raises the probability of share loss or an unusually lumpy order book. The comparison is not clean — Atleos's revenue includes a large ATM-network business DBD does not replicate, and DBD's Banking weakness was concentrated in Europe, MEA and emerging markets — but Brink's growth rate in outsourced cash services indicates the segment of the market DBD is targeting is expanding at a pace DBD is not capturing.

Retail is the clear positive: roughly 20% constant-currency growth against NCR Voyix suggests share capture in a hardware refresh cycle. The driver (competitor disruption, a European POS replacement wave, or an OS-driven refresh) is not established.

Net trajectory: gaining in a quarter of the business, losing or stagnating in three-quarters of it.

## Risk Assessment

**Banking constant-currency contraction.** The most material item. If the ~7-8% decline reflects replacement-cycle digestion after the DN Series recycler upgrade wave or share loss rather than delivery timing, the FY2026 revenue guide and the $800M 2025-2027 cumulative FCF target become materially harder.

**Q4-loaded cash flow.** With H1 FCF negative against full-year guidance, essentially the entire year resolves in H2 and predominantly in Q4. Seasonality precedent supports this, but the H1 sources included a payables stretch that cannot repeat.

**Tariff-recovery durability and memory cost inflation.** Together these determine whether the guided 25-50bps of gross margin expansion is achievable. Ex-tariff Q2 gross margin declined roughly 120bps, and higher-cost inventory flows to COGS with a two-to-three-quarter lag.

**Tax rate.** A 51-53% effective rate driven by limited interest deductibility on roughly $74M of annual cash coupon is a persistent drag on GAAP EPS and cash taxes.

**FX.** FY2025 recorded a $44.1M net FX loss, and the 10-K sensitivity puts a hypothetical 10% move at $37.8-46.2M of operating income — 16-19% of FY2025's $242.0M. Hedging has helped (H1 2026 FX loss $4.8M versus $40.7M), but a dollar reversal also removes the 2-4 points of currency tailwind carrying reported revenue.

**Share overhang.** Millstreet Capital Management sold approximately 1,112,240 shares between April 8 and June 26, 2026 at $82.08-$85.43, ending at 4,104,432 shares, roughly 12.1% of shares outstanding. A holder demonstrably willing to sell in the low-to-mid $80s is a supply cap on the next rally, and a portion of the company's Q2 buyback (751,648 shares at an average $79.82) absorbed that exit.

**Contingencies.** Indirect tax matters estimated at up to $44.9M above recorded liabilities; $140.6M of guarantees and standby letters of credit with no associated liability; annual goodwill impairment testing in Q4 against $627.8M of goodwill.

**Coverage gaps.** FY2026 guidance ranges are sourced from third-party summaries of company calls rather than a filing; FY2025 reported adjusted EBITDA was not obtained; Item 1A extraction was partial; and the contents of two Item 5.02 8-Ks (10/9/25 and 2/25/26) are unverified. These do not change the direction of the analysis but they widen the confidence interval around the EBITDA baseline.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 45.3% against 40.0% one-year realized is modestly rich (69th percentile within the current chain), with the term structure in backwardation (45.3% at the August 21 tenor versus 35.8% at six months) and an implied move of ±6.3% on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew of -2.1% is balanced and put/call open interest of 1.39 is a mild put tilt rather than a hedging build. With no earnings until late October, the near-term backwardation reads as residual uncertainty following the July 29 gap rather than positioning ahead of a dated catalyst. Combined with 16.5% short interest and 8.8 days to cover, the setup is two-sided: a clean Q3 print showing inventory conversion and stable Banking orders could force covering, while the same short base will press any further Banking deterioration. The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty without directional conviction, which is consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $100 (+35%)

Banking weakness proves to be delivery timing, and the ~$790M product backlog plus +3% order entry convert in H2. FY2026 lands at the upper end of guidance ($3.94B revenue, $535M adjusted EBITDA, $270M FCF). Q4 delivers $290-330M of adjusted EBITDA and the inventory build converts to cash without the payables stretch unwinding. The OEP savings hold as permanent overhead reduction, and Retail's segment operating margin continues expanding (already +40bps year over year) as European POS and North American self-checkout scale. Brink's spends 2027 integrating Atleos, and DBD's multi-vendor service offering wins re-tendered North American fleets. Management completes the remaining $57M of authorization and re-ups, taking share count below 33M. Amortization of the 3.2-year technology know-how tranche rolls off, lifting GAAP EPS mechanically. At 7.8x EV/EBITDA on $535M with a lower share count, the equity reaches roughly $100-107, consistent with the $95-100 analyst target band.

### Bear Case — $55 (-26%)

Banking's decline is structural — recycler replacement demand front-loaded into 2023-2025, with Brink's/Atleos taking outsourced managed-services share. Constant-currency revenue turns negative in FY2027 as the currency tailwind reverses. Tariff recoveries prove non-recurring, removing roughly $13M of quarterly gross profit, while capitalized memory costs flow through COGS and consume the guided 25-50bps of margin expansion. The OEP completes with $105M spent and no further cost-out lever available, so consolidated operating profit tracks segment profit, which was already declining. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA falls toward $450M; at 5.5x, enterprise value of about $2,475M less roughly $680M of net debt gives $1,795M of equity, or approximately $53 per share against a 52-week low of $54.48. A Q4 free-cash-flow miss would compound this by removing the buyback support that has driven per-share accretion.

### Probability weighting

Base $84 (45%), bull $100 (25%), bear $55 (30%) implies an expected return of roughly +7% over twelve months. Positive, but thin against a -26% bear branch, and dependent on a Q4 cash-flow outcome that will not be verifiable until February 2027.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026. That print is the first read on whether the $74.9M inventory build converts, whether the $85.0M payables position unwinds, whether Banking orders stabilize, and whether tariff recoveries repeat. Full resolution of the FCF guide requires the Q4/FY print in February 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Banking constant-currency revenue returns to flat or better in Q3, with product backlog holding at or above $790M. This would validate management's timing explanation and remove the central bear plank.
2. Q3 operating cash flow of $120M or more with inventories declining sequentially and accounts payable not falling by more than $30M — evidence the H2 cash build is operational rather than working-capital borrowing.
3. Gross margin excluding tariff recoveries at or above 25.0% in Q3, demonstrating that pricing is offsetting memory cost inflation.
4. Price weakness toward $62-65 (roughly 5.3x EV/EBITDA and a 12.5% FCF yield, near the blended repurchase cost of ~$63.8) with fundamentals unchanged would make the risk/reward compelling regardless of the Banking question.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any reduction to the FY2026 free cash flow guidance of $255-270M, or Q3 FCF materially below the roughly $90-110M needed to make the annual figure plausible.
2. A third consecutive quarter of Banking constant-currency decline of 5% or worse, which would shift the base case from timing to structural share loss.
3. Suspension or material slowdown of the repurchase program after the remaining $57M is exhausted without re-authorization, removing the per-share accretion lever.
4. Tariff recoveries absent in Q3 with gross margin below 24.0%, confirming underlying margin compression.

**What we would not treat as a signal:** further Millstreet distribution at prices near or above $80. A concentrated ex-creditor register normalizes through this kind of selling, and the buyback has absorbed it at accretive levels relative to blended cost.

## Conclusion

Diebold Nixdorf presents a legitimate cash-return proposition at a low multiple: 6.2x EV/EBITDA, a 10.4% free cash flow yield on guidance, 1.3x net leverage with nothing maturing before March 2030, an undrawn revolver, three rating upgrades in twelve months, and a share count falling roughly 8-10% annually. Management's incentive design is well constructed for this business — including constant-currency revenue as a performance measure fits a company whose reported growth is currency — and the three most senior operating executives bought stock in the open market between $72.29 and $79.56, including the CFO two days after the Q2 print at a price below today's.

The offsetting evidence is specific and quantified. Constant-currency revenue has been flat for two years. Banking, at 69-74% of the business, is contracting roughly 7-8% in constant currency while its closest comparable reported flat revenue. Q2 gross margin declined about 120bps once $13.0M of tariff recoveries of undisclosed durability are excluded. Segment operating profit fell 1.3% year over year, with the consolidated improvement supplied by $11.1M of corporate overhead removal from a program that is already two-thirds expensed. The effective tax rate of 51-53% caps GAAP earnings conversion. And essentially the entire year's free cash flow must arrive in H2, following an H1 in which operating cash flow was supported by an $85.0M payables stretch.

The central question — timing versus share loss in Banking — cannot be answered from the available disclosure, and it determines whether the base case is $84 or the bear case is $55. A probability-weighted expected return in the high single digits does not clear the bar for new capital at this price. Existing holders are compensated by an accretive buyback and a well-termed capital structure and should hold into the late-October print. Prospective buyers have a defined entry: the low $60s, or a Q3 report that shows Banking stabilizing and cash converting.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $84 (+13%), bull $100 (+35%), bear $55 (-26%).
