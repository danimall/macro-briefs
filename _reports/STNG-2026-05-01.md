## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is a $3.8B product tanker operator with ~89 vessels generating FY2025 net income of $344M ($7.03 diluted EPS) despite a 30% revenue decline from peak FY2024 levels. The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a confirmed rate inflection in Q1 2026 with LR2 TCE rates jumping 34% sequentially to $46,000/day, (2) a fortress balance sheet with $309M net cash and $1.7B total liquidity providing 45% liquidity-to-market-cap coverage, and (3) consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.88 — revised up 63% in the last 30 days — which places the stock at ~8.2x forward earnings versus an 18.9x peer median.

The stock has rallied 115% over the past year and trades within 2% of its 52-week high ($82.75), which creates a legitimate entry-timing concern. The magnitude of estimate revisions and the structural supply constraints in the product tanker market (limited orderbook from 2019-2022 underinvestment, Red Sea disruptions extending ton-miles, and refinery capacity shifts lengthening haul routes) suggest the rate recovery has further room to run. The primary risk is the inherent cyclicality of the freight rate market: a diplomatic resolution in the Red Sea or an industry-wide newbuilding wave could compress rates materially. At 8.2x forward earnings with net cash, the stock prices in meaningful rate normalization that may not arrive for 12-18 months.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates one of the largest publicly listed pure-play product tanker fleets globally, comprising approximately 37 LR2 tankers, 42 MR tankers, and 14 Handymax tankers with an average fleet age of ~9.8 years. Incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in Monaco, the company files as a foreign private issuer on Form 20-F. Approximately 72 of ~90 vessels operate through Scorpio commercial pools in the spot market, giving the company extreme operating leverage to prevailing freight rates. Technical management is outsourced to Scorpio Ship Management (SSM), controlled by the Lolli-Ghetti family — a related-party arrangement that bears ongoing governance monitoring.

The company is in an active fleet renewal phase, selling 2014-2016 vintage vessels at elevated secondhand prices ($32-57.5M per vessel) and ordering newbuildings for delivery through 2029, including a strategic pivot into the crude tanker segment with two VLCC orders ($128M each) at Hanwha Ocean for 2028 delivery.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 revenue of $766.2M (pool and spot) declined 30% from FY2024's $1,090.1M, reflecting lower average spot rates. Net income was $344.3M ($7.03 diluted EPS), with adjusted net income of $269.5M ($5.79 adjusted EPS) after stripping out $45.5M in vessel sale gains and $35.2M in fair value gains on the DHT Holdings stake (since liquidated). Q4 2025 adjusted EPS of $1.62 beat consensus of $1.61, marking the 4th consecutive quarterly beat, with surprises ranging from +0.7% to +37.6%.

**Balance Sheet:** The balance sheet transformation is the most compelling financial data point. STNG moved from $293M net debt (September 2025) to $309M net cash (February 2026) in approximately four months through vessel sale proceeds, $154.6M of unscheduled debt prepayments (covering amortization through December 2027), liquidation of the DHT Holdings equity position (~$54M), and operating cash flow. Total gross debt fell to $628M while cash grew to $937M. Combined with $767M of undrawn revolver capacity, total liquidity stands at ~$1.7B (45% of market cap). This is extraordinary financial flexibility for a cyclical shipper.

**Capital Return:** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.45 ($1.80 annualized, ~2.2% yield). $173.4M remains under the share repurchase authorization, and diluted share count declined from ~48.5M to ~47.1M (weighted average), indicating active buybacks. Combined with the dividend, STNG is returning capital at a 4-5% annualized rate.

**Q1 2026 Rate Data:** Booked TCE rates for Q1 2026 show LR2s at $46,000/day (+34% from Q4's $34,364), MRs at $27,500/day (+13%), and Handymax at $25,500/day (+6%). The LR2 uplift alone translates to roughly $39M of incremental quarterly revenue from the ~37 LR2 vessels. Q1 2026 results are due May 5, 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision data is among the strongest for any cyclical name in recent months. Current quarter (Q1 2026) EPS estimates have risen from $1.83 to $2.62 over 90 days (+43.4%), with the 30-day revision alone at +18.1%. Full-year FY2026 consensus has moved from $5.31 to $9.88 over 90 days (+85.9%), a $4.57/share upward revision. Next-year FY2027 estimates of $6.29 (36% below FY2026) embed an expectation of cyclical normalization.

The rate environment is being supported by structural factors with multi-quarter duration: Red Sea disruptions continue to reroute vessels around the Cape of Good Hope (adding 10-14 days per voyage), refinery capacity is shifting from the Atlantic Basin to the Middle East/Asia (lengthening average haul distances), and the global product tanker orderbook remains constrained after years of underinvestment. With elevated refined product trade volumes, ton-mile demand for product tankers increases as crude is refined further from consumption centers.

The VLCC orders represent a growth vector but also a thesis alteration. Two $128M crude tankers delivering in 2028 introduce exposure to a different market with different supply/demand dynamics. This is a medium-term strategic bet, not a near-term earnings driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.87 with consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.88, STNG trades at 8.2x forward earnings. This compares to a peer median P/E of 18.9x and closest comparable International Seaways (INSW) at 13.0x. The discount reflects the market's expectation that current elevated rates are cyclically unsustainable, which is reasonable for any shipping stock.

The discount appears excessive given: (1) STNG's net cash position reduces enterprise value by ~$309M, implying an EV/earnings multiple closer to 7x; (2) the rate inflection is confirmed in Q1 2026 booked data, supporting forward estimates; and (3) the $1.7B liquidity buffer provides significant downside protection against rate normalization.

On trailing adjusted earnings ($5.79), the stock trades at 14.0x — elevated for a shipper but not unreasonable given the confirmed rate improvement and balance sheet strength. The more relevant valuation metric is forward EV/EBITDA, which at these earnings levels likely sits in the 4-5x range — cheap by any standard if rates sustain.

The valuation score is moderated by the stock's proximity to its 52-week high (within 2%), the 115% rally over the past year, and the reality that shipping multiples contract when investors perceive peak earnings. The market is not wrong to apply cyclical caution; the open question is whether the caution is excessive at 8.2x.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG operates one of the largest pure-play product tanker fleets globally. Scale advantages include pool leadership (scheduling efficiencies, higher utilization), capital markets access, and chartering leverage with major oil companies. The scrubber-fitted fleet provides a $3,000-5,000/day fuel cost advantage over non-scrubber vessels, directly increasing TCE competitiveness, particularly when fuel spreads are wide.

The fleet renewal strategy is sound in principle: selling older tonnage at elevated prices and replacing with modern, fuel-efficient vessels. The timing risk cuts both ways — if rates remain strong through 2027-2029 delivery windows, the newbuilds will be accretive; if rates collapse, STNG takes delivery of expensive vessels into a weak market. The net cash position provides a significant buffer against this scenario that most peers lack.

Competitive position has improved over the trajectory period (6.5 → 8.0 in prior scoring), driven by the balance sheet transformation and confirmed rate inflection. I moderate this to 7.5 to reflect the VLCC strategic departure and embedded cyclical risk that differentiates a shipper from a structural grower.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Cyclicality:** The 30% revenue decline from FY2024 to FY2025 demonstrates the volatility inherent in this business model. With ~80% of the fleet in spot-oriented pools, a sustained rate downturn would compress earnings materially. FY2027 consensus of $6.29 already prices in some normalization.

**Geopolitical Dependence:** The current rate environment is partially supported by trade route disruptions (Red Sea, Russia sanctions). A diplomatic resolution of Middle East hostilities — currently assessed at low probability but not negligible — could reduce ton-mile demand and depress rates. The sustained Hormuz disruption is a tailwind for product tanker demand but introduces physical risk to vessels transiting the region.

**Newbuilding Timing:** $573M of remaining newbuilding commitments through 2029 represent significant capital deployment at cycle-elevated shipbuilding costs. If secondhand vessel values decline, the economic logic of "sell old, buy new" weakens.

**VLCC Strategic Risk:** The $256M VLCC commitment introduces crude tanker exposure that changes the company's risk profile. Investors buying STNG as a product tanker pure-play should monitor whether this becomes a larger program.

**Related-Party Governance:** The SSM management arrangement with the Lolli-Ghetti family creates ongoing conflict-of-interest risk that is partially mitigated by contractual terms but inherently difficult to monitor from outside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-100):** Product tanker rates sustain above Q1 2026 levels through FY2026 as Red Sea disruptions persist, refinery capacity shifts continue lengthening haul routes, and limited fleet supply constrains capacity. FY2026 EPS of $10-11 at a 9-10x multiple yields $90-110. Continued buybacks reduce share count by 3-5%. Newbuilding deliveries in 2027 arrive into a strong market.

**Bear Case ($55-60):** Diplomatic resolution normalizes trade routes, rates revert toward mid-cycle levels of $20,000-25,000/day across all classes, and FY2026 EPS prints closer to $6-7. Newbuilding deliveries contribute to oversupply. The stock de-rates to 8-9x mid-cycle earnings. The net cash position provides a floor — at $55, the stock would trade at ~5.5x trough-adjusted earnings with $6.50/share in net cash.

**Base Case ($92-95):** Rates moderate slightly from Q1 2026 peaks but remain above FY2025 averages due to sustained disruptions. FY2026 EPS of $9-10 at 9.5-10x forward earnings. The combination of earnings delivery, continued buybacks, and dividend growth drives 14-17% total return over 12 months.

## Conclusion

STNG merits a BUY recommendation based on confirmed rate inflection, extraordinary balance sheet strength, aggressive upward estimate revisions, and a valuation that embeds meaningful cyclical pessimism despite structural supply constraints supporting above-mid-cycle rates for 12-18 months. The 115% rally and proximity to the 52-week high create entry timing risk, and the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty around May 5 earnings. Sizing at 2-3% is appropriate given the cyclical nature of the business and concentrated exposure to freight rate outcomes. The primary risk is faster-than-expected rate normalization driven by geopolitical de-escalation or fleet supply growth, monitored through the TCE rate and consensus revision triggers above.
