## Executive Summary

Intuitive Machines is now two companies stapled together, and they have opposite financial characteristics. The January 2026 acquisition of Lanteris Space Holdings (formerly Maxar Space Systems) from Advent International for $853.3M turned a $210M-revenue lunar-lander contractor into a ~$950M-revenue-guided spacecraft manufacturer, tripling headcount from 525 to roughly 1,695. Reported Q2 revenue of $206.2M against $50.3M a year ago looks like 310% growth. The company's own pro forma disclosure in Note 3 — presented as if Lanteris had been owned since 1 January 2025 — shows revenue of $206.2M versus $203.4M in Q2 2025 (+1.3%) and $406.0M versus $421.6M for the six months (**-3.7%**). On a like-for-like basis the combined enterprise contracted in the first half.

The margin arithmetic is equally stark. Derived from the Q2 10-Q line items, product revenue (Lanteris) generated $47.4M of gross profit at a 28.4% margin, while legacy service revenue generated a **$(11.5)M gross loss at a -31.5% margin**, driven by $19.8M of net losses recognized on customer contracts in the quarter. IM-4 alone added $16.2M of contract losses in the first half at only 45% completion, with performance running through August 2028 — the fourth consecutive CLPS mission to deteriorate at mid-completion. Operating cash flow swung from roughly breakeven in 1H25 to **-$111.9M** in 1H26; cash fell from $582.6M to $367.4M, having troughed near $243.4M at 31 March before $238.8M of gross ATM proceeds rebuilt the balance.

Against that, the bookings side is genuinely strong: $920M of Q2 awards plus $300M quarter-to-date in Q3, backlog of $1.762B (from $213.1M at year-end 2025), national security revenue rising from 3% to 30% of the total, an 18-unit IM 300 bus award via L3Harris for the SDA's Tranche 3 missile-defense layer, and two NASA prime awards (LROC, ShadowCam) that are closer to recurring data operations than hardware delivery. The problem is price. At $19.34 on ~226 million fully-exchanged shares, economic equity is roughly $4.38B and enterprise value roughly $4.55B — about 4.8x FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint on negative Adjusted EBITDA, and 2.6x a backlog whose largest single component rests on a $45.0M authorization to proceed. The stock is 50% above the $12.92 at which we last rated it HOLD. The improvement in bookings does not offset the deterioration in cash conversion, margin structure and dilution capacity at a 50% higher entry. We move to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

The legacy business operates under a "Build–Connect–Operate" framing across delivery services, data transmission, and infrastructure-as-a-service, reported as a single segment. Its four pillars:

- **NASA CLPS lunar delivery.** IM-1 (Feb 2024) and IM-2 (Mar 2025) delivered; IM-3 is 91% complete through March 2027; IM-4 45% complete through August 2028; IM-6 awarded March 2026 ($160.1M fixed plus $18.3M constrained, through May 2031); IM-5 awarded June 2026 (base $68.6M, plus an unexercised $79.7M customer option and up to $15.0M of incentives).
- **NASA Near Space Network.** One of two Direct-to-Earth awardees and the **sole** awardee for lunar data relay requiring a satellite constellation. Service revenue cannot begin until at least one satellite reaches lunar orbit and five complete the constellation. Capitalized construction-in-progress on those satellites and ground network reached **$86.2M** at 6/30/26, up from $50.8M at year-end.
- **OMES III via Space Network Solutions**, 90%-owned and consolidated, but with OMES III profits split **47% to Intuitive Machines / 53% to KBR** — full revenue consolidation, minority economics.
- **Lunar Terrain Vehicle**, one of three primes; the revenue-generating phase completed in Q2 2025.

Lanteris brings the 1300-class GEO communications bus (builder of JUPITER 3/EchoStar XXIV), NASA's Gateway Power and Propulsion Element, and SDA Tranche 1/2 Tracking Layer bus work. Two bolt-ons bracket it: KinetX (deep-space navigation, $31.3M, October 2025) and Goonhilly Earth Station plus COMSAT LLC (closed 3 August 2026), which vertically integrates a ground network the company previously rented.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q2 2026 revenue $206.2M (product $166.7M, service $36.7M, grant $2.8M) against $50.3M in Q2 2025; operating loss widened to $(47.1)M from $(28.6)M; net loss $(62.8)M; diluted EPS $(0.29). For the half: revenue $392.9M, operating loss $(86.3)M, net loss $(115.4)M, diluted EPS $(0.54). Adjusted EBITDA improved to $(13.8)M in Q2 from $(25.4)M, and to $(11.1)M for the half from $(32.0)M.

Legacy service revenue fell 27% in the quarter and 30% for the half, with management attributing the decline to LTV completion (-$12.8M), NASA's cancellation of OSAM task orders (-$7.4M), CLPS down $13.5M on IM-2 completion, and — the item that matters most for the thesis — **NSN revenue down $8.1M on "schedule delay and an unfavorable change in the EAC."**

**Consolidated gross margin was 17.4% in Q2 and 16.8% for the half**, entirely supplied by the acquired manufacturer. An alternative reading the filings partly support: the negative service margin is concentrated in identified loss provisions on IM-3 (91% complete, $6.3M added in 1H26, down from $18.5M) and IM-4, and could inflect as IM-3 finishes without any operational change. That is plausible. It is also true that IM-4's cumulative losses now exceed $17.6M at 45% completion with 26 months of performance remaining, and that fixed-price lunar delivery has produced a loss on every mission to date.

**Cash flow is the sharpest deterioration in the file.** 1H26 operating cash flow -$111.9M (Q1 -$52.1M, Q2 -$59.8M by differencing), capex -$33.9M, free cash flow -$145.8M, acquisitions -$447.1M net, financing +$386.6M. I have not annualized either quarter: the half includes $27.9M of expensed transaction and integration cost, a $13.6M securitization settlement, payment of payables inherited at acquisition, and a deliberate inventory build (inventory $59.9M, new; advances to suppliers $32.6M from $3.4M; prepaids $20.6M from $5.7M). Management links the build to long-lead materials for lunar constellation satellites two through five and to awards won in the quarter. Stripping the transaction costs leaves an underlying burn materially smaller than the headline but still clearly negative — an estimate, not a measured figure.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets went from $757.2M to $1,887.3M, of which $379.2M is goodwill and $297.1M intangibles (both preliminary, subject to measurement-period adjustment). **Current ratio fell from 4.96x to 1.66x**; working capital from $494.0M to $263.0M. The reported shareholders' deficit of $(290.6)M is a definitional artifact of the Up-C structure — redeemable noncontrolling interests sit in mezzanine at redemption value that tracks the Class A price, with $275.4M of non-cash remeasurement in 1H26 charged against paid-in capital. Adding mezzanine back, total equity capitalization is ~$911.5M, reconciling to $1,887.3M of assets against $975.8M of liabilities. The more useful measure: **tangible net assets of $235.2M** (assets less goodwill, intangibles and total liabilities). Any Altman-style score built on GAAP book equity is uninterpretable for this issuer.

Obligations outside the "long-term debt" line matter: $345.0M face of 2.50% converts due October 2030 (conversion price $13.1125, deep in the money, market value $670.9M, capped calls effective only to a **$20.98 cap** — the current price sits just below it); **$49.4M of ING securitization liabilities** in other liabilities because orbital receivable transfers fail ASC 860 sale criteria; **$50.6M net pension liability** from frozen Lanteris plans; and $93.4M of operating leases that step up to roughly $109.1M following the July Palo Alto amendment extending occupancy to 2043. Net debt is only ~$27M; including pension and leases, net obligations are roughly $171M. The $40M Stifel revolver is effectively unavailable — Stifel halted borrowing and covenant obligations on 12 January 2026 in consenting to the acquisition.

**Share count is the most commonly mis-stated figure here.** At 6/30/26 Class A outstanding was 169,529,749 and Class C 55,851,004, the latter paired 1:1 with exchangeable LLC units (55,851,004 / 225,380,753 = 24.8%, matching the disclosed minority). Adding 960,649 Goonhilly shares gives **~226.3 million fully-exchanged economic shares** versus the ~160.5 million in the market-data feed. At $19.34, economic equity is ~$4.38B against a ~$3.10B headline — a 41% understatement in any screen. Net new economic shares issued in 1H26 were 45,470,688, a 25.3% increase on the 179.9M base, equivalent to **20.2% ownership dilution** (holders went from 100% to 79.8%). Contingent instruments excluded as anti-dilutive total 39,265,517 shares (+17.4%), and **roughly $261M of ATM capacity remains** on the $500M program.

One point in management's favor on execution: the Q2 ATM issued 8,259,379 shares for $238.8M gross, an implied average near **$28.9** — about 49% above the current basis.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is acquired. Organic momentum, measured the only way the filings permit, is negative: pro forma first-half revenue -3.7%. The forward case therefore rests entirely on backlog conversion and new awards, not on demonstrated expansion.

**The FY2026 guidance gap is the single most consequential open item.** Reaffirmed guidance is revenue of $900M–$1.0B and positive Adjusted EBITDA. First-half revenue was $392.9M, so the implied second half is **$507M–$607M — 29% to 55% above the first half**, or $254M–$304M per quarter against Q2's $206.2M. The same 10-Q states the company expects to recognize only **25%–30% of the $1.762B backlog over the remainder of 2026, i.e., $440M–$529M**. Even at the top of that conversion range, the low end of revenue guidance requires near-complete near-term conversion plus incremental book-and-burn work. On profitability, first-half Adjusted EBITDA of $(11.1)M means full-year positive requires **at least a $22M swing** to +$11.1M or better in the second half.

Consensus is not reconcilable with the reported numbers either. Current-year consensus EPS of $(0.44) against $(0.54) of GAAP diluted loss already booked in the first half implies positive second-half EPS — a heroic assumption on a company with four consecutive misses (Q2 $(0.16) versus $(0.10) estimated; Q1 $(0.25) versus $(0.08); Q4 2025 $(0.23) versus $(0.07)). Coverage is thin: one to four contributing analysts across the estimate table, and revisions are flat with no directional signal. This is not a case where estimate-revision momentum supports a re-rating; there is no revision momentum to speak of.

**Backlog quality requires a discount.** The company reassessed its backlog policy in connection with the acquisition to include "other binding customer authorizations" where management concludes a substantive commercial commitment exists. Under that revised policy, **$587.0M of the $1,761.9M backlog (33%) relates to a three-satellite commercial GEO program for which the company has received a $45.0M authority to proceed.** The press release calls it a "signed $600+ million contract"; the 10-Q calls it an ATP with anticipated value over $600.0M. The filing language governs. The customer is unnamed. Backlog reconciles to remaining performance obligations of only $814.7M, the $947.3M gap comprising that $587.0M, $316.0M of cost-reimbursable value excluded under the right-to-invoice expedient, and $44.3M of constrained variable consideration. Broadening backlog policy is normal practice for satellite primes and I infer no intent from the timing, but the resulting number is softer than "backlog" suggests.

The genuinely encouraging developments are on the national-security side: 18 IM 300 buses for the SDA's Accelerated Missile Defense Tranche 3 via L3Harris as prime (value undisclosed in both the 10-Q and press release), the SDA Tracking Layer position inherited with Lanteris, and NASA's roughly $20B Moon Base program with 20-plus robotic landings planned through 2029. Growth prospects score 5.8: real addressable-market expansion and a credible bookings ramp, offset by negative like-for-like revenue, a guidance bridge the company's own conversion disclosure does not support, and no earnings-revision confirmation.

That is 12% below the current basis.** Put differently, at $19.34 the market is already paying about 4x optimistic FY2027 revenue for a business whose combined pro forma revenue declined in the first half.

The bear frame is equally live: FY2026 landing near $800M with Adjusted EBITDA still negative, a $51.5M EchoStar write-off, further ATM issuance into weakness, and NSN slipping again would leave a 2.0x multiple on $900M — roughly **$8**, against a 52-week low of $7.78.

Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear: +75.8%, -12.1%, -58.6% → **approximately -4% expected return**. Valuation score 3.0. Prior appreciation is not the reason; the base case sitting below spot on the correct share count is.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive read is genuinely mixed, and it is the trajectory that matters.

**Improving:** the sole-source NSN data-relay award is a real franchise with no named competitor for constellation relay. The Lanteris integration is winning follow-on work under the Intuitive Machines banner — the AMDT3 award demonstrates that acquired capability transfers. The LROC and ShadowCam prime awards are the first evidence of the company operating NASA science data operations, structurally closer to recurring revenue than hardware milestones. National security revenue at 30% of total, from 3%, is a durable mix improvement.

**Deteriorating:** NASA is deliberately cultivating four-plus interchangeable lander suppliers. In the roughly $600M CLPS tranche awarded in mid-2026, **Astrobotic captured $298M across two contracts** versus IM-5's $68.6M base — a larger dollar share for a competitor. Four commercial landers (Blue Origin, Firefly, Intuitive Machines, Voyager Technologies) reached final qualification testing simultaneously. Since every CLPS mission to date has been a loss contract, expanding CLPS volume does not obviously expand CLPS profit. On the manufacturing side, the company's own FY2025 risk factors state that "many satellite operators in the communications industry have continued to defer new satellite construction awards to evaluate geostationary and other competing satellite system architectures" — a company that just paid $853M for a GEO manufacturer is telling investors the GEO order market may be in structural decline. The EchoStar/Hughes Chapter 11 is a direct hit on that franchise's historic anchor customer.

**Customer concentration has not improved as much as the mix shift implies.** Revenue mix went from 93% civil to commercial 32% / civil 37% / national security 30%. But four customers were 35%, 28%, 11% and 10% of Q2 revenue — **84% of the total** — against a single customer at 85% a year earlier. Mix diversification improved; aggregate concentration is essentially flat. Score 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**The second-half ramp.** Quantified above: 29-55% sequential-half revenue growth required against a disclosed 25-30% backlog conversion rate, and a ≥$22M Adjusted EBITDA swing. The Q3 print (November) is the first hard test.

**EchoStar/Hughes, unreserved.** Lanteris held $10.1M of accounts receivable and $41.4M of orbital receivables from EchoStar-affiliated entities at 6/30/26, with certain subsidiaries filing Chapter 11 on 30 June and Hughes Satellite Systems plus additional entities on 2 August. **No incremental credit-loss allowance and no revenue-recognition adjustment were recorded**, with the company stating its conclusion "remains subject to completion of management's contract-specific assessment." Bounded exposure of roughly $51.5M against $235M of tangible net assets. Mitigants: the active construction counterparty was a non-debtor as of 13 August, and satellite operators in Chapter 11 commonly keep paying to preserve fleet operations.

**Orbital receivables.** $248.3M net from 11 customers with the top three at 35%, 30% and 17%, contingent not only on credit but on **satellites continuing to meet in-orbit performance requirements**; $105.0M of contractual cash flows are scheduled after 2030. Partially monetized through the ING facility ($49.4M drawn, up to $250M discretionary through 1 December 2026, with a make-whole on customer prepayment, guaranteed by the parent). Industry-standard for GEO manufacturers, and invisible in standardized data.

**NSN execution.** No relay service revenue until one satellite reaches lunar orbit and five complete the constellation. NSN revenue already declined $8.1M on schedule delay and an unfavorable EAC change, and IM-3's overruns have been repeatedly attributed to aligning mission schedule with an internally developed lunar-orbit satellite. $86.2M of capitalized construction-in-progress depends on launch and on-orbit success.

**DOJ False Claims Act matter inherited with Lanteris.** An October 2023 Civil Investigative Demand on cybersecurity compliance; "in late 2025 the Department of Justice presented its initial civil investigation review to Lanteris that it alleged constitute False Claims Act violations." Seller parent Vantor Holdings has agreed to indemnify — but caps, escrow and survival are undisclosed, and the indemnity's value depends on Vantor's credit.

**Structure and governance.** Founders held approximately 52% of combined voting power via three-vote Class C shares. The Tax Receivable Agreement owes 85% of future cash tax savings to TRA holders, unrecorded because of a full valuation allowance against $353.9M of deferred tax assets — profitability brings the liability with it, and it accelerates on change of control. Related-party density is high and centers on the Chairman (KBR's 53% OMES III profit share, Aerodyne, X-Energy, Axiom). The company is an emerging growth company not subject to Section 404(b) auditor attestation while Lanteris is still being integrated into the control environment. Purchase accounting on $360.5M of goodwill and $297.1M of intangibles is preliminary.

**Overhangs.** Roughly $261M of remaining ATM capacity (management's funding narrative explicitly relies on it), Advent's remaining 11,495,514 shares (~6.8% of Class A) after selling exactly half its consideration at $12.84 on 28 July, and 39.3M contingent shares.

### Options Market Signal

The chain corroborates a high-uncertainty, event-driven setup rather than a directional confirmation. Discount the 167.5% near-term ATM IV — that is a one-day expiry with a ±3.5% implied move, mechanical pinning. The informative points are the 1-month tenor at 104.2% IV (±16.4% implied move) and 3-month at 99.9% (±26.8%) against 108.8% one-year realized, in **backwardation** against a 96.1% twelve-month reading. The market is pricing near-term event risk without a durable regime shift.

The most notable change is positioning: put/call open interest collapsed from **1.55 to 0.64** while the price rose 52.7% since 26 July, and ATM IV rose 57 percentage points. That combination — call-tilted open interest, rising IV, a 50% price move, and 27.1% short interest with only 3.7 days to cover — reads as speculative call buying plus short covering into the post-print reaffirmation, not as fundamental accumulation. The 3-month skew of -12.4% is call-heavy. For a REDUCE rating this is the principal tactical caution: crowded shorts and cheap upside optionality mean the path down is unlikely to be orderly, and the six-month tenor implies a ±46.5% move either way.

Risk score 2.8: multiple unquantified binaries (guidance bridge, EchoStar reserve, DOJ, NSN launch, IM-4 completion, preliminary purchase accounting) against a valuation that requires most of them to resolve favorably.

## Management Quality

Capital-raising execution has been good. The Q2 ATM cleared at an implied ~$28.9 average, roughly 49% above the current basis, and the Lanteris acquisition — whatever else it did — supplied the entire gross profit of the combined company at a moment when the founding business was generating negative gross margin.

Everything else in the insider file points one direction. Across the 25 most recent Form 4 transaction groups through 10 August 2026 there were **no open-market purchases by any officer or director**. Chairman and co-founder Kamal Ghaffarian runs a roughly biweekly exchange-and-sell program holding his Class A position flat near 3,494,768 shares while Class C fell from 35,781,539 to 34,362,449, with realized prices tracing $17.80 in late March to $38.50-$43.03 on 1 June down to $16.13-$16.68 on 10 August. CTO Timothy Crain sold 300,000 shares across March and June. On 15 April, the CEO, CFO, CLO and CTO all sold at the identical price of $23.608 — consistent with an administrative or tax-related transaction rather than four independent decisions. Director Michael Blitzer entered a forward sale contract obligating 1,608,000 shares against a 1,937,196-share position. Between 21 May and 2 July, all four named executives plus the Chairman adopted new Rule 10b5-1 plans (Ghaffarian up to 1,935,568 shares; Altemus 398,185; McGrath 113,468; Jones 48,756).

The fair counter: founder wealth is concentrated in illiquid LLC units, the Chairman retains ~34.4M paired units far exceeding what he has sold, and every plan carries minimum-price floors — features more consistent with programmatic diversification than a directional view. I do not infer a negative outlook from the plans themselves. The defensible observation is the **complete absence of offsetting insider buying at prices 54% below the June peak**, in a company that also repurchased 941,080 shares from a director for $20.7M (~$22.00) in February 2025, about 12% above today's basis. Executive compensation and pay-for-performance alignment could not be established from the proxy; that is a verification gap, not a positive. Score 3.8.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($34, +76%).** FY2026 revenue lands within guidance with positive Adjusted EBITDA confirmed in the March 2027 filing, proving the second-half ramp real rather than aspirational. The $587M GEO authorization converts to definitive contract. The first NSN satellite reaches lunar orbit, unlocking a sole-source relay service with recurring economics. AMDT3 expands beyond 18 buses under the Golden Dome program, and national security grows past 40% of revenue at Lanteris-class margins. Service margin inflects as IM-3 completes and IM-4 losses stop accreting. On $1.4B FY2027 revenue at 5.5x EV/sales, equity reaches roughly $34 — the consensus mean of $32.33 is consistent, and the $45 high requires re-rating the NSN franchise as a monopoly asset.

**Bear case ($8, -59%).** The second half misses, landing FY2026 near $800M with Adjusted EBITDA still negative and management withdrawing the profitability commitment. EchoStar recovery falls short, forcing a write-down against $51.5M of unreserved exposure. IM-4 losses widen further at 45% completion. NSN slips again, deferring relay revenue past 2027 and putting $86.2M of capitalized satellite assets under recoverability review. The company draws the remaining $261M of ATM capacity into weakness while Advent distributes its residual 11.5M shares. At 2.0x on $900M revenue, equity is roughly $8 — near the $7.78 52-week low.

**Base case ($17, -12%).** FY2026 revenue lands at or slightly below the low end of guidance, roughly $850-900M, with Adjusted EBITDA hovering near breakeven rather than clearly positive. Backlog converts at the disclosed 25-30% pace. Service margin improves as IM-3 completes but does not turn positive. EchoStar takes a partial reserve. NSN launches slip a quarter or two without failing. FY2027 revenue reaches ~$1.2B at 3.4x EV/sales, giving ~$17.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current post-print strength. The stock has moved 46% off the 27 July close of $13.31 (and roughly 50% off the intraday low near $12.79 that week) and 10.1% above yesterday's official close in the current session, on a print that missed GAAP revenue consensus (~$220M) by roughly 6% and missed on the bottom line. That is squeeze-and-call-buying mechanics against 27.1% short interest, not a fundamental re-rating. Reduce existing exposure by roughly half at or above $19; retain no more than a starter position ahead of the Q3 print if maintaining optionality on the NSN franchise. Do not initiate.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) Q3 2026 revenue of $250M or more with the FY2026 positive Adjusted EBITDA guidance reaffirmed rather than softened — this is the decisive test and it arrives in November. (2) Service gross margin turning positive on a quarterly basis with no incremental IM-4 loss provision. (3) Conversion of the $587M GEO authorization to a definitive contract with the customer named. (4) A price near $13-14, which would restore roughly 25% upside to base case and make the risk/reward two-sided again. (5) Clustered open-market insider purchases — none have occurred at any price since the June peak.

**Avoid triggers.** (1) FY2026 revenue or Adjusted EBITDA guidance withdrawn or cut at the Q3 print. (2) A material EchoStar reserve combined with disruption to the active non-debtor construction contract. (3) Drawing more than $150M of remaining ATM capacity below $15, which would compound dilution at depressed prices. (4) A failed NSN satellite launch or a further NSN schedule slip that pushes first relay revenue beyond 2028, putting the $86.2M of capitalized assets at impairment risk. (5) Any adverse resolution of the DOJ FCA matter that the Vantor indemnity does not cover.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report in November 2026 is the natural decision point, with the FY2026 10-K in March 2027 as the definitive verdict on both revenue guidance and the positive Adjusted EBITDA commitment. Interim monitoring items: 8-K disclosure of AMDT3 contract value, ATM utilization in the Q3 10-Q, EchoStar reserve treatment, and Advent's residual position.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has been improving (4.2 in June, 4.5 in July) and this report reverses it to 4.0 with a downgrade to REDUCE. That is not a reaction to price direction — price alone never justifies a rating change. It reflects three specific developments the Q2 filing made measurable that were not measurable before: like-for-like revenue contraction of 3.7% in the first half disclosed in the pro forma note; operating cash outflow accelerating to $59.8M in Q2 from $52.1M in Q1 with the current ratio collapsing from 4.96x to 1.66x; and a newly quantifiable contradiction between reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance and the company's own statement that only 25-30% of backlog converts in the remainder of the year. Layered on that: the EchoStar/Hughes Chapter 11 filings with $51.5M of unreserved exposure, and IM-4 loss accretion of $16.2M at 45% completion.

Those deteriorations arrive alongside a 50% higher entry price than our prior HOLD.
