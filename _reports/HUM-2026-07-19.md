**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Humana is the most Medicare-concentrated of the large U.S. managed-care organizations, with government programs supplying roughly 93% of FY2025 premiums and Medicare alone at ~86% of Q1 2026 revenue. The company has deliberately reshaped itself into a senior-focused pure play, completing its exit from employer-group commercial insurance in 2025 and building out CenterWell, its owned primary-care, home-health, and pharmacy arm, as a cost-control lever on the insurance book. After bottoming near $163 in March 2026, the stock has risen ~79% over the trailing year to $400, on two linked developments: a sharp rebound in Medicare Advantage (MA) membership (Individual MA +22.6% YoY in Q1 2026) and an industry-wide legal challenge to CMS's Star Ratings methodology that culminated in the May 2026 Clover Health court victory.

Price has run well ahead of realized earnings. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $9.84, and the stock trades at ~43x that figure. Trailing earnings were depressed by ~$1.3B of pre-tax one-time and non-cash charges ($7.38/share), so normalized earnings power is materially higher, but even on the current-year consensus of $8.96 and next-year $16.03, the market is capitalizing a recovery that has not yet been validated. Q1 2026 already showed the core tension: revenue grew 23.5% while insurance-segment operating income fell 8.8% and the benefit ratio jumped 240 bps to 89.4%, because the BY2026 Star Ratings revenue headwind and fast-onboarded new members at higher medical loss ratios (MLRs) squeezed margin even as the top line surged.

We rate HUM a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The membership recovery is real, the earnings-normalization path is credible, and the industry-wide Star Ratings litigation is a genuine structural positive. But the two decisive binary events (5th Circuit appeals on RADV and on Humana's own 2025 Star Ratings challenge, which Humana lost at the district level) are unresolved, the specific Clover recalculation is unlikely to directly lift Humana's largest MA contract, and the valuation now prices in a clean recovery. At ~43x on impaired earnings with symmetric outcomes, the risk/reward does not support a BUY, and the improving fundamentals do not support an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Humana operates two reporting segments. The Insurance segment carries Individual and Group Medicare Advantage, stand-alone Part D drug plans (PDP), Medicaid and state-based contracts, TRICARE (the T-5 East military contract), specialty products (dental/vision), and a PBM. This is the profit engine, generating $1.66B of segment operating income in FY2025. The CenterWell segment houses the vertically integrated care-delivery assets: senior-focused primary care clinics, CenterWell Home Health (the former Kindred at Home), and pharmacy solutions including mail-order and specialty. CenterWell generated $22.5B of segment revenue (largely intersegment) and $1.34B of operating income in FY2025.

The strategic thesis is integrated care delivery. By owning primary care and home health, and putting ~68% of Individual MA members in value-based provider relationships as of 12/31/2025, Humana aims to lower medical costs, improve quality, and thereby lift Star Ratings, which in turn fund richer benefits and support member growth. It competes directly with UnitedHealth/Optum, CVS/Aetna, Elevance, and Centene. Management itself flags low barriers to entry, price-based competition, and the annual MA enrollment window that lets members switch easily, which is the honest framing of a business with a narrow moat and high policy exposure.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financial statements require care because the XBRL "revenue" figure ($5.8B for 2025) captures only services revenue. The bulk of Humana's top line is insurance premiums, which the 10-K reports at $122.8B in FY2025, for total revenue of ~$129.7B, up 10.1% YoY. Growth was driven by higher per-member Medicare premiums (IRA direct subsidy), state-based and PDP membership growth, partially offset by a deliberate 7.3% decline in Individual MA membership as Humana exited unprofitable plans and counties.

FY2025 net income attributable to Humana was $1.20B, EPS $9.84, essentially flat versus $1.21B / $9.98 in 2024. Earnings were heavily depressed by the ~$1.3B of pre-tax charges: a put/call valuation adjustment on the WCAS minority investment ($513M), value-creation/restructuring charges ($449M), impairments ($253M), a loss on business sale ($67M), and litigation ($15M). The benefit ratio deteriorated 40 bps to 90.2% on mix shift toward higher-MLR PDP and Medicaid, aided by $1.0B of favorable prior-period reserve development worth ~80 bps.

The most important negative in FY2025 was cash flow. Operating cash flow collapsed to $921M from $2.97B in 2024 and $3.98B in 2023, driven largely by IRA-related receivable timing (a ~$1.5B net CMS receivable) and working-capital swings. FCF was only $375M. This is why buybacks were cut roughly 80% to $151M in 2025 (from $817M in 2024 and $1.6B in 2023), with $2.7B remaining on authorization. When the operating cash engine stalls and the company is funding a membership reset plus an acquisition, capital return is the first thing to give.

Q1 2026 cuts both ways. Total revenue rose 23.5% to $39.6B on a membership rebound: Individual MA +22.6% YoY to 6.39M, PDP +58.7%, Group MA +27.3%, with management guiding ~25% full-year Individual MA growth. But net income was $1.19B, EPS $9.83, down 4.6% YoY, and insurance-segment operating income fell 8.8% to $1.44B. The benefit ratio jumped 240 bps to 89.4% because of the BY2026 Star Ratings revenue headwind and because fast-onboarded new members run at higher MLRs before they can be steered into value-based care. Operating cash flow improved to $1.25B on favorable IBNR and working-capital timing.

On the balance sheet, total assets were $55.3B at Q1 2026, stockholders' equity $18.6B, debt-to-cap 43.0% against a 60% covenant maximum, and D/E ~1.77x. Ratings are investment grade at BBB (S&P) / Baa2 (Moody's). We do not treat the Altman Z of 1.55 as a distress signal; the model is calibrated for industrial firms, and for an insurer the low asset turnover (0.12) and thin EBIT/assets are structural given the float and benefits-payable liabilities. The relevant solvency read is $14.1B of aggregate statutory capital against a $7.5B minimum, a $5.0B undrawn revolver, ~$5B of cash, and ~$1.1B of ordinary dividend capacity to the parent in 2026. Two capital-structure notes bear watching: Humana issued $1.5B senior notes in March 2025 and $1.0B of 6.625% junior subordinated notes in March 2026, the sub-note structure suggesting attention to rating-agency equity-credit treatment. Parent-company liquidity is thin at $111M as of 3/31/2026 (down from $1.5B) after funding the MaxHealth acquisition and subsidiary capital contributions, leaving the parent dependent on regulated-sub dividends and debt issuance.

We score financial health at 5.0. The statutory capital position and investment-grade rating are genuinely sound and rule out solvency risk, but the 2025 collapse in operating cash flow, the throttled buyback, the WCAS put liability (discussed below), and thin holdco liquidity offset the balance-sheet strength. This is neither a fortress nor a stressed balance sheet; it is a levered insurer working through a reset.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a whipsaw. Humana intentionally shed 412K Individual MA members in 2025 to protect margin, then grew ~22-25% in 2026. Revenue growth is real and estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-quarter consensus rose 53.7% over 90 days to $7.00, next-year rose 6.0% to $16.03, and the earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats. On the surface this triggers the classic recovery profile of accelerating revenue plus rising estimates.

We are cautious about over-weighting that signal here for a specific reason: the revenue growth is being purchased at higher MLRs into a Star-Ratings-impaired revenue base, and the profit line is going the wrong way. The quality of the growth is exactly the contested question. This is not a clean trough-earnings recovery where volume growth drops through to margin; it is volume growth that is currently margin-dilutive, with the bet that value-based care seasons the new book profitably over 2027-2028. The current-year EPS estimate ($8.96) is flat-to-slightly-down over 90 days (-1.0%), and the next-quarter estimate swung negative (-120.9% revision), reflecting the near-term margin drag. The bullish revision picture is concentrated in the current quarter and the out-year, not the near-term profit line.

CenterWell adds a secular growth vector, but Q1 CenterWell operating income fell 26% on MaxHealth integration costs, so the near-term contribution is a drag. We score growth prospects at 5.5. There is genuine revenue re-acceleration and a credible normalized-earnings recovery, but the margin trajectory is deteriorating and the durability of profitable growth is unproven until the new members season and Star Ratings recover.

## Valuation Assessment

At $400, Humana trades at ~43x trailing FY2025 EPS and ~45x current-year consensus. The obvious counter is that FY2025 earnings absorbed the one-time charges, so normalized earnings power is materially higher; on next-year consensus the multiple compresses to ~25x. That is the case bulls make, and it is not unreasonable, but it requires two things to break right: the BY2026 Star headwind must reverse into a Star recovery, and the new MA cohort must season toward the segment's historical margin.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Managed Health Care peer median P/E is 33.4x, and UnitedHealth trades at 30.6x, Elevance at a much cheaper 17.0x, Molina at 53.7x. Humana at ~43x trailing sits above UNH and well above ELV, which is difficult to justify given that Humana is the most Medicare-concentrated payer, has the weakest Star Ratings litigation scoreboard (it lost its own 2025 challenge at the district level while UNH and Centene won on call-center metrics), and carries the thinnest holdco liquidity of the group. On next-year earnings the multiple is more defensible, but the next-year number is precisely what the two pending 5th Circuit appeals could swing.

The analyst target set frames the risk/reward: mean $346.70, median $342, high $502, low $195. The current price of $400 sits above both the mean and median targets, which is the signal to weight. On a genuinely cheap recovery stock, consensus targets lag reality and act as floors; here the stock trades above consensus at a premium multiple on earnings still depressed by a live headwind, which is the setup where targets are not independent confirmation of undervaluation.

We treat the "cheap on normalized earnings" framing with skepticism. A compressed forward multiple that depends on a Star Ratings recovery that Humana has so far lost in court, plus profitable seasoning of a book onboarded at 89.4% MLR, is a conditional cheapness rather than an identified mispricing. We score valuation at 3.5. The stock is priced for a clean recovery that has not been validated, it trades above the analyst mean and median, and it carries a premium to the two largest peers despite a weaker competitive and litigation position.

## Competitive Landscape

Humana's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on a strategic basis but faces genuine structural headwinds. The integrated CenterWell model is a real differentiator against payers without owned care delivery, and the value-based penetration (~68% of Individual MA) is a durable cost-control lever if it works as designed. The membership rebound also demonstrates Humana can regain share when it chooses to compete on price.

The offsetting reality is that Humana is behind peers on the single most important payer metric, Star Ratings, which directly funds benefit richness and thus member acquisition and retention. The number of Humana MA plans rated 4-star+ declined significantly for 2025, and Humana lost its own district-court Star challenge while UNH and Centene won theirs. The industry-wide Clover victory is a strategic positive because it establishes that CMS's methodology is legally vulnerable, and CMS is voluntarily recalculating 2026 Stars and 2027 quality-bonus payments (only upward). But per Capstone analysis, the recalculation "would likely not move the needle at all for Humana's largest MA contract," while UnitedHealthcare's and Aetna's largest contracts could actually decline. So the mechanical benefit accrues more to Humana's competitors than to Humana, even as the legal precedent helps Humana's separate appeals.

We score competitive position at 5.0. The strategic model is sound and share is recoverable, but Humana is competitively behind on Star Ratings, the recalculation tailwind favors peers, and management itself acknowledges low barriers to entry and easy member switching.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by binary regulatory and legal outcomes that are not fully priced.

**Star Ratings recovery** is the single most important swing factor for 2027+ earnings. Weak Stars reduce bonus dollars, which reduce benefit richness, which can trigger a self-reinforcing membership and margin spiral. This is both an operational challenge (the CenterWell integration is partly a Star play) and a legal one (the pending 5th Circuit appeal on Humana's 2025 challenge).

**RADV audit exposure** is the second large binary. CMS's Final RADV Rule eliminated the FFS Adjuster and extrapolates audit results from PY2018 forward across all eligible MA contracts. A Texas court vacated the rule in September 2025 (Humana v. Kennedy), but the government appealed in November 2025 to the 5th Circuit. An adverse final outcome could produce material extrapolated repayments; DOJ also has ongoing civil requests on Part C risk-adjustment practices.

**Concentration** is acute: Florida MA alone is ~14% of total revenue (~1.0M members), and CMS contracts renew annually.

**Litigation** includes the In re Humana Inc. Securities Litigation (D. Del.), parallel derivative suits, a DOJ-intervened qui tam on broker payments (Shea v. eHealth), and a certified TCPA class action.

**The WCAS put liability** is a real near-term cash claim, not accounting noise. The $1.38B put liability tied to the primary-care JV is callable, with the first two clinic cohorts callable in 2026 for ~$1.0-1.6B. That is a sizeable capital commitment competing directly with buybacks and dividends against thin holdco liquidity.

We score risk factors at 4.0. Two binary appeals with material earnings consequences are pending, the revenue base is heavily concentrated in Florida MA, and the WCAS call and cash-flow deterioration represent specific, near-term capital risks the market is discounting given the recovery narrative embedded in the price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is priced for calm at the near tenor and elevated uncertainty further out, which fits a story where the near-term earnings path is more or less known and the binary catalysts are dated later. Near-term ATM IV is 43.4% versus 50.8% trailing historical volatility, so front-month IV is cheap (spread -7.4%, 1st percentile in the current chain). The term structure is in contango, with 6-month IV at 51.6% and the 6-month put/call skew turning negative (-4.2%), signaling demand for downside protection over the horizon where the 5th Circuit appeals and 2027 bid pricing resolve. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.40 to 1.35 as the stock rose 8% since the prior snapshot, indicating hedging into the rally rather than fresh bullish positioning. Short interest is low at 4.1% of float with 2.8 days to cover, so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The net read supports the HOLD: the market is not pricing an imminent blow-up, but it is paying up for six-month downside protection, consistent with the unresolved binary risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$500).** FY2025 earnings were artificially depressed by the one-time charges, so normalized earnings power is well above the $9.84 reported. The MA membership rebound reflects disciplined re-entry that value-based care will season toward historical margins over 2027-2028, and next-year consensus proves out. The 5th Circuit reverses on RADV and/or the 2025 Star challenge, removing two overhangs, and Humana's operational Star improvement plus the favorable industry legal environment restores bonus payments into 2027. On $16-18 of normalized EPS at a low-30s multiple in line with the peer median, the stock reaches the analyst high of ~$502.

**Bear Case (~$195).** The membership growth was share re-bought at unprofitable MLRs into a Star-impaired revenue base, and the 89.4% Q1 benefit ratio proves sticky rather than transitional. The 5th Circuit rules against Humana on RADV, triggering material extrapolated clawbacks, and Humana's Star Ratings fail to recover while the Clover recalculation helps competitors more than Humana. The WCAS put call and continued weak operating cash flow further constrain capital return. The multiple compresses back toward the analyst low of ~$195 as the recovery narrative unwinds, a decline of ~51% from current levels.

The trajectory of this name (4.7 in February, 4.6 in May, 5.2 in June, HOLD → AVOID → HOLD) reflects genuinely improving growth prospects (4.0 → 6.0) and financial health (4.5 → 5.5) offset by deteriorating valuation (6.0 → 4.5) as the stock outran fundamentals. Our current read confirms that divergence rather than reversing it. The stock has appreciated ~79% while the profit line has weakened and the decisive catalysts remain pending; the fundamental improvement is real but the price has more than captured it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We do not have sufficient evidence to underwrite either the clean recovery required for a BUY or the collapse required for an AVOID; the outcome hinges on two dated binary legal events and the unproven seasoning of a newly grown MA book.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A 5th Circuit ruling favorable to Humana on RADV or the 2025 Star Ratings challenge, removing a material earnings overhang.
- Q2/Q3 2026 evidence that the benefit ratio is trending back below ~89% as new members season, confirming profitable rather than dilutive growth.
- A pullback toward the low-$300s that restores a reasonable margin of safety against the ~$16 next-year EPS trajectory (roughly 19-20x forward).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse 5th Circuit RADV outcome producing material extrapolated repayment exposure.
- A further benefit-ratio deterioration above ~90% or a downward revision to full-year 2026 MA guidance, signaling the growth was unprofitable.
- A dividend or buyback constraint driven by the WCAS put call or continued weak operating cash flow.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (benefit-ratio trend, membership economics, capital-return posture) and any 5th Circuit developments on the RADV and Star Ratings appeals, both of which are the dominant near-term swing factors.

## Conclusion

Humana has executed a real strategic and operational turnaround, rebuilding MA membership after a deliberate 2025 contraction and benefiting from an industry-wide legal challenge to CMS's Star Ratings methodology. The normalized-earnings recovery case is credible, and the balance sheet is solvent and investment grade. But the stock trades at a premium to consensus targets on earnings still depressed by the BY2026 Star headwind, and at a premium to both UnitedHealth and Elevance despite a weaker Star Ratings and litigation position. The growth is currently margin-dilutive, the two decisive 5th Circuit appeals are unresolved, and the Clover recalculation is unlikely to directly help Humana's largest contract. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric between the ~$500 bull case and the ~$195 bear case. We rate HUM a HOLD, uncertain, and would look to become constructive on a favorable appeals outcome, a benefit-ratio improvement confirming profitable growth, or a pullback into the low-$300s.
