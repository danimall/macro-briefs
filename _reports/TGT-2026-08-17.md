## Executive Summary

Target's operating inflection is real and quantified, not narrative. In the quarter ended May 2, 2026, net sales rose 6.7% to $25,443M, comparable sales turned positive for the first time in five quarters at +5.6% with **traffic** up 4.4%, gross margin expanded 80bp to 29.0%, and adjusted operating income rose 29.1% to $1,135M. All six merchandise categories grew, with Hardlines ("Fun 101") up 14.6% and Beauty up 9.6%. Consensus has followed the operating data: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 4.7% over 90 days to $8.46 and FY2027 up 5.5% to $9.03, against a record of three consecutive beats (+15.8%, +12.1%, +2.6%). The sell-side distribution has drifted marginally constructive (9 Buy / 24 Hold three months ago → 10 Buy / 23 Hold today).

The problem is price. At $154.48 the stock trades at 18.3x FY2026 consensus and 17.1x FY2027, roughly 8% above the analyst mean of $143.43 and 5% above the $146.50 median, and 1.3% below its all-time high of $156.47. It has appreciated 48% over twelve months and ~48% year to date. I want to correct an arithmetic error that has circulated in prior work on this name: the growth-adjusted multiple here is **not** stretched. FY2025 adjusted EPS of $7.57 to FY2026 consensus $8.46 is +11.8%, which puts the forward PEG at roughly **1.55**, not 2.7. The 2.7 figure is what you get if you divide the forward multiple by FY2027's +6.7% growth alone, which is the wrong denominator for a forward multiple. On a two-year basis ($7.57 → $9.03 = 9.2% CAGR) the PEG is ~2.0. That is fully valued, not egregiously valued, and it does not by itself support a downgrade.

What it also does not support is adding capital. The recovery is one quarter old and cycled a −3.8% prior-year comp — the two-year stacked comp is approximately +1.6%. Underlying after-tax ROIC, normalized for the $593M interchange gain and $250M transformation charge, has fallen to roughly 13.0% from 15.4% in FY2024, and management is committing ~$5B of capex in FY2026 (+34%) into that lower-return base while buybacks have gone to zero and per-share dividend growth has decelerated to 1.8% annually. The 600-store Ulta-to-Beauty-Studio conversion executes through back-to-school and holiday, in the company's second-fastest-growing category. Two days after this report's cutoff, Q2 results land with near-term implied volatility at 84.6% against 30.4% realized. Probability-weighting my scenarios produces roughly +1% price return and +4% total return over twelve months. That is a hold.

## Company Overview

Target operates 2,002 stores and 251.5M retail square feet as of May 2, 2026, essentially all in the United States, and reports as a **single segment** — the CEO reviews only consolidated results, so no segment margin data exists to analyze.

Merchandise mix (Q1 FY2026): Food & beverage 25%, Household essentials 18%, Apparel 16%, Beauty 14%, Hardlines 14%, Home furnishings 13%. Consumables are roughly 43% of merchandise sales, materially below Walmart U.S.'s grocery weighting. This is the single most important structural fact about the company: Target carries more discretionary exposure per sales dollar, which produced violent negative operating leverage through FY2023–FY2025 and is now producing violent positive leverage (a 6.7% sales increase generated a 29.1% adjusted operating income increase).

Owned and exclusive brands are approximately 30% of merchandise sales across 40-plus labels. The 10-K states these generally carry higher margins but require longer lead times and earlier ownership in the supply chain, which "exposes us to enhanced risks of supply chain disruptions and trade policy or tariff impacts." Roughly half of merchandise offered is sourced outside the U.S., with China the largest single country of origin, and Target is importer of record for most owned/exclusive merchandise, using the "first sale" customs valuation methodology with refund cycles that extend beyond one year.

Stores fulfilled 97.6% of total merchandise sales in each of the last three fiscal years and in Q1 FY2026; digitally originated sales are 20.3% of merchandise sales (comps +8.9%) with two-thirds flowing through same-day options. This is a disclosed fulfillment statistic, not a claim, and it is a genuine cost advantage: digital growth is being served off the existing store asset base rather than incremental warehouse capital.

The margin-expansion path runs through ancillary businesses: Roundel (retail media), Target Plus (third-party marketplace commissions), Target Circle and the paid Circle 360 tier, and Shipt. CVS Pharmacy operates in-store pharmacies under a perpetual agreement and pays Target occupancy income. The Ulta Beauty shop-in-shop operating agreement was mutually terminated and expires in August 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### The trough and its composition

Net sales fell from the FY2022 peak of $109,120M to $104,780M in FY2025, a cumulative −4.0%. FY2023 contained a 53rd week worth roughly $1.7B; on a comparable 52-week basis, FY2025's −1.7% was the sharpest genuine decline of the three years. Comparable sales: −3.7% (FY2023), +0.1% (FY2024), **−2.6%** (FY2025), with stores comps of −4.0% against digital +3.1%. FY2025 was a traffic problem localized in the physical store.

FY2025 GAAP operating income of $5,117M (4.9% margin) flatters the year. It includes a $593M pre-tax net gain from credit-card interchange litigation in which Target was plaintiff, offset by only $250M of business-transformation charges. Adjusting both:

| FY2025 | GAAP | Adjusted |
|---|---|---|
| Operating income | $5,117M (4.9%) | **$4,775M (4.6%)** |
| YoY change | −8.1% | **−14.2%** |
| Diluted EPS | $8.13 (−8.2%) | **$7.57 (−14.5%)** |

Gross margin fell to 27.9% from 28.2% on higher markdowns and purchase-order cancellation costs, partially offset by advertising growth and lower shrink. Adjusted SG&A rate rose to 20.9% from 20.6% on sales deleverage. At a ~20.6% SG&A rate on $104.8B of sales, each 1% of sales decline destroys roughly $210M of operating income before offsets — the mechanical reason a −1.7% sales year produced a −14% adjusted operating income year, and the mechanical reason the recovery is steep.

Two forward-relevant points from the margin decomposition. PO cancellation costs are a tariff-volatility artifact that should abate. But management states shrink rates "reached pre-pandemic levels" in FY2025, meaning **the shrink tailwind is largely spent** — a multi-year, largely non-operational gross-margin crutch that can no longer contribute much. Future gross-margin expansion must come from markdown discipline, retail media mix, and supply-chain productivity. All three showed up in Q1's +80bp, so the transition is underway; the bar is simply higher than it was.

The GAAP EPS decline to $1.71 from $2.27 is entirely the prior-year interchange gain. Non-merchandise revenue grew 24.5% to $549M, with the advertising line up 50.9% to $246M. Two cautions: (1) the $246M understates Roundel because the 10-Q states Roundel services are classified as Net Sales **or as a reduction of Cost of Sales or SG&A depending on the arrangement**, so a portion of Roundel economics flows through the gross margin rate invisibly — any attribution of margin expansion purely to merchandising overstates that; (2) non-merchandise revenue is only 2.2% of net sales and contributed $108M of the quarter's $1,597M sales increase. It is a margin story, not yet a consolidated growth driver. Credit card profit sharing fell 7.8% while sales rose 6.7% — a small divergence worth watching, since the 10-K identifies that line as sensitive to consumer credit conditions.

### Cash flow, capital allocation, and the return-of-capital squeeze

FY2025 operating cash flow was $6.6B (from $7.4B) on lower earnings and lower payables leverage. Capex was $3,727M. Derived free cash flow: **$2,873M**, or 2.7% of sales. Capital returned: $2,100M dividends plus $400M buybacks = $2,500M, or **87% of FCF**.

FY2026 capex guidance is approximately $5B (+34%) with ~30 new store openings versus 18, plus supply chain, technology and an expanded remodel program. Q1 capex of $1,035M (+31%) tracks the plan. Sensitivity, not forecast (Q1 is seasonally cash-negative and Q1 OCF of $716M cannot be annualized):

- OCF $7.0B against $5.0B capex → FCF ≈ $2.0B, marginally **below** the ~$2.1B dividend run-rate ($526M declared in Q1 × 4).
- OCF $7.5B → FCF ≈ $2.5B, covering the dividend with ~$0.4B to spare.
- OCF ~$8.0B needed to fund dividend plus meaningful buyback.

Management's own actions corroborate the arithmetic: buybacks fell from $1.0B (FY2024) to $0.4B (FY2025) to **zero in Q1 FY2026** with $8.3B of authorization unused, and the dividend has grown 1.8% per share in each of the last two years ($4.44 → $4.52 paid). There are two readings. Strain: the 2.95% yield is being funded with negligible FCF cushion during the capex ramp. Discipline: a rational allocator declines to repurchase at 18–19x after a 48% re-rating. Both are defensible; the observable consequence is the same — the total-return case now depends more on earnings growth and less on buyback-driven per-share accretion than it did two years ago.

### Balance sheet

At January 31, 2026: cash and equivalents $5,488M (including $4,611M short-term investments), inventory $12,304M, current ratio 0.94, total debt $16,456M, operating lease liabilities $3,834M, shareholders' investment $16,165M, total assets $59,490M. Credit ratings **A2 / A / A**. No commercial paper or revolver balance outstanding at any time during FY2025, FY2024 or Q1 FY2026; $4.0B of undrawn committed facilities.

Two screening artifacts deserve correction because they materially misstate condition. A reported Altman Z of 2.02 dropped both the retained-earnings and market-equity terms; supplying actual inputs (RE $9,297M; EBIT $5,212M; market equity $70.2B against total liabilities $43,325M) yields **Z ≈ 3.22**, safe zone. A reported Piotroski F-Score of 2 was flagged as computed from 3 of 9 signals; the full nine on FY2025 vs. FY2024 yields approximately **6 of 9** — positive net income, positive OCF, OCF > net income, lower LTD/assets, higher current ratio, no share issuance, against declining ROA (6.3% vs. 7.2%), declining gross margin, and declining asset turnover (1.787 vs. 1.884). A 0.94 current ratio and negative working capital are how a retailer with sub-three-month inventory turns and $12.6B of payables is supposed to look, not distress. Note the reflexivity in my own recomputation: the Z-score is inflated by a market cap that has risen ~48% on one quarter of results; at $110/share Z falls to roughly 2.9. Also note that the XBRL feed's "ppe_net" of $8,917M is a mis-tagged sub-component — audited PP&E net was $33,749M.

**Financial health: 6.0.** Investment-grade with genuine liquidity flexibility and no refinancing cliff, offset by a dividend thinly covered by FCF in the ramp year, three of nine Piotroski signals deteriorating, and declining asset turnover.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision picture is unambiguously positive and I want to weight it explicitly rather than dismiss it:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 90d revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $2.33 | $2.25 | $2.24 | **+3.9%** |
| Next quarter | $1.93 | $1.90 | $1.88 | +2.6% |
| Current year (FY2026) | $8.46 | $8.35 | $8.09 | **+4.7%** |
| Next year (FY2027) | $9.03 | $8.92 | $8.56 | **+5.5%** |

Rising estimates into a rising stock, with 31–36 analysts contributing and three consecutive beats behind it, is a signal that cuts against any bearish rating. It is also an order of magnitude below the velocity that marks a true earnings inflection in a cyclical trough (+30%+ over 90 days). The right characterization is a mid-single-digit upward drift consistent with a company delivering modestly better than a de-risked plan — supportive, not transformative.

Implied growth off the correct base: FY2025 adjusted EPS $7.57 → FY2026 $8.46 is **+11.8%**; FY2027 $9.03 is a further **+6.7%**; two-year CAGR **9.2%**. Management's guidance as externally reported (~4% sales growth, EPS toward the high end of $7.50–$8.50, growth in every quarter) is consistent with consensus at $8.46, though the GAAP-versus-adjusted labeling of that range is unverified.

The durability question is arithmetic. Q1's +5.6% comp cycled −3.8%, giving a two-year stack of approximately +1.6%. Q2 consensus of +2.3% comps against −1.5% is a +0.8% two-year stack. Deutsche Bank's framing is the fair counterweight — it remains sidelined "until there is greater evidence that recent same-store sales momentum reflects sustainable market share gains and improved competitive positioning, rather than cycling easier comparisons." Comparisons harden materially in H2 FY2026 and FY2027.

Against that, the merchandising reset appears substantive rather than cosmetic: 3,000 new beauty products across 60 brands, 75% of home decorative accessories reset, a back-to-school assortment more than 50% new, and the Hardlines transformation into "Fun 101" that delivered +14.6%. Breadth across all six categories argues against a single-category fluke. Retail media is the credible structural margin lever, growing 50.9% in the disclosed line with additional economics netted against costs.

The offsetting structural fact is returns. After-tax ROIC: 15.4% (FY2024) → 13.8% (FY2025) → 12.4% TTM through Q1 FY2026. Normalizing management's disclosed +0.8pp interchange benefit and −0.6pp transformation drag, underlying ROIC has been roughly **13.0%** in both windows, down ~200bp. Target is deploying its largest-ever capex program into a business earning less on capital than it did two years ago. One quarter of evidence suggests the program may reverse this; that is one quarter.

**Growth prospects: 5.5.** Genuine near-term acceleration with rising estimates and broad category participation, discounted for easy comparisons, a spent shrink tailwind, and a normalized return profile that has not yet recovered.

At **$154.48**:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailing FY2025 GAAP EPS $8.13 | 19.0x |
| Trailing FY2025 adjusted EPS $7.57 | 20.4x |
| TTM adjusted EPS $7.98 | 19.4x |
| FY2026 consensus $8.46 | **18.3x** |
| FY2027 consensus $9.03 | **17.1x** |
| Forward PEG (18.3x / 11.8% FY2026 growth) | **1.55** |
| Forward PEG (18.3x / 9.2% two-year CAGR) | ~2.0 |
| Dividend $4.56 forward | **2.95%**; 54% of FY2026 consensus |
| Enterprise value (Q1 balance sheet) | ~$82.0B; ~$85.8B incl. operating leases |
| vs. analyst mean $143.43 / median $146.50 | **+7.7% / +5.4% above** |
| vs. 52-week high $156.47 | −1.3% |

**Correcting the growth-adjusted case.** A PEG of ~1.55 on near-term growth, or ~2.0 blended over two years, describes a fully valued but not distorted multiple. The argument that Target is "paying a full multiple for low-single-digit growth" does not survive contact with the numbers — FY2026 earnings growth off the adjusted base is 11.8%. What survives is subtler: the growth decelerates sharply to 6.7% in FY2027, the multiple has already expanded from roughly 11x adjusted trailing at the November 2025 low of $83.44 to 20.4x today, and normalized ROIC of ~13% does not justify a premium terminal multiple.

**Peer reconciliation, done properly.** The peer median trailing P/E is 21.4x, so a headline read shows Target at a discount. Two adjustments are required. First, basis: comparing Target's 18.3x **forward** multiple to peers' **trailing** median is not like-for-like; on trailing GAAP Target is 19.0x, an 11% discount to the median. Second, composition: the median is anchored by Costco at 48.3x and BJ's at 21.4x — compounders with materially higher returns on capital and uninterrupted comp growth — while the closer analogues for a mid-single-digit-growth, ~13%-ROIC turnaround are Dollar General at 17.2x and Dollar Tree at 20.2x. Target sits between them. So: a modest discount to the compounder cohort, appropriately, and mid-pack against the value cohort. Target does carry by far the highest yield in the group (2.95% vs. 0.6% median), which is real support and is the strongest single element of the value case.

**The IEEPA refund, probability-weighted into the base rather than parked in the bull case.** On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court held that IEEPA tariffs were not statutorily authorized; the CIT ordered CBP to implement refunds and CBP deployed the CAPE system on April 20, 2026. Target confirms it incurred IEEPA tariffs, is filing claims, and that as of May 2, 2026 **no refunds had been received and no receivable was recorded**; subsequent to quarter-end it began receiving refunds that "to date have not been material." Because nothing is booked, this asset cannot disappoint the balance sheet — it can only surprise upward. Arithmetic anchor: each $1.0B of pre-tax refund ≈ $777M after tax at the 22.3% FY2025 effective rate ≈ **$1.70 per diluted share**.

Quantifying the option honestly requires two disciplines. First, the dutiable base is unknowable from disclosures — Target is importer of record only for "most owned and exclusive, and certain national brand" merchandise, and owned/exclusive brands are ~30% of merchandise sales, so the base is well below 50% of the $75.5B cost of sales; a CIT appeal remains possible. Second, a refund is **one-time cash and deserves ~1x, not a P/E multiple**. On a plausible range of $0.5–2.0B pre-tax with meaningful probability of partial recovery, the expected present value is roughly **$1–3 per share, or 0.6–2.0% of the current equity value**. That is genuine, non-symmetric upside and I have added the low end of it to my base case. It is not large enough to change the rating. Crucially, the relief is not durable: the 10-K states that after the ruling the government imposed non-IEEPA Section 122 tariffs, and the 10-Q notes the administration "has previewed future actions that could restore or exceed the level of the IEEPA tariffs." The run-rate cost of goods may not improve at all.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Approximately fair. Not a value trap (earnings are inflecting, the yield is covered by earnings, the balance sheet is investment-grade) and not a mispricing I can demonstrate (the stock trades above consensus mean and median, at 20.4x trailing adjusted, with the out-year decelerating to 6.7% growth). Deutsche Bank's observation that the stock "is increasingly trading on confidence in Target's ability to deliver $10 plus of EPS in fiscal year 2027" — 15.4x at today's price — captures what is embedded: roughly 18% growth above the FY2026 high end, versus consensus at $9.03.

## Competitive Landscape

The relative-performance gap is the governing fact. The proxy's five-year cumulative TSR index shows $100 invested in Target becoming **$66.89** while the peer index reached **176.65** — roughly 110 percentage points of relative destruction. Walmart and Costco captured share through FY2023–FY2025. The structural reason is mix: Target's ~43% consumables weighting is a weaker traffic anchor than Walmart's grocery-dominated basket, and its discretionary general merchandise is more import-intensive, so Target absorbs disproportionate tariff pressure per sales dollar while holding less pricing power in the categories where consumers trade down.

Trajectory is what matters for scoring, and trajectory has turned. Q1 traffic +4.4%, digital comps +8.9%, all six categories positive, and the assortment reset delivering in Hardlines (+14.6%) and Beauty (+9.6%) are evidence of stabilization at minimum. Telsey's read — "the stores look better, in-stocks are better, and we saw better trends. We're not fully turned around, but it's on the right path" — is the fair characterization.

Two structural threats deserve weight. First, the Ulta exit: the shop-in-shop agreement expires August 2026 and, per multiple outlets based on a Target announcement, 600-plus stores will be converted this fall to an in-house "Target Beauty Studio." Substituting Target-controlled prestige beauty should be gross-margin accretive if traffic holds, since owned/exclusive brands carry higher margins. But it is a 600-store physical reset executed through back-to-school and holiday in a category growing 9.6%, and the Ulta arrangement's revenue-recognition and margin economics were never disclosed, so the P&L effect of the swap cannot be modeled. Second, AI-agent disintermediation: the 10-K warns that consumers may use third-party AI channels to initiate shopping searches and place orders, making Target dependent on those algorithms, and that AI may lower barriers to entry. This threatens Target twice — it bypasses the curated discovery that justifies Target's price premium, and it devalues Roundel inventory, whose value derives from engagement with Target's own digital properties. Target gave Roundel a standalone risk factor this year, noting advertisers have no long-term commitments.

**Competitive position: 5.0**, up modestly from prior work on one quarter of broad-based improvement, held back because the improvement cycled a −3.8% comp and the H2 beauty conversion introduces self-inflicted execution risk in the strongest category.

## Risk Assessment

**Q2 event risk (immediate).** Results land August 19, 2026, two days after this cutoff, with consensus at $2.33 (31 analysts) versus prior-year GAAP $2.05 — implying +13.7%. Basis labeling (GAAP vs. adjusted) is not disclosed by the sources, so treat the comparison as approximate.

**Tariff run-rate.** Section 122 tariffs replaced IEEPA. Q1 gross margin expanded 80bp *despite* higher product costs, which is encouraging, but the administration has previewed actions that could restore or exceed prior tariff levels, and roughly half of merchandise offered is imported with China the largest origin.

**Securities litigation.** Two consolidated federal class actions (D. Minn.) allege §10(b), §14(a) and §20(a) violations relating to ESG/DEI disclosures and the 2023 Pride collection, class periods spanning March 2022–November 2024. Both plaintiffs marked their cases as related to a prior individual action **in which the court denied a motion to dismiss** — substantially similar claims have already survived a pleading challenge. No reserve is disclosed.

**Realized reputational two-sided risk.** The 10-K discloses that 2025 modifications to DEI initiatives produced "adverse reactions from some of our shareholders, guests, team members, and others, as well as consumer boycotts organized throughout 2025," alongside prior adverse reaction to the 2023 Pride assortment. FY2025 traffic fell 2.2%. Being criticized from both political directions is an unusual profile for a mass retailer whose differentiation *is* brand equity.

**Governance.** An exempt-solicitation "vote-no" campaign by SOC Investment Group, Trillium and Mercy Investment Services targeted Executive Chair Cornell and lead independent director Leahy at the June 10, 2026 meeting. No board seats or capital-allocation changes were sought — reputational pressure, not a control contest, and not a modelable catalyst. The June 10 vote results (8-K Item 5.07, filed June 12) were not retrievable; materially low support for Cornell would change this assessment. The August 14, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/1.02/2.03) was also not retrievable; the item combination matches Target's prior October credit-facility renewals and the 364-day facility expires October 2026, so a routine replacement is most likely but unconfirmed.

**Other.** IRS transfer-pricing audits for FY2021–FY2022, gross uncertain tax positions $468M. Shipt worker-classification challenges, unquantified. Six Item 5.02 8-K filings between January and July 2026 confirm heavy leadership churn around the transition.

**Positive asymmetry.** The unbooked IEEPA receivable (worth ~$1–3/share expected, discussed above) and low short interest at 3.7% of float with 4.3 days to cover mean the setup is uncrowded — no squeeze dynamic distorting the tape in either direction.

Read as tactical protection on a position up 48% into a binary, not as a bearish structural view. Selling volatility here is more attractive than buying it, which is one reason a covered-call or collar overlay is a reasonable expression for holders — noted as tactics, not as a change to the rating.

**Risk factors: 5.0.** The balance sheet removes solvency risk, and short interest removes crowding risk, but there are three identifiable near-term binaries (the print, the 600-store H2 conversion, tariff policy) plus litigation where similar claims have survived dismissal, against a price 1.3% below an all-time high.

## Management Quality

**Leadership.** Michael Fiddelke, 49, became CEO in February 2026 after 20 years inside Target (COO 2024–2026, CFO 2019–2024). Brian Cornell moved to Executive Chair. Lisa Roath and Cara Sylvester were promoted to COO and Chief Merchandising Officer. Jim Lee (ex-PepsiCo) has been CFO since September 2024 — the only recent external senior hire. Two experienced retail directors were added: Stephen Bratspies (April 2026) and Joseph DePinto, former 7-Eleven CEO (August 2026). Board refresh with operating-retail experience is a constructive governance signal.

**Compensation and outcomes.** The pay-versus-performance table is unflattering on results and defensible on design. Granted CEO compensation rose 7% to $21.83M in a year of −1.7% sales, −9.4% net income and a cumulative TSR index of 66.89 versus a peer 176.65. The mark-to-market mechanism does function — "compensation actually paid" fell to $7.81M from $18.63M, and was negative $9.6M in FY2022. Performance measures (Net Sales, Incentive Operating Income, ROIC, EPS growth, Relative TSR) are a defensible set; including ROIC and *relative* TSR is genuinely good design for a capital-intensive retailer.

**Insider activity.** Cornell sold 50,000 shares on May 27, 2026 at ~$130 (~21% of post-vest holdings). Sylvester sold 10,000 at $125.89, Roath 7,000 at $138.07, Liegel 2,053 at $117.19. There are **no open-market purchases anywhere in the record**. Fiddelke made no open-market sales — award settlements and mandatory tax withholding only. These sales followed large March 11 / April 7 award settlements (Cornell settled ~101,000 shares) and are plausibly pre-arranged 10b5-1 dispositions; Form 4 data cannot distinguish scheduled from discretionary sales and I draw no inference about motive. The one observation I do weight: every insider sale cleared between $117 and $138 versus $154.48 today, and the person with the most information about the turnaround's durability sold nothing.

**Capital allocation.** Zero buybacks in Q1 with $8.3B authorized while capex ramps 34% — either discipline (declining to repurchase at 18–19x after a 48% run) or constraint. Dividend growth of 1.8% per year for two consecutive years, against 235 consecutive quarterly payments, communicates tightness regardless of which reading is correct.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $190 (+23%)

The Q1 inflection is the start of a multi-year recovery, not a comparison artifact. Positive comps hold in every quarter of FY2026 as guided, the assortment reset (3,000 new beauty SKUs, 75% of home décor reset, "Fun 101") sustains traffic through harder H2 comparisons, and the Beauty Studio conversion is gross-margin accretive as owned/exclusive prestige beauty replaces the Ulta shop-in-shop while Target holds the traffic. Retail media compounds at 40%-plus with a growing share netted against COGS, driving gross margin toward 29%-plus structurally. The ~$5B capex program restores asset turnover, lifting normalized ROIC back toward 15%. FY2027 EPS approaches the $10-plus figure Deutsche Bank identifies as embedded, and a $1–2B IEEPA refund adds $1.70–$3.40 per share of unbooked one-time cash that restores FCF slack and permits buyback resumption from the $8.3B authorization. At 19x $10.00 = $190.

### Bear Case — $120 (−22%)

Q1's +5.6% comp was cycling −3.8%; the two-year stack of +1.6% is the honest run-rate. As comparisons harden through H2 FY2026 and FY2027, comps decelerate toward flat, and 20.6% of sales in SG&A means each point of shortfall costs ~$210M of operating income. The 600-store Beauty Studio conversion disrupts the strongest category through holiday, and Ulta customers follow Ulta to standalone stores. The shrink tailwind is spent, so gross margin has no easy source of expansion, while Section 122 tariffs keep product costs elevated with no durable relief. Capex of $5B lands on a ~13%-ROIC base without lifting asset turnover, FCF falls short of the $2.1B dividend, buybacks stay at zero, and the multiple compresses back toward the 14–15x that a mid-single-digit-growth, 13%-ROIC retailer deserves. 14.5x $8.30 ≈ $120.

### Base Case — $158 (+2.3% price; ~+5.3% total return)

FY2026 delivers close to consensus $8.46 with positive but decelerating comps; FY2027 reaches roughly $9.03. Gross margin holds the Q1 gain but does not extend it materially as tariff costs offset retail-media mix. Capex peaks near $5B, FCF roughly covers the dividend, buybacks stay minimal, and a modest IEEPA recovery (expected value ~$1–3/share) provides a small cushion. The market pays 17.5x FY2027 $9.03 = $158, plus ~$1 of refund optionality. Probability-weighted (bear 25% / base 50% / bull 25%): **+1.4% price, +4.4% total return**.

### Rebuttal to a more negative rating

I considered REDUCE and reject it. Three pieces of evidence prevent it. First, the growth-adjusted valuation: forward PEG of ~1.55 on FY2026's +11.8% earnings growth (~2.0 blended over two years) does not describe an overvalued equity — the earlier "PEG ~2.7" framing used FY2027 growth against a FY2026 multiple and was wrong. Second, estimate revisions of +4.7% (FY2026) and +5.5% (FY2027) over 90 days, three consecutive beats, and a sell-side distribution drifting from 9 to 10 Buy ratings are all rising into any downgrade — you do not downgrade against improving forward estimates without identifying specific fundamental deterioration, and I cannot. Third, the unbooked IEEPA refund is a live, non-symmetric positive option worth ~0.6–2.0% of equity value that cannot disappoint the balance sheet. Set against a modest discount to the peer-median trailing multiple, the highest yield in the group at 2.95%, and an A2/A/A balance sheet with zero drawn commercial paper across three years, the risk/reward is approximately balanced, which is a hold.

Equally, I reject ACCUMULATE. The stock trades 7.7% above the analyst mean and 5.4% above the median, 1.3% below an all-time high, at 20.4x trailing adjusted EPS after a 48% twelve-month advance; the turnaround has one quarter of evidence against a −3.8% comparison; normalized ROIC is ~200bp below its FY2024 level while capex rises 34%; the dividend is thinly covered in the ramp year; and the strongest category is being physically reconstructed through holiday. There is no margin of safety at this price.
