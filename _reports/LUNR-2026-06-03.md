**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Intuitive Machines is a Houston-based space-infrastructure company that went public via SPAC in February 2023 and is best known for the first U.S. soft lunar landing since 1972 (IM-1, February 2024). Over the past nine months it has remade itself through three acquisitions, the largest being the ~$851M purchase of Maxar Space Systems (renamed Lanteris, closed January 13, 2026), pivoting from a NASA-dependent lunar lander pure-play into a self-described "vertically integrated space prime." The stock has risen roughly 260% over the trailing twelve months to ~$39.57 (intraday June 2), a ~$6.3B market capitalization, driven by sector momentum (SpaceX IPO buzz, the Rocket Lab/Planet Labs rally), a late-May Space Force IDIQ win, and analyst upgrades premised on NASA Moon-base and "Golden Dome" missile-defense exposure.

The fundamental picture does not support the price. The headline "record revenue" of $186.7M in Q1 2026 is a consolidation artifact: on a like-for-like pro-forma basis, the combined IM+Lanteris business shrank ~8% YoY to $199.8M from $218.2M. Both legs of the business (the legacy CLPS lunar program and Maxar's mature GEO comsat franchise) are flat-to-declining. The "positive adjusted EBITDA" of $2.7M relied on adding back $20M of deal costs against a GAAP operating loss that widened to -$39.2M; free cash flow was -$64.6M for the quarter. The company has missed consensus EPS in all four trailing quarters (Q1 2026 by 232%), and consensus estimates are in freefall: current-year EPS has been revised from +$0.05 ninety days ago to -$0.38 today.

We rate LUNR AVOID. The valuation embeds future contract wins (NASA lunar permanence, Golden Dome) that are speculative and years from revenue. The capital structure depends on serial dilution (share count has roughly tripled in two years), and governance is a concern: founder super-voting control, an insider-favorable 85% Tax Receivable Agreement, and persistent founder/executive selling into strength with no offsetting buying. The acquired business brings inherited liabilities (a DOJ False Claims Act probe, a ~$57M underfunded pension). We are not recommending an active short given 23.3% short interest and severe momentum, but the asymmetry for new long capital is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Intuitive Machines (founded 2013) operates as a single reporting segment organized around three pillars: Build (spacecraft, landers, propulsion, avionics), Connect (communications, navigation, data relay: NASA Near Space Network, KinetX deep-space navigation, the pending Goonhilly ground stations), and Operate (mission operations, hosted payloads, PNT/data services). It is founded and chaired by Dr. Kamal Ghaffarian (also behind X-Energy and Axiom Space), with CEO Stephen Altemus and CTO Tim Crain as co-founders.

The corporate identity changed materially over the last twelve months. As recently as FY2024, ~90% of revenue came from a single NASA customer. Three acquisitions reshaped the company:

1. **KinetX** (October 2025, ~$31M) — deep-space navigation.
2. **Lanteris / Maxar Space Systems** (closed January 13, 2026, ~$851M: $403M cash + 22.99M shares + $44M bonuses) — the transformational deal, adding GEO commercial comsats (EchoStar, SiriusXM), national-security spacecraft (the SDA Tracking Layer), NASA's Gateway Power & Propulsion Element, an installed base of orbital receivables, and ~$613M of acquired backlog.
3. **Goonhilly** (UK/US ground stations, announced May 2026, ~£37M) — partly unexecuted; the MIPA was not signed as of the latest 10-Q.

The company now competes against established primes (Lockheed, Boeing, L3Harris, Northrop) in GEO comsats and defense spacecraft, alongside its first-mover-but-small CLPS lunar niche.

## Financial Analysis

**Pre-acquisition trajectory was already weak.** FY2025 revenue declined ~8% to ~$208M from ~$228M in FY2024, hurt by NASA's cancellation of the OSAM/OMES III task orders and the completion of IM-2. The standalone lunar business has never been profitable.

**Q1 2026 (first consolidated quarter with Lanteris):**
- Total revenue $186.7M vs $62.5M in Q1 2025, but Lanteris contributed $141.6M for ~11 weeks. On a pro-forma combined basis, Q1 2026 revenue of $199.8M was **down ~8%** from $218.2M pro-forma in Q1 2025. The growth headline is an accounting artifact of consolidation, not organic momentum.
- Revenue mix transformed: Civil 38% / Commercial 33% / National Security 27%, versus ~83% civil/NASA a year earlier. Customer concentration improved from one customer at 78% to four at 36/26/13/13%.
- Revenue is now 87% fixed-price (vs 61% prior year), which raises cost-overrun risk.
- GAAP operating loss widened to -$39.2M (from -$10.1M); net loss to Class A holders was -$37.5M (-$0.25/share).
- Adjusted EBITDA was nominally positive at +$2.7M, but flattered by $20M of acquisition costs plus $8.8M SBC and $13M D&A in add-backs. Quality is low.
- Free cash flow was -$64.6M; operating cash flow -$54.8M.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Cash fell from $582.6M at YE2025 to $231.6M after the Lanteris cash payment, partially offset by a $175M equity raise at $15.12 (February 2026, 11.6M shares). There are $345M of 2.5% convertible notes due 2030 (conversion price ~$13.11; capped-call cap $20.98) trading near $607.6M fair value, deep in the money and signaling probable future dilution. The reported -$333M shareholders' deficit is largely an Up-C/SPAC accounting artifact ($1.06B of redeemable noncontrolling interest from founder LLC units sits in mezzanine equity); it does not indicate insolvency, and working capital is positive at $89.8M. The company inherited a ~$57M underfunded frozen pension from Maxar ($392.8M PBO vs $336.1M assets) and now carries $217.5M of non-current orbital receivables backed by a $250M ING securitization facility, a complex working-capital structure new to the entity.

**Backlog and conversion.** Backlog jumped to $1.1B (from $213M), but remaining performance obligations are only $792M; the gap is constrained/variable consideration and IDIQ funded amounts. Roughly 60-65% is expected to convert in 2026.

**Earnings quality.** The track record is four consecutive misses (Q1 2026 -232%, Q4 2025 -338%, Q3 2025 -21%, Q2 2025 a triple-digit miss). The cash burn rate of ~$65M/quarter against ~$232M of cash implies the company funds itself through continued issuance.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case rests on two macro tailwinds: NASA's sustained Artemis/lunar presence and U.S. missile-defense/national-security space ("Golden Dome"). Both are real spending categories, and the Lanteris acquisition gives IM a credible seat at the defense-spacecraft table (SDA Tracking Layer) it did not have a year ago. The late-May Space Force IDIQ win and the IM-5 NASA award ($161.4M, launch ~mid-2030) are consistent with the national-security pivot.

The problem is that organic growth is negative today. Both segments — legacy lunar (NASA-budget-dependent, with OSAM/OMES III already cancelled out from under the company) and the acquired GEO comsat business (mature, structurally declining as the satellite market shifts to LEO constellations) — are contracting. The growth thesis requires new contract wins to reverse the pro-forma decline, and the contracts cited (NASA Moon base, Golden Dome) are largely unscoped, multi-year, and not yet in backlog as funded RPO.

Estimate revisions are the most damning forward signal. Current-quarter EPS has been revised from +$0.01 ninety days ago to -$0.09; current-year EPS from +$0.05 to -$0.38 (-862% over 90 days); next-year EPS from +$0.33 to -$0.12. Consensus is moving sharply in the wrong direction even as the stock has risen, a configuration of price rallying on narrative while earnings momentum deteriorates.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39.57 and ~$6.3B market cap on ~$200M of shrinking, low-quality, acquisition-inflated revenue, LUNR trades at ~30x trailing/annualized sales for a business with widening GAAP losses and negative free cash flow. There are no positive forward earnings to anchor a P/E, and the forward P/E is negative (-45x). The "cycle-adjusted trough earnings" framing that rescues some high-multiple names does not apply: this is not a depressed-margin business about to inflect upward, it is a perpetually loss-making business whose revenue is declining on a like-for-like basis.

Analyst targets span $11 (low) to $75 (high), with a $40.78 mean and $42 median, so the stock trades essentially at consensus mean. The recent Canaccord ($41) and Cantor ($43) targets reflect the same Golden Dome / Moon-base extrapolation embedded in the price, so they are not independent confirmation of upside. The price sits far above the $15.12 February PIPE and the ~$13 convert strike, underscoring how much of the move is momentum- and news-driven rather than fundamentally underwritten.

We score valuation low (3.0). The multiple is stretched against contracting fundamentals, and the gap is justified only by speculative future awards. A reasonable fair-value range on visible RPO and a generous space-prime revenue multiple sits well below the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

IM's first-mover CLPS lunar position is genuine but commercially small and entirely NASA-budget-dependent. The Lanteris acquisition repositions the company to compete in GEO comsats and national-security spacecraft, but here it faces entrenched primes (Lockheed, Boeing, L3Harris, Northrop) with deeper balance sheets and longer track records, and it inherited a mature franchise in a market migrating toward LEO constellations. Within the high-beta commercial-space cohort (Rocket Lab, Planet Labs, Firefly, Astrobotic), IM is a polarizing name with both momentum bulls and a large short base.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The defense pivot improves the addressable market and reduces single-customer concentration, which is a real positive. But the company is integrating three acquisitions in under nine months, doubling its size overnight, and management itself flags ongoing integration of Lanteris into its internal control environment. Execution risk on 87% fixed-price revenue — including IM-3 and IM-4, both contractually in a loss position — caps the competitive score at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

- **Funding dependence.** Civil plus national security is ~65% of revenue, overwhelmingly U.S. federal — exposed to shutdowns, continuing resolutions, NASA budget cuts (OSAM cancellation is a live precedent), and administration priorities.
- **Fixed-price loss contracts.** IM-3 (~89% complete, accruing losses) and IM-4 (~42%) are both in a loss position; ~10% of lunar contract value is variable consideration constrained to nil until a successful landing.
- **Mission execution.** IM-2's lander tipped on landing; a future failure forfeits constrained revenue and damages competitive standing. Single launch provider (SpaceX) dependence.
- **Lanteris DOJ False Claims Act probe.** A 2023 Civil Investigative Demand over cybersecurity compliance; DOJ presented its position alleging FCA violations in late 2025. Seller Vantor agreed to indemnify, but the matter is unresolved.
- **Dilution / convertible overhang.** Converts deeply in-the-money; ~26% of the operating LLC remains founder-held redeemable NCI exchangeable into Class A; serial equity issuance is structural.
- **Tax Receivable Agreement.** IM owes TRA holders (founders/insiders) 85% of cash tax savings should it become profitable — a long-tail, insider-favorable obligation.
- **Pension underfunding** (~$57M, newly inherited).

These are largely specific and not fully priced into a $6.3B valuation, which is why the risk score is low (3.0).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 158.6% against 104.4% historical volatility (rich by 54 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (158.6% near-term vs 118.7% six-month), so the market expects a near-term catalyst or resolution. Skew is modestly call-heavy (-6.6%), and put/call OI is 0.63, consistent with a momentum/speculative long base rather than heavy hedging. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.2%. Unusual activity shows two-sided positioning (June $36 calls and $34/$33 puts both active), reflecting the polarized nature of the name. For a prospective long, IV this rich means options are an expensive way to participate, and the backwardation flags that the market does not expect the current calm to persist.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The space sector is in a multi-year structural up-cycle; NASA's lunar-permanence ambitions and Golden Dome missile-defense spending create a large addressable market. The Lanteris acquisition gives IM a defense-prime platform and diversifies it away from single-customer NASA dependence. If the company converts its backlog and wins a share of new Golden Dome / lunar-infrastructure awards, revenue could re-accelerate and the EBITDA inflection becomes real. In a momentum tape with 23.3% short interest, positive news (a large dated contract win) could squeeze the stock toward the $75 high target.

**Bear Case.** The growth narrative is an accounting illusion: pro-forma revenue is shrinking ~8%, both segments are mature/declining, and the cited future contracts are unscoped and years away. Estimates are collapsing (-862% current-year revision), the company has missed four straight quarters, FCF is -$65M/quarter, and funding depends on dilution against a deeply in-the-money convert. Governance is misaligned: founder super-voting control, an 85% TRA, and near-biweekly founder/executive selling into strength with zero buying. If sector momentum fades or a mission/contract disappoints, the stock retraces toward the February PIPE price ($15.12) and the convert strike (~$13), implying ~60-67% downside to the analyst low of $11.

The weight of evidence favors the bear case for new capital: contracting organic fundamentals, deteriorating estimates, fragile earnings quality, and conspicuous insider selling all point the same direction, while the bull case depends on future awards not yet in funded backlog.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalysts are the next quarterly print (mid-August 2026, likely another miss given the revision trend and continued integration drag) and the eventual resolution of the Lanteris DOJ FCA matter (response due in coming months). Neither is a hard, single-day trigger of the kind that reliably breaks a momentum stock; the more probable de-rating mechanism is a fade in space-sector sentiment (the SpaceX-IPO-driven rally cooling) combined with the next earnings miss.

**Timing window.** A 6-to-12-week window into the August print is plausible, but the carry is expensive (see below) and the timing is sentiment-dependent rather than event-certain.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 23.3% of float with 2.4 days to cover, already crowded and squeeze-prone. The stock just printed a 52-week high (~$46.75 on May 28) on a Space Force win; momentum and squeeze dynamics are acute. This is the dominant reason not to short.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV (backwardated) makes puts very expensive, and the modestly call-heavy skew with low put OI shows the options market is not confirming a clean bearish thesis. High implied vol reflects elevated borrow/event cost.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Fundamental fair value sits closer to the $11-15 zone (PIPE/convert anchors), implying ~60% downside if the narrative breaks. But the upside risk on a squeeze or large contract win is open-ended (the $75 high target), and the immediate path is into a momentum tape. Reward:risk on a fresh short is roughly 1:1 to 1.5:1 at best given squeeze asymmetry, below the 2:1 bar.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish fundamental thesis is correct, but 23.3% short interest, 2.4 days to cover, a fresh 52-week high, and rich/backwardated options make the remaining downside unattractive relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not initiate a short here. Revisit only after a momentum break confirms (close below the 50-DMA on heavy volume) and short interest unwinds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID for new long capital.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would reconsider if: (1) the company demonstrates organic pro-forma revenue growth (not consolidation-driven) for two consecutive quarters; (2) estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive; (3) the company reaches sustainable positive free cash flow without further equity issuance, removing the dilution overhang; (4) valuation compresses to a level supported by funded RPO (a 3-5x sales multiple on visible backlog, materially below current), or the stock retraces toward the $15 PIPE / $13 convert anchors with the defense pivot intact; and (5) founder/executive selling ceases or open-market insider buying appears.

**Timeline.** The integration overhang, fixed-price loss contracts, and negative estimate momentum are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters. The Golden Dome / Moon-base contract wins that underpin the bull case are multi-year in nature, so the fundamental case is unlikely to mature within a 12-month window. Reassess at the August 2026 print and on resolution of the DOJ FCA matter.

## Conclusion

LUNR has re-rated ~260% over twelve months on sector momentum and contract-win optimism, but the underlying business is contracting on a like-for-like basis, generating large GAAP losses and -$65M/quarter free cash flow, missing every recent estimate, and funding itself through serial dilution. Consensus EPS estimates have collapsed even as the stock rose. Governance is misaligned via founder super-voting control, an 85% TRA, and persistent insider selling into strength. The valuation prices in speculative future awards rather than visible fundamentals. We rate the stock AVOID. We are not recommending an active short given 23.3% short interest, a fresh 52-week high, and richly priced options; the asymmetry favors staying out rather than betting against the momentum directly.
