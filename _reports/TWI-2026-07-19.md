**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Titan International is a global manufacturer of off-highway wheels, tires, undercarriage systems, and complete wheel/tire assemblies serving agricultural, earthmoving/construction, and consumer/powersports end markets. At ~$7.66 the stock trades near the low end of its 52-week range ($6.43–$11.70) with a ~$470M market cap, negative trailing EPS (-$1.00), and negative free cash flow. The business is in year two of a severe ag-machinery downcycle that has compressed operating margin from ~9.5% in 2022 to ~1.1% in 2025, layered onto a debt-funded 2024 Carlstar acquisition that was financed near the top of the cycle.

Titan is a competent operator: footprint rationalization, capex discipline, and productivity work held Q1 2026 gross margin at 14.1% despite lower volumes, and the wheel-plus-tire assembly bundle is a genuine niche moat. The open question is cycle timing and balance-sheet resilience. Three facts dominate: (1) the consolidated numbers mask a bifurcated company where the U.S. guarantor group backing the 2028 notes lost ~$81M in 2025 while foreign operations carried the enterprise, with $145M of $171M cash held offshore; (2) a $400M 7.00% senior secured note matures April 2028, and refinancing a loss-making domestic entity is the dominant multi-year overhang; and (3) American Industrial Partners holds ~8.9M shares (~14% of shares outstanding) with an active shelf and sold 3.04M shares at $8.95 in March 2026, above today's price, a structural supply cap.

Against that, earthmoving/construction is inflecting (+11% Q1 sales on mining strength), the stock trades near trough earnings, and estimate revisions have stabilized after a sharp cut earlier in the year. Consensus targets sit at $10–$13, well above spot. This is a leveraged cyclical with real optionality and real balance-sheet risk, and no open-market insider buying to anchor a bottom. We rate TWI a HOLD (uncertain), consistent with the trajectory upgrade from AVOID over the prior three reports, but we do not see an asymmetric entry here.

## Company Overview

Titan (roots in the Electric Wheel Company, 1890) is one of few players that manufactures both wheels and tires, enabling delivery of complete assemblies, a differentiator given the custom tooling, dies, and molds required. It operates three segments:

- **Agricultural (~41% of 2025 sales, $741M):** wheels/tires for tractors, combines, planters, irrigation, sold to OEMs and aftermarket. Brands include Goodyear Farm Tire (licensed to 2029), Titan Tire, Voltyre-Prom.
- **Earthmoving/Construction (~32%, $582M):** OTR tires, wheels, and vertically integrated undercarriage systems (track groups, rollers, idlers) for mining, construction, forestry, including a Spain-based integrated foundry.
- **Consumer (~28%, $506M):** largely the Carlstar/Titan Specialty business acquired February 2024 — powersports, outdoor power equipment, high-speed trailers under Carlisle (licensed to 2033), ITP, and others. The highest-margin segment (~20% gross margin) on aftermarket mix.

Key customers include Deere (10% of sales), Caterpillar, CNH, AGCO, Kubota, Volvo, and Liebherr; the top 10 are ~33% of sales. Roughly 50% of sales are outside the U.S., with manufacturing located in the markets served, a structure management frames as advantaged amid tariff turmoil.

## Financial Analysis

The core story is a cyclical earnings collapse layered onto debt-funded M&A.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,169M | $1,822M | $1,846M | $1,828M |
| Gross margin | 16.6% | 16.8% | 14.0% | 13.9% |
| Operating income | $206M | $149M | $33M | $21M |
| Operating margin | 9.5% | 8.2% | 1.8% | 1.1% |
| Net income | $176M | $79M | -$5.6M | -$63.5M |
| Long-term debt | $415M | $409M | $553M | $565M |

Revenue is flat since 2023 only because Carlstar masked underlying volume declines. Organic ag and E/C volumes fell on lower farmer income, high financing costs, and OEM destocking; consumer growth is entirely acquisition-driven. Operating margin fell roughly 840bp from 2022 to 2025, a classic ag-cycle downturn compounded by lost fixed-cost leverage.

The 2025 net loss of $63.5M is distorted by a $49.9M tax provision against only an $11.3M pre-tax loss, driven by valuation allowances established against U.S. and Luxembourg deferred tax assets. Mechanically this reflects the three-year cumulative loss test failing; interpretively it is management conceding it does not expect enough near-term domestic taxable income to use those assets. That is a candid bearish tell embedded in the financials.

The guarantor group (essentially U.S. operations plus guarantor subsidiaries) posted an $80.8M net loss and -$34.9M operating loss in 2025; the consolidated company stays afloat on European, Latin American, and Russian profitability. $181M of $203M cash was offshore at YE2025 ($145M of $171M at Q1 2026). Because the note guarantors are the U.S. entities, the credit story is weaker than the consolidated cash balance implies.

Cash flow quality deteriorated sharply: operating cash flow fell from $141M in 2024 (boosted by working-capital release and acquired working capital) to $30M in 2025, with FCF of -$24.6M after $54.6M capex. Balance sheet leverage is D/E ~2.24x with a current ratio of 2.3x and an Altman Z of 2.23 (grey zone). The revolver ($225M, matures February 2029) carried only ~$36–50M net availability, so effective liquidity is tighter than the headline cash suggests. The covenant structure adds risk: a fixed-charge coverage test of ≥1.0x triggers if availability falls below the greater of $17M or 10% of the line cap.

Q1 2026 revenue rose 2.9% to $505M, but entirely FX-driven (+3.7% from a stronger real/euro); organic ag and consumer volumes were still down. A $25.1M restructuring/impairment charge for the Jackson, TN plant closure ($23.5M impairment + $1.6M severance) drove a $24.3M net loss (-$0.38/share). Operating cash flow was -$46.5M on seasonal AR build. E/C inflected positively (+11% sales, mining strength), the one genuine operating bright spot.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is a cyclical-recovery bet, not a secular one. The mechanism is straightforward: mineral-commodity strength and mining capex are lifting earthmoving/construction demand, which flows to Titan's OTR tires, wheels, and undercarriage volumes. Large-ag remains weak as Deere, AGCO, and CNH cut production and push dealer destocking, and consumer/powersports remains soft in the Americas.

The forward earnings picture illustrates the leverage and the fragility. Estimate revisions cut deeply over 90 days — current-year EPS revised from +$0.04 to -$0.13 (-381%), current-quarter from +$0.04 to -$0.04 — before stabilizing over the trailing 30 days (no change across near-term periods). Next-year consensus is +$0.37 and was revised up +10.4% over 90 days, implying analysts expect a return to profitability in 2027 as the cycle turns and the Jackson closure and productivity work land. On the 30%+ estimate-revision buy signal, TWI fails the test: near-term revisions are flat and the 90-day trend was sharply negative. The positive next-year revision is real but modest and reflects trough-to-recovery normalization rather than an accelerating recovery.

The earnings surprise record is inconsistent: over four quarters, one beat, one in-line, two misses, including a -100% surprise in Q1 2026 (estimate +$0.04, actual $0.00). This argues for low confidence in near-term consensus and reinforces that operating leverage cuts both ways at a high-fixed-cost, thin-margin cyclical.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are meaningless here: the company is loss-making, so trailing/forward P/E of -5.7x is an artifact. The valuation debate is entirely about normalized earnings power and balance-sheet resilience through the 2028 refinancing.

Peer medians (P/E 26.7, dividend yield 1.2%) are of limited use because the peers (LNN, AGCO, TTC, CNH, DE) are larger, more diversified, and profitable through this downcycle. Titan's discount to peers reflects higher leverage, a loss-making U.S. entity, a refinancing wall, and a private-equity overhang. Applying a peer multiple to Titan's normalized earnings would be a category error.

On normalized power, the picture is more constructive but far from asymmetric. Next-year consensus of $0.37 puts the stock at ~21x forward on a recovery estimate, not cheap for a thin-margin cyclical carrying refinancing risk. On a mid-cycle basis, if the cycle turns and Titan can approach mid-single-digit operating margins on ~$1.9B revenue, normalized EPS could reach $1.00–$1.50, which would make ~$7.66 look inexpensive. But that path runs directly through the April 2028 note maturity, and the market is correctly withholding a recovery multiple until the refinancing is de-risked.

The AIP overhang is a hard valuation anchor: a large holder sold at $8.95 in March 2026 and still holds ~8.9M shares against an active shelf. The stock trades below where an informed seller was willing to exit, and further sales will likely cap rallies until AIP is out. Cheapness on trough earnings, without a catalyst to convert it, is the value-trap pattern that has punished "cheap" names; we do not reward the low absolute price on its own. We assign a valuation score of 4.5: there is genuine cycle-adjusted upside optionality, but no independent catalyst to unlock it and a specific overhang capping it.

## Competitive Landscape

Titan competes against larger, better-resourced players — Michelin, Bridgestone, Pirelli, and low-cost Balkrishna (BKT) and Alliance/Yokohama in tires (a persistent import threat, hence Titan's history of AD/CVD trade petitions against Indian/Sri Lankan OTR tires); Accuride, Trelleborg, Topy, Maxion, Wheels India in wheels; Berco and Caterpillar in undercarriage. Titan competes on the assembly bundle and custom engineering (LSW low-sidewall technology) rather than scale.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving in trajectory. The wheel-plus-tire vertical integration is a real, narrow moat: custom tooling and molds create barriers to entry, and OEMs value single-source assemblies. Carlstar diversified the mix toward higher-margin, less-cyclical aftermarket consumer, which is strategically sound. The E/C recovery and mining exposure are share-relevant tailwinds. Against that, the company's own disclosure shows TSR lag versus peers (161 vs. 291 in 2025), OEMs continually pressure suppliers on cost and could in-source wheel/tire production, and low-cost import competition is structural. On balance the moat is durable but narrow, and the trajectory is modestly favorable given the mining tailwind, supporting a competitive score of 5.5, consistent with the prior report's upgrade.

This is a Materials-sector name, one of the weakest sectors for company-specific selection alpha on our historical scoring. That reinforces the need for a distinct company catalyst before any positive call; the E/C recovery is real but not yet Titan-specific enough to clear that higher bar.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cyclicality plus earnings leverage.** The dominant risk. All three end-markets are cyclical, and high fixed costs mean small volume swings produce large margin swings, demonstrated by the 2022→2025 collapse.
2. **Debt maturity wall.** $400M of 7.00% notes due April 2028, to be refinanced while the U.S. entity is loss-making. Revolver net availability is thin (~$36–50M), and the fixed-charge coverage covenant triggers if availability falls further.
3. **Deferred tax valuation allowances** signal management's own reduced confidence in near-term U.S. profitability, a self-reported bearish tell.
4. **Tariff exposure.** The April 2025 IEEPA "reciprocal tariffs" were struck down by the Federal Circuit and affirmed by the Supreme Court in February 2026; the administration pivoted to a 10% (potentially 15%) global tariff under other trade laws, creating input-cost pressure, supply-chain uncertainty, unresolved duty-refund questions, and depressed farmer sentiment.
5. **AIP selling overhang.** Active shelf; a structural supply cap on a $470M-cap stock.
6. **Customer concentration.** Deere 10%; top 10 ~33%. OEM cost pressure and in-sourcing risk.
7. **Russia exposure.** 64.3%-owned Voltyre-Prom (~7% of assets, ~4% of sales) is subject to sanctions and non-repatriable earnings.
8. **Unhedged commodity risk** on steel, rubber, and carbon black.

These risks are largely known and partly priced; the stock sits near its 52-week low with a compressed absolute value. But several are asymmetric and not fully discounted: the 2028 refinancing is a binary de-risking or dilution event, and the tax valuation allowance and Russia exposure are idiosyncratic. We score risk factors 4.2, an improvement from the prior 3.5→4.5 trajectory but reflecting that the refinancing and overhang tails remain live.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 59.6% to 66.0% over the past month while the price was roughly flat (+1.2%), indicating the market is pricing increasing uncertainty ahead of the July 30, 2026 Q2 print rather than resolved calm. Short interest is moderate at 5.5% of float with 4.3 days to cover — uncrowded, meaning neither a squeeze setup nor strong informed bearish positioning. The rising IV into earnings warrants patience on any entry; a post-print IV crush and a cleaner read on E/C momentum versus ag weakness would be a more favorable timing window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Titan is a leveraged cyclical trading near trough earnings at a depressed cap. Earthmoving/mining is inflecting, Carlstar has diversified the mix toward higher-margin aftermarket, and self-help (Jackson closure, capex discipline, productivity) is protecting margins in the trough. If the ag cycle turns in 2026–2027 and mining strength persists, operating leverage could drive normalized EPS toward $1.00–$1.50, and successful refinancing of the 2028 notes removes the dominant overhang. Next-year consensus was revised up, and analyst targets of $10–$13 imply roughly 30–70% upside. Bull target ~$11–$12 (52-week high area, roughly next-year recovery earnings times a mid-cycle multiple).

**Bear case.** The U.S. guarantor group keeps losing money into the refinancing at higher rates, forcing dilutive equity or expensive debt; the ag downcycle extends; tariffs pressure input costs and farmer sentiment; AIP continues selling into any strength. FCF stays negative, the covenant tightens as revolver availability erodes, and the equity re-rates lower. Bear target ~$6.00–$6.40 (52-week low), roughly -16% to -22% from spot.

The setup is genuinely two-sided. There is no open-market insider buying to signal a bottom (the sole small COO purchase at $8.56 in August 2025 is now underwater), estimate revisions are flat near-term, and the largest informed seller exited above today's price. We lack a distinct, dated company catalyst to convert trough cheapness into upside, and the balance-sheet tail is real. That combination supports HOLD (uncertain), not BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 results on July 30, 2026 are the near-term catalyst — watch E/C momentum, guarantor-group profitability, revolver availability, and any refinancing commentary on the 2028 notes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive current-year EPS revisions (+15% or more) alongside E/C revenue growth sustained above 10%, confirming the cyclical recovery.
- A credible refinancing or extension of the $400M 2028 notes on manageable terms, or the U.S. guarantor group returning to operating profitability.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, or AIP fully exiting its position, removing the supply overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revolver net availability falling toward the covenant trigger (below ~$17M/10% of line cap) or any covenant breach.
- Further downward estimate revisions pushing next-year consensus back into loss territory, or a Q2 miss confirming ag weakness bleeding into E/C.
- A dilutive equity raise announced to address the 2028 maturity.

## Conclusion

Titan is a competently managed but structurally leveraged off-highway cyclical caught between a genuine earthmoving/mining recovery and a set of hard balance-sheet constraints: a loss-making U.S. guarantor group, a $400M note wall in April 2028, thin revolver liquidity, and a private-equity overhang selling above the current price. The stock is cheap on normalized earnings power but the cheapness lacks a catalyst to unlock it, and the risks that matter are asymmetric and not fully priced.

We rate TWI a **HOLD (uncertain)**, an overall score of 4.5, consistent with the improving trajectory (4.2 → 4.0 → 4.7) but stopping short of a constructive call. The recommendation history has moved from AVOID to HOLD as the mining recovery and self-help became visible; sustaining or upgrading requires evidence the reader can verify: a refinancing path on the 2028 notes, sustained E/C-led revision improvement, and removal of the AIP overhang. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced and there is no asymmetric entry. Patience through the July 30 print is warranted.
