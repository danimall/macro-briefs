**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Keysight is the global leader in electronic design, emulation, and test (D&T) instrumentation, in the middle of a sharp recovery driven by two of the strongest demand themes in the market: AI data-center build-out and defense modernization. Q2 FY2026 (ended April 30) delivered revenue of $1,717M (+31% YoY), adjusted EPS of $2.87 against a $2.33 consensus (a 23% beat), and orders up 56% YoY surpassing $2B — orders running ahead of revenue, which typically signals continued momentum. Management called it the best quarter in company history and raised FY2026 revenue guidance to high-20s% growth. Consensus EPS estimates have risen ~26% for the current year and ~28% for next year over the past 90 days.

The complication is the quality of those headline figures and the price already paid for them. H1 FY2026 results were meaningfully flattered by two non-recurring items: a ~$57M net operating-income benefit from an IEEPA tariff-refund receivable (roughly 4.6 percentage points of Q2 gross margin), and a $97M discrete tax benefit that drove the six-month effective tax rate to negative 2.3% versus a normalized ~16-20%. Strip those out and the underlying growth is still strong, but reported profitability overstates the run-rate. At $364, up roughly 113-127% over the trailing year, KEYS trades at ~31-36x stated forward EPS and closer to ~37-39x on normalized run-rate earnings — a ~40% premium to the 27x peer median.

The franchise quality, the IT-sector tailwind, and the estimate-revision momentum all argue for owning this name. The entry valuation and the quality-of-earnings flags argue against initiating at $364. We rate KEYS a HOLD (growth hold): the 12-month thesis is positive with concrete catalysts, but the current multiple already prices durable high-20s% growth that the company itself has cautioned may not be sustainable. The actionable path is to accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on confirmation that order growth and margins hold once the one-time benefits roll off.

## Company Overview

Keysight provides hardware instrumentation, software, systems, and services that customers use to design, validate, and manufacture electronic and communications products. It is the former Electronic Measurement business of Agilent (itself spun from Hewlett-Packard in 1999) and was incorporated as a standalone company in 2013. The fiscal year ends October 31.

The business reports in two segments:

- **Communications Solutions Group (CSG)** — roughly 69-72% of revenue. Serves commercial communications (wireless/wireline, the data-center ecosystem) and aerospace, defense & government (A/D/G). This is the AI-leveraged engine: 400G/800G/1.6-terabit Ethernet transceiver test, high-speed networking, 5G and early 6G, satellite/non-terrestrial networks, quantum, and defense modernization (radar, spectrum, space). In Q2 FY2026, CSG commercial-communications revenue grew 40% YoY and A/D/G grew 24%.
- **Electronic Industrial Solutions Group (EISG)** — roughly 28-31% of revenue. Semiconductor, general electronics, automotive/energy, and design/simulation software. Driven by advanced-node semiconductor R&D, fab capacity additions, software-defined vehicles, and digital health.

Keysight is a picks-and-shovels supplier to the AI infrastructure cycle, not a direct AI-chip or model play. It benefits from R&D and manufacturing test spend across many customers rather than betting on any single AI winner, and no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. Primary competitors include Rohde & Schwarz (private), Anritsu, NI/Emerson, Teradyne, and Viavi in test; and Ansys/Synopsys/Cadence in adjacent design-software areas.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory ($M):**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,420 | 5,464 | 4,979 | 5,375 |
| Operating income | 1,334 | 1,358 | 833 | 876 |
| Operating margin | 24.6% | 24.8% | 16.7% | 16.3% |
| Net income | 1,124 | 1,057 | 614 | 850 |
| Operating cash flow / FCF | — | 1,408 | 1,052 | 1,281 |

FY2024 was a genuine downcycle: revenue fell 9% and operating margin compressed roughly 8 points as fixed costs hit lower volume. That history is the central anchor of the bear case, because it demonstrates that Keysight's order-driven revenue is cyclical and capex-sensitive.

The recovery into FY2026 has been steep. Q2 FY2026 posted GAAP net income of $349M ($2.02 diluted). Gross margin reached 68.6% (+6 points YoY) and operating margin 23.7% (+8 points). Management guided Q3 revenue to $1.730-1.750B, a beat-and-raise.

**Quality-of-earnings caveat.** The H1 margin and EPS spike carried two material non-recurring tailwinds:

1. **IEEPA tariff refund.** After the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unlawful, Keysight booked a $100M refund receivable, recognizing a ~$57M net benefit to operating income alongside a $40M revenue reduction for customer surcharge refunds. Recovery timing and amount are uncertain.
2. **Discrete tax benefit.** A $97M favorable audit settlement drove the six-month effective tax rate to negative 2.3%. The normalized rate is ~16-20%.

Underlying growth is real — orders up 56% is not a tax artifact — but the reported profitability overstates the sustainable run-rate, and consensus EPS revisions partly reflect H1 figures inflated by these items.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Solid. Cash of $2,412M against total debt of $2,531M across four senior-note tranches (the $700M 4.6% 2027 notes are now current but fully covered by cash). Current ratio 2.35, debt/equity ~0.92, FCF margin ~24%. Capex is light (~$128M in FY25, guided ~$200M FY26 for capacity). The Altman Z of 2.13 ("grey zone") is a false signal driven by goodwill-heavy assets and recovering EBIT, not distress.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. A new $1.5B buyback was authorized in November 2025 ($1,192M remaining at April 2026), with ~$307M repurchased in H1 FY2026. The bulk of repurchases occurred while the stock was making new highs, which is mediocre timing. M&A is the primary use of capital.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on broad, confirming evidence rather than a single data point:

- **Order momentum.** Orders running ahead of revenue is the strongest leading indicator in the print.
- **Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus EPS rose to $10.19 from $8.11 ninety days ago (+25.7%); next year rose to $11.86 from $9.30 (+27.5%). The 30-day revisions (+14.4% current year, +14.7% next year) show the velocity is accelerating, not fading. This is just under the strongest +30% threshold but combined with >30% revenue growth, it is a high-conviction forward signal.
- **Beat consistency.** Four straight beats, with the magnitude widening (0.9% → 2.2% → 6.6% → 21.2%). The widening surprise is consistent with consensus systematically lagging an accelerating demand environment.
- **Structural tailwind breadth.** The AI test thread is concrete and disclosed — surging demand for 400G/800G/1.6T Ethernet test across both R&D and high-volume transceiver manufacturing as the data-center ecosystem scales networking. The Spirent and Synopsys-OSG acquisitions deepen positions in network assurance and optical/photonic design. Defense (radar, space, spectrum) provides government-funded, counter-cyclical ballast.

The durability question is the crux. Management added new risk language in the FY25 10-K and Q2 10-Q noting that its markets "have recently experienced significant growth which may not be sustainable," and added "market perceptions regarding artificial intelligence" as a stock-price volatility factor. A hyperscaler capex pause — the single most-watched risk in the current AI cycle — would hit CSG order flow directly. The growth is genuine; the debate is whether high-20s% is a new baseline or a cyclical peak.

## Valuation Assessment

At $364, KEYS carries a market cap of ~$62.2B.

- **Trailing GAAP P/E** is ~58-73x — not meaningful at the bottom of an earnings recovery, and I do not penalize on this basis.
- **Stated forward P/E** on current-year consensus of $10.19 is ~35.7x; on next year's $11.86 is ~30.7x. Forward PEG on ~27% growth is ~1.1-1.3.
- **Normalized forward P/E** is the more honest figure. Stripping the ~$57M IEEPA operating-income benefit and applying a normalized ~18% tax rate (versus the negative H1 rate), run-rate forward EPS is closer to ~$9.25-9.75. At $364, that is ~37-39x.

Peer median P/E is 27.0x (ROP 20.8x, TDY 31.6x, TRMB 26.1x, ZBRA 27.0x). KEYS at ~37-39x normalized forward sits at a ~40% premium to that median. The premium is partly justified by faster growth and a stronger AI-leverage story than most peers, but it is not a margin of safety. The bull DCF supports a $426 high target; an extreme bear DCF lands at $91. That dispersion shows the value is almost entirely a function of one assumption — the durability of high-20s% growth and ~33% segment margins.

Per the framework, the estimate-revision-as-buy-signal is strongest when the stock is cheap relative to the rising estimates (forward P/E <30x or PEG <1.5). KEYS is right at the boundary on stated numbers and above it on normalized numbers. Analyst targets here are not clean independent confirmation: at a ~31x stated/near-40x normalized multiple, both the targets and the price embed the same growth extrapolation, so proximity to the $383 mean / $390 median target is not a floor I would lean on.

The valuation score of 4.0 reflects a premium multiple on partly one-time-flattered earnings with a flagged cyclical-peak risk: not a value trap, but no asymmetric upside at $364.

## Competitive Landscape

Keysight's moat is genuine and stable-to-improving: a first-to-market R&D strategy (19% of revenue reinvested), a deep installed base, a broad multi-physics portfolio spanning the full design-to-manufacturing workflow, and high-margin software/services attach. It is gaining share in AI-network test, where its breadth across electrical, optical, and protocol layers is hard to replicate. The Spirent acquisition added wireless network test/assurance and positioning; the Synopsys OSG deal added optical/photonic design software; both are AI-network-adjacent.

The trajectory is positive, but two qualifiers keep the score at 7.0 rather than higher. First, a meaningful portion of the current revenue surge is cyclical AI/data-center capex rather than durable share gain, and the FY2024 downcycle showed how quickly the same demand can reverse. Second, integrating three deals at once (Spirent, OSG, PowerArtist) carries execution and amortization drag (acquisition-intangible amortization roughly doubled to ~$73M/quarter), and CSG already carries $709M of accumulated goodwill impairment from prior cycles.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI-capex cyclicality at a premium multiple.** The dominant risk. Revenue is order-driven and historically cyclical. Management explicitly flagged that recent growth "may not be sustainable" and added an AI-perception risk factor. A hyperscaler capex deceleration would hit CSG hard, and at ~37-39x normalized forward earnings the de-rating would be severe.
2. **Quality-of-earnings normalization.** As the one-time tariff and tax benefits roll off, the H2 comparisons get harder and the effective tax rate normalizes higher; reported momentum will optically decelerate even if underlying demand holds.
3. **Tax fragility.** The Malaysia tax incentive expired October 31, 2025 (renewal pending, not guaranteed); the Singapore incentive expires July 2029; and a $107M GILTI refund benefit is being litigated against the U.S. Adverse outcomes raise the effective tax rate that supports EPS.
4. **China and export controls.** Heavy China exposure in both supply chain and customer base; export restrictions can force loss of large orders with little notice. The IEEPA refund itself may be delayed, reduced, or denied.
5. **Acquisition integration / goodwill.** Goodwill rose to $3.42B and intangibles to $1.3B after the October 2025 deals; integration missteps could trigger impairment.
6. **Centripetal Networks patent litigation** (since 2022) is now trending strongly in Keysight's favor — most patents invalidated, ITC no-violation affirmed on appeal April 2026, EU UPC non-infringement December 2025 — so this is now a low-probability risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sanguine, which is itself a mild caution for a premium-multiple name. Near-term ATM IV is 42.1% against 38.0% one-year historical vol (modestly rich), but IV percentile sits at the 1st percentile within the current chain and the term structure is in contango (42.1% near vs. 46.7% long), a complacent configuration. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.4%) and put/call open interest is light at 0.51. IV fell 9.4 points over the past month even as the price rose 4.7%, indicating resolved near-term uncertainty after the Q2 beat. The market is not pricing meaningful downside risk, which fits a HOLD: there is no cheap-vol entry signal and no put-skew warning, just a quiet setup at a full price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
AI data-center networking test demand is structural, not a single-cycle spike. Orders +56% with revenue +31% means backlog is building. Estimate revisions will continue to pull consensus higher, and KEYS — in the only sector (Information Technology) that has reliably delivered selection alpha — re-rates further as durable high-20s% growth gets capitalized. Defense provides counter-cyclical ballast; the Spirent and OSG deals deepen the moat in AI-adjacent assurance and optical design. The franchise generates ~24% FCF margins with a clean balance sheet and a $1.2B remaining buyback. Bull target $426 (~17% upside), consistent with sell-side high and ~36x on next-year EPS.

### Bear Case
Normalized run-rate earnings put the stock at ~37-39x forward, a ~40% premium to peers, on growth the company itself warns may not be sustainable. A hyperscaler capex pause — the cycle's central risk — reverses CSG orders the way FY2024 reversed the prior cycle, and a premium multiple on cyclical-peak earnings compresses violently. Tax incentives (Malaysia expired, GILTI litigated) lift the effective rate. The extreme-bear DCF of $91 illustrates the downside if the growth assumption breaks; a more realistic bear at ~22x normalized forward EPS is ~$215, roughly 41% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is positive and supported by concrete catalysts, but the entry valuation at $364 does not offer asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3/Q4 FY2026 prints showing organic order growth holding above ~30% and operating margin sustained above ~22% **after** the IEEPA tariff benefit rolls off — i.e., evidence the run-rate, not the one-time items, supports the multiple.
- A pullback toward the low-$300s (roughly 30-32x normalized forward EPS), which would restore a reasonable margin of safety against high-20s% growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AI-capex deceleration (a Broadcom-type warning from the data-center networking ecosystem) cutting CSG order growth below ~10% YoY.
- Loss of the Malaysia tax-incentive renewal or an adverse GILTI ruling that lifts the effective tax rate above ~22% and compresses EPS.
- A guidance cut or a quarter where margins fall materially short ex-tariff-benefit, confirming the cyclical-peak thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings report (late August 2026), which will be the first clean read on whether order momentum and margins hold as the one-time benefits annualize away.

## Conclusion

Keysight is a high-quality franchise executing a genuine, broad-based recovery, and it sits squarely in the AI-infrastructure picks-and-shovels theme with confirming evidence across orders, revenue, estimate revisions, and four consecutive beats. The business is well capitalized, cash-generative, and competitively advantaged. None of that is in dispute.

What restrains the rating is price and earnings quality, not business quality. Normalize the one-time benefits and the stock trades at roughly 37-39x run-rate forward earnings, a ~40% premium to peers, capitalizing high-20s% growth that management has explicitly cautioned may not be sustainable. After a 113%+ run in twelve months, that is a fair-to-full price with limited margin of safety, and the dispersion between the $426 bull and ~$215 realistic-bear outcomes is wide enough that initiating here is a coin flip on a single assumption. Hold existing positions, monitor the Q3 order/margin data for confirmation that the run-rate supports the multiple, and accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$300s.
