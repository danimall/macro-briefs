**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CRISPR Therapeutics is a clinical-stage/early-commercial gene-editing company whose value rests on two distinct pillars. The first is CASGEVY (exa-cel), the world's only approved CRISPR gene-editing medicine, which functionally cures sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. Vertex Pharmaceuticals owns, manufactures, and commercializes CASGEVY; CRISPR participates as a 40% economic partner in the program's profit or loss. The second pillar is a wholly-owned pipeline of in vivo liver-editing (CTX310), next-generation allogeneic CAR-T (zugocabtagene geleucel), regenerative medicine, and an siRNA anticoagulant (CTX611, now in Phase 2). The company's independent equity value hinges primarily on the second pillar, since the economics of the first are controlled by a partner and offset by a deferred-cost overhang.

The financial picture is unusual for a clinical-stage biotech. CRISPR holds $2.44B in cash and marketable securities against roughly $400-450M of annual operating cash burn, funded partly by a $600M convertible note issued in March 2026 at a 1.125% coupon and a conversion price ~63% above the then-current price. Solvency is not the risk here; management guides to "at least 24 months" of runway, which is conservative given the raise. The risk profile is the classic gene-editing binary: everything beyond CASGEVY is clinical-stage, revenue is immaterial and lumpy (Q1 2026 total revenue was $1.5M), and net losses run near $500-580M annually. The July 2026 FDA approval expanding CASGEVY to ages 2+ broadened the addressable pediatric population and drove a >8% one-day move, but the program's contribution to CRISPR's cash flows is delayed by a $221.8M deferred cost claw-back owed to Vertex.

At ~$46.54, the stock sits down ~40.7% from its 52-week high of $78.48 and near the low end of its range. We rate CRSP a HOLD. The balance sheet and category leadership are genuine, but the stock is a catalyst-driven binary where the next 12-18 months of CTX310 and zugo-cel data will determine whether the platform translates leadership into shareholder value, a translation the market has doubted for five years (5-year TSR ~34 vs. a peer group ~120). We lack sufficient conviction on the clinical readouts to justify either a BUY or an AVOID, and we classify this as an uncertain hold rather than a thesis-driven one.

## Company Overview

CRISPR Therapeutics is a Swiss-domiciled (Zug), Nasdaq-listed gene-editing biopharmaceutical company with a market capitalization of ~$4.6B. Its portfolio spans four franchises plus an siRNA collaboration:

1. **Hemoglobinopathies — CASGEVY (exa-cel):** A one-time ex vivo autologous edit of the BCL11A enhancer that raises fetal hemoglobin. Approved in the US, EU, UK, Canada, Switzerland, and parts of the Middle East. Owned and commercialized by Vertex; CRISPR is a 40% economic participant.
2. **In vivo liver editing:** Lead asset CTX310 (targets ANGPTL3, for dyslipidemias/cardiovascular) in Phase 1b using LNP delivery; earlier-stage CTX340 (hypertension), CTX321 (Lp(a)), and CTX460 (AATD, using proprietary SyNTase editing).
3. **CAR-T:** Zugocabtagene geleucel (zugo-cel, formerly CTX112), a next-generation allogeneic CAR-T for autoimmune diseases and B-cell lymphomas, manufactured in-house in Framingham, MA. Holds RMAT designation; Eli Lilly collaboration to combine with pirtobrutinib.
4. **Regenerative medicine:** CTX213 (deviceless beta-cell replacement for type 1 diabetes), preclinical.
5. **siRNA (Sirius collaboration, May 2025):** CTX611, a long-acting Factor XI inhibitor now in Phase 2, targeting semi-annual dosing anticoagulation.

The competitive position rests on CRISPR being the only company with an approved CRISPR medicine on the market, combined with the Vertex partnership. Direct comparators include Intellia, Beam Therapeutics, and Editas, along with established modalities in each therapeutic area.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is not product-driven and is lumpy by design.** CRISPR records essentially no direct product revenue; CASGEVY sales flow through Vertex and reach CRISPR as its 40% share of net profit/loss, booked in "collaboration expense, net" rather than as revenue. Historical net income has swung with Vertex milestone recognition: +$378M in FY2021, –$650M in FY2022, –$154M in FY2023, –$366M in FY2024, and –$582M in FY2025.

**Q1 2026 (most recent):**
- Total revenue: $1.5M ($1.0M collaboration + $0.5M grant)
- R&D: $68.6M; G&A: $17.2M
- Collaboration expense, net (CASGEVY): $45.9M, down from $57.5M in Q1 2025. Management attributes the ~20% decline to an increase in CRISPR's share of CASGEVY revenue, an early sign the launch is beginning to offset the 40% loss share.
- Net loss: $122.9M (–$1.28/share), improved from –$136.0M
- Operating cash burn: $108.9M for the quarter, annualizing ~$400-450M

The collaboration-expense line is the most useful early indicator of the CASGEVY ramp, since the sales never appear as revenue. The 20% YoY decline in net collaboration expense, driven by CRISPR's growing revenue share, is consistent with a slow but improving launch, and the pediatric label expansion should extend that trend.

**Balance sheet — strong liquidity, now with first-ever debt:**
- $2.44B cash and marketable securities as of 3/31/26, up from $1.68B at YE2025.
- The increase reflects a $600M convertible senior notes offering (March 2026): 1.125% coupon (grossed up to 1.7308% for Swiss withholding), due 2031, conversion price ~$76.56, net proceeds ~$585M. This is the company's first debt; total liabilities rose from $343M to $911M.
- Accumulated deficit: $2.07B; shareholders' equity: $1.81B.
- Runway guidance: "at least 24 months," likely conservative given the raise.

Share count rose from ~86M (Q1 2025) to ~96M (Q1 2026), reflecting ATM issuance ($592M raised under the 2021 ATM at an average of $74.77; $557M still available under the 2025 ATM) and 1.84M shares issued to Sirius. The financial risk is dilution and burn over a long clinical horizon, plus dependence on CASGEVY economics and pipeline success.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term reported financials show no growth — revenue is immaterial and net losses are structural. The growth thesis is therefore forward-looking and event-driven, resting on three components.

The first component is the CASGEVY ramp. The July 2026 FDA approval expanding the label to ages 2+ (from 12+) for both SCD and TDT materially broadens the pediatric addressable population. Combined with the declining net collaboration expense, this points to a slowly improving contribution from the program. The offset is the deferred cost claw-back to Vertex, which will absorb the first dollars of program profitability, delaying CRISPR's cash benefit.

The second component is the in vivo pipeline, which the company and the field view as the key independent value driver. CTX310 (ANGPTL3) Phase 1b data is the single most important near-term de-risking event; whether one-time in vivo LNP editing can rival chronic therapies (PCSK9 inhibitors, siRNA like Leqvio, emerging Lp(a) drugs) is the central platform question. CTX321 (Lp(a)) and CTX340 (hypertension) follow.

The third component is allogeneic CAR-T in autoimmune disease (zugo-cel), a crowded and fast-moving arena where an off-the-shelf approach could differentiate on access and cost if durability and safety hold, backed by the Lilly collaboration.

On estimate revisions, consensus is broadly flat and modestly improving on a 90-day basis (current-year EPS revised from –$4.94 to –$4.88, next-year from –$4.06 to –$3.89), but has drifted slightly negative over the trailing 30 days. There is no positive signal of the magnitude that would flag a compelling growth setup for a pre-profit name; the loss estimates are simply narrowing at the margin. Management framed the company as entering a "second phase" beyond the CASGEVY launch, with multiple pipeline readouts expected over the next 12-18 months. The growth case is real in optionality terms but contingent on clinical data that has not yet arrived.

## Valuation Assessment

CRISPR does not lend itself to conventional earnings multiples — trailing and forward P/E are both negative (–7.6x on –$6.47 trailing EPS), and the company will not generate positive EPS on any near-term horizon. Valuation must be assessed on an asset-and-cash basis and on option value.

At ~$46.54 and a ~$4.6B market cap, the enterprise value is roughly $2.8B net of cash and adding back the convert as debt. The market is therefore paying roughly $2.8B for the combined value of CRISPR's 40% CASGEVY economic stake plus its wholly-owned pipeline. Whether that is cheap or expensive depends almost entirely on one's view of CTX310 and zugo-cel, neither of which has delivered the pivotal data that would resolve the question.

Analyst targets show extreme dispersion: a low of $44 (near the current price), a median of $80, a mean of $86, and a high of $291. This spread, with the high more than 6x the low, is characteristic of a binary biotech where the outcome is a distribution of a near-zero pipeline scenario against a platform-validation scenario, not a point estimate. The consensus is that the stock is worth substantially more if the pipeline works and roughly the current price if it does not.

We assign a neutral valuation score. The stock is not obviously mispriced in either direction. Cheapness on a price-to-cash or price-to-52-week-high basis is not a buy signal here — clinical-stage biotech trades at a discount for good reason, and a compressed price near the low end of the range reflects genuine uncertainty about pipeline outcomes rather than a market error we can identify. The balance sheet and category leadership prevent the stock from being clearly overvalued. Without a specific, dated catalyst we can underwrite (CTX310 data timing is not firmly disclosed), we cannot argue the market is systematically wrong. The valuation is a wide, unresolved distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

CRISPR sits at the front of the gene-editing field as the only company with an approved CRISPR medicine, which confers first-mover clinical and regulatory experience and a validated partnership with Vertex. That advantage is real but partly encumbered: CASGEVY's economics and commercialization are controlled by Vertex, so CRISPR's independent competitive value hinges on the wholly-owned in vivo and CAR-T programs.

In vivo liver editing is the key battleground. CRISPR competes directly with Intellia (ATTR, HAE) and Verve, and against chronic therapies in each indication. The proprietary SyNTase editing technology (used in CTX460) and the LNP delivery platform are differentiators, but the field remains pre-commercial for in vivo editing, so competitive position is a matter of relative clinical progress rather than market share.

Allogeneic CAR-T for autoimmune disease is a crowded and fast-moving arena. An off-the-shelf approach could be differentiated on access and cost against autologous CAR-T, but only if durability and safety hold up in the data.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: CRISPR retains category leadership, the CASGEVY loss share is shrinking, and the pediatric label expansion widens the franchise. The counterweight is a five-year record of underperformance versus biotech peers (TSR ~34 vs. ~120), which suggests the market has been skeptical that platform leadership converts to shareholder value. We score competitive position modestly above neutral, reflecting genuine leadership tempered by the dependence on unproven pipeline execution.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and material, and several are not fully priced given the binary nature of the setup.

1. **CASGEVY commercial ramp and lack of control.** The single approved asset is commercialized by Vertex, with CRISPR taking 40% of profit or loss. The therapy is a complex, ~$2M+, one-time treatment requiring myeloablative conditioning and apheresis at authorized centers — a slow, capacity-constrained launch. CRISPR bears 40% of ongoing losses until the program turns net-profitable.
2. **Deferred cost claw-back.** CRISPR deferred $221.8M of its CASGEVY cost share (2022-2024), payable to Vertex only as an offset against future CASGEVY profitability, delaying CRISPR's cash benefit from the first program profits.
3. **New leverage and dilution risk.** The Q1 2026 10-Q added two new risk factors tied to the $600M notes: debt-service/covenant risk and dilution upon conversion (up to ~11.4M shares). This is the most material recent change to the risk profile.
4. **Contingent IP liability.** A third-party licensor "formally engaged" in 2025; CRISPR accrued $13M (total liability $14.5M) in Q4 2025 and warns the ultimate resolution "could result in a material charge in excess of the amount accrued." The ToolGen suit alleging CASGEVY infringement was dismissed without prejudice in April 2026, a real de-risking, though refiling is possible.
5. **Pipeline binary risk.** Everything beyond CASGEVY is clinical-stage, carrying off-target editing, immunogenicity, and conditioning-toxicity risks. CTX310 Phase 1b data is the single most important near-term de-risking event, with an asymmetric outcome distribution.
6. **Structural unprofitability and dilution optionality.** Recurring losses, plus Swiss capital-band authorization to issue up to ~8M more shares through June 2028 and active ATM facilities ($557M available), give management standing dilution capacity.

The concentration of value in a handful of upcoming binary readouts, combined with the deferred-cost overhang and the new convert-related risks, argues for a below-neutral risk score. These are specific, quantified risks capable of moving the stock in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 72.4% against 59.1% historical volatility (rich by ~13.2pp), with a put/call skew of 20.8% (elevated put demand) and a term structure in backwardation (near-term 72.4% > long-term 67.0%), signaling the market expects imminent volatility. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±8.2%. IV has collapsed from 119.1% in early June to 72.4% now as the stock fell 18.4% over the same window; the June catalyst risk has partially resolved, but the market still prices a wide distribution. Short interest is high at 23.0% of float with 11.3 days to cover; combined with the elevated put skew, this creates two-sided tension of meaningful downside positioning and squeeze potential on a positive pipeline surprise. The options configuration is consistent with the fundamental read of a binary, event-driven name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$80-291, consensus median to high target):** CTX310 Phase 1b data validates one-time in vivo LNP editing as competitive with chronic cardiovascular therapies, re-rating the wholly-owned platform. Zugo-cel differentiates in autoimmune CAR-T on access and durability. CASGEVY's pediatric expansion accelerates the ramp, the loss share continues to shrink, and the deferred cost is worked off. The balance sheet funds all of this without near-term dilution at distressed prices. Category leadership converts to shareholder value, and the wide analyst dispersion resolves toward the high end.

**Bear Case (~$44 or below, at or below the analyst low):** CTX310 or zugo-cel data disappoints on efficacy or safety, collapsing the option value of the wholly-owned pipeline. The market reverts to valuing CRISPR primarily on its encumbered 40% CASGEVY stake, net of the deferred cost, plus cash. Continued burn erodes the cash cushion over the multi-year clinical horizon, forcing dilutive ATM issuance at low prices. The five-year pattern of peer underperformance persists, and the 23% short base is validated. Downside from the current ~$47 is on the order of 6% to the analyst-low target.

**Base Case (~$80, consensus median):** CASGEVY continues its slow, improving ramp with the pediatric tailwind, while pipeline readouts produce mixed but not thesis-breaking data over the next 12-18 months, keeping the platform option alive. The balance sheet carries the company comfortably. The stock re-rates modestly toward the consensus median as data de-risks incrementally, but not to the high-target scenario, which requires a clean in vivo win.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain).

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Positive, clinically meaningful CTX310 Phase 1b data demonstrating durable ANGPTL3 knockdown with a clean safety profile; (2) evidence that CASGEVY net collaboration expense turns to a net contribution (loss share fully absorbed and program net-profitable to CRISPR after the deferred cost); (3) constructive zugo-cel autoimmune data showing durable responses with a manageable safety profile.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) A CTX310 or zugo-cel readout showing efficacy failure or a serious safety signal (off-target editing, immunogenicity, conditioning toxicity); (2) accelerating cash burn combined with dilutive ATM issuance well below prior raise prices, signaling weakening capital discipline; (3) an adverse resolution of the open third-party IP dispute materially in excess of the $14.5M accrual, or a refiled ToolGen action gaining traction.
- **Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the CTX310 Phase 1b data disclosure and subsequent quarterly earnings; management has guided to multiple pipeline readouts over the next 12-18 months, making this a monitor-quarterly name with data readouts as the primary triggers.

## Conclusion

CRISPR Therapeutics is a well-capitalized category leader in gene editing whose independent equity value is a bet on unproven clinical assets. The balance sheet, funded cheaply via the March 2026 convert, removes solvency from the equation and buys time through the key readouts. The pediatric CASGEVY expansion and the shrinking loss share are incremental positives. The reported financials show structural losses, the CASGEVY economics are encumbered by a partner's control and the deferred cost, and the platform's value hinges on CTX310 and zugo-cel data that has not yet arrived. Five years of peer underperformance underscore that the market has not paid for leadership absent execution.

At ~$46.54, near the low end of a 52-week range and down ~40.7% from the high, the stock is neither clearly cheap nor clearly expensive; it is a wide, unresolved distribution reflected in analyst targets that span $44 to $291. The options market is pricing elevated near-term volatility (IV 72.4% vs. 59.1% HV, backwardation, 20.8% put skew), and the 23% short interest with 11.3 days to cover creates two-sided event risk. We rate CRSP a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is not a conviction call in either direction; the next 12-18 months of clinical data will determine the outcome, and we lack the evidence to front-run those readouts. Investors already holding should size the position as a binary event bet and monitor the CTX310 data as the primary catalyst; new capital has no urgency to enter ahead of the readouts.
