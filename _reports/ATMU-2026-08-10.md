## Executive Summary

Atmus is the former Cummins Filtration business, fully separated from its parent in March 2024, selling Fleetguard-branded fuel, lube, air and hydraulic filters into commercial trucking, off-highway equipment and, since January 2026, commercial HVAC and data centers via the $455M Koch Filter acquisition. Approximately 86% of FY2025 net sales were aftermarket replacement product, which ties revenue to the installed fleet and equipment utilization rather than to new build rates. That mix is the reason the business held revenue flat through a freight recession and the reason its earnings stream deserves a higher multiple than a pure OEM supplier.

The gap between reported and underlying growth is the central analytical fact. H1 2026 net sales rose 15.6% to $1,005.4M, but the company's own disclosed drivers decompose the $135.4M increase as Koch $80.8M, currency $27.2M, pricing $21.2M and volume $6.2M. Organic volume growth for the half was 0.7%. Management describes aftermarket demand as "soft" and first-fit as "depressed," with recovery expected in the latter half of 2026. Gross margin has expanded from 26.6% (FY2023) to 28.9% (H1 2026), but adjusted EBITDA margin was flat year over year in H1 at 20.3% and declined 30bp in Q2 to 20.7%, because Koch is dilutive at the margin line (18.9% Industrial Solutions versus 20.8% Power Solutions) and because the FY2025 gross-margin bridge was substantially pricing offsetting $36.2M of duties and logistics cost rather than productivity.

Two things argue for owning it and one argues for waiting. The catalyst is genuine and externally verifiable: North American Class 8 orders rose 231% year over year in June 2026 and roughly 68% in July, with 2026 build slots largely sold out and used-truck inventories declining, a stronger signal than the guidance implies, since FY2026 guidance embeds H2 revenue of $997M against H1 of $1,005M and H2 adjusted EPS of $1.43 against H1 $1.50. The balance sheet was extended: weighted-average debt tenor went from 1.9 years to 4.6 years, no principal is due until $18.8M in 2027, the $500M revolver is undrawn, and net leverage is 1.94x against ~$380M TTM adjusted EBITDA. What argues for waiting is price. At $50.05 the shares trade at 17.1x the midpoint of FY2026 guided adjusted EPS ($2.925) and 15.4x FY2027 consensus ($3.26), against adjusted EPS growth of 7.1% this year and ~10% next (a PEG near 1.6), with a ~3.6% trailing free-cash-flow yield against a 4.63% 10-year Treasury. Roughly 2.8% of revenue is tariff-recovery pricing that the company is now evaluating whether it must return to customers. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Atmus designs and manufactures filtration products and coolants/chemicals, with more than 65 years of operating history under the Fleetguard brand. It was incorporated in Delaware in April 2022, IPO'd in May 2023, and Cummins disposed of its entire remaining stake in March 2024.

Two reportable segments since Q1 2026:

- **Power Solutions** — the legacy Fleetguard business. Q2 2026 external sales $485.5M (92% of total). FY2025 mix: on-highway 58%, off-highway 42%. Q2 category detail: fuel $223.7M (from $195.2M), lube $95.7M (from $93.0M), air $80.3M (from $77.5M), other $85.8M (from $87.8M). Only fuel filtration grew materially.
- **Industrial Solutions** — the acquired Koch Filter business serving commercial/industrial HVAC, data centers, power generation and healthcare. Q2 2026 external sales $42.4M (8% of total); FY2026 guidance $155-165M.

The footprint comprises 10 wholly owned plants, 11 distribution centers and 5 technical centers, with joint ventures operating a further 10 manufacturing and 2 technical facilities. Roughly 46% of FY2025 sales were outside the US and Canada and 37% were non-USD denominated. The largest single plant is in San Luis Potosí, Mexico, supplying the US and global markets, which is relevant to the tariff discussion below. About 53% of the ~4,950 employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements. Materials (steel, filter media, petrochemical-derived plastics, rubber, adhesives) were about 60% of cost of sales in FY2025, and the company runs no commodity hedges, relying on contractual price-adjustment mechanisms with first-fit customers.

The stated moat rests on proprietary media (StrataPore, NanoNet, the current NanoNet N3 generation), ~1,200 active and pending patents, ~355 technical staff, and OEM part-number specification that converts first-fit wins into multi-year aftermarket annuities. Named competitors are MANN+HUMMEL, Donaldson, Parker Hannifin and MAHLE, with the balance of the market fragmented.

One structural detail is easy to miss: the 10-K states end markets are generally stronger in the first half, with more customer holiday closures in the second half. H1 run-rates should not be annualized.

**Margin quality.** The FY2025 gross-profit bridge disclosed in MD&A was +$50.0M pricing, +$21.0M volume, +$7.7M lower manufacturing cost and +$0.9M lower one-time cost, against −$36.2M unfavorable logistics and duties, −$5.8M currency and −$1.4M warranty. Roughly three-quarters of the pricing benefit was consumed offsetting cost. That is competent cost recovery, not structural productivity, and it matters because the duties component is now partly subject to refund and possible pass-back.

**Cash conversion.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $202.7M was below net income of $207.4M, driven by a $63.2M working-capital outflow and a structural non-cash gap between equity-method income recognized ($33.8M) and dividends actually received from investees ($21.0M), roughly 62% cash conversion on joint-venture earnings. H1 2026 OCF improved to $115.9M from $73.1M, but the improvement is mostly working-capital timing: receivables consumed $28.8M versus $67.8M a year earlier (a $39.0M favorable swing), offset by accounts payable turning from a $38.0M source to a $4.4M use (a $42.4M unfavorable swing), while net income rose only $7.7M. FY2024 is the cautionary precedent: OCF fell to $105.4M that year on a $103.3M working-capital build. H1 2026 adjusted FCF of $100.1M should not be doubled. FY2025 adjusted FCF of $158.3M plus a Koch contribution is a more defensible FY2026 anchor, implying a free-cash-flow yield near 4% on the current market capitalization.

**Balance sheet, restructured in January 2026.** Concurrent with the Koch close, Atmus entered a $1.0B term loan (fully drawn) plus a $500M revolver, both maturing January 7, 2031, priced at SOFR plus 1.125–1.75% depending on net leverage. Long-term debt rose from $540.0M to $992.0M; cash is $259.0M. Derived net debt is $739.3M and TTM adjusted EBITDA approximately $380.4M (FY2025 $353.5M + H1 2026 $203.6M − H1 2025 ~$176.7M), giving net leverage of 1.94x and gross leverage 2.63x. H1 interest coverage on operating income is 6.2x ($173.5M / $27.8M). The amortization ladder is back-loaded: $0 in 2026, $18.8M in 2027, $43.7M in 2028, $50.0M each in 2029-30, $837.5M thereafter. Rate sensitivity is disclosed at ~$1.3M of annual interest per 0.125% SOFR move.

Two qualifications. Substantially all assets are pledged, and covenant thresholds are not disclosed in the 10-Q (only that the company was in compliance at June 30). Tangible book value is now negative: equity $454.8M less goodwill $302.6M less intangibles $209.3M = −$57.1M. Separately, the frequently quoted 54.8% ROE and 2.57 debt/equity are artifacts of the separation: Atmus borrowed $650M at IPO and paid it to Cummins as partial consideration, permanently suppressing the equity base. ROA of 15.4% and asset turnover of 1.31x are the usable metrics and are genuinely good.

**Earnings quality of the Q2 beat.** Non-GAAP EPS of $0.82 against a non-GAAP consensus near $0.78 is a ~5% beat on matching bases. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.78 rose only 8.3% year over year while adjusted EBITDA rose 14.7%, because interest expense jumped to $13.7M from $8.5M, $2.9M of acquired-intangible amortization appeared, "other income" swung from +$3.5M to −$0.7M, and the effective tax rate rose to 22.8% from 21.9%. The four-quarter beat record (15.1%, 12.8%, 1.6%, 3.2%) is intact but the magnitude has compressed sharply; visibility is normalizing rather than expanding.

## Growth Analysis

The reported top line and the underlying franchise are moving at very different speeds.

**Q2 2026 drivers:** total +$74.4M = Koch $42.4M, price $12.9M, currency $10.8M, volume $8.3M. Organic growth ex-Koch was $32.0M on a $453.5M base (+7.1%), of which volume was 1.8pp.
**H1 2026 drivers:** total +$135.4M = Koch $80.8M, currency $27.2M, price $21.2M, volume $6.2M. Organic volume growth was 0.7%.

Geographic detail confirms the read: Q2 US sales rose to $288.4M from $220.0M (+31%), but essentially all Koch revenue is US-based, while other international grew 2.6% to $239.5M from $233.5M.

Flat volume in a documented freight downturn is defensible evidence of aftermarket resilience: Atmus held units while customer build rates were depressed. But it means the growth investors are paying for has to come from somewhere other than the last twelve months.

**The cyclical catalyst is real and dated.** North American Class 8 orders, the leading indicator for the 58% on-highway exposure, rose to 31,400 units in June 2026 (+231% YoY per ACT Research) and roughly 22,100 units in July (+68% per ACT, +75% per FTR). Cited drivers are improving freight rates and carrier profitability, fleet replacement, tightening capacity and a pre-buy ahead of EPA 2027 emissions rules; 2026 build slots are largely sold out, which is why July fell sequentially despite strong demand. Used commercial truck and trailer inventories declined through June. This corroborates management's expectation of first-fit recovery in H2 2026, and because Atmus is 86% aftermarket, a larger installed fleet is durably positive for filter consumption independent of when the trucks were bought.

Two hazards attach. A pre-buy pulls demand forward and creates air-pocket risk in 2028, and several sources note uncertainty over whether the EPA rules themselves will be revised, which would alter the pre-buy dynamic. Both effects are muted for an aftermarket-weighted supplier relative to a pure OEM vendor.

**Guidance embeds deceleration, not acceleration.** The August 7 raise moved revenue to $1,975-2,030M (from $1,945-2,015M), with adjusted EBITDA margin 19.75-20.25% and adjusted EPS $2.85-3.00. Working the midpoints: revenue $2,002.5M less H1 $1,005.4M implies H2 of $997.1M; adjusted EBITDA $400.5M less H1 $203.6M implies H2 of $196.9M, a 19.75% H2 margin versus 20.3% in H1; adjusted EPS $2.925 less H1 $1.50 implies H2 of $1.425. That is consistent with the disclosed first-half seasonality, but the raise was primarily a narrowing of the low end.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest**: current year $2.96 (+0.9% over 90 days), next year $3.26 (+1.5%), five contributing analysts. This is a business tracking slightly ahead of a plan the market already knows rather than an acceleration in estimate velocity.

**Koch's contribution is the second growth leg and is unproven.** Purchase price was $455.0M; the preliminary allocation is $215.0M intangibles (trade names $45M/15yr, customer relationships $170M/20yr, $2.9M quarterly amortization) and $218.0M goodwill. Annualizing H1 segment adjusted EBITDA of $16.3M gives ~$32.6M, implying ~14.0x EBITDA and ~2.8x revenue paid, above Atmus's own ~12.7x EV/EBITDA. Incremental debt of roughly $430M at the ~5.5% blended rate implied by Q2 interest costs about $23.6M annually; segment EBITDA less tangible D&A (~$3.2M) less intangible amortization ($11.5M) is ~$17.9M of EBIT, so the deal is roughly $0.05 dilutive to GAAP EPS and roughly $0.055 accretive on the adjusted definition, which adds back acquired-intangible amortization. The total-company arithmetic corroborates this: FY2026 guidance implies adjusted EBITDA growth of ~13.3% but adjusted EPS growth of only ~7.1%, the wedge being ~$21M of incremental interest. Economic value therefore depends on synergies and on Industrial Solutions outgrowing the legacy business, neither of which is quantified in filings, and Atmus deemed the acquisition immaterial to results of operations, so no pro forma financials and no audited standalone Koch history exist.

## Competitive Landscape

Power Solutions has three durable advantages: the Fleetguard brand and OEM part-number specification, which converts first-fit wins into decade-long aftermarket annuities; proprietary media plus ~1,200 patents; and distribution across 25+ countries with 11 distribution centers. The counterweight is that its largest customer is its former parent, which competitively bids the filtration business, carries no committed volume obligations, and retains a contractual right to procure supplemental supply of top-volume aftermarket products from alternative suppliers if Atmus fails on delivery performance. Cummins' share of net sales rose from 17.4% (FY2023) to 17.6% (FY2024) to 18.8% (FY2025). Top three (Cummins, PACCAR, Traton) reached 46.6% of FY2025 sales; the ten named OEMs about 70%. Koch changes the revenue mix, with 8% of Q2 sales now from end markets with no Cummins exposure, but the disclosed concentration measure has moved the wrong way for three consecutive years.

In Industrial Solutions, Atmus is a sub-scale new entrant. Koch at ~$160M of annual revenue is small against Donaldson's industrial franchise and dedicated HVAC filtration players. The logic — apply Atmus media science, global footprint and aftermarket distribution to Koch's product line and customers — is coherent, and data centers and power generation are growing end markets consistent with broader grid and compute buildout. Two quarters of ownership provide little evidence. Implied Q1 2026 segment results ($38.4M sales, $8.3M segment adjusted EBITDA, 21.6%) versus Q2 reported ($42.4M, $8.0M, 18.9%) show a 270bp sequential margin decline on higher revenue. Candidate benign explanations include product/customer mix, purchase-accounting inventory step-through, integration cost landing inside the segment and seasonal absorption; two data points do not establish a trend, but the entire fourth-pillar thesis rests on this segment earning a premium margin, so it needs watching.

One widely circulated relative-value claim does not hold. A May 2026 note argued to buy ATMU and sell Donaldson on the premise that Donaldson's industrial margins collapsed 420bp and guidance was cut. Donaldson subsequently reported record Q3 FY2026 sales, record adjusted operating margin and record adjusted EPS ($1.06 on $995.1M, +~6% YoY), with Industrial Solutions weakness attributed specifically to Aerospace & Defense. Investors should not underwrite ATMU on competitor impairment. Constructively, the source of Donaldson's industrial softness is not a read-through to Koch's HVAC/data-center markets either.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff pricing reversal (the most novel item in the file).** This was the only risk factor materially revised in the Q2 10-Q. Atmus raised prices to recover tariff-driven cost — pricing added $50.0M to FY2025 sales (~3.0% of the FY2024 base) and $21.2M in H1 2026, while FY2025 gross margin absorbed $36.2M of duties and logistics. On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court held IEEPA tariffs exceeded statutory authority without addressing refunds; on March 4 the Court of International Trade ordered CBP to begin refunds. Atmus received an immaterial refund amount in Q2, is evaluating whether portions must be returned to customers under contractual terms, states that returns would negatively affect revenue, and expects net EBITDA impact to be substantially neutral. The optics could deteriorate on revenue and improve on margin percentage simultaneously. Not all of the $50M relates to IEEPA — Section 232 steel/aluminum duties were unaffected and USMCA-compliant Mexican production was largely exempt — so the magnitude is genuinely unknown.

**Customer concentration.** Detailed above. No committed volumes from an 18.8% customer that runs competitive bids and holds a dual-sourcing right on the most profitable SKUs.

**Goodwill and intangibles now exceed equity.** Goodwill went from $84.7M to $302.6M and intangibles from zero to $209.3M in one quarter; combined $511.9M exceeds total equity of $454.8M. The purchase price allocation is explicitly preliminary, with a $1.2M measurement-period goodwill reduction already recorded in Q2. An Industrial Solutions shortfall would produce a write-down large relative to the equity base.

**Powertrain transition.** The 10-K states that substantially all net sales are filtration for internal combustion engines and that battery-electric vehicles may not use as much filtration content, and flags that Cummins' 2030 Scope 3 goals could shift its product preference. For an 86%-aftermarket business the installed-base annuity decays slowly, making this a 2030s cash-flow issue and a present-day multiple cap.

**Non-controlled joint ventures.** Equity, royalty and interest income from investees was $33.8M in FY2025, 12.7% of pre-tax income, from entities Atmus does not control (Shanghai Fleetguard 50% economic; FFPL India 49.491% economic/50% voting; Filtrum 49.75%). Volatility is already visible: H1 2026 FFPL income fell to $7.8M from $10.5M on remeasurement of employee benefit obligations following Indian labor law changes, with Shanghai partially offsetting; total investee income fell to $15.2M from $16.7M. Cash conversion on JV earnings has run ~62%, so a slice of reported EBITDA is not consolidated cash.

**Execution and labor.** About 53% unionized (Cookeville, TN to February 2028; Mexico, Brazil, France annual). A multi-year ERP/legacy-modernization program is underway, a recurring source of control and working-capital surprises at recent carve-outs. The transition to Atmus's own distribution network contributed to higher H1 logistics and warehousing cost. FY2025 included $8.4M of long-lived asset impairment on idled equipment.

**Residual Cummins constraints.** The tax matters agreement restricts Atmus's ability to enter acquisition, merger, liquidation, sale and stock-redemption transactions with respect to its own stock, and certain contractual rights granted to Cummins may discourage takeover attempts. This limits both large-scale M&A structuring and Atmus as a target.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Executive compensation detail, LTIP performance metrics, say-on-pay results and related-party disclosures could not be verified from the proxy; the Q2 call primary transcript was unavailable, so integration progress (>95% of the Koch TSA exited, completion by end of Q3 2026), the $20-40M 2026 buyback range and stated interest in an industrial water/liquid filtration "anchor investment" rest on a single secondary summary. Credit agreement covenant thresholds are undisclosed. An August 2023 8-K carried Item 4.02 (non-reliance on previously issued financial statements) roughly ten weeks after the IPO; the substance was not retrieved and there is no evidence of recurrence, but it belongs in the record for a recently carved-out issuer.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.4% against 39.8% trailing one-year realized is fairly priced (1.6pp spread, 55th percentile within the chain). The term structure is in mild backwardation — 41.4% at 10 days versus 34.4% at 38 days and 38.4% at 157 days — which is unusual with earnings already reported on August 7 and points to residual near-dated event risk rather than a scheduled catalyst. Put/call volume ratio of 1.67 (5.00 on the nearest tenor) sits against an open-interest ratio of 0.85, so recent flow is more defensive than the standing book. Near-tenor skew is balanced to slightly put-heavy (+1.0% to +1.7%), while the January 2027 tenor prints −5.5% with a ±21.6% implied move, so longer-dated positioning is not pricing an outsized left tail. Short interest of 4.1% of float and 3.5 days to cover means no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic. The market is pricing normal volatility for a mid-cap industrial with an unresolved tariff-refund item, consistent with a HOLD, and neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $67 (+34%)

Class 8 orders at +231% and +68% year over year are the leading edge of a first-fit recovery that management has guided to but not yet embedded in numbers; H2 guidance implies revenue slightly below H1 and adjusted EPS of $1.43 versus $1.50, so any genuine volume inflection beats the plan. The installed fleet grows through 2027 regardless of pre-buy timing, feeding the 86% aftermarket annuity for years. Koch integration completes in Q3, transaction and integration cost ($8.5M in H1) rolls off, purchase-accounting drags anniversary, and Industrial Solutions returns toward the 21%+ margin it printed in Q1 while growing faster than the legacy base on data-center and power-generation demand. Tariff refunds prove immaterial and the pricing gains stick. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~$3.55 and the market pays 19x for a mid-single-digit-volume, high-recurring-revenue filtration franchise with net leverage under 1.5x after debt paydown. Sell-side targets at $66-67 mean are met.

### Bear Case — $41 (−18%)

The organic franchise is volume-flat and the two non-durable growth legs reverse: tariff-recovery pricing (~2.8% of revenue) is partly returned to customers, compressing reported revenue, and the currency tailwind (3.1pp in H1) fades or turns. Aftermarket softness persists through 2027 because carriers defer maintenance rather than accelerate it, and the EPA-2027 pre-buy pulls 2028 forward into 2027, setting up a 2028 air pocket. Koch's Q2 margin decline proves to be mix and competition rather than integration noise, the goodwill and intangible balance of $511.9M against $454.8M of equity produces an impairment, and Cummins re-bids share away or exercises its dual-sourcing right. Interest expense of ~$55M annualized keeps adjusted EPS growth in the mid-single digits while the multiple compresses to 14x on $2.90, the multiple this asset carried before the February re-rating.

### Base Case — $57 (+14%)

Volume recovers modestly in 2027 as the installed fleet expands, tariff pricing partially reverses with roughly neutral EBITDA effect, Koch settles into a high-teens to 20% margin, and adjusted EPS reaches consensus $3.26. The market applies 17.5x, a discount to industrial filtration peers appropriate for the concentration and ICE terminal-value profile, for approximately $57, plus a 0.44% dividend.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: approximately +11% total return over twelve months. That is a fair-to-marginally-attractive return for a beta-~1.1 industrial when the 10-year yields 4.63%, and it does not clear the margin-of-safety bar for a staged accumulation program.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a specific, positive twelve-month thesis — Class 8 order inflection feeding first-fit recovery and installed-fleet growth — but the current price captures most of the base-case value.

**Base case target: $57** (17.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.26), +13.9% from $50.05.
**Bull case target: $67** (19x ~$3.55), +33.9%.
**Bear case target: $41** (14x ~$2.90), −18.1%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (early November) and the Q4/FY2027 guidance event (February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 organic volume growth of 3% or better in Power Solutions, disclosed in the sales-driver table, confirming first-fit recovery rather than price and currency.
2. Industrial Solutions segment adjusted EBITDA margin back above 21% for two consecutive quarters, validating the fourth-pillar premium-margin premise.
3. Resolution of the IEEPA refund question with a quantified customer pass-back that is immaterial to revenue, removing the ~2.8%-of-revenue overhang.
4. Price toward $42-44 (roughly 13x FY2027 consensus) with the above fundamentals intact, which would restore a margin of safety sufficient for a full position.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A disclosed customer pass-back of tariff-related pricing large enough to reduce reported revenue by more than ~2% with an EBITDA effect that is not neutral.
2. Any disclosure that Cummins has re-bid or dual-sourced material aftermarket volume, or a further increase in top-three concentration above ~50% with declining Power Solutions volume.
3. Industrial Solutions segment margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters, or any goodwill impairment charge against the $302.6M balance.
4. Net leverage rising above 2.5x on a further debt-funded platform acquisition before the Koch thesis is quantified.
5. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $2.85, or H2 free cash flow that fails to convert at least 80% of adjusted net income.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 results release (expected late October/early November 2026), which will provide the first quarter in which management's H2 first-fit recovery claim is testable against the Class 8 order data, and the first clean quarter of Koch results after TSA exit.

## Conclusion

Atmus is a good business at a fair price with two unresolved questions. The business quality is real: 86% aftermarket consumable revenue, OEM part-number specification that converts first-fit wins into multi-year annuities, gross margin up 230bp over three years, net leverage of 1.94x with no maturity before 2027, and a modest 3% capital intensity. The end-market cycle appears to be turning in its favor, with Class 8 orders up 231% year over year in June and roughly 68% in July, 2026 build slots sold out and used-truck inventories declining, a data set that is stronger than what FY2026 guidance embeds.

The two questions are whether the growth is durable and whether the price compensates. Organic volume grew 0.7% in H1 2026; reported 15.6% growth was 9.3pp acquisition, 3.1pp currency and 2.4pp price, and roughly 2.8% of revenue is tariff-recovery pricing the company is evaluating whether it must return to customers. Koch was purchased at approximately 14x annualized segment EBITDA against Atmus's own 12.7x, is dilutive to GAAP EPS in year one, has no audited standalone history or disclosed synergy target, and saw its segment margin fall 270bp sequentially in its second quarter of ownership. At 17.1x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS, 15.4x FY2027 consensus and a ~3.6-4.0% free-cash-flow yield against a 4.63% 10-year Treasury, the shares offer a base-case return near 14% with an 18% downside case: positive expected value, insufficient asymmetry to initiate.

Existing holders should stay. New capital should wait either for the Q3 print to confirm that first-fit volume is inflecting, or for a price in the low-$40s. **HOLD.**
