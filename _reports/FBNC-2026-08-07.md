## Executive Summary

First Bancorp is the holding company for First Bank, the fourth-largest bank holding company headquartered in North Carolina, with $13.04 billion of assets, 113 branches (100 NC / 13 SC) and a differentiated funding base: 32% of $11.1 billion of deposits are noninterest-bearing checking, brokered deposits are $5.0 million, and total borrowings are $74.7 million. That funding profile is why the bank generated a 3.71% net interest margin in Q2 2026 (up 39bp year over year), a 49.3% efficiency ratio, 1.56% annualized ROA and 16.9% adjusted ROTCE. Those are upper-tier metrics for a $10–20 billion regional bank, and they are not an accounting illusion: 1H26 diluted EPS of $2.35 followed four consecutive earnings beats (+4.3%, +2.1%, +12.2%, +7.7%), and tangible book value per share compounded from $28.23 to $29.84 in six months (~11.7% annualized).

Three data artifacts need clearing before the analysis holds together. The screened trailing P/E of 19.96x rests on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $2.68, which was suppressed by $71.6 million of deliberate securities-restructuring losses; on trailing four quarters of actual EPS ($4.55) the multiple is 14.1x. The screened debt/equity of 6.66x and Altman Z-score of 0.27 are meaningless for a depository — the "debt" here is customer deposits, and the Altman model's manufacturing inputs have no bank analogue. A widely circulated "+30.5% revenue growth" figure for Q2 does not reconcile: net interest income plus fees was $127.3 million versus $111.0 million, or **+14.7%**. Treat the higher number as unverified.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an upgrade is the intersection of price and mix. At $64.34 the stock trades at **2.156x** tangible book ($29.84) and **12.7x** FY2027 consensus ($5.05) against a regional-bank peer median of 12.0x, while yielding 1.49% versus a 3.0% peer median. It reaches that multiple exactly as the driver of the earnings recovery turns: roughly 80% of the $14.6 million year-over-year increase in net interest income came from margin rather than balance-sheet growth, and the margin's cheapest input is repricing upward — cost of interest-bearing deposits rose from 1.89% in Q1 to 1.94% in Q2, money-market cost rose 8bp, and MMDA balances supplied about 73% of 1H26 deposit growth. More than 100% of net loan growth in 1H26 came from investor CRE, construction and multifamily (+$308 million) while C&I fell 3.1%, pushing our derived investor-CRE-to-total-capital ratio to roughly 288% against the 300% supervisory guidance line. Nonperforming assets rose from $35.8 million to $44.9 million year over year while ACL/loans fell from 1.47% to 1.39% and ACL/NPL coverage fell from 348% to 282%.

The score trajectory (6.8 → 6.4 → 5.5) reflects prior-cycle valuation and governance downgrades. This cycle nudges the composite to 5.6: the First Carolina Bancshares acquisition announced July 14 is a well-structured, currency-disciplined deal, and Q2 delivered another beat. But the stock advanced 6.7% since the June review while consensus EPS moved less than 1.5%, so the multiple absorbed the good news. Fundamentals improved; risk/reward did not.

## Company Overview

First Bank operates as a single reporting segment, a conventional community-commercial bank with several niches invisible in a standard data pull: **CarBucks** (used-car floor-plan lending), **Magnolia Financial** (receivables factoring, inventory and purchase-order finance across the Southeast), an **SBA 7(a) platform** ($146.9 million on balance sheet at 6/30/26, plus $280.9 million of sold participations serviced), mortgage banking and investment advisory. Loan concentration is geographic rather than industrial: New Hanover (Wilmington) 9.5%, Wake (Raleigh) 9.4%, Mecklenburg (Charlotte) 8.2%, Buncombe (Asheville) 5.0% at 12/31/25 — the Carolinas' in-migration corridors.

Leadership has largely transitioned. Richard Moore (65), CEO since 2012 and Chair since 2022, remains, but Adam Currie (48, ex-PNC Capital Markets, ex-RBC Bank) became CEO of the bank in February 2025 and joined the boards in October 2025; he is the operating leader and the executive who articulated the merger rationale. Michael Mayer retired as Company President February 28, 2026. Elizabeth Bostian has been CFO since 2021.

On July 14, 2026 FBNC agreed to acquire **First Carolina Bancshares** (Carolina Bank & Trust, Florence, SC): $831 million assets, $596 million loans, $714 million deposits, 1.60% LTM ROA, 14 branches across six Pee Dee counties. Consideration is 1,967,017 FBNC shares plus $40 million cash, ~$166 million at the July 13 close of $64.22. Expected close Q4 2026 or early Q1 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power.** Q2 2026 net income was $50.5 million, diluted EPS $1.22, versus $38.6 million / $0.93 a year earlier. Net interest income rose 15.1% to $111.3 million; noninterest income rose 12.2% to $16.0 million (helped by $1.2 million more of SBA gain-on-sale). Noninterest expense grew 6.5% against 15% revenue growth — genuine operating leverage, and the source of the 49.3% efficiency ratio ($62.8M ÷ $127.3M).

**Where the growth came from.** Decomposing the $14.6 million NII increase: average earning assets rose $330.4 million, which at the prior 3.32% NIM contributes roughly $2.7 million per quarter; the 39bp margin expansion on $12.0 billion of average earning assets contributes roughly $11.7 million. Volume explains about 19%, margin about 80%. This is the single most important fact for valuation, because margin is a price, not a franchise: loan yield rose only 14bp year over year while cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 20bp. The expansion was funded largely by liability repricing, and liability repricing has reversed direction.

**The securities restructuring, framed properly.** FY2025 saw $536.3 million of AFS securities yielding 1.66% sold for a $71.6 million pretax loss ($55.0 million after tax), following ~$36.8 million pretax in Q4 2024. Combined realized pretax losses of ~$108 million equal about 6.7% of current tangible common equity. Two framings are both wrong. The losses were already embedded in AOCI, so realization did not destroy equity — retained earnings falls by exactly what leaves AOCI. And because the sale price already discounted the below-market coupon, the transaction is closer to present-value neutral than the ~250–270bp reinvestment spread implies. The real economics are narrower: accelerated recognition of a $16.6 million tax deduction, duration/credit repositioning, and materially better *reported* NIM and ROTCE going forward. The real cost is the GAAP earnings and a nominal earnback of roughly five years (the 10-K states the reinvestment yield inconsistently at 4.13% and 4.35%; either produces 5.0–5.4 years), which is long against the three-years-or-less benchmark most bank managements target. The 2025 TCE ratio improvement from 8.22% to 9.61% came from ~$102 million of rate-driven mark recovery plus $111.0 million of net income, not from the restructuring.

**Capital and marks.** At 6/30/26: CET1 14.09%, Tier 1 14.81%, total risk-based 16.06%, leverage 11.60%, TCE ratio 9.83%. Against the 10.50% well-capitalized total-RBC threshold on approximately $9.6 billion of RWA, excess total capital is on the order of $0.5 billion (derived, not disclosed). Offsetting: with no securities sold in 1H26, AFS pretax unrealized losses widened from $194.1 million to $204.5 million and HTM unrecognized losses from $64.6 million to $66.4 million (a 99%-municipal book). The combined $270.9 million pretax mark equals 21.9% of TCE; tax-adjusting the unrecognized HTM piece cuts TBVPS about 4%, to roughly $28.57. Given contingent liquidity (FHLB ~$1.4B, discount window ~$764M, on-balance-sheet liquidity 15.7%) this is an earnings and book-value drag, not a solvency question, but the balance sheet has not healed.

**Capital allocation.** 1H26: $19.9 million of dividends ($0.48/share), $7.27 million of buybacks (129,000 shares, Q2 average $58.47), with $32.8 million remaining under a $40 million authorization reauthorized January 27, 2026. The company used only a fraction of the buyback in the high-$50s and is now issuing stock at $64.22 for an acquisition. The sequencing is defensible, since issuing at 2.15x tangible book to acquire at roughly 2.0x is TBV-neutral currency use, but it does mean the cheapest capital-return window was left mostly unused.

## Growth Analysis

Three tailwinds are ending or reversing simultaneously:

1. **Deposit repricing has bottomed.** Interest-bearing deposit cost rose 1.89% → 1.94% sequentially, money-market cost 2.35% → 2.43%, and MMDA — the most rate-sensitive category — supplied ~73% of 1H26 deposit growth ($245M of $337M). Management's own 2025 incentive scorecard paid **zero** on retail deposit growth (1.98% actual versus a 2.3% threshold) while loans grew 7.76%. Core retail gathering is underperforming loan growth, which is why funding mix is drifting toward priced money.
2. **Purchase-accounting accretion is nearly exhausted.** Accretion contributed $0.934 million to Q2-26 NII versus $1.161 million a year earlier; unaccreted loan discount fell from $11.8 million to $6.6 million. Small (about 3bp of NIM) but directionally negative until First Carolina resets it.
3. **The Helene reserve release is spent.** A $13.0 million 2024 provision was reversed by $11.1 million during 2025, including $5.5 million (~$0.10/share after tax) in 1H25. This cuts the other way for comparisons: core 1H25 EPS was closer to $1.71, making 1H26's $2.35 a ~37% core improvement rather than the reported +37%.

Rate direction is genuinely two-sided. FBNC is asset-sensitive: the 10-Q simulation shows NII +5.7% in +200bp, +3.9% in +100bp, −2.8% in −100bp, −5.3% in −200bp. Annualizing Q2 NII at $445 million, a 100bp cut costs roughly $6.3 million per quarter pretax, about $0.24 per share after tax over four quarters — meaningful but not thesis-breaking. The FY2025 10-K notes the FOMC target range was 3.50–3.75% at year-end and that the FOMC has indicated an expectation of one 25 basis point rate cut during 2026, so a −100bp scenario would be more severe than the disclosed macro path.

**Loan growth composition is the more serious issue.** Net loan growth of $267.6 million in 1H26 breaks down as investor CRE +$131M, construction and land development +$95M (≈25% annualized), multifamily +$82M (≈31% annualized), against C&I −$32M (−3.1%) and residential −$8M. More than 100% of net growth came from the three asset classes the company's own 10-K risk factors flag, noting CRE prices remain high relative to fundamentals with elevated market delinquency. Two mitigants deserve weight: construction balances grow mechanically as previously committed projects draw, so part of this reflects prior underwriting rather than fresh appetite; and Carolinas in-migration genuinely supports multifamily absorption. The shrinkage in C&I — the higher-yielding, relationship-driven, less collateral-dependent book — is the counter-signal.

Consensus embeds this deceleration honestly: current-year EPS of $4.72 implies 2H26 of $2.37 against 1H26's $2.35, essentially flat, with FY2027 at $5.05 (+7.0%). Revisions are modestly positive (+1.4% on both CY and NY over 30 days) but nowhere near the >30% threshold that would justify overriding valuation.

**The acquisition adds a genuine leg.** Our derived deal math: the shares represent a **+4.75% increase in share count**, or **4.54% ownership dilution** post-issuance. At $166.3 million for $831 million of assets (20.0% of assets) and implied LTM earnings near $13.3 million at 1.60% ROA, the multiple is roughly 12.5x earnings versus FBNC's own ~14.2x — mechanically accretive on multiple arbitrage alone. Price to tangible book is not disclosed; at 9–11% tangible equity/assets the range is 1.82x–2.22x. On assumed cost savings of 25% of target expense, net of foregone earnings on $40 million of cash, we estimate roughly **+4% EPS accretion** (range +2% at zero savings to +5% at 35%) against **−0.6% to −1.8% TBV dilution**, implying a one-to-two-year earnback. Every input is our assumption; the company disclosed none of them. Strategically it roughly doubles the South Carolina footprint from 13 branches to 27 — a platform step, not a bolt-on.

Peers (WAFD 12.0x, TRMK 12.0x, NBTB 13.5x, TBBK 11.0x, BBT 22.1x) sit at a 12.0x median with a 3.0% yield. FBNC's premium is defensible on returns: a Gordon-style framework with 16.9% ROTCE, a 10% cost of equity and 5% growth supports roughly 2.4x tangible book, above the 2.16x paid today. The question is whether 16.9% ROTCE is the run rate or the peak. Given that ~80% of the earnings improvement came from margin, that deposit costs have turned, and that reserve coverage of nonperformers is thinning, we treat 16.9% as a cyclical high and mid-cycle ROTCE as closer to 14–15%, which supports roughly 1.9–2.1x tangible book.

A useful calibration on the reserve question: holding ACL/loans flat at the 12/31/25 ratio of 1.4171% would require $127.4 million versus the actual $124.9 million — a $2.5 million pretax gap, about $0.048 per share cumulatively across two quarters. The earnings improvement is margin-driven, not reserve-release-driven. That is a point in the bulls' favor and we credit it.

**Base case $67.50.** Blending 13.0x FY2027 consensus of $5.05 ($65.65) with ~2.05x mid-2027 tangible book (standalone $29.84 compounding ~11% less ~1.2% deal dilution ≈ $32.70, giving $67.00) lands at roughly $67–68, or **+4.9%** from $64.34. Adding the 1.5% dividend gives a ~6.4% twelve-month total return — approximately fair, not compelling.

**Bull case $77 (+19.7%).** Deposit costs stabilize near 1.95%, NIM holds above 3.70%, the deal delivers 5% accretion and 2027 EPS reaches ~$5.30, and the multiple expands to 14.5x on demonstrated margin durability.

**Bear case $50 (−22.3%).** Rate cuts plus MMDA competition compress NIM toward 3.40%, credit normalizes (net charge-offs from 0.05% annualized toward 0.30%, roughly $25 million of incremental pretax provision), 2027 EPS falls to ~$4.20, and the multiple compresses to 12x — or equivalently 1.65x tangible book.

The analyst distribution ($65 low, $69.40 mean, $72 high, 1 strong buy / 2 buy / 2 hold) is unusually tight and brackets our base case. On a stock at 13.6x forward with single-digit EPS growth, analyst targets are not meaningfully stale; consensus is not lagging a step-change here. Probability-weighting 60% base / 20% bull / 20% bear gives roughly +3.6% price plus 1.5% dividend, or about 5%: below a fair equity required return, above the threshold that would warrant trimming. Valuation scores 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat, such as it is, sits on the liability side. Noninterest-bearing checking at 32% of deposits with $5.0 million of brokered funding and a blended deposit cost near 1.32% is a structural advantage most $13 billion banks cannot replicate, and it is the entire reason NIM expanded 82bp from FY2024's 2.89% to Q2 2026's 3.71%. Scale is adequate to matter locally — fourth-largest NC-headquartered holding company, a $223.5 million legal lending limit — while remaining small enough to underwrite relationships in secondary markets. Pro forma for First Carolina, the company claims top-10 deposit share in both Carolinas.

Trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Positives: footprint doubling in South Carolina; office exposure that is a competitive mitigant rather than a risk (non-owner-occupied office ~6.2% of loans, ~$557 million, average loan size $1.4 million, largest $33.0 million, top-ten office loans under 2% of the portfolio, non-metro skew — granular small-tenant credit, not CBD towers); noninterest income growing 12%. Negatives: C&I balances shrinking 3.1% in six months while the industry's better franchises are competing hardest for exactly that book; retail deposit growth of 1.98% missing management's own 2.3% threshold; funding mix drifting toward MMDA. The FY2025 10-K added a **stablecoin disintermediation** risk factor — that customers may convert deposits into stablecoins backed by bank deposits, accelerating outflows and raising volatility, with digital-asset regulations expected in Q3 2026. For a bank whose entire earnings improvement rests on a 32% noninterest-bearing base, that is the most strategically relevant new disclosure in the filing, even if its near-term practical effect is speculative.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration approaching supervisory guidance.** Investor CRE + multifamily + construction of $4,442 million against estimated total risk-based capital of ~$1,542 million is roughly **288%**, versus the 300% guidance threshold (construction alone ~55% of capital against a 100% guideline). This is our derived estimate; regulators apply a precise definition we cannot replicate and FBNC does not disclose its own calculation. Crossing 300% typically brings heightened examination attention, and as a >$10 billion institution FBNC is already under enhanced supervision. This is also the most plausible source of friction in the First Carolina approval process, since the target adds CRE.

**Credit migration, slow but consistent.** NPAs $35.8M → $37.7M → $44.9M across the last three reporting dates; NPL/loans 0.42% → 0.49%; ACL/loans 1.47% → 1.39%; ACL/NPL 348% → 282%; classified accruing +8.9% to $63.7 million; collateral-dependent loans +73% to $16.7 million. Provision of $1.2 million in Q2 barely exceeded $1.0 million of net charge-offs and did essentially nothing to reserve $195 million of quarterly loan growth. Offsetting: special-mention accruing loans fell 17% to $24.4 million and foreclosed real estate is down to $659 thousand, so the early-stage pipeline is improving even as late-stage nonaccruals rise. The 10-K quantifies the tail: a downside macro scenario would add roughly $32 million to the modeled allowance — about $0.61 per share, 2.1% of TCE. A one-year earnings event, not a capital event.

**SBA is the worst-performing niche.** $15.5 million of nonaccrual SBA loans is 35.0% of all nonaccruals on a $146.9 million portfolio (a ~10.6% nonaccrual rate) against $9.1 million of guarantees. At 1.6% of total loans the loss content is contained, but a third of the bank's problem loans come from a rounding error of the balance sheet.

**Uninsured deposits.** Roughly $4.6 billion at 6/30/26, with the insured share declining three consecutive periods (61.0% → 60.2% → 58.9%; 65.7% including collateralized). Contingent liquidity roughly matches rather than comfortably exceeds uninsured balances.

**Goodwill.** $478.8 million equals 39% of TCE. No impairment has been taken across four acquisitions (2017 ×2, 2021, 2023), and the 10-K explicitly flags the risk of paying premiums that dilute tangible book.

**Governance.** Moore simultaneously chairs the Board, the Executive and Loan Committee — which "is authorized, between meetings of the Board, to perform all duties and exercise all authority of the Board" and doubles as the bank's loan committee, meeting 11 times in 2025 — and the Risk Committee, while serving as CEO. A sitting CEO chairing both the body exercising plenary board authority and the body responsible for independent risk oversight is not standard practice, particularly at a bank levering into CRE. Mitigants are real: James Crawford III is Lead Independent Director and chairs Compensation and Nominating, the Audit Committee is fully independent with a designated financial expert, 8 of 11 directors are independent, hedging and pledging are both prohibited, directors stand for annual election, and a Dodd-Frank clawback policy is in place.

**Incentive-metric adjustment.** The 2025 annual incentive paid 108.8% of target using Adjusted diluted EPS of $4.01. GAAP diluted EPS of $2.68 was below the $2.81 threshold; on a GAAP basis that 50%-weighted measure pays zero and total payout would have been 37.7%. The difference is roughly $908 thousand of incremental value to four NEOs, before twelve other senior participants, and three of the four also received discretionary cash bonuses. Excluding securities-restructuring losses is common practice, the adjustment is fully disclosed and reconciled, the same treatment was applied in 2024, and — as established above — realizing an embedded AOCI loss destroys no incremental equity, so there is a defensible economic case. We do not infer motive. The relevant fact for scoring is that GAAP results have missed the company's own incentive thresholds two years running, and management's compensation was insulated from a decision (a ~5-year earnback) whose costs land now and whose benefits accrue later. Absolute comp is genuinely modest — CEO total $1.64 million, pay ratio 27.8x, CEO/NEO ratio 1.5x, 94% say-on-pay — which is why this dimension scores 5.0 rather than lower.

**Insider activity.** Across roughly seven months of Form 4s, every acquisition was a grant; there were **no open-market purchases by any executive or director**. Mayer sold 44,324 shares at $58.58 on May 20 ($2.60 million, ~52% of his holding) after retiring February 28; Crawford sold 4,500 at $60.57; Capel sold 7,642 after retiring as a director. The pattern reads as retirement-driven diversification, and under our framework insider selling that co-occurs with rising estimates and record results is low-signal. The absence of any offsetting buying — including by a new bank CEO with a fresh 2030-vesting grant — is a soft negative rather than a thesis input.

**Verification gaps.** The Item 4.01 8-K covering the BDO-to-Crowe auditor change (filed March 10, 2025) was not read; surrounding evidence (flat fees — BDO $1.19M vs Crowe $1.18M — no disagreements disclosed, no restatement, effective ICFR in both the 10-K and 10-Q) points to routine rotation, but the authoritative document is unverified. The March 2 and April 28, 2026 Item 5.02 filings are also unread; the latter most likely covers the Hans and Nevin director additions inferred from June 2026 grant timing. ASU 2025-08 (purchased-loan gross-up) will be adopted in the quarter First Carolina closes, reducing the day-one EPS and TBV hit relative to prior CECL treatment; the quantitative effect is undisclosed.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is too thin to carry weight: 25 contracts across three expirations, with put/call open-interest ratio reported at 0.00. Taken at face value, near-term ATM IV of 36.9% against 27.3% one-year realized vol is rich (a 9.6-point spread), the term structure is backwardated (36.9% at 13 days versus 28.1% at six months), and the six-month put/call skew of 8.2% is characterized as elevated put demand — the classic signature of an imminent event. But Q2 was reported July 22 and the next print is late October, so there is no scheduled company catalyst inside the 13-day tenor; the plausible drivers are macro and merger-approval headlines. More informative is the ATM IV collapse from 76.6% on June 18 to 42.8% on August 7 alongside a 6.6% price gain — uncertainty resolving, not building. Short interest of 6.1% of float with 7.0 days to cover reflects thin volume (231k shares/day average) more than conviction. The options data neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view, and we do not weight it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($77, +19.7%).** The deposit franchise is the asset, and it holds. Interest-bearing deposit cost stabilizes near 1.95%, NIM holds above 3.70%, and 16.9% ROTCE proves to be run-rate rather than peak. First Carolina closes in Q4 2026, delivers 5% accretion on 35% cost savings, and doubles the South Carolina platform in a top-decile-growth market. With 12.5x LTM earnings paid against FBNC's own 14.2x, and stock issued at 2.15x tangible book to buy at roughly 2.0x, the deal is arithmetic-positive before any synergy. Credit migration proves idiosyncratic (a third of nonaccruals from a 1.6% SBA book, granular non-metro office), net charge-offs stay near 0.05–0.10%, and the securities marks accrete back as rates ease at the front end. Tangible book compounds at 11%+, four consecutive beats become six, and the market pays 14.5x for a bank compounding tangible book at double digits with 14% CET1 and $0.5 billion of excess capital available for further deals.

**Bear case ($50, −22.3%).** The margin engine reverses. Deposit costs continue climbing as MMDA supplies growth, the Fed cuts, and asset sensitivity bites (−2.8% NII per 100bp cut, ~$0.24/share). The loan book that grew is the wrong book: construction up 25% annualized and multifamily up 31% annualized into a market where the company's own filings note CRE prices remain high relative to fundamentals, with investor CRE at ~288% of capital limiting further growth or forcing capital retention. Credit follows the leading indicators already visible — collateral-dependent loans +73%, classified accruing +8.9%, ACL/NPL coverage down 66 points — and provisions normalize toward $25–30 million annually. 2027 EPS falls to ~$4.20 and the multiple compresses toward 12x and 1.65x tangible book. Governance offers no cushion: a CEO chairing the plenary board committee, the loan committee and the risk committee, with incentive metrics adjusted around two consecutive years of GAAP threshold misses.

**Our resolution.** The bull case is more likely than the bear, but the base case is already close to the price. Owning FBNC here is a bet on tangible-book compounding (~11% annualized, moderated to ~9–10% by deal dilution) at a flat-to-slightly-lower multiple, plus a 1.5% dividend. That is a fair, not compelling, proposition, which is what HOLD describes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment horizon: 12 months, with the Q3 2026 print (late October) as the first decision point and First Carolina's close (Q4 2026 / Q1 2027) as the second.**

**Targets and math from $64.34:**
- **Base $67.50 (+4.9%)** — blend of 13.0x FY2027 consensus $5.05 ($65.65) and ~2.05x mid-2027 tangible book of ~$32.70 ($67.00). Plus $0.96 dividend → ~6.4% total return.
- **Bull $77 (+19.7%)** — 14.5x 2027 EPS of ~$5.30 on demonstrated margin durability and 5% deal accretion.
- **Bear $50 (−22.3%)** — 12x 2027 EPS of ~$4.20, equivalently ~1.65x tangible book.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at $56–58** (roughly 1.9x current tangible book, 11.5x FY2027 consensus), which would restore a 15%+ margin of safety to base case with unchanged fundamentals.
2. **Cost of interest-bearing deposits flat or lower for two consecutive quarters** with NIM held at or above 3.65%, confirming the Q1→Q2 uptick was timing rather than trend.
3. **Loan mix normalizing**: C&I balances returning to growth while construction and multifamily growth decelerates below 10% annualized, with the derived investor-CRE-to-capital ratio moving back below 275%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **ACL/NPL coverage below 225%** or nonperforming assets above $60 million, with provision still below net charge-offs — reserve building deferred while problems accumulate.
2. **NIM below 3.55% in any quarter** on rising deposit costs, which would invalidate the 16.9%-ROTCE-is-durable premise underpinning the 2.16x tangible book multiple.
3. **Deal terms deteriorating**: a regulatory delay past Q1 2027, a disclosed CRE-concentration condition, or TBV dilution disclosed above 3%.
4. **Any goodwill impairment** against the $478.8 million balance (39% of TCE), or a reversal in tangible book per share for two consecutive quarters.

**What would take us to AVOID:** net charge-offs above 0.35% annualized combined with the investor-CRE ratio crossing 300%, which would convert the credit question from an earnings event into a growth-and-capital constraint.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** First Bancorp is a well-run bank with a scarce liability franchise, upper-tier profitability (1.56% ROA, 16.9% adjusted ROTCE, 49.3% efficiency), fortress capital (14.09% CET1, ~$0.5 billion of excess total capital), four consecutive earnings beats, and a disciplined, accretive acquisition that doubles its South Carolina platform at 12.5x earnings using stock issued at a premium to the price paid.
