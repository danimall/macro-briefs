**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Deere is the dominant global agricultural-equipment manufacturer, working through a pronounced cyclical downturn that has roughly halved net income from a $10.16B FY2023 peak to $5.0B in FY2025. The evidence on a trough is accumulating: Construction & Forestry sales rose 29% YoY in Q2 FY2026 with the segment outlook raised to roughly +20%, European ag retail is up double digits, and North American dealer combine inventories have fallen to 12% of trailing sales from 17%. This is the late-downcycle stabilization pattern that has historically preceded Deere's strongest forward returns. The Production & Precision Ag profit engine remains in contraction: PPA sales fell 14% and operating profit dropped from $1,148M to $706M in the quarter, with U.S./Canada large ag still guided down 15-20%.

The problem is price. At $588.47 the stock trades at roughly 33x trailing and ~33x forward earnings, above the level at which a cyclical recovery is reasonably underwritten. The Q2 FY2026 EPS beat ($6.55 vs $5.87 consensus) was substantially driven by a one-time IEEPA tariff refund rather than operational strength; the market saw through this, and the stock fell on the print. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been revised down 7.3% in the last 30 days, and while full-year FY2026 estimates have inched up 1.2%, the near-term momentum is negative. The recurring-revenue "Leap Ambitions" thesis that would justify a structural re-rating from cyclical machinery maker to tech-enabled platform is undercut by management's own 10-K admission of "slower than expected customer adoption" of precision technology and SaaS services.

We rate DE a HOLD. The business quality and competitive moat are genuine, the cycle is plausibly bottoming, and the company is a clean beneficiary of the precision-ag and autonomy themes. But the valuation prices in much of the recovery already, the FTC right-to-repair case poses an unresolved structural threat to the high-margin aftermarket, and the near-term earnings path is being revised lower. We would own this name on a pullback toward the low-$500s, not at 33x forward into a still-contracting large-ag market.

## Company Overview

Deere operates under the John Deere brand across four reporting segments. Production & Precision Ag (PPA, ~$17.3B FY2025 net sales) sells high-horsepower tractors and combines to large-scale row-crop operations; it is the most cyclical and most profitable segment. Small Ag & Turf (SAT, ~$10.2B) covers smaller tractors and turf/utility equipment. Construction & Forestry (C&F, ~$11.4B) covers earthmoving, roadbuilding and forestry. John Deere Financial is the captive finance arm that funds a large share of equipment sales globally.

The competitive moat rests on dominant North American large-ag share, a deeply entrenched independent dealer network, and the proprietary John Deere Operations Center digital platform, which aggregates farm and jobsite data used to train Deere's own machine-learning models. Precision-ag products including See & Spray targeted spraying and the S7 Series combines are the differentiators that support Deere's margin premium versus CNH Industrial, AGCO, and Kubota. The company's "Smart Industrial Operating Model" and "Leap Ambitions" framework (refined December 2025) are oriented toward precision agriculture, autonomy, and recurring software revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The downcycle is unambiguous in the multi-year numbers. Net sales and revenues fell from ~$61.3B (FY2023) to ~$51.7B (FY2024) to ~$45.7B (FY2025). Net income fell from $10.16B to $7.09B to $4.998B over the same period. The proxy's headline performance metric, Operating Return on Operating Assets, dropped from 52.5% to 21.7%, a mid-cycle reset rather than a structural impairment.

Q2 FY2026 (ended ~April 2026) showed the first signs of stabilization: net sales and revenues rose 5% YoY to $13.37B, with equipment operations margin of 16.9%. Net income of $1.77B (-2%) and diluted EPS of $6.55 (-1%) beat consensus, but the beat was largely a function of a one-time IEEPA tariff refund recovery following the Supreme Court matter, not operating improvement. Underlying PPA operating profit fell roughly 38% YoY. The segment picture is divergent: PPA sales -14% with profit down to $706M, SAT sales -16% but profit up to $574M, and C&F sales +29% with profit up to $379M.

FY2026 guidance: net income of $4.5-5.0B, equipment-operations operating cash flow of $4.5-5.5B, effective tax rate 24-26%, capex ~$1.4B, R&D up slightly. The C&F outlook was raised to ~+20% net sales. The earnings beat track record is solid (3 beats, 1 narrow miss over the last four quarters, with surprises of 11.5% and 14.7% in the two most recent), supporting reasonable confidence in management's ability to deliver against guidance.

On the balance sheet, Deere's reported leverage and any Altman Z-Score read will look distressed, but this is a structural artifact of the captive-finance model: John Deere Financial carries large receivables-funded debt that inflates consolidated leverage. Deere explicitly targets a single-A credit rating to preserve low-cost funding, and capital allocation runs in the order: maintain A rating, fund operations/growth, grow the dividend (25-35% of mid-cycle EPS payout target), then return remaining FCF via buybacks across the cycle. This is normal for an equipment-finance business and not a distress signal. The genuine financial-health concern is the rising allowance for credit losses on retail notes and leases, driven by elevated ag/turf delinquencies, the clearest real-time read on customer stress and a leading indicator worth monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is two-sided and currently more negative than positive on a near-term basis. The cyclical recovery signals are real: C&F strength, European ag retail up double digits, and improving dealer inventories all point toward the destock cycle passing. Construction-equipment demand benefits from the same infrastructure and data-center buildout visible across the broader industrials complex. Historically, late-downcycle stabilization precedes Deere's strongest forward returns as PPA volumes recover.

Against this, the near-term estimate trend is negative. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been revised down 7.3% over 30 days (to $4.71 from $5.09) and next-quarter down 2.6%. Full-year FY2026 estimates have risen a modest 1.2% to $18.10, but FY2027 has slipped 1.4% to $22.83. This is broadly falling near-term momentum with a stabilizing full-year picture, not the strong positive estimate-revision velocity that would signal a turn. U.S./Canada large ag, Deere's profit center, remains guided down 15-20%.

The structural growth thesis depends on the "Leap Ambitions" pivot toward recurring precision-ag and SaaS revenue. The FY2025 10-K concedes "slower than expected customer adoption of some of our precision technology solutions, and SaaS subscription services," and that building SaaS infrastructure "has delayed the timing of realization of the expected benefits." This admission undercuts the premium-multiple, tech-enabled-platform narrative that bulls use to justify the current valuation. The technology and data assets are real and defensible, but the monetization timeline is being pushed out by the company's own account.

## Valuation Assessment

At $588.47 against trailing EPS of $18.50, Deere trades at ~33x trailing and ~33x forward on the FY2026 consensus of $18.10. On the FY2027 estimate of $22.83 the forward multiple compresses to ~26x. Peer medians in the broad industrials set sit around 35x P/E, but that comparison is misleading: the peer group (ETN, UNP, BA, HON) is dominated by structural-growth and aerospace-recovery stories, not a cyclical ag-equipment maker mid-downcycle. A more relevant frame is Deere's own cycle: a 33x multiple on still-contracting large-ag earnings is full, not cheap.

The cycle-adjusted lens matters here. If you normalize to mid-cycle earnings power closer to FY2024's $7.09B net income (~$26 EPS at current share count) or above, the multiple looks more reasonable at ~22x. But that normalization requires the large-ag recovery to materialize on schedule, and the company is guiding that segment down another 15-20% this year. The Q2 beat being tariff-refund-driven removes one prop from the bull-on-fundamentals argument.

We do not penalize the stock simply for having appreciated 12.5% over the trailing year or for sitting below its $674 high. The issue is that current price embeds a confident recovery while near-term estimates are falling and the highest-margin profit engine is still shrinking. Analyst targets (mean $644, median $640, range $500-759) bracket meaningful upside to the mean, but on a name at 33x forward into a falling near-term estimate trend, those targets reflect the same recovery optimism as the price rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. We assign a below-average valuation score: the multiple is stretched relative to the earnings actually being delivered, and a low forward multiple here would require the recovery to be both fast and full.

## Competitive Landscape

Deere's competitive position is strong and largely stable, the highest-scoring dimension in this analysis. The company holds dominant North American large-ag share, and the synchronized sector downcycle (low crop commodity prices and high input costs) is hitting CNH, AGCO, and Kubota broadly, so margin compression is industry-wide rather than Deere-specific. Deere's premium precision-tech positioning, dealer network, and Operations Center data platform are the source of its margin advantage and the basis on which it should emerge from the trough with relative strength.

The trajectory has softened modestly versus prior assessments, which justifies a step down from the 8.5 competitive-position scores assigned earlier in the year. Two threads explain this. First, the FTC right-to-repair case (see Risk Assessment) directly targets the high-margin parts-and-service aftermarket and the dealer-network moat, a structural rather than cyclical threat. Second, management's admission of slower precision-tech adoption suggests the data/software advantage is converting to revenue more slowly than the bull case requires. Deere remains a clear category leader, but the moat is being actively contested on the repair/aftermarket front.

## Risk Assessment

**Ag business-cycle exposure** is the dominant risk, and it is largely priced: the 10-K states unfavorable FY2025 conditions caused lower volumes, greater reliance on sales incentives, and elevated receivable write-offs, and that "certain of these conditions [will] persist in fiscal year 2026."

**FTC right-to-repair** is the most important under-appreciated risk. The widely-reported $99M settlement resolves only the private farmer class action and is financially immaterial. The separate FTC plus five-state-AG antitrust lawsuit (filed January 2025) remains live; Deere's motion to dismiss was denied, and the company is in "preliminary discussions" toward resolution. The suit seeks a permanent injunction forcing open access to repair tools and resources, which could structurally erode the high-margin aftermarket and the dealer moat. Deere itself flags a potential "material adverse effect." Construction-segment right-to-repair pressure is now expanding as a second front. This is a genuine binary that the multiple does not appear to discount.

**Credit deterioration in Financial Services** — rising allowance for credit losses and elevated ag/turf delinquencies — is the canary for the cycle and a real downside vector if farm income stays weak.

**Tariffs and trade** carried a ~$600M direct incremental cost in FY2025. The IEEPA refund that flattered Q2 is a one-time recovery, not a recurring tailwind. Mexico-sourced production (skid-steers, compact-track-loaders) and Chinese rare-earth sourcing add exposure.

**Used-equipment overhang** pressured price realization in 2025, though destocking is progressing (combines down to 12% of trailing sales from 17%).

Labor (unionized workforce, staggered CBA expirations, strike history) and a steady cadence of director/officer changes through 2025-2026 round out the risk picture.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure shows ATM IV at 131.5% against one-year historical vol of 29.7% — extremely rich, in steep backwardation versus the 27.7% three-month tenor, with the chain at the 90th IV percentile. This reflects a near-dated event premium (the one-day expiry implies only a ±1.1% move, so the headline 131.5% is a thin-expiry artifact rather than a clean signal of imminent fundamental stress). The more informative reads are the call-heavy skew (-5.9%) and the P/C OI ratio collapsing from 1.06 to 0.37 since the prior snapshot, indicating positioning has tilted bullish/short-dated. Short interest is low at 1.9% of float (4 days to cover). The options market is not flashing a bearish warning that contradicts the fundamental HOLD; if anything, positioning leans mildly constructive into the recovery, consistent with our view that downside is more about valuation than imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$700-759):** The ag cycle troughs in FY2026, C&F and Europe lead the recovery, dealer inventories normalize, and large-ag volumes inflect higher into FY2027. FY2027 EPS reaches the ~$23 consensus or above; the market re-rates Deere toward mid-cycle earnings power and a tech-enabled-platform multiple as precision-ag/SaaS adoption finally accelerates. The FTC case settles on terms that preserve the aftermarket economics. At ~30x on normalized EPS approaching $25, the stock approaches the $759 high target.

**Bear Case (~$470-500):** Large-ag weakness persists into FY2027, farm income stays depressed, Financial Services credit losses accelerate, and the one-time tariff tailwind reverses to a headwind. The FTC injunction forces open repair access and compresses aftermarket margins structurally. The multiple de-rates toward ~20x on flat-to-down earnings of ~$22-23, taking the stock to the $500 low analyst target or below. This is roughly 15% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between these: the cycle stabilizes but recovers slowly, FY2026 lands near the midpoint of guidance ($4.75B net income), and the stock holds in a wide range as the market waits for confirmation of the large-ag inflection. At ~30x on the FY2026 consensus of $18.10, base-case fair value is approximately $540-560, below the current price, which is why we are not buyers here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings report (expected ~August 2026), with the FY2027 guidance update and any FTC resolution as the key intervening catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$500s, compressing the forward multiple to ~28x or below and restoring a margin of safety against the recovery.
- U.S./Canada large-ag guidance shifting from "down 15-20%" to stabilizing or rising, confirming the PPA inflection.
- A clean, operational earnings beat (not tariff-refund-driven) with positive estimate revisions, plus evidence of accelerating precision-ag/SaaS adoption.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse FTC right-to-repair remedy forcing open aftermarket access, with quantified margin impact.
- Accelerating Financial Services credit losses signaling a deepening rather than bottoming farm-income cycle.
- Current-year (FY2026) estimate revisions turning negative alongside guidance cuts, indicating the trough has not arrived.

## Conclusion

Deere is a best-in-class cyclical with a genuine moat, working through an industry-wide ag downturn that is plausibly bottoming. The construction segment, European recovery, and improving dealer inventories support the late-downcycle stabilization read, and management has a credible record of delivering against guidance. But the stock at ~33x forward earnings prices in much of the recovery before the highest-margin large-ag segment has stopped contracting, the Q2 beat was flattered by a one-time tariff refund, near-term estimates are being revised lower, and the unresolved FTC right-to-repair case poses a structural threat to the aftermarket that the multiple does not appear to discount. The recurring-revenue premium that would justify a higher multiple is undercut by management's own admission of delayed adoption.

We rate this a HOLD: the business is worth owning, but not at this entry. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$500s or on confirmed evidence of the large-ag inflection. The fourth consecutive HOLD reflects a name that has been range-bound around fair value while the cycle works through its bottom; the thesis has not changed enough to warrant a different call, and the bar for a BUY here is a better price or a clearer operational turn.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward P/E was overstated as ~32x (FY2026 consensus $18.10 on $588.47 = ~33x, with no FY2026 EPS-derived multiple below 33x), and the FY2026 midpoint EPS figure (~$17.6) and the "low-$500s = ~24x" upgrade math were not reproducible from the sources — none of these changes the HOLD direction but they remove part of the "valuation is full" cushion relied upon.
