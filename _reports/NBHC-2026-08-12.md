## Executive Summary

National Bank Holdings closed its tenth bank acquisition on January 7, 2026, buying Texas-based Vista Bancshares for $377.7M — $89.0M cash plus 7.3 million shares at $39.51. The strategic logic was sound and, in a narrow sense, has worked. NBHC's legacy originated loan book was flat in FY2025 (-0.2%), adjusted ROATCE had fallen 608 basis points in two years to 12.15%, and adjusted pre-provision net revenue was still 10.6% below its FY2023 level. Vista delivered Dallas, Austin and Lubbock — markets with 20-27% decade population growth against 5.7% nationally — and organic loan growth genuinely resumed. The H1 2026 cash flow statement shows a $434.3M net increase in loans versus a $237.1M net decrease in H1 2025, a swing of over $670M, annualizing near 9.3% on the post-deal base.

The price paid was real and the earnings that fund the story are lower-quality than the headline. Tangible book value per share fell from $27.80 at year-end 2025 to roughly $26.24 at June 30, 2026 — about 7% of transaction-driven dilution — while consolidated CET1 dropped from 14.89% to 12.3%. More important for forward estimates, three separate mechanisms are flattering reported results. Net interest margin of 3.94% in Q2 (down 12bp sequentially from 4.06%) is being supported by accretion of $40.2M of Vista purchase discounts: the acquired loan yield rose 53bp year over year to 6.69% while the originated yield fell 44bp to 6.09%. H1 provision expense of $5.5M is roughly 0.11% annualized against net charge-offs running 0.30%, and the early adoption of ASU 2025-08 routed the $31.9M day-one acquisition allowance to the balance sheet rather than the income statement, making the period's credit cost non-comparable to prior years and to non-adopting peers. And FY2025 fee income growth of 10.3% leaned on $7.0M of unrealized investment marks, one of which reversed into a $0.7M realized loss when monetized in H1 2026.

At $43.37 the stock trades at approximately 1.65x tangible book and 13.1x a current-year consensus of $3.30 that has been cut 4.9% in the last thirty days, with next year cut 4.8% to $3.93. For a bank earning ~12% adjusted ROATCE with a non-interest-bearing deposit share that has fallen from 40.2% in 2021 to 24.8%, a dated interchange-revenue hit arriving around July 1, 2027, an unconverted core system, and the acquired bank's CEO departed seven months after closing, that is not an attractive entry. Our base case of $44 returns roughly the dividend; the bear case of $34 is a 22% loss. We move to REDUCE from HOLD, the third consecutive review in a declining score sequence (6.4 → 5.7 → 4.8).

## Company Overview

NBHC is a serial acquirer of community banks, founded in 2010, now operating two charters: NBH Bank and the former Bank of Jackson Hole Trust, renamed 2UniFi Bank effective July 31, 2026. The footprint spans Colorado, greater Kansas City, Texas, Utah, Wyoming, New Mexico, Idaho and one Palm Beach, Florida banking center — roughly 90+ banking centers and about 1,300 employees. It reports a single operating segment.

Post-Vista, total assets are $12,586.1M (up 27.3% from year-end 2025), loans $9,774.1M (+31.5%) and deposits $10,389.4M (+25.3%). The loan mix at year-end 2025 was 62.8% commercial, 21.3% non-owner-occupied CRE, 15.7% residential. Three features distinguish the franchise. Municipal and non-profit lending was $1.27B (17.1% of loans), including $1.01B of tax-exempt credit at a 4.79% FTE rate — the reason the effective tax rate runs near 18% against a ~23% marginal rate, and the one legacy line that grew (15.1%) while the rest of the originated book stalled. Cambr is a proprietary deposit-gathering platform serving embedded-finance companies, which management's own MD&A classifies among unsecured wholesale-type funding sources. 2UniFi is a de novo digital SMB platform now housed on its own charter.

Revenue is overwhelmingly spread-based; FY2025 non-interest income was 15.94% of FTE revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**The pre-deal trajectory is the necessary starting point.** Net income fell from $142.0M (FY2023) to $118.8M (FY2024) to $109.6M (FY2025); diluted EPS from $3.72 to $2.85. Adjusted ROATCE went 18.23% → 14.20% → 12.15%. The adjusted efficiency ratio deteriorated from 54.31% to 58.43%. Total loans *fell* 4.1% in FY2025, with the originated book flat and the acquired book running off 22.1% — and this despite loan fundings of $1.59B, above FY2024's $1.54B. Payoffs, not origination capacity, were the constraint. The FY2025 NIM recovery of 9bp to 3.94% came entirely from the liability side: cost of funds down 22bp to 2.05% while earning-asset yields fell 13bp. That relief is finite.

**Post-deal results, six months ended June 30, 2026.** Q2 net interest income $109.3M (+25.0% YoY); provision $1.5M; fee income $19.8M; non-interest expense $95.0M including $11.2M of acquisition and restructuring cost; net income $26.5M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.58. H1 net income $47.3M, GAAP diluted EPS $1.04 versus $1.51 a year earlier; company-adjusted net income $67.9M (+16.6%), adjusted diluted EPS $1.50; adjusted PPNR FTE $95.3M (+11.5%).

**On the beat/miss record, a correction is required.** The historical surprise table shows three misses in four quarters, but two of those comparisons put GAAP actuals against non-GAAP estimates. Q1 2026's "$0.46 versus $0.66" compares GAAP EPS to an adjusted estimate; on a like-for-like basis adjusted Q1 EPS was $0.72, a beat. The Q4 2025 comparison ($0.60 versus $0.82) has the same defect. Only the June quarter is a clean like-for-like non-GAAP comparison: **$0.78 adjusted versus $0.83 consensus, a 5.7% miss.** The delivery record is therefore weaker than reported but not the disaster the table implies. What is unambiguous is the revision direction: current quarter -9.2%, next quarter -5.7%, current year -4.9% to $3.30, next year -4.8% to $3.93, all cut within the last thirty days by all four covering analysts.

**Margin quality.** Consolidated NIM FTE fell from 4.06% in Q1 to 3.94% in Q2, the low end of the "near 4%" guide. Decomposed: originated loan yield 6.09% FTE versus 6.53% a year earlier (-44bp); acquired loan yield 6.69% versus 6.16% (+53bp) on discount accretion. Part of the originated decline is simply the Fed cuts (100bp in 2024, 75bp in 2025) flowing through $3.68B of variable-rate loans — the interest-rate simulation confirms asset sensitivity, with +200bp lifting NII 4.65% and -100bp costing 1.95%. Both forces operate. The consequence either way is that the guided ~4% margin depends on a decaying accretion contribution plus continued deposit-cost relief.

**Provision and credit cost.** Reported H1 provision of $5.5M annualizes near 0.11% of loans while net charge-offs ran 0.30% annualized (following 0.34% in FY2025, up from 0.02% in 2023). Provisioning below charge-offs means the reserve is being drawn down. Separately, early adoption of ASU 2025-08 on January 1, 2026 recognized the $31.9M day-one acquisition allowance — including roughly $20.8M on non-PCD loans — as a balance-sheet gross-up rather than a "day two" provision charge. The standard was issued in November 2025 with early adoption expressly permitted and the treatment is defensible; we draw no inference about intent. The consequence for analysis is concrete: **H1 2026 provision expense cannot be compared to prior periods or to non-adopting peers.** As a labeled sensitivity, provisioning H1 at the 0.30% charge-off rate (≈$14.3M on the average book) would have cost roughly $8.8M pre-tax, $7.1M after tax at the 18.8% effective rate, or about $0.16 per diluted share — putting H1 adjusted EPS nearer $1.34 and the annualized run-rate multiple at ~16.2x rather than 14.5x.

**Capital and funding.** CET1 fell from 14.89% to 12.3%, Tier 1 leverage from 11.56% to 10.3%, Total RBC from 16.8% to 15.4% (cushioned by $150M of 5.875% Tier 2 subordinated notes issued February 11, 2026). Loans/deposits rose from 89.6% to 94.1%. All ratios remain comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds, but roughly 260bp of CET1 optionality was spent. On-balance-sheet liquidity rose in dollars to $915.3M but fell from 8.9% to 7.3% of assets, and supporting $3.87B of off-balance-sheet capacity required pledging $6.1B of loans, up from $4.3B. Combined AFS and HTM unrealized securities losses widened roughly $14M during the half to ~$125.7M pre-tax, of which the $61.5M HTM portion is unrecognized — approximately 5.3% of estimated tangible common equity. The portfolio is short (3.8-year duration) and entirely agency/GSE, so this is an opportunity-cost issue, not credit.

The new subordinated notes carry roughly $8.8M of annual pre-tax interest, about $0.16 per share after tax — a permanent drag against the accretion the deal delivered. A $39.9M note resets from 3.00% to 3M SOFR + 203bp in November 2026, adding perhaps $1.0-1.2M annually, though it becomes callable at par on the same date.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on one genuine improvement and three unproven extensions.

**The improvement is real.** Average originated loans in Q2 2026 were $6,762.5M versus $6,289.2M a year earlier, +7.5%. Net loan growth of $434.3M in H1 against a $237.1M decline a year prior annualizes near 9.3%, consistent with management's ~10% full-year guide. Vista's Texas markets are structurally faster-growing than legacy Colorado and Kansas City. That is the problem the acquisition was meant to solve, and it appears solved.

**The extensions are not.** Fee income guidance of $75-80M includes only $2-4M from 2UniFi, back-half weighted, against a ~$22M FY2026 expense run-rate for the platform (of which ~$10M is cash). The FY2025 10-K states plainly that "development of further capabilities is needed for the platform to reach its full potential and may require additional investment or a strategic partner," and management confirmed in April 2026 that no partnership had been executed. Recurring fee lines are shrinking: service charges -7.0%, bank card fees -6.0%, mortgage banking -1.3% in FY2025.

**A dated headwind sits inside the FY2027 consensus.** Crossing $10B in assets triggers loss of the Durbin interchange-cap exemption effective July 1 of the year following the crossing — approximately July 1, 2027 — plus direct CFPB supervision. Bank card fees of $17.8M are already declining. A meaningful fraction of that line disappears mid-2027 alongside incremental compliance cost, against a next-year consensus of $3.93 that four analysts have been cutting.

**The Q4 2026 target requires a steep ramp.** Management guides Q4 EPS above $1.00. On ~44.5M shares that implies roughly $45M of quarterly net income versus Q2 adjusted net income of $35.3M — about +28% in two quarters from earning-asset growth, the fee ramp and post-conversion cost saves. Arithmetically possible; the expense guide of $320-330M is internally consistent only on an adjusted basis (implying ~$77-82M quarterly in H2 against $83.7M adjusted in Q2), and management did not state the basis explicitly. The core system conversion was targeted for Q3 2026 and was in progress at the latest filing, with the acquired bank's CEO no longer at the company.

## Valuation Assessment

Reported multiples require reconciliation. The data feed shows trailing and forward P/E both at 18.03, which does not reconcile with $43.37 ÷ $2.85 FY2025 GAAP EPS (15.2x). We use our own computations.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price / tangible book (~$26.24) | **1.65x** |
| Price / current-year consensus $3.30 | **13.1x** |
| Price / next-year consensus $3.93 | **11.0x** |
| Price / H1 2026 adjusted EPS annualized ($3.00) | 14.5x |
| Price / H1 adjusted EPS at normalized provision (~$2.68) | ~16.2x |
| Dividend yield ($1.28) | 2.95% |
| Peer median P/E (Regional Banks) | 15.9x |
| Adjusted ROATCE (FY2025) | 12.15% |

The apparent discount to a 15.9x peer median is not the signal it looks like. Peer dispersion is wide — CASH at 10.9x, STBA at 13.5x, CHCO at 15.9x, TFIN at 51.5x — so the median is not a meaningful anchor, and NBHC's forward multiple sits inside the cluster of comparably-sized peers rather than below it. More importantly, the forward earnings on which the 13.1x rests are falling and are supported by three temporary or non-comparable items: purchase accretion in the margin, a provision below charge-offs under a newly adopted standard, and a fee base that included $7.0M of investment marks in FY2025.

The cleaner anchor is price to tangible book against return on tangible equity. A bank compounding tangible book at ~12% adjusted ROATCE, paying out 43% of adjusted earnings, generating roughly 7% internal TBV growth, generally supports 1.4-1.6x tangible book. At 1.65x, NBHC is at or slightly above that band while the return itself has fallen 608bp in two years and the non-interest-bearing deposit share has fallen from 40.2% to 24.8% — a funding franchise that is objectively worth less per dollar of assets than it was five years ago.

**Base case $44.** Normalizing FY2027 EPS to roughly $3.60 (consensus $3.93 less approximately $0.18 for the Durbin loss and modest accretion decay) at 12.2x, cross-checked against 1.6x an estimated mid-2027 TBVPS near $27.50. That is +1.5% from $43.37; with the dividend, total return near 4.5%.

**Bull case $53.** Conversion completes on schedule, cost saves land, Q4 2026 delivers above $1.00, and FY2027 reaches the current $3.93 consensus with the market awarding 13.5x. +22%.

**Bear case $34.** Ag and acquisition-and-development credit migrate to charge-off, provision normalizes to 0.35-0.40%, accretion decay drops NIM below 3.85%, and 2UniFi takes an impairment. EPS ~$3.10 at 11x, equivalent to ~1.27x tangible book. -22%.

Probability-weighting 25/45/30 yields an expected price return near zero, roughly 2.6% including the dividend. That is below fair compensation for the risk profile, and the asymmetry is unfavorable: the base case pays the dividend and little else.

The consensus target range of $46-51 (mean $48.5) sits entirely above spot, but sell-side sentiment is classified as degrading — one buy rating moved to hold in the past month — and the low target of $46 being above a post-earnings price suggests the distribution is stale relative to the July 22 reaction. We do not treat targets as independent confirmation here; the stock is not at a premium multiple, but the estimates behind those targets are being cut, which is the condition under which targets lag downward rather than upward.

## Competitive Landscape

Where NBHC screens well is on the tail risks that broke regional banks in 2023. Non-owner-occupied CRE was 127.1% of risk-based capital at year-end 2025, far below the 300% supervisory threshold. Office was 1.2% of loans including available credit and retail 1.9%; multifamily $298.5M. The securities book is 3.8-year duration, entirely agency/GSE. The company discloses no venture-capital or crypto deposit exposure and states no industry sector exceeds 15% of loans. On a mechanical CRE-and-duration screen, this is one of the cleaner $12B banks.

Where the position is weaker is the deposit side, and it is structural. The company's own filings disclose that top-three competitor combined deposit share is approximately 50% in Denver, 60% in Dallas, 69% in Salt Lake City and 78% in Jackson — NBHC competes for funding against institutions with lower structural funding costs and far larger technology budgets. The consequence is visible: non-interest-bearing deposits fell from 40.2% of total in 2021 to 24.8% at June 30, 2026, while money market accounts grew $1.26B (+40.7%) to 41.9% of deposits at a 2.74% cost. FDIC-insured coverage fell from ~76% to ~69%. Absolute NIB dollars rose $371.4M in H1, but that reflects acquired scale; the share decline establishes that the funding base has become materially more rate-sensitive. Vista arrived with a ~2.5% cost of deposits versus NBHC's ~1.94% — a remixing opportunity that carries attrition risk if executed aggressively.

The municipal and non-profit niche is a genuine, defensible advantage: $1.01B of tax-exempt loans producing a ~500bp gap between effective and marginal tax rates, and the one legacy line that grew when the rest of the originated book did not. Cambr is differentiated but its contribution to NBHC's own balance sheet is not disclosed in any filing — management's ">$2 billion" figure describes the program across the Cambr network, not deposits held at NBH Bank. Given that MD&A classifies Cambr among unsecured wholesale-type funding and risk factors warn explicitly that "increased concentration from program deposits or reliance on the Cambr program could have a material adverse effect," this is the largest unquantified item in the funding profile.

The company also flags the GENIUS Act stablecoin framework as a prospective competitive threat to deposit gathering — an unusual and forward-looking disclosure relative to peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit deterioration in identifiable pockets.** Loans 90+ days past due and still accruing went $591K (2023) → $14.9M (2024) → $15.4M (Dec 2025) → **$29.1M (June 2026)**, or 0.30% of loans, nearly doubling in six months. Composition: food and agribusiness $13.6M, C&I $6.7M, owner-occupied CRE $3.5M, residential $3.2M, CRE construction $2.1M. The ag figure equals 5.7% of that entire $238.5M loan class. Corroborating signals: collateral-dependent loans over $250K rose 71% to $48.2M with the real-property-secured portion up roughly 4x; accruing modified loans tripled to $43.8M in H2 2025 and stood at $46.5M, largest category non-owner-occupied CRE at $31.2M; fair-value losses on individually evaluated loans rose 2.9x to $27.2M in FY2025. Criticized loans fell from 4.23% to 3.05% of loans, but in dollars the decline was only $16.3M (-5.2%) against 31.5% loan growth — a denominator effect. Decomposing is more useful than the total: C&I criticized fell 14% and owner-occupied CRE 24% (genuine legacy healing), while food and agribusiness criticized rose 90% to $26.8M and acquisition-and-development appeared at $24.4M from a $0.3M base, almost certainly Vista-sourced.

Mitigants deserve equal weight. NBHC's policy retains 90+ day loans on accrual only when well-secured and in process of collection, so management has affirmatively made that judgment. Ag credits renew on crop cycles and technical delinquency without loss is common. Food and agribusiness is only ~2.4% of the post-Vista book, capping absolute severity.

**Off-balance-sheet exposure grew faster than the balance sheet.** Total unfunded commitments rose from $1,148.1M to $1,929.2M, and commercial and standby letters of credit from $8.0M to $160.0M — roughly 20x — against 31.5% loan growth. Standby LCs are guarantees of client performance carrying loan-equivalent credit risk, subject to the same underwriting, but sitting outside the loan and allowance tables most screens read. Most expire undrawn; the concern is the rate of change and the absence of granular disclosure on a book that is essentially new to the company.

**Integration and key-person risk.** John Steinmetz, Vista's CEO, joined as Executive Vice Chair on January 7, 2026 and transitioned out effective August 5, 2026 — seven months post-close and before the Q3 core system conversion. The package totals roughly $3.56M in cash ($66,000/month consulting through December 2027, $720,000 in 2027 installments, a $1,720,000 release payment) plus continued equity vesting; he holds 533,377 shares (~1.2% of outstanding), a potential overhang. Founder-CEOs of acquired banks routinely depart within a year and the arrangement may have been contemplated in his September 15, 2025 employment agreement, so we do not infer conflict. The operational point stands: the person who built the Texas franchise that justified the deal is no longer running it while the systems are being converted. Item 5.02 filings on February 19, May 12 and August 4, 2026 indicate multiple senior personnel changes clustered around integration.

**2UniFi.** Cumulative expense of $34.6M across 2024-2025 plus a guided ~$22M in FY2026 against $2-4M of expected revenue, with the 10-K stating additional investment or a strategic partner may be required and that the company is "continuing to evaluate the allocation of additional capital or other alternative paths." Capitalized costs sit in premises and equipment, which rose from $162.7M (2023) to $234.1M. Impairment risk is explicit, not inferred. The charter itself is tiny — $14.9M of total risk-based capital at a 38.4% Tier 1 leverage ratio implies roughly $39M of average assets.

**Governance.** Discretionary merger bonuses of $725,000 total were approved February 24, 2026 (Laney $400,000) outside the annual incentive framework, rewarding transaction completion in a period when the transaction was tangible-book-dilutive. Of 567,549 Vista-related equity grants, one-third vests December 15, 2026 half on "successful closing, integration and rebranding… as determined by the Board's Compensation Committee in its sole discretion" — not an objective hurdle. Stock-based compensation rose 3.6x from $3.7M to $13.4M year over year with $33.7M unrecognized. Capital allocation is internally inconsistent: 670,340 shares repurchased in H1 at a $40.64 average (~1.55x tangible book) in the same six months the company issued 7.3M shares at $39.51 (1.42x year-end TBVPS) as deal consideration. Offsetting these: compensation actually paid fell 47% to $2,719,246 tracking the share price, and 39,673 PSUs from the 2023 grant were cancelled on below-target performance — pay-for-performance is functioning. Five-year total shareholder return through December 2025 was 132.58 versus 152.74 for the KBW Regional Banking Index, with the entire gap opening in 2025 (-8.9% versus +6.5%).

Insider activity across roughly March-June 2026 consists of grants and Code F tax-withholding dispositions, plus one small open-market director sale (963 shares at $41.84). No open-market purchases by executives or directors. That is neutral-to-unsupportive rather than bearish; the absence of buying is weak evidence, and the window is short.

**Two fraud-adjacent disclosures in four years.** FY2025 included an $8.9M charge-off from a single credit "due to suspected fraud by the borrower," characterized as isolated. Separately, the FY2025 10-K still carries the 2022 disclosure of "irregularities in mortgage loan applications in one of our offices" with files that "may still be under review by outside stakeholders." No litigation or enforcement action is disclosed, the mortgage repurchase reserve is immaterial at $557K, and KPMG issued an unqualified opinion on internal control as of December 31, 2025. We assert no control failure. Two independent fraud-adjacent events at a bank this size is an underwriting-and-monitoring data point worth tracking.

A note on screens: an Altman Z-Score reading for NBHC should be disregarded entirely — the model is calibrated to non-financial manufacturers and its working-capital and sales-to-assets terms are meaningless for a deposit-funded bank. The correct solvency test is regulatory capital, which is adequate.

### Options Market Signal

The options data here should be treated with low confidence. The chain contains only 23 contracts across four expirations, and the change log shows ATM implied volatility moving from 990.9% to 40.3% between snapshots — an artifact, not a market signal. The nearest expiry (August 21, eight days) shows 76.6% ATM IV against 25.5% one-year historical volatility with a 13.9% implied move, in a window containing no scheduled catalyst; earnings were July 21 and the next print is late October. That combination in a thin chain reads as quoting noise or a single wide bid-ask spread rather than positioning.

What is marginally usable: the six-month tenor at 29.7% IV with a 6.4% put skew, described as elevated put demand, and put/call open interest reported at 0.00 (i.e., effectively no open put interest recorded). Those two readings conflict, which reinforces the reliability caution. Short interest is a cleaner read: 4.5% of float, 3.9 days to cover — modest, uncrowded, no squeeze dynamic in either direction. Nothing in the options complex materially confirms or challenges the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (25% probability) — $53, +22%

Vista restored the growth engine and the market has not yet paid for it. Organic loan growth of ~9.3% annualized against a legacy franchise that shrank is the single most important operational change in years, and the Texas markets acquired grow structurally faster than Colorado and Kansas City. The Q3 core conversion completes on schedule, unlocking cost saves that step adjusted quarterly expense down from $83.7M toward $77-82M and delivering the guided Q4 EPS above $1.00 — a ~$4.00 annualized run-rate that puts the stock near 11x. Purchase accretion, rather than being a crutch, provides two to three years of margin support while the deposit franchise remixes Vista's 2.5% cost of funds downward. Credit deterioration is confined to 2.4% of the book in a sector experiencing cyclical input-cost pressure, and the legacy commercial book is objectively healing (C&I criticized -14%, owner-occupied CRE -24%). Capital at 12.3% CET1 supports both the $72.8M remaining buyback and continued growth. FY2027 consensus of $3.93 is achieved, the market pays 13.5x, and the stock re-rates to $53.

### Bear Case (30% probability) — $34, -22%

The reported earnings power is partly manufactured. Strip the accretion contribution and the core margin is compressing — originated yields fell 44bp year over year while the funding mix deteriorated to 24.8% non-interest-bearing. Normalize provision to the 0.30-0.40% charge-off range and H1 adjusted EPS drops roughly $0.16 per half. Ag delinquency at 5.7% of that loan class and Vista's newly-appeared $24.4M of criticized acquisition-and-development credit migrate to charge-off through 2027 as the Fed holds and the consumer weakens. The Durbin exemption loss lands around July 1, 2027 against an already-declining $17.8M interchange line, with CFPB supervision costs on top. 2UniFi takes an impairment against a premises-and-equipment balance that has grown $71.4M in three years, with no strategic partner executed. The Q3 conversion slips or overruns without the acquired franchise's leader in place. Estimates continue the -5% quarterly cuts already visible, FY2027 lands near $3.10, and a 12% ROATCE bank with a rate-sensitive funding base re-rates to 1.27x tangible book — $34.

### Base Case (45% probability) — $44, +1.5% plus dividend

Conversion completes with modest slippage, cost saves arrive later and smaller than guided, and Q4 2026 lands near $0.90-0.95 rather than above $1.00. NIM holds 3.85-3.95% as accretion offsets core compression. Credit costs normalize to 0.30-0.35% without a discrete event. Durbin takes roughly $0.15-0.18 out of FY2027, landing EPS near $3.60. The market pays 12.2x, or 1.6x an estimated $27.50 mid-2027 tangible book, for $44. The 2.95% dividend, covered at 43% of adjusted earnings, is the return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Expected total return near 2-3% over twelve months on a probability-weighted basis, against a 22% bear case and a base case that pays only the dividend.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing positions into strength above $45, ahead of the October Q3 print that will disclose conversion status and the first post-conversion expense run-rate. We would not exit entirely: capital is adequate, CRE and duration risk are genuinely low, and the loan-growth recovery is real. A reduced position sized to the dividend rather than to appreciation is appropriate. New capital should wait for either evidence or a better price.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Q3 2026 reported NIM at or above 3.94% *with* the originated loan yield stable or higher sequentially — evidence that core repricing has stopped rather than that accretion has increased.
2. 90+ day accruing delinquencies back below $20M and food-and-agribusiness criticized loans below $20M, confirming the ag pocket was technical rather than credit.
3. Adjusted non-interest expense at or below $80M in Q3, confirming the conversion cost saves in the guide.
4. Price below $37 — approximately 1.40x tangible book and ~10.3x the current next-year consensus, at which the base case offers a genuine margin of safety.
5. Clustered open-market insider purchases by three or more executives or directors.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. Net charge-offs above 0.50% annualized in any quarter, or 90+ day accruing delinquencies above $40M.
2. A 2UniFi impairment charge, or disclosure that additional capital beyond the ~$22M annual run-rate is required without an executed partner.
3. Core conversion delayed beyond Q4 2026, or a fourth Item 5.02 senior departure tied to the Texas franchise.
4. Disclosure that Cambr-sourced deposits held at NBH Bank exceed 15% of total deposits, converting an unquantified item into a named funding concentration.

## Conclusion

NBHC solved its most pressing problem — a legacy franchise that could not grow loans — by paying roughly 1.50x tangible book for Vista Bank, diluting tangible book value per share about 7% and spending 260 basis points of CET1. The growth is real: a $670M year-over-year swing in net loan flow is not an accounting artifact. But the earnings that support the current 1.65x tangible book multiple depend on three items that will not repeat at the same magnitude: purchase-discount accretion holding reported margin near 4% while originated yields fell 44bp, provision expense running at 0.11% annualized against 0.30% charge-offs under a newly adopted standard that routed the acquisition allowance around the income statement, and a FY2025 fee base that included $7.0M of investment marks, one of which reversed on sale.

Against that, four analysts cut every forward estimate in the past thirty days, 90+ day accruing delinquencies nearly doubled to $29.1M with food-and-agribusiness at 5.7% of its loan class, the acquired bank's CEO left seven months after closing and before the core conversion, the Durbin interchange exemption disappears around July 1, 2027 against an already-shrinking fee line, and 2UniFi consumes ~$22M a year with explicit impairment language and no executed partner. Management's own compensation-actually-paid decline of 47% and the cancellation of prior-cycle PSUs indicate the board is not insulating executives from the five-year TSR shortfall against the KBW Regional index, which is a point in its favor, but the discretionary merger bonuses and sole-discretion integration vesting criterion pull the other way, as does repurchasing stock at 1.55x tangible book while issuing at 1.42x.

The overall score falls to 4.8 from 5.7, continuing a declining trajectory across three reviews, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend. The base case of $44 returns the 2.95% dividend and little more; the bear case is a 22% loss. We move to REDUCE. The re-entry price is nearer $37 — approximately 1.40x tangible book — or the evidence at the October print that core margin and credit have stabilized and the conversion cost saves are in the run-rate.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the valuation table's "normalized provision" line was labeled "$2.68" but the same paragraph derives H1 adjusted EPS "nearer $1.34" (i.e., ~$2.68 annualized); this is internally consistent and unchanged, but note the base-case FY2027 EPS bridge ($3.93 − ~$0.18 Durbin/accretion = ~$3.75, not $3.60) does not fully reconcile — the ~$3.60 figure implies additional unstated haircuts, so the +1.5% base-case price return rests on an under-documented earnings estimate.
