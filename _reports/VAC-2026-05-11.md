## Executive Summary

Marriott Vacations Worldwide is the largest publicly traded vacation ownership company globally, operating 120 resorts under Marriott, Sheraton, Westin, Ritz-Carlton, and Hyatt brands. The stock has partially recovered from its November 2025 collapse (which took shares from $67 to $45 on massive impairment charges) and now trades at $75 — roughly midway between its 52-week low of $44.58 and high of $86.33.

The investment case is a turnaround story. New CEO Matthew Avril and COO Michael Flaskey (both Diamond Resorts veterans) took over in February 2026 after a "kitchen sink" FY2025 that included $577M in impairments, $122M in modernization costs, and a GAAP net loss of $308M. Adjusted EBITDA of $751M was actually up 2% YoY, and the company is guiding 3-7% contract sales growth for 2026. At ~7.7x EV/EBITDA, the stock is discounted relative to peers if the turnaround thesis materializes. But with an Altman Z-Score of 0.92, S&P rating at B+ (negative outlook), $3.6B in corporate debt, negative free cash flow, and a Q1 2026 EPS miss of 25.5%, the leverage and execution risk are substantial.

The core question is whether Avril/Flaskey can stabilize operations and deleverage over 18-24 months while macro conditions actively work against a consumer discretionary business with 57% financing penetration. The risk/reward at $75 does not offer the margin of safety required for conviction in either direction.

## Company Overview

VAC earns revenue through four channels: vacation ownership interest (VOI) sales ($1.46B), resort management and services ($633M), consumer financing ($360M), and rental of inventory ($615M). The Vacation Ownership segment generates 96% of revenue; the Exchange & Third-Party Management segment (Interval International, Aqua-Aston) contributes the remainder.

The business model depends on marketing timeshare products to Marriott Bonvoy's 271M members and World of Hyatt's 63M members via exclusive long-term brand licenses extending through 2090-2095. Approximately 70% of sales come from existing owner upgrades, creating a recurring sales pipeline with lower customer acquisition costs than new-buyer channels.

VAC finances ~57% of its sales to consumers, creating a lending business that generates $360M in annual revenue but also exposes the company to consumer credit risk. The financing portfolio is securitized through the ABS market ($2.17B outstanding), with $10.7B issued historically.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Deterioration:**
Revenue grew marginally to $4.67B in FY2025 (+1% YoY), but the bottom line swung from $218M in net income to a $308M net loss. This loss was driven by $577M in impairment charges and $122M in modernization costs — both largely non-recurring. Adjusted EBITDA of $751M (up from $736M) demonstrates that the core business still generates positive economics.

**Balance Sheet Stress:**
Total corporate debt stands at $3.6B, plus $2.17B in non-recourse securitized debt. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA is 4.2x — elevated for a consumer cyclical business. S&P forecasts leverage remaining above 5.5x through end of 2026 (using their methodology, which includes certain adjustments). The debt/equity ratio of 3.9x and Altman Z-Score of 0.92 place the company firmly in the financial distress zone.

**Cash Flow Collapse:**
Operating cash flow fell to $28M in FY2025 versus a typical run-rate of $400M+. The primary drags: inventory spending exceeded cost of sales by $115M, and notes receivable originations exceeded collections by $351M. This reflects the capital-intensive nature of the business model during growth periods — money going out the door to finance new sales before it comes back through securitization.

**Liquidity Position:**
The company has $787M available on its $800M revolving credit facility (extended to 2030) and successfully issued $575M in 6.5% Senior Notes due 2033. Near-term maturities are manageable: the 2026 convertible notes were already repaid. However, $727M in debt payments are due within one year, and $1.19B within 1-3 years.

**Q1 2026 Performance:**
Revenue grew 4.8% YoY to $1.26B, but EPS of $1.24 missed consensus ($1.66) by 25.5%. This was the first miss after three consecutive beats, raising questions about whether the recovery is on track or whether operational issues are more persistent than the kitchen-sink thesis suggests.

**Consumer Credit Metrics (Improving):**
Average FICO scores increased to 740 (from 737 in 2024, 735 in 2023). Delinquency rates declined ~100 bps YoY. Management is holding reserves elevated pending "sustained evidence" of continued improvement — this creates potential for positive reserve releases that would boost revenue in coming quarters.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Growth Path:**
Management guides 3-7% contract sales growth for 2026. This is modest but represents stabilization after FY2025's 3% decline. VPG (volume per guest, the key productivity metric) declined 3% in FY2025 to $3,794 — a concerning trend that reflects either weaker consumer demand or lower-quality traffic.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current-year (FY2026) EPS consensus is $7.48, up 9.1% over 90 days but down 3.4% over 30 days — reflecting the Q1 miss pulling near-term estimates lower while the full-year turnaround thesis remains intact. Next-year consensus of $8.42 is up 15% over 90 days. These are positive revision trends but not at the velocity that would constitute a strong BUY signal.

**Turnaround Levers:**
1. $250-300M in non-core asset dispositions over 2 years (Cancun hotel for $50M already executed)
2. Outsourced HR and finance functions (~$20M annual savings)
3. Asia Pacific pullback eliminating money-losing expansion
4. Potential reserve releases from improving consumer credit
5. Exchange business could be a divestiture candidate ($91M EBITDA, declining)

**Headwinds:**
Consumer discretionary spending is under pressure broadly. With elevated rates and softening consumer confidence, the vacation ownership buyer pool faces headwinds. The Q1 miss suggests these headwinds are already manifesting in operating results.

## Valuation Assessment

At $75/share (~$2.6B market cap), with approximately $3.2B in net corporate debt, the enterprise value is ~$5.8B. Against $751M in Adjusted EBITDA, this yields an EV/EBITDA multiple of ~7.7x. On forward consensus EPS of $7.48, the stock trades at ~10.0x earnings.

**Peer Comparison:**
- Hilton Grand Vacations (HGV): 26.4x P/E
- Travel + Leisure Co. (TNL): 18.4x P/E
- VAC: ~10.0x forward P/E

The discount to peers (40-60%) partially reflects VAC's significantly higher leverage and weaker execution track record (cumulative TSR of 51% vs. peer group at 218%). The valuation is cheap on an absolute basis, but the discount exists for identifiable reasons: B+ credit rating with negative outlook, recent EPS miss, negative GAAP earnings, and executive turnover.

**Why the market could be wrong:** If the turnaround produces normalized earnings of $8-9/share (consistent with FY2027 consensus of $8.42) and the multiple re-rates to 12-14x (still a significant discount to TNL at 18.4x), fair value is $100-125. This is roughly what the analyst high target ($105) implies.

**Why the market might be right:** If macro conditions deteriorate further, consumer credit quality reverses, or the operational turnaround stalls, the leverage amplifies downside. At 4.2x net debt/EBITDA, a 10-15% EBITDA decline would push leverage above 5x and potentially trigger rating downgrades that increase borrowing costs further. The spiral risk is real.

The ~4.1% dividend yield provides some downside cushion, but dividend coverage on GAAP earnings is negative — it's covered by adjusted metrics and available liquidity, not reported profits.

## Competitive Landscape

VAC's competitive position rests on exclusive access to Marriott International's 271M Bonvoy members and Hyatt's 63M World of Hyatt members — the two largest upper-upscale/luxury loyalty ecosystems in hospitality. These license agreements, running through 2090-2095, create a durable marketing advantage that competitors cannot replicate.

However, the position is weakening on several dimensions:
- VPG declining 3% annually indicates difficulty converting tours to sales
- The Hyatt/Legacy-Welk integration has failed to achieve projected returns ($103M+ in impairments)
- The Exchange business (Interval International) is structurally declining as large developers internalize exchange functions
- Alternative lodging platforms (Airbnb, VRBO) compete for the leisure travel wallet

Against direct peers, VAC maintains its scale advantage (#1 market share at ~17% of U.S. timeshare sales) but has dramatically underperformed operationally. TNL and HGV have generated superior shareholder returns over the last several years, suggesting VAC's execution issues are company-specific.

The competitive moat from brand licenses remains intact. Whether management can convert that structural advantage into better operating results depends on the turnaround execution discussed above.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Leverage in a rising-rate environment:** If EBITDA disappoints or asset sales are delayed, further downgrades increase borrowing costs on the $3.6B corporate debt stack.

2. **Consumer credit deterioration:** 57% financing penetration creates direct exposure to consumer defaults. While current credit metrics are improving, the macro environment could reverse this trend over 6-12 months.

3. **Execution risk on turnaround:** New management has been in place for only ~3 months. The Q1 miss raises questions about whether they can deliver on guided growth while simultaneously executing cost restructuring, asset dispositions, and outsourcing transitions.

4. **Macro sensitivity:** Timeshare purchases are highly discretionary. A consumer recession would directly impair VPG, tour flow, and contract sales volume simultaneously.

5. **Short interest at 10.3% of float (4.5 days to cover):** Not extreme enough to create squeeze risk but signals informed skepticism about the turnaround.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 55.6% is approximately at historical volatility (54.8%), with the term structure in backwardation — the market is pricing near-term event risk above longer-term uncertainty. Put skew of 5.7% in the near-term expiry confirms modest elevated demand for downside protection. The 3-month implied move of 12.2% (±$9) suggests the market sees the stock potentially retesting the mid-$60s on the downside or reaching the mid-$80s on the upside by June expiry. This is consistent with a stock in transition where direction remains genuinely uncertain.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-105, +33-40%):**
Avril/Flaskey execute the turnaround playbook. Contract sales grow 5-7% in 2026. Asset dispositions yield $250-300M in proceeds, reducing net debt/EBITDA toward 3.8-4.0x. Consumer credit metrics continue improving, enabling reserve releases. The stock re-rates from 10x to 12-13x forward earnings on improved confidence. Flaskey's $1M open-market purchase and Diamond Resorts track record provide credibility.

**Bear Case ($45-55, -27-40%):**
Consumer spending deterioration accelerates. VPG declines further, contract sales miss the low end of guidance. ABS market tightens, increasing cost of financing. S&P downgrades to B or below. Dividend cut becomes necessary to preserve liquidity. The stock retests November 2025 lows.

**Base Case ($75-85, ±13%):**
The turnaround proceeds slowly. Some quarters beat, some miss. Leverage remains elevated but manageable. The stock oscillates in a wide range as the market waits for more evidence. Current consensus mean target of $83.70 reflects this middling outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats with VPG stabilization/improvement
2. Confirmed asset disposition proceeds exceeding $150M (demonstrating deleveraging path)
3. S&P outlook revision from negative to stable, or any credit upgrade signal

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. EBITDA declining below $700M in any quarter (annualized) — indicating operational deterioration, not just one-time factors
2. ABS market access restricted (failed securitization or significantly higher required spreads)
3. Dividend cut announced
4. Net leverage exceeding 5.0x corporate net debt/EBITDA

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will provide the next material data point on whether the Q1 miss was an aberration or trend.

## Conclusion

VAC presents a classic turnaround situation with legitimate upside if execution materializes, but the balance of risks at current levels does not support conviction in either direction. The new management team has credibility (Flaskey's $1M purchase is the strongest insider signal possible), the valuation is discounted versus peers, and the brand licenses provide a durable structural advantage. Against this: 4.2x leverage with a negative credit outlook, a 25.5% EPS miss, negative GAAP earnings, macro headwinds against consumer discretionary spending, and only ~3 months of operating history under new leadership.

This is a binary situation where the turnaround either works (stock goes to $100+) or it doesn't (stock retests $50). At $75, the market is pricing roughly 50/50 odds, which aligns with the available evidence. Accumulating a position here requires either stronger fundamental evidence of turnaround execution (Q2 beat, confirmed asset sales, credit metric improvement) or a significantly lower entry price (~$60-65) that provides adequate margin of safety for the leverage risk. Neither condition is met today.

HOLD with reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings.
