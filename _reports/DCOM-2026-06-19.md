**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dime is in the middle of a deliberate balance-sheet transformation from a legacy thrift/multifamily lender into a relationship-driven commercial bank serving the New York metro market. The strategy, accelerated after the 2023 failures of Signature Bank and First Republic allowed Dime to recruit displaced commercial bankers and deposit relationships, is producing measurable results: net interest margin expanded to 3.21% in Q1 CY2026 from 2.95% a year earlier and roughly 2.48% in 2024; the efficiency ratio improved to 50.8% from 63.1%; and business loans (C&I plus owner-occupied CRE) reached $3.36B, with originations of $170.3M in Q1'26 versus $42.6M a year earlier. Net income recovered from a $29.1M trough in 2024 to $110.7M in 2025. The apparent rebrand from "Dime Community Bancshares" to "Dime Commercial Bancshares" and KBRA's "Positive" ratings outlook on June 16, 2026 both reinforce that the transformation is real and externally validated.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the easiest part of the re-rating has been captured. The stock has risen 49% over twelve months, from a $25.63 low to roughly $39, and now trades at ~1.24x book value and ~14.6x trailing earnings, in line with the regional-bank peer median P/E of 14.2x. The forward picture has two specific headwinds the market is reckoning with. First, estimate revisions are uniformly negative (current-year consensus down 2.9% over 90 days, current quarter down 4.1% over 90 days), and Dime missed Q1 consensus by ~5.6%. Second, the multifamily book — where ~$1.08B of NYC collateral sits under rent-regulation constraints — produced an $8.2M charge-off on a $46.2M loan moved to held-for-sale, pushing the annualized multifamily net charge-off rate to 0.97% from near zero a year earlier. Asset sensitivity means a Fed cutting cycle is a direct NIM headwind (-200bp = -5.1% NII year-one).

The hold thesis rests on a credible, specific growth path: PPNR continues to build as the commercial deposit/lending franchise scales and the funding mix improves, supported by a clean capital position (Bank CET1 14.6%) and management's proactive clearing of problem CRE. The risk is that NYC multifamily credit deterioration outpaces the commercial-banking ramp, or that rate cuts compress the hard-won margin before the franchise transition is complete. On balance there is a positive 12-month earnings-growth case worth owning, but not enough valuation asymmetry or estimate momentum to justify a BUY at the current price.

## Company Overview

Dime Community Bancshares is the holding company for Dime Community Bank, founded 1910 and headquartered in Hauppauge, NY. It operates 62 branches across Long Island, the five NYC boroughs, Westchester County, and New Jersey, reporting as a single "Community Banking" segment. Total assets are approximately $15.0B, book value per share is $31.33, and shares outstanding are roughly 44.1M.

The defining strategic story is the conversion of a concentration-heavy, low-margin multifamily real-estate balance sheet into a higher-margin, relationship-driven commercial bank. Following the 2021 Bridge Bancorp merger and the 2023 regional-bank turmoil, Dime hired commercial deposit and lending teams (notably from the failed Signature Bank) and built out a "Private Bank"/commercial banking franchise. The evidence is concrete:

- Business loans are 32% of loans and are now the largest and fastest-growing category.
- Multifamily balances are deliberately shrinking, from $3.42B to $3.25B quarter-over-quarter.
- The apparent rebrand to "Dime Commercial Bancshares" (name appears in June 2026 filings; a May 28, 2026 8-K carried an Item 5.03 bylaw amendment) symbolically cements the commercial-bank identity.

The mechanism behind the strategy: replacing low-yielding multifamily mortgages funded by rate-sensitive deposits with higher-yielding C&I credit funded by stickier commercial operating deposits. If executed, this both lifts the margin and reduces the cyclicality of the deposit base, the two structural weaknesses that defined the legacy thrift model.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin recovery off a 2024 trough.** Q1'26 net income was $34.6M (diluted EPS $0.75) versus $21.5M (EPS $0.45) in Q1'25, though the prior-year quarter was depressed by a one-time $7.2M pension-settlement loss, a caveat that should be applied before extrapolating the headline YoY growth rate.

**Net interest margin is the core driver.** The expansion came as high-cost deposits repriced down (money-market cost fell 66bp, savings 58bp) while asset yields held. Net interest income rose roughly $18M YoY to $112.3M. ROA reached 0.92%, and ROE 9.20%.

**Liquidity and balance-sheet posture are conservative.** The loan-to-deposit ratio fell to 84.2% from 93.6%, with $2.06B in cash, a de-risked, liquid posture. Deposits declined $242M in Q1'26 (seasonal and managed runoff of higher-cost balances), and the bank paid down FHLB advances and redeemed $40M of sub debt (booking a $1.5M gain). The deposit decline bears watching: the entire margin recovery depends on rebuilding low-cost commercial deposits, and a sustained outflow of those balances would reverse the NIM gains.

**Capital is solid.** Bank CET1 stands at 14.6%, total RBC 15.6%; holding-company total RBC 16.2%, Tier 1 leverage 9.2% — comfortably well-capitalized. The company pays a $0.25/quarter common dividend (33% payout, ~2.5% yield), 5.50% Series A preferred, and 9.00% sub notes. 1.57M shares remain under buyback authorization but no repurchases were executed in Q1'26, indicating capital is being conserved for growth deployment rather than returned.

**Asset quality is the swing factor.** Provision rose to $12.3M from $9.6M. ACL/loans is 0.95%; NPLs are 0.54% of loans. Dime moved a $46.2M multifamily loan to held-for-sale and took an $8.2M charge-off, pushing the annualized multifamily net charge-off rate to 0.97%. NPLs rose to $57M, and 30–59 day delinquencies jumped to $72.4M from $28.8M. Management characterizes early-bucket delinquencies as noisy, but the trajectory is the right thing to monitor.

**Earnings-quality observation.** A HTM securities portfolio carries roughly $63M of unrecognized losses (fair value $585M versus $648M cost) — unrealized, but a tangible-book overhang should the bank ever need that liquidity. Given the $2.06B cash position, this is not a near-term concern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is specific and grounded in the commercial-banking ramp, not generic rate-cycle beta. The near four-fold increase in C&I plus owner-occupied CRE originations demonstrates the franchise is winning new commercial relationships at scale. As these higher-yielding loans displace runoff multifamily balances, the asset yield mix improves structurally, independent of where the Fed funds rate settles.

That said, near-term earnings momentum has turned negative, and this must be weighted honestly. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon: current quarter down 3.7% over 30 days and 4.1% over 90 days; current year ($3.37) down 2.9% over 90 days; next year ($4.36) down 0.9% over 90 days. The four-quarter beat/miss record is mixed (one beat, one in-line, two misses). This is a recovery story whose consensus expectations have modestly drifted lower, largely on the elevated provision and charge-off activity.

The growth that justifies holding is PPNR durability: net interest income up $18M YoY, efficiency at ~51%, and a still-mid-transition commercial franchise that has runway to grow C&I as a share of the book. Next-year consensus of $4.36 implies roughly 29% EPS growth off 2026's ~$3.37, which would re-rate the stock if delivered. The risk is that this consensus itself proves optimistic if rate cuts compress NIM (asset-sensitive: -200bp = -5.1% NII year-one) or if multifamily credit costs rise.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39, Dime trades at ~14.6x trailing earnings and ~1.24x book value of $31.33. The regional-bank peer set shows a median P/E of 14.2x (STBA 13.2x, CASH 9.9x, HOPE 23.8x, TFIN 63.1x, CHCO 14.2x) and a median dividend yield of 2.7% versus Dime's 2.5%. Dime sits right at the peer median on earnings and slightly below on yield. There is no material discount to exploit.

The valuation question is whether the market is mispricing the forward earnings power. Analyst price targets range from $39 (low, equal to current price) to $46 (high), with a $43 mean — implying ~10% upside to the mean and ~18% to the high. With estimates falling rather than rising, the consensus targets are appropriately calibrated to a recovery that has substantially played out. The stock's 49% twelve-month gain captured the bulk of the NIM-recovery re-rating from sub-2.5% to 3.21%.

A low multiple alone is not a buy signal here, and a cheap-looking P/E in a sector facing CRE credit normalization carries value-trap risk. Dime is reasonably priced for a bank in mid-transition with above-average geographic concentration risk. The forward P/E of ~14.6x against next-year consensus growth of ~29% gives a PEG well below 1.0 if that growth is delivered, but the negative estimate revisions and the asset-sensitive headwind to NIM in a cutting cycle are why the market is not paying up for that growth. We assign a neutral valuation score: not stretched, not offering the asymmetric upside that would warrant a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Dime competes in the intensely contested NY metro market against money-center banks, larger regionals, and community banks for both deposits and CRE/C&I lending. Its competitive trajectory is improving: the post-2023 strategy explicitly capitalizes on the disruption from Signature and First Republic failures, recruiting displaced commercial bankers and deposit relationships. The NIM and efficiency-ratio gains indicate the relationship-banking model is gaining traction.

The moat is modest and geographic. Dime's advantage is local density and the recruited Signature talent, not a structural cost or technology edge. The franchise is still mid-transition: business loans are growing fast, but multifamily plus non-owner-occupied CRE still represent 57% of loans (~$6.1B), a concentration that peers in more diversified geographies do not carry. The competitive position is improving on trajectory (share gains in commercial deposits and C&I), but the level remains that of a regional player exposed to a single, currently stressed CRE cycle.

KBRA's "Positive" ratings outlook is a credit-positive external endorsement of the transformation and capital build, lowering funding-cost risk at the margin and validating the strategic direction.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE/multifamily concentration is the dominant risk.** Multifamily plus non-owner-occupied CRE equals 57% of loans (~$6.1B), historically above regulatory supervisory concentration guidance, requiring heightened risk management. The charge-off and the jump in 30–59 day delinquencies show the stress is real and surfacing now.

**2. NYC rent-regulation and political risk — genuinely company-specific.** Roughly $2.60B of multifamily is in NYC, of which ~$1.08B is rent-regulated. The 2019 HSTPA law constrains landlords' ability to raise rents on regulated units, suppressing collateral values and refinance capacity. The mechanism: rent caps limit net operating income → depressed property valuations → impaired refinance capacity at maturity → elevated default and loss-given-default on this book. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags the tenant-friendly NYC political environment as a forward risk. This is the key non-rate variable for the multifamily portfolio and is largely outside management's control.

**3. Rate sensitivity cuts against the franchise.** Dime is asset-sensitive: +200bp benefits NII +9.6% in year one, but -200bp reduces NII -5.1% and EVE -19.4%. A Fed cutting cycle is a direct headwind to the margin recovery that has driven the stock.

**4. Deposit competition and funding mix.** The franchise is still rebuilding low-cost commercial deposits, and the entire NIM gain depends on sustaining that mix shift.

**5. Investor office exposure** of $527M (average LTV 59%) — modest but a sector pressure point.

**6. HTM securities overhang** of ~$63M unrecognized losses — unrealized, immaterial given liquidity, but a tangible-book consideration in a stress scenario.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 38.5% is rich versus 30.6% one-year historical volatility (a 7.8-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 38.5% > longer-dated 35.6%), indicating the market is pricing some imminent uncertainty consistent with the credit-cost trajectory and the next earnings print. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-5.3%) and the put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.50, so options positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the elevated 10.0% short interest. The reported jump in ATM IV to an implausible 869.9% in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded. Net read: the options market is pricing event risk around the next quarter but is not signaling a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$46, +18%)
The commercial-banking transformation continues to compound: C&I originations sustain their pace, business loans grow past 35-40% of the book, and commercial operating deposits rebuild the low-cost funding base. NIM holds above 3.2% even through modest rate cuts because the asset-mix shift toward higher-yielding C&I offsets the asset-sensitivity drag. Multifamily credit stress stays contained at sub-1% net charge-offs, the held-for-sale loan sale proves to be proactive clearing rather than the leading edge of a wave, and next-year EPS of ~$4.36 is delivered. At ~$4.36 EPS and a peer-consistent ~14x multiple plus a re-rating toward 1.4x book on a cleaner, higher-ROE franchise, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case (~$30, -23%)
NYC rent-regulated multifamily credit deteriorates faster than reserves anticipate; the 30–59 day delinquency surge proves to be a leading indicator, and net charge-offs climb past 1.5% on the multifamily book. The Fed cuts 100-200bp, compressing NIM toward 2.9% on the asset-sensitive book before the C&I mix shift can offset it. Estimates continue their downward drift, EPS lands closer to $3.00 than $3.37, and the stock de-rates back toward ~1.0x book (~$31) or below as the market reprices a NY-metro CRE lender in a cutting cycle.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is PPNR growth from the scaling commercial franchise, supported by a clean capital position and validated by KBRA's positive outlook, offset by negative estimate momentum and surfacing multifamily credit stress that cap the near-term upside.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 CY2026, late July) — the key data points are NIM trajectory, deposit trend, and multifamily net charge-offs/delinquency migration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Multifamily net charge-offs stabilize below 0.50% annualized and 30–59 day delinquencies revert toward the prior ~$30M range, removing the credit overhang.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus rising rather than falling) alongside continued C&I origination growth above $150M/quarter.
- A pullback toward ~$33-34 (~1.05x book) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring valuation asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Multifamily net charge-offs accelerate above 1.5% annualized or NPLs climb past $80M, signaling the held-for-sale loan was the leading edge of a credit wave.
- A sustained deposit outflow that forces reliance on wholesale funding and compresses NIM below 3.0%.
- Next-year EPS consensus cut below $3.75, indicating the growth runway is shrinking.

## Conclusion

Dime is executing a credible and externally validated transformation from a thrift/multifamily lender into a commercial bank, and the financial evidence — NIM at 3.21%, efficiency at ~51%, four-fold C&I origination growth, and the recovery in annual net income — supports owning the equity for the continued PPNR build. The growth path is specific enough to justify a hold on a 12-month horizon: next-year consensus of $4.36 implies meaningful earnings growth if the commercial franchise scales as planned and credit stays contained.

The reasons it is not a BUY are equally specific. The stock has captured most of the NIM-recovery re-rating in its 49% twelve-month run, now trades at the peer-median multiple and ~1.24x book with no valuation discount to exploit, estimates are drifting lower across every horizon, and the multifamily book — particularly the ~$1.08B of NYC rent-regulated collateral — is where credit stress is now visibly surfacing. Insiders, including the CEO and a large institutional holder, have been steady net sellers near current levels with no open-market buying, consistent with a stock seen as fairly valued after the rally. Asset sensitivity means a Fed cutting cycle works against the margin. We rate DCOM a HOLD, with a clear path to a BUY upgrade if multifamily credit stabilizes and a more attractive entry emerges, or a downgrade if delinquency migration accelerates.
