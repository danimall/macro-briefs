**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CME Group is the world's largest and most diversified derivatives exchange and central counterparty clearinghouse, operating CME, CBOT, NYMEX and COMEX across six asset classes plus the BrokerTec and EBS cash venues. The franchise is among the highest-quality in the public market: 64.9% FY2025 operating margin, ~$4.2B free cash flow on roughly $84M of capex, AA-/Aa3 ratings, and only $3.4B of corporate debt. Q1 2026 was a record — revenue $1,880M (+14% YoY), operating margin 69.7%, diluted EPS $3.18 (+21%), and aggregate ADV of 36.2M contracts (+22%). On the headline numbers this looks like a clear buy at a discount to its early-March high near $329.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and testable. The Q1 volume surge was concentrated in the asset classes most exposed to the Middle East conflict — Metals ADV +130% (gold +133%), Energy +37% (WTI +69%, Brent +91%) — and that volatility regime is a key swing factor to monitor. Consensus EPS estimates are roughly flat (current year +0.2% over 30 days, +1.5% over 90 days), so there is no earnings-inflection signal to convert the cheap multiple into a re-rating. A meaningful share of CME profit comes from the net spread on ~$144B of reinvested clearing collateral, and Fed easing (reinvestment rates already declining) compresses that stream. The IV collapse from 59.4% to 29.4% over the past week is the market's own confirmation that the volatility-driven earnings premium is deflating.

CME is cheap relative to peers (~20x forward versus a 26.2x cohort median), carries a meaningful total cash yield (regular plus variable dividend), and owns genuine under-modeled optionality in CMESC Treasury/repo clearing. Those features anchor a hold and remove any AVOID case. But with current consensus estimates at downside revision risk if H2 volumes normalize, with float income facing a rate-cut headwind, and with a credible new competitor (FMX) attacking the highest-margin rates franchise, the asymmetry does not yet support a BUY. We rate CME HOLD with an income-and-quality orientation, and would upgrade on either confirmation that volumes are holding above pre-conflict levels or a CMESC ramp that the Street is not modeling.

## Company Overview

CME operates a vertically integrated execution-clearing-settlement model in which the clearinghouse is the counterparty to every trade. This structure is the core of the moat: deep open-interest pools create liquidity that begets liquidity, and the clearinghouse's portfolio and cross-margining (with FICC and OCC) generated roughly $72B of average daily margin savings in 2025, a capital-efficiency benefit a competitor cannot replicate without an equivalent open-interest pool. CME holds exclusive long-dated index licenses (S&P 500 via the S&P Dow Jones Indices JV, Nasdaq through 2039, Russell through 2037) and administers CME Term SOFR, the only ARRC-endorsed term SOFR rate. Its "trading products" intangibles are carried at $17.2B with no term limit, reflecting perpetual CFTC authorizations.

The company is effectively two businesses in one income statement. The first is an asset-light, near-monopoly transaction and clearing franchise running 65% operating margins. The second is a large, rate-sensitive float business: CME reinvests roughly $144B of member collateral held at the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, earning a spread (FY2025: $5,254M earned, $4,843M rebated to clearing firms; Q1 2026: $1,371M earned, $1,249M distributed). The retained net spread is a meaningful, rate-sensitive contributor to profit that should not be treated as recurring volume-driven quality.

Capital return is disciplined and substantial. CME targets 50–60% of prior-year cash earnings via a regular quarterly dividend plus a large annual variable dividend. Buybacks ran $536M in Q1 2026 at ~$291.58 average, with $2.2B remaining on a $3.0B authorization. At the current ~$248, the repurchase price discipline favors shareholders, though the Q1 buyback at $291 was above today's level.

## Growth Analysis

Structural growth is real but moderate. Normalized organic revenue growth runs mid-single-digit, supported by secular derivatives adoption, micro-contract expansion of the retail base, and a persistently elevated macro-volatility regime. On top of that base sits genuine optionality:

- **CMESC (Treasury/repo securities clearing):** CME Securities Clearing received SEC approval as a clearing agency in December 2025 and plans to launch in 2026 ahead of SEC-mandated Treasury clearing (Dec 31, 2026) and repo clearing (Jun 30, 2027). This directly challenges DTCC/FICC's near-monopoly and carries natural cross-margining synergy with CME's rate complex. It is rarely modeled into estimates and is the cleanest upgrade catalyst.
- **FanDuel prediction-markets JV (Dec 2025):** retail event contracts on financial benchmarks and sports, with 24/7 crypto trading launching Q2 2026. Smaller and legally contested, but optional upside.

The constraint on the growth score is that the near-term reported growth is cyclical, not structural. Q1's +21% EPS leaned on a volatility spike, and consensus estimates do not corroborate continued acceleration: current-year EPS sits at $12.28 (revised +0.2% over 30 days, +1.5% over 90 days) and next year at $12.92 (+0.3%/+1.6%). These are stable-to-modestly-rising revisions, not the velocity that would signal an earnings inflection. The honest read is mid-single-digit forward growth with embedded downside risk to current estimates if H2 metals/energy volumes revert toward FY2025 levels. CMESC could shift this, but it is unmodeled and unproven.

Two things keep the valuation score at 6.5 rather than higher. First, the peer discount is partly deserved: CME's headline growth is more volatility-cyclical than MCO/SPGI/MSCI's recurring subscription/index revenue, and a falling-volatility, falling-rate environment pressures both the volume and float engines. Second, a cheap multiple without an estimate-revision catalyst tends to stay cheap; the discount converts to outperformance only if H2 volumes hold (validating current estimates) or CMESC scales. The drawdown has removed meaningful froth, but at the current level the multiple reflects a market that is repricing the volume tailwind as transient, not mispricing the franchise. The most likely outcome is range-bound trading with downside support from the total cash yield and upside contingent on a volume or CMESC catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

CME's moat in listed derivatives is wide and, in its core complexes, durable. The vertically integrated clearing model, the margin savings, exclusive index licenses, and SOFR term-rate administration are structural advantages. CMESC extends CME into FICC's Treasury-clearing turf, a net positive that turns a defensive franchise into an offensive one.

The trajectory risk is FMX, the BGC-backed Futures Exchange supported by a consortium including Goldman, Morgan Stanley and Citadel Securities, explicitly targeting CME's SOFR/Treasury rates stronghold with LCH cross-margining. The bank backing makes FMX the most credible challenger to the rates franchise in a decade. So far it has not dented CME — interest-rate ADV grew 24% in Q1 2026 — but that growth may be masked by the volatility tailwind. Rates is CME's largest and highest-margin complex (~$521M of Q1 clearing fees, ~34% of contract volume), so any traction by FMX attacks the crown jewel. This is the single competitive thread to monitor quarterly. A separate, lower-probability structural threat is the long-standing push by large clearing members to have clearinghouses operated as not-for-profit utilities, which would undermine the for-profit vertical model.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter here are concrete and partly two-sided:

1. **Volatility normalization (the swing factor).** A normalization of Middle East–driven volatility would reverse the volume premium and put current FY consensus ($12.28) at downside revision risk. Falsifiable: track Q2/Q3 metals and energy ADV against FY2025 levels.
2. **Float-income compression.** The average reinvestment rate is already declining even as balances grew, and the Fed easing cycle compresses this stream. Falsifiable: watch the net collateral-reinvestment income line.
3. **FMX competition in rates** (see Competitive Landscape) — the highest-value franchise, contested for the first time by a well-capitalized entrant.
4. **Operational/single-point-of-failure risk.** The November 27, 2025 CyrusOne data-center cooling failure forced CME to halt markets. It coincides with the most sensitive phase of the Google Cloud migration (clearing apps and markets moving to a new private cloud region). Management calls the outage immaterial but warns future events may not be.
5. **Concentration.** One clearing firm = 12% of FY2025 clearing & transaction fees; two resellers = ~26–30% of market-data revenue.
6. **Governance.** Class B members elect six directors, and nine board members own or are affiliated with firms owning trading rights — a structural conflict between member trading-fee protection and Class A shareholder returns, unusual at this scale.
7. **Regulatory/tax overhangs:** financial-transaction-tax proposals, potential repeal of favorable 60/40 futures tax treatment, and prediction-markets legal uncertainty (FanDuel JV litigation).

Mitigants: the Class B class-action overhang cleared with a unanimous July 2025 jury verdict for CME, and the OSTTRA JV sale ($306.1M gain) cleaned up the portfolio.

### Options Market Signal

The most informative recent options datapoint is the IV collapse: near-term ATM IV fell from 59.4% to 29.4% over the past week as the price declined 7.6%, and the term structure is flat (near 29.4% ≈ far 29.1%). This is consistent with the market resolving a volatility-event premium. IV remains rich versus 1-year HV of 21.6% (a 7.9pp spread), and there is mild elevated put demand (3.9% one-week skew, P/C OI 0.70), but nothing that signals imminent distress. The signal reinforces the fundamental read: the volatility regime that drove the record quarter is deflating, which is why the stock and the multiple are repricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($310–353):** Macro volatility stays structurally elevated (Fed-path uncertainty, tariffs, residual geopolitical risk, AI-driven equity swings), keeping ADV near the Q1 36.2M run-rate and validating current estimates. CMESC launches into the 2026–2027 Treasury/repo clearing mandate and takes share from FICC, adding an unmodeled growth leg. The multiple re-rates toward the 26.2x cohort median on ~$13 forward EPS. FMX fails to dent rates, as it has so far. Total cash yield of ~4.6% pays investors to wait. Target $310–353, roughly the consensus mean-to-high.

**Bear case ($230):** Metals and energy volatility normalize, and H2 ADV reverts toward FY2025's 28.1M, forcing downward estimate revisions. Fed cuts compress float income. FMX begins taking measurable SOFR/Treasury futures share. The micro-contract mix continues eroding rate-per-contract. The multiple stays at ~19–20x on flat-to-lower EPS, with the analyst low of $230 as the floor (~7% below current).

**Base case ($265):** Volumes settle above pre-conflict levels but below the Q1 peak; estimates hold roughly flat ($12.28–12.92); float income drifts lower with rates; CMESC contributes optionality without near-term numbers. The stock trades range-bound around ~20–21x forward, with the total cash yield providing support. Base target ~$265 (~22x on $12.28), about 7% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income/quality orientation). Timeframe: 12 months, with the next reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings (late July).**

The thesis is genuinely two-sided in the near term, anchored by quality and yield. The cheap multiple and ~4.6% total cash yield justify owning the franchise while the volume and float drivers play out, but flat estimates and a potentially reversing volatility regime prevent a confident appreciation call today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 ADV holds above ~32M contracts (well above FY2025's 28.1M), confirming the volume step-up is structural and validating current estimates — combined with a re-rating gap to the peer median still open.
- CMESC launches and reports early Treasury/repo clearing volumes the Street is not modeling, or current-year EPS estimates revise up >5% over 90 days.
- Forward P/E compresses below ~18x on stable estimates (price toward $220 with intact fundamentals).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 metals and energy ADV revert toward FY2025 levels and current-year consensus is cut >5%.
- FMX reports measurable SOFR/Treasury futures market share (e.g., sustained share gains in CME's rate complex with CME rate ADV declining).
- A material operational failure during the Google Cloud migration, or a quantified adverse outcome on the 60/40 tax treatment / financial-transaction tax.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) is the natural checkpoint to read H2 volume trajectory and the float-income spread against the Fed path.

## Conclusion

CME is a fortress-quality, asset-light derivatives monopoly with 65% operating margins, $4.2B of free cash flow, an AA-/Aa3 balance sheet, and the cheapest multiple in its peer cohort after a ~25% drawdown. Those facts make it a comfortable hold and rule out any AVOID. But the record Q1 was powered by a Middle East–conflict volatility spike whose durability is uncertain, consensus estimates are flat with no inflection signal, a meaningful slice of profit faces Fed-easing float compression, and FMX is attacking the highest-margin rates franchise for the first time with credible bank backing. The cheap multiple lacks the estimate-revision catalyst that would convert it into outperformance, and current estimates carry downside risk if H2 volumes normalize. We rate CME **HOLD**, supported by its ~4.6% total cash yield and franchise quality, with a base-case target of ~$265 and a clear path to upgrade on confirmation that volumes are holding or that CMESC is scaling.
