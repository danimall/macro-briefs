## Executive Summary

D.R. Horton enters the final quarter of fiscal 2026 with its earnings power roughly 39% below the FY2022 peak, its return on equity compressed from 19.9% (FY2024) to an implied ~12.4%, and its cost of demand — the mortgage rate paid by its first-time buyer — moving in the wrong direction. Nine-month pretax income fell 18.2% to $2,891.6M on revenue down 3.7%; home sales gross margin fell 170bps to 20.4%, and roughly 30bps of that decline was cushioned by a favorable litigation outcome and lower warranty costs, meaning the underlying compression was closer to 200bps. Management cut full-year guidance on July 21 to revenue of $32.5–33.0B and closings of 83,800–84,300, then Freddie Mac's 30-year average rose to 6.66% in the week of July 30 — the highest in a year. Consensus for the September quarter has been cut 9.6% in thirty days.

Against that, DHI is not a distressed or structurally impaired business. It sells at a $362,000 average price roughly 30% below the national new-home average, holds 82% mortgage capture through DHI Mortgage, has cut stick-and-brick cost per square foot 5% year-over-year, improved median cycle time about three weeks, and controls 78% of its ~570,000-lot position with specific performance provisions attaching to only $71.3M of $27.5B in remaining purchase commitments. Backlog inflected positive for the first time in this downturn, up 14% in units and 16% in value year-over-year, reversing FY2025's 11%/14% decline. Homebuilding-only debt of $3,685.3M sits against $21.3B of homebuilding inventory, with the rental, Forestar and mortgage facilities non-recourse to the parent.

The problem is the intersection: a 14.1x forward multiple on falling estimates, a slight premium to the 13.6x peer median, and consolidated leverage that rose 320bps in nine months to 23.0% of capital — above management's ~20% target — because $2.6B of shareholder returns was funded by $0.88B of operating cash flow plus roughly $2.05B of balance-sheet capacity. That timing mismatch is defensible if the Q4 cash harvest lands (FY2025's Q4 alone produced $2,471.8M of operating cash flow; the FY2026 guide needs only ~$2.1B). It is not defensible if closings land at the low end of the guide, which was set before the late-July rate move. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD and the score declines again, from 5.7 to 5.4, on price up 2.8% since the last review against estimates down and rates up.

## Company Overview

DHI has been the largest U.S. homebuilder by closings every year since 2002, operating in 126 markets across 36 states and 92 homebuilding divisions. Four segments:

**Homebuilding** (~94% of Q3 FY2026 revenue: $8,686.2M of $9,227.1M). Approximately 85% single-family detached. Q3 average closing price $362,000, down 2% year-over-year and flat sequentially. Sixty-five percent of DHI Mortgage's Q3 closings were first-time buyers.

**Financial Services** ($220.7M revenue, $70.3M pretax, 31.9% margin). DHI Mortgage originated financing for 82% of buyers, up from 81% in FY2025 and 78% in FY2024. Because the dominant sales incentive in this cycle is a mortgage rate buydown, owning the originator gives DHI direct control of the pricing and delivery of its own incentive.

**Rental** ($266.1M revenue, $31.0M pretax) with $3.0B of inventory ($2.7B multifamily, $321M single-family).

**Forestar Group** (62%-owned, consolidated; $407.0M revenue, $48.7M pretax). In Q3, $360.5M of Forestar's revenue (88.6%) and 3,370 of 3,659 lots sold (92%) went to D.R. Horton. Sixty-six percent of Forestar's owned lots are under contract with, or subject to a right of first offer to, DHI.

The competitive advantage is scale-driven cost position at the affordable price point combined with production velocity. Faster cycle times mean less capital per home and higher inventory turns, which is how DHI generates industry-leading returns on gross margins that are no longer exceptional.

**Nine months FY2026 vs. prior year:** revenue $23,672.2M vs. $24,572.6M (–3.7%); pretax income $2,891.6M vs. $3,535.1M (–18.2%), margin 12.2% vs. 14.4%; net income to DHI $2,147.6M vs. $2,679.9M (–19.9%); diluted EPS $7.45 vs. $8.53 (–12.7%) on 288.4M weighted diluted shares vs. 314.1M. **Q3 FY2026 was better than the nine-month trend.** Revenue $9,227.1M vs. $9,225.7M (flat); closings 23,983 vs. 23,160 (+3.6%); gross margin 20.7% vs. 21.8% but above the high end of DHI's own guidance; diluted EPS $3.20 vs. $3.36 (–4.8%). Consensus was $3.07 per the surprise table (Zacks $2.99 per external reporting), so a beat of 4.4–7% depending on the consensus source. DHI reports no material non-GAAP earnings adjustments, so the comparison is GAAP-to-GAAP. Track record over four quarters: three beats of 3.0–4.4% and one 8.3% miss (September 2025).

**Cash flow — read the seasonality correctly.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,420.9M against capex of $137.4M; for a homebuilder, land and homes under construction are inventory, not PP&E, so free cash flow approximates operating cash flow and the resulting 9.6% margin is not comparable to an asset-light business. Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was $880.8M vs. $949.1M. Management reaffirmed at least $3.0B for the full year. That is credible on seasonality: FY2025 full-year less nine-month implies Q4 FY2025 operating cash flow of $2,471.8M, so FY2026 needs roughly $2,119M — about 14% less than the prior-year fourth quarter delivered. Annualizing the nine-month figure (~$1.17B) would badly misstate the business.

**The interim funding gap is real.** Notes payable rose from $5,965.5M (9/30/25) to $7,112.6M (6/30/26), +$1,147.1M; cash fell from $2,985.4M to $2,078.7M, –$906.7M. Nine-month uses were $2.2B of buybacks (14.6M shares) plus roughly $381M of dividends. So approximately $2.6B of returns was funded by $0.88B of operating cash flow plus ~$2.05B of balance-sheet capacity. Debt/total capital rose to 23.0% from 19.8%; net debt/capital to 17.4% from 11.0%, against a stated long-term target of "around 20%." Management's separate statement that it generated $3.4B and returned all of it is accurate on a trailing-twelve-month basis; the nine-month picture differs. The Q4 buyback implied by the ~$2.5B full-year guide against $2.2B spent is only ~$300M, an 87% sequential step-down from Q3's $616M — consistent with management protecting the balance sheet.

**Debt structure matters more than the consolidated ratio.** Total notes payable of $7,112.6M breaks into homebuilding senior notes $2,977.3M, homebuilding revolver and other $708.0M, rental revolver $820.0M, Forestar notes $793.8M, and mortgage repurchase facilities $1,813.2M. The rental, Forestar and financial services facilities are not guaranteed by D.R. Horton or the homebuilding guarantor subsidiaries. Homebuilding-only debt of $3,685.3M against $21.3B of homebuilding inventory and $1.3B of homebuilding cash is genuinely conservative. Consolidated liquidity is $6.1B. The $600M of 1.3% senior notes maturing within twelve months would add roughly $22M of annual interest if refinanced near 5% — immaterial against a ~$4B pretax base, though it illustrates the ongoing repricing of the 2020–21 debt vintage. Investment grade from all three agencies.

One mechanical margin headwind: all interest was capitalized in the nine months because active inventory exceeded debt. Interest incurred rose to $189.9M from $173.2M, with homebuilding interest incurred up 52% to $106.4M on a 26% increase in average homebuilding debt. The capitalized balance grew 20% to $513.1M and flows into cost of sales as homes close. That drag persists while debt grows.

Screening output showing an Altman Z of 3.3 embeds an implausible EBIT/assets input of 0.009 against FY2025 pretax income of $4,739.9M on $35,471.2M of assets (13.4%); the correct input pushes Z materially higher. Altman Z is a poor discriminator for inventory-heavy homebuilders in any case. The failed Piotroski signal (operating cash flow below net income, ratio 0.954 in FY2025) reflects inventory investment, not accrual manipulation.

## Growth Analysis

**Guidance implies a second consecutive down year.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $32.5–33.0B is –3.6% to –5.1% versus FY2025; closings of 83,800–84,300 are –0.7% to –1.3% versus 84,863. Q4 closings guidance implies –1.6% to –3.7% versus Q4 FY2025's 23,368. Q3 starts were 23,900 and management said Q4 starts would be lower.

**Estimates are falling, and the September quarter revision is the sharpest.** Consensus for the current quarter fell 9.6% in thirty days to $3.04; current year fell 1.3% to $10.44; next year fell 3.0% to $11.69. The current-quarter cut is post-guidance and lines up with management's own reduction. Next-year consensus of $11.69 implies +11.9% growth off FY2026E of $10.44 — achievable only with rate relief, and it has already been trimmed once.

**The one genuine positive is the backlog inflection.** Backlog at 6/30/26 was 15,983 homes valued at $6,179.4M, up 14% in units and 16% in value year-over-year, reversing FY2025's decline. Backlog ASP of $386,600 (derived: $6,179.4M ÷ 15,983) is up roughly 1.8%, which management attributed largely to mix with some contribution from lower rate-buydown incentives flowing back to revenue. Nine-month net sales orders rose 5% in units and 4% in value while closings were flat.

Three qualifications keep me from treating this as a turn. The cancellation rate rose to 20% from 17% year-over-year and 16% sequentially, with management confirming the increase was back-half-of-quarter weighted and driven largely by qualification — so a 14% larger backlog converts at a lower rate. DHI is a spec builder; management noted that many sales and closings occur in the same quarter, making backlog a materially weaker forward indicator than for build-to-order peers. And average active selling communities rose 9% year-over-year, so the backlog build may reflect more stores rather than better per-store demand — absorption pace, which management described as "relatively flat over the last couple years," is the cleaner measure and it is not improving.

**The volume option and its cost.** DHI has opened roughly 30 new markets in five years and is number one in only half its markets. That infrastructure is currently a drag: homebuilding SG&A was 8.3% of revenue in Q3 (up 50bps) and 9.0% for nine months (up 50bps), with dollar SG&A up 8% on flat revenue. Management expects forward operating leverage once pricing and absorption stabilize. That is a conditional option, not an entitlement — if volumes stay flat two more years, the ratio stays elevated.

**Known FY2027 headwinds.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminated the energy-efficient home credit for homes closing after June 30, 2026. The credit was worth $39.5M in FY2025 and $70.4M in FY2024. FY2026 captures nine months; FY2027 loses it entirely, worth roughly $0.07–$0.14 per share of additional tax drag. Management already guided FY2026 to ~25% from FY2025's 23.6%.

## Valuation Assessment

**Deriving FY2026 EPS from guidance.** Nine-month net income to DHI was $2,147.6M ($7.45 diluted). Q4 guidance: revenue $8.8–9.3B (midpoint $9.05B), consolidated pretax margin 12.3–12.8% (midpoint 12.55%), tax ~25%. Midpoint pretax of $1,135.8M less 25% tax is $851.9M, less roughly $12M of non-controlling interest gives ~$840M. With only ~$300M of Q4 buyback implied, use ~279M shares: Q4 EPS ≈ $3.01, FY2026 ≈ $10.45. Cross-check on full-year weighted shares of ~286M: $2,988M ÷ 286M = $10.45. That is –9.7% versus FY2025's $11.57.

Peers: PHM 12.0x, LEN 13.6x, NVR 15.8x, TOL 11.1x, BLD 23.0x. DHI trades at a modest premium to the median and to PHM and TOL, which is defensible on scale, returns and balance-sheet quality but is a premium nonetheless — and DHI's estimate momentum is negative while its ROE compresses.

**On cycle-adjusted earnings power.** FY2022 peak EPS is not directly reported in the sources (the FY2022 diluted EPS cell is not populated in the multi-year table). Using FY2022 net income of $5,857.5M against FY2022 diluted shares of 354.8M implies roughly $16.51, so FY2026E of $10.45 is about 63% of that estimate. A reasonable mid-cycle figure with the current cost structure and a mortgage rate in the 5.5–6.0% range is $12–13, putting the stock at 11.3–12.3x mid-cycle. That is not a distressed multiple for a cyclical whose incremental demand depends on a rate path that is currently moving against it, and it sits against 1.74x book.

**The central capital-allocation tension.** DHI is repurchasing stock at ~1.74x book while earning ~12.4% ROE. The repurchase is EPS- and ROE-accretive (a 7.1% earnings yield against a ~3.4–4.0% after-tax marginal debt cost) but book-value-per-share dilutive relative to retention: each share retired costs $147.50 of cash to extinguish $84.85 of book equity. NVR has run this playbook successfully for two decades. The alternative reading is that in a cyclical whose drawdowns are historically measured against book, it reduces the cushion — and the nine-month version was partly debt-funded.

Analyst positioning is consistent with a fairly valued stock: 4 buy, 13 hold, 1 strong sell, described as stable, with a mean target of $164.17 (+11.3%), median $163.50, range $125–$206. On a name where estimates are falling and the forward multiple already sits at a peer premium, I do not treat consensus targets as a floor.

**Valuation score of 5.0.** The multiple is not stretched and the 7.3% capital-return yield provides real support, but there is no identified mispricing: the market is discounting declining earnings, a worsening rate backdrop, and compressing returns, and it is broadly right to do so. Cheapness against a falling estimate base is the classic pattern that traps value buyers in housing.

## Competitive Landscape

**Scale convergence at the top.** Lennar trimmed its FY2026 delivery target to approximately 82,000 homes against DHI's 83,800–84,300 guide. The two largest builders are within roughly 2–3% on volume, a narrower gap than the historical spread. DHI's number-one position by closings is intact but no longer commanding.

**The squeeze is industry-wide, which supports a sector rather than company-specific read on margin compression.** New single-family home sales ran at a 628,000 SAAR in June 2026, +1.6% month-over-month but –5.6% year-over-year, with inventory of 485,000 units and 9.3 months' supply against roughly 6 months for balance. The NAHB/Wells Fargo index was 35 in June. DHI's Q3 gross margin beating its own guidance while volumes came in soft indicates an industry managing inventory toward demand.

**Where DHI is genuinely differentiated:**

1. **Price point.** A $362,000 ASP roughly 30% below the national average means DHI competes for the buyer who can still qualify, largely against existing-home supply and rents rather than move-up new construction.
2. **In-house incentive delivery.** With 82% mortgage capture, the rate buydown is delivered internally. Economically the buydown is funded by the builder and reduces home sales revenue and gross margin; DHI Mortgage separately books gain-on-sale (nine-month gains on loan sales and MSRs of $394.0M vs. $423.9M).
3. **Land-light structure with genuine optionality.** 78% controlled, 1.5 years of owned supply (down from 1.7), and specific performance provisions on only $71.3M of $27.5B in remaining lot commitments secured by $2.5B of deposits. In a real downturn DHI can walk from the vast majority of its pipeline for the cost of deposits — materially better positioned than entering 2007.
4. **Cost and cycle-time discipline.** Stick-and-brick per square foot –5%, cycle time ~3 weeks faster, revenue per square foot –3%. Direct cost deflation is outrunning price deflation.

**Where the trajectory is negative.** Lot costs rose 5% per square foot with management guiding to a similar level into Q4 and development savings not reaching the P&L until "well into '27 and '28." Owned lots fell 13.2% to 126,600, and 67% of Q3 closings were on Forestar- or third-party-developed lots — so a portion of the margin that used to accrue to DHI's own land development is now paid to developers. Whether that +5% is pure land inflation or a mix effect from purchasing more finished lots is not disaggregated in the filings; both readings are consistent with the disclosure, and both mean gross margin is the price of the capital-efficiency choice.

**Regional performance is deteriorating almost across the board** (Q3 pretax margin, YoY): Northwest 10.5% from 16.7% (–620bps), Southwest 11.6% from 12.9%, South Central 13.7% from 15.0%, Southeast 12.0% from 11.7% (+30bps), East 11.8% from 13.8%, North 12.7% from 14.7%. Southeast was the only improvement, and it laps a base where FY2025 revenue fell 21%. The Northwest deterioration is not explained in the filings. Cancellation rates also spread: South Central 21% (from 16%), East 21% (from 18%), North 20% (from 18%).

Management said it has evaluated off-site and modular manufacturing and "haven't yet found anything that's replacing the way we've done it." Productivity gains are conventional process discipline, which supports durability but offers no discontinuous upside.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Affordability is the binding constraint and it worsened after the quarter closed.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average dipped below 6% in late February 2026 and then rose: 6.49% (7/9), 6.55% (7/16), 6.58% (7/23), 6.66% in the week of 7/30 — the highest in a year. Management's July 21 language was cautious about the intra-quarter trend: Q3 sales "were lower than our internal expected sales rate… demand softened a little bit as we went through the quarter and hence the reduction in our annual guide." On July trends, management declined to forecast. The Q4 guide issued July 21 likely does not reflect the late-July rate move, making the October 29 print the most immediate risk.

**2. Incentives are structurally elevated by management's own statement.** The 10-Q: "We currently expect sales incentives to remain elevated during the remainder of fiscal 2026 and into fiscal 2027." Management added that most of the available stick-and-brick offset has already been captured.

**3. Leverage above target into a soft quarter.** Debt/capital at 23.0% versus a ~20% target, with the rental revolver 78% drawn ($820M of $1.05B, leaving $230M). If Q4 closings land at 22,500 or gross margin slips below the 20.5% floor, DHI exits FY2026 above target and either slows repurchases or carries the debt into FY2027.

**4. Rental segment: a $3.0B position earning little and consuming cash.** Nine-month rental revenue $587.4M vs. $835.0M (–30%); pretax income $43.5M vs. $89.4M; gross margin 15.9% vs. 20.7%; segment SG&A 26.3% of revenue; the segment consumed $324.6M of cash. Single-family rental closings fell 44% in Q3 to 601 homes. Multifamily inventory is 13,830 units, of which 10,250 are substantially complete and in lease-up, up from 7,570 a year earlier — a growing pile of finished assets awaiting institutional buyers at elevated cap rates. Management has shifted the single-family model to delivering units on completion with buyers bearing lease-up risk, which reduces hold period but transfers less revenue per project.

**5. Construction-defect self-insurance.** Almost exclusively self-insured since June 1, 2021. Legal claims reserves $1,139.1M at 6/30/26 (4.8% of equity attributable to DHI), up 9.9% from $1,036.1M a year earlier; insurance receivables fell to $123.6M from $167.0M. Nine-month FY2026: 225 new claims notified (–41%) and 205 resolved for $65.8M, or ~$321K each, versus ~$94.7K in the prior nine months and ~$141K for full-year FY2025. Cash payments roughly doubled to $91.8M. Severity per resolved claim rising 2.3x while frequency falls could reflect a few large aggregated matters, backlog clearance of older claims, or genuine repair and litigation inflation; the filings do not disaggregate. Evidence cuts both ways — reserve additions were lower ($87.3M vs. $132.3M), pre-existing warranty liabilities were revised down $85.8M, and gross margin benefited 30bps from favorable litigation. The FY2025 sensitivity disclosure puts a 10% increase in both frequency and severity at $155.1M of additional pretax expense, roughly 3.3% of FY2025 pretax income.

**6. Forestar related-party governance and pending derivative action.** On April 29, 2025, the Mississippi Public Employees' Retirement System filed a derivative complaint in the Delaware Court of Chancery (C.A. No. 2025-0465-MTZ) on behalf of Forestar against D.R. Horton, Forestar's Executive Chairman, and certain Forestar directors, alleging breach of fiduciary duty arising from lot sale transactions between the two. The company disputes the claims; the action was pending as of the Q3 10-Q. The structural tension is that the intercompany transfer price allocates profit between DHI's wholly-owned homebuilding operations and a subsidiary in which minority holders own 38%. Derivative suits challenging controller-subsidiary dealings are common in Delaware and the filing is not itself evidence of unfair pricing; the material question is whether the pricing mechanism is durable if challenged. Related-party purchase commitments were $2.1B with $210.1M of earnest money. Separately, Forestar's tract land sales jumped to 765 acres for $73.2M in the nine months from 63 acres for $3.6M, while its lot position shrank from 99,800 to 91,700 — capital discipline, and also a pipeline that is not growing.

**7. Financial services counterparty mix shifted rather than diversified.** Agency execution (Fannie/Freddie/Ginnie) rose to 74% from 71%, while reliance on a single other major financial entity fell to 18% from 27%. Aggregate agency dependence increased, which matters given first-time buyers disproportionately use FHA/VA/USDA programs. Segment pretax margin compressed to 30.1% for nine months from 32.6%.

**8. Compensation-versus-performance.** CEO Paul Romanowski's total compensation was $25,128,033 in FY2025, essentially unchanged from $25,146,069, in a year when net income fell 24.6% and diluted EPS fell 19.3%. NEO average total compensation was $18,354,850, a CEO-to-NEO-average ratio of 1.4x — unusually flat and high in absolute terms for a $41B company. Performance measures are genuinely returns-based (pretax income, TSR vs. the S&P 500, ROA vs. S&P 500 companies, pretax ROA vs. a peer group, EPS growth), which aligns with the stated strategy. Five-year cumulative TSR of $235.46 per $100 beat the peer group's $214.30 but fell from $262.12, implying FY2025 absolute TSR of roughly –10.2%.

**9. Insider activity.** Reviewing recent Form 4s, activity was RSU vesting, tax-withholding dispositions and grants, with no open-market purchases and one small open-market sale (~260 shares at $167.55 on 2/13/26). The absence of buying while the stock fell roughly 12% from February is mildly negative, though DHI insiders are not historically open-market buyers, executives hold large positions, and blackout windows constrain timing. Contingent PSU dilution is immaterial: 374,025 units at maximum 300% payout is 1,122,075 shares, or 0.40% of shares outstanding (existing holders would retain 99.60% ownership) — dwarfed by the ~6% annual reduction from repurchases. Total stock-based compensation was $97.2M in nine months, about 3.3% of net income. No Item 5.02 executive-departure 8-Ks in fiscal 2026.

**10. Tariffs and inputs.** The FY2025 10-K expanded tariff language covering steel, aluminum and lumber. Management said recently announced Canadian tariff changes are not expected to be material but flagged fuel cost increases and "a slight lumber headwind again."

### Options Market Signal

Headline near-term ATM IV of 65.5% against 35.0% one-year historical volatility reflects a one-day expiry and should be discounted as same-day pinning mechanics; the implied move on that tenor is only ±3.1%. The informative tenors are the 1-month at 39.3%, 3-month at 38.3% and 6-month at 41.0%, all modestly rich to 35% realized — the market is paying up for optionality across the rate-path window rather than pricing imminent single-name stress. Put/call open interest fell sharply from 2.05 to 1.09 since July 21, consistent with hedges rolling off after the print, and near-dated skew is described as call-heavy. Against that, put/call volume ratios of 6.75 at the 3-month tenor and 8.00 at the 6-month tenor indicate heavy longer-dated put activity, which fits hedging the October 29 report and the FY2027 rate path. Short interest of 5.7% of float with 5.2 days to cover is uncrowded; there is no squeeze dynamic and no evidence that informed capital is pressing a bearish view in size. Net read: the options market corroborates a two-sided, event-driven setup with the risk concentrated three to six months out rather than immediately, which is consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $190 (+28.8%)

Mortgage rates fall back toward 6.0% as growth slows and the Fed's hawkish minority loses the sequencing argument. DHI's most rate-sensitive buyer re-qualifies, the 20% cancellation rate reverses, and absorption pace per community improves against a 9% larger community count — turning the SG&A infrastructure from a 50bp drag into operating leverage. Gross margin stabilizes at 21%+ as stick-and-brick deflation persists and buydown incentives shrink, with the backlog's 16% value increase converting at improving margins. Volume grows 5–8% in FY2027 on the community-count base, FY2027 EPS reaches $13.00 with the ~6% share count reduction compounding, and the multiple expands to 15x on visible re-acceleration. The land-light structure means this happens with minimal incremental capital, so the returns profile (top-quintile ROA among large caps by management's account) reasserts and the 7.3% capital-return yield continues throughout.

### Bear Case — $112 (–24.1%)

Rates hold at or above 6.66% or move higher on the fiscal-supply and Fed-independence premium at the long end. The October 29 print misses a guide that was set before the late-July rate move: Q4 closings land at 22,500 and gross margin below the 20.5% floor. Cancellations climb past 20% as qualification failures compound, incentives ratchet higher exactly as management says the stick-and-brick offset is largely exhausted, and lot costs keep rising 5% into falling prices. Industry months' supply above 9.3 forces price competition among builders with 485,000 units of standing inventory. FY2027 EPS falls to $8.75 on continued volume erosion, the energy-credit loss and higher interest expense from the growing capitalized balance. The multiple compresses to 12x on a second consecutive year of declining earnings, and the buyback slows to protect a balance sheet already 320bps above its leverage target. Book value support at ~$85 per share × 1.3x implies roughly $110; the bear target of $112 assumes DHI does not trade below the 1.3x book floor that has historically marked cyclical lows for the highest-quality builder.

## Conclusion

D.R. Horton is a well-run, investment-grade scale leader executing a deliberate trade of gross margin for return on capital and optionality, and it is executing that trade competently: stick-and-brick cost down 5%, cycle time three weeks faster, 78% of lots controlled rather than owned with walk-away rights on all but $71.3M of $27.5B in commitments, and backlog up 14% in units for the first time in this downturn. Those are real accomplishments in a market with 9.3 months' supply and an NAHB index at 35.

They are not enough to make the stock attractive at $147.50. The forward multiple of 14.1x sits at a premium to the 13.6x peer median on estimates that have been cut in all four measured periods, ROE has compressed from 19.9% to an implied 12.4% in two years, nine-month pretax income fell 18.2%, and the guidance that anchors the base case was issued before the mortgage rate reached a one-year high of 6.66%. Management is funding a 7.3% capital-return yield partly from a balance sheet that has moved 320bps above its own leverage target, which works if the Q4 cash harvest lands as the seasonal pattern says it should, and creates a second problem if it does not.

The bounded downside — 1.74x book, non-recourse subsidiary debt, $6.1B of liquidity, and lot commitments that can be abandoned for the cost of deposits — is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. The absence of any identified mispricing, with estimates falling and the rate path moving against the most rate-sensitive buyer cohort in the market, is why it is not a BUY. Existing holders should carry the position through October 29 and collect the capital return; new capital should wait for either a mortgage rate sustained below 6.25% or a price below $128.
