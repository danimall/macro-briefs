**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Permian Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin independent E&P that has spent the last twelve months converting an acquisition-built, leveraged balance sheet into an investment-grade, unsecured-debt structure with a simplified single-class equity. The operational story is strong: 2025 production grew 14% to 392,633 Boe/d and Q1 2026 reached 412,850 Boe/d, LOE fell to roughly $5.20/Boe, D&C costs were cut to ~$685/lateral foot, and the company generated $1.64B of free cash flow on a ~32% FCF margin while funding capex, a growing dividend, and bolt-on acquisitions entirely from operating cash. The headline GAAP earnings drag — 2025 net income of $935M (down from $985M) and a depressed Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.05 — is almost entirely non-cash, driven by a $270M convertible-note extinguishment loss in 2025 and a $370M mark-to-market derivative loss in Q1 2026 caused by oil prices rising into the company's swaps. Underlying cash generation improved, with Q1 2026 adjusted FCF of $513M.

The investment case rests on two pillars: a genuine credit-quality re-rating (Fitch, S&P, and Moody's investment grade; new $3.0B unsecured facility maturing 2031; redemption of the 8.00% 2027 notes; elimination of borrowing-base redetermination and hedge-collateral risk) and best-in-class cost discipline in the lowest-cost U.S. oil basin. Consensus EPS estimates have risen sharply — current-year estimates are up 48% over 90 days to $1.95 and next-year estimates up 30% to $2.12 — confirming a fundamental improvement the trailing 22x P/E obscures. On forward estimates the stock trades at ~9.8x current-year EPS.

The offsets keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The stock trades at roughly 9–10x forward EPS against a peer median near 10x, so the cheapness relative to E&P comparables is modest. Permian gas realizations turned negative (–$0.29/Mcf in Q1 2026) on takeaway constraints, oil hedges cap upside in a price spike, and the 2026 insider record is broad-based executive selling rather than buying. With shares 36% off the prior-year low and up against a peer cohort trading at single-digit multiples, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate PR a HOLD (growth_hold) and would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$15s or on confirmed Waha relief.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources operates exclusively in the Permian Basin, concentrated in the core of the Delaware Basin across West Texas and Southeast New Mexico. It is an oil-weighted operator — oil is roughly 47% of volumes but ~88% of revenue — controlling large, contiguous, operated acreage blocks with 96%+ of net acreage held by production as of year-end 2025. Operated control of the bulk of its wells gives management substantial discretion over the pace of capital deployment.

The company is a product of rapid consolidation: the 2022 Colgate Energy merger and the November 2023 Earthstone Energy merger took revenue from ~$2.1B in 2022 to ~$5.1B in 2025. Strategy combines a continuous bolt-on and grassroots acquisition program with disciplined organic development, both funded from operating cash flow. Differentiation comes from a low-cost structure, scale in the lowest-cost U.S. oil basin, and a firm-transportation portfolio that lets PR sell gas into premium Gulf Coast and Dallas–Fort Worth markets rather than the distressed Waha hub.

In January 2026 the company collapsed its Up-C structure into a single-class C-corp, eliminating the Class C/noncontrolling-interest complexity. Noncontrolling interest fell from ~10% in December 2025 to zero by March 31, 2026 as legacy Colgate/Earthstone holders converted Common Units to Class A, lifting the share count to ~837M. The business is jointly led by Co-CEOs William Hickey III and James Walter, with Guy Oliphint as CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and production.** Revenue progressed from $2.13B (2022) to $3.12B (2023) to $5.00B (2024) and $5.07B (2025). 2025 was roughly flat (+1%) as production growth was offset by a 14% decline in realized oil prices. Q1 2026 oil production was 192,349 Bbls/d.

**Profitability and cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $3.61B and free cash flow $1.64B. Operating margin was 28.9%, net margin 21.7%, ROE 10.7%, ROA 6.1%. D&C costs dropped to ~$685/lateral foot. The cost trajectory reflects operational discipline, not merely lower input prices.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt was reduced from $4.18B (2024) to $3.55B (2025), all fixed-rate senior unsecured notes maturing 2027–2033, with zero drawn on the revolver. D/E is 0.62 and stockholders' equity ~$11.3B at Q1 2026 after the reorganization. The Altman Z of 2.3 sits in the grey zone, typical for a capital-intensive E&P with large PP&E, and the Piotroski 4/5 is solid. The sub-1.0 current ratio (0.78) is normal for E&P (accrued capex and revenue payables) and the credit agreement's current-ratio test adds back undrawn revolver capacity, so covenant compliance is intact.

**Capital allocation.** 2025 deployed $1.97B of D&C capex, ~$1.1B of acquisitions, $502.9M of dividends/distributions, $464.5M of debt redemptions, and $73.7M of buybacks. 2026 capex is guided to $1.75–1.95B, fully funded from operating cash flow, and the base dividend was raised to $0.16/quarter (~3.5% yield).

The key analytical point: both the 2025 net income decline and the depressed Q1 2026 GAAP EPS are non-cash. The Q1 mark-to-market derivative loss was caused by oil rising into PR's crude swaps — an accounting hit, not a cash outflow. Adjusted FCF of $513M in Q1 2026 is the cleaner gauge.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two drivers: organic development and a repeatable acquire-optimize-develop program. Q1 2026 production of 412,850 Boe/d (+11% YoY) accelerated into the late-Q1 oil price spike, and management raised the 2026 oil guidance midpoint by 3.5 MBbls/d to 192.5 MBbls/d. Bolt-ons continue — the $608M Apache acreage deal (June 2025, ~13,000 net acres adjacent to the New Mexico position), ~$471M of other 2025 bolt-ons, and $204.9M of bolt-ons in Q1 2026.

The estimate-revision picture is the strongest forward signal. The current-quarter estimate has risen 63.3% to $0.52. Revision velocity of this magnitude, paired with double-digit production growth, marks a genuine earnings improvement that trailing P/E does not capture. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (two beats, two misses across the last four quarters), and the Q1 2026 reported $0.05 vs $0.38 estimate was an 86.7% miss, but that miss is the non-cash MTM derivative artifact, not an operational shortfall.

We weight the estimate-revision signal positively, with the caveat that for an oil-weighted E&P these upward revisions are substantially a function of the oil price assumption embedded in consensus. With PR hedged on ~70–80 MBbls/d of WTI swaps at ~$67–69 for 2026, the company participates only partially in spot oil above the swap strikes, so the revision tailwind is more muted in realized cash than the estimate trend implies. The growth is real and durable on volumes and cost, but the earnings beta to oil is hedge-capped.

## Valuation Assessment

PR trades at ~$18.57, with a market cap of ~$15.5B. Trailing P/E is ~22x on $1.28 EPS, but trailing earnings are depressed by non-cash items and are not the right anchor for an inflecting cyclical. On forward consensus, the stock trades at ~9.5x current-year EPS and ~8.8x next-year EPS. EV/FCF on 2025 FCF is reasonable for a grower funding its own capex and dividend.

Against peers, the relative case is modest. The Oil & Gas E&P peer median P/E is ~10.1x, and PR's forward multiple of ~9–10x sits roughly in line. APA (8.6x), AR (11.1x), EXE (6.5x), RRC (10.1x), and OVV (19.2x) bracket a wide range, and PR is not the cheap outlier. Its ~3.5% dividend yield exceeds the peer median of 2.1%, which provides some support, but the headline E&P comps already price in the cheapness that a low absolute P/E might suggest.

Analyst targets cluster well above the current price — low $23, high $30, mean $25.72, median $26 — with Mizuho at $27 (Outperform). For an inflecting name at a sub-10x forward multiple with rising estimates, these targets reasonably function as floors rather than ceilings, and the implied upside to consensus is meaningful (~39% to mean). We balance that against two structural offsets: the cheapness is shared by the entire E&P group (cheap multiples in this sector have historically sorted as value traps without an independent catalyst), and PR's earnings are oil-hedged, which dampens the upside in a sustained oil spike that would otherwise drive a re-rating. We score valuation 6.0 — constructive but not asymmetric. The independent catalyst that could lift this score is confirmation that the investment-grade re-rating lowers cost of capital in a way the market has not yet credited, or durable Waha relief.

## Competitive Landscape

PR competes in the most economic U.S. oil basin against larger, better-capitalized peers — Diamondback, EOG, ExxonMobil/Pioneer, Chevron/Hess, Devon, Occidental — amid continued Permian consolidation that strengthens rivals' ability to outbid for assets. Its competitive edge is cost: LOE ~$5.20/Boe and cash G&A ~$0.77–0.83/Boe place it among the lowest-cost operators, and operated control gives it capital flexibility.

The most differentiating asset is the firm-transportation portfolio. While many Permian peers realized negative gas prices in Q1 2026 on basin takeaway constraints, PR's contracted capacity to premium Gulf Coast and DFW markets and its basis-differential hedges let it capture premiums to Waha. Even with mitigation, PR's own realized natural gas price was –$0.29/Mcf before hedging in Q1 2026, underscoring that the regional gas market is currently a drag for everyone in the basin until new takeaway (Matterhorn and successors) ramps.

The competitive trajectory is improving: cost structure is widening its advantage, the credit re-rating lowers cost of capital relative to non-IG peers, and the simplified corporate structure improves access to capital. We score competitive position 7.0, slightly below the prior 7.5 to reflect that the gas-takeaway disadvantage is a shared, not idiosyncratic, headwind and that consolidation is strengthening the acquirers PR competes against for bolt-ons.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity concentration.** ~88% of revenue is oil; a 10% oil move shifts quarterly revenue ~$123M. WTI ranged from ~$59 in Q4 2025 to $102.88 on March 30, 2026 on Strait of Hormuz tensions. PR's hedges cap participation in an oil upside scenario while still exposing it to commodity-price downside.

**Waha gas.** Negative regional gas pricing is the standout company-specific risk, partly mitigated by transportation and basis hedges but a structural drag until new pipeline capacity comes online.

**Single-basin concentration.** 100% of reserves are in the Permian — concentrated exposure to takeaway constraints, induced-seismicity disposal regulation, water/drought, and power outages.

**Reserve execution.** 29% of proved reserves are PUDs; PV-10 swings ~$2.2B (±23–24%) on a 10% commodity-price change.

**M&A integration.** The acquisition-led model carries recurring risk of mis-assessed reserves or assumed liabilities (e.g., the Earthstone/Novo environmental matter, potential penalty >$1M).

Offsetting these, the 2026 balance-sheet transformation genuinely reduced financial risk: investment grade across all three agencies, an unsecured facility eliminating borrowing-base redetermination, removal of hedge-collateral requirements, and redemption of the highest-coupon near-term notes. The regulatory backdrop also turned more favorable (Endangerment Finding repeal, Paris withdrawal, OBBBA rollback of IRA methane programs). We score risk factors 5.0 — the financial de-risking is real, but commodity, single-basin, and Waha concentrations remain material and are only partially priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.0% sits slightly rich to the 33.3% one-year historical vol (a 3.7pp spread), at only the 12th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew of 3.5% and put/call volume ratio of 1.29 show modestly elevated near-term put demand, consistent with hedging an oil-leveraged name, but the put/call OI ratio of 0.27 is low and the term structure is in normal contango (37.0% near vs 39.4% long). ATM IV has fallen 10.1pp since early June (47.2%→37.0%) as the price drifted down 2.3%, indicating resolving near-term uncertainty rather than building risk. The options market is complacent-to-neutral and offers a reasonable entry on volatility if the fundamental case improves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** PR is a low-cost Delaware Basin operator that just completed a structural transformation — investment grade, unsecured facility, simplified single-class equity, NCI eliminated — that lowers cost of capital and removes financing-side risk. Production is growing double digits, LOE and D&C costs keep falling, FCF margins are ~32%, and consensus EPS is rising sharply. At ~9–10x forward EPS with a ~3.5% dividend, the stock is cheap relative to its growth and quality, and analyst targets imply ~39% upside to the mean. If oil holds in elevated ranges and Waha takeaway relief arrives, FCF and the dividend grow and the stock re-rates toward the high-$20s. Bull target: $28–30.

**Bear case.** The cheapness is shared across the E&P group, where low multiples have repeatedly been value traps without a catalyst. PR is single-basin and oil-concentrated, its hedges cap participation in an oil spike, and Waha gas realizations are negative. In a sustained low-WTI grind with persistent negative Waha, FCF compresses; broad insider selling at $18–20 and a still-developing co-CEO governance model add to the caution. Bear target: ~$14, ~25% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias and a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$15s (~8x forward EPS or below) restoring clear asymmetry, with estimate revisions intact.
- Confirmed Waha takeaway relief (new pipeline in service) turning regional gas realizations positive and lifting basin-wide FCF.
- Evidence the investment-grade re-rating is materially lowering realized cost of capital in a way not yet reflected in consensus, alongside open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained drop in WTI below the high-$60s, compressing realizations beyond the hedge floor, with current-year EPS estimates rolling over.
- Deterioration in capital discipline — a large debt-funded acquisition, dividend uncovered by FCF, or leverage rising back above ~1.0x D/E.
- Accelerating, larger-scale insider selling (>50% of holdings by multiple C-suite insiders) alongside guidance cuts.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, and any material shift in oil-price dynamics given PR's direct oil leverage.

## Conclusion

Permian Resources is a high-quality, low-cost Delaware Basin operator that has genuinely de-risked its capital structure over the past year — the investment-grade re-rating, unsecured facility, and structural simplification are real positives that standardized data pulls understate. Cash generation is strong, production is growing, costs are falling, and consensus estimates are rising sharply, all of which support a constructive view. The forward multiple of ~9–10x looks reasonable.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the absence of clear asymmetry: the cheapness is shared across the E&P peer group rather than idiosyncratic, oil hedges cap the upside in the price scenario that would otherwise drive a re-rating, Waha gas is a real and ongoing drag, and the 2026 insider record is broad-based selling, not conviction buying. The score trajectory (drifting from ~6.8 to ~6.5) reflects this stabilization at "good operator, fair price." We rate PR a HOLD (growth_hold) with analyst targets of $23 (low) to $30 (high), and would move to BUY on a mid-$15s pullback or confirmed Waha relief.
