**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Customers Bancorp is the ~$24.9 billion-asset holding company for Customers Bank, a Pennsylvania-chartered commercial bank that runs a branch-light, relationship-driven model built around recruited banking teams and national specialized-lending verticals. The 2025 operating results were strong: net income rose 23.5% to $224.1 million, net interest income grew 14.7% to $750.5 million, and the net interest margin expanded 17 basis points to 3.32% as deposit costs fell. Regulatory capital is comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds (CET1 12.99%, total capital 15.39%), loan credit quality is clean (NPLs 0.26%, net charge-offs 38 bps), and operating cash flow of $494.8 million exceeded reported net income. This is a genuinely profitable, cash-generative bank.

The reason CUBI trades at roughly 9.2x forward EPS ($77.05 against consensus 2026 EPS of $8.35), a discount to the regional-bank peer median of ~12.1x, is a specific and unresolved set of company-level problems. The August 2024 Federal Reserve Written Agreement and Pennsylvania Consent Order over digital-asset and BSA/AML compliance remains the central overhang, and I cannot confirm it has been terminated. The bank's deposit franchise leans on concentrated digital-asset "ecosystem" relationships and 33% brokered funding, which are cheap today but structurally less sticky. A securities portfolio the bank aggressively grew after 2020 into corporate, non-agency, and structured credit produced a $51.3 million impairment loss plus a $20.7 million credit-loss provision in 2025. An unusually dense cluster of executive and director departures across late 2025 into mid-2026 signals a governance situation still in flux.

The score trajectory has declined from 6.8 (May) to 6.2 (July), with valuation, management quality, and growth prospects all easing. My analysis confirms that trend rather than reverses it. Estimates are drifting modestly lower (current-quarter consensus down 2.5% over 30 days), the stock sits within a few percent of its 52-week high, and the largest thesis-moving item — the regulatory agreement's status — is unverified. The valuation discount is real but earned. I rate CUBI a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the operating engine works, but the risk cluster prevents a confident BUY and the fundamentals are too solid for an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Customers Bank operates a "high-tech, high-touch, branch-light" private-banking model. Rather than a retail branch network, it deploys relationship managers through a single-point-of-contact structure, complemented by digital deposit-gathering and a national specialized-lending franchise. The distinguishing operational tactic is recruiting whole banking teams — 10 teams in 2024, seven more in 2025 — to stand up niche verticals faster than larger, more bureaucratic competitors.

The loan book is 91.5% commercial ($15.4 billion), deliberately weighted toward floating and adjustable-rate credit (roughly $8.9 billion of the $11.3 billion in commercial loans maturing beyond one year is floating). Specialized lending is the largest segment at ~$7.1 billion (54.6% of the book), spanning commercial equipment finance, healthcare, real estate specialty finance, fund finance (lender finance and capital-call lines), and technology/venture-capital banking (a portfolio and team acquired in 2023 with coverage across the Bay Area, Boston, Austin, and Denver). Multifamily accounts for ~$2.5 billion (16.6%), CRE roughly $2.9 billion combined, mortgage-finance warehouse lending $1.6 billion, and consumer/installment ~$1.4 billion (8.5%), much of it originated through fintech partnerships.

On the deposit side, cubiX — a proprietary in-house B2B instant-payments platform launched in November 2024 to replace the sunset blockchain-based CBIT — services clients concentrated in the digital-asset industry (OTC desks, exchanges, market makers, liquidity providers) and is a key source of low- and no-cost deposits. Geographic concentration remains in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic with expansion into California and Nevada via recruited teams. The company does not pay a common dividend and does not intend to in the near term.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 income statement is the strongest part of the story. Net income to common was $209.2 million (+25.7%). Net interest income growth was driven by lower deposit costs, higher loan balances, and purchase-discount accretion; NIM expanded as total cost of deposits fell to 2.74% from 3.34% and cost of interest-bearing liabilities dropped 55 bps. The effective tax rate rose to 22.3% from 19.1% because 2024 clean-vehicle investment tax credits did not recur.

Balance-sheet growth was healthy: total assets +11.6% to $24.9 billion, loans/leases receivable +14.6% to $15.0 billion, deposits +10.3% to $20.8 billion. The deposit mix improved (non-interest-bearing demand +12.4% to $6.3 billion; savings/MMDA +23.2%; time deposits +21.7%), though brokered deposits stood at 33% of total deposits — a meaningful wholesale-funding reliance. The loan-to-deposit ratio was 81%, and the bank reported $10.6 billion of immediately available liquidity (cash plus FHLB/FRB capacity), roughly 124% of the estimated $8.6 billion in uninsured deposits.

Capital ratios are the metrics that actually matter for a bank, and they are strong: Tier 1 leverage of 8.72% sits above well-capitalized and Basel III thresholds, with 2025 fully reflecting CECL. Capital actions were constructive: the bank redeemed all Series E and F preferred ($142.5 million, at a $4.7 million redemption loss), raised $163.5 million in a September equity offering (2.19 million shares at $68.50), and issued $100 million of 6.875% subordinated notes in December to term out the $110 million 6.125% subs. Shareholders' equity rose to $2.1 billion.

Asset quality on the loan book is clean — ACL/loans of 1.03% (356% coverage of NPLs), and net charge-offs improving from 50 bps. The clear weak spot is the securities portfolio. The 10-K candidly acknowledges that after 2020 the bank moved away from its historically conservative agency-backed strategy into more aggressive corporate, non-agency MBS, and structured-credit positions and has repeatedly sold securities at losses (2025 charges of $72 million versus $3.6 million in 2024). There were $16.2 million of non-performing securities carrying an $18.8 million ACL. This is a self-inflicted problem distinct from the loan book, and the bank began rebalancing toward agency securities in 2025.

A note on the financial-health screen: the Altman Z-Score of 0.18 and D/E of 10.77 are not meaningful for a bank, since deposits are counted as liabilities and every bank screens as "distressed" on Altman. The relevant reads are the regulatory capital ratios (strong), asset quality (clean), ROA ~0.9%, ROE ~10.6%, and positive operating cash flow that exceeds net income. On that basis financial health is solid but not top-tier — the securities marks and the brokered-funding reliance keep it from a 7+.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is specialized lending, which grew the loan book 14.6% in 2025 and now represents over half the portfolio. The team-recruitment model gives the bank a repeatable mechanism for entering new verticals, and the floating-rate tilt of the commercial book protected NIM as deposit costs fell. Consensus EPS estimates show a business still growing: current-year $8.35 and next-year $9.50, implying roughly 14% earnings growth into 2027.

The near-term signal, however, is deceleration rather than acceleration. Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current-quarter consensus fell 1.7% over 90 days; next-quarter down 2.1% over 30 days; current-year down 1.5% over 30 days (though still up 0.8% over 90 days). This is negative earnings momentum, and it is the opposite of the estimate-momentum profile that would signal a fundamental turn worth chasing. The earnings track record is mixed-to-good — three beats and one miss over four quarters, with large beats in mid-2025 (16.2% and 11.8%) fading to a 1.8% beat and a 1.3% miss in the two most recent quarters, consistent with the deceleration the estimate revisions imply.

The durable question is whether the growth verticals are cyclical rather than structural. Venture banking, fund finance, and digital-asset payments are all exposed to cycles and, in the digital-asset case, to rapid regulatory change. The GENIUS Act stablecoin framework (signed July 2025) and FedNow are cited in the 10-K as competitive threats that could disintermediate the low-cost deposit base that funds the NIM. Growth is real, but its quality and durability are lower than the headline 14% would suggest, which is why I place growth prospects modestly below the sector-neutral midpoint.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the trailing EPS of $6.26 the multiple is ~12.3x. Either way, the stock is cheaper than the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.1x (TBBK 11.0x, BANR 11.3x, HTH 14.4x, MBIN 12.1x, NBTB 13.5x). Analyst price targets — low $76, median $94, high $104 — sit above the current price, implying meaningful headline upside to consensus.

The temptation is to treat the discount as a mispricing. I do not, for three reasons. First, the historical pattern in this system's scoring is that a cheap-looking multiple is more often a value-trap warning than a buy signal, and CUBI has specific reasons for its discount rather than being an overlooked quality compounder. Second, the discount is earned: an unresolved Federal Reserve Written Agreement, deposit concentration in a regulatorily fragile niche, a securities book generating impairment charges, and a cluster of executive departures collectively justify a lower multiple than peers without those issues. Third, estimate revisions are drifting lower, not rising, so the analyst targets are not obviously stale in the way they would be on a stock in a fundamental turn — the setup does not meet the "targets are floors" test, because that principle applies to names with positive estimate momentum and cheap multiples, and CUBI has only the latter.

Book value context matters. With $2.1 billion of common equity against ~30.6 million shares, book value is roughly $69 per share, so the stock trades near ~1.1x book — reasonable for a ~10.6% ROE bank but not cheap enough to represent a floor. The September 2025 equity raise at $68.50 illustrates that management itself accessed capital roughly 11% below today's price less than a year ago.

CUBI is fairly-to-slightly-attractively valued on forward earnings, but the discount to peers reflects genuine idiosyncratic risk rather than a market error. There is no specific catalyst I can point to that forces a re-rating within a defined window. That produces a valuation score at the midpoint — support on absolute earnings and book, but no asymmetric upside to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

CUBI's competitive edge is speed and specialization. The ability to recruit intact banking teams and stand up niche verticals — venture banking, fund finance, healthcare, equipment finance — faster than larger institutions is a real, if narrow, moat. The floating-rate commercial tilt and the low-cost digital-asset deposit base gave the bank NIM expansion in 2025 that many peers did not achieve.

That same specialization is the vulnerability. The model is highly people-dependent, and the deposit franchise leans on concentrated relationships (digital assets, fund finance) that could exit as an "ecosystem." The bank competes not only against community and money-center banks and fintechs, but increasingly against stablecoin issuers and tokenized deposits for its digital-asset base — a competitive vector that essentially did not exist five years ago and is now codified in federal law via the GENIUS Act. The 10-K explicitly references the 2022 FTX-era disruptions as a template for reputational contagion.

On trajectory, the competitive position is roughly stable rather than clearly improving. The bank is gaining share in its niches through recruitment, but its most differentiated franchise (digital-asset payments via cubiX) faces a structural disintermediation threat and sits under regulatory scrutiny. The broader post-2023 regional-bank backdrop — heightened focus on uninsured deposits and AOCI — bears directly on CUBI given its uninsured deposit base and its own securities marks. I score competitive position at the midpoint: a genuine niche moat offset by concentration and a regulatory/technological headwind to its most distinctive line.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant, company-specific risk is the regulatory overhang. On August 5, 2024, CUBI entered a Written Agreement with the Federal Reserve and a Consent Order with the Pennsylvania Department of Banking over compliance risk management for digital-asset services, board oversight, BSA/AML, and OFAC compliance. The financial footprint is visible — professional-services expense jumped 44% ($15.4 million) partly to enhance risk-management infrastructure. Such agreements can constrain growth, acquisitions, and capital actions until remediated. I could not confirm whether the agreement has been terminated as of mid-2026, and this is the single most important unresolved variable in the thesis. Until it clears, the stock carries a discount that no operating beat will fully remove.

Deposit concentration in digital assets is the second material risk. The 10-K states plainly that many of the largest deposit relationships are concentrated in the digital-asset industry and operate as ecosystems on cubiX; loss of a key relationship could trigger correlated withdrawals of low-cost deposits. Combined with the brokered funding, the low cost-of-deposits that drove 2025 NIM expansion is structurally less sticky than it looks.

Third, the securities portfolio: the post-2020 reach for yield produced repeated realized and impairment losses. Fourth, CRE and NY multifamily — roughly $1.2 billion of NY-state multifamily exposure, ~$849 million (69%) tied to rent-regulated properties impaired by NY's 2019 tenant-protection law, with multifamily net charge-offs rising in 2025 and further NYC rent-freeze proposals cited. Fifth, governance: a cluster of Item 5.02 executive/director departure filings (September 2025, January 2026, three in March 2026, plus May and July 2026), compounded by the July 2025 formation of a Special Litigation Committee following a June 2025 shareholder demand letter on AML/BSA disclosure. On the positive side, the December 2024 securities class action was voluntarily dismissed without prejudice in October 2025, removing one overhang.

These are concrete, identifiable, and not fully priced beyond the multiple discount — several are binary (regulatory agreement termination, SLC outcome) and could move the stock in either direction. That combination warrants a below-midpoint risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.8% against 32.9% one-year historical volatility — rich by 13 points, at the 70th percentile of the current chain, and up sharply from 37.1% just under three weeks ago on flat-to-down price action. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±10.4%, and the term structure is flat (near 45.8% ≈ far 45.0%), meaning the market sees uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single near-term event. Near-term skew is call-heavy (-4.0%) with a put/call volume ratio of 1.62 but a very low put/call OI ratio of 0.11 — a mixed signal that suggests active two-way positioning rather than a clear directional bet. Short interest of 10.1% of float (6.8 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional skepticism. The rising IV into flat price is consistent with the market waiting on an unresolved catalyst — most plausibly the regulatory agreement status or a governance development — which reinforces the case for patience rather than a fresh position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

CUBI is a profitable, well-capitalized specialty bank trading at a discount to peers. If the Federal Reserve Written Agreement is terminated, the primary overhang lifts, the professional-services expense drag eases, and the discount compresses toward peer levels. The specialized-lending franchise continues to compound the loan book at low-teens rates, NIM holds near 3.3% on the floating-rate tilt, and clean loan credit quality supports earnings power toward the $9.50 next-year consensus. Re-rating to ~11x on $9.00 blended forward EPS yields roughly $99, near the analyst high. Bull case: $100-104.

### Bear Case

The regulatory agreement remains open, constraining growth and capital flexibility. A digital-asset deposit ecosystem exits — accelerated by stablecoin disintermediation under the GENIUS Act — forcing the bank to replace low-cost funding with more expensive brokered or wholesale deposits, compressing NIM. Additional securities impairments continue as the portfolio de-risks, and the executive-departure cluster proves to reflect genuine instability rather than routine remediation. Estimate revisions keep drifting lower, the SLC surfaces adverse findings, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range on trailing earnings. Bear case: ~$59-62, roughly the 52-week low, a 20-23% decline.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July 2026), which should carry commentary on the regulatory agreement status, deposit trends, and securities repositioning. The regulatory agreement termination (or continuation) is the highest-value data point and could land at any time.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed termination of the Federal Reserve Written Agreement and PA Consent Order, removing the central overhang.
- A turn in estimate-revision velocity to positive (current-year and next-year consensus rising rather than falling) alongside stable or expanding NIM.
- Stabilization of the executive ranks (no further material Item 5.02 departures over two consecutive quarters) plus a clean resolution of the Special Litigation Committee process.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of digital-asset deposit outflows compressing NIM below ~3.0%, or a forced shift to more expensive funding.
- New or escalated regulatory action, or an adverse SLC finding.
- A material additional securities impairment (>$40 million) indicating the portfolio de-risking is not complete.
- Loan credit deterioration — NPLs rising above ~0.75% or net charge-offs above ~60 bps, particularly in NY multifamily.

## Conclusion

Customers Bancorp is a fundamentally sound bank — record 2025 earnings, expanding NIM, strong capital, and clean loan credit — trading at a discount to regional-bank peers. The problem is that the discount is earned rather than a mispricing. An unresolved Federal Reserve Written Agreement, deposit concentration in a regulatorily fragile digital-asset niche, a securities book that generated impairment charges, an unusually dense cluster of executive departures, and modestly falling estimates collectively explain why the market values CUBI below peers. None of these is fatal, but together they prevent a confident BUY, and the operating engine is too solid for an AVOID.

The declining score trajectory reflects real deterioration in the risk and governance picture, not merely price action, and my analysis confirms that trend. With the stock near its 52-week high, estimate momentum negative, and the single most important variable (the regulatory agreement's status) unverified, there is no asymmetric edge to capture today. I rate CUBI a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The path to a BUY is clear and monitorable — regulatory clearance plus a revision-momentum turn — and I would revisit promptly on either.
