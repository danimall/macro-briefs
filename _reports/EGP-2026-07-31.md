**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EastGroup owns roughly 65.7 million square feet of shallow-bay, multi-tenant industrial distribution space concentrated in Texas, Florida, California, Arizona and North Carolina, leased to approximately 1,700 tenants with no single tenant above 1.5% of annualized base rent. The balance sheet is the strongest in the sector we track: $1,609.5M of unsecured debt (net of issuance costs) against $540M of annualized EBITDAre (2.9x net), a February 2026 Moody's upgrade to Baa1 (stable), zero drawn on $675M of credit facilities, a 3.43% weighted average interest rate with all debt fixed or swapped, and 15.1x fixed-charge coverage in Q2. Q2 2026 delivered FFO of $2.36 per diluted share (+6.8% year over year), cash same-property NOI ex-lease-terminations of +8.3%, and a guidance raise that lifted the low end of the FFO range by $0.06 while leaving the high end at $9.66.

The complication is that the mechanism producing that NOI growth is decaying on a measurable schedule. Straight-line releasing spreads on new and renewal leases fell from +44.4% in Q2 2025 to +34.1% in Q2 2026, roughly ten percentage points of compression. The remaining 2026 expiration pool shrank from 13.1% of ABR at year-end to 3.6% by July 21, so the high-spread renewal opportunity for the balance of the year is nearly exhausted. Occupancy of 95.6% at June 30 sits below the 95.9%–96.5% range of the prior four quarters, and guided Q3 average occupancy of 95.2%–96.0% implies no recovery. Rent concessions granted in Q2 rose to $9.11M on 2,447,000 sf from $1.64M on 727,000 sf a year earlier, though a meaningful and unquantifiable share of that sits in first-generation development lease-up rather than second-generation renewals. Management's own guidance embeds the deceleration: 6.8% FFO/share growth in 2026 versus 7.5% realized in 2025, driven by fading mark-to-market, G&A guided to $26.7M from $24.0M, and interest expense up $2.35M in H1 alone as sub-3% debt rolls.

At $209.01 the stock trades at 21.8x the $9.59 guidance midpoint with a 2.97% yield, the lowest yield in its industrial peer group (peer median 4.1%). Our derived implied cap rate on stabilized assets is roughly 4.7% gross of G&A and 4.5% net of it, against a 10-year Treasury at [unverified]. The going-in yield offers little nominal spread to the risk-free rate, so the return case rests substantially on organic NOI growth and the ~290bp development spread. We rate EGP HOLD with a $220 twelve-month base case, and would upgrade on a demonstrated stabilization in releasing spreads plus pipeline lease-up above 40%, or on price weakness into the mid-$180s.

## Company Overview

Organized in 1969 and internally managed from Ridgeland, Mississippi with regional offices in Dallas, Los Angeles and Atlanta, EastGroup develops, acquires and operates industrial distribution property targeted at "location-sensitive customers (primarily in the 20,000 to 100,000 square foot range)." At December 31, 2025 the portfolio was 550 properties across 12 states and ~65.0 million square feet; by June 30, 2026 it was 557 properties and ~65.7 million square feet, with an operating portfolio of 62,523,000 sf. Business distribution product (510 properties, 59.3M sf) dominates; bulk distribution is only 4.9M sf.

Two structural features define the business. First, tenant granularity is genuine: ~1,700 leases with no tenant above ~1.5% of ABR materially reduces single-credit default risk versus peers carrying concentrated e-commerce or 3PL exposure. Second, the operating platform is unusually lean: 103 full-time employees manage ~65 million square feet, roughly 630,000 sf per employee, self-managing property management for 88% of the portfolio by area. FY2025 G&A of $24.0M on $721.3M of revenue is 3.3%.

Geographic concentration runs the opposite direction from tenant diversification. Dallas (6,428,000 sf, 10.9% of ABR) and Houston (7,108,000 sf, 9.5% of ABR) together exceed 20% of ABR, with additional exposure in Florida, California and Arizona. The 10-K flags hurricane, flood and earthquake risk, and recurring gains on involuntary conversion ($1.76M in 2025, $1.71M in 2024, $4.19M in 2023) confirm that physical damage events are a normal operating feature rather than a tail.

Growth comes primarily from development rather than acquisition. Six projects (1,231,000 sf, $128.8M cumulative cost) transferred to the operating portfolio in H1 2026 at a projected stabilized yield of 7.6% excluding the Los Angeles Dominguez redevelopment (9.4% including it; that project carried only $7.8M of cost on 262,000 sf, roughly $30/sf, and is not repeatable, so 7.6% is the representative figure).

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $487.0M in FY2022 to $721.3M in FY2025, a 12.7% increase in 2025 alone. PNOI grew 13.6% in 2025 to $528.3M. FFO per diluted share grew 7.5% to $8.98. The wedge between 13.6% aggregate NOI growth and 7.5% per-share FFO growth is equity issuance: diluted shares rose from 42,712k in FY2022 to 52,814k in FY2025, +23.7%, including +8.0% in 2025 alone.

That wedge is now closing, which is the most underappreciated element of the story. H1 2026 weighted diluted shares of 53,665k were +2.6% versus H1 2025, and full-year guidance assumes 53,726k, roughly +1.7% over FY2025. Gross capital proceeds are guided to $300M in 2026 against $517M raised in 2025, with dispositions guided to $75M versus $4M in 2025. Recycling is substituting for issuance. The H1 2026 dispositions realized $30.07M of gains on modest bases (Shaw Commerce Center in Fresno produced $36.3M of net proceeds against an $11.4M book basis), and a post-quarter Miami land sale at ~$14.3M should add ~$5M of gain in Q3.

Q2 2026 income from real estate operations was $193.3M (+9.0%), PNOI $142.9M (+10.6%), FFO $2.36 per share (+6.8%). H1 GAAP diluted EPS of $3.17 versus $2.35 looks like a 34.9% jump, but $0.56 of that is disposition gains excluded from FFO; the underlying FFO comparison is +7.6%. Any read of the earnings surprise table should carry this distinction: the reported $1.40 Q2 EPS against a $1.32 estimate is a GAAP-basis comparison inflated relative to the FFO trend.

The balance sheet is where EGP separates itself. At June 30: total assets $5,522.9M, liabilities $1,947.5M, equity $3,575.4M. Unsecured debt of $1,615.0M face carries a fair value of $1,539.1M, meaning EGP holds roughly $76M (about $1.41 per share) of economic value in below-market fixed-rate debt that appears in neither book equity nor FFO. That asset decays to zero at maturity rather than monetizing through action, but it does mean the cost-of-capital advantage erodes gradually. Net debt of $1,576.1M against annualized H1 EBITDAre of $540.2M is 2.9x, reconciling with the disclosed 3.0x gross. We treat the annualization as a sensitivity rather than a forecast: H2 should benefit from lease-up of 2026 development transfers, so full-year EBITDAre is more likely modestly above $540M and net leverage slightly below 2.9x.

Liquidity is ~$917.6M ($33.4M cash, $674.7M undrawn facilities, ~$209.6M of gross forward equity proceeds). FY2025 operating cash flow of $480.7M exceeded FFO of $474.3M; H1 2026 OCF was $301.9M versus $277.1M.

Two rate items net against each other. The maturity ladder rolls $315M of debt at a weighted 2.57% ($100M at 1.98% in October 2026, $40M at 3.75% in December 2026, $100M at 1.70% in March 2027, $75M at 3.89% in August 2027). At the recent marginal ~4.15% swapped rate, that adds roughly $4.98M of annual interest, or $0.093 per share, about 1% of FFO. Against that, $7,296k of AOCI is scheduled to reclassify as a *decrease* to interest expense over the next twelve months from nine outstanding swaps: $0.136 per share of current FFO benefit that is contractual and finite.

Dividend arithmetic: the quarterly rate rose to $1.55 (+10.7%), an annualized $6.20, which is 64.7% of the $9.59 FFO midpoint and a 2.97% yield at $209.01. This was the 186th consecutive quarterly distribution, with the dividend increased or maintained for 33 consecutive years and increased in 30 of them; the two claims are distinct and worth keeping separate.

Financial health scores 8.0. The leverage, coverage, liquidity and rating profile are best-in-class; the deductions are the REIT-structural reliance on external capital (the governing documents impose no debt limit, currently irrelevant at 12.9% debt-to-market-cap), the finite swap benefit, and $720.4M of remaining ATM capacity that gives management full optionality to re-accelerate issuance.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture has two clocks running in opposite directions.

Decaying: the mark-to-market engine. Releasing spreads went +40.1% for FY2025, +45.8% in H1 2025, +44.4% in Q2 2025, then +35.2% in H1 2026 and +34.1% in Q2 2026. Same-property average rental rate rose from $8.80/sf to $9.34/sf year over year; the loss-to-lease is being harvested, and each renewal cycle at a converging rent reduces the next cycle's contribution. The near-term opportunity set is thin: 2026 expirations fell from 13.1% of ABR at December 31 to 4.5% at June 30 and 3.6% by July 21. Q2's spread was achieved on only 4.5% of operating square footage, so the arithmetic contribution from re-leasing is shrinking on both price and volume.

The reported cash same-property figure is also decaying relative to its straight-line counterpart. Q2 cash SPNOI of +8.3% exceeds the +6.2% straight-line measure because the non-cash straight-line adjustment shrank (−$1,274k in Q2 2026 versus −$3,391k in Q2 2025). Cash rents are catching up to previously recognized straight-line rents, which is an earnings-quality positive, but it means +8.3% is the more flattering of two legitimate measures and +6.2% is the better proxy for GAAP durability. Comparisons against peer straight-line disclosures using EGP's cash figure are not like-for-like.

Building: per-share conversion. If aggregate PNOI can grow 8–10% while shares grow under 2%, per-share FFO growth should re-accelerate as the current pipeline stabilizes. The 2026 pipeline is 17 projects, 3,175,000 sf, $486.8M projected cost with $175.1M remaining to invest. At a 7.6% stabilized yield capitalized at a ~4.7% exit, stabilized value would be roughly $787M, implying ~$300M of embedded value or ~$5.58 per share. That figure is highly sensitive to the exit cap rate (at 5.5% it falls to ~$186M or ~$3.46 per share) and it assumes full stabilization on schedule, which the current 21.7% pipeline lease rate does not yet evidence.

Also building, at management's election: development starts guidance rose from $265M in April to $325M in July, and acquisitions from $160M to $215M. H1 actual starts were $123.3M and development capital invested was $99.5M versus $158.7M in H1 2025, with zero land or value-add acquisitions in H1 against $118.6M of land bought in FY2025. Achieving $325M implies roughly $200M of H2 starts, about 1.6x the H1 pace, drawn from an existing 10,992,000 sf entitled land bank carried at $379.8M. Consuming the land bank without replenishing it is capital-efficient. But start guidance is largely a management decision variable rather than a demand forecast, and accelerating starts into a pipeline that is only 21.7% leased extends the lease-up drag and the period during which earnings are supported by capitalized interest ($5.65M in Q2, $11.57M in H1).

Management's own arithmetic embeds deceleration: 6.8% guided FFO/share growth in 2026 against 7.5% realized in 2025, despite *raised* occupancy and cash SPNOI guidance. The drivers are spread compression, G&A guided +11% to $26.7M (Q2 G&A was +36% year over year at $7.21M, attributed to overhead and personnel supporting portfolio growth and plausibly including the expanded executive bench), and higher interest expense.

On estimate revisions: the "current year +10.4%" figure comes from a single analyst on a GAAP basis and is best read as marking to the $30.1M of H1 disposition gains, not as an earnings inflection; the same table shows the next-year estimate at $5.51, *below* the current year's $5.95, precisely because those gains do not repeat. The genuine signals are the quarterly revisions of +3.1% and +3.8% over 30 days, which are constructive but nowhere near the magnitude that would flag an inflection. The strong estimate-revision buy signal is not present here.

Growth prospects score 6.0: mid-to-high single-digit per-share FFO growth with a credible 2027-28 re-acceleration path, but with the near-term driver visibly decaying and the offsetting driver dependent on unproven lease-up.

## Valuation Assessment

At $209.01, EGP trades at 21.8x the $9.59 FFO midpoint. On the peer table supplied, EGP's GAAP P/E of 36.8x sits above the 29.2x peer median, and its 2.97% dividend yield is the lowest in the group (CUBE 5.1%, REXR 5.0%, STAG 4.1%, FR 3.2%, TRNO 3.1%; median 4.1%). GAAP P/E is a poor REIT metric because depreciation schedules and disposition gains dominate, so the yield comparison carries more information: investors are paying up for EGP's leverage profile and development engine, and the premium is not small.

The most useful anchor is the implied cap rate. Enterprise value of $12,776M ($11,200M equity plus $1,609.5M net debt less $33.4M cash) against annualized Q2 PNOI of $571.7M gives 4.47%, or 4.27% net of guided G&A. Removing non-income-producing assets from EV ($311.7M of development in progress and lease-up plus $379.8M of prospective development land) leaves $12,085M and produces 4.73% gross, 4.51% net of G&A on the stabilized portfolio.

A roughly 4.5% stabilized going-in yield offers little-to-no nominal spread against prevailing long-term Treasury yields. Real estate can be priced that way when NOI growth is high and financing is cheap; both conditions are weakening. EGP's marginal borrowing cost has already reset from a 3.43% book average to roughly 4.15%. If industrial cap rates reprice higher, the multiple compresses independent of operating results.

Against that, the development spread is the genuine value creator: 7.6% stabilized yields against a ~4.7% implied cap is roughly 290bp, and EGP can execute it off an entitled land bank while private developers struggle to obtain construction financing. That spread is what justifies a premium to the peer group. It is not, at 21.8x with 6.8% guided per-share growth, enough to justify an above-market entry today.

Sell-side positioning is constructive but unremarkable: 4 strong buy, 10 buy, 6 hold, no sells; mean target $227.45 (+8.8%), median $230.50, range $185–$268. The stock is 7.8% below its 52-week high of $226.71 and 31% above the 52-week low of $159.37. On our reading, the multiple already discounts a normal industrial recovery, so consensus targets here are not stale-and-rising in the way they are on genuine inflection names; the growth rate is decelerating, not accelerating.

Valuation scores 4.5. The multiple is defensible for the asset and balance-sheet quality, but there is no identifiable mispricing, the going-in yield offers little spread to the risk-free rate, and the growth rate that supports the premium is guided lower than last year's realized.

## Competitive Landscape

EGP competes for tenants in the 20,000–100,000 sf shallow-bay segment, which was structurally less oversupplied through the 2023–2025 industrial digestion cycle than the 500,000+ sf big-box product where speculative construction concentrated. Management's characterization of a "bifurcated" market (small tenants transacting while larger users operate cautiously) is consistent with EGP holding 95.6% occupancy while sector vacancy peaked. Third-party research (Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers, REJournals) converges on 2026 stabilization: demand exceeding deliveries, vacancy leveling, and rent growth moderating. Those two conditions, improving supply/demand with softening pricing power, describe exactly EGP's own disclosures.

The comparison set is First Industrial, STAG, Terreno and Rexford in multi-tenant/infill, and Prologis in big-box. EGP's differentiators are Sun Belt/Texas positioning rather than coastal infill, development as the primary growth engine rather than acquisition, low leverage at 3.0x Debt/EBITDAre, and tenant granularity that no peer matches. Loeb's articulated edge, capitalizing earlier than private peers using a Baa1 balance sheet and land and permits already in hand, is coherent, and the raised start guidance is the concrete expression of it. It remains a management assertion rather than a verified outcome.

On trajectory, the honest read is stable-to-marginally-softening. Occupancy is 40bp lower year over year and below the trailing four-quarter band; releasing spreads are down ten points; concessions per square foot rose from $2.25 to $3.72 in Q2. Some of the concession increase reflects first-generation development lease-up, where free rent is standard: EGP signed 16 leases on development and first-generation space totaling 1,101,000 sf between April 1 and July 21, and the disclosure does not separate them from the 2,447,000 sf concession figure. FY2025 full-year concessions ($10,894k on 4,555,000 sf) were *lower* than FY2024 ($12,192k on 4,932,000 sf), so this is a two-quarter phenomenon rather than a multi-year trend. Offsetting evidence on execution: the six H1 2026 development transfers were 95% leased by July 21, and the four Q2 transfers (669,000 sf) were 100% leased. Finished product is leasing; the low pipeline percentage reflects early-stage starts.

Competitive position scores 7.0: a durable niche and a genuine cost-of-capital advantage, with pricing power that is currently eroding at the market level rather than through share loss.

## Risk Assessment

**Cap-rate and duration exposure is the largest unpriced risk.** At a ~4.5% net implied cap rate against prevailing long-term Treasury yields, EGP's valuation depends on industrial cap rates staying inside a band that may not hold if the long end reprices. A 75bp cap-rate expansion on $571.7M of PNOI would reduce implied asset value by roughly 15%, and with only 12.9% debt-to-market-cap there is minimal leverage to amplify or cushion. The equity is effectively a long-duration instrument.

**Mark-to-market exhaustion.** Only 3.6% of ABR remained to expire in 2026 as of July 21. The high-spread renewal pool for the balance of the year is nearly gone, and 2027 spreads will be set against in-place rents already marked up to $9.34/sf.

**Pipeline lease-up.** $486.8M of projected cost at 21.7% leased, with $175.1M still to invest and $325M of new starts guided. The FY2025 10-K acknowledges EGP "routinely develops properties with no pre-leasing." If absorption slows, the drag persists longer and capitalized interest ($11.57M in H1) supports reported earnings for additional quarters.

**Geographic and physical concentration.** Texas alone is over 20% of ABR from Dallas and Houston. Recurring involuntary-conversion gains confirm regular storm damage across Florida, Texas and California.

**Refinancing and swap roll-off.** $315M rolling from 2.57% to roughly 4.15% costs ~$0.093 per share; the $7,296k of AOCI reclassifying as an interest-expense reduction over the next twelve months (~$0.136 per share) is finite and will not repeat as swaps mature at prevailing rates.

**Committed dilution.** 1,040,457 forward shares at a $199 weighted average forward price settle between March and June 2027, equal to 1.94% of shares outstanding at June 30 and 1.90% ownership dilution on a post-issuance basis. These are pre-sold and price-certain, materially lower-risk than open-market issuance. Contingent performance awards of zero to 167,472 shares are not in current fully diluted capitalization and would add at most a further 0.31%.

**Governance.** No restatements, auditor changes, covenant issues or material litigation appear in the 8-K record. The 9.8% REIT ownership limit remains, but EGP has opted out of the Maryland Control Share Acquisition and Business Combination Acts, which is shareholder-friendly. The December 2025 executive restructuring (Dunbar to President, Tyler to CFO, Wood to a new COO role, Rayner to CAO, Loeb remaining CEO) is confirmed by 10-Q signature blocks and officer titles; the narrative framing rests on trade press we could not corroborate against the 8-K text. An Equity Award Retirement Policy permitting accelerated vesting for retirement-eligible employees is worth monitoring alongside the restructuring.

**Unresolved items we do not treat as established:** the split of Q2 concessions between first-generation and second-generation leasing; going-in yields on the Phoenix Airgate ($28M, $196/sf) and Austin ($83M, $214/sf, under contract, Q3 close expected) acquisitions; and the cause of the drift from the July 21 close of $221.34.

Risk factors score 6.5. Binary company-specific risk is genuinely low: no litigation, no covenant pressure, no tenant concentration, no refinancing cliff. The deduction is for the live and only partially priced cap-rate sensitivity embedded in a 2.97% yield.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 20.1% versus 18.7% one-year historical volatility is fair (1.5pp spread), and the put/call skew at 0.7% is balanced; the options market is pricing no directional stress. The term structure is in mild backwardation (20.1% at the August 21 expiry, 19.8% at September 18, 17.2% at December 18). Because the Q2 print already cleared on July 22, that near-dated premium is not company-event risk; the near-dated expiries are consistent with a rate-event premium on a duration-sensitive REIT rather than anything company-specific. IV sits at only the 21st percentile within the current chain, so protection is not expensive relative to recent history. Short interest at 5.9% of float and 5.9 days to cover indicates neither a crowded bear position nor a squeeze setup. The options data corroborates that the main near-term variable is rates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $250 (+20%)

Industrial supply has peaked and 2027 is the recovery year. Releasing spreads stabilize in the high-20s to low-30s rather than continuing toward the teens, occupancy recovers to 96.5%+ as the 120bp leased-minus-occupied gap commences, and pipeline lease-up accelerates past 40% as the 2026 starts find tenants the way the H1 transfers did (95% leased). Aggregate PNOI compounds 8–10% against sub-2% share growth, so per-share FFO growth re-accelerates from 6.8% toward 9%+ in 2027-28. The 290bp development spread on the 11 million sf land bank converts to $3.50–$5.50 per share of value creation. If the long end retraces, industrial cap rates compress and 24x on ~$10.30 of 2027 FFO produces $247. Analyst high target is $268.

### Bear Case — $170 (−19%)

The long end continues to steepen, industrial cap rates reprice from ~4.7% to 5.5%, and EGP's multiple compresses to 17x. Releasing spreads keep sliding toward the mid-teens as loss-to-lease is exhausted, and same-property cash NOI growth decelerates from 8.3% toward 3–4% by late 2027. Occupancy leaks below 95% as concessions prove to reflect second-generation softness rather than development lease-up. The raised $325M of starts lands into a market that absorbs slowly, extending capitalized-interest-supported earnings and leaving $175M+ of remaining pipeline investment to fund at a 4.15% marginal cost against 7.6% projected yields that slip on higher construction costs from tariffed inputs. 17x on $9.90 of 2027 FFO is $168. Consensus low target is $185, so the bear case is below the Street's floor.

### Base Case — $220 (+5.3%, +8.2% with dividend)

2027 FFO of roughly $10.15 per share (6% growth off the $9.59 midpoint, reflecting continued spread compression offset by transfers, dilution deceleration and the swap benefit) at 21.5x, a modest premium to the peer group for the balance sheet and development engine, roughly in line with the current multiple. Total return of about 8% over twelve months, most of it earned rather than re-rated. That is a fair return for the risk, and it does not clear the bar for adding capital at the current level.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings in late October is the primary checkpoint. It delivers the two operating data points that decide the thesis and updated guidance.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Releasing spread stabilization** — Q3 straight-line spreads holding at or above 32% (versus 34.1% in Q2 and 35.2% in H1), which would mean the decay is flattening rather than continuing at ~10pp per year.
2. **Pipeline lease-up above 40%** on the development and value-add program (from 21.7% at July 21), validating the raised $325M of starts and de-risking the ~$300M of embedded value.
3. **Price into the mid-$180s** — at $185 the stock trades at 19.3x the 2026 midpoint with a 3.35% yield and an implied stabilized cap rate above 5.0%, which restores a spread to the risk-free rate and would make the 290bp development spread a paid-for option rather than a required assumption.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Occupancy below 95.0%** at a quarter end, combined with a full-year FFO guidance cut, which would indicate the mark-to-market exhaustion is running ahead of the sector recovery.
2. **Q3 concession disclosure showing second-generation leasing carrying materially elevated free rent** — specifically, concessions above $3.50/sf on space excluding first-generation development, which would resolve the most consequential open question against the bull case.
3. **Development yields on 2026 starts guided below 7.0%**, which would compress the spread to under 230bp and remove the primary justification for the premium multiple.

**What would not change our view:** a further 5–10% price decline on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward and moves the name toward the upgrade zone rather than the downgrade zone. Our HOLD reflects the multiple, not doubt about the assets.

## Conclusion

EastGroup is a well-run business with the strongest balance sheet in its peer group and a defensible niche in shallow-bay Sun Belt industrial. The Q2 print was solid, with guidance raised at the low end and operational raises across occupancy, same-property NOI, starts and acquisitions. Capital allocation is improving in a way the market has not fully credited: dilution decelerating from +8.0% in FY2025 to a guided +1.7%, with dispositions at large gains funding growth in place of ATM issuance.

The reason we stay at HOLD is arithmetic rather than sentiment. Releasing spreads have compressed ten points year over year, the remaining 2026 expiration pool is down to 3.6% of ABR, occupancy has slipped below its trailing range, and management guides 2026 per-share FFO growth of 6.8% against 7.5% realized in 2025. The stock holds a 21.8x multiple, a 2.97% yield that is the lowest among industrial peers, and an implied stabilized cap rate of roughly 4.5% net of G&A that offers little spread to prevailing long-term Treasury yields. The development spread is real and is the reason to own this over lower-quality industrial REITs, but it does not create asymmetry at the current price. Our overall score of 6.4 reflects the Q2 disclosures on spreads, occupancy and concessions plus the rate backdrop.

Own it for the compounding and the dividend record; wait for either the mid-$180s or evidence that spreads have stopped decaying before adding.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several macro anchors used to argue "no spread to the risk-free rate" (10-year at 4.67%, 30-year at 2007 highs, Fed on hold with three hawkish dissents, September hike near coin-flip) are not supported anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed/neutralized — the valuation-caution case rests on those unverified rate claims, so the HOLD rationale should be re-examined against actual rate data before publication. Additionally, the report's prior-period score anchor ("6.7 in June," "BUY we carried in May") is not in the sources and has been removed.
