## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is in the middle of the most profitable refining environment since 2022, and the market knows it. Externally reported Q2 2026 net income of $5.14B ($17.73 per share) on revenue of $52.34B compares with $511M and $1.73 per share in the March quarter — a swing driven almost entirely by a Refining & Marketing margin that reportedly rose to $36.33/bbl from $17.58/bbl a year earlier. The earnings-surprise record corroborates the figure ($17.73 actual against a $13.86 estimate, +27.9%), but no Q2 10-Q or earnings 8-K was retrievable, so segment detail, cash flow and buyback dollars for the quarter remain unverified. Consensus has responded violently: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 72.3% in 90 days to $39.08, and even FY2027 is up 35.1% to $27.77.

The analytical question is not whether the quarter was good. It is what fraction of a blended crack spread that has roughly doubled since January is durable. The drivers separate into structural components — a genuine global shortage of refining capacity, California rationalization that has left MPC's West Coast ANS 3-2-1 as its highest-margin region ($26.95/bbl in Q1 2026 versus $10.85 Mid-Continent) — and transitory ones: Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining capacity and the U.S.-Iran conflict, which MPC's own Q1 10-Q newly names as a forward-looking risk driver. Consensus itself treats the bulk as transitory, modeling FY2027 EPS 29% below FY2026.

At $307.03 the stock sits 6.1% below its July 22 high of $326.92, above the consensus mean target of $303.89, and at roughly 11x both FY2027 consensus and our estimate of normalized post-buyback earnings power. A sum-of-parts using the MPLX stake implies about $19,800 per barrel per day for the refining system, a full price by historical transaction standards. The torque is extraordinary in both directions: MPC discloses that $1.00/bbl on its blended crack equals $1,125M of annual R&M EBITDA, roughly $3.23 per share after tax. We see base-case value at $325 (+5.9%), a bull case near $380 (+23.8%) and a bear case at $190 (−38.1%) if cracks normalize. That asymmetry does not support new capital. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

MPC operates three segments. **Refining & Marketing** ran net throughput of 2,850 mbpd in Q1 2026 at 89% crude utilization, implying roughly 2,993 mbpd of calendar-day crude capacity (derived from 2,664 mbpd of crude refined ÷ 0.89; not a disclosed figure). Assets span the Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent and West Coast, with named high-return capital going to Galveston Bay, Robinson, El Paso and Garyville. Product moves through wholesale, spot, Marathon-branded independent outlets and long-term fuel supply contracts with ARCO-brand direct dealers — supply and brand-licensing relationships rather than a company-operated retail chain. Exports were 362 mbpd in Q1 2026.

**Midstream** is conducted through MPLX LP, in which MPC holds the general partner and approximately 64% of common units (647 million) at 3/31/26. MPC consolidates MPLX and books the ~36% public interest as a noncontrolling interest; MPLX creditors have no recourse to MPC. A majority of MPLX's pipeline and marine volumes and all refining-logistics services serve MPC's own R&M segment under long-term fee-based agreements, some obligating MPC to pay for 100% of available capacity regardless of use.

**Renewable Diesel** (Dickinson plus the Martinez JV) is small — $572M of Q1 external revenue, ~1.7% of consolidated — and swung to +$38M of segment adjusted EBITDA from −$42M, helped by $58M of 45Z clean fuel credits recognized after February 2026 proposed guidance.

The 10-K is candid on the structural gap: MPC produces none of its own crude, and concedes that integrated competitors "are at times better positioned to withstand periods of depressed refining margins or feedstock shortages."

## Financial Analysis

The four-year record shows how much of this business is spread, not volume:

| $M except per share | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 177,453 | 148,379 | 138,864 | 132,699 |
| Operating income | 21,469 | 14,514 | 6,796 | 8,291 |
| Net income to MPC | 14,516 | 9,681 | 3,445 | 4,047 |
| Diluted shares (wtd avg) | 516 | 409 | 341 | 306 |
| Derived diluted EPS | $28.13 | $23.67 | $10.10 | $13.23 |

Revenue has fallen three consecutive years while Q1 2026 throughput (2,850 mbpd) was essentially identical to Q1 2025 (2,849 mbpd). Revenue is the wrong lens; margin per barrel is the operative variable. FY2025 delivered operating cash flow of $8,253M, capex of $3,486M and free cash flow of $4,767M, with an effective tax rate of 16.2% because income attributable to MPLX's public unitholders is not taxed at MPC.

**Consolidated free cash flow overstates what MPC owns.** Roughly 36% of MPLX's cash generation belongs to public unitholders; distributions to noncontrolling interests were $407M in Q1 2026, on the order of $1.5B annually. The cleaner parent view is R&M/Renewable Diesel cash flow plus the MPLX distributions MPC actually receives: $697M in Q1 2026, up 12.6% year over year, an annualized $2.79B against roughly $1.18B of common dividends at $1.00/quarter on ~292M shares. That is 2.4x coverage from fee-based midstream income before any refining cash flow — the most durable feature of the structure and the reason MPC's dividend does not depend on crack spreads.

**Q1 2026 confirmed the inflection but with two significant offsets.** Income from operations rose to $1,404M from $687M; net income to MPC was $511M ($1.73 diluted) versus a $(74)M loss. R&M adjusted EBITDA reached $1,377M ($5.37/bbl versus $1.91). Against that: commodity derivative losses of $909M ($390M realized, $519M unrealized) versus $65M, because MPC does not designate its commodity book as accounting hedges and carried 114.9M bbl short crude and 110.8M bbl short refined products into a rising product market; and RIN expense of $593M versus $354M, up 68%. Unit costs drifted higher — refining operating costs $6.23/bbl (from $5.74), distribution costs $6.16/bbl (from $5.77) of which $3.97/bbl was fees to MPLX, turnaround costs $2.07/bbl (from $1.77).

The derivative line cuts both ways and should not be added back. $390M was realized cash. If margins stay elevated, further unrealized marks will depress reported results relative to cash economics; if margins revert, those positions become gains — a partial, unquantified natural hedge on the way down.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $33,272M at 3/31/26 splits into MPC-only $7,266M and MPLX $26,006M. MPC-only cash and short-term investments of $645M give parent net debt of $6,621M against $5,644M of parent liquidity. Both entities are Baa2/BBB/BBB with stable outlooks and no rating triggers accelerating principal. In April 2026 both replaced 2027 revolvers with five-year facilities maturing April 2031, and MPC extended its trade receivables facility to April 2029. Current ratio 1.18 (from 1.26). Consolidated total debt/equity 1.42x; against MPC stockholders' equity alone 1.99x, but that charges $26B of non-recourse subsidiary debt against parent equity and overstates parent risk. A recalculated Altman Z of ~3.5 sits above the 2.99 threshold.

Where leverage grew is worth naming: consolidated long-term debt rose from $24,432M to $30,505M during 2025, driven almost entirely by MPLX funding Northwind ($2.4B) and BANGL. In February 2026 MPLX retired $1.5B of 1.750% notes with $1.0B at 5.300% and $500M at 6.100%, adding roughly $57M of annual interest. Consolidated interest expense was $420M in Q1 2026 versus $352M, and FY2025 was $1,276M versus $839M in FY2024. That is a persistent drag independent of the margin cycle.

Goodwill of $9,335M plus intangibles of $2,658M equals $11,993M against $16,753M of MPC stockholders' equity, with $1.09B of the recent addition Permian-midstream-specific and Northwind's allocation still preliminary through 8/29/26.

## Growth Analysis

There is very little underlying volume growth here, and honest scoring has to say so. Throughput is flat. Refining capacity is not expanding. Revenue has declined three straight years. What growth exists comes from three places.

**Share count.** Weighted-average diluted shares fell from 516M in 2022 to 306M in 2025 and 295M in Q1 2026 — a 43% reduction in four years, executed through $56.42B of cumulative repurchases against $60.05B authorized since 2012. Remaining capacity at 3/31/26 was $3.63B, plus a new $5.0B authorization on 2026-05-05, totaling $8.63B. At $307.03 that retires ~28.1M shares, 9.6% of the count; a holder of 1.00% today would hold ~1.106% afterward. Authorization is capacity, not commitment, and the 10-Q states repurchases may be suspended at any time.

**Midstream.** MPLX growth capex was $886M in Q1 2026 versus $381M, up 132%, directed at Permian-to-Gulf Coast, long-haul pipelines and new gas processing in the Marcellus and Permian. Northwind's sour gas treating is expected to exceed 400 MMcf/d by 2H 2026 from 150 MMcf/d at acquisition, partly supported by producer minimum volume commitments. BANGL carries an earnout of up to $275M tied to 2026-2029 EBITDA growth ($239M liability at 3/31/26).

**Cyclical margin.** This dominates everything and is where the estimate revisions come from: current quarter $13.95 (+60.1% in 90 days), next quarter $15.50 (+106.7%), FY2026 $39.08 (+72.3%), FY2027 $27.77 (+35.1%). The magnitude and breadth (15-18 contributing analysts) are real, and the FY2027 revision matters most because it shows analysts marking up the out-year, not just the print. But the signal's predictive value is weaker than it looks in a commodity business where the same analysts model FY2027 29% below FY2026. Rising estimates driven by a spot spread the forecasters expect to revert are not the same as rising estimates driven by unit growth or margin structure.

Management's own view (Q1 MD&A) is that "global demand growth is expected to outpace the net impact of refining capacity additions and rationalizations through the end of the decade." That is a defensible structural claim. It is not a claim that Q2 2026's $36.33/bbl R&M margin is the new normal.

## Valuation Assessment

Every reasonable multiple for MPC depends on which earnings base you choose, so we show the range and then anchor.

| Basis | EPS | P/E at $307.03 |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 actual | $13.23 | 23.2x |
| Trailing four quarters (Q3'25-Q2'26) | $26.54 | 11.6x |
| FY2026 consensus | $39.08 | 7.9x |
| FY2027 consensus | $27.77 | 11.1x |
| Normalized 2022-2025 avg net income ($7.92B) on 292M shares | $27.12 | 11.3x |

The 7.9x on FY2026 is optically cheap for the same reason a 23x trailing multiple on FY2025 was optically expensive: the numerator is a windfall. The two anchors that matter are FY2027 consensus and normalized earnings power, and they converge at roughly 11x. Adjusting normalized EPS upward for full execution of the $8.63B authorization gives about $29.50-30.00, or 10.2-10.4x. US refiners have historically traded 8-12x mid-cycle. MPC is in the upper half of that band.

**Peer context.** Reported P/Es (PSX 17.6, VLO 18.6, DINO 10.6, PBF 10.8, median 17.6) are largely pre-Q2 and will compress across the group — Valero and HF Sinclair also posted their strongest quarters since 2022. The more useful comparison is that the entire group re-rated together: MPC, VLO and DINO were each up more than 80% year to date as of mid-July versus roughly 11% for the S&P 500. This was a sector margin event, not company-specific execution, which is exactly why relative-metric-heavy compensation design matters and why we do not credit MPC with selection alpha for the move. MPC's 1.30% dividend yield ($4.00 on $307.03) is less than half the 2.8% peer median.

**Sum of parts.** 647M MPLX units at the 3/31/26 close of $57.07 equal $36.93B. Implied enterprise value for R&M, Renewable Diesel and corporate = $89.63B equity + $6.62B parent net debt − $36.93B = **$59.3B**, or roughly **$19,800 per barrel per day** on ~2,993 mbpd of derived capacity. That is a full price for US refining assets and implies the market already capitalizes a mid-cycle materially above the 2024-2025 experience. The MPLX leg is stale — if MPLX re-rated alongside MPC, the implied refining value is lower — and the calculation ignores deferred tax on the stake and $473M of aggregate guarantees. The input errors mostly make refining look cheaper, so treat $19,800/bpd as an upper-bound stress rather than a point estimate. Price-to-book of 5.3x ($16,753M ÷ 292M = $57.4/share) is close to meaningless given $56.0B of treasury stock.

**Asymmetry.** Consensus mean target of $303.89 sits below spot; median $311; high $376; low $186. The analyst distribution — 3 strong buy, 6 buy, 7 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell — is unusually lukewarm for a company posting record results, and the presence of active sell ratings alongside +72% estimate revisions tells you the sell side expects multiple compression as earnings normalize. On our scenarios, expected value at 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear is approximately $298, slightly below spot.

Valuation scores 4.5. The cheapness is a function of the numerator, not a mispricing we can identify.

## Competitive Landscape

MPC's advantages are scale (one of the largest refining systems globally), three-region diversification across differing crack dynamics, and captive MPLX logistics. Regional differentiation currently favors it: West Coast ANS 3-2-1 at $26.95/bbl in Q1 2026 versus USGC MEH $14.65 and Mid-Continent WTI $10.85, a direct consequence of California capacity rationalization in an import-dependent, isolated market.

The captive-logistics advantage is partly a transfer-pricing artifact. MPC paid MPLX $3.97/bbl of its $6.16/bbl distribution cost in Q1 2026, and some agreements obligate MPC to pay for 100% of available capacity of certain marine and refining-logistics assets. In a downturn those minimum commitments are fixed cost at R&M and revenue at Midstream — neutral consolidated, but it means R&M's downside is structurally more geared than a pure-play's.

The conceded disadvantage is the absence of upstream production, which matters most in feedstock-scarce or depressed-margin periods. Against that, MPC's five-year cumulative TSR of $448.38 per $100 invested at 12/31/2020 versus a peer group at $329.43 is a real outperformance record, and MPC Relative TSR Percentile of 66.67 for 2025 is consistent with above-median but not dominant operating performance.

Trajectory: stable to modestly improving, driven by regional supply exits rather than share gains. Score 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Crack mean reversion is the entire investment case.** Disclosed sensitivities: $1.00/bbl blended crack = $1,125M annual R&M adjusted EBITDA (≈$3.23/share after tax at a 16% rate); $1.00/bbl on either sour or sweet differential = $520M; $1.00/MMBtu natural gas = $360M. Q1's blended 3-2-1 was $15.34/bbl. If the current environment reverts toward $15 from what Q2's $36.33/bbl realized margin implies, roughly $15-17B of annualized R&M EBITDA disappears. For context, FY2025 total net income to MPC was $4,047M.

**2. Political response to windfall profits — the most specific underpriced catalyst.** California's SB X1-2 gives the CEC authority to set a maximum gross gasoline refining margin with penalties and to regulate turnaround timing; AB X2-1 authorizes minimum inventory requirements. In August 2025 the CEC resolved not to act on a margin cap for at least five years — but that was adopted in a low-margin environment, and the CEC simultaneously opened an informational proceeding on minimum inventories. Record refiner profits and elevated pump prices, combined with the CEC's ongoing minimum-inventory proceeding, is the same fact pattern that produced SB X1-2. MPC's 10-K explicitly lists "windfall profit taxes, maximum margin penalties, minimum inventory requirements."

**3. California labor, live now.** Approximately 700 hourly represented employees in California were covered by agreements that expired 2026-01-31 and continue only "subject to a 24-hour termination notice by either party" while successor agreements are negotiated. These cover the assets earning the highest crack spread in MPC's system, which also strengthens the union's position. Rolling extensions usually resolve without a stoppage, but this is a dated, unresolved item.

**4. U.S.-Iran conflict is two-sided.** MPC's Q1 10-Q newly names the "U.S.-Iran conflict" among forward-looking risk factors. A resolution plus restoration of Russian refining capacity would compress cracks quickly. Conversely, escalation toward Gulf energy infrastructure extends the windfall. Neither outcome is forecastable, and the position carries the full torque of item 1 in both directions.

**5. Accounting and structural noise.** Undesignated derivatives produced a $909M Q1 loss and will keep reported results diverging from cash economics. The SPR exchanges — ~9.7M barrels received in Q2 2026 against ~11.8M barrels owed back in 2028, secured by $726M of letters of credit, accounted for as forward-crude-indexed derivatives — add a 2028 physical obligation ~21.6% larger than received (worth ~$147M at $70/bbl crude, ~$210M at $100/bbl) plus two years of mark-to-market noise in cost of revenues.

**6. MLP tax treatment.** MPC's 16.2% effective rate depends materially on MPLX's partnership status, which the 10-K flags as subject to legislative change, possibly retroactively.

**7. Impairment and litigation.** $11,993M of goodwill and intangibles against $16,753M of parent equity, with the 10-K noting prior significant goodwill impairments. Climate suits in Delaware and Multnomah County with liability not estimable; New York and Vermont climate superfund statutes under challenge; the Tesoro High Plains Pipeline BIA matter (originally ~$187M assessed, ~$4M paid) still in litigation with a segment shut down; $371M of environmental remediation accruals.

**8. MPLX as the dividend's foundation.** MPLX grew debt from roughly $21B to $26B in fifteen months while raising distributions ~12.5%, buying back units and more than doubling quarterly capex. Legal non-recourse protects MPC's balance sheet, not its cash flow: an MPLX distribution cut would directly impair the $2.79B stream covering MPC's dividend. The disclosed facts — fee-based contracts, minimum volume commitments, stable BBB/Baa2 — support the benign reading today.

**Verification gap.** All Q2 2026 figures come from an Associated Press auto-generated snapshot and aggregators, corroborated by the earnings-surprise record but not by any SEC filing. The reported "beat" compares an apparently GAAP per-share figure against a four-analyst Zacks adjusted estimate and should not be cited as a clean adjusted-EPS beat. Segment detail, cash flow, buyback dollars and guidance for the quarter are unverified. A 2026-06-29 Item 8.01 8-K could not be retrieved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.8% against 32.7% one-year realized volatility (87th percentile within the current chain) in a backwardated term structure — 47.8% at 16 days versus 38.7% at 135 days — prices imminent event risk rather than a directional view. Put/call skew is balanced at −0.3% near-term, widening to −4.4% at six months, and put/call open interest of 0.38 is call-tilted. ATM IV rose 4.7 points since the July 19 snapshot while the price fell 1.8%, and P/C open interest rose from 0.22 to 0.38. The ±8.3% implied move on 16 days points to the near-term event window as the priced catalyst. Interpretation: the market is paying up for near-term optionality in both directions and is not positioned for a one-sided outcome. For a fundamental holder this argues against paying elevated near-term premium and, given IV is rich to realized, favors selling upside calls over buying protection for anyone seeking to monetize the position.

Short interest of 2.5% of float and 2.6 days to cover is negligible — no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $380 (+23.8%)

Refining tightness proves structural rather than event-driven: no meaningful global capacity additions this decade, permanent impairment of Russian refining output, continued California exits, and MPC's own view that demand growth outpaces net capacity change through 2030. FY2027 delivers $32-35 rather than $27.77, and the market pays 11-12x. The $8.63B authorization is executed at pace, taking share count below 265M and permanently raising per-share earnings power roughly 10%. MPLX's Permian and Marcellus program converts to distribution growth, lifting the parent's fee-income stream above $3B annually. The West Coast CBA renews without disruption and California's five-year forbearance on margin caps holds. Management's four-relative-of-six performance metrics mean the windfall does not inflate incentive payouts, preserving the alignment that produced a 43% share-count reduction.

### Bear Case — $190 (−38.1%)

The U.S.-Iran conflict resolves and Russian refining capacity is restored. The blended crack retraces toward $12-15/bbl, removing $15B+ of annualized R&M EBITDA and taking FY2027 EPS to $12-15. The market applies a 12-14x multiple to trough earnings, which is where refiners trade at the bottom, and the stock finds $150-190 — the low analyst target is $186. Compounding factors: a windfall profits tax or a revived California margin cap adopted in response to record profits and high pump prices; a West Coast work stoppage on 24-hour notice at the highest-margin assets; goodwill impairment on the $1.09B of Permian midstream additions if producer activity slows; and a buyback executed near peak price and peak earnings — MPC repurchased at ~$148 average in Q1 2025 and ~$213 in Q1 2026, and the new authorization arrived with the stock near highs. Rising interest expense ($1,276M FY2025 versus $839M FY2024) persists regardless of margins.

### Base Case — $325 (+5.9%)

Cracks moderate from Q2's extremes but settle above the 2015-2019 norm on genuine capacity scarcity. FY2026 lands near consensus $39, FY2027 near $28. Applying 11.0x to FY2027 consensus gives $305; crediting partial buyback accretion (a ~7% weighted-average share-count benefit) gives roughly $325. MPLX distributions cover the dividend 2.4x throughout. The equity compounds through share retirement rather than multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should own the position through the cycle and let the buyback work. New capital should wait. At $307.03 the expected value across our scenario weights (25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear) is approximately $298, and the ratio of 5.9% base-case upside to 38.1% bear-case downside is not a risk/reward we would underwrite. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD on MPC, and the overall score is unchanged from July at 5.6 — but the composition has shifted materially. Financial health and confirmed earnings power improved (forward multiple compressed from ~10.3x to 7.9x on FY2026 with the price roughly flat), while risk asymmetry worsened as the political windfall channel became live and the near-term event window arrived. Those offset.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price in the low $250s** (approximately 9x FY2027 consensus), which restores double-digit upside to base case with the same fundamentals — a decline at unchanged estimates improves the case rather than weakening it.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of blended 3-2-1 above $22/bbl accompanied by an FY2027 consensus above $32**, evidencing that elevated cracks are structural rather than event-driven, combined with confirmation that MPC has deployed at least $4B of the $8.63B authorization.
3. **Explicit political de-risking** — California CEC formally closing the AB X2-1 minimum-inventory proceeding without adverse requirements, plus a signed West Coast collective bargaining agreement removing the 24-hour termination exposure.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Enactment or formal proposal of a federal windfall profits tax, or CEC action to implement a maximum gasoline refining margin with penalties.** This converts a cyclical peak into a permanently capped one.
2. **FY2027 consensus falling below $20** (from $27.77) alongside a blended 3-2-1 below $13/bbl, which would put fair value near $200 on a 10x multiple.
3. **An MPLX distribution cut or leverage-driven capex deferral**, which would break the 2.4x dividend coverage that is the most durable element of the structure. Watch MPLX debt above $28B against decelerating distribution growth.
4. **Buyback suspension at elevated prices while capex rises**, which would signal management sees the margin environment ending and remove the primary per-share value mechanism.

**Reassessment timeline.** Nearest hard catalysts: the Q2 2026 10-Q, which will verify segment detail, cash flow, buyback dollars and share count (all currently unverified); the near-term event window now open, which the options market prices at ±8.3% over 16 days; the Northwind purchase-price-allocation finalization by 2026-08-29; and the Q3 print in late October against a next-quarter consensus of $15.50 that has risen 106.7% in 90 days and now embeds near-Q2 profitability. Formal reassessment at the Q3 report or on any of the trigger conditions above.

## Conclusion

MPC is a well-financed, scale refiner with genuinely differentiated capital allocation — a 43% share-count reduction in four years, $8.63B of remaining authorization equal to 9.6% of shares, a dividend covered 2.4x by fee-based midstream distributions, parent net debt of just $6.6B against $5.6B of liquidity, and committed facilities extended to 2031. Compensation design is unusually appropriate for a cyclical, with four of six performance measures explicitly relative, meaning a sector-wide windfall should not inflate long-term incentive payouts.

None of that resolves the central problem. Approximately 100% of the variance in MPC's earnings comes from a crack spread that has roughly doubled since January on a combination of structural scarcity and identifiable transitory shocks, at $1,125M of annual EBITDA per dollar per barrel. The stock trades at 11x both FY2027 consensus and normalized post-buyback earnings power, at a full historical ~$19,800/bpd on the refining business, above the consensus mean target, with active sell ratings on the sheet and a backwardated 87th-percentile implied volatility surface pricing an imminent binary. Record profitability plus high pump prices adds a specific, dated political channel that the multiple does not appear to discount, alongside 700 California employees working under agreements terminable on 24 hours' notice at the system's highest-margin assets.

Own it if you own it. The buyback will do the compounding, and the MPLX distribution stream insulates the dividend from whatever the cycle does. Do not add here. **HOLD**, with a base case of $325, a bull case of $380, a bear case of $190, and a preferred entry in the low $250s.
