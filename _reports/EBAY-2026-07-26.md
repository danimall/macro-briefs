**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

eBay broke a decade-long stagnation. Revenue grew 8% in 2025 to $11.10B, its best year in the dataset, driven by a deliberate pivot to higher-margin "Focus Categories" (trading cards, motors parts, luxury, refurbished, sneakers) and a first-party advertising business that grew 22% to $1.99B, now 18% of revenue. GMV rose 7% to $79.6B and the take rate expanded to 13.94% from 13.77%. This operating turnaround at a business that had been written off is the reason eBay became attractive enough to draw a hostile bid.

The stock no longer trades purely on fundamentals. Since early May 2026, Ryan Cohen and GameStop have run an escalating takeover campaign: an unsolicited ~$56-57B cash-and-stock offer at roughly $125/share, rejected by eBay's board on May 12 as "neither credible nor attractive," followed by Cohen building a ~9.8-10% stake to become the largest shareholder. The deal's financing depends on a non-binding ~$20B TD Securities commitment conditional on the combined entity reaching investment grade, an arrangement viewed skeptically (Michael Burry exited GME over the debt math; Polymarket implied ~14% deal odds). At ~$109 versus a $125 bid the board calls non-credible, the stock embeds partial deal optionality with real downside if the bid fully collapses.

We rate EBAY a HOLD. The fundamental business justifies something in the mid-$90s to low-$100s on ~$6.12 current-year and ~$6.74 next-year EPS at a broadline-retail multiple; the current price carries a takeover premium of roughly $10-15/share for an event that is more likely than not to fail. The two theses point in opposite directions: a collapsed bid resets the stock lower, a credible tender or negotiated deal resets it higher, and neither has resolved. This is an uncertain HOLD. Consumer Discretionary is structurally the weakest sector for selection alpha in our framework, which raises the bar for any long here beyond a clean fundamental story.

## Company Overview

eBay operates one of the world's largest third-party online marketplaces, connecting ~135 million active buyers with sellers across 190+ markets and facilitating ~$80B of GMV in 2025 across 2.5 billion live listings. It reports as a single segment. The model is asset-light: eBay holds no inventory, earning a take rate on GMV via marketplace fees plus first-party advertising and shipping fees. This produces high margins and strong cash conversion without the working-capital and logistics burden of a 1P retailer like Amazon.

Under CEO Jamie Iannone (since 2020), eBay repositioned from a generic auction site into an "enthusiast" marketplace built around categories where unique, hard-to-source inventory creates real differentiation: collectibles (especially trading cards, with a PSA grading partnership), motors parts and accessories, luxury, refurbished goods, apparel, and sneakers. Trust and authentication programs (Authenticity Guarantee, eBay Refurbished, Money Back Guarantee) underpin the strategy. Recent M&A extends it: the ~$1.2B Depop acquisition (Gen-Z fashion recommerce, expected to close Q2 2026) and Caramel (automotive transactions, 2025).

The competitive setup is asymmetric. eBay's moat is genuine in used, collectible, and authenticated goods, but it lacks the ecosystem lock-in, logistics, and marketing scale of Amazon, Alibaba, and Meta's Facebook Marketplace, and it competes with specialists (Whatnot, StockX, Vinted, Poshmark) in individual categories.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue reacceleration is the core story:
- Revenue: $9.80B (2022) → $10.11B (2023) → $10.28B (2024, +2%) → **$11.10B (2025, +8%)**
- GMV: $73.2B (2023) → $74.7B (2024) → **$79.6B (2025, +7%)**
- Advertising: +22% to **$1.99B** (18% of revenue) vs. Marketplace +5%
- Quarterly revenue accelerated through 2025 ($2.59B Q1 → $2.97B Q4)

Advertising is the highest-quality, fastest-growing line and the margin engine. Net income was $2.03B (18.3% net margin), with ROE of 44% and ROA of 11.5%. The ROE figure is flattered by a shrinking equity base rather than pure operating strength.

Operating margin compressed to 20.5% from 22.5% in 2024. Roughly half of that is non-recurring: +$61M in legal accruals and +$91M in senior-leader-transition and restructuring costs. The rest reflects higher shipping-program and promoted-listings costs. Headline net income is noisy historically because of divested equity stakes: 2023's $2.77B was inflated by a $1.78B unrealized Adevinta gain, and 2022's net loss reflected Adevinta write-downs. Adevinta, Adyen, and Gmarket were all monetized in 2024, so go-forward earnings are cleaner and more operating-driven, which is a positive for forward visibility.

Cash flow is solid: operating cash flow of $2.0B (down from $2.4B, mostly a +$685M cash-tax swing) and FCF of $1.43B (12.9% margin). eBay returns essentially all of it: $2.5B buybacks plus $531M dividends in 2025 ($3.0B, ~6% of market cap), with share count down from 558M (2022) to 459M (2025), an ~18% reduction. The board added $2.0B to the buyback authorization in February 2026 and raised the dividend to $0.31/quarter.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. The Altman Z-Score of 1.67 nominally sits in the "distress" zone, but this is a financial-engineering artifact, not solvency risk. Aggressive buybacks shrank equity from $6.4B (2023) to $4.6B (2025) while retained earnings stand at $39.4B and D/E is 2.82. Against that, eBay holds $4.8B cash and investments, generates $1.4B FCF, carries $6.8B of well-laddered fixed-rate senior notes with ~9x interest coverage ($2.28B operating income vs. $246M interest), and has a $2.0B undrawn revolver. Piotroski of 4/5 confirms fundamentally sound quality. The thin equity cushion is a deliberate choice, and it is exactly what makes eBay attractive as a leveraged-buyout target.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three durable legs. First, Focus Categories are structurally faster-growing and higher-margin than the legacy marketplace, and they are where GMV growth is now concentrated; unique inventory in collectibles and used goods is a real moat that scaled generalists cannot easily replicate. Second, first-party advertising is the highest-quality revenue line and continues to expand take rate and margin per transaction. Third, the Depop acquisition adds a younger, recommerce-native demographic that eBay has historically struggled to reach.

Consensus estimates are stable-to-modestly-rising: current-year EPS of $6.12 (up 1.4% over 90 days), next-year $6.74 (up 1.8% over 90 days), implying ~10% forward earnings growth aided by continued buybacks. The 30-day revisions are flat to slightly negative (-0.2% to -0.4%), so momentum has plateaued rather than accelerated. This is not the >30% revision velocity that would flag a strong inflection buy signal; it is a company that has already re-rated on a real but now largely priced-in turnaround. The earnings surprise record is consistent (3 beats and 1 in-line over four quarters, beats of 3.1-3.6%), supporting reasonable confidence in the consensus but not the kind of visibility that would justify a growth premium.

The two structural growth risks are real and worth weighting. Tariff and de minimis changes (US exemption eliminated 2025, EU €150 exemption removed July 2026 with a €3/parcel fee, UK likely to follow) directly pressure eBay's cross-border China/Japan export flows, which the company disclosed caused increased canceled orders in 2025. AI-driven search disintermediation threatens both traffic acquisition (eBay depends on Google referrals) and the advertising model (if buyers bypass on-platform search). These are genuine headwinds to the durability of the current growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$109, EBAY trades at ~25x trailing EPS of $4.26 and ~17.9x current-year consensus / ~16.2x next-year. The trailing figure overstates the multiple because 2025 EPS absorbed the non-recurring legal and restructuring charges and the earlier divested-investment noise; the forward multiple is the cleaner lens.

Against Broadline Retail peers, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 20.7, with wide dispersion: Amazon at 28.9x, Etsy at 28.0x, versus distressed physical retailers (Macy's 10.3x, Kohl's 7.3x). eBay's ~17.9x forward is a discount to the marketplace-model peers (AMZN, ETSY) and a premium to the melting physical-retail names, which is appropriate given a genuine marketplace with a re-accelerating top line but slower growth and more structural exposure (tariffs, AI search) than Amazon.

Stripping out the takeover premium, a fundamentals-only fair value on the same EPS at 15-17x lands in the mid-$90s to ~$105, roughly where the stock traded through much of late 2025 and early 2026 before Cohen's bid. The current $109 embeds a modest deal premium. The rejected offer at ~$125 sets a ceiling that the market does not fully believe (the stock trades ~13% below it). Analyst consensus mean is $110.76 with a median of $114 and a $67-$135 range; the low end reflects the downside if the takeover collapses and the stock reverts to fundamentals, the high end approximates the bid.

Our valuation lessons argue against treating a moderate multiple as a buy signal on its own; "cheap" has been anti-predictive, and eBay is not cheap here anyway. There is no independent catalyst that clearly makes the market wrong at $109. The takeover optionality cuts both ways, and the fundamental multiple is fair rather than compelling. We score valuation modestly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

eBay's position is best characterized as a defensible niche within a structurally challenged category. It is not the leader in general e-commerce (Amazon) or C2C classifieds (Facebook Marketplace), and it competes with well-funded specialists across individual verticals. The 10-K itself acknowledges "barriers to competition are low" and that eBay "cannot match the resources of all competitors."

Where the trajectory is improving: Focus Categories are outgrowing the rest of the marketplace, and the authentication/trust programs plus unique inventory in collectibles and used goods create differentiation scaled generalists struggle to replicate. Advertising share-of-wallet is rising. Where the trajectory is at risk: AI-agent product discovery is the defining long-term industry question, threatening both eBay's Google-dependent traffic and the on-platform advertising engine that has driven the recent margin expansion. eBay's own filings concede its AI response may be "slower and less efficient than certain competitors."

Net, the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving in its niche but faces a credible structural disruption vector that the market has not fully resolved. Competitive position has been an unreliable and recently anti-predictive dimension in our framework, so we weight it lightly and hold it near neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the takeover situation itself, which introduces binary, asymmetric price outcomes not tied to operations. A collapsed bid resets the stock toward fundamental fair value (mid-$90s to low-$100s), a downside of roughly 10-15% from current levels. A credible escalation (tender offer, proxy fight victory, or negotiated deal) resets it toward or above $125. The base case is that the bid fails, but Cohen's stake and his stated intent not to stop keep the situation live and unpredictable.

Operational risks: (1) tariff/de minimis regime shifts directly pressuring cross-border GMV, with the EU change landing July 2026; (2) AI search disintermediation threatening traffic and advertising; (3) intense asymmetric competition; (4) consumer-discretionary macro sensitivity, relevant given macro pressure (inflation, rates, FX) that weighed on its three largest markets in 2025; (5) regulatory exposure (EU DSA fines up to 6% of global revenue, active German inquiry, EU AI Act, the $59M 2024 DOJ Controlled Substances settlement, and $61M new legal accruals in 2025). The leveraged capital structure, while not a solvency issue, leaves a thin equity cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is complacent given the live takeover battle. Near-term ATM IV of 33.5% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 39.1% (cheap, -5.6% spread) and at the 1st percentile of its own chain, a sharp collapse from 55.0% at the prior snapshot in mid-June, indicating the market has bled out the acute event premium. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.9%), put/call volume ratio is a low 0.55, and the term structure is in contango (near-term 33.5% < long-term 41.4%). The implied nearest-expiry move is only ±3.7%. The 1-month tenor shows a spike to 54.5% IV with -7.6% put skew, which is the one place the market is pricing a potential near-term catalyst. The overall read: the options market is not currently pricing an imminent deal resolution or a sharp downside, and low near-term IV makes protective positioning inexpensive for anyone holding the stock. Short interest is modest at 3.2% of float (4.3 days to cover), so no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$125-135).** The takeover thesis validates: Cohen's escalation forces a negotiated deal or a credible tender at or above the $125 bid, or a proxy contest succeeds and the market prices in a control premium. Independently, the fundamental turnaround continues: Focus Categories and advertising sustain high-single-digit revenue growth, margin normalizes as 2025's non-recurring charges roll off, and buybacks compound EPS toward $6.74+. A standalone re-rate to ~20x forward on continued execution supports the low-$130s even without a deal.

**Bear Case (~$85-95).** The bid collapses fully (Burry's debt-math skepticism proves correct, financing fails to materialize, institutional holders side with the board), and the stock reverts to fundamental fair value with the takeover premium removed. Layered on that, tariff/de minimis drag intensifies through the July 2026 EU change, AI search disintermediation begins to visibly pressure traffic and advertising, and consumer-discretionary macro pressure weighs on discretionary GMV. That combination pulls the multiple to 14-15x forward on flattening estimates.

**Base Case (~$100-108).** The bid ultimately fails but Cohen's presence provides a soft floor and keeps activist optionality alive; the fundamental business grows earnings ~10% on continued advertising strength and buybacks, offsetting modest tariff and macro drag. The stock holds a fair multiple with residual event optionality, roughly where it trades today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the fundamental and event theses genuinely conflict, and neither has resolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Takeover situation resolves favorably: a credible, financed tender offer or negotiated agreement at ≥$120, OR the bid collapses cleanly and the stock overshoots to the low-$90s, creating a fundamentals-only entry below ~15x forward EPS.
- Estimate revision velocity re-accelerates (current-year EPS revisions turn +5% or more over 90 days) alongside evidence advertising growth is sustaining above 20%, confirming the turnaround is durable rather than plateauing.
- Clear evidence that tariff/de minimis and AI-search headwinds are less damaging than feared (GMV growth holds high-single-digits through the July 2026 EU change).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The bid fully terminates AND fundamentals deteriorate simultaneously: GMV growth decelerates below ~3%, advertising growth drops below 10%, or tariff drag drives visible cross-border GMV declines.
- Estimate revisions turn decisively negative (current-year EPS cut >5% over 90 days).
- Material evidence of AI-search traffic erosion showing up in active-buyer or engagement metrics.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the takeover situation's evolution (any tender materials, SC 13D amendments, or a proxy timeline), the EU de minimis change in July 2026, and the next quarterly earnings release. The event overhang should resolve within 6-12 months.

## Conclusion

eBay has earned the operating credibility it lacked for years: 8% revenue growth, a 22% advertising engine, expanding take rate, and a cleaner go-forward earnings base after divesting its equity stakes. That transformation is what made it a takeover target, and Ryan Cohen's escalating campaign now sits on top of the fundamentals, distorting the price signal. At ~$109 the stock embeds a modest premium for a bid the board and most observers deem non-credible, leaving downside toward the mid-$90s if the deal fully collapses and upside toward $125 only if Cohen forces a resolution most of the market doesn't expect.

The score trajectory has drifted down (6.4 → 6.0) with valuation and management quality declining as the takeover situation clouded the picture; our current read holds at 5.85 overall. The fundamental business is a fair-value HOLD; the event overlay is a low-probability coin flip. Neither justifies a directional conviction call, and in a structurally weak-alpha sector, the burden of proof for a long is higher than the current setup meets. We recommend HOLD, with a bias to reassess when the takeover resolves in either direction; a clean collapse to the low-$90s would be the more actionable entry.
