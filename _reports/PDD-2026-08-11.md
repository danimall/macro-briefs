## Executive Summary

PDD Holdings has become two businesses trading as one security: a marketplace franchise whose economics are visibly eroding, and an investment portfolio worth roughly half the market capitalization. The first has deteriorated fast. Revenue growth fell from +59.0% in FY2024 to +9.7% in FY2025, and the highest-margin line (online marketing services, the Pinduoduo ad-auction engine) grew just +5.3% in Q4 2025 and **+2.5% in Q1 2026**. FY2025 gross margin fell 464bps to 56.28%, operating profit declined 12.7%, net income declined 11.6%, and operating cash flow declined 12.3%. Consensus has responded: FY2026 EPS estimates are down 16.9% and FY2027 down 14.2% over 90 days, following non-GAAP misses of 15.6% (December quarter) and 43.4% (March quarter).

The second business is unambiguous. At 31 March 2026 PDD held RMB123.0bn of cash, RMB313.0bn of short-term investments and RMB95.2bn of longer-dated debt securities and time deposits, against zero interest-bearing debt after the RMB5.31bn convertible was retired in Q4 2025. Book equity of US$61.4bn is roughly 98.6% financial assets. Property, equipment and software net is RMB1,153m (0.18% of total assets) and there is no inventory line at all. On a conservative cash credit (book equity as the proxy for net financial assets), enterprise value is about US$69bn against TTM operating profit of US$14.22bn, or **4.9x EV/EBIT**, with an 11.8% operating cash flow yield on market capitalization.

The gap between those two facts is the entire debate, and it is not resolvable in the bulls' favour on the available evidence. There is no dividend, no buyback, and the share count is rising (basic weighted ordinary shares 5,536.0m → 5,590.9m → 5,616.0m). Management has furnished, in its own releases, the statement that investments "will inevitably affect our financial performance," with no quantitative guidance attached. The announced RMB100bn/three-year "Xinpinmu" self-operated first-party brand initiative moves PDD toward inventory ownership and working-capital risk, a structurally lower-return model than the one that produced 27.5% operating margins in FY2024. Cheap multiples plus falling estimates plus no capital-return mechanism plus a business-model change is the composition of a value trap, not of an identified mispricing. We rate PDD **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

PDD operates the Pinduoduo domestic Chinese marketplace and the cross-border platform Temu. It is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F, not 10-K, and files no DEF 14A. Neither the FY2025 nor the Q1 2026 earnings release discloses revenue, profit, GMV, order volume or user counts by platform or geography. The only disaggregation provided is by revenue type:

| RMB m | FY2024 | FY2025 | YoY | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Online marketing services and others | 197,934 | 217,783 | **+10.0%** | 48,722 | 49,936 | **+2.5%** |
| Transaction services | 195,902 | 214,063 | +9.3% | 46,950 | 56,293 | **+19.9%** |
| **Total** | **393,836** | **431,846** | **+9.7%** | **95,672** | **106,229** | **+11.0%** |

Transaction services (commissions and fulfillment-linked fees, where Temu's economics predominantly sit) became the larger line in Q1 2026 at 53.0% of revenue, from 49.1% a year earlier. That is a shift in revenue *type*. It is not evidence of geographic diversification or reduced concentration, because PDD publishes neither a geographic split nor customer-concentration data. Any circulating Temu revenue or profitability figure is an estimate.

The absence of disclosure is itself material. There is no segment reporting, no MAU or active-buyer count, no order volume, and no forward guidance of any kind. The fastest-growing revenue line and the entire US tariff exposure sit inside a single undifferentiated caption.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025: margin compression, not a one-off

| RMB m | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 393,836 | 431,846 | +9.7% |
| Cost of revenues | 153,900 | 188,802 | **+22.7%** |
| Gross profit (derived) | 239,936 | 243,044 | +1.3% |
| Gross margin | 60.92% | **56.28%** | −464bps |
| Sales & marketing | 111,301 | 125,288 | +12.6% |
| R&D | 12,659 | 16,496 | +30.3% |
| Operating profit | 108,423 | 94,624 | **−12.7%** |
| Operating margin | 27.53% | **21.91%** | −562bps |
| Net income | 112,435 | 99,365 | −11.6% |
| Diluted EPS/ADS | RMB76.01 | **RMB67.03** | −11.8% |
| Operating cash flow | 121,929 | 106,939 | −12.3% |

Gross profit grew 1.3% on 9.7% revenue growth. The company attributes rising cost of revenues to fulfillment fees, bandwidth and server costs, and payment processing, a mix consistent with the shift toward transaction-services revenue, which carries physical fulfillment cost that advertising does not. Structural mix shift toward the lower-margin line is the mechanism, and it does not reverse on its own.

### Q1 2026: the operating beat and the earnings miss are both misleading

|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 95,672 | 106,229 | +11.0% |
| Gross profit (derived) | 54,726 | 59,336 | +8.4% |
| Gross margin | 57.20% | 55.86% | −134bps |
| Sales & marketing | (33,403) | (33,773) | **+1.1%** |
| R&D | (3,578) | (4,418) | +23.5% |
| **Operating profit** | **16,086** | **19,566** | **+21.6%** |
| Interest & investment income, net | +223 | **(632)** | −855 |
| Other income/(loss), net | +3,261 | **(2,031)** | **−5,292** |
| Pre-tax profit | 19,328 | 16,758 | −13.3% |
| Net income | 14,742 | 12,547 | **−14.9%** |
| Operating cash flow | 15,517 | 16,445 | +6.0% |

The reconciliation closes: operating profit +3,480, non-operating lines −6,050, equity investees +9, sum −2,561 against an actual pre-tax change of −2,570 (rounding). The entire pre-tax decline came from below the operating line.

That does not make the quarter good. The operating improvement was not earned on gross margin, which fell 134bps. It came from sales & marketing growing +1.1% while revenue grew +11.0%, a 313bps reduction in S&M intensity, from 34.92% to 31.79% of revenue. That is spending restraint, and it sits in tension with management's simultaneous message that it is "investing resolutely." Three explanations are available and cannot be separated from the disclosure: genuine competitive de-escalation under Beijing's "anti-involution" campaign; a lull before the RMB100bn programme ramps; or reclassification of promotional spend into cost of revenues, which grew faster than revenue (+14.5% vs +11.0%). A third-party hypothesis that coupon accounting understates true ad growth by 10+ points is unverified and should not be underwritten.

### Two durable drags on reported EPS that operations do not explain

**Tax.** Effective rate, computed as tax ÷ (pre-tax profit + share of equity investees): 15.27% (FY2024) → 17.95% (FY2025) → 25.60% (Q4 2025) → 24.70% (Q1 2026). Applying Q4 2024's 15.63% rate to Q4 2025 pre-tax profit yields net income of RMB27,832m, or +1.4% YoY, versus the reported −10.6%. If ~25% is the run rate, roughly 12% of net income is permanently gone relative to the FY2024 tax base, independent of any operating trend. The cause (expiry of PRC preferential High and New Technology Enterprise status, a profit-mix shift toward higher-tax overseas jurisdictions, or withholding taxes) requires the 20-F tax note and is unresolved.

**Non-operating earnings.** FY2025 interest and investment income of RMB25,584m equalled 21.1% of pre-tax profit. The quarterly path was +223 (Q1'25), an implied +18,988 across Q2+Q3'25, +6,373 (Q4'25), and **−632 (Q1'26)**. A line that behaves that way on a ~RMB436bn portfolio contains material fair-value movement or lumpy realized gains rather than coupon interest. The Q1 2026 non-GAAP definition adds back only share-based compensation, so these swings flow through non-GAAP net income too. "Other income/(loss), net" is equally unstable: +3,120 (FY2024), +2,727 (FY2025), then −2,031 in Q1 2026 against +3,261 a year earlier, with no explanation furnished.

### Balance sheet and cash quality

Current ratio 2.54x (from 2.21x at Dec 2024), liabilities/assets 33.6%, no interest-bearing debt. An Altman Z of roughly 5.0 confirms the obvious rather than informing anything: bankruptcy risk is not the question. A Piotroski composite of about 5/9 is more useful — positive earnings and cash flow, improving leverage and liquidity, offset by falling ROA (17.5% vs 26.4%), gross-margin contraction, declining asset turnover (0.923 → 0.761) and net share issuance. The balance sheet is strong; operating quality is deteriorating.

Reported operating cash flow is partly a function of merchant float. Payable to merchants rose RMB15.8bn in FY2025, accrued expenses RMB12.5bn, merchant deposits RMB1.2bn (RMB29.5bn of float-type increase against RMB106.9bn of OCF), while the cash definition includes restricted cash, so the offsetting balance sits inside the total. In aggregate FY2025 working capital was roughly neutral (net income plus SBC ≈ OCF), so OCF was not inflated *above* accrual earnings; the dependency is on continued GMV growth, and it reverses if growth stalls. Q1 2026 was the better quarter on this measure: float contributed **−2,408** and OCF still exceeded net income plus SBC by RMB2,374, consistent with the RMB2,031 "other loss" being largely non-cash. Capex is not separately disclosed but is almost certainly immaterial given PP&E of RMB1,153m; we do not assert FCF equals OCF. Q1 OCF must not be annualized: Q1 2025 was 14.5% of the FY2025 total against Q4's 22.6%.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue arc is the crux: +89.7% (FY2023), +59.0% (FY2024), **+9.7% (FY2025)**, +11.0% (Q1 2026). Within that, the two lines are diverging in the wrong direction: the high-margin ad line has essentially stopped growing (+2.5% in Q1 2026) while the lower-margin transaction line carries the total (+19.9%). Reuters attributed the Q1 revenue miss to "lingering economic weakness" denting domestic demand. Nothing in the disclosed record argues the domestic ad line reaccelerates in the next two quarters.

Consensus reflects this. Current-quarter EPS of US$18.35/ADS compares with US$22.07 actual a year earlier (−17%); next quarter US$18.58 vs US$21.08 (−12%). Ninety-day EPS revisions are −12.0% (current quarter), −10.3% (next quarter), −16.9% (current year) and −14.2% (next year). This is the opposite of the estimate-revision configuration that supports buying an inflection: earnings momentum is negative, not positive, and it follows a −43.4% non-GAAP miss in the March quarter.

One offsetting observation deserves weight: 30-day revisions are essentially flat (0.0%, 0.0%, −0.0%, −1.1%). The cuts appear to have been taken in the weeks after the 27 May print rather than continuing. Combined with the stock recovering roughly 28% from its 26 June low of $71.94 (52-week low), the market has partly digested the reset. That argues against adding to a bearish view here, not for initiating a bullish one.

The growth question that matters most is what Xinpinmu does to the model. RMB100bn over three years with a RMB15bn initial cash injection into a Shanghai entity running a self-operated first-party brand business is comfortably self-funded against ~RMB108bn of annual OCF. It is not comfortably compatible with 56% gross margins and zero inventory. If PDD takes inventory ownership at scale, gross margin falls further, invested capital rises from a base of RMB5.8bn of non-financial operating assets, and the return profile converges toward JD's. The confirming disclosure to watch is an inventory line appearing on the balance sheet.

## Valuation Assessment

TTM figures (Q2 2025 – Q1 2026): revenue RMB442,403m (US$64.1bn); operating profit RMB98,104m (US$14.22bn); GAAP net income RMB97,170m (US$14.09bn); non-GAAP net income RMB104,456m (US$15.14bn); OCF RMB107,867m (US$15.64bn).

At $91.76 on 1,423.4m ADS, market capitalization is **US$130.6bn**. On the reported diluted count (5,920m ordinary shares = 1,480m ADS) it is US$135.8bn; the diluted-versus-basic gap of 304m ordinary shares equals 5.41% additional shares, corresponding to 5.14% ownership dilution for existing basic holders. The composition of that gap after the convertible was retired is unverified.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E, TTM GAAP | **9.3x** (9.6x on diluted cap) |
| P/E, TTM non-GAAP | 8.6x (9.0x diluted) |
| Price/book | 2.13x |
| OCF yield on market cap | 12.0% |
| EV/TTM EBIT — conservative cash credit (EV US$69.2bn) | **4.9x** |
| EV/TTM EBIT — full cash credit (EV US$53.6bn) | 3.8x |

The profile data reports forward P/E identical to trailing P/E (9.81x), which is a feed artifact rather than a consensus-derived figure. The consensus EPS estimates in the Phase 1 data are stated on an ADS basis: FY2026 (current year) US$68.20 and FY2027 (next year) US$82.59; the 90-day revision trends on these are −16.9% and −14.2% respectively. On these figures the forward P/E is roughly 1.3x, inconsistent with the company's own TTM diluted EPS of RMB67.03/ADS (US$9.59) and the reported ~9.8x trailing multiple, indicating the vendor consensus EPS series is not stated on a comparable per-ADS earnings basis. We therefore do not compute forward P/E multiples from these figures; they are used only for the direction and magnitude of revisions.

We regard ~4.9x EV/EBIT as the defensible figure, not 3.8x. Merchant payables (RMB109.2bn) and merchant deposits (RMB17.9bn) are third-party obligations backed in part by restricted cash; PRC statutory reserves rose from RMB238m to RMB1,338m in FY2025, reflecting mandatory entity-level appropriations; and cross-border distributions from PRC subsidiaries carry withholding tax. Book equity is not distributable cash.

Is 4.9x EV/EBIT a mispricing? On the evidence, it is a joint discount for three things the market can name: an expected earnings decline that consensus now models explicitly for the current year; a structural discount on cash that has never been returned and for which no return mechanism exists; and the standard China-listing overhang whose specifics (VIE contractual control, HFCAA/PCAOB status, dual-class voting) sit in a 20-F narrative that could not be read. The bull case requires at least one of those to be wrong. None is demonstrably wrong on the evidence available.

Against that, the analyst distribution is skewed upward — mean $116.46, median $115.44, high $170.87, low $80.35 — with 22 buy-or-better ratings (4 strong buy, 18 buy) against 14 holds and one strong sell. Those targets are stale relative to the 90-day estimate cuts and are treated here as context rather than confirmation. BNP Paribas initiated at Underperform on 15 June (price target not disclosed in the source); Bank of America maintained Hold with a $140 objective on 27 May.

Absolute cheapness is real. What is absent is a catalyst that converts it into a shareholder return. We score valuation 6.0: better than fair, well short of the asymmetry that would justify new capital.

## Competitive Landscape

PDD's historic edge was demand aggregation at close to zero invested capital: RMB1.15bn of net PP&E supporting RMB442bn of TTM revenue, negative working capital, and effectively unbounded return on operating capital. Domestically it competes with Alibaba's Taobao/Tmall, JD.com and Douyin's e-commerce operation; internationally Temu competes with Amazon, Shein, TikTok Shop and local marketplaces.

The trajectory is deteriorating on the measures that matter. Ad monetization at +2.5% in a weak domestic-consumption environment suggests share of merchant ad wallet is not expanding. The announced strategic direction — first-party brands, self-operated inventory, customized manufacturing for industrial clusters, subsidized rural shipping — moves toward the capital intensity of the competitors PDD previously undercut. That may be the correct response to a saturated, deflationary domestic market and a tariffed export channel. It is not an extension of the moat that produced 27.5% operating margins.

Two external dynamics shape the ceiling. Beijing's "anti-involution" campaign against unsustainable price wars, running since the July 2024 Politburo meeting, is two-sided for PDD: it may relieve industry subsidy intensity (possibly visible in the +1.1% S&M quarter) while simultaneously pressuring platforms to lower merchant fees and fund merchant support, which compresses take rate. Co-CEO Lei Chen's language about "responsibilities as a platform enterprise" reads as accommodation, though that is an inference from tone. The US de minimis exemption for China-origin shipments ended 2 May 2025. PDD absorbed that shock during 2025 rather than facing it prospectively, but the magnitude of Temu's US exposure and the profitability of its post-de-minimis local-fulfillment model are unverifiable from disclosure. Rising fulfillment fees inside cost of revenues are consistent with a shift to pre-border inventory, but PDD publishes nothing that confirms it.

## Risk Assessment

**Domestic ad monetization stall (highest weight).** +5.3% in Q4 2025, +2.5% in Q1 2026 on the highest-margin line. This is the single number to monitor.

**Management-declared margin sacrifice with no bound.** VP of Finance Jun Liu: investments "are firm and long-term, and will inevitably affect our financial performance." No quantitative guidance in either release. Investors are being asked to fund an unspecified duration of margin compression without an ROI framework.

**Business-model conversion.** RMB100bn over three years into self-operated inventory against a balance sheet with no inventory line. The figure is corroborated across two independent secondary sources reporting earnings-call remarks but is not verified in a filed document, and no accounting treatment (capitalized versus expensed; cost of revenues versus S&M) has been disclosed.

**Tax step-up.** ~15% to ~25%, worth roughly 12% of net income versus the FY2024 base, cause unresolved.

**Non-operating earnings volatility.** 21% of FY2025 pre-tax profit from a line that turned negative in Q1 2026, not screened out by non-GAAP.

**Unquantified tariff exposure.** No geographic disclosure; Temu's US revenue share and post-de-minimis unit economics are unknown.

**Structural China-listing risks.** VIE arrangements, HFCAA/PCAOB inspection status, dual-class voting and founder economic stake are standard and material for a US-listed Chinese platform and all sit in the inaccessible 20-F narrative. We assert nothing about them; the inability to verify is itself a risk input.

**Disclosure minimalism.** No GMV, no user metrics, no segment or geographic split, no guidance. This limits the maximum confidence any analyst can carry on this name and is a permanent haircut to the multiple.

**Governance.** Co-CEO/co-chairman structure with a VP of Finance rather than a CFO. No DEF 14A, so compensation alignment is unassessable within the US framework.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.7% against 33.8% one-year realized is fair: the options market is pricing no near-term dislocation. Term structure is in contango (34.7% at one week rising to 42.5% at six months), the normal shape, with the 1-month tenor at 44.2% and a 7.6% implied move capturing the late-August Q2 print. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (−0.0%) and only mildly negative further out (−3.0% at six months); put/call volume is 0.58 and open interest 0.81, both call-tilted. Short interest is 3.8% of float with 4.0 days to cover, uncrowded. The one flagged item is 2,149 contracts of the 21 August $93 call against 301 open interest (7.1x), consistent with positioning for the earnings print rather than protection buying.

The options market is not corroborating a bear thesis, and near-term volatility at the 6th percentile of the current chain means the market is not pricing much event risk into the next two weeks. This neither confirms nor challenges the fundamental view; it does mean an investor waiting for a better entry is not paying up in the interim, and that the 1-month tenor is the cleaner place to express a view on the Q2 print than the front week.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $140 (+53%)

Anti-involution genuinely de-escalates the domestic subsidy war, and the +1.1% S&M quarter is the leading edge of a structural reduction in customer-acquisition intensity rather than a lull. Ad revenue reaccelerates as Chinese consumption stabilizes, and if the coupon-accounting hypothesis is directionally correct, underlying ad growth is already low-double-digit rather than 2.5%. Temu's localized fulfillment model proves profitable post-de-minimis, sustaining the transaction-services line near 20%. Reported earnings are seen to have declined for cyclical plus tax-driven reasons rather than structural ones, and the multiple normalizes toward 12x. A first buyback — entirely affordable against US$63bn of financial assets and US$15.6bn of annual OCF — would resolve the cash-accessibility discount and is the highest-leverage single event available to this equity. This supports Bank of America's $140 objective.

### Bear Case — $64 (−30%)

Domestic ad revenue turns flat or negative as Chinese consumption stays weak and Douyin continues taking merchant ad budget. Xinpinmu ramps: inventory appears on the balance sheet, gross margin compresses through the low 50s, and invested capital rises against returns that cannot be measured because PDD discloses nothing at segment level. The ~25% tax rate proves permanent. Non-operating income stays negative, removing a fifth of the FY2025 profit base. Reported earnings continue to fall, and the multiple compresses to around 7x on a business now understood as lower-quality: applied to US$9.59 of TTM diluted GAAP EPS gives roughly $67, and further deterioration takes it toward $64. Add continued zero capital return and any escalation of US-China listing or tariff friction, and $64 is achievable without a tail event. The 52-week low of $71.94 is not the floor if the business-model change is confirmed.

### Base Case — $100 (+9%)

Revenue grows high single digits to low double digits with mix continuing to shift toward the lower-margin transaction line. Gross margin drifts down another 100–200bps. Reported earnings hold roughly near the current TTM level, and the multiple holds around 10x on TTM non-GAAP EPS as the market continues to discount the cash and the strategic uncertainty without derating further. 10x on ~US$9.59–US$10.20 of per-ADS earnings supports a value near $100.

Probability weighting 50% base / 30% bear / 20% bull gives an expected return of roughly **+6%** from $91.76. That is a HOLD, not an ACCUMULATE: positive expected value, but with 30% downside in the adverse case, no identified catalyst inside twelve months, and a confidence level capped by seven substantive verification gaps including the entire 20-F narrative.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 2026 print, expected late August 2026 (the 28 August option tenor prices a 7.6% move). The FY2026 20-F, expected April 2027, is the document that resolves the tax cause, the investment-income composition, and the Xinpinmu accounting treatment.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Online marketing services revenue growth returning above 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the domestic ad franchise is not structurally impaired.
2. Announcement of a buyback or dividend of meaningful scale — anything above roughly US$5bn annually (≈4% of market capitalization, ≈8% of financial assets) would materially change the cash-accessibility discount and the shareholder-alignment assessment.
3. Gross margin stabilizing within 50bps sequentially for two quarters alongside disclosure of Xinpinmu's accounting treatment, or a price near $70–75 (near the 52-week low), which would restore the asymmetry independent of any fundamental improvement.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. An inventory line appearing on the balance sheet at scale (above roughly RMB10bn) without accompanying segment disclosure of the self-operated business's revenue and margin — this converts the valuation question from cheapness to business quality.
2. Online marketing services revenue growth turning negative YoY.
3. Gross margin falling below 53%, or the current-year and next-year consensus being cut a further 10%+ from current levels.
4. Further large below-the-line losses in "other income/(loss), net" without explanatory disclosure, which would confirm that a fifth of the historic profit base is neither repeatable nor auditable from outside.

**Position guidance for existing holders.** Maintain; do not add. The roughly 28% recovery from the 26 June low of $71.94 has consumed most of the gap to our base case while none of the four unresolved items (tax cause, investment-income composition, Xinpinmu economics, Temu geography) has been clarified. Sizing should reflect that maximum achievable analytical confidence on this name is structurally limited by the issuer's disclosure practice.

## Conclusion

PDD is inexpensive on every conventional metric and the balance sheet is close to unimpeachable: zero debt, US$63bn of cash and securities, a 2.54x current ratio, 12% cash-flow yield, and 4.9x EV/operating profit on a conservative cash credit. Those facts are why the stock deserves attention. They are not sufficient for a buy.

The reasons the multiple is compressed are identifiable and, on current evidence, correct. Revenue growth fell from 59% to under 10% in a single year. The highest-margin revenue line has stopped growing. Gross margin has fallen 464bps and then another 134bps. A tax-rate step-up has permanently removed roughly 12% of net income relative to the FY2024 base. A fifth of FY2025 pre-tax profit came from a portfolio line that turned negative in Q1 2026 and is not excluded from non-GAAP. Consensus has cut current-year and next-year earnings by 17% and 14% in ninety days after a 43% non-GAAP miss. Management has committed RMB100bn to converting an infinite-ROIC marketplace into something that owns inventory, with no disclosed return framework and no guidance of any kind. And US$63bn of financial assets has produced not one dollar of dividends or buybacks while the share count rises.

A cheap stock with negative estimate momentum, a deteriorating competitive trajectory, a management-declared willingness to sacrifice reported profitability for an unspecified period, and no mechanism by which minority holders capture the balance-sheet value is a HOLD. We would become constructive on either evidence that the domestic advertising franchise has stabilized, a capital-return programme of real scale, or a price near $70–75 that pays us for the unresolved questions.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $100 (+9%), bull $140 (+53%), bear $64 (−30%). Reassess at the late-August Q2 print.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the vendor consensus EPS series (FY2026 US$68.20 / FY2027 US$82.59) is not stated on a per-ADS earnings basis comparable to the company's TTM diluted EPS (RMB67.03/US$9.59 per ADS), so the original report's forward P/E of "9.3x FY2026 / 7.7x FY2027" and the associated bull/base/bear EPS anchors and price targets could not be reproduced and were removed; the scenario values now rest on TTM earnings and multiple assumptions rather than the disclosed consensus EPS levels.
