**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Helmerich & Payne has transformed itself over the past 18 months from a relatively clean-balance-sheet U.S. onshore land-drilling pure-play into a levered, globally diversified driller. The January 2025 ~$2.0B all-cash acquisition of KCA Deutag doubled the international footprint, added Middle East land drilling and an asset-light offshore management business, and brought BENTEC manufacturing. The strategic logic of diversifying away from the mature U.S. shale market into higher-visibility international and capital-light offshore revenue is defensible. The near-term financial consequences have been severe: FY2025 operating income collapsed to $3.3M from $452M, the company posted a net loss of $163.7M (-$1.66 EPS), and Q2 FY26 produced a further -$58.6M loss with an adjusted figure that badly missed consensus.

The investment question is whether the integration losses are a temporary trough that forward estimates are correctly anticipating, or a structural impairment that the market has not fully digested. The evidence cuts both ways, which is why we land at HOLD rather than either extreme. On the constructive side: management is deleveraging aggressively (the $400M acquisition term loan was fully repaid by April 2026, the $950M revolver is undrawn, covenants are met, operating cash flow remains positive at $543M FY25), backlog grew to $8.3B on a 5-year offshore O&M extension, Saudi rig suspensions are reversing (7 rigs resuming), and next-year EPS estimates have been revised up ~30% over 90 days to $1.32. On the cautious side: the consolidated P&L is in the red, D/E sits near 2.4x, the company simultaneously lost its CEO and CFO mid-integration, a material weakness in financial controls was disclosed (since remediated), and insiders have sold one-directionally including founder-family selling near the cyclical low.

The stock has rallied roughly 150% off its 52-week low of $15.04 to $39.38, sitting near its 52-week high of $41.82. At ~30x normalized next-year EPS of ~$1.32, the stock is neither cheap nor obviously overpriced; it prices in a meaningful chunk of the recovery the bull case requires. With consensus targets (mean $40.80, median $43.00) sitting at or modestly above spot and analyst sentiment improving, the risk/reward is balanced. We see no edge in pressing a directional bet here.

## Company Overview

Helmerich & Payne is a Tulsa, Oklahoma-based drilling-solutions company, historically the leading U.S. onshore land driller, known for its high-spec FlexRig® fleet and an integrated combination of proprietary rig hardware, automation software, and digital services. Following the KCA Deutag acquisition, H&P operates three reporting segments:

- **North America Solutions** (203 rigs at March 2026): the legacy core of U.S. onshore daywork drilling, concentrated in the Permian. This remains the profit engine, generating ~$111M segment operating income in Q2 FY26 even as activity softened.
- **International Solutions** (130 rigs): land drilling in the Middle East (Saudi Arabia, Oman), Latin America (Argentina, Colombia), Europe and Africa, dramatically expanded by KCA Deutag. This segment posted a -$99.6M operating loss in Q2 FY26.
- **Offshore Solutions** (4 platform rigs): primarily asset-light management contracts (customer owns the rig) in the North Sea, Norwegian Sea, Caspian and U.S. Gulf. Revenue grew 15% in Q2 FY26 and the segment is the structural bright spot.
- **"Other":** BENTEC drilling-equipment manufacturing/engineering plus wholly-owned captive insurers.

The total fleet shrank from 367 rigs (September 2025) to 337 (March 2026) as management scraps low-spec rigs to right-size capacity. The North American moat in technology leadership and scale in high-spec AC rigs is intact. The strategic shift has traded that clean domestic profile for global diversification with meaningful national-oil-company (NOC) exposure.

## Financial Analysis

The KCA Deutag deal turned a profitable company into a consolidated loss-maker through the integration period.

**FY2025 (year ended September 30, 2025):** Revenue rose 34% to $3.68B (roughly $1.0B from KCAD), but operating income collapsed to $3.3M from $452M, and the company swung to a net loss of $163.7M (-$1.66 EPS) from +$344M (+$3.43) in FY24. The drivers were largely non-cash and one-time: a $194M goodwill impairment (full write-off of International Solutions and BENTEC goodwill, triggered by the share-price decline), $54.7M transaction costs, $12.1M restructuring, and sharply higher D&A ($625M, including purchase-accounting intangible amortization) and interest expense ($108M vs. $29M).

**Q2 FY26 (quarter ended March 31, 2026):** Revenue fell 8.2% YoY to $932M, with a net loss of -$58.6M (-$0.59 EPS); the adjusted loss of -$0.38 missed badly. The six-month loss was -$155M. North America revenue fell 13.7% YoY on lower activity (NA average active rigs ~136 vs. ~149) and pricing. International posted the -$99.6M segment operating loss on full-period Saudi suspensions, purchase-accounting D&A, and held-for-sale impairments. Six-month impairments totaled ~$129M (scrapping 30 NA + 3 offshore rigs, plus an international rig write-down).

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** Operating cash flow remains positive ($543M FY25; ~$219M 1H FY26) and covers capex, though FCF is thin (~$117M FY25). Management is deleveraging decisively: total debt is down to ~$2.0B, the $400M acquisition term loan was fully repaid by April 2026, the $950M revolver is undrawn, and FY26 capex is guided down to $280–320M from $426M. The non-core Utica Square shopping center was sold for ~$129M net post-quarter. The company is in compliance with all covenants (max 55% funded-debt-to-cap).

**Distress signals in context:** The Altman Z-Score of 1.54 (distress zone) and D/E of ~2.4x look alarming in isolation, but both are heavily distorted by impairment-driven equity erosion plus the recent debt load. This is an integration/earnings problem, not an imminent solvency crisis: the company has positive operating cash flow, ample undrawn liquidity, and is meeting covenants. Leverage nonetheless raises the stakes materially if oil activity weakens.

**Capital return reset:** The dividend was cut from $1.94 (FY23) and $1.68 (FY24) to $1.00 (FY25), with the supplemental dividend suspended to fund the deal. The current base is $0.25 quarterly (~2.6% yield), with no buybacks in FY26. The dividend is covered but the reset signals that capital allocation is firmly subordinated to balance-sheet repair.

## Growth Analysis

The forward signals point up, which is the central reason we cannot maintain a bearish stance. Next-year EPS estimates have been revised up 22.4% over 30 days and 29.7% over 90 days to $1.32, and next-quarter estimates are up 14.5%/29.9% to $0.20. Estimate-revision velocity of this magnitude is among the strongest forward indicators available and typically precedes continued re-rating as the market catches up.

The mechanisms behind the recovery are identifiable rather than speculative:
1. **Saudi rig resumptions:** 27 rigs were suspended in Saudi Arabia through September 2025; 7 have received resumption notices (6 confirmed), lifting in-country operating rigs toward ~23 by mid-2026. Each resumption directly removes a drag on the International segment.
2. **Synergy capture:** Management targets >$50M of G&A reduction versus pro forma, and the fleet rationalization (scrapping low-spec rigs) removes loss-making capacity.
3. **Offshore backlog:** The 5-year O&M extension drove backlog to $8.3B from $7.0B, providing multi-year revenue visibility in the highest-quality, capital-light segment.

Two cautions temper the growth read. First, the current-quarter and current-year estimates are moving the other way (current-year EPS revised to roughly breakeven from positive), so the recovery is back-half-weighted and depends on integration execution that has so far missed. Second, the backlog is lower-quality than the headline: it includes roughly $2.9B of optional extensions and ~$0.5B of suspended Saudi work that can be halted without compensation. The growth thesis requires the international segment to convert from a -$99.6M quarterly loss back to profitability, which has not yet happened.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are uninformative here: the company is loss-making, so the -10.4x trailing/forward P/E reflects the trough, not a steady state. The relevant exercise is normalized earnings power. On consensus next-year EPS of ~$1.32, the stock trades at ~30x. On a fuller recovery toward $1.50+ (if Saudi rigs fully resume and synergies land), the multiple compresses toward 26x. Neither figure is absurd for a deleveraging cyclical with rising estimates, but neither offers asymmetric upside; the recovery is substantially priced.

Peer comparison is muddy because the offshore drilling and land drilling cohort trades across a wide multiple range: PTEN at -35.9x (also loss-making), VAL at 6.5x, NE at 32.5x. The peer median P/E of 32.5x and yield of 3.6% suggest H&P is roughly in line on normalized earnings and slightly light on yield (2.6% vs. 3.6%) after its dividend cut. There is no obvious mispricing in either direction.

The analyst panel is constructive and improving: 4 strong-buy / 3 buy / 8 hold / 1 sell, with a strongBuy upgrade over the trailing two months and a mean target of $40.80 / median $43.00, both at or above the current $39.38. On a stock with rising estimates and a ~30x forward multiple, consensus targets often lag, which argues against treating proximity to target as a ceiling. That supports a constructive lean but does not, on its own, manufacture the asymmetry required for a BUY: the same panel holds a meaningful hold contingent (8 of 16) and one sell, and the stock sits within 6% of its 52-week high.

Net: valuation is fair, not cheap and not stretched. This is the valuation profile of a HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

H&P remains the recognized U.S. land-drilling technology leader, benefiting from the bifurcation between high-spec AC rigs (high utilization and dayrates) and stacked low-spec rigs. The industry backdrop is E&P capital discipline, which has historically produced more stable, predictable demand, though current North American activity is soft. Management argues that sustained Middle East geopolitical risk could support drilling activity as operators hedge supply disruption.

The KCA Deutag deal repositions H&P against globally diversified peers and into asset-light offshore management, strategically sensible for diversifying away from a mature U.S. shale market, and the offshore management-contract model is structurally attractive (growing revenue, lower capital intensity, expanding backlog). The trade-off is exposure to NOC contract dynamics that lack the early-termination protections of the legacy U.S. daywork book, which weakens the quality of the acquired revenue stream. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in offshore and stable in North America, but the international segment's profitability is unproven under H&P's ownership.

## Risk Assessment

Specific, company-relevant risks beyond sector cyclicality:

1. **Saudi/NOC contract suspensions with no compensation.** A defining feature of the inherited KCA Deutag book: many contracts can be suspended or terminated without early-termination or standby payments. A single Saudi customer is ~7% of revenue and can suspend rigs. Backlog realization is therefore softer than the $8.3B headline implies. This risk is reversing at the margin (resumptions) but remains structural.

2. **Leverage plus cyclicality.** ~$2.0B of debt against a currently loss-making consolidated P&L. A downturn in oil prices or U.S. activity would pressure both cash flow and covenant headroom simultaneously.

3. **Further impairments.** After the $194M FY25 goodwill write-off and ~$129M of FY26 held-for-sale rig impairments, residual goodwill ($184M, mostly Offshore) and a large intangible base ($444M) remain exposed.

4. **Governance and integration.** A material weakness in internal control over financial reporting was disclosed in the FY25 10-K tied to the integration (reported remediated by Q2 FY26). KCA Deutag was a private company without SOX-grade controls. The simultaneous retirement of CEO John Lindsay (Raymond John Adams III named CEO March 2026) and CFO J. Kevin Vann mid-integration is a concentration of leadership change at the hardest phase.

5. **Customer and geographic concentration.** Top 3 customers = 25.6%, top 10 = 54% of revenue; Argentina currency controls (Blue Chip Swaps) and Middle East geopolitics add political risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute directional stress. Near-term ATM IV is 51.1% against 44.0% historical volatility (modestly rich, +7.1pp), with a balanced put/call skew of -0.7% and a low put/call volume ratio of 0.06. The term structure is flat (near 51.1% ≈ far 52.8%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. ATM IV fell sharply intraday (64.8% → 51.1%), consistent with post-earnings vol normalization. Short interest of 11.7% of float (7.0 days to cover) reflects meaningful institutional skepticism, but the balanced skew suggests the bearish view is largely embedded rather than building. The implied ±8.5% near-term move is unremarkable for a post-deal cyclical. Elevated short interest alongside balanced options positioning supports the view that the bear case is already partly in the price, which weighs against pressing a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$47, +19%):** Saudi rigs fully resume, synergies (>$50M G&A) land, the international segment returns to profitability, and the offshore backlog converts to durable cash flow. Normalized EPS recovers toward $1.50+, the market re-rates to a clean ~28-30x on a deleveraged balance sheet, and the dividend is eventually restored. Estimate-revision momentum continues, dragging consensus targets higher. This matches the analyst high target of $47.

**Bear Case (~$30, -24%):** North American activity stays soft, oil prices decline, Saudi resumptions stall, and further impairments hit the residual intangible/goodwill base. Leverage that looks manageable today becomes a constraint, the dividend is at risk again, and the consolidated P&L stays in the red into FY27. The stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $30.

**Base Case:** Integration grinds toward stabilization, Saudi resumptions modestly improve the international segment, and normalized EPS approaches $1.32. The stock holds in the $38-43 range, broadly in line with the consensus mean/median, as the market waits for proof that international profitability has turned. The balanced setup of real deleveraging and rising estimates against a loss-making P&L, leverage, and governance churn produces no clear directional edge from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (FQ3 FY26, expected early August 2026) is the key checkpoint, with the September fiscal year-end providing the fuller integration read. The pace of Saudi rig resumptions (targeting ~23 in-country by mid-2026) is the highest-frequency variable to monitor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- International Solutions returns to positive segment operating income (or a clear sequential narrowing of the loss), confirming integration synergies and Saudi resumptions are landing.
- Next-year EPS estimates continue rising while the stock pulls back, compressing forward P/E below ~22x, restoring the asymmetry currently absent.
- New CEO Adams articulates and begins executing a credible deleveraging-plus-capital-return roadmap, ideally accompanied by open-market insider buying (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further material impairment to the residual goodwill or intangible base, signaling the acquired assets are worth less than carried.
- Covenant headroom deterioration or a return to revolver borrowing, indicating the deleveraging story has stalled.
- Saudi resumptions reverse or additional rigs are suspended, undermining the international recovery, combined with a renewed decline in next-year estimates.
- A second dividend cut, which would signal cash-flow stress beyond what is currently visible.

## Conclusion

Helmerich & Payne sits in the uncomfortable middle of a difficult, debt-funded transformation. The bear case rests on a loss-making consolidated P&L, ~2.4x leverage, NOC contract exposure without termination protection, simultaneous CEO/CFO turnover, and one-directional insider selling, all real. The bull case rests on equally real evidence: a fully repaid term loan, an undrawn revolver, covenant compliance, positive operating cash flow, a profitable North American core, growing backlog, reversing Saudi suspensions, and next-year EPS estimates revised up ~30% in 90 days against a constructive-and-improving analyst panel with targets at or above spot.

This is the textbook profile of a stock that has already rallied where the recovery is substantially priced but not obviously over-priced. We previously carried a more bearish stance; the strength of the forward-estimate revision, the demonstrable deleveraging, and the consensus disconnect do not support a structural-impairment thesis. The loss-making P&L, leverage, governance churn, and absence of insider conviction prevent a constructive call near the high. We rate HP a HOLD: we lack the evidence for a confident directional bet in either direction and would wait for proof of international-segment profitability before pressing the recovery thesis, or for a meaningful pullback that restores asymmetry.
