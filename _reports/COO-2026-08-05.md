## Executive Summary

Cooper's reported financials no longer describe its economics. In FY2025, CooperVision produced $729.6M of segment operating income — 107% of the $682.9M consolidated total — while CooperSurgical contributed $43.4M and Corporate cost $90.1M. In Q2 FY2026 the divergence widened: CooperVision earned $215.6M on $723.5M of sales (30% margin, up from 28%), CooperSurgical lost $225.0M after a $271.6M net embryo-culture-media litigation charge, and consolidated operating income was negative $31.0M. Management has confirmed it is "advancing discussions with multiple parties interested in CooperSurgical as part of its strategic review." The investment question is what a lens pure-play is worth and what Cooper nets from the surgical business after the liability tail.

The valuation looks inexpensive on the headline metric and much less so underneath it. At $74.69 the stock trades at 16.1x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $4.63 and 14.9x FY2027's $5.00, against medtech peers generally in the high-teens to high-twenties. Consensus is a non-GAAP number that excludes roughly $199M of annual acquired-intangible amortization; GAAP diluted EPS was $1.87 in FY2025 versus $4.13 non-GAAP. On cash, FY2025 free cash flow of $433.7M against a market capitalization near $14.6B is a 3.0% FCF yield, and enterprise value of roughly $16.9B against FY2025 EBITDA of $1,060M is 15.9x. Those are fair multiples for a business guiding to 3.5–4.5% organic growth, not distressed ones.

The divestiture arithmetic does not support the simple unlock narrative. Removing CooperSurgical removes its non-GAAP earnings contribution — inflated by the amortization add-back that non-GAAP treatment restores — so a sale at $3.0B recycled entirely into buybacks is roughly neutral to slightly dilutive to non-GAAP EPS. The bull case therefore rests on multiple expansion for a cleaner, 30%-margin lens franchise, plus removal of the litigation overhang, rather than on earnings accretion. That is a plausible path with a real catalyst, and it produces a base case of $83 (+11%), but it is not the asymmetry that justifies new capital ahead of a late-August Q3 print, a pending cash litigation outflow of up to $324M, and an unprovisioned UK tax exposure on which a tribunal has already ruled against the company. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Cooper operates two segments across more than 130 countries with over 15,000 employees (52% Americas, 42% EMEA, 6% Asia Pacific).

**CooperVision** (FY2025 net sales $2,743.8M, 67% of total) is the number three or four global soft contact lens manufacturer, competing with Johnson & Johnson Vision Care, Alcon and Bausch + Lomb. The portfolio spans frequent-replacement (Biofinity, described in the 10-K as the company's highest-grossing product; Avaira Vitality), single-use (MyDay premium, clariti value), declining legacy hydrogel (Proclear, Biomedics), specialty ortho-k and scleral lenses, and MiSight — the only contact lens approved by the FDA (November 2019), China's NMPA (August 2021) and Japan's MHLW (August 2025) to slow myopia progression in children. Manufacturing sits in Belgium, Costa Rica, Hungary, Puerto Rico, the UK and the US.

**CooperSurgical** (FY2025 net sales $1,348.6M) holds more than 600 products across fertility ($524.6M: IVF media, micro-tools, donor gametes, genomics through a CLIA lab in New Jersey) and office/surgical ($824.0M: OB/GYN devices, cryostorage, and Paragard, a US-only hormone-free copper IUD regulated through FDA's drug pathway). The segment was built by acquisition, and its economics show it: FY2025 segment SG&A ran at 42% of segment sales versus CooperVision's 35%, and amortization at 13% of sales versus 1%.

No single customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in FY2025 or FY2024, though the 10-K states that key-account customers "now represent a larger proportion or concentration of our business." Fiscal Q1 (November–January) is structurally the weakest quarter for both segments.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin trajectory

| Net income | 294.2 | 392.3 | 374.9 | 52.9 |

FY2025 was a year of decelerating revenue and declining GAAP profit: revenue grew 5.1% while operating income fell 3.2% and net income fell 4.4%, on inventory and long-lived asset write-offs plus workforce-optimization severance recorded across all three reporting units.

The H1 FY2026 headline of +7.0% overstates underlying momentum. The 10-Q discloses favorable FX of approximately $28.4M in Q2 and $56.5M in H1 across CooperVision categories. On a $669.6M prior-year Q2 base, $28.4M is roughly 4.2 points of the 8% reported CooperVision growth, implying constant-currency segment growth closer to 4%. Geographically the business is bifurcating: Q2 CooperVision Americas +7%, EMEA +17% (FX-assisted), Asia Pacific **−6%**, with management citing softness in Japan. Asia Pacific was already flat in FY2025 ($555.1M vs. $553.8M), so this is a two-year deterioration in the region where myopia prevalence is highest and where MiSight received approval about a year ago.

Beneath the litigation charge, CooperSurgical's revenue trend improved: Q2 fertility +13% to $143.8M, office/surgical +4%, with H1 office/surgical held back by declining Paragard revenue.

### Cash flow and the tax distortion

FY2025 operating cash flow was $796.1M (+12.2% year over year, against falling net income), capex $362.4M, free cash flow **$433.7M** — a 10.6% FCF margin that exceeds the 9.2% GAAP net margin. H1 FY2026 OCF of $443.7M was up 55% year over year, but that improvement is not operating leverage. Three items inflate it: the $324.1M litigation accrual is non-cash in the period (management says so explicitly); net operating capital swung roughly $297M favorably versus H1 FY2025; and deferred income taxes added back $87.1M against a $90.9M total tax provision. I am not annualizing H1 FCF. A normalized full-year figure that reverses the working-capital swing sits closer to the FY2025 **$430–480M** zone before any litigation cash payment.

The tax mechanics distort every GAAP screen on this company. Deferred tax assets were $1,994.7M at April 30, 2026 — 16.0% of $12,482.3M total assets. In H1 FY2026, $87.1M of the $90.9M book tax provision appears as a non-cash deferred add-back in operating cash flow (H1 FY2025: $78.3M of $105.8M). That is consistent with a large deferred tax asset unwinding as non-cash expense while cash taxes remain far lower, which explains why the FY2025 effective rate reads 33.8% (63.2% in H1 FY2026), why ROE reads 4.6% on a goodwill- and DTA-inflated equity base, and why FCF margin exceeds net margin. The origin of the DTA is unconfirmed — the income tax footnote was not read, and the H1 ratio is flattered by a $42.8M discrete benefit on the litigation accrual — so I treat the mechanism as probable rather than established. The practical conclusion holds either way: GAAP EPS and ROE understate this company's cash generation, non-GAAP EPS overstates its accounting quality, and FCF is the correct anchor.

### Balance sheet

At April 30, 2026: cash $138.8M, total debt $2,460.2M, net debt **$2,321.4M**, against an FY2025 EBITDA proxy of $1,060.3M (operating income $682.9M + D&A $377.4M) — roughly **2.2x net leverage**. Equity $8,239.1M, but goodwill ($3,888.5M), intangibles ($1,494.3M) and the DTA ($1,994.7M) together are 59% of total assets, the signature of a serially acquisitive company. Tangible equity is $2,856.3M; excluding the DTA, $861.6M.

Liquidity is adequate. The 2024 revolver has a $2,300.0M limit with $911.7M drawn and **$1,383.0M available** to May 2029 at a 4.66% weighted-average rate. On February 3, 2026 Cooper extended $950.0M of the $1,500.0M term loan to February 2031; **$550.0M retains its December 17, 2026 maturity** — inside five months of this report, and the reason short-term debt jumped to $598.9M and working capital fell from $993.6M to $495.1M. That decline is reclassification plus the $324.8M litigation accrual moving current, not operating deterioration. Covenants were in compliance at both October 31, 2025 and April 30, 2026.

Two forward cost items: five interest rate swaps on $1.3B notional reduced interest expense by $11.5M in H1 FY2026, down from $20.1M a year earlier, with all maturities inside two years and $(19.4)M of the AOCI derivative balance expected to hit earnings over the next twelve months. FX translation is essentially unhedged — only $54.5M notional of forwards outstanding against a disclosed sensitivity of ±$35.5M of quarterly operating income per ±10% currency move.

The Altman Z-score of 2.73 sits in the grey zone, driven mechanically by asset turnover of 0.33 and EBIT/assets of 5.5% — consequences of carrying $7.38B of goodwill, intangibles and DTA against $4.09B of revenue. Covenant compliance, undrawn capacity, 2.2x leverage, growing OCF and an executed maturity extension argue this is purchase-accounting arithmetic rather than a solvency signal.

**Financial health: 6.0.** Cash conversion is genuine and leverage is moderate for a medtech company. Offsetting: a litigation cash outflow of up to ~$324M that would consume most of one year's FCF, a $550M maturity in December 2026, a decaying hedge tailwind, and a 3.0% FCF yield that leaves little cushion for error.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is total revenue of **$4.285–$4.321B** with organic growth of **3.5–4.5%**. The $4.303B midpoint implies +5.1% reported growth, so roughly 60–160bp of expected FX help. H1 delivered $2,105.6M (+7.0%), which means H2 must produce approximately $2,197M against $2,125.4M — about **+3.4%**, roughly half the H1 pace. Since the FX contribution to reported growth is modest at the guided midpoint, most of that implied deceleration is organic, consistent with Asia Pacific at −6%, Japan softness, and the Paragard decline. Coverage characterized the guidance as trimmed.

Where growth is genuinely working is mix. CooperVision toric and multifocal lenses grew 11% in both Q2 and H1 FY2026 versus sphere at +5%, and reached $364.9M of $723.5M (50.4%) of segment sales from 49.0% a year earlier. These are higher-value lenses, and the mix shift is what drove segment margin from 28% to 30%. The single-use modality shift plays to both MyDay (premium) and clariti (value). Legacy hydrogel decay is a self-inflicted drag on the sphere line that will eventually anniversary out.

MiSight is the most valuable growth option Cooper owns — a triple-jurisdiction regulatory approval in pediatric myopia control, with Japan only about a year old and largely unmonetized. Two constraints deserve weight. The FDA indication is narrow: ages 8–12 at initiation, −0.75 to −4.00 D, ≤0.75 D of astigmatism, which limits the addressable population well below the headline myopia-epidemic framing. The label is a regulatory advantage rather than a patent — competitors can pursue their own approvals — while Cooper bears the market-development cost through practitioner education, myopia specialists and direct-to-consumer campaigns.

Consensus reflects this profile: FY2026 non-GAAP EPS $4.63 (+12% on FY2025's $4.13, margin- and buyback-assisted rather than volume-driven), FY2027 $5.00 (+8%). Estimate revisions are stable rather than directional — current-year up 0.2% over 90 days, next-year down 0.5%, with current-quarter down 5.9% and next-quarter down 3.0%. The revision velocity that would signal an inflection is absent here.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Mid-single-digit organic revenue with high-single-digit EPS growth, positive mix migration and one genuine option in MiSight, set against implied H2 deceleration, a two-year decline in Asia Pacific, and CooperVision R&D down 14% in Q2 and 7% in H1 — spend reduction that flatters near-term margin.

## Valuation Assessment

Using the $74.69 basis and approximately 195.4M shares (H1 FY2026 basic weighted average), market capitalization is roughly **$14.6B** and enterprise value approximately **$16.9B**.

| Metric | COO |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus non-GAAP $4.63 | 16.1x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus non-GAAP $5.00 | **14.9x** |
| P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS $1.87 | ~40x |
| EV/FY2025 EBITDA ($1,060M) | 15.9x |
| FY2025 FCF yield ($433.7M) | 3.0% |
| PEG (14.9x / 8% FY2027 growth) | 1.86 |
| Dividend yield | none (eliminated Dec 2023) |

The forward P/E is a genuine discount to large-cap medtech and to Cooper's own historical range in the mid-twenties. But the discount is being applied to a non-GAAP number that adds back roughly $199M of annual acquired-intangible amortization (about $1.00 per share pre-tax) at a company whose growth model created those intangibles. On cash measures the stock is unremarkable: a 3.0% FCF yield and 15.9x EV/EBITDA for a business guiding to 3.5–4.5% organic growth, with a ~$324M litigation payment likely to land inside that FCF stream.

**The divestiture arithmetic, as a labeled sensitivity.** CooperSurgical carried $4,616.0M of identifiable assets at April 30, 2026 against FY2025 revenue of $1,348.6M and normalized segment operating income somewhere above the FY2024 figure of $118.3M. Fertility assets can command premium revenue multiples, but a blended 2.0–2.5x on the mixed portfolio implies proceeds of $2.7–3.4B — below carrying value, which would trigger a loss on sale or goodwill impairment. That would be non-cash; the cash proceeds are what matter.

Assume $3.0B of proceeds deployed entirely into buybacks near $80, retiring roughly 37.5M shares (about 19% of the count). Against FY2027 consensus of $5.00 on ~193M shares (~$965M of non-GAAP net income), CooperSurgical's own non-GAAP contribution is material because non-GAAP restores its 13%-of-sales amortization: FY2024 GAAP segment EBIT of $118.3M plus roughly $170M of amortization implies non-GAAP segment EBIT near $290M, or approximately $1.15–1.20 per share after tax. Removing that and shrinking the count leaves pro-forma non-GAAP EPS near **$4.65 — modestly dilutive**. At $4.0B of proceeds the arithmetic is roughly neutral. The allocation of amortization and taxes here is estimated rather than disclosed, so treat the magnitudes as directional. The conclusion is robust to the estimation error: a CooperSurgical sale is a margin and disclosure-quality event that depends on **multiple re-rating**, not earnings accretion, to create equity value. Post-divestiture margin expansion is mechanical and should not be confused with value creation.

On the sum of the parts: subtracting $2.7–3.4B of CooperSurgical value from the $16.9B enterprise value leaves CooperVision at $13.5–14.2B against FY2025 segment EBIT of $729.6M — 18–19x segment operating income before corporate costs. That is a fair multiple for a 30%-margin oligopoly participant growing roughly 4% constant-currency, not a hidden discount.

Analyst targets bracket a modest premium: low $66 (−11.6%), mean $81.21 (+8.7%), median $85 (+13.8%), high $92 (+23.2%). Sell-side sentiment improved marginally — the single sell rating moved to hold, leaving 4 strong buy, 6 buy, 6 hold. With estimates flat rather than rising, I treat the median target as an anchor rather than a floor.

**Valuation: 5.9.** A real discount on the headline forward multiple, supported by an identifiable catalyst, offset by a 3% FCF yield, a 1.86 PEG, a large non-GAAP-to-GAAP gap, and divestiture math that shifts the burden onto multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

The contact lens market is a four-player oligopoly. Cooper concedes in its own risk factors that J&J Vision Care, Alcon and Bausch + Lomb "may have substantially greater financial resources, larger research and development budgets, larger sales forces, greater market penetration and/or larger manufacturing volumes," and names increased product entries from Asia Pacific manufacturers as a further threat.

Cooper's advantage is defined in the 10-K as breadth: "high, medium and low-volume lenses made with a variety of materials for a broader range of market niches, as well as offering a wide range of lens parameters, leading to a higher rate of successful fitting for practitioners," backed by an "industry leading stock keeping unit range." That is a long-tail fitting-flexibility moat dependent on practitioner relationships, which is precisely the channel the 10-K identifies as being disintermediated: "more contact lenses are being fulfilled directly to the consumer by manufacturers and wholesalers via online platforms, telemedicine is gaining popularity and more vision correction prescriptions are being provided through online refractive exams." MiSight adds a defensible regulatory position in a category Cooper is trying to convert into standard of care.

Trajectory is mixed. Mix migration into toric and multifocal, and margin expansion from 28% to 30%, indicate improving position within the premium tier. Against that: two consecutive years of Asia Pacific weakness, CooperVision R&D declining while three larger rivals invest, and rising key-account concentration that hands negotiating leverage to consolidated optical chains. A revenue shift toward the segment that is 67% of sales and more than 100% of profit increases business-line concentration; it does not diversify.

In CooperSurgical, fertility competes with Vitrolife, Nexpring and Fairfax, plus clinics that vertically integrate; devices compete with J&J, Baxter, Medtronic and Hologic; cryostorage with ViaCord. Paragard faces the FDA-approved but unlaunched Miudella non-hormonal IUD, and H1 FY2026 office/surgical growth was already held back by Paragard declines.

**Competitive position: 5.8.**

## Risk Assessment

**1. Embryo culture media litigation — accrued but not capped.** Following a December 2023 voluntary recall of three lots of LifeGlobal embryo culture media, Cooper recorded a Q2 FY2026 charge of $324.1M less $52.5M of insurance recoveries, net $271.6M, with a $42.8M discrete tax benefit. As of June 5, 2026 there were **more than 140 lawsuits, three uncertified putative class actions, and over 1,500 claimants proffered**. Reassessment triggers disclosed were procedural acceleration of cases, additional claimant information, updated damage valuations and significantly higher projected defense costs. The entire $324.8M sits in current liabilities, implying a cash outflow of up to that amount within roughly twelve months. "Litigation Contingencies" was added as a new critical accounting policy. Sell-side commentary has characterized the matter as settled; the filing does not say that, and three uncertified class actions are the live tail — certification would change the exposure distribution. I do not treat the accrual as a ceiling, and I decline to infer that its timing or size was managed around the sale process, since the four disclosed triggers are ordinary and sufficient grounds for the accounting change.

**2. UK HMRC / Sauflon — an adverse ruling with zero provision.** On March 2, 2026 the UK First-tier Tribunal issued a decision "that largely supports HMRC's position that the Company had failed to properly withhold payroll taxes" on consideration paid to founders in the 2014 Sauflon acquisition. Cooper is appealing, describes an adverse outcome as reasonably possible but not probable, discloses a range of "0 to 71.7 million, plus accrued interest," and **has recorded no liability**. The currency of that figure is not stated in the text reviewed; GBP is the natural inference given the counterparty, which would make the dollar exposure larger, but I flag this as unverified rather than converting it. A tribunal has already ruled substantially against the company and interest compounds during appeal.

**3. Divestiture execution risk.** No definitive agreement has been filed, and the Q2 FY2026 10-Q contains no held-for-sale classification, no discontinued-operations presentation and no material impairment. That absence is informative: the held-for-sale criteria were not met at April 30, 2026. A sale below the $4,616.0M carrying value would produce a loss on sale or goodwill impairment.

**4. Control and integration history.** Internal control over financial reporting was not effective at October 31, 2024 due to a material weakness in CooperSurgical's US IT general controls tied to ERP implementation, remediated as of October 31, 2025, with disclosure controls effective at April 30, 2026. The ERP program at CooperVision is still ahead. The CFO signs as both principal financial and principal accounting officer, with no separate chief accounting officer.

**5. Refinancing and rate exposure.** $550M of term loan matures December 17, 2026; revolver capacity covers it, but at variable rates near 4.5–4.7% with the swap tailwind decaying. Net of swaps, the majority of Cooper's indebtedness remains subject to US-based variable interest rate risk. Purchase obligations were $585.1M at October 31, 2025 with $279.3M due within twelve months, and acquisition installments continue ($44.5M in H1 FY2026).

**6. Unquantified tariff and FX exposure.** Manufacturing sits in Costa Rica, Hungary, Puerto Rico, the UK, Belgium, the Netherlands and the US. Tariff risk is disclosed repeatedly and quantified nowhere. Roughly half of net sales are non-US with translation essentially unhedged.

**7. Governance-adjacent.** Four officer/director change 8-Ks in six months (December 4, 12 and 23, 2025; May 4, 2026) whose contents could not be retrieved — an unresolved evidence gap. The December 23 filing combined Items 1.01, 5.02 and 7.01, consistent with but not proof of an executive transition alongside a strategic disclosure. A July 1, 2026 first-time RSU grant to Paul A. Keel suggests a new director; unverified.

### Options Market Signal

Between the June 13 and July 19 snapshots, ATM implied volatility fell from 39.2% to 35.1% while the stock rose 5.6%, and put/call open interest moved from 0.10 to 0.45. Falling implied volatility into a rising price is consistent with the market treating the litigation charge as a clearing event and pricing less near-term uncertainty; the open-interest shift indicates positioning has become materially more put-weighted, though the ratio at 0.45 still skews toward calls overall. At 35% implied against the roughly 29% historical volatility Cooper itself used in its Q1 FY2026 Monte Carlo award valuation, near-term options are modestly expensive — appropriate given the Q3 FY2026 report due in late August, which last year moved the shares from $74.11 to $64.58 in a single session. Short interest of 4.6% of float with 2.7 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish position. Nothing in the options data contradicts the fundamental read; it corroborates a two-sided event in the next few weeks.

**Risk factors: 4.7.** The litigation is now quantified and partly insured, and the term loan is partly extended, which is an improvement on a quarter ago. Offsetting: an unprovisioned tribunal loss, a growing claimant population, an unsigned divestiture with carrying-value risk, a near-term maturity and an imminent binary print.

## Management Quality

The strongest positive is a genuine insider cluster. Between December 16 and 22, 2025, five insiders made open-market purchases totaling $1,304,416: CEO Albert White III 10,000 shares at $80.80 ($808,000); CooperSurgical President Holly Sheffield 1,230 at $80.75; and directors Kurzius, Rivas and Lucchese. These were code-P purchases, not grants or option exercises, and no discretionary open-market sales by executive officers appear in the 25 most recent transactions. Sheffield's purchase — the executive running the segment under review buying parent equity — is the most informative single data point. The signal is dated: every purchase was struck between $80.75 and $84.06, all are underwater at $74.69, and both the March 2, 2026 HMRC ruling and the Q2 litigation reassessment post-date them.

Capital allocation is mixed. The semiannual dividend was ended in December 2023 and redirected to repurchases, with authorization raised to $2.0B in September 2025 and $860.8M remaining at April 30, 2026. Execution has been lumpy: zero repurchases in FY2024, 4.1M shares for $290.1M at a weighted-average $69.30 in FY2025, then 1.3M shares for $105.6M at $81.21 in H1 FY2026 — higher average prices than the prior year — with only $13.1M bought in Q2 despite a lower price, and nothing in February or April. A sale-process blackout is at least as plausible an explanation as valuation judgment, and the filings do not distinguish. Share count fell about 2.2% year over year. Capex allocation is disciplined: $170.8M of the $188.6M H1 total (91%) went to CooperVision, and the company has committed to $140.2M of undiscounted lease payments on 20-to-24-year terms starting in fiscal Q4 2026 for manufacturing and R&D capacity — a hard-to-reverse commitment to the lens business, though the 10-Q does not identify which segment the leases serve.

The compensation record is the clearest negative. On the proxy's indexed basis (FY2021 = 100), Cooper's TSR stood at 67.09 at FY2025 year-end against a peer group at 103.97 — a 36.9-point gap and roughly 33% of absolute value destruction over four years — while CEO grant-date compensation rose every year to $16.05M. Realized pay is genuinely equity-sensitive: compensation actually paid collapsed from $39.8M in FY2024 to $1.77M in FY2025. But the company-selected performance metric is non-GAAP EPS, which excludes the ~$199M of acquired-intangible amortization that acquisitions mechanically create, at a company whose growth model is acquisition-led. Non-GAAP EPS rose from $3.20 to $4.13 across FY2023–FY2025 while GAAP net income fell in FY2025 and TSR fell a third. The new relative-TSR award granted in Q1 FY2026 (78,286 units, three-year relative TSR, 0–200% payout) addresses this directly, but is small next to the CEO's December 2025 grant of 84,613 RSUs plus 42,306 PSUs.

**Management quality: 5.3** — a modest improvement on the prior read, reflecting the advancing strategic review, the relative-TSR award and the capex/lease commitment to the better business, held down by the documented TSR-versus-pay record and the non-GAAP incentive design.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $94 (+26%)

CooperSurgical sells at or above $3.5B, the litigation accrual proves sufficient, and the HMRC appeal succeeds. Cooper becomes a lens pure-play with 30%-plus segment operating margins, 1%-of-sales amortization, mid-single-digit constant-currency growth, and MiSight monetizing in Japan and China. Proceeds fund buybacks against the $860.8M remaining authorization and beyond, shrinking the count 15–20%. The market applies a high-teens-to-20x multiple to a cleaner earnings stream — the sort of re-rating that follows removal of a loss-making segment plus a litigation overhang — putting the shares near $94–100 on roughly $5.00 of pro-forma non-GAAP EPS. Toric and multifocal mix continues at 11% growth, Japan stabilizes, and the four-year TSR gap begins to close.

### Bear Case → $60 (−20%)

Class certification in one of the three putative class actions expands the claim population beyond the $324.1M accrual; the HMRC appeal fails and interest compounds on an unprovisioned liability. The divestiture stalls or clears below the $4,616.0M carrying value, forcing an impairment and reducing expected proceeds. H2 FY2026 organic growth lands at the low end of 3.5% or below, with Asia Pacific still declining and Paragard eroding faster as Miudella launches. The FX tailwind reverses (±10% is ±$35.5M of quarterly operating income), the swap benefit expires, and $550M refinances at higher rates. The multiple compresses to 12.5x on $4.80 → $60, near the $58.89 52-week low.

### What the market is actually debating

The shares are 16.8% below the December 2025 high of $89.83 and 26.8% above the May 2026 low. That is a moderate drawdown, and the December high was set on an intraday spike ($89.66 open, $81.40 close) around the FY2025 report rather than a sustained level. The market's disagreement is narrow and identifiable: whether the litigation accrual is a ceiling or a floor, and whether a CooperSurgical sale produces cash proceeds above the segment's carrying value. Both resolve on disclosure, not on sentiment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** What justifies it is the divestiture arithmetic above, which shows the catalyst is close to non-GAAP EPS-neutral, reframes the bull case as multiple-dependent, and explains why a 14.9x forward multiple has not attracted a re-rating.
