**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cognex is the global leader in industrial machine vision, the hardware-plus-software systems that let factories and warehouses locate, identify, inspect, and measure discrete items. The business is deeply cyclical, and after a two-year trough (revenue fell 17% in 2023 before recovering in 2024–2025), it is now in a sharp upward recovery. Q1 CY2026 revenue grew 24% year-over-year to $268.4M, operating margin nearly doubled to 22% from 12%, and adjusted EPS of $0.34 beat consensus by 36%, the fourth consecutive earnings beat. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 18.2% over 90 days and next-year up 15.5%, with Q2 guidance (revenue $280–300M, adjusted EPS $0.40–0.44) landing roughly 8% above consensus.

The recovery is broad-based across all four core end markets, and layered on top of a new-CEO transformation (a $35–40M annualized opex-reduction target, salesforce overhaul, and a stated goal to double the customer base within five years). The balance sheet is strong: ~$622M cash and investments, zero long-term debt, an Altman Z-score of 3.81, and a 24% FCF margin off an asset-light model. Capital return is aggressive, with a fresh $500M buyback authorized in February 2026 after the prior authorization was nearly exhausted.

The problem is price. Cognex has climbed roughly 80% from its 52-week low of $33.45 to $62.05, and management itself was repurchasing shares at $39–49 in the same quarter the stock now trades 25–60% above. On a forward run-rate building toward ~$1.60+ annualized (Q2 guide $0.40–0.44), the stock trades near 42x forward earnings, a premium that requires continued flawless execution against a genuine AI-commoditization tail that management explicitly flags. The fundamentals justify a constructive stance; the entry point does not justify chasing. We rate CGNX a HOLD (growth_hold), with a preference to accumulate on pullbacks toward the low-$50s.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1981, Cognex operates as a single segment supplying machine-vision systems: In-Sight vision systems and sensors (2D/3D, with edge-learning and deep-learning AI), VisionPro software, DataMan barcode readers, and optical components (lenses and lighting from the 2023 Moritex acquisition). Roughly 85% of revenue comes from four end markets (logistics, packaging, consumer electronics, and automotive), with semiconductor and other applications making up the balance. The business is highly international: 2025 revenue split Americas 41%, Europe 25%, Greater China 16%, and Other Asia 18%.

The company runs an asset-light model. Hardware manufacturing is outsourced to contract manufacturers in Indonesia and Malaysia; optical components are made in-house in China and Vietnam. Capex is minimal ($9M in FY2025), which is why the FCF margin runs at ~24% even at mid-cycle profitability.

Under a new CEO (Matthew Moschner, effective June 2025), Cognex is executing a strategic pivot organized around three objectives: leadership in AI-for-machine-vision, best-in-class customer experience, and doubling the customer base within five years through a salesforce transformation that shifts from a headcount-heavy "emerging customer" model to a data-driven, partner-leveraged model targeting the underserved mid-market. This transformation, combined with a portfolio review and cost cuts, is the operational thesis underpinning the current re-rating.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue history shows the cyclicality clearly: FY2022 $1.006B (peak) → FY2023 $838M (−17%, reflecting post-pandemic logistics digestion, auto/consumer-electronics weakness, and an Indonesia fire) → FY2024 $915M (+9%) → FY2025 $994M (+9%). The turn has accelerated, and the Q1 print was broad-based across all major end markets (+21% constant currency).

Margins are recovering from a deep trough. Operating income moved from $246M (24% margin) in 2022 to $115M (13%) in 2024 to $163M (16%) in 2025. Gross margin followed a similar path: 72% (2023) → 68% (2024) → 67% (2025, dinged by a $13M Q4 excess-and-obsolete inventory charge tied to the portfolio review) → 71% in Q1'26 on favorable mix and volume leverage.

FY2025 net income was $114M (12% net margin), and reported EPS was essentially flat ($0.68 vs $0.62) despite the operating improvement. The distortion is a one-time $33M OBBBA (One Big Beautiful Bill Act) deferred-tax charge that spiked the effective rate to 37% versus roughly 17% ex-discrete. This is a non-cash item; the same legislation delivers an expected $12–15M annual cash-tax benefit through immediate R&D expensing. Screens keyed to reported net income understate the underlying earnings recovery; the trailing $0.68 EPS is not a clean read of earnings power, which is why forward metrics are the correct lens here.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Altman Z-score of 3.81 (safe zone), ~$622M cash and investments, zero long-term debt (D/E of 0.35 is essentially operating leases and tax liabilities), current ratio of 3.8, and equity at 74% of assets. FY2025 operating cash flow was $246M against $9M of capex, producing $237M of FCF. Capital return was aggressive: ~$151M of buybacks plus $55M of dividends in 2025. The prior $500M buyback (authorized March 2022) was nearly exhausted (~$16M remaining as of April 2026); the Board authorized a fresh $500M in February 2026. Diluted share count is declining (169M in 2025 to 166.5M in Q1'26), and the dividend was raised to $0.085/quarter. Durable cash generation, no leverage, and disciplined working capital in an asset-light model make this financial-health profile the single most reliable positive in the analysis and support a high score.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signals are strong and corroborated across multiple independent indicators. The earnings-beat track record is clean: four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +34.1%, Q4'25 +20.0%, Q3'25 +11.9%, Q2'25 +3.7%), with the magnitude of the beats increasing. Estimate revisions reinforce this: current-year EPS up 18.2% over 90 days (to $1.48) and next-year up 15.5% (to $1.78). The 90-day current-quarter revision of +36% clears the threshold that historically flags a genuine fundamental inflection rather than noise, and it is paired with the 24% revenue growth. Rapidly rising estimates plus accelerating revenue off depressed levels indicate consensus has been catching up to reality rather than getting ahead of it.

The growth is layered from two sources. First, the cyclical recovery in factory automation, logistics fulfillment, EV/battery inspection, semiconductor demand, and packaging traceability. Second, the company-specific transformation: the salesforce overhaul aimed at the mid-market, new AI products (two edge vision platforms launched in Q1'26, one powered by NVIDIA technology, plus the OneVision cloud platform and SLX logistics devices), and the $35–40M opex-reduction target that flows to margin. Q2'26 guidance of $280–300M revenue (~16.5% YoY at midpoint) and adjusted EPS $0.40–0.44 (~68% YoY growth) with adjusted EBITDA margin of 28–31% shows management expects the operating leverage to persist.

The durability question is whether the mid-market customer-base expansion delivers structurally higher served demand, or whether this is primarily a cyclical rebound that will flatten. The transformation is early, and its benefits are partly promised rather than proven. The growth-prospects score reflects genuine, multi-signal-confirmed momentum, tempered by the fact that the strongest part (the +24% revenue print) is at least partly cyclical rather than durable secular expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case turns cautious. On trailing EPS of $0.68 the stock trades at ~91x, but that figure is distorted by the OBBBA tax charge and trough-level earnings, so it overstates the multiple. The correct lens is forward: on the current-year consensus of $1.48, CGNX trades at ~42x; on next-year $1.78, ~35x; and on an annualized run-rate built from the Q2 guide (implying ~$1.60+ full-year), roughly 39x. By any of these, the stock is expensive in absolute terms.

Relative to peers, the picture is mixed and the peer set is heterogeneous. The Electronic Equipment & Instruments median P/E is 27.0 (ZBRA 27.0, TRMB 26.1), with Novanta at 115x and two names (RAL, PI) posting negative earnings. Cognex historically carries a premium multiple, and machine vision is a higher-margin, more differentiated business than barcode/data-capture peers, so some premium is warranted. But at ~35–42x forward against a peer median of 27x, the stock prices in a substantial portion of the recovery and transformation success already.

Analyst price targets sit between $62 (low) and $94 (high), with a mean of $77.6 and median $76.5, implying ~25% upside to the mean from the current price. On an inflection stock with rising estimates, consensus targets can lag reality and function as floors rather than ceilings. But that principle applies most cleanly when a stock is cheap relative to improving estimates (forward P/E under ~30x or PEG under 1.5). Here the forward multiple is elevated enough that the targets and the price largely reflect the same growth extrapolation, so the target-as-floor logic is weaker. On next-year EPS growth of ~20% and a ~35x forward multiple, PEG sits near 1.7, not egregious for a quality franchise, but not cheap.

The most telling valuation signal is management's own behavior: Cognex was repurchasing shares at $39–49 in Q1 2026 and is now trading 25–60% above those levels. That is not evidence the stock is overvalued today, but it does frame the entry as considerably less attractive than it was a quarter ago. The valuation score is held at 4.5: the multiple is stretched relative to fundamentals, and while there is a real catalyst (continued beats and transformation delivery), the price has already absorbed much of it. A rich multiple leaves a narrow margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Cognex is one of the top independent machine-vision players in a fragmented, competitive market. Competitors include other vision vendors, image-processing and sensor makers, system integrators, customers' internal engineering teams, and, increasingly, open-source and AI-based vision tools. Industry tailwinds are broadly supportive: factory automation, labor scarcity, e-commerce fulfillment automation, EV/battery inspection, semiconductor and AI-chip demand, and packaging traceability regulation. Multiple 2026 industry pieces group Cognex with the next wave of factory-automation AI beneficiaries.

The competitive trajectory is two-sided, which is why the score sits in the middle rather than high. On the positive side, Cognex is capturing revenue as consumer-electronics customers shift procurement out of China toward Europe and Other Asia, its edge-learning and OneVision products lower the barrier for the mid-market it is targeting, and its NVIDIA-partnered edge platforms position it as an AI beneficiary. The Q1'26 revenue growth substantially outpaced the broader industrial recovery, suggesting share gains or at least strong participation.

On the negative side, management warns in its own filings that "sophisticated AI algorithms and open-source machine vision platforms may lower barriers to enter our market," alongside free and low-cost tools from large technology companies. This is the central structural debate: the same AI wave that expands Cognex's addressable market also threatens to commoditize the high-end moat that supports its ~70% gross margins. The company is on the right side of the current cycle, but the long-run defensibility of its pricing is a legitimate open question. The trajectory is improving in the near term, but the moat is not clearly widening on a multi-year view.

## Risk Assessment

Several company-specific risks warrant attention, most of which are at least partly priced:

**End-market cyclicality and customer concentration.** Consumer electronics is described as "particularly volatile," and the filings note that in some recent years one customer represented a material portion of revenue (understood to be a large consumer-electronics OEM and large logistics/e-commerce accounts). Lumpy large-customer orders create forecasting risk and E&O inventory exposure; the $13M Q4'25 inventory charge is a live example. A cyclical peak followed by a digestion phase (as in 2022–2023) is the base-rate risk for this business.

**China and geopolitical exposure.** Greater China is 16% of revenue; components are sourced from China; optical plants sit in China and Vietnam; and IC-chip dependency runs through Taiwan. U.S. export controls, sanctions on Chinese semiconductors, and tariffs are explicit risk factors. The 2025 procurement shift out of China adds regional mix volatility even as Cognex captures the redirected demand.

**CEO transition and transformation execution.** The new CEO brings a different management style and strategic focus; the filings flag succession/culture-continuity risk and the risk that the operating-model overhaul fails to deliver the targeted $35–40M annualized opex reduction by end-2026. The transformation is the crux of the bull case, so execution risk is elevated.

**AI/open-source disruption.** Discussed under Competitive Landscape: a genuine long-run structural risk that the market has not obviously priced.

**Governance items.** Two flags. First, an auditor change filed as Item 4.01 in November 2025 with an 8-K/A amendment in February 2026; auditor changes always warrant scrutiny. Mitigants: Q1'26 disclosure controls were certified effective, no internal-control weaknesses or restatement were disclosed, and the change coincides with a broader finance-function refresh under the new CEO/CFO. Second, activist shareholder risk is disclosed as a standalone risk factor, unusual specificity that, combined with the CEO change, the historical TSR underperformance (5-year cumulative TSR of $46.23 per $100 invested vs. peer group $125.35), the portfolio review, cost cuts, buyback expansion, and Moritex trading divestiture, is consistent with a company responding to shareholder pressure. A named activist could not be confirmed.

**Impairment risk** on ~$386M goodwill plus ~$67–81M intangibles (mostly Moritex) exists but is not acute given the recovery.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 79.5% is rich against 56.4% one-year historical volatility (a 23-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (79.5% near-term vs. 64.6% at six months), consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±17.7%. ATM IV has collapsed from 235.3% to 79.5% since the mid-June snapshot, so much of the extreme event premium has already deflated. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%) and put/call OI ratio is low at 0.43, indicating no strong directional bearish positioning. Short interest is modest at 5.6% of float (3.3 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short. The market expects a large move around the next print but is not leaning decisively bearish. Rich IV argues against chasing entry ahead of the event; a post-earnings volatility crush could offer a better entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The cyclical recovery has legs and the transformation compounds it. If revenue growth sustains in the high-teens-to-20s and the $35–40M cost program lands, FY26 EPS reaches the high end of consensus ($1.50+) and FY27 pushes toward $1.78+ as operating margin normalizes toward the mid-20s. The mid-market customer-base expansion structurally raises served demand, and Cognex successfully positions its edge-AI and OneVision products as the differentiated, easy-to-deploy option that captures the AI wave rather than being commoditized by it. On sustained ~$1.78+ of FY27 earnings power at a premium multiple, the stock reaches the top of the analyst range ($94). The balance sheet funds continued buybacks and a possible accretive acquisition.

**Bear Case.** Consumer electronics and logistics order patterns prove lumpy, the +24% Q1 print marks a cyclical peak, and 2027 sees digestion. The transformation under-delivers, or open-source and large-tech AI vision tools begin eroding pricing at the high end, compressing the ~70% gross margins the valuation depends on. In that scenario, forward EPS estimates flatten or roll over, the ~42x multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~27x, and the stock re-rates back toward the mid-$40s (roughly where management was itself buying), a ~25–30% decline. The activist and governance flags add tail uncertainty around strategy and management.

The two cases are roughly symmetric at the current price, which is the core reason for a HOLD rather than a BUY. The business quality and near-term momentum are strong, but the ~80% run has closed the risk/reward gap.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (earnings momentum, rising estimates, and transformation optionality), but the entry after an ~80% move is unattractive, and the forward multiple leaves a thin margin of safety.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$50s (roughly 28x forward FY27 EPS), where risk/reward turns clearly favorable, or a post-earnings IV crush that offers a better entry.
- A fifth consecutive beat with FY26 EPS estimates revised toward $1.60+ and confirmation that the $35–40M cost program is on track, demonstrating durable (not merely cyclical) margin expansion.
- Evidence the mid-market customer-base expansion is producing net new customer growth ahead of plan, validating structural rather than cyclical demand.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarter showing revenue growth decelerating below ~5% YoY or a guidance cut, signaling a cyclical peak.
- Gross margin compression back below ~66% on competitive/pricing pressure (the AI-commoditization thesis materializing).
- A negative development from the auditor-change or activist situation: a restatement, control weakness, or disruptive proxy contest.
- Estimate revisions turning negative for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 CY2026 earnings report, where the ±17.7% implied move indicates the print will be decisive for the near-term trajectory.

## Conclusion

Cognex is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise in a confirmed cyclical-plus-transformation recovery. The evidence is strong and multi-sourced: +24% revenue growth, operating margin doubling to 22%, four consecutive beats, +18% current-year estimate revisions over 90 days, and Q2 guidance well above the Street. The balance sheet is pristine, and the new-CEO transformation offers genuine upside optionality on top of the cycle.

The constraint is valuation. The stock has re-rated ~80% off its low to roughly 35–42x forward earnings, a premium to the peer median that already embeds a large share of the recovery and transformation success. Management was buying its own shares at $39–49 in the same quarter the stock now trades near $62. With a real AI-commoditization tail risk to the long-run moat and roughly symmetric bull/bear cases at the current price, the appropriate stance is to hold and accumulate on weakness rather than chase. We rate CGNX a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.4, consistent with the improving trajectory of the past several reports but recognizing that price has caught up to fundamentals. The preferred re-entry is a pullback toward the low-$50s or a demonstrated, structural (not merely cyclical) step-up in margins and customer growth.
