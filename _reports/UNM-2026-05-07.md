## Executive Summary

Unum Group trades at $80.28, representing approximately 9.2x FY2026 estimated adjusted operating EPS of $8.75. This is a substantial discount to peers and the company's own growth trajectory. Management guides 8–12% EPS growth for 2026, with a stated outlook of $8.60–$8.90 per share based on a redefined 2025 base of $7.93 that excludes Closed Block results from adjusted operating income. The valuation discount exists primarily because of long-term care (LTC) reserve tail risk in the Closed Block, which generated a $643M charge in 2025 and pushed the net premium ratio to a precarious 97.5% (rising to 97.6% by Q1 2026).

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the Fortitude Re transaction closed in July 2025 reduced direct LTC exposure by ~21%; (2) the $1B annual buyback program at current prices retires a substantial portion of shares annually, creating a meaningful EPS growth tailwind; and (3) the core Unum US and Colonial Life franchises generate stable, growing premium income with competitive positioning in leave management that is under-appreciated. Q1 2026 results (adjusted operating EPS $2.14, +2.5% beat) demonstrated solid core business performance, and management reaffirmed full-year growth guidance while accelerating buyback execution. The Q1 2026 adjusted EPS figure excludes Closed Block results under the company's updated non-GAAP presentation, meaning Q1 reflects core segment strength but does not directly address Closed Block stabilization.

The key risk is another adverse LTC assumption update in 2026's annual review. However, the conservative decision to remove morbidity improvement assumptions in 2025 reduces (though does not eliminate) the probability of further material charges. At approximately 9x forward earnings with meaningful upside to consensus analyst targets, the asymmetry favors buyers willing to accept the LTC tail.

## Company Overview

Unum Group is a leading U.S. provider of employer-sponsored financial protection benefits with approximately $13.1B in annual revenue. The company operates through five segments:

- **Unum US** (65.4% of premium income): Group disability (long/short-term), group life & AD&D, and supplemental/voluntary products
- **Colonial Life** (17.0%): Worksite voluntary benefits sold through independent contractor agents
- **Unum International** (10.0%): Primarily UK-based group disability and life products
- **Closed Block** (7.6%): Run-off LTC and individual disability policies
- **Corporate**: Investment income and interest expense

The company employs approximately 10,910 people and maintains "A" financial strength ratings across AM Best, Fitch, Moody's, and S&P (all stable outlook).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:**
Adjusted operating EPS has grown from $4.48 (2021) → $6.40 (2022) → $7.66 (2023) → $8.44 (2024) → $8.13 (2025). The 2025 decline of 3.7% was driven primarily by elevated benefit ratios across segments. (The $643M LTC reserve charge affected GAAP earnings but was excluded from adjusted operating EPS, which by definition strips out reserve assumption updates.)

For 2026, management's stated outlook is $8.60–$8.90 in adjusted operating EPS, representing 8–12% growth versus a redefined 2025 base of $7.93 per share. The redefined base excludes Closed Block segment results, which management considers less relevant following the Fortitude Re transaction and other run-off actions. Consensus estimates sit at approximately $8.75 for the current year and $9.75 for next year. Q1 2026 results ($2.14 adjusted operating EPS, annualizing to ~$8.56) represent a run-rate consistent with the low end of the outlook range, with typical seasonal improvement expected in Q2-Q4.

**Capital Return:**
The buyback program is the primary EPS growth engine. Share count declined from 188.1M (2023) to 172.9M (2025), an 8% reduction in two years. In Q1 2026, Unum repurchased 5.4 million shares for $402.4 million, leaving approximately $601 million remaining under the December 2025 authorization. Shares outstanding were 160.7 million at March 31, 2026. If the full authorization is completed at prevailing prices, a further 7–8% share-count reduction is possible over the life of the program, though timing and execution price will determine the ultimate magnitude.

Total capital return in 2025: $1.01B buybacks + $306M dividends = $1.32B, representing approximately 10% of current market capitalization.

**Balance Sheet (updated for Q1 2026):**
- Holding company liquidity: $1.726B (as of March 31, 2026; down from $2.34B at year-end 2025 due to accelerated buyback activity)
- RBC ratio: ~460% (well above regulatory minimums)
- Investment portfolio: $33.1B (97.2% investment grade)
- Net unrealized loss: $1.7B (improved from $2.6B in 2024)
- The Altman Z-Score of 0.58 is structurally low for any life/health insurer carrying $52B in policy reserves — it does not indicate financial distress

**Cash Flow:**
Free cash flow of approximately $555M in 2025 (operating cash flow of $687.7M less capex of $132.3M). Operating cash generation supports the dividend ($306M) and partially funds buybacks, with excess capital from subsidiaries providing the remainder.

## Growth Analysis

**Premium Growth Trends (2025):**
- Unum US: approximately +2.9% YoY premium income
- Colonial Life: +3.1% premium income; sales +5.3%
- Unum International: +14.0% premium income (benefiting from favorable currency translation and growth in Unum UK/Poland)
- Total: approximately +3.2%

Growth is modest and defensive. The primary EPS growth driver is capital management (buybacks), not top-line acceleration. Revenue growth of 3-5% annually is realistic based on the competitive dynamics in employer-sponsored benefits.

**Structural Growth Tailwinds:**
- Expanding state-level paid family/medical leave mandates increase demand for UNM's integrated leave management platform
- Voluntary benefit penetration continues to grow as employers shift costs to employees
- Cross-selling opportunities across the product suite (Colonial Life agents accessing Unum US employer relationships)

**Growth Headwinds:**
- Group LTD premiums declined 3.6% in 2025; sales dropped 18.3% with persistency weakening (91.1% from 93.3%)
- Pricing competition in group disability limits rate increases
- Declining interest rates would pressure new money yields (current portfolio earns 4.35%)

Estimate revisions have been negative over 90 days (current year -6.0%, next year -4.5%), reflecting the 2025 earnings disappointment. However, the 30-day trend has stabilized (+0.5% current year, +0.4% next year), suggesting the trough in estimate downgrades has passed.

- **~17.4x trailing P/E** (distorted by GAAP charges)

**Versus peers:**
UNM's forward multiple is below most supplemental/voluntary benefit peers despite superior capital return and comparable growth. The discount reflects LTC overhang, but the magnitude appears excessive given:
1. The Fortitude Re transaction removed 21% of LTC exposure
2. Conservative assumption resets reduce forward charge probability
3. Core business ROE and capital generation remain intact
4. $1B annual buyback at these multiples is highly accretive

**Analyst consensus:** The mean analyst target is approximately in the mid-$90s, implying roughly 20% upside from current levels. Coverage is broadly constructive with no Sell ratings among major covering analysts as of the most recent compilation.

The market is pricing UNM as if LTC charges of the 2025 magnitude are recurring annually. If instead the 2025 assumption reset proves conservative (as intended), the multiple should re-rate toward 11-12x, implying $95-105 per share.

## Competitive Landscape

UNM occupies a top-3 position in U.S. employer-sponsored disability insurance alongside Lincoln Financial and The Hartford. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Integrated leave management platform**: As paid leave legislation expands across states, UNM's ability to administer both statutory and voluntary benefits through a single platform differentiates it from pure-play competitors
2. **Scale and claims expertise**: Decades of disability claims management data create underwriting advantages
3. **Distribution breadth**: Both broker-channel (Unum US) and direct-to-worksite (Colonial Life) distribution
4. **Product breadth**: The ability to bundle disability, life, dental, vision, voluntary, and leave management

**Competitive risks:** The group disability market is characterized as "competitive" by management, and 2025 showed clear evidence of pricing pressure (18.3% sales decline, 2.2pp persistency drop in group LTD). However, the supplemental and voluntary segments continue to grow, and the leave management services represent a structural moat that pure-play competitors cannot easily replicate.

## Risk Assessment

**1. LTC Reserve Deterioration (HIGH):**
The net premium ratio leaves minimal margin at current levels. Sensitivity: a 7% adverse change in lapses/mortality costs ~$430M; a 3% change in claim incidence costs ~$370M. Annual assumption reviews (typically Q3) are recurring binary events. The company has disclosed that reserve impacts may be asymmetrical if an assumption update causes the net premium ratio to be capped at 100% for any cohort, potentially triggering material charges. However, the removal of morbidity improvement assumptions in 2025 was specifically designed to reduce future adverse development risk — it front-loaded bad news rather than deferring it.

Additionally, Q1 2026 results revealed a Closed Block after-tax adjusted operating loss of $116.5 million ($0.70/share), which is now excluded from the company's headline adjusted EPS under the new presentation. This loss, while excluded from core metrics, demonstrates that the Closed Block continues to generate meaningful economic drag.

**2. Benefit Ratio Normalization (MODERATE):**
Broad-based deterioration in 2025 (Unum US +200bp, UK +370bp) could continue. Q1 2026 core segment results suggest partial stabilization, but if benefit ratios continue rising through 2026, the 8-12% growth target is at risk.

**3. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):**
A declining rate environment would pressure new money yields and increase LTC reserve present values. The current rate environment (30Y >5%) is favorable for UNM's investment portfolio — any rate normalization would modestly pressure earnings.

**4. Reinsurance Counterparty (LOW-MODERATE):**
$10.3B in reinsurance recoverables (primarily Fortitude Re) represents concentration risk. Trust collateralization mitigates but does not eliminate this long-duration exposure.

**5. Earnings Beat/Miss Pattern (MODERATE):**
UNM has missed estimates in 3 of the past 4 quarters (Q2, Q3, Q4 2025), with only Q1 2026 delivering a beat (+2.5%). This pattern reduces confidence in forward estimates and warrants a discount to fair value until execution improves over 2-3 quarters.

**6. Non-GAAP Comparability (MODERATE):**
The company's decision in Q1 2026 to exclude Closed Block results from adjusted operating EPS improves core business visibility but complicates year-over-year comparisons. The redefined 2025 base of $7.93 is lower than the previously reported $8.13, and investors should be aware that the "improvement" shown in 2026 guidance reflects both genuine core growth and the accounting reclassification.

## Conclusion

UNM offers an asymmetric risk/reward profile at approximately 9x forward earnings with meaningful upside to analyst consensus targets. The LTC tail risk is real but is being actively managed (Fortitude Re, conservative assumption resets, new enrollment closure) and is disproportionately reflected in the current multiple. The 3-quarter miss streak through 2025 justifies investor skepticism, but Q1 2026's beat and management's reaffirmed guidance suggest the trough is past. The accelerated buyback program creates a meaningful EPS growth tailwind even if operating performance merely stabilizes.

The market is applying a "recurring large LTC charge" discount, but the 2025 assumption reset (removing morbidity improvement entirely) was specifically designed to make future charges less likely and less severe. If 2026's annual review produces a neutral-to-modest result, the multiple re-rates toward 10.5-11x, generating 15-22% returns from current levels.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The 2026 EPS guidance base has been corrected from $8.78–$9.11 (derived from old $8.13 base) to management's stated $8.60–$8.90 (from redefined $7.93 base excluding Closed Block), which modestly reduces the base-case target price and narrows the implied upside. Additionally, Q1 2026 adjusted EPS excludes a $0.70/share Closed Block loss, meaning "stabilization" applies to core operations only, not the Closed Block, where LTC risk remains active at a 97.6% net premium ratio.
