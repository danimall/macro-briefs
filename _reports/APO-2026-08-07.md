## Executive Summary

Apollo is two businesses under one ticker, and the smaller one is doing all the work. FY2025 segment income of $6,227M split into Fee Related Earnings of $2,528M (40.6% of the total, +22.5% year over year), Spread Related Earnings from Athene of $3,361M (54.0%, +4.2%), and Principal Investing Income of $338M (5.4%, +24.7%). FRE contributed roughly $465M of the $668M of total segment-income growth — about 70% of the increase off a 41% base. The majority earnings engine has stalled: Athene's net investment spread compressed from 1.93% in FY2023 to 1.78% in FY2024 to 1.61% in FY2025, cost of funds rose 40bp to 3.69%, and average net invested assets grew $39.1B for $136M of incremental SRE. Holding the implied asset base constant, the 17bp FY2025 compression alone cost roughly $350M of SRE, more than double the growth actually delivered.

The valuation reflects some but not all of this. At $128.36 the stock trades at 15.4x FY2025 adjusted EPS of $8.33 ($5,195M ANI ÷ 623.5M shares), 14.6x the FY2026 consensus of $8.80, and 12.0x the FY2027 consensus of $10.71. A 12x forward multiple against 21.7% implied consensus earnings growth is a PEG of 0.55. The discount is not accidental. More than half of current earnings are annuity spread, which the market capitalizes at 8-10x rather than 20-24x; GAAP net income fell 24.2% in FY2025 to $3,395M while ANI rose 13.8%; book equity fell 14.5% in a single quarter to $19,951M on a $1.7B Bermuda deferred-tax write-off; and consensus is drifting lower, with the current quarter cut 3.0% in 30 days and the current year 1.7%. The quoted 45.5x trailing and forward P/E in the market-data feed is internally inconsistent with the same feed's $5.537 EPS (which implies 23.2x) and should be disregarded; the adjusted and consensus bases above are the usable ones.

This is Apollo's fifth consecutive HOLD in this coverage sequence, and the bar for repeating it unchanged should be high. The new evidence since the last review is mixed but net-negative at the margin: Q2 delivered record FRE and record SRE but missed adjusted EPS by 3.2% on weak realizations ($130M of realized performance fees in Principal Investing, which the Wall Street Journal contrasted unfavourably with rivals), AUM crossed $1.05 trillion, and the stock rose 7.9% from $119.04 on July 22 while estimates fell. A higher price on slightly weaker estimates is a narrower margin of safety, and that, not any change in business quality, is the reason this remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade. Base case $137 (+6.7% plus a 1.75% dividend), bull $165 (+28.5%), bear $100 (-22%).

## Company Overview

Apollo manages roughly $1.05 trillion of AUM as of June 30, 2026, up from $938.4B at December 31, 2025 (+25% in FY2025), with fee-generating AUM of $709.1B at year-end. The structural distinction from Blackstone or KKR is Athene, the wholly consolidated retirement services business that writes annuities, funding agreements and pension-risk-transfer contracts and holds the resulting liabilities against a large, long-duration asset portfolio. Consolidated total assets are approximately $458B (derived from an equity/assets ratio, not read directly from the balance sheet, and therefore approximate).

The competitive logic is vertical integration in credit origination. Apollo originates investment-grade and asset-backed private credit through platform companies; Athene provides captive, price-insensitive, long-duration demand for that paper; and the asset management business collects management fees, capital-solutions fees and affiliate transaction fees along the chain. The 10-K's conflicts-of-interest disclosure describes the mechanism candidly, including that origination platforms generate "management fees, incentive compensation payable or allocable to us, and additional transaction-based compensation payable to our affiliated service providers, including the capital solutions fees." Few competitors can originate, underwrite and permanently fund at this scale. The same integration is where the principal risks sit.

Apollo acquired Bridge Investment Group in an all-stock transaction closing September 2, 2025, adding approximately $34.2B of AUM (~3.6% of total). This matters for reading recent fee growth rates.

## Financial Analysis

**Fee earnings are the strong leg.** FRE progressed $1,267M (2021) → $1,410M → $1,768M → $2,063M → $2,528M (2025), a 4-year CAGR of 18.9%. Q1 2026 management fees rose to $696M from $508M (+37.0%) and advisory and transaction fees to $306M from $195M (+56.9%). Both headline rates overstate organic momentum: Q1 2026 contains a full quarter of Bridge and Q1 2025 contains none, and the filings reviewed do not disclose the organic/inorganic split. Bridge is small enough relative to total AUM that the distortion has a ceiling, but the 18.9% multi-year CAGR is the better guide to underlying capability than +37%.

**The spread engine is the problem.** SRE grew 4.2% to $3,361M on a $39.1B increase in average net invested assets. Using SRE ÷ 1.61% gives an implied asset base of roughly $208.8B; applying the FY2024 spread of 1.78% to that base yields ~$3,717M, or about $356M more than reported. SRE is stated net of ACRA third-party noncontrolling interests, so this is order-of-magnitude rather than an identity, but the direction is unambiguous and now two years old.

Two readings are available and I cannot adjudicate between them from the filings. The cyclical reading: annuities and funding agreements written at attractive 2023-2024 rates are repricing, crediting rates have caught up faster than reinvestment yields, and the gap reverses as the liability book turns over. The structural reading: the manager-owned insurance balance sheet has been widely replicated, and PRT and retail annuity pricing has become more competitive. The 10-K's own competition risk factor supports the second: "Some competitors may be willing or able to pursue opportunities or strategies or retirement-related transactions on more aggressive terms, including by accepting lower returns or greater risk." The same environment that could eventually help reinvestment yields also raises what competitors must credit to win volume.

**GAAP and adjusted earnings have diverged sharply.** GAAP net income: $(1,961)M (2022) → $5,001M → $4,480M → $3,395M (2025). The GAAP-to-ANI gap widened from ~$86M in FY2024 to ~$1,800M in FY2025. ANI excludes investment-related gains and losses, market-risk-benefit remeasurement and acquisition items; those items swung sharply negative in FY2025, consistent with growth in unrealized losses on Athene's available-for-sale book from $12.3B to $13.9B and AOCI moving from $(2,645)M to $(3,144)M in Q1 2026 alone. The exclusion is defensible for an insurer holding assets against matched liabilities where roughly 85% of deferred annuity balances carry surrender penalties. It also means reported adjusted earnings are increasingly insulated from balance-sheet outcomes that do affect equity and statutory capital. The effective tax rate rose to 19.1% from 14.3%, an independent GAAP headwind.

**Q1 2026 was weak and mostly non-economic.** Net loss attributable to common of $(1,930)M, diluted EPS $(3.27), on a $1,694M tax provision driven by writing off the Bermuda deferred tax asset. The cause is exogenous: following the OECD Inclusive Framework's January 5, 2026 agreement on a substance-based safe harbor for U.S.-headquartered multinationals, ACRA revoked its election into the Bermuda corporate income tax regime, and the 10-K states the revocation reduces other assets and increases the income tax provision "equal to the net amount of the Bermuda deferred tax assets of $1.7 billion." Apollo separately states it does not expect to owe cash tax under the Bermuda CIT, so prospective cash taxes may improve. I am not inferring motive from the structure. What is substantive: total equity fell from $23,341M to $19,951M (-14.5%), reconciled by ~$1.9B of net loss, $499M of other comprehensive loss, $634M of repurchases and $336M of dividends, offset by ~$195M of equity compensation. The book equity is gone, and Bermuda Tax Assurance Certificates run only to March 31, 2035 with the 10-K offering "no assurances" beyond.

**Funding mix is shifting toward wholesale.** FHLB funding agreements rose $23.3B → $28.2B while repurchase agreements fell $6.0B → $3.2B. On the cash-flow statement, deposits on investment-type policies fell 22.4% year over year to $19.65B while withdrawals rose 49.1% to $7.83B, a net swing from $20.06B to $11.82B. These lines are not Athene's reported gross/net inflows ($83.4B/$47.9B for FY2025), which include channels accounted for differently, so this establishes that investment-type deposit activity slowed and withdrawals accelerated, not that total inflows fell 41%. The 10-K anticipates exactly this substitution and flags its cost: institutional funding agreements may replace PRT inflows but there are "no assurances… that such other inflows would result in comparable spreads."

**Corporate leverage is modest; standardized distress screens are meaningless here.** Asset management debt rose from $5,516M to $6,380M after issuing 5.700% Senior Notes due 2036 on March 30, 2026, about 2.5x FY2025 FRE, and a materially higher cost of debt than legacy issuance. The 17.9x reported debt/equity and 0.11 Altman Z-score are artifacts of consolidating an insurance balance sheet (working capital forced to zero, revenue input understated by a large multiple, operating margin and net margin internally inconsistent) and should be disregarded as solvency signals. The relevant solvency metric is Athene's consolidated RBC ratio of 441% at December 31, 2025, which is comfortable. The 23.1% reported ROE appears computed on total equity including noncontrolling interests; the return attributable to common holders is lower.

**Interest-rate convexity is material.** The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses that an instantaneous +100bp move produces a $(4.7)B point-in-time pre-tax income impact against roughly $20.0B of total equity. This is a point-in-time disclosure on a liability-matched book, not a projection of realized loss, but the magnitude relative to equity is not trivial.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on FRE compounding while SRE stabilizes. AUM crossed $1.05 trillion at June 30, 2026 from $938.4B six months earlier, Q2 delivered record FRE and record SRE with record quarterly inflows, and next-year consensus of $10.71 implies 21.7% earnings growth off the $8.80 current-year figure.

The problem is estimate direction. The current quarter has been cut 3.0% in 30 days to $2.26; the current year 1.7% in 30 days and 1.5% in 90 days to $8.80; next year is flat at +0.2% to $10.71. That pattern — near-term cuts with the out-year held — is earnings deferral rather than a reset, and it means the FY2027 number embeds a spread recovery that has not begun. It is also the opposite of the estimate-revision signal that would justify treating consensus targets as stale-low: with revisions negative, the $152 mean target is more likely to drift toward the price than the reverse.

Q2's composition is instructive. Core recurring earnings beat; realizations missed. Principal Investing realized performance fees of $130M compare with an FY2025 PII run rate of roughly $85M per quarter, so the line was not obviously depressed against Apollo's own history; the miss says more about a high consensus realization bar and about Apollo's structurally PE-light mix (5.4% of FY2025 segment income) than about deterioration. Reuters noted Apollo "cashed in less on its own investments in a tougher environment for asset sales," and management deferred certain divestitures. In a market where the alternatives complex has derated on rate and redemption-gate fears, a manager whose realizations are the smallest earnings leg is less exposed to exit-market weakness than peers, but it also captures less of any exit-market recovery.

Two specific growth drags deserve naming. First, the ERISA pension-risk-transfer litigation: since March 2024, putative class actions have been filed against Athene's plan-sponsor customers (Athene is not a defendant) alleging ERISA violations in transferring pension obligations, seeking to have defendants guarantee the annuities and disgorge profits. Apollo's language is past-tense — negative perceptions "have adversely affected, and may continue to adversely affect" Athene's ability to attract and retain PRT customers. That distinguishes it from boilerplate and connects directly to both the funding-mix shift and the spread compression. Second, Fund VIII's escrow ratio fell from 87% to 81% against a 115% release requirement, and balance-sheet performance allocations fell from $3,554M to $3,073M, a modest economic issue on a legacy vintage, but a data point on private-equity mark quality.

Peer P/Es in the supplied comparables set (KKR 33.2, BX 31.0, BK 16.9, STT 16.7, AMP 11.2; median 16.9) are not on Apollo's adjusted basis and should not be compared line-for-line. The more useful observation is that KKR and BX both sit 33% below their 52-week highs while APO is 16% below its own; the complex is derating together, and APO has derated less.

The right framework is sum-of-parts, because a blended multiple obscures the mix. On FY2026 estimates — FRE growing ~20% to roughly $3.0B, SRE growing ~4% to roughly $3.5B, PII ~$0.35B — capitalizing after-tax FRE at 22-24x, after-tax SRE at 8-9x and after-tax PII at 10x, and deducting $6.4B of asset-management debt, produces $73.5B to $81.4B of equity value, or $118 to $131 per share on the 623.5M ANI share base. Current price sits at the upper end of that band. Pushing FRE to 24x and SRE to 9x, defensible if spread stabilizes, gets to roughly $131. Getting materially above the current price requires either a higher fee multiple than the derating cohort currently supports or a genuine SRE recovery.

That is why I score valuation at 5.5 rather than 7+. The multiple is low, and a 0.55 PEG on next-year consensus looks compelling in isolation. But cheapness without a catalyst is a common trap, and here the specific catalysts are running the wrong way: estimates falling, book value down 14.5%, the majority earnings engine flat, and the fee multiple set by a cohort in drawdown. The offsetting positives are real but second-order: a 5.4%-of-cap repurchase authorization being executed intelligently, a 1.75% dividend at a 26% payout with substantial coverage, and a mandatory convertible that converts July 31, 2026 without adding to the ANI denominator (the 623.5M base already reflects it; ownership dilution is roughly 2.5%, an estimate rather than a filing-read figure).

**Base case $137.** Roughly 15.5x the FY2026 consensus of $8.80, or 13.4x a modestly haircut FY2027 of ~$10.20, the low end of Apollo's recent forward band, consistent with the SOTP upper bound plus a small allowance for FRE compounding into FY2027. That is +6.7% from $128.36, or ~8.5% with the dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

Apollo's origination franchise is the genuine moat, and it is widening on the fee side. Fee-generating AUM of $709.1B growing ~25%, AUM crossing $1T, and record inflows in Q2 are evidence the capital-formation machine works. The company's positioning within the private-credit debate is more favourable than the sector headline: the bulk of Athene-facing origination is investment-grade and asset-backed paper rather than sponsor-backed leveraged loans, and observable credit data is mixed rather than alarming (commercial mortgage non-accrual UPB improved $992M → $860M; residential 90+ day delinquencies deteriorated $826M → $965M). The $13.9B of gross AFS unrealized losses is predominantly a rate mark on a hold-to-maturity-intent book, not a credit signal.

Where the competitive position is deteriorating is precisely where Apollo pioneered. The manager-owned insurance balance sheet has been replicated across the industry, and 32bp of two-year spread compression alongside the 10-K's acknowledgment that competitors may accept "lower returns or greater risk" is the clearest available evidence that this specific moat is narrowing. The PRT channel, historically a high-spread, high-volume franchise, is under litigation pressure that Apollo says has already affected customer acquisition, and the 10-K notes similar claims against other insurers' customers, suggesting an industry-wide volume reduction rather than a share shift.

On the private-equity side Apollo is structurally disadvantaged in exit-driven quarters, which Q2 demonstrated. That is a mix characteristic, not an execution failure, but it caps how much Apollo participates in any realization-led recovery in the alternatives complex.

## Risk Assessment

The risk stack here is unusually dense for a company at 12x forward earnings, which is much of why the multiple is where it is, and why I score risk factors at 4.7.

**Spread compression (dominant).** 54% of segment income growing 4.2% with two consecutive years of margin decline. A further 15bp of compression on a ~$210B implied base is roughly $300M of pre-tax SRE, about 5% of segment income.

**Athora concentration.** Apollo names Athora as "the most significant concentration risk exposure" of retirement services on a risk-adjusted basis. Carrying value $3,150M at March 31, 2026; a $2.0B conditional commitment announced July 3, 2025; retirement services platform investment funds rose $1,538M → $2,533M in Q1 2026. A concentrated, related-party, hard-to-mark European life position funded with capital backing U.S. policyholder liabilities, increasing during a quarter when consolidated equity fell 14.5%. Athora is separately regulated and capitalized and a large carrying value is not evidence of impairment, but the funded status of the commitment and the attribution of the $995M platform-fund increase are unverified.

**Regulatory capital reform aimed at the model.** The NAIC's principles-based bond project took effect January 1, 2025, determining whether asset-backed securities sit on Schedule D-1 or the less favourably capitalized Schedule BA; residual-tranche RBC base factors were raised for 2023-2024 reporting; the BMA revised BSCR technical provisions. Iowa designated AGM an Internationally Active Insurance Group with Athene as head in February 2024, bringing group capital standards to bear. Apollo does not expect a significant impact but cannot predict with certainty. Reclassification or higher residual factors taxes the flywheel's economics directly.

**Contingent and structural items.** Maximum performance fees subject to reversal of $5,984M against a $212M GP obligation (remote in full, but genuine); a $2.5B deferred purchase price on the Atlas/Credit Suisse securitized-products acquisition due February 8, 2028, supported by assurance letters from Athene Annuity Re and Apollo Asset Management (I use the filing's term and do not characterize these as guarantees); the 10-K's own caveat that perpetual capital "should not be viewed as being permanent without exception"; no key-man insurance in a strategy centered on Marc Rowan.

**Conflicts of interest are structural.** Apollo operates without permanent information barriers in asset management and discloses that affiliate fee arrangements create "an incentive to pursue investment opportunities with greater fee opportunities for our affiliates even at the expense of the investment returns sought by our clients." That is unusually candid disclosure of a real economic tension in the origination flywheel.

**AI-infrastructure exposure.** The 10-K's expanded AI risk factor cuts both ways, flagging that computing efficiency gains without commensurate adoption "could also negatively impact demand for, and the valuation of, digital infrastructure assets, a sector to which certain of our investment strategies have significant exposure." This is a candid acknowledgment of two-sided AI exposure through data-center investments.

**easyJet.** Reuters reported on August 6 that Apollo agreed to acquire easyJet for approximately £5.70bn (~$7.7bn), with rival bidder Castlelake withdrawing. The acquiring vehicle, financing and conditionality are unverified. Based on Apollo's disclosed operating model a control acquisition of a listed operator would customarily be executed by Apollo-managed funds, in which case economics reach APO shareholders through fees, carry and any co-investment rather than consolidation. Whether easyJet would be consolidated should not be assumed. Apollo's own risk factors identify travel and leisure and aviation as sectors where portfolio company performance has been negatively affected by macro conditions.

**One-time item to watch.** The Q1 10-Q disclosed that AP Grange called its ABS debt and Athene expects to recognize a $673M gain in Q2 2026. Whether this flows into SRE/ANI or is excluded as investment-related is not confirmed, and it matters for interpreting the "record SRE" claim.

### Options Market Signal

The 139.9% near-term ATM IV is a one-day-to-expiry artifact — the August 7 tenor carries a 2.0% implied move and a 0.02 put/call volume ratio, which is same-day pinning mechanics, not stress pricing. Discounting it, the term structure is close to flat and fairly priced: 36.1% at one month, 35.3% at three months, 38.8% at six months, against 36.2% one-year realized. The implied moves of 5.5% (1m) and 9.3% (3m) are unremarkable for a levered financial. Put/call open interest rose from 0.70 to 1.13 while the stock advanced 7.9% since July 22, and the data source characterizes the 5.0% front skew as elevated put demand, consistent with hedging accumulated into a rally rather than a directional bet. Six-month skew is -3.5%. Short interest of 5.5% of float and 5.7 days to cover indicate an uncrowded position. The options market is neither confirming nor contradicting the fundamental view; it prices ordinary uncertainty, which is consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($165, +28.5%).** New-money reinvestment yields eventually exceed crediting rates on maturing liabilities, and the 1.61% spread stabilizes or re-expands toward 1.70%+. FRE continues compounding near 20% off a $1.05T AUM base with record inflows, taking FY2027 ANI to roughly $11.00 per share. Realizations normalize as exit markets reopen, adding to PII. The $4.0B repurchase authorization retires 5%+ of the float at multiples management has already demonstrated it will lean into. Applying 15x, the middle of Apollo's recent forward band, gives $165. This case requires SRE stabilization, and it is the single variable that would also pull consensus targets (mean $152, high $173) back into play.

**Bear case ($100, -22%).** Spread compression proves structural: another 15-20bp of decline as PRT volumes stay suppressed by ERISA litigation and institutional funding agreements substitute at inferior spreads, taking SRE flat-to-down. NAIC bond-project reclassification or higher residual-tranche factors raise Athene's capital charges on Apollo-originated paper, taxing the flywheel. Private-credit marks deteriorate as the sector's first genuine stress test proceeds; AFS unrealized losses grow past $13.9B and book equity contracts further from $19,951M. The alternatives cohort continues derating alongside BX and KKR, compressing the fee multiple to 18-19x. FY2027 ANI at $9.50 on 10.5x gets to $100, essentially the 52-week low of $99.56 and the March 2026 trough where management was buying at a $106 monthly average.

**Base case ($137, +6.7% plus 1.75% dividend).** SRE grinds flat-to-low-single-digit with no recovery but no collapse. FRE compounds high-teens organically. FY2026 lands near the $8.80 consensus after the current 1.7% cut, FY2027 near $10.20 rather than $10.71. At 15.5x FY2026 or 13.4x haircut FY2027, fair value is $137, at the top of the SOTP band. Probability-weighting base 55%, bull 20%, bear 25% gives roughly +3% price return plus dividend, or about +5% total — positive, but below the cost of equity for a levered financial with this risk stack.

**On management.** This dimension is the one genuine bright spot and is why I hold it above the composite. Rowan's FY2025 total compensation of $913,367 for running a $1T-AUM, $74B-market-cap firm is a real alignment structure — his economics come from equity, and his direct 1.55M shares understate a total interest held largely through Apollo Operating Group vehicles. Five-year TSR of $331 per $100 invested versus $196 for the peer group is substantial outperformance. Q1 2026 repurchases were executed into the decline at monthly averages of ~$135.47, ~$125.26 and ~$106.43, leaning in as the stock fell rather than pausing. Against that: average NEO compensation rose ~59% to $37.35M; all four disclosed incentive metrics (FRE, ANI, Realized Performance Fees, SRE) are non-GAAP or segment measures with no GAAP earnings, book value or ROE counterweight, in a year when GAAP fell 24% and equity fell 14.5%; and there were no open-market insider purchases, only modest sales by Zito (~$6.4M) and CFO Kelly (~7,000 shares), gifts by Rowan and Kleinman, and 10b5-1 plans including Belardi's for up to 1.5M shares through April 2027. The selling is routine liquidity at modest scale and I do not read it as directional, but the absence of buying at the March lows while the company itself was buying is a genuine gap. The related-party items — Athora exposure growth, the $200M share-issued charitable contribution, disclosed executive family offices — warrant monitoring rather than alarm.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The classification is honest: the decisive variable is whether Athene's spread compression is cyclical or structural, and neither the filings nor two years of trend data resolve it. Existing holders are compensated by a 1.75% dividend at a 26% payout, a 5.4%-of-cap buyback being executed sensibly, and a fee engine compounding near 19%. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $128.36, seven percent above the July level and with consensus drifting down.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in early November 2026 is the natural decision point. It will be the first quarter with a clean read on whether Q2's record SRE was flattered by the $673M AP Grange gain, the first post-conversion quarter for the mandatory convertible (July 31, 2026), and the first opportunity to see whether the Q2 realization shortfall reverses.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. **Net investment spread stabilizes at or above 1.61% for two consecutive quarters**, disclosed in the Q3 and Q4 MD&A. This is the single most important number in the company.
2. **Current-year consensus turns positive** — a 30-day revision above zero on the FY2026 figure (currently $8.80, cut 1.7%), reversing the deferral pattern.
3. **Price at or below $112-115**, which restores double-digit upside to the $137 base case and puts the entry near the SOTP lower bound of $118. A decline to that level on unchanged fundamentals would be an improvement in risk/reward, not a deterioration in thesis.

**Upgrade to BUY additionally requires** evidence that PRT volumes have recovered (disclosed pension group annuity inflows returning toward FY2025 levels) or that institutional funding agreements are being written at spreads comparable to the retail channel — the specific "no assurances" the 10-K declines to give.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. **Net investment spread below 1.50%** in either the Q3 or Q4 2026 disclosure, which would confirm the structural reading and cut ~$230M+ of pre-tax SRE from the base case.
2. **Total stockholders' equity below $18.5B**, or gross AFS unrealized losses above $16B, indicating balance-sheet erosion beyond the one-time Bermuda charge.
3. **An adverse NAIC or BMA capital determination** materially reclassifying Athene's asset-backed holdings to Schedule BA, or an Athene financial-strength ratings downgrade or negative outlook (currently unverified and a genuine gap in the evidence base).
4. **Athene named as a defendant** in the ERISA PRT actions, or quantified disclosure of lost PRT volume.

**Downgrade to AVOID** if two or more of the above coincide, or if Athora exposure is written down materially against the $3,150M carrying value.

## Conclusion

Apollo is a high-quality origination franchise attached to an insurance balance sheet whose spread economics have narrowed for two consecutive years. At 14.6x FY2026 consensus and 12.0x FY2027, the stock is not expensive; the discount to the alternative-manager cohort is largely explained by mix (54% of segment income capitalized at insurance rather than fee multiples), by a widening GAAP-to-adjusted gap, and by a 14.5% single-quarter contraction in book equity. A sum-of-parts on FY2026 estimates at defensible multiples produces $118-131 per share, placing the current $128.36 at the top of that band, and the base case of $137 offers roughly 6.7% price upside plus a 1.75% dividend against a $100 bear case anchored on the 52-week low.

The score declines from 5.8 to 5.6, extending a trajectory that began at 6.1 in June. That reflects specific new evidence, not price action: adjusted EPS of $2.11 missed the $2.18 bar and ended a four-quarter beat streak, current-quarter and current-year estimates were cut 3.0% and 1.7% in 30 days, and the stock advanced 7.9% into that revision, a combination that narrows margin of safety on both sides of the ratio. Set against that, AUM crossed $1.05 trillion, both segments set records, FRE continues compounding near 19%, and management's capital allocation and compensation alignment remain genuinely above sector norms.
