## Executive Summary

Patrick Industries is a national-scale component solutions provider with ~$4B in revenue across five end markets: RV (45%), Manufactured Housing (17%), Marine (15%), Industrial (13%), and Powersports (10%). The company operates 191 manufacturing plants and 50 distribution facilities, generating $247M in free cash flow on $3.95B of 2025 revenue. Management has built the platform through aggressive serial acquisition ($560M deployed over three years) while maintaining covenant headroom and a current ratio above 2.5x.

The core investment tension is between operational execution and cyclical exposure. PATK outgrew its underlying markets in 2025 — RV revenue +9% vs. industry wholesale shipments +3%, marine revenue +6% vs. shipments -4% — demonstrating genuine content-per-unit expansion and market share gains. Gross margins expanded 60bps to 23.1% through labor automation. The company has beaten earnings estimates in each of the last four quarters by 5-13%. A director purchased $1.14M of stock in open-market transactions near current prices in March 2026.

Against this, the stock trades at ~27x trailing earnings with estimates declining across all forward periods (-14% on current quarter, -2% on current year over 90 days). Roughly 70% of revenue is tied to consumer discretionary spending on RVs, boats, and powersports vehicles — categories already showing retail weakness (RV retail -2%, marine retail -8% in 2025). Record-low consumer sentiment (47.6 Michigan), elevated fuel costs, and rising inflation create a hostile operating environment for these end markets. The options market is pricing imminent stress via backwardated implied volatility (57.7% near-term vs. 45% at 6 months) with a put/call OI ratio of 7.11.

## Company Overview

Patrick Industries operates two segments: Manufacturing (74% of sales) and Distribution (26%). The Manufacturing segment produces components including laminated products, cabinetry, fiberglass bath fixtures, electrical systems, aluminum fabrication, boat trailers, audio systems, and fuel tanks. Distribution handles pre-finished panels, drywall, lighting, wiring, plumbing, appliances, and flooring, supplemented by logistics services.

The business model centers on geographic proximity to customers — the company is headquartered in Elkhart, Indiana, the hub of U.S. RV manufacturing — and breadth of product offering. PATK serves ~4,500 active customers but faces meaningful concentration: Forest River (Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary) and Thor Industries together account for 28% of net sales, with no long-term contracts protecting either relationship. The top three OEMs in both RV and manufactured housing control 85-86% of their respective markets, giving buyers significant pricing leverage.

Growth has been driven primarily by acquisitions. The 2024 Sportech deal ($319M) added powersports exposure. RecPro ($78M, 2024) added e-commerce aftermarket capabilities. Three smaller 2025 deals totaled ~$117M. Goodwill ($840M) and intangibles ($743M) now represent 51% of total assets — a standard consequence of serial acquisition strategy but a meaningful impairment risk in a downturn.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $3.95B in 2025, up 6% YoY and the second consecutive year of recovery from the 2023 trough ($3.47B). Revenue remains 19% below the 2022 pandemic peak ($4.88B). Organic growth contributed meaningfully — PATK outgrew RV wholesale shipments by 6 percentage points and marine shipments by 10 percentage points, indicating real content-per-unit expansion beyond acquisition-driven growth.

**Profitability:** Gross margin expanded to 23.1% (+60bps YoY) driven by manufacturing automation reducing labor costs (~50bps) and overhead efficiency (~20bps). Operating margin was flat at 7.0% as SG&A increases (incentive compensation, technology investments) and higher freight costs offset gross margin gains. Net income declined 2% despite 6% revenue growth due to a $24.4M legal settlement (motor vehicle fatality case) and a higher effective tax rate (23.7% vs. 22.5%). Adjusting for the settlement, net income would have grown ~15%. Intangible amortization of $97.3M (~2.5% of sales) is an ongoing drag from the acquisition program.

**EBITDA:** Approximately $422M, essentially flat for three consecutive years ($405M in 2023, $425M in 2024, $422M in 2025) despite revenue growing from $3.47B to $3.95B. Operating-level margin compression is offsetting top-line expansion — a concerning pattern for a company trading at growth multiples.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $329M and FCF of $247M (6.2% FCF margin). FCF is nearly 2x net income, supported by $170M of depreciation/amortization. The company deployed $122M on acquisitions, $87M on shareholder returns (dividends + buybacks), and serviced $1.28B of long-term debt.

**Balance Sheet:** Total long-term debt of $1.28B against $26M of cash. Net leverage is approximately 3.1x EBITDA. The debt structure is manageable: $120M term loan (2029), $350M 4.75% senior notes (2029), $500M 6.375% senior notes (2032), and $259M 1.75% convertible notes (2028). The revolver ($875M capacity, $110M drawn) provides $792M of liquidity. Covenant headroom is substantial — secured net leverage at 0.34x vs. 2.75x covenant, interest coverage at 6.63x vs. 3.0x minimum. Current ratio of 2.51 is healthy.

**Convertible Note Risk:** The $259M convertible notes are in the money (triggered since Q4 2024). Conversion would require cash settlement of principal from the revolver. Diluted share count has already expanded from ~22M (2023) to ~35M, partially reflecting convertible dilution.

**Earnings Quality:** FCF/net income conversion above 180% signals high-quality, cash-backed earnings. The 4-for-4 earnings beat record (average surprise of +8.6%) supports confidence in management's operational execution and potentially conservative guidance tendencies.

## Growth Analysis

PATK's growth story has two components: organic share gains and acquisitions.

**Organic growth** is the more valuable signal. RV revenue growing 9% against 3% industry wholesale growth, and marine revenue growing 6% against a 4% decline in shipments, demonstrates that PATK is winning wallet share with OEM customers through broader product offerings and superior service. The "content per unit" strategy — selling more components per vehicle manufactured — is a legitimate secular growth lever that functions independently of industry unit volumes.

**Acquisition growth** has been prolific but is showing diminishing near-term returns. The 2025 acquisitions generated only $1.3M of operating income on $44M of revenue — essentially zero margin contribution in year one. While integration takes time, this argues against paying a premium multiple for acquisition-driven growth.

**Forward estimates are declining.** Current quarter EPS consensus has fallen 14% in 90 days (from $1.26 to $1.08). Full-year 2026 estimates dropped from $5.44 to $5.35. Next year estimates edged higher over 90 days (+1.1%) but have declined 1.2% in the last 30 days. The revision trajectory is unambiguously negative in the near term.

**Industry backdrop is mixed.** RV dealer inventories are "below historical norms," which creates a potential restocking catalyst once consumer demand stabilizes. But RV retail declined 2% in 2025 despite wholesale growth of 3% — meaning the restock has already begun and is running ahead of end-user demand. Marine remains in a deeper trough (retail -8%). Manufactured housing is constrained by tight MH lending conditions. Industrial is tied to residential construction, which faces high mortgage rate headwinds.

**The OBBBA tax benefit** (100% bonus depreciation permanence, R&D expensing) is a modest positive for after-tax FCF but will not meaningfully change the growth trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $106, PATK trades at approximately:
- **27.2x trailing EPS** ($3.90)
- **19.8x FY2026E EPS** ($5.35)
- **16.7x FY2027E EPS** ($6.34)
- **~8.5x EV/EBITDA** (based on ~$422M EBITDA and ~$4.8B EV)
- **1.0x P/B** (book value ~$3.5B)
- **~14x FCF** ($247M FCF on $3.5B market cap)

The trailing P/E of 27x is elevated for a cyclical industrial company, particularly one with flat EBITDA for three years. However, the trailing EPS of $3.90 reflects the $24.4M legal settlement; adjusting for that, normalized EPS would be closer to $4.50, yielding a ~24x P/E. The forward P/E of ~20x on 2026 estimates is more reasonable but vulnerable to further estimate cuts.

EV/EBITDA of 8.5x is within a normal range for building products/RV suppliers, though the cyclical nature of the business argues for a discount in uncertain macro environments. The 14x FCF multiple is attractive in isolation, but FCF durability depends on the cycle holding — in the 2022-2023 downturn, revenue dropped 29%.

The median analyst target of $140 implies 31% upside, with a range of $95-$155. The distribution is wide, reflecting genuine uncertainty about the cycle.

**Dividend yield of 1.8%** provides modest income support but is not a primary thesis driver.

The stock has rallied 35% from its April 2025 lows (~$75) to current levels, reclaiming much of the value lost during early 2026 volatility. The Feb 2026 earnings beat propelled shares from ~$130 to $148 before a subsequent decline to the current ~$107 level. The stock sits 28% below its 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

PATK's competitive advantages are real but narrow. The national footprint of 191 plants creates logistical proximity to OEM customers that regional competitors cannot easily replicate. Breadth of product offering enables a "one-stop shop" model that reduces procurement complexity for OEMs. Scale from $4B in revenue supports R&D investment (primarily in automation and materials science) and provides bargaining power with raw material suppliers.

However, the moat is shallow. The 10-K explicitly states the company competes with "numerous national, regional, and local manufacturers and distributors." Products are largely commodity components where switching costs are low. Pricing power is limited by OEM concentration — three customers controlling 85%+ of end-market production have significant leverage. Barriers to entry in any single product category are minimal; the barrier is replicating the full breadth and geographic coverage simultaneously.

The 262% five-year TSR (vs. 112% peer median) reflects strong execution through a volatile cycle. The acquisition strategy has been value-accretive on a multi-year basis, even if near-term returns on 2025 deals are marginal.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical consumer spending (Primary Risk).** With 70% of revenue in discretionary categories and consumer sentiment at its record low, PATK faces a hostile demand environment. RV retail is already declining. Rising fuel costs (oil above $95) directly reduce RV/boat usage and purchase intent. The 2022-2023 experience demonstrates that revenue can drop 29% in a single year during a discretionary spending pullback.

**Estimate deterioration.** Negative revision momentum typically precedes earnings weakness. If underlying demand softens further through spring/summer 2026, estimates likely face additional cuts.

**Customer concentration.** Forest River and Thor at 28% of revenue without contracts is a permanent structural risk. Any shift in sourcing strategy by either OEM would have material revenue impact.

**Tariff exposure.** Significant sourcing from China, Mexico, Canada, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Vietnam. The 10-K specifically flags tariff risk under the current administration. Plywood (a key input) is specifically mentioned. Pass-through to customers is uncertain given OEM pricing leverage.

**Acquisition integration.** Goodwill and intangibles at 51% of assets create impairment risk in a downturn. 2025 acquisitions generated negligible operating income relative to revenue.

**Convertible note dilution.** In-the-money convertibles will require cash settlement from the revolver upon conversion, reducing financial flexibility during potential stress.

**CFO departure.** Andrew Roeder resigned in February 2026 after less than two years as CFO, citing "interests outside the Company." While the replacement (Matthew Filer) has strong credentials and bought shares, mid-cycle CFO departures warrant monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term risk. Near-term ATM IV of 57.7% is 23 percentage points above the 34.5% historical volatility — a substantial premium. The term structure is in backwardation (57.7% → 48.8% → 45.0%), indicating the market expects a resolution of uncertainty within weeks rather than months. The put/call OI ratio of 7.11 is heavily skewed toward puts, with 8.2% put skew on the nearest expiry. The ±12.3% implied move on the nearest expiry brackets a range of roughly $93-$120. This options positioning diverges from the consensus analyst view (median target $140) and suggests institutional hedging activity around potential downside catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($130-140, +22-31%)
- RV dealer restocking accelerates in H2 2026 as inventories remain below historical norms
- Content-per-unit gains continue to drive above-market revenue growth regardless of industry volumes
- Gross margin expansion continues through automation, reaching 24%+ by FY2027
- OBBBA tax benefits enhance FCF conversion
- Director Welch's $1.14M open-market purchase near current levels proves well-timed
- Forward P/E compresses toward 16-17x as FY2027 EPS of $6.34 comes into view

### Bear Case ($75-85, -20-29%)
- Consumer spending deterioration accelerates — elevated fuel costs combined with record-low sentiment trigger RV/marine retail declines of 10-15%
- RV wholesale shipments reverse, following retail weakness with a 2-3 quarter lag
- Tariff escalation increases input costs that cannot be fully passed through, compressing margins
- EBITDA declines from $422M toward $350-370M, expanding net leverage above 3.5x
- Estimates face additional 10-15% cuts, de-rating the multiple toward 14-16x forward
- Goodwill impairment charges on recent acquisitions if segment performance deteriorates

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Current quarter EPS estimates stabilize or reverse** — 30-day revisions turning positive would signal a fundamental trough

- **Loss of Forest River or Thor as a customer** (partial or full), or any indication of meaningful share loss
- **Net leverage exceeds 3.5x EBITDA**, signaling balance sheet stress
- **FY2026 EPS estimates fall below $4.75** (an additional 11% cut from current consensus)

### Reassessment Timeline
Next catalyst is the Q1 FY2026 earnings report (expected May 2026). This will provide the first read on spring selling season demand across RV, marine, and powersports. Management commentary on tariff impacts and consumer demand trends will be critical. Reassess after this report with attention to organic revenue growth vs. underlying industry volumes.

## Conclusion

Patrick Industries is a well-managed, competitively positioned company executing above its end markets. The acquisition strategy has built genuine scale advantages, and gross margin expansion through automation is real. The earnings beat record and significant insider buying near current prices are positive signals.

The problem is timing and valuation relative to the cycle. At ~20x forward earnings with negative estimate revisions, the stock is priced for continued recovery in consumer discretionary categories that are showing early signs of weakening. With ~70% of revenue in RV, marine, and powersports — categories directly impacted by consumer sentiment, fuel costs, and interest rates — the near-term earnings trajectory is at risk. The options market's extreme put positioning and backwardated volatility structure confirm institutional concern about downside.

The company's financial flexibility (low covenant leverage, $792M revolver capacity, strong FCF) means the balance sheet can withstand a moderate downturn. This limits severe downside but does not justify paying a premium multiple for cyclical earnings that may be peaking. HOLD at current levels, with a clear path to BUY on either a valuation pullback to ~$85-90 or evidence that consumer demand is stabilizing.
