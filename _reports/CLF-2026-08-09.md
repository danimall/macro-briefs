**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cleveland-Cliffs is emerging from the worst earnings trough in its modern history. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA collapsed 95% to $37M on $18,610M of revenue, the Steelmaking segment itself generated negative $16M, and the company burned $1,023M of free cash flow. Q2 2026 broke that sequence: Adjusted EBITDA of $286M tripled Q1's $95M, Steelmaking cash margin per ton rose from $32 (Q2 2025) to $87, and management guided Q3 to approximately $575M with Q4 expected to exceed it. Interest coverage on Adjusted EBITDA moved from 0.06x for FY2025 to 1.83x in Q2 and would reach roughly 3.7x at the Q3 guide. The mechanism is straightforward: domestic hot-rolled coil averaged $1,079/nt in Q2, the highest quarterly average since Q2 2022, Section 232 duties were maintained at 50% and expanded in April 2026 to cover the full value of iron and steel articles, total imports fell 29% year over year through April, and the loss-making ArcelorMittal USA slab contract (~1.5M tons annually, unprofitable in both 2024 and 2025) expired in December 2025 and was not renewed.

The prior thesis on this name rested on deteriorating cash generation against $7.7B of debt. That specific basis has weakened. There are no contractual debt maturities in 2026 or 2027, the $895M ABL matures June 2028, liquidity was $3.1B at June 30 with roughly $3.0B of undrawn revolver capacity, and the only financial covenant is a springing 1.0:1.0 fixed-charge test that was not applicable at quarter-end. An imminent liquidity event is not the base case.

What has not improved is the price. At $12.26, market capitalization is $6.995B and enterprise value $14.70B against net debt of $7.70B. At a 6.0-6.5x EV/EBITDA multiple — normal for domestic flat-rolled — the current price discounts $2.25-2.45B of forward EBITDA, which is approximately the annualized H2 2026 run-rate carried unchanged into 2027 with no allowance for hot-rolled reversion. Consensus 2027 EPS of $0.51 implies roughly $2.0B of EBITDA, which on the same multiple and net debt supports about $8.25/share. The equity therefore already embeds the bull operating case while retaining bear-case leverage: 52% of enterprise value accrues to debtholders, so a $400M swing in EBITDA moves the equity roughly $4.20/share at 6x. Add an unhedgeable labor negotiation covering nearly every integrated plant and all iron ore mines in 2026, a forward HRC swap book that grew from 220,220 to 583,450 net tons and carries $71M of losses reclassifying into revenue over the next twelve months, and governance concentration that increased in July, and expected return is approximately flat against a 39% drawdown risk. Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Cliffs is a North American flat-rolled producer vertically integrated from iron ore through stamping and tubing. The FY2025 10-K reports seven blast furnaces and four EAFs with configured capability of approximately 20.0 million net tons of raw steel, roughly 25,000 employees (over 18,000 under collective bargaining agreements), five active iron ore mines with 20.1 million long tons of attributable rated capacity, 1.9 million metric tons of HBI capacity at Toledo, 4.0 million net tons of coke capacity, the Princeton met coal mine, and 21 Ferrous Processing & Trading scrap facilities. Steelmaking generated $17,953M of FY2025's $18,610M revenue and is the sole reportable segment.

FY2025 end-market mix was direct automotive 30%, infrastructure and manufacturing 29%, distributors and converters 28%, and steel producers 13%. Fixed-price contract coverage moved from 35-40% of flat-rolled shipments in the 10-K to 40-45% in the Q2 10-Q, leaving roughly 55-60% spot or index-linked. Annual D&A runs approximately $1.1B against $18-20B of revenue. That fixed-cost structure produces the extreme operating leverage visible in both directions — revenue fell 15.4% from 2023 to 2025 while Adjusted EBITDA fell $1,856M, a decremental margin near 55%, though roughly $300M of the FY2025 gross margin decline was incremental depreciation from indefinite idlings plus Stelco and ~$70M was idled-facility charges.

## Financial Analysis

**The trough, quantified.** Adjusted EBITDA ran $1,893M (2023), $773M (2024), $37M (2025). Operating income went from $677M to a $756M loss to a $1,579M loss. Net loss reached $1,428M in 2025. Average realized selling price per net ton fell from $1,081 to $1,005 even as the HRC benchmark rose 10% to $851/nt — a divergence consistent with mix deterioration (the unprofitable slab contract, a 6% decline in cold-rolled volumes) and with fixed-price automotive contracts resetting lower for 2025 and lagging the spot recovery.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $70M, receivables $2,044M, inventories $4,525M, PP&E net $9,239M, total assets $20,115M, long-term debt $7,703M, total equity $5,818M, and a retained deficit of $(911)M (from $+979M at end-2024). Debt principal totals $7,773M including $895M of ABL at a 5.143% effective rate; the fixed-rate notes carry coupons from 4.625% to 7.625% with $4,860M maturing after 2030. Maturity ladder: nothing in 2026-2027, $895M in 2028, $1,268M in 2029, $750M in 2030.

**Cash flow — the distinction that matters.** Q2 2026 operating cash flow was $230M against $157M of capex, so free cash flow was +$73M. For the six months, operating cash flow was $(95)M against $309M of capex, so H1 free cash flow was $(404)M, implying Q1 free cash flow of roughly $(477)M. Long-term debt rose $450M over the six months, funded by $444M of net ABL draw. Management's statement that the company returned to positive free cash flow and has begun reducing debt is accurate for the standalone quarter (the ABL was net repaid $63M in Q2) and inaccurate year to date.

Within the $230M of Q2 operating cash flow, the payables/accrued line contributed +$236M against a total working-capital source of +$99M. Excluding the payables build, Q2 operating cash flow was approximately break-even. The disclosed supply-chain finance balances ($20M in other current liabilities plus $71M within accounts payable against $2,159M total AP) are too small to explain the move, and the most plausible explanation is the one the CFO gave — genuinely higher purchase volumes and unit costs (busheling averaged $458/lt) in a quarter with extended April-May maintenance. The inflection in margin is real and is driven by a $211M sequential increase in Steelmaking cash margin. The cash conversion is not yet demonstrated as repeatable, and $73M of quarterly free cash flow should not be annualized, particularly with management flagging a Q3 working-capital build and the Burns Harbor C reline landing in 2027 (the 10-Q cites ~$900M of capex over the twelve months to June 2027 versus $700M for calendar 2026; the two figures cover different windows and are not in conflict).

**Distress screens, corrected.** A standard Altman recomputation on FY2025 10-K inputs — working capital $3,133M, retained deficit $(529)M, EBIT $(1,579)M, equity $6,116M, liabilities $13,689M, revenue $18,610M, assets $20,012M — yields approximately 1.09, materially worse than the 1.81 a screening output produced using an EBIT input inconsistent with the reported $1,579M operating loss. Rolling the same formula onto June 30 balances with H2-annualized EBIT of ~$1.35B still gives roughly 1.66, below the 2.99 safe threshold, because the drag is structural: asset intensity, a retained deficit, and $7.7B of debt against $5.8B of equity. The correct reading is that Cliffs has no margin for error on leverage, not that default is near — zero maturities before June 2028 and $3.0B of undrawn revolver contradict the latter.

**Dilution and capital allocation.** The October 30, 2025 issuance of 75 million shares for $951M net raised the count roughly 15.1% and diluted pre-existing holders' ownership by roughly 13.1% (they now hold 86.9%). Proceeds repaid ABL borrowings. Of the $1.5B April 2024 buyback authorization, $1,376M remains unused, and no shares were repurchased in 2025 or H1 2026 beyond tax withholding. Directing all capital to debt reduction is the correct choice at 3.2x leverage, and management declining below-threshold offers for HBI and Ferrous Processing rather than transacting is defensible.

**On the earnings surprise record.** The reported track record of one beat and three misses appears to mix bases. Q2 2026 GAAP EPS was $(0.25) and adjusted EPS was $(0.20) against a $(0.20) consensus; Q1 GAAP was $(0.42) and adjusted was $(0.40) against $(0.35). On a consistent adjusted-to-adjusted comparison, Q2 was in line rather than a 22% miss. The "slight earnings miss" framing in secondary coverage could not be verified as to basis. Confidence in forward consensus should be calibrated accordingly — the delivery record is less poor than the headline suggests, but it is not a record of beats.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are the strongest single piece of forward evidence: current quarter $0.21 (from $0.10 ninety days ago, +110.6%), next quarter $0.20 (from $0.03, +544.5%), current year $(0.27) (from $(0.45), +40.6%), next year $0.51 (from $0.44, +16.4%). The direction is unambiguous and corroborated by company guidance rather than resting on sell-side extrapolation.

Two qualifications matter for how much weight this carries. First, the largest percentages are computed off near-zero bases — the next-quarter move from $0.03 to $0.20 is $0.17 of absolute EPS — and the current-year figure remains a loss. Second, and more important, the revisions are largely catching up to disclosed guidance rather than pointing beyond it. Consensus 2027 EPS of $0.51 implies roughly $2.0B of EBITDA (working back through ~$570M of interest, ~$1.1B of D&A and a 25% tax rate), which is *below* the $2.4B implied by annualizing management's H2 guidance. The revision cycle from here is a convergence toward guidance, not the discovery of upside above it.

Growth drivers that are verifiable: the Q3 guide of ~$575M reconciles internally (Q2's $87/nt cash margin plus $55/nt ASP and $10/nt cost improvement on ~4.3M tons is ~$280M, plus ~275k incremental tons at ~$87 is ~$24M, giving ~$585M); Section 232 at 50% expanded to full article value plus derivatives including certain transformers; imports at 18% of domestic consumption in 2025 versus 23% in 2024; the accretive expiry of the slab contract, which explains roughly 400k tons of the 6% year-over-year shipment decline while revenue rose 5.9% on a +$200M favorable mix bridge; and Butler Works adding ~25% to GOES output by 2028 into a short transformer market.

Growth drivers that should carry no credit: the POSCO MOU, open eleven months with no disclosed structure or consideration and management stating it has no deadline; the ~$400M of property sale proceeds, sourced only to the earnings call with no purchase agreements, counterparties or balance-sheet appearance; the ~$500M 2027 fixed-price contract reset benefit, a management estimate with no supporting disclosure; and the Dearborn restart (2M+ tons), explicitly contingent on Ford converting Rouge production from aluminum back to steel. The MinnTac option — Cliffs' right to buy 25% of the mine from U.S. Steel for $500M through January 31, 2027, marked down from $95M to $30M — would consume cash directly against the deleveraging target if exercised.

Full-year volume guidance of 16.5-17.0M net tons requires H2 shipments of 8.37-8.87M against H1's 8,133k, i.e. +3% to +9% sequentially. The Q3 guide of >4.3M tons is consistent with the lower half of that range; the top end looks demanding against North American light vehicle production of ~3.9M units in Q2 2026, below ~4.0M a year earlier.

## Valuation Assessment

Market capitalization is $6.995B (570,540,047 shares at $12.26). Net debt is $7.703B. Enterprise value is $14.70B, so 52% of the enterprise accrues to creditors.

| Basis | EBITDA | EV/EBITDA at $12.26 |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 actual | $37M | not meaningful |
| Q2 2026 annualized | $1,144M | 12.8x |
| H2 2026 guidance annualized | ~$2,400M | 6.1x |
| Consensus 2027 implied | ~$2,060M | 7.1x |
| Management 2027 case (incl. $500M reset) | ~$2,900M | 5.1x |

Inverting the arithmetic is more informative than the multiples. At 6.0-6.5x EV/EBITDA, the current $14.70B enterprise value discounts $2.26-2.45B of forward EBITDA. That is the H2 2026 run-rate extrapolated into 2027, with the reset benefit and asset sales roughly offsetting any HRC reversion. The market has already paid for the guidance.

Equity sensitivity, assuming net debt falls to $7.30B by end-2027 (i.e. ~$400M of reduction) and a 6.0x multiple:

| 2027 EBITDA | Implied equity value/share |
|---|---|
| $1.6B | $4.03 |
| $2.0B | $8.24 |
| $2.4B | $12.45 |
| $2.9B | $17.70 |

Each $400M of EBITDA is worth roughly $4.20/share at 6x. That is the definition of a levered call option on hot-rolled coil, and it is why the stock traded between $7.73 and $16.70 over twelve months.

On earnings, $12.26 is 24x consensus 2027 EPS of $0.51 against a peer median P/E of 18.1 (CMC 17.4, WS 18.1); trailing EPS is $(2.90). Consensus price targets are mean $11.80, median $11.75, low $10.00, high $15.00 — the mean and median sit *below* spot, and the street high is below my bull case. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection names that are cheap relative to rising estimates; that condition is not met here, because the multiple already embeds the improved estimates and the target distribution is centered below the price. The rating mix has drifted mildly negative (13 covering analysts: 1 buy, 11 hold, 1 strong sell), unchanged in tone over three months.

Cheapness is not the case for this stock. The case, if there is one, would have to be that Section 232 at 50% permanently resets domestic HRC above the historical band and that consensus is wrong to embed reversion toward $851/nt. That is plausible but policy-dependent, and the USMCA mandatory joint review beginning in 2026 is a two-way risk to the melted-and-poured provisions the 10-K credits with limiting foreign substrate. I score valuation as approximately fair with symmetric-magnitude but asymmetric-probability risk, not as a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural setting is adverse and unchanged: EAFs are over 70% of U.S. steel production and more than 50% of flat-rolled. Management's thesis is that expensive prime scrap ($458/lt busheling in Q2 versus a ~$385-400/lt ten-year average) plus new EAF flat-rolled capacity widens the integrated cost advantage because Cliffs supplies the vast majority of its own ore. The mechanism is coherent. The evidence that it converts to superior returns is absent over the relevant period: H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA margin was 3.8%, FY2025 was 0.2%, and the five-year TSR index to December 2025 was 91.20 against a proxy peer group at 324.78 — a gap of about 233 points and a cumulative loss of roughly 8.8% for shareholders against roughly +225% for peers. At the utilization and price levels actually achieved in 2024-2025, fixed-cost absorption and ~$600M of annual interest swamped the ore advantage. Q2 2026 and the Q3 guide demonstrate the advantage is real at high utilization and high prices; it has not proven to be a through-cycle moat.

The footprint is shrinking. During 2025 six operations were fully or partially idled or permanently closed (Dearborn BF/BOF/caster idled; Steelton, Conshohocken and Riverdale closed at ~$300M of employee, impairment and exit costs; Minorca idled and Hibbing partially idled). In Q2 2026 the Gary plate finishing line and one of two Burns Harbor plate mills were idled, consistent with Q2 plate volumes down 21%. Rationalization improves unit economics and reduces the option value of a demand recovery.

Two positions are genuinely differentiated. Cliffs is the only U.S. producer of grain-oriented electrical steel, with Butler capacity rising ~25% by 2028 into a transformer market tightened by AI-driven electricity demand and now protected by the April 2026 derivative-product expansion; stainless and electrical together were only 552k of 16,229k FY2025 tons, so the niche is high-quality but small. Second, the 10-K discloses a Q4 2025 production trial in which Cliffs steel was stamped into exposed automotive parts with no defects using a customer's existing aluminum-forming equipment, with routine production and delivery following. Against 50% aluminum tariffs and a 2025 fire at a U.S. aluminum producer, that is the most specific competitive evidence in the filing, though the customer is unnamed and volumes unquantified. Management's claim of taking share "at will" from the other integrated producer is an assertion with no independent corroboration.

## Risk Assessment

**Labor is the largest unhedgeable binary.** CBAs expire in 2026 at Burns Harbor, Cleveland, Coatesville, Columbus Coatings, Coshocton, Indiana Harbor, Mansfield, Toledo HBI, Warren cokemaking, and all iron ore mining operations. Over 18,000 employees are covered. A stoppage during the strongest pricing environment since 2021 would be extraordinarily costly, and the bargaining backdrop includes two consecutive loss years, six 2025 idlings or closures, and $19.02M of 2025 CEO compensation. The company renegotiated all 2025 expirations without disruption and negotiations reportedly began constructively, so settlement is the base case — but the tail cannot be hedged and is not visible in the current multiple.

**HRC elasticity.** With roughly 55-60% of flat-rolled volume spot or index-linked, a reversion from the $1,079/nt Q2 average to the $851/nt FY2025 average — a ~$200/nt decline on ~57% of ~16.7M tons — is on the order of $1.9B of revenue and gross-margin headwind before cost offsets, enough to return EBITDA toward break-even. The 10-Q's disclosed $66M sensitivity to a 10% HRC move covers only the derivative book and should not be read as total exposure.

**The hedge book now caps upside.** HRC sales swaps grew from 220,220 net tons at year-end to 583,450 net tons at June 30 (July 2026-September 2027 tenor), with $71M of net losses on sales swaps scheduled to reclassify from AOCI into revenues over the next twelve months plus $23M into COGS. Whether this is prudent cash-flow certainty during deleveraging or an ill-timed short is not resolvable from the disclosure; the accounting effect is a drag on reported revenue if prices hold.

**Unquantified legal and legacy exposure.** The Mesabi Metallics adversary proceeding, with antitrust and tortious-interference claims subject to trebling, goes to trial in Delaware in May 2027 with no disclosed damages claim or reserve. The UMWA 1974 Pension Plan assessed a withdrawal liability during 2025 relating to mines divested in 2015; the amount is not disclosed. Revised NESHAP rules for taconite, integrated steel, lime and coke carry compliance obligations beginning in 2027, with the 10-K stating impacts "could be material" if upheld in current form; Empire's ARO includes a $143M discounted selenium liability with Tilden's share at $32M.

**Governance.** The July 24, 2026 Item 5.02 filing formalized Celso Goncalves — the CEO's son, CFO since 2021 — as President and CFO and elected him to the board, at a company where the father holds the combined Chairman, President and CEO roles. Q1 2026 equity awards shifted from relative TSR to approximately 2.8 million market stock units measured on *absolute* stock price appreciation from a $10.55 grant base with a 50-150% payout band, RSUs "reduced to a nominal level," and MSUs treated as fully vested at December 31, 2026 for retirement-eligible management. Whether payout timing as distinct from expense recognition accelerates is not established by the 10-Q text. CEO total compensation rose from $15.25M to $19.02M in a year with $37M of Adjusted EBITDA and a $1,428M loss. Each element is individually defensible; together they reduce the mechanisms for holding management accountable for relative underperformance at the point where relative underperformance is most severe.

**Insider activity.** The CEO sold 3,000,000 shares for $37.26M on February 11, 2026, two days after the 10-K reporting the largest loss in company history and containing conditional going-concern language, and received 650,900 RSUs plus 650,900 MSUs on February 18. The COO sold 200,000 shares at $10.46 on February 17. The CFO sold 214,308 shares at $13.41 on June 5, roughly 54% of his then-reported holdings, about seven weeks before the Q2 print. The only offsetting purchase was a director's $199,626 open-market buy. I do not infer motive from timing — the February transactions cluster around the annual grant cycle, plan adoption dates are not visible, and reported post-transaction holdings on a single Form 4 line do not establish total beneficial ownership. But this is clustered selling by three C-suite insiders within days, including one disposition exceeding half of reported holdings, which meets the threshold at which selling stops being routine wealth management. Aggregate insider selling substantially exceeded buying.

**Accounting.** A Q3 2025 error in the accrual for certain employment costs understated cost of goods sold across Q1/Q2 2025 and annual and interim periods for 2022-2024. It was deemed immaterial and prior periods revised, with controls concluded effective. A multi-year accrual error at a company with $19.5B of COGS warrants monitoring. The Q2 cash flow statement also carries an unexplained "Other" non-cash adjustment of +$73M in the quarter and +$130M in the half.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 62.7% against 69.3% one-year realized — options are modestly cheap to realized, and IV has collapsed from 137.5% on July 20 as the stock rose 32%. Open-interest positioning is call-tilted (put/call OI 0.37, put/call volume 0.51, skew described as call-heavy at -7.2%), consistent with bullish or short-covering positioning against 14.8% short interest and 4.2 days to cover. The term structure is backwardated (62.7% near versus 59.7% at six months), indicating priced near-term event risk, and there is one notable block: 1,872 contracts of the August 14 $12 put against 356 open interest (5.3x), roughly at the money. Read together, the structure shows bullish open interest with fresh near-dated protection layered on after the rally. For holders who want to retain upside through the Q3 print while capping the labor and HRC tail, protection is relatively inexpensive versus realized volatility. This does not change the fundamental view; it slightly supports the case for reducing exposure via hedges rather than outright sales for tax-constrained holders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $17.50 (+43%).** Section 232 at 50%, expanded coverage, and imports at 18% of consumption hold domestic HRC near the $1,079/nt Q2 average through 2027. The 2026 labor round settles without disruption. Fixed-price contracts reset up by approximately $500M for 2027, the ~$400M of property sales close in H2 2026, and gross debt falls toward $7.0-7.25B. 2027 EBITDA reaches $2.85-2.9B, leverage drops below 2.5x, and the multiple holds at 6.0-6.5x. Aluminum-to-steel substitution converts, and GOES capacity expansion captures transformer scarcity. Equity value: $17.50-19.50. This path requires three unverified items to compound simultaneously and would put the stock above the current street high of $15.00.

**Bear case — $7.50 (-39%).** New EAF flat-rolled capacity plus flat-to-down automotive (NA production ~3.9M units in Q2 2026 versus 15.3M annually against a ~17M pre-COVID average) pull HRC back toward $900/nt. The hedge book's $71M of AOCI losses lands into revenue. 2027 EBITDA falls to $1.5-1.6B, working capital consumes the H2 cash bridge, and gross debt stays at or above $7.7B, putting leverage near 5x. At 6x on $1.6B the model-implied equity is roughly $4/share; the stock more likely trades on asset and option value near the March 2026 low of $7.73, as it did when the trailing run-rate was near zero. A labor stoppage or an adverse Mesabi outcome extends the tail below that.

**Base case — $12.00 (-2%).** HRC moderates from the Q2 peak but stays well above 2025 levels. Q3 lands near guidance, Q4 is modestly better, and 2027 EBITDA settles at $2.2-2.3B — above consensus, below management's case, as the reset benefit partly materializes and property sales close late. Net debt falls to roughly $7.30B. At 6.0-6.4x that is $10.25-12.20 of equity value, consistent with the $11.80 consensus mean.

Probability-weighted at 28% bull / 44% base / 28% bear: $12.28, essentially the current price. Zero expected return with 39% quantified downside, a fat lower tail, and two unhedgeable binaries inside the horizon is not adequate compensation for the equity of a business carrying $7.7B of debt against $5.8B of book equity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $12.00 (-2%); bull $17.50 (+43%); bear $7.50 (-39%). Horizon 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength — the stock is up 32% since July 20 and 59% off the March low, and the price now discounts the guided run-rate. Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized to the labor outcome rather than the earnings trajectory, ideally before the USW negotiations reach the expiry dates concentrated in the second half of 2026. For holders unwilling to sell, near-dated puts are modestly cheap to realized volatility (62.7% IV versus 69.3% HV) and are a reasonable substitute. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) A ratified multi-year USW agreement covering the Burns Harbor / Cleveland / Indiana Harbor cluster and the iron ore mines without a stoppage, which removes the largest unpriced binary. (2) A Q3 print at or above $575M Adjusted EBITDA with operating cash flow above $400M *and* the payables/accrued contribution below $100M, demonstrating cash conversion independent of working-capital timing. (3) Gross debt at or below $7.30B at year-end 2026 with the ~$400M of property sales closed and visible in the cash flow statement. (4) A price near $9.50-10.00 on unchanged guidance, which would restore roughly 20-25% of upside to the base case and reprice the levered option with margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) A work stoppage at any integrated plant or the ore mines. (2) HRC sustained below $950/nt for a full quarter, which cuts roughly $600-700M from the annualized EBITDA bridge and pushes leverage above 4x. (3) Net ABL borrowings rising again in Q3 or Q4 2026 rather than falling, which would establish that the Q2 repayment was a working-capital artifact. (4) Exercise of the $500M MinnTac option, which would consume cash directly against the stated deleveraging target. (5) Any disclosed damages estimate or reserve on Mesabi Metallics or the UMWA withdrawal liability that is material relative to $3.1B of liquidity.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report in late October, which tests the $575M guide, the working-capital-neutral cash question, and the H2 debt path. Labor developments should be monitored continuously through year-end. The Burns Harbor C reline capex step-up in H1 2027 and the May 2027 Mesabi trial define the second-stage reassessment.

## Conclusion

The four-report record on this name has been AVOID at scores rising from 3.8 to 4.1. The operating basis for that stance has genuinely changed: Q2 2026 delivered a $211M sequential increase in Steelmaking cash margin, Adjusted EBITDA tripled to $286M, interest coverage moved from 0.06x for FY2025 to 1.83x, and the maturity schedule is empty until June 2028 with roughly $3.0B of undrawn revolver behind a covenant that only springs on a liquidity trigger. Calling this equity impaired is no longer supportable, and the estimate revision cycle — +110.6% on the current quarter, +40.6% on the current year — is real rather than manufactured.

What blocks an upgrade past REDUCE is price, not business trajectory. At $12.26 the $14.70B enterprise value already discounts $2.25-2.45B of forward EBITDA at a normal steel multiple, which is the guided run-rate carried unchanged into 2027. Consensus 2027 EPS of $0.51 implies closer to $2.0B, worth roughly $8.25/share on the same arithmetic, and the consensus target mean of $11.80 sits below spot. With 52% of enterprise value accruing to debt, every $400M of EBITDA variance moves the equity about $4.20/share, and the two largest sources of variance — the 2026 labor round covering nearly the entire integrated footprint and hot-rolled coil price levels supported chiefly by a 50% tariff and a USMCA review that begins this year — are outside management's control. Layer on a governance structure that concentrated further in July, clustered C-suite selling including a CFO disposition of 54% of reported holdings weeks before the beat, an equity plan redesigned to absolute price appreciation with a 50% floor off a $10.55 base, and a forward hedge book that caps upside, and the risk-adjusted expected return is below fair.
