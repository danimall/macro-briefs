**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Preferred Bank is one of the more profitable independent commercial banks in the United States, running a ~$7.7B balance sheet at an ROA of roughly 1.75%, an ROE near 17%, and an efficiency ratio in the low 30s, placing it in the top decile of the industry. The bank earns this through a focused Southern California commercial model built on commercial real estate, C&I, and trade finance, with a lean branch network and disciplined expense control. The current price of ~$101–103 puts it at about 9.5x trailing earnings and a 3.1% dividend yield, well below the regional-bank peer median of ~13.6x.

The dominant story over the past three quarters has been a credit scare and its resolution. In Q4 2025 management disclosed a single ~$121–123M classified loan relationship (roughly 2% of the book), where the borrower fell behind on interest because the guarantors became entangled in litigation with other lenders. That worry has substantially unwound: in Q2 2026, nonperforming loans fell 41.5% quarter-over-quarter to $98.9M and classified assets fell 33.9% to $103.8M, primarily through $68.4M of note sales at par (with only a $950K loss on one note) plus payoffs and upgrades. This is a materially better outcome than the workout-or-foreclosure scenario feared in January, and it validates management's claim that the exposures were well-secured.

Two features temper the enthusiasm. First, PFBC has an unusual negative sensitivity to Fed rate cuts: roughly 70% of loans are floating-rate and reprice down quickly, while its heavy time-deposit funding (over half of deposits) makes funding costs sticky. Most regional-bank theses want cuts; PFBC's margin is pressured by them. Second, the recent headline beats were flattered by one-time items — a $2.9M interest recovery in Q2 2026 and a $0.20/share OREO gain in Q4 2025 — while reserve coverage has thinned (ACL/loans 1.22%, ACL/NPL coverage below 1.0x) even as management plans to accelerate loan growth. With the stock near a two-year high, near consensus targets, and the largest known risk already resolving, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate PFBC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Preferred Bank is a state-chartered California commercial bank headquartered in Los Angeles, operating twelve California branches, two New York branches (Manhattan and Flushing), one branch in Sugar Land, Texas, and a loan production office in Sunnyvale. It serves small-to-mid-sized businesses, entrepreneurs, real estate developers, professionals, and high-net-worth individuals. Founded as a Chinese-American bank, its customer base is now described as predominantly "diversified mainstream market," while still benefiting from continued Chinese and East Asian migration to California.

A structural note that matters for data interpretation: PFBC is a bank, not a bank holding company, so it files its 10-K and 10-Q reports with the FDIC rather than the SEC/EDGAR. Standardized EDGAR/XBRL pipelines return nulls, and Altman-Z or Piotroski screens are both unavailable and inappropriate for a bank. The financial analysis here is built from the bank's earnings releases (which contain full unaudited statements) and management's earnings-call commentary.

As of 6/30/2026, gross loans stood at $6.24B: total CRE/residential mortgage of ~$3.91B (commercial RE $3.17B, residential RE $737M), construction ~$649M, and C&I ~$1.68B. The C&I segment is large and growing, providing some diversification away from a book that is still 63% CRE and mortgage. Total assets were $7.73B, deposits $6.47B, with noninterest-bearing deposits at just 12% (~$755M) and time certificates over half of deposits (~$3.5B), the structural reason funding costs are sticky.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is genuinely elite.** FY2025 net income was $134M, EPS $10.41. Q2 2026 delivered net income of $33.5M and diluted EPS of $2.78 (a ~$0.07 beat versus the $2.71 consensus), with ROA of 1.75% and ROE of 17.05%. First-half 2026 net income was $64.7M (+2.9% YoY), and diluted EPS of $5.32 rose 10.8% YoY; the gap between net income growth and EPS growth reflects the share buybacks that reduced the count roughly 7% over the year. The efficiency ratio in the low-30s versus 55–65% for typical peers is the source of this profitability and is a durable structural advantage.

**Net interest margin is the central financial dynamic and it is not straightforward.** NIM ran 3.85% (Q2 2025), 3.74% (Q4 2025), 3.57% (Q1 2026), 3.73% (Q2 2026). The Q2 2026 rebound was flattered by a $2.9M one-time interest recovery on resolved nonaccrual loans; the underlying clean NIM is closer to ~3.6%. Loan yields fell from 7.54% to 7.20% over the year while cost of deposits improved only gradually from 3.42% to 3.07%. With ~70% floating-rate loans and heavy CD funding, PFBC is asset-sensitive in a way that makes it the rare bank hurt by Fed cuts: loan income reprices down fast, funding relief comes slowly. Roughly $1.3B of CDs repriced in Q1 2026 (out at 3.96%, in at 3.70–3.80%), a smaller benefit than management had hoped.

**The balance sheet is clean and well-capitalized.** The securities book is modest (~$544M) with an AOCI mark of only -$20M, so there are no large hidden bond losses of the kind that plagued the 2023-era regional-bank episode. Capital ratios at 6/30/2026 were solid: TCE 10.26%, Tier 1 leverage 10.55%, CET1 11.06%, total capital 13.74%. Book value per share grew to $66.95 from $64.83 at year-end 2025, meaning the stock trades at roughly 1.5x book. The total capital ratio declined from 14.47% at year-end as capital was consumed by loan growth and buybacks, a manageable but worth-monitoring trend given the growth plans.

**Earnings quality is high but the recent prints deserve normalization.** Earnings are net-interest-income driven and cash-based. Both recent quarters, however, carried non-recurring benefits (the Q2 2026 interest recovery, the Q4 2025 OREO gain), and management has flagged that 2025 letter-of-credit fee income was "very, very strong" and hard to repeat. Normalized run-rate earnings power is modestly below the headline prints. The four-quarter surprise record is mixed: 2 beats, 2 misses, with the beats concentrated in quarters carrying one-time items.

## Growth Analysis

Loan growth was +7.3% in FY2025, and management is budgeting a higher rate for 2026 on reviving C&I and CRE demand. Q2 2026 loan growth was +2.0% linked-quarter ($124.8M), with deposits up 0.8%. This is respectable mid-to-high-single-digit balance-sheet growth for a bank of this size, but it is not a growth engine that re-rates the multiple on its own.

The earnings growth outlook is muted. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $10.65 and next-year at $11.24, implying roughly 5–6% forward EPS growth: a combination of loan growth, share-count reduction, and NIM stabilization. Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year +0.5% over 90 days, next year +1.3%, with no meaningful directional shift. This does not signal the kind of systematic underestimate of fundamentals seen with 30%+ revision velocity or 40%+ revenue growth. The growth story is steady compounding, not acceleration.

The macro overhang cuts against growth in a specific way here. If the Fed continues cutting, PFBC's floating-rate book compresses margin faster than funding relief arrives, offsetting some of the benefit of faster loan growth. Management explicitly declined to over-promise on 2026, citing policy and "very active government" uncertainty. R&D and market expansion are not relevant levers for a commercial bank; growth is a function of loan production, margin, and the rate path.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$101–103, PFBC trades at ~9.5x trailing EPS of $10.41 and ~9.4x forward EPS of $10.65, with a 3.1% dividend yield and ~1.5x book value. Against a regional-bank peer median P/E of 13.6x (HFWA 14.2, TMP 7.8, WABC 12.8, SBSI 14.4, CFFN 13.6), PFBC screens cheap on a headline basis.

The discount is not without justification. A single-digit P/E on a bank with 63% CRE/mortgage concentration, thinning reserve coverage, a counterintuitive negative rate-cut sensitivity, and recent earnings flattered by one-time items is at least partly a discount for genuine risk, not a pure mispricing. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal; it requires an identifiable catalyst for re-rating.

The catalyst that would support a higher multiple — resolution of the credit scare — has largely already occurred and is arguably already in the price. The stock has climbed from an $81.50 low in late January (the credit-disclosure trough) back toward a two-year high, reaching $112.26 intraday on 7/1, before settling in the low $100s. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $100, mean $105, median $102, high $116, with Stephens recently raising to $110. The stock trades essentially at consensus. Given the muted forward growth (~5–6% EPS) and flat estimate revisions, analyst targets are not stale floors that will be revised sharply upward; the revision data confirms consensus is stable, not chasing an accelerating fundamental.

On a normalized basis, applying ~10.5–11x to a normalized ~$10.50 EPS (adjusting the recent prints modestly downward for one-time items) yields a fair value in the $110–115 range, roughly in line with the top of the analyst range and offering single-digit upside from here. The absolute yield of 3.1% provides some support but is below the peer median of 3.4%. The valuation is fair-to-modestly-attractive, not the asymmetric setup that existed at $83 in January. I score valuation 6.0: cheap on headline metrics, but with the discount partly earned and the re-rating catalyst largely spent.

## Competitive Landscape

PFBC competes in the crowded Southern California commercial banking market against ethnic-Chinese-oriented and mainstream banks (East West Bancorp, Cathay General, Hanmi, Hope Bancorp) and national money-center banks aggressively pricing CDs in its footprint. Its differentiation is operational efficiency and profitability rather than scale. A sub-34% efficiency ratio and ~1.75% ROA are durable structural advantages built on a lean cost base and disciplined underwriting; competitors would need years to replicate this, and it is the reason the bank sustains a 17% ROE without excessive leverage.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-pressured rather than improving. The intense deposit competition, including money-center banks promoting CDs in PFBC's markets, is a headwind to funding costs and to the loan-to-deposit ratio during growth. Management noted the industry-wide phenomenon that deposit costs actually ticked up in some periods despite Fed cuts, evidence of how competitive the funding side has become. The C&I book's growth provides useful diversification and relationship depth. Net, the moat (efficiency, profitability, relationship lending) is intact, but the operating environment is not getting easier, which is why competitive position sits at 6.5 rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration is the dominant structural risk.** Roughly $3.9B of $6.2B loans (63%) are CRE/residential mortgage, plus $649M of construction, tying the book tightly to the California property cycle. This is permanently part of the model and largely priced, but it magnifies the impact of any single large credit.

**The classified relationship is resolving, which is the most important recent development.** The favorable outcome removes the most acute overhang, and it is the primary reason the stock recovered from $83 to the low $100s.

**Reserve coverage is thinning while the bank grows faster.** ACL/loans fell from 1.30% (YE2025) to 1.24% (Q1) to 1.22% (Q2), and ACL/NPL coverage swung to below 1.0x (0.84x in Q2). This partly reflects the well-secured nature of the NPLs (one multifamily nonaccrual had a $19.5M balance against a $49M appraisal), but combined with plans to accelerate loan growth and pull back buybacks, it means the bank is thinner-cushioned than a year ago if a new large credit surfaces. An analyst pointedly challenged management on this on the call.

**The counterintuitive rate-cut vulnerability is under-appreciated.** With 70% floating loans and heavy CD funding, PFBC's margin compresses on Fed cuts. Management cited policy uncertainty as a live variable, and continued easing would be a real, and unusually directioned, headwind relative to liability-sensitive peers.

**Guarantor-litigation contagion is a novel, hard-to-screen vector.** The classified relationship went bad not because the properties failed but because the guarantors got tied up in litigation with other banks, diverting cash flow. This kind of cross-lender entanglement is difficult to underwrite around in concentrated relationship lending.

**Key-person and governance risk.** Founder-Chairman-CEO Li Yu has run the bank for decades and remains hands-on, personally fielding detailed credit questions. Succession is a genuine long-term concern. Governance and insider-transaction data could not be sourced because PFBC files with the FDIC rather than the SEC, a data gap worth flagging, not a red flag in itself.

The elevated short interest (10.9% of float, 8 days to cover) reflects institutional skepticism, likely centered on the CRE concentration and the rate-cut sensitivity rather than an imminent distress thesis.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-constructive and does not challenge the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 29.2% versus 24.9% one-year historical volatility (modestly rich, spread of 4.4pp), with the IV having fallen from 34.7% to 29.2% since early June as the price rose 10%; uncertainty has drained out as the credit story resolved. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.6%), the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.10 (down from 0.33), and the term structure is flat (near 29.2% ≈ far 29.4%). The implied ±6.4% move at the nearest expiry is modest. There is no elevated put demand that would flag downside positioning against the recovery; options traders are pricing a calmer, resolved situation, consistent with the fundamental read that the acute credit risk has passed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115–125):** PFBC continues to deliver top-decile profitability (ROA ~1.75%, ROE ~17%) as the credit scare fully clears, NIM stabilizes in the ~3.6–3.7% range as CD costs finally reset lower, and loan growth accelerates into reviving C&I/CRE demand. The 9.5x multiple re-rates toward 11–12x as the market gains confidence that the classified relationship was an isolated event. Continued buybacks plus a 3.1% and rising dividend compound book value. If the Fed holds rather than cuts, the asset-sensitivity headwind reverses into a tailwind. Fair value in the mid-$110s to mid-$120s.

**Bear Case ($75–85):** The Fed cuts further, compressing PFBC's floating-rate margin faster than sticky CD funding can relieve, and clean NIM drifts below 3.5%. A new large CRE or construction credit surfaces into a book with thinning reserves (ACL/NPL below 1.0x) and a California property cycle under pressure, forcing elevated provisions and denting the elite ROA. One-time items normalize out of earnings, revealing run-rate EPS below consensus. The multiple compresses back toward the January trough as the "isolated credit event" narrative frays. Downside to the low $80s, roughly -18% to -25% from current levels.

The base case sits between these: steady mid-single-digit EPS growth, NIM in the ~3.6% area, the credit issue staying resolved, and the multiple holding roughly where it is. That produces a stock that grinds modestly higher on earnings and dividend accrual, with fair value around $110–115.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (next print ~late October 2026) and the Fed rate path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clean NIM (ex-one-time items) stabilizes at or above 3.65% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating that CD funding costs have genuinely reset and the rate-cut vulnerability is contained.
- Reserve coverage rebuilds toward 1.3% ACL/loans and ACL/NPL back above 1.0x while loan growth continues, removing the thinning-cushion concern.
- A pullback toward the high-$80s/low-$90s on no fundamental deterioration, restoring the asymmetric setup that existed in January.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new large classified credit relationship (>$75M) surfaces, especially if reserves prove inadequate and provisions spike.
- Clean NIM drifts below 3.4% as continued Fed cuts outrun funding relief, structurally impairing the ROA advantage.
- A dividend cut or a capital ratio decline signaling the growth-and-buyback plan is stressing the balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (~late October 2026), which will show whether the credit resolution held, how NIM behaved absent one-time recoveries, and whether reserve coverage rebuilt.

## Conclusion

Preferred Bank is a high-quality, elite-profitability commercial bank trading at an undemanding multiple, and the resolution of the Q4 2025 credit scare removes the sharpest near-term risk. The trajectory of our own scoring on this name — from a 7.0 BUY in May to a 6.0 HOLD in June — reflected the credit disclosure and NIM compression that have since partially reversed; the Q2 2026 de-risking (NPLs -41.5%, classified assets -33.9% via par note sales) stabilizes rather than continues that decline. But it does not restore a BUY. The stock has already recovered near a two-year high and near consensus targets, forward growth is muted at ~5–6% EPS with flat estimate revisions, recent beats were flattered by one-time items, and the counterintuitive negative rate-cut sensitivity plus thinning reserves against 63% CRE concentration keep the risk/reward balanced. The cheapness is partly earned rather than a clean mispricing, and the re-rating catalyst (credit resolution) is largely spent.

We rate PFBC a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale: this is a well-run compounder worth owning for the 3.1% yield, shrinking share count, and durable profitability, but the entry point at ~$101–103 offers only single-digit upside to a base-case fair value of ~$110–115. The asymmetric opportunity was at $83 in January. At today's price, patience for a pullback toward the low $90s, or confirmation that clean NIM and reserve coverage are rebuilding, would justify an upgrade. Overall score: 6.05.
