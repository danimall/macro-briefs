## Executive Summary

BJ's Wholesale Club is a structurally sound regional warehouse club with a membership annuity that produces roughly 62% of operating profit, a 90% tenured renewal rate, and net leverage of 0.64x. Those facts have not changed. What has changed over the past two reporting cycles is the composition of growth. Total comparable club sales accelerated from +1.0% in FY2025 to +6.3% in Q1 FY2026, but merchandise comps decelerated over the same interval from +2.6% to +1.5%, and from +3.9% in the year-ago first quarter. Gasoline comps swung from −1.6% to +4.8%, and gasoline-and-other went from 18% to 21% of net sales. Because the company explicitly targets a stable gross profit *per gallon* in absolute dollars, that revenue mix shift adds little incremental profit while diluting percentage margin. The most-quoted metric is improving while the profit-relevant metric is slowing.

The valuation reflects a mixed picture rather than a mispricing. At $94.19, BJ's trades at 21.6x trailing-twelve-month adjusted EPS of $4.36, 20.7x the current-year consensus of $4.54 (22 analysts, revised up 0.5% over 30 days), and 19.2x next year's $4.90. Peer median P/E in the group is 21.0x; Costco sits at 47.7x, Dollar General at 17.9x, Target at 19.8x. BJ's is neither cheap nor stretched against that set. The harder number is free cash flow: FY2025 adjusted FCF of $331.0M against a $12.03B market cap is a 2.75% yield, because capex of $702.0M ran at 2.43x depreciation and amortization of $288.6M. An investor at this price is underwriting the return on that reinvestment, including a first-ever out-of-region expansion into Texas whose returns will not appear in comparable-club data for at least thirteen months.

This is our fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it unchanged should be higher. It is not unchanged: the overall score moves from 5.7 to 5.4, extending a decline from 6.5 in early May. The rationale is not the share price, which is up 2.5% since the last review. It is that (a) the merchandise-comp deceleration and margin-investment language remain the operative facts with no new quarter to refute them, (b) an approximately 210bp effective tax rate step-up to 27.4% is a ~$0.13 per-share headwind embedded in the modest 3% current-year EPS growth consensus, and (c) a Q2 print is imminent with near-term implied volatility at 58.1% against 30.2% realized — the market is pricing a ±6.9% move one week out, and the May 22 precedent was a roughly 10% decline on an EPS beat with in-line guidance. Base case $100 (+6.2%), bull $118 (+25.3%), bear $79 (−16.1%). Probability-weighting those gives an expected return in the mid-single digits, below what equity risk should command. Own it if you own it; there is no reason to initiate ahead of the print.

## Company Overview

BJ's operated 263 clubs and 199 gas stations across 21 states at fiscal year end (January 31, 2026), rising to 267 clubs and 205 gas stations across 22 states by the Q1 10-Q filing date of May 28, 2026. The twenty-second state is Texas — a genuine departure from a footprint the 10-K describes as "concentrated primarily in the eastern half of the United States."

The economics are standard club: narrow high-velocity assortment bought direct, sold at low gross margin to fee-paying members, with inventory largely financed by vendor terms. The 10-K states the mechanism plainly — clubs "generate cash from the sale of a large portion of their inventory before they are required to pay merchandise vendors." Perishables, grocery and sundries were ~87% of FY2025 merchandise sales; general merchandise and services ~13%. Private label (Wellsley Farms, Berkley Jensen) is ~27% of net sales excluding gasoline. Membership fee income was $499.8M in FY2025 and $511.7M on a trailing-twelve-month basis through May 2, 2026, after fees rose to $60/$120 from $55/$110 effective January 1, 2025.

Geographic concentration is material and disclosed: New York 50 clubs, Florida 43, Massachusetts 26, New Jersey 24, with the New York metropolitan area at 23% of FY2025 net sales across 46 clubs. One reportable segment; no customer above 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profit.** FY2025 total revenue of $21,457M grew 4.7%; operating income of $816.6M grew 5.7% on a 3.81% margin; net income of $578.4M grew 8.2%; diluted EPS of $4.38 grew 9.5%. Q1 FY2026 revenue rose 9.9% to $5,661M, but operating income margin compressed to 3.67% from 3.95% and net income was $142.7M ($1.10 diluted).

Two identifiable drivers explain the Q1 margin compression, and they are different in character. Gasoline mix is dilutive to percentage margin by construction. Pre-opening expense of $14.0M versus $5.0M — a $9.0M swing — accounts for roughly 16bp of the 28bp decline on its own. Pre-opening expense is a growth-investment cost rather than unit-economics deterioration, but it is real cash and it persists while the opening cadence is elevated.

**The margin detail matters more than the headline.** Reported cost of sales improved to 83.3% of net sales in FY2025 from 83.5%, but the 10-K states merchandise gross margin rate "remained flat" — the 20bp improvement is mix arithmetic. In Q1 FY2026 cost of sales rose to 83.8% from 83.1%, with merchandise gross margin down roughly 10bp "primarily driven by the Company's continued investments in pricing, partially offset by tariff refund benefits recognized in the quarter." Two things follow: management is actively giving margin back to defend the value gap, and the offset was episodic. The refund magnitude is not quantified, so the underlying merchandise margin trend is somewhat worse than the reported 10bp.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $1,030.1M in FY2025 grew 14.3% and exceeded net income by $451.7M — a genuine earnings-quality positive. Adjusted FCF was $331.0M after $702.0M of net capex. Q1 FY2026 adjusted FCF was −$42.0M (OCF $140.0M less capex $182.0M). That figure should not be annualized: the 10-Q states cash flows are seasonally lowest in the first quarter, and Q1 absorbed a discrete $91.2M payment for purchased transferable tax credits. Adding that back as a sensitivity gives roughly +$49M against $67.6M in the prior-year quarter — still a decline, and a further ~$85M of credit purchases is committed for Q1 FY2027, so the outflow recurs rather than reverses.

**Balance sheet.** At May 2, 2026: total assets $7,929.0M, liabilities $5,802.7M, equity $2,126.3M. Company-defined debt of $775.0M comprises $375.0M drawn on the ABL revolver (from $120.0M at year end) plus a $400.0M first lien term loan, against $27.8M cash — net debt $747.2M, or 0.64x trailing adjusted EBITDA of $1,169.9M. The term loan has no financial maintenance covenants and the excess-cash-flow sweep triggers only above 3.50x. Operating lease liabilities of $2,087.3M sit outside that figure; adding them to net debt and rent back to EBITDA gives roughly 1.8x lease-adjusted leverage. Altman Z of 3.76 sits in the safe zone; ROE is 26.3%. Finance lease liabilities from new right-of-use assets were $68.0M in Q1 versus $0.5M a year earlier, and total debt-like obligations therefore exceed $775.0M by an amount the filings reviewed do not quantify — several hundred million would not change the leverage character.

The current ratio of 0.73 is structural rather than distressed: current liabilities include $1,438.9M of vendor payables financing inventory and $285.2M of deferred membership revenue, a performance obligation satisfied by opening the doors.

**The one dateable balance-sheet task** is the ABL revolver maturing July 28, 2027. It is a $1.20B borrowing-base-constrained commitment, $375.0M drawn, and with cash at $27.8M it *is* the liquidity. $816.2M remained undrawn. Nothing in the filings signals difficulty, but the facility is currently funding buybacks.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend, and the 10-K says none is expected. Under the $1.00B authorization effective February 2025, FY2025 repurchases were 2.599M shares for $252.4M (~$97.11 average) and Q1 FY2026 alone was 2.114M shares for $206.6M (~$97.73 average) — 82% of the prior full year in one quarter, with $545.0M remaining. Shares outstanding fell 3.3% year over year to 127.694M. Both repurchase averages sit ~3-4% *above* today's $94.19, so the program has not yet demonstrated price discipline relative to the current quote. And the Q1 arithmetic is unambiguous: OCF $140.0M, capex $182.0M, treasury purchases $225.7M, net ABL borrowings +$255.0M. The buyback was revolver-funded. On a 0.64x-levered balance sheet at the seasonal cash trough that is defensible treasury management; it also means shareholder returns are currently additive to leverage rather than funded from surplus.

**Earnings delivery.** The last four reported quarters produced adjusted EPS of $1.14, $1.16, $0.96 and $1.10 — four beats, by 1.4%, 2.9%, 0.8% and 3.7%. The sum, $4.36, reconciles exactly to trailing-twelve-month adjusted EPS derived from the filings. The beat cadence is consistent but small; this is not a company that surprises by 5-10%, so consensus should be treated as a reasonably tight anchor rather than a low bar.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question decomposes into three parts.

**Merchandise demand is decelerating.** Merchandise comps: +2.8% (FY2024) → +2.6% (FY2025) → +1.5% (Q1 FY2026, against +3.9%). Within Q1, general merchandise and services grew +7.1% on consumer electronics strength while perishables, grocery and sundries — 69% of net sales — grew +0.7%, held back by "deflation in perishables, especially eggs, as well as decreases in household cleaning and health and beauty categories." The bull reading is that +0.7% during category deflation implies positive unit growth, and that a single quarter against a tough comparison is not a trend; the two-year stacked merchandise comp of +5.4% is respectable. The bear reading is that household cleaning and health-and-beauty declines are traffic and share issues, not price. This is genuinely unresolved on one quarter of data, which is a principal reason the rating stays at HOLD rather than moving in either direction.

**Membership fee income is the profit engine and the pricing lever is spent.** MFI grew 9.5% in FY2025 and 9.9% in Q1, driven largely by the January 2025 fee increase now lapped. Deferred membership revenue rose 9.4% year over year to $285.2M, which is a more encouraging forward indicator than the merchandise comp — though it is a contract liability for obligations satisfied within twelve months, not a backlog. The renewal rate has been flat at 90% for two consecutive year ends, and higher-tier penetration improved to 42% of members and 52% of merchandise spend from 39%/50%. With price exhausted, the remaining MFI levers are unit growth and further tier mix, both slower and more capital-intensive.

**Unit growth is accelerating into unfamiliar territory.** Clubs went from 263 to 267 between January 31 and May 28, 2026, capex rose 29.5% in Q1, and pre-opening expense nearly tripled. Secondary reporting places the first Texas club in Forney (April 24, 2026) with four to five DFW-area openings planned in 2026; the filings confirm the state count and club count but do not name Texas or give a target, so treat the store math as unconfirmed. The strategic point stands regardless: BJ's documented advantage is density (roughly 3x the next-largest club competitor's count in New England, 46 clubs in NY metro), and Texas trades that away for addressable market. No brand awareness, no member base, no local perishables supply chain in a network the 10-K says has been contained in-house since mid-2022, and entrenched Costco and Sam's Club competition in Sam's home state.

**Consensus and revisions.** Current-year EPS of $4.54 is up 0.5% over 30 days and 0.4% over 90; next year's $4.90 is up 0.3%/−0.5%. The current-quarter estimate has been cut 3.3% over 90 days to $1.16 while the full year edged up, so the near-term bar has come down slightly. Importantly, the $4.54 figure corroborates and slightly upgrades the secondary-sourced ~$4.52 in circulation, and shows no post-Q1 estimate deterioration. But this is nowhere near the revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection — it is roughly 3% current-year growth and 8% next-year growth, with a 210bp tax rate step-up (25.3% to a projected 27.4%, about $16.3M or $0.13 per share) absorbing much of the operating improvement.

## Competitive Landscape

BJ's is the clear number three in a three-player US club market, and the 10-K concedes that Costco and Sam's Club "have significantly greater financial and marketing resources." Three dynamics matter.

**Fee-price parity has arrived.** BJ's moved to $60/$120 in January 2025. Sam's Club reportedly raised to the identical $60/$120 effective May 1, 2026 (USA Today network reporting, June 24, 2026; not confirmed by a Walmart filing, and BJ's has not commented). If accurate, BJ's historical position as the cheapest club membership is gone, and future MFI growth must come from units and tier mix rather than price. This is the single competitive fact we consider most underappreciated in the current price.

**BJ's is investing price and says so.** The Q1 10-Q attributes merchandise margin decline to "continued investments in pricing," and the 10-K risk factors state that "price is the major competitive factor" and that competitor price cuts "could adversely impact our margins." Against two players with superior purchasing scale, price investment is defensive necessity, and it structurally caps margin expansion.

**Offsets are real.** Private label at ~27% of ex-gas sales carries higher margin and is a stated priority. Digital comps grew ~28% year over year (+63% two-year stack) on the existing club asset base through BOPIC, curbside and third-party delivery rather than a separate fulfillment network — capital-efficient share gain, though decelerating from +31.0% in Q4. BJ's Media Edge, the retail media network, is a structurally high-margin pool that is not separately quantified. And a 205-station fuel network is a meaningful traffic and membership-value driver even though the fixed cents-per-gallon target means little incremental profit dollar.

Net trajectory: stable-to-slightly-deteriorating. The moat is density-derived cost advantage plus switching friction, and the company is voluntarily diluting the density advantage to enter Texas while surrendering the fee-price advantage involuntarily. **Competitive position: 5.2.**

## Risk Assessment

**Merchandise comp deceleration is the primary risk.** The 10-K links SG&A leverage directly to comp growth. If merchandise comps settle near 1%, operating leverage disappears and the 3.8% operating margin drifts lower while pre-opening expense and depreciation from owned clubs rise.

**Two reported-result flatterers are unquantified.** Tariff refunds partly offset the Q1 merchandise margin decline, and purchased transferable tax credits ($91.2M in Q1, ~$85M committed for Q1 FY2027) support cash taxes and contributed to the FY2025 effective rate falling to 25.3%. Neither is sized in the filings. The underlying margin trend is worse than reported, by an amount that cannot be determined.

**Concentration and policy.** New York metro is 23% of net sales across 46 clubs. SNAP/EBT exposure is a full named risk factor, and BJ's own description of its target customer — "a price sensitive demographic with large household sizes" — makes it more benefit-sensitive than Costco's base; SNAP eligibility and work-requirement changes are a live policy variable. Gasoline margin volatility explicitly "affects our overall results for a fiscal quarter."

**Governance.** Combined chairman/president/CEO in Robert Eddy. The company has elected out of DGCL Section 203, prohibits stockholder action by written consent, eliminates stockholders' ability to call special meetings, authorizes blank-check preferred, and requires a two-thirds supermajority to amend the charter or bylaws. Incentive plan metrics include "comparable club sales" — whether that is total (gasoline-inflated) or merchandise comps is not confirmable from available extracts, and in a year with +4.8% gasoline comps the distinction is material to pay-for-performance quality.

**Insider activity is one-directional.** Across roughly 50 Form 4 line items from April through July 29, 2026, there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director. CEO Eddy sold on a regular mid-month cadence at $89.15-$100.02, including a July 29 option exercise at $17.00 and sale of 73,016 shares at $100.02, leaving 282,330 shares plus 189,484 options. COO Schmadeke sold 16,500 shares at $92.17 on June 10, leaving 20,471 — the majority of his unrestricted position. Chief Growth Officer Morningstar sold 14,872 shares in April; the Controller sold 2,050. The mid-month regularity is the signature of a 10b5-1 plan and diversification by executives concentrated in one equity is ordinary. But with estimate revisions essentially flat rather than surging, the insider-selling signal is not offset by an earnings inflection, and the complete absence of buying at a price roughly 13% below the 52-week high means there is no insider confirmation of the value case while the company itself has repurchased above the current price. **Management quality: 5.0.**

**Positive risk-factor change worth noting:** the 2005 FTC consent decree on member data safeguarding expired by its terms on September 20, 2025 — a real reduction in standing regulatory obligation. The 8-K record since November 2024 shows no executive departure, restatement, material acquisition or adverse event.

**A non-issue to dismiss explicitly:** retained earnings fell $501.4M in FY2025 despite $578.4M of net income. This is the retirement of a $936.4M treasury block, with the excess over par allocated between APIC and retained earnings; total stockholders' equity *rose* from $1,847.5M to $2,197.7M. Screens reading falling retained earnings as a loss or distribution will misjudge this company. Under ASU 2025-12, adopted in Q1 FY2026, the entire excess now goes to APIC.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term chain is pricing an event, not a direction. ATM implied volatility jumped from 34.3% on July 23 to 58.1% on August 13 while the price rose 2.3% — an 88th-percentile reading against 30.2% one-year realized volatility, in backwardation (58.1% at one week versus 30.9% at six months). The August 21 expiry implies a ±6.9% move, consistent with a Q2 report landing in the coming week. The magnitude is credible: the May 22 release produced a roughly 10% decline despite an adjusted EPS beat, because the market re-rated the merchandise comp and the margin outlook rather than the printed number.

Positioning is mixed and should not be over-read. The supplied skew reading is call-heavy at −17.8% in the front week, put/call open interest is 0.62 (call-tilted), yet front-week put/call *volume* is 44.34 with an unusual 1,269-contract print in the August 21 $85 puts against 378 open interest (3.4x) at 52.9% implied volatility. Someone is buying downside 9.5% out of the money into the print while aggregate open interest remains call-leaning. Short interest of 7.5% of float and 5.7 days to cover is moderate — enough to fuel a relief squeeze on a good print, not enough to constrain a bad one.

Practical takeaway: volatility is expensive, the event is one week away, and there is no fundamental urgency to pay for it. That supports waiting rather than initiating. **Risk factors: 5.2.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+25.3%)

Q1 merchandise softness proves transitory: egg and perishables deflation reverses, household cleaning and health-and-beauty stabilize, and merchandise comps recover to 3%+ against easier comparisons, restoring SG&A leverage. Membership fee income keeps compounding high single digits on club additions and tier mix as Club+/co-brand penetration extends past 42% of members, with the 9.4% growth in deferred membership revenue as the leading indicator. Texas clubs open into a large addressable market with productivity at or above the fleet, validating the capex step-up and converting a 2.43x-depreciation spend rate into visible unit-growth earnings. A 205-station fuel network reinforces the membership value proposition and drives club traffic. The buyback continues retiring 3%+ of shares annually against a 0.64x-levered balance sheet, and a consumer trade-down cycle pushes share toward the club channel where BJ's is the value option. Multiple re-rates to 24x on FY2027 EPS of $4.90.

### Bear Case — $79 (−16.1%)

Merchandise comps settle at 0-1%. Price investment continues because Sam's Club at fee parity and Costco at greater scale leave no alternative, so merchandise gross margin grinds lower without episodic tariff-refund offsets. The 210bp tax step-up to 27.4% plus rising depreciation from owned clubs (net PP&E $1,337M in FY2022 to $2,534M at May 2026) and persistent pre-opening expense keep EPS growth in the low single digits. Texas underperforms against entrenched competitors with no local brand or perishables infrastructure, and the capex is stranded. SNAP benefit reductions hit a price-sensitive, large-household member base. The ABL refinancing in July 2027 arrives with $375M+ drawn into a higher rate environment, raising the cost of the facility funding the buyback. The multiple compresses toward 17x on FY2027 estimates cut to ~$4.60.

### Base Case — $100 (+6.2%)

Merchandise comps hold in the 1.5-2.5% range. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $4.54 (+3%) with the tax step-up absorbing operating gains; FY2027 reaches ~$4.90 (+8%) on unit growth and share count reduction. Gasoline continues flattering the headline without adding much profit. Margin pressure persists but does not accelerate. The stock earns roughly 20.5x FY2027 EPS of $4.90, or equivalently 22x current-year $4.54 — approximately $100, matching the analyst median and sitting 2.5% below the $102.6 mean.

Probability-weighting these (base 55%, bull 20%, bear 25%) gives an expected return in the mid-single digits with no dividend. That is below what equity risk should earn over twelve months, and it is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE, while the base case sitting above the current price and a 1.5:1 bull/bear ratio keep it above REDUCE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the first checkpoint in days — the Q2 FY2026 report, which options pricing places on or before August 21, 2026.

**Base case target $100** (20.5x FY2027E $4.90; equivalently 22x FY2026E $4.54), **+6.2%** from $94.19.
**Bull case $118** (24x FY2027E $4.90), **+25.3%**.
**Bear case $79** (17x a cut FY2027E ~$4.60, matching the low analyst target), **−16.1%**.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Merchandise comps of 2.5%+ for two consecutive quarters with perishables/grocery/sundries above 2%, confirming the Q1 softness was deflation-driven rather than traffic-driven.
2. Merchandise gross margin flat or expanding year over year *without* a tariff-refund offset, evidencing that the price investment has stabilized the value gap rather than becoming permanent.
3. A price near $78-82 — the bottom of the analyst range and roughly 16x FY2027 estimates — which would restore genuine asymmetry on an unchanged fundamental picture.
4. Adjusted free cash flow inflecting toward $500M+ annually (capex moderating below 2x depreciation as the opening cadence normalizes), lifting the FCF yield above 4%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Merchandise comps below 1% in Q2 or Q3, or perishables/grocery/sundries turning negative on a non-deflationary basis.
2. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut, or consensus FY2027 falling below $4.70 (a >4% reduction from $4.90).
3. Renewal rate falling below 90%, or membership fee income growth decelerating below 5% — the latter would confirm that fee parity with Sam's Club has bitten and that the annuity supporting 62% of operating profit is no longer compounding.
4. ABL borrowings above ~$600M at any quarter end without a corresponding refinancing announcement, or the July 2027 maturity approaching within nine months unaddressed.
5. Continued buybacks executed above $100 while adjusted FCF remains negative for a second consecutive quarter.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediately following the Q2 FY2026 release (expected on or before August 21, 2026), with specific attention to merchandise versus gasoline comp decomposition, merchandise gross margin excluding tariff refunds, pre-opening expense, and any disclosure of Texas club productivity or ABL refinancing plans. A second checkpoint at the Q3 report in November 2026, which will provide the two-quarter series needed to resolve whether the core has structurally slowed.

## Conclusion

BJ's remains a well-run, well-capitalized business with a defensible regional franchise and a membership annuity that supplies roughly two-thirds of operating profit. Nothing in the available evidence points to impairment: leverage is 0.64x, the term loan carries no maintenance covenants, the Altman Z is 3.76, operating cash flow exceeds net income by a wide margin, and the last four quarters produced four modest beats.

The issue is price against a decelerating core. Total comparable sales of +6.3% are gasoline; merchandise comps of +1.5% with perishables at +0.7% are the profit-relevant number, and the company is voluntarily investing price to defend against two competitors with better scale — one of which has now matched its membership fee. Capex at 2.43x depreciation holds the free cash flow yield at 2.75% while the earnings multiple sits at the peer median. A 210bp tax rate increase absorbs much of the FY2026 operating improvement. Buybacks, executed above today's price, are currently funded by a revolver that matures in July 2027.

At $94.19 the base case is roughly $100 and the bear case roughly $79. That is fair value with a thin cushion, ahead of an earnings print the options market is pricing at ±6.9% with implied volatility nearly double realized. Existing holders have no reason to sell a franchise this financially sound at a market multiple. Prospective buyers have every reason to wait for the Q2 merchandise comp — either it confirms the deceleration and offers a better entry near $80, or it refutes it and the stock earns a higher rating on evidence rather than on hope.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Overall score 5.4, continuing a decline from 6.5 in May, driven by the merchandise-comp and margin-investment evidence rather than by price action.
