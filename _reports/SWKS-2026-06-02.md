**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Skyworks is two distinct investment problems layered on top of each other. The first is a deteriorating standalone RF semiconductor business: revenue has fallen from $5.49B (FY22) to $4.09B (FY25), a ~25% decline, with net income down ~63% over the same period, driven principally by loss of socket share at its largest customer (Apple, historically ~60-65% of revenue). The Q2 FY26 print confirmed continued deterioration: revenue $943.7M (-1.0% YoY), operating margin collapsed to 4.5% from 10.2% a year prior, and net income of $35.6M ($0.24 GAAP EPS) versus $68.7M ($0.43) a year ago. The second problem is a binary special situation: on October 27, 2025, Skyworks agreed to acquire Qorvo in a cash-and-stock deal valuing the combined entity at ~$22B, and on February 5, 2026, the FTC issued a Second Request under HSR. Close is now expected early calendar 2027.

The stock action and options market indicate this is being traded as an event, not a fundamentals story. SWKS rallied from a $51.93 52-week low in March to ~$83 in late May before settling at ~$75.49, with 25.8% short interest and a near-term implied move of ±10.2%. Call-heavy skew (-19.5%) and a put/call OI ratio of 0.08 indicate positioning is leaning bullish into the deal/regulatory outcome, an uncomfortable setup against a franchise where management itself concedes "a decrease in market share at a significant customer."

The directional outcome here turns on a regulatory decision and the durability of Apple content, neither of which is forecastable with the confidence required to underwrite a directional call. Near-term estimate revisions are genuinely positive (+11.3% current quarter, +7.1% current year over 90 days), the balance sheet is investment-grade with near-zero net leverage, and four straight earnings beats provide some support. But the upgrade in estimates is small in absolute terms (FY26 EPS to $5.01) against a structural top-line decline, and the dominant variable — whether the FTC blocks, conditions, or clears a merger of the #2 and #3 RF players — is a coin-flip we cannot handicap from public data. This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale.

## Company Overview

Skyworks develops and manufactures analog and mixed-signal semiconductors, anchored in radio-frequency front-end components — power amplifiers, switches, and filters, including bulk acoustic wave (BAW) filters — used primarily in smartphones. It is vertically integrated, owning fabs and assembly/test operations (net PP&E ~$1.19B, ~10,000 employees, roughly 76% offshore in Mexico and Singapore). The company is attempting to diversify into a "broad markets" portfolio spanning Wi-Fi 7/8, automotive, IoT/connected home, infrastructure, industrial, medical, and defense. Emerging/diversified revenue reached $740M in FY25 (+24% YoY), but this remains a minority of the total.

The defining structural feature is concentration: ~60-65% of revenue historically derives from Apple, and ~73% of revenue is recognized in the United States. This single-customer dependence is both the source of the company's profitability over the past decade and the source of its current decline, as Apple dual-sources and insources RF content.

The pending Qorvo acquisition is the most important fact for the next 12-18 months. Skyworks is the acquirer; Qorvo holders receive 0.960 SWKS shares plus $32.50 cash per share, leaving Skyworks holders ~63% and Qorvo holders ~37% of the combined company. This consolidates the #2 and #3 RF front-end players into one platform.

## Financial Analysis

**The multi-year decline is the core fact.** Revenue: FY22 $5.49B → FY23 $4.77B → FY24 $4.18B → FY25 $4.09B. Net income: FY22 $1.28B → FY23 $983M → FY24 $596M → FY25 $477M. Operating income fell from $1.53B (FY22) to $500M (FY25), with operating margin compressing from ~28% to ~12%. GAAP gross margin held relatively firm around 41% (FY25 gross profit $1.68B, 41.2%), so the margin damage is operating-leverage-driven: a shrinking top line spread across rising R&D ($785M in FY25, ~19% of revenue) as the company funds diversification.

**Q2 FY26 shows the trend intact, with merger costs layered on.** H1 FY26 revenue $1,979M (-2.1%). The drivers were explicit: socket loss at the significant customer, Qorvo transaction costs ($19.7M in SG&A this quarter, $53.4M H1), and rising R&D (+14% YoY), partly offset by Wi-Fi strength. H1 operating cash flow fell to $445.8M from $786.6M, partly a working-capital drag as inventory built to $885.6M from $754.7M; inventory accumulation into a declining-revenue environment can presage future margin pressure.

**The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story.** Cash and marketable securities of $1.44B against total debt of $1.0B ($500M 1.80% notes due 2026, $500M 3.00% notes due 2031) leaves net leverage near zero. The $750M revolver is undrawn (3.0x leverage covenant). On a standalone basis, this is investment-grade and resilient. Post-merger, the company assumes Qorvo debt plus ~$1.5B of Goldman bridge financing (reduced from $3.05B after Qorvo's notes avoided a downgrade), which will materially raise leverage. Skyworks is exchanging into Qorvo's 2029/2031 notes rather than refinancing, a cost-efficient approach that signals confidence in combined-company credit.

**Capital allocation has shifted into deal mode.** The dividend was raised to $0.71/quarter (~$213M H1 FY26), yielding ~3.6%, and is well-covered by FY25 FCF of ~$1.1B (FCF yield ~9% on the current market cap). Buybacks, which consumed $500M in H1 FY25, have essentially stopped during the deal lock-up (~$1.2B remains on the $2.0B authorization). Share count fell from ~160M to ~150M via prior repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is negative. Revenue has declined three consecutive years and H1 FY26 continued the trend. The growth narrative rests entirely on two contingent items: (1) the diversification portfolio scaling enough to offset Apple erosion, and (2) the Qorvo acquisition delivering revenue scale, cost synergies, and pricing discipline.

The diversification story is real but small. Emerging revenue of $740M growing 24% is encouraging, but at ~18% of total revenue it cannot offset a declining ~60%+ Apple base. The math: if Apple revenue declines 10% off a ~$2.5B base (-$250M) while emerging grows 24% off $740M (+$178M), the company still shrinks. That is essentially what FY26 H1 shows.

Estimate revisions are positive and should not be dismissed: current-quarter EPS revised +11.3% over 90 days, current-year +7.1%, next-year +3.1%. Four consecutive beats (6.6%, 23.6%, 8.0%, 9.1%) give some credibility to forward consensus of $5.01 FY26 and $5.15 FY27. But the strong estimate-revision BUY signal that applies to inflection stocks requires revenue growth above ~30% alongside the revisions; here revenue is declining, so the revision velocity reflects cost discipline and Wi-Fi strength against a falling top line, not a demand inflection. The signal supports holding, not a directional buy.

## Valuation Assessment

At $75.49, SWKS trades at ~31x trailing earnings and ~15x consensus FY26 EPS. The trailing multiple is misleading because FY25 earnings were trough-like; the forward 15x is the more relevant number. Against semiconductor peers (QRVO 28.6x, CRUS 21.6x, peer median 28.6x), SWKS on forward earnings looks inexpensive, and the ~9% FCF yield plus 3.6% dividend yield provide genuine downside support. This is why a deep-value manager (Pzena, 8.2% stake) holds it.

A cheap multiple on a declining franchise is the classic value-trap signature unless there is a catalyst to stabilize the decline. Here there is a catalyst — the Qorvo deal — but it is binary. If the FTC clears the deal, the combined entity's scale, synergies, and reduced price competition could justify a re-rating toward $90-100+. If the FTC blocks it, the market is left with a standalone, share-losing franchise plus a $298.7M reverse termination fee, and the stock likely retraces toward its $51.93-55 trough range, where it traded as recently as March 2026.

Analyst targets bracket this uncertainty: low $55, median $72, mean $74.16, high $106. The current price sits right at the mean, consistent with the market pricing roughly even odds on the deal. We do not treat the consensus target as a floor here, because this is a contested special situation where targets span a -27% to +40% range precisely because the outcome is binary.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects that the cheap forward multiple is offset by genuine structural impairment and a binary outcome that the market is, correctly, pricing as a coin-flip. There is no asymmetric mispricing to identify; the dispersion in fair value is the honest answer.

## Competitive Landscape

Skyworks competes in RF front-end against Qorvo (its merger target), Broadcom (the high-end BAW leader), Murata, and Qualcomm. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating: management's own language — "a decrease in market share at a significant customer" — concedes socket loss at Apple, the dominant revenue source. ASPs "will continue to decline over time" per the filings, and the smartphone TAM is mature.

The strategic logic of the Qorvo acquisition is defensive consolidation: both companies face the same pressures (Apple insourcing/dual-sourcing, mature smartphone demand, ASP erosion), and combining removes a direct price competitor while pooling BAW/filter IP and scale. The bull interpretation is that this creates a larger, more diversified analog/RF platform with pricing discipline. The bear interpretation is that combining two share-losing, Apple/China-exposed franchises does not fix the demand problem, and that the FTC may force divestitures or block the deal precisely because they are the two closest competitors. Qorvo's own gross-margin recovery (near its FY28 47% target) supports deal logic but raises the question of whether Skyworks is overpaying.

Competitive position scores 4.0: trajectory is clearly negative on a standalone basis, and the fix is contingent on a regulatory decision outside management's control.

## Risk Assessment

**Apple concentration / socket loss.** With ~60%+ of revenue from one customer, content and share loss directly drive the top line down. This is the dominant operational risk and it is actively materializing, not hypothetical.

**Merger execution and antitrust.** The FTC Second Request is a serious escalation that pushed close to early CY2027. A break costs a $298.7M reverse termination fee (or $100M in certain antitrust-failure scenarios). This is the dominant near-term catalyst risk and is genuinely two-sided.

**Integration and leverage.** Combining two vertically integrated chipmakers is operationally complex; the deal adds bridge debt plus assumed Qorvo notes, materially raising leverage from a near-zero starting point.

**Governance instability.** FY25 saw a CEO change (Griffin out with a $13.6M severance; Brace in on a large front-loaded inducement package) and three CFOs in one year, culminating in permanent CFO Philip Carter (ex-AMD) in September 2025. New leadership is credible and heavily equity-incentivized (Brace's PSA hurdles run from $82 to $164), but the turnover is a real red flag. The 0% payout on FY23 TSR PSAs (-20% three-year TSR, 15th percentile) is honest evidence that the comp plan is not masking underperformance.

**Geopolitical/tax.** Heavy offshore manufacturing (Singapore, Mexico, China exposure), tariffs, export controls, GILTI/Pillar Two, OBBBA. The Denso BAW-related patent litigation was settled in H1 FY26 for an immaterial amount.

Risk factors score 4.0: the binary antitrust event and active customer-concentration erosion are specific, material, and not fully priced in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a contested, event-driven setup. Near-term ATM IV of 61.9% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 40.8% (a rich 21.1pp spread), reflecting elevated uncertainty around the regulatory timeline and the next earnings print. The structure is call-heavy: put/call skew of -19.5%, put/call volume ratio of 0.19, and put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.08 (from 0.68 a month ago) as price rose 17.3% over the same window. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.2%. Term structure is in mild contango (61.9% near vs 64.4% at six months), a relatively complacent shape.

Positioning has tilted bullish into the deal/regulatory window even as the standalone fundamentals deteriorate. Combined with 25.8% short interest and 5.5 days to cover, this creates a two-sided reflexive setup: a deal-positive headline could squeeze the heavy short base, while an antitrust setback would punish the call-heavy crowd. Neither tail is more probable from the public data.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$95-106).** The FTC clears the deal (possibly with minor remedies). The combined SWKS/QRVO platform realizes meaningful cost synergies, gains pricing discipline by removing a direct competitor, and accelerates diversification into automotive, infrastructure, and defense. Apple socket loss stabilizes as the combined company offers a broader, harder-to-displace content set. Forward earnings power on the combined entity supports a re-rating toward peer multiples on a larger, more diversified base. The 25.8% short base squeezes on deal-completion clarity. Target ~$95-106 (analyst high).

**Bear Case (~$52-55).** The FTC blocks the deal or demands divestitures so onerous that the parties walk, triggering the $298.7M reverse termination fee. The market is left with a standalone franchise still losing Apple share, declining revenue, compressed operating margins, and a buildup of inventory. The stock retraces toward its March 2026 trough of $51.93-55, where the value floor (FCF yield, dividend) ultimately sits. Downside ~-27% to -31% from current.

**Base Case (~$72-78).** The outcome remains unresolved through most of 2026, the stock oscillates with regulatory headlines and Apple data points, and the standalone business muddles along with positive cost-driven estimate revisions partly offsetting top-line decline. The current price near the analyst mean ($74.16) reflects roughly even-odds pricing of the deal.

The reason this is a HOLD and not a directional call: the spread between bull and bear is roughly +40% to -30%, centered on a binary regulatory event we cannot handicap. There is a plausible 12-month appreciation path (deal clearance + synergies), but it is contingent on a coin-flip rather than on improving standalone fundamentals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are the FQ3 FY26 earnings release (early August 2026) for the standalone trajectory and any FTC procedural update on the Second Request. The dominant catalyst — deal close — is not expected until early CY2027, which is a long carry for a binary outcome.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FTC clears the Qorvo deal (or signals clearance with only minor remedies), removing the binary risk and de-risking the synergy thesis.
- Standalone revenue stabilizes or returns to YoY growth for two consecutive quarters, indicating Apple socket loss has bottomed and diversification is offsetting.
- Operating margin recovers back toward double digits, demonstrating the Q2 FY26 4.5% level was a transaction-cost-driven trough rather than a new run-rate.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FTC formally moves to block the deal or demands divestitures that make completion uneconomic, leaving a standalone share-losing franchise plus the termination fee.
- Apple content loss accelerates (further explicit socket-loss disclosures, or a step-down in revenue guidance below the ~$3.9-4.0B run rate).
- Inventory continues building while revenue declines, signaling channel/demand weakness that will hit gross margin.
- Estimate revisions turn negative, reversing the current positive momentum.

## Conclusion

Skyworks is a special situation trading as a merger-arbitrage and regulatory-outcome bet, with a deteriorating standalone RF franchise underneath. The fundamentals are genuinely weak — three years of revenue decline, operating margin compressed to 4.5% in the latest quarter, and explicit Apple socket loss — but the balance sheet is sound, the forward multiple is inexpensive (~15x FY26 EPS, ~9% FCF yield, 3.6% dividend), and estimate revisions are positive. The catalyst that could re-rate the stock (Qorvo deal clearance and synergies) is real but binary, gated by an FTC Second Request, and not expected to resolve until early 2027.

The recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale. There is a credible 12-month appreciation path, but it runs through a regulatory decision we cannot forecast, not through improving standalone demand. Existing holders with a value orientation and patience for the deal timeline have downside support near the $52-55 trough and meaningful upside on clearance. New capital should wait for either deal-clearance clarity or evidence that standalone revenue and margins have bottomed before committing; the current price near the analyst mean offers no asymmetric edge. The prior HOLD trajectory (5.5 → 5.0) and the third consecutive HOLD are sustained here; the Financial Health improvement is real (clean balance sheet, deal-financing discipline), but it is offset by continued competitive-position deterioration and the unresolved binary at the center of the story.
