**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TXNM Energy trades as a merger-arbitrage security rather than a conventional regulated utility. Blackstone Infrastructure agreed in May 2025 to acquire the company for $61.25 per share in all cash, an approximately $11.5B enterprise-value transaction with no financing condition. At $58.40, the stock offers a gross spread of roughly $2.85, or 4.9%, to the deal price. Almost every element of the investment case is downstream of one variable: the probability and timing of the deal closing. Standalone fundamentals (strong Texas transmission-and-distribution growth, data-center-driven load, investment-grade credit) matter, but they are secondary to the deal outcome.

The transaction has cleared four of six required approvals: HSR antitrust, the FCC, FERC (which in February 2026 explicitly rejected data-center, private-equity, and cross-subsidization objections), and the Texas PUCT. The two remaining gates are the NRC and, decisively, the New Mexico Public Regulation Commission. In July 2026 the NMPRC voted 2-1 to order the unwinding of Blackstone's $400M pre-merger stock purchase, levied a $300,000 fine, and paused the merger procedural schedule. TXNM and Blackstone responded by extending the outside date to May 31, 2027, and TXNM took a $400M parent-level term loan (explicitly ring-fenced from PNM) to fund the unwind. The expected close slipped from 2H 2026 to 1H 2027. The market still assigns high completion odds, with the stock trading within 5% of the deal price, but the July event materially raised break risk relative to the "four-of-six done" framing that prevailed earlier.

The setup is asymmetric and binary. If NMPRC and NRC approve, the return is capped near $61.25 plus a small dividend stream to close, a mid-single-digit gross return over roughly ten months to backstop. If the deal breaks, the stock likely de-rates toward standalone value, plausibly the high-$40s to low-$50s, a double-digit percentage drawdown. New Mexico has a documented history of blocking utility M&A (it killed the Avangrid/Iberdrola acquisition of PNM in 2020-2021), which is why the spread has never fully closed. We rate the stock HOLD with an uncertain rationale, appropriate for a live event position where the dominant driver is a regulatory vote that is genuinely uncertain.

## Company Overview

TXNM Energy, renamed from PNM Resources in 2024, is a regulated electric utility holding company serving approximately 842,000 customers across New Mexico and Texas through two subsidiaries.

PNM (Public Service Company of New Mexico) is a vertically integrated, rate-regulated electric utility, the largest in New Mexico with roughly 557,000 customers, regulated by the NMPRC and FERC. It owns a 7.3% interest in Palo Verde Nuclear, gas plants, a 3,244 MW renewables and storage fleet, and a 13% interest in Four Corners coal (targeted for exit by 2031). PNM operates under New Mexico's Energy Transition Act, which mandates 100% zero-carbon generation by 2045; PNM's own goals are carbon-free by 2040 and coal-free by 2031.

TNMP (Texas-New Mexico Power) is a pure transmission-and-distribution "wires" utility in Texas, serving roughly 282,000 consumers through retail electric providers and regulated by the PUCT within ERCOT. TNMP is the higher-growth engine, benefiting from Texas grid-investment legislation and surging demand. The strategic story underneath the merger is a jurisdictional mix shift toward Texas and FERC (now more than half of consolidated rate base), which offers more constructive and timely cost recovery than the contentious New Mexico regime.

As a regulated monopoly, TXNM faces no product competition. The binding constraint is regulatory recovery, and in New Mexico that constraint has repeatedly proven adverse.

The headline EPS collapse from $2.67 to $1.48 is not fundamental deterioration. It was driven chiefly by a $58.8M pension settlement charge at PNM, plus higher interest expense, milder weather, a $41.4M drag from capacity-arrangement timing, and heavy 2025 equity issuance (average diluted shares rose from 90.6M to 102.4M). Management's ongoing earnings basis, reported as Incentive EPS near $2.77, grew year over year and is the truer read on earnings power. A screener keying on the reported EPS decline would misread the business.

Segment divergence tells the operational story. TNMP (Texas) earnings rose $20.8M on strong load growth and rate relief; PNM (New Mexico) earnings fell $104.6M, dominated by the pension charge, higher interest, and weather. Texas is the growth; New Mexico is the regulatory drag.

Demand tailwinds are concrete. TNMP data-center load rose 70.5% in 2025, and PNM industrial load rose 14.3%, including a Meta data center served via a renewable rider. This underpins the $11.1B 2026-2030 capital plan.

On the balance sheet, D/E is roughly 2.5x with $5.1B of long-term debt and a current ratio of 0.55. The Altman Z-Score of 0.89 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is a false signal: high leverage and low asset turnover are structural to a regulated utility, not a bankruptcy warning. All senior debt remains investment grade (S&P BBB/BBB/BBB+; Moody's Baa3/Baa2/Baa1, stable), and both agencies view the Blackstone merger as credit-supportive to neutral. Negative free cash flow of –$611M is normal and expected for a utility in a heavy-capex phase, funded by continuous debt and equity issuance. In 2025 the company raised capital prolifically, including $350M of 7.0% junior subordinated notes and two private placements. The $657.9M of debt maturing through February 2027 is a refinancing consideration but standard for the model.

For scoring, we treat financial health as adequate rather than strong: the leverage and negative FCF are structural, the credit is IG, and the earnings-power narrative is intact once one-time items are stripped, but the heavy capital-markets dependence and rising-rate refinancing exposure cap the score in the mid-range.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, TXNM has a credible growth story anchored on the Texas T&D franchise and data-center load. The $11.1B capital plan supports the sector-wide narrative that regulated utilities with genuine load growth can compound rate base at a healthy clip. New Mexico's clean-energy mandates add further capex runway.

The growth thesis is only faintly relevant to the current investment case. In a cash takeout at a fixed $61.25, shareholders do not participate in future compounding; they receive the deal price. The estimate-revision data reinforces this: current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates have risen 13-16% over 30 days, but current-year and next-year estimates are roughly flat to slightly negative (current year –2.1% over 30 days, next year –1.3%). Only two analysts contribute to the near-term revisions, and none of this constitutes an accelerating-inflection signal that would drive a standalone re-rating.

Growth prospects therefore score below neutral. The underlying franchise is fine, but in a merger-arb context the growth optionality accrues to Blackstone, not to public shareholders, and the earnings-surprise record (four consecutive misses over the trailing four quarters, ranging from –11.7% to –40.5%) undercuts confidence in near-term delivery.

## Competitive Landscape

TXNM operates regulated monopolies, so there is no product competition. Its position sits at the intersection of two utility-sector themes: data-center-driven load growth and private-capital pursuit of regulated utilities for their stable, inflation-protected cash flows. Blackstone's rationale is straightforward: TXNM needs large amounts of equity to fund its $11B+ plan and New Mexico's clean-energy mandates, and a private infrastructure owner can inject that capital without public-market dilution.

The competitive differentiator that matters is regulatory jurisdiction, and it cuts both ways. The shift toward Texas and FERC rate base is genuinely constructive, since those regimes recover costs more promptly than the NMPRC. But the New Mexico regulatory environment is among the least merger-friendly in the country, which is why TXNM's arb spread is wider than a typical utility LBO. The company's competitive position as an operating business is stable-to-improving on the Texas load-growth story; its position as a deal target is impaired by the July 2026 NMPRC setback. We score competitive position modestly above neutral, weighting the durable Texas franchise while acknowledging the New Mexico overhang.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is merger completion, and it is deal-specific and freshly elevated.

**Merger break risk.** The 2025 10-K added a full "Risks Relating to the Proposed Merger" section: a $210M termination fee, interim operating restrictions (no-shop, dividends capped at $0.4275/quarter for 2026, Blackstone consent for major actions), and shareholder litigation the company calls meritless. If the deal breaks, the stock likely de-rates to standalone value, plausibly the high-$40s to low-$50s, since the current price embeds high deal probability.

**New Mexico regulatory hostility — the crux.** In June 2026 the NMPRC's independent hearing examiners recommended reversing the $400M Blackstone stock sale; the July 2 commission order followed that recommendation. On July 10 the NMPRC set a public hearing on the acquisition, so the process is resuming. The interpretation matters: the setback centers on process (TXNM sold 8M shares to Blackstone at $50 in June 2025 before filing the merger with New Mexico regulators, and the PRC treated that as an unlawful, unapproved change-of-control step) rather than an outright rejection of the merger. TXNM's response, a parent-level term loan to fund the unwind and re-issue stock, signals active management of the PRC relationship. Both parties reaffirmed commitment. New Mexico approval remains the binary gate, and timing has slipped six to nine months.

**Standalone regulatory recovery.** $10.2B of 2026-2030 construction capex requires timely rate recovery. A TNMP base rate review (filed November 2025: $2.8B rate base, 10.4% requested ROE) is pending, and New Mexico rate lag is a persistent headwind if the deal falls through.

**Wildfire, nuclear, and refinancing.** PNM operates in arid New Mexico; TNMP absorbed Hurricane Beryl restoration costs on the Texas Gulf Coast. Palo Verde nuclear, the Four Corners exit by 2031, and San Juan mine reclamation all carry capped ratepayer recovery, meaning overruns hit earnings.

We score risk factors at 4.0. The July NMPRC vote is a specific, recently surfaced catalyst for material downside that is not fully priced (the spread implies the market still expects the deal to close), and the outcome is genuinely binary with asymmetric loss potential.

The options market corroborates the elevated event risk. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($61.25, +4.9%).** The NMPRC setback is procedural, not substantive. Blackstone's continued commitment, the deadline extension, and the ring-fenced unwind loan demonstrate both parties intend to close. FERC has already dismissed the private-equity and cross-subsidization objections that typically animate such reviews. New Mexico ultimately approves with conditions (rate credits, hold-harmless commitments), the NRC clears routinely, and the stock converges to $61.25 by 1H 2027, delivering the spread plus capped dividends to close.

**Bear case (~$48-52, –11% to –18%).** The NMPRC, true to its precedent of killing the Avangrid deal and facing editorial and examiner pressure to "end the merger," rejects the acquisition or imposes conditions Blackstone will not accept. The stock de-rates to standalone value. Standalone, the company carries a real growth franchise but also New Mexico rate lag, negative FCF, heavy refinancing needs, and four straight quarterly earnings misses: a fine but unexciting utility that would trade at a peer-like ~19-20x forward on ~$2.94 EPS, roughly $56-58, with downside toward the low-$50s if sentiment sours on the failed deal and the standalone capital needs.

The asymmetry is unfavorable in magnitude: limited upside to the deal price versus a larger drawdown if the deal breaks. What keeps this a HOLD rather than an AVOID is that the base-case probability of completion, given four approvals secured and both parties reaffirming, still appears more likely than not, but not by enough, and not with enough certainty, to warrant a BUY on a capped-return arb with a hostile regulator in the path.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a hold-to-catalyst, event-driven position with a binary outcome. It should be sized as an event bet, not a core utility holding.

**Reassessment timeline.** The critical near-term events are the resumed NMPRC public hearing on the acquisition (process reopened July 10, 2026) and any procedural schedule the commission sets thereafter, followed by the NRC decision. The outside date is May 31, 2027, with a targeted 1H 2027 close.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NMPRC issues a constructive procedural order or a settlement framework on the merger itself (not merely the stock-sale unwind), materially raising completion odds and widening the risk-adjusted spread.
- NRC approval is granted, removing one of the two remaining gates.
- The spread widens materially (stock toward mid-$50s) on headline noise without a substantive change in deal probability, improving the arb risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The NMPRC rejects the acquisition, or imposes conditions Blackstone publicly declines to accept.
- Blackstone signals reduced commitment, or the parties fail to further extend past the May 31, 2027 outside date.
- Termination of the merger agreement, which would collapse the arb premium and require re-underwriting the stock on standalone fundamentals.

## Conclusion

TXNM is a merger-arbitrage position whose value is determined almost entirely by the Blackstone deal. At $58.40, the 4.9% gross spread to the $61.25 cash offer compensates for real, recently elevated New Mexico regulatory risk. Four of six approvals are secured, but the July 2026 NMPRC order, the accompanying fine, the paused schedule, and the six-to-nine-month slip to a 1H 2027 close reintroduced genuine break risk into what had looked like a de-risking arb. The company's standalone fundamentals (Texas T&D load growth, data-center demand, investment-grade credit) are solid but secondary, and its reported earnings weakness is a one-time pension-charge artifact rather than deterioration.

The recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the upside is capped near the deal price, the downside on a break is a double-digit drawdown, and the deciding variable is a regulatory vote that a hostile commission has already precedent for blocking. For investors already holding, this is a hold-to-catalyst with the exit triggers above; for new capital, the risk/reward is not compelling enough to initiate. Position sizing should reflect the binary nature of the outcome.
