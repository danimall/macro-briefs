## Executive Summary

Ameris Bancorp is a $27.5 billion-asset regional bank headquartered in Atlanta with 163 branches across Georgia, Alabama, Florida, North Carolina, and South Carolina. The company delivered $6.00 in diluted EPS for 2025, up 54% from $3.89 in 2023, driven by NIM expansion from 3.56% to 3.79% and declining provision expense. Net interest income grew sequentially in every quarter of 2025 ($221.8M → $245.3M), and analyst estimates for 2026 continue to rise, with current-year consensus at $6.52 (+8.7% YoY) and next-year at $6.98 (+7.1%).

At $84.44, the stock trades at 14.1x trailing EPS and 12.9x forward FY2026 estimates, a premium to the regional bank peer median P/E of ~12x. That premium is earned: ROA of 1.54%, ROE of 10.52%, net charge-offs at 18 bps, CET1 of 13.17%, and 14.5% tangible book value growth in 2025 are all above-peer metrics. The primary risks are concentrated CRE exposure ($9.3B, including $1.26B in office), 47.7% uninsured deposit ratio, and mortgage banking revenue sensitivity. Analyst targets range from $85 to $97.50, and positive estimate revision trends support ownership through the upcoming Q1 2026 earnings cycle and into mid-2026.

## Company Overview

Ameris Bancorp operates through three business pillars: community banking (traditional C&I and CRE lending through a decentralized, relationship-driven model), mortgage banking ($4.5B in retail originations in 2025, generating fee income from production and servicing), and specialty lending (insurance premium finance at $1.3B, warehouse lending at $1.15B, equipment finance, and SBA government-guaranteed lending). The company transferred its listing from Nasdaq to NYSE in July 2024, reflecting management's intent to attract broader institutional ownership.

The geographic footprint is concentrated in high-growth southeastern U.S. markets benefiting from sustained population in-migration. Total loans were $22.1B, deposits $22.4B, and shareholders' equity $4.1B at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### Net Interest Income

Net interest income (tax-equivalent) expanded 10.3% to $940.7M in 2025, and the quarterly trajectory was monotonically positive: $221.8M (Q1), $231.8M (Q2), $238.0M (Q3), $245.3M (Q4). This was driven by a 23-basis-point NIM expansion from 3.56% to 3.79%, achieved through asymmetric repricing: the cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 51 bps (3.29% → 2.78%) while earning asset yields declined only 14 bps (5.77% → 5.63%). Interest expense fell $71.5M while interest income rose $16.2M.

The liability repricing tailwind has further room to run. With 30.5% of deposits being noninterest-bearing demand deposits (free funding) and a significant portion of the loan book carrying fixed rates that will reprice at higher coupons over the next 1-3 years, NIM should remain supportive even if the rate environment stabilizes.

### Profitability and Returns

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Net Income | $269.1M | $358.7M | $412.2M |
| Diluted EPS | $3.89 | $5.19 | $6.00 |
| ROA | 1.06% | 1.38% | 1.54% |
| ROE | 8.12% | 10.01% | 10.52% |

Net income grew 53.2% over two years. Return on assets at 1.54% places Ameris in the upper quartile of regional bank peers. Return on equity at 10.52% is moderated by the bank's strong capital levels (CET1 at 13.17%).

### Credit Quality

Net charge-offs were 18 bps in 2025, down from 19 bps in 2024 and 25 bps in 2023. Provision expense dropped to $47.4M from $69.8M in 2024 and $153.5M in 2023 — a 69% reduction over two years. The allowance for credit losses stands at 1.62% of loans, with 319.2% coverage of nonaccrual loans. Nonperforming assets as a percentage of total assets are 0.44%, or 0.35% excluding government-guaranteed GNMA loans.

These are benign credit metrics. However, under Moody's S-4 stress scenario (96th percentile), the quantitative ACL would require an additional ~$82.4M, suggesting non-trivial tail risk embedded in the portfolio.

### Capital Position

CET1 at 13.17% and total capital at 15.01% are well above regulatory minimums (7.0% and 10.5% respectively), providing ~600 bps of excess CET1. Tangible book value per share grew 14.5% to $44.18. The company returned $132M to shareholders in 2025 through $77.1M in buybacks (up from $8.0M in 2024) and $54.9M in dividends.

### Noninterest Income

Noninterest income declined 7.6% to $271.0M in 2025. Mortgage banking revenue fell 8.4% to $147.0M due to compressed gain-on-sale margins (2.20% vs. 2.37%). This was partially offset by a 41.1% increase in equipment finance activity. Mortgage banking contributes roughly 12% of total revenue and is the primary source of fee income volatility.

## Growth Analysis

Current-year (FY2026) consensus EPS stands at $6.52, up 4.2% over the past 90 days. Next-year consensus is $6.98, up 4.4% over the same window. The 30-day revision trend is flat to slightly positive (+0.4% for FY2026), indicating estimates have largely stabilized at higher levels rather than continuing to accelerate — a sign the market has largely absorbed the NIM expansion story.

Key growth vectors:
1. **Continued NIM expansion** from lagged liability repricing, particularly in time deposits that were locked at higher rates in 2023-2024 and will mature and reprice lower.
2. **Loan growth**: The diversified specialty lending platform (premium finance, warehouse, equipment finance) provides above-peer growth opportunities in national markets.
3. **Operating leverage**: FTE headcount declined while revenue grew, suggesting efficiency improvements can continue.
4. **Potential mortgage recovery**: If rates stabilize or decline, mortgage banking volumes and margins should improve, though this is not the base case given the current rate environment.

The counterweight is the current macro backdrop. Consumer spending is weakening, CPI is accelerating toward 4%+, and the Fed is on extended hold at 3.64%. For a southeastern regional bank with significant CRE exposure, a stagflationary environment where rates stay elevated and economic growth decelerates would compress loan demand and potentially elevate credit costs.

## Valuation Assessment

At $84.44, ABCB trades at:
- 14.1x trailing EPS ($6.00)
- 12.9x FY2026E EPS ($6.52)
- 12.1x FY2027E EPS ($6.98)
- 1.91x tangible book value ($44.18)
- 1.41x book value ($59.92)

The peer median P/E is approximately 12.0x (Hancock Whitney at 11.8x, Axos at 12.0x, Bank OZK at 7.7x, Atlantic Union at 18.6x). ABCB's premium to the peer median is approximately 17% on trailing P/E and ~8% on forward estimates. This premium is justified by: (a) above-peer ROA, (b) below-peer NCOs on an improving trend, (c) diversified fee income streams that most community bank peers lack, and (d) TBV growth well above the peer range of 5-10%.

The dividend yield at 0.95% is well below the peer median of 3.0%, reflecting the company's preference for retaining capital for growth and buybacks over payout. This is appropriate given the bank's growth trajectory but does limit income-oriented investor appeal.

Analyst consensus targets range from $85 (floor) to $97.50 (ceiling), with a mean of $89.93 and median of $88.00. The floor target is essentially at the current price, suggesting limited near-term downside in the analyst community's view.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameris occupies a differentiated position in the southeastern regional bank space. At $27.5B in assets, it is large enough to invest in technology and offer a full product suite (including national specialty lending platforms) while maintaining the decentralized, relationship-driven model that community banks rely on for customer retention. The insurance premium finance business ($1.3B, with just 13 bps in NCOs) is a particularly underappreciated niche — short-duration, self-liquidating, and uncorrelated with real estate cycles.

Deposit competition comes from traditional banks, credit unions, internet banks, and increasingly from money market funds and stablecoins. The 10-K explicitly names stablecoin competition as an emerging deposit risk, which is unusual for a regional bank to highlight.

CRE concentration at 262% of capital and C&D at 43% of capital are both below the 300%/100% regulatory thresholds, giving Ameris room to continue CRE lending without triggering enhanced supervisory attention. Several peers are at or above these limits, which constrains their growth.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Concentration
CRE totals $9.3B (43% of total loans). Within that, non-owner-occupied CRE includes $1.26B in office and $2.25B in retail. The office portfolio is the highest-risk segment, with $509M concentrated in the Atlanta market. Management has increased the qualitative factor in the ACL model for office CRE. Past-due CRE loans are just 8 bps, but office CRE fundamentals are deteriorating nationally and delayed loss recognition is a realistic scenario.

### Uninsured Deposits
At 47.7% ($10.67B), uninsured deposits are elevated, though $3.76B are collateralized municipal deposits. The remaining ~$6.9B in truly unsecured, uninsured deposits represents a flight risk in any future bank stress event, as the SVB/Signature episode in 2023 demonstrated.

### Mortgage Banking Volatility
At 12% of revenue, mortgage banking creates meaningful earnings volatility. In a rising-rate environment, production volumes and margins compress. The 8.4% decline in 2025 despite rate cuts demonstrates that structural challenges in the mortgage market can overwhelm cyclical tailwinds.

### Geographic Concentration
Five southeastern states account for virtually all operations. Hurricane risk is annual and uninsurable at the portfolio level. A regional economic downturn would have no offsetting geographic diversification.

### Goodwill at $1.016B
Goodwill represents 24.9% of shareholders' equity. A sustained stock price decline or deteriorating business conditions could trigger impairment testing and potential writedowns that would meaningfully impact book equity.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV is 34.4% vs. 25.2% historical volatility — a 9.2 percentage point premium indicating the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (34.4% near-term vs. 26.7% at 6 months), consistent with catalyst-driven stress pricing around the April 23 Q1 earnings release. Put/call skew at 4.2% reflects moderately elevated put demand. The implied move of ±6.9% on the nearest expiry suggests the market anticipates a meaningful reaction to earnings. The options pricing does not contradict the fundamental thesis but warrants awareness that the earnings event carries above-normal uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95-98)
NIM continues expanding through 2026 as time deposits reprice lower. Loan growth accelerates in specialty lending channels. Credit quality remains benign. The company sustains 10%+ EPS growth, and the stock re-rates to 15x forward earnings ($6.52 × 15 = $97.80). Mortgage banking stabilizes or improves. Buyback activity continues at the elevated 2025 pace, providing EPS accretion.

### Bear Case ($65-70)
CRE credit deterioration materializes, particularly in office. Provision expense reverts toward the $70-100M range. Rising CPI compresses consumer activity in southeastern markets, slowing loan demand. Mortgage banking revenue declines further. Stock de-rates to 10-11x forward earnings ($6.52 × 10 = $65.20), closer to the lower end of the peer range (Bank OZK at 7.7x).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $95.** 14.5x FY2026E EPS of $6.52. This represents a modest multiple expansion from the current 12.9x forward, justified by continued NIM expansion and rising estimates. Upside from current: 12.5%.

**Bull case target: $98.** 15x FY2026E EPS. Upside: 16.1%.

**Bear case target: $67.** 10.3x FY2026E EPS. Downside: 20.7%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (April 23, 2026), Q2 2026 earnings (July), and Fed rate decision trajectory through H2 2026.

**Upside/downside ratio:** 12.5% upside to base case vs. 20.7% downside to bear case = 0.6:1. The probability-weighted expected return is favorable given the strong estimate revision trend and clean credit metrics, but this is not an asymmetric setup.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 35 bps for any single quarter, signaling CRE credit deterioration is accelerating beyond reserves.
2. NIM contracts below 3.60% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the liability repricing tailwind has reversed.
3. Nonperforming assets exceed 1.0% of total assets, suggesting broad portfolio stress.

## Conclusion

Ameris Bancorp is a well-managed southeastern regional bank with above-peer profitability, clean credit metrics, fortress capital levels, and diversified revenue streams that distinguish it from typical community banks. The NIM expansion cycle still has momentum, and estimate revisions are trending positively. At 12.9x forward earnings with an improving fundamental trajectory, the valuation premium to peers is earned and modest. The primary risks — CRE concentration (especially office), uninsured deposits, and mortgage banking volatility — are real but currently well-managed. The stock is a BUY at current levels with a base case target of $95 over 12 months, though position sizing should reflect the macro backdrop of elevated energy costs, rising CPI, and a Fed on extended hold.
