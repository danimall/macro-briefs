**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pinnacle Financial Partners is a Nashville-based regional bank that completed a merger of equals with Columbus, Georgia-based Synovus Financial on January 1, 2026. The combined entity (informally "New Pinnacle") carries roughly $123 billion in assets, ~$14.6 billion of equity, and ~151 million shares outstanding, against the legacy Pinnacle standalone footprint of $57.7 billion in assets and ~78 million shares. The merger roughly doubled the company's size and gave it a dominant Southeast footprint across TN, GA, NC, SC, AL, FL, VA, KY and DC, with 400-plus branches.

On forward consensus, PNFP looks cheap relative to its peer group. Current-year EPS consensus of $10.32 and next-year $11.85 put the stock at ~9.3x and ~8.1x at $96.36, versus a regional-bank peer median of ~12.2x. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+1.4% over 90 days on the current quarter, +0.8% on the current year), the company beat in three of the last four quarters, and Q1 2026 — the first combined quarter — delivered roughly $150 million of net income with management citing the "highest pace of hiring in our history" (net +50 revenue-generating bankers). For a model built explicitly on recruiting producers from disrupted competitors, the hiring read-through is the most encouraging early integration signal.

We rate PNFP a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias rather than a BUY. The valuation discount is real but largely warranted by a cluster of concrete, near-term, partly binary risks: the Synovus integration is only one quarter old and synergy realization is unproven (the company's own new risk factor warns savings "may not be realized at all"); BHG — a 49%-owned, off-balance-sheet lending platform contributing ~18% of pre-tax income — is showing credit deterioration (allowance +57% to $376M, substitution liability up to 8.6% of sold loans); and the options market is in backwardation, pricing imminent stress while analyst sentiment is degrading. These are the specific items preventing an upgrade. They are falsifiable, and the next two earnings prints will resolve most of them.

## Company Overview

Pinnacle operates through Pinnacle Bank, a relationship-banking franchise targeting small-to-medium businesses and their owners across the southeastern U.S. The differentiating strategy is talent acquisition: management states explicitly that most loan growth comes "not of strong loan demand but rather of our ability to attract experienced financial services professionals." Producers are recruited from competitors and bring their books of business with them, making PNFP a talent-and-relationship engine rather than a retail-distribution bank.

Both companies merged into a newly formed holding company, with Synovus shareholders receiving 0.5237 New Pinnacle shares each. Pinnacle Bank also became a Federal Reserve System member bank as part of the transaction.

Beyond the core bank, PNFP holds a 49% equity-method stake in BHG (Bankers Healthcare Group), a national lending platform; a wealth management business (~$16 billion brokerage assets, ~$8.5 billion trust); and treasury management and specialty deposit operations. BHG is the most opaque and most volatile earnings contributor and warrants disproportionate attention in any forward model.

## Financial Analysis

The figures below reference legacy Pinnacle FY2025 standalone results unless noted, because the combined entity has only one reported quarter (Q1 2026). Any naive year-over-year per-share comparison is distorted by the merger and should be normalized.

**FY2025 standalone results (legacy Pinnacle):**
- Net income $642M versus $475M in 2024 (+35%); diluted EPS to common $8.07 versus $5.96 (+35%). The XBRL feed reports a higher $792M net income figure that appears to include combined/discontinued adjustments; the MD&A figure of $641.9M is the correct standalone basis.
- Net interest income $1.55B (+13.4%), with NIM expanding to 3.24% from 3.16% as funding rates fell faster (-48bps) than earning-asset yields (-38bps).
- Loans +10.3% organic to $39.2B; deposits +10.6% to $47.4B; loan/deposit ratio ~83%.
- Efficiency ratio improved to 56.8% from 59.6%.
- ROA 1.15%, ROE 9.35%, TBV accretion 13.3%.

**Capital and liquidity (standalone, pre-merger):** CET1 10.9%, total capital 13.0%, leverage 9.6% — all comfortably well-capitalized. Core funding was 84% of deposits, but ~$18 billion of deposits (~38%) are uninsured/uncollateralized, an elevated post-SVB sensitivity, partly mitigated by IntraFi reciprocal deposits counted as core. Securities portfolio unrealized losses persist; the company repositioned $271M in 2025 at a $16.6M loss (after $899M at a $71.9M loss in 2024).

**Noninterest income +36.5% to $507M**, but the quality is mixed. BHG equity income jumped +87% to $118M (≈18% of pre-tax income), and BHG dividends to Pinnacle rose to $163M from $72M. This is a less-repeatable, more-volatile stream tied to BHG's gain-on-sale-versus-retain decisions.

**Q1 2026 (first combined quarter):** Net income ~$150M total (~$134.7M to common per local reporting), characterized by management as a beat. Earnings surprise history is solid: +3.8% (Q1'26), -2.2% (Q4'25), +8.7% (Q3'25), +3.3% (Q2'25) — three beats, one miss.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.25 ("distress") is a methodological artifact for a bank, not a real signal — deposits are counted as liabilities, producing a D/E of 7.2x that is normal for the industry. Capital ratios are the correct lens, and they are healthy.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are in play. First, scale: the Synovus combination gives the franchise the size to absorb rising technology and compliance costs while competing for larger relationships. Second, geography: the Southeast remains a high-migration region (TN, GA, NC, SC, FL), supporting a structurally above-average loan-growth runway. Third, and most distinctive, the talent engine: management cited the "highest pace of hiring in our history" with net +50 producers added in Q1 2026 and more in early Q2. Because PNFP's growth is mechanically driven by producer recruitment, accelerating hiring post-merger is the leading indicator that integration is energizing rather than disrupting the franchise, the opposite of the typical merger talent-flight risk.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-quarter consensus rose +1.4% over 90 days, current-year +0.8%, next year roughly flat. This is healthy momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that would force a re-rating. Forward EPS of $10.32 (CY) and $11.85 (NY) implies roughly 15% year-over-year growth into the next year, supported by synergy capture and continued organic loan/deposit growth.

The growth thesis depends on integration delivering. The company's guidance language frames meaningful cost synergies and EPS accretion, but these are forward promises with one quarter of evidence. The hiring data is the best early proxy that the soft side of the merger is working.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings, PNFP trades at a clear discount to its peer set:

| Metric | PNFP | Peer median |
|--------|------|-------------|
| P/E (current-year fwd) | ~9.3x | 12.2x |
| P/E (next-year fwd) | ~8.1x | — |
| Dividend yield | 2.0% | 2.2% |

The trailing P/E of 14.8x is misleading because trailing EPS ($8.07) is the smaller, standalone legacy base; the combined entity's forward earnings power puts the multiple materially lower. Direct peers WBS (11.9x), EWBC (12.2x), FHN (12.2x), WTFC (12.6x) and UMBF (11.6x) all trade above PNFP's forward multiple. Analyst price targets bracket $100 (low) to $130 (high), with a mean of $116.79 and median of $120 — implying roughly 21% upside to the mean from $96.36.

The discount reflects three things the market is correctly pricing: integration execution risk on a deal that doubled the company's size, the opacity and recent credit deterioration in the BHG earnings stream, and a macro environment in which regional banks face scrutiny over CRE concentration and uninsured-deposit funding. A discount of this magnitude to peers is at the wide end of what integration risk alone would justify, which is why we see genuine value support and a constructive 12-month setup, but not enough asymmetry, given the binary integration question, to clear a high-conviction long bar today.

We assign a valuation score reflecting attractive but appropriately-discounted forward earnings: cheap relative to peers and to its own forward power, with the discount partly earned by execution risk.

## Competitive Landscape

PNFP competes against large nationals (rate and scale advantages), community banks (service parity), and non-bank/fintech and credit-union entrants (regulatory and tax advantages). The Synovus merger is a direct response to consolidation pressure — gaining scale to spread technology and compliance costs while preserving a relationship-banking culture (the CEO's "scale with a soul" framing).

The competitive position is improving on trajectory. The talent-acquisition model is working through the disruption window: poaching producers from competitors unsettled by their own M&A is precisely the environment in which PNFP gains share, and the record hiring pace confirms it. Southeast in-migration provides a structural demand tailwind that pure-share analysis understates. NIM and efficiency-ratio improvements indicate the franchise is converting that share gain into operating leverage. The bank is roughly rate-neutral now (EVE/earnings simulations show only ±0.3% NII sensitivity to a 200bp move and a modest +1.7% benefit in a 200bp decline), positioning it reasonably for an expected 2026 rate-cut environment.

## Risk Assessment

**Synovus integration (dominant, near-term, partly binary).** The 10-K added an entirely new risk section warning that anticipated cost savings, synergies and earnings accretion "may not be realized in full, may take longer than anticipated, or may not be realized at all." This is two large, culturally distinct banks integrating, and the entire forward earnings step-up depends on it. We have exactly one combined quarter of data. Key-employee retention on both sides is specifically flagged.

**BHG concentration and deterioration.** BHG operates through a 49%-owned, off-balance-sheet platform that Pinnacle does not control (2 of 5 board seats). The direction is the concern: BHG's allowance for credit losses jumped +57% to $376M, and its loan-substitution liability rose to $708.8M (8.6% of sold loans, up from 7.1%). Both indicate credit stress in that book. BHG dividends are large and lumpy, so a downturn there hits both the income statement and cash contribution.

**Concentrations.** Non-owner-occupied CRE plus multifamily plus construction equals 221% of risk-based capital (down from 242%). Named concentrations above 25% of capital include lessors of nonresidential buildings ($6.4B), residential buildings ($2.5B), homebuilders ($1.4B), and music publishers ($1.6B, up from $1.1B). A $16.9M CRE charge-off hit Q4 2025. This sits inside a macro backdrop where regional-bank CRE exposure is under heightened scrutiny.

**Funding.** Uninsured deposits and ~16% wholesale/brokered funding are sensitivities in a deposit-competition environment, with emerging stablecoin/crypto disintermediation explicitly cited.

**Goodwill.** ~$1.9B standalone, far larger post-merger; impairment risk is tied to bank-stock valuations and relative performance.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a near-term caution signal. The term structure is in backwardation — near-term ATM IV of 38.1% sits above the 6-month tenor at 31.1% — which typically prices an imminent event. IV is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 31.2% (a 6.9pp spread, 64th percentile within the chain), and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±6.2%. Put/call OI rose from 0.13 to 0.35 over the past month while the stock fell 5.8%. The skew is still call-heavy (-4.2%) at the front month, so positioning is not yet aggressively bearish, but the combination of backwardation, rich IV, rising put OI, and a degrading analyst sentiment trend supports patience over chasing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Synovus integration tracks to plan, cost synergies flow through, and the combined franchise delivers the ~$11.85 next-year EPS consensus implies. The talent engine keeps poaching producers from disrupted competitors (the +50 Q1 hiring pace continues), driving above-peer organic growth in a high-migration Southeast. As integration de-risks over 2026, the forward discount to peers compresses toward parity. Applying a peer-median ~12x to next-year EPS of $11.85 yields ~$142; even a partial re-rating to 11x on $11.85 reaches ~$130, matching the high analyst target. Bull case: ~$130 (+35%).

### Bear Case
Integration runs over budget or hits deposit/talent attrition; BHG credit deterioration accelerates (the +57% allowance and rising substitution liability prove leading, not conservative); a CRE or music-publisher concentration produces a larger charge-off; and the uninsured-deposit base proves rate-sensitive in a competitive funding environment. Synergy disappointment plus a credit scare would cap earnings near the standalone trajectory and widen the discount. A de-rating to ~8x on impaired ~$10 EPS yields ~$80. Bear case: ~$81 (-16%).

The base case sits in between: integration proceeds adequately, BHG stress remains contained, and the discount narrows only partially as the market waits for proof. Base case ~$110 (+14%) over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two earnings prints (Q2 2026, late July; Q3 2026, late October) are decisive. They are the first periods in which merger-cost run-rate, synergy capture, deposit retention, and BHG credit trajectory become observable in combined-entity data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2/Q3 2026 disclosures showing merger cost synergies tracking at or ahead of plan, with deposit base and producer count stable or growing (net hiring sustained above ~30 producers/quarter).
2. BHG credit metrics stabilizing — substitution liability ratio flat-to-down from 8.6% and allowance growth decelerating — confirming the 2025 reserve build was conservatism rather than the front edge of losses.
3. Forward CY/NY EPS revisions inflecting higher (toward +5% or more over 90 days) while the stock remains below ~10x forward, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Evidence of merger integration slippage — synergy targets pushed out, elevated one-time costs, or producer/deposit attrition disclosed in Q2/Q3.
2. A material BHG credit event or a step-up in CRE/music-publisher charge-offs beyond the Q4'25 $16.9M run-rate.
3. CET1 erosion below ~10% or a forced securities-loss realization that pressures capital.

## Why This Is a HOLD and Not a BUY

The overall score sits modestly above neutral, so the burden is to state precisely what prevents an upgrade. Three concrete, falsifiable concerns hold this at HOLD:

1. **Integration is unproven and binary.** The entire forward earnings step-up — the difference between trailing $8.07 and forward $10.32/$11.85 — rests on a merger of equals that closed only five months ago, where the company itself warns synergies "may not be realized at all." With one combined quarter, the thesis is contingent on something not yet demonstrated. Q2/Q3 2026 cost and attrition data will confirm or refute it.

2. **BHG is deteriorating, not merely opaque.** A 49%-owned platform supplying ~18% of pre-tax income raised its credit allowance +57% to $376M and its substitution liability to 8.6% of sold loans. That is concrete credit stress in a swing earnings driver Pinnacle does not control. Stabilization in those two metrics would remove the concern.

3. **The market is pricing near-term downside.** Options are in backwardation, IV is rich versus realized, put OI tripled to 0.35 as the stock fell, and analyst sentiment is degrading. The setup argues for waiting for the next print rather than paying up now.

These are specific, time-bound execution and credit risks that the forward discount is compensating for, and that the next two quarters will resolve. Until integration is demonstrably tracking to synergy plan and BHG credit stabilizes, the prudent posture is to hold for the synergy/hiring upside while monitoring the downgrade triggers above.

## Conclusion

PNFP is a well-capitalized, well-managed, talent-driven Southeast regional bank trading at a meaningful forward discount to peers after doubling its size through the Synovus merger of equals. The franchise quality, aligned management (comp tied to TBV accretion, EPS, ROATCE and asset quality), record hiring pace, and ~9.3x forward earnings support a constructive 12-month view. The valuation discount is earned: integration synergies are unproven after a single combined quarter, BHG is showing genuine credit deterioration in an earnings stream worth ~18% of pre-tax income, and the options market is pricing near-term stress. The risk/reward is favorable but not asymmetric enough, and not de-risked enough, to warrant a high-conviction long today.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth-oriented). Accumulate on confirmed integration progress and BHG credit stabilization in the Q2-Q3 2026 prints, or on weakness toward the low-$80s where the bear case is largely discounted. Upgrade to BUY on synergy delivery plus a revision inflection; downgrade to AVOID on integration slippage or a BHG/CRE credit event.
