**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Almonty Industries is a tungsten concentrate producer pursuing a transformation from a sub-scale Portuguese mining operation into the leading non-China supplier of a critical defense and semiconductor metal. The investment case rests almost entirely on one asset: the Sangdong mine in South Korea, one of the world's largest and highest-grade tungsten deposits (~0.51% WO₃, roughly three times the global average), which completed Phase 1 commissioning in March 2026 after a three-decade dormancy. At full Phase 2 capacity (targeted 2027), management claims Sangdong could supply approximately 40% of non-China global tungsten demand. The stock has risen roughly 268% over the trailing year and roughly 530% from its July 2025 low of $3.16, driven by a combination of a record APT (ammonium paratungstate) price move (+534% YoY to ~US$2,250/MTU, with spot reported above US$3,000 in some sources), China export restrictions, US post-2027 non-China procurement mandates, and a series of capital-markets repositioning moves (Montana HQ relocation, US domestication, Russell 1000/3000 inclusion).

The problem is the gap between the story and the financials. FY2025 revenue was just C$32.5M (≈US$23M), essentially all from the legacy Panasqueira mine, against a US$5.6B market capitalization — a trailing price/sales multiple near 240x. Adjusted EBITDA was negative C$17.1M and worsening year-over-year as the pre-production cost base scaled. The headline C$161.9M net loss is roughly 78% non-cash, driven by revaluation of convertible-debenture embedded derivatives and warrants that mechanically grow as the share price rises, so the loss overstates operational distress. But the business is not yet profitable on an EBITDA basis, and the entire equity value is a forward bet on the Sangdong ramp plus sustained record tungsten prices.

We rate ALM a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The strategic thesis is credible and the structural demand backdrop is real, but the valuation already prices in flawless execution and durable record pricing. With near-term implied volatility at 95.4% and options in backwardation, the market is pricing a binary outcome around execution and commodity prices that we cannot resolve in either direction with current evidence. The decisive data — Phase 1 throughput optimization, commercial production economics, and confirmation of offtake terms — arrives over the next two to three quarters.

## Company Overview

Almonty operates as a tungsten concentrate producer with a development pipeline anchored by four assets. **Sangdong (Gangwon Province, South Korea)** is the flagship: Phase 1 was commissioned in March 2026 at roughly 640,000 tonnes ore per year, yielding ~2,300 tonnes concentrate annually, with Phase 2 (2027) targeting ~1.2M tonnes ore per year and roughly double the concentrate output (~4,600 t/yr). **Panasqueira (Portugal)** is the existing producing mine and the source of essentially all current revenue. The development pipeline includes the Sangdong molybdenum deposit, a planned tungsten oxide refining facility (the "Korean Trinity" integrated value chain concept), a Spanish project, and the recently acquired Gentung Tungsten Project in Beaverhead County, Montana.

The strategic premise is concentration risk in the global tungsten supply chain. China produces an estimated 82–88% of world tungsten and has tightened export controls, while US defense procurement rules are expected to mandate non-China sourcing after 2027. Almonty is one of very few scaled Western producers positioned to fill that gap, which underpins both its "strategic pillar" narrative and its index/passive-flow inclusion.

The company is a foreign private issuer historically domiciled in Canada (TSX/ASX/Frankfurt listed), reporting under IFRS in Canadian dollars, that completed a Nasdaq IPO in July 2025 and is undergoing US domestication. This creates reporting complexity: standard SEC 10-K/10-Q XBRL data is unavailable, and financial detail comes primarily from press releases and SEDAR+ filings. All figures below are in CAD unless noted.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and operating income.** FY2025 revenue was C$32.5M, up only 13% from C$28.8M in FY2024, despite the +534% YoY move in average APT prices. The muted revenue growth reflects volume constraints pre-Sangdong (Panasqueira capacity is fixed) and ~C$3.1M of revenue deferred to Q1'26 on shipment timing. Income from mining operations was positive at C$2.4M (FY2024: C$2.0M), confirming the legacy operation is marginally profitable at the gross level. Q4'25 revenue was C$8.7M against C$6.3M in Q4'24.

**The net loss is largely an accounting artifact.** The C$161.9M FY2025 net loss is dominated by C$126.7M of non-cash charges: a C$97.4M loss on revaluation of embedded derivative liabilities (convertible debentures) plus a C$29.3M warrant revaluation. These instruments revalue upward as the share price rises, so the C$1.36 → C$12.07 (CAD) move in 2025 mechanically produced the loss. A screen reading "lost C$162M" badly mischaracterizes the cash picture. The offset is that these same instruments represent dilution and ongoing earnings volatility, and the dynamic recurs as the stock moves.

**Adjusted EBITDA is negative and worsening.** Adjusted EBITDA was −C$17.1M in FY2025 versus −C$3.1M in FY2024. G&A tripled to C$20.5M from C$6.2M, driven by legal, offering, and domestication costs plus C$11.1M of stock-based compensation. The business is pre-commercial-production at scale and consuming cash at the operating level.

**Liquidity is no longer the near-term concern.** Cash rose from C$7.8M (Dec 2024) to C$268.4M (Dec 2025) via the July 2025 Nasdaq IPO (US$90M) and a December 2025 follow-on (US$129.4M, 20.7M shares). This funds the Phase 2 build-out and removes the financing-cliff risk that historically dogged the company. The cost was material dilution: shares outstanding now stand at 284M.

**Earnings quality flags.** The financials carry several markers warranting scrutiny: heavy reliance on equity issuance and convertible/warrant structures, an expanding share count, revenue-recognition timing adjustments, large non-IFRS adjustments stripping out sizable items, and C$11.1M of stock-based comp against C$32.5M of revenue. None is disqualifying for a development-stage miner, but collectively they argue for conservative interpretation.

The earnings surprise record reinforces the visibility problem: three misses in four quarters (Q1'26 −300%, Q4'25 −892%, Q2'25 −17,547%) against one large beat (Q3'25 +2,449%). The swings reflect the derivative-revaluation noise and the pre-production cost base rather than a stable, forecastable earnings stream.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is genuinely large if it converts. Sangdong Phase 1 roughly doubles the company's production base, and Phase 2 doubles it again, potentially making Almonty a ~40% non-China tungsten supplier. Demand drivers are stacked: defense munitions (Rheinmetall and munitions ramps cited), aerospace, semiconductors and WF6 deposition (with named supply-chain stress at Samsung, DB HiTek, Kanto Denka, Central Glass), and AI hardware. Tungsten has moved from a stable niche (historically ~US$300–450/MTU) to a security-critical commodity (>US$3,000/MTU in recent reports), the central tailwind, and US post-2027 procurement mandates provide a policy floor under Western demand.

Estimate revisions are large: current-year EPS revised +282.7% over 30 days and +445.4% over 90 days to $1.68; next-year to $2.13 (+97.3% / +78.1%). Under our framework, EPS revisions of this magnitude combined with a structural demand shift are normally a strong forward signal. We weight this carefully here for three reasons. First, the estimates are produced by a single analyst (current year) and two analysts (next year), so this is not broad consensus, and the revision velocity reflects one model's assumptions about ramp timing and APT pricing, not a market-wide reassessment. Second, these forward EPS figures are extraordinarily sensitive to the tungsten price assumption, which is the least controllable variable in the model and currently sits at multi-decade highs. Third, the company has missed three of its last four quarters, so the track record does not yet support high confidence in forward delivery. The revision signal is real but thin, and it is a price/volume-driven extrapolation rather than confirmed earnings power.

The durability question dominates. Revenue growth in FY2025 was almost entirely price-driven (volumes constrained, prices up 534%). Record prices incentivize new supply and substitution over time. The bull case requires the deficit to persist long enough for Sangdong to ramp into it and lock in offtake at favorable pricing. That is plausible given China's structural dominance and policy-driven Western demand, but it is not guaranteed, and the specific offtake structures, pricing floors, and counterparties were not confirmable in available sources.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case breaks down at current levels. At a US$5.6B market cap on ~US$23M trailing revenue, ALM trades near 240x trailing sales and at a negative trailing/forward P/E (−43.7x on −$0.57 EPS) because it is not yet profitable. No conventional multiple supports the price; the entire valuation is a forward bet.

Working from the single-analyst forward estimates, the stock trades at roughly 11.9x current-year ($1.68) and 9.4x next-year ($2.13) EPS. Those multiples look reasonable for a high-growth producer if the estimates are achieved. But the estimates embed both a flawless Phase 1/Phase 2 ramp and sustained record APT pricing. Each is a material risk, and they are not independent: a tungsten price reversion would simultaneously cut margins and revenue while the ramp execution risk is fully unhedged. The forward "cheapness" is therefore conditional on the two least certain variables in the model both resolving favorably.

We apply cycle-adjusted thinking here, but the conclusion differs from a typical high-multiple growth stock. The standard adjustment — don't penalize a high trailing multiple at a cyclical trough — does not rescue ALM, because the trailing period is not a trough; it is a record. APT prices are at multi-decade highs, not lows. Normalizing toward a mid-cycle tungsten price (well below US$3,000/MTU) would compress the forward earnings power the bull case relies on. The market is extrapolating peak pricing into a forward multiple, which is the opposite of buying trough earnings cheaply.

On peer context, there are few directly comparable scaled Western tungsten producers, which is why the scarcity narrative supports the multiple; scarcity of comparables also means the price is set by narrative and passive flows (Russell inclusion) rather than by relative-value discipline.

Analyst price targets cluster from US$20.55 (low) to US$25.93 (high), mean US$23.85, median US$24.60, modestly above the current US$19.96. Under our framework, analyst targets act as floors only on stocks that are cheap relative to growth; here, with the stock at an extreme multiple of trailing fundamentals and the targets themselves built on the same ramp-plus-price extrapolation, the targets are not independent confirmation. They reflect consensus optimism vulnerable to any disappointment. We score valuation low (3.0): the price requires near-perfect execution and durable record pricing, leaving no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Almonty's competitive position is improving and structurally advantaged, which is the strongest pillar of the case. The moat is jurisdictional and grade-driven: Sangdong is a high-grade deposit in an allied jurisdiction, "conflict-free," and aligned with US post-2027 procurement mandates. With China controlling supply and tightening exports, Western and defense buyers face a narrow set of scaled alternatives, and Almonty is among the few that can supply at scale. The coordinated repositioning (Montana HQ, US domestication, a retired Brigadier General as COO, Russell 1000 inclusion) is a deliberate effort to convert the asset story into a US strategic-supplier designation, which broadens the investor base and supports demand realization.

The trajectory is positive: Phase 1 commissioning attended by 200+ South Korean political figures and US embassy representatives signals government-level backing, and the Gentung Montana asset adds a near-term US production angle. The caveat is that record prices invite new supply and substitution over a multi-year horizon, and the durability of the deficit is the key debate. The competitive edge is real today; its persistence depends on how quickly Western supply (including Almonty's own) closes the gap. We score competitive position 7.5 on improving trajectory and structural tailwind, tempered by the long-run supply-response risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution/ramp risk.** The entire valuation rests on a mine that has been in production for only months after 30 years of dormancy. Phase 1 throughput optimization and the Phase 2 2027 doubling are unproven. Mine restarts rarely proceed without friction.

**Valuation disconnect.** The revenue base and negative EBITDA against a US$5.6B market cap mean any ramp delay or tungsten price reversion is a significant downside catalyst with little support below.

**Tungsten price dependence.** Revenue growth was almost entirely price-driven and partly geopolitical (China curbs, defense/semiconductor demand). Commodity prices are cyclical; the structural-deficit thesis is plausible but not guaranteed.

**Capital structure/dilution.** Convertible debentures with embedded derivatives and warrants create ongoing earnings volatility and potential dilution. Two equity raises in 2025 diluted holders; the share-price-up-equals-accounting-loss dynamic will recur.

**Geopolitical/jurisdictional concentration.** The flagship asset sits in South Korea near a sensitive geopolitical region; revenue/cost exposure spans KRW, EUR, CAD, and USD (C$3.0M FX loss in FY2025).

**Offtake/demand realization.** The thesis depends on Western/defense offtake materializing post-2027. The specific structures, pricing floors, and counterparties were not confirmable in available sources and should be verified directly from the March 2026 AIF on SEDAR+.

**Reporting/governance friction.** Management flags increased reporting complexity through the junior-to-midcap transition; US domestication and multi-exchange listing add governance friction. A June 2026 shareholder vote is pending. The large stock-based comp (C$11.1M) relative to revenue warrants alignment scrutiny.

**Source quality.** A large share of bullish coverage is German-language/MSN/Stockhouse promotional content; audited financials and official press releases are reliable, but price-target and "frenzy" articles should be heavily discounted.

We score risk factors 3.0: multiple binary, concentrated, and not-yet-priced risks (ramp execution, commodity reversion, offtake confirmation) sit against a valuation that prices the favorable resolution of all of them.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, large two-way movement, not a settled direction. Near-term ATM IV is 95.4% versus 92.1% one-year historical volatility (rich by 3.3 points, 80th percentile within the chain), with a ±15.5% implied move on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (95.4% near-term vs 84.5% at six months), which signals the market expects a near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is call-heavy (−6.6% near-term, turning positive at +5.8% by six months) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.54, so positioning is not defensively bearish; near-term sentiment leans bullish/speculative while longer-dated skew turns more cautious. Short interest is negligible (3.26M shares, 0.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded short to squeeze and no informed bearish positioning to confirm a downside thesis. The composite read: high uncertainty, near-term speculative bullishness, and a market braced for a large move in either direction around upcoming production and commodity data.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** China's structural dominance and export curbs make non-China tungsten a security-critical supply gap, and US post-2027 procurement mandates create a policy-backed demand floor. Sangdong is a high-grade, allied-jurisdiction asset that, at Phase 2 capacity, could supply ~40% of non-China demand. With C$268M cash funding the build-out, financing risk is removed. If Phase 1 optimizes and Phase 2 ramps on schedule into sustained elevated APT pricing, forward EPS of ~$1.68–$2.13 is achievable, and the company re-rates as a defense-strategic supplier with passive-flow support. Bull target ~US$26 (the high end of analyst estimates, ~12x next-year EPS), representing ~30% upside.

**Bear Case.** The stock is priced at ~240x trailing sales on a single-mine ramp and record commodity prices. Any Phase 1 throughput shortfall, Phase 2 delay, or APT price reversion toward mid-cycle levels collapses the forward earnings the multiple depends on. The convertible/warrant overhang and stock-based comp dilute holders, and three of the last four quarters missed. If the ramp stumbles or tungsten reverts, the stock could retrace toward US$10–12 (roughly its January–February 2026 range), representing ~40–50% downside. With negative EBITDA and the entire value forward-dated, there is little fundamental support on the way down.

The asymmetry is unfavorable at the current price: ~30% to the bull case against ~40–50% to the bear case, with the upside requiring two uncertain variables to resolve favorably and the downside triggered by either failing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — we do not have sufficient evidence to underwrite either a confident long or a short, and the valuation precludes a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Phase 1 reaching steady-state commercial throughput (~2,300 t concentrate/yr run-rate) with disclosed unit cash costs that confirm positive operating margin at a normalized (not peak) APT price.
- Confirmation of binding long-term offtake agreements with named Western/defense counterparties and disclosed pricing floors (verifiable from SEDAR+/AIF), which would de-risk the demand side independent of spot pricing.
- A pullback toward US$12–14 that restores a margin of safety relative to forward earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Phase 1 throughput shortfall or Phase 2 schedule slip disclosed in a quarterly update.
- An APT price reversion of more than ~30% from recent levels that undermines forward EPS assumptions.
- Renewed equity issuance at depressed prices, or evidence the offtake/demand thesis is not materializing into 2027.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two to three quarterly updates, with particular attention to the first full quarter of Phase 1 commercial production economics and any offtake disclosures around the post-2027 procurement window. The June 2026 shareholder vote and any further capital-markets actions are interim checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Almonty is a credible strategic asset attached to a valuation that already discounts a near-perfect outcome. The non-China tungsten thesis is real, the Sangdong grade and scale are genuine, the balance sheet is funded, and the policy backdrop is supportive. What is in dispute is whether a US$5.6B equity value can be justified by ~US$23M of trailing revenue, negative EBITDA, a single-mine ramp with months of operating history, and forward earnings that depend on record commodity prices holding. We cannot resolve that binary with current data, and the options market, with near-term IV at 95.4% in backwardation, is pricing a large move it cannot direct.

We recommend HOLD. Existing holders with conviction on the ramp and the structural deficit have a funded, well-positioned asset; new capital does not have an attractive entry here, because the risk/reward is skewed against it (~30% upside versus ~40–50% downside) and the decisive evidence (commercial-production economics and offtake confirmation) is two to three quarters away. We would become constructive on physical confirmation of the Phase 1 ramp at positive normalized-price margins, or on a meaningful pullback that restores a margin of safety.
