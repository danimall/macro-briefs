**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everest Group is a top-tier Bermuda-domiciled global reinsurer executing a deliberate strategic contraction: exiting the global commercial retail primary insurance business, ring-fencing its problematic legacy U.S. casualty reserves through a $5.4B adverse-development cover (ADC), and refocusing on core treaty reinsurance and higher-margin global wholesale specialty. The Q1 2026 result was the first clean quarter demonstrating that the de-risked portfolio can produce sub-90s reinsurance combined ratios in a low-catastrophe environment: net income tripled to $653M ($16.21 diluted EPS), the consolidated combined ratio swung 11.5 points to 91.2%, and the Reinsurance Treaty segment posted an 87.2% combined ratio versus 104.7% a year earlier.

The central investment debate is unchanged and unresolved. Everest's 2024 earnings collapse (net income fell ~46% to $1.37B despite rising revenue) was driven by U.S. casualty reserve strengthening tied to social inflation, and IBNR of $24.5B out of $34.6B in total loss reserves keeps the book heavily estimate-dependent. The ADC caps the downside on the accident-year-2024-and-prior casualty book, and Q1 2026 delivered $33M of net favorable prior-year development, the first evidence the reserve narrative may be shifting from headwind to neutral. The stock trades at roughly 1.0x book value ($386 versus $383.75 BVPS), which prices continued skepticism rather than a growth story. If reserves hold and adjusted operating ROE normalizes toward the high-teens level seen in 2023 (18.2%, versus only 8.7% in 2024), there is room for the multiple to re-rate. If reserves re-deteriorate, book value itself is at risk.

Score trajectory has been declining (6.7 in February → 5.1 in June), driven by cuts to management quality, risk, and growth as the leadership overhaul and reserve overhang weighed. The Q1 2026 clean quarter, the ~12% price recovery off the June trough, and the demonstrated favorable development argue the trajectory has bottomed. We move the overall score modestly higher to 5.55 and retain a HOLD, classified as a growth hold: the recovery thesis has real optionality, but the reserve-adequacy question is genuinely open and no executive has bought stock in the open market near book. We recommend owning this name on evidence rather than expectation.

## Company Overview

Everest is a global reinsurer and specialty insurer with a ~50-year operating history and S&P 500 membership. Its two principal operating entities are Everest Reinsurance (Bermuda) Ltd., regulated by the Bermuda Monetary Authority, and Everest Reinsurance Company (Delaware, a Federal Home Loan Bank of New York member). Effective January 1, 2026, the company re-segmented into three reportable units:

- **Reinsurance Treaty** — the core franchise, roughly 68% of gross written premium ($2.67B GWP in Q1 2026) and $20.4B of reserves (59% of total). Global property, casualty, and specialty treaty reinsurance.
- **Global Wholesale & Specialty (GW&S)** — the growth engine ($793M GWP), covering specialty, professional liability, and accident & health lines. This is the retained and expanding insurance franchise.
- **Legacy** — run-off, containing the divested commercial retail insurance business, discontinued programs, and asbestos & environmental exposures.

The structural story is a deliberate shrink-to-quality. Everest is exiting the global commercial retail primary insurance business, having sold renewal rights to AIG (~$300M total, Oct/Dec 2025) and its Canadian operations to Wawanesa (C$410M, announced March 2026, expected to close H2 2026). This explains the ~18% YoY drop in headline GWP in Q1 2026 (Legacy GWP fell 80%), which understates the health of the retained book.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year net income path** illustrates the volatility that defines this name: $1.38B (2021), $0.60B (2022), $2.52B (2023), $1.37B (2024), $1.59B (2025). The 2023→2024 decline despite rising revenue was the casualty reserve-strengthening and catastrophe episode that remains the central concern.

**Q1 2026 results.** Net investment income rose 15.5% to $567M, boosted by a $94M jump in limited-partnership income, a volatile, NAV-driven, lag-reported line that should not be extrapolated at face value. Catastrophe losses were just $130M (1.7 points) versus $534M (13.9 points) in Q1 2025, which had carried $512M of California wildfire losses. The $33M of net favorable prior-year development was offset in part by $37M of adverse Russia/Ukraine attritional development.

The Q1 beat (actual $16.08 versus $14.39 estimate, +11.7%) followed a Q3 2025 miss of -49.1% and a Q4 miss of -2.2%, so the surprise track record is two-sided (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters). The catastrophe and reserve line items drive the quarter-to-quarter dispersion, which is inherent to the reinsurance model and cannot be forecast with precision.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets $62.3B; investments and cash $45.0B, high-quality fixed income with an AA- composite rating and 3.5-year duration. Loss & LAE reserves $34.6B; shareholders' equity $15.3B; book value per share $383.75. Debt of $3.6B (senior notes $2.35B, FHLB borrowings $1.0B, subordinated $218M) is very manageable for a reinsurer. The Altman Z-score screen (~0.8) is a false distress signal; the model was designed for manufacturers and is meaningless for an insurer whose balance sheet is dominated by investment assets and actuarial reserves. Statutory capital is strong: Bermuda Re economic capital $5.4B versus $2.5B required; Everest Re actual capital $8.9B versus $5.1B regulatory target.

**Capital allocation.** Everest repurchased $797M of stock in 2025 and $331M in Q1 2026, cutting share count from 43.0M to 39.8M YoY. The quarterly dividend is $2.00/share (~2.1% yield). Buying back stock near book value while shares underperform peers is defensible, and the ~$1.13B repurchased over five quarters is the strongest bullish signal management has sent, though it is a corporate action, not personal open-market conviction.

Financial health scores a 6.5: the capital position is strong, leverage is appropriate for the business model, and cash generation is solid, but the reserve-estimate dependence and the inherent earnings volatility keep this out of the 7+ range.

## Growth Analysis

Everest is deliberately shrinking headline premium, so top-line growth metrics are misleading. The retail exit drove the ~18% GWP decline, and that is by design. The retained franchise is the relevant growth engine: Reinsurance Treaty at 87.2% combined ratio and GW&S at ~97% show the core book is sound, and GW&S specialty lines are the area management intends to expand.

Estimate revisions are directionally mixed but stabilizing. Current-quarter consensus rose +2.6% over 30 days but is down -5.4% over 90 days; current-year at $52.44 is roughly flat (+0.6% / 30d, -0.3% / 90d); next-year at $60.45 is essentially unchanged. This is below the >30% upward-revision velocity that flags a strong inflection buy; consensus has settled after the Q1 rebound and is now waiting on the durability of the reserve recovery and the 2026 catastrophe season.

The forward growth thesis rests on ROE normalization rather than premium expansion. Adjusted operating ROE was only 8.7% in 2024 versus 18.2% in 2023. If the de-risked portfolio sustains sub-90s reinsurance combined ratios in benign-cat quarters and reserve development stays neutral-to-favorable, ROE recovers toward the mid-teens, and the earnings base grows without balance-sheet growth. That is a credible path, but it is contingent on the reserve question and on cat luck. Growth prospects score a 5.0: the recovery-of-earnings-power case is real but conditional, and there is no premium-growth tailwind to lean on.

## Valuation Assessment

At $386 against $383.75 book value, Everest trades at roughly 1.0x book and ~10.2x trailing earnings (trailing EPS $37.80). The forward multiple on the $52.44 current-year consensus is ~7.4x. Peer medians in the reinsurance group sit at ~8.1x P/E; RGA trades at 11.2x, RNR at 4.9x, MTG at 8.1x, SPNT at 6.0x, a wide dispersion that reflects heterogeneous business mixes rather than a clean comparable set. Against RGA (life/health reinsurer, more stable book) Everest is cheaper on a forward basis; against RenaissanceRe (a pure property-cat play that also trades below its own history on softening cat rates) the discount is narrower.

A compressed multiple on a reinsurer with an open reserve-adequacy question is precisely the kind of situation where cheapness can be a value trap. The specific reason the market may be mispricing this stock is the ADC. By retroceding $5.4B of problematic accident-year-2024-and-prior casualty reserves, Everest has capped the tail risk that destroyed 2024 earnings, and Q1 2026 returned to favorable development. If that de-risking is real and reserves hold, an 8.7% ROE recovering toward the mid-teens supports a re-rating from 1.0x book toward 1.2–1.4x, which is where healthier reinsurers with stable reserves trade. Analyst targets (median $395, mean $400.8, high $484, low $360) bracket modest upside to the base case and meaningful upside if the recovery confirms.

The offsetting reality is that the discount is not irrational. The market is pricing genuine skepticism about whether IBNR is now conservatively set, and about ratings risk. The multiple releases only on reserve confirmation, not on time passing. We score valuation 6.0: there is an identifiable, specific catalyst (reserve stabilization plus ADC ring-fencing) that could correct the discount, with absolute book-value support limiting downside, but the catalyst is contingent enough that this is not a 7+.

## Competitive Landscape

Everest is a top-tier global reinsurer competing with Munich Re, Swiss Re, SCOR, RenaissanceRe, and Bermuda peers, and it cedes to Munich Re America and Endurance as retrocessionaires. The industry backdrop is a softening property-cat rate environment following the 2023–2024 hard market, while U.S. casualty remains pressured by social inflation, the exact exposure that hurt Everest.

The company is responding pro-cyclically: reducing casualty pro-rata treaty business, trimming property as rates soften, and growing higher-margin GW&S specialty lines. Its third-party capital vehicles, Mt. Logan Re (collateralized reinsurer) and Kilimanjaro cat bonds, together ~$1.5B of limit, are competitive tools that let it manage catastrophe volatility without consuming its own balance sheet.

The competitive-position trajectory is two-sided. Everest is a scaled, rated, diversified reinsurer that retains pricing access and is deliberately upgrading its book quality. Against that, the proxy's five-year pay-versus-performance disclosure shows cumulative TSR of 161.6 versus the peer group's 234.3; Everest has materially lagged its reinsurance peers, evidence that its franchise has been ceding relative ground during the reserve troubles. The retail exit is a rational simplification but also an admission that the prior expansion into commercial retail did not create value. Competitive position scores 5.5: a strong franchise that is stabilizing and refocusing, but with a demonstrated period of relative underperformance and no clear evidence yet of share gains in the retained lines.

## Risk Assessment

**U.S. casualty reserve adequacy / social inflation — the defining risk.** Adverse development on long-tail casualty (accident years 2024 and prior) drove the 2024 collapse, and management's forward-looking statements explicitly flag the risk of insufficient reserves due to social inflation. The IBNR is heavily estimate-dependent. The ADC caps the legacy tail, and Q1's favorable development is encouraging, but a single quarter does not close this question. Any prior-year casualty re-strengthening would break the recovery thesis and pressure ratings.

**Financial-strength rating pressure.** Ratings from A.M. Best, S&P, and Moody's are existential for a reinsurer. The reserve actions and the ADC were partly defensive moves to protect ratings. Confirmation and stability of ratings and outlooks over the next 12 months is a key watch item.

**Catastrophe volatility.** Q1 2025 California wildfires and 2026 Middle East conflict exposure ($58M booked, with explicit uncertainty language) illustrate how cat losses can swing the combined ratio 10+ points quarter to quarter. The 2026 hurricane season, into softening property rates, is a live variable.

**Execution risk on the retail exit.** Multiple simultaneous divestitures with clawback provisions (up to $70M reimbursement to AIG if renewals fall short) and $100M+ of severance/retention/impairment costs already booked. The Wawanesa/Canada close (H2 2026) must land cleanly.

**Leadership overhaul.** The 8-K record shows an unusual density of officer/director changes across 2025–2026. Jim Williamson is now President & CEO (previously COO), a transition tied to the underperformance period. CFO Mark Kociancic retired April 30, 2026, with Elias Habayeb succeeding him (~$7.4M initial equity grants), and CAO Robert Freiling covered the interim gap. Ricardo Anzaldua departed under a separation agreement effective 3/13/2026. New-CFO reserve reviews are a specific catalyst; a first-review reserve action either way would be highly informative.

**Bermuda/OECD tax uncertainty.** The new 15% Bermuda corporate tax (2025), Pillar Two global minimum tax, and shifting OECD/U.S. positions create risk to deferred tax assets and the effective rate.

Risk factors score 5.0: the reserve, ratings, and cat exposures are meaningful and idiosyncratic, but the ADC has materially reduced the legacy tail and much of the skepticism is already priced into the 1.0x-book multiple. The score reflects a balanced setup: real binary risks (reserve re-strengthening, cat season, rating action) against a valuation that already discounts them and a downside floored near book value.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 29.2% against 24.0% one-year historical volatility (rich by ~5.1 points), the IV percentile within the current chain is 72%, and the term structure is in backwardation (29.2% near-term versus 27.0% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent move. Put/call skew of 3.2% at the front month indicates elevated put demand, though the six-month tenor flips to -3.1% skew (call-leaning), suggesting hedging is concentrated on near-term events rather than a structural bearish view. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.2%. Short interest is modest at 4.6% of float (3.0 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short. The read: the market is hedging a specific near-term catalyst, most plausibly the next earnings print and its reserve commentary, consistent with the fundamental view that reserve confirmation is what unlocks the thesis. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing and favors patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($440–484).** The ADC has genuinely ring-fenced the legacy casualty tail. Reserve development stays neutral-to-favorable (Q1's favorable print continues), the retained book sustains sub-90s reinsurance combined ratios in benign-cat quarters, and adjusted operating ROE recovers toward the mid-teens. The retail exit completes cleanly (Wawanesa closes H2 2026), ratings hold with stable outlooks, and the stock re-rates from 1.0x book toward 1.2–1.4x as skepticism fades. Continued buybacks near book compound per-share book value. The high analyst target of $484 reflects roughly a 1.25x book, mid-teens ROE scenario.

**Bear case ($300–320).** A prior-year casualty reserve re-strengthening event (the ADC caps the ring-fenced layer but not all future development) reignites the 2024 narrative, pressures ratings, and puts book value at risk. A heavy 2026 hurricane season into softening property rates compresses the combined ratio back above 100%. The multiple stays at or below 1.0x book, and any book erosion drives the price toward the October 2025 lows near $302–305. The lag between reserve reality and reported results means the market can be right to remain skeptical for several more quarters.

**Base case ($400).** Reserves hold roughly neutral, cat experience is average, ROE recovers to the low-teens, and the stock re-rates modestly toward ~1.04–1.10x book, in line with the analyst median-to-mean ($395–401). This implies mid-single-digit price upside plus the ~2.1% dividend over 12 months, with book value compounding underneath via buybacks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).**

- **Base case target: $400.** Reasoning: ~1.05x book value on a book that is compounding via buybacks, plus a modest re-rating as reserve development stabilizes. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and aligns with the $395 median / $400.80 mean analyst targets.
- **Bull case target: $484** (~1.25x book, mid-teens ROE, reserve recovery confirmed).
- **Bear case target: $305** (~19% downside; reserve re-strengthening or heavy cat season drives the multiple back to/below book and erodes BVPS).
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the next 2–3 earnings prints (reserve commentary), the Wawanesa close (H2 2026), and the 2026 hurricane-season outcome.
- **Upside/downside from current ($375.56):** +6.5% to base, +29% to bull, -19% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A second and third consecutive quarter of neutral-to-favorable prior-year casualty reserve development, confirming the ADC/reserving inflection.
2. Open-market share purchases by executives at or near book value, closing the tension between corporate buybacks and the absence of personal conviction.
3. Rating-agency affirmations with stable/positive outlooks alongside a clean Wawanesa close, or a pullback toward book (~$350) that widens the margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any prior-year casualty reserve re-strengthening, particularly on accident years outside the ADC-protected layer.
2. A rating downgrade or negative-outlook action from A.M. Best, S&P, or Moody's.
3. A combined ratio back above 100% driven by non-cat attritional deterioration (as opposed to a one-off large cat), signaling the retained book is not as clean as Q1 suggested.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (reserve development the key line item), the H2 2026 Wawanesa close, and end of 2026 Atlantic hurricane season.

## Conclusion

Everest is a scaled, well-capitalized global reinsurer mid-way through a credible de-risking pivot. The ADC ring-fences the legacy casualty book that destroyed 2024 earnings, and Q1 2026 delivered the first clean quarter of the new structure. At ~1.0x book, the stock prices skepticism rather than the recovery, and the asymmetry has improved: downside is floored near book value while a reserve-confirmed re-rating toward 1.2–1.4x book offers real upside. The ~12% recovery off the June trough and the demonstrated favorable development argue the declining score trajectory (6.7 → 5.1) has bottomed, and we nudge the overall score to 5.55.

We stop short of a BUY for three reasons: the reserve-adequacy question is genuinely open and one favorable quarter does not close it; no executive has bought stock in the open market near book despite the depressed valuation; and the leadership overhaul (new CEO, new CFO) means the reserving philosophy is not yet proven under the current team. The upgrade path is clear: two or three more quarters of neutral-to-favorable development confirming that the reserve narrative has inflected. The reinsurance sector carries negative selection alpha in aggregate, so the bar for conviction here is a company-specific catalyst (reserve confirmation), not sector beta. Until that catalyst prints, HOLD is the disciplined stance.
