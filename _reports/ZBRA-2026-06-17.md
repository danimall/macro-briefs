**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zebra Technologies is the dominant franchise in Automatic Identification and Data Capture (AIDC): the barcode scanners, mobile computers, RFID, printing, and machine-vision hardware that digitizes frontline operations in retail, logistics, manufacturing, and healthcare. After a 2023 destocking cycle that cut revenue 21%, the business has re-inflected: FY25 revenue rose 8.3% to $5.40B, Q1 2026 grew 14.3% reported (4.3% organic), and management raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $18.30–$18.70 alongside a Q1 beat. At $237, the stock trades at roughly 12.8x the midpoint of that guidance, a meaningful discount to the company's own history and to electronic-equipment peers at a ~31.6x median.

The cheapness is real but not unexplained. The stock has fallen ~33% from its 52-week high of $352.66 (reached July 31, 2025), short interest sits at 10.1% of float, and analyst sentiment is degrading despite a still-bullish 14-of-18 buy/strong-buy tilt. The market is pricing four specific concerns: DRAM/NAND memory cost inflation that compresses forward margins, a $1.55B Term Loan A and revolver both maturing May 2027 at ~5% rates, the structural "AI bear case" that automation erodes demand for handheld scanners over time, and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet ($4.7B goodwill + $0.8B intangibles, ~65% of assets) carrying $2.66B of debt against a deliberately drawn-down $114M cash balance.

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The forward multiple is low enough that a cyclical recovery plus modest cost execution generates attractive returns, and the estimate-revision trend is positive (+3.2% CY EPS over 90 days). But near-term options markets are pricing acute stress (91.2% one-week ATM IV vs. 42.6% historical vol, steep backwardation), the memory-cost margin question is unresolved, and the AI-substitution overhang is a genuine structural debate rather than a priced-in truism. We need confirmation that gross margins hold through the memory cycle before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Zebra operates the leading global franchise in AIDC and enterprise mobility. Effective Q4 2025, management realigned reporting into two segments:

- **Connected Frontline (CF)** — ~$2.96B FY25 sales. Mobile computing, point-of-sale, self-service kiosks/touchscreens, and workflow software. This is where the $1.3B Elo Touch acquisition (closed 9/30/2025) lands. CF gross margin was 47.2% in FY25, down 200bps.
- **Asset Visibility & Automation (AVA)** — ~$2.44B FY25 sales. Thermal printing and supplies, data capture, fixed industrial scanning, machine vision (Photoneo), RFID, and RTLS. AVA carries the richer margin, expanding to 50.0% in FY25.

The business sells through a direct sales force plus an extensive global channel-partner network. Its competitive advantages are a dominant installed base and brand in barcode/AIDC, recurring supplies and services revenue, and switching costs from enterprise deployments. The strategic direction is a shift from discrete hardware toward integrated frontline workflow solutions, software subscriptions, machine vision, and embedded "AI agents."

In March 2026, Zebra exited its robotics automation business, selling it to Skild AI for ~$20M (mostly a minority equity stake) rather than scaling autonomous-robot hardware. The company is positioning its AI strategy around embedded software intelligence and self-service (Elo), not robotics hardware.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** The cyclical arc is clear: FY22 $5.78B → FY23 $4.58B (–21%) → FY24 $4.98B (+8.7%) → FY25 $5.40B (+8.3% reported, +6.2% organic). Q1 2026 delivered $1.495B, up 14.3% reported and 4.3% organic, with growth in all four regions and APAC strongest at +21.9%. The mix of reported vs. organic growth reflects the Elo contribution layering on top of a modest underlying recovery.

**Margins and earnings.** FY25 gross margin was 48.1%, down 30bps on tariffs and lower software/services margin offsetting volume leverage. GAAP net income fell 20.6% to $419M ($8.18 diluted EPS) despite higher revenue, driven by $76M of restructuring/exit costs, Elo integration expense, higher share-based comp, and a tax-rate jump to 25.2% from 16.9% on 2025 U.S. tax legislation. The GAAP decline overstates operational deterioration: Q1 2026 non-GAAP operating margin was 21.9%, up 90bps, and the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is wide because of amortization, SBC, and restructuring add-backs. The 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance implies the recovery is intact at the operating level.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $917M and FCF $831M (15.4% margin), strong generation though down from $954M in FY24. Cash fell from $901M (FY24) to $114M (Q1 26), a deliberate deployment into Elo and buybacks rather than a liquidity problem given $1.06B of revolver availability. Total debt rose to $2.66B with D/E ~1.37x. The Term Loan A and the revolver both mature May 2027, a refinancing event at ~5% current rates; Senior Notes (6.5%) are due 2032.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.93 (grey zone) and current ratio of 0.97 look weak in isolation but are artifacts of a goodwill-heavy balance sheet and a minimized cash position, not operational distress. FCF comfortably covers debt service. A standardized distress screen would mis-flag this; the genuine concern is the 2027 maturity wall, not insolvency.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (Q1 26 +9.6%, Q4 25 –2.0%, Q3 25 +1.4%, Q2 25 +6.5%). The pattern suggests conservative-to-modest guidance with reliable delivery, supporting confidence in the raised 2026 guide.

**A hidden option.** Zebra paid ~$75M in IEEPA tariffs that the Supreme Court invalidated in February 2026. It is pursuing refunds but has recognized no benefit, an unrecognized upside not in current numbers, worth roughly $1.55/share pre-tax if realized.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on cyclical recovery plus two structural extensions. On the cycle, the 2023 downturn ended as customer destocking cleared and short-cycle demand inflected; Q1's broad-based regional growth and rising estimates corroborate this. Consensus CY EPS rose +3.2% over 90 days and NY EPS +2.6% over 90 days (+3.3% over 30 days), with the current quarter revised +4.0% in 30 days. These are positive but not the >30% revision velocity that would flag a forced re-rating; this is steady cyclical improvement, not a violent earnings recovery.

On structural extension, Elo (self-service kiosks/POS) and Photoneo (3D machine vision) widen the addressable market beyond traditional AIDC into customer-facing automation and industrial vision. The recurring software/supplies/services mix continues to grow as a share of revenue, which over time should reduce cyclical amplitude. Embedded AI agents within CF are management's response to the substitution concern, attempting to reposition Zebra as a frontline-workflow software platform rather than a scanner vendor.

The growth durability is moderate. The AIDC market is recovering but tied to retail and logistics IT capex, where budget visibility is limited, and the 21% revenue collapse in 2023 demonstrates how sharp the cycle can be. We model mid-to-high single-digit organic growth in a normal year, with operating leverage providing EPS growth in the low double digits if margins cooperate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $237 against guided 2026 non-GAAP EPS, the forward multiple is ~12.7–13.0x. On consensus CY EPS of $18.54 and NY EPS of $20.71, the figures are ~12.8x and ~11.5x. Against the electronic-equipment peer median P/E of 31.6x (Trimble 26.1x, Teledyne 31.6x, Roper 20.8x, Keysight 54.7x), Zebra trades at roughly 40% of the peer multiple. Analyst price targets span $267 (low) to $400 (high), with a mean of $329 and median $335, all materially above the current price.

The discount is wide enough to warrant a constructive valuation score, but several caveats temper it. First, ZBRA carries more cyclicality and lower secular software content than Roper or Keysight, so a full peer multiple is not the right anchor; a 16–20x forward multiple is more defensible for the business quality. Second, the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is large: on trailing GAAP EPS of $8.18, the stock trades at ~29x, and a buyer should be honest that the cheap multiple depends entirely on non-GAAP earnings power being the right denominator. Third, the analyst targets reflect a stock that has already disappointed; sentiment is degrading and the targets are stale, lagging the memory-cost concern that drove the post-earnings drawdown.

Cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and Zebra's persistent discount has lasted long enough (the stock is roughly flat YTD while lagging the market) that the market is demanding a catalyst before re-rating. The KeyBanc thesis — multiple expansion toward I-Tech peers on inflecting demand and improving cost execution, $305 PT — is the bull articulation. The valuation is favorable on a 12-month horizon if margins hold, which is the unresolved question. We score valuation a constructive 6.5: cheap relative to growth, but the discount is partly earned by the memory-cost and AI-substitution overhangs rather than purely a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Zebra holds a durable lead in AIDC against Honeywell, Datalogic, SATO, and regional competitors. Morningstar assigns an economic moat rooted in the installed base, channel network, and enterprise switching costs. The competitive trajectory is mixed. The recurring software/services mix is growing, which strengthens the moat, and Elo/Photoneo extend reach into adjacencies. But two trajectory concerns weigh on the score.

The first is margin trajectory within CF, which compressed 200bps in FY25 as Elo and lower-margin software diluted the segment; the richer AVA segment is holding up better. The second, and more important, is the structural AI-substitution debate: the concern that AI/automation could erode demand for dedicated handheld scanners and mobile computers over a multi-year horizon. This is not a near-term revenue threat, since enterprise deployments are sticky and the installed base is large, but it is a real question about terminal demand that the market is discounting, and it is why a hardware-heavy AIDC franchise does not command a software multiple. Management's pivot toward embedded AI agents and away from robotics hardware is the right strategic response, but it is unproven.

We score competitive position 5.5: a market leader with a defensible moat but a flat-to-slightly-deteriorating trajectory given CF margin compression and the unresolved substitution overhang.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory-cost margin pressure (near-term, highest weight).** Analysts flagged DRAM/NAND cost inflation as the key headwind for coming quarters, and this drove the ~12% pullback in the month after the May beat. Memory is a meaningful input to mobile computers and scanners; rising costs directly compress gross margin unless offset by price or mix. This is the single most important variable for the next few quarters and is not yet resolved in reported numbers.

**2027 debt maturity wall.** Refinancing the Term Loan A and revolver at ~5% rates against the company's debt-funded buyback program elevates sensitivity. This is a known event, and the refinancing cost is a live variable.

**AI-substitution structural risk.** The terminal-demand question for dedicated scanning hardware is genuine and underpins the depressed multiple. It is slow-moving and unlikely to surface in trailing data, which makes it the kind of risk that can erode a thesis quietly.

**Acquisition/goodwill risk.** The 10-K explicitly flags impairment risk if results disappoint, and Elo purchase accounting remains preliminary. A cyclical relapse plus memory-cost margin damage could trigger an impairment.

**Cyclicality and customer-capex exposure.** Demand is tied to retail/logistics IT capex with limited budget visibility. The 2023 –21% revenue shock is the cautionary precedent.

**FX.** Euro, GBP, and Czech Koruna exposure cuts both ways quarter to quarter.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. One-week ATM IV sits at 91.2% against 42.6% historical vol, a 48.6pp spread, in the 86th percentile of the current chain, with steep backwardation (91.2% near-term vs. 44.4% six-month). This pattern signals the market expects an imminent catalyst or event-driven move; the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.7%. ATM IV has fallen sharply since the prior snapshot (129.6% → 91.2%), so some uncertainty has resolved, but the elevated near-term level remains. The put/call volume ratio of 3.58 looks bearish, but the put/call OI ratio of 0.34 is call-heavy, and the one-week skew of +10.1% is call-skewed: mixed positioning rather than a clean directional signal. Short interest at 10.1% of float (3.3 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional bearishness, though not at squeeze-risk levels. The market is hedging an imminent move with no clear directional consensus, which counsels against deploying new capital ahead of the catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($305–$335)

Short-cycle demand continues to inflect, organic growth re-accelerates toward high single digits, and the 2025 Productivity Plan (~$35M annualized savings) plus memory-cost normalization protect gross margin. Non-GAAP EPS lands at or above the $18.70 high end of guidance and consensus revises 2027 toward $21–22. The multiple re-rates from ~12.8x toward 16–18x as the cyclical recovery proves durable and the AI-substitution fear recedes on traction in embedded AI agents. The ~$75M tariff refund is realized as a one-time gain. At 16x $20.71 NY EPS, the stock is ~$331; KeyBanc's $305 PT sits within this range. The buyback (new $1B authorization, ~$300M in Q1 alone) shrinks the share count and amplifies per-share gains.

### Bear Case ($175–$195)

Memory-cost inflation compresses gross margin by 150–250bps over the next several quarters, and a soft retail/logistics capex environment caps organic growth in the low single digits. EPS lands below guidance, estimates roll over, and the multiple stays at or below 11x on impaired forward earnings. The 2027 refinancing prices at a higher rate, raising interest expense and constraining buybacks. A cyclical relapse plus margin damage forces a goodwill impairment review. At 10x a cut $18 EPS, the stock is ~$180, roughly 24% below current.

The probability-weighted center of gravity sits closer to the bull case given the cheap entry multiple and positive revisions, but the bear case is live enough, and the near-term options stress acute enough, to hold rather than buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings release (the memory-cost margin read is the swing variable), and the May 2027 refinancing as it comes into view.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 gross margin holds at or above ~48% despite memory-cost headwinds, demonstrating pricing/mix offset — the single most important confirmation.
- Organic revenue growth sustains above ~5% for two consecutive quarters with continued positive estimate revisions.
- Refinancing of the 2027 maturities completed at a manageable rate (within ~50bps of current cost), removing the balance-sheet overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin compresses below ~46% on memory costs with no offset path, or management cuts 2026 guidance below ~$18.00.
- Organic growth turns negative, signaling a renewed capex downturn.
- A goodwill impairment charge or material deterioration in the refinancing outlook.

**Reassessment point:** Next quarterly earnings, with the memory-cost margin trajectory as the decisive data point.

## Conclusion

Zebra is a quality AIDC franchise trading at ~12.8x forward non-GAAP guidance after a ~33% drawdown, with a cyclical recovery underway, positive estimate revisions, and a reliable beat-and-raise track record. The valuation is attractive relative to the company's growth and to peers, and the score trajectory's prior declines (growth and management quality marked down through early 2026) now appear to have over-discounted a business that delivered a 14% revenue quarter and raised guidance; the Q1 beat is a modest positive reversal of that trend, which is why we hold rather than continue lowering.

But the discount is partly earned. Memory-cost margin pressure is unresolved and is the proximate driver of the recent pullback; the 2027 debt wall is a real refinancing event; the AI-substitution debate is a structural question that keeps a hardware-heavy AIDC name from earning a software multiple; and the options market is pricing acute near-term stress. The asymmetry favors the upside on a 12-month view, but not with enough conviction or clear-enough catalyst resolution to deploy new capital today. Existing holders should retain for the recovery and buyback support; new money should wait for margin confirmation in the next one or two prints before stepping up. We rate ZBRA HOLD with an overall score of 5.95.
