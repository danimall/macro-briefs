**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Capri Holdings is a two-brand accessible-luxury company following the December 2, 2025 sale of Versace to Prada for ~$1.395B gross. The proceeds transformed a stretched balance sheet: total debt fell from $1.49B in March 2025 to ~$234M by December 2025, with ~$1.28B of undrawn revolver remaining. The investment question has narrowed to whether management can stabilize and re-grow Michael Kors, which accounts for ~82% of revenue and is the source of the company's multi-year decline.

The data shows a genuine, early profitability inflection that the prior four AVOID ratings did not fully capture. Q3 FY26 continuing-operations operating margin nearly doubled to 4.5% from 2.4%, Jimmy Choo turned profitable, and Q4 FY26 adjusted EPS of $0.22 beat the $0.11 consensus on a ~5pt gross-margin jump to 64.8%. Consensus estimates are rising sharply: current-year EPS revised +14.4% over 90 days, current-quarter +23.5% over 90 days. Management has guided FY27 to ~$2.15 EPS on ~$3.525B revenue with ~200bps of gross-margin expansion. Against a ~$19.5 stock, that guide implies roughly 9x forward earnings. CEO John Idol bought 55,000 shares on the open market at $17.98 in March 2026, near the lows, the strongest insider signal available.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY for several reasons: the underlying continuing business is still contracting (9-month FY26 revenue −4.2%, Michael Kors −4.5%), the FY27 EPS recovery leans heavily on margin expansion and a $65M tariff refund of uncertain timing rather than demand recovery, management itself called Michael Kors footwear "the biggest issue inside the company," and the brand-revival thesis is unproven. The setup has improved enough to recognize the trajectory: financial health is materially de-risked and estimates are inflecting. But a Consumer Discretionary turnaround built on margin arithmetic over a shrinking top line does not yet clear the bar for a positive sector-relative call.

## Company Overview

Capri Holdings is a global fashion-luxury group incorporated in the British Virgin Islands and headquartered in London. After the Versace divestiture it operates two reportable segments:

- **Michael Kors (~82% of revenue):** An accessible-luxury accessories, ready-to-wear, and footwear house with three lines (Collection, MICHAEL Michael Kors, Mens) sold across 100+ countries via retail, e-commerce, department stores, and licensing. Years of over-distribution and over-promotion diluted the brand's premium positioning, the structural issue the failed Tapestry merger was meant to solve and which Capri must now address standalone.
- **Jimmy Choo (~18% of revenue):** A luxury footwear-led brand under creative director Sandra Choi, complemented by handbags, small leather goods, and licensed fragrance/eyewear.

The fleet stood at 908 retail stores as of December 2025, down from 971 a year earlier, reflecting ongoing optimization. E-commerce is ~21% of revenue and roughly 47% of revenue is generated outside the U.S. Michael Kors competes directly with Coach (Tapestry) and Kate Spade (Tapestry) in the crowded accessible-luxury tier, a competitive overlap the FTC explicitly cited when blocking Tapestry's $8.5B acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in multi-year structural decline.** Continuing operations stepped down from ~$5.62B (FY2023) to ~$5.17B (FY2024) to ~$4.44B (FY2025) to ~$3.47B (FY2026). Part of that reflects the Versace reclassification, but the continuing business is also shrinking organically: 9-month FY26 revenue fell 4.2% (−5.9% constant currency), with Michael Kors −4.5% and Jimmy Choo −2.5%.

**Profitability has swung from large losses toward stabilization.** Net income went from +$616M (FY2023) to −$229M (FY2024) to −$1.18B (FY2025, driven by a ~$524M deferred-tax valuation allowance plus ~$650M of impairments) to +$137M (FY2026, including a +$58M discontinued-ops/Versace gain). The relevant signal is segment-level: 9-month FY26 Michael Kors operating margin was 11.5% ($255M), Jimmy Choo was roughly breakeven (−$2M), with ~$167M of corporate drag. In the holiday quarter (Q3 FY26), continuing-ops net income jumped to $57M ($0.47 EPS) from $6M, and Jimmy Choo turned positive (+$3M). Q4 FY26 GAAP was roughly breakeven (−$0.04).

**The balance sheet was dramatically de-risked.** Versace proceeds repaid the 2025 Term Loans entirely and cut the revolver to $221M, leaving ~$234M total debt against ~$1.28B of undrawn revolver and compliance with the 4.0x net-leverage covenant. Cash was $154M, working capital $148M.

**The equity wipeout is the headline trap.** Total equity fell to just $108M (December 2025) from $1.6B a year earlier, the result of cumulative impairments, the FY25 valuation allowance, and a ~$432M YTD negative AOCI swing from FX/net-investment-hedge losses. This near-zero book equity drives an Altman Z-Score around 1.2 ("distress"), but that reading is misleading: the distress comes almost entirely from the denuded equity line, not from liquidity or leverage. With only ~$234M of debt, positive operating cash flow ($258M from continuing ops over 9M FY26), and a large undrawn revolver, this is a turnaround/value situation, not an imminent-bankruptcy one. A standardized screen would flag the Z-Score and miss the genuinely de-risked capital structure. Equity that thin is itself a vulnerability: any fresh impairment or adverse FX swing could push book equity negative and trigger covenant scrutiny.

**Cash flow is thin but positive.** Operating cash flow of $258M (down from $441M, on AR/AP/tax timing) against ~$45M of 9M capex (full-year guide $100–125M) leaves modest positive FCF. A new $1.0B three-year buyback was authorized November 4, 2025, to begin in FY2027, with repurchases reportedly starting in Q4. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated between a declining top line and a recovering earnings line, and which one dominates determines the thesis.

On the bear side of growth, revenue is still contracting. Management attributes the Michael Kors weakness to "strategic initiatives that did not perform as expected," and the CEO singled out MK footwear as "the biggest issue inside the company," now undergoing a strategic repositioning toward casual styles. Outlet-channel recovery is pushed to late calendar 2026. FY27 guidance itself assumes continued (modest) sales declines early in the year. Jimmy Choo is guided to ~$625M in FY27 (a return to growth) with a long-term target of $800M at low-double-digit margins, but that is a multi-year aspiration off a small, breakeven base.

On the bull side, the earnings inflection is real and the estimate revisions are unusually strong. Current-quarter consensus EPS rose +25.9% over 30 days and +23.5% over 90 days; current-year EPS is up +14.4%/+13.8%; next-year +11.4%/+11.9%. The 30-day moves exceed the 90-day moves on the near-term lines, indicating accelerating positive revision velocity. Management's FY27 guide against an FY26 base that included sub-$1 trailing earnings implies a sharp recovery. The earnings-surprise record reinforces some confidence — three beats in four quarters, including a 93% Q4 beat — though the −122% Q2 FY26 miss (quarter ended September 2025) shows the line item is volatile.

The critical caveat: this EPS recovery is margin-led and tariff-refund-assisted, not demand-led. The ~200bps gross-margin guide and the $65M IEEPA refund timing carry the FY27 number more than any return to unit or revenue growth. Margin-driven recovery on a shrinking top line is lower-quality and lower-durability than volume-driven growth, which is why I weight growth prospects below the midpoint despite the strong revision velocity. Growth_prospects is the most reliable winner-selection dimension in my framework, and here it sends a mixed signal — rising estimates against falling revenue — that does not resolve cleanly to a high score.

## Valuation Assessment

CPRI trades at $19.5 against management's FY27 EPS guide of ~$2.15, roughly 9x forward earnings, and against consensus current-year EPS of $2.14 (also ~9x). That is well below the peer median P/E of 16.7 (Carter's), with Columbia at 21.2 and G-III at 12.5. On a forward basis the stock screens cheap even adjusting for the lower quality of a margin-led recovery.

The valuation case has support from multiple angles. The trailing P/E of ~31x is meaningless: it reflects trough/transition earnings, the kind of cyclical-trough distortion where forward and normalized metrics govern. Analyst targets bracket wide: low $20, median $24, mean $25.7, high $45.5; the median implies ~23% upside and the mean ~32%. Morningstar carries a $66.70 fair value, a single third-party data point implying the shares are deeply undervalued if the brand recovers. The reference transaction, Tapestry's terminated $57/share all-cash bid, sits ~193% above the current price and underscores how much strategic value the market once ascribed to these assets.

I assign a 5.5 rather than a higher score for two reasons grounded in my own scoring discipline. First, cheapness has been a reliable value-trap warning rather than a buy signal; a low multiple alone is not a thesis, and here the discount partly reflects genuine structural impairment in Michael Kors plus near-zero book equity. The catalyst that would convert cheapness to upside — proof the FY27 margin guide holds and the top line stabilizes — is unproven. Second, Consumer Discretionary is among the worst alpha sectors in my framework, so a cheap multiple in this sector requires a higher bar of company-specific evidence than I can yet confirm. The valuation is the strongest single leg of the bull case, but it is a value-realization-pending situation, not a clean mispricing with an identified near-term trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

Michael Kors competes in accessible-luxury accessories against Coach (Tapestry) and Kate Spade (Tapestry). The most damning competitive fact is the direct comparison: Tapestry executed a far more successful Coach turnaround over the same period, which establishes that Capri's problems are substantially brand-specific (over-distribution, stale product, footwear missteps) rather than purely sector-wide. The FTC's blocking of the Tapestry deal on "accessible luxury handbags" overlap grounds confirms how directly these brands compete and that Coach is winning the comparison.

The brand's premium positioning was diluted by years of over-promotion, and the repositioning of footwear is early and unproven. Jimmy Choo only just turned breakeven-to-profitable; its $800M long-term revenue target is aspirational against soft Asia/China luxury demand.

The competitive trajectory is the weakest dimension here. Capri is losing share within its tier while a direct competitor demonstrates the turnaround is achievable, which sharpens the question of whether the failure is fixable or structural. The prior report's competitive-position downgrade (4.0 → 3.0) reflected this, and I keep it near the bottom at 3.5, a marginal uptick only because Jimmy Choo's stabilization and the de-distribution discipline (store closures, fewer promotions) are early steps in the right direction, even as Michael Kors share continues to erode.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is dense and several items are binary:

1. **Michael Kors revival execution** is the central risk. The turnaround is unproven, footwear is broken, and outlet recovery is pushed to late 2026. If FY27 sales decline more than guided, the margin-led EPS recovery loses its base.

2. **Tariffs cut both ways and are unusually fluid.** Tariffs compressed MK gross margin ~290bps in Q3 FY26. The $65M IEEPA refund receivable was booked after the Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unlawful (Feb 2026), but recovery timing is uncertain and the President replaced them with Section 122 tariffs (150 days, effective Feb 24, 2026). The FY27 margin guide is sensitive to which tariff regime prevails.

3. **Securities class-action litigation** tied to the terminated Tapestry merger names CEO Idol and former CFO Edwards. The amended complaint was dismissed without prejudice March 31, 2026; plaintiffs refiled April 30, 2026. Pending and unquantified.

4. **Full valuation allowance on deferred tax assets** signals management's own caution on near-term U.S. profitability and produces a volatile effective tax rate.

5. **July 2027 debt maturity:** the entire 2025 Credit Facility matures then. Leverage is low, but refinancing risk exists if markets tighten.

6. **$5.86B notional cross-currency hedges, now an $891M derivative liability,** with Credit Support Annexes effective June 2030 that could require cash collateral posting on adverse FX moves. A buried future liquidity item worth monitoring if the USD weakens.

7. **Wholesale channel deterioration,** including a wholesale customer Chapter 11 filing that drove an incremental credit-loss allowance.

Several of these (litigation, tariff regime, tax allowance) are binary and not cleanly priced, which keeps risk_factors below the midpoint. In the current regime risk-scoring has been weakly anti-predictive, so I treat this as context rather than a heavy driver, but the concentration of binary items here is genuine.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm-to-mildly-constructive, which partly contradicts the headline risk list. Near-term ATM IV of 49.4% sits essentially in line with 1-year HV of 48.7% (0.7pp spread, fair), and IV has collapsed from 79.2% to 49.4% since late May, indicating the post-earnings uncertainty has resolved. The put/call skew is −3.0% (call-heavy), put/call volume is 0.31 and OI 0.23; the positioning leans bullish, not defensive. The term structure is in contango (1m 49.4% < 6m 57.8%), a normal/complacent shape with no near-term event premium. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.4%. Short interest is moderate at 9.4% of float with 2.6 days to cover, not a crowded short. The options market is not pricing the binary risks aggressively, consistent with a market that views the de-risked balance sheet as removing the tail and is waiting on turnaround proof.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28–45)

Versace proceeds eliminated the balance-sheet risk, leaving a ~$234M-debt company with a $1.28B revolver and positive cash flow. The Q3/Q4 FY26 margin inflection is real and estimate revisions are accelerating. If management delivers FY27 EPS near the ~$2.15 guide and the top line stabilizes by late calendar 2026 as outlet recovery and footwear repositioning take hold, a re-rating from ~9x toward the peer ~16.7x on $2.15 EPS supports the mid-$30s. The CEO's ~$989K open-market buy signals conviction at the lows, the $1B buyback retires shares against a $2.2B cap, and the strategic-value backstop (Tapestry bid $57/share) plus Morningstar's $66.70 fair value mark the long-run upside. Bull target $28–45.

### Bear Case ($13–15)

The margin-led recovery proves fragile because it rests on a $65M tariff refund of uncertain timing and a tariff regime (Section 122) that could re-pressure MK gross margin. Michael Kors revenue continues declining beyond guidance, footwear repositioning fails, and Jimmy Choo stalls against soft Asia demand. Near-zero book equity ($108M) leaves no cushion against a fresh impairment or adverse FX swing that could push equity negative and complicate the July 2027 refinancing. The securities litigation produces a material settlement. The stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $20 and below, to ~$13–15, roughly 25–35% downside from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For this HOLD (rationale: uncertain):**

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Two consecutive quarters of Michael Kors revenue stabilization or growth (constant-currency decline narrowing to flat-or-better), confirming the turnaround is demand-led, not solely margin-led.
- FY27 gross-margin expansion tracking to the ~200bps guide with the $65M tariff refund collected, validating the EPS path independent of one-time items.
- Continued positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year EPS holding above $2.10) combined with execution of the announced buyback at sub-$22 prices.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- FY27 revenue declines materially worse than the modest guided dip, indicating the brand erosion is structural rather than cyclical.
- A fresh impairment or adverse FX swing pushing book equity negative, or any covenant-related disclosure ahead of the July 2027 maturity.
- A material securities-litigation settlement or adverse ruling, or a reversal in the tariff-refund collection.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 print (late summer 2026), which will be the first read on whether the FY27 guide is tracking and whether outlet/footwear recovery is materializing on the late-2026 schedule management laid out.

## Conclusion

Capri has moved off the worst of its distress: the Versace sale removed the balance-sheet tail, the Q3/Q4 FY26 results show a genuine margin inflection, estimates are rising fast, and the CEO put nearly $1M of his own money in near the lows. Those facts justify lifting the rating from the four prior AVOIDs to HOLD and tracking the company more constructively than the Altman Z-Score would suggest.

What keeps this short of a BUY is the quality of the recovery. The FY27 EPS guide leans on gross-margin expansion and tariff-refund timing rather than on a return to Michael Kors top-line growth. Revenue is still falling, the CEO calls footwear the company's biggest problem, Tapestry's success with Coach shows the failure is brand-specific and not merely sector-wide, and book equity is thin enough to offer no cushion. In a sector where company-specific outperformance evidence sets a high bar and cheap multiples have repeatedly proven to be value traps, ~9x forward earnings is a reasonable price for an unproven turnaround but not a confident one. The risk/reward is roughly balanced: meaningful upside if execution lands, 25–35% downside if it doesn't, and the honest classification is an uncertain hold pending the next two quarters of demand data.

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain). Overall score 4.7/10.**
