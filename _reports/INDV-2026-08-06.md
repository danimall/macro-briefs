## Executive Summary

Indivior enters this review as a fundamentally different security than it was two weeks ago. On August 1, 2026 the company signed a definitive all-stock merger with Supernus Pharmaceuticals. Indivior is the legal acquirer and its stockholders will own approximately 56.5% of the combined entity, but the surviving company will be named Supernus, Inc., trade as SUPN, be headquartered in Rockville, Maryland, and be run by Supernus's CEO Jack Khattar and CFO Timothy Dec. Indivior stockholders separately receive a $1.0 billion special cash dividend, roughly $8.47 per share on the current count, or about 21.6% of the current price. Any analysis that treats INDV as a standalone operating equity will misprice it.

The underlying operating story is strong and has improved since our last review. First-half 2026 net revenue was $660M, up 16.2%; stripping out revenue from performance obligations satisfied in prior periods on both sides ($64M in H1 2026, $45M in H1 2025) still leaves +14.0%, a volume-led result, in contrast to FY2025 where the entire reported 4.3% increase was accounted for by a $59M year-over-year swing in favorable gross-to-net true-ups. SUBLOCADE US revenue grew 27% in H1 on 19% dispense-volume growth and roughly 31,800 record new patient starts in Q1. Operating margin expanded to 45.0% from 24.3% as SG&A fell 23% and R&D 42% in Q2. Management guided FY2026 revenue *down* on February 26, then raised the range to $1,215–1,285M and adjusted EBITDA to $620–660M on April 30. Four consecutive quarters have beaten consensus by 19% to 92%, and current-year EPS estimates are up 16.9% in 30 days.

We are nonetheless downgrading to HOLD, and the reason is the structure of the transaction rather than the ~3% price decline since our July report. At parity multiples the deal transfers value away from Indivior holders: Indivior supplies roughly $640M of the $763M pre-synergy combined adjusted EBITDA (83.9%) and receives 56.5% of the equity plus $1.0B of cash, which is less than the equity value forgone at an identical multiple. The market agreed in the first two sessions: INDV fell from $40.01 to $36.50, a two-day decline of 8.8% on 6.7M and 7.2M shares, before recovering to $39.18. The arbitrage spread is now effectively closed: 1.5401 × ($39.18 − $8.47 dividend) = $47.30 against SUPN's quoted $47.43, a 0.3% gap that implies the market assigns high closing probability and leaves no residual mispricing. Against a base case of roughly $41 and a bull case near $47, an unquantified dental mass tort naming approximately 26,307 plaintiffs, with bellwether trials not expected before the end of Q1 2028, the primary carrier reserving rights on liability costs, and negative book equity of −$208M providing no cushion, is the dominant reason we will not pay up for a modest expected return.

## Company Overview

Indivior is the market leader in long-acting injectable medications for opioid use disorder. Demerged from Reckitt Benckiser in 2014, it operated as UK-listed Indivior PLC until completing redomiciliation as a Delaware corporation on January 26, 2026. It reports as a single segment from Richmond, Virginia.

The portfolio has narrowed to one asset that matters. **SUBLOCADE**, a monthly buprenorphine depot injection, was 69% of FY2025 net revenue and approximately 74% of Q2 2026 revenue, with more than 500,000 US patients prescribed since its 2018 launch. **SUBOXONE Film**, the legacy franchise, has seen US category share fall from above 50% in 2018 to a 14.2% average in 2025 against four generics, and Indivior is contractually prohibited from promoting it through 2032 under the 2020 DOJ Resolution Agreement and state settlement orders. **PERSERIS** promotion ended in 2024 ($24M FY2025, $10M H1 2026). **OPVEE** sales and marketing were discontinued in Q3 2025; the FY2025 10-K states the company "failed to realize the expected benefits" from the $146M Opiant acquisition. The United States was 85% of revenue in both 2025 and 2024, and the "Indivior Action Agenda" is exiting the UK, Ireland, Sweden, Israel, Finland and Italy.

The Q2 2026 10-Q contains the sentence that explains the merger: "The Company is not currently pursuing any pipeline activities." INDV-2000 (rocavorexant) failed its Phase 2 primary endpoint; INDV-6001 will not advance to Phase 3, with development and ex-US rights returned to Alar Pharmaceuticals and Indivior retaining only a contingent US commercial right conditional on Alar obtaining FDA approval.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality has inverted in the company's favor.** FY2025 reported net revenue of $1,239M (+4.3%) included $87M from performance obligations satisfied in prior periods versus $28M in FY2024 and negative $9M in FY2023. On a current-period basis revenue was $1,152M against $1,160M, a 0.7% decline. Management's own MD&A corroborates the mechanism: of the 13% US SUBLOCADE growth in 2025, seven points were volume and six were gross-to-net adjustments. That is why February guidance called for a revenue decline. Applying the identical adjustment to H1 2026 ($660M − $64M = $596M vs $568M − $45M = $523M) gives +14.0%, only 2.2 points below the headline. The distortion has largely washed out and the growth is now volume-driven.

**Cash flow is highly seasonal and must not be annualized from any single quarter.** FY2025 operating cash flow was approximately negative $27M against $210M of net income, a figure that looks alarming and is explainable: $208M of higher litigation settlement payments including a discretionary ~$295M prepayment in November 2025 that completed the entire $600M DOJ obligation. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $9M; H1 2026 was positive $220M, implying roughly +$229M in Q2. The driver is rebate settlement timing: accrued rebates and product returns stood at $610M at June 30, with H1 gross accruals of $814M against $721M of payments and credits.

**The balance sheet screens as distressed and largely is not.** Stockholders' deficit widened from −$98M to −$208M in H1 2026 and the Altman Z-Score sits near 1.49. Approximately $1,295M of the accumulated deficit was recognized at the 2014 Reckitt demerger as a share-capital-versus-net-investment difference, not accumulated losses. The H1 widening is explained by $302M of buybacks plus ~$45M of equity-award tax settlement against +$211M of net income: equity fell because a profitable company repurchased stock. Negative working capital of $142M at June 30 is a structural feature of a Medicaid-heavy gross-to-net model with a $610M rebate accrual and no offsetting current asset. The equity-based terms of the Z-Score are therefore misleading here; the current-ratio and liquidity observations remain informative.

**Debt is comfortable and was refinanced well.** In March 2026 Indivior repaid its entire $333M Note Purchase Agreement (SOFR+5.5%, secured on substantially all assets), taking an $18M extinguishment loss, funded by $500M of 0.625% convertible senior notes due March 2031 (effective rate 1.17%, carrying value $487M). Net debt at June 30 was approximately $251M ($500M principal less $222M cash and $27M long-term investments). On dilution, precision matters: the conversion price is $41.66, maximum shares issuable including make-whole is ~16.2M, but that is maximum contingent dilution; the notes use combination settlement with a cash election, they were not convertible at June 30, and they contributed zero incremental dilutive shares. At $39.18 the stock sits 6.0% below the conversion price. The notes' fair value was $607M against $500M principal, so the market does assign option value.

**Tax normalization matters for year-over-year comparisons.** FY2025's 12.2% effective rate was flattered by roughly $45M of one-time benefit from an intragroup royalty payment; the go-forward rate has normalized to 23–24%. The deferred tax asset fell from $323M to $271M in H1, a $52M deferred expense including a UK NOL write-off. Practically, FY2025's $1.64 GAAP EPS embedded a benefit that will not repeat; H1 2026's $1.67, struck at a normal rate, is the cleaner comparison.

**A note on the reported multiple.** The data feed shows trailing and forward P/E both at 13.99, which implies EPS of $2.80, inconsistent with both FY2025 diluted EPS of $1.64 (23.9x) and trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS of roughly $3.48 (11.3x). We use our own arithmetic throughout.

**Customer concentration is severe and flat.** FY2025: four customers at 20%, 18%, 13% and 13%, 64% combined, against 66% in FY2024. The top three wholesalers were 51% of global net revenue versus 55% prior. Product concentration is worsening (SUBLOCADE 58% → 64% → 69% → ~74%) while customer concentration is unchanged, so no diversification narrative is supportable on a standalone basis.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term trajectory is the best it has been since the demerger, and the estimate revision pattern reflects it: current-quarter consensus up 18.5% in 30 days, current year up 16.9% in 30 days and 13.8% in 90 days, next year up 13.1% in 90 days. Four straight beats averaging well above 40%. The direct-to-consumer campaign launched in October 2025, with advertising rising from $68M to $124M in FY2025, is the most visible candidate driver of the volume inflection, though it cannot be isolated from the concurrent commercial-model changes.

Two qualifications temper this. First, the guidance shape implies deceleration: the $1,250M midpoint less H1's $660M leaves approximately $590M for H2, a 10.6% sequential decline, with a range of $555–625M. Either February's badly-wrong-in-the-other-direction guide made management deliberately conservative, or it expects Film and rest-of-world erosion to steepen; the 10-Q's own language says the RoW downtrend "is expected to continue." We cannot resolve which, and the companies cancelled their separate Q2 earnings calls in favor of a joint transaction call on August 3, so there is no standalone management Q&A on the underlying franchise. That is a real information gap.

Second, growth is concentrated in two products against a substantial declining tail, and this remains true post-merger. Supernus contributes Qelbree at +26% ($304.7M FY2025) and ONAPGO ramping from a $17.3M partial year, against Oxtellar XR −59%, APOKYN −35% and Trokendi XR −33% on generic entry. Supernus's pipeline is real but early and unproven: SPN-817 in a ~258-patient Phase 2b for treatment-resistant focal seizures, SPN-820 in a follow-on ~200-patient Phase 2b in MDD after its February 2025 Phase 2b TRD failure, SPN-443 entering SAD/MAD in H2 2026.

The company-stated pro forma figures ($2.2B revenue, $888M adjusted EBITDA including $125M of annual cost synergies, ~$878M net debt, sub-1x leverage) are forward-looking non-GAAP estimates from the August 3 joint release, not filing facts. The net debt figure explicitly excludes transaction costs, financing fees and costs to achieve synergies; Supernus spent $72.9M achieving the far smaller Sage acquisition, so real post-close net debt will likely exceed $878M.

## Valuation Assessment

Standalone arithmetic at $39.18: equity $4.62B, plus $500M debt, less $249M cash and investments, gives EV of $4.87B against the $640M guided adjusted EBITDA midpoint, **7.6x**. Current-year consensus EPS of $4.47 (one analyst) puts the stock at 8.8x; the five-analyst next-year figure of $4.32 is 9.1x. Both are cheap in isolation.

But the next-year consensus cannot be a post-merger estimate. With share count nearly doubling and $1.0B of cash paid out, combined adjusted EPS on ~209M shares would run closer to $2.70–2.90 ($888M EBITDA less ~$60M cash D&A and ~$60M interest, taxed at 24%). Consensus EPS and the $46–59 analyst target range (mean $51.6) are therefore standalone-basis and stale relative to the announced structure. We will not treat them as independent confirmation.

The correct frame is the stub plus the dividend. At $39.18 with $8.47 of dividend, the stub is $30.71 per share, or $6.42B of equity across ~209M shares; adding $878M of pro forma net debt gives $7.30B of EV against $888M of synergy-inclusive EBITDA, **8.2x**, and roughly 11x combined adjusted EPS. The independent cross-check is Supernus itself: 1.5401 × $30.71 = $47.30 versus SUPN's quoted $47.43, a 0.3% spread. The market has priced this deal efficiently.

Scenario ladder, using ~209M shares, $900M of net debt (allowing modest transaction-cost drag) and the $8.47 dividend:

| Multiple on $888M | Stub | + Dividend | vs $39.18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7.0x | $25.44 | $33.91 | −13.4% |
| 8.5x (base) | $31.82 | $40.29 | +2.8% |
| 9.0x | $33.92 | $42.39 | +8.2% |
| 10.0x (bull) | $38.18 | $46.65 | +19.1% |

If synergies underdeliver and combined EBITDA lands at $800M, 8.5x gives $36.70 (−6.3%). A deal-break scenario is less punitive than it looks: standalone INDV at 8x $640M is roughly $41, so the equity is not primarily a deal-certainty bet.

Is 8.2x fair? Specialty pharma with meaningful generic erosion and litigation tails generally clears 5–9x. INDV's own standalone 7.6x is the relevant anchor, and the combined entity earns a modest premium for portfolio breadth, a pipeline and sub-1x leverage. The peer table supplied (median P/E 38.4) is distorted by APLS at 38.4x, AMRX at 44x and SUPN's negative print; the meaningful comparables are ACAD at 9.7x and OGN at 14.3x, against INDV's ~9x forward, a discount that has identifiable causes rather than being an unexplained mispricing.

Cheapness here is explained, not anomalous: single-asset dependence, zero standalone pipeline, three wholesalers at 51% of revenue, and a mass tort with no disclosed loss estimate. The merger is the catalyst intended to resolve part of that discount, and it is arithmetically multiple-dilutive at parity: Indivior is issuing equity valued at 7.6x to acquire a business at approximately 19.3x standalone, or roughly 9.6x if the entire $125M of synergies is credited to the acquired side. We score valuation 6.0: fairly priced with modest positive skew, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

SUBLOCADE's share of the long-acting injectable buprenorphine category "stabilized in the mid-70s%" per the FY2025 10-K against BRIXADI (Braeburn/Camurus). The word "stabilized" implies earlier share loss that has arrested. Third-party coverage describing this as "76% of the US opioid treatment market" is inaccurate: the figure is share of the LAI buprenorphine subset, excluding the far larger sublingual and generic buprenorphine market.

The genuine advantages are a monthly depot with favorable retention economics, entrenched positioning in the Medicaid, corrections and public-health channels that dominate OUD payment, vertical buprenorphine API integration at the retained Hull, UK plant, and a new Raleigh, NC SUBLOCADE facility ($27M of H1 capex, $10–15M more expected).

The trajectory is mixed. SUBLOCADE is improving on volume and share stability. Everything else is deteriorating: the sublingual franchise is structurally lost and legally unpromotable to 2032, PERSERIS and OPVEE are wound down, and the RoW footprint is being cut to four countries. Post-merger the portfolio broadens to nine commercial CNS products but adds three assets in steep generic decline. Channel power sits with three wholesalers, and payer concentration in Medicaid is why the $610M rebate accrual approaches half of annual revenue and why true-ups swing reported growth by four to six points in a given year.

## Risk Assessment

**Dental mass tort — the dominant unquantified exposure.** In Re Suboxone Film Products Liability Litigation (MDL 3092, N.D. Ohio) stood at approximately 1,967 cases naming ~26,307 plaintiffs as of July 24, 2026, up from over 25,000 in the 10-K, plus a Schedule A cohort reduced from ~10,000 to ~4,854, more than 950 tolled individual claims in Delaware, New Jersey and Virginia, and proposed Canadian class actions in Quebec and British Columbia. Indivior discloses **no loss estimate**. Two features aggravate it: the primary carrier is reimbursing defense costs but has issued a reservation of rights as to liability costs, and the 10-K warns product liability insurance for opioid products may be unavailable due to contractual exclusions. Bellwether trials are not expected before the end of Q1 2028. The defensive fact worth weighing is that the claims follow a June 2022 FDA-required dental label revision applied to all manufacturers of buprenorphine products dissolved in the mouth. With stockholders' equity at −$208M, there is no book cushion.

**Other unquantified legal matters.** A UK FSMA multiparty action alleging misleading statements including in the 2014 Demerger Prospectus now has a split trial directed: liability trial in Q4 2028 running eight weeks, judgment not expected until H1 2029, quantum trial late 2030 at the earliest. The *Miller* qui tam (W.D. Va.) has discovery stayed. Opioid MDL residual accrual declined from $80M to $56M after $26M of payments, with West Virginia NAS matters on appeal. Creditably resolved: the December 2024 securities class action was dismissed in Q3 2025, the $600M DOJ obligation is fully paid, and the antitrust MDL is settled and paid. FTC and 41-state compliance obligations run to November 2030.

**Merger-specific risks, newly added to the risk factors.** The 1.5401 exchange ratio is fixed and will not adjust for price moves. Closing requires both stockholder bases plus regulatory approvals, targeted Q4 2026, and the S-4 joint proxy has not yet been filed, so fairness opinions, the projections underlying the ratio, and Indivior executives' and directors' personal interests in the transaction are all unknown. CEO Joe Ciaffoni holds 790,330 shares and is not named to any role in the combined company. Termination fees are symmetric at 3.77% of each side's equity value ($174M / $101M), which argues against one party holding negotiating leverage. The accounting acquirer is undetermined (the 10-Q says the treatment "is still being evaluated"), so purchase accounting, goodwill and future amortization cannot be modeled. Most consequentially, if debt financing is unavailable the $1.0B dividend converts to $500M cash at closing plus a $530M "dividend payment right" payable on the first anniversary, turning roughly $4.40 per share of consideration into an unsecured one-year receivable on a company with negative book equity.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility is 60.1% against 36.2% one-year realized, rich by 23.9 points, with backwardation to 47.1% at six months and a one-month put/call skew the data source characterizes as elevated put demand. The nearest expiry (August 21) prices a ±11.6% move. Positioning is directionally at odds: put/call volume 0.07 and open-interest 0.13 are heavily call-tilted. ATM IV has compressed from 65.6% to 61.1% since July 24 while the price fell 3.5%. Two caveats: the chain is thin (95 contracts across five expirations), which limits reliability, and a 60% near-term IV on a stock in an announced stock-for-stock merger trading at a 0.3% arb spread is more plausibly illiquidity than informed positioning. Short interest of 11.3% of float with six days to cover is consistent with merger-arbitrage hedging (arbitrageurs buy SUPN and short the acquirer INDV), which creates a technical overhang until closing and a potential source of covering demand afterward. Rich near-term optionality argues against paying for exposure here and confirms our decision to wait.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($46–47).** SUBLOCADE's volume inflection is durable: 19% H1 dispense growth, 31,800 record quarterly new patient starts, DTC spend doubled to $124M and evidently working, LAI category share stabilized in the mid-70s. H2 guidance implying a 10.6% sequential decline proves conservative, as February's guide-down did. The merger closes on schedule in Q4, the $1.0B dividend is fully debt-financed, and $125M of synergies is delivered against a $2.2B revenue base with substantial commercial-infrastructure overlap in specialty CNS. The combined entity earns a 10x multiple on $888M of EBITDA as a diversified nine-product CNS platform with sub-1x leverage and a genuine pipeline, versus 7.6x for a single-asset company with a mass-tort tail. That path delivers $46.65 including the dividend, +19.1%, and merger-arb shorts covering post-close add a technical tailwind. Analyst targets of $46–59 sit at or above this range, though on a standalone basis we do not credit them.

**Bear case ($34).** Indivior holders are handing over 83.9% of pre-synergy combined EBITDA for 56.5% of the equity plus $1.0B of cash, which is less than the equity value forgone at identical multiples, and giving up the CEO, CFO, headquarters and corporate name; the market's 8.8% two-day markdown on 14M shares was the rational reaction. H2 revenue lands toward $555M as Film and RoW erosion steepens, confirming that guidance reflected real decline rather than conservatism. Synergies underdeliver or transaction and integration costs push real post-close net debt materially above the stated $878M, which the company's own footnote concedes excludes them. The combined entity is re-rated to 7x as investors mark it as a specialty pharma with a large declining tail, giving $33.91, −13.4%. The dental MDL remains unquantified through at least Q1 2028 with liability coverage under a carrier reservation of rights and no book equity cushion, and the S-4 discloses executive interests or ratio-supporting projections that further reduce the perceived value of what Indivior holders receive.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the S-4 joint proxy filing (expected within weeks and the single most important near-term document), then both shareholder votes, the Q3 2026 report in late October or early November, and the targeted Q4 2026 close.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. S-4 disclosure that (a) confirms the $1.0B dividend is fully committed at closing with no $530M deferral, (b) shows exchange-ratio projections consistent with the guided $620–660M EBITDA, and (c) reveals no executive-interest terms that materially reduce holder consideration, combined with the stub trading at or below 7.5x pro forma EBITDA (roughly INDV at $36–37 with the dividend intact).
2. H2 2026 revenue at or above $610M, against the ~$590M implied by guidance, with SUBLOCADE dispense volume growth sustained at 15% or better; this would resolve the conservatism-versus-erosion question in favor of conservatism.
3. Any development that bounds the dental MDL: a global settlement framework, written carrier confirmation of liability coverage, or a favorable pre-trial preemption ruling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. The dividend converting to $500M cash plus a $530M one-year payment right, which shifts roughly $4.40 per share into an unsecured claim on a company with negative book equity.
2. H2 2026 revenue below $555M, or SUBLOCADE volume growth decelerating below 10%.
3. Dental MDL deterioration: an adverse pre-trial ruling, plaintiff count accelerating past roughly 35,000, or explicit carrier denial of liability coverage.
4. Deal termination without a superior alternative, leaving a zero-pipeline single-asset company with restructured commercial infrastructure.

Base case $40–42 within twelve months (2.8% to 8.2% above the $39.18 basis), bull $46.65 (+19.1%), bear $33.91 (−13.4%).

## Conclusion

Our score declines from 6.4 to 5.7 and the recommendation from BUY to HOLD. This is a response to a structural change in the security, not to price: the stock is down about 3% since our July review while Q2 results were strong, margins expanded, and estimates rose. The prior BUY rested on a cheap single-asset company with an accelerating franchise and improving cost structure. That thesis is now mediated by a fixed 1.5401 exchange ratio into a company Indivior will not name, headquarter or run, on terms that are arithmetically multiple-dilutive at parity, with the arbitrage spread already closed to 0.3% and only 3% to 8% of base-case upside on offer.

Two dimensions moved most. Valuation falls from 7.5 to 6.0 because the relevant multiple is no longer 7.6x standalone EBITDA but 8.2x on synergy-inclusive combined EBITDA, a modest premium rather than a discount, and because consensus EPS and price targets are demonstrably standalone-basis and stale. Risk factors fall to 4.5 on the newly disclosed merger risk set (the fixed ratio, the dividend financing contingency, the undetermined accounting acquirer, the unfiled S-4) layered on a dental mass tort whose plaintiff count grew to ~26,307, whose bellwethers slipped to Q1 2028, and against which the company carries no book equity and compromised liability coverage. Management quality holds at 5.5: the debt refinancing, buyback timing and pipeline discipline were well executed, while the February-to-April guidance reversal, the cancelled standalone Q2 call, Citi's dual advisory-and-financing role, and the concession of CEO, CFO, headquarters and name are not.

We would own this at $36–37 with the dividend intact, or on evidence that H2 revenue exceeds the implied $590M. At $39.18, the operating momentum is real, the price reflects it, and the largest liability on the balance sheet has no number attached to it.
