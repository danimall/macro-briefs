## Executive Summary

Roper Technologies is a high-quality serial acquirer of vertical software businesses serving defensible niches — legal, healthcare, education, insurance, government contracting, freight, and faith-based organizations. The company generates $2.4B+ in annual free cash flow on $7.9B of revenue (30.8% FCF margin), with 67.3% of revenue now classified as recurring or reoccurring. Gross margins have held at ~69% through a period of massive acquisition activity ($9B deployed over three years), demonstrating disciplined target selection.

The declining score trajectory (7.1 → 6.7 → 6.5 over three reports) reflected legitimate concerns: rising leverage, goodwill concentration at 90% of total assets, and higher interest expense. Those risks remain. Net debt has risen to ~$9.1B, and interest expense nearly doubled from $165M to $325M between 2023 and 2025. However, two developments warrant reversing the decline and upgrading back to BUY. First, Roper's $3B share repurchase program — its first ever — has already deployed $1.81B at an average price of ~$380/share, including $1.31B at ~$353/share in early 2026. When management of a company with a 20+ year track record of disciplined capital allocation shifts from pure M&A deployment to buybacks at specific price levels, that is a high-conviction signal about their view of intrinsic value. Second, Application Software segment margins continued expanding (25.8% → 26.5% → 26.8%), confirming operating leverage in the largest segment.

The specific risks that kept prior reports at HOLD — leverage and goodwill — are structural features of the serial-acquirer model. Roper's FCF covers total debt at 3.8x, the $2.6B undrawn revolver provides ample liquidity, and the nearest debt maturities ($706M in 2026, $1.55B in 2027) are easily serviceable. The goodwill risk is real but unquantifiable absent impairment triggers, and management performed qualitative (not quantitative) tests in Q4 2025 — standard practice when no indicators exist. With the stock trading near management's own buyback execution price, the risk/reward tilts favorably.

## Company Overview

Roper Technologies operates 29 businesses across three segments, organized by software delivery model rather than end market:

- **Application Software** (56.7% of revenue, $4,483M): Twelve SaaS/cloud businesses serving highly specific verticals. Aderant serves large law firms, Deltek serves government contractors, Vertafore serves P&C insurance agencies, Frontline serves K-12 school districts, and CentralReach (acquired April 2025 for $1.85B) serves autism therapy clinics. These businesses share a common characteristic: they are mission-critical systems of record where switching costs are extreme.

- **Network Software** (20.3%, $1,601M): Nine businesses operating network-effect platforms. DAT is the dominant freight-matching marketplace, ConstructConnect is the leading pre-construction bid network, and iPipeline connects life insurance carriers with distributors. This segment produces the highest margins (43.5% operating) because network effects create natural monopolies.

- **Technology Enabled Products** (23.0%, $1,819M): Eight hardware-centric businesses including Verathon (GlideScope video laryngoscopes, BladderScan ultrasound), Neptune (smart water meters), and Northern Digital (precision measurement). Hardware margins are lower but still strong at 34.5% operating.

The corporate model is decentralized: each business unit operates autonomously with its own P&L, and the corporate team of ~70 people focuses exclusively on capital allocation, governance, and shared services. No single customer represents 10%+ of any segment's revenue. International revenue is 13% of total — primarily a U.S.-centric business.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Quality and Growth

Roper's revenue reached $7,902.5M in FY2025, up 12.3% YoY. Organic growth was 5.4%, with acquisitions contributing 6.7%. The more important metric is revenue composition: recurring revenue (SaaS subscriptions, term licenses, maintenance) reached $4,483M (56.7% of total), while reoccurring revenue (transaction/volume-based fees) added $834M (10.5%). Combined, 67.3% of revenue is highly predictable, up from 65.4% in 2024 and 63.8% in 2023. This trajectory toward higher recurring mix is the core quality improvement story.

Software revenue broadly (recurring + reoccurring + non-recurring software) now represents 77.5% of total revenue, up from 75.2% two years ago. The company is steadily diluting its hardware exposure through software-heavy acquisitions.

### Profitability

Gross margins have been remarkably stable: 69.2% (2025), 69.3% (2024), 69.7% (2023). This stability through $9B of acquisitions is a strong indicator that Roper is not reaching for lower-quality targets. Operating margins are similarly stable at 28.3% (2025) vs. 28.4% (2024).

Net income margin declined from 22.0% to 19.4%, entirely explained by non-recurring items: FY2024 included a $234.6M gain on equity investments (Certinia sale, Indicor revaluation) that did not recur, plus interest expense rose $66M due to acquisition-related debt issuance.

Segment-level margins tell a more granular story. Application Software operating margin expanded for the third consecutive year to 26.8%, reflecting integration synergies from Transact/CBORD and operating leverage across the portfolio. Network Software margin compressed from 45.2% to 43.5%, driven by the Subsplash acquisition's lower-margin payments revenue mix and higher amortization — this should normalize over 2-3 years as amortization runs off. Technology Enabled Products margin expanded to 34.5% from 33.9%, reflecting Neptune's AMI product mix shift.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow of $2,435.6M represents a 30.8% FCF margin — down slightly from 32.4% in 2024 but within the normal range. The capital-light model is evident: total capex plus capitalized software development was only $104.7M (1.3% of revenue). This is among the lowest capital intensity ratios in technology.

FCF conversion from net income is approximately 159%, well above the 100% threshold that signals high-quality earnings. The negative working capital position of $(1,390M) reflects the SaaS prepayment model — customers pay upfront, and Roper collects before it delivers.

One-time tailwind: $150M cash tax benefit from the OBBBA (Build Back Better Act successor) in 2025. The remaining benefit will be realized over 3-5 years.

### Balance Sheet

This is where the risk concentrates. Total debt rose $1.69B in 2025 to $9,356M, funding CentralReach ($1,850M), Subsplash ($800M), and bolt-on acquisitions. Net debt stands at $9,059M, roughly 2.9x estimated EBITDA and 3.7x operating cash flow.

Goodwill of $21,341M plus other intangibles of $9,764M total $31,105M — representing 90% of total assets ($34,571M). Tangible book value is deeply negative. This is the structural reality of a serial acquirer that has deployed $9B in three years across businesses with minimal physical assets. The goodwill concentration means that impairment charges, if triggered, would be large relative to equity ($19,882M).

Mitigating factors: (1) the debt covenant (Total Debt/Total Capital ≤ 0.65) has significant headroom at 0.32; (2) the nearest maturities are easily covered by annual FCF; (3) the undrawn revolver provides additional liquidity; and (4) interest coverage remains healthy at approximately 7x EBITDA/interest.

### R&D Investment

R&D spending of $852.5M (10.8% of revenue) is growing faster than revenue — up from $748M (10.6%) in 2024 and $646M (10.5%) in 2023. This acceleration reflects AI embedding across the portfolio: CentralReach, Deltek, Vertafore, ConstructConnect, DAT, and iPipeline all now incorporate AI capabilities. The AI investment is both an opportunity (enhanced product value, reduced churn) and a risk (competitive response, platform dependency on OpenAI/Anthropic/Google).

## Growth Analysis

Roper's growth framework has two engines:

**Organic growth (5.4% in 2025)** is driven by expanding recurring revenue within existing customers. The Application Software segment's organic growth is enhanced by land-and-expand dynamics: initial deployment of a core system of record, followed by cross-selling analytics, compliance, and workflow modules. The conversion from on-premise licenses to SaaS subscriptions provides a multi-year organic growth tailwind as customers migrate. CentralReach's penetration of the rapidly growing ABA therapy market (driven by rising autism diagnosis rates and expanding insurance mandates) could sustain above-average organic growth in that business specifically.

**Acquisition growth (6.7% in 2025)** is the company's primary growth lever. Roper has averaged ~$3B/year in acquisition spending over the last three years. The company claims to have $6B+ of M&A and buyback capacity for 2026. However, the introduction of the share repurchase program raises a question: is the acquisition pipeline thinning at acceptable valuations? Management executing $1.81B of buybacks rather than deploying that capital into acquisitions could signal either discipline (unwilling to overpay) or a reduced opportunity set.

The risk to the acquisition model is straightforward: software valuations remain elevated, and Roper is competing against PE firms (many with lower cost of capital due to leveraged fund structures) for the same vertical SaaS targets. If acquisition multiples remain high, organic growth of 5-6% alone is insufficient to justify the current premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

Without current price data from the data feeds, I'll anchor to the buyback execution data in the 10-K. Management repurchased shares at ~$446/share in Q4 2025 and ~$353/share in Jan-Feb 2026. The sharp decline implies the stock was trading in the $340-360 range in early 2026.

At a ~$355 share price (midpoint of recent buyback range) and ~103M diluted shares (post-buyback), market capitalization is approximately $36.6B. Adding ~$9.1B net debt yields an enterprise value of ~$45.7B.

- **EV/FCF**: $45.7B / $2.44B = 18.7x — reasonable for a 30% FCF margin, 67% recurring revenue business
- **EV/EBITDA**: $45.7B / ~$3.1B (estimated) = 14.7x — moderate
- **P/FCF**: $36.6B / $2.44B = 15.0x — compelling for the quality profile

These multiples represent a meaningful compression from Roper's historical 25-30x EV/FCF range. The compression reflects both the broader market environment and Roper-specific concerns about leverage and acquisition sustainability. If organic growth holds at 5%+ and FCF grows mid-to-high single digits, a 20-22x EV/FCF multiple implies $475-525 per share — 35-48% upside from the ~$355 buyback price.

The valuation is no longer stretched. Whether the quality warrants re-rating back toward historical multiples depends on whether the acquisition pipeline remains productive and organic growth sustains.

## Competitive Landscape

Roper's competitive positioning is its strongest attribute. Each portfolio company operates in a distinct vertical where it holds a #1 or strong #2 position:

- **DAT** dominates North American freight matching with the largest carrier/broker network — classic network effect where the platform with the most participants wins.
- **Vertafore** is the leading software provider to independent P&C insurance agencies, deeply embedded in agency workflows with high switching costs.
- **Deltek** is effectively the standard for project-based ERP among government contractors — compliance requirements create regulatory moats.
- **Verathon** holds dominant positions in video laryngoscopy (GlideScope) and portable bladder ultrasound (BladderScan), both FDA-cleared devices with established clinical workflows.

Diversification across 29 businesses in unrelated verticals provides portfolio resilience — no single business failure can materially impair the whole. The tradeoff: no single business can drive transformative growth either. Roper's growth is inherently a portfolio outcome.

The competitive threat from AI is nuanced. For network-effect businesses (DAT, ConstructConnect), AI enhances the platform's value proposition by improving matching algorithms and bid predictions, reinforcing the moat. For systems-of-record businesses (Deltek, Vertafore, Aderant), AI adds workflow automation features that increase stickiness. The risk is more acute for point-solution businesses where an AI-native competitor could replicate functionality at lower cost. Roper's dependency on third-party AI platforms (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft) introduces supply chain risk if terms or pricing change.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Goodwill impairment risk (HIGH)**: $21.3B of goodwill across 25 reporting units. Management performed only qualitative impairment assessments in Q4 2025. Recently acquired units (CentralReach at $1,347M goodwill, Subsplash at $485M) carry higher inherent impairment risk. A macro downturn affecting any major vertical (ABA therapy reimbursement changes, government spending cuts impacting Deltek, construction slowdown affecting ConstructConnect) could trigger multi-hundred-million-dollar write-downs.

2. **Leverage trajectory (ELEVATED)**: Net debt/EBITDA of ~2.9x is manageable but at the high end of Roper's historical range. If the company executes another large acquisition in 2026 while completing the remaining $1.19B buyback authorization, leverage could approach 3.5x. Rising weighted-average interest costs (as 1.0-2.0% pandemic-era notes mature and are refinanced at 4.5-5.1%) will compress FCF conversion.

3. **Acquisition model sustainability (MODERATE)**: The shift to buybacks may signal a thinner M&A pipeline at acceptable valuations. If organic growth reverts to 3-4% and acquisition contribution declines, the 12%+ total growth rate becomes 6-8% — still respectable but insufficient to support historical multiples.

4. **AI platform dependency (MODERATE)**: Roper's AI strategy relies on third-party LLMs and cloud platforms. Changes in API pricing, terms of service, or competitive dynamics among AI providers could affect product economics.

5. **Indicor minority investment (LOW-MODERATE)**: The 43.8% stake (valued at $796M using Level 3 estimates) is gradually diluting via in-kind payments to CD&R. The exit path (expected IPO) is uncertain in timing and valuation.

6. **Government spending sensitivity**: Deltek's end market is directly tied to federal procurement. Tariffs affect 13% of revenue from international operations.

### Mitigating Factors

- $2.4B+ annual FCF provides substantial debt service coverage
- $2.6B undrawn revolver
- No customer concentration (no customer >10% of any segment)
- Recession-resilient verticals (healthcare, education, insurance, legal) dominate the portfolio
- Management buyback at ~$353 demonstrates insider conviction at current prices

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($475-525, 35-48% upside from ~$355)

Organic growth sustains at 5-6% as AI-enhanced products drive upsell and reduce churn. Application Software margins continue expanding toward 28-30% on operating leverage. Roper deploys remaining buyback authorization ($1.19B) and resumes acquisitions at $1-2B/year, funded by FCF. Market re-rates the stock toward 22-25x EV/FCF as recurring revenue mix exceeds 70% and growth visibility improves. FCF grows to $2.7-2.8B by FY2027.

### Bear Case ($280-310, 15-20% downside from ~$355)

Organic growth decelerates to 3% as macro headwinds (government spending cuts, healthcare reimbursement pressure, construction slowdown) hit multiple verticals simultaneously. A goodwill impairment charge of $500M+ in one or more recently acquired units damages investor confidence. Rising interest costs compress FCF margins below 28%. The acquisition pipeline produces a below-average deal that dilutes returns. Market applies a 15-17x EV/FCF multiple — in line with leveraged industrial acquirers rather than premium software compounders.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target**: $465 — 20x EV/FCF applied to estimated FY2026 FCF of ~$2.6B, less ~$8.5B net debt, divided by ~102M shares. This implies ~31% upside from the ~$355 buyback price level.

**Bull case target**: $525 — 23x EV/FCF on $2.7B FCF.

**Bear case target**: $295 — 16x EV/FCF on $2.3B FCF (3% organic growth, margin compression).

**Timeframe**: 12-18 months. Key catalysts: FY2025 earnings release (likely February 2026 — confirming FCF and organic growth trends), completion of buyback program ($1.19B remaining), and any acquisition announcements in H1 2026.

**Upside/downside from ~$355**: +31% to base case, +48% to bull case, -17% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.8:1 (base) to 2.8:1 (bull) against the bear case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers**:
1. Organic revenue growth declines below 3% for two consecutive quarters — signals structural demand weakness across the portfolio
2. Net leverage exceeds 3.5x EBITDA without a clear deleveraging plan — signals capital allocation discipline breakdown
3. Goodwill impairment charge exceeding $500M — signals overpayment on recent acquisitions

## Conclusion

Roper Technologies is a high-quality compounder trading at compressed multiples following a period of elevated acquisition activity and rising leverage. The 67% recurring/reoccurring revenue base, 30%+ FCF margins, and dominant niche competitive positions justify a premium to the broader market. Management's aggressive buyback at ~$353/share — the company's first significant repurchase program in its history — is a meaningful signal from a team with a strong 20+ year capital allocation track record.

The declining score trajectory over prior reports (7.1 → 6.7 → 6.5) correctly identified rising risks from leverage and goodwill concentration. Those risks persist. But the stock has repriced to reflect them: at ~15x P/FCF, Roper is trading at a substantial discount to its historical 20-25x range. The previous HOLD recommendations lacked a specific, falsifiable barrier to BUY — leverage at 2.9x is elevated but not distressed, goodwill risk is structural to the model, and management choosing to buy back stock at current prices rather than pursue deals at unattractive multiples partially addresses the acquisition pipeline question. The risk/reward at ~$355 favors ownership with a 12-18 month horizon.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 3-5% portfolio sizing. The primary monitoring variable is organic revenue growth — sustained 5%+ organic growth validates the premium compounder thesis; deceleration below 3% invalidates it.
