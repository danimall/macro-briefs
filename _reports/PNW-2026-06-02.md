**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pinnacle West Capital is a pure-play, single-state regulated electric utility holding company whose entire earnings base flows through Arizona Public Service (APS), Arizona's largest investor-owned utility serving ~1.5 million retail customers. The investment case rests on a single distinctive feature: APS operates one of the highest-growth service territories in the U.S., driven by population migration plus an explosion of data-center, AI, and semiconductor (TSMC, Intel) load. Management guides to 4-6% retail sales growth in 2026 and 5-7% annual growth through 2030, multiples of the typical 1-2% a mature regulated utility delivers. That load growth feeds a ~$8B three-year capital plan that, if recovered on reasonable terms, compounds rate base at an above-peer rate.

Whether recovery happens on reasonable terms is currently unresolved and binary. APS filed a 2025 rate case seeking a $579.5M net base increase (+13.99%) at a 10.7% requested ROE. ACC Staff recommends ~$525M at 9.55-9.80% ROE; consumer advocate RUCO recommends as little as $200M at 9.0-9.2% ROE. Hearings began in May 2026 with active public opposition, and a decision is expected to set rates in H2 2026. The spread between the ask and RUCO's position is more than $300M of revenue requirement and ~150bps of ROE. The prior 2022 case landed at 9.55% ROE, well below the request, which suggests the realistic outcome sits in the lower half of that range. The accretive version of the growth story depends on APS's "growth pays for growth" cost-allocation reform surviving a politically charged fight that is being framed publicly as data centers versus residential ratepayers.

At $97.48, PNW trades at ~18.3x trailing earnings and a 3.65% yield, a modest discount to the peer median P/E of 21.1x but at a lower yield than the 3.8% peer median. The stock has appreciated ~7% over the past year and sits near the upper end of the analyst target range (mean $105, median $104, low $87, high $128). Near-term estimate revisions are negative (current-quarter EPS cut 9.1% over 90 days), operating cash flow fell ~42% YoY in Q1 while capex held steady, and the company is funding the cash-flow gap with debt and forward-equity dilution. The combination of a genuinely superior growth profile and an unresolved binary regulatory outcome with falling near-term estimates produces a HOLD. There is no edge in pre-positioning ahead of the rate-case order.

## Company Overview

Pinnacle West (~$31B consolidated assets, ~$12.0B market cap) derives essentially all revenue and earnings from APS, a vertically integrated, cost-of-service regulated monopoly. Rates are set by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC) for retail and FERC for transmission. Other subsidiaries (El Dorado venture investments, PNW Power transmission/wind JVs) are immaterial. This is a single-reportable-segment regulated electric utility.

The generation fleet is diversified across nuclear, natural gas, solar, wind, battery storage, and declining coal (Cholla coal units retired April 2025; Four Corners remains). The anchor asset is Palo Verde Generating Station, the largest carbon-free generator in the U.S. at ~32M MWh/year, of which APS owns/leases 29.1% and serves as operator. In March 2026 APS announced intent to renew all three Palo Verde licenses, extending operations from the mid-2040s to the mid-2060s.

The defining structural feature is location. Arizona's load growth is driven by in-migration and the Southwest data-center/semiconductor buildout. APS states that incremental large-load requests "far exceed available generation and transmission resource capacity in the Southwest region for the foreseeable future," forcing a prioritization queue. This is a multi-year rate-base tailwind, conditional on regulatory cost recovery.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** Operating revenue rose to $1,150M from $1,032M (+11.4%). Net income attributable to common swung to $33M from a $(5)M loss, EPS $0.27 versus $(0.04). Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter for this summer-peaking utility, and the swing was driven heavily by record-warm winter weather (the hottest February and March on record in the territory), higher transmission revenue (+$27M), lower O&M (-$23M from fewer planned outages), and customer growth. The headline +2331% earnings surprise versus a near-zero estimate is an artifact of small-denominator seasonal math, not a fundamental change.

**Cash flow is the key tension.** Operating cash flow fell to $235M from $402M YoY in Q1 (~42% decline), driven by higher O&M payments, working capital, fuel costs, and +$27M of interest paid. This deterioration occurred while capex held at ~$628M for the quarter. The mechanism is straightforward: capex of ~$2.6B (2026), $2.65B (2027), and $2.7B (2028) far exceeds internally generated cash, so the company is in a sustained negative-free-cash-flow phase funded by debt and equity.

**Balance sheet and financing.** Total debt is ~$10.4B carrying value. Pinnacle West debt/cap is ~61%; APS is ~51% against a 65% covenant ceiling. Interest expense is climbing (+$19M YoY in Q1) on higher balances; APS issued $600M of 5.10% notes in March 2026. Equity funding leans heavily on forward-sale agreements: ~$462M ATM forwards (weighted-average $95.08), residual February 2024 forwards struck at $64.51, and $525M of 4.75% convertible notes due 2027 (conversion ~$92.30). The convert is in the money at $97.48, so dilution is a live near-term consideration. Credit ratings are solid investment grade (PNW Baa2/BBB+/BBB; APS Baa1/BBB+/BBB+, all stable), but a downgrade below IG could trigger ~$710M of collateral calls on energy contracts.

**Off-balance-sheet-style obligations.** Operating lease ROU assets jumped to ~$4.06B, and the company discloses ~$11.8B of future fixed lease consideration for arrangements not yet commenced (mostly energy-storage PPAs and gas tolling). Q1 total lease cost more than doubled YoY ($86.9M vs $39.8M). These are recoverable through the power supply adjustor, but they represent a large, growing fixed claim tied directly to the growth bet, reflecting a shift toward contracted capacity over owned plant.

FY2025 net income was ~$632M, roughly flat to up versus 2024's $626M. The earnings base is stable; the question is the slope of growth from here, which the rate case determines.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the genuine differentiator. Retail customer growth was 2.2% in Q1 2026, guided to 1.5-2.5% annually through 2030. Weather-normalized retail sales grew 9.4% YoY in Q1, though ~2.0 percentage points came from a one-time prior-year unbilled-revenue adjustment, so underlying growth was somewhat lower. Management guides 4-6% sales growth in 2026 and 5-7% average annual growth through 2030, with data centers and large manufacturing alone contributing 3-5% in 2026 and 4-6% annually thereafter.

This is exceptional for a regulated utility, and the demand signal is corroborated by the broader Southwest data-center and semiconductor buildout. APS's prioritization queue for large-load requests is itself evidence the demand exceeds available capacity. Resource procurement reflects the scale: the 2023 ASRFP procured 3,606 MW battery, 2,649 MW solar, 517 MW gas, and 500 MW wind for 2026-2028, with a November 2025 ASRFP seeking ≥1,000 MW for 2029-2031. APS also executed a July 2025 gas-transportation precedent agreement supporting up to 2,000 MW of new flexible gas generation by ~late 2029, important because existing pipelines into Arizona are fully committed.

The translation from sales growth to EPS growth is not automatic, and the data flags caution. Near-term estimate revisions are negative: current-quarter consensus fell 9.1% over 90 days and 3.4% over 30 days. Current-year (FY26) EPS sits at $4.70, essentially flat over 90 days; next-year at $5.64 is flat to slightly down. The market is pricing roughly mid-single-digit forward EPS growth, not the headline sales-growth figure, because regulatory lag, dilution from equity forwards/converts, and rising interest expense absorb a meaningful share of revenue growth before it reaches the bottom line. The accretive scenario requires the rate case to deliver an adequate ROE plus a working formula-rate mechanism that compresses lag.

## Valuation Assessment

At $97.48, PNW trades at ~18.3x trailing EPS of $5.05 and ~20.7x FY26 consensus of $4.70 (with the stated forward P/E reported near identical to trailing). The peer median P/E in Multi-Utilities is 21.1x (OGE 21.1x, BKH 18.8x, MDU 22.9x, NWE 25.9x, AVA 16.5x), so PNW trades at roughly a 13% P/E discount to the median on a trailing basis. The dividend yield is 3.65%, below the 3.8% peer median.

The discount is defensible but not a clear mispricing. PNW's superior load-growth profile would normally argue for a premium, not a discount, to the group. The discount instead reflects two offsetting factors: the binary, contested rate-case overhang and the cash-flow/dilution profile. On a normalized basis, if the rate case settles near the Staff midpoint (~9.65% ROE, ~$525M), forward earnings power supports the current multiple but offers limited re-rating. If it lands near RUCO (~9.0-9.2%, ~$200-278M), forward EPS growth disappoints and the multiple is at risk of compressing toward the low-yield, low-growth quadrant. If APS achieves close to its 10.7% ask with a functioning FRAM, the stock re-rates toward the upper analyst target ($128 high).

The stock sits near the top of the analyst range (mean $105, median $104, low $87). For a regulated utility with negative near-term estimate revisions and a pending binary outcome, proximity to the consensus mean is not a floor: utility analyst targets do not lag reality the way they do for accelerating high-growth stocks, and the upside is gated by a regulatory decision rather than by consensus catching up to fundamentals. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside at the current price; it is approximately fair given the unresolved ROE outcome.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, APS faces no direct retail competition. Its moat is the regulatory franchise plus an irreplaceable physical asset base anchored by Palo Verde. The competitive question is trajectory, and on that axis PNW is positioned better than most peers because its territory's structural load growth is among the strongest in the country. Five-year peer-relative TSR (138 versus the peer group's 144) suggests roughly in-line, slightly lagging total returns historically, but the forward setup is more favorable than the trailing comparison if cost recovery holds.

Two competitive threats merit flagging. First, large customers could self-supply via behind-the-meter generation, which would erode the load APS is building infrastructure to serve. Second, the regulatory cost-allocation fight could blunt the "growth pays for growth" structure that makes the data-center boom accretive. If the ACC sides with residential ratepayers, APS may capture the load growth without capturing the shareholder returns, which would convert a competitive advantage into a capital-intensity burden. The competitive position is improving on demand fundamentals but contingent on regulatory outcomes that are outside management's control.

## Risk Assessment

**The 2025 rate case is the dominant risk and catalyst.** The ask is on a $12.5B rate base, with potential for zero post-test-year plant under RUCO's position. APS's April 2026 rebuttal raised the ask to $611-694M. The 2022 precedent (9.55% ROE, below the ask) suggests the ACC tends to come in well under management's request.

**Political/regulatory contention.** The case is publicly framed as data centers versus households, with advocacy groups rallying against the "14% hike." The "growth pays for growth" reform is the linchpin of the accretive thesis and the most politically exposed element.

**FRAM formula-rate fight.** The proposed Formula Rate Adjustment Mechanism faces a RUCO/ALCG legal challenge, remanded by the AZ Court of Appeals (Nov 2025) and pending at the AZ Supreme Court. Without formula rates, the historical test-year construct produces chronic regulatory lag against a 5-7% growth and multi-billion capex backdrop.

**Wildfire liability.** A rising, California-style tail risk. The ACC denied APS's request to defer wildfire/insurance costs (June 2025), pushing recovery into the rate case. A May 2025 Arizona law ties the standard of care to an approved mitigation plan (APS's 2026 plan under DFFM review). Mitigation includes a captive insurance cell, AI fire-detection cameras, and public safety power shutoffs.

**Capital intensity and financing.** The ~$8B three-year capex against falling operating cash flow forces continued debt issuance into a higher-rate environment plus equity dilution via forwards and the in-the-money 2027 convert. This is the structural source of the gap between strong sales growth and modest EPS growth.

**Nuclear and tax items.** Palo Verde concentration brings NRC oversight, retrospective premium exposure (~$144.9M/incident), and a $177M potential VIE payment tied to the remaining Unit 2 sale-leaseback. $33.4M (2024) + $39.6M (2025) of Nuclear PTC benefits are recorded as uncertain tax positions pending IRS guidance, and the IRS notified APS of intent to examine federal returns in March 2026.

**Partial offset.** EPA deregulation (2009 Endangerment Finding repeal, GHG power-plant rollback, MATS reversion, ELG deadline extensions) reduces near-term compliance cost risk on the Four Corners fossil fleet, though all are subject to litigation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 24.5% versus 15.9% one-year historical volatility, rich by 8.6 percentage points, consistent with the rate-case hearing timeline. IV has come down sharply from 35.2% on May 7, indicating some uncertainty resolved through the Q1 print, but the term structure is flat (near 24.5% ≈ far 24.4%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single near-term event. Put/call skew is balanced (2.1%), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.38 is call-tilted, so there is no acute bearish positioning. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.0%, rising to ±10.7% by the six-month tenor, appropriately wide given the rate-case order falls within that window. The options market is pricing two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional bet, which supports a HOLD posture rather than pre-positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The rate case lands near the Staff-to-APS range, say a 9.7-10.0% ROE with a functioning or partially functioning FRAM, preserving the "growth pays for growth" structure. Above-peer sales growth compounds rate base while the formula mechanism compresses lag, driving 7-9% EPS growth that the market re-rates toward a peer-premium multiple. Palo Verde license renewal extends carbon-free earnings power into the mid-2060s, and the data-center load surge proves durable. The stock approaches the high analyst target of $128 (+31%), with a growing dividend supplementing total return.

### Bear Case
The ACC sides with residential ratepayers, awarding closer to RUCO's $200-278M at 9.0-9.2% ROE and diluting the cost-allocation reform. APS captures the load but not the shareholder return; EPS growth disappoints at low-single-digits; operating cash flow stays negative, forcing more dilution and debt into higher rates. Regulatory lag persists without FRAM, a wildfire event materializes the tail risk, or a credit downgrade triggers collateral calls. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range and the yield re-rates, taking the stock toward the analyst low of $87 (-11%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The growth profile is genuinely superior, but the accretion case is hostage to a binary regulatory outcome resolving in H2 2026, and near-term estimates are falling. At $97.48, near the top of the analyst range, the risk/reward is roughly balanced pending the order.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ACC rate-case order delivering ≥9.7% ROE with a functioning or partial FRAM mechanism and material post-test-year plant treatment, confirming the cost-allocation model is intact.
- Operating cash flow inflecting positive on a trailing-twelve-month basis, reducing reliance on equity-forward dilution.
- A pullback toward the high-$80s/low-$90s (toward book/yield support) that restores a clear discount to peer-adjusted growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Rate-case order near RUCO's terms (≤9.2% ROE, ~$200-278M) with the FRAM struck down at the AZ Supreme Court, eliminating the lag-compression thesis.
- A credit-rating action toward or below investment grade, given the ~$710M collateral-call exposure.
- A material wildfire liability event without a recovery mechanism.

**Reassessment timeline:** The ACC rate-case decision (expected H2 2026, with hearings ongoing from May 2026) is the natural reassessment point. The secondary checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release for an update on weather-normalized sales and cash-flow trajectory.

## Conclusion

Pinnacle West owns one of the best structural demand setups in the regulated utility sector, with Arizona load growth underpinned by a real, broad-based data-center and semiconductor buildout, and a long-dated carbon-free anchor in Palo Verde now extended to the mid-2060s. That is the bull case, and it is credible. The offset is that converting load growth into shareholder return depends entirely on a contested 2025 rate case whose plausible outcomes span a ~150bps ROE range and more than $300M of revenue requirement, and on a formula-rate mechanism currently under legal challenge. Near-term estimates are falling, operating cash flow dropped while capex held steady, and the company is funding the gap with debt into higher rates and equity dilution that is live given the in-the-money 2027 convert.

The score trajectory has declined modestly (6.3 → 6.1 → 5.85 here), with Financial Health, Management Quality, and Risk Factors each marked down a notch over the past two readings, driven primarily by the cash-flow deterioration and the escalating regulatory contention rather than by price action. At $97.48, near the upper end of the analyst range with a balanced two-sided options market and no asymmetric valuation edge, the appropriate call is HOLD pending the rate-case order. The H2 2026 decision resolves the thesis in either direction, and there is no edge in pre-positioning ahead of it.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward P/E on FY26 consensus is ~20.7x (not ~18.4x), materially narrowing the apparent valuation discount to peers and weakening the "modest discount" framing that supports the HOLD's balanced risk/reward.
