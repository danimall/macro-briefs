**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cummins is in the middle of a business-mix transformation that the equity market has largely recognized but not fully digested. The headline top line looks stagnant (revenue moved from $34.1B in 2024 to $33.7B in 2025, a 1% decline), but that number conceals two offsetting cycles running in opposite directions. The legacy Engine and Components franchises are in a deep North American truck downcycle (heavy-duty shipments down 23% in 2025, another 16% in Q1 2026), while Power Systems and Distribution are riding a data-center and grid-power boom. Power Systems EBITDA grew 44% in 2025 with margin expanding to 22.7%, then accelerated to a 29.5% margin in Q1 2026 with power generation up 28% year-over-year. The company is increasingly being valued as an AI-power infrastructure play rather than a truck-engine cyclical, and the stock has re-rated accordingly, up 73% over the trailing twelve months.

The fundamental story is genuine. Estimate revisions are rising (+8.5% current-year EPS over 90 days, +11.1% next-quarter over 90 days), management raised 2026 revenue guidance to +8-11% in May, and the June Circe Energy natural-gas genset agreement for a West Texas AI-data-center microgrid crystallizes the power thesis with a real contract. Balance sheet quality is strong: net leverage 0.22x against a 0.65x covenant ceiling, A-rated by both agencies, $3.2B cash plus a $3.7B undrawn revolver, and resumed buybacks. This is a well-run, structurally advantaged industrial with genuine optionality.

The problem is price. At ~$642 (the shares traded from $610 to a $737 high and back to ~$642 over the sample period), Cummins trades at roughly 31x trailing and ~23x forward EPS on the $28.39 current-year consensus. Consensus mean target of ~$755 implies ~18% upside, but that target is stale relative to a stock that has already run hard, and the current-quarter estimate was just trimmed 1.5% over 30 days. The setup is balanced: real growth acceleration on one side, a full multiple plus two under-priced idiosyncratic risks (China JV earnings concentration and the deregulation threat to the emissions-compliance moat) on the other. We rate CMI a HOLD (growth hold), with a preference to accumulate on pullbacks toward the low-$500s where the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Cummins is a ~$34B-revenue global power systems company founded in 1919 and headquartered in Columbus, Indiana. It operates five reportable segments:

- **Engine (~26% of segment sales):** Diesel and natural-gas engines ≤15L for on-highway trucks and off-highway applications. The historical core franchise, now the cyclical drag.
- **Components (~25%):** Aftertreatment/emissions solutions, turbochargers, fuel systems, drivetrain, axles, brakes, automated transmissions. The filtration business (Atmus) was fully divested via tax-free split-off in March 2024.
- **Distribution (~30%, largest by sales):** Owned distributorships selling gensets, engines, parts, and service. The highest-growth channel for power generation.
- **Power Systems (~18%, highest margin):** Large engines ≥16L, standby/prime power generators, and industrial applications (mining, oil & gas, marine, rail, defense). This is the growth and margin engine.
- **Accelera (~1%):** Zero-emissions technologies (battery, electric powertrain), structurally loss-making and being aggressively downsized.

Cummins is the leading independent (non-captive) engine supplier, with entrenched OEM relationships (PACCAR, Traton/International, Daimler Trucks, Stellantis). Its moat rests on emissions-compliance expertise, systems integration, and a large service network (~640 distributor locations plus 13,000+ certified dealers across ~190 countries). About 40% of sales are international.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the mix-shift narrative.** Revenue moved $28.1B (2022) → $34.1B (2023) → $34.1B (2024) → $33.7B (2025). The flat-to-down top line reflects the Atmus divestiture (removed roughly $1.6B of run-rate revenue) and the North American truck downcycle, partially offset by the power-generation ramp. The segment divergence is the real story. In 2025 versus 2024: Power Systems EBITDA +44% to $1.69B (margin 22.7% from 18.4%); Distribution EBITDA +31% to $1.81B (margin 14.6%); Engine EBITDA −16%; Components EBITDA −12%. Q1 2026 sharpened the split: Power Systems EBITDA +48% with a 29.5% margin, Distribution +18%, and Engine EBITDA −39% as its margin fell to 10.4% from 16.5%. Power generation within Power Systems grew 28% year-over-year.

**Earnings quality requires normalization.** 2025 net income was $2.84B ($20.50 diluted EPS), down from 2024's $3.95B. But 2024 was inflated by a $1.3B non-taxable Atmus gain, and the effective tax rate distortion (17.0% in 2024 versus 25.4% in 2025) flatters the comparison further. On a like-for-like operating basis, results improved: gross margin rose 60bps to 25.3% and operating income grew 7% to $4.0B. Anyone comparing headline net income year-over-year will misread a deterioration where operating performance actually strengthened.

**Cash flow.** 2025 operating cash flow rebounded to $3.6B from $1.5B in 2024, but this jump is the absence of the $1.9B one-time emissions settlement payment that depressed 2024, not a fundamental step-change. Free cash flow was $2.39B (7.1% margin). Capex was steady at ~$1.2B, guided up to $1.35-1.45B in 2026 with more than 65% directed to North American power-gen capacity, a capital commitment consistent with management's confidence in the demand ramp.

**Balance sheet.** Debt/capital of 36%, net leverage 0.22x against a 0.65x covenant ceiling, and investment-grade ratings (S&P A / Moody's A2, both Stable). Global pension plans are 112% funded (U.S. 115%), with only a ~$51M contribution expected in 2026, a non-issue. Altman Z of 2.51 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for a capital-intensive industrial with meaningful leverage rather than a distress signal.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised ~10% to $2.00/quarter in July 2025 ($7.64/share for 2025, ~$1.1B total), continuing a consistent growth record. Buybacks were dormant in 2025 but resumed in Q1 2026 ($243M / 0.5M shares), with ~$2.0B remaining authorized. The current dividend yield of ~1.3% is thin, so this is a growth-plus-optionality story with a well-covered, growing payout as a backstop rather than an income story.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the durability of the power-generation ramp and the timing of a truck-cycle recovery. On the power side, the demand signal is broad and confirmed across the industrial complex: AI/data-center electricity demand and grid-reliability concerns are driving record orders for large gensets and prime/standby power. The June 2026 Circe Energy agreement for natural-gas generator sets supporting a behind-the-meter prime-power microgrid at a West Texas high-performance-computing site is a concrete multi-year contract, and Power Systems plus Distribution now generate the majority of segment EBITDA at expanding margins. This aligns with data-center power confirmation visible across the equipment chain, from hyperscaler capex to grid-scale generation to electrical equipment orders, which argues the demand is structural rather than speculative.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum. Current-year EPS consensus rose 8.5% over 90 days to $28.39, next-quarter estimates rose 11.1% over 90 days, and the earnings surprise track record is strong (three beats in the last four quarters, including +9.0% in Q1 2026 and +24.0% two quarters prior). The one miss, Q4 2025 (−17.2%), is consistent with the truck-cycle trough weighing on Engine/Components in the seasonally weak quarter. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to +8-11% with an EBITDA margin range of 17.75-18.5%, a beat-and-raise consistent with the power-gen strength and improving North American on-highway orders and spot rates.

The 90-day revision of +8.5% on current-year EPS is meaningful but sits below the +30% threshold that would flag a violent inflection, and revenue growth in the high single digits is solid rather than explosive. The truck-cycle recovery is the second leg: management expects North American on-highway to improve in H2 2026, which would add upside on top of the power-gen ramp. That recovery is a forecast, not a confirmed data point; Q1 2026 still showed heavy-duty shipments down 16% and medium-duty down 25%. Growth prospects are genuinely good but not in the top tier of the inflection cases, warranting a solid but not maximum score.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$642, Cummins trades at ~31x trailing EPS ($20.50, which itself is depressed by the truck trough and Accelera charges) and ~23x forward on the $28.39 current-year consensus. Against peers in Construction Machinery & Heavy Transportation Equipment, the peer median P/E is 25.1 (PCAR 25.0, WAB 37.1, OSK 14.9, FSS 25.1, TEX 43.3). On a trailing basis CMI trades at a premium to that median; on a forward basis it is roughly in line to slightly below. The forward multiple of ~23x on high-single-digit revenue growth and a rising-margin mix shift is defensible but not cheap; a PEG in the ~2.0 area is not the profile of a mispriced inflection stock.

The bull anchor is the consensus mean target of ~$755 (median $760, high $901, low $520), which implies ~18% upside from ~$642. On genuinely cheap inflection names, analyst targets act as floors that get revised up as consensus catches reality. Here the caution is that the stock has already re-rated hard (up 73% TTM), the forward multiple is full, and the current-quarter estimate was just trimmed 1.5% over 30 days even as the 90-day trend stayed positive. That deceleration in the most recent revision window suggests consensus is closer to fair than lagging. The valuation dimension has historically been an anti-predictive trap, where a "cheap" screen sorts the wrong way, so we are deliberately skeptical of treating the sub-peer-forward multiple as a buy signal. Here it is not even cheap; it is fair-to-full.

Normalizing helps the story but does not fully rescue the price. If the truck cycle recovers and Engine/Components margins revert toward mid-cycle while Power Systems holds ~25%+ margins, mid-cycle EPS power could approach $32-35 (consistent with the $32.29 next-year consensus). At ~$642 that is ~18-20x normalized mid-cycle earnings, reasonable for a company with improving mix and a genuine secular tailwind, but not a margin of safety. The valuation score reflects a business where the multiple already prices most of the good news, leaving the upside dependent on the truck recovery materializing and power-gen margins sustaining at record levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Cummins occupies a strong competitive position that is improving in its highest-value segments and eroding in its cyclically depressed core. In power generation, the company competes with Caterpillar, Generac, and Rolls-Royce (mtu), and its edge is portfolio breadth (fuel-agnostic diesel and gas), emissions/systems integration, and global service coverage that generates durable aftermarket annuity revenue. The Power Systems margin expansion in Q1 2026 is direct evidence of pricing power and mix leverage in a tight-supply market.

In the on-highway engine business, the position is more contested. A handful of large OEMs (PACCAR, Traton, Daimler, Stellantis) drive significant engine and component sales, and several of them can and do build their own engines. Cummins remains the leading independent supplier, but the vertical-integration risk is real and structural. The energy-transition dynamic cuts both ways: slower EV and hydrogen adoption has hurt Accelera (serial impairments) but extends the profitable life of the diesel/gas franchise, which is a net competitive positive given where the market has landed on adoption timing.

The trajectory analysis matters here. The prior report cut competitive position from 8.0 to 6.8, reflecting the deregulation threat to the emissions-compliance moat. The new data does not reverse that concern; the EPA's February 2026 rescission of the GHG endangerment finding is a genuine erosion of the differentiation Cummins spent decades building, but the offsetting strength in Power Systems supports a still-solid mid-6s score. The moat is stable to improving in power, stable to eroding in trucks, netting to a company gaining share in its growth segments.

## Risk Assessment

**China JV earnings concentration (the most under-discussed structural risk).** Nearly half of Cummins' $469M equity/royalty income flows through three 50%-owned Chinese JVs (Chongqing, Dongfeng, Beijing Foton) that Cummins does not control. A "substantial portion" of net income depends on these entities. Any China trade escalation, export-control action, or cash-repatriation friction directly threatens this stream. This is a bigger swing factor than the wholly-owned China business and is largely invisible to a standard screen, which means it is likely under-priced.

**North American truck downcycle.** Heavy-duty shipments fell 23% in 2025 and 16% in Q1 2026; medium-duty fell 25% in Q1. Engine segment margins compressed to 10.4%. Management's H2 2026 recovery expectation is a cyclical bet, not yet confirmed in the data.

**Deregulation (a genuinely idiosyncratic risk).** Cummins is one of the few large industrials for whom less environmental regulation is a competitive negative. The February 2026 EPA rescission of the GHG endangerment finding (effective April 2026) commoditizes the emissions-compliance moat and risks stranding R&D. The near-term cash hit is small (~$99M non-cash emission-credit exposure), but the strategic implication is larger and not obviously reflected in a premium multiple.

**Accelera write-down cycle.** Three consecutive periods of impairments ($312M in 2024, $458M in 2025, $199M in Q1 2026 on the fuel-cell divestiture), plus a stalled Amplify Cell Technologies battery JV with up to $418M of remaining committed contributions and flagged impairment risk. This is a controlled retreat that is earnings-accretive as losses shrink, but the next impairment (Amplify) is a live overhang.

**OEM concentration and vertical integration** and **tariff/trade** (net impact immaterial to date but repeatedly flagged; the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based tariffs in February 2026, after which new tariffs were imposed under other statutes) round out the specific risk set.

Netting these, the idiosyncratic risk profile is moderate but not benign; the China JV and deregulation vectors are real, concentrated, and under-priced, which caps the risk score in the mid-range rather than allowing a higher "risks are all priced" reading.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.9% sits well above the 35.8% one-year historical volatility (a 16.1pp premium) and in the 72nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (51.9% near-term versus 39.4% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress or a catalyst, consistent with an upcoming earnings print. The put/call skew is balanced (−0.2% near-term) and put/call volume is light at 0.30, so this is event-uncertainty pricing rather than directional bearishness. ATM IV collapsed from 135.1% to 51.9% between mid-June and late July as the June earnings-adjacent stress resolved, while the price fell 14.8% and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.37 to 1.20, a shift toward more defensive open interest. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here; a better entry follows the post-print volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$755-901):** The power-generation ramp sustains at record margins (Power Systems ~25%+), the North American truck cycle recovers in H2 2026 as management expects, and Engine/Components margins revert toward mid-cycle. On $32+ normalized EPS at a 25x multiple justified by the improved mix and secular data-center tailwind, the stock reaches the high-$700s to $800s, with the $901 street high requiring both cycles firing plus further AI-power contract wins beyond Circe. Balance-sheet optionality (net leverage 0.22x, $2B buyback authorization, M&A capacity) provides additional levers.

**Bear case (~$450-520):** The truck recovery slips into 2027, power-gen order growth decelerates as the initial data-center genset wave is absorbed, and China JV earnings take a hit from trade escalation. Compressed EPS toward the mid-$20s at a de-rated ~18-19x multiple (as the AI-power premium fades) pulls the stock to the low-$500s, roughly the $520 street low, or lower on a China-JV shock. The deregulation erosion of the compliance moat weighs on the multiple over time.

The base case sits between these: the power thesis is real and the balance sheet is strong, but the multiple already reflects most of the good news, and the two under-priced idiosyncratic risks cap the asymmetry. The hold rests on price, not on business quality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (power-gen ramp plus truck-cycle recovery optionality), but the current price near consensus does not offer asymmetric upside, and the entry is better on weakness.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$500s (near the $520 street low), where forward P/E compresses toward ~18x and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- Confirmation of the North American truck-cycle recovery in H2 2026 (heavy-duty shipment decline reversing to flat-to-positive year-over-year), which would add a second growth leg on top of power-gen.
- Current-year EPS revisions re-accelerating above +15% over 90 days alongside sustained Power Systems margins ≥27%, which would signal consensus is still lagging the inflection.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material China JV earnings shock (trade escalation, export controls, or repatriation restriction cutting the ~$469M equity-income stream).
- A large Amplify Cell Technologies impairment plus deterioration in Power Systems order growth, signaling the power-gen wave has peaked.
- Power Systems margins reverting below ~20% while the truck cycle fails to recover, which would remove both legs of the growth thesis and expose the full multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July / Q2 2026 print) is the immediate checkpoint for truck-cycle trajectory and power-gen order durability, with the H2 2026 on-highway recovery the decisive multi-quarter test.

## Conclusion

Cummins is a genuinely good business undergoing a favorable transformation from a truck-engine cyclical into a data-center and power-generation compounder, backed by a fortress balance sheet, rising estimates, and a concrete AI-power contract pipeline. The Power Systems margin at 29.5% and the +8-11% 2026 revenue guidance raise validate the thesis. The trajectory shows scores drifting lower (6.9 → 6.9 → 6.2) as the stock re-rated and management-quality/valuation dimensions were marked down; the new data confirms the fundamental improvement but does not reverse the valuation concern that drove those cuts.

The recommendation stays HOLD. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it should be high, but the analysis genuinely lands there. The business quality supports a solid overall score, the growth is real, and the balance sheet is strong; the offset is a full forward multiple (~23x), a stale consensus target on a stock that has already run 73%, and two under-priced idiosyncratic risks (China JV concentration and the deregulation threat to the compliance moat). As an Industrials name, CMI also carries the sector's default negative-alpha caution, which raises the bar for a company-specific BUY that the current price does not clear. Accumulate on pullbacks toward the low-$500s, where the power-gen thesis is available at an asymmetric price; hold existing positions for the growth optionality; do not chase into rich near-term implied volatility.
