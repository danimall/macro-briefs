**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

T. Rowe Price is a $25.7B-market-cap active asset manager caught in a well-defined structural squeeze: reported revenue is rising, but the underlying franchise is shrinking. FY2025 net revenue grew 3.1% to $7.31B while the firm bled -$56.9B in net outflows, and the annualized effective fee rate fell from 41.9 bps in 2023 to 38.4 bps in Q1 2026. The mechanism is straightforward: equity markets keep lifting average AUM faster than fees compress and assets leak, so revenue grows even as each dollar of AUM becomes worth roughly 8% less in fees than it was three years ago. This is the secular active-to-passive migration playing out on the income statement.

Against that structural backdrop sits a strong balance sheet and capital-return engine. TROW carries essentially no traditional debt (D/E 0.21, Altman Z 4.92), generates ~$1.48B of free cash flow, and returned $1.77B to shareholders in 2025 ($1.14B dividends plus $624.6M buybacks, nearly double the prior year). The ~4.4% dividend yield rests on a 39-year increase streak and is well covered. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 6-8% over the past 90 days, current-year to $10.17 and next-year to $10.38, and the Q1 2026 GAAP operating margin rebounded to 36.6% as the first-ever restructuring program moved past its charge phase.

The prior trajectory of this name — scores declining from 6.1 in February to 5.0 in May with a downgrade to AVOID — reflected a widening gap between price and deteriorating fundamentals at a lower share price. The stock has since rallied ~15% off its March low near $86 to ~$122, closing the valuation discount that made the AVOID reasonable while the fundamental story remains unchanged. That combination argues for a HOLD on the yield and capital return rather than either a chase or an active short. The rising-estimate signal is real but modest and driven by market-lifted AUM, not organic flows, so it does not clear the bar for a growth-driven upgrade.

## Company Overview

T. Rowe Price operates a single reportable segment (investment advisory), with more than 90% of revenue derived from advisory fees calculated as a percentage of AUM. As of Q1 2026, AUM stood at $1.71 trillion, down from $1.78T at year-end 2025 on $52.2B of market depreciation and $13.7B of net outflows. A subsequent market recovery lifted the May 2026 figure to a reported $1.89T, illustrating how directly the firm's fortunes track equity markets.

AUM composition at YE2025 was equity $878.5B (49%), multi-asset $627.0B (35%), fixed income $211.6B (12%), and alternatives $58.5B (3%). The largest component is the target-date retirement franchise at $561.4B, the bulk of multi-asset, anchored in defined-contribution plan penetration and a recordkeeping business with ~$314B under administration. This is the stickiest and still-growing part of the business; retirement flows are systematic, contribution-driven, and slow to move. Diversification efforts center on the 2021 Oak Hill Advisors acquisition (private credit), model-delivery managed accounts, and an emerging crypto/ETF product push. Non-U.S. clients represent only ~8.6% of AUM, leaving the firm heavily concentrated in U.S. markets.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years — $6.46B (2023), $7.09B (2024, +9.8%), $7.31B (2025, +3.1%) — with Q1 2026 net revenue of $1.86B up 5.3% YoY. This growth is a function of higher average AUM on rising markets rather than organic inflows.

FY2025 net income was essentially flat at $2.09B (-0.6%). GAAP operating margin fell to 29.9% from 32.9%, dragged by a $177.3M restructuring charge ($127.3M of accelerated depreciation and impairment on real estate slated for disposal, plus severance). Adjusted operating margin held up better and adjusted EPS rose to $9.72 from $9.33.

The central structural metric is the effective fee rate: 41.9 bps (2023) → 41.0 (2024) → 39.4 (2025) → 38.4 (Q1 2026). The 3.5 bps of erosion reflects client flows shifting AUM toward lower-fee products (fixed income, multi-asset, model delivery) and away from high-fee active equity. Revenue rises only because market appreciation on AUM outpaces the combination of fee compression and net outflows. If markets stall while outflows persist, revenue can decline outright.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Altman Z-Score of 4.92 sits deep in the safe zone, D/E is 0.21, equity is $10.86B, and the firm holds ~$3.7B of cash plus ~$6.6B of total cash and TROW-product investments. It maintains no external credit facility, funding everything from operations. Free cash flow was ~$1.48B in FY2025. Q1 2026 returned $628.8M including 3.7M shares repurchased at an average $91.16 — well below the current price, a favorable mark on the timing of those buybacks. Share count fell ~2% YoY. The dividend, a 39-year increase streak, is comfortably covered by FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is weak on any organic measure, and this is where the thesis is most exposed. Net outflows have been persistent and large: -$81.8B (2023), -$43.2B (2024), -$56.9B (2025), and -$13.7B in Q1 2026, driven overwhelmingly by growth-oriented equity strategies. News through mid-2026 confirms the pattern continued: -$10.6B in April 2026 and -$6.5B for Q2 2026, the latter prompting a stock decline on the report.

Investment performance is doing the franchise no favors in the near term. On an AUM-weighted basis versus passive peers, only 28% of equity AUM outperformed over one year and 33% over five years, with the Q1 2026 one-year figure a stark 6%. Ten-year numbers remain respectable, but redemptions respond to recent performance, and weak near-term equity results feed the outflow cycle.

The offsets are the target-date retirement franchise, which continues to attract systematic flows and represents the firm's most durable growth vector, and the alternatives buildout via OHA. The alternatives thesis is real but progressing more slowly and expensively than underwritten; the OHA earnout has been written to zero and trade-name impairments were taken in 2024, even as management extended the OHA commitment to 2030 with $249M remaining.

Estimate revisions have turned positive: current quarter +9.6% (30d) / +8.8% (90d), current year +6.6% / +7.5%, next year +7.7% / +8.5%. This is a meaningful tailwind and reflects the market-recovery-driven AUM rebound plus the restructuring-aided margin improvement. It does not warrant an upgraded growth score: the revisions are modest in magnitude and reflect beta (recovering markets lifting AUM) layered on cost control, not organic asset gathering. The firm beat in three of the last four quarters (Q3 2025 +9.7%, Q1 2026 +5.0%), which lends confidence to the current consensus, but the beats have not translated into a durable growth narrative.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$122 and a trailing/forward P/E of ~13x on $9.24 trailing EPS (and ~12x the $10.17 current-year consensus), TROW screens optically cheap. Against the peer group, the discount is stark: the Asset Management & Custody Banks peer median P/E is 30.0x, with ARES at 61.7x, CG at 30.0x, BEN at 24.3x, and the closest structural comparable, NTRS, at 17.9x. TROW's ~4.4% yield sits above the peer median 3.7%.

The discount is deserved rather than a mispricing, which is the key judgment. TROW trades where it does because the market correctly prices a structurally declining fee rate, persistent equity outflows, and mixed near-term performance against peers that own scaled passive and alternatives platforms. The five-year TSR gap makes this explicit: TROW's cumulative TSR of 84.1 versus the peer group's 206.4 means shareholders have materially underperformed the industry over five years. A low multiple that reflects genuine structural impairment is a value trap absent a recovery catalyst, and there is no organic-growth catalyst on the horizon.

The support for the current price is real but bounded: the covered yield, the balance sheet, and buyback-driven EPS accretion. Those elements underpin a floor but do not drive a re-rating. Analyst targets frame the ceiling more than the floor for this name — mean $110, median $108.5, high $124, low $86 — with the stock at ~$122 already trading above the mean and median. The targets reflect the same structural realities the price does; proximity to the high target ($124) suggests limited near-term upside rather than a stale consensus about to catch up. The stock has moved from a genuine discount at $86-95 in Q1 2026 to fair-to-slightly-full value today. The valuation score reflects that the setup which justified accumulation earlier this year has largely played out.

## Competitive Landscape

TROW sits directly in the path of the secular active-to-passive shift, and its own filings concede that investors "increasingly favor lower-fee passive products" and that passive-emphasizing competitors have gained and may continue to gain share. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, not stable: the firm is losing high-fee equity assets every year and defending revenue only through market appreciation.

The defensible pillars are the target-date/retirement franchise, which is genuinely sticky and still growing given its embedding in DC plans and recordkeeping relationships; the alternatives diversification through OHA; and selective fee cuts. These slow the erosion rather than reverse it. Competitors with scaled passive platforms (BlackRock most prominently) have delivered vastly superior shareholder returns, reflecting the market's preference for scaled passive and alternatives franchises over traditional active-equity houses. TROW's own risk disclosures now flag lower-cost AI-driven managers as a threat to the human-managed active model that is the firm's core.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the interaction of two structural forces: persistent equity net outflows and declining effective fee rates. Individually each is manageable; together, in a scenario where equity markets flatten or fall, they would drive revenue lower with high operating leverage in the wrong direction. This is the scenario the market is guarding against, and it is largely priced into the ~13x multiple.

Secondary risks include: advisory contracts terminable on short notice with annual board re-approval (a standard industry feature but a genuine tail risk); ~$2.64B of goodwill and intangibles carrying impairment risk, with the OHA earnout already zeroed as a warning of underperformance versus the 2021 deal thesis; and the newly emphasized disclosures around AI competition, government-shutdown-related SEC product-approval delays, and post-Chevron (Loper Bright) regulatory uncertainty. No material risk-factor changes were flagged in the Q1 2026 10-Q.

The clustered Item 5.02 leadership transitions (Oct/Nov 2025 and April/May 2026) warrant monitoring, though the large June 2026 grant to Sebastien Page (Head of Global Multi-Asset / CIO) is consistent with an expanded, constructive leadership role rather than disruptive turnover. On balance, most of the downside is already reflected in valuation and there is asymmetric recovery support from the balance sheet and yield, which keeps this from being a low-risk score, but the outflow/fee-compression combination is a live, non-priced-out tail if markets weaken.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 30.2% is modestly rich versus 24.7% one-year historical vol (spread 5.5%), with an IV percentile of 41%: the market is pricing mild elevated uncertainty, not a directional bet. The near-term put/call skew is balanced (0.2%), but the six-month term shows a -3.7% put skew and the put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.56 to 0.89 since late May, indicating accumulating downside hedging further out. Combined with the 14.5% short interest and 11.1 days to cover, institutional positioning leans defensive. The elevated short interest is worth flagging against the HOLD: it reflects the structural bear case but also creates modest squeeze potential on any positive AUM or flow surprise, and at 14.5% it is not crowded enough to make the short mechanically dangerous.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$130-140).** Equity markets continue their recovery, lifting average AUM and driving the market-appreciation engine that has carried revenue. Estimate revisions extend their positive run, and the restructuring-driven margin discipline (2026 non-GAAP expense growth guided 3-6%) converts AUM gains into operating leverage. The target-date franchise keeps gathering assets, alternatives inflows accelerate off a low base, and the yield plus a ~2%/year buyback compound into low-double-digit total returns. In this scenario the multiple holds at ~13-14x on rising EPS toward $10.50-11.00, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the low $130s.

**Bear Case (~$90, ~-26%).** Equity markets stall or correct. With no organic-flow cushion, AUM falls, and the fee-rate erosion plus continued net outflows drive revenue down with negative operating leverage. Investment performance stays weak, accelerating redemptions. Goodwill/intangible impairments on the alternatives book surface. EPS retreats and the multiple compresses back toward the ~10-11x it saw in Q1 2026, pulling the stock toward $90, near the analyst low target of $86.

The standoff between these cases is why the stock trades at a deep peer discount, and why the current risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month horizon, tied to quarterly AUM/flow reports and the December Fed decision that will shape the equity-market backdrop driving AUM.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A durable turn to positive net flows for two consecutive quarters, particularly any stabilization in equity outflows, signaling the structural bleed is arresting.
- Effective fee rate stabilizing at or above 38 bps for multiple quarters, indicating the mix shift has run its course.
- A pullback toward the low-$100s or below that restores the discount to intrinsic value (i.e., toward the Q1 2026 accumulation zone in the low-to-mid $90s), improving the yield-plus-buyback total-return math.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net outflows accelerating materially beyond the recent -$6-13B quarterly cadence combined with an equity-market drawdown, which would drive revenue lower with negative operating leverage.
- A guidance-level cut, a goodwill/intangible impairment on the OHA/alternatives book, or any signal of dividend-coverage stress (none currently — FCF comfortably covers the payout).
- Consensus estimate revisions turning negative and the current-year figure rolling back below ~$9.50.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release and the accompanying monthly AUM/flow disclosures, plus the December FOMC decision.

## Conclusion

T. Rowe Price is a high-quality balance sheet attached to a structurally challenged business. The capital position, FCF, and dividend streak are genuine and durable, and the recent positive estimate revisions plus the Q1 margin rebound justify owning the shares for income. But reported revenue growth is a market-appreciation artifact layered over persistent equity outflows and a fee rate that has fallen 3.5 bps in three years, and there is no organic-growth catalyst to re-rate the multiple. The ~15% rally off the March low has converted the earlier discount into fair-to-slightly-full value against an analyst mean target of $110 and the stock already at ~$122.

The score trajectory — declining from 6.1 to 5.0 with a prior downgrade to AVOID — was appropriate at a lower price where the setup offered no compensation. At today's price and with rising estimates, the calculus shifts to a balanced income HOLD: the yield and buyback support a floor, but the structural headwinds cap the upside. Own it for coupon-like total return in a recovering market, and exit into either a further re-rating toward the high-$120s or on any acceleration of the outflow/fee-compression dynamic. **Recommendation: HOLD (income).**
