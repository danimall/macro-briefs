## Executive Summary

ACM Research designs and manufactures semiconductor cleaning and plating equipment, with 99.6% of revenue generated in mainland China through its 74.6%-owned subsidiary ACM Shanghai (STAR Market-listed). The company is a direct beneficiary of China's forced localization of semiconductor equipment supply chains: U.S. export controls on Applied Materials, Lam Research, and Tokyo Electron have created a domestic vacuum that ACM is filling. Q1 2026 showed revenue re-acceleration to 34% YoY growth, gross margin recovery to 46.5%, and management reaffirmed guidance for $1.08B-$1.175B in full-year revenue (20-30% growth).

However, the stock at $81.31 (56x forward P/E) has appreciated 225% over 12 months while consensus EPS estimates are declining — current year estimates fell 22% over 90 days from $2.15 to $1.68. The company itself was placed on the BIS Entity List in December 2024, creating a paradox where sanctions both benefit ACM (by restricting competitors) and threaten it (by restricting component procurement). Free cash flow was -$67M in 2025 due to massive working capital absorption. The balance sheet shows $1.13B in cash, but most is trapped in China with limited repatriation pathways. Insiders have exclusively exercised options and sold shares — no open-market purchases have occurred.

The risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable: the growth story is real, but the stock price has run far ahead of fundamentals during a period of estimate compression, and the geopolitical risk is existential rather than incremental.

## Company Overview

ACM Research (Delaware, 1998) operates primarily through ACM Shanghai, which manufactures semiconductor process equipment at Lingang facilities in Shanghai. Core products include single-wafer wet cleaning tools (SAPS, TEBO, Tahoe technologies), electrochemical plating (ECP), vertical furnaces, and emerging platforms in PECVD, track coat/develop, and advanced packaging.

The corporate structure is complex: ACM Research owns 74.6% of a separately-listed Chinese subsidiary that generates virtually all revenue. ACM Shanghai has its own board, fiduciary obligations to STAR Market minority shareholders, and is subject to Chinese capital controls. The U.S. parent has minimal direct operations — an Oregon facility is under construction but not yet operational.

Market cap stands at ~$5.0B on 61.2M shares outstanding. Dual-class stock (Class B = 20 votes/share) gives insiders 62.3% voting control. Four customers account for 52.2% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $901M in FY2025 (+15% YoY), decelerating sharply from +40% in 2024 and +43% in 2023. Q1 2026 showed re-acceleration to $231M (+34% YoY), driven by ECP revenue tripling and cleaning backlog growth. Full-year 2026 guidance implies $1.08B-$1.175B.

**Margins:** Gross margin compressed 570bp in 2025 to 44.4% from 50.1%, driven by unfavorable product mix and a $15.5M inventory provision (up from $2.8M in 2024). Q1 2026 recovered to 46.5%. Operating margin dropped to 12.1% from 19.3% in 2024; Q1 2026 recovered to 18.1%.

**Profitability:** Net income attributable to ACMR declined to $66.3M in 2025 from $76M in 2024. EPS of $1.37 (trailing). Adjusted operating income fell 29% to $143M.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was -$10.3M in FY2025, a dramatic reversal from +$152.5M in 2024. Drivers: inventory build of $108M, AR growth of $116M, and $61M decline in customer advances. FCF was -$66.6M (FCF margin: -7.4%). Q1 2026 OCF was -$29.5M. Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 is $175M, 3x the 2025 level.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash and time deposits of $1.13B (boosted by ACM Shanghai's $623M private offering). Net cash ~$924M. Inventory of $738M (~82% of quarterly revenue run-rate). Accounts receivable $504M. Current ratio 3.27x. Altman Z-Score 2.79 (grey zone).

The cash position looks robust in aggregate, but the "cash trap" structure means most is effectively restricted to Chinese operations due to: (1) PRC capital controls, (2) STAR IPO/private offering use restrictions, (3) statutory reserve requirements, and (4) ACM Shanghai's independent fiduciary obligations.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on China's semiconductor equipment localization trajectory. Domestic vendors captured 35% of China equipment sales in 2025 (up from 23% in 2021), and U.S./Japan/Netherlands export restrictions are accelerating this trend. ACM's specific growth drivers:

1. **Cleaning tools (core):** Backlog up ~50% YoY in H1 2026. SPM cleaning ramp with 15-20 units expected delivered by year-end 2026.
2. **ECP:** Tripled YoY in Q1 2026. Both front-end and advanced packaging applications.
3. **New platforms:** PECVD (first system shipped April 2026), Track tools progressing to mass production qualification, vertical furnaces gaining traction.
4. **Capacity expansion:** Lingang second building opening later 2026, together supporting $3B annual output capacity vs. current ~$1B run-rate.

However, estimate revisions tell a different story. Current-year EPS consensus declined from $2.15 to $1.68 over 90 days (-21.8%). Next-quarter estimates fell 20.2% in the last 30 days alone. The earnings surprise record is mixed: 2 beats, 2 misses over the last 4 quarters, including consecutive misses of -34% and -39% in Q3 and Q4 2025 before the Q1 2026 beat of +70%.

The deceleration in 2025 (revenue growth slowed from 40% to 15%; shipments actually declined from $973M to $854M) raises questions about the sustainability of China's equipment investment cycle. First-tool shipments of $388M represent tools under evaluation with no guaranteed conversion to repeat orders.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.31, ACMR trades at 56x trailing and forward P/E on $1.37 EPS. On consensus 2026 EPS of $1.68, the stock trades at 48x. On 2027 estimates of $2.25, it trades at 36x.

**Peer comparison:** The semiconductor equipment peer group shows wide dispersion — Axcelis at 47x, Kulicke & Soffa at 98x, Veeco at 153x. The peer median of ~97x makes ACMR look cheap optically, but this comparison is misleading: the peers have minimal China exposure and different risk profiles. ACM's geopolitical risk premium should compress the multiple relative to peers, not expand it.

**The disconnect:** The stock has appreciated 225% in 12 months while EPS estimates declined 22%. This divergence is unsustainable — either estimates must inflect sharply higher to justify the price, or the price must correct. The Q1 2026 beat (+70%) catalyzed the recent surge from ~$55 to $81, but a single quarter doesn't confirm a trend given the two prior consecutive misses.

**STAR Market discount:** ACM Shanghai trades at an implied ~$14.65B valuation for 100% equity on the STAR Market. ACMR's $5B U.S. market cap represents 74.6% of that, implying ~$6.7B proportionate value vs. $5B price — a ~25% discount. This discount is rational given: trapped cash, Entity List risk, HFCA Act delisting risk, and dual-class governance.

At 48x current-year earnings with negative estimate momentum and existential geopolitical risk, the valuation leaves minimal margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

ACM operates in a favorable niche: it's one of the leading domestic Chinese equipment vendors in a market forcibly transitioning from foreign to domestic supply.

**Tailwinds:** U.S. export controls on AMAT/LRCX/TEL create an artificial void that domestic Chinese suppliers are filling. China's government policy mandates equipment localization. ACM's cleaning technology (particularly SAPS megasonic cleaning) is competitive at mature and mid-critical nodes.

**Limitations:** ACM is not yet competitive at leading-edge nodes (sub-5nm) where contamination requirements are most stringent. Global competitors retain technology leadership. ACM's own Entity List designation restricts its ability to source U.S.-origin components, potentially limiting technology advancement.

**Domestic competition:** NAURA, AMEC, Hwatsing, Piotech, and Kingsemi are all pursuing the same localization opportunity. The risk of overcapacity in the Chinese domestic equipment market is non-trivial as state-led investment could result in lower demand per vendor than expected.

The competitive trajectory is positive — share gains within China are real and accelerating — but the addressable market outside China remains near-zero ($3.3M of $901M in revenue), and the Oregon facility's ability to compete globally is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Entity List (Existential):** ACM Shanghai's December 2024 Entity List designation restricts procurement of U.S.-origin components. The company has not reported material production disruptions yet, but the risk of expanded restrictions or secondary sanctions on non-U.S. suppliers (Japan, Netherlands) is ongoing. This could fundamentally impair ACM's ability to manufacture competitive products.

**Geographic concentration (Structural):** 99.6% China revenue with 4 customers at 52.2% of total. Any change in Chinese government capex policy, customer insolvency, or relationship loss would be devastating.

**HFCA Act delisting risk:** ACM's auditor (Ernst & Young Hua Ming LLP) is mainland China-based. While PCAOB access has been maintained, political deterioration could trigger inspection denial and eventual Nasdaq delisting.

**Working capital intensity (Operational):** $1.24B in inventory + receivables vs. $901M revenue. Evaluation periods extend 24+ months. The inventory provision surge ($2.8M → $15.5M) suggests potential obsolescence. Negative FCF despite revenue growth indicates the business model consumes cash as it scales.

**Trapped cash (Structural):** The February 2026 minority share sale ($110M) was required specifically to fund U.S. operations — effectively demonstrating the parent's inability to access subsidiary cash.

**Tariff escalation:** U.S. tariffs at 145% on Chinese goods and China's retaliatory tariffs create additional friction for any future global expansion attempts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
China's semiconductor localization is a multi-decade structural trend. ACM's cleaning technology leadership positions it to grow revenue to $1.5B+ by 2027 with operating margins expanding toward 20%+ as new products scale. If 2027 EPS reaches $2.50+ and the market assigns a 35x multiple (growth premium for 25-30% grower), shares could trade above $85-90. The STAR Market valuation gap could close upward if geopolitical tensions ease. New product platforms (SPM, PECVD, Track) represent optionality that could expand the TAM from $3B to $6B+.

### Bear Case
Entity List restrictions tighten, cutting off critical component supply and impairing production capability. Estimate revisions continue lower as China semiconductor overcapacity emerges. Working capital deterioration forces a dilutive equity raise at the parent level. HFCA Act delisting forces conversion to OTC, destroying liquidity and institutional ownership. At 30x bear-case 2027 EPS of $1.50 (reflecting margin compression and growth deceleration), shares trade to $45 — a 45% decline from current levels. More extreme scenarios (full delisting, operational impairment from sanctions) could take shares below $30.

## Conclusion

ACM Research occupies a genuine structural growth position within China's semiconductor equipment localization effort. Revenue re-acceleration in Q1 2026, gross margin recovery, and an expanding product portfolio support a multi-year growth thesis. However, the stock at 56x trailing earnings and 48x current-year estimates has priced in this growth during a period when consensus estimates are declining 20%+ across all forecast horizons.

The risk profile is asymmetric to the downside: the stock has limited upside to consensus targets (median $70, mean $74 vs. current $81.31), while downside scenarios from geopolitical escalation, estimate compression, or structural impairment are severe. Insiders are net sellers. Options markets confirm elevated uncertainty with IV at 116% in backwardation. Negative free cash flow and customer concentration compound the Entity List risk.

HOLD with a reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings or upon resolution of Entity List status.
