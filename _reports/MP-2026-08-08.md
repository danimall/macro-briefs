## Executive Summary

MP Materials is two businesses at very different stages fused into one equity. The Materials segment at Mountain Pass is an operating asset with a 28-year reserve life, 5.89% average ore grade, and, following the July 2025 Department of War (DoW) agreements, a contractual NdPr price floor of $110/kg running through 2035. The Magnetics segment is a construction project: Independence in Fort Worth began NdFeB magnet manufacturing in December 2025, the 10X facility in Northlake, Texas broke ground in 2026 against a >$1.25B budget and 2028 commissioning, and the DoW guarantees 10X will generate at least $140M of EBITDA annually once operating. The equity at $9.10B is priced almost entirely on the second business.

The second quarter was operationally better than the tape suggested. Revenue rose 89% to $108.5M against roughly $99.2M of consensus; adjusted EBITDA swung from −$12.5M to +$28.5M; NdPr sales volumes reached 1,006 MT. Yet three facts constrain how much weight that deserves. First, PPA income of $59.9M in the first half equalled 91.9% of first-half adjusted EBITDA of $65.1M; the reported profitability inflection is dominated by a government price transfer, not by unit economics at current volumes. Second, REO production *fell* 16% year over year in Q2 and 5% in the half, annualizing to roughly 48,100 MT against a stated "Upstream 60K" ambition, while long-dated concentrate inventory rose from $31.7M to $46.0M. Third, consensus EPS has been cut hard: current-year to $0.19 from $0.33 ninety days ago (−42.6%), next year to $0.95 from $1.31 (−27.4%), including a further 8.3% next-year cut in the last thirty days, after the Q2 revenue beat.

At $51.11 the stock trades at ~53.8x next-year consensus EPS, ~70x annualized first-half adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of roughly $9.1B, and 4.65x book value of $10.99/share. Probability-weighted, we get a base case near $56, a bull case near $88 and a bear case near $32, an expected return of roughly +11% against 74% realized volatility and a −37% downside leg. That is a HOLD, and an uncertain one: the contractual floors and the ex-China NdPr shortage are real and cap the bear case above where a normal development-stage miner would trade, but the price requires nearly full execution of a 2030 plan whose funding, equipment supply and dilution are all still open questions. The overall score continues to drift lower (6.1 → 5.6 → 5.3), driven by valuation and risk, not by operating deterioration.

## Company Overview

Mountain Pass, San Bernardino County, is the only rare earth mining and processing site of scale in North America. SRK's reserve statement at 12/31/2025 shows 1.96 million short tons of contained REO within 28.96 million short tons of ore at 5.89% average grade, implying roughly a 28-year mine life. Mining, milling, separations and finishing sit on one site adjacent to I-15, four hours from Los Angeles/Long Beach. The bastnaesite mineralogy carries a process advantage: oxidative roasting converts cerium to a low-solubility tetravalent form that can be removed early, reducing the mass, reagents, energy and wastewater carried through separation.

The company ceased all sales to China in July 2025 and allowed the Shenghe concentrate offtake to lapse on expiry in January 2026. Revenue now comes principally from NdPr oxide and metal sold into the U.S., Japan (via Sumitomo through 2030), Korea and broader Asia, plus cerium, lanthanum and SEG+. Magnetics sells NdPr metal precursor to GM and, from December 2025, sintered NdFeB magnets.

The DoW package is the structural change and warrants precision about who pays whom:

| Instrument | Direction | Key terms |
|---|---|---|
| Price Protection Agreement | **DoW pays MP** the shortfall to **$110/kg** on designated NdPr | 10/1/2025 – 12/31/2035; MP designates Stockpile, Affiliate or Third-party volumes quarterly |
| PPA reverse leg | **MP pays DoW** 30% of excess above $110/kg | Only once 10X reaches full production capacity |
| 10X Offtake | DoW purchases all 10X magnets or syndicates; **guarantees ≥$140M EBITDA**, escalating | 10-year term; embedded lease; DoW reimburses pre-operation development costs |
| Series A Preferred | **DoW paid MP $400M**; owns 400,000 preferred | Convertible into 13,320,013 common at $30.03; liquidation preference ≥$428.1M, senior |
| Warrant | DoW holds warrant on 11,201,659 shares | $30.03 strike, expires 7/10/2035, DoW's settlement election |
| Samarium Project Loan | **DoW lent MP $150M** unsecured | 5.38%, matures 8/1/2037; carried at $87.3M, a $62.7M below-market discount |

In exchange MP committed to 3,000 MT/yr of magnets at Independence, 7,000 MT/yr at 10X, samarium and heavy rare earth (HREE) refining at Mountain Pass, and chlor-alkali recommissioning. A separate July 2025 Apple agreement covers magnets and recycling with $200M of milestone prepayments ($72M received; zero revenue recognized; obligations begin no earlier than 2H 2027).

GAAP diluted EPS was −$0.11 in Q2; adjusted diluted EPS −$0.01. Adjusted net income turned positive at +$4.6M for the half against −$41.3M a year earlier. Multi-year context matters: GAAP net income was +$289.0M in 2022, +$24.3M in 2023, −$65.4M in 2024 and −$85.9M in 2025. FY2025 revenue of $224.4M was roughly 10% above FY2024 and still below the 2021-22 level; MP is rebuilding revenue after losing the concentrate-to-China business and absorbing the 2022-24 NdPr price collapse.

**Earnings quality — the PPA arithmetic.** First-half PPA income of $59.853M is 23.1% of the $258.99M combined top line and 91.9% of adjusted EBITDA. In Q2 alone the ratio was 61.7%. Cash gross margin including PPA was 42.7% in Q2 and 43.4% in the half; excluding PPA it was 33.4% and 26.4% respectively, so the floor contributed roughly 17 points of first-half margin. This is a subtraction, not a counterfactual: absent the floor MP would have made different designation, stockpiling and shipment choices. The floor also cost real money: a $221.1M PPA Upfront Asset amortizes $22.1M per half through DD&A, so the *net* first-half benefit was $37.8M, not $59.9M.

The constructive corollary is that dependence is shrinking fast. Dividing PPA income by NdPr sales volume implies a benchmark ex-China NdPr price of roughly $68/kg in Q1 2026, ~$92.5/kg in Q2, and management's Q3 guide of high-$90s realized pricing with ~$10/kg of PPA implies ~$100/kg. The denominator is designated volume rather than third-party sales, so the derived prices are approximations; that management's independent Q3 guide is consistent with the Q2 derivation supports the method. PPA income has fallen $51.0M (Q4'25) → $42.3M (Q1'26) → $17.6M (Q2'26) and is guided down again. As the ex-China price converges on $110, the transfer disappears and MP retains 100% of the upside above the floor until 10X reaches full capacity; the reverse leg is not live before roughly 2029.

**Volumes.** NdPr production rose 51% in the half to 1,757 MT and NdPr sales 122% to 2,012 MT; the sales figure is flattered because MP changed the oxide-to-metal conversion ratio from 1.20 to 1.25 in Q1 2026 without recasting prior periods. Q2 revenue included $51.3M of bill-and-hold revenue (47.3% of Q2; 47.5% of 1H) where control transfers but product stays in MP's custody, a timing-sensitive component that argues against annualizing Q2 revenue. REO production fell 16% in Q2 and 5% in the half. Management cited an extended planned April shutdown and reliability issues on a limited number of circuits; the simultaneous build in non-current concentrate inventory ($31.7M → $46.0M) is more consistent with deliberate mill throttling because surplus concentrate has no buyer post-China. Either reading points to the same conclusion: separation capacity, not the ore body, is the binding constraint.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Cash and equivalents fell $737M to $429.1M while short-term investments rose $359M to $1,023.6M, a portfolio reallocation. The correct measure is combined liquidity: $1,830.3M → $1,452.6M, a $377.6M decline in six months. That reconciles to OCF +$4.9M, capex −$307.7M, 2026 Notes repayment −$67.5M, other debt −$2.2M, RSU withholding −$18.2M, plus $9.7M from the VREX investee and $4.1M of asset sales. Company-defined free cash flow was −$302.8M versus −$114.1M a year earlier. FY2026 capex guidance is $500-600M net of government awards, implying $192-292M in the second half.

Debt principal at 6/30/26 was ~$1,041.9M: 2030 Notes $862.8M (3.00%, due 3/1/2030), Samarium loan $150.0M, equipment notes $29.1M. The $275M JPMorgan revolver is undrawn. Net debt is negative; interest and investment income contributed $29.3M in the half. Covenants live only in the Credit Agreement: a ≥$500M unrestricted cash test until the earlier of $400M Consolidated EBITDA or fiscal Q2 2027, then total leverage <4.00x and cash interest coverage >3.0x. On the capex plan liquidity stays above $500M through 2027; because the revolver is undrawn, a subsequent trip would cost an unused facility rather than trigger acceleration. We have not verified whether the leverage test is gross or net, nor the permitted add-backs, so we do not treat it as a live risk.

At the current burn, $1.45B of liquid assets funds roughly two more years of the announced program without new capital. That is the central financial-health question: adequate today, not obviously adequate through 2028 commissioning.

**Dilution, stated both ways.** Reported basic and diluted weighted shares were identical at 178.41M because the loss made all potential shares anti-dilutive. The 10-Q separately discloses 66,524,304 excluded shares: 2030 Notes 39.68M at $21.74 (Stock Price Condition met in Q2 2026, so conversion is available during Q3 2026), Series A 13.32M at $30.03, Warrant 11.20M at $30.03, RSUs 1.61M, PSUs 0.71M. That is 37.4% of existing shares, or 27.2% of post-issuance ownership. The 2024-tranche capped calls cover 34.4M shares with a $21.74 strike and $31.06 cap; with the stock at $51.11 the hedge delivers a fixed maximum benefit of roughly 34.4M × $9.32 = $320.6M against conversion value $1,165.4M above the $862.8M principal, leaving net cost above principal near $845M (roughly 9.3% of market cap). MP may elect cash settlement and holds the liquidity to do so, which would consume a meaningful share of the balance sheet. Separately, a November 2025 board-approved evergreen adds 2% of outstanding shares annually 2026-2030, roughly 3.6M shares/year.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is contractually anchored but back-loaded.

**Near-term (next 12 months).** Q3 guidance: NdPr production >1,000 MT, realized NdPr oxide pricing in the high-$90s/kg, PPA income ~$10/kg with a slight sequential decline in total PPA income, Materials volumes "flattish." Terbium and dysprosium production is expected in 2H 2026 after the first heavy separation circuit reached mechanical completion in May. First commercial magnet shipments to GM are guided to Q4 2026 following in-vehicle qualification testing in Q2. A multi-year gadolinium oxide supply agreement with a U.S. aerospace and defense manufacturer was described on the call as a sizable nine-figure deal over multiple years with locked-in economics; it is not quantified in the 10-Q, and "nine figures cumulative" could be modest annually.

Working against this: the GM precursor stream, which carried >40% adjusted EBITDA margins in Q2, is being deliberately wound down as the remaining $45.5M of the $150M prepayment is delivered and metal capacity redirects to MP's own magnets. Apple contributes zero revenue before 2H 2027. Start-up costs ran $14.4M in Q2 versus $0.8M a year earlier and will rise as Independence and 10X ramp. Management expects benefits from throughput, process efficiency, lower maintenance intensity and the chlor-alkali restart to "build progressively through 2027," language that concedes 2026 is a transition year.

**Medium-term (2028-2031).** The valuation rests here: 10,000 MT/yr of combined magnet capacity, 10X carrying a DoW-guaranteed floor of ≥$140M EBITDA escalating ~2%/yr, HREE (Tb/Dy, samarium 2028, gadolinium), recycling, third-party feedstock tolling through the HREE circuit, and a Materials segment nearer nameplate. A fully executed 2030 scenario plausibly supports several hundred million to roughly $1B of EBITDA. Upside on 10X is shared: per the FY2025 MD&A, DoW takes the first ~$30M of EBITDA above the guaranteed level and 50% of further excess, and the PPA reverse leg gives DoW 30% of NdPr price above $110/kg once 10X is at full capacity.

**Estimate momentum contradicts the narrative.** Consensus current-year EPS fell to $0.19 from $0.23 thirty days ago and $0.33 ninety days ago; next quarter to $0.07 from $0.14; next year to $0.95 from $1.31. Revisions of this direction and magnitude, cut further *after* an ~9.4% revenue beat, indicate that the sell side is marking down PPA income, absorbing start-up costs and pushing magnet economics out in time. This is the mirror image of the estimate-revision signal that supports buying inflections. Growth prospects remain good in level; they are deteriorating in near-term trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value: $9,102M market cap + $1,042M debt principal + $414M preferred carrying − $1,453M cash and short-term investments ≈ **$9,105M**. Treating the preferred as equity gives ~$8,691M.

| Metric | Value |

| EV / Q2-annualized revenue ($434M) | 21.0x |
| EV / 1H-annualized adj. EBITDA ($130M) | 70.0x |
| EV / Q2-annualized adj. EBITDA ($114M) | 79.9x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($0.95) | 53.8x |
| P/E on current-year consensus ($0.19) | 269x |
| Price / book ($10.99/share) | 4.65x |

We are deliberately treating these as sensitivities rather than run rates: Q2 contained $51.3M of bill-and-hold revenue, roughly 650 MT of NdPr was on hand/in transit/at toll processors at quarter-end, the NdPr benchmark is rising, and $14.4M of start-up costs are transitional. Even on generous normalization the multiples are extreme relative to any producing-miner benchmark.

**Reverse-engineering the price.** The only way to reconcile $9.1B of EV with ~$130M of current adjusted EBITDA is the 2029-31 build. Take a mid-bull 2030 outcome of $800M EBITDA at 12x = $9.6B EV. Assume the 2030 Notes convert (39.7M shares) and DoW exercises (24.5M), plus five years of evergreen grants (~18M), giving roughly 260M shares against approximately zero net debt after the capex program. That is $37/share in 2030, which discounted at 12% for four and a half years is roughly $22 today. At $1B of 2030 EBITDA and a 15x multiple, the same arithmetic gives ~$58 in 2030 and ~$35 today. To justify $51.11 on a discounted basis you need close to $1B of 2030 EBITDA, a 15x+ multiple, minimal dilution and a lower discount rate than a 74%-vol single-site development story warrants.

**What offsets that.** The $110/kg floor through 2035 is a genuine contractual asymmetry no other rare earth producer has, and it operates *without* an upside cap on Materials until 10X reaches full capacity. If the ex-China NdPr price continues from ~$100 toward $150/kg on Chinese export restrictions, Materials economics transform with MP keeping the whole increment for the next several years. The DoW $140M EBITDA guarantee on 10X is an unusual downside truncation for a greenfield plant. There is also a strategic-scarcity premium in the price that a DCF will not capture: MP is the only U.S. asset of its kind, and buyers of that optionality are not clearing at 12x.

**Peer and analyst context.** Sell-side coverage is uniformly bullish: 18 ratings, all Buy or Strong Buy, none Hold, stable for four months, with targets from $60 to $100, mean $77.47, median $80. We do not treat those targets as independent confirmation. When a stock trades above 50x next-year consensus and that consensus is being cut 8-18% per month, targets and price reflect the same growth extrapolation; the targets are more likely stale on the downside than a floor.

Valuation score 3.8: not a value trap, but a price that demands near-complete execution with the identified catalyst several years out and shared upside when it arrives.

## Competitive Landscape

Global REO demand was roughly 252,000 MT in 2025 with a projected ~6.0% CAGR to 2040 and ~8.4% for NdPr. Two state-consolidated Chinese groups control substantially all Chinese concentrate and refining quota; outside China there is one other integrated operator at scale (Lynas, Australia/Malaysia). MP's structural position is strong: single high-grade co-located site, established separation capability, and the only U.S. magnet capacity being built at scale.

**China is now a two-sided force, and the sides pull in opposite directions.** On demand, Chinese export controls have created a severe ex-China shortage: negligible May magnet-rare-earth exports to Japan, a July ban on shipments to 14 European companies in chemicals, electric motors and defense, and reported spillover into the broader Japanese economy. MP sells into exactly those markets, which is the most plausible mechanism behind the $68 → ~$100/kg benchmark move across three quarters and behind management's statement that demand continues to outpace production growth. On inputs, MOFCOM added MP Materials to China's export control list on June 22, 2026 (confirmed across five outlets). MP no longer sells to China so direct sales impact is limited; the exposure is on the input side, plausibly reaching third parties reselling Chinese-origin product and equipment containing Chinese components, which bears directly on the 10X equipment schedule and the Independence ramp. Neither the 10-Q MD&A nor the Q2 call quantified it. MP shares fell 13.4% in June, most of it after this news.

**Competition is multiplying and government support is not exclusive.** USA Rare Earth produced commercial-grade dysprosium and NdPr oxide from recycled scrap in July; Ucore shipped NdPr qualification samples; Evolution Metals took first non-China NdPr metal from SRE Vietnam. The Pentagon extended a $150M conditional loan to Niron Magnetics for iron-nitride magnets that use no rare earths at all: the same counterparty underwriting MP's price floor and magnet offtake is concurrently funding a potential substitute and competing separators. That is long-dated but consistent with MP's own listed risk regarding its ability to compete with substitutions.

**Customer concentration worsened while mix diversified.** Q2 2026 top three customers were 40%, 32% and 15% = 87% of revenue, versus 82% in Q2 2025. The product and geographic mix genuinely improved (Chinese concentrate → NdPr oxide/metal into the U.S., Japan and Korea, plus Magnetics); customer concentration, by MP's own disclosure, got worse. A distributor relationship also means true end-demand sits one layer beyond MP's disclosure. Counterparty identities are not disclosed.

Competitive position score 6.5: a durable and genuinely scarce asset base, with a moat that is being widened operationally but eroded at the edges by policy-funded competitors, substitution research, and worsening customer concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Counterparty and policy concentration.** MP's economics depend on one entity acting simultaneously as price-floor guarantor, magnet offtaker, EBITDA guarantor, lender, preferred holder, warrant holder and grantor. The FY2025 10-K flags that the structure relies on an unconventional application of DPA Title III authority, that DoW may need to secure additional funds, and that the arrangements could be challenged. The Q2 forward-looking list leads with funding, support and challenges to the DoW transactions and MP's ability to obtain replacement funding. There is no obvious hedge for this.

**China export-control listing, unquantified and not addressed in risk factors.** The 10-Q states there have been no material changes to 10-K risk factors, notwithstanding the June 22 listing inside the quarter. Management's likely position, that existing Chinese trade-policy and sourcing language already covers it, is defensible, and the absence of quantification may mean no material impact to date. This is unresolved either way, and the exposure sits precisely on the equipment path for 10X.

**Single-site operations.** The 10-Q states revenue comes primarily from a single site in a single location and that any stoppage could adversely impact production. Q2 already absorbed an extended planned April shutdown and circuit reliability issues.

**Magnet execution and IP.** MP has never made NdFeB magnets at commercial scale. The forward-looking list explicitly cites uncertainty about obtaining IP rights or licenses to produce certain NdFeB magnets and precursors, a real hurdle given how concentrated sintered-magnet know-how is in Japanese and Chinese hands. GM commercial shipments are still ahead, not behind.

**Dilution mechanics turning live.** The 2030 Notes' Stock Price Condition was met in Q2 2026, making conversion available during Q3; fair value is $2,314.6M against $847.3M carrying. DoW's Series A plus Warrant equal 24.52M shares (13.8% of existing; 12.1% of post-issuance ownership) deep in the money at a $30.03 strike. The evergreen adds ~2%/yr.

**Governance and compensation.** CEO total compensation went from $6.04M (2024) to $28.01M (2025) on $224.4M of revenue and a $85.9M GAAP loss; five-year cumulative TSR of $157.04 trailed the company's own selected peer index at $169.21. The March 2026 PSU design benchmarks TSR against the S&P 500 for a stock whose own valuation model assumes 76.2% expected volatility, so outperformance is substantially a function of beta and the rare earth price cycle rather than execution. The mark-to-market "compensation actually paid" figure of $50.81M is a disclosure artifact of the 2025 share price rise and should not be read as a pay decision; the $28.0M grant-date figure is a decision.

**Insider divergence.** CEO Litinsky sold roughly 1,185,167 shares at $64.03-$71.31 between 4/17 and 6/3/2026 for approximately $77.9M gross, cutting his stake ~9.3% to ~11.6M shares (~6.5% of the company); the CFO sold ~$4.26M and the general counsel three tranches. Against that, COO Rosenthal made two open-market purchases — 17,000 at $56.62 on 5/20 and 10,000 at $54.30 on 6/9, roughly $1.51M — at prices within 6-11% of today's. We do not infer motive and cannot confirm whether the sales were pre-arranged 10b5-1 executions; two reported-holdings inconsistencies in the Form 4 record suggest data quality issues. Insider selling is low-signal when contradicted by rising estimates. Here estimates are falling sharply, which raises the weight the selling deserves. The COO's purchases are the higher-information element and partially offset it.

Risk score 4.0, unchanged from the prior review: nothing material has been resolved, and the China listing and live convert conversion are new.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+72%)

Chinese export controls persist or tighten, ex-China NdPr moves from ~$100 toward $140-150/kg, and MP captures the full increment because the PPA reverse leg does not activate until 10X reaches full capacity. Midstream debottlenecking plus the chlor-alkali restart lifts NdPr production toward 4,000-5,000 MT/yr. Tb/Dy production starts on schedule in 2H 2026 and the HREE circuit's third-party feedstock capability adds tolling revenue. First GM magnet shipments in Q4 2026 validate the Magnetics process; Apple ramps from 2H 2027; the nine-figure gadolinium agreement and Project Swarm drone aggregation convert into contracted volume. 10X stays on its 2028 commissioning path despite Chinese equipment controls. By 2028-29 EBITDA reaches $600M+, the market applies 25-30x to a monopoly U.S. asset with a government EBITDA floor, and the equity trades in the high-$80s to $100 range even after convert dilution.

### Bear Case — $32 (−37%)

Rare earth oversupply concerns validated by the IEA outlook prove correct, ex-China NdPr retreats toward $70-80/kg, and MP reverts to a business where over 90% of adjusted EBITDA is a government transfer with declining volumes behind it. Chinese input controls delay 10X equipment into 2029-30 while capex continues at $500-600M/yr, consuming liquidity and forcing either an equity raise or cash settlement of the 2030 Notes at roughly $845M above principal. Upstream REO production continues to decline and the concentrate stockpile keeps building. Magnet qualification slips past Q4 2026 and IP licensing proves harder than expected. Estimates keep falling from the already-cut $0.95 next-year base. The 17.4% short base presses; the stock revisits and breaches the July low of $37.81 toward 3x book value near $32.

### Base Case — $56 (+9.6%)

Execution proceeds broadly on plan but slower and more expensively than the plan implies. Ex-China NdPr stabilizes at $100-115/kg, PPA income fades to near zero through 2027 and is roughly offset by higher realized pricing. NdPr production reaches ~4,000 MT annualized by late 2027; Tb/Dy and gadolinium add modest revenue; Independence reaches ~1,000 MT of magnets with variable quarterly results as management has warned. FY2027 revenue lands in the $500-600M range with adjusted EBITDA of $200-250M, still with deeply negative free cash flow. The market continues paying a strategic premium of 35-45x EBITDA for the 2030 optionality, valuing the equity in the mid-$40s to mid-$50s. Probability-weighted at 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull, expected value is approximately $56, or +9.6% from $51.11.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders retain exposure on the contractual $110/kg floor, the DoW 10X EBITDA guarantee, negative net debt and a structural ex-China shortage that is visibly lifting realized pricing. New capital has no margin of safety at 53.8x next-year consensus with consensus falling 8.3% in thirty days and 27.4% in ninety, and with the first contractually guaranteed magnet dollar arriving no earlier than 2028 commissioning. An expected +9.6% against a −37% bear leg and 74% realized volatility is not adequate compensation for initiating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026), which should show whether NdPr production exceeded 1,000 MT, whether realized pricing landed in the high-$90s, and whether first GM commercial magnet shipments remain on track for Q4 2026. A secondary checkpoint is any 8-K or 10-Q disclosure quantifying the June 22 China export-control impact on 10X equipment procurement.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of ex-PPA adjusted EBITDA above $30M per quarter — i.e. the operating business standing on its own before the government transfer. First-half ex-PPA adjusted EBITDA was approximately $5M, so this is a real threshold.
2. REO production returning above 13,000 MT per quarter (roughly 52,000 MT annualized) alongside NdPr production above 1,100 MT, confirming the upstream decline was maintenance-driven rather than structural, with non-current concentrate inventory flat or falling.
3. Confirmed first commercial magnet revenue from GM in Q4 2026 plus a quantified gadolinium/HREE contract in the filings, and an explicit management statement that 10X long-lead equipment is not exposed to Chinese export controls.
4. Price below $40 with the above fundamentals intact — at $40 the base case offers roughly 40% upside against a $32 bear, which would clear the ACCUMULATE bar.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Next-year consensus EPS cut below $0.75, or a third consecutive quarter of double-digit downward revisions, without an offsetting increase in disclosed contracted volume.
2. Any announced slippage of 10X commissioning beyond 2028, or disclosure that Chinese export controls have affected equipment delivery schedules.
3. An equity or equity-linked raise, or combined cash plus short-term investments falling below $900M before the FY2026 10-K, which would materially compress the funding runway ahead of the 2027 covenant step-up.
4. Realized NdPr pricing falling back below $85/kg for two consecutive quarters, which would restore PPA dependence above 80% of adjusted EBITDA.
5. Clustered selling by three or more C-suite insiders within thirty days alongside falling estimates.

## Conclusion

MP Materials holds an asset that cannot be replicated in the United States within a decade and has negotiated one of the more favorable government support packages in recent industrial policy: a $110/kg NdPr floor through 2035, a $140M EBITDA guarantee on a plant it has not yet built, a $400M preferred at a $30.03 conversion price, a below-market $150M loan carried at $87.3M, $200M of Apple prepayments and roughly $200M of Texas incentives. Management deserves credit for that structuring. The second quarter was a genuine operational step forward: revenue up 89% and roughly 9.4% above consensus, adjusted EBITDA positive by $28.5M against a $12.5M loss a year earlier, adjusted net income positive for the half.

The problem is price against evidence. Roughly 92% of first-half adjusted EBITDA came from the government price transfer; upstream REO production is shrinking while long-dated concentrate stockpiles build; free cash flow was −$302.8M in the half against $500-600M of full-year capex; maximum contingent dilution is 37.4% of existing shares with the 2030 Notes' conversion condition now met; customer concentration worsened to 87% of revenue from three unnamed counterparties; China placed MP on its export control list inside the quarter and the company has neither updated its risk factors nor quantified the exposure; and consensus EPS has been cut 42.6% for this year and 27.4% for next, including further cuts after the Q2 beat. At $51.11 the stock trades at 53.8x next-year consensus and roughly 70x annualized first-half adjusted EBITDA, and a discounted 2030 build-out scenario supports $22-35 unless you assume close to $1B of EBITDA at a 15x multiple with minimal dilution.

Overall score 5.3, extending the decline from 6.1 in March, driven by valuation and unresolved risk rather than by operating deterioration. The rating is HOLD, and we classify it as an uncertain hold rather than a growth or income hold: the contractual floors are real enough to make shorting a poor idea into a rising ex-China price, and the multiple is demanding enough to make buying a poor idea into falling estimates. Over the next twelve months, watch ex-PPA adjusted EBITDA, upstream production, and whether Chinese equipment controls touch the 10X schedule.
