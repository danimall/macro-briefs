**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Newell Brands is a global consumer-products conglomerate (Sharpie, Rubbermaid, Coleman, Graco, Yankee Candle) carrying $9.46B of 2022 revenue that has eroded to $7.20B in FY25, sitting on $4.7-5.0B of debt against $2.39B of book equity depressed by a $3.23B retained deficit. The equity is a leveraged claim on a deleveraging turnaround: with Altman Z of 1.19 (distress zone), a B2 senior-unsecured credit rating, a weighted-average interest rate of 6.9%, and a secured-revolver leverage covenant that steps down on September 30, 2026, the investment case turns less on operating quality than on covenant survival and refinancing the 2027-28 maturity wall without distress.

The turnaround is showing real signs of life. Q1 2026 delivered core sales growth above plan across all three segments, gross margin up 100bps to 33.1%, and operating income up 62% to $34M, prompting management to raise full-year sales guidance to flat-to-+2% and reiterate normalized EPS of $0.56-$0.60. The Learning & Development segment (Sharpie/Parker writing plus Graco/NUK baby) is a genuinely high-quality asset — 17.2% operating margin and $464M of operating income in FY25, arguably worth more than the company's entire $2.1B equity value on its own. But the Q1 beat leaned on a $25M one-time favorable customer-program true-up, a $42M FX tailwind, and retailer order timing shifting into the quarter, so the underlying run-rate improvement is smaller than the headline suggests, and consolidated revenue is still shrinking.

We rate NWL AVOID. The stock has rallied roughly 50% off its March 2026 low near $3.30 to ~$5.03, restoring the equity to fair-to-full value against a capital structure with no margin for error. Free cash flow of $17M in FY25 cannot cover the ~$140M annual dividend, which management continues to pay, prioritizing signaling over deleveraging. The September 30 covenant step-down is a binary event the company itself concedes depends on operating performance it cannot fully control; a breach absent waiver triggers cross-default and acceleration across all debt. The risk/reward at $5 is unattractive relative to the tail.

## Company Overview

Newell (founded 1903, Delaware-incorporated, Atlanta HQ) sells in over 150 countries with roughly 21,900 employees, organized into three reporting segments:

- **Home & Commercial Solutions (H&CS)** — ~52% of sales ($3.77B FY25). Rubbermaid, Calphalon, Crock-Pot, Mr. Coffee, Oster, FoodSaver, Sistema, Yankee Candle, WoodWick. The structurally weakest segment: an FY25 operating loss of $138M (-3.7% margin).
- **Learning & Development (L&D)** — ~37% of sales ($2.69B FY25). Sharpie, Paper Mate, Parker, EXPO, Elmer's, Dymo, Prismacolor (Writing); Graco, NUK, Aprica (Baby). The crown jewel at 17.2% operating margin and $464M operating income.
- **Outdoor & Recreation (O&R)** — ~10% of sales ($741M FY25). Coleman, Marmot, Contigo, Campingaz. Loss-making but improving (FY25 operating loss -$25M vs -$86M FY24).

Distribution runs through concentrated big-box and e-commerce channels. Amazon is now Newell's largest customer at ~17% of sales (up from 13% in 2023); Walmart is ~13%. The company's stated moat rests on scale, brand equity, innovation cadence, and a differentiated U.S./Mexico manufacturing footprint (15 U.S. plants) that management frames as a tariff advantage over import-reliant competitors.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a multi-year secular revenue decline layered on a highly leveraged balance sheet.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $9.46B | $8.13B | $7.58B | $7.20B |
| Gross margin | 30.0% | 28.9% | 33.6% | 33.8% |
| Operating income | $312M | -$85M | $67M | $39M |
| Net income | $197M | -$388M | -$216M | -$285M |
| Diluted EPS | $0.47 | -$0.94 | -$0.52 | -$0.68 |

**Revenue has fallen ~24% since 2022**, driven by soft demand, distribution losses, and deliberate SKU/product-line exits. All three segments declined in FY25.

**Margin recovery is real but fragile.** Gross margin rebuilt from ~29% to ~34% through productivity and pricing, but roughly $114M of FY25 tariff costs plus inflation are eating into gains. FY25 operating income of just $39M (0.5% margin) was overwhelmed by $340M of non-cash tradename impairments, $90M of restructuring, and $321M of interest expense, producing a fourth consecutive year without GAAP profit (2022 excepted).

**The balance sheet is the central concern.** Total debt of ~$4.7B at FY25 rose to $4.97B in Q1'26 including revolver draws, against stockholders' equity of only $2.39B carrying a $3.23B retained deficit, for D/E of 3.48. The Altman Z-Score of 1.19 sits squarely in the distress zone. Goodwill ($3.1B) plus intangibles ($1.6B) equal ~44% of total assets, and multiple reporting units and tradenames carry fair values within 10% of book — further impairments are flagged as likely if the stock stays depressed. Cash on hand is only ~$201M against a current ratio of 1.07.

**Cash flow is thin.** FY25 operating cash flow fell to $264M (from $496M in FY24); after $247M of capex, free cash flow was just $17M. The company relies on receivables factoring (~$380M sold off-balance-sheet across two facilities) plus a supplier-finance program to manage working capital. This dependence bears watching, because it flatters reported cash conversion and can reverse quickly if terms tighten.

Credit metrics reflect the stress. Moody's downgraded senior unsecured to B2 in Q4 2025 (both S&P and Moody's downgraded earlier in 2025). The May 2025 refinancing priced $1.25B of 2028 notes at an 8.5% coupon, an expensive cost of capital that quantifies the default premium the debt market already assigns.

## Growth Analysis

The consolidated top line is not growing; it is contracting more slowly. Management's raised FY26 guidance of flat-to-+2% net sales would still leave revenue ~23-24% below the 2022 peak. The estimate revision picture is mixed rather than the sharp positive turn that would flag a re-rating: current-quarter EPS estimates were revised down 21.8% over 90 days (to $0.20) even as they ticked up 2.7% over 30 days; full-year estimates rose a modest 2.5% over 90 days to $0.58, and next-year 0.8% to $0.67. This is stabilization, not the +30% revision velocity that historically precedes a durable re-rating.

Growth durability is concentrated in one segment. L&D grew 4% in Q1'26 with margin expanding to 18.2%, and it is defensively positioned (writing instruments and baby products are relatively demand-inelastic). H&CS (discretionary kitchen and home fragrance) and O&R (Coleman outdoor) are more cyclical and demand-sensitive, precisely the categories most exposed to any weakening in mid-market discretionary spending. Newell's own customer concentration (Amazon 17% + Walmart 13% = ~30% of sales) means shelf-space and program-term decisions at two accounts can swing segment growth more than product innovation.

The Productivity Plan announced December 2025 targets more than 900 professional/clerical job cuts and ~20 Yankee Candle store closures, with $75-90M of charges through 2026. This should support margin, but it is cost-out, not demand-driven growth: appropriate for a deleveraging turnaround but not a revenue-expansion thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $5.03, Newell carries a ~$2.1B market cap. Trailing and forward P/E are negative (GAAP losses), so headline multiples are uninformative. On management's normalized $0.56-$0.60 EPS, the stock trades ~8.4-9.0x normalized earnings, and consensus $0.58 FY26 puts it at ~8.7x. Against a Consumer Discretionary peer median P/E of 13.5, that looks cheap on the surface.

The discount is largely earned rather than a mispricing, which is the crux of the AVOID. The enterprise lens changes the picture materially: on ~$4.97B total debt less ~$201M cash (net debt ~$4.77B) plus $2.1B equity, EV is roughly $6.9B against FY25 adjusted EBITDA in the ~$800-900M range, or ~8x EV/EBITDA, a full multiple for a business with declining revenue, distressed credit, and annual interest expense consuming the bulk of operating cash. The cheap-looking equity P/E is a function of financial leverage amplifying a thin operating base, not evidence the market has mispriced the underlying business.

The dividend yields ~5.2%, well above the peer median 1.7%, but the yield is a warning rather than support: it is funded well above free cash flow, and one third-party analyst already flags it as unsustainable. A cut would remove the primary reason income-oriented holders own the name.

The consumer-discretionary valuation lesson applies directly here: a compressed multiple that reflects structural leverage and secular revenue decline is a value trap absent an independent catalyst. The identifiable catalysts (tariff-refund optionality, a potential H&CS/O&R divestiture surfacing L&D value) are real but un-booked and unscheduled. Analyst targets bracket a wide $3.50-$9.00 range (mean $5.59, median $5.13), with the current price essentially at the median, consistent with a fairly-valued distressed equity, not an asymmetric setup. At >20% below the 52-week high the burden is on the bull to identify what sellers are missing; the answer is that the sellers are correctly pricing covenant and refinancing risk, and the ~50% rally off the March low has already restored the equity toward fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

Newell operates in mature, low-growth housewares and consumer-staples-adjacent categories where the dominant dynamic is retailer bargaining power and private-label encroachment. The 10-K explicitly notes retailers increasingly "import generic products directly from foreign sources" and expand private labels. Amazon rising from 13% to 17% of sales in two years reflects channel power shifting toward the buyer, structurally pressuring Newell's pricing.

Against this, the differentiation is genuine but uneven. Sharpie, Rubbermaid, Coleman, and Graco are real category leaders, and the L&D writing/baby portfolio is defensively positioned and margin-rich. The domestic manufacturing footprint (over half of U.S. revenue from products made in the U.S./Mexico) is a legitimate structural edge in a tariff regime, giving Newell a cost advantage over China-import-reliant competitors in select categories. But the H&CS home-fragrance and kitchen categories and O&R outdoor are discretionary and demand-sensitive, and the trajectory across the portfolio is one of share stabilization at best, with distribution losses still cited as a revenue driver. The competitive-position score reflects a stable-to-eroding trajectory rather than share gains; the prior report's downgrade of this dimension from 4.0 to 3.5 stands, with the new data neither reversing nor confirming a turn.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Covenant cliff — the most acute near-term risk.** The $1.0B secured Credit Revolver's Total Net Leverage Ratio covenant steps DOWN on September 30, 2026 and stays tighter thereafter. Compliance depends on operating performance management cannot fully control. A breach absent a waiver or amendment triggers cross-default and acceleration across all debt. As of Q1'26, $425M was drawn (up from $130M at YE25), leaving net availability of only ~$327M. The revolver matures August 2027.
2. **Refinancing cost and the 2027-28 wall.** ~$1.75B of debt matures in 2027-28; the 8.5% coupon on the 2028 notes shows what the company must pay to access capital today. Rising oil is separately lifting resin and freight costs.
3. **Continued impairment risk.** Market cap sits below book equity; management warns further stock-price or demand weakness could force additional material non-cash writedowns of the $4.7B goodwill-plus-intangibles base.
4. **Tariff whipsaw with un-booked refund optionality.** Newell paid ~$120M of IEEPA tariffs in 2025; the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking IEEPA and the April 2026 CIT refund order create potential refund upside (no receivable booked), partly offset by reimposed Section 122 tariffs.
5. **Customer concentration.** Amazon (17%) + Walmart (13%) = ~30% of sales.
6. **Litigation tail.** Two certified consumer class actions on the Baby (Graco/NUK) business; Lower Passaic River CERCLA Superfund (~$1.8B aggregate estimated across all PRPs, Newell in lowest responsibility tier); $80M IRS transfer-pricing dispute plus ~$34M penalties in Tax Court.
7. **Dividend sustainability.** The ~$140M annual dividend against $17M FCF prioritizes signaling over deleveraging; a cut is plausible and would pressure the shares.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 85.6% is rich versus 62.0% one-year historical vol (a 23.7pp spread), sits in the 83rd percentile within its chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (85.6% near-term versus 66.6% at six months): the market is pricing a defined near-term catalyst, consistent with the September 30 covenant step-down and intervening earnings. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±19.4%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.4%) and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.03 to 0.33 since mid-June, so positioning is not one-sidedly bearish despite the weak fundamentals, a caution for anyone considering the short. IV has fallen from 108.0% to 85.6% since mid-June even as the price held, indicating some resolved uncertainty after the recent rally.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$9, the analyst high):** The turnaround compounds — margin expansion holds, the Productivity Plan delivers cost-out, FY26 delivers the $0.56-$0.60 normalized EPS, the company clears the September covenant step-down without a waiver, and refinances the 2027-28 wall at improving rates. Tariff refunds arrive as un-modeled cash. Management surfaces L&D value through a divestiture of H&CS or O&R, and the equity re-rates toward a sum-of-the-parts where the writing/baby franchise alone supports the current market cap. In this scenario deleveraging drives equity value disproportionately given the leverage.

**Bear Case (~$3.30 or below, retesting the March low):** Revenue continues to shrink as the consumer weakens, EBITDA misses the covenant threshold, and the company requires a waiver on unfavorable terms or a distressed refinancing at 9%+ coupons. Additional goodwill/intangible impairments hit book equity, the dividend is cut, and factoring/supplier-finance capacity tightens. The equity, as a thin residual claim behind ~$5B of debt, compresses toward the low end of the analyst range.

The base case ($5.00-$5.60) is that Newell muddles through — clears the covenant with a modest amendment, holds margins roughly flat, and the equity trades near its current fair-to-full value with limited upside until the 2027-28 maturities are refinanced on visible terms. That is not a compelling risk/reward at $5.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most specific dated event is the September 30, 2026 covenant step-down. A Q2 or Q3 miss that jeopardizes compliance, or a dividend cut, would drive the stock materially lower. There is no single confirmed miss scheduled, and Q1'26 was a beat, so the catalyst is a probability-weighted risk rather than a confirmed trigger.

**Timing window:** ~2-4 months to the covenant step-down, with the Q2 print (typically late July/early August) and Q3 (late October) as the intervening reads. This is a reasonably short window, favorable for a short by the standard of carrying cost.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 21.6% of float with 3.6 days to cover and 57.9M shares short. This is a crowded short. At >20% SI, any positive surprise — a tariff-refund announcement, a covenant amendment on good terms, a divestiture, or another Q1-style assisted beat — carries meaningful squeeze risk, as the June 26 volume spike (81M shares) and the ~50% rally off the March low demonstrate.

**Borrow and options signal:** Near-term IV is rich and in backwardation, so options-based bearish expression carries a high premium and the market has already partly resolved the pre-rally uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced and the P/C OI ratio is low at 0.33: options positioning is not confirming a strong bearish view, which is a warning sign for a fresh short.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** A covenant scare or dividend cut could take the stock toward $3.30 (~-34%). But the upside risk on any deleveraging/refinancing/divestiture headline is large given the leverage: a re-rating toward $7-9 (+40% to +80%) is plausible in the bull case. Against a ~-34% downside, that is worse than 2:1 in the wrong direction, and the crowded 21.6% SI amplifies squeeze risk.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis on the capital structure is coherent, but short interest above 20% with meaningful catalysts capable of triggering a squeeze, rich borrow-implied IV, and a stock that has already rallied ~50% off the low make the remaining downside unattractive relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not press the short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria (what would move us to HOLD or BUY):**
- The company clears the September 30, 2026 covenant step-down without a waiver, or amends on favorable terms, removing the near-term binary — a HOLD trigger.
- Refinancing of the 2027-28 maturity wall executed at coupons meaningfully below 8.5%, demonstrating restored capital-market access — a HOLD-to-BUY path.
- Sustained organic revenue growth (two consecutive quarters of positive core sales without one-time program true-ups or FX help) plus FCF covering the dividend, which would validate the turnaround rather than the assisted Q1 print.
- A divestiture of H&CS or O&R at a credible multiple that deleverages the balance sheet and surfaces L&D value — the cleanest re-rating catalyst; would warrant a fresh look at BUY.
- Valuation compression back toward the low $3s on unchanged fundamentals would improve risk/reward materially, since the equity is a leveraged option and the same operating outcome is worth far more purchased cheaply.

**Timeline:** The covenant and refinancing overhangs persist through at least mid-2027 (revolver maturity August 2027, notes 2028). We expect the distressed-leverage headwind to dominate the equity story for 12-18 months. Reassess at the Q2 2026 print and again immediately after the September 30 covenant date.

## Conclusion

Newell is a leveraged bet on covenant survival dressed as a consumer-brands turnaround. The operating improvement is genuine — L&D is a high-quality, margin-rich franchise, Q1'26 margins expanded, and management is executing on cost-out — but it is layered on a balance sheet with D/E of 3.48, an Altman Z of 1.19, a B2 rating, an 8.5% cost of incremental debt, and a covenant step-down on September 30, 2026 that management admits depends on performance it cannot fully control. Free cash flow of $17M cannot cover the ~$140M dividend, and the company continues to pay it, choosing signaling over deleveraging.

At $5.03, the equity has rallied ~50% off its March low to a level that is fair-to-full against a distressed capital structure, and the options market is pricing imminent stress into the September window. The cheap-looking ~8.7x normalized P/E is an artifact of leverage, not a mispricing; on an EV basis the business trades at a full multiple for declining revenue and heavy interest burden. The identifiable upside catalysts — tariff refunds, a divestiture surfacing L&D value — are real but un-booked and unscheduled. We rate NWL **AVOID**, and while the bearish capital-structure thesis is coherent, the 21.6% short interest, squeeze risk, and rich borrow make this a crowded short rather than an active short candidate. This maintains the prior AVOID stance; the recent price recovery has not been accompanied by fundamental improvement sufficient to change the call.
