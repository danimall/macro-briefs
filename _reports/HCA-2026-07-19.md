**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HCA Healthcare is the largest for-profit hospital operator in the United States, running 189 hospitals and 149 outpatient surgery/endoscopy centers across 19 states plus England. The business is high-quality by hospital-sector standards: ~13% operating margins, $12.6B of operating cash flow, and $7.7B of free cash flow on $75.6B of FY2025 revenue. That cash engine is intact. What has changed is the payer-mix environment. The expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits at the end of 2025 is pushing exchange-enrolled patients into the uninsured pool faster than management projected, and on July 14, 2026 HCA cut FY2026 EPS guidance from $29.10–$31.50 to $28.70–$30.50, disclosing a ~$400M Q2 payer-mix hit and a full-year exchange impact now expected to exceed $1B.

The stock has repriced accordingly, from roughly $500 in early 2026 to ~$371, a decline of about 33% from its $556.52 52-week high. At the cut guidance midpoint of ~$29.60, HCA trades near 12.5x forward EPS, a clear discount to its own history and to the ~17.6x sector-facility median. The central question is whether the >$1B exchange impact is a one-time 2026 reset that pricing, cost discipline, and $9.18B of remaining buyback authorization can grow past, or the first leg of a multi-year margin compression as Federal Budget Act Medicaid financing cuts layer in through 2028. HCA collected $6.2B of state directed-payment (SDP) revenue in 2025 and expects that line to decline in 2026, with grandfathered payments cut 10 percentage points annually from 2028.

We rate HCA a HOLD. This is a fundamentally sound franchise trading at a below-market multiple, but the setup does not yet clear the bar for a BUY: consensus EPS estimates are still falling across every forward window (next-year down 1.8% over 90 days), the policy headwind is structural and layered rather than a single resolvable event, and there is no open-market insider buying at depressed levels to signal management conviction. The valuation discount is real but not yet a margin of safety against continued negative earnings revisions. We would upgrade on estimate stabilization plus evidence that resiliency initiatives offset the exchange drag, or on a further de-rating toward 10x forward.

## Company Overview

HCA operates a single business line, acute-care hospitals and related facilities, organized into three geographic groups (National, Atlantic, American). As of Q1 2026 the portfolio comprised 189 hospitals, 119 freestanding ambulatory surgery centers, and 30 endoscopy centers, roughly 50,400 licensed beds at 73% occupancy. The competitive model is local market density combined with national scale: HCA builds comprehensive service networks (cardiology, oncology, orthopedics, women's services, emergency and urgent care) in large, growing metro markets, then layers enterprise economies of scale across purchasing, revenue-cycle management, supply chain, and increasingly data and AI-assisted clinical documentation. This produces industry-leading margins for a hospital operator.

A defining structural feature is geographic concentration: Florida and Texas together generate roughly 51% of revenue and 59% of admissions. These are high-growth, business-friendly states with favorable commercial payer mixes, which supports margins, but the concentration also amplifies exposure to state-level Medicaid policy (Texas is specifically flagged on the SDP cuts) and to hurricane risk; the 2024 Helene/Milton storms cost roughly $250M.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily but is decelerating. FY2023 revenue of $65.0B rose to $70.6B in FY2024 (+8.6%) and $75.6B in FY2025 (+7.1%). FY2025 growth decomposed into +2.9% equivalent admissions and +4.0% revenue per equivalent admission. Q1 2026 revenue of $19.1B was up 4.3% year over year, but equivalent admissions grew only 1.1%, showing volume growth is slowing.

Profitability through 2025 was strong. FY2025 net income attributable to HCA was $6.784B, diluted EPS $28.33, up 17.8% year over year. That growth was flattered by two factors: a hurricane-free 2025 comparison against 2024's ~$250M storm hit, and a materially reduced share count. Salaries and benefits improved to 43.5% of revenue from 45.4% in 2023 as pandemic-era premium labor costs normalized, a genuine operating improvement. ROA ran near 12.8% with asset turnover of 1.25x.

Cash generation is the strongest part of the story. FY2025 operating cash flow was $12.6B against $4.9B of capex, yielding $7.7B of free cash flow (a 10%+ FCF margin). Planned 2026 capex of $5.0–5.5B supports ~$8.8B of projects under construction, a multi-year growth commitment.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. Stockholders' equity is negative, minus $6.0B at year-end 2025 and minus $6.3B at Q1 2026, which produces an Altman Z-Score of 1.14 that a standardized screen would read as distress. That reading is wrong. The equity deficit is the deliberate result of roughly $20B of debt-funded buybacks over three years retiring equity, not operational deterioration. HCA carries $48.0B of total debt (Q1 2026) at a ~5.0% average rate and an 11.7-year average life, predominantly fixed, for net leverage around 3x EBITDA, high but typical for HCA and manageable given the cash flow. The current ratio of 0.97 and slightly negative working capital reflect $2.2–3.7B of commercial paper, backstopped by an $8B unsecured revolver.

The caveat is that this leveraged-equity model magnifies EPS in good times and leaves thin cushion in a downturn. Buybacks ran $3.8B (2023), $6.0B (2024), and $10.1B (2025), with another $1.6B in Q1 2026, a pace that exceeds operating cash flow net of capex and dividends, meaning the incremental repurchases are debt-financed. Share count fell from ~272M in 2023 to ~222.5M at Q1 2026, an ~18% reduction that materially boosted EPS. With a higher-for-longer rate environment, the cost of sustaining this model rises at the margin.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture has deteriorated, and this is the crux of our cautious stance. Consensus estimates have followed the July guidance cut downward: current-year EPS consensus of $29.94 is down 0.7% over 30 days and 1.3% over 90 days, while next-year consensus of $32.74 is down 1.1% and 1.8% over the same windows. The next-quarter estimate has fallen 3.1% in 30 days. This is broad-based negative revision momentum, the opposite of the estimate-inflection signal that would support a constructive rating.

The mechanism is specific and quantifiable. As enhanced premium tax credits fall away, previously insured exchange patients drop coverage and present as uninsured. HCA's Q1 2026 uninsured admissions jumped 15.6% and charity care rose to $5.5B from $3.6B.

The second, slower headwind is the 2025 Federal Budget Act, which caps state directed payments to Medicare-based rates, limits provider taxes, and imposes Medicaid work requirements. SDP revenue rose to $6.2B in 2025 from $5.5B, a material line item, and the scheduled decline compounds the exchange story and extends the earnings-pressure window into 2028.

Offsetting these are real but unproven levers: continued outpatient/ASC expansion (149 centers), pricing on the favorable FL/TX commercial book, the enterprise EHR and AI-documentation investments, and management's "resiliency initiatives" designed specifically to offset the FBA/EPTC drag. The underlying volume and pricing engine remains healthy, with Q1 revenue still growing 4.3%. The bull case for growth is that 2026 is a one-time reset the company grows through; the bear case is that Medicaid cuts turn this into a multi-year compression. We do not yet have the evidence to resolve that, which is why we weight the falling estimates as the dominant near-term signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $371.18 against the cut FY2026 midpoint of ~$29.60, HCA trades at roughly 12.5x forward EPS, and against next-year consensus of $32.74 at ~11.3x. That is a discount to its own multi-year history (the stock traded near 17x when it changed hands around $500) and sits below the Health Care Facilities peer-median P/E of 17.6x, though the peer set is heterogeneous: Tenet at 9.0x and UHS at 6.1x trade cheaper (both face similar or worse payer-mix exposure and higher leverage/lower quality), while Encompass (17.6x) and the SNF operators Ensign (24.0x) and National Healthcare (24.9x) reflect different business models less exposed to the exchange shock.

The valuation dimension deserves discipline here. A compressed multiple driven by a genuine, quantified earnings headwind is not automatically a bargain; cheapness sorts the wrong way when it reflects deteriorating fundamentals rather than a mispricing. HCA's discount is doing real work: it prices in the exchange impact and the coming Medicaid cuts. For the multiple to re-rate upward, estimates need to stop falling. Until they do, "12.5x forward" is 12.5x of a number that is still being revised down, which erodes the apparent discount.

On a normalized basis, the case for patience is stronger. If 2026 proves a one-time reset and HCA returns to mid-single-digit revenue growth with restored payer mix, the earnings power that supported a $500 price would justify a mid-teens multiple and a meaningfully higher stock. The analyst mean target of $467.60 (median $462.50, range $369–$635) sits well above the current price, implying ~26% upside to the mean. On an inflection stock with rising estimates we would treat those targets as stale-low floors; here, with estimates falling, the targets are more likely to be revised toward the price than the reverse. We therefore treat the mean target as an aspirational rather than a base case. Our base case fair value sits closer to $415 (~14x the cut FY2026 midpoint), reflecting a modest re-rating once the 2026 reset is digested but no return to peak multiples while the Medicaid overhang persists.

## Competitive Landscape

HCA's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, weighted by structural policy headwinds rather than lost share. The company competes with other for-profit systems (Tenet, Community Health Systems, Universal Health), large not-for-profit systems, physician-owned specialty hospitals, and a proliferating base of freestanding ASCs, ERs, urgent care, and imaging centers. Its scale, purchasing leverage, and market density remain genuine advantages, and its ~13% operating margin is the best among for-profit hospital peers.

Two dynamics pressure the position. First, the site-of-care shift: volumes are migrating to lower-cost outpatient settings, and Medicare's phase-out of the inpatient-only list (2026) plus site-neutral payment policies squeeze inpatient economics. HCA has responded by building its own outpatient footprint (119 ASCs), which converts a threat into partial participation. Second, payer consolidation increases insurers' bargaining power, and utilization review, now AI-driven via the WISeR prior-authorization model, pressures reimbursement.

The EPTC expiration and FBA Medicaid cuts are an industry-wide policy shock, not a company-specific franchise problem. The July 14 warning triggered a sector selloff, with Tenet falling ~7% in sympathy. HCA's scale, favorable FL/TX commercial mix, and cost discipline position it to absorb the shock better than most peers, but its heavy exchange and Medicaid exposure mean it is not immune. Relative to the peer group, HCA remains the strongest operator; on an absolute basis, its competitive economics are being reset lower by policy.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risks are realized and quantified rather than speculative, which is why the risk score is below neutral: these are active, not tail, events.

1. **EPTC expiration (the #1 realized risk).** The magnitude ran ahead of management's earlier estimate, which is itself a warning about forecast visibility.

2. **FBA Medicaid financing cuts.** SDP revenue is expected to decline in 2026, with 10-point annual cuts to grandfathered payments from 2028. Texas exposure is specifically flagged. This is the slower-moving but arguably larger multi-year risk.

3. **Leverage and negative equity.** $48B of debt and negative book equity leave thin cushion; refinancing occurs in a higher-rate environment, and the debt-funded buyback pace raises the cost of the model at the margin.

4. **Geographic concentration.** FL/TX at 51% of revenue concentrates hurricane and state-policy risk.

5. **Reimbursement and utilization pressure.** Site-neutral Medicare payments, inpatient-only-list phase-out, WISeR AI prior authorization, 340B reductions, and 2% sequestration all chip at reimbursement.

6. **Labor.** Nurse/physician shortages, potential mandatory staffing ratios, and union activity.

7. **Execution.** Enterprise EHR migration risk, and the possibility that resiliency initiatives under-deliver against the policy drag.

There is one meaningful offset that keeps this from being an AVOID: the Florida Medicaid directed-payment application pending approval could add "significant" revenue, and the core operating engine (admissions, pricing, cost control) remains healthy.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 39.1% is rich versus 29.7% one-year historical volatility (a 9.4-point spread, 71st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (39.1% near vs 34.3% at six months), consistent with the aftermath of the guidance cut and the approach of the next earnings print. The P/C open-interest ratio has jumped from 1.42 to 2.60 since mid-June, and unusual put activity clustered at the $325 (down 12.4%) and $350 (down 5.7%) August strikes signals hedging or bearish positioning for another leg down. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±10%. This positioning corroborates our caution: the market is not treating the current level as a stable floor. The near-term put/call volume ratio of 31.83 reflects that hedging bid; longer-dated skew is only modestly put-heavy (-3.8% at six months), suggesting the fear is event-specific rather than a structural view of terminal decline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($480–$520).** The 2026 exchange hit is a one-time reset. Enhanced premium tax credits are reinstated or partially extended by Congress, and even absent that, HCA's pricing power on its commercial book, cost discipline, and resiliency initiatives absorb the drag by 2027. Estimates stabilize, then rise. The $9.18B remaining buyback authorization, executed at ~$371 rather than the ~$498 average paid in Q1 2026, compounds per-share value. The stock re-rates back toward 15–16x normalized ~$32–33 EPS, supported by the analyst mean target of $467.60 and high of $635. Upside of roughly 30–40%.

**Bear Case ($300–$320).** The exchange impact proves sticky as subsidies stay expired, and FBA Medicaid cuts compound from 2026 through 2028, turning 2026's reset into a multi-year margin compression. SDP revenue erosion accelerates, Texas exposure bites, and the leveraged-equity model amplifies EPS declines the same way it amplified gains. Estimates keep falling, the multiple stays compressed at 10–11x a declining number, and the stock drifts toward $300. Downside of roughly 15–20% from current.

**Base Case ($415).** The exchange drag is largely absorbed within 2026, Medicaid cuts create a modest but manageable multi-year headwind, and HCA grows EPS at a low-single-digit rate off a reset 2026 base. The multiple re-rates modestly to ~14x as estimates stabilize, but not to peak levels while the policy overhang persists. Upside of roughly 12% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 full results (following the July 14 pre-announcement), which will detail the payer-mix trajectory and any 2027 commentary. The Q3 print and 2027 guidance are the more decisive catalysts, alongside any congressional action on premium tax credits and the Florida Medicaid directed-payment approval decision.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimates stabilize or turn positive across the current-year and next-year windows for two consecutive months (currently both falling).
- Evidence in Q2/Q3 results that resiliency initiatives and pricing are offsetting the exchange drag, i.e., the exchange impact tracks at or below the disclosed >$1B and does not expand.
- Open-market insider buying at depressed levels, or a further de-rating toward 10x forward (~$300) that restores a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY2026 guidance cut again, or Q2/Q3 results showing the exchange impact materially exceeding $1B.
- Signs that FBA Medicaid SDP erosion is accelerating faster than the disclosed 2028 schedule, or an unfavorable Florida directed-payment ruling.
- Deterioration in the core engine — equivalent admissions turning negative, or salaries/benefits reversing back above 45% of revenue.

## Conclusion

HCA is the best-run operator in the US hospital sector, generating ~$7.7B of free cash flow and industry-leading margins, and its ~33% drawdown from the high is a policy-driven repricing rather than a franchise impairment. The negative book equity that flags on screens as distress is a manufactured artifact of buybacks, not operational weakness. But quality and a compressed multiple are not sufficient for a buy call when earnings estimates are still falling, the policy headwind is structural and layered across 2026–2028, and there is no insider-buying signal to indicate management sees a floor. The 12.5x forward multiple discounts a lot of the exchange shock, yet the number it multiplies is still being revised down, and the options market is pricing continued near-term stress.

We rate HCA a HOLD. The risk/reward is roughly balanced: ~12% to our $415 base case, ~30–40% in the bull case if the exchange hit proves one-time, against ~15–20% downside if Medicaid cuts compound into a multi-year compression. We want to see estimate stabilization or a further de-rating before moving to BUY. This is a franchise worth owning at the right entry; the current price is closer to fair than to compelling.
