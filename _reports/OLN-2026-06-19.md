**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Olin is a vertically integrated chlor-alkali, epoxy, and ammunition producer operating at the bottom of a multi-year chemicals downcycle, with a balance sheet that has deteriorated to the point of triggering a triple downgrade to junk and a February 2026 conversion of its revolver from unsecured to secured. As of June 16, 2026, Olin announced an all-stock merger of equals with Huntsman (54.5% Olin / 45.5% Huntsman) to form OlinHuntsman, a ~$12.5B-revenue combined entity headquartered in The Woodlands, TX, expected to close in H1 2027. The stock should now be evaluated as a deal-completion and balance-sheet-repair story rather than a standalone going concern.

The prior view on this name was AVOID, issued twice. After re-examining the data, that call is difficult to sustain at $22, near the 52-week low and ~28% below the $30.46 high reached in March 2026. The standalone distress — Altman Z of 1.54, $2,996M debt, 63.3% debt-to-cap, negative Q1 operating cash flow, junk ratings, the secured-revolver amendment — has been public since February 2026, and the 10-Q explicitly states no material change to risk factors. The market has traded this stock in an $18–30 band with all of that information known. The forward signals are inflecting, not deteriorating: current-year consensus EPS has moved from -$0.75 to +$0.31 over 90 days, next quarter is revised +157%, the company has beaten estimates four straight quarters (Q1'26 -$0.32 vs -$0.64 estimate, +49.7%), and the entire published sell-side target range ($25 low / $30.14 mean / $39 high) sits above the current price.

An AVOID implies asymmetric downside and a shortable setup. The evidence here does not support that. There is no dated near-term catalyst for further decline, short interest is already elevated at 13.8% of float (6.5 days to cover) creating squeeze risk, options positioning is balanced, and the merger introduces a credible path to a stronger combined balance sheet that the company's own creditors and Jefferies have flagged. The downside is not asymmetric, which is the operational definition of a HOLD. We move from AVOID to HOLD, classified as uncertain: the leverage risk is real and the cyclical bottom is not confirmed, but the bearish thesis is largely priced and the forward indicators are improving.

## Company Overview

Olin (Virginia corporation, founded 1892, HQ Clayton, MO) runs three segments:

- **Chlor Alkali Products & Vinyls (CAPV)** — ~54% of 2025 sales ($3.68B). The world's largest chlorine producer, making chlorine, caustic soda, EDC, VCM, chlorinated organics, hydrogen, and bleach. Olin runs an explicit "ECU margin over volume" model, idling capacity to defend per-unit pricing rather than chasing utilization in soft markets.
- **Epoxy** — ~20% of sales ($1.37B). Epoxy resins and precursors plus aromatics. Structurally loss-making — segment losses of -$31M (2023), -$85M (2024), -$104M (2025) — driven by subsidized Asian imports flooding Western markets.
- **Winchester** — ~25% of sales ($1.72B). A leading U.S. ammunition maker operating the Lake City U.S. Army Ammunition Plant. The most resilient segment; over-time government contract revenue more than doubled YoY in Q1'26 to $120.8M.

The competitive advantage rests on scale, vertical integration (chlorine feeds Epoxy at cost), and the Winchester defense franchise. The chemicals businesses are deeply cyclical commodity operations earning thin margins at trough.

## Financial Analysis

The downcycle off the 2022 peak has been severe:

| Gross margin % | ~23% | 17% | 11% | 7% | 5% |
| Op. cash flow | — | 974 | 503 | 474 | (48.6) |

Revenue has roughly halved from 2022. Company-defined Adjusted EBITDA fell from $2.49B (2021) to $651.8M (2025), a ~74% decline. The 2025 net loss of $101M included a $75M Shintech litigation charge; Q1'26 added a $36.1M legacy litigation charge, with ~$195M of cash outflow tied to Shintech in H1 2026 during a cash-negative quarter.

The balance sheet is the central concern. Total debt rose to $2,996M at Q1'26 (from $2,827M at YE2025) against $192M cash. Debt-to-total-cap climbed to 63.3% as equity eroded to $1.73B (from $2.54B in 2022) from losses, dividends, and prior buybacks. Altman Z of 1.54 sits in the distress zone. The capital structure holds $1.78B of fixed-rate senior notes (5.625% '29, 5.00% '30, 6.625% '33) and ~$1.23B variable-rate debt, where a 100bp SOFR move shifts annual interest by ~$12.3M.

The February 2026 credit amendment is the most telling item. Olin loosened its financial maintenance covenants and, in exchange, pledged liens on substantially all personal property and added domestic subsidiary guarantees, converting the revolver from unsecured to secured. The relief expires after September 30, 2027, bridging to the expected merger close. Within the same week, all three rating agencies cut Olin to high yield (Fitch BB+, Moody's Ba2, S&P BB, all negative outlook). This is a borrower negotiating from weakness.

Set against this, the earnings trajectory has inflected. Q1'26 reported -$0.73 GAAP (-$0.65 adjusted) but beat the -$0.64 estimate on the adjusted line, the fourth consecutive beat. Management guided Q2'26 EBITDA of $160–200M, expecting sequential improvement on seasonal demand and pricing. The 45V clean hydrogen credit qualified in Q3'25, delivering ~$34.5M of COGS benefit in 2025 and an expected $15–20M/yr pretax through 2028.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, Olin offers cyclical recovery rather than secular growth. The chemicals businesses are at or near a trough, and the question is when chlor-alkali pricing and epoxy demand normalize rather than whether the company can structurally outgrow its end markets. Epoxy remains a structural weak link with subsidized Asian competition driving persistent segment losses and ongoing restructuring (~$70M of further charges expected through 2030).

The estimate revision pattern is the strongest forward growth signal and warrants direct weighting rather than dismissal. Current-quarter estimates rose +184.4% over 90 days, and next-quarter +157.2%, alongside four consecutive earnings beats. This is the measurable inflection signature — rising estimates plus revenue normalizing off depressed levels — that typically precedes a re-rating as consensus catches up. The caveat is that the magnitude is trough-driven: moving from a loss to a small profit produces large percentage swings on small absolute numbers, and the next-year revision (+4.2% to $0.93) is far more muted, which tempers how durable the inflection is. The signal supports a constructive-to-neutral stance, not a continued AVOID, but it does not yet justify a BUY because the absolute earnings power remains thin and leverage caps the equity upside.

The merger reframes the growth story toward through-cycle resilience. Combining Olin's chlorine output with Huntsman's downstream MDI/polyurethanes and advanced materials targets $400M+ of identified cost synergies and a structurally less cyclical earnings stream. Jefferies cited longer-dated upside in electronics, battery, and defense materials (2029–2032). The growth case here is a 2027-onward integration story contingent on deal completion, not a near-term standalone catalyst.

I do not lower the valuation score for the price having declined; the ~$22 level on largely unchanged, already-disclosed fundamentals represents improving risk/reward versus the prior reports near $26, not deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

Olin's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-pressured rather than collapsing. In CAPV it retains the global scale lead and a deliberate margin-over-volume discipline that protects per-unit economics at the cost of utilization. Winchester is a genuine franchise asset with defense-linked, non-cyclical ballast — the Lake City plant and doubling government contract revenue provide stability the chemicals businesses lack, and it is explicitly retained in the merged entity.

The clear weak point is Epoxy, where subsidized Asian competition is a persistent, possibly secular, headwind that years of plant closures (Brazil, Korea, Netherlands, Germany) have not resolved. The merger's vertical-integration logic of converting commodity chlorine into Huntsman's higher-value derivatives is a credible response to the structural cyclicality, but execution and synergy capture remain unproven and are years out. The competitive trajectory is roughly neutral: scale and Winchester offset epoxy erosion, with the merger introducing a plausible but unproven improvement path.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risks are well-documented and already known to the market:

1. **Leverage and covenant pressure.** Secured revolver, loosened covenants expiring September 30, 2027, and management's explicit flag that weak conditions affect covenant compliance. This is the paramount concern but has been public since February 2026.
2. **Junk ratings.** All three agencies at high yield with negative outlooks, raising borrowing costs.
3. **Litigation.** Shintech (~$195M H1'26 cash outflow) and a new April 2026 Texas Clean Air Act suit against Blue Cube, layered on a ~$157M environmental reserve and ~$210M/yr environmental cash outlays.
4. **Epoxy impairment risk.** Persistent losses could trigger further non-cash goodwill impairment.
5. **Dividend sustainability.** The $0.20/quarter (~$91M/yr) payout is being funded with debt during cash-negative quarters; the company reserves the right to change it. A cut is plausible and would be a sentiment negative but a credit positive.
6. **Deal-completion risk.** The merger requires both shareholder votes, regulatory approval, and successful integration; failure would return the stock to standalone-distress framing.

These risks are not new and are substantially priced into a stock near its 52-week low. The 10-Q states no material change to risk factors. What is not yet priced symmetrically is the merger's balance-sheet-repair optionality and the cyclical bottom — both two-sided. The risk score sits below neutral because the leverage and covenant overhang are genuine and the path through September 2027 is tight, but not at the ≤4 distressed floor because the downside is disclosed rather than asymmetric.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not confirm a bearish break. Near-term ATM IV is 53.1% versus 56.1% one-year historical volatility — fairly priced, sitting at the 10th percentile of the current chain, with a balanced put/call skew of 1.7% and a low put/call volume ratio of 0.29. The term structure is in contango (53.1% near vs 57.0% long), a complacent rather than stressed shape. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.15 to 0.49 as the stock fell ~15% into the merger announcement, indicating some defensive positioning, but absolute levels remain unalarming. The market is pricing the merger event and elevated general uncertainty, not an imminent collapse. For a name carrying 13.8% short interest, the absence of a bearish options bid argues against further easy downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$30–39).** The chlor-alkali/epoxy cycle bottoms in 2026, Q2 EBITDA delivers within the $160–200M guide and improves sequentially, estimate revisions continue their upward trajectory, and the Huntsman merger closes in H1 2027 with a materially better combined balance sheet and a path toward $400M of synergies. In that scenario the stock re-rates toward the sell-side mean ($30) to high ($39), 36–77% upside, with the secured-revolver bridge having served its purpose.

**Bear Case (~$15–17).** Chemicals pricing stays at trough, Q2 EBITDA disappoints, covenant pressure forces a dividend cut, the Texas/Shintech litigation drains liquidity, and the merger faces regulatory delay or fails. With 63.3% debt-to-cap, equity absorbs the downside disproportionately; a return toward $15–17 (~25–30% downside) is plausible if the bottom proves elusive and the deal wobbles.

**Base Case (~$24–25).** A messy cyclical bottom with sequential improvement, the merger progressing on schedule, and the stock drifting toward the low end of the sell-side range ($25) as deal certainty builds, roughly 10–14% above current levels.

The setup is two-sided. The strongest forward signals (estimate inflection, four beats, above-spot targets, balanced options) lean constructive; the leverage, junk ratings, and deal-close risk cap the upside and keep this short of a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain: the evidence does not support either a confident appreciation thesis or an asymmetric-downside short.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2'26 EBITDA prints at or above the $160–200M guide with sequential margin expansion, confirming the cyclical bottom.
- Merger receives shareholder and regulatory approval with a concrete close path and combined-entity leverage guidance below ~3.5x net debt/EBITDA.
- Next-year EPS revisions accelerate above $1.10 with stable chlor-alkali pricing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Dividend suspension forced by covenant pressure combined with a merger delay or termination.
- Q2/Q3'26 EBITDA materially below guidance, pushing toward a covenant breach as the September 2027 relief window narrows.
- A further rating cut deeper into high yield, or a new material litigation/impairment charge that meaningfully erodes the equity cushion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, expected early August) is the near-term checkpoint for the EBITDA guide and bottom confirmation; the merger proxy/shareholder vote and regulatory milestones through 2026–H1 2027 are the structural catalysts.

## Conclusion

Olin presents a distressed-but-disclosed standalone profile wrapped in a merger that materially changes the forward setup. The bearish case of leverage, junk ratings, the secured revolver, and litigation is real but substantially in the price at $22, near the 52-week low and well below the March high. The forward indicators that matter most for sorting cyclical inflections (estimate revisions from -$0.75 to +$0.31, four consecutive beats, a sell-side target range entirely above spot, balanced options positioning) argue against extending the AVOID. At the same time, the leverage that caps equity upside and the unproven deal-completion path argue against a BUY. The call is HOLD: downside is not asymmetric, upside requires both a cyclical bottom and a clean merger close, and the next earnings print plus deal milestones will resolve the uncertainty. We upgrade from AVOID to HOLD and will reassess on Q2'26 results and merger progression.
