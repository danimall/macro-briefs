## Executive Summary

Rollins spent two decades earning a premium earnings multiple by delivering high-single-digit organic growth with steady margin expansion, funded internally, in a business where roughly 75% of revenue sits under recurring service agreements. On July 22-23, 2026 that formula visibly stopped working. Second-quarter revenue grew 7.9% to $1,078.6M but organic growth decelerated to 5.7%, residential organic growth fell to 3.6% from 5.0% in FY2025, gross margin contracted 100bps, and GAAP operating margin fell 110bps to 18.7%. The number that matters most: adjusted incremental EBITDA margin was 6.5% in the quarter against 22.5% for FY2025 and a reaffirmed medium-term target of 30%-plus. Pretax income declined 0.8% year over year on 7.9% revenue growth. Reported net income rose 1.7% only because the effective tax rate fell to 24.2% from 26.0%, and adjusted EPS of $0.32 grew 6.7% with essentially no contribution from operating performance, and still missed the $0.34 consensus.

Management cut FY2026 organic growth guidance from 7-8% to "at least 6%," set an incremental-margin "floor" of at least 10%, and raised interest-expense guidance from roughly $30M to roughly $40M as commercial paper funds the acquisition program. Consensus followed: FY2026 EPS estimates fell 6.0% to $1.17 and FY2027 estimates fell 6.5% to $1.30 over 90 days, with the current-quarter estimate down 8.5%. Bank of America downgraded to Underperform from Buy on July 23. Eight days later Rentokil, the largest global operator and Rollins' principal US competitor, reported a sequential North American slowdown, retired a margin target, and fell 17-18% to its lowest level since 2024. That read-across is the most useful external evidence in the file: it supports a market-wide rather than company-specific demand problem, and it confirms that the entire sub-industry's premium multiple structure is being reassessed, not just Rollins'.

At $37.74 the stock trades at 32.3x FY2026E and 29.0x FY2027E, a 3.6% FY2025 free-cash-flow yield, and a 1.94% dividend covered at roughly 50% of FY2025 FCF. Balance-sheet quality is high: 1.0x leverage, 21-25x interest coverage, capex at 0.75% of sales, FCF/net income conversion of 123% in FY2025. But the decisive question is unanswerable from current disclosure: whether the collapse in consumer-initiated residential lead volume is a late, weak pest season (management's view) or a durable change in how consumers find pest-control providers (the hypothesis three sell-side analysts pressed and management rejected on timing grounds while conceding low diagnostic confidence). Scenario values bracket the current price roughly symmetrically, and the next meaningful confirmation is the Q3 report in October, with management explicitly steering margin improvement to Q4. HOLD, classified as uncertain rather than as a conviction growth or income call.

## Company Overview

Rollins is a route-based pest, termite and wildlife control operator headquartered in Atlanta, serving more than two million residential and commercial customers from more than 800 company-owned and franchised locations across approximately 70 countries, with 21,946 employees at December 31, 2025 (from 20,265 a year earlier). Approximately 93% of revenue is domestic; foreign operations were roughly 7% of FY2025 revenue. Fewer than 2% of employees are unionized.

One reportable segment, three service lines. FY2025: Residential $1,693.2M (+10.3% total, +5.0% organic), Commercial $1,244.7M (+10.5%, +7.6% organic), Termite & ancillary $781.5M (+13.6%, +9.9% organic), Franchise and other $41.5M. Revenue mix by contract type is approximately 75% recurring, 10% ancillary, 15% one-time. The recurring base is the source of the subscription-like framing; the 25% that is not recurring is where the 2026 problem sits.

The company is a multi-brand roll-up: Orkin (the flagship and largest residential brand), Clark Pest Control, HomeTeam Pest Defense, Fox Pest Control, Saela, Northwest Exterminating, Western, Trutech, Critter Control and others, across more than 450 trademarks. It completed 94 acquisitions in the last three years, including 26 in 2025. Named competitors are Rentokil, Ecolab and Anticimex, in markets the 10-K itself describes as highly competitive, fragmented, with low barriers to entry and DIY substitution. R&D is explicitly not significant; the technology moat is operational, resting on the BOSS routing/scheduling/payment platform and the InSite commercial reporting tool rather than chemical innovation.

Seasonality is pronounced: FY2025 quarterly revenue ran $822.5M, $999.5M, $1,026.1M, $912.9M. Q2 and Q3 are the high-revenue, high-margin quarters, which is why staffing for 7-8% growth and delivering 5.7% did so much damage in the June quarter.

## Financial Analysis

**Long-run record, through FY2025.** Revenue compounded from $2,695.8M (FY2022) to $3,761.1M (FY2025), with operating margin rising from 18.3% to 19.3% and net income from $368.6M to $526.7M. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.09 on 484.1M shares; adjusted EPS $1.12; adjusted EBITDA $855.1M at a 22.7% margin.

**Cash generation is the strongest part of the story.** FY2025 operating cash flow $678.1M against capex of $28.1M, capital intensity of 0.75% of sales, because most of the vehicle fleet is leased ($424.5M of operating-lease ROU assets). FY2025 FCF was $650.0M, a 17.3% FCF margin, converting at 123% of net income. Structurally negative working capital (customers prepay; unearned revenue was $196.5M at June 30, 2026) is a cash-cycle advantage, not a liquidity problem: the reported current ratio of 0.63 and the "grey zone" Altman reading of 2.92 are artifacts of that structure and of a full year of operating-lease obligations sitting in current liabilities.

**Leverage and coverage are conservative.** Company-disclosed leverage was 1.0x at June 30, 2026 (adjusted net debt $1,030.0M / adjusted EBITDAR $1,071.8M). Gross debt of $703.0M ($215.9M commercial paper at 3.97% plus $487.1M carrying value of the 5.25% 2035 notes) is 0.82x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA. Interest coverage was 25.4x in FY2025 and 21.4x in Q2 2026 against a covenant ceiling of 3.50x net debt/EBITDA on the undrawn $1.0B revolver (maturing February 2028, $750M accordion). There is no refinancing cliff. The commercial paper carries an average remaining term of 4.4 days, a rollover dependency backstopped by the revolver rather than a risk.

**Two balance-sheet caveats.** First, at June 30, 2026 goodwill ($1,449.4M) plus identified intangibles (roughly $601.5M) totaled approximately $2,050.9M, about 61% of the $3,354.9M asset base, against reported equity of $1,429.6M; tangible book value is roughly negative $621M. That is the arithmetic consequence of a cash-funded roll-up rather than evidence of impairment (none disclosed), but the downside cushion is earnings power, not assets. Second, March 2025's Amendment No. 1 released all subsidiary guarantors (Orkin LLC, Northwest, Clark Pest Control of Stockton, HomeTeam) from the credit facility and, consequently, from their guarantee of the 2035 notes, which are now unsecured, unguaranteed parent obligations. Immaterial at 1.0x leverage; relevant only if leverage rises materially.

**The 2026 break.** Organic growth ran +6.9% in FY2025, roughly +6.6% in Q1 2026 (derived from the H1 and Q2 organic disclosures; treat as approximate), and +5.7% in Q2. Residential organic fell from +5.0% to +3.6%; commercial decelerated far more gently (+7.6% to +7.2%) and termite/ancillary held up (+9.9% to +8.9%).

The Q2 P&L composition:

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $999.5M | $1,078.6M | +7.9% |
| Gross margin | 53.8% | 52.8% | −100bps |
| SG&A % of revenue | 30.8% | 31.1% | +30bps |
| GAAP operating margin | 19.8% | 18.7% | −110bps |
| Pretax income | $191.2M | $189.8M | **−0.8%** |
| Effective tax rate | 26.0% | 24.2% | −180bps |
| Net income | $141.5M | $143.9M | +1.7% |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin | ~23.0% | 21.9% | −120bps |
| Adjusted EPS | $0.30 | $0.32 | +6.7% |

Management's cost attribution for the 100bp gross-margin decline: roughly 70bps from people-related costs (medical plan expense plus service-salary deleverage) and 20bps from fleet, chiefly fuel (approximately 1.8% of sales). The 10-Q corroborates: cost-of-services employee expense 30.5% of revenue versus 29.8%, fleet 4.4% versus 4.2%, materials and supplies 6.2% versus 6.0%. The CFO's own framing, "as demand trends softened in certain areas of the business during the quarter, our cost structure remained aligned with the stronger growth outlook we anticipated entering peak season," describes a fixed-cost-absorption failure in a high-operating-leverage route business.

**Cash flow with a timing distortion.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $290.9M versus $322.0M (−9.7%); FCF $277.3M versus $308.2M (−10.0%). The decline traces primarily to cash taxes of $134.8M versus $85.0M, a $49.7M increase management attributes to payment timing on its transferable federal tax-credit purchase strategy, expected to reverse in Q4 for a full-year-neutral effect. This is a forecast, not a fact, and it is load-bearing: FY2026 FCF conversion guidance of >100% and current dividend comfort both depend on it. Dividends were 50.4% of FY2025 FCF but 63.5% of H1 2026 FCF. H1 operating cash flow should not be annualized; H1 2026 FCF was only 43% of the prior full year, consistent with Q4-weighted collections plus the tax drag.

The same tax-credit strategy is worth roughly 180bps of the reported tax rate, and it is carrying reported EPS growth in the exact quarter operating income stalled. Separate the durable operating trajectory from the tax contribution.

## Growth Analysis

The guidance reset is the cleanest summary of what changed:

| Metric | FY2026 guide (Feb 2026) | FY2026 guide (July 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Organic revenue growth | 7-8% | "at least 6%" |
| Acquisition contribution | — | 2-3% (was 4.1% in FY2025) |
| Adj. incremental EBITDA margin | — | "at least 10%" (the floor) |
| Interest expense | ~$30M | ~$40M |
| FCF conversion | >100% | >100% |

Implied total revenue growth of roughly 8-9% for FY2026 versus 11.0% delivered in FY2025 marks deceleration in both the organic and inorganic components. Estimate revisions confirm the trajectory rather than lag it: current quarter $0.35 from $0.39 (−8.5%), FY2026 $1.17 from $1.25 (−6.0%), FY2027 $1.30 from $1.39 (−6.5%). Sixteen to nineteen analysts contributing; the revisions are broad, not one outlier. The earnings-surprise record over the last four reported quarters is two in-line and two misses (−7.0% in June 2026, −7.3% in December 2025), which argues for treating the reduced consensus as a fair rather than conservative anchor.

**Where the weakness is located, and where it is not.** The Q2 call drew a clear line through the portfolio. Brands dependent on consumer-initiated demand (search, digital media, inbound calls, with Orkin the largest) saw lead volume deteriorate progressively through the quarter before improving at the very end of June. Relationship-channel brands did not: HomeTeam grew double digits and Fox posted high-teens organic growth, both above the 7-8% target, with HomeTeam spending "almost nothing in the digital channel" because it sells prevention to home buyers at the point of purchase and Fox uses a door-to-door sales force. Management redeployed labor from Orkin into Fox.

There is a pattern in the acquisition program worth naming: Fox, Saela (April 2025, $207.2M) and Romex (April 2026, $95.7M) are all direct-sales or builder-channel businesses rather than inbound-digital businesses. Whether deliberate or opportunistic, that mix is currently functioning as a hedge against the specific channel that broke.

**The unresolved cause.** Management's diagnosis is a late, weak pest season compounded by soft demand in lower income bands: mosquito calls down significantly year over year with mosquito season starting late, residential rodent and carpenter ant also down, and volume weakness "across the entire United States" with no regional variation, which is why the weather hypothesis was abandoned. Three analysts pressed the LLM-search-disintermediation thesis. Management said it tested the search environment, pulled multiple levers, and "none of those things really moved the needle," concluding fewer people were searching for pest control year over year, and argued against a structural break on timing: nothing changed at the end of June, yet demand improved.

Both readings survive the evidence. The bear reading is stronger than management allows on one specific point: geographic uniformity defeats the weather hypothesis, but it is equally consistent with a nationwide change in search behavior and less consistent with pest pressure, which is normally regionally heterogeneous. Rentokil's July 30 disclosure confirms the problem was market-wide, which cuts against a Rollins-specific execution failure but does not distinguish a cyclical demand air pocket from a channel change affecting both operators. Management itself said "we don't believe there is a single explanation." The honest position is that the cause is unknown, and the FY2025 10-K's new AI risk factor was written about operational reliance on AI, not demand-channel disintermediation.

Medium-term targets from the May 2026 Investor Day, organic growth of at least 7% and incremental margins of at least 30% over time, were reaffirmed, not cut. Rentokil retired a margin target. Divergent posture is not evidence of divergent outcomes, but Rollins has more self-help runway (a "$50M margin opportunity" referenced on the call) than a competitor still digesting Terminix.

The supplied peer set (CLH 38.8x, VLTO 21.3x, GEO 14.4x, ABM 17.8x, CWST at a meaningless 817.9x) is heterogeneous and excludes the only true comparable, Rentokil, which is not US-listed. Peer median of 21.3x is not the right anchor for a business with Rollins' recurring-revenue mix and 40%+ ROE, but it does frame the issue: Rollins is being asked to defend a roughly 40%-plus premium to the median environmental-services multiple with pretax income growing −0.8% and organic growth guided to at least 6%.

The forward multiple has compressed (ROL has rarely traded below 30x forward in the last decade) but so has the earnings algorithm it prices. If organic growth settles at 5-6% with 2-3 points of M&A and incremental margins in the 10-20% range rather than 22-30%, EPS compounds at roughly 8-11% rather than the low-to-mid teens investors underwrote. A 27-31x multiple on that profile is defensible for a 75%-recurring, 1.0x-levered, 100%-plus FCF-converting business. Applied to FY2027 consensus of $1.30, that range is $35-40.

**Base case $40** (30.8x FY2027E $1.30): organic growth stabilizes near 6%, incremental margins recover toward the mid-teens in FY2027, M&A adds 2-3 points, and the multiple holds. +6.0% from spot, plus a 1.94% dividend.

**Bull case $48** (approximately 34x on FY2027 EPS of $1.40): residential lead volume normalizes as the late-June improvement extends, Q4-weighted cost actions land, incremental margins re-approach 25-30%, estimates inflect upward and the multiple re-expands toward the historical premium. +27%.

**Bear case $28** (approximately 23x on FY2027 EPS of $1.20): consumer-initiated demand erosion proves structural, residential organic settles at 2-4%, medical and fleet inflation persists independent of volume, incremental margins hold near the 10% floor, and the pest-control premium multiple resets toward broader services levels. −26%.

Probability-weighted at 30/45/25, expected value is roughly $39.40, about +4.4% price plus 1.9% dividend. That is a fair-return profile, not an asymmetric one, and it is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE or REDUCE. Analyst targets bracket this: mean $45.59, median $45.00, low $32.00, high $66.00. The high target is a legacy artifact of the pre-Q2 algorithm; the low target sits 15% below spot.

**On the 43% drawdown.** The burden of proof at this magnitude is on the bull case, and it is not met. What sellers are plausibly right about: the operating-leverage premium was the whole multiple, and it inverted; medical cost inflation is not a volume-recovery problem; the cause of the demand break is unknown and one plausible cause is permanent; the acquisition contribution is shrinking as private equity competes for the same targets. Where the market may be wrong: mosquito season timing is genuinely cyclical and reverses; the 10% incremental-margin floor is explicitly described as a floor by a CFO who just told investors he did not expect 6.5%; the cost levers named (no repeat of last year's staffing build, a new Wheels fleet agreement, procurement) are specific rather than aspirational; and Rentokil's simultaneous stumble makes a share-loss thesis hard to sustain. Neither side of that ledger is decisive, which is the finding.

## Competitive Landscape

Rollins' position in absolute terms remains strong: the largest US pest-control platform by branch count, more than two million customers, route density that lowers per-stop cost, a proprietary field platform in BOSS, and a training infrastructure competitors do not match. Commercial organic growth of 7.2% and termite/ancillary of 8.9% in Q2 indicate the contract-based and technical parts of the franchise are intact.

Trajectory is the harder read. Rollins' Q2 organic growth of 5.7% compares favorably to Rentokil's reported H1 revenue growth of roughly 4.5%, but the periods, geographies and segment definitions differ enough that this is suggestive rather than conclusive. Whether Rollins gained or lost residential share in Q2 2026 is unknown. What the Rentokil read-across does establish is that the two largest North American operators independently reported the same directional residential problem within eight days, which weakens a company-specific execution or share-loss explanation.

The structural vulnerability is channel concentration at the largest residential brand. Orkin's dependence on consumer-initiated digital demand is a genuine exposure if search referral patterns are changing, and Rollins has no visible technical answer beyond SEO adaptation. The multi-brand portfolio partially offsets this: HomeTeam's builder channel and Fox's door-to-door force delivered above-target growth in the same quarter Orkin faltered, and the recent M&A record has concentrated in exactly those channels. The industry remains fragmented with low barriers to entry, DIY substitution is a named risk, and the 10-K acknowledges that competition for acquisition targets is intensifying as financial investors enter the sector, which pressures both the pace and the price of the roll-up engine.

## Risk Assessment

**Unresolved demand causation (highest-impact).** If the residential lead-volume decline reflects a durable change in how consumers source pest control rather than a late pest season, the growth algorithm resets permanently lower and the multiple resets with it. No filing, disclosure or dataset available resolves this. Confirmation or refutation requires Q3 and Q4 residential organic growth.

**Cost inflation outside management's control.** Medical plan expense was the largest component of the 70bp people-cost headwind, and the CFO characterized it as an industry-wide problem. It does not reverse with volume. Fuel added 20bps at roughly 1.8% of sales.

**Impairment tail against negative tangible book.** Goodwill and intangibles at 61% of assets with tangible book at approximately negative $621M means a sustained deterioration in acquired-brand economics would eventually surface as impairment against an equity base with no asset backing. Not a near-term risk at current profitability; a real one in a prolonged demand reset.

**Regulatory and legal.** An FTC inquiry into post-employment restrictive covenants is unquantified: Rollins says a voluntary resolution is unlikely to be material but cannot predict the outcome and the FTC could litigate. California PAGA claims are ongoing. The company raised its Item 103 environmental-proceeding disclosure threshold to $1.0M, which reduces forward visibility into smaller matters. Pesticide-regulation restrictions and pest resistance to existing tools are slow-moving structural risks to service efficacy.

**Insurance retention.** High-deductible retained-risk programs across general liability, workers' compensation and auto for approximately 22,000 employees and a large fleet; letters of credit required by carriers rose to $84.6M from $82.4M. Adverse claims development flows straight to margin.

**Execution and governance.** A first-time CFO (Harkins, elevated internally from Principal Accounting Officer, effective June 15, 2026) inherited the guidance reset in his first reporting quarter, concurrent with an accountability restructuring at Orkin North America under Scott Weaver. No filing links the prior CFO's departure to the deterioration, and the stated reason was an external opportunity; the combination of first-time CFO, mid-year reset and simultaneous operational reorganization is an elevated-execution-risk period rather than a governance problem. The Rollins family group held approximately 38% at December 31, 2025, limiting float and preserving anti-takeover protection.

**Early consumer-credit signal.** The allowance for credit losses on financed receivables (largely termite/ancillary customer financing) rose from $11.0M to $13.0M against a $167.4M net balance, roughly 7.1% to 7.7%, while the trade allowance fell from $23.5M to $21.0M on a growing base. Immaterial to earnings, but directionally consistent with management's acknowledgment of pressure in lower income bands, and worth monitoring given termite/ancillary is now the growth engine.

**Timing risk.** Management flagged a hard Q3 comparison and steered margin improvement to Q4. Investors therefore get limited confirmation in October and full confirmation only in February 2027.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.9% sits essentially on top of 27.2% one-year realized (spread −0.3%), the IV percentile within the chain is 8%, put/call skew is balanced at −0.1%, and the term structure is flat (26.9% near, 26.7% far). Put/call volume ratio is 0.51 and open-interest ratio 0.88. Short interest is 4.5% of float at 2.9 days to cover, uncrowded. The prior snapshot's 1166% ATM IV reading was a data artifact and should be disregarded; the meaningful change since June 18 is the price (−16.1%) and a modest rise in put/call open interest (0.66 → 0.88).

Interpretation: the market has repriced the equity but is not positioning for a further discontinuity. That supports the view that the derating is a multiple reset already substantially absorbed rather than a distress situation, and it means there is no cheap-volatility entry advantage. For a name where the central uncertainty resolves only over two more quarters, evenly distributed implied volatility is consistent with the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($48, +27%).** The Q2 demand air pocket was cyclical (a late mosquito season plus soft low-income consumer demand) and the improvement at the end of June extends. Residential organic recovers toward 5%, total organic reaches or exceeds 6%, and the incremental-margin floor of 10% proves conservative as the company laps its 2025 staffing build, the Wheels fleet agreement lands, and procurement initiatives contribute. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.40 rather than $1.30, estimates inflect positive, and the multiple re-expands toward 34x as the compounder framing is restored. The balance sheet supports an accelerated M&A cadence at better prices as private-equity competition for targets cools with financing costs. Rentokil's decision to retire its margin target and invest more in North America hands Rollins relative pricing latitude.

**Bear case ($28, −26%).** Consumer-initiated demand erosion is structural: the geographic uniformity of the decline is more consistent with a nationwide search-behavior change than with pest pressure, and a gradual channel shift needs no strategy change to produce the partial recovery management points to. Residential organic settles at 2-4%, dragging total organic below 6%. Medical inflation persists independent of volume, incremental margins hold near 10%, and FY2027 EPS lands at $1.20. The market completes the reset of the pest-control premium multiple toward broader business-services levels at 22-24x. Dividend coverage tightens if the Q4 cash-tax reversal underdelivers, and a prolonged demand reset eventually raises goodwill-impairment questions against negative tangible book.

**Base case ($40, +6%).** Organic growth stabilizes near the 6% guide, incremental margins recover to the mid-teens in FY2027 without reaching the 30% medium-term target, M&A contributes 2-3 points, and EPS reaches consensus $1.30 for FY2027. The multiple holds near 31x on a recognizable but lower-growth compounder. Total return of roughly 8% including the dividend over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has declined from 6.7 (February) to 6.1 (May) to 5.7 (June) to 5.2 now. The decline is warranted by confirmed fundamental deterioration rather than by price action: guidance cut on both organic growth and incremental margin, pretax income down 0.8% year over year, consensus down 6% across both forward years, and a two-notch sell-side downgrade. The 16% price decline since the June review partially offsets that (the forward multiple compressed from roughly 36x to 32.3x on FY2026) but not enough to convert a fair-return profile into an asymmetric one.

**Base case target: $40** (30.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.30). **Bull: $48. Bear: $28.** Upside to base +6.0%, to bull +27%; downside to bear −26%. Twelve-month horizon; the resolving events are the Q3 report in October 2026 and the Q4/FY2026 report in February 2027, when the Q4-weighted margin improvement and the cash-tax reversal are both testable.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 residential organic growth at or above 5%, or total organic at or above 6.5%, confirming the demand break was cyclical.
2. Adjusted incremental EBITDA margin at or above 18% in any quarter, demonstrating the cost structure has re-aligned and that 10% was in fact a floor.
3. Clustered open-market insider buying (three or more officers or directors) or a materially accelerated buyback at prices below $40, given no insider has purchased through a 43% drawdown.
4. Price toward $32-34 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $1.28, which would put the stock near 25x on an intact algorithm.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 2026 residential organic growth below 3%, or total organic below 5.5%, which would make the structural-channel reading the base case.
2. FY2026 organic guidance cut again below 6%, or the incremental-margin floor of 10% breached in Q3.
3. Q4 2026 free cash flow that fails to reverse the H1 cash-tax drag, taking FY2026 FCF conversion below 100% and dividend coverage above roughly 70% of FCF.
4. Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or an unfavorable, quantified FTC resolution on restrictive covenants.
5. A bounce to $44-46 without confirming organic-growth data. Sell into it, since that level restores the full pre-reset multiple on reduced earnings.

**Reassessment date: the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026).** That print carries the hard comparison management flagged, the first read on whether the late-June lead improvement extended, and the first quarter in which the reduced staffing plan should show in incremental margin.

## Conclusion

Rollins remains a high-quality operator: 75% recurring revenue, more than two million customers, 1.0x leverage, 21-25x interest coverage, capex at 0.75% of sales, and free cash flow that converted at 123% of net income in FY2025. Nothing in the second quarter threatens solvency, covenants, or the dividend.

What the quarter did threaten is the premise the multiple rested on. Adjusted incremental EBITDA margin of 6.5% against 22.5% for FY2025, pretax income down 0.8% on 7.9% revenue growth, reported EPS growth carried by a 180bp tax-rate benefit and a shrinking share count, organic growth guidance cut to at least 6% from 7-8%, and estimates down 6% across both forward years constitute a genuine reset of the earnings algorithm rather than a single soft print. Management cannot identify the cause of the residential lead-volume decline with confidence, and neither can we; the Rentokil read-across confirms the problem is market-wide without distinguishing cyclical from structural.

At 32.3x FY2026E and 29.0x FY2027E with a 3.6% FCF yield and a 1.94% dividend, the stock is priced closer to fair than to distressed after a 43% drawdown. Probability-weighted scenarios produce roughly a mid-single-digit total return with a −26% bear case that requires only that the structural reading prove correct. That is a hold: existing owners are compensated by a covered dividend and a strong balance sheet while the demand question resolves; new capital has no urgent reason to commit before the October print, and a better entry exists in the low $30s if the Q3 data disappoints. The honest classification of this HOLD is uncertainty, not conviction.
