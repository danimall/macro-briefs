**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Globus Medical is the #2–3 pure-play spine company globally, built through two acquisitions: the all-stock NuVasive merger (closed September 2023, roughly doubling the company) and the all-cash Nevro deal (closed April 3, 2025, adding spinal cord stimulation). The core business is high quality: a vertically integrated robotics-plus-implant ecosystem (ExcelsiusGPS), ~68–69% underlying gross margin, ~20% free-cash-flow margin, and a near-debt-free balance sheet with ~$799M of cash and an Altman Z-score around 6.2. Organic US spine momentum is real: Q1 2026 spine implants rose ~$38.9M and neuromonitoring ~$10.1M independent of the Nevro contribution.

The complication is that the reported numbers overstate the fundamental improvement. The 2024→2025 gross-margin surge (COGS falling from ~41.1% to ~32.6% of sales) is largely the roll-off of a $215.4M NuVasive inventory fair-value step-up, a one-time accounting tailwind rather than operating leverage. FY2025 net income of $537.9M was further flattered by a $118.8M non-taxable bargain-purchase gain on Nevro and a deferred-tax valuation-allowance release that pushed the effective rate to 11.1%. A scoring framework anchored on trailing GAAP net income or trailing P/E would misread the trajectory. On a cleaner forward basis — consensus FY26 EPS of $4.74 rising to $5.15 next year — the stock trades around 17x, a reasonable multiple for mid-single-to-high-single-digit organic growth plus the Nevro turnaround optionality.

We rate GMED HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The valuation is supportive and estimate revisions are positive, but three specific, monitorable overhangs prevent an upgrade to BUY: an FDA Warning Letter on the flagship ExcelsiusGPS platform open roughly 19 months with no disclosed resolution; a dual-class structure concentrating ~66% of voting power in the Paul family; and a Nevro integration where the bargain-purchase gain signals a distressed franchise, not a growth engine. None of these is fatal, but each is a falsifiable downside catalyst the market has not fully resolved.

## Company Overview

Globus Medical, founded in 2003 and headquartered in Audubon, PA, is an engineering-driven musculoskeletal device maker reporting a single segment managed across two product categories:

- **Musculoskeletal Solutions (~96% of revenue; $732.98M of $759.85M in Q1 2026):** spine implants, orthopedic trauma, hip/knee/extremities, regenerative biologics, intraoperative neuromonitoring via NuVasive Clinical Services, and, new via Nevro, spinal cord stimulation for chronic pain (the Senza/HFX 10kHz platform).
- **Enabling Technologies (~4%; $26.87M in Q1 2026):** the ExcelsiusGPS robotics/navigation/imaging platform plus the Excelsius XR augmented-reality system launched in 2025. This is the strategic differentiator and the "razor" that pulls through implant sales, though it is small, lumpy, and tied to long capital sales cycles.

The competitive structure pits GMED against Medtronic, DePuy Synthes (J&J), Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, Smith+Nephew, and the disruptive small-cap Alphatec. Roughly 20% of sales are international. The moat is moderate: scale in spine, a vertically integrated robotics-plus-implant ecosystem (ExcelsiusGPS is one of few credible competitors to Medtronic's Mazor), heavy in-house manufacturing, and rapid product cadence. Most products are 510(k)-cleared with limited long-term clinical data, and the industry faces structural pricing pressure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** reflects the two-merger story: $1.02B (2022) → $1.57B (2023) → $2.52B (2024) → $2.94B (2025), the last representing +16.7% YoY (US +18.4% to $2,367.6M; International +10% to $571.3M). Nevro contributed roughly $254M of partial-year revenue, but organic spine momentum is independently visible.

**The gross-margin shift is the most important and most misunderstood P&L dynamic.** COGS fell to ~32.6% of sales in 2025 from ~41.1% in 2024. The cause is the $215.4M non-cash NuVasive inventory fair-value step-up rolling off, leaving only a small Nevro step-up (~$19M). This normalizes reported profitability to GMED's true ~68–69% gross-margin level rather than representing a sustainable margin expansion. Operating income reached $479.8M (2024: $166.0M) and net income $537.9M (2024: $103.0M), with the ~$46M valuation-allowance release producing an 11.1% effective tax rate. Operating cash flow was ~$753M against ~$165M capex (largely surgical instrument sets loaned to hospitals), yielding ~$589M FCF and a high-quality ~20% FCF margin.

**Q1 2026** continued the trend: revenue $759.85M (+27% YoY; US +25%, International +35.6%), with Nevro adding $82.7M. Net income was $124.3M (+65%), diluted EPS $0.90 versus $0.54, with gross COGS down further to 30.8% of sales and an effective tax rate of 20.9% (down from 27.2%) helped by OBBBA benefits and integration. Operating cash flow was $202.4M.

**The balance sheet is fortress-grade.** Cash plus marketable securities of ~$799M, total equity of $4.73B, and effectively no debt after the $450M NuVasive convertible notes were retired in March 2025. The $400M revolver is undrawn, the current ratio is ~4.6, and D/E is ~0.16. The principal soft item is goodwill ($1.44B) plus intangibles ($721M), together ~40% of assets, with no impairments to date.

**Capital-allocation flag:** a new $500M buyback was authorized in May 2025 with $390M remaining at 3/31/26. The company repurchased ~$215M in 2025 but **zero in Q1 2026** despite the stock falling ~20% from January highs. That pause is inconsistent with an opportunistic repurchaser and merits monitoring; it could signal capital being reserved for additional M&A.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two legs. First, organic spine share gains: the Q1 2026 US spine-implant and neuromonitoring increases independent of Nevro indicate the core franchise is taking share in a low-growth industry, consistent with the robotics-led "procedural" strategy of bundling ExcelsiusGPS and Excelsius XR with consumables to defend price and win surgeon loyalty. Second, the Nevro/SCS optionality, which is better characterized as cheap cross-selling into the existing surgeon base than as a growth engine.

The earnings-surprise record is strong (four consecutive beats) and estimate revisions are positive but moderate: current-year EPS revised +6.4% over 90 days to $4.74, next-year +4.2% to $5.15. This is a constructive momentum profile, but it sits below the +30% revision velocity that would mark a true earnings inflection. The revision magnitude reflects a healthy, beating business rather than a market systematically underestimating a structural step-change. Headline GAAP growth (net income +422% in 2025) materially overstates the underlying improvement because of the accounting items described above.

Durability is reasonable but not exceptional. Spine faces persistent pricing erosion, payor scrutiny of lumbar fusion, hospital/payor consolidation, and physician-owned distributorships. GMED's scale and in-house manufacturing are genuine advantages within those headwinds, but they do not convert a mid-single-digit organic industry into a high-growth one.

## Valuation Assessment

GMED trades at ~$80, a market cap of ~$9.1B, against forward consensus EPS of $4.74 (FY26) and $5.15 (FY27), or roughly 17x and 15.5x forward. On trailing reported EPS of $3.92, the multiple is ~18.8x, but that trailing figure is distorted by the one-time gains inflating net income while the share count and bargain-purchase mechanics muddy comparability, so the forward figure is the more reliable anchor.

Peer benchmarking is instructive but heterogeneous. The Health Care Equipment cohort shows a median P/E of 44.3, dragged by names like Masimo (44.3x) and Insulet (35.7x) and rendered partly meaningless by negative-earnings peers (Bruker, Baxter, Glaukos). GMED at ~17x forward sits at roughly 40% of the peer median P/E. A discount is warranted given slower organic growth and the governance/regulatory overhangs, but the gap is wide enough that the stock is not stretched. The FCF yield (~$589M on ~$9.1B cap, ~6.5%) provides absolute support for a company with no debt and net cash.

The estimate-revision signal is positive but modest, and the stock has de-rated ~20% from its January 2026 high near $100 without identifiable fundamental deterioration; the May 8 Q1 print was a beat. That combination of rising estimates, lower price, and intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward and argues against a low valuation score. Analyst targets of $87 (low) to $122 (high), mean $110, imply ~9% to ~53% upside, with Needham at $117 (Buy) post-Q1. On stocks with positive revisions and sub-20x forward multiples, consensus targets tend to lag, but GMED's modest revision velocity tempers how aggressively to treat those targets as floors. We score valuation 7.0: supportive and asymmetric to the upside, but not a deep-value mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

GMED has consolidated into a scaled spine pure-play behind Medtronic and DePuy Synthes, with Stryker and Zimmer Biomet formidable and Alphatec disrupting the low end. The differentiator is the robotics-led procedural ecosystem — ExcelsiusGPS plus Excelsius XR augmented reality plus implants, biologics, and neuromonitoring — which bundles enabling technology with consumables to defend price and capture share. Organic Q1 spine and neuromonitoring growth supports the thesis that the core is gaining share. The Nevro acquisition pushes into neuromodulation/chronic pain, but that SCS market (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Saluda) is competitive and the acquired franchise is in decline; the strategic logic is surgeon-channel synergy and cheap optionality rather than buying a growth franchise.

Trajectory is the relevant question, and it is stable-to-modestly-improving in the core spine business but offset by a distressed acquired asset and a flagship platform under regulatory cloud. That nets to a 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-Q reports no material change to risk factors from the 10-K. The most company-specific risks:

1. **Open FDA Warning Letter on ExcelsiusGPS (issued July 2024, unresolved as of February 2026).** It cites complaint-handling and Medical Device Reporting procedural deficiencies at the Audubon facility, not a product-safety or shutdown issue, but it sits on the flagship growth platform and could delay clearances or escalate. The roughly 19 months open with no disclosed resolution is the single most important idiosyncratic overhang.
2. **Litigation.** The Pimenta matter produced a November 4, 2025 jury verdict of $28.7M against NuVasive; GMED accrued $43.1M (including interest and costs) in SG&A in 2025 and is appealing. The 4WEB and Moskowitz patent cases carry no accrual with loss not estimable.
3. **Double integration.** NuVasive and Nevro are being integrated simultaneously under ongoing "2024 Synergy Plan" and "2025 Strategic Integration Plan" restructurings (~$5M charges in Q1 2026).
4. **Nevro is a turnaround.** The bargain-purchase gain means GMED paid below fair value for a distressed SCS business whose value sits substantially in acquired NOLs rather than franchise strength.
5. **Structural industry pressure:** spine pricing erosion, payor scrutiny of fusion, consolidation, PODs, and residual tariff uncertainty (some tariffs struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026).
6. **Governance.** Executive Chairman David C. Paul and family control ~16.5% of shares but ~66% of voting power via Class B super-voting stock (10 votes/share). GMED uses NYSE controlled-company exemptions, so the board need not be majority-independent. With ~193M authorized-but-unissued Class B shares, the board can perpetuate control concentration. Minority Class A holders bear concentrated governance risk.

Short interest is modest at 3.5% of float (2.0 days to cover), indicating no crowded bearish positioning.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 25.1% against trailing 1-year realized volatility of 44.5%, a -19.5pp spread that places IV in the 3rd percentile of its own chain; options are cheap, signaling complacency rather than fear into any near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (2.0%) and the put/call volume ratio is just 0.10, with no elevated downside hedging. The term structure is in contango (25.1% near-term versus 33.7% at three months), a normal/complacent shape. Since the prior snapshot (May 27), ATM IV fell 9.4pp while price slipped 3.1%, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.21 to 0.76, a move toward balance but not a directional warning. The options market is not pricing an imminent shock and is not contradicting the fundamental view; cheap IV would favor patient option-based positioning for anyone wanting exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($117–$122):** Organic US spine share gains continue at a high-single-digit pace, the ExcelsiusGPS Warning Letter closes cleanly, Nevro stabilizes and contributes cross-sell synergy, and the net-cash balance sheet funds buybacks (the $390M authorization) or accretive tuck-ins. On ~$5.15 FY27 EPS at ~23x, justified by the procedural ecosystem moat and balance sheet, the stock reaches the low-$120s, consistent with the high analyst target.

**Bear Case ($60–$65):** The Warning Letter escalates or delays ExcelsiusGPS clearances, the Pimenta appeal fails (adding to the accrual), Nevro proves a slow-melting asset requiring further write-downs against the $721M intangibles, and spine pricing pressure compresses organic growth. On ~$4.50 EPS at ~14x amid de-rating, the stock revisits the mid-$60s, roughly -19% to -25% from current levels.

**Base Case ($90):** Organic spine growth in the high-single digits, Nevro neutral-to-modestly-additive, no Warning Letter escalation but no clean close either, and continued share gains offset by industry pricing. ~$5.15 FY27 EPS at ~17.5x supports ~$90, +12% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a quality franchise at a fair price held back from BUY by specific, resolvable overhangs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Disclosed closure or successful remediation of the ExcelsiusGPS FDA Warning Letter, removing the central platform overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of organic US spine growth sustained above ~8% with stable or expanding underlying (ex-step-up) gross margin, confirming durable operating leverage rather than accounting normalization.
- Resumption of buybacks under the $390M authorization at current prices, demonstrating capital-allocation conviction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Escalation of the Warning Letter to a consent decree, product hold, or clearance delay on ExcelsiusGPS.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment tied to Nevro, signaling the SCS franchise is deteriorating faster than modeled, or evidence of declining organic spine revenue.
- An adverse Pimenta appeal outcome materially above the $43.1M accrual, or new material litigation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), with interim monitoring of 8-K filings for Warning Letter status and board/officer changes following the June 4, 2026 annual-meeting cycle.

## Conclusion

Globus Medical is a financially pristine spine pure-play — near-debt-free, ~20% FCF margin, fortress balance sheet — with genuine organic share gains and a reasonable forward valuation that offers asymmetric upside relative to a de-rated price and rising (if modest) estimates. The overall score of 6.6 reflects those strengths. It stops short of a BUY for three concrete, falsifiable reasons rather than vague "valuation full" language: (1) the flagship ExcelsiusGPS platform carries an FDA Warning Letter open roughly 19 months with no disclosed resolution, a regulatory catalyst that could delay clearances or escalate; (2) ~66% of voting power rests with one family under controlled-company exemptions, leaving minority Class A holders with limited governance recourse and the ability to issue ~193M additional super-voting shares; and (3) the Nevro integration is a distressed-asset turnaround, evidenced by the bargain-purchase gain, and the headline FY25 earnings surge is largely a non-recurring inventory-step-up roll-off plus one-time gains rather than durable operating leverage. The buyback pause through a 20% drawdown and the absence of open-market insider buying reinforce a wait-and-confirm posture. Resolution of the Warning Letter or two quarters of confirmed organic operating leverage would justify an upgrade; escalation of any of these overhangs would justify exiting.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).**
