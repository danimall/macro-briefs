**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TSMC manufactures the chips that the AI buildout runs on. It holds roughly 70% of the global foundry market and a substantially higher share of leading-edge (sub-7nm) production, where its principal competitors, Samsung Foundry and Intel Foundry, trail by years. Intel Foundry generated approximately $174M in external revenue in Q1 2026 against TSMC's ~$35.9B; that gap is the clearest measure of the moat. The structural appeal of owning TSMC rather than any single chip designer is that it is architecture-agnostic: it fabricates Nvidia's GPUs, Apple's SoCs, AMD's 2nm EPYC, and any custom ASIC a hyperscaler designs. A shift in demand from training GPUs to inference ASICs, or from one accelerator vendor to another, reshuffles TSMC's customer mix without changing its total order book. That insulation makes this name the highest-quality way to express the AI-infrastructure thesis even as individual chip designers face share-vector risk.

The fundamentals are running hot. Q1 2026 was the fourth consecutive record-profit quarter: revenue NT$1.134 trillion (+35% YoY), net income NT$572.48B (+58% YoY), and a 66.2% gross margin that is extraordinary for a capital-intensive manufacturer and reflects pricing power in a sold-out environment. Management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to >30% in USD terms, guided Q2 to $39.0–40.2B, and flagged 2026 capex at the high end of $52–56B. Every 2nm wafer through 2026 is reportedly pre-booked across five Kaohsiung fabs, giving near-term revenue unusual visibility for a cyclical business. Consensus EPS estimates have risen ~9–10% over 90 days across all forward periods, and the company has beaten on EPS for four straight quarters.

The case is constrained by one risk that operating performance cannot mitigate: the concentration of leading-edge capacity in Taiwan against a backdrop of cross-strait tension, underscored by the reported Xi–Trump exchange over Taiwan in May 2026. Overseas fabs in Arizona, Japan, and Germany diversify geography but only partially hedge a conflict scenario, which would be catastrophic and is genuinely unhedgeable. We rate TSM a BUY, scaled rather than maximized, with the Taiwan tail as the explicit reason conviction is capped below where the operating fundamentals alone would place it.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, a pure-play contract manufacturer that fabricates chips designed by fabless customers (Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm) and integrated device makers, rather than selling its own branded products. Its competitive position rests on three pillars: a multi-year process-technology lead (N3 ramped, N2/2nm now ramping across five fabs in Kaohsiung), unmatched advanced-packaging capacity through CoWoS that is the gating resource for AI accelerators, and deep co-design relationships that lock in customers years ahead of production.

The demand base has shifted structurally. High-performance computing, which now includes AI accelerators, reached 61% of Q1 2026 revenue, displacing smartphones as the dominant driver. Advanced nodes (≤7nm) accounted for roughly 74% of wafer revenue, with sub-3nm at ~25%. TSMC raised its global semiconductor TAM forecast to >$1.5 trillion by 2030, up from a prior $1 trillion estimate, framing the AI transition as a multi-year demand expansion rather than a single-cycle spike.

TSMC files as a foreign private issuer (Form 20-F, most recent April 16, 2026, covering FY2025) rather than a 10-K, so standard US-GAAP XBRL detail and certain governance/insider disclosures are lighter than for domestic filers. The financial figures here are drawn from reported results and corroborated reporting; segment and balance-sheet granularity below the headline level is therefore less complete than for a comparable US company.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 delivered revenue exceeding $120B and net income of approximately $54.55B, a net margin in the mid-40s percent range. Q4 2025 alone produced revenue of $33.73B (+20.5% YoY) and net income of $15.2B (+35% YoY), with momentum accelerating into 2026.

Q1 2026 beat consensus across the board: revenue (~$35.9B) topped the NT$1.127T estimate, net income exceeded the NT$543.32B estimate, and gross margin of 66.2% beat guidance. A 66% gross margin while simultaneously absorbing the lower yields of an N2 ramp and the cost of three sets of overseas fabs is the single most impressive figure in the financials. It indicates that TSMC is pricing into a supply-constrained market, with management noting that demand still significantly outpaces supply. Monthly revenue cadence remains strong, with May 2026 at roughly $13.2B (+30.1% YoY).

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats — +4.4% (Q1 2026), +8.0% (Q3 2025), +5.2% (Q1 2025), +3.7% (Q2 2025). Estimate revisions are uniformly positive: current-year consensus EPS has risen 9.7% over 90 days, next-year 9.0%, with current quarter up ~8.6% and next quarter up ~8.9% over the same window. These are not trough-recovery revisions off a depressed base; they are upward revisions on a company already at peak profitability, which is rarer and reflects genuine consensus catch-up to a demand surprise.

The principal financial tension is capex. The 2026 budget, now expected at the high end (up as much as ~37% YoY), funds the N2 ramp, CoWoS packaging expansion, and overseas fabs. Rising depreciation will pressure free-cash-flow conversion even as margins stay elevated; the bull case requires demand to stay ahead of this supply wave. TSMC historically runs a net-cash balance sheet with strong operating cash flow, which gives it the capacity to self-fund this cycle. [unverified — refinancing/covenant detail not in sources] The Arizona fab generated $514M of profit in its first full year and exceeded that in Q1 2026 alone, which materially de-risks the long-standing concern that overseas expansion would be heavily margin-dilutive.

## Growth Analysis

Three demand signals reinforce the growth thesis with unusual durability. First, the order book is constrained by TSMC's own capacity rather than by demand, the largest single-node concentration ever attempted. Second, the customer ramp pipeline: Nvidia's Vera Rubin is straining the Taiwan supply chain, AMD began ramping its 6th-Gen EPYC "Venice" on N2 in May 2026, and Apple remains a long-standing top customer. Third, the breadth of the AI-capex commitment from hyperscalers, which TSMC services as the common manufacturing layer regardless of which chip architecture wins.

The estimate-revision signal is material. Current-year EPS consensus has risen to $15.62 from $14.24 ninety days ago (+9.7%), and next-year to $19.53 from $17.91 (+9.0%). Combined with the guided revenue growth and four consecutive beats, the momentum is intact. The revision velocity is meaningful but does not reach the kind of magnitude that signals a trough inflection; this is steady upward drift on an already-strong base, which is appropriate to weight as a confirmation of durable growth rather than as a deep-value signal.

The growth score is held at 8.0 rather than higher because the demand is now concentrated in a single theme. With ~61% of revenue tied to HPC/AI, an air-pocket in AI capex would expose the high fixed-cost base more sharply than the diversified smartphone-led demand of prior cycles. The growth trajectory in the score history declined from 9.0 to 8.0 over recent reports, and that adjustment reflects this single-theme concentration risk maturing, not a deterioration in the demand signal itself, which remains robust.

## Valuation Assessment

At the current price of ~$409–413, TSM trades at roughly 35x trailing EPS of $10.43. That trailing figure is the wrong anchor given the earnings trajectory. On consensus current-year EPS of $15.62, the forward multiple is ~26x; on next-year EPS of $19.53, it is ~21x. Against high-teens-to-20%+ forward earnings growth, that produces a PEG comfortably near or below 1.5, which is reasonable for the dominant franchise in a structurally growing market.

The thematic context matters for the valuation anchor. The semiconductor complex is re-rating on a genuine demand expansion, with multiple companies across the AI-hardware chain posting 30%+ revenue growth simultaneously. Using TSMC's pre-AI historical P/E range as a benchmark would understate fair value in this regime. At ~26x forward on the highest-quality, most-insulated name in that cohort, the multiple is not stretched. By contrast, the premium-multiple software names trade above 50x, where valuations already price continued beats; TSMC at ~26x forward leaves room for the multiple to hold while earnings compound.

Analyst targets support upside: consensus mean $467.84, median $472.50, high $600, low $354. With the stock near $409, the median sits ~16% above current and the mean ~14%, and on a name where estimates are rising those targets tend to lag and get revised upward. The recommendation distribution is constructive — 5 strong buy, 12 buy, 2 hold, 0 sell — and has improved (a hold was upgraded in the most recent period).

The valuation score is held at 6.5 rather than higher for two reasons. First, the multiple, while reasonable for the growth, is not a deep discount; the easy re-rating from the trough has happened, with the stock up ~92% over the trailing year. Second, and more importantly, the Taiwan concentration risk is a structural reason a discount to a hypothetical "no-geopolitics" fair value is warranted, and that discount is appropriate rather than a mispricing to be arbitraged. The price appreciation itself is not a reason to mark down valuation — it tracked fundamental improvement — but the absence of a clear discount-to-intrinsic-value, combined with the unhedgeable tail, keeps this dimension below 7.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position is the strongest in the semiconductor industry and arguably among the widest moats in any sector. The process-technology lead at N2/2nm is multi-year, and the gap to competitors is widening rather than narrowing: Intel Foundry's external Q1 revenue against TSMC's is a roughly 200-to-1 disparity at the leading edge. Samsung Foundry remains a distant second. CoWoS advanced packaging is a separate chokepoint that TSMC dominates and that is essential to AI accelerators, compounding the foundry lead.

The architecture-agnostic position is the key structural advantage. When the world model flags risk that custom inference ASICs (D-Matrix and similar) erode Nvidia's GPU dominance, or that memory ASP durability fades, those are share-vector risks for specific designers and memory makers. TSMC manufactures across all of those outcomes. A migration from training GPUs to inference ASICs changes which customer's wafers fill the fabs, not whether the fabs are full, which is why TSM screens as a cleaner AI expression than the chip designers themselves.

The competitive-position score declined from 9.5 to 7.5 across the recent report history, a sharp move that warrants scrutiny. The operating evidence does not support a deterioration in TSMC's competitive standing — share, technology lead, and pricing power all strengthened over the period. The downward adjustment appears to reflect the maturing recognition that TSMC's dominance is now so complete that it carries systemic/concentration risk (it is >40% of Taiwan's TAIEX) and that the durability question has shifted from "can it lead" to "can the AI demand sustain the capacity it is building." We set the score at 8.5, modestly above the most recent reading, because the underlying competitive trajectory is improving, not stable or eroding, and the framework rewards trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is Taiwan concentration. The bulk of leading-edge capacity remains on the island, and the reported Xi–Trump exchange over a possible clash regarding Taiwan in May 2026 is a live reminder that the largest risk is exogenous, binary, and not reflected in operating fundamentals. A conflict, blockade, or escalation would be catastrophic for TSMC and global chip supply. Overseas fabs (Arizona ~$165B committed plus an additional ~$20B injection in May 2026, Japan's Kumamoto, Germany) diversify geography but only partially hedge this tail. This is the single reason the risk score is held at 5.0 — not because the probability is high in any given quarter, but because the magnitude is total and the hedge is incomplete.

Customer concentration is secondary but real: Nvidia is now the largest customer, with Apple and AMD also concentrated. AI revenue is concentrated among a handful of hyperscaler/accelerator buyers, so a pause in AI capex or in-sourcing by a major customer would hit hard. The single-theme dependence amplifies this. Input-supply risk from Middle East conflict (helium, hydrogen, energy) was addressed by management as mitigated by multiple sourcing and safety inventory, consistent with the world model's view that the Hormuz disruption is a real but managed tail.

The capex-versus-free-cash-flow tension is a financial risk rather than a solvency one: the spending against rising depreciation tests FCF conversion, and the thesis depends on demand outrunning the supply wave. Tariff and trade-policy uncertainty shapes capex decisions but is a wildcard rather than a quantified threat. A governance/insider-data gap exists due to foreign-filer disclosure limits; we flag it as unresolved rather than assume it benign.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that we read as event-hedging rather than a fundamental warning. Near-term ATM IV of 59.6% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 36.4% (a rich 23.2pp spread, 76th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (59.6% near-term vs. 48.2% at six months), the signature of hedging into a known catalyst. Put/call skew of 8.9% near-term and a put/call volume ratio of 1.43 confirm elevated near-term put demand, and unusual activity in the $360 June put (10.2x volume/OI) reflects downside hedging. IV rose 3.6pp as the price fell 3.2% over three sessions, and the P/C OI ratio ticked from 1.14 to 1.26. The disciplined read is that elevated front-end vol around scheduled catalysts overstates imminent fundamental stress; the longer-dated structure (48.2% at six months) and the modest longer-dated skew are more consistent with a stable underlying than the front end suggests. The recent pullback from ~$447 to ~$409 on unchanged fundamentals improves entry rather than signaling deterioration.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

TSMC is the indispensable, architecture-agnostic manufacturing layer of a multi-year AI buildout. The 2nm book is sold out, margins are holding despite overseas dilution that is proving milder than feared (Arizona profitable in year one), and the competitive moat is widening, with competitors two-plus nodes behind at the leading edge. On next-year EPS of $19.53, a 28–30x multiple (justified by the moat and growth) supports a price of $545–585, broadly consistent with the $600 Street high. Bull case: **$560**, roughly +37% from current.

### Bear Case

AI capex disappoints and the order book softens, or, more severely, cross-strait tension escalates into a blockade or conflict that impairs Taiwan capacity. In a demand-air-pocket scenario without geopolitical escalation, the multiple compresses to ~18x on next-year EPS as growth re-rates, implying ~$350, near the Street low of $354 and roughly -14% from current. A genuine Taiwan conflict scenario is not modelable as a price target; it is a capital-impairment tail that the position size, not the target, must account for. Bear case (ex-conflict): **$350**, roughly -14%.

### Base Case

Demand stays ahead of the supply wave, margins hold near 65%, and estimates continue to drift higher. A ~24x multiple on next-year EPS of $19.53 yields **$470**, in line with the analyst median of $472.50 and roughly +15% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $470** (24x next-year EPS of $19.53), ~+15% from current ~$409.
- **Bull case target: $560** (~29x next-year EPS), ~+37% from current.
- **Bear case target: $350** (ex-conflict, ~18x next-year EPS), ~-14% from current.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the next earnings report (mid-July 2026 for Q2) and the late-July hyperscaler capex guides as the key checkpoints; Q2 revenue guided to $39.0–40.2B is the near-term test.
- **Upside/downside from current (~$409):** +15% to base, +37% to bull, -14% to bear (ex-conflict).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY26 revenue growth guidance cut below +20% in USD terms, or a quarter where revenue growth decelerates below +20% YoY, signaling the AI demand air-pocket.
2. Gross margin falling below ~58% on a sustained basis, indicating either pricing erosion in a no-longer-sold-out market or worse-than-expected overseas dilution.
3. 90-day EPS estimate revisions turning negative, reversing the current upward momentum.
4. Material cross-strait escalation (blockade, kinetic action) — this is a hard exit irrespective of the operating fundamentals, given the unhedgeable nature of the tail.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the cleanest, highest-quality way to own the AI-infrastructure buildout: it manufactures across every chip architecture and customer, insulating it from the share-vector risks that threaten individual designers. Q1 2026 set a fourth consecutive profit record, margins are extraordinary, the 2nm book is sold out, estimates are rising, and at ~26x forward 2026 and ~21x forward 2027 EPS the valuation is reasonable for the growth and moat. The recommendation is BUY, sized modestly rather than at maximum, with the Taiwan concentration tail as the explicit and unhedgeable reason conviction is capped. The recent pullback on unchanged fundamentals improves the entry; near-term elevated options volatility is event-hedging into the catalyst window rather than a fundamental warning. We would add into further weakness toward the high-$300s and hold for the 12-month base case of ~$470, monitoring the invalidation triggers, particularly any sign that demand is no longer outrunning the capacity TSMC is building.
