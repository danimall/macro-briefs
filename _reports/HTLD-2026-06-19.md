**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Heartland Express is an asset-based dry-van truckload carrier sitting at the trough of the worst freight recession in a generation, now showing the first clear signs of a cyclical turn. Revenue collapsed from a $1.21B peak in 2023 to $806M in 2025 (down 33%), the operating ratio blew out to 107.1%, and the company posted a $52.5M net loss (-$0.67/share). The investment question is not solvency: net leverage is negligible (D/E 0.03), the balance sheet has been deleveraged by $337M since the 2022 CFI acquisition, and the business generated $89.3M of operating cash flow in 2025 (11.1% of revenue) even while reporting GAAP losses. The question is operating leverage on a cycle recovery.

Q1 2026 supplied the turn: operating ratio improved to 101.9% from 106.8% a year earlier, and the net loss narrowed to $4.8M (-$0.06) from $13.9M (-$0.18). Estimate revisions are rising sharply — current-year EPS consensus moved from -$0.22 ninety days ago to +$0.02, and next-year sits at $0.51. Management frames the recovery as "started, but may not fully materialize until later in 2026," citing industry capacity reductions. The CEO's open-market purchase near the cyclical low (~$245K at ~$8.20 in August 2025) reinforces the trough-buying thesis.

The complication is price. At $14.44 the stock has appreciated roughly 65% from its 52-week low of $6.99 and trades above the $12.40 mean analyst target, above the $12.00 median, and at roughly 1.5x book. Normalized earnings support a higher number, but the company's own hedging on recovery timing, an elevated diesel environment, and a self-insurance retention that has nearly doubled in three years leave thin margin for a delayed upcycle. The cyclical thesis is correct; the entry price has largely caught up to it. We rate HTLD a HOLD with an upgrade path on confirmed sequential operating-ratio improvement and a pullback toward book.

## Company Overview

Heartland Express (founded 1978, public since 1986; North Liberty, Iowa) hauls non-perishable freight for major shippers, concentrated in the short-to-medium-haul market — roughly 77% of loads run under 500 miles, with average length of haul below 400 miles. The model competes on premium service at "compensatory rates" rather than price, targeting just-in-time shippers in retail, manufacturing, and parcel.

The company operates under the Heartland Express, Millis Transfer, and Smith Transport brands, plus CFI for Mexico cross-border. It runs 26 terminals (25 U.S., 1 Mexico), is overwhelmingly company-driver based (98% of miles; independent contractors are now under 2%), and reports as a single segment. Stated financial goals are an operating ratio in the low-to-mid 80s, profitable organic and acquisitive growth, and a return to a debt-free balance sheet (the company operated debt-free until the 2022 CFI acquisition).

The competitive differentiators are quality-based rather than cost-based: an unusually young fleet (tractors averaging 2.6 years, trailers 7.3 years), a top "satisfactory" DOT safety rating across all carriers, driver compensation positioned among industry-best with below-industry turnover, and a long roster of customer "Carrier of the Year" awards. In a fragmented, commoditized industry with thousands of carriers, this is a service/safety moat, not a structural cost advantage.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year cyclical collapse is unambiguous:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 968.0 | 1,207.5 | 1,047.5 | 805.7 |
| Operating income ($M) | 188.4 | 42.4 | (20.2) | (57.4) |
| Net income ($M) | ~133 | ~15 | (29.7) | (52.5) |
| Operating ratio | ~80% | ~96% | 101.9% | 107.1% |
| Long-term debt ($M) | 399.1 | 290.7 | 191.7 | 154.1 |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | 855.5 | 865.3 | 822.6 | 755.3 |

Revenue fell 23% in 2025 alone, driven by both deliberate fleet right-sizing (headcount dropped from ~5,700 average in 2024 to ~3,630 by end-January 2026) and soft rates. The 107.1% operating ratio (104.7% adjusted) sits dramatically above the low-80s target.

The cash-flow profile is what separates this from a distressed situation. Despite the GAAP loss, the business produced $89.3M of operating cash flow in 2025 and $23.2M in Q1 2026 (13.1% of revenue), bridged by large non-cash D&A of $159M. Reported 2025 free cash flow was -$66.9M because capex ($156M) exceeded OCF, but that figure is gross: the company received $129.9M from equipment sales, leaving net investing use at only $26M. Equipment disposal gains are a meaningful and recurring earnings lever: $23.4M in 2025, $7.3M in Q1 2026 alone, with management guiding $25-35M for 2026. Heartland's declining-balance depreciation on new tractors deliberately sets up these gains, and they are real cash, but they flatter results and mask the depth of the core operating loss. Adjusted operating ratio excluding gains would be worse, and any rigorous read of the recovery should separate core trucking economics from equipment-trading gains.

The balance sheet is the clear strength. Since the 2022 CFI/Smith acquisitions, Heartland has repaid $337M of debt. By Q1 2026 it had fully retired the Smith Transport debt and carried $149.9M on the CFI term loan (maturing August 31, 2027, with minimum payments pre-funded through maturity). D/E is 0.03, net leverage well inside the 2.75x covenant (in compliance every quarter since inception), and cash had built to $44.5M. The Altman Z-Score sits firmly in the safe zone; the Piotroski F-score of 3/5 reflects current losses offset by positive OCF that exceeds net income. Bankruptcy is not a concern — the issue is profitability.

Capital allocation priorities are CFI debt paydown, then maintaining the modest $0.02/quarter dividend (~$6.2M/year, well under the $10M covenant cap). Buybacks are paused (1.2M shares repurchased in 2025, none in Q1 2026; 4.8M remain authorized). Management states it is "nearing the place where all alternatives will be equally available again."

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is entirely a cycle-recovery story, and the leading indicators are turning. The Q1 2026 operating ratio improved roughly 490bps YoY, with management noting March was materially stronger than the weather-impacted January and February. The thesis rests on excess trucking capacity finally exiting the market: small-carrier bankruptcies, plus FMCSA enforcement of English-proficiency rules and restrictions on non-domiciled CDLs, both of which tighten effective driver supply and support rate recovery.

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal in the data. Current-year EPS consensus has moved from -$0.22 (90 days ago) to +$0.02, a +110.2% revision; next-year EPS sits at $0.51, revised up 26.9% over 90 days. The earnings-surprise record is supportive — three beats in the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters beating by 50% and 87% on a narrowing-loss basis.

The operating leverage math is the core of the bull case. On ~$806M of revenue at a 107% OR, even a return to a mid-90s OR swings the company to material profitability; a return toward the low-80s target would produce earnings power well above anything visible in trailing results. This is why trailing P/E (-25.6x) is meaningless — the trailing period is trough earnings. The relevant question is whether and when the cycle delivers that operating leverage.

The growth signal is tempered in two ways. First, management itself hedges that the recovery "may not fully materialize until later in 2026." Second, the estimate revision velocity applies to a stock that has already moved 65% off its low and trades above consensus targets; the inflection signal is strongest when the price has not yet caught up to the rising estimates, which is no longer cleanly the case here.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis tightens. Trailing P/E is uninformative at a cyclical trough, so the relevant frames are forward/normalized earnings, book value, and peer comparables.

On book value, equity of $755M against ~77.5M shares puts book around $9.75/share, so $14.44 is roughly 1.5x book, a premium for a business currently losing money on a GAAP basis, justified only if normalized earnings power is materially above trailing.

On forward earnings, next-year consensus of $0.51 implies a forward P/E around 28x, and current-year consensus near breakeven ($0.02) is not usable. A 28x multiple on the first year of a recovery is not obviously cheap, though it can be defended if 2027 earnings continue to ramp toward the low-80s OR target.

The peer set trades at distorted multiples because most carriers are also at or near trough earnings — MRTN at 102.9x, WERN and RXO negative, SNDR at 68.2x, ARCB at 70.1x (median 70.1x). These multiples reflect depressed denominators, not a stable comp; they confirm the sector is mid-recovery rather than offering a clean relative anchor.

The cleanest valuation signal is the analyst target structure: mean $12.40, median $12.00, high $16.00, low $10.00, with the current $14.44 price sitting above both the mean and median and near the upper end of the range. On the usual rule that analyst targets lag rising estimates on cheap inflection stocks, targets may be revised up. But that adjustment applies most forcefully when a stock is cheap relative to improving estimates; here the stock has already outrun the consensus target, the forward multiple is not low (~28x), and the recovery is partially priced. The recent two-day pullback from $16.18 (June 8) to $14.44 is consistent with the market re-testing how much recovery is already embedded.

We score valuation at 4.0. A compressed or negative trailing multiple is not a buy signal in itself, and a stock trading above its mean target, above 1.5x book, and at ~28x forward in the first recovery year does not offer the asymmetric upside that a higher score requires. The market has done much of the work the cycle thesis would deliver.

## Competitive Landscape

The truckload industry is highly competitive and fragmented, with thousands of carriers and low switching costs. Heartland competes primarily against other dry-van truckload carriers in the short/medium-haul segment, with brokers, LTL, and intermodal at the margins. The 2023-2025 freight recession was a sector-wide capacity-versus-demand imbalance — peers including Werner, Schneider, and Knight-Swift all ran depressed operating ratios — so Heartland's losses reflect the cycle, not idiosyncratic share loss.

Heartland's structural advantages should help it recapture premium-rate freight as demand returns: the youngest fleet in its peer group (lower maintenance cost, less capex catch-up, strong used-equipment gains), a debt-light balance sheet that survives the downturn comfortably, and an industry-leading service and safety reputation. These are genuine differentiators, though they are quality-of-service advantages rather than a durable cost moat.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Customer concentration is creeping up — top 5/10/25 customers rose to 32%/47%/63% of 2025 revenue from 26%/40%/60% in 2024, and for the first time in years one customer exceeded 10% of revenue. Part of this reflects deliberately shrinking the fleet to the most profitable freight, but it increases the downside from any single bid loss in an industry where contracts are short-term and re-bid, often annually. The CFI integration has underperformed materially, culminating in a $19M trade-name impairment at end-2025. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on cycle recovery, capped by integration drag and rising concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Prolonged freight recession (dominant risk).** Demand has been weak for three straight years, longer than management expected, and recovery timing depends on capacity exiting the market, a process that is observable but not guaranteed on management's late-2026 timeline. Tariffs, consumer spending, and inventory cycles all add volatility. Until rates and volumes recover, the company runs at a loss.

**CFI integration (partially crystallized).** The largest deal in company history has underperformed; management has been unable to achieve typical post-acquisition results. The $19M trade-name write-off in 2025 is taken, but $322.6M of goodwill and ~$69.5M of intangibles remain on the balance sheet, with further impairment possible if performance lags.

**Self-insurance / litigation severity (escalating).** This was the one risk factor management amended and restated in the Q1 2026 10-Q. Auto-liability retention has ratcheted from $3.0M/claim (April 2023) to $5.0M/claim with a $6.5M aggregate corridor (April 2026). Insurance and claims expense rose to 7.2% of revenue in 2025 (from 4.9%) and 7.3% in Q1 2026. Reinsurers are exiting trucking amid "nuclear verdicts." The company also carries no D&O insurance and no corporate-wide cybersecurity insurance, which is unusual and adds tail risk.

**Fuel volatility (acute near-term).** DOE diesel spiked to ~$5.18 in March 2026 and ~$5.50 in April from ~$3.65 in January-February, attributed to Middle East conflict. Fuel surcharges do not cover empty, out-of-route, or idle miles — all elevated in a soft freight environment — so rising fuel is a direct margin headwind. A $1.00/gal increase equals roughly $12.6M pretax. This is the most concrete near-term threat to the recovery narrative, and it is tied to the unresolved Middle East conflict.

**Governance / concentrated control.** The Gerdin family and insiders control ~45% of stock; the company has opted out of Nevada's control-share statute. Michael Gerdin holds the combined CEO/President/Chairman roles. There is also an unusually dense cluster of Item 5.02 director/officer-change filings (October 2025 through May 2026) — five in roughly seven months. This could reflect post-CFI restructuring but warrants monitoring against the company's own flagged key-employee retention risk.

These are largely priced (the sector cyclicality and balance-sheet strength are well understood). The under-priced elements are the escalating self-insurance retention and the fuel spike, both of which could pressure margins even as freight recovers. We score risk factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

Options data is limited to a price-change reference (the stock moved from $13.22 on May 9 to $14.44 on June 19, +9.2%), with no current skew or IV surface available. Short interest is moderate at 5.2% of float and 4.3 days to cover — neither crowded nor a squeeze setup, which means informed positioning is not strongly tilted either way. The absence of elevated put demand is mildly consistent with the constructive cyclical read, but there is insufficient options data to confirm or challenge the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The freight recession is ending, capacity is exiting, and Heartland's operating leverage on a cycle recovery is substantial. From a 107% OR on $806M revenue, a return to a mid-90s OR alone swings the company to clear profitability, and the low-80s target implies normalized EPS well above the current $0.51 next-year consensus. Q1 2026 already delivered ~490bps of YoY OR improvement, March was strong, estimates are rising sharply (+110% on current-year EPS over 90 days), and the CEO bought near the bottom. The debt-light balance sheet, youngest fleet in the peer group, and premium-service reputation position Heartland to recapture high-rate freight first as demand returns. Bull case: $18.

**Bear case.** The recovery slips to late-2026 or 2027 as management hedges, diesel stays elevated near $5.00+, and the self-insurance retention at $5.0M/claim adds P&L volatility that offsets rate gains. A stock that has already run 65% off its low, trades above its mean analyst target and at ~1.5x book, derates as the priced-in recovery fails to arrive on schedule. Further CFI goodwill/intangible impairment ($322.6M goodwill at risk) or a single large-customer bid loss (one customer now >10% of revenue) deepens the disappointment. Bear case: $10 (roughly -31% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the cyclical thesis is sound but the entry price has largely caught up to it, leaving balanced near-term risk/reward.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July 2026), which will show whether the sequential OR improvement continued through the seasonally important spring/summer freight period and whether the elevated diesel environment damaged margins.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 operating ratio improves sequentially below ~100% (returning to operating profitability ex-gains), confirming the recovery is real and not weather/equipment-gain-driven.
- A pullback toward book value (~$10-11) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, which would restore the asymmetric upside the current price lacks.
- Diesel normalizing back toward ~$3.65-4.00 (e.g., a verified easing of the Middle East conflict), removing the acute fuel headwind.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating ratio fails to improve sequentially or deteriorates, signaling the capacity-exit thesis is stalling.
- A material customer loss (the >10% customer) or a further goodwill/intangible impairment against the $322.6M CFI goodwill balance.
- Net leverage rising back toward the covenant amid sustained cash burn, or a dividend reduction.

## Conclusion

Heartland Express is a well-capitalized, OCF-positive truckload carrier at the trough of a multi-year freight recession, with a genuine cyclical turn now visible in Q1 2026 results and in rising estimate revisions. The balance sheet removes solvency from the discussion, the CEO's trough purchase signals conviction, and the operating leverage on a recovery is real and potentially large. The constraint is price: at $14.44 the stock trades above its mean analyst target and at ~1.5x book, and sits near 28x first-year recovery earnings, leaving little room for the recovery-timing slippage that management itself flags, the elevated diesel environment, and a self-insurance retention that has nearly doubled in three years. The cyclical thesis is correct and partially priced. We rate HTLD a HOLD, with an upgrade path on confirmed sequential operating-ratio improvement at Q2 or a pullback toward book value.
