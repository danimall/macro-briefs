**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FactSet is a subscription financial-data and analytics platform serving 9,101 clients and 241,352 users, with revenue almost entirely recurring and tracked internally through Annual Subscription Value (ASV). It owns the exclusive global CUSIP/CINS identifier franchise (CGS), retains >95% of ASV and 91% of clients annually, and generates roughly 27% free-cash-flow margins. The business is structurally sound. The stock has fallen roughly 48% from its $453 high to $237.56, and the question that matters is whether that de-rating reflects a permanent impairment of the data-aggregation business model or a multiple compression that has largely run its course.

My prior assessment moved from HOLD to BUY as the multiple compressed. I am stepping back to HOLD. Three things temper the bull case without validating an outright AVOID: adjusted operating margin has fallen roughly 280 basis points in 18 months (37.8% → 35.0%) as AI/cloud build-out, software amortization, and compensation inflation outrun mid-single-digit revenue growth; the company carries a multi-year, still-unremediated material weakness in IT controls over revenue, accounts receivable, and deferred revenue (disclosure controls concluded "not effective" as of February 28, 2026); and the C-suite has turned over broadly, with a new CEO under a May 2025 contract and an apparent CFO handoff in 2026.

Against those concerns, the deterioration narrative does not survive close inspection. The Q2 FY2026 GAAP net-income decline of 8.1% was driven by a one-time ~$10.3M equity-investment impairment and non-recurring new-CEO make-whole compensation; on an adjusted basis EPS grew 4.2% to $4.46. Organic ASV growth re-accelerated to 6.7% from a ~5.7% trough, revenue grew 7.1%, the two most recent quarters beat consensus, and forward estimates have drifted up (+1.4% current-year, +2.2% next-year over 90 days). A 15x forward P/E against a ~32x peer median on a 95%-retention franchise is not the profile of a structurally dying business. The bull and bear cases roughly offset, which warrants a HOLD rather than a conviction call in either direction.

## Company Overview

FactSet sells an integrated financial-data and workflow platform on a recurring subscription basis. It reports three geographic segments: Americas (~65% of revenue), EMEA (~25%), and Asia Pacific (~10%), sold across four firm-type verticals: Institutional Buyside, Dealmakers, Wealth, and Market Infrastructure (renamed from "Partnerships and CGS" in FY2026). Roughly 82% of organic ASV is buy-side.

CUSIP Global Services is the core asset. FactSet is the exclusive issuer of CUSIP and CINS security identifiers globally, a quasi-monopoly franchise acquired from S&P Global that carries high margins and is embedded in the plumbing of US securities markets. That franchise is also the subject of antitrust litigation (discussed below), the principal idiosyncratic tail on an otherwise high-quality profit pool.

Competitively, FactSet sits behind Bloomberg (private) and the LSEG/Refinitiv–S&P Global complex, competing on breadth and depth of connected data, workflow integration, open architecture (APIs, cloud feeds), and client service. The moat indicators are strong on a trailing basis (>95% ASV retention, 91% client retention). The forward debate is whether those retention metrics survive a world in which large language models and agentic tools can assemble, normalize, and query financial data at marginal cost.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and ASV.** Revenue grew from $1.84B (FY2022) to $2.32B (FY2025), a +5.4% FY2025 print (+4.4% organic). Organic ASV growth decelerated to ~5.7% in FY2025 but re-accelerated to 6.7% in Q2 FY2026, with organic ASV reaching $2,449M and Q2 revenue up 7.1% YoY to $611M. APAC organic ASV is growing fastest (~10%), EMEA slowest (~4%). This re-acceleration is the single most important data point in the file and it cuts directly against a "decelerating into terminal decline" reading.

**The margin story.** Headline GAAP operating margin looks stable-to-up (FY2025 32.2% vs FY2024 31.8%), but that comparison is flattered by a prior-year sales-tax charge. The cleaner signal is adjusted operating margin, which has compressed: FY2024 37.8% → FY2025 36.3% → Q2 FY2026 35.0% (vs 37.3% in the prior-year quarter). GAAP operating margin in Q2 FY2026 fell to 30.3% from 32.5%. The drivers are rising compensation (merit increases, higher variable comp, one-time new-CEO make-whole cash and equity); sharply higher amortization of capitalized internal-use software (cost of services up 130–160 bps); higher cloud-hosting costs (+40–60 bps); and India Labor Codes Reform raising APAC benefit costs from November 2025.

**Reconciling the earnings picture.** The reported Q2 FY2026 GAAP net-income decline of 8.1% (EPS $3.59 vs $3.76) is the headline that anchors the bear case. It should not be read as fundamental deterioration. The decline includes the equity-investment impairment recorded in Other assets and non-recurring new-CEO make-whole compensation, both explicitly transitory. On an adjusted basis, EPS grew 4.2% to $4.46. The earnings-surprise record reinforces stabilization: after two misses in mid-2025 (−1.6% in June, −2.9% in September), the company beat in the two most recent quarters (+2.2% December, +0.9% March). Forward estimates have moved up: current-year consensus of $17.71 is +1.4% over 90 days, next-year $19.39 is +2.2%. A business with rising forward estimates and a return to beats is not in a fundamental downtrend; it is absorbing a cost-timing bulge tied to leadership change and AI/cloud investment.

The honest residual concern is that the margin compression is partly structural, not purely timing. Capitalized software amortization and cloud hosting are not one-time; they reflect a deliberate "invest through the disruption" posture. If revenue growth stays in the mid-single digits while these costs persist, adjusted margins may stabilize at a lower plateau rather than recover to 37.8%. That is a real headwind, but it is a margin-mix question, not an existential one.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $726M and FCF $617M (~27% margin). Capex rose 27% to $109M, driven by capitalized software. The company returned $460M in FY2025 and has raised its dividend for 26 consecutive years (now $1.10/quarter, ~2.0% yield). Buyback authorization expanded to $400M (June 2025) plus $600M (December 2025), with $697M remaining as of February 2026. Management repurchased aggressively into the decline (average prices falling from ~$287 in December to ~$207 in February). This supports per-share metrics and signals perceived undervaluation, but it also consumes FCF that could de-lever and masks stock-comp share-count drag.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is $1.375B ($500M 2.90% notes due 2027, $500M 3.45% notes due 2032, $375M term loan due 2028). D/E is ~0.97 against a 3.75x leverage covenant (in compliance). The Altman Z of 2.5 ("grey zone") is misleading for an asset-light recurring-revenue model; it reflects the intangible-heavy balance sheet (intangibles $1.88B + goodwill $1.29B ≈ 75% of assets), not distress. Interest coverage and FCF generation are robust. The $500M 2027 maturity is the nearest refinancing event and is comfortably manageable given FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis hinges on whether the organic ASV re-acceleration is durable. Three forces support it: front-office expansion from the LiquidityBook OMS/trade-execution acquisition ($243M), Dealmakers-vertical expansion from Irwin ($120M, investor-relations/capital-markets), and APAC growth running ~10%. Two forces work against it: the active-to-passive shift compressing buy-side seat counts (82% of ASV is buy-side), and financial-industry consolidation reducing the client universe.

The estimate-revision data is mildly constructive rather than a signal that would justify a BUY, with the most recent 30-day window flat. This is a stabilization signal, not an acceleration signal. Combined with mid-single-digit revenue growth, the growth profile is steady-but-modest, which earns a middling Growth Prospects score — the dimension I weight most heavily for sorting winners, and here it is neither strongly positive nor negative.

The upside optionality is AI as additive: if FactSet's curated, normalized, workflow-embedded data becomes the trusted layer atop which clients run AI tools, ASV growth and pricing power could surprise to the upside. The downside is AI as substitutive: if LLMs commoditize aggregation and normalization, retention and pricing erode. Neither outcome is provable today.

## Valuation Assessment

FDS trades at 15.3x forward EPS versus a peer-group median of ~31.7x (CBOE 25.3x, MSCI 34.0x, NDAQ 26.1x, MCO 31.7x, COIN 56.0x). That is roughly a 50% discount to peers for a business with comparable retention characteristics and a monopoly identifier franchise. The 2.0% dividend yield, backed by 26 years of increases and ~27% FCF margins, provides downside support.

On the surface this looks cheap, and the cycle-adjusted logic argues the discount overstates the impairment. But the calibration history here is unambiguous: a low multiple is not, by itself, a reason to buy — cheapness has been a value-trap signal more often than a catalyst, and the discount only converts to upside if there is a specific reason the market is wrong. The reason the market is applying a 50% discount is identifiable and rational: (1) the AI-disintermediation overhang, which FactSet's own 10-K flags with unusual emphasis; (2) adjusted-margin compression that breaks the historical "steady margin-expander" model; and (3) governance friction (material weakness, C-suite turnover). These are not phantom fears. The discount is partly earned.

The question for the valuation score is whether the discount is now excessive. At 15x forward on rising estimates with a re-accelerating ASV line, the bar for the bull case is modest: the market is pricing meaningful structural decay, and even flat-to-modest growth would support a re-rating toward the low-20s multiple. The consensus mean target of $252.25 (median $244, high $380, low $200) implies modest upside. I treat the peer median as aspirational rather than a base case, because the AI overhang is a real differentiator that the peer set (exchanges, index providers with passive-flow tailwinds) does not share to the same degree. A 6.0 valuation score reflects a discount that is real but partly justified, asymmetric enough to preclude an AVOID but not asymmetric enough to anchor a BUY without confirmation that AI is not eroding the franchise.

## Competitive Landscape

FactSet's competitive position is stable-to-eroding rather than improving, which is why the trajectory matters more than the level. The trailing moat is strong: deeply embedded workflows and the CGS/CUSIP monopoly. But the structural growth rate has stepped down from historically higher levels to mid-single digits, the active-to-passive shift is a persistent seat-count headwind, and the AI threat is asymmetric: it can only hurt the aggregation layer, not help it. The five-year TSR underperformance (111.53 vs peer-group 231.96) is the market's verdict that FactSet has been losing relative ground, with the gap widening sharply in FY2024–FY2025.

The bull rebuttal is that FactSet must spend now (cloud, AI, capitalized software) precisely to defend and extend the moat, and that curated proprietary data plus workflow embedding is the durable defense against AI commoditization. That is plausible but unproven; the spend is visible in compressed margins today, the revenue payoff is not yet visible. I score competitive position 5.5 — a strong franchise with a deteriorating relative trajectory and a genuine, unresolved structural threat.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully priced are concentrated and specific:

1. **AI disintermediation.** The 10-K explicitly cites competitors with "significant AI capabilities and funding," third parties using AI to "create technology that could reduce demand for our products," and the secular risk of "free or relatively inexpensive information… especially with the deployment of AI tools." This is the central debate and it is genuinely binary over a multi-year horizon.

2. **Unremediated material weakness.** First disclosed in FY2024 over IT general controls for revenue, AR, and deferred revenue, it remains unremediated as of Q2 FY2026 with disclosure controls "not effective." For a subscription company where revenue recognition is the core of the model, an 18+ month control failure is a material quality concern, even absent a restatement.

3. **CGS/CUSIP antitrust litigation** (Dinosaur Financial Group v. S&P Global) threatens a high-margin monopoly profit pool. An adverse pricing or exclusivity outcome would impair a structurally important earnings stream.

4. **C-suite turnover.** A new CEO (May 2025 contract) and an apparent CFO handoff in 2026, with a cluster of Item 5.02 filings across June 2025, December 2025, March 2026, and April 2026 — elevated execution risk at the moment the AI strategy must be proven.

5. **Concentration:** single dominant cloud provider, two data suppliers each >10% of content costs, ~80% of employees offshore (India/Philippines) exposing the company to wage and labor-law risk.

These justify a Risk Factors score of 4.5 — there are specific, non-boilerplate catalysts for material downside, and the material weakness plus AI overhang are the kind of risks the market may be under- or mis-pricing in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 65.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 41.7% (a rich 23.4-point spread, 70th percentile within the chain). The term structure is in backwardation (65.1% near-term vs 52.6% at three months), and put/call ratios are elevated (1.87 volume, 1.94 OI) with a 5.6% put skew at the front. This aligns with the 18.1% short interest (6.6 days to cover). The market is positioned bearishly and hedging a near-term move. For a HOLD, this is informative rather than decisive: it confirms that sentiment is already negative, which limits incremental downside surprise but also means any positive catalyst (durable ASV, margin stabilization, material-weakness remediation) could force covering. The IV has fallen modestly since mid-May (67.6% → 65.1%) even as the price rose 13.1%, suggesting some stress is resolving.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** FactSet is a 95%-retention recurring-revenue franchise with a monopoly identifier business, trading at 15x forward versus ~32x peers. Organic ASV re-accelerated to 6.7%, adjusted EPS is growing, the last two quarters beat, and forward estimates are rising. The GAAP earnings dip is one-time. The current margin compression is deliberate investment in cloud/AI that, if it makes FactSet's curated data the trusted layer beneath client AI tools, drives ASV re-acceleration and a multiple re-rating toward the low-20s. Aggressive buybacks at depressed prices and a 26-year dividend-growth record provide downside support. Re-rating to 20x on $19.39 next-year EPS implies ~$388.

**Bear case.** Generative AI commoditizes the data-aggregation-and-normalization layer FactSet monetizes; retention and pricing erode; the active-to-passive shift compounds the seat-count drain. Margins stay compressed because the AI/cloud spend is structural, not transitory, and revenue growth never accelerates enough to leverage it. The material weakness signals deeper process fragility; C-suite turnover impairs execution; the CGS antitrust case clips the highest-margin profit pool. The stock de-rates further toward 12x on flat earnings, implying ~$212.

The two cases are roughly balanced in probability and magnitude, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The path to resolution runs through two to three quarters of ASV and margin data plus tangible evidence on whether AI is additive or substitutive.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two earnings reports (Q3 FY2026 and Q4 FY2026), which will reveal whether the 6.7% ASV re-acceleration holds and whether adjusted margins stabilize off the 35.0% trough.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic ASV growth sustains ≥6.5% for two consecutive quarters AND adjusted operating margin stabilizes or expands off 35.0%, demonstrating the AI/cloud spend is reaching a turn.
- Tangible evidence that AI is additive — new AI-driven product ASV, pricing increases, or retention holding >95% with stated AI-assisted use cases — combined with remediation of the material weakness.
- Forward estimate revisions accelerate above +5% over 90 days while the stock trades below ~18x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic ASV growth decelerates back below ~5% AND retention slips below 93%, signaling AI-driven disintermediation is taking hold.
- Adjusted operating margin falls below ~33% with no stabilization path, confirming structural rather than transitory compression.
- An adverse CGS/CUSIP antitrust ruling, OR a restatement stemming from the unremediated material weakness.

## Conclusion

FactSet is a high-quality, cash-generative, high-retention data franchise at a steep discount to peers, facing a genuine but unresolved structural threat. The audit correctly flags that the earnings "decline" is GAAP-only and one-time-driven, that adjusted EPS is growing, that the company has returned to beats, that forward estimates are rising, and that organic ASV has re-accelerated — collectively undercutting any structural-impairment AVOID, particularly after the stock has already fallen roughly 48% from its high and rallied off its February low. Issuing an AVOID into 18.1% short interest with only modest remaining downside and a re-entry threshold barely below current price would be a poorly asymmetric call, and the franchise quality argues against it.

At the same time, the AI-disintermediation overhang, the ~280 bps adjusted-margin compression, the unremediated material weakness, and broad C-suite turnover preclude a BUY without confirmation that the franchise is defending its moat and that margins have found a floor. The positives and negatives roughly cancel. My recommendation is **HOLD**, classified as uncertain — I lack sufficient evidence to make a confident directional call in either direction, and the next two quarters of ASV and margin data, plus any read on whether AI is additive or substitutive, are the events that should resolve it.
