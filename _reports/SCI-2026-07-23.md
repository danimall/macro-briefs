**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Service Corporation International is North America's largest deathcare company, operating 1,487 funeral service locations and 503 cemeteries across 44 U.S. states, 8 Canadian provinces, and additional territories. It serves roughly 700,000 families annually under the Dignity Memorial brand and holds an estimated 18% share of a highly fragmented industry where the next-largest public competitors are a fraction of its size. The business combines an unusual degree of revenue visibility (a $17.0 billion preneed backlog and multi-billion-dollar trust portfolios) with recession resistance and high, stable cash generation. FY2025 delivered $4.31B revenue (+2.9%), $978M operating income, $543M net income, and roughly $943M operating cash flow at a ~22.7% operating margin and 33% ROE.

The investment question concerns the source and durability of near-term earnings growth rather than business quality, which is high. Net income has been essentially flat over four years ($519M–$565M range), while diluted EPS climbed from ~$3.53 (2022) to $3.80 (2025) almost entirely through a ~10% reduction in share count. Buybacks accelerated to $464M in 2025 at an average price (~$79.15) near where the stock trades today. The organic growth thesis rests on a longer-dated demographic bet — the Baby Boomer atneed wave — while the near-term picture shows funeral volumes falling 6% YoY in Q1 2026, cremation mix rising to 64.5%, and consensus EPS estimates drifting lower (current-year -3.0%, current-quarter -9.3% over 90 days).

We rate SCI a HOLD. The stock has de-rated from an April high near $88.39 to ~$77, and fundamentals are intact rather than deteriorating, which improves risk/reward at the margin. The drift lower in estimates, the absence of a near-term operating catalyst, and a valuation (~20x forward EPS) that sits at the sector median with no growth premium justification leave the shares balanced. The score trajectory has declined from 6.8 (March) to 6.0 (June), driven by cuts to Risk Factors, Competitive Position, and Growth Prospects; our current work confirms the growth and risk pressures while pushing back modestly on the competitive-position derating.

## Company Overview

SCI operates the largest deathcare network in North America across three lines: funeral services, cemeteries, and cremation. The 314 combination funeral-cemetery locations reflect a deliberate co-location strategy that lowers shared costs and cross-sells services. The company markets under a national brand umbrella (Dignity Memorial) plus specialized brands including National Cremation Society, Neptune Society, and Funeraria del Angel.

The competitive moat has several sources. Scale allows a "market cluster" cost structure, centralized procurement, and access to top-tier trust money managers at favorable fees that independent operators cannot replicate. The preneed model — 3,800 sales counselors selling contracts funded through trusts and insurance — locks in future revenue and share while remaining roughly cash-flow-neutral at the point of sale. The cemetery segment carries genuine barriers to entry (land, permitting, capital), and SCI owns roughly 90% of its real estate, including ~36,000 acres of cemetery land (~66% developed, ~2,000 developed acres unsold). The preneed backlog is the franchise's most distinctive asset.

Two structural forces define the industry's trajectory. Cremation continues to gain share and carries lower average revenue than burial, applying persistent mix pressure. Offsetting this over the long run is the Baby Boomer demographic wave, into which SCI is pre-selling today; preneed cemetery production rose 10% and preneed funeral production 6% in Q1 2026, building future atneed conversion that plays out over 10–20 years.

## Financial Analysis

SCI's headline balance-sheet ratios are misleading. The Altman Z-Score of 0.56, debt/equity of 11.4x, and current ratio of 0.55 are artifacts of trust-fund gross-up: ~$18.7B in total assets are dominated by preneed trust investments (~$7.4B) and perpetual-care trusts (~$2.4B), offset by matching deferred revenue and "deferred receipts held in trust" liabilities. Standard distress screens are non-informative here. The operating reality — 33% ROE, ~$943M operating cash flow, a Piotroski score of 4/5, and comfortable covenant headroom — reflects a durable cash-flow business.

FY2025 revenue rose 2.9% (funeral +3.5% to $2.41B, cemetery +2.2% to $1.90B). Operating margin held near 22.7% and FCF margin near 13%. Trust income was a meaningful 2025 tailwind: $202M preneed plus $111M perpetual-care income, with trusts returning 15.1%.

EPS growth of +7.6% was driven substantially by share-count reduction, not operating expansion. Diluted shares fell from ~160M (2022) to ~143M (2025), while net income itself has been range-bound. This is a capital-return story in the near term; the organic growth engine is the longer-dated preneed-to-atneed conversion.

On the debt side, SCI carries ~$5.1B principal and refinanced in November 2025 into a $750M term loan plus $1.75B revolver due 2030 at improved pricing. The leverage ratio of 3.67x sits well within the 5.0x covenant maximum and within management's 3.5x–4.0x target. Floating-rate exposure is a modest headwind: Q1 2026 interest expense rose to $64M as average floating balances grew ~$250M, partly offset by lower rates (6.8% to 5.8%). For a business with this cash-flow stability, the leverage is appropriate rather than aggressive, which supports a financial-health score above the midpoint but below the 7+ tier given the earnings-quality question and rate sensitivity.

The earnings surprise record is a caution. The last four reported quarters show one beat and three misses (Q1 2026 -4.4%, Q4 2025 -1.4%, Q3 2025 +3.8%), a deterioration from prior visibility. Combined with falling estimates, this argues against high confidence in current consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is soft. Q1 2026 funeral volumes fell 6% YoY against a tough comparison (a strong 2025 flu season), and the gross margin on the funeral segment dropped 300 bps (24.4% to 21.4%) as a $17.4M revenue decline hit a high-fixed-cost base. Rising cremation mix is structurally deflationary to average revenue per case, and longer life expectancy continues to suppress near-term atneed volumes.

The cemetery segment is the current growth engine: Q1 2026 revenue +7.1%, gross profit +10.9%, margin +120 bps. The business is increasingly cemetery-led, and preneed production growth builds the backlog that eventually converts to atneed revenue.

Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $4.05–$4.35 (implying ~7%–14% growth over $3.80) and an 8%–12% long-term EPS growth framework. The guidance leans heavily on continued buyback contribution rather than organic acceleration, and estimate revisions are moving the wrong way: current-year consensus fell 3.0% and next-year 2.7% over 90 days, with the current-quarter estimate cut 9.3%. This is the opposite of the >30% positive revision velocity that would flag an earnings acceleration; SCI's growth score reflects a mature franchise with a soft near-term operating backdrop and a longer-dated demographic tailwind whose timing is uncertain.

The demographic thesis is real but not near-term. Preneed contracts written today convert over a decade-plus horizon, so the Boomer wave supports the 8%–12% long-run framework without providing a catalyst inside a 12-month window. Growth Prospects earns a 5.0 — directionally positive over the long run, but with thin near-term separation and falling estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

SCI trades at ~20.4x trailing EPS ($3.80) and roughly 19x the current-year consensus of $4.13. Against the Specialized Consumer Services peer median P/E of 19.2, the stock is at parity, carrying no growth premium and no meaningful discount.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited direct comparability: H&R Block (6.4x, a mature tax franchise), Frontdoor (19.2x), ADT (9.3x), Mister Car Wash (21.5x), and Matthews International (83.1x, distorted by trough earnings). None share SCI's trust-backed backlog economics or death-care demand profile. This weakens any purely relative-multiple conclusion; SCI's own history and cash-flow characteristics matter more.

The valuation guardrails cut against a high score here. This is not a cyclical trough with tripling earnings: trailing and forward P/E are essentially the same, estimates are falling rather than rising, and the trailing period is not depressed. The stock's ~20x multiple reflects a stable, high-quality but slow-growing franchise fairly priced for what it delivers. Analyst targets (low $90, high $100, mean $96.33) sit well above the current ~$77, but on a mature, slow-growth name with falling estimates, those targets carry less weight than on a cheap acceleration story; they reflect a modeled fair value that the market has been unwilling to pay while funeral volumes soften.

On a cash-flow basis the case is more supportive. ~$554M FCF against an ~$11.0B market cap is roughly a 5% FCF yield, and the ~1.9% dividend plus aggressive buyback provides a real all-in shareholder return. There is genuine hidden asset value in the ~2,000 unsold developed cemetery acres, 90% owned real estate, and the planned monetization of the existing Houston HQ. None of this is a mispricing the market is demonstrably missing; it is embedded value that supports the current price rather than a catalyst for re-rating.

Our history flags cheapness as an anti-predictive signal: a low multiple absent a catalyst is a value-trap warning, not a buy. SCI is fairly valued rather than cheap. Valuation earns a 5.5: no stretched multiple to short, but no identified mispricing to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

SCI's competitive position is the strongest element of the story and the one where recent score cuts (from 8.5 to 7.0) look somewhat overdone. The company is many times larger than any competitor in a fragmented industry, and its scale advantages — cost clustering, procurement, trust economics, national preneed insurance agreements, and a 3,800-person sales force — are structurally difficult to replicate. The backlog and cemetery land bank represent durable barriers.

The trajectory is nuanced rather than deteriorating. Cremation mix is a genuine headwind to average revenue, but SCI is monetizing it through memorialization products, celebration-of-life services, and cemetery cremation property (niches, gardens). Cemetery preneed strength suggests the strategy is working, and the segment mix is shifting toward the higher-barrier, higher-margin cemetery business. Share is not eroding; the moat is compressing at the funeral margin line via cremation, not via competitive displacement. That is a mix headwind, not a competitive-position loss. We hold Competitive Position at 6.5 — a strong, stable moat with a structural (not competitive) revenue-mix pressure.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are real but mostly not binary in the near term:

- **Cremation mix shift** is structural and persistent, compressing average revenue and, at the funeral segment, margin. Already partly priced.
- **Death-rate/demographic timing** creates near-term volume volatility against a longer-dated Boomer tailwind whose timing is uncertain.
- **High fixed-cost operating leverage** amplifies volume shortfalls, as the Q1 2026 funeral margin drop demonstrated.
- **Trust-fund market exposure** (~60% equities) is a live watch item: Q1 2026 trust returns turned negative (-0.7%) after the 2025 tailwind. A severe market decline could reduce recognized trust income and, in certain states, trigger replenishment obligations, meaningfully pressuring recognized cemetery and perpetual-care revenue.
- **FTC Funeral Rule review** (ongoing since 2020) is an underappreciated regulatory tail. Mandatory online pricing transparency could compress the pricing power management explicitly cites as a lever. No dated resolution.
- **Surety/escheatment/legal exposures**: cancellation of all surety bonds would require ~$259M cash into trusts; unclaimed-property laws applied to the backlog and cemetery burial-practice litigation are ongoing exposures; an IRS audit of the 2022 return is pending. Individually manageable, collectively a diffuse tail.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects the combination of the trust-return reversal (an emerging, not-yet-priced watch item), the FTC review tail, and the demonstrated downside sensitivity of the fixed-cost model to volume, offset by the fact that most of these are gradual rather than binary and the balance sheet is genuinely sound once the trust gross-up is stripped out.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.8% sits rich to 23.5% one-year historical vol (a 9.3-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (32.8% near-term vs. 26.9% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the next earnings print. Put/call skew of 3.7% shows modestly elevated put demand, and the six-month tenor carries a -3.2% skew. Put/call volume and OI ratios are very low (0.03), so the raw positioning is thin and dominated by the skew rather than directional flow. The implied one-month move is ±7.1%. The options market is pricing a specific near-term event with downside tilt but is not signaling structural distress. Rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry and for selling premium rather than buying it if establishing a position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$95–$100):** SCI is a fortress cash-flow franchise with an unmatched scale moat, a growing backlog, and disciplined capital return. Cemetery preneed production and the Boomer demographic wave eventually lift atneed volumes; funeral volume softness proves comp-driven and transient. Continued buybacks at ~$79 (below the stock's intrinsic value) compound EPS, and the 8%–12% long-term framework holds. Trust returns normalize, and hidden real-estate/land value crystallizes. Analyst targets ($90–$100) are reached as guidance is met and estimates stabilize. This is the base scenario the sell side models.

**Bear Case (~$62–$65):** Funeral volume weakness persists as cremation mix accelerates and life expectancy suppresses atneed demand; the fixed-cost base drives further margin compression. Trust returns stay negative, cutting recognized income and, in a market drawdown, triggering replenishment. The FTC Funeral Rule review produces mandatory pricing transparency that compresses pricing power. EPS growth is revealed as buyback-dependent on flat net income, and the multiple compresses toward 16x on ~$4.00 EPS. Rising floating-rate interest expense and slowing buyback capacity remove the EPS crutch.

The base case sits around $82–$85 (roughly 20x on $4.13 current-year consensus, allowing modest normalization of trust income and stable buyback contribution), a mid-single-digit gain from the current ~$77 plus a ~1.9% dividend. The distribution is roughly symmetric, with the bull case dependent on volume normalization the data has not yet confirmed and the bear case dependent on a trust-return and regulatory tail that has not yet materialized.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which will test whether the Q1 funeral volume decline was comp-driven or the start of a trend, and whether trust returns recovered. The 2026 guidance range will be recalibrated then.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Funeral volumes stabilize (flat-to-positive YoY) with funeral gross margin recovering toward 24%, confirming the Q1 weakness was comp-driven; combined with a turn in estimate revisions (current-year consensus rising rather than falling).
- A pullback toward the low-$70s / 52-week low ($68.41), which would push the FCF yield above 6% and re-establish asymmetric upside toward the $90+ analyst target band.
- Trust returns recover meaningfully (back toward mid-single-digit-plus quarterly), removing the recognized-income headwind.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of funeral volume declines of 5%+ with continued margin compression, indicating structural demand erosion rather than a comp effect.
- Sustained negative trust returns forcing replenishment charges, or an adverse FTC Funeral Rule outcome mandating pricing transparency.
- A dividend or buyback pace cut, or leverage climbing toward the 4.0x upper band, removing the EPS-per-share support.

## Conclusion

SCI is a high-quality, wide-moat cash-flow franchise that is fairly, not cheaply, valued and that lacks a near-term operating catalyst. The de-rating from ~$88 to ~$77 has improved the entry point without changing the fundamentals materially: funeral volumes remain soft on cremation mix and demographic timing, estimates are drifting lower, and near-term EPS growth is buyback-engineered on flat organic net income. The competitive position and balance-sheet strength are genuine and arguably better than the recent score cuts imply, but they support the current price rather than argue for meaningful re-rating.

We rate SCI a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the durable franchise and shareholder-friendly capital return justify continuing to own the shares, but the balanced risk/reward, falling estimates, rich near-term implied volatility, and absence of open-market insider buying preclude a stronger call. Investors seeking a defensive, low-beta cash-flow holding can own it here; those seeking appreciation should wait for either a funeral-volume/estimate-revision turn or a pullback into the low-$70s that restores a clearer margin of safety.
