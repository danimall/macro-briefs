**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Healthcare Realty Trust is the largest pure-play owner and operator of medical outpatient buildings (MOBs) in the United States, with roughly $10.3B gross in 502 consolidated properties across 27 states. The investment case is a management-driven operational turnaround rather than a distressed-asset story. Following the underwhelming 2022 HTA merger, the company has installed a new CEO (Peter Scott) and CFO (Daniel Gabbay, effective January 2026), refreshed its board, changed auditors, right-sized the dividend, sold $1.1B of properties at a 6.7% cap rate, and repurchased stock at ~$17 while the shares now trade at $21.40. Same-store cash NOI accelerated to +7.1% in Q1 2026 (from +4.9% for FY2025), management raised 2026 normalized FFO guidance to $1.59–$1.65, and the reduced dividend is now covered on a FAD basis.

The complication is that the market has already begun to reward it. The stock is up ~31.8% over the trailing year and has re-rated to roughly 13x FFO. It trades at $21.40 against a consensus mean target of $21.33 and median of $21.00, with an $18.00 low and $24.00 high. The 4.5% dividend yield sits below the health-care REIT peer median of ~6.1%, so income buyers are not being paid a premium to wait. The recurring, durable component of the thesis — closing a ~90.5% occupancy gap toward best-in-class peers — is a multi-year process, and much of the easy re-rating from the sentiment trough has occurred.

We rate HR a HOLD (growth_hold). The trajectory is improving and the score has moved from 4.5 (April) to the mid-5s, tracking the operational reset. But three structural constraints keep the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric: ~$1.3B of 2026–2027 debt maturities being refinanced into a higher-for-longer rate environment, a short 4.4-year weighted-average lease term that amplifies re-leasing exposure, and a stock now sitting at consensus with a sub-peer yield. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$18s or on evidence that portfolio occupancy is inflecting higher.

## Company Overview

Healthcare Realty is a self-managed Nashville-based REIT created by the April 2022 merger of legacy Healthcare Realty Trust and Healthcare Trust of America. Its strategy centers on MOBs located on or adjacent to acute-care hospital campuses of leading health systems, and roughly 72% of expiring 2026 leases are on or adjacent to campuses. The portfolio is 481 medical office/outpatient buildings (~93% of gross investment), 14 inpatient, and 4 office assets, spanning ~29M square feet, plus a ~30%-weighted interest in 62 unconsolidated JV properties.

The tenant base is a structural strength: 30+ physician specialties with no single tenant exceeding 10% of revenue. This diversification insulates HR from the operator-credit concentration that has damaged senior-housing and skilled-nursing REITs. Lease structure is ~58% net, ~30% modified-gross, ~8% gross, and ~4% absolute net, with 96% of leases carrying fixed escalators averaging ~2.9%. The weighted-average remaining lease term is short at 4.4 years, which provides frequent mark-to-market repricing opportunities but also creates recurring re-leasing exposure. HR self-manages leasing and property management for ~92% of the portfolio, giving it a scale operating platform. The offsetting weakness is portfolio occupancy of only ~90.5%, materially below best-in-class peers, which is the value-creation lever the new management team is pursuing.

## Financial Analysis

REIT GAAP net income is dominated by non-cash depreciation and impairments and is not the right lens here. HR reported a GAAP net loss of −$246M in 2025, −$654M in 2024 (which included a $250.5M goodwill write-off), and −$278M in 2023, driven by ~$586M of real-estate D&A and $361M of 2025 impairments largely tied to the disposition program. The trailing EPS of −$0.71 and the negative headline P/E reflect this accounting reality, not cash distress. The Altman Z-Score of 1.31 nominally reads "distress" but is a well-known false positive when applied to depreciation-heavy REITs; actual incurrence leverage sits at 35.8% (versus a 60% max covenant) and debt-service coverage at 3.5x (versus a 1.5x minimum), both comfortably compliant.

On cash metrics, the story improves. Normalized FFO/share was $1.61 in 2025, up from $1.56 in 2024 and $1.57 in 2023, and Q1 2026 came in at $0.41/share versus $0.39 a year prior. Same-store cash NOI is the clearest operating signal: +4.9% for FY2025 ($601.9M versus $573.1M ex-JV) and +7.1% in Q1 2026 ($153.6M versus $143.5M), with 2026 guidance raised to 3.75%–4.75%. FAD was $448M in 2025 versus $419M in 2024; Q1 2026 FAD of $112.9M against ~$84M of dividends paid represents a ~74% FAD payout.

Operating cash flow was $457M in 2025. Capital allocation has been coherent: 70 properties sold for $1.1B at a 6.7% cap rate, long-term debt cut from $5.35B (2022) to $3.9B, and share count reduced from 365.6M to ~346.5M. Total debt is ~$4.1B at a weighted effective rate of ~4.5% and weighted maturity of ~3.2 years, with $1.2B available on the revolver. In February 2026 the company launched an inaugural $600M commercial paper program ($251M drawn at ~4.20%, cheaper than the revolver) and reset $400M of swaps into a $400M swap at 3.32% maturing January 2029. The watch item is ~$1.3B of debt maturing in 2026–2027; this is manageable given a ~$10.4B gross unencumbered asset base and the new commercial paper and exchangeable-notes financing avenues, but it is being refinanced into a higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the marginal cost of capital.

The earnings surprise record is noisy — 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, with large swings driven by impairment and disposition timing — but the two most recent quarters (Q4 2025 and Q1 2026) both beat, consistent with the operational stabilization.

## Growth Analysis

HR's growth is a re-leasing and occupancy story layered on top of a durable secular tailwind. The shift of medical procedures to lower-cost outpatient settings and aging demographics support long-run demand for on- and near-campus MOBs. Management's own guidance is the near-term anchor, and the +7.1% Q1 same-store print running above the full-year range suggests conservatism in the guide.

Estimate revisions are broadly rising, which we weight as a forward indicator. Current-year consensus improved +50.8% over 30 days and +75.2% over 90 days (from −$0.12 to −$0.03), and next-year moved to +$0.02 from −$0.10 ninety days ago (+120%). One caveat before over-weighting this: these are revisions on near-zero GAAP EPS numbers, where percentage moves are mechanically enormous and the underlying figures are dominated by non-cash items. The estimate-revision "strong buy" signal that applies to inflection stocks with revenue growth above 40% and expanding margins is not cleanly present here; HR is a low-single-digit organic grower (same-store NOI ~4–7%), not a revenue-accelerating cyclical inflection. The revisions confirm sentiment stabilization and modest FFO momentum, but they do not signal the kind of systematic underestimation that precedes a large re-rating.

The single most important operating KPI is occupancy. At ~90.5% with ~15% of leases rolling annually and quarterly retention of only 77–80%, the entire upside case rests on closing the gap toward peers. If retention improves and occupancy climbs 100–200bps over the next 18–24 months, the operating leverage on a substantially fixed cost base flows disproportionately to NOI and FFO. The short 4.4-year WALT means repricing can compound quickly in either direction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.40 the stock trades at ~13x normalized FFO with a 4.5% dividend yield. On the peer table, HR's negative trailing P/E is not comparable (a REIT accounting artifact), but the yield comparison is informative: the health-care REIT peer median yield is ~6.1% (CTRE 4.1%, SBRA 6.4%, LTC 6.1%, MPT 7.2%, AHR 2.1%). HR's 4.5% sits well below median, meaning income investors are not being compensated with a premium yield to hold through the turnaround. That is a legitimate constraint on the value case.

The bull argument on cheapness rests on FFO multiple and self-help capital allocation. Management is selling assets at ~6.7% cap rates (an implied ~15x) and repurchasing its own stock at ~$17 (a higher implied yield), which is accretive value creation while the cost of equity remains elevated. If one credits a mid-cycle occupancy recovery, intrinsic value sits above the current price. But the stock has already re-rated ~32% off its trough, and it now trades essentially at the consensus mean and above the median. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle applies to cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks; here the stock is neither at a premium multiple nor deeply discounted. It is a moderate-growth REIT sitting at fair value, where consensus targets are more likely to function as a reasonable range than a stale floor.

We treat the FFO multiple with appropriate skepticism. A ~13x FFO looks inexpensive against larger diversified peers, but HR's persistent underperformance, higher implied cost of equity, and sub-peer occupancy justify part of that discount. The "cheap" signal in isolation has repeatedly proven to be a value-trap warning rather than a buy trigger, and it requires an independent catalyst — occupancy inflection, a decisive refinancing at attractive terms, or evidence the dividend is fully covered by operating cash flow without revolver support — before it becomes actionable. That catalyst is developing but not yet confirmed. We score valuation at 5.5: fairly priced, with balanced rather than asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

HR is the largest pure-play MOB owner-operator, and its on-/near-campus concentration is structurally advantaged under CMS site-neutral payment policy, which pressures off-campus hospital-owned outpatient departments more than on-campus facilities. The tenant diversification (no tenant >10%) and in-house operating platform are durable moats relative to operator-concentrated senior-housing/SNF peers.

The competitive weakness is honest and disclosed: HR concedes in its 10-K that some competitors have a lower cost of capital. Its elevated implied cost of equity is why HR has been a net seller of assets and a repurchaser of its own stock rather than an acquirer; it cannot compete for external growth against Welltower, Ventas, and better-capitalized MOB buyers. That is a real constraint on the growth ceiling. The competitive trajectory is improving at the operating level (accelerating same-store NOI, better retention discipline under new management, the Prospect/Hartford resolution) but the balance-sheet-driven inability to grow externally caps the moat-widening case. We score competitive position at 5.7: a strong asset base with an improving operational trajectory, offset by a cost-of-capital disadvantage in the acquisition market.

## Risk Assessment

**Occupancy and re-leasing.** With a 4.4-year WALT and 77–80% quarterly retention, the bull case hinges on execution. A stall or reversal in occupancy would directly undercut the NOI-growth thesis.

**Refinancing.** The 2026–2027 maturities have only $500M notionally swapped. The commercial paper program and exchangeable-notes offering (announced May 4, 2026) provide cheaper avenues, but refinancing into a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of capital and pressures asset values and transaction liquidity.

**Dividend sustainability.** The dividend was cut ~23% to $0.24/quarter from $0.31. FAD covers the reduced payout, but Q1 2026 dividends were partly funded by the revolver due to interest-payment timing. Management expects full-year operating cash flow to cover the dividend; confirmation of that without recurring revolver support is a key monitoring item.

**Purchase-option and reinvestment risk.** ~$1.14B of properties are subject to tenant purchase options (many appraisal-based); exercises would force reinvestment at potentially lower yields.

**Regulatory.** CMS site-neutral policy and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act are under analysis; on-campus concentration is relatively advantaged, but the regulatory environment for REIT ownership of healthcare assets is tightening.

**Prospect Medical.** The Chapter 11 filing (January 2025) is largely resolved — Hartford Health assumed 65,477 of ~80,912 sq ft via direct leases effective January 1, 2026 — but some space remains to be re-let.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 21.8% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 21.2%, so the options market is pricing normal uncertainty and offering neither a rich premium nor an unusually cheap entry. The term structure is in contango (near-term 21.8% below long-term 25.0%), a complacent/normal structure. Put/call skew of 6.6% shows modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.33 is call-tilted — a mixed, slightly two-sided read. The nearest-expiry implied move of ±10.4% and IV percentile of 18% within the chain suggest no imminent binary catalyst is being priced. Short interest at 9.8% of float (4.8 days to cover) is meaningful but not extreme, reflecting the genuine debate between turnaround bulls and skeptics rather than a one-sided bear thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($24–$26).** New management executes the occupancy recovery: portfolio occupancy climbs toward 92–93%, same-store cash NOI sustains the upper end of guidance (4.5%+), normalized FFO grows toward $1.70+ in 2027, and the market re-rates HR toward 15x FFO as the turnaround de-risks. Accretive buybacks at ~$17–$19 compound per-share FFO, the refinancing wall is cleared at reasonable rates via the commercial paper and exchangeable-notes programs, and full operating-cash-flow dividend coverage is confirmed. Value: ~$24–$26.

**Base case ($21–$22).** The operational reset continues at a measured pace: same-store NOI in the guided range, occupancy improving gradually, FFO around $1.62 for 2026. The stock holds near the current level as the improving fundamentals are offset by a sub-peer yield, the refinancing overhang, and a valuation already at consensus. Value: ~$21–$22, plus the 4.5% dividend.

**Bear case ($17–$18).** Occupancy stalls at ~90%, retention slips, and re-leasing spreads compress. Refinancing the 2026–2027 maturities comes at materially higher rates, pressuring FFO and the dividend, and forcing continued reliance on the revolver. Sentiment reverts toward the low-$18 analyst floor, roughly 16% below current. Value: ~$17–$18.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the occupancy trajectory, same-store NOI trend, dividend-coverage detail, and refinancing progress the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Portfolio occupancy inflects above ~91.5% with quarterly retention sustained above 80%, confirming the value-creation lever is working.
- Full-year operating cash flow demonstrably covers the dividend without revolver support, removing the sentiment overhang.
- A pullback toward the high-$18s that restores asymmetric upside to the base/bull case while the fundamental trajectory remains intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store cash NOI decelerates below ~3% or occupancy declines sequentially, signaling the turnaround has stalled.
- 2026–2027 maturities are refinanced at rates materially above the current ~4.5% weighted cost, compressing FFO and threatening dividend coverage.
- A second dividend cut or evidence of recurring revolver-funded distributions.

## Conclusion

Healthcare Realty is a credible, improving turnaround backed by a high-quality, well-diversified MOB portfolio, a reset management team, accelerating same-store NOI, raised guidance, and disciplined capital allocation (asset sales funding buybacks below intrinsic value). The score trajectory from 4.5 in April to the mid-5s reflects real fundamental progress, and new data confirms rather than reverses that trend. Management quality is the standout dimension, supported by an open-market director purchase at $18.09, new-team equity grants at ~$17, and a coherent capital-return program, though the 5-year TSR of 77.6 versus peers' 181.4 and elevated realized compensation warrant continued monitoring.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because the market has already priced much of the good news. At ~13x FFO, sitting at consensus mean/median targets, with a 4.5% yield below the ~6.1% peer median, and with a ~$1.3B refinancing wall to clear in a higher-for-longer rate environment, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The occupancy-recovery upside is real but multi-year, and the durable payoff is not yet cheap enough to demand ownership here. We would move to BUY on confirmed occupancy inflection, clean operating-cash-flow dividend coverage, or a pullback toward the high-$18s. This is a growth-oriented hold: worth owning for a patient investor tracking the turnaround, but not a compelling entry at $21.40.
