**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dexcom is one of two dominant players in the global continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) market, operating a razor/razorblade model that combines durable hardware with recurring disposable sensor sales at 60%+ gross margins. After a 2024 growth scare that triggered securities litigation, revenue reaccelerated to +16% in 2025 ($4.66B) and +15% in Q1 2026 ($1.19B), and gross margin rose sharply in Q1 2026 to 62.9% from 56.9% a year earlier. The margin recovery is the cleanest bullish data point in the file: management raised its operating- and EBITDA-margin outlook while reaffirming roughly $5.16B+ FY2026 revenue guidance, indicating the manufacturing and quality problems that pressured 2024–2025 margins are being converted into efficiency gains.

The case against full conviction rests on a tightly linked cluster of idiosyncratic risks that the duopoly-and-margins story does not neutralize. A March 2025 FDA warning letter citing manufacturing and quality-management-system non-conformities remains unresolved. It does not currently restrict production or 510(k) clearances, but additional enforcement is possible, and the same operational issue spawned a wave of six G6/G7 consumer class actions alleging "adulterated or misbranded" devices plus two securities class actions, one specifically targeting statements about G7 accuracy and manufacturing. Separately, CMS extended DMEPOS competitive bidding to CGMs, with reimbursement cuts taking effect in 2028 under a single payment set at the 75th percentile of winning bids on a monthly-rental model. These are specific, dated, falsifiable threats.

At $74.49 the stock trades at roughly 29x the current-year consensus EPS of $2.59 and ~24x next-year $3.08, with estimates broadly rising (+3.8% CY and +2.9% NY over 90 days). That is a fair-to-modestly-attractive multiple for mid-teens growth and a margin recovery, but not the asymmetric mispricing that would justify a BUY. The combination of an unresolved binary regulatory overhang, the absence of any insider open-market buying, a paused buyback into a depressed stock, and unusually heavy management turnover keeps this a HOLD with a positive growth bias. The decision point is the FDA warning letter resolution and continued margin delivery.

## Company Overview

Dexcom designs, manufactures, and commercializes CGM systems for diabetes management and, increasingly, broader metabolic health. The core franchise is the G6/G7 line, including the G7 15-Day sensor launched in late 2025, serving insulin-using diabetes patients. Dexcom One/ONE+ is the value-tier international product. Stelo, launched August 2024 as the first OTC glucose biosensor in the U.S., targets the large non-insulin Type 2 and prediabetes population as a TAM-expansion bet. At its May 14, 2026 Investor Day, management unveiled the G8 as a "completely new product platform" and laid out a 2030 vision built on double-digit revenue growth, broader insurance coverage, international expansion, and margin improvement.

Revenue is heavily distributor-driven (~85% via distributors, with certain individual agreements each exceeding 10% of total revenue), split roughly 72% U.S. / 28% international. Manufacturing spans Mesa AZ, San Diego CA, Penang Malaysia (new, with a tax holiday begun in 2025), and Athenry Ireland (under construction). The company reports as a single segment. Its competitive moat rests on brand strength in insulin-intensive diabetes, AID/insulin-pump integration partnerships, a large accumulated CGM data set, duopoly scale, and the recurring-revenue economics of disposable sensors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** $2.91B (2022) → $3.62B (2023, +24%) → $4.03B (2024, +11%) → $4.66B (2025, +16%). The 2024 deceleration was the trigger for the securities litigation; the reacceleration to 16% in 2025 on ~600–700K net new customers (ex-Stelo) is material evidence that the growth scare was a stumble rather than a structural ceiling.

**Profitability.** FY2025 gross profit was $2.80B at 60.1% margin (down slightly from 60.5% in 2024 on supply-availability inefficiencies, lower yields, and replacement costs). Operating income reached $911.8M (+52%, 19.6% margin) and net income $836.3M (+45%, 17.9% margin), helped by SG&A staying flat as ~$87M of legal expense fell off after the December 2024 Abbott patent settlement.

**Q1 2026 margin recovery.** Gross margin came in at 62.9% versus 56.9% in Q1'25, a 600-basis-point jump on manufacturing efficiencies, better fixed-cost absorption, and additional quality testing and material validation tied directly to remediating the FDA issues. Operating income rose 91% to $255.3M, net income 89% to $199.5M, and operating cash flow was $525.6M. The same quality-remediation spend that pressured 2024–2025 margins is now being absorbed into a more efficient production base, so the margin gain reflects both fixed-cost leverage and the tail end of remediation rather than one-off cost cuts.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.44B (+46%) and FCF $1.08B (23% FCF margin), a ~3.8% FCF yield on the $28.7B market cap. The company ended Q1 2026 with $2.42B in cash and short-term securities. It repaid the $1.21B 2025 convertible notes entirely in cash in November 2025. Remaining debt is $1.25B of 0.375% convertible notes due 2028 with a $162.41 conversion price, deeply out of the money at ~$74, so near-term dilution and cash-settlement risk are low (the if-converted dilutive share count already fell from 15.7M in Q1'25 to 7.7M in Q1'26). D/E is 1.31, current ratio ~1.9, debt-to-assets ~0.19, ROE 30.5%, ROA 13.2%. The Altman Z of 2.63 sits in the grey zone, but that reflects capital intensity and the convert, not distress; the Piotroski profile is strong.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters: +6.8% (Q2'25), +5.3% (Q3'25), −32.1% (Q4'25, the December guidance/seasonality stumble), and +17.0% (Q1'26). The Q4 miss is a reminder that visibility is not perfect, but the Q1 rebound and the margin raise re-establish confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs. First, the core insulin-using franchise continues to compound at mid-teens with the G7 15-Day extending wear duration and the G8 platform positioned for the next product cycle. Second, the OTC/non-insulin opportunity through Stelo addresses a far larger Type 2 and prediabetes population, though this is exactly where Abbott's Libre competes head-to-head, so penetration economics are uncertain. Third, international expansion and AID integration provide stickiness and incremental coverage.

Estimate revisions support the durability of the near-term trajectory: current-year EPS consensus moved to $2.59 from $2.50 ninety days ago and next-year to $3.08 from $2.99, with revenue guidance reaffirmed implying low-double-digit growth. These are positive, broad-based revisions across 23 analysts, but they do not reach the +30% velocity that would flag an inflection BUY signal under any reasonable reading. This is a steady high-quality grower with rising estimates, not a trough-earnings recovery story. The margin guidance raise is the genuine upside surprise; revenue is being guided conservatively against a base case that already grows double digits.

GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are an ambiguous force. Management frames them as a companion tailwind (more diagnosed metabolic patients, more monitoring), but acknowledges potential substitution for CGM in some Type 2 patients. This is a multi-year wildcard rather than a near-term swing factor.

## Valuation Assessment

On the data provider's stated trailing/forward P/E of ~32x (against $2.09 trailing EPS), the optical multiple looks higher than the consensus-based figures, but the forward consensus is the relevant anchor given the earnings are growing, not troughing. Against the Health Care Equipment peer median P/E of 22.6 (RMD 18.9, GEHC 15.5, STE 26.8, ZBH 22.6, HOLX 31.3), Dexcom commands a premium. That premium is defensible: Dexcom grows revenue at 15–16% versus low-to-mid-single digits for most of the peer set, generates 30% ROE and 23% FCF margins, and is mid-recovery on gross margin. A grower of this quality at ~24x next-year EPS with rising estimates is reasonably valued, arguably slightly cheap relative to its growth-adjusted peers.

What prevents a high valuation score is the absence of a specific, identifiable mispricing with asymmetric payoff. The PEG sits around 1.5–1.6 on next-year EPS, which is fair rather than compelling, and the FCF yield of ~3.8% is unremarkable. The stock has de-rated meaningfully from its 2025 high of $89.98, but prior appreciation is not a valuation input, and the de-rating coincides with a genuine new risk (the FDA warning letter and litigation cluster) that did not exist at the prior highs, so the lower multiple is partly a rational repricing of binary regulatory risk, not pure sentiment. Analyst targets span $65 low to $112 high with an $83.83 mean and $83.00 median, implying roughly 11% upside to the median. With estimates rising, those targets are likely to drift higher if the FDA letter resolves cleanly, but they are not a floor while a binary regulatory overhang is unresolved. The valuation is fair, with upside contingent on the regulatory and margin path rather than embedded in the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

The CGM market is an intensely competitive duopoly between Dexcom and Abbott's Libre, with Medtronic (Simplera), Roche, and emerging Chinese entrants on the periphery. Dexcom's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving: the December 2024 Abbott patent settlement (royalty-free cross-license, covenant not to sue through December 2034) removed a major overhang and ~$87M of annual legal cost, the G7 15-Day matches the field on wear duration, and the G8 platform is positioned to extend the accuracy/duration/connectivity contest where it competes.

The trajectory is not unambiguously positive. The Q1'26 securities suit alleging misstatements about G7 accuracy and the consumer "adulterated or misbranded" class actions create reputational risk on the one axis (sensor accuracy) where the moat is most contestable. Stelo's OTC expansion runs directly into Abbott's OTC offering, so the largest growth pool is also the most contested. The FDA's January 2026 broadening of "general wellness devices" could invite new wearable competitors over time. Distributor concentration (multiple agreements each over 10% of revenue) is a structural vulnerability. The competitive position is sound but carries enough cross-currents to warrant a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**FDA warning letter (the central idiosyncratic risk).** Following inspections of San Diego (Oct–Nov 2024) and Mesa (June 2024), Dexcom received a March 2025 warning letter citing manufacturing-process and QMS non-conformities. It does not yet restrict production, marketing, or clearances, but it remains unresolved, and escalation to a consent decree or import restriction would be materially damaging given offshore Malaysia/Ireland manufacturing. This is a binary overhang the market cannot fully price because the outcome distribution is wide. It is the key monitorable.

**Litigation cluster.** Six G6/G7 consumer class actions (Sep 2025–Jan 2026), two securities class actions (one over the 2024 guidance miss that partially survived a motion to dismiss, one S.D.N.Y. case from Oct 2025 alleging G7 accuracy/manufacturing misstatements), plus tracking derivative suits. These link a real operational problem to legal and reputational tail risk.

**2028 Medicare competitive bidding.** Contracting begins 2027, reductions take effect 2028, explicitly expected to lower Medicare CGM reimbursement. A country-of-origin question adds risk given offshore manufacturing. This caps the long-run pricing assumption.

**Reimbursement and policy.** Ongoing rebate eligibility and channel-mix headwinds (already a 2025 drag); Medicaid/ACA coverage cuts under the 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" could reduce covered lives.

**Management turnover.** A dozen-plus Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks since early 2025, an auditor change in March 2025, the founder-CEO transition to Jake Leach, and the Executive Chairman (Kevin Sayer) departure (separation Feb 2026). For a company simultaneously defending securities suits alleging management misstatements, this volume of change deserves scrutiny: it may be orderly succession, but it is not the stability profile of a clean compounder.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 48.2% versus 41.3% one-year historical volatility (rich by ~6.9pp), with the term structure in backwardation (48.2% near-term vs 41.5% three-month), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress around a near-dated event. The put/call volume ratio is low at 0.17 and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 1.89 to 0.70 over the past month as the stock rallied +21.4%, indicating positioning has shifted decisively less bearish. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.6%. The three-month skew of −6.1% shows some longer-dated downside hedging, consistent with the regulatory tail. The options market is not flagging an acute bearish bet, but the rich near-term IV and backwardation warn that the next catalyst (which could be FDA or quality-related news) is expected to move the stock, reinforcing the case to wait rather than chase after a sharp run.

Short interest is modest at 5.2% of float (2.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short and squeeze dynamics are limited.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Q1'26 gross-margin recovery is real and structural, not a one-off; management is leaning into it by raising margin guidance while reaffirming sales. Revenue has reaccelerated to 15–16%, the Abbott overhang is resolved through 2034, the G8 platform and Stelo extend the TAM, FCF is robust at ~$1.08B with $2.42B of cash and short-term securities, and the balance sheet is benign. If the FDA warning letter resolves without escalation and the litigation cluster settles modestly, the multiple re-rates toward the high-quality-medtech range and estimates drift higher. Bull case ~$100, roughly 24x a $4.10 normalized FY27 EPS as margins fully normalize and reimbursement risk proves manageable.

**Bear case.** The FDA warning letter escalates to a consent decree or import restriction, disrupting offshore-manufactured supply; the G7-accuracy securities suit and consumer class actions produce a material settlement and reputational damage; 2028 Medicare reimbursement cuts and Medicaid coverage reductions compress the pricing assumption; and Stelo penetration disappoints against Abbott. Bear case ~$55 (near the 52-week low and ~26% below current), roughly 18x a flat-to-pressured EPS as the multiple compresses to the bottom of the peer range on regulatory and pricing overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive growth bias (growth_hold). The thesis is that the margin recovery and mid-teens growth support continued appreciation, but the unresolved FDA warning letter and litigation cluster prevent the asymmetric setup required for a BUY at ~24x next-year EPS.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next quarterly earnings (early August 2026) for confirmation that the 62.9% gross margin holds and revenue tracks the guide, plus any 8-K disclosure on the FDA warning letter status.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Formal resolution or closure of the March 2025 FDA warning letter without production/clearance restrictions, removing the binary overhang.
- A second consecutive quarter confirming gross margin sustained above ~62% alongside revenue growth held at 14%+ YoY.
- Clustered open-market insider buying or a resumed/expanded buyback at current prices, signaling management conviction (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FDA escalation to a consent decree, import alert, or production restriction.
- Gross margin reversing below ~57% (the Q1'25 level) or revenue growth decelerating below ~8% YoY.
- A material adverse ruling or large settlement in the G7-accuracy securities action, or evidence the quality remediation is incomplete (new inspection findings).

## Conclusion

Dexcom is a high-quality CGM duopolist with reaccelerating mid-teens revenue, a genuine and arguably structural Q1'26 gross-margin recovery, strong free cash flow, and a healthy balance sheet. At ~24x next-year EPS on rising estimates, the valuation is fair for the growth and quality, above a peer median that grows far slower. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: an unresolved March 2025 FDA warning letter creates a binary regulatory overhang the market cannot fully price, it is directly linked to a cluster of G7-accuracy and "adulterated/misbranded" litigation, and 2028 Medicare competitive-bidding cuts are a dated structural headwind. Those risks are reinforced by the absence of any insider open-market buying, a buyback paused into a depressed stock with $250M of authorization expiring June 30, 2026, and unusually heavy management turnover. The risk/reward is balanced — roughly 11–34% upside to consensus median and bull cases against ~26% downside to the bear case — which is a HOLD profile. A clean FDA resolution plus a second quarter of confirmed margin strength would convert this to a BUY; escalation would convert it to an AVOID. The next earnings report and any FDA-related disclosure are the decision points.
