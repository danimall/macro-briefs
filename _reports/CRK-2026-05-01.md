## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is the largest operator in the Haynesville shale, producing approximately 1.2 Bcf/day of natural gas from North Louisiana and East Texas. The company is controlled by Jerry Jones (71% ownership) and is in the middle of a capital-intensive expansion into the Western Haynesville, a frontier play featuring some of the deepest horizontal shale wells drilled globally. CRK has recently announced a partnership with NextEra Energy for a 5.2 GW natural gas-fired power generation hub to serve data centers, which could provide a captive ~1 Bcf/day demand source by 2031.

Despite these growth ambitions, the financial profile is strained. The company generated negative free cash flow of approximately $450M in FY2025, carries $2.8B in debt against $24M in cash, and has an Altman Z-Score of 1.48 (distress zone). FY2025 net income of $396M was flattered by a $292M gain on asset sales; underlying earnings were roughly $104M. Consensus estimates are declining, with current-year EPS revised down 10.7% over the past 30 days, and the stock carries 25.3% short interest with 9.2 days to cover. The analyst community is overwhelmingly cautious: 10 Holds, 3 Sells, and only 1 Buy among covering analysts.

The combination of extreme commodity price sensitivity, structural negative FCF, heavy leverage, declining near-term estimates, and limited minority shareholder protections makes CRK unsuitable for most portfolios at current prices. The NextEra partnership is aspirational, pre-definitive agreement, and years from contributing meaningful cash flow.

## Company Overview

Comstock Resources operates approximately 1,074 of its 1,724 producing wells across 1.07 million gross acres in the Haynesville and Bossier shale formations. The company is essentially a pure-play natural gas producer — oil and condensate are negligible contributors. CRK's strategic position near the Gulf Coast provides favorable basis differentials for LNG export and petrochemical feedstock markets.

The Western Haynesville represents CRK's primary growth vector. The company has 520,000 net acres and estimates 2,561 net drilling locations, with 30 successful wells turned to sales through end of 2025. These are technically challenging wells drilled to depths approaching 19,200 feet under extreme pressure and temperature conditions. The company also operates Pinnacle Gas Services, a midstream subsidiary with 246 miles of pipeline infrastructure serving the Western Haynesville.

CRK is a controlled company. Jerry Jones holds approximately 71% of outstanding shares and has invested over $1 billion into the Western Haynesville program. The board consists of just three outside directors, and the company's governance structure provides limited protections for minority shareholders.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

CRK's financial trajectory is almost entirely a function of natural gas pricing. Revenue collapsed from $3.63B in FY2022 (when gas averaged ~$5.79/Mcf) to $1.25B in FY2024 ($1.98/Mcf), then recovered to $2.22B in FY2025 ($3.17/Mcf). Production declined 15% YoY to 450 Bcf in FY2025 due to reduced drilling activity in early 2024.

Operating income was $646M in FY2025 versus a $169M operating loss in FY2024. However, the $292M gain on Shelby Trough and Cotton Valley property sales materially flattered reported results. Adjusting for this, underlying operating income was approximately $354M, and adjusted net income was roughly $104M on $2.2B of revenue — a 4.7% adjusted net margin.

Interest expense consumed $223M in FY2025, representing approximately 25% of operating cash flow. The gas services segment (Pinnacle plus marketing) is essentially a breakeven-to-loss-making pass-through operation, with $500M in revenue against $516M in costs.

### Cash Flow and Capital Structure

Operating cash flow of $900M was insufficient to cover $1.35B in capital expenditures, producing negative free cash flow of approximately $450M. The company funded this deficit through its revolving credit facility ($260M drawn against a $1.5B commitment) and $432M in asset sale proceeds.

The balance sheet carries $2.8B in total debt principal against just $24M in cash. The current ratio is 0.49 — weak even by E&P standards. Total liquidity of $1.3B (revolver availability plus cash) provides adequate near-term runway, but the company is structurally cash-flow-negative at current gas prices and spending levels.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.48 places CRK in the distress zone. While E&P companies often operate with elevated leverage due to their asset-backed capital structures, this reading reflects genuine financial stress: heavy debt, minimal cash, negative working capital, and commodity-dependent earnings create vulnerability to a sustained gas price downturn.

### Forward Guidance and Spending Plans

FY2026 capital expenditure guidance is $1.5–1.65B (including $100–150M midstream), with plans to drill 66 operated horizontal wells and turn 72 to sales. Future development costs for proved undeveloped reserves total $4.7B — a multi-year capital commitment that cannot easily be deferred without reserve writedowns. An additional $440M is committed for the Quantum Capital buyout of Pinnacle interests, to be funded via equity sales in Pinnacle and a new $150M credit facility.

## Growth Analysis

CRK's growth thesis rests on three pillars: Western Haynesville delineation, LNG export demand proximity, and the NextEra data center power partnership.

**Western Haynesville:** Management estimates 2,561 net drilling locations representing decades of inventory. The play has been successfully extended with wells like the Olajuwon step-out (41 MMcf/day IP, 24 miles from the nearest producer). However, these are among the most technically demanding wells in the industry, and costs remain elevated. Only 30 wells have been drilled, and performance sustainability at scale is unproven.

**LNG Export Proximity:** CRK's Haynesville position provides a geographic advantage for serving Gulf Coast LNG terminals. With multiple LNG export facilities under construction (Plaquemines, Golden Pass, etc.) and additional capacity planned through 2028, structural demand for Gulf Coast gas should increase. This advantage is shared with other Haynesville operators but is particularly relevant for CRK given its scale.

**NextEra Partnership:** The March 2026 announcement of a 5.2 GW natural gas-fired power generation hub in Anderson County, Texas, selected under a U.S.-Japan $550B investment framework, is the most significant potential catalyst. CRK would supply up to ~1 Bcf/day by 2031, effectively doubling current production into a captive customer. However, this project remains subject to definitive agreements, permitting, and multi-year construction timelines. The $16B total project cost implies significant execution and financing risk for NextEra, and CRK's revenue contribution would not materialize until at least 2029–2031.

**Estimate Trajectory:** Current-year EPS has been revised down 10.7% over 30 days (from $0.92 to $0.82). Next-quarter estimates have fallen 24.6% in 30 days and 33.8% in 90 days. FY2027 estimates have risen 5.2% in 30 days, but this likely reflects gas strip recovery expectations rather than company-specific improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.33, CRK trades at approximately 13x trailing EPS ($1.33) and ~21x FY2026E EPS ($0.82). On FY2027E of $1.42, the forward P/E compresses to ~12x, but this assumes a meaningful gas price recovery.

Relative to peers: CNX Resources trades at 9.9x, Magnolia Oil & Gas at 17.5x, and California Resources at 16.4x. CRK's trailing P/E is in line with the peer median, but CRK is significantly more leveraged, has no dividend (peers yield 2.4% median), and is generating negative free cash flow. The trailing P/E reflects FY2025 earnings that included a $292M asset sale gain; on adjusted underlying earnings, the effective P/E is substantially higher.

The consensus price target distribution ranges from $13 (bear case, ~25% downside) to $29 (bull case, ~67% upside) with a median of $19. Given the 10 Holds and 3 Sells among 14 analysts, the consensus median target of $19 implies only ~10% upside — insufficient to compensate for the risk profile.

The proved reserve PV10 of $4.5B at SEC prices vs. the enterprise value of approximately $7.9B ($5.1B market cap + $2.8B debt - $0.024B cash) means the market is assigning substantial value to unproved resources and the Western Haynesville option. This premium is justified only if gas prices remain elevated and the exploration program succeeds at scale.

## Competitive Landscape

CRK is the largest Haynesville operator, with a dominant acreage position and company-owned midstream infrastructure. Its proximity to Gulf Coast demand centers provides a basis differential advantage over Appalachian producers (EQT, CNX) and reduces exposure to Waha-related Permian associated gas discounts.

The Western Haynesville exploration program differentiates CRK from peers in terms of growth inventory, but at the cost of significantly higher well costs and execution risk. CRK's horseshoe well designs and extended lateral techniques represent genuine operational innovation in an extremely challenging geological environment.

Key competitive vulnerabilities include pure natural gas exposure (no oil diversification to buffer gas price weakness), the scale of the balance sheet relative to peers, and the controlled-company governance structure that limits minority influence. CNX Resources, the most directly comparable peer, operates with lower leverage, a lower P/E (9.9x), and more conservative capital allocation.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Natural Gas Price Collapse:** CRK's all-in cost structure (operating costs ~$0.73/Mcfe, DD&A ~$1.42/Mcfe, interest ~$0.50/Mcfe) requires gas prices above approximately $2.65/Mcf to generate positive operating income. In FY2024 at $1.98/Mcf, the company posted a $230M net loss. A return to sub-$2 gas — plausible given Marcellus/Haynesville supply surges, warm weather, or economic slowdown — would push the company toward covenant stress and potential borrowing base reductions.

**Structural Negative FCF:** At $1.5–1.65B in guided 2026 capex and current gas strip prices producing ~$900M in operating cash flow, CRK will burn approximately $600–750M in cash this year. The company cannot sustain this without asset sales, revolver drawdowns, or gas price increases. The $440M Quantum buyout adds to near-term funding pressure.

**Leverage and Refinancing:** The bank credit facility matures November 2027. Semi-annual borrowing base redeterminations create event risk — a significant gas price decline could trigger a base reduction that curtails liquidity. The 6.75% senior notes due 2029 will require refinancing in a potentially unfavorable rate environment.

**Reserve Price Sensitivity:** Proved undeveloped reserves jumped from 1.0 Tcf to 4.2 Tcf solely due to the SEC pricing change from $1.84/Mcf to $3.07/Mcf. If gas prices decline, these PUDs would be written down, potentially triggering impairment charges and covenant concerns.

**Controlled Company Governance:** With 71% ownership, Jerry Jones effectively controls all corporate decisions. The 2024 private placement to Jones-controlled entities ($100.5M at prevailing prices) was dilutive to minorities. The board has only three independent directors.

**Lease Expirations:** ~125,000 net undeveloped acres expire by 2028, creating drill-or-lose pressure that may force capital spending even in unfavorable environments.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two identifiable catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings (likely May–June, not yet announced) where the next-quarter estimate decline ($0.15 → $0.11) could disappoint if gas prices weaken further; (2) the November 2027 bank credit facility maturity, which creates a refinancing catalyst window starting mid-2027. More immediately, the semi-annual borrowing base redetermination (typically spring and fall) could reduce available liquidity if gas strip prices decline.

**Timing window:** Near-term (1–3 months for earnings catalyst); medium-term (12–18 months for refinancing/liquidity catalyst). The earnings catalyst is actionable; the refinancing catalyst is too distant to trade now.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 25.3% SI with 9.2 days to cover, this is a crowded short. Jones's 71% stake reduces the effective float, making the days-to-cover figure meaningful. Any positive catalyst (gas price spike, NextEra definitive agreement) could trigger a violent squeeze — CRK rallied 68% from $18.27 to $30.74 in just 5 weeks (April 30 to June 18, 2025) and then gave back most of the move.

**Borrow and options signal:** The 13.2pp IV richness to HV confirms elevated cost-to-carry for options-based shorts. The 3.86x put/call volume ratio in near-term options suggests active put buying, but the put/call OI ratio of 0.61 indicates this is flow-driven rather than sustained structural positioning. The backwardated term structure (70.2% → 60.4%) implies near-term event pricing.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $10–13 represents 42–25% downside from $17.33. Upside squeeze risk to $25–29 represents 44–67% upside. The reward:risk ratio on a short is approximately 0.6:1 to 1.0:1 — well below the 2:1 minimum threshold for an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis is fundamentally sound — negative FCF, heavy leverage, commodity dependence, declining estimates — but the 25.3% SI, 9.2 days to cover, and binary catalyst exposure (gas prices, NextEra announcement) make this unsuitable for new short entry. The stock has already declined 44% from its 52-week high. Avoid, but do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** CRK would become investable under the following conditions:
1. **Gas strip above $3.50/Mcf for 2+ years** with visible path to FCF positivity (operating cash flow exceeding capex + interest by 10%+)
2. **NextEra partnership advances to a definitive agreement** with committed volumes, timelines, and pricing
3. **Debt reduction below $2.0B** or net leverage below 2.0x EBITDA, demonstrating the capital program can be self-funded
4. **Share price below $13** (analyst low target), which would compress EV/proved PV10 closer to 1.0x and provide a margin of safety

**Timeline:** The headwinds are structural, not cyclical. Negative FCF, Western Haynesville delineation risk, and the debt burden are multi-year issues. The NextEra partnership, if it progresses, would not contribute material cash flow until 2029–2031. Reassess at Q2 2026 earnings for updated capex guidance and gas price sensitivity.

## Conclusion

CRK offers exposure to a potentially large Western Haynesville natural gas resource base and an aspirational data center power partnership, but the current financial profile is incompatible with the risk level embedded in the equity. The company is burning cash at a rate of $450–750M annually, carries $2.8B in debt with a Z-Score in the distress zone, provides no dividends, and is controlled by a 71% shareholder with limited minority protections.

The score trajectory shows deterioration from 5.6 to 4.5 over the prior two reviews, and the current analysis confirms that decline was warranted. No new data point has emerged that reverses the fundamental concerns. At 13x trailing earnings (which include a $292M asset sale gain), the stock is not cheap enough to compensate for the leverage, commodity sensitivity, and execution risk.

Recommendation: **AVOID.** The stock is not suitable for new long positions. Aggressive gas bulls should consider lower-leverage alternatives such as CNX Resources (9.9x P/E, lower debt) or diversified E&Ps with oil optionality.
