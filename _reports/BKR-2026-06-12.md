**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes is executing a deliberate rotation away from oil-price-levered oilfield services (OFSE) toward a structurally-growing gas, power, LNG, and industrial-technology franchise (IET). That rotation is visible in the numbers: in FY2025 IET revenue grew 10% to $13.4B while OFSE fell 8% to $14.3B, and IET EBITDA rose 21% to $2.48B against OFSE EBITDA of $2.62B. Of the company's $35.9B remaining performance obligations at year-end 2025, $32.4B (90%) sits in IET, providing multi-year revenue visibility largely insulated from oil-price swings. On data-center power, BKR booked $1B of data-center-related equipment orders in 2025 and projects roughly $3B cumulative through 2027, supplying gas turbines and compression modules into third-party AI/electrification buildouts. Q1 2026 produced record IET orders of $4.9B.

The bull case is that BKR is becoming a gas/power/industrial-infrastructure name with recurring service revenue and a widening moat in turbines and compression, which should command a higher multiple than its oilfield-services lineage implies. The counterweight is the ~$13.6B Chart Industries acquisition. To pre-fund it, BKR raised roughly $9.9B in bonds in March 2026, tripling gross debt to $16.2B and pushing cash to $14.8B. That cash is earmarked, not free, and carries a 101% special-mandatory-redemption penalty if the deal breaks. The EU has set a June 26, 2026 decision deadline, and June 11 Reuters reports indicate bundling concerns were raised to the Commission, which could force remedies/divestitures or delay the targeted Q2 close.

We rate BKR a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.15, consistent with the gradual decline in our composite from 6.8 in February to the low-6s now. The thesis is intact and the IET franchise is genuinely improving, but forward EPS estimates have fallen sharply (-10.7% for the current year over 90 days, -22.5% for the current quarter), the stock trades at ~20x trailing with no valuation cushion, and the single most important catalyst is a binary regulatory event landing in roughly two weeks. The risk/reward does not clear the bar for a new long here.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes is an energy-technology company operating in 120+ countries with approximately 53,000 employees, down from ~56,000 at year-end 2025 following divestitures. It emerged from the 2017 merger of Baker Hughes Incorporated and GE Oil & Gas, a lineage that still shapes its supplier relationships, technology licenses, and balance sheet. The company runs two reporting segments:

- **Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE)** — Four product lines: Well Construction, Completions/Intervention/Measurements, Production Solutions, and Subsea & Surface Pressure Systems. This is the cyclical, oil-price-levered business that competes with SLB, Halliburton, NOV, Weatherford, and TechnipFMC.
- **Industrial & Energy Technology (IET)** — Five product lines anchored by Gas Technology Equipment (turbines/compressors) and high-margin Gas Technology Services (aftermarket), plus Industrial Products, Industrial Solutions, and Climate Technology Solutions. Competitors include Siemens Energy, Solar (Caterpillar), Mitsubishi Heavy, Sulzer, Flowserve, and Emerson.

The strategic story is the shift toward IET, which now produces EBITDA roughly equal to the larger-revenue OFSE segment and is growing while OFSE shrinks. IET's order book is the asset that matters most: 90% of total RPO, including 10+ year service agreements on installed turbine fleets. The competitive moat rests on scale across the gas value chain (LNG liquefaction equipment, aeroderivative and heavy-duty gas turbines, compression), a large installed base driving recurring service revenue, and privileged access to GE Aerospace/GE Vernova turbine technology via long-term licenses and a 50/50 aeroderivative joint venture.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY2025 revenue was essentially flat at $27.7B versus $27.8B in 2024, with IET (+$1.2B) almost fully offsetting OFSE (-$1.3B). Net income fell 13% to $2.6B, but the decline is largely below-the-line: a $103M loss on mark-to-market equity securities in 2025 versus a $367M gain in 2024 (a ~$470M swing), plus transaction costs. Underlying segment EBITDA improved, with IET up 21%. Net margin was ~9.5% and FCF margin ~9.1%, generating $2.54B of free cash flow (OCF $3.81B less capex $1.27B). Capex remains disciplined at 4-5% of revenue.

The reported net-income decline overstates operating deterioration. The earnings quality of the operating business improved in 2025; the headline figure was dragged down by a non-cash equity mark-to-market swing and deal costs.

**Tax tailwind is largely spent.** The effective tax rate has been artificially low due to valuation-allowance reversals ($308M in 2025, $664M in 2024) as U.S./U.K. entities returned to sustained profitability. Management guides to ~$1.1B of cash taxes in 2026, implying a normalizing (higher) tax rate that will be an earnings headwind not present in 2024-2025.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $6.59B (+2% YoY, a beat). Reported net income of $930M ($0.93 diluted EPS) versus $402M a year ago, but $721M of that was one-time gains on the PSI and Surface Pressure Control dispositions. Segment EBITDA rose 11% to $1.24B, driven by IET EBITDA up 35% to $678M (CTS orders jumping from $148M to $1.26B). OFSE fell 7% on Middle East disruption and the SPC divestiture.

**Balance sheet — the Chart financing reshapes the picture.** At YE2025, total debt was $6.1B, equity $18.8B, cash $3.7B. At Q1 2026, total debt rose to $16.2B and cash to $14.8B after the March 11, 2026 bond offering ($6.5B USD notes plus €3.0B notes, rates 3.2%-5.85%, maturities to 2056).

A reported D/E of 2.15 and an Altman Z-Score in the "distress zone" are model artifacts, not genuine solvency signals. The Z-Score is depressed by persistently negative retained earnings (-$3.3B, a legacy of post-merger GE Oil & Gas impairments rather than current losses) and a distorted EBIT input that reflects one-off items. BKR is investment-grade, in compliance with all covenants, generates $2.5B+ of FCF, and has a fully undrawn $3.0B revolver. The genuine change comes post-Chart: net interest expense rises (Q1 already showed +$35M net interest, including a $43M write-off of bridge-facility fees). The durability of the IET order book becomes the key offset to materially higher leverage.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 returned ~$1.3B to shareholders ($910M dividends + $384M buybacks), with the dividend raised to $0.23/quarter. Buybacks were paused in Q1 2026 (zero versus $188M in Q1 2025) to preserve capital for Chart, with ~$1.35B remaining authorized. The dividend was maintained. The buyback pause signals that management is prioritizing balance-sheet capacity over per-share returns near-term, a temporary headwind to per-share metrics.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on IET. Three durable tailwinds support it: roughly 7% LNG demand growth in 2025 with continued LNG/gas-infrastructure buildout; gas-fired power for electrification and AI/data centers, where natural gas is positioned as the dispatchable backbone; and new-energy optionality (CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal), though management now hedges the latter with explicit "transition slowdown" language. The order book confirms the demand is real and broad-based rather than speculative, with management reiterating a ~$40B order ambition.

The data-center thesis is genuine but constrained. The constraint is supply-chain: management repeatedly flags aeroderivative supply-chain tightness and extended lead times, a capacity ceiling on throughput that is also a GE-linked dependency. BKR is an equipment supplier into data-center buildouts (turbines, compression modules), not an operator or an AI company; the end demand is third-party. This positioning caps how much of the AI-capex multiple BKR can plausibly claim.

The countervailing signal is estimate revisions, which are the most concerning data point in this report. Consensus EPS has fallen across every forward horizon: current quarter -22.5% over 90 days, next quarter -19.2%, current year -10.7% (to $2.31), next year -4.3% (to $2.78). The 30-day revisions have stabilized (roughly flat), so the worst of the cutting may be behind, but a falling-estimate backdrop is the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that would justify a more aggressive call. The cuts reflect soft OFSE conditions (worldwide rig count fell to 1,818 in 2025 from 2,087 in 2023, with management expecting weak OFSE through most of 2026 and a significant decline in Middle East upstream spending) plus the spent tax tailwind. IET growth is offsetting OFSE weakness at the EBITDA line, but not fast enough to keep total EPS estimates rising.

The earnings-beat record is strong (four consecutive beats: 16.2%, 15.1%, 9.3%, 12.2%), which lends confidence that management hits the marks it sets. But beats against a falling estimate bar are less valuable than beats against a rising one.

## Valuation Assessment

At $63.63, BKR trades at ~20.3x trailing earnings on $2.6 EPS, and ~27.5x the falling current-year consensus of $2.31. Against the energy peer set, the trailing multiple sits below the peer median P/E of 26.9, but the peer comp is heterogeneous and not cleanly comparable: OXY (77.0x) and FANG (198.4x) are distorted by trough earnings, while the midstream names KMI (21.4x), TRGP (26.9x), and OKE (15.8x) are stable-cash-flow utilities-adjacent businesses. BKR is neither a pure E&P nor a pure midstream; it is an equipment-and-services hybrid mid-rotation toward an industrial profile. A ~20x trailing multiple is reasonable for the IET-weighted future but not obviously cheap.

The dividend yield of ~1.46% provides only modest support, below the peer median of 2.3% and well below the midstream comps. There is no income cushion to underwrite the stock during a regulatory delay.

This is not a setup that warrants ignoring trailing multiples. The estimate revisions are negative; the rising-estimate revision signal that would flag systematic underestimation is absent and the sign is reversed. The valuation work cannot rely on "estimates are rising, consensus targets are stale." Consensus targets sit at a mean of $71.24 and median $74.00, roughly 12-16% above the current price, with a range of $48-85. With estimates falling, those targets are at risk of downward revision rather than serving as floors.

The fairest read: at ~20x trailing earnings, the market is paying a fair-to-slightly-cheap price for a business whose operating mix is improving but whose near-term EPS trajectory is soft and whose biggest catalyst is a binary regulatory event. There is no margin of safety to absorb a Chart break or remedy outcome. We score valuation 5.7 — not stretched, but not offering the asymmetric upside that would justify a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

BKR's two segments face opposite cycles. OFSE is in a soft patch (declining global rig counts, OPEC+ idled-supply overhang, Middle East spending cuts) and competes against far larger pure-plays SLB and Halliburton, where BKR is the third player. IET occupies a more differentiated and less-contested position in gas turbines and compression, where the competitive field narrows to Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy. The installed-base service annuity (10+ year contracts) is a genuine moat that pure-play oilfield-services peers lack.

The Chart Industries acquisition is the strategic linchpin. Chart makes cryogenic and process equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling (LNG, industrial gases, hydrogen), highly complementary to IET's compression/turbine franchise. The combination is meant to deliver a fuller LNG/gas value-chain offering. The strategic conviction is evident in management having outbid and broken up a prior Chart-Flowserve merger, paying a $258M termination fee on Chart's behalf. The same bundling synergy that excites BKR is what is drawing EU scrutiny, so the deal's strategic logic and its regulatory risk are linked.

The GE dependency cuts both ways. Privileged access to GE Aerospace/GE Vernova turbine technology and the 50/50 Aero JV is a competitive advantage in IET, but it is also a structural reliance: Q1 2026 purchases from the JV were $210M, and aeroderivative supply-chain tightness is a named operational constraint. Loss or underperformance of these counterparties would directly impair the IET franchise. On balance, the competitive trajectory is improving — IET share of EBITDA is rising, the moat in turbines/compression is widening, and the order book is at records — which supports a competitive-position score of 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Chart integration and financing risk (dominant near-term).** BKR is paying $210/share cash, ~$13.6B enterprise value, funded largely by fresh debt. Risks span integration execution, dis-synergies, regulatory conditions, and the special-mandatory-redemption penalty if the deal breaks.

2. **EU antitrust overhang (near-dated and binary).** Outcomes range from clean approval (bullish, removes the overhang) to remedies/divestitures (dilutes synergies) to delay or block (forces the special mandatory redemption and disrupts the strategy). This is the single most important catalyst/risk to monitor.

3. **GE dependency.** Structural reliance on GE Aerospace/GE Vernova for heavy-duty turbine supply, aeroderivative technology, IP licenses, and the Aero JV, with named aeroderivative supply-chain tightness constraining the growth story.

4. **OFSE cyclicality.** Management expecting soft OFSE through most of 2026 and a significant decline in Middle East upstream spending. [unverified — the brief does not support a Hormuz-specific oil-price scenario; the brief references "Middle East disruption" and "Middle East conflict risk" generally.]

5. **Energy-transition whipsaw.** The 10-K reframes the risk toward a potential slowdown of the transition (stranding CCUS/hydrogen/geothermal investments), the inverse of the usual concern.

6. **Concentration.** Pemex receivables and associated CDS exposure (notional down to $159M, running off by Sept 2026); U.S. at 18% of gross receivables; broad national-oil-company exposure.

7. **OBBBA tax-law changes** (enacted July 2025), aggregate impact described as still uncertain, layered on the spent valuation-allowance tailwind.

These risks justify a Risk Factors score of 5.3 — below average because two genuinely material, near-dated risks (the binary EU decision and the tripled-leverage Chart financing) are only partly priced, consistent with the trajectory decline from 6.0 to 5.0 in prior reports.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is signaling moderate, not acute, concern. Near-term ATM IV is 38.0% versus 33.1% one-year historical volatility (a 5pp rich premium), with IV at only the 19th percentile within the current chain — uncertainty is elevated but not extreme. The term structure is in contango (38.0% near-term versus 41.2% at six months), a normal/complacent shape with the bulk of priced risk pushed out toward the deal-resolution window rather than the immediate front end. The 1-week put/call skew of 3.9% (elevated put demand) reflects hedging into the June 26 EU decision, but the put/call volume ratio of 0.45 and OI ratio of 0.52 are not bearishly skewed. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±4.3%. The P/C OI ratio drifted up from 0.45 to 0.52 alongside a 3.7% price decline since late May, consistent with incremental hedging rather than informed directional selling. The options market is pricing a known binary event without panic, which fits the HOLD posture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The IET rotation continues to compound: IET EBITDA overtakes and pulls away from OFSE, the IET order book converts at improving margins, and the data-center/gas-power tailwind delivers the $3B cumulative 2025-2027 orders. The Chart deal closes cleanly or with minor remedies, adding a complementary cryogenic/process franchise and a fuller LNG value chain. As the market reprices BKR from an oil-price beta toward a gas/power/industrial-infrastructure compounder, the multiple expands toward the high-20s on a normalized ~$2.90-3.10 EPS base in 2027. Bull target ~$85 (in line with the high end of the consensus range), roughly +34% from current.

**Bear Case.** The EU blocks or imposes onerous remedies on Chart, triggering the special mandatory redemption and forcing BKR to unwind the strategy while carrying the deal costs and disruption. OFSE weakness deepens with falling rig counts and Middle East spending cuts, and the spent tax tailwind plus higher interest expense compress EPS. Estimate cuts continue, the multiple de-rates toward 16-17x on $2.30 EPS, and the stock retraces toward the low end of the analyst range. Bear target ~$48, roughly -25% from current.

The base case sits between: Chart eventually closes (possibly with remedies or a modest delay past Q2), IET growth offsets OFSE softness, estimates stabilize around $2.31-2.78, and the stock trades at ~21-22x a normalizing earnings base. Base target ~$70, roughly +10% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale: a credible 12-month appreciation thesis exists, but the near-term risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the dominant catalyst is binary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clean EU approval (or minor, clearly-defined remedies) of the Chart deal by/around June 26, 2026, removing the binary overhang and confirming the strategic path.
- A reversal in EPS revision trend — two consecutive months of rising current-year and next-year estimates — confirming IET growth is outpacing OFSE drag at the consolidated EPS line.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore valuation asymmetry (forward P/E toward 19-20x against the $2.78 next-year estimate).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- EU blocks the Chart deal or imposes remedies severe enough to trigger the special mandatory redemption, forcing a strategy reset.
- IET orders decelerate materially (a quarter well below the ~$40B annualized order ambition) alongside continued OFSE contraction, signaling the rotation has stalled.
- Evidence the data-center order pipeline is shrinking or the aeroderivative supply-chain bottleneck is materially capping conversion.

**Reassessment timeline:** The EU decision around June 26, 2026 is the immediate reassessment point, followed by the next quarterly print (Q2 2026 results) for IET order momentum and estimate stabilization.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes is rotating capital and earnings toward a gas/power/LNG/industrial franchise with recurring service revenue, a widening moat in turbines and compression, and a genuine data-center power tailwind. The operating business improved in 2025 even as headline net income fell on non-cash marks, and the 90% IET share of the order book gives multi-year visibility that pure oilfield-services peers cannot match. We hold the competitive-position trajectory in high regard.

The stock does not offer the asymmetric setup required for a new long. Forward EPS estimates have fallen across every horizon over the past 90 days, the valuation at ~20x trailing earnings carries no margin of safety, the dividend yield is thin, and the single most important catalyst is a binary EU antitrust decision landing in roughly two weeks against a balance sheet now carrying tripled gross debt with a 101% break penalty. The score trajectory — from 6.8 in February through the low-6s now, with Management Quality, Risk Factors, and Financial Health each declining — reflects the accumulation of these near-term risks against an intact long-term thesis. We rate BKR a HOLD at an overall 6.15, and would look to upgrade on clean EU approval, a reversal in estimate revisions, or a pullback that restores valuation cushion.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report repeatedly characterized BKR as trading at "~20x forward," but forward P/E on the falling current-year consensus of $2.31 is ~27.5x (not ~21.8x), materially weakening the "fair-to-slightly-cheap" valuation read that underpins the HOLD-not-AVOID stance.
