**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arcus Biosciences is a late clinical-stage oncology and immunology company whose equity story has narrowed to a single asset over the past seven months. Two Phase 3 futility failures in the domvanalimab (anti-TIGIT) program — STAR-221 in gastric cancer (December 2025) and STAR-121 in first-line metastatic NSCLC (April 2026) — have effectively spent the company's antibody franchise, leaving casdatifan (AB521), a wholly-owned HIF-2α inhibitor for clear cell renal cell carcinoma, as the dominant driver of value. The stock has risen 210% from its 52-week low of $8.84 to $27.37, powered by differentiated casdatifan monotherapy data (15.1-month median PFS and 45% confirmed ORR in the 100mg cohort of ARC-20, versus roughly 5.6 months for the approved competitor belzutifan) and a June 2026 Bristol Myers Squibb clinical trial collaboration that supplied a replacement combination partner after AstraZeneca exited.

The financial position is adequate but not comfortable. Cash and marketable securities stood at $876M at the end of Q1 2026 against a burn of roughly $130-138M per quarter, giving runway into at least 2H 2028 — enough to reach the pivotal PEAK-1 (casdatifan + cabozantinib) readout. The company has no product revenue; the ~$17M of Q1 2026 revenue is deferred-revenue amortization from old Gilead and Taiho upfronts, is declining, and is 88% concentrated in Gilead. The equity is structurally dependent on future milestones, a positive casdatifan outcome, or dilutive financing. Share count has already grown roughly 36% (92.2M to 125.5M) since YE2024.

Our recommendation is HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The bull case (best-in-class ccRCC agent, large market, blue-chip validation) and the bear case (single-asset binary risk, two recent Phase 3 wipeouts, informed partners trimming exposure, safety-driven combination halt) are both credible and roughly balanced. Two informed-insider signals — Gilead declining its $100M option-continuation payment and AstraZeneca walking away from the casdatifan/volrustomig combination — argue for caution because the most-informed parties are reducing exposure. With the stock having tripled off its low and the options market pricing a ±24% near-term move in backwardation, we see no asymmetric edge on either side of the trade at $27.37.

## Company Overview

Arcus is headquartered in Hayward, California, operates as a single reportable segment, and has no approved products. Its business model pairs internal small-molecule discovery with partner-funded development. Three partnerships have historically defined it:

- **Gilead Sciences** — a 25.0% shareholder with three board seats, holding licenses to domvanalimab, AB308, quemliclustat, and zimberelimab. Gilead paid $175M upfront (2020), $725M (2021), and a $100M option-continuation payment (2024).
- **Taiho Pharmaceutical** — Asian rights (ex-China) to the pipeline, including casdatifan (exercised Q4 2025 for $15M).
- **AstraZeneca** — partner on the PACIFIC-8 domvanalimab trial.

The strategic center of gravity has shifted to casdatifan, a wholly-owned (ex-Taiho Asian rights) HIF-2α inhibitor competing directly against Merck's approved belzutifan (Welireg) in kidney cancer. The June 2026 BMS collaboration is a clinical trial and supply agreement — casdatifan remains Arcus-owned and Arcus-led, with BMS supplying its PD-L1/VEGF-A bispecific for a combination arm. It is validation and a replacement combination partner, not a monetization event.

Arcus is pre-revenue in economic substance and cash-burning, but reasonably capitalized for its stage.

Cash and investments fell from $1.01B (December 2025) to $876M (March 2026), a ~$134M sequential decline, with roughly $138M of operating cash outflow in Q1 2026. Management guides to cash sufficiency into at least 2H 2028, aided by the wind-down of domvanalimab development, which reduces near-term R&D burn.

Capital structure carries $100M drawn on a $250M Hercules term facility at roughly 12.85% effective rate, maturing 2030, secured by substantially all assets, with a minimum-cash covenant beginning July 2027 (conditionally waived above a market-cap threshold). A $200M ATM equity program is active. In 2025 the company raised approximately $438M gross through two equity offerings (29.4M shares). The mechanism is straightforward: a pre-revenue biotech at this burn rate either reaches a value-inflecting clinical readout or returns to equity markets to refill the tank; the ATM and the covenant timeline signal ongoing dilution potential.

The runway reaches the pivotal casdatifan catalysts without a forced raise, which is the single most important financial fact here. We score financial health at 5.0 — the cash cushion and extended runway offset the structural burn and the reliance on future capital, but this is not a self-funding business and the Hercules covenant plus active ATM cap the score. This holds the prior read of 5.0 and reflects no new deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are entirely clinical and binary rather than commercial. There is no revenue trajectory to model; the reported revenue is declining deferred-revenue amortization.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and net-negative in recent momentum. Over 90 days, consensus loss estimates narrowed materially (current year -3.48 vs. -4.15 90 days ago, +16.1%; next year -3.10 vs. -3.58, +13.4%; next quarter +23.2%), driven by the reduced burn from winding down domvanalimab. Over the most recent 30 days, however, the current-quarter estimate deteriorated (-5.1%) as the near-term picture stabilized. This is not the classic inflection signal — the "improvement" reflects lower spending on a failed program, not accelerating revenue, so the estimate-revision BUY signal that applies to revenue-inflection stories does not apply here.

The real growth engine is casdatifan. If PEAK-1 confirms the ARC-20 profile in a randomized setting, the addressable ccRCC opportunity is large and the value re-rating would be substantial. The tempering caveats are that ARC-20 is open-label and single-arm, and that STAR-221 was a textbook example of promising Phase 2 data (EDGE-Gastric) failing to translate to a Phase 3 OS benefit. We score growth prospects at 5.5 — the casdatifan opportunity is real and differentiated, but it is a single binary event rather than a diversified growth pipeline, and two of the three Phase 3 programs just failed. This is down 0.5 from the prior 6.0, reflecting the further concentration of the story into one asset and the safety-driven eVOLVE-RCC02 halt.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are not meaningful for a pre-revenue biotech — trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-8.24). Valuation here is an option on a single clinical outcome. The market cap of roughly $3.46B against $876M net cash implies an enterprise value of approximately $2.6B ascribed almost entirely to casdatifan (plus residual quemliclustat and PACIFIC-8 optionality).

Against a differentiated HIF-2α asset in a large ccRCC market, $2.6B is defensible if PEAK-1 succeeds and casdatifan reaches even a mid-single-digit-billion peak-sales trajectory. It is expensive if PEAK-1 fails, in which case the equity likely reverts toward cash value less two-plus years of burn — a scenario consistent with the $8.84 low. The peer table is of limited use given the heterogeneity of biotech (peer median P/E 18.1 reflects revenue-generating names like CPRX and ADMA, not comparable clinical-stage single-asset stories; CRSP at -8.2 P/E is the closest structural analog).

Analyst targets frame a wide two-sided range: mean $35.75, median $34.50, high $47.00, low $22.00, against the current $27.37. The mean sits ~31% above spot, but on a pre-catalyst binary the target dispersion is the message — the low of $22 (below current) reflects the downside embedded in a failed readout. We do not treat the analyst mean as a floor here because this is not a cheap-relative-to-growth inflection stock; it is a binary option where consensus targets are probability-weighted guesses that will gap violently in either direction on PEAK-1.

We score valuation at 4.5. The stock has already re-rated to price in much of the best-in-class casdatifan thesis, leaving limited asymmetric upside at $27.37 absent the confirmatory Phase 3 data, while the downside on a failure is severe. This is a modest reduction from the prior 5.5 — not because the stock has appreciated (prior appreciation is not a valuation input), but because at $27.37 the market is now pricing casdatifan success with meaningful probability, compressing the reward-to-risk versus the low-$20s where the option was cheaper.

## Competitive Landscape

Casdatifan's arena is the HIF-2α inhibitor space, where Merck's belzutifan (Welireg) is the approved incumbent. Arcus's differentiation thesis rests on a genuine efficacy gap in late-line ccRCC. Kidney cancer is a large, high-unmet-need indication with room for a better-in-class agent, and continued big-pharma interest (AZ initially, now BMS) validates the backbone potential of the molecule. If the ARC-20 profile holds in a randomized Phase 3, casdatifan would have a credible best-in-class positioning.

The anti-TIGIT space (domvanalimab) has been a graveyard across the industry — Roche's tiragolumab and multiple others have repeatedly disappointed. Arcus's back-to-back STAR-221 and STAR-121 futility outcomes are consistent with a class problem as much as a company problem, which mildly softens the read on management's execution but leaves the franchise largely spent, with only PACIFIC-8 (Stage 3 NSCLC) remaining as a meaningful TIGIT shot.

The competitive trajectory is genuinely two-sided. Casdatifan's data is improving Arcus's position in ccRCC; the domvanalimab failures and the AZ combination exit are deteriorating factors. We score competitive position at 5.5 — casdatifan's differentiation is real and improving, but the position is narrow (one asset, one indication) and partly dependent on partner relationships that have contracted (AZ exit) as readily as expanded (BMS entry).

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic, recently-crystallized risks dominate the profile and are largely what drives the overall score.

1. **Single-asset binary.** After the domvanalimab failures, casdatifan is effectively the entire equity story. A negative PEAK-1 readout would be existential for the valuation, implying a reversion toward cash value less burn.

2. **Two Phase 3 futility failures in four months.** Both discontinued for futility. This is a material deterioration since the prior year and a live reminder that Phase 2 signals in this pipeline have not translated to Phase 3.

3. **Casdatifan combination safety halt.** In March 2026, Arcus and AstraZeneca agreed not to re-open enrollment in eVOLVE-RCC02 (casdatifan + volrustomig) following observations of potential immune-mediated adverse events — a safety-driven halt that warrants monitoring across the broader casdatifan combination strategy.

4. **Gilead declined the $100M option-continuation payment (April 2026);** its option period ends July 14, 2026, after which it loses rights to Arcus's early-stage pipeline (CCR6, CD89, CD40L programs). A 25%-owner with three board seats and full visibility choosing not to pay is a quiet vote of reduced confidence and narrows a future funding source.

5. **WuXi/BIOSECURE exposure.** WuXi Biologics (China) is the sole manufacturer of zimberelimab and domvanalimab, creating supply-chain and cost risk under the BIOSECURE Act, though this matters less as domvanalimab winds down.

6. **Covenant/dilution risk.** The Hercules minimum-cash covenant begins July 2027, and the active ATM plus a history of large offerings signal continued dilution potential.

We score risk factors at 3.5 — the combination of an unresolved existential binary, two recent Phase 3 wipeouts, a safety-driven combination halt, and informed-partner de-risking represents concentrated, high-magnitude idiosyncratic risk that is only partly offset by the cash cushion. This is the lowest of the six scores by design.

The options market is corroborating, not contradicting, the view that this is a binary setup with no directional edge at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Casdatifan's ARC-20 data is a genuine best-in-class signal in a large, high-unmet-need ccRCC market. PEAK-1 confirms the profile in a randomized setting, the BMS collaboration validates the combination backbone, and the cash cushion funds the company to that catalyst without a forced dilutive raise. In this scenario the stock re-rates materially — analyst high target $47 (+72% from current) is a reasonable proxy, and a clean Phase 3 win could push higher over time.

**Bear Case.** Casdatifan is a single-asset binary and the pipeline has already delivered two Phase 3 futility failures in four months plus a safety-driven combination halt. Informed partners are trimming exposure (Gilead declined its option payment; AZ exited the volrustomig combo). A negative or ambiguous PEAK-1 readout, or a broader casdatifan safety signal, sends the equity toward cash value less two-plus years of burn — the $8.84 low is the reference point, implying downside on the order of 60-70%. Continued ATM issuance and the 2027 covenant add dilution pressure along the way.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive PEAK-1 readout confirming the ARC-20 efficacy profile in a randomized setting.
- Additional casdatifan readouts in 2026 (cabozantinib combo update, early-line data) that reinforce the best-in-class thesis without new safety signals.
- A pullback toward the low-$20s that restores the option value that has been compressed by the run to $27.37.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Negative or ambiguous PEAK-1 data, or any new casdatifan safety signal echoing the eVOLVE-RCC02 halt.
- A dilutive equity raise well below expectations before reaching the pivotal readout.
- Evidence of tightening liquidity relative to the July 2027 Hercules covenant.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the next set of casdatifan readouts expected through 2026 and, most importantly, PEAK-1 — the pivotal binary that governs the entire thesis. The July 14, 2026 lapse of Gilead's option period is a near-term informational marker but not itself thesis-changing.

The score trajectory (5.3 → 5.2 → 4.75) is declining, and this read confirms rather than reverses the trend: the growth and valuation scores step down as the story narrows further into a single binary and the stock re-rates toward pricing success. Two prior HOLDs raise the bar for continuing unchanged, but the fundamental situation — a well-capitalized single-asset binary bet ahead of its pivotal readout — remains one where neither BUY nor AVOID is warranted.

## Conclusion

RCUS is a well-financed casdatifan binary with a large cash cushion attached. The asset is genuinely differentiated, the market is large, and the runway reaches the catalyst without a forced raise. Against that sit two Phase 3 futility failures in four months, a safety-driven combination halt, informed-partner de-risking, and a stock that has already tripled off its low to price meaningful casdatifan success. Short interest at 16.5% reflects genuine two-sided conviction. With no asymmetric edge at $27.37 ahead of the pivotal PEAK-1 readout, we rate the stock HOLD. Overall score: 4.75.
