**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Westlake Corp is a vertically integrated producer of chemicals and building products operating two structurally different businesses: a profitable Housing & Infrastructure Products (HIP) segment (~$4.1B 2025 sales, $587M operating income) and a deeply loss-making Performance & Essential Materials (PEM) commodity-chemicals segment (~$7.0B sales, -$2.1B operating loss). The consolidated 2025 result, a $1.5B net loss and -$11.70 EPS, is dominated by roughly $1.38B of largely non-cash impairment and closure charges in PEM, including a $727M goodwill write-off of the entire North American Chlorovinyls unit. The trailing P/E of -6x is mechanically meaningless; the relevant question is where mid-cycle earnings power sits and when the chlorovinyls cycle bottoms.

The case for owning WLK rests on three pillars: a deep cyclical trough that should eventually mean-revert, aggressive self-help (closing high-cost VCM/chlor-alkali/styrene/epoxy capacity in Louisiana, Mississippi, the Netherlands, and China), and counter-cyclical M&A (buying VYNOVA's Wilhelmshaven PVC/VCM site out of insolvency, plus ACI compounds). Consensus EPS estimates have risen sharply, current-quarter +160.6% and current-year +149.5% over 90 days, signaling the market is rebuilding a forward earnings curve off a trough base. Against that, the evidence for caution is concrete: free cash flow was -$530M in 2025 and -$303M in Q1 2026, the dividend (~$272M/yr) and acquisitions are being funded from a shrinking cash pile, low-coupon debt is being refinanced into 5.55%–6.375% notes, JP Morgan and Citigroup both downgraded the stock after Q1, and insiders sold into strength in March 2026 with no open-market buying since.

We rate WLK a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The chemicals segment is at or near a cyclical bottom and the self-help/distressed-acquisition strategy is the correct playbook for a well-capitalized owner, but the timing of the cycle turn is unknowable, the dividend-plus-M&A capital plan is stretched against negative FCF, and the setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would turn more constructive on confirmation of two consecutive quarters of PEM margin recovery or a meaningful pullback that restores asymmetry; we would turn negative on a dividend cut, a covenant breach, or an adverse DOJ outcome.

## Company Overview

Westlake operates two reportable segments:

- **Housing & Infrastructure Products (HIP)** — Westlake Royal Building Products (vinyl siding, trim, Cultured/Eldorado Stone, DaVinci roofing, windows, Zuri decking), Westlake Pipe & Fittings (PVC pipe), and Westlake Global Compounds. The segment is tied to North American new construction and repair/remodel. It remained profitable through the downcycle.
- **Performance & Essential Materials (PEM)** — Chlorovinyls (PVC, VCM, chlor-alkali; one of the world's largest producers), Olefins/Polyethylene, and Epoxy (acquired from Hexion in 2022). This is a globally competitive commodity business.

The core structural advantage is deep vertical integration: ethane → ethylene (via the OpCo/LACC joint ventures) and salt → chlor-alkali → VCM → PVC → downstream building products. The US natural-gas feedstock position is a cost advantage relative to naphtha-based competitors in Asia and Europe, which management is emphasizing in 2026 given the elevated oil environment.

The company is controlled by the Chao family (TTWF LP), which owns approximately 72% of shares. CEO Jean-Marc Gilson runs the business; Albert Chao serves as Executive Chairman and James Chao as Senior Chairman. A CFO transition is underway, with Jonathan Baksht receiving an initial CFO RSU grant in June 2026 as longtime CFO Steven Bender hands off.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a textbook cyclical peak-to-trough collapse. Revenue fell from a $15.8B peak in 2022 to $11.17B in 2025 (-8% YoY). Net income swung from $2.25B in 2022 to a -$1.5B net loss in 2025. The mechanism is straightforward: lower PVC, polyethylene, and chlorine prices and volumes combined with higher energy costs (US natural gas +51%, ethane +33% in 2025) compressed gross margin from 16% in 2024 to 7% in 2025, and EBITDA flipped from +$2.21B to -$248M.

Most of the 2025 loss was non-recurring. Roughly $1.38B of charges hit PEM: the $727M Chlorovinyls goodwill impairment, $393M of North American chlorovinyls and Lake Charles styrene closures, a $247M Pernis (Netherlands) epoxy closure, and a $9M Suzhou (China) PVC closure. These charges depress reported EBIT and drive the distress-zone Altman Z-Score of 1.62, but they overstate the underlying operating deterioration.

The balance sheet remains adequate. Year-end 2025 cash was $2.72B ($2.27B at Q1 2026), the current ratio was 2.24, the $1.5B revolver (refinanced April 2026, maturing 2031) is fully undrawn, and the company is in compliance with its leverage covenant. Total debt is approximately $5.58B with D/E of 1.21, reasonable for a vertically integrated cyclical chemicals producer.

Q1 2026 confirmed the trough persists but showed stabilization. The net loss was -$169M (-$1.31 reported, -$0.77 adjusted) on revenue of $2.65B (-7% YoY). HIP operating income fell to $56M, hit by a $67M PVC pipe antitrust settlement charge; PEM's operating loss widened to -$211M. EBITDA returned to +$150M and the segment closures should structurally lift PEM margins by removing high-cost capacity.

The capital-allocation tension is the central financial concern. Westlake is maintaining and even slightly raising its dividend (~$0.53/quarter), funding acquisitions (ACI ~$124M; Wilhelmshaven), and absorbing closure costs while burning free cash. November 2025 financing replaced cheap legacy debt with high-coupon notes (5.55% 2035, 6.375% 2055), pushing Q1 2026 interest expense up 44% YoY to $56M. This is sustainable from the cash hoard for now, but a prolonged downturn would pressure the dividend.

The earnings surprise record adds a note of caution to forward visibility: three misses and one beat over the last four quarters, including a -264.8% Q1 2026 surprise, indicates the trough has been difficult to forecast in real time. We weight the rapidly rising consensus estimates as a positive signal but discount them somewhat given that track record.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic "growth" story here in the conventional sense; the forward case is a cyclical earnings recovery off a depressed base, layered with consolidation upside. Estimate revisions capture the recovery expectation clearly:

- Current-quarter EPS: $1.95, up +14.9% over 30 days and +160.6% over 90 days
- Next-quarter EPS: $2.03, up +16.4% over 30 days and +183.2% over 90 days
- Current-year EPS: $3.99, up +1.5% over 30 days and +149.5% over 90 days
- Next-year EPS: $3.93, up -1.7% over 30 days but +30.8% over 90 days

The magnitude of the 90-day revisions meets the threshold that typically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to a fundamental recovery. The qualifying detail is that current-year revisions have flattened over the most recent 30 days (+1.5%) and next-year has ticked slightly negative (-1.7%), which suggests the steepest part of the upward revision is behind us and the Citi downgrade narrative (cooling PVC prices, weak housing recovery) is partially offsetting the recovery thesis. The revision velocity is real but decelerating, which is why we treat it as a constructive signal rather than a high-conviction buy trigger.

The structural growth levers are: (1) PEM margin recovery as high-cost capacity exits the supply curve and the cycle bottoms; (2) HIP's repair/remodel exposure, which is less cyclical than new construction; and (3) accretion from distressed acquisitions made at trough valuations. Each is plausible, but each depends on a chlorovinyls cycle turn whose timing is outside management's control. Housing remains a headwind — starts were 1,359k in 2025 with NAHB's 2026 outlook at 1,333k (declining) — which caps the near-term HIP contribution.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are uninformative at a cyclical trough: the -6x trailing P/E reflects an impairment-driven loss year. The relevant lens is forward and normalized earnings power.

On current-year consensus EPS of $3.99, WLK trades at approximately 19x forward earnings at $76.48, in line with the specialty chemicals peer median P/E of 21.2x (EMN 21.2x, AXTA 19.1x, NEU 19.2x; the higher-multiple ESI at 69.9x and SOLS at 69.3x are growth-oriented and not appropriate comps for a cyclical commodity producer). On next-year EPS of $3.93, the multiple is also roughly 19x. That places WLK at a slight discount to the most relevant peers, but not a deep discount that signals mispricing, and the equity lessons here are instructive: a low or in-line multiple in Materials is not, by itself, a reason to buy, because the sector has structurally negative selection alpha and cheapness has been anti-predictive.

The analyst target range — low $90, mean $113.40, median $114, high $133 — implies 18% to 49% upside to the mean/low from the current $76.48. We treat these as informative but stale-skewing: Citigroup cut its target to $95 from $125 in late May citing softening PVC pricing and a slower building-products recovery, and JP Morgan moved to Underweight. The targets reflect a normalized earnings recovery that has not yet been confirmed in reported results. Because the stock trades at roughly peer multiples on forward estimates that are still being debated, we do not view the gap to consensus targets as the kind of clean asymmetry that warrants a high valuation score.

A sum-of-the-parts framing is more favorable than the consolidated view: a profitable, less-cyclical HIP segment generating ~$0.6B of operating income deserves a building-products multiple, while the loss-making PEM segment is being valued near a cyclical floor. If PEM normalizes toward even modest mid-cycle profitability, the combined entity is underpriced. This requires the cycle to turn, and the timing is genuinely uncertain. We score valuation at 5.0, neither clearly cheap nor stretched on forward fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

In PEM, Westlake competes against Dow, LyondellBasell, Formosa, Olin, OxyChem, and Shintech in chlorovinyls/olefins, and against Olin, Nan Ya, Huntsman, and Kukdo in epoxy, plus low-cost Asian exporters. In HIP it competes with James Hardie, Cornerstone Building Brands, CertainTeed, JM Eagle, Diamond Plastics, and Trex.

The downcycle is industry-wide, driven by Chinese and broader Asian overcapacity and exports, weak China and Europe demand, and trade-barrier-induced domestic oversupply (US PVC export markets are now blocked by antidumping duties of EU 58%, UK 56%, Brazil 43.7%, pushing supply back into the domestic market). The competitive-position trajectory is mixed but tilts modestly favorable: Westlake's US gas-based feedstock position is a durable cost advantage when oil/naphtha is elevated, and its strong balance sheet is allowing it to consolidate at the trough while weaker competitors (e.g., VYNOVA) enter insolvency. Closing its own uneconomic capacity while buying distressed competitor assets is the correct share-gaining playbook for a well-capitalized incumbent.

The offsetting factor is that the trade-barrier dynamic redirects export supply into the domestic market, pressuring US PVC pricing, the precise concern Citi cited. Net, we see competitive position as stable-to-modestly-improving on trajectory, which supports a 5.5 rather than a higher score, with the caveat that the recent signal on this dimension across the market has been roughly half its all-time strength and should be confirmed rather than relied on alone.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Chlorovinyls/epoxy oversupply and Asian import flood** — the central driver of the 2025 collapse. Antidumping duties abroad redirect supply into the US market, capping domestic pricing recovery.
2. **Commodity cyclicality and energy-cost whipsaw** — margins swing with the gas/oil spread. The US gas position helps when oil/naphtha is high but hurts when gas spikes, as it did in 2025.
3. **Weak housing demand** — high mortgage rates and declining starts pressure the otherwise-profitable HIP segment.
4. **Capital-allocation stretch** — maintaining/raising the dividend and pursuing M&A while burning free cash and refinancing into 6%+ coupons. Sustainable from the cash hoard, but a continued downturn would force a dividend reassessment.
5. **DOJ criminal antitrust investigation into PVC pipe** — a genuine tail risk beyond the $67M civil settlement already booked. Discovery stay runs to July 2026; indirect/Canada claims and the Brazilian Di Gregorio/PPG indemnification (~R$550M) are pending.
6. **Controlling-shareholder governance** — the Chao family owns ~72%, minority holders cannot influence votes, and the charter waives the corporate-opportunity doctrine for the principal stockholder.
7. **Rising leverage/refinancing cost** in a higher-for-longer rate environment amid negative FCF.

Several of these risks are at least partly priced: the cyclicality is permanently embedded, and the impairments are taken. But the DOJ probe, the dividend-funding tension against negative FCF, and the uncertain timing of the cycle turn are real, somewhat asymmetric downside catalysts that justify a below-average risk score of 4.5.

Cheap near-term IV makes the stock a reasonable place to express a view via options if one had conviction, which we do not at present.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Westlake is a quality, vertically integrated franchise caught in a deep cyclical trough that should mean-revert. PEM's impairments and the closure of high-cost capacity structurally improve the segment's go-forward margins; HIP remains profitable and provides a floor. Consensus EPS is rebuilding fast, and the company is using a fortress balance sheet to consolidate the industry at the bottom (Wilhelmshaven out of insolvency, ACI compounds), positioning for outsized operating leverage when chlorovinyls pricing recovers. On a sum-of-the-parts basis, a normalizing PEM plus a building-products-multiple HIP supports the $113–133 analyst target range. Bull case: $130, roughly +70% from current, on a confirmed cycle turn and PEM margin recovery toward mid-cycle.

### Bear Case

The chlorovinyls downcycle persists longer than expected as antidumping duties abroad flood the US market and Chinese overcapacity continues. Housing weakness drags HIP, removing the profit subsidy. Negative free cash flow continues, forcing a dividend cut and curtailing M&A, while 6%+ refinancing raises interest expense. The DOJ probe produces an adverse outcome. Estimates roll over as they began to in the most recent 30 days. Bear case: ~$56 (revisiting the 52-week low), roughly -27% from current.

### Base Case

The cycle grinds along a bottom through 2026 with gradual, uneven PEM improvement, HIP holding up on repair/remodel, and the dividend maintained but not comfortably covered. Forward estimates prove roughly achievable but visibility stays poor. Base case: ~$88, roughly +15% from current, reflecting modest re-rating toward forward earnings power partially offset by the unresolved cycle and capital-allocation overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The chemicals trough is real and the self-help/consolidation strategy is sound, but the cycle-turn timing is unknowable, the dividend-plus-M&A plan is stretched against negative FCF, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The honest classification is uncertain: we lack sufficient evidence of a confirmed turn to justify a BUY, and the franchise quality and rising estimates argue against an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of PEM operating-loss narrowing with positive EBITDA confirmation that capacity closures are lifting margins
- Free cash flow turning positive (or clearly inflecting toward positive) while the dividend is maintained, removing the funding-tension overhang
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that restores genuine asymmetry to forward earnings power

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or suspension
- A covenant breach or a forced drawdown of the revolver, or refinancing at materially worse terms
- An adverse DOJ antitrust outcome beyond the booked $67M, or current-year estimates rolling over by more than 15% over 90 days

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026) and the July 2026 expiration of the DOJ discovery stay are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

Westlake is a well-capitalized, family-controlled cyclical chemicals and building-products producer at or near a downcycle bottom, executing the right counter-cyclical playbook of closing high-cost capacity while buying distressed competitor assets. The trailing loss overstates operating deterioration — roughly $1.38B of the $1.5B 2025 net loss was impairments and closures — and forward estimates are rising sharply. But the recovery's timing is genuinely uncertain, free cash flow is negative while the dividend and M&A consume cash, the stock trades at roughly peer multiples on still-debated forward estimates, dual analyst downgrades and defensive options positioning reflect near-term caution, and insiders sold into strength with no buying since. The setup is balanced. We rate WLK a **HOLD** and would require confirmation of PEM margin recovery and FCF inflection, or a more attractive entry price, before turning constructive.
