**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Keysight Technologies sits at the center of two of the strongest secular demand vectors in technology today: AI data-center buildout (400G/800G/1.6-terabit Ethernet test, optical transceiver manufacturing) and defense modernization (radar, space, satellite, spectrum). The order data confirms the theme is real and broad. Q2 FY26 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) revenue rose 31% year-over-year to $1,717M, orders jumped 56% to $2,051M, and half-year orders were up 43% — a book-to-bill well above 1.0 that signals continued revenue momentum into the back half of the fiscal year. Consensus EPS estimates have moved up 14-18% across all forward windows over the past 90 days, and the company has beaten four consecutive quarters, most recently by 21%.

The business quality is not in question. Keysight generates high-quality free cash flow (FY25 FCF $1,281M, a 23.8% margin), carries appropriate fixed-rate debt for its scale, and is steadily shifting revenue mix toward higher-margin, stickier software and simulation content via the ESI, Synopsys OSG, PowerArtist, and Spirent acquisitions. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. This is a durable franchise in an oligopolistic test-and-measurement industry.

The issue is price. At $296 the stock trades near 59x trailing and roughly 29x current-year EPS ($10.20 consensus), a premium to the peer median of ~27x, after roughly doubling over the trailing year. The market already discounts continuation of an upcycle that management explicitly cautions "may not be sustainable." Q2's headline margins were flattered by ~3.8 points from a one-time IEEPA tariff refund that carries reversal risk. Insiders have sold uniformly across the C-suite at $275-$361 with no open-market buying, and a five-year TSR that trails the comp peer group by roughly 145 points sits uncomfortably against rising CEO pay. The fundamental trajectory supports holding the position; the valuation does not support adding at current levels. We rate KEYS a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Keysight is a global leader in electronic design, emulation, and test solutions: the instruments and software engineers use to design and validate communications, semiconductor, aerospace/defense, and automotive products. The company descends from the Hewlett-Packard/Agilent test-and-measurement lineage and was spun out in 2014. Its fiscal year ends October 31. Headcount is roughly 16,500-16,800.

The company reports two segments:

- **Communications Solutions Group (CSG)** — approximately 69% of FY25 revenue, rising to 72% in Q2 FY26. Serves commercial communications (wireless, wireline, data-center) and aerospace, defense and government. This is the AI-leveraged segment, driving 400G/800G/1.6-terabit Ethernet test, transceiver manufacturing capacity, 5G/6G, radar, and space/satellite.
- **Electronic Industrial Solutions Group (EISG)** — approximately 31% of revenue. Semiconductor, general electronics, automotive/energy, plus a growing design/CAE simulation software franchise built from ESI Group, Synopsys OSG, and PowerArtist.

The competitive moat rests on first-to-market solutions for cutting-edge standards, deep integration into customer R&D workflows, a large installed base, and increasing software and recurring-revenue content. Peers include Rohde & Schwarz (private), Anritsu, National Instruments/Emerson, and, in adjacent design/simulation, Synopsys, Cadence, and Ansys.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory shows a clear cyclical inflection.** Revenue moved from $5.42B (FY22) to a $5.46B peak (FY23), down to a $4.98B trough (FY24, -9%), recovering to $5.375B in FY25 (+8%). The recovery then accelerated sharply into FY2026: H1 revenue +27% to $3,317M. Acquisitions contributed roughly 7 points of the growth, meaning organic growth is running in the low-to-mid twenties, a genuine demand acceleration rather than purely M&A optics.

**Orders are the leading tell and they are strong.** H1 orders +43% to $3,696M, implying book-to-bill comfortably above 1.0. This is the single most reassuring data point in the file for near-term revenue durability.

**Margins are improving but the Q2 print is partly optical.** FY25 gross margin was 62.1% (down 1 point on tariffs/mix); GAAP operating margin 16.3%, weighed down by acquisition amortization and integration costs. Segment operating margins are much higher (CSG 26.5%, EISG 24.7% in FY25). Q2 FY26 GAAP operating margin jumped to 23.7% and gross margin to 68.6%, but approximately 3.8 points of the operating margin came from the IEEPA tariff-refund benefit (+$57M to operating income). Stripping that out, underlying margin expansion on volume and mix is still real, but a naive read of the headline overstates run-rate profitability.

**Cash generation is high-quality.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1,409M and FCF $1,281M (a 23.8% FCF margin, implying roughly 150% FCF/net-income conversion). H1 FY26 OCF was $942M. R&D of $1,007M in FY25 (19% of revenue) funds the product velocity that sustains the moat.

**The balance sheet is solid but more leveraged after the M&A spree.** Cash was $2.4B at April 2026 against total debt of roughly $2.53B, all fixed-rate senior notes (3.00%-5.35%, laddered 2027-2034). Net debt is modest and the current ratio is 2.35. The Altman Z of 2.38 ("grey zone") is a mechanical artifact of $2.9B of newly added goodwill and intangibles plus higher debt from the October 2025 acquisitions, not a distress signal; liquidity and coverage are healthy. The 2027 notes ($699M) are now current; refinancing risk is negligible given cash on hand and the new $750M five-year revolver.

**Capital allocation is buyback-only.** There is no dividend. Growth is M&A-led, and shareholder return runs entirely through repurchases: a $1.5B program authorized November 2025 with $1.19B remaining after $307M repurchased in H1 FY26. Share count drifts down slowly (~171M). This is capital-efficient enough to avoid a cash-hoarding penalty, but it is not a differentiated allocation story.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest part of the file. Three independent signals confirm an inflection:

1. **Revenue acceleration:** organic growth in the low-twenties on top of a cyclical recovery off the FY24 trough.
2. **Order momentum:** book-to-bill well above 1.0, providing visibility into H2 revenue.
3. **Estimate revisions:** current-quarter EPS +16.1%, next-quarter +17.8%, current-year +14.4%, and next-year +15.0% over 90 days. Revisions have stabilized in the most recent 30 days (essentially flat), meaning the sharp upgrade already happened and consensus has caught up rather than continuing to accelerate.

The demand is anchored in confirmed structural themes. AI data-center infrastructure is driving Ethernet and transceiver test in CSG; defense modernization is driving radar, space, and spectrum; and AI-driven semiconductor capacity plus design software is driving EISG. The same data-center demand vector shows up across the electronics supply chain, which raises the durability of the near-term thesis.

Two caveats temper the score. First, management itself added explicit language that recent growth in some markets "may not be sustainable," and visibility beyond the current backlog is limited with a substantial fixed-cost base. Second, roughly 7 points of the reported Q2 growth is inorganic; the organic base is strong but the headline overstates the trend rate. The estimate-revision signal is meaningful, but with revisions now flat over 30 days and the stock at a full multiple, the "systematic underestimation" dynamic that would justify treating targets as floors has largely played out. We score growth prospects a 7.5, a step down from the prior 8.0, reflecting that the inflection is now well-recognized and partly priced.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the crux of the call. At $296, KEYS trades at roughly 59x trailing EPS ($5.02 GAAP) and approximately 29x current-year consensus EPS. On next-year consensus ($11.88), the forward multiple is ~25x. Against a peer median P/E of 27.0 (Roper 20.8, Teledyne 31.6, Trimble 26.1, Zebra 27.0, Cognex 73.1), Keysight sits at a modest premium on current-year earnings and roughly in line on next-year.

The bull framing is that trailing P/E is meaningless at a cyclical inflection and forward metrics are more appropriate. That is a fair point: the ~29x current-year multiple is the right anchor, not the 59x trailing. But even on forward earnings the stock is not cheap. A ~25x next-year multiple on ~15% projected EPS growth implies a PEG near 1.7, above the 1.5 threshold at which the "analyst targets are floors" logic applies. Consensus is pricing continuation of a strong upcycle, not a deeply mispriced inflection stock. Analyst price targets (mean $388, median $396, range $250-$426) sit above the current price and imply upside, but those targets embed the same AI/defense growth extrapolation as the stock, so they are not independent confirmation of undervaluation.

Two factors argue against rewarding the current level with a high valuation score. First, the Q2 margin print, and by extension part of the earnings base analysts are extrapolating, is flattered by the tariff refund that could be reduced, delayed, or reversed. Second, management's own caution that growth "may not be sustainable" means the forward estimates carry cyclical downside that a straight-line model would miss. If demand normalizes toward mid-single-digit organic growth, a 25-29x multiple compresses meaningfully.

We assign a valuation score of 4.5. The business is high-quality and growing, but at these multiples there is no identifiable mispricing in Keysight's favor. The price already reflects the good news, leaving limited margin for error. This is a low score because current price sits at or above fair value on forward fundamentals with asymmetric downside if the upcycle fades.

## Competitive Landscape

Keysight's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The test-and-measurement industry is oligopolistic, with high barriers around standards leadership, installed base, and R&D intensity. Keysight's strategic direction of moving up the value chain from hardware instruments into software, simulation (EDA/CAE via ESI, OSG, PowerArtist), and end-to-end network assurance (Spirent) is the right one, increasing recurring revenue and customer stickiness. The software/recurring mix is building and supports a structurally higher multiple if integration executes.

The main competitive risk is customer consolidation: the communications end-market is concentrating into fewer, larger hyperscaler and carrier buyers, which raises single-customer sensitivity over time even though no customer today exceeds 10% of revenue. On segment-level growth versus industry, CSG and EISG are both growing well ahead of the broader instrumentation market on the AI/defense tailwinds, evidence of share gains or at least share holding within a rising tide. We score competitive position 6.5: a strong, stable moat with a favorable software-mix trajectory, but not a company demonstrably widening its lead in ways the market has not already recognized.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff refund reversal.** Keysight booked a $100M IEEPA refund receivable after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling and recognized $57M in Q2 operating income, but explicitly warns recovery could be delayed, reduced, or denied depending on customs processes and appropriations. This is both a realized tailwind and a live reversal risk that inflated the most recent margin print.

**Tax cliff.** The Malaysia tax incentive expired October 31, 2025 (renewal in process, not guaranteed); the Singapore incentive expires 2029. A separate $107M tax-refund lawsuit against the U.S. carries a warning that a loss would "most likely result in a material increase in effective tax rate and income tax liability." The favorable recent ETR is a swing factor that a standard extrapolation would miss.

**Demand sustainability.** Management's addition of "may not be sustainable" language is a self-flagged cyclicality risk. With a substantial fixed-cost base, a demand normalization would compress margins and de-rate the multiple simultaneously.

**Integration risk.** Three deals closed within days in October 2025 (Spirent $1.56B, OSG $580M, PowerArtist $26M) plus an immediate Viavi divestiture ($399M), adding $2.9B of goodwill/intangibles and a growing amortization headwind (~$146M in H1 FY26 vs ~$67M prior year).

**Litigation (resolving favorably).** The multi-year Centripetal Networks patent campaign has broken Keysight's way: patents invalidated, non-infringement rulings, and Federal Circuit affirmance in April 2026. Residual appeals remain but the risk is diminishing.

On balance, the idiosyncratic risks (tariff reversal, tax cliff, and demand sustainability) are specific and not fully priced into a stock at a full multiple. We score risk factors 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 68.0% is rich versus 41.4% trailing historical vol (a 26.6-point spread) and sits at the 90th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (68.0% near-term versus 56.0% at six months), and IV has risen sharply from 42.1% to 68.0% while the price fell 18.6% since the prior snapshot, with the put/call OI ratio doubling from 0.51 to 0.99. The nearest expiry implies a ±13.7% move. This positioning is consistent with the market bracing for the upcoming Q3 FY26 report (~August 2026), the key near-term catalyst. Skew is balanced (-0.6%), so this is uncertainty pricing rather than a directional bearish bet; the rich IV signals the market expects a large move, and the burden is on the print to sustain the growth narrative that the multiple embeds.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($388-426)
Order momentum carries revenue growth into FY27 as AI data-center and defense demand proves durable. Organic growth stays in the high-teens-to-twenties, software/simulation mix lifts blended gross margin structurally above 65%, and integration of Spirent/OSG delivers cross-sell. Estimates continue rising, EPS reaches the ~$12 next-year consensus and beyond, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of $388 to the high of $426. This case requires the demand cycle to extend rather than normalize.

### Bear Case ($230-250)
AI data-center test demand normalizes as hyperscaler transceiver capacity is built out, defense budgets face procurement disruption, and the IEEPA refund partially reverses. Organic growth decelerates to mid-single digits, the tax incentives lapse without renewal (lifting the ETR), and margins give back the tariff-driven flatter. On a normalized ~$9-10 EPS at a de-rated 24-25x multiple, the stock falls toward $230-250, roughly the analyst low target and 15-22% below current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 FY26 print (~August 2026), Q4/FY26 results, and FY27 guidance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$240s (near the analyst low target), where forward P/E compresses below ~22x and the PEG falls under 1.3 against ~15% growth.
- Confirmation that the order surge is durable — a second consecutive quarter of book-to-bill above 1.0 with organic revenue growth sustained above 15% — alongside stabilized underlying (ex-tariff) gross margin above 64%.
- Positive estimate-revision re-acceleration (a fresh +10% move over 30 days) that would signal consensus is again underestimating the trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q3/Q4 organic revenue growth decelerating below 8% YoY, or orders turning book-to-bill below 1.0, confirming the "may not be sustainable" caution.
- Material reversal of the IEEPA tariff refund or an adverse ruling on the $107M tax-refund lawsuit that raises the forward ETR.
- Guidance cut or a sequential gross-margin decline that is not explained by mix/one-time items.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY26 earnings report in August 2026, given the options market's ±13.7% implied move around it.

## Conclusion

Keysight is a high-quality franchise executing well against genuine, broad-based AI-data-center and defense demand. The order book, revenue acceleration, cash generation, and improving software mix are all constructive, and the fundamental trajectory has clearly improved over the past two years. Financial health is strong (score 7.0), growth prospects are compelling (7.5), and the competitive moat is stable-to-improving (6.5).

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation and margin quality. At ~29x current-year and ~25x next-year EPS with a PEG near 1.7, the stock already prices the continuation of an upcycle that management flags as possibly unsustainable, the most recent margin print was flattered by a reversible tariff refund, insiders are uniform sellers with no open-market buying, and near-term options are pricing a large move around Q3 earnings. The risk/reward at $296 is roughly balanced: mid-teens upside to the analyst mean against 15-22% downside to the bear case if demand normalizes.

We rate KEYS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: own it for the durable AI/defense growth thesis, but the full multiple argues against adding here. A pullback toward the low-$240s or a second quarter of confirmed order durability would materially improve the entry and warrant an upgrade. This is consistent with the stable HOLD trajectory over the past four reports; new data confirms the growth improvement while reinforcing that the valuation gates a stronger call.
