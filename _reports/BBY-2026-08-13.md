## Executive Summary

Best Buy has done what it needed to do operationally. Revenue grew 0.4% in FY2026 after two consecutive declines of 6.1% and 4.4%, comparable sales turned positive at +0.5%, and Q1 FY2027 accelerated to +1.9% revenue and +2.0% comps with adjusted diluted EPS of $1.28, up 11.3%. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters. The balance sheet is genuinely strong: $1,749M of cash against $1,169M of funded debt (a net cash position of $580M), 38x interest coverage on adjusted operating income, BBB+/A3 stable ratings, a fully undrawn $1.25B revolver expiring 2030, and lease-adjusted net obligations of roughly 0.9x an EBITDA proxy. A widely circulated screening figure of "debt-to-equity 4.95" is the equity multiplier (total assets $14,670M ÷ equity $2,964M), not a leverage ratio; properly computed, funded debt to equity is 0.40x.

The problem is price against earnings power. At $85.45 the stock is 13.0x current-year consensus EPS of $6.56 and 12.1x next-year $7.05, above the $80.90 consensus mean and $80.00 median targets, and 6.4% below the 52-week high after a 55% advance from the May 13 low of $55.52. Adjusted diluted EPS has been $6.37, $6.37 and $6.43 across FY2024–FY2026 — three years of flat earnings held up by cost-out and share count against a 4.0% cumulative revenue decline. Consensus for FY2027 of $6.56 implies just 2.0% growth, and the quarterly path embeds deceleration: Q2 at $1.34 (+4.7%), Q3 at $1.40 (flat year over year), and a residual fourth quarter of roughly $2.54 against $2.61 last year. Investors are paying the upper end of Best Buy's own three-year forward multiple range for a business consensus expects to grow low single digits.

The near-term variable is the AI-driven memory shortage, and it is two-sided rather than simply negative. Higher average selling prices on a percentage-margin business can lift gross profit dollars, and Best Buy deliberately pulled inventory forward in Q1 (merchandise inventories $5,598M, up 7.8% on 1.9% revenue growth) to sell pre-increase cost into higher retail. Against that, third-party research cited in June projects 2026 PC units down 10.4% and smartphone units down 8.4% against prices up 17% and 13%, and the recurring line in both the 10-K and 10-Q — gross margin gains from Marketplace, Ads and services "primarily offset by lower product margin rates" — says product economics are already eroding. Layer on Appliances comps of -13.6% into a housing-turnover-linked category, a credit-card profit-share arrangement worth roughly $459M of revenue against $1,785M of adjusted operating income and levered to consumer delinquencies, $298M of Best Buy Health goodwill still carried on discounted cash flows with management explicitly flagging further impairment risk, and a new CEO (roughly two months' tenure) and outside CFO (three months) executing a quarter that carries about 41% of annual adjusted EPS. Probability-weighted return from here is roughly +4% including the 4.5% dividend, below what the risk warrants.

## Company Overview

Best Buy operates two segments. Domestic (U.S. plus Best Buy Health) generated $38,278M of FY2026 revenue, 91.8% of the consolidated $41,691M; International (Canada) generated $3,413M. The company ran 1,068 stores at January 31, 2026 and 1,065 at May 2, 2026, with approximately 82,000 employees. Online was $13,166M, or 34.4% of Domestic revenue.

Domestic revenue mix under the FY2026 taxonomy: Computing and Mobile Phones 47%, Consumer Electronics 28%, Appliances 11%, Entertainment 7%, Services 6%, Other 1%. One definitional caution: effective Q1 FY2027 Best Buy moved credit card revenue and digital content revenue out of product categories into Services, so the 10-K's "Services = 6%" and the 10-Q's "Services = 10%" are not comparable. The company recast the prior-year quarter, making the like-for-like read 9% to 10%.

Two newer profit streams carry the equity story: Best Buy Ads, a retail media network being extended in FY2027 from merchandise vendors to agencies and demand-side platforms, and Best Buy Marketplace, a third-party seller platform launched in the Domestic segment during FY2026 that generates commission and advertising revenue without inventory investment. Neither is quantified anywhere in the FY2026 10-K or the Q1 FY2027 10-Q — no revenue, GMV, commission rate, seller count or profitability. Anyone underwriting "scaling new profit streams" is doing so without segment disclosure.

Supplier concentration is severe: the 20 largest suppliers were approximately 80% of merchandise purchased in FY2026, and five — Apple, Samsung, HP, LG and Sony — were approximately 55%. Best Buy generally has no long-term written contracts with them, and several operate competing direct retail channels. The figure is disclosed for FY2026 only, so no trend can be established; growth in Marketplace and Ads shifts the revenue mix but is not evidence that merchandise-purchase concentration improved.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2023 $46,298M; FY2024 $43,452M (-6.1%); FY2025 $41,528M (-4.4%); FY2026 $41,691M (+0.4%); Q1 FY2027 $8,936M (+1.9%). Revenue remains 10.0% below FY2023, but the direction has clearly changed and Q1 accelerated versus the full-year rate.

**Category dispersion is the substance behind the headline comp.** Q1 FY2027 Domestic comps: Entertainment +38.1% (gaming), Services +5.5%, Computing and Mobile Phones +4.2%, Consumer Electronics -2.7%, Appliances -13.6%, Other -20.1%. Appliances worsened sequentially against FY2026's -8.9% and Q1 FY2026's -8.0%; the three-period sequence is -14.8%, -8.9%, -13.6%. Management attributes it to large appliances, a housing-turnover-linked category. The "Other" collapse reflects the near-total wind-down of the Yardbird outdoor-furniture format (21 stores to 2).

**Margins.** Gross margin: 22.1% (FY2024), 22.6% (FY2025), 22.5% (FY2026), 23.5% (Q1 FY2027). Adjusted operating margin: 4.1%, 4.2%, 4.3%, 4.1%. Adjusted diluted EPS: $6.37, $6.37, $6.43, $1.28. GAAP diluted EPS: $5.68, $4.28, $5.04, $1.31.

Two points matter. First, three years of flat adjusted EPS on falling revenue is a real cost-management accomplishment, but it is not earnings growth — FY2026 adjusted EPS grew 0.9%. Second, in Q1 FY2027 GAAP EPS of $1.31 exceeded adjusted EPS of $1.28 because a $9M restructuring credit ran through GAAP operating income, including a -$7M reversal of accrued Domestic termination benefits attributed to "higher-than-expected employee retention from previously planned organizational changes." Either fewer positions were eliminated than budgeted, or staff were deliberately retained because sales inflected; the filing does not distinguish these and I make no claim about intent. Either way some anticipated SG&A savings will not appear on the original schedule, and $1.28 adjusted is the cleaner comparison.

A tax headwind also sits in the FY2027 path. FY2026 adjusted effective tax rate was 24.8%; both Q1 FY2027 and Q1 FY2026 ran 27.0%. Holding adjusted pretax income at the FY2026 level of roughly $1,809M, the higher rate costs approximately $0.20 of adjusted EPS, or about 3%, before any operating improvement.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $1,470M (FY2024), $2,098M (FY2025), $1,962M (FY2026); capex $795M, $706M, $704M. Derived free cash flow: $675M, $1,392M, $1,258M. FY2026 FCF margin was 3.02% and FCF yield on the $18.0B market cap is 6.98%. Accrual quality is sound — FY2026 OCF was 1.84x net income of $1,069M, and the sequential OCF decline was attributed to accounts-payable timing on inventory purchases.

Q1 FY2027 OCF of $375M against $34M a year earlier should not be annualized: Q1 is structurally the weakest quarter, and the $341M improvement was explicitly attributed to lapping a $330M prior-year payables outflow. As a labeled sensitivity, if FY2027 OCF held at $1,962M against guided capex of roughly $750M, FCF would be about $1,212M; a 20% OCF decline to $1,570M would leave FCF near $820M, still covering the roughly $807M dividend run-rate but with essentially no cushion.

**Balance sheet.** At May 2, 2026: cash $1,749M plus $287M restricted (product protection and self-insurance, not freely available); funded debt $1,169M ($500M 4.45% notes due October 2028, $650M 1.95% notes due October 2030, plus small finance leases); net cash $580M; operating lease liabilities $2,957M at fiscal year-end; shareholders' equity $3,083M. There is no refinancing cliff. Lease-adjusted net obligations of roughly $2,395M against FY2026 adjusted operating income plus depreciation of $2,616M is about 0.92x. Interest coverage is approximately 38x. Covenant compliance is confirmed, though the numerical threshold on the maximum quarterly cash flow leverage ratio is not disclosed, so headroom cannot be quantified.

The one genuine caveat on the balance sheet is the thin equity base — 20.2% of assets, reduced by $1,268M of cumulative goodwill impairments against $2,058M gross goodwill (International fully written off at $608M). Reported ROE of 36.1% therefore overstates operating quality; ROA of 7.3% is the honest measure.

**Two under-disclosed profit drivers.** Approximately 25% of FY2026 Domestic revenue was transacted on branded cards, and profit-sharing revenue from the credit card arrangement approximated 1.2% of Domestic segment revenue — roughly $459M against consolidated adjusted operating income of $1,785M. Best Buy does not own the receivables and does not disclose the associated program costs, so the contribution to operating income is below $459M and cannot be bounded; it is plausibly a substantial minority of consolidated operating profit, exposed to delinquencies, bad-debt rates, interest rates and promotional-financing regulation. Separately, a 10% change in the vendor funding deferral would affect net earnings by approximately $45M, about 4.2% of FY2026 net income, resting on a single estimation judgment.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks: $340M (FY2024) at an average $72.52, $500M (FY2025) at $86.42, $273M (FY2026) at $69.18, and zero in Q1 FY2027 versus $100M a year earlier at $64.39. $3.0B remains on the authorization. Dividends: $801M, $807M, $801M, and $202M in Q1, now at a $0.96 quarterly rate ($3.84 annualized). Dividends were 63.7% of FY2026 FCF; dividends plus buybacks were 85.4%. The pattern — most aggressive repurchase at the highest average price, paused as the stock recovered — is either cash preservation ahead of the inventory build and C-suite transition or price discipline. The filings do not distinguish them, but the realized outcome is unfavorable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs, and each carries an offset.

**Leg one: comp inflection.** Comps went from -6.8% to -2.3% to +0.5% to +2.0%, and management reports a ninth consecutive quarter of positive computing comps. This is real. But the composition is narrow — Entertainment at +38.1% on a gaming cycle is not durable, and two of six Domestic categories representing about 39% of mix are declining.

**Leg two: memory-driven ASP inflation.** Incoming CEO Jason Bonfig stated in June that Best Buy saw staggered price increases in Q1, expects ASPs to increase in Q2 with unit elasticity impacted, and brought in more inventory in Q1 to mitigate. The inventory pull-forward is visible: merchandise inventories of $5,598M versus $5,194M a year earlier and $5,230M at fiscal year-end, with accounts payable at $5,118M (up 9.6%) and supply-chain-financing participation at $861M versus $600M at January 31. Financing part of the build through the disclosed SCF facility is why Q1 OCF improved despite a $367M inventory outflow; the roughly $260M sequential SCF increase against a roughly $370M inventory build is proportionate and I see no evidence of stress. Selling pre-increase cost into post-increase retail should be a gross-margin tailwind for Q2 and Q3.

The offset is unit volume. Third-party research cited in late June projected 2026 PC shipments down 10.4% and smartphone shipments down 8.4%, with PC prices up 17% and smartphone prices up 13%, and normalization not expected until end-2027. Those unit forecasts, if approximately right, imply category volume declines that could overwhelm the ASP benefit — and the arithmetic in a retail P&L is that ASP inflation lifts gross profit dollars only while units hold. The most likely shape is FY2027 benefiting from ASP inflation and pre-buy, with the volume payback landing in FY2028. I have no evidence establishing which dominates, and the sourcing on the DRAM-price and 2027-capacity claims is tier-3 and unconfirmed.

**Leg three: Ads and Marketplace mix.** Q1 Domestic gross margin gains came from Marketplace, Ads and services, and the filings say those gains were "primarily offset by lower product margin rates." That phrase appears in both the FY2026 10-K and the Q1 10-Q, and it is the single most important recurring line in the margin narrative: product margins are falling and the asset-light mix is holding the consolidated rate up. Since neither Ads nor Marketplace revenue is disclosed, the durability and slope of that offset cannot be modeled.

**Consensus.** Current-year EPS estimates of $6.56 are up 1.1% over 90 days and 0.1% over 30 days; next-year $7.05 is up 0.8% over 90 days and down 0.3% over 30. This is stability, not an inflection. The estimate-revision signal that would justify paying up for momentum — 30%-plus 90-day revisions — is not present here. Consensus growth of 2.0% this year and 7.5% next year against a 13.0x forward multiple implies a PEG of roughly 2.4 near-term and 1.7 out-year.

Portfolio reshaping is a modest positive. Total stores fell from 1,108 to 1,065, but the decline is concentrated in non-core formats (Yardbird 21 to 2, Canada standalone mobile 29 to 12, Outlet Centers 24 to 18) while core U.S. stores held roughly flat at 884, with approximately four Domestic net additions planned by fiscal year-end — the first stated net additions in years. The incoming CEO's reported small-format emphasis rests on August 1–3 headlines with no confirmed format economics, capex or unit targets; it should be treated as an unconfirmed direction.

The supplied peer set is not a clean comparator: Lennar and NVR are homebuilders at 13.7x and 16.5x, Aramark is a food-service contractor at 41.8x, and Genuine Parts' 602x is an accounting artifact. The stated 18.0x median is not a multiple Best Buy should be expected to earn. The more informative anchor is Best Buy's own recent range, roughly 10x to 13x forward adjusted EPS across FY2024–FY2026, and on that basis the stock sits at the top of the band.

Consensus targets say the same thing from a different direction. Mean $80.90, median $80.00, high $95.00, low $62.00 — the current price is 5.6% above the mean and 6.8% above the median. The pattern in which analyst targets function as floors applies to inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rapidly rising estimates; here estimates are flat and the stock has already crossed above the target distribution's center. The ratings distribution corroborates: 5 buy, 18 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell, with the strong sell added in the last month.

What would make the stock cheap? Two things. Absolute yield support is real — a 4.49% dividend covered at 63.7% of FCF, backed by net cash, investment-grade ratings and no maturity before October 2028, is a defensible income proposition and puts a soft floor under the equity. And FY2028 consensus of $7.05 at 12.1x is not demanding if the memory cycle proves revenue-accretive rather than volume-destructive.

What makes it not cheap today is that the multiple already reflects the inflection. The market paid 9-10x forward for this business in April and May when comps were turning; it now pays 13.0x for the same business with consensus embedding a fourth quarter down year over year, a roughly 3% tax headwind, product margin rates in decline, and a category shock whose direction is genuinely uncertain. I score valuation 4.5 — not a value trap in the structural sense, but with no margin of safety at the current price and an identifiable path to a mid-$60s revisit that the analyst low target of $62 already contemplates.

## Competitive Landscape

Best Buy's stated advantages are physical stores as an omnichannel asset, vendor store-within-a-store concepts, Geek Squad in-home services, curated assortment, and scale-based vendor partnerships. Management concedes the cost consequence directly: because the strategy rests on superior service and a full range of complementary services, "our cost structure may be higher than some of our competitors, creating additional margin pressure."

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. On the positive side, management states it "stabilized our market share position" in FY2026 while navigating tariffs (its own framing, unquantified), comps have turned, and asset-light additions — Marketplace, Ads, Bell Canada-operated Express locations in Canada, which Best Buy brands and supplies but does not operate or count as stores — extend reach without capital.

On the negative side, three structural pressures are documented in the filings. First, supplier power: five suppliers are about 55% of merchandise purchased with no long-term contracts, and the 10-K notes vendors "may be able to leverage their competitive advantages — for example, their own stores or online channels … to our detriment." Apple, the most important of them, both supplies Best Buy and competes with it directly, and it was Apple that raised device prices up to $500 in June. Second, price transparency: the 10-K states that flexibility to modify selling prices is limited by real-time comparison shopping, "a challenge that is further amplified by the increasing use of AI-driven tools and shopping platforms." Third, agentic commerce cuts both ways. Best Buy's FY2027 priorities include partnering with platform providers so customers can purchase directly from its catalog — Best Buy is the merchant and catalog owner, the AI platforms are third-party distribution. If discovery migrates to agents, the store as a discovery venue is partially disintermediated and the price-transparency disadvantage worsens. The counter-hypothesis, that a licensed catalog partner with fulfillment and service capability captures agentic demand currently leaking to marketplaces, is equally unproven. No financial evidence exists either way.

Marketplace itself introduces exposure Best Buy did not previously carry: unsettled law on retailer responsibility for third-party product liability and IP claims, anti-money-laundering and bank-account compliance requirements, and competition against third-party sellers on its own platform that cannibalizes first-party margin.

The five-year record is the cleanest external read on positioning. The April 2026 proxy shows cumulative indexed TSR of 74.07 against a peer group at 164.12 — roughly 90 percentage points of underperformance, a -26% absolute return against peers' +64%. Part of that gap is category (post-pandemic electronics normalization against a broad retail re-rating) rather than execution, but the direction is unambiguous. Competitive position scores 4.7: comps stabilizing and mix shifting toward higher-margin asset-light revenue, offset by falling product margin rates, unchanged supplier concentration and a documented AI-discovery threat.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory shortage on 47% of Domestic revenue.** The FY2026 10-K added specific new language: the cost and availability of memory components "have been, and may continue to be, affected by industry-wide supply constraints," and mitigating actions "may not fully offset higher costs and inventory constraints." This lands on the one category producing growth. Management expects Q2 ASPs up and units impacted. Third-party unit forecasts are materially negative. The pull-forward inventory is a Q2–Q3 buffer, not a solution.

**Appliances into a housing-linked category.** Three periods of -14.8%, -8.9% and -13.6% with no stated stabilization path, in a category the 10-K links to housing market conditions. Mix has fallen from 12% to 10% of Domestic revenue, which limits the drag but confirms the erosion.

**Credit-card profit-share.** Roughly $459M of revenue at minimal incremental cost, against $1,785M of consolidated adjusted operating income, dependent on third-party banks, with Best Buy sharing in some partner losses. Delinquency and promotional-financing regulation risk is real, and is disclosed only as a percentage footnote in Item 1A.

**Best Buy Health.** $475M of goodwill and intangible impairment in FY2025, $171M goodwill/intangible plus $21M long-lived in FY2026 (including $118M in Q3), plus a $111M Q1 FY2026 restructuring charge. The trigger was "a change in Best Buy Health's customer base," with management citing pressures in Medicaid and Medicare Advantage. $298M of goodwill remains, fair-valued primarily on DCF — the least observable method — with management stating further impairment is possible. These are non-cash and do not affect the dividend or covenants, but they cap book equity and signal a poor acquisition record.

**Fourth-quarter concentration under a new C-suite.** Q4 FY2026 carried roughly 33% of revenue and about 41% of adjusted EPS (quarterly figures from an earnings-call summary, not verified against a filing). Corie Barry departs as CEO on October 31, 2026; Jason Bonfig, a 27-year internal veteran from the merchandising side, succeeds her; Matt Bilunas stepped down as CFO effective July 31 and Anne Bramman, previously at Nordstrom and Carnival, starts August 19. Bonfig's internal continuity limits strategic-discontinuity risk, but Bramman is the first genuine outsider in the finance seat in nearly two decades, which raises the probability of a fresh look at guidance philosophy, the Best Buy Health carrying value and the capital-return framework. Departure dates rest on press reporting corroborated by the existence and Item 5.02 classification of the April 22, June 22 and August 3 8-Ks; the exhibits themselves were not read.

**Tariff optionality, unquantified.** The Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unauthorized on February 20, 2026. Best Buy is participating in the CBP refund process and recognized nothing in Q1. It directly imports only about 1% to 3% of assortment, so its own refund is likely modest, but it is unquantified. Relief may not be durable — further actions under other trade authorities were already in process at the 10-K date, and Best Buy's real exposure is indirect through vendor pass-through.

**Governance.** CEO grant-date compensation reached a five-year high of $17.35M in FY2026 (a reported 7% raise) in the fifth consecutive year of peer TSR underperformance, though compensation actually paid fell to $2.73M, 16% of grant value, so the realized mechanism worked. The company-selected incentive metric, Compensable Enterprise Operating Income, has been flat at approximately $1.78B for three years and is down 42.6% from $3.102B in FY2022.

**Insider activity.** Founder Richard Schulze sold 1,900,350 shares for approximately $151.1M between May 29 and July 14 at an average $79.54, reducing his stake from 11,430,936 to 9,530,936 shares (16.6% of his April holding, 0.90% of shares outstanding, residual roughly 4.5% of the company). The regularity and round-number daily totals point to a pre-arranged or programmatic disposition, and charitable gift transactions appear in the same window; estate and philanthropic planning by an octogenarian founder is at least as plausible as a valuation view. I do not treat this as a fundamental signal. All executive sales observed were same-day-as-vesting sell-to-cover at a single price ($64.019 on March 23, 2026). There were no open-market purchases by any executive or director in the reviewed window — weak evidence, but the absence of buying at $85 by insiders who watched the stock at $64 five months ago is worth noting alongside the paused buyback.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a meaningful near-term binary. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 47.8% sits 10.8 points above 37.1% one-year realized volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (47.8% near-dated versus 44.5% at six months). The one-month tenor expiring August 28 carries 56.5% IV and a 9.1% implied move — the Q2 FY2027 print, the first full quarter of memory-driven price increases and the first reported under a transitioning finance function. IV has risen sharply since July 22, from 36.9% to 47.8%, while the price fell 1.8%, and the put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.70 to 0.78. Put/call volume is 1.08 with a 3.0% put/call skew indicating elevated put demand.

Short interest of 8.5% of float at 4.6 days to cover is moderate — enough to produce a squeeze on a strong print, not enough to constitute a crowded position. The composite read is that the market is not pricing directional collapse but is pricing a wide two-sided outcome around a specific date. That is consistent with the fundamental setup and argues against paying up in front of the print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $105 (+22.9%)

Memory-driven ASP inflation proves net revenue- and gross-profit-accretive: units decline less than the 10.4% PC / 8.4% smartphone forecasts because four-to-five-year replacement cycles make consumers poor price anchors, and scale gives Best Buy leverage to defer increases longer than smaller competitors. Pre-buy inventory sells at post-increase retail through Q2 and Q3, lifting gross margin rate above the 23.5% Q1 level. Marketplace and Ads scale enough to offset falling product margin rates rather than merely matching them, pushing adjusted operating margin toward 4.6-4.8%. Appliances stabilizes as the compare eases. The tariff refund is recognized as an unbooked gain. Buybacks resume against the $3.0B authorization at $300-500M annually, and the new CFO resets the incentive metric mix. FY2028 adjusted EPS reaches $7.50 against consensus $7.05, and the stock earns 14.0x on a re-established growth record: $105, plus the 4.5% dividend.

### Bear Case — $64 (-25.1%)

Unit elasticity dominates ASP inflation from Q3 onward. Computing and Mobile Phones comps turn negative as $500 price increases meet a pressured consumer facing a housing-linked appliance slump and a category shock. Product margin rates continue falling faster than Marketplace and Ads can offset, and the mix benefit slows because both are being built against far larger retail media competitors. Appliances stays at -13% or worse as housing turnover declines. Credit-card profit-share compresses on rising delinquencies, removing high-margin revenue with no offsetting cost. The new CEO and CFO reset guidance conservatively in their first full quarters, and Best Buy Health takes a further impairment against the $298M residual. FY2027 adjusted EPS comes in near $5.80 rather than $6.56, the multiple compresses to 11x as the "inflection" framing breaks, and the yield backs up to 6.0% — precisely where the stock traded in April and May 2026: $64.

### Base Case — $88 (+3.0%)

Comps hold in the +1% to +2% range on ASP inflation offsetting modest unit declines. Gross margin rate holds roughly flat as mix gains match product margin erosion. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $6.56 with the tax headwind absorbed, and FY2028 reaches $7.05. The multiple stays near 12.5x on FY2028 earnings — the top of Best Buy's own range, justified by the balance sheet and yield but not expanded further absent proof of durable growth. $88, plus 4.5% dividend, for a roughly 7.5% total return over twelve months.

**Probability-weighted:** base 47%, bull 23%, bear 30% → approximately +3.7% total return including the dividend. Below what an equity with a 9.1% implied move into the next print and a category shock of uncertain sign should return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Existing holders should trim into the current strength — the stock is up 55% from the May low and sits 5.6% above the consensus mean target with an expected return below fair. Prospective buyers should wait.

**De-risking plan.** Reduce position size ahead of the late-August Q2 FY2027 print rather than after it; the options market is pricing a 9.1% move and the payoff is two-sided. Retaining a partial position is reasonable for income-oriented holders given the 4.49% yield covered at 63.7% of FCF and the net cash balance sheet — the dividend is not at risk in any scenario I can construct. Full exit is not warranted; this is a fairly-valued business with genuine operating improvement, not an impaired one.

**Targets.** Base $88 (+3.0% from $85.45), bull $105 (+22.9%), bear $64 (-25.1%). Twelve-month horizon anchored to the Q2 print (late August 2026), the Q3 print (late November, first under CEO Bonfig), and the Q4 print (early March 2027, which carries roughly 41% of annual adjusted EPS).

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Price in the $68-72 range on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore an 11x forward multiple, a 5.3-5.6% dividend yield and a 9%-plus FCF yield.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which Domestic gross margin rate expands *without* the qualifier "primarily offset by lower product margin rates" — evidence the Ads and Marketplace mix is additive rather than compensatory.
3. Computing and Mobile Phones comps of +4% or better in Q3 FY2027 *with* Domestic unit-driven commentary rather than ASP-driven, indicating elasticity is holding.
4. Resumption of buybacks at $150M-plus per quarter against the $3.0B authorization, or quantified disclosure of Best Buy Ads and Marketplace revenue.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Domestic comps turning negative in any quarter through FY2027, which would break the inflection framing that supports the current multiple.
2. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance or consensus cut below $6.20 (a 5%-plus reduction from $6.56).
3. A further Best Buy Health impairment against the $298M residual, or any impairment review extended to the $492M Domestic goodwill.
4. Appliances comps worse than -15% combined with Consumer Electronics comps worse than -5%, indicating housing and discretionary weakness broadening beyond one category.

**Reassessment date.** The Q2 FY2027 print, expected late August 2026, is the natural decision point. The specific items to check: Q2 gross margin rate versus the 23.5% Q1 level, Computing and Mobile Phones comp with ASP-versus-unit attribution, whether the inventory pull-forward has been sold through, whether buybacks resumed, and the first guidance commentary from the incoming CFO.

## Conclusion

Best Buy's operating turn is real and the balance sheet is a genuine strength — net cash of $580M, 38x interest coverage, no maturity before October 2028, roughly 7% free cash flow yield and a 4.49% dividend covered at 64% of FCF. Widely cited screening figures understate this materially: the reported 4.95 "debt-to-equity" is an equity multiplier, and the Piotroski score of 3 is a truncated single-filing calculation that computes to roughly 7 of 9 on the full FY2025/FY2026 data. Score trajectory has improved on exactly this dimension, from 5.5 to 7.0, and that improvement holds.

What has not improved is the price paid for the earnings. Adjusted EPS has been $6.37, $6.37 and $6.43 over three years; consensus expects $6.56 this year with a fourth quarter down year over year, and $7.05 next year. At $85.45 the stock is 13.0x and 12.1x those figures, at the top of its own three-year range, 5.6% above the consensus mean target, and up 55% from the May low. The bulk of the re-rating from 9-10x to 13x has already happened, and it happened on one quarter of 11% adjusted EPS growth in the year's easiest comparison and its smallest earnings quarter.

The forward variable is a memory-cost shock whose sign is genuinely uncertain, sitting on 47% of Domestic revenue, alongside Appliances at -13.6% into a housing-linked slump, a high-margin credit-card profit pool levered to consumer credit, $298M of DCF-valued goodwill that management says may impair again, and a CEO of two months and CFO of three executing the quarter that produces 41% of the year's earnings. The options market has repriced near-term volatility from 36.9% to 47.8% and is pricing a 9.1% move on the late-August print. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +4% including the dividend does not compensate for that distribution.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength; retain partial exposure for the covered yield if income is the mandate; revisit toward $68-72, or on evidence that the Ads and Marketplace mix is additive to gross margin rather than merely offsetting product margin erosion.
