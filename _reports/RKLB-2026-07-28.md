**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rocket Lab is a vertically integrated space company in the middle of a strategic identity change, moving from a launch-and-manufacturing pure-play into a would-be end-to-end operator via a pending ~$8B acquisition of Iridium Communications. The operating story is genuinely improving: revenue grew 38% in FY2025 to $601.8M and accelerated to +63% year-over-year in Q1 2026 ($200.3M), gross margin expanded from 21% (2023) to 34.4% (2025) to 38.2% in Q1 2026, and backlog rose to $2.22B on the back of an $806M Space Development Agency Tranche 3 award. The Space Systems segment (~67% of revenue) is larger and faster-growing than the launch business.

The problem is that none of this is visible in the bottom line. The company posted a -$228.8M operating loss in FY2025 (-38% margin) and burned $321.8M of free cash flow, funding itself almost entirely through equity issuance — $1.12B of at-the-market proceeds in 2025 and $445.6M in Q1 2026 alone, with a fresh $1.0B program signed in March 2026. The reported "fortress" balance sheet (Altman Z 3.22, $1.48B cash, 4.08 current ratio) exists because the company sells stock at elevated prices, not because operations generate cash. At ~$38B market cap on ~$600-800M run-rate revenue, RKLB trades at roughly 50-60x trailing sales, a multiple that prices flawless execution of two unproven catalysts: the Neutron medium-lift rocket (delayed to Q4 2026 after a first-stage tank failed qualification testing in January 2026) and the transformation into a profitable operator via Iridium.

We maintain a HOLD. The score trajectory has drifted up from 4.6 (May) to 5.3 (June) as financial health improved on backlog and margin momentum, and we hold it here. The stock has fallen ~55% from its May high of $150 to ~$67, partly on SpaceX-IPO rotation (a sentiment/technical driver, not fundamental deterioration) and partly on justified skepticism about a richly priced, binary-catalyst-dependent name. At current levels the risk/reward is roughly balanced: the operating trajectory is real and the backlog is bankable, but the valuation leaves no margin of safety if Neutron slips again or if Iridium integration proves harder than management frames.

## Company Overview

Rocket Lab operates two reportable segments. **Launch Services** (~33% of 2025 revenue, $199M) is anchored by the Electron small-lift rocket, the world's second-most-launched orbital vehicle in 2025, with 75 successful launches and 200+ spacecraft deployed through year-end 2025 (81 launches by 3/31/2026). Electron carries up to 300 kg to low Earth orbit; a suborbital variant, HASTE, serves the hypersonic-test market. The structural advantage here is range control: Rocket Lab owns LC-1 (Mahia, New Zealand, up to 120 launches/year under a US-NZ bilateral treaty), LC-2 (Wallops, Virginia), and LC-3 under construction for Neutron.

**Space Systems** (~67% of 2025 revenue, $403M) manufactures spacecraft, components (reaction wheels, star trackers, solar cells, separation systems, radios, batteries), optical/EO-IR payloads, and provides on-orbit management. It was built through a serial acquisition strategy: Sinclair Interplanetary (2020), Advanced Solutions and Planetary Systems (2021), SolAero (2022), GEOST (Aug 2025, $290M, optical/missile-tracking payloads), Mynaric (Apr 2026, laser optical comms, first European footprint), and Motiv (agreed May 2026, robotics/solar array drive assemblies).

The strategic pivot underway rests on two bets. First, Neutron — a reusable medium-lift rocket (13,000 kg to LEO) targeted for constellation and government launch, which is where the medium-lift TAM sits. Second, the June 29, 2026 announcement of an ~$8B cash-and-stock acquisition of Iridium Communications, which would move Rocket Lab downstream into recurring satellite services and spectrum ownership. Iridium holders would receive $54/share ($27 cash + Rocket Lab stock); Iridium brings ~$872M revenue, ~$495M EBITDA, 2.55M subscribers, a 66-satellite operational LEO constellation, and valuable L-band spectrum. Management frames it as "significantly accretive to cash flow." The chief competitor across the stack is SpaceX, alongside ULA, Firefly, Blue Origin (launch), and Airbus/Lockheed/L3Harris/Maxar/Redwire (systems/components).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and accelerating.** FY2023 revenue of $244.6M grew to $436.2M in FY2024 (+78%) and $601.8M in FY2025 (+38%). Q1 2026 revenue of $200.3M (+63% YoY) nearly matched a full year of 2022 revenue in a single quarter. Backlog expanded from $1.07B (YE24) to $1.85B (YE25) to $2.22B (Q1 26), driven by the $806M+ SDA Tranche 3 contract (18 tracking satellites, awarded Dec 2025) and GEOST. Approximately 36% of backlog is recognizable within 12 months, which provides real forward visibility.

**Margins are improving but the bottom line remains deeply negative.** Gross margin climbed from 21% (2023) to 26.6% (2024) to 34.4% (2025) to 38.2% in Q1 2026, evidence of operating leverage. Launch economics improved as well: revenue per launch rose to $8.5M (2025) and $9.3M (Q1 26) while cost per launch fell to $4.8M, making Electron contribution-positive at the mission level. Below gross profit, however, R&D jumped 55% to $270.7M in FY2025 (Neutron-driven), producing a -$228.8M operating loss (-38% margin) and a -$198.2M net loss. Q1 2026 operating loss narrowed to -$56.0M (-27.9% margin) from -48.3% a year earlier, though the company is a long way from profitability.

**Cash flow is negative and growth is equity-funded.** FY2025 operating cash flow was -$165.5M, capex $156.3M, and free cash flow -$321.8M (-53.5% FCF margin). The funding mechanism is dilution: $1.12B in ATM proceeds in 2025, $445.6M in Q1 2026 alone, and a new $1.0B equity distribution program (including forward sales) signed in March 2026. This lifted cash to ~$1.48B ($1.2B cash + $271M marketable securities) at 3/31/26, producing the solvency metrics (Altman Z 3.22, current ratio 4.08, D/E 0.35). Those metrics are a product of selling stock at high prices, not internal generation. Shares outstanding rose from ~496M (2024) to ~576M (Apr 2026), and the mechanism only works while the stock stays elevated, a reflexive dynamic that becomes hostile if sentiment turns.

**Deleveraging via conversion.** The $355M 4.25% convertible notes (due 2029, ~$5.13 conversion price) are converting to equity as the stock trades well above the cap. Outstanding principal fell from $155.7M (YE25) to $37.6M (Q1 26). This removes debt but adds shares.

The earnings surprise record is mixed but improving: over the last four quarters RKLB posted two beats, one in-line, and one miss, with the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) beating a -$0.08 estimate at -$0.07. Losses are narrowing on schedule, but consensus does not model GAAP profitability in the near term.

Financial Health scores a 5.5. The liquidity is genuine and the backlog reduces revenue risk, but the negative FCF, structural dilution, and dependence on external capital cap the score below the level that operating momentum alone would suggest. This is consistent with the improvement from 4.5 in prior reports, and we hold it here: the balance sheet is safe but capital-inefficient.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is the clearest positive in the story. Three consecutive years of 38-78% growth, +63% in Q1 2026, and a backlog that grew ~20% year-over-year point to durable demand. The SDA Tranche 3 win validates the defense proliferated-architecture thesis, which sits squarely in a favorable government spending environment (SDA/defense proliferated constellations, NASA), and the Space Systems segment — the larger and faster-growing of the two — is where most of that backlog concentrates.

The estimate revision picture is nuanced and warrants care. Near-term revisions are negative: current-quarter EPS was cut 28.1% over 90 days and current-year estimates fell 10.1%. But the direction reversed over the most recent 30 days across most windows (current-year +5.2%, next-quarter +0.8%, next-year +30.1%), consistent with losses narrowing and margin leverage confirming. This is not the >30% positive 90-day revision velocity that would flag a strong inflection BUY signal — the 90-day trend is still negative — so we do not apply that signal here. The 30-day stabilization is encouraging but insufficient on its own.

The two forward catalysts define the growth ceiling. Neutron unlocks the medium-lift constellation and government launch market, where per-mission economics dwarf Electron. A successful Q4 2026 debut would open a materially larger TAM; another slip pushes revenue recognition right and burns more cash. Iridium, if it closes, transforms the growth profile from cash-consuming manufacturer to operator with ~$495M of EBITDA and 2.55M recurring subscribers, providing the first profitable, self-funding revenue base and reducing the dilution treadmill. Both are real, but both are unproven in the reported numbers.

Growth Prospects score a 7.0. The revenue trajectory, backlog, and margin leverage are strong and broad-based; the primary caveat is that the highest-value growth (Neutron, Iridium services) is not yet in the financials.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$38B market cap on ~$600-800M run-rate revenue, RKLB trades at roughly 50-60x trailing sales with negative earnings and negative free cash flow. Trailing and forward P/E are both meaningless (negative, at -204x on the reported EPS figure). The peer group in Aerospace & Defense trades at a median P/E of 65.9 (ESLT 73.9, AXON 179.4, CW 55.5, ATI 65.9, FTAI 48.1), but those are profitable companies; RKLB has no earnings to anchor a multiple, so the peer comparison is of limited use. The relevant benchmark is other pre-profit space and high-growth names, and even against those the price-to-sales multiple is aggressive.

The valuation framework we apply is cycle-and-catalyst-adjusted rather than trailing, given the earnings inflection underway. But the guardrails cut against a high score here. First, this is not a stock trading below its growth rate on forward earnings — there are no positive forward earnings. Second, analyst targets (mean $114, median $120, high $150, low $77) sit well above the current ~$67, but these targets are catalyst-dependent extrapolations of Neutron and Iridium success, not independent confirmation of value at premium multiples. On a name where the entire valuation rests on future promise, we treat those targets as reflecting the same optimism embedded in the bull case, not as a floor.

The ~55% drawdown from $150 to $67 does not by itself make the stock cheap. Prior appreciation is not a valuation input, and a lower price on intact fundamentals improves risk/reward, but at 50-60x sales with negative FCF, "improved" still leaves the stock expensive relative to what it currently delivers. The market is not obviously wrong at $67; it is pricing a wide distribution of outcomes on two binary catalysts. There is no identifiable mispricing where forward fundamentals clearly exceed the price, and the cheapness that does exist relative to the May peak reflects justified de-risking, not a value opportunity.

Valuation scores a 3.5. This is up modestly from 2.5-3.0 in prior reports (the price has fallen while backlog grew), but the multiple remains stretched against current fundamentals with the justifying growth still unproven. We deliberately resist rewarding the drawdown as "cheapness," because a compressed price on a pre-profit story priced for perfection requires a catalyst before we assign value, and the catalysts here are unresolved.

## Competitive Landscape

Rocket Lab's differentiation is vertical integration across launch, spacecraft buses, components, optical payloads, laser comms (Mynaric), robotics (Motiv), and now potentially spectrum and services (Iridium). Owning its own private launch range under a US-NZ treaty that permits US technology is a genuine structural advantage that competitors cannot easily replicate. Electron's flight heritage — the second-most-launched orbital vehicle in 2025, with a 10th consecutive 100% mission in June 2026 — establishes reliability credibility that matters for government and defense customers.

The competitive trajectory is improving in Space Systems, where segment revenue is growing faster than the launch business and the SDA/GEOST wins signal share gains in defense payloads and tracking. The Iridium deal reframes the competitive story: rather than attempting to out-build Starlink's ~10,000-satellite broadband fleet (a losing proposition), Rocket Lab would buy an existing profitable operator in defensible niches — maritime, aviation, IoT, government, alternative-PNT. Management calls it a "shortcut" to recurring, cash-generative revenue, which is an honest framing of the strategic logic.

The dominant risk is SpaceX, which leads on launch cadence and reusability (Falcon 9) and constellations (Starlink). External reporting (CNBC) ties RKLB's drop partly to SpaceX's IPO — investors who held RKLB as a "pure-play space proxy" rotated into newly available SpaceX shares. That is a technical/sentiment driver rather than fundamental deterioration, and it does not change RKLB's competitive positioning. Neutron will compete directly with Falcon 9 in medium-lift, where SpaceX's cost and cadence advantages are formidable, so the competitive win there is far from assured.

Competitive Position scores a 6.5. The trajectory is improving through vertical integration and defense share gains, and the range-control moat is real, but the SpaceX overhang and the unproven Neutron competitive position keep this below the top tier.

## Risk Assessment

**Neutron execution is the central swing factor.** The Q1 2026 update says "on track for debut later this year" but flags continued risk. Any further slip delays the entire medium-lift revenue thesis and forces additional R&D and capex, extending the cash-burn window and the dilution it requires.

**Reliance on external capital.** With negative FCF and explicit 10-K language that additional financing "may not be available on acceptable terms," the growth model depends on selling stock at elevated prices. This is a reflexive risk: a sustained decline in the share price raises the dilution cost of the same capital, which pressures the equity further.

**Customer and government concentration.** A single government customer was 36% of Q1 2026 revenue; Kratos SRE was 21% of receivables. Government funding priorities, budget cycles, and termination-for-convenience rights create binary contract risk. In the current environment of federal procurement reform and budget-efficiency pressure, defense/space budgets are favored, but concentration remains a specific vulnerability.

**Contract accounting.** Percentage-of-completion revenue recognition means cost overruns trigger cumulative catch-up adjustments (a $7.9M downward adjustment already hit 2025). The MDA Corp. 17-bus contract has delivery delays with two-way liquidated-damages exposure, and a SolAero legacy fixed-price solar contract carries a $4.8M loss provision.

**M&A integration intensity.** Four acquisitions in ~12 months (GEOST, Mynaric, Motiv, Iridium) strain integration capacity, add ~$209M goodwill and intangibles risk, and divert management attention. The Iridium deal is far larger than anything previously attempted, requires significant cash funding plus dilutive stock, and remains subject to regulatory and shareholder approval — it is a definitive agreement, not a closed transaction. Iridium's own Q2 2026 EPS miss ($0.09 vs $0.26 estimate) and Polymarket skepticism about integration temper the bull framing.

**Key-person risk.** Sir Peter Beck (Founder/CEO/Chairman) holds Series A Convertible Participating Preferred Stock (~40.95M shares via The Equatorial Trust) that locks in control. Investors back a founder-controlled company, and his departure would be materially disruptive.

One overhang has cleared: the putative securities class action alleging Neutron misstatements was dismissed with prejudice on April 16, 2026, with related derivative suits stayed and likely to be dropped.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning confirms elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 113.6% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 93.4% (20.2pp spread), at the 80th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 113.6% vs long-term 98.9%), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress ahead of Q2 results. Put/call OI ratio rose to 1.22 (from 1.04) alongside the 37.6% price decline, and near-term put/call skew of 4.5% shows elevated put demand. The flow is not uniformly bearish: unusual activity is dominated by call buying (11,099 contracts at the $70 July 31 strike, 3.3x volume/OI; and 3.9x volume/OI at the $65 July 31 strike), suggesting some positioning for a bounce into earnings. The rich IV means options are expensive as a hedge, and the backwardation argues against fresh long exposure ahead of the print. For a fundamental holder, the signal reinforces waiting for the August earnings resolution rather than adding at current levels.

Risk Factors score a 4.0. The combination of a specific pending binary (Neutron Q4 debut), structural dilution dependence, government concentration, and an unclosed transformative acquisition represents material idiosyncratic risk not fully discounted at a 50-60x sales multiple. The litigation dismissal is a genuine positive that keeps this from scoring lower.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$120-150)

Neutron debuts successfully in Q4 2026, validating the medium-lift thesis and opening a materially larger constellation and government-launch TAM. The Iridium acquisition closes and delivers on management's "significantly accretive to cash flow" claim, converting Rocket Lab into an operator with ~$495M EBITDA, 2.55M recurring subscribers, and self-funding cash generation that ends the dilution treadmill. Space Systems continues to gain defense share (SDA follow-ons, GEOST payloads), backlog compounds past $3B, and gross margin approaches 40%+. In this scenario the vertical-integration thesis is realized, the company approaches sustained profitability, and the stock re-rates toward analyst high targets of $150. Morgan Stanley's diversification bull case captures this outcome.

### Bear Case (~$40-50, ~25-40% downside)

Neutron slips again into 2027 after another test setback, resetting the growth narrative and forcing more R&D and capex-driven cash burn. Iridium integration proves harder than framed — the acquired entity already missed Q2 EPS — and the cash-funding requirement plus dilutive stock issuance pressures the share count. With the stock lower, the ATM funding mechanism becomes more expensive per dollar raised, compounding dilution in a reflexive loop. Government budget renegotiation or a concentrated contract termination hits the 36%-of-revenue government exposure. The stock, priced for perfection at 50-60x sales, de-rates toward the low end of analyst targets ($77) and below as the multiple compresses on a pre-profit name with slipping catalysts. The 52-week low of $37.57 shows how far sentiment can swing.

The base case sits between these: continued strong revenue growth (+40-60%), further margin expansion, backlog compounding, but ongoing losses and dilution as the market waits for Neutron and Iridium to resolve. In that scenario the stock trades in a wide band around current levels, resolving decisively only when the catalysts land.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction at $67, because the two dominant value drivers (Neutron, Iridium) are unresolved binaries.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Neutron completes a successful first orbital launch in Q4 2026 with no further qualification failures — this would remove the single largest execution overhang and validate the medium-lift TAM.
- The Iridium acquisition closes on terms that demonstrably improve consolidated free cash flow, ending or materially reducing the equity-dilution dependence.
- A path to operating breakeven becomes visible in reported results (operating margin improving toward -10% or better) combined with a sustained turn to positive 90-day estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second Neutron test failure or a slip of first launch beyond mid-2027.
- The Iridium deal breaks or requires materially more dilutive financing than framed, or Iridium's standalone results continue to deteriorate.
- Backlog stalls or a major government contract is terminated/de-scoped, or gross margin reverses below the low-30s.
- Evidence that ATM funding is becoming difficult to place (widening issuance discounts, halted programs).

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 results, due ~August 10-11, 2026, which will show margin trajectory, backlog, cash burn, and any Neutron/Iridium updates. The decisive checkpoint is the Neutron Q4 2026 debut. We would revisit the rating immediately on either event.

## Conclusion

Rocket Lab is a genuinely improving operating business — accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a $2.22B backlog, and a differentiated vertical-integration strategy — trapped inside a valuation that requires flawless execution of two unproven catalysts. The Neutron debut and the ~$8B Iridium acquisition will determine whether the ~$38B market cap is justified or excessive. Until they resolve, the stock is a reflexive, sentiment-driven instrument funded by dilution that only works while the price stays elevated.

The ~55% drawdown improves the entry versus the peak but does not make the stock cheap at 50-60x sales with negative free cash flow, and the ongoing near-term negative estimate revisions (despite a 30-day stabilization) argue against treating the current level as a clear inflection buy. The insider signal is mixed: Beck's voluntary cancellation of unvested RSUs and his retained ~40.9M preferred shares argue long-term alignment, while the ~$290M+ CEO sale and broad C-suite selling near the $150 peak (largely programmatic 10b5-1, but large in dollar terms) is a caution flag on a stock priced for promise.

We maintain a HOLD. The operating trajectory earns the improving score, but the balance of a stretched valuation, binary catalyst dependence, structural dilution, and unresolved transformation keeps the risk/reward roughly symmetric at $67. This is a name to hold with defined triggers and reassess at the August print and the Q4 Neutron debut, not to add to ahead of that resolution.
