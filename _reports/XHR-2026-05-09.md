## Executive Summary

Xenia Hotels & Resorts is a luxury/upper upscale hotel REIT that has delivered strong recent operational results. Q1 2026 RevPAR growth of 9.1% outperformed the industry's 3.8%, driven largely by the Grand Hyatt Scottsdale renovation ramp-up. Management has executed an aggressive and well-timed share repurchase program, reducing shares outstanding by approximately 19% since 2022 at an average price of ~$12.80, creating substantial value for remaining shareholders now that shares trade at $17.22.

The fundamental challenge is that the stock has already moved to reflect much of this operational improvement, up 48% from its 52-week low and now trading within 1% of its 52-week high at $17.23. With the analyst consensus mean at $17.20 and the current price at $17.03, the market is pricing in a fairly optimistic scenario. While the $19 bull case target represents 12% upside, achieving that would require continued above-trend RevPAR growth in an environment where consumer confidence and inflationary pressures pose headwinds, and the travel industry faces risks from tariff-driven cost pressures and potential recession.

The company's $1.38 billion debt load (5.53% weighted average rate, 26% variable) and cyclical exposure to luxury travel spending make this a name where timing matters. Holding is appropriate given the positive fundamental trajectory and reasonable dividend yield (3.3%), but initiating new positions at these levels offers insufficient margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Xenia Hotels & Resorts is a self-advised, self-administered REIT headquartered in Orlando, FL, focused on luxury and upper upscale hotels across the top 25 U.S. lodging markets and key leisure destinations. The portfolio consists of 30 hotels with 8,868 rooms across 14 states, operated by third-party managers under brands including Marriott, Hyatt, Kimpton, Fairmont, Loews, Hilton, and Davidson. The company employs only 42 corporate staff, with all hotel-level employees managed by the operating partners.

The operating model is pure real estate ownership — Xenia leases properties to its taxable REIT subsidiary, which engages management companies. Revenue is generated entirely from room revenue, food & beverage, and ancillary hotel income. The focus on upper-tier properties means higher ADR ($288.62 in Q1 2026) and greater sensitivity to luxury consumer demand trends.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue has grown from $998M (2022) to $1,079M (2025), reflecting 2-4% annual growth. Q1 2026 revenue was $295.4M. The growth rate is modest but reflects a portfolio that has been pruned (Fairmont Dallas sold, Lorien Hotel sold) while same-store performance improves.

**Profitability metrics:** Adjusted EBITDAre reached $258.3M in 2025 (+8.9% YoY) and $81.4M in Q1 2026 (+11.6% YoY). Hotel EBITDA margins expanded to 29.7% in Q1 2026 from 27.4% a year earlier, a 230bp improvement indicating pricing power and cost discipline. Adjusted FFO was $174.7M / ~$1.76 per share in 2025.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $176.5M (2025) with $86.6M capex produces approximately $89.9M in free cash flow (8.3% FCF margin). Capital intensity declined from $140.6M in 2024 as the Grand Hyatt Scottsdale renovation completed.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $1.38B against a market cap of $1.59B implies net debt/EBITDAre of approximately 5.0x, elevated but within industry norms for hotel REITs. Liquidity is adequate at $679M (cash + undrawn revolver). The weighted-average debt maturity of 3.2 years is short, with meaningful refinancing needs starting in 2027 ($103M mortgage) through 2030. At current rates (5.53% weighted average), the refinancing environment could be benign or painful depending on the rate path by 2028-2029.

**Earnings surprise record:** Three beats in four quarters, including massive upside surprises in Q2 2025 (+81.8%) and Q4 2025 (+131%). Q1 2026 beat by 15.5%. This pattern suggests either conservative guidance or genuine operational acceleration — likely both.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts:** Management raised 2026 guidance to target 2.75%-5.25% RevPAR growth and ~7% Adjusted FFO per share growth. The Grand Hyatt Scottsdale renovation ramp continues to provide above-portfolio-average growth as the property returns to stabilized occupancy. Q1's 9.1% RevPAR growth substantially exceeded the guidance range, suggesting further upward revisions are possible if the macro environment cooperates.

**Growth constraints:** Revenue growth is structurally limited by the absence of acquisitions since 2022. The portfolio has shrunk from 34 to 30 hotels since 2022. Management appears to be choosing buybacks and debt reduction over external growth — rational at sub-NAV prices but limiting the top-line growth story. With $97.5M in buyback authorization remaining but no repurchases in Q1 2026 (likely because the stock is no longer cheap enough), the primary growth driver going forward must be organic RevPAR and potential acquisitions.

**Industry context:** U.S. lodging demand increased 2.0% in Q1 2026 vs. supply growth of 0.6%, a favorable setup. However, the demand mix is normalizing — the post-COVID leisure surge is fading while business travel and group bookings recover. XHR's positioning in upper upscale (heavy convention/resort properties) benefits from group recovery but faces headwinds if consumer spending weakens.

**Macro headwinds:** Management has explicitly noted monitoring "consumer confidence, inflationary pressures, trade disputes, potential recession." The luxury/upper upscale lodging segment faces meaningful demand risk if these headwinds intensify in H2 2026. Hotel REITs are among the most cyclically sensitive real estate subsectors.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.03, XHR trades at:
- **P/E:** 24.1x trailing (based on $0.64 GAAP EPS — heavily distorted by depreciation)
- **Price/FFO:** ~9.7x on 2025 Adjusted FFO ($1.76/share); approximately 9.0-9.5x on 2026E FFO assuming 7% growth (~$1.88)
- **EV/EBITDAre:** ~11.0x on 2025 ($258M); ~10.5x on 2026 midpoint ($266M)
- **Dividend yield:** 3.3%

**Peer comparison:** Among hotel REITs, XHR trades at a premium to most peers — PEB (negative earnings), PK (negative earnings), INN (negative earnings) all show the industry's cyclical challenges. DRH at 22.6x P/E and SHO at 94x P/E frame XHR's 24x as roughly mid-pack. On EV/EBITDA, XHR's ~10.5-11x is reasonable for the quality tier.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean of $17.20 and median of $17.00 — essentially where the stock trades today. The high target of $19.00 implies 12% upside; the low of $16.00 implies 6% downside. This tight range with consensus at current price suggests the stock is fairly valued by the Street's assessment.

**NAV considerations:** Aggressive share buybacks at ~$12.80 suggest management believed intrinsic value was materially above that level. At $17.22, the stock is 35% above their buyback price, and the pause in Q1 2026 buybacks may signal management views shares as less attractively priced now.

## Competitive Landscape

XHR competes for acquisitions and capital with Apple Hospitality, Pebblebrook, Park Hotels, RLJ Lodging, Sunstone, and private equity. The company's differentiation lies in its focus on luxury/upper upscale properties below replacement cost with operational upside — a strategy that has produced good results (Grand Hyatt Scottsdale) but limits deal flow.

The portfolio's heavy Marriott/Hyatt brand concentration (23 of 30 hotels) provides access to powerful loyalty programs and reservation systems but creates dependency risk. The recent operator transition at four hotels (with associated $3.6M non-recurring costs) suggests active portfolio management — possibly moving properties to Davidson from underperforming operators.

XHR's cumulative TSR of 104.56 vs. peer group TSR of 124.28 shows meaningful underperformance over time. The 48% rally from the 52-week low has closed some of this gap, but the stock has historically traded at a slight discount to peers, possibly reflecting the smaller portfolio, higher leverage, or lack of external growth.

Alternative accommodations (Airbnb/VRBO) remain a structural competitive threat, though the luxury/upper upscale segment is somewhat insulated given the service component, brand loyalty, and group/convention demand that cannot easily migrate to home-sharing platforms.

## Risk Assessment

**Macro/cyclical risk:** This is the dominant concern. Luxury hotel demand is highly sensitive to economic conditions. Management has explicitly flagged potential recession risk.

**Interest rate risk:** $378M in variable-rate debt means each 100bp rate increase costs ~$3.3M annually. The debt stack maturing over 2027-2030 faces refinancing into a potentially uncertain rate environment.

**Operator transition risk:** Four hotels (13% of portfolio) undergoing simultaneous operator changes creates short-term operational disruption risk — booking gaps, staff turnover, system changeovers.

**Debt covenant risk:** The 2025 covenant violation (cured) flags at least one underperforming asset. Another property-level stress event could require additional cash reserves.

**Geographic concentration:** 22% California, 18% Texas, 13% Florida — exposed to wildfire, hurricane, and regulatory risk.

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 RevPAR growth decelerates below 2% (below low end of guidance range)
2. Management cuts dividend or breaches additional debt covenants

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026)

## Conclusion

Xenia Hotels & Resorts has executed well operationally — the margin expansion, share count reduction, and consistent earnings beats demonstrate competent management creating value from a focused luxury hotel portfolio. Raised guidance suggests continued near-term momentum.

However, at $17.03 the stock has largely priced in this operational improvement. Trading near its 52-week high, at consensus analyst targets, and in an environment where luxury consumer demand faces genuine macro headwinds, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The 3.3% dividend yield provides some downside cushion but is insufficient to compensate for the cyclical risk embedded in the business model.

HOLD with a growth-hold orientation — the positive trajectory warrants continued ownership but not incremental capital deployment at these levels. A pullback toward $14.50-$15.00 driven by macro fears (with operational metrics intact) would create a compelling entry point.
