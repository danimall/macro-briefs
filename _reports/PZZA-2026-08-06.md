# Investment Research Report: Papa Johns International, Inc. (PZZA)

**Price basis: $29.75 (NASDAQ official close, authoritative for all arithmetic in this report). A pre-market print of $27.39 on August 6, 2026 (−7.9% vs. close) followed the Q2 FY2026 10-Q and is treated as diagnostic only.**

**Recommendation: AVOID | Overall Score: 3.5/10 | Prior: 3.9 (AVOID, June 19, 2026)**

---

## Executive Summary

Papa Johns' Q2 FY2026 results (10-Q filed August 6, 2026) resolved the central open question from our prior work in the wrong direction. North American demand deteriorated further. Domestic company-owned comparable sales fell 8.9% and North America franchised comps fell 8.2%, both worse than the already-negative 7.4% first-half figures, and management attributes the decline to lower transaction volumes rather than price or mix. Alongside the print, the Board suspended the quarterly dividend beginning with the Q3 2026 payment and cut the FY2026 outlook. The shares are 18% lower than at our last review, but comps, unit count, estimates, litigation exposure and management continuity all moved against the equity in the interim.

Q2 Adjusted EBITDA was flat year-over-year at $52.7M against $52.6M, adjusted diluted EPS rose from $0.41 to $0.46 (a 1.5% beat versus the $0.45 consensus), and first-half domestic four-wall EBITDA margin held at 11.5% despite a 7.4% comp decline. Management raised its G&A savings target from at least $25M to at least $30M and has captured roughly $16M of a $60M systemwide supply-chain program. Covenant leverage sits at 3.3x against a 5.25x maximum, interest coverage at 3.3x against a 2.00x minimum, revolver availability is $468.7M, and the nearest maturity is the $400M 3.875% notes in September 2029. This company is not near distress, and the dividend suspension — which conserves roughly $61M annually, about 6% of the equity value — is the correct decision on the disclosed cash math.

The problem is arithmetic, not narrative. Roughly half the Q2 G&A relief came from a $5.4M cut in supplemental advertising and a $5.6M reduction in management compensation. Cutting marketing into a traffic-driven decline risks deepening it, and lower incentive accruals are a consequence of missing targets rather than a savings program. Meanwhile the profit pools that matter are contracting: North America franchising generated $105.4M of FY2025 segment Adjusted EBITDA on $191.0M of royalty revenue (~55% flow-through), while commissary produced $78.8M on $929.9M of revenue (~8.5%). Franchised closures ran 31 in FY2024, 86 in FY2025, and 93 in the first half of FY2026 alone, with trailing-four-quarter net unit growth at −11 versus +106 a year earlier. Every closed franchised unit permanently removes royalty dollars worth roughly 6.5x commissary dollars at the margin. At $29.75 the market is pricing continued deterioration but not collapse, which is approximately the right answer and leaves no asymmetry for new capital.

## Company Overview

Papa Johns operates and franchises pizza delivery and carryout restaurants under the Papa John's trademark, with dine-in in some international markets. At June 28, 2026 the system comprised 5,978 units across 51 countries — 469 company-owned (456 North America, 13 international) and 5,509 franchised, so approximately 92% franchised by unit. Unit count peaked at 6,083 at FY2025 year-end and has since contracted.

Five revenue lines produced FY2025 revenue of $2,053.8M, down 0.3% from $2,059.4M: company-owned restaurant sales $675.7M (−6.8%), franchise royalties and fees $191.0M (+2.1%), commissary revenues $929.9M (+3.4%), other revenues $90.5M (+8.2%), and advertising funds revenue $166.7M (+1.5%). Two structural features distort the consolidated top line. First, advertising funds are a consolidated variable interest entity designed to run at break-even — FY2025 revenue of $166.7M against $167.6M of expense — so roughly $167M of annual revenue and expense carries essentially zero economic margin. Adjusting for the gross-up leaves approximately $1,887M of operating revenue. Second, commissary is the largest reported line at ~45% of revenue but is a low-margin distribution business; domestic franchisees are contractually required to purchase only pizza sauce, dough and designated proprietary items from the Quality Control Centers, with everything else voluntary.

The profit pool therefore sits almost entirely in franchising. FY2025 segment Adjusted EBITDA: North America franchising $105.4M, North America commissaries $78.8M, domestic company-owned restaurants $28.4M, International $22.6M, totaling $235.2M against consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $201.1M (the difference being unallocated corporate). This is why a −8.2% franchised comp with accelerating closures is materially more damaging than the headline revenue trend implies.

Strategy is a "barbell" — premium positioning on the six-ingredient, never-frozen-dough platform combined with value entry points (Papa Pairings), plus 2026 menu extensions in pan pizzas and oven-toasted sandwiches. Management concedes in Item 1A that premium inputs mean "we may use ingredients that cost more than the ingredients some of our competitors may use," creating a structural margin disadvantage if the quality claim does not resonate.

## Financial Analysis

**Comparable sales trajectory.** FY2024 total system comps were −3.1%, improving to −0.6% in FY2025 as International inflected to +5.0%. That stabilization reversed decisively: North America total comps went from −2.5% in FY2025 to −7.4% in the FY2026 first half and −8.3% in Q2. The Q2 FY2025 comparison base was itself positive (+0.3% domestic company-owned), so part of the decline reflects a harder comparison; a two-year stack of roughly −8.6% (1.003 × 0.911 − 1, our calculation — the company does not present stacked comps) nonetheless indicates absolute traffic erosion. International remains positive for a seventh consecutive quarter but is decelerating: +5.0% FY2025, +2.5% first half, +1.5% in Q2, on a base of only ~9.2% of consolidated revenue.

**Revenue.** Q2 FY2026 revenue was $482.4M against $529.2M (−8.8%); first-half revenue $961.0M against $1,047.5M (−8.3%). Management attributes roughly $50M of the first-half decline to the November 24, 2025 refranchising of 85 domestic restaurants, which reduces the organic decline to approximately −3.5%. Refranchising converts high-revenue, low-margin company sales into low-revenue, high-margin royalty and commissary streams, so revenue decline overstates the economic hit, but the organic comp decline is doing genuine damage on top of it.

**Earnings and margins.** FY2025 operating income fell $67.6M to $89.1M (4.3% margin from 7.6%), GAAP diluted EPS fell 65% to $0.90, adjusted diluted EPS fell to $1.43 from $2.34, and Adjusted EBITDA fell to $201.1M from $227.3M. The decline decomposes usefully: a $24.2M year-over-year swing in asset-sale gains embedded in G&A ($17.1M FY2025 refranchising gain versus a $41.3M FY2024 sale-leaseback gain) plus $18.4M of accelerated depreciation on retired technology assets together account for $42.6M, or roughly 63%. The genuinely operational degradation was approximately $25.0M, concentrated in domestic four-wall economics and a $21.2M increase in marketing spend.

**Q2/H1 FY2026.** Operating income of $23.2M versus $24.5M in Q2; net income $8.5M versus $9.5M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.24 versus $0.28. The mechanism behind the flat Adjusted EBITDA was a $11.2M (−15.9%) reduction in G&A, with adjusted unallocated corporate expense falling from $18.6M to $10.3M. The effective tax rate rose approximately 590bp to 36.4%, and "All Other" adjusted EBITDA fell from $9.2M to $2.9M as franchisee technology fees were reduced beginning in the second half of 2025 — a $6.3M headwind absorbed within the flat result.

The cost work is real. First-half four-wall EBITDA margin holding at 11.5% against a −7.4% comp, achieved through labor productivity, lower local advertising and commodity deflation, is a legitimate operating accomplishment. But the absolute dollar base is shrinking: four-wall EBITDA fell from $39.8M to $32.2M in the first half and from $21.9M to $15.6M in Q2.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $126.0M and company-defined free cash flow $61.3M, but management attributed the improvement to working-capital and advertising-fund timing and lower cash taxes rather than earnings quality. First-half FY2026 operating cash flow fell 46% to $35.8M and free cash flow to $9.5M from $36.5M, on lower net income, an advertising-fund timing reversal, and $8.3M of restructuring severance payments. We deliberately do not annualize $9.5M — first-half flows were front-loaded in FY2025 (H2 contributed $24.8M of the $61.3M). As a labeled sensitivity: if H2 FY2026 repeats last year's $24.8M, full-year FCF lands near $34M. A normalized alternative — FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $201.1M less approximately $40M cash interest, $75M capex midpoint and cash taxes — implies roughly $75–85M of pre-restructuring discretionary cash, against which $24–31M of transformation cash costs and the POS deployment compete.

**Balance sheet.** At June 28, 2026: cash $28.5M, total assets $805.0M, total liabilities $1,246.2M, stockholders' deficit $(442.3)M, outstanding debt $733.5M, current ratio 0.83. The deficit is a buyback artifact rather than accumulated losses — treasury stock of $(1,098.8)M against $455.7M of paid-in capital and positive retained earnings of $195.3M. This structure is common among heavily refranchised, buyback-heavy franchisors and does not indicate insolvency. It does mean there is no loss-absorbing equity cushion, and it renders equity-ratio-based distress screens uninterpretable for this name.

Debt comprises $400M 3.875% notes due September 15, 2029, a $200M term loan (amortization commenced June 30, 2026), and $133.5M of revolver draws, with facilities maturing March 26, 2030. Covenant leverage of 3.3x against a 5.25x maximum implies, on constant debt, that covenant EBITDA would need to fall from an implied ~$222M to ~$140M to breach — roughly 37% of cushion. Covenant "Consolidated EBITDA" is a defined term with add-backs differing from reported Adjusted EBITDA, so treat this as an approximation.

**Capital allocation.** Dividends were $61.1M in FY2025 against $61.3M of free cash flow — a 99.7% payout of FCF, 129% of adjusted EPS and 204% of GAAP EPS. In the first half of FY2026, $30.9M of dividends against $9.5M of FCF was funded with net revolver borrowings of $11.3M. Buybacks have been zero since FY2023 with $90.2M authorized. The suspension beginning Q3 2026 removes a leverage-funded distribution and is credit-positive; management frames it as accelerating transformation investment, which is consistent with the $125–140M POS commitment and restructuring cash needs. Both readings — coverage failure and reinvestment priority — fit the disclosed facts, and the covenant position does not suggest lender compulsion.

Note that the reported 5.99% dividend yield in market data is now stale.

## Growth Analysis

There is no credible 12-month growth thesis. Revenue is declining, comps are deteriorating sequentially, and the unit base is contracting with management explicitly guiding to "elevated levels of restaurant closures in North America during 2026 and 2027." First-half actions included 101 North American closures (8 company-owned), 17 additional approved company-owned closures, and a ~7% corporate workforce reduction.

Consensus estimates confirm the direction. Current-year EPS has been revised from $1.51 to $1.46 over 90 days (−3.0%) and next year from $1.80 to $1.72 (−4.3%), with next-quarter estimates down 3.1%. Fourteen analysts contribute; the trend is broadly negative and predates the August 6 outlook cut, whose specific revised ranges are not verifiable from the filings. The earnings record is three misses in four quarters (−22.6%, −2.4%, −11.6%) before the +1.5% Q2 beat, which does not support high confidence in forward consensus.

Growth optionality reduces to three items, all constrained. International has inflected and stayed positive for seven quarters, with first-half segment Adjusted EBITDA up 41% to $15.5M and 61 gross openings — but 82 international franchised closures in the same period produced net −21 units, and the segment is ~9% of revenue. Supply-chain savings of "at least $60 million" are explicitly *systemwide*, accruing substantially to franchisee four-wall margins rather than to Papa Johns' P&L; adding that figure to consolidated EBITDA would double-count a franchisee benefit. The G&A program, raised to at least $30M across FY2026–27, is the one item that lands directly in company earnings, and much of the near-term realization is annualization of the headcount cut.

Set against this, the franchisee attrition channel is the binding constraint on any recovery. Franchisee notes receivable were reserved at 73% at FY2025 year-end (from 52% a year earlier), the accounts receivable allowance rose from $12.1M to $14.3M in the first half on a $3.7M provision against only $1.5M of write-offs, and the company is extending royalty waivers, marketing-fund rebates, food-cost subsidies and image-improvement incentives. The bull reading — deliberate pruning of unprofitable units leaving a healthier system — is genuinely possible, and the filings do not disclose closed-unit average volumes, so the closure data cannot by itself distinguish deliberate pruning from involuntary failure. On current evidence the pace (93 franchised closures in six months versus 86 in the prior full year) is running ahead of what a controlled program would suggest.

Growth Prospects: **2.5**, down from 3.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.75 with 32.902M shares outstanding, market capitalization is $979M. Adding $733.5M of debt and deducting $28.5M of cash gives an enterprise value of approximately $1,684M.

| Metric | Calculation | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| EV / FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA | $1,684M ÷ $201.1M | 8.4x |
| EV / H1 FY2026 annualized Adj. EBITDA | $1,684M ÷ $201.0M | 8.4x |
| P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS $0.90 | $29.75 ÷ $0.90 | 33.1x |
| P/E on FY2025 adjusted EPS $1.43 | $29.75 ÷ $1.43 | 20.8x |
| P/E on current-year consensus $1.46 | $29.75 ÷ $1.46 | 20.4x |
| P/E on next-year consensus $1.72 | $29.75 ÷ $1.72 | 17.3x |

Trailing GAAP EPS is depressed by accelerated depreciation and restructuring, so the adjusted and forward figures are the relevant anchors. Neither is cheap in absolute terms for a business with declining revenue, a contracting unit base and 3.65x gross leverage on negative book equity. And the forward multiples are optically flattered: current-year consensus of $1.46 was set before the August 6 outlook cut and has already been revised down 3.0% over 90 days, so 20.4x is a ceiling on the true forward multiple rather than a fair reading.

The peer set offers limited help. Restaurant comparables of similar size show a median P/E of 33.0x, but the constituents are individually distressed: Bloomin' Brands 33.0x, Cracker Barrel 39.5x, BJ's 26.0x, Dave & Buster's negative, Wendy's 8.7x on an 8.4% yield and 46% below its 52-week high. The median is computed on depressed earnings across companies with their own structural problems, so a "discount to peers" argument here has no analytical content. Wendy's is arguably the more instructive comparable — another mature, franchised QSR losing share — and it trades at less than half PZZA's forward multiple while still paying a dividend.

We do not treat the 47% decline from the $55.74 October 2025 high as a valuation input in either direction. What matters is that the equity now offers no identifiable mispricing. The dividend is gone, so absolute yield support has been removed. There is no earnings inflection: estimate revisions are negative in every window, and the Q2 adjusted EPS increase was produced by cutting advertising and incentive accruals rather than by operating recovery. There is no visible catalyst that re-rates the multiple within 12 months other than a comp inflection that the last four quarters argue against.

Analyst targets — low $30.00, mean $36.80, median $34.50, high $45.00 — all sit at or above the $29.75 basis. These are stale relative to the August 6 dividend suspension and guidance cut and will likely be reduced; the recommendation distribution has already shifted, with one analyst moving to strong sell in the last month against 4 buy and 9 hold. Targets above spot on a company that just cut guidance and eliminated its dividend are not independent confirmation of value.

Our base case values the equity at roughly $26 (8.5x an FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$185M, less ~$705M net debt, over 32.9M shares), approximately 13% below the current basis. Cheapness on optically low multiples against a shrinking earnings base is the signature of a value trap, and we score accordingly.

Valuation: **4.2**, down from 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Papa Johns is losing share, and management says so. Item 1A states that "certain of our larger competitors have also recently employed price competition and promotional strategies in order to win market share," in response to which the company "previously reduced the prices for some of our products and implemented more value and promotional pricing… which may adversely affect our profitability." The proxy's pay-versus-performance table supplies the cleanest external benchmark available: five-year TSR of 51.02 against a peer-group TSR of 94.71 — a 44-point relative shortfall with shareholders down roughly half while the peer set was approximately flat.

The strategic bind is structural. Premium inputs raise cost of goods — domestic company-owned product costs were 30.7% of segment revenue in FY2025 and 31.6% in Q2 FY2026 — in a category where competitors are competing on price. The company's own risk language concedes it "could have lower profit margins or lower sales than some of our competitors if we are not able to establish a quality differentiator that resonates with consumers." The barbell strategy is the attempted resolution; the −8.9% Q2 comp is evidence it is not yet working.

Third-party aggregators compress the model from a second direction. Papa Johns pays fees to these platforms to receive order flow; the platforms simultaneously compete for the end-customer relationship and for the delivery-driver labor pool, control promotional placement, and can raise fees or preferentially promote competitors. Aggregator fees sit inside "COS – Other" for domestic company-owned restaurants, and Item 1A warns that "an increased percentage of orders delivered through third-party aggregator services could reduce the profitability of these orders." The Q2 10-Q describes management "refining our aggregator channel strategy" through more targeted promotional mix — an adjustment to the company's own paid participation, not a change in platform economics it can control.

Supply concentration adds fragility: a sole supplier for all domestic mozzarella and substantially all international cheese (the largest food cost), a sole source for garlic sauce, and California tomato-crop exposure for pizza sauce. Two Quality Control Centers were tornado-damaged during FY2025.

The one area of demonstrated competitive improvement is International, where the completed transformation plan produced seven consecutive positive comp quarters and 41% first-half segment EBITDA growth. This proves the playbook can work when applied — but at ~9% of revenue, with net units declining 21 in the first half, and with disclosed Middle East franchisee boycotts and suspended Russia support, it cannot offset North America. The rising International share of revenue (8.0% to 9.2%) is largely a denominator effect and should not be read as diversification; the operative concentration is the North American franchised system, and that measure is worsening.

Competitive Position: **3.0**.

## Risk Assessment

**Rivera v. JNE — the largest unquantified exposure.** Newly disclosed in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q and absent from the FY2025 10-K legal proceedings discussion: a personal-injury action arising from a 2021 automobile accident involving a delivery driver employed by a Florida franchisee, in which Papa John's Franchising, LLC was named under a vicarious-liability theory. In May 2026 a jury returned an adverse verdict on liability. No damages have been awarded; a separate damages trial is expected in 2027. The company states it believes there are substantial grounds to challenge the verdict and is "unable to reasonably estimate the possible loss or range of loss." We assume no dollar figure. The significance is twofold: liability is established with only quantum outstanding, and the theory — franchisor liability for a franchisee's employee's driving — carries precedent risk across a roughly 2,983-unit North American franchised delivery system. Insurance is self-funded to retentions "ranging up to $0.8 million" with higher retentions for certain coverages; FY2025 insurance reserves were $61.8M, primarily auto liability and workers' compensation. With no book-equity cushion, an adverse damages outcome lands directly on cash flow and the revolver.

**Franchisee credit transmission.** The 73% reserve rate on a $24.4M franchisee notes book is immaterial to earnings in dollars but informative as management's own forward judgment on franchisee collateral and cash flows. The material channel is unit attrition: 93 franchised closures in six months against a royalty stream that converts to segment EBITDA at ~55%.

**Technology commitment.** A multi-year POS and restaurant-operations software agreement signed in Q1 FY2026 runs through December 23, 2034 with a total estimated contractual commitment of $125–140M and deployment minimums by November 30, 2026 and November 30, 2027. Management expects the amounts to be "largely recovered through technology fees charged to our franchisees" — a forward-looking expectation, not a contracted recovery, and one that sits awkwardly against the reduction in franchisee technology fees implemented in the second half of 2025 that is currently depressing Other revenues. The deployment minimums obligate the company to migrate franchisees on a schedule that requires cooperation it cannot compel. Penalty terms for missed minimums are not disclosed.

**UK lease mismatch.** The UK subsidiary holds master leases for nearly all UK locations, subleasing approximately 325 restaurants to franchisees with head-lease terms generally 15 years against sublease terms of 5–10 years. Papa Johns remains the lease obligor with a shorter-dated receivable. Approximately 450 units are in the UK. Separately, $10.6M of undiscounted maximum contingent lease-guarantee exposure on ~75 domestic leases plus $17.1M of surety bonds sit off balance sheet.

**Governance.** Two items surfaced in the Q2 filing. First, under DGCL Section 204 the Board on July 31, 2026 ratified CEO equity awards covering a maximum aggregate 232,254 shares granted March 3, 2025 and March 2, 2026 "in excess of the individual sublimit" under the 2018 Omnibus Incentive Plan, together with a retroactive plan amendment effective January 1, 2025. Section 204 is a legitimate Delaware cure and the company describes the grants as inadvertent; the maximum contingent share count equals ~0.71% of the 32.902M shares outstanding, which if fully issued would leave existing holders at ~99.3% of prior ownership — immaterial as dilution. The finding is a control deficiency in equity administration, cured by board action rather than a fresh shareholder vote, occurring in the same period CEO total compensation reached a five-year high of $8.44M against a five-year-low Adjusted EBITDA and TSR of 51 versus peers at 95. Second, a $1.0M overstatement of Q1 FY2026 domestic company-owned sales was disclosed and corrected in the six-month figures — immaterial in dollars, a second data-integrity item in one filing.

**CFO transition.** An 8-K and Business Wire release dated June 30, 2026 announced a CFO transition; the 10-Q filed August 6, 2026 is signed by Chris Collins as Principal Financial and Accounting Officer, and Ravi Thanawala (previously CFO & President, North America) is absent from the signature block. Whether Collins is interim or permanent, and the reason for Thanawala's exit, are not verifiable — the 8-K body and full press release were not retrievable. What is confirmed is a senior-finance leadership change mid-turnaround, with the departing executive having held accountability for the North American segment that just posted −8.2%/−8.9% comps.

**Macro.** The consumer discretionary restaurant category is under pressure, and Papa Johns is a share loser within it; management itself cites a "soft consumer environment" (WSJ, August 6, 2026) as the backdrop for the outlook cut.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 23.6% of float with 6.5 days to cover and 5.74M shares short. That is a two-sided risk: it confirms the bearish consensus and simultaneously creates squeeze potential on any comp stabilization, strategic action or take-private interest at a $979M market capitalization.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 99.0% against 50.5% one-year realized volatility (91st percentile within the chain) with a pronounced backwardation to 60.6% at six months indicates the market priced a large, imminent, event-driven move — consistent with the earnings release and the ±16.4% implied move on the August 21 expiry. The pre-market decline is the beginning of that resolution. Put/call open interest rose sharply from 0.20 to 1.71 since the prior snapshot, indicating that downside structure has been established alongside the short base. The reported skew is call-heavy at −3.9% across tenors, which in a heavily shorted name is consistent with squeeze protection being bid concurrently with put hedging. The prior snapshot's 636% ATM IV reading is not credible and we disregard it. Near-term volatility is rich, six-month volatility at 60.6% versus 50.5% realized is only modestly rich, and the structure supports our conclusion that this is a name to avoid owning rather than one to short into elevated premium.

Risk Factors: **3.2**.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($40–42, +34% to +41%)

Cost execution proves durable and North American transactions inflect. The evidence supporting this path is not trivial: flat Q2 Adjusted EBITDA against the comp decline, four-wall margin holding, the raised G&A target, captured supply-chain benefits against a $25M end-2026 milestone, and the cash freed by the dividend suspension. Covenant headroom of ~37% on EBITDA and no maturity before September 2029 buy real time. International proves the transformation playbook works when applied, and Phase 2 closures leave a smaller system with better unit economics generating more royalty dollars per surviving unit. If comps trough in Q3 FY2026 and recover toward flat during FY2027, Adjusted EBITDA returns to ~$215M and the multiple re-rates to 9.5x, implying roughly $41 per share; with 23.6% short interest, the move would likely overshoot. A take-private or franchisee-consortium bid at a $979M equity value is a live secondary possibility.

### Bear Case ($17–19, −36% to −43%)

Marketing cuts deepen the traffic decline, franchisee attrition accelerates beyond deliberate pruning, and the royalty and commissary bases shrink structurally. Comps remain at or below −8% through FY2027; closures exceed the 2026–27 "elevated levels" management has flagged; Adjusted EBITDA falls toward $165M; the multiple compresses to 7.5x as covenant cushion narrows and the POS commitment consumes discretionary cash. An adverse Rivera damages award in 2027, landing against negative book equity with self-funded retentions, would compound. That path implies roughly $17–19 per share.

### Base Case ($26, −13%)

Comps trough near −6% to −8% in the second half of FY2026 and improve slowly but do not reach flat within 12 months. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of approximately $185M at 8.5x EV/EBITDA, less ~$705M net debt over 32.9M shares, gives roughly $26. The dividend suspension and cost program prevent balance-sheet deterioration; the absence of a demand inflection prevents re-rating. Modest downside from $29.75 with a wide distribution around it.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The next dated event capable of moving the stock materially is the Q3 FY2026 report in early November 2026, which tests whether North American transactions stabilized after the Q2 print and the marketing reduction. Secondary: the November 30, 2026 POS deployment minimum, whose consequences for non-compliance are not disclosed. The Rivera damages trial is expected in 2027 — too distant to underwrite a short. There is no near-dated, high-conviction trigger.

**Timing window.** Roughly three months to the Q3 print. The August 6 event has already occurred and is being priced in the pre-market decline, so the immediate catalyst is spent.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 23.6% of float short, 6.5 days to cover, 5.74M shares. This is a crowded short in a $979M-capitalization company already 47% below its 52-week high and trading near its 52-week low. Any comp stabilization, refranchising announcement, activist stake or strategic-interest headline forces covering into limited liquidity (average volume 1.25M shares).

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV of 99.0% against 50.5% realized means put protection is expensive; the term structure's steep backwardation makes short-dated optionality a poor expression. Put/call open interest has already moved to 1.71, so the hedging trade is established rather than available. The call-heavy skew across all three tenors indicates upside optionality is bid — a warning for shorts. Cost of carry is now marginally lower with the dividend suspended, which is the only mechanical improvement for the short case.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $17–19 against the $29.75 basis is roughly 40% of downside; bull case $40–42 is 34–41% of upside, with squeeze overshoot risk beyond that. Base case is −13%. Expressed as reward-to-risk against the plausible upside, this is approximately 1:1 — well below the 2:1 threshold that would justify carrying a short with elevated premium, high borrow crowding and no dated catalyst inside three months.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis is largely correct and largely known. With 23.6% short interest, the stock near its 52-week low, an already-consumed near-term catalyst, and a plausible +34% squeeze path on any comp stabilization, the remaining downside does not compensate for the mechanics. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit on any of the following, verified in filings rather than press coverage:

1. **Two consecutive quarters of improving North American comparable sales** — specifically domestic company-owned comps improving sequentially from −8.9% to better than −5% and then better than −3%, driven by transactions rather than price, with marketing spend not being further reduced to achieve it. This is the single decisive variable.

**AVOID. Overall score 3.5/10, down from 3.9.** The score decline reflects specific, verifiable fundamental deterioration rather than the 18% price decline since our June review: domestic comparable sales worsened from −7.4% to −8.9% on falling transactions, the dividend was suspended, the FY2026 outlook was cut, a franchisor vicarious-liability verdict was newly disclosed with an unquantified 2027 damages trial, the CFO departed mid-turnaround, consensus estimates fell 3.0% for the current year and 4.3% for next, and one analyst moved to strong sell.
