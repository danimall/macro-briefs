**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Organic subscription growth has re-accelerated to +7.0% in Q3 FY26 (fiscal year ends August 31), the best in several years, driven by AI-embedded data-solutions demand. At the same time, GAAP operating margin collapsed to 26.7% from 33.2% year-over-year, and net income fell 14.7%. This is a deliberate spend-through: restructuring and severance tied to a ~10% technology-workforce cut, one-time make-whole compensation for a new CEO, higher stock-based comp, and rising cloud/AI-token consumption costs. Management is defending EPS optics through heavy buybacks (~$506M in nine months, ~2M shares / ~5% of the count retired) while GAAP earnings decline.

FDS trades at ~$243, down roughly 43% from its 52-week high of $427, at ~13.6x trailing earnings and ~15.8x free cash flow. Versus its own history of 25-30x, this is cheap. Versus the sector-selection evidence — where a low valuation multiple has been a persistent value-trap signal rather than a buy trigger — the compression demands scrutiny of what the discount reflects. Here, the discount reflects a five-year cumulative TSR of 111.53 against a peer group at 231.96 (per FactSet's own proxy), an unremediated material weakness in the internal controls governing revenue, receivables, and deferred revenue that has persisted across two-plus fiscal years, active antitrust litigation over the CUSIP franchise, and a full C-suite reshuffle in the middle of a strategic pivot.

We rate FDS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case — margins normalize as one-time items roll off, organic re-acceleration proves durable, and a low-teens multiple re-rates — is plausible but unproven. The bear case — the front edge of a structurally lower-margin, more competitive era in which AI commoditizes financial data faster than FactSet can monetize it — is equally live. Absent a resolution of the margin debate or the control remediation, the risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the ongoing decline in our competitive, management, and risk scores across five prior reports reflects real deterioration, not just price action.

## Company Overview

FactSet is a global financial-data and analytics platform serving the investment community on a subscription model. As of May 31, 2026, it served ~9,130 clients and ~247,766 users. The franchise is highly recurring: ASV (annual subscription value) retention exceeds 95%; client-count retention runs ~90-91%. Buy-side clients represent ~82% of organic ASV.

Revenue splits geographically into Americas (~65%), EMEA (~24-25%), and Asia Pacific (~10%), and is managed by firm type: Institutional Buyside, Dealmakers, Wealth, and Market Infrastructure (renamed from "Partnerships & CGS" in FY2026). The competitive assets are workstations, portfolio analytics, and enterprise data feeds/APIs, increasingly AI-embedded, plus deep workflow entrenchment: clients hand FactSet their proprietary portfolio holdings, which creates high switching costs.

The distinctive asset is CUSIP Global Services (CGS): FactSet is the exclusive global issuer of CUSIP/CINS identifiers and the U.S. ISIN numbering agency, a quasi-monopoly franchise carried as a $1.58B intangible amortized over 36 years. This asset is both a high-margin revenue engine and the subject of antitrust litigation.

FactSet competes against Bloomberg, S&P Global Market Intelligence, and LSEG (Refinitiv) as the largest rivals, plus BlackRock Aladdin, MSCI, and Morningstar. It is the smaller, more open and flexible player. Roughly 68% of its ~12,700 employees sit in India and the Philippines, a structural cost advantage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY22 $1.84B → FY23 $2.09B → FY24 $2.20B → FY25 $2.32B, a ~8% CAGR but clearly decelerating. FY25 growth was +5.4% total and +4.4% organic, modest for a business that historically earned a premium multiple.

**FY2025 results:** Net income $597.0M (+11.2%, flattered by a $17.2M divestiture gain and the prior-year absence of a $54M Massachusetts sales-tax charge); diluted EPS $15.55; adjusted diluted EPS $16.98 (+3.2%). GAAP operating margin 32.2%. The tell was adjusted operating margin falling to 36.3% from 37.8%, the first crack in the margin story that dominates FY26. FCF was $617.5M, a healthy 27% margin.

**Q3 FY2026 — the central development:** Organic revenue and ASV growth accelerated to +7.0% and +7.1%, yet profitability deteriorated sharply. GAAP operating margin fell to 26.7% from 33.2%; the nine-month figure was 29.5% versus 33.1%. Adjusted operating margin dropped to 34.0% from 36.8%. Net income fell 14.7%; GAAP EPS was $3.50 (−9.6%); adjusted EPS was $4.53 (+6.1%). The drivers: restructuring/severance from a ~10% technology-workforce cut, one-time new-CEO make-whole cash and equity comp, higher stock-based comp, rising technology/cloud/AI-token costs, and an FX loss.

The reported quarter beat consensus — revenue of $622.9M against ~$618M, adjusted EPS $4.53 versus $4.27 a year ago — but the stock fell. The market read flat, reaffirmed guidance alongside visible margin compression as evidence the spend-through is not obviously temporary.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:** Total debt is ~$1.39B ($500M 2.90% notes due 2027, $500M 3.45% notes due 2032, $375M term loan, small revolver draw). D/E is ~0.97. The Altman Z of 2.5 sits in the grey zone, but this is driven by leverage and an asset base that is ~74% goodwill and intangibles, not by distress: interest coverage is very high and the company sits well inside its 3.75x leverage covenant. ROE is 27.3%, ROA 13.9%. The company repurchased $506M in nine months of FY26 (share count down to ~36.2M diluted from ~38.4M), with $494M remaining on an expanded authorization (June 2025 $400M plus December 2025 $600M). It has raised its dividend for 27 consecutive years, now $1.16/quarter, for a ~1.9% yield.

The financial-health read is solid but not pristine: high recurring cash generation and manageable leverage, offset by a persistent internal-control weakness (addressed under Risks) and GAAP earnings being propped optically by buybacks while net income falls. We score Financial Health 6.0.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is improving at the top line. Organic ASV re-accelerated to +7.1% in Q3 FY26 from the +4.4% organic pace of FY25, which management attributes to AI-driven data-solutions demand. New CFO Joshua Warren indicated revenue and EPS are "tracking toward the high end" of FY26 ranges. Estimate revisions confirm modest positive momentum: current-year consensus of $17.83 is up +0.7% over 90 days, and next-year $19.70 is up +1.6% over 30 days. The four-quarter earnings record shows three beats and one miss, with recent beats in the 0.6-2.2% range: a company that delivers close to consensus rather than blowing past it.

The magnitude of the revision signal matters. These are +0.5% to +1.6% revisions, not the +30%-plus velocity that flags a true inflection worth overriding valuation concerns. FactSet is a steady mid-single-digit-to-7% organic grower re-accelerating modestly, not a trough-earnings recovery story. Growth is durable and recurring but not explosive.

The AI theme cuts both ways. It is the vector re-accelerating ASV, and management is investing through the P&L and reallocating the tech workforce to build enterprise-AI capability. It is also a cost pressure (compute, cloud, token consumption) and a potential commoditizer of the underlying data. The FY25 acquisitions — LiquidityBook ($243M cloud-native OMS/trading) and Irwin ($120M investor-relations platform) — add modest inorganic growth but pushed goodwill to $1.28B and increased amortization. We score Growth Prospects 5.5: the acceleration is real and welcome, but its durability is unproven and it is being funded at the expense of earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing metrics, FDS looks cheap. At ~$243 it trades at ~13.6x earnings and ~15.8x FCF, against a historical range of 25-30x. Against the peer set — MORN at 18.1x, MKTX 13.8x, CBOE 25.3x, with a peer median of 18.1x — FactSet trades at a discount to the median.

The critical question is whether this discount is a mispricing or a warranted derating. Several factors argue it is at least partly warranted rather than a clean opportunity. First, unlike a cyclical trough, FactSet's earnings are not depressed by a downcycle that will mechanically reverse: the GAAP margin compression is driven by a deliberate strategic choice (spend-through investment plus one-time comp), and there is no guarantee margins snap back to prior levels rather than settling structurally lower. Second, the modest estimate revisions (+0.7% to +1.6%) do not clear the bar for treating current analyst targets as stale floors; consensus mean of $255 and median of $246.5 sit essentially at the current price, with a range of $210 to $340. Third, the five-year TSR gap quantifies a market that has been derating FactSet relative to peers for years, not a sudden dislocation.

The bull case for the multiple rests on margins normalizing once restructuring and one-time CEO comp roll off, taking adjusted operating margin back toward ~37% and re-rating the multiple toward the high teens or low twenties. That would imply meaningful upside. The bear case is that AI competition and compute costs cap margins structurally lower and the 13.6x multiple is the new normal for a slower-growing, lower-margin data vendor.

Given the genuine cheapness against history but the specific and unresolved reasons for the discount, we score Valuation 5.5. The franchise is intact and cash-generative, so this is not a value trap in the impairment sense, but it is not a clear mispricing either. The multiple will re-rate only if the margin story resolves favorably, and that catalyst has no fixed date.

## Competitive Landscape

FactSet sits in an oligopolistic market dominated by Bloomberg, S&P Global, and LSEG. Its differentiation is openness and flexibility (APIs, configurable workstations, enterprise feeds) plus deep buy-side workflow integration and the unique CUSIP franchise. Retention metrics remain strong (>95% ASV retention, ~90-91% client retention), which speaks to an intact moat at the account level.

The trajectory, however, is under pressure. The five-year TSR underperformance versus data-industry peers is the clearest market signal that investors doubt FactSet can out-innovate larger, better-capitalized rivals. Structural demand risks compound this: the active-to-passive shift reduces active-manager seat count, financial-services consolidation removes clients, and generative AI accelerates the proliferation of free and cheap data. FactSet is investing to embed AI, but the same technology could let competitors leapfrog or commoditize the core product.

The competitive-position score has fallen from 7.8 to 5.3 across prior reports, and the current evidence supports that decline rather than reversing it. Retention is high, but the trajectory — relative underperformance and margin pressure from a defensive investment posture — is what matters. We score Competitive Position 5.0: an entrenched but no longer clearly advantaged player defending share by spending.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and material, and several are not fully resolved by the current valuation.

**Internal-control material weakness.** First disclosed in the FY2024 10-K, this remains unremediated as of May 31, 2026, and disclosure controls were again concluded not effective. It concerns IT general controls supporting revenue, accounts receivable, and deferred revenue, the core of a subscription business. Management targets FY2026 completion and has engaged a third-party advisor and a new head of internal audit, but has not demonstrated sustained operating effectiveness. For a company whose entire thesis rests on recurring subscription metrics (ASV, retention), a two-year-old open weakness in exactly those controls caps confidence in the reported figures until remediated. This is the single most concerning governance item.

**CUSIP antitrust litigation.** The Dinosaur Financial Group class action attacks the CUSIP licensing franchise. Because CGS is a meaningful, high-margin revenue stream carried as a $1.58B intangible, an adverse outcome or forced pricing/structural change would be material.

**Leadership transition and execution risk.** New CEO Sanoke Viswanathan (ex-JPMorgan, ex-McKinsey) started September 8, 2025, with an upside-levered make-whole package including 297,902 PSOs that vest only if the 30-day VWAP reaches ≥150% of grant-date level within five years. CFO turnover compounds this: Helen Shan departed under a separation agreement, with Joshua Warren becoming CFO (agreement dated April 2, 2026). Multiple Item 5.02 filings across 2025-2026 confirm a broad executive reshuffle in the middle of a strategic pivot.

**Structural and concentration risks.** Two data suppliers each exceed 10% of data costs, and one cloud provider supplies the majority of cloud compute (single-vendor dependency). FX exposure spans GBP, EUR, INR, and PHP. $500M of notes come due March 2027 and have been reclassed to current debt; ~$395M carries floating-rate exposure. Client concentration is low (no client >3.5% of revenue), which is a mitigant.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.9% sits rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 45.1% (a 5.8pp spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is only 7%, suggesting current volatility pricing is toward the low end of its own recent range. The put/call skew is balanced (1.9%), and the term structure is flat (near 50.9% ≈ far 50.8%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific near-term catalyst. The put/call OI ratio of 1.64 leans defensive, and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±11.7%. The read is a market pricing elevated but not extreme uncertainty, with no acute directional bet, consistent with a stock in a strategic transition whose outcome is genuinely two-sided.

We score Risk Factors 4.5, reflecting the unremediated control weakness, live antitrust exposure, and concurrent CEO/CFO turnover — specific downside catalysts that are only partly priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Organic ASV re-acceleration to +7% is durable and AI-driven, proving FactSet can grow the top line faster than the market fears. The margin compression is transitory: restructuring severance, one-time CEO make-whole comp, and elevated AI-investment costs roll off, and adjusted operating margin recovers toward ~37%. The material weakness is remediated in FY26 as guided, removing a governance overhang. Buybacks at ~13.6x compound per-share value at attractive prices. The multiple re-rates from ~13.6x toward the high teens as earnings stabilize and grow, taking the stock toward $300-340 (the high end of analyst targets).

**Bear Case.** The margin compression is structural, not transitory: AI raises compute costs permanently and commoditizes data faster than FactSet can monetize its AI investments, capping margins in the low-to-mid 30s (adjusted) or worse. Organic growth fades back toward the mid-single digits as passive investing and consolidation erode the seat count. The CUSIP antitrust suit forces adverse pricing or structural changes to a high-margin franchise. The control weakness slips past FY26, deepening the governance discount. In this scenario the 13.6x multiple is the new normal or compresses further, and the stock drifts toward the $210 low target or below.

The five prior reports show scores declining from 7.1 (February) to 5.6, with a brief BUY in May that reverted to HOLD. Competitive position, management quality, and risk have each fallen ~2 points. The current data confirms rather than reverses this trend: the Q3 margin collapse and continued control weakness are the fundamental deterioration behind the price decline, not the reverse. The ~43% drawdown from the high is a rational derating against a genuine margin-versus-growth question that remains unanswered.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4/full-year FY26 results (roughly October 2026) are the natural checkpoint. That report should reveal whether adjusted margins are stabilizing as one-time items roll off, whether the material weakness is remediated as guided, and whether organic ASV growth holds near 7%.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted operating margin stabilizes or expands sequentially in Q4 FY26 (above ~34%), with management providing a credible bridge back toward ~37%, demonstrating the spend-through is genuinely transitory.
- The revenue/AR/deferred-revenue material weakness is remediated and disclosure controls are concluded effective in the FY26 10-K.
- Organic ASV growth sustains ≥7% with next-year EPS estimates continuing to revise upward, at a multiple still below ~18x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adjusted operating margin compresses further in Q4 FY26 or FY27 guidance implies a structurally lower margin band, confirming the bear thesis.
- The material weakness persists past FY26 or expands in scope.
- An adverse development in the CUSIP antitrust litigation, or organic ASV growth decelerating back below 5%.

**Base case:** At ~$243, with no clear near-term resolution of the margin debate, the stock is roughly fairly valued around the $246-255 consensus mean/median. Upside to the high-teens re-rating scenario is ~$300-340 (+23% to +40%); downside to the bear scenario is ~$210 (−14%) or lower. The asymmetry is modest and two-sided, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Conclusion

FactSet is a high-quality recurring-revenue franchise in the middle of a genuine transition. The top line is re-accelerating, and the market has derated the stock to ~13.6x earnings, cheap versus its own history. But the compression is not a clean cyclical trough waiting to reverse. It is the product of a deliberate spend-through strategy whose payoff is unproven, layered on top of an unremediated two-year-old material weakness in the controls governing subscription revenue, active antitrust litigation over the CUSIP franchise, and a simultaneous CEO and CFO reshuffle. The five-year relative TSR gap quantifies a market that has been skeptical of this business for years, not a sudden dislocation.

The estimate revisions are positive but modest (+0.7% to +1.6%), well short of the inflection velocity that would justify overriding the risk factors. Buybacks are defending EPS optics while net income falls. Until margins stabilize and the control weakness is remediated, both plausibly resolving over the next two to three quarters, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate FactSet a HOLD (uncertain), and would upgrade on evidence the margin compression is transitory and the governance overhang is clearing, or downgrade if the margin band proves structurally lower.
