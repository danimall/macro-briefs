**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts is the world's largest hotel franchisor by property count: over 8,300 hotels and ~869,000 rooms across 25 brands, concentrated in the economy, midscale, and upper-midscale segments. The model is pure-play, asset-light franchising: ~5% royalty on franchisee gross room revenue plus marketing/reservation fees on 10–20 year contracts, generating a 22.5% FCF margin on ~$1.4B revenue with only ~$45M of annual capex. That funds the shareholder-return program: $266M of buybacks plus $127M of dividends in 2025, with a dividend raised to $0.43/quarter in February 2026.

The 2025 headline obscures the operating reality. GAAP net income fell 33% to $193M ($2.50 EPS) from $289M, but that drop is almost entirely the $160M Revo Hospitality charge (an $86M impairment plus a $74M loss provision tied to the January 2026 insolvency of a large European franchisee) plus higher interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA rose 3% to $718M, and Hotel Franchising segment EBITDA reached $781M (+2%). Q1 2026 continued the pattern: adjusted EBITDA +8% to $156M on flat net income, with rooms +4% YoY and a record development pipeline of ~2,200 hotels / 259,000 rooms. The core constraint is demand: global RevPAR fell 3% in 2025 (U.S. -4%) and was essentially flat in Q1'26.

This is a balanced setup rather than a clear directional call. On the positive side: a durable moat in value lodging, modest real leverage (first-lien 2.0x against a 5.0x covenant), strong FCF, and uniformly constructive sell-side (zero sell ratings; the lowest analyst target of $89 sits above the current $85.45). On the negative side: the core royalty line is declining, franchisee-credit risk is increasing as the company expands development-advance lending, RevPAR shows no organic momentum, and the forward multiple of ~17.8x leaves limited asymmetry. We rate WH a HOLD (income-oriented), with an upgrade path on RevPAR re-acceleration or a multiple reset toward the low-$70s.

## Company Overview

Wyndham was spun off from Wyndham Worldwide (now Travel + Leisure Co.) in May 2018 as a focused franchisor. It licenses 25 brands — including Super 8, Days Inn, La Quinta, Ramada, Microtel, Howard Johnson, Travelodge, Baymont, and the upscale Wyndham/Wyndham Grand — to more than 6,200 franchisees, no single one of which represents more than 2% of hotels. The concentration is in economy and midscale chain scales, primarily in secondary and tertiary U.S. markets; roughly 80% of the U.S. population lives within 10 miles of a Wyndham-affiliated hotel, and the company holds about 19% of U.S. branded rooms.

The Wyndham Rewards loyalty program has 122M+ members (up 7% in 2025 and 24% since 2022), drives 37% of global check-ins and 53% in the U.S., and has been ranked the #1 U.S. hotel loyalty program for eight consecutive years. Royalty geography in 2025 was U.S. 77%, EMEA 8%, Canada 6%, Asia Pacific 6%, LATAM 3%. The fastest-growing revenue lines are ancillary: the co-branded credit card program and the Travel + Leisure licensing partnership under the Wyndham trademark.

The competitive edge is scale leadership in value lodging, a more recession-resilient but structurally lower-RevPAR segment than the upscale-skewed majors (Marriott, Hilton, IHG). Capital intensity is minimal, which is the source of the high FCF conversion and the buyback program.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Fee-related revenue was $1,429M, up 2% from $1,404M, driven by a +$41M lift in ancillary (credit card) revenue and system growth, partly offset by the RevPAR decline. Operating margin was 28%, net margin 13.5%, FCF margin 22.5%, with $321M of free cash flow.

**Q1 2026.** Net revenue was $327M (+3% YoY), net income roughly flat at $61M ($0.80 diluted EPS vs. $0.78), and adjusted EBITDA grew 8% to $156M. Global RevPAR was flat reported (-1% constant currency), with the U.S. flat (including a ~40bps hurricane-comp drag), China -5%, and Latin America -4%, offset by Canada +8%, SE Asia/Pacific +5%, and EMEA +1%. Rooms grew 4% YoY (U.S. flat on loss of legacy affiliated rooms; international +9%). The watch item: royalties and franchise fees declined $12M YoY in Q1, weighed by lower franchise fees and Revo fee deferral; ancillary revenue (+$15M) carried the quarter.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Total debt is ~$2.65B as of Q1'26. The reported ~8x D/E and Altman Z-Score of 0.87 ("distress zone") are artifacts of an asset-light model that has repurchased nearly all of its book equity, not signs of stress. The relevant credit metric is first-lien leverage of 2.0x against a 5.0x covenant maximum, with ~$1.1B of liquidity. In February 2026 the company issued $650M of 5.625% senior notes due 2033, repaying the revolver and term loan A and pushing the nearest meaningful maturity to 2028; ~95% of the term loan B is swapped to fixed at 3.3–3.8%. Credit ratings are Ba1/BB+/BB+, one notch below investment grade. Shareholder returns: 28M treasury shares retired in 2025; since 2018, 27.9M shares repurchased at an average of $69.37 (~$1.9B). Q1'26 saw $51M of buybacks against a $223M remaining authorization.

The quality-of-earnings nuance: the core room-royalty line is declining ($541M FY25 vs. $555M FY24), masked by booming ancillary/credit-card revenue. The growth story is increasingly dependent on partnership monetization rather than the same-store royalty engine.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two components moving in opposite directions. Net unit growth is the structural driver and remains healthy: a record pipeline (+3%) and management guidance of 4–4.5% net room growth for 2026. International development is the strongest area. Against this, organic same-store growth is weak, with the value/economy customer base exposed to inflation, soft consumer sentiment, and recession risk.

On estimates, the consensus picture is stable rather than deteriorating. Current-quarter EPS estimates were revised +1.9% over 90 days; current-year sits at $4.80 (-0.6% over 90 days) and next-year at $5.37 (-0.4%) — essentially flat. The one soft line is next-quarter (-6.4% over 90 days), but that is a single seasonal data point against an otherwise unchanged full-year and out-year consensus, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (Q1'26 a +8.0% surprise at $0.96 vs. $0.89). The read is a company delivering against flat-to-stable expectations, not one whose estimates are being cut. That supports holding, but it does not generate the rising-estimate momentum that would justify a BUY.

The structural growth ceiling is RevPAR. With same-store flat, total return depends on net unit growth plus buybacks rather than pricing power. The growth-prospects score is the binding constraint here, and it earns a below-average mark.

## Valuation Assessment

WH trades at ~17.8x forward EPS ($85.45 against the $4.80 current-year consensus; ~15.9x against next-year $5.37). That is a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 27.8x in Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Choice Hotels (the closest franchising comp) trades at 14.5x, while timeshare and resort names (HGV 27.8x, MTN 31.2x, VAC negative) skew the median upward. Against Choice, Wyndham's premium reflects its larger scale and stronger loyalty franchise, and the gap is modest. The 2.1% dividend yield is below the peer median 3.4%.

The asymmetry math should be built on the realized trailing range, which is $69.21–$92.69 (the $113.07 figure that appears in the report's research narrative is not supported by the price data in the Phase 1 dataset, where the 52-week high is $92.69). At $85.45, the stock sits in the upper portion of its realized range, roughly 4% below the lowest analyst target of $89 and ~15% below the $100 median target. A reasonable forward multiple on stable-to-slightly-growing adjusted earnings supports a fair value in the high-$80s to mid-$90s. That is constructive but not a mispricing; the multiple already reflects both the durable franchise quality and the soft RevPAR.

We do not treat the cheapness-to-peer-median as a buy signal in isolation. A discount on a name with flat organic growth and an emerging franchisee-credit overhang is appropriately earned, not an inefficiency. The valuation is reasonable, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID, but it lacks the catalyst or growth re-acceleration that would make it a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Wyndham's differentiation is scale leadership in economy and midscale value lodging. It competes against Choice, Marriott, Hilton, IHG, and Best Western in franchising, plus short-term rentals and OTAs. The loyalty program and franchisee diversification (no single franchisee >2%) are genuine moats, and net unit growth shows the brand is still winning conversions and new construction, especially internationally.

Two trajectory concerns temper the competitive read. First, the asset-light model is showing strain at the edges: Wyndham lends to franchisees through development advance notes (~$355M outstanding) to win higher-FeePAR units, and that lending is what produced the Revo loss. The company plans to *increase* development-advance funding to ~$110M in 2026, so this credit-extension risk is growing. Foreclosing on and now owning two European hotels (~$23M net assets plus $15M assumed mortgages) is a small departure from the pure-franchisor thesis. Second, the 10-K added explicit new language on large language models potentially sourcing hotel information from third-party intermediaries rather than directly from Wyndham, which could reduce brand visibility, raise customer-acquisition costs, and divert direct bookings away from brand.com and loyalty channels. This is an industry-wide threat to the direct-booking moat, and Wyndham acknowledges it is "working to provide" inventory data to LLM providers while conceding it may lose channel control. Neither is acute today, but both push the competitive trajectory toward stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchisee credit risk (the defining 2025 event).** The Revo Hospitality insolvency also triggered a write-down of the Vienna House trademark. The allowance for loan losses jumped from $1M to $55M YoY. The proceedings "may not be resolved for several years," and the company is expanding the same lending strategy that created the exposure. This is the most company-specific risk and the one most likely to recur.

**Soft U.S. RevPAR / demand sensitivity.** A value/economy franchisor is structurally exposed to the lower-income leisure and small-business traveler. With a customer base sensitive to inflation and weak consumer sentiment, there is downside if a 2026 consumer cliff materializes.

**LLM/OTA disintermediation.** A medium-term structural threat to the direct-booking and loyalty advantage, newly emphasized in the 10-K.

**Travel + Leisure dependency.** Material license/royalty fees come from T+L's vacation-ownership sales under the Wyndham trademark; softness there flows directly to Wyndham fees. Two Wyndham directors sit on T+L's board (a governance flag).

**Human trafficking litigation.** ~70 pending matters naming Wyndham/subsidiaries; accruals are small ($2M, max ~$7M above accruals stated), but reputational and tail risk persists.

**Takeover/activism.** The 10-K flags that another unsolicited bid (after the 2024 hostile takeover attempt that cost $47M to defend) would again be costly. The 2024 pursuer is widely reported to have been Choice Hotels at a bid around $90/share, but the research brief notes this acquirer identity was not independently verifiable from the provided primary sources [unverified]. This is a downside distraction and a potential embedded upside option.

**Leverage optics.** The Altman Z of 0.87 is a false positive driven by buyback-eroded equity; first-lien leverage and liquidity mean no solvency concern. Equity holders do bear leverage, and the model depends on continued strong FCF to service the debt load.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that is not corroborated by the fundamental data. Near-term ATM IV is 62.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 30.4% — rich, at the 77th percentile of the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (62.8% near-term vs. 33.6% at six months) signaling an imminent event. The implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±3.1%, and the elevated near-term level is concentrated in the front weekly tenor; one-month IV drops to 34.1%. The 6.0% put/call skew reflects modestly elevated put demand, consistent with 10.8% short interest (5.7 days to cover). The P/C OI ratio fell from 1.09 to 0.68 over the past month even as IV spiked, suggesting positioning is becoming less bearish. The spike in near-term IV is most likely event-driven (a dated catalyst or broader market stress repricing) rather than a fundamental signal, but it does flag that this is not a low-uncertainty entry, and the rich IV would favor selling premium over buying it for anyone expressing a view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The development pipeline is at a record, net unit growth is guided to 4–4.5% for 2026, and the capital-return engine is self-funding. The GAAP net-income drop is a one-time credit event, not core decay. Sell-side is uniformly constructive (4 strong buy, 10 buy, 3 hold, 0 sell; median target $100, low $89). If RevPAR re-accelerates and ancillary monetization continues, the stock re-rates toward the $100 median target. Bull case ~$105.

**Bear case.** RevPAR stays flat or turns negative as the lower-income consumer weakens; the core royalty line continues to decline while ancillary growth decelerates; franchisee-credit losses recur as the company expands development-advance lending; and LLM/OTA disintermediation gradually erodes the direct-booking moat. In a 2026 consumer-cliff scenario the multiple compresses toward Choice's 14.5x on flat or falling earnings. Bear case ~$69 (roughly -19% from current), near the realized 52-week low of $69.21.

The base case lands in between: stable adjusted EBITDA, 4% unit growth, modest buyback accretion, and a forward multiple holding near 17–18x produce a fair value around $88–$92, essentially the current price plus the dividend. That balance of a durable franchise and strong FCF against flat same-store growth and an expanding credit overhang is the profile of a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July), which will show whether RevPAR is stabilizing, whether the next-quarter estimate softness materializes, and the status of the Revo foreclosure/recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Global RevPAR returns to sustained positive territory (+2% or better) for two consecutive quarters, confirming organic re-acceleration.
- A multiple reset toward the low-$70s (~14–15x forward), which would restore genuine asymmetry against the $100 median target.
- Evidence that franchisee-credit losses are contained and the allowance for loan losses stabilizes, removing the recurring-Revo overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. RevPAR turns persistently negative (-4% or worse) alongside falling full-year EPS revisions, signaling a genuine consumer-driven earnings cut rather than flat estimates.
- A second material franchisee-credit charge, confirming the development-advance lending strategy is structurally impairing the asset-light model.
- A material weakening of the credit profile (first-lien leverage rising toward the covenant) or a buyback suspension that removes the capital-return support under the stock.

**Income rationale:** This is held for the combination of a 2.1% growing dividend, a self-funding buyback, and durable FCF, with the understanding that total return depends on net unit growth plus capital return rather than same-store strength. New capital lacks asymmetric entry at $85; existing holders are compensated to wait.

## Conclusion

Wyndham is a high-quality, asset-light franchisor with a durable moat in value lodging, a 22.5% FCF margin, modest real leverage, and a disciplined capital-return program. The 2025 GAAP net-income decline was a one-time Revo credit event; underlying adjusted EBITDA is growing, and the company beats estimates more often than it misses. Set against that, organic growth is constrained by flat-to-soft RevPAR, the core royalty line is declining behind ancillary growth, franchisee-credit risk is expanding as the company lends more to win units, and the forward multiple — reasonable versus peers — offers limited asymmetry from $85.

The evidence describes a balanced franchise with soft momentum, not a structurally impaired business and not a clear mispricing. There is no solvency, governance, or secular-collapse trigger, EBITDA is growing, and the stock already sits below the lowest analyst target, which rules out an AVOID. The absence of rising estimates, the flat RevPAR, and the credit overhang rule out a BUY. We rate WH a HOLD, with an upgrade path on RevPAR re-acceleration or a pullback toward the low-$70s.
