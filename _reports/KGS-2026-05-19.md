## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the market leader in U.S. large-horsepower contract compression, operating ~4.5 million HP at 98% utilization with 91% of revenue under fixed primary-term contracts. The company's Q1 2026 results demonstrated continued operational excellence: record EBITDA of $190M at 55% margins, Contract Services gross margins expanding to 70.6%, and EPS beating consensus by ~11%. The April 2026 closure of the $687M DPS acquisition adds ~395 MW of distributed power generation capacity targeting data centers and manufacturing, creating a second growth vector with comparable margin economics.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. KGS trades at $76.17, 114% above its price one year ago, at 100x trailing P/E, and well above the analyst consensus target of $79. On 2026 guidance of $820M-$860M EBITDA (midpoint $840M), Enterprise Value of approximately $9.6B implies ~11.4x EV/EBITDA — a significant premium to peer Archrock at 21x P/E (approximately 8-9x EBITDA). The premium partially reflects KGS's data center power optionality, but that business contributes only $95M-$125M in year-one revenue and requires $400M-$500M annually in growth capex through 2030. The undisclosed DOJ/OFAC investigation related to Mexico cartel payments represents a tail risk with uncertain magnitude that appears under-recognized by the market.

The growth thesis is real — U.S. gas production expansion, LNG export capacity additions, anti-flaring regulation, and AI-driven power demand all structurally benefit KGS. But at current prices, the stock requires flawless execution on power infrastructure scaling, continued compression pricing gains, and benign resolution of the DOJ matter to justify further appreciation.

## Company Overview

Kodiak Gas Services operates the largest fleet of large-horsepower contract compression equipment in the United States. The business model is infrastructure-like: KGS deploys compression units at customer sites under fixed-revenue contracts with 3-5 year primary terms, earning monthly fees regardless of gas throughput. Customers provide fuel gas at no cost to KGS, eliminating commodity exposure. The installed base of large HP units creates meaningful switching costs given the expense and operational disruption of moving equipment.

**Business Segments (2026 structure):**
- **Compression Infrastructure** (formerly Contract Services, ~90% of revenue): Fixed-fee compression services for E&P and midstream operators, predominantly in the Permian Basin (82.8% of deployed assets).
- **Power Infrastructure** (new via DPS acquisition, ~7-9% of FY26 revenue): Distributed power generation for data centers, microgrids, and manufacturing facilities. ~395 MW acquired capacity with plans to reach 2 GW by end of decade.
- **Other Services** (~10% of revenue): Station construction, maintenance, freight, and parts.

The company IPO'd in July 2023 at ~$17/share and has since delivered 271% total shareholder return versus 174% for its peer group. The private equity sponsor (EQT AB) fully exited by December 2025, removing a persistent selling overhang.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Revenue grew from $850M (2023) to $1,159M (2024, +36% including CSI acquisition) to $1,308M (2025, +13% organic + pricing). Q1 2026 revenue was $346M, and full-year 2026 guidance implies $1,250M-$1,280M for compression plus $95M-$125M for power, totaling approximately $1,345M-$1,405M.

Contract Services adjusted gross margin expanded from 65.7% (2024) to 68.4% (2025) to 70.6% (Q1 2026). Adjusted EBITDA margins rose from 52.6% to 54.7% to 55.0% over the same period. This demonstrates genuine pricing power in a tight utilization environment: 2025 revenue grew 14.2% on only 2.5% HP growth, implying ~12% price/mix contribution.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow nearly doubled from $328M (2024) to $600M (2025). Company-defined free cash flow was $230M in 2025 (up from $122M), with discretionary cash flow of $462M. The 2026 guidance of $520M-$570M in discretionary cash flow implies continued improvement despite higher growth spending.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of ~$2.8B post-DPS represents the primary financial risk. Net leverage stands at 3.5x (Q1 2026, credit agreement definition), within the 5.25x maximum covenant. Interest expense of ~$200M annually consumes approximately 24% of guided midpoint EBITDA. Debt maturities are well-staggered: $750M (2029), $465M ABL (2030), $770M (2033), $630M (2035). The $1.5B of available ABL capacity provides adequate liquidity for the $645M-$775M growth capex program.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 (distress zone) is a structural artifact of the capital-intensive infrastructure model, not indicative of operational distress. With 98% utilization, 70%+ gross margins, and fixed-revenue contracts with investment-grade counterparties, the operational risk profile is utility-like despite the leveraged capital structure.

**Capital Allocation:**
2026 growth capex of $645M-$775M (3-4x the 2025 level of $200M) reflects management's conviction in both compression and power opportunities. The dividend ($1.96/share, ~2.6% yield) consumes ~$174M annually. Share repurchases ($104M in 2025) may slow as capital is redirected to organic growth.

**Valuation Thesis:**
The stock has appreciated 114% in one year and now trades above the analyst consensus targets (median $82.50, mean $79.33). At 29x forward 2026 earnings with $2.8B in debt, the risk/reward is compressed. The power infrastructure business contributes only 7-9% of 2026 revenue — the 2 GW/decade-end vision requires sustained 300-500 MW/year deployment at $400-500M annually with no execution missteps. The market is pricing in substantial success on this second platform.

The 114% one-year appreciation was justified by genuine fundamental improvement (earnings inflection, DPS acquisition, EQT overhang removal), but at current prices, the appreciation has caught up with or exceeded the near-term fundamental improvement. Further upside requires successful power infrastructure scaling, continued compression pricing gains, and DOJ resolution.

## Competitive Landscape

KGS holds the strongest competitive position in U.S. large-HP contract compression:

1. **Scale advantage**: 4.5M fleet HP is the largest in the market; standardization reduces maintenance costs
2. **Geographic density in Permian**: 82.8% asset concentration enables lower logistics costs and faster response times
3. **Contract structure**: 91% under primary term (vs. lower for smaller competitors), providing revenue visibility
4. **Utilization premium**: 98.0% utilization vs. industry average of ~90-93% indicates KGS captures the highest-quality contracts
5. **Large HP focus**: Units >1,000 HP have longer asset lives, higher switching costs, and fewer competitors
6. **Expanding moat via power**: DPS acquisition creates a differentiated platform combining compression and power for the same customer base

Gross margins expanding from 65.7% to 70.6% in two years demonstrates pricing power that only the market leader can sustain. The 12-18 month lead times for new large HP units create a structural supply constraint that benefits incumbents with existing deployment capacity.

The power infrastructure pivot, while early-stage, could expand KGS's addressable market significantly. Data center distributed power is a nascent market with limited competition from established infrastructure providers at KGS's scale.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage Risk (HIGH):** $2.8B debt at ~$200M annual interest, 3.5x net leverage. While covenants are comfortable today and maturities are staggered, any prolonged downturn in natural gas activity or pricing pressure would stress debt service. Rising rates increase refinancing costs on the $750M 2029 maturity.

**2. DOJ/OFAC Investigation (MODERATE-HIGH, ASYMMETRIC):** The Mexico cartel payment investigation is the most under-appreciated risk. Voluntary self-reporting typically results in reduced penalties, but OFAC fines for sanctions violations can be substantial. No media coverage suggests this is not in the market price. Criminal resolution (unlikely but possible) would be catastrophic.

**3. Execution Risk on Power Infrastructure:** The $400M-$500M/year power capex program in a new market with different competitive dynamics carries meaningful execution risk. Equipment procurement delays, permitting challenges, or weaker-than-expected data center demand could result in stranded capital.

**4. Geographic Concentration:** 82.8% Permian/Eagle Ford exposure. Any regulatory disruption (federal leasing policy, methane regulations) or basin-level production decline would disproportionately impact KGS.

**5. Valuation Risk:** At 29x forward P/E and 11.4x EV/EBITDA, any disappointment on growth, margins, or power infrastructure progress would likely result in meaningful multiple compression. The stock trades above analyst consensus targets.

**6. Customer Concentration:** Top 4 customers represent 32% of revenue. While these are investment-grade companies, loss of any single relationship would be material.

## Conclusion

KGS is a high-quality infrastructure business with genuine competitive advantages, strong growth vectors, and capable management. The compression business is best-in-class: 98% utilization, 70%+ gross margins, fixed-revenue contracts with investment-grade customers, and structural demand tailwinds from U.S. gas production growth and LNG expansion. The power infrastructure pivot adds optionality that could materially expand the company's addressable market.

The problem is price. At $76.17, the stock trades above analyst consensus targets, at 29x forward 2026 P/E and ~11.4x EV/EBITDA — a significant premium to peers. The upside from here requires sustained execution on a power infrastructure build-out in its first quarter of operation, continued compression pricing gains already running at elevated levels, and a benign resolution of the DOJ/OFAC investigation that the market appears to be largely ignoring.

The risk/reward at current levels is approximately balanced. HOLD with a growth orientation, recognizing that the fundamental trajectory supports the stock but the price already reflects substantial forward progress. A pullback to the $60-65 range on broader market weakness or sector rotation would create a compelling entry point for a business of this quality.
