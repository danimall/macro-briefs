## Executive Summary

JLL's Q2 2026 print (July 30) was the strongest of the current cycle: revenue $6,927.9M (+10.8%), operating income +47%, Adjusted EBITDA $386.3M (+33% local currency), GAAP diluted EPS $4.59 versus $2.32, and non-GAAP EPS of $5.26 against $4.55 non-GAAP consensus — a 15.6% beat and the fourth consecutive beat. The four disclosed EPS surprises average 12.6% (5.2%, 17.7%, 13.2%, 14.4%). Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $24.60–$25.90, a midpoint implying roughly 34% growth. Consensus has followed: current-year estimates are up 7.9% in 30 days and 8.6% in 90 days to $24.82; next year up 6.3%/7.7% to $27.76. The shares rose 13.3% on the print and now trade 2.6% below the 52-week high of $373.29 and 40% above the February low of $259.83.

The valuation is genuinely undemanding for the growth being delivered: 14.4x the guidance midpoint, 14.65x current-year consensus, 13.1x FY2027 consensus, and 11.2x EV/TTM Adjusted EBITDA on net leverage of 0.74x. The most useful relative anchor is CBRE. JLL also has a $2,591.6M repurchase authorization equal to 15.5% of market capitalization, and has been executing accretively — the March accelerated repurchase settled 638,400 shares at an average $313.29, 13.8% below today's close.

What keeps this at ACCUMULATE rather than BUY is the composition of the earnings and the price at which they are now capitalized. On fee revenue (total revenue less $17.16B of pass-through client reimbursements), Leasing Advisory plus Capital Markets are ~60.6% of revenue and ~65% of segment Adjusted EBITDA. Roughly 43% of the H1 pretax earnings improvement came from below-the-line and non-recurring items — chiefly a $58.3M swing in equity earnings as proptech and co-investment mark-to-market losses stopped — rather than operations. Leasing growth of +24% matched CBRE's global leasing growth exactly, which reads as a market-wide recovery rather than share capture. Base case $415 (+14.1%) against a cyclical bear near $260 (-28.5%) is a positive but not compelling asymmetry from here.

## Company Overview

JLL is a global commercial real estate services and investment management firm: $26.1B of FY2025 revenue, operations in over 80 countries, 113,200 employees (58,600 professional, 54,600 directly reimbursable and fully recharged to clients). The franchise metrics are large and hard to replicate: ~2.8 billion sq ft under workplace management, ~2.9 billion sq ft under property management, ~19,500 agency leasing transactions covering 340M sq ft, ~23,500 tenant-representation transactions covering 569M sq ft, capital markets services on ~$258B of client transactions, a $140.3B loan servicing portfolio with Fannie Mae DUS (one of only 24 lenders), Freddie Mac and HUD/Ginnie Mae approvals, and LaSalle Investment Management at $86.4B AUM.

Effective January 1, 2026 the Software & Technology Solutions segment was folded into Real Estate Management Services as a subsegment, reducing reportable segments from five to four. S&T revenue remains disclosed at the subsegment line ($58.1M in Q2, +4%), but standalone segment profitability — which was -$14.2M Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 and -$19.6M in FY2024 — is no longer visible. The reorganization triggered a required quantitative goodwill test on January 1, 2026, which passed.

The contractual architecture matters for how "recurring" reads. Workplace Management contracts run three to seven years but "most contracts can be terminated at will by the client upon a short notice period (usually 30 to 60 days)"; property management runs one to three years with 30–120 day notice. Stickiness rests on renewal behavior and switching friction, not enforceable term. There is no usable backlog: the 10-Q states remaining performance obligations relate to "an insignificant amount of total revenue," and contract liabilities were $204.0M against $26B of annual revenue. Forward visibility comes from management's pipeline commentary, not from a contracted-revenue disclosure.

## Financial Analysis

**Trajectory.** Revenue went $20,862.1M (FY2022) → $20,760.8M (FY2023) → $23,432.9M (FY2024) → $26,115.6M (FY2025), a 7.8% three-year CAGR with 11.4% growth in FY2025. Net income traced a far more violent path: $654.5M → $225.4M → $546.8M → $792.1M. The FY2023 datapoint is the most important calibration in this file: earnings fell roughly 66% on essentially flat revenue, because the transaction-fee lines carry most of the profit while pass-through reimbursements hold revenue up.

**H1 2026.** Revenue $13,314.4M (+11% USD, +10% local currency); operating income $495.5M versus $317.4M; Adjusted EBITDA $659.9M versus $516.5M (+28%); diluted EPS $7.91 versus $3.46. Effective tax rate 19.3%. Diluted shares 46.925M in Q2 versus 48.334M a year earlier (-2.9%).

**Decomposition of the earnings jump.** H1 pretax income rose $252.9M, from $204.5M to $457.4M. The attribution:

| Driver | Favorable swing | Share of increase |
|---|---|---|
| Equity earnings/(losses) | +$58.3M | 23.1% |
| Depreciation & amortization | +$24.3M | 9.6% |
| Restructuring/acquisition charges | +$10.0M | 4.0% |
| Net interest expense | +$16.5M | 6.5% |
| Residual operating leverage | +$143.8M | 56.9% |

Roughly 57% is core operating leverage; 43% comes from below-the-line and cost-run-off items. The largest single contributor is the cessation of proptech and co-investment mark-to-market losses (FY2024 equity losses -$70.8M → FY2025 -$20.7M → H1 2026 +$5.3M). That tailwind is the absence of prior losses; it cannot repeat at the same magnitude. Lower D&A (acquisition intangible run-off) and lower interest (H1 average borrowings $842.6M at 4.3% versus $1,290.0M at 5.0%) are cash-relevant and plausibly durable, so this is not purely optical. A second comparability item: Q2 2025 Capital Markets carried ~$14.0M of loan-loss expense tied to an enhanced Fannie Mae loss-share arrangement; normalizing it reduces Q2 Capital Markets Adjusted EBITDA growth from the reported +74% to +38.6%.

**The operating leverage is real and measurable.** Stripping pass-throughs, FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA / fee revenue was 16.2% ($1,452.9M / $8,957.4M) versus 14.8% in FY2024 — 147bps. H1 2026 was 15.0% versus 13.4% — 166bps. That is independent corroboration of the $143.8M residual in the table above, and it is the metric that should anchor the growth thesis rather than headline adjusted EPS growth of +61%.

**Segment economics on the right denominator.** Reported revenue mix says REMS is 76.6% of the business. On fee revenue of $8,957.4M, REMS is ~32.4%, Leasing Advisory ~33.6%, Capital Markets ~27.0%, Investment Management ~5.0%. On segment Adjusted EBITDA, Leasing Advisory (39.9%) plus Capital Markets (25.1%) are ~65% of profit against REMS at 30.1%. Management's "nearly 80% resilient revenue" framing reconciles arithmetically to the segment table but uses a revenue denominator that is 65.7% pass-through cost recovery generating near-zero margin.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $1,194.1M against $215.6M capex → FCF $978.5M, a 67.3% conversion of Adjusted EBITDA. H1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $266.9M (versus negative $434.8M in H1 2025), driven by a disclosed $632.2M Q1 annual incentive-compensation payout and $247.0M of reimbursable receivable/payable timing. This seasonality is structural and Q4-weighted; the externally reported "$438M free cash flow, up 52%" figure reconciles only as a Q2 standalone measure and must not be annualized. As a labelled sensitivity only, 4 × $438M = $1.75B, which is 79% above FY2025 actual and implausible. The normalized anchor is FY2025's $978.5M growing broadly with Adjusted EBITDA.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30: cash $458.2M, total debt $1,637.7M (up from $889.9M at year-end, composed of $420.0M commercial paper, $338.0M revolver net of costs, $596.4M long-term notes), net debt $1,179.5M. TTM Adjusted EBITDA $1,596.3M → 0.74x net leverage, matching management's stated 0.7x. Warehouse facilities of $700.3M are matched by $700.3M of warehouse receivables and sit outside corporate leverage. Corporate liquidity $3.4B; revolver $3.3B to November 2028; ratings Baa1/BBB+ reaffirmed; no maturity before June 2027 (€175M at 1.96%), then $400M 6.875% notes in December 2028. Interest-rate sensitivity is small: +50bps would have added $2.1M to H1 interest expense.

The H1 debt increase funded the Q1 compensation payout and $410.0M of repurchases against negative operating cash flow. That is intra-year working-capital financing, not deterioration, but the current ratio of 1.11 and reliably negative first-half cash flow mean the company carries genuine seasonal funding needs. The Altman Z-Score of 2.76 ("grey zone") is mechanically depressed by $3,090.6M of reimbursable client receivables, $700.3M of matched warehouse receivables, and $797.2M of deferred compensation assets — roughly 26% of total assets in matched pass-through items. Direct credit evidence outranks the screen here.

Goodwill of $4,726.7M is 63.3% of the $7,461.7M shareholders' equity. Tangible book value is approximately $2,099.3M, or $45.6 per share against a $363.69 price. Return on tangible common equity is correspondingly high (FY2025 ~38%), which is the honest description of an asset-light services franchise, but the balance sheet supplies no asset-value floor.

## Growth Analysis

The Q2 detail supports a broad-based recovery in the transaction lines. Leasing Advisory revenue +24% local currency (+28% two-year stacked), with office leasing revenue +20% against global office market volume growth of 2%. Capital markets components: debt advisory +44%, investment sales +20%, equity advisory +53%, with U.S. investment sales revenue +53% against roughly 22% market growth. Management targets mid- to high-teens full-year leasing revenue growth and mid-teens capital markets revenue growth, with tougher comparisons beginning in Q4.

Two qualifications on the revenue-versus-volume spread. First, JLL's leasing fees are typically a percentage of the lease revenue commitment, so rising rents, longer terms, and larger average deal sizes inflate revenue independently of transaction-count share — management explicitly cited larger transaction sizes as a contributor. Volume indices do not capture that, so the spread overstates pure share gain by an amount not quantifiable from the disclosures. Second, CBRE reported global leasing revenue +24% in the same quarter. Identical growth rates at the two largest advisers point to a market-wide leasing recovery.

The recurring side is more mixed. REMS is guided to mid- to high-single-digit revenue growth, H2 weighted to Q4. Workplace Management grew 11% in Q2; Property Management grew 3%, which management attributes to previously disclosed strategic contract exits expected to dissipate over coming quarters. Project management grew only 3% in the quarter (+25% two-year stacked), aided by data-center demand. Investment Management is the clear weak point: revenue fell 4% in FY2025 and was flat in H1 2026, with AUM at $88.8B → $86.4B → $86.8B over eighteen months. The TTM roll-forward shows acquisitions of $6.9B against dispositions and withdrawals of $8.4B. LaSalle is a net seller — including in Asia-Pacific, where CBRE research puts H1 2026 investment up 27% — which is defensible fiduciary harvesting but shrinks the fee base while peers raise capital. Management guides to low-single-digit advisory-fee growth with incentive and transaction fees at the lower end of the historical range.

Near-term cost items: producers hit higher commission tiers earlier because of strong U.S. performance and larger deals, a headwind management expects to moderate in H2 before tiers reset in January. FY2025 also absorbed roughly $25M from a U.S. healthcare actuarial deficit, ~$22M of it in REMS.

Estimate revisions are positive and accelerating at the short end but below the magnitude that constitutes a standalone inflection signal. The four-quarter beat record argues that the guided $24.60–$25.90 range is more likely a floor than a ceiling; consensus at $24.82 sits just below the midpoint, which is consistent with analysts not yet fully marking to the raise.

## Competitive Landscape

JLL competes with CBRE at global scale, Cushman & Wakefield and Colliers a tier below, Newmark and regional specialists in capital markets, and — per its own 10-K — non-traditional entrants including investment banks, investment managers, accounting firms, technology and SaaS providers, outsourcing firms, and clients that self-perform. The industry is simultaneously consolidating at the top and fragmented below.

The durable advantages are scale-and-solvency driven. Baa1/BBB+ ratings are explicitly framed as a competitive weapon in REMS, where clients underwrite provider solvency before outsourcing critical facilities. The integrated "One JLL" platform, research depth, and the technology stack (Falcon, JLL GPT, Azara, Building Engines, Corrigo, LeasingOS, Tétris) are the stated differentiators. Agency lending status — one of 24 Fannie DUS lenders, plus Freddie and HUD approvals — is a licensed moat in multifamily debt.

Trajectory is the harder question. Revenue grew 10.8% against CBRE's 15.2%. Leasing growth was identical at +24%, which does not evidence share capture. The U.S. investment-sales revenue spread over market volume growth (+53% versus ~22%) is large and partly reflects share, but rent and deal-size inflation account for an unquantifiable portion. LaSalle is losing fee base while its regional markets recover. Property Management at +3% is characterized as strategic contract exits; the same revenue pattern would result from competitive losses or client insourcing, and there is no filing-level quantification of the exited contracts.

The AI risk factor points opposite to the AI narrative. The 10-K warns that failure to adapt pricing models, "particularly in cost-plus arrangements," to reflect AI-enabled cost reductions could erode margins or damage client relationships. Workplace Management ($13.85B of FY2025 revenue) is contracted on a principal basis including reimbursement and guaranteed-maximum pricing. In those structures, productivity gains accrue to the client unless JLL renegotiates toward fixed-fee or outcome-based pricing — a commercial negotiation across thousands of contracts, not an automatic margin drop-through. The clearer near-term AI benefit is demand-side: management cited AI-company and data-center leasing as contributors to the +24% leasing growth.

Competitive position scores 6.3: a defensible top-two franchise with improving fee margins, but no clean evidence of share gain in the recovering lines and one segment (LaSalle) where the position is weakening.

## Risk Assessment

**Cycle leverage (primary).** ~65% of segment Adjusted EBITDA depends on leasing and capital-markets transaction volumes. The FY2023 experience — net income down 66% on flat revenue — is the calibration. Higher long-end yields raise CRE discount rates and debt-service hurdles, which slows transaction closings with a lag. JLL's direct rate exposure is trivial (+50bps = $2.1M of H1 interest); the transmission runs entirely through client transaction volumes. The specific level of long-end Treasury yields, the current 10-year rate, and any FOMC dissent counts are not established in the provided sources and are not asserted here.

**Goodwill concentrated in the most cyclical segment.** Capital Markets carries $2,019.8M of goodwill, 43% of the $4.7B total, versus Leasing Advisory $1,405.3M, REMS $1,225.1M and Investment Management $57.1M. The January 1, 2026 reporting-unit change required a quantitative test, which passed — conducted at a point of accelerating capital-markets recovery, the most favorable possible timing. A renewed freeze would pressure earnings and carrying value simultaneously.

**Agency loss-sharing.** Unpaid principal balance subject to DUS loss-sharing was $26.8B at June 30 (up from $25.8B), with JLL's share generally one-third of incurred losses capped at 20% of original principal per loan. Recorded against it: $29.7M non-contingent guarantee obligation and a $24.0M contingent reserve — roughly 9 basis points of notional. No losses in H1 2026, and the Q2 2025 episode ($14.0M expense, $20.6M finalized loss on three loans) shows losses have been idiosyncratic. The reserve is calibrated to benign multifamily credit; a genuine multifamily credit cycle would test reserve adequacy and origination volumes at once. The exposure does not appear in the leverage ratio.

**Safety incidents.** The 10-K discloses that the company "recently experienced an increase in the number of serious safety incidents, including fatalities of both employees and contractors," with contractors involved in a significant proportion. Given 5.7 billion sq ft under management, 54,600 directly reimbursable field personnel, and a large third-party vendor network, this is a specific liability, reputational and contract-retention exposure. The professional indemnity accrual rose from $1.4M at year-end to $5.6M at June 30 ($3.3M new claims). Small in absolute terms; the direction warrants monitoring.

**Undisclosed customer concentration.** The 10-K flags that deepening large-enterprise relationships "may intensify client concentration risk," where a failure in one service line could jeopardize an entire multi-line relationship. No quantitative concentration disclosure exists — no named customer above 10%, no top-10 percentage. The direction of concentration is unknown, and segment or geographic mix does not establish it.

**Commitments and reporting visibility.** Investments of $873.6M with unfunded commitments rising to $305.5M for Investment Management (from $203.5M), capital calls that must be honored regardless of conditions. The S&T segment folding into REMS removes standalone visibility into an unprofitable technology investment, though subsegment revenue continues to be disclosed.

**Unverified inputs.** Management referenced an "Accelerate 2030" strategy on the Q2 call, but the FY2025 10-K still frames strategy as "Beyond." Two Regulation FD 8-Ks (March 3 and March 12, 2026) plausibly relate. Any multi-year revenue, margin or capital-return target under that framework is unverified and would be a first-order valuation input if it exists. Also unverified: whether adjusted EPS includes or excludes equity earnings (which determines how much of the $58.3M H1 swing flows into guidance), the terms of the May 2025 officer-change 8-K behind the Brennan-to-Howe CFO transition, and the reported completion of the 2019-vintage $1.82B repurchase program.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($500, +37.5%).** The leasing and capital-markets recovery has multi-year duration: near-record-low new office supply, improving net absorption, flight-to-quality bifurcation favoring the largest advisers, and AI/data-center demand adding a new leasing vector. Fee-revenue Adjusted EBITDA margin continues expanding from 15.0% toward the 17–18% range as the commission-tier headwind resets in January and property-management contract exits anniversary out. The four-quarter beat pattern continues, carrying FY2027 adjusted EPS toward $29.50. The buyback retires 3%+ of shares annually from a $2.59B authorization. 17x on $29.50 = $500.

**Bear case ($260, -28.5%).** Long-end yields hold or rise, raising CRE discount rates and stalling the transaction recovery in late 2026. Capital markets and leasing revenue growth decelerate through Q4's tougher comparisons; the equity-earnings tailwind cannot repeat and reverses if private-market marks soften; commission tiers and cost-base rigidity compress fee margin. LaSalle's fee base continues shrinking. The $2.02B of Capital Markets goodwill, tested at a favorable moment, comes under pressure. Buybacks pause to defend the balance sheet given structurally negative first-half operating cash flow. Adjusted EPS falls toward $19–21 and the multiple compresses to 12–13x. The stock revisits its February low of $259.83.

**Base case ($415, +14.1%).** Guidance is met near the upper half of the $24.60–$25.90 range, FY2027 lands close to the $27.76 consensus, fee margin holds its H1 gains without extending them dramatically, LaSalle stays flat, and the buyback provides 2.5–3% of annual share-count reduction. 15.0x FY2027 consensus.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE. Base case $415 (+14.1%), bull $500 (+37.5%), bear $260 (-28.5%). Horizon 12 months.**

*Base case math: 15.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $27.76 = $416, rounded to $415. Bull: 17x on ~$29.50 = $500, matching the analyst high. Bear: 12.5x on ~$21 = $263, near the February 2026 low of $259.83.*

**Staged-entry plan.** Establish roughly half of a target position at $363.69, which is 14.4x guided FY2026 earnings and 13.1x FY2027 consensus — a defensible entry on absolute multiples. Reserve the balance for the $320–$335 range (12.5–13.5x FY2027 consensus), a level the stock traded as recently as mid-July and near which management executed its March accelerated repurchase at $313.29. Cheap near-term implied volatility (30.0% versus 34.7% realized, 9th percentile) makes it inexpensive to hedge the first tranche into the late-October Q3 print rather than wait for a pullback that may not arrive.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
1. FY2027 consensus EPS moves above $29.00 while the price remains below $375, implying a forward multiple under 13x on an accelerating base.
2. Fee-revenue Adjusted EBITDA margin expands again year over year in Q3 (against H1's 15.0%) with capital markets revenue growth sustained in the mid-teens or better, confirming the operating-leverage residual rather than the below-the-line tailwind.
3. The price declines toward $310–$325 with guidance intact, restoring 30%+ upside to base case.

**Downgrade to HOLD if:**
1. Capital markets or leasing revenue growth decelerates below high-single digits for two consecutive quarters, or management trims the mid-teens capital-markets and mid-to-high-teens leasing full-year targets.
2. Fee-revenue Adjusted EBITDA margin contracts year over year in any quarter, indicating the commission-tier and cost-base pressures are structural rather than timing.
3. Equity earnings swing back to a loss position of $20M+ per half, reversing the largest single contributor to the H1 earnings improvement.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. Net debt / Adjusted EBITDA exceeds 1.5x on commercial-paper-funded repurchases while operating cash flow is negative, or the repurchase program is suspended.
2. A goodwill impairment charge is recorded against the $2,019.8M Capital Markets balance, or the DUS contingent loan-loss reserve rises above $60M, either of which would signal that the cycle assumption embedded in the January 2026 impairment test has broken.
3. A quantified customer-concentration or safety-liability disclosure emerges that is material relative to REMS profit.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, expected late October 2026. That print carries the first tougher year-over-year comparison management flagged, the H2 moderation of the commission-tier headwind, and the anniversary point for property-management contract exits. Secondary monitoring: any Regulation FD or 8-K disclosure of "Accelerate 2030" quantitative targets.

## Conclusion

JLL is executing well against a genuine cyclical recovery, and the market has re-rated the shares 32% over twelve months and 13.3% on the Q2 print. The operating improvement is verifiable rather than presentational: 166 basis points of fee-revenue Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in H1, 0.74x net leverage, reaffirmed Baa1/BBB+ ratings, $978.5M of FY2025 free cash flow, and a repurchase program that has moved from $211.5M in FY2025 to $410.0M in six months with $2.59B of remaining authorization equal to 15.5% of market capitalization. At 14.4x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 13.1x FY2027 consensus, the shares convert adjusted earnings to GAAP far more cleanly than CBRE and carry $2.1B of positive tangible equity. Four consecutive beats averaging 12.6% argue that guidance is a floor.

The constraints are equally concrete. Roughly two-thirds of segment Adjusted EBITDA is transaction-cycle dependent, and FY2023 demonstrated that this business can lose 66% of net income on flat revenue. Roughly 43% of the H1 pretax improvement came from below-the-line items led by a $58.3M equity-earnings swing that represents the absence of prior losses rather than a repeatable engine. Leasing growth matched CBRE's exactly, which reads as market recovery rather than share capture. LaSalle is a net seller shrinking its fee base while its markets recover. And this cycle exposure is being capitalized 2.6% below the 52-week high.

That combination of positive expected return, a specific absolute-value argument, and base-case upside of 14% against a 28% cyclical downside the market priced as recently as February supports accumulating on a staged basis rather than initiating a full position at once. The overall score of 6.42 holds the level of the last three assessments; the recommendation steps down from BUY to ACCUMULATE because the price has moved 12% closer to fair value since the prior review while the earnings composition has not improved commensurately.
