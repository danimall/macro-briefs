## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue (~$88.6B FY2025), operating across three segments — Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, and Raytheon — that collectively hold a $268B backlog providing approximately three years of revenue visibility. Free cash flow surged 48% to $7.9B in FY2025, operating margins expanded ~530bp from the 2023 trough, and the company has beaten EPS consensus for four consecutive quarters with an average surprise of 9.6%. The geopolitical backdrop — a proposed $1.5T FY2027 defense budget, $185B Golden Dome missile defense program, and active US military operations in the Gulf — provides demand visibility that is structurally unusual for a company of this scale.

The primary risk concentration has shifted since early 2025. The Powder Metal issue at Pratt & Whitney is moving toward resolution (customer compensation accrual down from $2.8B to $0.7B), but Airbus has escalated to formal arbitration over GTF engine delivery delays, introducing open-ended legal and financial uncertainty. Simultaneously, Executive Order 14372 could restrict capital return programs if defense contractors are deemed to be underperforming on government contracts. At ~29.4x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.84, RTX trades at a premium to traditional defense peers (LMT 22.5x, NOC 19.6x) but at a discount to GE Aerospace (38.3x), reflecting the market's partial recognition of its commercial aerospace growth contribution. We maintain a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $230, representing ~14% upside.

## Company Overview

RTX was formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies' aerospace businesses with Raytheon Company. The resulting entity straddles both commercial aerospace and defense, giving it exposure to two large, independently growing end markets.

**Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B revenue, 34% of total) supplies avionics, aerostructures, interiors, and mission systems across commercial and military platforms, with 16.3% segment margins — the highest of the three segments. Its installed base generates recurring aftermarket revenue with strong pricing power.

**Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B revenue, 37%) produces engines for commercial, military, and business aviation. The GTF engine family (PW1100G for A320neo) is the flagship commercial platform. The F135 engine powers the F-35 Lightning II. Segment margins at 7.9% remain depressed by the Powder Metal remediation but are expanding.

**Raytheon** ($28.0B revenue, 32%) is the premier Western missile and air defense manufacturer. Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk, Stinger, Standard Missile, and LTAMDS/SPY-6 systems constitute franchise positions in programs with multi-decade replacement cycles. Segment margin at 11.5% with room for expansion as fixed-price development program losses roll off.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $88.6B represented 9.7% growth, following 17.1% growth in FY2024 (which included the first full year of normalized P&W operations post the 2023 charge). Organic growth has been broad-based: P&W aftermarket (+$2.9B), Raytheon land/air defense (+$1.6B), Collins commercial aftermarket (+$1.4B), and P&W military (+$1.0B).

Operating margins expanded to 10.5% from 5.2% at the FY2023 trough. Adjusted segment margins reached 11.9% in Q1 2025, up 120bp YoY. The path to 12-13% consolidated operating margins is supported by three drivers: (1) mix shift toward higher-margin aftermarket revenue, which grew 21-28% across segments in recent quarters; (2) roll-off of Powder Metal compensation costs (~$0.7B remaining vs. $2.8B initial accrual); and (3) improved program execution reducing negative EAC adjustments (though $386M in FY2025 shows this remains a work in progress).

Diluted GAAP EPS of $4.96 understates earnings power due to ~$2.0B/year in acquisition-related amortization. Adjusted EPS of $6.88 grew 10% YoY and has compounded at a 12.7% CAGR from $4.27 in 2021.

### Cash Flow

FY2025 operating cash flow of $10.6B (up 48% from $7.2B) was the strongest in company history. After $2.6B in capex, FCF was $7.9B, a 9.0% FCF margin. FCF conversion of net income exceeds 1.0x — high-quality earnings with no accrual concerns. FY2024 was depressed by $1.5B in one-time settlement payments, making the YoY comparison favorable but the underlying trajectory is clearly positive.

Management guided FY2025 FCF at $7.0-7.5B, which was exceeded by $400M-$900M, suggesting conservatism in guidance. FY2026 consensus expects continued cash flow growth, though Powder Metal-related cash payments of ~$0.7B and potential arbitration-related costs could create headwinds.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt declined from $42.5B to $34.5B since 2023, with $3.35B repaid in FY2025 alone. Net debt stands at ~$27.1B. Debt-to-capitalization improved to 36%. Moody's upgraded the outlook to Baa1/stable (March 2025), and S&P affirmed BBB+/stable. The $5.0B undrawn revolver provides adequate liquidity.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.62 superficially flags distress, but this is an artifact of the merger-created balance sheet: $53.3B in goodwill and $31.8B in intangibles inflate total assets to $171B, mechanically depressing asset-based ratios. For a company generating $7.9B in FCF with investment-grade ratings and declining leverage, this metric is not informative.

The $85.1B in goodwill and intangibles (50% of total assets) does represent real risk in a different sense: any impairment would be material. However, qualitative testing passed in 2025, and the demand environment makes a near-term impairment trigger unlikely.

### Capital Allocation

Dividends ($2.72/share annualized, ~1.35% yield) consume approximately $3.7B annually. Buybacks were negligible at $50M in FY2025, with only $0.6B remaining under the 2023 authorization. This prioritization of debt reduction over buybacks is appropriate given the $34.5B debt load, DPA constraints, and the risk that EO 14372 could restrict repurchases. Divestitures of non-core businesses ($3.2B in combined proceeds from Actuation, Simmonds, and CIS) fund deleveraging and portfolio focus.

## Growth Analysis

### Defense Demand Cycle

The current defense spending cycle is the strongest since the end of the Cold War. Three overlapping demand drivers support multi-year growth:

1. **Golden Dome missile defense**: $185B estimated program cost, with $24.4B specifically allocated. RTX is named as a prime contractor alongside LMT and NOC. RTX's portfolio — Patriot, SM-3, sensors, radars, and command/control systems — maps directly to the program's requirements.

2. **Munitions replenishment**: Active US military operations, including the Gulf naval blockade, are consuming munitions inventories of Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk, and Stinger systems that RTX produces. Raytheon booked $40B in defense orders in FY2025, including major awards across these programs.

3. **Budget trajectory**: The proposed $1.5T FY2027 defense budget includes $65B for shipbuilding, substantial missile defense funding, and $25.4B in munitions/supply chain resiliency. European rearmament adds incremental demand, particularly for Patriot systems.

### Commercial Aerospace

P&W's commercial aftermarket grew 28% in Q1 2025 and contributed $2.9B of incremental revenue in FY2025. The GTF installed base continues expanding: new engine orders from Vietjet (44 engines) and Frontier (91 A321neos) extend the long-term aftermarket revenue stream. Collins Aerospace commercial aftermarket grew similarly, benefiting from airline traffic recovery and fleet utilization.

The $161B commercial backlog (60% of total) provides visibility independent of defense spending decisions.

### Estimate Revisions

FY2026 consensus EPS has risen from $6.75 to $6.84 over 90 days (+1.3%), and FY2027 estimates from $7.49 to $7.53 (+0.6%). The revisions are modest but consistently positive. Given RTX's four-quarter beat streak averaging 9.6% surprise, consensus estimates likely remain conservative — particularly for FY2026, where defense demand acceleration from the Gulf conflict and Golden Dome procurement may not yet be fully captured.

## Valuation Assessment

At $201.56, RTX trades at approximately:
- 29.4x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.84
- 26.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $7.53
- 40.6x trailing GAAP EPS (distorted by amortization)
- ~$270B market cap / ~$7.9B FCF = 34x FCF (or ~25x excluding ~$0.7B Powder Metal payments and assuming FY2026 FCF growth to $9B+)

**Peer comparison**: RTX's 29.4x forward P/E sits between the traditional defense primes (LMT 22.5x, NOC 19.6x) and the commercial aerospace-weighted names (GE Aerospace 38.3x, HWM 55.3x). Boeing at 87.5x is not comparable given its negative earnings profile. The peer median of 38.3x suggests RTX trades at a 23% discount to the median A&D comparable, though this is heavily influenced by the GE and HWM outliers.

The premium to LMT and NOC reflects RTX's dual commercial/defense revenue exposure and faster organic growth. A 30-32x multiple on $6.84 FY2026E yields a $205-219 range. If FY2026 adjusted EPS trends toward $7.00+ (consistent with the beat pattern), a 31-33x multiple yields $217-231.

The stock has appreciated 59% over the past year, moving from $127 to $201. At the 52-week low of $112.63 (April 2025), the entry was exceptionally attractive. At current levels, valuation is no longer cheap in absolute terms, but remains reasonable relative to growth, backlog, and peers.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive moat rests on four pillars:

**Installed base lock-in**: Engine and avionics OEM positions generate aftermarket revenue streams lasting 30+ years. A GTF engine sold today locks in maintenance, repair, and overhaul revenue through the 2050s. This recurring revenue carries structurally higher margins than OEM sales.

**Incumbency on franchise defense programs**: Patriot, F135, AMRAAM, and Tomahawk are embedded in US and allied military doctrine. Switching costs are measured in decades and billions of dollars. Raytheon's $107B defense backlog reflects this incumbency.

**Technology barriers**: Geared turbofan propulsion (PW1100G delivers 16-20% fuel savings vs. prior generation), advanced AESA radar (SPY-6), and hypersonic interceptor development create high barriers to entry. These capabilities require billions in R&D investment and decades of institutional knowledge.

**Scale**: $88.6B in revenue and 185,000 employees create procurement leverage, R&D amortization advantages, and the ability to self-fund large development programs. The three-segment structure provides internal diversification that pure-play competitors lack.

## Risk Assessment

### Airbus Arbitration (HIGH)

Airbus launched formal arbitration proceedings (March 2026) seeking damages over P&W GTF engine delivery delays that grounded hundreds of A320neo aircraft. The financial exposure is difficult to quantify — Airbus's claim could range from production line disruption costs to lost aircraft delivery revenue. The $0.7B remaining customer compensation accrual may prove insufficient if arbitration results in an adverse ruling. This risk is not yet fully reflected in consensus estimates.

### Executive Order 14372 and Capital Return Restrictions (MEDIUM-HIGH)

The January 2026 Executive Order allows the Secretary of Defense to restrict dividends and buybacks for defense contractors deemed to be underperforming. Proposed legislation (Hawley-Warren bill, DFARS implementation 2026) could codify these limits. For RTX, the immediate buyback impact is minimal ($0.6B remaining authorization), but dividend restrictions would affect the $3.7B annual payout and the stock's yield-oriented investor base.

### Fixed-Price Contract Risk (MEDIUM)

$386M in negative EAC adjustments in FY2025 demonstrates ongoing cost pressure on fixed-price development programs. While this is down from the $0.6B Raytheon Contract Termination charge in 2024, the risk persists across multiple programs. Inflation and supply chain disruptions amplify this exposure.

### Tariff Uncertainty (MEDIUM)

RTX imports components and materials subject to US tariffs, and suppliers face similar exposure. Management excluded tariff impacts from FY2025 guidance, deferring to the Q1 earnings call. If 50% China tariffs are implemented, supply chain costs for electronics, materials, and subcomponents could increase, though defense programs may receive pass-through allowances.

### Legal and Compliance Overhang (MEDIUM)

Two active DPAs (FCPA, defective pricing), an SEC order, and a State Department consent agreement create ongoing compliance costs and constrain strategic flexibility. Breach of any agreement could trigger criminal prosecution. An independent compliance monitor is expected by end of Q1 2026.

### China Sanctions (LOW-MEDIUM)

China has sanctioned Raytheon for Taiwan arms sales. This creates uncertainty for RTX's commercial aerospace business in the Chinese market, though China revenue is a small portion of total sales.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.6% exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 25.4% by 6.2pp, pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (31.6% near-term vs. 28.4% at 6 months), consistent with imminent event pricing — likely reflecting the broader Gulf blockade environment and potential tariff impacts. Put/call skew of 8.3% at the 1-week tenor shows elevated put demand, though the OI put/call ratio of 0.70 is relatively balanced. IV compressed from 36.6% to 31.6% over the past five days as the stock declined 1.3%, suggesting partial resolution of near-term uncertainty, though levels remain elevated. The options market is pricing ~2.7% weekly and ~6.5% monthly implied moves, reflecting genuine uncertainty about near-term catalysts (Gulf operations, tariff clarity, potential Q2 preview).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($240-250)

Defense spending acceleration exceeds current consensus estimates. Golden Dome procurement awards begin flowing in H2 2026, adding $5-10B+ to Raytheon's backlog. GTF Powder Metal remediation is completed ahead of schedule, restoring P&W margins to 10%+. Airbus arbitration settles for less than the remaining $0.7B accrual. Adjusted EPS reaches $7.50+ in FY2026, and the market applies a 32-33x multiple reflecting the durability of the growth profile. FCF exceeds $9B, enabling resumed buybacks once debt targets are met.

### Bear Case ($165-175)

Airbus arbitration results in a multi-billion dollar adverse ruling, forcing a material charge that resets earnings expectations. EO 14372 is broadly enforced, restricting dividends and signaling political risk for the sector. Tariffs compress margins on commercial programs. Fixed-price development losses escalate. The stock de-rates to 24-25x a lower EPS base of $6.50-6.70, representing ~15-18% downside from current levels.

### Base Case ($230)

Adjusted EPS of $7.00 in FY2026 (above $6.84 consensus, consistent with beat pattern) × 33x multiple = $231. FCF grows to $8.5-9.0B. Backlog remains above $260B. Powder Metal costs continue declining. Airbus arbitration is protracted but doesn't produce a catastrophic ruling. Defense budget trajectory supports sustained high-single-digit organic growth at Raytheon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target**: $230 — derived from 33x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $7.00 (consensus $6.84 + expected beat). Represents ~14% upside from $201.56.

**Bull case target**: $240-250 — 33x FY2026E of $7.50+ if defense procurement accelerates and P&W margins normalize. ~19-24% upside.

**Bear case target**: $165-175 — 25x reduced FY2026E of $6.50-6.70 on arbitration loss + margin compression. ~13-18% downside.

**Upside/downside**: Base +14%, bull +19-24%, bear -13-18%. Risk/reward approximately 1:1 on base case, improving to 1.5:1 on bull.

**Timeframe**: 12 months. Key catalysts: Q1 FY2026 earnings (late April 2026), Golden Dome contract awards (H2 2026), Airbus arbitration timeline (12-24 months), FY2027 defense budget passage.

**Thesis invalidation triggers**:
1. Airbus arbitration ruling exceeding $2B in damages or requiring fundamental restructuring of the GTF program
2. FY2026 FCF falling below $7.0B (would indicate operational deterioration beyond Powder Metal remediation costs)
3. Defense backlog declining below $95B, signaling loss of competitive position or program cancellations

## Conclusion

RTX offers dual exposure to the strongest defense spending cycle in decades and a recovering commercial aerospace aftermarket, underpinned by a $268B backlog that provides multi-year revenue visibility. FCF generation has inflected sharply, the balance sheet is improving, and estimates are trending upward. The four-quarter consecutive beat streak with average 9.6% surprise suggests consensus remains conservative.

The stock's 59% appreciation over the past year has compressed the margin of safety. At ~29x forward earnings, RTX is no longer cheap in absolute terms, and the Airbus arbitration introduces genuine uncertainty to the earnings trajectory. Elevated put skew in options markets reflects this concern.

On balance, the structural demand drivers — Golden Dome, munitions replenishment, commercial aftermarket growth — outweigh the quantifiable risks. The BUY recommendation is maintained with a $230 base case target, recognizing that the most favorable entry point was the $112-130 range in April-May 2025. Position sizing should reflect the reduced margin of safety at current levels versus the prior recommendation.
