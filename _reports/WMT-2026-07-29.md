**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Walmart is the clearest large-cap example of a scale retailer converting physical and digital reach into higher-margin ancillary revenue. The investment case rests on a margin-mix shift: advertising (Walmart Connect), membership (Walmart+, Sam's Club fee hikes), and a growing third-party marketplace scaling faster than the low-margin retail base, structurally lifting a ~4% operating margin over time. The evidence supports the thesis: gross margin rose to 24.2% in FY26 from 23.7% in FY24, membership and other income reached $6.75B, and Q1 FY27 net sales reaccelerated to +7.1% ($175.7B) with eCommerce up ~26% and now improving on margin. EPS climbed from $1.91 (FY24) to $2.73 (FY26), and Q1 FY27 delivered $0.67 vs $0.56.

The problem for new capital is price. At $113 on ~$2.73 trailing EPS and $2.90 consensus current-year EPS, Walmart trades near 39x forward, a premium to its Consumer Staples merchandise-retail peer group median of ~18.5x and roughly 16% above where the stock changed hands in mid-2025 (~$98 in late July 2025). The margin-transformation story is real and broad-based, but it is also well understood by the Street, which is why the multiple is where it is. The stock is down ~16% from its February 2026 high of ~$135, but consensus estimates have been essentially flat over that window (current-year EPS revised -0.5% over 90 days), so the pullback reflects multiple compression rather than fundamental deterioration. That improves the entry marginally without creating a genuine mispricing.

We rate WMT a HOLD (growth_hold). The business is a fortress with a credible multi-year margin re-rating underway, and we expect modest appreciation as advertising and membership income compound. But at 39x forward with mid-single-digit sales growth and low-single-digit operating leverage, the multiple already discounts much of that path. This continues a string of HOLD ratings; the through-cycle thesis has not changed, and the current price does not offer the asymmetry required to upgrade.

## Company Overview

Walmart operates 10,955 units across the U.S., Africa, Canada, Central America, Chile, China, India, and Mexico, plus eCommerce platforms, reporting in three segments:

- **Walmart U.S.** (~68% of net sales, $483.0B FY26): supercenters plus omnichannel eCommerce, the highest-margin core segment at ~5.2% operating margin, roughly 60% grocery.
- **Walmart International** (~19%, $130.4B FY26): Mexico/Central America, China, Canada, Flipkart/PhonePe (India), Chile, Africa. Fastest-growing (+7.0% FY26; +18% in Q1 FY27, FX-aided).
- **Sam's Club U.S.** (~13%, $93.0B FY26): membership warehouse clubs, ~$1.8B operating income ex-fuel.

The strategic frame is an "interconnected ecosystem": retail plus membership, a third-party marketplace, retail-media advertising (Walmart Connect), fulfillment/data services, health & wellness, and the OnePay fintech venture. Competitive advantages are unmatched scale (~$706B net sales), everyday-low-price positioning that gains share in inflationary environments, ~90% of the U.S. population within 10 miles of a store enabling store-fulfilled delivery, and AA/Aa2 credit that lowers the cost of capital.

## Financial Analysis

Growth and quality are both improving. Net sales moved from $605.9B (FY23) to $706.4B (FY26), roughly 4.7% annualized, and Q1 FY27 (ended Apr 30, 2026) grew 7.1%. eCommerce is the growth engine, contributing +5.2 points to Walmart U.S. comps, with management noting improving eCommerce margins as fulfillment density rises.

The margin-mix shift is visible in the numbers. Management repeatedly credits "higher-margin businesses, including advertising" for gross-profit gains, the crux of the bull case. Net income advanced to $21.9B (FY26) from $11.7B (FY23); diluted EPS $2.73 (FY26) vs $2.41 (FY25).

Profitability is high on returns despite a thin operating margin. ROE is 22.4%, ROA 8.2%, ROI ~15%, a volume/velocity model with 2.48x asset turnover. The operating margin is structurally ~4.2%, which magnifies cost shocks: FY26 operating margin slipped 13bps on ~$0.9B higher self-insured general-liability claims, a $0.7B PhonePe share-comp charge, and higher depreciation. Q1 FY27 opex/sales rose 33bps on depreciation, ~$0.2B reorganization charges, and medical-cost inflation. At 4% margins, these line items matter more than at a typical S&P company.

The balance sheet is a fortress: AA (S&P/Fitch), Aa2 (Moody's), total debt ~$41B against $99.6B equity, Altman Z-Score 3.43. The 2.86x screener "D/E" and 0.79 current ratio are artifacts of a supplier-financed working-capital-deficit model ($22.6B deficit at FY26, ~$26.2B at Q1 FY27, funded by ~$63B of payables plus ~$5.9B of supplier-financing-program obligations), not a distress signal. Real leverage is low.

Cash flow: FY26 operating cash flow was $41.6B; FCF $14.9B. Capex is climbing, from $20.6B (FY24) to $26.6B (FY26), guided $25–27B FY27, as automation, supply chain, and tech investment intensify. Q1 FY27 FCF was negative $1.9B, driven by seasonal inventory build plus a $1.7B capex increase; this fits the seasonal pattern rather than signaling a problem, but the multi-year capex ramp is the item to watch, because ROI has ticked down to ~14.9% and the returns thesis depends on the automation spend converting to margin.

Capital allocation is disciplined. The dividend was raised to $0.99/share (FY27), $7.5B paid in FY26. Buybacks stepped up to $8.1B in FY26 at an average $95.13, below the current price. A new $30B authorization was approved February 2026 ($28.2B remaining), but the pace slowed to $2.1B in Q1 FY27 as the stock ran to $125+, which is the right instinct.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth is in the higher-margin ancillary businesses, not the retail base. Retail net sales grow mid-single digits, but advertising, membership, and marketplace grow faster and carry structurally higher incremental margins. Three concrete drivers support the next 12 months:

1. **Advertising (Walmart Connect)**: retail-media revenue grounded in grocery traffic, with an Amazon-ads-style margin profile. External Street coverage frames this as the key margin-expansion lever, and the filings repeatedly credit it for gross-profit gains.
2. **Membership**: the Sam's Club fee hike effective May 1, 2026 ($50→$60 Club; $110→$120 Plus) flows through membership income over the coming year as a high-margin, recurring revenue lift, on top of Walmart+ growth.
3. **eCommerce profitability inflection**: ~26% growth with improving margins converts a former drag into a contributor.

Earnings-surprise history is unremarkable: 2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss over four quarters, including a -9.2% miss in the quarter reported Sep 2025, so consensus visibility is decent but not a source of upside surprise. Estimate revisions are flat: current-year EPS $2.90 (-0.5% over 90 days), next-year $3.28 (flat). The strong estimate-revision buy signal that would justify overriding a premium multiple is absent. Growth is real and broad-based, but the pace is mid-single-digit sales with modest operating leverage.

Optionality that is not in the numbers: Walmart paid IEEPA tariffs as importer of record and is now claiming refunds through the CBP process; nothing is booked, so any recovery would be incremental upside.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the case for new capital weakens. The peer group — Costco 49.1x, Target 17.5x, Dollar General 16.2x, Dollar Tree 18.5x, BJ's 20.9x, median ~18.5x — puts Walmart at roughly a 110% premium to the median. Costco is the only peer at a comparable multiple, and it carries a similar quality/membership premium.

The premium is defensible on quality (AA credit, 22.4% ROE, structural share gains) and on the margin-mix re-rating. But at 39x with ~5–7% sales growth and modest operating leverage, the PEG is well above 1.5, so the multiple already prices in the transformation. Analyst targets — mean $138, median $140, high $155, low $81 — imply ~22% upside to the mean, but on a stock at 39x forward with flat estimate revisions, those targets reflect the same margin-expansion optimism embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. On a name this richly multiplied with stable-to-declining revisions, we treat targets as consensus extrapolation, not a floor.

A "cheap on low P/E" argument does not apply here — the multiple is elevated, not compressed — but the mirror-image caution holds: a premium multiple with no near-term revision momentum is vulnerable to any disappointment, because beats are expected (no re-rating) while a miss derates. Fair value on ~$3.28 next-year EPS at a still-premium 32–34x lands around $105–112, close to the current price. We score valuation 4.5, marginally improved by the ~16% pullback but still without the asymmetry that a higher score requires.

## Competitive Landscape

Walmart's competitive position is stable-to-improving, which is the relevant trajectory. It is gaining eCommerce share (growth faster than the market), consolidating grocery share as higher-income shoppers trade down, and building a genuine omnichannel moat from ~90% population proximity that enables store-fulfilled delivery and pickup at costs pure-play eCommerce cannot match. The differentiated angle is monetizing physical and digital scale into retail media, marketplace, and fulfillment-as-a-service, an Amazon-style high-margin flywheel grounded in grocery traffic.

The primary structural threat, newly and explicitly expanded in the FY26 risk factors, is "agentic" AI shopping platforms that could route product search and discovery around Walmart's owned channels, reducing store traffic and cross-sell. This is a legitimate long-term disintermediation risk that argues against maximum conviction on the moat, but it is a multi-year threat rather than a near-term earnings driver, and Walmart's scale and its own AI investment provide partial defense. Competition against Amazon (eCommerce/logistics/ads), Costco (clubs), Target, Kroger, and the dollar stores is intense but familiar, and Walmart is holding or gaining in most fronts.

## Risk Assessment

Because solvency is not in question (Z-Score 3.43, AA credit), the risk work centers on execution and legal/regulatory exposure.

- **Tariffs / trade policy** is the most acute macro risk. Management flags a continuing "dynamic tariff environment" into FY27; incremental import tariffs impacted FY26. Less than one-third of U.S. merchandise is imported (China, Vietnam, Mexico, India, Canada), bounding but not eliminating exposure.
- **Margin thinness magnifies cost shocks.** At ~4% operating margin, self-insured claims, share-comp charges, reorganization costs, and medical inflation each move the needle, as FY26 and Q1 FY27 both demonstrated.
- **Capex conversion.** The ramp to $25–27B FY27 must produce the promised automation/AI productivity and margin. ROI already slipped to ~14.9%; if the spend does not convert, returns could stall while the multiple is elevated.
- **Legal aggregate (slow-burn tail).** Opioid MDL (~130 cases pending; a directed verdict in Walmart's favor in the Florida Health Sciences case in May 2026 is a modest positive), DOJ opioid civil trial Nov 2027, Asda UK equal-value claims (~73,000 claimants, Phase 3 Nov 2026), FTC Spark $100M judgment (~$37M accrued), Mexico/India antitrust and FDI matters, EPA refrigerant finding. None individually material-and-estimable, collectively a persistent headline drag.
- **Sam's Club fuel and eCommerce fulfillment drag** (fuel swings comps ±2pts; club-fulfilled delivery pressured Sam's gross margin -26bps in Q1 FY27).

We score risk 6.0: the balance sheet is bulletproof, but the AI-disintermediation threat, capex-conversion uncertainty at ~14.9% ROI, and the multi-front legal aggregate are genuine idiosyncratic exposures that are only partly priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 31.6% against 24.6% one-year historical volatility, rich by ~7 points, with the term structure flat (near ≈ far ~31.8%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single catalyst. The put/call OI ratio ticked up to 1.09 and near-dated put/call skew is a modestly elevated 3.7%, signaling some downside hedging demand, though the 3-month tenor shows negative skew (call demand) at a 2.25 put/call volume ratio. ATM IV rose 7.7 points since early July on a roughly flat price, pricing more uncertainty into the next print without a directional lean. Net: options positioning is mildly cautious on the short end but not a strong directional signal, consistent with a balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$140–155):** The margin-mix transformation accelerates. Walmart Connect advertising and membership income compound at high incremental margins, the Sam's fee hike flows through, eCommerce margin keeps improving, and the automation capex delivers productivity gains that lift operating margin toward 5%. On $3.28+ FY27 EPS the multiple holds at ~40–45x on the strength of the re-rating story, and a tariff-refund recovery adds incremental upside. This is the analyst-target zone ($138 mean, $155 high).

**Bear Case (~$81–95):** The premium multiple compresses toward the peer range as sales growth stays mid-single-digit, operating leverage disappoints, and capex fails to convert to margin (ROI drifts below 14%). A cost shock (tariffs, self-insurance, or medical inflation) pressures the thin operating margin, and AI-shopping disintermediation begins to show in traffic. A derating to 30x on ~$3.00 EPS implies ~$90; the analyst low of $81 reflects a deeper multiple reset. The asymmetry of a 39x multiple with flat revisions is unfavorable: beats are expected, misses derate.

The base case sits between these: modest appreciation driven by earnings growth with a stable-to-slightly-compressing multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold), 12-month horizon.**

- **Base case target: ~$118.** 36x on ~$3.28 next-year EPS. This reflects earnings growth of ~13% partially offset by mild multiple compression from 39x toward the mid-30s as the re-rating story matures. Upside ~4% from $113.
- **Bull case target: ~$145** (~28% upside). ~44x on $3.28 EPS as the margin-mix re-rating sustains a premium multiple, plus tariff-refund optionality.
- **Bear case target: ~$90** (~20% downside). ~30x on ~$3.00 EPS as multiple compresses toward peers on capex-conversion disappointment or a cost shock.
- **Timeframe:** Next 2–4 quarters, anchored to the Q2 FY27 print and the trajectory of advertising/membership income disclosure.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward P/E compresses below ~30x (a pullback toward $95–100) while estimate revisions stay flat-to-positive, restoring asymmetry.
- Advertising/membership income shows accelerating disclosed growth that lifts operating margin above ~4.5%, with current-year EPS revisions turning positive by >2%.
- A confirmed, booked tariff-refund recovery that adds a discrete EPS step-up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating margin compresses year-over-year for two consecutive quarters as capex fails to convert (ROI below 14%).
- eCommerce growth decelerates below ~10% or reverts to margin drag.
- Current-year EPS estimates revised down by more than ~5% on a demand or cost shock.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY27 earnings, plus the Asda Phase 3 hearing (Nov 2026) and any further senior-executive turnover following the Furner/McMillon transition.

## Conclusion

Walmart is a high-quality fortress executing a genuine, broad-based margin-mix transformation: advertising, membership, and marketplace lifting a thin retail margin while eCommerce turns profitable and sales reaccelerate. The trajectory data shows a modestly declining score (6.6 in May to 6.1 now), driven mainly by the normalization of Management Quality (from a backward-looking halo of 8.0 to 5.5 after the CEO succession and routine insider selling) and Competitive Position (from 9.0 to 6.8 as the AI-disintermediation threat became explicit in the filings). The current data confirms rather than reverses that reset: the business is excellent, the leadership handoff to Furner is an orderly continuity succession, and the fundamentals are intact and improving.

What holds the rating at HOLD rather than BUY is price. At ~39x forward with mid-single-digit sales growth, flat estimate revisions, and an absent rapid-revision buy signal, the stock prices in most of the margin re-rating it is delivering. The ~16% pullback from the February high improves the entry marginally but does not create the asymmetry an upgrade requires, and the premium-multiple risk profile is unfavorable: beats are expected, misses derate. We remain HOLD with a bias to add on a pullback toward $95–100, where forward P/E would compress below 30x and the risk/reward would turn genuinely attractive.
