**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Saia is a national less-than-truckload (LTL) freight carrier that spent the past three years building capacity ahead of demand, most aggressively after the 2023 collapse of Yellow Corporation freed up terminals, real estate, and share. The company invested more than $2.5 billion over five years, opening terminals (213 at year-end 2025, 216 by mid-2026) into a freight recession. The result is a timing mismatch: revenue was roughly flat in 2025 (+0.8% to $3.23B) while operating income fell 27% to $352M and the operating ratio deteriorated from 85.0% to 89.1%. The margin compression was driven by depreciation, headcount, and start-up drag from new terminals landing on a flat revenue base, not by lost customers or pricing erosion. Revenue per shipment ex-fuel rose 1.2% in 2025, confirming pricing discipline held.

The investment question is whether the operating leverage Saia built into its network is about to reappear. Two levers are turning simultaneously. Capex is falling from ~$1,041M (2024) to $544M (2025) to guided $350–400M (2026), which sets up a free-cash-flow inflection against ~$248M of annual D&A. Volumes appear to be recovering: April/May 2026 tonnage per workday rose +6.9%/+8.4% versus the -2.1% posted in Q1, with QTD shipments +4.6%. If both persist, a largely fixed-cost national network filling with incremental freight is the setup that should drive the operating ratio back toward the mid-80s and produce disproportionate operating income growth. Consensus estimates are rising in line with this thesis: current-year EPS revised +7.9% and next-year +6.2% over 90 days.

The problem is price. At $423.68 the stock trades at roughly 45x trailing and ~37x current-year EPS, with the shares down about 14% from a 52-week high of $494.71 but still up ~36% over the trailing year. The recovery is substantially priced in. The operating ratio has not yet confirmed the turn (Q1 2026 OR deteriorated further to 91.7%), self-insured health and claims inflation is a persistent non-cyclical drag, and insiders show no open-market conviction buying. We rate SAIA a HOLD (growth_hold): the earnings-recovery thesis is real and improving, but the valuation demands the operating ratio actually bend before the risk/reward turns favorable at this level.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1924 and headquartered in Johns Creek, Georgia, Saia derives roughly 97% of revenue from transporting LTL shipments (100–10,000 lb) across the 48 contiguous states, with brokered truckload, expedited, and logistics services rounding out the offering. As of year-end 2025 the company operated 213 terminals, ~7,700 tractors, and ~26,500 trailers with a union-free workforce of ~14,500.

Saia is one of a small group of national LTL carriers. The LTL industry is structurally more attractive than truckload because of high barriers to entry: the capital required to build a dense terminal network, revenue equipment, and technology, plus lower driver turnover and more pricing discipline than the fragmented truckload market. Saia's differentiators are service quality (on-time performance, low cargo claims), a non-union cost and flexibility advantage, and the national network it built out following Yellow's exit. In January 2024 Saia acquired 17 owned and 11 leased Yellow terminals out of bankruptcy and has added greenfield terminals since. That land-grab is the central lens through which the current financials must be read: the earnings depression of 2024–2025 is the cost of expansion, incurred during a freight downturn, ahead of the revenue it was meant to generate.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trend shows aggressive expansion colliding with a soft freight economy:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2.79B | $2.88B | $3.21B | $3.23B (+0.8%) |
| Operating income | $470M | $460M | $482M | $352M (−27%) |
| Operating ratio | ~83% | ~84% | 85.0% | 89.1% |
| Net income | $357M | $355M | $362M | $255M (−30%) |
| Net PP&E | $1.48B | $1.76B | $2.56B | $2.84B |
| CapEx (net) | — | $437M | $1,041M | $544M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $27M |

The 2025 margin compression traces to costs outrunning flat revenue. Salaries, wages, and benefits rose $91.9M (including $37.3M of group health inflation, a 3% October 2025 and 4.1% July 2024 wage increase, and higher headcount from new terminals). Depreciation and amortization rose $38.5M (+18.3%) from the equipment, terminal, and technology buildout. Claims and insurance rose $15.1M. Revenue grew only $25.2M because pricing gains (general rate increases of 7.9% in 2024 and 5.9% in 2025) were largely offset by slightly lower shipment volumes. Fixed and semi-fixed costs were added faster than the freight to absorb them.

The balance sheet is conservative in absolute terms. Equity stood at $2.58B against total funded debt of ~$164M at year-end 2025, putting actual funded debt/equity near 6%, well below the utility-like leverage that an asset-heavy transport network could carry. The screen's higher D/E figure reflects total liabilities including operating leases, not funded debt. The company operates within its 3.50x maximum leverage covenant with substantial headroom and roughly $500M of revolver availability. Free cash flow was minimal in 2025 ($27M) only because of the capex surge, not because of any cash-generation weakness in the operating business; operating cash flow remains robust.

Management guided 2026 net capex to $350–400M, and Q1 2026 capex was just $63.7M versus $202M a year earlier. With D&A running ~$248M annually, the drop in gross capital spending should translate into a material free-cash-flow inflection in 2026 even before any margin recovery.

Q1 2026 showed the pressure had not yet abated. Revenue rose 2.4% to $806M, but almost entirely on fuel surcharge (16.5% of revenue versus 15.1% prior year); tonnage was -2.1% and revenue per shipment ex-fuel fell 1.2%. The operating ratio deteriorated further to 91.7% from 91.1%. EPS was flat at $1.86, a 0.4% beat versus the $1.85 estimate. Operating cash flow rose to $139.6M. The quarter is the low point the recovery thesis has to escape from.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with a large 24.2% beat in Q3 2025 offset by an 8.8% miss in Q4 2025 and only marginal beats since. This signals volatile near-term visibility rather than a clean beat streak, appropriate for a company at a cyclical hinge point.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the June 2, 2026 operating-data 8-K, which shows a sharp volume reversal:

| | Apr 2026 vs Apr 2025 | May 2026 vs May 2025 | QTD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shipments/workday | +5.6% | +3.7% | +4.6% |
| Tonnage/workday | +6.9% | +8.4% | +7.6% |
| Weight/shipment | +1.3% | +4.5% | +2.9% |

This is a decisive turn from Q1's -2.1% tonnage. Against a built-out, largely fixed-cost network, incremental volume is precisely what Saia invested for: small revenue gains drop disproportionately to operating income as the operating ratio normalizes. BofA frames the setup as a national network under-earning at the cycle trough, positioned for share gains into a trucking upcycle.

Estimate revisions corroborate the momentum without reaching extremes: current-quarter EPS revised +12.4% over 90 days to $3.39, current-year +7.9% to $11.46, and next-year +6.2% to $14.55. These are steady, positive revisions consistent with a recovering cyclical, not the +30%+ velocity that would flag a trough-to-inflection re-rating on cheap forward earnings. The distinction matters because the stock is not cheap on those rising estimates. It trades at ~37x current-year EPS, so rising estimates carry a different risk profile than rising estimates on a compressed multiple: beats are largely expected, and misses would be punished.

The durability of the growth depends on the freight cycle turning up, which is macro-dependent. LTL demand tracks non-service sectors of the economy, and Saia's own filings note that tariff and trade-policy volatility had already reduced demand. Two months of strong volume data is encouraging but not yet a confirmed cyclical bottom. The company's structural share position after Yellow's exit gives it a durable multi-year growth runway; the near-term slope of that curve is uncertain and depends on whether the volume trend holds through Q3.

## Valuation Assessment

On peer comparables, SAIA looks reasonable in isolation but the peer group is distorted by trough earnings across the sector. The Cargo Ground Transportation median P/E is 68.2 (Knight-Swift at 393.7, Landstar 62.2, Schneider 68.2, all reflecting depressed cyclical earnings), so a headline multiple comparison is not meaningful. The relevant benchmark for a high-quality LTL asset is the national/super-regional LTL cohort, which historically trades at premium multiples to the broader trucking group.

On forward earnings, SAIA trades at ~37x current-year consensus ($11.46) and ~29x next-year ($14.55). Even crediting the operating-leverage recovery, this is a full multiple. The implied next-year EPS growth of ~27% ($11.46 to $14.55) puts the PEG near 1.1x on next-year earnings, not extreme but not cheap either. The market is already paying for the recovery it expects the operating ratio to deliver.

The core tension: for the stock to work from $423.68, the operating ratio has to compress meaningfully toward the mid-80s. A return to an 85% OR on 2026 revenue would restore roughly $130M+ of operating income versus 2025, and on continued volume growth that supports the $14–15 EPS the bull case requires. If that plays out, ~29x next-year is defensible for a structurally advantaged franchise. If the OR stalls in the high-80s to low-90s while volumes recover only modestly, EPS lands below $13 and the multiple looks stretched.

The valuation dimension scores below neutral here not because the business is low-quality but because the current price offers little asymmetry. The market has already extrapolated the recovery. Historically, paying premium multiples for cyclicals at the point where the recovery is consensus rather than contrarian has been a poor entry, and the setup here fits that pattern: the volume trend is now widely reported and analyst targets have moved up (median $490, high $550) to reflect it. The consensus low of $295 (a ~30% drawdown) frames the downside if the freight recovery disappoints and the OR fails to bend.

## Competitive Landscape

Saia's competitive position is improving structurally and stable-to-improving operationally. The Yellow bankruptcy permanently removed a large LTL competitor, tightening industry capacity and creating an opportunity to acquire terminals and share. Saia was among the most aggressive players in that land-grab and now owns a national network it did not have at comparable density five years ago. The 2025 revenue softness reflects a sector-wide freight recession, not company-specific share loss, evidenced by ex-fuel revenue per shipment rising, which shows pricing power was maintained.

The competitive set includes national/super-regional LTLs — Old Dominion, XPO, Estes, ArcBest, and TForce — several with greater scale. Closing the operating-ratio gap to the industry's margin leaders on a mature network is the multi-year value-creation path. Saia's non-union cost structure is a genuine advantage, though expansion into new geographies and the absorption of former Yellow (Teamster) facilities raises unionization risk over time. Service quality (on-time performance, low cargo claims) remains a differentiator in an industry where reliability drives customer retention.

Saia has more capacity, more terminals, and a broader national footprint than at any prior point, positioned to capture disproportionate volume as the cycle turns. The moat is the terminal network's replacement cost and density, which a new entrant cannot easily replicate.

## Risk Assessment

**Expansion execution / operating-leverage risk (central risk).** Saia explicitly warns that a delay between expansion outlays and new revenue could hurt the business and that it may experience decreased profitability "until we are able to fully realize the benefits of the investment, if ever." 2025 is that period playing out in real time. The entire thesis rides on volumes filling the new capacity. The June data suggests it is beginning, but a macro relapse would strand fixed costs and keep the operating ratio elevated.

**Self-insurance / claims volatility.** Saia self-insures auto liability, workers' comp, cargo, and group health. Claims and insurance rose $15.1M in 2025; group health rose $37.3M in 2025 and another ~$7.9M in Q1 2026. Rising jury verdicts and insurers exiting trucking coverage are industry headwinds that hit Saia's self-insured layer directly. This is a persistent, non-cyclical margin drag that will partly offset volume-driven leverage regardless of the cycle.

**Cyclicality / macro sensitivity.** The business is closely correlated with non-service sectors of the economy, and tariff/trade-policy volatility is called out as already having decreased demand. Two months of strong volume data does not confirm a durable cyclical bottom, and a macro relapse would test freight demand durability.

**Non-union status.** A key cost advantage that expansion into new geographies and absorption of former Yellow facilities could erode by raising unionization risk. Loss of non-union status would damage the core cost model.

**Yellow assumed liabilities.** Terminals acquired as-is from Yellow's bankruptcy carry assumed environmental and health/safety liabilities that could exceed expectations.

**Driver regulation.** New FMCSA English-proficiency enforcement and non-domiciled CDL restrictions (2025–February 2026) are shrinking the eligible driver pool and could push wages higher.

For a holder, the rich near-term vol argues against chasing and favors patience around the upcoming print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Two levers turn together: falling capex produces a free-cash-flow inflection while volumes recover. The largely fixed-cost national network built after Yellow's exit fills with incremental freight, the operating ratio compresses from 89.1% toward the mid-80s, and operating income grows disproportionately. Pricing discipline holds (ex-fuel revenue per shipment already rising), and Saia captures share into an LTL upcycle. Next-year EPS reaches $14.55 or higher, and the market rewards the franchise with a low-30s multiple. Bull case: ~$550, roughly +30% from current, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear case.** The freight recovery proves shallow or reverses under macro pressure. The operating ratio stalls in the high-80s to low-90s despite modest volume gains because self-insured health/claims inflation and wage growth offset the leverage. The recent volume gains prove to be seasonal noise rather than a cyclical bottom. EPS lands below $13, and a ~37x forward multiple compresses. Bear case: ~$295, roughly -30% from current, matching the low analyst target.

**Base case.** Volumes recover gradually, the operating ratio improves modestly toward the mid-to-high 80s over 2026–2027, and EPS reaches roughly $12.50–13.50 for the current year with continued growth. The stock re-rates only slightly because the recovery is already priced. Base case: ~$465, roughly +10% from current, near the consensus mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$465** (roughly +10% from $423.68). Reasoning: applying a premium multiple to current-year EPS of ~$11.46–12.50 assumes the operating ratio begins to bend but the recovery is largely reflected in the current multiple. This sits near the consensus mean of $464.86 and between the bear and bull scenarios.

**Bull case target: ~$550** (+30%). Requires the operating ratio to compress toward the mid-80s and next-year EPS to reach ~$14.55+.

**Bear case target: ~$295** (-30%). The freight recovery disappoints, the OR fails to improve, and the premium multiple compresses.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months**, keyed to quarterly operating-ratio prints and the monthly operating-data releases. The next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) is the near-term catalyst that tests whether the April/May volume gains converted into margin recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating ratio improves sequentially to the high-80s or better in Q2 2026, confirming the volume trend is translating to margin recovery.
- Sustained tonnage growth of +5% or better for a full quarter with revenue per shipment ex-fuel positive, demonstrating the cycle turn plus pricing power together.
- A pullback that compresses the forward P/E below ~30x on rising estimates, restoring margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating ratio deteriorates or stalls above 90% into H2 2026 despite volume growth, indicating fixed costs are not being absorbed and the leverage thesis has failed.
- Tonnage growth reverses back toward flat or negative, signaling the volume recovery was seasonal noise rather than a cyclical bottom.
- A material step-up in self-insured claims/health inflation or an unfavorable unionization development that structurally impairs the cost model.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release and the intervening monthly operating-data 8-Ks, which provide near-real-time confirmation of the volume trend.

## Conclusion

Saia is a high-quality, structurally advantaged LTL franchise at the trough of a self-inflicted invest-ahead-of-demand cycle. The 27–30% earnings decline in 2025 is almost entirely growth spending — depreciation, headcount, and terminal start-up drag — hitting during a flat-revenue freight recession, not competitive erosion. The setup is improving: capex is falling sharply, volumes inflected positively in April/May 2026, estimates are rising, and the management compensation structure (CEO comp-actually-paid collapsed to $1.45M from $7.4M as operating income fell) demonstrates real alignment. The recovery thesis is credible.

The reservation is price. At ~37x current-year and ~29x next-year earnings, the stock has already priced most of the recovery, and the operating ratio — the single most important metric — has not yet confirmed the turn. There is no open-market insider conviction buying to signal a bottom, near-term options vol is rich and in backwardation ahead of the next print, and self-insured health/claims inflation is a persistent non-cyclical drag on the leverage story. The trajectory of scores has drifted up modestly (5.1 to 5.85) alongside the improving operating data, and the current analysis confirms rather than reverses that direction, but the valuation keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric.

We rate SAIA a **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The 12-month growth thesis is intact and has specific catalysts (the Q2 print, monthly volume data, capex-driven FCF inflection), which is why this is a growth hold rather than an uncertain one, but the entry offers little margin of safety. Owners should hold through the volume recovery and watch the operating ratio; the case upgrades to BUY on a confirmed OR turn toward the high-80s or a pullback that compresses the forward multiple below ~30x. We would not add at the current price.
