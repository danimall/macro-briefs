**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Halozyme is a royalty-and-licensing platform built around ENHANZE, its recombinant human hyaluronidase (rHuPH20), which converts multi-hour IV biologic infusions into minutes-long subcutaneous injections. The model is structurally attractive: Halozyme owns no manufacturing, collects royalties on partner sales, and converts roughly 46% of revenue to free cash flow. 2025 revenue grew 38% to $1.397B, royalties grew 52% to $868M, and management guides 2026 revenue to $1.71-1.81B with non-GAAP EPS of $7.75-8.25. At $70.50 the stock trades around 8.5-9.1x the midpoint of forward EPS guidance, which is cheap for a business compounding royalties at 30%+.

The thesis is a race against a clock. Core rHuPH20 composition patents expire in 2027 (US) and 2029 (EU). The bull case rests on co-formulation/method-of-use patents extending protection plus the 2025 Hypercon and Surf Bio acquisitions, whose IP runs into the mid-2040s. Two developments since the prior review cut against the durability narrative: in May 2026 the PTAB issued final written decisions invalidating two MDASE patents central to Halozyme's litigation against Merck's subcutaneous Keytruda, and CMS deferred (rather than resolved) the question of whether ENHANZE fixed-combinations receive 13-year protection from Medicare price negotiation, signaling intent to revisit for 2029. Neither directly impairs current ENHANZE royalties, but both erode the "royalties well into the 2040s" credibility on which the multiple depends.

The score trajectory has been positive (6.2 in March to 6.9 in May, HOLD to BUY). I am stepping the recommendation back to HOLD with a growth bias. The valuation is genuinely cheap on forward earnings and the royalty momentum is real and broad-based, but the patent cliff is a binary, dated event sitting inside the 12-18 month window, the MDASE losses dent the moat narrative, 14.9% short interest reflects sophisticated bearish positioning on exactly this risk, and there is no insider buying to point to. The risk/reward is favorable but not clean enough to carry a high-conviction long against a structural cliff.

## Company Overview

Halozyme licenses ENHANZE to large pharmaceutical partners and earns revenue across four streams: upfront and target-nomination fees, development/regulatory/sales milestones, bulk rHuPH20 product sales, and royalties on partner product sales. Royalties are the dominant and most durable stream. The partner roster is blue-chip and wide: Roche, Takeda, Pfizer, Janssen, AbbVie, Eli Lilly, BMS, argenx, ViiV, Chugai, Acumen, Merus, and Skye Bioscience. Eleven commercialized ENHANZE products span 100+ markets.

The three royalty engines are DARZALEX FASPRO (J&J, multiple myeloma), VYVGART Hytrulo (argenx, myasthenia gravis/CIDP), and Phesgo (Roche, breast cancer). DARZALEX alone contributed roughly $483M of the 2025 royalties, about 55%.

In 2025 management moved to diversify beyond the rHuPH20 enzyme. It acquired Elektrofi (renamed Hypercon, microparticle hyperconcentration to ~500 mg/mL, November 2025) and Surf Bio (polymer-based hyperconcentration to 500 mg/mL, December 2025), both carrying IP into the mid-2040s and explicitly intended to extend the royalty horizon past the rHuPH20 cliff. Halozyme also operates an auto-injector/device business (Antares, acquired 2022) and owns proprietary products Hylenex and XYOSTED.

## Financial Analysis

The business generates high-quality cash flow on an asset-light base. Revenue progression: $660M (2022), $829M (2023), $1,015M (2024), $1,397M (2025). Within 2025, royalties carried the growth — DARZALEX $483M (+29%), VYVGART Hytrulo $157M (+444% off a small base), Phesgo $106M (+51%). Product sales were $376M (+24%, with bulk rHuPH20 +54%); collaborative revenue was $152M and is lumpy by nature.

Margins are strong. Operating margin ran ~33.6%, net margin ~22.7%, and FCF margin ~46% on $645M of free cash flow with negligible capex ($7M). Operating cash flow rose to $652M in 2025 from $479M in 2024. 2025 net income of $317M was depressed versus 2024's $444M by a $285M acquired IPR&D charge (Surf Bio) and a $49M impairment (ATRS-1902 discontinuation); both are non-recurring and the underlying operating trend improved.

Q1 2026 reinforced the momentum: revenue $377M (+42% YoY), royalties $241M (+43%), with EPS of $1.60 beating the $1.56 estimate. The earnings record shows three beats in four quarters; the one large "miss" (Q4 2025 at -$0.24 vs. $2.24 estimate) is the GAAP distortion from the Surf Bio IPR&D charge, not an operating shortfall.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. Headline metrics — an Altman Z-Score of 1.51 in the "distress zone" and debt/equity around 51x — are artifacts of a deliberate debt-funded buyback program, not financial weakness. Stockholders' equity is only ~$48.8M because Halozyme has repurchased over $1.5B of stock since 2021, taking share count from 137M (2022) to ~120M (2025). Long-term debt of $2.14B is entirely low-coupon convertible notes (0.00% 2031s, 0.875% 2032s, 1.0% 2028s, 0.25% 2027s), with interest expense of only ~$18M/year against operating cash flow. Current ratio is 4.66, the $750M revolver is undrawn, and the company is within all covenants (max net leverage 4.5x). In November 2025 it refinanced into $1.5B of new 2031/2032 converts (conversion price ~$87.20, capped calls to $136.78) and repaid most 2027/2028 converts, pushing out maturities. A new $1B buyback was launched in May 2026 on top of the existing $750M program.

The leverage is therefore not a solvency issue, but the thin equity cushion and embedded convert dilution above ~$87 are real structural features. The Financial Health score of 6.5 reflects excellent cash generation offset by the financially engineered capital structure and the dilution optionality, which is genuine and not purely cosmetic.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is well-supported and broad-based. Management guides 2026 royalties to $1.13-1.17B (+30-35%), adjusted EBITDA to $1.13-1.21B, and revenue +23-30%. Crossing $1B in royalties arrives a year ahead of prior projections. Consensus estimate revisions are modestly positive but not dramatic: current-year EPS at $8.14 (+1.6% over 90 days), next-year $9.89 (+0.5%). This is stable-to-rising, not the +30% revision velocity that would mark a clear acceleration signal — the growth here is durable rather than accelerating off a trough.

The growth pipeline beyond existing products is the contested variable. New 2025 collaborations include Takeda (ENTYVIO/vedolizumab for IBD, a new indication area), Merus (head and neck cancer), Skye Bioscience (obesity via SC nimacimab, a GLP-1-adjacent entry), and a Viatris commercial auto-injector deal. 2025 partner approvals were numerous (RYBREVANT FASPRO across US/EU/Japan/China, Opdivo SC EU, DARZALEX smoldering myeloma, VYVGART CIDP/prefilled syringe), which expand the royalty base.

The structural question is whether new platform royalties (Hypercon, Surf Bio) and co-formulation patents bridge the gap before rHuPH20 protection lapses in 2027/2029. Management cites partner Phase 1 starts "bending the curve in the 2029+ period," which is encouraging but unproven. The Growth Prospects score of 7.5 reflects strong, multi-partner near-term growth tempered by the unresolved long-tail durability question.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.50, HALO trades at ~8.5-9.1x the forward non-GAAP EPS guidance and ~8.7x the $8.14 consensus current-year estimate. This is cheap for a 30%+ royalty grower with 46% FCF margins. Peer comparison is awkward — the biotech "peer" set (KRYS 40x, RGEN 134x, with CYTK/ARWR/PTGX unprofitable) is not directly comparable to a profitable royalty business, and a 134x median P/E says more about the heterogeneity of the group than about HALO's fair multiple. A cleaner frame is to compare HALO to royalty/specialty-pharma cash-flow comps, where a high-single-digit forward multiple sits well below what durable royalty streams typically command.

The reason the multiple is compressed is the patent cliff, and that compression is rational rather than a clean mispricing. The market is pricing meaningful probability that royalties peak and decay after 2027/2029. The valuation question reduces to: how much of "royalties into the 2040s" do you believe? If co-formulation/method patents and the new platforms extend the runway, the stock is materially undervalued — a 15x multiple on $8 EPS implies $120. If the cliff bites and royalties plateau then erode, a high-single-digit multiple on stagnating earnings is appropriate and the stock is fairly valued.

The analyst target range frames this: low $57, high $96, mean $83.9, median $89. The mean/median sit well above the current price, implying the sell-side weights the durability case more favorably than the short base does. On balance the asymmetry is favorable — there is more upside if durability holds than downside if it doesn't, given the cheap starting multiple. The Valuation score of 6.5 reflects a genuinely low forward multiple offset by a real, dated structural reason for the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Halozyme occupies a near-monopoly niche as the commercially validated leader in IV-to-SC conversion. The moat combines the rHuPH20 patent estate, co-formulation IP held jointly with partners, deep regulatory and alliance-management expertise, and entrenched multi-decade partner relationships with high switching costs. Hylenex is the only FDA-approved recombinant human hyaluronidase.

The competitive threat is real and intensifying. Alteogen's ALT-B4 is the most-cited rival modified hyaluronidase, licensed to other pharma and tied to Merck's subcutaneous Keytruda. The 2027/2029 expirations open the door to biosimilar hyaluronidases. Halozyme's defense is twofold: litigate to protect its modified-hyaluronidase (MDASE) IP, and acquire next-generation hyperconcentration platforms (Hypercon, Surf Bio) whose enzyme-free approach could leapfrog the rHuPH20 mechanism and re-extend the runway.

The competitive trajectory has weakened. In May 2026 the PTAB invalidated two key MDASE patents ('600 and '262) for lack of written description and enablement, with 12 additional PGRs pending. This is distinct from the rHuPH20/ENHANZE IP that drives current royalties, so it does not impair the ~$868M base, and the Keytruda Qlex product at issue generated only ~$40M in 2025. But it removes an upside option (damages/royalties from the world's top-selling drug) and dents the "MDASE moat" narrative at the same moment the cliff is approaching. The Competitive Position score of 5.5 reflects a strong current position with a deteriorating trajectory at the margin.

## Risk Assessment

**rHuPH20 patent cliff (central long-term risk).** Core composition patents expire 2027 (US) / 2029 (EU). The entire "royalties into the 2040s" case depends on co-formulation/method-of-use patents extending protection and the new platforms generating fresh streams before expiry.

**Customer concentration.** DARZALEX is ~55% of royalties. Any clinical, competitive, or pricing setback at a top partner product flows directly to Halozyme with no offset, and Halozyme depends wholly on partner commercialization and manufacturing.

**IRA fixed-combination negotiation risk.** May 2025 CMS draft guidance raised uncertainty over whether ENHANZE co-formulations get the 13-year protection single-source drugs enjoy. September 2025 final guidance deferred the issue for 2028 but signaled intent to revisit for 2029. A live regulatory overhang on the royalty model's durability for top US products.

**Single-enzyme concentration.** Any adverse safety or regulatory finding on rHuPH20 would hit nearly all revenue lines simultaneously.

**Convert dilution.** Above ~$87 (2031/32s) the conversion features trigger, creating dilution above the capped-call levels and potentially reclassifying debt to current.

**Litigation.** The Merck/Keytruda MDASE position weakened materially with the May 2026 PTAB decisions, though the December 2025 German preliminary injunction was a partial win.

These risks are specific, several are dated, and the cliff sits inside a plausible holding horizon. The Risk Factors score of 4.5 reflects identifiable catalysts for material downside that are not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 52.6% versus 30.2% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 75th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (52.6% near-term falling to 39.5% at three months). This is consistent with event-hedging rather than a directional signal; the longer-dated stability suggests the market is not pricing durable stress. Skew is call-heavy (-8.3% near-term, P/C OI ratio 0.46), which cuts against the short interest — options positioning is not confirming the bearish equity view. ATM IV has compressed from 78.1% a month ago, indicating resolved uncertainty around the May earnings event. The 14.9% short interest with 7.5 days to cover is the more meaningful bearish tell, and it maps directly onto the patent-cliff thesis; it also creates squeeze potential on any durability-positive catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Royalty momentum (+52% in 2025, guided +30-35% in 2026) proves the model is mid-growth, not late. Co-formulation and method-of-use patents extend rHuPH20 protection well past 2027/2029, and the Hypercon/Surf Bio platforms (mid-2040s IP) generate new royalty streams that bridge the cliff. New indication entries (IBD via Takeda, obesity via Skye) open large markets. At ~9x forward EPS for a 30% grower with 46% FCF margins, re-rating to even 14-15x implies $110-125, toward the $96 high target and beyond as estimates rise. The $1B buyback and convert-driven share reduction compound EPS. Bull case ~$96-110.

**Bear Case.** The 2027/2029 cliff bites; biosimilar hyaluronidases (Alteogen) erode the moat; the May 2026 PTAB losses confirm the MDASE IP is weak; CMS strips fixed-combination protection in 2029 rulemaking, pressuring US royalty bases. DARZALEX concentration (55%) magnifies any single-product setback. Royalties plateau by 2028-29 and a high-single-digit multiple on stagnating EPS holds. The thin equity base and convert dilution above $87 cap upside. Bear case ~$57, roughly -19% from current.

The base case sits between: durable near-term growth carries the stock, the cliff remains an unresolved overhang that keeps the multiple compressed, and the stock grinds with earnings. Base case ~$80-85, the analyst mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias).**

This is a growth hold rather than an income hold (no dividend). There is a credible 12-18 month appreciation path toward the $80-85 analyst mean driven by continued royalty growth and EPS compounding, but the binary patent-cliff overhang, the weakened MDASE position, elevated short interest, and the absence of insider buying conviction keep this short of a high-conviction long.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected August). Also monitor CMS 2029 rulemaking developments and any partner-product clinical/competitive news.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that co-formulation/method patents or the Hypercon/Surf Bio platforms are generating committed new royalty streams that materially extend the post-2029 runway (e.g., new partner agreements with mid-2040s IP and disclosed milestone/royalty economics).
- 2027 US rHuPH20 expiry passes with no material royalty erosion and management raises the durability outlook with specifics on protected products.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>10% over 90 days) while the stock stays below ~12x forward, widening the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- DARZALEX, VYVGART, or Phesgo royalty growth decelerates sharply (sub-10% YoY) on competitive or pricing pressure.
- CMS rulemaking strips fixed-combination protection for 2029, with a quantified hit to US royalty bases.
- Adverse PGR outcomes or a definitive loss on rHuPH20-related (not just MDASE) IP that opens biosimilar entry earlier than expected.
- Stock breaches the convert conversion thresholds and management signals net dilution above capped-call levels.

## Conclusion

Halozyme is a high-quality, cash-generative royalty business trading at a cheap forward multiple, with genuine near-term growth and a credible path to the $80-85 analyst mean. The bull/bear divergence is a race between durable royalty momentum and a dated, structural patent cliff in 2027/2029, with the MDASE litigation setbacks and IRA deferral both arriving on the bear's side of the ledger since the last review. The forward valuation and the favorable analyst target skew argue against being negative; the binary cliff inside the holding window, the 55% DARZALEX concentration, 14.9% short interest, and the lack of any insider-buying signal argue against high conviction on the long side.

The prior upgrade to BUY ran ahead of the resolution of these structural questions. With the PTAB losses and CMS deferral now in hand, the appropriate posture is HOLD with a growth bias: own it for the near-term royalty compounding and the cheap multiple, but require concrete evidence that the post-2029 runway is being secured before treating it as a high-conviction long. The reassessment point is Q2 earnings in August and the CMS 2029 rulemaking track.
