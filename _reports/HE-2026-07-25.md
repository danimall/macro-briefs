**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hawaiian Electric Industries (HEI) is a regulated electric utility monopoly serving ~95% of Hawaii's population that was nearly destroyed by the August 2023 Lahaina wildfires. The equity today is a leveraged option on a defined-schedule liability resolution. The core regulated utility is functioning and profitable again — Q1 2026 net income for common was $30.5M ($0.18 EPS), the utility earns roughly 10% ROACE, and revenue is stable at ~$744M/quarter under a decoupled, performance-based rate framework. Sitting on top of that operating business is a $1.92B wildfire tort settlement, of which the first ~$479M installment was paid April 10, 2026, with three more roughly equal installments due in April 2027, 2028, and 2029.

The stock has re-rated meaningfully off its lows — up ~25% over the trailing year to $13.50, and up sharply from the sub-$11 levels of mid-2025 — as a series of de-risking events landed: the first settlement payment cleared, Moody's upgraded both HEI (to Ba2) and Hawaiian Electric (to Ba1) in April 2026, S&P moved its outlook to Positive, the securities and derivative litigation settled (the latter fully funded by D&O insurance), and Hawaii passed securitization legislation (Act 258) that provides a credible path to term out the remaining liability. The utility has resumed paying dividends up to the parent, an internal signal of stabilization.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the central variable — how the remaining $1.44B is funded — is unresolved and binary in its impact on per-share value. Securitization (recovering the cost through a ratepayer charge, on the California AB 1054 template) would largely neutralize the dilution overhang and support a re-rating. A new equity raise near $13.50 would compound the ~57% share-count increase already absorbed in 2024 and cap the equity. Management is explicit that a failed financing plan could trigger going-concern doubt, covenant defaults, and potential bankruptcy. With the dividend suspended at the parent, there is no income to underwrite the wait, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal conviction. The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided.

## Company Overview

HEI is the holding company for Hawaiian Electric Company and its subsidiaries (Hawaii Electric Light, Maui Electric), which operate five separate island grids across Oahu, Maui County, and Hawaii Island, serving ~475,000 customer accounts. The utility runs under a Performance-Based Regulation framework overseen by the Hawaii PUC, with an authorized ROACE of 9.5%. Revenue decoupling insulates the top line from kWh volume declines, relevant in a territory where ~24% of customers and ~44% of single-family homes on the main islands have rooftop solar.

The company has been radically reshaped since the fires. It sold American Savings Bank on December 31, 2024 (retaining a 9.9% stake), which explains the collapse in total assets from ~$17.2B to ~$8.9B and removed a historically significant earnings contributor. It wound down Pacific Current, its non-regulated renewables arm, selling Hamakua Energy (March 2025) and the solar/BESS portfolio (August 2025), with the last biomass asset held for sale. In March 2026 the PUC approved suspension of Affiliate Transaction Requirements, enabling a corporate integration in which HEI employees move to Hawaiian Electric, the boards merge, and executives become dual-hatted. HEI is deliberately becoming a cleaner, more understandable pure-play regulated utility, which over time should attract traditional utility investors back once the wildfire liability is termed out.

The moat is that of any regulated monopoly — exclusive service territory, fuel and purchased-power pass-through mechanisms, and decoupling. The structural challenge is that Hawaii has the highest electricity rates in the U.S. (heavy oil dependence), creating persistent affordability pressure alongside an aggressive 100%-RPS-by-2045 mandate.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Utility revenue is stable and low-growth: Q1 2026 electric utility revenue was $744.0M versus $738.4M in Q1 2025 (+0.8%). The business is profitable again after the catastrophic 2024, which carried a ~$1.7B operating loss driven by the $1.99B wildfire settlement accrual. Q1 2026 net income for common of $30.5M ($0.18) improved from $26.7M ($0.15) a year earlier, though utility-only net income fell to $35.3M from $47.8M on higher O&M (storm response and insurance costs). Hawaiian Electric's trailing 12-month ROACE reached ~10%, but Maui Electric lags badly at a 3.6% rate-making ROACE due to cost under-recovery.

**Balance sheet — the crux.** Total assets are $8.91B against total equity of only $1.64B, and retained earnings sit in a deficit of ($635M) from the settlement charge. The wildfire tort liability totals $1.92B ($479M current, $1.44B noncurrent). Long-term debt is $2.4B, and the consolidated capital structure runs ~59% debt / 41% equity. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.88 sits deep in the distress zone, though this metric is materially distorted for a regulated utility carrying a one-time tort liability that will be funded and, ideally, securitized rather than repaid out of ongoing cash flow. The underlying regulated business is not distressed; the holding company's balance sheet is temporarily deformed by the liability.

**Dilution — already severe.** Share count expanded from ~110M to ~172.6M, driven by the September 2024 raise of 62.2 million shares (~$557.7M net) that funded the first settlement provision. This ~57% increase is the single largest destroyer of per-share value in the story to date, and additional issuance to fund future installments is a live risk.

**Liquidity and cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $61M against capex of $103.5M (rising on grid hardening). Total available liquidity of ~$1.5B included $453M cash, $479M restricted (since deployed on the April 2026 payment), and undrawn facilities ($300M HEI revolver, $300M utility revolver, $218M ABL). The dividend at the HEI level remains suspended since Q3 2023; payout ratio is nil. Hawaiian Electric resumed paying dividends up to HEI ($10M/quarter in 2025, $11M in Q1 2026).

**Earnings quality signal.** The surprise history is choppy: a 191% beat in Q3 2025 (against a low bar), an in-line Q4, then a 37% miss in Q1 2026 ($0.18 vs. $0.29 estimate). The Q1 miss reflects the higher O&M drag and warrants tempered confidence in near-term consensus. Full-year 2026 EPS estimates have drifted down ~9.8% over 90 days to $0.97, with next-year at $1.13 (roughly flat).

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth for a decoupled island monopoly with declining kWh sales (solar penetration) is inherently modest. The forward earnings driver is rate base and rate cases, not volume. Two catalysts matter:

The MRP2 rate case is the primary lever. The current multi-year rate period ends May 31, 2026, and MRP2 begins January 1, 2027. The Utilities filed a joint re-basing proposal seeking +$170M in target revenue ($125M year one, $45M year two). That increment is roughly 2x current quarterly utility net income annualized — material to forward earnings power. The outcome is the near-term item that shapes the utility's standalone earnings trajectory.

Capex is rising on grid hardening and generation. The Waiau repower project ballooned from $847M to an estimated $1.16B, and the PUC capped recoverable costs at ~$908M, leaving a potential unrecovered gap. GE Vernova turbine contracts were signed April 2026. Rate-base growth from grid hardening supports EPS over time, but cost overruns above recoverable caps directly hit equity.

The 100%-RPS-by-2045 mandate provides a long runway of capital investment, but the July 2025 sunsetting of federal renewable tax credits and new tariffs raise project costs and threaten HEI's IPP-dependent renewable pipeline; the 70%-by-2030 carbon target is now expected to slip. The JERA proposal for a ~500-MW hybrid combined-cycle/LNG facility on Oahu (2030 COD, with JERA exploring equity investment) is a potential capital/decarbonization partner, but this is a proposal to the State, not a signed HEI deal. Estimate revisions are stable-to-slightly-negative, consistent with a low-growth utility.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.50, HE trades at ~18.4x trailing EPS of $0.71 and ~14x the 2026 consensus of $0.97 — a discount to the electric-utility peer median P/E of ~19.7x, but not a deep one, and the discount is well-explained by the balance sheet. Peer comparables (MGEE 19.7x/2.4% yield, OTTR 13.3x/2.6%, UTL 15.9x/3.7%, POR 22.4x/4.3%, TXNM 42.1x/2.9%) all carry dividends; HE carries none. On a yield basis, HE is structurally disadvantaged versus every peer until the dividend is restored, which is unlikely before the settlement funding is resolved.

The analyst target set is unusually tight: low $11.75, mean $12.75, median $13.00, high $13.50. The stock trades at the top of the sell-side range, with the mean ~6% below spot. The analysts covering it (two Hold, one Sell) see limited upside and modest downside from here. Treating the current price as fully reflecting the visible good news is the correct default.

The valuation case is bimodal. If the remaining $1.44B is securitized and recovered through a ratepayer charge — the credible base case given Act 258 — the equity is worth materially more than $13.50 as the utility re-rates toward peer multiples and eventually restores a dividend. If the funding requires equity issuance near current prices, per-share value erodes further and the multiple stays depressed. A "cheap" headline multiple here demands a specific catalyst rather than a buy signal; cheapness in a name with a going-concern-conditional liability is the value-trap configuration to treat with skepticism. I score valuation modestly below neutral: there is real optionality on securitization, but no margin of safety and no yield to be paid for waiting.

## Competitive Landscape

As an island-grid monopoly, HEI faces no direct competitor for regulated service; the competitive-position question is about regulatory relationship and structural exposure, not market share. On that axis the trajectory is improving: two credit upgrades, PUC approval of the Waiau repower and the affiliate-transaction suspension, and supportive legislation (Acts 191, 258, 301) collectively signal a constructive state posture toward stabilizing the utility. The corporate integration should cut holding-company costs.

The structural headwinds are real but slow-moving. High distributed solar penetration erodes kWh sales (largely mitigated by decoupling) and complicates grid management. The highest-in-nation electricity rates create permanent affordability and political pressure. The RPS transition is now more expensive after the federal tax-credit sunset. None of these are novel or acute, and most are already reflected in how the market and regulators treat the name. The California/PG&E parallel is instructive: the emergence of Hawaii securitization and a potential liability cap mirrors California's AB 1054 framework, the template for how HEI could term out its liability and stabilize the equity. I score competitive position at neutral-plus: the monopoly is durable and the regulatory trajectory is improving, but structural sales erosion and affordability pressure cap the upside.

## Risk Assessment

**The remaining $1.44B settlement funding gap is the dominant risk.** Three ~$479M installments are due April 2027, 2028, and 2029. Management states plainly that it is "working with financial advisors on a financing plan" and that failure could trigger going-concern doubt, covenant defaults, acceleration, and potential bankruptcy. This is an existential, time-boxed overhang. Funding will require some combination of new debt, securitization, insurance recoveries, asset sales, and potentially more dilutive equity, and the mix determines per-share outcomes.

**Liabilities in excess of accruals.** ~80 plaintiffs opted out and have not signed releases; a $500M holdback fund is reserved to address them, but additional liabilities above accruals "cannot reasonably be estimated" and could be material.

**Regulatory recovery risk.** ~$80.5M of wildfire-related costs are deferred as regulatory assets, but PUC prudence review is separate and recovery is not guaranteed. The Utilities have committed that customers won't bear the $1.9B settlement cost, which constrains the recovery path for the tort liability itself (as distinct from securitizable prospective costs).

**Wildfire recurrence and insurance limits.** Hawaii's climate exposure is structural. The company retains only ~$490M property coverage and is litigating its own property insurers (XL, Allianz) over coverage, with a settlement expected in Q2 2026. Any recovery directly improves the funding math and is an underappreciated potential offset.

**Cost-overrun risk.** The Waiau gap between the $1.16B estimate and the ~$908M recoverable cap is a direct equity exposure.

**Governance overhangs largely cleared.** The $47.8M securities class action (final hearing August 13, 2026) and the $100M derivative settlement (fully funded by D&O insurance) remove the major legal overhangs.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates the binary-event framing. Near-term ATM IV of 42.1% is rich versus 31.9% one-year historical vol (a 10.2% spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.1% near-term versus 36.1% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with the settlement-funding-plan uncertainty and the upcoming rate-case transition. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.7%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.7%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.23 and 0.09), so there is no overwhelming directional put demand despite the fundamental overhang; the market is pricing volatility, not a one-sided collapse. Short interest is elevated at 12.9% of float with 8.8 days to cover, which introduces some squeeze potential on any positive funding-plan headline but also reflects genuine institutional caution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$18-20).** The remaining installments are securitized under Act 258, recovering costs through a ratepayer charge on the AB 1054 model. Dilution risk collapses, credit ratings continue climbing toward investment grade, the MRP2 rate case delivers a constructive share of the +$170M ask, property-insurer recoveries improve the funding math, and the dividend is eventually restored. HE re-rates from a distressed multiple toward the ~19.7x peer median on normalized ~$1.10-1.20 EPS, and a restored yield attracts utility investors back.

**Bear case (~$9-10).** The financing plan leans on equity issuance near $13.50, compounding the ~57% dilution already absorbed. Opt-out liabilities exceed the holdback, the Waiau overrun bites, the MRP2 case disappoints, and credit remains sub-investment-grade, keeping the cost of capital elevated. A worst-case funding failure reintroduces going-concern language and covenant risk. Per-share value erodes and the multiple stays depressed. The analyst mean target of $12.75 already sits below spot, framing the downside as real and near.

**Base case (~$13-14).** The most likely path is a mixed funding solution — securitization for the bulk, with some incremental debt and modest equity — that avoids the tail outcomes but does not fully clear the overhang until the 2027-2029 installments are visibly financed. The stock oscillates around current levels as milestones land, with the equity fairly valued at the top of the current analyst range given the offsetting optionality and dilution risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction until the funding path is defined.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A concrete securitization financing plan announced under Act 258 that funds the 2027-2029 installments without material equity dilution (the single most important catalyst).
- A constructive MRP2 rate-case outcome delivering a substantial share of the +$170M revenue ask, providing forward EPS visibility.
- A credit-rating move to investment grade at Hawaiian Electric, and/or a meaningful property-insurance recovery that improves the funding math.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Announcement of a large dilutive equity raise near current prices to fund upcoming installments.
- Reappearance of going-concern language, covenant-pressure disclosure, or opt-out liabilities materially exceeding the $500M holdback.
- A Waiau cost overrun above the recoverable cap combined with an adverse MRP2 outcome that impairs forward earnings power.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (and any update on the property-insurer settlement and financing plan), the August 13, 2026 securities-settlement final hearing, and any disclosure of the 2027-installment financing structure ahead of the April 2027 payment. The thesis should be revisited on each of these.

## Conclusion

HEI has moved from survival to stabilization. The first settlement payment cleared, two rating agencies took positive action (Moody's upgrades to Ba2/Ba1 and an S&P outlook revision to Positive), the litigation overhangs resolved, the utility earns ~10% ROACE and resumed paying dividends up to the parent, and Hawaii's securitization legislation provides a credible template for terming out the remaining liability.

The equity remains a leveraged bet on the terms of $1.44B in future funding. Securitization would justify a BUY; equity issuance near $13.50 would justify an AVOID; and the market — with the stock at the top of a tight analyst range, no dividend, elevated short interest, and rich near-term implied volatility in backwardation — is pricing this uncertainty. The financial-health profile is weak on a headline basis (distorted but real), valuation offers optionality without a margin of safety, and there is no insider conviction to lean on. The appropriate posture is to hold and wait for the funding plan to define the outcome, upgrading or downgrading decisively when it does. **HOLD.**
