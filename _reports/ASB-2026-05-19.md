## Executive Summary

Associated Banc-Corp is Wisconsin's largest bank holding company, now managing approximately $50 billion in assets following its April 2026 completion of the American National Corporation acquisition. The company has undergone a meaningful balance sheet transformation over 2024-2025, absorbing ~$479M in nonrecurring repositioning losses to shift from low-yielding mortgage and agency securities into higher-returning assets, producing record 2025 net income of $475M ($2.77 EPS, 13.6% ROATCE).

At $27.33, ASB trades at 9.5x forward earnings with a 3.5% dividend yield, below peer median P/E of 13.1x. Estimates are rising modestly across all periods (+3.7-5.6% over 90 days), and the company has beaten earnings for 4 consecutive quarters. The 56% adjusted efficiency ratio is best-in-class for its asset size. The stock has appreciated 13.9% over the past year, reflecting the earnings normalization.

Two specific, near-term risks prevent a more constructive recommendation: (1) the Q3 2026 systems conversion for American National creates a 3-4 month binary execution event with asymmetric downside risk, and (2) ASB's $8.4B CRE portfolio (183% of risk-based capital) faces refinancing pressure as 30Y rates above 5.1% compress cap rates and commercial property values, directly threatening the 16% of CRE-investor exposure concentrated in office. These are quantifiable, timed, and specific to ASB relative to peers with lower CRE concentration.

## Company Overview

Associated Banc-Corp operates through Associated Bank, a nationally chartered commercial bank with 200+ branches across Wisconsin, Illinois, Minnesota, Missouri, Iowa, and Nebraska. The company operates three segments:

- **Corporate and Commercial Specialty** ($17.5B average earning assets): C&I lending, CRE, capital markets, renewable energy lending
- **Community, Consumer, and Business** ($12.6B average earning assets): Residential mortgage, auto finance (growing rapidly to $3.1B), wealth management ($16.1B AUM), retail deposits
- **Risk Management and Shared Services**: Investment portfolio management, ALM, corporate functions

The American National acquisition (closed April 1, 2026) added ~$5B in assets, expanded into Omaha and enhanced Twin Cities presence, and added over 200 total banking locations. Systems conversion is scheduled for Q3 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability Recovery:**
The 2025 results represent clean earnings power after the repositioning distortion. Net interest income grew 15% ($154M) to $1.20B as NIM expanded 25 bps to 3.03%. Deposit costs fell 53 bps while asset yields declined only 16 bps — a textbook asymmetric repricing benefit from rate cuts. The efficiency ratio improved to 56.01% (adjusted), down from 59.34% in 2024.

**Balance Sheet Strength:**
- CET1: 10.49% (well above 4.5% regulatory minimum, up from 10.01%)
- TCE: 8.29% (up from 7.82%)
- ACLL coverage of nonaccrual loans: 418% (extremely strong provisioning)
- Net charge-offs: 12 bps annualized (down from 23 bps)
- Uninsured/uncollateralized deposits: $9.4B (26.5%), covered at 146% by secured funding

**Credit Quality:**
Nonaccrual loans at 0.32% of total (down from 0.41%). Provision expense declined 36% to $54M. CRE-investor charge-offs did tick up ($18M vs $11M prior year), which is directionally concerning given the rate environment.

**Capital Return:**
Dividend payout ratio normalized to 33.3% (from a distressed 122% in 2024). Current yield of 3.5% is well covered by earnings and sustainable.

**HTM Portfolio Risk:**
The HTM book carries $412M in unrealized losses (amortized cost $3.6B, fair value $3.2B) at a 2.88% weighted average yield. These losses don't flow through capital metrics but represent economic impairment if liquidation were ever required.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Growth Drivers:**
- American National integration: Management guided 17-19% period-end loan growth and 19-21% customer deposit growth for FY2026 (inclusive of acquisition)
- Auto finance: Grew from $1.8B (2023) to $3.1B (2025), prime/super-prime focused across 16 states
- Wealth management: $16.1B AUM (+9% YoY)
- Capital markets fees growing as relationship-driven model deepens

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current quarter EPS: $0.73 (+5.6% over 90 days). FY2026: $2.98 (+3.7% over 90 days). FY2027: $3.23 (+1.6%). These are positive but moderate — insufficient to trigger the high-conviction revision signal (which requires >30% revision velocity). The direction is right; the magnitude is modest.

**Structural Constraints:**
Revenue growth is bounded by Midwest economic growth and rate environment. With the Fed at 3.50-3.75% and inflation elevated, further rate cuts look unlikely, removing one tailwind (continued deposit repricing benefit). If rates rise, ASB's slight asset sensitivity would help NII modestly, but CRE credit stress would likely offset.

## Valuation Assessment

ASB at 9.5x forward P/E vs. peer median of 13.1x represents a 27% discount. The discount has compressed from deeper levels a year ago as the balance sheet repositioning completed and earnings normalized.

**Why the discount exists and whether it's justified:**
1. CRE concentration (183% of risk-based capital) warrants some discount vs. peers with lower exposure
2. Integration risk from a transformational acquisition (crossing $50B)
3. Midwest-concentrated franchise lacks the growth premium of Sun Belt peers like IBOC or TCBI

**Why partial closure is likely:**
- 13.6% ROATCE is above peer average and should command a premium, not a discount
- 4 consecutive earnings beats suggest estimates are still conservative
- Dividend yield of 3.5% (vs. peer median 2.0%) provides valuation floor
- If integration executes cleanly and CRE credit holds, a re-rating toward 11-12x forward earnings ($33-36) is achievable

At 9.5x FY2026 earnings of $2.98, the stock implies the market assigns roughly a 20-25% haircut for execution/credit risk. This appears fair given the specific near-term risks but creates upside if those risks dissipate by year-end.

## Competitive Landscape

**Position: Stable with modest improvement trajectory**

ASB is the dominant franchise in Wisconsin with 160+ years of history. The American National deal extends into Omaha and Iowa — attractive growth markets. Wealth management and capital markets fees diversify beyond pure spread lending.

Differentiators include:
- Renewable energy lending expertise (68% of utilities exposure)
- Auto finance platform offering national reach
- CRA "Outstanding" rating (relationship depth)

Weaknesses relative to peers:
- Geographic concentration in slower-growth Midwest markets
- Smaller scale than national competitors for C&I lending
- Limited digital banking differentiation vs. fintech entrants

The competitive position is stable. The acquisition adds geographic diversification without fundamentally changing the competitive dynamic. The renewable energy lending niche is genuinely differentiated but represents a concentrated sector bet.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Refinancing Pressure (HIGH — Specific and Timed):**
$8.4B CRE portfolio (183% of risk-based capital). Office is 16% of CRE-investor exposure (~$840M). The 10-K explicitly warns of "some degree of instability in CRE markets" as loans are refinanced in markets with higher vacancy rates. While nonaccrual CRE is currently only $8.7M, CRE-investor charge-offs already ticked up to $18M from $11M. At the current rate environment, CRE credit deterioration accelerates over the next 6-12 months.

**Acquisition Integration (MEDIUM-HIGH — Binary Event):**
The Q3 2026 systems conversion is the highest-risk operational event in ASB's near-term. Historical precedent suggests conversion failures at regional banks can trigger 10-15% selloffs. Additional risks: customer attrition (99% ownership concentration in two shareholders creates unusual dynamic), elevated cyber vulnerability during conversion, and the $50B threshold implications if the OCC's proposed rule change is delayed.

**Rate Environment:**
ASB is slightly asset-sensitive (+1.5% NII per 100bp rise). A rate hike scenario would modestly benefit NII but could accelerate CRE credit stress. The more concerning scenario is rates staying elevated without further cuts, removing the deposit repricing tailwind that drove 2025's NIM expansion.

**Tariff Exposure:**
Midwest manufacturing and agricultural customer base faces trade policy uncertainty. This is a genuine idiosyncratic risk for ASB relative to peers in less trade-exposed regions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.2% vs. 26.7% historical volatility represents a 10.5pp premium. The term structure is in backwardation (37.2% near-term > 30.9% 6-month), indicating the market expects a volatility event within 1-3 months. Low put/call OI ratio of 0.39 suggests relatively light downside hedging, but the IV premium suggests informed participants see binary risk ahead. This likely reflects Q2 2026 earnings uncertainty and early-stage integration concerns. The options market is confirming near-term uncertainty without heavily directional positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-36, +21% to +32%):**
American National integration executes smoothly (Q3 conversion on time, minimal customer attrition). CRE credit holds as Midwest commercial markets prove more resilient than coastal office. FY2026 EPS reaches $3.10-3.20 (above current $2.98 consensus) on better-than-expected cost synergies. Multiple re-rates to 11-12x forward earnings as execution de-risks. Rate environment stays accommodative enough to sustain NIM above 3.0%.

**Bear Case ($22-24, -15% to -20%):**
Systems conversion encounters problems in Q3 (data migration errors, customer-facing outages). CRE credit deteriorates as office and retail properties hit refinancing walls at 5%+ rates — provision expense doubles to $100-120M annually. Midwest economic weakness from tariff impacts on manufacturing/agriculture compresses loan demand. Multiple compresses to 8x on elevated provisioning, taking the stock to $22-24.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Successful completion of Q3 2026 systems conversion with <2% customer deposit attrition (verifiable in Q4 2026 earnings)
2. CRE nonaccrual ratio remains below 0.50% through next two reporting periods (Q2 and Q3 2026)
3. Stock pulls back to $25 or below on market-wide regional bank selloff without ASB-specific fundamental deterioration (would create ~8.4x forward P/E, well below peer median)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. CRE nonaccrual ratio exceeds 1.0% or provision expense exceeds $40M in any single quarter
2. Systems conversion delayed beyond Q3 2026 or management acknowledges material customer attrition
3. Net interest margin compresses below 2.80% for two consecutive quarters

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) for credit quality and pre-conversion update; Q3 2026 earnings (late October) for integration assessment.

## Conclusion

ASB is a well-managed regional bank executing a strategic transformation with clean earnings power ($2.77 EPS), improving profitability metrics (13.6% ROATCE, 56% efficiency ratio), and an attractive valuation at 9.5x forward earnings with 3.5% dividend support. The 27% P/E discount to peer median creates potential for multiple expansion.

The specific barrier to a BUY recommendation is the convergence of two timed, quantifiable risks: (1) the Q3 2026 systems conversion, a binary execution event within 4 months where failure carries asymmetric downside of 10-15%, and (2) $8.4B in CRE exposure (183% of risk-based capital) facing refinancing stress in markets with higher vacancy rates, with office exposure of ~$840M in the most vulnerable segment. These are falsifiable: if conversion succeeds and CRE credit holds through Q3 2026, the discount closes and the stock reaches $31-34.

The appropriate posture is HOLD with a positive lean, collecting a 3.5% dividend yield while waiting for execution proof on both integration and credit.
