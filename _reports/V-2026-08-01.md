**Research Date: August 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Visa's Q3 FY2026 print (quarter ended 6/30/2026, reported 7/28/2026) shows the business is more than a levered bet on nominal consumer spending. Net revenue rose 14% to $11,633M while payments volume crossed $4 trillion in a quarter for the first time, growing roughly 10%. The ~4-point wedge between revenue and volume growth comes from value-added services (+33%), cross-border (+14% excluding intra-Europe), and net yield. Nine-month revenue of $33,764M was up 15% on FY2025's ~$40.0B base. Non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.32 rose 11%, beating consensus of roughly $3.22 by ~3%; GAAP EPS of $2.97 rose 10%, the gap driven mainly by a $253M litigation provision and a $563M severance charge.

The stock has not been cheap at any point in this cycle and is not cheap now. At $366.13 Visa trades at 27.7x the FY2026 consensus of $13.20 and 24.5x the FY2027 consensus of $14.95, within 2% of its 52-week high of $373.97, with estimate revisions of +1.0% over 90 days. Consensus mean and median targets of $412 and $420 imply 12-15% upside; my base case of $395 (26.4x FY2027) implies about 8%, against a bear case near $299 (20x FY2027, roughly the 52-week low) if the litigation release is narrowed or the debit-routing attack lands.

Two developments in the current filings justify keeping the rating at HOLD rather than upgrading on the strength of the print. First, the *Potayto-Potahto* class action directly challenges the forward-looking release in the amended interchange settlement, which is the release's principal value to Visa; the escrow has already fallen to $888M from $2,990M and accrued litigation to $1,274M from $3,033M, so the market is treating the overhang as retired, and a narrowed release would re-open it. Second, shareholder distributions of roughly $22B annualized in buybacks plus ~$5B of dividends appear to run at or modestly above free cash flow, total equity has declined to $35.2B, and the commercial paper program was raised from $3B to $7B in July 2026. The CP expansion has at least one benign explanation — new Brazilian rules requiring Visa to extend settlement guarantees to sellers — but the combination of above-FCF distributions and a thinning equity cushion belongs in the downside case.

## Company Overview

Visa operates a four-party payments network and is explicit in its FY2025 10-K that it does not issue cards, extend credit, set consumer rates, or receive interchange reimbursement fees (IRFs) or merchant discount rates (MDRs). Interchange flows from acquirers to issuers; Visa's fees come separately from issuers and acquirers. This matters for interpreting interchange headlines: reductions in swipe fees compress issuer economics first, transmitting to Visa second-order through issuer willingness to fund incentives, issue cards, and support premium programs. The channel is real but weaker than most coverage implies.

Scale as of FY2025: 329 billion Visa-branded payments-and-cash transactions, of which 258 billion were processed on VisaNet; $17 trillion of total payments and cash volume; ~5 billion credentials; >175 million merchant locations; ~14,500 financial institution clients; >16 billion tokens provisioned. Headcount grew 8% to ~34,100, with over 60% outside the U.S.

The gap between the two transaction figures is the single most useful number in the filing. 329B minus 258B implies roughly 71 billion transactions — about 21.6% of Visa-branded volume — already clearing on other rails, principally U.S. PIN-debit networks under Regulation II and IFR-driven scheme/processing separation in Europe. Item 1 confirms that when a processor bypasses VisaNet on a Visa-branded transaction, Visa "may earn only service revenue," forgoing data processing revenue. Some of that 71B is structurally unaddressable (cash/ATM activity, PIN-debit Visa never processed), so it is not a clean measure of foregone revenue, but it quantifies the surface area exposed to routing mandates and the DOJ debit case.

Three strategy pillars: Consumer Payments (>$40T addressable spend ex-Russia/China, >$20T underserved; Tap to Pay at 79% of global face-to-face transactions and 66% in the U.S.); Commercial & Money Movement Solutions (~$200T of non-C2B flows on a 2022-vintage third-party estimate; Visa Direct processed >12.5B transactions for >650 partners in FY2025); and Value-Added Services (company-estimated $520B revenue opportunity across issuing, acceptance, risk/security, and advisory, with >200 products). Stablecoin settlement reached a $2.5B annualized run rate as of 9/30/2025 — trivial against $17T of volume, and best read as treasury-plumbing optionality rather than an earnings driver.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 net revenue was approximately $40.0B, with pre-tax income of ~$24.19B, tax of ~$4.14B, derived net income of ~$20.06B, and diluted EPS of ~$10.29 on roughly 1,949M as-converted Class A-equivalent shares. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $11,633M (+14%) and 9M revenue of $33,764M (+15%) imply prior-year comparables of ~$10,204M and ~$29,360M respectively, and a Q4 FY2025 base of ~$10,640M. Extending Q3's growth rate gives FY2026 revenue of roughly $45.9B; a simple 9M × 4/3 annualization gives ~$45.0B. Both are sensitivities rather than forecasts — fiscal Q4 seasonality differs, the FIFA World Cup falls in fiscal Q3/Q4 and both lifts volume and inflates marketing, and FX translation on international transaction revenue is volatile.

**Margins and the FY2026 cost humps.** Two items compress FY2026 GAAP margins and both are identifiable. Personnel expense rose 40% in Q3 on a $563M severance charge tied to a reported ~2,600-role reduction (~7.6% of the FY2025 year-end headcount of 34,100, at roughly $217,000 per role — consistent with senior technology/product levels plus non-U.S. statutory severance regimes). Marketing rose 54% on the FIFA World Cup 2026 and Milano Cortina Olympics. The severance is non-recurring by construction and excluded from non-GAAP; the sponsorship calendar steps down in FY2027 with no World Cup or Winter Olympics. Both mechanically support FY2027 GAAP margin expansion provided the cost base does not re-inflate, and Visa is a perennial sponsor (NFL, Super Bowl, Formula One, ongoing FIFA relationship), so marketing normalizes relative to a peak year, not toward zero. Note the sequence: headcount grew 8% in FY2025 and was then cut ~7.6% in FY2026. That is a reversal, not a trim. Whether it reflects genuine AI-driven productivity re-basing (the 10-K discloses ~26,000 employees running >261,000 internal GenAI chats between March and September 2025) or margin defense ahead of interchange-driven pressure on issuer economics is not established by the filings, and I would not infer management motive from the charge's size or timing.

**Client incentives — the pricing-power tell.** FY2025 client incentives grew approximately 14% against gross revenue growth of roughly 11%. Incentives are the contra-revenue line where Visa's bargaining position with large issuers, acquirers, and co-brand partners is priced. Sustained incentive growth above gross revenue growth is the cleanest available proxy for erosion of network leverage. It can equally reflect a heavy renewal cycle (renewals are lumpy and front-load incentives) or deliberate share investment in high-growth geographies. Incentive growth alone does not establish pricing-power loss, but it is the metric I would watch quarterly.

**Tax.** The 9M FY2026 effective rate was 16%, including a $351M discrete deferred tax benefit. Backing that out implies an underlying rate near 17.5-18% on approximate pre-tax income, so reported 16% flatters year-over-year EPS growth by roughly 1.5-2 points. The inputs are estimated; treat this as an order-of-magnitude adjustment.

**Balance sheet and capital returns.** At 6/30/2026: total equity ~$35.2B and declining, because buybacks exceed retained-earnings accretion; senior notes of ~$22.4B carrying value against ~$20.4B fair value (fixed-rate debt worth less than book to a buyer — cheap legacy funding, a mild economic positive for the issuer, not a credit concern); debt/equity ~0.64x on carrying values. Buybacks were $16.5B in 9M FY2026 with ~$28.4B remaining authorized. The litigation escrow fell to $888M from $2,990M and accrued litigation to $1,274M from $3,033M, with $2,977M paid to opt-out merchants and the injunctive-relief class. Reconciling those balances implies roughly $1,218M of litigation provisions recorded across 9M FY2026 (of which $253M in Q3); the escrow decline and the accrual decline are separate accounts with different scopes and should not be conflated.

Identifiable nine-month cash uses total roughly $21.0B before dividends and capex: $16.5B of buybacks, ~$3.0B of litigation payments, and ~$1.5B for the Prisma/Newpay acquisition. Annualizing buybacks alone gives ~$22B; adding a dividend of roughly $5B implies ~$27B of annual shareholder returns against free cash flow very likely in the high-teens to low-$20s billions. Two mitigants: the ~$3.0B of litigation payments drew down a pre-funded escrow rather than consuming FY2026 operating cash, and the CP expansion is plausibly a response to the newly disclosed Brazilian requirement to extend settlement guarantees to sellers, which the 10-Q states will increase settlement exposure and which requires larger intraday liquidity backstops. I am not asserting buybacks are debt-financed. I am asserting that flat-to-declining equity, distributions at or above FCF, and a quadrupled short-term facility deserve explicit weight in the downside case.

**A note on the Altman Z-Score of ~2.1.** Visa's asset base is inflated by $30B+ of goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles (largely the 2016 Visa Europe transaction) plus gross settlement receivables and payables, producing asset turnover near 0.4x versus the manufacturing base on which Z was calibrated. A grey-zone reading here reflects balance-sheet composition, not distress — the company earned ~$20B and repurchased $16.5B of stock in nine months. It does, however, correctly capture the real fact that the equity cushion relative to total liabilities is thin and shrinking by design.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, in descending order of durability.

**Yield expansion above volume.** Revenue +14% against volume +~10% is the central fundamental fact. VAS at roughly 27% of FY2025 revenue growing 32-33% contributes most of the wedge, with cross-border (Visa's highest-yield line) at +14% ex-intra-Europe contributing the rest. The important qualification: VAS is a management-defined metric distributed across the service, data processing, and other revenue lines, not an audited GAAP segment, and the filings I reviewed do not decompose it into organic versus acquired. Pismo (FY2024), Featurespace (December 2024), and Prisma/Newpay (~$1.5B, February 2026) all feed the line. A 33% growth rate that is partly inorganic supports the revenue trajectory but weakens the inference about underlying franchise momentum.

**Volume growth from nominal spending.** This is where the macro backdrop cuts both ways. Nominal consumer spending supports ticket sizes, but a real discretionary slowdown in H2 2026 would show up first in transaction counts. Visa's cross-border line is additionally exposed to travel demand, where higher airfares can reduce cross-border transaction counts even as they raise ticket values. [Note: specific macro figures — core/headline PCE, 30-year mortgage rate, crude-price moves, and IATA airline-profit forecasts — are not present in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed as unverified.]

**Share-count reduction.** At ~$22B of annual buybacks against ~$688B of as-converted equity value, Visa is shrinking the share base roughly 3% per year. Combined with mid-teens revenue growth and modest operating leverage, that supports the FY2027 consensus of $14.95 (+13% on FY2026's $13.20). A meaningful portion of forward EPS growth is a share-count phenomenon funded at the edge of free cash flow.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus rose 1.0% and next-year 1.0% over 90 days; the current quarter slipped 0.1% over 90 days (and 0.3% over 30 days). This is steady positive drift, well short of the magnitude that signals a fundamental change in trajectory. The four-quarter surprise record — +0.8%, +4.8%, -1.0%, -1.5% — shows a well-calibrated consensus in a low-surprise regime. Do not expect earnings surprises to drive re-rating in either direction; the multiple will move on rates and on litigation/regulatory news flow.

**Remaining performance obligations of ~$5.6B** relate primarily to VAS contracts and are explicitly not total backlog; they exclude variable and volume-based consideration, which is the overwhelming majority of Visa's revenue. This figure should not be used as a forward revenue indicator.

## Valuation Assessment

The 10-Q reports ~1,880M as-converted shares following the May 2026 Class B exchange (down from ~1,930M). As-converted equity value is therefore roughly $688B, about 13% above the quoted figure, and Class A holders own approximately 88% of the as-converted economics. On forward earnings the two are within a few turns of each other, with Visa carrying the larger network (1.68x Mastercard's payments volume, 1.52x transactions) and a slightly more productive credential base ($2,796 of payments volume per card versus $2,547). **Where the mispricing case would have to come from.** There is one under-modeled item: the FY2027 GAAP margin step-up from the non-recurring severance and a normalizing sponsorship calendar, combined with a litigation provision run-rate falling sharply as the U.S. MDL resolves. That narrows the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap and improves reported earnings quality. It is a genuine tailwind but a modest one relative to a 24.5x multiple, and consensus at +13.3% FY2027 growth may already capture part of it.

Against that, three things argue the stock is priced approximately right. The multiple sits in the upper half of Visa's recent range at a moment when the discount rate on long-duration compounders is elevated. Estimate revisions are +1%, not +30%. And the stock is 2% below its 52-week high with roughly 12-15% to consensus targets, which is a normal 12-month expectation for a quality compounder rather than evidence of mispricing.

**Base case $395** = 26.4x FY2027 consensus of $14.95, +7.9% from spot, plus a 0.73% yield. **Bull case $449** = 30x FY2027, +22.6%, consistent with the $450 analyst high. **Bear case $299** = 20x FY2027, -18.3%, approximately the 52-week low of $293.89. That distribution is roughly symmetric with a slight positive skew — adequate for holding, insufficient for adding.

Valuation score: 5.5. Fairly priced against quality and growth, with no identified mispricing large enough to underwrite new capital at this level.

## Competitive Landscape

Per the Nilson Report (issue 1288, CY2024) as reproduced in the 10-K, Visa's $13,433B of payments volume represents 56.5% of the five listed networks' combined total — a figure that excludes UnionPay and therefore overstates true global share. Visa leads Mastercard by 1.68x on payments volume, 1.52x on transactions, and 1.53x on cards. American Express generates $11,905 of payments volume per card, roughly 4.3x Visa's, which quantifies precisely why Amex competes for the affluent segment Item 1 identifies as Visa's most attractive consumer opportunity.

The trajectory question matters more than the level. Three forces are compressing the protected part of the revenue base:

**Routing and deconsolidation, already 21.6% of branded transactions.** Regulation II mandates routing choice and limits network exclusivity in U.S. debit; the EU/UK IFR forces scheme/processing separation. This is a quantified, established leakage in revenue per transaction rather than a prospective risk.

**Real-time and account-to-account rails.** RTP networks have launched in at least 80 countries (FedNow, PIX, UPI named), with cross-border linkages such as PayNow-UPI emerging. The 10-K states in Visa's own words that these "will compete with our cross-border business," the highest-yield line.

**Mix shift toward a contested revenue pool.** VAS is roughly 27% of revenue growing at 32-33% while the network franchise grows at high single digits to low teens. The competitive set for VAS — technology companies, consulting firms, information services providers, merchant services companies, and governments, per Visa's own enumeration — has materially lower barriers to entry than a global four-party network. This is simultaneously the bull case (TAM expansion into a $520B pool) and the long-run bear case (a rising share of revenue earned in a market where Visa is one competitor among many rather than one of two).

The offsetting structural hedge is real and underappreciated: Visa Protect for A2A, Visa Advanced Authorization, Visa Consumer Authentication Service, and Verifi's dispute platform are all sold network-agnostically, and the risk-factor discussion notes that RTP networks "can also be customers for value-added services." If volume migrates to A2A rails, Visa can monetize a services layer rather than lose the transaction outright. Services yield on a competing rail is almost certainly a fraction of Visa's own network yield, so this mitigates disintermediation without neutralizing it.

On agentic commerce, Visa has built an MCP server for its Intelligent Commerce APIs, published a Trusted Agent Protocol so sellers can distinguish legitimate agents from bots, and ran a live agentic-token pilot in 2025. This positions Visa as infrastructure for AI-mediated transactions rather than a bystander, though nothing in the filings quantifies revenue from it. Competitive position: 7.2 — leading and defending intelligently, with mix shifting toward a less-protected revenue pool.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The forward-looking release is under direct legal attack.** The amended U.S. interchange settlement (11/10/2025) received preliminary approval on 6/9/2026 with a final-approval motion filed 7/15/2026, and Visa has separately settled individual actions covering ~95% of opt-out merchant volume. The *Potayto-Potahto* class action challenges the forward-looking release, which is the settlement's primary value to Visa. Escrow at $888M and accrued litigation at $1,274M indicate the market is pricing the overhang as retired. If the release is narrowed or struck, the "final resolution" framing collapses. This is the highest-probability source of a negative re-rating and it is not, in my judgment, priced.

**2. The DOJ debit monopolization suit.** Filed September 2024 in S.D.N.Y. and, per the brief, its current procedural posture could not be verified. It attacks exactly the channel where the 21.6% off-network leakage lives. Any adverse structural remedy would compress data processing revenue per transaction on the largest debit franchise in the U.S.

**3. European claims outside the U.S. escrow mechanics.** Merchants filed UK High Court damages claims in April 2026 covering 1/1/2019 forward, with additional filings in May and June. The 2/18/2026 UK Competition Appeal Tribunal ruling that interchange was not passed on except in certain merchant categories is a meaningful liability-limiting precedent for most claimants. Net exposure is genuinely uncertain, and the Class A insulation argument is weaker here because VE-territory covered litigation has different funding mechanics from U.S. covered litigation.

**4. The Retrospective Responsibility Plan and the Makewhole Agreement.** U.S. covered litigation is escrow-funded, and escrow deposits trigger a downward adjustment to the Class B conversion rate — so the economic burden falls on Class B/B-3 holders (largely former bank members) via reduced as-converted Class A equivalents, not on Class A holders. Mastercard, lacking this structure, bears its share directly. The May 2026 exchange (accepting ~3M Class B-1 and ~120M Class B-2 shares, issuing ~61M B-3 and ~23M Class C) came with a Makewhole Agreement filed as an 8-K exhibit on 5/12/2026 and a certificate-of-incorporation amendment on 7/15/2026. Whether that structure progressively transfers residual covered-litigation risk toward common equity, or simply prices a now-small and quantifiable residual because the MDL is nearly resolved, cannot be determined without reading the exhibit. Both readings are defensible; the timing is fully explained by settlement progress. I flag this as an unresolved verification gap rather than a scored negative.

**5. Newly expanding balance-sheet exposure.** New Brazilian rules will require Visa to extend settlement guarantees to sellers, which the 10-Q states will increase settlement exposure. This moves risk into a category Visa has historically avoided.

**6. Market-access restrictions.** Item 1 names China, India, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, and South Africa as jurisdictions favoring domestic providers, imposing local ownership or data localization, or mandating in-country processing. Visa's own TAM figures exclude Russia and China entirely, which caps the geographic optionality a global-GDP framing would assume.

**7. Consumer cycle.** A discretionary slowdown in H2 2026 would show up first in U.S. debit and credit transaction counts and second in cross-border travel. [Note: specific macro rate and commodity figures previously cited here are not supported by the brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed.]

**8. Client concentration.** ~14,500 financial institution clients sounds diversified, but revenue and incentive economics are dominated by a small number of very large issuers and co-brand partners. The disclosed concentration threshold was not verified. The VAS mix shift should not be read as customer diversification — VAS is sold largely to the same issuers and acquirers.

**Explicitly not established:** the widely reported ~$38B settlement value. Visa carried only $1,274M of accrued litigation and $888M in escrow at 6/30/2026 and recorded ~$1.2B of provisions in nine months. The only reading consistent with those balances is that the headline represents estimated multi-year merchant savings from forward-looking interchange reductions and rule changes, borne primarily by issuers, rather than a cash obligation of Visa. Treat "Visa owes $38 billion" as almost certainly wrong.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 22.5% against 21.8% one-year realized is fair value (spread 0.7%), and IV sits in the 7th percentile within the current chain — the market is pricing no imminent catalyst, consistent with earnings already behind us on 7/28. The term structure is flat (near 22.5% ≈ far 22.4%), so uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated in any specific tenor. The put/call volume ratio of 0.35 is call-heavy, OI ratio 0.86 is balanced, and the source characterizes near-term put/call skew of 1.2% as balanced. Unusual activity is entirely on the call side and out of the money: 2,029 contracts at the August 14 $385 strike (13.4x open interest), 1,975 at the September 18 $390 (3.3x), and 1,474 at the September 18 $405 (5.3x). That is positioning for continued upward drift rather than hedging. Short interest of 1.4% of float with 2.4 days to cover confirms no bearish institutional positioning.

The takeaway: options do not corroborate the litigation and regulatory tail risks I have flagged, and cheap IV in the 7th percentile means downside protection is inexpensive if you want to own the stock through the final-approval and *Potayto-Potahto* docket. Absence of hedging demand is not evidence that the risks are absent; it is evidence the market has not priced them.

Risk factors score: 5.4. Several specific, dated, unresolved catalysts with asymmetric downside against a valuation that assumes resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $449 (30x FY2027 EPS of $14.95, +22.6%)

Final approval of the amended settlement clears without narrowing the forward-looking release, the DOJ debit suit resolves without a structural remedy, and the UK pass-on ruling caps European damages in most merchant categories. FY2027 arrives with no World Cup, no Winter Olympics, no severance charge, and a sharply lower litigation provision, producing GAAP margin expansion and a narrowing GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap that improves reported earnings quality. VAS compounds above 25% with a demonstrable organic contribution, cross-border holds low-double-digit growth, and revenue continues to outgrow volume by 3-4 points. Roughly 3% annual share-count reduction lifts EPS growth into the mid-teens, and the multiple expands toward 30x as the litigation overhang is retired and rate pressure eases.

### Bear Case — $299 (20x FY2027 EPS of $14.95, -18.3%)

The *Potayto-Potahto* challenge narrows or strikes the forward-looking release, re-opening a liability the balance sheet no longer reserves for and re-establishing an indefinite overhang. The DOJ obtains a debit remedy that expands routing away from VisaNet beyond the current 21.6%, compressing data processing revenue per transaction. Client incentives continue growing above gross revenue as issuers absorb interchange reductions and push back at renewal, capping net revenue growth in the high single digits. An H2 2026 consumer slowdown materialises: transaction counts decelerate, cross-border travel volumes fall, and the revenue-over-volume wedge narrows as VAS growth normalizes post-acquisition. Distributions running above free cash flow against $35.2B of equity force either a buyback slowdown — removing 3 points of EPS growth — or further reliance on short-term facilities. At 20x a flat-to-modestly-growing $15 EPS, the stock trades back to its 52-week low.

### Base Case — $395 (26.4x FY2027 EPS of $14.95, +7.9%)

Settlement receives final approval with the release substantially intact but the *Potayto-Potahto* docket unresolved into 2027; the DOJ case grinds without a near-term ruling. FY2026 revenue lands near $45-46B, FY2027 non-GAAP EPS near consensus $14.95, and the multiple holds in the mid-20s as GAAP margin normalization offsets a rising discount rate. Total return of roughly 8-9% including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is the fifth consecutive non-BUY rating and the score has drifted from 7.8 in April to 6.54 now, so the bar for continuing unchanged is higher. The decline is not price-driven — the stock is at essentially the same level as at the last review and up 6% over twelve months. It reflects two things: the multiple has re-rated to the upper half of its range against unchanged low-teens EPS growth, and the filings have surfaced new specific risks (the *Potayto-Potahto* challenge to the release, the unread Makewhole Agreement, distributions at or above free cash flow with a quadrupled CP facility) that the escrow drawdown had made look resolved. The competitive-position decline from 9.5 to 7.2 across this sequence is justified by the mix shift toward the contested VAS pool and the quantified 21.6% off-network leakage, not by any deterioration in Visa's core franchise.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Price at or below $322 — 21.5x FY2027 consensus, which restores a base-case return above 20% and matches the prior add-level discipline.
2. Final approval of the amended settlement granted with the forward-looking release intact **and** *Potayto-Potahto* dismissed or its challenge to the release denied.
3. Two consecutive quarters with client incentive growth at or below gross revenue growth, evidencing that network bargaining power is stable.
4. FY2027 guidance implying non-GAAP EPS above $15.50 (i.e., >4% above current consensus) on a GAAP operating margin above 68%, confirming the severance and marketing normalization flows through rather than re-inflating.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. Court narrows or strikes the forward-looking release, or final approval is denied.
2. An adverse DOJ ruling or consent decree that mandates further debit routing expansion beyond current Regulation II requirements.
3. Net revenue growth decelerating below 8% for two consecutive quarters with the revenue-over-volume wedge narrowing below 2 points.
4. A buyback reduction below $15B annualized, or commercial paper outstanding exceeding $4B while the equity balance falls below $30B — either would indicate distributions have outrun cash generation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY2026 results in late October 2026, which will carry initial FY2027 guidance and the first read on whether the reduced cost base holds. Interim checkpoints: the final-approval ruling on the interchange settlement (motion filed 7/15/2026, decision plausible in H2 2026) and any docket activity in *Potayto-Potahto* or *United States v. Visa*.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** Own it. With IV in the 7th percentile of the current chain, longer-dated protection is unusually inexpensive relative to the litigation docket ahead, and buying a six-month collar or outright puts is a more efficient way to manage the identified tail than reducing the position. New capital should wait for the low $320s or for the settlement release to be confirmed intact.

## Conclusion

Visa remains one of the highest-quality compounders in the market: high operating margins on a two-sided network, revenue growing 14-15% against 10% volume growth, and roughly 3% annual share-count reduction.
