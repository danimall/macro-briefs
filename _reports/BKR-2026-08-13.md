## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes has spent the last eighteen months converting itself from a diversified oilfield service company into a gas-infrastructure and industrial-power equipment franchise with an oilfield attachment. The mechanics are visible in the segment data rather than in the consolidated revenue line, which was flat in FY2025 (-0.3% to $27,733M) and flat again in H1 2026 ($13,329M vs $13,337M). Underneath, Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) revenue grew 10% in FY2025 while Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE) fell 8%; IET's EBITDA margin expanded from 15.1% (FY2023) to 18.5% (FY2025) to 20.6% in Q2 2026, crossing above OFSE (17.5%, down 120bp) for the first time in this cycle. IET generated 53% of segment EBITDA in Q2. The July 16 close of the $13.6B Chart Industries acquisition adds $4.3B of FY2025 revenue in air and gas handling, thermal management and lifecycle services across gas infrastructure, nuclear, data centers and carbon capture, and pushes the pro forma oilfield-cyclical share of revenue well below half.

The Q2 order book is the strongest evidence for the thesis and it is specific rather than thematic. Total orders of $10,501M rose 49% year over year (book-to-bill 1.6x, IET 2.2x), with Gas Technology Equipment orders of $4,913M against $781M a year earlier. The named counterparties are verifiable: Venture Global (12 liquefaction modules), Cheniere/Bechtel (Sabine Pass Train 7, ~6 MTPA), Golar (four compressor trains for a fourth FLNG vessel), Dynamis (76 NovaLT16 turbines, ~1.3 GW) and Kodiak Gas Services (1 GW initial with a 1.8 GW framework), the latter two explicitly tied to North American data-center and energy-infrastructure power demand. Total remaining performance obligations reached $40.1B, of which IET is $37.1B and Gas Technology Services — the multi-decade installed-base annuity, on contracts averaging over ten years and only ~13% complete by costs incurred — is $16.7B. Consensus has responded: 2026 EPS estimates are up 13.7% over 90 days to $2.61 and 2027 up 12.4% to $3.16, on the back of four consecutive beats averaging 16.7%.

What keeps this a hold rather than a purchase is the price and the balance sheet that now sits behind it. Net debt moves from $526M at June 30 to an estimated ~$14B post-close (roughly 2.3-2.4x estimated pro forma EBITDA of $5.9-6.0B), against a company-disclosed target of 1.0-1.5x within 24 months. Buybacks were zero in both Q1 and Q2 2026 despite $1.35B of remaining authorization, and the share count rose from 986.8M to 992.3M. Post-close interest expense steps up roughly $490M annualized (~$0.37 per share after tax) as $15.7B of pre-funding interest income disappears, and management guides to $0.9-1.0B of 2026 cash taxes after $308M and $664M of valuation-allowance releases flattered 2025 and 2024. At 24.4x 2026E and 20.2x 2027E consensus, roughly 13x estimated pro forma EV/EBITDA and a 4.0% trailing FCF yield, the base case of ~$70 offers about 10% upside against a bear case near $49. That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted return.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates in over 120 countries with roughly 54,000 employees. Through H1 2026 it reported two segments; a third was added effective July 2026.

**OFSE** (FY2025 revenue $14,324M, EBITDA $2,618M, 18.3% margin) spans Well Construction ($3,646M), Completions/Intervention & Measurements ($3,750M), Production Solutions ($3,806M) and Subsea & Surface Pressure Systems ($3,122M). It is ~74% international ($10,551M vs $3,773M North America).

**IET** (FY2025 revenue $13,409M, EBITDA $2,482M, 18.5% margin) comprises Gas Technology Equipment ($6,619M), Gas Technology Services ($3,028M), Industrial Products ($1,991M), Industrial Solutions ($1,123M) and Climate Technology Solutions ($647M). This is the LNG liquefaction and compression turbomachinery franchise plus the installed-base service annuity.

**Chart** (new segment, $4.3B FY2025 revenue, 50+ countries) will be run standalone under SVP Jim Apostolides, who has led integration planning since July 2025.

One structural feature deserves emphasis because it is frequently misread: Baker Hughes depends on GE Vernova and GE Aerospace as *suppliers*, not customers, under a long-term heavy-duty gas turbine supply agreement, a supply and technology development agreement, IP licences, and a 50/50 aeroderivative joint venture with GE Vernova. Related-party purchases from the Aero JV were $226M in Q2 and $435M in H1 2026. In power generation, GE Vernova is simultaneously supplier, JV partner and competitor.

Portfolio activity over twelve months: acquired Continental Disc (~$554M), sold PSI to Crane (~$1.2B, ~$497M gain), contributed Surface Pressure Control into a JV with Cactus ($479M total consideration, ~$225M gain, 35% retained), agreed to sell Waygate Technologies to Hexagon for ~$1.45B (closing H2 2026), and closed Chart.

Revenue compounded ~9.4% from FY2022 to FY2025 and then went flat in FY2025. FY2025 net income fell 13% on flat revenue, but the decline was substantially non-operational: "Other (income) expense" swung roughly $584M adversely, of which ~$470M was mark-to-market on equity securities moving from a $367M gain to a $103M loss, plus $107M of Chart transaction costs. Operating cash flow improved every year to $3,810M, with FCF of $2,537M (9.1% of revenue), helped by $713M of working-capital release.

Two adjustments matter for anyone reading the headline numbers.

First, H1 2026 GAAP net income attributable of $1,611M (+46% on flat revenue) is almost entirely explained by $697M of disposition gains (PSI ~$497M, SPC ~$225M) flowing through "Other (income) expense," which swung from $6M of expense to $691M of income. Underlying operating earnings were roughly flat. The profile's separate "EPS $2.60" figure corresponds to FY2025 GAAP, and the quoted 20.64x trailing P/E is computed against that FY2025 GAAP base. Because trailing GAAP earnings embed the H1 disposition gains, forward adjusted figures are the more reliable lens.

Second, Q2 cash flow should not be annualized. H1 OCF was $1,845M against Q2's $1,345M — the half was extremely back-loaded. Working capital contributed $523M in the quarter and progress collections $803M in the half (versus a $258M use in H1 2025), which are customer down-payments tied directly to the record equipment bookings, partly offset by a $348M contract-asset build. Applying FY2025's 9.1% FCF margin to a similar revenue base implies roughly $2.5B of 2026 FCF, not the ~$4.4B a Q2 run rate would suggest.

Pre-deal leverage was conservative: total debt/equity 32% and net debt/adjusted EBITDA 0.49x at year-end 2025 ($2,372M net debt against $4,820M adjusted EBITDA, a figure that reconciles to segment EBITDA of $5,100M less ~$280M of corporate cost). Any screen-driven distress flag on the FY2025 balance sheet is a data artifact — a recomputed Altman Z using actual EBIT (~$3.1B) and market equity lands near 2.70 rather than 1.73, and the negative retained-earnings term is residue from the ~$14.8B 2020 goodwill impairment, unwinding from -$10,761M (2022) to -$1,641M at June 30, 2026.

The post-close picture is materially different. Debt of $16,253M at June 30 plus $2.0B of two-year term loans drawn July 15 gives ~$18.3B; cash of $15,727M plus those proceeds less ~$13.6B deployed at close leaves roughly $4.1B, implying **estimated net debt near $14B**. Against estimated pro forma adjusted EBITDA of $5.9-6.0B (H1 2026 annualized ~$4.9B plus an estimated $1.0-1.1B for Chart), that is roughly **2.3-2.4x**. These are derived estimates, not disclosed figures — Chart's standalone EBITDA is unverified and the split between repaid and assumed Chart debt is unknown. The direction is corroborated by management's own stated target of 1.0-1.5x within 24 months, which implies $5-8B of net-debt reduction by mid-2028.

New USD coupons run 4.05% (2029) to 5.85% (2056); EUR tranches 3.226% to 4.737%, with the €3.0B designated a non-derivative net investment hedge. The interest arithmetic is the near-term earnings issue: Q2 net interest was only $66M because ~$130M of interest income on the pre-funding offset $196M of gross expense. Post-close, roughly $196M gross plus ~$27M term-loan interest less ~$35M residual interest income gives ~$188M per quarter, or about **+$490M of annualized pre-tax drag (~$0.37 per share after tax)**. Chart's contribution plausibly offsets this initially, with the $325M of year-three cost synergies providing the accretion.

Funding for deleveraging is identifiable: Waygate (~$1.45B, held for sale at $592M of net assets, which is why goodwill fell from $6,068M to $5,566M — reclassification, not impairment), ~$323M of deferred SPC proceeds, the EU-mandated Chart divestiture of unknown size, and $2.5-3.5B of annual FCF less ~$0.9B of dividends. That covers the target, with little slack if oil-linked activity weakens.

### Capital allocation

The dividend has been held at $0.23 per quarter since Q1 2025 ($456M paid in H1 2026, ~$913M annualized, covered 2.8x by FY2025 FCF, yielding 1.44% against a peer median of 1.8%). Buybacks fell from $538M (FY2023) to $484M (FY2024) to $384M (FY2025) and to **zero in both quarters of 2026**, with $1.35B still authorized, while shares outstanding rose to 992.3M. Balance-sheet repair now outranks shareholder return, and a support that contributed to 2023-2025 total return is unlikely to resume meaningfully before deleveraging is well advanced.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth engines are running at different speeds.

**IET / gas infrastructure — accelerating.** IET orders of $7,088M in Q2 doubled year over year, driven by GTE at $4,913M against $781M. IET RPO of $37.1B is an all-time high, up 19%. Management raised the Horizon 2 (2026-2028) IET orders outlook to more than $45B from a prior >$40B objective, and cited its own decision to expand capacity as the reason. The named awards — Venture Global, Cheniere/Bechtel, Golar's fourth vessel, Nigeria LNG Train 7 services extension, Aramco/SNSH compression, Dynamis and Kodiak power — are specific counterparties with disclosed scope, which is a higher grade of evidence than thematic order commentary.

Two qualifications. RPO converts ~53% within two years, 76% within five and 91% within fifteen, and the company is explicit that actual revenue timing may differ substantially and that contracts are generally terminable for convenience. IET revenue was roughly flat year over year in Q2 despite the order surge, so the backlog supports durability more than near-term growth. And Climate Technology Solutions orders collapsed to $54M from $923M — though this line is genuinely lumpy (Q1 2026 was $1,257M, and H1 2026 CTS orders of $1,312M exceeded H1 2025's $1,071M), so a single quarter should not be extrapolated in either direction.

**Chart — additive and strategically aligned.** $4.3B of revenue in air and gas handling, thermal management and lifecycle services, with stated end markets in gas infrastructure, nuclear, data centers, CCS, space and geothermal. It raises aftermarket content and extends the industrial mix. Standalone margins are unverified, which is the main gap in any accretion model.

**OFSE — contracting.** Revenue fell 8% in FY2025 and 5% year over year in Q2, with Middle East/Asia down 13% and Europe/CIS/Sub-Saharan Africa down 13%. Margin compressed 120bp to 17.5%. Roughly half the reported decline is attributable to the PSI and SPC dispositions rather than market share, and Latin America grew 15% while North America land was stable at $933M. The Q2 10-Q turned modestly more constructive than the 10-K, expecting improvement in global upstream spending through the remainder of the year "absent further oil price downside," while still guiding Middle East upstream spending lower.

**Estimate momentum.** Current-year consensus rose from $2.30 to $2.61 (+13.7% in 90 days) and next year from $2.81 to $3.16 (+12.4%), with 11 contributing analysts and acceleration in the 30-day window (+12.4% and +12.8%). Four consecutive beats — 9.3%, 15.1%, 16.2%, 26.2% — support confidence in those estimates. This is a genuine positive-revision signal, though it sits below the magnitude (>30%) associated with deep cyclical inflections, and it is arriving on flat reported revenue rather than accelerating revenue. The re-rating case rests on mix and margin, not volume.

## Competitive Landscape

Baker Hughes' economic moat sits in the IET installed base rather than in oilfield technology. Long-term product services agreements average over ten years in term and are on average only ~13% complete measured by costs incurred, which is the mechanical source of the $16.7B Gas Technology Services RPO. Once an LNG facility or FLNG vessel standardizes on a compressor and gas-turbine platform, subsequent trains and the multi-decade service agreements tend to follow — Golar's fourth vessel and the Nigeria LNG Train 7 services extension are direct evidence of that switching cost.

In distributed and behind-the-meter power generation, the competitive set is GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power and Solar Turbines/Caterpillar. The binding constraint is capacity rather than demand: management cited its decision to expand capacity when raising the Horizon 2 orders target, and both the 10-K and 10-Q flag aeroderivative supply-chain tightness and extended lead times. The counterparty structure of the data-center-linked awards should be read literally — Dynamis and Kodiak are Baker Hughes' customers; they own and deploy the power assets and serve end users. Baker Hughes supplies turbines and generators. The same applies to the Mantle Reach geothermal agreement, where Mantle Reach leads development, ownership and financing.

The GE relationship is the structural awkwardness: in heavy-duty and aeroderivative power generation, GE Vernova is supplier, 50/50 JV partner and competitor simultaneously, and loss of GE Vernova or GE Aerospace as suppliers is a disclosed material risk.

In OFSE the picture is conventional and harder. Revenue is contracting, though new subsea awards from Equinor (with a new Dusavik facility), Azule Energy, McDermott and Petrobras indicate the franchise remains competitive and that the revenue line is being reshaped by portfolio actions more than by share loss. The emerging adjacency — Leucipa's first deployment outside oil and gas in a European geothermal and lithium project, a Helmerich & Payne geothermal rig collaboration, Cordant digital agreements with SINOPEC, Petrobras and KNPC — is small but represents a credible path to reusing OFSE assets in growth markets.

Competitive trajectory is mixed but net positive: IET's moat is widening and its margin advantage is now demonstrated, while OFSE erodes. I hold competitive position at 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Post-close leverage and integration.** The largest transaction in Baker Hughes' post-2019 history, funded roughly 87% with new debt, closed four weeks before the last reporting date. Estimated 2.3-2.4x net leverage against a 1.0-1.5x target requires $5-8B of net-debt reduction by mid-2028. The identifiable sources cover it only if Waygate and the EU-mandated divestiture close on schedule and FCF holds near $2.5-3.5B. Credit-rating risk is a disclosed risk factor, and the company's own forward-looking language flags the need to generate sufficient cash to service and repay the new debt.

**The inverted oil-price relationship.** Q2 2026 Brent averaged $102.63 against $68.07 a year earlier (+51%) while Baker Hughes' revenue fell 2%. The 10-Q describes disruption across key energy corridors including the Strait of Hormuz as having "now become a structural feature," and OFSE Middle East/Asia revenue fell 13% in the quarter and 16% in the half. The CEO framed full-year confidence at the *midpoint* of guidance specifically because of Middle East uncertainty. Investors treating crude as a proxy for BKR earnings will misjudge this cycle: the transmission runs through customer capex plans, logistics and cost inflation, not spot price. Roughly half the Q2 revenue decline reflects the PSI and SPC dispositions, and Middle East activity improved 6% sequentially, so this may prove a timing shift — but it is management's stated base case, not a shock scenario.

**Tax normalization.** FY2025 tax expense of $253M on $2,877M of pre-tax income included $308M of valuation-allowance reversals ($664M in 2024). Management guides to $0.9-1.0B of 2026 cash taxes against $381M paid in H1. Gross unrecognized tax benefits are $525M ($656M with interest and penalties).

**Off-balance-sheet and contingent items.** Approximately $6.5B of surety bonds, letters of credit and guarantees at June 30 — normal for long-cycle turbomachinery, but it scales with the record RPO. The Cactus SPC JV carries reciprocal put and call options exercisable around January 2028 on the retained 35% interest, a conditional cash event in either direction. A terminated $2.5B interest-rate swap locked $91M of loss into AOCI for amortization.

**Fixed-price and consortium exposure.** With $40.1B of RPO and equipment revenue recognized over time ($8.4B in FY2025), estimate revisions on long-duration fixed-price contracts carrying joint and several consortium liability are a real earnings-quality exposure.

**Other.** Tariff risk is specifically called out for goods manufactured in Europe, Mexico and China — pointed for a company with large Florence turbomachinery manufacturing selling into US LNG and power projects. SAP Central Finance was implemented in Q2 2026, modifying finance processes and internal control over financial reporting; disclosure controls were concluded effective, but large ERP cutovers warrant monitoring in Q3/Q4 filings. The energy-transition risk factor has reversed direction — the company now warns that a *slowdown* in the transition could hurt demand for its clean-energy technologies. The C3.ai securities class action was dismissed as to the company on March 12, 2026, removing one overhang.

**Governance and insider activity.** Lorenzo Simonelli holds the combined Chairman/CEO role. Across roughly twelve months of Form 4 filings through July 1, 2026 there were **no open-market purchases** by executives or directors and a steady pattern of sales: Simonelli exercised options at $35.55-$35.70 strikes and sold roughly $38M across three dates while retaining ~703,444 shares; the CFO sold 20,000 shares plus a separate line to zero; the Chief Growth & Experience Officer sold roughly 78% of her position across two transactions; the Chart segment head sold at $66.42. The CEO pattern is a routine exercise-and-sell consistent with option expiry rather than a valuation signal, and annual grants on March 8, 2026 replenished exposure. The defensible reading is an absence of insider conviction buying alongside consistent selling into the $54-$66 range, with the highest-price sale above today's level. Compensation design is reasonable — Adjusted EBITDA, FCF, ROIC and relative TSR, with a distinct Horizon 2 (2026-2028) PSU class tied to the named plan — and indexed five-year TSR of 248 versus a peer group at 165 means high realized CEO pay largely tracked shareholder outcomes. Note that the >$45B IET orders target is a figure management is compensated against, which is relevant when assessing how aggressive it is.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.9% against 32.6% one-year realized is fair (2.3pp spread, 42nd percentile within the chain), with a flat term structure (34.9% near versus 36.2% at six months) and a ±4.6% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Put/call skew reads -1.9% near-dated widening to -3.6% at six months; the data source characterizes near-term skew as balanced. Since the July 28 snapshot, ATM IV fell from 37.5% to 34.9% while the price rose 5.3%, and put/call open interest declined from 0.60 to 0.42 — post-earnings volatility compression with reduced put positioning. Open interest remains call-tilted at 0.42, which does not corroborate a bearish view. The one flag is in the December tenor: put/call volume ratio of 11.80 and a single $60 December put line trading 600 contracts against 138 open interest (4.3x), consistent with someone hedging the integration and oil-path window into year end. Overall, options positioning is neutral-to-constructive near term with identifiable longer-dated hedging — it neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental read, and offers no volatility-based entry edge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $85 (+33%)

The mix shift completes and the market re-rates Baker Hughes as a power and gas-infrastructure company rather than an oilfield service company. IET margin holds above 20% and expands toward the low 20s on the record equipment backlog and a growing services annuity; Chart delivers EBITDA at the upper end of the estimated $1.0-1.1B range with the $325M synergy target pulled forward; Horizon 2 IET orders track above the >$45B outlook as data-center power demand converts through customers like Dynamis and Kodiak; Waygate and the EU-mandated divestiture close on schedule, pulling net leverage below 1.5x by late 2027 and reopening the buyback. On $3.40-3.50 of 2028 earnings power at 25x, the shares reach the mid-$80s, in line with the high analyst target. The order momentum plus 12-14% estimate revisions plus four consecutive beats give this path real support.

### Bear Case — $49 (-23%)

Middle East disruption persists as management now expects, keeping OFSE — still nearly half of revenue — in decline with further margin compression below 17%. The interest step-up (~$0.37 per share) and cash-tax normalization ($0.9-1.0B) consume the Chart contribution, leaving 2027 EPS nearer $2.90 than $3.16. Chart integration slips, purchase-accounting amortization exceeds expectations, or the EU-mandated divestiture is larger and lower-priced than assumed, pushing the deleveraging timeline past mid-2028 and keeping buybacks suspended. Long-dated fixed-price equipment contracts produce negative estimate revisions. The multiple compresses to 16-17x on lower estimates, taking the stock toward $49, above the $51 low analyst target but consistent with a de-rating back toward Halliburton and Weatherford multiples.

### Base Case — $70 (+10%)

IET margin holds near 20%, OFSE stabilizes at mid-teens margins as international spending improves modestly, Chart is roughly EPS-neutral in year one, and 2027 EPS lands near $3.16-3.30. At 21-22x, that is $69-70, consistent with the analyst mean of $71.39 and just below the median of $73. Adding the 1.44% dividend gives roughly 11% total return over twelve months. Probability-weighted across the three scenarios (25% bull / 55% base / 20% bear) gives an expected return near +9%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Horizon: 12 months.**

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher, so the reasoning needs to be explicit. Since the July review the price rose 5.1% ($60.59 to $63.705) while 2027 consensus rose 12.4%, so forward valuation improved from 21.6x to 20.2x. That is a better setup, not a worse one. What offsets it is the confirmed conversion of pre-funded cash into roughly $14B of estimated net debt, a suspended buyback, and a ~$490M annualized interest step-up — genuine changes in balance-sheet resilience that arrived after the prior review and that justify the small reduction in financial health and the overall score to 6.0. The energy-services group has a poor record of delivering company-specific outperformance versus the broad market, which raises the evidence bar for initiating here; a 10% base-case return with 23% bear-case downside does not clear it.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 2026 10-Q (expected late October) disclosing actual post-close net leverage at or below 2.0x on a reported basis, with Chart segment EBITDA margin at or above 23% — this resolves the largest verification gap in the thesis.
2. IET segment EBITDA margin at or above 21% for two consecutive quarters *with* OFSE margin stabilizing at 17.5% or better and OFSE revenue no longer declining year over year.
3. A price near $55-57 (roughly 17.5-18x 2027E) on unchanged estimates, which would restore a 20%+ base-case return.
4. Resumption of buybacks against the $1.35B authorization, signaling management's own confidence that the deleveraging path is secured.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. 2027 consensus EPS revised below $2.90 (a ~8% cut from $3.16), or two consecutive quarters of negative revisions.
2. IET book-to-bill falling below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, or a Horizon 2 orders outlook cut from >$45B.
3. Evidence the deleveraging plan is slipping: Waygate failing to close in H2 2026, an EU-mandated divestiture materially larger than assumed, or management withdrawing the 1.0-1.5x within-24-months target.
4. A material EAC charge on long-duration fixed-price equipment contracts, or an adverse control finding following the SAP cFIN cutover.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October, which will be the first consolidated period including Chart, the first presentation of the three-segment structure, and the first disclosure of actual post-close net debt, interest expense and Chart segment economics. That print resolves three of the four largest unverified inputs in this analysis.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes is executing a legitimate transformation. IET is now the larger, faster-growing and higher-margin segment; its order book is backed by named LNG and power-generation counterparties rather than thematic commentary; the $16.7B Gas Technology Services RPO on contracts averaging over ten years and ~13% complete is a durable annuity; and Chart extends the industrial and aftermarket mix into nuclear, data-center thermal management and gas infrastructure. Consensus is following, with 2026 and 2027 EPS up 13.7% and 12.4% over 90 days after four consecutive beats.

The offsets are equally concrete and all landed in the last quarter. Net debt goes from $526M to an estimated ~$14B; the buyback is off while the share count rises; interest expense steps up roughly $490M annualized; cash taxes rise to a guided $0.9-1.0B as valuation-allowance releases end; OFSE — still nearly half of revenue — is contracting with Middle East disruption now management's base case rather than a risk scenario; and insiders have sold consistently without a single open-market purchase over twelve months. At 24.4x 2026E and 20.2x 2027E, roughly the peer median on a forward basis, the shares price much of the mix-shift story already.

Overall score 6.0. **HOLD.** Existing holders should stay long the IET and Chart thesis through the October print, which will convert the largest estimates in this analysis — Chart's EBITDA, actual post-close leverage, and the real interest run rate — into disclosed facts. New capital should wait for either that confirmation at leverage of 2.0x or better, or a price near $55-57.
