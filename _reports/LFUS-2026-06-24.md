**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Littelfuse is a diversified industrial-protection components maker (circuit protection, power control, and sensing) that screens poorly on trailing metrics for one reason: a $301.2M non-cash goodwill impairment of its Electronics-Semiconductor (Dortmund wafer fab) unit in Q4'25 turned a profitable year into a reported net loss of -$2.89 per share. Strip out the impairment and the business expanded gross margin to 38.0% (from 35.9%), grew revenue 8.9% to $2,386.3M, lifted segment operating income 34% to $363.9M, and generated $366.1M of free cash flow. Q1 FY2026 confirmed an accelerating upcycle: revenue +18.5% YoY to $657.0M, EPS of $2.96 versus $1.75, operating margin up to 15.4%, and a year-end backlog of $1,070.9M against $664.9M a year earlier. The trailing P/E of -289x and forward P/E shown in the data feed are mechanical artifacts of the impairment and carry no analytical content.

On normalized earnings power the picture is clean and improving. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised to $14.76 (+14.4% over 90 days) and next-year to $17.27 (+15.2%), and the company has beaten in four straight quarters, twice by ~14-20%. At $466.96 that puts the stock at roughly 32x current-year and ~27x next-year EPS, with a PEG near 2 against high-teens growth. The stock benefits from the AI/data-center power-protection theme through Electronics, the Basler grid/utility acquisition in Industrial, and a recovery off the 2024 electronics-destocking trough, all real and broad-based.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because of price and risk, not thesis. The stock has roughly doubled off its FY25 low and trades near a 52-week high, capturing most of the operating recovery. Against that, three unresolved items prevent paying up here: $238.5M of semiconductor goodwill remains exposed to further impairment after the new management team's strategic reassessment; CEO pay of $19.6M reported ($25.2M actually paid) in a net-loss year with severe TSR underperformance is a genuine governance flag; and an open fuse-related product recall carries a "reasonably possible" but unquantified loss. The setup is a quality cyclical with momentum, fully priced, awaiting either a pullback or post-Investor-Day confirmation.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1927 with roughly 17,000 employees across more than 20 countries, Littelfuse manufactures circuit-protection, power-control, and sensing components. Its GICS classification of Information Technology / Electronic Components understates how diversified and industrial the business actually is. Three reportable segments:

- **Electronics (~56% of sales, $1,345.5M FY25):** the broadest protection portfolio in the industry (fuses, PTC resettable fuses, TVS diodes, varistors, gas discharge tubes, electromechanical switches (C&K)) plus a power-semiconductor business (MOSFETs, IGBTs, SiC) anchored by the Dortmund 200mm wafer fab. Split into Passive Products & Sensors and Semiconductor.
- **Transportation (~28%, $676.4M):** passenger-car protection/sensing, commercial-vehicle power distribution and switches (Carling), and automotive sensors being rationalized.
- **Industrial (~15%, $364.4M):** industrial fuses, protection relays, controls/sensors, electrical-shock protection (Western Automation), and the recently added Basler Electric (grid, utility, power-generation, data-center excitation and protection).

The company sells to over 4,000 direct customers and distributors and more than 100,000 end customers. Arrow Electronics is the only customer above 10% of sales (9.5% in FY25). Roughly 65% of sales are outside the U.S. and ~24% are to China. Stated growth priorities (data centers, AI infrastructure, aerospace and defense, battery energy storage, and grid/utility infrastructure) all lever the secular shift toward higher voltage and higher energy density.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 reported loss is an accounting artifact. Pretax income was a thin +$3.6M; because the $301.2M impairment is not tax-deductible, the $75.3M tax expense produced a ~2,088% effective rate and a -$71.7M net result. Underneath, the operating engine strengthened:

- **Revenue $2,386.3M, +8.9% YoY**, recovering off the 2024 cyclical trough of $2,190.8M (the multi-year arc: $2,513.9M in 2022 → $2,362.7M in 2023 → $2,190.8M in 2024 → $2,386.3M in 2025).
- **Gross margin 38.0%** versus 35.9%.
- **Segment operating income $363.9M** versus $270.8M: Electronics +29.6% to $220.1M, Transportation +44.7% to $84.8M, Industrial +39.5% to $59.0M.
- **Free cash flow $366.1M** (15.3% margin); operating cash flow $433.8M, up ~$66M YoY.

Q1 FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) confirms acceleration: net income $75.1M ($2.96 diluted), operating margin 15.4% versus 12.7%. Electronics operating income rose ~50% to a 19.4% margin, Industrial +59% on the Basler addition, Transportation +27%. Electronics-Semiconductor revenue rebounded to $175.7M from $158.3M. The backlog, up 61%, is a strong leading indicator across all three segments.

The balance sheet supports a 7.5 on Financial Health. Cash was $563.4M at FY25; current ratio 2.69; debt/equity ~0.63. The Altman Z of 2.72 sits in the grey zone only because the impairment mechanically depresses EBIT; it is not a distress signal. In March 2026 the company retired its $300M term loan, expanded the revolver from $700M to $800M, and extended maturity to 2031, cutting total debt to $631.5M (Q1'26) from $802.6M. There is no refinancing cliff and no covenant stress. Earnings quality is high: FCF conversion well above net income (which the impairment depressed) and strong cash generation through the cycle. The four-for-four beat record (surprises of 14.1%, 4.4%, 3.4%, 20.2%) raises confidence in forward consensus.

The one efficiency note: buybacks have effectively paused ($27.4M in FY25, $0 in Q1'26 against $270.6M still authorized) while capital is steered to M&A and deleveraging. That is defensible given the stock's run, but it means no EPS support from repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest part of the thesis, and growth_prospects is the most reliable dimension for sorting winners in this environment. The evidence is broad-based rather than a single-company story:

- **Revenue acceleration**: from the 2024 trough (-7.3% YoY) to +8.9% in FY25 to +18.5% in Q1'26.
- **Estimate revision velocity**: current-quarter EPS revised +13.3% over 90 days, current-year +14.4%, next-year +15.2%. These are positive across every horizon and accelerating slightly into the most recent 30 days. Rising estimates of this magnitude combined with high-teens revenue growth typically precede further consensus catch-up.
- **Backlog +61% YoY** at year-end, spread across all segments.
- **Secular overlay**: data-center power protection (Electronics), grid/utility/data-center power-gen via Basler (Industrial), and electrification/energy-storage demand. This aligns with the broader confirmation of data-center load growth visible across the power and electrical-equipment supply chain.

I treat the estimate-revision signal seriously, but it falls short of the highest-conviction threshold for two reasons: the revisions are ~14% rather than >30%, and the stock is not cheap relative to those rising estimates (it trades at ~27x next-year EPS, well above the sub-20x range where the signal is most powerful). The growth is durable and the direction is right; it does not by itself force a BUY at this price.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline P/E figures are meaningless: both trailing and forward show negative numbers driven entirely by the impairment. The correct anchor is forward/normalized EPS. At $466.96 against consensus current-year EPS of $14.76 and next-year of $17.27, the stock trades at roughly **32x current-year and ~27x next-year**, with a PEG near 2 on ~16% earnings growth.

Peer comparison is unhelpfully noisy: the Electronic Components median P/E of 85.0 is distorted by Corning (85x), Vishay (5,518x), and Knowles (55x), several of which carry their own impairment or trough-earnings distortions. On a clean forward basis, ~27-32x for a high-teens-growth, 38%-gross-margin, 15%-FCF-margin diversified protection franchise is a full multiple. It is not a discount.

The valuation score is 4.5, and I am deliberately skeptical of any instinct to reward this stock as "cheap." Prior appreciation is not a valuation input; the doubling off the low is not itself a reason to mark down. The issue is the opposite: at ~27-32x forward with the secular recovery now consensus (Zacks flipped to #1 Strong Buy, the data-center narrative is widely cited), the stock is priced for execution, not for surprise. The consensus mean target of $486.25 sits ~4% above spot and the median $510 ~9% above, modest upside that does not represent the "analyst targets are floors" dynamic, because that principle applies to cheap stocks with rising estimates, and this stock is not cheap on forward earnings. The asymmetry is roughly balanced to slightly unfavorable here, which is why valuation caps the overall score rather than supporting it.

## Competitive Landscape

Littelfuse is a scaled, design-in incumbent with the broadest protection portfolio in its industry, competing against larger players including Eaton, Infineon, onsemi, STMicro, TE Connectivity, Sensata, Vishay, Bourns, and Amphenol. The moat rests on portfolio breadth, embedded engineering relationships, in-region manufacturing, and safety certifications rather than scale or cost leadership; the company concedes it "may not always be able to compete on price."

The competitive trajectory is mixed, supporting a 6.0. On the positive side, the Basler and Western Automation deals deliberately tilt the portfolio toward higher-margin, more-secular grid/utility/data-center power protection, and segment operating margins are expanding across all three units. Customer concentration is low (top customer 9.5%). On the negative side, the power-semiconductor push into SiC/IGBT against Infineon, STMicro, and onsemi is capital-intensive and sub-scale, and the Dortmund stumble shows the company is not positioned to win in that arena. The Electronics-Semiconductor unit's impairment is a competitive admission as much as an accounting event. Net trajectory is stable-to-improving in the core protection franchise, deteriorating-to-uncertain in power semis.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score of 5.0 reflects specific, partly-unpriced idiosyncratic exposures rather than generic cyclicality (sector cyclicality is permanently priced and not a novel risk):

1. **Further semiconductor goodwill impairment.** After the write-down, $238.5M of goodwill remains in the Electronics-Semiconductor unit, with management explicitly warning of more if Dortmund volumes/strategy disappoint. The Industrial Controls & Sensors unit passed its 2025 test by only 22%, the thinnest cushion of any unit and already impaired once in 2024.
2. **Dortmund integration failure.** The fab acquired from Elmos (closed December 2024) underperformed badly enough to trigger impairment within twelve months, a direct hit to the acquisition-led growth thesis.
3. **Open product recall.** A customer recall "potentially due to certain Littelfuse fuses" carries a loss deemed "reasonably possible," with no accrual and no estimable range.
4. **Material weakness, recently remediated.** Inventory valuation/existence errors at non-U.S. plants forced a $13.5M out-of-period adjustment. Management states this was remediated in 2025, but Basler's controls were excluded from the FY25 assessment, leaving integration control risk.
5. **Tariffs / China / commodities.** 24% of sales to China; tariff exposure and a Q4'25 precious-metals cost spike (silver, gold, ruthenium); FX swung from a $9.2M gain in 2024 to a $16.6M loss in FY25.
6. **Leadership transition.** New CEO and CFO mid-strategic-reset; execution is unproven and the May 14, 2026 Investor Day is the first articulation of long-term strategy.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is two-sided and offers no strong directional confirmation. Near-term ATM IV of 53.8% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 37.3% (a 16.5pp spread), so the market is pricing elevated uncertainty: an implied move of ±10.6% on the nearest expiry and ±28.5% out six months. IV percentile within the chain is low (9th), and the term structure is flat, suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than clustered on a single catalyst. The put/call skew is balanced (-2.5%) and put/call volume is effectively nil, but the put/call open-interest ratio of 3.09 is elevated, consistent with hedging of a position that has doubled. Net read: rich IV argues against chasing here and supports patience for a better entry, without signaling directional conviction either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The cyclical recovery has further to run, backlog converts to revenue, and the data-center/grid/electrification tailwinds drive Electronics and Industrial above consensus. The new management team stabilizes the semiconductor unit at the May Investor Day, avoiding further impairment, and Basler integrates cleanly. EPS reaches the high end of the $17+ next-year range and beats continue (the four-for-four record supports this), driving further re-rating and upside toward the high end of the analyst range (~$535 target).

**Bear Case.** Another semiconductor impairment hits the remaining $238.5M (and/or the thin-cushion Industrial Controls unit), confirming the Dortmund deal as a structural error. The product recall accrues a material loss, the cyclical recovery decelerates as China/tariff pressure bites, and the multiple compresses on flatter estimates, with downside toward the low end of the analyst range (~$390 target).

**Base Case.** High-teens earnings growth continues, the company avoids fresh impairment but the semiconductor strategy remains a work in progress, and consensus drifts toward the $510 median target, roughly 9% upside over twelve months, modest given the run.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the 12-month earnings trajectory is positive and catalyst-rich, but the price already discounts most of it.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a pullback that restores asymmetry (roughly 20x next-year EPS, i.e., the mid-$300s); (2) the May 14 Investor Day delivers credible long-term targets and a clear resolution of the semiconductor strategy that removes further-impairment overhang; (3) two consecutive quarters of double-digit revenue growth with the backlog holding above ~$1B and estimate revisions continuing to rise.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) a second goodwill impairment of the remaining $238.5M semiconductor goodwill or the thin-cushion Industrial Controls unit; (2) a material accrual on the product recall, or re-emergence of the inventory material weakness (including at Basler); (3) revenue growth decelerating below ~5% YoY alongside falling estimate revisions.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected early August) and the Q3 UK pension settlement charge ($6-8M non-cash). The semiconductor strategy update flagged for the Investor Day is the most important near-term swing factor.

## Conclusion

Littelfuse is a high-quality diversified protection-components business in a genuine, broad-based cyclical recovery, with secular data-center and grid tailwinds, expanding margins, strong free cash flow, a clean balance sheet, and an accelerating order book. The FY25 net loss is a non-cash impairment artifact, and anyone screening on trailing P/E will misread the company entirely. The earnings inflection is real, the estimate-revision momentum is positive across every horizon, and the four-for-four beat record supports forward consensus.

The recommendation is HOLD, not BUY, for reasons of price and risk rather than thesis. At ~32x current-year and ~27x next-year EPS near a 52-week high after roughly doubling off the low, the stock prices in the recovery, leaving only modest upside to the $510 median target and balanced-to-unfavorable asymmetry. Three unresolved items (exposed semiconductor goodwill, an open unquantified product recall, and an outsized-CEO-pay-in-a-loss-year governance flag) argue against paying up here. The constructive trajectory (overall score 5.7 → 6.7 across prior reports, with Financial Health and Management Quality both improving as new data confirmed the operating recovery) is intact; the current 5.95 reflects a richer entry price absorbing some of that improvement rather than fundamental deterioration. I would add on a pullback toward the mid-$300s or on a clean Investor-Day resolution of the semiconductor strategy. Until then, hold for the earnings momentum and monitor the impairment and recall contingencies.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward multiples were understated — at $466.96 against $14.76/$17.27 EPS the stock trades at ~32x current-year / ~27x next-year (PEG ~2), not the ~26x/~22x (PEG ~1.4) stated, making the valuation notably richer than the report's narrative implied.
