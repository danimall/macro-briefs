## Executive Summary

Lululemon has moved from a premium-multiple compounder to a statistically cheap apparel company with a shrinking core market. The arithmetic that matters is at the segment profit line: in Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended May 3, 2026), Americas operating income fell $180.9M year over year while China Mainland added $49.6M and Rest of World *lost* $3.4M despite 13% constant-currency revenue growth. Consolidated operating income fell $161.7M to $276.9M, an 11.2% margin against 18.5% a year earlier. The "international offsets domestic" argument fails at the profit line by roughly 3.6 times. Americas still produced 71.0% of FY2025 segment profit; its declining share of the total reflects margin collapse, not diversification.

Management's June 4, 2026 guidance reset put FY2026 diluted EPS at $10.95–$11.15 versus prior guidance of $12.10–$12.30 and FY2025 actual of $13.26, with North America guided to a high-single-digit revenue decline and Q2 to a low-double-digit decline. Consensus has followed: current-quarter estimates are down 34.5% over 90 days, current year down 10.3%, next year down 14.1%. Over the trailing 30 days revisions have flattened (current quarter 0.0%, current year -0.1%, next year -0.9%). The cut cycle has paused, but at a materially lower level, and there is no operating evidence yet that Americas comps have inflected.

Against that, the balance sheet is not in question — $1,514.7M of cash, no funded debt, an undrawn $600M revolver to October 2030, Altman Z of 4.19, and $1,042.5M of remaining buyback authorization equal to 7.7% of the economic market cap. That combination is why this is a REDUCE rather than an AVOID: there is no refinancing cliff, no covenant risk, and no liquidation dynamic. At 10.8x the guided FY2026 EPS midpoint, with a base case of $132, a bear case of $88 and a probability-weighted expected return near +4% against a 4.6% risk-free rate, the asymmetry is unfavorable and the next dated catalyst, the Q2 print in early September, is guided to be the worst quarter of the cycle.

## Company Overview

Lululemon designs and sells technical athletic apparel, footwear and accessories under a single owned brand through a vertically integrated direct-to-consumer model in 30 countries. FY2025 (year ended February 1, 2026) revenue was $11,102.6M, up 4.9%, split Americas $7,847.0M (-1.0%, 70.7% of revenue), China Mainland $1,754.8M (+28.9%, 15.8%), and Rest of World $1,500.8M (+15.6%, 13.5%). Category mix is women's 63%, men's 24%, accessories 13%.

Company-operated stores grew from 767 to 811 during FY2025 and to 816 by May 3, 2026, but US stores declined from 379 to 377 in Q1, the first net US closures in the periods reviewed. Sales per square foot fell to $1,426 from $1,574, a 9.4% decline, against square footage growth of roughly 11%. That divergence is the cleanest single operating metric in the file: the company is adding selling space into falling productivity.

Supply is concentrated. Approximately 51 finished-goods vendors with the top five at 47%; roughly 65 fabric suppliers with the top five at 48% and the largest at 20%. Fabric originates 34% from Taiwan and 29% from China Mainland. There are no long-term contracts with the majority of suppliers, and the 10-K acknowledges that intellectual property in the fabrics is generally owned or controlled by suppliers rather than by Lululemon. The moat is brand and execution rather than patents, a distinction that matters for terminal value when the 10-K itself names "dupe" products as a risk factor.

Leadership is in transition. The prior CEO departed effective January 31, 2026; CFO Meghan Frank and President/CCO André Maestrini have served as interim co-CEOs since; Heidi O'Neill, a 26-year Nike veteran most recently President of Consumer, Product and Brand, begins September 8, 2026. A proxy contest launched by founder Chip Wilson (approximately 8.7% of common) on December 29, 2025 was resolved by a Cooperation Agreement dated May 26, 2026, with Marc Maurer (former co-CEO of On Holding) and Laura Gentile joining the board, confirmed by June 25, 2026 Form 4 director grants.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year P&L shows a business that kept growing revenue while losing earnings power:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 8,110.5 | 9,619.3 | 10,588.1 | 11,102.6 |
| Gross margin | — | — | 59.2% | 56.6% |
| Operating income ($M) | 1,328.4 | 2,132.7 | 2,505.7 | 2,210.6 |
| Operating margin | 16.4% | 22.2% | 23.7% | 19.9% |
| Net income ($M) | 854.8 | 1,550.2 | 1,814.6 | 1,579.2 |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $14.64 | $13.26 |
| Diluted shares (K) | 128,017 | 127,060 | 123,935 | 119,068 |

FY2025 gross profit was $6,284.1M versus $6,270.8M, up 0.2% on 4.9% revenue growth. One qualifier: FY2024 was a 53-week year and FY2025 a 52-week year (derived from disclosed period-end dates), so underlying growth was probably 1.5–2 points higher than reported. The company did not quantify the 53rd week in the sections reviewed.

Q1 FY2026 accelerated the deterioration. Revenue $2,471.6M, +4.3% reported but +2% constant currency (FX contributed $52.2M). Gross margin 54.2%, down 410bps. SG&A 42.9% of revenue versus 39.8%. Operating income $276.9M, down 37%. Net income $195.0M, down 38%. Diluted EPS $1.69 versus $2.60. Total comps +1% reported and -2% constant currency, with Americas -5% (-6% cc).

Management's gross margin bridge for Q1 (relayed from the June 4 call, not primary-verified): tariffs -280bps gross, offset by +100bps of enterprise efficiency; markdowns -40bps; fixed-cost deleverage -140bps; FX +60bps. The composition matters. Markdowns contributed only 40bps of the 410bp decline, which argues the margin problem is tariffs plus deleverage rather than an inventory liquidation spiral. Americas product margin fell 500bps to 66.3%; China Mainland product margin *rose* to 79.0%, and China's segment operating margin of 42.4% now sits about 17 points above the Americas'.

**Tariffs.** FY2025 gross profit was reduced approximately $275M on an unmitigated basis by higher tariffs and de minimis removal, roughly 248bps of revenue. The company remitted $216M of IEEPA tariffs during FY2025 and had paid $230M cumulatively by May 3, 2026. The Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026, but the administration re-imposed tariffs under alternative authorities and the de minimis removal is unaffected. Refund claims including interest have been submitted, and the company has explicitly **not recognized an asset**. Applying Q1's 280bps to guided FY2026 revenue implies roughly $310M of gross tariff drag this year, above FY2025's $275M. The one-time refund option exists against a rising permanent cost.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $1,514.7M (from $1,807.2M at year-end); total assets $8,531.4M; equity $4,825.6M; no funded debt drawn; $600M revolver maturing October 15, 2030 with only $6.4M of letters of credit; covenant compliant. Lease liabilities total $2,136.0M, with ROU assets jumping to $1,948.7M from $1,630.2M in one quarter, consistent with $278.5M of distribution-center leases committed but not commenced. Current ratio 2.26, Altman Z 4.19, ROA 18.7% (from 23.9%), ROE 31.8%. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/9 correctly reflects a solvent business with a negative rate of change.

**Inventory, previously a red flag, has been corrected.** The FY2025 year-end balance of $1,700.8M was up 17.9% against 4.9% revenue growth. By May 3, 2026 the balance was $1,687.1M, up only 2% year over year, and inventory was a $23.6M source of cash. FY2026 guidance is a mid-single-digit balance increase with units slightly down. The caveat is that clean inventory can reflect clearance at lower realized prices, which the 500bp Americas product margin decline is consistent with.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,602.5M, down $670.2M, with roughly $357.5M of the decline attributed to working capital and tax-payment timing. Capex $680.8M, free cash flow $921.7M, an 8.3% FCF margin. Q1 FY2026 OCF swung to +$214.4M from -$119.0M, but that must not be annualized: roughly 37% of full-year operating profit lands in Q4, and the quarter carried large tax-timing effects. A normalized view — FY2025's $1,602M adjusted for the guided ~20% EPS decline with roughly flat D&A of $496.2M, less guided capex of $700–720M — points to approximately **$650–790M of FY2026 free cash flow**, a 4.8%–5.8% yield on the economic market cap. That is an estimate, not company guidance.

**Cost structure.** FY2025 SG&A rose $304.2M (+8.1%) to 36.6% of revenue from 35.5%, including $5.1M of proxy contest costs and $15.2M of executive transition costs. Q1 FY2026 head office costs rose $63.1M including $11.4M of proxy costs alone, roughly $0.07 per diluted share after tax. With the Cooperation Agreement dated May 26, 2026, that line should largely cease after Q2. Working the other way, marketing is guided from 5.6% of sales to 6.0%–6.5%, roughly $72M of incremental recurring spend on $11.075B of guided revenue. Bulls should not treat FY2026's depressed margin as purely transitory.

One accounting item worth flagging: FY2025 included a $26.3M reversal of stock-based compensation expense in Q2 driven by a change in the assessed probability of achieving PSU performance conditions. This is a routine ASC 718 mechanic and I would not read management intent into it, but it flattered reported FY2025 earnings and is consistent with internal forecasts falling below performance thresholds.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is revenue of $11.0–$11.15B, flat to down 1%, with EPS of $10.95–$11.15 versus $13.26. Segment guidance: North America high-single-digit decline, China Mainland approximately +20%, Rest of World mid-teens. Q2 is guided to revenue of $2.45–$2.475B (-2% to -3%), gross margin down roughly 410bps, and EPS of $1.76–$1.81 against $3.10.

Consensus tracks guidance closely: current year $11.04, next year $11.46 — implying only 3.8% EPS growth in FY2027 off a depressed base. Twenty-two analysts cover next year and have cut it 14.1% in 90 days. The recommendation distribution is 1 buy, 29 hold, 3 sell, 1 strong sell. Over three months the count moved from 2 buys/29 holds/1 sell/0 strong sell to 1 buy/29 holds/3 sells/1 strong sell, a net deterioration in the distribution.

Three growth qualifiers matter:

**China is decelerating and the Q1 print was calendar-flattered.** Management stated the Chinese New Year shift added eight percentage points to China Mainland's Q1 growth. Reported +20% (+13% cc) less roughly eight points implies an underlying constant-currency rate near 5%, down from +41% in FY2024 and +28.9% in FY2025. Maestrini added that China momentum slowed toward the end of the quarter as negative commentary increased.

**Rest of World grows revenue while shrinking profit.** +13% constant-currency revenue produced a $3.4M *decline* in segment operating income because margin compressed 350bps to 18.7%. Growth at negative incremental profit is not value creation, though China's 42.4% margin demonstrates that leverage is achievable at scale.

**The Americas decline is management-attributed to brand and product, not macro.** On the June 4 call, Frank cited "spikes of negative commentary in the media and on social channels with regard to our brand, which had an impact on traffic," and product launches that "have not met our expectations," specifically that the "new look of yoga" campaign across the Align and Groove franchises "has not produced the anticipated halo effect." Self-help is underway: chasing 20% more volume than last year, compressing mainline product development from 18–24 months to 15–16 months with a 12–14 month goal, targeting 35% assortment newness versus 23% (currently ~30%), and stepping up marketing. Even on the compressed cycle, product decisions made today reach shelves in roughly five quarters.

The estimate-revision pattern deserves explicit treatment because the magnitude cuts both ways. A +30% 90-day revision would be a strong buy signal; here the 90-day revisions are *negative* 10.3% (current year) and *negative* 14.1% (next year). The 30-day flattening is a stabilization signal, not a positive inflection, and I weight it as removing a reason to be more bearish rather than as a reason to be bullish.

Growth Prospects: **3.5** — unchanged from the prior review. Nothing in the last 30 days changed the trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

Share count discipline first. As of May 29, 2026 there were 108,437,957 common shares plus 5,115,961 exchangeable shares with matching special voting shares, or approximately 113,554,000 economically equivalent shares. The widely quoted $12.95B market cap reconciles to common shares only (108.4M × $119.375 = $12.94B). The economically correct figure is **$13.55B** (113.554M × $119.375). Reported diluted WASO of 115,482K already includes the exchangeable shares, so EPS-based multiples were never distorted.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price (canonical basis) | $119.375 |
| Economic market cap | $13.55B |
| TTM diluted EPS (derived: $13.26 − $2.60 + $1.69) | $12.35 |
| TTM P/E | 9.7x |
| FY2026 guided EPS midpoint $11.05 | **10.8x** |
| FY2027 consensus $11.46 | 10.4x |
| EV ex-lease ($13.55B − $1.515B cash) | $12.04B |
| FY2025 EBITDA (op income $2,210.6M + D&A $496.2M) | $2,706.8M |
| **EV/EBITDA (ex-lease)** | **4.45x** |
| FY2025 FCF yield | 6.80% |
| FY2026E FCF yield (est. $650–790M) | 4.8%–5.8% |
| Remaining buyback authorization / economic cap | 7.7% |

Against peer medians (P/E 21.8x, yield 2.1%), LULU at 10.8x forward is a 50% discount. But the peer set is heterogeneous and the comparison is less flattering than it looks. KTB trades at 15.5x and COLM at 14.8x, both lower-growth, wholesale-weighted businesses without negative core-market comps. RL at 24.9x sits within 4% of its 52-week high with positive momentum. LULU's discount is not obviously excessive relative to a business guiding its 71%-of-profit segment to a high-single-digit revenue decline with escalating input costs.

The valuation case therefore requires a specific reason the market is wrong. The candidates:

1. **Buyback support.** $1,042.5M remaining, equal to 7.7% of the economic market cap. Q1 FY2026 repurchases were 2,170,890 shares at approximately $166.6 average, with declining monthly averages of $177.68, $163.08 and $155.68, and 0.9M shares for $111.0M post-quarter through May 29 at roughly $123. Deployment is continuing at current prices.
2. **The unrecognized tariff refund.** $230M pre-tax, roughly $2.03 per economically equivalent share, or about 18% of one year of guided net income. It sits outside the balance sheet, outside guidance, and outside consensus.
3. **Structurally lower share count.** Diluted shares fell from 128,017K in FY2022 to 115,482K in Q1 FY2026, a 9.8% reduction, permanently.

The offsets are equally specific. The denominator is unstable — FY2027 consensus has been cut 14.1% in 90 days and implies an Americas inflection not yet evidenced. Implied FY2026 operating income is approximately $1.79B on a ~16.2% margin, roughly 29% below the FY2024 peak of $2,505.7M and about 750bps of operating-margin compression in two years. And the sell side sees essentially no upside: mean target $127.92 (+7.2%), median $122.50 (+2.6%). The principle that analyst targets act as floors on inflection stocks does not apply where estimates are falling — here the targets are following the estimates down.

On my numbers the forward multiple has *expanded* since the prior review (price +5.3% to $119.375 against marginally lower next-year estimates), so there is no valuation improvement to record.

Valuation: **4.5** — cheap on trailing metrics against an earnings base that is still resetting, with no identified re-rating catalyst inside twelve months. The low multiple functions as a warning rather than a signal.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's competition discussion is generic — "highly competitive," "frequent new entrants," no named competitors. The revealing disclosures are elsewhere: the dupe risk factor combined with the admission that fabric IP generally sits with suppliers, and the newly framed risk that AI-enabled shopping tools may reduce the company's control over consumer decision-making. For a business whose margin structure depends on full-price DTC sell-through and brand-directed traffic, the second is more pointed than it would be for a wholesale-dependent peer.

External evidence: Vuori is entering China (WSJ, June 23, 2026), targeting the one segment carrying Lululemon's earnings growth. Alo Yoga's parent agreed to separate the brand from its wholesale blank-apparel business (Inc., June 24, 2026), which the source frames as a possible precursor to a sale — speculation, not a confirmed transaction.

The open question I cannot resolve is whether the North America decline is company-specific share loss or a category-wide premium-activewear slowdown. No comparative growth data for Alo, Vuori, On or Nike in the relevant period was obtainable. The available evidence leans company-specific: management attributed the shortfall to its own brand commentary and its own product launches, and simultaneously guided China to +20% and Rest of World to mid-teens growth. A company absorbing a category-wide demand shock would not typically guide two of three segments to double-digit growth. The plausible alternative is that the US premium-athleisure category is saturating while newer international markets remain in their growth phase, which would be structural rather than share loss, and would carry worse terminal-value implications, not better.

Competitive Position: **3.5** — unchanged. Trajectory is negative on the metrics that matter (Americas comps -6% cc, sales per square foot -9.4%, product margin -500bps, net US store closures), partially offset by China's 42.4% segment margin and the 79.0% product margin there.

## Risk Assessment

**Ranked by decision relevance:**

1. **North American brand desirability.** Americas comps -3% in FY2025 and -5% (-6% cc) in Q1 FY2026, guided to a high-single-digit FY2026 decline and low-double-digit Q2 decline. The substance of the "negative media and social commentary" that management cites as a primary traffic driver is not identified in the 10-K, the 10-Q, or the call. Whether it was a transient news cycle or a durable perception shift is the single highest-value unresolved question in the file.

2. **Escalating tariff drag.** $275M unmitigated FY2025 gross profit impact; 280bps in Q1 FY2026; Q2 gross margin guided down ~410bps primarily on higher tariff costs. The IEEPA invalidation created a $230M claim while replacement tariffs and permanent de minimis repeal keep the cost in place. The company also uses the US first-sale declaration program; limitation would raise duties further.

3. **Canadian transfer pricing with no APA cover.** The bilateral IRS/CRA advance pricing agreement expired at the end of 2020, and the CRA **withdrew from bilateral APA negotiations in October 2025**. The company continues applying its existing methodology while reapplying in FY2026. Net investment in the Canadian subsidiary is $3.3B, of which $1.6B is designated indefinitely reinvested; the effective tax rate is already 29.5% (FY2025) and 31.8% (Q1 FY2026), suggesting limited planning cushion. A withdrawal is a procedural event rather than an assessment, and no related reserve increase was disclosed, but this is a genuine, underdiscussed tail risk to both cash taxes and reserves.

4. **Litigation with a partially adverse ruling.** *Patel v. lululemon* (S.D.N.Y.), a securities class action covering December 8, 2023–July 24, 2024, survived in part on March 31, 2026 when the court granted in part and denied in part the motion to dismiss. Six consolidated derivative suits are stayed pending that action per a May 18, 2026 order. Separately, *Neuman v. Lululemon USA* (E.D. Mich., filed March 27, 2026) seeks restitution of the "tariff-cost component" of prices charged, expressly referencing potential governmental reimbursements — meaning part of any IEEPA refund may be contested by the consumers who paid the surcharged prices. Early-stage equitable-restitution theories frequently fail at the pleading stage and no liability has been recorded.

5. **Leadership discontinuity.** Roughly seven months without a permanent CEO during the worst operating stretch in a decade. O'Neill arrives September 8, 2026; her compensation terms were not obtained. The market reaction to the April 22 announcement was negative, and Wilson publicly raised concerns (Reuters via US News, April 29, 2026).

6. **Fabric concentration.** 34% of fabric from Taiwan and 29% from China Mainland — 63% from two jurisdictions on the same geopolitical axis, with no long-term contracts for most suppliers.

7. **Governance entrenchment.** Classified board, no cumulative voting, no action by written consent, 66⅔% supermajority for removal for cause, special meetings callable only by the chairman, CEO or board majority, DGCL §203 applicable. Wilson's nomination notice included a non-binding declassification proposal.

Not established and not carried as fact: Elliott Investment Management's involvement. Bloomberg opinion (April 24, 2026) and WSJ (May 1, 2026) reference an activist, but no Elliott Schedule 13D or 13G appears in the filing history.

Risk Factors: **4.2** — the priced risks (Americas decline, tariffs, CEO transition) are largely in the multiple; the unpriced ones (Canadian transfer pricing against a $3.3B net investment, the surviving securities class action, Taiwan fabric concentration, further FY2027 estimate cuts) are real and asymmetric. Offsetting these, the absence of funded debt eliminates the refinancing and covenant channel that usually converts operating deterioration into equity impairment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.6% against 46.2% trailing one-year realized volatility — options are cheap relative to how the stock has actually traded, and the IV percentile within the current chain is 8%. The term structure is in contango: 41.6% at one week, 43.0% at one month, **51.6% at three months (September 25 expiry, 14.5% implied move)**, and 48.3% at six months. That shape locates the priced event risk at the early-September Q2 report, not in the next two weeks.

Positioning is mixed. Put/call open interest is 1.42, elevated but not extreme, and it has eased from 1.50 on July 22. Put/call *volume* is 0.52 near-dated but rises to 1.33 at three months and 10.29 at six months — the latter on a small volume base, so I read it as directionally indicative of longer-dated put accumulation rather than as a precise measure. The near-term skew is described as balanced (-2.6%).

The market is complacent about the next two weeks and is paying up for protection around the September print and into December, consistent with the fundamental read that near-term newsflow is thin and the resolution point is the Q2 report. For an existing holder trimming exposure, cheap near-dated vol makes collars or covered-call overwriting economically attractive relative to outright sales.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $170 (+42%)

Americas comps trough in Q2 FY2026 at the guided low-double-digit decline and improve sequentially as the compressed 15–16 month product cycle delivers 35% assortment newness through FY2027. The negative brand commentary proves to have been a news cycle rather than a perception shift, consistent with Maestrini's observation that it "has since subsided." Marketing at 6.0%–6.5% of sales restores traffic and converts to operating leverage rather than permanent margin loss. O'Neill, with 26 years of Nike product and brand experience, resets the North American assortment. Tariff mitigation plus the $230M IEEPA refund provides a one-time earnings and cash benefit. China sustains mid-teens underlying growth at 42%+ segment margins while Rest of World leverages toward China-like economics. The remaining $1.04B buyback (7.7% of the cap) is deployed near current prices. FY2028 EPS recovers to roughly $12.60 and the multiple re-rates to 13.5x. Balance sheet: no debt, $1.5B cash, so the option is free of financing risk.

### Bear Case — $88 (-26%)

The Americas decline is structural. Premium athleisure in the US has saturated, dupes have compressed the willingness to pay a $128 legging price, and Vuori, Alo and Nike take share in both the US and China. Americas comps stay negative through FY2027; sales per square foot continue falling against 11% square-footage growth, forcing accelerating store optimizations and impairments. The tariff run rate escalates past $310M with no refund recognized, and *Neuman* claims a portion of whatever is recovered. China decelerates from ~5% underlying toward flat as Vuori enters and the local commentary problem recurs. Rest of World keeps growing revenue while shrinking profit. Operating margin compresses toward 14%, EPS to $8.50–9.00, and a 10x multiple on a shrinking DTC apparel brand produces $85–90 — which is where the low sell-side target ($88) already sits. The Canadian transfer-pricing exposure crystallizes as a cash-tax and reserve charge.

### Probability-Weighted View

Base case $132 = 12x $11.00 of FY2027 EPS. The 12x anchor sits between COLM/KTB (14.8x/15.5x, but with stable-to-positive comps) and a structurally impaired discount, and reflects the fortress balance sheet and buyback support against negative core-market comps.

| Scenario | Target | Return | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | $170 | +42% | 20% |
| Base | $132 | +11% | 45% |
| Bear | $88 | -26% | 35% |

Expected value: 0.20(170) + 0.45(132) + 0.35(88) = **$124.2, or +4.0%** over twelve months. Reward-to-risk from base to bear is 0.41:1. An expected return below the 4.63% ten-year Treasury yield, on a Consumer Discretionary equity with a 47% drawdown and negative estimate momentum, is a less-than-fair risk-adjusted return.

### Addressing the Drawdown Explicitly

LULU is 47% below its 52-week high of $225.98. That magnitude requires a falsifiable statement about what the sellers are missing, and my conclusion is that they are largely not missing anything. The segment-profit arithmetic, the 750bps of two-year operating-margin compression, the 9.4% decline in sales per square foot against 11% square-footage growth, and management's own attribution of the shortfall to brand and product all point to genuine deterioration in earnings power rather than sentiment overshoot. The market is broadly right about the direction. What the market may be underweighting is the balance sheet: 4.45x EV/EBITDA on an unlevered business with $1.5B of cash and a 7.7% buyback authorization limits how far this can fall on operating news alone. That argues against AVOID, not for accumulation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Trim exposure into current strength; prospective buyers should wait.

**De-risking plan.** The stock has rallied 14.3% off the June 22 low of $104.44 and 5.3% since the prior review on no fundamental improvement. Existing holders should reduce into this rally rather than hold into the early-September Q2 print. Given near-dated IV at 41.6% versus 46.2% realized, buying protection or overwriting is cheaper than usual for holders unwilling to sell outright. A residual position of no more than one-third of a normal weight is defensible for holders who want exposure to the O'Neill turnaround option and the $230M tariff refund, both of which are genuinely unpriced.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Americas or North America comparable sales improve sequentially for two consecutive quarters — specifically, a Q2 constant-currency Americas comp better than -8% followed by a Q3 comp better than -5%.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS stops falling and turns up, defined as a positive 90-day revision on the next-year line (currently $11.46, cut 14.1% in 90 days).
3. Gross margin decline decelerates to under 200bps year over year in any quarter, evidencing tariff mitigation or a halt to fixed-cost deleverage.
4. Recognition of any portion of the $230M IEEPA refund as an asset, or a *Neuman* dismissal that clears the claim on it.

**Upgrade trigger to ACCUMULATE:** the combination of a confirmed Americas comp inflection plus a price near the bear-case zone — roughly $95–100, which would put the stock near 8.5x $11.00 and above a 6% forward FCF yield on my normalized $650–790M estimate.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A second FY2026 guidance cut at the September print, or FY2027 guidance below $10.00 in EPS.
2. Evidence of markdown-driven revenue — inventory growing faster than revenue again alongside a further Americas product-margin decline below 64%.
3. Any material Canadian transfer-pricing assessment or reserve increase, given the $3.3B net Canadian investment.
4. Suspension or material slowdown of the buyback with the $1,042.5M authorization outstanding, which would signal management sees a liquidity or earnings-durability problem it has not disclosed.
5. China Mainland constant-currency growth decelerating below 10%, which would remove the only segment currently expanding margin.

**Reassessment timeline.** Early September 2026, on the Q2 FY2026 report — the same date the three-month options tenor is pricing a 14.5% move — followed by O'Neill's September 8 start.
