## Executive Summary

WSFS Financial has spent two quarters converting from a chronic relative underperformer into a premium-multiple regional bank. The stock has moved from $57.92 in late January 2026 to $81.47, up 50.9% over twelve months and within 1.8% of the $82.94 fifty-two-week high, on the back of four consecutive earnings beats (Q2'26 non-GAAP EPS $1.66 versus $1.53 consensus, +8.5%) and consensus 2026 EPS revisions of +6.4% over ninety days to $6.58. Fiduciary assets crossed $100 billion in the quarter, reaching $101.7B, and WSFS Institutional Services fee income grew 40% year over year in the first half. The franchise improvement is real and located in a specific, defensible business: corporate trusteeship and loan agency work run out of Delaware under a federal savings bank charter, where WSFS ranked as the fourth most active U.S. ABS/MBS trustee in 2025.

The problem is what an investor pays for it and how much of the reported inflection repeats. Consolidated H1 2026 pretax income rose $43.1M year over year; $27.0M of that (63%) came from the provision line, including a single $15.7M recovery on C&I loans to an office-property fund that WSFS had charged off in Q1 2025. Excluding that recovery, first-half net charge-offs annualize to roughly 0.29%, inside a normal range rather than an exceptional one. Diluted share count fell 9.2% year over year, which mathematically accounts for roughly a third of the 36.4% reported first-half EPS growth. Those buybacks were executed at an average of $67.39 in H1 against tangible book value per share of $33–34, and tangible common equity declined from $1,768.6M to $1,759.3M over a half-year in which the company earned $171.2M.

At $81.47 the stock is 12.4x 2026 consensus, 11.8x 2027 consensus, 13.2x a normalized $6.18 earnings run-rate that strips the recovery and other identified one-offs, and 2.38x tangible book. The peer median P/E is 13.7x, so on earnings WSFS screens in line to slightly cheap; on tangible book it carries a large premium justified by a ~20% core ROTCE that is itself partly a product of a shrinking equity denominator and a rate-dependent internal funds-transfer credit. Base case $88 (+8.0%), bull $100 (+22.7%), bear $67 (−17.8%). Probability-weighting those outcomes produces mid-single-digit expected total return including the 0.98% dividend. That is a HOLD: a good franchise at a fair-to-full price with a narrower margin of safety than at any point in the past year.

## Company Overview

WSFS is a savings-and-loan holding company in Wilmington, Delaware, operating through Wilmington Savings Fund Society, FSB — 194 years old, one of the ten oldest continuously named U.S. bank and trust companies, and the largest locally managed bank and trust company headquartered in the Greater Philadelphia/Delaware corridor. The federal savings bank charter, converted from a state mutual savings charter, permits a broader activity set than most bank charters, and it is the legal foundation of the fee businesses now driving earnings.

Three segments, with sharply divergent trajectories:

**WSFS Bank** — 114 offices (PA 58, DE 38, NJ 14, plus FL/NV/VA), gross loans $13.467B at 6/30/26: commercial mortgage $3.884B (29%), C&I $3.014B (22%), owner-occupied CRE $1.930B (14%), consumer $1.815B (13%), residential $1.233B (9%), construction $1.003B (7%), small-business leases $584M (4%). Q2'26 segment pretax income $60.478M, down 1.7% from $61.510M.

**Cash Connect** — ATM vault cash and smart-safe logistics, managing roughly $1.3B of cash across ~23,100 non-bank ATMs and ~12,100 smart safes. Q2'26 pretax $2.952M, down 10.4%. The serviced non-bank ATM count has fallen ~19% in eighteen months (28,600 → 24,000 → 23,100). Bailment fees fell 20.2% in the quarter.

**Wealth and Trust** — Bryn Mawr Trust Private Wealth, Bryn Mawr Trust Company of Delaware, and WSFS Institutional Services (indenture and owner trustee, loan agency, bankruptcy administration, custody, commercial domicile). Q2'26 pretax $47.7M, now 42.9% of consolidated pretax income versus 32.2% a year earlier.

The company has been systematically pruning: the Upstart unsecured consumer portfolio, Powdermill, the Commonwealth Financial advisory partnership, Spring EQ (runoff), and in Q2'26 the credit card portfolio ($36.3M book, $1.7M gain, converted to an interchange-share arrangement with a third-party issuer). This is coherent risk-surface reduction, and it is one reason absolute earnings have grown slowly while returns have improved.

Efficiency improved to 58.9% in Q2'26 from 59.7% in FY2025, but the improvement is thin: H1 expenses grew 5.8% against 6.7% revenue growth.

**Earnings composition is the central issue.** The $43.1M H1 pretax improvement decomposes exactly: provision +$27.0M, net interest income +$22.9M, fee income +$11.2M, expenses −$18.0M. The provision contribution dominates, and $15.7M of it is the office-fund recovery — real cash on a written-off asset, but not repeatable. Strip that plus $2.796M of restructuring expense, a $4.1M equity-investment impairment, a $1.7M credit-card gain and a $1.3M card-related provision release, and normalized H1 net income is roughly $162.3M, or $3.09 per diluted share — about $6.18 annualized as a sensitivity, not a forecast. Normalized ROA of ~1.48% remains well above the 1.10–1.20% regional-bank norm, so the underlying franchise return is genuinely strong; the *rate of improvement* is what is overstated.

**Per-share arithmetic.** H1 net income rose 23.9% while diluted shares fell 9.2% (57.83M → 52.52M). 1.239 ÷ 0.908 = 1.364, matching the reported EPS move from $2.39 to $3.26. Roughly one-third of reported EPS growth is denominator.

**Balance sheet: liquid, not lending.** Total assets grew $1.34B (+6.3%) in six months to $22.654B, but net loans grew only $207.8M (+1.6%), after +1% in FY2025. Deposits grew $1.361B (+7.7%) to $19.004B, with the surplus going into cash (+$871.5M to $2.571B) and AFS purchases ($533.5M). Loan-to-deposit fell to 69.9% from 74.2%. Average other interest-earning assets (largely Fed balances) rose from $934M at 4.49% to $1.979B at 3.75%. That mix dilution is why NIM slipped 2bp year over year despite falling deposit costs.

**The funding story is the strongest and most fragile item.** Noninterest-bearing deposits jumped $1.432B (+25.7%) in six months to $7.008B — 37% of total — while interest-bearing deposits fell $70.6M. This is institutional trust and escrow money generated by Institutional Services, and it is what supports a 3.87% NIM on a 70% loan-to-deposit ratio. The cost: uninsured deposits rose to $8.5B (45% of client deposits) from $7.1B (40%), and uninsured-and-uncollateralized "unprotected" deposits to $7.3B (38%) from $5.3B (30%). Contingent liquidity of $11.2B (FHLB $6.0B, discount window $2.1B, Fed cash $2.0B, unpledged securities $1.1B) covers unprotected deposits 1.53x, which is adequate. The risk profile deteriorated inside two quarters, and the CFO has cautioned that the NIB growth rate may not repeat.

**Securities.** AFS amortized cost $4.359B against ~$3.841B fair value — roughly $519M pretax unrealized loss, 11.9% of cost. Total AOCI of −$466.2M equals 26% of tangible common equity, already absorbed. The AFS book yielded 2.13% at year-end 2025 with 82% maturing after 2035, so reinvestment at current yields is a genuine but slow tailwind (MBS yields rose to 2.61% from 2.37% in the quarter). HTM is not a hidden hole: $97.5M unrealized loss with only ~$21.6M unrecognized (1.2% of TCE).

**Credit is good with two chronic sore spots.** NPAs $80.96M (0.36% of assets), up from $72.10M but down sharply from $127.4M at 12/31/24. ACL/loans 1.32%, ACL/nonaccruals 260% (improved from 250%). Criticized assets improved materially: C&I special mention halved to $30.3M, construction substandard fell to $16.8M from $35.4M, troubled loans to $105.7M from $144.3M. Against that: NewLane small-business leases charged off 1.83% in FY2025, 2.72% in FY2024, and 1.30% annualized in H1'26; construction charged off 0.73% annualized on 2023-vintage credits. Neither book is large enough to threaten capital. The 10-K discloses that the allowance is built on a single third-party economic scenario rather than a probability-weighted set, which makes the reserve mechanically sensitive to one macro view.

**Capital and rate positioning.** CET1 13.76% against a stated ~12% target implies roughly $288M of excess ((13.76% − 12.00%) × $16.346B RWA), plus $272.2M of holding-company cash with partial overlap. Holdco debt is modest ($197.0M senior, $91.1M trust preferred, $18.8M other) after the December 2025 refinancing into $200M of 5.375% 2035 notes. WSFS is asset-sensitive: +100bp lifts NII 6.1%, −100bp cuts it 3.1%. Purchased floors ($46.3M premium on $2.3B notional) partially offset, but the premium amortization is now a real NII drag at $8.0M in H1 versus $4.1M a year earlier.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has moved up in every window: current quarter $1.61 (+5.5% in 30 days, +6.7% in 90), current year $6.58 (+5.0%/+6.4%), next year $6.89 (+3.7%/+4.8%). Four consecutive beats (6.8%, 19.4%, 12.2%, 8.5%) support confidence in those numbers. This is positive earnings momentum, but revisions of 5–6% on a bank already trading near its high are supportive, not transformative. Implied 2026→2027 growth is 4.7%.

Where the growth actually sits:

| Fiduciary revenue line | Q2'25 | Q2'26 | YoY | H1'25 | H1'26 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WSFS Institutional Services | $19.852M | $26.658M | +34% | $37.119M | $51.873M | +40% |
| Bryn Mawr Trust of Delaware | $7.999M | $9.628M | +20% | $15.027M | $18.566M | +24% |
| Private Wealth Management | $15.923M | $15.174M | −4.7% | $30.909M | $30.148M | −2.5% |

The recurring AUM-fee business that investors normally pay premium multiples for is contracting; the Powdermill sale and Commonwealth unwind explain part of that, but the composition point stands. All growth is institutional trustee, agency and bankruptcy-administration work. The bank segment's pretax income declined 1.7% and Cash Connect's fell 10.4% with management guiding top-line revenue lower while margins rise on pricing actions and smart-safe mix.

Two structural qualifications on the wealth growth. Of Wealth & Trust's $84.035M of Q2 reported revenue, only $50.649M came from external clients; $33.386M is inter-segment, of which $32.810M is a funds-transfer-pricing credit for the deposits the segment gathers (up from ~$28.1M). Roughly 40% of the segment's apparent revenue is an internal transfer price whose value falls with the policy rate. Institutional trustee revenue is also levered to ABS/MBS issuance and to corporate restructuring volumes. H1 Institutional Services revenue annualizes to about $104M, but the split between new-deal fees and recurring administration fees is not disclosed, and league-table volumes are lumpy.

The forward growth stack, then: mid-single-digit consensus EPS growth, a multi-year AFS repricing tailwind, buyback accretion, and continued institutional share gains — offset by a declining Cash Connect, a flat core bank, 1.6% loan growth, and asset-sensitivity into a Fed that cut three times in 2025 to a 3.50–3.75% target. Any further easing carries a 3.1%-per-100bp headwind on NII on top of the FTP compression inside the wealth segment. That is a correlated, not independent, exposure.

## Valuation Assessment

| Basis | EPS | Multiple at $81.47 |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 GAAP | $5.09 | 16.0x |
| LTM GAAP | $5.96 | 13.7x |
| Q2'26 core annualized | $6.64 | 12.3x |
| 2026 consensus | $6.58 | 12.4x |
| 2027 consensus | $6.89 | 11.8x |
| Normalized H1 annualized | $6.18 | 13.2x |

Peer set (UCB 12.6x, CATY 12.3x, INDB 16.2x, RNST 17.6x, CVBF 13.7x) has a 13.7x median and a 2.9% median dividend yield. On forward earnings WSFS is slightly below that median while earning a normalized ~1.48% ROA versus a ~1.10–1.20% peer norm — the bull's cleanest argument. On tangible book the picture inverts: 2.38x P/TBV (and 1.54x P/B) is a large premium to typical regional-bank marks, and the 0.98% dividend yield provides roughly a third of the peer yield support.

Both readings are defensible because the ROTCE that justifies the TBV premium is partly manufactured. Core ROTCE near 20% benefits from a denominator that management has been actively shrinking above book, and from an FTP credit inside Wealth & Trust that scales with the Fed funds rate. Adjust for those and the franchise still earns above-peer returns, but the case for 2.4x tangible book weakens.

**Base case $88.** 12.8x 2027 consensus $6.89, or 13.4x 2026 consensus $6.58 — a modest premium to the peer median P/E for a genuinely above-peer ROA and one-third fee-revenue mix. That is +8.0% from $81.47, and it sits inside the analyst range (low $83.00, mean $88.92, median $89.50, high $92.50). Because the stock is not at a premium multiple, those targets carry some information; they are also clustered tightly enough that the sell-side sees the same modest upside.

**Bull case $100.** 14.5x 2027 consensus, requiring institutional-services growth to hold above 25%, net charge-offs to stay at the low end of the guided 25–35bp range, a rate path that is flat-to-higher rather than easing (+6.1% NII per 100bp), and continued buyback accretion. +22.7%.

**Bear case $67.** 10.8x normalized $6.18, or roughly 1.85x tangible book. Triggered by a structured-finance issuance slowdown that hits Institutional Services fees and the escrow deposit base together, a normalization of the provision toward 35bp, and multiple compression back toward the peer median on a company whose core bank is not growing. −17.8%.

Weighting these 55/20/25 gives roughly +4.5% price return plus ~1% dividend. That is fair-value territory, not asymmetric upside. The valuation score reflects a stock that is reasonable on forward earnings, expensive on tangible book, and priced with the good news already in.

## Competitive Landscape

The institutional trustee franchise is a real moat around roughly 43% of pretax income. The FY2025 10-K cites Asset-Backed Alert ranking WSFS the fourth most active U.S. ABS/MBS trustee in 2025; management stated on the Q2'26 call that WSFS ranked third in H1 2026 by deal count with share rising to 14% from 11.7%, and that corporate trust grew 28% and global capital markets 58%. The H1 ranking and share figures come from a third-party call summary rather than a filing and should be treated as unconfirmed; the #4 2025 ranking is a filing fact. Delaware trust and entity law, the federal savings bank charter, and 194 years of trustee standing are difficult to replicate quickly, and gaining share against far larger corporate-trust franchises is a genuine achievement.

Outside that business, competitive trajectory is flat to negative. The bank segment's pretax income declined 1.7% year over year with 1.6% half-year loan growth in a Delaware/southeastern-Pennsylvania footprint that is not a growth market. Cash Connect has lost 19% of its serviced non-bank ATM count in eighteen months, with smart safes (10,000 → 12,100) only partially offsetting; management's own framing is declining revenue with rising margins. Private wealth fee income is down 4.7% year over year.

So the composition is one improving segment carrying two that are stable-to-eroding. That mix supports a modestly above-peer multiple, and it is why fee income at roughly one-third of revenue is the strongest structural argument here. It does not support extrapolating 40% institutional growth across the enterprise. The identity of WSFS's corporate-trust competitors and the pricing dynamics of that market are not characterized in the available evidence, which is a real gap in assessing durability.

## Risk Assessment

**Deposit-risk build is the same phenomenon as the growth engine.** Unprotected deposits rose from 30% to 38% of client deposits in two quarters, driven by the institutional escrow inflows that produced the fee growth. Escrow deposits tied to administration mandates are operationally stickier than rate-shopping retail money, and 1.53x contingent liquidity coverage is adequate. In a structured-finance slowdown, fee revenue and the free-funding base contract together, hitting both NII and noninterest income. A segment-level view obscures that correlation.

**Rate direction is a two-sided exposure.** Asset sensitivity of −3.1% NII per 100bp of cuts compounds with FTP compression inside Wealth & Trust. Any further easing from the current 3.50–3.75% target is material to 2027 estimates.

**Cash Connect operational risk.** The 10-K states that profitability depends on accurately distributing, tracking and settling large cash amounts through third parties and that Cash Connect "has experienced such occurrences" of misappropriation in the past, with insurance possibly insufficient. Few banks carry an operational risk of this literal form.

**Trustee litigation tail.** Gibbons v. WSFS Bank produced a $15.0M default judgment (6/6/2023) against the Bank in its capacity as owner trustee of an RMBS trust, arising from an alleged premises injury; the plaintiff moved to domesticate in Philadelphia on 1/3/2025. WSFS discloses a $0.0–$15.0M reasonably possible loss range, has notified insurers and disputes both the judgment and the property connection. At $15.0M this is 0.85% of TCE. The significance is structural: scaling trustee mandates 40% a year scales this contingent category too.

**Securities and goodwill.** AOCI of −$466.2M is 26% of TCE and will not recover quickly. Goodwill of $885.2M sits against $1,759.3M of TCE, and the 10-K explicitly ties goodwill-impairment risk to a sustained decline in stock price and market capitalization — a risk that grows as the multiple grows.

**Credit and consumer exposure.** Excluding the recovery, H1 net charge-offs annualize to ~0.29%, inside guidance. NewLane leases and 2023-vintage construction are structurally higher-loss books. The $1.815B consumer book and the residential and construction origination lines face a slower environment than the last two quarters imply.

**Governance and capital allocation.** Compensation metrics are Adjusted ROA, Adjusted ROTCE and Adjusted EPS — all three flattered by buybacks executed above tangible book, with no TBV-growth or relative-TSR measure. The proxy's five-year indexed TSR table shows WSFS below its own selected peer group in each of 2021–2025 (+31% cumulative versus +53%), though that table ends 12/31/25 and excludes the 2026 re-rating.

### Options Market Signal

The options data are thin and internally inconsistent, and I weight them lightly. Only 33 contracts trade across three expirations; put/call open-interest ratio is 0.04 and put/call volume 0.00, meaning there is effectively no put open interest. The reported near-term ATM IV of 42.0% against 23.6% realized, in backwardation versus 22.1% at six months, would normally flag imminent event risk, but the nearest expiry is August 21, well before the late-October earnings date, and the prior snapshot recorded ATM IV at 419.4%, which is a data artifact. The reliable read is the open-interest structure: essentially no downside hedging demand on a stock 1.8% below its 52-week high after a 51% twelve-month move. That reflects complacency rather than confirmation of the bull case, and it argues against expecting an options-driven catalyst either way. Short interest of 6.2% of float with 4.0 days to cover is uncrowded and unremarkable.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($100, +22.7%)

WSFS has built a top-three U.S. securitization trustee franchise inside a $4.2B bank, and the market is still valuing it on regional-bank forward earnings (11.8x 2027) rather than on the fee franchise. Institutional Services fee income grew 40% in H1, fiduciary assets crossed $100B, corporate trust grew 28% and global capital markets 58%. That business generates $7.0B of noninterest-bearing escrow deposits — up 25.7% in six months — which supports a 3.87% NIM on a 70% loan-to-deposit ratio, an economic advantage that peers cannot replicate. Normalized ROA of ~1.48% and core ROTCE above 20% are genuinely top-decile. Four consecutive beats and rising consensus (+6.4% for 2026 over ninety days) suggest management is still under-promising. Credit is improving on the metrics that lead: criticized assets down sharply, ACL/nonaccrual coverage up to 260%, charge-off guidance lowered to 25–35bp. There is $288M of excess CET1 and a 9.2M-share authorization worth $749M, and a 20% ROTCE franchise buying stock at 2.4x book can still be NPV-positive. If the rate path turns flat-to-higher, +100bp adds 6.1% to NII. Every analyst target sits above the current price, with a $92.50 high.

### Bear Case ($67, −17.8%)

The 2026 inflection is largely accounting and arithmetic. Sixty-three percent of the H1 pretax improvement came from the provision line, including one $15.7M recovery on the office exposure the company had written off a year earlier; ex-recovery charge-offs are a normal 0.29%. A third of reported EPS growth came from a 9.2% share-count reduction bought at 2.0x tangible book — tangible common equity fell from $1,768.6M to $1,759.3M in a half-year of $171.2M in earnings, and the average repurchase price has escalated from $52.86 (FY25) to $67.39 (H1'26) to $74.25 (June) against $81.47 now. Underneath, the bank segment's pretax income is down 1.7%, loans grew 1.6%, Cash Connect is shrinking with a 19% eighteen-month decline in serviced ATMs, and private wealth fee income is down 4.7%. Roughly 40% of Wealth & Trust's reported revenue is an internal rate-dependent transfer credit, so the segment's 55% pretax growth compresses if the Fed cuts. FY2025 net income of $287.3M is only 5.9% above FY2021 despite the 2022 Bryn Mawr acquisition. Uninsured deposits at 45% and unprotected at 38% are a two-quarter deterioration; a structured-finance slowdown would hit fees and funding simultaneously. Compensation is paid on the three metrics buybacks flatter, and not one of nine named executives bought a share in the open market during a 40% rally — the CEO exercised and sold 65,446 shares at ~$74, marginally reducing his net position, and another executive sold at $81.74.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** — I expect modest appreciation driven by continued estimate revisions and institutional-services momentum, but the entry point does not support new full positions.

**Base case target: $88** — 12.8x 2027 consensus EPS of $6.89, or 13.4x 2026 consensus $6.58, a modest premium to the 13.7x peer median P/E justified by ~1.48% normalized ROA versus a 1.10–1.20% peer norm and one-third fee-revenue mix. **+8.0%** from $81.47, plus ~1.0% dividend.

**Bull case: $100** (14.5x 2027 consensus), **+22.7%.** Requires institutional-services growth above 25%, charge-offs at the low end of 25–35bp, and a rate path that is flat-to-higher.

**Bear case: $67** (10.8x normalized $6.18, ~1.85x tangible book), **−17.8%.**

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3'26 print (late October 2026) and the Q4/full-year print in late January 2027, which will be the first clean year-over-year comparison against the Q1'26 recovery quarter.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Price at $70–73 (roughly 10.6x–11.1x 2027 consensus, ~2.05–2.13x tangible book) with 2026/2027 estimates holding at or above $6.58/$6.89. A 10%+ decline on unchanged estimates restores the asymmetry that is missing today.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Institutional Services fee growth above 25% **combined with** external-client (non-inter-segment) Wealth & Trust revenue growth above 15%, which would show the fee franchise compounding independently of the funds-transfer credit.
3. Net loan growth accelerating above 5% annualized with the loan-to-deposit ratio moving back above 74%, converting the $2.57B cash pile from ~3.75% Fed balances into ~7% loans.
4. A shift in buyback pricing discipline — repurchases paused or executed below ~1.9x tangible book — or the addition of a tangible-book-growth or relative-TSR metric to the incentive plan.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. Quarterly net charge-offs above 40bp annualized, or NPAs above 0.50% of assets (from 0.36%), particularly if driven by construction or the NewLane lease book.
2. Institutional Services fee growth decelerating below 15% year over year, or noninterest-bearing deposits declining more than 5% in a quarter — either would signal the fee/funding engine reversing, and the two are likely to move together.
3. Unprotected deposits exceeding 45% of client deposits (from 38%) without a commensurate increase in contingent liquidity coverage above the current 1.53x.
4. A Fed easing cycle of 75bp or more that shows up as NIM compression below 3.60% with no offsetting fee acceleration.
5. Continued repurchases above $85 (≥2.5x tangible book) while tangible common equity declines for a third consecutive half-year.

**Reassessment timeline:** Late October 2026 (Q3 print), with an interim check at the next FOMC decision given the −3.1%-per-100bp NII sensitivity.

## Conclusion

WSFS is a better business than it was two years ago and a worse purchase than it was six months ago. The improvement is genuine and locatable: a Delaware-chartered institutional trustee franchise that moved from fourth to reportedly third in U.S. ABS/MBS deal count, grew fee income 40% in the first half, crossed $100B in fiduciary assets, and generates $7.0B of near-free institutional deposits that let the bank earn a 3.87% NIM while lending out only 70% of deposits. Normalized ROA near 1.48% and core ROTCE above 20% are well above the regional-bank norm, and four consecutive beats with rising consensus argue the operating momentum is real.

What has changed is the price and the composition of the reported numbers. Sixty-three percent of the first-half pretax improvement came from the provision line, including a single non-repeating $15.7M recovery; roughly a third of reported EPS growth came from retiring 9.2% of the shares at approximately 2.0x tangible book, a transaction that left tangible common equity lower after a half-year of $171.2M in earnings. Beneath the fee growth, the bank segment's pretax income is down 1.7%, Cash Connect is shrinking, private wealth is shrinking, and 40% of the wealth segment's reported revenue is a rate-dependent internal credit. At 12.4x 2026 consensus, 13.2x normalized earnings, and 2.38x tangible book — 1.8% below the 52-week high after a 51% twelve-month move — the base case offers about 8% upside against 18% downside, with essentially no put open interest indicating anyone is hedging.

Existing holders should stay. The AFS book repricing off a 2.13% yield is a multi-year tailwind, the institutional franchise is compounding, and $288M of excess CET1 plus a 9.2M-share authorization provide support. New capital should wait for either $70–73 or for evidence that external-client fee revenue is growing independently of the funds-transfer credit. **HOLD.**
