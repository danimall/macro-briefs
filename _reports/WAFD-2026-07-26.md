**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

WaFd is a ~$27.6B-asset Seattle-based regional bank in the middle of a deliberate identity change. After more than a century built on single-family residential mortgages, management exited consumer mortgage and HELOC origination entirely in January 2025 and is now running the legacy residential book down while rebuilding the balance sheet around commercial lending. Commercial loans reached 62.4% of gross loans at March 31, 2026 and represented roughly 95% of new originations. The near-term earnings story is favorable: net interest margin expanded to 2.81% in Q2 FY26 from 2.55% a year earlier as funding costs repriced down faster than asset yields, driving net interest income up 10.4% and diluted EPS to $0.82 from $0.65. Net income growth (+16.5% YoY) has been amplified by aggressive buybacks that cut the share count from ~78.2M to ~73.9M over the year.

Two developments buried in the filings materially improve the setup. First, the FDIC's Supervision Appeals Review Committee upgraded WaFd's Community Reinvestment Act rating from "Needs to Improve" to "Satisfactory" on January 16, 2026, removing restrictions on M&A, branch expansion, and expedited applications. Second, the CEO made open-market purchases of the Series A preferred in late 2025 at roughly a 35% discount to par, a rare unambiguous insider conviction signal. Q3 FY26 (June) delivered EPS of $0.84 versus $0.73 a year prior, with revenue up 10.3% and loan growth called the headline.

The counterweight to the improving operating picture is threefold. The stock has already appreciated 24% over the trailing year to $36.73, sitting near its 52-week high of $39.49. Consensus EPS estimates are drifting modestly lower across every window (next-year -2.0% over 90 days). And a credit disclosure most screens would miss — $404M of loans under 90 days delinquent but classified substandard, which would push NPAs/assets from 0.48% to 1.94% if reclassified — argues the credit picture is more fragile than the headline suggests, and is rotating rather than uniformly healing. At ~11.6x earnings and offering a ~2.9% dividend plus a large buyback, WaFd is a reasonable holding through the recovery, but the asymmetry that would justify a BUY has compressed after the re-rating.

## Company Overview

WaFd, Inc. is the holding company for WaFd Bank (formerly Washington Federal Bank), founded in 1917 in Ballard, Seattle. The company completed a staged rebranding, with the bank charter name change effective September 25, 2025. It operates 208 branches across nine western states — Washington (27% of loans), California (14%), Oregon (12%), Texas (12%), Arizona (11%), plus Idaho, Utah, Nevada, and New Mexico.

The defining feature of the company is its strategic transition. WaFd is dismantling its historical thrift model (borrow short, lend long on single-family residential mortgages) and rebuilding as a commercial-focused community bank. The January 2025 exit from consumer mortgage and HELOC origination is structural, not cyclical. As of March 31, 2026, the loan book was multi-family 22.2%, commercial real estate 17.1%, C&I 13.1%, construction 9.1%, land acquisition & development 0.9%, and single-family residential 35.5% (declining). The March 2024 acquisition of Luther Burbank Corporation, a California-focused multifamily lender, expanded the California footprint, added the FHLB-San Francisco borrowing relationship, and is a primary driver of the multi-family concentration. Subsidiary WAFD Insurance Group provides fee income and supports small tuck-in acquisitions.

The fiscal year ends September 30. The most recent primary filings are the FY2025 10-K (filed November 18, 2025), the Q2 FY2026 10-Q (period ended March 31, 2026, filed May 5, 2026), and the DEF 14A (December 19, 2025).

## Financial Analysis

The earnings inflection is driven by margin. The mechanism is liability-sensitivity to rate declines: the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 50bps to 2.79% while the yield on earning assets declined only 19bps to 5.14%. As short rates fall, WaFd's deposit and borrowing costs reprice down faster than its asset yields. Deposit cost fell to 2.41% at March 2026 from 2.60% at September 2025. External reporting cites five consecutive quarters of NII growth.

The deposit mix is shifting intentionally: transaction deposits rose $440M (+3.6%) while time deposits fell $754M (-8.3%) over six months, moving away from higher-cost CDs. Management targets a 20% noninterest-bearing deposit mix by 2030.

Capital is solid. Company CET1 stood at 11.35%, total risk-based at 14.37%, and Tier 1 leverage at 9.10% (down from 9.59% at FY25-end because of buybacks). Bank-level ratios are stronger (CET1 13.03%). Shareholders' equity was $2.98B, or 10.81% of assets. Securities marks are modest for a regional bank: net unrealized losses of ~$16.8M on AFS and ~$33.6M on HTM are small against $3.0B of equity, a favorable contrast to peers that took large duration losses in 2022–2023.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly. WaFd declared its 172nd consecutive quarterly common dividend ($0.27/quarter). Buybacks have been aggressive: 4.69M shares repurchased in 1H FY26 (~$145M) at an average ~$30.98, with the board raising authorization to 10M shares in February 2026. The share-count reduction is meaningfully accretive to EPS. A 4.875% Series A preferred ($300M par) remains outstanding.

One item warrants monitoring: the balance sheet grew $869M over six months largely through securities purchases funded with FHLB borrowings, which jumped from $1.77B to $3.06B (borrowing cost rose to 3.01%). This is a carry strategy: roughly +$1.3B of FHLB borrowings deployed into ~+$0.9B of agency MBS/securities. It supports near-term NII but increases wholesale-funding dependence and rate sensitivity. Whether this is opportunistic deployment or a crutch to defend the margin narrative is unresolved.

The earnings surprise record is decent but not pristine: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with the September 2025 quarter missing by 8.4%. Beats have been small (1.1% to 7.0%), suggesting management guides tightly rather than sandbagging heavily.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs. First, NIM inflection continues to drive earnings, though management guides near-term NIM to be relatively flat, which signals the easy funding-cost tailwind is moderating. Second, the return to balance-sheet growth: after deliberate contraction as the residential book ran off, management called loan growth the headline for both the March and June 2026 quarters, with C&I up 17% and construction up 11% over six months. Forward guidance targets 8%–12% loan growth.

The CRA upgrade is the structural enabler here. Under the prior "Needs to Improve" rating, WaFd faced restrictions that handcuffed a bank pursuing growth. The upgrade removes those restrictions, with the next CRA exam expected around 2027, and directly supports the tuck-in acquisition and de novo branch strategy management is now describing.

The offsetting reality is that consensus is not rewarding this narrative. Estimate revisions are negative across every window: current quarter -0.3%, next quarter -0.6%, current year -0.3%, next year -2.0% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the strong positive-revision inflection signal that would elevate the growth score. Current-year consensus sits at $3.23 and next year at $3.36, implying roughly 4% forward EPS growth after the 90-day trims. The growth is credible but modest, and the strategic bet — abandoning a century-old residential competency for commercial lending in a more competitive, chunkier-credit segment — remains unproven through a full credit cycle. On balance, growth prospects earn a middle score: real cyclical improvement and a genuine catalyst (CRA), tempered by falling estimates and moderating margin tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

WaFd trades at ~11.6x trailing/forward earnings with a ~2.9% dividend yield at $36.73. Against the regional-bank peer set (median P/E 13.5, median yield 2.2%), WaFd screens as reasonably valued to modestly cheap on the multiple and offers an above-median yield. TRMK trades at 12.0x, NBTB at 13.5x, TBBK at 11.0x, FBK at 19.8x, and BBT at 22.1x, placing WaFd toward the lower-middle of the range.

The discount is partly justified rather than a clear mispricing. WaFd has underperformed its peer group on total shareholder return (5-year cumulative TSR of 90 vs. 111 for the peer group), carries meaningful CRE/multi-family concentration, and has the $404M substandard-but-current credit overhang. A modest multiple discount for an unproven strategic pivot with a shadow credit pipeline is arguably appropriate, not evidence the market is wrong.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the case to a high score. This is not a trough-earnings situation with a triple-digit trailing P/E: earnings are recovering off cyclical lows, but consensus estimates are falling, not rising, so there is no positive revision velocity to argue that consensus is stale and lagging reality. The stock trades below the mean and median analyst targets (low $36.00, high $40.00, mean $39.00, median $40.00), leaving ~6–9% upside to consensus. On inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, analyst targets act as floors; here, with estimates drifting lower, the mean target sits only modestly above the current price. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark down the valuation, but the combination of a full-cycle-uncertain strategy, negative revisions, and limited residual upside to consensus targets removes the asymmetry. Fair value is close to the current price. The valuation supports holding for yield and modest earnings growth but does not offer the specific catalyst-driven upside required for a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

WaFd competes in the crowded western-US regional/community banking space against larger regionals with greater resources, a dynamic the company flags as a risk (consolidation creating larger competitors). Its repositioning mirrors an industry-wide reality: the traditional thrift model has become structurally unattractive in a volatile-rate environment, pushing WaFd toward higher-yielding, relationship-based commercial banking. That shift moves the company into segments where competition is fiercer and credit risk is chunkier, and where it lacks the multi-decade track record it built in residential.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. On the positive side, the commercial mix is expanding, the CRA constraint is gone, and the liability-sensitive positioning is a relative tailwind that differentiates WaFd from peers whose margins compress when rates fall. On the negative side, the 5-year TSR underperformance versus peers indicates the franchise has not translated its repositioning into superior shareholder returns yet, and the commercial pivot is early. Modest AOCI marks, a granular deposit base, and ample FHLB/Fed contingent liquidity (FHLB-DM line up to 45% of assets) give it a solid defensive footing against regional-bank confidence risk. Overall the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving, not a clear share-gaining widening-moat story.

## Risk Assessment

The most important company-specific risk is the credit picture beneath the clean headline. Non-accrual loans improved to $123.9M (0.61% of loans) at March 2026, and NPAs fell to 0.48% of assets. But the $404M of substandard loans under 90 days delinquent, if deemed non-performing, would push NPAs/assets to 1.94% — a large shadow pipeline of criticized credit. Credit is also rotating rather than simply healing: C&I non-accruals spiked to $54.7M from $11.0M as a large problem credit migrated in, while a $70M CRE non-accrual was substantially resolved (down to $4.7M). Delinquencies ticked up to 0.78% from 0.60%, substandard loans total $516M (2.6% of loans), and the provision rose to $7.5M for 6-mo FY26 from $2.75M. Net charge-offs remain low ($4.3M over six months) and ACL sits at 1.05% of gross loans, so this is a watch item rather than a realized loss event, but it is the key thing to monitor.

Additional risks: the CRE/multi-family concentration (multi-family 22% + CRE 17% + construction 9% + C&I 13%) in western markets; geographic concentration; the wholesale-funding carry-trade leverage that raises rate and liquidity sensitivity; and explicit "activist shareholder" risk-factor language, which combined with the peer TSR underperformance suggests possible activist pressure. Interest-rate positioning is asymmetric: a -200bp shock would add ~$89M (+11.8%) to NII, a favorable near-term tailwind, but a +200bp shock would cut economic value of equity by 22%, so a rate reversal would be a genuine headwind. As a Financials-sector name, the group carries a structural negative-alpha history against the market, which raises the bar for any bullish call.

The strategic-execution risk from the mortgage exit is real: WaFd is dismantling its core competency and betting on commercial lending, and the bet is unproven through a full credit cycle.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$44–48):** NIM expansion continues longer than the "flat" guidance implies as funding costs keep repricing down; loan growth hits the 8–12% target and the CRA upgrade enables accretive tuck-in M&A; the $404M substandard pool cures without material charge-offs; buybacks continue shrinking the share count and drive EPS toward the mid-$3 range; and the peer multiple discount narrows as the commercial pivot proves out. At ~13.5x on ~$3.40+ of normalized EPS plus continued buyback accretion, the stock reaches the mid-$40s.

**Bear Case (~$27–29):** The substandard pipeline migrates to non-accrual, provisions rise, and the C&I problem credit signals broader stress in the newer commercial book; the carry-trade leverage proves a crutch and NIM rolls over as the rate cycle stabilizes; the mortgage-exit bet underdelivers on relationship-banking growth; and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range on a growth stock that isn't growing. That path takes the stock back toward $27–29, roughly 20–26% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (next report following the July 16, 2026 print) and progress on loan-growth and NIM guidance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision trend turns positive (30-day and 90-day EPS revisions swing to +2% or more), confirming the market is catching up to the operating inflection rather than trimming.
- Credit clarity: the $404M substandard-but-current pool declines materially without migrating to non-accrual, with net charge-offs staying below ~0.10% of loans.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A meaningful portion of the substandard pool migrates to non-accrual, driving NPAs/assets above ~1.0% and provisions materially higher.
- NIM contracts sequentially for two consecutive quarters, signaling the funding-cost tailwind has fully reversed.
- Loan growth stalls below ~4% annualized, invalidating the return-to-growth thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any 8-K disclosing credit migration, an activist filing, or an M&A announcement enabled by the CRA upgrade.

## Conclusion

WaFd sits at a positive cyclical turning point. Margin is expanding, NII has grown for five straight quarters, the CRA restriction that hobbled the growth strategy is resolved, loan growth has returned, capital is solid, securities marks are modest, and the company is returning capital aggressively through dividends and buybacks. The CEO's open-market preferred purchases add a genuine conviction signal. These are real positives and support continuing to hold the name for the earnings recovery plus its above-peer yield.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete. The stock has re-rated to near its 52-week high and trades below consensus targets but with limited residual upside, with estimates drifting lower rather than rising — the opposite of the revision velocity that would flag an under-appreciated inflection. The $404M substandard-but-current credit pool means the 0.48% NPA headline understates the credit risk, and the commercial pivot remains unproven through a full cycle. In the Financials sector, where selection has historically added little alpha, the burden is on a distinct company catalyst, and here the strongest catalyst (CRA) is already known and partly reflected. The result is a balanced risk/reward at ~11.6x. Maintain the HOLD, own it for the recovery and the yield, and look to add on either a pullback into the low-$30s or a positive turn in estimate revisions accompanied by credit clarity.
