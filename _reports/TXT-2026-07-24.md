**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Textron is a diversified aerospace and defense manufacturer with a leading position in business aviation (Cessna Citation, Beechcraft King Air) and military/commercial rotorcraft (Bell), generating $14.8B in FY2025 revenue. The investment case rests on two developments: the April 30, 2026 announcement of a planned separation of the low-margin Industrial segment, which would convert Textron into a cleaner pure-play A&D company; and the MV-75 tiltrotor program for the U.S. Army, a multi-decade franchise win that anchors Bell's future while creating near-term margin drag and a disclosed government-funding cliff.

The stock trades at roughly 15x forward consensus EPS ($6.51 CY, $7.28 next year), a substantial discount to A&D peers trading at 20-50x, and shows a supportive insider signal: director Thomas Kennedy (former Raytheon CEO) bought ~$989K of stock at $95.98 the day after Q1 earnings and the separation announcement. The company beats estimates consistently (four straight beats, most recently +10.1%) and returns nearly all free cash flow via buybacks (~$822M in FY25). Against this, Bell segment margin compressed from 10.3% to 8.5% in FY25 and to 6.7% in Q1 FY26 on MV-75 development mix, management has telegraphed a $60-110M unfavorable fixed-price catch-up, and the Q1 10-Q discloses that MV-75 FY2026 funds could exhaust in Q3 2026 with a stop-work risk absent $350M in additional Army appropriations.

We rate TXT a HOLD (growth_hold). The re-rating thesis from the Industrial separation is credible and specific, and current-year estimates are stable-to-slightly-rising, but the payoff is 12-18 months out and gated on two things not yet resolved: whether the MV-75 funding cliff clears without a stop-work charge, and whether Bell margin inflects as the program transitions from development to production. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising, which argues against the strongest inflection-driven BUY signal. The stock offers a reasonable entry with limited downside but no asymmetric upside until the funding and margin questions clear.

## Company Overview

Textron operates five reporting segments as of the start of FY2026 (the former eAviation segment was folded into other units):

- **Textron Aviation (~40% of revenue; $5.96B FY25):** Market leader in general and business aviation via Cessna Citation business jets, Beechcraft King Air turboprops, Caravan, and the T-6 military trainer. Two revenue lines: new aircraft and higher-margin aftermarket parts/services ($2.0B). FY25 deliveries: 171 Citation jets, 146 turboprops. New Gen3 light jets (Garmin Autoland) due 2027; Beechcraft Denali turboprop entering service 2026.
- **Bell (~29%; $4.28B):** Military and commercial rotorcraft/tiltrotor. Anchored by the MV-75 (Future Long Range Assault Aircraft) tiltrotor for the U.S. Army, plus V-22 and H-1 production/support and commercial helicopters (407, 429, 505; 525 in certification). ~13,000 Bell helicopters in the installed base drive recurring aftermarket revenue.
- **Textron Systems (~8%; $1.25B):** Defense electronics, Ship-to-Shore Connector (Navy hovercraft), Aerosonde UAS, ATAC air-combat training, Lycoming piston engines. Highest backlog growth (+27%).
- **Industrial (~22%; $3.21B):** Kautex (automotive fuel systems, EV battery enclosures) and Textron Specialized Vehicles (E-Z-GO golf carts, Cushman, Jacobsen turf). Slated for separation.
- **Finance (~0.5%):** Captive finance arm supporting Aviation/Bell sales.

The competitive moat is entrenched brands, a large installed base generating recurring aftermarket revenue, and a marquee defense franchise (MV-75) positioning Bell for a multi-decade Army production cycle. Lisa M. Atherton became President & CEO on January 4, 2026, succeeding Scott Donnelly (CEO since 2009, now Executive Chairman). Atherton is an internal promotion from the CEO roles at Bell and Textron Systems, giving her direct familiarity with the two defense franchises now central to strategy.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and profitability (FY2025).** Revenue rose 8% to $14.8B, led by Bell (+20%) and Aviation (+13%), partly offset by Industrial (−9%, reflecting the Powersports divestiture). Segment profit grew 14%. Net income was $921M (flat vs. 2023, up from $824M in the 2024 strike year). Q1 FY26 EPS was $1.25 vs. $1.13 a year prior. The company has beaten consensus in all four recent quarters (+6.4%, +5.7%, +0.5%, and +10.1% most recently), which supports confidence in forward estimates.

**Margin trajectory is the key watch item.** Consolidated gross margin declined from 20.5% (2023) to ~17.8% (FY25/Q1 FY26). This is structural, not one-off, driven by mix shift toward lower-margin MV-75 development work and inflation. Segment-level detail sharpens the picture:

- Aviation margin recovered to 11.7% (FY25) post-strike.
- Bell margin compressed from 10.3% to 8.5% (FY25), and from 9.2% to 6.7% in Q1 FY26, as MV-75 development dilutes margin even while driving revenue growth of 25-28%. Management has flagged an expected $60-110M unfavorable cumulative catch-up when the fixed-price LRIP option is awarded (late 2026/early 2027).
- Industrial remains thin (4.5% FY25, improving to 5.1% in Q1 FY26 on restructuring).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1.3B; FCF ~$930M (6.3% margin). Q1 is seasonally cash-negative (operating cash −$117M on working-capital build); full-year cash generation is positive. The balance sheet is healthy: manufacturing net-debt-to-capital ~17-20%, current ratio 1.82, long-term debt ~$3.5B, no revolver drawn on a $1.0B facility (expires 2030). The company issued $500M notes at 4.95% (Oct 2025) and $500M at 5.50% (Feb 2025). Inventory built from $4.28B to $4.56B in Q1, consistent with the production ramp but a working-capital drag. (Note: the financial-health tool's ROA of 105.6% and asset turnover of 16.97 are mis-scaled data artifacts; true ROA is ~5% and turnover ~0.82x — the underlying "financially healthy" conclusion holds.)

**Capital allocation** is buyback-heavy: $822M repurchased in FY25 (10.7M shares) and $1.1B in 2024, against a token dividend (~$0.08/yr, ~0.1% yield). Share count fell from ~183M to ~174M. A new 25M-share authorization was approved in February 2026. This is disciplined at ~15x forward earnings, and the reduction in share count directly supports EPS. One risk to the primary capital-return lever: the Q1 10-Q cites an Executive Order on defense-contractor underperformance that could constrain dividends/buybacks, a low-probability but non-trivial overhang given Textron returns nearly all capital via repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

Textron's growth is bifurcated between a recovering aviation cycle and a defense franchise in early ramp. Aviation posted +22% revenue growth and 26% profit growth in Q1 FY26, reflecting post-strike recovery, higher pricing, and volume, supported by an $8.0B Aviation backlog and the recurring aftermarket base. Bell revenue grew 9% in Q1 but profit fell 20% on MV-75 mix; the revenue trajectory is strong but the earnings quality is currently poor because development work carries low margin.

Total backlog rose to $19.2B at Q1 FY26 (Aviation $8.0B, Bell $7.6B, Systems $3.6B) from $18.8B at year-end. New orders included a $1.3B MV-75 prototype award and a $475M ATAC Navy contract. Systems shows the highest backlog growth (+27%), a positive read on defense-electronics and training demand.

Consensus captures modest growth: current-year and next-year EPS imply ~12% EPS growth, aided partly by buyback share reduction rather than pure operating leverage. The critical variable is whether Bell margin inflects as MV-75 transitions from margin-dilutive development to production over 2027-2028, which is the difference between low-single-digit and low-double-digit consolidated earnings growth. The bull case requires the gross-margin decline from 20.5% to 17.8% to reverse as MV-75 moves up the fixed-price learning curve and aftermarket mix reasserts. That reversal is plausible but not yet visible in the data. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year +0.5% over 90 days, next-year −0.1%), which places TXT in the "stable, not accelerating" category rather than the earnings-inflection cohort.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93-96, TXT trades at ~15x forward CY EPS and ~13x next-year. The stock sits well below its A&D peer group: peer median P/E is 46.4, with comparables ranging from HII at 19.6 to KTOS at 342.5 and BWXT at 51.9. The peer set is heterogeneous and skewed by high-multiple pure-play defense names, but even against the most comparable diversified/shipbuilding names (HII ~20x), Textron trades at a discount.

The discount is partly earned. Textron's ROIC is sub-8%, its 5-year TSR (181.1) has lagged the A&D peer group (237.6), and its consolidated gross margin has compressed. A conglomerate mix that includes a ~4.5%-margin auto-cyclical Industrial segment plus a margin-dilutive MV-75 ramp justifies a below-peer multiple today. The valuation question is whether the Industrial separation closes that gap.

The specific mispricing thesis: if separation removes the lowest-margin segment and leaves a cleaner Aviation-aftermarket + Bell/Systems defense-backlog story, the RemainCo should command a higher multiple. A move from ~15x to ~17-18x forward EPS supports a fair value in the $115-130 range over an 18-24 month horizon, consistent with the analyst high target of $115 and the mean of ~$102. Analyst consensus (mean $101.89, median $99.62) offers ~6-9% upside to the mean, with the high target ~20% above spot.

I score valuation at 6.0 rather than higher for two reasons. First, the calibration record shows "cheap" multiples are a persistent value-trap warning absent a specific, dated catalyst, and the separation catalyst is 12-18 months out with tax-structure and execution uncertainty, so the market's discount is not obviously wrong today. Second, estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, so this is not the earnings-inflection setup where trailing multiples understate forward power. The cheapness is real but the near-term margin drag and the funding cliff temper the case. This is a value-with-catalyst situation where the catalyst has not yet begun to resolve.

## Competitive Landscape

**Business aviation:** Textron Aviation competes with Gulfstream (General Dynamics), Bombardier, Embraer, and Dassault. Its edge is the breadth of the Citation light/midsize range and the largest business-jet aftermarket/service network, which generates recurring, higher-margin revenue. The 2024 strike temporarily ceded ground, but FY25/Q1 FY26 reflect recovery with pricing and volume gains. The Gen3 jets and Denali turboprop extend the product cycle into 2026-2027. Competitive trajectory here is stable-to-improving.

**Rotorcraft/defense:** Bell competes with Lockheed's Sikorsky and Boeing. The MV-75 win over the Sikorsky-Boeing team (Dec 2022) is a multi-decade Army program that positions Bell as the incumbent on the Army's next-generation vertical-lift platform. This is a structural share gain the market has only partially priced given the near-term margin drag masking its long-term value. The offsetting concern is commercial helicopter softness (20 deliveries in Q1 vs. 29).

**Structural context:** Elevated defense budgets and healthy bizjet demand are tailwinds. The multi-front defense environment (Middle East, ongoing Army modernization) supports sustained platform demand. Textron's below-peer 5-year TSR reflects that the market has not yet rewarded the MV-75 franchise value; the separation is the mechanism by which that gap could close. Competitive position is improving on trajectory (MV-75 incumbency, Systems backlog +27%), but the improvement is not yet reflected in margins, keeping the score at 6.2.

## Risk Assessment

**MV-75 funding cliff (most acute near-term risk).** Per the Q1 10-Q Note 13, on April 28, 2026 the U.S. Army Contracting Command notified Bell it is pursuing $350M in additional FY2026 funds. Textron expects to exhaust available FY2026 MV-75 funds in Q3 2026; absent approval, Bell could be forced to stop work, risking program delays, supplier claims, and unrecoverable costs. Management believes a stop-work would not extend into Q4 (FY27 shows $2.3B planned). This is a genuine, disclosed material risk on the company's #1 growth engine, and it resolves within the next one to two quarters.

**MV-75 fixed-price margin risk.** The LRIP option is largely fixed-price with a telegraphed $60-110M unfavorable catch-up. Fixed-price development/production on a first-of-kind tiltrotor carries cost-overrun risk that Textron absorbs above the ceiling.

**Government concentration (27% of revenue):** subject to appropriations, continuing resolutions, shutdowns, termination for convenience/default, and DCAA/DCMA audit. The risk factors cite the Executive Order on defense-contractor underperformance and possible limits on dividends/buybacks, directly relevant given Textron's buyback-centric capital return.

**Other risks:** business-jet/helicopter demand cyclicality; ~29% unionized U.S. workforce (the 2024 strike materially hurt production); tariff/USMCA exposure (~31% international revenue; the Feb 20, 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling deemed immaterial by management); Middle East conflict (new risk factor); and separation execution/timing/tax uncertainty.

The concentration of near-term binary risk (funding cliff in Q3, fixed-price catch-up telegraphed) alongside real margin compression justifies a risk score of 5.0. These are specific, dated catalysts for downside rather than generic sector risk, and the stop-work risk in particular is not fully de-risked in the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 33.3% vs. 26.3% historical volatility (7pp rich), with the term structure in backwardation (33.3% near-term > 27.1% long-term) and put/call skew of 5.7% (elevated put demand). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.6%. IV rose 3.5pp since early June alongside a 4.6% price gain, and the put/call OI ratio climbed from 0.01 to 0.32. This is consistent with the fundamental read: the market is hedging the Q3 MV-75 funding resolution and the next earnings print (last reported April 30). The elevated put demand signals the market shares the near-term funding/margin concern. Short interest is low (4.3% of float, 3.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded position on either side.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($115-130).** The Industrial separation completes over 12-18 months, converting Textron into a pure-play A&D company with a recurring aviation aftermarket base and a Bell/Systems defense backlog. The market re-rates the RemainCo from ~15x to ~17-18x forward EPS. MV-75 clears its funding cliff without a stop-work charge, transitions from margin-dilutive development to production, and Bell margin recovers toward double digits. Aviation continues its post-strike recovery with pricing and Gen3/Denali product-cycle support. Buybacks compound EPS on a shrinking share count. Director Kennedy's $1M open-market purchase proves prescient. This drives EPS toward $7.50+ and a multiple in the high-teens, supporting $115-130.

**Bear case ($75-80).** The MV-75 funding cliff triggers a Q3 2026 stop-work, generating supplier claims and unrecoverable costs alongside the telegraphed fixed-price catch-up. Bell margin stays compressed below 7%, and consolidated gross margin fails to reverse from 17.8%. The Industrial separation slips or realizes a disappointing tax-inefficient structure. A defense-appropriations shutdown or the defense-contractor Executive Order constrains buybacks, removing the primary EPS lever. Business-jet demand softens on a consumer/macro downturn. The stock retests its 52-week low of $75.80, ~21% below current.

The bull case requires two things to break right (separation execution + margin inflection) while the bear case requires only one near-term binary (funding cliff) to break wrong. That asymmetry, plus flat estimate revisions, is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- MV-75 FY2026 funding is approved ($350M or equivalent) without a Q3 stop-work charge, removing the acute near-term risk.
- Bell segment margin inflects back above 8-9% in a reported quarter, confirming the development-to-production transition, OR estimate revisions turn positive by +5% or more over 90 days.
- The Industrial separation reaches a defined structure (spin vs. sale) with a firm timeline and favorable tax treatment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Q3 2026 MV-75 stop-work is triggered and extends beyond a single quarter, or the fixed-price catch-up materially exceeds the $60-110M guided range.
- Consolidated gross margin declines below 17% in a reported quarter with no near-term recovery path.
- The separation is abandoned or delayed indefinitely, removing the re-rating catalyst, OR a defense-contractor Executive Order materially restricts the buyback program.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 earnings release (late July/August), which will report on the MV-75 funding situation and Bell margin. The Q3 FY2026 print is the more decisive event, as it should resolve the funding-cliff binary. The separation timeline (targeted late 2027) is the longer-term catalyst to monitor across the next four quarters.

## Conclusion

Textron is a reasonably priced A&D business with a genuine re-rating catalyst and a franchise defense program, held back near-term by margin compression and a live government-funding cliff. At ~15x forward earnings with a supportive insider buy and consistent estimate beats, the downside appears well-contained (bear case ~21% below current), but the upside is gated on two developments that have not yet begun to resolve: the MV-75 funding/margin question and the Industrial separation. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising, so this is not the earnings-inflection setup that warrants a full BUY. The score trajectory (6.1 → 6.9 → 6.4) reflects a stock that oscillates between HOLD and BUY as these catalysts advance and stall; the current data supports staying at HOLD with a positive medium-term bias. We would upgrade to BUY on confirmation that the funding cliff clears cleanly and Bell margin inflects, and we view accumulation on weakness toward the low-$80s as constructive given the limited downside. HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 6.1.
