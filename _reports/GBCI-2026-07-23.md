**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Glacier Bancorp is a $31.7B-asset, Montana-based multi-bank holding company running an unusual decentralized model: a single bank charter (Glacier Bank) operated through 18 separately-branded local divisions across nine Western and Mountain states, now including Texas following the Guaranty acquisition. The investment story today is a net-interest-margin recovery. Tax-equivalent NIM expanded 76bps year-over-year to 3.80% in Q1 2026, driven simultaneously by rising loan yields (6.16%, +39bps) and a falling deposit cost (1.20%), with the company paying off all $440M of remaining high-cost FHLB advances during the quarter. Diluted EPS rose to $0.63 from $0.48 a year earlier, and net income jumped 50% to $82.1M. This is a high-quality earnings recovery rather than financial engineering.

The counter-narrative is credit. Non-performing assets nearly doubled year-over-year ($39.3M to $79.5M, +102%), and early-stage delinquencies (30-89 days) surged 98% to $91.8M, concentrated in non-owner-occupied CRE and C&I — the pockets the market is watching across the regional-bank complex. Absolute levels remain historically low and reserve coverage of NPLs is above 300%, but the direction is deteriorating against a loan book that is 67% commercial real estate. Earnings are inflecting up while asset quality inflects down.

We maintain a HOLD. The recovery is real and the sub-1.25% funding franchise is differentiated, but the stock trades at roughly 26x trailing and 16x forward earnings near its 52-week high, after a 12% run over the trailing year, and the four-quarter earnings-miss streak argues for humility on consensus. The valuation offers no margin of safety for the credit trend, and the NIM tailwind is now rate-path dependent: the bank's own simulation shows it is modestly asset-sensitive, so a Fed easing cycle would erode the very margin driving current results. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward tangible book multiples in the low-$40s or on a stabilization in the delinquency trend.

## Company Overview

Glacier operates a "community bank federation" model that is distinctive among regionals. Each of its 18 divisions carries its own local brand, management team, and advisory board, while a corporate division centralizes the securities portfolio, wholesale funding, and back-office functions. The footprint spans 282 locations (237 branches) across Montana, Idaho, Utah, Washington, Wyoming, Colorado, Arizona, Nevada, and Texas. Montana (22.8% of CRE), Utah (15.3%), and Idaho (14.1%) anchor the book, with Texas, Arizona, and Colorado added through acquisition.

The loan portfolio stands at $21.0B: CRE at 67% ($13.9B), other commercial 17%, residential real estate 10%, home equity 5%, and consumer 2%. The competitive advantage is the deposit base — 30% non-interest-bearing, total deposit cost of just 1.20% — sourced from less-contested rural and suburban Western markets where relationship banking still carries pricing power. Glacier is a serial acquirer; goodwill of $1.38B against total equity of $4.25B reflects a long history of rolling up community banks across the Rockies and Southwest.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 print was the strongest in the recent trajectory. Net income of $82.1M compares to $63.8M in Q4 2025 and $54.6M in Q1 2025. ROAA climbed to 1.05% (from 0.80% a year prior) and ROAE to 7.82% (from 6.77%). The efficiency ratio sits at 63.1%, improved from 65.5% a year ago though up slightly from Q4's 61.0% on acquisition costs.

The NIM bridge is the core mechanism. Margin moved from 3.04% to 3.80% year-over-year through three levers working together: loan yields rose 39bps to 6.16% as the book repriced; deposit costs fell as the 2023-24 deposit-cost squeeze reversed; and the company retired $1.52B of high-cost FHLB advances over the trailing year, eliminating an expensive wholesale funding drag. Net interest income rose 41% year-over-year to $269M, and non-interest income rose 17% to $38M, for total quarterly revenue near $307M.

The multi-year trend confirms a trough-and-recovery pattern: net income of $223M in FY2023, $190M in FY2024, and $239M in FY2025. The company's headline internal metric, ROTE ex-AOCI, fell from 14.8% in 2021 to 8.2% in 2024 and has begun recovering toward roughly 9.5% in 2025. The Q1 annualized run-rate implies continued improvement if margin holds.

Capital is a clear strength. Bank CET1 is 12.89%, total risk-based capital 14.13%, and the leverage ratio 9.80% — all comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. Tangible common equity to tangible assets improved to 9.15% from 8.18%, and tangible book value per share is $21.29. The securities book carries about $489M of gross unrealized losses (AFS $240M plus HTM $249M), but these are essentially all agency and investment-grade paper deemed rate-driven with no credit allowance; accumulated other comprehensive loss narrowed to $176M from $309M a year ago as rates rallied. Capital sits comfortably above minimums even absorbing these marks. The dividend is $0.33 quarterly, the 164th consecutive payout, well-covered as EPS normalizes toward $0.70 operating from the trough levels where the payout ratio ran above 66%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a blend of cyclical margin recovery and acquired scale. On the earnings side, the near-term inflection is powerful: Q2 2026 consensus is roughly $0.76 EPS (+69% year-over-year), with current-year consensus at $3.17 and next-year at $3.65. Estimate revisions are flat, however — current-year estimates moved just +0.1% over 90 days and next-year +0.1%. This is not the accelerating positive-revision pattern that flags a systematically underestimated inflection; the Street already has the recovery in its numbers, which reduces the probability of upside surprise-driven re-rating.

On the franchise side, loans grew 22% and deposits 20% year-over-year, but only roughly 4% of loan growth and 2% of deposit growth was organic — the balance came from Bank of Idaho (closed April 2025) and Guaranty Bancshares/Texas (closed October 2025). This is growth "rented" with equity: shares outstanding rose from ~113M to ~130M, roughly 15% dilution funded by $765M of stock. Tangible book value per share still grew 10% year-over-year, so the deals were not obviously value-destructive on a TBV basis, but the per-share earnings power understates absolute growth and the model depends on continued accretive M&A execution.

The critical nuance for the growth outlook is rate sensitivity. The bank's own simulation shows NII rising in up-rate scenarios (+2.1% at +200bp) and falling in down-rate scenarios (-1.5% at -200bp, and -6.9% over two years). The margin recovery driving current results is therefore partly rate-cycle dependent, and the balance sheet is now modestly asset-sensitive. A sustained Fed easing cycle would work against the earnings engine, capping the durability of the growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.17, GBCI trades at approximately 26x trailing EPS of $1.99 (a figure distorted by the trough year) and roughly 16x forward consensus. On a normalized basis, forward earnings of $3.17-3.65 imply the stock is neither cheap nor expensive relative to its own recovering fundamentals — a mid-teens forward multiple for a bank generating a ~1.05% ROAA and ~9.5% ROTE is fair, not compelling. Price-to-tangible-book of roughly 2.4x ($51.17 on $21.29 TBV) is a premium multiple that prices in a franchise-quality deposit base and continued margin normalization; it leaves little cushion if credit deteriorates or the margin tailwind fades.

Analyst targets bracket $54 to $60 with a mean of $56.58, implying single-digit-to-mid-teens upside. Under the framework that treats consensus targets as floors on cheap inflection stocks, this name does not qualify — the estimate revisions are flat, not rising, and the stock is not cheap relative to growth (forward P/E of 16x against low-double-digit EPS growth once the acquisition base laps gives a PEG near or above 1.5). The proximity to consensus reflects a fairly-valued name, not a stale target.

We are deliberately cautious on rewarding the modest forward multiple. In this system's experience, a compressed-looking multiple on a bank recovering from a trough, absent an independent catalyst and with a deteriorating credit trend, is more value-trap than value opportunity. The four consecutive earnings misses (Q1 2026 at $0.63 vs. $0.68 estimate, -7.7%; and three prior quarters missing 3-9%) undercut confidence in the forward estimates that make the stock look reasonable. Assigning a valuation score of 4.5 reflects a fairly-valued-to-slightly-rich name near its high, where the burden is on the bull to identify why the market is wrong, and the evidence does not support that.

## Competitive Landscape

Glacier's moat is its funding franchise. A 30% non-interest-bearing deposit mix and a 1.20% total deposit cost are top-decile characteristics that most regionals cannot replicate, and they are the direct source of the NIM recovery's magnitude. The decentralized division model, embedding local brands and underwriting in less-contested Western and Mountain markets, is the structural reason those deposits are sticky and low-cost. Management explicitly frames the geographic dispersion across diverse local economies (commodities, tourism, real estate) as a credit-risk mitigant.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on funding and scale, but the competitive picture is not uniformly positive. The NIM recovery is partly a sector-wide tailwind — regional banks broadly benefited from the end of the 2023-24 deposit-cost squeeze — so a portion of the margin story is beta, not idiosyncratic share gain. On the loan side, organic growth is low single digits, meaning the franchise is expanding through acquisition rather than winning share organically. The rising CRE and office delinquencies echo an industry-wide theme; GBCI's relative position looks better than average on funding cost and capital but worse on its outsized 67% CRE concentration. Management cites limited central-business-district office exposure (office is 12.4% of CRE, ~8.3% of total loans, diversified into suburban and rural markets) as mitigation. On balance the competitive position is solid and improving on funding, which supports a 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The central idiosyncratic risk is the intersection of CRE concentration and deteriorating credit. Management touts a 57% weighted-average LTV, small average loan size, and geographic dispersion as mitigants, but the credit trend is the clearest negative in the data. NPAs rose to 0.25% of assets from 0.14%; 30-89 day delinquencies rose to 0.44% of loans from 0.27%; non-owner-occupied CRE 30-89 delinquencies jumped from $0.3M to $21.5M; and C&I non-accruals rose to $26.5M. The Q4 2025 provision spike to $32.5M was largely a $27.2M acquisition-related CECL "double count" on Guaranty loans (a one-time item), and provisioning normalized to $3.5M in Q1 2026 — but the underlying delinquency trend is not one-time.

Secondary risks include integration and goodwill: two deals closed in the past year, Guaranty's core-system conversion completed in Q1 2026, and $1.38B of goodwill against $4.25B equity carries an impairment tail if the acquired books underperform or the stock derates. Rate risk cuts the other way from the usual bank concern — Glacier is now asset-sensitive, so aggressive Fed cuts erode the margin tailwind driving earnings, a nuance the market may under-appreciate. Key-person dependence on CEO Chesler, senior management, and the division Presidents is a structural feature of the decentralized model. Two Item 5.02 director/officer events (November 2024, February 2026) appear in the 8-K index; the CEO and CFO remain in place and were buying stock, suggesting these are routine transitions, but the specifics were not independently confirmed.

Screening tools flagging GBCI with an Altman Z-Score near 0.3 ("distressed") are producing a false signal — the model is built for industrial firms and mechanically penalizes deposit-funded bank balance sheets. GBCI is well-capitalized at 12.9% CET1, and this signal should be disregarded.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.5% sits modestly rich versus 29.5% historical, with the term structure in backwardation (33.5% near-term vs. 28.4% at six months) — the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the Q2 earnings print. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew of -22.3% and a put/call OI ratio that collapsed from 1.93 to 0.54 over the prior month indicate the options market is not positioned for downside, a mildly constructive-to-complacent signal into the print. The ±9.8% implied move for the nearest expiry frames the binary risk around Q2 results. Short interest is moderate at 7.5% of float with 6.5 days to cover — not a crowded short, but enough to add fuel if results surprise positively.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60):** The NIM recovery has further to run as the remaining high-cost funding and lower-yielding legacy securities reprice, pushing ROAA toward 1.10%+ and ROTE back toward the low double digits. Credit deterioration proves shallow and stabilizes, with reserve coverage absorbing losses without meaningful provision drag. The Guaranty and Bank of Idaho integrations deliver expense synergies, driving the efficiency ratio back below 60%. EPS reaches the $3.65 next-year consensus, and the market re-rates the franchise-quality deposit base to ~16-17x forward, supporting the high analyst target near $60.

**Bear Case (~$42, roughly -18%):** CRE and C&I delinquencies continue to accelerate, forcing provision builds that consume the margin recovery and drop EPS below consensus, extending the four-quarter miss streak. A Fed easing cycle compresses the now-asset-sensitive margin (-1.5% to -6.9% NII sensitivity), removing the primary earnings driver. Goodwill impairment risk surfaces if acquired books underperform. The stock derates toward ~2.0x tangible book (~$42) as the market discounts a lower-quality, credit-impaired recovery. This maps to the mid-$40s where the stock traded through much of late 2025 on the same credit concerns.

The base case sits between: EPS lands near current-year consensus of $3.17 with modest continued margin support offset by rising provisions, credit deterioration proves manageable but not resolved, and the stock holds a ~16x forward multiple. That implies a base-case value around $52-53 — essentially flat from current levels, which is the core of the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q2 2026 print and the subsequent two quarters of credit and margin data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (roughly 2.0x tangible book), which would restore a margin of safety and re-establish asymmetric upside on the recovery thesis.
- Stabilization or reversal in the delinquency trend — 30-89 day delinquencies flattening below the Q1 0.44% level and NPAs ceasing to climb for two consecutive quarters.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising more than +3% over 90 days), signaling the recovery is being systematically underestimated rather than fully priced.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Non-performing assets or 30-89 day delinquencies accelerating meaningfully further, particularly a second consecutive quarter of double-digit sequential increases in non-owner-occupied CRE past-dues, coupled with a provision build that cuts EPS below $0.60 quarterly.
- A dividend-coverage problem or evidence of goodwill impairment on the acquired books.
- Confirmation of a sustained Fed easing path that materially compresses the asset-sensitive margin.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which the ±9.8% implied move frames as a genuine binary for the near-term thesis.

## Conclusion

Glacier Bancorp is a well-capitalized, well-managed community-bank franchise executing a net-interest-margin recovery on the back of a cheap and sticky deposit base. Management's open-market buying near $41 in November 2025 (CEO and CFO simultaneously) was a constructive tell, and the balance sheet has been actively de-risked through FHLB paydowns and securities repositioning. Those are real positives.

The stock has already captured much of the recovery, trading at ~16x forward earnings and ~2.4x tangible book near its 52-week high, with flat estimate revisions and a four-quarter miss streak that undercuts confidence in forward numbers. Against that fair-to-full valuation sits a credit trend moving the wrong way in exactly the CRE and C&I pockets the market is watching, and an earnings engine now vulnerable to Fed cuts. The scores have drifted lower across three prior reports (6.2 to 6.0), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: a strong Q1 headline offset by worsening asset quality and no valuation cushion. We rate GBCI a HOLD as a growth-oriented position — own it for the recovery and the franchise, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced at current levels, and better entry points are likely on either a credit-driven pullback or clearer evidence that delinquencies have stabilized.
