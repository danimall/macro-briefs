## Executive Summary

Pool Corporation is the world's largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool supplies, operating 456 sales centers serving ~125,000 customers across North America, Europe, and Australia. The stock has fallen 49% from its 52-week high of $345 to $175, reflecting three compounding forces: a cyclical earnings decline from pandemic-era peaks (EPS from ~$18 to ~$11), an abrupt CEO departure on May 4, 2026, and deteriorating macro conditions that directly impair the company's rate-sensitive and discretionary revenue streams.

At 16x forward earnings, POOL trades at a substantial discount to its 10-year average multiple of 25-35x. Former CEO Manuel Perez de la Mesa — who built Pool Corp into the dominant national distributor over 18 years — has purchased $5.8M in stock over the past three months, with additional cluster buying from three other directors. This insider signal is among the strongest I've seen in the consumer space. However, the current macro environment (mortgage rates ~7.5%, negative real wages, CPI 3.8%, PPI 6%) directly impairs the 36% of revenue tied to discretionary remodeling and new construction, and there is no identifiable catalyst for near-term improvement absent rate cuts that appear unlikely given the Fed's hawkish trajectory under Warsh.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation despite the compressed valuation and strong insider signal is the absence of a dated, non-consensus catalyst. The consensus already expects low single-digit growth in 2026; the macro headwinds to the discretionary segment are intensifying, not abating; and CEO transition risk adds execution uncertainty over the next 2-3 quarters. The bull case requires mortgage rate relief, and current rate policy points away from that relief materializing within 6 months.

## Company Overview

Pool Corp distributes ~200,000 SKUs across 700+ product lines to swimming pool builders, service companies, and specialty retailers. The business has a critical structural characteristic: **64% of revenue is non-discretionary maintenance and minor repair** (chemicals, parts, cleaning equipment), which recurs annually regardless of economic conditions because pool owners must maintain their pools. The remaining 36% splits between remodeling/renovations (22%) and new construction (14%).

Geographic concentration is notable: California, Florida, Texas, and Arizona represent ~53% of sales. The company operates five distribution networks (SCP, Superior, Horizon, NPT, Sun Wholesale) and has proprietary brands that enhance margins.

The installed base of ~5.5 million in-ground pools in the U.S. provides a growing annuity stream — even at depressed construction rates of ~60,000 pools/year, the maintenance-addressable pool count continues expanding. This structural characteristic provides a floor on revenue that pure-play cyclical companies lack.

Revenue has declined 14.4% from peak while operating income fell 43.5%, demonstrating the significant operating leverage in the business model. Q1 2026 showed the first meaningful positive quarter with +6% revenue growth and +8% adjusted EPS growth.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt/equity at 2.06x with total debt of $1.2B. Leverage ratio of 1.67x vs. 3.25x covenant maximum provides substantial headroom. The $1.3B credit facility (refinanced July 2025) matures September 2029. Interest rate swaps at 0.67-0.76% on $300M provide ~$10-12M annual savings but expire in February 2027.

**Cash Flow and Capital Allocation:** FCF was $310M in 2025 (5.9% margin). The company repurchased $341M in stock during 2025 and $64M in Q1 2026 at average prices well above current levels (~$203/share in March). Dividends total $185M annually ($5.00/share, yielding ~2.9% at current price). Share count declined ~2% from 37.1M to 36.4M over the year.

**Inventory:** Up 14% YoY to $1.66B at Q1 2026. Management attributes this to pre-buying ahead of price increases. Inventory turns declined from 2.8x to 2.6x. This creates downside risk if demand disappoints, as Pool Corp's largest balance sheet asset becomes a liability requiring markdowns.

## Growth Analysis

Pool Corp's near-term growth profile is constrained:

**Positive signals:**
- Q1 2026 showed +6% revenue growth — first positive quarter in three years
- Equipment sales +7%, building materials +5% in Q1
- The installed pool base grows annually regardless of new construction rates
- Sunbelt population migration provides a secular tailwind

**Headwinds:**
- New pool construction at ~60,000 units (down from 100,000+ at peak), with mortgage rates at 7.5% suppressing any recovery
- Consumer real wages turning negative for the first time in three years
- PPI at 6% compresses margins on imported products and tariff-exposed categories
- Gas prices >$4.50 in key Sunbelt markets reduce discretionary spending
- Management's own 2026 guidance calls for only low single-digit revenue growth and essentially flat EPS ($10.87-$11.17)

**Medium-term recovery math:** If new construction normalizes to 80,000-90,000 pools (still below 2021-2022 peaks) and remodeling activity recovers modestly, revenue could reach $5.8-6.0B with operating margins returning to 13-14%. This implies mid-cycle EPS power of $14-16. The critical prerequisite is lower mortgage rates, which the current Fed trajectory makes unlikely within 6-12 months.

Estimate revisions show a mixed picture: 90-day revisions are negative (-4.4% current year, -4.9% next year), while 30-day revisions have turned marginally positive (+0.7% current year, +1.2% next year). This suggests the bottom in estimates may be forming, but there is no momentum acceleration that would constitute a strong buy signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.94, POOL trades at:
- 16.0x guided 2026 EPS of $11.00
- 14.5x consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.04
- ~20x trailing FCF ($310M / $6.4B market cap)
- 2.9% dividend yield

**Historical context:** POOL's 10-year average P/E is approximately 28-32x. The stock has rarely traded below 20x outside of severe market dislocations. At 16x, the market is pricing in either permanent earnings impairment or further cyclical deterioration.

**Peer comparison:** LKQ trades at 13.4x with a 4.3% yield. GPC trades at 234.6x (distorted by a one-time charge year) but typically at 18-22x normalized earnings. ARMK at 40.7x. POOL's 16x multiple is at the low end of the distribution spectrum, but Pool Corp's quality characteristics (dominant market position, 64% recurring revenue, consistent FCF generation) historically commanded premium multiples.

**Implied assumptions at current price:** At 16x and $11 EPS, the market prices no multiple expansion and no earnings recovery — essentially a permanent impairment scenario. If EPS recovers to $14 (achievable at mid-cycle activity levels) and the multiple re-rates to even 20x (still below historical average), fair value would be $280. This represents 60% upside from current levels.

**Why the market may be right (for now):** The rate environment that would trigger recovery is actively deteriorating. Mortgage rates at 7.5% suppress the exact activities (new construction, remodeling) that would drive earnings back to mid-cycle. The stagflationary macro environment has no clear resolution mechanism in the near term.

## Competitive Landscape

Pool Corp's competitive position remains strong and is arguably improving through the downturn:

**Structural advantages:**
- 456-location network provides same-day/next-day delivery that regional competitors cannot match
- Purchasing volume enables vendor rebate programs (material to gross margin) unavailable to smaller competitors
- POOL360 digital platform creates customer switching costs
- Proprietary brands (Regal, E-Z Clor, SuperPro, PoolStyle) enhance margins

**Risk factors to competitive position:**
- Three suppliers (Pentair 20%, Zodiac 12%, Hayward 11%) represent 43% of COGS — disintermediation risk
- The 10-K explicitly states "barriers to entry in our industry are relatively low"
- Internet retailers continue to erode commodity product sales

The pool distribution industry rewards density and proximity — customers need products delivered quickly to job sites. This creates a self-reinforcing advantage for the scaled player. During downturns, smaller regional competitors often exit or become acquisition targets, and Pool Corp has historically consolidated during weakness. The current downturn may create acquisition opportunities that further widen the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Macro/Rate Environment (HIGH — and worsening):** Each additional quarter at elevated rates delays the recovery in new pool construction and remodeling activity. With the Fed under Warsh unlikely to cut rates, this headwind has no clear expiration date.

**2. CEO Transition (MODERATE — partially mitigated):** The new CEO (John Watwood, from Motion Industries) has no pool industry background. However, the business is operationally decentralized — 456 sales centers run by local managers with established customer relationships — limiting key-man risk. The postponed Investor Day reduces visibility into strategic direction.

**3. Inventory Risk (MODERATE):** At 2.6x turns (declining), the company is betting on price increases sticking and volume recovering. Markdown risk materializes if demand disappoints.

**4. Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH):** The 36% of revenue tied to discretionary activity faces the worst consumer configuration since 2008-2009, with negative real wages and gas prices >$4.50 in Pool Corp's core Sunbelt markets.

**5. Short Interest (ELEVATED):** At 10.9% of float with 4.6 days to cover, bears are positioned aggressively. This creates reflexive downside pressure but also potential short-squeeze dynamics if positive catalysts emerge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-300, +49-71%):**
- Cyclical trough confirmed by Q1 2026 inflection; maintenance base provides earnings floor
- Multiple re-rates from 16x to 22-25x as visibility into recovery improves
- Insider buying signals conviction from people with deep knowledge of the business
- Share buyback at prices above current levels supports floor
- If rate cuts materialize by 2027, new construction and remodeling volumes could recover 20-30%
- Mid-cycle EPS of $14-16 at 22x = $308-352

**Bear Case ($135-155, -11-23%):**
- Rates stay elevated through 2027, compressing discretionary volume further
- New CEO proves unable to execute or makes strategic errors
- Inventory markdowns compress margins below guidance
- Consumer spending deterioration accelerates — negative real wages + gas prices squeeze pool maintenance budgets (even the "recurring" 64% has some elasticity)
- Stock de-rates further to 12-13x on prolonged earnings trough, implying $132-143
- Stagflation persists: oil >$100 + mortgage rates 7.5%+ through 2027

**The Pivot Condition:** The difference between the bull and bear case is mortgage rates. At 6% mortgage rates, pool construction and remodeling activity would recover meaningfully. At 7.5%+, they remain suppressed. The current macro trajectory (30Y >5.1%) points toward the bear case environment persisting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Mortgage rates decline below 6.5% (sustained for 30+ days), signaling the release valve for new construction and remodeling demand
2. Two consecutive quarters of revenue growth >8% with operating margin expansion of >50bps, confirming cyclical recovery
3. New CEO establishes credibility via a rescheduled Investor Day with specific margin recovery targets and a strategic framework that makes operational sense

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Management cuts 2026 EPS guidance below $10.00, indicating the maintenance base itself is deteriorating
2. Inventory turns fall below 2.2x, suggesting demand is not absorbing pre-bought inventory
3. Two additional quarters of earnings misses, extending the pattern to 5 misses in 6 quarters

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) is the next natural evaluation point. Q2 is Pool Corp's largest revenue quarter (61% of operating income in Q2-Q3). If Q2 confirms the Q1 trend with continued mid-single-digit growth, the upgrade case strengthens materially.

## Conclusion

Pool Corporation at $175 presents a compelling long-term value proposition: a dominant distributor with 64% recurring revenue, strong insider buying, and a stock trading at a 40-50% discount to historical multiples. The installed pool base continues to grow, the competitive position is intact, and the cyclical trough appears to be forming based on Q1 2026 data.

The recommendation remains HOLD because the specific catalyst for multiple re-rating — lower mortgage rates driving recovery in the 36% discretionary revenue — has no identifiable timeline given the current stagflationary macro environment. The conditions for a sustained pool construction recovery are actively deteriorating. Additionally, the CEO transition introduces 2-3 quarters of execution uncertainty that the market has not fully digested (stock fell 6.3% on the news and has continued declining).

The insider buying signal is powerful — Perez de la Mesa's $5.8M in purchases from someone who knows this business better than anyone alive represents genuine conviction. But insider timing can be early by 6-12 months, and the macro headwinds here are structural rather than sentiment-driven. The stock could grind lower for another 2-4 quarters before the recovery thesis materializes.

For investors with a 2+ year horizon and tolerance for interim drawdowns, initiating a partial position here (50% of intended weight) with plans to add on either a fundamental confirmation (Q2 beat + guidance raise) or further price decline toward $150 (14x forward EPS) is reasonable. For those requiring near-term catalysts, the entry point is premature.
