## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is one of the largest publicly traded mineral and royalty interest companies focused on the Permian Basin, with approximately 86,639 net royalty acres (as of March 31, 2026) generating ~130,000 BOE/d of attributed production. The zero-capex business model yields cash operating margins of approximately 91% of royalty income, and following the February 2026 Non-Permian Divestiture ($617M proceeds used to eliminate the term loan and revolver), the balance sheet is structurally sound with ~$1.62B in total debt against approximately $1.48B in available revolving credit capacity.

At $46.85, VNOM trades at approximately 19.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.43, offering an annualized dividend yield of approximately 5.7% (based on Q1 2026 total distributions) and 23% upside to the $57.76 mean analyst target. The stock has been a consistent BUY across prior analyses, and the thesis has strengthened: Q1 2026 delivered a 28% EPS beat, oil prices are materially above the $64.73 average realized in FY2025, and 90-day estimate revisions of +68% for FY2026 confirm that forward earnings power is substantially higher than trailing results suggest. The primary risks are commodity price exposure (partially mitigated by an active hedging program) and Diamondback's approximately 38.9% control position, which it is actively monetizing.

## Company Overview

Viper Energy operates as a pure mineral and royalty interest vehicle in the Permian Basin. It has no employees — all management and administrative services are provided by Diamondback Energy (FANG) under a Services and Secondment Agreement. The company earns royalty income on production from its acreage without bearing any drilling, completion, or operating costs. Cash operating expenses are just $3.52/BOE (production taxes plus allocated G&A).

The 2025 fiscal year was transformational: the $4.0B all-equity Sitio Royalties acquisition and $3.5B Drop Down from Diamondback/Endeavor nearly tripled acreage and doubled production. In February 2026, the company completed the Non-Permian Divestiture ($617M) and became a pure-play Permian vehicle. It holds 406,035 MBOE of proved reserves (78% proved developed producing) with a reserve life of approximately 8.6 years at current production rates.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Cash Flow:** FY2025 royalty income reached $1.346B (+58% YoY), driven by 91% production growth partially offset by a 16% decline in realized oil prices ($63.27 vs. $75.48 in 2024). Operating cash flow was $1.053B (+70%), demonstrating the volume gains more than offset price weakness. Q1 2026 royalty income of $496M annualizes at ~$2.0B, reflecting both higher production rates and significantly improved oil realizations.

**GAAP vs. Economic Reality:** The reported FY2025 net loss of $(206)M and trailing EPS of -$0.48 are artifacts of $768M in non-cash ceiling test impairments and $607M in depletion charges. These impairments were triggered by accounting mechanics (full cost method + Drop Down recorded at Diamondback's historical carrying value exceeding market value). Operating cash flow of $1.053B and Q1 2026 net income of $97M ($0.53 per Class A share) confirm no economic distress exists.

**Balance Sheet:** As of March 31, 2026, total debt stands at approximately $1.62B ($500M at 4.9% due 2030, $1.1B at 5.7% due 2035, and $20M in revolver borrowings). Revolving credit facility availability is approximately $1.48B. Credit ratings are BBB-/BBB-/Ba1 (S&P/Fitch/Moody's). At approximately 0.8x net debt/annualized Q1 EBITDA, leverage is modest. The pending Riverbend acquisition (announced May 2026) would add approximately 3,064 net royalty acres and take pro forma leverage to approximately 1.1x at $55 WTI.

**Capital Returns:** The company paid $745M in total dividends in FY2025 and has a $1.75B repurchase authorization (~$1.2B remaining). The annual base dividend of $1.52/share yields 3.2%, with variable components bringing total annualized yield to approximately 5.7% based on Q1 2026 distributions. This yield is based on the current quarter's base-plus-variable dividend and is not a guaranteed forward yield, as the variable component is commodity-sensitive. Management has signaled intent to return >100% of cash available for distribution going forward as it approaches its $1.5B net debt target.

## Growth Analysis

Updated standalone production guidance for 2026 is 126–130 MBOE/d, representing the first full year reflecting the Sitio and Drop Down acquisitions. The pending Riverbend acquisition is expected to add approximately 1,000 BO/d to the midpoint of standalone 2026 oil guidance if closed. With 88 gross rigs currently operating on Viper's acreage, 1,370 wells in active development, and 1,351 "line of sight" wells (as of March 31, 2026), the production trajectory is supported by visible operator activity.

Estimate revisions tell the growth story: FY2026 EPS consensus has risen from $1.45 to $2.43 over the past 90 days (+68%), driven by both volume ramp and oil price improvement. However, 30-day revisions are -4.4%, reflecting recent oil price volatility and some normalization from peak estimates. This suggests the acceleration phase of the revision cycle may be decelerating, though the absolute level of forward earnings remains well above trailing results.

Natural gas represents 26% of reserves and provides additional optionality. Waha Hub prices averaged just $1.08/Mcf in 2025 (vs. Henry Hub $3.62) due to pipeline bottlenecks. New takeaway capacity should narrow this differential, providing incremental revenue without any capital expenditure from Viper.

The minerals/royalty space remains highly fragmented, and Viper's scale (~86,600 net royalty acres, one of the largest publicly traded Permian-focused minerals/royalty companies) positions it as a natural consolidator for the next cycle of transactions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.85, VNOM trades at 19.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.43 and 19.5x FY2027E of $2.40. Against the peer median P/E of 20.5x and considering the zero-capex model, investment-grade balance sheet, and approximately 5.7% annualized dividend yield, the current multiple is reasonable.

The critical valuation lens for VNOM is oil price sensitivity. At current oil prices above the depressed 2025 average, Viper's economics are substantially better than trailing metrics suggest. If oil sustains at elevated levels, FY2026 earnings could exceed consensus (given the 28% Q1 beat pattern). The analyst consensus target of $57.76 (range $49-$65) implies 23% upside from current levels.

Against the peer group: AM (25.4x), KNTK (20.5x), DTM (33.1x) all trade at comparable or higher multiples with less favorable business models (midstream assets require maintenance capex and have regulated rate exposure). VNOM's zero-capex royalty model should arguably command a premium, suggesting the 19.3x forward multiple undervalues the business quality.

## Competitive Landscape

Viper's competitive position is structurally advantaged:

1. **Zero-capex model:** Unlike E&Ps or midstream operators, Viper bears no drilling, completion, operating, or maintenance costs. This makes margins insensitive to cost inflation (oilfield services pricing, steel, labor).

2. **Scale in fragmented market:** With ~86,600 net royalty acres and the Sitio/Drop Down integration progressing (Sitio represented approximately 32% of consolidated assets in FY2025, with integration still underway per the 10-K), Viper has data analytics advantages and deal-sourcing capabilities that smaller competitors cannot match.

3. **Diamondback activity:** 55% of royalty income comes from Diamondback, which is among the most active and efficient operators in the Permian. This provides visibility into development pace.

4. **Operator diversification improving:** ExxonMobil represents 14% of income, with the remaining 31% spread across multiple operators. Sitio's integration broadened operator exposure.

5. **Basin quality:** The Permian Basin accounts for 46% of U.S. horizontal drilling activity, and Viper's acreage is concentrated in the most prolific zones (Midland and Delaware sub-basins).

Scale is increasing, costs per BOE are declining, and geographic concentration (post-divestiture) reduces operational complexity.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** This is the dominant risk. Viper's revenue is approximately 70% oil-linked. While Viper does actively use commodity derivative instruments — including WTI puts, WTI/Brent basis positions, WTI roll swaps, Henry Hub collars, and Waha basis swaps — its hedging program only partially mitigates price exposure. A sustained decline in oil prices would still reduce annual revenue materially. Under a sustained $55–60 WTI scenario, the stock would likely trade $35-38 based on historical valuation at those price levels.

**Diamondback Control & Selling (MODERATE-HIGH):** Diamondback sold ~$589M of VNOM shares in March 2026 at ~$45.69/share, reducing its beneficial ownership to approximately 38.9% as of March 31, 2026 (down from 42.1% at year-end 2025). While still retaining significant economic interest and governance rights (which persist while it holds at least 25%), this active monetization creates persistent supply overhang. The certificate of incorporation explicitly states Diamondback has no duty to refrain from competing with Viper, creating structural governance conflicts.

**Depletion Rate Escalation (MODERATE):** The near-doubling of depletion to $17.48/BOE reflects acquisition cost allocation that will suppress GAAP earnings indefinitely. While economically irrelevant to cash flows, it creates a persistent disconnect between operating performance and reported results.

**Operator Activity Dependence (MODERATE):** If Diamondback implements further capital discipline (reducing rig count in the Permian), Viper's organic production growth decelerates. Viper has zero control over drilling pace.

**Integration Risk (MODERATE):** The Sitio acquisition integration remains in progress. Viper's FY2025 10-K disclosed that it was "in the process of integrating" Sitio entities and excluded Sitio from its internal controls assessment. Sitio represented approximately 32% of consolidated assets and 12% of total operating income in FY2025.

**Acquisition/Leverage Risk (MODERATE):** The pending Riverbend acquisition would increase pro forma net debt to approximately $1.8B and leverage to approximately 1.1x at $55 WTI, partially reversing the deleveraging achieved through the Non-Permian Divestiture.

**Bear Case ($35-38):** Oil prices decline significantly. Diamondback accelerates share sales creating additional supply. Multiple contracts to 16-17x on reduced estimates of ~$2.10-2.20 EPS. Downside of ~20-25% from current levels.

**Base Case ($55-58):** Oil prices moderate from current levels but sustain above long-term averages. FY2026 EPS of $2.50-2.80. Stock re-rates toward consensus target range. Total return of 15-20% including dividends.

## Conclusion

VNOM warrants a BUY rating at $46.85. The zero-capex royalty model operating in the current elevated oil price environment generates substantially higher cash flows than consensus fully captures, given the 90-day estimate revision of +68% combined with Q1's 28% EPS beat. The post-divestiture balance sheet carries approximately $1.62B in debt at ~0.8x net debt/EBITDA, with investment-grade ratings from two agencies, and the approximately 5.7% annualized dividend yield provides income support during holding periods.

The primary constraint on conviction is commodity price sensitivity. While Viper actively hedges using derivative instruments, its revenue remains highly oil-price-dependent. If oil prices decline materially, earnings would contract meaningfully and the stock would likely revert to the mid-$30s. This keeps position sizing moderate (2-3% of portfolio). The broad analyst consensus (18 Buy ratings, 1 Hold), combined with Jefferies' recent upgrade and $55 target, confirm institutional confidence in the fundamental quality of the asset.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Leverage is higher than initially stated (~0.8x current, potentially ~1.1x pro forma for Riverbend), current acreage is ~86,639 (not 96,003) net royalty acres post-divestiture, and Viper does actively hedge commodity exposure — these corrections moderate the "zero risk balance sheet" and "fully unhedged commodity binary" framing but do not invalidate the BUY thesis at current prices.
