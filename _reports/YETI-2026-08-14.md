## Executive Summary

YETI reported Q2 FY2026 on August 13 with net sales of $483.9M (+9%), GAAP EPS of $0.94, adjusted EPS of $0.67 against a non-GAAP consensus near $0.55, and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance from $2.83–2.89 to $2.94–3.00. The stock fell approximately 12%. The reconciliation explains the reaction. Q2 cost of goods sold was reduced by a net $42.6M of IEEPA tariff refunds relating largely to duties expensed in fiscal 2025. Strip that out and gross margin was 57.9% versus 57.8% a year earlier — flat — and operating income was $50.9M versus $62.0M, down 18%. Management's own non-GAAP build corroborates this by a different route: adjusted gross margin expansion of 170bps decomposes into +110bps operational, +170bps from 2026-related refunds, and −110bps from higher realized tariffs, meaning the operational gain and the tariff drag cancelled exactly. Adjusted operating income was $68.2M, down 7%; adjusted operating margin 14.1% versus 16.4%. Adjusted net income fell 8% while adjusted EPS rose 2%, the difference being a 9.2% reduction in diluted shares (75.782M from 83.462M).

The second issue is concentration of the year's earnings. Reconstructing the guide from disclosed inputs — sales of roughly $2,009M at the +7–8% midpoint, adjusted operating margin of ~14.9% giving ~$299M, and +10–12% growth implying an FY2025 base of ~$270M — leaves an implied H2 adjusted operating income of ~$204M against ~$161M, or +27% growth on ~+7% sales growth, and an implied H2 margin of ~17.9% versus ~15.1%. That +280bp swing matches management's stated disclosure precisely, which validates the arithmetic and simultaneously establishes that roughly two-thirds of full-year adjusted operating income and more than all of its growth must arrive in a single seasonal peak, immediately after a half in which adjusted operating margin contracted 260bps. The bridge has identifiable mechanical support (the "Four Letters" brand campaign moved from Q4 FY2025 into Q2 FY2026; H2 FY2025 absorbed peak tariffs and China-transition inventory constraints; $10.0M of refunds sits capitalised in inventory awaiting release into H2 COGS). It is also unforgiving: FY2026 is a 52-week year lapping FY2025's 53 weeks with the missing week falling in Q4, operating expense guidance was *raised* to +6–8% from +4–7% on distribution, fulfilment and commodity inflation, and the guide assumes U.S. tariff rates step back to approximately 20% in September under an assumption the CFO explicitly said the company has no line of sight on.

Against that, the price is 15.8% below the August 5 high of $53.99 and the valuation is defensible in absolute terms: 15.3x the $2.97 guided adjusted EPS midpoint (15.7x stripping the disclosed $0.08 refund benefit), 13.7x FY2027 consensus of $3.33, ~11.2x EV to guided adjusted operating income, and a 6.4% free cash flow yield on the company's definition (~5.5% if capitalised intangibles are deducted). Valuation has improved since our last review while earnings quality and balance-sheet flexibility have deteriorated. Those offset. We maintain HOLD at a slightly lower overall score, with the composition shifting — financial health down from 7.2 to 6.2, valuation up from 4.8 to 5.9.

## Company Overview

YETI designs and sells premium, durability-positioned outdoor and lifestyle products through a single reportable segment, with production outsourced. Revenue disaggregates three ways, and all three matter.

**By category (Q2 FY2026):** Drinkware $241M (+2%) and Coolers & Equipment $232M (+16%). The near-convergence is the most important structural change in the business. Drinkware was historically the dominant category and the market's proxy for the whole company; Coolers & Equipment — hard and soft coolers, the Roadie 8/15 smaller formats, DayTrip bags, Camino totes, the GoBox storage family — is now within roughly $9M of it and growing eight times faster.

**By channel:** DTC $266M (+7%), wholesale $218M (+10%). DTC includes yeti.com, Amazon and other marketplaces, owned retail (Boston and Atlanta opened in Q2), and a corporate/custom business that declined slightly year over year but improved sequentially.

**By geography:** U.S. $391M (+6%), International $93M (+19%), international at roughly 19% of sales. YETI expects to be live in 11 international markets by the end of FY2026 versus 4 a year earlier, with Japan in its first full year direct (500+ wholesale doors, e-commerce recently launched), Europe growing across all four major categories, and Korea, China, Indonesia and Taiwan in the pipeline. Canada underperformed on cautious wholesale inventory purchasing despite healthy sell-through.

The moat is brand pricing power and retail shelf productivity rather than manufacturing cost. Two Q2 data points support it. The CFO attributed 110bps of adjusted gross margin favourability to pricing discipline and product cost management, clarifying under questioning that this was promotional and price-realisation discipline rather than list-price increases — YETI held price in a value-seeking consumer environment. Separately, sell-through has exceeded sell-in for multiple quarters and tracked channel inventory exited Q2 down, which is the cleanest available evidence that demand is pulling product rather than the company pushing it. Management guided that sell-in and sell-through re-balance in H2 rather than continuing to decouple. The 10-Q states no single customer represented more than 10% of gross sales.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (53 weeks ended January 3, 2026) was an earnings recession inside a growth story.** Net sales $1,868.5M, +2.1% on the XBRL FY2024 figure of ~$1,829.9M; gross margin 57.4% (−70bps); operating income $213.6M, down 13%; operating margin 11.4%, down 200bps; net income $165.4M; SG&A 46.0% of sales, up 130bps. Tariffs cost roughly 230bps of gross margin and the transition of production out of China created inventory constraints that management said cost sales. The reported +2.1% also flatters comparable-week growth: FY2025 contained a 53rd week in the seasonally heaviest quarter, so on a 52-week basis growth was likely closer to flat-to-+1%. That cuts both ways — it makes FY2025 weaker, and it makes the FY2026 guide of +7–8% against an inflated base roughly +9% on comparable weeks.

FY2025 operating cash flow was $254.7M with $42.7M of PP&E purchases, giving the company-defined free cash flow of $212.1M, which reconciles exactly to the $212.07M disclosed in the proxy as the FY2025 incentive-compensation performance measure.

**Q2 and H1 FY2026.** The normalisation is set out in the Executive Summary. Below the gross margin line the picture is worse than at it: adjusted SG&A of $220M grew 19% and deleveraged 410bps to 45.4% of sales, driven by the brand campaign timing shift, a higher short-term incentive accrual (which the CFO stated was a year-over-year normalisation unrelated to tariffs), and inflation in distribution and fulfilment. First-half adjusted operating income was $94.9M versus $108.4M, down 12%, with adjusted operating margin of 11.0% versus 13.6%, and adjusted EPS of $0.92 versus $0.97, down 5%.

**Balance sheet transformation.** Cash fell to $59.8M at July 4, 2026 from roughly $270M a year earlier and $188.3M at fiscal year end. Total debt (excluding finance leases and deferred fees) rose to approximately $102M from roughly $76M, with the revolver partially drawn. The net position swung from about +$194M net cash to about −$42M net debt in twelve months. Inventory was $359M, +5%, which management called the healthiest position in some time.

Leverage in absolute terms is modest — roughly $102M of debt against ~$299M of guided adjusted operating income is about 0.34x — so this is a flexibility question rather than a solvency question. The buffer that funded opportunistic repurchases has been spent. YETI repurchased $297.6M in FY2025 at an average of approximately $36.49, and 2.797M shares for $130M in Q2 FY2026 at an average of approximately $46.48. Shares outstanding fell from 74.99M at January 3, 2026 to 72.99M at August 6, 2026, and roughly 14% over two years against ~85M basic shares in FY2024. Approximately $370M remains under the $500M authorisation. Further repurchases now depend on H2 free cash flow arriving as guided.

**Two cash-flow-quality items.** First, YETI's free cash flow definition excludes capitalised intangibles. H1 operating cash flow of $29.8M less $25.5M of PP&E gives the $4.3M the company reported, but the cash flow statement separately shows $14.5M of intangible additions (roughly $21M in FY2025). On an all-in basis, guided FY2026 free cash flow of $200–225M overstates total capitalised-investment-adjusted cash generation by roughly $25–30M, taking the yield from 6.4% to about 5.5%. The definition is disclosed and applied consistently; this is a cross-company comparability adjustment, not a disclosure problem.

Second, supplier finance program obligations rose from $54.0M at January 3 to $90.3M at July 4, a $36.3M increase, while accounts payable rose from $140.2M to $209.8M and the H1 operating cash flow swing improved roughly $49M year over year, substantially on payables and accrued liability movements. A meaningful share of the H1 improvement therefore reflects third-party-financed extension of supplier payment terms rather than operational cash conversion. The obligations sit within accounts payable and are properly disclosed. The alternative explanation — a 5% inventory build into the seasonal peak mechanically raises payables regardless of financing arrangements — is equally consistent with the facts and both are probably operating. The conclusion is narrow: H1 operating cash flow is a lower-quality run-rate input than the headline improvement implies.

On seasonality, H1 free cash flow of $4.3M must not be annualised. FY2025 operating cash flow of $254.7M against H1 FY2025 of −$19.1M implies roughly $274M generated in H2. A thin mid-year cash balance is structurally normal for this business.

**Earnings surprise record.** Four consecutive beats: +2.4%, +2.1%, +43.1% and +20.5%. The two large recent beats were on low absolute bases ($0.18 and $0.56 estimates) and the most recent was substantially refund-driven, so the record supports confidence in consensus for the *reported* line more than in the underlying operating trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance: sales +7–8%, growth relatively consistent across remaining quarters; Coolers & Equipment high-single to low-double-digit; Drinkware mid-single-digit; wholesale high-single to low-double-digit; DTC mid-single-digit; U.S. low- to mid-single-digit; international high-teens to 20%; adjusted gross margin 57.5–58.0% (raised 100bps); operating expense +6–8% (raised); adjusted operating margin ~14.9%; adjusted operating income +10–12%; adjusted EPS $2.94–3.00 including $0.08 of refund benefit; tax ~24%; diluted shares 75.4M (cut from 76.6M); capex $60–70M; free cash flow $200–225M.

Three growth vectors are credible and one is impaired.

**International is the genuine engine.** Going from 4 to 11 live markets in a year, with Japan at 500+ doors, Europe growing across all four categories, and Australia/New Zealand growing despite acknowledged macro weakness, is a distribution build with visible mechanics. At 19% of sales it takes time to move the consolidated rate. Note the gap flagged on the call: international was +14% year to date against a high-teens-to-20% full-year guide, requiring H2 acceleration — one more back-half-weighted assumption.

**Coolers & Equipment at +16%** demonstrates the category diversification thesis working in reported numbers rather than in strategy language. Bags, soft coolers, cases and storage have nearly matched Drinkware in absolute dollars.

**Drinkware is the impaired vector, with a disclosed and bounded — but unverifiable — headwind.** Management disclosed for the first time a roughly 600bp drag on U.S. Drinkware growth in FY2026 from three SKUs, tied to what the CEO called trend-driven momentum and share swapping in the category over recent years. U.S. Drinkware was flat in Q2 while U.S. sell-through grew mid-single digits, so the gap is channel de-stocking of those specific SKUs. Management says the affected SKUs largely complete their lap by year end, resetting the base into 2027, and the CFO confirmed the drag was already embedded in the full-year guide. The alternative reading cannot be excluded from disclosure: if those three SKUs represent structurally lost share in the viral-tumbler category rather than a one-time inventory reset, the promised 2027 base reset does not materialise and the category run rate is permanently lower. The filings do not name or size the SKUs.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest**, not an inflection signal: current year $2.89 (+2.8% over 90 days), next year $3.33 (+2.6%), 17 analysts. Note current-year consensus of $2.89 sits below the guided $2.94–3.00 midpoint, which suggests the revision series has not fully absorbed the August 13 raise and there is mechanical upward drift ahead. That is a mild positive, well short of the magnitude that would justify overriding valuation or quality concerns.

Composing it: mid-to-high-single-digit revenue growth, roughly flat underlying operating margin absent the H2 mechanical items, and a 4–5% annual reduction in share count gives low-double-digit adjusted EPS growth. That is a real algorithm. It is not the double-digit revenue growth the Goldman analyst asked management to reaffirm on the call, and the CEO's answer emphasised "outsized EPS" driven by operations and free cash flow rather than restating a top-line number.

| P/E, FY2027 consensus | 13.7x | $45.47 / $3.33 |
| EV / guided FY2026 adj. operating income | ~11.2x | ~$3.36B EV / ~$299M |
| FCF yield (company definition) | 6.4% | $212.5M / ~$3.32B |
| FCF yield (deducting intangibles) | ~5.5% | ~$183M / ~$3.32B |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | ~20x | FY2025 net income $165.4M |

Enterprise value: 72.99M shares outstanding at $45.47 gives ~$3.32B, plus ~$42M net debt, ~$3.36B. On the guided 75.4M diluted count the equity value is ~$3.43B, consistent with the ~$3.5B in the market data.

The peer set is close to unusable for relative anchoring. Median P/E of 40.3 is set by Callaway at 40.3 and Peloton at 40.6, with Polaris at negative earnings, Thor at 16.1 and Mattel at 10.6 — a dispersion from 10x to 41x with one negative print does not describe a coherent multiple regime. The honest reading is that YETI at 15.3x forward sits between the profitable durable-goods cohort (Thor, Mattel) and the growth/turnaround cohort, and that leisure products as a group is trading on idiosyncratic stories rather than a sector multiple. Barron's characterisation of the post-print level as a steep discount to consumer discretionary is an externally reported opinion we have not independently verified.

Two things argue the multiple is not obviously wrong. First, the earnings base contains non-recurring content: $0.08 of the FY2026 guide is refund benefit, $10.0M of further refunds will release into H2 COGS as capitalised inventory sells, and the H2 comparison is eased by a brand-campaign timing shift. A buyer paying 15.3x is paying that on a number with identifiable non-repeating support. Second, underlying adjusted operating income declined 7% in Q2 and 12% in H1. A 15x multiple on a business whose operating profit is currently shrinking is not self-evidently cheap; it is the price of an unresolved question.

What argues the other way: the price is 15.8% below the August 5 high on a *raised* guide, the analyst mean of $54 implies 18.8% upside with a low of $41 (−9.8%), sentiment has drifted positive (2 strong buy / 8 buy / 6 hold versus 2 / 7 / 7 three months ago), the share count is compounding down 4–5% annually with $370M of authorisation remaining, and there is a dated catalyst on September 17. Absolute yield support at 5.5–6.4% free cash flow with sub-0.4x leverage limits how far the multiple can compress on sentiment alone.

We score valuation 5.9 — improved from 4.8 because the price fell 12% while guidance rose, which is a genuine improvement in risk/reward, but held below 7 because the cheapness is partly compensation for earnings quality and back-half concentration rather than a clean mispricing. A cheap multiple on declining underlying operating profit is exactly the configuration that produces value traps.

## Competitive Landscape

The Drinkware category is normalising after the viral insulated-tumbler cycle, and YETI's disclosure — three SKUs, ~600bps of U.S. Drinkware drag, "share swapping" over the past few years — is a description of that deflation. What matters strategically is that U.S. sell-through grew mid-single digits while U.S. Drinkware sell-in was flat: retailers are working down inventory of specific items, not consumers abandoning the brand. Channel inventory exited Q2 down.

The company's answer is category breadth, and the numbers show it. Coolers & Equipment at +16% has nearly matched Drinkware in dollars. This establishes *product* diversification and reduces dependence on a single trend-exposed family. It does not establish customer diversification — that is a separate measure, and it happens to be independently supported by the 10-Q statement that no customer exceeded 10% of gross sales. Those two facts should be cited for what each shows.

Retailer behaviour is the industry's current tell and it cuts both ways. U.S. wholesale grew 10% with strong sell-in and sell-through and partners expanding commitments across the portfolio. Canada missed because wholesale partners maintained cautious inventory purchasing despite healthy consumer sell-through. The CEO's framing — retailers are selective, input costs are fluid, category competition is dynamic — accurately describes a channel unwilling to carry inventory risk on a discretionary premium brand.

The structural threat is the consumer backdrop management described as uneven with pockets of caution and value seeking, specifically naming constrained discretionary spending in Europe and Australia. For a brand whose model is charging a premium for durability, sustained trading-down is the core risk. The most encouraging counter-evidence in the quarter is that YETI held price rather than promoting, and still grew 9%.

Two acquisitions — Mystery Ranch (technical packs, 2024) and Helimix (shaker bottles, ~$38.0M of FY2025 acquisition payments) — extend the platform. Management frames M&A as acquiring materials, designs, talent and capabilities rather than scale, which is consistent with an outsourced manufacturing model.

Trajectory assessment: the core category is losing ground while the adjacent categories and international are gaining it, with the net effect roughly stable and the composition improving. That supports 5.5 — stabilising after prior decline, not recovering.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — two-sided and unresolved (most material).** Tariffs cost roughly 230bps of FY2025 gross margin. A Supreme Court decision on IEEPA legality triggered the refunds that dominated Q2. FY2026 guidance assumes U.S. rates return to approximately 20% from September. Asked for line of sight, the CFO said the company has no particular insight beyond knowing investigations remain underway with intent to potentially introduce more tariffs, and that the outlook is deliberately conservative. The FY2025 10-K separately flags substitution of IEEPA duties under Section 232, Section 301 or similar authorities. Rates below 20% are unmodeled upside; a new action at a higher rate is unmodeled downside. The ~$69M refund receivable in other current assets carries collection and timing risk the filings do not resolve.

**The H2 margin swing.** Roughly 68% of full-year adjusted operating income and more than 100% of its growth must land in the back half, at a +280bp margin improvement, in a 52-week year lapping a 53-week one with the missing week in Q4, after a half in which margin fell 260bps and with operating expense guidance raised. The sources of the swing are identifiable and largely mechanical, which is why we treat this as execution risk rather than thesis impairment. It is nonetheless unforgiving of any Q4 demand shortfall.

**Drinkware SKU question.** Bounded reset versus permanent share loss cannot be resolved from disclosure. If the latter, the 2027 base reset does not occur.

**Supply chain and input costs.** The call named oil markets, raw material cost pressure, ocean and parcel headwinds, and shipping delays across certain Asia trade lanes, and attributed the operating expense guidance raise primarily to distribution, fulfilment and commodity inflation. These flow into both gross margin and SG&A.

**Governance.** The Chairman and CEO roles are combined. A cooperation agreement with an unidentified stockholder was signed in March 2025 and has since expired, having cost roughly $4.5M in advisory and legal fees across two fiscal years but only ~$74K in Q2 FY2026, and the FY2025 10-K carries a risk factor covering activism, unsolicited takeover proposals and short sellers. The expired standstill leaves the counterparty free to re-engage, which is two-sided — a >$600M buyback and a 14% share-count reduction look like a delivered activist agenda, and the near-zero Q2 legal spend argues the matter is closed rather than re-escalating. The counterparty is not identified in retrievable filings and we do not name one. A new non-GAAP adjustment line, "technology transformation costs" (~$932K in H1), warrants monitoring for scope creep.

**Insider and supply items.** No open-market purchases by officers or directors in the retrieved window; one executive disposition around $45 in December 2025. The CEO adopted a 10b5-1 plan on June 19, 2026 covering 500,750 shares underlying options with sales commencing on or about September 24, 2026. Total company options outstanding at July 4 were approximately 548,000 at a ~$19.75 weighted-average exercise price, so the plan covers roughly 91% of the entire pool — the probable explanation is monetisation of legacy deep-in-the-money grants approaching expiration, and we decline to infer any view of the stock from the plan's existence. It does create a modest supply overhang of roughly 0.7% of shares outstanding, beginning one week after the Investor Day.

**Macro.** Premium discretionary demand faces a consumer that management repeatedly described as uneven with pockets of caution and value seeking, and specifically named constrained discretionary spending in Europe and Australia. Consumer discretionary has been a difficult area for company-specific selection, and a cheap multiple in this sector has frequently reflected demand risk rather than mispricing.

**Short interest.** 14.2% of float short, 8.0 days to cover, 8.35M shares. That is meaningful bearish institutional positioning, and it makes the position two-sided — a clean Q3 print or an Investor Day reframing could force covering, while a Q4 miss has a crowded exit.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.2% sits essentially at one-year realised volatility of 40.1% (spread −0.9%, fair), at only the 18th percentile within the current chain, with balanced put/call skew of −2.0% and put/call open interest of 0.81. The term structure is flat (near 39.2% versus far 39.5%). Implied volatility collapsed from 47.6% to 39.2% while the price fell 9.3% between snapshots — the event premium was crushed by the print rather than repriced upward, which means the options market does not see a near-term catalyst it fears. The three-month tenor prices a ±17.8% move, which straddles the early-November Q3 report, the single event that confirms or breaks the H2 bridge.

The notable divergence is between 14.2% short interest and balanced options positioning. Institutional shorts are expressing the view in stock, not in options, and are not paying up for downside convexity. For a HOLD that is neutral information. It also means downside protection is available at fair-to-cheap prices for holders who want to carry the position through the Q3 print without full exposure to the +280bp requirement failing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($62, +36%).** H2 delivers the +280bp swing on its identifiable mechanics: the brand campaign expense sits in Q2 rather than Q4, the H2 FY2025 comparison absorbed peak tariffs and China-transition inventory constraints, and $10.0M of capitalised refunds releases into COGS. Tariffs stay below the assumed 20%, adding unmodeled upside. FY2026 lands at the $3.00 top of the guide, and FY2027 reaches $3.40–3.45 on mid-to-high-single-digit revenue growth, a Drinkware base that has genuinely reset, international scaling from 11 markets, and continued 4–5% share-count reduction. The September 17 Investor Day reframes the long-term algorithm credibly around EPS and free cash flow compounding, and the market pays 18x. Optionality: 14.2% short interest covering, and an expired standstill in a business generating >$200M of free cash flow with a recognisable brand is a plausible strategic target.

**Bear case ($35, −23%).** The H2 bridge is where the guide fails. A soft Q4 in a value-seeking consumer environment, compounded by the missing 53rd week and raised operating expenses, means the +27% H2 adjusted operating income growth arrives as +10% or less. Tariffs return above 20% or under a substitute authority, removing the refund tailwind and re-adding the 230bp drag. The three Drinkware SKUs prove to be permanently lost share, so the 2027 base reset does not occur and U.S. Drinkware runs flat-to-down structurally. FY2027 EPS lands at $2.60–2.75, the multiple compresses to 13x on a business with declining operating profit, and the buyback slows because cash generation has to service a balance sheet that is now in a net debt position rather than holding $194M of net cash. $35 is roughly 10% above the 52-week low of $31.66.

**Base case ($52, +14%).** H2 delivers most of the bridge but not all of it — margins expand 200–250bps rather than 280 — and FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near the $2.94–2.97 lower half of the guide. FY2027 comes in around consensus $3.30 on +6–7% revenue and continued buyback. The market pays 15.5x, giving ~$52. Probability-weighting roughly 45% base, 25% bull and 30% bear produces an expected return near +8%, which is a positive but unremarkable risk-adjusted return for a discretionary name with a binary quarter in front of it.

**Addressing the trajectory.** This is a fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted from 5.8 to 5.6. The composition matters more than the level. Financial health moves down from 7.2 to 6.2 on a genuine fundamental change — the swing from +$194M net cash to −$42M net debt, cash at $59.8M, supplier finance obligations nearly doubling to $90.3M, and a free cash flow definition that omits ~$25–30M of annual capitalised intangibles. Valuation moves up from 4.8 to 5.9 because the price is 12% lower against raised guidance. Those two moves largely cancel. What would break the tie in either direction is Q3 evidence on the H2 margin bridge, which does not exist yet. Repeating HOLD here is a statement that the decisive evidence is dated and pending, not that nothing changed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 6–9 months, spanning the September 17 Investor Day, the early-November Q3 report, and the Q4/full-year print in late February 2027 where the H2 bridge is fully settled.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 FY2026 adjusted operating margin expansion of at least ~250bps year over year with adjusted operating income growth above +20%, confirming the H2 bridge is tracking rather than being deferred into Q4.
2. Investor Day on September 17 delivering a quantified FY2027–FY2029 margin and free cash flow framework that does not depend on tariff relief, plus explicit reaffirmation or a credible reframing of the long-term growth algorithm.
3. Tariff clarity settling materially below the 20% September assumption, or collection of the ~$69M receivable confirmed with no clawback exposure — either would be unmodeled upside to a guide management has described as deliberately conservative.
4. A price near $38–40, which would put the stock at roughly 11.5–12x FY2027 consensus with a 7%+ free cash flow yield on the company definition. At that level the base-case upside to $52 would exceed 30% and we would upgrade on valuation alone.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 adjusted operating income down year over year, or any reduction to the +10–12% FY2026 adjusted operating income growth guide. That would mean the entire year's growth is deferred into a single quarter and would invalidate the bridge.
2. U.S. Drinkware declining with sell-through decelerating to flat or negative — the specific evidence that would convert the three-SKU drag from a bounded inventory reset into structural share loss.
3. Supplier finance obligations expanding beyond ~$110M alongside flat or negative operating cash conversion, which would indicate the cash flow improvement is financing rather than operating.
4. New tariff action above 20% under Section 232, Section 301 or a substitute authority, restoring the FY2025 230bp gross margin drag without an offsetting refund.

**Reassessment timeline:** September 17 (Investor Day) for the long-term framework, then the Q3 report in early November for the first hard read on the H2 margin swing. We would not add new capital ahead of the Q3 print given that the three-month tenor prices a ±17.8% move and the outcome hinges on a variable — Q4 discretionary demand — that neither management nor we can currently observe.

## Conclusion

YETI is a durable brand with demonstrated pricing power, a genuinely diversifying category mix, an early and credible international build, no customer above 10% of gross sales, sub-0.4x leverage, and a share count compounding down 4–5% a year — trading at 15.3x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 13.7x FY2027 consensus and a 5.5–6.4% free cash flow yield. That combination is not expensive.

It also just reported a quarter in which underlying adjusted operating income fell 7%, a half in which it fell 12%, and a guide requiring roughly 68% of the year's adjusted operating income and more than all of its growth to arrive in the back half on a +280bp margin swing, against a tariff assumption management says it cannot verify, in a 52-week year lapping a 53-week one, with operating expense guidance moving up. The 12% selloff on a beat-and-raise was the market correctly separating a $42.6M prior-period tariff refund and a 9.2% share-count reduction from operating improvement.

Valuation improved and earnings quality deteriorated by roughly equal amounts.
