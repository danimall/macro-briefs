**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Darden is the largest full-service restaurant operator in the U.S., running 2,196 company-owned restaurants across 11 brands anchored by Olive Garden (944 units) and LongHorn Steakhouse (608 units). The investment question today is whether a recent acceleration in same-restaurant sales (SRS) can offset accelerating beef and commodity inflation that is compressing margins on the same brands driving the growth. Both forces are visible in the same quarter, which is why this is a balanced hold rather than a clean call in either direction.

The bull data point: blended SRS accelerated from +2.0% in FY25 to +4.2% in Q3 FY26, with LongHorn posting +7.2% SRS on both check (+3.9%) and traffic (+3.3%). Positive traffic growth in casual dining is unusual and suggests genuine share gains, not just pricing. The offsetting data point: Q3 operating income fell roughly 2.8% YoY and continuing-ops EPS slipped to $2.68 from $2.74, as food and beverage costs rose to 30.7% of sales on beef inflation and a Bahama Breeze impairment hit the quarter. Nine-month EPS is still up 9.7%, so the full-year story remains one of growth, but the margin trajectory is the constraint on the multiple.

At $211 (down ~6% from the prior report and ~7% below the 52-week high), DRI trades at ~22.3x trailing and ~19x next-year consensus EPS of $11.38, with a 2.9% dividend yield. That is a fair price for a high-quality, cash-generative operator, but not an asymmetric one. The company has missed consensus EPS in four straight quarters (all by small margins), estimate revisions are flat, and the stock sits in the Consumer Discretionary group most exposed to the H2 consumer-spending cliff we see building. We rate DRI a HOLD (growth_hold), with an upgrade contingent on either evidence that pricing power holds without surrendering traffic, or a pullback toward the low-$180s.

## Company Overview

Darden operates an asset-heavy, mostly-leased, company-owned restaurant model spanning four reportable segments:

- **Olive Garden** (944 units) — flagship casual Italian, the volume and profit anchor (~22% segment margin), historically growing via check rather than discounting.
- **LongHorn Steakhouse** (608 units) — casual steak, the clear growth standout with both traffic and check rising.
- **Fine Dining** — The Capital Grille, Ruth's Chris (acquired FY24 for ~$701M), Eddie V's; the most economically sensitive special-occasion business.
- **Other Business** — Cheddar's, Yard House, Chuy's (acquired Oct 2024 for $649M), Seasons 52, Bahama Breeze (being wound down).

Darden's competitive edge rests on scale-driven purchasing and a self-operated distribution network, a diversified multi-brand model that spreads concept risk, and a deliberate value-perception strategy. The company is rated investment-grade (BBB / Baa2 / BBB) and explicitly manages to maintain that rating. Revenue has compounded from $9.63B (FY22) to $12.08B (FY25, +6.0%), a mix of low-single-digit SRS, unit growth, and M&A.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY25 delivered $12.08B revenue (+6.0%), operating income of $1.36B (11.3% margin), net earnings from continuing operations of $1.05B, and diluted continuing-ops EPS of $8.88 (+4.1%). Operating cash flow was $1.71B against $645M capex, producing roughly $1.06B of free cash flow (8.8% FCF margin). This is a genuinely cash-generative business.

**Q3 FY26 (quarter ended Feb 22, 2026).** Sales rose 5.9% to $3.35B. The segment detail is the story: LongHorn +7.2% (check +3.9%, traffic +3.3%), Olive Garden +3.2%, Fine Dining +2.1%, Other Business +3.9%. Operating income declined ~2.8% YoY, pressured by F&B inflation of +1.5% in the quarter and a $25.1M Bahama Breeze impairment. LongHorn's segment margin compressed 110 bps as steak costs bit into the brand winning the most traffic.

**Guidance.** FY26 guidance, raised through the year, calls for ~9.5% total sales growth (including ~2% from a 53rd week), ~4.5% SRS, ~70 new units, and $750–775M capex.

**Balance sheet.** The headline screens flag distress (Altman Z-Score 1.58, current ratio 0.42, reported D/E 4.45x), but these are artifacts of the restaurant model, which intentionally runs negative working capital: Darden collects cash immediately, floats vendor payables, and carries a $689M gift-card deferred liability. The covenant-relevant metric, adjusted debt/EBITDAR, was 2.1x at FY25 year-end against a 3.50x covenant, with no revolver borrowings. The Piotroski score (4/5: positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF > net income) confirms underlying financial strength. This is a healthy business with appropriate leverage for its model, not a distressed one.

**Earnings quality watch.** Darden has missed consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (Q3 FY26 −0.8%, Q2 −2.0%, Q1 −2.6%, FY25 Q4 −0.7%). All small, but a streak of misses after a long beat history signals that consensus is running slightly ahead of delivery, which is relevant when the multiple already prices in execution. Shareholder returns are being funded increasingly at the margin with leverage: $290M of commercial paper was outstanding in Q3, and the retained-earnings line has gone to a ~$217M deficit as combined dividends and buybacks ($658M + $418M in FY25) modestly exceed reported net income. This is sustainable at current leverage and FCF, but it caps the room for aggressive future M&A or buybacks without ratings pressure.

## Growth Analysis

The core-brand acceleration is recent, and LongHorn's simultaneous traffic-plus-check growth is the strongest competitive result in the portfolio. This underpins the raised ~4.5% SRS guidance and the stock's outperformance over the trailing six months.

Beyond comps, growth comes from ~70 new units annually, the Chuy's contribution (entering the SRS comp base in Q4 FY26), and off-premise/catering initiatives. The Uber Direct rollout, where Darden controls the order and Uber provides last-mile logistics, extends convenience without ceding the customer relationship, at a modest margin cost (~0.1–0.2% of restaurant expense). OBBBA's restoration of 100% bonus depreciation and R&D expensing, combined with FICA tip credits, keeps the effective tax rate around 12–13%, a structural earnings support.

The constraint is the consumer backdrop. Fine Dining traffic remains soft (FY25 guest counts −5.2%; Q3 FY26 −0.1%), reflecting pressure on the higher-end consumer. Olive Garden has grown historically through check rather than traffic, raising the open question of how much pricing headroom remains before traffic erodes. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year EPS $10.62, +0.5% over 90 days; next-year $11.38, essentially unchanged); the consensus is not extrapolating acceleration, which is appropriate given the margin pressure. We do not see the high-velocity estimate-revision pattern or the depressed-trough setup that would signal a high-conviction inflection buy; this is steady mid-single-digit growth without a re-rating catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

DRI trades at ~22.3x trailing EPS of $8.88 and ~19x next-year consensus of $11.38, with a 2.9% dividend yield. Against the restaurant peer set, this sits mid-pack: Texas Roadhouse 26.8x, Chipotle 28.6x, Domino's 18.0x, with high-growth names (CAVA 169.7x, Dutch Bros 102.6x) skewing the median to 28.6x. On growth-adjusted terms, DRI's ~19x forward against mid-single-digit SRS and high-single-digit EPS growth is reasonable but not cheap; the PEG is roughly 2x, which does not screen as a mispricing.

The dividend yield of 2.9% (raised to $6.00 annualized) provides absolute support and is well-covered by $1B+ FCF, but it is not high enough to anchor an income thesis on its own. Analyst targets bracket the range widely: low $156, median $230, high $276, mean $226, implying ~7% upside to consensus median from $211. We would not treat the median as a floor here; DRI is a fairly-valued operator where targets and price both reflect the same mid-cycle assumptions, not an inflection stock at a compressed multiple where targets lag reality.

The honest read is that DRI is priced for what it is delivering. There is no identified catalyst for re-rating, the margin trajectory argues against multiple expansion in the near term, and the four-quarter miss streak suggests modest downside risk to estimates. We assign a neutral valuation score: fairly valued, not a value trap, not asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Darden competes in intensely competitive full-service dining against national chains, regional players, independents, and increasingly grocery prepared-foods and delivery aggregators. Its durable edge is scale plus value perception plus brand diversification. The current cycle shows a bifurcated consumer: value-oriented casual brands (LongHorn, Olive Garden) are gaining traffic while special-occasion Fine Dining sees soft guest counts.

LongHorn's simultaneous check-and-traffic growth is the clearest evidence of share gains in casual steak, a competitive result that is hard to manufacture and points to genuine brand strength. Disciplined portfolio management reinforces the picture: the Olive Garden Canada sale to a royalty/franchise model (July 2025) and the Bahama Breeze wind-down (~14 closures, ~14 conversions, $22.4M impairment) show management culling weak assets and shifting toward capital-light structures where appropriate. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the casual brands, offset by structural softness at the high end. We trim the competitive score modestly from prior reports given the margin compression now affecting LongHorn, the very brand carrying the growth; winning traffic at the cost of margin is a weaker competitive outcome than winning both.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Beef and commodity inflation** — the live issue. F&B inflation drove LongHorn margin compression and a YoY decline in operating income despite strong sales. Beef costs are cyclically elevated and largely outside Darden's control, and the 10-K flags tariff/trade-policy risk on imported produce and seafood as an added FY25–26 cost. The central forward question is whether Darden can hold pricing without surrendering the traffic it just won.

2. **Consumer discretionary sensitivity** — full-service dining is highly cyclical, and the macro backdrop points to a building H2 consumer-spending cliff. Fine Dining traffic is already negative. DRI sits in the sector showing the weakest risk-adjusted returns in our framework.

3. **Pricing-power exhaustion** — Olive Garden's check-led growth model has finite headroom before price increases erode traffic.

4. **Chuy's integration** — $649M acquisition with $267M goodwill, synergies unproven at scale; the brand doesn't enter the SRS comp base until Q4 FY26.

5. **Goodwill/trademark impairment** — $1.66B goodwill and $1.35B trademarks could face non-cash charges in a demand shock; the Bahama Breeze impairment is a recent reminder.

6. **Lease-heavy model** — ~$5.7B total lease liabilities, with closures carrying ongoing obligations.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, event-driven stress. Near-term ATM IV spiked to ~85% (1-week) against 25.3% one-year historical volatility, with the term structure in steep backwardation (85.5% near-term vs ~34.8% six-month), the classic signature of an upcoming binary, here the Q4 FY26 earnings release scheduled June 25. ATM IV jumped 62 percentage points since the prior snapshot. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.17, so the elevated vol reflects expected earnings movement rather than a directional bearish lean. The ±5.6% implied move into the print is consistent with the company's recent pattern of small misses driving modest moves. Short interest at 6.6% of float (5.7 days to cover) is uncrowded and not a squeeze setup. The market expects a meaningful earnings reaction but is not positioned for a directional collapse, consistent with our balanced view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The SRS acceleration is durable. LongHorn continues to gain traffic-led share, Olive Garden holds mid-single-digit comps, and beef inflation normalizes through FY27, allowing the margin compression to reverse and operating leverage to reassert. Chuy's enters the comp base accretively, the capital-light portfolio pruning lifts blended margins, and the 12–13% tax rate plus $1B+ FCF funds a growing dividend and buyback. The stock re-rates toward the high end of the peer range. Bull case ~$255 (~22x next-year EPS of ~$11.50 on margin recovery + continued comps).

**Bear Case.** The H2 consumer cliff materializes, casual-dining traffic rolls over, and Olive Garden's pricing model hits its ceiling. Beef inflation persists, margins keep compressing, and the four-quarter miss streak extends into outright estimate cuts. Fine Dining weakness deepens. The multiple compresses toward Domino's-like 17–18x on falling estimates. Bear case ~$170 (~16x a flat-to-lower ~$10.60 EPS), roughly −20% from current.

**Base Case.** Mid-single-digit SRS holds, beef inflation moderates gradually rather than reversing sharply, and EPS lands near the $11.38 next-year consensus. The stock tracks earnings growth at a roughly stable ~19x, with the dividend providing total-return support. Base case ~$225 (~20x $11.38), ~7% upside plus ~3% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is real but the risk/reward is symmetric at the current price, and the margin trajectory plus consumer backdrop prevent a higher call.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY26 earnings on June 25, 2026 is the immediate checkpoint; the options market is pricing a ±5.6% move into it. The Chuy's comp-base entry (Q4 FY26) and FY27 guidance are the next material inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that pricing power is holding without traffic erosion — i.e., blended SRS sustains ~4%+ while operating margin stabilizes or expands YoY (reversing the Q3 decline).
- A pullback toward the low-$180s (~17x next-year EPS), where the dividend yield approaches 3.3% and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- Beef/commodity inflation rolling over decisively, restoring LongHorn segment margin.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Blended SRS decelerating below ~2% YoY, signaling the consumer cliff has reached full-service dining.
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss accompanied by downward estimate revisions (current-year consensus falling below ~$10.40).
- A guidance cut on FY27 SRS or margin, or a dividend coverage scare from leverage funding payouts.

## Conclusion

Darden is a high-quality, cash-generative, investment-grade operator with a recent SRS acceleration led by LongHorn's rare traffic-plus-check growth. That positive is offset by beef inflation compressing margins on the same brands carrying the growth, a four-quarter EPS-miss streak, flat estimate revisions, and a Consumer Discretionary sector facing a building H2 demand cliff. At ~19x next-year EPS with a 2.9% yield, the stock is fairly priced for what it delivers, with neither a clear re-rating catalyst nor a value-trap discount. We rate DRI a HOLD and would look to upgrade on either margin stabilization alongside sustained comps or a pullback toward the low-$180s that restores asymmetry.
