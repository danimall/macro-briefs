## Executive Summary

Verisign operates a regulated monopoly on .com and .net domain name registry services, collecting a wholesale fee on every registration and renewal of the ~173.5 million domains in its base. The business generates 68% operating margins, 64% FCF margins, and requires minimal capital expenditure — characteristics that rank among the best of any publicly traded company. The .com Registry Agreement was renewed through November 2030, and the DOC Cooperative Agreement auto-renewed simultaneously, securing the franchise for the foreseeable future.

Three specific factors prevent a BUY at $274: (1) Verisign has missed EPS consensus in all four reported quarters of FY2025, with the miss widening from -2.3% in Q1 to -4.3% in Q4, indicating deteriorating execution relative to expectations; (2) FY2026 consensus estimates have been revised downward by 2.3% over 90 days, and the next structural pricing catalyst (.com's 7% annual increase authorization) does not begin until approximately 2027; and (3) the stock trades at 31x trailing and 29.5x FY2026E earnings for a business guiding to 3.5-4.7% revenue growth, a premium valuation for the growth delivered. The thesis converts to BUY when price increases begin, earnings momentum inflects, or the stock pulls back to the low $240s.

## Company Overview

Verisign is the exclusive registry operator for .com and .net top-level domains under agreements with ICANN and the U.S. Department of Commerce. The company charges registrars (GoDaddy, Namecheap, etc.) a per-domain wholesale fee — currently $10.26 for .com and $10.91 for .net — for every registration and renewal. With 928 employees generating $1.66B in annual revenue, Verisign produces roughly $1.79M in revenue per employee.

The company also operates two of thirteen global internet root servers and performs the Root Zone Maintainer function. It has maintained 28 consecutive years of 100% DNS uptime, an operational record that underpins its franchise value. The domain base stood at 173.5 million registrations at year-end 2025, with new registrations of 41.7 million (the highest since 2021) and renewal rates improving to 75.4%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue of $1.657B grew 6.4% YoY, accelerating from 4.3% in FY2024, driven by both the September 2024 price increase (to $10.26 for .com) and a 2.6% rebound in the domain base after a 2% decline in 2024. Q4 2025 revenue of $425M represented 7.5% YoY growth. FY2026 guidance of $1.715-1.735B implies a deceleration to approximately 3.5-4.7% growth as the price increase annualizes and domain base growth moderates to 1.5-3.5%.

**Profitability:** Operating margins have been stable at 66-68% for four consecutive years. Cost of revenue as a percentage of sales has declined from 14.1% (FY2022) to 11.8% (FY2025), demonstrating operating leverage on a largely fixed-cost infrastructure. Net margins of ~50% reflect a normalized tax rate of ~22.7%. FY2025 diluted EPS of $8.81 grew 10.1%, benefiting from margin expansion, revenue growth, and ongoing share retirement.

**Cash flow quality:** Operating cash flow surged 21% to $1.091B in FY2025, producing a free cash flow margin of approximately 64.5% after just $22.8M in capex. The FCF jump was boosted by the restoration of immediate R&D deductions under recent tax legislation. FY2026 capex guidance of $55-65M (roughly 3x the FY2025 level) will modestly reduce FCF margins, reflecting end-of-life equipment replacement and AI-driven hardware cost inflation.

**Balance sheet:** Stockholders' equity is deeply negative at -$2.15B, the accumulated result of $11.8B+ in lifetime share repurchases. Total debt of $1.79B is manageable against $1.1B in annual operating cash flow (1.6x leverage). The Altman Z-Score of 3.42 sits in the safe zone because EBIT/assets of 84.5% overwhelms the negative equity. The current ratio of 0.49x appears alarming but reflects $1.33B in current liabilities dominated by deferred revenue, a normal characteristic of subscription-like businesses. There is no liquidity risk.

**Capital allocation:** FY2025 returned $1.07B to shareholders through $858.6M in repurchases (3.4M shares at an average of $252.42) and $215.2M in dividends. The share count has declined 13% over three years, from ~108M to ~93.8M diluted shares. The dividend, initiated in April 2025, now stands at $3.24 annualized (~1.2% yield) and was recently increased 5.2%.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the principal constraint on valuation. Revenue has compounded at approximately 5% annually over the past four years, driven by periodic price increases and modest domain base expansion. The growth algorithm is transparent:

- **Volume:** Domain base growth of 1.5-3.5% annually (guided for FY2026). The rebound in 2025 was encouraging, but global domain growth is a mature market. AI-driven demand for web presence is a modest tailwind; social media substitution and alternative platforms are a modest headwind. Net effect is low-single-digit volume growth.

- **Price:** The key structural catalyst. Under the renewed .com agreement, Verisign may increase prices up to 7% per year during years three through six of each six-year period. The current period began October 2024. Management indicated on the Q4 call that an earlier pricing announcement could come in April 2026 for an October 2026 effective date, utilizing remaining capacity from the prior pricing period. The next full round of permitted 7% annual increases begins approximately 2027 and runs through 2030.

- **New services:** CEO Bidzos signaled on the Q4 call that the company has "strong candidates for new services" leveraging DNS and security technology. This is the first concrete indication of potential new revenue streams in years. Details are expected in coming months. This optionality is not reflected in current estimates but could matter at the margin over 2-3 years.

- **EPS kicker:** Continued share repurchases add 3-4% to annual EPS growth on top of revenue growth.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026E EPS of $9.31, FY2027E of $10.09. The 90-day revision trend is negative: FY2026E cut 2.3%, FY2027E cut 0.4%. The FY2025 pattern of four consecutive quarterly misses (widening from -2.3% to -4.3%) is a meaningful negative signal. Either the cost structure is deteriorating slightly, the tax rate is less favorable than analysts model, or there are operational headwinds not fully captured in consensus. Until this pattern reverses, there is reason to discount forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $274, VRSN trades at:
- 31.1x trailing EPS ($8.81)
- 29.4x FY2026E EPS ($9.31)
- 27.2x FY2027E EPS ($10.09)
- ~23.4x FY2025 FCF ($1.068B estimated, on 91.1M shares)

Analyst consensus targets range from $250 (low) to $305 (high), with a median of $284. The current price sits 3.6% below the median target — limited upside per the Street.

The valuation is defensible for a regulated monopoly with 64% FCF margins and predictable growth, but it is not cheap. The stock peaked near $310 in July 2025 before falling 30% to $209 in February 2026, then recovering to $274 — a round trip driven primarily by the Q4 earnings miss and broader market conditions. The current multiple embeds the expectation of resumed price increases and domain base stability. If growth disappoints (FY2026 below the low end of guidance) or if the EPS miss pattern continues, the stock would re-test the $240-250 range.

Applying 29-30x to FY2026E EPS of $9.31 produces a fair value range of $270-280, approximately where the stock trades today. For the stock to reach $305+ (the bull case), the market needs to see the pricing cycle begin and earnings momentum inflect positively.

## Competitive Landscape

Verisign's competitive position is extraordinary and essentially without parallel. The .com registry is a natural monopoly reinforced by contractual exclusivity, network effects (every browser, email system, and DNS resolver in the world interacts with Verisign's infrastructure), and 28 years of operational credibility. There is no realistic scenario in which ICANN transfers the .com registry to another operator absent a catastrophic failure.

Competitive threats exist at the margins and over long timeframes. Alternative TLDs (.io, .xyz, country-code domains, new gTLDs) have not materially eroded .com's dominance despite 1,200+ new options available since 2014. Blockchain-based namespaces remain experimental with negligible browser support. Social media profiles and e-commerce platform storefronts substitute for standalone websites in some segments but coexist with domain growth. AI-mediated internet navigation could reduce the importance of typing domain names directly, but AI agents and crawlers still require DNS resolution to access content.

The franchise is regulated rather than unregulated, which caps pricing power but also provides extraordinary visibility. The 7% annual pricing cap over years 3-6 of each period is well above inflation in most environments, and the contractual framework is transparent.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings momentum deterioration:** The widening EPS miss pattern is the most actionable near-term risk. If FY2026 continues this trend, estimate revisions will accelerate downward and the multiple could compress. Elevated FY2026 capex guidance ($55-65M vs. $23M) and cost pressures from AI-driven hardware demand are likely contributors.

**Pricing cycle gap:** The 7% annual increases are permitted only in years 3-6 of the current period (approximately 2027-2030). Management flagged a potential April announcement for an October 2026 effective date using residual capacity from the prior period, but the full pricing cycle has not yet commenced. Revenue growth in the interim depends primarily on domain base expansion, guided at 1.5-3.5%.

**AI disruption:** The 10-K explicitly flags AI as a transformational risk to domain name demand. If AI fundamentally changes web navigation patterns, domain registrations could structurally decline. Management's counter-narrative — that AI agents still use DNS and drive higher query volumes — is plausible for now. This is a 5-10 year risk, not a 1-2 year risk.

**ICANN/DOC agreement risk:** While renewal carries a "presumptive" right, the agreements are not perpetual. Any political or regulatory pressure to alter the pricing framework or transfer the registry would be existential. The probability is very low but the impact would be total.

**Key person risk:** CEO Bidzos (age 70) has led the company for over a decade and has been associated with Verisign for nearly three decades. There is no publicly identified successor. Six Item 5.02 (officer/director change) 8-K filings in 2025, including the CFO transition, suggest organizational reshuffling is underway.

**5-year TSR underperformance:** Verisign's 5-year TSR of approximately 113 (indexed) vs. the peer group's 258 illustrates the market's concern about growth limitations. The franchise is strong, but the stock's return profile has materially lagged peers over multi-year periods.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($305-310, +11-13%):** The pricing cycle commences earlier than expected (October 2026 effective date), a new services announcement provides a credible second revenue stream, and domain base growth sustains at the high end of guidance (3.5%). EPS achieves $9.50+ in FY2026 (above consensus), and the market re-rates to 32-33x forward as growth visibility improves. Share buybacks accelerate at lower prices. The compounding effect of 7% annual .com price increases through 2030 creates a path to $12+ EPS by FY2029.

**Bear Case ($225-240, -12-18%):** EPS misses continue through FY2026, confirming cost pressures and potentially softer domain demand. AI disruption fears intensify, compressing the multiple. No pricing action in 2026 pushes the revenue growth catalyst further out. Domain base growth comes in at or below the low end of guidance (1.5%). At 25-26x trough earnings, the stock revisits the February lows.

**Base Case ($280-290, +2-6%):** Revenue growth of ~4%, EPS of approximately $9.20-9.40, with the market maintaining a 30-31x multiple as the pricing cycle approaches. The stock consolidates in the $270-290 range until the next pricing catalyst crystallizes. Dividend provides modest income support at 1.2% yield.

2. ICANN or DOC signals material changes to pricing framework or agreement terms
3. FY2026 EPS tracks below $9.00 (more than 3% below current consensus)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q1 FY2026 earnings (expected early May 2026). A pricing announcement could come as early as April 2026 based on management commentary. Both events could shift the thesis materially.

## Conclusion

Verisign is a regulated internet monopoly generating 64% free cash flow margins with 28 years of 100% uptime and a contractual pricing escalator built into its agreements. The long-term compounding math is attractive: 3-4% volume growth + 7% annual price increases (when active) + 3-4% share count reduction can produce low-teens EPS growth through the 2027-2030 pricing window.

The specific problem at $274 is timing and execution. Four consecutive EPS misses, downward estimate revisions, a pricing gap until 2027 (or optimistically late 2026), and a 31x trailing multiple leave insufficient margin of safety for a BUY. The stock is fairly valued in the base case and would require either a pullback to the low $240s or a positive inflection in earnings momentum to warrant upgrading. HOLD with a growth thesis: the franchise is intact, the pricing cycle is approaching, and the stock should be accumulated on weakness rather than chased here.
