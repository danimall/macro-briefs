## Executive Summary

Hasbro has completed a transformation that its own filings now describe more candidly than most sell-side notes do. In the first half of 2026, Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming produced $567.7M of segment operating profit against $522.8M of consolidated operating profit — more than 100% of the company's earnings — because Consumer Products lost $62.0M. In FY2025 the same asymmetry was starker: WotC earned $1,006.8M while the consolidated line was $11.1M after a $1,021.9M non-cash goodwill impairment on the toy business. Underwriting Hasbro means underwriting the durability of MAGIC: The Gathering and treating the toy business as an option on a turnaround rather than a source of earnings.

The operating results are genuinely good. Q2 2026 revenue rose 16.2% to $1,139.6M, H1 revenue rose 14.6%, MAGIC revenue grew 33.8% in the half, and management raised FY2026 guidance to 5-7% constant-currency revenue growth, 25-26% adjusted operating margin, and $1.45-1.50B of adjusted EBITDA. The company has beaten consensus four quarters running. Consensus FY2026 EPS has risen 3.4% over 30 days (and 4.8% over 90 days) to $6.16, and the balance sheet is in better shape than a screen would suggest: the November 2026 maturity is pre-funded with Treasuries, the revolver was extended to February 2031 at $1.1B undrawn, all $3.56B of debt is fixed-rate, and net debt to guided adjusted EBITDA is roughly 1.5x against a 3.75x covenant.

The price already reflects most of this, and the composition of forward growth is weaker than the revenue line. Adjusted EPS in Q2 2026 was $1.28, *down* 2% year over year on a 16.2% revenue gain, because the incremental MAGIC dollar carries a third-party royalty, digital spend peaks in 2026, and $20M of incremental H2 marketing plus $50-75M of 2027 Wizards marketing is being pushed through the P&L. Management has guided WotC's FY2026 operating margin to the "low-40s" from 46.0% and Q4 MAGIC revenue to decline low single digits. At 15.6x FY2026 and 14.7x FY2027 consensus, with ~6% consensus EPS growth into 2027, the multiple is fair for a business with this much single-franchise concentration and an unresolved securities class action aimed at exactly the MAGIC growth narrative. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name: earlier holds reflected an unproven inflection, this one reflects a proven inflection at a fuller price.

## Company Overview

Three reported segments, with wildly different economics:

**Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming** — FY2025 revenue $2,186.9M (+44.7%), operating profit $1,006.8M (46.0% margin). Tabletop Gaming $1,686.6M (+62.2%) and Digital & Licensed Gaming $500.3M (+6.1%). MAGIC revenue grew $641.5M year over year in FY2025 and $256.6M (+33.8%) in H1 2026, driven by "Universes Beyond" sets built on licensed IP — Final Fantasy, Marvel's Spider-Man, Avatar, Edges of Eternities, plus in-house sets Lorwyn Eclipsed and Secrets of Strixhaven. Hasbro does not disclose MAGIC's absolute revenue; backing into it from Tabletop totals and disclosed deltas suggests roughly $1.4-1.6B, which is an inference, not a disclosed figure.

**Consumer Products** — FY2025 revenue $2,437.6M (-4.2%), operating loss $942.6M including the $1,021.9M impairment. Excluding it, operating profit was approximately $79.3M, a 3.3% margin, down from 4.5% in FY2024. Q2 2026 revenue $463.0M (+4.7%) with a $14.5M GAAP operating loss. Guided to 6-8% FY2026 operating margin.

**Entertainment** — $76.8M FY2025 revenue, a rounding item after the December 2023 sale of eOne's film and TV business to Lionsgate.

Hasbro also earns pure royalties. MONOPOLY GO!, developed and operated by Scopely under license from Hasbro, contributed $168.0M of Hasbro revenue in FY2025 and $85.7M in H1 2026. Management's "$8 billion lifetime revenue" reference is the game's gross consumer revenue on Scopely's platform, not Hasbro's take.

### Revenue and margin

FY2025's +13.7% is the first growth year since 2021 and is almost entirely WotC (+$675.6M) against CP (-$106.3M). On a trailing-twelve-month basis through June 28, 2026 — FY2025 less H1 2025 plus H1 2026 — revenue was $4,973.2M, operating profit $1,161.4M (23.4% margin), and net income attributable $794.1M, or roughly $5.55 of diluted EPS. That TTM figure lapses the goodwill impairment (Q2 2025) while retaining the $56.4M digital-games write-off (Q2 2026), which reconciles with the reported 17.2x trailing multiple.

The margin composition deserves attention. FY2025 cost of sales improved to 27.6% of revenue from 28.5% and SD&A improved to 25.0% from 29.3%. Working the other way, **royalties rose to 7.8% from 6.9%** and **product development rose to 8.2% from 7.1%**. The 10-K attributes the royalty increase directly to Universes Beyond sets "for which the Company is obligated to pay a royalty," and management guided a further step-up in the back half of 2026. Q2 2026 cost of sales rose to 23.9% from 23.0% and SD&A to 29.7% from 28.8%. Neither of those pressures is one-time.

### Cash flow — the number to get right

FY2025 operating cash flow was $893.2M. A conventional screen reports FY2025 free cash flow near $830M by subtracting only $63.3M of PP&E. That omits $135.0M of capitalized software development disclosed in the investing section, which is recurring investment in the digital gaming business. Recomputed: $893.2M − $63.3M − $135.0M = **$694.9M**, a 5.1% FCF yield on $13.56B. Dividends paid were $392.5M, so coverage on the stricter definition was 1.77x (a 56% payout).

H1 2026 operating cash flow of $604.4M against $209.4M looks spectacular and should not be annualized. Roughly half is a $306.6M accounts-receivable inflow as the record December 2025 receivable balance ($1,059.8M) collected down to $751.7M, plus the absence of a $57.4M deemed-repatriation payment. Hasbro books approximately 60% of revenue in H2, which consumes working capital. FY2026 cash capex is guided to ~$250M versus $198.3M of combined PP&E and software spend in FY2025. A reasonable FY2026 FCF range, taking the $1.475B adjusted EBITDA midpoint less ~$165M cash interest, ~$250M capex and roughly 23% cash taxes, is $700-850M before working capital, restructuring outflows ($15.4M paid in H1 alone) and any tariff refund. Against dividends of ~$396M and a buyback target raised to at least $200M, that is covered but not lavishly.

### Balance sheet

At June 28, 2026: cash $880.5M, short-term investments $497.7M, total debt principal $3,556.9M, shareholders' equity $730.3M. Net debt $2,178.7M — approximately 1.5x guided adjusted EBITDA against covenants of 3.75x net leverage (4.00x in Q3) and 3.00x minimum interest coverage.

The debt stack is entirely fixed-rate, spanning 3.50% 2027 notes to 6.60% 2028 debentures, with $400M of new 4.65% 2031 notes issued in March 2026. A fully fixed stack with the nearest maturity ($497.0M, November 2026) already pre-funded in Treasuries is a real advantage relative to leveraged consumer peers rolling floating debt.

Book equity of $730.3M against $3.56B of debt produces an alarming ratio, and a mechanical Altman Z built on book-equity substitutions lands in the "distress zone." That reading is a measurement artifact: goodwill has fallen from $3,470.1M (FY2022) to $1,256.2M (June 2026), roughly $2.2B of write-offs that reduce book equity without changing cash obligations. Rebuilding the standard model with market equity and retained earnings gives Z ≈ 3.0, or ≈ 3.6 adding back the non-cash impairment to EBIT. Corroborating behavior: a bank syndicate extended a $1.1B revolver to 2031 in February 2026, Hasbro issued unsecured paper at 4.65% in March, repurchased $125.0M of long-dated notes discretionarily in H1, and authorized $1.0B of buyback. The genuine observations are thin book equity and the fact that FY2025 GAAP interest coverage really was 0.07x; excluding the impairment it was 6.3x, and H1 2026 was 5.9x ($522.8M / $88.3M).

Two balance-sheet items warrant monitoring. The accounts-receivable credit-loss allowance opened 2025 at $25.8M and opened 2026 at $61.3M, standing at $57.9M in June — a more-than-doubling with no named counterparty or region. It may simply scale with the record Q4 2025 receivable and greater hobby-channel exposure, or reflect Latin America and Asia Pacific collectability given those regions' double-digit declines. It is an unexplained anomaly, not evidence of a retailer failure. Separately, $383.4M of unamortized capitalized software remains after the $56.4M write-off, subject to the same recoverability test.

The peer-median comparison is the weakest argument in the bull case. The Leisure Products median of 25.5x is set by Acushnet and Yeti, which are single-category consumer brands with different economics; Brunswick's P/E is negative and uninformative. The relevant comparables are Mattel at 10.6x and Thor at 16.1x. A high-quality games franchise arguably deserves better than a toy multiple; that is the re-rating argument, and it is partially in the price already given Mattel trades meaningfully cheaper.

A sum-of-parts cross-check: WotC at a $1.0B FY2026 operating profit run-rate on a 14-16x EBIT multiple is $14.0-16.0B; CP at $175M on 8x is $1.4B; Entertainment at $39M on 10x is $0.4B. On growth-adjusted terms: 15.6x on the ~6% consensus growth into FY2027 is a PEG of about 2.5. Cheapness on a headline P/E is not, on its own, a reason to buy a business whose earnings depend on one franchise the company itself has warned may face "franchise fatigue or oversaturation."

Because Hasbro trades well below 30x forward earnings with rising estimates, those targets carry some weight as an independent anchor rather than as extrapolation of the price. The spread is wide, and the low target of $85 is only 11.5% below spot, which tells you the sell side sees limited downside and moderate upside — the same conclusion the sum-of-parts reaches.

## Competitive Landscape

MAGIC is a real moat: a 30-plus-year IP with a specialty-hobby distribution channel, an active secondary market, and switching costs embedded in collections. Its 46.0% FY2025 segment operating margin is not a toy margin, and it is what justifies Hasbro trading above Mattel.

The trajectory within that moat is where the score is capped. MAGIC's growth is disproportionately from Universes Beyond sets built on IP Hasbro does not own. Three consequences appear directly in the filings: royalties rose 90bp of revenue in FY2025 with a further back-half 2026 step-up guided; the 10-K names licensor dependence explicitly (Final Fantasy, Spider-Man, Avatar, Lord of the Rings) and warns that minimum guaranteed royalties "in some cases may be greater than what we are ultimately able to recoup from actual sales"; and Hasbro added a new risk factor stating that "frequent product releases, cross-brand collaborations or expanded licensing initiatives may contribute to franchise fatigue or oversaturation." A company writing that down about the strategy it is executing is worth taking at its word.

Consumer Products faces structural pressure Hasbro also documents. The 10-K states that "the barriers to entry for new participants in the play industry are low," citing TikTok and influencer distribution, private label from large retailers, and AI-enabled entry. Hasbro's response is to reduce capital intensity — original design manufacturers (which the 10-K concedes "may reduce our direct control over product design, quality, sourcing"), licensing out rather than manufacturing, and a $30.3M FY2025 cut in CP advertising explicitly "to offset the operating profit impact of tariffs." Regionally, H1 2026 North America grew 5.3% and Europe 6.5% while Asia Pacific fell 8.9% and Latin America 8.2%, extending FY2025 declines of 13.0% and 18.0%.

Customer concentration is high and not improving: top five retail customers were approximately 35% of FY2025 revenue, with Amazon around 11% and Walmart around 9%, all on purchase orders with no binding long-term commitments. The revenue *mix* has shifted toward hobby, direct and licensing channels, but the disclosed customer percentages have not visibly moved. Mix diversification and customer diversification are different things, and only the first is demonstrated.

Within card games, competition is live. Hasbro's own MY LITTLE PONY trading-card licensing revenue fell $40.5M (-47.0%) in FY2025 and a further $10.8M in H1 2026, and DUEL MASTERS declined in H1 2026 — internal evidence that card franchises can roll over quickly.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Profit concentration exceeding 100% of consolidated operating profit.** The Q2 10-Q's forward-looking factors newly reference "brands such as Magic: The Gathering in which we have seen an increasing concentration of our sales and profits." A single bad set cycle moves the whole company. Partial offset: CP's H1 loss included cyber disruption and tariff costs, so a CP normalization to the guided 6-8% margin mechanically reduces concentration without MAGIC weakening.

**2. Securities class action targeting the MAGIC narrative.** *West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Hasbro* (S.D.N.Y., No. 24-cv-8633) covers purchasers between September 16, 2021 and October 26, 2023 and alleges misleading statements "regarding the growth and success of Magic: The Gathering... including statements attributing Magic's growth to a consumer-driven 'segmentation' strategy." Amended complaint November 26, 2025; motion to dismiss February 6, 2026; no loss range estimable. A related derivative action (*Sbriglio v. Stoddart et al.*, R.I. Superior Court) is stayed behind it. The class period predates the current strategy, and dismissal would remove the overhang entirely; until then there is a disclosure-quality question hanging over the exact mechanics (set cadence, channel inventory) that the thesis depends on.

**3. Further goodwill and software impairment risk, specifically quantified.** Remaining goodwill is $1,256.2M (WotC $370.2M, CP $560.8M, Entertainment $325.2M). The FY2025 10-K discloses that the Family Brands reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~15%, with $325.2M of goodwill allocated, tested at a ~9.5% discount rate and 3.0% terminal growth. The FY2025 annual test was performed qualitatively only across all units, a lower-evidence procedure than the quantitative Q2 test. Separately, $383.4M of unamortized capitalized software remains after the $56.4M Q2 write-off.

**4. Cyber incident — realized, with an open legal tail.** Unauthorized network access identified in late March 2026 forced manual order processing; management sized the Q2 revenue loss at ~$25M (down from an initial $40-60M estimate) plus $10.8M of incremental costs, with no insurance recovery recognized. The Q2 10-Q rewrote the risk factor from conditional to realized and disclosed that the incident "resulted in disruptions to the Company's... internal control over financial reporting during the second quarter," requiring additional manual controls, with normal operation restored by June 28. A putative class action (*Standing v. Hasbro*, D.R.I., No. 1:26-cv-00219) is pending.

**5. Guided Q4 MAGIC decline plus a $40M timing shift.** A negative headline is scheduled into the Q4 print by management's own guidance.

**6. Execution load.** The Rhode Island-to-Boston headquarters relocation added $221.8M of gross operating-lease right-of-use assets and liabilities in H1 2026 on 10-12 year terms, with $439.7M of total undiscounted future lease payments. Cumulative Operational Excellence restructuring charges have reached $177.2M since 2022. The 10-K warns that "the simultaneous pursuit of multiple strategic initiatives... may strain management attention, organizational capacity and capital resources."

**7. Unresolved disclosure gaps.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (January 22 and July 27, 2026) concerning officer or director departures/elections were not retrievable. The July 27 filing lands six days after earnings and three days before the 10-Q; a senior personnel change is possible and would matter for the management assessment. This is flagged as unverified, not assumed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 29.2% sits marginally below 30.5% one-year realized (a -1.3pp spread, effectively fair) and at only the 6th percentile within the current chain, so options are not pricing a near-term event. The term structure is in contango (29.2% at one week rising to 31.2% at six months), which the data characterizes as a normal-to-complacent structure; the six-month tenor prices a ±15.7% move. Near-term put/call skew of 6.3% is flagged as elevated put demand, though the put/call volume ratio of 0.40 and open-interest ratio of 0.53 are call-tilted at the aggregate level. The more informative reading is the change since July 23: ATM IV fell 5.5pp from 34.7% while the price rose 7.8%, and the put/call open-interest ratio dropped from 0.93 to 0.53. That combination is consistent with hedging demand unwinding after the earnings beat rather than positioning for downside. Short interest is unremarkable at 6.0% of float with three days to cover. Options corroborate a fair-value, low-near-term-catalyst read and offer no independent support for either a bullish or bearish tilt. For an existing holder wanting downside protection into the Q4 MAGIC comparison, longer-dated volatility at 31.2% is not obviously mispriced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $125 (+30.2%)

Hasbro is a games company being valued as a toy company. WotC generates over $1.0B of operating profit at a low-40s margin from a franchise with 30 years of collector base, hobby-store distribution and a secondary market. At 15.6x FY2026 earnings and 10.7x guided adjusted EBITDA, the market pays a toy multiple for that. Four consecutive beats, consensus estimates rising 4.8% in 90 days, and sell-side sentiment improving to 13 buy-equivalents against 2 holds suggest consensus is still catching up. Three things could push earnings above the current $6.53 FY2027 line: the Harry Potter global primary toy licence starting in 2027 alongside the HBO series, which is the first credible catalyst for CP to reach the top of its 6-8% margin band; the full $150M cost-transformation target ($70M delivered); and at least 25% annual digital-spend reductions from a 2026 peak, converting today's expensed development drag into 2027-28 margin. A CBP refund on a majority of the IEEPA tariffs Hasbro paid — against ~$62.6M of disclosed cumulative tariff cost — would be incremental to guidance. Capital allocation supports the case: a $1.0B authorization with $958M remaining, a buyback target raised from $100M to at least $200M, and H1 repurchases weighted to the lower prices (296,488 shares at ~$84.2 versus 95,620 at ~$92.0). Balance-sheet risk is low: all fixed-rate debt, the 2026 maturity pre-funded, ~1.5x net leverage against a 3.75x covenant, and a $1.1B undrawn revolver to 2031.

### Bear Case — $75 (-21.9%)

More than 100% of consolidated operating profit comes from one franchise whose growth is bought with third-party IP. Royalties rose from 6.9% to 7.8% of revenue in FY2025 with a further step-up guided; WotC's margin is guided down from 46.0% to the low-40s; and Q2 adjusted EPS fell 2% year over year despite 16.2% revenue growth. Management has guided Q4 MAGIC revenue lower and is adding $50-75M of Wizards marketing in 2027, so the franchise now requires more spend to hold its cadence. Licensor concentration is a genuine cliff: non-renewal of Final Fantasy, Marvel, Avatar or Lord of the Rings, or renewal at higher minimum guarantees the 10-K warns may exceed recoverable sales, would hit the only profitable segment. Hasbro itself added a "franchise fatigue or oversaturation" risk factor. CP has not been fixed — an H1 operating loss, ~3.3% ex-impairment FY2025 margin, APAC down 8.9% and LatAm down 8.2% — while the 10-K concedes barriers to entry in toys are low. A securities class action attacks the MAGIC growth narrative directly; $325.2M of Family Brands goodwill sits on a 15% cushion tested qualitatively at year-end; $383.4M of capitalized software remains impairable after the $56.4M Q2 write-off; and the AR credit-loss allowance more than doubled during 2025 without explanation. Insiders sold in a four-transaction cluster immediately after the July print with zero open-market purchases in the March-July window, while CEO compensation-actually-paid of $35.3M coincided with a $322.4M GAAP net loss and a five-year TSR index of 108.2 against a peer group at 151.1. A collectibles-led earnings stream is a discretionary-spending exposure that would weaken first if the consumer softens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

## Conclusion

Hasbro has executed a real strategic pivot: a 46%-margin trading-card business now carries the entire company, revenue grew 14.6% in H1 2026, four consecutive quarters have beaten consensus, and the balance sheet was refinanced with all-fixed debt, pre-funded maturities and ~1.5x net leverage. That work deserves credit, and it is why this remains a hold rather than a reduce.

What it does not support at $96.03 is a purchase. The company has guided WotC's margin down from 46.0% to the low-40s, guided Q4 MAGIC revenue lower, and reported Q2 adjusted EPS 2% *below* the prior year on 16.2% revenue growth, because the incremental MAGIC dollar carries a third-party royalty on IP Hasbro does not own, and because holding the franchise's cadence now requires $20M of incremental H2 2026 marketing and $50-75M more in 2027. Consensus FY2027 EPS growth of 6% at 14.7x is fair. Against that: a securities class action aimed precisely at the MAGIC growth narrative, $325.2M of goodwill on a 15% cushion, $383.4M of impairable software, a toy segment the company's own 10-K says operates behind low barriers to entry, and an insider cluster that sold into the post-earnings strength with no offsetting purchases.

Overall score 5.7, a marginal step down from 5.8 driven by 7.8% of price appreciation since the prior review compressing an already modest margin of safety, with risk factors unchanged and low. **HOLD.** The name becomes interesting again in the mid-$80s, or on evidence that Consumer Products can carry its own weight.
