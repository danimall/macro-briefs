**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Freedom Holding Corp. is a Nevada-incorporated, New York-headquartered financial holding company whose operations are concentrated in Kazakhstan (~84% of revenue, ~63% of assets). It runs an integrated fintech ecosystem spanning brokerage, digital banking, insurance, payments, and developmental telecom/AI ventures, anchored by the Freedom SuperApp (5.2M registered users, 2.59M monthly active). The business has real momentum: FY2026 revenue rose 9% to $2.19B, net income doubled to $153.3M, banking customers reached 5.0M (doubled YoY), and brokerage accounts grew to 858K. On surface metrics this looks like a high-growth emerging-market platform.

The complication is that the company's growth engine and its most acute legal jeopardy are the same fact. A single institutional market-maker customer at the Freedom Global subsidiary generated $345.5M, or 71% of total fee-and-commission income (up from 56% the prior year). That relationship (internalized trades, settlement practices, and dealings with institutional market makers at non-U.S. broker-dealers) is precisely the conduct the SEC has been investigating since 2021. On March 11, 2026, the SEC staff issued Wells Notices to both FRHC and CEO Timur Turlov, a preliminary determination to recommend civil enforcement for federal securities-law violations. This is a binary, unresolved action that strikes at the company's largest single revenue line and its controlling shareholder personally.

Layered on top are governance concentration (Turlov owns ~69%, serves as CEO and Chairman, and is the counterparty on multiple related-party transactions), Kazakh sovereign concentration (92% of sovereign holdings and ~83% of trading income tied to Kazakhstan), lower-quality earnings (the net income doubling was driven by a $217M swing in trading-securities gains while core insurance shrank 29%), and a funding model increasingly reliant on 9-12% coupon debt to finance speculative telecom and a ~$2B AI data center. At 56.6x trailing earnings, the stock is not pricing this risk stack. We rate FRHC **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

Freedom Holding operates four reporting segments for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026:

- **Brokerage** — $831.5M revenue (+16%): retail brokerage, margin lending, and investment banking via the Tradernet platform; ~858K accounts.
- **Banking** — $689.2M (+36%): Freedom Bank KZ, a fast-growing digital bank with 5.0M customers (doubled YoY), a $2.05B loan book (+29%), and $2.52B deposits (+46%).
- **Insurance** — $497.8M (-22%): Freedom Life and Freedom Insurance, a leading online general insurer in Kazakhstan.
- **Other** — $172.8M (+20%): Freedom Pay, the Arbuz e-commerce platform, ticketing, and developmental telecom/media/cloud ventures.

The strategic centerpiece is the Freedom SuperApp, which bundles banking, payments, insurance, lending, government-service integration, and lifestyle commerce. The model's defensibility rests on cross-selling and network effects within Kazakhstan, where it competes against the dominant local fintech Kaspi.kz, plus Halyk Bank and Bank CenterCredit. In Europe it competes against Interactive Brokers and eToro.

The company is controlled by founder, Chairman, and CEO Timur Turlov, who owns roughly 69% of the stock. It uses Nasdaq "controlled company" governance exemptions and is incorporated in Nevada.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Total revenue net of interest expense reached $2,191.3M (+9%); net income rose 101% to $153.3M from a restated $76.2M. The revenue mix: interest income 41% ($882M, +2%), fee and commission 22% ($490M, -3%), net insurance 18% ($402M, -29%), trading gains 7% ($159M, swung from a -$58M loss), with FX and derivatives roughly 6%.

**Earnings quality.** The headline doubling is lower quality than it appears. It was driven by a $217M swing in trading-securities results, including roughly $164M of realized gains on selling Kazakh sovereign debt, gains management itself characterizes as inherently volatile and non-recurring. Approximately 83% of trading income derives from interest on Kazakhstan government and quasi-government debt, and 92% of sovereign bonds held are Republic of Kazakhstan paper. Earnings are effectively a leveraged position on Kazakh sovereign credit and rates. Insurance revenue fell 29% on a regulatory cap on agent commissions for bank/microfinance-linked policies, a structural headwind. Banking fee income went negative (-$74M) because SuperApp cashback paid in "Freedom Currency" is netted against fee income.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Total assets reached $13.2B (+33%) against $1.49B of equity and $11.7B of liabilities, including $7.1B of customer liabilities. Margin lending receivables grew to $4.7B from $3.3B, a large, market-sensitive exposure. Long-term debt (Freedom SPC bonds) nearly tripled to $1,261M from $469M at 9-12% coupons. Trading securities of $2.3B are 44% pledged under repo at a 12.2% effective rate. Operating cash flow fell to $185M from $1.68B (driven by margin-lending and trading-book movements), and after $199M of capex, free cash flow was negative at roughly -$13.5M. There is no dividend, and the effective tax rate rose to 32.1% after Kazakhstan began taxing sovereign-securities income at 10%.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.25 ("distress") should be discounted here, as the model is inappropriate for banks and brokers whose business is inherently high-leverage (D/E 7.83). Liquidity is supported by ~$6.97B of net liquid assets and ~$2.7B of aggregate excess regulatory capital. The binding risks are regulatory, concentration, and governance, not classic insolvency.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signals are genuine and broad-based on a unit basis: SuperApp MAU +154% YoY, banking customers doubled to 5.0M, deposits +46%, loan book +29%, brokerage accounts up from 683K to 858K. The Kazakh financial sector is benefiting from post-2022 capital inflows and a young, digitizing population.

Expansion is also moving cross-border: agreement to acquire ~99.32% of Turkish Bank A.S. (pending regulatory approval), a UAE brokerage license obtained December 2025, and planned bank/brokerage entities in Georgia and Türkiye. These extend the franchise beyond a single country.

Two caveats temper the growth thesis. First, the most profitable engine, the 71%-of-fees market-maker arrangement, is the subject of the enforcement action; if the SEC forces a change in those practices, the fee line that has driven brokerage growth is directly exposed. Second, the new telecom and ~$2B NVIDIA-linked AI data center ventures (the latter a non-binding MOU dependent on external financing and exposed to GPU export-control risk) are expected to be loss-making for years and are being funded with expensive debt. Growth in headline users does not equate to growth in durable, defensible earnings.

There is no usable consensus estimate trend in the available data, and the historical surprise record is too thin (a single -237.6% miss in 2022) to calibrate forward confidence. Recent quarterly EPS prints ($0.50, $0.63, $1.25) are volatile, consistent with the trading-driven earnings profile.

## Valuation Assessment

FRHC trades at 56.6x trailing earnings (forward identical at 56.6x in the available data), with a market cap of ~$8.5B against $153.3M of net income. There are no published analyst price targets to anchor against.

The bull framing would argue this is a fast-growing fintech that deserves a premium multiple. We do not accept that here, for two reasons. First, the earnings on which the multiple sits are low quality: roughly $164M of realized sovereign-bond trading gains plus FX gains carried the doubling, while the recurring insurance segment shrank 29% and banking fee income turned negative. Normalized for non-recurring trading gains, the underlying earnings power supporting the multiple is materially lower than the reported $2.51 EPS implies. Second, a binary enforcement overhang on 71% of fee income does not warrant paying 56x. The appropriate response to an unresolved Wells Notice that could force changes to the company's largest revenue line is a discount, not a premium.

The cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not rescue the stock. FY2026 earnings are not trough earnings where trailing P/E understates normalized power; if anything, they are flattered by trading gains and may overstate sustainable run-rate. A high multiple on potentially peak, low-quality earnings, with a regulatory tail attached, is a poor risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

FRHC's competitive position within Kazakhstan is real but contested. Its differentiation is the integrated brokerage + banking + insurance bundle and direct access to U.S. and EU markets for emerging-market investors, a combination domestic incumbents do not replicate. The SuperApp's government-database integration and cross-sell create switching costs.

Against this, it competes with Kaspi.kz (the dominant local fintech benchmark), Halyk Bank, and Bank CenterCredit, all larger and better capitalized in their core domains, plus Interactive Brokers and eToro in Europe. The trajectory in user metrics is improving, which supports a mid-range competitive score, but the moat is concentrated in one country and partially dependent on a regulatory regime that has already moved against it (insurance commission caps, a new Tax Code, and a new Banking Law effective January 2026). The cross-border expansion is positive for diversification but unproven.

## Risk Assessment

This is where the thesis is decided. The risk stack is unusually dense and specific.

1. **SEC investigation and Wells Notice (most material).** Since 2021 the SEC has subpoenaed FRHC and officers regarding settlement practices, relationships with institutional market-maker customers of non-U.S. broker-dealer subsidiaries, accounting for internalized trades, disclosures, and internal controls. The company is contesting the Wells Notices. Outcome could include monetary and non-monetary relief and severe reputational damage. The company has also fielded OFAC inquiries about transactions involving sanctioned individuals via clearing arrangements.

2. **Single-customer concentration tied to the probe.** The institutional market-maker customer at Freedom Global also generated $21.4M of margin interest, with no exclusivity and no minimums. This is the same conduct the SEC is examining.

3. **Sovereign/country concentration.** Kazakhstan exposure subjects the company to tenge volatility (depreciated 21.6% then partially recovered), 11% inflation (March 2026), NBK base rates of 10.25-18%, oil-export-route vulnerability (CPC terminal attacked November 2025), and 2022-style civil unrest risk.

4. **Related-party governance.** Loan-claim purchases from Turlov-controlled FFIN Credit (discontinued September 2025, legacy loans remain), a $10M chess-federation sponsorship where Turlov holds a role, funding of a Turlov-owned youth football league, and margin loans to management.

5. **Capital-intensive new ventures funded by expensive debt.** The April 2026 Reg-S Kazakhstan equity-offering consideration signals potential dilution.

6. **Founder dependence and structure.** Turlov is CEO, Chairman, ~69% owner, related-party counterparty, Wells Notice recipient, and on Ukraine's NSDC sanctions list (since October 2022). The company carries no key-man insurance, uses a Nevada/controlled-company structure, and has a history of restatements and a remediated material weakness (FY2023). Enforcing U.S. judgments in Kazakhstan is difficult.

The risk score reflects that several of these are not priced into a 56x multiple and at least one (the Wells Notice resolution) is genuinely binary.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV rose to 52.9% from 42.0% over the prior week while the price fell 13.0% to $137.97, and the term structure is in backwardation (52.9% near-term vs 48.4% three-month). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.8%. IV is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 39.3% (a 13.6-point spread), indicating the market is paying up for protection around a near-term catalyst. The put/call OI ratio jumped to 0.73 from 0.22, a meaningful increase in hedging demand, though the skew remains call-heavy at -13.8% near-term. Short interest is modest at 5.3% of float but with 13.4 days to cover, reflecting thin borrow rather than a crowded short. The configuration is consistent with elevated uncertainty around the enforcement matter and the recent earnings/10-K release (June 1), not with a clean directional setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** FRHC is a genuine emerging-market fintech compounder. User growth is broad and real (banking customers and SuperApp MAU doubling), the cross-sell ecosystem creates switching costs, and cross-border expansion (Turkish Bank, UAE, Georgia, Türkiye) diversifies away from single-country risk over time. Regulatory capital (~$2.7B excess) and net liquid assets (~$7.0B) provide a solid base. Director Sergey Lukyanov bought ~$2.4M of stock (20,000 shares near $120) in late February 2026, close to the Wells Notice window, a modest insider vote of confidence. If the SEC matter resolves with a manageable settlement, the overhang lifts and the multiple can re-rate on continued growth. Cumulative indexed TSR of 248 vs a peer group at 147 shows the franchise has outperformed cumulatively, though the TSR index fell from 497 in FY2024 to 248 in FY2025, reflecting a significant stock decline.

**Bear case.** The growth engine and the legal jeopardy are the same line item. A 71%-of-fees single customer, engaged in the exact practices the SEC is pursuing, is one enforcement outcome away from impairment. The Wells Notice names Turlov personally, the ~69% owner on whom the entire structure depends and who is already on a foreign sanctions list. Earnings quality is poor (trading gains carried the doubling; insurance shrank 29%; banking fee income went negative). Funding is shifting to 9-12% debt and possible dilution to bankroll speculative telecom and AI capex with no near-term return. The "Freedom Currency" cashback ETN is reflexively linked to FRHC's own stock, an unusual circular dynamic. At 56x trailing on flattered earnings with a binary regulatory tail, the downside is asymmetric.

The bear case is more probable and better supported by the primary filings.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The SEC enforcement track is the live catalyst. The company is contesting, and the path from Wells Notice to a formal enforcement action or settlement typically runs several months to over a year with no fixed date. There is no single dated event the market can mark a calendar to, which weakens the short setup despite the strong qualitative thesis.

**Timing window.** Open-ended. Enforcement resolution could be 6-18+ months out, well beyond the window where carry is justified by a defined trigger. Interim earnings are volatile (trading-driven), making quarterly prints an unreliable short catalyst.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 984,314 shares, and days to cover is high at 13.4, reflecting thin float liquidity (~69% is held by the insider). Low float plus high days-to-cover creates meaningful squeeze risk on any positive headline (a benign SEC settlement, a Turkish Bank close, the AI data center advancing). This is the structural problem for shorts.

**Borrow and options signal.** Rich, backwardated near-term IV raises the cost of expressing the view through options. The recent jump in put OI confirms hedging demand, but the persistent call-heavy skew indicates options positioning is not uniformly bearish. High IV plus thin float makes the carry expensive.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** If enforcement forces a change to the market-maker arrangement, fee income could be materially impaired and a multiple de-rate toward ~25-30x on lower-quality earnings could take the stock to roughly $80-100. But the squeeze upside on any favorable resolution or expansion milestone is comparable in magnitude given ~69% insider lock-up and low float. The asymmetry does not clear a 2:1 reward/risk bar.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental concerns are real and specific, but the absence of a dated catalyst, the thin float with 13.4 days to cover, rich options carry, and squeeze risk from a single positive headline make this an avoid-not-short. The thesis is "concentrated, legally exposed, expensive" rather than "imminent collapse."

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the name on any of the following: (a) the SEC matter resolves with a quantified, manageable settlement that does not require dismantling the market-maker arrangement and does not bar Turlov; (b) fee-income concentration falls below ~40% of total fees as cross-border subsidiaries (Türkiye, UAE, Georgia) scale and diversify the revenue base; (c) earnings quality improves such that recurring banking and brokerage income, not trading gains, drives net income growth for two consecutive quarters; and (d) the valuation compresses meaningfully (toward ~25-30x) to reflect the legal and concentration risk.

**Timeline.** The enforcement track is likely to persist for 6-18+ months. We expect the Kazakh macro and sovereign-concentration headwinds (tenge volatility, 11% inflation, elevated NBK rates) to remain through at least the next several quarters. The new venture (telecom, AI data center) losses are expected to weigh for years.

We will reassess at the next quarterly earnings release and on any 8-K disclosure relating to the SEC matter, the Turkish Bank acquisition close, or the Reg-S equity offering.

## Conclusion

Freedom Holding has built a fast-growing, founder-controlled fintech ecosystem with genuine user momentum across banking, brokerage, and the SuperApp. The problem is the structure of the earnings and the risks attached to them. The most profitable line, 71% of fee income from a single market-maker customer, is the precise practice the SEC has escalated to a Wells Notice against both the company and CEO Turlov, who also owns ~69% of the stock and sits on a foreign sanctions list. The FY2026 net income doubling was carried by non-recurring sovereign-bond trading gains while the recurring insurance segment shrank and banking fee income went negative. Funding is shifting to expensive debt to bankroll speculative ventures, and the stock trades at 56x trailing on flattered earnings with a binary regulatory overhang. The options market is pricing near-term stress.

The upside narrative and the downside risk run through the same handful of facts, and the current price does not discount the downside. We rate FRHC **AVOID**, while noting it is not an active short given the absence of a dated catalyst and meaningful squeeze risk from a thin float.
