## Executive Summary

The balance sheet carries no borrowings, $530.4M of cash, deposits and marketable securities at June 30, and net financial income of $4.7M in the quarter. The Board authorized a new $500M repurchase program on June 4, 2026 after completing a $200M program.

Three items temper the print. First, the guidance raise is largely inorganic: of the +$80M added to the revenue midpoint, Passport Global (closed July 1, 2026) contributes $55–59M, leaving an organic raise of roughly $23M, or +1.8%. Second, Q3 guidance implies organic revenue growth of about +30% (guide midpoint $312M less ~$25M Passport, against derived Q3 2025 revenue of $220.8M), down from +39.2% in Q2, and a sequential organic decline of ~4% where the prior year showed +2.7%. Set against that, the quality of the operating leverage is real and independent of accounting noise. The Shopify commercial agreement asset — which drove $148.6M of FY2024 and $66.0M of FY2025 non-cash amortization through sales and marketing — is now fully consumed (balance $66.5M at Dec-24, $0.5M at Dec-25, zero at Jun-26). At 27x current-year and 21x next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS, with a debt-free balance sheet and a buyback authorization of ~6.7% of capitalization, the risk/reward is positively skewed but not wide enough for a full position given an undisclosed Shopify channel concentration and an untested EU duty regime change that took effect July 1. **ACCUMULATE.**

## Company Overview

Global-e operates a cross-border direct-to-consumer enablement platform serving over 1,500 brands and retailers selling into 200+ destinations. The platform localizes checkout (currency, pricing, payment methods, language), calculates and collects duties and taxes at the point of sale, and arranges international shipping and returns. In many transactions Global-e acts as merchant of record, contracting directly with the shopper and assuming payment, tax-remittance and compliance obligations. That structure, together with reliance on the personal-importation model, is the foundation of the economics and the principal regulatory exposure.

Revenue splits into two roughly equal lines: service fees ($139.4M, 47% of Q2 2026 revenue) and fulfillment services ($159.6M, 53%). The mix has been stable at 53% fulfillment across FY2024, FY2025 and H1 2026. Geographic disclosure is by *merchant outbound* region, not shopper location: FY2025 United States $507.1M (53%), United Kingdom $194.0M (20%), European Union $175.6M (18%), Other $81.8M (9%).

Global-e supplies the infrastructure behind Shopify's Managed Markets offering. Shopify is a distribution channel and commercial counterparty, not a customer; merchants pay Global-e. Managed Markets 2.0 launched for US merchants in Q4 2025, extended to Canada and the UK in Q2 2026, and all remaining merchants were migrated off version 1.0. The company is domiciled in Israel and files as a foreign private issuer (20-F annually, 6-K interim), which means no 10-Q, no DEF 14A proxy, and no earnings-transcript record in the sources reviewed.

FY2025 was the first GAAP-profitable year, a $143.8M swing. The composition matters. The operating income bridge from −$67.9M to +$71.7M decomposes as gross profit +$96.8M, R&D −$17.3M (higher spend), S&M +$60.5M (lower expense), G&A +$0.5M. Of the $139.6M swing, $82.6M — 59% — is the reduction in commercial agreement amortization, a non-cash contra-item tied to the Shopify partner warrant, not incremental operating performance. Screens keyed to GAAP operating margin will misstate the FY2024-to-FY2026 trend.

The counterweight is that the underlying leverage is genuine. Stripping the item, S&M grew from $102.1M (FY2024) to $124.1M (FY2025), +21.6% against revenue +27.8%; in H1 2026, ex-item S&M rose 20.3% against revenue +36.2%. Share-based compensation was flat in dollars ($39.2M FY2024 → $39.3M FY2025), falling from 5.2% to 4.1% of revenue. Capex was $3.1M on $962M of revenue — 0.32% of sales. Non-GAAP gross margin has been essentially flat (46.4% FY2024, 46.3% FY2025, 46.07% H1 2026 versus 45.99% H1 2025), though Q2 2026 dipped 120bp year over year to 45.3% without a category-mix explanation.

**Take rate.** Revenue divided by GMV fell from 15.50% (FY2024) to 14.65% (FY2025) — 85bp — with a quarterly path of 15.28%, 14.78%, 14.60%, 14.26% through 2025, then 14.47% and 14.32% in Q1 and Q2 2026. The band has broadly stabilized at 14.3-14.5%. Two explanations are plausible and neither is confirmed: GMV is defined to include duties, taxes and shipping, so rising duty content mechanically inflates the denominator without any pricing concession; alternatively, mix has shifted toward larger enterprise merchants and the Shopify channel at lower blended rates. The timing coincidence with US de minimis repeal favors the first, but the company does not disclose duty content within GMV.

An important measurement caution: Passport will inflate the reported take rate. Its guided $55–59M of H2 revenue sits against only ~$60M of merchant-of-record GMV, because shipping revenue largely bypasses the GMV metric. Blended FY2026 implied take rate is $1,330M ÷ $8,960M = 14.84%; ex-Passport it is $1,273M ÷ $8,900M = 14.30%. Anyone reading the blended figure as pricing improvement is reading acquisition accounting.

**Cash flow quality.** FY2025 free cash flow of $280.7M exceeded Adjusted EBITDA of $198.5M — 141% conversion. Decomposing the $283.8M of operating cash flow: net profit $68.3M plus non-cash addbacks of $126.3M (depreciation $2.3M, SBC $39.3M, commercial agreement $66.0M, intangible amortization $18.7M) leaves a $89.2M residual from working capital and FX, dominated by accrued expenses and other liabilities (+$88.7M) and funds payable to customers (+$58.7M) — merchant-settlement float and accruals building into the holiday peak. Accrued expenses rose from $141.6M (Dec-24) to $231.7M (Dec-25) and fell back to $186.0M (Jun-26).

Seasonality is extreme: Q4 2025 alone generated $216.2M of FCF, 77% of the year. Q1 is reliably negative (−$72.6M in 2025, −$72.9M in 2026). H1 2026 FCF was $0.271M. The float and accrual component scales with GMV *growth*, not level, so deceleration compresses FCF faster than it compresses EBITDA. That is the single most important quality-of-earnings caveat in the file. It does not make the float a one-time benefit — it has grown every year, and full-year conversion improved from 119% (FY2024) to 141% (FY2025) — but it does mean any single-quarter annualization is unreliable, including the tempting $73.2M × 4.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $285.4M, short-term deposits $170.4M, marketable securities $74.7M = $530.4M gross. Zero borrowings; total liabilities of $451.6M are trade payables, accruals, customer float and leases. No covenants, no interest expense, no refinancing risk. Funds receivable of $157.8M exactly offsets funds payable to customers of $157.8M — pass-through merchant money that should not be counted as company cash. Current ratio 2.07x, or 2.69x excluding the offsetting float on both sides. Goodwill of $375.4M is 30.7% of assets, from prior acquisitions; tangible book equity is $487.8M. Note that the accumulated deficit grew from −$536.3M to −$585.2M in H1 2026 despite $78.1M of profit — the $126.9M difference is share repurchases charged directly against the deficit, consistent with Israeli retirement practice. Accumulated deficit is no longer a measure of cumulative losses.

**Capital allocation.** $72.2M repurchased in FY2025 and $126.9M in H1 2026, completing the $200M plan; a new $500M authorization followed on June 4, 2026. Pro forma for Passport's ~$175M cash component paid July 1, gross investments are roughly $355M, so the new authorization and the up-to-$75M earn-out must be funded substantially from future free cash flow. Authorizations are not obligations, so this is a pacing question rather than a solvency question.

Financial health scores 7.5: debt-free, capital-light, self-funding, with the offsets being float-dependent cash conversion, heavy seasonality and goodwill weight.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 2026 delivered revenue +39.2% and GMV +43.7%. H1 2026 revenue was $551.1M (+36.2%) on GMV of $3,830.8M (+42.1%). Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 20.88% in the quarter, up 298bp.

Guidance progression tells a more nuanced story. FY2026 revenue guidance moved from $1,211–1,271M (February) to $1,220–1,280M (May) to $1,305–1,355M (August). The +$80M midpoint raise in August includes $55–59M of Passport, leaving ~$23M organic (+1.8%). The Adjusted EBITDA midpoint moved +$12M, of which Passport is $3–4M.

On a full-year organic basis, management's claim of acceleration holds: organic FY2026 revenue of ~$1,273M against FY2025's $962.2M is +32.3%, versus +27.8% in FY2025. But organic GMV growth of ~35.5% is statistically indistinguishable from FY2025's +35.2%. The revenue acceleration is arithmetically a deceleration in take-rate erosion. That is a lower-quality driver: volume growth can compound, whereas take-rate stabilization is a one-time change in the second derivative.

Q3 guidance implies organic revenue of ~$287M (midpoint $312M less ~$25M Passport) against derived Q3 2025 revenue of $220.8M — +30.0%, down from +39.2%. Sequentially that is −4.0% versus +2.7% in the prior year, a break in seasonal pattern. Two readings are available. Management has been habitually conservative: Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA came in above the top of its range, Q4 2025 printed at or above the top end across all metrics, and Q2 2026 revenue beat consensus by roughly $16M. Alternatively, Q2 GMV was flattered by identifiable one-off volume — the company cited Pokémon expanding to support "significantly more volume tied to its highly anticipated, viral product drops" — making Q2 the anomaly rather than Q3 the warning. The contribution is unquantified. On the evidence, conservatism is the more likely explanation, but Q3 is the first quarter that also absorbs the EU duty change, so the print carries genuine information.

Growth vectors beyond the base business: Managed Markets 2.0 international expansion (Canada and UK newly available), duty drawback and customs reclaim as a new value-added revenue line created by the tariff regime, TikTok Shop integration, and Passport's logistics capability. Estimate revisions are positive but modest — current-year consensus up 4.9% and next-year up 5.0% over 90 days, from six analysts. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a sharp earnings inflection; it is steady upward drift consistent with a beat-and-raise cadence.

The beat record is mixed rather than uniform: 2 beats, 1 in line, 1 miss across the last four quarters, with a −4.1% miss in Q1 2026 and a +22.9% beat in Q2. Growth prospects score 7.0 — high absolute growth with confirmed margin leverage, marked down for the inorganic composition of the raise, the implied Q3 organic deceleration, and the non-repeating nature of the take-rate driver.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive question is whether rising cross-border compliance complexity helps or hurts Global-e. The evidence so far favors help. The US de minimis exemption on sub-$800 shipments ended in 2025, adding charges reported at up to 50%, and GMV growth *accelerated* through and after that window: +37.8% in Q4 2025, +40.2% in Q1 2026, +43.7% in Q2 2026. The plausible mechanism is that higher fixed compliance costs raise the in-house breakeven for cross-border D2C and push merchants toward a specialist, while collected duties inflate the GMV metric by construction. The company also converted the regime into a revenue line via duty drawback and customs reclaim.

The moat rests on accumulated duty/tax/compliance logic across 200+ destinations, an asset-light logistics network, and the Shopify distribution channel. Enterprise logos added or expanded in Q2 (Ferrari, Officine Universelle Buly, J.M. Weston, Peter Millar, G/FORE, Universal Music Japan, FIGS, Pokémon) are consistent with genuine enterprise capability, though logo lists are marketing disclosure and quantify nothing.

The structural vulnerability is that the largest distribution channel is also the party best positioned to internalize the function. The observable evidence points the other way for now: Managed Markets 2.0 rolled out, all merchants migrated onto it, and availability extended beyond the US for the first time. The ~$215M of warrant value transferred to secure that channel is fully expensed, so the channel's economics appear in clean GAAP numbers going forward. But the proportion of GMV or revenue flowing through Shopify is not disclosed anywhere in the reviewed materials, nor are the agreement's exclusivity, duration, revenue-share or renewal terms. That is the largest unquantified dependency in the file.

Against the platform, marketplaces (Amazon, TikTok Shop) offer merchants an alternative route to international demand that bypasses D2C entirely, and higher landed costs suppress shopper conversion regardless of who handles compliance. Take-rate compression of 85bp in FY2025 is at minimum consistent with pricing pressure from large accounts, even if part of it is denominator mechanics.

Trajectory reads as improving on volume and share, flat-to-deteriorating on price. Competitive position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Shopify channel concentration (highest-magnitude unpriced risk).** Unquantified in every reviewed source. If the channel is a large share of GMV, pricing power and renewal risk concentrate in one counterparty with the capability to insource. The August 2026 cautionary list newly names "our ability to adapt our platform and services for the Shopify platforms," which at minimum indicates management sees adaptation burden as material.

**Trade-policy regime.** The EU's removal of its €150 duty-free threshold, reported effective July 1, 2026, lands squarely inside Q3 and touches Global-e's second-largest merchant region (20% of FY2025 revenue). The mechanics are unconfirmed and the company has not quantified exposure. The US precedent argues the platform benefits, but Q3 is the first clean test. Broader merchant-of-record and personal-importation dependency remains the standing structural exposure.

**Take-rate and margin composition.** Ex-Passport take rate of ~14.30% for FY2026 implies continued mild erosion. Passport dilutes blended Adjusted EBITDA margin ~70bp and inflates the reported take rate, so consolidated metrics will be harder to read from Q3 onward.

**Cash-flow conversion tied to growth.** 31% of FY2025 operating cash flow came from working capital and float that scale with GMV growth. H1 2026 FCF was $0.271M. Any deceleration compresses FCF disproportionately.

**Disclosure and governance deficit.** As a foreign private issuer, Global-e files no 10-Q and no proxy. Executive compensation, board independence and related-party transactions are unexamined because no proxy exists. The FY2025 20-F risk factors were not retrievable, so all risk-factor analysis rests on press-release cautionary lists. Merchant concentration is undisclosed, and the "over 1,500 brands" figure is repeated verbatim between February and August 2026, so merchant growth cannot be measured. A shifting regional revenue mix (Other rising from 9% to 11%) does not establish reduced customer concentration; those are different measures and the concentration measure is simply absent.

**Insider activity.** Thirty Form 4 filings between June 1 and August 10, 2026 are all sales, with zero open-market purchases: Schlachet (CEO) 268,290 shares for ~$10.4M, Tamari (COO) 160,520 for ~$5.8M, Debbi (President) 138,414 for ~$5.3M. The cadence — repeated identical 24,999-share tranches with odd-lot residuals — is characteristic of 10b5-1 plan execution, and each founder retains 3.9–4.4M shares (roughly 2.3–2.6% of basic each), so absolute alignment remains substantial. The CEO's largest single sale (100,000 shares at ~$42.20) came five days before the beat, which under the plan reading is coincidental. I do not infer negative conviction; the defensible conclusion is that all three founders are net reducing and none is adding. Notably, no Form 3 or Form 4 existed before eight Form 3s filed March 16, 2026, while 20-F and 6-K filing continued — an unexplained change in reporting posture that should not be read as a loss of FPI status.

**Israel domicile** and related hostilities remain a retained risk factor.

**Consumer backdrop.** Cross-border discretionary purchasing sits downstream of the global consumer, and higher landed costs from the tariff regime pressure conversion at the shopper level even where Global-e captures the compliance work. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $63 (+54%)

Organic revenue growth holds above 32% as Managed Markets 2.0 scales in Canada and the UK and duty drawback becomes a material value-added line. Take rate stabilizes at or above 14.3% ex-Passport, confirming that FY2025's 85bp compression was duty-denominator mechanics rather than pricing concession. Passport integration lifts its ~6% Adjusted EBITDA margin toward the mid-teens, removing the blended dilution and validating the logistics capability purchase. GAAP earnings converge on non-GAAP with the commercial agreement asset at zero, re-rating the stock on GAAP screens. The $500M authorization is executed at the H1 2026 pace, taking net share count down against a 4.5% award overhang. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $2.10 against consensus $1.94, and the market pays 30x for a debt-free 30%-grower. That yields $63, in line with the $64 street high.

### Bear Case — $28 (−31%)

Q3 confirms genuine organic deceleration rather than conservatism: organic growth settles in the low-20s as the EU duty change suppresses cross-border conversion and the Pokémon-type volume that flattered Q2 does not repeat. Take rate resumes falling as enterprise merchants and the Shopify channel gain leverage. FCF conversion drops toward 100% or below as float growth slows, cutting free cash flow from $280M toward $200M and slowing the buyback just as the earn-out competes for cash. Shopify signals deeper internalization of cross-border merchant-of-record functions, and the market applies a channel-dependency discount. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS lands at $1.75 and the multiple compresses to 16x, giving $28 — just above the $26.845 52-week low.

### Weighing them

Base case (50%): +15% to $47. Bull (25%): +54%. Bear (25%): −31%. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +13% over twelve months, with a wide dispersion band. That is a positive expected return with an inadequate margin of safety for a full position, which is precisely the ACCUMULATE case. The differentiator versus HOLD is that fundamentals are improving on measurable axes — margin up 298bp, revenue +39%, GAAP profitability now structural, net cash and a live buyback — at a forward PEG below 1.0. The differentiator versus BUY is that the largest structural dependency is undisclosed, the near-dated regulatory test is unresolved, the guidance raise was 72% acquisition, and insiders are net sellers.

At $40.86 the shares trade at 24.5–27.1x current-year and 21.1x next-year non-GAAP earnings, 22.5x FY2026 guided EBITDA on enterprise value, and a forward PEG near 0.95. The permanent disappearance of the Shopify commercial agreement amortization gives GAAP earnings a mechanical tailwind that will pull the stock onto value screens over the next four quarters without any change in operations.

The reasons to stage rather than commit fully are specific. Seventy-two percent of the August guidance raise came from an acquisition bought at roughly 44–58x annualized Adjusted EBITDA that dilutes blended margin by ~70bp. Q3 guidance implies organic growth stepping down from 39% to 30% just as the EU removes its duty-free threshold. The full-year organic acceleration comes from take-rate erosion slowing, a driver that cannot repeat. Free cash flow is float-assisted and growth-dependent. And the single largest dependency — the share of the business flowing through Shopify — is not disclosed anywhere in the record, while all three founders sell steadily and none buys.

Accumulate on the current evidence, sized for a stock the options market is pricing to move ±12.9% in eight days, with the Q3 print as the event that should decide whether this becomes a full position or a trim.
