## Executive Summary

Attruby (acoramidis), approved November 2024 and launched December 2024, generated $180.6M of U.S. net product revenue in Q1 2026 — up 392% year over year and 24% sequentially — inside a transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) market that is still expanding faster than share is shifting between competitors. On July 9, 2026, AstraZeneca and Ionis reported that the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial of eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in 1,432 ATTR-CM patients, 57% of whom were on a background TTR stabilizer. That removed the best-capitalized prospective entrant and provided mechanism read-through consistent with BridgeBio's core clinical argument: a well-stabilized patient may already be near the achievable benefit ceiling. Three additional U.S. filings are in flight (encaleret NDA accepted ~July 22; BBP-418; infigratinib NDA guided for Q3 2026), which is the difference between a one-drug company and a platform.

The problem is the claim structure sitting between Attruby's cash flows and the common equity. At 3/31/2026 BridgeBio carried $2,505M of convertible principal across four tranches, ~$892.7M of deferred royalty obligations, total liabilities of ~$3.64B against ~$1.37B of assets, and a stockholders' deficit of ~($2.27B). The royalty monetizations are the most expensive capital in the stack: FY2025 non-cash accretion of ~$125.1M on an estimated average balance of ~$638M implies an effective rate of roughly 18–20%, and the December 2024 Funding Agreement takes 5% of **global** acoramidis net sales with a disclosed potential step-up to 10% in 2027 — commercial success mechanically increases the burden. In July, $933.9M of convertible preferred was funded (Sixth Street $800M, HealthCare Royalty $133.9M) at a $137.79 conversion price with no maturity, which materially de-risks funding but adds a 7% cumulative senior claim ahead of common.

The operating leverage that the equity is being priced on has not yet appeared. FY2025 revenue rose $280M while operating loss improved only ~$70M, because SG&A roughly doubled from $289M to $531M. In Q1 2026, revenue rose ~$78M year over year and operating loss was essentially flat (~$106.0M vs. ~$104.4M), with operating cash outflow widening to $197.3M against an FY2025 quarterly average of ~$111M. Consensus already discounts the inflection: next-year EPS of $0.57 (revised up 109% over 90 days) puts the stock at ~140x. We move from BUY to HOLD because the price now embeds the growth thesis while the two dimensions that most reliably separate winners from losers here, balance-sheet resilience and management alignment, both score below 5.

## Company Overview

BridgeBio operates a "hub-and-spoke" model: autonomous subsidiary teams focused on individual genetic diseases, supported by centralized clinical, regulatory, manufacturing and commercial infrastructure. A practical consequence visible in the financials is that subsidiaries have historically been consolidated, deconsolidated and partnered, making year-over-year comparisons noisy — FY2024's reported net loss of $543.3M was flattered by a ~$178.3M non-cash deconsolidation gain.

The commercial franchise is one product. Attruby is an oral near-complete TTR stabilizer for ATTR-CM. U.S. list price was $18,759.12 per 28-day supply through December 2025, raised to approximately $19,790 in January 2026 — roughly $258,000 of annual gross list revenue per patient on 13.04 cycles, a derived figure gross of rebates, 340B/Medicaid discounts, copay support and Part D manufacturer obligations. Net-to-gross is not disclosed and cannot be validated.

Ex-U.S. economics are royalty and milestone streams, not consolidated sales: Bayer holds European rights, Alexion (AstraZeneca Rare Disease) holds Japan. A portion of the Bayer royalty has already been sold forward — the June 2025 Royalty Purchase Agreement assigns 60% of Bayer royalties on the first $500M of EU net sales, capped at 145% × $300M = $435M.

The pipeline carries the multi-product case: BBP-418 for LGMD2I/R9, encaleret for ADH1 (NDA accepted per company release ~July 22, 2026; PDUFA date unverified), and infigratinib for achondroplasia (positive Phase 3; Q3 2026 NDA filing per third-party coverage rather than a confirmed filing).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: quality improving, headline base more fragile than it looks

| Item | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $221.9M | $502.1M | $116.6M | $194.5M |
| Net product revenue (Attruby) | $2.9M | $362.4M | ~$36.7M | $180.6M |
| License & services | $218.8M | $128.3M | — | ~$4.4M |
| Royalty | $0.2M | $11.4M | — | ~$9.5M |

The "revenue more than doubled" framing needs care in both directions. FY2024 revenue was almost entirely partner milestones ($218.8M of $221.9M), and FY2025 still carried $128.3M of license/services. By Q1 2026 that line had collapsed to ~$4.4M as milestones ran off. So the recurring component (product plus royalty) went from ~$3.1M in FY2024 to ~$373.8M in FY2025 to ~$190.1M in a single quarter — better in quality than the headline suggests, while the reported revenue base is more exposed to lumpy milestone timing than it appears.

Four times Q1 product revenue is $722M, which we treat as a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast. Specialty-pharmacy channel inventory, Medicare Part D coverage-gap dynamics that typically depress Q1 net-to-gross, gross-to-net true-ups and the January price increase all distort any single quarter. A more conservative normalized read is that the product is on a trajectory consistent with management's "blockbuster in 2026" language, but the shape within the year is uncertain.

The company disclosed 6,629 unique Attruby patient prescriptions from 1,632 prescribers as of 12/31/2025. Prescriptions written are not paid, persistent patients, and no reconciliation has been provided — the gap between the three is where launch economics are determined.

### Operating leverage: the decisive absence

FY2025 loss from operations was ($523.4M) versus ($593.0M) in FY2024 — a $70M improvement on $280M more revenue, because SG&A went from ~$289M to ~$531M. Q1 2026 sharpens the point: operating loss of ~($106.0M) against ~($104.4M) a year earlier, with roughly $57M more SG&A and $15M more R&D absorbing the entire revenue gain. Operating cash flow was ($197.3M) in Q1 2026 versus an FY2025 quarterly average of ~($111M).

Part of this is legitimately pre-revenue investment for three simultaneous launches, and Q1 carries annual bonus and payroll-tax burdens. Management's own targets — P&L breakeven in 2027, cash generation in late 2027, ~$600M of profit by 2028 — imply a sharp reversal. Those are three separately framed management targets rather than filing facts, and they have not been reconciled to each other. The company has also missed consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (Q1 2026: −$0.84 vs. −$0.69, a 21.4% miss), while beating on revenue. That pattern of revenue beats paired with EPS misses is the spend-outrunning-revenue dynamic the statements show.

### Capital structure: adequate liquidity, expensive and encumbered

Cash and marketable securities were ~$940.2M at 3/31/2026 (after ~$619.3M of net January 2033 Note proceeds). Adding the ~$933.9M of preferred funded in July and subtracting an estimated Q2 burn of $150–200M gives roughly $1.65–1.70B pro forma — a derived figure, before any repurchase execution. At the recent burn rate that is roughly eight to ten quarters of runway before the March 2027 note settlement.

|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 Notes (due 3/15/27) | $550.0M | $42.71 | ~12.88M |
| 2029 Notes | $747.5M | $97.04 | ~7.70M |
| 2031 Notes | $575.0M | $49.81 | ~11.54M |
| 2033 Notes | $632.5M | $110.58 | ~5.72M |
| **Total** | **$2,505.0M** | | **~37.84M** |

Plus ~$892.7M of deferred royalty obligations. Combined remaining potential cash outflow under the two royalty agreements is on the order of $1.35–1.39B against that carrying value; the gap accretes through non-cash interest. FY2025 total interest cost was ~$178M ($53.1M cash on the notes, $125.1M royalty accretion) against ~$19.9M of interest income.

The 2027 Notes are the near-term item. At $79.88, conversion value above par is ($79.88 − $42.71) × 12,878,305 ≈ $478.7M; the capped call (strike $42.71, cap $62.12) pays ($62.12 − $42.71) × 12,878,305 = $250.0M, leaving ~$228.7M of uncovered economic cost above $550M of principal — roughly $779M of total cash settlement cost. Remaining 2033 proceeds after the $82.5M January repurchase were ~$537M, short of that. Settled instead in shares: ~12.88M delivered less ~3.12M received via the capped call (≈$250.0M ÷ $79.88), for net dilution of ~9.76M shares — about 5.0% more shares, leaving existing holders with ~95.2% of prior ownership. The 2031 Notes ($575M, $49.81 strike) appear to carry no capped-call protection and are the next in-the-money tranche; that conclusion is derived from the 10-Q and should be confirmed against the notes' footnotes.

Maximum contingent convert dilution is ~37.84M shares (~19.3% of current count; ownership to ~83.8%), a ceiling requiring the 2029 and 2033 tranches — currently out of the money — to be in the money at settlement. Separately, a probability-unweighted preferred scenario: $933.9M compounding at 7% PIK for five years reaches ~$1,309.7M, or ~8.55M shares at the $153.10 stepped-up conversion price (~4.4% of current shares), conditional on PIK election throughout, no redemption, and shares above conversion.

**Financial health: 4.0.** Liquidity is genuinely adequate and the preferred converted a funding need into permanent equity at a >100% premium. But negative book equity of ~($2.27B), a dated $550M maturity with partial hedge coverage, ~$200M quarterly operating cash burn, capital priced at 18–20% on the royalty channel, and revenue growth that has not yet reached the operating line together define a fragile structure. The Altman Z-Score of ~−0.66 is a mechanical artifact of negative book equity and is not a meaningful default probability for a $15.7B-cap company with ~$1.65B of liquidity; the leverage inputs driving it, however, are real.

## Growth Analysis

The commercial ramp is the strongest fact in the file. Sequential product-revenue growth of 24% in Q1 2026 implies Q4 2025 of roughly $145–146M (back-derived, not disclosed). Total revenue grew 67% year over year in Q1. The ATTR-CM market itself is expanding as non-invasive diagnosis (PYP scintigraphy, genetic testing) enlarges the treated population faster than share shifts between products, which is why Attruby and Alnylam's Amvuttra have been able to ramp simultaneously off tafamidis's installed base.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction. Next-year consensus EPS moved from $0.27 to $0.57 over 90 days (+109%) and +7.9% over 30 days; current-year improved modestly to −$2.14 from −$2.21. Current-quarter estimates, however, moved the wrong way over 90 days (−$0.49 to −$0.57, a 16.3% deterioration) before recovering 2.7% in the last 30 days — consistent with the Street marking down near-term spend while marking up the 2027 profit turn.

On the mechanics we normally treat as a strong buy signal — revenue growth above 40% with 90-day estimate revisions above 30% — the guardrail applies rather than the signal. That combination is most predictive when the stock is cheap against the improving estimates (forward P/E under ~30x or PEG under 1.5). Here, $79.88 against next-year consensus of $0.57 is roughly 140x, so the revisions are already capitalized. The revision velocity is real and supportive of the growth score, but it does not carry a valuation-driven BUY here.

Three near-term launches determine whether this becomes a platform: encaleret (NDA accepted; PDUFA unverified), BBP-418, and infigratinib for achondroplasia, which faces an established incumbent. Each launch requires incremental SG&A — the same line that has consumed Attruby's gross profit.

**Growth prospects: 8.0** (trimmed from 8.5). Revenue trajectory and market expansion are excellent; the earnings-growth leg remains unproven given four consecutive EPS misses and a flat year-over-year operating loss.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here (EPS −$3.785). The relevant frames are enterprise value against revenue, forward earnings against consensus, and a peak-sales DCF net of the royalty strip and dilution.

Enterprise value, approximately: $15.66B equity + $2.505B converts + $0.893B royalty obligations + $0.934B preferred − ~$1.65B pro forma cash ≈ **$18.3B**. Against 2026 revenue of ~$900–960M, that is roughly **19–20x EV/revenue**. Against next-year consensus EPS of $0.57, the multiple is ~140x. Peer medians in biotech are 23.5x P/E (EXEL 17.7x, ASND 23.5x, NBIX 25.0x on current earnings; AXSM and ARWR unprofitable). Ascendis is arguably the closest structural comp — similar market cap, rare-disease commercial inflection — and it is profitable at 23.5x.

A peak-sales sanity check: assume $3B of U.S. Attruby peak revenue plus ~$150M of partner royalties, 35–40% operating margin at scale after the 5–10% acoramidis royalty strip, and ~215–230M diluted shares. That produces roughly $4.00–4.75 of peak EPS around 2030, worth ~$85–95 at 20x and roughly $60–70 discounted back four years at 10%. Reaching today's price on Attruby alone therefore requires either a higher peak or a richer exit multiple. Getting to the Street's $101–105 median/mean requires meaningful pipeline contribution — roughly $1–1.5B of non-Attruby revenue at maturity — on top of a $3.5–4B Attruby.

That is not an unreasonable base case, but it is an aggressive one, and it is the case the current price already reflects. Two additional deductions belong in the equity value: the Funding Agreement's 5%-of-global-net-sales royalty with a potential doubling to 10% in 2027 (capped at $950M) is a direct tax on the asset being valued, and Section 382 limitation risk on ~$1.7B of federal NOLs — a live question after a large preferred issuance — could raise the effective tax rate exactly in the 2028+ profit window.

The Street distribution says more about crowding than it provides independent confirmation: 22 positive ratings, 2 holds, no sells, with a low target of $80.00 (essentially at spot) and a mean of $105.26, median $101.00, high $157.00. Citi initiated at Neutral in May and has since raised its target to $93; H.C. Wainwright raised to $120. At ~140x forward earnings, analyst targets are not an independent floor; they largely reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price.

**Valuation: 5.5.** No identifiable mispricing at $79.88. There is a credible path to $100+, but it requires successful execution across three launches plus continued Attruby compounding, and the encumbrance (converts, royalty strip, preferred) meaningfully reduces the common's share of that outcome. The stock has fully round-tripped the July 9 competitive windfall ($93.42 intraday high to $79.88), which we read as the market having digested and then discounted that news rather than ignoring it.

## Competitive Landscape

ATTR-CM has three mechanisms in play: stabilizers (Pfizer's tafamidis, BridgeBio's Attruby), silencers (Alnylam's Amvuttra, approved for ATTR-CM in March 2025), and prospectively gene editing. BridgeBio's differentiation is near-complete stabilization versus tafamidis's partial stabilization, oral administration versus quarterly injection, and 54-month open-label extension data (389 ATTRibute-CM completers, widening relative benefit through month 54, per reporting in *AJMC*, June 3, 2026). The OLE is not a randomized comparison against an active stabilizer.

The eplontersen failure is the single most consequential competitive development of the year and cuts three ways in BridgeBio's favor: it removes AstraZeneca's commercial muscle as a near-term entrant; it reads through to stabilizers as the therapeutic backbone (57% of CARDIO-TTRansform patients were already on a stabilizer and the trial failed to show significant added benefit on a week-140 composite); and it lowers the probability of silencers replacing stabilizers. The counterarguments deserve equal weight: the failure may be molecule- or dose-specific rather than a class effect; the nominally significant monotherapy subgroup suggests silencing retains value in stabilizer-naïve patients; the demanding endpoint design in a stabilizer-heavy population could mask real benefit (one analyst publicly called the market reaction an over-reaction); and it does not read across to Amvuttra, a different modality with its own positive ATTR-CM outcomes trial.

Alnylam remains formidable — $1.04B of total net product revenue in Q1 2026, +121% year over year across four rare-disease drugs — giving it payer leverage and a cardiology infrastructure BridgeBio is still building. Pfizer remains the volume incumbent at roughly price parity, so Attruby is competing on data and convenience, not price. The most favorable long-run outcome for BridgeBio is a combination approach; the eplontersen result complicates that story rather than clarifying it.

**Competitive position: 7.0**, maintained. Trajectory is improving (share gains among newly diagnosed patients, a removed entrant, durability data), but the differentiation is clinical rather than structural and can be eroded by data or formulary decisions.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-product concentration.** Attruby was ~93% of Q1 2026 revenue ($180.6M of $194.5M). Debt service, royalty obligations, preferred dividends and three launch build-outs are all underwritten by one drug in one indication.

**The 2027 maturity and settlement gap.** $550M due March 15, 2027, deeply in the money, reclassified to current liabilities, with capped-call coverage only to $62.12. Cash settlement costs ~$779M at the current price against ~$537M of remaining 2033 proceeds; the preferred largely closes that gap, but at the cost of a senior claim.

**Royalty step-up.** The Funding Agreement royalty may double from 5% to 10% of global acoramidis net sales in 2027 if sales conditions are met. Success triggers the burden.

**Preferred terms are unread.** The July 2 8-K carried Items 3.03 (modification of securityholder rights) and 5.03 (charter amendment), which indicates existing common holders' rights were modified. Liquidation preference, consent/veto rights, board designation and change-of-control provisions are unverified. This matters disproportionately because part of the bull case is takeout optionality; a change-of-control put or consent right could constrain exactly that. HealthCare Royalty, majority-owned by KKR, is simultaneously the June 2025 royalty purchaser, a preferred holder, and connected to two KKR-affiliated directors — a related-party fact pattern requiring disclosure review, not evidence of wrongdoing.

**Channel concentration that changed character without improving.** Five customers each exceeded 10% of Q1 2026 gross revenue (18.5%, 24.1%, 21.5%, 14.4%, 16.7%), together ~95%. This replaced Bayer's ~59.6% in Q1 2025. Measured counterparty concentration is not lower; the mix shifted from one licensing partner to five channel intermediaries. It establishes receivable and channel-execution risk and says nothing about prescriber or payer breadth, which is not disclosed.

**FDA promotional enforcement.** The FY2025 10-K discloses an untitled letter regarding a direct-to-consumer broadcast advertisement for Attruby — a company-specific filing fact that constrains DTC latitude mid-launch. Untitled letters are the lower enforcement tier and typically resolve through revised materials; resolution status is unverified.

**Near-term technical supply.** A new S-3ASR was filed July 24, described in third-party coverage as registering 24.63M shares for selling stockholders (a resale registration, not a primary offering) — about 12.6% of shares outstanding. Composition and holder identity are unverified. Separately, a May 8 8-K reported entry into *and* termination of a material definitive agreement; content unknown and potentially material.

**Other:** three simultaneous launch execution risk with incremental SG&A; IRA/most-favored-nation pricing exposure on a ~$258K gross list price; Section 382 risk to $1.7B of NOLs; CMO dependence with limited redundancy.

**Risk factors: 4.0.** Several of these have dated resolution paths, and the near-term binary (Q2 print) sits directly ahead.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.5% against 48.1% one-year historical vol is fair (spread 2.4%), with IV in only the 23rd percentile of the current chain and a flat term structure (near 50.5% ≈ far 50.1%). Skew is call-heavy (−12.1% at one month), put/call volume ratio is 0.02, and put/call open interest is 0.78. The nearest expiry (Aug 21) implies a ±10.2% move, consistent with a Q2 earnings event inside the window.

The read: the options market is not paying up for downside protection despite 14.7% short interest and 5.1 days to cover. Call-tilted positioning alongside heavy short interest is a genuinely two-sided setup — a strong Q2 print could squeeze, but the absence of hedging demand means there is no cushion beneath a disappointment against a four-quarter miss streak. Cheap IV in percentile terms argues that expressing a directional view through options is less costly than usual; it does not resolve direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to $130, toward $157 on pipeline plus strategic interest)

Attruby compounds toward $3.5–4B of U.S. peak revenue in a diagnostic-expansion market with the strongest prospective entrant removed and mechanism data supporting stabilizers as the backbone. Encaleret and BBP-418 approve and launch into small, uncontested rare-disease markets; infigratinib takes achondroplasia share. SG&A stops growing once the three launches are staffed, delivering the P&L breakeven management targets for 2027 and ~$600M of profit in 2028, sheltered initially by $1.7B of NOLs. The 2027 notes settle largely from preferred proceeds without a marketed equity raise. At $5–5.5B of mature revenue and 40% operating margins on ~225M shares, $8+ of EPS supports $130–165 in the 2030–2031 window, and the de-risked commercial asset attracts strategic interest at a premium.

### Bear Case (to $50–55, −31% to −37%)

Attruby plateaus in the $1.8–2.2B range as Amvuttra's payer leverage and cardiology footprint take a durable share of newly diagnosed patients, and gross-to-net erodes with IRA and MFN pressure on a ~$258K list price. SG&A does not decompress because three launches require permanent field infrastructure, keeping operating losses near current levels into 2028 and burning $600–800M more of cash. The Funding Agreement royalty steps to 10% of global net sales in 2027 just as growth decelerates. The 2027 notes settle in shares (+5.0% count) and the 2031 tranche follows unhedged; the preferred's PIK accretion compounds a senior claim; a further raise becomes necessary. On $2.2B of mature revenue and 30% margins across ~235M shares, the equity is worth $55–65 at 20x, and the multiple compresses further while profitability remains prospective.

### Why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY

Prior reports moved this name from 5.5 (June 3) to 5.8 (June 22) to 6.2 and a BUY (July 19). The stock is essentially unchanged since ($80.76–$81.38 then, $79.88 now), so this downgrade is not a reaction to price. Two things drive it. First, the July 9 competitive windfall has been fully round-tripped — the stock spiked to $93.42 and gave it all back within three weeks, which is evidence the market has priced the eplontersen read-through rather than ignored it, and removes the specific mispricing that supported the prior upgrade. Second, on closer reading of the capital structure and the cash-flow trajectory, the dimensions that most reliably determine outcomes here score poorly: balance-sheet resilience (negative equity, a dated maturity, 18–20% royalty capital, ~$200M quarterly burn) and management alignment (broad insider selling with zero open-market buying, a revenue-based incentive metric while operating losses stay flat, FY2025 TSR indexed at 108 against a peer group at 125 alongside $75.35M of CEO compensation actually paid).

On the insider data specifically: no open-market purchases across roughly 25 transaction groups from April to July 2026, with sales by the CEO (monthly cadence, $65.72–$75.55), CFO, Chief Accounting Officer, and four directors — including a 112,422-share director sale at $90.00 on July 9, the day of the competitor's failure. We do not infer a view of intrinsic value from this. The metronomic cadence is consistent with pre-established 10b5-1 plans (unverified, since Form 4 footnotes were not read), several director exercises were at strikes of $8.45–$29.00 consistent with near-expiry mechanics, and executives hold enormous unrealized equity for which diversification is rational. What it does establish is the absence of any offsetting conviction signal at $65–90 — relevant when the incentive metric is revenue rather than profit or cash flow.

**Management quality: 4.5.** Offsetting the above, the preferred was well executed — a >100% conversion premium, no maturity, no holder redemption — and the convert program has been managed with capped calls on two tranches. The $500M repurchase authorization announced in May while burning ~$200M per quarter with negative book equity is a capital-allocation question; execution status beyond the ~$82.5M January repurchase is unverified.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 results, expected early August 2026 (last reported May 7), then the encaleret PDUFA and the infigratinib NDA acceptance.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **First evidence of operating leverage.** Q2 product revenue at or above ~$205M *combined with* a sequential narrowing of operating loss below ~$85M. Revenue growth alone is not sufficient — the specific failure of recent quarters is that revenue gains have been fully absorbed by SG&A.
2. **A better entry.** A decline into the low-to-mid $60s (where the stock traded in June) without fundamental change would put the peak-sales case at a genuine discount and would shift risk/reward decisively, given the Street's low target of $80.
3. **Structural de-risking.** Confirmation that the 2027 Notes are retired without a material cash drain or marketed equity raise, plus disclosure that the preferred's certificate of designations does not carry change-of-control or consent provisions that constrain strategic flexibility.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Attruby sequential revenue growth below ~8% for two consecutive quarters — the plateau signal that would break the peak-sales bridge to the current price.
2. Operating cash burn sustained above ~$200M per quarter through Q3 2026 accompanied by announcement of a further capital raise.
3. Disclosure that the Funding Agreement royalty steps to 10% of global net sales in 2027 while product revenue run-rate is below ~$1.2B.
4. A clinical or regulatory setback on two of the three pipeline filings (encaleret, BBP-418, infigratinib), which would collapse the platform leg of the valuation.

**Reference scenarios:** bull $130 (toward the $157 Street high), bear $50–55 (−31% to −37% from $79.88). The Street's mean of $105.26 (+32%) and median of $101.00 (+26%) represent a successful-execution case rather than a base case we would underwrite at this price.

## Conclusion

BridgeBio is a genuine commercial success story attached to one of the more encumbered capital structures in large-cap biotech. Attruby's ramp is real, the competitive field just got materially easier with eplontersen's failure, and three additional filings could convert this into a rare-disease platform within twelve months. Those facts support high growth and competitive-position scores and a constructive twelve-month view.

At $79.88 the equity capitalizes that outcome. The company carries $2.505B of convertible principal, ~$893M of royalty obligations priced at roughly 18–20% with a possible doubling of the acoramidis royalty rate in 2027, a $934M senior preferred whose non-economic terms remain unread, and a ~$2.27B stockholders' deficit — while operating loss has been essentially flat year over year and operating cash outflow widened to $197.3M in Q1. Next-year consensus EPS of $0.57 implies ~140x, and the Street's low target sits exactly at spot. The July 9 windfall has been fully round-tripped.

We hold. The growth thesis is intact and the catalysts are dated, which is why this is a growth hold rather than an uncertain one. We would want either the first quarter of demonstrated operating leverage or a price in the low-to-mid $60s before adding, and we would not chase into the Q2 print with ±10.2% implied and four consecutive EPS misses behind it.
