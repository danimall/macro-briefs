**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

STERIS is a structurally advantaged infection-prevention and sterilization franchise that has spent three years simplifying its portfolio (divesting Dental and CECS), repairing its balance sheet (debt-to-total-capital down to 21.1% from 25.2%), and rebuilding earnings (net income from $107M in FY23 to $615M in FY25, with $565M already booked in the first nine months of FY26). The crown jewel is Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST), a ~20%-of-revenue segment running ~46% operating margins on +11% growth with mid-single-digit pricing power. Recurring consumables and service revenue dominate the mix, demand is largely non-discretionary, and the company generates roughly $1B of free cash flow annually.

The stock has fallen 13.6% over the period covered here and sits 23% below its 52-week high of $269.44, closing at $206.29. Fundamentals did not break during that decline; revenue grew 9.3% over nine months and free cash flow rose 13%. The drawdown reflects multiple compression and sector rotation rather than a fundamental fracture, which improves the entry math: the stock now trades at 17.0x forward earnings versus a trailing 26.0x and versus a historical premium multiple that prevailed near $250-269.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. Earnings momentum is decelerating, not inflecting. STERIS missed consensus EPS in the two most recent quarters (-1.6% and -1.1%) after beating in the prior two, and consensus revisions are running at +0.5% to +0.7% on the current year and slightly negative on next year, an order of magnitude below the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. There is no dated catalyst inside six months that would force a re-rating, management has lagged its medical-device peer set on five-year total shareholder return, and insiders are net sellers (small, routine, but no open-market buying to anchor a bottom). The valuation discount is real and supports owning the stock, but a BUY requires a trigger for the multiple to re-expand, and none is identifiable on the current calendar.

## Company Overview

STERIS is an Irish-domiciled global leader in infection prevention, structured around three segments after exiting Dental (May 2024) and CECS (April 2024):

- **Healthcare (~71% of revenue; $3.07B over 9M FY26, +8.9%; ~24.5% operating margin):** consumables, capital equipment (sterilizers, washers, surgical tables and lights, automated endoscope reprocessors, OR integration), maintenance, scope/instrument repair, and outsourced sterile-instrument reprocessing for hospitals, ASCs, and endoscopy suites.
- **Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST) (~20%; $849M over 9M FY26, +11.1%; ~46% operating margin):** outsourced contract sterilization (ethylene oxide, gamma/cobalt-60, X-ray, e-beam) and lab testing for medical-device and pharma manufacturers. Capital-intensive, recurring, regulated, and sticky.
- **Life Sciences (~10%; $426M over 9M FY26, +8.4%; ~42% operating margin):** capital equipment, consumables, and services for biopharma aseptic manufacturing.

The competitive moat rests on an entrenched installed base that pulls through recurring consumables and service revenue, regulatory and validation complexity that raises switching costs (especially in AST), a technology-neutral sterilization network, and scale across the testing-to-sterilization value chain. No single customer represents ≥10% of any segment's revenue. The fiscal year ends March 31; references to FY2026 mean the year ending March 31, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (9M FY26, ended December 31, 2025):**
- Total revenue $4,347.5M, +9.3% YoY; constant-currency organic growth +8.2%, broad-based across all three segments and driven by both volume and pricing.
- Q3 FY26 revenue $1,496.2M, +9.2%; diluted EPS $1.96 versus $1.75.
- Gross margin 44.4% (9M), roughly flat YoY. The bridge: favorable pricing (+120bps) and productivity (+60bps) offset by tariffs (-90bps) and inflation (-80bps). STERIS is repricing against a live tariff headwind, which demonstrates pricing power but caps near-term margin expansion.
- Operating income $785.0M (9M), +20.7%, helped by sharply lower restructuring charges ($3.1M versus $33.1M).
- Net income $564.8M (9M). The multi-year recovery (FY23 $107M, FY24 $378M, FY25 $615M) reflects portfolio simplification plus operating leverage.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- 9M FY26 operating cash flow $1,006M (+13%); free cash flow $737.6M. FCF conversion is strong and earnings quality is high on a cash basis, though GAAP earnings are depressed by ~$200M/yr of intangible amortization from the acquisitive history.
- Total debt ~$1.90B against $424M cash; debt-to-total-capital 21.1%. Interest expense fell to $45.6M from $69.1M as term loans were retired with Dental proceeds.
- Current ratio ~2.5x; total equity $7.17B.
- Caveat: goodwill ($4.23B) plus intangibles ($1.69B) equal ~56% of $10.6B total assets, a legacy of Synergy and Cantel. No current impairment indicators, but the acquired-asset base is large.

**Capital allocation** is disciplined and shareholder-friendly: an annually raised dividend ($180M / $1.83 per share over 9M FY26), $150M of buybacks with a larger authorization announced in May 2026, and a shift to small tuck-in M&A ($23M over 9M) after the deleveraging phase. The financial profile justifies a high financial-health score: strong cash generation, conservative leverage for the business model, and improving coverage.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth of +8.2% is solid for a defensive healthcare-infrastructure business, and it is broad-based: Healthcare +8.9%, AST +11.1%, Life Sciences +8.4%. Structural tailwinds support durability: aging demographics, rising procedure and endoscopy-screening volumes, outsourcing of sterilization by device and pharma manufacturers, and tightening infection-prevention standards. AST is the standout, compounding double digits at ~46% margins with mid-single-digit pricing, and it deserves more weight than its 20% revenue share implies because it is both the best growth engine and the highest-margin profit pool.

The forward picture is one of steady mid-to-high-single-digit growth, not acceleration. This matters for the recommendation. Consensus puts FY26 EPS at $11.16 and FY27 at $12.11, roughly +8.5% earnings growth. Estimate revisions are mildly positive on the current year (+0.5% over 90 days) and slightly negative on next year (-0.6%), well short of the magnitude that would signal an inflection. The earnings-surprise record reinforces this: two beats (FQ1, FQ2 FY26: +2.4%, +4.0%) followed by two small misses (FQ3, FQ4: -1.1%, -1.6%). The misses are minor and do not indicate deterioration, but they remove the "consistent beat" tailwind that would lift confidence in upward estimate drift. This is a durable grower, not a stock at an earnings inflection point, and the growth score reflects that distinction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $206.29, STE trades at 17.0x forward earnings ($11.16 current-year, $12.11 next-year consensus) and 26.0x trailing. The forward multiple is the relevant one given the steady earnings trajectory and the amortization drag on GAAP figures. On forward earnings, this is the cheapest the stock has been relative to its own recent history; it closed at $252.13 on December 31, 2025 and traded above $260 as recently as January 2026, so the drawdown from the 52-week high is meaningful multiple compression against fundamentals that continued improving.

The peer benchmark provided (BridgeBio, a loss-making clinical-stage pharma) is not comparable; STE's true comp set is medical-device and life-science-tools names. Against that cohort, a defensive infection-prevention compounder with ~$1B FCF, ~46%-margin AST, and 21% leverage at 17x forward is reasonable-to-attractive: a discount to where high-quality, recurring-revenue healthcare infrastructure typically trades when growing high-single-digits with strong cash conversion.

The case against a higher valuation score is the absence of a mispricing trigger. The decline appears driven by sector rotation and de-rating rather than a fundamental error the market is making. A 17x forward multiple on ~8.5% earnings growth implies a PEG around 2.0, which is full, not cheap, on growth-adjusted terms. The asymmetry has improved (analyst targets of $225 low, $269 median, $256.86 mean, $280 high all sit well above the current price, implying 9% to 36% upside), but consensus targets here are not stale "floors" the way they are on a sub-30x inflection stock; they reflect the same steady-grower thesis and have been drifting lower (sentiment tagged "degrading"). The valuation supports owning the stock; it does not by itself justify a BUY without a catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

STERIS's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The integrated infection-prevention platform plus the AST network is difficult for competitors to replicate at scale. In AST, the principal competitor is Sterigenics (Sotera Health), whose far larger EO-litigation losses frame STERIS's contained exposure favorably while also marking the EO modality as an industry-wide regulatory overhang. In Healthcare capital equipment and endoscopy/instrument care, STERIS competes with Getinge, Belimed, and Olympus-adjacent players.

The trajectory signals are constructive: AST margins are expanding on mid-single-digit pricing, recurring revenue dominates the mix, and switching costs are high in regulated sterilization. The modality transition away from EO toward X-ray and e-beam is a genuine strategic effort but carries execution risk; STERIS impaired an internally developed X-ray accelerator in its FY2024 restructuring, a reminder that the transition is not frictionless. Net, the company is holding or gaining share in structurally growing, non-discretionary markets, supporting an above-average competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Ethylene oxide litigation — the signature idiosyncratic risk, but contained.** STERIS reached binding settlements (October 29, 2025) to pay up to $48.2M (~$43.2M still accrued at December 31, 2025) to resolve substantially all pending Cook County EO personal-injury claims tied to a Waukegan/Lake County, Illinois facility (operated 2005-2008). No liability admission, walkaway rights retained, and the amount is ~5% of one year's FCF. The residual concerns are future claims at other facilities and the durable industry-wide regulatory risk to the EO modality, which the entire device-sterilization industry depends on.

2. **Irish tax domicile exposure.** Effective tax rate is rising (24.0% over 9M FY26 versus 22.4%) on geographic mix. The under-appreciated item is an IRS Notice of Deficiency (November 2023) over 2018 deemed-dividend/withholding matters carrying ~$50M potential liability with no reserve established, contested in U.S. Tax Court. Not material to financial position but capable of denting a single period. OECD Pillar Two and the risk of U.S.-domestic-corporation treatment are longer-tail overhangs.

3. **Tariffs and input-cost inflation** are currently compressing gross margin 80-120bps, partly offset by pricing. A watch item if trade policy escalates.

4. **AST supply concentration** in EO and cobalt-60 (limited global gamma supply) plus energy intensity, against an X-ray/e-beam transition that has already produced one impairment.

5. **Goodwill/intangible impairment risk** given that ~56% of total assets are acquired intangibles, and **capital-equipment cyclicality** in Healthcare tied to hospital capex.

The risk score sits at the low end of "average" because the EO and IRS items are specific and partially uncovered, but neither is existential and the EO settlement frames the tail favorably.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** STERIS is a defensive compounder trading at a multi-year-low forward multiple after a rotation-driven drawdown. AST keeps compounding double digits at ~46% margins; pricing offsets tariffs; FCF runs ~$1B with a deleveraged balance sheet funding a raised dividend, an expanded buyback, and tuck-in M&A. If the multiple normalizes toward a higher forward multiple on $12.11 FY27 EPS, the stock approaches the analyst median target near $269, implying ~30% upside.

**Bear case.** Growth is decelerating, not inflecting; two consecutive small misses and flat-to-negative forward revisions argue the easy operating-leverage gains from restructuring and deleveraging are behind the company. Tariffs persist, the EO regulatory overhang and the uncovered ~$50M IRS exposure cap sentiment, and five-year cumulative TSR has lagged peers (~$169.5 versus ~$211.1 per the proxy's indexed $100 base). In that scenario the multiple stays compressed near 16-17x on roughly flat estimates, and the stock holds the $200-210 range or drifts toward the $195 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**: a positive 12-month total-return thesis (mid-single-digit earnings growth plus a 1.2% dividend, with optional multiple re-expansion) but no near-term catalyst to justify a BUY today.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings (early August 2026) and the May 2026 buyback execution pace are the natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year EPS revisions accelerate to +5% or more over 90 days, or the company returns to consistent beats (two consecutive quarters beating by >3%), confirming reaccelerating momentum.
- AST organic growth sustains >10% with margins holding ≥45% while gross-margin compression from tariffs reverses (pricing/productivity net positive for two quarters).
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, or aggressive buyback execution that materially shrinks share count at current prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth decelerates below 5% YoY, or a guidance cut.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment, an adverse Tax Court ruling materially exceeding the unreserved ~$50M, or new EO litigation at additional facilities.
- AST margins compress below ~42% on EO regulatory action or modality-transition execution failure.

## Conclusion

STERIS is a high-quality, defensively positioned infection-prevention franchise with a repaired balance sheet, strong free-cash-flow generation, and a genuinely advantaged AST segment. The decline over the period and the drawdown from the 52-week high reflect multiple compression and rotation, not fundamental deterioration, which makes the risk/reward more attractive than it was at $250-260. At 17x forward earnings with analyst targets 9-36% above the current price, the downside appears reasonably contained.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete: the stock is a steady grower priced at a PEG near 2.0, with decelerating earnings momentum, no dated catalyst inside six months to re-rate the multiple, management TSR that has lagged peers over five years, and insiders selling rather than buying. The de-rating supports owning the stock for its compounding and yield, but a BUY requires a trigger for multiple expansion that is not currently visible on the calendar. We rate STE a HOLD with a constructive bias and would upgrade on reaccelerating revisions, sustained AST margin strength, or evidence of aggressive capital return at these prices.
