## Executive Summary

American Electric Power is undergoing a strategic transformation from a traditional regulated utility into a high-growth infrastructure platform positioned at the center of the AI/data center electricity demand surge. The company's 63 GW incremental load pipeline backed by signed agreements — representing expected load growth through 2030 — combined with the nation's largest transmission network (40,000+ line miles including 2,100+ miles of 765-kV ultra-high-voltage lines) creates a growth profile unprecedented for a utility of this scale. Q1 2026 earnings of $1.64 operating EPS (+6.5% YoY) demonstrated strong execution, and commercial load in the T&D segment grew 33.3% YoY, confirming the demand thesis is translating to revenue.

The substantial senior leadership overhaul, drawing heavily from Berkshire Hathaway Energy and Sempra, signals a board-level strategic reset. The $78B five-year capital plan (raised from $72B just four months prior) with line of sight to $10B+ beyond, targets ~11% annual rate base growth and >9% operating earnings CAGR through 2030. At ~21.0x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance ($6.30), AEP trades at a slight premium to peer median P/E (20.0x) while offering growth 2-3x the sector average. The primary risks are execution on the unprecedented capex plan, balance sheet tension near credit rating thresholds, heavy reliance on external financing, and concentration in data center demand that could face slowdown or cancellation.

## Company Overview

American Electric Power, founded in 1906 and headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, serves 5.6 million customers across 11 states through its regulated utility subsidiaries. The company operates through four primary segments: Vertically Integrated Utilities (generation, transmission, and distribution), Transmission & Distribution Utilities (AEP Texas in ERCOT and AEP Ohio in PJM), AEP Transmission Holdco ($17.1B in-service assets), and Generation & Marketing (~922K competitive retail accounts).

AEP owns approximately 25,400 MW of generation capacity. By net generation output, the mix is approximately coal (43%), natural gas (22%), nuclear (19%), and renewables (16%). Authorized ROEs across jurisdictions range from 9.25% to 10.50%, with FERC-regulated transmission assets earning 10.35-10.50% — providing superior returns on the company's fastest-growing segment.

The company is materially dependent on external capital markets — including debt issuance, equity issuance, asset sales, and partnership transactions — to finance its growth plan.

## Growth Analysis

AEP's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Data Center Demand (Primary Driver):** The incremental load pipeline of 63 GW through 2030 — backed by signed agreements with well-capitalized industrial customers, hyperscalers, and data center developers — represents a transformational opportunity (up from 56 GW four months ago). Seven GW of new load agreements were signed in Q1 2026 alone. The SoftBank/DOE 10 GW data center campus in Ohio — with approximately $33B in associated generation — exemplifies the scale of individual projects. AEP has indicated it would provide significant new electric infrastructure to support this development, though specific investment figures remain subject to regulatory approval.

41 GW of the 63 GW pipeline is in AEP Texas, where timing is highly dependent on generation supplied by third parties. The 63 GW figure represents expected incremental customer load, not AEP-owned or contracted generation capacity — AEP will not necessarily build or own generation equivalent to that entire load figure.

The Ohio data center tariff (PUCO-approved July 2025) requiring 85% minimum capacity commitment over 12-year terms reduces risk on this pipeline by converting speculative demand into committed revenue streams. Similar tariff proposals have been filed in eight jurisdictions, with four approved and four pending as of March 2026. The pipeline reduction from 30 GW to 5.7 GW after tariff implementation (in Ohio alone) confirms that remaining contracted load represents genuine, creditworthy demand. However, regulatory, counterparty, timing, and generation-supply risks remain — particularly for the Texas pipeline where AEP does not control generation buildout.

**2. Transmission Investment:** $33B of the $78B capex plan (42%) is dedicated to transmission. AEP's 765-kV expertise — unmatched in the US — positions it to win RTO-awarded interstate projects across SPP, PJM, and MISO. New 765-kV project awards occurred in Q1 2026. Transmission earns the highest authorized ROEs (10.35-10.50% at FERC) and faces less state-level regulatory friction than distribution rate cases.

**3. Rate Base Growth:** The company targets ~11% annual rate base growth through 2030, approximately double the utility sector average of 5-6%. This mechanically supports >9% earnings CAGR given regulatory lag dynamics and premium transmission returns.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates show modest positive revisions (+0.8% over 90 days to $6.37; +1.0% for FY27 to $6.88). These are stable, not accelerating — consistent with a utility where growth is highly visible but incremental surprises are modest given regulated structures.

## Competitive Landscape

AEP's competitive position is differentiated across three dimensions:

**Transmission Infrastructure Moat:** The largest US transmission network is physically irreplaceable. New transmission permitting takes 5-10+ years. AEP's existing rights-of-way, engineering expertise, and RTO relationships create durable barriers. No other utility can serve the geographic and technical scope of transmission projects AEP competes for.

**Geographic Positioning:** AEP's 11-state footprint spans Ohio (PJM), Texas (ERCOT), Virginia, Indiana, and other data center growth corridors. This diversity provides multiple growth vectors while limiting single-state regulatory concentration.

**Regulatory Innovation:** The Ohio data center tariff structure sets a national precedent that reduces demand risk and creates up to $16B in cost offsets for existing customers. This is a replicable model across jurisdictions.

**Competitive Risk:** Vistra (VST at 70.5x P/E) and other power generators compete on the supply side for data center contracts, but AEP's regulated utility model provides certainty of cost recovery that merchant generators cannot match. The regulated model is attractive to hyperscalers seeking reliable, long-term power.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Data Center Demand Concentration (HIGH):** With the vast majority of the 63 GW pipeline driven by data centers, any slowdown in AI/hyperscaler buildout directly impacts AEP's growth thesis. The 10-K explicitly warns that demand may not materialize "as projected or be sustained as projected." The 12-year minimum commitment tariff structure substantially reduces risk on contracted projects in approved jurisdictions, but does not eliminate regulatory, counterparty, timing, or stranded-asset risk across the full pipeline.

**2. Execution Risk on $78B Capital Plan (MODERATE-HIGH):** This plan is approximately equal to AEP's entire market capitalization and represents the largest investment program in the company's history. Supply chain disruptions, labor shortages, tariff-driven cost inflation on imported equipment, and regulatory approval delays are all plausible impediments. The tariff risk factor is new to the 10-K and reflects current trade policy uncertainty.

**3. External Financing Dependence (HIGH):** AEP requires continuous access to debt and equity markets. A credit downgrade would increase borrowing costs across the $78B program and could impair market access. The KKR partnership model provides one mitigation path, but sustained equity issuance will dilute shareholders.

**4. Regulatory Recovery Risk (MODERATE):** Rate case outcomes across 11 states are inherently uncertain. The Pirkey Plant partial disallowance in Texas demonstrates that regulators can and do deny full recovery. However, AEP's track record of constructive regulatory outcomes and the customer-benefit structure of data center contracts reduce this risk relative to typical utility capex.

**5. Rising Interest Rates (MODERATE):** With 30Y above 5% and the ERP compressed, utility stocks face headwinds from duration competition with risk-free rates. AEP's growth profile partially offsets this, but a prolonged high-rate environment increases both borrowing costs and the discount rate applied to regulated cash flows.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term ATM IV of 20.1% versus 17.9% historical volatility represents a modest 2.1pp premium — the market is pricing slightly elevated uncertainty following earnings. The term structure is in normal contango (20.1% → 23.9%), and put/call OI of 0.48 indicates no unusual put demand. IV percentile at 3% suggests the options market views current vol levels as very low relative to recent history. This is consistent with a utility stock where the fundamental story is well-understood and no binary catalyst is imminent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($155-$169):**
- Data center demand materializes at or above contracted levels, with the 63 GW pipeline proving conservative
- Transmission awards accelerate as RTO planning catches up to demand growth
- Multiple re-rates toward 22-24x as market recognizes sustained >9% earnings CAGR — approaching growth-utility multiples seen in Entergy or NextEra
- KKR partnership model replicated across other transmission assets, providing non-dilutive capital
- Texas SB6 implementation/rulemaking rollout (summer 2026) unlocks accelerated interconnections for the 41 GW Texas pipeline
- FY27 EPS at 22-24x = $151-$165

**Bear Case ($115-$120):**
- AI/data center buildout decelerates; hyperscalers postpone or cancel capacity commitments
- Credit rating downgrade increases borrowing costs by 25-50 bps across the $78B plan
- Multiple state regulatory commissions deny full recovery on accelerated capex
- Tariff-driven cost inflation adds 10-15% to capital program, compressing earned ROEs below authorized levels
- 30Y rates persist above 5.5%, compressing utility valuations sector-wide
- Stock re-rates to 17-18x forward = $117-$124

## Conclusion

AEP offers a rare combination in the utility sector: visible, agreement-backed growth materially above peers with a durable competitive moat in transmission infrastructure. The new management team's BHE pedigree, the 63 GW load pipeline, and the $78B capex plan create a credible path to ~$7 EPS by FY27 and $8+ by FY29. At ~21x current earnings — slightly above peer median but with 2-3x the growth rate — the stock offers favorable risk/reward for investors willing to underwrite execution risk on a large-scale capital program.

The primary concerns — external financing dependence, balance sheet tension, data center demand concentration, and execution on an unprecedented investment program — are real but manageable given AEP's regulatory cost recovery mechanisms, the risk-reducing tariff structures requiring long-term minimum commitments, and the KKR partnership model for non-dilutive capital. The ~2.87% dividend provides downside support. This is a **BUY** for investors with a 12-18 month horizon seeking infrastructure-driven growth within a regulated utility framework.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** AEP is materially negative on free cash flow after growth capex and dependent on external financing; the 63 GW pipeline represents expected incremental load (not AEP-owned capacity) with 41 GW in Texas dependent on third-party generation; and tariff protections reduce but do not eliminate demand/stranded-asset risk — these factors collectively introduce more execution and financing risk than the original thesis conveyed.
