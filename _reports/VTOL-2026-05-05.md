## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is the world's largest helicopter services provider, serving offshore energy producers and government agencies across 15 countries. The company has completed a multi-year transformation from post-bankruptcy losses to $129M in net income (FY2025), with two specific catalysts ahead: Government Services margin normalization (operating margins recovering from a depressed 1% toward 15%+ as contract transitions complete) and offshore contract repricing at substantially higher rates through calendar 2026.

At ~11x trailing and forward earnings with a $1.3B market cap, the stock is trading at a steep discount to OFS peer medians (54x) and below all three analyst targets ($56-$66). The discount partially reflects two consecutive quarterly EPS misses and a seasonal Q1 2026 FCF shortfall. However, the misses were driven by tax normalization and one-time debt extinguishment costs rather than operational deterioration — underlying Adjusted EBITDA of $59M was in-line. With offshore production activity structurally supported, Bristow's production-levered (vs. exploration-levered) business model provides relative insulation from oil price volatility above $40/bbl.

The primary risks are customer concentration (top 3 = 36% of revenue), supply chain constraints on S92 parts, and geopolitical exposure in Nigeria (13% of revenue). Options markets are pricing acute near-term stress (80% IV vs 32% HV, steep backwardation), likely reflecting the recent earnings miss and thin liquidity in a $1.3B market cap name.

## Company Overview

Bristow Group provides helicopter transportation services primarily to offshore oil and gas platforms (66% of revenue) and government search-and-rescue programs (26% of revenue). The company operates 214 aircraft across the Americas, Europe, Africa, and Asia-Pacific, holding 12 Air Operator Certificates. Following its 2019 Chapter 11 emergence and 2020 merger with Era Group, the company has been managed by CEO Christopher Bradshaw since 2014.

**Revenue Mix (FY2025, $1.49B):**
- Offshore Energy Services: $990M (66%) — personnel transport to/from production platforms
- Government Services: $379M (26%) — UK SAR, Ireland Coast Guard, Netherlands, Dutch Caribbean
- Other: $121M (8%) — Airnorth regional airline, dry-leasing, Humberside Airport

The government services segment provides long-duration, utility-like cash flows (10+ year contracts) that partially offset offshore energy cyclicality.

## Growth Analysis

**Two distinct growth engines:**

1. **Offshore Contract Repricing (2026-2027).** Approximately half of Bristow's Offshore Energy contracts reset in 2026. Leading-edge rates are significantly above legacy contract rates due to historically tight helicopter supply (limited new builds during 2014-2021 downturn, military order crowding, 24-month delivery lead times). Even without increased offshore activity, contract repricing alone drives mid-single-digit Offshore Energy revenue growth and high-single-digit margin expansion.

2. **Government Services Margin Normalization.** This segment generated just $5M operating income in FY2025 (1% margin) due to $28M in elevated subcontractor costs for UKSAR2G/IRCG transitions. With the IRCG contract fully operational as of February 2026 and final UKSAR2G base transitions completing in late 2026, management's guidance implies Government Services Adjusted Operating Income of $70-80M — a $65-75M improvement from the depressed FY2025 level. This is the single largest near-term earnings catalyst.

**Longer-term optionality:**
- NATO defense spending commitments (5% GDP by 2035) create demand for outsourced SAR and military support services
- Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) — deposits on BETA, Electra, and Vertical Aerospace aircraft (2029-2030 delivery, terminable if certification fails)
- Offshore wind support services in early stages

Revenue growth guidance of 6-13% for FY2026, combined with operating leverage and Government Services margin normalization, implies EBITDA growth of 26-39%. This translates to 25-45% EPS growth (from $4.32 to ~$5.43-$6.28).

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow operates in a structurally favorable competitive environment:

**Supply constraints create pricing power.** The global offshore helicopter fleet is aging, new deliveries are limited by military order priority, and lead times exceed 24 months. S92 parts shortages (29% of Bristow's fleet) ground aircraft industry-wide. This tight supply allows operators to price at premium rates during contract renewals.

**High barriers to entry.** Air Operator Certificates in 12 jurisdictions, safety certifications from major oil companies, pilot training infrastructure, and fleet investment create durable barriers. New entrants cannot acquire these capabilities in under 3-5 years.

**Scale advantages.** Bristow's 214-aircraft fleet provides operational flexibility (repositioning aircraft between markets), maintenance efficiencies, and the ability to serve multinational customers with consistent standards globally. Smaller operators cannot match this global reach.

**Government Services differentiation.** Long-term SAR contracts (10+ years) with sovereign counterparties require demonstrated safety records, specialized equipment (night vision, winch systems), and operational track records that few competitors can provide.

Contract repricing captures the supply scarcity premium, government services expansion diversifies away from pure oil dependence, and the fleet investment cycle is creating modern assets that competitors haven't matched.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration:** Top 3 customers = 36% of revenue, top 10 = 63%. Loss of any major customer would be material, though long-term contracts (3-10+ years) provide visibility.

**Geopolitical Exposure:** Nigeria contributes ~13% of revenue in a politically unstable environment. Brazil and Trinidad have capital controls affecting cash repatriation. Trade/tariff uncertainty has been cited as an emerging risk by management.

**Supply Chain Risk:** S92 parts shortages create both opportunity (pricing power) and operational risk (grounded aircraft, penalty clauses). Management is managing this by accelerating AW189 acquisitions as alternatives.

**Earnings Volatility:** Two consecutive misses (-56.5% and -31.7% vs estimates) have damaged near-term credibility. Tax rate normalization and one-time items were the drivers, but the market is unlikely to extend full credit until Bristow delivers a beat.

**COO Government Services Retirement:** Alan Corbett's April 2026 departure creates execution risk during the critical margin normalization period.

**Foreign Ownership Regulations:** Complex regulatory structure (FAA 75% U.S. ownership, EEA/UK requirements for European operations) limits strategic flexibility and potential acquirers.

## Conclusion

Bristow Group presents an attractive risk/reward at ~9x forward earnings with identifiable catalysts (Government Services margin normalization, offshore repricing cycle) and structural tailwinds (tight helicopter supply, elevated oil prices, NATO spending commitments). The stock's discount to peers and analyst targets reflects near-term credibility damage from two EPS misses, but the underlying operational trajectory remains intact — revenue guidance was affirmed, and both transition-cost headwinds are scheduled to subside through calendar 2026.

Position sizing should reflect the small-cap illiquidity ($9M ADV), oil price binary risk, and elevated near-term options pricing. A 2% position with disciplined monitoring of the thesis invalidation triggers captures the asymmetric upside while managing downside exposure. Insider selling (CEO at $47.51, CFO at various prices) is a cautionary data point but not disqualifying given retained positions exceeding $13M.
