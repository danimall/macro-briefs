**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola enters mid-2026 in better operating shape than at any point in the prior twelve months. Q1 2026 delivered organic revenue growth of 10% (8% volume, 2% price/mix), a reversal of the flat-volume malaise that defined FY2025, alongside operating-margin expansion to 35.0% and EPS of $0.91 (+18%). North America unit-case volume turned positive (+4% versus -1% in FY2025), Greater China grew 8%, and the foreign-exchange line flipped from a 2% 2025 headwind to a 3% Q1 tailwind. The business is reaccelerating off what now looks like a 2025 trough (5% organic, the lowest in four years).

The investment question is whether the price compensates for a business that is plainly good, with a ~62% gross margin, ~29% structural operating margin, 38% ROE, and a 64th consecutive annual dividend increase. At $79.93 the stock trades at roughly 24.5x the $3.27 current-year consensus and 23x next year's $3.48, near its 52-week high of $84.04 and at a modest premium to the peer median P/E of 23.2. The dividend yields ~2.7%, below the peer median of 2.9% and well below PepsiCo's 4.1%. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (+1.0% over 90 days on the current year), which means the market has already priced the reacceleration. KO is a quality compounder that is fully valued.

Two idiosyncratic overhangs cap conviction. The IRS transfer-pricing dispute over 2007–2009 has already cost a $6.0B deposit, carries only a $520M reserve, and implies potential aggregate exposure of ~$14B across 2010–2025 if the methodology is upheld, a binary that the October 2025 8th Circuit reversal in the analogous 3M case improved but did not resolve. Separately, Henrique Braun assumed the CEO role in early 2026 with James Quincey moving to Chairman, an orderly but unproven transition. We rate KO a HOLD for income, with upside contingent on a favorable 11th Circuit ruling rather than on multiple expansion from current levels.

## Company Overview

Coca-Cola is the world's largest total-beverage company, with trademarked products sold in more than 200 countries and roughly 2.2 billion of the world's ~65 billion daily beverage servings. The economic model is asset-light and two-tiered:

- **Concentrate operations** (~59% of revenue, ~85% of volume): KO sells high-margin concentrate and syrup to independent and consolidated bottlers under an incidence-based pricing model. This is the profit engine behind the ~62% gross margin and ~29% operating margin.
- **Finished-product operations** (~41% of revenue, ~15% of volume): consolidated bottling (Bottling Investments), Costa retail, and finished-goods sales, at materially lower margins (~7–12% operating).

The brand portfolio spans Trademark Coca-Cola, sparkling flavors (Sprite, Fanta), water/sports/coffee/tea (Costa, BodyArmor, Powerade), juice and value-added dairy/plant (fairlife, Minute Maid, Simply), and emerging categories (energy via the Monster relationship; alcohol via a firewalled subsidiary). Reporting segments are EMEA, Latin America, North America (~41% of revenue), Asia Pacific, and Bottling Investments. The moat rests on brand equity, the global bottler distribution network, pricing power, and marketing scale (~$5.4B advertising in 2025).

The ongoing refranchising strategy, selling capital-intensive bottling to partners while retaining the high-margin concentrate economics, structurally lifts margins and ROIC. The most recent example is the agreement to sell a portion of Africa bottling operations to Coca-Cola HBC (~$5.2B held for sale, close expected end-2026), following the Nigeria finished-products sale (October 2025) and refranchising of India, Philippines, and Bangladesh territories.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Net revenue was $47.9B (+2% reported, ~+6% organic). Volume was flat at 33.8B unit cases, concentrate +1%, price/mix +4%, with FX a 2% headwind. GAAP operating income jumped 38% to $13.76B and margin to 28.7%, but this is flattered by the absence of 2024's $3.1B fairlife contingent-consideration remeasurement charge; the underlying improvement was more modest. Gross margin expanded to 61.6%. Net income reached $13.1B (+23%), with ROE of 38.3% and ROA of 12.5%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow of $7.4B and FCF of $5.3B (an ~11% FCF margin) were artificially depressed by the $6.1B fairlife final earnout paid in March 2025. Normalized FCF run-rate is roughly $11–12B. Cash plus short-term and marketable securities stood at ~$15.8B; long-term debt at $42.1B; debt-to-equity at ~3.06. The Altman Z-Score of 1.66 is a model artifact: it penalizes KO's deliberately leveraged, low-asset-turnover staples model and should be disregarded for a company rated A+/A1 with a 64-year dividend-growth record. Leverage at this level is normal for a consumer-staples balance sheet engineered to maximize ROE and is not a stress signal.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $2.12/share for 2026, the 64th consecutive annual increase (~$8.8B paid in 2025, ~2.7% yield). Buybacks are modest (~$634M net in 2025, largely offsetting dilution); KO prioritizes the dividend and bolt-on M&A over repurchases. Dividend coverage against normalized FCF is comfortable.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $12.47B (+12%), organic +10% (8% volume, 2% price/mix), aided by a 3% FX tailwind and 6 extra selling days (which inflated concentrate +8% versus unit cases +3%). Operating margin expanded to 35.0% from 32.9%; EPS of $0.91 (+18%). North America volume turned positive (+4%), Asia Pacific +5%, Greater China +8%. The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one in-line print over the last four quarters, with surprises in the 1.5–2.9% range, a company that delivers in a tight band rather than a serial sandbagger.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has genuinely improved, and the trajectory data confirms it: the prior growth-prospects read moved from 5.5 to 6.0, and Q1 2026 validates that direction. The 2025 organic figure was the lowest in four years (versus ~12% in 2024 and 16% in 2022). Q1's print, with volume returning to growth, is the clearest evidence that 2025 was a cyclical and FX trough rather than structural decay.

Management guides FY2026 to 4–5% organic revenue and 8–9% comparable EPS growth, consistent with the long-term algorithm. We treat this as credible given Q1's start and the FX reversal, while noting Q1 was helped by 6 extra selling days that will not recur evenly through the year. The durable growth drivers are: pricing power (price/mix has carried volume for several years), EM penetration (Greater China and Asia Pacific), and the asset-light refranchising that mechanically lifts reported margins.

The constraint on the growth score is that none of this is accelerating beyond the long-term algorithm in a way the market has not priced. Estimate revisions are flat to marginally positive. This is the profile of a steady mid-single-digit revenue compounder with high-single-digit EPS growth, not a stock where consensus is systematically lagging. The strong-buy signal that would attach to large positive estimate revisions is absent here; this is a 6/10 growth profile, durable but not explosive.

The structural demand threat from GLP-1/weight-loss drugs reshaping sweetened-beverage consumption is a slow-burn risk that has not yet shown up in volume. It is a genuine multi-year question rather than a near-term growth input, and KO's portfolio diversification (water, dairy, coffee, zero-sugar) provides partial insulation.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the peer set, KO's ~24.5x forward multiple is a modest premium: PepsiCo at 22.6x, Keurig Dr Pepper at 23.2x, and Monster at 44.5x, for a peer median of 23.2x. KO commands roughly a 1.3-turn premium to the median, justifiable on its superior moat, margin profile, and EM exposure, but it is not a discount.

The dividend yield sits below the peer median and well below PepsiCo's, which is relevant because PEP trades 16% below its 52-week high while KO sits near its high. For an income-oriented buyer, PEP offers more absolute yield support today.

On the valuation framework, the relevant question is whether the market is mispricing the stock. The evidence says no. Estimate revisions are flat, the stock is within ~5% of its 52-week high, growth is in line with the long-term algorithm rather than inflecting, and the forward multiple already embeds the Q1 reacceleration. This is not a cyclical trough where trailing P/E understates earnings power; KO's earnings are not depressed, so the cycle-adjustment logic that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple does not apply. A premium multiple on in-line growth, near a high, with no rising-estimate catalyst, is a fair-value-to-slightly-rich setup.

The one path to a re-rating is the IRS case: a favorable 11th Circuit ruling would return the $6.0B deposit plus interest and remove a multi-billion-dollar tail, which could support a modest multiple expansion. That is a binary and externally timed catalyst, not a reason to pay up today. We score valuation 5.0: not a value trap, not stretched into absurdity, but offering no asymmetric upside at the current price. A pullback toward the low-$70s (the analyst low target of $71.38, ~22x forward) would materially improve the risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

KO's competitive position is stable-to-improving, which supports a 7/10. The company claims 20+ consecutive years of overall value-share gains, and Q1's North America volume turnaround suggests it is defending and extending share against private label and value brands rather than ceding it. The refranchising strategy improves ROIC and lets KO concentrate capital on brand-building and the high-margin concentrate layer.

The portfolio shows a clear bifurcation in M&A execution: fairlife is a quiet winner (the $6.2B final earnout reflects outperformance in value-added dairy), while BodyArmor is a disappointment ($1.72B cumulatively written off, including $960M in Q4 2025, against a remaining carrying value of $2.44B with fair value now approximately equal to carrying value, so further impairment is plausible). The BodyArmor stumble against Gatorade and Prime shows KO is not invincible in adjacent categories, but it is a small fraction of the enterprise.

The structural moat from brand equity, global bottler distribution, pricing power, and marketing scale remains intact and is the reason KO sustains ~29% operating margins. The principal long-term competitive/structural threat is the health-and-regulatory vector (GLP-1 demand effects, sugar taxes, SNAP exclusion proposals, ingredient scrutiny), which is more an industry headwind than a KO-specific share loss.

## Risk Assessment

**IRS transfer-pricing litigation (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** The dispute over 2007–2009 tax years has required a $6.0B deposit (parked in other noncurrent assets). The Tax Court sided with the IRS; KO is appealing to the 11th Circuit (reply brief filed August 2025). KO carries only a $520M reserve, reflecting its "more likely than not" view of prevailing, a view strengthened by the 3M reversal. The exposure is asymmetric: a win returns the deposit plus interest and removes the overhang; a loss implies potential aggregate additional tax and interest of ~$14B for 2010–2025 and a ~3.8%/year increase in the effective tax rate going forward. This is the single most important swing factor and the primary reason the risk score is below 6.

**CEO transition.** Henrique Braun signed the Q1 2026 10-Q certifications as CEO, with John Murphy continuing as President & CFO. The handover appears orderly and well-telegraphed, but a new CEO at a mega-cap introduces execution and strategic-continuity uncertainty until a track record is established.

**FX exposure.** $28.8B of 2025 revenue is international; a strong dollar cut 2025 operating income by 12%. The Q1 2026 tailwind is welcome but volatile, and EM currencies (Argentine peso, Turkish lira, Brazilian real, Mexican peso, Indian rupee) remain a source of quarter-to-quarter noise.

**Health and regulatory.** GLP-1 demand effects, obesity scrutiny, potential SNAP exclusion of sugary drinks, sugar taxes, and U.S. "childhood chronic disease" initiatives (ingredient scrutiny on synthetic colors and HFCS) are explicitly cited risk factors. These are slow-moving but structurally adverse to the core sparkling franchise.

**Other.** Bottler concentration (one bottler at ~10% of revenue), BodyArmor impairment risk, packaging/plastic regulation, and private-label trade-down round out the risk set.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 31.7% versus 16.8% historical, a rich 14.9-point spread at the 73rd percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (31.7% near-term versus 22.2% six-month): the market is pricing imminent near-term stress, with the 1-week implied move modest at ±0.5%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.6%) and put/call volume and OI ratios sit below 1.0 (0.67 and 0.68), so there is no pronounced bearish tilt; the elevated near-term IV reflects event positioning rather than a directional bet against the stock. Unusual activity included a large $79 put block (3,264 contracts, 4.8x OI) for late-June expiry, consistent with short-dated hedging around the current price. The ATM IV jump from 21.1% to 31.7% since early May on a roughly flat price indicates rising near-term uncertainty, not a fundamental signal. None of this overrides the fundamental read; it argues for patience on entry rather than chasing into elevated short-dated vol.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Q1's reacceleration proves durable: organic growth holds in the 4–5% range, volume stays positive in North America and EM, margins continue expanding via refranchising, and FX remains a tailwind. The 11th Circuit rules in KO's favor (the 3M precedent supports this), returning the deposit plus interest and removing the ~$14B tail, a catalyst for modest multiple expansion. The dividend compounds at high-single digits. Under this scenario the stock re-rates to ~26x the next-year estimate, or roughly $90, near the analyst high target of $92.

**Bear case.** The IRS appeal is lost, crystallizing multi-billion-dollar liability and a structurally higher tax rate (~3.8%/year drag on EPS), while GLP-1 effects begin to bite volumes and FX reverts to a headwind. The CEO transition introduces missteps. The stock derates toward 21x trailing earnings, or roughly $70 (the analyst low of $71.38), an ~11% decline from current levels.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-modestly-unfavorable from the current price: ~13% upside to the bull case against ~11–12% downside to the bear, with the IRS ruling the dominant binary in both directions. With estimates flat and the stock near its high, there is no fundamental catalyst forcing a re-rating absent the tax outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** Own KO for its ~2.7% growing dividend, defensive cash flows, and the optionality on a favorable IRS ruling, not for near-term appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July, testing whether the Q1 reacceleration holds ex the 6-extra-selling-days benefit) and any docket activity on the 11th Circuit appeal.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable 11th Circuit ruling that returns the deposit and removes the tail, materially de-risking the multiple.
- A pullback to the low-$70s (~22x forward, near the $71.38 analyst low), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters of organic growth at the high end of guidance (≥5%) with sustained positive North America volume and rising consensus estimates on the forward year.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse 11th Circuit ruling crystallizing material IRS liability and a structurally higher effective tax rate.
- Organic revenue growth decelerating below 3% with North America volume turning negative again, signaling the Q1 reacceleration was transitory.
- A further BodyArmor impairment combined with broader portfolio M&A missteps under the new CEO.

## Conclusion

Coca-Cola is a high-quality, wide-moat compounder showing a real operating reacceleration off a 2025 trough, with returning volume and margin expansion. The dividend record (64 consecutive increases) and balance-sheet strength make it a legitimate defensive holding. The constraint is price: at a premium forward multiple, near a 52-week high, with flat estimate revisions and a yield below the peer median, the stock is fairly valued to slightly rich and offers little asymmetric upside. The IRS transfer-pricing case is the dominant swing factor, favorably tilted by the 3M precedent but unresolved, and the CEO transition adds execution uncertainty. The trajectory of declining overall scores (6.7 → 6.6) reflects the offsetting improvement in growth against the rising idiosyncratic risk profile, and the current analysis confirms a hold rather than a reversal in either direction. We rate KO a HOLD for income, with a clear path to a more constructive view on either a favorable tax ruling or a pullback toward the low-$70s.
