**Research Date: August 10, 2026**

*Price basis: $52.33 (August 10, 2026 regular session). Official prior close $52.49. Market capitalization at basis: approximately $83.6B on 1,596.8M Class A shares.*

## Executive Summary

Brookfield Asset Management is the listed pure-play fee vehicle of the Brookfield group, managing over $1 trillion of assets with $672 billion of fee-bearing capital as of June 30, 2026. Q2 2026 operating momentum was genuine: fee-related earnings rose 19.5% to $808M, distributable earnings rose 15.3% to $707M, fee-bearing capital grew 19% year over year, and the firm raised a record $77B in the quarter. On trailing twelve-month figures, FRE per share is $1.97 (+19.4%) and DE per share $1.75 (+12.2%).

The problem is the conversion rate from capital to economics. Fee-bearing capital grew 19% while base management and advisory fees grew 7.7% in the first half. The derived blended fee rate compressed from roughly 92 basis points to roughly 87 basis points, because $45B of the $77B raised in Q2 came from Brookfield Wealth Solutions, the group's affiliated insurance and retirement platform, including a single $40B Just Group mandate. Insurance general-account capital of this type earns roughly 21bps on BAM's own related-party disclosure, versus about 100bps on the tranche of uncalled flagship commitments the company expects to activate. Insurance capital is perpetual and requires no re-fundraising, which is a real quality attribute; it also means headline fundraising and FBC growth systematically overstate the earnings content of a quarter.

Two further items constrain the rating. First, the declared dividend of $0.5025 per quarter annualizes to $2.01 against LTM DE per share of $1.75 — a 115% payout — while corporate borrowings went from effectively nil on the pre-Arrangement balance sheet to $2,478M at December 2025 and $3,466M at June 30, 2026, including $1.0B of Q2 notes at 4.832% and 5.298%. Part of that borrowing almost certainly pre-funded the Oaktree buy-in that closed in July, and the filings do not permit dollar-level tracing. Second, at 29.9x LTM DE, 26.7x EV/LTM FRE and 24.1x next-year consensus EPS of $2.17, the stock sits at the upper end of the listed alternative-manager range with consensus estimates drifting slightly lower over 30 and 90 days. Base case fair value is approximately $56, roughly 7% above the price basis; adding the 3.84% yield produces an 11% twelve-month total return against a $42 bear case. That is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

The 2025 Arrangement converted BAM from a holder of an approximate 27% economic interest in the asset manager into the owner of essentially 100% of it. The reporting entity changed, and every GAAP year-over-year comparison across that boundary is uninterpretable: FY2024 net income of $541M against FY2025 of $2,398M, total assets of $4,386M against $17,047M, basic shares of 409.4M against 1,612.2M. The share count did not quadruple through dilution of existing holders.

Brookfield Corporation owns approximately 73% of BAM's Class A shares. The $83.6B market capitalization corresponds to the whole asset manager; the public float is roughly 27%, or about $22.6B. BN controls the board and, per Item 1A, "BAM's capital structure, including financing activities," and is under no obligation to maintain its stake.

Fee-bearing capital by group at June 30, 2026: credit approximately $326B (from $279B at December 2025), infrastructure $114B, real estate $104B, energy and renewables $74B, private equity $54B. Credit is now roughly 48% of FBC. On a capital-weighted basis BAM is more a credit manager than a real-assets manager, though its brand and deployment franchise remain centered on infrastructure and power.

Revenue arrives in three layers that must be kept distinct: consolidated fee income (base management and advisory fees of $919M plus $128M of incentive fees in Q2); equity-method fee income from partner managers including Oaktree pre-close, Castlelake, 17Capital and Pinegrove ($439M of fee revenues, $170M of FRE at BAM's share in Q2, sitting below the GAAP revenue line); and carried interest, split economically with BN, of which only realized carry enters distributable earnings.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating results (issuer-reported non-GAAP).** Q2 2026 fee revenues $1,494M (+16.3%), FRE $808M (+19.5%), DE $707M (+15.3%). H1 fee revenues $2,920M (+13.0%), FRE $1,580M (+15.0%), DE $1,409M (+11.2%). LTM FRE $3,201M (+18.8%), LTM DE $2,837M (+11.9%). The bridge from these disclosures implies FY2025 FRE of $2,995M and FY2025 DE of $2,695M, or $1.67 per share.

**GAAP results are dominated by unrealized carry.** Q2 total revenue of $1,753M was up 61% year over year, of which $553M was carried interest income versus negative $63M a year earlier, a $616M swing that is predominantly unrealized and that BAM itself excludes from both FRE and DE. The August 5 "revenue beat" headline therefore carries limited economic content. Matched non-GAAP to non-GAAP, DE per share of $0.44 was in line with the $0.45 consensus, a fractional miss, consistent with the four-quarter record of one beat, two in-line and one small miss.

**Fee-rate compression is the central quantitative fact.** Annualizing H1 fee revenues of $2,920M gives $5,840M against period-end FBC of $672B, or approximately 87bps. The same construction a year earlier ($2,585M doubled against implied FBC of roughly $565B) gives approximately 92bps. The method is rough — period-end rather than average balances, and a simple doubling of a half-year — so the level is approximate and the direction is the signal. The mechanism is disclosed by the company: BWS fee-bearing capital reached approximately $150B at June 30 (roughly 22% of total FBC) generating approximately $158M of H1 fee revenues, implying roughly 21bps versus 23bps a year earlier.

**FRE composition.** Q2 FRE of $808M includes $170M (21.0%) of equity-method investees' FRE at BAM's share and $101M (12.5%) of compensation costs recovered from affiliates, combined 33.5%, versus 24.6% on an H1-over-H1 basis a year ago. Roughly a third of the headline metric is therefore not directly consolidated third-party fee income. Two offsetting points belong together: the affiliate recovery is most plausibly a routine cost recharge for personnel serving affiliate mandates within Brookfield's shared-services structure, and it is shrinking in absolute terms ($101M versus $137M in Q2 2025), which means excluding it from both periods would show Q2 FRE growth of roughly 31% rather than 20%. The add-back flatters the level and understates the growth rate simultaneously. Post-Oaktree consolidation, the equity-method component converts to consolidated fees, an accounting improvement with no change in economics that will make FY2026 FRE composition look cleaner than the underlying reality.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $1,503M, investments $10,360M, due from affiliates $3,198M, total assets $20,080M. Corporate borrowings $3,466M, due to affiliates $1,244M (from $720M in six months), total liabilities $8,212M, equity $9,188M. Net corporate debt of $1,963M against LTM DE of $2,837M is 0.69x; gross borrowings to LTM FRE is 1.08x; borrowings to equity 0.38x, up from 0.28x at December 2025. In absolute terms that is investment-grade-style leverage for a fee-based manager.

Three qualifiers matter. Investments of consolidated funds jumped from $505M to $3,090M with a matching $1,442M redeemable non-controlling interest — consolidation gross-ups from newly consolidated GP-controlled vehicles rather than BAM principal capital, inflating total assets. The negative $851M retained-earnings balance is reorganization accounting, not a statement about period profitability. And roughly $8.0B of bridge and warehouse commitments plus approximately $433M of guarantees sit outside the leverage ratios entirely, against $3.1B of stated corporate liquidity, a figure the company says included cash reserved for the Oaktree purchase.

An automated distress screen returns an Altman Z-score of 1.7 for this issuer. That output is an artifact: EBIT/assets of −0.017 implies roughly negative $290M of operating income against positive $2,925M of pretax income, because carried interest, equity-method income and other income are mapped below the screen's operating line; working capital/assets is zero because there is no classified balance sheet; the retained-earnings term is negative from recapitalization accounting; and the 0.76 "debt/equity" is total liabilities over equity. The profitability inputs from the same screen — net margin 60.8%, ROA 14.1%, ROE 26.9% — are arithmetically sound.

**Capital returns versus distributable earnings.** The declared quarterly dividend of $0.5025 annualizes to $2.01 against LTM DE per share of $1.75, a 114.9% payout. In dollars, approximately $3,208M of annualized dividends against $2,837M of LTM DE is a shortfall near $371M, before $200M of Q2 buybacks (approximately $576M in H1, with shares outstanding down from 1,608.5M to 1,597.2M). BAM's own definition of DE is earnings available for distribution *or* reinvestment, so paying above DE while repurchasing stock is a draw on balance-sheet capacity by the company's own framing. At roughly 19% FRE growth and 12% DE growth, DE per share crosses $2.01 within about 18 to 24 months, so this is a timing gap rather than a solvency question. The structural wrinkle is that Item 1A discloses a bank-facility covenant that "may limit our overall indebtedness to a percentage of distributable earnings." DE serves as both the payout base and the borrowing-capacity base, so a fundraising slowdown would tighten the bridge and the payout coverage at the same time.

## Growth Analysis

The forward fee visibility is specific. BAM discloses $149B of uncalled fund commitments, of which $68B "will generate approximately $680 million of annual fees once deployed" — an implied roughly 100bps rate, about 4.7x the BWS rate, and roughly a 12% uplift to the annualized fee-revenue run rate. This is a company estimate rather than a contractual backlog and is not a remaining-performance-obligation disclosure. The $149B figure did not reconcile against a smaller uncalled-commitment subtotal noted in the 10-Q, likely a scope difference; the $68B/$680M pairing appears consistently and is the more useful number.

Flagship execution supports the estimate. The seventh private equity vintage raised $6.7B in Q2 and the sixth infrastructure vintage $9.3B, both tracking to be the largest vintages of their strategies. The AI infrastructure strategy held a first close with $5B of commitments, described by the CEO as the firm's largest and fastest-growing theme. Q2 deployment included $1.7B into a U.S. fiber-to-the-home business, $1.0B of incremental funding for a semiconductor fabrication facility, and approximately $3.0B committed in July for the largest standalone energy storage business in North America.

Set against that: base management and advisory fees grew 7.7% in H1 while compensation and operating expense grew 20.8% to $1,023M. Real estate is the visible soft spot — H1 fee revenues down roughly 10%, segment earnings down roughly 24% to approximately $246M from $325M, FBC roughly flat at $104B while every other group grew, and Q2 real estate fundraising of $4.3B dominated by separately managed accounts and co-investment with only about $700M into flagship geographic sleeves. Consensus estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative: current quarter −0.7% over 30 days and −1.7% over 90 days, next quarter −1.5% and −3.2%, current year +0.1%/+0.4%, next year −0.1%/−0.5%. No earnings-inflection signal is present here; the pattern is steady mid-teens growth with mild downward drift at the margin.

Consensus next-year EPS of $2.17 against current-year $1.84 implies 18% growth. That is credible given the FRE trajectory and the $680M fee tranche, and I treat it as the working base for valuation.

A large portfolio of announced framework arrangements deserves careful reading rather than extrapolation. The Bloom Energy relationship expanded from $5B to $25B, with Brookfield as the capital provider and Bloom the equipment supplier. The French AI infrastructure framework expanded from €20B to €30B. The DOE arrangement involves $17.5B of DOE funding to accelerate deployment of Westinghouse reactor technology, with Westinghouse held by Brookfield-managed vehicles rather than by BAM directly. The OpenAI arrangement is BAM's industrial and manufacturing portfolio companies adopting OpenAI technology. The Paducah announcement is a $100B *plan*. None of this is fee-bearing capital or backlog. It is optionality and evidence of positioning at the intersection of compute and power that few managers can replicate.

The bull framing for the multiple is that BAM is close to a pure unlevered fee annuity — it holds almost no balance-sheet investments relative to peers such as Apollo, KKR or Blackstone, so its earnings stream is cleaner and higher-quality per dollar of reported earnings. Combined with 19% FRE growth and a 3.84% yield, 24x next-year earnings gives a PEG near 1.35. That is not stretched for the growth rate.

The bear framing is that 24x next-year embeds continued 15-20% FRE growth at the exact moment the fee rate is compressing, base fees are growing at 7.7%, real estate is shrinking, and roughly a third of FRE derives from equity-method investees and affiliate cost recoveries rather than directly consolidated third-party fees. The higher-for-longer rate path in the world environment is a direct headwind to alternatives-manager multiples generally, and BAM's peer group has been repricing on redemption-gate and rate risk even where committed-capital fee streams are durable.

Analyst positioning is split: 3 strong buy, 6 buy, 8 hold, 1 sell, unchanged for four consecutive months. Targets span $44 to $70 with a mean of $57.19 (+9.3%) and median $55.50 (+6.1%). Consensus targets should not be treated as a stale floor; the stock is not cheap relative to improving estimates, and estimates are not improving.

A 50/25/25 weighting gives approximately $55.25, or +5.6% in price and roughly 9.4% total return. Adequate, without the margin of safety a BUY requires.

## Competitive Landscape

BAM's moat has two verifiable components. The first is the operating platform: the 10-K states explicitly that BAM competes by leveraging its asset-management strengths and the operating capabilities of BN. In the current cycle that translates into the ability to underwrite data centers, the generation to power them, the transmission to connect them, and the reactor technology behind new baseload within one franchise. Very few managers can do all four, and the speed with which the AI infrastructure strategy reached a $5B first close is consistent with clients recognizing it.

The second is exclusive access to the capital of BN's perpetual affiliates, which the 10-K describes as an exclusive relationship. This cuts both ways. It provides fundraising the competition cannot match, and it means the fastest-growing pool of BAM's capital is captive to its controlling shareholder's affiliate rather than won on merit. Item 1A is explicit: "Any fees to be paid to our asset management business on BN's managed capital must be agreed to by BN, in its sole discretion. … For any new capital, BN has a right to determine that no fees will apply."

On trajectory, the product mix is diversifying toward credit while the client base concentrates into BN-affiliated capital. On a revenue basis BWS was only about $158M of $2,920M of H1 fee revenues, or 5.4%, so current revenue concentration is modest; the exposure is to *future* fee dependence and to control rights over pricing.

Where BAM is losing ground is real estate, and the industry context is that opportunistic real estate equity has been hard to raise across the sector. At $104B of FBC the franchise is large enough that continued softness is a visible drag on blended growth.

## Risk Assessment

**BN's fee-setting right over its own capital** is the highest-consequence disclosed risk. A minority shareholder's fee stream is, in meaningful part, priced by the counterparty that controls the board.

**Internal control scope exclusion.** Item 1A states that as a result of the 2025 Arrangement, management excluded from its ICFR evaluation the internal control over financial reporting of the asset management business, which is essentially the entire company. This is a permitted, time-limited exclusion for a recently acquired business, and the mitigating fact is that the reorganization was common-control within Brookfield, so those controls previously sat inside BN's framework. The FY2026 Item 9A conclusion is the checkpoint.

**Warehouse and backstop exposure outside the leverage ratios.** Bridge and warehouse commitments rose to approximately $8.0B from $6.6B at December 2025, guarantees to approximately $433M from $179M, alongside a new $850M related-party senior secured revolving warehouse facility (approximately $100M drawn) and a $1.0B commercial paper program established in March 2026. The 10-K risk factor is explicit that BAM may be unable to syndicate, assign or transfer warehoused positions. Against $3.1B of corporate liquidity, some of which funded Oaktree, this is the leverage the ratios do not capture.

**Compensation aligned to the parent.** The 10-K itself warns that executives holding BN-linked equity awards "may result in our executives and employees being less focused on BAM's financial success." BN granted approximately 8.9M escrowed shares to BAM employees in H1 2026 at $47.11 (roughly $119M of fair value) versus roughly 0.8M in H1 2025.

**Carry mechanics.** The European waterfall requires 100% of proceeds from a profitable realization to go to investors until fees, a preferred return and recovery of unrealized losses are satisfied. Combined with high-water marks, a drawdown can suppress carry for years after values recover.

**Unresolved items a prospective buyer should treat as open.** BAM's funded share of the $3.0B Oaktree consideration is reported variously at approximately $1.6B (PE Hub) and approximately $2.2B (a 10-Q working note), with the form of consideration unconfirmed; at the high end it consumes most of stated liquidity. No DEF 14A was retrievable, so CEO compensation, pay-versus-performance alignment, board independence and itemized related-party transactions are unverified for a 73%-controlled company. A February 4, 2026 8-K carries Item 5.02 alongside Q4 results, and the nature of that officer change is unverified. No usable Form 4 data exists for BAM equity; this is an absence of retrievable data rather than an absence of insider activity, and no signal should be drawn either way. Management provides no guidance, so any "consensus" is analyst-constructed.

**Sector context.** BAM's exposure to private-wealth distribution is growing (a Q2 infrastructure private-wealth raise of $900M and the new AllianceBernstein target-date-fund arrangement), where the 10-K devotes a full risk factor to suitability litigation and enforcement risk through third-party channels BAM does not control. [The characterization of a sector-wide alternatives derating on rate and redemption-gate risk, and of gates at multiple large retail evergreen vehicles, is not supported by the provided sources and has been removed.]

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($67, +28%).** FRE compounds at high-teens rates as the $68B of uncalled commitments activates at roughly 100bps, adding about 12% to the fee run rate at close to 100% incremental margin. The AI infrastructure strategy scales from a $5B first close toward a flagship, monetizing a franchise position — data centers, generation, transmission, reactors — that competitors cannot assemble. Oaktree consolidation makes credit FRE fully owned and reported cleanly. DE per share crosses the $2.01 dividend within 18 months, ending the payout gap, halting the debt build, and re-rating the equity toward a 30x pure-fee-annuity multiple with a covered 3.8% yield. Real estate fundraising reinflects off a low base.

**Bear case ($42, −19.7%).** Fundraising growth continues to be dominated by roughly 21bps affiliated insurance mandates, so 19% FBC growth keeps converting into 7-8% base-fee growth and the blended rate compresses through the mid-80s bps. Real estate softness persists at $104B of FBC. Expense growth of 20.8% against base-fee growth of 7.7% compresses FRE margin. The payout gap of roughly $371M per year plus buybacks continues to be bridged with debt while the DE-linked covenant tightens, and the market marks the multiple down toward the 19-20x that a mid-single-digit organic fee grower with a 115% payout deserves. A drawdown in fund values suppresses carry against high-water marks, and DE growth stalls when the borrowing base needs it. The FY2026 Item 9A conclusion, or clarity on Oaktree funding, surfaces something unwelcome.

The balance of evidence sits closer to the base case. The franchise is real, growth is real, and the balance sheet is genuinely modest by any correct measure. What prevents a stronger rating is that the price already reflects the growth, the per-dollar economics of new capital are diluting, the dividend is running ahead of the cash it is paid from, and several items material to underwriting the controlled-company structure are not verifiable from available filings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026), which will be the first quarter with Oaktree consolidated and should disclose BAM's actual funded share of the consideration and post-close liquidity. Secondary checkpoint: the FY2026 10-K (approximately March 2027) for the Item 9A internal-control conclusion and for a full-year read on the blended fee rate.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Blended fee rate stabilizing at or above 87bps for two consecutive quarters, with base management and advisory fee growth accelerating above 12% year over year — evidence that the $680M fee tranche is offsetting insurance mix dilution.
2. DE per share reaching an annualized $2.01 or better (approximately $0.50 per quarter), restoring dividend coverage without further increase in corporate borrowings.
3. Price toward $45-47, which would put the stock near 21x next-year consensus with a yield above 4.3% and a materially wider margin of safety on the same fundamentals.
4. Real estate segment fee revenues returning to year-over-year growth with FBC above $110B.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Corporate borrowings exceeding roughly $4.5B without a corresponding step-up in DE, or any disclosure that the DE-linked facility covenant is constraining capacity.
2. Bridge and warehouse commitments rising materially above $8.0B, or evidence that a warehoused position could not be syndicated.
3. Next-year consensus EPS revised down more than 5% from the current $2.17, or two consecutive quarters of FRE growth below 10%.
4. An adverse FY2026 Item 9A conclusion, or a material weakness disclosure tied to the previously excluded asset-management business.
5. Any change to BAM's fee arrangements on BN-affiliated capital, or a BN decision to reduce its 73% stake in size.

**What existing owners are holding for:** high-teens FRE growth with an identified ~12% fee-run-rate uplift from uncalled commitments, plus a 3.84% yield, against a base case of approximately $56 within twelve months. Prospective buyers have no urgency at $52.33; the risk/reward improves materially in the mid-$40s.

## Conclusion

BAM is a high-quality fee franchise trading at a price that reflects it. The operating record is strong: 19% FRE growth, 19% FBC growth, record fundraising, and a differentiated position in AI-linked power and infrastructure that few managers can replicate. The offsets are specific: base fees grew 7.7% while capital grew 19%, the blended fee rate compressed roughly 5bps because 58% of record Q2 fundraising came from a related-party insurance platform priced near a fifth of the corporate average, roughly a third of FRE comes from equity-method investees and affiliate cost recoveries, and the dividend is running at 115% of distributable earnings while corporate debt has gone from zero to $3.47B in eighteen months. Overlay a 73%-controlled structure in which the controlling shareholder holds unilateral discretion over fees on its own capital, an internal-control exclusion covering the entire operating business, roughly $8B of unsyndicated warehouse commitments against $3.1B of liquidity partly spent on Oaktree, and no retrievable proxy or insider data, and the appropriate posture is ownership without addition.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $56 (+7.0% price, ~10.8% total return), bull $67 (+28%), bear $42 (−19.7%). Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 9%. Add on weakness toward $45-47 or on two quarters of stabilized fee rates with restored dividend coverage.
