**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Campbell's is a structurally challenged branded packaged-food company whose reported FY2025 revenue growth of 6% masked an underlying business in volume decline. Strip out the Sovos/Rao's acquisition (+8 pts) and a 53rd week (+2 pts), and the organic base was flat-to-negative. FY2026 has removed any ambiguity: through nine months net sales are down 4%, both segments are down 4%, and Q3 volume/mix fell 5%. The center-of-store franchises that define the company — condensed and ready-to-serve soup, SpaghettiOs, V8, Pace, and most of the Snyder's/Lance/Cape Cod snack portfolio — are losing volume to private label and premium competitors. U.S. soup fell 8% in Q3.

The deterioration is now compounding through the income statement. Gross margin compressed roughly 190-200 bps year-to-date (27.5% in Q3 versus 29.4% prior year), driven primarily by tariffs that have escalated from a $0.02 EPS drag in FY2025 to roughly $0.17 year-to-date FY2026, a 310 bps gross-margin hit in Q3 alone. Tinplate steel for cans and imported ingredients from Canada, Mexico, and the EU are the specific exposures, and the magnitude is largely outside management's control. Segment operating earnings are down 18% year-to-date (Snacks -26%, Meals & Beverages -15%). Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 10-19% across all forward periods over the last 90 days, with the negative momentum persisting in the most recent 30 days.

The stock is down 36% over the trailing year and trades at roughly 10x falling forward EPS with a ~7% dividend yield. That valuation looks cheap on a screen, but the combination of a distress-zone balance sheet (Altman Z-score 1.15, debt/equity 2.82, current ratio 0.77, ~$2.59B of trademarks carrying less than 10% fair-value cushion including Rao's and Snyder's), still-declining estimates, an absence of insider buying, and no identifiable re-rating catalyst points to a value trap rather than a value opportunity. We maintain AVOID.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1869 and headquartered in Camden, New Jersey, Campbell's generates roughly $10B in annual revenue across two segments:

- **Meals & Beverages (~59% of sales, $6.05B FY2025):** Campbell's condensed and ready-to-serve soups, Swanson broth, Pacific Foods, Prego, Pace, SpaghettiOs, V8, and, since the March 2024 Sovos acquisition, the premium Rao's pasta-sauce franchise and Michael Angelo's frozen.
- **Snacks (~41%, $4.20B FY2025):** Pepperidge Farm (Goldfish, cookies, fresh bakery), Snyder's of Hanover pretzels, Lance, Cape Cod, Kettle Brand, Late July, Snack Factory.

The company's competitive position rests on strong brand equity in soup, a category it effectively defines, and meaningful snacking scale. Customer concentration is high: the top five customers represent roughly 47% of sales, with Walmart alone at approximately 21%. Snacks distributes through an asset-light direct-store-delivery model relying on roughly 4,500 independent contractor distributors, whose bank loans Campbell's guarantees up to a maximum $570M exposure.

Legacy soup and most snack brands are in structural volume decline, and management has been acquiring growth (Sovos/Rao's for $2.9B) to offset erosion in its core franchises. The operating playbook is now explicit: harvest declining legacy brands for cash, cut costs aggressively, and reinvest behind Rao's and premium positioning.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2025 net sales rose 6% to $10.25B, but the gain was entirely inorganic — roughly +8 points from Sovos and +2 points from a 53rd week, partially offset by divestitures and negative volume/mix and pricing. With Sovos now lapped, FY2026 reveals the underlying trend: nine-month net sales down 4% to $7.61B, Q3 revenue of $2.37B missing consensus of ~$2.39B.

**Margins.** Gross margin compressed to 28.4% year-to-date from 30.4%. Tariffs are the dominant driver — 240 bps of the year-to-date hit. The GAAP operating margin appears negative in screening data because FY2025 EBIT absorbed $176M of trademark impairments and restructuring; normalized segment operating earnings were $1.64B, FY2025 net income was $602M (net margin ~5.9%), and ROE was approximately 15.4%.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.13B and free cash flow roughly $705M (margin ~6.9%). Year-to-date FY2026 operating cash flow slipped to $839M from $872M on lower cash earnings, against capex of roughly $420-426M per year.

**Balance sheet — the binding constraint.** This is where the AVOID case concentrates:
- Altman Z-score of 1.15 sits in the distress zone, driven by heavy leverage and thin/negative working capital rather than imminent insolvency (the company remains profitable and investment-grade).
- Debt/equity of 2.82, total debt of roughly $6.86-7.0B, and a current ratio of 0.77 (negative working capital of ~$395-674M).
- Leverage ballooned to fund the $2.9B Sovos deal; interest expense rose from $188M in FY2023 to $345M in FY2025.
- Goodwill of $4.99B plus intangibles of $4.36B equal roughly 63% of $14.9B total assets, against equity of only ~$3.9-4.0B.
- The company manages liquidity through commercial paper (~$340M) and supplier-finance/extended payables ($253M) — working-capital engineering that becomes a vulnerability if credit conditions tighten or a rating downgrade impairs commercial-paper access.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend of roughly $1.55-1.56 per share (~$459M/year) yields ~7% at the current price. Coverage from free cash flow is adequate today but thinning as earnings fall toward the guided $2.15-2.25 adjusted EPS. Buybacks are minimal and purely anti-dilutive.

**Earnings momentum.** The beat/miss record is mixed (three beats, one miss over four quarters), but the absolute level is falling fast — Q3 adjusted EPS of $0.50 beat a lowered $0.48 estimate but was down from $0.73 a year earlier. The estimate-revision picture is uniformly negative: current-year consensus down 10.3% over 90 days, next-year down 18.8%, with continued cuts in the trailing 30 days. Falling estimates plus a balance sheet under stress is a double-negative signal.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here. The two segments are each declining 4% with the acquisition lapped, and the largest category — soup — fell 8% in Q3. The only genuine share-gainer is Rao's, which is why management acquired 49% of La Regina (the Italian producer of all Rao's tomato sauces) in a $286M vertical-integration deal that closed in May 2026 to secure supply and hedge Italian-tomato tariff and ingredient risk. Rao's is real and valuable, but it is not large enough to offset structural erosion across condensed soup, broth, V8, Pace, pretzels, crackers, and chips.

The forward earnings story is one of cost containment, not growth. Management raised its targeted annual savings from $250M to $375M by end of FY2028 (announced September 2025), has realized $200M program-to-date, and launched a voluntary early retirement program in Q3 FY2026 with most departures by December 2026, plus a salaried pension freeze effective August 2028. These are sensible defensive actions, but cost cuts on a shrinking revenue base produce a lower-quality earnings profile, not durable growth. Consensus reflects this: estimates are being cut across every forward period.

## Valuation Assessment

CPB trades at roughly 10x the $2.17 current-year and $2.09 next-year consensus EPS, with a ~7% dividend yield. Against the Packaged Foods & Meats peer median P/E of 19.5 and median yield of 4.3%, the stock looks cheap on both metrics. The discount, however, is warranted rather than anomalous.

The peer set is itself distressed — Conagra and Smucker show negative trailing P/Es (impairment/charge-driven), McCormick trades at 7.6x, and several peers sit 28-45% below their 52-week highs. The packaged-food complex is being repriced for structural volume decline, tariff cost inflation, private-label share gains, and retailer pricing power. Campbell's is more exposed than most: soup is its largest and most-declining category, its can-heavy packaging maximizes tinplate-steel tariff exposure, and its snack brands are serial impairment candidates.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not help here. Estimates are falling, gross margins are compressing, and the tariff drag is accelerating rather than reversing. A low multiple on falling earnings with an unresolved structural decline is the textbook value-trap configuration, and the ~7% dividend yield is the market pricing dividend-cut risk rather than offering income support. The mean analyst target of $22.06 sits essentially at the current price; the median of $20.00 sits below it. The $56 high target is a stale outlier inconsistent with current fundamentals.

There is a residual point of support: the dividend is covered by FCF today, the company is investment-grade and profitable, and at some price the equity discounts enough of the bad news. We assign a modestly below-neutral valuation score (4.5) rather than a deep-value reading; the stock is cheap, but for identifiable structural reasons with no catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Campbell's competitive trajectory is deteriorating. Private label is growing and price-advantaged precisely as consumers trade down; retailers (Walmart at ~21% of sales) extract supplier-funded promotions; and the company's largest category is in secular decline. The serial trademark impairments — Snyder's of Hanover written down by $150M (now carried at $470M), plus Pop Secret, Late July, and Allied brands — are direct evidence of share and value erosion in snacks. Soup's structural low-growth profile caps the Meals & Beverages ceiling.

The one bright spot is Rao's, a genuine premium-sauce share-gainer whose supply chain is now vertically integrating. Regulatory pressure adds a competitive headwind: the FDA's April 2025 push to phase out petroleum-based synthetic dyes and the MAHA Commission's focus on diet bear directly on a processed/canned-food portfolio. On balance, the company is a market leader losing share in its core, with a single growth asset that is too small to reverse the trend.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs.** The dominant near-term risk and the largest earnings swing factor, accelerating through FY2026. Duration and magnitude are uncertain and largely outside management's control. Tinplate steel for cans is a specific, can-heavy exposure peers do not share to the same degree.

**Impairment overhang.** Structural, not one-off. Management discloses indefinite-lived trademarks with ≤10% fair-value cushion, including Rao's, Snyder's of Hanover, Pace, and Pacific Foods. A 1% WACC increase alone would trigger roughly $95M of Rao's and $65M of Snyder's impairments. With margins compressing, the FY2026 Q4 annual impairment test is a live risk event.

**Leverage and credit rating.** Distress-zone Z-score, negative working capital, and commercial-paper dependence. A downgrade — explicitly flagged in the filings — would raise borrowing costs and impair commercial-paper access at a moment when liquidity is engineered through extended payables.

**Customer concentration and DSD classification.** Retailer leverage is concentrated in the top-five customers; independent-contractor reclassification carries the ~$570M of loan-guarantee exposure.

**Dividend.** Covered by FCF today, but the cushion is thinning as EPS falls toward $2.15-2.25 against a ~$1.56 payout. A cut is not the base case but is no longer remote.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 72.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 29.0% (a 43.7 pp spread, 84th percentile within the current chain) sits in steep backwardation — 72.8% at one week falling to 44.1% at six months — signaling the market expects an imminent event. ATM IV jumped from 40.8% to 72.8% over the prior two weeks. Put/call skew of 8.4% at the front (10.0% at one month) reflects elevated put demand. Short interest is extreme at 32.6% of float with 7.2 days to cover. This configuration confirms the bearish fundamental thesis but flags meaningful squeeze risk that argues against any active short despite the weak fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Tariffs prove transitory and reverse in H2 FY2026, restoring 200-300 bps of gross margin. The $375M cost-savings program and voluntary retirement deliver, stabilizing EPS near $2.20+. Rao's and the La Regina vertical integration drive a premium-mix recovery. At a ~7% covered yield and 10x earnings, the stock re-rates toward peer multiples as sentiment normalizes, with upside toward $28-32. The 32.6% short interest provides squeeze fuel on any positive surprise.

**Bear Case.** Tariffs persist or escalate, pushing FY2027 EPS below $2.00. Soup and snack volume declines continue; the FY2026 Q4 impairment test triggers further Rao's/Snyder's write-downs, denting equity and pressuring the leverage covenants. A rating downgrade raises funding costs and questions commercial-paper access. The dividend cushion thins to the point of a cut, removing the yield support that holds marginal holders. The stock retests the $15 low analyst target, roughly 30% below current levels.

The asymmetry favors the bear case. Estimates are falling, margins are compressing, the balance sheet is distressed, there is no insider buying at multi-year lows, and the only catalysts on the horizon (Q4 impairment test, continued tariff drag) are downside events.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The FY2026 Q4 annual impairment test (likely reported with the year-end 10-K in September 2026) is the cleanest dated downside catalyst, given the disclosed trademarks with <10% cushion and continuing margin compression. Continued tariff escalation and falling estimates provide a secondary, ongoing drag. No single near-term event is large enough to drive a sharp gap lower in the near term.

**Timing window.** The impairment catalyst is roughly 3 months out (September 2026 fiscal year-end reporting). That is a meaningful carry period given a ~7% dividend the short pays and elevated borrow costs.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 32.6% of float with 7.2 days to cover — extreme. This is a crowded short. The thesis is widely shared (Zacks rated it Strong Sell), and any positive surprise (tariff relief, EPS beat, dividend reaffirmation) could trigger a violent squeeze. The recent move from $20.55 to $21.68 alongside surging IV illustrates the dynamic.

**Borrow and options signal.** Front-end IV versus HV and 8-10% put skew confirm the bearish positioning is consensus, which is itself a warning — informed bears are already in. Elevated near-term IV implies a meaningful cost to express the view via options, and the ~7% dividend plus borrow fees raise the cash carry.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear-case retest of $15 implies ~30% downside. Squeeze/upside risk toward $28-32 on any positive surprise implies ~30-48% upside. With 32.6% short interest, the asymmetry does not favor adding to the crowd; reward:risk is roughly 1:1 at best on a mark-to-market basis.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis is correct, but short interest at 32.6% of float, a ~7% dividend carry, and extreme front-end IV make this a poor active short despite the weak business. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the AVOID view):**
- Tariff resolution or a credible legal/trade development that removes the gross-margin drag, with gross margin recovering toward 30%.
- A return to flat-or-positive organic volume in either segment for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the structural decline has stabilized.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (current-year and next-year consensus rising rather than falling), confirming an earnings inflection rather than continued erosion.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by the CEO/CFO, which is conspicuously absent today despite a multi-year-low stock.
- A deleveraging path that lifts the Altman Z-score out of the distress zone and restores positive working capital.

**Timeline.** The structural headwinds (soup/snack volume decline, private-label share gains, retailer power) are multi-year and unlikely to reverse on their own. The tariff drag is the swing variable and could resolve faster if trade policy shifts. We expect the AVOID stance to persist through at least the FY2026 year-end report (September 2026), with the Q4 impairment test as the key reassessment point.

## Conclusion

Campbell's is a structurally declining packaged-food franchise whose headline growth has depended on acquisitions and a calendar quirk while the base business shrinks. FY2026 has stripped away the ambiguity: revenue down across both segments, gross margin compressing, an accelerating tariff drag, and consensus estimates falling. The balance sheet — distress-zone Z-score, 2.82x debt/equity, negative working capital, and trademarks carrying less than 10% fair-value cushion — amplifies rather than cushions the operating deterioration. The ~7% dividend yield reads as the market pricing cut risk, not as income support.

The stock is cheap at ~10x falling forward EPS, but for identifiable structural reasons with no catalyst to close the gap and several downside events ahead. The extreme 32.6% short interest makes it a poor active short despite the weak fundamentals. We maintain **AVOID**, consistent with the deteriorating score trajectory, and would only revisit on confirmed tariff relief, organic volume stabilization, or positive estimate revisions.
