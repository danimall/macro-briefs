## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation delivered FY2025 results that confirmed the post-merger operating model is working: $88.6B revenue (+9.7%), operating margins at 10.5% (highest since the 2020 merger), operating cash flow of $10.6B (+48% YoY), and free cash flow of $7.9B. The $268B backlog — up 23% year-over-year — provides multi-year revenue visibility across all three segments. Q1 2026 extended this trajectory with 10% organic sales growth and 21% adjusted EPS growth, beating consensus by 16%.

The stock has declined 14% from its March highs ($214.50 to $174.26), driven by three converging pressures: (1) the Airbus arbitration against Pratt & Whitney over GTF engine delays, which introduces a quantifiable but bounded legal risk; (2) broader defense sector concern over production bottleneck narratives and tariff exposure; and (3) post-earnings selling despite a beat-and-raise quarter, suggesting the market had priced in an even larger raise. This pullback brings the forward P/E on adjusted EPS to approximately 25x FY2026E, which compares favorably to GE Aerospace (37x) and Howmet (67x) though it trades at a premium to pure defense peers like Lockheed Martin (28x) and Northrop Grumman (23x).

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: secular defense spending growth driven by the current geopolitical environment and Golden Dome funding ($24.4B), a commercial aftermarket cycle that has years of runway as the installed GTF fleet matures, and demonstrated capital discipline (total debt reduced by $4.5B since 2023). Risks are real but bounded: the Airbus arbitration, DPA compliance obligations, and China sanctions each have identifiable downside ranges. The pullback provides a reasonable entry point for a business with this quality of backlog visibility and cash flow generation.

## Company Overview

RTX is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue, operating through three segments:

**Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B, 34% of revenue): Avionics, mechanical systems, interiors, and mission systems for commercial and military platforms. The most diversified segment, with margins of 16.3% (up 170bps YoY), driven by strong aftermarket demand and OE rate increases.

**Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B, 37% of revenue): The GTF engine family powers the A320neo (duopoly with CFM), with the F135 powering the F-35. Margins of 7.9% (up 70bps) remain the lowest of the three segments but are improving as Powder Metal remediation costs decline. The GTF Advantage received EASA certification in April 2026, offering 4-8% more thrust and becoming the production standard by ~2028.

**Raytheon** ($28.0B, 32% of revenue): Missiles (Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk), integrated defense systems (SPY-6), and sensors. Book-to-bill of 1.43x in FY2025, with margins of 11.5% (up 180bps). Backlog of $107B in defense alone.

The revenue mix has shifted toward commercial (48% in 2025, up from 41% in 2023), while U.S. government direct sales are 38% and foreign military sales approximately 15%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue momentum is broad-based and accelerating.** FY2025 organic growth was $8.9B across all three segments. Pratt & Whitney contributed $4.8B (commercial aftermarket up 21%), Collins $2.6B, and Raytheon $1.7B. Q1 2026 continued at 10% organic growth, and full-year 2026 consensus estimates imply $93-95B in revenue.

**Margin expansion has room to continue.** Operating margins reached 10.5% in FY2025, up 240bps YoY. The improvement was driven by volume leverage, declining Powder Metal costs (accrual down from $1.7B to $0.7B), and operational efficiency. Adjusted segment margins are higher: Collins at 16.3%, Raytheon at 11.5%, and Pratt & Whitney at 7.9%. The ~$2B annual amortization of acquisition intangibles depresses GAAP margins by approximately 220bps relative to segment reporting. As this amortization runs off over the next 5-8 years, reported margins will converge toward segment-level profitability.

**Cash flow quality is the strongest signal.** Operating cash flow surged 48% to $10.6B. Free cash flow of $7.9B exceeded GAAP net income of $6.7B, a 118% FCF conversion ratio, driven by the non-cash amortization flowing through the income statement but not affecting cash. The company used $3.35B of this cash for debt reduction, bringing total debt from $42.5B (2023) to $37.9B (2025). Management has guided FY2026 FCF above the $7.0-7.5B initial 2025 target, implying $8B+ is achievable.

**Balance sheet is deleveraging on schedule.** Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.2x, appropriate for a defense prime with investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa1). Both Moody's and S&P revised outlooks from negative to stable in 2025. Upcoming maturities of $3.4B (2026), $2.9B (2027), and $3.5B (2028) are manageable given $7.4B cash, $5.0B undrawn revolver, and $8B+ annual FCF. The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 is misleading: it penalizes the $85B of goodwill and intangibles from the 2020 merger, which bear no relationship to operational distress risk for a company generating $7.9B of free cash flow.

**Earnings surprise pattern is strong.** Four consecutive beats averaging +11.8% surprise, with the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) beating by 16.2%. This pattern supports confidence that current consensus ($6.87 FY2026E adjusted EPS) is achievable and likely beatable, particularly given that estimate revisions for the current year are modestly positive (+2.2% over 90 days).

## Growth Analysis

RTX's growth is driven by three distinct, partially independent engines:

**1. Commercial aftermarket (secular, multi-year).** The installed GTF fleet is growing rapidly and each engine generates recurring MRO revenue for decades. Commercial aftermarket grew 21% in Q1 2025 and continued at a strong pace through FY2025. As the Powder Metal remediation fleet returns to service, these engines require shop visits, generating revenue at higher margins than OE. The GTF Advantage certification expands the addressable market further. This growth channel has at least 5-7 years of runway before the installed base matures.

**2. Defense bookings surge.** Raytheon's 1.43x book-to-bill in FY2025 reflects global missile defense restocking driven by geopolitical tensions, NATO spending increases, and the $156.2B DoD supplemental. The $24.4B Golden Dome allocation and $25.4B munitions funding directly benefit RTX's Patriot, SPY-6, AMRAAM, and Tomahawk programs. The $107B defense backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility that is less sensitive to single-year budget fluctuations.

**3. Margin expansion from mix shift and cost normalization.** Pratt & Whitney margins (7.9%) have significant upside as Powder Metal costs decline (~$0.7B in 2026, converging toward zero by 2027-2028) and the higher-margin aftermarket becomes a larger share of segment revenue. Collins at 16.3% still has room relative to pre-merger UTC levels. Raytheon at 11.5% is improving but below mature defense peer margins.

Consensus estimates project adjusted EPS of $6.87 (FY2026) and $7.53 (FY2027), representing 10-13% annual earnings growth. The estimate revision trend is modestly positive for the current year (+2.2% over 90 days) but slightly negative for next quarter (-2.5%), likely reflecting tariff-related uncertainty in the near term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.26, RTX trades at approximately:
- 25.3x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($6.87)
- 23.1x FY2027E adjusted EPS ($7.53)
- 35.1x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.96)
- 3.4% FCF yield (on $7.9B FY2025 FCF)

RTX straddles commercial and defense aerospace, complicating direct peer comparison. Pure defense peers trade at meaningful discounts: LMT at 28.3x, NOC at 23.1x. Commercial-tilted peers trade at premiums: GE Aerospace at 37.1x, Howmet at 66.7x. RTX's blended exposure (48% commercial, 52% government) justifies a premium to pure defense but a discount to pure commercial. At 25x forward, RTX sits in the reasonable range of this spectrum.

The 3.4% FCF yield is adequate given the growth profile but leaves limited margin of safety if FCF disappoints. The $268B backlog translates to approximately 3x annual revenue in committed orders, providing unusual revenue visibility that partially justifies a growth premium.

The consensus median price target is $222.50, implying 28% upside, with a range of $179-$242. The low target of $179 is only 3% above the current price, suggesting limited downside consensus even among bears.

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms. At 25x adjusted earnings, the market is already pricing in continued margin expansion and double-digit earnings growth. The stock would need to trade closer to 22-23x forward ($151-158) to offer genuine valuation asymmetry. However, the combination of growth quality, backlog visibility, and the 14% pullback from highs makes the current level acceptable for entry rather than compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX holds entrenched competitive positions across its portfolio:

**Pratt & Whitney** is one of two suppliers of narrowbody engines globally (duopoly with CFM/GE Aerospace-Safran). Switching costs are effectively infinite: once an airframe commits to an engine platform, the airline is locked into that engine's MRO ecosystem for 20-30 years. The GTF Advantage certification strengthens the competitive position relative to CFM's LEAP engine.

**Raytheon** holds sole-source or dominant positions in Patriot missile defense (deployed in 17+ countries), AMRAAM, SPY-6 naval radar, and SM-3 ballistic missile interceptors. These positions are being reinforced by the current geopolitical environment, which has accelerated Patriot and NASAMS orders from European and Middle Eastern customers.

**Collins Aerospace** benefits from deep OEM relationships and aftermarket lock-in across avionics, power, and environmental control systems. Its 16.3% margin reflects strong pricing power in a market where switching costs are high and qualification cycles are long.

The competitive position is improving. Defense bookings at 1.43x book-to-bill signal accelerating demand for RTX's core platforms. The company's $7.7B in total R&D (company-funded plus customer-funded) sustains technological barriers. The primary competitive risk is from next-generation defense competitors (Anduril, Palantir) capturing software-defined defense budgets, but RTX's hardware expertise in missiles, radars, and engines remains structurally advantaged.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Airbus Arbitration (High Impact, Moderate Probability).** Airbus launched formal arbitration in March 2026 seeking damages over GTF engine delays that left hundreds of A320neo aircraft grounded and pushed deliveries ~20% below target. The existing accrual of $0.7B may be insufficient if arbitration results in a materially larger settlement. The upside scenario is that the GTF Advantage certification and MRO capacity expansion demonstrate good-faith remediation. A reasonable downside range is an additional $1-3B charge, which would reduce FY2026 GAAP EPS by $0.50-1.50 but would be largely non-recurring.

**2. Deferred Prosecution Agreements.** RTX operates under two DPAs and an SEC administrative order from October 2024 (FCPA violations, defective pricing). An independent compliance monitor is expected to be in place by end of Q1 2026. Any breach could theoretically trigger debarment from U.S. government contracting, an existential risk for a company deriving 53% of revenue from government customers. The probability of actual debarment is very low (historical precedent suggests agencies avoid debarring critical sole-source suppliers), but the compliance costs and operational constraints are real.

**3. Tariff Exposure.** Management cut its 2025 profit outlook citing tariff-related costs on steel and aluminum. While pursuing exemptions and price increases, the net impact is modestly negative to near-term margins. The 38% U.S. government revenue is largely insulated (cost-plus contracts pass through tariffs), but commercial OE and international defense sales face margin pressure.

**4. China Sanctions.** China has sanctioned the Raytheon business and a Collins joint venture, with a fine equal to twice the value of arms sold to Taiwan since 2020. If enforced, this could disrupt RTX's commercial aerospace business in China (Collins supplies systems to Chinese commercial aircraft). The probability of enforcement severe enough to materially affect RTX is low but non-trivial given the Taiwan arms sales pipeline.

**5. Executive Order on Defense Contractor Performance.** The January 2026 executive order authorizing the Secretary of Defense to limit dividends and share repurchases for "underperforming" defense contractors introduces a new governance risk. RTX's share repurchase program is already de minimis ($50M in FY2025), so near-term impact is limited, but this creates a precedent that could constrain capital return policy.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($230, 32% upside):** Defense spending acceleration continues through FY2027+ as geopolitical tensions sustain elevated missile and radar demand. GTF Advantage ramp drives Pratt & Whitney margins above 10% by FY2028. Airbus arbitration resolves within existing accruals or modestly above. Operating margins reach 12%+ as amortization declines and aftermarket mix improves. Adjusted EPS reaches $8.00+ by FY2027, and the stock re-rates to 28x forward = $224-230.

**Bear Case ($150, 14% downside):** Airbus arbitration results in $3B+ additional charges, resetting the Powder Metal remediation timeline. Defense budget uncertainty from procurement reforms or diplomatic de-escalation reduces Raytheon bookings. Tariff costs prove more persistent than management expects. The stock de-rates to 20x FY2026E adjusted EPS = $137-150.

**Base Case ($210, 21% upside):** RTX delivers $6.87-7.00 adjusted EPS in FY2026, FCF of $8.0-8.5B, and the Airbus arbitration results in a modest additional charge ($0.5-1.0B). The stock trades to 28-30x FY2026E adjusted EPS as defense spending visibility improves and margin expansion continues = $192-210.

## Conclusion

RTX's 14% pullback from its March highs provides a reasonable entry point into the world's largest aerospace and defense company at a moment when its core markets — missile defense, commercial aftermarket, and military engines — are in structural growth. The Airbus arbitration and DPA compliance risks are real but bounded, and the valuation at 25x FY2026E adjusted EPS is fair given the growth and quality profile.

The prior BUY recommendation trajectory (HOLD → BUY → BUY) reflects improving fundamentals, particularly in financial health and growth. The decline in risk scores from 6.5 to 5.5 over prior assessments is appropriate given the Airbus arbitration development. The current pullback re-opens valuation room that had been compressed at $205-210. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $210.
