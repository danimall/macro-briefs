## Executive Summary

Kinetik Holdings is a mid-cap integrated midstream company concentrated in the Delaware Basin portion of the Permian, operating gas gathering/processing, crude oil gathering, produced water disposal, and equity interests in major NGL and gas takeaway pipelines. The company trades at ~18x trailing earnings and ~37x FY2026E EPS ($1.28 consensus), yielding ~6.8% on its $3.24 annualized dividend. Revenue grew 19% in 2025 to $1.76B, but adjusted EBITDA grew only 2% to $988M as cost of sales and operating expenses outpaced topline, compressing operating margins from 12.1% to 9.3%.

The 12-month growth thesis rests on three identifiable catalysts: (1) the ECCC Pipeline reaching in-service in Q2 2026, adding ~150 MMcf/d of capacity connecting New Mexico to Texas; (2) the Kings Landing complex ramping beyond its initial 200+ MMcf/d capacity with the Acid Gas Injection project (year-end 2026); and (3) management's guided 2026 EBITDA of $950M–$1.05B (7% growth at midpoint), which if achieved would push the dividend coverage ratio toward the 1.5x target.

The counterweight is substantial. Leverage sits at ~3.9x Net Debt/EBITDA with negative stockholders' equity, $233M in annual interest expense consuming ~24% of EBITDA, and $2.2B of debt maturing in 2028. I Squared Capital has been aggressively liquidating its position ($179M sold in February 2026 alone, ~18M units remaining), creating persistent supply overhang. Earnings have been erratic (644% beat one quarter, 91% miss the next), making forward estimates unreliable. At 11.3% short interest and options pricing implying extreme near-term stress (110.9% near-term IV vs. 37.6% HV), the market is pricing meaningful skepticism about near-term execution.

## Company Overview

Kinetik Holdings was formed through the 2022 merger of EagleClaw Midstream and Altus Midstream, creating the 4th largest natural gas processor in the Delaware Basin. The company operates through two segments:

**Midstream Logistics (~64% of segment Adj. EBITDA):** 4,200+ miles of gas gathering pipeline, 825,000+ HP compression, 2.4 Bcf/d cryogenic processing across 8 complexes, ~280 miles of crude oil gathering, and ~370 miles of produced water gathering with 580,000 bbl/d permitted disposal capacity.

**Pipeline Transportation (~36%):** Equity interests in Permian Highway Pipeline (~55.5%, 2.65 Bcf/d), Breviloba/Shin Oak NGL pipeline (33%, 600+ MBbl/d), plus wholly-owned Kinetik NGL Pipeline System (580 MBbl/d) and Delaware Link Pipeline (1.0 Bcf/d).

The ownership structure is a complex holding company: Kinetik Holdings Inc. owns ~40% LP interest in Kinetik Holdings LP (the operating entity). Blackstone and I Squared Capital hold significant stakes with board designation rights at ≥10% ownership.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Margin Trajectory

Revenue accelerated from $1.26B (2023) to $1.48B (2024) to $1.76B (2025), driven by volume growth (gathered gas +17% YoY, crude +55% YoY) and the Durango ($341M, June 2024) and Barilla Draw ($176M, Jan 2025) acquisitions. Product revenue (NGL, condensate, residue gas sales) accounts for ~74% of total revenue, with service/fee-based revenue at ~25%.

The margin picture is less favorable. Operating margins compressed from 12.7% (2023) to 12.1% (2024) to 9.3% (2025). Cost of sales rose 27% versus 19% revenue growth; operating expenses surged 38% as the acquired asset base required higher utility costs (+$20.5M) and maintenance. Adjusted EBITDA grew just 2% to $988M despite 19% topline growth. This revenue-profitability mismatch warrants close monitoring through 2026.

### Balance Sheet & Leverage

Total debt stands at $3.63B against $7.1B of assets and negative stockholders' equity of $(565M). The negative equity is a structural artifact of the MLP/partnership accounting (accumulated deficit of $806M), but it constrains financial flexibility. Key debt components:

- $1.05B 6.625% Senior Notes due 2028
- $1.00B 5.875% Senior Notes due 2030
- $1.15B Term Loan due May 2028
- $453M drawn on $1.6B Revolver (due 2030)
- $165M A/R Facility (due March 2026, needs renewal)

Net Debt/EBITDA of ~3.9x is within management's target range but above the ~3.5x achieved at year-end 2024. A 100bp rate increase on the ~$1.77B variable-rate facilities would add ~$17.7M annually.

Available liquidity improved to $1.22B (from $661M at year-end 2024) following the May 2025 refinancing.

### Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow was $604M in 2025 versus $637M in 2024. Capital expenditures nearly doubled to $492M (from $264M) driven by Kings Landing construction and organic growth. Free cash flow was approximately $112M.

The company returned $676M to shareholders ($500M dividends + $176M buybacks) against $604M operating cash flow. The shortfall was funded by $504M in EPIC pipeline sale proceeds, $250M in new notes, and net revolver draws. Shareholder returns currently exceed organic cash generation, requiring asset monetization or incremental borrowing to sustain.

Management guides $450–510M capex for 2026, $950M–$1.05B adjusted EBITDA, and targets dividend coverage of 1.5x by year-end 2026 (currently ~1.2x).

### Earnings Volatility

The earnings surprise record is erratic: Q4 2025 beat by 645%, Q3 2025 missed by 91%, Q2 2025 beat by 29%, Q1 2025 missed by 85%. This volatility is driven by one-time items including the $415M EPIC gain and various impairments, making EPS-based valuation inherently noisy. Consensus current-year EPS of $1.28 has been revised down 33% over 90 days.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth thesis has three specific catalysts with defined timelines:

**1. ECCC Pipeline (Q2 2026 in-service).** This pipeline connects Eddy County, NM to Culberson County, TX with ~150 MMcf/d initial capacity. It addresses takeaway constraints in the New Mexico portion of the Delaware Basin where Kinetik has been building its position through the Durango acquisition and Kings Landing complex.

**2. Kings Landing Acid Gas Injection (year-end 2026 in-service).** FID in Q3 2025, this project handles elevated H₂S and CO₂ at the Kings Landing complex, enabling processing of gas streams that would otherwise be uneconomic. This extends the capacity utilization of the 200+ MMcf/d complex that achieved commercial in-service in September 2025.

**3. New Mexico Expansion.** ~70% of 2026 capex ($315–357M) is directed toward New Mexico growth assets, building on the volume ramp from Durango and Barilla Draw acquisitions. Management's EBITDA guidance embeds 7% growth at midpoint, predominantly from these assets.

Beyond the near-term, Kinetik benefits from secular Delaware Basin activity growth. Crude oil volumes grew 55% YoY in 2025 and gathered gas volumes 17% YoY. The company's integrated model (gathering → processing → takeaway) captures multiple fee points per mcf/barrel.

Elevated oil prices ($100+) incentivize Permian Basin drilling activity, which directly feeds Kinetik's gathering systems. Natural gas futures have risen ~11% on Hormuz disruption concerns, benefiting the residue gas revenue component.

Counterbalancing the growth: the portfolio rotation from lower-growth pipeline interests (EPIC, GCX — sold for ~$1B combined) to higher-growth gathering/processing assets increases operating risk and commodity sensitivity.

On the forward estimate of $1.28 FY2026 EPS, the stock trades at ~37x, which is expensive by any midstream standard.

The better valuation framework for midstream is EV/EBITDA. At an enterprise value of approximately $7.2B ($3.3B equity + $3.63B net debt) and guided 2026 EBITDA midpoint of $1.0B, KNTK trades at ~7.2x EV/EBITDA. This is within the 6–8x range typical for gathering-focused midstream companies, neither cheap nor expensive.

The 6.8% dividend yield is substantially above the 3.9% peer median and provides meaningful total-return carry while waiting for the growth thesis to materialize. However, the current ~1.2x coverage ratio and the shareholder return/operating cash flow gap create sustainability questions.

Analyst consensus targets range from $46 (low) to $55 (high) with a $50 median and $50.14 mean across 15 covering analysts. At $47.38, the stock is at the low end of the consensus range, implying ~6% upside to median targets. The 11 Buy / 4 Hold / 0 Sell distribution with recent upgrades from Wells Fargo, Truist, and RBC suggests improving sell-side sentiment.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik occupies a specific niche: the 4th largest gas processor in the Delaware Basin, substantially smaller and more concentrated than diversified peers like Enterprise Products, Energy Transfer, and Kinder Morgan. This concentration cuts both ways.

**Advantages:** Deep infrastructure footprint in the fastest-growing portion of the most productive U.S. basin. Integrated gathering/processing/takeaway model. Equity interests in major takeaway pipelines (PHP, Shin Oak/Breviloba) provide downstream connectivity.

**Disadvantages:** Single-basin exposure means no geographic diversification. Higher commodity sensitivity (~74% product revenue) than large-cap peers who have successfully shifted toward fee-based contracts. Smaller scale limits bargaining power with E&P customers and capital markets. Upstream consolidation (Diamondback/Endeavor, ConocoPhillips/Marathon) could shift drilling activity away from KNTK-dedicated acreage.

Raymond James specifically flagged KNTK as a "realistic takeout target" — its Delaware Basin footprint would be strategically valuable to a larger midstream company seeking Permian exposure. This provides a floor of sorts, though M&A is inherently unpredictable.

## Risk Assessment

### Financial Risks
- **Leverage:** $2.2B of debt matures in 2028. A/R Facility needs March 2026 renewal.
- **Dividend sustainability:** Coverage ratio of ~1.2x has limited margin for error. The funding gap between shareholder returns and operating cash flow depends on continued asset monetization or borrowing.
- **Estimate deterioration:** FY2026 EPS estimate cut 33% in 90 days (from $1.90 to $1.28). FY2027 cut 23% (from $2.62 to $2.01). Estimate revisions are decisively negative.

### Operational Risks
- **Single-basin concentration:** All wholly-owned midstream assets in the Delaware Basin. Regulatory action, weather events, or producer reallocation would have outsized impact.
- **Commodity sensitivity:** 74% product revenue creates direct NGL/gas price exposure. NGL/condensate prices declined 13–16% in 2025; further declines would compress margins.
- **Waha gas pricing:** Permian Basin gas pricing has been volatile, occasionally going negative due to takeaway constraints. The ECCC Pipeline partially addresses this but won't fully resolve it.
- **Seismic/regulatory:** Texas Railroad Commission has curtailed injection volumes near Kinetik's disposal operations. Stricter regulation could impair the water disposal business.

### Corporate Governance Risks
- **ISQ overhang:** Continued liquidation of remaining ~18M units creates persistent supply pressure.
- **TSR underperformance:** 5-year cumulative TSR of 206 trails peer group at 347 — a 41% relative shortfall.
- **Complex structure:** The holding company / operating partnership structure reduces transparency and creates potential conflicts between LP and GP interests.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term implied volatility of 110.9% versus 37.6% historical volatility represents an extreme dislocation — the options market is pricing an implied move of ±9.2% in the nearest expiry. The term structure is in steep backwardation (110.9% near-term vs. 34.9% at 6 months), indicating the market expects a specific near-term event to resolve uncertainty. Put skew of 4.4% at the 1-month tenor and 8.8% at 3 months shows elevated demand for downside protection. Combined with 11.3% short interest (5.2 days to cover), this diverges from the sell-side Buy consensus but aligns with the deteriorating estimate revision trend.

The near-term IV spike may be partially explained by the 1-day-to-expiry option structure (110.9% for a 1-day contract is mechanically inflated by gamma), but the broader term structure (45.2% at 1 month, 43.5% at 3 months, both well above 37.6% HV) confirms the market is pricing elevated uncertainty through mid-2026.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55–58, +16–23%)
- Kings Landing, ECCC Pipeline, and Acid Gas Injection projects deliver on schedule, pushing 2026 EBITDA to the upper end of $1.05B guidance and positioning 2027 EBITDA toward $1.1–1.2B.
- Elevated energy prices ($100+ oil) sustain strong producer activity in the Delaware Basin, driving volume growth above management expectations.
- Dividend coverage reaches 1.5x, triggering accelerated dividend growth from the current 3–5% annual pace.
- Takeout premium: a larger midstream company acquires KNTK at 8–9x EBITDA ($55–65 per share).
- Successful A/R Facility renewal and continued debt reduction improve credit profile.

### Bear Case ($32–36, -24–32%)
- Commodity price decline (oil below $80, NGL prices weaken further) reduces producer drilling activity in the Delaware Basin, causing volume growth to stall.
- Estimate cuts accelerate: the 33% FY2026 EPS reduction continues, pushing forward P/E above 50x and triggering multiple contraction.
- ISQ continues to liquidate, creating sustained supply pressure on the equity.
- Refinancing risk materializes: 2028 maturities become more expensive if credit spreads widen.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates below 1.0x, forcing a cut that destroys the yield thesis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. ECCC Pipeline achieves Q2 2026 in-service on schedule AND Q1 2026 EBITDA prints ≥$240M (quarterly run-rate consistent with $1.0B annual guidance).
2. ISQ overhang clears: remaining ~18M units sold or locked up, removing persistent supply pressure.
3. Net Debt/EBITDA declines to ≤3.5x, demonstrating deleveraging trajectory.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. FY2026 EPS estimate falls below $1.00 (a further 22% cut from current $1.28), indicating fundamental deterioration beyond one-time items.
2. Dividend coverage falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, signaling unsustainable distribution.
3. Delaware Basin gathered gas volumes decline YoY for any quarter, indicating loss of producer commitment.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next reassessment at Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April / early May 2026). Key data points: quarterly EBITDA trajectory, ECCC construction progress, ISQ unit conversion/sale activity, and A/R Facility renewal status.

## Conclusion

Kinetik Holdings presents a genuine but conditional growth thesis centered on its New Mexico expansion (ECCC Pipeline Q2 2026, Kings Landing ramp, Acid Gas Injection year-end 2026) and guided 7% EBITDA growth. The 6.8% dividend yield provides meaningful carry. However, the position is weighed down by elevated leverage on negative equity, shareholder returns exceeding organic cash flow, persistent ISQ overhang, deteriorating EPS estimates, and single-basin concentration risk. The options market and short interest are pricing materially more risk than the sell-side consensus suggests. HOLD pending execution on Q2 2026 infrastructure milestones and evidence that EBITDA guidance is achievable.
