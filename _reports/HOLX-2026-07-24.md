**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hologic is no longer a public company. On October 20, 2025, Hologic agreed to be acquired by funds managed by Blackstone and TPG, and the take-private transaction closed on April 7, 2026 at a value of up to $79 per share, implying an enterprise value of roughly $18.3 billion. The common stock was delisted from Nasdaq at close. There is no longer a tradeable HOLX equity to buy, hold, or short, which is why the empty price and analyst fields in the standard data pull are not a data error — they reflect a security that has ceased to exist.

The merger consideration was structured as $76.00 in cash plus a contingent value right (CVR) of up to $3.00 per share tied to global Breast Health revenue milestones in fiscal 2026 and 2027. The "$79" is the maximum, not a guaranteed figure. Whether legacy holders realize $76 or the full $79 depends on Breast Health hitting revenue targets, and management repeatedly flagged a capital-equipment "gantry slowdown" in the quarters before the deal, so the CVR carries genuine binary payout risk and could settle at zero. The CVR is the only residual public-facing instrument, and it is a narrow, event-driven claim rather than a business investment.

The fundamentals that made Hologic an attractive LBO target were real: strong free cash flow (~$998 million in FY2025 on only $58.8 million of capex), a cash-rich, low-leverage balance sheet (net debt only ~$0.55 billion pre-buyout), and sticky razor/razorblade recurring revenue from the Panther diagnostics and mammography installed bases. But the growth story had stalled: revenue was roughly flat around $4.0–4.1 billion after the COVID testing cliff, and FY2025 margins and net income both fell despite modestly higher revenue. That combination of high cash generation, low leverage, and a slow-growing core is precisely the profile private equity buys. Given the delisting, the recommendation is AVOID: the common equity is no longer investable.

## Company Overview

Hologic is a global leader in women's health, medical devices, and diagnostics, headquartered in Marlborough, Massachusetts, with a fiscal year ending in late September (FY2025 ended September 27, 2025). The company operates four reportable segments:

- **Breast Health** — 3D digital mammography systems (Selenia Dimensions and the new Envision platform), breast biopsy, and a large recurring service business on the global installed gantry base. AI is central to the franchise (Genius AI Detection PRO, 3DQuorum, contrast-enhanced mammography via I-View 2.0). The 2024 Endomagnetics acquisition added wire-free surgical localization and lymphatic tracing (Magseed, Magtrace, Sentimag), contributing roughly $54.9 million of incremental product revenue in FY2025.
- **Diagnostics** — Molecular diagnostics on the Panther and Panther Fusion platforms (razor/razorblade economics), with a menu spanning STIs, vaginitis (BV/CV/TV), respiratory, and the Aptima HPV franchise. Also includes Cytology & Perinatal (ThinPrep Pap, Genius Digital Diagnostics) and Biotheranostics (Breast Cancer Index).
- **Surgical** — MyoSure, NovaSure, and related GYN surgical devices. This unit is now being marketed for sale by the new private-equity owners.
- **Skeletal Health** — the smallest segment (bone densitometry).

The competitive moat rests on a large installed base of Panther instruments and mammography gantries generating high-margin recurring consumables and service revenue, leadership positions in mammography and molecular women's-health diagnostics, and deep AI/imaging IP. Named competitors include GE HealthCare in imaging and Abbott, Becton Dickinson, and Quest Diagnostics in diagnostics.

Several points stand out. First, the COVID cliff dominates the multi-year picture: revenue fell roughly 17% from the pandemic-inflated $4.86 billion peak in FY2022 to a ~$4.0 billion core base in FY2023–2024, with FY2025 showing only modest re-growth to $4.10 billion (+1.7% YoY). Second, FY2025 exhibited margin compression that outweighed the revenue gain: gross margin fell from ~55.3% to ~51.6%, operating income declined to $714.3 million (op margin ~21.9% to ~17.4%), and net income dropped to $565.7 million. The drivers cited were mix shift, the gantry slowdown, Endomag integration, and deal costs. Third, cash generation and the balance sheet were strong: FY2025 OCF of ~$1.06 billion and FCF of ~$998 million, cash of $1.96 billion against total debt of ~$2.51 billion, and a current ratio of ~3.75x. Fourth, aggressive buybacks cut the diluted share count by roughly 10% from FY2022 to FY2025, supporting EPS through the revenue trough. Finally, goodwill and intangibles totaled ~$4.23 billion, or ~47% of $9.0 billion in total assets, reflecting the acquisitive history (Gen-Probe, Cynosure, Endomag, Biotheranostics).

The earnings surprise track record over the trailing four quarters was mixed: two beats and two misses, including a 6.4% miss for the December 2025 quarter ($1.04 actual vs. $1.11 estimate). This deceleration in delivery is consistent with the margin pressure visible in the financials and with the timing of the board's decision to accept a buyout rather than grind through a slow public re-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth outlook was modest at best. Revenue was essentially flat for three straight years after the COVID testing wind-down. Within that, Diagnostics revenue in Q1 FY2026 was $464.4 million (down 1.3% YoY), with Molecular Diagnostics down 3.5% on fading COVID testing and legacy STI assay erosion. Offsetting growth vectors — vaginitis testing, Panther Fusion menu expansion (including GI assays), Aptima HPV clinician-collected primary screening (FDA-approved February 2026), and Genius digital cytology — are real but incremental against a large, slow-decaying base. Breast Health, the segment tied to the CVR, faced capital-equipment cyclicality and hospital-budget-dependent gantry sales that management characterized as slowing. ThinPrep Pap sales were pressured by U.S.–China tensions and Chinese policy, dragging Asia-Pacific.

The growth score reflects a business that stabilized off its post-COVID base but showed no acceleration. There is no earnings-recovery-from-trough dynamic here that would warrant cycle-adjusted treatment: the FY2025 numbers show declining margins and net income. Under private ownership, the growth story is now being reshaped through a sum-of-the-parts strategy rather than organic acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no valuation to assess in the conventional sense. The common stock is delisted and no longer trades.

The only residual instrument with valuation uncertainty is the CVR, worth $0 to $3.00 per legacy share depending on whether Breast Health hits FY2026–2027 global revenue milestones. Given management's repeated gantry-slowdown commentary and the capital-equipment cyclicality of that segment, the CVR is a genuine binary that could pay nothing. It is an event-driven contingent claim held by former shareholders. The valuation score is low because the security offers no forward asymmetric upside to a new buyer: there is nothing to buy, and the one contingent instrument is a coin-flip on a soft segment. This is a closed transaction, not a cheap value trap awaiting a re-rating catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Hologic's competitive position within its niches was solid and stable. The razor/razorblade economics of the Panther molecular platform and the recurring service base on the mammography gantry fleet provide sticky, high-margin recurring revenue. The company holds leadership positions in mammography and molecular women's-health diagnostics, and it has deep AI/imaging IP (Genius AI Detection, Genius Digital Diagnostics). Structural tailwinds include AI adoption in breast imaging and the expansion of HPV primary-screening and self-collection markets.

The competitive set is well-capitalized — GE HealthCare in imaging, Abbott and Becton Dickinson in diagnostics, and Quest in lab services — but Hologic's franchises proved sticky enough to stabilize revenue after the industry-wide post-COVID diagnostics normalization. The recurring-revenue, high-FCF, low-leverage profile is what made the company an attractive LBO candidate. Competitive position is the strongest of the qualitative dimensions, but it now accrues to the private owners rather than to public shareholders.

## Risk Assessment

With the equity delisted, the relevant risks pivot from equity risks to deal, CVR, and operational risks for the now-private entity:

- **CVR payout risk (the one residual public-facing uncertainty).** If Breast Health underperforms — plausible given the flagged gantry slowdown and hospital capex softness — the CVR could pay $0.
- **Breast Health capital-equipment cyclicality.** Mammography gantry sales are lumpy and hospital-budget-dependent; the recurring service business only partially offsets the volatility.
- **Diagnostics decline drivers.** Fading COVID testing and legacy STI assay erosion continue to pressure Molecular Diagnostics.
- **China/geopolitical exposure.** ThinPrep Pap and Asia-Pacific revenue are pressured by U.S.–China tensions and Chinese policy.
- **LBO leverage.** The buyout loaded substantial debt onto a previously low-leverage balance sheet. Within roughly three months of close, Reuters and the FT reported (July 9, 2026) that the sponsors are seeking more than $4 billion for the Surgical unit explicitly to pay down debt — a signal about the debt burden and a deleveraging/sum-of-the-parts play.
- **Reimbursement and regulatory.** Ongoing dependence on FDA clearances (Aptima HPV self/clinician-collected screening, Panther Fusion GI assays) and reimbursement dynamics for high-throughput testing.

The residual risk profile is dominated by the CVR binary and private-entity leverage, neither of which is a public-equity consideration. The risk score is low not because the underlying business is fragile, but because the residual instrument (the CVR) carries asymmetric downside toward zero with a capped $3.00 upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** There is no bull case for HOLX common equity, which no longer exists as a tradeable security. For holders of the residual CVR, the bull case is that Breast Health achieves its FY2026–2027 revenue milestones and the CVR pays the full $3.00 per legacy share on top of the $76.00 cash already received, for a total of $79.00. For the private business itself, the value-creation case rests on the sum-of-the-parts strategy (Surgical divestiture at $4 billion-plus), operational improvement under a high-pedigree operator (José Almeida, ex-Baxter CEO), and eventual re-IPO or strategic sale at a higher multiple, though none of this accrues to public investors.

**Bear Case.** For the CVR holder, the bear case is that Breast Health misses its milestones amid the gantry slowdown and the CVR settles at $0, capping total proceeds at the $76.00 already paid. For anyone attempting to establish a position in HOLX today, there is no security to buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: There is no re-entry into HOLX common equity because it has been delisted and the transaction has closed. The only way public investors regain exposure is if the private owners pursue a re-IPO or a partial listing at a future date, at which point a fresh analysis on the then-current capital structure (post-LBO leverage, post-Surgical-divestiture business mix) would be warranted. Legacy holders of the CVR should monitor Breast Health FY2026–2027 revenue disclosures to gauge the probability of the contingent payment, but this is a wind-down of an existing claim, not an investment decision.

**Timeline**: The take-private is complete. The residual CVR resolves on Breast Health revenue outcomes across FY2026 and FY2027. Any future public listing of the reconstituted business would be a multi-year horizon under standard PE hold periods.

## Conclusion

The recommendation is **AVOID**, which in this case is a statement that the security is uninvestable rather than a directional view on the underlying company. Hologic's common stock was acquired by Blackstone and TPG and delisted from Nasdaq. There is no public equity to own.

The business that was taken private was a financially healthy, cash-generative, low-leverage leader in women's-health diagnostics and breast imaging, with sticky razor/razorblade recurring revenue — the classic profile of an attractive LBO target. But it was also a slow-growth, margin-pressured franchise, and that combination is why the board accepted a buyout at a premium rather than continue as a public company. For former shareholders, the only remaining consideration is the CVR, a binary contingent claim on a soft segment that could pay anywhere from $0 to $3.00. For any new investor, there is nothing here to buy.
