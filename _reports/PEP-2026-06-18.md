**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PepsiCo enters mid-2026 as a stalled franchise that the market has repriced from a quality compounder to a yield-and-turnaround story. The stock trades at $141.59, down from a 52-week high of $171.48 (a ~17% drawdown) but still ~11% above its 52-week low of $127.60. GAAP earnings fell 14% to $6.00 diluted EPS while core EPS was essentially flat ($8.14 vs $8.16); the GAAP decline is almost entirely a $1.9B non-cash intangible impairment (primarily the Rockstar energy brand) plus acquisition charges. A scoring framework keyed on headline net income would materially overstate the operating deterioration. The genuine operating problem is narrower and harder to fix: North America volumes are negative across both core segments (PFNA savory snacks -3%, PBNA beverages -3%), the product of years of price hikes that pushed consumers toward private label and smaller competitors.

The investment debate is now an activist turnaround question. Elliott Management disclosed a ~$4B stake in September 2025, and its agenda — snack price cuts, ~20% SKU rationalization, North America supply-chain integration, and possible bottling refranchising — is visibly embedded in management's own language. Q1 FY2026 delivered an early data point in the bulls' favor: revenue +8.5% to $19.44B and core EPS of $1.61 beating the $1.57 estimate, with international offsetting NA softness. The bear counterpoint is equally concrete: Coca-Cola is decisively out-executing PepsiCo in beverages (KO Q1 2026 +12% revenue / +10% organic, Coke Zero Sugar volume +13% across all regions), so PepsiCo's weaker half is losing share to its primary competitor while its stronger half (snacks) sits in a self-inflicted affordability air-pocket.

We rate PEP a HOLD on an income basis. The case to own it is the ~4.1% dividend yield (a Dividend King, raised 4% to $5.92/share for 2026) at a ~22x forward multiple that sits below the 26x peer median and well below the stock's own historical range, plus free optionality on the Elliott turnaround inflecting NA volume. We are not buyers because the volume inflection remains unproven, core EPS growth is zero, the GAAP payout ratio is stretched (~$5.92 dividend vs ~$6.00 GAAP EPS), and there is no insider buying despite a beaten-down stock and activist involvement.

## Company Overview

PepsiCo is a global beverage and convenient-food company selling in 200+ countries through company-owned operations, authorized bottlers, and contract manufacturers. The snack portfolio (Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Quaker) accounts for roughly 58% of revenue; the beverage portfolio (Pepsi-Cola, Mountain Dew, Gatorade, SodaStream) is the other ~42%. The company reports six segments:

| Segment | FY25 Revenue | FY25 Op. Profit | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| PFNA (Frito-Lay/Quaker NA) | $27.5B | $6.17B | -2% |
| PBNA (Beverages NA) | $28.2B | $1.09B | -3% |
| IB Franchise (intl bev + SodaStream) | $5.0B | $1.77B | +1.5% |
| EMEA | $18.0B | $2.11B | mixed |
| LatAm Foods | $10.5B | $2.01B | +1% |
| Asia Pacific Foods | $4.6B | $0.37B | +4% |

North America is ~59% of revenue (US alone $52.2B, 56%); international is ~44%. The durable advantage is food/beverage complementarity and Direct-Store-Delivery (DSD) scale, particularly Frito-Lay, which has historically carried best-in-class ~22% segment operating margins. The structural weakness is PBNA, where FY25 operating profit collapsed on impairments and volume fell 3%, against Coca-Cola, which is gaining share with a cleaner, more focused global beverage portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY25 revenue grew 2.3% to $93.9B (~2% organic), with a -2% volume decline fully offset by +4% effective net pricing. The reliance on price rather than volume is the crux of the problem: pricing is what drove consumers to trade down. GAAP operating profit fell 11% ($12.9B → $11.5B; margin compressed 14.0% → 12.2%) and net income fell 14% to $8.24B. Core EPS was flat, not collapsing. The reconciling items are the $1.9B impairment (predominantly Rockstar, $1.5B+ across PBNA/EMEA/IB), poppi acquisition charges, a ~5-point commodity cost headwind (including ~6-point tariff impact in PBNA), and rising restructuring.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $12.1B (down from $12.5B); free cash flow was ~$8.2B after $4.4B capex (down from $5.3B). FCF margin is ~8.7%, FCF yield on market cap ~4.2%. This covers the ~$7.9B 2026 dividend but leaves limited cushion, which is why buybacks have shrunk to a token ~$1.0B per year for three consecutive years.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$49B (long-term debt up to $42.3B from $37.2B, after $8.2B of 2025 note issuance partly funding poppi/Siete). Net interest expense is rising ($1.12B vs $919M). Equity is only $20.4B, depleted by ~$41.8B cumulative buybacks and a $15.0B accumulated OCI loss (largely FX). ROE is 40.4%, ROIC 14.3% (core net ROIC 19.1%). The Altman Z of 1.97 reflects the asset-light, buyback-heavy staples capital structure, not distress; PepsiCo retains investment-grade and Tier-1 commercial paper access. The leverage is appropriate for the business model, but the combination of rising rates, rising interest expense, and a stretched GAAP payout ratio means capital-return flexibility is more constrained than the "Dividend King" label implies.

**Earnings quality and surprises.** The four-quarter record is 3 beats, 1 in-line, with the most recent (Q1 FY26) a +2.8% beat. Estimates are stable: current-year consensus $8.65, next-year $9.11, with revisions flat to slightly negative over 30/90 days (-1.8% current quarter, +0.3% current year). A strong forward revision signal is absent. This is a flat-fundamentals stock, not an inflecting one.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth of ~2% with negative volume is a pricing story running out of runway. The structural growth question splits cleanly:

- **Beverages (PBNA, ~30% of revenue):** the structural laggard. The head-to-head Q1 2026 comparison with Coca-Cola shows PepsiCo losing the beverage execution battle. This is share loss to a stronger competitor, not a cyclical dip.
- **Snacks (PFNA, ~29% of revenue):** the crown jewel in a self-inflicted air-pocket. Frito-Lay over-extended pricing; the -3% savory volume reflects affordability fatigue and private-label encroachment rather than secular decline. The Elliott price resets are designed to rebuild volume elasticity, at the cost of near-term margin.
- **International (~44%):** the genuine growth engine in FY25, with LatAm, APAC, and IB Franchise all posting positive volume.

The portfolio pivot toward "better-for-you" — poppi ($1.95B + earnout), Siete ($1.2B), the remaining 50% of Sabra, and the Celsius/Alani Nu distribution arrangement — is a credible defensive response to GLP-1 and ultra-processed-food headwinds, but these are bolt-ons that reshape the mix rather than re-accelerate the top line. The growth thesis depends on whether the Elliott-driven price cuts inflect NA volume positively within the next 12-18 months. Q1 FY26 is one encouraging quarter, not yet confirmation.

## Valuation Assessment

PEP trades at ~22.2x trailing/forward P/E with a ~4.1% dividend yield. On the cleaner core-EPS basis (~$8.14 FY25, $8.65 consensus FY26), the forward multiple is ~16-17x, a meaningful discount to PepsiCo's historical mid-20s range and to the staples-beverage cohort.

PEP is the cheapest of the large-cap peers on P/E and offers the highest yield. The discount is justified by genuinely worse fundamentals — negative NA volume, beverage share loss to KO — so the cheapness is not a clean mispricing. The valuation does provide downside support: the yield is well above peers and above the stock's own history, and the core-earnings multiple in the mid-teens prices in the volume malaise. Analyst price targets (low $132, mean $168.5, median $171.5, high $195) bracket the current price with the median ~21% above, but the bull targets rest entirely on the Elliott turnaround executing.

We score valuation a 6.0, modestly above neutral. The yield support and below-peer multiple are real, but the discount reflects a real fundamental gap, not a market error. The historical lesson that "cheap" sorts the wrong way absent a catalyst is directly relevant: the catalyst here (Elliott turnaround) exists but is unproven, which keeps this from a high valuation score. Two hidden assets add modest support: the $1.85B Celsius preferred stake carrying an $860M unrealized gain, and the capital-light economics of the Alani Nu distribution arrangement.

## Competitive Landscape

PepsiCo's moat is its DSD distribution scale and food/beverage complementarity, and that moat is stable rather than improving. The trajectory is the issue. In beverages, the company is losing share to Coca-Cola, whose Q1 organic growth roughly doubled PepsiCo's beverage trend and whose flagship zero-sugar line is growing volume double digits globally. In snacks, Frito-Lay remains dominant but is ceding volume to private label and challenger brands at the affordability end, a reversible dynamic the price cuts target, but reversal is not yet evident. Channel shift toward e-commerce, hard discounters, and (newly flagged in the 10-K) AI shopping agents pressures the traditional shelf advantage DSD provides; management acknowledges some customers "moving away from DSD systems."

The competitive picture is therefore mixed: a strong but decelerating snack franchise and a structurally weaker, share-losing beverage franchise. The portfolio pivot into functional/better-for-you brands is a sensible adaptation but does not yet change the trajectory. A 5.5 score reflects a stable moat with a deteriorating near-term share trend, weighted toward the trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**North America volume stall** is the central risk: if the price cuts compress margin without inflecting volume, the turnaround thesis fails and the stock de-rates further. **GLP-1 weight-loss drugs** are explicitly named as a demand threat to snacks and sugary beverages. **Health and regulatory pressure** is tangible and dated: Texas's artificial-color warning-label law takes effect Jan 1, 2027, and Mexico doubled its sweetened-beverage tax effective Jan 1, 2026 (~$0.09 → $0.17/liter), a direct hit to PepsiCo's #2 market ($6.9B revenue). **Russia concentration** is an underappreciated overhang: 5% of revenue and 20% of consolidated cash sit in Russia under currency controls, with Russia revenue actually growing to $4.77B in 2025. **Customer concentration**: Walmart/Sam's is ~14% of consolidated revenue. **SodaStream goodwill** "narrowly exceeded carrying value," flagging possible future impairment after an $862M write-down in 2023. The **OECD 15% global minimum tax** will raise the effective rate as it phases in through end-2026. **FX translation** affects 44% of non-USD revenue, with peso and lira headwinds in 2025.

Several of these are partly priced (the regulatory and GLP-1 risks are well-publicized sector themes), but the combination of a stretched GAAP payout ratio, rising interest expense, and stranded Russian cash means capital-return flexibility is genuinely more constrained than headline metrics suggest. We score risk 5.5, below neutral, reflecting specific dated catalysts (Mexico tax, Texas labeling) and the unproven-turnaround binary, partly offset by the defensive staples demand base.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options picture shows a sharp anomaly: 1-week ATM IV of 66.9% against 21.7% one-year historical vol (45.2pp rich, 86th percentile), with the term structure in steep backwardation (66.9% near vs 23.9% at six months). The 1-week implied move is only ±0.9%, however, and the put/call skew is heavily call-heavy (-134.7%), with put/call volume and OI ratios both below 1.0 (0.67 and 0.59). ATM IV jumped from 38.8% to 66.9% since mid-May as the price fell 5.1%. The signal is mixed: elevated near-dated vol pricing an imminent event, but positioning is call-skewed rather than bearish, and the tiny implied move suggests the spike is concentrated in a single very-near expiry rather than a broad fear signal. The market expects a discrete near-term catalyst (likely event-driven) without strong directional conviction. The 2.0% short interest and 3.7 days to cover indicate no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$185, ~31% upside)
Elliott's playbook works: snack price cuts restore volume elasticity, NA snack volume inflects positive within 12-18 months, SKU rationalization and supply-chain integration lift margins, and possible bottling refranchising unlocks capital. International continues its mid-single-digit growth. Core EPS re-accelerates toward $9.50+, the multiple re-rates toward the 26x peer median, and the yield provides a floor while investors wait. The Celsius preferred stake and Alani Nu distribution add capital-light optionality. Bull target ~$185, consistent with the high analyst range of $195.

### Bear Case (~$120, ~15% downside)
The price cuts compress margin without restoring volume while Coca-Cola continues taking beverage share. GLP-1 adoption and ultra-processed-food regulation structurally cap snack/beverage demand. Mexico tax, Texas labeling, and the global minimum tax all bite. The stretched payout forces a slowing of dividend growth, removing the income support that underpins the multiple. The multiple compresses toward the mid-teens on no growth, and the stock revisits the low-$120s near its 52-week low.

The base case sits in between: a flat-to-modestly-positive core-EPS path, a yield that holds, and a multiple that stabilizes around the current level while the turnaround proves itself slowly.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income hold with turnaround optionality, evaluated on a 12-month horizon tied to quarterly NA volume prints and Elliott-related disclosures.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive NA snack (PFNA) volume, confirming the price-cut elasticity thesis.
- Core EPS estimate revisions turning positive >+5% over 90 days alongside positive organic volume.
- Concrete value-unlock announcement (bottling refranchising, accelerated buyback restoration) that adds a capital-return catalyst beyond the dividend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NA volume remaining negative through 2026 while gross margin compresses from the price cuts, the failure mode where the turnaround destroys value.
- A dividend-growth slowdown or freeze signaling the payout has become unsustainable.
- Beverage share loss to KO accelerating, with PBNA volume worsening beyond -3%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY26, expected July 2026) is the primary checkpoint for the NA volume inflection. The Mexico sweetened-beverage tax impact and any Elliott proxy/agreement developments are the other near-term inputs.

## Conclusion

PepsiCo is a high-quality staples franchise whose stronger half (snacks) is in a self-inflicted affordability air-pocket and whose weaker half (beverages) is losing share to a better-executing Coca-Cola. The GAAP earnings decline is largely cosmetic, a $1.9B Rockstar-led impairment masking flat core EPS, so the business is not broken, but it is stalled. The bull case rests on Elliott's turnaround inflecting NA volume, for which Q1 FY26 is one encouraging but insufficient data point.

We rate PEP a HOLD on an income basis. The reason to own it is the covered ~4.1% dividend yield at a ~22x forward / mid-teens core-EPS multiple that sits below the 26x peer median and the stock's own history, plus free optionality on the activist turnaround. The reason it is not a BUY is that the volume inflection is unproven, core EPS growth is zero, estimate revisions are flat, the GAAP payout ratio is stretched, and management is a net seller with no open-market buying to signal conviction. Investors holding for the yield are paid to wait; investors seeking appreciation should require confirmation that NA volume has inflected before adding. The next two quarterly volume prints will determine whether this becomes a BUY or an AVOID.
