## Executive Summary

Independent Bank Corp, parent of Rockland Trust Company, has emerged from its Enterprise Bancorp acquisition ($503M, closed July 2025) with a stronger earnings profile that the market has only partially recognized. GAAP EPS of $4.44 in 2025 was depressed by $39.6M in merger charges and a $34.5M non-PCD loan provision. Operating EPS was $5.64, and Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.68 confirms the earnings trajectory is improving. At $78.63, the stock trades at approximately 10.8x the FY2026 consensus estimate of $7.28 (per vendor consensus data), a discount to intrinsic value given improving fundamentals.

Full-year 2025 net charge-offs of $54.6M (driven primarily by classified CRE loans) declined to $4.8M in Q1 2026. However, management noted during the Q1 2026 earnings call that the office portfolio remains a primary area of diligence, with a new $17.7M nonperforming office loan identified and a $2.8M specific reserve established. Management's confidence is demonstrated through actions: $124M in buybacks completed in Q1 (retiring 3.59% of shares), a planned new repurchase authorization expected in Q2 2026, and an 8.5% dividend increase.

The primary risk remains CRE concentration at 44.7% of total loans ($8.3B), particularly office exposure in a hybrid-work world. However, the average CRE loan of $1.9M indicates a granular, diversified portfolio rather than concentrated large-credit risk. Geographic concentration in Massachusetts/New England is a secondary concern that limits the addressable growth market but provides deep franchise value in an economically resilient region.

## Company Overview

Independent Bank Corp is a $24.9 billion-asset bank holding company operating exclusively through Rockland Trust, a 117-year-old Massachusetts trust company. Post-Enterprise acquisition, the bank operates 151+ retail branches across Eastern Massachusetts, Worcester County, southern New Hampshire, and Rhode Island. The loan portfolio is 77.2% commercial (CRE, C&I, construction) and 22.8% consumer real estate. A wealth management division administers $9.2 billion in assets, generating approximately $45M in annual gross fees.

The Enterprise Bancorp acquisition, completed July 1, 2025, added $3.9B in loans, $4.4B in deposits, and 27 branches, moving INDB firmly into the upper tier of New England community banks.

## Financial Analysis

**Net Interest Margin Expansion:** NIM recovered to 3.57% in 2025 (from 3.28% trough in 2024), driven by acquired higher-margin Enterprise loans and 8bps of purchase accounting accretion. Cost of deposits declined 10bps to 1.53%. Q1 2026 NII of $212.5M annualizes to $850M, consistent with sustained NIM above 3.5%.

**Revenue Growth:** Total revenue increased 24.4% to $857.5M in 2025. Net interest income grew 26.2% to $708.8M; non-interest income grew 16.2% to $148.7M. Q1 2026 revenue was up 41.9% YoY, reflecting the first full quarter with Enterprise.

**Operating Earnings Power:** The distinction between GAAP and operating results is material:
- GAAP EPS 2025: $4.44
- Operating EPS 2025: $5.64 (excludes $39.6M merger costs, $34.5M non-PCD provision)
- Q1 2026 adjusted EPS: $1.68
- Consensus FY2026E: $7.28; FY2027E: $8.34 (source: vendor consensus estimates as of May 2026)

Note: Annualizing a single post-acquisition quarter ($1.68 × 4 = $6.72) provides a directional floor for forward earnings power but should be interpreted cautiously given purchase-accounting accretion effects and seasonal variability.

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 12.86% is well above regulatory minimums. Tangible equity/tangible assets at 9.88% provides adequate loss absorption. Total liquidity capacity of $9.0B against $832M in outstanding borrowings represents exceptional buffer.

**Capital Return:** Combined 2026 capital return (buybacks + dividends) is running at an elevated pace. INDB completed $124M in buybacks in Q1 2026 and management indicated plans to establish a new repurchase authorization in Q2 2026. The 8.5% dividend increase to $0.64/quarter brings total capital return to a level signaling management confidence in forward earnings.

**Credit Quality Trajectory:**

The Q1 2026 improvement in charge-offs ($4.8M vs. $13.7M average quarterly rate in 2025) suggests the bulk of 2025 credit costs were related to a discrete set of classified credits. However, management's Q1 commentary noted ongoing office portfolio stress, including a $4M CRE charge-off, a new $17.7M nonperforming office loan, and a $2.8M specific reserve — indicating the credit cycle is improving but not fully resolved.

## Growth Analysis

Organic loan growth is modest (low-to-mid single digits typical for New England community banks). The primary growth drivers are:

1. **Earnings normalization:** As merger costs fully roll off (expected to be largely complete by mid-2026, following the July 2025 acquisition close), GAAP and operating EPS converge toward the $7.28-$8.34 range in 2026-2027.
2. **NIM stability/expansion:** Fixed-rate loan repricing continues to provide tailwind. The bank's slight asset-sensitivity means NIM is relatively protected in various rate scenarios (-200bp shock = only -1.1% NII impact).
3. **Fee income growth:** Investment management ($9.2B AUA) grows with market appreciation and net flows; mortgage banking recovers with any rate normalization.
4. **Accretive buybacks:** At 10.8x forward earnings, share repurchases at current prices provide meaningful EPS accretion. If the planned new authorization is established and executed near current prices, the share-count reduction would contribute materially to per-share earnings growth.
5. **Organic C&I growth:** Management commentary on the Q1 2026 call emphasized organic C&I lending growth as a priority, with the core system conversion positioned to enhance operational capacity.

Note: Management indicated during the Q1 2026 earnings call that INDB is currently "pencils down" on bank acquisitions while focused on the core system conversion and organic growth, though selectively interested in non-bank opportunities such as wealth management or deposit-gathering platforms. Near-term bank M&A is therefore unlikely to be a growth driver in the base case.

Consensus estimates project 64% EPS growth from 2025 GAAP ($4.44) to 2027E ($8.34). Even against 2025 operating EPS ($5.64), the path to $8.34 represents ~48% cumulative growth. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY2027 EPS up 0.9% over 90 days.

**Current Metrics:**
- P/E (GAAP trailing): 17.7x — optically elevated due to merger-depressed earnings
- P/E (Operating trailing): 13.9x — reflects normalized 2025 earnings
- P/E (FY2026E consensus $7.28): **10.8x**
- P/E (FY2027E consensus $8.34): **9.4x**
- P/TBV: 1.65x ($78.62 / $47.55)
- Dividend yield: 3.3%

**Peer Comparison:**
Based on vendor peer data (FBP, BANF, WSFS, RNST, UCB), the estimated peer median P/E is approximately 12.7x forward earnings. At 10.8x FY2026E, INDB trades at an estimated 15% discount to this peer group despite: (a) improving asset quality trends, (b) an aggressive capital return program, (c) strong franchise value (longest operating history, CRA "Outstanding," top workplace designation), and (d) a wealth management operation providing fee income diversification most peers lack.

The discount appears attributable to two factors: (1) the market anchoring on GAAP trailing P/E of 17.7x rather than forward operating earnings, and (2) residual CRE risk premium. Factor (1) resolves mechanically as quarters pass. Factor (2) is real but quantifiable — even a 200bp charge-off rate on the office subset (~$1.5B estimated) would produce $30M in additional losses, roughly $0.50/share after-tax — meaningful but not thesis-breaking.

**Fair value range:** At an estimated peer-median 12.7x applied to FY2026E of $7.28, fair value is $92.5. At 13x FY2027E of $8.34, fair value is $108. Current price represents 16-27% upside to these benchmarks.

## Competitive Landscape

INDB occupies a defensible position as one of the largest independent community banks in New England. The 117-year operating history, 17 consecutive years as a "Top Place to Work," and "Outstanding" CRA rating create barriers to replication. The Enterprise acquisition expanded the footprint into northern Massachusetts and southern New Hampshire, filling a geographic gap.

Competitive threats include large national banks (BofA, Citizens) with broader product suites and technology investment, as well as fintech challengers in payments and lending. INDB's core competitive advantage — deep relationship banking with small-to-upper middle market commercial clients — is relatively resistant to digital disruption. These borrowers value local decision-making, speed, and relationship continuity.

The planned 2026 core system upgrade introduces near-term execution risk but positions the bank to modernize digital delivery and operational efficiency.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary):** At 44.7% of loans, CRE is the dominant risk. The 2025 charge-off spike ($43.4M in CRE NCOs) demonstrated this vulnerability. Mitigants: (a) average loan size of $1.9M indicates granular diversification rather than whale-credit risk, (b) Q1 2026 charge-offs declined substantially, and (c) management explicitly states they are "actively monitoring upcoming maturities" in the office subset. Q1 2026 results still included a $4M CRE charge-off and a newly identified $17.7M nonperforming office loan, indicating that while the trajectory is improving, office CRE stress has not been fully resolved.

**Geographic Concentration:** All loans are to Massachusetts/New England borrowers. A regional recession would hit disproportionately. Massachusetts benefits from a diversified knowledge economy (biotech, education, healthcare, technology) that has historically provided economic resilience.

**Integration/Technology Risk:** The Enterprise merger integration plus a simultaneous core system upgrade creates operational complexity. System conversions carry downtime, customer attrition, and cost overrun risk.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** The bank is approximately neutral but slightly asset-sensitive. A sustained rate decline would compress NIM from current 3.57% toward 3.40-3.45%. This is manageable but limits NIM-driven upside in a cutting environment.

**CRE Refinancing Environment:** With 30Y rates above 5% and CRE values under pressure nationally, upcoming maturities in the CRE portfolio face refinancing stress. The 10-K's explicit mention of "upcoming maturities within the office subset" suggests management awareness and proactive management, but doesn't eliminate the risk of further charge-offs in H2 2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($92-97, +17-23%):** Enterprise acquisition synergies exceed expectations. Merger costs fully roll off, driving FY2026 GAAP EPS toward the $7.30+ consensus. NIM sustains above 3.55%. CRE credit quality continues improving through 2026. Planned buyback authorization is established and executed at current prices, reducing share count meaningfully. Multiple re-rates from 10.8x toward estimated peer median of 12.7x as market recognizes operating earnings normalization. Price target: 12.7x × $7.28 = $92.5, or 13x with credit momentum confirmation = $94.6.

**Bear Case ($62-67, -15-21%):** CRE office maturities produce another charge-off wave in H2 2026, with NCOs reverting to $40-50M annually. **Base Case ($88-92, +12-17%):** Earnings land in line with consensus ($7.28 FY2026E, $8.34 FY2027E). CRE credit remains manageable. Multiple expands modestly toward 12x as trailing GAAP P/E catches up to operating reality. Target: 12x × $7.28 = $87.4 (conservative); 12.5x × $7.28 = $91.0.

## Conclusion

INDB represents a well-managed New England community bank trading at a discount to estimated peers (10.8x vs. ~12.7x median) during a period of improving fundamentals. The market appears to be anchoring on GAAP trailing earnings that include non-recurring merger charges, rather than the forward operating earnings trajectory. With Q1 2026 demonstrating credit improvement, aggressive capital return, and stable NIM, the path to re-rating toward peer multiples is supported by concrete data. CRE concentration — particularly ongoing office stress — justifies some discount to peers, but the granular loan portfolio, improving trends, and management awareness limit the probability of a thesis-breaking credit event. The combination of 3.3% dividend yield, buyback accretion potential, and 10-15% multiple expansion potential creates a total return profile of 18-25% over 12 months. BUY with a base case target of $89-91.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The $200M buyback authorization referenced in the original thesis has not been confirmed as approved; available evidence indicates management plans to establish a new authorization in Q2 2026 but it may not yet be in effect. Additionally, Q1 2026 credit improvement coexists with ongoing office CRE stress ($17.7M new nonperforming loan, $2.8M specific reserve), and management is "pencils down" on near-term bank M&A. These factors moderate the certainty of the capital return and growth pillars supporting the price target.
