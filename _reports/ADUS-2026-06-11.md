**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is a multi-state in-home care provider that has compounded revenue from $951M (FY2022) to $1.423B (FY2025) through a disciplined acquisition strategy, most notably the December 2024 Gentiva personal-care deal. The business is asset-light, converts earnings to cash efficiently (FY2025 FCF $103.8M on $96M net income), and has deleveraged rapidly post-Gentiva: long-term debt fell from $218M (FY2024) to $91.3M at Q1 2026. On those metrics alone the company looks like a high-quality compounder trading at a reasonable 17.2x trailing earnings.

The reason the stock has fallen roughly 21% over the past twelve months, and now sits near its 52-week low ($87.95) at $93.17, is policy and concentration risk that does not show up in the trailing financials. Addus generates the bulk of revenue from government payors, and a single customer, the Illinois Department on Aging, represents approximately 18% of total revenue and 23% of net receivables. Layered on top is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 2025), which the company's own 10-Q states is "expected to result in Medicaid spending reductions." For a Medicaid-centric personal-care roll-up, that is the dominant swing variable over the next several years, and it is the most plausible explanation for why earnings keep rising while the multiple compresses.

I rate ADUS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The fundamentals are intact and improving, and a falsifiable bull case exists if Medicaid funding proves more durable than feared and 2026 rate increases (Texas +$17.7M, Illinois +$17.5M annualized) flow through. I cannot justify a BUY because the central risk is genuinely binary and unquantified: there is no near-term catalyst that resolves the OBBBA overhang, and the single-payor Illinois concentration can reprice the equity materially without warning. The Q1 2026 revenue miss and organic deceleration (personal care same-store growth slipped to 6.5% from 7.4%, home health organic turned negative at -6.6%) confirm that top-line momentum is softening even as cash generation stays strong.

## Company Overview

Addus operates 263 offices across 23 states through three segments:

- **Personal Care (~77% of revenue, $281M in Q1'26):** Non-medical assistance with activities of daily living, billed hourly, predominantly to state Medicaid/HCBS programs and managed care organizations (MCOs).
- **Hospice (~18%, $66M in Q1'26):** Palliative care, ~94% Medicare-funded, daily-rate billing.
- **Home Health (~5%, $17M in Q1'26):** Skilled nursing/therapy on an episodic basis, ~61% Medicare.

The strategic logic is consolidating a fragmented home-care market and building all three care levels within shared geographies, capturing patients as care shifts from institutions to the home and from fee-for-service toward managed care (MCOs were 38.3% of revenue in Q1'26, up from 36.4%). Clients are predominantly "dual eligible," qualifying for both Medicare and Medicaid. The competitive position rests on scale in a fragmented industry, government-relations expertise (a dedicated C-level Government Affairs function), and an acquisition machine, all riding an aging-demographics tailwind.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the case.

**Revenue and earnings:** FY2022 $951M → FY2023 $1.059B → FY2024 $1.155B → FY2025 $1.423B (+23% YoY, Gentiva-driven). Net income grew $46M → $63M → $74M → $96M, with net margin expanding to 6.7%. Adjusted EBITDA reached ~$180M in FY2025 from ~$98M in FY2021.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue +7.7% to $363.6M; net income +18% to $25.1M ($1.36 diluted EPS). Gross margin held at 31.9%; operating margin improved to 9.4%. Revenue came in below Street expectations, attributed to weather-related service disruptions and slower organic growth.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** FY2025 operating cash flow $111.5M, FCF $103.8M on capex of only ~$8M. Q1'26 operating cash flow surged to $52.4M (vs. $18.9M Q1'25) on AR timing and stimulus-fund flows. Debt paid down aggressively: $218M (FY2024) → $121M (FY2025) → $91.3M at Q1'26 (repaid $30M in the quarter). The $650M revolver matures July 2028 with $547.8M available; net leverage sits well below the 3.75x covenant. Cash $103M, equity $1.12B. DSO improved to 36 days.

The one structural caveat: goodwill ($997M) plus intangibles ($100M) equal ~76% of total assets, a function of the roll-up. The book is dominated by acquisition premiums, which makes impairment the key balance-sheet watch item if reimbursement deteriorates and acquired cash flows fall short of the carrying value.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record:** Over the last four quarters, two beats, one in-line, one miss, all within ±2.4% — visibility is high but the magnitude of beats is modest, which limits the prospect of upward earnings surprises driving a re-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two engines: organic same-store volume plus rate increases, and accretive tuck-in M&A funded by the asset-light cash flow and undrawn revolver.

The organic picture is decelerating. Q1'26 segment detail shows personal care same-store growth at 6.5% (down from 7.4%), hospice organic at +7.7% (down from 9.9%), and home health organic at -6.6%. Home health is structurally shrinking and faces a CMS-estimated 1.3% Medicare payment cut for CY2026, though it is only ~5% of revenue. Management cited weather for part of the Q1 softness, which is plausible but does not fully account for the trend.

The 2026 rate tailwinds are real and quantified: Texas +$17.7M annualized (effective Sep 1, 2025) and Illinois +$17.5M annualized (effective Jan 1, 2026), both at low-20% margins. Against a ~$1.42B revenue base, these add roughly 2.5 points of revenue growth and meaningful EBITDA, partially offsetting the organic deceleration.

M&A remains the swing factor. Recent tuck-ins — Helping Hands/PA ($21.4M), Gold Horses/TX ($7.4M), HomeCourt/Indiana ($12.5M, closed May 2026) — show the machine running. With ~$548M of undrawn revolver and net leverage low, there is substantial dry powder, and a federal crackdown on self-directed care operators may create cheap client-book acquisition opportunities. The catch is that M&A-driven growth is lumpy and does not by itself address whether the underlying reimbursement base is secure.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS estimate rose to $6.99 from $6.90 ninety days ago (+1.3%), next year to $7.47. This is positive momentum but well short of the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. Growth is steady rather than accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.17 with FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.99, ADUS trades at ~13.3x forward earnings; on next-year $7.47 it is ~12.5x. Trailing P/E is 17.2x. The peer set (Pediatrix 11.0x, Progyny 33.8x, with several names at negative earnings) is heterogeneous and not a clean comp, and the peer median of 33.8x is distorted by money-losing comparables. ADUS at low-teens forward earnings is not expensive in absolute terms for a business growing revenue high-single-digits to low-double-digits with strong cash conversion.

The valuation question is why the multiple has compressed from the high-teens to low-teens while earnings rose. The answer is the Medicaid policy overhang, not a deterioration in current results. This is a >20% drawdown from the 52-week high, which means sophisticated investors are selling, so the bull case requires a specific claim about what they are missing. The honest read is that they are not missing anything obvious: they are pricing a genuine, unquantified risk (OBBBA Medicaid cuts plus the CMS 80/20 rule) that has no near-term resolution. That is a rational reason for a lower multiple, not a mispricing.

The valuation supports a HOLD: the downside is cushioned by the cheap absolute multiple, low leverage, and strong FCF, but the upside re-rating requires the policy overhang to lift, which is outside management's control and unscheduled. Analyst targets (mean $132.69, median $135, range $92–$155) imply substantial upside, but those targets predate the full digestion of the OBBBA risk and the Q1 organic deceleration, and 12 of 14 analysts still rate it Buy/Strong Buy — consensus that has not yet adjusted to the policy reality reflected in the price.

## Competitive Landscape

Home-based care is large and fragmented with durable secular tailwinds: an aging population and a payor-driven shift away from costly institutional settings. Addus competes on scale and government-relations sophistication, and its three-segment model within shared geographies is a coherent way to capture patients across care levels.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on share (the acquisition machine consolidates the market) but the moat is narrow in one specific sense: the entire sector's economics are set by government reimbursement, not by competitive differentiation. Addus's heavy weighting to personal care/Medicaid insulates it from the CY2026 Medicare home-health rate cut but increases exposure to state Medicaid budget risk, which is precisely where OBBBA bites. The shift toward MCOs is a mild positive for payor diversification but introduces managed-care pricing pressure. I score competitive position modestly above neutral: the consolidation position is real, but the dependence on a policy regime the company cannot control caps how high the moat can be rated.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on this name and the reason it is not a BUY.

1. **Illinois single-payor concentration (idiosyncratic, high-severity).** The Illinois Department on Aging alone is ~18% of total revenue and ~23% of net receivables; Illinois overall is ~32% of company revenue. Any adverse change to Illinois budget or rate policy is a material, concentrated hit. The mitigant is that Illinois just *raised* rates for 2026 (in-home rate to $30.80/hr) and DSO from this payor is improving. Concentration of this magnitude in a single state agency can reprice the equity sharply.

2. **OBBBA / Medicaid funding cuts (macro-political, elevated, unquantified).** The 10-Q flags the Act as expected to reduce Medicaid spending, with greater restrictions in Medicaid-expansion states. There is no defined timeline and no way to quantify the impact yet, which is why it functions as an overhang on the multiple.

3. **CMS 80/20 rule.** By mid-2030, states must direct ≥80% of Medicaid HCBS payments to direct-care-worker compensation. If enforced as written, this structurally compresses personal-care margins. Implementation is uncertain (litigation likely), but it is a real long-dated margin risk.

4. **Labor and wage inflation.** Caregiver recruitment and retention in a tight market; minimum-wage increases (Illinois $18.75/hr for direct workers) without offsetting rate increases compress margins.

5. **Goodwill impairment.** ~76% of assets are goodwill and intangibles; reimbursement deterioration that impairs acquired cash flows would trigger writedowns.

These are specific, identifiable catalysts for material downside that the market is actively pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 58.9% against 31.1% one-year historical vol (rich by 27.8 points), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (58.9% near-term vs. 32.7% six-month). ATM IV jumped from 34.7% to 58.9% in ten days while the price rose 4.3%, pricing a near-term event around the June 18 expiry, with an implied move of ±5.7%. The skew is call-heavy (-52.6%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.24, so the positioning is not one-sided bearish; it reads as event-hedging into a catalyst with two-sided risk rather than a directional short. Combined with the policy overhang, this reinforces a wait-and-see HOLD rather than committing capital ahead of an unresolved binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$130–155):** OBBBA Medicaid reductions prove milder than feared and largely spare HCBS personal care; the Texas and Illinois 2026 rate increases (~$35M annualized combined at low-20% margins) drive EBITDA higher; the tuck-in M&A engine continues deploying the revolver accretively, potentially picking up cheap client books from distressed self-directed operators; organic growth re-accelerates as weather effects pass. In this scenario the multiple re-rates back toward the high-teens on $7.50+ forward EPS, and the $135 median target is validated. Implies ~40–65% upside.

**Bear Case (~$70–80):** OBBBA-driven Medicaid retrenchment pressures state budgets, forcing rate freezes or eligibility tightening; Illinois is squeezed; the CMS 80/20 rule advances and compresses personal-care margins; organic deceleration continues; and goodwill impairment becomes a question if acquired cash flows underperform. In this scenario forward EPS estimates fall and the multiple compresses further. Implies ~15–25% downside.

The asymmetry looks favorable on paper, but the bear case is a slow-burn policy outcome with no clean trigger to falsify, while the bull case requires a policy "all-clear" that may not arrive for quarters. That is the structure of a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) for confirmation that the Q1 organic deceleration was weather-driven and that 2026 rate increases are flowing through. Beyond that, any concrete CMS/state guidance on OBBBA Medicaid implementation is the key gating event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Concrete evidence that OBBBA implementation spares HCBS personal care (e.g., final CMS/state guidance that does not cut HCBS rates or eligibility), removing the central overhang.
- Personal care same-store organic growth re-accelerating above 7.5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Q1 softness was transitory.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions exceeding +10% over 90 days alongside re-accelerating organic growth, which would shift the risk/reward decisively.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An OBBBA implementation outcome or Illinois budget action that cuts Medicaid HCBS rates or eligibility in a way that meaningfully reduces the personal-care revenue base.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a covenant-relevant deterioration in cash flow.
- Personal care same-store growth turning negative or home health/hospice organic declines broadening across segments.

## Conclusion

Addus is a well-run, financially healthy compounder with real demographic tailwinds, strong cash conversion, and an accretive M&A model funded by a clean balance sheet. The 21% decline over the past year reflects neither balance-sheet stress nor an earnings collapse — earnings have risen — but a rational repricing of Medicaid policy and single-payor concentration risk that the trailing financials do not capture.

The validation question is whether the score (6.05 overall) justifies a BUY. It does not, for a specific and falsifiable reason: the dominant risk is binary and unscheduled. OBBBA is expected by management's own disclosure to reduce Medicaid spending, the company derives roughly 18% of revenue from a single state agency, and there is no near-term catalyst that resolves this overhang. A BUY requires either a quantifiable mispricing or a catalyst within ~60 days that closes the gap to fair value; neither is present here. The forward multiple is reasonable but not so cheap that it compensates for an unquantified Medicaid funding cut, and the steep options backwardation signals the market itself expects a near-term volatility event. The right posture is to hold through the policy resolution: own it if you already do, monitor Q2 organic trends and OBBBA implementation guidance, and upgrade only when the central overhang lifts or organic growth demonstrably re-accelerates.
