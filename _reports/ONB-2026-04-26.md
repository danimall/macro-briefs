## Executive Summary

Old National Bancorp is a $9 billion market cap Midwest-focused commercial bank that has undergone a significant transformation through its May 2025 acquisition of Bremer Financial Corporation, which added roughly $17 billion in assets. By Q1 2026, the integration is effectively complete: 100% of targeted cost savings have been realized, the adjusted efficiency ratio has compressed to a record 45.7%, and adjusted ROATCE is running at 18.4%. The $5.5 billion record commercial loan pipeline suggests organic growth momentum is building on top of the acquired base.

The stock trades at 12.0x forward earnings and 1.70x tangible book value, roughly in line with its regional bank peer group median. Consensus estimates are trending upward — FY2027 EPS of $2.90 implies 10.7% growth over FY2026's $2.62 estimate, and revisions over the past 90 days have been modestly positive. The sell-side consensus mean target of $27.91 implies approximately 19% upside from the current $23.36.

The principal risk is a 45% commercial real estate loan concentration ($22.1 billion), with $828 million of non-owner-occupied CRE maturing within 18 months at rates below 4%, creating refinancing stress for borrowers in the current environment. Net charge-offs have risen from 17bps to 25bps over two years, and CRE-specific charge-offs nearly tripled in FY2025. The bank's capital position (CET1 11.08%), liquidity buffers ($19.6 billion), and deposit cost advantage (1.72%) provide meaningful cushion.

## Company Overview

Old National Bancorp, headquartered in Evansville, Indiana, is the 6th largest commercial bank headquartered in the Midwest, with $72.2 billion in total assets, $48.8 billion in loans, $55.1 billion in deposits, and approximately 346 banking centers across Indiana, Illinois, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, Tennessee, North Dakota, and surrounding states. The bank also manages roughly $39 billion in wealth and investment assets.

The revenue mix is predominantly spread-based: net interest income constitutes 82% of total revenue, with the remaining 18% generated through wealth management ($144 million), service charges ($100 million), and capital markets/mortgage banking. The loan book is concentrated in commercial real estate (45%), commercial & industrial (31%), residential real estate (17%), and consumer (7%). Post-Bremer, Minnesota represents the largest single-state exposure at 21% of loans, followed by Illinois (17%) and Indiana (11%).

ONB has been among the most active acquirers in Midwest banking, completing three transformative transactions since 2022: First Midwest Bancorp (Chicago expansion), CapStar Financial Holdings (Tennessee footprint), and Bremer Financial Corporation (Minnesota/North Dakota). Management has signaled a shift toward organic growth and capital returns for 2026, though the 10-K explicitly states the company expects to continue to acquire, suggesting M&A will resume eventually.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability and Efficiency

FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.79 understates the earnings power of the franchise due to merger-related charges and $75.6 million in Day 1 CECL provisioning from Bremer. Adjusted EPS of $2.21 better reflects the run rate, and the quarterly trajectory confirms this: adjusted EPS progressed from $0.53 in Q2 2025 (the noisy Bremer close quarter) to $0.62 in Q4 2025 and $0.61 in Q1 2026.

The adjusted efficiency ratio compressed from 50.2% in Q2 2025 to 45.7% in Q1 2026 — a 450bps improvement in three quarters. For a $72 billion bank, sub-46% efficiency places ONB among the best operators in the mid-cap regional bank universe, confirming that 100% of targeted Bremer cost savings have been achieved.

Net interest margin peaked at 3.65% in Q4 2025 and compressed 10bps to 3.55% in Q1 2026 as the lag effects of 2025 rate cuts flowed through to asset yields. Deposit costs declined 8bps sequentially to 1.72%, partially offsetting the asset yield compression. The 3.55% NIM compares favorably to many peers, reflecting ONB's low-cost, granular deposit franchise.

Return on average tangible common equity has been running at 15-18% over recent quarters, with Q1 2026 at 18.4%. Sustaining this level of profitability through an integration cycle is strong evidence of franchise quality.

### Balance Sheet and Capital

Regulatory capital ratios are well above minimums: CET1 at 11.08%, total capital at 13.71% (boosted by a $450 million subordinated note issuance in Q1 2026). The loan-to-deposit ratio of 89% is conservative and provides capacity for incremental loan growth without stretching wholesale funding. Wholesale funding represents just 12% of total funding.

Tangible book value per share grew 15% in FY2025 to $13.71, a key management metric. At the current price of $23.36, the stock trades at 1.70x TBV — a reasonable premium given the ROATCE profile but not particularly cheap.

AOCI-related unrealized losses have improved meaningfully: AFS portfolio losses narrowed from $891 million to $570 million, and HTM losses improved from $484 million to $355 million, reflecting the benefit of 2025 rate cuts. Investment portfolio duration was reduced to 4.51 years from 5.09, lowering future rate sensitivity.

Free cash flow was $637 million in FY2025 (operating cash flow of $681 million less $44 million capex). Capital deployment has tilted toward buybacks: 3.9 million shares repurchased in Q1 2026, with up to $150 million of remaining authorization. The quarterly dividend of $0.14/share ($0.56 annualized) produces a 2.4% yield with a conservative 31% payout ratio.

### Credit Quality

This is where the risk concentrates. Net charge-offs rose to 25bps from 17bps in 2024 and 17bps in 2023. CRE-specific charge-offs escalated from $8.8 million (2023) to $15.9 million (2024) to $43.6 million (2025). Criticized and classified assets reached $3.1 billion, up from $2.5 billion even after adjusting for Bremer-acquired loans. Nonaccrual loans stood at $521 million (1.03% of loans in Q1 2026, improving from 1.07%).

The CRE maturity wall is the most specific near-term concern: $828 million of non-owner-occupied CRE matures within 18 months at rates below 4%. Borrowers refinancing at 6-7% rates face materially higher debt service costs, which could push marginal credits into distress. Office exposure at $2.7 billion ($49.2 million nonaccrual) and multifamily at $6.6 billion ($105 million nonaccrual) are the segments requiring the closest monitoring.

The allowance for credit losses is $560 million (1.15% of loans), which provides a reasonable buffer but is not excessive given the CRE concentration and rising charge-off trend.

## Growth Analysis

Organic loan growth of 5% in FY2025 (excluding Bremer) and an 8% annualized pace in Q1 2026 demonstrate that the franchise is generating real commercial banking momentum. The record $5.5 billion commercial pipeline (up 14% from the prior quarter) suggests this pace can sustain through at least mid-2026. Management guided for 4-6% full-year 2026 loan growth.

Consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.62 and FY2027 EPS of $2.90 imply approximately 19% and 11% growth respectively over FY2025's adjusted $2.21. The progression from integration-burdened GAAP EPS of $1.79 to the $2.60+ run rate reflects the full benefit of the Bremer cost synergies plus modest organic growth. Estimate revisions over the past 90 days are modestly positive: current-year up 0.7%, next-year up 1.2%.

The growth trajectory beyond 2027 depends on three factors: (1) NIM stability (with further downside if rates are cut again), (2) continued strong commercial loan production, and (3) whether management resumes acquisitions. The company's track record of successful integration provides optionality on the M&A front, though near-term focus is on harvesting the existing platform.

Fee income growth from wealth management ($39 billion AUM) is a secondary driver, contributing roughly 18% of revenue. This diversification provides some NII offset but is not large enough to materially change the growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.36, ONB trades at 12.0x trailing and forward P/E, 1.70x tangible book value, and offers a 2.4% dividend yield. Against the peer group median P/E of 12.6x and yield of 2.8%, ONB is trading at a modest discount on earnings and a slight discount on yield.

Given ONB's superior efficiency (45.7% adjusted vs. typical peer ranges of 50-60%), the stock arguably deserves a premium rather than a discount. The specific mispricing case rests on the market not yet fully pricing in the run-rate earnings power: if the $2.62 FY2026 estimate is met and the bank sustains 46-48% efficiency, a 12.5-13x multiple would place the stock at $33-34, implying roughly 40% upside. However, CRE credit concerns and macro uncertainty provide reason for the market's caution.

A more conservative framework: 12.0x the $2.62 FY2026 estimate = $31.44. At the bear-case multiple of 10x, the stock would be at $26.20 — still above the current price, providing some downside protection.

Tangible book value of $13.71 provides a floor-like reference. In a severe credit stress scenario, banks typically trade down toward 1.0-1.2x TBV, implying a floor of $13.71-$16.45 — a potential 30-41% drawdown from current levels. This is the tail risk scenario, not the base case.

## Competitive Landscape

The Bremer acquisition expanded the franchise into Minnesota and North Dakota, filling a geographic gap and creating the 6th largest Midwest-headquartered commercial bank. The deposit cost advantage of 1.72% reflects a granular, relationship-driven funding base that is difficult for competitors to replicate.

The 45.7% adjusted efficiency ratio places ONB among the best-run regional banks in the country by cost discipline. Lower costs allow for competitive pricing on loans while maintaining margin, which drives market share gains that further improve scale economics.

The principal competitive risk is from larger national banks with deeper product sets and lower funding costs, as well as from fintech competitors and — as management specifically flagged — stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act framework who could compete for deposit-like balances. The stablecoin risk is early-stage but worth monitoring as regulatory frameworks mature.

The 45% CRE concentration creates both competitive advantage (deep expertise in commercial real estate lending) and concentrated risk. The direction of competitive positioning depends heavily on whether CRE credit cycles remain manageable.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**CRE Concentration (HIGH).** A broader CRE downturn, particularly in office and multifamily, could materially impair earnings and capital given the 45% loan book concentration. The $828 million maturity wall at sub-4% rates amplifies near-term refinancing risk.

**Credit Quality Trajectory (MODERATE-HIGH).** The charge-off trend is moving in the wrong direction. While management attributes some of this to acquired PCD loan runoff, the trend requires monitoring through at least two more quarters before it can be considered stabilized.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE).** Under a -100bp rate shock, 2-year NII declines 0.91%, and under -300bp, NII declines 8.77%. The Fed funds target was at 3.50-3.75% at year-end 2025 (effective rate 3.64%). The bank is positioned roughly neutral with modest downside sensitivity to falling rates.

**Macro/Geopolitical Overhang (MODERATE).** Economic uncertainty creates a headwind for commercial loan demand and increases the probability of credit losses across the banking sector. ONB's Midwest/Southeast geographic focus provides some insulation from coastal market volatility but is not immune.

**Uninsured Deposit Concentration (MODERATE).** $23.7 billion in uninsured deposits. While ONB has not experienced meaningful deposit flight and maintains $19.6 billion in available liquidity, the post-SVB environment means this concentration remains a risk factor in any banking stress scenario.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.2% is more than double the 28.3% historical volatility, placing options in deeply rich territory. The term structure is in steep backwardation (58.2% near-term vs. 31.0% at 6 months), which typically signals the market is pricing a specific near-term event — likely the earnings report on April 22, which has now passed. The call-heavy put/call skew (-23.5%) at the near-term expiry suggests the options market leans bullish post-earnings. The elevated IV creates expensive entry via options but does not contradict the fundamental thesis. As IV normalizes toward 30% post-earnings, options strategies would become more attractive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28-30)

Bremer integration is complete and the franchise is operating at peak efficiency. If ONB sustains 45-48% adjusted efficiency, grows loans 5-6% organically, and maintains NIM above 3.45%, FY2026 adjusted EPS of $2.62 is achievable with upside potential toward $2.75+. At a 12-13x multiple reflecting superior efficiency and improving ROATCE, the stock reaches $28-34. Share buybacks at current levels are accretive to TBV growth. CRE credit concerns prove manageable with charge-offs stabilizing at 25-30bps. Multiple re-rating occurs as the market recognizes the post-merger earnings trajectory.

### Bear Case ($18-20)

CRE credit deterioration accelerates. The maturity wall at sub-4% rates produces a wave of modifications and charge-offs. NCOs rise toward 40-50bps, requiring reserve builds that compress EPS to $2.00-$2.20. NIM continues to compress as the rate environment shifts. The market applies an 8-9x multiple reflecting elevated credit risk, producing a stock price of $16-20. A broader recession would compound loan losses and reduce fee income from wealth management and capital markets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $27.50 — derived from 10.5x FY2027E EPS of $2.90 discounted modestly for execution risk, or equivalently ~12.5x FY2026E EPS of $2.62 assuming modest multiple expansion as credit concerns abate. Upside from current: ~18%.

**Bull case target:** $30.00 — consistent with the analyst consensus high target. Approximately 11.5x FY2026E adjusted EPS with credit quality stable and buybacks accelerating. Upside from current: +28.4%.

**Bear case target:** $19.00 — 9x reduced FY2026E EPS of ~$2.10 in a credit deterioration scenario. Downside from current: -18.7%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q2 2026 earnings (July), Q3 earnings (October) to verify CRE credit trends across two more reporting cycles. Full-year efficiency ratio trajectory will determine whether the 45-46% run rate is sustainable.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 35bps for two consecutive quarters, indicating CRE credit deterioration beyond acquired-loan runoff
2. Adjusted efficiency ratio rises above 50% for two consecutive quarters, suggesting cost savings are eroding
3. NIM falls below 3.30%, signaling the deposit cost advantage is narrowing or asset yields are compressing faster than expected

## Conclusion

Old National Bancorp has executed well on the largest acquisition in its history. The Bremer integration is producing measurable results: record-low efficiency ratios, 18%+ ROATCE, rising tangible book value, and a growing commercial pipeline. At 12x forward earnings with modestly positive estimate revisions and a 2.4% yield, the stock is trading at a slight discount to peers despite superior operating metrics.

The CRE concentration is the primary risk that prevents a higher-conviction recommendation. The 45% CRE exposure, rising charge-off trend, and near-term maturity wall at low rates create a plausible scenario where credit costs absorb the efficiency gains. The current macro environment amplifies this risk.

On balance, the risk/reward favors ownership. The efficiency transformation is demonstrated across multiple quarters. The commercial pipeline is at record levels. The valuation does not require aggressive assumptions. The 2.4% dividend provides income while the thesis develops. BUY with a 2-3% position size, with the understanding that the CRE credit cycle will ultimately determine the magnitude of returns.
