## Executive Summary

Scholar Rock is a pre-revenue biopharmaceutical company whose equity value is, on the company's own arithmetic, almost entirely an option on one regulatory decision. The August 6, 2026 prospectus supplement discloses net tangible book value of $253.8M, or **$2.09 per share**, against a market price of $50.855. Roughly 96% of the price is the present value of apitegromab, a myostatin-activation inhibitor that met its primary endpoint in the pivotal Phase 3 SAPPHIRE trial in non-ambulatory Type 2/Type 3 spinal muscular atrophy, and whose BLA carries a **September 30, 2026 PDUFA action date**.

The central fact pattern is unusual and matters more than any financial metric. The September 2025 Complete Response Letter was caused by a third-party fill-finish facility (Form 483 in July 2025, an OAI inspection classification, and a **Warning Letter in November 2025**, after the Type A meeting). The 10-Q states repeatedly that the CRL "did not cite any other approvability concerns, including apitegromab's efficacy and safety data or the third-party drug substance manufacturer." Management's remediation was structural rather than corrective: the March 2026 resubmission included **two** third-party fill-finish facilities, and the August 6, 2026 release describes "two independent paths to an FDA approval decision," with the second site's data package submitted, review "progressing well," and "ample supply" available at approval. That is a genuine de-risking of the date, though not a resolution. Catalent Indiana LLC's April 2026 inspection classification is disclosed as **pending**, the second facility's review is characterized as progressing rather than complete, and the second site is not named in the filings.

The financing picture is funded but constrained. Cash and securities rose $124.5M in the half to $492.1M, financed by ATM equity ($160.8M net in six months) and a doubling of secured debt to $200M under a February 2026 Blue Owl facility. Shares outstanding rose **12.1% in six months**, to 121.6M at June 30. The Blue Owl structure carries a minimum-unrestricted-cash covenant equal to 60% of outstanding principal, a **$120M floor** on the $200M drawn, which reduces usable liquidity to roughly $372M against a normalized burn above $330M annualized. A further $150M delayed-draw commitment becomes available **only from the date of FDA approval**, which aligns non-dilutive capital precisely with launch working-capital needs in the approval case and removes it in the non-approval case. On base/bull/bear values of $58 / $75 / $30, probability-weighted expected return from $50.855 is high-single to low-double digits against a roughly 40% downside tail resolving within seven weeks. That is approximately fair risk-adjusted compensation, with no margin of safety. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Scholar Rock (Cambridge, Massachusetts; new office in Zug, Switzerland from January 1, 2026) develops monoclonal antibodies against the *latent and precursor* forms of TGF-β superfamily growth factors rather than the mature signaling forms. The commercial thesis rests on a single asset.

**Apitegromab** binds pro- and latent myostatin in skeletal muscle, inhibiting activation of a negative regulator of muscle mass. The lead indication is SMA, where the drug is positioned as an **add-on** to SMN-targeted standard of care (nusinersen, risdiplam, onasemnogene abeparvovec) rather than a replacement. It holds FDA Orphan Drug, Fast Track (May 2021) and Rare Pediatric Disease designations, plus EMA PRIME (March 2021) and EC orphan designation.

Secondary programs: Phase 2 **FORGE** in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy initiated in Q2 2026 (~60 patients, 1:1, 10 mg/kg IV q4w, 52 weeks, primary endpoint mean lean muscle volume at 12 months; FSHD affects an estimated 30,000 people across the U.S. and Europe); Phase 2 **OPAL** in infants and toddlers under two already on gene therapy or SMN2-targeted therapy; the **ONYX** long-term extension; a completed Phase 1 of a high-concentration subcutaneous apitegromab formulation; and **SRK-439**, a subcutaneous anti-pro/latent myostatin antibody in Phase 1 with topline guided to 2H 2026 in the 10-Q and "late 2026" in the same-day press release.

Assets the company is explicitly shopping rather than funding: SRK-181 (Phase 2-ready latent TGF-β1 inhibitor in solid tumors), SRK-373 (fibrosis), SRK-256 (iron-restricted anemias), and myostatin inhibition combined with GLP-1 weight loss following the positive Phase 2 EMBRAZE study, which showed statistically significant preservation of lean mass during tirzepatide-induced weight loss. External SRK-181 spend fell 98.9% year over year in Q2 2026, to $9 thousand. No partnership has been announced for any of these.

Revenue generation has been limited to two historical collaborations; the last revenue year was 2022 ($33.2M). There is no product revenue.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze in the trailing data; there is a step function conditioned on a single date.

**The approval case.** SAPPHIRE enrolled non-ambulatory Type 2/Type 3 SMA patients, which the 10-Q describes as "the majority of the current prevalent SMA patient population in the U.S. and Europe." Apitegromab layers on top of existing SMN-targeted therapy, so adoption does not require switching, the patient population is identified and already treated at established centers of excellence, and the commercial team is described as launch-ready. Approval would also unlock $150M of committed non-dilutive capital and potentially a Rare Pediatric Disease priority review voucher — historically nine-figure assets, though the 10-Q lists four disqualifying conditions and voucher eligibility is not guaranteed.

**What the growth is worth.** Sizing peak sales for a rare-disease add-on requires assumptions the filings do not supply. Illustratively, on a US-plus-Europe non-ambulatory Type 2/3 base in the low tens of thousands, net annual pricing in the $250-350K range and 25-35% penetration, peak revenue lands roughly in the $1.0-1.8B band. At the current $5.90B enterprise value that is 3.3-5.9x peak sales for an asset that is not yet approved. Treat the range as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Pipeline optionality, unfunded.** FORGE in FSHD is the largest second indication, but its primary endpoint is lean muscle volume with functional measures exploratory, so a positive result will not by itself establish functional benefit. SRK-439 topline arrives late 2026. The GLP-1 lean-mass adjacency following EMBRAZE is the most commercially interesting non-SMA asset and is explicitly being partnered rather than funded — a deliberate decision to fund one launch, with the alternative reading that the company cannot compete in obesity against larger balance sheets and that a partnership may not materialize on attractive terms.

**The EU leg has moved out.** The 10-Q states the EMA "has a mutual recognition agreement with the FDA, and we believe satisfactory resolution of the OAI classification will be necessary for MAA approval," and that GMP non-compliance "could, in the most adverse scenario, even result in a negative CHMP opinion and refusal of the MAA." The August 6 release confirms the EMA is awaiting Catalent Indiana's classification, that adding the second site to the MAA is a "potential," and that CHMP timing guidance will be provided upon alignment. European revenue should be pushed out and haircut in any model. A newly expanded risk factor on EU pricing and reimbursement — reference pricing, budget-impact assessment, mandatory discounts, possible demands for additional real-world evidence — is directly relevant to a therapy that asks payers to fund an *additional* rare-disease product on top of existing high-cost regimens.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no earnings, cash-flow or asset anchor. Trailing EPS is $(3.48); next-year consensus is $(2.58). Net tangible book value is $2.09 per share; net cash is roughly $2.40 per share. The relevant exercise is scenario probability.

**Bear — second CRL, an extended delay, or a materially narrowed label: $30.** The prior CRL took the stock to a $27.07 52-week low. A second regulatory setback now, with a $6.19B capitalization embedding approval and roughly $50M/quarter of standing commercial G&A, removes the $150M delayed draw, leaves $200M of secured floating-rate debt against a $120M cash floor, and would likely force equity issuance at a depressed price. **−41% from $50.855.**

**Base — approval on or near September 30, U.S. launch begins, EU delayed: $58.** This is close to the analyst median of $58.50 and consistent with capitalizing a $1.2-1.5B peak-sales estimate at roughly 4-5x, discounted for launch execution and post-approval dilution. **+14%.**

**Bull — approval at the full SAPPHIRE population, PRV monetized, EU path clarified, FSHD optionality retained, plus mechanical squeeze pressure from 18.4% short interest: $75.** Slightly above the $70 street high. **+47%.**

**Implied market probability.** Solving $50.855 across a $30 bear and a $65 blended approval outcome gives roughly 60% implied approval probability. My own read is higher — perhaps 75-80% — because the FDA has already reviewed and not questioned efficacy, safety or the drug substance manufacturer, and because the resubmission created a second, independent supply path. Weighting 65% base / 15% bull / 20% bear gives an expected value of about $55, or **+8%**; a more generous 60/20/20 gives about $56, or **+10%**. Either way the expected return is high-single to low-double digits over seven weeks, with the entire distribution mass sitting at either +14% or −41%.

Two further considerations. First, the stock is 13% below its July 10 high of $58.49 while the operating picture arguably improved over the same period (second fill-finish data package submitted, cash up $124.5M). That improves entry mechanics at the margin. Second, the consensus target set (18 analysts, 3 strong buy, 15 buy, 0 hold or sell, mean $59.44) is not independent confirmation here: at a pre-revenue single-asset company, targets and price reflect the same approval extrapolation. A unanimous buy list with the low target at $48 — 5.6% below spot — indicates sell-side positioning is uniformly long into a binary, which is a crowding observation rather than a valuation input.

Valuation score of 5.0: the expected value is modestly positive, the identified reason the market may be underpricing approval odds is specific and falsifiable, and there is no margin of safety whatsoever.

## Competitive Landscape

Apitegromab is additive to the installed SMA base rather than competitive with it. SMN-targeted therapies address the genetic driver but do not restore lost muscle; SAPPHIRE dosed apitegromab on top of standard of care, and OPAL enrolls infants already on gene therapy or SMN2-targeted therapy. Adoption mechanics are favorable: no switching, an identified and already-treated population, established prescribing centers.

The dependency runs both ways, and the 10-Q says so explicitly: patients in ONYX and OPAL receive apitegromab in conjunction with an approved SMN-targeted treatment, and if access to nusinersen or risdiplam becomes limited or unavailable, the company "may be forced to pause or stop" those trials. Biogen and Roche are neither partners nor customers; they are independent commercial parties whose marketed products form the required background therapy. That structure also defines the payer conversation: a second high-cost rare-disease therapy layered on an existing one, which is precisely the dynamic the expanded EU pricing risk factor describes.

On class positioning, management's claim to "the world's first ever regulatory approval of a myostatin inhibitor" is accurate as a statement about the class if approval occurs, and a reminder that the class has a long record of failure in muscle indications. That history makes SAPPHIRE differentiating and also guarantees that payers and regulators will scrutinize the functional relevance of muscle-mass gains.

The structural weakness is manufacturing concentration. Third-party fill-finish capacity is concentrated among a handful of CDMOs; a single site's OAI classification and Warning Letter cost roughly twelve months of commercialization time on an asset whose efficacy was never in question. Management's response of adding a second site rather than waiting on a third party's remediation it does not control is the strongest competitive-position development in the filing, and the reason the trajectory reads as improving despite the setback.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory binary (dominant).** The 10-Q concedes there is "no guarantee of the timing of the resolution of the OAI classification of the facility that was reinspected or that the FDA will approve apitegromab." The second facility's review is "progressing well," not complete, and that site is unnamed. The November 2025 Warning Letter is an escalation of the first facility's compliance status.

**No asset floor.** Net tangible book value $2.09 versus a $50.855 price. Book value is a poor proxy for intrinsic value at any drug developer — it excludes IP, clinical data, the $921.9M federal NOL and regulatory designations — but there is no valuation support beneath the option value.

**Covenant and financing.** $120M minimum-cash floor on $200M drawn, unquantified milestone relief, floating-rate pricing at SOFR+500 with cash interest already rising ($4.4M in the half). The company states it has "no committed external source of funds" and will in any event require additional capital.

**Dilution.** Reserved shares total 34,208,694 and anti-dilutive equivalents 21,936,735 (3,598,746 RSUs; 1,450,809 PSUs; 6,686,383 options; 10,558,065 pre-funded warrants already in the EPS denominator). The reserved figure is not current fully diluted capitalization — it includes unissued plan capacity requiring future board grants and, for PSUs, satisfaction of unmet performance and market conditions. Treat it as scenario input, not current count. Unrecognized equity compensation is $194.2M.

**Label narrowing, EU pricing, commercial execution from zero, FDA staffing disruption** (a dedicated risk factor cites 2025 executive orders and reductions in force affecting FDA review capacity), **PRV non-materialization**, and **Section 382** limitation on the $921.9M federal / $861.4M state NOLs — a risk the company's own heavy equity issuance can help trigger.

**Governance.** Combined Chair/CEO reduces independent oversight. The 2025 leadership transition was expensive. Loss of smaller-reporting-company status without yet requiring auditor attestation on ICFR (non-accelerated filer through December 31, 2026) leaves a control-environment gap the 10-Q itself flags. The two unread Item 4.01 auditor-change filings from May-June 2025 remain an open question.

**Insider activity.** Every Form 4 from February through July 2026 is a sale, grant or option exercise; there are **no open-market purchases**. Four executives sold at an identical $49.5745 on April 16, 2026 (Hallal 30,615 of 1,639,034 held after the sale, or ~1.8%; Vaishnaw 12,246; Sinha 10,410; Woods 10,220), and five directors sold July 1 at $53.49-$54.98. The identical-price clustering is most consistent with pre-scheduled 10b5-1 execution under plans adopted before Q2 2026 (the 10-Q confirms no plan was adopted, modified or terminated during Q2), and executives at a company facing a PDUFA date are frequently barred from open-market purchases by blackout policy. I draw no conclusion about management's expectations for the FDA decision from this data. The absence of insider buying provides no offsetting confirmation at a moment when consensus loss estimates are widening, which is why management quality scores at 5.0 rather than higher despite genuinely good strategic decisions on the second fill-finish path and the approval-contingent debt structure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.6% sits marginally below 61.4% one-year realized (spread −2.7%, characterized as fair) and at only the 14th percentile within the current chain, so front-month volatility is not expensive. The term structure is in contango — 58.6% at 13 days, 58.9% at 41 days, 65.4% at 160 days — with implied moves of ±10.0%, ±16.9% and ±36.2% respectively. The September 18 expiry **does not span the September 30 PDUFA date**; the January 2027 tenor is where the event is priced, and a ±36.2% implied move there is a reasonable market estimate of the binary's magnitude, close to my own bear-case downside of −41%.

Positioning has shifted toward calls: put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.84 on June 4 to 0.30, with a put/call volume ratio of 0.11, while ATM IV rose 9.3 points and the price gained 11.3%. The near-term skew of 3.0% is characterized as elevated put demand at the one-month tenor; the six-month reading of −4.3% is reported without a directional claim. The divergence is between 18.4% short interest with 9.8 days to cover and call-heavy options positioning — two sets of institutions expressing opposite views on the same catalyst, which is the mechanical basis for the squeeze component of the bull case. For an existing holder wanting to carry through September 30 with defined risk, October or January tenors are the correct instruments; the front month is cheap but expires before the event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75, +47%)

The FDA has already reviewed apitegromab's efficacy, safety and drug substance manufacturing and raised no approvability concerns; the only obstacle was a third-party fill-finish site. Management bought a parallel path rather than waiting on a remediation it does not control, and the second site's data package is submitted with review progressing. Approval on or shortly after September 30 delivers the first-ever myostatin inhibitor and the first muscle-targeted therapy in SMA, into a population already identified, already treated and served by established centers, with a launch-ready commercial organization and $150M of committed non-dilutive capital unlocking on the approval date. A priority review voucher, if granted, is a nine-figure non-dilutive asset. FSHD, subcutaneous apitegromab, SRK-439 and a potential GLP-1 lean-mass partnership are all unpriced. Against 18.4% short interest and 9.8 days to cover, approval would force covering into a thin float.

### Bear Case ($30, −41%)

Catalent Indiana's classification is still pending, its Warning Letter has not been resolved, and the second site's review is not complete. A second CRL or a multi-quarter delay strands roughly $50M per quarter of standing commercial G&A, removes access to the $150M delayed draw at exactly the moment equity is least attractive, and leaves $200M of secured floating-rate debt against a $120M cash floor and $372M of usable liquidity burning above $330M annualized. Financing would come at a depressed price into an ATM already responsible for $257.7M of cumulative proceeds and a 12.1% six-month share-count increase. Even in approval, EU timing guidance has been withdrawn and the MAA as filed contains only Catalent Indiana; European pricing and reimbursement for an add-on rare-disease therapy on top of existing high-cost regimens is a newly expanded, specifically elaborated risk. Label narrowing below the SAPPHIRE population would compress the addressable base. Net tangible book value is $2.09 per share, so there is nothing beneath the option.
