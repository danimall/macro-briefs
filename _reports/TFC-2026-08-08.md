## Executive Summary

Truist's June quarter was, on the headline, the best print since the BB&T/SunTrust merger: diluted EPS of $1.23 versus roughly $1.09 consensus (+12.9%), a fourth consecutive beat, ROTCE of 15.4% against 12.3% a year earlier, pre-provision net revenue up 10.1%, and 320 basis points of positive operating leverage. Management simultaneously raised its 2026 fee-income growth target to approximately 10% and **lowered** its full-year revenue and net interest income guidance. Both facts describe the same quarter: capital markets and the tax line are running ahead of plan, and net interest income — 69% of revenue — is running behind it.

The composition matters. Decomposing the $0.32 year-over-year EPS increase against 10-Q inputs, roughly 38% is attributable to core revenue growth net of expenses; the balance comes from a 5.1% lower share count (+$0.06), a 360bp lower effective tax rate (+$0.05), a $93M lower provision (+$0.06), and lower preferred dividends (+$0.02). None of those four is illegitimate — the tax and provision improvements are at least partly structural — but they are not the same as spread revenue compounding. Net interest income grew 0.9% while average loans grew 5.7%, because the total loan yield fell 33bps (C&I fell 47bps), noninterest-bearing deposits slipped to 25.6% of average deposits from 26.6%, and about $7–9B of the loan growth was funded with wholesale debt at 4.77%.

At $52.35 the stock is 1.57x tangible book value per share of $33.40, 1.09x reported book of $48.04, ~11.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.62 and ~10.5x FY2027 consensus of $4.99. On trailing FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.82 it is 13.7x — essentially at the 13.6x peer median, which is the honest apples-to-apples comparison. A Gordon-style justified multiple at 15% ROTCE, 3% growth and a 10% cost of equity is 1.71x tangible book; at an 11% cost of equity, 1.50x. Current 1.57x is therefore approximately fair if the 15% return is durable, and cheap only if the recently raised 16–18% longer-term target is credible from a CEO who has not yet started. The 3.97% dividend, alongside a $5B buyback plan, produces an ~11.9% total distribution yield and supports ownership. It does not produce the asymmetry required for a higher rating. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Truist Financial is a Charlotte-based bank holding company with $556.0B of total assets at June 30, 2026, operating 1,927 branches and employing 38,711. Its principal subsidiary, Truist Bank, is a North Carolina state-chartered bank supervised by the FDIC and the NCCOB. The company is a Category III banking organization, subject to LCR and NSFR at 85% of the full requirement, annual supervisory stress testing, single-counterparty credit limits and 165(d) resolution planning.

Two segments. **Consumer and Small Business Banking** carried ~$133B of average loans, ~$217B of average deposits and $835M of Q2 net income. **Wholesale Banking** — commercial and corporate banking, investment banking and capital markets, wealth management and payments — carried ~$196B of average loans, ~$149B of average deposits, $350B of total client assets and $960M of Q2 net income.

The durable asset is the deposit franchise. Four states account for roughly 70% of deposits — Florida (22%, 4th share), Georgia (21%, **1st**), Virginia (14%, 3rd) and North Carolina (13%, 2nd) — with a top-five position in Tennessee and top-four positions in Maryland and South Carolina (both 3rd) as well. That concentration in above-average-growth Southeastern markets is the concrete basis for a 1.56% total deposit cost (down 29bps year over year), an 80.6% loan-to-deposit ratio and a 113% LCR.

Worth noting is the candor of the company's own competition disclosure, which names disintermediation from "limited-purpose banks and nonbanks involved in digital assets, stablecoins, cryptocurrencies, tokenization," concedes that certain competitors have "greater scale, financial and operational resources, investment capacity," and acknowledges rivals "willing to suffer meaningful financial losses in the pursuit of disruptive innovation." Its stated countervailing advantage rests on "purpose, mission, and values" — not a quantifiable moat.

## Financial Analysis

### Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025

| | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income (TE) | $3,667M | $3,635M | +0.9% |
| Noninterest income | $1,644M | $1,400M | +17.4% |
| Total revenue (TE) | $5,311M | $5,035M | +5.5% |
| Noninterest expense | $3,055M | $2,986M | +2.3% |
| PPNR (TE) | $2,256M | $2,049M | +10.1% |
| Provision | $395M | $488M | −19.1% |
| Effective tax rate | 14.4% | 18.0% | −360bps |
| Diluted EPS | $1.23 | $0.90 | +36.7% |
| Diluted shares | 1,239.0M | 1,305.0M | −5.1% |
| NIM (TE) | 2.98% | 3.02% | −4bps |
| Efficiency ratio | 58.0% | 59.9% | −190bps |
| ROTCE | 15.4% | 12.3% | +310bps |

The expense discipline is real: 2.3% cost growth against 5.5% revenue growth is the cleanest thing in the quarter, and the 190bp efficiency improvement is genuine progress even if 58.0% remains high versus the best-run large regionals.

**The net interest income problem is mechanical, not cyclical noise.** NIM has compressed for two consecutive quarters (3.07% in Q4 2025 → 3.02% in Q1 2026 → 2.98% in Q2 2026). Average C&I loans grew 6.5% to $168.8B while the C&I yield fell 47bps to 5.25%. Average deposits grew only 1.1% (~$4.4B) against ~$17.8B of average loan growth; the gap was closed by shrinking securities (−3.0%) and other earning assets (−6.2%) and by borrowings — average long-term debt rose 19% to $40.6B at 4.77%, short-term borrowings rose 10% to $28.9B at 3.97%. Buying loan growth with price and funding it with wholesale debt is a recognizable pattern, and it is why 5.7% loan growth produced 0.9% NII growth. Management's alternative framing — deliberate migration toward relationship commercial lending and away from lower-return consumer books — is consistent with the same data and with the disclosed pruning of marine, RV and non-prime auto originations.

The offsetting tailwind is quantified and independent of Fed policy: new fixed-rate loans are running on at ~7.37% against a 5.71% weighted-average rate on the existing $131B fixed-rate book, with a securities portfolio yielding 2.96%. Each dollar of runoff reprices materially higher. That is a multi-year mechanical lift that the current NII guidance of +1.0–1.5% partly masks.

### Balance sheet, capital and the tangible book problem

HFI loans $329.8B; securities $114.0B carrying value (98.6% UST/GSE/agency MBS); deposits $409.4B; total equity $64.1B. CET1 10.9% (preliminary) on $434.8B RWA, Tier 1 12.2%, total capital 14.0%, SLR 8.2%. Available secured borrowing capacity of $180.1B plus $32.0B of Fed cash equals $212.1B, which the company states is 4.8x wholesale funding maturing within one year. All four rating agencies at stable outlook.

Liquidity is a genuine strength and there is no funding vulnerability visible in the filings.

The item the EPS headline hides is that **tangible book value per share went nowhere in H1: $33.48 at December 31 to $33.40 at June 30, despite $2,896M of net income available to common.** Reconstructing the bridge: +$2,896M earnings, −$1,281M common dividends, −$947M other comprehensive income, −$2,355M share repurchases, +~$221M other items, for a ~$1,466M decline in tangible common equity. Two mechanics:

1. **AOCI deteriorated $947M**, almost entirely from cash flow hedges on commercial loans (the AOCI hedge component moved from −$173M to −$1,035M on a −$1,223M pre-tax H1 OCI loss). A further $401M after tax is scheduled to reclassify into earnings over the next twelve months — a known, quantified NII headwind already sitting on the balance sheet.
2. **Buybacks above tangible book are TBVPS-dilutive.** 46.577M shares were retired for $2,355M in H1, roughly $50.56 per share including excise tax, against $33.40 of TBVPS — consuming approximately $800M of tangible book ((50.56 − 33.40) × 46.577M).

At a 15.4% ROTCE and a ~10% cost of equity, retiring shares at 1.5x tangible book is defensible EPS and ROTCE arithmetic. The relevant point for investors is that the capital plan converts book value into per-share earnings rather than compounding book value — and "TBVPS + Dividend Growth" is one of the company's own executive compensation performance measures.

### The buyback's dependence on RWA reduction

The board authorized a $10B repurchase program in December 2025, of which $7.7B remained at June 30, and guidance targets ~$5B of repurchases in FY2026 versus $2.5B in FY2025. The Q2 total payout ratio was 121%. RWA fell $8.46B in H1 (from $443.3B to $434.8B) while HFI loans grew $1.2B.

Working the arithmetic: on FY2026 consensus of roughly $4.62–4.70, net income to common lands near $5.7–5.8B against common dividends of ~$2.6B plus $5.0B of buybacks — about $7.6B of distributions against $5.75B of capital generation, implying ~$1.8B of CET1 depletion. Holding CET1 at 10.9% would then require roughly $16.8B of RWA reduction, almost exactly H1's annualized pace of $16.9B. The capital-return story and the balance-sheet-optimization story are one story with a finite runway, and it sits in tension with 5–7% loan growth ambitions.

The mitigant is that CET1 of 10.9% is roughly 390bps — about $17.0B, or 27% of market cap — above the 7.0% minimum-plus-stress-capital-buffer requirement. Management has operated in a 10.8%–11.0% band, so this is regulatory headroom rather than distributable capital, but it does mean the buyback can be funded by letting CET1 drift lower if RWA optimization slows. A separate unresolved question: RWA fell while loans grew, so risk-weight density improved. The filings do not identify which exposures drove the decline, and improved standardized risk weights are not necessarily lower economic risk.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance for FY2026: revenue-TE growth of 3.5%–4.0% (lowered), noninterest expense growth ~1.75%, NII growth 1.0%–1.5% (lowered), fee income ~10% (raised), NCO ratio ~55bps, effective tax rate ~14.5%, repurchases ~$5B. Q3: revenue up ~1% sequentially, NII up ~1.5%. The NII outlook **assumes a 25bp Fed hike in September 2026**.

That assumption deserves attention. Truist's disclosed rate sensitivity is close to neutral in absolute terms (+200bps gradual = −0.2% NII; −200bps gradual = −1.2%, roughly $175M on ~$14.5B of annual NII), so a no-hike outcome is a guidance shave rather than a thesis break. It does, however, mean the low end of the NII range is the more likely outcome if the hike does not materialize.

Fee income is doing the growth work. Investment banking and trading revenue of $352M rose 71.7% year over year, with 1H26 advisory revenue up 27% across M&A, ECM and financial risk management. Wealth management fees rose 7.8% to $375M. This is real revenue, and the raised 10% fee target reflects genuine momentum. It is also the most cyclical line the company has — a 72% quarterly comparison is not a run rate, and capital-markets revenue mean-reverts with issuance windows.

Underlying franchise momentum is better than the NII line suggests. Consumer disclosed 5.4M active mobile users (+4%), a 39% year-over-year increase in new-to-bank deposit production, and deposit production per Premier advisor +23%. Wholesale disclosed a 35% increase in 1H26 commercial and corporate new-client acquisition, four consecutive quarters of client deposit growth, and 6% growth in commercial and middle-market deposits. Client acquisition of that magnitude eventually shows up in balances; it has not yet shown up in spread income.

Estimate revisions are the discipline here, and they are mixed-to-negative: current quarter −1.8%, next quarter −4.0%, current year **+1.7%**, next year **−2.3%** (18 analysts). The current-year raise reflects the Q2 beat; the out-year cut reflects the NII guidance reduction. Analyst distribution has degraded from 4 strong buy / 7 buy / 7 hold / 1 sell / 1 strong sell three months ago to 1 / 5 / 12 / 1 / 2 today. Set against four consecutive beats averaging +7.2%, this is a company delivering on quarters while consensus trims the trajectory — a pattern that argues for reasonable confidence in FY2026 and less in FY2027.

Multi-year targets: ROTCE ~14% in 2026, ~15% in 2027, 16%–18% longer term, the last of which was raised in April 2026. The 16–18% target implies either substantial efficiency gains or materially higher fee mix; it is now the incoming CEO's target to defend or reset.

Peer comparables — PNC 13.6x, USB 12.1x, WFC 12.7x, BAC 13.7x, C 17.2x — put TFC's trailing multiple at the median, not at a discount. The forward multiple of 11.3x looks cheaper, but peers would also compress on forward earnings; the honest statement is that Truist trades in line with large-bank peers on comparable bases, with a 175bp dividend-yield premium reflecting both a higher payout ratio and the market's discount for chronic underperformance.

(Note a data inconsistency: the quote feed reports identical trailing and forward P/E of 12.09, implying EPS of ~$4.33, which matches neither FY2025 GAAP $3.82 nor FY2026 consensus $4.62. The multiples above are computed directly from reported EPS and consensus.)

The most useful cross-check is return-based. A sustained 15.4% ROTCE at 1.57x tangible book implies an earnings yield on price of 9.8%. The justified multiple at 15% ROTCE, 3% growth and a 10% cost of equity is (15−3)/(10−3) = 1.71x; at 11% cost of equity, 1.50x. Current 1.57x sits inside that band. There is no identifiable mispricing at this price — the market is paying approximately what a 15% ROTCE is worth, and the residual upside depends on the 16–18% target becoming credible, which requires the new CEO to deliver an efficiency or mix program that does not yet exist.

Two adjustments cut the other way. HTM securities carry $46,351M of amortized cost against $38,145M of fair value, an $8,206M gross unrealized loss on a 7.2-year duration book. Roughly $1,812M after tax is already captured in the AOCI HTM component; the incremental unrecognized loss is on the order of $4.5B after tax, or ~$3.69 per share, about 11% of TBVPS. Adjusting for it, effective price-to-tangible-book rises toward 1.76x. This is an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precise mark, and it does not affect regulatory capital today — but it is the reason a 1.57x multiple on stated TBV is not the bargain it appears.

Consensus targets: mean $54.92, median $55.00, high $61.00, low $45.00. Mean upside is 4.9%. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here out-year estimates are falling, so the targets are better read as a fair-value band than as a stale ceiling.

## Competitive Landscape

Truist competes well where deposits are concerned and poorly where spread income is generated. The franchise holds top-four deposit share in states representing ~70% of deposits, including #1 in Georgia and #2 in North Carolina, at a 1.56% blended deposit cost with an 80.6% loan-to-deposit ratio. Those are the marks of a real retail franchise, and the 39% increase in new-to-bank deposit production suggests the trajectory is improving rather than eroding.

The structural challenges are equally clear. NII grew 0.9% on 5.7% average loan growth. Loan yields fell 33bps while noninterest-bearing deposit share slid ~100bps year over year (from 26.6% to 25.6% of average deposits). Funding growth came from wholesale debt at 4.77% rather than deposits. The 58.0% efficiency ratio, improved but still high, leaves less room to absorb spread pressure than better-run peers have. And the fee growth carrying the quarter is concentrated in the most cyclical line on the income statement.

The five-year record frames the debate. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative TSR of 129.86 for Truist against 196.02 for its peer group — $29.86 of return per $100 invested versus $96.02 — with Truist below its peer group in each of the five reported years. RBC's Gerard Cassidy calculated that the shares rose only 2%–3% from the day before the December 2019 merger announcement through June 2026. Adjusted EPS ran $5.53 (2021) → $4.96 → $3.81 → $3.81 → $4.00 (2025), though the 2021–22 figures include Truist Insurance Holdings, sold in 2024, whose proceeds funded the buybacks that lowered the share count. The decline is substantially portfolio composition rather than pure operating deterioration. Even discounting for that, six and a half years without price appreciation is the reason an outsider was hired.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Proposed inclusion of AOCI in regulatory capital.** On March 19, 2026, the FDIC, FRB and OCC issued joint notices of proposed rulemaking that would require Category III and IV organizations, Truist included, to recognize most AOCI elements in regulatory capital over a proposed five-year transition. AOCI at June 30 was −$6,716M (AFS −$3,486M; HTM −$1,812M; cash flow hedges −$1,035M; pension/OPEB −$386M). CET1 capital of roughly $47.4B less $6.7B on $434.8B RWA implies ~9.36%, a ~154bp reduction. Softening factors: the five-year transition, the paired standardized-RWA revisions that could shrink the denominator, and the natural pull of AOCI toward par as instruments roll off. The perverse interaction deserves flagging — because the drag is now driven mainly by cash flow hedges on commercial loans, rising rates would help NII while eroding regulatory capital under the proposed rule. This is a proposal, not a rule.

**2. Wholesale Banking goodwill sensitivity.** Goodwill is $17,125M. The FY2025 10-K disclosed that in the October 1, 2025 quantitative test, a 100bp increase in the discount rate would push the Wholesale Banking reporting unit's fair value below carrying value under the income approach. The Q2 10-Q reaffirms the estimate as critical and concludes no impairment was more likely than not at June 30. Mechanically, an impairment would not reduce CET1 or TBVPS — goodwill is already fully deducted from both — so its significance is informational: it would be management conceding that WB's discounted cash flows have fallen below carrying amount in the segment that generated $960M of Q2 net income and the entire fee-growth story.

**3. Credit stress being managed through modifications.** Headline metrics are benign: total NCOs 0.50% annualized, ALLL/loans 1.51%, nonperforming loans 0.51% of HFI. Beneath: indirect auto modifications of $991M in H1, 4.16% of that portfolio, of which $945M were payment delays; loans modified within twelve months and in payment default rose to $408M from $192M, with C&I renewals in default rising from $23M to $186M; nonaccruals within the modified population rose from $305M to $874M. The indirect auto NPL jump from $267M to $569M is explicitly definitional (a January 1, 2026 change making cumulative extensions of ≥12 months trigger nonaccrual), and auto NCOs actually improved sequentially to 1.73% from 2.14%. Modification activity typically leads charge-offs by two to four quarters. In context, $408M is 0.12% of loans, indirect auto carries 4.2% allowance coverage, and management's pruning of marine, RV and non-prime auto suggests it is ahead of the trend rather than behind it.

**4. NDFI exposure.** Finance and insurance is $32,524M, or 19.3% of C&I, of which approximately 56% is nonbank financial institutions — about $18.2B, roughly 5.5% of HFI loans. That exposure was essentially flat in H1 (+1.3%), and NPLs across the whole finance-and-insurance category are $6M. These facilities are typically senior and over-collateralized. The caution is that NDFI credit is characteristically zero-loss until it is not, and the filings disclose neither collateral composition nor borrower-base quality. This is the exposure most worth monitoring, though the sector-wide framing here is analyst inference from the disclosed data rather than an externally verified view.

**5. CEO transition.** Michael P. Lyons becomes President and CEO on September 1, 2026, the first outsider to lead Truist since formation — 13 years at PNC (where he helped lead more than $15B of acquisitions including BBVA USA, and was named President in February 2024), then roughly a year as CEO of Fiserv, which "hit an unexpected earnings shortfall" in Q3 2025. William Rogers becomes Executive Chair through the 2027 annual meeting, with full retirement planned for April 2027, retained partly via an $8.5M 2027 PSU award contingent on continued service. Lyons receives ~$17.5M of target compensation plus approximately $40M of replacement awards for forgone Fiserv compensation. The shares fell 6% on the June 15 announcement. The risks are a guidance and target reset (the 16–18% ROTCE ambition is now inherited, not owned), an eight-month dual-authority period that may slow decisive action, and a large share of the incoming CEO's near-term wealth being non-contingent on Truist performance.

**6. Interchange regulation.** The FRB's proposed Regulation II revisions would lower the debit interchange cap applicable to Truist Bank, and the Illinois Interchange Fee Prohibition Act took effect July 1, 2026 without being enjoined, with other states considering broader versions. Consistent with pressure already arriving: card and payment-related fees were $353M in Q2, up just 0.6%, even as card spend rose $1.4B to $32B.

**7. Resolved risk.** The 16-year-old *Bickerstaff* Georgia usury class action, with theoretical exposure of ~$478M, settled for up to $240M with final court approval on May 26, 2026 and a September 14, 2026 claims deadline. Aggregate reasonably possible losses in excess of accruals across all matters were up to ~$150M — immaterial against $556B of assets.

**8. Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director between February and July 2026. Three sales clustered after Q2 earnings: Rogers 13,250 shares at $52.363 (July 20), Wilson 13,280 at $51.85 (July 31), director Boyer 3,986 at $50.70 (July 23). The sales are small relative to holdings (a March filing showed Rogers at 968,216 shares) and consistent with routine post-vest diversification; large banks also restrict purchase windows. The absence of buying at 1.57x tangible book during a leadership transition is a mild non-endorsement rather than a signal, though it carries slightly more weight than usual because out-year estimates are falling rather than rising.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is calm and slightly constructive. Near-term ATM IV of 24.0% sits essentially on top of 24.2% one-year realized volatility (spread −0.2%, 7th percentile within the chain), with a balanced 1.0% put/call skew, a put/call volume ratio of 0.33 and OI ratio of 0.45 — both call-tilted. Term structure is in contango (24.0% near versus 28.4% at six months), the normal shape. ATM IV has fallen from 27.6% on August 3 to 24.0% while the price rose 0.5%, and the OI ratio eased from 0.50 to 0.45. The nearest expiry prices a ±3.8% move. Short interest is 2.5% of float at 2.9 days to cover.

The read: the options market is not pricing the CEO transition, the AOCI rulemaking or credit migration as near-term events, and shows no demand for downside protection. That corroborates the HOLD framing — there is neither a stress signal to trade against nor cheap vol to buy — but it also means any negative surprise around the September 1 handover or the Q3 print would land into complacent positioning. The only mildly notable feature is the −2.4% six-month skew, at a tenor that captures both the transition and the November-quarter results.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $63 (+20%)

The fixed-rate repricing engine does the work that guidance understates: $131B of fixed-rate loans at 5.71% rolling into a 7.37% run-on rate, plus a $114B securities book at 2.96%, delivers NII acceleration in 2027 regardless of Fed policy. Fee momentum proves broader than one strong capital-markets quarter — 35% growth in commercial new-client acquisition and 39% growth in new-to-bank deposit production convert into balances, restoring deposit-funded loan growth and arresting the noninterest-bearing mix decline. Expense discipline holds at ~1.75% growth, driving the efficiency ratio through 56% and ROTCE to the guided 15% in 2027. Lyons arrives with a credible efficiency and technology agenda, reaffirms the 16–18% longer-term target with a specific plan, and the market re-rates toward 1.85x tangible book — roughly $63, above the $61 street high. The $5B buyback retires ~7.8% of shares at a price the company continues to judge accretive at 15%+ ROTCE, and the 2.5% SCB floor extended into October 2027 removes capital-requirement uncertainty for the duration.

### Bear Case — $43 (−17.9%)

The September hike embedded in guidance does not arrive; the Fed cuts instead, and Truist's newly asymmetric rate profile (−1.2% NII on a −200bp gradual path versus −0.2% on +200bps) bites. NII lands at or below the low end, and the $401M of after-tax cash-flow-hedge losses scheduled to reclassify over the next twelve months compounds the drag. Capital-markets revenue mean-reverts from a 71.7% growth quarter, and the raised 10% fee target is cut. Modification activity converts: $408M of modified loans in payment default and $874M of nonaccruals within the modified population migrate into charge-offs above the guided 55bps, forcing provision above plan. The Wholesale Banking goodwill test fails on a higher discount rate, which — while capital-neutral — signals a downgraded view of the fee engine's value and invites a reset of the 16–18% target. RWA optimization slows, the $5B buyback is trimmed, and the EPS growth that depended on it disappears. Multiple compresses to 1.25x tangible book, roughly $43, near the March 2026 low of $43.83 and just below the $45 street low.

### Base Case — $56 (+7.0%), ~11% total return with the dividend

FY2026 EPS lands near $4.62–4.70 with fees offsetting the NII shortfall and the ~14.5% tax rate delivering as guided. FY2027 consensus settles near $4.95 after the transition-related trimming. TBVPS accretes modestly to $34.50–35.00 as AOCI stabilizes, partly offset by continued above-book repurchases. Applying 1.60x tangible book gives $55–56; 11.2x forward EPS of $4.95 gives $55.4. Base $56, against the consensus mean of $54.92 and median of $55.00. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear produces roughly 4% of price return plus a 4% dividend — about 8% total, at or slightly below a reasonable 10% cost of equity for a bank of this quality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (fourth consecutive).** The prior three HOLDs at scores of 5.8 → 6.0 → 5.9 have proven directionally right — the stock has compounded roughly with its dividend. The score moves to 5.7, and the reason is specific rather than price-driven: since the May report the shares have risen about 8% (from $48.46 on May 27) while FY2027 consensus fell 2.3% and management lowered revenue and NII guidance. Simultaneously, three risks that were previously abstract became concrete: the CEO handover is now dated (September 1), the AOCI-in-capital proposal is now quantifiable (~154bps), and the modified-loan payment-default series has more than doubled. Financial health holds at 6.4 on genuine capital and liquidity strength; valuation and risk absorb the decline. This is an income hold, not a growth hold — the base case offers 7% of price upside, and the reason to own the shares is the 3.97% dividend inside an ~11.9% total distribution yield.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in mid-October, which will be the first quarter of Lyons' tenure and the first read on whether guidance survives a new CEO's review. The September FOMC decision is the nearer input, since guidance embeds a 25bp hike. A strategy update or investor day under new leadership — plausibly late 2026 or early 2027 — is the more consequential event.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at or below $46–47 (≈1.40x current TBVPS, ~10.1x FY2027 consensus) with FY2026 guidance intact — that entry restores a double-digit expected return against the same fundamentals.

## Conclusion

Truist is a well-capitalized bank with a genuinely valuable Southeastern deposit franchise — top-four share in states holding 70% of deposits, a 1.56% deposit cost, an 80.6% loan-to-deposit ratio and $212B of contingent liquidity — that is currently converting book value into per-share earnings faster than it is compounding either. The June quarter's 15.4% ROTCE and 320bps of operating leverage are real improvements; so is the fact that net interest income grew 0.9% on 5.7% loan growth, that tangible book per share was flat through six months of $2.9B earnings, and that management lowered its revenue and NII outlook in the same release that beat consensus by 13%.

At 1.57x tangible book against a 15% ROTCE, the shares are priced at approximately what that return is worth using a 10–11% cost of equity. The upside case requires the 16–18% longer-term target to become credible, which is now a question about an incoming CEO who starts September 1 and whose predecessor remains Executive Chair through the 2027 annual meeting. The downside case requires no more than the September hike embedded in guidance failing to arrive, capital-markets revenue mean-reverting, and the disclosed modification pipeline converting into charge-offs.

**HOLD.** Base case $56 (+7.0%), bull $63 (+20%), bear $43 (−17.9%); probability-weighted total return of roughly 8% including the dividend. Own it for the covered 3.97% yield and the ~11.9% total distribution yield. New capital should wait for $46–47, or for evidence that net interest margin has turned and the new CEO owns the return target rather than inheriting it.
