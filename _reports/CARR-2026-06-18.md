**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carrier Global has completed one of the most aggressive portfolio reshapings in the industrials sector: a $13B acquisition of Viessmann Climate Solutions (closed January 2024), the full exit of Fire & Security and Commercial Refrigeration (~$10B in divestitures across 2024), and a pending Riello sale (~$430M). The result is a pure-play HVAC and climate-solutions company with FY25 net sales of $21.7B and a four-segment structure dominated by Climate Solutions Americas (CSA), which generates most of the profit at normalized 20%+ margins.

The investable tension is a divergence between the income statement and the order book. Reported numbers are weak: FY25 revenue fell 3% (-1% organic), and Q1 2026 showed severe margin compression — gross margin down 440bps to 23.3%, CSA segment margin falling from 22.2% to 14.9%, driven by U.S. residential volume down 12% and China down 13%. Orders inflected hard: Q1 orders +11%, global commercial HVAC +35%, CSA commercial HVAC +80%, and global data-center orders up more than 500%. Management reaffirmed FY26 guidance of ~$22B sales and ~$2.80 adjusted EPS, with a $1.5B data-center sales target.

This is a trough-earnings, building-backlog setup. The bull case is that 2026 margins are the bottom and the backlog converts into 2027 earnings, supporting consensus FY27 EPS of $3.20. The bear case is that residential and China weakness persists while the market already prices in the recovery at ~46x trailing / ~22x forward earnings, with a thin (~14%) goodwill-impairment cushion on the European Viessmann unit and a newly disclosed industry antitrust action carrying treble-damage exposure. We rate CARR a HOLD on a growth basis: the order book gives a credible 12-month appreciation path, but the valuation and overhangs prevent a higher conviction call.

## Company Overview

Carrier was spun out of United Technologies (now RTX) in April 2020. It now reports four HVAC/climate segments:

- **Climate Solutions Americas (CSA)** — ~$10.5B sales, FY25 segment margin 20.5%. The profit engine: residential, light commercial, and commercial HVAC.
- **Climate Solutions Europe (CSE)** — ~$5.0B, Viessmann-heavy, margin only ~8.8%.
- **Climate Solutions APMEA (CSAME)** — ~$3.3B, margin ~13.4%; China is a meaningful drag.
- **Climate Solutions Transportation (CST)** — ~$2.9B, Carrier Transicold truck/trailer/container refrigeration, margin ~15.6%.

The mix is 72% new equipment / 28% parts & service, with international (including exports) ~52% of sales. Key brands include Carrier, Viessmann, Toshiba, Automated Logic, Bryant, CIAT, and Carrier Transicold. The company operates ~58 JVs, including the Watsco "Carrier Enterprise" distribution JVs and Midea/AHI manufacturing JVs, and holds ~11,000 patents. Differentiation rests on digitally-enabled aftermarket (Abound, Lynx cold-chain), heat-pump electrification (Viessmann), and an emerging data-center cooling offering (QuantumLeap, combining traditional and liquid cooling).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY25 revenue was down 3% YoY (-1% organic, +1% FX, -3% net acquisitions/divestitures). Organic weakness centered on U.S. residential (CSA volume -9%) and China. GAAP operating profit fell 18% to $2.17B; adjusted operating profit fell 7% to $3.29B, a ~15% adjusted margin. The reported FY24 net income of $5.6B included ~$4.5B of discontinued-operations gains from the Fire & Security exits. FY25 net income attributable to common was $1.48B, while continuing-ops earnings rose 29% to $1.56B, helped substantially by a 13.4% effective tax rate (vs. 46.7% in 2024, distorted by a $650M VCS reorganization tax charge).

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $5.34B, +2% reported but -1% organic (+3% FX). Margin compression was severe: GAAP operating profit fell 59% to $259M; adjusted operating profit fell to $594M from $848M. CSA segment margin fell to 14.9% and CSAME from 14.6% to 9.7%, driven by residential volume -12% (under-absorption), China -13%, $108M of restructuring (vs. $8M prior year), and higher SG&A. Adjusted EPS of $0.57 beat consensus by ~$0.05 and the stock rose 7-8% on the order-book commentary.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $2.09B and FCF $1.70B (~7.8% FCF margin). Q1 2026 OCF dropped to $66M on seasonal working-capital build and lower earnings — normal seasonality but worth monitoring. Total debt was $11.8B, net debt $10.3B (net debt/net cap 42%), with $1.6B cash (94% offshore). Ratings are investment grade (S&P BBB+ stable, Moody's Baa1 positive). The maturity ladder is manageable (largest tower $2.0B in 2030), weighted-average rate ~3.65%. The defining balance-sheet feature is $21.8B of goodwill and intangibles against ~$14B equity, a direct consequence of Viessmann; the CSE reporting unit passed its 2025 impairment test with fair value only ~14% above carrying value.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.53 ("distress" range) is a structural artifact of the goodwill-heavy, acquisition-financed balance sheet: the EBIT/assets and working-capital/assets ratios mechanically penalize intangible-laden firms. Carrier is investment-grade, covenant-compliant, and FCF-positive. The genuine monitorable the Z-score points to is the thin European impairment cushion, not solvency.

**Capital allocation.** Return of capital has been aggressive: $12.1B buyback authorization (raised $5B in October 2025), ~$5.0B remaining at Q1 2026; FY25 buybacks $2.9B plus $772M dividends ($0.90/share, raised to $0.24/quarter). Share count fell from ~898M (FY24 basic) to ~835M (Q1 2026). The buybacks have been executed across a declining stock price through 2025, which is reasonable on a cash-return basis but has not yet earned a per-share-value premium.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is about whether the order book converts. Reported organic growth is negative, but Q1 leading indicators were strong:
- Total orders +11%
- Global commercial HVAC orders +35%
- CSA commercial HVAC orders +80%
- Global data-center orders +500%

Management targets $1.5B of data-center sales in 2026 (~7% of revenue), embedded in the reaffirmed FY26 guidance. Consensus FY27 EPS of $3.20 implies ~14% growth off the FY26 base if the backlog converts and residential/China volumes bottom.

The data-center adjacency is real but should not be over-extrapolated. The DC-cooling category is currently led by Vertiv and Eaton (Vertiv cites a ~$15B backlog); Carrier is a credible secondary supplier through QuantumLeap rather than the category leader. [unverified — cross-sector hyperscaler capex, power generation, and grid-equipment confirmation is not in the provided sources] The commercial HVAC order growth is meaningful, but the durability of the demand pull beyond the reported order data cannot be independently substantiated here.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than the strong positive inflection a high-conviction BUY would require. Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen -4.1% over 90 days (residential/China drag), while current-year (+0.2%) and next-year (+0.8%) revisions are flat-to-marginally-positive. There is no estimate-revision velocity signal of the +30% magnitude that would mark a clean earnings inflection; what exists is a backlog story that the consensus has yet to translate into rising near-term numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

CARR trades at $69.49 with a trailing P/E of ~46x and a forward P/E (~$2.80 FY26) of ~25x. The forward multiple is the relevant anchor given the trough earnings, but it does not clear the bar for a high valuation score.

Against Building Products peers — JCI 44.2x, TT 35.1x, LII 22.9x, MAS 18.3x, ALLE 18.3x (peer median P/E 22.9x) — Carrier on trailing looks expensive and on forward sits above the peer median. The HVAC-pure-play comps that matter most (TT, LII) span 23-35x, so Carrier is not a relative outlier on forward earnings. The market is already paying a normalized multiple while CSA margins are running ~700bps below normal. To justify the price, the recovery must materialize; the stock is not cheap on current earnings power and offers no margin of safety if the residential/China trough extends.

The dividend yield is modest at ~1.3%, slightly above the 1.1% peer median but not a support level for the stock. Argus raised its target to $75 (Buy) on valuation and portfolio-alignment, while a competing analysis flagged price-to-sales at ~2.5x as stretched versus history. The analyst price-target band is $60 low / $90 high, mean $76.25, median $75.

On a cycle-adjusted basis, if CSA margins recover toward 20%+ and the commercial/DC backlog converts, normalized EPS power approaches $3.20+ on FY27, and a 22-23x multiple supports the high-$60s to mid-$70s — close to where the stock trades. The valuation embeds the recovery rather than offering it at a discount. We score valuation below neutral: the multiple is full for the delivered earnings, and "cheap on forward" requires accepting the recovery as given.

## Competitive Landscape

Carrier is a global top-tier HVAC player alongside Trane, Daikin, Lennox, and Johnson Controls. Its competitive trajectory is mixed. The commercial HVAC and data-center positions are improving, with the order book signaling share participation in the strongest demand pocket in the building-products space. The aftermarket/digital franchise (Abound, Lynx) is a credible recurring-revenue layer.

The offsetting concern is that the Viessmann integration has not yet proven the margin-uplift thesis: CSE earns sub-9% margins versus CSA's 20%+, and the European residential heat-pump market faces regulatory and incentive uncertainty. Carrier is also a secondary, not leading, player in DC cooling (behind Vertiv/Eaton), so the most-hyped growth narrative is one where it competes rather than dominates. Distribution depends heavily on the Watsco and Midea/AHI JVs, and transport-refrigeration customers' affiliates compete directly in containers/trailers. The trajectory is net-positive in commercial/aftermarket and net-negative in European margins, which nets to a moderately favorable but unconfirmed competitive picture. The prior assessment marked competitive position down from 7.0 to 6.5; the Q1 order data supports holding at 6.5 rather than reversing it.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/intangible impairment — Europe.** The CSE unit's fair value is only ~14% above carrying value amid weak European demand and heat-pump incentive uncertainty. A discount-rate increase or further volume deterioration could trigger a non-cash impairment that would dent reported equity and sentiment.

**Residential and China cyclicality.** The highest-margin business (U.S. residential) is in a destocking/affordability trough, and China is structurally weak. These drive the current margin compression; recovery timing is tied to housing, rates, and refrigerant-transition pre-buy dynamics, all uncertain.

**New HVAC antitrust class actions (March 2026).** Carrier and other manufacturers face putative price-fixing class actions in E.D. Michigan covering equipment sold since January 2020, seeking treble damages. This is brand-new (post-10-K), industry-wide, and not yet quantifiable — a materially under-discussed risk relative to the data-center attention.

**AFFF / Kidde-Fenwal liability.** Inherited at separation; proposed settlement of $615M cash over five years plus ~$115M asset-sale proceeds. Carrier booked a $565M liability and expects insurance (up to $2.4B) to cover payments, but no recoveries are booked and 17,000+ suits remain pending court approval.

**Viessmann share overhang.** Max Viessmann sold ~12.1M shares for ~$750M at $62.01 on May 20, 2026, with ~38M shares remaining and registration rights enabling further flow. This is a lockup-driven liquidity event, not a conviction signal, but it is a persistent technical overhang.

**Tariff cross-currents.** Carrier fully mitigated 2025 IEEPA tariffs (~$200M pricing); new Section 232 steel/aluminum/copper tariffs landed April 2026. There is unmodeled refund optionality (courts ruled IEEPA tariffs unauthorized; Carrier booked zero refund benefit), partly offsetting the new tariff cost.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.3% versus 35.6% one-year historical vol — modestly rich (5.7pp spread), with IV at only the 20th percentile of its own chain, so the market is not pricing an acute event. Put/call skew is elevated at 15.5% (downside-protection demand), but the put/call volume ratio (0.35) and OI ratio (0.08) are call-heavy, indicating net bullish positioning despite the protective skew. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.4%, and the term structure is flat (41.3% near ≈ 42.9% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded into a binary. The reported 471.5% ATM IV in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Net read: options positioning is broadly consistent with the fundamental view — recovery optionality priced with measured downside hedging, no signal of imminent stress.

Short interest is benign at 2.5% of float, 2.4 days to cover — no squeeze dynamic, and no crowded bearish thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The order book is the leading indicator: commercial HVAC, CSA commercial HVAC, and data-center orders convert into 2026-2027 revenue as residential and China volumes bottom. CSA margins recover toward 20%+, Viessmann integration begins delivering synergy uplift in Europe, and the $1.5B 2026 DC target proves a floor rather than a ceiling. Normalized EPS approaches $3.20+ on FY27, a 24-25x multiple in line with TT supports the low-$80s, and aggressive buybacks ($5B remaining) accelerate per-share growth. Bull target $85-90 (matching the high analyst target and a re-rating on confirmed recovery).

### Bear Case
Residential and China weakness persists through 2026, CSA margins fail to recover, and the backlog converts more slowly than guided. A European goodwill impairment hits reported equity and sentiment. The antitrust class action advances toward a material settlement. The Viessmann overhang pressures the technical bid. With the stock already at ~25x forward earnings on a recovery that doesn't arrive, the multiple compresses toward Masco/Allegion (18x) on $2.60-2.70 of disappointing EPS, taking the stock to the high-$40s/low-$50s. Bear target $50-55, roughly -25% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $76.** ~24x forward on ~$3.15 blended FY26/FY27 EPS, reflecting partial backlog conversion and CSA margin recovery toward the high-teens, in line with the analyst median ($75) and Argus ($75). This is +9% from $69.49.

**Bull case target: $88.** ~25x on $3.20+ FY27 EPS with full margin recovery and DC upside; +27% from current.

**Bear case target: $52.** ~18x on $2.65-2.85 with a stalled recovery and multiple compression; -25% from current.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next 2-3 earnings reports (Q2 2026 late July, Q3 late October) where backlog-to-revenue conversion and CSA margin recovery become visible.

**Thesis invalidation / downgrade triggers:**
- CSA segment margin fails to recover above ~17% by Q3 2026 (signals the residential trough is not lifting).
- Total or commercial-HVAC order growth decelerates below +5% YoY, indicating the backlog story is fading.
- Management cuts the FY26 adjusted EPS guide, or a European goodwill impairment is announced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of CSA margin expansion toward 19-20% with order growth sustained above +10%.
- DC-cooling sales tracking above the $1.5B 2026 target with visible 2027 pipeline, alongside positive (+5%+ in 90 days) near-term EPS revisions.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that compresses forward P/E below ~22x while fundamentals hold.

## Conclusion

Carrier is a quality HVAC franchise at the trough of its highest-margin business, with a genuinely inflecting order book offset by a full valuation, a thin European impairment cushion, and a new treble-damage antitrust overhang. The 12-month appreciation path is credible and catalyst-rich, with backlog conversion, CSA margin recovery, and DC-cooling growth all carrying the stock toward the mid-$70s base case, but the market is already paying a normalized ~25x forward multiple for earnings that have not yet recovered, leaving limited margin of safety and a -25% bear-case tail. We rate CARR a **HOLD** with a growth-oriented rationale: own it for the backlog-to-earnings conversion thesis, but await either confirmed CSA margin recovery or a cheaper entry (low-$60s) before adding conviction. The next two earnings reports will show whether the order book is translating into the income statement.
