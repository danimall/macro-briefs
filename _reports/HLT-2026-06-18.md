**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hilton operates one of the highest-quality business models in the consumer-discretionary universe: an asset-light hospitality franchisor and manager that owns roughly 1% of the 1.36 million rooms it brands across 9,260 properties. The other 99% are operated under management and franchise contracts in which third-party owners supply the capital, while Hilton collects royalty, management, and licensing fees at near-pure-margin economics. The result is a business that converted $12.0B of FY2025 revenue into ~$3.7B of Adjusted EBITDA and ~$2.0B of free cash flow on only $101M of capex, a 16.8% FCF margin with almost no capital intensity. Net unit growth ran 6.7% in 2025 with a record 527,000-room development pipeline, giving Hilton a multi-year fee tailwind that is largely independent of the lodging cycle.

The Q1 2026 print confirmed a genuine demand inflection. After a soft 2025 (U.S. RevPAR -0.8% on weaker inbound international and business travel), system-wide RevPAR rose 3.6% in Q1 with operating income up 26% and adjusted EPS of $2.01. Management raised full-year RevPAR guidance to 2-3% from 1-2% and reaffirmed ~$3.5B of capital return. The fundamental story is intact and improving at the margin versus the prior two reports.

The constraint is price. At $349-353, HLT trades at roughly 39x consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.03 and 53x trailing, a premium to nearly every lodging and travel comparable except Marriott, and one that already embeds continued unit growth, RevPAR recovery, and the EPS accretion from a relentless buyback. Estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative on the near quarter (-7.7% over 90 days on the current quarter), so this is a high-multiple quality compounder where the easy upside has been realized rather than an inflection-driven re-rating candidate. We rate HLT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: own it for the franchise and the compounding, but the current entry offers limited asymmetry and we would prefer to add on a pullback toward the low-$300s or below, particularly given the H2 consumer-discretionary risk building in the macro backdrop.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1919, Hilton is among the largest global hospitality companies, with 9,260 properties and 1.36 million rooms across 144 countries as of Q1 2026. The defining characteristic is the asset-light structure: Hilton owns or leases just 46 hotels (15,287 rooms), and operates the remaining ~99% under management or franchise agreements.

The company reports two segments. **Management & Franchise** is the value engine: it earns royalty fees as a percentage of gross room revenue, base and incentive management fees, and high-margin licensing fees (the co-branded American Express card, strategic partners, and the Hilton Grand Vacations timeshare license). In segment reporting this line carries no allocated expenses, so it functions as pure fee margin; Q1 2026 segment revenue was $893M versus $804M a year earlier. The **Ownership** segment (46 leased hotels) generated $249M of Q1 revenue but only $15M of segment Adjusted EBITDA, economically minor but a source of operating-leverage and lease risk in a downturn.

The moats are threefold. Hilton Honors reached 251 million members in Q1 2026 (+15% YoY), driving direct bookings that reduce OTA commission leakage and anchoring the co-branded card economics. The brand portfolio spans 24+ brands from luxury (Waldorf Astoria, Conrad, LXR) through focused-service workhorses (Hampton at ~360K rooms, Hilton Garden Inn) to newer conversion and extended-stay concepts (Spark, LivSmart, and the 2026 "Select by Hilton" launch). And scale compounds: a 3,768-hotel pipeline with roughly half under construction, nearly all fee-based, supports 6-7% net unit growth. Primary competitors are Marriott, IHG, Hyatt, Accor, Choice, and Wyndham.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year revenue and profitability trend shows steady high-single-digit top-line growth with margin expansion:

| Metric ($M) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 8,773 | 10,235 | 11,174 | 12,039 |
| Operating income | 2,094 | 2,225 | 2,370 | 2,693 |
| Net income | 1,255 | 1,141 | 1,535 | 1,457 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 2,599 | 3,089 | 3,429 | 3,725 |
| Operating CF | — | — | 2,013 | 2,129 |

FY2025 operating margin reached 22.4% and Adjusted EBITDA grew 8.6%. The headline net income dip versus 2024 is an artifact, not deterioration: 2024 carried a one-time $270M tax benefit (foreign tax basis on brand assets) that depressed its effective rate. Pre-tax income actually rose from $1,783M to $2,072M. Fee growth is the engine: FY2025 franchise and licensing fees rose 6.9% to $2,780M, with licensing alone up $104M on credit-card and timeshare activity.

The capital-light economics are the defining strength: $101M of capex against $2,129M of operating cash flow yields roughly $2.0B of free cash flow. With virtually no maintenance capital intensity, essentially all of that flows to buybacks and dividends.

Q1 2026 showed clear acceleration. Total revenue was $2,937M (+9.0%), operating income $678M (+26%), Adjusted EBITDA $901M (+13%), and diluted EPS $1.66 versus $1.23. Operating cash flow rose 36.7% to $618M. The RevPAR gain came on occupancy +1.4pts and ADR +1.5%, a sharp turn from the negative U.S. trend that characterized 2025.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. Reported stockholders' equity is -$5.9B and the current ratio is 0.66, which mechanically produces an Altman Z-Score of 1.14 ("distress zone"). This is a financial-engineering artifact, not financial distress. The negative book equity is the cumulative result of repurchasing stock far above book value; treasury stock now stands at -$15.3B. For a fee-based, high-FCF franchisor, negative book equity is structural and shared with peers like Marriott. Real leverage is moderate: ~$12.5B total debt against ~$3.9B run-rate Adjusted EBITDA is roughly 3.2x gross, well inside the 5.0x secured-net-leverage covenant. The revolver is fully available ($1.9B), there are no material maturities before April 2027, and notes are laddered through 2034. The Piotroski score of 4/5 confirms positive net income, ROA, operating cash flow, and OCF exceeding net income.

Earnings quality and consistency are good: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters, ranging from +1.2% to +6.8%. The track record supports confidence in consensus, though the magnitude of beats has been compressing (1.2% on the most recent quarter), consistent with a fully-followed name where the surprise edge is thin.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth vectors drive Hilton. The first, and the more durable, is net unit growth. The 6.7% NUG in 2025 and 6.3% trailing into Q1 2026 are powered by a record pipeline of 3,768 hotels and 527,000 rooms, with more than half outside the U.S. and roughly half under construction. Because nearly all of those rooms will be fee-based, this stream of fee revenue grows largely independent of RevPAR, a structural advantage over owner-operators. This is the part of the story I weight most heavily, and it is genuine.

The second vector is RevPAR, which tracks the lodging cycle. The Q1 inflection (system-wide +3.6%, U.S. +3.4%, Europe +6.9%, Asia-Pacific +4.7%) was strong enough for management to raise FY2026 RevPAR guidance. The open question is durability versus timing. Part of the Q1 strength was calendar-driven: earlier spring break, holiday shifts, and Winter Olympics tailwinds to Europe. Management is betting the U.S. business-travel and inbound recovery is durable; the macro backdrop, with a consumer-discretionary cliff probability building in H2, argues for caution on that assumption.

Estimate revisions do not support an inflection thesis. Current-year EPS sits at $9.03, essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days. Next-year is anchored at $10.42, unchanged. The current quarter has been cut 7.7% over 90 days to $2.25. This is a company delivering steady mid-teens EPS growth (helped materially by the buyback), not one where consensus is racing upward to catch a fundamental surprise. The +30% revision velocity that would flag a re-rating opportunity is absent. Growth prospects are solid and above-sector, justifying a 6.8 score, but the trajectory has softened modestly from prior reports (7.5 → 7.0 → now 6.8), reflecting the near-quarter cut and the macro overhang on the cyclical leg.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case for restraint is clearest. At $349-353, HLT trades at roughly 39x consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.03 and 53x trailing EPS of $6.12. Against the peer set, the premium is stark:

| ABNB | Airbnb | 32.1 | — | — |

| EXPE | Expedia | 19.7 | 0.9% | 1.26 |
| **Peer median** | | **19.7** | **0.9%** | **1.78** |

Hilton trades at roughly 2x the peer-median P/E. The closest comparable on business model, Marriott at 41.6x, is the only name that justifies the cohort. The premium to RCL, CCL, EXPE, and even ABNB is large, and is warranted only to the extent Hilton's superior unit growth, fee-margin durability, and FCF conversion are not fully captured at lower multiples. On a growth-adjusted basis, mid-teens forward EPS growth (consensus implies ~15% from $9.03 to $10.42) against ~39x forward yields a PEG well above 2, not a level that signals mispricing in either direction, but one that leaves the multiple vulnerable.

I deliberately avoid penalizing HLT for prior appreciation (the stock is up ~41% over the trailing year). The relevant question is price versus forward fundamentals today, and on that basis the multiple already prices the flywheel. There is no specific catalyst that the market is missing: the Q1 beat-and-raise is known, the buyback is known, and estimate revisions are flat. The dividend yield of ~0.17% provides no valuation floor. Analyst targets cluster tightly around the current price (mean $347, median $354, high $390, low $260), which means consensus does not see this as cheap. This is a stock above the threshold where analyst targets function as floors: at ~39-53x earnings, targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. The asymmetry favors waiting.

I score valuation 4.0. A compressed multiple reflecting structural impairment is one path to a low score; a clearly stretched multiple with growth already embedded is the other. Hilton sits closer to the latter. Cheapness has historically been a poor predictor, but paying a full premium with no identified mispricing is precisely the setup that caps forward returns. The score reflects price relative to forward fundamentals, not a quality judgment on the business.

## Competitive Landscape

Hilton's competitive position is strong and broadly stable-to-improving, which supports a 7.8 score (modestly below the prior 8.0 on a small-magnitude reassessment, not a deterioration). Three factors stand out. First, the asset-light model substantially insulates Hilton from the operating leverage that hits owner-operators in downturns: earnings fall faster than revenue at the hotel level, but Hilton bears little of that because owners hold the fixed costs. Second, Hilton Honors at 251 million members and growing 15% YoY is the primary defense against OTA commission leakage and the engine behind direct-booking economics. Third, the 6-7% net unit growth with a record pipeline outpaces most peers and validates execution: cumulative TSR of 261.4 versus a peer-group 226.6 confirms Hilton has out-returned the lodging cohort.

The competitive threats are real but slow-moving. OTA bargaining power persists, and the newly-flagged risk of AI/LLM travel agents disintermediating direct bookings is repeatedly emphasized in the 10-K, both as a competitive vector and a compliance/reputational risk from Hilton's own AI adoption. Alternative lodging (home-sharing) remains a structural overhang, though Hilton's brand breadth and the launch of conversion/extended-stay concepts (Spark, Select by Hilton) extend its addressable footprint. None of these has visibly eroded share, and the trajectory of unit growth and loyalty enrollment argues the moat is widening at the network level even as channel dynamics evolve.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical/discretionary demand sensitivity.** Fee revenue tracks RevPAR with a lag, and RevPAR tracks the macro cycle. The FY2025 U.S. RevPAR decline driven by reduced inbound international and softer business travel shows how quickly demand softens. Incentive management fees ($313M in FY2025) are geared to hotel profitability and fall fastest in downturns. With consumer-discretionary spending under pressure and an H2 consumer cliff a live macro risk, the cyclical leg of the thesis carries elevated near-term uncertainty.

**Middle East concentration.** MEA was Hilton's best-growing region in 2025 (RevPAR +11.5%) but reversed to -1.7% in Q1 2026 amid the ongoing conflict; the 10-Q forward-looking language specifically names Iran. This is a current, named headwind tied directly to the regional geopolitical situation.

**Third-party owner financing dependency.** Hilton's growth depends entirely on owners' access to capital. Elevated rates have caused opening delays, and owners in distress may terminate contracts or default on fees. Guarantee exposure is modest ($20M performance + $53M debt).

**Channel disintermediation, including AI.** OTAs and emerging AI/LLM travel agents could divert bookings from direct channels and raise cost of sale, flagged prominently in the 10-K.

**Tax dispute on loyalty accounting.** The IRS is auditing 2011-2020 returns, challenging the §1.451-4 deferral method Hilton uses for Hilton Honors. On April 22, 2026 the Seventh Circuit vacated the adverse Tax Court ruling and remanded, a near-term positive, but the matter is unresolved and could affect tax liability given the $2.4B+ loyalty liability.

**Insurance cost inflation.** Management guides to higher general/excess liability insurance costs in 2026 and flags renewal risk on the U.S. Terrorism Risk Insurance Program (authorized through Dec 2027).

**Structural negative equity / buyback dependency.** The negative book equity and 1.14 Z-Score are artifacts of buying back stock above book, not distress. The corollary is that EPS growth is meaningfully amplified by a shrinking share count (~5.6% reduction in 15 months). The equity story is partly financially engineered and sensitive to any FCF interruption that forces a buyback pause.

I score risk factors 5.5. The Z-Score "distress" flag is a false positive that should be overridden by the leverage and FCF facts. The genuine, not-fully-priced risks are the cyclical/consumer-cliff sensitivity into H2 and the named Middle East drag, both idiosyncratic enough to warrant a below-neutral score, but neither is binary or company-threatening.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in steep backwardation: near-term (1-day) ATM IV of 80.5% against a 1-year historical vol of 22.9% and long-dated (6-month) IV of 25.8%. The 80.5% near-term print is an expiry-day artifact (the 1-week tenor at 1 day to expiry); the more informative tenors are the 1-month at 23.6% and 3-month at 29.0%, both modestly above realized vol. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%), put/call volume ratio is low at 0.22, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.04 to 0.25 since early May. Positioning has shifted toward calls as the stock rallied ~10%. There is no elevated put demand contradicting the constructive business view; the options market reads as complacent-to-mildly-bullish, consistent with a fully-priced quality name rather than one pricing imminent downside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Hilton compounds at mid-teens EPS growth through the cycle, driven by 6-7% net unit growth that is largely RevPAR-independent, a U.S. travel reacceleration that proves durable rather than calendar-driven, and continued ~$3.5B annual capital return shrinking the share count ~5% per year. The Q1 beat-and-raise validates the inflection; the Seventh Circuit tax development removes a tail risk; and the loyalty/network moat widens. In this scenario the multiple holds at ~39-42x as the fee-margin durability and FCF conversion justify a Marriott-like premium, and the stock reaches ~$420 on ~$10.42 FY2027 EPS at ~40x.

### Bear Case
The H2 consumer-discretionary cliff materializes, business and inbound travel soften as they did in 2025, incentive fees compress, and the Middle East drag broadens. Q1's RevPAR strength proves partly calendar-driven and full-year RevPAR comes in at the low end or below. A premium multiple on a cyclically-exposed fee base de-rates toward the mid-20s/low-30s as growth visibility fades. In this scenario the stock retraces toward $260-290 (the analyst-low anchor of $260 and the lower end of the 2025 trading range), a decline of roughly 17-26% from current levels.

The base case sits between these: steady execution on unit growth, RevPAR within the 2-3% guide, continued buyback, and modest multiple compression as the premium normalizes against a softer macro. That path supports ~$370-385 over 12 months, single-digit upside that does not adequately compensate for the bear-case downside, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY at current prices.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will test whether the Q1 RevPAR inflection was durable or calendar-driven, and the full-year RevPAR guide will be updated against the 2-3% raise.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$300s or below (~$300-310) on unchanged fundamentals, compressing forward P/E toward ~33x and improving the risk/reward; fundamentals-intact weakness is a stronger entry, not a weaker thesis.
- Q2 RevPAR sustaining ≥3% system-wide with the U.S. reacceleration confirmed (not calendar-driven), accompanied by a further FY2026 RevPAR guidance raise above 3%.
- Positive EPS estimate revisions of meaningful magnitude (current-year above $9.30) that signal consensus catching up rather than the current flat-to-down trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- System-wide RevPAR turning negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling a genuine demand downturn rather than a soft patch.
- A buyback pause or material reduction in the ~$3.5B capital-return commitment, which would remove the EPS-accretion support underpinning the premium multiple.
- Net unit growth decelerating below ~5% as owner financing constraints bite and pipeline conversion slows.

## Conclusion

Hilton is a best-in-class business: asset-light, high-FCF, structurally growing units at 6-7% with a record pipeline, defended by a 251-million-member loyalty network and a 24-brand portfolio. The Q1 2026 beat-and-raise confirmed a demand reacceleration and management's confidence is reflected in the lifted RevPAR guidance and reaffirmed capital return. The balance-sheet "distress" signals are accounting artifacts of an aggressive, sustainable buyback, not financial stress; real leverage is a moderate 3.2x.

The limiting factor is valuation. At ~39x forward and ~53x trailing EPS, roughly 2x the peer median, the multiple already prices the fee-growth flywheel and the buyback accretion. Estimate revisions are flat-to-down, there is no identified mispricing, and analyst targets cluster at the current price. Against a macro backdrop with a building H2 consumer-discretionary risk and a named Middle East headwind, the asymmetry is unattractive: limited upside to base case versus meaningful downside if the cyclical leg softens. The scores have drifted down across the last three reports (6.7 → 6.3 → 6.1), and the current data confirms that drift rather than reversing it: the business is as good as ever, but the price leaves little room. We rate HLT a **HOLD** (growth-hold), and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on confirmation that the U.S. RevPAR recovery is durable through Q2.
