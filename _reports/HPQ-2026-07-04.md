**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HP Inc. trades at $21.93, down roughly 15% since our last review and about 26% below its 52-week high of $29.65, giving a market capitalization near $20.1B. The stock now changes hands at approximately 8.1x forward earnings against a well-covered 5.5% dividend yield. The Q2 FY2026 print (reported May 27) was better than feared on the top line and adjusted EPS ($14.4B revenue, +9% YoY; $0.86 adjusted EPS versus ~$0.71 consensus, a 19.8% beat), and full-year consensus EPS has drifted higher over the past 90 days (+4.4% to $3.02). A materially lower price on a Q2 beat with rising current-year estimates improves the risk/reward relative to our prior assessments at higher prices.

That improvement is real but bounded. The dominant near-term dynamic is the memory/DRAM cost crisis: AI-datacenter demand is starving the PC supply chain of memory and storage, inflating input costs across the industry. HP has passed those costs through aggressively — Personal Systems grew revenue 13.2% in Q2 on a +22.3% ASP increase while units fell 7% — but this is demand-destructive at the margin, and management narrowed its FY26 profit guidance and warned that margins will remain pressured through year-end. Printing, the profit engine at ~19% operating margin, is in secular decline. A CEO transition is underway, disclosed through "CEO transition costs" now embedded in restructuring charges. There is no open-market insider buying to corroborate a bullish inflection.

We move to HOLD from our prior AVOID stance, classified as an income hold. The prior AVOID calls were issued at $25 and above; the combination of a 15% price decline, a clean Q2 beat, rising current-year estimates, a well-covered dividend, and IEEPA tariff-refund optionality has compressed the downside without creating an appreciation catalyst. HP is a mature cash-return vehicle whose durable return is its yield and buyback, not a multiple re-rating. We would own it for income and reassess on margin visibility, not chase it for capital appreciation.

## Company Overview

HP operates in more than 170 countries with roughly 55,000 employees across three reportable segments:

- **Personal Systems (~70% of revenue):** Commercial and consumer PCs, notebooks, workstations, thin clients, retail POS, displays, and AI PCs. FY2025 segment revenue was $38.5B (+6.5%), with Commercial ($27.4B) the larger, higher-margin driver and Consumer at $11.1B. Segment operating margin is thin at ~5.3%.
- **Printing (~30% of revenue, but the profit engine):** Hardware, supplies (ink/toner), commercial/graphics, and 3D. FY2025 revenue $16.7B (–3.7%) at an ~18.7% operating margin. Supplies ($10.9B) is the recurring, high-margin core.
- **Corporate Investments:** Small incubation projects that run at a loss.

HP is #1 or #2 globally in both PCs (vs. Lenovo, Dell, Apple, Asus, Acer) and printing (vs. Canon, Epson, Brother, Xerox, Ricoh). The strategic pivot is toward services and subscriptions (Instant Ink, HP All-In Plan, Device-as-a-Service, Managed Print), with AI PCs central to the near-term mix story — 44% of Q2 shipments, up from 35% the prior quarter.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY2025 revenue was $55.3B (+3.2%), following $53.6B in FY2024 and $53.7B in FY2023 — low-single-digit growth driven entirely by Personal Systems on the Windows 11 refresh, offset by Printing's secular decline. The margin trajectory is the central issue: FY2025 gross margin fell 150bps to 20.6%, operating margin dropped to 5.7% from 7.1%, and net income declined to $2.53B from $2.78B in FY2024 and $3.26B in FY2023. Higher memory/DRAM and storage costs, tariffs, and unfavorable FX drove the compression, only partly offset by pricing and Future Ready cost savings.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended April 30).** Revenue rose 9.0% YoY (6.3% constant-currency). Printing was flat. GAAP net earnings were $450M ($0.49 diluted). Six-month operating margin slipped to 4.8% from 5.6%, with $491M of restructuring charges (including a $280M non-cash special termination benefit from the voluntary early-retirement program) weighing on GAAP results. Management guided Q3 EPS to $0.61–$0.71.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3.7B and free cash flow $2.8B (5.1% margin). H1 FY2026 operating cash flow jumped to $1.3B from $0.4B on a favorable cash conversion cycle. FY2025 returned $1.9B to shareholders ($1.1B dividends, $0.8B buybacks), with buybacks cut sharply from prior years and roughly $8.0B still authorized. The dividend is covered roughly 2.5x by free cash flow, which underpins the income thesis.

**Balance sheet — read cash flow, not book equity.** HP carries negative stockholders' equity of –$346M (FY2025), improving to –$144M in Q2. This produces an Altman Z-Score of 1.38 (nominal "distress zone") and a current ratio of 0.77. Both screens misclassify HP: the negative equity is the arithmetic residue of a decade of buybacks (accumulated deficit of ~–$2.0B), not operating distress. HP holds $3.7B cash, carries investment-grade credit, and has an untapped $5.0B revolver plus an untapped $6.0B commercial paper program. Total debt is stable at ~$9.7B (4.6% weighted-average rate), laddered through 2041, with the nearest $522M maturity due June 2026 — manageable against annual FCF.

The genuine balance-sheet sensitivity is the ~$9.1B of supplier finance program obligations sitting in accounts payable, combined with a negative cash conversion cycle of about –40 days. HP is effectively financed by its suppliers, a structural feature of the PC/print model that becomes a liquidity vulnerability only if supplier terms tighten. It removes the equity cushion that a fortress balance sheet would provide, which is why we score financial health as adequate rather than strong: strong cash generation offset by a thin, supplier-dependent liquidity structure.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest link in the thesis, and we weight it heavily because it is the most reliable discriminator of hardware winners. HP is a low-single-digit revenue grower with two offsetting engines: Personal Systems, currently benefiting from the Windows 10 end-of-support / Windows 11 refresh and AI PC adoption, and Printing, in structural decline.

The Q2 revenue figure overstates underlying demand. The growth is priced, not volumetric — Personal Systems units fell 7% while ASPs rose 22%. This reflects HP passing memory-cost inflation through to a captive enterprise refresh cycle, prioritizing margin and premium mix over volume. It is impressive execution, but it is not sustainable secular growth; it depends on how long enterprises tolerate higher PC prices and how long the DRAM shortage persists. AI PCs at 44% of shipments support ASPs and mix, but the refresh cycle carries pull-forward risk — demand borrowed from FY2027–2028.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but far from an inflection signal. Current-year EPS has risen to $3.02 (+4.5% over 30 days, +4.4% over 90 days), current-quarter EPS to $0.66 (+3.5% / +5.0%). Next-quarter EPS was cut 5.0% over 30 days to $0.67, and next-year EPS is essentially flat at $3.01. This is a business where consensus expects roughly flat earnings power into FY2027 — not the +30% revision velocity that would justify treating the stock as an earnings-inflection story. Printing's continued erosion disproportionately pressures earnings given its margin, and any acceleration there would offset PC gains.

## Valuation Assessment

At 8.1x forward earnings on $3.02 consensus, HP is inexpensive in absolute terms and against its own history. The peer set (SMCI 16.8x, NTAP 25.3x, HPE 43.7x, PSTG 126x, CRSR 100x; median 43.7x) is heterogeneous and skewed by higher-growth storage and AI-server names — not a clean comparable for a thin-margin, low-growth PC-and-print franchise. The relevant benchmark is HP's own earnings power and the durability of its dividend, not a growth-adjusted peer multiple.

The valuation case has to clear a specific bar: a low multiple alone is not a buy signal, and a compressed multiple on a structurally challenged business often reflects genuine impairment rather than mispricing. HP is closer to a fair-value hold than a value trap because it still generates $2.8B of real free cash flow, holds investment-grade credit, and covers its dividend comfortably — this is not a business in decline toward zero. But the identifiable re-rating catalysts are limited:

- **Tariff-refund optionality.** The Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling that IEEPA-based tariffs were not statutorily authorized raises the possibility of recovering previously paid tariffs. HP is still assessing; this is unquantified upside not in the numbers.
- **Restructuring savings.** The Fiscal 2026 Plan targets 4,000–6,000 headcount cuts for future productivity, but at the cost of ~$650M in charges and near-term GAAP drag.
- **AI PC mix.** Supports ASPs and premium mix but is cyclical and demand-destructive at current price points.

Against these sit the memory-margin overhang through year-end and secular printing decline. The analyst mean target of $22.9 and median of $24.5 imply modest upside (roughly 4% to mean, 12% to median), with a high of $29.6 and low of $18.0. That distribution — a bounded downside with modest, uncertain upside — is consistent with a fairly valued name where the yield is the return, not the multiple. We score valuation at 5.5: genuinely cheap with dividend support and some catalyst optionality, but without the clean re-rating trigger that would justify a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

HP holds durable #1/#2 positions in both PCs and printing, competing on brand, security features, distribution breadth, and a services/subscription pivot. The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. In Personal Systems, HP is defending margin by ceding units — a rational choice in a cost-inflated market, but one that surrenders share to competitors willing to absorb costs. The AI PC adoption rate is a genuine mix positive and evidence HP is not losing the premium segment.

In Printing, HP faces industry-wide structural headwinds — digitization and hybrid work reducing print volumes, non-original (refill/remanufactured) supplies pressuring the high-margin cartridge business, big-tank printers cannibalizing cartridge economics — compounded by yen-advantaged Japanese competitors (Canon, Epson, Brother). HP has largely prevailed in its Dynamic Security supply-authentication litigation, which protects the supplies annuity, but the secular direction is negative.

The memory-cost squeeze is a sector-wide phenomenon (Dell is navigating the same DRAM crisis), not an HP-specific competitive disadvantage: it does not signal share loss so much as an industry margin reset. Competitive position is stable, without the share gains or moat widening that would earn a higher score.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory/DRAM cost crisis (dominant near-term).** HP has entered variable-price processor purchase obligations (total purchase commitments rose to $2.2B) to secure supply, creating overpayment risk if prices normalize. This is largely priced into an 8x multiple, but the timing and magnitude of margin recovery are uncertain.

**Demand destruction from pricing.** The +22% ASP / –7% unit dynamic is not indefinitely sustainable. Enterprise tolerance for higher PC prices is finite; a demand air pocket into FY2027 is a real risk if the refresh cycle exhausts and prices stay elevated.

**Secular printing decline.** Any acceleration in printing erosion disproportionately hits earnings given the segment's margin contribution.

**CEO transition.** The inclusion of "CEO transition costs" in restructuring, alongside March 2026 executive retention RSU grants (CFO Parkhill, PS President Patel, Printing President Olson, CCO McQuarrie), signals a leadership change under Enrique Lores (CEO since November 2019). Successor identity was not confirmable. This introduces strategic-continuity risk that broader data screens would miss.

**Balance-sheet structure.** Negative equity, a 0.77 current ratio, and $9.1B of supplier-financed payables mean HP has no equity cushion and depends on supplier terms holding. Not distress, but a sensitivity worth monitoring.

**Offsetting/receding items.** The IEEPA tariff ruling is a potential tailwind. The decade-old Autonomy matter produced a $1.24B damages judgment against the Lynch estate (a potential recovery to HP, shared 50/50 with HPE, not yet booked). The York County securities class action was settled/dismissed. An India customs dispute (~$386M exposure) awaits a Tribunal decision.

Much of this risk is reflected in a stock at 8x earnings and down 26% from its high. The residual unpriced risks are the depth and duration of the margin squeeze, demand destruction from ASP hikes, and CEO-transition continuity.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.6% is modestly rich to 1-year historical vol of 39.6% and sits at only the 18th percentile of its own chain, indicating no acute near-term stress. The put/call skew is balanced (–1.7%) and the 1-week implied move is ±5.1%. The term structure is in contango (near-term 43.6% below longer-dated 50.7%), a normal/complacent shape. The one signal worth flagging is the 6-month tenor: a –6.8% put skew and a 4.71 put/call open-interest ratio point to some longer-dated downside hedging, consistent with the margin-overhang and CEO-transition uncertainties. ATM IV has collapsed from 133.6% around the May earnings event, reflecting resolved event risk. Short interest at 10.8% of float (3.5 days to cover) shows meaningful but not extreme bearish positioning. The options market corroborates a fundamentally two-sided setup rather than an imminent directional catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (target ~$29):** The memory shortage proves temporary, margins recover in FY2027, and HP retains the ASP gains as costs normalize — operating leverage on a re-based cost structure (post-restructuring savings) lifts earnings. The AI PC refresh sustains longer than feared, the IEEPA tariff refund materializes as a one-time cash windfall, and the market re-rates the stock toward 9–10x on a stabilized earnings base. The dividend and $8B buyback authorization provide a floor and steady per-share accretion. This maps to roughly $28–$30, near the 52-week high and the top analyst target ($29.6).

**Bear case (target ~$18):** The DRAM squeeze persists into 2027, HP overpays on its $2.2B in purchase commitments, and enterprise resistance to elevated PC prices creates a demand air pocket that hits both units and ASPs. Printing decline accelerates. The CEO transition disrupts strategy execution. Earnings compress and the multiple stays pinned, taking the stock to ~$18 (the low analyst target, roughly –18% from current). The supplier-finance dependency amplifies any working-capital stress.

**Base case (target ~$23):** Margins remain pressured through year-end but stabilize in FY2027, earnings hold roughly flat at ~$3.00, and the multiple stays near 8x. The dividend delivers a 5.5% cash return with modest capital appreciation as buybacks reduce the share count. This maps to $23–$24, between the analyst mean ($22.9) and median ($24.5). The bulk of the total return over the next twelve months comes from the yield, not the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income hold with a 12-month horizon, anchored to the FY2026 earnings cadence and the memory-cost cycle.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands sequentially for two consecutive quarters, confirming the DRAM squeeze is passing and pricing gains are sticking (watch Q3/Q4 FY2026 prints).
- A quantified, confirmed IEEPA tariff refund that materially lifts cash flow and provides a discrete re-rating catalyst.
- A clean CEO-succession announcement with strategic continuity, removing the leadership overhang, combined with open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Personal Systems units decline accelerates beyond ~10% YoY alongside stalling ASPs, signaling demand destruction has outrun pricing power.
- FY27 consensus EPS is cut below $2.80 on sustained margin pressure or accelerating printing decline.
- A dividend cut or a supplier-finance/working-capital disruption that impairs the cash-return thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q3 FY2026 earnings (guided EPS $0.61–$0.71), which will test whether the margin overhang is deepening or stabilizing, and whether a CEO successor has been named.

## Conclusion

HP is a well-run, cash-generative hardware franchise trading at a valuation that already discounts most of its known problems. At 8.1x forward earnings with a dividend covered roughly 2.5x by free cash flow, the downside is reasonably bounded, and the Q2 beat plus rising current-year estimates on a lower stock price improve the risk/reward relative to our earlier, higher-priced AVOID calls. What is missing is a catalyst for meaningful capital appreciation: the memory-cost squeeze pressures margins through year-end, Printing is in secular decline, the pricing-driven revenue growth is demand-destructive, and a CEO transition adds strategic uncertainty.

We rate HPQ a HOLD for income. The durable return is the yield and buyback, not a re-rating, and we would own the stock for that profile while monitoring the specific upgrade and downgrade triggers above. An upgrade to BUY requires evidence that margins have troughed and pricing gains are permanent; a downgrade to AVOID requires demand destruction or a broken cash-return story. Absent either, this is a fairly valued cash-return vehicle to hold, not chase.
