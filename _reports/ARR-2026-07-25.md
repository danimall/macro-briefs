**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ARMOUR Residential REIT is an externally managed agency mortgage REIT (mREIT) that buys Agency MBS, finances them with short-term repo at roughly 7.5x leverage, and hedges the interest-rate exposure with swaps, swaptions, futures, and TBAs. The economic engine is the net interest spread between MBS yields (~4.97%) and repo funding costs (~3.83%). That spread has improved to 1.10% in Q2 2026 versus 0.36% in Q2 2025, as the Fed cut to 3.50–3.75% while asset yields held. This is the real positive in the story and it drove a strong Q2 2026 GAAP print of $111.5M to common ($0.86/share).

The problem is what sits underneath the ~17% headline dividend. In H1 2026, ARMOUR raised roughly $434M through its common ATM program (24.5M shares at ~$17.69 average) while paying $179M in common dividends. Share count went from 62.4M at YE2024 to 111.9M at YE2025 to 136.4M by June 2026, more than doubling in 18 months. Distributable earnings of ~$93M in the strong Q2 barely covered ~$96M in total dividends. The dividend is being funded substantially by the return of shareholders' own capital, and issuing stock at or below book value steadily erodes NAV per share. The external-management fee is charged on gross equity raised, not on performance or book value, which directly incentivizes the manager to grow the equity base through the dilutive issuance that is happening.

We rate ARR a HOLD (income_hold) with an overall score of 4.0. The stock trades around 1.0x book (~$16.81 estimated book value versus $16.27 current price), so there is no obvious NAV discount to exploit, and no genuine open-market insider buying to signal conviction. This is a vehicle for investors who want agency-MBS carry with a high monthly distribution and accept the return-of-capital dynamics, not a total-return compounder. The score has drifted up from 4.0 to 4.6 over three prior reports as the carry improved, but the structural governance and dilution issues cap the upside.

## Company Overview

ARMOUR is a single-segment agency mortgage REIT incorporated in Maryland in 2008 and based in Vero Beach, FL. It owns Agency MBS guaranteed by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and Ginnie Mae, plus some U.S. Treasuries, and finances the portfolio with short-term repurchase agreements. As of June 30, 2026, total assets were $22.7B (Agency securities $20.6B, Treasuries $0.6B), funded by $19.4B of repo against $2.58B of stockholders' equity.

The defining structural feature is external management. ARMOUR Capital Management LP (ACM) employs all of ARR's executive officers: CEO Scott Ulm, CFO Gordon Harper, and Co-CIOs Sergey Losyev and Desmond Macauley. ARR itself has no employees. The management fee is calculated on gross equity raised: 1.5% on the first $1B and 0.75% above $1B. This structure rewards asset-base growth regardless of whether that growth creates per-share value.

ARR also holds a 10.8% equity stake in BUCKLER Securities LLC, a FINRA broker-dealer under common control with ACM. BUCKLER is simultaneously ARR's largest repo counterparty (~47% of borrowings, ~$9.1B) and a sales agent on the ATM equity program, earning commissions on issuance. In February 2026 ARR increased its subordinated loan commitment to BUCKLER from $50M to up to $275M, deepening the entanglement. There is no durable competitive moat here: Agency MBS are commodity assets, and AGNC, Annaly, Dynex, and Orchid pursue nearly identical strategies.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Debt/equity stood at 7.54x at June 30, 2026, with implied leverage including TBAs around 7.7x. This is normal for the agency mREIT model and not a red flag in itself; these companies are deliberately, structurally leveraged financial balance sheets. Average repo haircut was 2.51% and weighted-average repo maturity was just 24 days, the classic short-borrow/long-lend liquidity mismatch inherent to the sector. Liquidity was $1.22B ($84M cash plus $1.14B unencumbered securities).

**Earnings trajectory.** GAAP results are dominated by non-cash mark-to-market swings and are inherently volatile. FY2025 net income was +$323M, a swing from losses of −$14M (2024), −$68M (2023), and −$230M (2022). In Q1 2026, ARR posted a −$54.9M net loss amid a rate/spread selloff tied to a late-February Middle East conflict and tariff uncertainty. Q2 2026 rebounded to +$114.8M, with NII of $76.8M and $108M of derivative gains offsetting MBS mark losses.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** This is the central concern. The dividend is being funded largely through equity issuance rather than earnings. ARR also raised $10.5M in preferred equity in H1 2026. The $0.24/month common dividend (~$2.88/year, ~17% at $16.27) sits right at the edge of coverage in good quarters and is uncovered in weak ones. The buyback authorization was raised to 5.0M shares in April 2026, but only ~125K shares were actually repurchased in H1 2026 while tens of millions were issued.

**Book value.** Estimated book value per share was ~$16.81 as of July 21, 2026, roughly flat to slightly above the current $16.27 price, so the stock trades around 1.0x book. Issuing shares at or below book value is directly dilutive to NAV per share, which is the real scoreboard for this business.

**Tax attributes.** ARR carries $257M of indefinite NOLs plus large capital-loss carryforwards ($732M from 2022, $472M from 2023), legacy artifacts of prior book-value destruction that can shield future capital gains.

## Growth Analysis

"Growth" is the wrong frame for an agency mREIT; the goal is total economic return (book value change plus dividend), not top-line expansion. What ARR has done is grow its asset and equity base through continuous ATM issuance, which grows the manager's fee base but does not reliably grow per-share value.

The genuine tailwind is the widening carry. If the Fed continues to hold or ease and MBS spreads tighten, the core earnings picture improves and dividend coverage strengthens. The Fed ended balance-sheet runoff on December 1, 2025 and is running a modest reserve-management purchase program, which is marginally supportive of MBS technicals.

Estimate revisions are mixed and roughly stable. Current-quarter and next-quarter consensus edged up (+1.7% and +3.5% over 30 days), but current-year EPS was cut −6.1% and next-year −0.8% over the same window. This is below the >30% positive revision velocity that would signal a fundamental change; it is a company with two-sided macro exposure and a stable-to-slightly-negative full-year estimate trend. The earnings-surprise record is unimpressive: 1 beat, 1 in-line, and 2 misses over the last four quarters, including a −9.3% miss in Q4 2025, which caps confidence in forward estimates. Growth prospects score 4.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.27, ARR trades at approximately 1.0x estimated book value of ~$16.81, and a trailing/forward P/E of ~7.4x on the reported $3.30 EPS figure (a number heavily influenced by non-cash marks and therefore of limited use). For an agency mREIT, price-to-book and dividend sustainability are the relevant lenses, not P/E.

Against peers, ARR's ~17% yield is well above the Mortgage REIT peer median of 11.0% (ARI 9.2%, TWO 11.0%, BXMT 10.3%, ABR 13.1%, PMT 16.2%). A yield materially above the peer median is not a bargain signal; it reflects the market's discount for the return-of-capital dynamics and the external-management conflict. The peer-median P/E of 13.4x is not comparable given the marks-driven nature of these earnings.

Trading at book with no discount, ARR offers no obvious mispricing to exploit on the long side. The valuation is defensible for an income holder who wants agency carry, but there is no asymmetric upside catalyst. The analyst consensus targets ($18.00 low, $19.00 high, $18.375 mean) imply roughly 11–17% upside, but for a name trading near book with a diluting share count, we treat these as optimistic anchors rather than reliable floors; the mechanical dilution works against per-share appreciation. We also apply appropriate skepticism to the "cheap on headline yield" reading; in this system, apparent cheapness that reflects structural impairment has repeatedly proven to be a value trap rather than a buy signal. Valuation scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

ARR competes in a crowded agency mREIT field where the underlying assets are commodities and the strategy is nearly identical across names. There is no product differentiation and no customer moat. Sector fortunes are macro-driven: the group benefits when the curve steepens, repo costs fall, and MBS spreads tighten, and suffers when volatility widens spreads.

ARR is disadvantaged relative to best-in-class peers on two counts. First, it is externally managed with a fee on gross equity raised, the structure most sophisticated investors specifically avoid, and one that Annaly internalized years ago precisely to eliminate the growth-for-fees conflict. Second, its five-year total shareholder return of 73.74 has materially lagged the peer group's 125.29, quantitative confirmation of book-value erosion and dilution over time. The company's own selected measure, "Total Economic Return," was negative every year from 2021 through 2024 (−32.4% in 2022) before turning +12.8% in 2025.

The one differentiator, the BUCKLER affiliate providing repo access, is double-edged. It may offer financing continuity in stress, but it concentrates ~47% of funding with a related, relatively small broker-dealer that ARR itself must capitalize (up to $275M subordinated loan commitment). The competitive trajectory is stable-to-deteriorating, not improving. Competitive position scores 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Serial dilution to fund an above-earnings dividend.** The ATM machine issues stock at or near book value to pay the dividend. When shares trade below book, issuance is dilutive to NAV per share. This is the single most important dynamic and a structural headwind to per-share value.

**Related-party conflicts.** The fee-on-gross-equity structure incentivizes ACM to grow the equity base via ATM issuance regardless of per-share value creation. BUCKLER earns simultaneously as the top repo lender and as ATM sales agent on the very issuance the fee structure encourages.

**Terminated fee waiver and entrenchment.** ACM's voluntary ~$1.65M/quarter fee waiver was terminated effective February 2026, a permanent expense increase. The management agreement was extended to March 31, 2033 with a termination fee of 4x trailing annual fees, severely limiting the board's ability to renegotiate. Contractual fee commitments now total $383M through 2033.

**Spread and rate sensitivity.** Per the Q2 2026 10-Q, a +25bp MBS spread widening cuts equity by ~10.2%, and a +100bp parallel rate move cuts equity ~4.7%. At 7.5x leverage, small spread moves swing book value materially, as Q1 2026's loss demonstrated.

**Liquidity mismatch and counterparty concentration.** 24-day average repo funding against 26-year assets, with covenants that cap leverage at 12x and permit termination on significant equity reductions.

**GSE reform.** Renewed political discussion of releasing Fannie/Freddie from conservatorship could disrupt the Agency MBS market ARR depends upon entirely.

The Altman Z-Score of ~0.26 is not a meaningful distress signal here; it is an artifact of applying a manufacturing bankruptcy model to a deliberately leveraged financial balance sheet. The real risk gauges are book-value spread sensitivity and repo rollover. Many of these risks are known and partly priced, which is why the risk score sits at 4.0 rather than lower, but the dilution-plus-conflict combination is idiosyncratic and not fully reflected at 1.0x book.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 21.0% versus 24.4% one-year historical volatility, making options modestly cheap (−3.4% spread) and IV percentile low at 17%. The term structure is in contango (21.0% near versus 26.1% at six months), a normal/complacent structure. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.3% near-term and 11.4% at three months, and the put/call volume ratio of 2.61 signals active put demand, consistent with hedging into the July 22 earnings and macro rate/spread uncertainty. The P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.29 to 0.59 over the past month while the price fell 4.0%. Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float (8,420 shares), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The options picture reinforces caution on downside protection rather than signaling an imminent directional break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** If the Fed holds or eases and MBS spreads tighten, distributable earnings comfortably cover the ~$2.88 annual dividend, the ATM issuance becomes accretive if shares re-rate above book, and total economic return continues the +12.8% recovery seen in 2025. In this scenario, the stock re-rates toward analyst targets of $18–19 and the ~17% yield is delivered without meaningful NAV erosion, roughly a $19 bull case (~17% upside plus yield).

**Bear Case.** A renewed spread-widening shock (geopolitical, tariff, or inflation-driven Fed hike) hits book value hard; a +25bp MBS spread move alone cuts equity ~10%. The dividend, already uncovered in weak quarters, continues to be funded by dilutive equity issuance below book, eroding NAV per share the way it has over the prior five years. The manager keeps growing the equity base for fees while shareholders absorb the leakage. In this scenario book value drifts toward the mid-$14s and the price follows to roughly $14 (~14% downside before the yield), with the dividend eventually trimmed if coverage deteriorates further.

The base case sits between these: carry stays favorable enough to roughly cover the dividend, book value holds near ~$16.50–17.00, and the stock trades in a band around book. Base case ~$16.50, essentially flat to current with the dividend as the return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** This is an income vehicle for holders who want agency-MBS carry with a high monthly distribution and accept the return-of-capital and dilution dynamics. It is not a total-return appreciation story, and we do not see asymmetric upside from 1.0x book.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Distributable earnings sustainably cover the full dividend across two or more consecutive quarters without net ATM issuance (i.e., the dividend stops being funded by capital raises).
- The stock trades at a meaningful discount to book (e.g., <0.90x) while spread and coverage remain intact, creating an actual NAV entry point.
- Evidence of genuine open-market insider buying by the C-suite (not phantom-stock grants), signaling conviction alignment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net interest spread compresses back toward the ~0.36% seen in Q2 2025, or an MBS spread-widening shock drives a book-value decline exceeding 10% in a quarter.
- A dividend cut, which would confirm the coverage math has broken.
- Continued ATM issuance materially below book value while the buyback authorization sits unused, accelerating NAV-per-share leakage.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 2026, roughly late October 2026), with the monthly dividend declarations and any ATM/book-value updates as interim checkpoints. The Fed path and MBS spread environment are the dominant swing factors.

## Conclusion

ARMOUR Residential REIT offers a ~17% headline yield supported by an improved 1.10% net interest spread, but the yield is funded substantially by dilutive equity issuance rather than durable earnings. Share count more than doubled in 18 months, distributable earnings barely covered the dividend in the strong Q2 2026, and the external-management fee on gross equity raised aligns the manager with asset-base growth rather than shareholder returns. The stock trades around 1.0x book with no discount to exploit, five-year TSR has lagged the peer group, and there is no genuine insider conviction buying.

We rate ARR a HOLD with an overall score of 4.0, classified as an income_hold. The score has drifted up modestly across prior reports as the carry improved, and the improved spread environment justifies holding for the distribution rather than avoiding outright. The structural governance conflicts, the return-of-capital dynamics, and the NAV-per-share leakage cap the upside and keep this out of BUY territory. Own it for the monthly income if you accept those trade-offs; do not own it expecting appreciation.
