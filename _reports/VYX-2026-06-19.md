**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NCR Voyix is what remains of the old NCR Corporation after a three-stage breakup: the Atleos ATM/self-service spin in October 2023, the $2.45B sale of Digital Banking to Veritas Capital in September 2024, and the ongoing exit from banking technology (Japan unit sold March 2026 for $32M). The continuing business is a pure-play unified-commerce platform for retailers (~70% of revenue) and restaurants (~30%), anchored by the cloud-based Voyix Commerce Platform plus POS hardware/software, payments processing, and services. The April 2026 completion of the Ennoconn ODM transition shifts hardware to a net (agent) revenue basis, which is why 2026 revenue guidance of $2.188B–$2.303B looks like collapse on a screen but is largely accounting and portfolio surgery rather than demand loss.

The bull case rests on a real and underappreciated cash-flow inflection. Q1 2026 operating cash flow swung from –$42M to +$42M year-over-year, adjusted unrestricted FCF was +$30M, adjusted EBITDA rose 5% to $78M, and recurring revenue grew 4% to $419M (now 69% of total). Retail segment EBITDA grew 20%. After years of separation-driven negative FCF, a de-levered, cash-generative profile is genuinely emerging, and the board is repurchasing stock against a $300M authorization.

The problem is that the equity sits behind a capital structure that dominates the risk/reward. The company carries $1.1B of fixed-rate notes (first maturity October 2028) and a $207M Series A convertible preferred that becomes put-able starting March 16, 2027 — roughly twelve months out — against $232M of cash. Balance-sheet health metrics sit in distress territory because goodwill ($1,519M) exceeds equity ($928M), GAAP earnings are negative, and the Restaurants segment, which should be the SaaS growth engine, saw revenue fall 6% and EBITDA fall 8%. With 34.4% short interest, a –36.6% drawdown from the 52-week high, and a 28.7% cut to the current-quarter EPS estimate over 90 days, the market is pricing structural risk. The underlying business stability is real but does not clear the company-specific risk bar required to own this here. I rate VYX **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

NCR Voyix describes itself as a platform-powered leader in unified commerce for shopping and dining. It sells the Voyix Commerce Platform (microservices-based SaaS), POS hardware and software, payments processing, and maintenance/support across two reportable segments:

- **Retail** (~70% of revenue, Q1 2026 revenue $427M): enterprise and mid-market in convenience/fuel, grocery/drug/mass, and department/specialty. Products span POS, inventory, loss prevention, loyalty, and self-checkout.
- **Restaurants** (~30%, Q1 2026 revenue $179M): QSR, table-service, and fast-casual, anchored by the Aloha platform plus payments and managed services.

The defining feature of the company is that it is in the final stages of a multi-year deconstruction. Consolidated revenue fell from ~$7.8B in 2022 to a continuing-operations base of ~$2.4–2.6B, and 2026 guidance steps down further because hardware revenue will be recognized net (commission/agent basis) under the Ennoconn outsourced design-and-manufacturing arrangement. This removes low-margin hardware pass-through and mechanically shrinks the top line without reflecting underlying demand. The metrics that matter going forward are recurring revenue, adjusted EBITDA, and free cash flow, not reported revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026):**
- Revenue $606M, down 1% YoY ($612M prior year). Product $149M, Service $457M.
- Recurring revenue $419M, +4% YoY, now 69% of total. Software & services represent 78% of revenue.
- Gross margin 21.5% (flat YoY). Product gross margin a thin 4.7%, illustrating why exiting hardware ownership is strategically rational.
- Operating loss $(19)M, improved from $(21)M.
- Adjusted EBITDA $78M, +5% YoY (12.9% margin). Retail adjusted EBITDA +20% on cost cuts and software mix; Restaurants adjusted EBITDA –8% on a 6% revenue decline.
- Net loss from continuing operations $(2)M, helped by a $39M tax benefit (valuation-allowance/geographic mix). Net loss to common $(8)M, or $(0.06)/share.
- Operating cash flow +$42M (vs –$42M prior year), driven by working capital. Adjusted unrestricted FCF +$30M.

The cash-flow swing is the single most important operating data point and is real. After years where separation and transaction drag produced large negative FCF (trailing figures show roughly –$375M), the de-levered business is producing cash.

**Balance sheet (3/31/26):**
- Cash $232M. Total debt $1.1B, all fixed-rate; term loan fully repaid; revolver undrawn with ~$475M availability.
- Notes: $650M at 5.00% (2028), $403M at 5.125% (2029), $52M at 5.25% (2030).
- Goodwill $1,519M (Retail $1,049M, Restaurants $470M) against stockholders' equity of $928M.
- Series A convertible preferred: $207M redemption value, 5.5% dividend, with holder put/redemption rights beginning March 16, 2027 (then 2030, 2033).
- Capitalized software $306M. Leverage covenant tightened to 4.25x as of Q3 2025; company reports compliance.

The earnings track record is strong on the metrics management chooses: four consecutive EPS beats, including 44.7% (Q2 2025) and 38.5% (Q3 2025) surprises, narrowing to 21.2% in Q1 2026. But the GAAP picture remains a small loss to common, and the Q1 net result was flattered by a tax benefit that will not recur at that scale. The combination of distress-zone health metrics (goodwill > equity, negative GAAP earnings, ~4.25x leverage covenant) and the 2027 preferred put against current cash is why financial health scores below average despite the cash inflection.

**Capital allocation tension:** The company repurchased $9M of stock in Q1 (down sharply from $62M in Q1 2025), with $291M remaining on the authorization. Buying back equity into a turnaround at a depressed price signals board conviction, but it consumes scarce cash that could otherwise pre-fund the 2027 preferred put. The reduced Q1 pace suggests management may be recognizing this tension.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is recurring revenue and payments attach, not top-line expansion. Recurring revenue growth is driven primarily by payments processing and hardware maintenance. This is the correct direction for a platform business and supports a higher-quality, stickier revenue base over time.

The constraints are material. Reported revenue is flat-to-down and will optically shrink further as net hardware accounting takes effect, which complicates the market's ability to read the underlying trend. More concerning, the Restaurants segment, which should be the SaaS showcase given the Aloha platform, is shrinking on lower SaaS/install revenue. A SaaS-led turnaround thesis where the flagship SaaS franchise is shrinking is a yellow flag, not a green one. Retail is inflecting, but it is the more mature, hardware-and-services-heavy segment.

Estimate revisions are mixed and lean negative on the near term: current-quarter EPS was cut 28.7% over 90 days (to $0.15) even as it ticked up 5.2% over 30 days; full-year EPS was trimmed 4.0% over 90 days to $0.91. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a clean inflection. Consensus describes the trend as stable, which is the honest read. There is no demand acceleration to underwrite; the case is margin and cash-flow normalization on a roughly flat revenue base.

## Valuation Assessment

VYX trades at $7.49, a market cap of ~$1.1B, on a stated forward P/E of ~25x against $0.91 FY26 consensus EPS (the trailing and forward figures converge near $0.16 EPS depending on the period referenced; on the $0.91 full-year consensus the multiple is closer to ~8x). The disparity in P/E framing reflects the noise in GAAP earnings, where a tax benefit and separation items distort any single-period multiple. The cleaner lens is enterprise value to EBITDA. With ~$1.1B equity, $1.1B debt, $207M preferred, and $232M cash, enterprise value is roughly $2.2B against annualized adjusted EBITDA of ~$312M (4 × $78M) — about 7x EV/EBITDA. That is not demanding for a 69%-recurring software-and-services business, and it is the strongest part of the bull case.

Against peers in IT Consulting & Other Services, the group is heterogeneous and mostly distressed: ASGN at 15.1x P/E, KD at 13.3x, TDC at 7.5x, against software-led names DXC (88x) and GDYN (106x) carrying impaired or trough earnings. Peer median P/E is 15.1x. VYX screens cheap on EV/EBITDA but the discount is earned by the capital structure rather than mispriced. The equity is a thin sliver behind the notes and the preferred with a near-term put; the EBITDA multiple compresses the equity's claim, and any deterioration in EBITDA or any cash drain to satisfy the 2027 put hits equity holders directly.

The analyst price-target range of $9–$18 (mean $12.86, median $12.00) sits well above the current price, and on a cheap-looking stock these targets would normally be treated as floors. But the targets are stale relative to the –36.6% drawdown and the 90-day estimate cut, and they predate the full reckoning with the 2027 preferred cliff and the optical revenue distortion. I assign a valuation score of 5.5: the EV/EBITDA discount is genuine, but cheapness without a catalyst to refinance or remove the capital-structure overhang is a value-trap profile. The discount reflects real risk, not a clean mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

NCR Voyix competes in retail/restaurant commerce technology against a deep field with greater resources: Toast, Block/Square, Shift4, PAR Technology, Oracle (Micros), and Fiserv/Clover in restaurants and payments, plus enterprise retail platforms. The space is consolidating and increasingly cloud/software-led.

The company's historical edge was the integrated hardware-plus-software-plus-services-plus-payments suite that is difficult to match for large enterprise customers. The strategic tension is that it is simultaneously exiting hardware ownership through the Ennoconn ODM, so the moat increasingly rests on platform stickiness and payments attach rather than the integrated-stack story. Payments is the explicit growth lever, and bringing in a payments-industry CEO (James Kelly, ex-Global Payments) reinforces that direction. But the competitive trajectory is at best stable and arguably eroding in the most important showcase: the Aloha-led Restaurants franchise is losing revenue and margin in a segment where Toast and Square are aggressively winning. A challenger gaining share scores well; a legacy incumbent ceding ground in its strategic growth segment does not. I score competitive position below average on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The company-specific risks here are concentrated and dated, which is the core of the AVOID:

1. **Series A preferred put (March 16, 2027).** The $207M preferred becomes put-able in ~12 months against current cash and ahead of the October 2028 notes maturity. This is a real, dated liquidity cliff that screens do not surface. Satisfying it would consume essentially all current cash or require refinancing into a higher-rate environment, with a ratings downgrade raising capital costs.

2. **ODM/Ennoconn transition execution.** From April 2026, a substantial majority of hardware revenue is no longer recognized by the company. Q1 already booked a $12M loss on the $101M inventory sale to Ennoconn. The company now bears reputational and relationship risk on hardware delivery without controlling production, and the implementation was already delayed once.

3. **Optical revenue distortion.** Net hardware accounting will make reported revenue drop sharply in Q2+, which investors not reading footnotes may misread as demand loss, pressuring the equity even if underlying metrics hold.

4. **Restaurants/SaaS softness.** The segment that should validate the platform thesis is shrinking, undermining the SaaS-led narrative.

5. **Legacy environmental and indemnity tail.** Kalamazoo River reserve of $110M that could more than double under adverse assumptions; Fox River reconciliation (~$23M+); active AT&T/Nokia indemnity litigation/arbitration filed February 2026 with AT&T withholding payments; a $48M deferred-comp settlement (April 2026, 50% indemnified by Atleos). These are real cash claims with uncertain offsets.

6. **Distress-zone balance sheet.** Goodwill exceeds equity; an impairment would erode the thin equity cushion materially.

Set against this, the favorable risk note is that the term loan is fully repaid, the revolver is undrawn (~$475M available), prior material weaknesses were remediated in FY2024, and disclosure controls are effective. But the binary, dated nature of the 2027 put and the ODM execution risk dominate.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning has shifted in a way that supports caution but does not signal panic. ATM implied volatility fell from 84.8% to 76.6% over the past six weeks while the price fell 12.4%, and the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.02 to 0.28. The rising put/call ratio indicates growing hedging or bearish positioning, while IV moderating off elevated levels suggests the post-earnings catalyst (May 7) has passed. The dominant signal is the 34.4% short interest with 10.8 days to cover, an unusually crowded short that reflects conviction in structural deterioration. That crowding creates squeeze risk on any positive surprise, which is why this is an AVOID rather than a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($12–$18):** The cash-flow inflection is real and durable. Recurring revenue compounds at mid-single digits toward 70%+ of mix, adjusted EBITDA holds in the $312M+ range, and the de-levered balance sheet plus undrawn revolver allows the company to refinance or absorb the 2027 preferred put without equity dilution. Payments attach accelerates under a payments-veteran CEO, Retail's EBITDA growth continues, and Restaurants stabilizes. On a re-rating from ~7x EV/EBITDA toward peer software multiples, the equity doubles, and the short base is forced to cover, amplifying the move. Analyst targets to $18 reflect this scenario.

**Bear Case (~$4–5, –35% to –45%):** The 2027 preferred put forces a cash drain or dilutive refinancing into a higher-rate environment; a goodwill impairment erodes the thin equity cushion; Restaurants continues to lose SaaS share to Toast and Square; the optical revenue collapse from net hardware accounting spooks the marginal buyer; and the legacy environmental/indemnity tail (Kalamazoo doubling, AT&T litigation) consumes cash. The equity, levered behind the notes and preferred, compresses toward distressed levels.

The asymmetry is not favorable enough to own. The bull case requires multiple things to go right (refinancing, Restaurants stabilization, payments acceleration) while the bear case requires only the dated, structural overhangs to play out as the filings describe. The base case is a stock that trades sideways-to-down as the 2027 cliff approaches and reporting optics deteriorate.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The clearest negative catalysts are the optical revenue decline beginning in Q2 2026 reporting (net hardware accounting from April 1) and the March 2027 Series A preferred put. Neither is a clean, single-date earnings miss; the revenue optics are well-telegraphed and the put is ~11 months out. The Restaurants segment's continued decline is a slow bleed rather than a binary event.

**Timing window:** The preferred put resolves around March 2027, too far out to carry efficiently. The Q2 2026 reporting distortion arrives within weeks but is already disclosed and partly anticipated.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 34.4% of float with 10.8 days to cover. This is a crowded short, and a crowded short is the wrong setup to add to. Any positive surprise (refinancing announcement, payments win, activist catalyst, or a takeout given the depressed valuation and prior $2.45B asset sale) could force violent covering.

**Borrow and options signal:** Borrow on a 34.4%-short name is likely expensive, adding meaningful carry. Options are not screaming additional bearishness; if anything, the IV decline suggests the easy bearish positioning is already in place.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear-case downside to ~$4.50 is roughly 40% from $7.49. But the squeeze risk on a heavily shorted, $1.1B-cap name with a plausible takeout angle caps the practical reward:risk below 2:1.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The structural thesis is defensible, but the crowded short, expensive borrow, and a credible squeeze/takeout vector make the remaining downside unattractive relative to the squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not short here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would move VYX to a HOLD or BUY):**
1. A concrete resolution of the 2027 Series A preferred put — either a refinancing, conversion, or pre-funding plan that removes the liquidity cliff without material dilution. This is the single largest gate.
2. Restaurants segment revenue and EBITDA returning to growth for two consecutive quarters, validating that the SaaS platform thesis is intact rather than ceding share to Toast/Square.
3. Sustained positive adjusted FCF (two-plus quarters above the $30M Q1 run-rate) with stable-to-rising recurring revenue, confirming the cash inflection is structural rather than working-capital timing.

**Timeline:** The capital-structure overhang persists through at least early 2027 (the preferred put). The optical revenue distortion clouds reporting through 2026. I expect the headwinds to dominate the equity for the next 9–12 months absent a refinancing announcement.

**What would deepen the AVOID:** A goodwill impairment, a leverage-covenant breach, a ratings downgrade, AT&T indemnity litigation escalating, or the Kalamazoo reserve approaching its doubled scenario.

## Conclusion

NCR Voyix has executed a credible portfolio cleanup and is producing a genuine cash-flow inflection. On EV/EBITDA the equity looks inexpensive at ~7x. If the only question were whether the underlying business is stabilizing, the answer would lean positive.

But the equity is a thin claim behind the notes and a preferred put-able in twelve months, against current cash and distress-zone balance-sheet metrics where goodwill exceeds equity. The segment that should validate the SaaS turnaround is shrinking, 2026 reporting will optically distort revenue, and a litigious legacy environmental/indemnity tail sits on top. The 34.4% short interest and the 28.7% cut to near-term EPS estimates tell you sophisticated capital is pricing structural risk. The cheapness is earned, not mispriced, and there is no near-term catalyst to remove the capital-structure overhang. I rate VYX **AVOID** and would revisit only on a concrete resolution of the 2027 preferred put or two quarters of Restaurants re-acceleration.
