## Executive Summary

Blackbaud is the system of record for fundraising, donor management, tuition billing, and grantmaking across nonprofits, foundations, independent schools, and corporate CSR programs. The quality of the revenue base is unusual: in Q2 2026, $285.3M of $290.6M in revenue — 98.2% — was recurring. Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 38.0%, gross margin expanded 160bp year over year on GAAP basis, and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance on 7/29/26 while indicating results would land in the upper half of the range on revenue and EBITDA and at the high end on non-GAAP EPS ($5.15–5.25) and free cash flow ($280–290M). At $45.87 the shares trade at approximately 7.4x FY2026 EV/EBITDA, 8.7x guided non-GAAP EPS, ~14.3x trailing GAAP earnings, and a 13.6% equity FCF yield — deep discounts to an application-software peer median P/E of 22.8x.

The discount is explained rather than mysterious. Organic revenue growth decelerated from +3.6% in H1 2025 to +3.6% in H1 2026 and +3.0% in Q2 2026 (+2.8% constant currency), while the Q2 10-Q disclosed gross dollar retention of approximately 91% for the twelve months ended 6/30/26 — lower than the trailing period ended 12/31/25 — precisely as a 2026 renewal cohort with contracted ARR roughly 40% larger than 2025's comes due. Management expects retention to recover by end-2027. At 91% GDR, roughly nine points of recurring revenue must be replaced annually simply to hold flat, so the stated 4–6% organic target through 2030 requires 13–15 points of gross additions from a mature installed base. FY2026 revenue guidance itself embeds an H2 step-up to +4.3% to +5.4% from +3.0% in Q2, which H1 has not demonstrated.

The equity has moved violently: $70.71 high in late October 2025, a $25.58 low on 6/22/26, and $45.87 now — up roughly 79% from the trough, with a 17% single-day gain on the 7/29 print. That recovery has converted an unusually cheap entry into a merely cheap one. My base value is $52–53 (8.1x FY2026 EBITDA, 9.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.89), bull $65, bear $37. Probability-weighting those outcomes produces a high-single-digit to low-double-digit expected return with wide dispersion and low confidence on the revenue line. That is a HOLD: own it if you do, but the entry signal that existed near $28 no longer exists at $46.

## Company Overview

Blackbaud sells vertical software into the social-impact economy across four functional areas: fundraising and donor engagement (the Raiser's Edge / Blackbaud CRM lineage), education management for K-12 and higher education, financial and accounting management for nonprofits, and corporate CSR/grantmaking. Operations span the U.S., Australia, Canada, Costa Rica, India, and the U.K.

Two structural features define the model. First, near-total recurring revenue (98.2% in Q2 2026), which makes revenue highly predictable quarter to quarter and shifts the entire growth debate onto retention and pricing rather than bookings volatility. Second, an embedded payments float: at 6/30/26 the balance sheet carried $846.6M of restricted cash and $6.4M of customer funds receivable against $851.8M due to customers. That is customer money in transit and must not be netted against corporate debt; it also distorts every standardized working-capital and solvency screen applied to the company.

The moat is switching-cost and data-asset driven rather than technology driven — decades of donor histories and gift records at institutions that have used the platform for years, marketed as the largest philanthropic data set. Contract terms have been lengthened, which staggers renewals (approximately 40% of contracts renew in 2026, 30% in 2027, 30% in 2028) while reducing the frequency of repricing opportunities.

The current strategic push is agentic AI. A fundraising development agent is in general availability as an add-on subscription priced in the "tens of thousands of dollars" annually, with four additional "Agents for Good" announced and a K-12 Admissions Agent in development. Running in parallel is a margin program: transferring roles to a Global Capability Center in Hyderabad, with $1.97M of GCC workforce transition costs added back in Q2 ($3.0M in H1).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $1,058.1M → FY2023 $1,105.4M (+4.5%) → FY2024 $1,155.5M (+4.5%) → FY2025 $1,128.4M (−2.3%). The FY2025 decline reflects the EVERFI divestiture, not organic contraction: FY2024 carried a $270.5M GAAP operating loss and intangibles collapsed from $581.9M (12/31/23) to $132.9M (12/31/24), while FY2025 cost of revenue fell $51.2M and R&D fell to $138.1M from $160.6M against only a $27.1M revenue decline. A cost base left with a revenue base.

**Margins.** GAAP gross margin improved from 59.7% to 61.3% year over year in Q2; non-GAAP from 63.5% to 64.2%. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose 140bp to 38.0%. One nuance deserves attention: non-GAAP operating margin *declined* 110bp to 32.6% in the same quarter. The divergence is mechanical — adjusted EBITDA adds back amortization of capitalized software ($12.8M in Q2, from $12.3M) and depreciation, which non-GAAP operating income does not. Investors anchoring on the EBITDA headline should note the operating line moved the other way. Separately, the H1 GAAP operating margin comparison (14.0% → 19.8%) is flattered by a prior-year one-timer: H1 2025 G&A included a $24.3M charge to exit the Washington, D.C. office lease, explaining nearly all of the $24.8M G&A decline.

**Cash flow, correctly defined.** A standardized screen computes FY2025 FCF as operating cash flow of $265.6M less $7.8M of PP&E = $257.8M (22.8% margin). Blackbaud's own definition also deducts capitalized software development, which ran $27.8M in H1 2025 and $26.1M in H1 2026 and is guided at $52–62M for FY2026. On the company definition, FY2025 FCF was closer to $200–210M — an 18% margin, not 22.8%. Reconciling forward: H1 2026 company-defined FCF was $112.3M (OCF $142.5M − PP&E $4.1M − capitalized software $26.1M) against a $280–290M full-year guide, implying $168–178M in H2, or about 60% of the year. That back-half weighting is consistent with history (H1 2025 FCF was only $39.2M against a ~$200–210M year), which is why a single quarter must not be annualized: Q2 alone produced $75.3M, but Q1 operating cash flow was only $51.4M.

The H1 improvement is real but should be normalized. H1 2026 FCF of $112.3M versus $39.2M is +186%; adding back the $24.3M lease payment to the prior-year base gives roughly +77% underlying. Stock-based compensation of $46.6M in H1 (~$93M annualized) equals roughly a third of guided FCF and is an economic cost here because shares must be repurchased to offset issuance. An SBC-burdened owner-earnings anchor of ~$192M is the conservative figure.

**Leverage — what the screens get wrong and what they get right.** A standardized health screen returns an Altman Z of 0.68 ("distress"), D/E of 27.1x, current ratio 0.79, and ROE of 135%. All four are artifacts. Working capital of −$252.0M at 12/31/25 includes $369.0M of current deferred revenue, a non-cash obligation to deliver software already paid for; excluding it, working capital is +$117.0M. The customer float is self-offsetting. Book equity of $85.1M (12/31/25) and $67.0M (6/30/26) sits against $1,452.4M of treasury stock at cost, so D/E and ROE carry no information. Piotroski fails on exactly one signal — the deferred-revenue-driven current ratio — while capturing positive net income, positive ROA, positive OCF, and 2.3x OCF-to-net-income conversion in FY2025.

The measures that do carry information: total debt of $1,150.0M at 6/30/26 against $34.4M of unrestricted cash gives net debt of ~$1,115.6M; company-reported net leverage of 2.58x (versus 2.52x at 12/31/25 and a 2.91x peak at 3/31/25) reconciles with my cross-check of $1,115.6M / $434M guided EBITDA = 2.57x. Covenant maximum is 3.75x, with restricted payments including buybacks requiring pro forma leverage 0.25x below that, so the operative ceiling is 3.50x — roughly 0.9 turns of headroom for continued repurchases. Interest coverage on guided EBITDA and $62–66M of interest expense is ~6.8x. Revolver availability was ~$374M.

The genuine constraint is not solvency but the absence of any book-equity buffer: $67.0M of equity against $1,055.9M of goodwill. The weighted-average interest rate rose from 5.29% to 5.60%, so floating-rate exposure is repricing upward in a higher-for-longer environment, not downward.

**Reconciliation note.** The supplied trailing P/E of 14.27 implies TTM GAAP diluted EPS of ~$3.21 (45.87 / 14.27). I use ~14.3x as the trailing GAAP multiple; the FY2025 annual GAAP diluted EPS of $2.37 shown in the profile EPS field is the full-year figure, not a TTM figure. Similarly, the profile share count of 44.72M differs from the 45,513,708 shares outstanding at 6/30/26; the lower count is plausibly the result of continued Q3 repurchases. I use 45.5M for consistency with the 6/30/26 balance sheet, which produces a $2,088M market cap. The ~1.7% difference does not change any conclusion.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the weak link and it is decelerating: +3.6% in H1 2025, +3.6% in H1 2026, +3.0% in Q2 2026 (+2.8% constant currency), with organic recurring revenue at +3.3%. FY2026 GAAP revenue guidance of $1,173–1,179M against H1 actual of $571.7M implies H2 revenue of $601.3–607.3M versus H2 2025's $576.4M, or +4.3% to +5.4%. Blackbaud does have real H2 seasonality from year-end giving and the tuition cycle, and FX turned from a ~$2.9M H1 headwind toward neutral, so the shape is not implausible. But the guide requires acceleration in the same period in which retention was disclosed as deteriorating. Full-year implied growth at the midpoint is +4.2%, sitting at the bottom of the company's 4–6% organic target through 2030.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus rose 1.1% over 90 days to $5.25, next year 0.5% to $5.89, with the next-quarter estimate down 0.7%. This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is a stable, slowly improving non-GAAP EPS line driven mostly by margin and share count rather than revenue. The earnings track record is consistent with that read: four quarters of 0.0% to 2.8% surprises — a company that manages closely to guidance rather than one whose estimates are systematically too low.

The two identifiable growth levers are both credible and both bounded. Agentic AI add-ons priced in the tens of thousands of dollars annually monetize the installed base without new logo acquisition, but the company has disclosed no ARR contribution and one product in general availability. The Hyderabad GCC has demonstrably lifted gross margin, but at 38% adjusted EBITDA margin the offshoring lever has less travel remaining than for most mid-cap software companies attempting the same transition. Remaining performance obligations of $1.6B with ~45% recognizable in twelve months implies roughly $720M of contracted revenue against a forward-year base near $1.19B — about 60% coverage, with the balance dependent on transactional revenue and renewals not yet contracted. That is not total backlog; the practical expedient excludes variable and cancellable consideration.

On the question of whether analyst targets are floors: that principle applies to inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates. The valuation score of 5.8 reflects a real discount with a real explanation. ## Competitive Landscape

Blackbaud's addressable market grows roughly with charitable giving and institutional IT budgets, both low-single-digit series. That is the structural ceiling on the 4–6% target and the reason value creation is engineered through margin expansion plus share-count reduction rather than growth.

Competitive pressure arrives from three directions. Horizontal CRM vendors, principally Salesforce's nonprofit offering, compete for larger institutions with internal IT resources; Blackbaud's counter is purpose-built depth and the philanthropic data asset. Public-sector funding contraction is the nearest-term dynamic — the FY2025 10-K elevated exposure to U.S. federal and state grant funding flowing to nonprofit customers, and a nonprofit that loses a grant downgrades seats or fails rather than shopping for a new CRM. Generative AI is the category-level bear case: if AI collapses the cost of replicating workflow software, only data and distribution remain as moats, which is the profile the market has been derating across application software while rewarding infrastructure.

The trajectory evidence points down, not sideways. GDR at ~91% and falling into the largest renewal cohort is the cleanest available measure of competitive position, and it deteriorated in the most recent disclosure. Gross margin expansion of 160bp is genuine but sourced from labor arbitrage in Hyderabad rather than from pricing power or scale. Management's own risk factor stating that market *perception* of AI may hurt the stock price regardless of operating performance is an unusual thing to write down; it is self-serving but not obviously wrong at 8.7x non-GAAP earnings.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Retention into the 2026 renewal cohort (dominant).** GDR ~91% TTM 6/30/26, below the TTM 12/31/25 rate, with the 2026 cohort's contracted ARR roughly 40% larger than 2025's and recovery guided only by end-2027. Two explanations are equally consistent with the disclosure and lead to opposite conclusions: customers pushing back on cumulative price escalators as multi-year contracts roll (a pricing-power problem, likely permanent), or involuntary attrition from customers losing grant funding (cyclical, consistent with the end-2027 recovery language). This is the single most valuable open question and it is unresolved.

**2. Guidance shape.** The reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guide requires H2 growth of +4.3% to +5.4% against +3.0% in Q2. The EPS and FCF lines are far better supported than revenue — H1 FCF of $112.3M against a $280–290M guide is ahead of the ~80% H2-weighted 2025 shape — so a revenue miss coupled with an FCF beat is a realistic outcome and would be read badly by the market.

**3. Leverage in a higher-for-longer environment.** 2.58x net leverage against a 3.50x effective buyback ceiling is manageable, but the weighted-average rate rose to 5.60% and guided interest expense of $62–66M consumes roughly 22% of guided FCF. A retention-driven revenue miss compresses EBITDA and raises the ratio simultaneously. Management's own framing in the 10-K is the tell: leverage is elevated "primarily due to stock repurchases," and absent repurchase activity the ratio would have declined further.

**4. No book-equity cushion.** $67.0M of equity against $1,055.9M of goodwill leaves no capacity to absorb a large non-cash charge. Goodwill impairment specifically appears unlikely — if reporting-unit fair value is anchored on market capitalization plus a control premium against a carrying value approximating total equity, the cushion was still on the order of 15–19x even at the June trough — but that inference rests on single-reporting-unit and carrying-value assumptions not verified line by line.

**5. Tax and regulatory items.** A $125.6M deferred tax asset remains under valuation allowance because of a three-year cumulative loss (2023 +$1.8M, 2024 −$283.2M, 2025 +$115.0M = −$166.4M). As FY2024 rolls out of the window during FY2027, a valuation-allowance release becomes plausible — a one-time GAAP earnings benefit and potentially a lower structural effective rate, but non-cash in the year recorded. Separately, settlements from the 2020 security incident commit Blackbaud to a specified cybersecurity program through May 2044; cash costs are now immaterial ($0 in Q2), but the company operates under a long consent regime while custodying donor PII and ~$850M of customer funds.

**6. Positioning.** Short interest is 16.5% of float (4.84M shares, 4.9 days to cover). Some of the 79% move off the low is plausibly short covering rather than fundamental re-rating, which means the marginal buyer at $46 is less well identified than the marginal buyer at $28 was.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 54.4% sits 7.3 points above one-year realized volatility of 47.2%, and the term structure is in backwardation (54.4% at 11 days, 50.9% at 39 days, 47.4% at 130 days). The market is pricing an ±8.8% move on the nearest expiry despite no scheduled company event before the Q3 print. Put/call skew is reported as call-heavy (−3.5% near-term, −7.1% at three months), which alongside 16.5% short interest is consistent with continued upside-call demand from short-covering and momentum flow rather than with hedged institutional accumulation. The prior-snapshot ATM IV of 1,514.8% is a data artifact and I disregard it; what is usable is that the price rose 69.6% between snapshots while implied volatility settled to a level still above realized. For an owner, rich near-term volatility argues for monetizing upside through covered calls rather than adding equity exposure here. For a prospective buyer, backwardation plus call-heavy skew is the profile of a crowded short-term move, not of a mispricing being quietly corrected.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $65 (+42%)

Retention proves cyclical. The GDR decline is dominated by involuntary attrition from nonprofits absorbing federal and state grant cuts, and as those budgets stabilize GDR recovers toward 93–94% on management's end-2027 timeline. The 2026 renewal cohort completes without further degradation, H2 revenue delivers the guided +4.3% to +5.4%, and FY2027 organic growth returns to 4%+. Agentic AI add-ons at "tens of thousands of dollars" annually begin contributing measurable ARR from the installed base, converting the AI narrative from threat to ARPU lever. Guidance lands at the high end: non-GAAP EPS $5.25, FCF $290M. The company deploys ≥50% of FCF to repurchases at prices below intrinsic value, delivering 3–5% net annual share reduction, while the FY2027 valuation-allowance release lowers the effective tax rate. On $450M of FY2027 EBITDA at 9.4x, and roughly 11x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.89, the equity is worth $65. A strategic or sponsor approach is a tail possibility given the reported (unverified) history of unsolicited proposals at materially higher levels, though a classified board and DGCL §203 raise the difficulty of any hostile route.

### Bear Case — $37 (−19%), severe case $28–30 (−35%)

Retention proves structural. Customers resist cumulative price escalators as multi-year contracts roll, Salesforce and AI-assisted alternatives erode the switching-cost moat at the margin, and GDR settles at 89–90% rather than recovering. Gross additions of 13–15 points from a mature installed base fail to materialize, H2 2026 revenue comes in near +3% and misses the guide, and FY2027 organic growth falls to 1–2%. The margin lever is largely exhausted at 38% EBITDA, so Rule of 40 — the company-selected incentive metric — deteriorates for the first time in five years. EBITDA flattens near $420M while the weighted-average interest rate continues repricing above 5.60%, pushing net leverage toward 3.0x and constraining the buyback as the 3.50x restricted-payment threshold approaches. The multiple compresses to 6.5–6.7x EV/EBITDA, consistent with a structurally challenged, levered, low-growth vertical software asset, implying $37. If a revenue miss coincides with a broader application-software derating, the June low near $28 is revisited.

### What Would Change My Mind

The decisive datapoint is disclosed GDR, which management now reports on a trailing-twelve-month basis. A stabilization or improvement in that figure at the Q3 or Q4 print — combined with delivery of the H2 revenue step-up — converts this from a cheap low-growth asset into a mispriced one and justifies an upgrade. A further decline toward 89–90%, especially if paired with an H2 revenue miss, validates the bear case regardless of how good the EPS and FCF optics look.

## Conclusion

Blackbaud is a high-quality revenue base attached to a low-growth end market, run by management that has correctly redirected capital from destructive M&A (the EVERFI acquisition was written down substantially and then exited at a cash cost) toward repurchasing stock at 8.7x non-GAAP earnings. The cash generation is genuine — $280–290M guided, 2.3x OCF-to-net-income conversion in FY2025, 98.2% recurring revenue — and the solvency screens flagging distress are artifacts of deferred revenue and $1.45B of treasury stock rather than evidence of financial stress.

Three things keep this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, gross dollar retention at ~91% and deteriorating into the largest renewal cohort in recent history is the single most important operating metric, and the disclosure does not permit distinguishing a cyclical grant-funding cause from a structural pricing-power cause. Second, the reaffirmed revenue guide requires an H2 acceleration to +4.3%–5.4% that H1's +3.6% and Q2's +3.0% have not demonstrated, and the EPS and FCF lines are supported well enough that a revenue miss with an EPS beat is a live outcome. Third, the 79% recovery from the June low has already closed most of the valuation gap: at $45.87 against a base value of $53, the remaining upside of roughly 15% is not adequate compensation for a −19% bear case in a business with no book-equity cushion, floating-rate debt repricing upward, and a buyback program that in H1 consumed more cash than the business generated.

On the capital-allocation record, the direction is right and the execution has been uneven. The gross repurchase headline of 6.24% of shares in H1 translated to a 2.55% net reduction after 1.72M shares of equity-award issuance, and the company deployed $82.5M at an average of $51.53 in Q1 against $28.25M at an average of $35.13 in Q2 — three times the dollars at 47% higher prices — despite Q2 operating cash flow being nearly double Q1's. Plausible non-adverse explanations exist (leverage discipline near the restricted-payment threshold, front-loading of the annual authorization, blackout windows), and I draw no inference about motive. Combined with zero open-market insider purchases across the reviewed Form 4 window, a CFO sale at $31.31 days from the company's own $28.23 repurchases, a COO sale at $45.43–45.72, and five-year TSR trailing the proxy peer group by roughly 38 index points, the management dimension does not currently support a premium.

Own it for the cash yield and the buyback if you already do. Wait for either a retention datapoint that resolves the central question or a price near $38–40 before adding.
