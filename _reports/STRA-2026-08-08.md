## Executive Summary

Strategic Education is a for-profit post-secondary operator whose reported earnings have roughly doubled since 2021 while the stock has gone nowhere. The company's own proxy pay-versus-performance table quantifies it: $100 invested at year-end 2020 was worth about $97.87 at year-end 2025, against a peer index at roughly $198.2, while GAAP net income moved from $55.1M to $126.6M. The entire gap is multiple compression. The investment question is whether that discount is proportionate to the disclosed regulatory exposure or whether the market is mispricing a structurally lower-cost business.

The operating evidence from Q2 2026 is better than the headline suggests. Revenue of $337.3M grew 4.9% and beat the ~$327.5M consensus; adjusted diluted EPS of $1.76 came in about 2.2% below the ~$1.80 non-GAAP consensus, but that figure absorbs the full $13.9M reserve for back-payment to Torrens casual academic staff for 2020–2025 marking hours, recorded after the Full Federal Court of Australia reversed a prior favorable ruling on March 17, 2026. That charge was not excluded from adjusted results. At the 29% adjusted tax rate it is worth $0.454 per share on 21,736K diluted shares, so ex-reserve adjusted EPS was approximately $2.21, and ex-reserve, ex-restructuring operating margin was roughly 19.8% versus a FY2025 adjusted operating margin of 15.5%. USHE trailing four-quarter persistence reached 89.0%, a high in the disclosed eight-quarter series, and bad debt improved to 3.3% of revenue from 4.0%.

What keeps this at HOLD rather than an upgrade is the composition of the growth and the timing of the principal risk. Revenue growth of 4-5% sits on top of USHE average enrollment down 0.5% and ANZ enrollment down 5.2%; the earnings improvement is cost structure (instructional and support costs fell in absolute dollars in FY2025 while revenue grew, on a real-estate footprint reduced more than 25%) plus a 7.6% year-over-year reduction in diluted shares. Meanwhile, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminated Federal Direct Grad PLUS loans effective July 2026, with a $20,500 annual and $100,000 aggregate graduate cap and pro-rata reduction for part-time enrollment. Capella had 22,391 master's and 7,827 doctoral students in fall 2025, roughly 58% of its body is graduate, and roughly 72% of USHE students study part-time. The fall 2026 term is the first test, and it will not be visible until the late-October Q3 print. At $84.88 the stock is 5.4% below its 52-week high and 21.8% above its April low. Buying ahead of an unobservable binary at the top of the range, in a name with a five-year record of cheapness failing to convert, is not an adequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Three reportable segments as of December 31, 2025:

**U.S. Higher Education (USHE) — $868.2M FY2025 revenue, 68.5% of total.** Capella University (100% online, Higher Learning Commission accredited, reaffirmed January 2023 with next review 2032-33) had 51,753 fall 2025 students concentrated in nursing, counseling, psychology, social work, health administration and education, with a deliberate graduate tilt. Capella holds Department approval for FlexPath, a direct-assessment (non-credit-hour) competency-based model sold as a flat 12-week subscription — an approval few institutions carry. Strayer University (Middle States accredited since 1981) had 33,553 students, predominantly bachelor's, across roughly 18 states plus D.C. The combined student body is working-adult: ~70% age 31+, ~72% part-time, ~75% female, ~61% minority among self-reporting students.

**Australia/New Zealand (ANZ) — $251.6M, 19.8%.** Torrens University (self-accrediting, re-registered with TEQSA on September 3, 2025 for the maximum seven years, with two conditions the 10-K does not describe), Think Education, and Media Design School, which became a wholly owned subsidiary and international additional location of Strayer University on September 8, 2025 while remaining in the ANZ segment. Year-end 2025 enrollment 19,514, split roughly 55% domestic / 45% international.

**Education Technology Services (ETS) — $148.4M, 11.7%.** Employer partnership development, Workforce Edge (education-benefits administration, 80 employer partners), and Sophia Learning (low-cost ACE-credit-recommended general education courses transferable to 100+ partner institutions).

One structural point on ETS materially affects interpretation. The 10-K states that a significant portion of ETS revenue is driven by enrollment volume from employer relationships, that enrollments are attributed to ETS based on employment status and the existence of a corporate partnership, and that they remain attributed there until the student graduates or withdraws even if employment status changes or the contract expires. ETS therefore carries tuition economics from employer-affiliated students taught by Capella and Strayer. The filings do not disaggregate Workforce Edge platform fees, Sophia subscription revenue and attributed tuition. ETS revenue grew $43.5M in FY2025 while employer-affiliated enrollment rose only about 1,955 students, which implies roughly $22,250 per incremental student — above plausible annual tuition — so Sophia and Workforce Edge must contribute materially. But the split cannot be determined from disclosure, and the third-party claim circulating that ETS is "46% of operating income" does not reconcile: FY2025 segment math gives $58.8M ÷ ($101.9M + $35.5M + $58.8M) = 30.0%, and Q2 2026 adjusted for the ANZ charge gives 29.3%.

## Growth Analysis

Volume is flat to negative in both principal segments. USHE average enrollment fell 0.5% in Q2 2026 after a 1.4% FY2025 decline; ANZ fell 5.2% after 2.5% in Q1 and 1.8% in FY2025, decelerating in the wrong direction. Against sector data cited in the 10-K, total U.S. post-secondary enrollment rose 1.0% in fall 2025 to ~19.4 million while private for-profit enrollment fell about 1.2%, the first annual decline since 2021. STRA's USHE decline is roughly in line with its cohort, so the deterioration reads as sector-wide rather than company-specific.

Three genuine forward levers exist:

**Retention.** Trailing four-quarter USHE persistence of 89.0% is a series high, up from 88.3% at Q3 2025 and 86.9% at Q3 2024. Roughly 200 basis points of persistence on an ~86,000-student base is meaningful revenue durability and shows up before new-start volume in the reported numbers.

**Employer channel.** Employer-affiliated enrollment moved from 29.6% of USHE (FY2024) to 32.3% (FY2025) to 34.7% in Q2 2026, and trailing Title IV per credit earned has declined for at least eight consecutive quarters (−5.9% at Q1 2026). Management frames this as enrollment "less reliant on federal funding." The precise reading matters: revenue mix is shifting away from federal loan dollars per credit, but Capella still derived 68.30% of cash-basis revenue from federal funds in FY2025 and Strayer 87.98%. Mix improvement and funding dependence are separate measures, and only the first is improving.

**Margin.** Management signaled roughly 200 basis points of EBIT margin expansion on the Q2 call, per secondary summaries; no transcript was filed with EDGAR, so I treat the figure as unverified. The Q2 ex-reserve, ex-restructuring margin of 19.8% is consistent with it, though Q2 is seasonally stronger than Q3 for USHE (summer is the low term) and is not a full-year run rate.

Set against those: Australian growth was explicitly deferred to 2027, and the lever management had pointed to has been removed — the 2027 National Planning Level for international students, announced July 3, 2026, is flat with 2026. ANZ's ~45% international cohort is administratively rationed, so growth must come from domestic enrollment, a lower-margin vector than the international business Torrens was acquired to capture. And Grad PLUS elimination directly targets the highest-value part of the USHE mix.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +2.5% over 90 days, current year +0.3%, next year +4.4% to $8.45. This is nowhere near the magnitude that signals an underestimated earnings inflection; it reads as consensus marking to a good revenue print and margin commentary.

The honest reading of this multiple is that it is cheap and has been cheap for five years without converting. Two identifiable reasons the market applies the discount:

**Australian goodwill.** ANZ goodwill of $526.5M plus $66.2M of indefinite-lived trade name at June 30, 2026 equals $592.7M, roughly 31% of market cap. Management's own trigger language is "a sustained decline in international students without corresponding growth in domestic enrollment." Cycle-adjusted, this is not a trough-earnings situation requiring a normalized-earnings lens — margins are at or near record and the incremental margin has already been harvested from real-estate and cost reduction. Analyst targets ($90 low, $105 high, $98.33 mean, $100 median) all sit above spot, but on three-analyst coverage and with a consensus that likely does not model the fall enrollment cycle, I treat them as a directional data point rather than independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Management's own Item 1 language is unhelpful to any moat narrative: the market is "highly competitive, with no private or public institution holding a significant market share," and as online learning matures "the intensity of the competition we face will continue to increase." The addressable market is large — ~64.0 million U.S. adults 25+ with no more than a high school education and ~32.2 million with some college and no degree — but for-profit share of it is shrinking.

The advantage visible in the numbers is cost structure. Strayer's high-cohort faculty model, Capella's fully online delivery, FlexPath's flat-subscription direct-assessment pricing and Sophia's self-paced architecture together produced a 52.9% two-year incremental adjusted operating margin and allowed absolute cost dollars to fall while revenue grew. That is durable against traditional non-profit competitors carrying campuses and tenure, and it is not durable against other online-first operators.

Two narrower structural assets: FlexPath's direct-assessment approval, which few institutions hold, and Sophia's credit-transfer network into 100+ partner institutions, which doubles as a low-cost funnel into Capella and Strayer. Sophia's franchise value depends entirely on ACE credit recommendation and partner acceptance, which makes the AI academic-integrity question analysts pressed on the Q2 call more consequential than it is usually treated. Management said controls are being implemented; that is an assertion relayed through secondary sources, not verified data. An integrity failure that caused partner institutions to stop accepting Sophia credit would impair the fastest-growing, highest-margin product and degrade the affordability funnel simultaneously.

Australia is policy-controlled rather than demand-controlled. Ministerial Direction 111 (December 2024) and the National Planning Level framework administratively ration international enrollment; SEI enrolled to its 2025 cap, the 2026 cap rose, and 2027 is flat. The growth vector management identifies — domestic enrollment — is structurally lower-margin.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Grad PLUS elimination (highest near-term weight).** Effective July 2026 with limited grandfathering, plus a $20,500 annual / $100,000 aggregate graduate cap, $50,000 / $200,000 for "professional" students, a $257,500 lifetime Title IV cap, and pro-rata reduction of annual eligibility for less-than-full-time enrollment — directly relevant to a body that is ~72% part-time. The RISE final rules (May 1, 2026) define "professional degree" restrictively by CIP code; clinical psychology is on the enumerated list, nursing is not. Capella holds both CCNE and APA accreditation, so the practical outcome differs materially by program. Reported litigation (*AANP v. McMahon*) and a reported June 2026 Department interim list expanding the professional category to some graduate nursing programs are not confirmed in SEI's filings and should not be relied on. This is the single highest-leverage open item for FY2027 earnings, and the first data arrives in the late-October Q3 print.

**2. ANZ goodwill and trade name — 31% of market cap, 17% measured headroom in October 2024, enrollment −5.2%.** Non-cash if impaired, with the composite-score interaction noted above.

**3. Australian casual-academic back-pay.** The $13.9M reserve covers 2020–2025. The Q2 10-Q also discloses that SEI began compensating casual academic staff for marking hours related to 2026 terms, recognized within instructional costs and not separately quantified. A crude $13.9M ÷ six years suggests ~$2.3M annually, but that is inference; the ruling's scope, current staffing and appeal posture are not disclosed. Do not assume immateriality.

**4. Strayer 90/10.** 89.64% (FY2024) improved to 87.98% (FY2025), from 36 to 202 basis points of headroom. Under the July 2024 financial-responsibility regulations, failing 90/10 is a mandatory trigger allowing the Department to demand financial protection of 10-50% of prior-year Title IV funds, with triggers stackable. Two plausible drivers of the improvement — employer-funded revenue growth and the Department's July 7, 2025 interpretive guidance that the 10-K says "effectively rescinded" prior restrictive language — are both consistent with the filings, which do not attribute the change.

**5. Program-level earnings accountability.** A graduate program loses Direct Loan eligibility if, in two of three years, median completer earnings fall below those of bachelor's-only workers aged 25-34. Capella's concentration in counseling, psychology, human services, social work and education is precisely the exposed profile. Timing is later than headline reading suggests: final rules June 29, 2026, most provisions effective July 1, 2027, first metrics 2027-28, sanctions no earlier than 2028-29. The FY2025 10-K states neither Capella nor Strayer is identified as a "lower earnings" institution under the December 2025 FAFSA tool — a specific screen pass, but an institution-level one, while the OBBBA test operates at program level.

**6. Cybersecurity litigation.** Incident detected February 25, 2026; putative class actions consolidated as *In re Strategic Education Data Breach Litigation* in E.D. Va. on June 26, 2026. No loss provision recognized; insurance recovery expected. Unquantified.

**7. Partial offsets that headline framing misses.** OBBBA delayed the borrower-friendly 2022 Borrower Defense rule to July 1, 2035 and reinstated the 2019 rule — three-year limitation from separation, no group claims, borrower burden of proof, monetary-harm requirement, exclusion of harm attributable to labor-market conditions. Against several thousand pending BDTR claims, that is genuine and quantitatively meaningful de-risking. The January 2026 negotiated-rulemaking consensus would also eliminate the debt-to-earnings gainful-employment metric.

**8. Committed spend.** A $42.8M media advertising purchase commitment through December 2028 with annual minimums was fully outstanding at 12/31/2025. If LLM-based search degrades paid lead-generation efficiency, this cannot be flexed quickly. Management said on the Q2 call that LLMs have not affected inquiry volumes; that is an assertion, not data.

**9. Governance items.** Compensation performance measures are Revenue, EBIT and Diluted EPS, with the company-selected revenue figure of $1,274.0M corresponding to the constant-currency adjusted number rather than GAAP $1,268.2M. No enrollment, persistence, completion or compliance metric appears among disclosed measures. All three metrics can be advanced through price, cost reduction and repurchase during flat-to-declining enrollment, which is descriptively what FY2025 and H1 2026 look like. This is an observation about incentive design, not an allegation. A March 3, 2025 Item 4.01 change in certifying accountant remains unresolved — predecessor, successor and any disagreements are unknown, and that should be closed before finalizing a governance view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $115 (+35%)

Grad PLUS proves manageable because the shift toward employer-funded enrollment (34.7% of USHE and rising) and FlexPath's low-cost flat-subscription pricing keep total borrowing needs inside the new caps, while any expansion of the "professional degree" definition to include graduate nursing protects Capella's largest program cluster. Persistence at 89.0% holds, USHE returns to modest enrollment growth, and management delivers the roughly 200 basis points of EBIT margin expansion signaled on the Q2 call. ANZ stabilizes on domestic enrollment as management guides for 2027, avoiding impairment. The company executes the remaining $140.7M authorization and renews it, retiring another 7%+ of shares. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches or exceeds the $8.45 consensus, and the multiple re-rates toward the 15.6x peer median. At 13.6x $8.45 the stock is $115; at the peer median it is above $130, which is why the sell-side high target sits at $105 with room above.

### Bear Case — $70 (−17.5%)

Fall 2026 graduate and doctoral starts decline materially as prospective Capella students without Grad PLUS access defer or drop to part-time (where annual eligibility is now pro-rated). USHE revenue turns negative in 2027, and because roughly half of the cost base was already harvested through the real-estate and personnel reductions, the operating leverage that produced a 52.9% incremental margin on the way up runs in reverse. ANZ enrollment deteriorates past −7% against a flat 2027 planning level, forcing a quantitative goodwill test and an impairment against $592.7M of carrying value, which cuts book equity into the Department's financial-responsibility composite calculation. The prospective Australian marking-hour cost proves larger than the $2-3M annual inference, and the data-breach litigation requires a provision. FY2027 EPS falls to roughly $7.00 and the multiple compresses to 10x. That is $70, essentially the April 2026 low of $69.70.

### Probability-weighted view

Assigning 45% to a $94 base (13x FY2026 adjusted EPS of ~$7.20, 11.1x FY2027 consensus, plus continued share shrink), 25% to the $115 bull and 30% to the $70 bear gives an expected value near $92, roughly +8.4% on price and +11.2% including the dividend, against −17.5% in the bear case. Reward-to-risk of roughly 1.1:1 to base and 1.7:1 to bull does not clear the bar for adding capital, particularly when the dominant uncertainty resolves observably within one quarter. The rational course is to let the October print price the Grad PLUS effect rather than pay for the option now at 5.4% below the 52-week high.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (fourth consecutive non-buy call). The score trajectory has been 6.8 → 6.5 → 5.6 → 5.5, and the flattening reflects offsetting developments since the June review rather than stability: the price rose from $75.33 while Q2 confirmed underlying margin expansion (19.8% ex-reserve), record persistence and a 7.6% share-count reduction, and while ANZ deteriorated from −2.5% to −5.2%, a new retrospective Australian labor charge appeared with an unquantified recurring tail, and the 2027 Australian planning level came in flat, removing the growth lever management had cited. Improving operations and a narrower valuation gap net to unchanged.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, expected late October 2026. This is the first quarter to include fall-term enrollment under the post-Grad PLUS regime.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 disclosure showing USHE graduate and doctoral enrollment flat or better year over year, with total USHE enrollment no worse than −1%.
2. Buyback authorization renewed beyond December 31, 2026 at or above the H1 pace (roughly $70M per half), with average repurchase price below intrinsic value.
3. ANZ enrollment decline narrowing to better than −3% and, ideally, a disclosed quantitative goodwill test showing headroom above 20%.
4. A price of $72-75 with fundamentals intact, which restores roughly 9x next-year consensus and a ~25% discount to base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. USHE graduate enrollment down more than 5% year over year in Q3 or Q4 2026, or new-start volume deterioration explicitly attributed to loan-limit changes.
2. ANZ goodwill impairment exceeding $100M, or ANZ enrollment worse than −7%.
3. Strayer 90/10 rising back above 89%, or any Department demand for financial protection.
4. A material loss provision established in the data-breach litigation, or quantified prospective Australian marking costs above roughly $6M annually.
5. Buyback suspension, or the authorization lapsing unrenewed at year-end.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. The 2.83% dividend at 37% of FCF, no funded debt, an undrawn $250M revolver and an 8-9% FCF yield support the current price; there is no fundamental case for exiting into the fall data.

## Conclusion

Strategic Education is a genuinely cheaper business than it was three years ago and a genuinely better-run one on cost: absolute instructional dollars falling on rising revenue, a real-estate footprint down more than 25%, bad debt at 3.3% of revenue, persistence at a series high of 89.0%, and a diluted share count down 7.6% year over year at repurchase prices below the current quote. At 11.8x current-year and 10.0x next-year consensus, roughly 24-36% below the education-services peer median, with a combined distribution yield in the high single digits and no funded debt, the valuation is not the problem.

The problem is that the discount has an identifiable cause and the cause has not yet been tested. Grad PLUS elimination took effect in July 2026 against an institution where 58% of Capella's students are graduate, 7,827 are doctoral, and roughly 72% study part-time under newly pro-rated annual eligibility.
