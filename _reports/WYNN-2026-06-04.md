**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wynn Resorts operates four luxury integrated resorts across Macau, Las Vegas, and Boston, plus a 40% stake in the first casino resort under construction in the United Arab Emirates. The business is high-quality at the property level (18 Forbes Five-Star awards, premium pricing power, and demonstrated share gains in a softening Las Vegas market), but the equity carries three structural overhangs: ~52% revenue concentration in Macau under a concession that becomes redeemable from 2030, a leveraged holding-company balance sheet with negative book equity, and a UAE development that has slipped on supply-chain disruption. Over the past four reports this name has been downgraded from BUY to AVOID as estimates fell and Macau VIP weakened.

This report moves the recommendation back to HOLD. The downgrade thesis that drove the prior AVOID (falling estimates, Macau VIP collapse, decelerating Macau GGR) has substantially played out. The stock fell from a $134.72 high in December 2025 to roughly $95 in mid-May 2026, a ~29% drawdown, and has since recovered to ~$105. My own fair-value work lands at $85–$100, which brackets the current price; with downside to the bear case of roughly 10–15% and upside to a normalized base case of similar magnitude, the reward/risk is close to 1:1. An AVOID rating requires the bearish view to be unpriced or the business to be structurally impaired. Neither condition holds: the bearish view is largely in the tape, the company generated $692M of FCF in FY2025, it remains covenant-compliant, and the negative equity is a buyback artifact, not insolvency.

Two upside swing factors that cut against an AVOID deserve explicit weight. First, 18 of 19 tracked sell-side analysts rate WYNN a Buy or Strong Buy with a low target of $118 (about 14% above the current price) and zero sells. Second, Wynn's largest shareholder, Tilman Fertitta (~12.3%), just agreed to take Caesars private for $17.6B all-cash in May 2026, putting Wynn in the path of a consolidating sector with an activist-capable holder on the register. Against a premium multiple and a converging-but-still-negative estimate trend, this is a balanced situation and HOLD is the appropriate call.

## Company Overview

Wynn designs, develops, and operates luxury integrated resorts at the premium end of the global casino-hotel market. The portfolio comprises four segments:

- **Macau (~52% of FY2025 revenue, $3.72B):** Wynn Palace (Cotai) and Wynn Macau (downtown), held through ~72% ownership of Hong Kong-listed Wynn Macau, Limited. The gaming concession runs January 2023 through December 2032.
- **Las Vegas (~36%, $2.57B):** Wynn Las Vegas plus Encore (4,748 rooms on 215 Strip acres) and a 50.1% Retail Joint Venture.
- **Encore Boston Harbor (~12%, $847M):** A Category 1 Massachusetts license with a 15-year term from 2019.
- **Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE):** A 40% equity stake in the joint venture building the first casino resort in the UAE (Ras Al Khaimah), a ~$5.1B project.

The competitive identity is luxury, design, and service rather than scale. In Macau, Wynn is one of six concessionaires alongside Sands China, Galaxy, SJM, Melco, and MGM China. Its moat is brand prestige and a high-end customer base; its core vulnerability is concentration, with all current cash flow coming from a handful of properties skewed toward Chinese and Asian premium customers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has plateaued after the post-COVID rebound.** The sequence runs $3.76B (2022) → $6.53B (2023) → $7.13B (2024) → $7.14B (2025). FY2025 revenue was essentially flat year-over-year (+0.1%), confirming the reopening rebound has matured into a steady-state base.

**FY2025 profitability fell for non-operational reasons.** Net income attributable declined 34.7% to $327.3M (diluted EPS $3.14 vs $4.35). The drop was driven by a +$101M swing in the income tax provision (2024 benefited from a foreign tax credit valuation-allowance release) and $64M lower interest income. Operating income was roughly stable at $1.118B (~15.7% margin). Adjusted Property EBITDAR was ~$2.22B: Las Vegas $902M, Wynn Palace $683M, Wynn Macau $402M, Encore Boston $237M.

**Q1 2026 was a clear acceleration.** Revenue rose 9.2% to $1.857B; net income attributable rose 65.6% to $120.5M; adjusted EPS of $1.25 beat the reported $1.04 GAAP diluted EPS. The drivers were Wynn Palace (revenue +23%, EBITDAR +26% on strong VIP and mass table win) and Las Vegas (revenue +5.9%, ADR $592, +12.3%). The quality of this beat is mixed: it was helped by a $46.8M derivative gain, and Wynn Macau's VIP business ran abnormally low hold with turnover down 59%, a hold headwind rather than a tailwind in the consolidated Macau number, which weakens any "transitory beat" framing in either direction. Wynn Macau EBITDAR fell 16% on higher gaming taxes. The Macau strength is concentrated at Palace, not the downtown property.

**Cash flow is solid; the balance sheet reads worse than it is.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.353B, capex $660M, FCF ~$692M (9.7% FCF margin). Cash stood at $1.46B plus $602M short-term investments. Total debt is $10.63B with 82% fixed-rate; a 100bp move on the floating portion (~$1.9B) costs ~$19M annually. The headline red flags, negative stockholders' equity (-$275M; total deficit including NCI -$1.03B) and an Altman Z of 0.92 in the "distress" zone, are artifacts of years of buybacks (accumulated deficit driven by repurchases), not operating insolvency. The company is profitable, FCF-positive, covenant-compliant, and Piotroski-healthy (5/5 partial, current ratio 1.63). The leverage is real and elevated and deserves to weigh on the financial-health score, but the negative book equity should not be read as distress.

**Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and disciplined.** FY2025 returned $380M via buybacks (4.57M shares) plus $174.7M in dividends ($0.25/quarter), totaling ~$555M; $401M of buyback authority remained as of Q1'26. The effective tax rate is unusually low (~6.3% in Q1'26) due to the Macau 12% Complementary Tax exemption on gaming profits, in effect through 2027, a renewal item to monitor.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided, which is why the estimate trajectory matters more than any single quarter.

**Forward estimates are falling, but the miss magnitude is converging.** Current-quarter consensus has been cut 14.3% over 30 days and 16.7% over 90; current-year EPS is down 6.0%/8.6%; next-year down ~12.6%. That is negative earnings momentum and the central reason this is not a BUY. At the same time, the realized miss trend is improving sharply: the company missed by 27.9% in Q3'25, 22.2% in Q4'25, 11.4% in Q2'25, and only 2.3% in Q1'26. Citing "four consecutive misses" as a flat negative overstates the visibility problem; the trajectory is toward in-line.

**Macau mix is shifting toward more durable revenue.** Market-wide Macau GGR reached $30.9B in 2025 (vs $28.4B in 2024), with visitation up 14.7%, but growth is decelerating and increasingly mass-market and non-gaming led. Wynn Palace's mass and slot strength fits this; downtown Wynn Macau's VIP shrinkage is consistent with the same structural shift. The mix toward mass play is healthier long-term even as it pressures near-term VIP-heavy EBITDAR.

**Two greenfield growth options.** The Enclave at Wynn Palace, a 432-suite non-gaming tower, budget $900–950M, construction starting H2 2026, opening ~2029, extends the Macau franchise and helps satisfy concession non-gaming investment commitments. Wynn Al Marjan Island is the larger and higher-variance option: a first-mover position in the first UAE casino resort, with life-to-date contributions of $1.01B and $350–450M remaining. The opening has slipped from 2027 on supply-chain disruption tied to the regional conflict, and the company expects to quantify the revised timeline in coming months. This is genuine optionality with genuine execution and completion-guarantee risk attached.

**Las Vegas is taking share in a softer market.** Total Vegas visitor volume fell 7.5% in 2025 (38.5M vs 41.7M) and Strip occupancy dropped to 83.2% from 86.4%, yet Wynn delivered ADR of $592 (+12.3%) and table-win growth. The high-end is outperforming the broader Strip.

## Valuation Assessment

At $103.43, WYNN trades at ~30x trailing EPS of $3.14. That trailing multiple overstates the optical expensiveness because FY2025 earnings were depressed by the tax-provision swing; on the current-year consensus of $4.72, the forward P/E is ~21.9x, and on next-year $5.19 (even after a 12.6% cut), ~19.9x. EV/EBITDAR on ~$2.22B against an enterprise value of roughly $19–20B sits around 8.5–9x, in line with where premium casino operators trade.

The Consumer Discretionary peer screen (median P/E ~18.2) is a poor comparison set; Texas Roadhouse, Domino's, Murphy USA, GameStop, and Penske are not casino operators and do not share Wynn's geographic or operating profile. Within gaming, an EV/EBITDAR in the high-8x range for a premium operator with a UAE option and Las Vegas share gains is reasonable, neither cheap nor stretched.

The most important valuation fact is that the de-rating has already happened. The estimate cuts and Macau VIP weakness that justified the prior caution were absorbed during the ~29% decline from the December high. My fair-value range of $85–$100 brackets the current price. Bear case sits near $85–$90 (10–15% downside); a normalized base case on a stabilized $4.72–$5.19 earnings stream at a high-single-digit EV/EBITDAR supports $110–$120 (7–16% upside). That is roughly 1:1 reward/risk, the profile of a fairly valued stock, which argues for HOLD rather than either a conviction long or an active short.

The full sell-side panel reinforces this read: low target $118, mean $135.89, median $136.00, high $150, with the most bearish target ~14% above spot, 18 of 19 ratings Buy/Strong Buy and zero sells. These targets do lag deterioration, but the absence of a single sell against a stock already ~23% off its high indicates the downside is largely priced.

## Competitive Landscape

Wynn's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the property level even as the macro backdrop softens. In Las Vegas, the company is taking high-end share against a market down 7.5% in visitation, posting ADR and table-win gains while Strip-wide gaming win was flat. In Macau, Wynn Palace is growing into the mass-market shift that now leads regional GGR, while the downtown property's VIP exposure is the weaker link. The brand moat (18 consecutive years of Forbes Five-Star recognition, the longest streak of any independent hotel company) supports premium pricing that is difficult for scale-focused competitors to replicate.

The sector is consolidating. Fertitta's take-private of Caesars (May 28, 2026) signals an industry entering a deal phase. For Wynn, this cuts two ways: it raises competitive intensity if a privately-held Caesars invests aggressively, but it also introduces strategic optionality given that Fertitta is Wynn's largest holder. The UAE position is a genuine first-mover advantage in a market no competitor can yet enter, though it carries first-of-kind regulatory and execution risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Macau concentration and concession risk is the dominant exposure.** Roughly half of revenue and EBITDAR come from Macau. The concession is rescindable without compensation for failure of obligations (including national-security grounds), and from year 8 (2030) the government may redeem it with one year's notice; Wynn is currently in year 4. Layer in PRC visa policy, anti-corruption campaigns, RMB capital controls, smoking restrictions, and typhoon exposure (Typhoon Ragasa closed Macau operations one day in September 2025).

**UAE execution and off-balance-sheet exposure.** Wynn holds only 40% (limited control) and has provided a completion guarantee on the project's $2.4B facility (jointly with the RAK government, terminating no later than June 30, 2028), plus a smaller completion guarantee and equity pledge for a district cooling plant. This is an unproven market with a new regulatory framework.

**Leverage and holding-company structure.** Debt sits against a parent dependent on subsidiary distributions, with WML and WRF dividend-restricting covenants and a $150M/yr cap on the Macau IP license fee to the parent. The negative book equity is a buyback artifact, but the absolute debt load is real and limits flexibility in a downturn.

**Premium/credit concentration and tax renewal.** A limited number of premium customers drive table revenue; 73.9% of markers are owed by non-US (mostly Asian) customers, where gaming-debt enforceability is limited. The Macau Complementary Tax exemption requires renewal beyond 2027, and OECD Pillar 2 is an open item. The Sept 2024 DOJ non-prosecution agreement ($130M forfeiture re: AML/foreign-agent activity at Wynn LV) is resolved.

**Upside risk that cuts against the downside.** Fertitta's demonstrated sector M&A appetite, combined with his Wynn stake, creates real consolidation/strategic optionality. His Form 4 activity through March–June 2026 has been covered-call writing against his position (premium generation/partial hedging) rather than outright share sales, indicating his underlying ownership is intact even as his attention is split by Caesars.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 40.5% sits modestly rich to 1-year realized of 35.4% (a 5.1pp spread, 59th percentile), and the term structure is flat (near 40.5% ≈ far 40.0%), indicating uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a single near-term catalyst. The put/call OI ratio of 0.16 is call-heavy, and IV has compressed 4.4pp (44.9% → 40.5%) as the price recovered 4.1%, both consistent with easing downside fear rather than building short pressure. Short interest at 10.8% of float (6.3 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme; with sell-side overwhelmingly long and OI tilted toward calls, the positioning does not corroborate an active short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$120–$150).** Macau GGR continues its mass-led recovery, Wynn Palace sustains 20%+ EBITDAR growth, and downtown Macau VIP normalizes. Las Vegas holds its high-end share gains and ADR premium. The UAE resort opens on a clarified timeline and de-risks into a first-mover monopoly position. Estimate cuts reverse as the miss-magnitude convergence (27.9% → 2.3%) continues toward beats. Fertitta or another consolidator surfaces strategic value. Sell-side targets ($136 median, $150 high) are realized as consensus catches up.

**Bear Case (~$85–$90).** Macau decelerates further, downtown VIP weakness spreads to Palace, and the concession-redemption clock (2030) begins pressuring the multiple. UAE slips again or runs over budget, triggering completion-guarantee exposure. Las Vegas visitation softness deepens beyond the high-end's ability to offset it. Leverage limits buyback support, and the 2027 tax-exemption renewal becomes an overhang. Estimates continue to fall and the multiple compresses toward 8x EV/EBITDAR.

The base case sits between these: a fairly valued operator with stabilizing-but-not-yet-rising estimates, genuine but slow-burning growth options, and an activist-capable holder providing a floor of strategic optionality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August 2026), which will show whether the miss-magnitude convergence continues and whether downtown Macau VIP stabilizes. The UAE timeline quantification and any clarification of Fertitta's post-Caesars intentions are the other near-term catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Current-year consensus EPS revisions turn positive over a 30/90-day window (currently -6.0%/-8.6%), confirming the estimate cuts have bottomed.
2. Wynn Macau VIP turnover and EBITDAR stabilize (Q1'26 turnover was -59%; a return toward flat would remove the largest drag).
3. A concrete, on-budget UAE opening date that de-risks the largest growth option, OR a credible strategic development from the Fertitta stake (e.g., a stated intention to increase ownership).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A Macau-specific structural shock — concession action, materially tightened visa/capital controls, or a sustained GGR contraction — that impairs the ~52% revenue base.
2. UAE timeline slips materially again with a budget overrun that activates completion-guarantee liability beyond the disclosed contribution range.
3. Estimate cuts re-accelerate (current-year revisions worse than -15% over 90 days) alongside renewed Las Vegas margin compression, signaling the earnings base is eroding rather than stabilizing.

## Conclusion

WYNN is a high-quality luxury operator trading at a fair price after a ~23% de-rating from its December high. The estimate-cut and Macau-VIP weakness that justified earlier caution have substantially played out, and the stock has recovered ~10% off its May lows. With my fair-value range bracketing the current $103.43, downside to the bear case of 10–15% against comparable upside, sell-side targets uniformly above spot, FCF-positive and covenant-compliant operations, and real consolidation optionality from an activist-capable holder, the situation is balanced rather than impaired. The falling forward estimates and the genuine concentration/leverage/UAE risks keep this from being a long; the absence of structural impairment and the already-priced bear case keep it from being an active short.

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a fairly valued name where the next two earnings prints and the UAE/Fertitta developments will determine the direction. I would turn constructive on confirmed estimate stabilization or a de-risked UAE timeline, and would move to AVOID on a Macau-specific structural shock or renewed estimate acceleration to the downside.
