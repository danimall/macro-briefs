**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Church & Dwight is the smallest-cap of the major U.S. household-products names but runs one of the more disciplined operating models in consumer staples: seven "power brands" (Arm & Hammer, OxiClean, Touchland, Batiste, Waterpik, TheraBreath, Hero) generate roughly 70% of sales and profits, six of the seven were acquired since 2001, and the portfolio is split 66% premium / 34% value to hedge consumer trade-down. The business converts earnings to cash at a high rate (FY2025 FCF margin 17.6%, operating cash flow above net income) and carries well-laddered fixed-rate debt. These are real, durable strengths.

The investment debate is about price, not business quality. At ~$96.83, CHD trades at roughly 25.5x forward earnings against a household-products peer median near 19x, while delivering only ~2.6% adjusted EPS growth in FY2025 and yielding 1.25% versus a 3.9% peer median. The premium rests on quality and defensiveness rather than growth, which compresses the margin of safety. Two specific overhangs add to the caution: a telegraphed Waterpik impairment risk (fair-value cushion compressed from 35% to 17% in one year on a $644.7M trade name) and decelerating earnings-beat magnitude (8.6% and 8.8% surprises in mid-2025 narrowing to 1.8% and 1.0% in the two most recent quarters).

We rate CHD a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The reason to hold for appreciation, rather than avoid, is concrete and dated: reported organic growth was depressed in Q1 2026 by an 8.1-point drag from deliberately exited businesses, masking +5.3% underlying organic volume — momentum that outpaced P&G's largely price-driven ~3%. As that exits drag laps out across H2 2026, reported growth should converge toward the underlying volume rate, supporting the premium multiple and the sell-side's $105 median / $115 high targets. We do not see a buy here because the valuation already embeds much of that reacceleration and the Waterpik thread is unresolved.

## Company Overview

Church & Dwight develops, manufactures, and markets household, personal care, and specialty products. Three reporting segments in FY2025: Consumer Domestic (77% of sales), Consumer International (18%), and Specialty Products Division (5%, primarily sodium bicarbonate and animal nutrition). Approximately 82% of sales are U.S.-based, and online represents 21.4% of consumer sales.

The competitive model avoids head-on commodity battles with P&G, Clorox, Colgate, Reckitt, and Unilever, instead leading defensible niche categories: baking soda, cat litter, condoms (Trojan), pregnancy tests (First Response), dry shampoo (Batiste), acne patches (Hero), and dry mouth/oral care (TheraBreath). The moat rests on category leadership in those niches, a low-cost manufacturing culture, and a repeatable acquisition-integration playbook. The most recent addition, premium hand-sanitizer brand Touchland (acquired July 2025 for $656M cash plus a $158.7M earnout and $50M of founder stock), was immediately designated the seventh power brand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $5,376M (2022) → $5,868M (2023) → $6,107M (2024) → $6,203M (2025, +1.6%). The headline understates underlying activity because management has been deliberately pruning the portfolio. FY2025 organic decomposed to +0.8% volume, -0.1% price/mix, +1.9% from the Touchland acquisition, and -1.0% from exited businesses.

Q1 2026 is the more informative data point: reported sales rose only +0.2%, but that headline absorbed an 8.1-point drag from exited businesses while underlying organic volume grew +5.3%. The exits — Flawless, Spinbrush, Waterpik showerheads (May 2025) and the VitaFusion/L'il Critters VMS portfolio (sold December 2025 for ~$160M) — are deliberate cleanups, not demand erosion.

**Profitability:** FY2025 gross margin was 44.7% (down ~100bps on exit charges and a tough prior-year tariff-refund comparison), operating margin 17.4%, net margin 11.9%. GAAP EPS of $3.02 grew 27%, but nearly all of that reflects the non-recurrence of 2024's $357M vitamins/minerals/supplements impairment; adjusted EPS of $3.53 was only +2.6%. Q1 2026 gross margin improved to 46.4% (+140bps) on productivity gains (+150bps) and Touchland mix, even after absorbing a 180bps manufacturing-cost and tariff headwind. The margin recovery is genuine and supports the H2 reacceleration thesis.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** Operating cash flow of $1,215M and FCF of $1,093M (17.6% FCF margin), high-quality earnings with OCF exceeding net income. Long-term debt of $2,205M is entirely fixed at a 4.1% weighted-average rate, laddered across 2027–2052 maturities, which insulates CHD from the higher-for-longer rate path. Offsetting positives: D/E of 1.23, a current ratio of 1.07, an Altman Z of 2.23 (grey zone), and a balance sheet where $6.1B of $8.9B total assets are goodwill and intangibles. Cash fell from $964M to $409M during 2025, reflecting the Touchland purchase and $900M of buybacks, so the cash buffer is thinner heading into 2026.

**Earnings surprise pattern:** Four consecutive beats, but the magnitude is decelerating — 8.6% (Q2 2025) and 8.8% (Q3 2025) compressed to 1.8% (Q4 2025) and 1.0% (Q1 2026). This pattern signals consensus catching up to a business with limited upside slack against tougher comps, and it tempers confidence in further estimate-beating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture requires looking through two distortions. Reported revenue (+1.6% FY2025, +0.2% Q1 2026) makes the company look stagnant, but that reflects portfolio surgery; underlying organic volume was +5.3% in Q1. Conversely, the +27% GAAP EPS growth in 2025 is almost entirely the absence of a 2024 impairment; the real adjusted figure was +2.6%.

The 12-month growth thesis hinges on a mechanical, dated catalyst. The ~8% drag from exited businesses laps out over H2 2026, so as Q3 and Q4 2026 print, reported organic growth should converge toward the underlying volume rate near 5%. That convergence is the headline catching up to a business that is already growing faster than it looks, not new demand, but a reported reacceleration of this magnitude typically supports staples sentiment and multiple maintenance. Management reiterated full-year guidance after Q1, and Q1 organic of ~5% beat the ~3% guide.

Longer-dated optionality comes from the 2030 strategy, which the new compensation plan hard-wires into PSUs: Arm & Hammer net sales $2B→$3B, international $1B→$2B, and oral care (TheraBreath) $1B→$1.5B. These are credible white-space and international targets, and the M&A playbook (Touchland being the latest) provides inorganic upside.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than confirming an inflection. Next-year consensus rose +2.1% over 30 days and +1.2% over 90 days, and current-year is roughly flat (-0.5% over 90 days). But the current quarter was cut -8.3% over 90 days, a near-term softening that argues against extrapolating acceleration. There is no +30% estimate-revision velocity here; this is a low-single-digit earnings grower with stronger-than-apparent volume, not a trough inflection story.

CHD's valuation is the binding constraint on the rating. The stock trades at ~25.5x forward earnings (consensus current-year EPS $3.75, next-year $4.09) against a household-products peer median of 19.0x:

CHD commands a ~34% multiple premium to the median while paying less than a third of the peer dividend yield and delivering low-single-digit adjusted EPS growth. On a PEG basis the premium is hard to defend: ~25.5x against ~2.6% adjusted EPS growth implies a PEG well above 3. The premium is justifiable only on quality grounds: higher FCF margin, volume-led (not price-led) growth that is more durable in a soft-pricing environment, lower category cyclicality, and a longer dividend-growth record. Those merits are real but largely priced.

The value-trap warning that haunts low-multiple names does not apply here; the risk runs the other way. At a premium multiple, beats are expected (limited re-rating reward) and any disappointment — a Waterpik write-down, a tariff step-up, or a volume stumble against tough comps — carries asymmetric downside. Analyst targets imply modest upside (median $105, +8%; RBC $114, +18%; mean $102.53), but on a 25.5x stock those targets reflect the same defensive-quality premium embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is stretched relative to the growth it funds, even after crediting the quality premium.

## Competitive Landscape

CHD's competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating. On the positive side, Q1 2026 organic volume (+5.3%) outpaced P&G's largely price-driven ~3%, a healthier composition in an environment where pricing power is fading and consumers are trading down. Category leadership in niches, the 70%-of-profits power-brand concentration, and the consistent integration of acquisitions (Touchland, Hero, TheraBreath, Batiste) all support a defensible position. The 66/34 premium/value split provides a genuine trade-down hedge: value SKUs (Xtra detergent, Arm & Hammer value tier) gain when consumers downtrade.

The deteriorating threads are specific. The FY2025 10-K newly emphasizes that some of CHD's largest customers launched private-label brands competing directly in stain fighters, diagnostic kits, and oral analgesics, a structural pressure on three meaningful categories. Waterpik is losing distribution and facing value-brand water-flosser competition plus tariff-driven cost pressure (the company ceased importing substantially all Waterpik product from China). Customer concentration is structurally high: top four customers at 44% of sales, Walmart alone ~23%, and most sales run on individual purchase orders rather than long-term contracts. The net is a still-strong but not widening moat, consistent with a 6.0 score and the gradual decline in this dimension across our prior reviews.

## Risk Assessment

**Waterpik impairment overhang (most acute).** The Waterpik trade name carries at $644.7M with fair value at only 117% of carrying value, down from 135% a year earlier. Management explicitly warns that further declines could trigger an impairment. Given CHD's demonstrated willingness to impair-then-divest (the 2024 VMS write-down followed by the 2025 divestiture; Flawless and Spinbrush exits), Waterpik is the leading candidate for a future write-down or sale. The charge would be non-cash, but it would dent reported EPS and signal a category misstep, a tangible near-term asymmetric downside not fully reflected at a premium multiple.

**Tariffs and input costs.** A 180bps gross-margin headwind in both FY2025 and Q1 2026, partially offset by productivity. Q1 2026 disclosures newly flag Middle East / Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption, which connects to the broader oil/freight-cost risk currently embedded in the macro backdrop.

**Customer concentration and private label.** Walmart at ~23% of sales creates single-counterparty risk, and the newly disclosed private-label launches by major customers pressure margins in affected categories.

**Leadership turnover.** The entire top of the house turned over within roughly a year: CFO Rick Dierker promoted to President & CEO and Lee McChesney installed as the new CFO. The insider promotion preserves CHD's cost-disciplined, M&A-driven culture, but it is an unproven configuration during a period of tougher comps.

**Thinner cash and intangible-heavy balance sheet.** Cash at $409M after $900M of buybacks and the Touchland earnout; $6.1B of intangibles means category deterioration converts quickly to non-cash charges.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface shows a near-term anomaly: 1-week ATM IV at 94.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 21.8%, with the term structure in steep backwardation. Treat the 94.3% near-dated reading with skepticism: it is a 1-day expiry on a thin 90-contract chain (put/call volume ratio 0.00), and it is inconsistent with the 1-month (23.3%), 3-month (24.5%), and 6-month (23.5%) tenors, which all sit in a normal range for a low-beta staple. The signal that carries information is the modestly elevated near-term put/call skew (4.9%) and the jump in ATM IV across the snapshot (24.2% → 69.4%), pointing to some hedging demand into the next catalyst window. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.2% of float (5.1 days to cover), consistent with a name that is neither a crowded short nor a high-conviction long. The options picture marginally reinforces caution but does not override the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $114, +18%)
Underlying organic volume momentum (+5.3% in Q1, outpacing P&G) is real and underappreciated by headline-fixated investors. As the ~8% exits drag laps out through H2 2026, reported organic growth re-accelerates toward 5%, the gross-margin recovery (46.4% in Q1, +140bps) extends, and consensus revises EPS upward. In a stagflation/consumer-cliff macro, defensive staples with volume-led growth attract rotation flows and hold premium multiples. The 2030 strategy targets (A&H $3B, international $2B, oral care $1.5B) and continued bolt-on M&A provide a multi-year compounding path. RBC's reiterated Outperform and $114 PT anchor this case.

### Bear Case (target $74, -24%)
A 25.5x forward multiple for ~2.6% adjusted EPS growth is unsustainable as comps toughen and beat magnitude compresses (8.8% → 1.0% over the last four quarters). Waterpik takes a VMS-style impairment, denting reported earnings and confidence. Private-label encroachment by Walmart-scale customers pressures margins in three categories. Tariff and freight costs persist. The multiple compresses toward the 19x peer median, which on $4.00 of EPS implies roughly $76, close to the $74 analyst low. The thin 1.25% yield offers little support during the de-rating.

The base case sits between: reported reacceleration materializes and supports the multiple, but the premium prevents meaningful re-rating, leaving the stock trading in a band around the $105 analyst median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)**

This is a hold for appreciation, not income; the 1.25% yield does not support an income thesis. The appreciation case rests on the H2 2026 reported-growth reacceleration converging toward the underlying ~5% volume rate, supporting the premium multiple and the sell-side's targets over the next 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back toward $80–85 (roughly 20.5–22x forward), compressing the premium to a level where the quality/defensiveness justifies the multiple and asymmetry turns favorable.
- Waterpik risk resolves cleanly — either a divestiture removes the overhang or the fair-value cushion stabilizes/expands in the next impairment test — alongside two consecutive quarters of reported organic growth above 4%.
- Estimate-revision velocity turns decisively positive (next-year EPS revisions accelerating, current-quarter revisions returning to positive).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Waterpik impairment of material size is announced, confirming the category misstep and a pattern of buying brands that subsequently deteriorate.
- Organic volume growth decelerates below 2% in H2 2026, undercutting the core reacceleration thesis.
- Gross-margin recovery reverses (tariff/freight costs overwhelm productivity) and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August), which will show whether the gross-margin recovery and volume momentum held and provide the next Waterpik fair-value commentary. The Q3 print is the cleaner test of whether the reported reacceleration is materializing as the exits drag laps out.

## Conclusion

Church & Dwight is a genuinely high-quality consumer staple: 17.6% FCF margin, OCF above net income, fixed-rate well-laddered debt, category leadership in defensible niches, and volume-led growth that is healthier than peers' price-led growth in a soft-pricing world. The headline numbers understate the business (organic volume +5.3%, masked by deliberate exits), and that gap closes mechanically across H2 2026, a real, dated catalyst supporting a hold for appreciation.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation discipline and a specific overhang. At ~25.5x forward for ~2.6% adjusted EPS growth, the multiple is ~34% above the peer median while yielding less than a third of the peer payout, and the Waterpik trade name (cushion compressed from 35% to 17%) sits as a telegraphed, VMS-shaped impairment risk. The score has drifted lower across our reviews (6.3 → 5.8 → 5.5) as growth, competitive trajectory, and risk each softened; the current data confirms that trend while the still-intact volume momentum and defensive profile prevent a downgrade to AVOID. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$80s or on a clean resolution of the Waterpik thread paired with confirmed reported reacceleration. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced around the analyst median near $105.
