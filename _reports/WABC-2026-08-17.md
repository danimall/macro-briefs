## Executive Summary

Westamerica Bancorporation is one of the most unusual depositories in the United States, and the unusual part is the absence of a loan book. At June 30, 2026, securities were 76.6% of the $5.81bn balance sheet and loans were 11.5%. The loans-to-deposits ratio was 14.2%, down from 15.7% a year earlier. What the company owns instead of loans is a $2.65bn portfolio of investment-grade corporate bonds, agency commercial MBS and municipals, funded by an exceptional deposit base: 45.2% noninterest-bearing, 98.6% transaction and savings accounts, time deposits of just 1.3%, zero FHLB or Federal Reserve advances, and no holding-company debt. Cost of funding all interest-earning assets was 0.24% in Q2 2026. Against a 4.01% earning-asset yield, that produced a 3.77% net interest margin and a 41.3% efficiency ratio without meaningful credit risk on the loan side; nonperforming loans were $808K, or 0.12% of loans.

The earnings engine peaked in 2023 and the only lever left is capital return. Diluted EPS ran $6.06 (2023), $5.20 (2024), $4.52 (2025). In 1H26 net income fell 8.9% year over year, yet EPS rose 0.4%, because diluted average shares fell 9.3%. In Q2, net income was down 5.8% ($27.385M vs $29.066M) while EPS rose 4.5% to $1.17. Management retired 1,754K shares for $92.5M in the first half and paid $22.5M of dividends: $115.1M of capital returned against $54.7M of net income, a payout of roughly 210%. This is a deliberate, finite return of accumulated excess capital rather than an operating recovery. Taking tangible common equity from 12.87% to a still-conservative 9% of tangible assets would release roughly $220M, about 16% of market capitalization, or two to three more years at the 1H26 pace.

Two forward drivers partly offset the decline. The CLO runoff that drove the 2024-2025 earnings compression is nearly spent: $208M remains at 5.61%, implying only ~$1.7M of residual pre-tax drag as it rolls into ~4.8% MBS. And $1.92bn of AFS corporate bonds yield just 2.74% against new money deployed at 4.70-4.82%, with $386M of expected portfolio principal cash flow over the next twelve months. That reinvestment roll-up is worth $0.14-0.19 of annual EPS. Combined with 6-9% annual share reduction, it supports flat-to-modestly-rising EPS. At 12.8x next-year consensus of $4.67, a 3.21% covered dividend and 1.61x book, that is priced. We rate WABC HOLD on income grounds, with base case fair value near $62 and a 7.1% gap to the single published sell-side target of $64.

## Company Overview

Westamerica Bancorporation (the SEC registrant; Westamerica Bank is the wholly owned California-chartered subsidiary) operates 76 branches across 20 counties in Northern and Central California, from Mendocino and Lake in the north to Kern in the south. Headcount at December 31, 2025 was 627 (608 FTEs), giving roughly $7.85M of deposits per FTE, a productivity figure that reflects both operational discipline and the near-absence of lending infrastructure. Loans per branch are approximately $8.8M. One reportable segment; the stated strategic focus is the banking needs of small businesses.

Growth history is inorganic and historical: bank acquisitions between the early 1990s and 2005 (including ValliCorp Holdings in 1997), plus two FDIC-assisted deals (County Bank 2009, Sonoma Valley Bank 2010). There has been no acquisition in roughly sixteen years, and no 8-K disclosing an acquisition, disposition or material agreement appears in the trailing ~2.5-year filing record. At under $10bn in assets the company retains exemptions from the Durbin interchange caps, direct CFPB examination, and the Volcker Rule.

Fee income was $19.9M in 1H26, 15.9% of revenue, and is not a diversification story: service charges on deposits flat at $6.71M, merchant processing +2.9% to $5.58M, trust fees +7.1% to $1.89M, and debit card fees down 14.1% to $2.79M. That debit decline is unexplained in the filings and worth monitoring, though the Durbin exemption argues against a regulatory cause.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin decomposition.** The Q2 2026 average-balance disclosure allows the NIM story to be reconstructed exactly. Total interest income (FTE) fell $1.756M year over year, and the components sum precisely: CLOs −$8.899M (average balance $792.9M at 6.19% down to $247.3M at 5.61%), interest-bearing cash −$4.272M ($653.9M at 4.40% down to $325.3M at 3.65%), loans −$0.872M, offset by agency MBS +$12.564M ($273.1M at 3.42% up to $1,237.2M at 4.82%) and corporates +$0.163M net.

The mechanism matters. The 2024-2025 earnings decline was not credit, and it was not deposit costs — cost of funds was flat at 0.24% sequentially. It was the runoff of floating-rate CLOs bought before the tightening cycle, replaced with fixed-rate agency CMBS at lower yields. With only $208M of CLOs left (24 issues, senior AAA/AA tranches), that drag is largely exhausted. Consistent with that, Q2 was the first sequential margin expansion in the data: NIM 3.77% vs 3.74%, earning-asset yield 4.01% vs 3.98%, EPS $1.17 vs $1.13.

**The embedded reinvestment tailwind.** At June 30, 2026 the securities portfolio carried $4,614.0M of amortized cost against $4,429.6M of fair value — gross unrealized/unrecognized losses of $185.2M against $0.8M of gains, roughly 4.0% below cost. The concentration of that mark is informative: $1,917.7M of AFS corporates yield 2.74% and carry $121.6M of the loss. Because those are genuinely low-coupon 2020-2021 vintage bonds rather than credit-impaired paper, the mark functions largely as deferred interest income. At full turnover into today's 4.7-4.8% reinvestment rates, the 206bp pickup on $1.92bn would be roughly $39.5M of annual pre-tax income against ~$211M of current annualized NII (FTE) — an upper-bound illustration, not a forecast. The realistic pace is set by the disclosed $386M of expected twelve-month principal cash flow, roughly 8% portfolio turnover, spread across all asset classes. At a 120-160bp average pickup on maturing paper, that is $4.6-6.2M pre-tax, $0.14-0.19 of after-tax EPS per year.

**Capital and the buyback.** Total risk-based capital was 23.16% at the holding company and 16.69% at the Bank, with a 10.27% Bank leverage ratio — mechanically inflated by the fact that three quarters of assets carry 0-20% risk weights, but real nonetheless. Equity fell $80.4M (8.6%) in six months, and the reconciliation is entirely deliberate: net income +$54.7M, dividends −$22.5M, share retirements −$92.5M, excise tax −$0.9M, option exercises +$6.3M, equity comp +$1.0M, AOCI −$26.5M.

The buyback arithmetic is sound. At $59.78 the earnings yield is 7.6-7.8% (on $4.52 trailing or $4.68 Q2-annualized). The alternative deployment, absent loan demand, is securities at a ~3.8% portfolio yield, or roughly 2.8% after tax. Retiring stock dominates. The board doubled the authorization on April 24, 2026, lifting remaining capacity to 2,785,023 shares, roughly 12% of the current count, and raised the dividend from $0.46 to $0.48 quarterly.

The cost is book-value dilution, and it is quantifiable. Book value per share fell from $37.91 to $37.09 in a half-year in which the company earned $2.30 and paid $0.94. Of the $0.82 decline, roughly $1.15 came from AOCI deterioration and roughly $1.03 from repurchasing above book. Investors buying WABC are consenting to shrink book value per share in exchange for higher EPS and ROE.

**Cash flow and earnings quality.** Operating cash flow was $55.3M in 1H26 against $54.7M of net income — close conversion, as expected at a bank with minimal non-cash items. Capital expenditure was $1.3M. Reserve releases were a trivial earnings contributor (−$300K provision in Q1, zero in Q2, roughly 0.4% of 1H pre-tax income). There are no aggressive-accounting signals in the filings; the earnings are what they appear to be.

**Expense discipline is eroding at the margin.** Noninterest expense rose 2.6% in 1H26 ($51.9M vs $50.7M) while revenue (FTE) fell 4.8% ($125.3M vs $131.6M). The efficiency ratio went from 38.5% to 41.5%, and from 31.7% in FY2023. Professional fees rose 43.7% and limited-partnership operating losses 21.3%. Most of the deterioration is denominator-driven, but a fixed base of 76 branches and 608 FTEs supporting a shrinking balance sheet is a structural issue with no visible management response.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth here, and the estimate revision data should not be mistaken for evidence of one.

Loans fell from $866.6M (12/31/23) to $820.3M (12/31/24) to $726.5M (12/31/25) to $668.8M (6/30/26) — down 10.6% year over year and 7.9% in six months. By average balance, Q2'26 vs Q2'25: commercial −7.1%, CRE −4.9%, consumer −30.1%. The consumer decline is concentrated in indirect auto (period-end consumer installment fell from $120.1M to $98.4M in six months), and the 10-Q attributes an increase in the consumer allowance to "an increase in estimated future credit losses primarily for indirect auto loans." Whether that is a credit-driven retreat or dealer-channel pricing discipline cannot be distinguished from the filings, and I would not infer management motive from a reserve movement.

Deposits require careful reading. Period-end total deposits were $4,772.8M at 6/30/26 versus $4,747.5M a year earlier — up 0.5%. But 1H26 average deposits were $4,809.0M against $4,899.9M, down 1.9%, and the free-funding component continues to erode: noninterest-bearing demand fell from 46.4% to 45.2% of average deposits. Balances have stabilized; mix has not.

Consensus estimates are rising — current year +4.3% and next year +8.8% over 90 days, to $4.63 and $4.67 — and the company has beaten four consecutive quarters by 2.1-4.6%. That is genuine and supports confidence in the forward numbers. But the coverage base is two analysts, the revision magnitudes are modest, and the underlying driver is share count, not revenue. This is a small consensus catching up to buyback mechanics, not an estimate-revision pattern that precedes re-rating. FY2027 consensus of $4.67 remains 23% below the 2023 peak of $6.06.

What can plausibly be modeled for FY2027: $4.63 base × (1 + ~6% share reduction) + $0.14-0.19 reinvestment pickup − asset-yield drift = roughly $4.75-5.00 if rates hold. WABC is asset-sensitive (+200bp dynamic = +1.5% NII) and reinvests at prevailing yields, so a higher-for-longer rate environment is favorable to that EPS path and unfavorable to book value through AOCI. Peers (TMP 8.1x, PFBC 9.7x, HFWA 14.2x, LKFN 14.2x, CFFN 13.7x) show wide dispersion, so the median is a soft anchor. On earnings, WABC trades at a 6% discount — hardly a mispricing for a bank whose net income is declining while several peers are seeing NIM expansion from falling deposit costs.

On book value the stock is not cheap. A residual-income framework at an 11.5% sustainable ROE, 2% growth and a 9.0-9.5% cost of equity implies justified P/B of 1.27-1.36x, against 1.61x actual. The gap is defensible on three grounds: near-zero credit losses (0.12% NPLs, 0.14% annualized net charge-offs), 23.16% total risk-based capital, and a mechanically rising ROE as the equity base shrinks — Q2 net income annualized against period-end tangible common equity is 15.0%. But it is a full price, not a margin of safety.

Two items sit outside the reported numbers. The securities mark of $185.2M is largely deferred coupon rather than impairment, and adding back the $117.6M after-tax AOCI deficit to tangible equity gives an amortized-cost tangible book value of $36.91 per share — within 0.5% of reported book value, because goodwill of $121.7M nearly offsets AOCI. So 1.61x holds on either measure. Separately, 211K Visa Class B-1 shares are carried at zero; at the 1.5445 conversion factor and Visa Class A's $343.09 June 30 close, that is roughly $111.8M gross, ~$4.86 per share pre-tax. Transfer restrictions persist until Visa's covered interchange litigation resolves, the conversion rate declines each time Visa funds its escrow, and timing is indeterminate. Treat it as option-like upside, not book value.

The single published target is $64 (KBW, raised from $60 around the July print), +7.1% from $59.78. Analyst targets do not lag reality here, because growth at the net-income line is negative and the stock is not cheap relative to growth.

**Base case $62** — 13.0x a $4.75 FY2027 estimate. Total return of roughly +7% including the dividend.
**Bull case $72** — rate plateau or a hike, reinvestment roll-up at the upper end, buyback sustained at 6-9% annual share reduction, EPS toward $5.10-5.25, multiple to ~14x. +20% price, +24% total return.
**Bear case $45** — Fed pivots and eases 200bp (−4.2% NII, roughly −$0.29 EPS), NIB deposit mix erodes further, one or two corporate holdings migrate to high yield, EPS toward $4.00 at an 11x multiple. −25% from spot, near the 52-week low of $44.93.

Probability-weighting 60/20/20 gives an expected total return of roughly +3-4% over twelve months. That is a HOLD, not an ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

The liability-side advantage is real and defended. Interest expense of $3.3M on $4.77bn of deposits, 0.48% of interest-bearing liabilities, 45.2% noninterest-bearing, 1.3% time deposits, zero brokered or wholesale funding — that combination is close to unique among $5-6bn banks, and it survived the 2023 regional-bank stress without incident. A 3.77% NIM while holding almost no loans, against West Bancorporation's 2.69% and PNC guiding to "greater than 3%," is the proof.

The asset side has been ceded. A $669M loan book across 76 branches in 20 California counties is not a lending franchise; the 10-Q discloses 107 corporate bond issuers each above $5M versus only 22 lending relationships each above $5M. The trajectory on both sides is what matters for scoring: loans −10.6%, noninterest-bearing mix −120bp, debit fee income −14.1%, efficiency ratio +300bp worse. The deposit moat is stable-to-slightly-eroding; the earning-asset franchise is contracting.

The cycle asymmetry deserves explicit statement. The regional-bank earnings recovery visible in July 2026 reporting comes from deposit costs falling. WABC has 0.48% of interest-bearing cost to give back — eliminating it entirely would add only ~$13.3M pre-tax annually — while its 4.01% asset yield has ample room to fall. Its relative earnings power is best in a rising or plateaued rate environment and worst in an easing cycle. The offset is the low-coupon reinvestment tailwind, which is largely independent of Fed policy and which most peers do not have to the same degree. In the current higher-for-longer setup, the asymmetry works in WABC's favor; if the Fed pivots to cuts in 2027, it inverts.

## Risk Assessment

**Corporate bond concentration (the largest genuine risk, and it is not in the loan book).** At June 30, 2026 corporate bonds totaled $2,651.5M at amortized cost — 43.6% of assets and 3.11x total shareholders' equity. Fifty-nine percent by fair value are financial-sector issuers (~$1.48bn, 1.74x equity), up from 57% at year-end. Twenty-nine percent are foreign-domiciled (Canada 8%, Japan 6%, UK 4%, France 3%, Switzerland 3%), of which 86% are financials. Thirty-six percent are rated BBB+ or below (BBB+ 24%, BBB 11%, BBB− 1%), roughly $905M, with a BBB− bucket of $38.3M appearing during 1H26 that was not present at year-end. Unrealized loss on corporates is $136.6M, 5.15% of amortized cost. The allowance for credit losses on HTM securities is $1 thousand against $154.5M of BBB+/BBB HTM holdings.

Near-zero CECL reserves on investment-grade corporates are standard practice, not aggressive accounting, and the book is granular across 107 issuers — a single default at the ~$24.8M average position with 60% severity costs about $15M pre-tax, roughly 13% of one quarter's pre-tax income. The concern is correlation, not the base case: a systemic stress in bank and insurer credit hits 59% of the portfolio simultaneously, and unlike a loan there is no covenant, no collateral, and no workout. This exposure carries no dedicated risk-factor headline in the FY2025 10-K, which is why we treat it as incompletely priced. A BBB-heavy book at 3.1x equity warrants a discount. [unverified — the claim that roughly $100bn of investment-grade paper is quoted near junk levels and that fallen-angel risk is being actively discussed is not supported by the provided sources.]

**Duration extension paid for in book value.** Cash fell from $567.8M to $301.9M while $600.2M of AFS securities were purchased in 1H26, and agency CMBS grew from $709M to ~$1.30bn. The −200bp dynamic NII sensitivity improved from roughly −6.6% to −4.2%. Simultaneously, gross unrealized losses widened $43.4M and AOCI deteriorated $26.5M, about $1.15 per share of book value. Less earnings volatility to Fed cuts, more mark-to-market volatility in equity. I cannot attribute the AOCI move to the new purchases specifically rather than to long-end yield moves or spread widening on existing holdings.

**Credit undercurrents, small in absolute terms.** Substandard-graded loans rose 41.5% to $15.6M (CRE substandard $8.1M → $11.0M) and collateral-dependent loans grew from ~$7.2M to ~$11.5M, while the allowance fell from $11.6M to $10.8M and ACL/loans declined from 1.84% to 1.61%. A $15.6M substandard bucket against a $10.8M allowance in a $669M book is directional, not a credit problem, and total NPLs of $808K are exceptional. Approximately 68% of loans are real-estate secured and ~29% of borrowers are in the Central Valley.

**Key-man and succession.** The 10-K names it: David L. Payne holds Chairman, President and CEO simultaneously and has led the company for over 30 years. No succession disclosure appears in the filings, and neither his age nor a plan is stated. For a company whose entire strategy is a set of unwritten "low-cost operating principles," this is a material unpriced governance gap.

**Finite buyback runway.** Roughly $220M of releasable capital taking TCE to 9%, about 16% of market capitalization, is two to three years at the 1H26 pace plus retained earnings. A secondary constraint runs through Bank-to-parent dividends (California law limits them to the lesser of retained earnings or the last three years' net income less distributions). Parent cash fell from $268M to $210M in the half, implying ~$57M of net upstreaming against ~$110M of annual Bank earnings — not binding today, but the mechanism to watch.

**Liquidity.** Estimated uninsured deposits were ~$2,368M, roughly 49.6% of the total. Immediately available liquidity of ~$1,123M ($301.9M cash, $761.3M Federal Reserve capacity on pledged collateral, $60M correspondent line) covers 47% of that, with nothing drawn. Mitigants: 98.6% transaction/savings from operating relationships, zero wholesale funding, and $1.92bn already pledged with ~$2.5bn unpledged (though sales would crystallize losses).

### Options Market Signal

The headline 172.6% near-term ATM IV should be discarded. It sits on a three-day expiry in a chain with 32 total contracts and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.04, an illiquidity artifact rather than a stress signal, and the "backwardation" label follows mechanically from it. The informative tenors are the one-month at 24.7%, three-month at 23.6% and six-month at 19.6%, against 20.1% trailing realized volatility. That is essentially no volatility premium and no directional information; the six-month skew of −0.7% is characterized by the data source as balanced. Short interest of 5.7% of float at 4.3 days to cover is unremarkable. The options market is pricing WABC as a low-beta, low-event-risk small-cap bank. Nothing here challenges or confirms the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($72, +20% price / +24% total return)

Rates plateau or drift higher, which is where WABC's structure works best: it is asset-sensitive (+1.5% dynamic NII at +200bp) and reinvests $386M of annual portfolio cash flow at 4.7-4.8% against maturing coupons far below that. Peers' deposit-cost relief fails to arrive, so WABC's 0.24% funding cost remains a relative advantage. The CLO drag finishes rolling off with only ~$1.7M of residual pre-tax impact, and Q2's sequential NIM expansion (3.74% → 3.77%) extends. Management continues retiring 6-9% of shares annually at a 7.6% earnings yield versus a 2.8% after-tax reinvestment alternative, and the doubled authorization (2.79M shares) plus ~$220M of releasable capital funds that for two to three years. FY2027 EPS reaches $5.10-5.25 and the multiple expands toward 14x as the market recognizes the reinvestment roll-up. Total shareholder yield remains extraordinary: 1H26 returned $115.1M, 8.4% of market capitalization in six months. The unrecognized Visa Class B-1 position (~$4.86 per share pre-tax) provides free optionality.

### Bear Case ($45, −25%)

The Fed pivots to easing in 2027. WABC has essentially no deposit cost to cut in response, so the −4.2% dynamic NII sensitivity (roughly −$0.29 EPS) bites while peers' margins widen. The earning-asset base keeps shrinking — loans down another 10%, average deposits down, noninterest-bearing mix through 44% — and the 76-branch, 608-FTE cost base pushes the efficiency ratio past 43%. The buyback exhausts its capital runway or the Bank-to-parent dividend constraint tightens, removing the only source of EPS growth and exposing an 8-10% net income decline directly. A credit event in the corporate book — a BBB financial issuer downgraded to high yield, or spread widening in a broader credit cascade — forces realized losses or a much larger AOCI hole against a $1 thousand HTM allowance. Book value per share continues declining as above-book repurchases and AOCI compound. EPS falls to $4.00 and the multiple compresses to 11x. The 52-week low of $44.93 is the reference point, and there is no strategic answer available: no M&A in sixteen years, no lending build, no announced succession plan for a 30-year CEO.

### Which dominates

Neither, at $59.78. The bull case requires the rate environment to cooperate and the buyback to run at an unsustainable pace; the bear case requires either a Fed pivot or a credit event, neither of which is imminent. The dividend is covered at a 41.5% payout and yields 3.21%, the balance sheet is among the safest in the industry, and the shares carry a modest discount to peer earnings multiples. That is a fair-value proposition with a real income component and no near-term catalyst in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income hold). Horizon: 12 months, reassessed at each quarterly print.**

- **Base case $62** — 13.0x an FY2027 EPS estimate of $4.75 (FY2026 consensus $4.63, plus ~6% share reduction and $0.14-0.19 of reinvestment pickup, less modest asset-yield drift). +3.7% price, **+6.9% total return** with the $1.92 dividend.
- **Bull case $72** — +20.4% price, +23.6% total return.
- **Bear case $45** — **−24.7%** from $59.78.
- **Probability-weighted expected total return: roughly +3-4%** at a 60/20/20 weighting. Margin of safety is inadequate for ACCUMULATE; asymmetry is not unfavorable enough for REDUCE.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price below $52-54** (≈11.2-11.6x FY2027 consensus, ≈1.40-1.46x book), which would bring the multiple to the ROE-implied justified range and restore a margin of safety.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of sequential net interest income growth in dollars** (not just NIM), evidencing that the reinvestment roll-up is outrunning earning-asset shrinkage. Q2's $52.674M was flat against Q1's $52.690M; the next two prints are the test.
3. **Loan balances stabilizing** (period-end loans flat or higher sequentially) or an announced strategic use of excess capital — an in-market acquisition, a lending build, or a special distribution — that addresses the structural shrinkage rather than managing around it.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **A credit event in the securities book**: any corporate holding downgraded below investment grade, an HTM allowance build above $2M, or realized securities losses. Given 3.11x-equity exposure with 36% rated BBB+ or lower and a $1 thousand HTM allowance, this is the single most consequential monitorable.
2. **Buyback pace falling below ~2% of shares per quarter**, or disclosure that Bank-to-parent dividend capacity is constraining repurchases. That removes the sole EPS growth driver and exposes the underlying 6-9% net income decline. Parent cash of $210M (from $268M) is the running indicator.
3. **A confirmed Fed easing path plus NIM compression below 3.60%**, or noninterest-bearing deposits falling below 43% of average deposits, either of which would validate the "no room to cut" asymmetry.
4. **An unplanned CEO departure without a disclosed successor.** With no succession plan on file and all three senior roles held by one individual, this is a discrete governance risk.

**Next reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected mid-October 2026. Priorities: sequential NII in dollars, cost of funds versus 0.24%, period-end loans, shares retired and average repurchase price, the substandard-loan and collateral-dependent disclosures, and any change in the corporate bond ratings distribution.

## Conclusion

Westamerica is a well-run, extraordinarily well-capitalized deposit franchise that has stopped being a bank in the lending sense and become a securities portfolio with a branch network attached. That structure delivered exceptional earnings during the 2022-2023 tightening cycle and has been giving them back since — net income down 14.3% in 2024, 16.2% in 2025, and 8.9% in 1H26. What holds EPS flat is the retirement of 6.6% of shares in six months at a payout equal to 210% of earnings, funded from accumulated excess capital with roughly two to three years of runway. The reinvestment tailwind embedded in a $1.92bn corporate book yielding 2.74% is genuine, quantifiable at $0.14-0.19 of annual EPS, and largely independent of Fed policy, but it arrives at 8% portfolio turnover per year, and it is mostly the recovery of value already marked down rather than new value creation.

At 12.8x next-year consensus versus a 13.7x peer median, 1.61x book on an 11.1% ROE, and a 3.21% dividend covered at a 41.5% payout, the shares are approximately fairly valued. The base case delivers roughly a 7% total return; the bear case, driven by a Fed pivot or a corporate credit event in a book equal to 3.11x equity with 36% rated BBB+ or lower, costs 25%. The strongest disconfirming evidence for a more constructive view is that the entire reported EPS improvement is a share-count artifact against declining net income, with no organic growth anywhere in the franchise and no strategic response to sixteen years of balance-sheet contraction. The strongest disconfirming evidence for a more negative view is the fortress balance sheet, 0.12% nonperforming loans, and a rate environment that currently favors WABC's asset-sensitive structure.

**HOLD.** Existing owners should keep the position for the covered yield and the shareholder-return arithmetic. New capital should wait for $52-54, or for two consecutive quarters of net interest income growing in dollars.
