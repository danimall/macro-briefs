## Executive Summary

Marten Transport is a 2,524-tractor refrigerated truckload carrier with no debt, $104.0M of cash and $766.0M of equity, emerging from what management describes as the longest freight recession on record. The operating evidence of a turn is real: Truckload revenue per tractor per week net of fuel surcharge rose 9.1% year over year in Q2 2026 to $4,592, non-revenue miles improved to 10.6% from 11.0%, insurance and claims expense fell to 5.3% of revenue from 6.3% in FY2025, and consensus EPS for FY2027 has been revised from $0.44 to $0.66 over 90 days. Management is backing the view with capital, guiding to roughly $109M of net capital expenditure for the remainder of 2026 against $127.3M of equipment purchase commitments after spending only $14.8M on revenue equipment in the first half.

The difficulty is the price. At $14.87 the shares trade at 22.5x the FY2027 consensus of $0.66 and 1.59x book value of $9.38. Working from Q2 2026 revenue per tractor, a boom-level revenue per tractor per week of roughly $5,600 (Truckload) and $4,300 (Dedicated) on the current 2,586-tractor average base, plus brokerage, produces about $858M of revenue net of fuel surcharge; at a 90% operating ratio and a 25% tax rate that is roughly $65M of net income, or $0.80 per share. The FY2022 peak of $1.34 is not reachable on this asset base, because average Truckload plus Dedicated tractors have fallen 23.2% from FY2023 and the $127M of committed capex is largely catch-up replacement of units aging past the company's 4.2-year replacement point rather than capacity addition. Investors are being asked to pay a mid-cycle multiple on mid-cycle earnings in year one of a recovery whose ceiling has been lowered by two years of fleet shrinkage.

Two accounting props also come out from under reported earnings in the second half. Gains on disposition of revenue equipment, reported inside operating income, were $12.067M in FY2025 — 52.7% of reported operating income — and have already fallen to $3.093M in H1 2026 from $6.847M in H1 2025 as the company stops selling trucks and starts buying them. Cash income taxes were $754K in H1 2026 versus $7.390M in H1 2025. Both flatter the current run rate. Consensus of $0.10 for the September quarter implies a 233% increase over the $0.03 earned in Q3 2025 against seasonally weaker refrigerated demand and diesel at $5.35/gallon versus $3.56. Marten has missed two of its last four quarters, including a 19.3% miss in June. We move from AVOID to REDUCE on the 11.7% lower entry price, not on an improved thesis.

## Company Overview

Marten hauls temperature-sensitive freight, founded in 1946 in Mondovi, Wisconsin and still controlled by the founding family. Roughly 59% of Truckload plus Dedicated revenue is temperature-controlled and 41% dry van; approximately 99% of revenue is generated inside the United States, so direct tariff and cross-border exposure is minimal. Average length of haul was 405 miles in FY2025, and Dedicated average miles per trip fell to 299 from 335 in FY2023 — the business has migrated toward regional work.

Following the September 30, 2025 sale of the Intermodal business to Hub Group for $51.8M in cash (no gain or loss recognized; $5.0M placed in escrow to secure Hub Group indemnity claims through December 2026), three segments remain. FY2025: Truckload $421.7M revenue at a 99.8% operating ratio and $0.8M of operating income; Dedicated $278.4M at 94.0% and $16.7M; Brokerage $149.8M at 95.2% and $7.2M. Segment operating income reconciles exactly to the consolidated $22.9M.

The fleet is 2,524 tractors at June 30, 2026 (down from 2,928 a year earlier) and 5,107 trailers, of which 2,863 are refrigerated. Only 77 tractors are independent-contractor units, so this is an owned-asset, company-driver model with full used-equipment residual risk — the 10-K discloses no manufacturer residual guarantees or repurchase agreements. Walmart is the largest customer. Management states Marten is the largest or second-largest temperature-sensitive carrier for eight of its top ten customers, nine of which have been significant customers for more than ten years.

## Financial Analysis

**The depth of the downcycle.** Revenue fell from $1,263.9M in FY2022 to $883.7M in FY2025 (-30.1%); net income fell from $110.4M to $17.4M (-84.2%); diluted EPS from FY2023's $0.86 to $0.21. The consolidated operating ratio widened from 92.0% (FY2023) to 97.4% (FY2025) and to 97.7% net of fuel surcharge in H1 2026.

**Equipment gains carry the reported result.** Gains on disposition of revenue equipment run through operating income as a contra-expense: $13.612M (FY2023), $4.971M (FY2024), $12.067M (FY2025). Stripping them out, operating income was $76.5M, $28.2M and $10.8M respectively — a 61.6% decline in FY2025 rather than the reported 31%. The H1 2026 10-Q splits the gains by segment (Truckload $1.822M, Dedicated $1.271M), which implies Truckload operating income ex-gains of **negative $0.349M** in H1 2026 against negative $2.265M in H1 2025. The segment generating roughly half of revenue is improving but still does not earn an operating profit from hauling freight. Dedicated ex-gains fell to $2.791M from $7.914M, down 64.7%.

The gains are recurring and legitimate — every truckload carrier cycles equipment, and Marten's conservative depreciation (five-year tractor lives, salvage cut from 25% to 20% for units placed in service from 2024, actual replacement at 4.2 years) means gains partly reflect understated book value rather than windfall. But they scale with trucks *sold*, and 2026 reverses direction.

**Cash flow: better than screens suggest in 2025, worse in 2026.** A gross-capex screen shows FY2025 free cash flow of negative $51.1M (OCF $93.5M less capex $144.6M). That ignores $101.7M of revenue-equipment disposal proceeds. Net revenue-equipment capex was $40.0M, total investing outflow $42.9M, so FY2025 generated **+$50.6M** after all investing activity. The mechanism was fleet harvesting: equipment additions fell from $227.8M (FY2024) to $141.6M while disposal proceeds hit a record, and gross revenue equipment on the balance sheet fell from $1,028.9M to $957.5M.

H1 2026 pushed this further — $14.8M of additions against $22.6M of proceeds produced a $9.1M investing *inflow*, and cash rose from $43.3M to $104.0M. The second half inverts it. Against ~$109M of guided net capex and ~$9.8M of dividends, an H2 operating cash flow equal to H1's $60.7M implies roughly a $58M cash draw and year-end cash near $46M. If pricing gains and the collapsed cash-tax line lift H2 OCF toward $75-80M, the draw narrows to roughly $40M. Either path is comfortably financeable with zero debt; both mean 2026 free cash flow is materially worse than 2025.

**The cash-tax swing.** FY2025 cash income taxes were $27.0M against book tax expense of $6.9M, with a $17.3M deferred benefit and the deferred tax liability falling from $107.0M to $89.7M — the accelerated-depreciation shield reversing as the company underinvested. H1 2026 cash taxes of $754K and a positive $4.641M deferred line are consistent with resumed buying restoring the shield, though estimated-payment timing cannot be separated from the 10-Q alone. Investors should not extrapolate H1 2026 cash conversion.

**Balance sheet.** June 30, 2026: cash $104.0M plus $5.0M restricted escrow, total assets $955.5M, total liabilities $189.5M, equity $766.0M, zero debt, zero interest paid in FY2024 and FY2025, current ratio 1.86. The Altman Z of 4.77 sits in the safe zone but decomposes revealingly — the equity-to-liabilities term contributes 4.214 while EBIT/assets contributes 0.024. Solvency comfort comes from the absence of leverage, not from earnings.

One structural quirk: the revolver was upsized from $30.0M to $35.0M on June 12, 2026, but $33.2M is consumed by standby letters of credit collateralizing the self-insurance program (up from $24.1M at year-end), leaving $1.8M of committed undrawn capacity. With $104M of cash this is a disclosure nuance rather than a liquidity risk, but the rising LC balance is the direct financial footprint of an escalating insurance retention, and a further retention increase in the June 2027 policy year would likely require another upsize or cash collateralization.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 dividends of $19.562M against net income of $17.444M is a 112% payout; retained earnings fell $2.118M, reconciling exactly. The same pattern held in H1 2026 (retained earnings -$3.074M). No shares have been repurchased since Q2 2022 despite $33.2M of remaining authorization and a stock that closed 2025 at $11.38, roughly 1.2x book.

**Trailing multiple discrepancy.** The quote feed shows a trailing P/E of 91.75, which reflects TTM EPS of roughly $0.16-0.17 (Q3'25 $0.03 + Q4'25 $0.05 + Q1'26 $0.02 + Q2'26 $0.07). On the FY2025 fiscal figure of $0.21, the multiple is 70.8x. Both are trough measures and neither is the right anchor.

## Growth Analysis

The top line is contracting while unit economics improve. Q2 2026 revenue fell to $223.5M from $229.9M, and revenue net of fuel surcharge fell 9.1% to $185.2M (H1: -9.3%). That decline is a capacity decision — average Truckload tractors were cut to 1,553 from 1,690 and Dedicated to 1,033 from 1,239, while revenue per tractor per week rose 9.1% and 2.9% respectively. Brokerage loads rose to 24,590 from 24,094.

The supply-side case is filing-supported rather than merely rhetorical. The FY2025 10-K states that stricter non-domiciled CDL standards, entry-level driver training rules, and enforcement of English Language Proficiency and B-1 visa regulations "are expected to decrease industry capacity," and discloses that approximately 202,000 drivers were removed from the industry through the Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse between January 2020 and December 2025. Marten's own random drug-testing rate of 76% versus the 50% requirement becomes a competitive asset if enforcement tightens.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Consensus revisions are large in percentage terms: current quarter $0.06 → $0.10 (+66.7% in 30 days), next quarter $0.07 → $0.12, FY2026 $0.22 → $0.30 (+36.4%), FY2027 $0.47 → $0.66 (+39.7% in 30 days, +49.4% in 90). The threshold of +30% over 90 days is met. Three qualifications limit how much weight this carries. First, the denominators are tiny — a $0.02 change on a $0.07 base is a 29% revision, so percentage velocity overstates the informational content. Second, the paired condition of accelerating revenue is absent: revenue net of fuel surcharge is down 9.3% year over year. Third, only three analysts contribute, two of three now rate the shares Hold, sentiment is classified as degrading, and Stephens reportedly cut its target to $16 from $18 after the July print. Marten has missed two of the last four quarters. We treat rising estimates as a moderate positive that confirms direction, not as a buy trigger.

The specific near-term bar is demanding. September-quarter consensus of $0.10 requires a 233% increase over Q3 2025's $0.03, against seasonally weaker refrigerated demand (Q3 2025 EPS was $0.03 versus Q2 2025's $0.09), diesel up 50% year over year with a surcharge recovery lag, declining equipment-disposal gains as the fleet-replacement cycle begins, and fixed-cost deleverage on a 13.8% smaller tractor count.

Longer term, growth requires either fleet re-expansion or Dedicated recovery. Neither is visible. Average Dedicated tractors have fallen from 1,632 (FY2023) to 1,033 (Q2 2026), a 36.7% contraction in supposedly multi-year contracted business, with the operating ratio deteriorating from 88.2% to 96.9%. The filings do not explain whether this reflects non-renewals, deliberate exits from unprofitable accounts, or shipper volume declines within intact contracts. Dedicated revenue per tractor per week rose to $3,917 from $3,807, which is consistent with disciplined pruning, but contracts already lost do not return with freight rates.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value at $14.87 |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2025 EPS $0.21 | 70.8x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $0.30 | 49.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $0.66 | 22.5x |
| Price / book ($9.38) | 1.59x |
| EV / FY2025 EBITDA ($128.3M) | 8.6x |
| EV / FY2025 EBITDA ex-gains ($116.2M) | 9.5x |
| Dividend yield ($0.24) | 1.61% |

Enterprise value is $1,110M ($1,214M market cap less $104.0M cash). Trailing multiples are meaningless at a trough, so the relevant question is what mid- and peak-cycle earnings are worth.

Peer P/E comparison is uninformative: HTLD -43.5x, WERN -48.3x and RXO -34.9x are loss-making, ARCB is at 191.8x and SNDR at 55.9x. The median of 191.8x tells us only that the entire group is at trough earnings. Two relative observations do carry information. MRTN sits 19.5% below its 52-week high versus HTLD -29%, RXO -29%, WERN -23% and ARCB -22%, and the stock is up 25.2% over twelve months while the group is broadly flat to lower. Marten has already re-rated on the recovery expectation more than its peers have.

**Mid-cycle.** FY2027 consensus of $0.66 is close to our own estimate of normalized earnings power. At the 17-20x that truckload carriers typically command on mid-cycle EPS, fair value is $11.20-$13.20. At 22-23x — a premium reflecting the debt-free balance sheet and refrigerated specialization — fair value is $14.50-$15.20. Book value provides a second anchor: 1.55x a projected year-end 2027 book of roughly $9.70 gives $15.04.

**Peak-cycle.** This is where the fleet shrinkage binds. Applying boom-level revenue per tractor per week of $5,600 (Truckload) and $4,300 (Dedicated) to the current 2,586-tractor average base gives $683M net of fuel surcharge, plus roughly $175M of brokerage revenue, for $858M. At a 90% operating ratio, that is $86M of operating income and roughly $65M of net income, or $0.80 per share. The FY2022 peak of $110.4M in net income was earned on approximately 3,300 tractors including Intermodal; on a 23% smaller asset base without Intermodal, the comparable peak is $0.80-$1.00. Trucking equities rarely command more than 15-18x peak EPS, which caps a cycle-peak valuation near $14-$18.

Combining these, base fair value sits at roughly $14.00-$15.00 against a $14.87 price. There is no margin of safety, and the upside case requires both peak earnings and a peak multiple to arrive simultaneously.

The analyst target range of $18.00-$19.00 (mean $18.50, three contributors) implies 21-28% upside. On a stock at 22.5x next-year earnings, targets can lead price. But those targets predate or only partly reflect the July print, one contributor moved to Hold, and the brief reports Stephens cutting to $16. With three analysts and degrading sentiment, we do not treat the published range as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantages are a debt-free balance sheet in a capital-intensive cyclical industry, refrigerated capability with multi-decade incumbency at large food and retail shippers, and a young fleet (2.3-year average tractor age at year-end 2025). Those attributes let Marten survive a four-year downturn without dilution or covenant stress, and survival matters when smaller carriers exit.

Evidence of pricing power is thin. A 99.8% Truckload operating ratio in FY2025 is prima facie evidence of a commodity service at trough. The 10-K's own list of principal competitive factors places freight rates second, behind service — an honest framing. At 2,524 tractors Marten is sub-scale against Knight-Swift and Werner, which have far more network density.

The trajectory reads worse than the level. The proxy's own five-year pay-versus-performance table shows Marten's cumulative TSR at $75.33 from a $100 base at year-end 2020, against a peer index (Covenant, Heartland, Knight-Swift, PAMT, Werner) at $144.83 — minus 24.7% against plus 44.8%. Marten did not merely absorb the industry cycle; it underperformed carriers absorbing the same cycle. Dedicated, the segment that should anchor a premium multiple, has lost roughly two-thirds of its operating income and more than a third of its tractors while its operating ratio deteriorated 870 basis points from FY2023 to H1 2026. The +9.1% Truckload revenue per tractor is a genuine positive datapoint on the other side of the ledger.

The May 2026 Supreme Court decision in *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II* — unanimously holding that state common-law negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers fall within the FAAAA safety exception and are not preempted — is presented by management as a tailwind, on the logic that brokers facing negligent-hiring exposure will steer freight toward compliant, well-insured asset carriers. That reasoning is sound for roughly 82% of Marten's revenue. It cuts the other way for the Brokerage segment, $149.8M of FY2025 revenue and 18% of H1 2026 revenue, where Marten becomes a member of the newly exposed defendant class and self-insures brokerage liability. A Dallas County jury returned a $604M verdict against C.H. Robinson in July 2026 arising from a 2021 crash; that outcome is subject to appeal and post-trial reduction, and federal courts have already held that cargo-theft claims remain preempted. Marten's July press release presents only the favorable side.

## Risk Assessment

**Self-insurance retention has risen five-fold in four policy years.** Auto liability self-insured retention per claim: $1.0M (policy years beginning June 2022 and 2023), $2.0M (June 2024), $3.0M (June 2025), **$5.0M (June 2026)** — plus a $5.0M annual aggregate for claims between $10M and $20M for the 2024-2026 policy years and, new for the June 2026 year, an additional $5.0M aggregate for claims between $5M and $10M. Auto liability and workers' compensation claims reserves were $35.9M at year-end 2025 and are Grant Thornton's designated Critical Audit Matter. Insurance and claims expense rose to 6.3% of revenue in FY2025 from 5.0% in FY2023 — on $883.7M of revenue, each point is $8.8M against ex-gain operating income of $10.8M. The H1 2026 improvement to 5.3% is attributable to favorable claims experience, which is inherently reversible, and it arrives exactly as first-dollar exposure triples. A single adverse accident year now flows through the P&L with far more leverage than in any prior cycle. This is the least-priced risk in the name.

**Customer concentration is worsening at the single-name level.** Walmart was 20% of revenue excluding fuel surcharges in FY2025 and 24% of trade receivables (roughly $20.6M), versus 20% and 21% in FY2024. The Business section highlights top-30 concentration falling from 78% (2010) to 71% (2025), which establishes that the revenue tail has broadened but says nothing about dependence on the largest account, which is flat on revenue share and worse on receivables share.

**Fuel.** DOE national average diesel rose to $5.35/gallon in Q2 2026 from $3.56, a 50% increase. Fuel and fuel taxes rose $13.2M (+40.4%) in the quarter to 20.5% of revenue from 14.2%; net fuel expense after surcharge recovery rose to 5.8% of Truckload/Dedicated revenue net of surcharge in H1 2026 from 4.9%. The 10-K sensitivity puts a 5% diesel increase at roughly $6.3M of additional expense. Surcharges recover most but not all of an increase, with a lag.

**Litigation.** Four active wage-and-hour class actions (*Martinez*, filed July 2020 in San Diego; *Wallace/Partridge/Rogers*, W.D. Wash., May 2024; *Jackson*, C.D. Cal., August 2024; *Courtney*, King County, filed January 16, 2026). Management deems resolution not likely to be materially adverse taking existing reserves into account. The California matters have been pending more than five years.

**Governance.** Randolph Marten (73) reassumed the combined Chairman and CEO role effective October 1, 2025 after Timothy Kohl's September 30, 2025 retirement, which included a discretionary $620,000 lump-sum Separation Agreement and Release of Claims and forfeiture of all outstanding performance awards — notable because the proxy states executives are at-will with no severance entitlement absent a change in control. The board averages roughly 72.7 years with tenures up to 35 years. Two of four Compensation Committee members have commercial relationships with the company (Bauer Built tires: $30K direct in 2025 plus $2.3M paid to manufacturers for tires supplied by BBI, which receives manufacturer commissions; Durand Builders, $0 in 2025). Dollar amounts are immaterial at roughly 0.26% of revenue; the structural overlap while setting CEO pay is the flag. All incentive compensation keys off a single metric, year-over-year change in net income, with no ROIC or operating-ratio gate — off a depressed $17.4M base, ordinary mean reversion produces maximum vesting without requiring an adequate return on approximately $780M of net PP&E.

Offsetting this, pay quantum is genuinely low: FY2025 CEO total compensation of $1,154,303, 69.4% base salary, no cash bonus for three consecutive years, a 7.5% salary cut effective September 2024, a CEO-to-median pay ratio of 15x against a 59x peer average, and say-on-pay support above 98%.

**Insider activity.** Across roughly 25 recent Form 4 filings there are no open-market purchases by any officer or director — only director and performance grants, tax withholding at $13.90 in February 2026, and gifts. Management publicly declares the recession over while no insider has bought stock. Randolph Marten already owns 21.7% (family holdings roughly 28.1%), which mutes the signal from his own non-purchase, and Form 4 windows constrain timing. It remains a mild negative tell.

**Unresolved items.** Item 1A risk-factor text was not retrieved, and the Q2 10-Q discloses that risk factors *were* modified in the unread Q1 2026 10-Q — plausibly related to *Montgomery* or the retention increase, but unverified. The cause of the 37% Dedicated contraction is unexplained in the filings. Reported GAAP EPS versus consensus for Q2 2026 is disputed between secondary sources (meet at $0.07 versus a $0.01 miss); the ~3.4% revenue miss is consistently reported.

### Options Market Signal

Options in MRTN are too thin to be informative. Put/call open interest moved from 0.00 to 0.04 between June 19 and August 12, which reflects negligible contract activity rather than positioning. The more useful positioning datapoint is short interest at 9.7% of float with 5.4 days to cover — a meaningful bearish position in a name up 25% over twelve months, consistent with a market divided between the cyclical-recovery view and a normalized-earnings view. It is not squeeze-level crowding, and it does not meaningfully change the risk/reward on either side.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($19.00, +27.8%).** Federal enforcement against non-domiciled CDLs, English Language Proficiency violations, ELD fraud and chameleon carriers permanently removes capacity; *Montgomery* pushes broker freight toward compliant asset carriers; refrigerated tightens faster than dry van. Truckload revenue per tractor per week continues advancing from $4,592 toward $5,400-5,600, the Truckload operating ratio moves from 99.8% toward 93%, and Dedicated stabilizes near 1,000 tractors at improving revenue per truck. FY2027 EPS reaches $0.80. At 24x, that supports $19.20. The debt-free balance sheet means all incremental margin accrues to equity, insurance expense stays near 5.3% of revenue, and the restored depreciation shield keeps cash taxes low through 2027.

**Bear case ($10.50, -29.4%).** The Q2 revenue miss and the 9.3% first-half decline in revenue net of fuel surcharge indicate the recovery is a pricing story on a shrinking book, not volume growth. Equipment-disposal gains fall toward zero in H2 as $109M of net capex lands, removing the prop that supplied 52.7% of FY2025 operating income and leaving reported earnings closer to the ex-gain reality of roughly $11M. Diesel at $5.35 continues to leak through the surcharge lag. One adverse accident year against a $5.0M per-claim retention, or an adverse brokerage verdict post-*Montgomery*, wipes out multiple years of segment income. FY2027 EPS lands at $0.30 rather than $0.66, and the shares revert toward 1.1x book, roughly $10.30.

**Base case ($14.75, -0.8%).** Pricing continues to improve but at a decelerating rate; FY2027 EPS reaches $0.62, slightly below the $0.66 consensus given a two-of-four miss record and the fuel drag. At 23x — a premium to the historical truckload mid-cycle range, justified by zero debt — that is $14.26; at 1.55x a projected $9.70 book, $15.04. Averaging the two gives $14.75. With a 1.61% dividend, total return is roughly flat over twelve to eighteen months.

Probability-weighting bear 30% / base 45% / bull 25% gives an expected price return of approximately -2%, or roughly -0.5% including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Horizon: 12-18 months.**

**Base case $14.75 (-0.8%) | Bull case $19.00 (+27.8%) | Bear case $10.50 (-29.4%)**

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing positions into strength rather than immediately. The natural window is ahead of the Q3 2026 report (late October), where consensus of $0.10 requires a 233% year-over-year increase against negative refrigerated seasonality, materially lower equipment-disposal gains, and diesel at $5.35. Investors seeking refrigerated cyclical exposure have better entry arithmetic in peers trading 22-29% below their 52-week highs. Reduce to a residual position or exit; the debt-free balance sheet argues against shorting.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) Truckload operating income positive *excluding* equipment-disposal gains for two consecutive quarters — the single cleanest evidence that half the revenue base can earn its keep. (2) Dedicated average tractors stabilizing at or above 1,050 with the operating ratio back below 93%, plus management disclosure of why the fleet shrank 37%. (3) Price toward $11.50-12.50 (roughly 18-19x the FY2027 consensus, 1.25x book), which would restore a margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals. (4) Clustered open-market insider purchases, or resumption of buybacks under the $33.2M authorization at or below book value.

**Triggers that would move this back to AVOID.** (1) An adverse auto-liability or brokerage verdict, or a claims-development charge, that pushes insurance and claims expense back above 6.3% of revenue. (2) Full-year 2026 net capex materially above the guided ~$109M for the second half without a corresponding advance in revenue per tractor, taking year-end cash below $40M. (3) Any disclosure indicating reduced Walmart volumes or contract renegotiation, given 20% of revenue ex-fuel-surcharge and 24% of receivables. (4) A dividend cut, which would confirm the 112% payout is unsustainable through the capex cycle.

**Reassessment date.** The Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which will show whether H2 operating cash flow supports the capex program, whether equipment gains have collapsed as expected, and whether pricing gains held sequentially. A second checkpoint is the June 1, 2027 insurance renewal, where a further retention increase would signal continued deterioration in the company's risk-transfer economics.

## Conclusion

Score trajectory has run 4.9 (May) → 4.8 (June) → 4.9 now, with the recommendation moving HOLD → AVOID → REDUCE. The upgrade from AVOID is entry-price arithmetic. The prior call was made at $16.85; at $14.87 the shares are 11.7% cheaper while FY2027 consensus rose from $0.47 to $0.66, so downside asymmetry has narrowed from unacceptable to merely unattractive. Nothing in the Q2 filing changed the thesis. Revenue net of fuel surcharge fell 9.3%, operating income fell 45% in the first half, Dedicated's operating ratio deteriorated to 96.9%, and the segment ex-gain analysis still shows Truckload losing money on freight.

Within the six dimensions, financial health (6.0) and valuation (4.0) pull in opposite directions and largely define the call. The balance sheet is genuinely strong — zero debt, $104M cash, $766M equity, no covenant or refinancing risk — but it is also capital-inefficient, generating a 2.3% return on equity while paying out 112% of earnings and declining to repurchase stock at 1.2x book. Valuation is the binding constraint: 22.5x FY2027 consensus and 1.59x book already embed a mid-cycle outcome, and the 23% reduction in the tractor base since FY2023 caps realistic cycle-peak EPS near $0.80-0.85 rather than the $1.34 earned in FY2022. Management quality (4.5) reflects a five-year TSR of -24.7% against a same-cycle peer index at +44.8%, a combined Chairman-CEO seat returned to a 73-year-old founder, a compensation plan keyed solely to net-income growth off a depressed base, and no insider open-market buying. Risk factors (4.0) is dominated by a self-insured retention that tripled to $5.0M per claim on June 1, 2026 against roughly $11M of ex-gain operating income.
