## Executive Summary

Laureate Education is now a simple business: five degree-granting institutions in two countries, Mexico and Peru, serving approximately 501,400 students as of June 30, 2026, funded almost entirely by private household payment with no material government loan programs in either market. After a decade of divestitures (Walden, Brazil, and the rest of a formerly global portfolio), the remaining asset generates a 25.3% GAAP operating margin, a 30.5% adjusted EBITDA margin, 23.9% ROE, and FY2025 free cash flow of $263.1M on $1,701.9M of revenue. Net debt at June 30, 2026 was $61.5M, roughly 0.10x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA. The capital allocation story is unambiguous: cumulative buyback authorizations have gone from $100M (September 2024) to $550M (July 2026), roughly $503M has actually been spent, and shares outstanding have fallen from 150.8M to 137.7M — an 8.6% reduction in eighteen months.

The problem is the entry price rather than the business. At $37.95 the stock trades at 18.3x the $2.07 midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and 15.6x the $2.43 FY2027 consensus, against the closest listed comparables (Adtalem at 18.8x, Grand Canyon at 18.5x) — in line, not discounted. The headline growth rate is also considerably better than the economics. First-half 2026 revenue rose 17% as reported but only 6% in constant currency, with $85.9M of the $128.2M increase (67%) coming from peso and sol translation. Adjusted EBITDA rose 13% reported but 3% constant currency. FY2026 guidance embeds the same wedge: +13% reported revenue versus +6-7% constant currency, +14-15% reported EBITDA versus +8-9%. The company's own disclosed sensitivity — a 10% adverse move in average FX rates would have cut FY2025 adjusted EBITDA by $57.3M, or 11% — quantifies how much of the current optics is borrowed from currency.

Underneath the FX, two things are working and one is worth interrogating. New enrollments grew 10% year-over-year in the first half (Mexico +7%, Peru +14%) against total enrollment growth of 6%, which is a genuine leading indicator given four-to-five year programs and 79% average annual retention. Peru, the higher-margin segment at roughly 40% adjusted EBITDA versus Mexico's 26%, grew constant-currency EBITDA 15% in the first half. Against that, discounts, waivers and scholarships rose as a share of gross revenue by roughly 200bps in Mexico (to 36.2%) and roughly 250bps in Peru (to 12.6%) in the first half, and bad debt expense rose 31.6% to $31.2M against 6% constant-currency revenue growth. Part of the enrollment growth is being bought with price. That is not disqualifying, but it does argue against paying a premium multiple for the enrollment acceleration. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Laureate operates two brands at two price points in each of its two markets, off a shared in-country cost base. In Mexico: UVM (132,200 students, premium/traditional, QS 4-star) and UNITEC (137,200, value/teaching, described as the largest private university in Mexico), across more than 30 campuses. In Peru: UPC (78,400, QS 5-star), UPN (129,900, value/teaching, third-largest private university in Peru) and CIBERTEC (20,000, technical/vocational), across 18 campuses per the Q2 10-Q — down from 20 stated in the FY2025 10-K, a change neither filing explains.

Three structural features matter more than the growth narrative:

**Private-pay removes U.S.-style regulatory tail risk.** With no material government-sponsored loan programs in either country, Laureate carries none of the Title IV, 90-10 or gainful-employment exposure that repeatedly destroyed listed U.S. for-profit operators. The offset is that enrollment is geared directly to household disposable income and to Laureate's own credit extension to students — which is where the rising bad debt line becomes relevant.

**Licensing is the real moat.** Roughly 75% of post-secondary enrollment sits in Medicine & Health Sciences, Engineering & IT, and Business & Management, and the company operates 24 medical schools and nine dental schools. Medical licensing is the hardest credential to obtain in both jurisdictions. A new entrant cannot replicate 24 medical school licenses on any relevant timeframe. This is a stronger defense against AI-native content competitors than the company's own risk-factor language on AI implies, because the product being sold is a licensed, employable credential rather than content delivery.

**Program duration creates revenue visibility.** Programs average four years with 79% five-year average annual retention, so roughly three-quarters of the enrolled base is in flight at any time. This makes the new-enrollment metric a leading indicator with a multi-year tail, and it is the main reason a 10% new-enrollment print deserves weight.

Laureate is a Delaware public benefit corporation. Directors have a statutory duty to balance shareholder pecuniary interests against those of students, employees and the stated public benefit, and the 10-K explicitly notes the board "may be influenced by the interests of our employees, students, teachers and others" in responding to an acquisition proposal. Combined with the Peruvian nonresident capital gains mechanics discussed below, this materially raises takeover friction. Do not underwrite a control-premium thesis.

The margin expansion is operational, not currency-driven. Of the $135.3M FY2025 revenue increase, $92.4M came from organic enrollment, $40.5M from mix and pricing, and only $2.5M net from FX — a $45.4M sol tailwind almost exactly offsetting a $42.9M peso headwind. Constant-currency incremental margins were roughly 44% in Mexico and 47% in Peru. That is the underlying earnings engine, and it is good.

**FY2026 to date presents a cleaner separation of signal and noise.** Q2 revenue of $615.9M rose 17% reported, 8% constant currency; adjusted EBITDA of $250.6M likewise +17%/+8%. For the first half, revenue of $888.5M rose 17% reported but 6% constant currency, and adjusted EBITDA rose 13% reported but only 3% constant currency. The segment split is where the operational question sits: Mexico's first-half constant-currency revenue rose only 3% and Mexico constant-currency adjusted EBITDA *fell* 11% ($12.5M), while Peru rose 8% and 15% respectively.

Management attributes the Mexico shortfall to roughly $12M of intra-year academic calendar timing (later semester start dates in 2026) plus higher D&A from campus expansion, and states the roughly $9M consolidated timing impact reverses in the second half. The explanation is internally coherent — Mexico's primary intake falls in Q3, so a start-date shift moves revenue across the Q2/Q3 boundary — and Q2-standalone Mexico constant-currency growth of +10% revenue and +9% EBITDA is consistent with it. But the reversal is a forecast, not a fact. The verified fact is an 11% constant-currency EBITDA decline in the segment that carries the large majority of the company's goodwill.

**Guidance (raised July 30, 2026):** enrollment 518,000-523,000 (+4-5%); revenue $1,920-1,930M (+13% reported, +6-7% cc); adjusted EBITDA $593-599M (+14-15% reported, +8-9% cc); adjusted EPS $2.04-2.10 (+19-22%) on ~139M diluted shares. FX assumptions are disclosed: actual rates through July, then MXN 17.55 and PEN 3.41 for August-December. The raise was modest — roughly two cents at the midpoint on adjusted EPS, from $2.00-2.08 — and management attributed it to both operations and more favorable FX.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Gross debt plus finance leases rose from $129.1M at year-end to $223.2M at June 30, and net debt from approximately $18M net cash to $61.5M. That is 0.10x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA — very low. Including the $511.9M present value of operating leases as debt-like brings the ratio to roughly 0.96x, though note that operating lease rent already sits inside adjusted EBITDA as expense, so a strict lease-adjusted comparison would require adding rent back; the 0.96x figure is directionally conservative rather than precise. Either way, solvency is not the question here.

Two movements deserve monitoring rather than alarm. Operating lease liabilities rose $124.1M in six months, of which approximately $134M of ROU assets and liabilities were recognized in Q1 2026 on the *extension* of existing Mexico campus leases — a non-cash recognition event that converts optionality into long-dated fixed obligation but involves no new spending. Separately, UPN borrowed PEN 205M (~$60M) at a fixed 6.45% maturing July 2033, pledging assets at four campus locations, with quarterly financial covenants effective September 30, 2026. This is the first campus-level asset pledge visible in the filings. The economics look sensible — fixed-rate local-currency funding for local-currency assets, a natural hedge for Peru capex — but it is a change in the character of the balance sheet.

The revolver went from $0 drawn to $75.0M, and the 10-Q is explicit that $67.4M of higher net revolver borrowings partly funded share repurchases. Crossing 25% utilization activated the ≤3.0x consolidated senior secured leverage covenant for the first time in the periods reviewed; the company reports compliance with ample headroom at 0.10x net leverage.

**Cash conversion has one persistent drag.** FY2025 cash taxes paid of $163.4M against pretax income of $400.9M is a ~40.8% cash rate versus a ~29.3% book rate — a $46.1M gap. The pattern continued in the first half, with cash taxes up $20.2M to $83.3M on higher estimated payments, tax audit payments and higher taxable income. Recorded income tax contingency liabilities were $74.4M at June 30. Any model applying the book rate to forecast free cash flow will overstate generation by roughly 11 points of pretax income.

Management provided no capex or FCF guidance. As an illustrative sensitivity rather than a forecast: applying FY2025's 70.6% OCF/adjusted EBITDA conversion to $596M of guided EBITDA gives ~$421M of operating cash flow; less $120-140M of capex (FY2025 was $103M and rising, with first-half capex of $35.5M versus $17.9M a year earlier) implies roughly $280-300M of FCF, or a 5.4-5.7% FCF yield at the current market capitalization. That is respectable but not a valuation-anchoring yield.

## Growth Analysis

The strongest data point in the file is the enrollment funnel. First-half new enrollments of 142,400 grew 10% (Mexico 70,200, +7%; Peru 72,200, +14%) against total enrollment of 501,400, up 6%. With four-to-five year programs and 79% retention, new-cohort growth running four points above total-base growth mechanically pulls forward revenue growth over the next several years. Guided FY2026 total enrollment of 518,000-523,000 is +4-5%.

The composition of that growth is where the picture gets more complicated. Discounts, waivers and scholarships as a percentage of gross revenue rose in both segments: Mexico first-half from 34.15% to 36.17% (+202bps) and Peru from 10.10% to 12.59% (+249bps). Simultaneously bad debt expense rose 31.6% to $31.2M against 6% constant-currency revenue growth, and the allowance for doubtful accounts stood at $128.3M against $287.8M of gross receivables.

There are innocuous readings. If list prices are rising faster than net prices, the discount ratio inflates while realized price still grows. Mix shifts toward the value brands (UNITEC, UPN) or toward online working-adult programs with different list conventions would move the ratio without any competitive price change. Mexico's statutory 5% scholarship minimum scales with registered students. And the allowance ratio is consistent with a policy of carrying receivables for two years before write-off. Laureate does not disclose net revenue per student, so the question cannot be resolved from the filings. But the honest reading is that a portion of the 10% new-enrollment growth was purchased with price and credit, and that the enrollment acceleration therefore does not by itself justify multiple expansion.

The stated growth levers are organic and capital-intensive: integrate in-country operations, add programs and "lift and shift" existing programs to campuses that don't offer them (with a health-sciences focus), broaden into working adults and lifelong learners, and add capacity at existing and new campuses. The capital-efficiency lever is online delivery — management targets 40-60% of credit hours online because more students can be accommodated in existing campus space. That lever is closer to a regulatory ceiling than the strategy narrative implies: Peru caps face-to-face programs at 30% virtual credits and hybrid at 70%, and Mexico requires a new REVOE for any program moved to a new campus, which is an administrative tax on precisely the lift-and-shift strategy.

Consensus is not extrapolating acceleration. Current-year EPS estimates have moved from $2.16 (90 days ago) to $2.12, and next year from $2.49 to $2.43. Every period in the revision table is negative over 90 days, and next-year estimates fell 2.1% in the last 30 days alone. This is the opposite of an inflection signal; it is modest, steady erosion. The most likely reconciliation with the guidance raise is that consensus is trimming out-year FX assumptions faster than it is adding operational upside.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.95:

- **18.3x** FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $2.07
- **17.9x** FY2026 consensus of $2.12
- **15.6x** FY2027 consensus of $2.43
- **8.9x** EV/adjusted EBITDA ($5,227M market cap + $61.5M net debt = $5,289M EV over $596M)
- **~5.4-5.7%** illustrative FY2026 FCF yield
- **0%** dividend yield; capital return is entirely via buyback

The peer table requires caution — the Duolingo entry (15.6x P/E, 72% below its 52-week high) is inconsistent with that company's actual profile, so the stated $15.6 median is not reliable. The usable comparables are Adtalem at 18.8x and Grand Canyon at 18.5x, both U.S.-domiciled operators with materially less FX and single-country political exposure. Stride at 11.6x and Graham Holdings at 9.6x are structurally different businesses. Against ATGE and LOPE, Laureate at 18.3x forward is priced roughly in line while carrying two-country emerging-market concentration and an FX exposure that its own filings size at 11% of EBITDA per 10% adverse currency move. That argues for a discount to those comparables, not parity.

The bull arithmetic is not unreasonable. Mid-to-high-single-digit constant-currency EBITDA growth plus a ~5-6% annual reduction in share count produces low-to-mid-teens per-share compounding without any multiple change, and at 18x that is a fair-to-slightly-generous multiple for such a profile. Consensus targets sit at a $41.21 mean and $41.00 median, with a $34.50 low and $45.00 high — an 8.6% mean upside. On a stock trading in line with comparables and with estimates being revised down, those targets are not a floor; they are approximately where a fairly valued name should sit.

Two anchors argue the price is full rather than cheap. First, the company repurchased 2,237K shares in Q2 at a $33.80 blended average (1,275K at $32.62 in May, 962K at $35.34 in June), roughly 11% below the current quote. The board's own revealed willingness to pay has been in the low $30s. Second, roughly two-thirds of reported first-half growth was translation. A buyer at 18.3x is paying a full multiple for a reported growth rate that the constant-currency disclosure shows to be about half as fast.

**Scenario framework (from the $37.95 basis):**

- **Base $42 (+10.7%)** — 17.3x FY2027 consensus $2.43. Assumes the Mexico first-half timing gap reverses in the second half as management guides, constant-currency EBITDA lands within the +8-9% guided band, MXN/PEN hold near the guidance assumptions of 17.55/3.41, and the buyback continues to retire ~5% of shares annually.
- **Bull $48 (+26.5%)** — ~19.5x FY2027 EPS revised toward $2.45-2.50. Requires Peru's 14% new-enrollment momentum and 15% constant-currency EBITDA growth to persist, Mexico's second-half recovery to more than reverse the timing drag, discount intensity to stabilize, and FX to hold or improve.
- **Bear $30 (-20.9%)** — a 10% adverse average FX move takes adjusted EBITDA down roughly $60M and adjusted EPS to ~$1.80 after tax, with the multiple compressing to ~16.5x on visible constant-currency deceleration. This scenario does not require operational failure, only currency mean reversion plus continued discount escalation.

Probability-weighting 45% base, 25% bull and 30% bear gives an expected return of roughly +5%, which is consistent with a fairly valued security rather than an asymmetric one.

## Competitive Landscape

Laureate describes its markets as highly fragmented with large numbers of local competitors, and it competes on price, quality, reputation, brand, location and facilities. The structural demand source is public-sector capacity rationing plus curriculum mismatch: public universities are cheap or free but capacity-limited and selective, and the 10-K notes that many non-elite public institutions are "less focused on practical programs aligned around career opportunities." Private providers are roughly 39% of Mexico's higher-education market (47% in the states where Laureate operates) and roughly 76% of Peru's.

The company concedes real disadvantages: public institutions receive government subsidies and can offer substantially lower tuition, and not-for-profits access grants and tax-deductible contributions unavailable to for-profits. Management states plainly, in both the 10-K and the Q2 10-Q, that it expects competition to increase as both markets develop.

The Peru margin advantage is structural rather than an execution gap, and understanding why matters for forecasting mix. Peru's first-half facilities cost was 3.8% of segment revenue versus Mexico's 11.7%; Peru runs roughly 14,000 students per campus versus Mexico's roughly 8,300; and Peru's discount burden is 12.6% of gross revenue versus Mexico's 36.2%. Labor is essentially identical as a share of revenue (37.1% versus 36.9%), so the gap is facilities density and pricing power, not staffing. Consolidated margin will therefore drift with the revenue mix, and reported mix has been tilting toward lower-margin Mexico (52% of FY2025 revenue, 54% of first-half 2026) largely on peso strength.

Two regulatory developments cut against the incumbency thesis. Peru's Congress removed SUNEDU's power to approve new careers, schools and faculties in July 2022, which lowers the barrier for rivals to launch programs — competitively negative for an established operator even as the August 2024 shift to permanent licenses de-risks Laureate's own franchise. In Mexico, the per-program, per-campus REVOE requirement is a friction on Laureate's own expansion. The offsetting moat is licensing depth in high-barrier disciplines: 24 medical schools and nine dental schools cannot be replicated quickly, and this is where the majority of the enrollment mix sits.

A distinctive vulnerability: a dedicated risk factor notes that adverse coverage of *unaffiliated* for-profit institutions could damage Laureate's reputation and reduce demand. Brand equity is partly hostage to third-party conduct.

## Risk Assessment

**Two-country concentration is the defining exposure.** Five institutions, two Latin American markets, and no remaining geographic diversification to absorb a shock. The lead risk factor enumerates regional economic pressures, political instability including student unrest, changes to laws and regulatory regimes including new tariffs and trade policy, and expropriation of assets by governments. Tuition is a discretionary household outlay in both markets; if Mexican or Peruvian real household income deteriorates, the private-pay model that insulates Laureate from U.S. regulatory risk becomes the transmission channel.

**FX is currently a tailwind of unusual size and can reverse.** The disclosed sensitivity is a 10% adverse average-rate move reducing FY2025 revenue by $170.2M, operating income by $49.7M and adjusted EBITDA by $57.3M — about 11% of EBITDA. First-half 2026 translation contributed $85.9M of revenue benefit. Separately, remeasurement of intercompany loan arrangements swung from a $75.7M loss in 2023 to a $50.7M gain in 2024 to a $34.6M loss in 2025, which is why FY2024 GAAP net income of $296.5M exceeded FY2025's $281.6M despite operating income rising 15%. GAAP net income is a poor year-over-year comparator here.

**Peruvian nonresident capital gains mechanics.** Holders selling Laureate shares may owe Peruvian tax at 30% on gains multiplied by the "Peru Ratio," once transferred value crosses a threshold of roughly $65M aggregated over any 12-month period; absent an advance tax basis certificate, basis is deemed zero. More consequentially for the company, Peruvian subsidiaries may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax where a transferor held more than a 10% direct or indirect interest during the preceding 12 months. This is a contingent corporate liability arising from a third party's tax behavior, and a real friction on large-holder entry and exit and on any strategic transaction. It has existed since before the IPO and may be adequately priced.

**Tax contingencies are being settled with cash.** Recorded income tax contingency liabilities were $74.4M at June 30; non-income-tax contingencies accrued were ~$13.0M with an additional ~$20.5M assessed as reasonably possible and unrecorded. A ~$7.9M Peruvian bank guarantee related to 2014-2015 audits was resolved by payment in Q2 2026; a ~$12.8M Mexican guarantee on a 2017 audit remains outstanding. Recent book effective rates were flattered by non-repeating discretes, including a $37.9M FY2024 benefit from entity restructuring.

**Goodwill concentration in the weaker segment.** Goodwill and other intangibles were ~$816M at June 30, roughly 34% of total assets and 72% of stockholders' equity, with Mexico carrying $569.6M of $648M of goodwill. The 10-K warns that a significant, sustained decline in the stock price could be an impairment indicator. An impairment would be non-cash and would not affect adjusted EBITDA, covenant EBITDA or cash taxes, but it would hit GAAP earnings and book equity.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** Peru's campus count fell from 20 (FY2025 10-K) to 18 (Q2 10-Q) with no explanation in either filing, no impairment recorded, and simultaneous land purchase and new-campus construction in Peru. Consolidation into higher-density facilities is the most plausible reading and fits Peru's margin performance, but reclassification or closure of underperforming sites cannot be ruled out. Separately, an Item 5.02 director/officer change was bundled into the May 27, 2026 8-K with annual-meeting results; the identity and reason are unverified.

**Physical and social disruption.** The 10-K cites the 2017 magnitude 7.1 Mexico earthquake, which suspended activity at several UVM and UNITEC campuses for an average of 12 days, and Peru's El Niño exposure. With fixed campus costs and revenue recognized only when classes are in session, multi-week closures are directly earnings-dilutive.

**Parent-level structural dependency.** The parent holds no significant assets beyond approximately $21M of corporate cash and the equity of its subsidiaries, and relies on subsidiary dividends, distributions and intercompany loan repayments to service USD obligations. Of $161.7M total cash at June 30, $154.9M sat at foreign subsidiaries under an indefinite-reinvestment assertion. This is a plausible reason the revolver was drawn to bridge buybacks rather than repatriating cash, and it is the mechanism generating the volatile intercompany FX line.

Short interest is 3.9% of float with 2.9 days to cover — no crowding and no squeeze dynamic, and no informed short constituency to corroborate a bear thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($48, +26.5%)

The per-share compounding math is genuinely attractive. Constant-currency adjusted EBITDA guided at +8-9%, incremental margins in the mid-40s at the segment level, and a buyback retiring roughly 5-6% of shares annually combine to produce low-to-mid-teens per-share earnings growth with no multiple expansion required. Guided adjusted EPS growth of +19-22% off +8-9% constant-currency EBITDA is precisely that arithmetic, amplified by FX.

New enrollments grew 10% against a 6% total base, and Peru — the 40%-margin, 12.6%-discount, 14,000-students-per-campus segment — grew new enrollments 14% and constant-currency EBITDA 15%. Because cohorts persist four to five years at 79% retention, that funnel supports revenue acceleration through 2028 or beyond. Leverage is 0.10x net, capex is being funded internally plus a fixed-rate 6.45% local-currency term loan, and the balance sheet has meaningful capacity to fund both campus expansion and continued repurchases. The regulatory environment improved in Peru with the August 2024 shift to permanent licenses, and the licensing depth in medicine and health sciences is a moat a challenger cannot replicate. Filings are clean: no restatements, no material weaknesses, controls effective, no impairments, and corporate overhead shrinking from $45.2M to $39.1M.

### Bear Case ($30, -20.9%)

Two-thirds of first-half reported revenue growth was currency translation, and the guidance embeds explicit FX assumptions (MXN 17.55, PEN 3.41) that a 10% adverse move would invalidate to the tune of roughly 11% of EBITDA. Strip FX and the business grew 6% on revenue and 3% on EBITDA in the first half, with Mexico's constant-currency EBITDA down 11%. Management's timing explanation for Mexico is plausible but unproven until Q3 and Q4 file.

The enrollment growth is partly purchased: discount intensity rose ~200bps in Mexico and ~250bps in Peru simultaneously, and bad debt expense grew 31.6% against 6% constant-currency revenue growth — credit losses expanding five times faster than underlying revenue. Cash taxes run roughly 11 points above the book rate, so headline earnings overstate distributable cash. Capex rose 43% in FY2025 and has roughly doubled year-over-year in the first half, D&A rose 35%, and the buyback is now partly revolver-funded while a Peru subsidiary has pledged four campuses as collateral. Estimates are falling in every period; JPMorgan downgraded to Neutral roughly a week after the beat and raise. At 18.3x forward the stock is priced at parity with better-diversified U.S. comparables, having risen 55% in twelve months to within 7% of its 52-week high, and the board itself has been buying at $33.80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a positive twelve-month path — Mexico's second-half timing reversal, continued buyback, and the multi-year enrollment tail — but base-case upside of roughly 11% against roughly 21% downside in a currency-reversion scenario is not an adequate margin of safety for new capital at this price.

**Targets from the $37.95 basis:**
- Base case **$42** (+10.7%) — 17.3x FY2027 consensus $2.43
- Bull case **$48** (+26.5%) — ~19.5x FY2027 EPS revised toward $2.45-2.50
- Bear case **$30** (-20.9%) — ~16.5x adjusted EPS of ~$1.80 after a 10% adverse FX move
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026)

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at $32-34**, near the company's own Q2 repurchase average of $33.80, which would take the multiple to roughly 13-14x FY2027 consensus and restore genuine asymmetry.
2. **Q3 2026 confirms the Mexico reversal**: Mexico constant-currency adjusted EBITDA back to positive year-over-year growth, recovering the ~$12M first-half timing gap, with consolidated constant-currency EBITDA growth inside or above the +8-9% guided band.
3. **Discount intensity stabilizes or reverses** — Mexico discounts as a share of gross revenue at or below the 36.2% first-half level and Peru at or below 12.6% for two consecutive quarters, with bad debt growth decelerating toward the constant-currency revenue growth rate. This would confirm the enrollment acceleration is not price-bought.
4. **Estimate revisions turn positive** on FY2027, reversing the current -2.5% ninety-day trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **Q3 2026 fails to recover the Mexico timing gap**, indicating the first-half constant-currency EBITDA decline was price/discount-driven rather than calendar-driven, or FY2026 constant-currency adjusted EBITDA guidance is trimmed below +8%.
2. **A 10%+ adverse move in average MXN or PEN rates versus the disclosed 17.55/3.41 assumptions** without a corresponding cut in reported guidance — which would signal the guide is being defended with operational assumptions rather than currency reality.
3. **Discount ratios rise another 150bps+ in either segment**, or bad debt expense growth exceeds 30% for a second consecutive half against single-digit constant-currency revenue growth.
4. **Buyback pace materially decelerates** while revolver utilization rises further, or net leverage moves above 1.0x excluding leases — which would remove the per-share growth leg that makes the current multiple defensible.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) are the binary. They resolve management's timing explanation, provide the first post-guidance-raise read on discount intensity, and show whether the enrollment funnel is converting to constant-currency revenue. The September 30, 2026 effective date for UPN's new term-loan covenants and the October 1 annual goodwill test are secondary items on the same calendar.

## Conclusion

Laureate is a better business than its share price is a bargain. Twenty-five percent operating margins, 24% ROE, 0.10x net leverage, roughly $280-300M of illustrative free cash flow, and a buyback that has retired 8.6% of shares in eighteen months at prices below today's quote constitute a legitimate compounding engine. The enrollment funnel — new enrollments +10% against a base growing 6%, with Peru at +14% — gives that engine multi-year visibility that few consumer-facing businesses can match.

What the current price does not offer is asymmetry.
