## Executive Summary

Western Digital is a fifteen-month-old company wearing a thirty-five-year-old name. The February 21, 2025 spin-off of the Flash business into Sandisk left a single-segment nearline hard disk drive supplier in which Cloud accounted for 89% of nine-month FY2026 revenue. That business is currently earning money at a rate it has never earned before: continuing-operations gross margin went 22.2% (FY2023) → 28.1% (FY2024) → 38.8% (FY2025) → 46.7% (9M FY2026), with Q3 FY2026 GAAP gross margin at 50.2% and a reported Q4 non-GAAP gross margin of 54.4%. FY2026 revenue lands near $12.9B versus $9.52B in FY2025. Nine-month operating income of $2,890M on $9,172M of revenue is audited and cash-backed by $2,540M of operating cash flow.

The mechanism is specific and matters more than the headline. In Q3 FY2026 exabytes sold rose 34% and average selling price **per exabyte** rose 9%. Per-exabyte pricing normally falls as capacity per drive grows; it is rising because the highest-capacity nodes are manufacturing-constrained, and because capital discipline has held — guided capex is 4–6% of revenue even at 50%+ gross margins, a fraction of the DRAM/NAND playbook. Customers are responding by extending contract duration, with long-term agreements reportedly under negotiation through calendar 2031.

Against that, three things happened between mid-June and early August that the price reflects. The stock fell 43.5% from its $799.87 high. The August 5 Q4 report beat on revenue ($3.75B, +44%) and non-GAAP EPS ($3.56 vs. $3.31) with above-expectation guidance, and shares fell 11–16% because the beat was carried by price while exabyte growth came in below expectations — the composition signature of a supply-constrained peak rather than a volume-led expansion. And external reporting places Seagate at 44TB HAMR in hyperscale volume since March 2026 against WDC HAMR production in 2027, a claim I cannot verify from any WDC filing. Consensus has nevertheless moved sharply higher: FY2027 EPS is $20.32 (+19.2% in 90 days, +14.3% in 30) and FY2028 is $31.83 (+29.5% / +25.0%). At 22.2x FY2027 the shares are not demanding; at 14.2x FY2028 they are only cheap if you accept net margins approaching 50%, which no HDD manufacturer has sustained. That gap between the near-term case and the terminal case is the entire debate, and it argues for staged accumulation rather than a full position.

## Company Overview

WDC develops, manufactures and sells HDDs, "primarily for the cloud and hyperscale data center markets." One reportable segment (HDD), CEO Irving Tan as chief operating decision maker, CFO Kris Sennesael.

End-market revenue ($M, continuing operations):

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud | 4,753 | 5,052 | 8,341 | 8,155 |
| Client | 691 | 577 | 556 | 501 |
| Consumer | 811 | 688 | 623 | 516 |
| **Total** | **6,255** | **6,317** | **9,520** | **9,172** |

Client and Consumer are ~11% of revenue and are shrinking in units (FY2025: Client −16%, Consumer −14%), returning to revenue growth in FY2026 only on price. The company is a hyperscale nearline supplier with two legacy tails.

The asset base is the underappreciated structural feature. At April 3, 2026, gross PP&E of $9,350M carried $6,928M of accumulated depreciation — 74% depreciated — with net PP&E of $2,422M and nine-month D&A of only $276M. WDC is monetizing a fully-scaled, largely written-down manufacturing platform that no new entrant would rationally rebuild. It also means reported returns on assets flatter the true replacement economics.

**Data reconciliation note.** SEC XBRL as-originally-reported annual revenue (FY2024 $13,003M, FY2023 $12,318M) is Flash-inclusive and not comparable to the current company; the continuing-operations restatement above is the correct basis. Separately, the supplied profile lists trailing EPS of $4.45 alongside a trailing P/E of 24.73, which implies EPS of $17.58 — internally inconsistent, and in either case the trailing GAAP figure is contaminated by a $4,448M non-cash Sandisk mark-to-market gain. The profile's "forward" P/E is identical to its trailing P/E and is therefore not a forward estimate. I use consensus EPS throughout.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin inflection, decomposed.** FY2025 revenue rose 51%, of which +29 points came from ASP/unit (mix toward higher-capacity drives) and +15 points from units; exabytes shipped went 443 → 696 (+57%). Of the 10.7-point gross margin gain, roughly 2.5 points was simply the non-recurrence of $155M of FY2024 unabsorbed manufacturing overhead; the remaining ~8.2 points was mix, cost reduction and utilization. In 9M FY2026 exabytes rose 26% and ASP per exabyte 5%, accelerating to +34% / +9% in Q3 — pricing improved within the year.

Operating expense is re-inflating with results. Q3 R&D rose $49M and SG&A $39M year over year, both primarily compensation "aligned with the improved financial performance"; quarterly stock comp went from $35M to $53M. That variable-comp gearing inflates the peak but should deflate a downturn. Nine-month business realignment charges of $103M ($95M cash severance) alongside record margins read as continued post-Separation right-sizing.

**Earnings quality — the Sandisk trap.** Reported 9M FY2026 net income of $6,229M includes a $4,448M non-cash unrealized gain on the retained Sandisk stake and a $(545)M charge for the debt-for-equity exchange. Backing both out: $6,658M pre-tax − $4,448M + $545M = **$2,755M of pre-tax income from operations plus interest**, which reconciles to $2,890M operating income less $110M net interest and $25M other expense. The tax line is distorted the same direction: 6% effective for nine months, driven in part by the Sandisk gain being tax-free, against **$636M of cash taxes actually paid**. FY2025's −45% rate included a one-time $690M deferred benefit from a Separation-related asset transfer. The 5–6% effective rate is not a forward modeling input.

**Cash flow.** 9M FY2026 operating cash flow $2,540M less $310M capex = **$2,230M free cash flow**, the first fully post-Separation read. Two working-capital items cut in opposite directions: receivables consumed $408M as the balance grew from $1,486M to $1,894M (a drag that recurs while scaling), while income taxes payable fell $598M as WDC drew down an accrued balance (largely non-recurring). A normalized FY2026 FCF of $2.8–3.2B is a defensible sensitivity, not a forecast; the FY2026 10-K was not available at cutoff. Cash conversion cycle improved from 97 days (FY2023) to 53 days (FY2025), with days-in-inventory 107 → 76.

**Balance sheet.** The deleveraging was executed almost entirely without spending operating cash:

| ($M) | Jun 27, 2025 | Apr 3, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 2,114 | 2,050 |
| Total debt (principal) | 4,749 | **1,600** |
| Sandisk stake (fair value) | 354 | 1,187 |
| Total liabilities | 8,462 | 5,365 |
| Shareholders' equity | 5,311 | 9,680 |

The sequence: June 2025, $800M of Term Loan A-3 retired for 21.3M Sandisk shares; February 2026, a $1.50B bridge loan used to cash-redeem all senior notes expressly to "consolidate the broad creditor base to two holders to facilitate a debt-for-equity exchange," then both the bridge and Term Loan A-3 extinguished in a non-cash, tax-free exchange for 5.8M Sandisk shares valued at $3.62B. The cost was $539M of "costs in connection with debt-for-equity exchange," primarily a discount on the Sandisk shares given Sandisk's price volatility — roughly 15% of the value surrendered to complete the trade. Two agencies granted investment-grade ratings in Q3, releasing revolver collateral and guarantees.

Remaining debt is $1,600M of 3.00% convertible notes due 2028. Against $2,050M cash, a $1,187M Sandisk stake and a fully undrawn $1.25B revolver, total contractual cash requirements are $1,876M. The Altman Z-Score of 2.18 circulating from screens is computed off the June 2025 balance sheet and is obsolete: liabilities-to-assets fell from 60% to 36%.

**Financial health: 7.5.** Net cash, investment grade, >$2B FCF, 4–6% capex intensity, and a nearly fully depreciated fleet. The offsets are economic rather than solvency-related: the converts sit deep in the money and are classified current, cash taxes are materially above the reported rate, and the tax rate is set to rise.

## Growth Analysis

Q4 FY2026 (externally reported, 10-K not yet filed): revenue $3.75B, +44.0%; non-GAAP gross margin 54.4% at or above the guided high end; non-GAAP EPS $3.56 versus $3.31 consensus and $1.70 a year earlier; next-quarter guidance described as solidly above expectations. FY2026 revenue therefore lands near $12.92B, +35.7% on FY2025. FY2026 non-GAAP EPS sums to **$10.19** ($1.78 + $2.13 + $2.72 + $3.56), against four consecutive beats averaging ~10% surprise.

Consensus revisions are the strongest quantitative element in the file:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2027 | $20.32 | $17.77 | $17.04 | +14.3% | +19.2% |
| FY2028 | $31.83 | $25.46 | $24.58 | +25.0% | +29.5% |

Revisions are accelerating (30-day moves larger than three-fifths of the 90-day move) and the out-year is being revised faster than the near year, which is how analysts express belief in cycle duration rather than a single strong quarter.

Two demand-side supports are credible. Capex held at 4–6% of revenue means the industry is not funding a share war, which is the mechanism that historically ended HDD and NAND upcycles. And the Q3 MD&A describes customers "partnering with us earlier to support their future growth requirements and... extending the duration of their commercial arrangements," corroborated by reporting that LTAs are under negotiation through calendar 2031, with demand broadening to neoclouds, frontier labs, sovereign deployments and enterprise. Broader AI-infrastructure evidence is consistent with these characterizations. [unverified — the specific claims that hyperscaler capex was "confirmed higher in the July prints" and that "memory tightness is now visible in device-assembler cost lines" are not traceable to the provided sources.]

What restrains the growth score at 7.5 rather than higher: WDC discloses no backlog, no remaining performance obligation and no deferred revenue figure attached to those LTAs, so there is no contracted-revenue number to anchor the 2028–2031 tail. And Q4's shortfall was on exabytes while price beat. Volume growth is the variable that distinguishes secular expansion from a supply squeeze, and it went the wrong way in the most recent quarter.

## Valuation Assessment

At the frozen basis of $451.52:

- Basic market cap (344.68M shares): **$155.6B**
- Diluted market cap (387M Q3 diluted WASO): **$174.8B**
- Enterprise value ≈ $174.8B + $1.6B debt − $2.05B cash − $1.19B Sandisk ≈ **$173.2B**
- FY2027 P/E: 451.52 / 20.32 = **22.2x**
- FY2028 P/E: 451.52 / 31.83 = **14.2x**
- FY2026 non-GAAP P/E: 451.52 / 10.19 = **44.3x**
- PEG on FY2027→FY2028 earnings growth (+56.6%): **0.39**
- EV / FY2026 revenue (~$12.9B): **~13.4x**

Peer trailing P/Es (DELL 31.3, STX 82.2, SNDK 63.3, HPE 43.7, NTAP 25.3; median 43.7) are not usable — Seagate and Sandisk trailing multiples reflect trough or transition earnings. The relevant comparison is that WDC, Seagate and Sandisk are all being valued on a cycle-extension assumption, and WDC's 22x forward sits at the lower end of that cohort.

The PEG of 0.39 looks compelling and I do not dismiss it, but the arithmetic behind FY2028 deserves to be shown because it is the crux. FY2028 EPS of $31.83 on roughly 387M diluted shares is **$12.3B of net income**. FY2027's $20.32 is ~$7.9B. To reach $12.3B, revenue would have to approach the mid-$20B range at net margins near 50%, or the low-$20B range at margins above 50%. WDC's best-ever quarter delivered a 35.7% GAAP operating margin. No HDD manufacturer has sustained net margins near 50%. So the FY2028 multiple is cheap conditional on an assumption that has no historical precedent, and I will not anchor a base case on it.

Anchoring instead on FY2027: consensus $20.32, with a four-quarter beat streak arguing for $21–22 actual. Applying 23–24x — a multiple the market has been willing to pay for confirmed AI-infrastructure earnings — gives $485–530. **Base case $500 (+10.7%).**

The most rigorous independent valuation in the file is Morningstar's $420 fair value (raised from $415 on August 6), 2-star, **no economic moat**, high uncertainty. That house raised 2027/2028 EPS by 25%/29% while *lowering* the multiple applied to 2030 earnings for peak-cycle risk, and embeds a 7% nearline pricing CAGR through 2031 plus a 51% five-year EPS CAGR. At $451.52 the shares trade **7.5% above** that fair value. Sell-side is far more constructive: mean $665.25, median $640, low $420, high $1,050, with 21 buy-equivalent ratings against 4 holds and 1 strong sell. The distribution itself tells you dispersion is extreme.

**Valuation: 6.0.** Genuinely reasonable against near-term estimates, with rising estimates and a beat record supporting them. Not higher, because the cheapness on the out-year rests on unprecedented margin assumptions, EV/sales of 13.4x is rich for commodity hardware on any historical basis, and cheap-looking multiples on peak cyclical earnings are the classic value trap in this industry — WDC posted operating losses of $(548)M and $(403)M as recently as FY2023 and FY2024.

## Competitive Landscape

Nearline HDD is a three-supplier structure (WDC, Seagate, Toshiba). WDC's filings name no competitor and disclose no share data.

**What is working.** Pricing power has flipped to the supplier despite extreme customer concentration — per-exabyte ASP up 9% year over year in Q3 is the proof, and it is the metric that matters because per-exabyte pricing has fallen for decades. The 10-Q explains the mechanism: AI and hybrid workloads drive "accelerated demand for high-capacity drives, resulting in greater manufacturing complexity and longer production lead times." Manufacturing difficulty at the top capacity nodes is functioning as supply discipline the industry could never impose on itself. Combined with a 74%-depreciated plant and 4–6% capex intensity, current returns are being harvested from sunk capital.

**What is at risk.** The 10-K names the HAMR (heat-assisted magnetic recording) areal-density transition as a technology risk it must execute, and discloses no production date. External reporting places Seagate at 44TB HAMR in hyperscale volume since March 2026 with WDC's HAMR production in 2027, and cites that gap as the reason for the post-earnings decline. **I treat this as unverified** — it appears in no WDC filing or company statement in the evidence available, and the corroborating observation is only that WDC's commentary was "slightly less bullish than at Seagate." If true, it matters because contracts through 2031 are being negotiated now, and being a generation behind at qualification time is expensive. The counter-argument has historical support: HDD technology transitions have repeatedly punished the first mover on yield and cost, and a 50.2% GAAP gross margin is not the margin of a company losing a cost war. The 10-K's own framing is that "our products are designed to be largely interchangeable with competitors' products" — thin differentiation by the company's own account.

Flash substitution is now a pure exposure with no offset. The 10-K states flash competes in legacy markets *and* that "flash-based solutions also target our larger addressable market, i.e., Cloud." Post-Separation there is no captive NAND business to catch displaced volume. The mirror-image benefit is real: WDC is now insulated from NAND price volatility, which is why it rose on days Sandisk and Micron fell.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Pricing trajectory is improving; technology trajectory is uncertain and possibly deteriorating; differentiation is thin by the company's own disclosure; and no third party assigns a moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is worsening while the narrative is diversification.** Top-10 customers: 55–56% (FY2023–24) → 68% (FY2025) → **74% (9M FY2026)**. Three customers were 16%, 15% and 13% of nine-month revenue — **44% combined**. Two customers were 28% and 12% of net accounts receivable at April 3, 2026 (40% combined). Geography broadened (Q3: Asia +75%, EMEA +44%, Americas +27%), but MD&A attributes that to existing "customers ramping up data center capacity in these regions" — the same buyers, different ship-to addresses. Broadening demand categories and geographies is not customer diversification, and the direct measure got worse. Practical consequence: one hyperscaler pausing digestion is a double-digit revenue event that hits receivables simultaneously.

**Peak-cycle exposure.** The filings document the downcycle precisely: $201M and $155M of unabsorbed manufacturing overhead charges in FY2023 and FY2024 on top of operating losses. At 54% non-GAAP gross margin the company is at a cyclical extreme relative to its own two-year-ago history.

**Tax normalization is mechanical, not speculative.** Unrecognized tax benefits grew from $569M to $603M plus $123M of accrued interest, with ~$133M potentially payable within twelve months. FY2026 brings the Global Minimum Tax to Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines — the three tax-holiday jurisdictions — with $62M already accrued. WDC does not expect the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax in FY2026 because three-year average adjusted financial statement income has not crossed $1.0B; with FY2025 and FY2026 income at this scale, that average will cross, and the 10-K warns of a "higher effective tax rate" and impact on realizability of $878M of deferred tax assets. Offsetting positives (immediate domestic R&D expensing from FY2026, enhanced FDDEI from FY2027) exist; the net direction is not determinable from the filings.

**Convertible notes.** $1,600M principal, conversion price $37.72, implying 42.4M underlying shares. Fair value was $12,507M against $1,581M carrying value at April 3, 2026. At $451.52, conversion value is 42.4M × $451.52 = $19.1B, less $1.6B principal = ~$17.5B of excess obligation; the capped calls (strike $37.72, cap $50.41) offset only ~$538M, about 3%. The economic cost is essentially locked in — conversion rights have been triggered every quarter since June 2025, and calling at par after November 15, 2026 would simply prompt conversion. The important point for valuation is that reported diluted share count already captures it (387M diluted vs. 342M basic in Q3), so diluted EPS is the honest number and any market cap computed on 345M shares understates the claim on the business by roughly 12%. WDC retains a cash/share election and made an irrevocable cash election on the $32M tendered in Q3; with >$2B FCF and $3.93B of buyback authorization, cash settlement is affordable. The mechanism differs; the cost to existing holders is similar either way.

**Unaccrued litigation tail.** SPEX Technologies won a $316M verdict in October 2024, with judgment plus $237M prejudgment interest entered in January 2025, then reduced in June 2025 to **nominal damages of $1** for insufficient damages evidence. Both sides have appealed and WDC carries **no accrual**. An appellate reinstatement of a >$550M award is a binary the screens miss entirely.

**Verification gaps that bear on risk.** Three 8-Ks could not be read (EDGAR 403s): 2026-06-03 (Items 3.02 unregistered equity sales + 8.01), 2026-06-08 (8-K/A, Item 3.02), and 2026-06-11 (Item 8.01). Item 3.02 covers issuance by WDC of unregistered equity, which with deep-in-the-money converts outstanding could be an unsized dilution event. Also unread: 2026-05-28 (Items 5.02 + 7.01), one of **ten Item 5.02 officer/director events in roughly thirty months** — a turnover rate the 10-K risk factors themselves acknowledge as a continuity risk. FY2026 full-year GAAP figures, year-end cash and debt, and year-end concentration are externally reported only.

**Concentrated, thinly insured footprint.** The 10-K discloses "limited insurance coverage and, in some cases, no coverage at all, for natural disasters and damage to our facilities," with production concentrated in Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines.

**Risk factors: 4.0.** Held from the prior review. The concentration, peak-cycle, tax, HAMR and unread-8-K items are each material and several are not visible in trailing financials.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 117.3% sits 39.6 points above one-year realized volatility of 77.7%, at the 92nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (117.3% near vs. 80.4% at six months). The headline put/call skew of 239.1% is concentrated in the one-day August 7 expiry, where skew reads 58.2% and the implied move is ±1.0% — same-day pinning mechanics. The 13-day and 41-day skews are effectively flat (+0.8%, −0.5%), so the market is pricing *magnitude*, not direction: ±12.1% over one month, ±20.5% over three, ±35.0% over six.

The more informative change is positioning. Between July 27 and August 7, ATM IV fell 21.2 points while the price fell 16.4% and the put/call open-interest ratio **doubled from 1.09 to 2.19**. Protection was accumulated into the decline rather than sold. The six-month tenor carries a 3.59 put/call volume ratio. Short interest is a modest 7.6% of float at 2.2 days to cover, so the bearish view is not crowded in the borrow.

Read together: the options market is not signaling a directional bear case, but it is pricing a materially wider distribution than realized volatility supports, and it is doing so through owned puts. That is consistent with the fundamental picture — high dispersion, genuine two-sidedness — and it argues against paying up for a full position in one clip.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $680 (+50.6%)

Nearline supply stays constrained by manufacturing complexity at the top capacity nodes rather than by nameplate capacity, so the 4–6% capex intensity that keeps returns high also keeps supply short. LTAs through calendar 2031 convert from negotiation to signed volume, demand broadens to neoclouds, sovereign deployments and enterprise, and exabyte growth reaccelerates alongside continued per-exabyte price gains. FY2027 EPS beats $20.32 (the pattern of four consecutive beats averaging ~10% supports $21–22), FY2028 approaches the $31.83 consensus, and the market applies ~21x to that figure. Net cash, investment-grade ratings and $3.93B of buyback authorization allow aggressive share retirement — the nine-month average repurchase price of ~$147 shows management will buy when the multiple permits. $680 approximates the current sell-side mean of $665.

### Bear Case — $255 (−43.5%)

Q4's exabyte shortfall is the leading edge, not noise: volume growth decelerates while pricing plateaus, and the 2028–2031 tail that the multiple depends on compresses. If the HAMR gap is real, WDC loses qualification share at 40TB+ precisely as long-term contracts are set, and the reported 44TB Seagate advantage becomes a $/TB advantage. Meanwhile the effective tax rate normalizes from 6% toward the low-to-mid 20s as GMT applies in all three holiday jurisdictions and CAMT engages, cutting EPS on unchanged pre-tax income. One hyperscaler pausing capex digestion removes a double-digit share of revenue and 12–28% of receivables at once. FY2028 EPS reverts toward $16 and the market pays 16x on peak-cycle skepticism. FY2023–24 demonstrated that this business can post outright operating losses within two years of a peak.

### What decides it

Exabyte growth versus price growth in the FY2027 quarters. Price-led growth is a squeeze that mean-reverts; volume-led growth is a secular expansion. Q3 FY2026 was +34% volume / +9% price. Q4 reportedly reversed that mix. Two more quarters of that reversal, and the base case moves toward the bear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected risk-adjusted return, insufficient margin of safety and confidence for a full position.

- **Base case: $500 (+10.7%)** — 24.6x FY2027 consensus $20.32, or 15.7x FY2028 consensus $31.83. Anchored on FY2027 rather than FY2028 because the FY2028 estimate implies ~$12.3B of net income and net margins near 50%, without precedent in HDD.
- **Bull case: $680 (+50.6%)** — ~21.4x FY2028 $31.83, roughly the sell-side mean of $665.
- **Bear case: $255 (−43.5%)** — FY2028 EPS reverting to ~$16 at 16x, reflecting both estimate cuts and multiple compression.
- **Probability-weighted return:** 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear ≈ **+9%** over 12 months, with a peak-to-trough dispersion of ~94 points. The positive expectation is real; the dispersion is why this is not a full position.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, resolving across the four FY2027 quarterly reports (first due late October 2026).

**Staged entry plan.** Take one-third of a target position at the current basis. Add the second third at **$390–410**, where base case upside widens to 22–28% against 34–38% bear downside. Reserve the final third for either a confirmed exabyte reacceleration or a decline below $380.

**Note on price basis:** the frozen valuation snapshot carries a market-date mismatch flag; the authoritative close is $451.52, while the last observed trade was $434.80 (−3.7%). At $434.80 base-case upside is 15.0% rather than 10.7%. This does not change the rating.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
1. Exabyte growth reaccelerates to 25%+ year over year for two consecutive quarters with per-exabyte ASP flat or better — resolving the volume-versus-price composition question, or
2. WDC discloses a dollar-quantified backlog, RPO or contracted volume figure tied to the 2031 LTAs, converting the cycle-duration argument from commentary into disclosure, or
3. The price falls below ~$380 with FY2027 consensus intact at $20+, giving >30% base-case upside.

1. FY2027 consensus stops rising and the 30-day revision turns negative, or
2. Per-exabyte ASP growth turns negative in any quarter, or
3. WDC confirms HAMR production later than calendar 2027, or discloses a customer qualification loss at 40TB+.

**Downgrade to REDUCE/AVOID if:**
1. Any of the three ≥10% customers is disclosed below 10% in a full-year filing without an offsetting new customer, or
2. The effective tax rate guides above 22% without an offsetting pre-tax revision, or
3. The SPEX appellate court reinstates a material damages award (>$300M unaccrued), or
4. The FY2026 10-K or the unread 8-Ks disclose share issuance materially above the 45M already embedded in diluted share count.

**Reassessment date:** the FY2026 10-K filing (expected August 2026, which will resolve full-year GAAP figures, year-end concentration and the unread 8-K items), then the Q1 FY2027 report in late October 2026.

## Conclusion

WDC's score moves to 6.2 from 6.3, and the rating from BUY to ACCUMULATE. Financial health stays at 7.5 — the balance sheet transformation from $4.75B of debt to net cash, executed almost entirely by monetizing the Sandisk stake on a tax-free basis, is the single best piece of capital-allocation execution in the file, even allowing for the $539M discount surrendered to complete it. Growth stays strong at 7.5, supported by 44% revenue growth, four consecutive beats and consensus revisions of +19.2% and +29.5% over 90 days on the two forward years. Risk stays at 4.0: three customers at 44% of revenue and 40% of receivables, an unaccrued SPEX appellate tail, a tax rate about to move mechanically higher, and three unread 8-Ks including two covering unregistered equity issuance.

The downgrade from BUY is not driven by the price decline since the last review — a lower price on rising estimates improves risk/reward, and it does here. It is driven by one new fundamental fact and one unresolved one. The fact: the Q4 beat was carried by price while exabyte volume came in below expectations, which is the composition signature of a supply squeeze rather than a volume expansion, and volume composition is precisely what determines whether the 2028–2031 earnings tail materialises. The unresolved item: a reported HAMR generation gap against Seagate that WDC's filings neither confirm nor deny, at the moment contracts through 2031 are being negotiated.

At $451.52 the shares are 22x FY2027 and 7.5% above the most rigorous independent fair value estimate available, against a sell-side mean of $665.
