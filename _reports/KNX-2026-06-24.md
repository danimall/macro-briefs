**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Knight-Swift is North America's largest truckload carrier, covering 1.8 billion loaded miles in 2025 across a four-segment portfolio anchored by Truckload (65% of revenue) and a fast-growing LTL franchise (20%) built through acquisition since 2021. The business sits at the trough of a severe freight recession that has compressed consolidated operating income roughly 80% from the 2022 peak, to $216M on a 97.1% operating ratio. GAAP diluted EPS of $0.41 in 2025 is a multi-year low, and the trailing P/E of ~353x is meaningless at trough earnings.

The investment debate is narrow and well-defined. The question is not whether the business is good (it is the scale leader with a sensible diversification strategy), but whether the cyclical recovery management is promising is already in the price. The evidence says it largely is. The stock has appreciated 68% over the past twelve months, from ~$44 to ~$74, while reported earnings deteriorated. Forward estimate revisions are constructive: next-year consensus EPS rose +17.8% over 90 days to $3.48, which signals the market and analysts now expect a genuine recovery. At $74, the stock trades at ~21x that already-elevated forward number, a multiple that prices in the rebound rather than discounting it. Citigroup's mid-June downgrade to Neutral, and the ~10% drawdown from the June high near $82, reflect the same conclusion: the recovery is priced.

We rate KNX a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case (operating leverage at a 97% OR runs hard in both directions if rates inflect) and bear case (the Q1 2026 miss showed how thin-margin quarters get derailed, and the stock already discounts the upturn) are roughly balanced from current levels. We would want to see realized rate increases from bid season translate into the Q2 guided EPS jump ($0.09 actual Q1 → $0.45-0.49 guided Q2) before underwriting further upside, or a pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s to restore asymmetry.

## Company Overview

Knight-Swift was formed via the 2017 Knight/Swift merger and operates through four reportable segments:

- **Truckload ($4.87B, 65%):** ~21,400 tractors and ~85,000 trailers across irregular-route, dedicated, refrigerated, expedited, flatbed, and cross-border Mexico service. Brands include Swift, Knight, and U.S. Xpress (acquired 2023). Fleet average age is 2.7 years.
- **LTL ($1.48B, 20%):** The strategic growth engine, built via the AAA Cooper (ACT), MME, and DHE (July 2024) acquisitions. ~180 service centers, ~6,690 doors, ~4,200 tractors. Effective January 1, 2026, all LTL brands consolidated under the AAA Cooper name.
- **Logistics ($570M, 8%):** Asset-light brokerage plus power-only, leveraging the large trailer fleet.
- **Intermodal ($365M, 5%):** ~12,500 containers, drayage plus rail. Currently loss-making at a 102% operating ratio.

The competitive moat is scale, lane density, a modern fleet, a nationwide terminal network, and breadth of service. The truckload industry is highly fragmented — KNX competes against thousands of smaller carriers — and management frames its leading one-way truckload presence as increasingly valuable to shippers as capacity exits the market.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings collapse defines the financial picture. The deterioration from peak is steep:

| Metric | 2022 (peak) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7.43B | $7.14B | $7.41B | $7.47B |
| Operating income | $1,092M | $338M | $243M | $216M |
| Net income | $771M | $217M | $118M | $66M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $0.73 | $0.41 |
| Operating ratio | ~85% | — | 96.7% | 97.1% |

Revenue has been flat to slightly up across the down-cycle, while operating income fell ~80%. The 2.9% GAAP operating margin understates underlying profitability: excluding $98M of 2025 impairments and $77M of intangible amortization, the consolidated adjusted operating ratio was 94.1% (vs 94.7% in 2024). Even normalized, profitability is depressed.

Segment dynamics show a divided picture. Truckload revenue per tractor rose 3.3% and adjusted OR improved 80bps to 94.8%, a tentative early-cycle positive. LTL, the growth thesis, deteriorated: adjusted operating income fell 17% and adjusted OR worsened 310bps to 93.2% as aggressive service-center buildout and DHE integration costs ran ahead of the 20.6% revenue growth. Intermodal remains unprofitable.

A critical caveat on cash flow: reported 2025 operating cash flow of $1.27B and FCF of $763M are flattered by a one-time accounting change. Roughly $478M of the OCF gain came from converting the AR securitization facility to a true-sale structure, moving receivables off-balance-sheet. Underlying FCF is closer to ~$471M. Analysts anchoring to the $763M headline overstate cash generation.

The balance sheet carries ~$2.4B of debt plus finance leases, net debt ~$2.1B, and D/E ~0.69, appropriate for an asset-heavy carrier. The sub-1.0 current ratio (0.86) and $144M working-capital deficit are partly structural, a consequence of selling receivables off-balance-sheet rather than distress; liquidity is ~$1.1B and covenants are met. The Altman Z of 2.37 (grey zone) reflects cyclically crushed EBIT (EBIT/assets just 1.8%), not a solvency problem. Goodwill ($3.9B) plus intangibles ($1.9B) total ~$5.9B, roughly 83% of equity, an acquisition-heavy balance sheet with ongoing impairment exposure ($98M of impairments in 2025 vs $19M in 2024).

The earnings surprise record is a concern for forward visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including a severe Q1 2026 miss ($0.09 actual vs $0.20 estimate, -56%). Management attributes the Q1 shortfall to largely one-time items: an unfavorable LTL arbitration award on a 2022 incident (~$0.08), deferred warehousing business (~$0.05), an adverse Mexico VAT decision ($0.02), and winter weather plus a March fuel spike ($0.05-0.06). The recurrence of one-off claims and weather items at a thin 97% OR demonstrates how easily a quarter gets derailed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests entirely on the cyclical recovery. The freight industry has endured an unusually long down-cycle (2023-2025) driven by post-COVID goods-demand normalization, inventory destocking, and capacity oversupply. The recovery thesis hinges on capacity exit finally outpacing demand. Management argues the Q1 2026 winter-weather and fuel shocks accelerated small-carrier failures, tightening the spot market and prompting a "rapidly evolving" bid environment. CEO Miller states he is "more optimistic about the earnings opportunity than three months ago."

Estimate revisions support a recovery read. Next-quarter consensus rose +15.0% over 90 days, and next-year EPS rose +17.8% to $3.48, an acceleration that signals the market is systematically marking up KNX's forward earnings power. Under the framework that treats +30%+ 90-day revisions combined with strong revenue growth as a powerful buy signal, KNX falls short on both legs: revisions are positive but below the 30% threshold (the strongest line, next-year, is +17.8%), and revenue growth is flat (the recovery is a margin story, not a volume story). The recovery is real but moderate, not the kind of violent earnings-trough rebound that the strongest revision signal flags.

The Q2 2026 guide of $0.45-0.49 implies roughly a 5x sequential EPS jump from Q1's $0.09 actual. That is an aggressive bet on the Q1 one-offs not recurring plus genuinely improving freight fundamentals. Much hinges on bid season translating into realized rate increases, and the difference between guided and realized outcomes is where execution risk lives.

The structural growth lever is LTL. KNX is a fast-growing entrant in a higher-barrier, structurally more profitable oligopoly. The buildout toward 48-state in-house coverage is a deliberate diversification away from truckload cyclicality. But 2025 LTL margins worsened on start-up costs, and management is now slowing the expansion pace; the segment is a multi-year investment, not a near-term earnings tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~353x is uninformative at a cyclical trough. The relevant frame is forward and normalized earnings. At $74, the stock trades at ~38x current-year consensus EPS of $1.93 and ~21x next-year consensus of $3.48. The 21x forward number is the right anchor, and it tells the central story: the market already discounts a substantial 2026-2027 recovery. To get to mid-cycle earnings power (the 2024-style $0.73 was itself depressed; a true mid-cycle would be well above the $3.48 estimate), the stock would re-rate further, but $3.48 already embeds a near-doubling of earnings from the depressed base.

Against peers, the picture is mixed and the comp set is heterogeneous. Peer-median P/E is 62.2 (Cargo Ground Transportation), but the peer trailing multiples are all distorted by the same trough dynamics (SAIA 51x, LSTR 62x, ARCB 70x, RXO negative). On forward earnings, KNX at ~21x is not obviously cheap nor obviously stretched relative to a recovering cyclical. The MSN argument that KNX looks cheap on forward price/sales versus the industry is offset by the Citi view that valuations are "starting to look toppy."

We score valuation 4.0. This reflects the calibration lesson that "cheap" is more often a value-trap warning than a buy signal, and that a low multiple alone is never sufficient. Here the issue is the inverse: the multiple is not low, the recovery is in the price, and there is no specific reason to believe the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. The 68% one-year run, driven by recovery expectations rather than realized earnings, has consumed the asymmetry that existed at $44. We are not penalizing the stock for the prior appreciation per se; we are noting that at 21x a forward number that already assumes the rebound, the current price offers little margin of safety. A pullback to the high-$50s/low-$60s (where Chairman insider sales occurred in February) would meaningfully improve the setup.

## Competitive Landscape

KNX's competitive position is the strongest part of the story and is stable-to-improving. As the largest one-way truckload carrier, it holds the highest operating leverage to a rate inflection of any player in the space: at a 97% OR, modest rate gains drop through powerfully. The Truckload segment's 80bp adjusted OR improvement and 3.3% revenue-per-tractor gain in 2025 are early evidence the core franchise is bottoming and gaining pricing traction as capacity exits.

The LTL build is a deliberate, multi-year effort to widen the moat in a structurally more profitable, higher-barrier segment. High fixed-cost service-center networks deter entrants, and KNX is one of few players with the scale and balance sheet to build national coverage organically plus through acquisition. The consolidation under the AAA Cooper brand simplifies the offering, though the 2025 margin deterioration and the LTL arbitration award show integration is not free.

Customer concentration is a moderate watch item: the largest customer is 13.1% of revenue (up from 12.6%), top 5 is 25.6%, and top 25 is 49.3%, weighted toward retail and discount retail. Contracts (ex-dedicated) carry no volume guarantees, tying results to consumer goods demand. The mid-market consumer crack visible elsewhere in the economy is a tail risk to volume assumptions, though it is partly offset by capacity exit on the supply side.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is that the cyclical recovery underperforms the priced-in expectation. The 10-K itself describes 2023-2025 as a prolonged downcycle that had not transitioned to a sustained recovery by year-end 2025. At a 97% OR, the operating leverage cuts both ways: a stalled rate environment leaves the stock expensive against still-depressed earnings.

Specific risks:
- **LTL execution.** Aggressive service-center buildout could disrupt operations and may not be profitable; 2025 margins deteriorated on start-up costs.
- **Insurance and claims volatility.** Self-insured with high retentions; a 10% adverse claims swing adds ~$41M to reserves. This materialized in the Q1 2026 LTL arbitration award. Industry "nuclear verdicts" are a structural threat.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $5.9B of goodwill plus intangibles (83% of equity) remains exposed; $98M was impaired in 2025.
- **Customer concentration** tied to discretionary consumer goods demand.
- **Regulatory and labor:** driver supply constraints, independent-contractor reclassification risk (AB5/PRO Act), and a fluid emissions regime (EPA rescinded the 2009 endangerment finding and Phase 3 GHG rules in February 2026, reducing near-term capex but creating a California patchwork).
- **The Q1 miss pattern.** Three misses in four quarters erodes confidence in the aggressive Q2 guide and the recovery cadence.

A soft negative governance tell: Executive Chairman Kevin Knight made open-market sales of ~140,000 shares (~$6M) in February 2026 near $60, and there has been no open-market insider buying at the trough. If insiders believed a sharp recovery was imminent and the stock cheap, purchases would be the expected signal. Director grants at $59.90 in May 2026 are compensation, not conviction buys.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced and reflects no extreme positioning. Near-term ATM IV of 41.9% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 39.3% (a 2.7pp spread, "fair"), with IV at the 26th percentile within the current chain; the market is not pricing a near-term catalyst as unusually risky. Put/call OI ratio of 0.36 and a balanced -2.5% near-term skew indicate no aggressive hedging. The term structure is flat (near 41.9% ≈ far 42.2%), suggesting uncertainty is distributed evenly across the recovery timeline rather than concentrated around a single event. The implied move into the next monthly is ±8.1%. None of this contradicts the fundamental HOLD view; it confirms a market that is neither panicked nor euphoric.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-94).** Capacity exit accelerates following the Q1 weather/fuel shocks, bid season delivers realized rate increases, and the 97% OR's operating leverage drives a sharp margin recovery. Q2 guidance of $0.45-0.49 is met and the cadence continues, validating next-year EPS of $3.48 with upside toward mid-cycle power. The LTL franchise stabilizes and resumes profitable growth. At 26-27x a confirmed $3.48 (a recovery-cyclical premium), the stock reaches the analyst high of $94.

**Bear Case ($54-58).** The rate inflection disappoints, bid season produces flat-to-modest gains, and the recovery slips into 2027. Earnings stall well below the priced-in $3.48 trajectory, LTL margins remain pressured, and another one-off claims/weather quarter materializes. The multiple compresses as the recovery premium unwinds, taking the stock to the analyst low of $54, roughly a 27% decline from current levels, near where the Chairman sold in February.

**Base Case ($72-78).** A gradual, uneven recovery roughly in line with current consensus. Q2 delivers near the guided range, rates improve modestly, and the stock holds in a range as the market waits for confirmation that the recovery is durable. This is the most probable outcome given the recovery is already substantially priced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (next major catalyst).**

The risk/reward from $74 is balanced. The bull and bear cases bracket the current price almost symmetrically (~+25% to bull, ~-27% to bear), with the base case essentially flat. The recovery thesis is credible but already discounted, and the Q1 miss plus the absence of insider buying temper conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 adjusted EPS lands within or above the $0.45-0.49 guide AND management raises full-year guidance on realized (not just guided) rate increases.
- Truckload adjusted OR improves below 93% with revenue-per-tractor accelerating, confirming the pricing inflection.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s that restores valuation asymmetry against the recovery trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 misses the guided range, signaling the recovery is stalling while the stock still trades at a recovery multiple.
- LTL adjusted OR deteriorates further (above 95%) with continued integration drag, undermining the diversification thesis.
- A material goodwill impairment or a second large adverse claims/arbitration award, indicating the thin-margin quarters cannot absorb shocks.

## Conclusion

Knight-Swift is the scale leader in North American truckload at the trough of a multi-year freight recession, with genuine operating leverage to a rate inflection and a sensible long-term LTL diversification. The fundamentals are bottoming, and forward estimate revisions (+17.8% next-year over 90 days) confirm the market expects a recovery. The problem for new capital is price: at $74, up 68% in a year while earnings fell, the stock trades at ~21x a forward number that already embeds a near-doubling of earnings. The recovery is in the price, the Q1 miss showed how easily thin-margin quarters derail, and insiders have sold rather than bought into the trough.

We rate KNX HOLD. The trajectory of prior scores (4.2 → 5.0 → 4.9, three consecutive HOLDs) reflects a business improving off the bottom while valuation runs ahead of fundamentals, and the latest data confirms rather than reverses that pattern. We would turn constructive on confirmation of the recovery in Q2 results or on a pullback that restores margin of safety. We would turn negative if the guided recovery fails to materialize. For now, the asymmetry favors waiting.
