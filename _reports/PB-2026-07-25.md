**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Prosperity Bancshares is a Houston-based regional bank running a serial-acquisition strategy that has doubled the balance sheet's growth cadence over the past 18 months. Four deals — Lone Star, American Bank Holding (closed 1/1/26), Southwest Bancshares (closed 2/1/26), and the ~$2.0B Stellar Bancorp (closed 7/1/26) — have taken assets from ~$38.5B at year-end 2025 to $43.6B at 3/31/26, and pro forma above ~$52–53B with Stellar consolidated. The company brings a low-cost core deposit franchise (roughly a third of deposits noninterest-bearing), a historically top-decile efficiency ratio, and a fortress capital position (CET1 15.45%) to a fragmented, business-friendly Texas banking market where it functions as the consolidator of choice.

The central analytical tension is between reported and normalized earnings. Q1 2026 diluted EPS of $1.16 fell from $1.37 a year earlier, but that decline is almost entirely $42.5M of merger costs; net interest income grew 21% YoY and NIM expanded 37 bps to 3.51%, with management guiding to a ~3.70% combined NIM exit rate for 2026. The forward story — a much larger post-Stellar earnings base, expanding margin, and cost synergies harvested through the March 2027 systems conversion — points to materially higher normalized profitability in 2027. The 2026 P&L is a poor guide to run-rate power.

We rate PB a HOLD. The bank is well-run and durable, but the setup argues for patience rather than accumulation at current levels. The stock trades at ~13.2x trailing / ~11.7x on FY26 consensus and ~10.2x FY27, roughly in line with its regional-bank peer median (13.0x). Consensus estimates are drifting lower (FY26 -0.6%, FY27 -1.4% over 90 days), the company has missed EPS four straight quarters (most recently by 19% on merger noise), and a Q1 net charge-off spike to $41.3M from $2.7M a year earlier is a genuine yellow flag that needs to resolve. There is no independent catalyst to re-rate the multiple before integration proves out. This is a growth hold: we expect appreciation as 2027 normalized earnings materialize, but the near-term risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Prosperity Bancshares operates through Prosperity Bank, a community/commercial bank with ~311 full-service banking centers (pre-Stellar) across Texas and Oklahoma, organized around Houston, Dallas/Fort Worth, San Antonio/Central Texas, Corpus Christi, the West Texas energy corridor (Midland-Odessa, Lubbock), and Oklahoma. Net interest income is the dominant revenue line at ~87% of the total ($321M NII vs. $46M noninterest income in Q1 2026).

The funding base is the franchise's structural advantage: noninterest-bearing deposits of ~$10.6B, about one-third of total deposits, provide a low-cost, sticky liability profile that becomes more valuable as the Fed cuts rates. The loan book is real-estate-heavy (~80% of loans): 1-4 family residential (~34%), commercial real estate including multifamily (~28-29%), construction/land (~13%), C&I (~14%), plus a distinctive ~$1.4B Warehouse Purchase Program of short-duration mortgage participations.

The strategy is a 40+ year, ~100-deal M&A roll-up under long-tenured Senior Chairman and CEO David Zalman. Prosperity positions itself as the acquirer of choice for smaller Texas franchises seeking scale and liquidity, and its centralized operating infrastructure is designed to absorb acquired volume. Post-Stellar, the company added 52 offices concentrated in greater Houston, Beaumont, and Dallas, densifying its home market and bringing Stellar's Houston leadership onto the board and management team.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale transformation is the headline.** Q1 2026 total assets reached $43.62B (+13.4% QoQ), loans $25.29B (+16%), deposits $32.63B (+14.6%), and equity $8.21B (+7.8%). This growth was almost entirely acquisition-driven, funded largely with stock ($306.8M for American, $282.6M for Southwest).

**Margin expansion is real and continuing.** Net interest income of $321.2M rose 21.0% YoY. NIM expanded 37 bps to 3.51% on a tax-equivalent basis, driven by asset repricing, purchase-accounting accretion, and paydown of higher-cost borrowings. The cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 31 bps to 2.08%, the deposit-rich, asset-repricing dynamic that a rate-cutting Fed rewards. Management's guided ~3.70% combined NIM exit rate for 2026 implies further improvement.

**Reported earnings are distorted by merger noise.** Net income to common was $116.3M ($1.16 diluted) versus $130.2M ($1.37) a year earlier, depressed by $42.5M of merger-related expenses. The efficiency ratio rose to 59.2% from 45.7% on the same one-time costs. FY2025 net income was a record $542.8M, up from $479.4M in 2024 and $419.3M in 2023 (the 2023 trough reflected NIM compression during the hiking cycle). Underlying, ex-merger profitability remains strong; normalized ROA has historically run ~1.3-1.4%, though reported Q1 ROE of just 5.70% reflects both merger costs and the drag of an over-capitalized balance sheet.

**Capital is a fortress.** CET1 of 15.45%, total capital 16.63%, and leverage 11.22% at 3/31/26 sit well above "well-capitalized" thresholds even after funding three deals. This supports the dividend, buyback, and further M&A capacity, but it is excess capital that dilutes ROE. The dividend was raised to $0.60/quarter (declared 7/22/26; ~$2.40 annualized, a ~3.3% yield). The buyback is active — a new 5% (~4.9M share) authorization was announced 1/26/26, and ~837K shares were repurchased in Q1 at an average $68.15, signaling management views the stock as attractive in the high-$60s.

**Two items warrant normalization.** First, the securities book of $11.95B (27% of assets, predominantly agency MBS held-to-maturity) carries a ~$914M gross unrealized loss on HTM securities. This is a rate mark, not a credit issue, but it is a real economic drag on tangible book value. Second, purchase-accounting accretion is flattering reported NIM: ASU 2025-08 adoption added ~$91M of Day-1 acquired-loan reserves without a P&L provision hit, and $77M of accretable discount will boost future loan yields. Legitimate, but partly non-recurring — run-rate NIM is likely somewhat below the accretion-boosted reported figure.

## Growth Analysis

Prosperity's growth is inorganic by design. The four-deal cadence has grown the earnings base substantially, and the Stellar close adds a full quarter of incremental NII beginning Q3 2026. The forward earnings math is constructive: a larger post-Stellar loan and deposit base, an expanding NIM guided to ~3.70%, and cost synergies as three franchises integrate through March 2027 all point toward materially higher normalized earnings in 2027 than the merger-suppressed 2026 print suggests. FY27 consensus of $7.17 versus FY26 $6.26 embeds a ~15% step-up that is largely the roll-off of merger costs plus synergy realization.

The organic story is more muted. Texas remains one of the faster-growing US banking markets, and Prosperity's business-friendly footprint and low-cost deposits give it a durable share position. But standalone loan and deposit growth ex-M&A is mid-single-digit at best; the growth thesis rests on continued accretive acquisitions and successful integration, not organic expansion.

The estimate-revision trend is a caution. Consensus is broadly falling: current quarter flat at $1.55, next quarter -0.4%, FY26 +0.3% but FY27 -0.6% over 30 days and -1.4% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive-revision trend that would signal the market catching up to an underappreciated recovery. Combined with four consecutive EPS misses (Q1 2026 by 19.4%, prior three by 0.4-2.4%), the visibility on near-term consensus is degraded even as the longer-term normalized story remains intact.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.15, PB trades at ~13.2x trailing EPS ($5.72), ~11.7x FY26 consensus ($6.26), and ~10.2x FY27 consensus ($7.17). Against a regional-bank peer median P/E of 13.0x (FNB 11.3x, VLY 13.0x, UBSI 12.6x, CBSH 13.6x, GBCI an outlier at 22.8x), Prosperity is fairly valued to modestly cheap on a forward basis. The ~3.3% dividend yield is above the peer median of 2.9% and well-covered even on merger-suppressed earnings.

The case for a higher valuation score rests on cycle-adjusted earnings power: FY26 is depressed by one-time merger costs, so the trailing and current-year multiples understate normalized profitability. On FY27 estimates the stock is ~10x, a discount to peers, and if the NIM exit rate and post-Stellar synergies deliver, run-rate earnings could exceed even the $7.17 FY27 consensus. That is the crux of the growth hold.

But two factors hold the valuation score at 5.5 rather than higher. First, estimate revisions are falling, which removes the signal that would justify treating consensus targets as floors. Analyst targets (mean $79.62, median $80.00, range $68-$90) imply ~9% upside to the mean, but the sentiment trend is degrading (a StrongBuy downgrade in the last month), so treating those targets as forward guidance is unwarranted here. Second, and consistent with the broader pattern that a compressed multiple in Financials without a distinct catalyst tends to be a value trap rather than a bargain, cheapness alone is not a buy signal. Prosperity's forward multiple is reasonable, but there is no identified catalyst to compress the peer discount before integration proves out through March 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

Prosperity's competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory, which is the relevant dimension. The bank is gaining share the way it always has — by acquisition — and the Stellar deal materially strengthens its position in the critical Houston market, adding density and bringing over experienced local leadership (Robert Franklin Jr. as Vice Chairman, Ramon Vitulli as Houston Area Chairman). The differentiation is real: efficiency, low-cost sticky deposits, and a scaled centralized operating platform that lets Prosperity absorb acquired banks at costs smaller competitors cannot match.

The industry backdrop favors the model. The Fed's rate cuts (three in 2024, three in 2025) are a tailwind for a deposit-rich, asset-repricing bank — falling deposit costs while assets reprice up drove the 37 bps NIM expansion. The Texas market remains fragmented and ripe for consolidation, and Prosperity's fortress capital and NIB deposit base position it as a consolidator, not a target, in a post-2023 environment where regional-bank confidence still matters.

The offsetting consideration is that the moat is not widening organically. Prosperity competes against national banks, other Texas regionals, credit unions, and fintechs on the deposit and lending fronts, and its edge comes from execution and scale in M&A rather than a structural product advantage. As long as the roll-up machine runs cleanly, the position improves; if integration falters, the same aggressive cadence becomes a liability.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration/execution risk is the single most important idiosyncratic item.** Four deals in 18 months is a heavy integration load. Stellar systems do not fully convert until March 2027, so operational integration risk — deposit/customer attrition, cost overruns, management distraction — persists well into next year.

**The Q1 2026 credit signal deserves scrutiny.** The net charge-off spike was driven by a $33.9M increase in C&I charge-offs. The annualized NCO ratio spiked to 0.67% from 0.05% — a sharp break from Prosperity's historically pristine record. Mitigating factors: NPAs actually fell 19% QoQ to $122.1M (0.48% of loans), and the ACL covers nonperformers 353%, suggesting this is resolution of one or a few large idiosyncratic credits rather than broad deterioration. But acquired PCD loans carry a 5.18% NPA ratio, so credit trends across the enlarged, acquisition-heavy book are a key watch item.

**Goodwill and intangibles are building.** Goodwill was $3.5B at 12/31/25 and rises with each deal (American added $185M + $31M CDI; Southwest $134M + $34M CDI; Stellar adds substantially more). Rising intangibles dilute tangible book value and create future impairment risk if deals underperform.

**Concentration risks are structural but partly priced.** ~100% Texas/Oklahoma exposure and ~80% real-estate loans (including ~26.5% CRE ex-farmland) leave the bank exposed to the Texas economy and the CRE cycle. Direct energy loans are only ~2%, but the broader Texas economy is oil-sensitive. The HTM unrealized loss is an economic capital overhang, not an accounting event.

The composite of a live credit signal, heavy integration load through 2027, and building intangibles against a fortress capital base and clean governance puts the risk score below neutral at 5.3.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.6% is rich against 22.5% one-year historical volatility (a 12.1pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (34.6% near vs. 30.8% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst. The 1-month put/call skew is -8.4% (call-heavy), which does not confirm a strong bearish lean; positioning is tilted toward calls despite the elevated overall vol. Short interest is low at 4.4% of float (2.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing entry ahead of the next print; a better risk-adjusted entry would follow the post-earnings volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$88, ~20% upside).** Integration proceeds cleanly, the ~3.70% NIM exit rate is achieved, and Stellar synergies plus roll-off of merger costs drive normalized 2027 EPS toward and above the $7.17 consensus. The over-capitalized balance sheet funds continued accretive M&A and buybacks, and the market re-rates the enlarged franchise toward the higher end of its historical range and analyst targets ($90 high). A deposit-rich bank in a rate-cutting cycle compounds tangible book while the Texas consolidation runway remains long.

**Bear Case (~$61, ~17% downside, near the 52-week low).** The Q1 C&I charge-off proves to be the leading edge of broader credit deterioration in the acquired books, integration stumbles cause deposit attrition and cost overruns through the March 2027 conversion, and estimate revisions continue falling. Purchase-accounting accretion fades faster than run-rate NIM improves, disappointing the NIM guide. Goodwill impairment risk crystallizes if any of the four deals underperform, and the fifth consecutive EPS miss compresses the multiple back toward the low end of the peer range.

The base case sits between these: the bank delivers on NIM and integration broadly on plan, but the near-term drag of merger costs and a degraded revision trend keeps the multiple range-bound until 2027 earnings power becomes visible in reported results.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Reassessment timeline: next 2-3 earnings prints, with the March 2027 Stellar systems conversion as the key structural milestone.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus FY27 EPS revisions turn positive (versus the current -1.4% 90-day trend), signaling the market is beginning to price normalized post-integration earnings.
- Q2/Q3 2026 net charge-offs normalize back toward historical levels (NCO ratio returning below ~0.15%), confirming the Q1 C&I spike was idiosyncratic.
- A pullback toward the high-$60s (where management itself repurchased stock at $68.15), which would restore forward-multiple asymmetry against the ~10x FY27 base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of elevated charge-offs or rising NPAs across the acquired portfolios, indicating systemic rather than idiosyncratic credit deterioration.
- Evidence of integration failure — meaningful deposit attrition, disclosed cost overruns, or a delayed Stellar conversion.
- A goodwill impairment charge on any of the four recent deals.

**Base case target: ~$80** (~12.9x the ~$6.20 blended normalized 2026-27 earnings, in line with the peer median and consistent with analyst median of $80.00), roughly 9% above the current $73.15, over a 12-18 month horizon. **Bull case: ~$88** (~20% upside). **Bear case: ~$61** (~17% downside).

## Conclusion

Prosperity Bancshares is a durable, well-capitalized, efficiently-run Texas franchise executing a large M&A wave that has expanded scale and margin while temporarily masking underlying earnings with merger costs. The long-term thesis — normalized 2027 earnings power on a larger base, an expanding NIM, and continued accretive consolidation in a fragmented Texas market — is sound. Governance is clean, insider signals are neutral, and the dividend is well-covered.

The reasons to hold rather than buy today are near-term and specific: falling consensus estimates, four straight EPS misses, an out-of-character Q1 charge-off spike that needs to resolve, reliance on purchase-accounting accretion in reported NIM, heavy integration load through March 2027, and a forward multiple that is fair-to-cheap but lacks an independent catalyst to re-rate before the integration proves out. Rich near-term implied volatility further argues for patience on entry. We would become more constructive on positive estimate-revision velocity, normalized credit trends, or a pullback into the high-$60s, and would step back on evidence of broadening credit deterioration or integration failure. HOLD.
