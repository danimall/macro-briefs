**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liquidity Services operates e-commerce marketplaces for the reverse supply chain, connecting business and government sellers of surplus, returned, and end-of-life assets with a global base of 6.3 million registered buyers. The investment story turns on an accounting nuance: GMV, not reported revenue, is the cleaner growth metric, because roughly 81% of GMV runs through a consignment model where only commission is booked as revenue. A revenue-only screen sees +3.7% YoY growth in Q2 FY26 and concludes the business is stalling. The underlying economics are accelerating: GMV +6.1%, segment direct profit +18%, Adjusted EBITDA +37%, and operating cash flow tripling to $29.0M in H1. The most important driver is RSCG direct-profit margin expanding from 20.0% to 25.7% YoY as mix shifts away from low-margin gross-revenue purchase deals toward higher-margin consignment and direct-to-consumer channels.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength: ~$204M of cash and short-term investments, zero drawn debt, and an undrawn $35M revolver. The Altman Z grey-zone reading and the 0.85 debt-to-equity figure that surface on standard screens are artifacts of the $77M payables-to-sellers float intrinsic to a marketplace model, not distress signals. Founder-CEO William Angrick holds roughly 5.2 million shares, aligning management with shareholders.

The constraint is price. The stock has risen 62% over the trailing twelve months and trades at ~39x trailing P/E and ~25x the $1.50 current-year consensus. The Adjusted EBITDA acceleration is real, but the market has largely caught up to it, and the $44 analyst target implies roughly 16% upside against company-specific risks that have not gone away: Amazon supplier concentration in RSCG, contract-renewal dependency on a concentrated set of large sellers, and used-asset cyclicality. We rate LQDT a HOLD with a positive bias, upgrading on a pullback or sustained evidence the RSCG margin gain is structural rather than mix-cyclical.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1999 as Liquidation.com, Liquidity Services has roughly 25 years of operating history in remarketing surplus, returned, and end-of-life assets. Over the trailing three fiscal years it conducted more than 3.1 million transactions generating $4.1 billion in GMV, with a five-year GMV CAGR of approximately 20.4%. The company reports four operating units:

- **GovDeals** — surplus property and real estate for U.S. and Canadian government entities. Approximately 95% consignment, asset-light, with H1 FY26 segment direct-profit margin near 94%.
- **RSCG (Retail Supply Chain Group)** — excess and returned consumer goods. This unit carries most of the company's COGS because it includes purchase-model transactions booked gross. Channels include Liquidation.com (B2B) and Retail Rush / AllSurplus Deals / Secondipity (D2C).
- **CAG (Capital Assets Group)** — heavy equipment, industrial, oil & gas, biopharma. Mostly consignment, with direct-profit margin of roughly 85-90%.
- **Machinio & Software Solutions** — an equipment search engine plus SaaS auction software, expanded by the January 2025 Auction Software / Auction.io acquisition. Recurring subscription revenue.

The defensibility rests on two-sided network effects: aggregated buyer demand (6.3 million buyers, 900+ asset categories, 160 country pairings) attracts sellers, whose supply attracts more buyers. The consolidated AllSurplus/GovDeals platform cross-lists assets to widen the bidder pool. Switching costs for large sellers derive from integrated logistics, compliance, and the Asset Zone management portal. The company holds no patents or registered copyrights and relies on trade secrets and its buyer database for protection, a meaningful caveat to the moat.

## Financial Analysis

The central interpretive point is that purchase transactions are roughly 19% of GMV but about 64% of reported revenue, because purchase model revenue is recorded gross while consignment revenue is recorded net of seller proceeds. This makes headline revenue a misleading proxy for activity.

**Q2 FY26 (quarter ended 3/31/26):**

| Metric | Q2 FY26 | Q2 FY25 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidated GMV | $389.9M | $367.4M | +6.1% |
| Total revenue | $120.7M | $116.4M | +3.7% |
| Income from operations | $9.6M | $6.8M | +41.4% |
| Net income | $7.5M | $7.1M | +6.7% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $16.7M | $12.2M | +37% |
| Adjusted EPS | $0.35 | — | beat $0.33 est. |

H1 FY26 shows GMV +4.6% to $787.8M, Adjusted EBITDA +37% to $34.7M, and operating cash flow of $29.0M versus $9.5M a year earlier. Direct profit and EBITDA are growing several times faster than reported revenue, which is the signature of operating leverage in a marketplace whose costs are largely fixed relative to GMV.

RSCG margin expansion replaces the compression a trailing snapshot suggested. RSCG direct-profit margin rose from 20.0% to 25.7% YoY in Q2 (20.6% to 25.9% for H1), with management attributing the gain to strong buyer demand for purchase-model sortable goods, robust consignment participation, and lower transaction-processing fees. RSCG revenue was roughly flat-to-down even as GMV grew about 10%, confirming a mix shift toward higher-margin consignment and D2C. A revenue-only read misreads this favorable shift as stagnation.

The balance sheet is conservative. Cash and equivalents of $195.3M plus $8.7M of short-term investments total approximately $204M, with no drawn debt and only $9M of standby letters of credit against the undrawn $35M Wells Fargo revolver. Equity is $222M; goodwill is $103M with no impairment indicators. The $77M payables-to-sellers balance is float held on sellers' behalf, a working-capital positive. Capex is light (~$4.4M in H1). The company is over-capitalized relative to its needs.

Two items separate GAAP optics from cash economics. Stock-based compensation rose 75% YoY to $10.5M in H1, and the effective tax rate jumped to 29.2% from 19.0% on lower stock-comp tax benefits and nondeductibles. Together these explain why GAAP net income (+6.7%) lagged Adjusted EBITDA (+37%). Adjusted EBITDA overstates distributable earnings to the extent the rising SBC reflects genuine dilution from the enlarged LTIP pool (raised from 22.8M to 25.725M shares), which investors should haircut.

The earnings record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats, with surprises of 7.2%, 15.9%, 25.1%, and 11.1% over the trailing four quarters.

## Growth Analysis

The structural tailwind is real. The reverse-logistics opportunity is anchored in online return rates near 20% (versus 8-10% in-store), more than $100B per year of returned merchandise flowing into secondary markets, and rising corporate and government pressure to remarket rather than landfill. The market is fragmented and growing, and outsourcing surplus disposition is a secular shift. Registered buyers grew about 8% YoY to 6.3 million, expanding the demand side of the network.

The near-term growth engine is profitability rather than volume. GMV growth in the mid-single digits is solid but not explosive, and management guided to continued GMV growth in Q3. The more powerful lever is margin: if RSCG can sustain ~26% direct-profit margins against a prior ~20% base, EBITDA continues to outgrow GMV materially. Consensus reflects this — current-year EPS of $1.50 stepping to $1.70 next year implies roughly 13% earnings growth, well above GMV growth.

I do not see a strong inflection BUY signal here. Estimate revisions are reported as stable with no significant directional shift, revenue growth is in the low single digits, and the EBITDA acceleration is at least partly a mix-driven margin event rather than a volume re-rating. The growth is genuine and improving, but it is a margin-and-mix story with a finite runway once RSCG mix normalizes, not an open-ended revenue ramp. CAG margin volatility (89.7% to 85.5% in H1 on lower-margin international spot purchases) is a reminder that segment mix swings both ways.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.89, LQDT trades at ~39x trailing P/E and roughly 25x the $1.50 current-year consensus, falling to ~22x the $1.70 next-year estimate. The peer set in Diversified Support Services carries a median P/E of 21.9 (CXW 21.9, UNF 37.3, KBR 10.4, with VSTS and OPLN negative). On forward earnings, LQDT sits at the upper end of that range, justified to a degree by its debt-free balance sheet, marketplace economics, and faster EBITDA growth.

The case for treating analyst targets as a floor does not fully apply here. The $44 target represents only one data point (high and low both $44 from a thin two-analyst coverage), and the stock at ~25x forward is not cheap relative to its ~13% earnings growth, a PEG near 1.9. This does not fit the sub-30x-forward, sub-1.5-PEG profile where consensus targets reliably lag. The market has already digested the EBITDA acceleration: the stock is up 62% over twelve months and sits within 2% of its 52-week high of $38.83.

The bull on valuation would argue that Adjusted EBITDA, not GAAP EPS, is the right metric, and on EV/EBITDA the picture is more favorable. Enterprise value of roughly $976M ($1.18B market cap less ~$204M net cash) against trailing Adjusted EBITDA approaching $68-70M annualized puts EV/EBITDA near 14x, reasonable for a debt-free network-effect business with mid-teens EBITDA growth. That is the more defensible framing and the reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. But it does not produce asymmetric upside: the cheapness needed to justify a high valuation score requires either a clearer catalyst or a lower entry point. I score valuation 4.5: the multiple is full against the growth on offer, with no specific mispricing to exploit at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real but bounded. Two-sided network effects and an aggregated demand pool are difficult to replicate, and the consolidated cross-listing platform improves recovery rates and bidder depth. Integrated logistics, compliance, and the Asset Zone portal create switching costs for large institutional sellers, and the ~95% consignment GovDeals franchise is sticky given government procurement relationships.

The structural risk is seller in-sourcing. Competition is broad and low-barrier (other B2B marketplaces, auction sites, liquidators, and fixed-site auctioneers), but the more important threat is that large sellers (Amazon, major retailers, government agencies) build their own disposition channels. The absence of patents means the defensibility is execution and scale, not protected IP. On trajectory, the company is gaining buyers (+8%) and expanding margins, which supports a stable-to-improving competitive position. The RSCG margin gain on lower transaction-processing fees suggests scale advantages are accruing. I score competitive position 6.5: a defensible niche leader with improving economics, capped by the in-sourcing threat and the lack of hard IP.

## Risk Assessment

**Amazon supplier concentration (the single most important company-specific risk).** Liquidity Services purchases merchandise from Amazon under multiple vendor contracts to resell through RSCG's purchase model. Amazon inventory on the balance sheet rose to $15.9M from $10.1M at FY25-end. Purchase model is under 20% of GMV but the most significant component of consolidated COGS. Loss or renegotiation of this supply relationship would hit RSCG materially. This is a procurement dependency, not a strategic alliance, and it has no natural offset.

**Seller-supply / contract-renewal dependency.** The 10-Q explicitly flags the risk that commercial sellers supplying a significant portion of inventory change their disposition approach or terminate contracts. The model depends on continuous flow from a concentrated set of large corporate and government sellers.

**Macro and used-asset cyclicality.** Used-vehicle price indices, tariffs, slowing EV adoption affecting fleet supply, and consumer-sentiment swings all affect asset mix, volumes, and recovery rates. GovDeals real-estate volumes were dented by winter weather in Q2 FY26.

**Rising dilution.** SBC up 75% YoY and the LTIP pool raised to 25.725M shares mean Adjusted EBITDA increasingly overstates per-share economics. This is a slow leak rather than a binary risk, but it is the main reason GAAP earnings lag.

**Idiosyncratic items.** A former CMO discrimination/retaliation suit goes to trial July 13-17, 2026, with the insurer accepting tender (not deemed material). A new Israel-Iran conflict risk factor was added in the Q2 10-Q, primarily a macro/energy spillover flag given no material Middle East operations. Credit concentration exists among a small number of qualified buyers with payment terms up to six months.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 40.1% against one-year historical volatility of 34.6%, leaving IV modestly rich (a 5.5% spread) but at only the 17th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew of 6.0% indicates elevated put demand, and the term structure is flat (40.1% near versus 40.4% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±16% (±11.2% on the overall chain). The elevated put skew is a mild caution against the improving fundamental trajectory, consistent with a stock near its 52-week high where some holders are hedging gains rather than expressing a fresh bearish thesis. Short interest is low at 3.5% of float (4.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The RSCG margin expansion (20% to 26%) is structural, driven by lower transaction-processing fees and a durable mix shift to high-margin consignment and D2C. Operating leverage continues, with Adjusted EBITDA compounding in the high teens to low twenties while GMV grows mid-single digits. The debt-free balance sheet funds tuck-in M&A and modest buybacks, and the secular reverse-logistics tailwind sustains buyer and GMV growth. On EV/EBITDA near 14x for a debt-free network business, the stock re-rates toward the high $40s as consensus catches up to cash earnings power. Bull case ~$47-50.

**Bear case.** RSCG margin gains prove mix-cyclical and revert as purchase-model demand normalizes; CAG margin volatility compounds; Amazon renegotiates or reduces supply, hitting RSCG GMV and COGS economics. Rising SBC dilution erodes per-share value while the ~39x trailing multiple compresses toward the peer median. A used-asset cyclical downturn pressures recovery rates and volumes. The stock de-rates to the mid-$20s, near its 52-week low of $21.67. Bear case ~$26-28, roughly -28% from current.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-single-digit GMV growth, EBITDA growth moderating but staying positive, and a multiple that holds rather than expands, producing a fair value near $40-42.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive bias and a growth-hold rationale: the fundamental trajectory is improving and there is a credible path to appreciation, but the current price already discounts much of the EBITDA acceleration, leaving insufficient margin of safety to justify a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY26 earnings release (early August 2026), which tests whether RSCG margin gains and GMV growth persisted past the winter-affected Q2.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $32-34 (roughly 18-20x forward EPS / ~12x EV/EBITDA) that restores margin of safety, with fundamentals intact.
- Two consecutive quarters confirming RSCG direct-profit margin holds at or above ~25%, demonstrating the mix shift is structural rather than transient.
- Evidence the Amazon supplier relationship is diversifying or de-concentrating, removing the largest idiosyncratic overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material reduction or renegotiation of the Amazon supply relationship that visibly reduces RSCG GMV.
- RSCG direct-profit margin reverting toward 20% alongside flat-to-declining GMV, indicating the margin gain was cyclical.
- A guidance cut or a quarter where Adjusted EBITDA growth turns negative while SBC dilution continues to climb.

## Conclusion

Liquidity Services is a well-run, debt-free, founder-aligned marketplace whose economics are genuinely improving: Adjusted EBITDA +37%, operating cash flow tripling, and RSCG margins expanding meaningfully on roughly flat headline revenue. The standard screens that flag leverage and "flat revenue" misread a float-driven balance sheet and a favorable mix shift. The fundamentals support a constructive view.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because of price, not business quality. At ~25x forward earnings and a PEG near 1.9, having risen 62% in twelve months to within 2% of its 52-week high, the stock has largely priced the EBITDA acceleration, and the lone $44 analyst target implies only modest upside against real Amazon-concentration, contract-renewal, and cyclicality risks. We would become buyers on a pullback to the low $30s or on confirmation that the RSCG margin gain is durable across multiple quarters.
