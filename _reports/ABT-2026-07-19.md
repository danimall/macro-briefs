**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Abbott Laboratories enters mid-2026 as a diversified healthcare company whose underlying operations are accelerating while its risk profile has widened. The Medical Devices segment (roughly half of sales) is compounding at double digits led by FreeStyle Libre CGM ($7.6B, +17% FX-neutral in 2025) and a broad structural-heart franchise, with segment operating margin at 33.7%. The Q2 2026 print (reported July 15-16) confirmed the operational strength: 13% reported revenue growth, ~4.8% organic, adjusted EPS of $1.31, and a raised full-year adjusted EPS guide to $5.45-$5.60. Diagnostics jumped 42% on the newly consolidated Exact Sciences cancer-screening business.

The complicating factor is that the balance sheet the market was pricing six months ago no longer exists. The $20.6B Exact Sciences acquisition closed March 23, 2026, funded with $20B of new senior notes. Long-term debt jumped from $9.9B to $29.6B, goodwill rose to $35.2B, and net intangibles rose to $17.9B in a single quarter. This is still investment grade (A+/Aa3), and Abbott generates ~$9-10B of operating cash flow, so the leverage is serviceable. But the step-up removes balance-sheet slack precisely as the single largest idiosyncratic risk, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) infant-formula litigation, has turned more adverse in state courts while Abbott carries a $0 reserve against it.

The stock has recovered from its 52-week low of $81.97 (reached May 2026) to roughly $101, aided by the guidance raise and post-earnings pop from $89.27 to $98.83 on July 16. That recovery, combined with a forward P/E near 32x against a Health Care sector where selection skill is roughly market-neutral, leaves the risk/reward balanced. We rate ABT a HOLD with a positive growth bias, contingent on litigation clarity and continued Exact Sciences integration progress.

## Company Overview

Abbott is an Illinois-incorporated global healthcare company (founded 1900, ~115,000 employees, 61% of sales international) operating four reportable segments:

- **Medical Devices (~50% of sales, growth engine):** Diabetes Care (FreeStyle Libre CGM), Electrophysiology (Volt PFA), Structural Heart (MitraClip, TriClip, Navitor, Tendyne TMVR), Heart Failure (HeartMate LVAD, CardioMEMS), Rhythm Management (Aveir leadless pacemakers), Vascular, and Neuromodulation.
- **Diagnostics (~20%):** Core Laboratory (Alinity), Rapid/Molecular, in decline (-4.5% FX-neutral in 2025) on COVID-test roll-off ($297M in 2025 vs. $1.6B in 2023) and China volume-based procurement (VBP) pressure. Now augmented by Cancer Diagnostics (Exact Sciences: Cologuard, Oncotype DX, Cancerguard) as of March 2026.
- **Nutrition (~18%):** Similac, Ensure, Glucerna, Pedialyte. Roughly flat; U.S. pediatric declining (Q1 2026 nutrition down 7.7% organic).
- **Established Pharmaceuticals (~13%):** Branded generics sold in emerging markets (Creon, Duphaston, Influvac), growing 7-9% FX-neutral.

The strategic rationale for Exact Sciences is to replace the structural decline in COVID/routine diagnostics and China VBP with a fourth high-growth diagnostics leg in cancer screening, a large secular-growth TAM. The diversification across four verticals reduces single-product concentration; no individual customer or patent is material to the whole, and there are no meaningful patent cliffs in the next three years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $43.7B (2022) → $40.1B (2023, COVID roll-off) → $42.0B (2024) → $44.3B (2025, +5.7%). Q1 2026 sales were $11.16B (+7.8% reported, +3.8% organic). The Q2 reported acceleration was driven by Exact Sciences consolidation.

**Profitability (2025):** Operating income $8.05B (18.2% margin, up from 16.3% in 2024); gross margin 52.6% (up from 50.9%); net margin 14.7%. A critical adjustment: 2024 GAAP net income of $13.4B is distorted by a one-time $7.5B non-cash valuation-allowance tax benefit — 2024 pretax income was only $7.0B, so normalized net income was ~$6.5B, roughly flat versus 2025's $6.5B. Naive YoY net-income comparisons therefore understate the operational improvement.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $9.6B (2025), FCF of $7.4B (16.7% margin) on steady ~$2.2B capex. FCF conversion is high-quality.

**Balance sheet, post-Exact Sciences (Q1 2026 10-Q):**

|---|---|---|
| Cash & equivalents | $8.5B | $6.8B |
| Long-term debt | $9.9B | $29.6B |
| Total debt | ~$12.9B | ~$34.0B |
| Goodwill | $24.0B | $35.2B |
| Intangibles, net | $5.5B | $17.9B |
| Total assets | $86.7B | $110.4B |
| Shareholders' equity | $52.1B | $52.7B |

Post-deal debt/equity is ~0.65x, a meaningful step-up but manageable for a company generating ~$9-10B OCF. Interest expense will rise: the new notes carry coupons of 3.7%-5.6% (the 2056/2066 tranches at 5.5%/5.6%). Amortization of the $12.8B of acquired intangibles adds ~$2.6B of non-cash GAAP drag in 2026, which is one reason to lean on adjusted rather than GAAP EPS this year.

One data-quality note: automated screens returned an Altman Z-Score of 0.95 ("distress"). This is a mapping artifact — the components zeroed out equity despite $52B of book equity. It should be disregarded. The Piotroski F-score (5/5 partial), current ratio of 1.58, ROA of 7.5%, ROE of ~12.5%, and positive FCF confirm a financially healthy company, not a distressed one.

**Capital allocation:** Abbott is a Dividend King (53rd consecutive annual increase), raising the quarterly dividend +6.8% to $0.63 in December 2025 and paying ~$4.1B in 2025 (current yield ~2.5%). Buybacks are paused for 2026 to prioritize deal deleveraging — no repurchases in Q1 2026, with $6.7B remaining on the $7B authorization. This is disciplined sequencing: pausing repurchases while carrying $34B of debt.

## Growth Analysis

The device franchise is the durable driver. FreeStyle Libre at $7.6B and 17% of sales grew +17% FX-neutral; structural heart, EP (Volt PFA), and heart failure lines are compounding at double digits. The Q2 2026 guidance raise, alongside management's characterization of Exact Sciences integration as "proceeding better than expected," signals that the acquired cancer-screening business (Cologuard, Oncotype DX, and the earlier-stage Cancerguard MCED test) is additive rather than a distraction.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not spectacular: current-year EPS revised +0.5% over 90 days to $5.51, next-year +0.1% to $6.07, next-quarter +0.8%. This is stable-to-rising momentum, consistent with a quality compounder, but well short of the +30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings acceleration. The earnings surprise record is unremarkable — 1 beat and 3 narrow misses over the last four quarters, all within ±1.5% — indicating tightly-tracked consensus rather than systematic under-estimation.

Growth durability rests on: (1) continued CGM expansion into the basal/non-insulin and over-the-counter segments; (2) structural-heart new-product cycles; and (3) the cancer-screening TAM offsetting Diagnostics decline. The offsets are real: Diagnostics ex-Exact is shrinking on COVID roll-off and China VBP, Nutrition is flat with U.S. pediatric declining, and FX remains a swing factor on 61% international sales. Net, we see a mid-single-digit organic grower with a device-led mix that supports low-double-digit adjusted EPS growth if litigation and integration proceed cleanly.

## Valuation Assessment

At $100.68 the stock trades at ~32.6x trailing and ~18.3x the current-year consensus of $5.51 (or ~16.6x the $6.07 next-year estimate). The trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because 2026 GAAP earnings carry the ~$2.6B intangible-amortization drag and one-time deal costs; adjusted forward multiples are the correct lens.

**Peer context** is mixed. Peer-median P/E in Health Care Equipment is 35.4x, and Abbott's headline trailing 32.6x sits below that. But the peer set is heterogeneous — ISRG at 50.2x and EW at 46.5x are pure-play high-growth names, while MDT (21.5x) and BSX (19.7x) are closer comparables for a diversified device-plus-diagnostics business. On a forward basis (~16.6x next-year), Abbott is priced in line with the more mature end of the peer group, reflecting its slower composite growth and heavier balance sheet.

Consensus targets — low $92, mean $118.33, median $119, high $135 — put the stock ~18% below the mean. On a high-quality name with rising estimates and a forward multiple below 20x, analyst targets are typically closer to floors than ceilings, which supports upside. Against that, the NEC litigation tail is a specific downside catalyst not fully captured by DCF or peer multiples, and the added leverage caps multiple expansion. We assign a 5.8 to valuation: not a value trap, but not an asymmetric setup after the recovery from $82 to $101. The valuation dimension has been a poor return predictor historically (cheap names have tended to underperform), so we resist rewarding the sub-sector-median headline multiple absent a clear catalyst, and the primary catalyst here (litigation resolution) is two-sided.

## Competitive Landscape

Abbott competes across four distinct arenas. In medtech, FreeStyle Libre is a CGM category leader against Dexcom, with structural heart positioning strong versus Edwards, Medtronic, and Boston Scientific. In diagnostics, it faces Roche, Danaher/Beckman, and Siemens Healthineers, and now, via Exact Sciences, Guardant and Grail in cancer screening. Nutrition competes with Danone and Reckitt/Mead Johnson (a co-defendant in the NEC suits), and Established Pharmaceuticals holds durable emerging-market positions.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The Exact Sciences deal adds a leading colorectal-screening franchise (Cologuard) with a secular tailwind, offsetting the structural erosion in COVID/routine diagnostics. The device segment continues to gain from new-product velocity (Volt PFA, Aveir, Tendyne). The watch item flagged on the Q2 call is intensifying CGM competition — Dexcom plus potential new entrants — which matters given Libre's 17% revenue weight; any pricing or share erosion there would dent the growth engine directly. On balance, the moat is widening in cancer diagnostics while defending in CGM, and share is broadly stable-to-gaining. The competitive-position score of 6.8 reflects a mild moderation from prior reads (8.0 → 6.5 → 6.8 here), as CGM competitive pressure and the execution risk of entering a new diagnostics market temper the improving trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**NEC infant-formula litigation is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Abbott faces 1,760 pending lawsuits (as of Jan 31, 2026) alleging its cow's-milk-based preterm formula (Similac Special Care) causes necrotizing enterocolitis. Abbott records a $0 reserve and states it cannot estimate the loss range. The Q1 2026 10-Q escalated the language to warn resolution "could be material to Abbott's financial position, cash flows, or results of operations," a tonal shift from the 10-K. Venue outcomes diverge sharply: Abbott won summary judgment in the first three federal MDL bellwether cases (favorable), but a $495M Missouri verdict was upheld on appeal in May 2026, and a new $70M Cook County verdict (including $17M punitive) landed in April 2026. With 1,760 cases and no balance-sheet reserve, this is a multi-billion-dollar tail exposure that a headline-metrics screen would miss.

**DOJ criminal and civil False Claims Act exposure (Sturgis).** An ongoing DOJ criminal investigation into Sturgis, MI powdered-formula manufacturing was joined in November 2025 by DOJ intervention in a qui tam False Claims Act suit, with state AGs joining in December 2025. Six shareholder derivative suits (surviving motion to dismiss) are consolidated in N.D. Illinois. No reserve recorded.

**Integration and leverage.** Execution risk in a new market plus $35.2B of goodwill create impairment risk if cancer-screening growth disappoints.

**Other:** China VBP and COVID roll-off continue shrinking core Diagnostics; Nutrition is flat with U.S. pediatric declining; IRS SNODs (~$1.05B aggregate on transfer pricing) are in Tax Court, and a $413M Malaysian assessment (upheld October 2025) is on appeal; FX moves reported results on 61% international sales.

The risk-factors score of 4.5 (down from 6.5 earlier in the trajectory) reflects that the NEC tail is specific, adverse in state courts, unreserved, and now escalated in tone by management, with an asymmetric downside profile that is not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.5% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 26.3% (+5.2pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 31.5% > 6-month 27.6%), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk consistent with post-earnings digestion and ongoing litigation headlines. The put/call skew is modestly elevated at 4.6% (elevated put demand), though the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.99 to 0.56 as the stock rallied +12.2% since mid-June, and IV compressed 5pp, a de-risking as the guidance raise resolved the earnings binary. A single unusual call print ($105 strike, July 24 expiry, 6.4x vol/OI) suggests some near-term upside positioning. The elevated put skew warrants attention against any constructive stance, but it is modest and consistent with normal litigation-headline hedging rather than a directional bearish tell. Short interest is negligible (1.5% of float, 1.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135, +34%):** The device franchise sustains double-digit growth, FreeStyle Libre defends and extends into over-the-counter/non-insulin segments, Exact Sciences integration delivers accretion ahead of plan (Cologuard TAM expansion, Cancerguard MCED optionality), and the NEC litigation resolves via a manageable global settlement that markets can quantify and de-risk. Deleveraging proceeds, buybacks resume in 2027, and the multiple re-rates toward the high-growth-diversified peer set. Adjusted EPS compounds low-double-digits toward $6.50+ in 2027, supporting a high-$120s to $135 valuation at ~20x.

**Bear Case (~$82, -19%):** NEC state-court verdicts continue to compound (the upheld $495M Missouri case plus new verdicts), forcing a large reserve or a multi-billion-dollar settlement against a $0 base, and/or the DOJ/qui tam matters produce material penalties. CGM competition erodes Libre pricing/share, Diagnostics core decline outpaces the Exact ramp, and higher rates raise the cost of servicing $34B of debt while goodwill impairment risk surfaces. The stock retests the 52-week low near $82.

**Base Case (~$113, +12%):** Mid-single-digit organic growth, adjusted EPS toward the top of the guide in 2026 and ~$6.07 in 2027, Exact integration on track, and NEC litigation remaining an overhang without a defining resolution. ~18.5x the $6.07 next-year estimate yields ~$113.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $113** (~18.5x next-year consensus EPS of $6.07), representing ~12% upside from $100.68 over a 12-month horizon.

**Bull case target: $135** (~+34%), on litigation de-risking plus multiple re-rating.

**Bear case target: $82** (~-19%), on adverse litigation escalation or CGM erosion, a retest of the 52-week low.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next several quarterly prints (integration and CGM data points) and the cadence of NEC litigation developments.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A quantifiable NEC resolution — a global settlement or reserve that caps the tail at a market-digestible number, removing the $0-reserve overhang.
- Continued Exact Sciences accretion evidence with Diagnostics organic growth turning positive, plus FreeStyle Libre holding share against Dexcom (no pricing erosion in reported numbers).
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating meaningfully (current-year revisions moving well above the recent +0.5%/90d).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A large new adverse NEC verdict or a DOJ/qui tam penalty that forces a multi-billion-dollar reserve, materially impairing the balance sheet.
- CGM pricing/share erosion visible in Diabetes Care segment results, or a goodwill impairment charge on Exact Sciences.
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the $5.45 floor, signaling operational deterioration rather than litigation noise.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 2026, late October) and any material NEC verdict/appeal or DOJ development.

## Conclusion

Abbott's operating business is in good shape: a device-led franchise compounding at double digits, a Q2 2026 beat, a raised full-year adjusted EPS guide, and an Exact Sciences integration running ahead of plan that gives the company a fourth secular-growth leg in cancer diagnostics. Management's capital discipline — pausing buybacks to deleverage while sustaining the 53-year dividend-increase streak — is appropriate, and the open-market director purchases near the lows (Starks at $92.65, Stratton at $86.82) are a conviction signal from medtech veterans.

The offsetting reality is a much larger, all-debt-funded balance sheet ($34B total debt, $35.2B goodwill) and an unreserved NEC litigation tail that management now warns "could be material," with state-court juries turning hostile. After the recovery from $82 to $101 on the guidance raise, the forward multiple (~16.6x next-year) is reasonable but no longer cheap enough to compensate for the specific, two-sided litigation catalyst. Health Care selection has historically added little alpha, and there is no distinct company catalyst here that clears a higher bar for a BUY absent litigation clarity.

We rate ABT a **HOLD** with a positive growth bias. The stock is worth owning for the device-and-diagnostics growth trajectory and the dividend, but the risk/reward is balanced until the NEC overhang is quantified. We would upgrade to BUY on a market-digestible litigation resolution combined with continued Exact Sciences accretion, and downgrade to AVOID on a large adverse verdict forcing a multi-billion-dollar reserve.
