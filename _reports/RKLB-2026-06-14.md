**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rocket Lab is one of two Western companies that have scaled orbital launch, and the operational data are strong. Revenue grew 63% YoY in Q1 2026 to a record $200.3 million, gross margin expanded to 38.2% from the high-20s a year earlier, backlog reached $2.22 billion (up ~108% YoY), and the company holds roughly $1.48 billion in liquidity after a year of aggressive equity raising. The business has transformed from a small-launch provider into a space-systems and defense-payload prime, with Space Systems now ~67% of revenue and U.S. government exposure rising from 33% to 47% in a single year. On business quality alone, this is a high-conviction name.

The problem is price. At roughly $60 billion market cap on ~$600 million trailing revenue, RKLB trades at approximately 100x EV/sales with a -38% operating margin, a -53% FCF margin, and a per-share earnings figure that is and will remain negative for the foreseeable horizon. Forward EPS estimates are falling: next-year consensus collapsed from a positive $0.01 ninety days ago to -$0.04 now. The valuation embeds years of flawless hyper-growth and successful Neutron execution. The Neutron Stage-1 tank ruptured during a January 2026 qualification test, pushing first launch to a Q4 2026 target that management itself flags as at-risk. This is the dominant fundamental wildcard.

The stock has appreciated ~288% over twelve months and is now ~32% off its late-May high of $151, with options markets pricing an implied ±11% near-term move and term-structure backwardation signaling expected stress. The call is HOLD. The business merits patience through the Neutron milestone, but the multiple offers no margin of safety and the funding model is structurally dependent on the share price staying elevated. I would neither chase here nor short a name executing this well into a sector-wide flow tailwind.

## Company Overview

Rocket Lab is a vertically integrated, end-to-end space company headquartered in Long Beach, California, with operations in the U.S., New Zealand, Canada, and now Germany. It reports two segments:

**Launch Services** centers on the Electron small-lift orbital rocket, the leading dedicated small launcher with 81 successful missions through Q1 2026 and the #2 most-frequently-launched orbital rocket globally in 2025. It also includes HASTE, a suborbital hypersonic test variant. The in-development Neutron medium-lift reusable vehicle (~13,000 kg to LEO) is the centerpiece of the growth thesis and is explicitly aimed at the constellation-deployment market dominated by SpaceX's Falcon 9.

**Space Systems** spans spacecraft manufacturing, components (reaction wheels, star trackers, radios, separation systems, solar cells, batteries), software, mission operations, and optical/laser communications. This is the larger and faster-growing segment, representing ~67% of 2025 revenue and ~$1.3 billion of the $2.2 billion backlog.

The strategic model is a vertical-integration roll-up: Sinclair Interplanetary (2020), Advanced Solutions and Planetary Systems (2021), SolAero (2022), GEOST ($290M, Aug 2025, EO/IR national-security payloads), Mynaric (~$155M, closed Apr 2026, laser optical comms and first European footprint), and Motiv Space Systems (up to $60M, signed May 2026, robotics). The defensible elements are a privately owned launch range at Mahia, New Zealand giving manifest control, flight-proven heritage, vertical integration into high-margin components, and deepening defense relationships.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating off a strong base.** The trajectory runs $211.0M (2022) → $244.6M (2023, +16%) → $436.2M (2024, +78%) → $601.8M (2025, +38%), with Q1 2026 hitting $200.3M (+63% YoY), the best quarter on record. Q1 bookings included 31 launch contracts plus 5 Neutron launches.

**Margins are improving but the business is deeply unprofitable.** Gross margin expanded from 21% (2023) to 26.6% (2024) to 34.4% (2025) to 38.2% (Q1 2026), reflecting the mix shift toward higher-margin Space Systems. But the operating loss was -$228.8M in 2025 (-38% margin), and the 2025 net loss was -$198.2M. Q1 2026 narrowed the operating loss to -$56.0M (-27.9%) from -$59.2M (-48.3%) a year earlier. R&D is the swing factor: $270.7M in 2025 (45% of revenue, +55% YoY), driven by Neutron, with $80.5M in Q1 2026 alone.

**The balance sheet has been materially strengthened through dilution.** Cash rose from $828.7M at year-end 2025 to $1.21B in Q1 2026, plus ~$271M in marketable securities for ~$1.48B total liquidity. The Altman Z-score of 3.22 sits in the safe zone, current ratio is 4.08, and D/E is only 0.35. The 4.25% 2029 convertible notes (conversion price ~$5.13) have largely converted to equity as the stock rose: outstanding principal fell from $355M to $155.7M (YE25) to just $37.6M by Q1 2026. Net debt is now minimal, but the de-leveraging mechanism is dilution: $118M of principal converted into 23M shares in Q1 2026 alone.

**Cash burn is heavy.** 2025 operating cash flow was -$165.5M, capex $156.3M, for FCF of -$321.8M (-53.5% margin). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was -$50.3M, capex $27.1M, with $130M of construction-in-process tied to Neutron and launch infrastructure.

**Funding is almost entirely equity.** 2025 financing brought $1.12B of net ATM proceeds; Q1 2026 added $445.6M more. A new $1.0B equity distribution program signed in March 2026 uses forward-sale and collared-forward structures, with 7.45M shares already sold via collared forwards. Diluted shares grew from ~496M (2024) to ~605M weighted-average in Q1 2026, over 20% share-count growth in roughly a year. This is intelligent capital management while the stock is high, but it makes shareholders structurally dependent on a high share price. If the stock falls sharply, the funding flywheel slows precisely when Neutron still needs capital.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is genuine and multi-pronged. Revenue is compounding above 50% with margin expansion, the combination that matters most. Backlog provides forward visibility, with ~36% expected to convert within twelve months. The transformative win was the December 2025 SDA Tracking Layer Tranche 3 agreement to build and operate 18 satellites for a total potential value of $816M (base $806M), delivering in 2029.

The Space Systems pivot rides a real structural tailwind: the Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture, national-security payload demand (GEOST), and rising U.S. space spending. Defense-driven demand here is distinct from discretionary commercial cycles and is supported across multiple government programs rather than a single contract. The acquisition cadence (Mynaric for laser comms, Motiv for robotics, GEOST for EO/IR) is building vertical capability into bottlenecked constellation supply chains.

Neutron is the larger prize and the larger uncertainty. A working, scalable, reusable medium-lift vehicle would open the constellation-deployment market and re-rate the business meaningfully. But it is not yet flying, and management flags continued schedule risk on the Q4 2026 target.

I weight the estimate-revision data heavily here, and it cuts against the near-term growth narrative. Current-year EPS estimates fell 15.2% over 90 days; next-quarter estimates fell 27.9%; next-year estimates went negative. This is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated turn. Revenue is accelerating, but the path to profitability is being pushed out, not pulled in. The growth is real; the timeline to it mattering for the bottom line is lengthening.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis breaks down for new capital. Third-party estimates put EV/sales at ~124x trailing, compressing to ~37x by 2028 only if execution is flawless. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative and meaningless given the loss profile.

I have applied the cycle-adjusted framework deliberately and it does not rescue the multiple. The principle that trailing P/E is meaningless at a trough applies when forward estimates are rising into the turn; here forward EPS estimates are falling, and the company will not generate positive EPS or FCF on any near-term horizon. There is no normalized earnings power to anchor to yet; profitability is a 2028+ event contingent on Neutron scaling.

The peer comparison underscores the gap. Aerospace and Defense peers trade at a median P/E of 33.9 (LHX 33.9, GD 22.6, NOC 17.3, TDG 39.3) on profitable, cash-generative businesses. The only peer in the cohort with a comparable optionality premium is Axon at 179x, and even Axon is profitable. RKLB is being valued on a SpaceX-proxy and pre-revenue-Neutron basis, not on its current financials.

Analyst price targets are informative here because of the premium multiple. When a stock at this valuation trades near consensus, the target reflects the same growth extrapolation as the price, not independent confirmation. The mean target of $105.28 and median of $102.50 sit essentially at the current $102.39, with a low of $60 (-41%) and high of $150 (+46%). The wide dispersion captures the binary nature of the Neutron outcome. Deutsche Bank's raise to $120 from $73 in May tracked the price up rather than leading it. At this multiple, beats are expected (no re-rating) and misses are punished (sharp de-rating), an asymmetric setup that argues for a low valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Rocket Lab's competitive position is improving and the trajectory is what matters. It is the clear #2 Western launch player behind SpaceX and the dominant dedicated small-launch provider, with Electron's flight heritage (81 successful missions) creating a reliability moat that is genuinely hard to replicate. The vertical-integration strategy is widening the moat into high-margin components and into the constellation supply chain, where Mynaric's laser comms addresses a known bottleneck.

The gross-margin trajectory (21% → 38% over three years) is direct evidence of strengthening pricing power and mix, and it outpaces what a commoditizing position would produce. The defense pivot positions RKLB as a quasi-prime in a multi-front demand environment where U.S. space spending is rising structurally.

The competitive risks are concentration-driven rather than displacement-driven. Top-5 customers are ~49% of revenue and ~77% of backlog; Kratos alone is 21% of receivables. All U.S. government contracts are fixed-price, so RKLB bears cost-overrun risk, already evidenced by a $7.9M downward catch-up in 2025, potential liquidated damages on the MDA 17-bus contract, and a $4.8M loss provision on a legacy SolAero contract. The October 2025 government shutdown already delayed awards, payments, and licensing. The business is becoming a fixed-price defense contractor layered under a high-multiple launch story, which adds budget-cycle and execution exposure that the headline backlog figure obscures.

The SpaceX IPO narrative is a meaningful sentiment driver: RKLB has been the closest liquid proxy retail had to owning SpaceX, and an eventual SpaceX listing is argued to lift the sector. This is a flow tailwind, not a fundamental one, and it cuts both ways: a SpaceX listing could also drain the proxy premium out of RKLB.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific, material, and only partly priced, which is why this dimension scores low.

**Neutron execution is the central risk.** Management explicitly flags continued timeline risk on the Q4 2026 first launch. Slippage is the single most likely catalyst for a de-rating given how much of the valuation depends on Neutron working and then scaling to reliable, high-cadence, reusable production.

**Valuation and dilution risk compound each other.** Any disappointment derates the multiple sharply, and the funding model depends on a high share price. A sharp drop slows the equity flywheel exactly when capital is needed. The share-count growth is structural dilution that continues under the new $1.0B distribution program.

**Concentration and fixed-price exposure** (77% of backlog in five customers, all government contracts fixed-price) create reversal and cost-overrun risk. **Key-person risk** is acute and candidly disclosed: founder Peter Beck is described in filings as the source of most ideas, participates in high-risk activities, and the company carries no key-person life insurance. He controls the company via ~46M shares of super-voting Series A Preferred. A **Department of Commerce funding agreement restricts buybacks and dividends** without approval.

The securities class action alleging overstated Neutron progress was dismissed with prejudice on April 16, 2026, removing a legal overhang while leaving the execution risk fully live. A June 5, 2026 8-K flagged an officer/director departure whose details warrant follow-up.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 109.8% versus 91.3% one-year historical vol, rich at the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (109.8% near-term vs 95.8% six-month). The nearest expiry implies a ±11.2% move. Near-term IV spikes during volatile single-name regimes overstate resolution, and term structure is the primary tool; the backwardation here is consistent with a known near-term catalyst (Nasdaq-100 inclusion flows, the broad insider selling window, and Neutron milestone proximity) rather than a durable directional view.

Put/call skew is balanced (-1.9% near-term, widening to -5.2% at six months, modest downside hedging further out). The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.65 to 1.04 as the price fell 15.5%, indicating some defensive repositioning. Unusual activity is heavily call-skewed at upside strikes ($120 and $125 calls for June expiry at 4-18x normal volume), suggesting speculative upside positioning persists despite the pullback. Short interest is low at 5.5% of float with 1.1 days to cover; this is not a crowded short, and the absence of heavy bearish positioning is itself a caution against any short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Neutron debuts successfully in Q4 2026 and scales toward reliable cadence through 2027, opening the constellation-deployment market and validating the medium-lift thesis. Space Systems continues compounding above 40% on the SDA Tranche 3 ramp and the GEOST/Mynaric/Motiv integration, gross margins push toward the mid-40s, and the path to profitability shortens. The defense pivot makes RKLB a structural beneficiary of rising U.S. space spending. A SpaceX listing re-rates the entire sector. The stock approaches the $150 analyst high (+46%) and beyond if 2028 EV/sales compression toward ~37x is achieved on flawless execution.

### Bear Case

Neutron slips beyond Q4 2026, plausible given the tank failure, and the market reprices the optionality premium. Fixed-price cost overruns and government budget volatility pressure margins. The dilution flywheel stalls if the stock falls, forcing capital raises at lower prices precisely when Neutron consumes cash. The SpaceX listing drains the proxy premium rather than lifting RKLB. Forward estimates continue falling. The stock retraces toward the $60 analyst low (-41%), consistent with a multiple compressing from ~100x toward a still-rich but more defensible level on disappointed growth.

The gap between these cases is almost entirely about how much future is already priced in, not about whether the business is good.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business quality argues for ownership, the valuation argues against adding, and the binary Neutron outcome means I lack the evidence for a confident directional call at this price. Existing holders should retain through the Neutron milestone; new capital lacks an asymmetric entry here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A successful Neutron first launch (Q4 2026 target) that validates the medium-lift thesis and de-risks the largest single uncertainty.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s to low-$70s (the $60 analyst low to a level where EV/sales compresses meaningfully) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety.
- A turn in the EPS estimate-revision trend from negative to positive, signaling the path to profitability is shortening rather than lengthening.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Neutron first launch slips materially beyond Q4 2026, or a second qualification failure.
- Gross margin reverses below ~32%, signaling the mix-shift thesis is stalling.
- A fixed-price contract overrun or liquidated-damages event (MDA 17-bus is the named risk) that materially impairs Space Systems margins.
- Backlog conversion stalls or backlog declines, indicating demand softening behind the headline figure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, then the Neutron first-launch event in Q4 2026, the decisive fundamental binary for the name.

## Conclusion

Rocket Lab is executing as well as any company in its sector: accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a record backlog, a strengthened balance sheet, and a credible transformation into a defense-and-space-systems prime with genuine vertical-integration advantages. The business deserves a high competitive and growth score, and the founder's voluntary forfeiture of ~$11M in unvested RSUs in March 2026 is a real shareholder-aligned signal.

What it does not deserve at $102 is new capital. The stock trades near 100x EV/sales with deep losses, a -53% FCF margin, falling forward EPS estimates, over 20% annual dilution, and a valuation that prices flawless Neutron execution before the vehicle has flown. The January tank failure keeps that execution risk live, and the analyst targets cluster at the current price because the optionality is binary. The prudent stance is to hold the operational story while respecting that the price embeds the bull case, and to wait for either Neutron de-risking or a valuation reset before treating this as a buy. Shorting a name executing this well into a sector flow tailwind, with low short interest and balanced options skew, is not warranted either.
