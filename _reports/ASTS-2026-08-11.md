## Executive Summary

AST SpaceMobile is delivering cellular broadband from low Earth orbit directly to unmodified smartphones, inside mobile network operators' own licensed low-band spectrum, with traffic terminating on the operators' existing ground infrastructure. The supporting assets are real and verifiable: FCC commercial authority granted 2026-04-22 to operate in 700/800 MHz partner spectrum in coordination with Verizon, AT&T and FirstNet; authorization for a 248-satellite network; 60-plus MNO partnerships covering more than 3 billion subscribers, including definitive commercial agreements with AT&T and Verizon; 13 spacecraft in orbit with production running through BlueBird 46; and roughly $3.3B of unrestricted pro forma liquidity against modest net debt of about $430M.

The problem is what happened to the deployment path in 2026 and what the market is paying for it. BlueBird 7 was placed in an unrecoverable orbit by a New Glenn upper stage on 2026-04-19 and de-orbited, producing a $125.9M loss net of $32.5M of insurance (gross approximately $158.4M for one satellite plus its launch). New Glenn itself was then destroyed on the pad at LC-36 on 2026-05-28 during a pre-launch engine test. The FY2025 10-K target of 45-60 Block 2 satellites by end-2026 became "approximately 45 BB satellites in early 2027" in the Q2 10-Q — a target that includes the five Block 1 units, so roughly 40 Block 2, pushed one to two quarters. Commercial service slipped to 2027. Weeks after signaling adequate funding, the company raised $1.15B of 1.625% converts and stated it needed to "secure additional access to orbit." Falling back to Falcon 9 means more launches per satellite delivered, at higher per-satellite launch cost, purchased from the company that is simultaneously pivoting into retail direct-to-consumer wireless.

Using total economic units rather than the Class A count alone, equity value is approximately $27.3B and enterprise value approximately $27.8B — 139x to 185x FY2026 revenue guidance of $150-200M, on revenue that is roughly four-fifths low-margin gateway hardware (Q2 product gross margin 8.3%) and government milestones, with 21.1% of first-half revenue coming from AST's own 50/50 Vodafone joint venture. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 62.5% in 90 days; the company has missed EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters, by 54% to 189%. There is no margin of safety at this price for a company whose per-satellite capital cost, launch access, and Ligado spectrum closing are all unresolved.

## Company Overview

AST deploys very large phased-array apertures rather than small satellites with narrow beams — approximately 20,000 square feet of combined aperture across the 13 spacecraft now in orbit, which management describes as the largest phased arrays ever placed in LEO. The architecture is the differentiator and the source of the capital intensity.

The commercial model is partner-first rather than carrier-competitive. AST does not intend to sell retail service under its own brand; it integrates with MNOs, operates in their licensed low-band terrestrial spectrum under supplemental-coverage-from-space frameworks, and expects revenue primarily from revenue-sharing. Claimed moats per the 10-K: 38 patent families with roughly 3,900 claims (about 2,100 granted or allowed, earliest expirations 2039); vertically integrated assembly, integration and test with capacity for up to six Block 2 satellites per month; the proprietary AST5000 ASIC providing up to 40 MHz per beam and up to 10 GHz of processing bandwidth; and a spectrum position combining shared MNO spectrum with controlled MSS spectrum targeting roughly 100 MHz of U.S. access.

Three revenue lines exist today and none is the SpaceMobile service, which has not launched commercially and has generated no revenue: gateway equipment sales to partners, U.S. Government services and milestones, and (from 2026) sales into the Vodafone JV.

The capital structure is an Up-C. AST SpaceMobile, Inc. holds only equity in AST LLC; legacy holders own LLC units exchangeable 1-for-1 into Class A and hold non-economic Class B/C voting shares. Non-controlling interest was 23.0% of AST LLC at 6/30/26. Chairman and CEO Abel Avellan controlled 72.0% of voting power; the company is a "controlled company" without a majority-independent board, and debt covenants require his retention as Chairman and CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Start with the share count.** The 10-Q shows 299,731,073 Class A shares at 6/30/26 plus 11,215,111 Class B and 78,163,078 Class C non-economic voting shares whose holders own exchangeable LLC units. Total economic units are therefore 389,109,262, and 89,378,189 ÷ 389,109,262 = 22.97%, reconciling to the disclosed 23.0% NCI. At $70.24 that is approximately **$27.3B of economic equity value**, roughly 31% above the $20.9B Class-A-only headline. Adding $4,172.2M of pro forma debt and deducting $3,742.6M of pro forma cash gives enterprise value of approximately **$27.8B**.

**Revenue is growing while revenue quality declines.**

| FY2024 | $4.4M | — | — |
| FY2025 | $70.9M | $44.4M | $26.5M |
| Q1 2026 | $14.7M | $13.4M | $1.3M |
| Q2 2026 | $31.5M | $24.4M | $7.1M |

Product gross margin fell from 25.7% in FY2025 to 11.6% in H1 2026 to **8.3% in Q2 2026**. Services (predominantly U.S. Government) carried a 79.2% H1 margin, the high-quality line. H1 product revenue included $9.77M of related-party sales to the Vodafone JV: 25.8% of product revenue and 21.1% of total revenue, with intra-entity profit eliminated through an increased equity-method loss. AST sells partners (and a vehicle it half-owns) the ground hardware they must install before AST's own service can operate, at close to cost. That may be a deliberate commercial choice to accelerate partner readiness; it is not evidence of the high-margin, revenue-shared service economics the equity story requires.

**The guidance bar is demanding.** FY2026 guidance of $150-200M was reaffirmed on 2026-08-10. H1 actual of $46.255M implies H2 revenue of $103.7M-$153.7M, or 2.24x-3.32x the first half. Even the low end requires an H2 quarterly average of about $51.9M against Q2's $31.5M; the high end requires roughly $76.9M per quarter. Government milestones and hardware shipments are lumpy, so this is achievable in principle, but it depends on awards and deliveries that have not been contracted-and-dated in public disclosure.

**Losses and their composition.** H1 2026 net loss before NCI was $549.5M; loss attributable to common stockholders was $421.9M, or $1.43 per share. Accumulated deficit moved from $831.685M to $1,253.606M — exactly $421.921M, internally consistent. Q2 operating expense of $329.1M was double Q1's $164.1M, driven by the $125.9M BB7 loss, $63.5M of stock-based compensation (H1 SBC $118.8M expensed plus $4.1M capitalized, versus $18.4M in H1 2025, a 6.5x increase), and engineering services of $87.3M versus $28.6M a year earlier. Management's adjusted operating expense excluding adjusted cost of revenues rose from $79.8M in Q1 to **$95.9M in Q2**, a 20% sequential increase in the underlying cash cost base. Below the line, H1 other expense of $103.4M was dominated by $88.7M of induced conversion expense on the February note repurchases, almost all in Q1.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026: operating −$145.2M; investing −$979.7M (capex $859.2M, Ligado capital advances $100.0M, spectrum intangibles $42.1M, offset by $21.6M of BB7 insurance proceeds); financing +$1,068.3M. Combined operating-plus-investing outflow was **$1,124.9M in six months**, roughly $187M per month. That figure should be read as a sensitivity, not a forecast: capex includes advance launch payments and milestone-driven materials, and working capital swung (receivables −$41.6M, inventory −$16.4M, against +$45.6M payables and +$40.0M contract liabilities). A normalized alternative: approximately $384M/year of cash opex (Q2 adjusted opex ex-COGS annualized) plus rising cash interest (H1 cash interest paid $23.6M) plus the ≥$80M/year Ligado L-band obligation, on top of a satellite build-and-launch program whose annual cost is a direct function of launch cadence.

One caution on the prior year: FY2025 operating cash flow of −$71.5M was flattered by an approximately $175M STC advance recorded as a working-capital inflow. Customer prepayments are financing in economic substance; contract liabilities stood at $266.9M at 6/30/26 ($14.8M current, $252.1M non-current).

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $5,854.9M, liabilities $3,457.3M, equity $2,397.6M including $503.6M NCI. Debt at 6/30/26 was $3,022.2M face across converts struck at $26.99, $72.07, unspecified, and $116.30, a $420M UBS bridge at SOFR+2.0% due 2028, and a $48.6M Trinity equipment loan. The July 2026 issuance of $1,150M of 1.625% converts due 2034 (conversion price $79.57, net proceeds $1,131.2M, $111.4M spent on capped calls with a $149.20 cap) brings pro forma debt to $4,172.2M and total cash including restricted to $3,742.6M, reconciling to management's ">$3.7 billion" figure. Pro forma net debt is approximately $430M.

Two liquidity qualifications matter. $428.4M of restricted cash is collateral posted at 102% against the UBS bridge and is unavailable for operations, leaving roughly **$3,314M unrestricted**. And the $550M Sound Point delayed-draw facility remains entirely undrawn: drawing it requires FCC approval of the Ligado transaction, carries SOFR+8.0% plus a 3% upfront fee, is non-recourse to AST SpaceMobile and AST LLC, and **availability expires 2026-10-05** absent a 1%-fee, 180-day extension.

The FY2025 Altman Z-Score of 1.67 sits nominally in the distress zone. Read correctly, it is depressed because retained-earnings-to-assets and EBIT-to-assets are structurally negative and sales-to-assets is near zero by design on a $5.9B asset base. It does not signal imminent insolvency given $3.3B of unrestricted cash. What it accurately flags is that solvency depends on continued capital-markets access rather than operations, and the company has drawn on that access repeatedly and heavily.

## Growth Analysis

The addressable opportunity is not in dispute. Direct-to-device connectivity for standard handsets addresses coverage gaps for billions of subscribers, and the demand signals from the carrier side are concrete: definitive agreements with AT&T and Verizon, a proposed direct-to-device joint venture among the three largest U.S. carriers, and Japan's J-LEO initiative with an explicit sovereignty motivation. AST reports approximately $1.30B of aggregate contracted revenue and government awards, roughly 50 gateways in various stages, about 3,000 digital cells activated across the continental U.S., and more than $125M of cumulative U.S. Government awards.

Three qualifications materially reduce what that converts into near-term earnings power.

First, the backlog measures are not interchangeable. The $1.30B is a management non-GAAP aggregate; the 10-Q's ASC 606 remaining performance obligation is approximately $1.2B, of which only **6.6% (~$79M) is expected within twelve months**, and RPO explicitly excludes constrained variable consideration from MNO revenue sharing. The exclusion cuts both ways — eventual contracted value from revenue-share could be understated — but it also means almost none of the headline backlog reflects committed subscriber revenue, and 93.4% sits beyond a year.

Second, attribution on the two most-cited growth catalysts is looser than the headlines suggest. The U.S. carrier JV is being formed **by** AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile; AST issued a release "commending" it and is not a disclosed party, owner or contractual counterparty, and no definitive agreement with such a JV appears in the 10-Q. Reuters confirmed that Japan will grant up to ¥148 billion (~$912M) **to Rakuten Group**, with an expected roughly 50/50 satellite-operations JV; AST's participation would most plausibly come via satellite sales into that JV plus its equity share, and AST itself characterizes the selection as preliminary and pending government approvals and final agreements.

Third, the schedule moved against the disclosed coverage thresholds. Those thresholds are approximately 25 satellites for non-continuous service in attractive markets, 45-60 for continuous U.S./Europe/Japan service, and about 90 for all targeted markets. With 13 in orbit and the ~45 milestone now in early 2027, continuous U.S. coverage remains distant. Beta service in 2026 is still targeted but is explicitly non-commercial and with select strategic partners, and coverage gaps at 13 satellites will span hours.

Consensus reflects all of this. Current-year EPS estimates moved from −$1.42 (90 days ago) to −$1.51 (30 days) to −$2.30; next-year from −$0.52 to −$0.65 to −$0.98. Those are 62.5% and 88.3% ninety-day deteriorations. The estimate-revision signal is present with the opposite sign to what would support aggressive positioning.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no earnings-based anchor: trailing and forward P/E are both negative (−40.3x on −$1.34 EPS), and consensus does not model profitability inside the estimate window. Valuation therefore has to rest on revenue multiples and scenario work.

On the corrected economic base, $27.8B of enterprise value against FY2026 guidance implies **185x at the $150M low end, 159x at the $175M midpoint, and 139x at the $200M high end**. The composition matters more than the multiple: roughly 82% of H1 revenue was products at an 8.3% Q2 gross margin, and 21.1% of H1 revenue came from a half-owned affiliate with intra-entity profit eliminated. The company is being valued on a service that has not launched, using capacity that will not reach continuous-coverage scale before 2027 at the earliest.

The most useful valuation lens is the funding-adequacy question, because it determines how much of the eventual enterprise accrues to today's shareholders. Going from 13 to approximately 90 satellites requires roughly 77 more units. At AST's own $21-23M all-in guidance that is $1.62-1.77B, comfortably covered by $3.31B of unrestricted cash net of roughly $384M/year of cash opex plus ≥$80M/year of Ligado payments and rising cash interest. At a blended $33-42M all-in — using an external, non-company-sourced Falcon 9 estimate of $55-65M for three satellites plus a $15-20M build cost — the requirement is $2.54-3.23B and the cushion largely disappears. BB7's approximately $158.4M gross write-off for one satellite plus its launch is a single, plausibly unrepresentative data point (first-article costs, allocated non-recurring engineering, a dedicated rather than shared launch, and AST warns initial launches run above the range). But it and the external launch estimate are the only two observable anchors, and both point the same direction. The decisive disclosure — actual capitalized cost per satellite for the BB8-BB13 cohort — has not been made.

Dilution channels compound the per-share arithmetic. AST retired $706.5M of convertible principal by issuing roughly 23.6M shares across five transactions totaling about $1,625M of consideration, with $89.8M of H1 induced conversion expense. The $800M ATM was exhausted and terminated in March 2026. Anti-dilution exclusions at 6/30/26 comprise 89.4M Class B/C-linked units, 12.16M shares from equity awards and 25.82M from convertible conversion — before the 2034 notes. Management's "less than 2% effective dilution" framing for the 2034 notes uses the **capped call cap of $149.20** ($1,150M ÷ $149.20 = 7.71M shares, 1.98% of 389.1M units), not the $79.57 conversion price, at which noteholders could receive up to 14.45M shares with the capped-call offset provided by financial counterparties between $79.57 and $149.20. Aggregating contingent instruments (award vesting is service- and performance-conditioned; conversion depends on price and AST's election to settle in cash or shares) gives approximately 441.5M units, leaving current holders at 88.1% of the post-issuance base — 11.9% ownership dilution. These belong in scenarios rather than in today's capitalization, but they are the reason a correct enterprise value is 31% above the screen figure.

Analyst targets span $42.50 to $108.00, mean $79.66, median $80.00, with a recommendation distribution of 1 strong buy, 3 buy, 7 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell. The mean sits 13.4% above the current price. On a pre-commercial company at 139-185x forward revenue, targets are not independent confirmation of value — they embed the same deployment and service-launch assumptions as the price, and the same assumptions that consensus has been cutting by 50%-plus per quarter. Treating them as a floor is not supportable here.

**Scenario framework (from $70.24):**
- **Bear $28** (−60%): all-in per-satellite cost lands materially above $23M, Blue Origin does not return to flight inside the guidance window, Ligado FCC approval slips past the Sound Point deadline, and the company funds the gap through equity or high-cost non-recourse debt. The 52-week low is $36.08, so this requires deterioration beyond what has already been priced once.
- **Base $60** (−15%): beta demonstrates workable throughput but with the expected coverage gaps, H2 revenue lands near the low end of guidance, the ~45-satellite milestone holds in early-to-mid 2027 on Falcon 9 at higher per-satellite launch cost, and one further capital raise occurs. Value accrues but ownership dilutes.
- **Bull $115** (+64%): Ligado closes with FCC approval, Blue Origin returns to flight by end-2026 restoring high-capacity launch, beta validates near-200 Mbps Block 2 throughput, J-LEO converts into satellite sales plus JV equity, and the funding plan holds at guided cost.

Probability-weighting 25% / 45% / 30% gives approximately $64, roughly 9% below the current price, with a dispersion of −60% to +64%. A mildly negative expected value with that spread, on binaries that cannot be underwritten from disclosed information, is the arithmetic case against owning it here.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position has two components moving in opposite directions.

**Strategic alignment improved.** SpaceX's pivot toward retail Starlink Mobile — with public claims that it will beat AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon — makes AST's partner-first, MNO-spectrum, traffic-stays-in-country model more attractive to carriers as a defensive counterweight. The proposed three-carrier JV and Japan's explicit desire not to depend on a single foreign provider are direct evidence of demand for a non-SpaceX alternative. AST's claimed peak Block 2 rates approaching 200 Mbps (with nearly 100 Mbps demonstrated on Block 1) against a Starlink direct-to-consumer offering currently characterized as texting-capable is a real product differentiator if it holds at scale. It remains a management assertion pending beta results.

**Structural position weakened.** SpaceX owns its launch vehicles; AST buys launch, and as of mid-2026 buys it from a supplier set that includes its most formidable competitor. Every BlueBird on a Falcon 9 is revenue for SpaceX. This asymmetry is what makes the "secure additional access to orbit" language economically rational and what makes launch-provider acquisition speculation plausible, though no 8-K Item 1.01 or 2.01 disclosing such a transaction has been filed, and that speculation should not be carried as fact.

The carrier JV is also not unambiguously positive. It aggregates demand and could simplify the route to national coverage, but it consolidates AST's three most important prospective U.S. customers into a single negotiating counterparty at precisely the moment AST needs service revenue. No definitive commercial terms with the JV are disclosed.

Trajectory on the observable margin data is negative: gateway gross margin compressed by roughly two-thirds in two quarters. That is consistent with a deliberate near-cost pricing strategy to accelerate partner readiness, and the government services line at 79.2% margin shows the company can price high-value work. The test is whether service revenue with the promised economics appears in 2027.

## Risk Assessment

**Launch access is now the binding constraint.** BB7's loss on New Glenn 3 and the vehicle's destruction on the pad six weeks later removed the high-capacity path around which the deployment plan and the $21-23M per-satellite cost guidance were built. Blue Origin targets return to flight by end-2026 with a redesigned pad, and AST is reportedly entitled to a replacement launch for BB7, so the constraint could ease inside the guidance window. Management said on the Q2 call it is "not betting" on New Glenn.

**Ligado: $520M paid, closing not achieved.** AST has paid $520M of a $550M contingent payment, plus a minimum $80M/year L-band Annual Payment and a Crown Castle Annual Payment (cash plus a 30% premium) that began accruing 2025-06-23, for spectrum it cannot yet use commercially. $239.2M is capitalized as advanced consideration for spectrum usage rights. The $100M paid in March 2026 was placed into escrow by Bankruptcy Court order dated 2026-04-02; closing requires satisfactory FCC approval, unobtained as of 2026-08-10; and Inmarsat's appeal remains pending in the District of Delaware. A Backstop Commitment from Ligado's financial sponsors is intended to refund the $520M if approvals are not obtained, but the sponsors' identity and capacity to perform are not disclosed. The 2026-04-22 FCC grant covers **partner MNO spectrum** and does not resolve the Ligado approval; conflating the two is the most common error on this name.

**Governance and control.** Avellan holds 72.0% of voting power through Class C shares carrying up to 88.31% of total voting power; AST is a controlled company without a majority-independent board; and debt covenants require his retention as Chairman and CEO, converting a succession event into a potential credit event. His 2026-06-22 Form 4 reports a "Forward contract (obligation to sell)" over 2,500,000 shares under transaction code J — roughly 3.2% of his position, establishing a future delivery or settlement obligation while preserving interim voting control. The economic terms (strike, floor, cap, tenor, upfront proceeds) are not established.

**Compensation and dilution.** FY2025 CEO compensation was $14.22M ($18.16M actually paid) against $8,995 reported in 2021; average other-NEO compensation was $7.30M. H1 2026 SBC of $118.8M is 2.6x H1 revenue, with $289.8M unrecognized over 2.4 years and $325M of grant-date RSU value issued in six months. Cash paid for employee taxes on stock compensation was $62.0M in H1 versus $6.0M a year earlier. The company-selected performance measure is revenue, which does not incentivize capital discipline, currently the scarcest resource. The liability-management sequence has a constructive reading: retiring $26.99-strike notes and replacing them with $72.07/$79.57/$116.30 strikes plus capped calls at coupons of 1.625-2.375% raised the effective dilution strike substantially for roughly 3.2M incremental shares of "sweetener." Whether that was accretive depends on the terminal share price.

**Estimate and delivery credibility.** Four consecutive EPS misses (−54.1%, −188.5%, −59.3%, −98.0%) alongside 50-88% ninety-day estimate cuts, a target pushed one to two quarters, and a $1.15B raise weeks after signaling adequate funding. Receivables grew from $37.7M to $79.3M in six months, outpacing revenue growth, worth monitoring for collection quality. Verizon's $45.0M prepayment triggered by the April approval was described as due but not received as of the 10-Q date.

**Other:** Israeli engineering operations represent ~1% of assets and ~5% of H1 operating expense. A Tax Receivable Agreement obligates 85% of realized tax savings to TRA holders (no liability recorded given the full valuation allowance), a real cash claim on any future path to taxable profitability.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 111.3% is essentially fair to 110.3% one-year realized (spread 0.9%), but sits in the 89th percentile within the current chain and the term structure is in backwardation (111.3% near versus 90.9% at six months); the market is pricing near-dated event risk rather than a directional view. Skew is call-heavy across every tenor (−4.7% at one week through −6.8% at six months) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.71. Put/call open interest has risen from 0.49 on 2026-06-04 to 0.91 as the price fell 34.5% from $106.83, so protection has been accumulated on the way down while flow remains call-tilted. The six-month tenor prices a ±48.5% implied move.

The combination of call-heavy skew, 19.9% short interest with only 3.1 days to cover, and 111% implied volatility is the least favorable configuration for expressing this view through short exposure: squeeze risk is material and carrying cost is high. For a fundamental investor the takeaway is that the market agrees the outcome distribution is extremely wide and does not lean bearish, which argues for absence rather than opposition.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($115, +64%).** Blue Origin returns to flight by end-2026, restoring the high-capacity launch path and the per-satellite cost curve; the BB8-BB13 cohort discloses capitalized cost inside $21-23M, confirming the "fully funded to ~90 satellites" claim; beta service in 2026 demonstrates near-200 Mbps throughput and validates the broadband-versus-messaging differentiation against Starlink; the FCC approves the Ligado transfer, unlocking L-band and the $550M Sound Point facility; H2 2026 revenue delivers the 2.2-3.3x sequential-half ramp on U.S. Government awards and gateway deliveries; the Rakuten J-LEO JV converts into satellite sales plus AST equity; and the carrier JV becomes an aggregated demand channel rather than a buyer-power problem. In that path, the company reaches continuous U.S. coverage in 2027-2028 with a service that no competitor can replicate inside carrier spectrum, and today's ~$27.8B enterprise value looks early.

**Bear case ($28, −60%).** All-in cost per satellite lands at $33-42M rather than $21-23M because launch consumes $18-22M per unit on Falcon 9; reaching 90 satellites then requires $2.5-3.2B against $3.3B of unrestricted cash net of $384M/year of cash opex, ≥$80M/year of Ligado payments and rising interest, forcing another raise at a lower price; Ligado FCC approval slips past the 2026-10-05 Sound Point deadline and the $520M paid becomes dependent on undisclosed sponsor backstop capacity while the Inmarsat appeal runs; beta reveals coverage gaps that delay carrier commercial launches into 2028; gateway margins stay in single digits and service revenue-share arrives later and smaller than modeled; and the equity absorbs the ownership dilution — 11.9% from disclosed contingent instruments alone before any new issuance.

The bear case does not require the technology to fail. It requires only that the cost per satellite, the launch cadence, and the regulatory calendar land at the pessimistic end of ranges the company has not disclosed enough information to bound.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Three dated items exist: the Sound Point facility's 2026-10-05 availability expiry (contingent on FCC approval of Ligado, unobtained); the Q3 2026 report in November, which is the first check on whether H2 revenue can deliver the required $51.9-76.9M quarterly run-rate against Q2's $31.5M; and any disclosure of BB8-BB13 capitalized cost per satellite. None is a hard, self-executing trigger — the facility can be extended for a 1% fee, and revenue timing is milestone-driven and lumpy.

**Timing window.** Two to four months to the Q3 print, longer for launch-cadence resolution. Blue Origin's end-2026 return-to-flight target is a bullish catalyst inside the same window, which cuts against short duration.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 19.9% of float short, 59.3M shares, only 3.1 days to cover. The position is already crowded, and the low days-to-cover means a positive catalyst can be covered quickly and violently. The stock rallied from $53.03 on 2026-07-29 to $71.94 on 2026-08-07 — a 36% move in seven sessions — without a change in the fundamental picture.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning does not confirm the bearish thesis: skew is call-heavy at every tenor and put/call volume is 0.71. Near-term IV of 111.3% makes long-put expression expensive, and 89th-percentile IV within the chain plus backwardation means paying up for near-dated protection. The six-month tenor prices ±48.5%, so a directional short faces roughly symmetric priced risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $28 (−60%); bull case $115 (+64%). At roughly 25/45/30 probabilities, reward-to-risk on a short is close to 1:1 before borrow cost, well below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish analysis is defensible, but 19.9% short interest with 3.1 days to cover, call-heavy options positioning, a competing bullish catalyst (Blue Origin return to flight) inside the same window, and 111% implied volatility make this an unattractive short. The correct action is absence, not opposition.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: AVOID.** Do not own or initiate at $70.24.

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move the rating to HOLD; all four with a price below $45 would support ACCUMULATE:**

1. **Disclosed capitalized cost per satellite for the BB8-BB13 cohort at or below $25M all-in.** This is the single most decisive missing number. It determines whether $3.31B of unrestricted cash funds the path to ~90 satellites with cushion ($1.62-1.77B requirement) or without ($2.54-3.23B).
2. **FCC approval closing the Ligado transaction**, converting $520M of paid-but-unusable consideration into commercially usable L-band and unlocking the $550M Sound Point facility (or a disclosed extension past 2026-10-05 with acceptable terms).
3. **Beta service results demonstrating throughput materially above messaging-class rates** with a dated carrier commercial-launch commitment, converting the 200 Mbps claim from assertion to evidence.

## Conclusion

AST SpaceMobile has assembled assets that are difficult to replicate: FCC commercial authority in premium partner low-band spectrum, definitive agreements with the two largest U.S. carriers, 60-plus MNO partnerships, a patent portfolio with 2039-plus expirations, vertically integrated satellite assembly, and 13 large-aperture spacecraft in orbit. Its partner-first positioning has become strategically more valuable as SpaceX moves toward competing with carriers directly.

None of that resolves the three questions that determine per-share outcomes from $70.24. What does a satellite actually cost all-in now that the high-capacity launch vehicle is out of service? Does the FCC approve a spectrum transfer on which $520M is already paid and $80M a year is accruing for unusable capacity? And does H2 2026 revenue triple sequentially as guided, or does the fourth consecutive quarterly miss become the fifth? At 139-185x FY2026 revenue guidance on an economic equity base 31% larger than the screen shows, with consensus current-year EPS cut 62.5% in 90 days, stock compensation at 2.6x revenue, zero open-market insider purchases against roughly $280M of insider and 10%-owner selling, and 11.9% of ownership dilution embedded in disclosed contingent instruments before any new issuance, the price requires the optimistic answer to all three.

The probability-weighted expected value is modestly below the current price with a −60% to +64% dispersion, and the binaries driving that dispersion are not underwritable from what has been disclosed. That combination argues for absence. The stock becomes interesting again when the per-satellite cost is disclosed and inside guidance, when Ligado closes, or at a price that pays for the uncertainty — the low-to-mid $40s, near the analyst low target of $42.50 and above the $36.08 fifty-two-week low.
