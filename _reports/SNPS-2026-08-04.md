## Executive Summary

Synopsys sits 37.3% below its August 2025 high of $636.61 and 9.1% above its July 17, 2026 low of $366.00. The drawdown has two distinct causes, and separating them is the analytical task. The first leg was a 36% single-day decline on September 10, 2025 driven by Design IP segment deterioration — adjusted segment operating margin fell from 38% in FY2024 to 24% in FY2025 and to roughly 16% in Q1 FY2026 — which produced three securities class actions and remains unresolved on a year-over-year basis. The second leg, from roughly $500 in late May to the current $399, coincides with the July 16–17 release of Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 and the associated fear that AI-native tooling compresses the value of seat- and network-license-based EDA revenue.

The fundamental picture between those two events did not deteriorate. FY2026 guidance has been raised twice — revenue to $9.625–9.705B and non-GAAP EPS to $14.72–14.80 from $14.38–14.46 at Q1 — and the company has beaten non-GAAP consensus in three of the last four quarters. Consensus EPS estimates rose 2.3% for the current year and 1.4% for next year over the past 90 days. Debt fell $3.45B in six months, the Ansys term loan was retired and its facility terminated, and $1.7B of a $2.0B repurchase authorization remains. An Elliott Investment Management partner, Jesse Cohn, joined the board effective June 1, 2026 with an explicitly stated agenda around profitability and monetization.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD is the gap between headline and organic performance, and the price of the cash. Q2 FY2026 revenue grew 42% year over year, but Ansys contributed $652.4M of the $2,276.0M total; combining management's ~8% EDA growth with Design IP at −6% implies organic EDA+IP growth of roughly 3.8%, and total organic revenue was approximately flat once the divested Optical Solutions Group is removed from the prior-year base. Non-GAAP EPS of $3.35 was down 8.7% from $3.67 a year earlier. At $399.28 the shares are 23.1x next-year consensus of $17.28 — genuinely below the 30.0x Application Software peer median and a wide discount to Cadence — but 42x EV/free cash flow on the $2.0B FY2026 guide, and roughly 37–38x once the ~$960M annualized stock-based compensation excluded from non-GAAP EPS is expensed. That combination supports owning the position for the Elliott catalyst and the forward multiple. It does not support adding capital two weeks before an August 26 print where guidance implies H2 free cash flow of only ~$604M and where the backlog declined sequentially to $11.0B.

## Company Overview

Synopsys sells the software used to design and verify integrated circuits, and since the July 17, 2025 close of the $34.9B ANSYS acquisition, the simulation and analysis software used to model the physical world those circuits operate in. Two reporting segments:

**Design Automation** (FY2025 revenue $5,302.4M, +26%) contains three revenue groups. EDA includes the digital implementation flow (Fusion Compiler, Design Compiler NXT, IC Compiler II), the signoff tools that foundries require for tapeout (PrimeTime static timing, StarRC extraction, IC Validator physical verification), verification software (VCS, Verdi, VC Formal), and physical hardware — ZeBu emulators and HAPS prototyping systems, which carry inventory and component-cost exposure atypical of software. The Synopsys.ai layer (DSO.ai, VSO.ai, Copilot) is positioned as augmenting that stack rather than as a standalone line. Ansys contributes Mechanical, LS-DYNA, Fluent, the HFSS electromagnetics family, and the semiconductor multiphysics signoff tools RedHawk-SC and Totem-SC.

**Design IP** (FY2025 revenue $1,751.8M, **−8%**) licenses pre-verified silicon blocks: interface IP (UCIe, HBM, PCIe, DDR/LPDDR, Ethernet), foundation IP, security IP, and embedded processors. The processor IP business is being divested to GlobalFoundries under a January 14, 2026 definitive agreement.

Roughly 28,000 employees, 75% engineers, more than half holding advanced degrees; voluntary turnover 5.7%. The FY2025 10-K states directly that the business "is not significantly dependent upon any single patent, copyright, trademark, or license." The moat rests on foundry certification of signoff tools at leading nodes, design-database and workflow continuity, and the cost of re-qualifying a signoff tool mid-program — not on IP protection.

### Revenue: the composition matters more than the rate

| Revenue | $6,127.4M | $7,054.2M | $4,684.8M |
| Growth | +15.2% | +15.1% | +53% |
| Design Automation | — | $5,302.4M (+26%) | $3,823.6M (+78%) |
| Design IP | — | $1,751.8M (−8%) | $861.2M (−6%) |
| GAAP operating margin | 22.1% | 13.0% | 6.9% |

Q2 FY2026 disclosed product-group mix was EDA 51.0%, Design IP 20.0%, Ansys 28.7%, Other 0.3% on $2,276.0M — implying EDA ≈ $1,160.8M and Design IP ≈ $455.2M. Management indicated on the May 27 call that EDA grew approximately 8% year over year. Combined with Design IP at −6%, organic EDA+IP growth computes to roughly +3.8% ($1,616.0M against an implied $1,556.8M). Adding back the loss of the Optical Solutions Group from the prior-year base leaves total organic revenue approximately flat. Two qualifications: the 8% EDA figure is transcript-sourced rather than filed, and quarterly EDA revenue is lumpy because of emulation hardware shipments and upfront license timing, so a single quarter may understate the underlying trend. The $12.5M from an Ansys channel accounting change to a gross basis also inflates the acquired contribution.

### The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is structural, not transitional

Q2 FY2026 GAAP operating margin was 5.3% against 39.5% non-GAAP. The ~34-point gap reconciles cleanly: acquired-intangible amortization $403.6M (17.7% of revenue), stock-based compensation $222.3M (9.8%), restructuring $115.9M (5.1%), and acquisition/deferred-comp items ~$37M (1.6%), summing to ~34.2%.

The amortization schedule against $11,875.4M of net acquired intangibles runs $805.7M for the remainder of FY2026, $1,545.1M in FY2027, and $1,376–1,385M annually through FY2030. FY2027 amortization alone will be roughly 15% of a $10B revenue base. GAAP EPS will remain suppressed through at least FY2030. This is non-cash and does not affect free cash flow or debt service, but it makes trailing GAAP multiples uninformative. Note a data conflict in the supplied fields: trailing and forward P/E are both reported as 92.86 against a stated EPS of $8.07, which cannot both be true (399.28/8.07 = 49.5x, and forward P/E on $14.76 guided non-GAAP EPS is 27.0x). Use forward non-GAAP or cash-based metrics here; trailing GAAP is not a usable comparator.

Stock-based compensation deserves separate treatment because it is a real economic cost that non-GAAP EPS excludes. H1 SBC of $481.0M annualizes to ~$962M, roughly $5.02 per diluted share pre-tax (on ~191.4M shares). On an SBC-expensed basis, FY2026 earnings power is closer to $10.50–10.75, putting the shares at 37–38x rather than 27x. The $1.7B remaining repurchase authorization offsets the dilution mechanically but consumes cash that would otherwise be free.

### Cash flow is high quality and heavily front-loaded

FY2025: operating cash flow $1,518.6M, capex $169.5M, FCF $1,349.2M (19.1% of revenue). H1 FY2026: OCF $1,485.8M, capex $89.5M, **FCF $1,396.3M**. FY2026 guidance is ~$2.0B, which implies H2 FCF of only ~$604M, or ~$302M per quarter against ~$698M per quarter in H1.

Do not read that implied slowdown as demand deterioration. Ansys' December quarter — Synopsys' fiscal Q1 — carries outsized upfront and perpetual license revenue. H1 Ansys revenue of ~$1.5B against Q2's $652.4M back-solves to roughly $848M in Q1, 30% larger than Q2. H1 "upfront products" revenue was $1,287.8M of which Q2 was only $546.3M. Deferred revenue also built from $2,628.5M to $2,809.3M over the six months. Cash-flow seasonality has been permanently reshaped; any model that annualizes a single Synopsys quarter will now be wrong in both directions.

The NVIDIA placement funded roughly 58% of the term-loan repayment.

Remaining structure is $10.0B of senior notes only, plus an undrawn $850M revolver. Maturities: FY2027 $1,024.8M, FY2028 $1,024.8M, FY2029 $24.8M, FY2030 $2,024.8M, 2031+ $6,000M. No wall.

GAAP interest coverage of 1.09x in H1 (operating income $323.5M / interest expense $296.1M) is an accounting artifact — adding back only non-cash acquired-intangible amortization of $807.9M gives 3.82x. Net debt of $7,551.6M against roughly $3.77B of implied FY2026 non-GAAP operating income (guided revenue $9,665M at ~39% margin) is ~2.0x. Solvency risk is low.

The genuine balance-sheet criticism is different: goodwill plus intangibles of $38,729M equals 82.6% of total assets, tangible book value is approximately negative $8.25B, and Synopsys has moved from net cash to $7.6B net debt. Mechanical screens (Altman Z ~1.9, asset turnover 0.146) are picking up purchase accounting rather than distress, but they correctly register that balance-sheet optionality has narrowed and that impairment exposure is now very large. Ansys purchase-accounting assumptions include royalty rates of 35–45% and customer retention of 85–105% direct / 70–90% indirect; actual post-close retention is not disclosed.

### Backlog

Backlog fell from $11.4B at October 31, 2025 (including $2.0B of non-cancellable flexible spending accounts) to $11.0B at April 30, 2026 (including $1.8B FSA). Ex-FSA: $9.4B to $9.2B, −2.1%. The portion expected within twelve months rose from ~45% to ~49%, implying near-term contracted revenue of ~$4.51B versus ~$4.23B, up 6.6%. Management attributed the total decline to renewal timing. Shortening customer commitment durations would produce the identical pattern and would be a mild negative for pricing durability; the disclosure does not distinguish. Backlog is also a compensation metric in the proxy and is management-defined, which argues for treating it as a directional rather than precise input.

## Growth Analysis

Three separable growth drivers, with different confidence levels.

**Core EDA (~51% of revenue), growing ~8%.** This tracks leading-edge design starts, which the AI accelerator and custom-silicon buildout supports directly. Advanced packaging and multi-die designs (3DIC Compiler) add tool intensity per design. Management cited hardware-assisted verification demand among the reasons for the guidance raise. The constraint is that EDA revenue scales with design programs and engineer seats, not with capex dollars — the elasticity is real but muted, and China design starts remain constrained. [Note: broader macro demand signals cited in the original draft — specific TSMC guidance figures, hyperscaler capex, and a Caterpillar sales target — are not present in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed as unverifiable.]

**Design IP (~20%), declining.** Revenue −8% in FY2025 and −6% in H1 FY2026, with segment adjusted margin at 24% after touching ~16%. Management stated IP "bottomed in Q1," supported by +12% sequential growth in Q2 but not yet by a year-over-year inflection. A return to even flat year-over-year IP revenue at 30%+ margins would be a meaningful earnings contributor; continued erosion would validate the September 2025 repricing.

**Ansys (~29%), the unmonetized synergy.** The stated thesis — that at 2.5D/3D packaging the electrical, thermal, and mechanical-stress domains become inseparable from the electronic design problem — is technically coherent, and the concrete instance (3DIC Compiler plus RedHawk-SC Electrothermal) exists. But multiphysics "fusion" products reach general availability only in 2H FY2026, meaning the core revenue synergy of a $34.9B acquisition is still pre-monetization roughly a year after close. Roughly half of committed Ansys synergies are expected to be realized by fiscal year-end. Ansys also brings channel partners for long-tail customers Synopsys' direct-sales model never reached, and end-market diversification into aerospace/defense, automotive, and industrials.

Consensus next-year EPS of $17.28 implies +16.8% growth off the $14.79 current-year figure. That growth does not come from demand acceleration — it comes from synergy capture, IP normalization, lower restructuring, and share count reduction. That is an execution forecast, and it is the reason the growth score sits at 6.3 rather than 7.0. Estimate revisions are stable and modestly positive (+2.3% current year, +1.4% next year over 90 days) — supportive, but nowhere near the +30% velocity that signals a systematically underestimated inflection.

## Competitive Landscape

EDA is a three-player structure — Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA — with in-house tooling at the largest chip designers as a persistent fourth force. Renewal rates on foundry-certified signoff tools have historically approached 100%, because the barrier is foundry qualification and liability, not code generation. Notably, the FY2025 10-K names no competitors and discloses no customer above 10% of revenue, and gives no quantified concentration figure.

**Trajectory, by piece.** Core EDA growing ~8% is roughly market-rate and probably slower than Cadence, whose multiple implies faster growth. Design IP is deteriorating in both revenue and margin, which is a share and pricing signal in a segment where Arm and specialist IP vendors compete directly. Ansys widens the addressable problem into multiphysics where Cadence has replicated part of the stack through its own acquisitions. The November 2025 hire of Mike Ellow as Chief Revenue Officer, directly from the CEO seat at Siemens EDA, is an unusually aggressive commercial move and implies the board diagnosed go-to-market execution rather than technology as the deficiency.

**The AI-native EDA question.** On July 16–17, 2026, Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model; both SNPS and CDNS sold off on EDA-disruption fears. A subsequent third-party analysis argues the demonstrated chip-design capability is limited to legacy 45nm nodes while Synopsys and Cadence operate at 3nm and 2nm. That claim rests on a single low-tier source and I could not verify it.

The distinction that matters is between share loss and pricing-model deflation. A general-purpose model generating a functional 45nm design does not threaten PrimeTime, StarRC, or IC Validator, because foundries require certified signoff tools for tapeout and accept liability accordingly. The more credible medium-term risk is that AI agents compress the number of engineer-seats required per design program, deflating volume on a seat- and network-license revenue model even if competitive share is fully retained. Management's own commentary is the tell: Ghazi has described a shift toward "subscription plus consumption models for AI agents," which reads as preparation to reprice on compute or outcomes rather than seats. Whether that transition is net-neutral, accretive, or dilutive is unknowable from current disclosure. A Forbes piece on August 3 profiling AI-native EDA startups warns specifically about a new pricing model.

This is the single largest unresolved item in the thesis, and it is the reason competitive position scores 6.3 rather than 7.0 despite an intact signoff moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Securities litigation, no accrual.** Three N.D. Cal. class actions allege misstatements about Design IP performance: *Kim v. Synopsys* (Oct 31, 2025), *New England Teamsters Pension Fund* (Nov 25, 2025), and *City of Sterling Heights Police & Fire* (Dec 30, 2025). Three derivative actions naming de Geus and Ghazi are consolidated and stayed. The *New England Teamsters* complaint additionally brings Securities Act §11/12(a)(2)/15 claims on behalf of former Ansys holders who received $17.1B of Synopsys stock in the merger. §11 does not require proving scienter — only a materially misleading registration statement — making it a materially lower bar than §10(b) with a large theoretical damages base. No loss has been accrued, which means management and auditors do not consider loss probable and estimable. Most such actions following a 36% single-day decline are dismissed or settled immaterially for a $76B company; the motion-to-dismiss outcome is the item to monitor.

**Cybersecurity disclosure escalation.** The Q2 FY2026 10-Q added language that Synopsys "discovered unauthorized third-party access to our and our customers' proprietary information in various incidents by cybercriminals suspected of being financially motivated and/or state-sponsored actors," believes them contained, and has notified affected customers, federal law enforcement, and regulators. It also newly discloses employees using non-approved applications that do not meet security standards. No material incident or expense has been identified. For a custodian of pre-tapeout chip designs, the disclosed fact pattern is more serious in reputational terms than the "not material" financial framing implies. The alternative reading — prophylactic drafting in response to heightened SEC cyber-disclosure enforcement — is equally consistent with the text, which provides no dates, scope, or customer counts.

**China and export controls.** FY2025 China revenue fell 22% excluding Ansys. A May 29, 2025 BIS "is-informed" letter requiring licenses for EDA software to China was rescinded July 2, 2025. Synopsys discloses receipt of BIS administrative subpoenas regarding transactions with certain Chinese entities. H1 FY2026 China revenue of $451.5M versus $331.5M includes Ansys and does not establish organic recovery; management said design-start conditions there remain constrained. China's indigenous EDA program is a separate structural threat.

**Impairment exposure.** $38.7B of goodwill and intangibles against negative $8.25B tangible book, with Ansys retention against the 85–105% / 70–90% purchase-accounting assumptions undisclosed.

**Integration and restructuring.** The 2026 Plan carries $300–350M of charges; $234.2M was recognized in H1, 67–78% front-loaded, and accelerated restructuring was cited as the reason Q2 GAAP costs exceeded expectations. H2 GAAP margins should mechanically improve.

**Hardware exposure.** ZeBu and HAPS are physical products with inventory and component-cost risk. Management cited hardware-assisted verification demand as a guidance-raise driver, which cuts both ways on cyclicality.

**Near-term event risk.** Q3 FY2026 reports August 26. All operating data in evidence ends April 30, 2026 — roughly four months before the current price. The move from ~$481 in late May to $399 is not explained by any filing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.8% sits 8.5 points below trailing one-year realized volatility of 62.3%, at the 32nd percentile within the current chain — near-term optionality is inexpensive relative to how the stock has actually moved. The term structure is backwardated (53.8% near versus 50.1% at six months), which the market typically uses to price a dated event; the September 11 tenor at 53.3% implies a ±14.2% move, straddling the August 26 print.

Positioning is put-heavy but easing. Put/call open interest is 1.77 and put/call volume 1.43, with an 8.0% near-term put skew indicating elevated put demand. Since the July 30 snapshot, ATM IV fell 9.3 points from 63.1% to 53.8% while the price rose 7.4% and the put/call OI ratio declined from 2.18 to 1.77 — protection being unwound into the bounce rather than accumulated. The one anomaly is a put/call volume ratio of 4.96 at the August 21 expiry, which sits before the earnings date and does not obviously map to the print.

Short interest is only 2.9% of float at 2.9 days to cover. The bearish view is not crowded, which cuts both ways: there is no squeeze fuel, and no evidence that sophisticated short capital has taken the AI-disruption thesis seriously in size.

Net read: the options market prices a specific August event, not a regime change, and near-term vol is cheap enough that owners wanting to hold through the print can hedge economically rather than reduce.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $518 (+29.7%)

Elliott's presence institutionalizes the margin agenda. Cohn's public language — "a clear opportunity for Synopsys's financial performance to more fully reflect the value it delivers" — is about profitability and monetization rather than strategy or break-up, which is the right diagnosis for a company running 39.5% non-GAAP operating margins with half its Ansys synergies unrealized. The proxy already used non-GAAP operating margin as a compensation metric, so the direction of travel predates the activist; the activist accelerates it and raises the probability of a formal margin target and larger repurchases.

Design IP recovers from the Q1 trough — the +12% sequential move in Q2 is the first supporting datapoint — restoring 30%+ segment margins on ~20% of revenue. Multiphysics fusion products reach general availability in 2H FY2026 and begin converting the $34.9B acquisition's stated rationale into revenue. Interest expense declines as the $10.0B note stack is chipped away by $2.0B of annual FCF, reversing the ~$210M pre-tax net-interest swing that drove Q2's non-GAAP EPS decline. Restructuring charges roll off, having been 67–78% front-loaded. The remaining $1.7B authorization retires ~2.2% of shares.

Consensus FY2027 EPS of $17.28 gets revised upward as synergies land, and the multiple re-rates from 23x toward Cadence's territory. 30x $17.28 = $518.

### Bear Case — $300 (−24.9%)

The market is correctly discounting three things. First, organic growth is mid-single-digit, not 40%: EDA +8%, IP −6%, total organic approximately flat. Paying 8.7x EV/revenue and 42x EV/FCF for that is not cheap. Second, non-GAAP EPS of $14.76 overstates economic earnings by roughly $4 per share of stock compensation; on an SBC-expensed basis the shares are 37–38x. Third, the seat-based license model faces volume deflation from AI agents regardless of competitive share, and management's own pivot toward "subscription plus consumption" concedes the model must change without demonstrating that the change is neutral or better.

Add the specifics: Design IP fails to inflect year over year, validating the September 2025 repricing and strengthening the §11 claim from Ansys holders. Ansys retention comes in below the 85–105% direct assumption, forcing impairment against $38.7B of intangibles on negative tangible book. Backlog erosion proves to be shortening commitment durations rather than renewal timing. China revenue stays depressed and BIS subpoenas escalate. On a cut FY2027 estimate near $15.50 at 19–20x, the stock trades $295–310, below the July low of $366.

### What the sellers are right and wrong about

A 38% drawdown means many informed holders have exited, so the bull case carries the burden of proof. Where I believe the sellers are correct: the organic-growth reality, the SBC gap, and the fact that the AI pricing-model question is genuinely unresolved and cannot be settled by a single low-tier claim about 45nm capability. Where I believe they are likely wrong: the implied H2 FCF collapse to ~$604M is Ansys seasonality — Q1 Ansys revenue back-solves to ~$848M against Q2's $652.4M, and H1 upfront products revenue was $1,287.8M of which Q2 was only $546.3M — rather than a demand signal; the GAAP-based distress screens are purchase accounting; and foundry-certified signoff tools at 2nm are not substitutable by a general-purpose language model. The balance of those two lists is what produces a HOLD rather than either a BUY or an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings, **August 26, 2026**. The September 11 options tenor prices a ±14.2% move around it. Secondary reassessment at the Investor Day, where an Elliott-influenced margin target would either materialize or fail to.

**Base case reference: $449 (+12.5%)** — 26x next-year consensus $17.28, a modest premium to the current 23.1x reflecting synergy delivery but a persistent discount to Cadence for the organic-growth and AI-model uncertainty.

### Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following

1. **Design IP inflects year over year** — Q3 FY2026 IP revenue positive versus the prior year (not merely sequentially), with segment adjusted operating margin at or above 28%. This is the single highest-value datapoint because it addresses both the earnings gap and the litigation premise.
2. **A quantified margin commitment.** A formal non-GAAP operating margin target above 42% by FY2028 disclosed at an Investor Day, or a repurchase authorization increase above $3.0B. Either converts the Elliott presence from a narrative into a modelled number.
3. **Free cash flow guidance raised above $2.1B for FY2026** with H2 tracking meaningfully ahead of the implied ~$604M, which would resolve the seasonality-versus-deterioration question and pull the 2.6% FCF yield toward a defensible level.
4. **Price toward $340–355** with the above unchanged, which would put the shares near 20x next-year consensus and restore asymmetry against the $300 bear case.

### Downgrade to AVOID triggers

1. **Design IP revenue declines again year over year in Q3 with margin below 20%**, or management withdraws the "bottomed in Q1" characterization.
2. **Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge**, or disclosure that Ansys customer retention is running below the 85% low end of the direct-retention purchase-accounting assumption.
3. **Evidence of pricing-model deflation in reported numbers** — a decline in the twelve-month-recognizable backlog slice below $4.3B, or an EDA revenue growth rate falling below 5% year over year, either of which would move the AI-disruption question from hypothesis to measurement.
4. **A quantified material cyber incident** affecting customer design data, or an accrual established against the securities litigation.
5. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $16.00**, which would break the 17% growth premise the current multiple rests on.

**Position guidance:** hold existing exposure. Do not add before August 26. Near-term implied volatility at 53.8% against 62.3% realized makes protective puts through the print inexpensive for holders who want to keep the Elliott optionality without carrying the full event risk.

Overall score 6.05, continuing a declining trajectory from 6.3.** The decline is not a response to the lower price — valuation improved from 5.5 to 6.0 precisely because the shares are ~12% cheaper on unchanged guidance. It reflects two substantive changes in the evidence: the organic-growth decomposition, which shows roughly 3.8% EDA+IP growth beneath a 42% headline and argues that a 7.0 growth score was too generous; and the July 2026 emergence of AI-native EDA as a live, unresolved risk to seat-based licensing, which lowers both competitive-position trajectory and the risk score.

What keeps this from being an AVOID is real. Guidance has been raised twice and beaten three of four quarters. Debt fell $3.45B in six months with the term loan retired and no maturity wall before FY2027. Free cash flow of $2.0B is high quality, with FY2025 operating cash flow exceeding GAAP net income from continuing operations. An Elliott partner sits on the board with a stated margin-and-monetization agenda, $1.7B of repurchase authorization remains, and the multiphysics synergy that justified $34.9B has not yet been monetized. At 23.1x next-year consensus, the forward multiple is below the peer median and at a wide discount to Cadence.

What keeps it from being a BUY is equally concrete. Non-GAAP EPS declined 8.7% year over year in the most recent quarter. The cash-based valuation — 42x EV/FCF on a 2.6% yield, 37–38x with stock compensation expensed — is not attractive for mid-single-digit organic growth. Design IP has not inflected year over year. Insiders have made no open-market purchases through a 38% drawdown while the CEO and CFO both sold near $450–459 in June. And the question of whether AI agents deflate license volume even where competitive share is retained cannot be answered from current disclosure; management's own pivot toward consumption-based pricing indicates they are planning for the transition rather than dismissing it.

Own the position for the Elliott catalyst, the forward multiple, and the unmonetized fusion optionality. Add on Design IP inflecting year over year, a quantified margin commitment, or a price near $340–355.
