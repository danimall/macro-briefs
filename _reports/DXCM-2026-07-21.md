**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DexCom is a pure-play continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) company operating in a premium-end duopoly with Abbott's Libre franchise. The investment case rests on a single interconnected thread: the FY2024–2025 manufacturing and quality problems that compressed gross margin to 60.1%, triggered a March 2025 FDA warning letter, and spawned a cluster of quality-linked litigation appear to be resolving. Q1 2026 delivered the first hard evidence: gross margin rebounded to 62.9% from 56.9% a year earlier, operating income rose 91%, and operating cash flow jumped 186% to $525.6M. If that recovery holds, three of the company's largest risks de-risk simultaneously.

Against that operational improvement sits a structural overhang the market can model but not yet quantify precisely. CMS extended its DMEPOS competitive bidding program to include CGMs in late 2025, with contracting beginning in 2027 and payment cuts taking effect in 2028. Management explicitly expects Medicare reimbursement to decline beginning 2028 across both competitive and non-competitive areas. This caps the U.S. per-unit pricing outlook even as volumes grow, the classic medtech tension between volume expansion and price erosion as a category matures.

At ~$75, DXCM trades at roughly 29x the current-year consensus EPS of $2.59 and ~24x next-year's $3.08, a premium to the Health Care Equipment peer median of ~22.6x but reasonable for a company compounding revenue in the mid-teens. The stock has fallen ~17% from its 52-week high of $89.98 and sits well below its ~$118 May-2023 level. Estimate revisions are stable-to-modestly-positive (+3.8% current-year over 90 days), and analyst sentiment has turned constructive (Piper Sandler $88, Deutsche Bank initiated Buy). We rate DXCM a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the margin recovery is real and the franchise is durable, but the reimbursement and regulatory tails keep risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on FDA warning-letter resolution or favorable clarity on the 2027 bid process.

## Company Overview

DexCom commercialized its first CGM in 2006 and operates as a single reportable segment. Its economic model is razor/razor-blade: high-margin recurring disposable sensors plus reusable transmitters and receivers. The product portfolio spans G7 and the late-2025 G7 15 Day (flagship real-time CGM for insulin-using diabetics), Dexcom One/ONE+ (international value tier), and Stelo, the first OTC glucose biosensor cleared in the U.S., launched August 2024. Stelo targets the large non-insulin Type 2 and prediabetes/metabolic-wellness market and is the primary growth-optionality lever.

DexCom is one of two dominant real-time CGM players globally, competing directly with Abbott's Libre, the largest competitor by sensor volume. Secondary competitors include Medtronic, Roche, LifeScan, and Ascensia, with emerging Chinese entrants at the low end. Competitive advantages include brand recognition among endocrinologists, a reputation for sensor accuracy, integration partnerships with insulin-pump and automated-insulin-delivery makers, and an accumulating CGM dataset used for machine-learning insights. The company added roughly 600,000–700,000 net customers (excluding Stelo) in 2025.

Revenue splits roughly 70% U.S. / 30% international, with about 85% flowing through distributors; certain distribution agreements each exceed 10% of revenue, a real concentration point. Manufacturing spans Mesa (AZ), San Diego (CA), Penang (Malaysia), and Athenry (Ireland, under construction).

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trend shows durable top-line growth with a margin trough that now appears to be reversing.

| Gross margin | 64.7% | 63.2% | 60.5% | 60.1% |
| Operating income | 391 | 598 | 600 | 912 (+52%) |
| Net income | 341 | 542 | 576 | 836 (+45%) |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | 990 | 1,441 |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | 1,077 |

FY2025 gross margin dipped to 60.1% on inefficiencies tied to ensuring supply availability, lower-yield build configurations, and elevated replacement costs, the same operational weaknesses that produced the FDA warning letter and the G7-reliability litigation. Operating income still grew 52% on operating leverage, and FCF reached $1,077M (a ~23% FCF margin).

Q1 2026 marks the turn. Revenue was $1,191.9M (+15% YoY), net income rose 89%, and management attributes the margin recovery to improved manufacturing efficiency, better fixed-cost absorption on higher volumes, and additional quality testing and material validation directly tied to FDA remediation. Full-year 2026 effective tax rate is guided to ~22.7%, aided by a new Malaysia tax holiday.

The balance sheet is strong. DexCom held $2.42B in cash and short-term securities at Q1'26 with no revolver borrowings and a 1.95 current ratio. Debt consists of a single $1.25B 0.375% convertible note due May 2028, with a conversion price of $162.41 (deeply out-of-the-money at ~$75; capped calls lift the effective dilution cap to $212.62). The prior $1.21B 2025 note was repaid entirely in cash in November 2025, a strong liquidity signal. The Altman Z-Score of 2.63 ("grey zone") is a mechanical artifact of the convert (D/E ~1.3x), not a distress signal; interest coverage and cash generation are excellent. ROE ran ~30.5% in 2025.

Two watch items temper the picture. Excess and obsolete inventory charges rose to $38.6M in Q1'26 from $28.2M a year earlier, and inventory rose from $629M to $694M amid the G7 15 Day product transition, normal for a launch but worth monitoring for write-down risk. Capital allocation is the second: DexCom deployed $500M of a $750M buyback in 2025 (7.7M shares) but repurchased nothing in Q1'26 with ~$250M remaining and the stock near multi-year lows. That is either capital discipline preserving capex/M&A optionality or a sign management isn't opportunistically aggressive.

## Growth Analysis

The secular tailwind is intact. CGM remains underpenetrated against roughly 40M U.S. diabetics, and the 2023 Medicare LCD extended coverage to any insulin user and to problematic hypoglycemia. Revenue growth of 16% in 2025 and 15% in Q1'26 is well above the medtech average, and management's diversification into the OTC/wellness market via Stelo opens a materially larger non-insulin addressable population.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating. Current-year EPS consensus of $2.59 has moved +3.8% over 90 days but is flat over 30 days; next-year $3.08 is +2.7% over 90 days and flat recently. This does not clear the >30% revision-velocity bar that would flag a strong inflection buy signal; the revenue growth is durable but not accelerating, and the earnings recovery is driven by margin normalization more than a step-change in demand. The earnings surprise record is mixed: three beats and one sizable miss (Q4'25 at -32.1%) over the last four quarters, with a strong +17.0% beat in Q1'26. That pattern argues for moderate rather than high confidence in forward estimates.

The GLP-1 debate has shifted in DexCom's favor. The Street increasingly frames GLP-1 therapies as complementary, with CGM guiding dosing and lifestyle response in Type 2 patients, rather than a pure demand threat. Management concedes GLP-1s could reduce CGM demand among some Type 2 patients, so this remains a two-sided variable, but the companion framing is now the base case.

The counterweight to the growth thesis is reimbursement. The CMS 2028 competitive-bidding cut caps U.S. Medicare per-unit pricing even as unit volumes grow. The durable growth story is therefore a volume-over-price story: DexCom needs to add customers faster than per-unit reimbursement erodes. Stelo's OTC channel, which sidesteps Medicare pricing entirely, is strategically important precisely because it diversifies away from this pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$75 and a market cap of ~$28.9B, DXCM trades at ~29x current-year consensus EPS ($2.59) and ~24x next-year ($3.08). Against the Health Care Equipment peer median P/E of 22.6, DexCom carries a premium, which is defensible given its mid-teens revenue growth versus slower-growing peers like GE HealthCare (15.5x), ResMed (18.6x), and Zimmer Biomet (22.6x). On a growth-adjusted basis the premium is modest, a PEG near 1.5 on next-year growth.

Cheapness relative to the stock's own history is not the argument. The shares have declined from ~$118 in May 2023, but that decline reflected genuine fundamental deterioration (margin trough, FDA letter, litigation), not a mispricing. The relevant question is price versus current and forward fundamentals. On that basis the Q1'26 margin recovery supports the current multiple: if gross margin holds in the 62–63% range and revenue compounds 13–15%, EPS power expands toward the $3.08 next-year figure and beyond, and the ~24x forward multiple is reasonable rather than stretched.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $65, mean $85.75, median $85, high $112. With the stock below the mean target and estimate revisions modestly positive, the setup is constructive but not compelling. We do not treat proximity to consensus as a ceiling here because the multiple is not premium (below 30x forward), but the estimate-revision velocity is too muted to argue targets are meaningfully stale.

We score valuation 6.0. The stock is fairly-to-modestly-cheaply valued for its quality and growth, but the 2028 CMS reimbursement cut is a model-able headwind that legitimately caps the long-term terminal-value case, and the earnings recovery is not yet proven durable beyond one strong quarter. There is no identified catalyst for near-term re-rating beyond continued margin execution.

## Competitive Landscape

DexCom holds a strong, defensible position at the premium end of a duopoly, but the trajectory is stable rather than improving. Against Abbott's Libre, the volume leader, DexCom competes on accuracy, AID integration, and endocrinologist relationships. The G7 15 Day launch is a direct competitive response to Libre's longer wear time, and it matters that DexCom is now matching rather than lagging on that spec.

The competitive frontier is shifting toward the metabolic-wellness/OTC market, where Stelo competes with Abbott's Lingo. Here the moat is thinner: the FDA's January 2026 guidance broadening "general wellness devices" invites Big Tech and consumer-electronics entrants into the glucose-wearable space, and low-end Chinese entrants are pressuring the value tier internationally. DexCom's accumulating CGM dataset and machine-learning insights are a genuine long-term differentiator, but data moats in medical devices are slower to compound than in software.

The competitive-position score declined from 8.0 to 6.5 across prior reports, and we hold it at 6.0. The core insulin-using CGM franchise is durable, but the incremental growth arenas (OTC wellness, international value tier) are more contested, and the reputational damage from the G7-reliability issues and the "adulterated/misbranded" consumer litigation is a modest headwind to the premium-brand positioning that underpins pricing power. This is a strong incumbent facing more competition on its growth margins, not a share-gainer widening its moat.

## Risk Assessment

**CMS competitive bidding (structural, dated).** The single most important overhang. CMS will set the single payment amount at the 75th percentile of winning bids, reclassify CGMs as items requiring "frequent and substantial servicing," and phase in monthly rental payments bundling the receiver over a 60-month useful life. A country-of-origin question in the bid could disadvantage DexCom's offshore (Malaysia/Ireland) manufacturing. The 2027 bid process is the near-term data point to watch.

**FDA warning letter (unresolved, quality-linked).** Followed 2024 inspections of San Diego and Mesa (Form 483s), citing manufacturing-process and quality-management-system deficiencies. It does not restrict production, marketing, or 510(k) clearances but remains open and could escalate. The Q1'26 margin recovery, driven partly by additional quality testing and material validation, suggests remediation is progressing.

**Litigation cluster (quality-linked).** Two securities class actions (one on FY2024 guidance surviving in part; a newer S.D.N.Y. case specifically alleging misstatements about G7 accuracy, reliability, and manufacturing), multiple derivative suits, and six consumer class actions (Sept 2025–Jan 2026) alleging G6/G7 devices were "adulterated or misbranded." The "adulterated/misbranded" framing echoes the FDA findings; plaintiffs are leveraging the regulatory record. Not yet financially quantified, but reputationally and legally material.

**Interconnection is the key analytical point.** The FDA letter, the margin trough, and the G7 quality litigation are one story, not three. A clean resolution of the manufacturing/QMS problems de-risks all three simultaneously, which is why Q1'26's margin rebound is disproportionately important to the thesis.

**Secondary risks.** Broader reimbursement erosion (channel mix, payor consolidation, the July 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" cutting Medicaid ~$1T and reducing ACA enrollment); GLP-1 demand substitution among Type 2 patients; manufacturing concentration and single/sole-source components (ASIC, polymers) with a history of G7 supply issues; and category commoditization at the OTC/wellness frontier.

We score risk 5.0. The risks are largely known and dated (CMS 2028, FDA remediation timeline), which limits pure surprise, but they are specific and asymmetric rather than generic sector risk; the CMS cut and an FDA escalation are both genuine binary-ish downside catalysts not yet fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV rose to 47.8% over the two days into July 21 (from 42.6%), with the term structure in backwardation (47.8% near-term versus 43.9% at six months), consistent with the upcoming earnings event. IV is rich relative to 41.6% historical volatility (a 6.2-point spread), so near-dated premium is expensive. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.28 to 1.05 and near-term put/call skew is elevated at 4.3%, indicating hedging demand ahead of the print. The six-month skew is negative (-3.6%), meaning longer-dated positioning is less bearish, with the caution concentrated in the near-term event. Near-term put demand against constructive longer-dated positioning fits a HOLD-into-earnings stance rather than a directional conviction either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95–112).** The Q1'26 margin recovery to 62.9% proves durable and expands further as Malaysia/Ireland capacity scales and the FDA remediation completes, resolving the letter and blunting the quality litigation. Revenue compounds 13–15% on continued customer additions, G7 15 Day narrows the competitive gap with Libre, and Stelo opens a large OTC/wellness market that diversifies away from Medicare pricing. GLP-1s prove complementary, expanding the Type 2 TAM. EPS pushes toward $3.50+ by FY27, and the stock re-rates to the high-20s on that base, reaching the ~$88 mid-target range and higher toward the $112 high target.

**Bear Case ($54–60).** The 2027 CMS bid process produces a steeper-than-expected 2028 reimbursement cut, and non-competitive-area payments follow, compressing U.S. margins just as international value competition and OTC entrants pressure pricing elsewhere. The Q1'26 margin gain proves partly a favorable-comparison artifact rather than a durable structural fix; the FDA letter escalates or lingers, keeping the litigation cluster active. GLP-1 substitution bites Type 2 demand. Estimate revisions turn negative and the multiple compresses toward the peer median, taking the stock back toward its 52-week low of $54.11.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** This is a quality franchise with a genuine operational recovery underway, but the reimbursement and regulatory tails keep risk/reward balanced. The score trajectory (6.5 → 6.6 → 6.1) and recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → HOLD) reflect exactly this: the May BUY captured the margin-inflection optimism, and the subsequent moderation reflects the CMS overhang and muted revision velocity reasserting themselves. Our current read confirms the moderation: the fundamentals are solid, the price decline reflects genuine structural questions (CMS 2028) rather than a clean mispricing, and one strong quarter does not yet prove a durable margin fix.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July/early August 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; a second consecutive quarter of 62%+ gross margin would materially strengthen the durability case. The 2027 CMS bid process is the critical multi-quarter catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FDA warning letter formally resolved/closed, which would simultaneously de-risk the quality litigation and support the margin narrative.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin sustained at 62%+ confirming the Q1'26 recovery is structural, not a comparison artifact.
- Favorable clarity on the 2027 CMS bid outcome (reimbursement cut milder than the market fears) or Stelo/OTC revenue scaling to a level that visibly diversifies away from Medicare pricing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin reverses back below ~58%, signaling the manufacturing/quality fix did not hold.
- FDA escalation beyond the warning letter (consent decree, import restriction, or production constraint).
- The 2027 CMS bid process points to a reimbursement cut materially worse than management's "expect a decrease" framing, or negative estimate-revision momentum (>10% cuts over 90 days).

## Conclusion

DexCom is a high-quality CGM duopolist whose thesis turns on one connected question: whether the Q1'26 margin recovery marks the durable resolution of the FDA/quality/margin problems that dragged the stock from ~$118 to ~$75. The early evidence is encouraging: gross margin +600bps YoY, operating cash flow +186%, and a fortress balance sheet with the 2025 convert repaid in cash. Financial health is the standout dimension. But the offset is real and dated: the 2028 CMS competitive-bidding cut caps the long-term U.S. pricing case, the FDA letter remains open, and a quality-linked litigation cluster leverages that same regulatory record. Leadership churn and the absence of any open-market insider buying despite a depressed price add to the caution.

At ~24x forward EPS with mid-teens revenue growth and stable-to-positive estimate revisions, the valuation is fair rather than compelling, and there is no near-term catalyst for re-rating beyond continued margin execution. We rate DXCM a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale: constructive on the operational trajectory and willing to own the franchise, but wanting confirmation that the margin fix is durable and the reimbursement/regulatory tails resolve favorably before upgrading to BUY.
