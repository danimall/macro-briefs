## Executive Summary

América Móvil is the dominant integrated telecom operator across Latin America — Telcel and Telmex in Mexico, Claro across Brazil, Colombia, Peru, Argentina, Chile, Central America and the Caribbean, and A1 in Central/Eastern Europe. The operating business is performing reasonably: Q2 2026 service revenue grew 5.1% and EBITDA 5.3% at constant currency (6.7% excluding a one-off regulatory fine), EBITDA margin was 39.8%, and net debt excluding capitalized leases stood at MXN 402bn, or 1.331x LTM EBITDA-after-leases, inside management's explicit 1.2x–1.5x policy band. Mexico, the highest-margin market, is accelerating on both sides of the house: mobile service revenue +6.5% (prepaid growth accelerating to +5.3% from +3.1%) and fixed-line revenue +2.7% versus +1.7% the prior quarter, ending a multi-year deceleration.

Two things prevent that from translating into a buy case at $23.56. First, the ADR's return is dominated by the peso, not by operations. The peso appreciated roughly 12% against the dollar and 9% against the euro year over year, which suppressed reported MXN growth (revenue +3.1%) while inflating the dollar value of MXN earnings for ADR holders. A meaningful share of the ADR's 25% trailing-year gain is a currency effect that is reversible and that I have no edge in forecasting. Second, the decline from the June 12 high of $28.46 is not a case of unchanged fundamentals meeting a lower price. Current-year consensus EPS has been cut from $2.03 to $1.88 over 90 days (−7.4%), and the Q2 print missed the $0.48 consensus by 16.6% at $0.40. Decomposing the move: at $27.75 on June 12 against the then-$2.03 estimate the ADR carried 13.7x; at $23.56 against $1.88 it carries 12.5x. Roughly one point of multiple compression, the balance an earnings downgrade. Risk/reward has improved, modestly, but the market repriced something real.

What remains is a fairly valued, mid-single-digit constant-currency compounder with low financial leverage, a 2.6% dividend, and an unusually dense set of dated near-term risks: Mexican prepaid SIM re-registration for the existing base begins August 15, 2026 and runs into December; TIM and Vivo turned promotional in Brazil from May 2026 with all three carriers launching roughly R$30/month entry hybrid plans; and a MXN 1.3bn asymmetric-regulation fine on Telnor (a Telmex affiliate, not Telmex) became final in June 2026 at the statutory minimum of 6% of revenue. Management has also explicitly reserved balance-sheet capacity for M&A over accelerated buybacks. HOLD.

## Company Overview

América Móvil operates mobile, fixed-line, broadband and pay-TV services across Latin America and Central/Eastern Europe, with controlling stakes in most regional subsidiaries. It reports in Mexican pesos under IFRS and files as a foreign private issuer (20-F and 6-K, no 10-K/10-Q, no DEF 14A, and no Section 16 insider filings). The absence of Form 4 data is structural and carries no signal in either direction.

The Q2 2026 earnings call provides the clearest available read on where growth is actually generated:

- **Mexico** is the profit engine and the best-performing market. Mobile service revenue +6.5%, postpaid +7.2%, prepaid accelerating to +5.3%. Fixed-line revenue growth accelerated to +2.7%. Largest contributor to broadband net adds at 170,000.
- **Brazil** is the largest non-Mexican market and the current soft spot. Competitors turned promotional from May 2026; management's posture is explicitly reactive ("if they do more promotions then we're going to do more promotions"). B2B growth improved to +7.5% and fixed revenue growth has climbed over four quarters from +1.5% to +4.1%. 83,000 broadband adds. Management also flagged a possible Brazilian macro slowdown.
- **Colombia** progressed from ~7% to ~10% mobile revenue growth, attributed to first-to-5G positioning and number-portability gains; 74,000 broadband adds.
- **Peru** is strongest on the fixed side at roughly 12% growth.
- **Argentina** added 154,000 postpaid subscribers but faces a newly consolidated "big competitor" following a competition-authority resolution.

The stated competitive differentiator is network quality rather than price, with growth framed as an ARPU-mix story: prepaid-to-postpaid migration, higher recharge denominations ("instead of charging 50 pesos, they are charging 80 or 100"), and fiber/convergence attach. Q2 postpaid accesses grew 9.1% and fixed broadband accesses 6.1% year over year, alongside a 3.0 million prepaid net *loss* from base clean-up in Colombia and Argentina. Net subscriber change was likely near flat or negative once netted — headline postpaid adds cited by secondary press should not be read as subscriber growth.

FY2025 audited results were not retrievable in the XBRL company-concept data; the most recent indexed annual facts are FY2024, with interim data through 9M2025. That is a genuine gap in the record and it constrains confidence.

**Reported MXN earnings are largely an FX pass-through.** FY2024 revenue rose 6.5% while net income fell 65.8%, with derived 4Q24 revenue up ~18% year over year against net income of only MXN 5,557m (2.3% net margin). Roughly half of revenue is earned outside Mexico while a large share of debt is hard-currency, so peso weakness inflates translated revenue and simultaneously produces FX losses in comprehensive financing cost. The mechanism reversed in 2025 (9M net income +207% on +10.5% revenue) and reversed again in Q2 2026, where a 12% stronger peso compressed reported revenue growth to 3.1% while comprehensive financing cost rose to MXN 10.4bn on lower FX gains. I cannot exclude that impairments, a discrete tax item or provisions contributed to part of the FY2024 decline — the MD&A was unreadable — but the 2025 symmetry supports the FX reading.

The practical consequence: reported MXN growth rates carry little information about operating momentum, and screens built on net income, ROA and leverage change would have scored FY2024 as sharp deterioration when EBITDA-level operations were intact. Management's constant-currency series is the usable one.

**Cash conversion is adequate and not improving.** OCF margin peaked at 30.4% in FY2023, fell to 27.5% in FY2024 (OCF −3.5% against revenue +6.5%), and ran 26.7% in 9M2025 (OCF +18.1%, better than revenue). Cash generation is heavily fourth-quarter weighted — derived 4Q OCF was 32–44% of the full-year figure across 2022–2024 — so no single quarter should be annualized. Management guided FY2026 capex to approximately US$7bn against 1H26 capex of MXN 48bn.

**Two leverage optics point in opposite directions, and the net-debt figure is the right one with a caveat.** Book leverage looks heavy: total liabilities/equity of 3.15x at FY2024, equity only 24.1% of assets. But most of the liability stack is non-debt — payables, deferred revenue, deferred taxes, lease liabilities and Telmex labor obligations — while book equity has been ground down by decades of buybacks and translation. Management's stated 1.331x net debt/EBITDAaL, with net debt down MXN 30.9bn in cash-flow terms in 1H26, is the economically meaningful measure. The caveat matters: that ratio *excludes* capitalized leases, and AMX became a very large tower lessee after spinning off its tower business. Deriving from management's own inputs (MXN 402bn ÷ 1.331 implies LTM EBITDAaL of ~MXN 302bn, against Q2 EBITDA before leases of MXN 96bn) points to an annual lease burden on the order of MXN 80bn, which means a lease-inclusive leverage figure would be materially above 1.33x. That figure was not obtainable.

**A data reconciliation the reader should be aware of.** The market-data profile reports trailing and forward P/E of 279.1x on EPS of $0.076. That is inconsistent with the reported quarterly actuals ($0.40, $0.39, $0.32, $0.38 = $1.49 trailing) and with consensus of $1.88 for the current year and $2.10 for next year. I treat the 279x figure as a stale or mis-scaled input and use the consensus and actual EPS series throughout. Note also that summing 1H26 actuals ($0.79) with the next two quarterly estimates ($0.46 + $0.48) gives $1.73, below the $1.88 "current year" line — a basis inconsistency in the estimate set that argues for using a range rather than a point.

## Growth Analysis

The honest framing is mid-single-digit constant-currency growth on a base whose nominal-peso topline has gone backwards in real terms. On a consistent accounting basis, FY2020 revenue of 815,399 to FY2024 revenue of 869,221 is a four-year CAGR of 1.61% in nominal pesos, against Mexican CPI generally running 4–5%. That series mixes translation of roughly half the revenue base, so it is not organic; the ~5% constant-currency service revenue figure is the better read. Either way, this is not a growth asset.

A trap worth flagging for anyone building a long-run series: 2019 revenue appears as 1,007,348 in the FY2019/FY2020 20-Fs but 851,483 in the FY2021 20-F; 2020 appears as 1,016,887, then 839,707, then 815,399 across three successive filings. These are almost certainly discontinued-operations reclassifications tied to the 2021 TracFone sale and 2022 tower spin-off. FY2021 net income of 196,326 is likewise not a run-rate figure and should not appear in any trend line.

**Estimate revisions are the clearest negative.** Current-quarter consensus is $0.46, down 0.9% in 30 days and 10.1% in 90 days. Current-year consensus is $1.88, down 7.4% in 90 days. Next year at $2.10 is up 1.9% in 90 days, so the cuts are concentrated in the near term — consistent with FX translation and the Telnor fine rather than with a structural downgrade. Still, the direction is negative and the beat/miss record is 2-for-4 with the two misses at −16.6% and −17.4%. That combination argues against assuming consensus is conservative.

**Where incremental growth comes from.** Fiber tuck-ins inside the existing footprint: Desktop in Brazil (expected to close and be paid for at end-2026, terms unverified) and WOW Peru, agreed in July 2026 from Liberty Latin America and Narvik Capital Partners, described by the CEO as more than 3 million homes passed with fiber and about half a million customers outside Lima, conditional on Peruvian competition approval. Price undisclosed. Management expects "more opportunities like Desktop, like WOW." Management explicitly ruled out geographic expansion outside existing markets. A disclosed cost lever — "starting to use a lot more AI agents… it's going to help us to reduce costs" — is unquantified but consistent with EBITDA outgrowing revenue.

**Near-term drag with a date attached.** Mexican prepaid re-registration for the existing base begins August 15, 2026 and runs to roughly end-November/early-December, staggered by the last digit of the phone number. Postpaid is exempt. The CEO already observes behavioral distortion: "some customers decide not to renew because if they renew they have to register… a little bit less sales but less disconnection." Expect elevated, mechanically-driven Mexican prepaid disconnections in 3Q26–1Q27 that will look like operating deterioration. The revenue impact should be far smaller than the subscriber-count impact since unregistered SIMs skew toward low-recharge users, but the optics land in two consecutive prints in AMX's best market.

Growth score of 5.0 reflects genuine constant-currency mid-single-digit expansion and a real Mexican fixed-line inflection, offset by negative near-term revisions, sub-inflation nominal growth, and a Brazilian price war whose margin cost management has pre-committed to absorbing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.56 the ADR trades at:

- **12.5x** FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.88; **13.6x** on the sum-of-quarters $1.73; **11.2x** FY2027 consensus of $2.10
- **~6.0–6.2x** EV/EBITDAaL, derived from management's own figures: equity value ~$80bn (secondary-sourced), net debt ex-leases MXN 402bn, LTM EBITDAaL ~MXN 302bn, at an assumed USD/MXN of 17.5–18.5
- **2.6%** dividend yield, implying roughly a 33–35% payout on $1.73–1.88 of EPS — comfortably covered
- **~15.8x** trailing on $1.49 of reported quarterly actuals

Peer context matters here and it cuts against the "deep value" reading. Global incumbent telecoms with low-single-digit growth trade broadly in the 8–13x forward earnings and 6–8x EV/EBITDA range. AMX at 11.2x FY2027 and ~6x EV/EBITDAaL sits inside that band rather than at a conspicuous discount, notwithstanding better growth (mid-single-digit constant currency versus low-single-digit for developed-market peers) and lower financial leverage (1.33x versus 2.5–3.0x typical). The genuine discount is modest and it is compensating for identifiable things: peso translation volatility in reported earnings, asymmetric regulation that scales penalties with revenue, a controlled-company structure with no proxy disclosure, and an M&A-first capital policy.

Sell-side targets span $20.80 to $35.00 with a mean of $29.44 and median of $30.00 — the mean implies +25% from spot. I do not treat that as independent confirmation. Sentiment is flagged as degrading, the recommendation mix has drifted marginally worse over three months (strong buys 2→1, holds 5→5, one strong sell persisting), and the most recent single rating cited in the research trail was a Hold at $20.80, 11.6% *below* spot. Mean targets on emerging-market ADRs are chronically slow to reflect currency reversals, which is precisely the dominant variable here.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a stock that is not expensive and offers real absolute-yield and balance-sheet support, but where I cannot identify a specific mispricing the market is missing. A ~12x multiple on mid-single-digit constant-currency growth with falling near-term estimates is approximately correct. The clearest identifiable re-rating catalysts — a committed capital-return step-up in 2027, monetization of the retained Verizon stake, or clarity that Mexican asymmetric obligations are being relaxed — are all uncommitted or unverified.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural theme running through the quarter is the retreat of European incumbents from Latin America and the resulting consolidation. It is double-edged for AMX.

Where rivals combine, AMX faces fewer but stronger competitors. In Argentina, a competition-authority resolution on consolidation produces what the CEO called "a big competitor there, but nothing else to do." In Mexico, Movistar has been announced as changing hands (not yet approved; acquirer unnamed in the transcript), and AMX's preponderant-agent status makes it implausible that AMX could be the buyer. In Colombia, a Telefónica-Colombia transaction closed; the transcript does not name the acquirer and does not state that AMX bought the asset, so the conservative reading — a competitor transaction, not an AMX acquisition — should stand. In Brazil, TIM and Vivo turned promotional from May 2026 in both prepaid and postpaid, and the CEO declined to raise Claro's hybrid pricing.

Where distressed or non-core fiber assets come to market, AMX is a natural cash-rich buyer of in-footprint infrastructure: WOW Peru and Desktop Brazil both fit that profile, and management is explicit that "consolidation of the market will be important."

On the offense, AMX's stated edge is network quality — "the only ones that have a very good 5G network" in Mexico, first-to-5G in Colombia — converting into portability wins and plan upgrades rather than promotional share buying. That is corroborated by the ARPU pattern: Mexican prepaid revenue accelerating to +5.3% while Mexican prepaid net adds were only +146,000, meaning growth is coming from recharge denomination rather than volume.

One comparability caveat: the CEO clarified that Nusel, a Brazilian MVNO, operates as an *acreditado* (commercial agent) whose customers count in Claro's reported base while revenue is split. AMX's reported Brazilian subscriber base therefore includes agent-model customers whose economics AMX only partially captures.

Level of competitive position is high; trajectory is flat to slightly negative as rivals consolidate into stronger units in three of AMX's markets simultaneously. Score 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**FX translation and hard-currency debt.** The dominant earnings risk, demonstrated in both directions. FY2024 net income fell 65.8% on +6.5% revenue; Q2 2026 comprehensive financing cost rose to MXN 10.4bn on lower FX gains, with the CFO noting that hedging means "when you are gaining on the FX, to the extent that those gains had already been hedged, you are giving back part of your expenses." A strong peso also degrades dollar capex purchasing power. For a USD ADR holder, peso direction is the single largest determinant of 12-month return, and it is not something the operating thesis controls.

**Asymmetric regulation, crystallized.** A MXN 1.3bn fine on Telnor for alleged violation of asymmetric measures — conduct from 2017, imposed 2020, contested, final June 2026 — at 6% of revenue, "which is the minimum in accordance with the law." Part landed in other financial expenses, which reached ~MXN 6.9bn in the quarter, up ~40% year over year. Management calls the fines "absurd" and does not expect more; that is an expectation, not evidence. The mechanism to note is that penalties are calibrated as a percentage of revenue, so exposure scales with the business, and enforcement can lag conduct by nearly a decade. Whether Mexico's regulatory architecture or AMX's preponderant-agent designation changed in 2025 is unverified in this pass and should not be assumed in either direction.

**Mexican prepaid re-registration.** Dated (August 15 through roughly early December 2026), unquantified as to the at-risk base, and landing in the highest-margin market. Subscriber-count optics risk in 3Q26 and 4Q26 prints.

**Brazilian competition plus macro.** Named counterparties, named timing, and a management posture that accepts margin cost. The CEO volunteered a possible Brazilian slowdown as a reason revenues are "decreasing a little bit."

**Telmex labor and pension.** MXN 8.4bn of labor-obligation service in 1H26. An analyst asked directly what annual cash outflow to model given plan maturity; the answer addressed accrual accounting and deflected on the cash trajectory. The forward cash pension path is unquantified.

**Governance opacity.** No DEF 14A exists, and 20-F Items 6 and 7 (compensation, major shareholders, related-party transactions) were unreachable. Controlling-family voting position, board composition and any Grupo Carso / Inbursa related-party dealings are unverified. I am not asserting figures from memory. This is not evidence of a problem; it is an absence of verification on a controlled company, which limits the confidence ceiling.

**Verification gaps that constrain the whole analysis.** FY2025 audited results, the entire 20-F narrative including risk factors, the debt currency-composition and lease notes, Desktop and WOW Peru terms, and FY2024–25 capex actuals are all unverified. I also cannot confirm which risk factors were added or modified in the FY2025 20-F.

**Macro overlay.** The current rate regime is a headwind for a 2.6%-yielding EM asset, though the specific level of long-end US yields and any Fed-independence premium are not established in the research trail and I make no specific claim on them. The peso has been a beneficiary of carry dynamics; that cushion is not permanent.

Risk score 4.8 reflects the count and datedness of the idiosyncratic items, particularly the two-quarter subscriber-optics window and the demonstrated FX pass-through, against a low-leverage balance sheet with no visible refinancing cliff.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning shows no priced catalyst. Near-term ATM IV is 24.2% against 26.7% one-year realized (spread −2.4%, fair to marginally cheap), and IV sits in the 13th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is flat — 24.2% at 10 days, 20.9% at 38 days, 22.4% at 101 days — meaning uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a dated event. Notably, the 101-day tenor spanning the entire Mexican prepaid re-registration window is priced at only 22.4% IV with an ±8.6% implied move, so that risk is not being paid for. Put/call open interest of 1.48 is mildly put-tilted. Reported skew values are +10.5% (1w), +5.7% (3m) and −2.5% (6m); the source characterizes the aggregate reading as balanced, and I do not infer direction from the sign without a defined convention. Short interest is negligible at 0.5% of float (6.7m shares, 5.5 days to cover) — nobody is positioned against this name.

Read together: options and short-interest data neither confirm nor contradict the fundamental view. They indicate a market that regards AMX as a low-drama holding, which is consistent with a HOLD but also means near-term optionality is cheap relative to the identifiable 3Q–4Q26 event calendar. An investor wanting to own the operating story while hedging the peso leg can do so at reasonable cost.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $31.00 (+32%)

Constant-currency service revenue and EBITDA growth hold at 5–6%, driven by the Mexican inflection (prepaid recharge upgrades, fixed-line growth off its multi-year floor), Colombia at ~10%, and Peru fixed at ~12%. Desktop and WOW Peru close and add fiber ARPU and small-business cross-sell inside the existing footprint at accretive multiples. The Mexican prepaid re-registration cycle proves revenue-immaterial as expected, removing an overhang after 1Q27. Net leverage stays inside the 1.2–1.5x band while EBITDA grows, and by 2027 management commits surplus capacity — potentially including monetization of the second half of the retained Verizon stake — to a step-up in buybacks. On that path, FY2027 EPS reaches roughly $2.20 and the multiple re-rates to 14x, in line with better-capitalized developed-market incumbents. The peso holds or firms, adding rather than subtracting from the ADR.

### Bear Case — $19.00 (−19%)

The peso gives back a meaningful part of its 12% appreciation, reversing the ADR's largest 2026 tailwind and re-introducing hard-currency FX losses in comprehensive financing cost. Brazilian promotional intensity persists past 2026 as consolidated rivals defend share, and AMX's pre-committed reactive posture compresses Brazilian EBITDA margin. Mexican prepaid disconnections during re-registration run heavier than management expects, producing two consecutive quarters of negative headline subscriber prints in the core market. Additional asymmetric-regulation actions surface with the same multi-year lag that produced the Telnor fine. The Desktop payment at end-2026 pushes leverage toward the top of the band, deferring any capital-return step-up further. FY2027 EPS lands near $1.75 and the multiple compresses to ~10.5x, taking the ADR toward its 52-week low of $18.18.

### Base Case — $26.50 (+12.5%)

Constant-currency growth of ~5% continues with Mexico offsetting Brazil, reported results remain FX-noisy, and consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.10 is broadly delivered. The multiple stays near 12.5x, giving roughly $26.25, plus a 2.6% dividend. Probability-weighting the three cases at 50/30/20 gives an expected price return of about +7.5%, or roughly 10% total return including the dividend. For an emerging-market telecom with peso translation risk and a controlled-company governance discount, that is approximately a fair, not an attractive, risk-adjusted outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the two natural decision points being the Q3 2026 print (late October) and the FY2025/FY2026 20-F filing (expected late April 2027), which should close the audited-results and risk-factor verification gaps.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at $20.50–21.50** against unchanged FY2027 consensus near $2.10 — that would put the ADR under 10x forward earnings and below the lowest published target of $20.80, restoring the margin of safety this rating lacks.
2. **A committed capital-return step-up**: an announced buyback pace above roughly MXN 15bn per half (versus MXN 4.6bn in 1H26), or a disclosed decision to monetize the remaining Verizon stake with proceeds earmarked for repurchase.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of constant-currency EBITDA growth at or above 6%** with Brazilian service revenue growth reaccelerating, confirming that the May 2026 price war is not structurally impairing margin.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **Constant-currency service revenue growth falling below 3.5%** for two consecutive quarters, or Brazilian EBITDA margin contracting more than 150bp year over year.
2. **Net debt/EBITDAaL exceeding 1.5x** after the Desktop payment without a stated deleveraging path, which would remove the balance-sheet quality that supports the current multiple.
3. **A second asymmetric-regulation penalty** or any adverse change to Mexican asymmetric obligations, which would confirm the regulatory tail scales with revenue rather than being a legacy 2017 matter.
4. **Current-year consensus EPS falling another 7%+** while the ADR holds above $23, which would push the forward multiple back above 14x on deteriorating estimates.

**What I am watching in the Q3 print specifically:** the magnitude of Mexican prepaid disconnections attributable to re-registration versus the underlying recharge trend; Brazilian service revenue growth versus the +4.1% fixed / promotional-pressured mobile split; whether other financial expenses normalize from the ~MXN 6.9bn Telnor-inflated level; and whether management quantifies the annual Telmex pension cash outflow it deflected on in July.

## Conclusion

América Móvil is a competently run, conservatively financed incumbent with genuine mid-single-digit constant-currency growth, ~40% EBITDA margins, net leverage of 1.33x, and a covered 2.6% dividend, trading at roughly 12.5x FY2026 and 11.2x FY2027 consensus earnings and ~6x EV/EBITDAaL. Those are reasonable numbers, and the balance sheet is the strongest part of the case.

They are not, however, mispriced numbers. The discount to developed-market telecom peers is modest and compensates for identifiable features: reported earnings dominated by peso translation, asymmetric regulation whose penalties scale with revenue and crystallize with multi-year lags, a controlled-company structure whose governance detail I could not verify, and a capital policy that explicitly prioritizes M&A optionality over shareholder returns — management deflected twice when pressed on whether surplus cash or the Verizon stake would fund buybacks. Current-year consensus has been cut 7.4% in 90 days, sentiment is degrading, and two of the last four quarters missed by roughly 17%.

Against that, the identifiable near-term risks are dated and clustered: Mexican prepaid re-registration from August 15 into December, Brazilian promotional intensity from May 2026, and the Desktop payment at end-2026. Options markets are pricing none of them, with the 101-day tenor at 22.4% IV against 26.7% realized volatility.

Probability-weighted 12-month total return of roughly 10% is approximately fair compensation for this risk profile and does not clear the bar for adding capital. Existing holders should keep the position; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal and a clearly defined better one in the $20.50–21.50 range. **HOLD.**
