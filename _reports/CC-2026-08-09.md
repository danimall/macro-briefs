## Executive Summary

Chemours reported Q2 2026 on August 4 and the shares fell 18.6% the following session, from $17.93 to $14.59, before recovering to $15.75. The print delivered three things simultaneously: a roughly breakeven pre-tax result (-$1M) that flattered underlying performance via a $266M land-sale gain, a $273M income tax provision on that $1M pre-tax loss driven by a full valuation allowance against all U.S. federal and state deferred tax assets, and Q3 guidance implying a 34-41% sequential decline in Thermal & Specialized Solutions segment EBITDA. Total Chemours stockholders' equity crossed into negative territory at -$49M from $250M at year-end. Current-year consensus EPS has been cut 31.2% in thirty days, to $0.88 from $1.28.

The operating business is not collapsing. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA is guided to $775-825M against $742M in 2025, gross debt principal fell $269M in six months to $3,914M, net leverage improved to 4.4x from 4.6x a year earlier, and management targets roughly 3.8x by year-end. Free cash flow is guided to $195-245M, an 8.2-10.3% yield on the current equity value. On enterprise value to forward EBITDA the stock trades at 7.0x — unremarkable rather than expensive for a chemicals company. The problem is the distance between enterprise cash generation and equity value. Interest expense of $270-280M, capex of $250-280M, and dividends of roughly $52M consume $592M of the $800M midpoint before a dollar reaches shareholders, and the metric on which that $800M is measured excludes roughly $456M of annual litigation and environmental charges that are the dominant cause of three GAAP losses in four years (-$569M cumulative 2023-2025 against $2,523M of cumulative Adjusted EBITDA).

Two risks are new enough that the market has had little time to price them. In June 2026 the European Chemicals Agency's Risk Assessment Committee adopted an opinion recommending trifluoroacetic acid be classified as Reproductive Toxicity Category 1B and as persistent/mobile/toxic; in July, EFSA lowered the acceptable daily intake. TFA is an atmospheric degradation product of the HFO refrigerants, including R-1234yf, that anchor the Opteon franchise — the business that produced $213M of the $289M in Q2 segment Adjusted EBITDA (73.7%) at a 36% margin. Separately, the Q2 10-Q discloses approximately 13,400 AFFF matters, of which roughly 12,500 are non-water-provider claims, predominantly personal injury, for which a loss is described as reasonably possible but not estimable — meaning no reserve is carried, against negative book equity and a $2.4B market cap. Neither risk is determined; both are unresolved and high-impact. That combination, set against an 11% base-case return, argues for trimming rather than owning.

## Company Overview

Chemours was spun out of DuPont in July 2015 and operates 28 major production facilities across eight countries, serving roughly 2,400 customers with approximately 5,700 employees. Three segments carry radically different economics.

**Thermal & Specialized Solutions (TSS)** sells refrigerants and thermal management fluids under the Opteon (low-GWP hydrofluoroolefin) and Freon (legacy hydrofluorocarbon) brands. FY2025 net sales $2,066M on $670M of segment Adjusted EBITDA — a 32.4% margin, rising to 36% in Q2 2026. This is the franchise. Demand is legislated by the U.S. AIM Act and the EU F-Gas Directive, and the moat is patent-based: 3,892 granted patents company-wide, with R-1234yf composition patents expiring "through the end of the decade" and blend, method and application patents running into the mid-2030s.

**Titanium Technologies (TT)** produces Ti-Pure titanium dioxide pigment via a chloride process, with roughly 1.1 million metric tons of nameplate capacity across two U.S. plants and one in Mexico. It is the largest segment by revenue — $2,429M in FY2025, 42% of the consolidated total — and among the least profitable, generating $145M of Adjusted EBITDA (6.0%), 7% in Q2 2026. The company's own 10-K estimates 2025 global demand at approximately 7.3Mt against roughly 10Mt of nameplate capacity, about 73% notional utilization. Mines supply under 10% of ore feedstock, so Chemours is predominantly a merchant ore buyer.

**Advanced Performance Materials (APM)** sells fluoropolymers under Teflon, Viton, Krytox and Nafion. FY2025 sales $1,263M, Adjusted EBITDA $108M (8.6%), $26M in Q2 2026. Internally it splits into a declining mature business (Advanced Materials, -14% YoY in Q2) and the genuine growth engine (Performance Solutions, +8% YoY and +42% sequentially, serving data-center cooling and semiconductor fabrication). Performance Solutions was $142M of a $1,591M quarter, roughly 9% of consolidated sales.

Customer concentration warrants a specific note. At the consolidated level no customer exceeded 10% of FY2025 net sales. At the segment level, one Titanium Technologies customer represented over 20% of TT sales and the top ten over 44% — implying more than $486M, or above 8.4% of consolidated revenue, from a single counterparty. The consolidated disclosure is accurate and materially understates the exposure in the segment producing 42% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is being held together by price against eroding volume.** Q2 2026 net sales of $1,591M declined 1% year over year, decomposed by management as +2% price, -4% volume, +1% currency. H1 2026 sales of $2,972M were 0.4% below H1 2025. Gross margin was 16.8% in H1 2026 versus 17.2%.

Within TSS the mix is counterintuitive. Freon (legacy HFC) sales rose 22% year over year in Q2 to $150M while Opteon, the strategic growth product, fell 10% to $337M. The AIM Act phase-down tightens legacy quota supply and drives Freon pricing, so the declining-volume legacy product is currently contributing more growth than the new-technology franchise. Management attributes Opteon weakness to a difficult comparison (Q2 2025 captured initial aftermarket channel fill for the U.S. stationary AC transition) and cites elevated market inventory as the reason Q3 TSS sales should fall mid-teens to 20% sequentially. Whether this is destocking or a demand ceiling is the central near-term uncertainty; the filings support destocking but do not establish it.

**The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is the analytical crux.** Adjusted EBITDA has declined 46% from the 2022 peak of $1,361M to $733M TTM. Over the same window GAAP net income was negative in three of four years. For the twelve months ended June 30, 2026, the reconciliation adds back $272M of litigation-related charges (of which $256M relates to PFOA/PFAS), $184M of environmental charges, and $209M of income tax adjustments. Roughly $456M per year of litigation and environmental cost is excluded from the metric used to value the company and to compensate management.

Q2 2026 shows the mechanics. Loss before income taxes was -$1M because a $266M Kuan Yin land-sale gain offset $225M of litigation charges and $144M of environmental charges embedded in SG&A, which rose to $469M from $424M. Net loss was -$274M (-$1.81 per diluted share) because of the $273M tax provision from the full U.S. DTA valuation allowance, including approximately $208M relating to prior years. Adjusted net income was $64M ($0.42 per share) versus $91M ($0.61) a year earlier, down 30%.

**Book equity is now negative.** Total Chemours stockholders' equity moved from $250M at December 31, 2025 to -$49M at June 30, 2026. Retained earnings fell $329M, exactly the $303M H1 net loss plus $26M of dividends — the reconciliation is clean. Treasury stock of $1,801M from historical buybacks left little cushion. Negative equity is an accounting outcome, not a solvency event: the credit agreement maintenance test is a senior secured net leverage ratio, not a net-worth test, so no covenant is tripped. But it removes any accounting buffer against further legal or environmental charges and drives screening models toward distress readings. Recomputing Altman Z at June 30 using TTM Adjusted EBITDA less D&A as the EBIT proxy gives 1.58; using the strict GAAP proxy (TTM pre-tax loss of -$52M plus $273M net interest) gives 1.49. Both sit below the 1.81 threshold. With $1,624M of disclosed liquidity and no 2026-2027 maturity wall, the correct reading is "no margin for error," not imminent default.

**Cash flow is genuinely improving but back-loaded.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $114M against $93M of capex produced $21M of free cash flow, a $167M year-over-year improvement. The FY2026 guide of $195-245M requires $174-224M in the second half; with Q3 guided to "at least $50M," the implied Q4 requirement is roughly $124-174M — approximately 51-71% of the annual figure in one quarter. Q4 is structurally a working-capital release quarter for a business whose peak selling season is Q2-Q3, so a skew is expected. What raises the bar is the FY2025 disclosure that the company accelerated collection of approximately $414M of receivables through customer supply-chain financing, including roughly $149M in Q4 2025 that would otherwise have arrived in Q1 2026, alongside a new approximately €180M factoring facility. These are disclosed, legitimate and common tools that shift timing rather than create cash. Offsetting that concern: receivables rose to $907M at June 30 from $679M at year-end, consistent with reduced reliance on pull-forwards in H1 2026 and supportive of the durability of the reported improvement. The evidence does not settle which reading dominates.

**Debt structure and two dated step-downs.** March 2026 issuance of $700M of 7.875% notes due 2034 retired $495M of 5.375% 2027 notes and roughly $188M of 5.750% 2028 notes, buying maturity runway at 210-250 basis points of incremental spread. Q2 repayment of €230M of the B-3 euro term loan was funded by Kuan Yin proceeds. FY2026 net interest guidance is $270-280M. Coverage at the $800M EBITDA midpoint is 2.9x on interest and 1.9x on EBITDA less capex. Total fixed charges of roughly $592M (interest $275M, capex $265M, dividends $52M) leave approximately $208M, reconciling to the guided FCF range less dividends.

Two items deserve monitoring. The May 2025 credit agreement amendment increased the maximum permitted senior secured net leverage ratio through Q3 2026, after which the tighter original test resumes; companies do not negotiate headroom from a position of comfort. And $220M of the $1.0B revolver matures October 7, 2026, with the balance in May 2030 — meaning reported liquidity of $1,624M ($671M cash plus $953M availability) may step down by up to $220M shortly after the last reported balance sheet. Whether that tranche has been extended is not established in the available filings.

Reaching the 3.8x year-end target on $800M of EBITDA requires net debt of roughly $3,040M, a further $203M reduction from $3,243M. Guided FCF less H2 dividends gives $169-219M, plus the tenth Kuan Yin parcel at approximately $55M. Achievable with essentially no slack.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $775-825M implies 4.5-11.2% growth over 2025's $742M, and net sales are guided +1% to +5%. That masks a deteriorating composition.

Q3 segment guidance is where the assumptions concentrate. TSS is guided to $125-140M against $213M in Q2 — a 34-41% sequential decline against a mid-teens-to-20% sales decline, implying severe operating deleverage. TT is guided to $70-80M against $48M in Q2 and $18M in Q1, meaning **TT is expected to earn more in Q3 alone than in all of H1 ($66M)**. In an industry running at roughly 73% notional utilization, holding an announced June 1 global price increase with stable volumes is the single heaviest assumption in the FY2026 guide. Implied Q4 Adjusted EBITDA of $184-204M is internally consistent with the annual range.

Estimate revisions run firmly against the guide. Current-quarter consensus fell 36.8% in thirty days to $0.34; current-year fell 31.2% to $0.88; next-quarter fell 22.4%. Next year, at $2.10, has been cut 6.2% in thirty days and 7.3% in ninety. The current-year cut largely reflects taxes rather than EBITDA — consensus of $0.88 on approximately 152M diluted shares implies $134M of adjusted net income, squarely inside the $120-145M guide, whereas the prior $1.28 sat above it. The full valuation allowance means foreign-source income is taxed without a U.S. shield, structurally raising the cash tax rate. The earnings power available to equity has been marked down even where EBITDA has not.

The structural growth story is APM Performance Solutions: Nafion membranes for chlor-alkali, flow batteries and hydrogen electrolysis; Teflon for semiconductor infrastructure; Krytox lubricants; Opteon 2P50 for two-phase immersion cooling in high-density compute. Growth of 8% year over year and 42% sequentially is credible and consistent with the broad data-center capex confirmation visible across industrial suppliers this cycle. But at $142M of a $1,591M quarter, and with the whole APM segment producing $26M of Adjusted EBITDA, it cannot offset a $2.4B pigment business at 7% margins.

Two long-dated constraints bound the growth thesis. Freon pricing strength is, by construction, a terminal annuity — declining volume, rising price, no incremental capital, and a legislated end. And R-1234yf composition patents lapse through the end of the decade, with the layered blend/method/application portfolio slowing rather than preventing margin decay in a 36%-margin business facing named Chinese F-gas competition.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.75 on 150.5M shares, market capitalization is $2,370M. Adding June 30 net debt of $3,243M gives enterprise value of $5,613M, meaning 58% of the enterprise is debt-funded.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / TTM Adjusted EBITDA ($733M) | 7.66x |
| EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA ($800M mid) | 7.02x |
| Adjusted P/E on FY2026 guide ($0.79-0.95) | 16.6x - 19.9x |
| P/E on current-year consensus ($0.88) | 17.9x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($2.10) | 7.5x |
| FCF yield on equity ($195-245M) | 8.2% - 10.3% |
| Dividend yield | ~2.3% |
| FY2026 GAAP guidance | net loss $230-255M |

The tension is straightforward. On EV/EBITDA and free cash flow yield the stock screens inexpensive. On adjusted P/E — the metric that reflects the interest burden actually borne by equity — it does not: 17.9x current-year against a peer median of 16.1x (Cabot; Olin and Ashland both carry negative trailing P/Es, so the peer set offers limited discipline).

A defensible adjustment capitalizes the legacy liabilities into enterprise value, since Adjusted EBITDA excludes the charges that fund them. Adding $795M of accrued environmental remediation and approximately $658M of accrued litigation gives an adjusted EV of roughly $7,066M, or 9.6x TTM and 8.8x forward Adjusted EBITDA. That reframes the stock from cheap to fairly valued at best. The adjustment is not free of double-counting: roughly $1,300M remains under the $4,000M DuPont/Corteva cost-sharing cap, a $150M contingent PFAS insurance recovery is disclosed, and payments spread over decades (the New Jersey settlement runs 25 years), so undiscounted capitalization overstates present value. The two framings usefully bracket the range.

The price fell 10.1% since the prior review while current-year estimates fell 31.2%. On the earnings measure available to equity, the stock did not get cheaper. On EV/EBITDA, where guidance was raised above 2025, it did. Both statements are true and they explain why the rating moves to REDUCE from AVOID without the underlying dimension scores improving.

Analyst targets — low $19.00, mean $23.22, median $22.00, high $29.00 — sit 21% to 84% above spot, with a rating distribution of 1 strong buy, 4 buy, 4 hold and no sells that has been unchanged for four months. Treat these as stale: the distribution predates the August 4 print and the 18.6% single-session decline, and a "low" target above spot is a signature of targets not yet updated. They are not independent confirmation of value here.

## Competitive Landscape

**TSS** competes against Solstice Advanced Materials, Arkema, Orbia, Daikin and Chinese F-gas producers. The regulatory apparatus that created the franchise can also constrain it, and both effects are now visible: the AIM Act and F-Gas Directive legislate HFO demand and tighten HFC supply, while the European Commission imposed a 200% quota reduction penalty applicable in 2026 on the Dordrecht Works site, with the interim suspension application dismissed in January 2026. TSS EMEA H1 sales of $195M versus $210M are consistent with a modest headwind. Trajectory: strong position, eroding protections.

**TT** faces structural overcapacity with no visible fix; 6-7% segment margins follow directly from roughly 73% industry utilization. Chemours' response has been cost-out — the Kuan Yin closure delivered $50M of savings attributable to 2023 and $140M to 2024 — and its three-plant Western Hemisphere footprint is an advantage in a tariff-inflected trade environment and a gap in Asian market access. One flag: the 10-K describes "ongoing discussions regarding project development, including the projected cost and timeline" for the PCC Group chlor-alkali facility at DeLisle, Mississippi, and the impact on the chlorine supply agreement. That is unusually hedged language for a signed agreement.

**APM** competes with Daikin, Syensqo, AGC, Gujarat Fluorochemicals and Dongyue, and holds roughly 90 years of fluoropolymer know-how largely as trade secrets. The Performance Solutions position in data-center cooling and semiconductor infrastructure is genuine and hard to replicate. Operational reliability is the offset: a January 2026 disruption at Washington Works cost APM an estimated $20-25M in Q1, traced to equipment affected by an August 2025 utility outage — a five-month-lagged consequence — and Q2 APM EBITDA fell 48% year over year. Against FY2026 capex guidance of $250-280M versus D&A of approximately $320M, and gross PP&E 70% depreciated, that raises a fair question about maintenance adequacy, though the outage was externally caused and the decline also reflects the deliberate SPS Capstone line closure.

One unpriced item: Chemours states it is currently the only domestic U.S. producer of monazite, a rare-earth feedstock. Mining co-products were under 5% of TT sales in 2025, so this is immaterial to earnings but represents policy-linked optionality no model reflects.

## Risk Assessment

**Unreserved AFFF personal-injury exposure.** Approximately 13,400 AFFF matters, of which roughly 900 are water-provider cases and approximately 12,500 are non-water-provider, predominantly personal injury, with significant new filings after August 2025 case-management orders. For the personal-injury cohort the company states a loss is reasonably possible but not estimable, so no reserve is carried. Against $2.4B of market capitalization and negative book equity, a resolution at a scale comparable to the water-provider settlements would be existential for the equity. The outcome is genuinely unknowable from public information; the absence of a reserve is the point.

**TFA regulatory threat.** The ECHA Risk Assessment Committee's June 2026 opinion and EFSA's July 2026 reduction of the TFA acceptable daily intake attack the degradation chemistry of the molecule underpinning roughly three-quarters of segment profit at a 36% margin. A hazard classification is procedural, not prohibitive; the pathway to restriction under REACH or a revised F-Gas regime is multi-year and contestable, and HFOs are the EU's own mandated replacement technology with no drop-in alternative at scale, so restriction would force a regulatory contradiction. This is a live, high-impact, unresolved risk rather than a determined negative — but it is new, and it is the risk least likely to be priced.

**Environmental escalation.** Remediation accruals rose from $618M to $795M in six months, driven by the 2026 Consent Decree with EPA and West Virginia DEP entered in Q2. The Washington Works site accrual rose from $24M to $174M, including approximately $113M for an off-site drinking water program. The FY2025 10-K disclosed that reasonably possible remediation costs could exceed the recorded accrual by approximately $620M. Partial offset: EPA moved in September 2025 for partial vacatur of the HFPO-DA maximum contaminant level, with a hearing noted for September 2026.

**Capital-structure step-downs.** Covenant headroom from the May 2025 amendment expires after Q3 2026, and $220M of revolver commitments mature October 7, 2026. Both are dated and monitorable; both fall within roughly one quarter of the last reported balance sheet.

**Accounting reliability.** Two rounds of corrections in roughly two years — the February 2024 Audit Committee internal review, then FY2025 revisions of prior periods for immaterial errors in income tax expense timing and deferred tax valuation allowances spanning January 2023 through September 2025. The second episode touches precisely the area that produced the Q2 $273M provision. No restatement of audited financials or material weakness conclusion was identified.

**Liquidity language.** The Q2 10-Q states sufficient liquidity through at least the end of August 2027, but adds that adverse resolution of legal or environmental matters could have a material adverse effect on liquidity and that risks exist regarding the ability to achieve liquidity objectives. More hedged than boilerplate, and not a going-concern qualification.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 59.6% sits below one-year realized volatility of 66.8%, a -7.3 point spread, with the term structure flat (59.6% near, 61.1% at six months) and IV in the 28th percentile of the current chain. ATM IV has collapsed from 76.5% on July 20 to 59.6% — the earnings event has passed and the market is not pricing a near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.0% on the front week and modestly negative further out; put/call volume ratio is 0.09 and open-interest ratio 0.25, both call-tilted. Short interest of 10.9% of float at 4.0 days to cover is elevated but not crowded.

The reading: options positioning is not corroborating the bearish fundamental case. Nobody is paying up for downside protection despite negative book equity and unreserved litigation. Two interpretations are consistent with the data — either the market considers the legacy liabilities adequately discounted at 7x forward EBITDA, or the tail risks here (litigation verdicts, multi-year regulatory processes) do not resolve on listed-option timeframes and therefore are not expressed in the chain. Cheap volatility relative to realized does favor expressing a negative view through long-dated puts rather than outright short exposure for those inclined, and it does not support adding equity length.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~25% probability) — $27.00

Opteon weakness proves to be destocking rather than a demand ceiling, and TSS volumes normalize in 2027 as channel inventory clears. TT's June price increase holds through 2027 in a market where Western-Hemisphere supply is structurally advantaged, lifting segment EBITDA toward $350-400M annualized from the $145M FY2025 base. APM Performance Solutions compounds at 20%+ on data-center cooling and semiconductor demand, and Washington Works reliability normalizes. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA reaches approximately $900M in 2027, next-year consensus EPS of $2.10 is delivered, net debt falls toward $2,850M, and the market pays 8.0x — $7,200M EV less $2,850M net debt is $4,350M of equity, roughly $28.60 per share. The TFA process resolves into labeling and monitoring obligations rather than restriction, and the EPA's partial vacatur motion on the GenX MCL is granted, reducing future drinking-water obligations. Call it $27, +71%.

### Base Case (~45% probability) — $17.50

FY2026 lands inside guidance at roughly $800M of Adjusted EBITDA and $220M of free cash flow. Year-end net debt reaches approximately $3,040M including the tenth Kuan Yin parcel. Enterprise value holds roughly constant at today's $5,613M — no multiple expansion, because TFA and AFFF remain unresolved and TSS growth is visibly dependent on a terminal Freon annuity — and the entire equity return comes from debt paydown transferring value across the capital structure: $5,613M less $3,040M is $2,573M, or $17.05 per share, plus modest credit for the final land parcel. Roughly $17.50, +11% over twelve months, plus a 2.3% dividend.

### Bear Case (~30% probability) — $9.50

Either TT price increases fail to hold in a 73%-utilization market, or TSS destocking extends into 2027, or both. Adjusted EBITDA reverts toward $700M, the Q4-weighted free cash flow guide misses on working-capital timing, and the 3.8x leverage target slips. With the covenant step-down resuming after Q3 2026 and a marginal cost of debt near 8%, the market applies 6.5x: $4,550M EV less $3,150M net debt is $1,400M of equity, roughly $9.30 per share. The dividend, currently costing about $52M annually against a guided GAAP loss, becomes a candidate for elimination. Call it $9.50, -40%. A materially adverse development in the AFFF personal-injury cohort or a European move from TFA classification toward restriction would take the equity below this level, and neither is capped by anything on the balance sheet.

Probability-weighted, this distribution produces roughly $17.50, an 11% expected return. For a stock with 67% realized volatility, negative book equity, 4.4x net leverage, and a left tail driven by non-mean-reverting legal and regulatory events, 11% is inadequate compensation.

### Why REDUCE rather than AVOID or HOLD

The prior AVOID rested on deterioration that the Q2 print confirmed. What has changed is price and evidence of execution: the shares are 10% lower and 45% below the May high, FY2026 EBITDA guidance sits above 2025, $269M of gross debt was retired in six months, the 2027 maturity was cleared, and $287M of Kuan Yin proceeds were received and applied. There is no imminent liquidity event — $1.6B of disclosed liquidity, no near-term maturity wall, and a leverage-based rather than net-worth-based covenant. That is enough to move off "do not own."

It is not enough for HOLD. Current-year estimates fell three times faster than the price, the tax valuation allowance is management's own assessment that U.S. taxable income will not materialize, and the two largest risks are unreserved and newly disclosed. Existing owners should trim into strength; prospective capital should wait for a lower price or resolution of the September-October catalysts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now into the post-print recovery toward $16-17, targeting a reduction of at least half of any existing position. Retain residual exposure only if the position is sized such that a -40% outcome is tolerable. Do not add. For those with a mandate to express the negative view, six-month implied volatility at 61.1% against 66.8% realized makes long-dated puts a cheaper vehicle than short stock, given 10.9% short interest and 4.0 days to cover.

**Base case target: $17.50** (+11%). Derivation: enterprise value held at the current $5,613M, net debt reduced to approximately $3,040M per management's 3.8x target on $800M of EBITDA, plus credit for the final ~$55M Kuan Yin parcel; equity of roughly $2,600M on ~151M shares.
**Bull case: $27.00** (+71%) — 8.0x on $900M of 2027 EBITDA less $2,850M net debt.
**Bear case: $9.50** (-40%) — 6.5x on $700M less $3,150M net debt.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints inside four months.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD:**
1. Q3 2026 (early November) Adjusted EBITDA at or above the $175-205M guide **with** TT delivering $70-80M, confirming the announced price increase held with stable volumes.
2. Confirmation that the October 7, 2026 revolver tranche has been extended or replaced, and compliance with the stepped-down senior secured net leverage test after Q3 2026.
3. Q4 free cash flow of at least $124M delivered without a year-over-year increase in receivables-acceleration programs, validating the FY guide as earnings-driven rather than timing-driven.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE** (all three required): the above, plus a price near or below $12 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $2.00, plus evidence that Opteon volumes have reinflected year over year rather than being carried by Freon pricing.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Any TFA development moving from hazard classification toward a use restriction or authorisation requirement for R-1234yf in the EU.
2. A first bellwether outcome, aggregate settlement framework, or reserve establishment in the approximately 12,500-matter AFFF personal-injury cohort at a scale material relative to $2.4B of market capitalization.
3. A miss on Q3 or Q4 guidance that pushes year-end net leverage above 4.2x, or a dividend suspension, or a further credit agreement amendment seeking covenant relief.
4. TT segment EBITDA below $60M in Q3, which would indicate the price increase did not hold.

**Reassessment timeline.** Three dated events cluster in the next quarter: the Q3 2026 covenant step-down, the October 7 revolver tranche maturity, and the September 2026 hearing on the EPA's partial vacatur motion for the HFPO-DA maximum contaminant level. Next scheduled reassessment is the Q3 print in early November 2026; escalate immediately on any 8-K touching the credit agreement, the AFFF docket, or European F-gas regulation.

## Conclusion

Chemours is a business with one genuinely good franchise, one commodity franchise trapped in structural overcapacity, one small franchise with credible secular growth, and a legacy liability structure that consumes the difference. Enterprise cash generation is real — $733M of TTM Adjusted EBITDA, $775-825M guided for 2026, $195-245M of guided free cash flow — and the deleveraging path from 4.4x toward 3.8x is credible if the Q4-weighted cash flow arrives. But equity holders sit behind $3.24B of net debt at a marginal cost near 8%, behind roughly $456M of annual litigation and environmental charges that never appear in the headline metric, behind approximately $1.45B of accrued legacy liabilities that convert the 7.0x forward EV/EBITDA multiple to 8.8x when capitalized, and behind approximately 12,500 unreserved personal-injury claims with negative book equity underneath them.
