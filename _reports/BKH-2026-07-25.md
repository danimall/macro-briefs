**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Black Hills Corp has ceased to be a standalone equity story. The August 2025 all-stock, tax-free merger of equals with NorthWestern Energy Group converts BKH into a deal-arbitrage and merger-completion instrument. Under the fixed exchange ratio (0.98 BKH shares per NWE share), the combined "Bright Horizon Energy" will be ~56% owned by legacy Black Hills holders, span ~2.1 million customers across eight states, and carry ~$15.4B in total value. Because the deal was struck at essentially no premium, BKH's price no longer discounts a takeout; it discounts synergy realization, integration execution, and the probability and timing of remaining regulatory approvals. Shareholder votes are complete on both sides, the South Dakota PUC approved unanimously on June 24, 2026, and Montana plus federal (FERC/FCC/HSR) approvals are the remaining gates for a targeted 2H 2026 close.

On standalone fundamentals, BKH is a serviceable but unremarkable regulated multi-utility. 2025 net income to common rose to $291.6M ($3.98 diluted EPS) on a Gas Utilities operating-income jump of $49.5M, partly offset by a $10.5M decline in Electric on unplanned coal-plant outages (coal availability fell to 77.7% from 89.8%). The genuine differentiator is data-center load growth in Wyoming — industrial GWh rose to 2,615 in 2025 from 2,170, with peak demand hitting new records at 379 MW, driven by Microsoft and Meta as tariff customers. The tension is capital intensity: 2025 capex of $819.8M against $673.4M operating cash flow produced free cash flow of –$146.4M, and a ~$4.7B five-year capex plan keeps the company reliant on debt and equity issuance, driving share-count growth that acts as a mild EPS headwind.

At ~$75.29, BKH trades at ~19.3x earnings, below the peer median of 21.1x, but that modest discount reflects higher leverage (D/E ~2.84x, 55.1% debt-to-cap), negative free cash flow, and near-term estimate cuts (current-quarter EPS revised down 21.1% over 90 days, next-quarter down 18.8%). Analyst targets ($80 low, $86 median, $87 high) sit above the current price and reflect deal-close economics rather than standalone upside. We maintain HOLD, classified as uncertain: the return here is a bounded, event-driven convergence toward Bright Horizon Energy economics, and the equity offers no asymmetric standalone re-rating catalyst while carrying genuine Montana-approval and integration tail risk.

## Company Overview

Black Hills Corp, headquartered in Rapid City, South Dakota, is a regulated multi-utility serving roughly 1.37 million customers across 800+ communities in eight states (Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska, South Dakota, Wyoming) under the Black Hills Energy brand. It operates two segments:

- **Electric Utilities** (~227,000 customers in CO, MT, SD, WY): vertically integrated with 1,386 MW of generation and 9,478 miles of transmission and distribution. It owns a Powder River Basin coal mine (WRDC, ~172M tons of reserves, ~51-year life) that feeds adjacent plants by conveyor at ~$1.26/MMBtu. The generation mix is shifting away from coal (25.5% of supply in 2025 versus 35% in 2023) toward natural gas and purchased renewables.
- **Gas Utilities** (~1,138,000 customers in AR, CO, IA, KS, NE, WY): 4,581 miles of intrastate transmission, ~44,840 miles of distribution mains and service lines, and seven storage sites.

As a regulated monopoly, BKH's economics are set by eight state commissions plus FERC, with authorized ROEs generally in the 9.3%–9.9% range. Its recovery-mechanism suite (fuel/gas cost adjustments, integrity riders, weather normalization in some jurisdictions) reduces regulatory lag. The pending merger with NorthWestern Energy will reconstitute the board (five of eleven seats to NorthWestern designees), rename the entity Bright Horizon Energy, and materially change the shareholder base and capital structure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Reported revenue moved from $2.55B (2022) to $2.33B (2023) to $2.13B (2024) to $2.31B (2025). These swings are dominated by pass-through gas commodity costs and are a poor gauge of underlying health; regulated utility margin has grown more steadily. Net income to common rose from $258M (2022) to $262M (2023) to $273M (2024) to $291.6M (2025). Diluted EPS was $3.98 in 2025 versus $3.91 in 2024. Operating income grew to $537.5M from $503.1M.

The 2025 segment divergence matters. Gas Utilities operating income jumped $49.5M to $320.8M on new rates, riders, and favorable weather. Electric Utilities operating income fell $10.5M to $222.5M, hurt by unplanned generation outages (Wygen III coal, Pueblo Airport Gen, Busch Ranch) that cut coal availability, forced higher-cost purchased power, and coincided with lower transmission revenue. This is an operational rather than a demand problem — retail electric volumes actually grew on data-center load — but it flags aging-fleet reliability risk in units dating to the 1970s–1990s.

**Margins.** Operating margin 23.3%, net margin 13.0%, ROE 7.8%, ROA 2.8% — typical for a capital-intensive rate-base utility.

**Cash flow and capital intensity — the central tension.** Operating cash flow was $673.4M in 2025, down from $719.3M in 2024. Capex was $819.8M, up from $744M (2024) and $556M (2023), driven by the Ready Wyoming transmission project (in service December 2025) and Lange II generation (expected Q4 2026). Free cash flow was –$146.4M (FCF margin –6.3%). This is structural: the ~$4.7B 2026–2030 capex plan (~$900M–$980M per year) means persistent negative FCF funded by debt and ATM/secondary equity issuance. Diluted share count grew to 73M in 2025 from 65M in 2022, a mild ongoing EPS headwind that the pending merger will amplify with ~60.2M new shares.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt $4.70B; stockholders' equity $3.82B; debt-to-capitalization 55.1%; D/E ~2.84x. Goodwill of $1.3B (12% of assets), no impairments to date, tested at a 6.7–7.2% WACC. Liquidity is adequate at $929.6M ($182.8M cash plus $746.8M revolver/CP availability). Ratings were affirmed in August 2025 at S&P BBB+ / Moody's Baa2, both Stable. A $400M 3.15% note matures January 2027 and will be refinanced at higher current rates; interest expense already rose to $200M in 2025.

The reported Altman Z-Score of 1.11 ("distress zone") is a model artifact for a regulated utility. The Altman model penalizes low asset turnover (0.21) and high leverage, both normal for a rate-base utility whose debt is backed by regulator-approved returns. Investment-grade ratings, covenant compliance, ample liquidity, and 55 consecutive years of dividend increases all indicate a sound, if capital-hungry, business. We do not treat the Z-Score as a bankruptcy signal.

**Dividend.** BKH raised its dividend for the 55th consecutive year in 2025 and lifted the Q1 2026 rate to $0.703/quarter ($2.812 annualized), a ~3.75% yield. The payout ratio has crept to 68%, above the 55–65% target. Under the merger agreement, dividend growth is capped at ≤4%/year without NorthWestern's consent.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is mid-single-digit and rate-base driven. NorthWestern reaffirmed 4–6% long-term rate-base EPS growth on its May 1, 2026 call and 2026 EPS guidance of $3.68–$3.83, framing the combined entity's trajectory. BKH's own current-year and next-year consensus EPS ($4.34 and $4.61) imply high-single-digit reported growth, though near-term quarterly estimates are being cut sharply, reflecting the electric-segment outage overhang and weather normalization rather than a demand break.

The one genuine, quantifiable growth driver is data-center load. Meta's AI data center transitioned to permanent service in Q1 2026. This aligns with the broader data-center load surge now confirmed across regulated utilities, and it differentiates BKH's rural footprint from slower-growing peers. Microsoft and Meta are customers buying power under the large-power-contract-service (LPCS) tariff, not assets BKH owns; the upside is incremental rate-base load, not equity participation in AI economics.

Colorado Electric received CPUC approval for 250 MW of new renewables (50 MW battery plus a 200 MW solar PPA), and Ready Wyoming completed in December 2025 interconnecting the SD and WY systems. These support the rate-base story but do not change the character of the growth: regulated, capital-intensive, dilution-funded, and mid-single-digit.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$75.29 and ~19.3x trailing/forward earnings, BKH trades at a discount to the Multi-Utilities peer median P/E of 21.1x. That discount is not a mispricing to exploit — it is priced for higher leverage (D/E ~2.84x versus a peer median beta of 0.38 and generally lower leverage among the comps), structurally negative free cash flow, and falling near-term estimates. A cheap-looking multiple in a regulated utility with dilution and negative FCF is a value-trap pattern rather than an entry signal, and there is no independent catalyst to close the gap on a standalone basis.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, BKH's competition is essentially with its regulators over ROE and rate base rather than with rival firms. The moat is the franchise itself, reinforced by a broad recovery-mechanism suite that limits regulatory lag. The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving: data-center load growth in Wyoming is a real, quantifiable share-of-load tailwind the market has only partly priced, and the merger adds scale to fund energy-transition and grid capex more efficiently across a larger customer base.

Offsetting factors keep this from a high score. The 2025 coal-outage episode exposed aging-fleet reliability risk. Affordability and political scrutiny are rising — a critic at the SD PUC hearing labeled the combined entity a "super monopoly" and objected to the review's speed, though South Dakota law sets a low approval bar. Long term, electrification is a tailwind for the electric segment but a slow headwind for gas demand, and coal-retirement pressure (with EPA's June 2025 proposed repeal of Clean Power Plan 2.0 leaving policy uncertain) complicates generation planning. We score competitive position 6.0 — a durable franchise with a genuine load-growth differentiator, tempered by operational reliability and regulatory-affordability pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion and integration (dominant).** The 10-K added an extensive merger risk section covering completion conditions, a $100M mutual termination fee, "force-the-vote" provisions that limit alternative bids, integration risk, dilution, combined leverage, and Section 382 NOL-utilization limits. Montana (MPSC) is the last major state gate, with FERC/FCC/HSR also required. A Montana condition that materially degrades deal economics, or an outright delay/failure, is the principal binary risk. The $100M fee and force-the-vote provisions lower break risk but also foreclose a competing bid for BKH.

**Regulatory recovery (structural).** Earnings depend entirely on eight commissions plus FERC approving prudent-cost recovery and reasonable ROEs. Adverse or delayed decisions, amplified by affordability pressure, hit earnings directly.

**Operational/generation.** The unplanned coal outages measurably dented electric earnings and are a recurring reliability concern in an aging fleet.

**Wildfire liability.** A growing Western-utility exposure. BKH stood up a PSPS program in June 2025 and filed mitigation plans; Wyoming (HB0192, effective July 2025) and Montana provide liability protections for compliant utilities, but exposure and insurance costs are rising.

**Capital-markets dependence.** Persistent negative FCF plus the $4.7B capex plan require continual debt/equity access; a downgrade below investment grade would raise costs and trigger collateral postings. The higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the January 2027 refinancing and directly pressures a negative-FCF, dilution-funded model.

**Data-center concentration.** Microsoft and Meta load is a growth engine but concentrates forecasting risk if a large customer scales back.

**Governance transition.** A CEO handoff (Marne Jones elevated from SVP–Utilities; Linden Evans shown as President & CEO into early 2026) is occurring simultaneously with the merger and a reconstituted board. We do not yet know who will lead the combined company.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 35.3% versus 22.6% trailing realized, a rich 12.7-point spread, with the term structure in backwardation (35.3% near-term versus 25.6% at six months) and an implied ±7.7% move on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.6% (with a sharp –25.7% one-month skew). ATM IV rose from 29.0% to 35.3% since early June even as the price rose 4.8%. The market is pricing an imminent binary — consistent with the Montana approval decision and merger-close timeline — and leaning defensively. This confirms the read that BKH is now an event-driven position with a specific, dated resolution risk. Short interest is also elevated at 16.4% of float (7.7 days to cover), some of which is likely merger-arb hedging against NWE.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$85–$92).** Montana and federal approvals clear cleanly on the 2H 2026 target, and BKH's price converges toward NWE's higher ~25.5x multiple on merger completion, with synergy realization and combined scale supporting a re-rate. Data-center load growth continues to compound Wyoming rate base, the electric segment's outage issues prove transitory, and Bright Horizon Energy delivers 4–6% rate-base EPS growth with restored dividend-growth capacity post-close. Analyst high target $87, with modest additional upside on synergy delivery.

**Bear case (~$60–$64).** A Montana condition materially impairs deal economics, or the merger slips well beyond 2H 2026, and BKH reverts to standalone economics: ~19x earnings on a negative-FCF, dilution-heavy utility with rising interest costs and a payout ratio already at 68%. Continued coal-outage-driven electric weakness, an affordability-driven adverse rate outcome, or a wildfire-liability event compounds the derating. This maps to roughly the 52-week low of $55.79 to low-$60s, a ~15–20% decline from current levels.

The base case is an orderly close and bounded convergence toward the low-to-mid $80s, with the return dominated by deal outcome rather than standalone fundamentals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The equity is a fixed-ratio, premium-free merger-close position; the standalone business offers mid-single-digit rate-base growth with no asymmetric re-rating catalyst, and the deal offers bounded convergence upside against genuine Montana-approval tail risk.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Montana (MPSC) approval on terms that preserve deal economics, plus FERC/HSR clearance, materially de-risking a 2H 2026 close and widening the NWE-convergence path.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that restores standalone valuation support (~16–17x forward with the ~3.75% yield) alongside evidence the electric-segment outage issues are resolved.
- Confirmed synergy quantification post-close and restored ≥5% dividend-growth capacity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Montana condition that strips a material portion of deal synergies, or a credible signal the merger will fail or slip beyond mid-2027.
- A ratings action toward the BBB-/Baa3 boundary that raises financing costs and threatens collateral postings.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (payout sustained above ~75%) or a wildfire-liability event exceeding insurance limits.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Montana MPSC decision and any accompanying FERC/HSR progress, plus the next quarterly print, both expected within the 2H 2026 close window.

## Conclusion

Black Hills is a competent regulated multi-utility with a real data-center load-growth differentiator, but its equity is now a proxy for the NorthWestern merger. The fixed 0.98x all-stock structure with no premium means the price tracks NWE and completion odds rather than standalone earnings, and the return profile is a bounded convergence toward Bright Horizon Energy economics against a specific Montana-approval binary. At ~19.3x earnings — below the 21.1x peer median but backed by negative FCF, D/E of 2.84x, falling near-term estimates, and rising financing costs — the standalone valuation is a value-trap-shaped discount rather than an opportunity. Analyst targets of $80–$87 reflect deal economics, not standalone upside, and the elevated, backwardated implied volatility confirms the market is pricing an imminent, dated resolution. We maintain HOLD, uncertain, with the trajectory of recent scores (5.9 → 6.0 → 5.8) consistent with a business whose fundamentals are stable while the investment case narrows to a single event outcome. The bar for a BUY is Montana clearance on economics-preserving terms or a standalone-value pullback into the low-$60s; the bar for AVOID is a deal-impairing condition or a financing/credit deterioration.
