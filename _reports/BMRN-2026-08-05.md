## Executive Summary

BioMarin is a profitable, cash-generative rare-disease company that has, over roughly seven months, converted a net-cash balance sheet into approximately $4.3B of gross debt, written off its gene therapy franchise, lost monopoly status in its fastest-growing indication, and trimmed EPS guidance while raising revenue guidance. The stock trades at 12.3x the $4.95 midpoint of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance and 9.5x next-year consensus of $6.39. Both figures look cheap against any large-cap pharmaceutical benchmark. Neither is a like-for-like comparison to a GAAP earnings stream: BioMarin's non-GAAP measure excludes stock-based compensation that ran $43.5M in Q1 2026 alone (~$174M annualized, ~$0.88/share pre-tax on 197.7M diluted shares) plus intangible amortization the company states will "significantly increase" following the Amicus close. Adjusting for both, GAAP earnings power for FY2026 is plausibly $3.00–3.30, which puts the stock at 18–20x GAAP, in line with, not below, integrated pharma.

The operating question is narrower and more important than the multiple. VOXZOGO, ~$926.9M of FY2025 revenue and about 29% of the total, grew **2.9%** in Q1 2026 ($219.9M vs $213.7M) after growing roughly 26% in FY2025 and 56% in FY2024, and that deceleration occurred in a quarter that closed *before* Ascendis' once-weekly YUVIWEL (navepegritide, FDA accelerated approval February 27, 2026; orphan drug exclusivity granted April 6, 2026) became commercially available. BioMarin spent 28% more on VOXZOGO sales and marketing in the quarter ($34.5M → $44.3M) to produce that 2.9%. Separately, of the $25.5M of total net product revenue growth in Q1, approximately $22.2M is attributable to the year-over-year swing in disclosed FX impact (+$8.6M in Q1 2026 versus −$13.6M in Q1 2025), leaving roughly $3.3M — about 0.4% — of underlying growth, itself carried by lumpy government orders in the Middle East and Latin America.

We are maintaining a HOLD, but the composition of that HOLD has changed. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD and the score has declined from 6.5 in May to 5.3 in July to 4.9 now. The justification for not moving to AVOID is timing, not conviction: the first quarter consolidating Amicus is due within days of this report's cutoff, near-term implied volatility of 62.9% against 33.4% realized prices a ±10.6% move, and post-close net debt, purchase accounting, VOXZOGO's response to Yuviwel and any guidance revision all resolve in a single disclosure. Shorting into that with 7.7 days to cover, call-tilted open interest and an $87.92 consensus mean target is poor mechanics. Existing holders should wait for the print; new capital should not be committed ahead of it.

## Company Overview

BioMarin operates as a single reporting segment with consolidated net income as the segment profit measure, so there is no divisional P&L to triangulate. Eight commercial products as of March 31, 2026, with Q1 2026 net product revenue of $760.1M:

| Product | Indication | Q1 2026 | Y/Y |
|---|---|---|---|
| VOXZOGO | Achondroplasia | $219.9M | +2.9% |
| VIMIZIM | MPS IVA | $210.2M | +11.6% |
| NAGLAZYME | MPS VI | $130.1M | +13.8% |
| PALYNZIQ | PKU | $89.6M | −4.0% |
| BRINEURA | CLN2 | $47.1M | +16.6% |
| ALDURAZYME | MPS I | $36.7M | −25.1% |
| KUVAN | PKU | $23.9M | −4.8% |
| ROCTAVIAN | Hemophilia A | $2.6M | −75% |

Two structural features matter more than the list. First, the moat rests on **orphan drug exclusivity rather than composition-of-matter patents**: the 10-Q states that patent protection for several products and candidates is "particularly uncertain because key information on some of our product candidates has existed in the public domain for many years." Second, revenue is lumpy by design: management attributes VIMIZIM and NAGLAZYME growth to "timing of orders in countries that place large government orders, primarily from countries in the Middle East and Latin America," and warns the timing "will continue to be inconsistent." Roughly two-thirds of revenue is ex-U.S. (Q1 2026: U.S. $242.9M, Europe $218.8M, Latin America $108.2M, RoW $153.4M, plus $36.7M ALDURAZYME sold by Sanofi). No backlog or remaining performance obligation is disclosed, so forward order visibility is zero from outside.

On April 27, 2026 BioMarin closed the acquisition of Amicus Therapeutics at $14.50/share in cash (~$4.8B equity value) plus repayment of ~$433.0M of Amicus debt, ~$5.2B aggregate. It adds GALAFOLD (oral migalastat, Fabry), POMBILITI + OPFOLDA (Pompe) and U.S. rights to DMX-200 (Phase 3, FSGS). Both commercial assets are lysosomal storage disease therapies adjacent to the existing enzyme replacement franchise, which is the stated strategic rationale and is credible on commercial-infrastructure grounds.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025: revenue up, everything below it down

Revenue reached approximately $3.22B, up ~12.9% from ~$2.85B. Cost of revenue rose ~23.6%, compressing gross margin from 79.7% to 77.7%; R&D rose ~23% to ~$922M; SG&A rose ~14% to ~$1,153M. Operating income fell to ~$409.5M from ~$484M, and net income to ~$348.9M from ~$426.9M. A 12.9% revenue increase produced a ~15% operating income decline.

The dominant driver was the decision to withdraw ROCTAVIAN, carrying ~$240.0M of restructuring charges (inventory write-off, long-lived asset impairment, severance). Adding that back gives roughly $650M of adjusted operating income, a ~20% margin, so underlying profit did grow. The economic content of the charge is that a decade-long gene therapy investment was written to zero.

### Q1 2026: the deterioration accelerated, with one identifiable one-time cause

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenues | $766.2M | $745.1M | +2.8% |
| Cost of sales | $195.0M | $151.6M | +28.6% |
| Gross margin | 74.5% | 79.7% | −520bp |
| R&D | $178.8M | $158.7M | +12.7% |
| SG&A | $258.3M | $206.1M | +25.3% |
| Income from operations | $129.6M | $223.9M | −42.1% |
| Interest expense | $15.0M | $2.9M | +417% |
| Diluted GAAP EPS | $0.54 | $0.95 | −43.2% |

The margin collapse has a named cause: a $31.0M charge for an unsuccessful process qualification campaign to expand NAGLAZYME manufacturing capacity. Removing it gives cost of sales of $164.0M and gross margin of 78.6% — still down ~110bp year over year. Adjusted operating income becomes $160.6M, a 21.0% margin versus 30.0%, so even excluding the charge operating leverage ran sharply negative. SG&A is the culprit: G&A alone rose 28.8% ($103.6M → $133.4M) on corporate initiatives and Amicus pre-close costs, while sales and marketing rose 21.9%.

**A correction to the reported surprise record.** The supplied beat/miss table shows three misses in four quarters, including −137.7% and −142.2% in Q3 and Q4 2025. Those compare GAAP actuals (which absorbed the ROCTAVIAN and Inozyme IPR&D charges) against what are almost certainly non-GAAP consensus estimates — a basis mismatch that overstates the operating shortfall. The same applies to Q1 2026: the table shows $0.54 against $0.77, but Q1 non-GAAP EPS was $0.76, essentially in line. The genuine negative signal is not the surprise history; it is the **estimate revisions**, which are internally consistent: current-year consensus $4.93 is down 7.8% over 90 days and next year $6.39 is down 5.3%. Near-quarter estimates are marginally higher (+3.2% and +5.0% over 90 days), which is consistent with back-half loading rather than with improving full-year earnings power.

### Cash flow and capital structure

Q1 operating cash flow was $220.7M against capex of $20.9M, implying ~$199.8M of free cash flow. This should not be annualized: it included a $44.5M inventory release, a favorable receivables swing (−$7.2M versus −$57.6M) and cash taxes of only $14.6M against a $35.7M book provision. A four-quarter run rate would be ~$800M; a normalized figure stripping the inventory and receivables timing benefits lands nearer **$500–600M**, and that is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

The balance sheet at March 31, 2026 was the last clean picture: cash and equivalents $2,222.4M plus $850.0M restricted, total assets $8,591.0M, total liabilities $2,378.9M, equity $6,212.1M (book value $32.14/share, tangible $30.07/share), long-term debt $1,430.3M ($850M of 5.5% 2034 Notes plus $600M of 1.25% convertible 2027 Notes). Deferred tax assets were $1,500.6M, 17.5% of assets. Inventory was $1,273.2M — roughly 6.5x quarterly cost of sales — with work-in-process rising from $801.1M to $1,054.6M while finished goods fell from $391.3M to $120.8M.

The acquisition changed the capital structure materially. Funding was ~$1.6B of cash on hand, $850M of 2034 Notes, and **$2.8B of senior secured term loans** ($2.0B Term Loan B due April 2033; $800M Term Loan A due April 2031), plus a new $600M undrawn secured revolver due 2031. The company states directly that total indebtedness rose from ~$1.5B to **~$4.3B**. Estimated post-close BioMarin legacy cash is ~$620M plus whatever came with Amicus (not disclosed), putting net debt in a **$3.3–3.7B** range. Against a pro-forma EBITDA build of roughly $1.5–1.6B (non-GAAP net income midpoint ~$990M plus ~$200M net interest, ~$300M taxes, ~$60M depreciation), net leverage is approximately **2.1–2.4x** and gross leverage **2.7–2.9x**. These are approximations with two soft inputs and are not covenant calculations; the specific financial maintenance tests on the 2026 secured facilities were not disclosed in the filings, which is a genuine gap.

The nearest hard obligation is the **$600M convertible due May 2027**. Implied conversion price is $137.46 ($600M / 4,365 thousand shares in the diluted count), or roughly 130% above the current price, so it will be settled in cash. Refinancing a 1.25% coupon at current rates is a further step-up in interest expense. The offsetting fact is that four quarters of even normalized free cash flow before May 2027 could cover it, and the revolver is undrawn.

A separate dilution note for accuracy: 13,803 thousand equity-award shares were excluded as anti-dilutive in Q1 — 7.1% of shares outstanding, or 6.7% of post-issuance ownership if all were issued. They are anti-dilutive because they are underwater. They belong in a recovery scenario, not in current fully diluted capitalization. The pool grew from 11,686 thousand a year earlier, which is itself a measure of how far grant prices sit above the market.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 revenue guidance of $3,825–3,925M implies 19–22% growth over ~$3.22B. Essentially all of it is the Amicus consolidation from Q2. Organic growth in Q1 was approximately 0.4% once the year-over-year FX swing is neutralized. The narrower and fully defensible version of that statement — that Q1 2026 carried an $8.6M (~1.1%) FX tailwind — still leaves underlying growth in the low single digits, and BioMarin's hedging program partly offsets the reported translation benefit in cash terms ($12.4M of cash-flow-hedge losses reclassified into revenue in Q1 2026 versus $12.5M of gains a year earlier).

The composition of that flat organic base matters. Growth came from VIMIZIM (+$21.9M) and NAGLAZYME (+$15.8M) on government order timing, against declines in PALYNZIQ, ALDURAZYME, KUVAN and ROCTAVIAN. VOXZOGO contributed $6.2M.

**Guidance shape is the sharpest near-term risk.** Non-GAAP EPS guidance was trimmed to $4.85–5.05 from $4.95–5.15 *while revenue guidance was raised*: acquisition financing costs are biting harder than the acquired revenue helps. Q1 non-GAAP EPS was $0.76 against $1.13 a year earlier, and management indicated Q2 modestly above Q1 with roughly two-thirds of the year in the second half. At the $4.95 midpoint, H1 lands near $1.56–1.61, leaving **$3.34–3.39 for H2, or 2.1–2.2x the H1 run rate**. That bridge requires two full quarters of GALAFOLD and POMBILITI+OPFOLDA contribution, synergy capture, non-repeat of the $31M NAGLAZYME charge and Amicus pre-close costs, and cooperative government-order timing — simultaneously. Back-half loading is normal for this business and an acquisition closing one month into Q2 mechanically front-loads cost, so the shape is not itself evidence of an unachievable number. It does leave no margin for one soft order quarter.

The pipeline has produced more subtractions than additions over the last three quarters. Positives: CANOPY-HCH-3 Phase 3 succeeded in hypochondroplasia (May 2026, with three-year data at ENDO 2026 in June); PALYNZIQ was approved for adolescents 12+ in February 2026; the VOXZOGO full-approval sNDA was accepted by the FDA in July 2026; BMN 351 showed dose-dependent dystrophin increases in DMD (March 2026, against historical matched controls — hypothesis-generating only); BMN 333, BioMarin's own long-acting CNP, enrolled its first registration-enabling patient in April 2026. Negatives: ROCTAVIAN withdrawn; BMN 401 met the plasma pyrophosphate co-primary but missed the radiographic co-primary in May 2026; and three VOXZOGO Phase 2 programs (Turner syndrome, SHOX deficiency, ACAN deficiency) were discontinued in March 2026, narrowing the label-expansion story precisely as the achondroplasia base came under pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

**Achondroplasia (VOXZOGO, ~29% of FY2025 revenue): position degraded.** BioMarin held a de facto monopoly from 2021 until February 27, 2026. Yuviwel is once-weekly against a daily injection in a pediatric population, with pivotal data showing a 1.49 cm/year greater annualized growth velocity versus placebo. That Ascendis obtained orphan drug exclusivity notwithstanding VOXZOGO's prior approval implies the FDA treated navepegritide as either structurally distinct or clinically superior — either determination weakens the exclusivity shield BioMarin relies on in place of strong patents. BioMarin's own risk factor on VOXZOGO competition was flagged as substantively updated, and the MD&A cross-references it directly in the revenue discussion.

The critical nuance is that VOXZOGO's Q1 growth predates the competitor's availability. The most plausible explanation is that the diagnosed, launched-market patient base is approaching saturation and new starts no longer outrun discontinuations, with share loss still ahead of the reported numbers. An alternative is anticipatory — prescribers pausing starts ahead of an approved alternative — which is competitive but leaves the underlying base healthier. Q2 and Q3 VOXZOGO revenue distinguishes these, and nothing in the current data set does. BioMarin's three responses (BMN 333, years from market; hypochondroplasia expansion, first-mover; full approval conversion) are all valid but none defends the installed base in 2026.

**Enzyme replacement (VIMIZIM, NAGLAZYME, ALDURAZYME, BRINEURA): durable but slow.** Entrenched ultra-rare biologics with no biosimilars today. The 10-Q flags that these could serve as reference products for biosimilar applications and that FDA biosimilarity requirements are loosening. The company also carries a risk factor noting that widespread gene therapy adoption would reduce ERT demand — a threat that, having written off its own gene therapy program, would now arrive from a competitor's platform. ALDURAZYME is not fully controlled: Sanofi markets it exclusively under an agreement terminable by either party on one year's notice.

**PKU (PALYNZIQ, KUVAN): already eroded.** KUVAN is genericized in the U.S. and EU and has fallen to $23.9M a quarter. PALYNZIQ carries a REMS program requiring prescriber and pharmacy certification, patient enrollment and co-prescribed epinephrine — real adoption friction. The February 2026 adolescent approval is the growth lever on a product that declined 4% in Q1.

**Fabry and Pompe (acquired): contested markets, different risk profile.** GALAFOLD is an oral small molecule, which introduces ANDA/Hatch-Waxman generic exposure that BioMarin's biologics do not carry — the 10-Q's Amicus risk factor names Hatch-Waxman litigation explicitly among potentially assumed liabilities. Both indications have established large-cap incumbents. The genuine synergy is BioMarin's ~35-country rare-disease commercial and access infrastructure carrying additional molecules at low marginal cost.

**Sector-wide compression applies to everyone:** IRA Medicare negotiation reaching Part B in 2028 and 20 drugs annually from 2029; CMS proposed mandatory MFN demonstration models (December 23, 2025); EU HTA regulation already producing downward pricing pressure; EU political agreement (December 11, 2025) to shorten regulatory exclusivity, which is directly material to a company leaning on exclusivity rather than patents.

The acquisition genuinely diversifies **product and indication** exposure, reducing single-asset dependence on VOXZOGO. It does not demonstrate reduced **customer** concentration. Q1 2026 top three customers were 15%/11%/10% = 36% of net product revenue versus 37% a year earlier, and one customer represented 20% of accounts receivable. Rare-disease distribution runs through the same narrow set of specialty pharmacies, distributors and government purchasers regardless of molecule count. The measure to watch in Q2 is the customer table, not the product table.

## Risk Assessment

**Yuviwel's commercial traction is entirely unquantified.** The approval is confirmed; uptake, payer coverage and switching data are not available from any source in this data set. This is the largest single unmeasured variable in the earnings model.

**Q2 2026 results are unreported and are the dominant near-term binary.** No Q2 earnings 8-K appears in the index through July 13, 2026. Post-close operating performance, actual Amicus contribution, post-close net debt and cash, purchase price allocation, and whether FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed are all unknown. Based on the prior-year calendar (Q2 2025 reported August 4, 2025), the release falls within days of this report's information cutoff.

**Leverage and covenants.** Gross debt is now secured (a structural change in the relative asset position of the 2027 and 2034 noteholders), with interest expense up 417% year over year in Q1 and expected to increase significantly, against interest income that will decline. Specific financial maintenance ratios and current headroom on the 2026 facilities were not disclosed. The $600M convertible matures in May 2027 in cash.

**DOJ subpoena, unaccrued, fourth year.** BioMarin received a DOJ subpoena regarding sponsored testing programs relating to VIMIZIM and NAGLAZYME, first disclosed in the FY2023 10-K, with no assurance regarding outcome. Item 1 Legal Proceedings reads "None," which under the company's stated accrual policy implies no probable and estimable loss has been recorded — not that exposure is absent. VIMIZIM and NAGLAZYME together were $340.3M, or ~45% of Q1 net product revenue.

**Pharmaceutical tariffs, direct channel.** A proclamation imposing 100% tariffs on patented pharmaceutical products and ingredients has been announced; BioMarin manufactures VIMIZIM and BRINEURA and packages VOXZOGO and PALYNZIQ in Shanbally, Cork. The Supreme Court's invalidation of IEEPA-based tariffs leaves the magnitude genuinely unresolved, and BioMarin cannot quantify it.

**Single-site manufacturing.** Galli Drive, Novato, California is the sole facility for ALDURAZYME, NAGLAZYME, VOXZOGO and PALYNZIQ and one of two for VIMIZIM, in a seismically active area. The $31M failed NAGLAZYME qualification sits underneath one of the few growing franchises and against sole-site supply.

**Inventory concentration.** $1,273.2M of inventory against $195.0M of quarterly cost of sales concentrates write-down risk, as the ROCTAVIAN write-off within the $240M charge demonstrated. The WIP/finished-goods rotation is a composition change most plausibly reflecting campaign timing or the newly completed ERP conversion; no impairment was recorded, and this is a monitoring item rather than evidence of deterioration.

**Governance.** Five-year cumulative TSR of $67.77 per $100 invested versus $124.75 for the peer group (−32.2% vs +24.8%). A December 2023 cooperation agreement with Elliott expired in December 2024, and a dedicated activist risk factor remains. A director departure was disclosed June 4, 2026 without stated reason.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $86 (+41.6%)

BioMarin is a profitable rare-disease commercialization platform trading at 9.5x next-year consensus while the market extrapolates one quarter of VOXZOGO deceleration into permanent franchise decline. Q1's headline weakness was substantially explained by two items that do not repeat: the $31M NAGLAZYME qualification charge (which alone accounts for roughly 400bp of the 520bp gross margin decline) and Amicus pre-close costs inside a 28.8% G&A increase. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.76 was in line with consensus once bases are matched. Amicus adds two commercial lysosomal storage disease products into an existing 35-country access infrastructure at low marginal cost, materially reducing single-asset dependence. Near-term catalysts are dense and dated: FDA acceptance of the VOXZOGO full-approval sNDA (July 2026), first-mover position in hypochondroplasia after a positive Phase 3, PALYNZIQ adolescent expansion, and BMN 333 in registration-enabling studies. Free cash flow of $199.8M in a single quarter — even normalized to $500–600M annually — covers the May 2027 convertible from operations, and net leverage of ~2.2x is unremarkable for pharma. If FY2026 guidance is delivered and FY2027 lands at $6.39, the stock re-rates toward the $86 analyst median.

### Bear Case — $44 (−27.5%)

The growth engine has stopped and the balance sheet flexibility that used to absorb setbacks is gone. VOXZOGO grew 2.9% on a 28% increase in promotional spend *before* a once-weekly competitor with its own orphan exclusivity reached the market; the base case for Q2 onward is sequential decline. Total organic growth was ~0.4% ex-FX and depends on Middle East and Latin America government orders the company itself says will be inconsistent. FY2026 guidance requires 2.1–2.2x the H1 EPS run rate in the second half, and EPS guidance was already trimmed once while revenue was raised — financing costs are outrunning acquired contribution. Full-year estimates have fallen 7.8% (2026) and 5.3% (2027) in 90 days. Gross debt tripled to ~$4.3B, now secured, with undisclosed maintenance covenants and a $600M cash maturity ten months out that will refinance far above a 1.25% coupon. Tangible book of ~$30/share will be largely consumed by goodwill and intangibles, thinning the equity cushion behind secured debt. Management has written off ROCTAVIAN, failed a manufacturing qualification, killed three Phase 2s, delivered one mixed Phase 3, produced a −32% five-year TSR against +24.8% for peers, and has bought no stock in the open market. At 18–20x estimated GAAP earnings with a shrinking core franchise, there is no valuation support at $60.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold_rationale: uncertain).** The decisive evidence is days away, and neither the bull nor the bear case can be settled without it. Score trajectory has declined 6.5 (May) → 5.8 (Jun) → 5.3 (Jul) → 4.9 now, driven by competitive position and risk. A fifth consecutive HOLD reflects a balanced setup at a decision point, not conviction; the standing bias for new capital is negative.

**Upgrade triggers (would move toward BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 VOXZOGO revenue flat or better sequentially versus $219.9M, with management quantifying Yuviwel's share impact. This is the single most important number in the release.
2. FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance reaffirmed at $4.85–5.05 *with* disclosed post-close net debt at or below ~$3.4B and Amicus contribution consistent with the H2 bridge. Reaffirmation alone is insufficient — the H2 arithmetic must be supported.

## Conclusion

BioMarin is priced as though its 12x non-GAAP multiple is the operative fact. The operative facts are that VOXZOGO — 29% of revenue and the entire growth narrative for three years — grew 2.9% in a quarter that closed before its first competitor reached pharmacies, that organic revenue growth net of FX was approximately 0.4%, that management responded to this by levering the balance sheet from $1.5B to $4.3B to buy diversification, and that consensus full-year estimates have fallen 7.8% while the guidance requires more than two-thirds of annual EPS in the second half. Adjusting the multiple for stock compensation and rising acquisition amortization puts the stock at 18–20x GAAP, in line with integrated pharma that offers a dividend and a broader base.

Against that, the business generates real cash, the balance sheet at ~2.2x net leverage is serviceable, the acquired products fit the existing commercial infrastructure, and there is a genuine catalyst calendar in hypochondroplasia and VOXZOGO full approval. Our base case of $66 offers 8.7% against a bear case of $44 — insufficient reward for the leverage and competitive uncertainty, but not sufficiently negative to warrant selling into an unreported quarter with 62.9% implied volatility and a consensus mean target 45% above the market.

**HOLD.** Existing holders should carry the position through the Q2 release, which resolves post-close net debt, Amicus contribution and the VOXZOGO trajectory in one disclosure. New capital should wait for either that resolution or a price near $50. If Q2 shows sequential VOXZOGO decline alongside any guidance trim, the correct call becomes AVOID and the exit should be taken without waiting for a better price.
