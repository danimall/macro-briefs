## Executive Summary

Stewart is the fourth-largest U.S. title insurance underwriter, and its FY2026 results so far illustrate the difference between revenue growth and earnings growth in this business. Q2 2026 revenue rose 24.5% to $899.2M, beating consensus by 6.2%. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.39 missed the $1.63 consensus by 14.7%. The mechanism is visible in the segment note: title operating revenue grew 15.4% to $683.6M, but amounts retained by independent agencies grew 24.9% to $314.9M, so revenue net of agency retention grew only 8.3% while title employee and other operating costs grew 10.7%. Title segment pretax income fell 1.4% and segment margin compressed to 6.9% from 8.1%. Gross-recognized agency revenue contributes roughly 16.5 cents of gross margin per dollar; it inflates the top line and dilutes the margin, and a quarterly mix shift toward that channel is sufficient to produce a revenue beat and an earnings miss simultaneously.

Two other things changed since our June review. First, the housing assumption underpinning the cyclical-recovery leg of the thesis has been marked down twice: the FY2025 10-K (February 2026) cited third-party forecasts of +7% existing home sales for 2026, the Q2 10-Q (August 4) cites approximately +3%, and management framed it at roughly +2% on the July 23 call, keeping volumes "solidly in the low $4 million" range. Q2 closed orders fell 8% to 61,309, purchase closed orders were flat, and domestic non-commercial direct title revenue fell 1%. The growth came from commercial orders (+21%) and higher fee per file. Second, consensus has responded: FY2026 adjusted EPS estimates fell from $6.16 to $5.52 over 90 days (-10.3%), the current quarter fell 13.3%, and FY2027 fell 3.7% to $7.28. That is negative earnings momentum, not the estimate-revision profile that supports a purchase.

Against that, the business is not deteriorating in an absolute sense. Half-year title pretax income rose 20% with margin improving to 5.6% from 5.4% — Q2 was the weak quarter, not the year. Employee costs fell to 28.1% of operating revenue from 32.2% in 2023, real labor leverage on a headcount that grew from 6,800 to roughly 8,200. Claims experience is improving: total claim payments fell to $76.6M in 2025 from $85.4M and roughly $104M in the two prior years, and reserves sit above the actuarial midpoint. The dividend has been raised four consecutive years to a $2.10 annual rate (3.1% yield) and was covered 2.3x by FY2025 free cash flow. At 15.0x TTM GAAP EPS and 1.25x book, the shares are neither obviously cheap nor stretched. We move to a lower overall score on genuine fundamental developments — the Q2 earnings miss, the estimate cuts, the reduced housing framing — while holding the rating at HOLD, with $73 base, $88 bull and $54 bear cases.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1893 and headquartered in Houston, Stewart operates three reporting segments.

**Title insurance and related services** (~78-80% of FY2025 operating revenue) runs two channels with very different economics. Direct operations — Stewart's own offices and centralized service centers — generated $1,157.5M of FY2025 revenue. The independent agency channel books revenue gross, with 83.0% retained by agents in FY2025 and 83.5% in Q2 2026, implying gross agency revenue of roughly $1,262M in FY2025. Geographic concentration: Texas 15% of title operating revenue, New York 11%, international (Canada, U.K., Australia) 6%, Ohio 6%, California 5%, Florida 5%.

**Real estate solutions** has grown from roughly 11.9% of operating revenue in 2023 to 15.3% in 2025 and 21.8% in H1 2026. It includes credit and property data (Informative Research, PropStream, BatchLeads), valuation management, online notarization and closing, and — new in Q4 2025 — property preservation and field services via the Mortgage Contracting Services carve-out. This is a pass-through-heavy business: in Q2 2026 other operating expenses of $144.6M on $197.4M of segment revenue means roughly 73% of segment revenue is third-party appraiser and service cost.

**Corporate** is the parent and centralized support. The regulated underwriter, Stewart Title Guaranty Company, is rated A− by Fitch and A.M. Best; holding-company senior notes are rated BBB by Fitch.

## Financial Analysis

### Screening artifacts that must be discarded

Two widely circulated metrics on STC are wrong. SEC XBRL returns "revenue" of $438.3M for FY2025 and $358.6M for FY2024 — those are real estate solutions *segment* revenue, reconcilable to the 10-K's disclosed segment growth ($263.9M → $358.9M → $438.6M) to within $0.3M, not consolidated operating revenue of $2.42B and $2.86B. Any net margin computed off that base (29.7% appears in screens) is meaningless; true FY2025 net margin attributable to Stewart is roughly 4.0% ($115.5M ÷ ~$2.86B). Asset turnover corrects from 0.13 to 0.88 ($2,860M ÷ $3,252.8M assets).

The 1.46 Altman Z-Score is similarly unusable: two of five inputs (working capital/assets, EBIT/assets) returned zero because insurers do not present a classified balance sheet, and a third used the mis-tagged revenue. Substituting corrected asset turnover and populating EBIT/assets (pretax $165.6M + interest $20.4M = $186.0M ÷ $3,252.8M = 0.057) puts a reworked score near 2.2-2.4 — mediocre, not distressed. Corroborating evidence: company-calculated debt-to-equity of 39% and debt-to-capitalization of 28% at both 12/31/25 and 6/30/26, $914.9M of total cash and investments, FY2025 operating cash flow of $205.7M, BBB senior notes. Note also that $524.5M of the liabilities in the crude "total liabilities ÷ equity" ratio is the actuarial title-loss reserve, not borrowed money.

### Margin trajectory

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating revenue | ~$2,213M | $2,420M | $2,860M | $1,645.5M |
| Pretax income (pre-NCI) | $60.9M | $114.3M | $165.6M | $78.7M |
| Pretax margin | 2.7% | 4.6% | 5.7% | 4.8% |
| Net income attrib. STC | $30.4M | $73.3M | $115.5M | $54.2M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $2.61 | $4.05 | $1.76 |

Pretax margin was 13.1% in 2021. That peak is not the right normalization target — 2021 was a refinancing boom, and the mix has since shifted toward pass-through-heavy real estate solutions, which structurally lowers consolidated margin even when the underlying businesses perform. The useful cost detail: employee costs fell 32.2% → 28.1% of revenue across 2023-H1 2026 (genuine leverage), other operating expenses rose 22.9% → 27.9% (almost entirely the real estate solutions pass-through), and the title loss provision fell 4.1% → 3.1%.

### Cash flow, with the seasonality caveat

FY2025: operating cash flow $205.7M less $73.4M of long-lived asset purchases = $132.3M free cash flow against $58.5M of dividends, 2.3x coverage. H1 2026: operating cash flow $56.0M less $41.9M capex = $14.1M against $32.7M of dividends — uncovered in the half. The comparison that matters is H1 2025 operating cash flow of $23.5M against a full-year $205.7M, meaning H2 2025 alone produced $182.2M. Q1 is the least active title quarter, and H1 2026 absorbed a $39.6M receivables increase consistent with 26% revenue growth and the MCS consolidation. If H2 2026 repeats H2 2025, FY2026 operating cash flow reaches roughly $238M and free cash flow roughly $153M against ~$65M of dividends. A flat-H2 scenario with no working-capital release produces roughly $112M of operating cash flow, $27M of free cash flow, and an uncovered dividend. Disclosed seasonality argues for the first case; the range is the honest presentation.

### Balance sheet: the equity base is nearly all intangible

At 6/30/26: total assets $3,291.5M, equity attributable to Stewart $1,663.4M, goodwill $1,293.8M, net intangibles $321.6M. Goodwill plus intangibles of $1,615.5M equals 97.1% of attributable equity and 49.1% of total assets, implying tangible book value of roughly $48M, or about $1.58 per share against stated book of $54.63. Stewart passed quantitative impairment tests at every reporting unit in both 2024 and 2025, and MCS purchase accounting remains provisional within its one-year measurement window (H1 2026 already recorded $4.1M of adjustments increasing real estate solutions goodwill). For a title insurer, the binding capital constraint is Guaranty's statutory surplus (implied roughly $827M from the disclosed $165.4M threshold representing 20% of surplus), not tangible book. Still, the reported 1.25x P/B materially understates the accounting sensitivity to a writedown.

Debt is $646.7M ($446.4M of 3.6% senior notes plus a $200.0M revolver draw). H1 2026 interest expense of $15.1M is up 52% year over year, implying roughly $30M annualized at a blended cost near 4.3%; the incremental revolver cost derives to roughly 5-6%. Credit facility covenants, pricing and maturity are not disclosed in the filings reviewed, and the senior-notes maturity was not verified — both are open items.

### Parent-company liquidity

Cash at the parent and unregulated subsidiaries fell from $150.3M at 12/31/25 to $123.4M at 6/30/26. Guaranty upstreamed $173.0M to the parent in 2025 and $30.0M in 2024, but $0 in H1 2026 and $0 in H1 2025. Parent obligations run roughly $95M annually (~$65M common dividends plus ~$30M interest). The pattern is second-half-weighted upstreaming, so this is a timing dependency rather than a shortfall — but it is the precise channel through which a Texas Department of Insurance objection or a ratings-driven surplus constraint would reach the common dividend.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is strong and its composition is unfavorable. Of the 24.5% Q2 revenue increase, the title contribution was 15.4% gross but only 8.3% net of agency retention, and real estate solutions carries roughly 73% third-party cost. The consequence is that consolidated revenue can grow 25% while consolidated pretax income grows in the single digits.

Volume is not the driver. Q2 total closed orders fell 8%, purchase closed orders were flat at 35,870, and "other" closed orders (real-estate investor and reverse mortgage) fell 51% to 6,939. The offsets were commercial closed orders +21% and average domestic residential fee per file rising to $3,200 from $2,900. Nationally, existing home sales were essentially flat in 2025 at 4.08 million while the median existing-home price hit an all-time high of $440,600 in June 2026 — the 36th consecutive month of year-over-year price increases. Title premiums scale with price, so Stewart's revenue can grow without volume recovery, but fee-per-file expansion is a weaker and more mean-reverting driver than order growth, and record prices are themselves what suppresses volume.

The commercial line deserves specific attention. FY2025 domestic commercial revenue rose 35% to $339.2M, attributed in the 10-K to "data center, energy, retail and mixed-use asset classes," and H1 2026 commercial fee per file rose 15% to $18,800 "primarily driven by higher data center transaction size and volume." Commercial has grown from 21.6% of direct title revenue in 2023 to 29.3% in 2025. Stewart has become a second-order participant in the AI infrastructure buildout, since premiums scale with transaction value and a handful of large closings can move a quarter. That is differentiated upside while data-center financing remains available — and concentrated, unsizable downside if it tightens. Stewart discloses no commercial revenue by asset class. The signal to watch: Q2 2026 commercial fee per file was $16,900, flat year over year, described as reflecting transaction mix. One quarter does not distinguish an early plateau from ordinary mix noise, but the tailwind stopped compounding in the most recent print.

The acquisition path is the third growth leg. MCS (Q4 2025, the largest acquisition in company history, externally reported at $330M but unverified in filings) was funded with a $200M revolver draw and 2,185,000 new shares for $140.8M net — roughly 7.2% post-issuance ownership dilution. Management has said it is positioned to close several small-to-midsize deals, mostly "micro-deals designed to fill geographic or product gaps," and a July 2026 investor deck reportedly targets up to $1 billion of annual lender-services revenue within three years against $438.6M in FY2025 and roughly $717M annualizing off H1 2026. That target reaches us only through a third-party summary of an exhibit we could not retrieve; treat it as issuer framing, not guidance. Achieving it would require substantial further acquisition, and therefore further capital.

The countercyclical logic of MCS is genuine: property preservation and field services are performed on delinquent, foreclosure and REO properties, so volumes move opposite to purchase and refinance title activity. That is a sensible hedge for a company whose 2023 pretax margin collapsed to 2.7%. The corollary is that in a benign credit environment, MCS underdelivers — and Stewart bought it at 2025 pricing with equity issued near $64.

## Valuation Assessment

Anchoring on the authoritative $68.42 close:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| TTM GAAP diluted EPS | $4.57 → **15.0x** |
| TTM constant-share EPS ($134.7M ÷ 30.853M) | $4.37 → **15.6x** |
| FY2026 adjusted consensus $5.52 | **12.4x** |
| FY2027 adjusted consensus $7.28 | **9.4x** |
| Book value $54.63 | **1.25x** |
| Dividend $2.10-2.12 annualized | **3.1% yield** |
| TTM ROE ($134.7M ÷ $1,663.4M equity) | **~8.1%** |

The forward multiples are the seductive part of this story and require two adjustments. First, the consensus is adjusted EPS, which adds back roughly $0.24 per quarter of acquisition intangible amortization. For a company whose growth strategy is acquisition, that amortization is a recurring economic cost of the model, not a one-time item; on a GAAP basis the FY2027 figure would be lower, and the market is paying a higher GAAP multiple than the 9.4x adjusted figure implies. Second, and more important, the consensus rests on three analysts and is falling — down 10.3% for FY2026 and 3.7% for FY2027 in 90 days, with the current quarter cut 13.3%. Estimates falling at that pace argue against treating either the multiple or the $82.25 mean price target as a floor. A cheap-looking multiple built on a declining estimate is not a margin of safety.

The cleaner cross-check is return on equity against price-to-book. Stewart earns roughly 8% on equity. At a 9-10% cost of equity, a justified P/B is 0.8-0.9x; the market is paying 1.25x, which means the price embeds partial cyclical recovery already. That is defensible — mid-cycle earnings power is above trailing — but it removes the claim that the stock is mispriced downward.

Normalizing rather than extrapolating: on the FY2026 revenue run rate of roughly $3.4-3.5B, a 7-8% pretax margin (versus 5.7% in 2025 and 4.8% in H1 2026, and against 13.1% in the 2021 refi boom that the current mix cannot replicate) produces $245-280M pretax, roughly $185-210M net, or $6.00-6.80 of EPS. At 13-14x that is $80-95, the bull case. A base case of $5.00-5.50 of FY2027 GAAP EPS at 13-14x gives $70-77; we set base at **$73**, roughly 7% above the current price, with the 3.1% dividend taking the base-case total return to about 10%. The bear case — housing volumes flat, data-center commercial slows, the loss-provisioning tailwind reverses toward the 3.9% 2024 rate — puts EPS near $4.00 at 13x, or $52, and 1.0x book at $54.63; we use **$54**, about 21% below spot.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives roughly a 5% price return plus 3% dividend. That is a fair, not compelling, expected return.

Peer context is weak here. The same-sector comparables show P/E dispersion from 7.1x (HCI) to 126.4x (EIG) around a 16.9x median — a heterogeneous set with no title-insurance pure play. STC's 15.0x TTM GAAP against that median is unremarkable, and its 3.1% yield against a 1.7% median is a genuine relative support. The more relevant comparison, against FNF, First American and Old Republic, is not in the supplied data; Stewart's structurally lower margin and lower ROE argue for a discount to those names rather than parity.

## Competitive Landscape

Stewart's own 10-K concedes that Fidelity National Financial, First American and Old Republic each has "substantially greater gross revenues" and holding companies with "significantly greater capital." No market-share percentage appears in the filing; an 11% U.S. title-premium share claim comes from an investor deck we could not retrieve.

The trajectory is mixed. On the positive side: commercial closed orders +21% in Q2, agency share gains in 15 target states per management, employee costs down four percentage points of revenue since 2023, and a real estate solutions platform that now approaches 22% of revenue. On the negative side, the 10-K states that financial-strength ratings are "a significant component in determining the competitiveness of insurance companies with respect to commercial title policies" and that large commercial customers "look to the size and financial strength of a title insurer." Stewart is pushing hardest into commercial, the segment where its A− rating and smaller capital base are weakest relative to peers. Management's own framing attributes commercial wins to hiring specialist teams rather than balance-sheet strength, which is a plausible alternative reading — an A− rating clears most counterparty thresholds — but it makes the wins dependent on retained talent rather than structural advantage.

The mix question deserves correction of a common assumption. Gross agency revenue was 44.6% of operating revenue in 2023, 43.1% in 2024, 44.1% in 2025 and 43.1% in H1 2026 — essentially flat. Stewart is not diluting itself into the agency channel on a full-year basis; Q2's agency skew was a quarterly mix effect. The genuine structural change is real estate solutions, and that shift moves revenue toward a pass-through-heavy business. It does not establish customer diversification: Stewart discloses no customer concentration, and MCS serves mortgage servicers, a customer set that is concentrated in a small number of large institutions.

Structural product substitution is a live long-term risk the company names first among its strategic risk factors: title insurance waivers, attorney opinion letters (cheaper, less protective), government initiatives, and AI-driven disruption of search and exam. The AI thread cuts both ways — employee costs are 28% of revenue, making automation Stewart's principal margin lever.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing-cycle assumption already marked down twice.** From +7% cited in February to ~+3% in the 10-Q to ~+2% from the CEO in July. The "cyclical normalization" leg of the bull case is weakening in real time, and Q2 volumes confirm it. By June 2026, mortgage rates averaged 6.4% in Q2 2026 and Q2 originations were up 14% year over year driven by higher refinancing — the direction of risk to residential volumes into 2027 remains tied to affordability at record prices and elevated rates.

**Data-center commercial concentration, unsizable.** A meaningful share of the 2025-26 earnings improvement is levered to AI capex through transaction-value-scaled premiums. No asset-class revenue disclosure exists. Q2 fee per file already flattened.

**Loss-provisioning tailwind is finite, with a 2026 counter-signal.** The 10-K discloses ~$24.2M of FY2025 pretax sensitivity per 100bp of provisioning rate; the ~70bp decline from 2024 to 2025 was worth roughly $17M, about 10% of pretax income, and the further decline to 3.1% in H1 2026 adds roughly $6.5M. Claims data supports the releases as experience-driven. But H1 2026 claim payments rose 21% year over year, with prior-policy-year payments of $37.2M versus $27.9M, while the provisioning rate fell further. That tension warrants monitoring rather than a conclusion about reserve adequacy.

**Goodwill concentration with provisional purchase accounting.** 97.1% of attributable equity is goodwill and intangibles, and MCS purchase accounting is open through Q4 2026.

**Parent liquidity via regulated upstreaming.** Zero dividends from Guaranty in H1 2026 and H1 2025 against ~$95M of annual parent obligations and $123.4M of parent cash. Also unreconciled: Guaranty upstreamed $173.0M in 2025 against a disclosed extraordinary-dividend threshold of ~$165.4M, and the filings do not state whether statutory net operating income was the higher measure or pre-approval was obtained.

**Agency defalcation and regulatory attribution.** The 10-K notes regulators "increasingly seeking to hold title companies responsible for the actions of these title agents," that Stewart is liable in some states regardless of contractual limitations, and that its audit scope is limited by attorney agencies citing client confidentiality. Net defalcation losses were immaterial in each of the last three years.

**Activist exposure.** A standing risk factor addresses prior activist actions and the prospect of a threatened proxy contest. Read against five-year cumulative TSR of $169.53 per $100 versus a peer-group $239.15, this is a live dynamic — arguably a two-sided one, since activist involvement can be a catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (33 contracts across three expirations) so read it as directional color rather than positioning evidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.2% against 31.2% one-year realized is rich by 11 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (42.2% at 13 days versus 32.3% at 104 days), with a 13.7% put/call skew at the September tenor described as elevated put demand. The nearest expiry prices a ±7.6% move. That configuration is consistent with hedging around the Q3 print rather than a structural bearish bet, and it corroborates our reluctance to add capital before the report: the market is paying up for near-term protection on a stock whose last print produced a single-day decline of roughly 8% (from $69.71 on July 22 to $64.25 on July 23). Short interest of 3.6% of float and 3.4 days to cover shows no crowded bearish position, and open interest is negligible on the put side (P/C OI 0.01), so there is no informed-flow signal to weigh against fundamentals. Rich near-term volatility argues that patient buyers are better paid selling puts into the print than buying stock ahead of it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+29%)

Housing volumes trough in 2026 and normalize modestly in 2027 while median prices hold, so fee-per-file gains compound on recovering orders. The front-loaded hiring management cited in Q2 — "significant additional investments in individuals and teams" in three title businesses — converts to revenue in 2027, restoring title margin toward the 8%+ level of Q2 2025 and beyond. Employee costs continue falling as a percentage of revenue on automation, real estate solutions scales toward management's lender-services ambition with MCS providing a countercyclical offset, and commercial data-center title work continues at 2025-26 intensity. Pretax margin reaches 7-8% on a $3.5B revenue base, producing $6.00-6.80 of EPS; at 13-14x that is $80-95. Activist pressure or a strategic transaction in a consolidating industry provides an additional path.

### Bear Case — $54 (-21%)

Existing home sales stay at roughly 4.0-4.1 million through 2027 as mortgage rates remain elevated, purchase orders stay flat and "other" order declines persist. Data-center title volume slows as AI-infrastructure financing repriced — the Q2 flat commercial fee per file was the early tell — removing the single largest driver of the 2025 earnings improvement. The loss-provisioning rate reverses toward 3.9% as H1's 21% increase in claim payments continues, taking roughly $20M off pretax income. Acquisition integration costs and rising interest expense (already +52% year over year) absorb the labor leverage, and a further equity raise to fund the lender-services build dilutes per-share earnings again. EPS settles near $4.00; at 13x, or at 1.0x book, the stock is $52-55. A goodwill writedown against a $48M tangible equity base would be an accounting event with real covenant and rating consequences.

### Base Case — $73 (+7%, ~10% with dividend)

Volumes are flat, price and commercial mix drive mid-single-digit net-of-retention revenue growth, and margin recovers partially as Q2's hiring costs anniversary. FY2027 GAAP EPS reaches $5.00-5.50 at 13-14x. The dividend continues at $2.10-2.20, covered on a full-year basis but not every half.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** The prior trajectory — 6.3/BUY in May, 6.0/HOLD in June — declines again to 5.2 on identifiable fundamental developments rather than price action (the stock is up roughly 3% since the June review at $66.46). Specifically: the Q2 adjusted-EPS miss of 14.7% with title segment pretax income down 1.4% on 15.4% revenue growth; FY2026 consensus cut 10.3% in 90 days; and management's cited housing outlook reduced from +7% to ~+2%. Valuation and risk scores absorb most of the decline. This is the third consecutive non-BUY, and the bar for continuing it is met by the visible deterioration in earnings composition.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the Q3 2026 print (late October) the first decision point.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Title segment net-of-retention revenue growing faster than title operating costs for two consecutive quarters, with segment pretax margin back above 8%. This is the direct reversal of the Q2 mechanism.
2. FY2027 consensus stabilizing or rising for two consecutive months after five months of cuts, combined with a price below $60 (below 1.1x book and under 11x normalized $5.50 GAAP EPS).
3. H2 2026 operating cash flow above $150M, confirming the seasonal pattern and full-year dividend coverage of at least 2x, with Guaranty resuming upstream dividends to the parent.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Commercial closed orders turning negative year over year, or commercial fee per file declining, which would confirm the data-center driver is fading rather than mixing.
2. The title loss provisioning rate rising back above 3.6%, or claim payments continuing to grow above 15% year over year — either would remove roughly $20M of pretax income.
3. Any goodwill impairment charge, a further equity issuance to fund the lender-services target, or disclosure of a credit-facility covenant constraint. Also: a second consecutive quarter of declining title segment pretax income on positive revenue growth.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 results (expected late October), with the Q3 10-Q the first filing to include final MCS purchase accounting. Existing holders should stay for the 3.1% yield and the option on cyclical normalization; new capital has no urgency at $68.42 against a $73 base case, and the rich near-term implied volatility means the print is priced as a two-sided event.

## Conclusion

Stewart is executing a coherent strategy — diversify away from a title cycle stuck at multi-decade-low volumes, build lender services with a countercyclical component, and extract labor leverage from automation — and the strategy has produced real results: revenue up 24%, employee costs down four points of revenue since 2023, claims experience improving, four consecutive dividend increases. The problem at $68.42 is that the market is already paying 1.25x book for an 8% ROE and roughly 15x trailing GAAP earnings, which prices partial recovery, while the two engines of the 2025 earnings improvement — a falling loss-provisioning rate and data-center-driven commercial premiums — are both finite and, on the most recent data, decelerating. The Q2 print showed how quickly gross revenue growth can fail to reach the bottom line in a business where 83.5% of agency revenue goes to the agent, and consensus has cut FY2026 estimates 10% in response.

Management quality is the weakest dimension. CEO compensation rose 45% to $7.87M in a year when five-year cumulative TSR trailed the company's own selected peer group by roughly 70 points, the incentive scorecard contains no return-on-equity or return-on-invested-capital gate while the company issues equity and draws a revolver to buy revenue, and the CFO sold roughly 15% of his position near the top of the 2026 range against a single director's roughly $191,000 of open-market accumulation. None of this is disqualifying; it is not alignment that earns a premium either.

HOLD. Own it for the yield and the recovery option; wait for either title-segment margin evidence or a price near $60 before adding.
