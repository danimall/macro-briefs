## Executive Summary

McKesson reported its June-quarter results on 5 August 2026 and raised FY27 adjusted EPS guidance to $44.20–$45.00 from $43.80–$44.60, the second raise in three months. The operating detail underneath was better than the headline: revenue grew 8% to $105.38B, gross margin expanded to 3.50% from 3.35%, operating expenses grew 5% against 8% revenue growth, and pre-tax income rose 24%. Oncology & Multispecialty revenue grew 33% with segment profit up 53%. GAAP diluted EPS nevertheless fell 18% to $5.15, entirely because of a $374M non-cash adjustment marking redeemable noncontrolling interests toward redemption value ($293M related to the Apollo preferred in Medical-Surgical, $81M to Core Ventures) — roughly $3.14 per share on 119.2M weighted diluted shares. Any screen reading GAAP EPS will record McKesson's earnings as falling while the operating business accelerated.

The stock rose 5.6% on the print, then gave back roughly 4% the following session. At $841.48 it sits 15.8% below the 3 March high of $999 and 32% above the August 2025 low of $637, trading at 18.9x the guided FY27 midpoint of $44.60, 16.7x next-year consensus of $50.38, and a 5.5% free cash flow yield on the stricter FCF definition (operating cash flow of $6.155B less $436M of PP&E and $309M of capitalized software). Against a health care distributor peer median P/E of 24.3 — Cencora 21.6, Cardinal Health 33.8, Henry Schein 24.3 — that is a discount. Consensus targets bracket the stock at $812 low, $947 mean, $1,065 high.

The discount is partly earned rather than a pure mispricing, and that is the reason this remains a HOLD rather than a return to the BUY rating of May and June. Item 1 of the FY26 10-K discloses that CVS accounted for approximately 24% of consolidated revenue (~$96.8B) and 21% of trade accounts receivable, on a distribution agreement extended in fiscal 2023 only to **June 2027**. No filing or news source confirms renewal, extension, or a competitive re-bid. Separately, three regulatory measures disclosed in the FY26 10-K target distributor fee economics directly rather than drug prices: the CMS bona fide service fee rule effective 1 January 2026 requiring manufacturer-obtained certifications that wholesalers do not pass fees to customers; Executive Order 14297, which the filing notes directs HHS to enable direct-to-consumer purchasing at most-favored-nation prices "which may bypass supply chain intermediaries"; and HRSA's evaluation of retrospective 340B rebates in place of upfront discounts. None is quantified in guidance. Base case $945 (+12.3%) against a $690 bear case (-18.0%) is roughly 0.7:1 reward-to-risk before the bull case — adequate for existing holders, insufficient for new capital until the CVS question resolves.

## Company Overview

McKesson is a North American pharmaceutical distribution utility with higher-margin specialty-provider and technology businesses grafted onto it. FY26 revenue was $403.43B on 1.54% consolidated operating margin and 4.9x asset turnover. R&D expense was $103M — 0.026% of revenue — which is the appropriate reality check on any framing of the company as a technology business. Headcount exceeds 43,000.

The company recast into four segments in Q2 FY26:

| Segment (FY26) | Revenue | Segment profit | Margin | % of revenue | % of pre-corporate profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| North American Pharmaceutical | $336.65B | $3,658M | 1.09% | 83.4% | 49.6% |
| Oncology & Multispecialty | $48.42B | $1,149M | 2.37% | 12.0% | 15.6% |
| Prescription Technology Solutions | $5.81B | $1,044M | 17.98% | 1.4% | 14.1% |
| Medical-Surgical Solutions | $11.51B | $938M | 8.15% | 2.9% | 12.7% |
| Other (Norway, divested 1/30/26) | $1.04B | $590M | — | 0.3% | 8.0% |
| Corporate | — | $(931)M | — | — | — |

The five positive segment lines sum to $7,379M; consolidated operating income was $6,212M, and the ~$236M residual after Corporate is not reconciled to specific amortization or other items in what was read. Treat the percentage mix as indicative rather than audited.

Two things matter in this table. Prescription Technology Solutions generates 14% of pre-corporate segment profit on 1.4% of revenue at an 18% operating margin, with roughly 39% service revenue against under 1% in the core distribution segment; neither Cencora nor Cardinal discloses a directly comparable asset at that margin. And Medical-Surgical, at 12.7% of segment profit, is being separated as **Wellverse** — the standalone name was unveiled on 5–6 August, with the transition expected in 2027 and the Henrico County, Virginia headquarters retained. The FY26 10-K describes the transaction as "a split-off or spin-off, or a combination of both," intended to be tax-free; an external report characterizing it as IPO-bound is not confirmed by any filing.

## Financial Analysis

**FY26 (year ended 31 March 2026).** Revenue $403.43B (+12%), gross profit $14.55B (+9%), operating expenses $8.338B (−6%), operating income $6.212B, interest expense $247M, effective tax rate 17.8% versus 20.1%, net income attributable to McKesson $4.762B (+45%), GAAP diluted EPS $38.38 versus $25.72 on 124.1M weighted diluted shares. Adjusted diluted EPS was $39.11 (proxy pay-versus-performance table).

The narrow $0.73 gap between FY26 GAAP and adjusted EPS should not be read as evidence of clean earnings. FY26 GAAP included a $480M net gain on the Norway divestiture (roughly $3.87 per share pre-tax), a $210M LIFO credit, and a 230bp reduction in the effective tax rate. The Q1 FY27 gap of $4.78 is the more representative structural difference given intangible amortization from Core Ventures and PRISM Vision, three concurrent restructuring programs, separation charges, and recurring redeemable-NCI marks.

**The reported gross margin decline was a comparison artifact.** Consolidated gross margin fell from 3.71% to 3.61%. Antitrust settlement gains recorded in cost of sales were $444M in FY25 against $23M in FY26. Adjusting only for that, margin moved from 3.583% to 3.601%, or +1.8bp. Adjusting also for the LIFO swing (a $210M FY26 credit versus an $82M FY25 charge), margin moved from 3.606% to 3.549%, or −5.7bp. Underlying margin was approximately flat, and Q1 FY27's expansion to 3.50% from 3.35% supports that read. The cause of the LIFO credit matters and is unverified: the LIFO reserve fell from $309M to $99M, which is consistent with branded price deflation — the precise condition the 10-K's inflation-linked manufacturer compensation risk factor warns about — but also with inventory mix or quantity shifts.

**Q1 FY27 segment normalizations.** Three of the four reported segment growth figures require adjustment:

- North American Pharmaceutical profit rose 52% to $903M, margin 1.04% versus 0.72%. The comparative quarter included a $189M Rite Aid receivable provision. Back-solving prior-year profit to ~$594M and adding back $189M gives ~$783M normalized, implying underlying growth near 15%. The segment attribution of that provision is an inference from Rite Aid being a distribution customer, not a filing fact.
- RxTS profit fell 11% to $226M with margin down 320bp to 14.43%. Adding back the $61M restructuring charge gives $287M, +13%, at an 18.3% margin. The best business is not deteriorating operationally.
- Medical-Surgical profit fell 45% to $122M. Adding back $45M of separation charges still leaves −25%, which the filing attributes to ambulatory-care softness. Segment margin has gone 8.15% (FY26) to 4.33% (Q1 FY27).

**Cash flow.** Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was −$220M against −$918M in the prior-year quarter, a $698M improvement achieved despite $701M of higher income tax payments. Q1 is structurally cash-negative for this business; annualizing it would imply −$880M against $6.155B actually generated in FY26. Cash generation is back-half weighted and highly sensitive to payables timing. Note also that the $496M opioid settlement payment fell in July 2026 — Q2 FY27, not Q1.

**Balance sheet and the screening traps.** Total McKesson stockholders' equity was −$4.240B at 30 June 2026, down from −$2.172B at 31 March. The roll-forward reproduces almost entirely from repurchases: −$2,172M + $614M net income − $102M dividends − $2,530M repurchases ≈ −$4,190M against the reported −$4,240M. Negative equity here is a buyback artifact. Negative working capital of −$9.807B and a 0.85 current ratio are the mechanism of the business model — $24.2B of inventory financed by larger payables. An Altman Z of 5.01 with the equity terms floored at zero is neither reassuring nor alarming and should be disregarded for this capital structure.

Total debt rose from $6.526B to $9.729B. Roughly $3.24B of that sits at the MMS borrower group and is explicitly non-recourse: the 10-Q states that "neither McKesson Corporation nor any of its subsidiaries outside of the MMS Borrower group guarantees or otherwise has any obligation with respect to the Amended MMS Credit Agreement." That debt departs with Wellverse. Actual credit support is $6.155B of FY26 operating cash flow against $247M of interest expense, plus a new $5.0B parent revolving facility established 28 April 2026. Claims ranking ahead of common equity include the $5.660B opioid liability and $2.563B of redeemable noncontrolling interests. The 30 June cash balance was not captured, so no precise net-debt or enterprise-value figure is asserted here.

One genuine deterioration: the allowance for doubtful accounts fell to 0.8% of gross trade and notes receivable from 2.1%. The credit cushion is thin heading into a period when OBBBA-driven Medicaid enrollment reductions may pressure customer solvency, with top-ten customers representing 43% of trade receivables.

## Growth Analysis

FY27 adjusted EPS guidance of $44.20–$45.00 implies 14.0% growth at the midpoint against FY26's $39.11. Decomposing: if share count declines roughly 4%, about 4 percentage points is buyback-driven and about 10 points operating. Consensus for the following year is $50.38, a further 13.9%. The five-year adjusted EPS record from the proxy — $23.26, $26.37, $27.44, $33.05, $39.11 — is a 13.9% CAGR through FY26.

The growth is real but its composition deserves precision. Revenue grew 12% in FY26 and 8% in Q1 FY27 at a 3.5% gross margin, driven substantially by specialty, oncology, and GLP-1 volume — high-dollar, low-margin pass-through that inflates the top line while barely moving gross profit dollars. This is a sector-wide condition, not a share gain: Cencora raised its own annual adjusted forecast on 5 August 2026 citing "strong demand for specialty medicines," and Cardinal's growth has been attributed to the same prescription-trend, specialty, and GLP-1 drivers. Revenue growth at a 1% operating-margin business is not evidence of competitive gains.

Where growth is genuinely company-specific: Oncology & Multispecialty revenue up 33% with profit up 53% in Q1, built on the U.S. Oncology Network, Sarah Cannon Research Institute (51%), Ontada, PRISM Vision (80%, acquired for $875M), and Core Ventures (70% of Florida Cancer Specialists' administrative services organization, acquired for $2.5B). And RxTS compounding low-to-mid teens at an 18%+ margin once the restructuring charge is added back.

Estimate revisions are mixed and net roughly flat. The current quarter is unchanged over 30 days and −1.3% over 90; the next quarter is −1.5% and −1.9%; the current year is −0.0% and +0.3%; next year is +0.1% and +1.1%. The quarterly softness is what generates a "falling estimates" label, but the annual figures — where 17 analysts contribute — moved slightly higher over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here in either direction, and nothing resembling the +30% velocity that would change the framework.

A subtlety in the guidance raise: a $0.30 non-GAAP beat produced a $0.40 full-year raise, implying a nine-month remainder of $34.67 at the midpoint. Management passed through slightly more than the quarter's outperformance but did not raise the balance-of-year outlook. On a June-quarter print that is most plausibly ordinary conservatism, but it is worth checking at Q2.

The separation is mix-accretive and dilutive to absolute profit. McKesson gives up $11.5B of revenue and $938M of FY26 segment profit while shedding a segment whose margin has halved and roughly $3.24B of associated debt.

## Competitive Landscape

U.S. drug distribution is a durable three-firm oligopoly. Item 1 names Cencora and Cardinal Health and states the advantage plainly: "We believe that our scale and the breadth of our product and service portfolio are key competitive advantages." That is an accurate description of a cost-per-unit and switching-friction moat, not a technology moat.

Where McKesson differentiates: RxTS, with disclosed network reach across more than 50,000 pharmacies, over one million providers, most PBMs and health plans, and 650+ biopharma brands, producing an 18% operating margin inside a 1%-margin distributor. The oncology and ophthalmology provider platform — U.S. Oncology Network, Core Ventures, PRISM Vision, SCRI, Ontada — is a vertically integrated physician-aligned model neither peer replicates at scale. ClarusONE, the generic sourcing joint venture with Walmart, supplies purchasing scale.

Where McKesson is structurally more exposed: customer concentration. CVS at 24% of revenue and 21% of trade receivables, top ten at 73% of revenue and 43% of receivables, with the next two customers at 11% and 10% individually. That concentration did not improve in FY26. The segment mix is shifting toward higher-margin specialty and technology, and the Wellverse separation will mechanically raise blended margin, but mix improvement and customer diversification are different measures and should not be conflated — the direct concentration data is unchanged.

One additional item deserves attention. The FY26 10-K flags that "laws may be proposed to restrict certain healthcare ownership structures or arrangements, such as vertical integration involving physician practice administrative or management services and pharmaceutical distribution services, where traditional antitrust standards might not be implicated." The company is disclosing legislative risk to precisely the strategy it is pursuing with Core Ventures and PRISM Vision, into which it has deployed roughly $3.4B of cash.

Trajectory assessment: the moat is stable to modestly widening on mix, but the evidence that Q1's strength was shared across all three distributors, combined with a dedicated restructuring program inside the highest-margin franchise, argues against the competitive score held in prior reports. Companies do not typically restructure a growing 18%-margin business without a reason, and no filing evidence distinguishes proactive cost optimization from anticipated pressure on copay-assistance and access economics from IRA Part D redesign or the CMS fee rule.

## Risk Assessment

**CVS contract expiry, June 2027.** The largest identifiable risk and the nearest-dated. Approximately $96.8B of annual revenue and 21% of trade receivables on a contract that expires in roughly ten months, with no public evidence of renewal or re-bid. On 1.09% segment margins the profit at stake is far smaller than the revenue figure implies, but the operating leverage in a 27-distribution-center fixed-cost network is severe, and the working-capital swing from a customer of that receivable weight would be a multi-billion-dollar cash event in either direction. Renewal on materially worse economics is arguably more probable than outright loss. Historical base rates favor renewal — these are decades-long relationships and switching a distributor of this scale is operationally forbidding, and such contracts are often renewed without a press release. The absence of news is genuinely uninformative. This is unresolved in both directions, not a negative finding.

**Regulatory attack on the fee model.** Prior drug-pricing reform was largely neutral for distributors because they earn fees on units rather than price. Three new measures are different in kind. The CMS bona fide service fee rule effective 1 January 2026 requires manufacturers to obtain certifications that wholesalers do not pass fees to customers, with the initial Q1 2026 reporting deadline waived and scope "subject to change." Executive Order 14297 directs HHS to enable direct-to-consumer purchasing at most-favored-nation prices, which the 10-K itself notes "may bypass supply chain intermediaries." HRSA is evaluating retrospective 340B rebates in place of upfront discounts, with its pilot successfully challenged before the 1 January 2026 start and litigation continuing. All three are early-stage, partially enjoined, or of changing scope, and incumbent distributors could plausibly become the DTC fulfillment layer rather than be bypassed. Timeline and magnitude are not modelable from the filings; they are unquantified in guidance.

**Opioid liabilities.** Accrued at $5.660B (from $5.692B), $602M current, against the $7.9B national Distributor Settlement running through 2038 plus state and class components. Annual cash outflow of roughly $500–600M is 9–11% of free cash flow — absorbable. The tail risk sits in appellate outcomes: the Fourth Circuit reversed and remanded the Cabell County/Huntington verdict on 28 October 2025, resurrecting a case McKesson had won at trial in 2022. Offsetting, the Supreme Court of Maryland vacated the Baltimore judgment on 24 April 2026 and dismissed with prejudice, allowing a $32M accrual reduction. Philadelphia and Allegheny County appeals are pending at the Pennsylvania Supreme Court, and a British Columbia class action has a common-issues trial set for 22 February 2028.

**Separation execution.** The MMS separation risk factor is new in the FY26 10-K. Conditions include a tax opinion, registration statement effectiveness, NewCo capital structure, and final board approval. Tax-free treatment is intended, not assured, and the contingent tax indemnity still carried from the 2020 Change Healthcare split-off is direct precedent for long-tailed obligations from these structures. Wellverse's leverage is a live question: $3.24B of secured debt against FY26 segment profit of $938M is 3.45x, but against a Q1-annualized run rate the ratio is materially higher, and quarterly annualization is unreliable given the ambulatory-care softness and charge timing.

**Pre-separation cash flows.** MMS-level borrowings ($993M net in April, $2.2B net in June from a $2.25B Term Loan B at SOFR+225) plus $1.238B net from Apollo's convertible preferred, closing 1 June 2026, moved roughly $4.43B of cash to the parent through repayment of intercompany loan principal, with the debt non-recourse to McKesson Corporation. The parent spent $2.530B on repurchases in Q1 FY27 alone. Establishing an independent capital structure is a standard and explicitly disclosed condition of any spin-off, and a negotiated third-party investment provides valuation validation; no management motive is inferred here. What is verifiable is the direction of cash and the non-recourse language. Apollo's ~13% interest is in the MMS subsidiary, so there is no dilution to McKesson's 116.59M share count — economic ownership of MMS moves from 100% to roughly 87% on an as-converted basis, per the company's own characterization. The conversion mechanics, dividend rate, and redemption formula driving the $374M mark were not read; if the formula is value-linked, further marks are likely as separation approaches.

**Other.** Supplier concentration (largest 11% of purchases, top ten 71%). Unrecognized tax benefits of $1.6B at 30 June, though scoping differences versus the ~$1.2B of uncertain tax positions disclosed at 31 March mean no sequential increase should be inferred. New state taxes on controlled-substance distribution. DSCSA unit-level traceability compliance. New and expanded AI risk factors, with Item 1 conceding the company is "in the early stages of exploring potential AI capabilities."

**Governance.** The CFO transition is confirmed but unexplained: Britt Vitalone delivered FY27 guidance as CFO on 8 May 2026; Kenny Cheung signed the Q1 10-Q as EVP & CFO on 5 August 2026, with RSU grants dated 29 May. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (5 March and 1 July 2026) bracket the change, and neither was retrieved. A finance-function handoff concurrent with a spin-off, a $1.25B structured minority investment, a 50% increase in consolidated debt, and a 24%-of-revenue contract expiry is a meaningful concentration of change without disclosed explanation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,060, +26.0%).** CVS renews at flat or better economics, removing the largest overhang and re-rating the FY28 base. Oncology & Multispecialty continues compounding revenue in the 20–30% range with margin expansion from Core Ventures and PRISM integration. RxTS completes its restructuring with margin restored above 18% and growth in the low-to-mid teens, and the market begins valuing the segment separately from distribution. Wellverse separates cleanly and tax-free, lifting remaining-company blended margin and removing a deteriorating asset plus $3.24B of debt. The ~$5B FY27 buyback shrinks the count another 5%, and a 21x multiple on next-year consensus of $50.38 delivers $1,060.

**Bear case ($690, −18.0%).** CVS renews on concessionary economics that consume a meaningful share of a 1.09%-margin segment's profit, or moves volume to a competitor. The CMS bona fide service fee certification requirement forces a restructuring of fee pass-through arrangements with measurable gross-profit consequence. Branded price deflation — the hypothesis behind the $210M LIFO credit and the reserve's fall from $309M to $99M — erodes the inflation-linked component of manufacturer compensation. OBBBA Medicaid reductions push customer credit losses higher against an allowance thinned from 2.1% to 0.8% of receivables. Restructuring and NCI marks keep recurring, forcing investors toward GAAP earnings and a lower multiple. 15.6x FY27 consensus of $44.25 gives $690.

**Base case ($945, +12.3%).** CVS renews on modestly less favorable terms without a step-change; oncology and RxTS growth offsets; Wellverse separates on schedule; buybacks deliver 4–5% share reduction. 18.8x next-year consensus of $50.38, a ~13% discount to the peer median multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive twelve-month thesis — raised guidance, 14% adjusted EPS growth of which roughly 10 points is operating, 5.5% combined shareholder yield, a mix-accretive separation — but the reward-to-risk on the base case is approximately 0.7:1 with the CVS resolution inside the horizon. Existing holders should stay. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Confirmed CVS renewal — a filing, 8-K, or company statement extending the pharmaceutical distribution agreement beyond June 2027 — without disclosed material concession to distribution economics.
2. A price near $760–780 (roughly 17x guided FY27 midpoint, 15.2x next-year consensus) with FY27 guidance intact, which would restore roughly 21%+ upside to base case against a bear-case loss of ~11%.
3. Two consecutive quarters of RxTS segment profit growth above 12% at 18%+ margin with the $200–250M restructuring program declared complete, confirming the highest-quality earnings stream is intact.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Disclosure that the CVS agreement will not be renewed, or renewal on terms management characterizes as materially affecting segment economics.
2. FY27 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the current $44.20 floor, or the balance-of-year implied remainder ($34.67 at the midpoint) reduced at the Q2 print.
3. Quantified guidance impact from the CMS bona fide service fee rule or Executive Order 14297 direct-to-consumer implementation.
4. An adverse Cabell County retrial outcome or a Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling requiring a material opioid accrual increase beyond the $5.660B carried.
5. Allowance for doubtful accounts rising back above 1.5% of gross receivables alongside a customer bankruptcy filing, indicating the thin credit cushion is being consumed.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q2 FY27 earnings, expected early November 2026, is the primary checkpoint: it will show whether management raises the balance-of-year outlook, whether MMS/Wellverse margin stabilizes above 4.33%, whether RxTS margin recovers toward 18%, and whether further redeemable-NCI marks recur at Q1's magnitude. The Wellverse separation Form 10 or registration statement, when filed, will resolve the separation form (spin-off versus split-off versus IPO), the final NewCo capital structure, and Apollo's conversion mechanics. Any 8-K disclosing the CVS outcome supersedes all of the above.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report — 7.1 in February, 7.1 in May, 6.8 in June, 6.3 in July — reflected accumulating risk and governance findings rather than operating deterioration, and the Q1 FY27 print largely confirms that read. Operating momentum is intact and arguably improving: gross margin expanded 15bp, expenses grew slower than revenue, pre-tax income rose 24%, oncology revenue rose 33%, and guidance was raised for a second time. The 18% GAAP EPS decline is a non-cash redeemable-NCI remeasurement worth $3.14 per share and should not be read as an operating event. The price is essentially unchanged from the prior report's basis while guidance moved higher, which modestly improves risk/reward and lifts the overall score to 6.37 from 6.3, driven by the valuation dimension.

What has not improved is the risk register. The CVS contract — 24% of revenue, 21% of trade receivables, expiring June 2027 — remains unresolved with no public evidence in either direction, and no standardized dataset captures it. Three new regulatory measures target the distributor fee model itself rather than drug prices, and none is quantified in guidance. Adjusted earnings quality is drifting as three restructuring programs run concurrently and NCI marks recur. Insider activity is one-directional selling — the CEO disposed of 21,855 shares for roughly $17.0M across June and July, with three other officers also selling and no open-market purchases — which reads as vest-and-sell diversification after a 35.5% annualized five-year TSR rather than a bearish signal, but no insider chose to buy at these levels either, and the CFO handoff remains undisclosed as to reason and effective date.

At 18.9x guided FY27 adjusted EPS, 16.7x next-year consensus, a 5.5% free cash flow yield, and a 5.5% combined shareholder yield against a 24.3x peer median, McKesson is reasonably valued for a business compounding EPS in the mid-teens.
