## Executive Summary

Medpace runs the most capital-efficient business in the contract research organization (CRO) universe: FY2025 free cash flow of $681.9M on $31.4M of capex, zero indebtedness, ROA of 22.8%, and a 15% reduction in diluted share count since FY2022 funded entirely from internal cash. Q2 2026 was a clean beat — revenue $707.3M (+17.2%), GAAP EPS $4.25 versus $4.05 consensus, net new awards $795.7M (+28.2%), book-to-bill 1.13x — and management raised FY2026 guidance across every line. The stock has risen 34.7% over twelve months and 7% since our July review.

Two adjustments matter more than the headline. First, reimbursed out-of-pocket expenses, which flow through both revenue and cost at zero margin, reached 43.5% of H1 2026 revenue versus 36.5% of FY2024 revenue. Stripping them out, service revenue grew 10.6% in H1 2026 against 21.7% reported, and 11.5% in FY2025 against 20.0% reported. Second, the same adjustment reverses the apparent margin story: core service EBITDA margin was 38.0% in Q2 2026 versus 35.7% a year earlier (+230bp), against a reported margin that looked flat at 21.7%. Medpace is a ~10-11% grower with genuinely expanding unit economics.

The problem is price relative to that reality, compounded by structurally thin visibility. At $575.08 the shares trade at 32.7x the FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance midpoint of $17.60, 29.8x next-year consensus of $19.31 (implying ~9% consensus EPS growth), and 24.7x EV/FY2026 EBITDA guidance. Only about 67% of forward-year revenue was covered by twelve-month convertible backlog, backlog itself declined $13.0M sequentially in H1 2026 while revenue grew 21.7%, metabolic exposure rose to 31-32% of revenue against management's own expectation that it would fall, and two lawsuits filed in April and June 2026 name the CEO, President, CFO and the entire board over statements about the book-to-bill ratio. The realized earnings-day gaps of -15.9% (Feb 10), -22.6% (Apr 23) and +14.7% (Jul 23) reflect how much a single bookings print can move this equity. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Medpace is a single-segment, full-service CRO providing Phase I-IV clinical development to biopharmaceutical, biotechnology and medical device sponsors, with approximately 6,500 employees across 46 countries as of June 30, 2026 (up from 6,200 at year-end 2025). Headquarters and the primary clinical campus are in Cincinnati. The company owns the infrastructure it sells: central laboratories in Cincinnati, Leuven, Shanghai and Singapore; a bioanalytical laboratory and Phase I clinic in Cincinnati; cardiovascular and imaging core labs; and ClinTrak, its proprietary trial information system.

Two structural facts define the investment case. First, customer mix: 82% of FY2025 net revenue came from small biopharmaceutical companies and 13% from mid-sized companies, with no customer at 10% or more and top-ten concentration of 35.1%. Second, and disclosed explicitly in the 10-K, Medpace generally declines the long-term strategic collaborations with large pharma that IQVIA, ICON, PPD/Thermo Fisher and Fortrea actively pursue. That choice buys pricing power and margin; it forgoes committed multi-year volume. The majority of contracts are fixed-fee, most customers can terminate on 30 days' notice, and most contracts carry an upfront fee collected at signing, which is why customer prepayments ("advanced billings") of $904.7M sit in current liabilities and produce structurally negative working capital.

Growth has been entirely organic. Goodwill has been unchanged at $662.4M from 2022 through mid-2026, customer-relationship intangibles are essentially fully amortized, and no acquisition 8-K exists in the filing history.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the pass-through distortion.** Reported revenue grew from $1,460.0M (FY2022) to $2,530.2M (FY2025), a 20.0% increase in the final year. Reimbursed out-of-pocket expenses were $770.7M in FY2024 and $1,037.5M in FY2025, so revenue net of pass-throughs was $1,338.4M and $1,492.7M respectively — growth of 11.5%. The same arithmetic for H1 2026: $1,413.9M − $615.1M = $798.8M versus $1,161.9M − $439.9M = $722.0M, or +10.6%. Management disclosed this dynamic on the Q4 2025 call, guiding reimbursables to 41-42% of 2026 revenue and warning that normalization would create a headwind to reported growth. Reimbursables have instead run above that range at 43.5% of H1 revenue.

**Margin.** Reported EBITDA margin was 22.8% (FY2024), 22.0% (FY2025) and 21.7% in Q2 2026 versus 21.6% in Q2 2025 — flat to eroding. On revenue net of pass-throughs the picture inverts: 35.9% (FY2024) → 37.4% (FY2025), and 35.7% → 38.0% in Q2. Corroborating evidence for genuine productivity rather than an accounting artifact: direct service costs excluding D&A fell from 32.3% of revenue (FY2024) to 28.9% (FY2025) to 28.6% in Q2 2026, and headcount grew about 5% in 2025 against 20% reported revenue growth. The caveat is that cost-to-cost revenue recognition means favorable revisions to estimated total contract cost can flatter a period's margin, and the filings do not disaggregate enough to separate mix and estimate effects from durable productivity.

**Q2 2026 earnings quality.** GAAP net income of $121.4M (+34.5%) grew far faster than EBITDA (+17.6%). The gap is non-operating: the effective tax rate fell to 20.0% from 27.5%, and interest income rose to $5.0M from $1.1M. Net income margin of 17.2% versus 15.0% should be read with that in mind. The FY2026 guidance now assumes a 19.0-19.5% tax rate (raised from 18.5-19.5%) and interest income of $21.1M (cut from $24.3-29.2M as cash moved into buybacks), so neither tailwind extends.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $713.2M on net income of $451.1M is 1.58x conversion, with capex of $31.4M producing $681.9M of free cash flow — a 4.16% FCF yield on the current market capitalization. The quality qualifier is that $143.8M of FY2025 OCF came from increased advanced billings. Customer prepayment is a permanent feature of the model (the CFO cited negative 58.7 days net DSO for Q4 2025), but the *growth* in that float depends on bookings growth. It is already decelerating: advanced billings contributed $50.3M to H1 2026 OCF versus $98.2M in H1 2025, and OCF/net income fell to 1.28x from 1.34x. H1 2026 OCF was $313.8M with capex of $30.7M; we do not annualize this, since FY2025 was H2-weighted (38%/62%) and capex intensity has stepped up to 2.17% of revenue from 1.24%.

**Balance sheet, and why the screens mislead.** Zero indebtedness at both 12/31/25 and 6/30/26. The only facility is a $10.0M unsecured revolver, undrawn, expiring April 30, 2027 — reduced to $10.0M by amendment in July 2025. Cash of $502.7M, of which only $26.3M is offshore and none restricted. Shareholders' equity of $433.9M is down from $825.5M at 12/31/24 purely because share repurchases above par were charged to retained earnings, producing an accumulated deficit of $511.4M. An Altman Z-Score of 2.47, reported debt/equity of 3.3 (that is total liabilities to equity; interest-bearing debt is zero) and a current ratio of 0.74 all reflect customer prepayment float and voluntary equity retirement, not borrowing. The residual, legitimate signal is that Medpace operates with $433.9M of book equity, negative $385.1M of working capital and a $10M credit line — essentially no external liquidity backstop, and complete dependence on continued customer prepayment. In a scenario where bookings stall for several quarters, that combination tightens faster than the reported cash balance suggests.

Financial health scores 7.5: cash generation and leverage are among the best in health care services, offset modestly by the growth-contingent float and the deliberate absence of a committed backstop.

## Growth Analysis

**Bookings.** FY2025 net new awards of $2,646.8M against revenue of $2,530.2M gives 1.046x. The reconstructed quarterly path — 0.90x, 1.03x, 1.20x, 1.04x through 2025, then 0.88x in Q1 2026 and 1.13x in Q2 2026 — leaves H1 2026 at exactly 1.00x. Q2's $795.7M of awards (+28.2% year over year) is the strongest single data point in the file and is the reason the stock gapped 14.7% higher on July 23.

**Backlog.** $2,902.2M (12/31/24) → $3,027.2M (12/31/25) → $3,014.2M (6/30/26). The company frames this as +4.9% versus 6/30/25. On a balance-to-balance basis, backlog fell $13.0M during H1 2026 (roughly $9.4M ex a $3.6M FX headwind) while reported revenue grew 21.7%. Remaining performance obligations for active projects also declined, from approximately $3.6B to $3.5B. These are two distinct disclosures on different definitions — backlog excludes study amounts extending beyond three years — and neither is contractually committed given 30-day termination rights; the 10-K states plainly that backlog is not necessarily a meaningful predictor of future results.

Flat backlog alongside 21.7% revenue growth is arithmetically what high conversion looks like (24.1% quarterly backlog conversion in Q2 2026 versus 23.6% in Q4 2025), and conversion acceleration is finite. The offsetting positive: twelve-month convertible backlog rose from approximately $1.89-1.91B at 12/31/25 to approximately $1.95-1.97B at 6/30/26.

**Coverage.** Against the raised FY2026 revenue midpoint of $2.845B, the $1.90B of twelve-month convertible backlog entering the year covered roughly 67%. That is the structural reason a single quarter at 0.88x translated into a 22.6% single-day decline in April: with a third of next year's revenue uncontracted at any point in time, order intake is the entire forward signal.

**Estimates and delivery.** Consensus has moved higher but modestly: current quarter $4.44 (+3.6% over 90 days), current year $17.70 (+3.5%), next year $19.31 (+4.0% over 90 days, +2.9% over 30 days). Four consecutive beats (+4.9%, +8.1%, +9.6%, +7.5%) support confidence in the current-year number. This is positive earnings momentum, but a 3-4% revision on a stock at 30x forward is already in the price.

**Guidance.** The July raise was smaller than the headline suggests. Revenue midpoint +1.4%, EBITDA midpoint +1.6%, GAAP net income midpoint +1.0%, but GAAP EPS midpoint +3.0% — because assumed diluted shares fell from 29.2M to 28.6M. Roughly two-thirds of the EPS raise is share count already retired. Guidance excludes any repurchases after June 30, 2026, and $527.0M of authorization remains (3.3% of market capitalization at $575.08), which is genuine unbudgeted EPS upside.

Growth prospects score 5.5: durable double-digit service growth with expanding unit economics, held back by decelerating underlying growth, a flat order book, and low forward coverage.

## Competitive Landscape

Medpace's moat is operational rather than contractual. Owned central labs in four countries, a bioanalytical lab, a Phase I clinic, imaging and cardiovascular core labs and the ClinTrak platform allow the company to deliver an integrated program internally rather than subcontracting, which is also why so much reimbursable activity runs through its own P&L. The 10-K identifies the barriers to becoming a global full-service provider as therapeutic depth, complex-program management expertise, global infrastructure and multi-jurisdiction regulatory capability. Approximately 75% of 182 newly filled management roles between October 2024 and September 2025 were internal promotions, which is credible evidence that the operating model is a trainable system rather than a marketing claim. Headcount growth of about 5% in 2025 against double-digit service revenue growth is the quantitative expression of it.

The trajectory has two negatives. Metabolic revenue reached $219.1M in Q2 2026, 31.0% of quarterly revenue and 32.3% of H1 revenue, versus 25.5% in H1 2025 — up roughly seven points, despite management stating on the Q4 2025 call that it expected metabolic to *decline* as a share of revenue in 2026 as mix normalized. "Other" therapeutic revenue fell 16.8% in Q2. The 10-K's own risk factor on running multiple trials in a single therapeutic class involving similar drugs — where new scientific information or a class-wide regulatory decision can trigger correlated cancellations — is directly applicable, and management identified metabolic as the area most skewed in the Q4 2025 cancellation spike.

Second, artificial intelligence is a two-sided exposure the company has itself flagged. Troendle indicated no expected productivity advantage in 2026 because investment offsets early benefit, and suggested longer-term gains "could benefit clients and potentially reduce the amount of staffing required." For a business whose economics rest on billing skilled labor against fixed-fee contracts, an efficiency technology whose surplus accrues to sponsors is a genuine multi-year pricing question, even if it is invisible in current results.

One governance-adjacent positive strengthens the competitive read: related-party revenue fell to $12.0M in H1 2026 from $30.7M in H1 2025 (0.85% of revenue from 2.64%), with CinRx down from $26.8M to $9.6M. None of 2026's growth comes from CEO-affiliated customers, so third-party growth is marginally better than headline. This does not establish improving customer diversification generally — we have FY2025 top-ten concentration of 35.1% with no prior-year comparison.

Competitive position scores 6.0: a real and widening operating advantage in the small/mid-biotech niche, offset by rising single-therapeutic-area concentration and the absence of a contractual volume floor by design.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Bookings volatility against 67% forward coverage.** The dominant risk, and the direct consequence of declining preferred-provider arrangements. Q1 2026 at 0.88x preceded the 22.6% single-session decline on April 23. A sub-1.0x Q3 print would immediately reopen the question the Q2 beat closed.

**2. Small-biotech funding dependence, with an uncapped tail.** 82% of FY2025 revenue came from small biopharmaceutical companies. The 10-K goes beyond boilerplate: if a customer becomes unwilling or unable to fund a trial in progress, Medpace "may be legally or ethically bound to complete or wind down the trial at our own expense." No reserve is disclosed against this.

**3. Metabolic concentration, worsening.** 31-32% of revenue in a therapeutic area management expected to shrink, characterized by class-correlated cancellation risk and by the elevated pass-through intensity that is distorting reported growth and margin.

**4. Securities and derivative litigation aimed at the bookings metric.** *Durbin v. Medpace, Troendle, Geiger and Brady* (S.D. Ohio, filed 4/6/26) alleges §10(b)/20(a) and Rule 10b-5 violations from statements "related to the Company's book-to-bill ratio," with a reported class period of April 22, 2025 through February 9, 2026. *Bushansky* (filed 6/22/26) is a derivative action naming all five named officers and all six directors on the same subject. No liability has been recognized in either case, there is no restatement, no auditor issue, and disclosure controls were affirmed effective. These are unproven allegations, and stock-drop suits routinely follow large single-day declines in high-multiple names. The relevant procedural fact is that Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q states there have been no significant changes to risk factors despite both actions being filed after the 10-K.

**5. Fixed-fee contracts with cost-to-cost recognition.** Cost overruns are borne by Medpace, change-order revenue cannot be recognized until written authorization while associated cost is expensed as incurred, and revisions to estimated total cost produce cumulative revenue adjustments. This makes reported revenue and margin sensitive to management estimates in both directions.

**6. Key-person and control concentration.** Troendle is founder and CEO and controls approximately 19.0% of shares. Related-party real estate is the more entrenching item: $117.0M of $189.5M total undiscounted future operating lease payments (61.8%), including $73.8M in later years, is payable to entities wholly owned by the CEO or his family, on leases running to 2032 and 2040 with CPI escalators and multiple 10-year extension options. Annual P&L impact is small (~$5.5M), and the arrangements are disclosed and Audit Committee-reviewed under a related-person policy; no conclusion on pricing fairness can be drawn from the filings.

**7. Unresolved disclosures.** Item 5.02 events (departure/election of directors or officers) in 8-Ks dated 4/22/26 and 6/4/26 could not be retrieved. No inference is warranted, but the substance is unknown and is decision-relevant given that President Jesse Geiger is a named defendant in both lawsuits and sold 100% of his 31,707 exercised shares in March 2026, retaining 15,000. Also unexplained: a ~$13.9M Q1 2025 stock-compensation charge in SG&A against $3.5M for all of H1 2026.

Risk factors score 4.0. The concentration, coverage and litigation items are specific, currently active, and capable of producing 15-20% single-day moves, as three of the last four earnings dates demonstrated.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($690, +20%).** Q2's 1.13x book-to-bill was the first data point in a bookings recovery rather than a single quarter's noise. Two more prints above 1.10x would restore backlog growth, lift twelve-month convertible coverage above 70%, and validate that the Q4 2025 cancellation cluster was cycle-related rather than structural to the metabolic franchise. Core service margin continues expanding from 38.0% as utilization and retention improve, delivering FY2027 GAAP EPS above $20.50 including deployment of the $527.0M remaining authorization at prices below intrinsic value. At 33-34x — the multiple the market has repeatedly paid for this asset — that is roughly $690. Supporting facts: four consecutive beats averaging 7.5%, zero debt, 4.16% FCF yield, disciplined organic-only growth history, and a management team that repurchased 2.96M shares at an average of $308 in FY2025 and 705,616 at $417.70 in Q2 2026.

**Bear case ($435, -24%).** Reported growth has been delivered by accelerating conversion of a flat order book plus zero-margin pass-throughs; underlying service growth is 10.6% and decelerating. A single sub-0.95x bookings quarter — the outcome in two of the last six — re-prices a 30x multiple on a business with 67% forward coverage and 82% small-biotech exposure. Metabolic at 32% of revenue is one class-level regulatory or scientific development away from correlated cancellations, and "Other" therapeutic revenue is already contracting 16.8%. The advanced-billings float that produced 1.58x cash conversion stops growing when bookings stall, pulling OCF toward net income precisely when equity is $433.9M and the committed backstop is a $10M revolver. Litigation and derivative claims against the entire board over the disclosure of the very metric investors must trust add a discount that has no clean resolution date. Twenty-four times $18.00 of FY2027 EPS is $432; the April 2026 low of $373 demonstrates the market can overshoot below that.

**Base case ($595, +3.5%).** Bookings normalize around 1.0-1.10x, FY2026 lands near the upper half of guidance ($17.60-17.95 GAAP EPS), and FY2027 delivers approximately $19.60 including modest incremental buyback beyond the guidance assumption. Applying 30x — a compression from today's 32.7x that reflects the 9% consensus growth rate and unresolved bookings visibility — gives $588; rounding for buyback optionality, $595. The base case sits close to consensus mean ($581.83) and median ($604.50), which is what a fairly priced high-quality asset with a two-sided operating signal should look like.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is our fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has moved from 5.3 in mid-July to 5.35 now. The July review was struck before the Q2 print and was driven by the Q1 bookings collapse to 0.88x; Q2's 1.13x and +28.2% award growth materially reduced that specific concern, which argues for an upgrade. Against that, the price rose 7% (from $537.73 to $575.08), backlog declined sequentially in H1, metabolic concentration rose rather than normalized, and the securities and derivative actions were filed. The two forces approximately cancel. We are explicit that this is an uncertain hold rather than a growth hold: at 30x forward for 9% consensus EPS growth, we cannot articulate a 12-month appreciation thesis with sufficient conviction, and we cannot articulate a downside thesis strong enough to sell a debt-free 38% core-margin franchise that beats consistently.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Two consecutive quarters of book-to-bill at or above 1.10x with backlog growing sequentially on a balance-to-balance basis** (i.e., Q3 2026 backlog above $3,014.2M and Q4 above that), which would restore the coverage ratio and make FY2027 consensus of $19.31 look conservative.
2. **A price near $470-490** with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $19.31 — approximately 24-25x forward, where the multiple no longer requires bookings recovery to be pre-paid. This is the level the stock traded at in late May/June 2026 on materially worse bookings information.
3. **Metabolic falling below 27% of quarterly revenue while total service revenue growth (ex-pass-throughs) holds above 10%**, which would demonstrate broadening demand rather than dependence on one wave.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A quarter below 0.95x book-to-bill accompanied by a guidance cut**, particularly if cancellations are again described as skewed to metabolic. This combination would confirm the bear mechanism.
2. **Service revenue growth ex-pass-throughs decelerating below 7%** in any quarter, or reimbursables exceeding 45% of revenue while core service margin stops expanding.
3. **Denial of a motion to dismiss in *Durbin* coupled with any restatement, internal-control exception, or adverse change in the reported bookings/backlog definition or presentation** — the specific risk here is not the damages number but a change in the credibility of the primary forward metric.
4. **Named-officer departure disclosed in connection with the bookings disclosure**, or evidence linking the unretrieved Item 5.02 events of 4/22/26 and 6/4/26 to the litigation. We currently make no such inference.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (expected late October 2026) is the decisive event; it delivers the third bookings data point of the year and will determine whether H1's 1.00x was a trough or a plateau. The FY2026 fourth-quarter release in February 2027 sets initial FY2027 guidance and initial twelve-month backlog coverage, which is the single most predictive disclosure this company makes. Holders concerned about gap risk should note that near-term implied volatility is cheap to realized and that October-expiry protection is priced at a discount to the last three earnings-day moves.

## Conclusion

Medpace is a better business than its multiple is a good price. The operating model produces 38% core service EBITDA margins, 22.8% ROA, $681.9M of annual free cash flow on $31.4M of capex, and a 15% reduction in share count achieved at average prices of $308 and $417.70 — well below today's $575.08. Nothing in the Q2 filings suggests deterioration in that machine; the reverse, since core service margin expanded 230 basis points year over year while reported margin looked flat.

What the price does not adequately compensate is the visibility deficit that Medpace has deliberately accepted. Declining large-pharma preferred-provider contracts means no committed volume floor, which means roughly one-third of any year's revenue is uncontracted at the start of it, which means quarterly order intake carries the entire forward signal. That signal was 0.88x in Q1, 1.13x in Q2, and 1.00x for the half, with backlog down $13.0M sequentially and remaining performance obligations down $100M. Underlying service growth of 10.6% is respectable and decelerating. At 32.7x guided GAAP EPS and 29.8x next-year consensus for 9% growth, with 82% small-biotech exposure, 32% metabolic concentration, and active litigation over the disclosure of the metric that matters most, the asymmetry is roughly balanced: +3.5% to base, +20% to bull, -24% to bear.

**HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated by a genuinely excellent operating business and an ongoing buyback with $527.0M of authorization; the October bookings print is the reassessment point. New capital should wait for either two consecutive quarters of bookings above 1.10x with backlog growth, or a price near $470-490.
