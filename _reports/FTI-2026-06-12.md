**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TechnipFMC is the global leader in subsea oil and gas production systems, and it has executed one of the cleaner multi-year turnarounds in energy services: from a $107M net loss in 2022 to $964M of net income in 2025, with revenue rising to $9.64B (+9.1%) and accelerating to +11.6% YoY in Q1 2026. The improvement is margin-led, which is the durable part. Subsea operating margin expanded 300bps YoY to 15.8% in Q1 2026, and management guided Q2 Subsea adjusted EBITDA margin to roughly 23%. The driver is structural: the Subsea 2.0 standardization platform (claimed up to 25% lower product cost, up to 12-month shorter delivery) and the integrated iEPCI commercial model are widening the moat against SLB-OneSubsea and Baker Hughes while lifting profitability. The balance sheet is strong: $540M net cash, full investment-grade ratings at all three agencies (Moody's upgraded to Baa2 in September 2025), and $1.9B of remaining buyback authority that has already retired ~11% of shares since 2022.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. Inbound Subsea orders fell ~30% YoY in Q1 2026 ($1.9B vs. $2.79B), which annualizes to roughly $7.6B against management's $10B 2026 target; the target therefore requires a sharp, unproven back-half acceleration. Consensus forward-year EPS was revised down 1.1% over 90 days (next-year $3.47 vs. $3.51), the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that justifies paying a premium multiple. The earnings inflection has already been captured: the stock is up ~106% over twelve months and trades near its 52-week high of $77.78 at ~24x current-year and ~20x next-year EPS. The C-suite (CEO, CFO, Subsea President, CTO) sold large blocks in March 2026 with zero offsetting open-market buying. None of these individually kills the thesis, but together they remove the asymmetric risk/reward required to add capital at the current price.

This is a high-quality business at a full entry point inside a sector where Middle East geopolitical risk is unresolved. The order-conversion thesis is worth holding into Q2 results but not yet worth chasing.

## Company Overview

TechnipFMC is a UK-incorporated, Houston-headquartered subsea energy equipment and services company operating two segments:

- **Subsea (~89% of revenue; $2.21B of $2.49B in Q1 2026):** subsea production systems (SPS), umbilicals/risers/flowlines (SURF), EPCI project management, and services on a growing installed base. Differentiation rests on two pioneered models: iEPCI (bundling SPS + SURF + installation into a single contract) and Subsea 2.0 (pre-engineered, configure-to-order hardware).
- **Surface Technologies (~11% of revenue):** wellhead and pressure-control equipment, ~65% international (ADNOC, Saudi Aramco), with US land under 5% of total company revenue. The international tilt deliberately reduces US shale-cycle exposure.

The investment is a leveraged play on the offshore/deepwater capex cycle. Management's case is that improved deepwater breakevens, driven by standardization and integration, are pulling a growing share of global E&P capital offshore, with activity expected to stay strong through the end of the decade. Named backlog projects span TotalEnergies Mozambique LNG and GranMorgu (Suriname), bp Tiber/Kaskida/NEP, Equinor Raia and Johan Sverdrup Phase 3, Petrobras Mero 3 HISEP, and ExxonMobil Hammerhead/Whiptail.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trajectory is unambiguous:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.49B | $7.55B | $8.84B | $9.64B |
| Net income | -$107M | $56M | $843M | $964M |
| Pretax income | $69M | $207M | $940M | $1,270M |

Q1 2026 delivered net income of $260.5M (+83.5% YoY) and diluted EPS of $0.64, a ~14% beat. Subsea revenue rose 14.1% YoY, driven by backlog conversion concentrated in Brazil (+$190M), Mozambique (+$105M), and Suriname (+$100M). The effective tax rate fell to 27.0% from 37.8% (a prior-year one-time accrual), which flatters the YoY net-income comparison.

The company-defined Adjusted EBITDA margin climbed from 8.8% (2021) to 18.5% (2025). The Q2 guide of ~23% Subsea adjusted EBITDA margin (+~300bps) signals the standardization benefit is still compounding rather than plateauing.

Net cash is $540M (cash $961M vs. total debt ~$420M), with investment-grade ratings at S&P (BBB-), Fitch (BBB-), and Moody's (Baa2). The upgrade released collateral and lowered borrowing costs. Capital return is buyback-led: a $3.8B total authorization (raised $2.0B in October 2025), $265M repurchased in Q1 alone, and share count down from ~449M (2022) to ~400M. The dividend is nominal (~0.3% yield), a function of the UK-PLC dividend restriction tied to the merger-legacy accumulated deficit of -$3.76B (a non-operating accounting artifact, not a solvency concern).

One quality caveat: SG&A and corporate expense rose meaningfully because share-based comp jumped to $55.5M from $14.1M YoY, reflecting the now-probable Value Creation Plan PSUs (up to 3.6M shares). This is a real dilution and expense item to monitor, though it does not impair cash generation.

Earnings quality is supported by four consecutive beats (11.8%, 37.1%, 12.2%, 15.9%), which raises confidence in near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers, and they currently diverge.

The realized layer is strong: +11.6% revenue growth, 300bps of Subsea margin expansion, and $16.5B of remaining unsatisfied performance obligations (RUPO), roughly two years of revenue visibility, with ~34% converting in 2026 and 66% thereafter. This backlog underpins consensus and supports the Q2 margin guide.

The forward layer is softer than the headlines suggest. Q1 2026 inbound orders fell ~30% YoY ($2.15B total / $1.9B Subsea vs. $3.09B / $2.79B). Orders are genuinely lumpy quarter to quarter, but the $10B 2026 Subsea order target implies a back-half well above the Q1 run-rate; $1.9B annualizes to ~$7.6B, about 24% below target. Management points to ~$3B of newly added subsea opportunities within a ~$30B 24-month pipeline, which is encouraging but not yet bookings.

This is why the most powerful BUY signal in this framework — EPS estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days combined with accelerating revenue — is not present. Current-year EPS estimates rose only +1.4% over 90 days, and next-year estimates were revised down 1.1% ($3.51 → $3.47). The earnings inflection is behind the stock, and the order line, the leading indicator of the next leg, is decelerating rather than accelerating. Growth is durable but not re-accelerating, which warrants a 7.0 rather than a higher score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.85, FTI trades at ~24x current-year consensus EPS ($2.96) and ~20x next-year ($3.47). On next-year EPS against ~17% forward growth, the PEG is roughly 1.2: not stretched, but not cheap.

The peer set is heterogeneous and limits its usefulness as an anchor: NOV at 85x and KGS at 88x sit alongside WFRD at 15.8x and AROC at 19.6x, with a median of 30.8x. FTI below the median does not establish a discount, because the median is distorted by low-base-earnings names. The cleaner read is absolute: a high-quality, net-cash, IG subsea leader at ~20x forward earnings is fairly priced for its growth and quality, not obviously mispriced in either direction.

The conditions that would justify treating analyst targets as floors (a stock cheap relative to rising estimates) are absent here. Estimates are flat-to-down on the forward year, and the stock has already re-rated ~106% over twelve months. The consensus mean target of $76 implies ~7% upside; the high of $90 implies ~27%; the low of $45 implies ~36% downside. That dispersion, among the widest in the peer group, signals that the offshore-cycle outcome is two-sided and substantially priced.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects that the market is not demonstrably wrong. If anything, the order deceleration argues the consensus growth path is at modest risk, not under-appreciated. There is no identifiable catalyst-driven mispricing to underwrite a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

TechnipFMC occupies the leading position in a consolidating subsea oligopoly, competing primarily with SLB-OneSubsea (the SLB/Aker Solutions/Subsea7 JV) and Baker Hughes (Subsea Connect). Its structural edge is the integrated iEPCI model and Subsea 2.0 standardization, which lower customer cost and cycle time and lock in higher-margin pull-through services on a growing installed base. The 300bps of YoY Subsea margin expansion is the empirical evidence the moat is translating into pricing and cost advantage rather than marketing claims.

The industry backdrop is favorable: improved deepwater breakevens, new basins (Suriname, Namibia, Mozambique), and gas/LNG demand growth. Operators executing greenfield projects in parallel play directly to FTI's standardization and integration strengths. The flip-side competitive risk is capacity: a hot cycle can strain industry-wide vessel and manufacturing throughput, a constraint analysts explicitly probed on the Q1 call. On trajectory, the company is gaining structural share of E&P capital with widening margins, supporting an 8.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Order momentum is the binding near-term risk.** The $10B target is heavily back-half weighted and unverified. A weak Q2 booking would directly challenge the forward growth path.
2. **Fixed-price EPCI execution risk.** A large share of Subsea revenue is recognized over time on fixed-price contracts with liquidated-damages clauses. An accelerating cycle raises cost-overrun and capacity-strain risk.
3. **Customer/geographic concentration.** Latin America (chiefly Brazil) was $880M of Q1 Subsea revenue, and the order book concentrates in a handful of NOCs/IOCs (Petrobras, TotalEnergies, bp, Equinor, ExxonMobil). Delay or loss of a mega-project would be material.
4. **Commodity cyclicality and Middle East geopolitical risk.** The thesis rests on sustained offshore capex. Management explicitly discusses Middle East conflict, noting it slightly dented Surface Technologies activity and could re-rate regional risk; maritime/piracy threats to vessels and crews are also named. A sharp oil-price decline would pressure new orders. FTI is partly insulated by its $16.5B backlog and improved breakevens, but it is not immune.
5. **Bonding/guarantee exposure:** $2.5B maximum undiscounted potential payments under performance/financial guarantees, plus a $245M JV debt guarantee.
6. **Governance/comp:** combined CEO/Chair role; $56.5M comp "actually paid" to the CEO; VCP dilution of up to 3.6M PSUs.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.2% sits roughly fair to one-year realized volatility of 31.2% (3.0pp spread, 4th percentile within the current chain), so the market is not pricing acute event risk into the front. The term structure is in contango (34.2% near vs. 36.6% at six months), a normal-to-complacent shape. The one caution is an elevated near-term put/call skew of 8.8%, indicating some downside hedging into the June expiry, though the put/call volume ratio (0.19) and OI ratio (0.33) are low, signaling no broad bearish positioning. IV has fallen 4.6pp since late May as the price rose 5.1%, consistent with resolved near-term uncertainty rather than a building catalyst. Short interest is light at 2.6% of float (1.7 days to cover), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor an informed-short signal. Net read: options confirm a fairly-priced, two-sided setup, with mild near-term downside hedging, supportive of HOLD, not of adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The offshore super-cycle delivers, the $10B 2026 Subsea order target is met on back-half acceleration, and the ~$30B 24-month pipeline converts. Subsea EBITDA margin reaches ~23% and holds, earnings compound toward $3.50+ and beyond, and the buyback retires another 5-7% of shares. Re-rating to ~25x next-year EPS plus the high analyst target supports ~$90 (+27%).

**Bear case.** Inbound orders disappoint into the back half, confirming the Q1 deceleration was signal not noise; or a sharp oil-price decline chills marginal FIDs; or a fixed-price project takes a charge as capacity tightens. With the stock near highs and forward estimates already drifting down, multiple compression to ~16-17x on flat estimates takes the shares toward $55-58 (-18% to -22%), with the analyst low at $45 marking the tail.

**Why HOLD, specifically (rebuttal to a BUY).** The score reflects genuine business quality, but four concrete, falsifiable conditions block a BUY at $70.85:
- **Decelerating orders.** Q1 Subsea inbound annualizes to ~$7.6B, ~24% below target. A BUY requires evidence the back-half acceleration is materializing; it is not yet in the data.
- **Forward estimates falling.** Next-year EPS was revised down over 90 days. The accelerating-revision signal that justifies a premium multiple is absent.
- **Bear-case downside exceeds 15%.** From $70.85, the bear path is -18% to -22%, breaching the threshold required to add capital in this sector.
- **No management conviction signal.** The CEO (~$45M), CFO, Subsea President, and CTO sold large blocks in March 2026 with zero offsetting open-market buying near the highs.

Each is checkable against the next earnings release. If Q2 Subsea inbound clears ~$2.5B and forward estimates turn positive, the BUY case strengthens materially.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) is the decisive near-term checkpoint; it tests the Subsea margin guide and the order-conversion run-rate against the target.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 Subsea inbound orders of ~$2.5B+ (the run-rate consistent with the $10B annual target), confirming back-half acceleration.
- Forward-year (next-year) EPS estimate revisions turn positive, ideally combined with a pullback toward the low-$60s that compresses next-year P/E below ~17x.
- Clustered insider open-market buying, or a material order award that de-risks the 2026 target.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 inbound orders below ~$1.8B with no credible path to $10B, signaling the cycle is stalling rather than accelerating.
- A fixed-price EPCI charge or liquidated-damages event indicating execution/capacity strain.
- Forward EPS revisions turning sharply negative (>5% cut over 90 days) alongside a guide-down.

**Next natural reassessment:** Q2 2026 results (late July), with the Middle East geopolitical situation monitored continuously as a macro overlay.

## Conclusion

TechnipFMC is a high-quality, structurally advantaged subsea leader with a strong balance sheet, expanding margins, and a real competitive moat in Subsea 2.0 and iEPCI. Those attributes earn a high composite score of 6.7 and keep the name worth owning for existing holders into the order-conversion thesis. The recommendation is **HOLD (growth_hold)** rather than BUY because the entry point does not offer the asymmetry required to add: inbound orders are decelerating against an unproven target, forward-year estimates are drifting lower, the stock is near its 52-week high after a ~106% twelve-month run, insiders have been net sellers, and the bear case carries -18% to -22% downside inside a sector where Middle East geopolitical risk is live. The cleanest path to an upgrade is concrete: a Q2 order print of ~$2.5B+ and positive forward revisions. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced.
