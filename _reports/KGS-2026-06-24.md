**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest US provider of contract compression services, operating roughly 4.5 million horsepower across approximately 4,700 units, with about 83% of assets deployed in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford. The core business is an infrastructure-leasing model: fixed-revenue, take-or-pay-like contracts of one-to-seven year primary terms, 97.7% fleet utilization in 2025, and Contract Services adjusted gross margins of 68.4% in 2025 rising to 70.6% in Q1 2026. This is a stable, utility-like cash engine that generated $599.7M of operating cash flow and roughly $284M of free cash flow in 2025 (FCF margin ~22%).

The stock has roughly doubled — from a ~$41.64 deal VWAP in early February to ~$71.69 now — on the strategic pivot. On April 1, 2026, Kodiak closed the ~$675M acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions (rebranded Kodiak Power Solutions), entering behind-the-meter distributed gas-fired power generation targeting the data center / AI power-demand market. Management has procured 260+ MW of capacity, targets 650+ MW, and plans 300–500 MW/year of growth through 2030. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised to $820–860M to include power. Current-year EPS consensus rose +29.2% over 90 days and current-quarter +60.8%.

The problem is that most of the power-generation value is now anticipatory. The segment has barely started contributing, purchase accounting was incomplete as of the Q1 10-Q, and the stock trades at a 94.8x trailing GAAP P/E (depressed by D&A, interest, and one-time charges) and ~27x current-year EPS of $2.65. The balance sheet carries $2.85B of debt with ~$200M of annual interest consuming the bulk of operating income, and the company is simultaneously funding a dividend (~$172M/yr), buybacks, ~$200M/yr compression growth capex, and a multi-hundred-million-dollar power build-out. We see a durable growth thesis with a fair-but-not-cheap entry near the 52-week high, elevated put skew, and binary tail risks (Mexico FCPA matter). We rate the shares HOLD with a positive growth bias.

## Company Overview

Kodiak owns large-horsepower (>1,000 hp) gas compressors — about 80% of its fleet — and leases them to upstream and midstream operators under fixed-revenue contracts with guaranteed 95–98% mechanical-availability standards and annual inflation escalators. Compression is non-discretionary infrastructure: associated and produced gas cannot flow to gathering and processing without it, which gives the leasing model durable demand tied to Permian and Eagle Ford production volumes rather than spot commodity prices.

Two legacy segments structure the company: Contract Services (~90% of revenue, the compression-leasing core, to be renamed Compression Infrastructure) and Other Services (station construction, overhauls, parts — capital-light, cross-sold). With the April 1, 2026 close of the DPS acquisition, a third reportable segment (Power Infrastructure) begins reporting in Q2 2026.

The DPS transaction repositions Kodiak from a pure oilfield-services lessor toward an energy-infrastructure / power-for-data-centers profile. The operating logic is coherent: Kodiak's core competency is owning, deploying, and maintaining large fleets of gas-fired rotating equipment under long-term contracts, and that competency transfers directly to behind-the-meter gas-fired power generation for data centers facing grid-interconnection bottlenecks. The mechanism that supports the demand thesis runs as follows: AI/data-center load growth → multi-year grid interconnection queues → behind-the-meter on-site gas generation as a bridge → demand for turnkey gas-fired power assets under long-term contracts.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue scaled from $850M (2023) to $1,159M (2024) to $1,308M (2025, +12.8%), the step-up largely driven by the 2024 CSI Compressco acquisition. Q1 2026 revenue was $345.8M (+4.9% YoY), with Contract Services up 6.2% on price increases and higher revenue-generating horsepower. Margins are strong and expanding: Contract Services adjusted gross margin reached 70.6% in Q1 2026 (from 67.7%), and consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin runs near 55%.

The net line is modest because of heavy depreciation and ~$200M of annual interest: 2025 net income was $80.5M (6.2% net margin), GAAP diluted EPS $0.89 versus adjusted EPS $1.54. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS dropped to $0.20 from $0.33, but the decline was driven by a one-time $36.5M loss on debt extinguishment tied to defeasing the 2029 notes; adjusted EPS was $0.60 and beat consensus ($0.54) by ~10.2%. The earnings record is mixed — two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the Q3 2025 miss (-29.6%) and Q4 2025 miss (-19.2%) bracketed by Q2 2025 (+16.1%) and Q1 2026 (+10.2%) beats. Forward confidence rests more on the rising estimate trend than on a clean delivery streak.

Cash flow is the attraction of the leasing model. 2025 operating cash flow ran against $315M of total capex ($200M growth + $76M maintenance + $63M other). FY2026 guidance is adjusted EBITDA $820–860M and discretionary cash flow $520–570M.

The balance sheet is the central financial issue. Total debt rose to $2.85B by Q1 2026 from $2.6B at year-end 2025, with D/E around 2.58x, a current ratio of 0.84, and an Altman Z-Score of 1.17 in the nominal "distress" zone. That Z-Score is misleading in isolation: it reflects a leveraged equipment-leasing structure, not financial fragility. Kodiak is covenant-compliant (max leverage 5.25x, min interest coverage 2.5x), holds ~$1.6B of liquidity ($94M cash + ~$1.5B ABL availability), and generates robust operating cash flow. Liability management has been disciplined: issuing $1.0B of 5.875% notes due 2031 to redeem 7.25% 2029 notes lowers cash interest and pushes maturities out, with nothing major due until 2030. The constraint is that interest consumes the bulk of operating income, leaving limited cushion if production volumes or contract pricing weaken, and the company is funding four competing uses of capital (dividend, buyback, compression growth capex, power build-out plus a committed additional ~$244.8M for power-gen assets) on an already-levered balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth vectors are at work. The first is the legacy compression business, which carries durable single-digit organic tailwinds: rising Permian associated-gas volumes, Gulf Coast LNG export growth, and the electric-compression trend. Pricing power is intact (Contract Services revenue +6.2% in Q1 on price and added horsepower), utilization is near a structural ceiling at 98.0%, and the recurring contract base provides high visibility.

The second vector — the reason for the re-rating — is distributed power. The 650+ MW capacity target with 300–500 MW/year of growth through 2030 is a materially larger TAM than incremental compression horsepower, and it carries a higher-multiple narrative because the end-customer is the AI/data-center buildout rather than the oilfield. The raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance signals management's confidence that power begins contributing this year.

The estimate-revision data is consistent with an earnings inflection: current-quarter EPS +60.8% over 90 days, current-year +29.2%, next-quarter +21.6%, next-year +17.3%. Current-year revisions cross the +30% velocity threshold that typically precedes continued re-rating, and they accompany double-digit revenue growth. We weight this as a genuine positive growth signal. Growth Prospects is the dimension we lean on most heavily given its reliability for sorting outcomes, and Kodiak earns a 7.0 here: the compression core is stable and pricing-positive, the power pivot expands the addressable market into a structurally growing demand pool, and consensus is rising rather than falling.

One caution: next-year EPS was revised down 4.2% over the trailing 30 days even as the longer-window trend stayed strongly positive, suggesting analysts are beginning to mark in the financing and execution costs of the power build-out. That is the right tension to watch.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP EPS of $0.89 the stock screens at 94.8x, but that figure is meaningless given the $36.5M extinguishment charge, heavy D&A, and the M&A-driven cost base. The relevant lens is forward earnings: at $71.69 against current-year consensus of $2.65, the stock trades at ~27x, and ~24x next-year's $2.97. Against the peer median P/E of 30.8x for Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, KGS is roughly in line to modestly below. Direct compression comparable Archrock trades at 19.6x, which frames the leasing-core multiple; the gap between Archrock's ~20x and Kodiak's ~27x is the power-generation premium the market is already paying.

The dividend yield of 2.83% is well above the peer median of 1.3% and provides genuine valuation support; the payout (~$172M/yr) is covered by the ~$284M FCF base, with room to grow as power contributes. Analyst targets span $76 low to $94 high (mean $84.07, median $84), all above the current price, implying ~18% upside to consensus. On an inflection name with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, those targets are more likely to drift higher than to cap the stock, and we read them as a floor rather than a ceiling.

The reason we do not score valuation above mid-range is that the asymmetry is no longer clearly favorable at this price. The power-generation segment that justifies the premium has barely started contributing, purchase accounting is incomplete, and a low trailing multiple here would be a value-trap signal in any case. The stock sits within ~8% of its 52-week high after roughly doubling, and the market is paying for execution that remains in front of the company. We assign a 4.5: not stretched, but not offering the asymmetric mispricing that would warrant a higher mark. The cheapest framing — "27x forward for a data-center power play is reasonable" — depends on the power thesis converting, which is the open question.

## Competitive Landscape

Kodiak is the scale leader in US contract compression following the CSI integration, competing primarily with Archrock, USA Compression Partners, and other large-hp operators in a consolidating industry. Its competitive position in the core business is improving on trajectory: gross margin is expanding, utilization is at a structural high, pricing is moving up, and the large-hp tilt secures longer contracts and higher margins than the small-hp commodity end of the market. The 95–98% mechanical-availability guarantee and the integrated Other Services cross-sell deepen customer stickiness.

The complete EQT exit (from 43.1% to zero ownership in 2025, with Kodiak repurchasing 2.7M shares from the sponsor) is an underappreciated structural positive: it removed both a governance overhang and the share-supply pressure that had weighed on the float. That improves the equity's positioning independent of operations.

The power pivot is the differentiating bet. If Kodiak's equipment-operations competency transfers cleanly to behind-the-meter generation, it positions the company against a structural tailwind that the market is only beginning to price for a name in this subsector. The risk is that distributed power is a genuinely different market — data centers, microgrids, and electricity-demand customers rather than E&P operators — and execution in a new business line acquired via M&A is unproven. We score competitive position 6.5: the core trajectory is clearly improving and the power optionality is real, tempered by the fact that the differentiating segment has not yet demonstrated competitive durability.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** D/E ~2.58x, current ratio 0.84. The ABL is floating-rate with only $325M notional swapped, so a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the financing-dependent growth model. Growth, dividends, and the power build-out all depend on continued capital-market access. This is normal leverage for an equipment-leasing model, but it leaves limited room for a production or pricing downturn.

**Power-gen execution and incomplete accounting.** Kodiak is entering a new business via the DPS acquisition, with purchase accounting unfinished as of the Q1 10-Q and an additional ~$244.8M committed to power-gen assets over ~4 years. Integration risk, customer-retention risk, and the absence of a disclosed signed mega-contract mean the data-center "market" reference reflects target customers, not booked revenue. The central debate is whether the re-rating is justified or ahead of itself.

**Mexico FCPA / sanctions matter.** An internal investigation found that the acquired CSI Mexican business likely made payments to persons associated with a cartel-designated (FTO/SDGT) organization to protect employees and secure site access. Kodiak self-reported to DOJ, OFAC, and the SEC, sold the Mexican operations on 9/30/2025 (booking a $33.3M loss), and deems amounts immaterial. The investigation is substantially complete, but criminal/civil enforcement outcomes are unpredictable — a binary tail risk a standardized screen would miss.

**Concentration.** ~83% of assets in two basins; top four customers ~31–32% of revenue (one >10%). A regional disruption or top-customer loss would be outsized. About 9% of revenue-generating horsepower is month-to-month and cancellable on short notice.

**Capital-allocation tension.** Funding a growing dividend, buybacks, compression capex, and a multi-hundred-million-dollar power build-out simultaneously, on a levered balance sheet, without further leverage or equity issuance, is the key financial story of 2026–2027.

The Texas sales-tax dispute ($102.3M accrued at YE25) was settled and paid in Q1 2026, reducing one overhang. On balance, the combination of high leverage, an unproven new business priced into the equity, and an open FCPA matter warrants a below-average risk score of 4.5 — these are specific, partly binary risks not fully reflected at a 52-week-high valuation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.0% sits rich to 34.7% 1-year historical volatility (a 10.3pp spread), with put/call skew of 9.3% indicating elevated put demand and an implied ±9.0% move into the nearest expiry. The term structure is flat (45.0% near, 43.6% far), distributing uncertainty evenly. Since June 9 the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 1.19 to 0.45 as the stock rose 8.3% and IV fell 6.5pp — bearish positioning unwound into the rally. The persistent put skew is the divergence to flag against a constructive growth view: the options market is paying up for downside protection even as directional positioning turned less bearish, consistent with a stock that has run hard and carries binary tail risks. Short interest is modest at 7.9% of float with 2.8 days to cover, so neither a crowded short nor meaningful squeeze fuel is present.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($90–94).** Power-generation execution proves out: Kodiak signs behind-the-meter data-center contracts, ramps toward 650+ MW, and the segment begins materially contributing to the EBITDA base. The leasing competency transfers cleanly, the market continues to pay a power-infrastructure multiple, and rising estimates keep dragging consensus targets higher. The compression core compounds at single digits with expanding margins, the dividend grows, and the EQT-overhang-free float supports re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range. At ~$94 the stock would trade ~32x current-year EPS, defensible only if power converts.

**Bear case ($50–55, roughly -23% to -30%).** Power execution disappoints or slips, purchase accounting reveals dilution or unexpected liabilities, and the market re-rates KGS back toward its compression-comparable multiple (Archrock ~20x), implying a ~$53 stock on $2.65 EPS. A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs and pressures the multiple on the levered model. The DOJ/OFAC matter produces a material penalty. Capital-allocation strain forces equity issuance or a slower build-out. The elevated put skew prices a version of this scenario.

The base case sits between: the compression core delivers, power contributes modestly but on schedule, and estimates continue rising. At ~24–25x next-year EPS of $2.97 the stock supports roughly $74–78. The risk/reward from $71.69 is close to balanced: meaningful upside if power converts, comparable downside if the multiple compresses to the leasing comp.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment horizon 12 months.**

The trajectory over the prior six reports has been a steady HOLD with improving sub-scores (valuation 3.5→4.8, financial health 4.5→5.5, risk 4.0→5.0). New data is consistent with that improvement — adjusted EPS beat, raised guidance, disciplined refinancing, completed Texas tax settlement, EQT fully exited — but the stock has appreciated alongside the fundamentals, so the risk/reward has not improved enough to justify upgrading to BUY. We have now issued HOLD four consecutive times; the bar for continuing it unchanged is met because power-gen execution has not yet resolved, and paying up for it near the 52-week high with persistent put skew is not asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- First disclosed long-term behind-the-meter power contract(s) with named data-center/hyperscaler counterparties, or evidence of MW deployed converting to contracted EBITDA.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry against the $84 mean target.
- Finalized DPS purchase accounting confirming no material adverse liabilities, plus continued positive next-year estimate revisions (reversing the recent -4.2% 30-day mark).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material DOJ/OFAC enforcement outcome on the Mexico matter.
- Equity issuance to fund the four competing capital uses, or net leverage approaching the 5.25x covenant.
- Power-gen targets cut or timeline slipped, or a top-four customer non-renewal.
- Q3/Q4 2026 EBITDA tracking below the $820–860M guide.

**Next natural reassessment:** Q2 2026 earnings (first reporting period with the Power Infrastructure segment and finalized DPS accounting) is the decisive near-term checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Kodiak is a high-quality compression-leasing business with durable recurring cash flow, expanding margins, near-ceiling utilization, and a cleaned-up float after EQT's full exit. The distributed-power pivot is strategically coherent and aimed at one of the strongest demand vectors in the market, and rising estimates confirm an earnings inflection is underway. Those strengths support a positive growth bias.

At ~$71.69, however, the stock already pays a meaningful premium over its compression comparable for power-generation execution that has barely begun, on a levered balance sheet, with an unresolved FCPA matter and elevated put demand in the options market. The reward to the bull case (~$90–94) is roughly matched by the downside to the bear case ($50–55). We rate KGS HOLD with a growth bias, and would turn more constructive on the first signed power contracts or a pullback toward the low-$60s.
