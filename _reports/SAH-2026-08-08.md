**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sonic Automotive delivered record consolidated revenue of $3,934.0M in Q2 2026 (+7.6% year over year) and, per trade press, a record quarterly gross profit dollar figure. Neither statistic describes the earnings trajectory. Gross profit rose only 2.3% while SG&A rose 7.8%, pushing SG&A to 72.2% of gross profit from 68.5%, a 370 basis point deterioration. Pre-impairment segment income fell 22% to $80.2M and Adjusted EBITDA fell 8.7% to $157.7M. The GAAP swing from $(1.34) to $1.79 in diluted EPS is entirely an artifact of the $172.4M impairment booked in the prior-year quarter and tells the reader nothing about the operating trend.

The mechanism is gross profit per unit compression across every vehicle line. Same-store franchised new retail GPU fell $559 (−16%) to $2,872; used retail GPU fell $214 (−13%) to $1,401; wholesale losses widened from $(113) to $(569) per unit; F&I GPU fell $108 to $2,619. Aftermarket product penetration fell 2,730 basis points. Volume is compensating: consolidated retail new plus used units rose 8% to 77,540 and EchoPark units rose 17%, but the per-unit economics are eroding faster than volume compounds. Management's externally reported 2026 EchoPark GPU target of $3,100–$3,300 sits below both the Q2 actual of $3,292 and the H1 actual of $3,396, which means the company is guiding to more of the compression already visible in exchange for units.

Two things restrain a more negative rating. First, capital returns have been well executed: 2.2M Class A shares were repurchased in H1 2026 at a derived average of $64.55 versus today's $82.44, cutting total shares outstanding 5.86% in six months, with $527.9M of authorization remaining. Second, at $82.44 the shares are 27.5% below the July 29 high of $113.67 and trade at 11.8x the $6.97 FY2026 consensus, with 22.5% short interest against a free float of roughly 9.7M shares and 7.7 days to cover, mechanics that punish aggressive bearishness. The rating is REDUCE rather than AVOID: trim into strength, do not short. The prior two reports on this name were HOLD; the Q2 disclosures supply specific fundamental deterioration (negative operating leverage, negative GAAP operating cash flow, debt-funded distributions, a new 364-day bridge facility) at a share price essentially unchanged from the prior review at $80.73, which is worse risk/reward.

## Company Overview

Sonic operates three reportable segments as of June 30, 2026. Franchised Dealerships (~85% of revenue) comprises 107 stores holding 127 new-vehicle franchises across 24 brands in 17 states, plus 15 collision centers. EchoPark (~15%) is 18 pre-owned-only stores in 10 states targeting one-to-four-year-old vehicles, including Northwest Motorsport. Powersports (~2%) is 46 franchises at 20 locations, heavily Harley-Davidson weighted.

Brand mix is the defining structural feature. Q2 2026 franchised new-vehicle revenue was 62% luxury (BMW 22%, Mercedes-Benz 12%, Land Rover 9%, Lexus 6%, Audi 4%, Porsche 4%, Cadillac 4%), 23% mid-line import, 15% domestic. Land Rover rose from 5% to 9% following the 2025 acquisition of five JLR dealerships (four purchased from US Auto Trust for roughly $359.9M gross, plus JLR Santa Monica), making Sonic the largest-volume JLR retailer in the U.S. with 11 stores. Chrysler/Stellantis fell from 5% to 3%.

The luxury weighting supports higher absolute gross profit per unit and a richer Fixed Operations annuity: same-store Fixed Operations gross margin held at 51.0% in Q2, down only 30bp. It also concentrates roughly 34% of franchised new-vehicle revenue in two franchisors (BMW and Mercedes-Benz) and roughly 51% in German and British imports exposed to vehicle tariff policy.

A data correction is necessary before any multiple is computed. The supplied market capitalization of $1,615M equals 19,585,427 Class A shares times $82.44. Sonic also has 12,029,375 Class B shares outstanding, economically pari passu on dividends (H1 2026 declared $16.0M Class A, $9.5M Class B) and included in the 32.1M diluted share count. Total shares are 31,608,830 and the correct equity value is $2,606M. Any screen using $1.6B understates equity value by 38% and makes the stock look materially cheaper than it is.

FY2025 gross margin expanded to 15.72% from 15.42%, and SG&A as a percentage of gross profit improved to 70.4% from 71.9%. The 45% decline in net income is explained by a $173.8M non-cash impairment recorded at the April 30, 2025 annual test date: $165.9M of franchise assets in Franchised, $6.5M in Powersports, plus small PP&E items. Pre-impairment segment income rose 33% to $346.5M.

That 33% is not a clean operating figure. FY2025 Franchised segment income of $316.1M included a $40.0M CDK cyber-incident insurance recovery, against −$5.0M storm damage, −$5.5M disposition losses and −$0.7M legal. FY2024's $257.6M included −$13.0M CDK excess compensation, −$8.3M storm, +$3.5M JV gain, +$10.0M cyber recovery and −$2.2M severance. Adjusting both: $287.3M versus $277.6M, or +3.5% underlying. FY2025 was better than the GAAP headline and considerably weaker than the pre-impairment headline.

Q2 2026 reverses the direction of the distortion. Reported ($M): revenue 3,934.0 (+7.6%), gross profit 616.2 (+2.3%), SG&A 444.6 (+7.8%), net income 57.4 versus $(45.6), diluted EPS $1.79 versus $(1.34). Pre-impairment segment income $80.2M versus $103.3M (−22%). Adjusted EBITDA $157.7M versus $172.7M (−8.7%). GAAP overstates the quarter as thoroughly as it understated FY2025.

The reported adjusted EPS beat ($1.82 versus $1.75 non-GAAP consensus, per Zacks) requires two adjustments to be like-for-like. Prior-year Q2 adjusted EPS of $2.19 included a $10.0M pretax cyber-insurance benefit worth roughly $0.21 after tax on 34.1M shares, implying ~$1.98 ex-item. Current-quarter results benefited from a change in depreciable useful-life estimates that reduced depreciation by ~$4.6M in Q2 (~$9.2M in H1), worth roughly $0.10 after tax on 32.1M shares, implying ~$1.72 ex-item. Underlying decline of roughly 13%, against a consensus that had already been reduced. The useful-life change is a routine auditor-reviewed estimate revision; its analytical significance is only that it affects EPS while leaving Adjusted EBITDA and cash flow untouched, which is why Adjusted EBITDA at −8.7% is the cleaner read.

Balance sheet at June 30, 2026 versus December 31, 2025 ($M): cash 19.2 versus 6.3; inventories 2,324.5 versus 2,012.9; floor plan notes 2,239.6 versus 1,932.7; current maturities of long-term debt 193.3 versus 52.4; long-term debt 1,565.5; finance leases 391.8; equity 1,025.6 versus 1,068.1.

Debt structure: $650.0M 4.625% notes due November 2029 (fair value $635.4M), $500.0M 4.875% notes due November 2031 (fair value $480.0M), $421.6M Mortgage plus Sidecar facilities maturing November 17, 2027 and amortizing 1.875% quarterly, a $150.0M unsecured PNC Bridge Facility entered March 27, 2026 at SOFR+250bp maturing at the earlier of 364 days or refinancing of the Mortgage Facility, and $54.6M of notes to finance companies. The revolver is undrawn with $286.9M available.

Derived leverage: TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $604.5M (FY2025 $614.6M less H1 2025 $316.7M plus H1 2026 $306.6M). Debt plus finance leases of $2,150.6M equals 3.56x; adding operating leases of $281.6M gives 4.02x. Net debt ex-floor plan to equity is 2.08x. These are our definitions, not covenant definitions; the 10-Q states compliance with a 1.20x minimum fixed charge coverage and 5.75x maximum lease-adjusted leverage covenant but discloses no computed values, so actual headroom is unknown.

Company-defined liquidity of $676.1M compares with $701.6M at year-end 2025 and $861.6M at year-end 2024, an $185M decline over eighteen months. Composition matters: $275.0M is a floor plan deposit (down from $340.0M at end-2024), $286.9M is revolver availability, $95.0M is mortgage facility availability, and only $19.2M is unrestricted cash against a $6.4B asset base.

Cash flow is where the deterioration is sharpest. H1 2026 GAAP operating cash flow was negative $64.7M versus positive $332.6M. The company's adjusted measure, which reclassifies non-trade floor plan borrowings from financing to operating, was $235.7M versus $632.1M, down 63% even on the more generous basis. Capex nearly doubled to $154.5M ($83.9M real estate, $40.7M facility construction, $29.9M other), leaving derived free cash flow of $81.2M against $142.0M of buybacks, $25.3M of dividends and $66.3M of acquisitions. The $152M gap was funded with $190.0M of new borrowings and $60.0M of disposition proceeds.

H1 should not be annualized. Q1 is seasonally weakest, the $314.3M inventory build was deliberate (EchoPark days' supply raised to 47 from 41), and cash taxes paid rose $44.3M to $52.4M. But the direction is unambiguous, and $67.5M of facility construction and aircraft commitments are substantially due within twelve months.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is real and volume-driven; earnings growth is not. Consolidated retail units rose 8%, EchoPark units rose 17%, and Sonic added six Harley-Davidson locations for $66.3M gross. Against that, industry demand is decelerating: H1 2026 total SAAR was 15.9M (−2%) with retail SAAR 13.2M (−4%), and the company guides FY2026 SAAR to 15.5–16.0M, down 2–5%.

EchoPark is the clearest illustration. Units +17%, combined used plus F&I GPU down $455 (−12%) to $3,292, segment income down 38% to $7.2M, segment Adjusted EBITDA down to $13.9M from $16.4M, and SG&A up to 73.4% of gross profit from 68.0%. At a guided $3,100–$3,300 GPU with a 73% SG&A ratio, incremental EchoPark units contribute very little consolidated earnings. H1 EchoPark segment income of $23.4M also includes a $3.6M lease-termination gain.

Powersports carries 160 days of new-vehicle supply (versus 135 a year earlier) and 103 days of used against H1 segment income of $0.3M, following a $6.5M FY2025 franchise-asset impairment. The offset is that H1 Powersports Adjusted EBITDA improved to $4.8M from $1.3M and $40.3M of the $66.3M purchase price went to real estate, which limits downside.

Fixed Operations is the durable growth line: same-store revenue +3%, gross profit +2%, margin 51.0%. An aging U.S. fleet and luxury warranty work support this annuity, and it is the reason the franchised model does not collapse when vehicle margins normalize.

Consensus estimates are stable to marginally rising: current year $6.97 (+3.4% over 90 days, +1.1% over 30), next year $7.60 (+3.1%, +0.9%), current quarter $1.84 (−1.0% over 30 days). This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection; it is drift, and it is being supported partly by a shrinking share count rather than growing profit. Share count fell from 33,576,002 to 31,608,830 in six months. Holding FY2026 EPS near $7.00 while Adjusted EBITDA declines mid-single digits requires the buyback to keep running, which requires continued borrowing.

Growth Prospects score 4.5, down from 5.5 in the prior review. The downgrade reflects the confirmed conversion of positive revenue growth into declining segment profit and management's guidance to further GPU compression.

## Valuation Assessment

On the correct 31,608,830 shares at the $82.44 official close: equity value $2,606M; enterprise value including finance leases and excluding floor plan of $4,737M ($2,606 + $1,758.8 debt + $391.8 finance leases − $19.2 cash).

- EV / TTM Adjusted EBITDA ($604.5M): **7.8x**
- P/E on FY2026 consensus $6.97: **11.8x**
- P/E on FY2027 consensus $7.60: **10.8x**
- P/E on FY2025 proxy-disclosed adjusted EPS $6.72: 12.3x
- Dividend: $0.41 quarterly, $1.64 annualized = **1.99% yield**, 24.4% payout on adjusted EPS

The supplied trailing EPS of $3.42 equals FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS, which is depressed by the $173.8M impairment; a 24x trailing multiple computed from it is meaningless. The supplied 13.57x P/E is not reproducible from either the GAAP or consensus figures and should be disregarded in favor of the consensus-based multiples above.

Peer context requires care. The supplied peer median P/E of 22.9x is distorted by MNRO at 585.6x and AAP at 54.1x, which are aftermarket parts and service businesses, not franchised dealers. The relevant comparables are Group 1 at 12.5x and Asbury at 7.1x. Sonic at 11.8x forward sits between them and near Group 1, a company with lower leverage and without Sonic's EchoPark and Powersports drag. There is no visible discount to closest peers.

The EBITDA base is the crux. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $614.6M with H1 at $316.7M and H2 at $297.9M, so H2 is not seasonally stronger on this measure. H1 2026 was $306.6M. A flat-to-slightly-down H2 puts FY2026 near $585–595M. At 7.5–8.0x that band, enterprise value is $4,388–4,760M, implying equity of $2,257–2,629M, or **$71–83 per share**. On earnings, 10.5–11.5x a FY2027 estimate haircut to $7.00–7.20 for GPU compression and higher post-refinancing interest gives **$74–83**.

Both approaches place fair value at or modestly below the current price. Valuation score 4.5: the multiple is not stretched, but neither does it embed a margin of safety, and the compressed-looking earnings multiple sits on an EBITDA base that management is guiding lower. A low headline multiple on deteriorating unit economics with a 2027 refinancing wall is the classic setup for a value trap, and we do not credit cheapness in the absence of a catalyst.

The analyst mean target of $99 (+20%) and high of $139 deserve limited weight here: consensus sentiment is degrading, a sell rating was added in the past month, and the targets predate or only partially reflect the Q2 GPU trend. On a name with falling segment income, consensus targets are not a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonic's advantages are portfolio composition and operating execution, not a durable moat. Franchise value derives from manufacturer-granted territorial exclusivity; there is no proprietary technology or customer lock-in. Three specifics matter.

The 62% luxury mix delivers the highest-quality Fixed Operations annuity among the public dealers and higher absolute GPU. It also concentrates exposure in imported nameplates subject to tariff and manufacturer incentive policy, with manufacturer floor plan assistance of $16.1M in Q2 and $30.6M in H1 providing a partial offset that is itself franchisor-controlled.

The JLR concentration is a franchise-level bet no peer has taken. It is producing volume, with Land Rover rising from 5% to 9% of franchised new-vehicle revenue, but it concentrates Sonic in a manufacturer that suffered a production-halting cyberattack in September 2025 and whose U.S. franchise economics are unproven at this scale.

EchoPark competes directly with CarMax and Carvana on price. It reached scale and profitability, but the combination of a guided GPU decline and a 73.4% SG&A-to-gross-profit ratio indicates it is a price-taker in that competition rather than a cost leader.

The most useful external benchmark comes from Sonic's own proxy. Indexed to $100 at year-end 2020, SAH TSR closed 2025 at 176.79 versus a peer group at 193.86, after 177.26 versus 136.65 a year earlier; SAH returned −0.3% in 2025 while its chosen peer group returned +41.9%. That 42-point single-year relative miss coincided with the impairment and the JLR acquisition program. On a cumulative 2020–2025 basis the gap narrows to 77% versus 94%, and peer-group composition was not verified, so this should inform rather than determine the assessment.

Competitive Position score 4.7. Level is respectable; trajectory is negative across new GPU, used GPU, F&I attachment (−2,730bp penetration), EchoPark unit economics and relative TSR. An alternative reading, that GPU normalization is industry-wide mean reversion as post-pandemic scarcity unwinds, supported by new-vehicle days' supply of only 56 versus 54 a year ago, is legitimate and is the core of the bull case. It does not change the direction of the earnings effect over the next twelve months.

## Risk Assessment

**GPU normalization is the dominant earnings variable.** Roughly $100 of new-vehicle GPU across approximately 110,000 annual new retail units equals about $11M of pretax income. Given a 16% same-store decline already realized and 56 days of supply still below historical norms, the compression plausibly has further to run.

**The 2027 refinancing wall.** $421.6M of Mortgage and Sidecar debt matures November 17, 2027, and the $150M Bridge Facility matures at the earlier of 364 days (roughly late March 2027) or refinancing of the Mortgage Facility. Current maturities of long-term debt already jumped to $193.3M from $52.4M. The 2029 and 2031 notes trade below par ($635.4M and $480.0M against $650M and $500M carrying), implying refinancing above the 4.625%/4.875% coupons. This is being negotiated with $19.2M of unrestricted cash and negative GAAP H1 operating cash flow.

**Interest rate exposure is effectively unhedged at current levels.** $2.6B of variable-rate debt; a 100bp move equals $12.9M per six-month period. Interest rate caps of $400M/$200M/$400M are all struck at 5.50% versus one-month Term SOFR near 3.65%, with a combined fair value of $0.2M, protection against a spike, not against current carry.

**Impairment recurrence.** Accumulated impairments already total $1.1B in Franchised and $202.9M in EchoPark. Franchise assets are Level 3 valuations under the multi-period excess earnings method, directly sensitive to projected operating margins, which are compressing. The April 30, 2026 test produced no impairment, but Franchised goodwill was assessed only qualitatively on prior-year cushion, a lower-rigor procedure than the quantitative DCF applied to Powersports. The April 2027 test is a live risk.

**Tariff and franchisor concentration.** Approximately 51% of franchised new-vehicle revenue is German or British imports; BMW and Mercedes together are roughly 34%. Franchise agreements impose facility-image and capital investment mandates, visible in the $40.7M of H1 facility construction, and carry transfer restrictions and rights of first refusal.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure with the Smith family holding all 12.0M super-voting Class B shares; Class A holders own roughly 62% of the economics and a small minority of votes. Three Smith family members sit in senior governance or management roles. CEO compensation rose 82% from $6.51M (2021) to $11.83M (2025) while company-selected Adjusted EPS fell 17% ($8.05 to $6.72) and net income fell 66%. An Item 5.01 "Changes in Control of Registrant" 8-K filed April 25, 2024 and two Item 5.02 officer/director filings (February 13 and May 8, 2026) remain unread, open questions at a controlled company.

**Insider activity is genuinely two-sided.** Paul P. Rusnak, a 10% owner, made six open-market purchases between February 5 and 17, 2026 totaling roughly 100,000 shares for ~$5.99M at ~$60, bringing holdings to exactly 5,100,000 shares (26% of Class A, 16% of total). Against that, President Jeff Dyke sold approximately 100,000 shares for ~$8.4M across June 8–10 at $82.97–$85.22, and CFO Heath Byrd sold 9,526 shares at $85.00 on May 28 and 5,061 at $85.00 on June 2. PSU grants were made May 6, 2026, so a portion of the selling is routine post-vest liquidity. The pattern of an outside 10% owner buying at $60 and the operating President selling ~$8.4M in three consecutive days near the 52-week-high zone is not disqualifying on its own but does not support a high management score alongside the compensation record.

**Sector context.** Consumer Discretionary is the weakest area for stock selection in our framework, and the transmission channel is active: higher financing costs compress affordability, unit demand and F&I attachment simultaneously. Sonic sits at the intersection of that channel and its own floor plan interest cost.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 52.4% against 43.6% one-year realized is rich by 8.7 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (52.4% at 12 days, 44.0% at 40 days, 45.2% at 103 days). The market is pricing elevated near-term event risk, with an implied move of ±8.3% on the August 21 expiry. The put/call skew is reported call-heavy at −9.3% for the front tenor, flattening to −3.2% at six months. Combined with the 22.5% short interest and 7.7 days to cover, the call-heavy front-month positioning is most consistent with squeeze-oriented and hedging demand rather than directional confirmation of the bear case. Expressing a negative view through short stock or long puts is expensive and carries meaningful gap-up risk. Trimming long exposure, or writing covered calls into rich front-month vol, is the more sensible expression.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $104 (+26%)

New-vehicle GPU compression is industry-wide mean reversion, not Sonic-specific share loss, and days' supply of 56 remains historically lean, so the compression bottoms in late 2026. Fixed Operations at 51.0% gross margin on an aging fleet keeps compounding through the vehicle-margin trough. EchoPark hits 12–15% unit growth and stabilizes GPU near $3,300, adding operating leverage as the 18-store base absorbs fixed cost, with 2–4 new stores in 2027. The $527.9M remaining buyback authorization retires further shares over eighteen months at prices below the February–June average of $64.55, holding EPS near $8.00 even on flat EBITDA. Any Fed easing relieves both floor plan interest (~$78M annually) and the $2.6B variable-rate book, and makes the November 2027 refinancing routine. Rusnak's 5.1M-share position at ~$60, combined with 22.5% short interest on a 9.7M-share float, produces a violent re-rating on the first quarter of GPU stabilization. At 13x $8.00, the stock is $104.

### Bear Case — $56 (−32%)

GPU compression continues as management has effectively guided, with EchoPark GPU landing at the $3,100 low end and franchised new GPU falling toward $2,600. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA declines to roughly $520M. The Mortgage Facility refinances at 250–300bp above the current blend, adding $15–25M of annual interest, and the bridge facility forces the refinancing to be completed on the lender's timetable rather than Sonic's. Adjusted EPS falls to $5.50 despite the buyback, and the buyback slows because it is being funded with debt against $19.2M of cash. The April 2027 impairment test takes another charge against $454.1M of franchise assets with minimal cushion. At 9–10x $5.50, the stock is $50–55; the 2025 relative TSR precedent shows the market will discount Sonic specifically rather than the sector.

### Base Case — $76 (−8%)

FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near consensus at $6.90–7.00, supported more by the 5.9% six-month share count reduction than by profit growth. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA finishes at $585–595M, down 3–5%. FY2027 EPS of $7.00–7.20 is achieved with buyback support offsetting a $15–20M interest headwind. At 7.75x $590M of EBITDA, enterprise value is $4,573M, equity $2,442M, and per share $77.25; at 10.8x $7.10 of FY2027 EPS the result is $76.70. We use $76.

Probability weighting 45% base, 25% bull, 30% bear yields an expected return of roughly −6% to −7% before the 2.0% dividend, which is why the rating is REDUCE rather than HOLD.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing positions on strength toward $88–92, which is the zone the stock traded in during the first week of August and roughly the top of the base-case range. Retain no more than a half position into the Q3 print (late October 2026), the next test of whether new and used GPU stabilize sequentially. Do not initiate new long exposure at $82.44. Do not short: the 22.5% short interest, 7.7 days to cover, front-month IV at 52.4% versus 43.6% realized, and a 10% owner holding 5.1M shares make the borrow expensive and the squeeze risk material.

**Base case target $76** (−8% from $82.44), derived as 7.75x $590M FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA less $2,131M net debt plus finance leases, cross-checked at 10.8x $7.10 FY2027 EPS. **Bull $104** (+26%). **Bear $56** (−32%).

## Conclusion

Sonic is a competently run franchised dealer with the best luxury mix among the public groups, a 51% gross margin Fixed Operations annuity, and a management team that repurchased 5.9% of the share count in six months at an average price 22% below today's. It is also a business whose Q2 2026 disclosures showed 7.6% revenue growth converting into a 22% decline in pre-impairment segment income, with per-unit gross profit falling across new, used, wholesale and F&I simultaneously and management guiding EchoPark GPU below the levels already achieved. The earnings support at the per-share line is increasingly a debt-funded buyback: H1 free cash flow of $81.2M against $233.6M of discretionary outflows, closed with $190.0M of new borrowings including a 364-day bridge that is itself tied to refinancing $421.6M of mortgage debt in November 2027 against $19.2M of unrestricted cash.

At $82.44 on the correct 31.6M share count, the shares trade at 11.8x FY2026 consensus and 7.8x TTM Adjusted EBITDA, near Group 1 at 12.5x and well above Asbury at 7.1x, with more leverage and more segment drag than either. Fair value on both EBITDA and earnings approaches is $71–83, placing the current price at the upper end of a range that assumes the EBITDA base does not decline further than the mid-single digits already visible. That is not enough compensation for a 2027 refinancing negotiated at the wrong point in the rate cycle, a Level 3 intangible balance with minimal cushion, and a governance structure in which Class A holders own 62% of the economics and a small minority of the votes.
