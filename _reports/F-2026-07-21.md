**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ford is a legacy automaker in the middle of a strategic reset that produces GAAP results poorly correlated with underlying operating performance. FY2025 showed an $8.2B GAAP net loss, driven almost entirely by $17.4B of pre-tax special items — roughly $10.7B of Model e EV asset impairments and a ~$3.2B charge tied to unwinding the BlueOval SK battery JV. Stripping those out, adjusted pre-tax earnings were +$5.5B. Q1 2026 then delivered an operational turnaround: revenue $43.3B (+6% YoY), GAAP net income $2.55B, adjusted EBIT $3.49B at an 8.1% margin (up from 2.5% a year earlier). Headline loss figures and standard bankruptcy/leverage screens mischaracterize the business, because they consolidate Ford Credit's ~$137.5B self-liquidating finance debt with a net-cash-positive, investment-grade industrial company.

The value is concentrated. Ford Pro (commercial/fleet plus recurring software subscriptions) posted an 11.4% Q1'26 margin with software subscribers up ~30% YoY, and is guided to $6.5-7.5B EBIT for 2026, deserving a higher multiple than the consolidated auto business implies. Ford Blue (ICE/hybrid) is the profit workhorse. Against these, Ford Model e is structurally loss-making (Q1'26 EBIT –$777M, a –63% margin) and is guided to lose $4.0-4.5B in 2026, a narrowing from prior expectations.

The balance of considerations leaves us at HOLD. The operating recovery is real and the dividend (~4.2% yield) is supported. But the risk/reward is symmetric: ~$4B of EV-cancellation charges and a ~$3.5B BOSK charge are still to flow through 2026, the Q1 beat was partly inflated by a one-time ~$1.3B IEEPA tariff benefit that masks an ongoing ~$1.0B tariff drag, a ~$2.8B tariff receivable of uncertain collectibility sits on the balance sheet, and commodity/supply-chain headwinds (Novelis aluminum, >$2.0B commodity drag) are quantified and material. At ~$14, near the middle of the $10.68-$17.78 52-week range, there is no clear mispricing to underwrite. We prefer to see confirmation that adjusted EBIT tracks into the $8.5-10.5B guide and that Model e losses actually narrow before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Ford is one of the world's largest automakers, reorganized around four customer-centric reportable segments rather than geography:

- **Ford Blue** — ICE and hybrid Ford/Lincoln retail vehicles, including China JV equity income (Changan Ford, JMC). Q1'26 EBIT $1.94B at an 8.1% margin. The profit workhorse.
- **Ford Pro** — commercial/government/fleet vehicles (Super Duty, Transit, Ranger EU) plus telematics, EV charging, and paid software subscriptions. Q1'26 EBIT $1.69B at an 11.4% margin, subscribers +30% YoY. FY2026 guide $6.5-7.5B EBIT. The highest-quality franchise.
- **Ford Model e** — EVs and software/digital vehicle technologies. Q1'26 EBIT –$777M (–63% margin). FY2026 guided loss of $4.0-4.5B (narrowing from prior ~$4.8B).
- **Ford Credit** — captive finance arm. Q1'26 EBT $783M, ROE 18.2%. Self-funding via its own ~$137.5B debt balance sheet matched by finance receivables, separate from industrial Ford.

Ford's durable competitive advantages sit in trucks/utilities and commercial vehicles — F-Series, Super Duty, Transit — where brand loyalty and dealer/fleet service networks are genuine moats and margins are structurally higher. The Ford Pro software attach model is an emerging recurring-revenue stream the market is beginning to value on its own.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 GAAP net loss of –$8.2B** was an accounting and strategic reset, not operational collapse. The $17.4B of pre-tax special items comprised ~$10.7B of Model e impairments and a ~$3.2B BOSK JV disposition charge. Adjusted pre-tax earnings were +$5.5B and adjusted EBIT was positive.

**Q1 2026 confirmed the operating recovery.** Revenue $43.3B (+6% YoY); GAAP net income $2.55B versus $471M a year earlier; diluted EPS $0.63; net margin 5.9%; adjusted EBIT $3.49B at an 8.1% margin versus 2.5% prior year; trailing-4Q adjusted ROIC 12.6%. The earnings surprise history reinforces the volatility of visibility: the March quarter beat consensus by 245% ($0.66 vs $0.19 estimate), but the prior December quarter missed by 32%. This is a business where quarterly outcomes swing on commodity, tariff, and mix factors that are difficult to forecast.

**FY2026 guidance:** adjusted EBIT $8.5-10.5B; adjusted FCF $5.0-6.0B. Segment guide: Ford Pro $6.5-7.5B, Ford Blue $4.5-5.0B, Model e loss $4.0-4.5B, Ford Credit ~$2.5B. Capex guided to $9.5-10.5B.

**Balance sheet — industrial Ford ("Company excluding Ford Credit"):** company cash $22.0B, liquidity $43.1B at 3/31/26; industrial debt ~$18.7B; net cash of ~$3.3B (down from $7.7B at YE25, reflecting repayment of the $2.3B convertible in cash plus seasonally negative Q1 FCF). Ford targets ≥$20B cash. Investment-grade at Fitch/DBRS/S&P (BBB–/BBB(low)); Moody's Ba1, one notch below IG, with S&P on negative outlook. The consolidated Altman Z-Score and debt/equity ratios are distorted because Ford Credit's finance debt is self-liquidating and matched by receivables; a low consolidated Z-Score should not be read as distress.

**Cash flow quality:** Q1'26 adjusted FCF was –$1.9B (seasonal; Q1 is typically negative on working capital). Consolidated operating cash flow was $1.3B. Ford Credit US loss-to-receivables ticked up to 72 bps from 63 bps on higher repossessions — low, but worth monitoring as consumer credit normalizes.

On the calibration principle that financial health is the most reliable discriminator, Ford earns a middling score: the industrial balance sheet is genuinely resilient and IG-rated, but net industrial cash has eroded, the dividend is prioritized over supplemental returns, and the >$2.0B commodity headwind plus $1.5-2.0B of temporary Novelis-related costs pressure 2026 cash generation.

## Growth Analysis

Ford's top line grew 6% YoY in Q1'26, and the growth thesis is mix-driven rather than volume-driven. The identifiable growth levers:

- **Ford Pro software/services** — recurring, high-margin, subscribers +30% YoY, and the segment posting 11.4% margins. This is the most credible structural growth story and is being valued increasingly on its own merits.
- **Universal EV Platform / ~$30K electric truck (2027)** — Ford's own next-gen, lower-cost architecture (California skunkworks), backed by ~$1.9B to convert Louisville Assembly. Engineered for parts –20% and workstation –40%. Genuine IP, but the payoff is 2027+ and depends on execution against a demand environment stripped of the $7,500 federal EV credit.
- **Ford Energy (launched May 2026)** — a wholly-owned subsidiary making U.S.-assembled grid-scale battery storage (BESS) for utilities/data centers using CATL-licensed LFP tech, repurposing stranded EV battery capacity (~20 GWh/yr target, Kentucky). First customer EDF Power Solutions on a five-year deal. This monetizes a stranded liability into the data-center power theme, but it is early-stage and its magnitude is unproven.

Against these, the near-term growth picture is constrained. Estimate revisions are mixed: current-year EPS estimates rose +8.3% over 90 days (to $1.64), but next-quarter estimates were cut –24.0% and next-year revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative. This does not meet the threshold of a durable earnings-inflection signal — the strong +8.3% current-year revision is offset by material near-term downgrades, and next-year growth is essentially flat. Model e will lose $4.0-4.5B this year and the ~$5.9B of raw-material offtake commitments through 2035 place demand risk on Ford if EV volumes stay depressed. Growth is real in Pro, uncertain-to-negative elsewhere, and the overall trend does not clear the bar for an accelerating-growth call. This dimension has decayed across prior reports (5.5 → 4.3), and the current data confirms only partial stabilization rather than reversal.

## Valuation Assessment

Ford trades at ~$14.26, a market cap of ~$55.8B, and a ~4.2% dividend yield. The trailing/forward P/E is not meaningful (negative on the GAAP loss). On adjusted numbers, consensus current-year EPS of $1.64 implies ~8.7x forward earnings; next-year $1.83 implies ~7.8x.

Peer context is heterogeneous and not directly comparable: GM trades at ~31.7x (though on temporarily depressed earnings), Tesla at ~366x (a different business model), Winnebago at ~19x. The peer median P/E of 31.7 is a poor anchor. A cleaner frame is that Ford is a low-multiple legacy OEM whose adjusted earnings power supports the current price but offers limited re-rating without a sustained Model e turnaround or a discrete revaluation of Ford Pro as a software-adjacent business.

The calibration evidence is directly relevant here: a low headline multiple is anti-predictive and functions as a value-trap warning absent a specific catalyst. Ford's cheapness on adjusted earnings is not, on its own, a buy signal — it reflects structural EV losses, tariff overhang, and a warranty/quality burden that the market is discounting. There is no clean mispricing to underwrite. The identifiable upside catalyst (Pro re-rating, Model e narrowing, Ford Energy scaling) is real but slow and unconfirmed. The stock sits near the middle of its 52-week range after a ~24% rise off the July 2025 lows, and analyst consensus (mean $15.05, median $14.25) implies negligible upside from spot. Given the stretched-multiple guardrail does not apply (Ford is cheap, not premium), consensus targets could lag if Pro or Ford Energy surprise, but the absence of a strong revision signal keeps us from treating targets as floors. Symmetric risk/reward warrants a below-neutral valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Ford's competitive position is mixed by segment. In trucks and commercial vehicles it is strong and defensible: F-Series and Super Duty carry pricing power and brand loyalty, and Ford Pro's fleet-service and software attach model builds switching costs that pure hardware OEMs lack. This is where Ford's moat is widening.

In EVs the position is weaker and the trajectory has been negative — Ford has written down EV investments twice, cancelled three planned vehicles, ended F-150 Lightning production, and is unwinding the BOSK JV. GM is viewed by the market as further along on EV cost structure; Chinese EV makers and China's rare-earth export leverage are structural threats repeatedly cited in the filings. The offsetting point: the entire legacy-OEM EV reset is industry-wide, and Ford's ~$8B loss is smaller than Stellantis's ~$26.3B, with a healthier underlying ICE/commercial franchise. The elimination of the $7,500 federal EV credit and curtailed state ZEV authority removed the demand-side support that underpinned Ford's original EV thesis, which is the root cause of the write-downs and a headwind that persists.

Net trajectory is roughly stable — a strong and improving commercial/truck franchise offset by an eroding, loss-making EV position. That balance keeps the competitive score at neutral. Consistent with the calibration caveat that competitive position is a low-signal dimension recently, we do not lean on it heavily.

## Risk Assessment

Ford carries several specific, quantified risks that are only partly priced:

1. **EV stranded-asset / additional charges.** Up to ~$4B of pre-tax EV-cancellation charges (incl. ~$500M cash) and a ~$3.5B BOSK JVDA charge (closing ~Q2'26) still to flow through 2026, keeping GAAP results noisy. The ~$5.9B of raw-material offtake commitments through 2035 is a genuine tail liability if EV volumes stay low.
2. **Tariffs and trade policy.** Ford now leads its risk factors with policy uncertainty. 2026 net tariff impact guided ~$1.0B (Ford Blue ~$700M, Ford Pro ~$500M), excluding the one-time IEEPA benefit that inflated Q1'26 EBIT. A ~$2.8B tariff-reimbursement receivable sits on the balance sheet with collectibility tied to Court of International Trade / Supreme Court rulings — a real asset-recovery risk. The quality of the Q1 beat is partly one-time.
3. **Supply-chain fragility.** Novelis aluminum plant fires (Sept & Nov 2025) disrupted Super Duty production; 2026 guidance embeds $1.5-2.0B of temporary costs. China rare-earth export restrictions are flagged as an active production risk.
4. **Warranty / quality.** Warranty reserve at ~$17B (up from ~$14B). Ford operates under a 2024 NHTSA consent order with a third-party monitor. Takata/ARC/Delphi airbag inflator populations remain unresolved contingent exposures.
5. **Commodity headwinds.** >$2.0B commodity drag assumed for 2026, ~$1B higher than prior estimate, largely aluminum.
6. **Concentration in large trucks/utilities.** Any consumer shift or plant disruption is disproportionately damaging.

These are consumer-discretionary sector risks where calibration counsels demanding company-specific outperformance before rewarding a thesis. The risks are largely known but their timing and magnitude (tariff receivable collection, additional charges) create binary elements that are not fully priced. That justifies a below-neutral risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 40.3% versus 1-year historical vol of 36.7% — modestly rich (spread +3.7%), sitting at only the 11th percentile within the current chain, so options are not expensively priced in absolute terms. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 40.3% > long-term 36.5%), and put/call skew is a mildly elevated 3.3%, consistent with the market pricing some imminent event stress and modest downside hedging. IV has fallen sharply since mid-June (51.3% → 40.3%, –11pp) while the price is roughly flat, suggesting the market has grown less concerned about near-term dislocation. The one flagged unusual print — 2,000 January 2027 $10 puts (–30% OTM) at 4.4x open interest — signals some tail-hedging but is small in the context of a $56B market cap. On balance the options complex is neither strongly confirming nor contradicting a bearish view; it reflects normal event uncertainty ahead of earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (→ $18-20).** Ford Pro compounds its software/services attach at 11%+ margins and the market begins valuing it as a higher-multiple recurring-revenue business rather than as commodity auto EBIT. Model e losses narrow faster than guide as EV cancellations reduce cash burn, and the 2027 ~$30K Universal EV Platform truck reframes Ford's EV cost structure. Ford Energy scales into data-center/utility BESS demand, monetizing stranded battery capacity. Tariff receivables are collected, commodity headwinds ease, and adjusted EBIT lands at the top of guidance with $5-6B adjusted FCF supporting the dividend. This maps to the high analyst target of $20.

**Bear Case (→ $11).** Additional EV-cancellation and BOSK charges keep GAAP results deeply negative and pressure the balance sheet; the tariff receivable is impaired by adverse court rulings; commodity and Novelis costs exceed the embedded assumption; Model e losses fail to narrow; and a consumer slowdown (weighing on discretionary demand, plus rising Ford Credit repossessions) hits truck/utility volumes disproportionately. Adjusted EBIT undershoots guidance, S&P's negative outlook converts to a downgrade, and the dividend faces scrutiny. This maps to the low analyst target of $11.

**Base Case (→ $14-15).** The operating recovery holds, adjusted EBIT lands mid-guide (~$9-10B), Ford Pro remains the value driver, Model e loss narrows modestly, and charges flow through as expected. The stock trades in line with adjusted earnings power at ~8x, with the dividend providing the bulk of the return. This is roughly the current price and consensus mean/median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, expected late July 2026) is the key near-term checkpoint — watch whether adjusted EBIT tracks into the annual guide and whether the BOSK charge closes as expected. The 2027 Universal EV Platform truck launch and Ford Energy contract wins are the longer-horizon catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (→ BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of Model e EBIT loss narrowing below the $4.0-4.5B annualized run-rate, confirming the EV reset is reducing cash burn.
- Ford Pro EBIT margin sustained at ≥11% with continued double-digit software-subscriber growth, supporting a sum-of-the-parts re-rating.
- Resolution of the tariff receivable favorably, or clear evidence commodity/Novelis headwinds are rolling off ahead of the embedded cost assumption.

**Downgrade triggers (→ AVOID):**
- Adjusted EBIT guidance cut below $8.5B, or adjusted FCF guidance below $5.0B.
- Ford Credit US loss-to-receivables rising above ~90 bps, signaling consumer credit stress feeding into the captive finance book.
- Additional EV/BOSK charges exceeding the ~$7.5B combined already flagged, or an S&P/Fitch downgrade below investment grade.
- Dividend cut, which would signal the ≥$20B cash target is under pressure.

## Conclusion

Ford is a fundamentally sounder business than its FY2025 GAAP loss suggests — the industrial company is net-cash-positive and investment grade, Ford Pro is a genuinely attractive high-margin franchise, and Q1'26 delivered a real operational beat. But the investment case does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a BUY. The EV reset remains a multi-quarter drag with ~$7.5B of charges still to flow through, the Q1 beat was partly a one-time tariff benefit masking an ongoing tariff drag and a receivable of uncertain collectibility, commodity and supply-chain headwinds are quantified and material, and the growth signal is mixed. At ~$14, near the middle of its 52-week range and roughly at consensus, the stock is fairly valued on adjusted earnings power with the ~4.2% dividend providing the return floor. The management picture is mildly mixed — a modest open-market director purchase (Thornton, ~$149K at $14.05) is a small positive, but the $49.2M "comp actually paid" in an $8.2B-loss year and the cautious dividend posture temper enthusiasm.

We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD on Ford, and the current data confirms the pattern rather than breaking it: financial health has improved as the balance-sheet resilience becomes clearer, but growth and valuation remain constrained. We would upgrade on confirmed Model e loss narrowing and a Ford Pro re-rating, and downgrade on a guidance cut, credit-metric deterioration, or dividend risk.
