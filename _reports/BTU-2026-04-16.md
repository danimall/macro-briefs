## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy, the world's largest private-sector coal producer, posted a net loss of $53M in FY2025, a $457M deterioration from FY2024's $404M profit. Adjusted EBITDA declined 48% to $455M as seaborne coal prices collapsed and the failed Anglo American acquisition consumed $79M in termination charges. Free cash flow turned negative ($-78M) for the first time this cycle, and the company's key growth asset, the Centurion underground met coal mine in Queensland, immediately encountered commissioning failures upon start-up, with Q1 2026 sales guidance slashed from ~700K tons to ~250K tons within weeks of longwall commencement.

The stock has risen 126% over the past year, driven by geopolitical energy tailwinds (Iran blockade, dual supply compression, Japanese coal plant operating rate increases) and a pro-coal U.S. regulatory environment. At $28.03, BTU trades at roughly 10.3x FY2026E consensus EPS of $2.73 and 5.4x trailing Adj. EBITDA. Analyst consensus targets range from $33.50 to $42.00, implying 20-50% upside. However, the deteriorating operational fundamentals, near-term execution risk on Centurion, the December 2026 surety bond renewal, the March 2028 convertible note maturity, and CFO insider selling paint a risk profile that is incompatible with confident capital commitment. The 16.3% short interest suggests institutional investors share these concerns.

The central question is whether the geopolitical tailwind to coal pricing persists long enough and is large enough to overcome the margin collapse evident in FY2025 results. Current consensus estimates embed a recovery to $2.73 EPS in FY2026, requiring substantial improvement from Centurion, seaborne pricing, and cost management. Given the commissioning failures already reported, this estimate carries high execution risk.

## Company Overview

Peabody Energy operates 16 active coal mines across the United States and Australia, organized into four reporting segments:

- **Seaborne Thermal** (Wilpinjong Mine, NSW): 10.5M tons in FY2025, low-sulfur thermal coal for Asian export markets.
- **Seaborne Metallurgical** (Queensland, NSW, Alabama): Hard coking coal, semi-hard, and PCI coal. Key assets include Coppabella/Moorvale, Shoal Creek, Metropolitan, and the newly-commissioned Centurion mine.
- **Powder River Basin** (Wyoming): Three massive surface mines led by North Antelope Rochelle, producing 84.5M tons in FY2025, the largest coal mine in the U.S.
- **Other U.S. Thermal** (Indiana, Illinois, New Mexico, Colorado): Higher-sulfur, higher-Btu bituminous coal for domestic utilities.

The company controls approximately 2.0 billion tons of proven/probable reserves and maintains a backlog of ~238 million tons (up from 153M a year prior). Market cap is $3.3B. Shares outstanding: ~122M.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue declined 22% from the 2022 peak of $4.98B to $3.86B in FY2025. Operating income swung from $445M profit (FY2024) to an $80M loss. Adjusted EBITDA fell 48% YoY to $455M.

**Segment-level margin compression was severe:**

| Segment | EBITDA/Ton FY2024 | EBITDA/Ton FY2025 | Change |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Seaborne Thermal | $26.17 | $14.42 | -45% |
| Seaborne Met | $22.20 | $6.57 | -70% |
| Other U.S. Thermal | $10.34 | $5.33 | -49% |
| Powder River Basin | $1.74 | $2.08 | +20% |

The Seaborne Met segment generated only $56.4M in Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025, down from $242.5M (which included $80.8M in Shoal Creek insurance recovery). At $6.57/ton, this segment is barely above breakeven. Any further price decline pushes it into losses.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow declined to $333.7M (from $606.5M). Capital expenditures rose to $411.4M on Centurion development spending, producing negative free cash flow of $77.7M. Dividends consumed $36.5M, and share repurchases slowed dramatically ($183M less than prior year).

Balance sheet headline numbers appear adequate: $575.3M cash, $942.1M total liquidity, $336.4M total debt (primarily $320M in 3.25% Convertible Senior Notes due March 2028), and $3.54B stockholders' equity. Current ratio is 1.85x; debt-to-equity is 0.62x.

But the real liquidity picture is more constrained. Approximately $844M in cash is restricted or collateralized, supporting ~$1.05B in off-balance-sheet surety bonds, letters of credit, and bank guarantees for mine reclamation obligations. The surety bond agreement requires minimum liquidity of the greater of $400M or the difference between the penal sum of bonds and posted collateral ($487.3M at 12/31/25). This effectively floors accessible cash.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.6 places Peabody in the "grey zone." EBIT/Assets turned negative at -0.014.

### Convertible Note Maturity Wall

The $320M convertible notes mature in March 2028. During Q4 2025, the conversion feature was triggered (stock price exceeded threshold). Management intends to settle conversions in shares. If the stock remains above conversion price, equity dilution is the risk. If it falls below, a $327M cash payment in 2028 on top of ongoing capital requirements could materially pressure liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

### Centurion: The Bull Case and Its Immediate Failure

The Centurion mine, an underground longwall met coal mine in Queensland idle for 8 years, commenced full-scale longwall production in February 2026. This asset was expected to add ~3.5M tons of premium hard coking coal annually, materially improving the met segment's volume and quality mix. Premium hard coking coal commands $50-80/ton more than PCI coal.

Within weeks of commencement, Peabody slashed Q1 2026 sales guidance from ~700K tons to ~250K tons due to "greater-than-anticipated mine commissioning challenges." The stock dropped ~10%. Full-year guidance of ~3.5M tons was maintained, but this requires substantial backloading: delivering 3.25M tons over Q2-Q4, or ~1.08M tons/quarter versus the ~250K achieved in Q1. Underground longwall mines that stumble at commissioning frequently experience recurring geological and mechanical problems.

### Near-Term Tailwinds

Several factors provide short-term support:
- **Rising U.S. electricity demand** from data centers/AI is increasing coal consumption.
- **Japan increased coal plant operating rates** for energy security.
- **EPA rescinded GHG endangerment finding** and repealed emission standards for fossil fuel plants, deferring coal plant retirements.
- **Federal royalty rate reduction** (~$19M annual benefit).
- **Met coal designated as critical mineral**, eligible for Section 45X tax credit (~$5M annual).
- **U.S. coal inventories declined 20M tons below year-ago levels**, supporting pricing.
- **Rare earth element initiative** — early-stage pilot at Powder River Basin ($6.25M Wyoming grant).

### Estimate Trajectory

Current quarter EPS estimate: $0.10 (down 64.4% in 30 days, down 58.7% in 90 days). This collapse in near-term estimates directly reflects the Centurion commissioning failure. Next quarter ($0.79) and FY2026 ($2.73) estimates are relatively stable. FY2027 estimates have been revised up 17.5% over 90 days to $4.18, reflecting expectations that Centurion will eventually ramp and coal pricing will benefit from geopolitical dynamics.

The divergence between collapsing near-term estimates and rising out-year estimates is the core tension in this name. If Centurion ramps successfully and seaborne pricing holds, the path to $4+ EPS is plausible. If Centurion continues to underperform, or if geopolitical conditions normalize and coal prices decline, the company remains stuck near breakeven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.03, BTU trades at:
- 10.3x FY2026E EPS ($2.73), though forward P/E is listed as negative (-64.8x) based on trailing losses
- ~6.2x FY2026E Adj. EBITDA (assuming ~$550M at consensus revenue)
- 6.7x FY2027E EPS ($4.18)
- ~0.8x book value ($3.54B equity / ~$3.3B market cap)

Relative to energy peers at $3B market cap, the peer median P/E is 19.0x and median yield is 1.2%. BTU's 1.1% dividend yield is roughly in line, but the negative trailing P/E places it in a different category: a turnaround/recovery story rather than a stable earnings generator. Par Pacific (4.9x) and International Seaways (7.8x) trade at lower multiples, suggesting the market is already embedding meaningful recovery expectations into BTU.

Analyst targets range from $33.50 to $42.00, with a consensus mean of $39.08. Five of six covering analysts rate it BUY. This consensus appears anchored to the Centurion ramp thesis and geopolitical coal pricing support.

The valuation is not obviously expensive on out-year numbers. But the distance from current earnings power (negative) to the estimates requires multiple assumptions to hold simultaneously: Centurion ramps to plan, seaborne prices stabilize or recover, the surety bond agreement is renewed, and no adverse resolution of the Anglo American arbitration.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody's competitive position has two distinct components:

**PRB thermal:** North Antelope Rochelle's scale provides a structural cost advantage. The segment generates thin but consistent margins ($2.08/ton EBITDA) and benefits from the federal royalty rate reduction. U.S. domestic coal demand is experiencing a rare near-term uptick.

**Seaborne:** Peabody competes against BHP, Glencore, Whitehaven, and Yancoal in Australian export markets. Without Centurion operating at scale, the met coal portfolio lacks premium product and is margin-challenged. Take-or-pay infrastructure obligations (~$1.0B) lock Peabody into continued Australian production even during unfavorable pricing.

The coal industry broadly faces ESG-driven divestment and financing restrictions. Since 2010, U.S. coal power capacity has fallen ~46%. Renewables now generate 19% of U.S. electricity vs. coal's 16%. The structural trajectory is against Peabody, though the current geopolitical environment and AI-driven electricity demand create a cyclical offset.

## Risk Assessment

### Operational Risks
1. **Centurion commissioning failure**: Already materialized. Recovery timeline uncertain for underground longwall operations.
2. **Surety bond renewal (Dec 2026)**: Failure to renew would threaten mining permits. Some banks/insurers have reduced support for thermal coal companies.
3. **Anglo American arbitration**: Damages from the $3.6B+ terminated acquisition are unquantified. ICC proceedings ongoing.

### Financial Risks
4. **Convertible note maturity (March 2028)**: $327M cash obligation if stock declines below conversion price.
5. **Negative FCF trajectory**: 2026 capex guided at ~$340M (declining from $411M) helps, but earnings must recover.
6. **Restricted cash**: $844M tied up in reclamation collateral limits actual available liquidity.

### Structural/Regulatory Risks
7. **Coal demand secular decline**: 46% U.S. coal capacity retired since 2010.
8. **Climate Superfund laws**: New York and Vermont statutes could impose historical emissions liabilities.
9. **Australian royalties**: Queensland progressive tiers up to 40% at elevated prices.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 73.7% vs. 58.5% historical volatility (a 15.2pp rich premium) confirms the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (73.7% near-term vs. 59.0% at 6 months), indicating the market expects near-term stress relative to longer-term conditions. The 1-month put/call volume ratio of 3.65 shows aggressive put buying. OI P/C of 1.12 is mildly bearish. Combined with 16.3% short interest and 3.5 days to cover, institutional positioning is meaningfully bearish.

### Insider Activity

CFO Mark Spurbeck sold 30,000 shares on March 4, 2026 at $35.58 (~$1.07M), reducing his position by approximately 31%. This was an open-market voluntary sale, not a tax withholding. No open-market purchases by any insiders were detected. The timing — after the annual filing but before the Centurion shortfall announcement — adds a negative informational signal.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: Centurion mine production reporting in Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August). If Q2 production remains materially below the ~1.0M+ tons/quarter needed to reach full-year guidance, the stock would face a second downward revision and the full-year $2.73 EPS estimate becomes untenable. The surety bond renewal process (H2 2026) could also surface adverse terms that constrain capital returns and signal institutional reluctance to support coal.

**Timing window**: Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) for Centurion production data; H2 2026 for surety bond renewal clarity. The catalyst is 3-4 months out with defined reporting dates.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: SI at 16.3% of float with 3.5 days to cover is elevated but below the 20% threshold where squeeze risk becomes severe. Institutional ownership is moderate. The stock has already rallied 126% over 12 months, so there is momentum risk. However, the recent decline from the $41 high to $28 has likely shaken out some momentum longs.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options positioning confirms bearish sentiment (backwardated IV, elevated put/call ratios). Borrow should be available given the $3.3B market cap, though cost-to-borrow may be elevated given the short interest level.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear case target of $15-18 represents 36-46% downside from $28. Upside risk to $35-38 on a Centurion recovery represents 25-36% upside. Risk/reward is approximately 1.3:1 to 1.5:1 based on midpoints, below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The fundamental thesis is sound and the timing catalyst is identifiable, but the risk/reward is insufficient for an active short. The stock has already corrected 32% from its 52-week high, meaning much of the near-term negative news is partially discounted. The 16.3% short interest creates meaningful squeeze risk, and geopolitical coal tailwinds provide upside catalysts that are difficult to predict. The correct expression is to avoid owning the name rather than actively shorting it.

## Conclusion

Peabody Energy is a secularly declining business experiencing a cyclical/geopolitical reprieve. The FY2025 financials reveal the underlying fragility: seaborne margins collapsed 45-70%, FCF turned negative, and the company posted a net loss. The Centurion mine immediately encountered commissioning failures, and the CFO sold ~31% of his stake at materially higher prices. The surety bond agreement renewal in December 2026 carries existential risk with no visibility. Institutional positioning (bearish options skew, backwardated IV curve) reflects these concerns.

The bull case requires simultaneous success across Centurion ramp, sustained elevated coal pricing, successful surety renewal, and benign arbitration resolution. The probability of all four holding is materially below the probability of at least one failing. At 10.3x uncertain FY2026 earnings with an elevated risk profile, the stock does not offer adequate compensation. AVOID.
