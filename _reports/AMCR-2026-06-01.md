**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amcor became the global leader in consumer packaging on April 30, 2025, when it closed an all-stock merger with Berry Global. The combination created a ~$23B pro forma sales base across 400+ facilities in 40+ countries, but it also produced a balance sheet carrying $14.3B of net debt and $18.6B of goodwill plus intangibles sitting above an $11.7B equity base. Nearly every reported line item is distorted by purchase accounting: GAAP net income fell 30% in FY25 on $307M of restructuring/transaction charges and step-up amortization, while 9M FY26 revenue surged 72% almost entirely because Berry is now in the comparison base. The standalone business is not growing; organic volumes are down roughly 2-3% on soft consumer demand, customer destocking, and tariff uncertainty.

The investment case is execution-dependent rather than organic. Management raised its FY26 synergy target to $270M (toward a ~$650M end-FY28 program) at the May 6 Q3 print, beat on revenue, raised full-year guidance, and increased the dividend. It simultaneously cut FY26 free-cash-flow guidance to $1.5-1.6B from $1.8-1.9B. Over the first nine months of FY26, the company paid $894M in dividends against only $556M of operating cash flow, a gap funded partly by debt, with a $1.1B working-capital outflow doing the damage. Management states explicitly that protecting the investment-grade rating could require a dividend cut, asset sales, or reduced capex. The dividend that anchors Amcor's investor appeal is therefore a watch item even though it was just raised.

We rate AMCR a HOLD on an income basis. At $38.82 the stock yields ~6.7% and trades at ~24.3x trailing / ~9.8x forward EPS of $3.98, with the new CFO having made a $1M open-market purchase in December 2025, the strongest form of insider signal and a genuine contrarian-positive. That yield and the synergy-delivery optionality are enough to hold, but the cut FCF guide, the dividend-versus-cash-flow gap, the impairment exposure on intangibles, and a Materials-sector backdrop with negative organic volumes cap the upside. We hold for the coupon with downside protection eroding, and would not buy the name for appreciation.

## Company Overview

Amcor is incorporated in Jersey, Channel Islands, listed on the NYSE and ASX, and plans to relocate select corporate functions to a new Miami headquarters in 2027. Following the Berry merger it employs ~77,000 people and operates two reportable segments:

- **Global Flexible Packaging Solutions** (~$10.9B FY25 sales, ~13.4% adjusted EBIT margin): flexible and film packaging for food, beverage, medical/pharma, and personal care. This is the higher-margin, more defensible franchise, anchored by Amcor's legacy strength and healthcare exposure.
- **Global Rigid Packaging Solutions** (~$4.1B FY25 sales, ~9.1% margin rising to ~10.6% in 9M FY26): rigid containers and closures, lower-margin and more commoditized, where most acquired Berry rigid/beverage assets and the divestiture candidates sit. LatAm flexibles were moved into Rigid as of January 2026.

No single customer is more than 10% of sales, though concentration exists within specific business lines. The company also undertook two structural changes that complicate analysis: a 1-for-5 reverse stock split effective January 14, 2026 (the current $38.82 reflects this; all pre-split per-share data must be divided by five), and a fiscal year-end change from June 30 to December 31 effective 2026, which will produce a stub transition period (July-December 2026) before the first full calendar year in 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**The merger dominates the optics.** Total assets jumped from $16.5B in FY24 to $37.1B in FY25; goodwill rose from $5.3B to $11.3B (now ~$12.0B); revenue rose from $13.6B to $15.0B with only ~2 months of Berry included.

FY2025 (year ended June 30, 2025):
- Revenue $15.0B (+10%, with +12pts from Berry offset by negative organic price/mix)
- Net income $511M, down 30% YoY; GAAP diluted EPS $0.32 pre-split
- Operating margin compressed to 6.7% from 8.9%, driven by $307M of restructuring/transaction/integration expense, $133M inventory step-up amortization, and $246M acquired-intangible amortization
- Adjusted EBIT $1,723M; adjusted net income $1,136M

9M FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026), the first near-full-year combined view:
- Revenue $17.1B (+72%); net income $717M (+30%); GAAP diluted EPS $1.55 post-split
- Adjusted EBIT $1,977M; adjusted net income $1,293M
- Q3 FY26: revenue $5.91B (+77%), net income $278M (+42%), adjusted EPS $0.96 (+6% YoY), gross margin 20.1%, adjusted EBITDA ~$892M (+87%)

**The organic story is the crux.** Strip out Berry, FX, and raw-material pass-through, and underlying volumes were down ~2-3% through 9M FY26 (down ~2% in Q3). Essentially all reported growth is acquired. The base business is not expanding, so the merger thesis has to carry the value creation.

**Cash flow quality is the most important concern.** 9M FY26 operating cash flow was $556M against $717M net income, dragged by a $1.1B working-capital outflow. The company paid $894M in dividends over the same nine months, so dividends exceeded operating cash flow, with the difference funded partly by debt. The mechanism that matters: if working capital does not normalize and synergies do not convert to cash, the company is funding part of its dividend with borrowing on a balance sheet that is already levered.

**Balance sheet is the central risk.** Net debt is $14.3B as of March 2026, up from $13.3B at June 2025 and ~$6.1B pre-merger. Total debt is $15.85B with ~17% variable-rate. Goodwill ($12.0B) plus intangibles ($6.7B) total $18.6B, exceeding total equity of $11.7B, which creates high impairment vulnerability. The bank covenant leverage cap is 3.9x (stepping to 4.25x post-acquisition); the company reports compliance and remains investment-grade rated by all three agencies. Liability management has been active: €1.5B issued in November 2025 and $1.5B in March 2026, with $1.525B, $750M, and $600M of (mostly Berry legacy secured) notes redeemed in 2026.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.18 ("distress zone") is an artifact of purchase accounting: goodwill and intangibles inflate the asset base while equity-to-assets and EBIT-to-assets ratios are depressed by merger charges and partial-period earnings. The right lens is deleveraging trajectory and synergy delivery, not solvency. The genuine financial-health concern is leverage and refinancing in a higher-rate environment combined with a dividend that currently outruns cash generation, which is why we score Financial Health 4.5 rather than treating the screening flag as a false alarm and moving on.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth to underwrite at present. Reported revenue growth is acquired, and underlying volumes are negative. The forward growth case rests on three levers, all execution-dependent:

1. **Synergies.** The Berry Plan targets ~$530M cost + $60M financial + $60M growth = ~$650M pre-tax by end of FY2028. The raised FY26 target of $270M is a credible interim milestone that supported the Q3 beat and the ~4% stock reaction.
2. **Portfolio simplification.** An August 2025 strategic review identified ~$2.5B of non-core sales, explicitly including North American Beverage. Five businesses (~$500M revenue) were classified held-for-sale in Q3 FY26 (two sold, three under agreement post-quarter), and the ePac equity stake was sold for ~$79M. Divesting lower-margin commoditized assets should lift mix over time but shrinks the top line.
3. **Mix shift.** Rigid segment margin improved to ~10.6% in 9M FY26 from ~9.1%, and the flexible franchise carries higher-margin healthcare/pharma exposure that is structurally defensive.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than the powerful upward inflection that would signal a buy. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +2.7% over 30 days but are down -1.5% over 90 days; current-year is roughly flat (+0.8% / -0.2%); next-year estimates have been cut (-2.0% / -3.0%). This is stabilization, not acceleration. The earnings track record reinforces caution: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, including a -9.6% miss in June 2025 and small misses in the two most recent quarters. Consensus FY26 EPS of $3.98 implies modest growth, and FY27 of $4.29 embeds synergy delivery that has not yet been proven in cash.

Tariff disruption and Q3 FY26 Middle East energy-cost spikes pressure both demand and input costs. Packaging volumes track consumer staples and discretionary consumption, both of which are soft. We score Growth Prospects 5.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.82 the stock trades at ~24.3x trailing EPS of ~$1.60 and ~9.8x forward EPS of $3.98. The wide gap between trailing and forward reflects that trailing earnings are depressed by merger charges while forward consensus captures a fuller combined-entity earnings base; this is the correct lens for a company digesting a transformational deal, and on forward earnings the multiple is undemanding.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Paper & Plastic Packaging peer median P/E is 26.6 (PKG 26.6, SW 57.2, AVY 17.9, IP negative). Amcor's ~9.8x forward multiple is below the cohort, but the discount is warranted by its leverage (net debt versus much cleaner balance sheets at PKG and AVY), its negative organic volumes, and its integration risk. The dividend yield of ~6.7% is well above the peer median of 4.4% and provides absolute yield support, the strongest single element of the valuation case, but a yield that high partly reflects market skepticism about its durability given the FCF-versus-dividend gap.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $41, mean $48.21, median $48, high $60, with Macquarie at $72 on synergy gains and cost control. The mean implies ~24% upside. We treat these as informative but not as floors here: this is not an inflection stock with rising estimates and a cheap-versus-growth setup, so the "targets lag reality" dynamic does not apply. Next-year estimates are being cut, which means the target distribution is more likely to drift lower than higher if FCF and synergy conversion disappoint.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced rather than favorable. The forward multiple is cheap if synergies convert to cash and the dividend holds; it is fairly valued if working capital stays a drag and the dividend is cut to protect the rating. We score Valuation 5.5: the absolute yield and below-peer forward multiple offer support, but there is no specific, identifiable mispricing the market is missing, which is the bar for a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

Amcor competes in a fragmented, competitive global packaging market on price, innovation, sustainability, service, and quality. The Berry merger is a scale-and-cost play: it creates the global consumer-packaging leader with greater procurement leverage and a broader flexible-plus-rigid portfolio across North America, LatAm, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. The synergy program is the primary value-creation lever precisely because organic growth is anemic.

Trajectory matters more than level for this dimension. Amcor's scale position is improving on paper, but its competitive position is being tested by negative volumes, customer consolidation that pressures pricing, and secular plastic-reduction regulation (EPR, PFAS restrictions, plastic bans, customer sustainability mandates). Offsetting tailwinds include healthcare/pharma packaging, which is defensive and higher-margin, and rising post-consumer-recycled-content demand. The pay-versus-performance disclosure is telling: Amcor's cumulative TSR (~111) has materially lagged its peer group (~172), evidence that the market has rewarded competitors more over the relevant period. We score Competitive Position 5.5: enhanced scale is real, but it has not yet translated into share gains or relative outperformance, and the secular regulatory headwind on plastics is genuine.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Integration execution on the largest deal in company history.** The 10-K dedicates an entire risk category to Berry integration. Purchase accounting remains preliminary as of March 2026, with an $880M upward goodwill revision and a $621M downward PP&E revision already recorded, large measurement-period swings that signal the fair-value picture is unsettled and raise the odds of further adjustments.
2. **Leverage and credit-rating pressure.** Management states explicitly that maintaining the investment-grade rating could force a dividend cut, asset sales, or reduced capex/working capital. With dividends exceeding operating cash flow, this is a live tension, not a theoretical one.
3. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $18.6B of goodwill plus intangibles sits above $11.7B of equity. Any deterioration in cost-of-capital or cash-flow assumptions could trigger a material non-cash charge.
4. **Weak organic demand and tariff disruption.** Soft consumer volumes, customer order volatility, shifting U.S. tariff policy, and Middle East energy-cost spikes pressure both ends of the P&L.
5. **Controls flag.** Out-of-period inventory adjustments tied to discrepancies in Asia operations over 13 quarters (~$15M+) led to closure of three Asian facilities. Deemed immaterial, but a quality-of-controls concern while Berry's controls are excluded from the SOX assessment for the first post-merger year.
6. **New 10-Q risk factor:** the portfolio strategic review itself carries disruption risk with no assurance of value-accretive outcomes.
7. **Structural/legal:** Jersey incorporation creates uncertainty around enforcing U.S. civil liabilities; Argentina hyperinflation (now <1% of assets); FX translation across EUR, BRL, CHF, CNY, GBP.

We score Risk Factors 4.5: the impairment exposure, the dividend-versus-cash-flow tension, and the controls flag are specific, material, and not fully priced into a stock yielding 6.7% on management's own raised-guidance optimism.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$48-60)
Synergies convert to cash on schedule and the working-capital outflow reverses, restoring FCF toward the original $1.8-1.9B guide. Deleveraging brings net debt/EBITDA back toward the low-3x covenant comfort zone, the IG rating is secured, and the dividend holds while the 6.7% yield re-rates lower as the market gains confidence, pulling the price toward the $48 median target. Portfolio simplification lifts mix as ~$2.5B of low-margin sales exit. The CFO's open-market purchase looks prescient. Upside to Macquarie's $72 requires a full synergy beat plus volume recovery.

### Bear Case (~$30-33)
Organic volumes stay negative, the working-capital drag persists, and FY26 FCF lands at or below the cut guide. With dividends running above operating cash flow, management cuts the dividend to protect the IG rating, removing the single largest pillar of the equity story and triggering a yield-driven repricing toward $30-33 (-15% to -23%). A goodwill/intangible impairment charge against the intangible base, or further negative measurement-period revisions, compounds the de-rating. Tariff and energy costs squeeze margins faster than synergies offset.

The base case sits between these: the dividend holds at a strained payout, synergies deliver but cash conversion lags, and the stock grinds in the high-$30s to low-$40s while paying its coupon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The 12-month thesis is yield-based, not appreciation-based. The ~6.7% dividend is currently covered on an earnings basis (FY26 EPS $3.98 versus ~$1.27 annualized dividend) even though it is not covered on a 9M FY26 free-cash-flow basis, and the synergy program plus the CFO's open-market purchase provide enough optionality to hold the coupon while integration plays out. This is a hold with bearish lean: the FCF-guidance cut and the dividend-versus-cash-flow gap erode the downside protection that justifies an income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26 free cash flow recovers toward $1.8B with the working-capital outflow reversing, demonstrating the dividend is self-funding.
- Synergy capture runs ahead of the $270M FY26 target with evidence of conversion to cash, AND organic volumes turn positive.
- Net debt/EBITDA falls meaningfully toward the low-3x range, de-risking the IG rating and the dividend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FCF guidance is cut again, or the dividend is cut/suspended to protect the IG rating.
- A material goodwill/intangible impairment charge is recorded, or further large negative purchase-accounting revisions emerge.
- Organic volumes deteriorate beyond -3%, or synergy delivery slips below plan, with no offsetting mix benefit.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (the fiscal-year-end change to December 31 creates a July-December 2026 stub transition period; watch the 10-K/T closely), with the FCF trajectory and synergy update as the key checkpoints.

## Conclusion

The merger created genuine scale and a credible ~$650M synergy program, and management is executing the early milestones: raised synergy target, raised guidance, portfolio cleanup, and a contrarian $1M CFO purchase. Against that, the standalone business is shrinking, dividends are outrunning cash generation, FCF guidance was just cut, and $18.6B of intangibles sits above an $11.7B equity base in an unfavorable rate environment. The 6.7% yield and below-peer forward multiple support the price; the cash-flow gap and impairment exposure cap it. We hold for the coupon with eyes on the FCF trajectory, and would exit on any sign the dividend is at risk or that synergies are failing to convert to cash. Overall score 5.0, HOLD on an income basis.
