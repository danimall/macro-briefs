**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ASML occupies the single most defensible chokepoint in the semiconductor supply chain. It is the world's only producer of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, the technology required to manufacture every leading-edge logic and memory chip underpinning the AI buildout. The business is performing at the top of its cycle: Q4 2025 net bookings reached a record €13.2 billion (including €7.4 billion of EUV orders, roughly double consensus), year-end backlog stood at €38.8 billion, Q1 2026 revenue of €8.8 billion beat expectations at a 53.0% gross margin, and management raised FY2026 net-sales guidance to €36–40 billion on AI-driven demand. Consensus estimates are rising across every horizon: current-year EPS +6.1% over 90 days, next-year +10.8%. By every measure of business quality, this is among the best-positioned companies in technology.

The constraint is price. At $1,929 the ADR has appreciated 153% over twelve months and 77% year-to-date, and trades at roughly 61x current-year and 46x next-year consensus EPS. The stock sits *above* the Wall Street consensus mean target of approximately $1,685 and near the median of $1,706, an unusual configuration in which the market price has outrun even the sell-side's bullish base case. At these multiples, beats are the baseline expectation and offer little incremental re-rating, while any disappointment carries asymmetric downside. This is the recurring vulnerability of premium-multiple stocks in correct themes: the demand story can be entirely right while the entry price still produces a poor forward return.

The specific near-term swing factor is China. Management deliberately set the FY2026 guidance band wide to absorb the uncertain outcome of ongoing export-control negotiations, while the CEO has stated he expects China net sales to "decline significantly" in 2026. The market has underwritten the bull case—AI demand more than offsetting China weakness—at a 46x+ forward multiple. We rate ASML a HOLD: a franchise worth owning, at a price that no longer offers asymmetric upside. We would add aggressively on a pullback toward the low-$1,400s or on resolution of the China overhang at the favorable end of the guidance band.

## Company Overview

ASML supplies photolithography systems that pattern the circuitry onto semiconductor wafers. Its franchise rests on a monopoly position in EUV lithography, the only technology capable of producing the most advanced logic (AI accelerators, leading-edge GPUs) and memory (HBM, advanced DRAM) nodes. Its customer base is the concentrated set of leading-edge chipmakers: TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, and Micron.

The product portfolio spans three economic layers. The core EUV franchise (NXE/NXT platforms) consists of scanners costing well over $150 million each, of which ASML is the sole global producer. The next-generation High-NA EUV platform (EXE) raises the lens numerical aperture from 0.33 to 0.55, enabling denser patterning that management states can "reduce the number of process steps from 100 to 10"; each system reportedly costs $350 million or more, with Intel as an early adopter and SK Hynix indicating adoption plans. Underneath sits Installed Base Management, a service and field-upgrade line tied to the large deployed fleet, providing a higher-margin, less cyclical recurring revenue stream that cushions equipment-order volatility.

Roadmap progress continues to widen the moat: ASML has demonstrated a 1,000-watt EUV source and lifted NXE:3800F throughput to 260 wafers/hour. The competitive barriers rest on decades of accumulated IP, an exclusive supply chain (Zeiss optics, the ASML-owned Cymer light sources), and capital and engineering requirements that no competitor has approached. Nikon and Canon compete only in older DUV/i-line segments.

A data note: ASML is a Dutch-domiciled foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K. Standard SEC EDGAR and XBRL parsers returned only share counts and filing metadata. Financial figures here are sourced from ASML's own press releases, investor relations, and financial press, and should be confirmed against the FY2025 20-F (filed February 25, 2026) and Q1 2026 6-K (filed April 15, 2026).

## Financial Analysis

ASML is operating at the top of its cycle with re-acceleration confirmed in hard order data. Q4 2025 net bookings of €13.2 billion came against consensus of roughly €6.85 billion, and year-end backlog of €38.8 billion (~$45 billion) provides forward-revenue visibility well into 2027.

Q1 2026 EPS was €7.15 (~$8.37), beating the $6.67 estimate by 7.1%, and the 53.0% gross margin landed at the high end of guidance. ASML's structural gross margins run in the low-to-mid 50s%, among the best in capital equipment, reflecting the pricing power of a sole-source supplier. The earnings track record over the last four quarters is mixed—two beats (Q1 2026 +7.1%, Q2 2025 +11.2%) and two misses (Q4 2025 −4.8%, Q3 2025 −1.4%)—consistent with the lumpiness of large-tool order timing rather than a deterioration in demand.

The €4 billion width of the FY2026 guidance band is the single most important signal in the financials: it quantifies the China uncertainty management itself is carrying, having been explicitly designed to "accommodate potential outcomes of the export control discussions that are currently ongoing."

On capital allocation, ASML launched a €12 billion share-buyback program running through December 31, 2028, alongside a growing dividend (current yield ~0.47%). Share count has declined steadily—385.4 million shares in 2025 versus 402.6 million in 2021. The balance sheet historically runs net cash with very high returns on capital. The combination of high margins, net cash, strong free cash flow, and record backlog makes the financial profile unambiguously healthy, though the absence of a verifiable 20-F balance sheet through standard tools warrants a slightly tempered Financial Health score pending confirmation.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest dimension of the thesis and rests on demand breadth, not a single-customer story. The AI capex supercycle is driving sustained leading-edge equipment investment across logic (AI accelerators) and memory (HBM/advanced DRAM), with management noting memory customers are "sold out for 2026."

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum. Current-year consensus EPS has risen to $31.52 from $29.69 ninety days ago (+6.1%); next-year to $41.58 from $37.51 (+10.8%); next quarter to $8.30 from $7.56 (+9.8%). These are meaningful upward revisions and validate the order-book strength. The one soft spot is the current quarter, revised down 1.4% over 90 days to $6.85, likely reflecting near-term China timing rather than structural weakness.

The next leg of growth depends on High-NA EUV adoption. Management expects the "first chips made with High-NA machines" within months as of May 2026, with Intel as lead adopter and SK Hynix indicating plans. Advanced packaging is an emerging adjacency: ASML is developing a second advanced-packaging tool, signaling expansion beyond pure lithography. Installed Base Management provides a growing recurring layer that stabilizes the cyclical equipment line.

The growth-prospects signal is strong and broad-based, which I weight heavily. The caveat is that this growth is already reflected in both the rising estimates and the stock price. The €36–40 billion FY2026 guidance, even at the high end, requires the AI strength to fully offset a "significant" China decline, meaning the growth thesis and the China risk are two sides of the same number.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the binding constraint on the recommendation, and where I diverge from the strength of the underlying business.

At $1,929, ASML trades at approximately 61x current-year consensus EPS ($31.52) and 46x next-year ($41.58). On trailing earnings the multiple is ~65x. These are premium multiples even for a monopoly franchise growing earnings 15–20%. The PEG on next-year estimates is roughly 2.5–3.0x, not the sub-1.5 zone where rising estimates reliably precede further re-rating.

The most telling data point is that the stock trades *above* the analyst consensus. The high target is $2,239 (16% above current) and the low is $886 (54% below). When a stock at 46x+ forward trades above the consensus base case, analyst targets are not acting as a floor: they reflect the same growth extrapolation already embedded in the price, and offer no independent confirmation of further upside.

The estimate-revision strength does not rescue the valuation here. Rising estimates are a powerful BUY signal for stocks trading cheaply relative to those estimates (forward P/E under 30x, PEG under 1.5). ASML fails both screens. At a premium multiple, the asymmetry inverts: beats are expected and produce little re-rating, while a miss—most plausibly on China—produces sharp multiple compression. The SaaS de-rating of 2025–2026 demonstrated that correct sector themes do not protect premium-multiple stocks from 40–50% drawdowns when sentiment shifts.

I am explicitly not penalizing the stock for having appreciated 153%. Price history is not a valuation input. The low valuation score reflects current price versus current and forward fundamentals: at 46x+ forward with the stock above consensus and a guided China revenue decline, there is no identifiable mispricing in the bull direction. The asymmetry favors downside, which earns a Valuation score of 3.5.

## Competitive Landscape

ASML's competitive position is near-best-in-class and has strengthened over the past six months rather than weakened. It remains the sole global supplier of EUV lithography with no commercial alternative. Roadmap gains—the 1,000W source, 260 wph throughput, High-NA ramp—have widened the lead. Within semiconductor equipment, ASML occupies the highest-value, most defensible position, complementary to KLA (inspection/metrology), Applied Materials, and Lam Research, but with a structural monopoly none of those peers possess.

The only credible long-tail competitive threat is xLight, a free-electron-laser EUV light-source startup chaired by ousted Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger and backed by up to $150 million in CHIPS Act funding plus a U.S. government equity stake, targeting first silicon by 2028. As of mid-2026 it remains lab-stage. The U.S.-government-backed nature gives it a strategic patron worth monitoring beyond 2028, but it poses no near-term threat. The competitive trajectory—share, moat width, and product velocity all improving—supports a high score of 8.5.

## Risk Assessment

**China exposure and export controls (most material near-term risk).** China has represented a very large share of ASML's DUV revenue. The CEO expects China total net sales to "decline significantly" in 2026. This is the single biggest swing factor in the numbers and the one the market has chosen to discount in favor of the AI-offset narrative. It is specific, dated to the ongoing negotiations, and not fully priced, which drives the below-neutral Risk score.

**Geopolitical / Taiwan concentration.** TSMC is ASML's largest customer and the locus of leading-edge production. Any cross-strait disruption would severely impair both demand and the global chip supply chain: low probability, extreme impact.

**High-NA execution and adoption.** The next growth leg depends on customer ramp of EXE tools. Delays or yield issues would pressure the growth narrative that justifies the premium multiple.

**Cyclicality and customer concentration.** Revenue is concentrated in a handful of customers and is highly cyclical with semiconductor capex. The October 2024 guidance cut, which triggered a sector sell-off, is a recent reminder of how fast orders and sentiment can swing.

**Valuation as a risk.** At 46x+ forward and above consensus target, the stock has minimal margin for error. A disappointment on any of the above triggers multiple compression layered on top of any earnings revision.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent, which is itself a mild caution for a stock at these levels. Near-term ATM IV of 52.8% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 42.8% (a 9.9% spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is just 13%—implied volatility is low relative to its own recent range. The put/call skew is balanced at −2.9%, put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.64, and the term structure is in contango (52.8% near vs 55.1% long-term). Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV fell from 58.6% to 52.8% even as the price rose 24.9%; the market priced *out* uncertainty as the stock melted up. Short interest is negligible at 0.3% of float, 0.7 days to cover. There is no positioning signal warning of a near-term catalyst, but the lack of hedging demand at all-time highs means there is little cushion if the China outcome lands at the unfavorable end of the band.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

ASML is the indispensable bottleneck of the AI buildout, with a monopoly moat that is widening and a backlog providing multi-year visibility. AI demand fully offsets the China decline, High-NA ramps on schedule with first chips within months, and the buyback steadily reduces share count. Earnings compound at 15–20% with best-in-class margins, and the stock re-rates toward the high target of ~$2,239 (+16%) as consensus targets chase the rising estimates upward. In a sustained AI capex supercycle running into 2027, the premium multiple holds and earnings growth carries the stock higher.

### Bear Case

China export controls land at the unfavorable end of the band, China DUV revenue falls more than the AI offset can absorb, and FY2026 revenue comes in at or below €36 billion. A High-NA delay or yield issue compounds the disappointment. At a premium multiple with the stock above consensus target and minimal hedging demand, even a modest miss triggers multiple compression. The stock de-rates toward the low-$1,400s to low-$1,300s (−25% to −32%), with the analyst low target of $886 representing the tail outcome in a sharp cyclical reversal akin to the October 2024 episode.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (mid-July 2026), which will provide an updated read on China revenue trajectory within the guidance band and early High-NA ramp data. The resolution of export-control negotiations is the more important, less scheduled trigger.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback toward the low-$1,400s, compressing forward P/E toward ~34x next-year EPS and restoring asymmetric upside, with fundamentals intact.
- China export-control outcome resolving at the favorable end of the band (FY2026 revenue tracking toward €40 billion), removing the principal overhang.
- Confirmed High-NA volume ramp with customer yield validation, de-risking the next growth leg.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY2026 guidance cut below €36 billion, signaling the China decline is outrunning the AI offset.
- A High-NA delay or yield failure that breaks the growth narrative underpinning the premium multiple.
- A bookings reversal—a quarter with net bookings falling meaningfully below the prior run-rate—indicating the AI order cycle is rolling over.

## Conclusion

ASML is a business worth owning at the right price. The franchise quality is exceptional: a structural EUV monopoly, record bookings, a deep backlog, 53% gross margins, rising estimates across every horizon, and disciplined capital return. The growth and competitive dimensions are among the strongest in the technology universe, and the moat has widened over the past six months.

The recommendation is HOLD, not BUY, because the price has outrun the opportunity. At $1,929—46x+ next-year earnings, 153% above its twelve-month low, and trading above the Wall Street consensus target—the stock has fully priced the AI-offsets-China bull case. Management itself set a deliberately wide guidance band to absorb the China export-control uncertainty and warned that China revenue will decline significantly. That is a specific, falsifiable downside the market has chosen to discount, and at this multiple the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: beats are already expected, misses are punished. The score of 6.45 reflects a high-quality business carrying a low valuation score and a below-neutral risk score driven by the China overhang and the premium multiple's lack of margin for error.

For existing holders, the franchise justifies continued ownership; the growth thesis is intact and the backlog provides visibility into 2027. For new capital, we would wait for a pullback toward the low-$1,400s or for the China outcome to resolve favorably before adding. We expect the business to compound, but we are not paying up for it at all-time highs above consensus.
