## Executive Summary

Corcept has spent eighteen months converting a single-product, patent-expired franchise into a two-product commercial company, and Q2 2026 was the first quarter in which that conversion showed up in the income statement. Revenue of $256.1M grew 32% year over year, Lifyorli (relacorilant in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer) contributed $47.6M in its first full quarter with more than 1,300 patients started, and management raised FY2026 guidance for the third time this year, to $1.1–1.2B from $950M–$1.05B in April and $900M–$1.0B in February. Consensus responded violently: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 366% over 90 days to $2.13 and FY2027 up 185% to $4.52. On those numbers the stock trades at 23.9x FY2027 earnings with implied earnings growth above 100%, which is a reasonable multiple by any standard.

The problem is what those estimates embed and what they exclude. Trailing-twelve-month operating income through June 30, 2026 is approximately $6.4M on $830.8M of revenue — the company spent its entire operating margin building a salesforce for two launches, and only one arrived. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $16.8M versus $49.1M a year earlier. FY2026 guidance requires H2 revenue of $679–779M, or $339.5–389.5M per quarter, 32.5% to 52.1% above the Q2 run rate. And two unquantified binaries sit inside the next eight months: the December 17, 2026 PDUFA for relacorilant in hypercortisolism, following a December 30, 2025 complete response letter, and the March 2027 trial in Teva's antitrust suit challenging the exclusive specialty-pharmacy channel that is the reason Korlym revenue grew 57.8% from 2023 to 2025 despite a generic entering in January 2024. Federal antitrust damages are trebled, four national insurers have filed a parallel action in Alameda County, and the Q2 10-Q states that Corcept has recorded "no losses and no provision for a loss contingency with respect to any matter."

Score trajectory has run 4.8 → 6.0 → 6.4 → 6.0 → 6.0 across four consecutive HOLDs, with growth prospects up 2.0 points and risk factors up 2.0 points over that span. New data confirms the growth improvement and does not confirm the risk improvement. The stock is 19% higher than it was the day before the Q2 release and 22% above its level at the prior review; the fundamental news was good, and the price took it. I keep a HOLD at 5.9 overall, with the recommendation resting on a genuine 12-month growth thesis rather than on income or on absence of conviction.

## Company Overview

Corcept is a single-segment biopharmaceutical company built on cortisol modulation at the glucocorticoid receptor, a mechanism it has pursued since 1998. It holds patents on more than 1,000 proprietary selective cortisol modulators across four to five structurally distinct chemical series, had 730 employees at December 31, 2025, and outsources all manufacturing — cost of sales has run 1.6–1.8% of revenue.

Three products are sold in the United States. Korlym (mifepristone 300 mg), approved in 2012 for hyperglycemia secondary to hypercortisolism, and Corcept's own authorized generic of Korlym, launched June 2024 five months after Teva's generic. Together these are the "hypercortisolism products," roughly 81% of Q2 2026 revenue. Third is Lifyorli (relacorilant), approved March 2026 in combination with nab-paclitaxel for platinum-resistant ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer, launched April 1, 2026, and added to NCCN Guidelines as a preferred regimen in April 2026.

Distribution architecture matters more here than at a typical specialty pharma. Hypercortisolism products are sold to patients through a single exclusive specialty pharmacy — Optime Care from 2017 through January 8, 2026, now Curant Health Georgia under an agreement running to June 2028. The 10-Q states Curant handles collection of payments from insurers representing more than 99% of hypercortisolism revenue. Lifyorli uses a broader channel of specialty pharmacies plus distributors serving community oncology, GPOs, and hospitals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2025 revenue was $761.4M, up 12.8%, decomposed in the 10-K as volume +37.0% offset by average price −17.7% as mix shifted into Corcept's own lower-priced authorized generic. Management also disclosed that FY2025 revenue would have been materially higher had the prior specialty pharmacy met demand — a realized operational loss, not a hypothetical risk.

The 2026 quarterly path:

| | Q1 2025 | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hypercortisolism | $157.2M | $194.4M | $164.9M | $208.6M |
| Lifyorli | — | — | — | $47.6M |
| **Total** | **$157.2M** | **$194.4M** | **$164.9M** | **$256.1M** |

Hypercortisolism grew 4.9% year over year in Q1 and 7.3% in Q2 — a marked deceleration from FY2025's 12.8%. Sequentially, though, it jumped 26.5%. The 10-Q attributes 76.1% of the Q2 year-over-year increase to volume, with the remainder from an August 2025 price increase and "improved payor reimbursement rates for our Hypercortisolism Products at our current specialty pharmacy." That last item is a channel reset following the Curant transition rather than incremental demand, and it is unlikely to repeat as a growth contributor.

**Margins and the cost base.** SG&A rose from roughly 38% of revenue in FY2023 to 58.9% in FY2025 (≈$279M to $448.7M), then 61.3% in Q2 2026 and 71.8% in H1 2026. Both the 10-K and 10-Q attribute this to salesforce and headcount build for existing and expected products — Corcept staffed for a Cushing's relacorilant launch that has not happened and an oncology launch that arrived seven months later than planned.

The arithmetic that follows is the single most important number for framing valuation. FY2025 operating income was $44.8M ($761.4M − $13.0M COGS − $254.9M R&D − $448.7M SG&A). H1 2025 operating income was $30.1M; H1 2026 was an operating loss of $8.3M. Trailing-twelve-month operating income through June 30, 2026 is therefore approximately **$6.4M on TTM revenue of roughly $830.8M**. Q2's $41.3M operating profit (16.1% margin) is the first quarter of recovery and it is one quarter. Note also that Lifyorli's Q2 cost of sales was near zero because pre-approval manufacturing was expensed to R&D; reported gross margin will normalize downward as capitalized post-approval inventory sells through.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 pretax income was $66.5M, down from $161.5M in 2024; reported net income of $99.7M reflects a $33.2M income tax **benefit** from excess stock-option deductions and R&D credits, an effective rate near −50%. H1 2026 repeated the pattern in smaller form (pretax $1.2M, tax benefit $10.0M, net income $11.2M), while Q2 alone flipped to a $2.9M tax expense as option-exercise deductions declined. The benefit is real cash tax savings, but it is a function of share price and exercise timing, not operations. Net deferred tax assets of $178.7M equal 20% of total assets.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow went $126.7M (FY2023) → $198.3M (FY2024) → $142.0M (FY2025), with FY2025 capex of $0.2M implying free cash flow near $141.8M. H1 2026 OCF was $16.8M, reconciling from $11.2M of net income plus $52.5M of stock compensation less roughly $50M of combined working-capital and deferred-tax drag (receivables −$17.1M, inventory −$7.5M, accounts payable −$25.9M, accrued R&D −$12.3M, partially offset by +$24.9M accrued liabilities). This should not be annualized: the payables reduction is a timing item and receivables grew with the launch. As a labeled sensitivity, if H2 repeats H1's working-capital drag, full-year OCF lands well under $50M; if Q2's $41.3M operating income run rate holds with normal working-capital build, the answer is materially higher. Operating cash generation is currently thin against an $11.7B market capitalization.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and investments of $544.6M at June 30, 2026 (up from $515.4M at March 31), total assets $888.7M, total liabilities $182.9M, equity $705.8M, and **no debt**. Liabilities are payables, accrued rebates ($52.0M), accrued compensation ($38.2M), leases ($9.4M), and accrued income taxes ($15.5M). Current ratio 2.92 at year-end; Altman Z of 4.69. Solvency is not in question. What the balance sheet does not carry is any provision for the antitrust matters.

**Dilution and capital returns.** Q2 basic weighted shares 105.377M versus diluted 118.385M — an 11.0% ownership dilution on a treasury-stock basis, with a further 3.0M anti-dilutive options excluded in Q2 that would enter at higher prices. Stock compensation of $53.1M in H1 equals 12.6% of revenue. The January 2024 $200M repurchase authorization has $11.4M remaining; FY2025 saw $172.9M of open-market repurchases and H1 2026 saw **zero** (the $31.4M treasury increase came entirely from shares tendered for option exercise cost and withholding). Mechanically the authorization is nearly exhausted; the board has not expanded it.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs, of which one is confirmed, one is probable, and one is binary.

**Confirmed: Lifyorli launch.** $47.6M in the first quarter of availability, 18.6% of revenue, more than 1,300 patients started as of July 29 with demand described as accelerating each month, and an NCCN preferred-regimen listing. The underlying data supports uptake: ROSELLA met dual primary endpoints with PFS HR 0.70 (p=0.008, blinded independent review) and OS HR 0.65 (p=0.0004), median overall survival 16.0 versus 11.9 months, no incremental safety burden, and no biomarker requirement — relevant against mirvetuximab (FRα-high, roughly one in three patients), trastuzumab deruxtecan (HER2), and pembrolizumab (PD-1, 50–60%). Patients *started* cannot be converted into patients *on therapy*, so per-patient economics and persistence remain unverifiable.

**Probable: hypercortisolism volume expansion.** CATALYST found 23.8% of 1,057 patients with difficult-to-control type 2 diabetes had hypercortisolism, with the randomized phase showing HbA1c −1.47% versus −0.15% for placebo (p<0.0001); MOMENTUM found 27.3% of 1,086 resistant-hypertension patients affected. Both published in *Diabetes Care*. Corcept is funding epidemiology that expands its own addressable market, which is the most durable element of the story. The constraint is that Korlym's label is narrow and the pending qui tam action makes off-label promotion risk concrete, so diagnosis expansion has to be threaded without promotional overreach.

**Binary: relacorilant in Cushing's, PDUFA December 17, 2026.** The December 2025 CRL acknowledged that pivotal GRACE met its primary endpoint (OR 0.17, p=0.02 on loss of blood-pressure control in randomized withdrawal) and that GRADIENT provided confirmatory evidence, but required additional efficacy evidence. In April 2026 the FDA asked for additional analyses of data already in the NDA; Corcept resubmitted June 17, 2026 on what appears to be a Class 2 clock. That the agency requested re-analysis rather than a new trial is materially better than a typical efficacy CRL. The counter is that GRADIENT's own primary endpoint was not statistically significant versus placebo (systolic BP −6.6 mm Hg from baseline, p=0.012, but not significant between arms), so if the concern is whether one randomized-withdrawal trial plus one failed placebo-controlled trial constitutes substantial evidence, re-analysis may not cure it.

**Guidance bridge.** FY2026 guidance of $1.1–1.2B less H1 actual of $421.05M implies H2 revenue of $679–779M, or $339.5–389.5M per quarter versus Q2's $256.1M — 32.5% to 52.1% above the current run rate. Management has raised guidance twice already and has been credible, but the required acceleration rests almost entirely on Lifyorli scaling from $47.6M and on hypercortisolism holding a step-up that was partly a reimbursement reset.

**Estimate revisions.** The pattern here is the strongest quantitative signal in the file: current quarter +385.7% over 90 days, current year +366.3% to $2.13, next year +185.4% to $4.52, with 30-day revisions still running +81% to +338%. Combined with 32% revenue growth, this is the revision-velocity configuration that historically precedes further re-rating, and FY2027's 23.9x multiple is well inside the range where that signal has teeth. Two qualifications: the panel is three analysts, and the revisions are largely mechanical catch-up to a guidance raise that is already public — the stock gapped 27% on July 30. Revisions of this magnitude are usually informative because they lead price; here they followed it within a single session.

**Pipeline catalysts through 2027.** EMA decision on relacorilant in ovarian cancer by end-2026; BELLA Part A and MONARCH (miricorilant in MASH, n=175) by end-2026; Phase 3 ALS trial beginning early 2027; BELLA B/C, STELLA, TRIDENT, SYNERGY by end-2027. On ALS, DAZALS missed its functional primary endpoint but showed 84% reduction in death risk at 52 weeks (HR 0.16, p=0.0009) and 87% at 104 weeks. A survival-only signal from a trial that missed its primary is a plausible but unproven basis for a pivotal program, and the FDA's handling of the Cushing's CRL is a caution about how post-hoc findings may be received.

## Competitive Landscape

**Hypercortisolism.** Corcept created this market and still dominates it, but the legal moat is gone. Korlym's composition-of-matter patent has expired; the D.N.J. found Teva's generic non-infringing in December 2023, the Federal Circuit affirmed February 19, 2026, and rehearing en banc was denied July 10, 2026. Sun and Hikma hold settlements permitting mifepristone sales once approved. What remains is commercial: the exclusive specialty pharmacy, patient-support infrastructure, prescriber relationships, the authorized generic's price positioning, and diagnostic market-making. That architecture has worked remarkably well — franchise revenue went from $482.4M in 2023 to $761.4M in 2025, up 57.8%, through a generic launch. Competitive pressure is increasing at the margin: Recordati's osilodrostat (Isturisa) had its label broadened in April 2025 to adults with any etiology of hypercortisolism, previously limited to Cushing's disease.

Relacorilant, if approved, is a clean within-franchise upgrade: no progesterone-receptor affinity, no hypokalemia (versus 44% in Korlym's pivotal trial, a leading cause of discontinuation), no QT prolongation, and seven years of U.S. orphan exclusivity. That is the strategic reason the December 17 date matters more than any single quarter's revenue.

**Oncology.** Platinum-resistant ovarian cancer has been a therapeutic graveyard, and a four-month median OS extension from an oral, biomarker-free agent explains the NCCN preferred listing. The competitive set is now crowded: bevacizumab, mirvetuximab (since November 2022), trastuzumab deruxtecan (April 2024), and Merck's pembrolizumab, approved February 2026 for PD-1-expressing tumors covering 50–60% of patients — one month before Lifyorli. Corcept's biomarker-free positioning is genuine differentiation but not exclusivity.

Trajectory assessment: product diversification improved (Lifyorli is 18.6% of revenue), the legal moat deteriorated to zero, the commercial moat is proven but under litigation, and vendor concentration in the remaining 81% of revenue got worse rather than better. That combination supports a competitive position score in the middle of the range rather than the top.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Antitrust — the largest unquantified item.** Teva's suit (N.D. Cal., filed June 2024) alleges federal and state antitrust violations tied to the exclusive specialty-pharmacy arrangement; the Third Amended Complaint added claims about the Curant agreement, the court partially denied dismissal again on May 5, 2026, and **trial is set for March 2027**. Aetna, HCSC, Humana, and Molina filed substantially similar claims in Alameda County in February 2025; on July 20, 2026 the court denied Corcept's motion to stay and partially denied dismissal. Federal antitrust damages are trebled and the plaintiffs allege overcharges on a product generating $675–761M annually. Corcept has recorded **no accrual and disclosed no damages estimate**, which is what GAAP requires when loss is not both probable and estimable and is not evidence about the merits. I will not fabricate a number. What can be said is that the exposure is unbounded in the disclosure, that adverse structural relief would expose the franchise to the generic erosion it has so far avoided, and that $544.6M of cash is the entire buffer.

**2. December 17 PDUFA.** Binary, discussed above. Failure strands the Cushing's-directed portion of a salesforce for a second consecutive year and removes the exclusivity reset from the franchise.

**3. Mifepristone political risk.** HHS announced in September 2025 that it will re-examine mifepristone's safety for pregnancy termination; the 10-Q states there is no assurance the review will not restrict distribution for **any** use. This is a non-diversifiable, non-actuarial risk against roughly 81% of revenue.

**4. Reimbursement policy.** The IRA Part D redesign "may materially reduce our revenue and profits in 2026 and beyond" per the 10-Q. Newly added in Q2 is a full risk factor on the proposed CMS GUARD model, which would apply most-favored-nation rebates to Part D drugs, **explicitly names Lifyorli**, would cover 25% of Part D beneficiaries, and would fix 2027–2031 rebates on the lowest ex-U.S. price offered in 2027. The 10-Q states participation "would likely be mandatory." This interacts directly with the Q4 2026 EMA decision: EU pricing negotiated in 2027 could anchor the U.S. Medicare reference price for five years.

**5. Vendor concentration.** More than 99% of hypercortisolism insurer collections now run through Curant, whose agreement began June 2025. The 2025 revenue shortfall from Optime's inability to meet demand is a realized loss, not a hypothetical.

**6. Securities and derivative litigation.** Allegheny County Employees' Retirement System v. Corcept (N.D. Cal., filed February 20, 2026) alleges 10b-5 violations for the October 31, 2024 – December 30, 2025 class period regarding the Cushing's NDA. LeRiger v. Belanoff (April 10, 2026) is a derivative action naming all current directors, seeking disgorgement of insider trading profits, stayed pending the securities case.

**7. Qui tam.** *U.S. ex rel. Elliott v. Corcept* (D.N.J., 2017), FCA claims tied to Korlym sales. DOJ and the NJ USAO declined to intervene in May 2026 — favorable — but the relator served an amended complaint on June 2, 2026 to proceed alone, with Corcept's response due September 21, 2026.

Risk factors score at 3.8. The prior review lifted this dimension from 2.5 to 4.5; the July 10 en banc denial, the July 20 Aetna rulings, the newly added GUARD risk factor, and the relator proceeding alone argue that idiosyncratic risk has not declined, while the price is 22% higher, meaning less of the tail is priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to $175, +62%)

Operating leverage is the entire argument, and Q2 proved it works: $41.3M of operating income on $256.1M of revenue, achieved with SG&A that was built for two launches. If H2 delivers guidance, quarterly revenue of $340–390M against a largely fixed cost base produces operating margins in the high twenties to low thirties, and FY2027 consensus EPS of $4.52 becomes conservative rather than aggressive. December 17 approval then transforms the hypercortisolism business from a patent-expired product defended by channel control into a differentiated product with seven years of orphan exclusivity, removing both the generic overhang and, arguably, the commercial rationale for the antitrust claims. Lifyorli's TAM extends through BELLA B/C, STELLA, and TRIDENT, MONARCH reads out in MASH by year-end, and the ALS survival signal, if replicated, is optionality the market is not paying for. Zero debt and $544.6M of cash fund all of it without dilution beyond the existing option overhang. On FY2028 EPS near $6.25 at 28x, the stock is $175.

### Bear Case (to $52, −52%)

Trailing operating income is $6.4M and H1 operating cash flow was $16.8M — the market is paying $11.1B of enterprise value for one good quarter and a guidance number that requires 32.5–52.1% sequential-run-rate acceleration. Part of Q2's hypercortisolism strength was a one-time payor reimbursement reset at the new specialty pharmacy, and the underlying year-over-year growth of 7.3% is a fraction of FY2025's 12.8%. If H2 lands at the bottom of guidance or below, the leverage story compresses immediately. A second CRL on December 17 — plausible given GRADIENT's failure versus placebo — leaves the Cushing's salesforce stranded for a second year and hands the franchise's future to a channel arrangement that goes on trial in March 2027, where treble damages against a defendant with $544.6M of cash and no accrual is a solvency-adjacent question rather than a valuation adjustment. Layer on GUARD naming Lifyorli, HHS re-examining mifepristone, pembrolizumab entering the same indication one month ahead, and osilodrostat's broadened label, and the $28.66 low from February is a reminder of how fast this security reprices.

## Conclusion

The fundamental news since the last review was good and the price captured it.
