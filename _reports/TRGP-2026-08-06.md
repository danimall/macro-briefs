## Executive Summary

Targa reported its Q2 2026 results on the morning of this analysis: Adjusted EBITDA of $1,603M, up 38% year over year, a record quarter, with FY2026 guidance moved to "toward the top end" of the $5.7–5.9B range (implying roughly $5,800M). The operationally important detail is the context in which that record was set. Targa's Gathering and Processing segment realized a natural gas price of **negative $2.48/MMBtu** in the quarter and negative $0.97/MMBtu for the first half — the company was on average paying to dispose of residue gas — and still set a record. That is a clean, real-world stress test of a predominantly fee-per-unit-of-throughput contract structure, and it substantiates the central structural claim in the 10-K.

Two qualifications matter as much as the headline. First, fee-based contracts protect **unit margin**, not **throughput**, and throughput is what the fee is levied on. Permian wells were being shut in during July specifically because of low gas prices (Midland Reporter-Telegram, 2026-07-18), a volume risk that had not yet appeared in Q2. Second, the quarter's composition was flattered by items that are unlikely to repeat at the same magnitude: a $41.2M natural gas hedge gain, and a realized condensate price of $90.57/Bbl versus $63.79 a year earlier — a 42% increase driven by the crude complex. Hedge coverage of equity volumes decays over time by the company's own disclosure, and basis risk is explicitly not fully hedged.

Financially, the story is a coherent leveraged compounding strategy that is working and is also the binding constraint. Adjusted EBITDA compounded at 24.7% annually from $2,052M (2021) to $4,957M (2025). Net leverage improved to roughly **3.35x** at mid-2026 ($19,446M net debt against guided $5,800M EBITDA) from ~3.48x at year-end 2025, even as total debt rose $2,338M in six months, because EBITDA grew faster than the debt. But 2026 net growth capex of ~$4.5B is ~78% of guided EBITDA, and FY2025 free cash flow of ~$590M covered only ~55% of the ~$1,076M annualized dividend. The dividend and buyback are, on a post-growth-capex basis, funded with incremental debt. That is normal for the strategy and rational at a ~16% pre-tax return on invested capital — provided Permian volumes keep growing and the investment-grade debt market stays open. Four Item 1.01+2.03 debt-issuance 8-Ks in thirteen months show the funding cadence.

At $269.95 the shares trade at 23.6x FY2026 consensus EPS ($11.42), 22.6x FY2027 ($11.94), and ~13.3x forward EV/EBITDA — a premium multiple within Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, with a 1.9% yield against a 3.7% peer median. Base case $295 (+9.3%) against a $195 bear case (-27.8%) is the wrong shape to add capital. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Targa Resources is a Delaware C-corporation running an integrated North American midstream natural gas and NGL system through two reportable segments.

**Gathering and Processing** performs wellhead gathering, compression, treating and cryogenic processing, plus crude gathering and terminaling. The footprint is concentrated in the Permian (Midland and Delaware), with additional positions in the Badlands (Williston), Central (Oklahoma/North Texas) and SouthTX/Coastal.

**Logistics and Transportation** covers the downstream half: NGL transportation on Grand Prix from the Permian to Mont Belvieu, fractionation at Mont Belvieu, NGL storage and terminaling, and LPG export at Galena Park on the Houston Ship Channel.

The mechanism that distinguishes Targa from a single-segment gatherer is that one molecule of rich Permian gas can pay Targa a separate fee at each of five steps — gathering, processing, NGL transport, fractionation, export loading. Incremental Permian volume therefore converts to EBITDA at a higher rate than for a competitor holding only one link. Owned fractionation and dock capacity is the scarce end of that chain and is hard to replicate on permitting and Gulf Coast land grounds.

Commercial arrangements are described in the 10-K as "predominantly fee-based," with an explicitly disclosed minority of percent-of-proceeds arrangements that carry commodity price exposure. Most gathering pipelines are FERC-exempt as gathering facilities; a named set of entities is regulated, including Grand Prix and Targa Badlands as ICA common carriers. The 10-K states that **Forza Pipeline LLC "will be subject to FERC jurisdiction under the NGA"** — future tense, confirming a new interstate gas pipeline in development rather than in service.

## Financial Analysis

### The growth series

Adjusted EBITDA, the company-selected performance measure in the proxy, is the cleanest multi-year operating series:

| Fiscal year | Adjusted EBITDA ($M) | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 2,052.0 | — |
| 2022 | 2,901.1 | +41.4% |
| 2023 | 3,530.0 | +21.7% |
| 2024 | 4,142.3 | +17.3% |
| 2025 | 4,957.4 | +19.7% |
| 2026E | ~5,800 (guided top end) | ~+17% |

The 2021–2025 compound rate is 24.7% ($4,957.4 ÷ $2,052.0 = 2.416x over four years). Revenue is a poor metric here — FY2025 revenue of ~$17.03B includes large commodity purchase-and-resale flows at minimal margin, so revenue growth tracks gas and NGL price levels more than business performance.

### FY2025 base and mid-2026 position

FY2025: operating income $3,331.2M on ~$17,030M revenue (19.6% operating margin); operating cash flow ~$3,920M; capex ~$3,330M; derived free cash flow ~$590M, a 1.0% FCF yield on today's $57.94B market capitalization. Total assets $25,218.4M against total equity of $3,067.9M including noncontrolling interests — 12.2% equity/assets. Current assets $2,362.7M against current liabilities $3,546.8M gives negative working capital of $1,184.1M and a 0.67x current ratio.

At 6/30/2026: total consolidated debt $19,578M, cash $132M, net debt $19,446M. Against guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$5,800M, that is **3.35x** — inside management's stated 3.0–4.0x band and lower than the ~3.48x implied at 12/31/2025 ($17,240M ÷ $4,957.4M). I would not annualize Q2's $1,603M to reach 3.03x: the quarter carried a crude/condensate price spike, a $41.2M gas hedge gain and a specific basis position, and the propane/butane business skews toward Q4/Q1. Note also that midstream issuers commonly report leverage pro forma for EBITDA from projects under construction, so the company's own stated ratio may be lower than my derived figure and is not directly comparable.

### The distress screen is a false signal — and the underlying weaknesses are real

A mechanical Altman Z-Score of 0.87 appears in screening data, which would place Targa in the distress zone. That reading is not usable. It implies EBIT/assets of 0.001, irreconcilable with $3,331.2M of operating income on $25,218.4M of assets (0.132), and the equity term appears to use book equity rather than the market value of equity the specification requires. Recomputing with correct inputs — X₁ −0.0470, X₂ 0.0910, X₃ 0.1321, X₄ 2.6184 ($58.0B market equity ÷ $22,150.5M liabilities), X₅ 0.6753 — yields **Z ≈ 2.75**, the grey zone. The two-point correction changes the verdict entirely.

Equal weight belongs on the other side of that correction. Z ≈ 2.75 is not a fortress reading, and the specific weaknesses the screen picked up are genuine: negative working capital of $1,184.1M, 12.2% equity/assets, 3.35x leverage. The thin book equity has a mechanical explanation — years of buybacks and the 2022 buy-in of Targa Resources Partners public units consumed book equity without reducing asset value — but it is thin nonetheless. The better external check on credit quality is the three investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa2/BBB), plus the disclosure that a one-notch downgrade below investment grade by both Moody's and S&P would **not** require collateral posting under the ISDAs against a $104.1M net derivative liability.

### The capital allocation tension, stated arithmetically

Total debt rose from ~$17,240M (12/31/2025) to $19,578M (6/30/2026), an increase of ~$2,338M over six months. In the same period Targa paid roughly $538M of dividends (~$270M Q1, ~$268M Q2) and repurchased $80M of stock in Q2 (308,102 shares at a $259.93 weighted average; $1,239M authorization remaining).

FY2025 makes the point without any 2026 estimation: free cash flow of ~$590M against an annualized dividend obligation of ~$1,076M is **~55% coverage**, with the balance and the buybacks drawn from the balance sheet. For 2026, ~$4.5B of net growth capex plus ~$250M maintenance against ~$5,800M of EBITDA is ~82% of EBITDA committed to capital spending before any distribution.

Two facts push against reading this as strain. A **$4.4B federal NOL carryforward** with no expiration under current law, plus management's statement that it does not anticipate paying corporate alternative minimum tax near term, means cash conversion materially exceeds a fully-taxable peer's. And the return check supports the borrowing: FY2025 operating income of $3,331.2M against roughly $20.3B of invested capital ($17.24B debt + $3.07B equity) implies a **~16.4% pre-tax ROIC**, close to the after-tax figure given minimal cash taxes. A company earning ~16% on capital and borrowing at BBB rates is behaving rationally by borrowing to build.

The vulnerability is narrow and specific: the model requires Adjusted EBITDA to grow faster than debt accretes. If Permian volume growth flattens, leverage rises toward the top of the band at the same moment EBITDA-multiple compression hits the equity. Those two exposures are correlated, which is what makes this the risk that matters rather than one of many.

## Growth Analysis

The demand mechanism is straightforward and currently confirmed. The Permian is the only major U.S. basin still growing liquids volumes; rich gas produced there requires processing, NGL takeaway, fractionation and export; Targa owns capacity at every step. Q2's 38% EBITDA growth came from volume plus the condensate price, not from a one-time item alone.

Consensus is moving in the right direction on the near term and flattening on the out-year:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $2.84 | $2.66 | $2.40 | +6.7% | +18.3% |
| Next quarter | $2.83 | $2.81 | $2.57 | +0.5% | +9.8% |
| FY2026 | $11.42 | $11.23 | $10.44 | +1.6% | +9.3% |
| FY2027 | $11.94 | $12.19 | $11.66 | **−2.0%** | +2.4% |

The FY2026 revision velocity of +9.3% over 90 days is positive earnings momentum, not the >30% inflection magnitude that would flag a structural mispricing. The more informative line is FY2027: down 2.0% in 30 days, and implying only +4.6% EPS growth over FY2026. Consensus is modelling a sharp deceleration after this year — from ~+17% EBITDA growth in 2026 to something closer to mid-single-digit EPS growth in 2027. That deceleration, if correct, is what makes the current multiple hard to defend; if wrong, it is where the upside lives.

Three growth vectors deserve specific treatment:

**Egress construction.** Sustained negative Waha basis strengthens producers' willingness to sign long-term firm take-or-pay gas egress. Targa is building into that: Forza is pre-service and FERC-jurisdictional under the NGA, alongside in-service Bull Run and Buffalo Run. Unlike a pure gatherer, Targa monetizes the shortage. The counter-mechanism is already visible — Kinder Morgan's Gulf Coast Express expansion (+570 MMcf/d) came online in late May 2026 and Waha basis has been tightening since. If basis largely normalizes before Targa's new capacity enters service, initial take-or-pay volumes still get paid, but re-contracting and expansion economics degrade. That is the channel through which a $4.5B annual program earns less than underwritten.

**NGL export.** Galena Park LPG loading is the scarcest link and the least substitutable. This is where incremental Permian barrels ultimately clear.

**Volume risk on the same fee base.** The 10-K states plainly that Targa has "no control over producers or their drilling, completion or production decisions" and that operators "may engage in curtailment or shut-ins." Permian wells were reported shut in during July on low gas prices. Fee-per-unit contracts convert a volume decline directly into an EBITDA decline. Q2 volumes did not show this; Q3 is the test.

An underappreciated tailwind runs alongside: the FY2025 10-K documents a broad regulatory retreat that lowers forward compliance cost — CRA repeal of the methane fee rule and OBBBA deferral of the methane fee to 2034, EPA reconsideration of OOOOb/OOOOc with compliance deadlines extended, a Supreme Court stay of the Good Neighbor Plan, BLM flaring-rule injunctions in Texas and North Dakota with enforcement halted through end-2026, D.C. Circuit vacatur of four Gas Mega Rule standards, and a proposal to narrow WOTUS. Multi-year compliance capex assumptions embedded in older midstream models are probably too high. Each of these reversals is administration-dependent and several remain in litigation.

## Competitive Landscape

Targa's position is improving on trajectory, which matters more than level.

The Q2 result is direct evidence. Realized G&P gas at negative $2.48/MMBtu with record EBITDA demonstrates that the fee architecture works where it claims to. The same crude rally that damaged gas-weighted Permian producers lifted Targa's condensate realization 42% to $90.57/Bbl. And Targa is constructing egress rather than queuing for it.

The competitive set — ONEOK, Williams, Energy Transfer, Enterprise Products, MPLX, Kinder Morgan — is described in the 10-K as having "greater financial resources and access to supplies" in some cases. Two structural pressures deserve more attention than a scale narrative usually gives them:

**Customer self-build.** The 10-K flags that "customers who are significant producers of natural gas may develop their own gathering, processing, storage, terminaling and transportation systems in lieu of using those operated by us." As Permian E&Ps have consolidated into a smaller number of very large, well-capitalized operators, their capacity to self-build has risen. This is a slow pressure on contract renewal terms, not a near-term revenue risk, but it is the mechanism by which midstream fee rates erode over a decade.

**Joint venture governance.** Several of the largest growth projects are JV-structured. The 10-K discloses that JV governance requires at least a majority in interest for basic activities and sometimes 100% for significant ones — large expenditures, construction, borrowing, distributions — and that Targa may be unable to cause a JV to act even where it believes doing so is in its interest. A headline capex figure obscures how much of the program Targa actually controls. Ownership percentages, operator roles and in-service dates for individual named projects are not verified here and should not be assumed.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Volume, not price, is the load-bearing exposure.** Fee-per-unit contracts insulate unit margin and do nothing for throughput. Permian shut-ins on low gas prices were reported in July. Q3 volumes are the direct test.

**2. Hedge decay and unhedged basis.** The 10-K states that hedge coverage of expected equity commodity volumes "decrease[s] over time" and that hedges "may not fully protect us against volatility in basis differentials." Q2's $41.2M gas hedge gain is tied to a specific basis position that will roll off. Exchange-traded futures carry margin requirements that create cash-flow variability.

**3. Crude-linked component of the Q2 upside.** The condensate realization of $90.57/Bbl versus $63.79 reflects a crude complex that benefited from a war-driven rally on the crude side (per Rigzone, 2026-06-08). If that premium unwinds, the condensate realization and the percent-of-proceeds contribution that flattered Q2 would retrace. The direction of crude prices from here is not established in the sources, but the asymmetry on this line item points down.

**4. Capital committed against forecasts the company does not verify.** The 10-K states Targa "does not possess reserves estimation expertise, and we typically do not obtain independent evaluations of natural gas or crude oil reserves connected to our gathering systems," while also warning that projects may be built "to capture anticipated future growth in production in a region in which such growth does not materialize." Against ~$4.5B of annual growth capex, this is the sharpest asymmetry in the story.

**5. Correlated leverage and multiple risk.** Flattening volumes would push leverage toward 4.0x at precisely the moment the EBITDA multiple compresses.

**6. Two unverified 8-Ks.** A 2026-07-17 Item 5.02 (director/officer departure or election) and a 2026-07-06 Item 1.01 (material definitive agreement filed **without** an accompanying Item 2.03, distinguishing it from the four recent debt issuances) are both unresolved. A senior operating or financial officer departure mid-program would be material; a routine promotion would not. The July 6 agreement could be a JV, a long-term commercial contract, or an acquisition. Neither should be assumed either way.

**7. Badlands tribal jurisdiction.** A significant portion of the Badlands G&P assets sits on the Fort Berthold Indian Reservation. The Three Affiliated Tribes is a sovereign nation with independent enforcement authority, and the Tenth Circuit has held that tribal ownership of even a small fractional interest in allotted land bars condemnation of any interest in the allotment. Since Targa does not own most of the land under its pipelines and facilities, a lapsing right-of-way there has no clean legal cure.

**8. Counterparty credit.** The 10-K devotes substantial space to the possibility that distressed customers could have gathering agreements **rejected in Chapter 11**, noting that some courts have found such agreements do not create covenants running with the land. Long-dated fee contracts are worth less than they appear if a counterparty can shed them. Customer concentration was not verified precisely; my reading suggested a single customer in the low double digits of L&T segment revenue, and there is no evidence concentration is improving.

**9. Unquantified legal matters.** A New Mexico Environment Department enforcement matter and litigation involving a counterparty named MIECO appear in working notes without verified amounts, procedural status or accrual treatment. Neither is characterized here as material or immaterial.

**10. No employment agreements, no key-man insurance.** Targa has not entered employment agreements with any named executive officer and maintains no key-man life insurance, in a market where demand for Permian and Badlands personnel has risen. Retention rests entirely on unvested equity — which is precisely the equity that vested in January 2026 and was partly sold in February and March.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is benign and mildly constructive, and does not challenge the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV of 31.0% against 27.6% one-year realized is modestly rich (3.4-point spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is 7% — the low end of its own recent range. Put/call skew is 0.9% (balanced) at one month and the term structure is flat (31.0% near, 30.0% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated at a catalyst. Put/call volume of 0.10 and open-interest ratio of 0.15 are strongly call-tilted. The OI ratio rose from 0.01 to 0.15 alongside a 4.4% price decline since mid-July, which is hedging appearing where there was essentially none, from a very low base. Short interest of 2.3% of float with 3.0 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded. The one item to note is a −3.3% skew at the six-month tenor against balanced near-dated skew: whatever protection is being bought sits in December, not August.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $335 (+24.1%)

Permian volumes continue growing through the Waha basis normalization, so the July shut-ins prove to have been a price-driven pause rather than a production inflection. Targa's egress projects enter service into a market where takeaway remains constrained to at least 2027 (per producer commentary), locking in firm take-or-pay terms at attractive rates. FY2026 lands at or above the $5.9B top end and FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$6.6B rather than the mid-single-digit EPS deceleration consensus models. Leverage falls through 3.2x on EBITDA growth alone, and the regulatory cost rollback — methane fee deferred to 2034, OOOOb/OOOOc reconsidered, BLM flaring enforcement halted — reduces compliance capex below embedded assumptions. The $4.4B NOL keeps cash taxes near zero. At 14.0x on $6,600M EBITDA less ~$20.75B net debt, equity is $71.6B, or **$334/share**, essentially the top of the analyst range.

### Bear Case — $195 (-27.8%)

Permian gas curtailment persists and broadens into liquids-directed activity as producers respond to negative residue economics, so G&P throughput flattens in H2 2026. Crude retraces, taking the condensate realization back toward $65 and removing the percent-of-proceeds contribution that flattered Q2. Hedge coverage rolls off as disclosed, and unhedged basis widens again. FY2027 EBITDA stalls near $6.0B while debt reaches $21B to fund a capex program already committed, pushing leverage to ~3.5x with no growth to absorb it. The multiple compresses toward 10.5x as the market re-rates TRGP from a growth compounder to a levered infrastructure asset, and the 1.9% yield offers no support. Equity: $63.0B EV less $21.0B net debt = $42.0B, or **$196/share**. Cross-check: 17x on $11.50 FY2027 EPS = $195.

### Base Case — $295 (+9.3%)

FY2026 lands near the $5.8–5.9B top end. FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$6,450M (roughly +11%, between guidance-implied momentum and the consensus EPS deceleration). Net debt rises to ~$20.75B as capex outruns EBITDA and the dividend stays debt-supported. At 13.0x, EV is $83.85B and equity $63.1B, or **$294/share**. Cross-check on earnings: 24.5x FY2027 consensus $11.94 = $293. Call it $295, consistent with the $297 analyst mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — EBITDA compounding at a high-teens rate with leverage falling — but base-case upside of 9.3% against bear-case downside of 27.8% is roughly 1:3 and does not justify new capital at $269.95.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026) are the decisive checkpoint, because they will show whether the July Permian shut-ins converted into a G&P throughput decline and whether the condensate realization retraced with crude. Interim checkpoints: any 8-K resolving the 2026-07-17 Item 5.02 and 2026-07-06 Item 1.01, and the next debt issuance coupon.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 2026 G&P throughput growing year over year with FY2027 consensus EBITDA revised above $6.6B — this would break the consensus deceleration and support a 14x multiple.
2. A price near **$230–240**, which at unchanged FY2027 estimates is 19–20x EPS and ~12x forward EV/EBITDA, restoring roughly 25% upside to base case against a bear case that has already partly de-risked.
3. Net leverage confirmed at or below 3.0x on the company's own stated definition alongside growth capex guidance for 2027 falling below 60% of EBITDA — the point at which the dividend becomes FCF-covered.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the $5.7B low end, or FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 8% from $11.94.
2. Net leverage exceeding 3.9x on the company's stated basis, or any negative rating action from Moody's or S&P.
3. Growth capex raised above ~$5.0B for 2027 without accompanying contracted-EBITDA disclosure, which would signal capital being committed against unverified basin forecasts.
4. Confirmation that the 2026-07-17 Item 5.02 involved the departure of the CFO, President or a senior operating officer mid-program.

## Conclusion

The Q2 2026 print did what a bull needed it to do: it showed a predominantly fee-based midstream system setting an EBITDA record while the gas it processes carried a negative price. It also showed leverage improving to ~3.35x from ~3.48x despite $2.3B of new debt in six months, because EBITDA is still growing faster than the balance sheet. Both facts justify arresting the score decline from 5.8 to 5.9 rather than extending it, and the corrected Altman Z of ~2.75 removes a false distress signal from the record.

What the print did not do is make the shares attractive. At $269.95 the market pays 13.3x forward EV/EBITDA, 22.6x FY2027 consensus EPS, a 1.0% FCF yield and a 1.9% dividend covered ~55% by FY2025 free cash flow, for a business whose out-year consensus EPS was just cut 2% and whose consensus growth decelerates to +4.6% in 2027. Part of Q2's upside came from a 42% jump in condensate realizations tied to a crude premium and from a $41.2M hedge gain on a position that rolls off. The company commits ~$4.5B a year of growth capital against basin production forecasts it discloses it does not independently verify, and it funds the dividend and buyback with incremental debt. Insiders sold roughly $28M of stock into a 46% advance with no open-market purchases across 50 Form 4 filings, in a cluster five to six weeks after a January vesting event — routine post-vest diversification is the most likely explanation, and the CEO's retained 712,291 shares (~$192M) supports the benign reading, but no one on the inside was buying.

Own it for the EBITDA compounding. Do not add here. The price at which the risk/reward re-opens is $230–240.
