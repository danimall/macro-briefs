**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AptarGroup makes the high-value dispensing hardware (pumps, valves, elastomeric stoppers, metered-dose components) that sits on top of pharmaceutical and consumer-product containers. The Pharma segment (46% of sales, ~69% of segment EBITDA at a ~35% margin) is a genuinely good business: regulated, sticky, royalty-bearing, with multi-year qualification cycles and a real tailwind from GLP-1 and biologic injectables. The rest of the company (Beauty at 35% of sales, ~12% margin, and Closures at 19%, ~16% margin) is structurally lower-margin and competitive, and the consolidated growth engine has stalled. FY2025 core (organic) sales grew just +2%, and Q1 2026 core sales were flat, with the reported +11% headline driven entirely by an +8% FX tailwind and +3% from acquisitions.

The Q1 2026 print is the central concern. Operating margin fell to 10.9% from 12.8%, cost of sales rose to 64.2%, and all three segments saw EBITDA margin declines, including Pharma (33.3% from 34.8%) on a -10% core decline in prescription-drug sales attributed to difficult emergency-medicine comparisons. Adjusted EPS fell to $1.19 from $1.30 a year earlier, and FY2025 adjusted EPS declined ($5.74 vs $5.81) even as GAAP EPS rose 7%, flattered by a one-time $26.5M BTY remeasurement gain worth roughly $0.40/share. The underlying earnings engine is decelerating while buybacks and FX flatter the per-share optics.

The stock has fallen about 27% from its July 2025 high of $164 to roughly $120. The decline tracks the deterioration described above rather than presenting a mispricing to exploit. At ~20.5x trailing and forward earnings on flat-to-falling consensus estimates, ATR trades at a premium to its packaging peers (median P/E 16.4) without the organic growth or margin trajectory to justify it. The quality of the Pharma franchise, a 32nd consecutive dividend increase, and ample balance-sheet headroom support a HOLD, but the combination of stalled organic demand, margin compression, a CEO transition, and the ARS litigation overhang leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting the prior report's AVOID more than its earlier HOLDs.

## Company Overview

AptarGroup, headquartered in Crystal Lake, Illinois, employs roughly 14,000 people across about 20 countries and operates on a "produce where it's sold" regional model, with the majority of output outside the U.S. (Europe 49% of sales, Asia/LatAm ~20%, domestic ~31%). The company reports three segments:

- **Pharma (46% of sales):** Nasal spray pumps, MDI valves, injectable elastomeric components (PremiumCoat coated stoppers/plungers), active material science (Activ-Polymer), and digital health. Sub-markets: prescription drug 51%, consumer healthcare 20%, injectables 19%, active material science 10%. The crown jewel, at ~35% EBITDA margin and ~69% of segment EBITDA.
- **Beauty (35% of sales):** Fragrance, skincare, color cosmetics, personal and home care dispensing. The competitive, cyclical, margin-challenged segment at ~12% EBITDA margin.
- **Closures (19% of sales):** Dispensing closures for food, beverage, and personal/home care, ~16% margin.

The moat rests on precision injection molding (tolerances to 1/1000 inch), clean-room pharma manufacturing, deep customer co-development, FDA combination-product registration expertise, and geographic breadth. The customer base is highly diversified (roughly 5,000 customers, none more than 4% of sales), a genuine risk-mitigant relative to packaging peers reliant on a handful of CPG accounts.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Reported sales rose from $3.32B (FY2022) to $3.78B (FY2025), a +5% reported gain in the latest year. The composition matters more than the headline: FY2025 core sales grew only +2%, with +2% from FX and +1% from acquisitions. Pharma core +3%, Beauty core +2%, Closures core +1%. Q1 2026 reported sales rose +11% to $982.9M on flat core sales (+8% FX, +3% acquisitions, 0% organic). Organic demand has stalled, and the reported growth is currency- and M&A-driven. If the euro weakens against the dollar, reported growth could turn negative against soft organic volumes.

**Margins.** FY2025 operating margin was 13.3%, down from 13.8%, and net margin held at 10.4%. Cost of sales rose to 62.8% of sales from 62.2% on adverse mix and operational inefficiencies. The Q1 2026 deterioration was sharper: operating margin fell to 10.9% from 12.8%, cost of sales jumped to 64.2%, and EBITDA margins compressed across all three segments (Pharma 33.3% from 34.8%, Beauty 11.1% from 12.1%, Closures 13.1% from 15.8%). The Pharma decline is the most important, driven by lower high-margin emergency-medicine volumes (prescription-drug core sales -10%) plus weaker tooling margins and isolated operational issues. The mix that flatters Aptar's blended margin is the mix that is currently weakening.

**Earnings quality.** Per-share optics are supported by a shrinking share count (diluted shares 67.7M FY2024 → 66.7M FY2025 → 64.8M Q1 2026). The earnings track record on a beat/miss basis remains clean (four consecutive beats, the largest +5.1% in Q2 2025), but the beats are small (0.7%–5.1%) and sit against estimates that have themselves been trimmed (next-quarter consensus -4.9% over 90 days, next-year -1.9%).

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $570M (down from $643M on tax-payment timing), with FCF of roughly $300M and an FCF margin near 7.9%. Capex ran ~7% of sales (~$270M) and is guided up to $310–320M net for 2026, a headwind to free cash flow. The company returned $485.8M to shareholders in FY2025 ($120.8M dividends + $365M buybacks) and raised the dividend for the 32nd consecutive year. A new $600M buyback was authorized in February 2026, with ~$500M remaining at Q1. Q1 2026 buybacks were executed at roughly $141/share, well above today's $120; recent capital-allocation timing has been unfavorable.

**Balance sheet.** The Altman Z-Score of 2.22 sits in the grey zone, but this reflects a capital-intensive, leveraged structure rather than distress. On management's basis, Net Debt/Net Capital was 28.6% at FY2025 (30.0% at Q1 2026), the consolidated leverage ratio just 1.43x against a 3.50x covenant, and interest coverage 13.7x, with roughly $1.7B of additional borrowing capacity. Debt rose in FY2025 with the issuance of $600M of 4.75% senior notes (due 2031) against repayment of $125M of 3.6% notes, lifting both the quantum and the average rate; interest expense rose 20% in FY2025 and 49% YoY in Q1 2026. Goodwill of $1.08B is ~20% of assets, with $370.8M allocated to the structurally weak Beauty unit, which passed a 2025 quantitative impairment test with only a >10% cushion. That is the most likely future write-down candidate if European/skincare demand stays soft.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story splits cleanly. **Injectables is the bright spot** — core sales +11% in FY2025 and +20% in Q1 2026, driven by GLP-1, biologics, and antithrombotic elastomeric components. This is a real, durable secular tailwind tied to the obesity/biologics boom, and it is the most attractive vector in the portfolio. PremiumCoat coated stoppers and plungers are well-positioned for the shift to high-value injectable delivery.

Outside injectables, the picture is soft. Prescription-drug core sales fell 10% in Q1 2026 on difficult emergency-medicine comparisons, and emergency medicine is precisely the high-margin product line, so the volume decline hits both revenue and mix. Beauty core grew +2% in FY2025 against persistent low-cost Asian competition and weak European/skincare demand. Closures core grew +1%. Consolidated core growth does not support a premium growth multiple.

The estimate-revision picture is consistent with the deceleration: current-year EPS consensus is unchanged at $5.51 over 90 days, but next-quarter estimates are down 4.9% and next-year down 1.9%. There is no positive revision velocity here; the strong signal that would favor a more aggressive stance (>30% upward EPS revisions plus >30% revenue growth) is entirely absent. Growth prospects score 4.5: the injectables vector is genuinely good and prevents a lower mark, but it is not large enough to offset flat organic volumes and margin pressure across the rest of the business.

## Valuation Assessment

ATR trades at roughly 20.5x trailing and forward earnings (current price ~$120, FY EPS consensus $5.51). Against same-sector peers, this is a premium: Silgan 15.1x, Ball 16.4x, Greif 21.5x, with a peer median of 16.4x (O-I Glass is loss-making and not comparable). The dividend yield of ~1.6% is below the peer median of 2.1%.

The case for the premium is the Pharma/injectables quality differential: Aptar's blended margins and end-market exposure are superior to commodity glass and metal-can peers, and a quality premium is defensible. The case against is that the premium is paid for a business whose organic growth is flat and whose margins are compressing, while estimate revisions are flat-to-negative. This is the setup the calibration evidence warns against: a "reasonable" multiple is not a buy signal when there is no catalyst to re-rate it, and cheapness relative to fundamentals matters less than the trajectory of those fundamentals.

The stock's decline does not by itself create value. It tracks the deterioration in adjusted EPS and organic volumes; it is not a case of unchanged fundamentals being marked down. At a >30% drawdown threshold the burden of proof shifts to the bull case to identify what the market is missing, and here the sellers appear to be correctly pricing flat organic growth, falling adjusted EPS, and margin compression rather than overreacting. We do not see a specific mispricing.

Analyst targets span $140 (low) to $220 (high), with a $160 mean and $150 median, all above the current price. On a stock with falling near-term estimates and no inflection, we treat these as aspirational rather than as floors; the guidance that analyst targets behave as floors applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, which ATR is not. A modest unbooked upside exists from the February 2026 SCOTUS IEEPA tariff ruling, under which Aptar is pursuing refunds of previously paid duties as a gain contingency, but this is unquantified and not a thesis. Valuation scores 4.5: fairly valued to modestly rich for the current growth and margin trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

Aptar's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable in Pharma and stable-to-pressured in Beauty and Closures. In Pharma, the moat is real: clean-room manufacturing, regulatory expertise, multi-year qualification cycles, royalty streams, and 5,000-customer diversification with no customer over 4% of sales. The secular shift from non-dispensing to dispensing formats, and the GLP-1/biologics injectables wave, are genuine structural tailwinds the company is positioned to capture. In Beauty and personal care, low-cost Asian competition pressures pricing, though customers often prefer local suppliers for lead-time and supply security, and the segment's chronic ~12% margins reflect that the moat is thinner there.

The trajectory is the concern. A market leader losing margin and seeing core volumes go flat is a lower score than a challenger gaining share. Pharma remains a leader, but the high-margin emergency-medicine sub-line is contracting and is also the subject of the ARS litigation. Competitive position scores 5.5: a strong franchise whose near-term trajectory is flat-to-softening rather than improving. Per the calibration evidence, competitive position is a useful but recently weakened discriminator, so we do not over-weight the franchise quality against the deteriorating data.

## Risk Assessment

**Stalling organic growth + margin compression (most pressing).** If FX reverses, reported growth could turn negative.

**ARS Pharmaceuticals litigation.** Aptar sued ARS (March 2025, S.D.N.Y.) for trade-secret misappropriation; ARS counter-sued (September 2025, S.D. Cal.) alleging Sherman Act and Clayton Act violations for refusing to sell certain components. The procedural posture improved materially in Q1 2026: the court denied ARS's motion to dismiss Aptar's case in substantial part (March 2026), and ARS filed no antitrust counterclaim in the New York case, leaving the antitrust claim only in the separate California action that Aptar is moving to dismiss or transfer. The dispute sits squarely on the emergency-medicine product line (ARS's neffy epinephrine nasal spray uses Aptar's Unidose device). No loss is estimable; discovery has commenced.

**Nemera patent litigation (ophthalmic).** Parallel German (trial June 2026) and French (trial January 2027) suits. An EPO panel invalidated Nemera's main patent claim (October 2025) but allowed an amended claim; the validity picture is trending in Aptar's favor but unresolved.

**Concentration in single high-margin emergency-medicine products and royalty-cliff risk.** Pharma earns royalties under contracts with fixed expiration dates; loss on expiry hits high-margin revenue directly.

**Sole-source supply** for some Pharma resins/rubber, hard to remediate given validation timelines.

**Beauty goodwill impairment.** $370.8M allocated goodwill passed the 2025 test with only a >10% cushion against structurally weak and falling margins.

**FX and tariff exposure.** Euro-dominant translation is a large two-way swing factor; cross-border manufacturing leaves tariff exposure, partly offset by the unbooked IEEPA refund contingency.

These risks are partly but not fully priced. The margin compression and organic stall are visible in the data and reflected in the multiple compression; the ARS antitrust outcome and a potential Beauty write-down are genuine binary/idiosyncratic risks not fully priced. Risk factors score 4.5, consistent with the prior report; the calibration evidence treats this dimension as low-weight and recently anti-predictive, so we keep it as context rather than a swing factor.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 30.3% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 25.7% (a rich 4.6pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-month 30.3% > 6-month 26.2%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress around the next catalyst. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 2.31 from 1.06 over the past month while the price rose 4.3%: elevated put demand building into a higher price, a mild bearish/hedging signal. The 1-month implied move is ±6.1%; the 6-month put/call skew of -4.5% shows incremental downside protection demand at the longer tenor. The options market is not confirming a clean bullish setup; it is consistent with a stock carrying near-term event risk, which supports a wait-and-see posture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Pharma is a high-quality, regulated franchise with a genuine injectables tailwind (core +20% in Q1 2026 on GLP-1/biologics). Organic growth and margins normalize over 2026–2027 as emergency-medicine comparisons ease and operational issues resolve; FX provides a reported-growth tailwind; the $500M remaining buyback supports per-share earnings; the ARS litigation resolves favorably (procedural posture already improving); and the IEEPA tariff refund provides modest upside. Re-rating toward the high-teens P/E on a recovering $6.27 next-year EPS supports the analyst mean near $160. Bull case: ~$165 (~26x recovering injectables-led earnings plus litigation resolution).

**Bear case.** Organic growth stays flat-to-negative as FX reverses, turning reported growth negative; Pharma margins remain pressured by the emergency-medicine decline; Beauty goodwill is written down; the ARS California antitrust claim survives and creates a quantifiable liability; and the multiple compresses toward the packaging-peer median of ~16x on falling estimates. Bear case: ~$95 (~16x on a flat-to-falling ~$5.50–6.00 EPS), roughly 21% below the current price.

The base case sits between these: a fairly valued business at ~20x with flat-to-low-single-digit organic growth, margin stabilization but not recovery, and per-share support from buybacks. Base case: ~$120–125, essentially the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The injectables franchise quality and 32-year dividend record justify holding existing positions, but flat organic growth, margin compression, and unresolved litigation prevent a constructive growth or income call. This is not a confident hold; the evidence is genuinely two-sided.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic (core) sales growth re-accelerates above +3% for two consecutive quarters, with next-year EPS estimate revisions returning to positive territory.
- Pharma EBITDA margin recovers toward 35%+ as emergency-medicine comparisons normalize and operational issues resolve.
- Favorable resolution or dismissal of the ARS California antitrust action, removing the idiosyncratic legal tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 adjusted EPS misses the $1.32–$1.40 guidance range, or core sales turn negative.
- A Beauty goodwill impairment is announced, confirming structural deterioration.
- The ARS antitrust claim survives a motion to dismiss with a quantifiable damages exposure, or a material adverse ruling in the Nemera proceedings.
- Operating margin fails to recover off the Q1 2026 10.9% trough through 2026.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, on the guided $1.32–$1.40 adjusted EPS range), and the Nemera Germany trial (June 2026). The CEO transition (Gael Touya succeeding Stephan Tanda, with agreements dated March 16, 2026) is a watch item for capital-allocation and operational continuity.

## Conclusion

AptarGroup is a quality business going through a soft patch that the market is pricing correctly rather than overreacting to. The Pharma franchise, particularly injectables riding the GLP-1/biologics wave, is genuinely attractive and anchors the long-term case. But the consolidated picture in early 2026 is one of flat organic growth, broad margin compression, declining adjusted EPS masked by FX and buybacks, and a high-margin emergency-medicine line that is both contracting and entangled in litigation. At a premium to packaging peers on flat-to-falling estimates and with no inflection signal, the stock offers balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

We maintain HOLD, consistent with the declining score trajectory (6.0 → 5.4 → 5.4, with the prior report at AVOID). The drawdown from the 2025 high does not create value because it tracks real fundamental softening; new data confirm rather than reverse the deceleration. The overall score of 4.85 reflects a good franchise diluted by stalled growth, margin pressure, and idiosyncratic legal and impairment risks. We would become buyers on either a clean operational re-acceleration (core growth and margins recovering for two quarters) or a more meaningful valuation reset toward the mid-teens; we would move to AVOID on a guidance miss, a Beauty write-down, or an adverse ARS antitrust development.
