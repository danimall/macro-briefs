## Executive Summary

OGE Energy Corp is a vertically integrated electric utility serving ~913,000 customers across Oklahoma and western Arkansas through its sole operating subsidiary, Oklahoma Gas and Electric (OG&E). The company is in the early stages of structural load growth: commercial MWh sales surged 46% over 2023-2025, weather-normalized retail load is up 24%+ since 2021, and a 1 GW data center contract ("Customer X") is being finalized that could add another ~20% to system sales once connected after 2027. This load growth trajectory supports a $7.3B committed capital plan through 2030 with billions more in identified incremental opportunities, driving ~9% rate base CAGR and management's 5-7% EPS growth target.

The stock trades at ~20.3x FY2026E EPS of $2.43, roughly in line with multi-utility peers (median 20.6x), with a 3.6% dividend yield. The market appears to be pricing OGE as a standard regulated utility rather than one with among the strongest organic load growth profiles in the sector. Three specific risks temper the thesis: Moody's and S&P have both revised outlooks to negative citing capex pressure and wildfire exposure; the "no additional equity through 2030" claim likely breaks if Customer X and SPP transmission assignments materialize; and single-customer concentration in data center load introduces demand volatility uncommon for regulated utilities.

The investment case centers on the probability that OGE's load growth converts into sustained rate base and earnings compounding at 5-7%+ for the next 4-5 years, supported by constructive regulatory frameworks in both Oklahoma and Arkansas. At current valuation, the market is offering a utility with above-peer growth at peer-average multiples — a modest but identifiable mispricing.

## Company Overview

OGE Energy Corp, incorporated in 1902, is the holding company for Oklahoma Gas and Electric, the largest electric utility in Oklahoma. OG&E generates, transmits, distributes, and sells electricity across a 30,000-square-mile service territory covering Oklahoma City, Fort Smith (Arkansas), and surrounding areas.

**Generation portfolio:** 6,921 MW total capacity — 65.9% natural gas, 22.5% coal, 4.6% dual fuel, 6.5% wind, 0.5% solar. The fleet is predominantly gas-fired, though coal dispatch increased materially in 2025 (34% of generation, up from 18% in 2024) as natural gas prices rose from $2.64 to $3.91/MMBtu equivalent while coal costs declined.

**Regulatory framework:** Approximately 88% of revenue is regulated by the Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC), 7% by the Arkansas Public Service Commission (APSC), and 5% by FERC. Both states have enacted constructive legislation: Oklahoma's SB 998 provides CWIP recovery for new gas generation, and Arkansas's Act 373 enables CWIP recovery for "strategic investments."

**Strategic transformation:** OGE completed its evolution into a pure-play electric utility by selling its Enable Midstream equity position in 2022. The company now focuses entirely on regulated electric operations, eliminating commodity earnings volatility that previously characterized the business.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

Revenue grew from $2.61B (2023) to $3.19B (2025), a 22% increase driven by rate recovery of capital investments, new customer additions, and load growth. Operating income expanded from $650M to $799M over the same period, a 23% increase, demonstrating that revenue growth is translating to earnings with slight margin expansion.

Diluted EPS progressed from $2.08 (2023) to $2.32 (2025). The 2026 guidance midpoint of $2.43 implies ~5% year-over-year growth, with management targeting 5-7% consolidated EPS growth through 2030. The historical 5-year net income CAGR of ~7% supports the credibility of this range.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow reached $1.14B in 2025, up 40% from $813M in 2024. Capital expenditures of $1.05B consumed nearly all operating cash flow, leaving free cash flow of only $83M. This thin FCF is characteristic of a utility in heavy investment mode and will persist: the $7.3B capex plan averages ~$1.46B annually through 2030, up from the $1.05B spent in 2025.

The dividend ($1.6925/share, ~$349M annually) is covered by earnings but not by FCF, meaning the company relies on external financing to fund both investment and dividends. This is structurally normal for high-growth utilities but limits financial flexibility.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $14.4B support long-term debt of $5.37B and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.89x. Credit ratings are investment grade: A3/A-/A at OG&E (with negative outlooks from Moody's and S&P) and Baa1/BBB/BBB+ at the holding company. Liquidity of $868M under revolving credit facilities is adequate for near-term needs.

The FFO-to-debt target of ~17% through 2030 is thin relative to current rating levels. Moody's explicitly cited capex pressure and higher holding company debt in its April 2025 outlook revision. If Customer X and SPP transmission investments layer onto the existing plan, FFO-to-debt could compress below 15%, increasing downgrade probability.

### Financing

The November 2025 equity offering raised ~$397M (9.2M shares at ~$43), with $193M still under forward sale agreements. Management asserts this covers equity needs through 2030 under the current plan. However, the current plan excludes Customer X generation infrastructure, the SPP Seminole-Shreveport 765 kV transmission assignment, and RFP results for 1.9 GW of new capacity. Additional equity issuance of $300-500M is probable if even half of these incremental opportunities materialize, implying 3-5% dilution beyond what is currently modeled.

### Earnings Surprises

The four-quarter track record shows 2 beats, 1 in-line, and 1 miss. The Q1 2025 beat was substantial (+23.6%), while the Q3 2025 miss was minor (-1.2%). This mixed record provides moderate confidence in consensus estimates but does not indicate systematic conservatism by analysts.

## Growth Analysis

### The Load Growth Story

OGE's growth trajectory is anchored by organic load growth that is exceptional for a regulated electric utility:

- Weather-normalized retail load up **24%+ since 2021**
- Commercial MWh sales: 8.5M (2023) → 10.5M (2024) → 12.4M (2025) — a **46% increase in two years**
- Total system sales: 29.7M MWh (2023) → 33.6M MWh (2025) — **13% increase**
- Customer count growing ~1% annually, reaching 913,000
- 2026 projected load growth of **4-6%**, *excluding* Customer X

This commercial load surge is almost certainly driven by data center and large commercial buildout in Oklahoma, confirmed by the Customer X discussion and the broader trend of hyperscaler interest in low-cost power markets.

### Customer X — The Catalyst

The 1 GW data center contract represents the most material incremental growth catalyst. If connected, Customer X could add 6-8M MWh annually, roughly 20% of current system sales. The timeline extends beyond 2026, but the infrastructure investment (generation, transmission, and distribution) begins earlier and would flow into rate base.

The Oklahoma Data Center Customer Protection Act (HB 2992) passed the Senate Energy Committee unanimously in April 2026, requiring the OCC to create separate tariffs ensuring data center costs don't burden residential ratepayers. OG&E is proactively working on this, and the legislation's bipartisan support suggests regulatory alignment, but adds execution complexity.

### Rate Base Growth

The $7.3B committed capex plan through 2030 supports ~9% rate base CAGR. This comprises:
- Distribution: $3.76B (52%)
- Transmission: $1.33B (18%)
- Generation capacity: $600M (8%)
- Generation reliability: $900M (12%)
- Technology/fleet: $700M (10%)

Incremental opportunities (Customer X, SPP transmission, RFP results) could add $2-4B to this total, pushing rate base growth toward 10-11%, well above the utility sector average of 7-8%.

### EPS Growth Path

Management targets 5-7% consolidated EPS growth through 2030, supported by rate base growth partially offset by equity dilution and regulatory lag. FY2026E consensus of $2.43 and FY2027E of $2.60 imply 7% growth, consistent with the target range. If incremental capex materializes with constructive cost recovery, EPS growth could sustain at the upper end of the range.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.10, OGE trades at:
- **20.3x FY2026E EPS** of $2.43
- **18.1x FY2027E EPS** of $2.60
- **Dividend yield**: 3.6%

Against peer multi-utility medians of 20.6x P/E and 3.7% yield, OGE trades at a modest discount on both metrics. This discount is difficult to justify given OGE's load growth profile, which is among the strongest in the sector.

The analyst consensus mean target of $49.05 (median $51.00) implies 4-8% upside before dividends. The range spans $41 to $59, reflecting significant dispersion in how analysts weigh the data center opportunity versus credit/dilution risks.

A DCF-based framework using 5-7% EPS growth for 5 years, a terminal multiple of 18x (appropriate for a mature utility), and a 7.5% cost of equity suggests fair value of $50-54. Applying a 20x multiple to FY2027E EPS of $2.60 yields $52. Both approaches suggest ~10-15% upside from current levels, with dividends adding 3.6% annually.

The key valuation question is whether the market is appropriately pricing the data center demand trajectory. If Customer X converts and OGE secures constructive rate treatment, the company's earnings power in 2028-2030 could exceed current consensus by 10-15%, which would support re-rating to the upper end of the peer range (22-24x).

## Competitive Landscape

### Low-Rate Advantage

OG&E's electricity rates are among the lowest in the nation and the lowest in Oklahoma. The company has maintained O&M per-unit growth of less than 1% over the past decade. This cost discipline is the foundation of the company's competitive position: low rates attract commercial load (data centers are highly price-sensitive), maintain regulatory goodwill (making constructive rate outcomes more likely), and support customer growth.

### Oklahoma's Positioning

Oklahoma offers data center operators a compelling combination: low electricity costs, available land, favorable business environment, and growing grid capacity. OGE is the primary beneficiary of this positioning as the state's largest electric utility. The state's proactive approach to data center regulation (HB 2992) reflects political support for this economic development strategy.

### Generation Flexibility

The ability to swing between coal and gas based on fuel economics is a near-term operational advantage. In 2025, coal's share of generation doubled to 34% as gas prices rose, reducing fuel costs. This optionality provides earnings stability, though it increases environmental exposure.

### Competitive Trajectory

OGE's competitive position is improving. Load growth is accelerating, the regulatory framework is becoming more constructive, and the company's low-cost positioning is attracting incremental demand. The primary risk to competitive position is if a credit downgrade raises OGE's cost of capital, narrowing the rate advantage that underpins the growth story.

## Risk Assessment

### Credit Rating Pressure (High Impact, Moderate Probability)

A one-notch downgrade would not impair operations but would increase borrowing costs by an estimated 10-25 bps across the debt stack, reducing the spread between earned ROE and cost of capital. This risk is partially priced in through the peer-discount valuation.

### Equity Dilution (High Probability, Moderate Impact)

The stated "no additional equity through 2030" claim excludes Customer X, SPP transmission, and RFP-sourced generation. Additional dilution of 3-5% is probable, mechanically reducing per-share earnings growth by ~0.5-1.0 percentage points annually below rate base growth.

### Customer Concentration (Moderate Probability, High Impact)

Customer X's 1 GW commitment could represent 15-20% of system peak demand once connected. Data center demand can be volatile — crypto mining operations in particular have been unreliable utility customers. If Customer X reduces or cancels after OGE has committed generation and transmission capital, the stranded investment risk is significant. The separate tariff structure being developed would mitigate residential ratepayer impact but not the stranded asset risk.

### Wildfire Liability (Low Probability, Very High Impact)

S&P explicitly flagged wildfire risk in its outlook revision. Oklahoma has experienced increasing wildfire activity, and OGE's 30,000-square-mile service territory includes significant rural and semi-arid areas. While Oklahoma's legal framework for utility wildfire liability is less plaintiff-friendly than California's, this is a tail risk that could be catastrophic (as demonstrated by PG&E and Hawaiian Electric).

### Environmental Regulation

The Good Neighbor FIP carries potential compliance costs of $2.4-2.8B, though it is currently stayed. The 2024 EPA GHG rules require 90% carbon capture for coal plants operating past 2039, though EPA proposed repeal in June 2025. OGE's increased coal reliance in 2025 creates exposure if environmental rules tighten under a future administration.

### Estimate Revisions

Current quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 30.1% over 30 days (from $0.36 to $0.25), reflecting seasonal true-up rather than fundamental deterioration — Q1 is historically OGE's weakest quarter. Full-year FY2026 and FY2027 estimates are stable to slightly rising (+0.3% and +0.9% over 90 days, respectively), indicating no deterioration in the fundamental outlook.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.7% is dramatically elevated relative to 1-year historical volatility of 15.4%, a 26.3 percentage point spread. The term structure is in steep backwardation (41.7% near-term vs. 20.7% at 6-month), and put/call skew is elevated at 25.5%. This pricing structure indicates the market expects a near-term event — likely tied to the May 2026 earnings report and potential updates on Customer X regulatory filings. However, the very thin options volume (30 contracts across 3 expirations) suggests this pricing may reflect a single large hedge position rather than broad market conviction. The fundamental thesis is not altered by this options signal, but investors considering entry may benefit from waiting until the near-term IV premium resolves.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($54-59, +15-25%)
- Customer X converts on schedule, adding ~20% to system sales by 2029
- Regulatory treatment is fully constructive — separate tariff passes, CWIP recovery secured
- No additional equity issuance beyond current FSAs (management guidance holds)
- Rate base growth of 10-11% including incremental capex drives EPS toward $2.85-3.00 by 2028
- Market re-rates OGE from peer discount to peer premium (22-24x) reflecting above-average growth
- At 22x $2.60 FY2027E = $57; at 20x $2.85 FY2028E = $57

### Bear Case ($38-41, -13-19%)
- Credit downgrade raises cost of capital, compressing earned-actual ROE spread
- Customer X delays or downsizes, reducing growth profile to utility-average levels
- Additional equity dilution of 5-7% (not the guided 0%) reduces per-share earnings trajectory
- Wildfire event or environmental compliance costs crystallize
- Stock de-rates to lower end of peer range (17-18x): 17x $2.43 = $41

### Base Case ($50-53, +6-13%)
- Customer X proceeds with moderate delays; load contribution begins 2028-2029
- One additional equity issuance of $200-300M (2-3% dilution)
- Constructive regulatory outcomes with normal lag
- EPS growth of 5-6% annually, reaching $2.60-2.75 by 2028
- Multiple expands modestly from 20.3x to 20-21x as growth trajectory becomes more visible
- At 20x $2.60 = $52, plus 3.6% annual dividend yield

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $52 (20x FY2027E EPS of $2.60). 10% upside plus 3.6% dividend yield = ~14% total return over 12-18 months.

**Bull case target:** $57 (22x FY2027E or 20x FY2028E). Requires constructive Customer X regulatory filing by mid-2026, no additional equity offering announcement, and credit outlook stabilization.

**Bear case target:** $41 (17x FY2026E). 13% downside from current levels.

**Upside/downside from current ($47.10):** Base +10%, bull +21%, bear -13%. Reward-to-risk of approximately 1.6:1 on base case, improving to 2.4:1 if the data center thesis develops.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with key catalysts in the next 6 months: (1) Q1 2026 earnings and Customer X update (May 2026), (2) Oklahoma rate review filing (mid-2026), (3) HB 2992 legislative outcome (2026 session).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Credit downgrade at either Moody's or S&P (not just outlook revision — actual downgrade)
2. Customer X contract cancellation, material downsizing (below 500 MW), or delay beyond 2030
3. FY2026 EPS guidance reduced below $2.30 (implying growth deceleration to <0%)

## Conclusion

OGE Energy offers a differentiated growth opportunity within the regulated utility sector. The commercial load growth, the data center contract in development, and committed capital investment create an earnings compounding trajectory that the current 20.3x multiple does not fully reflect. The 3.6% dividend yield provides income support while the growth thesis develops.

The risks are real and quantifiable: credit outlook deterioration, probable additional equity dilution, single-customer concentration in data centers, and emerging wildfire exposure. These risks prevent a high-conviction sizing recommendation and explain the peer-discount valuation. But the balance of probabilities favors the growth thesis converting — Oklahoma's regulatory environment is constructive, the load growth is confirmed in hard data (not projections), and management's operational execution (sub-1% O&M per-unit growth over a decade) supports confidence in cost recovery.

At $47.10, the risk-reward profile is moderately favorable: ~10-13% upside to base case, 3.6% dividend yield, and specific near-term catalysts (rate filing, Customer X regulatory submission) that could trigger re-rating. BUY with 2-3% position sizing, recognizing that credit and dilution risks warrant position discipline rather than maximum conviction.
