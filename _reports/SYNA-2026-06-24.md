**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Synaptics is a fabless mixed-signal semiconductor supplier repositioning from its legacy human-interface (touchpad/touch-controller) roots toward an Edge AI plus wireless connectivity platform, anchored by the January 2025 acquisition of Broadcom's Wi-Fi assets and a new CEO (Rahul Patel, ex-Qualcomm/Broadcom) installed June 2025. The stock has roughly doubled off ~$74 lows in March 2026 to $135, driven by a Q3 FY2026 beat-and-raise, improving Core IoT growth (+30.8% YoY), and broad AI-narrative enthusiasm that is re-rating the semiconductor complex. The score trajectory (4.9 → 5.7 over the past quarter) reflects genuine fundamental improvement across growth, financial health, and competitive position.

The central analytical tension is the gap between the cash-generative business and the persistent GAAP losses. Synaptics produced ~$116M FCF in FY2025 (10.8% margin) while reporting a -$47.8M GAAP net loss, with the difference driven almost entirely by non-cash items: $115M+ of stock-based compensation over nine months of FY2026 and ~$95M of intangible amortization. The SBC, running near 10% of revenue, is a real economic cost that the non-GAAP framing the stock trades on understates. Management disclosed that excluding a customer-rebate release and IP-license revenue, the legacy Enterprise & Automotive segment would have declined year-over-year; the genuine growth is concentrated in Core IoT, much of which is the acquired Broadcom business rather than organic strength.

At $135 the stock now trades above the consensus mean target of $129.63 and above Northland's recently raised $123 target, with options markets in backwardation (near-term IV 81.1% vs. long-term 78.6%) and elevated put skew signaling imminent stress. The re-rating has been momentum- and narrative-driven; fundamentals have improved but not transformed. We maintain a HOLD. The growth engine and cash flow are real, but the price has run ahead of both the sell-side and the underlying earnings power, and chronic C-suite turnover plus a weak shareholder-alignment history cap the quality premium we can assign.

## Company Overview

Synaptics operates as a single reportable segment but discloses three product categories (Q3 FY2026):

- **Enterprise & Automotive (57% of Q3 revenue / $476.8M over 9 months):** PC touchpads, biometric fingerprint sensing (Natural ID), DisplayLink/DisplayPort video interface, and automotive touch/display. This is the mature cash core, where underlying demand is soft.
- **Core IoT (30% / $285.1M over 9 months):** Veros wireless (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/UWB/GNSS) and Astra AI edge processors. This is the growth engine, +30.8% YoY in Q3 and +40.8% in units over nine months, turbocharged by the Broadcom Wi-Fi asset acquisition.
- **Mobile (13% / $127.3M over 9 months):** OLED touch/display controllers for smartphones. Structurally declining, with ASPs down 11-12% and end-of-life shipments to a large U.S. customer rolling off.

The strategic thesis is "end-to-end AI IoT connectivity": pairing Astra compute, Veros connectivity, and human-interface sensing in integrated designs. The competitive assets are a portfolio of ~2,516 active patents, sticky multi-year OEM design-in relationships, and an asset-light fabless model. The leadership bench is now heavily populated with Broadcom and Qualcomm connectivity veterans, reflecting a deliberate bet to re-found the company around wireless and edge AI.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is cyclical and well below peak.** The trajectory: FY2022 $1,740M → FY2023 $1,355M → FY2024 $959M (trough) → FY2025 $1,074M (+12%). Q3 FY2026 came in at $294.2M (+10.4% YoY), with nine-month FY2026 revenue of $889.2M (+12.3%). The cyclical recovery is real but leaves revenue ~38% below its FY2022 peak.

**GAAP profitability is negative, but losses are narrowing and largely non-cash.** Q3 FY2026 operating loss improved to -$12.7M (-4.3% margin) from -$26.3M (-9.9%) a year earlier. FY2025 net loss was -$47.8M; nine-month FY2026 net loss was -$43.4M. The drivers are non-cash: SBC (up from $81.7M) and intangible amortization. Adjusted/non-GAAP profitability is materially positive; current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS sits at $4.61, with next year at $5.27.

**Cash flow generation is the bull-case anchor.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $142M with $116M FCF; nine-month FY2026 OCF was $81.8M. The business converts to cash despite GAAP losses. The qualifier: SBC near 10% of revenue is a recurring dilutive cost, so "FCF before considering dilution" overstates the economic return to existing shareholders.

**The balance sheet is leveraged but liquid.** As of March 2026: cash of $404.4M against $850M principal debt ($400M 4.00% 2029 Notes plus $450M 0.75% convertible 2031 Notes). The FY2025 refinancing repaid a $582M term loan (which cost ~$46M/year) with the 0.75% convert plus cash, cutting interest expense from ~$65M to ~$40M annually. Current ratio is 2.78; D/E is 0.85. The Altman Z-score of 1.98 sits in the grey zone, but this is a function of negative EBIT, not liquidity stress; the company is covenant-compliant with no near-term solvency concern. Goodwill ($872M) plus intangibles represent ~42% of $2.52B total assets, creating ongoing impairment risk (a $13.8M impairment was taken in FY2025).

**Capital allocation has been aggressive and well-timed.** Buybacks of $128M in FY2025 and $82.6M over nine months of FY2026 were executed at depressed prices ($74-92 in early calendar 2026), ahead of the recovery to $135. $67.4M remains on a $150M authorization. No dividend. The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent (March 2026) a +6.0% beat. Current-quarter EPS estimates were revised up +8.0% over 30 days, though current-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat over the same window; the upward momentum is concentrated in the near term rather than broadly carrying through the model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on Core IoT and the Edge AI/connectivity thesis. The Broadcom Wi-Fi acquisition adds Wi-Fi 7/Wi-Fi 8 combo, UWB, and GPS/GNSS IP, expanding the addressable market into AR/VR, Android smartphones, and consumer audio. The Edge AI theme is a genuine secular tailwind that the market is now rewarding.

Two cautions temper the growth score. First, the growth quality is uneven: management explicitly disclosed that, excluding a customer-rebate liability release (+$8.9M in Q3 alone) and IP-license revenue, the legacy Enterprise & Automotive segment would have declined YoY. The underlying PC and automotive demand is soft, so the consolidated +12% top line overstates organic momentum. Second, much of the Core IoT growth is M&A-driven rather than purely organic, and ~$96.8M of the Broadcom consideration remains a prepayment for roadmap technology not yet delivered (through May 2028), an execution risk on the very assets underwriting the growth narrative.

Mobile is in structural decline, which partially offsets Core IoT strength. The net effect is a real but moderate growth profile that would not, on its own, justify a premium multiple. We weight growth prospects as a positive but discount it from the 7.0 of the prior report given the disclosed cosmetic component in the legacy segment.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here given GAAP losses (reported -107.8x), so we anchor on forward non-GAAP earnings. At $135 against current-year consensus EPS of $4.61 and next-year $5.27, the stock trades at roughly 29x current-year and ~26x next-year non-GAAP EPS. Against the disclosed peer set, the medians are distorted by extreme outliers (POWI at 261x, PENG at 92x, with the median ~92x); these are not clean comparables. A more sober read places Synaptics at a forward multiple typical of a mid-cycle semiconductor recovering from trough, which is neither distressed nor cheap.

The valuation does not offer the asymmetry that would warrant a high score. At $135 the stock trades above the median target of $125 and above the most recent sell-side raise (Northland $123, May 8). On a high-momentum semiconductor name this proximity to targets would normally be a floor rather than a ceiling, but two factors argue against treating it that way here: the GAAP earnings remain negative, and analyst sentiment is characterized as degrading, with the hold count steady and no recent upward target revisions documented. The capped-call hedge on the 2031 convert protects to $150.48, and the stock now sits near that ceiling, a level above which incremental upside introduces convertible dilution mechanics.

We assign a valuation score of 4.0. The price reflects a re-rating to fair-to-full on improving but not transformed fundamentals, and the historical record across this system is that "cheap" sorts the wrong way while stretched multiples without commensurate growth carry downside. Synaptics is screening fully valued for the organic growth it is actually delivering once the rebate release and IP-license items are stripped out.

## Competitive Landscape

Synaptics competes against substantially larger, better-resourced players across fragmented fronts: NXP, Infineon, STMicroelectronics, and Ambarella in edge AI/MPU; Qualcomm, Broadcom, Espressif, and Nordic in wireless connectivity; and various vendors in touch/display controllers. Its differentiation is integration, combining AI compute, connectivity, and sensing in a single design, supported by a ~2,516-patent portfolio and sticky OEM design cycles.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The Broadcom Wi-Fi acquisition secures a credible wireless roadmap (Wi-Fi 7/8, UWB, GNSS) that the company explicitly frames as competitive coverage "for the next five years," and the Astra/Veros pairing gives a coherent edge-AI story. The research collaboration with Google's team on a next-generation Edge AI platform is a positive signal, though it is a research partnership in which Synaptics develops the silicon, not a guaranteed design win, and we treat its catalyst value conservatively.

The legacy core (PC touchpads, mobile OLED controllers) faces soft demand and structural ASP erosion, and the company's largest revenue region (China/HK $411.5M, Taiwan $270.5M of $889M over nine months) is directly exposed to China's domestic semiconductor push and U.S.-China trade barriers. The three-year cumulative TSR of 107.8 versus a peer-group TSR of 277.8 confirms chronic relative underperformance against semiconductor peers; the company is a challenger improving its hand, not an established leader extending a moat. We hold competitive position at 6.0, reflecting genuine improvement balanced by the soft legacy core and unproven integration thesis.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and geographic concentration.** Customer A is 18% of revenue (17% over nine months); top customers drive ~45-48% of receivables. Asia dominates billings, exposing the company to China demand softening and trade-policy shocks.

**Quality-of-revenue.** The disclosed reliance on a rebate release and IP-license revenue to keep the legacy segment from declining is a flag; these are non-recurring and inflate the reported growth rate.

**Memory shortage.** The 10-Q flags industry-wide tightness in memory chips used alongside Synaptics products, corroborated by Computex 2026 reports of an AI-driven memory/HBM shortage projected to persist. This can constrain customers' device builds and disrupt ordering.

**Tariff and geopolitical.** A proposed 100% U.S. tariff on imported semiconductors (announced Aug 6, 2025), combined with heavy Asia supply-chain reliance and Israel R&D exposure amid Middle East conflict, presents a tail risk. Management states current direct exposure is limited.

**Convertible dilution.** The 2031 convert can require cash settlement of principal on conversion; ~4.5M shares of potential dilution exist, with the capped-call hedge protecting to $150.48, a ceiling the stock is now approaching.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** With ~$1.1B of goodwill and intangibles and an acquisitive strategy, further impairment is a recurring risk.

We score risk factors at 4.0. After a ~2x run, the market is pricing the recovery, and the asymmetric downside risk from any execution or guidance disappointment is no longer cushioned by a low entry price. The 19.8% short interest with 4.2 days to cover represents meaningful institutional skepticism, providing some squeeze potential on positive surprises but also confirming a substantial bearish constituency.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a near-term caution. ATM IV at 81.1% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 55.3% (a 25.8-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation, indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, likely around the next earnings print. Put/call skew of 4.4% shows elevated put demand at the near tenor, and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±16.4%. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV rose 3.4 points while the price fell 10.4% and the P/C OI ratio nearly doubled (0.07 → 0.13). For a BUY thesis this divergence would be a clear warning; for our HOLD it reinforces that risk/reward near $135 is roughly symmetric and that a meaningful post-earnings move in either direction is being priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($165-180)
Core IoT sustains 30%+ growth as the Broadcom Wi-Fi roadmap delivers and the Astra/Veros edge-AI platform wins designs, while the legacy PC/auto cycle recovers organically and Mobile stabilizes. Non-GAAP EPS compounds toward $5.50-6.00, the stock re-rates to a clean ~30x forward multiple on durable mid-teens growth, and the Google Edge AI collaboration converts into a tangible volume win. The 19.8% short interest fuels a squeeze on a beat-and-raise. This reaches the analyst high of $180.

### Bear Case ($85-95)
The June-quarter guidance disappoints or the next print misses, exposing the cosmetic component of legacy-segment "growth" once the rebate release and IP-license items lap. Memory shortages constrain customer builds, China/tariff exposure pressures the largest revenue region, and the ~$96.8M undelivered Broadcom prepayment becomes an impairment or write-down. The momentum re-rating reverses toward the consensus low of $95 and below, a ~30%+ decline from current levels.

### Base Case ($120-130)
Synaptics delivers moderate growth with Core IoT carrying the model, legacy demand stabilizing slowly, and non-GAAP EPS reaching ~$4.60-5.30. The multiple de-rates modestly from the current ~29x toward the high end of the sell-side range as enthusiasm normalizes, settling the stock near the consensus mean/median of $125-130, slightly below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The growth engine, cash generation, and improving competitive position are real, but the stock trades above consensus targets on a partly cosmetic top line, near-term options stress is priced, and management/governance history weighs on the quality premium. The evidence does not support a confident call in either direction at $135.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A meaningful pullback toward the low-$100s (re-establishing a forward multiple <25x with the growth intact), OR
- Two consecutive quarters of organic (ex-rebate, ex-IP-license) Enterprise & Automotive growth turning positive, confirming the recovery is broad-based rather than narrative-driven, OR
- A confirmed, quantified design win from the Google Edge AI collaboration or Astra platform that materially expands the revenue base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A June-quarter guidance cut or a Q4 FY2026 miss exposing the cosmetic legacy growth, OR
- A goodwill/intangible impairment tied to the undelivered Broadcom prepayment, OR
- Renewed C-suite departures (continuation of the near-quarterly Item 5.02 pattern) signaling the strategic transition is faltering.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (June-quarter FY2026 results), the catalyst the options market is pricing a ±16% move around. The print is the natural decision point for confirming or reversing the re-rating.

## Conclusion

Synaptics has earned its score improvement: revenue is recovering off the trough, Core IoT is growing at 30%+, the refinancing materially reduced interest expense, FCF is genuinely positive, and the Broadcom-anchored connectivity strategy is coherent. The new data confirms the improving trajectory the prior two reports identified, but the stock has now run ahead of those fundamentals. At $135 it trades above the consensus mean ($129.63), above the median ($125), and above the most recent sell-side raise ($123); the organic growth is weaker than the headline once disclosed non-recurring items are removed; GAAP losses persist on ~10%-of-revenue dilutive SBC; the options market is in backwardation pricing imminent stress; and the governance record (chronic executive turnover, negative comp-actually-paid, three-year TSR of 107.8 vs. peers' 277.8) limits the quality premium. The risk/reward at this level is roughly symmetric.

We maintain HOLD with a base-case target of $120-130 (forward multiple normalizing toward the consensus range), a bull case of $165-180 (Core IoT durability plus a short squeeze), and a bear case of $85-95 (guidance disappointment exposing the cosmetic legacy growth). We would turn constructive on a pullback to the low-$100s or on confirmation that the legacy segment is growing organically.
