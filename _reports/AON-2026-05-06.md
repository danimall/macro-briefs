## Executive Summary

Aon is trading at $308-315, down 19% from its 52-week high of $381, creating an opportunity to own the #2 global insurance broker at an attractive valuation relative to peers. The discount has widened despite fundamentals that are stable to improving: Q1 2026 delivered 6.5% revenue growth, 70 bps adjusted margin expansion, and an EPS beat. Organic growth of 5% in Q1 2026 reflects a modest deceleration from 6% full-year 2025, though management reaffirmed mid-single-digit or greater organic growth guidance for 2026. The NFP acquisition integration is progressing (Moody's upgraded outlook to Positive, signaling confidence in deleveraging), and the Accelerating Aon United (AAU) restructuring is on pace to deliver $450M in annualized savings by 2027.

The stock's decline reflects macro concerns (rising rates compressing broker valuations broadly, with peers WTW down 26% and MMC down 29% from highs), as well as company-specific factors including the organic growth deceleration from 6% to 5% and lower fiduciary investment income. Estimate revisions are modestly negative (-0.2% over 90 days for FY2026), reflecting industry-wide caution. At this valuation, the market appears to be pricing minimal credit for: the remaining $180M in AAU savings not yet realized, continued mid-single-digit organic growth in a structurally favorable risk environment, and $1B+ in annual buybacks reducing the share count.

The key risks are genuine: $15.2B in debt, integration complexity (AAU cost creep from $1.0B to $1.3B), and a CEO compensation structure ($73.7M) that raises governance questions given peer-lagging TSR. These prevent a high-conviction call, but at current valuations they appear substantially priced in given the quality of the franchise and cash flow generation.

## Company Overview

Aon operates two segments serving complementary corporate needs across 120+ countries with approximately 60,000 employees:

**Risk Capital (66% of revenue, $11.3B):** Commercial insurance brokerage, reinsurance, insurance-linked securities, and risk consulting. This segment carries a 34.3% adjusted operating margin and benefits from both volume growth (client count) and rate-driven revenue lift in P&C markets where pricing has been modestly positive in commercial lines, though reinsurance market impact has been slightly unfavorable.

**Human Capital (34% of revenue, $5.9B):** Health benefits consulting/brokerage, retirement consulting, pension administration, and investment advisory. FY2025 adjusted operating margin of 32.2%, though Q1 2026 Human Capital adjusted margin declined to 34.4% from 35.3% year over year, reflecting mixed margin signals as the AAU program rationalizes overlapping capabilities from the NFP acquisition.

The business model is capital-light (capex only $263M on $17.2B revenue), fee-based with high retention rates, and generates recurring cash flows that support aggressive capital return. No single client exceeds 1% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $12.5B (FY2022) → $13.4B (FY2023) → $15.7B (FY2024) → $17.2B (FY2025). The FY2024 step-up was driven by the NFP acquisition (headline enterprise value of approximately $13.4B, with a GAAP purchase price of $9.1B including $3.2B cash and $5.9B in shares; closed April 2024). Organic growth was 6% for both FY2024 and FY2025, moderating to 5% in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 — a function of net new business wins, high retention, and modestly positive P&C pricing in commercial lines.

**Profitability:** Adjusted operating margin expanded from 31.5% (FY2024) to 32.4% (FY2025), with Q1 2026 at 39.1% (seasonally strong quarter). Management guides 70-80 bps annual expansion through 2027 as AAU savings ($270M realized of $450M target) flow through.

**Cash flow:** $3.2B FCF in FY2025 (+14% YoY) on only $263M capex. Based on FY2025 free cash flow and a ~$67B market cap, this implies approximately a 4.8% FCF yield — attractive for a business growing mid-single digits organically. (Note: Q1 2026 FCF was $363M, reflecting typical seasonality.)

**Balance sheet:** $15.2B total debt against $2.8B cash. Using gross debt against an estimated EBITDA of approximately $5.3B, the leverage ratio is approximately 2.9x. This is elevated but manageable given the predictability of cash flows and the deleveraging trajectory. Credit ratings: A- (S&P Stable), Baa2 (Moody's Positive), BBB+ (Fitch Stable). The Moody's upgrade to Positive outlook signals confidence in Aon's deleveraging and liquidity trajectory following the NFP transaction.

**Equity position:** Aon's stockholders' equity turned positive at year-end 2024 ($6.3B), largely aided by the share issuance in the NFP transaction, after being negative through 2023 due to $26.2B in cumulative share repurchases. Equity grew further to $9.4B at year-end 2025, driven by earnings accumulation and the NFP Wealth sale gain.

**Capital return:** $1.0B in buybacks during FY2025 (2.7M shares at avg $366). Q1 2026 repurchases were $500M, with approximately $0.8B remaining under current authorization. Dividend increased 10% in April 2026, the sixth consecutive year of double-digit hikes. Current yield approximately 1.0%.

**Q1 2026 specifics:** Revenue $5.03B (+6.5%), beating $4.97B consensus. The total revenue growth was driven by 5% organic growth, approximately 4% FX tailwind, and approximately 3% divestiture headwind. Adjusted EPS $6.48 vs. $6.37 consensus — a beat of approximately 1.7%. Guidance reaffirmed: mid-single-digit or greater organic growth, 70-80 bps margin expansion.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth drivers (mid-single-digit baseline):**
1. Commercial P&C market pricing remains modestly positive, though reinsurance market impact has turned modestly negative — the environment is supportive but not uniformly firming across all lines
2. Rising global risk complexity (cyber, climate, geopolitical) expands addressable market and cross-selling opportunities
3. NFP integration unlocks US middle-market access (management has highlighted a significant addressable market opportunity in risk and health)
4. Health care cost inflation drives demand for benefits consulting/optimization
5. New product development: Data Center Lifecycle Insurance Program positions for AI infrastructure buildout

**Margin expansion catalyst:** AAU program targets $450M in annualized savings by end-2027, with $180M remaining to be realized. Using a conservative 32% tax rate assumption (above Aon's recent 21% effective rate, to account for potential Pillar Two and other tax headwinds) on incremental income and ~213M shares, this contributes roughly $0.57/share in incremental EPS — meaningful against a ~$19 FY2026 base. At Aon's current effective tax rate of approximately 21%, the contribution would be closer to $0.67/share.

**Estimate revision context:** FY2026 EPS estimate has declined -0.2% over 90 days (from $19.09 to $19.05). FY2027 has declined -0.5% (from $21.50 to $21.40). These are modest negative revisions that appear to reflect industry-wide rate deceleration expectations. The four-quarter earnings beat streak (all positive) suggests estimates have been conservative.

**Growth limitations:** Organic growth is structurally mid-single-digit. The recent deceleration from 6% to 5% bears monitoring. The thesis rests on compounding (5-6% organic + margin expansion + buybacks = low-teens total return) at an undemanding valuation, not on growth acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current valuation:** At $308-315 and FY2026E EPS of approximately $19.05, Aon trades at approximately 16.2-16.5x forward earnings. On FY2027E of approximately $21.40, it trades at 14.4-14.7x.

**Peer comparison:** Based on available market data as of early May 2026, Aon trades at a meaningful discount to the peer group median. The broader insurance brokerage sector has experienced significant drawdowns from 52-week highs (peers WTW -26%, MMC -29%, AJG -41%), but Aon's relative discount to peers on a forward P/E basis appears wider than historical norms. Historically, Aon has traded at a premium to the broader market given its scale, FCF quality, and buyback-driven EPS growth.

**Why the market may be undervaluing Aon:** The current multiple implicitly prices limited credit for: remaining AAU savings (which should add ~$0.57-0.67/share by 2027), continued deleveraging (potential Moody's upgrade), and buybacks removing approximately 1.5% of shares annually.

**FCF yield support:** An approximately 4.8% FCF yield on a business growing mid-single digits with margin expansion provides valuation support relative to fixed-income alternatives. However, equity drawdowns can exceed what FCF yield alone implies if organic growth slows further, integration issues arise, or multiples compress.

**Analyst targets:** As of early May 2026, consensus analyst estimates suggest meaningful upside from current levels, though these are time-sensitive and should be verified against current market data sources.

## Competitive Landscape

Aon occupies the #2 position globally behind Marsh McLennan, with structural advantages:

**Scale and data:** Operating in 120+ countries with proprietary analytics platforms (Capital Insights Explorer, ReMetrica, PathWise) creates switching costs. The "Aon United" strategy connects risk and human capital data across the platform to offer integrated solutions competitors cannot easily replicate.

**NFP strengthens middle-market position:** The acquisition provides access to a segment where Arthur J. Gallagher and Brown & Brown have been gaining share. This expands the addressable market without cannibalizing existing large-account relationships.

**Competitive position is stable, not improving rapidly.** Aon's TSR of 174 (indexed) has underperformed its peer group TSR of 203 over the proxy measurement period. This market-relative underperformance may reflect valuation starting point, interest-rate sensitivity, capital structure effects, or market perception of execution — likely a combination of factors versus peers like Marsh McLennan, which has been rewarded with a premium multiple.

**InsurTech risk:** Management explicitly acknowledges "generative and agentic artificial intelligence" as a competitive threat. However, the complexity of large-account insurance placement (relationship-intensive, judgment-dependent) creates a natural barrier. The greater risk is in smaller-account commoditized business — exactly where NFP's middle-market focus adds exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and leverage (PRIMARY RISK):** $15.2B debt post-NFP. AAU cost creep (30% increase to $1.3B) signals complexity. If organic growth slows further or the integration creates client disruption, the leverage amplifies downside. However, FCF of $3.2B provides ample debt service coverage, and rating agency actions signal institutional confidence.

**Rate environment:** Higher-for-longer rates are mixed for Aon: positive via fiduciary investment income ($271M annually, though declining from $315M in 2024), but negative via sector-wide P/E compression and potentially slower economic activity reducing commercial insurance demand.

**Tax risk:** Pillar Two global minimum tax could pressure the 21.2% effective rate (Q1 2026: 20.2%). Management acknowledges "significant uncertainty." Based on FY2025 pretax income of $4.76B and approximately 217M diluted shares, each 100 bps of effective tax rate increase reduces EPS by approximately $0.22.

**CEO governance concern:** $73.7M compensation in FY2025 (up from $26.2M) with a 9.6x ratio to other NEOs and TSR underperformance versus peers is a governance red flag. This doesn't directly impair business value but signals potential misalignment and could attract activist attention or investor pushback.

**Macro sensitivity:** A recession could slow premium growth and delay hiring decisions that drive human capital consulting revenue. The business is relatively defensive (insurance is non-discretionary) but not immune to economic cycles. The deceleration from 6% to 5% organic growth may reflect early signs of macro softening.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($370-390, 20-27% upside):** Aon achieves FY2027 EPS of $21.40+ as AAU savings flow through, NFP integration drives cross-sell revenue, and organic growth returns to 6%+. Re-rates to 17.5-18.5x forward as deleveraging reduces risk premium. Buybacks provide additional per-share accretion. Catalyst: Q2/Q3 2026 earnings demonstrating organic growth stabilization or re-acceleration with margin expansion; potential credit rating upgrade from Moody's.

**Bear Case ($265-280, 10-14% downside):** Recession slows organic growth to 2-3%, NFP integration generates client attrition, rate environment turns soft across all lines, and AAU savings disappoint. Multiple compresses to 14x FY2026 earnings. Tax rate increases 200+ bps on Pillar Two. FCF generation and dividend growth provide valuation support, but this could result in 12-18 months of dead money below $300.

**Base Case ($355-375, 15-22% upside):** Mid-single-digit organic growth continues, AAU delivers on schedule, and the P/E reverts toward 18-19x FY2026 EPS as rate/macro concerns moderate and deleveraging progresses. 18.5x × $19.05 = $352 on current year; 17x × $21.40 = $364 on next year.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $360 (17% upside). Math: approximately 18.5x FY2026E EPS of $19.05, plus credit for partial FY2027 re-rating as AAU savings accelerate in H2 2026.

**Bull case target:** $385 (25% upside). 18x FY2027E of $21.40, achievable if integration execution impresses and Moody's upgrades to A3.

**Bear case target:** $275 (11% downside). 14.5x FY2026E if organic growth decelerates below 4% and margin expansion stalls.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q2 2026 earnings (late July), potential Moody's rating action (H2 2026), and AAU savings progression through year-end.

**Upside/downside from current ($308-315):** +15-25% to base/bull case; -10-11% to bear case. Risk/reward approximately 2:1.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic revenue growth declines below 4% for two consecutive quarters (would indicate client attrition or market deterioration)
2. Adjusted operating margin contracts YoY in any quarter (would indicate AAU savings failing to offset cost pressures)
3. Moody's reverses Positive outlook to Negative (would signal deleveraging failure)

## Conclusion

Aon represents an opportunity to buy a high-quality, capital-light compounder at a discount to both peers and its own historical valuation. The forward P/E of approximately 16.5x appears undemanding given 5-6% organic growth, expanding margins, and $3.2B in annual FCF. The primary risks (integration complexity, leverage, governance) are real but appear substantially priced at this level, as evidenced by Moody's Positive outlook and consistent earnings beats.

The catalyst path (AAU savings realization, continued organic growth, credit upgrades) plays out over 12 months rather than 12 weeks. The approximately 4.8% FCF yield and dividend growth provide valuation support, though this does not constitute a hard floor in adverse scenarios. The risk/reward at approximately 2:1 makes this an attractive entry point for investors willing to hold through near-term macro uncertainty.

Rating: **BUY** at $308-315 with a 12-month base case of $360 and bull case of $385.
