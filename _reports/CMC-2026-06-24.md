**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commercial Metals Company is the largest US producer and fabricator of rebar, operating a vertically integrated electric-arc-furnace (EAF) model that recycles scrap, melts it in mini mills, and fabricates long steel products for the early-stage construction sector. The investment case rests on two questions that the current financials cannot resolve cleanly: whether the sharp Q2 FY26 metal-margin recovery (North America steel margin of $623/ton, up 31% YoY) is durable, and how a binary ~$370M antitrust judgment now before the 9th Circuit resolves. Both sit on top of an aggressive capital-deployment year that deployed roughly $2.5B into a new precast-concrete platform, more than quintupled goodwill to $2.13B, and quadrupled quarterly interest expense to ~$41M.

The stock has appreciated ~44% over the trailing twelve months and trades at ~15.9x trailing EPS that is distorted by a one-time litigation charge, or roughly 11x consensus FY26 EPS of $6.49. That forward multiple looks optically cheap, but the cheapness is the kind that historically marks a value trap rather than a setup: current-year earnings estimates have been cut 9% (and current-quarter estimates 15%) over the past 90 days, the company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters (including an 11.9% miss in the quarter ended March 2026), and the headline forward EPS is flattered by a non-recurring 48C tax credit that cut the six-month effective tax rate to 7.6%. The Materials sector also carries a structurally negative alpha profile against the market, raising the bar for any company-specific BUY.

We maintain a HOLD. The metal-margin rebound and trade-policy tailwinds are real and visible in the data, but they are offset by the litigation binary, integration and leverage risk from the precast pivot, and the absence of a positive estimate-revision signal that would justify pressing the cyclical recovery. At ~$72, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; nothing in the current data warrants either an upgrade or a downgrade, but the deteriorating estimate trend and the unresolved PSG appeal keep us from leaning constructive.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1915 as a single Dallas scrap yard, CMC is a vertically integrated EAF steelmaker whose model captures margin across the chain: recycle scrap (roughly 98% of inputs are recycled), melt in EAF mini and micro mills, roll into long steel products (rebar, merchant bar, wire rod), and fabricate downstream. Fiscal year ends August 31.

Three reportable segments generated FY25 net sales of $7.80B:

- **North America Steel Group (~78%, $6.08B):** 42 recycling facilities, 6 EAF mini mills, 3 micro mills, 1 rerolling mill, 53 fabrication plants, concentrated in the Sun Belt with coast-to-coast reach.
- **Construction Solutions Group (~10%; renamed from "Emerging Businesses Group" in Q1 FY26):** Tensar geogrids/Geopier, CMC Construction Services, performance rebar (Galvabar, ChromX), CMC Bridge Systems, and the new precast-concrete platform (Foley + CP&P).
- **Europe Steel Group (~12%, $0.92B):** Vertically integrated mini mill, recycling, and fabrication in Poland; #2 in rebar/wire rod and #1 in merchant bar there.

The structural advantages are genuine: low-cost captive scrap feedstock, captive downstream fabrication demand that buffers import pressure, Sun Belt positioning in high-construction-demand markets, and modern lower-carbon EAF technology. CMC competes against larger, better-capitalized peers (Nucor, Steel Dynamics, Gerdau) and against imports.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY25 net sales were $7.80B, down 2% from FY24's $7.93B and well below the FY22 cyclical peak of $8.91B. FY25 net earnings collapsed to $84.7M ($0.74 diluted EPS), down 83% from FY24's $485.5M ($4.14). Two drivers explain the collapse: a $362.3M PSG litigation charge (~$274M after-tax) and North America metal-margin compression (NA steel metal margin fell to $509/ton from $534).

**The Q2 FY26 recovery.** The quarter ended February 28, 2026 produced net sales of $2.13B (+22% YoY) and net earnings of $93.0M ($0.83). The North America steel metal margin rebounded to $623/ton from $476 a year earlier (+31%), lifting NA Steel adjusted EBITDA +97%. Six-month FY26 net earnings reached $270.3M ($2.41) versus a prior-year loss of $(150.2)M. The recovery is real, but two caveats matter. First, the quarter still missed consensus: adjusted EPS of $1.16 fell short of the $1.30 estimate despite a revenue beat, and analysts cut estimates afterward. Second, the six-month EPS was flattered by a 48C Advanced Energy tax credit ($53.8M recognized in 1H FY26) that pushed the effective tax rate to 7.6%, a non-recurring boost that normalizes upward.

**Segment quality.** FY25 segment adjusted EBITDA: NA Steel $742.5M (−21%), Construction Solutions $137.7M (+6%), Europe $69.3M (+208% off a depressed base). Europe's profitability is unusually dependent on lumpy Polish energy/carbon government subsidies: $78.7M in FY25 but only $15.6M in 1H FY26 versus $48.1M prior-year, which hurt the segment. A meaningful slice of European profit is policy-driven and volatile.

**Balance sheet transformation.** Roughly $2.5B of M&A reshaped the balance sheet. Total assets rose to $9.56B (Feb 28, 2026) from $7.17B (Aug 31, 2025). Goodwill ballooned to $2.13B from $387M (now ~22% of total assets, up from ~5%); intangibles rose to $496M. Long-term debt jumped to $3.31B from $1.31B via $1.0B 5.750% notes (2033) and $1.0B 6.000% notes (2035) issued November 2025 to fund Foley. Cash fell to $495M from $1.04B. Gross debt/equity is ~0.75x; net debt ~$2.9B. Quarterly interest expense quadrupled to $40.9M (~$160M+ annualized). The revolver was upsized to $1.0B (undrawn) and covenants are met.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** 1H FY26 operating cash flow was $370.5M, up from $245.5M. FY26 capex is guided to ~$600M, driven by the West Virginia micro mill. The dividend (244th consecutive quarterly, $0.18/share, ~1.1% yield) is well-covered. Buybacks slowed to $57.2M in 1H FY26 from $98.4M a year earlier as cash was redirected to M&A; $147.8M of authorization remains.

The financial-health picture is mixed: ample liquidity and covenant compliance, strong operating cash flow, but materially higher leverage, a more than quintupling of goodwill, quadrupled interest expense, and a ~$370M contingent litigation liability sitting as a current liability. This warrants a mid-range financial-health score rather than a strong one; the capital structure has moved from conservative to leveraged at a cyclical, margin-volatile company.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two components. The cyclical component, metal-margin recovery, is driven by Section 232 tariffs (now 50% ex-UK) and antidumping cases that CMC itself petitioned for (March 2026 Commerce preliminary affirmative determinations on rebar from Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, and Vietnam; an Algeria countervailing rate of 72.94%). These tailwinds support domestic rebar pricing.

The structural component is the precast pivot. CMC deployed CP&P ($675M cash, closed Dec 1, 2025) and Foley ($1.84B debt-funded, closed Dec 15, 2025) to build a precast-concrete platform diversifying toward less import-exposed, project-driven products with cross-selling potential into the reinforcement franchise. Early contribution is modest (Foley $6.4M pretax; CP&P a small loss since closing), and the integration is into a newer industry for CMC. The West Virginia micro mill #4 (~$550-600M, novel continuous-process technology) is expected to reach melt-shop production in 2026, adding domestic capacity.

The signal that matters most for growth scoring is the estimate-revision trend, and here the picture is cautionary. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 15.0% over 90 days; current-year estimates fell 9.0% (to $6.49); next-year fell 4.0% (to $7.01). The most recent 30-day moves are modestly positive (+1.8% current year, +1.2% next year), suggesting the worst of the downgrades may have passed, but the 90-day trend is clearly negative. Combined with three EPS misses in four quarters, this argues for a measured rather than enthusiastic growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$72, CMC trades at ~15.9x trailing EPS (distorted downward by the PSG charge) and ~11x consensus FY26 EPS of $6.49. The forward multiple is optically inexpensive, and on a pure cycle-adjusted basis normalized mid-cycle earnings power (closer to the FY24 $4.14 or the 1H FY26 annualized run-rate) supports a higher price. But several factors prevent us from treating the cheapness as a buy signal.

First, the peer comparison is unhelpfully noisy. The Steel peer median P/E of 36.0 is distorted by negative and outlier earnings (CLF −6.0x, AMR −63.0x, HCC 36.0x), so a clean relative-value read is hard to construct. CMC's ~11x forward is below most rationally-earning peers, but steel multiples compress at cycle peaks precisely because the market discounts that peak earnings are unsustainable.

Second, the forward EPS itself is being revised down, not up, so the multiple looks cheap partly because the denominator is falling. A cheap multiple on a falling estimate base is the classic value-trap configuration, and history shows cheapness in this sector sorts the wrong way without an independent catalyst.

Third, normalizing the 48C-flattered tax rate raises the effective forward multiple.

Analyst targets (low $68, mean $81, median $82, high $89) imply ~12% upside to the mean from ~$72. On a high-momentum inflection stock with rising estimates, those targets would be floors. Here, with current-year estimates falling over 90 days and the stock 15% below its 52-week high of $84.87, the targets are more likely stale-to-fair than conservative. We score valuation at the midpoint: the absolute multiple is not stretched, but there is no identifiable mispricing the market is missing, and the binary litigation overhang caps any re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

CMC's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. Vertical integration and Sun Belt positioning are durable structural advantages, and the current trade environment is a meaningful tailwind for domestic rebar pricing, visible directly in the Q2 metal-margin rebound. CMC is the largest US rebar producer and #1 merchant-bar producer in Poland, genuine leadership positions.

The trajectory is balanced rather than clearly positive. On the favorable side, the precast pivot diversifies the revenue base toward project-driven construction products and creates cross-selling potential. On the cautionary side, the long-steel market faces new domestic EAF capacity coming online over the next 1-3 years (industry-wide overcapacity risk that could compress the very margins now recovering), persistent import exposure, and competition from larger, better-capitalized peers (Nucor, Steel Dynamics). The trade-policy tailwind is favorable but reversible; if tariffs are relaxed, challenged, or expire, import pressure on rebar prices would resume. We score competitive position modestly above neutral, reflecting durable structural advantages against a balanced trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**PSG antitrust litigation (the dominant company-specific risk).** A November 2024 jury verdict of $110M was trebled to $330M; with fees and post-judgment interest, CMC accrued $362.3M, now a $369.7M current liability. The post-trial motion was denied (Sept 29, 2025), and CMC has appealed to the 9th Circuit (notice filed Oct 24, 2025). Management states explicitly that, unless overturned or reduced, the judgment "would have a material adverse effect on liquidity and financial condition." A second PSG suit (San Diego, ~$29M subject to trebling) had summary judgment denied with no accrual. CMC amended its Credit Agreement on Oct 31, 2025 so certain monetary judgments will not trigger an event of default, a defensive move directly tied to this exposure. The appeal outcome is a genuine binary: reversal releases ~$370M back to earnings/equity; affirmation requires a material cash payment.

**Integration and leverage risk.** ~$2.5B deployed into a new industry. Goodwill plus intangibles now ~$2.6B; deterioration could trigger impairment. Leverage and interest costs rose materially at a margin-volatile company. Executive turnover (Item 5.02 departures on Nov 4, 2025 and Apr 13, 2026; CHRO departed) is worth monitoring during integration.

**Cyclicality.** Earnings hinge on the scrap-to-steel spread. Fixed-price downstream contracts (1-2 year duration) can compress margins when input costs rise mid-project, as FY25 demonstrated.

**Trade-policy dependence.** The favorable tariff/antidumping backdrop is a tailwind that could reverse.

**Europe subsidy and FX dependence.** Polish segment profitability is unusually reliant on lumpy, declining government energy subsidies and zloty FX.

These risks are largely specific, partly binary, and not fully priced; the PSG appeal in particular is a catalyst the market cannot resolve in advance. This supports a below-neutral risk-factors score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$89, ~+24%).** The metal-margin recovery proves durable as Section 232 tariffs and antidumping rulings hold, NA steel margins sustain above $600/ton, the precast platform contributes meaningfully as integration completes, the WV micro mill ramps on schedule, and the 9th Circuit reverses or materially reduces the PSG judgment, releasing reserves. Normalized EPS power approaches $7-7.50, and the multiple re-rates toward mid-teens. This matches the analyst high target of $89.

**Bear case (~$56, ~−22%).** New domestic EAF capacity and any softening in the construction cycle compress metal margins back toward FY25 levels; the 9th Circuit affirms the judgment, forcing a material cash outflow at a now-leveraged company; precast integration disappoints and goodwill is impaired; and the 48C-flattered tax rate normalizes, pressuring reported EPS. The stock de-rates toward the FY25-trough valuation.

**Base case (~$78, ~+8%).** Margins hold near current levels but do not expand further; the PSG appeal remains unresolved, capping the multiple; precast contributes modestly; estimates stabilize near the current $6.49/$7.01 path. The stock drifts toward the lower analyst targets. This sits between the bull and bear scenarios and reflects roughly symmetric risk/reward at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY26 print (reported late June 2026) is the immediate catalyst, with the 9th Circuit PSG appeal the dominant medium-term swing factor (timing uncertain, likely 6-18 months). Next natural reassessment is the Q3 release and any appellate scheduling news.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A 9th Circuit reversal or material reduction of the PSG judgment, removing the binary overhang and releasing reserves.
- Estimate revisions turning durably positive (current-year EPS revised up >5% over a 90-day window) alongside sustained NA metal margins above $600/ton.
- Evidence the precast platform is contributing materially (segment EBITDA accretion) without goodwill impairment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A 9th Circuit affirmation forcing a material cash payment that strains liquidity or covenants.
- NA metal margins reverting toward FY25 levels (~$509/ton) on new-capacity or construction-demand weakness.
- A goodwill impairment on the precast acquisitions, or further executive departures signaling integration distress.

## Conclusion

CMC offers a real cyclical recovery: the Q2 FY26 metal-margin rebound and the trade-policy tailwinds are visible in the numbers and supported by company-specific antidumping wins. But the recovery is not yet confirmed by estimate revisions, the company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters, the forward EPS is flattered by a non-recurring tax credit, and the entire setup is gated by a binary ~$370M antitrust appeal and a ~$2.5B debt-funded pivot into a new industry. At ~11x forward earnings the stock is optically cheap, but the cheapness rests on a falling estimate base and an unresolved litigation overhang, the configuration that historically rewards caution rather than conviction.

We maintain **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the modestly declining score trajectory (5.9 → 5.4) reflects rising leverage and the deteriorating 90-day estimate trend rather than any price-driven thesis change. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric at ~$72; we would turn constructive on a favorable PSG resolution paired with positive estimate revisions, and turn negative on an adverse ruling or a margin reversion.
