## Executive Summary

Teleflex is roughly halfway through cutting itself in half. On December 9, 2025 the company signed two definitive divestiture agreements — OEM to Montagu and Kohlberg for ~$1.5bn, and Acute Care plus Interventional Urology to Intersurgical for ~$530m — against total guided net after-tax proceeds of ~$1.8bn. The OEM leg **closed August 3, 2026** (Item 2.01 8-K; the business was relaunched as INGENYX). The Intersurgical leg was guided to 2H 2026 and we found no filing confirming completion. Simultaneously the company authorized a $1.0bn share repurchase, none of which had been executed as of March 31, 2026, and stated an intent to retire ~$800m of debt.

The arithmetic behind the bull case is straightforward and mostly mechanical. Management guides 2026 adjusted diluted EPS from continuing operations to $6.25-$6.55 — a **down year**, −6.2% to −10.5% — explicitly excluding any divestiture-funded buyback or debt paydown. Consensus for next year sits at $10.58, which is where the 12.9x forward multiple comes from. Rebuilding that number from disclosed inputs: $6.40 midpoint × 44.2m shares = $283m adjusted net income; add ~$34.6m after-tax interest savings on $800m retired at ~5% and a 13.5% tax rate; add roughly half the $90m stranded-cost gap; add modest operating growth; divide by ~37.3m shares after a fully executed $1.0bn buyback at ~$140. That produces ~$10.15. Consensus is reachable, but only with the buyback, the debt paydown, the second divestiture close, and material stranded-cost recapture all delivered.

Each of those is individually plausible and none is confirmed. Meanwhile the evidence in hand is mixed to poor: continuing-operations free cash flow of **$1.4m** in FY2025 (operating cash flow $96.7m less capex $95.2m) against $60.3m of dividends paid, funded by the businesses being sold; adjusted gross margin down 470bp and adjusted operating margin down 510bp year over year in Q1 2026; EMEA segment operating margin collapsing from 15.0% to 3.1% and Asia from 22.6% to 6.6% as the BIOTRONIK VI acquisition lands; and near-term consensus cut 7.0% (current quarter) and 9.4% (next quarter) over 90 days. The stock closed 5.1% below its 52-week high of $144.19 and 36.5% above its January low of $100.18. Our base-case fair value of ~$141 sits 3% above spot against a bear case 22% below. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Teleflex designs and manufactures single-use devices for high-acuity, emergent and interventional procedures. Founded 1943 as a maker of mechanical push-pull controls for military aircraft, it became an exclusively medical-device business in 2011. It reports through three geographic segments — Americas, EMEA, Asia — while managing commercially by product category. At December 31, 2025 it employed ~15,500 people, with 76% of full-time employees and 70% of net PP&E outside the United States.

The retained portfolio, on Q1 2026 revenue:

| Category | Q1 2026 revenue | Share | Q1 pro forma adj. cc growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vascular Access & Emergency Medicine | $236.8m | 43% | +4.8% |
| Interventional | $204.7m | 37% | +3.0% |
| Surgical | $106.8m | 19% | +9.9% |

Vascular covers Arrow central venous and specialty catheters, EZ-IO intraosseous access, Arrow FAST1 and QuikClot hemostats. Interventional covers GuideLiner/Turnpike/TrapLiner guide-extension catheters, MANTA vascular closure, Arrow OnControl, plus the acquired BIOTRONIK VI portfolio of drug-coated balloons, coronary and peripheral stents and balloon catheters. Surgical covers Weck ligating clips, MiniLap, Pleur-Evac, Deknatel, Pilling, KMedic and the Titan SGS bariatric stapler.

Most products are Class I/II cleared via 510(k) or exempt. The 10-K states explicitly that backlog is not indicative of future results because product ships within days or weeks — there is no contracted-revenue visibility to underwrite. A specific risk factor flags that a significant portion of U.S. revenue comes through distributors and is exposed to destocking.

## Financial Analysis

### The reported loss is a writedown, and the writedown is the information

FY2025 headline net loss was $(905.6)m. That decomposes cleanly: continuing-operations pretax income of $24.553m plus a $33.977m tax benefit gives **income from continuing operations of $58.5m**; discontinued operations lost $(964.2)m. The two sum to the reported figure within rounding, and cross-check against retained earnings falling $966.1m (net loss $905.6m + $60.3m dividends).

The discontinued loss came from a held-for-sale valuation allowance plus goodwill impairments at Acute Care and Interventional Urology North America. The Q1 2026 10-Q quantifies the allowance at **$747.070m** at 12/31/2025, rising to **$776.072m** at 3/31/2026 on reassessment of fair value less costs to sell. Against gross held-for-sale assets of ~$1,865.7m, that is a ~41.6% markdown. Layered on a $240.0m non-deductible goodwill impairment at Interventional Urology North America in FY2024, the record documents substantial capital destruction in that franchise. Management separately expects a **gain** on the OEM leg, so the loss is concentrated in Acute Care/IU rather than distributed across the disposal group.

The practical implication: earnings-based screens keyed to net income are mechanically depressed by non-cash items. Recomputing the classic Altman Z with market equity of ~$6,046m (44.2m shares × $136.78) against total liabilities of $3,822.5m gives **Z ≈ 2.13**, or ~2.30 adding back discontinued revenue to the sales/assets term. Grey zone, driven by a goodwill-and-intangibles-heavy balance sheet (56% of total assets) mid-restructuring rather than solvency stress.

### Margin compression is real and multi-causal

Continuing-operations GAAP gross margin: 61.0% (FY2024) → 56.2% (FY2025) → 56.1% (Q1 2026). On the company's adjusted basis, Q1 2026 gross margin was 61.4% versus 66.1%, and adjusted operating margin 18.1% versus 23.2%. Management attributes the decline to tariffs, the VI business's inventory step-up and intangible amortization plus its structurally lower gross margin, quality remediation charges tied to a third-party supplier disruption, higher logistics costs, and excess-and-obsolete inventory charges.

Q1 2026 produced the first continuing-operations quarterly loss in this dataset: $(4.838)m, or $(0.11) per diluted share, versus $52.334m and $1.14 a year earlier. The externally reported $1.39 "beat" was non-GAAP against non-GAAP consensus — a legitimate comparison, but not the same statement as GAAP profitability.

### Segment profitability outside the Americas

This is the most underweighted item in the file:

| Segment | FY2024 rev | FY2024 op profit | Margin | FY2025 rev | FY2025 op profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $1,156.9m | $426.5m | 36.9% | $1,279.2m | $469.2m | 36.7% |
| EMEA | $340.3m | $50.9m | 15.0% | $472.4m | $14.6m | **3.1%** |
| Asia | $202.3m | $45.8m | 22.6% | $241.1m | $16.0m | **6.6%** |

EMEA revenue grew 38.8% while operating profit fell 71.4%; Asia revenue grew 19.2% while operating profit fell 65.1%. VI contributed $102.3m in EMEA, $51.1m in Asia and $49.0m in the Americas — weighted precisely to the two segments that deteriorated. Segment profit is measured before unallocated corporate costs and before restructuring/impairment (verified against the Q1 2026 reconciliation: $158.466m segment profit − $121.438m unallocated − $16.845m restructuring = $20.183m, equal to reported continuing EBIT), so this is not an overhead-allocation artifact.

The honest caveat: we could not verify whether acquisition intangible amortization and inventory step-up are allocated to segments or held at corporate. If allocated, some of the collapse is purchase accounting rather than economics. China volume-based procurement pricing and tariffs on EU-manufactured product independently pressure the same two segments.

### Cash flow is the weakest link

Continuing-operations operating cash flow: $206.1m (FY2023) → $301.9m (FY2024) → **$96.7m (FY2025)**. Against $95.2m of capex, **FY2025 continuing free cash flow was $1.4m**. Discontinued operations provided $207.5m of net cash — the businesses being sold funded the retained business's shortfall and the $60.3m dividend. Management attributes the $205.2m OCF decline to weaker results and working capital, notably a receivables build because Teleflex did not acquire certain VI trade receivables, absence of a prior-year pension-termination inflow, tariffs and integration spend.

Q1 2026 continuing FCF was $46.662m − $18.791m = $27.871m. A ×4 annualization gives ~$111m, which we treat only as a sensitivity: revenue is seasonally strongest in Q4; Q1 carried $30.1m of separation costs and $17.1m of restructuring termination benefits; the $25.0m patent settlement lands in Q2; and $15-19m of cash restructuring is guided for 2026. A normalized build — ~19% adjusted operating margin on $2,290m ($435m), less ~$105m cash interest, less ~13.5% cash taxes, less ~$80m capex, less restructuring — points to roughly **$150-190m of 2026 free cash flow before separation costs**. That is our estimate, not guidance.

A separate cash drain bypasses operating cash flow: cross-currency swap settlements consumed a net **$53.494m** in Q1 2026 (versus a $21.1m receipt in FY2025). The risk factors quantify the tail — if the dollar has declined 10% from inception rates at expiration, Teleflex owes ~$100m on euro swaps or ~$60m on Swiss franc swaps.

### Balance sheet and the 2027 wall

At 3/31/2026: total assets $6,783.0m; goodwill $2,297.4m plus net intangibles $1,485.9m (56% of assets); equity $3,084.4m; total debt $2,617.4m ($103.1m current + $2,514.3m long-term); cash $309.4m continuing plus $39.4m inside held-for-sale assets. **Net debt ≈ $2,268-2,308m.** The FY2025 risk factors cite total consolidated indebtedness of $2.7bn on a broader definition.

Composition: $500m 4.625% notes due 2027; $500m 4.25% notes due 2028; a $700m delayed-draw term loan and $425m drawn revolver **both maturing November 4, 2027**; and a fully drawn $75m receivables securitization. Covenants — maximum 4.50x total net leverage, minimum 3.50x interest coverage — were in compliance at 12/31/2025.

FY2025 GAAP continuing operating income of $118.4m against $100.2m interest expense is **1.18x** coverage. That is not the covenant metric (which is consolidated with add-backs and includes businesses still owned), and FY2025 carried $137.4m of restructuring, impairment and separation charges. Forward-looking, the guided ~19% margin on $2,290m ($435m) against guided $105m interest gives ~4.1x. The FY2025 figure is a signal of how thin GAAP earnings were relative to debt service, not evidence of covenant risk.

The 2027 wall is being addressed. A $500m senior notes private offering was launched and priced on or about June 1, 2026 (corroborated by 8-K Items 7.01/8.01 on 6/1 and 6/2, and Items 1.01/2.03 on 5/27 and 6/15). **Coupon, maturity and final size are unverified** — we could not retrieve the release text or exhibit. Management "expects to refinance our Senior Credit Facility prior to maturity."

### Proceeds allocation

~$1.8bn guided net after tax across both legs, against $800m of debt paydown plus a $1.0bn buyback — fully allocated, with nothing for reinvestment or balance-sheet cushion. With only OEM closed, ~$1.35bn of net proceeds (applying the guided net-to-gross ratio to $1.5bn) covers the debt paydown plus roughly half the authorization. **Full buyback execution requires the Intersurgical close.**

Pro forma net debt after $800m of paydown ≈ $1,508m. Against continuing-operations adjusted EBITDA we derive at $476-515m (Q1 adjusted operating income of 18.1% × $548.262m = $99.2m annualized to $397m plus $79.4m annualized depreciation; or guided ~19% on $2,290m = $435m plus ~$80m), pro forma leverage is ~2.9-3.2x versus ~4.5-4.8x on the same continuing-ops-only basis today.

## Growth Analysis

FY2025 continuing revenue grew 17.2% (+$293.2m), decomposing per MD&A into +$202.4m from acquired VI, +$35.7m existing-product volume, +$24.2m new products, and +$15.2m from a favorable Italian payback reserve adjustment. **Organic growth ex-acquisition and ex-Italy was ~$60m, or 3.5% on the FY2024 base.**

2026 guidance: GAAP continuing revenue growth +14.4% to +15.4% ($2,280-2,300m); pro forma adjusted constant-currency growth **+4.5% to +5.5%**; adjusted operating margin ~19.0%; adjusted diluted EPS $6.25-$6.55, or −6.2% to −10.5%. Stated assumptions include $90m of annualized stranded costs, $105m interest, ~13.5% tax, no material change in share count, and **no** divestiture-funded paydown or buyback. Management frames 2027 as the first "representative view of our standalone, steady-state financial profile."

Category detail is not encouraging where it matters most. Interventional — 37% of revenue and the segment Teleflex just spent $825.2m to expand — grew **+3.0%** pro forma constant-currency in Q1, below the corporate 4.5-5.5% guide, with management citing "further integration of the Biotronik Vascular Intervention business" and pointing to "improving momentum in the second half of 2026" from cath-lab cross-selling. Surgical's +9.9% was driven by instrument order timing that management explicitly said is lumpy and guided to moderate in Q2. Vascular's +4.8% is the durable number.

The estimate revision picture confirms near-term deterioration with out-year hope:

| Period | Current | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $1.28 | +1.2% | **−7.0%** |
| Next quarter | $1.81 | −1.3% | **−9.4%** |
| Current year | $6.67 | −0.5% | −0.8% |
| Next year | $10.58 | +0.3% | +2.1% |

The +30%-in-90-days revision signal that flags genuine earnings inflection is absent. What is present is a 7-9% cut to the next two quarters against a 2.1% lift to the out year — consensus marking down the transition and marking up the destination. R&D at 7.3% of revenue (up from 6.4%) is respectable for the category and supports the new-product contribution.

Growth is therefore mid-single-digit topline with an EPS step-up sourced from share retirement and interest savings rather than operating acceleration. That is a real return to shareholders if executed, but it is not compounding growth, and it does not repeat.

## Valuation Assessment

At $136.78 on ~44.2m shares, equity value is ~$6,046m. Adding net debt of ~$2,308m gives EV ~$8,354m. Deducting ~$1,350m of OEM net proceeds gives a pro forma EV of **~$7,004m** for the retained business — **3.06x** guided 2026 revenue of $2,290m and **13.6-14.7x** our derived adjusted EBITDA of $476-515m. If Intersurgical closes and adds ~$475m net, pro forma EV falls to ~$6.53bn: 2.85x sales and 12.7-13.7x EBITDA.

On earnings:
- **21.4x** the guidance midpoint of $6.40 for 2026 adjusted continuing EPS
- **20.5x** the current-year consensus of $6.67 (which sits above the guidance high end)
- **12.9x** the next-year consensus of $10.58

The 12.9x figure is the entire bull case, and it requires four things. Rebuilding it from disclosed inputs:

| 2027 build | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 adj. net income (guide midpoint × 44.2m) | $283m | $283m | $283m |
| After-tax interest savings on $800m at ~5%, 13.5% tax | +$35m | +$35m | +$35m |
| Stranded-cost recapture (of $90m pretax, after tax) | $0 | +$39m | +$78m |
| Operating growth | +$15m | +$22m | +$35m |
| Adjusted net income | $333m | $379m | $431m |
| Weighted shares (buyback execution) | 40.6m | 37.3m | 37.0m |
| **2027 adjusted EPS** | **$8.19** | **$10.15** | **$11.64** |
| Multiple applied | 13x | 14x | 16x |
| **Implied value** | **$106** | **$141** | **$186** |

Base case $141 is 3.1% above spot. Bear case $106 is 22.5% below. Bull case $186 is 36.0% above.

Two things about the multiple. Medical instruments and supplies is a heterogeneous group: slower-growth diversified names trade around 9-12x EBITDA, while premium-growth medtech trades at 20x+. Teleflex post-transformation — 4.5-5.5% constant-currency growth, ~19% adjusted operating margin, 2.9-3.2x pro forma leverage, a subscale interventional franchise — belongs in the middle. 14x on base-case earnings is not a punitive assumption. Applying 14x to a number that arrives in early 2028 does not produce a compelling 12-month return from $136.78.

The analyst target set is unusually tight and unusually clustered above spot: low $138, median $145, mean $149.45, high $160 — a target low above the current price. That would be a supportive signal if estimates were rising. They are not: near-term consensus is being cut 7-9%, and eight of thirteen ratings are HOLD. Targets clustered just above a stock that has recovered 36.5% off its low, against a wall of neutral ratings, are not independent confirmation of undervaluation.

The historical record argues for skepticism on the cheap multiple. $100 invested in TFX at the end of FY2020 was worth **$30.62** at the end of FY2025 — a 69.4% cumulative loss — while the peer group index reached $122.51. Teleflex has looked statistically inexpensive for most of that span. A compressed forward multiple built on stacked execution assumptions is exactly the profile that has previously failed to convert.

Yield support is thin: $0.34 quarterly, $1.36 annualized, **0.99%** at spot, and FY2025 continuing free cash flow of $1.4m did not cover the $60.3m paid. A specific risk factor states the dividend may be reduced or eliminated.

**Valuation score 5.0.** The buyback arithmetic — $1.0bn retiring ~7.1m shares at ~$140, ~16.1% of the count, lifting non-selling holders' proportional ownership accordingly — is genuine optionality that the guidance excludes. That prevents a lower score. But the discount only exists on an out-year number we cannot underwrite, and base-case fair value sits at spot.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K claims no moat, describing competition on "clinical superiority, innovative features, reliability, performance, support and cost-effectiveness," and names three structural pressures: competitor consolidation, customer vertical integration, and competition "from providers of alternative medical therapies, such as pharmaceutical companies."

The retained portfolio splits into two different businesses.

**Vascular access and emergency medicine (43%)** is entrenched. Arrow central venous catheters, EZ-IO and QuikClot are procedure-embedded consumables with high incumbency, sold into tender and group-purchasing structures where cost containment is the operative pressure. Growth is +4.8%, and that is likely close to the ceiling.

**Interventional (37%)** is the swing factor and the weaker position. By acquiring BIOTRONIK VI, Teleflex entered drug-coated balloons, coronary stents and balloon catheters — a market characterised by large-scale players and heavy clinical-evidence requirements. Teleflex paid $825.2m cash, added ~$17m of annual interest expense in FY2025 (interest expense rose from $83.5m to $100.2m), and in the first full quarters the asset grows at roughly half the corporate aspiration while EMEA and Asia segment margins fell to 3.1% and 6.6%. The strategic logic — a fuller coronary bag pulls guide-extension and closure products deeper into cath labs — is coherent and management has framed it as a 2H 2026 story. It has not appeared in reported numbers.

**Surgical (19%)** carries the clearest documented disruption. Titan SGS showed impairment indicators in Q1 2025 from "reduced bariatric surgery demand due to GLP-1 adoption"; the recoverability test passed by only ~10% of carrying value; Teleflex took a **$100.0m impairment** in Q3 2025, leaving $25.1m of remaining Titan intangibles. This is one of the better-documented cases of GLP-1 therapy impairing a medtech asset, and it is a direct instance of the pharmaceutical-substitution risk the 10-K names.

Geographic exposure has shifted against the company. Non-U.S. revenue rose from 35% (2023) to 36% (2024) to **41% (2025)** — a direct consequence of the EMEA/Asia-weighted acquisition. That adds euro translation exposure, EU MDR re-registration cost (December 2027 and 2028 deadlines by risk class), China VBP pricing, and EU-origin tariff cost.

The 10-K's own forward characterization is candid: "we will be a less diversified company than we are today, with a more limited business," with "diversification of revenues, costs and cash flows" diminishing such that results "may be subject to increased volatility." It also warns of possible "reduced access to the capital markets or increased cost of borrowings, including as a result of a credit rating downgrade," and that the stock "may not match some holders' investment strategies or meet the minimum criteria for inclusion in stock market indices."

Against all of that, the divestitures do remove the two franchises with the worst records — commoditized Acute Care and a twice-impaired Interventional Urology. Retained gross margin and growth are structurally better than what leaves. That mix improvement is why we hold the score at 4.5 rather than cutting further. No customer above 10% of revenue was disclosed in the sections reviewed, but absence of disclosure is not evidence of low concentration, and the filings do establish distributor-channel dependence with an explicit destocking risk factor.

## Risk Assessment

**Stranded costs and the 2028 bridge.** Guidance assumes $90m of annualized stranded costs, offset by transition- and manufacturing-services agreements with the buyers, while announced restructuring delivers only ~$50m of pre-tax annualized savings **upon completion in mid-2028**. TSAs are time-limited by construction — the 10-K notes BIOTRONIK ancillary agreements run "up to 36 months." If service income rolls off while cost remains, the durable gap is on the order of $40m pre-tax, ~1.7% of guided revenue and roughly $0.95 per share pre-tax on ~37m post-buyback shares. Manufacturing-services agreements can be genuinely multi-year and profitable where Teleflex keeps plant utilization, and additional cost actions may follow. This is the single item most worth pressing management on, and it is the largest input to the 2027 consensus number.

**Second divestiture unconfirmed.** No source establishes the Intersurgical close. Without it, roughly half the buyback authorization is unfunded, and the share-count assumption in consensus fails.

**Buyback execution unconfirmed.** The full $1.0bn remained available at 3/31/2026. Management said it expected to begin opportunistic open-market repurchases in Q2 2026. No post-Q1 filing confirms execution.

**Sterilization single point of failure.** The 10-K names Sterigenics U.S. facilities at Smyrna, Georgia and Santa Teresa, New Mexico as contract EtO sterilizers for "some of our vascular and surgical products." Both have faced litigation over EtO; the EPA's April 2024 rule requires compliance by April 6, 2026 or April 5, 2027 depending on facility. Because the retained portfolio is precisely vascular and surgical, this exposure grows proportionally post-divestiture.

**Tariffs with added legal uncertainty.** The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses a **February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA-based tariffs** with potential refunds, followed by new Executive Orders under alternative statutory authority. Exposure is concentrated in EU-sourced goods and non-USMCA-compliant Mexican production.

**Litigation and retained liabilities.** On April 29, 2026 former Palette Life Sciences shareholders sued in Delaware Superior Court seeking **$46.7m** of Milestone Payments plus damages; Teleflex denies the claims and cannot estimate a range of loss. Separately, Teleflex **retains** Palette's pre-acquisition foreign tax liabilities after the divestitures, with $4.6m accrued, a reassessment denied in October 2025 and renewed in December, and a November 2025 audit notice for 2023-2024, with disclosure that amounts could materially exceed the accrual. Contingent consideration was $47.5m at 3/31/2026, mostly Palette, with payment anticipated in 2026.

**FX swap tail.** $100m (euro) or $60m (Swiss franc) owed at expiration on a 10% dollar decline from inception rates, settling outside operating cash flow.

**China VBP.** A named risk factor: regional and national programs "are likely to have an adverse impact on future results due to reduced pricing." Asia was ~12.6% of Q1 2026 continuing revenue.

**Refinancing.** $1.625bn of 2027 maturities across the 4.625% notes, term loan and revolver. Terms of the June 2026 $500m offering are unverified, and the credit-facility refinancing rests on management's stated expectation. Credit ratings were not retrieved despite the 10-K flagging downgrade risk as a specific post-divestiture concern.

**Leadership.** Jason Weidman became CEO on **June 8, 2026** — roughly two months before this report — following Liam Kelly's January 8, 2026 departure and an interim period under director Stuart Randle. A new CEO-transition risk factor was added to the FY2025 10-K. His specific prior roles at Medtronic are not confirmed in the sources; the Q1 deck describes "almost two decades at Medtronic" without naming business units.

**Activism, cutting both ways.** Irenic Capital Management (self-reported ~2% stake, unverified against a 13D/G) publicly demanded a new Chair and independent advisors to evaluate strategic alternatives on March 27, 2026; a new risk factor naming Irenic was added to the Q1 10-Q. Board concessions followed on April 9 — Chair succession to Andrew Krakauer, addition of former STERIS CFO Michael Tokich, a new Growth & Operating Committee. Teleflex says it is open to strategic alternatives. **No filing or credible report establishes that an actual sale process, bidder, or indication of interest exists.** Optionality that cannot be sized is not a thesis.

**Near-term event.** Q2 2026 results were scheduled for August 6, 2026, the day after our price basis, with the stock 5.1% below its 52-week high and the two nearest quarters cut 7-9% over 90 days.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from **62.4%** (June 12, 2026) to **40.8%** (July 19), with the price up 3.0% over the same window — consistent with resolution of the June financing and activist-response uncertainty. At 40.8%, IV remains elevated for a name with a 0.809 beta, which is the market pricing genuine event risk rather than complacency. We have no current skew or open-interest data, so we make no directional inference from the options structure.

Short interest at **9.6% of float** with **6.2 days to cover** is meaningfully above typical for medical instruments and supplies. Some portion is likely event- or arbitrage-related rather than directional. It cuts against a bear position — any Q2 beat or Intersurgical close into that positioning would be amplified — and is one reason we do not extend the HOLD toward AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $186 (+36%)

The transformation completes on plan. Intersurgical closes in 2H 2026, bringing total net proceeds to ~$1.8bn. The full $1.0bn buyback executes at an average near current prices, retiring ~7.1m shares and lifting non-selling holders' proportional ownership. $800m of debt retires, cutting interest expense by ~$40m pre-tax and pro forma leverage to ~2.9-3.2x. Manufacturing-services agreements prove durable and profitable, and the mid-2028 restructuring program plus further actions close the $90m stranded-cost gap. VI integration disruption clears and the cath-lab cross-sell thesis converts, taking Interventional from +3.0% toward the corporate 4.5-5.5% while EMEA and Asia margins normalize as inventory step-up rolls off. 2027 adjusted EPS reaches $11.60+. At 16x — a modest premium reflecting 5%+ growth, ~20% margins and a clean balance sheet — that is $186. Activist pressure, a new CEO with two decades at Medtronic, a refreshed board and a stated openness to strategic alternatives create an unsized tail where the whole company is monetized.

### Bear Case — $106 (−22%)

The Intersurgical leg slips or reprices — the disposal group has already been marked down 41.6%, and the allowance grew $29.0m between December and March. Without those proceeds, only half the buyback funds and the share-count assumption in consensus fails. ## Conclusion

Teleflex has done the hard, credit-positive part of its restructuring: $1.5bn of cash arrived on August 3, 2026, converting the de-levering plan from intention to substantially funded, and the two franchises with the worst records are leaving. Pro forma leverage falls from ~4.5-4.8x to ~2.9-3.2x. A $1.0bn authorization sits against a ~$6.05bn market cap. A new CEO with two decades of Medtronic experience is in the seat, the board has been refreshed under activist pressure, and management has said it is open to strategic alternatives.

None of that resolves the question the price already answers optimistically. At $136.78 the stock trades at 21.4x the midpoint of management's own 2026 guidance — a guided **down** year — and at 13.6-14.7x pro forma EBITDA, which is a full multiple for a business growing 4.5-5.5% with a 37%-of-revenue interventional franchise growing 3.0% and EMEA operating margin at 3.1%. The 12.9x that makes it look cheap rests on a next-year number requiring the second divestiture to close, the buyback to fully execute, and roughly half of $90m in stranded costs to be permanently eliminated. Rebuild that number with any of those legs discounted and fair value lands at $141 — where the stock already trades.

The company generated $1.4m of continuing free cash flow in FY2025 on $95.2m of capex, paid $60.3m of dividends out of proceeds from the businesses it is selling, and cut near-term consensus 7-9% over the past 90 days. Analyst targets cluster $138-160, but eight of thirteen ratings are HOLD and the estimates behind those targets are falling near-term, so they are not independent confirmation. The 69.4% five-year total return against a peer group up 22.5% is the relevant reminder that a compressed forward multiple at Teleflex has repeatedly been a description of the problem rather than the opportunity.

We would buy this closer to $115-120, where base-case 2027 earnings power of ~$10.15 is available at 11.5-12x and the bear case does no real damage. At $136.78, one day before an earnings print we cannot see, with the second divestiture unconfirmed and the buyback unexecuted, the risk/reward is balanced and the honest position is to wait. **HOLD.**
