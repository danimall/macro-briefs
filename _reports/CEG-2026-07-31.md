## Executive Summary

Constellation is the largest owner-operator of nuclear generation in the United States and, following the January 7, 2026 acquisition of Calpine, describes itself as the largest private-sector power producer in the world at roughly 55 GW. It is classified in Utilities but is not rate-regulated; it is a competitive generator plus a retail/wholesale marketing platform serving ~2.5 million accounts. The stock has fallen 36% from its October 2025 high of $412.70 and 25% from the $354.58 merger-close reference price, touching $228.63 on July 1 before recovering to $263.56.

The drawdown has identifiable causes, and they are not purely sentiment. Two rule changes went against the most aggressive versions of the bull case. First, FERC's December 18, 2025 order found PJM's co-located-load tariff unjust and unreasonable and directed revisions; PJM had not filed final compliance revisions as of the May 11 10-Q, and secondary reporting indicates FERC acted again in June 2026 requiring further revisions plus show-cause orders to all RTOs on large-load interconnection. That framework determines whether Constellation can sell behind the meter to hyperscalers at premium pricing — the highest-margin increment in the story. Second, FERC accepted a PJM capacity price collar of roughly $325/MW-day cap and $175/MW-day floor for the 2028/29 and 2029/30 auctions. Since 2027/28 cleared at $333, the ceiling sits *below* the most recent clear, bounding a revenue line the market had been extrapolating. The $175 floor is a genuine offset, but the two-sided nature of that change is frequently omitted from bullish framings.

Against that, the valuation and the growth algorithm have improved materially in risk/reward terms. At $263.56 the shares trade at 22.5x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $11.71 (management guidance $11.00–$12.00, affirmed May 11) and 19.7x FY2027 consensus of $13.38, versus a peer median P/E of 23.8x for names growing at low-to-mid single digits. Management's guided "base" EPS path — $6.65–$6.75 (2026) to $11.40–$11.90 (2029), a 20.3% CAGR — is driven by three mechanical items: the Illinois Carbon Mitigation Credit give-back rolling from 53 TWh to zero by 2028, 2% annual inflation escalation in the nuclear PTC floor from $44.75 to $49.88/MWh, and contracted clean volumes repricing from $70 to $85/MWh. None requires spot power prices to rise. The balance sheet is investment grade (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, both affirmed post-close) at roughly 2.5–2.7x net debt to adjusted EBITDA, with $5.0B of contracted divestiture proceeds pointing toward ~2.0x.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name, so the bar for repeating it should be high. The justification is specific and time-bounded: three of the four unresolved questions that explain the analyst dispersion ($296 Neutral target versus $441 high, mean $352.86) resolve inside eight weeks — the Q2 print, the PJM co-location compliance filing, and the September 4 DOJ divestiture deadline. Near-term implied volatility is rich (61.4% versus 47.1% realized) with a backwardated term structure, which argues against paying up immediately ahead of that information. Upgrade triggers are defined below.

## Company Overview

Post-Calpine, roughly 70% of generating resources sit in PJM (a concentration the 10-K flags explicitly), with ~93% of 2029 baseload clean MWh in PJM. Calpine added approximately 23 GW post-divestitures of predominantly gas combined-cycle and cogeneration capacity, the Geysers geothermal complex in California, and a retail platform serving roughly 62 TWh annually.

Q1 2026 revenue net of purchased power and fuel by segment: Mid-Atlantic $812M, Midwest $854M, New York $409M, ERCOT $209M, Other Power Regions $267M, Calpine $1,126M, Other $1,093M — $4,770M total versus $2,404M in Q1 2025. Calpine was immediately the largest single contributor, which makes the integration the dominant near-term execution variable.

The core economic engine is management's disclosed nuclear volume-and-price schedule: 179 TWh total nuclear in 2026 rising to 189 TWh in 2029, split into Illinois CMC units (53 TWh at $34.09 in 2026, zero by 2028), available nuclear at the PTC floor (101 TWh at $44.75 in 2026 rising to 147 TWh at $49.88 in 2030), and contracted clean (36 TWh at $70.00 rising to 53 TWh at $88.00). The strategic logic of Calpine is pairing scarce 24/7 clean attributes with dispatchable gas and a larger retail hedge channel, plus "powered land" — gas sites with interconnection already in place, which is the scarce input for data-center development.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP versus non-GAAP requires care here.** FY2025 GAAP net income fell 38% to $2,319M from $3,749M, and operating income fell 29% to $3,086M. A screen reads that as deterioration. The dominant driver was nuclear PTC revenue collapsing from $2,080M to $320M — which is the credit working as designed, phasing out as gross receipts rise from $26.00 toward $44.75/MWh. Over the same period EMAAC capacity cleared $51.89 → $179.79/MW-day and ComEd $31.09 → $169.50/MW-day. Market revenue rose as PTC revenue fell; the two move in opposite directions by construction.

Non-GAAP adjusted operating earnings, the metric management guides and compensates on, compounded from $1,335M (2022) to $2,944M (2025) — a 30.1% three-year CAGR — with FY2025 per-share of $9.39 versus $8.67 in 2024 (+8.3%). The divergence is mostly unrealized derivative marks and nuclear decommissioning trust accounting.

**FY2025 cash flow and leverage:** operating cash flow $4,237M, capex $2,949M, free cash flow $1,288M (5.0% of revenue). Total debt $8,992M against $14,517M equity; cash $3,641M; interest coverage 6.0x.

**The post-Calpine balance sheet is a different company.** At 3/31/2026: total assets $96,911M (from $57,249M), PP&E $40,769M, goodwill $11,527M (from $420M), assets held for sale $5,735M, cash $800M (from $3,641M), short-term borrowings $5,102M (from $1,650M), total debt $22,466M, net debt ~$21,666M, equity including NCI $33,820M, shares 362.4M.

Two standard screens mislead badly on this asset class and should be discarded: a total-liabilities-to-equity ratio near 2.9x is not debt-to-equity (the liability stack is dominated by $13.2B of asset retirement obligations matched against a $19.3B decommissioning trust asset), and Altman Z-Score near 1.3 was calibrated on manufacturers. The relevant credit evidence is BBB+/Baa1/BBB affirmed in January 2026 after the close, and derived leverage of roughly 2.5–2.7x net debt to adjusted EBITDA of $8.1–8.7B (built from $11.00–$12.00 guided EPS × ~361M shares, grossed up at the guided 26% tax rate, adding back ~$0.9–1.0B interest and $1,825M D&A, and conservatively excluding ~$1.0B of nuclear fuel amortization sitting in fuel expense). Pro forma for $5.0B of LS Power gross proceeds applied to debt, that falls toward ~2.0x — before adjustments and cash taxes.

**Q1 2026 results were strong on the guided metric and require adjustment on GAAP.** Revenue $11,122M (+63.8%); GAAP diluted EPS $4.49 versus $0.38; adjusted operating EPS $2.74 versus $2.14 (+28%). The bridge is decisive: GAAP included a $721M ($2.03/share) unrealized fair-value gain and a $174M ($0.49) decommissioning benefit, offset by $154M of acquired commodity contract amortization and $119M of merger costs. **$2.74 is the representative figure; $4.49 is not usable.**

**The cash-flow verification gap is the most important open financial item.** Q1 operating cash flow was $425M against $1,275M capex — negative $850M of free cash flow. Identifiable contributors: a $1,377M accounts-payable working-capital drag, $789M of other asset/liability movement, front-loaded merger cash costs, and a $505M increase in posted cash collateral ($1,904M versus $1,399M). This quarter must not be annualized: Q1 was distorted by Winter Storm Fern cost-to-serve, merger cash outflows peak at close, and collateral posting reverses. A naive 4x would imply −$3.4B, which is plainly inconsistent with guidance of $8.4B cumulative free cash flow before growth for 2026–2027 (~$4.2B/year) and $11.5–13.0B for 2028–2029. The normalized figure is the defensible anchor, but the gap between −$850M realized and ~$1.05B/quarter implied is exactly what the Q2 and Q3 statements must close. On the guidance-implied figure, FCF-before-growth yield is ~4.5% on market cap — noting that "before growth" excludes the $3.9B of committed growth capital, so it is not distributable cash.

**Earnings quality watch item:** Level 3 net derivative assets jumped to $2,173M at 3/31/2026 from $140M at 1/1/2026 ($1,290M from acquired Calpine contracts), with $848M of Level 3 gains through Q1 net income of which $568M unrealized. Constellation excludes these marks from adjusted earnings, which is the correct treatment and means guidance is not propped up by them. The concern is the growing share of the GAAP income statement and balance sheet dependent on unobservable inputs.

**Data conflict to flag:** the supplied profile shows trailing EPS of $7.40 alongside a P/E of 22.5x, which cannot both be right at $263.66 ($263.66/$7.40 = 35.6x). The 22.5x figure reconciles to FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $11.71. All valuation work here uses consensus and guided adjusted figures on a consistent basis.

## Growth Analysis

**The guided growth is substantially mechanical, and that is the strongest part of the case.** Using management's own disclosed modeling inputs, base nuclear gross margin computes to $8,847M in 2026 [(53 × $34.09) + (101 × $44.75) + (36 × $70.00)] and $11,872M in 2029 [(146 × $49.88) + (54 × $85.00)] — a $3,025M pre-tax uplift, +34% over three years. After tax at 26% and on ~361M shares that is $6.20/share of gross uplift versus the $4.95 of base EPS growth guidance implies ($6.70 → $11.65). The ~$1.25 gap is consistent with higher O&M, D&A and interest, which makes guidance look internally coherent rather than aggressive.

The honest qualification: the 54 TWh of 2029 "contracted clean" at $85/MWh embeds contracts not all signed. If hyperscaler contracting slows, that bucket does not reach 54 TWh and the volumes fall back into the PTC-floor bucket at ~$49.88, removing roughly $1.9B of the $3.0B uplift. That single line is where the "contractual" framing becomes an expectation.

**Optionality is quantified and material.** For 2029, management discloses 1 GW of nuclear PPA = $0.40–$1.00 of EPS (on a $20–$50/MWh premium to the PTC floor), 1 GW of gas powered land = $0.20–$0.50, and a $1–$3 move in power/spark = $0.45–$1.35. Signed powered-land progress: 780 MW at Thad Hill (TX) with exclusivity on an incremental 380 MW; CyrusOne contracted 380 MW at Freestone with a Phase 2 exclusive; PUCT net-metering approval obtained at Freestone. The 835 MW Crane Clean Energy Center restart under a 20-year Microsoft PPA is the flagship.

**Demand backdrop confirms, at the sector level.** Management cites hyperscaler capex of $410B actual in 2025 rising to ~$580B guided in 2026. This is a third-party datapoint Constellation does not control, and management's own 10-K risk factor states AI/data-center demand "may not grow at the pace currently anticipated by the market" and that "the price of our common stock could be adversely impacted."

**Estimate revisions are the counterweight.** Current quarter $2.40 (-0.5% 30d, -5.3% 90d), next quarter $3.62 (-2.4%, -3.4%), current year $11.71 (+0.3%, +0.6%), next year $13.38 (-1.1%, -1.6%). The full-year 2026 number is stable, but three of four horizons are drifting lower. There is no positive-revision inflection here of the kind that would justify overriding the regulatory overhangs. Consensus growth of +14.3% from FY2026 to FY2027 implies a PEG near 1.4 on the forward multiple — reasonable, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real and improving in some dimensions, compressing in others.

**Improving:** Calpine adds 23 GW of dispatchable gas, remedying Constellation's structural disadvantage in flexibility versus gas-heavy peers, and expands the retail hedge channel. Management states new-build replacement cost of the ~55 GW fleet exceeds 3x current enterprise value — with the stock down 36%, that gap has widened. The asset retirement obligation revision at Calvert Cliffs, FitzPatrick, Limerick and Nine Mile Point (nuclear ARO down $1,277M on changed assumed retirement dates) reflects a management view that these plants run longer, consistent with license renewal and uprate plans. Interconnection queues, not generation economics, are the binding industry constraint, which is what makes owned "powered land" scarce.

**Compressing:** FERC's co-location intervention reduces the ability to monetize the scarcest attribute — 24/7 clean baseload delivered behind the meter — at premium pricing. The capacity collar caps rent capture in the region holding 70% of resources.

**Pricing evidence from the 10-Q forward curves (3/31/2026):** NIHub around-the-clock $44.02 (2026)/$41.13 (2027); PJM-West $67.79/$63.05; NY Zone A $65.02/$61.65; ERCOT-North sparks $18.55/$21.21. The Illinois hub sits right at the PTC floor while PJM-West is 50%+ above it — the basis that explains why the Midwest fleet is effectively floored and the Mid-Atlantic fleet is not. Management notes only 5% of the nuclear fleet is in the PTC zone in 2026 and 0% in 2027, because gross receipts include capacity revenue (PJM at $329–$333/MW-day adds roughly $13–14/MWh at high capacity factors). The PTC is currently unexercised downside insurance running through 2032, not a revenue source.

**Operations:** Q1 nuclear capacity factor 92.3% versus 94.1%, on 40 TWh with 99 refueling outage days versus 88 — outage-schedule driven, not concerning in isolation. Fifteen planned refueling outages are guided for both 2026 and 2027; outage execution is a measurable earnings variable and is in the comp plan. CCGT/cogen capacity factor 47.1% on 23 TWh with 5.1% equivalent forced outage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Co-located load regulation remains unresolved and bears directly on the highest-margin path.** FERC's December 18, 2025 order directed PJM tariff revisions covering four service options, behind-the-meter rules, a transition period and a grandfather clause. PJM had not filed final compliance revisions as of 5/11/2026. Secondary reporting (legal-industry commentary, June 30–July 7, 2026) indicates FERC acted again on June 18 and issued show-cause orders to all jurisdictional RTOs on large-load interconnection; this is not verified against a filing or docket and should be treated as directionally suggestive only. The direction of travel is more process.

**2. The PJM capacity collar caps a driver the market extrapolated.** Clears: $270 (2025/26), $329 (2026/27), $333 (2027/28); collar ~$325 cap / $175 floor for 2028/29 and 2029/30. The ceiling is below the last clear. PJM's board reportedly directed further proposals to FERC in late July; the regime is not settled.

**3. Illinois CMC makes higher power prices partly self-cancelling near-term.** The CMC averaged −$26.18/MWh in Q1 2026 versus −$2.04 a year earlier; state program revenue was −$285M versus +$110M. Constellation refunded ~$450M in Q1 for the 2024 program year with $740M of refund liability remaining. On ~53 TWh of ComEd nuclear in 2026 and 23 TWh in 2027 the company is a net payer when market revenue exceeds the $33.50/MWh bid price. The roll to zero by 2028 is the cleanest dated tailwind in the story — and it also removes a floor.

**4. Goodwill of $11,527M is 11.9% of assets and 34.4% of equity, not tax deductible.** The 10-K added explicit impairment language keyed to discount rates, energy prices and cash flows. A write-down would be non-cash and would not affect adjusted operating earnings, free cash flow, covenants or ratings — it affects GAAP equity and GAAP EPS. High goodwill is also not itself evidence of overpayment: shares were issued at $354.58, and identical assets acquired at today's price would have produced roughly $4.4B less recorded goodwill.

**5. Credit trigger with a hard number.** A three-notch downgrade to below investment grade would require approximately $3.0B of incremental collateral ($2,972M at 3/31/2026). Liquidity is ample — $15.05B of aggregate bank commitments, revolver raised from $4.5B to $7.0B, $6,682M available — but 26% of available facilities are with European banks, 8% Canadian and 13% Asian, a funding-source concentration worth noting in a stressed-credit environment.

**6. Crane execution with contractual penalties.** PJM's Phase I study identified contingent transmission upgrades with in-service dates as late as December 2030; a FERC waiver to transfer capacity interconnection rights from retiring Eddystone units was requested with a June 1, 2026 decision date (outcome unknown to us). The 10-K states failure to meet contractual timelines "could result in significant penalties" and failure to obtain approvals could impair capitalized amounts. A DOE guarantee of up to $1.0B is in place with no borrowings drawn.

**7. DOJ divestiture deadline September 4, 2026.** Five PJM plants (~4.4 GW) are under agreement to LS Power for $5.0B before adjustments; the required ERCOT plant (609 MW) remained unsold as of 5/11/2026. Assets held for sale carried at $5,735M with no impairment.

**8. Calpine-specific new risks:** Geysers geothermal resource decline and steam-field lease renewal, California wildfire and earthquake exposure including public-safety power shutoffs, drought affecting water injection, DOE cost-share grant compliance with suspension/debarment tail, and Zion Energy Center (503 MW) potentially required to shut by January 1, 2030 under Illinois CEJA absent relief.

**9. Commodity profile flipped.** Net notional electricity moved to −638M MWh (net seller) from −260M and gas to +1,576M MMBtu (net buyer) from +33M; interest rate swap notional rose to $4.6B from $1.4B. The 10-Q states a $10/MWh power decline plus a $5/MWh spark decline would not be material to 2026–27 — protective of guidance, but it also means 2026–27 results say little about long-run realization, and the disclosed sensitivity does not extend beyond 2027.

**10. Share overhang.** The first 50% tranche of the 49.376M Calpine consideration shares unlocked at 6/30/2026, with the remainder locked until 6/30/2027. No filing or trade data confirms actual selling; the timing coincides with June/July weakness. Dilution, stated both ways: the new shares increased the count by 15.8%, and pre-merger holders' ownership fell to 86.2% (13.8% ownership dilution).

**Not a risk, but a common misreading:** remaining performance obligations of $12,034M are not total backlog. The 10-Q states RPO excludes derivatives and variable-volume/variable-price power and gas contracts, which is most of the commercial book.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $370 (+40%)

The PJM compliance filing establishes workable co-location service options with a usable grandfather clause, and Constellation signs 1–2 GW of incremental nuclear PPAs at a $20–$50/MWh premium to the PTC floor, adding $0.40–$2.00 of 2029 EPS on management's own sensitivities. Powered-land contracting extends beyond the current 1,160 MW at Thad Hill and Freestone. The LS Power sale closes, net debt/EBITDA falls toward 2.0x, and the $4.7B remaining buyback authorization is deployed in the $250–$300 range, shrinking the count while EPS compounds. The Illinois CMC give-back rolls to zero by 2028, converting a −$285M quarterly drag into a tailwind. Total 2029 adjusted EPS approaches management's implied ~$17.3; at 21x that is $363, and 22x is $381. Q2 and Q3 cash flow statements reverse the Q1 working-capital and collateral drag, validating ~$4.2B/year of FCF before growth.

### Bear Case — $215 (-18%)

PJM's compliance filing restricts behind-the-meter economics, and the show-cause process pushes large-load interconnection into 2027 and beyond. The 2029 contracted-clean bucket stalls short of 54 TWh, and volumes fall back to the PTC floor at ~$49.88, removing roughly $1.9B of the $3.0B base-margin uplift. Capacity clears at or below the $325 collar ceiling in both 2028/29 and 2029/30. Higher long-end rates keep compressing the multiple on capital-intensive growth. Q2/Q3 cash flow fails to close the gap to guidance, raising questions about integration cash costs. Forward power curves soften and a goodwill impairment lands against the $11.5B carrying value — non-cash, but a sentiment event. At 16x FY2027 consensus of $13.38 the shares are $214.

### Base Case — $300 (+14%)

Co-location resolves with workable but constrained economics — more process, some behind-the-meter capability, no premium bonanza. FY2026 lands within the $11.00–$12.00 guidance. FY2027 delivers near consensus $13.38. The LS Power sale closes and leverage falls toward 2.0x. Buybacks continue at a measured pace. Applying 22.4x to FY2027 consensus of $13.38 gives $300 — a multiple in line with the peer median for a business growing several times faster, which is the discount the unresolved regulatory questions justify keeping.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2026 guidance cut below $11.00**, or a 2027 base-EPS revision below $7.60.
2. **Failure to sign the required ERCOT divestiture by the September 4, 2026 DOJ deadline**, which would introduce a consent-decree compliance problem on top of the integration.

4. **Cumulative operating cash flow through Q3 2026 below ~$2.0B**, which would suggest the Q1 shortfall was structural rather than timing.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 print (imminent — the 13-day options expiry is pricing a 9.7% move) is the first checkpoint, and the September 4 DOJ deadline plus any PJM compliance filing is the second. Both fall inside eight weeks.

## Management Quality Assessment

The compensation design is better than average for the asset class: adjusted operating earnings, CFO/Debt, dispatch match, free cash flow before growth, fleetwide capacity factor, and customer satisfaction. Including a leverage metric and two operational metrics that are the actual value drivers of a nuclear fleet is a genuine alignment feature. TSR since separation indexed at 734 versus a peer group at 162 as of 12/31/2025.

Capital allocation reads competently on the evidence available. Constellation issued 49.376M shares at $354.58 for Calpine — a price 34% above today's — which is favorable use of equity on the consideration leg regardless of the goodwill outcome. It repurchased zero shares in Q1 while merger cash needs peaked, then ~$338M for ~1.2M shares in April–May at an average of $281.67, with $4.7B of the $5.0B authorization remaining. The dividend rose 10% to $0.4265 quarterly, a 14.8% payout of guided EPS. Post-close, $2.75B of new senior notes were issued, $2.29B of Calpine notes exchanged into CEG paper, and $2.5B of term loans plus $1.25B of Calpine secured notes and $1.4B of Calpine unsecured notes repaid — rapid refinancing of acquired debt into the investment-grade parent, which is what the Baa1/BBB+ affirmations reflect.
