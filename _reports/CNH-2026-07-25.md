**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNH Industrial is the world's #2 pure-play agricultural equipment manufacturer, behind Deere and ahead of AGCO, with a smaller construction business and a captive finance arm (CNH Capital). The company sits at or near the bottom of a severe cyclical downturn. Revenue has fallen roughly 30% over two years, from $21.5B in 2022 to $15.3B in 2025, and net income collapsed from $2.37B (2023) to $0.51B (2025). Q1 2026 showed the earnings trough deepening even as revenue stabilized: Industrial Activities Adjusted EBIT swung to negative $45M (-1.4% margin) from positive $101M (+3.2%) a year earlier, and net income attributable to shareholders fell to $7M ($0.01 EPS) from $131M ($0.10). Agriculture segment EBIT margin compressed to 1.0% from 5.4%, and construction went negative.

The investment case is now a bet on H2 2026 operating leverage. Two catalysts have landed after the last reported quarter: the June 2026 cut in U.S. tariffs on farm/construction equipment from 25% to 15%, which directly reverses the margin headwind that hurt 2025–early-2026 results, and industry commentary (UBS, D.A. Davidson) pointing to a potential early-stage ag upcycle following classic bottoming signals. Against these tailwinds runs a quantified credit deterioration in Brazil inside the captive finance book, a nearly complete C-suite turnover steering the company through the trough, and a structural competitive disadvantage against Deere's connected-machine ecosystem and dealer scale.

At $11.15, roughly 17% below the 52-week high of $13.48 and about 24% above the low of $9.00, the stock is fairly valued for a mid-cycle recovery that remains 12+ months out. The Altman Z-Score of 0.75 that flags "distress" is a false signal driven by consolidated captive-finance debt; Industrial Activities net debt is only ~$2.5B against ~$8.9B liquidity, and the company holds an investment-grade profile. CNH is a cyclically depressed but solvent franchise, priced roughly at fair value, with balanced risk/reward. We rate CNH a HOLD.

## Company Overview

CNH was formed in 2013 from the combination of Fiat Industrial and CNH Global. It is legally domiciled in the Netherlands, tax-resident in the U.K., and operationally headquartered in Basildon. The business runs across three segments:

- **Agriculture (~82% of net sales; $2.6B of $3.17B Q1'26 net sales):** flagship brands Case IH (large/row-crop, high-horsepower) and New Holland (broad-line, livestock, specialty), plus STEYR and Raven precision-ag technology. This is the core profit engine.
- **Construction (~18%; $574M):** CASE and New Holland Construction, a distant follower behind Caterpillar, Komatsu, Deere, and Bobcat/Doosan.
- **Financial Services (CNH Capital):** captive lender providing retail and dealer floorplan financing, with a managed portfolio around $28B. This segment drives the consolidated leverage profile.

Strategy is branded "Iron + Tech," embedding precision agriculture, automation, and connectivity across the fleet, built through acquisitions of Raven (2021), Hemisphere GNSS (2023), Augmenta (2023), and Sampierana (2021). Distribution runs through roughly 2,300 ag dealer owners (5,000+ locations) and ~400 construction dealers. Engines are sourced from FPT Industrial (part of Iveco Group) under a 10-year supply agreement, a related-party dependency. EXOR N.V., the Agnelli family holding company, controls ~45.6% of voting power through a loyalty voting program.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant story is a synchronized cyclical downturn. The trajectory:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 21.5 | 22.1 | 17.1 | 15.3 |
| Net income ($B) | 2.03 | 2.37 | 1.25 | 0.51 |
| Pretax income ($B) | 2.68 | 2.70 | 1.46 | 0.62 |
| Adj. Diluted EPS (proxy) | $1.46 | $1.63 | $1.05 | $0.55 |

FY2025 net margin was ~3.3%, down from ~10.7% in 2023, with ROE around 6.5% and ROA ~1.2%. The Q1 2026 data are the important incremental read: revenue was roughly flat YoY at $3.83B, but Industrial Activities Adjusted EBIT went negative, meaning operating deleverage, tariff costs (COGS rose to 82.2% of sales), higher SG&A/R&D, and elevated credit provisions overwhelmed a stabilizing top line. The trough in earnings lagged the trough in revenue.

Regional divergence in Q1'26 ag sales was pronounced: EMEA +19.8%, Asia Pacific +1.0%, but North America -3.3% and South America -27.8%. Management described North American demand as "historically low."

On the balance sheet, FY2025 operating cash flow was $2.5B. Consolidated debt of $26.8B decomposes to $22.9B inside Financial Services, which is normal for a captive lender funded by a matched receivables book. Industrial Activities net debt is roughly $2.5B, the more relevant leverage figure, and it rose from $2.0B during Q1'26 seasonal inventory build. Total equity is $7.8B. The company holds ~$8.9B of available liquidity, extended its €3.25B revolver to April 2031, and retains ~$6.4B of undrawn committed facilities. The Altman Z-Score of 0.75 signaling "distress" is distorted by the consolidated captive-finance debt and should be disregarded as an insolvency indicator; the correct lens is a profitable-through-the-cycle earner with sharply diminished current earnings power.

Capital allocation has been prudent rather than aggressive: a $500M buyback authorization (Feb 2024) with ~$26M repurchased in Q1'26 at $10.40–$11.07 and ~$224M remaining. The dividend yield is nominal at ~0.9%. The modest buyback pace amid the downturn reflects cash preservation, which is reasonable given the negative industrial EBIT.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative to flat, and the forward case rests entirely on cycle timing plus margin recovery. Consensus EPS estimate revisions have been modestly two-directional: the current-year 2026 estimate at $0.41 is down 1.0% over 90 days but up 1.5% over 30 days, and the next-year 2027 estimate at $0.73 implies a ~78% earnings rebound off the trough. The current-quarter estimate of $0.11 was cut 8.0% over 90 days but nudged up 1.4% over 30 days. This is a stabilizing, not accelerating, revision profile. It does not reach the +30% 90-day revision velocity that would flag a strong inflection buy signal, and the top line is not growing at 40%+; the estimate momentum is best read as consensus finding a floor.

The structural growth pillars are the Iron + Tech precision-ag build-out (Raven, Hemisphere, Augmenta) and the counter-cyclical parts-and-service annuity, reinforced by the April 2026 acquisition of a 30% stake in Abilene Machine, a U.S. aftermarket/remanufactured ag-parts distributor. These improve mix over time but do not close the R&D and ecosystem gap with Deere quickly.

The forward variable is the convergence of two H2 2026 tailwinds. Tariff relief is a margin tailwind independent of volume. UBS's early-upcycle thesis, if it plays out, adds volume torque. Because CNH is ~82% agriculture and ag is the later-recovering segment (construction is recovering faster), the setup is asymmetric within the cycle: more downside sensitivity now, more upside torque when ag inflects. That inflection has not shown up in reported results, and management explicitly expects trough conditions to persist through fiscal 2026.

The trailing P/E of 34.3x is a cyclical-trough artifact and should not be the primary metric. On the 2027 consensus of $0.73, CNH trades at ~15.3x forward earnings, a discount to Deere's 32x and below the peer median. On a mid-cycle earnings power closer to the $1.05 (2024) to $1.46 (2022) range, the stock trades at 8–11x. CNH is priced roughly at fair value for a company one to two years away from a normalized earnings recovery.

The reason not to score valuation more generously despite the depressed trailing multiple is that a low multiple here reflects genuine structural discount factors, not pure mispricing. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 93 versus a peer group at 191 quantifies the market's persistent skepticism about CNH's #2 competitive position and the EXOR loyalty-voting overhang that caps takeover optionality. Cheapness in this name has historically been a value trap absent a confirmed earnings inflection, and the inflection is projected rather than realized. Analyst targets bracket the current price widely: low $10.00, median $12.00, mean $13.28, high $20.50, with the wide dispersion itself signaling that the recovery timing is contested. At current levels there is ~8% upside to the median target and meaningful downside if Brazil credit or a slower ag recovery pushes the trough into 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

CNH holds a solid #2 position in agriculture with real brand equity in Case IH and New Holland, and a large recurring parts-and-service tail. The current downturn is a demand-side cycle affecting all majors (Deere, AGCO, and CNH are all trough-earning), so weak numbers are not primarily a share-loss story.

The structural challenge is durable. CNH under-invests relative to Deere in precision ag and autonomy, and Deere's connected-machine ecosystem and dealer scale are dominant. The 93-vs-191 relative TSR captures the market's verdict on this gap. The Iron + Tech strategy is the attempt to close it, but the company remains sub-scale in R&D dollars. Within the cycle, being ~82% ag while construction recovers first means CNH carries more near-term downside and more later-stage upside. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving, which is why this dimension sits at a neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully priced:

1. **Brazil / South America credit deterioration.** The Financial Services allowance for credit losses rose to $632M in Q1'26 from $572M at YE2025, with Brazilian retail reserves up $131M YoY and 30+ day delinquencies climbing to 3.5% from 2.3%. CNH is refinancing distressed Brazilian farmer receivables ($130M of installments on $521M of ag receivables) showing elevated 90+ day delinquency. This is a quantified credit event inside the captive book that can drive negative earnings surprises even if North American volumes stabilize. Brazilian financing depends heavily on BNDES government subsidy programs.

2. **Cycle depth and duration.** Management expects trough conditions to persist through fiscal 2026. Farm income is pressured by low grain prices, high input costs, and elevated financing rates, all outside the company's control.

3. **New management steering into the trough.** Nearly the entire C-suite turned over in 2024–2025, including CEO Gerrit Marx (ex-Iveco), CFO James Nickolas (ex-Martin Marietta), and a Chief Legal & Compliance Officer from Nikola. This is both execution risk and potential cost-discipline upside; the new team's operating credibility is unproven through a full cycle.

4. **Governance overhang.** EXOR's ~45.6% loyalty-voting control concentrates decision-making and caps takeover premium, contributing to a persistent valuation discount.

5. **Poland antitrust.** A December 2025 preliminary decision fined CNH Polska/dealers ~€57M; CNH is appealing and has recorded no reserve, with resolution 2+ years out.

6. **Tariff reversal risk.** The June 2026 tariff cut is favorable but policy-driven and reversible; the tailwind is not contractually locked.

The offsetting factor is that much of the cyclical bad news is already in the price and results, and the ~$8.9B liquidity plus investment-grade access remove genuine solvency risk. The net risk score sits below neutral because of the escalating Brazil credit deterioration and the unproven new team, both of which introduce asymmetric near-term downside not yet reflected in trough-level Q1 financials.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.91 to effectively 0.00 in the most recent snapshot alongside a +6.6% price move, which reads as thin, non-informative options positioning rather than a directional signal. Short interest at 8.9% of float with 6.7 days to cover is moderate: neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup, and consistent with a market that views the name as fairly valued and uncertain rather than a high-conviction bet in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($16–20).** Ag cycle bottoms in 2026 and inflects in 2027; tariff relief flows straight to H2 2026 margins; North American demand normalizes off historically low levels; operating leverage snaps earnings back toward $1.05–$1.46 mid-cycle EPS; the market re-rates toward the peer median 26.7x on recovering earnings. Analyst high target of $20.50 embeds this scenario. The parts-and-service annuity and Iron + Tech mix-shift improve normalized margins above the last cycle.

**Bear case ($8–9).** Ag downturn extends into 2027; Brazil credit losses accelerate and drive negative earnings surprises through the captive book; tariff relief is partially reversed or offset by softer volumes; the new management team fails to deliver on cost cuts; construction recovers but ag stays "stuck in a slower, more tentative recovery" (UBS). The stock revisits the 52-week low near $9.00, roughly 19% below current.

The base case sits between: stabilizing but not recovering earnings, gradual margin repair from tariff relief, and a stock that holds in the $10–13 range until the ag cycle confirms a turn. Consensus 2027 EPS of $0.73 supports a ~$12 fair value at ~16x, close to the analyst median target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the risk/reward is balanced, and neither the bull nor bear case has enough confirmation to warrant a directional call at $11.15.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of Industrial Activities Adjusted EBIT returning positive and expanding, confirming the earnings trough is behind.
- Brazil Financial Services 30+ day delinquencies stabilizing or improving below 3.0% and the credit-loss allowance flattening.
- Ag segment order intake or North American retail demand showing a confirmed sequential uptick, plus consensus 2026 EPS revisions turning firmly positive (>+5% over 90 days).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Brazil delinquencies accelerating above 4.5% or a material further reserve build (another $100M+ YoY increase) signaling a spreading credit event.
- Reversal of the June tariff relief or a guidance cut extending trough conditions into 2027.
- Q2/Q3 2026 Industrial EBIT deepening further into negative territory rather than recovering.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) is the checkpoint to test whether the tariff relief and any early ag stabilization are flowing into the operating line, and to re-read the Brazil credit trajectory.

## Conclusion

CNH is a financially sound, cyclically depressed #2 agricultural-equipment franchise at or near an earnings trough, run by a brand-new management team and controlled by EXOR. The "distress" Z-Score is a captive-finance artifact and should be disregarded. The forward case is a bet on H2 2026 operating leverage from the June tariff cut and a potential early ag upcycle, offset by escalating Brazil credit deterioration, unproven new leadership, and a structural competitive gap behind Deere.

The score has oscillated between AVOID and HOLD over the past four months (5.1 → 5.0 → 4.4 → 5.2), reflecting genuine uncertainty rather than a stable thesis. The current data confirm the trough is still biting at the operating line while the recovery catalysts remain projected rather than realized. At $11.15, roughly fair value for a mid-cycle recovery still 12+ months out, we maintain a HOLD. The stock is worth owning for investors already positioned for the ag cycle turn, but there is no confident directional edge to establish a new position here. The Q2 2026 print is the next test for evidence that tariff relief is reaching margins and that Brazil credit is not deteriorating further.
