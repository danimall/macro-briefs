## Executive Summary

KBR is a $4.7B government services and energy technology company trading at 8.7x forward earnings, a 73% discount to its industrials peer median P/E of 32.6x. This discount exists because of three overhanging concerns: Q4 FY2025 revenue declined 11% YoY on government procurement delays, total debt stands at $2.6B (1.69x D/E), and the planned spin-off of the Mission Technology Solutions (MTS) segment into a standalone public company in H2 2026 introduces meaningful execution risk. The valuation discount exceeds what the risks warrant.

The investment case rests on four pillars: (1) adjusted EBITDA margins expanding from 11.3% to 12.4% despite flat revenue, demonstrating operational quality; (2) $16.9B backlog providing ~2.2x revenue visibility with $23.2B including options; (3) NATO's 5% GDP defense target by 2035, the UK's commitment to 2.5% by 2027 ramping to 3.0%, and Australia's expanding defense budget creating a multi-year tailwind for MTS; and (4) STS's proprietary technology portfolio (85+ patents, 22.5% EBITDA margins) positioned at the center of energy security and transition spending as global energy supply chains face persistent disruption. The MTS spin-off, if executed cleanly, should unlock value by allowing each business to attract sector-appropriate capital and simplify capital allocation.

The primary risks are concentrated in execution: FY2027 brings $551M in debt maturities requiring refinancing during or shortly after the spin-off, government spending disruptions could persist, and seven executive departures in 18 months suggest organizational turbulence. At 8.7x forward earnings with a 4/4 earnings beat track record, the risk-reward skews favorably for a 12-month holding period.

## Company Overview

KBR, Inc. provides science, technology, engineering, and logistics solutions across two segments. Mission Technology Solutions (MTS, ~72% of FY2025 revenue at $5.6B) delivers full life-cycle support to defense, intelligence, space, and aviation missions — primarily for the US Department of Defense, UK Ministry of Defence, and Australian Department of Defence. Services span systems engineering, C5ISR, cyber analytics, space domain awareness, and supply chain management. Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS, ~28% at $2.2B) licenses 85+ proprietary, patented process technologies in ammonia/syngas, petrochemicals, clean refining, and circular economy, supplemented by engineering, consulting, and equipment sales.

The company employs approximately 36,000 people across 28 countries. The US government accounts for 57% of revenues ($4.4B), the UK government 9% ($663M). KBR's asset-light model requires minimal capex ($42M in FY2025 against $557M operating cash flow from continuing operations), generating high cash conversion relative to reported earnings.

In September 2025, KBR announced plans to spin off MTS into a standalone public company, targeting H2 2026 completion. Post-spin, KBR would retain STS as a focused energy technology company with ~$2.2B in revenue and materially higher margins.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

Revenue grew from $6.6B (FY2022) to $7.8B (FY2025), though growth decelerated sharply to 1% in FY2025 from 11% in FY2024. The deceleration was concentrated in Q4, where revenue fell 11% YoY to $1.89B on EUCOM scope reductions, government procurement delays, and funding restrictions from civilian agencies. MTS was essentially flat year-over-year; STS grew 2%.

The quality signal is in margins. Operating income rose 18% to $778M despite flat revenue, pushing operating margin from 8.5% to 10.0% (+150bps). Adjusted EBITDA reached $968M (12.4% margin, +110bps). MTS EBITDA margin improved from 9.8% to 10.4%; STS from 20.4% to 22.5%. The margin expansion was driven by a $103M increase in equity earnings from unconsolidated affiliates (primarily LNG project earnings at KZJV and JKC), favorable mix, and cost discipline.

Adjusted EPS of $3.93 grew 18% YoY. On a quarterly basis, KBR has beaten consensus in all four reported quarters, with surprises ranging from 1.9% to 12.6%, suggesting management guides conservatively.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $557M, up 24% YoY. Capital expenditures were a minimal $42M, yielding approximately $515M in continuing-operations free cash flow. The XBRL-reported negative FCF of -$75M includes discontinued operations (HomeSafe) cash outflows and should be disregarded as a representation of ongoing business economics.

Capital allocation has been aggressive: $329M in share repurchases (up from $218M in FY2024) plus $84M in dividends, returning ~$413M or roughly 80% of continuing operating cash flow to shareholders. Share count declined from 134M to 129M diluted.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt of $2.6B against $500M in cash produces net debt of ~$2.1B, or 2.2x adjusted EBITDA — elevated but within serviceable range for a company with $968M EBITDA and a government-backed revenue base. The Altman Z-Score of 1.74 technically falls in the distress zone, but this metric penalizes asset-light, goodwill-heavy services businesses disproportionately. Goodwill and intangibles of $3.4B (52% of total assets) reflect acquisition history, not distressed economics.

The debt maturity schedule requires attention: $49M due FY2026, $551M in FY2027, $285M in FY2028, and $788M in FY2029. Interest expense reached $158M annually (+10% YoY). The FY2027 maturity wall coincides with the post-spin-off period, creating a refinancing window that will depend on the capital structure allocated to each entity.

### FY2026 Guidance

Management guides FY2026 to $7.9B-$8.36B revenue (~4% growth at midpoint), $980M-$1.04B adjusted EBITDA (~4% growth), adjusted EPS of $3.87-$4.22 (~3% growth), and adjusted operating cash flow of $560M-$600M. Given the 4/4 beat track record, the low end of these ranges has reasonable credibility. The midpoint EPS of ~$4.05 at the current ~$36.90 stock price yields 9.1x earnings.

## Growth Analysis

KBR's growth operates on two distinct timelines.

**Near-term (FY2026-FY2027):** Revenue growth of 4% at the guidance midpoint reflects recovery from Q4 FY2025's procurement disruption. MTS Q4 book-to-bill of 0.5x was concerning but reflects award timing rather than structural demand loss. The total backlog of $16.9B (2.2x revenue) with $23.2B including options provides visibility. Key watch: MTS book-to-bill recovery in the first half of FY2026 would confirm the timing interpretation.

**Medium-term (FY2027+):** NATO, UK, and Australian defense spending commitments (detailed in the Executive Summary) create a structural demand environment for MTS. KBR's positioning as a platform-agnostic capability integrator aligns with the DoD's Acquisition Transformation Strategy. STS benefits from energy security spending acceleration — proprietary technologies in ammonia/syngas, sustainable aviation fuel (PureSAF), and plastics circularity (Hydro-PRT) address needs that become more acute as global energy supply disruptions persist. STS's 1.6x book-to-bill in Q4 supports this view.

The MTS spin-off, targeted for H2 2026, is the primary growth catalyst. Separate entities can pursue dedicated capital allocation, attract sector-specific investor bases, and eliminate conglomerate discount. Post-spin, STS as a standalone entity with IP-driven recurring revenue should command a meaningfully higher multiple than the current blended forward P/E.

## Valuation Assessment

KBR trades at 8.7x forward P/E and 10.6x trailing P/E against an industrials peer median of 32.6x. The discount is striking even after adjusting for the fact that KBR's peer set (AAR Corp, Fortune Brands, ExlService, Mercury Systems, Casella Waste) is heterogeneous. More relevant defense services peers — Leidos (~16x), SAIC (~14x), Booz Allen Hamilton (~20x) — all trade at meaningful premiums.

A sum-of-the-parts framework illustrates the embedded discount:

- **MTS ($5.6B revenue, 10.4% EBITDA margin, $583M EBITDA):** At 10x EBITDA (below Leidos/SAIC range of 11-14x), MTS is worth ~$5.8B.
- **STS ($2.2B revenue, 22.5% EBITDA margin, $495M EBITDA):** At 12x EBITDA (discount to technology licensing peers at 15-20x), STS is worth ~$5.9B.
- **Total EV:** ~$11.7B less $2.1B net debt = ~$9.6B equity value, or ~$74 per share.
- **Current market cap:** $4.7B.

Even applying aggressive haircuts for execution risk, leverage, and JV earnings quality, the current price implies both businesses are valued at trough multiples simultaneously. The current 1.8% dividend yield provides modest downside protection, and annual buybacks at these depressed levels are accretive.

Analyst consensus targets range from $42 to $61, with a mean of $51.75 and median of $51.50 — implying 40% upside from the current $36.90. Five analysts rate KBR as Buy, four as Hold, with no Sell ratings.

## Competitive Landscape

KBR competes across two distinct competitive arenas.

In defense services, KBR faces Leidos (LDOS), SAIC, and Booz Allen Hamilton (BAH). KBR's differentiation lies in its international footprint (significant UK/Australian defense contracts), security-cleared workforce, and positioning as a platform-agnostic integrator. The Q4 revenue decline and low book-to-bill suggest near-term competitive pressure from procurement delays, but the $12.7B MTS backlog and seats on major IDIQ vehicles ($151B Missile Defense Agency SHIELD, $10B NAVSUP) indicate sustained competitive positioning.

In energy technology, STS competes with Technip Energies, Honeywell UOP, and various engineering firms. KBR's advantage is its portfolio of 85+ proprietary, patented technologies — particularly in ammonia/syngas (where KBR has historically dominant market share), sustainable aviation fuel, and plastics circularity. The technology licensing model creates high barriers to entry and generates recurring revenue with minimal capital intensity. The margin differential between STS (22.5% EBITDA) and MTS (10.4%) reflects this structural advantage.

The planned spin-off should sharpen competitive positioning for both entities by allowing dedicated management focus and investment.

## Risk Assessment

### Government Spending Disruption (HIGH)
57% of revenue derives from the US government. Q4 FY2025 demonstrated the vulnerability: EUCOM scope reductions, procurement delays, and civilian agency funding restrictions drove an 11% revenue decline. The January 2026 Executive Order potentially limits dividends and buybacks for defense contractors deemed underperforming. Government shutdown risk recurs annually. While NATO spending commitments provide a tailwind, funding must pass through appropriations, procurement, and contracting processes that each introduce delay risk.

### Spin-Off Execution Risk (HIGH)
The spin-off requires IRS private letter ruling or favorable tax opinion, debt restructuring across two entities, establishment of independent corporate functions, and management allocation. Executive turnover may be spin-related preparation but introduces organizational uncertainty. Stranded costs, loss of scale, and potential dis-synergies are real. If the spin-off is delayed or cancelled, the stock likely remains at current depressed levels.

### Leverage and Refinancing (MODERATE-HIGH)
The $551M FY2027 maturity coincides with the post-spin-off period. Refinancing as two separate, smaller entities in a higher-rate environment could increase cost of capital. Interest expense is manageable against current EBITDA but is rising 10% annually.

### Joint Venture Earnings Quality (MODERATE)
$210M in equity earnings from unconsolidated affiliates (up from $107M) is a significant component of profitability. These earnings don't convert to cash on the same timeline as consolidated operations, complicating FCF analysis. The Ichthys LNG (JKC) project carries residual contingent liability with $82M in letters of credit related to paint/insulation deterioration.

### HomeSafe Termination (LOW-MODERATE)
USTRANSCOM's termination of the HomeSafe Global Household Goods Contract in June 2025 resulted in a $55M net loss from discontinued operations. The financial impact appears contained, but the termination raises questions about government relationship management.

### Tariff Exposure (LOW)
KBR is primarily a services business, limiting direct tariff exposure. Some contracts involve materials procurement that could be affected, and international operations could face retaliatory measures. The company reports no material cost impacts to date.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $52-55 (40-50% upside)
The MTS spin-off executes on schedule in H2 2026, unlocking a sum-of-the-parts revaluation. MTS book-to-bill recovers above 1.0x in the first half of FY2026 as procurement delays normalize. NATO spending commitments drive international MTS growth acceleration. STS, as a standalone high-margin technology licensor, attracts energy-technology multiples of 14-16x EBITDA. FY2026 adjusted EPS comes in at the high end of guidance ($4.22). At 13x $4.22, the combined entity would be worth ~$55.

### Bear Case — $28-32 (13-24% downside)
The spin-off is delayed or cancelled due to IRS ruling complications, market conditions, or management distraction. Government spending disruptions worsen as procurement reform creates a longer-than-expected transition period. MTS book-to-bill remains below 1.0x through mid-2026, signaling structural award losses. Rising interest costs compress earnings. JV earnings from LNG projects decline as project phases complete. The market applies a 7-8x multiple to the low end of guidance ($3.87), yielding $27-31 per share.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $48 within 12-15 months. Derived from 12x the midpoint FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.05 = $48.60. This assumes partial rerating toward defense services peer multiples (14-20x) as spin-off progress becomes visible, without requiring full sum-of-the-parts realization.

**Bull case target:** $52-55. Requires spin-off execution on schedule plus MTS book-to-bill recovery above 1.0x. 12-18 month horizon.

**Bear case target:** $28-32. Spin-off delay or cancellation, persistent government spending disruption, MTS backlog erosion. Represents 13-24% downside from current $36.90.

**Upside/downside:** +30% to base case, +41-49% to bull case, -13-24% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.5:1 to 2.5:1 (base-to-bear).

**Timeframe:** 12-15 months, anchored by the H2 2026 spin-off target and FY2026 quarterly earnings cadence.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. MTS book-to-bill remains below 0.8x for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural award losses rather than timing delays.
2. The spin-off is formally delayed beyond Q1 2027 or cancelled, removing the primary rerating catalyst.
3. Net leverage exceeds 3.0x EBITDA or the company signals difficulty refinancing the FY2027 maturity, shifting the risk profile to balance sheet stress.

## Conclusion

KBR at 8.7x forward earnings is priced for permanent impairment in a business generating $557M in operating cash flow, expanding margins, and sitting on $16.9B in backlog with structural defense and energy-security tailwinds. The discount reflects real risks — government spending disruption, elevated leverage, spin-off execution uncertainty — but the 73% discount to peer median P/E is disproportionate to those risks.

The MTS spin-off provides a specific, dated catalyst (H2 2026) that should narrow the valuation gap. Management's aggressive buyback program at depressed prices creates compounding value, and consensus estimates appear achievable based on recent beat history. The 0.499 beta provides partial insulation from broader market volatility.

Position sizing should reflect the leverage profile and spin-off binary: high-conviction thesis with real execution risk, warranting a standard-weight position rather than maximum concentration. The risk-reward at $36.90 with a $48 base case and $28-32 bear case favors ownership.

**Recommendation: BUY**
