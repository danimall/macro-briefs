**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

IDACORP is a pure-play regulated electric utility holding company whose subsidiary Idaho Power serves roughly 668,000 customers across southern Idaho. The investment case rests on a single distinctive fact: service-territory growth that is roughly three times the national utility average. Customer count rose 2.3% year-over-year (about 15,000 net adds) in the twelve months ended March 2026, and the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan lifted the five-year retail MWh sales-growth forecast to 8.3% with peak demand up 5.1%, driven by a handful of large commercial and industrial customers, most prominently Micron's Boise-area memory fab and other data-center-scale loads. For a regulated utility, that demand profile is extraordinary and supports a multi-year rate-base CAGR in the high-single to low-double digits, which in turn underpins above-average EPS and dividend growth.

The earnings algorithm protecting that growth is among the most de-risked in the sector. Three regulatory mechanisms work in concert: the Power Cost Adjustment passes roughly 95% of power-cost variance to customers, the Fixed Cost Adjustment decouples revenue from residential and small-commercial usage, and the ADITC accelerated-amortization mechanism provides an earnings floor supporting a minimum 9.12% Idaho ROE (now capped at $55M annually under the 2025 settlement). Q1 2026 demonstrated the cushion in action: operating income rose 48% to $81.2M even as headline revenue fell on lower pass-through PCA collections.

The constraint is the funding model and the per-share math. The 8.3% sales forecast requires $6.3–7.2B of capex over five years against an $8.1B market cap and a ~$3.6B equity base. That gap is funded by debt and equity, and roughly $715M of forward-equity sale agreements (~5–6M shares, ~10% dilution) sit pending settlement through late 2026, a meaningful drag on per-share metrics. Moody's downgraded both entities in March 2026, leaving IDACORP one notch above non-investment grade. At ~24x forward earnings against a peer-median ~20x, the stock already prices the load narrative without the per-share growth velocity to justify a clear discount. We rate IDA HOLD as a growth-plus-income compounder, with accumulation favored on weakness toward the high-$120s/low-$130s.

## Company Overview

IDACORP's principal subsidiary, Idaho Power Company, is a vertically integrated regulated electric utility serving roughly 24,000 square miles of southern Idaho and (currently) eastern Oregon. Utility operations are the only reportable segment; the non-utility subsidiaries (IFS affordable-housing tax-credit investments, Ida-West small hydro) are immaterial.

The generation mix is hydro-heavy: hydropower supplied 52% of owned generation in 2025 (54% in 2024), anchored by the Hells Canyon Complex. A fuel transition is underway: North Valmy coal units convert to gas by mid-2026 and the two Jim Bridger units by 2030, after which roughly 40% of nameplate capacity will be gas-fired. Idaho's regulatory jurisdiction is widely viewed as one of the most utility-constructive in the United States, reinforced by the unique ADITC earnings-support mechanism.

A February 2026 definitive agreement to sell the Oregon electric distribution business and certain Oregon transmission assets to Oregon Trail Electric Consumers Cooperative for a base $154M will concentrate the company into a pure Idaho-jurisdiction utility, removing the OPUC regulatory burden and booking an expected gain at closing.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** Net income to IDACORP was $68.0M versus $59.6M a year earlier (+14%); diluted EPS was $1.21 versus $1.10. Total operating revenues fell to $403.4M from $432.5M, but the decline was driven by lower pass-through PCA collections and lower wholesale sales, both largely earnings-neutral. The more relevant figure is operating income, which rose to $81.2M from $54.9M (+48%).

The earnings bridge is instructive: +$18.0M from the January 2026 Idaho base-rate increase, +$5.0M from customer growth, and +$19.1M from the Fixed Cost Adjustment, partially offset by –$10.7M from lower usage per customer (mild winter) and –$13.0M from much lower ADITC amortization ($6.3M in Q1 2026 versus $19.3M in Q1 2025). The two largest negatives in the quarter were weather-driven (weak heating demand) and a deliberate management lever (less ADITC needed because underlying ROE was healthier), making this a higher-quality quarter than the flat-ish EPS optics suggest.

**Capital intensity dominates the financial story.** Capex guidance is $1.3–1.5B (2026), $1.4–1.6B (2027), and $3.6–4.1B cumulative (2028–2030), roughly $6.3–7.2B over five years. Q1 2026 capex was $371M versus $201M a year earlier. Operating cash flow ($76M in Q1) covers only a fraction; the gap is funded by external capital. Free cash flow is structurally negative by design under the rate-base growth model, not a distress signal. The mechanism is straightforward: rate base grows, allowed return applies to a larger base, earnings grow, but the buildout outruns internal cash generation, forcing recurring debt and equity issuance.

**Balance sheet and financing.** Long-term debt rose to $3.68B (March 2026) from $3.33B (December 2025) after a $350M first-mortgage bond issuance at 4.85% in February. Capital structure is roughly 54% debt / 46% equity at the IDACORP level against a 65% covenant limit, leaving headroom. Equity issuance is heavy via forward sale agreements: ~$561M outstanding from a May 2025 offering (forward price ~$107.67, settle by November 2026) plus ~$154M under the ATM program (forward price ~$139.61). Collectively this is ~$715M of pending equity, or roughly 5–6M shares against 55.4M outstanding, a ~10% prospective share-count increase that will dilute EPS as settled.

The dividend is $0.88/quarter (up from $0.86), funded from Idaho Power dividends to the parent. Idaho Power's IPUC-approved 35% minimum equity ratio constrains upstream dividends, but at 47% the cushion is comfortable. The current yield is ~2.5%, in line with the peer median of 2.6%.

In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the negative-FCF financing model carries a direct cost: each refinancing and new issuance reprices at elevated yields, and the equity component dilutes per-share growth precisely when the equity is being raised below the current price. This is the structural headwind that has weighed on the broader regulated-utility complex and explains the Financial Health score of 5.5: solvency is strong (high Z-Score, ample covenant headroom), but capital efficiency is pressured by the dilution-and-leverage funding mix.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is unusually durable for a regulated utility because it rests on confirmed, multi-source demand rather than a single-customer story. The 8.3% five-year retail sales-growth forecast is roughly an order of magnitude above the typical regulated utility's 1–2%. This is corroborated by the broader regional pattern: Pacific Northwest utilities are competing to serve hyperscale data-center and chip-fab large loads, with peers such as Avista signing single-customer deals equal to half their residential base. The demand is regional and structural, driven by Idaho's low power costs, available land, and Micron's hometown fab expansion.

The resource buildout to serve this load is concrete and progressing: IPUC approval of an 80 MW solar PPA and the 167 MW Bennett Mountain gas CPCN, pending CPCNs for 222 MW (South Hills) and 430 MW (Peregrine) gas plants, 1,400 MWh of batteries, multiple transmission lines (GWW Segment E-8 filed April 2026), and the 45%-owned B2H transmission line under construction for late-2027 service. A new $1.6B fuel-purchase obligation (commencing 2030–2045) supports the gas-heavy pivot.

This rate-base buildout converts to EPS through the regulated return on equity, supporting high-single to low-double-digit rate-base growth and, by extension, above-average EPS/dividend growth. The earnings-stability mechanisms (PCA, FCA, ADITC floor) make that growth more bankable than a typical merchant or unregulated story.

The qualification is the per-share translation. Consensus EPS estimates are essentially flat-to-slightly-negative over 90 days: current-quarter estimate at $1.81 (down 1.5% over 90 days), next quarter $2.46 (down 4.9%), with current-year ($6.38) and next-year ($6.95) roughly unchanged. The sales-growth narrative is not yet translating into rising consensus EPS, in large part because the equity dilution offsets a meaningful share of the rate-base earnings growth. The earnings beats are modest and inconsistent (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters; +7.9% in Q1 2026 but small misses in mid-2025). The growth is real and durable, which supports a 6.8 score, but the per-share velocity is muted relative to the load headline.

## Valuation Assessment

At $146.27, IDACORP trades at ~24x trailing and forward earnings (the two are nearly identical given the flat EPS profile). Against the Electric Utilities peer set, this is a premium: peer-median P/E is 19.7x, with the comparables ranging from Otter Tail's 13.3x to TXNM's 42.1x. Closer comparables Portland General (22.4x) and MGE Energy (19.7x) cluster well below IDA.

The premium is partially defensible. IDACORP's load-growth profile is genuinely superior to most peers, its regulatory jurisdiction is among the most constructive in the country, and the ADITC earnings floor lowers business risk relative to merchant-exposed names. A modest premium to the peer median is warranted on quality and growth grounds.

The premium is not, however, asymmetrically attractive at the current price. Estimate revisions are flat to negative, not the velocity that would justify cycle-adjusted treatment. There is no compressed-multiple value case (the stock is near the high end of its range, ~3% off its 52-week high) and no cheap-entry catalyst. The forward-equity overhang is a known headwind to per-share metrics that screens may not yet fully reflect; as those shares settle through late 2026, the denominator grows ~10% while the rate-base earnings grow into it, capping near-term EPS leverage.

The stock is not cheap, and a low-multiple value framing would be the wrong lens. The 4.5 valuation score reflects a premium multiple against peers with flat estimate revisions and a structural dilution headwind. Analyst targets bracket the picture: mean $152.5, median $156.5, with a $121 low and $167 high. The mean implies ~4% upside, the median ~7%, modest total-return support when combined with the 2.5% dividend, but not the asymmetry that would support a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Idaho Power is a monopoly franchise utility with no direct competition for retail service. The relevant competitive dynamics are structural rather than rivalrous: regional transmission constraints limit imported power during peaks, pushing the utility toward self-build (favorable for rate-base growth), and a Pacific-Northwest-wide scramble to serve large load validates that Idaho's demand wave is part of a confirmed regional trend.

The company's competitive position is improving on trajectory. It is adding customers at ~3x the national rate, expanding rate base aggressively, and operating in a regulatory home that lets it earn on that investment with a protective floor. The Oregon sale concentrates the company in its single best jurisdiction, removing a second-regulator burden. The hydro-heavy mix provides low marginal generation cost, an advantage in attracting price-sensitive large load.

The offsetting structural risk is hydrology. With hydro above 50% of generation, low snowpack directly raises power-supply costs: 2026 hydro generation is guided to 5.5–7.0M MWh against a 20-year average of 7.3M MWh, a notable downside skew that prompted a $51.6M PCA increase filing in June 2026. The PCA passes ~95% of the variance to customers, neutralizing the earnings impact while creating a cash-flow lag. On balance, the improving-share, widening-rate-base trajectory supports a 6.5 competitive-position score, with the hydrology dependence preventing a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Large-load concentration is the double-edged thesis.** The sales forecast depends on a handful of named large customers (Micron's pending "Triton" special-contract case at the IPUC, plus other large loads) ramping as planned. The 10-K is unusually explicit about stranded-cost risk if ramp rates don't materialize, and about higher-cost purchased power or accelerated capex if demand exceeds capacity. Idaho Power is building 167/222/430 MW gas plants, batteries, and transmission largely to serve this load. A marquee-customer slowdown or cancellation could produce write-downs or disallowances.

**Hydrology/drought** is the most immediate operating risk, with the 2026 downside skew already triggering the PCA filing. **HCC relicensing** is a longer-dated overhang: $547M of relicensing costs sit in CWIP, a new FERC license isn't expected until 2027+, and a prudence-determination request (December 2025) covering 2016–2025 spend carries disallowance risk. **Wildfire liability** is mitigated by Idaho's Wildfire Standard of Care Act (effective July 2025; WMP approved April 2026), a meaningful shield versus California-style strict liability, though not absolute; the WMP deferral balance is $89.6M.

**Credit/financing risk** rose with the March 2026 Moody's downgrade (IDACORP to Baa3/P-3; Idaho Power to Baa2, FMBs A3). Given the enormous external funding needs, a further downgrade would raise capital costs and could trigger ~$32M of derivative collateral posting on a below-IG event. The higher-for-longer rate path compounds this by raising the cost of the recurring negative-FCF financing.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that several of these risks (large-load concentration, dilution overhang, fresh downgrade) are specific and not fully reflected in a premium multiple, while the regulatory cushion (PCA/FCA/ADITC) partially offsets them. This continues the downward drift in the risk dimension (from 5.0 to 4.5 across prior reports; we hold at 5.0 here given the regulatory protections that limit how much of the operating risk reaches earnings).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The sales-growth forecast proves durable as Micron and data-center loads ramp on schedule; the constructive Idaho regulator approves the pending CPCNs and large-load tariffs on favorable terms; rate base compounds at low-double-digit rates and EPS growth accelerates past the dilution drag into 2027–2028 as the forward-equity settlement completes and the capex base earns. The ADITC floor and PCA/FCA mechanisms keep earnings stable through hydrology volatility. The stock re-rates modestly higher and total return reaches the bull target of ~$167, roughly +14% from current, plus dividend.

**Bear case.** A marquee large-load customer slows or cancels, triggering stranded-cost write-downs and disallowance; drought deepens beyond the 2026 guide, straining cash flow through PCA lag; HCC prudence determination produces disallowances; a further Moody's downgrade into non-investment grade raises capital costs and forces collateral posting. The ~10% dilution settles into flat EPS, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median. The stock derates toward the bear target of ~$121, roughly -17% from current.

**Base case.** Load growth proceeds broadly as forecast but per-share EPS growth stays in the mid-single digits as dilution offsets a meaningful share of rate-base earnings growth; estimates remain stable; the multiple holds near ~22–23x. Combined with the 2.5% dividend, this is a high-single-digit total-return compounder with no near-term re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on a growth-hold rationale: a durable load-growth thesis exists, but the per-share translation and premium multiple cap near-term asymmetry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimate-revision velocity turns positive (current-year and next-year estimates rising >3% over a 90-day window), signaling the load growth is outrunning the dilution drag.
- A pullback toward the high-$120s/low-$130s (~12–18% below current), restoring a meaningful margin between price and the ~$152–156 analyst target band.
- IPUC approval of the Micron/Triton special-contract tariff and the pending South Hills/Peregrine CPCNs on favorable, recovery-assured terms, de-risking the large-load capex.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A marquee large-load customer cancels or materially slows, with the company guiding to stranded-cost write-downs or capex deferral.
- A further Moody's downgrade to non-investment grade, raising capital costs and triggering collateral obligations.
- 2026 hydro generation materially below the 5.5M MWh low end of guidance, straining cash flow beyond PCA recovery, combined with an unfavorable PCA disallowance.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which will show hydro performance against the 5.5–7.0M MWh guide and any FSA settlement progress, plus updates on the pending CPCN and large-load tariff dockets.

## Conclusion

IDACORP offers a genuinely differentiated regulated-utility growth profile: 2.3% customer growth, an 8.3% five-year sales forecast anchored in confirmed regional large-load demand, and one of the most protective earnings frameworks in the sector. The Q1 2026 operating-income jump demonstrated that the regulatory cushion works. The Oregon sale concentrates the company in its strongest jurisdiction.

The constraints keep this from being a buy at the current price. The capex program requires pending equity dilution that caps per-share earnings growth; consensus estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative despite the load narrative; the Moody's downgrade and a higher-for-longer rate path raise the cost of the negative-FCF financing model; and the multiple already prices the quality and growth. The score trajectory has drifted down (6.2 → 5.9 across prior reports); we hold at an overall 5.6.

This is a high-single-digit total-return compounder to own through weakness. **HOLD**, with accumulation favored on a pullback toward the high-$120s/low-$130s or on a clear positive turn in estimate-revision velocity.
