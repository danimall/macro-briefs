## Executive Summary

Occidental Petroleum is a simplified pure-play E&P company following the January 2026 sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway for $9.7B. The company operates primarily in the Permian Basin (~55% of production), deepwater Gulf of America, and international assets in Oman and Algeria, producing approximately 1,434 Mboe/d. Post-divestiture, OXY has reduced principal debt from ~$25B to ~$15B with a favorable maturity profile (only $453M due through 2029).

The stock has rallied ~45% from its December 2025 low of $38.80 to $56.18, driven by the successful deleveraging trajectory and four consecutive earnings beats (averaging ~51% surprise). Consensus FY26 EPS has risen from $1.15 to $5.42 over 90 days (+371%), reflecting the oil price tailwind. At current prices, OXY trades at ~10.4x forward FY26 EPS — a discount to EOG (13.2x) and DVN (13.0x) — but carries higher leverage, a $2.3B unresolved Tronox tax contingency, CEO transition risk, and zero commodity hedging in an environment where oil resolution would collapse earnings.

The investment case rests on whether oil remains elevated through 2026-2027. At $100+ WTI, OXY generates ~$8-10B in annual operating cash flow and $3.5-5B in FCF, making the stock cheap. Below $70 WTI, FCF compression is severe given the ~$60/bbl breakeven, and the stock reverts to the $40-45 range where it spent most of H2 2025.

## Company Overview

Occidental Petroleum operates two continuing segments post-OxyChem:

**Oil & Gas (~97% of revenue):** The company is the 4th-largest deepwater Gulf of America producer with 1,434 Mboe/d (2025), holding 4,603 MMboe of proved reserves across the Permian Basin (786 Mboe/d), Gulf of America deepwater, Oman (Block 53 with a 15-year extension signed in 2025), Algeria, and Qatar. OXY holds 8.9 million net domestic acres and operates approximately 10% of total Permian oil production.

**Midstream & Marketing:** Includes a 40.9% stake in Western Midstream Partners (WES), the Dolphin Pipeline (24.5%), Al Hosn Gas processing, and Oxy Low Carbon Ventures (OLCV) — which operates STRATOS, the world's first commercial-scale Direct Air Capture facility, expected to begin operations in 2026.

**Berkshire Hathaway Relationship:** Berkshire owns ~27% of common equity (~265M shares), $10B in preferred stock (8% coupon = $800M/year in preferred dividends), and acquired OxyChem. This anchor ownership provides implicit financial backstop but the preferred dividend is a material cash outflow regardless of oil prices.

- Loss of OxyChem diversification makes earnings profile more volatile than peers

**Competitive Position Assessment:** OXY's Permian position is excellent and its carbon management optionality is unique among E&P peers. However, the balance sheet disadvantage relative to EOG, DVN, and COP means that in a downturn, OXY faces disproportionate stress. The competitive position is stable but the financial structure amplifies both upside and downside relative to peers.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Price Collapse (CRITICAL):**
OXY has zero commodity hedges. Every $10/bbl move in WTI impacts annual pretax income by approximately $2.0-2.5B. A reversion to $65 WTI would compress FY26 EPS to ~$2.00-2.50, implying the stock at $25-35. The probability of a diplomatic Iran resolution is low but the consequence is extreme for an unhedged producer with high leverage.

**2. Tronox Tax Liability ($2.3B):**
Binary outcome. The U.S. Tax Court ruling on whether Anadarko's $5.2B Tronox settlement payment was deductible is pending. Adverse ruling = $2.3B cash outflow (~4% of market cap, ~15% of current cash + available credit). The Company has not reserved for this. Trial is complete; ruling could come at any time.

**3. IRS Audit of $2.7B Tax Benefit:**
A 2022 legal entity reorganization generated a $2.7B tax benefit that the IRS is reviewing. If disallowed, another material cash outflow. Combined with Tronox, the tail-risk tax exposure is $5.0B.

**4. CEO Transition:**
Vicki Hollub retiring after 40 years. Richard Jackson (COO) succeeding. No evidence yet of strategic divergence, but the transition occurs during a complex period (deleveraging, STRATOS commissioning, post-divestiture integration). New CEO untested with equity markets.

**5. Retained Environmental Liabilities:**
$925M DASS reserve increase (Q4 2024) from OxyChem legacy operations retained by OXY. Long-tail liability with uncertain total cost. OXY indemnified Berkshire on all environmental obligations.

## Conclusion

OXY is a leveraged oil play trading at a modest discount to peers (10.4x vs. 13x), with that discount justified by structural disadvantages (leverage, unhedged position, tax contingencies, CEO transition). At $56.18, the risk/reward is roughly balanced: 10-28% upside if oil holds, 25-32% downside if oil mean-reverts.

The 12-month thesis that justifies holding: sustained $100+ oil through 2026 generates $3.5-5B in annual FCF, enabling the company to reach its $14.3B debt target, initiate capital returns, and potentially re-rate toward peer multiples. Q2 and Q3 earnings will confirm or deny this thesis. The Hormuz conflict duration supports elevated oil prices as the central case.

However, this is fundamentally a commodity bet with maximum leverage and no hedging. Investors seeking E&P exposure with lower risk should prefer EOG or DVN. OXY is appropriate only for investors with high conviction in sustained oil above $90 and willingness to accept binary Tronox risk.

**Recommendation: HOLD** — conditional on oil remaining above $90. Below that level, the thesis breaks rapidly.
