**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EVERTEC is the dominant transaction processor in Puerto Rico, the Caribbean, and an increasingly large footprint across Latin America, anchored by its owned ATH PIN-debit network and the ATH Móvil/Business digital wallets. The stock has fallen 32.7% over the past twelve months and ~36% from its June 2025 high near $38, trading at $24.47 against trailing GAAP EPS of $2.20 (~11.1x) and current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $3.92 (~6.2x). On the surface this is a cheap, cash-generative payments compounder with rising estimates and a cluster of insider buying. The complicating reality is that two material, dated risks converge in 2026-2028, and the company's adjusted earnings increasingly diverge from GAAP as it funds an aggressive Brazilian acquisition program.

The bull case rests on real signals: management raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to roughly $1.08B (about 4.8% above consensus) on the Q1 print, Latin America Payments grew 32% year over year, free cash flow margin is ~22%, and four senior insiders bought roughly $1.8M of stock on the open market in May 2026 near current prices. The bear case is concrete and timed: Banco Popular, ~26-29% of revenue, already imposed a 10% discount on certain master-service-agreement services in October 2025 (pushing the Business Solutions segment down 9% in Q1), the broader MSA expires in 2028, and the 4% Puerto Rico preferential income-tax rate covering >90% of PR taxable income expires December 31, 2026. The effective tax rate has already climbed from 6.4% in 2025 to 14.6% in Q1 2026 as income mix shifts to higher-tax jurisdictions and OECD Pillar Two takes hold.

We rate EVTC a HOLD. The valuation is undemanding and the insider buying is the strongest class of management signal, but the two binary 2026-2028 catalysts are not yet resolved, the roll-up is loading the income statement with amortization and deal costs (GAAP operating income fell 10% in Q1 despite +8% revenue), and a ~$130M Pix fraud event at a recently acquired Brazilian subsidiary is a live operational-control concern. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on clarity around either the Popular renewal or the tax-grant extension; we would downgrade on evidence the roll-up is destroying value or on a Popular renewal at materially worse terms.

## Company Overview

EVERTEC processes over ten billion transactions annually across 26 countries from its San Juan headquarters, with roughly 5,300 employees (45% in Brazil, 23% in Puerto Rico/US). Its competitive anchor is ownership of the ATH network, one of the leading PIN-debit rails in the region, plus the ATH Móvil and ATH Business wallets that dominate person-to-person and merchant payments in Puerto Rico. The business operates in four segments:

- **Latin America Payments & Solutions** — now the largest and fastest-growing segment ($110.3M in Q1 2026, +32% YoY), driven by Brazilian acquisitions (Sinqia, Tecnobank) and FX. Includes core-banking software, fraud/risk, and processing.
- **Payment Services – Puerto Rico & Caribbean** — highest margin (~59% segment EBITDA in Q1), the ATH network plus card/issuer processing and EBT.
- **Merchant Acquiring** — POS and e-commerce acceptance, tied closely to Banco Popular's referral channel; segment margin fell to 40.3% in Q1.
- **Business Solutions** — core-bank processing and IT outsourcing; the only non-bank cash-services provider to the U.S. Federal Reserve in the Caribbean. Revenue fell 9% in Q1 on the Popular discount.

The strategic arc is a transformation from a Puerto Rico-centric processor into a pan-Latin American fintech, with Brazil as the center of gravity. Roughly 61% of revenue still comes from Puerto Rico, with 39% from LatAm and the Caribbean, but that mix is shifting rapidly via M&A.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown from $618M (2022) to $695M (2023), $845M (2024), and $932M (2025, +10%). Q1 2026 revenue was $247.9M, up 8% year over year. Much of the multi-year growth is acquisition-driven (Sinqia closed November 2023, Tecnobank October 2025), with solid organic growth layered on top.

Profitability metrics are strong on an adjusted basis: 2025 operating margin 20%, net margin 15.5%, ROE 23.3%, FCF margin ~22% ($204M FCF), and Adjusted EBITDA of $373M (40% margin). The widening gap between 2025 Adjusted EPS of $3.62 and GAAP diluted EPS of $2.20 is the central analytical issue. It is driven by acquisition-related amortization, contingent consideration, and deal costs. In Q1 2026, GAAP operating income fell 10% year over year even as revenue rose 8%, because SG&A jumped 32% (professional fees plus contingent consideration on prior deals) and D&A rose 31% (Tecnobank intangible amortization). Adjusted EPS still nudged up to $0.90 from $0.87. Investors anchoring to Adjusted EBITDA and EPS need to track whether these "non-recurring" deal costs become a recurring feature of a perpetual roll-up.

The balance sheet carries roughly $1.1B of total debt, mostly SOFR-variable but partly hedged via three swaps totaling $550M notional at 3.4-4.2% fixed. A 100bp rate move adds about $5.6M in annual interest. Cash was $306M at year-end 2025, most of it ($264M) held offshore in Brazilian and LatAm subsidiaries. Total secured net leverage was 2.15x at March 31, 2026 against a 4.0x covenant ceiling, leaving comfortable headroom. The Altman Z-score of 1.48 (nominally a "distress zone" reading) is misleading for an asset-light processor with a large goodwill base; FCF generation and Piotroski signals are healthy. Goodwill rose from $423M (2022) to $892M (2025) and intangibles from $200M to $553M, reflecting the M&A cadence.

Capital allocation is balanced but acquisition-heavy. The company repurchased $69.3M of stock in 2025 and $20M in Q1 2026 (at ~$29.32, above the current price), pays a token $0.05 quarterly dividend (~0.8% yield), and reset a $150M buyback authorization in February 2026. The bulk of available cash is flowing into Brazilian acquisitions — Dimensa (~$197M, closed April 30, 2026) was funded with cash plus revolver.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is Latin America, and the secular driver is genuine: cash-to-digital migration across under-penetrated LatAm markets supports a projected mid-teens regional payments CAGR. EVERTEC is consolidating Brazilian fintech infrastructure (Sinqia, Tecnobank vehicle-financing registry, Dimensa B2B fintech) and buying out minority stakes (Rosk Software, Compliasset). This is a credible diversification away from Puerto Rico concentration, with Popular down from 29% to 26% of revenue.

Estimate revisions are constructively positive: current-year EPS consensus moved to $3.92 (+4.5% over 90 days), and next-year to $4.34 (+7.2% over 90 days). The 90-day revision velocity (mid-single digits) is positive but well short of the +30% magnitude that would mark a true earnings trough recovery. Growth here is steady compounding plus M&A, not a violent re-rating setup.

The earnings track record is solid but not pristine: three beats and one miss over the past four quarters, with Q1 2026 a marginal miss (revenue -0.27%, adjusted EPS $0.90 vs $0.94 estimate, a -4.3% surprise). The quality concern is that reported growth is substantially inorganic and that the cost of acquiring it (amortization, deal fees, integration risk, FX) is rising. A $3.7M FX remeasurement loss hit Q1 as Brazilian Real and Chilean Peso exposure grew.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.47, EVTC trades at ~11.1x trailing GAAP EPS, ~6.2x current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $3.92, and ~5.6x next-year adjusted EPS of $4.34. Against the peer group in transaction and payment processing, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is roughly 20.2x. Western Union trades at 6.0x (a declining business with an 11.4% yield), Euronet at 10.2x, NCR Atleos at 20.2x, Payoneer at 25.0x, and Shift4 at 47.9x. EVTC's GAAP multiple sits near the bottom of the cohort, between Western Union (structurally challenged) and Euronet.

Analyst price targets span $25 (low) to $40 (high) with a $31 mean and median, implying ~27% upside to the mean from the current price. Those targets were largely set before the post-Q1 drawdown and against an environment where guidance was raised, which makes the consensus mean look like a reasonable anchor rather than an aggressive one. We do not treat the mean as a floor here, however, because the two unresolved binary catalysts (Popular renewal, tax-grant expiry) are precisely the kind of overhangs that justify a discount.

The valuation is undemanding on adjusted numbers but requires belief that the adjusted figures are the right measure. If a meaningful share of the M&A-related "adjustments" is genuinely recurring, the true earnings multiple is closer to the trailing GAAP level, and the discount narrows. The PR tax rate expiry is the most concrete valuation input: a full normalization of the effective tax rate toward 15%+ would compress net income materially relative to the trailing base, raising the effective P/E. We score valuation a 6.5 — cheap enough to support a constructive lean, but not the clean asymmetry that would justify a higher mark given the tax and contract overhangs.

## Competitive Landscape

EVERTEC occupies a defensible regional niche. Global players (FIS, Fiserv, Global Payments) compete but lack the owned ATH network, the local relationships, and the language/culture advantages that give EVERTEC a one-stop-shop model in its core markets. The ATH network is a genuine moat in Puerto Rico, with multi-year recurring contracts and mission-critical embedded software raising switching costs.

The trajectory is the key question. In Puerto Rico, the competitive position is mature and under modest pressure — the Popular discount is direct evidence of customer bargaining power at renewal. In Latin America, the company is buying its way to scale rather than winning organically, which introduces integration and execution risk against established local players (dLocal, TOTVS) and global entrants (PayPal, Block, Marqeta). The August 2025 Pix incident at Sinqia — roughly R$710M (~$130M) of unauthorized transactions in Brazil's real-time payment system within a year of integration — is a tangible operational-control failure that undercuts confidence in the Brazilian platform's risk infrastructure. We assess competitive position as stable in core markets with improving scale in LatAm, offset by pricing pressure at Popular and integration risk in Brazil, for a score of 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Banco Popular concentration and contract renewals — the dominant risk.** The amended-and-restated MSA expires in 2028, the ISO merchant agreement runs to 2035, and the ATH agreement to 2030. The 2028 renewal could come at materially worse terms; the existing 10% service discount is a preview of pricing power on Popular's side.

**Puerto Rico tax-grant expiry.** The income-tax portion of the 4% preferential rate expires December 31, 2026. With Pillar Two's 15% global minimum effective January 2026, this is a concrete earnings headwind that the market may not be fully discounting in adjusted EPS estimates.

**Cybersecurity.** The Pix incident is a genuine red flag. Management deemed it immaterial and customers reportedly recovered significant portions, but it drove a measurable 2025 cost-of-revenue spike and demonstrates the operational risk embedded in aggressively acquired Brazilian platforms.

**Leverage and variable-rate exposure.** Mostly variable-rate debt, partly hedged, in a still-elevated rate environment. Continued debt-funded M&A (Dimensa via revolver) raises exposure.

**Concentration and FX.** Puerto Rico carries a fragile economy and hurricane exposure, and Real/Peso exposure is growing. Redeemable non-controlling interest ($94M mezzanine) creates put/call obligations on Brazilian subsidiaries.

Because these risks are specific, dated, and not yet fully resolved, we score Risk Factors a 4.5 — below neutral. They are identifiable catalysts for downside, not generic sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 157.6% against one-year historical volatility of 36.7% — a 120.9pp spread, in the 95th percentile of the current chain, with an implied move of roughly ±20% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in steep backwardation (157.6% at one month versus 40.9% at six months), indicating the market expects a discrete event soon. ATM IV has already decompressed from 194.8% to 121.6% over the prior three weeks as the post-earnings shock faded. This is consistent with a stock that gapped down 17% on the May 7 session (from $28.16 to $23.36) and is now digesting the converging contract/tax overhangs. The elevated near-term IV argues against chasing the name on momentum and favors patience; for holders, near-record IV makes covered-call overwriting attractive. Short interest is modest at 4.8% of float with 3.9 days to cover, so positioning is not crowded in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** EVTC is a cash-generative regional payments leader trading at ~6.2x current-year adjusted EPS after a ~36% drawdown, with a credible LatAm growth engine, rising estimates, raised 2026 guidance, and a ~22% FCF margin. The standout signal is the May 2026 cluster of open-market insider purchases — EVP Vizcarrondo ($491K), Director D'Angelo ($468K), COO Castrillo-Salgado (~$455K across three buys), and General Counsel Rodriguez-Gonzalez ($410K) — roughly $1.8M near $22-24, the strongest class of management conviction signal, struck right after a selloff that came despite raised guidance. If the Popular MSA renews on workable terms and the PR tax grant is extended, the stock re-rates toward the peer median and the mean target of $31. Bull case: ~$36 (roughly the prior high, on a re-rating to ~9x adjusted EPS plus continued LatAm growth), about +47% from current.

**Bear Case.** The adjusted earnings overstate true economics: GAAP operating income is falling even as revenue grows, deal costs are recurring, and the roll-up is destroying value while inflating headline figures. The Popular MSA renews at materially worse terms in 2028 (the 10% discount becomes a steeper reset), the PR tax grant lapses and pushes the effective rate above 15%, and another Pix-style operational failure surfaces in Brazil. Net income compresses, leverage constrains buybacks, and the multiple stays low on GAAP earnings. Bear case: ~$18 (roughly 8x a tax-and-discount-impaired GAAP earnings stream), about -26% from current.

The base case sits between these: steady mid-single-digit organic growth plus M&A, a partial tax-grant extension or phased normalization, and a Popular renewal that trims but does not gut the relationship. Base case target ~$28, about +14% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The valuation is cheap and the insider signal is genuine, but two unresolved binary catalysts and a widening GAAP/adjusted gap keep the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We lack sufficient resolution on the Popular renewal and tax-grant outcomes to justify a BUY, and the cash generation, insider buying, and rising estimates rule out an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Confirmation that the Puerto Rico tax grant's income-tax portion is extended or renewed beyond December 31, 2026, removing the largest dated earnings headwind.
2. An early or favorable Popular MSA renewal (or clarity that the 2028 renewal carries no further pricing discount beyond the existing 10%).
3. Evidence the GAAP/adjusted gap is narrowing — GAAP operating income growth reconverging with revenue growth as deal/integration costs roll off — confirming the roll-up is creating value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A Popular MSA renewal at materially worse terms (additional double-digit pricing concession), or a Popular decision to in-source.
2. A second material cybersecurity/operational failure in the Brazilian platform, signaling systemic control weakness.
3. Leverage rising above ~3.0x secured net (toward the 4.0x covenant) on continued debt-funded M&A, combined with falling estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) for guidance follow-through and tax-rate trajectory; year-end 2026 for the tax-grant expiry resolution; and any 8-K disclosure on the Popular relationship. The near-term options market is pricing a discrete event within ~16 days, so the June expiry window is the immediate watch point.

## Conclusion

EVERTEC is a regional payments franchise trading at a depressed multiple after a meaningful drawdown, with rising estimates, raised guidance, and a high-conviction cluster of insider buying near current levels. These are real positives, and on adjusted earnings the stock looks inexpensive. The offsetting reality is that two specific, dated risks — the Banco Popular MSA renewal and the December 2026 expiry of the 4% Puerto Rico tax-grant income-tax portion — converge over the next 6-30 months, the effective tax rate is already rising, GAAP earnings are diverging from a heavily-adjusted base, and a recent Brazilian cyber-fraud event raises operational-control questions about the very platform on which the growth thesis now rests. The combination produces a balanced, two-sided setup rather than a clear mispricing. We rate EVTC a HOLD with a constructive lean, looking to upgrade on resolution of either binary catalyst and to downgrade on evidence of value destruction in the roll-up or a worse-than-expected Popular outcome.
