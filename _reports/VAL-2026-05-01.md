## Executive Summary

Valaris Ltd is one of the world's premier offshore contract drilling companies, operating 46 rigs globally with a fleet anchored by 12 seventh-generation drillships — the most in-demand and highest-specification assets in the industry. The company has executed a strong operational turnaround since emerging from Chapter 11 in April 2021, growing its backlog to $4.67B (+29% YoY), generating $546M in operating cash flow in FY2025, and reducing its share count by ~6% through buybacks at prices well below today's levels.

The defining event for VAL is the February 9, 2026 announcement of an all-stock merger with Transocean (RIG) at a fixed exchange ratio of 15.235 RIG shares per VAL share. At Transocean's current price of approximately $6.60, this implies a merger consideration of ~$100.55 per Valaris share — almost exactly where VAL trades today ($100.66). The market is pricing the deal as near-certain to close. The stock has effectively become a merger-arbitrage vehicle: upside requires either a deal break followed by standalone re-rating, or Transocean stock appreciation. Downside materializes if Transocean shares decline or the deal collapses.

With FY2025 reported EPS of $13.86 heavily distorted by a $523M tax valuation allowance reversal, normalized earnings power is closer to $6-7/share, placing the stock at roughly 14-17x normalized earnings — a premium to standalone offshore drilling valuations. Analyst consensus targets ($47-$98 range, $70.68 mean) sit uniformly below the current price, reflecting pre-announcement fundamental valuations. We rate VAL a HOLD pending merger clarity, with the stock's trajectory now determined almost entirely by the Transocean exchange ratio and deal completion probability.

## Company Overview

Valaris Limited, incorporated in Bermuda, operates one of the largest and most modern offshore drilling fleets globally. The company's 46 rigs include 13 drillships (12 seventh-generation), 2 semisubmersibles, and 31 jackup rigs. Valaris also holds a 50% equity interest in ARO, a joint venture with Saudi Aramco that owns 9 rigs and leases 7 additional rigs from Valaris.

Operations span six continents with 86% of 2025 revenues generated outside the United States. Key clients include Petrobras (13% of revenue), BP (12%), and Azule Energy (10%). The company's strategic focus has been on high-specification floaters and geographic diversification, while actively rationalizing lower-spec assets — 7 rigs were sold or retired in 2025 alone.

The pending Transocean merger, if completed, would create the world's largest offshore drilling company with 73 rigs, 33 floaters, and a $10B pro forma backlog, with $200M+ in identified synergies.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue was essentially flat at $2,369M ($2,363M in FY2024), as fewer operating days offset higher average daily revenues. Operating income surged 35% to $477M, with operating margin expanding from 14.9% to 20.1%. This improvement was driven by a $141M reduction in contract drilling expenses from stacking lower-spec floaters, reduced mobilization costs, and a favorable patent litigation resolution.

Reported net income of $983M (net margin 41.5%) requires significant adjustment. The $523M tax valuation allowance reversal and other discrete tax items inflated net income well above operating reality. Stripping out the tax benefit, underlying net income was approximately $460M, or roughly $6.50/share — the appropriate baseline for valuation.

Average daily revenues improved materially across the fleet:
- **Drillships:** $386K/day (up from $345K in FY2024, +12%)
- **Jackups:** $138K/day (up from $121K in FY2024, +14%)

Contracted backlog rates for new awards range from $416K-$433K/day for drillships, indicating continued repricing momentum that will flow through revenues over the next 2-3 years.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow of $546M represented a 54% increase from $355M in FY2024. Capital expenditures declined to $344M from $455M as reactivation capex for DS-7 and mobilization costs for DS-13/DS-14 wound down. This produced free cash flow of $203M — the first meaningfully positive FCF year since emergence from bankruptcy.

The balance sheet is in reasonable condition for an offshore driller:
- **Cash:** $599M
- **Long-term debt:** $1,086M (8.375% Second Lien Notes due 2030)
- **Undrawn revolver:** $375M (matures 2028)
- **Total liquidity:** ~$975M
- **Debt/equity:** 0.34x (down from 0.48x in FY2024)
- **Altman Z-Score:** 2.19 (grey zone — typical for capital-intensive drillers)

No principal payments are due until 2030, providing substantial runway. However, the merger with Transocean would materially alter this picture — Transocean carries approximately $7B in debt, and the combined entity's leverage profile would be significantly higher than standalone Valaris.

### Share Repurchases

Valaris repurchased $425M of the $600M authorized buyback program at average prices of $49-57/share, reducing shares outstanding from ~75M to ~70.6M. These repurchases were executed at roughly 50-55% of the current stock price. The program is now frozen during the merger pendency period.

## Growth Analysis

### Backlog Provides 2-3 Years of Revenue Visibility

Total backlog of $4.67B (+29% YoY) breaks down as:
- Floaters: $3.03B (+50% YoY)
- Jackups: $1.13B (down slightly due to VALARIS 120 suspension)
- ARO/Other: $0.52B (+90%)

Revenue realization: $1.7B in 2026, $1.8B in 2027, $1.2B in 2028+. This provides strong near-term visibility, though the 2027+ pipeline requires continued new contract awards.

### Industry Supply-Side Tightening

The structural growth thesis rests on supply dynamics. The global floater fleet has contracted 45% from ~280 rigs at the 2014 peak to approximately 150 rigs at end-2025. Fourteen benign-environment floaters were retired in 2025 alone. No new floater construction is underway due to high costs and limited shipyard capacity. This supply attrition is the primary driver of improving day rates.

### Growth Constraints

Revenue growth has plateaued at ~$2.37B. Consensus estimates project $3.33 in EPS for the current year (FY2026), recovering to $6.99 in FY2027 — the wide gap between trailing reported EPS ($13.86) and forward estimates reflects the non-recurring tax benefit washout. Current-quarter estimates have deteriorated significantly: the consensus EPS estimate has fallen from $0.16 ninety days ago to -$0.06 today, a downward revision of $0.22. This signals near-term earnings pressure, likely from contract gaps between rig engagements and mobilization timing.

Marketed fleet utilization declined from ~88% to ~85% by end-2025 for floaters. Three drillships are preservation-stacked without contracts. Saudi Aramco recalled 7 jackups in early 2026, but 37 were originally suspended — the jackup market in the Middle East remains soft.

## Valuation Assessment

At $100.66, Valaris trades at:
- **7.3x trailing EPS** ($13.86) — misleading due to tax benefit
- **~14-17x normalized EPS** (~$6-7)
- **~30x FY2026E EPS** ($3.33 consensus)
- **~14.4x FY2027E EPS** ($6.99 consensus)

Against peers: Noble Corp (NE) trades at 35.2x P/E; Patterson-UTI (PTEN) and Helmerich & Payne (HP) both report negative earnings. The peer median of 35.2x (based on the one profitable comparable) is not particularly useful — the offshore drilling sector is notoriously cyclical and does not command premium multiples on a sustained basis.

VAL's current price of $100.66 almost exactly equals the implied merger consideration (15.235 × ~$6.60 RIG = ~$100.55). The stock is trading as a merger-arb instrument, not as a standalone equity. Analyst price targets range from $47 to $98 with a mean of $70.68 and median of $70.20 — all below the current price. This strongly signals that the market's pricing reflects merger-implied value rather than fundamental intrinsic value.

If the deal breaks, standalone fair value is likely in the $55-75 range based on 8-12x normalized earnings, consistent with analyst targets. This represents 25-45% downside risk. If the deal closes, Valaris shareholders receive Transocean shares — converting a cleaner balance sheet driller into a stake in a more leveraged combined entity.

## Competitive Landscape

**Fleet quality:** 12 of 13 drillships are seventh-generation — the most technologically advanced class, commanding the highest utilization rates and day rates. Many competitors operate older, less capable fleets.

**Geographic diversification:** Operations across six continents reduce dependence on any single basin. The ARO joint venture provides captive access to Saudi Arabia, though Saudi Aramco's 2024 contract suspensions demonstrate this access is not unconditional.

**Consolidation advantage:** The offshore drilling industry has consolidated aggressively. If the Transocean merger closes, the combined entity would control a significant share of the global ultra-deepwater fleet, providing pricing power, operational leverage, and client relationship density that smaller operators cannot match.

**Supply-side dynamics:** With no floater newbuilds underway and the fleet continuing to attrite, the supply of available rigs is shrinking. Seventh-generation drillship operators like Valaris capture a disproportionate share of improving day rates.

The competitive position is improving on a trend basis: rising day rates ($345K to $386K on drillships), growing backlog ($3.6B to $4.67B), and continued fleet attrition all favor Valaris.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Merger Execution — Binary and Dominant
The Transocean merger overwhelms all other risk factors. The all-stock structure at a fixed exchange ratio means Valaris shareholders are exposed to Transocean stock price risk with no collar or minimum value protection.

Key merger risk vectors:
- **Shareholder rejection:** Both Transocean and Valaris shareholders must approve. Activist opposition is possible given Transocean's debt profile.
- **Regulatory delays:** Global operations require multi-jurisdictional review.
- **Market conditions:** If oil prices decline sharply, both stocks would fall, but the deal structure doesn't compensate for this.
- **Leverage absorption:** Post-merger, Valaris shareholders would own ~47% of an entity with ~$8B+ in combined debt, versus their current $1.1B standalone exposure.
- **Termination fee:** $173M payable by Valaris in specified circumstances; $58M expense reimbursement if shareholders reject.

### 2. Oil Price Sensitivity
The 10-Q notes crude oil declined from mid-$70s to low-$60s during 2025 due to OPEC+ production increases. While approximately 70% of expected deepwater project sanctioning through 2030 has breakeven below $50/bbl, further oil price weakness could reduce customer spending and pressure day rates, particularly for floater contracting which slowed since mid-2024.

### 3. ARO Joint Venture Contingent Liability
The $1.1B potential capital contribution obligation for ARO's newbuild program represents a material contingent drain. ARO has never paid cash distributions to Valaris, and the $401M in Notes Receivable are paid in kind. This is a strategic asset with real long-term value but contributes no current cash flow.

### 4. Cyclicality
Offshore drilling is deeply cyclical. Marketed floater utilization has declined from 88% to 85%. Three drillships are stacked without contracts. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised downward over 90 days. Whether this represents a cyclical turn or a temporary pause in a structurally improving market remains unclear, and the distinction matters for post-merger combined entity valuation.

### 5. Short Interest
Short interest at 14.2% of float with 7.1 days to cover is elevated. This likely reflects merger-arbitrage positioning (short VAL/long RIG to capture spread compression) rather than directional bearishness, but it adds event-driven volatility risk.

### Options Market Signal

IV at 59.6% is modestly rich versus 56.2% historical volatility (3.4pp spread), consistent with merger-related uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (59.6% near-term vs. 49.5% six-month), pricing elevated near-term uncertainty that declines as merger close approaches. Put/call OI ratio at 0.70 is call-heavy, consistent with merger-arb positioning. IV compressed 23.2pp over the past two weeks (from 82.8% to 59.6%) as the stock rallied 6.7%, suggesting the market has significantly reduced its assessment of near-term event risk. The 3-month put/call volume ratio of 2.00 with -3.9% put skew indicates some institutional hedging further out, likely sizing for deal-break scenarios.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$120-130)
If the Transocean merger closes and Transocean stock appreciates 20-30% from current levels (plausible if offshore fundamentals continue improving), VAL shareholders would receive shares worth $120-130. Alternatively, if the deal breaks and oil prices remain elevated with continued supply tightening, standalone Valaris could trade at 15-18x normalized $7 EPS = $105-126, supported by backlog and tight supply conditions.

### Bear Case (~$55-70)
If the deal breaks in an unfavorable oil price environment, standalone Valaris would trade at 8-10x normalized EPS of $6-7 = $48-70, consistent with analyst consensus targets. If the deal closes but Transocean stock declines (leveraged balance sheet concern, oil price weakness), the implied VAL value could fall to $75-85. In the severe scenario (deal break + oil collapse), VAL could revisit the $45-55 range seen in late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Deal break with stock pullback to $55-70**: Would present a fundamental entry point at 8-10x normalized earnings for a best-in-class fleet in a supply-constrained industry.
2. **Transocean stock appreciation above $7.50**: Would imply deal value above $114/share, creating clear merger-arb upside from current levels.
3. **New contract awards lifting backlog above $5.5B**: Would indicate accelerating cycle with higher day rates, supporting a standalone valuation premium.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Transocean stock decline below $5.50**: Would imply deal value of ~$84/share, representing 16%+ downside with limited recourse.
2. **Floater marketed utilization drops below 80%**: Would signal cyclical deterioration that undermines both standalone and combined entity value.
3. **FY2026 consensus EPS revised below $2.50**: Would indicate meaningful earnings power degradation.

### Reassessment Timeline
- **Next earnings report** (likely May 2026): Near-term cycle visibility, contract pipeline update
- **Merger shareholder vote**: Expected H2 2026 — the key binary event
- **Transocean Q1/Q2 results**: Will inform the quality of what VAL shareholders would be receiving

## Conclusion

Valaris is a well-run offshore driller with a premium fleet, growing backlog, and improving operational metrics. At a lower stock price, this would be a compelling energy investment thesis with structural supply tailwinds.

At $100.66, however, the stock trades at the implied Transocean merger consideration with essentially zero spread. The investment case reduces to a bet on (a) Transocean stock appreciation, or (b) a deal break at prices that would create a buying opportunity, not a return. Normalized standalone earnings power of $6-7/share places the stock at 14-17x — a premium valuation for a cyclical driller that requires sustained elevated oil prices and continued backlog growth to justify.

The risk/reward at current levels is approximately symmetric: 15-25% upside if the deal closes with Transocean stock appreciation, 25-45% downside if the deal breaks in an unfavorable macro environment. With an all-stock merger structure offering no downside protection and consensus estimates projecting a current-quarter loss, the prudent positioning is HOLD, with the expectation that more attractive entry points will emerge as merger clarity develops or if the stock pulls back toward standalone intrinsic value.
