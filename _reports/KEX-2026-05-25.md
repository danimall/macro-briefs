## Executive Summary

Kirby Corporation is the largest domestic tank barge operator in the United States, controlling approximately 28% of the inland fleet with 1,124 active inland tank barges (as of March 31, 2026), a position protected by the Jones Act, which prohibits foreign-flagged vessels from domestic shipping routes. The company has compounded EPS at a 46% CAGR from 2022 to 2025 ($2.04 → $6.33), driven by marine pricing power in a supply-constrained market and aggressive share repurchases. A second growth vector has emerged in the Distribution & Services segment: power generation equipment for data centers now represents 43% of KDS revenue (up from 36% in 2024), growing at 45% YoY in Q1 2026.

The stock currently trades at $142.42 (as of report date; source: Yahoo Finance), representing approximately 20.3x forward FY2026 EPS (consensus $7.01). The investment case rests on three factors: (1) structural supply scarcity in both inland (~600 barges over 30 years old that could face retirement, only 60-70 new builds for 2026) and coastal markets (zero new ATBs under construction), (2) the power generation tailwind providing secular growth to offset cyclical marine fluctuations, and (3) management's demonstrated commitment to buybacks (3.7M shares repurchased at $96.27 average in 2025, with substantial authorization remaining). The primary risk is a continued softening of inland spot rates, which declined 4-6% in Q4 2025 after rising 6-8% earlier in the year.

Based on available analyst coverage data (source: Yahoo Finance, subject to change), all six covering analysts rate the stock Buy, with a consensus target of approximately $166.33 (16.8% upside) and a range of $158-$175. Estimate revisions have been positive: FY2026 consensus has risen approximately 3.7% over the past 30 days and FY2027 sits at $8.27, implying 18% EPS growth. The combination of a strong regulatory moat, improving capital returns profile, and secular demand exposure from data center buildouts creates a favorable risk/reward at current levels.

## Company Overview

Kirby operates two segments:

**Marine Transportation (KMT) — 58% of 2025 revenue, 74% of operating income:**
Transports petrochemicals (48%), black oil (26%), refined products (23%), and agricultural chemicals (3%) via 1,124 inland tank barges (following Q1 2026 acquisition of 23 barges) and 28 coastal tank barges. The inland fleet operates under 70% term contracts (1-5 year durations), while coastal is 100% contracted. Operating margins reached a record 19.3% in 2025, up from 13.9% in 2023.

**Distribution & Services (KDS) — 42% of 2025 revenue, 26% of operating income:**
Three sub-segments: Commercial & Industrial (46% of KDS), Power Generation (43%), and Oil & Gas (11%). The power generation business distributes and services behind-the-meter power systems, data center electrical distribution equipment, and backup generation — serving data centers, prime power, backup power, nuclear facilities, utilities, municipalities, petrochemical plants, manufacturing facilities, and other industrial users. Oil & Gas declined 32% in 2025 as the industry shifts from diesel to electric fracturing.

The company generated $670M in operating cash flow and $406M in free cash flow in 2025 on revenue of $3.36B. As of March 31, 2026, total debt stood at $983.4M (22.3% debt-to-capitalization), with approximately $635.4M in available liquidity following an amended and restated credit agreement in March 2026 that extended facility maturity to 2031 and increased revolver commitments to $750M.

## Growth Analysis

**Marine Transportation:**
The supply/demand dynamic is the central growth thesis. The industry fleet has been roughly flat at ~4,000 barges since 2019. Approximately 600 barges are over 30 years old, with about 350 of those over 40 years old — aging that could lead to retirement according to Kirby's 10-K. Only 60-70 new barges are on order for 2026. In 2025, 66 new barges entered service while 65 were retired, keeping the fleet approximately stable. New construction costs remain elevated due to steel prices and labor scarcity. In the coastal market, no new specialized ATBs are under construction, which enabled 24-26% price increases in H1 2025 term contracts.

Near-term, inland spot rates declined 4-6% in Q4 2025 and utilization fell to the mid-80s from the low-to-mid 90s. Management attributes this to lighter feedstock mix and seasonal factors, guiding for "continued improvement in pricing as the year progresses" in 2026. The Q1 2026 beat ($1.50 vs. $1.41 estimate) supports this view, though management flagged Q2 headwinds from fuel-cost contract lags (which typically take 30-120 days to flow through escalator/de-escalator clauses).

**Power Generation (Secular Growth Engine):**
This segment grew 26% in 2025 and 45% YoY in Q1 2026. Behind-the-meter power systems and specialized electrical distribution for data centers position Kirby as a beneficiary of the multi-year data center buildout cycle. OEM supply chain delays may push some 2026 revenue into 2027, creating a positive backlog effect. However, the 10-K notes that changes in data-center demand could adversely affect KDS results, and the business is not entirely insulated from broader economic cycles.

**EPS Growth Path:**
FY2026 guidance: EPS up 5-15% (implying $6.65-$7.28). FY2027 consensus (per Yahoo Finance): approximately $8.27 (18% growth). Estimate revisions have been positive across forward periods except the current quarter (-4.7%, likely reflecting the fuel-cost lag headwind management flagged). The full-year and out-year upward trajectory indicates fundamental momentum is intact.

## Competitive Landscape

**Jones Act Moat:** Federal law restricts domestic waterborne transport to U.S.-built, U.S.-flagged, U.S.-owned, and U.S.-crewed vessels. This effectively prevents foreign competition in domestic waterborne transport. While waiver risk exists (as Kirby itself notes in risk factors), such waivers have been politically difficult to obtain historically.

**Scale Advantage:** Kirby's 1,124 inland barges (28% share) dwarf the next largest operators. Scale provides operating efficiency, customer bargaining position, and the financial capacity to acquire distressed competitors. The company purchased 14 barges + 4 towboats for $97.3M in March 2025, 2 barges + 1 towboat for $9.2M in August 2025, and 23 barges in Q1 2026 — a steady consolidation pattern.

**Cost Advantage vs. Alternatives:** Inland barge transport produces 40% less carbon than rail and costs significantly less per ton-mile than trucking. With carbon regulation tightening, structural demand for barge transport strengthens over time.

**Supply Constraint:** With approximately 350 barges over 40 years old that could face retirement and only 60-70 new builds annually, the industry faces a tightening supply outlook absent significant new construction investment. In 2025, new builds roughly matched retirements (66 vs. 65), keeping the fleet stable rather than growing. At current steel and labor costs, new barge economics favor operators of existing tonnage.

**Data Center Power:** Kirby's distribution and service capability for behind-the-meter power systems and data center electrical distribution equipment positions it in a growing niche with limited competition. The "substantial backlog" cited by management and the Meridian fund provides revenue visibility.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Inland Marine Pricing Cyclicality (Moderate-High):**
If petrochemical production volumes decline due to elevated natural gas prices or trade disruption, inland margins could compress from the 19.3% record. A decline to 16-17% (2024 levels) would reduce EPS by approximately $0.40-0.50.

**2. Mariner Shortage / Labor Cost Inflation (Moderate):**
Crew costs are the largest single expense. Industry-wide shortages are driving salary increases (July 2025 adjustments already impacting). This is structural — licensing requirements limit supply response. Management can partially offset via pricing but with a lag on term contracts.

**3. Oil & Gas Exposure Wind-Down (Low-Moderate):**
The $56.3M impairment in Q4 2024 on diesel fracturing equipment demonstrates the risk of technology transitions. Oil & gas is now 11% of KDS (down from 18%) and declining. Further impairments are possible but diminishing in magnitude.

**4. Customer/Commodity Concentration:**
Five KMT customers represent 17% of total revenue. Petrochemical transport (48% of KMT) is tied to the U.S. ethane/propane cost advantage. If Gulf Coast petrochemical production growth stalls due to trade policy changes or higher feedstock costs, volume growth would moderate.

**5. Tariff/Trade Disruption:**
Management explicitly flagged tariff uncertainty in Q1 2026 commentary. Petrochemical export volumes could be impacted by retaliatory trade measures. The current geopolitical environment (elevated oil prices, shipping disruption) adds uncertainty but also supports pricing power on existing domestic routes.

**Options Market Signal:**
Options activity is extremely thin — only 97 contracts across 3 expirations with zero put volume (per report-date options data). This reflects the stock's relatively low liquidity and institutional ownership character rather than informed positioning. The implied move of ±6.5% (1-month) is consistent with the 36.1% historical volatility. No meaningful options signal to interpret given the lack of activity.

## Conclusion

Kirby Corporation offers a differentiated investment in the Industrials sector: a business protected by strong regulatory barriers with structural supply scarcity, improving capital returns, and an emerging secular growth exposure via data center power generation. The stock trades at 20.3x forward earnings with 18% EPS growth expected in FY2027 — a PEG of ~1.1x for a business that faces minimal competitive threat due to Jones Act protection.

The near-term risk of inland pricing softness appears cyclical rather than structural, given the fleet is aging with minimal replacement activity. The combination of tightening supply conditions, data center demand exposure, and aggressive buyback deployment at prices well below current levels creates favorable risk/reward for a 12-month holding period.

The principal risk that prevents higher conviction is the inland market's demonstrated pricing cyclicality — the Q4 2025 weakness shows this business is not immune to short-term volume fluctuations. However, the structural supply tightness, power generation growth, and capital return program support the thesis.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Analyst consensus targets, current price, and valuation multiples are sourced from Yahoo Finance and are time-sensitive; the peer-comparison discount argument relies on an imperfect comp set; and the supply-scarcity thesis reflects aging dynamics that could lead to fleet tightening rather than confirmed mandatory retirements. These factors, if materially different from stated, could reduce the calculated upside.
