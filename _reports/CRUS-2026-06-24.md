**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cirrus Logic is a fabless mixed-signal semiconductor designer that has built a durable, high-margin franchise as the dominant supplier of audio codecs and amplifiers in premium smartphones, with a growing high-performance mixed-signal (HPMS) portfolio. FY26 (ended March 28, 2026) was a record year: revenue $2.0B (+5%), gross margin 52.8%, operating margin 23%, net income $414M (20.7% margin), and free cash flow of $637M at a 31.9% margin. The balance sheet carries roughly $1.1B in liquidity against effectively zero interest-bearing debt. On business quality, this is a top-decile semiconductor operator. The debate is whether the price compensates for a single, large, and worsening dependency.

That dependency is Apple, which represented 91% of FY26 revenue (up from 89% in FY25 and 87% in FY24) and 94% of Q3 FY26 revenue. The diversification strategy management has marketed for years is not yet visible in the revenue mix; the near-term content wins (a FaceID smart power IC, battery/power-management products, the Apple American Manufacturing Program participation) deepen the Apple relationship rather than reduce reliance on it. Layered on top is a quantified IRS transfer-pricing dispute (~$168.3M tax plus ~$63.7M penalties for FY2017–2019, no incremental accrual) that the market may under-appreciate. The stock trades at ~20x trailing/forward earnings after a 49% gain over the past year.

We rate CRUS a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamental momentum is real: four straight earnings beats, +21.9% current-quarter estimate revision over 90 days, and accelerating guidance into the next iPhone cycle. The concentration cliff and the IRS tail leave the risk/reward roughly balanced at the current multiple rather than asymmetric. This continues the rating arc from a single prior BUY to two consecutive HOLDs; we believe HOLD remains correct, and we use this report to make the upgrade and downgrade triggers explicit.

## Company Overview

Cirrus Logic, based in Austin, Texas, designs low-power, high-precision mixed-signal processing chips. It reports one operating segment with two product lines:

- **Audio products** — $1.16B, 58% of FY26 revenue. Smart codecs (including a new 22nm smart codec) and custom boosted amplifiers, primarily for smartphones and increasingly PCs.
- **High-Performance Mixed-Signal (HPMS)** — $837M, 42% of revenue, +10% YoY. Power-management ICs, camera controllers, haptic drivers, and battery/power components.

The competitive moat is the company's design-in position within a specific customer's system architecture, deep analog/mixed-signal IP, and joint-development collaboration with that customer. Custom silicon co-developed over multiple product cycles is hard to displace mid-cycle. The same dynamic produces the central risk, because the customer is overwhelmingly Apple.

Management's three-part strategy is to maintain smartphone audio leadership, grow HPMS content per smartphone, and diversify into PCs, automotive (a new haptics family), professional audio, industrial, and imaging/camera controllers. The first two strategies are working and visible in the numbers. The third remains small relative to the smartphone base.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trend shows steady revenue with consistent margin expansion and strong cash conversion:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.90B | $1.79B | $1.90B | **$2.00B** (+5%) |
| Gross margin | 50.4% | 51.2% | 52.5% | **52.8%** |
| Operating income | $249M | $343M | $410M | **$460M** |
| Net income | $177M | $275M | $332M | **$414M** |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $444M | **$651M** |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | **$637M** |

Several points stand out. Gross margin has risen ~240bps over three years on favorable product mix even as the company conceded pricing on certain products to its largest customer; cost reductions more than offset the ASP erosion. R&D held roughly flat near $434M while declining from 24% to 22% of sales, producing operating leverage. The balance sheet is strong: ~$801M cash plus ~$300M marketable securities, an undrawn $300M revolver, and no meaningful interest-bearing debt. The 1.17 debt/equity figure in screening tools is an artifact of total-liabilities-to-equity accounting (leases and non-current taxes), not borrowings. Altman Z of 3.72 and a current ratio of 7.4 confirm the resilience.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly through buybacks: $280M repurchased in FY26 and $261M in FY25, taking diluted share count from 57.2M to 52.8M over three years. A $500M authorization (March 2025) had ~$344M remaining as of December 2025. There is no dividend.

Two items deserve emphasis for forward modeling. First, the effective tax rate fell from 25.5% (FY25) to 16.6% (FY26), driven by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminating mandatory R&D capitalization under GILTI. Q3 FY26's rate was 13.6%. This is a recurring, structural EPS tailwind, not a one-time benefit. Second, FY26 operating cash flow of $651M was flattered by a ~$103M favorable working-capital swing: inventory drew down from $299M to ~$189M and prepaid-wafer balances unwound. Normalized FCF runs somewhat below the headline 31.9% margin, so we treat the reported figure as a near-term peak rather than a run-rate.

The earnings-surprise record is clean: four consecutive beats (35.1%, 17.1%, 19.5%, 8.6% across the trailing four quarters), which raises our confidence in current consensus. Q4 FY26 adjusted EPS of $1.95 beat the $1.80 consensus, and management guided Q1 FY27 to ~13% YoY revenue growth, an acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a content-per-device and volume story fighting a persistent ASP-erosion treadmill in a maturing smartphone market. The near-term momentum is positive and supported by multiple confirming signals:

- **Estimate revisions are rising at the front end.** Current-quarter consensus moved +21.9% over 90 days to $1.81 (and was essentially flat over the last 30 days, suggesting the move is mostly captured). Next-year EPS ticked up +1.0% (30d) / +1.4% (90d). The current-year figure slipped -3.4% over 90 days, so the picture is front-loaded acceleration rather than a uniform across-the-curve raise.
- **New content catalysts.** A smart power IC supporting Apple's FaceID (the company's first product in this application), battery/power-management content, PC share gains across segments with new amplifiers/codecs/haptics shown at CES 2026, an AI-PC voice-interface component, and a new automotive haptics family.
- **HPMS expansion**, +10% YoY to $837M, is the content-growth engine and the most direct lever on dollar-content-per-phone.

The complication is that the largest growth vectors deepen Apple dependence rather than diversify it. The FaceID IC, battery products, and AMP/Malta NY participation all entrench Cirrus further with one customer through roughly 2030. That raises the revenue ceiling but also the cliff risk. The genuinely diversifying lines — PCs, automotive, general market — remain small against a ~$1.8B Apple base.

We weight growth_prospects above sector-noise dimensions because growth has been the most reliable discriminator of forward returns. Here the signal is solidly positive but not exceptional: ~5% reported revenue growth accelerating to a guided ~13%, with the front-end estimate move largely already in the price after a 49% run. The +21.9% 90-day current-quarter revision is meaningful, but it sits against a stock that is no longer cheap and a growth rate (~5–13%) below the >40% threshold that would mark a true trough inflection. We score growth_prospects 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$157.78 (~$7.9B market cap), CRUS trades at roughly 20x trailing EPS of ~$7.85 and ~16.5x next-year consensus of $9.48. Peer P/E comparison is largely uninformative because the screened semiconductor comparables (MXL, CBRS, SLAB, QRVO, ALGM) carry distorted multiples — several have negative or triple-digit P/Es reflecting losses or extreme growth premiums. The peer median P/E of 494x is meaningless as an anchor. The relevant benchmark is the company's own normalized earnings power and the durability of that power.

On an absolute basis, ~16–20x forward earnings for a debt-free business generating a low-30s% FCF margin, expanding gross margins, a recurring tax tailwind, and a guided acceleration is not stretched. PEG on the guided ~13% growth plus the tax benefit is roughly 1.3–1.5x — reasonable, not cheap. The constraint on a higher valuation score is what the multiple is being paid for. A 91%-Apple revenue base means the entire valuation rests on a single customer relationship that can be reduced "with limited notice and little or no penalty," with no minimum-purchase commitment and Apple's in-house silicon capability as a standing threat. The IRS dispute (~$232M before interest) is an additional unaccrued tail.

Analyst targets (low $140, mean $184.25, median $198.5, high $200) imply meaningful upside to the mean and median. On a high-momentum name with positive front-end revisions and a sub-20x forward multiple, we would normally treat consensus targets as floors rather than ceilings. We apply that lens partially here — the upside is real — but temper it because the Apple-concentration risk is the kind of binary that targets tend to under-discount until it materializes. We score valuation 6.0: reasonable asymmetry to the upside, with a genuine cliff that prevents a higher mark. We deliberately avoid rewarding the ~20x multiple as "cheap" on its own; cheapness without a catalyst that de-risks the concentration is not a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Cirrus competes against Qualcomm, Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, Synaptics, Realtek, Goodix, Awinic, AKM, ST, Skyworks, and Renesas — most with greater scale, broader portfolios, and their own fabs. Two structural disadvantages are worth flagging:

- As a fabless company, Cirrus is ineligible for CHIPS Act subsidies that integrated competitors can capture. The Apple AMP / Malta, NY domestic-manufacturing arrangement partly addresses the customer's desire for U.S.-based supply without requiring Cirrus to build its own fab.
- Chinese competitors (Goodix, Awinic) are rising as China builds a domestic semiconductor industry. Export controls could accelerate Chinese customers toward them, relevant given that ~61% of ship-to revenue is China.

On trajectory, the picture is mixed. Within its core franchise, Cirrus is gaining content per device with Apple and winning early share in PCs, which argues for an improving position. The company's competitive standing is increasingly a function of one customer's roadmap rather than broad market-share gains across diversified end markets. The moat is deep but narrow, and narrowing further as Apple's revenue share climbs. We score competitive_position 5.5: strong in its niche, but the trajectory toward greater single-customer dependence offsets the content-gain story, and we confirm rather than over-weight this dimension given its recently softer predictive strength.

## Risk Assessment

**Apple concentration (the defining risk).** Top-10 customers are 96–97% of revenue. Any architecture change, in-sourcing, or share-shift by Apple would be severe and could materialize with little notice. This is the single largest unpriced-tail risk and the primary reason the overall score is capped.

**Geographic and supply-chain concentration.** 99% of sales are international. Wafers come from TSMC (Taiwan) and GlobalFoundries (Singapore/Germany/Malta NY). Taiwan-China tensions, tariffs, and export controls are live exposures.

**GlobalFoundries Capacity Reservation Agreement.** A take-or-pay wafer commitment through calendar 2026 ($60M reservation fee, $10M remaining; $195M prepayment, $33M remaining). Undershooting demand creates excess inventory and higher unit costs; falling wafer market prices would leave Cirrus disadvantaged versus the contract.

**IRS transfer-pricing dispute (quantified).** Cirrus disputes the assertion, is in IRS Appeals since July 2024, and has not accrued beyond existing ASC 740 reserves. An adverse outcome, potentially litigated and years away, could be material to cash flows. This partly offsets the OBBBA tax tailwind.

**Structural ASP erosion** in a maturing smartphone market, and a newly added H-1B weighted-lottery risk factor (Q3 10-Q) that raises talent-cost risk for this R&D-heavy firm.

**Unread material event.** The 5/6/2026 8-K carried Item 1.01 (material agreement) and Item 2.03 (new direct financial obligation). With the existing revolver maturing July 8, 2026, this is most likely a refinancing and/or a GlobalFoundries amendment — probably benign, but unconfirmed and worth verifying.

We score risk_factors 4.0. The combination of a worsening single-customer dependency that can reverse abruptly and a quantified ~$232M tax tail represents specific, material downside not fully reflected at ~20x. Low-risk scores have not reliably predicted outperformance recently, so we treat this as a downside flag on the thesis rather than a primary scoring driver.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$200, ~27% upside).** Apple content per device keeps rising — FaceID smart power, battery/power management, and HPMS expansion drive dollar-content growth into the next iPhone cycle, validating the ~13% Q1 FY27 guide and beyond. PC and automotive diversification begins to show in the mix, reducing the perceived cliff risk and supporting multiple expansion. The OBBBA tax rate holds near 16%, the IRS dispute settles for an immaterial amount, and buybacks shrink the share count further. On ~$9.50–$10.50 forward EPS at ~19–20x, the stock reaches the $200 analyst high.

**Bear case (~$118, ~25% downside).** Apple shifts architecture, in-sources, or pushes additional pricing concessions, and revenue concentration becomes the channel for a sharp earnings reset. The IRS dispute resolves adversely, taking cash and re-rating the tax-rate assumption higher. A China demand or export-control shock hits the ship-to base. On a derated multiple against pressured earnings, the stock falls toward the mid-teens P/E on lower numbers, roughly $115–$120.

**Base case (~$178, ~13% upside).** Content growth and the tax tailwind deliver next-year EPS near $9.48, the multiple holds around 18–19x, and Apple concentration neither worsens dramatically nor reverses. The IRS dispute stays unresolved but contained. This lands between the analyst median and the bear case and reflects a high-quality compounder whose upside is throttled by concentration risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$178** (≈18.8x next-year EPS of $9.48), +13% from current. The multiple is consistent with a debt-free, high-FCF franchise growing low-double-digits with a structural tax benefit, discounted modestly for concentration.

**Bull case target: ~$200**, +27%, matching the analyst high.

**Bear case target: ~$118**, -25% from current.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the Q1 FY27 print (testing the ~13% guide), the new iPhone cycle ramp in the September/December quarters, and progress on the IRS Appeals process.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Genuine diversification appears in the mix — non-Apple revenue rising as a percentage of total for two consecutive quarters, or PC/automotive crossing a material share threshold.
2. A pullback toward the low-to-mid $130s (≈14x next-year EPS), restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
3. Favorable IRS resolution at an immaterial amount, removing the ~$232M tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any signal of Apple reducing content, in-sourcing, or forcing a step-change in pricing concessions (watch the Apple revenue-share disclosure and gross-margin trajectory).
2. An adverse IRS Appeals or litigation outcome.
3. Two consecutive quarters of declining HPMS revenue or a guidance cut below mid-single-digit growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q1 FY27, typically late July/early August), with an interim check on the 5/6/2026 8-K exhibit details and any China/export-control developments.

## Conclusion

Cirrus Logic is a high-quality, well-managed, debt-free semiconductor franchise generating exceptional free cash flow, expanding margins, a clean four-quarter beat record, and accelerating near-term guidance, supported by a recurring tax tailwind. None of the business-quality concerns that would drive a low rating are present. What caps the rating is a concentration profile that is worsening rather than improving — Apple at 91% of FY26 revenue and 94% in the latest quarter — combined with a quantified ~$232M IRS tax tail, both sitting under a ~20x multiple after a 49% one-year advance.

The result is a reasonable but not asymmetric setup. The growth momentum and balance-sheet quality justify holding for the content-growth thesis; the concentration cliff and tax tail prevent us from paying up for it as a BUY at the current price. We rate CRUS HOLD (growth_hold), with a clear path to upgrade on either genuine diversification, a meaningful pullback, or resolution of the IRS dispute, and to downgrade on any sign of Apple reducing content or an adverse tax outcome. This maintains the prior two HOLD calls; we base the continued HOLD on the unchanged structural concentration risk rather than on price action, and note that the ~8% decline since the last reset modestly improves entry without altering the core constraint.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q4 FY26 EPS beat was vs. the $1.80 consensus in Phase 1 data (not $1.76), and next-year forward P/E is ~16.5x (not 17.6x) on $9.48 EPS — a modestly cheaper valuation than stated, marginally more supportive of the upside case.
