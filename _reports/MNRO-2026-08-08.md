## Executive Summary

Monro operates 1,115 company-owned tire and undercar service stores in 32 states. The June 2026 quarter confirmed the operating turnaround is working; the investment question is whether the activist-driven event path is worth enough to offset a deteriorating cash-generating base and a 2027 refinancing.

The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended June 27, 2026) is the decisive data point. Sales fell 4.6% to $287.1M, comparable store sales turned **-1.7%** after the company closed 145 unprofitable stores in the prior year and reported +1.4% comps for fiscal 2026, and **adjusted operating income fell 85% to $2.157M from $14.034M**. Adjusted diluted EPS swung from +$0.22 to -$0.09. GAAP operating income of $3.695M exceeded the adjusted figure because a $2.960M net gain on store closures was stripped out, so GAAP profitability is currently supported by asset-disposal gains. Operating cash flow was **-$30.4M** against -$1.9M a year earlier, and the revolver balance went from $60.0M at fiscal year-end to **$108.4M** in three months while $8.7M of dividends was paid. Current-year EPS estimates are down 82% over 90 days to $0.12, and next year is down 56% to $0.41, from five covering analysts.

Against that, the equity carries real option value. Carl Icahn holds roughly 16% at a reported average cost of $19.08, about 50% above today's price, with a history of taking auto-aftermarket targets private (Pep Boys, Federal-Mogul). The board announced a strategic-alternatives review on May 27, 2026, the Class C veto structure that had blocked shareholder action for decades converted away on June 18, 2026, and the one-year rights plan adopted with a 17.5% trigger (roughly 0.6 points above Icahn's then-16.9% position) **expires November 6, 2026**. With 37.0% of the float short and 5.5 days to cover, any credible bid produces a violent move higher. That combination argues against shorting and against AVOID. It does not make the expected return positive: on base/bull/bear values of $10.50 / $18.00 / $7.50 and a reasonable weighting, probability-weighted value is roughly $11.20, about 12% below the current price. **REDUCE** — trim into event-driven strength; new capital has no reason to be here.

## Company Overview

Monro is a single-segment U.S. retail tire and automotive service operator, incorporated in New York in 1959 and headquartered in Fairport, NY. It serviced approximately 3.8 million vehicles in fiscal 2026 across a roll-up of regional banners: Tire Choice (297), Mr. Tire (297), Monro Auto Service (296), Tire Warehouse (51), Car-X (49), Ken Towery's (30), Mountain View (29), Tire Barn (26) and 40 others, plus 46 franchised Car-X locations. There is no single national marque and no customer concentration; roughly 6,440 employees, none unionized.

Fiscal 2026 revenue mix: tires 48%, maintenance service 27%, brakes 13%, steering 9%, batteries 2%, other 1%. Management states explicitly that tires are a lower-margin category, so the drift toward tires (47% in FY2025 → 48% in FY2026 → 46% in Q1 FY2027, with brakes at 15%) is a margin-mix signal rather than diversification.

Two structural features deserve emphasis. First, in June 2022 Monro sold its wholesale operations and internal tire distribution assets to American Tire Distributors and simultaneously contracted with ATD as its **supplier** of certain tires and as the **provider** of category management, ordering and inventory managed services. Monro sold a core capability and now rents it. Second, purchasing concentration is severe: approximately 43 vendors, with the ten largest at **88% of stocking purchases** and the largest single vendor at **35%**, and certain contracts requiring up to 100% of annual purchases of specific products from one supplier.

The Business section of the 10-K describes no quantified cost advantage, no proprietary technology and no exclusive real estate. Stated advantages — scale purchasing, a tire pricing system, "Good, Better, Best" packages, staffing software, Monro University — are operational practices available to competitors. The 10-K's own competition discussion concedes that some competitors "have greater financial resources, have access to more developed distribution networks, have business models with lower operating costs, are more geographically diverse and have better name recognition than we do."

## Financial Analysis

### Five years of revenue erosion and extreme operating deleverage

| Fiscal year (ends late March) | Revenue | YoY | Operating income | Net income | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $1,359.3M | — | — | $61.6M | — |
| FY2023 | $1,325.4M | -2.5% | $79.8M | $39.0M | ~$1.20 |
| FY2024 | $1,276.8M | -3.7% | $71.4M | $37.6M | ~$1.18 |
| FY2025 | $1,195.3M | -6.4% | $12.6M | -$5.2M | -$0.22 |
| FY2026 | $1,157.2M | -3.2% | $20.0M | $2.2M | $0.03 |

Revenue compounded at **-4.4% annually** from FY2023 to FY2026 (13% cumulative), while operating income fell 75%. That ratio — roughly 6x operating decline per unit of revenue decline — quantifies how fixed the cost base is: occupancy on 1,115 leased and owned locations plus technician labor.

Fiscal 2026's headline improvement ("operating income 59.4% higher") is a comparison artifact; the prior year carried $24.4M of store impairment charges. On management's own reconciliation, **adjusted operating income fell 11.2% to $35.774M and adjusted diluted EPS fell 12.5% to $0.42**. The +1.4% comp was produced with the 145 worst stores removed from the base and **$14.1M of incremental store advertising**.

### Q1 FY2027: the mechanism reversed

Comps went to -1.7% with the purge complete and advertising up another $4.9M. Gross margin fell 50bps to 35.0%, driven by -90bps of occupancy deleverage partially offset by +40bps of technician labor productivity; the two components indicate the problem is volume through a fixed store footprint, not labor cost. Adjusted operating income of $2.157M on $287.1M of revenue is a 0.75% adjusted operating margin.

### Cash flow: the dividend is not earned

This is the decision-critical arithmetic, and it is reproducible from the filings.

Fiscal 2026:
- Operating cash flow **$70.438M**
- Less capital expenditures **-$31.657M**
- Less principal on finance leases and financing obligations **-$38.700M**
- = discretionary cash flow **≈ $0.08M**
- Dividends paid **-$35.0M** ($1.12/share)

Monro's store base is heavily financed through finance leases and financing obligations ($230.0M of liabilities at fiscal year-end; $278.6M undiscounted). Principal on those sits in financing, not operating, so the conventional "OCF less capex" figure of $38.8M materially overstates discretionary cash. Once finance-lease principal is deducted, fiscal 2026 covered essentially none of the dividend from recurring operations. The gap was bridged by **$27.0M of proceeds from disposing of closed stores plus $3.5M of final wholesale-divestiture proceeds** — non-recurring sources. Management would fairly argue corporate cash is fungible and that closures reduce future burn; both readings are defensible. What is not in dispute is that recurring operations did not cover the payout after lease principal.

Q1 FY2027 is starker: OCF -$30.388M, capex $7.531M, finance-lease principal $9.275M, dividends $8.743M, funded by a **net $48.435M revolver draw**. I will not annualize this figure — Q1 is the seasonal trough (January–April and September are the slowest months) and the quarter contained roughly $43M of accounts-payable and accrued-expense reversals management attributes to payment timing, including accrued payroll falling from $21.9M to $13.1M. The meaningful number is the **$28.4M year-over-year deterioration** against a prior-year Q1 of -$1.9M.

As a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast: if fiscal 2027 OCF simply repeated fiscal 2026's $70.4M, capex ($25–35M guided) plus finance-lease principal (~$37–39M) consumes $62–74M before any dividend. The $35M payout would again require asset sales — of which little remains, assets held for sale having fallen from $13.0M to **$3.2M** — or further borrowing.

### Balance sheet: goodwill is the equity

At June 27, 2026: total assets $1,556.5M, total liabilities $974.0M, shareholders' equity $582.5M. **Goodwill $736.4M** — unchanged at exactly that figure since fiscal 2023 despite revenue falling 13% and operating income 75% — plus $7.1M of intangibles. Tangible book equity is therefore **-$161.0M**. Goodwill is 47.3% of assets and **1.85x the entire market capitalization**.

Debt structure: revolver $108.4M, finance leases and financing obligations $220.9M, operating lease liabilities $198.5M, cash $9.5M. Cash was $2.4M on May 15, 2026 — the company runs on the revolver.

Derived enterprise value at $12.73: $398.0M equity + $108.4M revolver + $220.9M finance leases - $9.5M cash = **$717.8M**. Against fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $97.4M (adjusted operating income $35.774M + D&A $61.674M), that is **7.4x**. Net debt of $319.8M is **3.28x** that EBITDA. Add operating leases and enterprise value is ~$916M.

The working capital deficit of $241.1M is substantially a financing structure, not a distress read: the **supplier finance program** carried $205.9M of eligible obligations inside accounts payable at quarter-end. But note the direction — the balance fell from $231.7M a year earlier, a ~$26M decline that mechanically consumes cash, and participation is at the discretion of both suppliers and the financing bank.

Screening scores read consistently but need care. Altman Z of 1.46 places Monro in the distress zone; the score penalizes deliberately negative retail working capital while simultaneously being flattered by $736M of goodwill in its equity terms. The genuinely alarming component is **EBIT/assets of 1.3%**, which is an operating fact rather than an artifact.

### Trailing earnings, reconciled

The data set reports EPS of $0.027 (fiscal 2026 full year) alongside a trailing P/E of 55.4x. Those are inconsistent with each other; 55.4x corresponds to trailing-twelve-month diluted GAAP EPS of **$0.23**, which reconciles as $0.03 (FY2026) - (-$0.28) (Q1 FY2026) + (-$0.08) (Q1 FY2027). On that basis: 55x trailing GAAP earnings, roughly 116x trailing adjusted EPS of ~$0.11, and 30x fiscal 2026 full-year adjusted EPS of $0.42. The reported forward P/E of 55.4x is identical to the trailing figure and should be disregarded as a stale field; against the current-year consensus of $0.12, the forward multiple is above 100x.

The dividend yield is reported at 8.52%; the $0.28 quarterly rate maintained in Q1 annualizes to $1.12, or **8.80%** at $12.73. Payout is 487% of trailing GAAP EPS and roughly 10x trailing adjusted EPS.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis to underwrite, and the estimate revisions are unambiguous:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 90d revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.05 | $0.16 | $0.23 | **-76.9%** |
| Current year | $0.12 | $0.33 | $0.67 | **-82.0%** |
| Next year | $0.41 | $0.62 | $0.93 | **-55.9%** |

A -82% current-year revision over 90 days from five analysts, following a Q1 miss of -712% against a $0.01 estimate, is about as negative a forward signal as this data set produces. The only positive line — next quarter revised +21.6% over 90 days — is a rounding artifact within a collapsing full-year number.

Structural demand headwinds identified in the 10-K are specific rather than boilerplate. EVs lack traditional engines, transmissions and related parts; management expects demand for transmission, exhaust and oil-change services to decrease, and notes OEMs may restrict access to diagnostic tools and repair information. That exposure lands squarely on maintenance service (27% of revenue, largely oil changes) and exhaust — the category the company was founded on. Tires (46–48%) are EV-neutral to EV-favorable given heavier vehicles and faster wear, which limits the aggregate damage but does nothing for margin, since tires are the acknowledged low-margin line.

Consumer behavior confirms trade-down: Q1 maintenance-service comps were **-5%** while batteries were **+8%** — deferral of discretionary work, continued purchase of failure-driven replacement. Management's own language is that as vehicles age, willingness to pay higher prices decreases and customers may delay or forgo maintenance.

Self-help levers are largely spent. Store closures took the base from 1,260 to 1,115. Assets held for sale are down to $3.2M. The impairment-comparison tailwind is gone. Management guided in May 2026 to year-over-year comparable store sales growth in fiscal 2027; Q1 delivered -1.7%. Two of the last four quarters missed consensus, both of them the most recent two.

Store-level economics constrain any recovery: revenue per store is approximately **$1.04M** ($1,157.2M / 1,115), against a footprint carrying $198.5M of operating-lease and $220.9M of finance-lease obligations.

## Valuation Assessment

Three lenses, all pointing the same direction once you use current rather than trailing earnings power.

**Earnings multiples.** 55x trailing GAAP EPS of $0.23, above 100x current-year consensus of $0.12, and 31x next-year consensus of $0.41. The peer median in automotive retail is 22.9x (SAH 26.7x, PHIN 22.9x, AAP 54.1x, GPI 12.5x, ABG 7.1x). Monro trades at a premium to that median on collapsing earnings; the multiple is a denominator problem, not a valuation support.

**Enterprise value.** 7.4x fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $97.4M is not obviously expensive in isolation, but that EBITDA is stale. Q1 adjusted operating income fell $11.9M year over year; if the full year retains even half of that decline, adjusted EBITDA lands near $80–85M and the multiple becomes 8.4–9.0x for a business with four consecutive years of revenue decline, negative tangible equity and a 2027 maturity. Including $198.5M of operating leases, enterprise value is ~$916M, or 9.4x fiscal 2026 EBITDA before any adjustment for rent.

**Takeout math, which is the actual bull case.** Working backwards from net debt of $319.8M (revolver + finance leases - cash), an acquirer paying 8x $85M EBITDA generates $680M of enterprise value, $360M of equity, **$11.51/share**. At 9x: $14.24. At 10x: $16.97. To reach Icahn's $19.08 average cost requires roughly 11x forward EBITDA for a shrinking single-segment service retailer, or a materially higher EBITDA base than Q1 implies. The leverage means EBITDA multiples translate into per-share values with a $10.20/share debt drag, which constrains the bull case.

**Yield support is unreliable.** An 8.8% dividend at 487% of GAAP earnings, funded by revolver draws, is not a valuation floor. There is also a concrete contractual mechanism: the Sixth Amendment requires **minimum liquidity of $200M to declare dividends**. Liquidity at June 27, 2026 was approximately $271M ($261.5M availability plus $9.5M cash) — roughly **$71M of cushion**. The Q1 draw alone was $48.4M. Two more quarters resembling Q1 would block the dividend contractually, independent of any board decision.

**Analyst targets are stale and should be treated as such.** The published range is low $15.00, high $40.00, mean $22.00, median $16.50. Every target sits above the current price, none appears to have been reset for a -82% current-year estimate revision, and the $40 high is more than 3x the current price. The five-analyst distribution (2 buy, 3 hold, unchanged for four months) has not been marked to the July 29 print. On a stock where estimates fell 82% while targets did not move, targets are not independent confirmation of anything.

A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong. Here, the compressed EV/EBITDA reflects genuine structural impairment — negative tangible equity, a shrinking revenue base, a refinancing cliff, and an unfunded payout — with a real but unquantified event catalyst layered on top. That combination supports a **4.0**, not a value score.

## Competitive Landscape

The most credible evidence on competitive position comes from Monro's own proxy. Over the five-year pay-versus-performance measurement window, indexed total shareholder return went to **28** (a -72% cumulative return) while the company's self-selected peer group went to **137** (+37%). That is roughly 109 percentage points of company-specific underperformance in a sector that performed well. This is not a sector problem.

The structural threat is channel migration. The 10-K names "online tire merchandisers that partner with local service centers to provide installation services" among competitors. If the tire purchase — 46–48% of revenue and the traffic driver — migrates online, Monro is left competing for the lower-value installation while carrying $419M of combined lease obligations. Having sold its tire distribution assets to ATD and contracted the capability back, Monro has less control over assortment and availability than it did in 2022, in a category where those attributes drive traffic.

Industry fragmentation cuts both ways. It is the basis for Monro's stated consolidation opportunity and presumably part of Icahn's thesis given Icahn Automotive. It also means thousands of independent operators with lower fixed costs and no public-company overhead — the "lower operating costs" competitors the 10-K concedes exist. Monro's own experience is the cleanest evidence: it closed 11.5% of its store base as unprofitable, and the surviving base then produced negative comps.

Where cost discipline has been applied, it has worked. Other selling, general and administrative expense fell $19.8M in fiscal 2026 with closed stores contributing $25.1M of savings; net interest expense fell from $22.9M (FY2023) to $17.2M (FY2026); technician labor productivity improved 40bps in Q1. The closure program generated $22.1M of gross gains and $25.3M of proceeds against $14.8M of closing costs. The problem is not execution on cost; it is that the revenue line will not cooperate.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing, November 10, 2027.** The amendment history is the clearest external read on the credit. Three consecutive annual amendments (May 2024, May 2025, May 2026) reduced the minimum interest coverage covenant from 1.55x to as low as 1.15x and now 1.25x through maturity, while progressively broadening EBITDAR add-backs from store-closure items to non-cash impairment to non-cash pension accounting charges. The facility was **permanently cut from $600M to $500M to $400M** — a third of committed capacity — at a 225bp spread. The minimum liquidity test for declaring dividends stepped down $400M → $300M → $200M. Monro has never breached and was compliant at both March 28 and June 27, 2026. But by the time the fiscal 2027 10-K is filed around May 2027, the $108M drawn balance plus a $30.1M letter of credit becomes a **current** liability at a company with -$161M of tangible equity and deteriorating EBITDA. Markets typically price that well before the maturity date. Covenant compliance cannot be reproduced from disclosed data — the credit agreement's definitions of interest coverage, adjusted debt and EBITDAR are not disclosed in sufficient detail — so any leverage estimate should be treated as indicative.

**Goodwill impairment.** The fiscal 2026 10-K discloses that a sustained fourth-quarter market-cap decline was treated as a **triggering event**, and that a quantitative single-reporting-unit test as of March 28, 2026 found fair value exceeded carrying value — supported by "operational changes... that we believe will lead to improvements in the performance of the business and cash flows," with an explicit warning that failure to realize those benefits could produce impairment. One quarter later comps were -1.7%, adjusted operating income was down 85%, and the shares fell a further 18% on July 29. The probability of impairment at the next test date is materially higher than it was in May. Two qualifications matter: impairment is **non-cash**, would not consume liquidity, and the Fifth Amendment already permits non-cash impairment add-backs in covenant EBITDAR — so this is a book-equity and sentiment risk rather than a solvency event. The margin by which fair value exceeded carrying value is undisclosed and could be large.

**Dividend.** Both discretionary risk (487% GAAP payout, funded by borrowings) and contractual risk (~$71M of cushion above the $200M liquidity test). A cut removes the yield buyer base.

**Off-balance-sheet items.** $18.2M of maximum potential payments under guarantees on 36 assigned leases (options expiring September 2026 to January 2044) against a $1.3M recorded liability — Monro is the guarantor, the assignee the primary obligor, the landlord the beneficiary. On assignee default Monro retains the right to assume the lease. The closure program did not fully sever the liabilities.

**Supplier finance dependency.** $205.9M of eligible obligations, with participation at the discretion of both supplier and financing institution, and a disclosed caveat that actual receivables sold may be lower. A pullback here is an immediate working-capital drain.

**Governance and event risk, cutting both ways.** The 17.5% rights-plan trigger was set roughly 0.6 percentage points above Icahn's then-16.9% stake, freezing the accumulation while preserving the appearance of not disenfranchising him. I describe calibration, not motive — a 17.5% threshold is within ordinary practice, and the plan's one-year duration and unanimous adoption are governance-positive features. The plan **expires November 6, 2026**. The Class C veto — which had required 60% of Class C shares to validate any common-shareholder action — converted on June 18, 2026, one business day before the annual-meeting record date, at a cost of 1,204,908 new shares (+4.0% of the pre-conversion base, reducing existing holders to ~96.1% of their prior proportional ownership). The 2026 annual meeting is the first shareholder vote without a controlling veto.

**Not shortable.** Short interest is **37.0% of float** (6.34M shares, 5.5 days to cover). At that level, with a live strategic review and a pill expiry inside four months, the squeeze risk on any transaction headline dominates the incremental fundamental downside. This is a position to exit, not to short.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 22 contracts across four expirations — so read it with low confidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 62.8% against 57.9% one-year realized, modestly rich, having collapsed from 104.4% on August 2 as the earnings event resolved. The term structure is in **backwardation** (62.8% at one month versus 56.6% at six months), which the data source characterizes as pricing imminent stress. The most informative change is positioning: put/call open interest went from **0.29 to 1.59** in six sessions, and put/call volume is 1.45 — protection or bearish exposure being accumulated after the print rather than before it. The one-month skew of -21.8% is labeled call-heavy while the three- and six-month tenors show mildly positive readings; given the contract count and the mixed sign across tenors, I would not draw a directional conclusion from skew here. The six-month implied move of ±31% is the cleanest signal: the market is pricing a genuine binary over the window that contains the pill expiry and any strategic-review outcome, which is consistent with the fundamental read of a wide, two-sided, event-driven setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.00 (+41%)

A transaction. Icahn holds ~16% at a reported $19.08 average cost, has taken two auto-aftermarket companies private after activist campaigns, and now faces a board that has already announced a strategic review, lost its dual-class veto, and holds a rights plan that lapses November 6, 2026. A buyer paying 9–10x a normalized $85–90M of adjusted EBITDA generates $14–17/share after $320M of net debt; add value for owned real estate, franchise assets or a sponsor willing to underwrite the closure playbook, and $18 is reachable. With 37% of the float short, the announcement move would overshoot fair value. Secondary supports: the CEO bought ~$502K of stock in the open market in February 2026 at a weighted-average $19.23 and is now ~34% underwater with no offsetting officer open-market sales; cost discipline has demonstrably worked where applied ($19.8M of OSG&A reduction, interest expense down $5.7M since FY2023); the pension is overfunded by ~$1.4M; and Q1 is the seasonal trough, so a Q2–Q4 comp recovery into the tire seasons would validate the goodwill DCF and keep the dividend intact.

### Bear Case — $7.50 (-41%)

The review ends without a transaction, which is the modal outcome for announced strategic reviews. Comps stay negative into fiscal 2027 as consumers defer maintenance; occupancy deleverage compounds at roughly 90bps per quarter of negative comp; adjusted EBITDA settles near $70–75M. The dividend is cut or contractually blocked once liquidity approaches $200M, and the yield-driven shareholder base exits. A goodwill write-down takes book equity toward zero, and the November 2027 maturity becomes a current liability in the fiscal 2027 10-K with the equity valued as a stub on $320M of net debt plus $198M of operating leases. At 6.5x $72M EBITDA, enterprise value of $468M leaves $148M of equity, or **$4.73/share**; $7.50 assumes a partial recovery from that or credit for asset value.

### Base Case — $10.50 (-17%)

Fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITDA of $82–85M (fiscal 2026's $97.4M less roughly $13–15M, consistent with Q1's $11.9M deterioration partially offset by seasonal recovery). At 7.5x, enterprise value of $625M less net debt of $311M (assuming seasonal revolver paydown) yields $314M of equity, **$10.04/share**; $10.50 allows for modest credit for the strategic review remaining live. The dividend is trimmed rather than eliminated.

Probability weighting — 25% transaction, 35% muddle-through, 40% deterioration — produces roughly $11.20 per share, about 12% below the $12.73 basis. That negative expected value with no margin of safety is the recommendation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Horizon: 3–9 months, bounded by the November 6, 2026 pill expiry and the fiscal 2027 second- and third-quarter prints.**

**De-risking plan.** Holders should trim on strength rather than at the current level. Two specific windows: (1) any strategic-review or bid headline, which with 37% short interest should produce a spike materially above fair value — sell into it; (2) the November 6, 2026 pill expiry, which either catalyses renewed accumulation or removes the last near-term event support. Target residual exposure of zero to one-third of a normal position, held only as a call option on a transaction, and sized so that a dividend cut and a move to $8 is tolerable. No new capital.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD (need at least two):**
1. Q2 FY2027 comparable store sales positive **and** adjusted operating income above $14M (i.e., at least flat year over year), demonstrating Q1 was seasonal-plus-timing rather than trend.
2. Operating cash flow for the first half of FY2027 positive and the revolver balance back below $80M, showing the Q1 working-capital reversal genuinely unwound.
3. A confirmed refinancing or extension of the November 10, 2027 facility, at a spread not materially wider than 225bp and without a further capacity reduction.
4. A definitive agreement to sell the company at $16 or above.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE would additionally require** the dividend rebased to a level covered by cash flow after finance-lease principal (roughly $0.10–0.12 per quarter on current economics), plus evidence of positive comps for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Dividend suspended **and** no strategic-review outcome by the fiscal 2027 third-quarter print — removes both the income and event supports simultaneously.
2. Liquidity (revolver availability plus cash) falling below approximately $230M, putting the $200M dividend-declaration test within one quarter's reach.
3. Public termination of the strategic-alternatives review without a transaction, or a 13D/A indicating Icahn is reducing.
4. A seventh credit-agreement amendment, or any further permanent facility reduction below $400M.
5. Q2 or Q3 comps worse than -3%, which would put fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITDA below $75M and make the 2027 refinancing genuinely difficult.

**Next reassessment: the fiscal 2027 second-quarter report (late October/early November 2026), which lands within days of the November 6 pill expiry.** That is the single highest-information date in the calendar for this name.

## Conclusion

Monro's score trajectory has moved 4.1 → 3.9 → 3.9 → 4.0, with three AVOID ratings followed by a HOLD. The July 29 print supplies specific fundamental deterioration that justifies moving lower on both score and rating: comparable store sales reversed to -1.7% after the closure program removed the worst 11.5% of stores, adjusted operating income — the metric that strips closure noise in both directions — fell 85%, operating cash flow deteriorated $28.4M year over year, the revolver draw rose $48.4M in a single quarter, and five analysts cut current-year EPS 82% in 90 days. This is a price decline with an identifiable fundamental cause.

What prevents an AVOID is that the event path is real, dated and asymmetric against bears. Icahn sits at roughly 16% with a cost basis 50% above the market, the board is running a strategic review, the dual-class veto that protected incumbents for decades disappeared in June, the rights plan lapses November 6, and 37% of the float is short with 5.5 days to cover.
