**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Paper enters mid-2026 as a company whose reported financials badly misrepresent its operating reality. The 2025 GAAP net loss of $3.52 billion, the Altman Z-score of 1.71 ("distress"), and the Piotroski score of 1/9 are almost entirely products of non-cash, one-time charges tied to a transformative reshaping: a $2.47 billion goodwill impairment on the just-acquired PS EMEA (DS Smith) unit, $958 million of accelerated depreciation from mill closures, $626 million of restructuring, and a $1.07 billion impairment on the divested Global Cellulose Fibers business. Strip these out and 2025 adjusted EBITDA was roughly $3.0 billion with operating cash flow of $1.70 billion. The standardized data pull showing a -6.7x trailing P/E and a -$5.61 EPS would lead a casual reader to mistake a leveraged, mid-transformation cyclical for a bankruptcy candidate. It is neither.

Q1 2026 confirmed the operating recovery: continuing-operations earnings of +$76 million ($0.14 EPS, versus -$124 million a year earlier), adjusted EBITDA of $677 million, and free cash flow of +$94 million against -$618 million in the prior-year quarter. Management directed $660 million of GCF divestiture proceeds to debt paydown, cutting long-term debt from $8.84 billion to $8.18 billion. The 2026 guide is $3.2-3.5 billion adjusted EBITDA and $300-500 million free cash flow. The investment debate has shifted from solvency to whether the planned EMEA spin-off (12-15 months out), the $710 million of actioned cost-out, and the 80/20 operating overhaul produce a sum-of-the-parts re-rating before the soft packaging cycle and elevated leverage erode the equity.

We rate IP a HOLD with a growth lean. The specific 12-month thesis is the EMEA separation as a value-unlock catalyst, supported by two open-market director purchases at $31-39 and a maintained dividend yielding 5.5%. We do not upgrade to BUY because leverage (~$9.1 billion debt, ~2.0x D/E, Moody's negative outlook), a demand cycle that remains subdued on both continents, a fresh price-fixing antitrust class action, and a track record of three earnings misses in the last four quarters keep risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Following its 2025-2026 transformation, International Paper is the world's largest producer of fiber-based (corrugated) packaging, organized into two geographic segments:

- **Packaging Solutions North America (PS NA)** — ~$15.2 billion 2025 sales, ~11 million tons of U.S. containerboard capacity, ~170 corrugated plants, 15 recycling plants. About 75% of board production is internally converted into boxes (an integrated model that smooths margins).
- **Packaging Solutions EMEA (PS EMEA)** — ~$8.5 billion 2025 sales (versus $1.4 billion in 2024, pre-DS Smith), with 14 containerboard mills, 148 corrugated plants, and 20 recycling plants across Europe.

Three strategic moves reshaped the company:

1. **DS Smith acquisition** (closed Jan 31, 2025) for ~$9.9 billion in stock, a transatlantic packaging combination in which DS Smith holders received ~34% of the combined company.
2. **Global Cellulose Fibers divestiture** to American Industrial Partners for ~$1.5 billion (closed Jan 23, 2026), exiting pulp/fluff and making IP a pure-play packaging company.
3. **Planned EMEA spin-off** (announced Jan 29, 2026) into a separate dual-listed public company within 12-15 months, with IP retaining ~20%.

CEO and Chairman Andrew Silvernail (formerly of Idex) is running an "80/20 performance system": concentrate resources on the most profitable 20% of customers/products and rationalize the rest. This drove closures of 3 NA mills, 6 NA box plants, and 17 EMEA plants, plus ~1,400 headcount reductions in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

The headline numbers require a translation layer. Reported 2025 net sales were $23.6 billion (up from $18.6 billion in 2024 on the DS Smith addition), but the net loss was dominated by the impairments and closure charges itemized above. On an adjusted operating basis, 2025 EPS was approximately -$0.20, roughly breakeven operationally, while adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations rose to $2.98 billion from $1.64 billion. The disconnect between $1.70 billion of operating cash flow and -$159 million of free cash flow reflects capex roughly doubling to $1.86 billion (from $0.92 billion) as the company invests in mill conversions and greenfield capacity.

Q1 2026 is the cleaner read on run-rate economics:
- Net sales $5.97 billion
- Continuing-operations earnings +$76 million (+$0.14 EPS)
- Adjusted EBITDA $677 million
- Free cash flow +$94 million
- $660 million of debt repaid in the quarter

The balance sheet is leveraged but not distressed. Total debt is ~$9.1 billion with D/E around 2.0x, elevated post-DS Smith but consistent with a capital-intensive integrated packaging producer. IP holds investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB stable; Moody's Baa2, downgraded to a negative outlook in Q1 2026), is in covenant compliance (minimum net worth $9 billion, debt/cap <60%) with headroom, has no commercial paper drawn and $1.9 billion+ of undrawn facilities, and runs an overfunded pension (+$148 million net). The Altman Z and Piotroski scores are mechanically depressed by the one-time negative EBIT and net income; they do not describe the going-concern reality.

The quarterly earnings record is a genuine caution flag and should temper confidence in consensus. The last four quarters: Q2 2025 missed by 52.5%, Q3 2025 missed by 188.8%, Q4 2025 missed by 131.1%, and Q1 2026 was an in-line $0.15 versus $0.15 estimate (a +3.4% surprise on a rounding basis). Three misses in four quarters during a transformation means forward estimates carry wide error bands, and the demand environment has repeatedly come in below management's own expectations.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a combination of structural share gain inside a flat-to-declining market and self-help margin recovery, not top-line acceleration. IP's box volumes have outpaced the broader market by roughly 3% for three consecutive quarters, a real competitive signal, but the underlying market has been soft. Box demand was described as "weaker than expected" through 2025 (tariff uncertainty, soft housing starts, weak consumer sentiment, low industrial production), and Q1 2026 industry demand remained "subdued."

The earnings recovery is driven primarily by cost-out rather than volume. IP actioned ~$710 million of run-rate cost reductions in 2025 (including $200 million+ in EMEA), with the 80/20 system rationalizing low-return capacity. The 2026 EBITDA guide against the 2025 base implies 7-17% EBITDA growth largely from synergy capture and closures, not demand recovery.

Estimate revisions are negative, which matters. Current-year EPS consensus has fallen 23.7% over 90 days (to $1.38 from $1.81) and 14.4% over 30 days; next-year consensus is down 8.5% to $2.68. The current-quarter estimate swung to -$0.04 from +$0.29 ninety days ago, partly a seasonal artifact (Q2 is the peak NA maintenance-outage quarter and management guided sequentially lower EBITDA) but also a genuine demand markdown after the Q1 print. This is the opposite of the positive estimate-revision velocity that signals a buyable inflection. The earnings recovery is real, but the Street is marking down its pace, not up.

Growth optionality sits in the portfolio actions: the NORPAC bolt-on ($360 million, agreed Apr 16, 2026, adding West Coast recycled containerboard), a $225 million greenfield plant in Rankin County, MS (online Q4 2027), and mill conversions at Riverdale, AL and a Waterloo, IA box plant. These extend NA capacity for the post-spin pure-play but contribute little to the 12-month picture.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless given the impairment-driven loss. On forward earnings, IP trades at ~24x the marked-down 2026 consensus of $1.38 and ~12.5x the 2027 consensus of $2.68. The more relevant frame is EV/EBITDA: at ~$17.8 billion market cap plus ~$9.1 billion total debt (net of cash, net debt is lower), enterprise value is in the ~$26-27 billion range, or roughly ~8x the midpoint of 2026 guidance ($3.35 billion). That is a reasonable, not cheap, multiple for a leveraged cyclical at a soft point in the cycle.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The packaging cohort median P/E is 26.6x (PKG 26.6x, AMCR 25.7x, SW 57.2x, AVY 17.9x), and the median dividend yield is 4.4%. IP's 5.5% yield is the most attractive in the group, and the FCF guide against the ~$980 million annual dividend is the central tension: coverage depends on hitting guidance, with deleveraging and rising capex (~$2.0-2.1 billion, 103-108% of D&A) competing for cash. The dividend is being maintained but is not comfortably covered by guided 2026 FCF; a shortfall would force a hard choice.

The valuation case is a sum-of-the-parts story. A focused NA packaging pure-play could command a PKG-like multiple, while the EMEA spin (retaining ~20%) crystallizes value the consolidated entity is not getting credit for. Two directors made open-market purchases: Anders Gustafsson ~$1.0 million in March 2026 at ~$37.83-38.84, Scott Tozier ~$313K in May 2026 at ~$31.30, a constructive signal at prices near the current ~$33.61. Analyst targets span $32 low to $45 high (mean $39.36, median $39), implying ~16% upside to the median.

We score valuation 5.5. The stock is not stretched, the yield provides support, and a break-up catalyst exists. But the multiple does not offer asymmetric upside: a soft demand cycle, negative estimate revisions, and a balance sheet prioritizing debt over equity returns keep the entry merely fair rather than compelling. The market is correctly pricing a leveraged transformation with a 12-15 month catalyst timeline, not mispricing it.

## Competitive Landscape

IP plus DS Smith creates the clear scale leader in transatlantic corrugated packaging, competing with Packaging Corporation of America, Smurfit WestRock, and Mondi/regional EMEA players. The integrated model (75% of NA board internally converted) and the 80/20 lowest-cost-producer ambition are genuine structural advantages, and the ~3% above-market box-volume growth is evidence of share gain.

The secular tailwind is the shift toward fiber-based recyclable packaging and away from plastics, supporting long-run demand for corrugated. Containerboard is partly commoditized, so pricing power is cyclical rather than structural, and freight (diesel) is the most significant near-term cost pressure, recovered through pricing with a lag. We score competitive position 6.0: the trajectory is improving via share gain and cost leadership, but the moat in a commoditized product is moderate and the EMEA business faces a weaker European macro backdrop.

## Risk Assessment

**Separation execution risk (new).** The EMEA spin is "complex," conditioned on SEC registration, UK FCA prospectus approval, and final board approval, with "no assurance" of completion. Dis-synergies, stranded costs, and management distraction are real, and the combined value of two entities may not exceed the current single entity.

**DS Smith internal-control material weakness (new).** DS Smith carried material weaknesses in IT general controls (user access, change management, third-party management) across FY2022-FY2024. IP excluded DS Smith from its 2025 SOX 404 assessment and is mid-remediation targeting 2026. Combining a $9.9 billion acquisition that was not SOX-compliant while simultaneously separating businesses raises financial-reporting risk.

**Leverage and rating pressure.** ~$2.1 billion of debt is variable-rate, with ~$4.0 billion subject to rate step-ups on downgrade. The Moody's negative outlook is a live watch item; a downgrade would raise borrowing costs and could remove CP access. GCF proceeds are being steered to deleveraging specifically to protect the rating.

**Antitrust class action (new, July 2025).** *Artuso Pastry Foods v. Packaging Corp.* alleges 12 containerboard producers, including IP, conspired to fix prices from November 2020. Treble damages are sought; IP flags a possible "material impact" while saying it is too early to estimate. Separate Italian ICA antitrust exposure exists for IP Italy and DS Smith Italy.

**Goodwill yellow flag.** Paying $9.9 billion for DS Smith in January 2025 and writing down $2.47 billion of EMEA goodwill by December 2025 is a poor mark on deal value or a fast deterioration in European conditions. Management notes PS EMEA fair value now only approximates carrying value and is "sensitive" to assumptions; further EMEA impairment risk remains, right as the business is prepared for spin-off.

**Other contingencies.** Brazilian Sylvamo tax matter (~$274 million capped exposure), environmental reserves (~$271 million across Kalamazoo River, San Jacinto, Cass Lake, Versailles Pond), asbestos reserve (~$110 million), and tariff/trade whiplash following the Feb 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking IEEPA-based tariffs (uncertain input-cost and refund implications).

We score risk factors 4.0. Multiple specific, non-priced catalysts for downside exist (a Moody's downgrade, an adverse antitrust development, a further EMEA impairment, or spin-off slippage) alongside a dividend whose coverage depends on hitting guidance.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress without a clear directional bet. Near-term ATM IV of 51.1% sits 9.7 points above the 41.4% one-year realized vol (rich), and the term structure is in backwardation (51.1% near-term versus 45.5% long-term), signaling the market expects an event-driven move soon. The 1-week put/call skew of -16.7% is call-heavy, and the put/call OI ratio of 0.62 is below 1, both inconsistent with aggressive bearish positioning despite the 15% short interest. The reading is elevated event uncertainty rather than one-sided directional conviction. The IV decline from 60.7% to 51.1% since early May suggests some of the post-earnings stress has resolved.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$45, +34%).** The EMEA spin closes on schedule, and the market re-rates a focused NA packaging pure-play toward the PKG ~26x multiple while assigning standalone value to EMEA. The cost-out plus DS Smith synergies push EBITDA toward the top of the guide, FCF comfortably covers the dividend, the Moody's outlook stabilizes on continued deleveraging, and box demand recovers as housing and industrial production turn. NORPAC and greenfield capacity extend the NA growth runway. Director open-market buying at $31-39 marks the bottom.

**Bear Case (~$26, -22%).** European demand stays weak, triggering a further EMEA goodwill impairment ahead of the spin and complicating its valuation. The antitrust class action advances toward a material liability. Box demand remains subdued, FCF lands at the $300 million low end and fails to cover the dividend, forcing a cut or further leverage. A Moody's downgrade raises borrowing costs via the $4.0 billion of step-up debt. The DS Smith control remediation slips, surfacing reporting issues. The spin is delayed or abandoned. The miss streak continues.

**Base Case (~$37, +10%).** The spin proceeds on the stated timeline with normal friction. EBITDA lands near the $3.35 billion midpoint, FCF in the $400 million range keeps the dividend intact but uncovered by reported FCF (bridged by balance-sheet capacity). The stock grinds toward the analyst median ($39) as the break-up narrative gains traction, partly offset by the soft cycle and falling near-term estimates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EMEA spin-off receives formal SEC/FCA registration approval with a confirmed timeline, de-risking the central catalyst.
- Estimate revisions inflect positive (current-year consensus stabilizes or rises over a 90-day window), confirming demand recovery rather than continued markdown.
- Two consecutive quarters of FCF tracking to the upper half of guidance, demonstrating dividend coverage and deleveraging on plan.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Moody's downgrade below Baa2, activating the $4.0 billion of step-up debt and pressuring FCF.
- A second EMEA goodwill impairment or a material adverse development in the *Artuso* antitrust case.
- A dividend cut, or FCF guidance lowered below $300 million.
- Spin-off delay beyond 18 months or abandonment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (the peak maintenance-outage quarter where management already guided sequentially lower EBITDA), followed by any formal spin-off registration filing over the next 12-15 months. The thesis is catalyst-dated to the EMEA separation.

## Conclusion

International Paper is a leveraged, mid-transformation cyclical that the standardized data mischaracterizes as distressed. The operating reality (~$3.0 billion adjusted EBITDA, $1.7 billion operating cash flow, a Q1 2026 return to profitability and positive FCF, and active deleveraging) supports a going-concern with a credible self-help and break-up story. The specific 12-month holding case is the EMEA spin-off as a sum-of-the-parts catalyst, reinforced by the actioned cost-out, ~3% above-market box-volume growth, a 5.5% dividend yield, and two directors buying in the open market.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the balance of those positives against a soft demand cycle that has produced three earnings misses in four quarters and negative estimate revisions (current-year consensus -23.7% over 90 days), elevated leverage with a Moody's negative outlook, a fresh price-fixing class action, recurring EMEA impairment risk, and a dividend not comfortably covered by guided FCF. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric (+10% base, +34% bull, -22% bear) rather than asymmetric. We rate IP HOLD with a growth lean, anchored to the EMEA separation catalyst, and would become more constructive on formal spin-off registration or a positive turn in estimate revisions.
