**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Public Service Enterprise Group is a New Jersey utility holding company built around a regulated transmission-and-distribution monopoly (PSE&G) that generates roughly 78% of consolidated earnings, paired with a carbon-free merchant nuclear fleet operating inside PJM. The regulated core delivers a clean, visible earnings runway: a $22.5–25.5B 2026–2030 capital plan targeting a 6.0–7.5% rate-base CAGR, funded against investment-grade ratings and $3.5B of liquidity. Q1 2026 was strong on the surface: revenue +19% YoY to $3,848M, net income +26% to $741M, diluted EPS $1.48 vs. $1.18, though a chunk of the revenue growth is zero-margin commodity pass-through and the earnings improvement was partly flattered by a smaller mark-to-market drag ($30M after-tax loss vs. $135M a year earlier). Underlying operating growth is real but more modest than the headline.

The stock has drifted from the high-$80s to ~$79, roughly 9% below where our prior work sat and about 13% below the 52-week high of $91.26. On unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, that price action improves entry math at the margin, but this is not a clean mispricing to buy into aggressively. PEG trades at a slight premium to the electric-utility peer median (~17.5x forward EPS vs. peer median ~16.6x) because of the nuclear optionality: the possibility of long-term contracts with hyperscalers plus rising PJM capacity revenue. That same scarcity value has provoked a severe affordability backlash in New Jersey, and the outcome of the political and regulatory response is the master variable. A possible September 2026 PJM "reliability backstop auction," a new governor directing rate mitigation, a summer disconnection moratorium, and escalating FERC scrutiny of PSE&G's transmission formula rate all sit on the bear side of the ledger.

We maintain a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The regulated compounder is worth owning for a mid-single-digit total return (EPS growth plus a well-covered ~3.4% yield), and the nuclear/data-center optionality is a free-ish call option. But near-term EPS revisions have flattened (current-quarter estimate down 3.7% over 30 days), the score trajectory has been declining (6.8 → 6.2 → 5.8), and the binary regulatory catalysts cut both ways. The risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

PSEG operates two reportable segments:

**PSE&G** is a regulated T&D utility serving ~2.3M electric and ~1.9M gas customers across New Jersey. Distribution is regulated by the NJ Board of Public Utilities; transmission earns a FERC-approved annual formula rate that includes a 50bp ROE adder for PJM membership (~$40M/yr of net income at stake if that adder were lost). PSE&G contributed $577M of consolidated Q1 2026 net income. A Conservation Incentive Program decouples distribution revenue from volume, removing weather/usage risk, a constructive regulatory feature.

**PSEG Power & Other** is now a focused carbon-free nuclear operator (Salem 1 & 2, Hope Creek, co-owned interests in Peach Bottom) after the company exited fossil generation in 2022, converting Power from a volatile commodity business into a nuclear-baseload operator inside PJM. Power contributed $164M in Q1 2026, up from $43M a year ago, aided by higher capacity revenue and a smaller MTM drag. The segment also includes the fee-based PSEG Long Island O&M contract for LIPA, recently extended through 2030.

The structural setup: a regulated monopoly franchise with volume-decoupled revenue, a large carbon-free nuclear fleet (~80%+ of New Jersey's carbon-free power) with a federal nuclear production tax credit floor through 2032, and a favorable position in a capacity-short PJM.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $1,271M (up from $1,049M) against $693M of capex. The business self-funds a meaningful portion of its capital program but relies on ongoing debt issuance to fund the balance. FY2025 revenue grew ~18% and net income ~19%. The quality caveat: a large share of revenue growth is BGS/BGSS commodity pass-through carrying no margin, and the Q1 earnings improvement was partly a function of a smaller non-cash MTM loss. Strip those out and the durable growth drivers are PSE&G rate-base expansion (T&D plus clause programs) and higher capacity revenue at Power.

**Rate base and capital plan.** Regulated rate base grew from ~$34B (YE2024) to ~$36B (YE2025). The regulated capital plan (total plan $24–28B) drives the targeted rate-base CAGR. Recent additions include the November 2025 GSMP III gas-main modernization program ($1.05B plus $360M). This is the recoverable earnings engine.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total capitalization is $39,968M, of which long-term debt is ~$22.7B. Leverage is high but structurally normal for a regulated utility. Ratings are investment-grade across the complex with Stable outlooks (PSEG Baa2/BBB; PSE&G A1/A; Power Baa2/BBB). The Altman Z-Score screening flag (~0.95) is a false positive typical of asset-heavy utilities and should not be weighted. The real financial-health headwind is rising interest expense: $272M in Q1 2026, up 13% YoY on incremental debt and refinancing at higher rates. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the negative-FCF, externally-financed utility model faces a persistent cost-of-capital drag that erodes some of the per-share benefit of rate-base growth. A two-notch downgrade of PSEG Power to below investment grade would trigger ~$691M of additional collateral posting, a tail risk rather than a base case but a real sensitivity.

**Dividend.** The dividend was raised to $0.67/quarter ($2.68 annualized) from $0.63, well covered by earnings. The buyback is limited to offsetting equity-comp/ESPP dilution under a 10b5-1 plan, so there is no meaningful share-count reduction lever.

## Growth Analysis

The regulated growth story is durable and quantifiable: the 6.0–7.5% rate-base CAGR through 2030 maps closely to operating-EPS growth in the mid-single digits. This is the base case, and it is high-confidence given the visibility of regulated capex recovery.

The optionality layer is where the range widens. PSEG sits at the center of the PJM resource-adequacy theme. The December 2025 capacity auction cleared at the FERC price cap of $333.44/MW-day (it would have been $529.80 uncapped), following a July 2024 auction that cleared roughly 10x the prior year and a still-higher July 2025 auction. This directly lifts nuclear capacity revenue. Management repeatedly flags exploring "the potential sale of power, capacity and/or emission credits from our nuclear facilities pursuant to long-term agreements," the data-center PPA path that peers Talen, Constellation, and Vistra have already walked. A binding hyperscaler nuclear PPA would be a meaningful, currently-unpriced catalyst. The April 2026 NJ legislation repealing the state nuclear-construction moratorium opens a longer-dated new-build avenue as well. Q1 2026 nuclear capacity factor was a strong 95.5%, and PSEG now assumes a 20-year license extension for Salem 1/2 and Hope Creek with Salem uprates planned.

The counterweight: near-term estimate revisions have gone flat-to-slightly-negative. Current-quarter consensus is $0.80, down 3.7% over 30 days and 4.1% over 90 days. Full-year consensus is ~$4.38 (essentially flat), and next-year ~$4.68 (down 0.4% over 30 days). The company beats regularly (3 beats, 1 in-line over the last four quarters, with two ~9-10% beats), which supports confidence in delivery, but there is no upward revision momentum. This is a steady compounder, not an accelerating one, and the data-center language remains deliberately conditional, with no binding agreement disclosed. Treat it as a call option, not a base-case catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$79, PEG trades at ~17.5x forward EPS on the ~$4.38 current-year consensus, a modest premium to the Electric Utilities peer median of ~16.6x. The peer set shows wide dispersion driven by idiosyncratic factors: EIX at 7.9x (wildfire liability overhang), FE at 25.4x, WEC at 22.5x, EXC at 16.6x, ES at 14.6x. On yield, PEG's ~3.4% sits below the peer median of ~4.0%, reflecting the growth and optionality premium the market assigns to the nuclear franchise.

The valuation case is two-sided. The premium is defensible on the strength of the regulated growth runway plus nuclear optionality that most peers lack. But that same premium means there is limited margin of safety: the stock is not cheap on any absolute metric, and the "cheapness" that a low headline multiple might suggest elsewhere in the group here reflects the market correctly pricing quality rather than mispricing it. We are wary of treating a modest multiple as a buy signal in a sector where cheap names (EIX) are cheap for structural reasons.

Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $88.24, median $88.00, low $75.00, high $103.00. The mean implies ~12% upside plus the dividend. We do not treat these as floors, because this is a stable-multiple utility with flat revisions rather than a sub-30x stock with rising estimates, so consensus targets are more likely fair anchors than stale lowballs. The base case reflects the regulated compounding plus modest optionality being partially realized; the bull case requires a hyperscaler PPA or a favorable PJM outcome; the bear case reflects an affordability-driven clawback.

## Competitive Landscape

PSEG's competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory. The regulated T&D franchise is a monopoly with constructive decoupling, and rate-base growth is the primary driver of a widening earnings base. The nuclear fleet's scarcity value is rising as PJM capacity prices climb and firm carbon-free baseload becomes more valuable in a supply-short market with surging data-center and electrification demand. FERC's 2025 order facilitating generator co-location for large loads, plus its large-load interconnection rulemaking, structurally favor owners of firm nuclear capacity.

Relative to merchant-heavy peers, PSEG offers a lower-risk earnings profile (78% regulated) while retaining meaningful upside optionality, a favorable position within the group. The key competitive caveat is that the nuclear value proposition is exposed to the New Jersey political environment in a way that peers in more constructive jurisdictions are not. Any hyperscaler deal would make the counterparty a customer of PSEG's output, with PSEG retaining ownership and operation, favorable economics if the value flows to shareholders rather than being redirected to ratepayers.

## Risk Assessment

**PJM capacity politics + NJ affordability backlash (the master variable).** Record capacity prices benefit Power's earnings but have provoked a severe political reaction. A new NJ governor (took office January 2026) issued executive orders directing the BPU to mitigate rate increases with state funding; NJ enacted a summer disconnection moratorium (raising bad-debt expense); and the National Energy Dominance Council signed a statement of principles with all 13 PJM-state governors to overhaul the capacity market, including a "reliability backstop auction" targeted for September 2026. Management explicitly flags "collection risk for both PSE&G and its customers" if utilities are forced to purchase backstop capacity. This is the dominant swing factor and it is genuinely binary.

**Nuclear PTC ambiguity.** The IRA production tax credit (up to $15/MWh through 2032) is currently a floor, not a subsidy, because 2026 realized nuclear value is expected above the PTC floor. But the definition of "gross receipts" driving the phase-out remains unresolved pending Treasury guidance, and adjustments "could be material." An unresolved ZEC-refund mechanism (BPU March 2026 proposal) adds a retroactive-adjustment tail.

**FERC transmission scrutiny (escalating).** In March 2026 FERC rejected a settlement and directed PJM to recalculate and resettle cost allocations back to 2015. FERC commenced an April 2026 audit of PSE&G's transmission formula rate, and a January 2026 complaint targets Roseland–Pleasant Valley cost recovery (already tied to a $6.6M penalty). These threaten the most reliably profitable business.

**Merchant nuclear collateral/rating sensitivity.** Q1 2026 95% one-day VaR ranged $58M–$314M, up sharply from a $17–71M full-year 2025 range, signaling elevated commodity volatility.

**Environmental long tail.** Lower Passaic River cleanup totals ~$2.85B across PRPs (PSEG ~$66M accrued, warns of material additional costs); Newark Bay, Hackensack River (new OU3 identified Sept 2025), MGP sites ($176M), and CCR obligations add uncertainty.

**Litigation.** A July 2025 putative class action (D. Maryland) alleges 26 nuclear operators including PSEG conspired to fix nuclear-worker compensation, seeking treble damages.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 25.7% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 19.0% (a 6.7pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (25.7% at 1m vs. 21.2% at 6m), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk consistent with the August 4 earnings call and the building regulatory calendar. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%) and put/call OI is neutral (1.01), so there is no strong directional bias. Short interest is low at 2.6% of float (3.5 days to cover), consistent with a widely-held utility rather than a contested name. The implied ±5.5% move into the nearest expiry is modest. The options market is pricing an ordinary event, not a crisis, though the backwardation flags elevated near-term uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$103, ~30% upside).** The regulated rate-base CAGR compounds on schedule; PJM capacity value proves durable; and PSEG signs a long-term nuclear PPA with a hyperscaler at attractive terms, re-rating the stock toward the peer high-multiple cohort. The NJ backstop auction resolves in a way that preserves nuclear scarcity value for shareholders, and the new-nuclear moratorium repeal opens a credible long-dated growth avenue.

**Bear case (~$65–70, ~11–18% downside).** New Jersey's affordability response claws back nuclear scarcity value through rate mitigation, disconnection moratoriums, and a backstop auction that shifts procurement obligations and collection risk onto PSE&G. FERC's transmission resettlement and formula-rate audit reduce recoverable transmission returns. Higher-for-longer rates keep pressuring the externally-financed model, and the PTC "gross receipts" definition delivers a material retroactive adjustment. The multiple compresses toward the peer median with no optionality premium.

**Base case (~$88, ~11% upside plus ~3.4% yield).** Regulated compounding delivers mid-single-digit EPS growth, capacity revenue holds at capped levels, the data-center optionality remains an unexercised call, and the political overhang is managed rather than resolved decisively either way. Total return in the high-single to low-double digits over 12 months, most of it earnings-driven.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The regulated compounder is worth owning for mid-single-digit EPS growth plus a well-covered yield, with nuclear/data-center optionality attached at little incremental cost. The balance of near-term risks (flat revisions, binary regulatory catalysts, rate-cost drag) prevents the asymmetry required for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A binding long-term nuclear PPA with a hyperscaler or large-load customer, disclosed with pricing that flows scarcity value to shareholders.
- A constructive resolution of the September 2026 PJM backstop-auction/capacity-market process that preserves capacity value without material NJ clawback, combined with a turn to positive EPS revision velocity.
- A pullback toward the low-$70s (near the $75 analyst floor), which would restore a clear margin of safety on the regulated base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A NJ regulatory outcome or backstop-auction structure that shifts material procurement obligations and collection risk onto PSE&G, or a rate-mitigation mechanism that meaningfully redirects nuclear value to ratepayers.
- An adverse FERC transmission formula-rate audit outcome or a materially unfavorable 2015 resettlement.
- A material adverse PTC "gross receipts" adjustment, or an out-of-plan equity raise that dilutes per-share growth beyond the current expectation.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the August 4, 2026 Q2 earnings call (revision trajectory, capacity/data-center commentary) and the September 2026 PJM backstop-auction process. The 12-month horizon is gated by these two events.

## Conclusion

PSEG is a well-run, investment-grade regulated utility with a visible rate-base growth runway, a low-risk 78%-regulated earnings mix, and genuine carbon-free nuclear optionality into a capacity-short PJM. At ~17.5x forward EPS with a ~3.4% yield, the market is pricing quality and optionality correctly rather than mispricing the stock, so the entry is fair rather than compelling. The recent ~9% price drift reflects flat estimate revisions and a building regulatory overhang more than any fundamental deterioration; the earnings engine is intact, but the New Jersey affordability backlash and the September 2026 PJM backstop-auction process are genuinely binary and cut both ways. The declining score trajectory (6.8 → 6.2 → 5.8) is driven by rising rate-cost pressure and escalating regulatory scrutiny, both of which the current data confirm rather than reverse.

We rate PEG a HOLD. Own it for the regulated compounding and the dividend, treat the data-center/PPA optionality as a free call option, and wait for either a constructive regulatory resolution paired with a revision inflection, or a pullback toward the low-$70s, before adding conviction. The risk/reward from here is balanced.
