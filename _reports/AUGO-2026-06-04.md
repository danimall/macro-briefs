**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aura Minerals is a mid-tier gold and base-metals producer operating six mines across Honduras, Brazil and Mexico, with a development pipeline in Guatemala, Colombia and Brazil. The company listed on Nasdaq in mid-2025 and has posted strong headline financial growth: FY2025 net revenue rose 55% to $921.7M, adjusted EBITDA doubled to $547.8M, and Q1 2026 revenue grew 136% year-over-year to $382.6M. The balance sheet is strong (net debt of $115.2M, or 0.16x LTM EBITDA, against $267.8M cash) and the company funds a meaningful quarterly dividend (most recently $0.78/share, ~$65M) alongside aggressive growth capex and M&A. On operating cash generation and leverage, this is a high-quality miner.

The complication is that the headline growth is overwhelmingly a gold-price effect, not a volume effect. Realized gold prices reached roughly $4,873/oz in Q1 2026, up ~70% year-over-year, while production grew far more modestly (280,414 GEO in 2025; 82,137 GEO in Q1, annualizing to roughly the midpoint of 2026 guidance of 340–390k GEO). EBITDA margin expanded from 45% to 64% almost entirely on price. That makes current earnings power cyclical-peak earnings, and gold has now declined for three consecutive months as real yields rather than safe-haven flows have set the marginal price. AUGO's own price action reflects this: the stock has fallen roughly 42% from its April high of $110.32 to $64.23, with the most recent sessions ($77 → $72 → $68 → $64) showing a clean repricing lower.

We rate AUGO a HOLD. The business is fundamentally sound, the dividend and balance sheet are supportive, and the volume pipeline toward a stated >600k GEO target is real. But four concrete, falsifiable factors prevent a buy recommendation: (1) earnings sit at a commodity-price peak while gold is in a multi-month decline; (2) the company has missed consensus EPS in all four reported quarters, twice by more than 90%, eliminating any credibility for the forward estimates that make the stock look cheap; (3) a legacy collar book with a $2,400/oz ceiling forces Aura to forgo roughly $2,000+/oz on ~184,000 hedged ounces and bleed real cash ($33.3M of realized hedge losses in Q1 2026 alone) through mid-2028; and (4) AISC is inflating (Q1 2026 AISC of $1,829/GEO blew through guidance) while capex was raised to $386–453M. These are specific, monitorable conditions, not generic caution.

## Company Overview

Aura is incorporated in the British Virgin Islands and operationally headquartered in Brazil, filing as a foreign private issuer (Form 20-F annual, 6-K interim). It is triple-listed on Nasdaq (AUGO), B3 in Brazil (AURA33 BDRs) and historically the TSX. The mid-2025 Nasdaq IPO raised roughly $200M and materially increased liquidity, lifting average daily trading volume from ~$31M in Q4 2025 to ~$94M in Q1 2026, with inclusion in 58 ETFs.

The producing portfolio comprises Minosa (gold, Honduras, the largest and most consistent asset); Almas, Apoena, Borborema and the newly acquired MSG/Serra Grande (gold, Brazil); and Aranzazu (copper-gold-silver-molybdenum, Mexico, reported in gold-equivalent ounces). The development pipeline includes Era Dorada (gold-copper, Guatemala, Board-approved for construction in April 2026, first production targeted 1H 2028), Matupá (Brazil), Tolda Fria (Colombia) and Carajás copper exploration (Brazil).

Management markets a "360° Mining" stakeholder philosophy and is executing an aggressive growth plan targeting >600k GEO/year within a few years, up from ~280k GEO in 2025, driven by the Borborema ramp, the MSG turnaround, Era Dorada construction, Matupá, and Almas/Borborema expansions. The competitive edge is a portfolio of relatively low-cost assets (consolidated AISC ~$1,458/GEO in 2025) operating into a strong gold-price environment.

## Financial Analysis

The operating business is robust and the balance sheet is among the cleanest in the mid-tier space.

| Production (GEO) | 267,232 | 280,414 | 82,137 |
| Net Revenue | $594.2M | $921.7M (+55%) | $382.6M (+136% YoY) |
| Gross Margin | 42% | 58% | 60% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $266.8M | $547.8M (+105%) | $243.9M (+199% YoY) |
| Adj. EBITDA Margin | 45% | 59% | 64% |
| GAAP Net Income | $(30.3)M | $(79.3)M | $95.2M |
| Adjusted Net Income | $81.5M | $205.7M | $109.5M |
| Operating Cash Flow | $222.2M | $305.2M | $117.9M |
| AISC ($/GEO) | $1,320 | $1,458 | $1,829 |

Two financial-statement features matter most.

First, the GAAP-versus-adjusted disconnect. The company reported GAAP net losses in both 2024 and 2025 despite booming operations, driven almost entirely by non-cash mark-to-market charges on out-of-the-money gold collars (–$281.5M in 2025) as gold rallied past the $2,400/oz hedge ceiling. The trailing GAAP EPS of –$1.01 and the resulting 58x trailing P/E are therefore not meaningful descriptions of earnings power. On adjusted net income — $205.7M in 2025 and $109.5M in Q1 2026 — the business is highly profitable. A naive screen on GAAP EPS would badly mischaracterize the company. Q1 2026 flipped to GAAP profitability ($95.2M) because the within-quarter gold move was smaller.

Second, the balance sheet. Gross debt of $409M against $267.8M cash leaves net debt of just $115.2M, or 0.16x LTM EBITDA, down from 0.88x a year earlier. Debt has fallen despite the MSG acquisition ($72.8M cash) and a heavy capex program. Recurring FCF was $253.7M in 2025 and $94.9M in Q1 2026. This combination of minimal leverage, strong free cash flow, and a funded dividend earns a high Financial Health score.

The caveats on cash flow quality bear stating. Reported operating cash flow is reduced by large cash tax payments (~$51.5M in Q1 alone) and by realized hedge losses ($56.5M in 2025; $33.3M in Q1 2026) that are genuine cash costs, not optics. Q1 2026 AISC of $1,829/GEO blew through full-year guidance ($1,720–$1,865), partly reflecting MSG's ~$3,735/GEO cost base, FX (BRL/MXN appreciation), and GEO-conversion math at Aranzazu. Stripping out MSG and holding metal prices constant, AISC was ~$1,512, up only ~4% YoY, so the underlying cost discipline is intact, but the consolidated trajectory is rising at the same time gold may be peaking.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth rates are extraordinary but require careful attribution. FY2025 revenue grew 55% and Q1 2026 revenue grew 136% year-over-year. Decomposing these, realized gold prices rose ~49% in 2025 and ~70% YoY in Q1 2026, while GEO production grew only ~5% in 2025 (267k → 280k). The bulk of the reported growth is price, not volume. Price-driven growth on a commodity at a cyclical peak does not have the durability of volume-driven growth.

The volume pipeline, by contrast, is genuine and somewhat insulated from gold-price direction. Proven and probable reserves more than doubled (~+110% YoY) to 7.2M GEO since the IPO, using conservative reserve prices ($2,600/oz gold), driven by MSG, Borborema and Era Dorada. The Borborema road-relocation agreement with Brazil's DNIT lifted reserves there by 82% to 1.5M oz and extended life-of-mine to 21–36 years. Era Dorada received full Board construction approval in April 2026 with ~$382M capex, an NPV of ~$1,344.5M and after-tax IRR of 35.6% at a conservative $3,177/oz gold assumption (economics that improve substantially at spot). First production is targeted for 1H 2028.

Estimate revisions have been large: current-year consensus EPS rose to $10.47 from $5.26 ninety days ago (+99.1%), and next-year to $13.61 from $5.50 (+147.4%). Ordinarily, revisions of this magnitude are a powerful forward signal. Here, two factors temper the read. The revisions are driven by the gold-price assumption embedded in the estimates, not by a structural volume inflection; they are commodity-price extrapolation. And the company has missed every one of its last four reported quarters, so the consensus that is being revised upward has no demonstrated predictive track record for this name. The Growth Prospects score reflects a real volume pipeline discounted by the commodity-price dependence of the headline numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E (58x) is uninformative given the GAAP hedge noise. On adjusted earnings, the picture is optically attractive: $5.6B market cap against ~$438M of annualized adjusted net income (Q1 run-rate) implies ~13x adjusted earnings. EV/EBITDA on annualized Q1 EBITDA (~$976M) against an EV of ~$5.7B is ~5.8x; on FY2025 EBITDA of $547.8M it is ~10.4x. The dividend yield is ~4.3% on current price (the brief cites ~7.4% on a trailing basis as quoted in mid-2025). On these metrics the stock looks cheap relative to most mid-tier producers.

The problem is that these are peak-cycle earnings on peak gold. Q1 EBITDA reflected realized gold of ~$4,873/oz. The mining sector's cardinal valuation error is treating cycle-peak earnings as normalized. Gold has declined for three consecutive months as the real-yield channel rather than safe-haven demand has set the marginal price [unverified — futures curve shape not in sources]. If gold normalizes toward Aura's own conservative reserve assumption ($2,600/oz) or its Era Dorada base case ($3,177/oz), earnings compress sharply, and the "5.8x EV/EBITDA" expands materially. The ~42% drawdown to $64.23 is the market beginning to make exactly this adjustment.

Analyst targets — mean $101.34, median $111, high $125, low $51 — imply substantial upside, but these are not independent confirmation. They were set when both the stock and gold were higher, and they embed the same elevated gold-price assumption that the recent decline is challenging. This is a high-beta commodity producer whose intrinsic value today is a function of an unpredictable gold price that has turned lower. We score Valuation 5.5: optically cheap on peak earnings, with two-sided asymmetry rather than a clean mispricing the market is missing. There is genuine downside support from low leverage, the dividend, and a low-AISC ex-MSG asset base, which keeps this above a value-trap reading.

## Competitive Landscape

Aura's structural advantage is a low-cost asset portfolio (consolidated AISC ~$1,458/GEO in 2025, ex-MSG ~$1,512 in Q1 2026) feeding into a strong price environment, producing dramatic operating leverage. It differentiates from peers by paying a meaningful quarterly dividend while still funding aggressive organic growth and M&A, a relatively unusual combination for a miner mid-build. Reserve growth (+110% to 7.2M GEO) and the Era Dorada/Borborema additions point to an improving competitive trajectory.

The offsetting factors are that gold is a commodity with no pricing power, MSG is a high-cost ($3,735/GEO) and unproven turnaround, and the LatAm asset base (Honduras, Mexico, Brazil, Guatemala, Colombia) carries higher permitting, community, tax and FX risk than North American or Australian peers. The trajectory is favorable (growing reserves, expanding production base, disciplined consolidation), but there is no durable moat in a commodity business. Competitive Position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where the case for restraint concentrates, and several risks are only partly priced.

**Commodity-price peak.** The dominant risk. Earnings are at cycle peak on gold near $4,600–4,900/oz while gold is in a multi-month decline driven by real yields. A reversion toward $3,000–3,200 would cut EBITDA and earnings substantially and would expose the apparent multiple cheapness as a peak-earnings artifact.

**Legacy hedge book.** ~184,000 oz of put/call collars (floor $1,745, ceiling $2,400) cover ~80% of Borborema's first three years, expiring April 2026–June 2028. With gold far above the ceiling, Aura forgoes ~$2,000+/oz on hedged ounces and incurs real cash settlements. This is the single biggest company-specific drag and persists until mid-2028.

**Consensus credibility.** Four consecutive EPS misses — Q1 2026 –34.0%, Q4 2025 –114.2%, Q3 2025 –92.6%, Q1 2025 –372.3%. Even allowing for GAAP/hedge distortion, the company has not hit a published estimate. The forward estimates that make the stock look cheap therefore deserve heavy discounting.

**Cost inflation and FX.** Q1 2026 cash costs rose ~30% QoQ on MSG, mine sequencing and BRL/MXN strength. As a USD reporter with LatAm cost bases, margin is structurally exposed to local-currency appreciation.

**Jurisdiction and tax leakage.** Provisions for partial non-recoverability of VAT credits in Honduras (~$8.25M) and discounted sale of ICMS credits in Brazil are recurring, real tax-leakage risks in concentrated higher-risk jurisdictions.

**Execution on the capex ramp.** The path to >600k GEO requires simultaneous delivery of Era Dorada construction, Borborema expansion/road relocation, Almas underground/plant expansion and Matupá, with 2026 capex raised to $386–453M.

**Governance.** Aura is widely understood to have a controlling shareholder with concentrated voting and limited public float, and share-price-linked compensation (rising DSU liabilities). These governance specifics were not retrievable from structured tooling and should be confirmed directly from the April 1, 2026 Form 20-F.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 64.7% is in line with one-year realized volatility of 65.0% (spread –0.3%), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling an imminent catalyst, and IV sits at the 23rd percentile within its own chain. The put/call skew is –10.2% (call-heavy) at the front and remains call-tilted at –6.7%/–7.3% in the 3-month and 6-month tenors, with put/call OI near balance (0.99). The term structure is flat (near 64.7% ≈ far 64.5%), implying uncertainty is distributed evenly across time rather than concentrated near a binary event. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.4%. The options market is not pricing acute downside stress and skews modestly bullish, which mildly offsets the equity's recent weakness but does not change the fundamental commodity-peak concern. Short interest at 9.2% of float (4.0 days to cover) is moderate, neither a crowded short nor a clear bull signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($101–125).** Gold holds above $4,000/oz or resumes its rally, Borborema and Almas ramp on schedule, MSG turns the corner in 2H 2026/2027, and the hedge book rolls off in 2028, removing a multi-year cumulative cash drag. Production scales toward 600k GEO at a sub-$1,600 ex-MSG AISC, adjusted earnings sustain a ~$10–13 run-rate, and the stock re-rates to peer EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle earnings while the ~4–7% dividend pays the wait. Analyst targets ($101 mean, $111 median, $125 high) are reached.

**Bear case ($45–51).** Gold mean-reverts toward $3,000–3,200 as real yields stay elevated, compressing EBITDA margins from 64% back toward the mid-40s. AISC continues to inflate on MSG and FX, the fifth consecutive earnings miss further erodes consensus credibility, and the capex ramp consumes free cash just as the cycle turns. The multiple that looks like 5.8x EV/EBITDA on peak earnings re-rates to a normalized 7–9x on much lower EBITDA, taking the stock toward the analyst low of $51 or below.

The two-sided nature of the case, anchored on an unpredictable and currently declining gold price, is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The validation question — why not BUY given a respectable score — resolves on concrete, monitorable grounds: earnings are at a commodity peak with gold in a confirmed multi-month decline; the company has missed consensus four straight quarters by margins as large as 114%, so the rising estimates that make it look cheap are unreliable; the hedge book caps the gold-rally upside and drains cash through mid-2028; and AISC is inflating into a raised capex program. Each of these is falsifiable against the next two earnings releases and the gold tape.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: a real volume pipeline and dividend prevent an avoid, but the thesis hinges on an unpredictable gold price at what appears to be a cyclical peak, so we lack sufficient evidence for a confident appreciation call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of meeting or beating consensus EPS, restoring credibility to the forward estimates.
- Gold stabilizing above ~$4,000/oz alongside consolidated AISC declining back below $1,650/GEO as MSG turns and the 2H 2026 cost guidance is delivered.
- Confirmation from the Form 20-F that float and governance are not materially more restrictive than assumed, plus evidence Era Dorada and Borborema are tracking on schedule and budget.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gold breaking decisively below ~$3,000/oz with no stabilization, turning peak earnings into a derating event.
- AISC continuing to climb above the $1,865 guidance top with no MSG turnaround visibility, or a capex overrun that pressures the dividend.
- A fifth consecutive material earnings miss or a guidance cut to production.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly results (Q2 2026, expected early August) and the trajectory of the gold price over the next one to two months are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

Aura Minerals is a well-run, low-leverage, cash-generative mid-tier gold producer with a credible volume-growth pipeline and a meaningful dividend. The balance sheet is a genuine strength, and the GAAP losses are a hedge artifact rather than operational weakness. Those qualities keep the stock well clear of an avoid rating.

What prevents a buy is that the reported earnings power is overwhelmingly a function of a gold price that has now declined for three months, the company has missed consensus in all four reported quarters, a legacy collar book caps upside and drains cash through mid-2028, and AISC is inflating into a raised capex program. The stock's slide from its April high reflects the market beginning to discount peak earnings, not a clear mispricing the market is missing. We rate AUGO a **HOLD**, with an upgrade contingent on demonstrated estimate delivery and gold-price stabilization, and a downgrade contingent on a further leg lower in gold or continued cost and execution slippage.
