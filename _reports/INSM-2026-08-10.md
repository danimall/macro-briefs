## Executive Summary

Insmed has completed the transition from a single-product rare-respiratory company to a two-product commercial platform with a plausible line of sight to operating breakeven. BRINSUPRI (brensocatib), approved August 12, 2025 for non-cystic-fibrosis bronchiectasis, generated $309.2M in Q2 2026 — up 48.7% sequentially from a derived $207.9M in Q1 — and management raised FY2026 BRINSUPRI guidance to $1.25-1.40B from "at least $1B." Combined with reiterated ARIKAYCE guidance of $450-470M, implied FY2026 revenue is $1.70-1.87B against FY2025's $606.4M. The ENCORE confirmatory trial met its primary endpoint and all multiplicity-controlled secondary culture-conversion endpoints in March 2026, an sNDA was filed in July 2026, and if approved it would move ARIKAYCE's U.S. addressable population from 12,000-17,000 refractory MAC patients toward a 95,000-115,000 diagnosed base. FY2027 consensus EPS went from $0.33 ninety days ago to $0.81, and current-year consensus improved 41% over the trailing 30-day window. The stock rose approximately 34% on the August 6 print.

The question is what remains unpriced at $134.19. The enterprise trades at roughly 16.1x FY2026 guided revenue midpoint ($1.785B) and, on our FY2027 estimate of approximately $2.85B, near 10x forward sales. Working backward through a normalized-margin model, the current capitalization requires revenue in the neighborhood of $6B by 2030 at a 30% net margin and a 25x exit multiple to justify itself at a 10% discount rate. That is well below management's aggregate >$14B peak aspiration but well above what a probability-weighted view supports when TPIP's two Phase 3 programs began only in Q4 2025 and April 2026, and when more than half the disclosed diagnosed bronchiectasis population sits in geographies where Insmed holds approval but is deliberately not launching.

Three specific items temper the print. First, the reported ~84% gross margin (COGS 15.7% of 1H26 revenue, down from 23.6% in FY2024) is flattered by pre-approval BRINSUPRI inventory that was expensed to R&D and now flows through COGS at essentially zero carrying cost, and it is arithmetically difficult to reconcile with an AstraZeneca royalty that steps from high single digits to mid-teens, so margin should be modeled compressing, not expanding. Second, FY2026 guidance itself embeds sequential deceleration: implied 2H26 BRINSUPRI of $733-883M translates to roughly +21% and +15% sequential quarters at the midpoint, versus +49% just delivered. Third, the Q2 GAAP net loss of $13.2M included a $99.8M non-cash gain on share-settled contingent consideration; the operating loss excluding that item was $101.3M. We rate the shares HOLD with a $150 base case, $215 bull, and $85 bear.

## Company Overview

Insmed is a Bridgewater, New Jersey biopharmaceutical company (incorporated in Virginia, 1999) operating as a single reportable segment across three stated therapeutic areas. Two products are commercial.

**ARIKAYCE** (amikacin liposome inhalation suspension) received U.S. accelerated approval in September 2018 for refractory *Mycobacterium avium* complex lung disease, plus EU (2020) and Japan (2021) approvals. It is a drug/device combination delivered exclusively through PARI's Lamira nebulizer; the company states it cannot sell ARIKAYCE without it and does not intend to seek an alternative supplier. Combined orphan and Qualified Infectious Disease Product exclusivity runs 12 years from 2018, with Orange Book patents to May 2035 (four listed patents expire December 5, 2026).

**BRINSUPRI** (brensocatib), a once-daily oral reversible DPP1 inhibitor licensed *from* AstraZeneca in 2016, was FDA-approved August 12, 2025 for NCFB in patients aged 12 and over, EC-approved November 2025, and MHRA-approved February 2026. Drug-substance patents run to January 2035, drug-product patents to 2039-2040, plus five-year NCE exclusivity.

The clinical pipeline centers on TPIP (inhaled treprostinil palmitil prodrug) with Phase 3 programs in PH-ILD (started Q4 2025) and PAH (started April 2026) and planned starts in PPF (2H 2026) and IPF (1H 2027). INS1033, a second internally designed oral DPP1 inhibitor, had its IND cleared in July 2026 for rheumatoid arthritis. Two AAV gene-therapy programs (DMD, ALS) are in Phase 1. Headcount was 1,664 at year-end 2025, including 555 commercial employees — infrastructure built for ARIKAYCE that BRINSUPRI was layered onto, which explains the speed of the launch curve.

Q2 2026 revenue of $425.5M splits BRINSUPRI $309.2M (U.S. $308.6M; international $0.6M) and ARIKAYCE $116.3M (U.S. $70.2M, +2%; international $46.1M, +19%).

The derived BRINSUPRI quarterly path — approximately $28M in Q3'25 (seven weeks), $144M in Q4'25, $208M in Q1'26, $309M in Q2'26 — is one of the steeper specialty launches on record. The offsetting datapoint is that **U.S. ARIKAYCE revenue was flat**: $133.07M in 1H26 versus $132.96M in 1H25. All ARIKAYCE growth is international (+21%). The domestic refractory-MAC population appears close to saturation at current penetration, which makes the ENCORE label expansion necessary to the ARIKAYCE growth case rather than incremental to it.

### Margins and margin quality

COGS moved from 23.6% of revenue (FY2024) to 20.3% (FY2025) to 15.7% (1H26), with Q2'26 at 15.8% versus 26.1% a year earlier. Two disclosed mechanics inflate this. Pre-approval BRINSUPRI inventory was expensed to R&D rather than capitalized, so it now passes through COGS at effectively no cost; as that inventory is consumed and replaced with capitalized production, the COGS percentage rises. Separately, the AstraZeneca license carries tiered royalties from high single digits to mid-teens on brensocatib net sales. A blended 15.7% total COGS is hard to reconcile with a mid-teens royalty plus manufacturing cost, which implies either that royalty tiers have not stepped up at current volumes, that royalties sit outside COGS, or that the zero-cost inventory benefit is currently large. The precise classification is not disclosed in the filings reviewed, so this is an unresolved definitional question rather than an established finding, but in every resolution of it, gross margin normalizes downward from here. Also known: a $35.0M one-time payment to AstraZeneca upon first achievement of $1.0B in annual brensocatib net sales, which FY2026 guidance would trigger.

### Operating loss and the path to breakeven

FY2025 R&D was $771.1M (+28.9%, including a one-time $40.0M Opsidio/INS1148 asset-acquisition charge) and SG&A $701.2M (+52.1%), producing a $1,246.8M operating loss. In Q2'26, R&D was $210.0M (+19%) and SG&A $247.5M (+60%, with professional fees and other external costs up 135% to $133.3M).

Reported Q2'26 GAAP operating loss was only $1.5M, but that includes a $99.8M non-cash gain on remeasurement of share-settled contingent consideration. Excluding it:

$425.5M − $67.2M COGS − $210.0M R&D − $247.5M SG&A − $2.1M amortization = **−$101.3M operating loss**

The equivalent 1H26 figure is −$301.6M (reported −$154.9M less the $146.7M gain). Because the Motus/AlgaeneX contingent consideration is settled in shares and marked to fair value, **GAAP earnings move inversely to the share price**: FY2025 recorded a $252.0M charge as the stock rose; 1H26 recorded a $146.7M gain as it fell. The liability moved from $372.1M to $170.6M over the half, including $54.9M of share settlements. Any screen using INSM's GAAP net income is anti-correlated with the share price.

This also reframes the headline earnings surprise. The reported 92.5% Q2 "beat" against a −$0.80 GAAP estimate was substantially the remeasurement gain. The properly matched comparison — adjusted loss of $0.52 against non-GAAP consensus of −$0.69 — was a genuine beat, but a smaller one.

Breakeven sensitivity: if Q4'26 revenue reaches roughly $551M (BRINSUPRI near the guided high-end ramp plus ARIKAYCE ~$118M) and total operating costs grow about 5% per quarter from Q2's $526.8M to ~$581M, the operating gap narrows to roughly −$30M before net interest. Adding back share-based compensation of roughly $43M per quarter (1H26 SBC: $87.0M), cash operating breakeven is plausibly a late-2026/2027 event and GAAP operating breakeven a 2027 event. This is conditional on SG&A growth decelerating from 60% year-over-year and on gross margin holding — the two assumptions we consider least secure.

Liquidity fell $269.7M in 1H26. Operating cash outflow was −$311.6M (1H25: −$467.7M), offset by $56.7M of option/ESPP financing inflows. Two normalizations matter: accrued compensation fell $49.9M on the Q1 annual bonus payout, and receivables rose $74.5M to $215.3M on a product growing 49% sequentially. Adjusting for both, underlying 1H26 outflow was closer to −$188M to −$262M. A single-quarter run rate should not be annualized here.

Debt is two non-convertible instruments. The Pharmakon term loan carries $546.8M of principal plus capitalized PIK at 9.6% fixed, repayable in eight equal quarterly installments of roughly $68.3M **beginning January 3, 2028** (accelerable to February 1, 2028 on prespecified events), final maturity September 2029, with a 2% exit fee and covenants restricting liens, debt, investments, M&A and dividends. The OrbiMed revenue-interest financing pays 4.5% of ARIKAYCE global net sales plus 0.75% of brensocatib net sales, capped at 1.8x the $150M purchase price ($270M, maximum $285M); carrying value $161.7M. At FY2026 guidance that stream costs roughly $30.6M. It is a capped, finite obligation of about $200M residual rather than a perpetual royalty; the AstraZeneca royalty is the durable economic leakage.

Net of the term loan, Insmed holds roughly +$613M of net cash before the OrbiMed liability, with no principal amortization for 18 months. That funds the company well past mid-2027 on current trajectory. The January 2028 amortization start against a still-negative cash flow base is the structural pressure point, and it argues for a refinancing (a company with a de-risked $1.3B+ product should access better than 9.6%) rather than necessarily an equity raise.

Dilution history is heavy and relevant: approximately $2.2B raised net since January 2023 (including 8.98M shares at $96.00 in June 2025 and 14.51M at $51.50 in May 2024), with weighted-average shares up 76.7% from 123.0M in FY2022 to 217.4M in Q2'26. Outstanding awards plus contingent acquisition shares total 24.1M, equal to 11.0% of current shares (9.9% post-issuance ownership dilution). The 4.98M Motus/AlgaeneX contingent shares carry a 28% weighted-average probability of success and are not part of current fully diluted capitalization; probability-weighted they represent roughly 1.4M shares.

A reported Altman Z-Score of −0.1 correctly identifies that Insmed cannot self-fund and depends on capital-market access, but the model was calibrated on manufacturers with positive EBIT. With $1.16B of liquidity, a 3.83x current ratio, interest-bearing debt/equity of 0.74x (not the 2.06x total-liabilities/equity figure), and no amortization until 2028, it should not be read as a near-term insolvency signal.

The near-term growth case is the strongest element of the file and is corroborated by estimate revision data that is unusually large even by launch standards:

Sixteen analysts moved FY2027 consensus, and the recommendation distribution is 5 Strong Buy / 18 Buy / 0 Hold / 0 Sell. Revisions of this magnitude combined with revenue growth above 100% normally precede further re-rating. We weight the signal, but discount it for two reasons: the base is a pre-profit company where small absolute changes in an EPS approaching zero produce enormous percentage revisions, and the underlying multiple is not compressed relative to the improving estimates (166x FY2027 consensus EPS; approximately 10x FY2027 revenue). The revision signal is most powerful when the stock is cheap against the rising numbers, which is not the case here.

**What must go right, and what the guidance already concedes.** Implied 2H26 BRINSUPRI of $733-883M against 1H's $517.0M is a large absolute add — $366M to $441M per quarter — but it embeds deceleration to roughly +21% and +15% sequential growth from +49%. That is normal launch physics. It also means the Q3 print is a two-sided event: a $375M-plus quarter validates the ramp, while $350M raises the question of whether the guidance high end is reachable.

**ARIKAYCE label expansion.** ENCORE's success removes accelerated-approval withdrawal risk and reopens a domestic growth path for a franchise that was flat in the U.S. The 7x TAM arithmetic overstates the practical opportunity. ARIKAYCE carries a boxed warning for respiratory adverse reactions; in ARISE, discontinuation was 22.9% versus 7.8% on comparator and dysphonia 41.7% versus 3.9%. Newly diagnosed patients with milder disease and an oral guideline regimen available face a different risk-benefit calculus than refractory patients. Management's own >$1B peak estimate implies roughly 2.3x current revenue, not 7x, which is consistent with that friction. Reported target for label expansion is 1H 2027.

**What has been foreclosed.** DPP1 inhibition failed twice outside bronchiectasis in eight months: BiRCh in chronic rhinosinusitis without nasal polyps (December 2025) and CEDAR in hidradenitis suppurativa (April 2026), both missing primary and secondary endpoints at all doses, both discontinued. The FY2025 10-K named HS as *the* Immunology & Inflammation clinical program; the August 2026 10-Q no longer names it. The "pipeline-in-a-product" framing for brensocatib is substantially retired; BRINSUPRI should be modeled as a bronchiectasis-specific asset. Management's simultaneous launch of INS1033, a second in-house DPP1 inhibitor targeting rheumatoid arthritis, indicates retained conviction in the target biology but commits new spending against a mechanism with two consecutive Phase 2b misses outside the airway.

**The ex-U.S. decision.** BRINSUPRI is EC-approved and UK-approved, yet 1H26 international revenue was $1.287M, generated through France's compassionate-access and named-patient programs. Both the 10-K and 10-Q state that the company continues to evaluate evolving U.S. policies "which will then impact the timing for future potential international commercial launches," and the 10-K goes further: if products are deemed subject to CMS's proposed GUARD model, "we may determine to forego commercializing our products outside of the US." Insmed already sells ARIKAYCE internationally ($153.5M in FY2025, +40.9%), so it has the ex-U.S. infrastructure. The most probable reading is that management judges a lower ex-U.S. reference price more costly, through MFN-based U.S. rebates, than the foregone ex-U.S. revenue. The alternative — ordinary 12-24 month reimbursement lag — cannot be excluded. Either way, growth is far more U.S.-dependent than the disclosed epidemiology (600,000 EU5 and 150,000 Japanese diagnosed bronchiectasis patients versus 500,000 U.S.) implies.

Two framings pull in opposite directions. On forward sales, 10x FY2027 for a company compounding above 100% with two monopoly indications and ~80% gross margins is defensible against commercial-stage rare-disease comparables. On normalized earnings power, the picture is tighter. Insmed's run-rate operating expense is already $1.83B annualized (Q2 R&D plus SG&A). At $3B of 2027 revenue with COGS normalizing to 20% and opex at $2.0B, EBIT is roughly $400M. At $4B of 2028 revenue with opex at $2.2B and COGS at 20%, EBIT approaches $1.0B, or roughly $800M net — about $3.50 per share on 228M shares. Reaching today's $134 requires the compounding to continue: approximately $6B of revenue by 2030 at a 30% net margin and a 25x exit multiple, discounted at 10%, yields about $128 today. That is below management's >$14B aspiration but materially above a probability-weighted view that discounts TPIP (two Phase 3 programs started within the last nine months in an indication the 10-K itself calls competitive with established treprostinil formulations) and assigns limited credit to ex-U.S. bronchiectasis while the pricing-policy overhang is unresolved.

Additional deductions the peak-multiple math omits: the AstraZeneca royalty (high single digit to mid-teens on brensocatib), the $35M sales milestone triggered by FY2026 guidance, the time value of a peak with no stated achievement year, and roughly 10% ownership dilution from outstanding awards.

**On sell-side targets.** Mean $200.32, median $197.50, range $169-243. The lowest published target sits 26% above the current price, and there are no Hold or Sell ratings among 23 analysts. On a growth stock at a compressed multiple, that configuration would argue targets are stale floors. Here the stock trades at 166x next-year EPS and 2.1x an aspirational peak, so the targets and the price plausibly reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation. A $200 target implies roughly 15x FY2027 revenue or 3.1x management's peak estimate — full credit for both TPIP and eventual ex-U.S. launch. We take the sell-side range as a marker of consensus enthusiasm rather than as evidence of mispricing, and note explicitly that our $150 base sits below the $169 low target.

Valuation score: 5.0. Not stretched enough to warrant REDUCE; not discounted enough to create asymmetry at this entry.

## Competitive Landscape

**Bronchiectasis.** BRINSUPRI is the only approved therapy in the U.S. and EU. Three threat vectors, in order of near-term materiality. First, off-label generic long-term macrolides remain the incumbent practice, and a January 2026 network meta-analysis concluded that macrolides showed a more pronounced exacerbation effect than DPP-1 inhibitors. That is an indirect cross-trial comparison rather than a head-to-head study, but it is a usable payer-leverage argument against a premium branded agent whose own ASPEN primary endpoint was a 21.1% (10 mg) and 19.4% (25 mg) exacerbation reduction, with severe-exacerbation reduction *not* statistically significant (p=0.1277 and p=0.1025) and FEV1 improvement significant only at 25 mg. Second, the 10-K confirms "several other entities currently conducting clinical trials for the treatment of bronchiectasis with a DPP1 inhibitor"; HSK31858 (Haisco) published Phase 2 results in *Lancet Respiratory Medicine* in March 2025. Third, NCE exclusivity does not preclude FDA from accepting or approving a 505(b)(1) NDA, so a structurally distinct DPP1 inhibitor faces only Insmed's compound-specific patents, not its exclusivity.

**NTM/MAC.** Structurally the more defensible franchise: no competing approved inhaled therapy in North America, Europe or Japan; guideline recommendation in refractory MAC; 12 years of combined orphan and QIDP exclusivity from 2018 with patents to 2035; and no known trials in refractory MAC. The offsets are tolerability-driven, as discussed.

**Pulmonary hypertension.** The 10-K states plainly that PAH is competitive with established marketed products including other treprostinil formulations. TPIP's differentiation is once-daily dosing versus 4-9 times daily, with lower Cmax and longer half-life. The controlled Phase 2b showed a placebo-adjusted 35% PVR reduction (p<0.001) and 6MWD +35.5 m (p=0.003). The July 2026 twelve-month open-label extension observations (6MWD +55.7 m, NT-proBNP down roughly 60%) are uncontrolled secondary observations and are not placebo-controlled evidence of efficacy.

**Channel structure.** Top four customers rose from 70% of gross product revenue (1H25) to 92% (1H26); the top three moved from 65% to 78%. These are specialty pharmacies and a specialty distributor — intermediaries, not end payors — so the exposure is receivable-collection and channel-access risk rather than end-demand risk. The product mix diversified from one commercial product to two; customer concentration deteriorated. Those are two different measures, and the first does not establish the second.

Trajectory is improving (share capture in a virgin indication, ENCORE de-risking, guidelines inclusion), which supports a 6.5. The ceiling is set by modest efficacy magnitude against a cheap incumbent, the 505(b)(1) gap, and a supply chain in which PARI is the sole nebulizer manufacturer, both commercial products are manufactured in Canada, TPIP's API comes from a single Taiwanese supplier, and Thermo Fisher subsidiaries simultaneously serve as commercial manufacturer, capacity partner, and pivotal-trial CRO.

## Risk Assessment

**Drug-pricing policy is the dominant, partially unpriced risk.** The 10-K states that ARIKAYCE is *believed* excluded from IRA negotiation on orphan designation but that "BRINSUPRI is likely to be subject to IRA price negotiation in the future," and that a significant portion of U.S. revenue for both products depends on Medicare reimbursement. Executive Order 14297 (May 2025) on MFN pricing, the GENEROUS Medicaid model (November 2025), and the GUARD Part D and GLOBE Part B proposed rules (December 2025) form the policy stack that has already caused the company to withhold an approved product from Europe. GUARD's current regulatory status is unverified; we treat it at its proposed-rule stage. Because Insmed's response has been to forgo ex-U.S. revenue rather than accept a lower reference price, the policy risk manifests as a growth ceiling rather than a price cut, which is harder for the market to observe and therefore more likely to be underweighted.

**Gross-margin normalization.** As discussed, 15.7% COGS is not a sustainable run rate once zero-carrying-cost inventory is consumed and AstraZeneca royalty tiers step up. A move to 22-25% COGS on $2.85B of 2027 revenue costs roughly $190-265M of gross profit relative to a flat-margin model — material against a projected $400M EBIT.

**Gross-to-net true-up.** Accrued sales allowances rose from $70.9M (12/31/25) to $146.4M (6/30/26). The benign reading is ordinary rebate payment-cycle timing on a growing base. The adverse reading is that accruals are building faster than payments on a steeply ramping Medicare-exposed product, creating true-up risk to reported net revenue. Both are consistent with the data.

**2028 amortization.** $546.8M at 9.6% amortizing from January 3, 2028 in eight quarterly installments, accelerable to February 2028 on prespecified events. At the realized 1H26 net cash decline of $269.7M per half, liquidity carries into late 2028, which intersects the wall. Refinancing at better terms is the likely path, but not guaranteed.

**Long-term safety on chronic therapy.** BRINSUPRI labeling discloses ALT elevations >3x ULN (1.2% at 10 mg, 0.9% at 25 mg versus 0% placebo), alopecia (1.5%/1.6% versus 0.4%) and skin cancers (0.5% at 10 mg, 1.9% at 25 mg versus 1.1% placebo). None is disqualifying; all are watch items for a therapy intended for indefinite use in a 500,000-patient U.S. population.

**Other disclosed items.** Goodwill of $136.1M subject to impairment testing; a French ARIKAYCE price refund obligation ($9.1M accrued); a 2023 Japanese 9.4% price cut; the BIOSECURE Act with five-year grandfathering for existing China relationships; and PPD project-addendum costs of $295.7M anticipated.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $215

BRINSUPRI reaches the high end of guidance ($1.40B) with Q3 above $380M, confirming that the U.S. bronchiectasis market is larger and faster-adopting than the launch-skepticism camp assumed (ClearBridge cited "concerns over the pace of Brinsupri's launch" as a Q2 detractor six weeks before the stock rose 34%). The ARIKAYCE sNDA is approved in 1H 2027, moving the domestic franchise off its flat base toward management's >$1B peak. Gross margin normalizes to only ~20% COGS. SG&A growth decelerates from 60% as launch spending annualizes, delivering cash operating breakeven in late 2026 and GAAP profitability in 2027, which removes the equity-raise overhang and enables a term-loan refinancing well below 9.6%. TPIP's PALM-ILD and PALM-PAH programs progress on schedule, keeping the >$6B peak claim alive as embedded optionality. On $3.2B of FY2027 revenue at 15x EV/sales, equity approaches $48.5B, or roughly $215 per share on 226M shares — consistent with the sell-side mean-to-high range.

### Bear Case — $85

Q3 BRINSUPRI comes in near $350M, and the market re-rates a growth stock whose sequential slope broke. Gross margin compresses toward 25-28% COGS as zero-cost inventory clears and AstraZeneca royalty tiers step, taking $250M-plus out of projected gross profit. Gross-to-net accruals true up against reported revenue. GUARD or GLOBE is finalized in a form that either forces MFN rebates on U.S. Medicare volume or permanently forecloses the 750,000-patient EU5/Japan diagnosed population, capping BRINSUPRI well below $7B. A DPP1 competitor advances into Phase 3, or payers press the macrolide comparison. SG&A keeps growing near 60%, cash breakeven slips into 2028, and the January 2028 amortization forces an equity raise on the heels of $2.2B already raised and a 76.7% increase in weighted-average shares. On $2.2B of FY2027 revenue at 8x EV/sales and 235M shares, the equity is roughly $76-85 — near the June 2026 low of $90.39.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires two things the company does not fully control (policy resolution and payer acceptance of a 21% exacerbation benefit against generic macrolides) and one it does (spending discipline, where the August 6 release now explicitly lists "control spending, prudently deploying capital" among four stated priorities and notes preclinical programs consume less than 20% of expenditures). The bear case requires only that the launch curve flatten faster than guided while margins normalize. Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected value near $146, or roughly +9% from $134.19 — positive, but thin against a −37% bear outcome.

## Conclusion

Insmed has delivered the operational result that matters most: a first-in-indication oral therapy scaling from a standing start to a $1.25-1.40B annual run rate inside twelve months, executed on commercial infrastructure already paid for by ARIKAYCE, alongside a confirmatory trial success that removes accelerated-approval withdrawal risk and reopens a domestic growth path for the older franchise. Consensus has responded by moving FY2027 EPS from roughly breakeven to $0.81 and by placing all 23 published ratings at Buy or better. The business is better than it was three months ago, and the estimate revision momentum is real.

The constraint is price. At $134.19 the enterprise carries 16.1x guided current-year revenue and roughly 10x our FY2027 estimate, embedding a path to approximately $6B of revenue by 2030 — attainable if BRINSUPRI approaches management's aspiration, unattainable if it plateaus in the $2.5-3B range as a U.S.-only asset. The specific items we cannot resolve favorably are the ones that determine which path applies: a reported ~84% gross margin that filings indicate is flattered by expensed pre-approval inventory and unresolved royalty tiering; a deliberate decision to leave 750,000 diagnosed EU5 and Japanese patients uncommercialized on U.S. pricing-policy grounds; channel concentration that moved from 70% to 92% of gross product revenue in twelve months; guidance that itself concedes sequential deceleration; and a management team that sold on every window from $94 to $129 — including on the day of the Q2 release — without a single open-market purchase in the window reviewed.

Existing holders have a positive twelve-month thesis with dense catalysts and should stay invested. New capital does not have adequate margin of safety at $134.19 following a 34% single-day gap, with a probability-weighted expected return near +9% against a −37% bear case. We would add toward $105-110, or on a Q3 print that pairs a $380M-plus BRINSUPRI quarter with COGS at or below 20% and visible SG&A discipline.

**Rating: HOLD. Base $150 / Bull $215 / Bear $85.**
