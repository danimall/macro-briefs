## Executive Summary

Tetra Tech is a $7.3B market cap consulting and engineering services firm focused on water, environment, and sustainable infrastructure. The stock has declined 35% from its February 2026 high of $43.14 to $28.03, driven primarily by the cancellation of virtually all USAID contracts (10.6% of FY2025 revenue) following Executive Order 14169 in January 2025. However, the financial impact has been far less severe than the stock price suggests: H1 FY2026 adjusted EPS was essentially flat year-over-year ($0.69 vs. $0.68) despite an 11.4% revenue decline, because USAID work was predominantly low-margin pass-through.

The company now trades at ~18x FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($1.50-$1.58 midpoint), a significant discount to the peer median P/E of 26.6x, with backlog growing 8% sequentially to $4.28B and segment margins expanding 220 basis points in GSG. The margin improvement is structural: the mix shift away from USAID pass-through toward higher-value consulting and fixed-price work permanently improves earnings quality. Analyst consensus targets range from $35 to $48, implying 25-71% upside.

Despite the attractive valuation, the path to re-rating requires visible revenue growth inflection, which likely won't appear in reported results until late FY2026 or early FY2027. The stock lacks a near-term dated catalyst beyond the gradual backlog conversion, and government spending policy remains unpredictable under the current administration. The setup is asymmetric—more upside than downside from current levels—but without the timing precision required for a high-conviction BUY. The position warrants accumulation on further weakness below $26, with upgrading contingent on sequential revenue growth or additional large contract wins.

## Company Overview

Tetra Tech provides consulting and engineering services across two segments: Government Services Group (GSG, ~45-49% of revenue) and Commercial/International Services Group (CIG, ~52-56%). The company operates in 100+ countries with 25,000+ employees, executing over 100,000 projects annually. Its client base includes U.S. federal agencies (31.6% of FY2025 revenue), international governments and multilaterals (37.4%), U.S. state/local (14.5%), and U.S. commercial (16.5%).

The company holds the #1 ENR ranking in Water Treatment and Desalination for 12 consecutive years, along with top positions in environmental management, consulting studies, and wind power. Its "Tetra Tech Delta" technology platform integrates AI, digital twins, and advanced analytics into traditional engineering services.

Revenue grew from $3.5B in FY2022 to $5.4B in FY2025, primarily through the $784M RPS acquisition in 2023 and organic growth. The capital-light model generates exceptional free cash flow ($439M in FY2025 on only $18.6M capex), enabling aggressive share repurchases ($350M since FY2025) and accretive acquisitions.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**

FY2025 revenue of $5.44B grew 4.7% over FY2024. Adjusted operating income reached $603.6M (+18.3%), with segment margins expanding to 16.0% (GSG) and 14.3% (CIG). GAAP results were distorted by the $115M Hunters Point settlement and $92.4M USAID-related goodwill impairment.

H1 FY2026 revenue declined 11.4% to $2.43B, almost entirely attributable to the $324M USAID revenue loss. Adjusted operating income was essentially flat at $265.0M vs. $267.2M prior year, and adjusted EPS grew 1.5% ($0.69 vs. $0.68). GSG margins expanded from 14.2% to 16.4% as low-margin pass-through work disappeared.

**Cash Flow:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was strong at $439M (8.1% FCF margin). H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $237.6M, aided by collections on terminated USAID contracts. Capital expenditures are minimal ($18.6M annually), making virtually all operating cash flow available for shareholders.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt is approximately $890M ($575M convertible notes at 2.25% maturing August 2028 + $315M drawn on the credit facility). Net leverage is 1.32x, well within the 3.50x covenant maximum. The company has $223.6M cash and $484.3M available on its $1.5B revolver (extended to 2030). The Altman Z-Score of 2.48 reflects the goodwill-heavy balance sheet typical of professional services M&A, not genuine financial distress.

**Capital Allocation:**

$498M remains on the buyback authorization. Management repurchased $100M in H1 FY2026 at ~$34/share average. The dividend was recently raised to $0.288/share annually (1.04% yield). The Halvik acquisition ($210M) adds cybersecurity capabilities. Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-aligned.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term headwinds (FY2026):** Revenue will decline ~5-8% for the full year as the USAID roll-off completes. Disaster response revenue is also lower year-over-year. These are one-time adjustments, not structural deterioration.

**Growth catalysts beyond USAID:**
1. **Water infrastructure demand:** EPA PFAS mandates, aging U.S. systems, Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act funding allocated to water. Data center cooling requirements (AI boom) are a direct demand driver for TTEK's core water engineering expertise.
2. **Backlog inflection:** $4.28B backlog grew 8% sequentially in Q2 FY2026, with ~72% converting within 12 months. New contract wins are more than offsetting the USAID loss.
3. **International diversification:** International revenue grew from 35.4% to 43.9% of the mix in H1 FY2026. UK, Australian, and other markets are growing.
4. **Halvik acquisition:** Adds federal cybersecurity/data analytics revenue, a high-growth niche.
5. **Army Corps of Engineers:** Five-year contract win in February 2026 confirms continued federal relevance beyond USAID.

**Growth limitations:** Organic revenue growth excluding acquisitions has historically been mid-single-digits. The company will not be a 15%+ organic grower. FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.72 implies only 12% growth over FY2026 guidance midpoint, which is respectable but not exceptional.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive: current year EPS revised +1.5% over 90 days, next quarter +2.6% over 30 days. This confirms stabilization rather than deterioration, but lacks the +30% revision velocity that signals a strong inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.03 with FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus of $1.54, TTEK trades at 18.2x current-year earnings. Against FY2027 consensus of $1.72, the forward P/E compresses to 16.3x.

**Peer comparison:** The Construction & Engineering peer median P/E is 26.6x. TTEK's discount is substantial:
- MYRG: 48.3x
- ECG: 34.5x
- ACA: 26.6x
- PRIM: 24.3x
- FLR: 20.3x
- **TTEK: 18.2x** (current year) / 16.3x (next year)

TTEK is the cheapest name in its peer set by a meaningful margin. The discount is attributable to USAID uncertainty and the recent drawdown, but the margins are actually superior to most peers (13%+ adjusted operating margins vs. typical 5-8% for construction/engineering firms).

**What is the market mispricing?** The market is pricing TTEK as if the USAID revenue loss represents permanent impairment to the business. In reality: (1) margins are improving because USAID was low-quality revenue, (2) backlog is growing, (3) the remaining business is structurally higher-quality, and (4) the revenue gap will be partially filled by new wins within 12-18 months. The market is anchoring to trailing revenue decline rather than forward earnings power.

**Fair value framework:** At 22x FY2027 EPS of $1.72 (a modest premium to the 20.3x peer floor, reflecting superior margins and competitive positioning), fair value is $37.84. At the peer median of 26.6x, fair value is $45.75. These bracket the analyst consensus target range of $35-$48.

**FCF yield:** At $28.03 on ~$430M normalized FCF, the FCF yield is approximately 5.9%, attractive for a capital-light professional services company with secular growth drivers.

## Competitive Landscape

TTEK's competitive position is strong and improving:

1. **Market leadership:** #1 ENR ranking in water treatment for 12 consecutive years. Top positions in environmental management, consulting, and wind power. These rankings reflect both capability depth and institutional client relationships.

2. **Technology differentiation:** Tetra Tech Delta platform (AI, digital twins, advanced analytics) creates switching costs and justifies premium billing rates. The Halvik acquisition adds cybersecurity capabilities.

3. **Client diversification improving:** The USAID loss has forced a healthier client mix. U.S. federal dropped from 33.4% to 24.0% of revenue, while international grew to 43.9%. Less concentration in any single political decision.

4. **Margin superiority:** 13%+ adjusted operating margins vs. industry-typical 5-8% reflects higher-value work and effective utilization management. Fixed-price contracts growing to 48.1% of revenue (from 38.1%) signals confidence in execution and pricing power.

5. **Scale in water infrastructure:** As EPA PFAS regulations and climate adaptation mandates drive multi-decade spending cycles, TTEK's 12-year leadership position creates a meaningful incumbency advantage for contract wins.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Government spending policy (HIGH):** 24% of current revenue from U.S. federal agencies. DOGE-driven spending cuts, continuing resolutions, or additional program cancellations could create further revenue pressure. However, the USAID cancellation was the most extreme possible manifestation, and the company survived with flat earnings.

**2. Revenue visibility gap (MEDIUM):** While backlog is growing, the translation to reported revenue growth will take 2-3 quarters. During this period, quarterly YoY comparisons will show revenue declines, which may keep the stock range-bound.

**3. Convertible note dynamics (LOW-MEDIUM):** The $575M notes at 2.25% maturing August 2028 need to be refinanced or repaid. Current interest rate environment makes refinancing more expensive. However, with $700M+ in available liquidity and strong FCF, this is manageable.

**4. Fixed-price contract execution (LOW-MEDIUM):** Growing to 48.1% of revenue. Cost overruns on fixed-price work could compress margins. The Hunters Point settlement ($115M) demonstrated that legacy project risks can be material. However, current project portfolio is primarily consulting-oriented rather than heavy construction.

**5. Goodwill ($2.2B, 51% of assets):** Serial acquisition strategy creates impairment risk if acquired businesses underperform. Most reporting units have >38% excess fair value cushion, but this bears monitoring.

**6. CEO transition (LOW):** Roger Argus is a 30-year veteran who ran both segments. Continuity, not disruption. Dan Batrack remains as Chairman.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Why HOLD rather than BUY despite the 6.79 score:** The specific obstacle is timing. The stock is statistically cheap (18x current year, 16x next year vs. 26.6x peer median) and the business is fundamentally improving (margins up, backlog growing, USAID headwind absorbed). However, the next earnings report is approximately 3 months away (Q3 FY2026, likely late July/early August 2026). Between now and then, there is no specific catalyst to close the valuation gap. Revenue will still show YoY declines in the next reported quarter. The broader government spending environment remains uncertain with no legislative resolution. Short interest at 5.5% with 4.7 days to cover indicates meaningful skepticism that won't reverse without positive data. The risk of dead money for 3-6 months while waiting for revenue inflection—especially in an environment where elevated Treasury yields and potential rate hikes create sustained multiple compression for Industrials—keeps this at HOLD rather than BUY. A BUY would require either: (a) price below $26 providing sufficient margin of safety to compensate for timing uncertainty, or (b) a visible catalyst within 30-60 days.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock declines below $26 (providing >40% upside to base case with limited additional downside)
2. Q3 FY2026 earnings show sequential revenue growth (indicating inflection has begun)
3. Material new contract win announcement (>$200M) in water or federal IT

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Additional federal program cancellations affecting DoD or EPA contracts
2. Backlog declines sequentially in Q3 FY2026
3. Management cuts FY2026 EPS guidance below $1.40

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY2026, expected late July/early August 2026). If revenue shows sequential improvement and margins hold, upgrade to BUY.

**Base case target:** $37.84 (22x FY2027 EPS of $1.72), representing 35% upside from current price, achievable over 12-18 months as revenue growth visibly inflects.

## Conclusion

Tetra Tech is a high-quality business temporarily impaired by the USAID cancellation, to which the market has overreacted in price terms. The financial reality—flat earnings, expanding margins, growing backlog, strong FCF—is materially better than the 35% stock decline implies. At 16-18x forward earnings vs. a 26.6x peer median, the valuation discount is disproportionate to the remaining risk.

However, the catalysts for re-rating are not imminent. Revenue inflection requires 2-3 quarters of backlog conversion. Government spending clarity requires legislative action or additional contract awards. The timeline for recovery is measured in quarters, not weeks. Maintain HOLD with a positive bias, accumulate below $26, and reassess at next earnings for upgrade to BUY.
