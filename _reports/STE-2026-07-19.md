**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

STERIS is a defensive, recurring-revenue infection-prevention business that has spent the past two years reshaping its portfolio (Dental and CECS divested, restructuring largely complete) into three higher-margin core segments: Healthcare, Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST), and Life Sciences. FY2026 delivered record revenue of $5.94B (+8.7% reported, +7.3% constant-currency organic), operating income of $1,102M (+27%), and free cash flow near $983M at a 16.4% FCF margin. The balance sheet is investment-grade (BBB/Baa2/BBB) with debt-to-capital down to 21.3%, and management has pivoted to shareholder returns via a new $1.0B buyback authorization (~5% of market cap) alongside a ~20th consecutive dividend increase.

The stock trades at $217.84, roughly 19.5x the FY27 adjusted-EPS midpoint of ~$11.20 the company guided to on May 11. That is a modest discount to where high-quality med-tech typically trades and below the current analyst target range ($225 low / $280 high, $256.86 mean). The valuation is reasonable but not compelling: FY27 growth is guided at 7-8% organic, so this is a mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit compounder. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (current-year +0.5% over 90 days, next-year flat), which does not clear the threshold that would argue for aggressive re-rating.

We rate STE a HOLD (growth_hold). The business quality and capital-return support ownership, and the ~19% drawdown from the January 2026 high of $269.44 has removed most of the froth without any corresponding fundamental deterioration. The combination of a decelerating analyst-sentiment trend, an EO litigation/regulatory tail concentrated in the highest-margin segment, newly elevated tariff exposure, and an Irish tax structure that faces Pillar Two headwinds keeps this below a BUY. The score trajectory (6.8 → 6.8 → 6.7) reflects a stable, high-quality name whose thesis has not materially changed across three reads; our current 6.35 acknowledges that the price decline has improved risk/reward while the offsetting overhangs remain.

## Company Overview

STERIS is a global provider of infection-prevention products and services, incorporated in Ireland (a legacy of the 2019 Synergy Health combination). The model is razor-and-blade: installed capital equipment (sterilizers, washers, endoscope reprocessors) pulls through high-margin consumables and long-tail service contracts. Revenue splits by type into Service ($2,876M, 48%), Consumables ($1,808M, 30%), and Capital Equipment ($1,252M, 21%), giving recurring revenue (service + consumables) of roughly 79% of total, the source of the company's earnings visibility.

Three reportable segments remain after the Dental divestiture:

- **Healthcare (~71% of revenue, $4,209M):** sterile processing and procedural products/services for hospitals, ASCs, and endoscopy suites. Operating margin ~24.6%. The volume engine, tied to procedure demand.
- **AST — Applied Sterilization Technologies (~19%, $1,139M):** outsourced contract sterilization for medical-device and pharma manufacturers via a global network of 60+ facilities, technology-neutral across gamma/cobalt-60, ethylene oxide (EO), and e-beam/X-ray. Operating margins near 46% make this the most profitable segment. Competes primarily with Sterigenics/Sotera.
- **Life Sciences (~10%, $589M):** consumables, capital, and services for biopharma aseptic manufacturing. ~42.6% operating margin.

Competitive advantages include an entrenched installed base with high switching costs, a regulatory compliance moat (FDA/EPA/NRC), scale in a fragmented market, and, in AST, near-irreplaceable sterilization capacity where EO is often the only viable modality for certain devices.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash-generation profile are the strongest parts of the story. Altman Z-score screens deep in the safe zone, debt/equity is 0.49, debt-to-total-capital fell to 21.3% (from 23.6%), and the current ratio is 2.09. This maps to a financial_health score of 8.0, and the improvement from 7.0 in prior reads reflects genuine de-leveraging following the Dental sale, not accounting cosmetics.

Revenue has compounded steadily: FY2023 $4,958M → FY2024 $5,139M → FY2025 $5,460M → FY2026 $5,936M. FY26 organic constant-currency growth was balanced across segments (Healthcare +7.6%, AST +6.7%, Life Sciences +6.7%), driven by mid-single-digit volume plus low-single-digit pricing.

Gross margin edged up to 44.2% from 44.0%. The composition matters: pricing added ~120bps and productivity contributed, but tariffs took away ~80bps and inflation another ~70bps. Tariffs are now a visible, recurring headwind rather than a one-off. Operating income rose 27.1% (18.6% margin), though a chunk of that reflects the absence of the FY25 EO litigation charge ($48.2M) and much lower restructuring ($3.4M vs. $62.3M) rather than pure operating acceleration. Net income of $782M (13.2% margin) is up from $615M and, before that, a depressed $378M in FY24, a reminder that GAAP earnings have been noisy from litigation, restructuring, and $265M of acquired-intangible amortization. Underlying operating income before adjustments grew a steadier ~9%.

Cash flow is high quality. Operating cash flow was $1,341M. Capex of ~$369M funds Healthcare/AST capacity, cobalt-60, and a new multi-year IT/service-workflow investment for FY27. The company paid $242M in dividends, repurchased $225M of stock, and authorized the new buyback on May 5, 2026. Interest expense fell to $61M from $86M as debt was retired with Dental proceeds.

The one soft spot is intangible concentration: goodwill of $4.2B plus intangibles of $1.6B equals ~54% of the $10.7B asset base, creating impairment risk if any reporting unit underperforms acquisition assumptions. Total debt of ~$1.93B is well-laddered (senior notes due 2031/2051, private placements, revolver largely undrawn with ~$1.05B available).

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the trailing four quarters, with the two most recent quarters missing by 1.1-1.6% (Q4 adjusted EPS $2.83 vs. $2.88 consensus). This is not a company that reliably clears the bar, which tempers confidence in forward estimates and argues against paying a premium multiple for beat-and-raise optionality.

## Growth Analysis

FY27 guidance calls for 7-8% revenue growth and adjusted EPS of $11.10-$11.30 (midpoint modestly above the prior $11.10 consensus). This is a durable mid-to-high-single-digit compounder underpinned by secular tailwinds: an aging population, rising procedure and endoscopy volumes, and continued outsourcing of sterilization to AST. Demand is procedure-linked and largely non-discretionary, which supports growth durability through economic softness.

Estimate revisions are the key qualifier. Current-year EPS estimates have risen just +0.5% over 90 days and next-year is flat; current-quarter and next-quarter revisions are within ±0.5%. This is stable, not accelerating, and falls well short of the +30% revision velocity that would signal an underestimated inflection. STERIS is compounding, not inflecting, so we weight growth_prospects at 6.0: solid and reliable, but without the acceleration that would justify a higher score. The strongest dimension in our framework (growth) discriminates winners, and STE screens as an above-average but not exceptional grower.

Within the mix, AST is the disproportionate profit driver: only ~19% of revenue but ~46% margins and double-digit growth in prior periods, so it contributes an outsized share of operating income. The growth thesis is therefore partly a bet on continued AST capacity utilization, which ties directly back to the EO regulatory environment discussed below.

## Valuation Assessment

At $217.84, STE trades at ~27.5x trailing GAAP EPS of $7.93 (the trailing number is depressed by amortization and prior-year noise, so forward EPS is the right anchor). Against the health-care-equipment peer set, the peer-median P/E is 22.6 (ZBH 22.6, HOLX 31.3, RMD 18.6, GEHC 15.5, DXCM 32.2). STERIS's forward multiple sits modestly below that median, which is defensible given its superior recurring-revenue mix and margin quality but not a screaming discount.

The stock trades below the entire analyst target range ($225 low / $269 median / $280 high). On an inflection stock with rising estimates, we would treat those targets as floors that lag reality, but the estimate-revision trend here is flat and the analyst-sentiment trend is degrading (one strong-buy downgrade and one new strong-sell in the recent revision window). That combination argues the targets are not stale in the way they would be for a fast-riser; the sell-side is simply modeling a stable compounder trading at a fair-to-slightly-cheap multiple.

Our valuation discipline treats cheapness without a catalyst as a warning rather than a buy signal. Here, the modest discount to peers lacks an independent catalyst to close it: no earnings inflection, no de-rating reversal, no forced structural re-rating. The buyback provides mechanical per-share support and is a genuine positive, but it is priced. We score valuation 5.5: fairly valued with modest upside, not the asymmetric setup that would warrant a higher mark. Base case fair value of ~$246 (22x $11.20) implies ~13% upside; the multiple would need to expand toward the high end of the peer range to reach the $280 bull target.

## Competitive Landscape

STERIS occupies a structurally attractive, defensive niche. In AST, the competitive set is thin (chiefly Sterigenics/Sotera and in-house sterilization), regulation is a moat, and margins reflect oligopolistic dynamics, though this is also where the EO regulatory overhang concentrates. In Healthcare, competition is broader (Getinge, Ecolab, Olympus, Stryker, Fortive/Advanced Sterilization), and pricing power is more contested amid provider consolidation. Life Sciences competes in biopharma aseptic manufacturing against broad-line and niche players.

Trajectory is stable-to-improving: the company posted balanced organic growth across all three segments, gross margin ticked up despite tariff drag, and TSR outperformed its comp peer group (index 121.45 vs. 106.94 in FY26). Its FY27 growth guide is competitive within large-cap med-tech, comparable to Medtronic's ~6.75-7.25% organic guide, and its tariff exposure appears more contained than some peers'. We score competitive_position 6.8: a widening or at least stable moat in AST, defensible share in Healthcare, and structural tailwinds, but with real pricing pressure in the larger Healthcare segment and an AST-insourcing tail that caps the level.

## Risk Assessment

**Ethylene oxide litigation and regulation — the defining company-specific risk.** STERIS's AST subsidiary operates EO sterilization facilities, and the EPA classified EO as a human carcinogen in 2016. Operators including Sterigenics have faced mass-tort exposure and verdicts exceeding $400M. STERIS settled substantially all pending Cook County, Illinois EO personal-injury claims for up to ~$48.2M (final agreements October 29, 2025), a fraction of the Sterigenics precedent, which suggests contained exposure. The settlement retains walkaway rights, the claims process is subject to court approval, and future EO litigation in other jurisdictions or tighter EPA emissions rules remain open tails. Because EO capacity underpins the highest-margin segment, this is central to the thesis rather than peripheral.

**Tariffs and trade policy (newly elevated).** The 10-K flags the USMCA six-year joint review commencing July 1, 2026 as a specific new risk to the Canada/Mexico manufacturing footprint. This is a genuine escalation in the risk profile versus prior filings and a swing factor for FY27 margins.

**Irish tax structure.** As an Irish-parented multinational, STERIS does not qualify for the OECD Pillar Two "side-by-side" safe harbor (only U.S.-parented groups do), exposing it to incremental GloBE taxes. The effective rate already rose to 25.0% (from 23.2%) on geographic mix and withholding taxes, and there is perennial risk of IRS challenge to the structure.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** ~$5.8B of goodwill plus intangibles means any segment underperformance versus acquisition assumptions could trigger material non-cash charges.

**Other:** OBBBA Medicaid funding cuts could pressure hospital customers; healthcare-provider consolidation pressures Healthcare pricing; AST insourcing by large device manufacturers is a structural threat to the highest-margin segment.

We score risk_factors 5.5. The EO tail and the newly elevated tariff/USMCA and Pillar Two exposures are specific, partly binary, and not fully priced, but the balance-sheet strength and the contained size of the EO settlement relative to precedent argue against a lower mark.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is quiet and does not contradict the fundamental view. Put/call volume and OI ratios are extremely low (0.03 and 0.07), indicating no meaningful hedging demand or bearish positioning. One-year historical volatility is 25.6%, and the nearest expiry implies a ±7.4% move, rising to ±13.9% at six months, a normal upward term structure with no signs of an imminent binary event being priced. Short interest is negligible at 2.4% of float (2.6 days to cover), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor an informed-short signal. The absence of protective put demand is mildly reassuring for a HOLD, though it also means the market sees little catalyst in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($280):** STERIS compounds revenue at the high end of its guide, AST margins hold near 46% as EO regulatory risk stays contained, the buyback shrinks the share count while FCF grows toward $1.1B, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer high (~25x) as the market rewards durable non-discretionary demand and the completed portfolio simplification. EPS reaches ~$11.30+, and the stock closes the gap to the analyst high target.

**Bear case ($185):** Tariff and USMCA disruption compress gross margin beyond the current drag, EO litigation reopens in a new jurisdiction or EPA tightens emissions rules and curtails capacity, Pillar Two lifts the effective tax rate further, and Healthcare pricing erodes under provider consolidation and OBBBA-driven hospital budget pressure. EPS stalls near $10.50-$10.80 and the multiple compresses toward the low-teens on a growth scare, implying ~15% downside.

**Base case ($246):** 22x on ~$11.20 FY27 EPS. The company delivers guidance, the buyback provides per-share support, and the stock re-rates modestly as the drawdown stabilizes. This implies ~13% upside over 12 months plus a ~1.1% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $246** (22x FY27 adjusted EPS of $11.20). ~13% upside from $217.84.
**Bull case target: $280** (25x $11.30). ~28% upside.
**Bear case target: $185** (~17x $10.90 on a margin/tax setback). ~15% downside.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the FY27 earnings cadence (next print, USMCA review commencing July 1, 2026, and any EO litigation developments).

This is a growth_hold: we expect appreciation to base case but the upside is not large enough, nor the setup asymmetric enough, to warrant a BUY given the identified tails.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions turn decisively positive (+5% or more over 90 days) alongside a stabilization/reversal in the degrading analyst-sentiment trend.
- A clean, final resolution of EO litigation exposure (no walkaway-right reversion, no new jurisdictional filings) that de-risks the AST margin profile.
- A pullback toward $195-200 (the 52-week low area) with fundamentals intact, restoring clear asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material EO development — a new large-verdict jurisdiction, EPA emissions rule that curtails EO capacity, or reversion of the Cook County settlement.
- FY27 gross margin compresses more than ~150bps on tariffs/USMCA, or the effective tax rate steps materially above 26% on Pillar Two.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge in any segment, signaling a reporting unit is underperforming acquisition assumptions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the July 1, 2026 USMCA review commencement are the natural checkpoints.

## Conclusion

STERIS is a high-quality, defensive compounder with a fortress balance sheet, near-$1B in FCF, a durable AST sterilization moat, and capital return via the buyback and steady dividend growth. The ~19% decline from the January high has removed valuation excess without corresponding fundamental deterioration, which improves risk/reward. The stock trades at a fair-to-slightly-cheap ~19.5x forward with flat estimate revisions, a degrading sell-side sentiment trend, two consecutive small earnings misses, and specific unpriced tails in EO litigation, tariffs/USMCA, and Irish tax structure. That mix supports ownership of a quality name but not a BUY.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Own it for the durable franchise and mid-teens base-case upside; look to upgrade on a positive estimate-revision turn, a clean EO resolution, or a pullback toward the low-$200s/high-$190s that restores clear asymmetry.
