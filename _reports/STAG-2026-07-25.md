**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

STAG Industrial is a single-segment industrial REIT that owns 601 buildings across 41 states (~120.3 million square feet, ~96.4% leased), pursuing a granular, secondary-market acquisition strategy that differentiates it from coastal/infill-focused peers like Prologis and Rexford. The investment case rests on two durable pillars: a conservative, well-hedged balance sheet and a large embedded mark-to-market re-leasing tailwind. These are offset by a re-leasing engine that is normalizing off peak levels and a valuation that has caught up to fundamentals after a 15.6% run over the trailing year.

The most important fundamental thread is the deceleration in cash leasing spreads: +28.3% in 2024, +24.0% in 2025, and management guidance of 18-20% for 2026. That trajectory is still exceptional and confirms in-place rents remain well below market, but the peak-cycle tailwind is fading, and same-store occupancy slipped 120 basis points year-over-year in Q1 2026 (96.6% vs. 97.8%). Current-year FFO estimates have been revised up modestly (+9.8% over 90 days), and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, which supports mid-single-digit FFO growth. Same-store NOI grew 4.7% in FY2025 and 4.1% in Q1 2026 — solid but decelerating.

At $41.11 the stock trades near its 52-week high of $42.61, at roughly 28x forward FFO per share and offering a ~3.75% dividend yield that now sits above the industrial-REIT peer median. The balance sheet is a genuine strength (99%+ fixed-rate debt, ~38% net debt to real estate cost basis, $805.7M liquidity, investment-grade), but that same rate protection caps the benefit of any rate decline while each future refinancing re-prices upward. We rate STAG a HOLD on an income basis. The reliable return here is the well-covered dividend plus mid-single-digit organic growth; we do not see an asymmetric re-rating catalyst at the current price and would want a pullback toward the mid-$30s to turn constructive.

## Company Overview

STAG operates as an UPREIT, owning approximately 98% of its operating partnership, and focuses exclusively on industrial (primarily warehouse/distribution) properties. The defining feature of the business is diversification and a deliberate focus on individual assets and small portfolios across the broader set of Tier 1 markets — secondary and tertiary industrial hubs like Chicago, Greenville SC, Minneapolis, Pittsburgh, Columbus, and Detroit. This "relative value" approach targets less-competitive deals with higher going-in cap rates.

The diversification is structural and serves as the primary risk mitigant. No single tenant exceeds 2.8% of annualized base rent (Amazon, spread across seven leases). No single industry exceeds 11.1% (Air Freight & Logistics). The top 20 tenants represent only 16.3% of ABR, and the top 20 markets 59.2%. The portfolio is majority single-tenant and predominantly triple-net, so tenants bear most operating expenses. The company is a lean operator — 93 employees managing a $7B-plus portfolio.

STAG's tenants (Amazon, Schneider Electric, FedEx, DHL and similar logistics/distribution operators) are customers, not affiliates. The company is 100% industrial, which is both its focus advantage and its concentration risk: there is no property-type diversification to cushion an industrial downturn.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue grew from $657M (2022) to $845M (2025), a steady mid-to-high single-digit trajectory. Reported net income of $279M in 2025 was inflated by $93.8M of property-sale gains (versus $32M in 2024), so GAAP earnings substantially overstate the year-over-year operating improvement. The cleaner operating metrics are NOI of $673M (+9.9%) and FFO of $488M (+6.4%). Operating cash flow was roughly flat at $463M.

**Earnings optics are noisy and should be handled carefully.** GAAP EPS swings with property-sale gains. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.32 fell from $0.49 a year prior, but that decline was driven entirely by lower gains ($15.1M vs. $49.9M) — FFO actually rose 8% to $126.6M, or roughly $0.66 per share. The four-quarter consensus beats (40.5%, 91.7%, 14.1%, 20.3%) reflect a Core-FFO-versus-GAAP-consensus mismatch and should not be read as clean, repeatable operating beats. The reliable operating figure is FFO per share.

**Same-store performance.** Cash rent change on new and renewal leases was +38.2% on a straight-line basis in FY2025. Same-store occupancy softened to 96.6% in Q1 2026 from 97.8% a year prior.

**Balance sheet — the genuine strength.** Total debt is roughly $3.2B. Over 99% of debt is fixed or swapped to fixed; only the revolver ($200M drawn at Q1'26) floats. Weighted-average interest rate is ~4.42%, weighted-average maturity ~4.3 years. Liquidity of $805.7M at Q1 2026 was mostly the undrawn $1.0B revolver. The company is investment-grade and in compliance with all covenants (max leverage 0.60x; actual well below). Only 0.1% of debt is secured.

**A note on screening artifacts.** A generic screen flags STAG's Altman Z-Score of 1.43 as "distress zone" and shows negative retained earnings of ~$1.0B. Both are false positives for a mandatory-payout REIT. The Z-Score penalizes real estate's inherently high asset base and low revenue-to-assets ratio, and negative retained earnings simply reflect the requirement to distribute ~90%+ of taxable income. The healthy signals — positive net income, positive ROA, operating cash flow exceeding net income, 38% net leverage, full covenant compliance — indicate a well-capitalized REIT, not a distressed one. We score financial health on that basis.

**Capital allocation.** Acquisitions have slowed deliberately since 2022 — one building/$80.7M in Q1 2026 versus $457M in FY2025 and $707M in FY2024 — as management awaits better risk-adjusted pricing. Growth is funded with a disciplined mix of ATM forward equity ($750M program), disposition proceeds, and unsecured notes. The development pipeline is 7 projects, ~$149M construction-in-progress, targeting ~7.4% yields.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. Organic growth remains healthy: mid-single-digit same-store NOI plus a substantial embedded mark-to-market gap that continues to convert into higher cash rents. The 2026 cash rent spread guidance confirms in-place rents remain well below market even in secondary markets — a legacy of the 2021-2022 industrial boom that STAG will harvest over multiple years as leases roll.

The concern is trajectory. The spread engine is decelerating in a clear, three-year pattern. This is the single most important trend to monitor. If market rents cool while in-place rents catch up, the +20-40% mark-to-market re-leasing tailwind that has driven FFO growth fades toward normalized 3-4% escalators. Meanwhile, management describes demand as "moderating relative to recent peaks" while "gradually recovering toward long-term average."

The estimate-revision picture is constructive but modest. Current-year FFO/EPS estimates rose to $1.12, and next-quarter estimates rose +4.1%. These are positive but well below the +30% velocity that would signal a genuine acceleration. The revisions reflect steady upward drift. Next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.8% over 90 days). Applying the calibration lesson: growth prospects here are directionally positive but not a top-tier standalone signal — the recent separation is modest, and the external-growth lever (acquisitions) is throttled back by management's own discipline.

Management's April 2026 tone was constructive — CEO Crooker noted that "the multiyear weakness in demand for big box product has reversed." Structural tailwinds (e-commerce, onshoring/nearshoring, supply-chain resilience, higher inventory-to-sales ratios) remain intact, and subdued speculative construction is favorable for landlords. But these are slow-burn demand drivers, not near-term catalysts.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price tightly: low $40, high $46, mean $41.92, median $42.00 — the stock is trading essentially at consensus mean, with only ~12% upside to the high target and effectively at the low target.

The calibration record on valuation is instructive: a cheap-looking multiple has historically been an anti-predictive value-trap warning, and analyst targets function as floors only for cheap, high-momentum stocks. STAG is neither cheap nor at a premium multiple that lets targets act as ceilings — it is a fairly-valued name at consensus. The FFO growth (mid-single-digit) does not justify a premium to the peer median, and the multiple does not offer the compressed-valuation-versus-improving-estimates setup that would flag a mispricing. This is a stock priced for what it is, without asymmetric upside. We score valuation below neutral. The valuation trajectory in prior reports (5.5 → 5.0) reflected this compression as the stock ran; the further move to the 52-week high reinforces that direction, and we mark it at 4.5. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input; the fundamentals (decelerating spreads, flat next-year estimates, at-consensus pricing) do not support a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

STAG's competitive position is stable-to-improving within its niche. Its granular, secondary-market strategy is genuinely differentiated from Prologis, EastGroup, and Rexford, all of which concentrate in coastal/infill gateway markets. The persistence of +20-40% cash rent spreads even in secondary markets validates that STAG acquired well below-market in-place rents and faces less deal competition, supporting higher going-in cap rates.

The diversification moat is real: the granular tenant, industry, and market concentration insulates STAG from any single tenant or market shock better than more concentrated peers. The industry backdrop is favorable at the margin — speculative construction is subdued and the supply pipeline is tilting toward build-to-suit, which limits new competitive supply.

The offsetting consideration is scale. STAG is the smaller-scale, higher-organic-growth name relative to Realty Income and the large industrial platforms; its capital cost and access are structurally worse than the mega-cap REITs. And 100% industrial exposure means no property-type hedge. On balance the trajectory is favorable — share within its niche is defensible, the moat is granular and hard to replicate, and structural onshoring/e-commerce tailwinds support demand — which supports a competitive-position score modestly above neutral, consistent with the prior report's improvement to 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Re-leasing normalization** is the primary fundamental risk. The spread engine's deceleration is not vacancy risk but rent-spread compression: if market rents cool while in-place rents catch up, FFO growth reverts toward baseline escalators. This is the trend to watch quarter by quarter.

**Occupancy softening.** Management cites moderating demand and an active (though build-to-suit-skewed) supply pipeline.

**Interest-rate/refinancing risk.** The book is well-hedged today, but each refinancing re-prices upward — several sub-3% notes and a 2.06% Term Loan A mature 2026-2027. Interest expense rose 16.8% in 2025 and 10.3% in Q1 2026. In a higher-for-longer environment, this is a structural FFO headwind, and STAG's fixed-rate protection also caps the benefit of any rate decline.

**Tariff/trade-policy exposure.** The 10-K added expanded language on 2025 tariffs harming tenants reliant on imported goods; Air Freight & Logistics is 11% of ABR. This is a newer macro-driven risk factor.

**Capital-markets dependence.** As a ~90%+ payout REIT, STAG cannot self-fund growth and relies on equity/debt markets. The stock currently trades near/below NAV per the research brief, so accretive external growth is only marginally feasible; a lower price would further constrain it.

**Governance.** Standard Maryland REIT provisions (9.8% ownership cap, board authority to issue preferred, anti-takeover provisions, exclusive-forum bylaws) entrench the board. The UPREIT structure creates potential tax-interest conflicts on asset sales (management/LPs hold ~2% of OP units). These are typical for the structure and largely priced.

Most of these risks are known and reflected in the current valuation. There are no binary regulatory or litigation catalysts, and the concentration risk is deliberately minimized. We score risk factors at 6.0 — few idiosyncratic surprises, but real and identifiable fundamental headwinds (spread deceleration, occupancy softening, refinancing) that are not yet fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 24.8% versus 20.4% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich, with the term structure in backwardation (24.8% near-term vs. 19.3% at six months), suggesting the market is pricing some imminent event stress (likely the next earnings print). IV has risen 6 points since late June (18.9% → 24.8%) as the stock rallied 5%. Put/call skew is mildly elevated at 4.8%, and the implied one-month move is ±6.1%. Put/call volume (0.09) and open-interest (0.11) ratios are low, and OI shifted heavily toward calls (0.40 → 0.11), indicating little defensive positioning. The market is pricing a near-term move but not a directional collapse, consistent with a fairly-valued name into an earnings catalyst. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($46-48).** The demand recovery CEO Crooker described accelerates, big-box absorption strengthens, and cash spreads hold at the upper end of the 18-20% guide or surprise higher. Occupancy stabilizes near 97%, same-store NOI reaccelerates toward 5%+, and FFO/share compounds at high-single-digits. Rate declines allow STAG to re-lever accretively and resume acquisitions at attractive cap rates. A ~30x forward FFO multiple on rising FFO drives the stock to the high analyst target of $46 and beyond, plus the ~3.75% yield.

**Base Case ($42-44).** Spreads settle in the middle of guidance, occupancy stabilizes in the high-96% range, same-store NOI runs ~4%, and FFO/share grows mid-single-digits. The multiple holds near the peer median. Total return is roughly the dividend yield plus modest FFO growth and slight multiple support — high-single-digit total return over 12 months, landing near the consensus mean/median of ~$42.

**Bear Case ($33-35, ~15-20% downside).** Industrial demand disappoints, spreads compress faster than guided toward baseline escalators, occupancy erodes another 100-150 bps, and refinancing headwinds compress FFO growth to flat. The multiple de-rates toward the low end of the peer range as the growth premium evaporates. The stock reverts toward its 52-week low of $33.72, cushioned by the well-covered dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The reliable return here is the ~3.75% dividend yield, well covered by FFO (~$1.56 annualized dividend against ~$2.60+ annualized FFO/share, a payout in the normal REIT range), plus mid-single-digit FFO growth. Near the 52-week high and at consensus mean, we do not see an asymmetric appreciation setup.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$30s (roughly 15% below current), which would restore a yield above 4% and a discount to peer FFO multiples with the same fundamentals intact.
- Cash leasing spread guidance stabilizing or re-accelerating above 20% for 2027, reversing the multi-year deceleration.
- Same-store occupancy recovering above 97.5% alongside same-store NOI reaccelerating above 5%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cash spreads compressing below 15% on a reported basis, signaling the mark-to-market engine has largely run its course.
- Same-store occupancy falling below 95% or a dividend-coverage deterioration (FFO payout above ~90%).
- A refinancing that materially raises the weighted-average interest rate above ~5.5% and compresses FFO growth to flat/negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), with particular attention to the reported cash-spread figure, same-store occupancy, and any change to the full-year guidance. The trajectory of the leasing-spread engine, more than any single quarter's FFO, determines whether this stays an income hold or becomes a re-rating candidate.

## Conclusion

STAG Industrial is a high-quality, conservatively financed industrial REIT with a genuinely differentiated secondary-market strategy and a large embedded mark-to-market re-leasing tailwind. The balance sheet is a clear strength — the fixed-rate debt, low net leverage, ample liquidity, and investment-grade ratings give above-average protection in a higher-for-longer environment. The organic growth engine remains healthy at mid-single-digit same-store NOI.

The offsetting reality is that the re-leasing tailwind is decelerating in a clear pattern, same-store occupancy is softening, the external-growth lever is throttled by management discipline, and the stock trades near its 52-week high at a peer-median multiple with a yield now above the peer median. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly, and there is no identifiable catalyst for a meaningful re-rating. This has been a HOLD across the prior three reports, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that call — the fundamentals are steady-to-modestly-improving, but the price has caught up. We rate STAG a HOLD on an income basis, with the well-covered ~3.75% dividend plus mid-single-digit growth as the reliable return, and would look to a pullback toward the mid-$30s to turn constructive.
