## Executive Summary

Mettler-Toledo delivered a good second quarter and raised full-year guidance, and the stock has responded: from a May 13 close of $1,025.53 the shares have recovered 38.9% to $1,425.11, now 6.6% below the 52-week high of $1,525.17. Adjusted EPS of $11.46 beat the non-GAAP consensus of $10.78 by 6.3%, FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance moved to $47.15–$47.50 from $46.30–$46.95, and local-currency sales growth guidance rose to +4–5% from ~4%. China inflected sharply positive — Q2 external sales +16% in USD and +9% in local currency, segment profit +14% — reversing the 2023–2024 pattern that caused the earnings plateau.

The problem is that the underlying profitability did not improve at anything like the pace of the multiple. Q2 contained a $52.4M tariff recovery from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, of which $27.8M was committed back to customers, for a $24.6M net pretax benefit. Management correctly excluded it from adjusted EPS (a −$0.92 reconciling item), which is a genuine disclosure-quality positive. But normalizing both items, Q2 gross margin was 59.3% against 59.0% a year earlier — thirty basis points — and pretax margin was 25.1% versus 25.3%, flat to slightly down. For the half, adjusted operating margin fell to 27.7% from 27.9%, and adjusted free cash flow declined 10.3% to $366.7M from $408.7M. Buybacks of $412.5M exceeded adjusted FCF by roughly $46M. The guidance raise is real but back-half loaded against a business with one-to-two months of backlog and a fourth quarter that carries 27–30% of annual sales.

At $1,425.11 the shares trade at 30.1x the guided FY2026 adjusted midpoint of $47.33 and 27.6x the FY2027 consensus of $51.69, against a life-sciences-tools peer median P/E of 29.9x. The analyst mean target of $1,468.91 sits 3.1% above spot; the recommendation mix has degraded (strong buys 2→1 over three months, with a strong sell appearing). The May 8 print demonstrated what this multiple costs when organic growth disappoints — a 14.8% single-session decline and 22.5% peak-to-trough over four sessions. Base-case twelve-month upside of roughly 3% against that demonstrated asymmetry produces a probability-weighted expected return near zero. This is the fifth consecutive review of MTD; the prior four were HOLD, and the improvement in fundamentals since the last review has been smaller than the improvement in price. We move to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Mettler-Toledo manufactures and services precision measurement instruments, selling into more than 140 countries with direct commercial presence in roughly 40. FY2025 revenue of $4,026.4M split laboratory ~56% (balances, pipettes and tips, titrators, thermal analysis, pH and density instruments, spectrophotometers, automated chemistry, process analytics, LabX software), industrial ~39% (industrial weighing, vehicle and rail scales, transport and logistics dimensioning, and product inspection — metal detection, x-ray, checkweighers, machine vision), and food retailing ~5%. Q2 2026 mix was 55/40/5. Geography: 42% Americas, 29% Europe, 29% Asia and other, with emerging markets roughly one-third of sales and China alone 16%.

Two structural features define the investment case. First, service was ~25% of FY2025 sales at 54.4% gross margin, up from 24% in FY2024 and 23% in FY2023, and grew faster than product revenue in each of those years (+10% USD, +9% local currency, +7% organic in Q2 2026). Roughly 9,300 of ~18,100 employees sit in sales, marketing and service across ~40 countries. The 10-K states that of more than 5,600 patents and trademarks, no single patent is material; the durable asset is the field organization and the installed base it serves. Second, no end-customer exceeded 1% of FY2025 net sales, an unusually strong diversification disclosure.

The counterweight is visibility. Backlog is typically one to two months; the company builds to order. Deferred revenue of $257.5M at June 30, 2026 is not backlog; the 10-Q states that performance obligations beyond customer prepayments and deferred revenue on contracts longer than one year are immaterial. Revenue is a near-real-time read on customer spending with no order book to cushion an air pocket, and Q4 carries 27–30% of annual sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Correcting the screen.** An automated financial-health screen returned an Altman Z-Score of 1.14 and a 0.4% operating margin. Both are artifacts. Rebuilding FY2025 from the 10-K: gross profit $2,390.6M less R&D $199.4M, SG&A $998.3M, amortization $74.5M and restructuring $17.9M gives operating profit of $1,100.5M, a 27.3% margin; less interest expense $68.5M plus other income $16.8M gives pretax income of +$1,048.8M, matching MD&A. EBIT/assets is $1,100.5M / $3,712.6M = 29.6%, not 0.005. Book equity of −$23.6M at December 31, 2025 reflects $9,839.4M of treasury stock against $9,238.2M of retained earnings — buyback-manufactured, and equity crossed back to +$12.8M by June 30, 2026.

**Leverage and coverage are conservative.** Total debt of $2,152.2M less cash of $66.9M gives net debt of $2,085.3M at year-end. Against an EBITDA proxy of $1,226.1M (EBIT plus $51.1M depreciation and $74.5M amortization), net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.70x and interest coverage $1,100.5M / $68.5M = 16.1x. Covenants require net debt/EBITDA ≤3.5x and coverage ≥3.0x, with confirmed compliance. This EBITDA proxy is not the contractually defined measure, but the conclusion of leverage under 2x and coverage in the mid-teens is robust to definitional differences. At June 30, 2026, debt was $2,112.0M: $1,248.6M of private-placement notes with coupons of 1.06%–5.45% maturing 2029–2037, and $783.1M drawn on a $1.35B revolver maturing May 2029 with $562.7M available across 13 lenders. Fair value of the debt is $187.0M below carrying value, an unrecognized economic asset from below-market fixed-rate borrowing.

**Cash conversion is high and stable.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $955.8M (FY2024 $968.3M, FY2023 $965.9M) against capex of $107.1M, giving FCF of $848.7M, or 21.1% of revenue and 97.6% of net income. Capex is guided to ~$130M in 2026.

Revenue compounded at +0.9% over FY2022–FY2025 and net income declined 0.1% in absolute dollars. Diluted share count fell 9.0%, producing GAAP EPS growth of +3.1% annually and adjusted EPS growth of +2.9%. Substantially all per-share earnings growth over three years came from share-count reduction. The causes of the plateau are identifiable and arguably transitory — the 2023 China downturn, post-COVID pharma destocking, a Q4 2023 European logistics disruption, and roughly $50M of 2025 tariff costs — but the record is the correct base against which to judge a 30x forward multiple.

**Q2 2026 and the first half.** Reported net sales were $1,027.3M (+4% USD, +3% local currency); excluding the $27.8M customer refund, $1,055.1M, +7.3%. GAAP diluted EPS was $11.55 versus $9.76; adjusted EPS $11.46. The GAAP-to-adjusted bridge adds back $0.26 of purchased-intangible amortization, $0.22 restructuring, $0.04 tax timing and $0.31 of acquisition contingent consideration, and subtracts $0.92 for the tariff refunds net of tax. That cross-checks: $24.551M × (1 − 0.245) ÷ 20.166M shares = $0.92, implying ~24.5% tax on a U.S.-only item, consistent with combined federal-plus-state incidence.

Adjusted operating profit was $309.3M, 29.3% of ex-refund sales, up 50bp from 28.8%. But the two items sitting below that line — an $8.4M charge to increase acquisition contingent-consideration liabilities and $5.45M of restructuring — are both added back in adjusted EPS, and FY2025 by contrast recorded a $4.4M net *benefit* from contingent-consideration reductions, a roughly $12.8M year-over-year swing flowing through adjusted EPS asymmetrically. Normalizing both tariff items, Q2 pretax income was $264.8M on $1,055.1M, a 25.1% margin, against $248.7M on $983.2M, or 25.3%. Adjusted EPS growth of +14% therefore overstates underlying pretax progress, which was flat.

For the half: net sales $1,974.4M (+6% USD, +3% local currency reported, +3% organic); adjusted EPS $20.35 versus $18.27 (+11%); adjusted operating margin 27.7% versus 27.9%; adjusted FCF $366.7M versus $408.7M, down 10.3%. GAAP operating cash flow of $450.2M included the $42.9M tariff cash refund; excluding it, ~$407.3M, down ~5%, which the 10-Q attributes primarily to tax-payment timing. Q1 2026 net earnings grew only 3.6%. Inventories rose to $411.6M from $387.2M. Buybacks of $412.5M (325,348 shares at an average $1,267.85) exceeded adjusted FCF by roughly $46M, bridged by a cash drawdown from $66.9M to $51.4M.

**Tax is a real headwind.** The FY2025 reported effective rate of 17% included $13.7M of uncertain-tax-position releases and a $5.8M valuation-allowance benefit. The projected FY2026 rate before discrete items is 19.0%. At constant pretax profit that is a 2.4% drag on net income ((1−0.19)/(1−0.17)−1), meaning guided +10–11% adjusted EPS growth requires roughly 12–13% pretax growth plus buyback accretion. A $68.1M valuation allowance remains as potential future offset; OECD Pillar Two pulls the other way.

**A note on data conflict.** The supplied earnings-surprise table shows a June 2026 quarter estimate of $8.79 against an actual of $8.91, and duplicate identical rows for December 2025 and March 2026. These do not reconcile with the filed and press-released figures ($11.46 adjusted against a $10.78 non-GAAP consensus, a 6.3% beat). We use the filing and earnings-release basis throughout and treat the surprise table as an unreliable feed; the directional conclusion of four consecutive modest beats is unaffected.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is for local-currency sales growth of +4–5% excluding tariff refunds to customers, and adjusted EPS of $47.15–$47.50, +10% to 11%. Q3 guidance is ~+4% local-currency sales and $12.00–$12.15 adjusted EPS, +8–9%. Consensus has drifted up modestly: current-year estimates +1.5% over 30 days and +1.8% over 90 days to $47.34; next year +0.8%/+1.0% to $51.69. This is positive earnings momentum, but nowhere near the magnitude that signals an underestimated inflection: a low-single-digit revision pace on a stock at 30x forward is confirmation of guidance, not evidence of mispricing.

The regional composition matters more than the aggregate. Q2 organic local-currency growth: Americas +1%, Europe +4%, Asia and rest of world +9%. China external sales were $187.2M, +16% USD and +9% local currency, with Chinese segment profit of $102.1M, +14%, following FY2023 $718.8M → FY2024 $628.4M (−13%) → FY2025 $634.8M (+1%) in external sales. China has genuinely inflected. But the Americas — 42% of revenue — is nearly stalled, and the FY2025 risk factors name "recent cuts in U.S. governmental funding related to medical and scientific research" and "policies reducing drug pricing like Most Favored Nation pricing" explicitly. The filings do not connect those policies to the +1% Americas number; the juxtaposition is suggestive but remains an inference. If Americas growth stays near +1%, that is the binding constraint on 2026–2027, not China.

Segment profit shows the same bifurcation: U.S. $129.6M (+40% including the $24.6M net tariff benefit; roughly +13% excluding it against a prior-year base depressed by 2025 tariff costs), Chinese $102.1M (+14%), Other $33.2M (+7%), Swiss $68.9M (−2%), Western European $48.9M (−6%). Swiss Operations is the manufacturing and IP hub — $836.2M of its $1,047.1M FY2025 segment sales were intersegment — so its profit is transfer-pricing-driven and is not a demand read.

The most underquantified growth input is tariffs. In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA tariffs; the replacement Section 122 10% tariff expired at end-July 2026, and on July 23, 2026 new Section 301 rates took effect at 12.5% (Switzerland, China) and 10% (EU, UK, non-USMCA Mexico). Against rates in force at the start of 2026 — China 30%, Mexico 25%, Switzerland 15%, EU 15% — MTD's tariff burden has fallen on every major sourcing lane. The company raised prices in 2025 to "fully offset" tariffs and has not disclosed rolling them back; Q2 commentary cited favorable price realization and lower tariff costs together. If retained, this is a structural margin tailwind separate from the one-time refund. The magnitude is not disclosed, and the evidence so far is modest: ex-item gross margin improved only 30bp, partly offset by unfavorable FX and higher transportation costs. A 50–80bp gross-margin benefit on ~$4.2B of revenue would be roughly $21–34M pretax, or $0.85–$1.35 of EPS, 2–3% — helpful, partly embedded in the raise, and reversible given four regime changes in eighteen months.

Growth prospects score 5.5, unchanged. The China inflection and guidance raise are real positives; the Americas stall, the 200bp tax step-up, and a three-year record of ~1% revenue and ~0% absolute earnings growth cap the score.

Peer medians in life-sciences tools stand at 29.9x P/E with zero yield: ILMN 30.1x, WAT 52.2x, IQV 22.4x, A 26.0x, MEDP 29.9x. MTD at 32x trailing is a modest premium to the median and cheap only against Waters. The dispersion is wide enough that the median carries limited information; what it does establish is that MTD is not a valuation outlier in either direction.

The relevant question is what 30x buys. Guided adjusted EPS growth of 10–11% includes roughly 3pp of buyback accretion at the FY2026 plan of $825–875M, leaving 7–8% of operating per-share growth against a three-year history of flat absolute earnings. On that basis PEG is approximately 2.7–3.0 on adjusted EPS growth, or higher on operating growth alone. A 3.1–3.2% FCF yield with no dividend requires substantial multiple persistence to deliver an equity-appropriate return.

There is no identified mispricing at this price. Baron Capital's Q2 letter described adding at "a valuation the stock had not seen in many years"; the May monthly average repurchase price was $1,139.92, roughly 24.1x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS. That gap has closed: the stock is up 25.0% from the May average and 38.9% from the May 13 close, and now trades at 30.1x. Baron's "mid-teens or better annual earnings growth" expectation exceeds both the FY2022–FY2025 adjusted EPS CAGR of ~2.9% and current guidance; it should be read as a long-horizon argument, not a forecast.

Analyst targets do not provide support. The mean of $1,468.91 is 3.1% above spot and the median $1,458.00 is 2.3% above; the low is $1,200 and the high $1,600. On a stock at 30x forward with single-digit revision velocity, consensus targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. The recommendation mix has degraded over three months: strong buys 2→1, buys 5→4, and a strong sell appearing where there was none.

Valuation score 4.2. Quality and a real 10% EPS growth guide argue against a lower mark; the absence of any margin of safety, the 3% FCF yield, the PEG near 3, and the demonstrated fragility of the multiple argue against a higher one.

## Competitive Landscape

MTD's defensible advantages are evidenced rather than asserted. Service at ~25% of revenue and 54.4% gross margin, growing faster than product for three consecutive years, is a recurring, locally delivered stream that a low-cost competitor cannot replicate without building a field force in 40 countries. R&D of $574M over three years (~5% of sales) with ~1,600 engineers is scale advantage against regional and product-specialized rivals. The 1%-maximum customer concentration means no competitor can dislodge MTD through a single account. A tiered brand structure — Mettler-Toledo for premium and regulated applications, Ohaus for value and education through indirect channels — allows defense against low-cost entrants without cannibalizing the premium line. Bolt-on M&A that buys out distributors converts channels to direct, capturing margin and the service relationship. The proxy's indexed TSR shows MTD beating its peer index in all five years 2021–2025 (122.33 versus 105.51 in 2025), though it remained ~18% below its own 2021 indexed peak at year-end 2025, a relative outperformance within a de-rating sector.

Three threats deserve weight. Chinese domestic-purchasing requirements favoring local suppliers attack a segment producing 25–29% of profit (the 10-K risk factor says ~29%; summing the FY2025 segment table gives 25.1% — the denominator reconciling the two is not identified, and neither figure should be quoted as precise). The 10-K frames AI two-sidedly: compliance burden from embedding AI features, and price pressure from competitors using lower-cost AI systems if MTD fails to adopt. And the filing notes that "larger companies are increasingly targeting life sciences and instruments," which raises the price of the bolt-on acquisitions the strategy depends on. Channel concentration also exists where end-customer concentration does not: the 10-K notes reliance on one or a few key distributors for certain products or markets.

The trajectory read is stable with a soft edge: leadership positions and the service annuity are intact, but Americas organic growth of +1% and Western European segment profit −6% do not evidence share gains in developed markets. Competitive position 6.8.

Independent sector and peer commentary could not be obtained during this review; the competitive assessment rests on MTD's filings plus one external fund letter and should be corroborated.

## Risk Assessment

**China triple exposure.** 16% of sales, 25–29% of segment profit, 29% of production. China is simultaneously market, manufacturing base and disproportionate profit contributor. Named risks include domestic purchasing preferences, weak consumption tied to property, government approval required to repatriate earnings ($19.2M of cash in Chinese subsidiaries at year-end), and explicitly nationalization or confiscation. A December 2025 agreement with the government of Xuhui provides ~$31M of proceeds through 2030 ($18M against future PP&E, $13M against future operating expenses); $12.4M received cumulatively including $6.2M in H1 2026. Management excludes these from adjusted FCF, but the operating-expense offsets flow through reported opex and modestly flatter margins.

**No visibility.** One-to-two-month backlog with Q4 at 27–30% of sales. Guidance for +10–11% adjusted EPS growth is back-half loaded after a first half in which adjusted operating margin declined and adjusted FCF fell 10.3%. This is the risk that converted into a 14.8% single-session decline on May 8 and a 22.5% four-session drawdown.

**Single points of failure.** The 10-K states many products are developed and manufactured at single locations with limited alternate facilities; a Q4 2023 European logistics disruption distorted two years of growth comparisons. Blue Ocean ERP has >95% of users on one global instance, and the company concedes greater consequences from any disruption.

**Policy.** Item 1A names Most Favored Nation drug pricing and U.S. research-funding cuts. Tariffs have changed regime four times in eighteen months; the 10-K notes discussion of further tariffs and potentially ending the USMCA exemption for Mexican imports, and cautions that price-based mitigation "may also reduce our competitiveness and customer demand."

**Balance-sheet structure.** Negative-to-minimal book equity leaves no cushion, and Item 1A ties buyback capacity to the ability to repatriate international cash flows, the mechanism supplying most per-share growth. Debt agreements permit substantial additional borrowing, so leverage discipline is voluntary. International pension PBO of ~$1.0B carries a 1.8% weighted-average discount rate; a 1% discount-rate change moves after-tax expense ~$8.9M. Two non-U.S. pension plans have lent $39.6M (CHF 38M) to a wholly owned subsidiary at SARON + 87.5bp, renewed annually — disclosed and legitimate, but it means plan assets are partly an intra-group receivable.

**Currency.** More CHF expenses than CHF revenues; more EUR revenues than EUR expenses. A 1% CHF strengthening versus EUR reduces pretax earnings $2.8–3.1M; a 1% CNY weakening versus USD, $2.2–2.6M. Each is 0.2–0.3% of pretax income individually, material in aggregate against a 10% EPS growth plan. FX forwards notional was $1.1B at June 30.

**What is not a risk.** Q2 2026 Legal Proceedings reads "None," and the 10-K describes only ordinary-course matters plus legacy environmental remediation. There is no litigation overhang. Short interest is 4.5% of float at 3.7 days to cover — no crowding, no squeeze dynamic.

Risk factors score 5.3. Most of these are disclosed and partly priced; the combination of a 30x multiple, no backlog, and Q4-weighted guidance is the specific asymmetry that is not.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 25.7% sits below one-year realized volatility of 32.8% (spread −7.1%) and in the 14th percentile of the current chain, having collapsed from 37.1% on July 19 as the stock rose 8.8%. Term structure is flat (25.7% near, 26.9% at six months). Put/call open-interest ratio is 0.10 and skew is call-heavy at −4.0%, with the six-month tenor the only one showing a −2.4% reading. The picture is post-event complacency: uncertainty was resolved by the July 30 print, positioning is tilted to calls, and volatility is priced below what the stock has delivered. For a holder, cheap IV at the 14th percentile makes downside protection inexpensive relative to realized volatility; that is the more useful takeaway than any directional read. Options do not challenge the fundamental view, and hedging demand is absent into a back-half-loaded guidance year on a name with two months of visibility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,700, +19.3%).** China's +9% organic momentum proves durable, Americas recovers from +1% toward mid-single digits as research-funding pressure abates, and the lower July 23 Section 301 tariff regime combines with retained 2025 price increases to deliver 50–100bp of gross-margin expansion. Absolute earnings growth resumes for the first time since 2022. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $54 against consensus $51.69, and the market pays 31–32x for a re-accelerating franchise with 25% recurring service revenue and 24.6% NOPAT-on-assets. Buybacks of $825–875M annually continue at ~3% of shares. Cheap IV and a 2.4% discount to the analyst median are the entry conditions this case would need.

**Bear case ($1,175, −17.6%).** Americas organic growth stays near +1% while China gives back the 2026 recovery on domestic-purchasing preferences, and the tariff regime reverses for a fifth time. H2 fails to deliver the back-half-loaded plan, and with one-to-two months of visibility the miss arrives without warning in the quarter carrying 27–30% of sales. The 200bp tax step-up bites at constant pretax profit. Absolute earnings stay flat for a fourth year, exposing per-share growth as buyback arithmetic. The multiple compresses to the ~24x last seen in May 2026 on FY2027 EPS of ~$49. The May 8 session, down 14.8% in a day on a single soft organic print, is the precedent.

**Base case ($1,470, +3.2%).** Guidance is delivered at or slightly above the $47.33 midpoint, helped by easier comparisons and lower tariffs; FY2027 lands near consensus $51.69; the multiple holds around 28.5x. Twelve-month total return of roughly 3% with no dividend.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately 0.2% — 0.50(3.2) + 0.20(19.3) + 0.30(−17.6) — against a required equity return of 7–8%. That gap, not any deterioration in business quality, drives the rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $1,470 (+3.2%), bull $1,700 (+19.3%), bear $1,175 (−17.6%), twelve-month horizon.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into current strength; the asymmetry is unfavorable now. Holders unwilling to exit entirely should reduce to a below-benchmark weight ahead of the Q3 report (expected late October 2026, guided to $12.00–$12.15 adjusted EPS). Given near-term IV at the 14th percentile and 7.1 points below realized volatility, hedge the residual position rather than hold it naked into a fourth quarter that carries 27–30% of annual sales with two months of backlog visibility.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) Price of $1,250 or below without fundamental deterioration, which restores roughly 26x guided FY2026 and 24x FY2027 consensus, a level at which the quality of the franchise is adequately compensated; ACCUMULATE at $1,150–1,200. (2) Two consecutive quarters in which Americas organic local-currency growth exceeds +4%, confirming that the 42%-of-revenue region is no longer the constraint. (3) Adjusted operating margin expanding year over year on a normalized basis excluding tariff items; H1 2026 was 27.7% versus 27.9%, and a return to 28.5%+ on clean comparisons would evidence that the lower Section 301 rates and retained pricing are converting to durable margin.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $47.15, indicating the back-half plan has failed with no order book to diagnose it. (2) China local-currency organic growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters, which would reopen the 2023–2024 pattern against 25–29% of segment profit. (3) Any disclosure restricting repatriation of international cash flows, which the 10-K links directly to buyback capacity, the mechanism supplying roughly 3pp of the guided 10–11% EPS growth.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural decision point is the Q3 2026 report in late October, which tests the back-half-loaded plan and the durability of both China momentum and the tariff margin benefit. A secondary checkpoint is the FY2026 10-K in early February 2027, for the realized effective tax rate against the 19.0% projection and for any change to the risk-factor language on U.S. research funding and drug pricing.

## Conclusion

Mettler-Toledo is a better business than its 30x multiple implies risk in: 25% of revenue recurring at 54.4% gross margin, no customer above 1% of sales, 16x interest coverage, 97% free-cash conversion, and a compensation plan that measures sales at budgeted currency rates and produced NEO average actually-paid of $309,455 in the weak 2023 TSR year. Management excluded a $24.6M tariff windfall from adjusted EPS when it could have flattered the beat, and has repurchased $412.5M of stock in the first half at an average of $1,267.85, below today's price.

None of that resolves the arithmetic. The stock has recovered 38.9% from its May low while normalized Q2 pretax margin was flat at 25.1% versus 25.3%, H1 adjusted operating margin declined to 27.7% from 27.9%, and H1 adjusted free cash flow fell 10.3%. Absolute net income has not grown since 2022; per-share growth has come from a 9.0% reduction in the share count. Forward guidance of +10–11% adjusted EPS growth carries roughly 3pp of buyback accretion and must absorb a 200bp tax-rate step-up, loaded into a second half of a business that can see two months ahead.
