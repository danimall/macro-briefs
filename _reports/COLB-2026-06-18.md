**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Columbia Banking System is a ~$66B-asset western-US super-regional bank whose investment case turns almost entirely on the integration of Pacific Premier Bancorp, an all-stock deal (~$2.4B, ~87.6M shares) that closed August 31, 2025. The first full combined quarter (Q1 2026) produced net income of $192M and diluted EPS of $0.66, with NIM of 3.96% tax-equivalent and ROTCE of 13.88%. Reported results remain distorted by $24M of merger/restructuring expense and $41M of quarterly intangible amortization. Management guides that all cost synergies will be realized by June 30, 2026, with full P&L effect from Q3 2026, which makes the second half of 2026 the proof window for the thesis.

The fundamentals underneath the noise are solid for a regional bank mid-integration. The funding mix is improving (cost of interest-bearing liabilities down 56bps YoY to 2.24%, brokered deposits cut from $2.4B to $1.6B, 33% non-interest-bearing deposits), fee income is expanding on the acquired trust/HOA/custodial platform (+26% YoY), and the balance sheet carries an unusually honest mark: essentially all securities are AFS, so the $410M of gross unrealized losses sit in AOCI rather than being parked in a hidden HTM book. Liquidity covers 129% of estimated uninsured deposits. CET1 of 11.58% is comfortably above well-capitalized.

We rate COLB a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias. The clean earnings power emerging in H2 2026 is a genuine catalyst, and the stock has appreciated ~37% over the past year on improving fundamentals. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: the shares trade at roughly 1.6x tangible book and a slight premium to the regional-bank peer median P/E, consensus EPS revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative over the past 90 days, and two specific overhangs (a doubling of agriculture non-accruals attributed to a single relationship, and integration execution risk) keep us from upgrading until Q3 confirms synergy capture and credit stability.

## Company Overview

Columbia Banking System is the holding company for Columbia Bank, a relationship-focused commercial bank headquartered in Tacoma, Washington, operating across nine western states (Arizona, California, Colorado, Idaho, Nevada, Oregon, Texas, Utah, Washington). The current franchise is itself the product of the 2023 Umpqua Holdings merger, and the August 2025 Pacific Premier acquisition is the second transformative deal in three years.

The Pacific Premier transaction lifted total assets to $66.0B from ~$51.5B (Q1 2025 average), raised average earning assets from $47.7B to $60.8B, deepened Southern California presence (now 32% of the CRE book), and added Pacific Premier's custodial-trust and HOA-banking businesses, which drove financial-services and trust revenue up roughly 200% YoY. The business model spans retail and commercial banking, SBA lending, institutional/corporate banking, equipment leasing through subsidiary FinPac, and wealth management. Management frames its strategy as becoming the "Business Bank of Choice," deliberately remixing the balance sheet away from low-yield transactional loans and high-cost wholesale funding toward relationship-driven loans, core deposits, and recurring fee income. The company reports as one segment.

## Financial Analysis

Standard cross-sector screens (Altman Z, debt/equity, "net margin") are not meaningful for a depository institution, because high leverage is structural when deposits are liabilities. The relevant lenses are net interest margin, capital ratios, credit quality, and returns on tangible equity.

**Earnings.** Q1 2026 net income was $192M, EPS $0.66 diluted, up sharply from $87M / $0.41 a year ago (the prior-year quarter included a $55M legal settlement accrual that depressed the comparison), but down from $215M / $0.72 in Q4 2025. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.72 beat consensus by $0.03. The QoQ decline is largely mechanical: Q4 2025 benefited from a one-time $12M time-deposit premium amortization (8bps of NIM) and a $5M accelerated loan repayment (3bps) that did not recur.

**Margin and funding.** Net interest income was $594M (vs. $425M a year ago), with NIM of 3.96% tax-equivalent, up from 3.60% in Q1 2025 but down from 4.06% in Q4 2025. The core story is funding-mix improvement, with non-interest-bearing deposits at a healthy 33% of total. The balance sheet is modestly liability-sensitive, so the path of the Fed (held at 3.75%, projected stable through 2026) and deposit pricing are the key NIM swing factors.

**Returns.** ROA 1.18%, ROE 10.0%, ROTCE 13.88% — solid mid-integration, though each is down from Q4 (1.27% / 10.92% / 15.24%). The H2 2026 synergy realization is the lever expected to move these back up as merger expense and amortization roll off.

**Capital and the AOCI story.** CET1 was 11.58%, total risk-based 13.38%, both comfortably above well-capitalized, with the modest QoQ dip driven by buybacks. The cleanest distinction versus peers: COLB keeps essentially no securities in HTM ($18M), so the gross unrealized losses on the AFS book are fully reflected in AOCI of $(291)M and in tangible book value, rather than hidden in an HTM bucket. Tangible common equity / tangible assets is 8.63%, and TBV is ~$5.5B against an ~$8.8B market cap, putting the stock at roughly 1.6x TBV. EVE analysis shows the fair value of equity above book once core-deposit-intangible value is recognized.

**Liquidity.** Total available liquidity of $27.1B equals 41% of assets and covers 129% of estimated uninsured deposits (uninsured deposits are 39% of total). This directly addresses the 2023 deposit-flight concern that still shadows the regional cohort.

**Capital allocation.** The $0.37 quarterly dividend now carries a 56% payout ratio, down from an unsustainable 88% in Q1 2025. A new $700M buyback was authorized in October 2025; $200M (6.5M shares at ~$30.74) was repurchased in Q1 2026, with $400M remaining through November 2026. Buying back stock at ~1.6x TBV while integrating and while credit is normalizing supports EPS but consumes the capital buffer at a moment when prudence might argue for retention.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is primarily inorganic and synergy-driven rather than organic loan growth. Loans were $47.7B, slightly down on intentional runoff of low-yield transactional balances, while deposits were $53.5B (89% loan/deposit ratio). The earnings inflection is expected to come from two sources: (1) full realization of Pacific Premier cost synergies by mid-2026, lifting clean PPNR above what current GAAP shows, and (2) continued fee-income expansion from the acquired trust/HOA/custodial platform.

Consensus current-year EPS sits at $3.06, rising to $3.38 next year (~10% growth), implying clean earnings power well above the GAAP print as merger drag fades. The earnings-surprise record is constructive — four straight beats (3.0%, 12.5%, 20.7%, 12.5% over the last four quarters) — which lends credibility to forward estimates. The organic effort is the April 2026 launch of a Franchise Banking Team (initial focus on restaurant lending), leveraging the larger platform.

The cautionary signal is estimate-revision direction. Over the past 90 days, current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have been revised down modestly (-0.9% and -0.8%), while full-year revisions are barely positive (+0.6%). This is the opposite of the rapid upward revision velocity that signals an underappreciated inflection. The synergy thesis is real, but the Street is not yet revising it up; consensus appears to want Q3 proof before re-rating estimates higher. That tempers the Growth Prospects score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.05–$30.55, COLB trades at ~11.9x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $2.30 trailing; consensus current year implies ~9.9x on clean forward power) and ~1.6x tangible book. Against the regional-bank peer set, the picture is roughly fair-to-slightly-rich:

| Metric | COLB | Peer Median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E | ~11.9 | 12.7 |
| Dividend yield | 4.8% | 2.5% |
| Price vs 52w high | -8% | varies |

On headline P/E, COLB looks in line to slightly cheap versus the 12.7 peer median, and the 4.8% dividend yield is well above the 2.5% peer median, providing meaningful absolute yield support with a now-sustainable payout. Cheaper-looking peers exist (WAL at 9.6x, SSB at 10.5x), so COLB is not the cheapest name in the group.

The case for upside rests on the gap between GAAP and clean earnings: if H2 2026 delivers the guided synergies and amortization rolls off, normalized EPS toward $3.30–$3.40 at ~11–12x would support a low-$40s valuation. The case against treating this as deeply mispriced: the stock already trades above the cohort average where many peers sit closer to book, estimate revisions are flat-to-down, and a slight premium-to-peer P/E does not scream value-trap recovery either. The analyst consensus target of $32.08 (range $29–$36) sits only ~5% above the current price, consistent with a balanced rather than asymmetric setup.

We score valuation slightly below neutral. Cheapness alone — particularly on a "cheap" multiple without rising estimates — has historically been a poor predictor of forward returns, and COLB's multiple is not cheap enough relative to peers, nor are revisions rising, to justify a high valuation score. The yield support and the synergy-driven earnings step-up keep it from being lower.

## Competitive Landscape

Columbia is now a ~$66B-asset super-regional, large enough to matter across the western US but competing against far larger national banks with bigger technology budgets, plus credit unions, fintechs, and — flagged explicitly in the 10-K — stablecoin issuers competing for deposits. That stablecoin disclosure is an unusually forward-looking risk acknowledgment relevant to the durability of the low-cost deposit base.

The deposit franchise is the competitive asset: 33% non-interest-bearing, with the newly acquired HOA and custodial-trust niches bringing sticky, low-cost, fee-generating balances. The strategic remix toward relationship banking and away from wholesale funding is improving the funding cost structure in a measurable way (56bps of YoY improvement). Office CRE exposure, the sector's pressure point, is small and suburban-skewed ($3.6B, ~7% of loans, office non-accruals 0.11% of CRE), which is favorable relative to urban-CBD-heavy peers.

The trajectory is improving on funding and fee mix, which supports an above-neutral competitive score, but the franchise still operates in a single geographic region with concentration risk and faces structurally larger competitors on technology spend. The competitive position is solid and improving at the margin rather than a wide moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration risk (the dominant near-term risk).** The 10-K dedicates a risk-factor section to Pacific Premier integration — potential failure to realize cost savings, key-employee attrition, customer/deposit runoff. Goodwill from the deal is $453M (total goodwill $1.5B); a stumble pressures earnings and raises goodwill-impairment risk. Systems conversion is complete and nine branches consolidated, which de-risks the operational piece, but synergy capture is unproven in reported numbers until Q3 2026.

**CRE concentration.** 76% of gross loans are real-estate-secured; CRE is $27.6B (~58% of loans), of which multifamily is $11.4B (24% of the book) and office $3.6B (~7%). Office quality looks contained, but this is the structural exposure regulators scrutinize.

**Credit normalization.** NPAs rose to 0.40% of assets (from 0.30%) and NPLs to 0.55% of loans QoQ, driven largely by a single agricultural relationship (agriculture non-accruals jumped from $22M to $49M). Management frames this as normalization. The quieter signal worth watching is collateral-dependent loans rising from $177M to $248M QoQ. Reserve coverage remains healthy (ACL 1.00% of loans, 183% of NPLs), and net charge-offs are 0.30% annualized, but the "single relationship" narrative needs to hold in coming quarters. FinPac leasing continues to run ~$14M/quarter of charge-offs.

**Litigation.** The iCap Ponzi-scheme aiding-and-abetting suit (filed September 2025) references ~$90M of investor claims with broader pleadings citing ~$290M; the company intends to defend vigorously. The 2023 MOVEit data breach (~429,000 customers) is consolidated in a Massachusetts MDL. Neither is currently quantified as a material accrual, but both are open tails.

**Capital buffer.** Buying back stock at 1.6x TBV mid-integration drew CET1 down, still comfortable, but it reduces the margin for error during a period when integration and credit uncertainty argue for retaining capital.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.2% sits modestly below 1-year historical vol of 30.4% (spread -2.2%, IV percentile 8%), indicating cheap-to-fair near-term optionality and a complacent, low-fear setup. The put/call volume ratio of 0.29 and OI ratio of 0.35 are call-skewed, and near-term skew is essentially balanced (-0.8%). The term structure is in contango (28.2% near vs. 34.0% at six months), with the six-month tenor showing a notable -8.8% put/call skew, consistent with hedging demand around the H2 2026 integration/synergy proof window rather than near-term distress. The reported jump in ATM IV to 388.9% in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Net read: the options market is not pricing imminent stress, which aligns with a HOLD but does not provide an independent bullish tell.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$40, +33%).** H2 2026 delivers the guided synergies on schedule. Merger expense and the intangible amortization roll off, lifting clean EPS toward $3.30–$3.40 and ROTCE back above 15%. The agriculture non-accrual proves to be the single isolated relationship management claims, credit stabilizes, and the improving funding mix holds NIM near 4%. The market re-rates the cleaner earnings stream toward ~12x on normalized power, and the yield plus active buyback compound the return. Honest TBV (no hidden HTM losses) and uninsured-deposit liquidity coverage support a premium to the cohort.

**Bear case (~$24, -20%).** Integration stumbles — synergy capture slips past Q3 2026, deposit/customer runoff exceeds plan, or goodwill faces impairment pressure. The credit "blip" broadens beyond the single agricultural relationship as collateral-dependent loans keep rising, forcing higher provisions. CRE/multifamily concentration draws regulatory or market scrutiny. Buybacks erode the capital cushion just as it is needed. Estimate revisions turn decisively down, and the stock compresses toward book value with the yield offering only partial support.

**Base case (~$33, +9%).** Synergies largely land but the re-rating is gradual; clean EPS approaches consensus this year and next; credit normalizes without broad deterioration; the stock tracks the analyst consensus target with the dividend providing the bulk of total return. This is the most likely outcome given flat revisions and a multiple already near peer levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $33.** ~10.8x on clean forward EPS of ~$3.06, plus the dividend. This sits between the bear ($24) and bull ($40) scenarios and aligns with the consensus mean.

**Bull case target: $40** (+33% from current). ~12x on normalized EPS of ~$3.35 once synergies fully land and amortization rolls off.

**Bear case target: $24** (-20% from current). ~1.1x TBV / ~7.8x clean EPS on integration stumble plus broadening credit deterioration.

**Timeframe: 9–12 months,** anchored to the Q2 2026 report (synergy-realization-complete milestone, ~late July) and the Q3 2026 report (full synergy P&L effect, ~late October) as the decisive catalysts.

**Upside/downside from current:** +9% to base, +33% to bull, -20% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results show clean EPS visibly stepping above $0.80/quarter as merger drag rolls off, with ROTCE recovering above 15%.
- Agriculture non-accrual and collateral-dependent-loan trends stabilize or reverse, confirming the "single relationship" framing.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days) as the Street catches up to synergy capture.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPLs continue rising past 0.70% of loans with new credits beyond the agricultural relationship, forcing materially higher provisions.
- Synergy realization slips past Q3 2026 or management signals goodwill-impairment risk.
- CET1 falls below ~11% on continued buybacks without earnings recovery.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings (late July), with the definitive read at Q3 2026 (late October).

## Conclusion

Columbia Banking System is a well-capitalized western-US super-regional in the late innings of integrating a transformative acquisition. The fundamentals are improving — funding costs are falling, fee income is expanding on the acquired trust/HOA platform, TBV is honest, and liquidity is strong — and the cleanest part of the thesis is that current GAAP understates the earnings power that should surface in H2 2026 as merger costs and intangible amortization roll off. The four-quarter beat streak and the well-covered dividend add support.

The reasons to wait rather than chase: the stock already trades at ~1.6x TBV and a slight premium to the peer-median P/E, estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than rising, the consensus target offers only ~5% upside, and two specific risks — credit normalization (the agriculture non-accrual doubling and rising collateral-dependent loans) and integration execution — keep the setup balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate COLB a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias. The right action is to own it for the H2 2026 synergy inflection if already positioned, and to upgrade to a more aggressive stance only when Q2/Q3 results convert the synergy guidance into reported clean earnings and confirm that credit is stabilizing.
