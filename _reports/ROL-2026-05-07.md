## Executive Summary

Rollins is one of the world's largest pest control companies, operating a high-quality, asset-light services business with ~75% recurring revenue, $650M in annual free cash flow, and a demonstrated ability to grow at 9-11% through a combination of organic expansion (6-8%) and tuck-in acquisitions (2-4%). The company's route density advantages, proprietary technology platform, and 20+ brand portfolio create durable competitive advantages in a fragmented market with secular demand tailwinds.

The investment challenge is valuation. At ~50x trailing earnings and ~43x FY2026 consensus of $1.25, Rollins is priced at a 63% premium to its environmental/facilities services peer median P/E of 30.4x. This premium has been persistent — Rollins has traded at elevated multiples for years due to its quality characteristics — but the stock has declined 18% from its February 2026 high of $66.14 amid a confluence of headwinds: the FTC non-compete enforcement action (April 2026), slight negative estimate revisions (-2.7% over 90 days for FY2026), a Q4 2025 earnings miss, and broader market weakness. The current setup offers a quality business with visible growth at a still-premium price, warranting a HOLD rather than accumulation at these levels.

The key debate is whether the FTC non-compete ruling and management's admission that "we lose way too many customers every year" represent structurally deteriorating unit economics, or temporary headwinds being actively addressed through leadership investment and cultural retention strategies. The May 14 investor day should provide clarity.

## Company Overview

Rollins provides essential pest, termite, and wildlife control services to over 2 million residential and commercial customers across approximately 70 countries from 800+ locations. Revenue splits approximately 45% residential, 33% commercial, and 21% termite/ancillary services. The business operates under a portfolio of 20+ brands, with Orkin as the flagship, supplemented by HomeTeam Pest Defense, Fox Pest Control, Saela, Clark Pest Control, Critter Control, and others.

The company has completed 94 acquisitions over the past three years, funded by exceptional free cash flow generation ($650M in 2025 on only $28M of capex). The Rollins family retains approximately 38% ownership, providing governance stability but limited minority shareholder influence. CEO Jerry Gahlhoff (promoted January 2025) is the first non-family CEO in the company's history.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $3.76B in 2025, up 11.0% YoY (6.9% organic + 4.1% acquisitions). Growth has been remarkably consistent: 14.0% (2023), 10.3% (2024), 11.0% (2025). Q1 2026 continued at 10.2% total (6.6% organic, accelerating to 8%+ in March). Management guides 7-8% organic + 2-3% M&A for FY2026, implying ~$4.1B revenue.

**Margins:** Gross margin at 52.8% in 2025, stable YoY. Adjusted operating margin improved 10bps to 20.0%. EBITDA margin: 22.7% ($855M). Q1 2026 showed temporary compression (50.8% gross margin) due to insurance claims timing, pre-season staffing costs, and absence of vehicle gain benefits. Management characterized this as transitory.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $678M (+11.6% YoY). FCF of $650M (17.3% FCF margin, 123% conversion of net income). Capex of only $28M (0.7% of revenue) reflects the extremely asset-light nature of services businesses. This cash generative profile is the primary quality characteristic supporting the premium valuation.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of ~$524M against $855M EBITDA = 0.6x leverage. Total debt of $624M ($500M in 10-year 5.25% Senior Notes + $114M commercial paper). Goodwill of $1.37B has grown 62% in three years, reflecting aggressive acquisition activity. While the 0.6x current ratio signals negative working capital, this is structural for subscription businesses collecting advance payments.

**Capital Return:** Dividends of $328M ($0.68/share, +9.7% YoY, 1.3% yield). $200M share repurchase concurrent with the Rollins family secondary in November 2025. Acquisitions: $310M across 26 transactions. Total capital deployment of ~$838M against $650M FCF, with incremental debt funding the gap.

## Growth Analysis

Rollins' growth algorithm is straightforward: 7-8% organic growth (price + volume) plus 2-4% from acquisitions. The organic component benefits from:

1. **Non-cyclical demand**: Pest control is an essential service with low discretionary elasticity. Even during recessions, customers maintain pest prevention contracts.
2. **Pricing power**: The company has demonstrated consistent ability to pass through inflation (7-8% revenue growth includes meaningful price contribution).
3. **Cross-selling**: Multiple service lines (pest, termite, wildlife, crawlspace) create upsell opportunities within the existing customer base.
4. **International expansion**: Currently ~7% of revenue, representing a long runway in a global pest control market growing at mid-single digits.

The acquisition component leverages the industry's extreme fragmentation. Rollins can acquire local operators, apply its technology platform and operational best practices, and extract route density synergies. With 94 deals in three years and a "robust pipeline," this growth pillar appears sustainable.

**Concerns on growth quality:** Estimate revisions are modestly negative: FY2026 EPS consensus has declined 2.7% over 90 days ($1.28 → $1.25). FY2027 declined 1.9% ($1.42 → $1.39). While not alarming, this is inconsistent with a growth stock at 43-50x earnings. The recent Q4 2025 miss (-7.3% EPS surprise) and Q1 2026 meeting consensus only in-line (0.8% beat) suggest the business is performing adequately but without the positive surprise engine that typically justifies premium multiples.

Management's admission of excessive customer churn is significant. If Rollins is generating 6-8% organic growth despite losing "way too many customers," improving retention represents a potential catalyst. However, this also implies current growth is being achieved through heavy customer acquisition spending, which is less capital-efficient than retention-driven growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $54.19, Rollins trades at:
- 50x trailing EPS ($1.09)
- 43x FY2026E EPS ($1.25)
- 39x FY2027E EPS ($1.39)
- 31x EV/EBITDA (on $855M)
- 40x FCF ($650M)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 30.4x (Republic Services), with Waste Management at 33.3x and Veralto at 23.1x. Rollins' 43x forward P/E represents a 41% premium to the closest comparable (WM). This premium is partially justified by Rollins' higher growth rate (10%+ vs. WM's 5-7%) and superior FCF conversion, but the magnitude is difficult to sustain as estimates decline.

**Valuation math on growth:** At $1.25 FY2026E growing at 10-12% annually (reflecting both organic + M&A contributions), a PEG-based framework gives: PEG of 3.6x (43 P/E ÷ 12% growth). For context, a PEG of 2.0x at 12% growth would imply 24x P/E or ~$30/share. The market is assigning a massive quality premium for the predictability and durability of Rollins' earnings stream.

**Analyst targets** (consensus mean $64.25, median $67.00) imply 19-24% upside. The range is $50 to $72. At 18% below the 52-week high, the stock has partially de-rated but remains expensive by any conventional metric.

**Where is upside?** The bull case requires: (1) retention improvements unlocking 100-200bps of additional organic growth, (2) continued M&A execution at accretive multiples, and (3) stable-to-expanding margins. At $1.39 FY2027 growing to $1.55-$1.60 by FY2028, a 40x multiple would imply $62-64. This requires flawless execution on all vectors simultaneously.

## Competitive Landscape

Rollins operates in a fragmented industry where scale provides meaningful advantages in route density, technology investment, recruiting, and M&A execution. The two largest operators (Rollins and Rentokil) collectively control less than 20% of the global market, leaving substantial consolidation opportunity.

**Competitive strengths:**
- Route density creates cost advantages that compound as more customers are added in existing geographies
- BOSS technology platform enables scheduling optimization and data-driven service delivery
- 20+ brand portfolio allows market segmentation (premium Orkin vs. value-oriented brands)
- Training infrastructure (27,000 sq ft Learning Center) creates service quality consistency
- Financial flexibility from FCF generation enables sustained acquisition activity

**Competitive risks:**
- FTC non-compete ruling removes a key retention tool for 18,000+ technicians who build direct customer relationships
- Rentokil's acquisition of Terminix created a larger competitor with potentially superior scale in certain geographies
- Low barriers to entry allow continuous formation of new competitors, particularly from departing technicians (now unrestrained)
- Technology-enabled startups could disrupt customer acquisition economics

The competitive position is strong but has modestly weakened since the FTC action. The 5-year TSR underperformance versus peers (163 vs. 190) may partially reflect this dynamic.

## Risk Assessment

**FTC Non-Compete Enforcement (Moderate-High):** The April 2026 consent agreement prevents Rollins from enforcing non-competes for non-senior employees. In a business where technicians are the customer relationship, this could increase turnover and enable competitors to recruit experienced staff. The long-term impact on retention costs and competitive dynamics is uncertain but directionally negative.

**Estimate Revision Momentum (Moderate):** The slow bleed in consensus (rather than a sharp cut) creates multiple compression risk at 43x forward P/E. The earnings surprise track record (2 inline, 2 misses in recent quarters) doesn't support expectations of upside surprises.

**Family Governance and Overhang:** The Rollins family's November 2025 sale of ~20M shares at $57.50 (above current price) creates lingering overhang. With ~38% ownership, additional sales could create periodic selling pressure. Public shareholders have limited governance influence.

**Insurance Claims Volatility:** Management flagged insurance/claims as creating unpredictable quarterly margin swings. With 21,000+ employees driving daily routes, auto liability exposure is structural and can create negative earnings surprises.

**Acquisition Integration:** $1.37B in goodwill accumulated rapidly over 3 years. While the company's track record is solid, the pace has accelerated (26 deals in 2025, $310M deployed). Integration execution is key, and impairment risk rises with the absolute size of the goodwill balance.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing above historical norms: ATM IV of 28.9% versus 23.0% realized volatility, representing a 5.9pp rich spread. The put/call volume ratio of 10.89 is elevated (though put/call OI ratio is 0.52, suggesting this reflects recent positioning rather than structural bearishness). The flat term structure indicates uncertainty is evenly distributed across timeframes rather than concentrated around a specific event. This elevated implied volatility likely reflects residual uncertainty from the FTC action and the upcoming May 14 investor day.

Short interest at 3.1% of float is unremarkable and doesn't suggest crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($62-67, 15-24% upside):**
- Retention initiatives (to be detailed at May 14 investor day) unlock 100-200bps of incremental organic growth
- Acquisition pipeline continues at current pace, adding 2-4% growth annually at accretive multiples
- FTC non-compete impact proves manageable as Rollins shifts to compensation/culture-based retention
- Margin expansion resumes as insurance headwinds normalize and scale benefits compound
- Valuation: 43-45x × $1.45-1.50 FY2027E = $62-67

**Bear Case ($45-48, 12-17% downside):**
- FTC ruling causes measurable technician churn increase (5-10%), elevating replacement costs and disrupting customer relationships
- Estimate revisions continue negative trajectory, with FY2026 EPS declining toward $1.15-1.20
- Consumer weakness from stagflation environment causes some residential customers to cancel non-essential pest services
- Market assigns lower multiple (35-38x) as growth quality deteriorates
- Valuation: 37-40x × $1.15-1.20 = $43-48

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price decline to $45-48 range (35-38x FY2026E), providing meaningful valuation support
2. Estimate revisions reverse to positive (specifically, FY2026 EPS consensus rising above $1.30) indicating growth reacceleration
3. May 14 investor day provides quantified retention improvement targets with credible execution framework

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS consensus declines below $1.18 (indicating fundamental deceleration beyond current guidance)
2. Organic growth falls below 5% for two consecutive quarters (suggesting demand softening or competitive share loss)
3. Rollins family conducts another secondary offering of >15M shares within 12 months (signaling continued monetization)

**Reassessment timeline:** May 14, 2026 investor day (near-term catalyst for retention/growth thesis clarity). Q2 2026 earnings (July 2026) for first full-quarter read on post-FTC consent order operational dynamics. Re-evaluate after both events.

## Conclusion

Rollins is an exceptional business: highly recurring, asset-light, growing above GDP, and generating outsized free cash flow. These characteristics command a premium valuation, and the company has historically sustained elevated multiples. However, the current setup offers insufficient margin of safety: estimates are declining modestly, the FTC action introduces genuine competitive uncertainty, the stock trades at 43x forward earnings (41% above peer median), and the earnings surprise record has deteriorated.

The stock is 18% below its 52-week high, but this is insufficient to make the valuation attractive given the headwinds. A further decline to the $45-48 range would create a more compelling entry point where the quality premium is adequately discounted for current risks. At $54, the risk/reward is balanced: genuine upside exists if retention initiatives succeed and acquisitions continue accretively, but downside of 12-17% is plausible on continued estimate softness and multiple compression.

HOLD with a growth thesis intact but insufficiently discounted in price. The May 14 investor day and Q2 earnings are the next reassessment points.
