**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Affiliated Managers Group is a holding company that takes minority equity stakes in independent, partner-owned investment boutiques ("Affiliates") and collects a contractual share of their economics. As of Q1 2026 it held interests in dozens of Affiliates managing roughly $882 billion in AUM, up 24% year over year. The economic engine is the structured partnership interest: a fixed share of Affiliate revenue at the legacy consolidated firms, or a share of revenue-less-expenses at the newer alternatives Affiliates held under the equity method. That structure produces an optically strange GAAP profile (a negative consolidated operating margin alongside a high net margin) because the fastest-growing economics flow through equity-method income rather than consolidated revenue. The right lens is management's non-GAAP Economic Net Income and Adjusted EBITDA.

On those measures, Q1 2026 was a record quarter: aggregate fees across all Affiliates of $1,909.9M (+50% YoY), equity-method income of $147.4M (+96%), Economic EPS of $8.23 (+58%), and controlling Adjusted EBITDA of $317.3M (+39%). The core equity story is mechanical: high cash conversion funds roughly $500M of annual buybacks against a $9.3B market cap, shrinking the share count from 29.2M (Q1'25) to ~26.4M (May 2026) and compounding Economic EPS far faster than aggregate fees grow. Treasury stock ($5.07B) now exceeds total stockholders' equity ($3.09B), a marker of how dominant the anti-dilution discipline has become. Management settled a $514.6M convertible in cash rather than issuing shares in late 2025, reinforcing the point.

We rate AMG a HOLD with a positive 12-month bias. Two concrete, falsifiable concerns prevent an upgrade at $355. First, the record Q1 rests heavily on a single "significant" Affiliate whose net income tripled to $305.6M (from $82.0M) on a surge in liquid-alternatives performance fees that are lumpy and subject to high-water marks; management's own Q2 Adjusted EBITDA guide of $290-305M is below the Q1 print, confirming the run-rate steps down rather than extrapolates. Second, the stock has appreciated ~95% over twelve months to within ~8% of the $381 mean analyst target, with a $300 low target implying ~15% downside. The asymmetry that made this attractive in the high-$290s has largely been captured. We would add on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on evidence that the elevated performance-fee contribution is sustainable.

## Company Overview

AMG functions as a strategic capital partner rather than an operating asset manager. Approximately 5,600 employees work at the Affiliates; AMG's own headquarters staff in Jupiter, Florida numbers roughly 250. The model deliberately preserves Affiliate independence (minority stakes, retained majority equity and day-to-day control at the boutique, plus succession, distribution, and capital support), which differentiates AMG from private equity firms, sovereign wealth funds, and insurers competing for the same stakes by absorbing them.

AUM by strategy at 3/31/26:
- Equities (long-only): $297.8B (34%), in persistent net outflows
- Liquid Alternatives: $261.5B (29%), the fastest grower
- Multi-Asset & Fixed Income: $174.7B (20%)
- Private Markets: $148.0B (17%)

Management is explicitly rotating the portfolio away from declining active equities toward alternatives, where fee rates are higher, capital is stickier through long-dated lock-ups, and carried interest provides upside. Recent minority investments in BBH Credit Partners (Brown Brothers Harriman's taxable fixed-income/credit franchise), Garda Capital Partners (liquid-alts relative value), and HighBrook Investors (real-estate private markets), all closed in Q1 2026, exemplify the strategy. AMG is also building a vertically integrated wealth-distribution platform and has launched evergreen funds and an active ETF to push alternatives into the U.S. wealth channel.

## Financial Analysis

The accounting is the single most important thing to understand here, and it is a recurring source of headline noise. Because the high-growth alternatives Affiliates are held under the equity method, their economics do not appear in consolidated revenue; they show up net, one line down, as equity-method income. A standardized screen reads a negative GAAP operating margin (consolidated revenue below consolidated expenses) alongside a high net margin, and analysts who model the consolidated revenue line report a "revenue miss" every strong quarter, as happened in Q1 2026.

Q1 2026 highlights:
- Consolidated revenue: $544.9M (+10% YoY) — understates the business
- Aggregate fees (all Affiliates): $1,909.9M (+50% YoY)
- Equity-method income (net): $147.4M (+96% YoY)
- Net income (controlling): $110.4M (+52%); GAAP diluted EPS $3.84 (+75%)
- Economic EPS: $8.23 (+58%); controlling Adjusted EBITDA: $317.3M (+39%)
- Performance fees up roughly $239M, primarily in liquid alternatives

Cash flow and the balance sheet are strong and the cash conversion is real. Q1 operating cash flow of $299.3M was driven by $294.4M of distributions received from equity-method Affiliates, which are cash returns rather than accrual marks. Total debt of ~$2.95B (senior bank $565M, senior notes $1,172M, junior subordinated $1,216M) carries an investment-grade A3 (Moody's) / BBB+ (S&P) rating and a weighted-average maturity of roughly 22 years, with no maturity before the $350M senior notes due June 2030. The junior subordinated notes receive equity credit from raters. Cash fell to $376M (from $586M) after settling the convertibles. The balance sheet is roughly 75% intangibles and investment carrying value ($2.5B goodwill, $1.6B acquired client relationships, $3.0B equity-method investments), which is the source of recurring impairment risk discussed below.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters, with the December 2025 quarter beating by 6.2% and the March 2026 quarter by 0.9% on Economic EPS. Estimate revisions are positive but moderate, not explosive: the current quarter has been revised +8.8% over 30 days and +5.2% over 90 days, current-year up +4.4% over 90 days, next year +2.5% over 90 days. This is healthy upward momentum, not the +30% revision that signals a systematic mispricing of an earnings trough.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth rates (aggregate fees +50%, Economic EPS +58%) overstate the durable run-rate. Two adjustments matter.

First, the growth is concentrated. A single Reg S-X "significant" Affiliate generated $441.9M of revenue and $305.6M of net income in Q1 2026, up from $189.3M/$82.0M a year earlier. That one boutique appears to be the primary driver of both the performance-fee surge and the 96% jump in equity-method income. The profile of surging liquid-alternatives performance fees points to a large multi-strategy or systematic-macro manager. Performance fees are inherently lumpy and subject to high-water marks; the brief is explicit that Q1 is not a clean run-rate.

Second, management's own Q2 Adjusted EBITDA guide of $290-305M sits below the Q1 print. A sequential step-down in guided EBITDA after a performance-fee-heavy quarter is the clearest available confirmation that the Q1 contribution from the concentrated Affiliate does not repeat at the same pace.

Underlying organic growth is mixed. Equities, 34% of AUM, saw -$9.1B of net outflows in Q1 alone, consistent with the secular active-to-passive shift, and this drove $43M of aggregate intangible impairments in Q1'26 (after $74M in Q1'25). Alternatives inflows partially offset this. The durable growth engine is therefore less about organic AUM expansion and more about per-share compounding: the annual buyback shrinks the share count ~8-10% per year, amplifying even modest aggregate-fee growth into double-digit Economic EPS gains. The strategic rotation toward private markets and liquid alternatives, and the wealth-channel distribution build, is a credible multi-year tailwind that raises the quality and stickiness of the fee base over time.

## Valuation Assessment

On its true earnings base, AMG screens cheap. At $355, the stock trades at roughly 10.2x the current-year Economic EPS estimate of $34.87 and ~9x the next-year estimate of $39.25. On GAAP EPS of $22.72 the trailing/forward P/E is 14.4x, below the peer median of 15.0x (JHG 10.2x, SEIC 15.0x, VCTR 19.5x, HLNE 13.3x, CRBG 59.6x). The mean analyst target is $381 (median $376), with a $300 low and $454 high.

The independent catalyst that distinguishes this from a generic value trap is the buyback program: a mechanical, recurring per-share tailwind rather than just a low multiple. That earns valuation some credit. But two factors temper the score. The apparent cheapness rests on Economic EPS estimates that are themselves carried disproportionately by the concentrated, performance-fee-driven Affiliate; the part of the earnings base that makes the multiple look low is precisely the part most at risk of normalizing, and the Q2 guide already points that way. And the stock has run ~95% over twelve months to within ~8% of the mean target, so the gap to fair value that existed in the high-$290s has largely closed. We are not penalizing the stock for having appreciated; current price versus a forward earnings base of uncertain durability leaves limited margin of safety. The reward:risk against the $381 mean (+8%) and $300 low (-15%) is roughly 1:2 on the analyst range alone.

## Competitive Landscape

AMG competes in two arenas. Its Affiliates compete in asset management against scale players like BlackRock, passive/ETF providers driving fee compression, and multi-manager platforms; AI is a longer-term threat to active-management fee pools. AMG itself competes to acquire stakes against PE firms, sovereign wealth funds, insurers, and other consolidators. Its edge is structural: founders who reject outright consolidation accept AMG's minority-stake-plus-autonomy model, and AMG layers on distribution and succession support.

The trajectory is mixed but tilting favorably on mix. The equities book is structurally declining, and that erosion is recurring and visible in the impairments. Against that, AMG is gaining position in the higher-fee, stickier alternatives and private-markets segments and building a wealth-distribution channel that few independent boutiques could fund alone. Net, the competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving as the portfolio rotates, but the legacy drag keeps it from a high score.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-Affiliate concentration (dominant, partly unpriced).** The most important risk is that one boutique produced $305.6M of net income in a single quarter, 3.7x the prior year, and appears to drive the bulk of recent earnings growth. The 10-K explicitly warns that "certain Affiliates contribute more significantly… could have a disproportionate adverse impact." A weak performance year, key-person departure, or restructuring at that one firm would hit results out of proportion to its share of AUM. This is a specific, falsifiable risk, and the Q2 guide already signals the high-water-mark dynamics that make performance fees lumpy.

**Structural equities decline and impairment drag.** Persistent net outflows from the long-only equities book trigger recurring intangible impairments and a ~$4.2B intangible base that is sensitive to declining fee/AUM assumptions.

**Capital obligations.** Affiliate-equity put obligations (redemption value $458.2M at 3/31/26; ~$65M net purchases expected for the rest of 2026), $420.9M of unfunded co-investment commitments, and $577.3M of contingent earn-out payments (mostly 2027) are recurring, hard-to-predict cash drains.

**Operational gearing.** The newer revenue-less-expenses structures expose AMG to Affiliate cost inflation, unlike the legacy gross-revenue structures, making earnings more operationally geared as the mix shifts.

**Governance and key personnel.** Orderly but notable C-suite churn: President/COO Thomas Wojcik departed via a February 2026 separation agreement, and CFO Dava Ritchea is relatively new, against a backdrop of no executive employment contracts and explicit key-person dependence.

**No income support.** The dividend is a token $0.01/share (yield ~0.01%); total return depends entirely on price and buyback-driven compounding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($454, +28%).** Performance fees at the concentrated alternatives Affiliate prove more durable than feared, the rotation into private markets and liquid alts continues to lift the blended fee rate, the wealth-channel distribution build scales, and the buyback machine keeps shrinking the share count ~8-10% per year. Economic EPS compounds toward and beyond $39+ next year, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward the peer median on a higher-quality, stickier fee base. At ~9x next-year Economic EPS, the re-rating optionality is real if the run-rate holds.

**Bear case ($300, -15%).** The Q1 performance-fee surge normalizes, confirmed in part by the Q2 EBITDA step-down, and the concentrated Affiliate gives back gains. Equities outflows accelerate, impairments recur, and the Economic EPS estimates that make the stock look cheap prove too high. With the stock already near its 52-week high after a 95% run and no dividend to cushion, multiple and estimate compression combine to retrace toward the low analyst target.

**Base case ($381, +8%).** Buyback-driven per-share compounding continues and the alts rotation grinds forward, but performance fees moderate from the Q1 peak and equities outflows persist, capping near-term upside. The stock converges toward the mean analyst target over the next 12 months. At ~10x current-year Economic EPS the downside is supported by cash generation and balance-sheet quality, but the upside is limited by the run-up and the run-rate question.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (early August), which directly tests the run-rate via the Adjusted EBITDA guide and performance-fee disclosure.**

The score of 6.1 reflects a financially healthy compounder whose headline growth is concentrated and performance-fee-inflated, trading near its 52-week high after a 95% advance with minimal margin of safety to the mean target. This is the specific, falsifiable reason it is not a BUY at $355: the cheap ~10x Economic EPS multiple rests on estimates carried disproportionately by one Affiliate whose contribution management's own Q2 guide signals is stepping down, and the asymmetry that existed in the high-$290s (where the prior BUY was issued) has compressed to roughly +8% upside / -15% downside on the analyst range.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA prints at or above the top of the $290-305M guide AND performance-fee/equity-method income disclosure shows the concentrated Affiliate's contribution holding rather than reverting — evidence the run-rate is durable.
- A pullback toward the low-$300s that restores ≥20% upside to the $381 mean target on unchanged fundamentals.
- Acceleration in current-year Economic EPS revisions beyond +10% (vs the current +4.4% over 90 days), signaling the market is systematically underestimating earnings rather than catching up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sequential Economic EPS decline driven by performance-fee reversal at the concentrated Affiliate, or a key-person/restructuring event at that firm.
- Equities net outflows accelerating materially beyond the ~$9B/quarter pace with a step-up in intangible impairments.
- A break in capital-return discipline: meaningful share issuance to fund Affiliate buyouts/earn-outs, or a curtailment of the ~$500M buyback program.

## Conclusion

AMG is a well-run, cash-generative financial-engineering compounder with a sound strategic rotation toward higher-quality alternatives and a disciplined, anti-dilutive capital-return program that drives most of the per-share return. Investors who own it should continue to hold. The reason not to add at $355 is concrete: the record Q1 was concentrated in one Affiliate's performance fees, management's Q2 EBITDA guide sits below the Q1 print and confirms the step-down, and the stock has run ~95% to within ~8% of the mean analyst target with no income support. We would re-engage on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on August evidence that the elevated performance-fee contribution is durable. **HOLD.**
