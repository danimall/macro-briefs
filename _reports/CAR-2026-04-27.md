## Executive Summary

Avis Budget Group combines a fundamentally stressed car rental business with a post-squeeze stock trading at roughly 2x the consensus analyst price target. The company generated $3.3B in operating cash flow in 2025 but lost $889M on a GAAP basis, driven by $3.0B in vehicle depreciation and $518M in EV fleet impairments. Total debt stands at $25.3B against negative stockholders' equity of $3.1B. Revenue has declined for two consecutive years and pricing power in the Americas segment is deteriorating (revenue per day down 3% in 2025).

The stock's journey from ~$100 in late March to $848 on April 22 and back to $204 was driven by a mechanically inevitable short squeeze: short interest at the time stood at 54% of free float (per Forbes/Trefis reporting), SRS Investment Management holding ~50% of shares, and Pentwater Capital exercising options to acquire ~22% — leaving more shares sold short than were freely tradable. Management opportunistically launched an ATM offering of up to 5 million shares during the squeeze, which could raise $1-2B+ to de-lever the balance sheet. This is the single most important near-term variable. The stock at $204 still prices in operational recovery that the fundamental data does not support, with seven covering analysts targeting prices between $85 and $165.

The current macro environment compounds the operational challenges: tariff uncertainty directly impacts fleet acquisition costs from foreign manufacturers, and consumer spending weakness threatens rental demand.

## Company Overview

Avis Budget Group operates three brands — Avis (premium), Budget (value), and Zipcar (car sharing) — across approximately 180 countries through ~10,000 rental locations. The Americas segment (76% of revenue, ~$8.9B) and International segment (24%, ~$2.75B) constitute the operating structure. The company manages an average fleet of ~684,000 vehicles, of which 84% are "risk" vehicles where Avis bears residual value exposure rather than receiving manufacturer buyback guarantees.

Key recent strategic developments include a partnership with Waymo for autonomous fleet management services in Dallas (public launch planned 2026), the "Avis First" premium curbside service, and an aggressive fleet reset targeting sub-1-year average fleet age in the U.S. by end of Q1 2026. The Interpace Ventures transaction in Q4 2025 created a separately financed entity for EV/alternative fleet assets, issuing $965M in asset-backed securities.

International grew 3% with RPD up 6%, creating a divergence that suggests Americas-specific execution issues beyond macro factors.

### Profitability Collapse

The company swung from $2.76B in net income (2022) to cumulative losses of $2.71B over 2024-2025. The primary driver: vehicle depreciation expense nearly quadrupled from $828M (2022) to $3.02B (2025) as post-COVID used car price inflation reversed. Layered on top were $2.47B in fleet impairments (2024) and $518M in EV-specific impairments (2025), plus $390M in other fleet charges in 2025. Combined fleet-related impairment and other charges over the two years totaled approximately $3.4B — roughly equal to the pre-squeeze market capitalization.

Adjusted EBITDA of $748M in 2025 (up 19% from $628M) provides a cleaner operating view but missed management's own $900M-$1B guidance range. International EBITDA surged 80% to $290M while Americas was flat at $552M.

Approximately $19.2B of debt is vehicle-backed ABS financing, which is standard industry structure. Corporate debt of ~$6.1B is the more relevant figure for assessing financial distress, though even this amount is substantial. The company issued $600M in 8.375% senior notes in May 2025 — a coupon that reflects the credit market's assessment of risk. Available liquidity of $818M ($519M cash + $299M revolver) is thin against the debt load.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.05 places the company deep in the distress zone, though this metric is partially distorted by the ABS-heavy capital structure common in fleet-intensive businesses.

### Cash Flow: The Counter-Narrative

Operating cash flow of $3.3B provides the primary argument against imminent financial distress. Depreciation of ~$3.0B is the largest non-cash charge, meaning the business generates substantial cash even while reporting GAAP losses. Investing cash flows of ($5.16B) — almost entirely fleet purchases — consume more than operating cash flow generates, requiring net borrowings of $1.86B to fund the gap.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The last four quarters show an erratic pattern: two beats and two misses. The Q4 2025 miss was severe (EPS of -$3.23 vs. estimate of -$0.42, a -666% surprise), while Q3 2025 beat by 26%. This volatility reflects the business's sensitivity to unpredictable events — Q4 was devastated by a government shutdown that reduced Americas commercial rental days by 11% in November.

## Growth Analysis

Current-year EPS estimates have been revised down 66.5% over 90 days (from $10.75 to $3.61), and next-year estimates down 31.6% (from $11.23 to $7.68). The current quarter estimate of -$7.50 is more than double the -$3.65 estimate from 90 days ago, reflecting deteriorating near-term expectations.

Organic growth prospects are limited. Revenue per day in the Americas is declining. Fleet utilization ranges from 68-72%, providing some room for improvement but also indicating pricing discipline challenges. The competitive landscape is intensifying with Sixt expanding in North America and ride-hailing platforms competing for short-distance trips.

The Waymo partnership represents a genuine long-term strategic opportunity in autonomous vehicle fleet management. Financial impact is likely immaterial for the foreseeable future — Dallas is a single-market pilot with no disclosed revenue terms.

Tariff risk is a direct headwind to fleet acquisition costs. CAR sources vehicles from Toyota, Hyundai/Kia, VW, Renault, and others with significant foreign manufacturing exposure. Even modest tariffs on imported vehicles could add meaningfully to annual fleet costs.

## Valuation Assessment

At $204, CAR trades at a negative trailing P/E (due to -$25.26 trailing EPS loss). Using forward consensus estimates:

- FY2026E EPS: $3.61 → Forward P/E: ~56x
- FY2027E EPS: $7.68 → Forward P/E: ~27x
- EV/Adj. EBITDA (using $748M 2025 EBITDA): ~40x+

Analyst consensus targets range from $85 (low) to $165 (high), with a median of $112. The current price of $204 sits 24% above the highest analyst target. JP Morgan downgraded to Underweight during the squeeze reversal. The most recent month's analyst distribution: 0 strong buys, 1 buy, 4 holds, 1 sell, 2 strong sells.

Peer medians show industrial companies at 32x P/E, but these are capital-light businesses with positive equity and stable margins, making the comparison uninformative. Against Hertz (HTZ), the only direct comparable, both companies are navigating fleet impairments and EV write-downs.

There is no fundamental justification for the current price at $204. The stock remains elevated due to residual short-squeeze dynamics and uncertainty around ATM offering proceeds. If the ATM raised $1.5B+ at elevated prices and that capital reduced the $6.1B corporate debt load by 25%, the long-term financial health improvement would be meaningful but would support a price closer to the $120-140 range, not $204.

## Competitive Landscape

Avis maintains a leading share of airport car rental revenue in North America, Europe, and Australasia, but competitive positioning is deteriorating on multiple fronts. The Americas/International RPD divergence indicates Americas-specific pricing pressure, likely from Enterprise Holdings (private) gaining share during Avis/Hertz fleet disruptions.

The car rental industry is fundamentally capital-intensive and cyclical, with limited differentiation. Airport concession agreements, brand recognition, and loyalty programs provide some competitive barriers, but pricing is largely a function of fleet cost economics and demand cycles. The 84% risk vehicle mix (vs. manufacturer-guaranteed program vehicles) is an aggressive bet on residual values that has cost the company dearly over the past two years.

The Waymo partnership is a genuine differentiator in fleet management capabilities, but its economic value depends entirely on autonomous vehicle adoption timelines that remain uncertain.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Residual value deterioration.** With 84% risk vehicles, any further decline in used car values directly compresses margins. Tariff uncertainty compounds this — tariffs on imported vehicles simultaneously increase acquisition costs while reducing demand and resale values for foreign-brand vehicles already in the fleet.

2. **Refinancing risk.** Virtually all $25.3B in debt matures within 5 years. The 8.375% coupon on the most recent senior note issuance signals credit market concern. Rising interest rates and potential recession increase the cost and difficulty of refinancing.

3. **SRS change-of-control provision.** If SRS ownership exceeds 50% (currently just under), it could trigger 101% buyback offers on all senior notes. The combination of SRS (~50%), Pentwater (~22%), and other concentrated holders creates an unusual governance dynamic with specific, quantifiable debt acceleration risk. A Fourth Amended and Restated Cooperation Agreement requires SRS to vote shares above 45% in proportion to other shareholders.

4. **Consumer spending weakness.** Car rental demand is discretionary spending. Weakening employment trends and macro uncertainty pressure the company's core customer base.

5. **Continued EV write-downs.** The $518M Q4 2025 EV impairment may not be the last. Management's admission that EV useful life had to be halved from 36 to 18 months reveals fundamental analytical failures in the original EV deployment. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) enacted July 2025 eliminated/limited EV tax credits, further pressuring EV residual values.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 150.3% is 39.3 percentage points above historical volatility of 111% — itself extraordinarily elevated reflecting the squeeze. The term structure is in steep backwardation (150.3% near-term vs. 99.6% at 6 months), consistent with continued near-term turbulence pricing. Put/call OI ratio of 3.17 indicates heavy put positioning, while the call-heavy put/call skew (-8.6%) suggests active speculative interest on both sides. The implied move of ±26.8% to nearest monthly expiry underscores that options are pricing a wide range of outcomes, consistent with a stock still in post-squeeze price discovery where fundamental anchoring has not yet reasserted.

Short interest currently stands at 80% of float with 2.8 days to cover. This creates ongoing squeeze risk in both directions: forced covering can spike the price, while forced liquidation by holders of squeeze-purchased shares can crater it. The stock is untradable from a fundamental standpoint while short interest remains at these levels.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most immediate catalyst is the next quarterly earnings report (likely May 2026), where current quarter consensus is -$7.50 EPS and estimate revisions have been sharply negative (-105.3% over 90 days). Disclosure of actual ATM offering proceeds will resolve a key uncertainty — if proceeds are lower than the market assumes, the balance sheet improvement narrative weakens substantially.

**Timing window:** 30-60 days to next earnings report. Post-squeeze price discovery is actively ongoing — the stock has moved from $848 to $204 in days, and further normalization toward analyst consensus ($112 median) would represent additional 45% downside.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 80% of float with 2.8 days to cover, creating severe squeeze risk. While the original squeeze participants (Pentwater) are unlikely to repeat the same mechanics, any positive news flow could trigger violent covering rallies. The concentrated ownership structure (SRS ~50%, Pentwater ~22%) means the effective float remains tiny relative to short interest.

**Borrow and options signal:** The 150.3% near-term IV and steep backwardation suggest very high borrow costs. Put/call OI ratio of 3.17 confirms heavy bearish positioning already in place.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Fair value range based on fundamentals and analyst consensus is $90-140. From $204, that implies 30-55% downside. However, squeeze risk creates realistic upside scenarios of $300-400+ on any covering event. The reward:risk ratio is approximately 1.5:1 at best — insufficient given the extreme short squeeze mechanics.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis for lower prices is strong (stock trades 24% above the highest analyst target), but 80% short interest creates unacceptable squeeze risk. The thin float, concentrated ownership, and residual post-squeeze volatility make this position mechanically dangerous regardless of fundamental merit. The stock should be avoided until short interest normalizes below 30% and price discovery re-anchors to fundamentals.

2. Short interest declines below 25% of float, allowing fundamental price discovery

**Timeline:** Headwinds are likely to persist for 6-12 months. The combination of consumer spending weakness, tariff risk on fleet costs, continued EV residual value uncertainty, and post-squeeze price normalization argues against any near-term fundamental catalyst. Q3 2026 results (the peak summer travel season) will provide the first clean read on operational recovery under the new fleet strategy.

## Conclusion

Avis Budget Group at $204 is disconnected from its fundamentals. The underlying business has $3.1B in negative equity, $25.3B in debt, declining revenue, and consecutive years of GAAP losses driven by a costly EV strategy error and post-COVID used car value normalization. Operating cash flow of $3.3B is consumed by fleet investment and debt service. The ATM offering during the squeeze was a shrewd capital allocation move that could meaningfully improve the balance sheet, but even in the most optimistic ATM scenario, the stock trades well above fundamental value.

The 80% short interest makes the stock untradable from either direction on a risk-adjusted basis. The prudent course is to avoid the position entirely until the float normalizes, fundamentals stabilize, and the price reflects the actual operating performance of a capital-intensive, cyclical business with substantial execution risk.
