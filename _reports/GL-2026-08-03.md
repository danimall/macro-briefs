## Executive Summary

Globe Life sells small-face-amount life and supplemental health protection to lower-middle and middle-income U.S. households through six distribution channels, four of which are exclusive captive agency forces. The economics are unusually good where the moat is real: American Income Life produced a 49% life underwriting margin on $1.92B of FY2025 premium and accounted for 49.6% of consolidated underwriting margin on 39.2% of premium. Consolidated operating ROE ex-AOCI was 16.0% in FY2025 and 18.4% year-to-date at mid-2026. The investment portfolio is deliberately positioned opposite the private-credit-heavy life cohort: A− average rating, BBB at 41% of fixed maturities and falling, below-investment-grade at 2.7%, roughly 1% in senior direct lending and asset-based finance, no securities lending, no CDOs.

The problem is the composition of per-share growth. Reported operating EPS grew 17.4% in FY2025 to $14.52 and is guided to $15.55-$15.95 for FY2026. Stripping the annual third-quarter assumption unlock, which contributed roughly $1.31 per share after tax in 2025 versus $0.42 in 2024, normalized operating EPS growth was about 10.5% in 2025 and is guided to 9-10% in 2026, figures that reconcile to management's own disclosures on the Q2 call. Converting back to dollars, normalized operating income grew approximately 4.6% (2024), 1.7% (2025) and 3.9% (2026E). Nearly all of the per-share growth above that comes from a diluted share count that fell from 96.4M in 2023 to roughly 78M in 2026E. The repurchase earnings yield has compressed from about 11.5% at the FY2025 average execution price of $126.41 to roughly 8.0-8.6% at $182.25, and management explicitly attributed part of the modest guidance raise to "the reduced impact of share repurchases due to the higher share price." Planned FY2026 distributions of $765-795M exceed the 10-K's projected parent excess cash flow of $650M; the gap was bridged by increasing the term loan from $250M to $450M.

At 11.6x guided 2026 operating EPS, 11.0x next-year consensus of $16.52, and 1.82x book value ex-AOCI of $100.04, the shares are not expensive relative to the sector, but they are no longer cheap relative to their own history or to the growth they can deliver on unmanufactured earnings. The stock is up 29.7% over twelve months and sits 4.9% below the 52-week high of $191.55, roughly 6% below the sell-side mean target of $193.91. I score this a HOLD with a low-conviction classification: the incremental information since the last review is minimal, three of the last four quarters missed consensus, and the highest-severity risk in the file, the September 2024 EEOC determination that agents affiliated with one State General Agent were employees rather than independent contractors, is neither quantified by the company nor visible in options or short-interest positioning.

## Company Overview

Globe Life is a McKinney, Texas insurance holding company. Reporting segments are life, health and investments, but the operating unit of analysis is the distribution channel. FY2025 premium of $4,890.2M and underwriting margin of $1,899.5M (38.8% of premium) broke down as:

| Channel | Premium | % premium | UW margin | % UW margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| American Income Life (exclusive; unions/affinity) | $1,916.3M | 39.2% | $942.4M | 49.6% |
| Direct to Consumer | $1,057.5M | 21.6% | $323.0M | 17.0% |
| Liberty National (exclusive; worksite) | $580.6M | 11.9% | $273.7M | 14.4% |
| Family Heritage (exclusive; ROP supp. health) | $468.1M | 9.6% | $174.9M | 9.2% |
| United American (non-exclusive brokers; Med Supp) | $666.8M | 13.6% | $38.0M | 2.0% |
| Other (independent agencies) | $201.0M | 4.1% | $147.3M | 7.8% |

Products are fixed-benefit protection contracts. Reserves use locked-in original discount rates and benefits are not equity- or rate-sensitive, so the balance sheet does not carry the interest-sensitive liability profile of annuity-heavy peers. The majority of the annuity block was ceded to a third-party reinsurer in 2024, taking average annuity policy liabilities from $640.5M to $191.3M. Financial strength ratings at 12/31/2025 were S&P AA− on four subsidiaries and A.M. Best A on five.

## Financial Analysis

**Headline trend (10-K MD&A).** Total revenue moved from roughly $5.45B (2023) to $5.78B (2024) to $5.99B (2025). Premium grew 5% to $4,890M. GAAP net income was $1,161.2M and GAAP diluted EPS $14.07 (+17.8%); operating EPS $14.52 (+17.4%). GAAP ROE 20.9%, operating ROE ex-AOCI 16.0%. BVPS $74.17; BVPS ex-AOCI $96.16. Operating cash flow was $1.40B in both 2024 and 2025.

Q2 2026: net income $288M, GAAP diluted EPS $3.65 (+20%); net operating income $285M, operating EPS $3.61 (+10%). Premium +7% (life +3% to $861M, health +16% to $437M). Life underwriting margin 42% of premium (from 41%); health margin 23% (from 26%). BVPS ex-AOCI $100.04 (+11% y/y). YTD ROE 18.4%.

**Earnings quality.** The company includes annual third-quarter assumption remeasurement gains inside net operating income. Those gains were $3.2M pretax in 2023, $46.3M in 2024 and $134.3M in 2025. At the FY2025 operating tax rate of 19.2% and the implied diluted share counts, the after-tax per-share contribution runs approximately $0.03, $0.42 and $1.31. The 2024→2025 increase of $0.89 accounts for roughly 41% of the $2.15 rise in reported operating EPS. Management's newly disclosed normalized life underwriting margin went 40% → 41%, against a headline 41% → 45%. FY2026 guidance embeds $110-130M of Q3 remeasurement (life $90-100M, health $20-30M), slightly less per share than 2025, which is why reported 8.5% guided growth understates the 9-10% normalized figure management cited.

**The mechanical point.** Normalized operating income of roughly $1,024M (2023), $1,072M (2024), $1,090M (2025) and $1,133M (2026E) grows 2-5% annually. Normalized EPS grows 9-11%. The delta is share count: −7.0%, −7.9%, −5.5%. FY2025 repurchases were 5.42M shares for $685.2M at $126.41 average from parent excess cash flow, plus 1.469M shares/$189.7M funded by option-exercise proceeds and the related tax benefit. Q1 2026 executions averaged $141.57; Q2 2026 averaged $154.28. Cumulative repurchases since 1986 total $11.0B.

The buyback has been value-creating; $126.41 average against $182.25 today is unambiguous, but the accretion per dollar deployed falls as the price rises. At $182.25 the operating earnings yield on repurchase is 8.0% on trailing $14.52 and 8.6% on guided $15.75, versus about 11.5% at the FY2025 average price.

**Funding.** The 10-K projects FY2026 parent excess cash flow of $650M against $890.3M actual in 2025 (which included $80M of nonrecurring extraordinary subsidiary dividends; 2024 included $192M). Planned FY2026 distributions are $670-700M of buybacks plus roughly $95M of dividends, a gap of about $115-145M. At the end of Q2 2026 the term loan principal was raised from $250M to $450M with maturity extended to June 2029, the revolver extended to June 2031, and buyback guidance raised $100M at the midpoint, which management tied directly to "the additional term loan proceeds." Management also framed the term loan as partly a term-out of commercial paper, which softens but does not eliminate the point: marginal repurchases are now debt-supported at a higher purchase price. At roughly 5.0% blended pre-tax debt cost (financing costs $141.2M in 2025, +11%) against an 8.0-8.6% repurchase yield, the arithmetic still works.

**Balance sheet and cushions.** Debt of roughly $2.8B par against approximately $7.8B of equity ex-AOCI implies 26-27% debt-to-capital ex-AOCI, unremarkable for a AA− rated insurer. Consolidated Company Action Level RBC was 316% at YE2025 against a 300-320% target, but management disclosed on the Q2 call that this represents only about $95M of excess capital above the 300% target. Parent liquid assets were $76M at 12/31/2025 and roughly $110M at 6/30/2026 against a stated year-end target of $50-60M, backstopped by a $1B revolver ($579M available at YE2025) and a $500M P-CAPS facility carrying a 1.789% annual fee on the unexercised portion. Net unrealized loss on fixed maturities was $1.2B at YE2025 and $1.4B at 6/30/2026; the AFS book is held with maturity capacity, so this is an AOCI and book-value issue rather than a liquidity one.

A screening Altman Z-score of 0.61 appears in third-party data. It is not applicable here: for a life insurer, total liabilities are predominantly pre-funded policyholder reserves and asset turnover is structurally near 0.19x, so both the equity/liabilities and sales/assets terms are mechanically depressed. RBC, ratings, operating cash flow and hold-to-maturity capacity are the relevant solvency evidence.

**Investment segment.** Excess investment income fell 16% in 2025 to $138.4M as fixed-maturity acquisitions dropped from $1,540M (2023) to $918M (2025); the 10-K attributes this to reinsurance transactions and higher subsidiary dividends to the parent reducing investable funds. This is reversing: Q2 2026 net investment income +4% to $294M, excess investment income +10% to $38M, $399M invested at a 6.27% new-money yield, with FY2026 excess investment income growth guided near 7%.

**Financial Health: 5.8.** Strong operating cash generation, high-quality portfolio, moderate leverage and top-tier ratings, offset by deliberately thin parent liquidity, only ~$95M of subsidiary capital above the RBC target, distributions running ahead of internal generation, and a Bermuda reinsurance structure ($1.2B of ceded statutory reserves to GL Re) whose capital efficiency depends on BMA rules not changing.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance for FY2026: operating EPS $15.55-$15.95 (+8.5% at midpoint, raised roughly $0.10); total premium +6.5-7%; life premium +2.5-3%; health premium +14-16%, including approximately $65M from approved individual Medicare Supplement rate increases; full-year life underwriting margin 43-45% (normalized 41-42%); health margin 23-27%; United American premium +25-35% with H2 margin near 7%.

Where the growth comes from is the issue. The two channels producing roughly 67% of underwriting margin both had declining net life sales in Q2 2026:

- **American Income Life:** net life sales −2%; average producing agent count 11,391, down 7% year-over-year (up 3% sequentially) after 10,579 → 11,741 → 11,920 in 2023-2025. Management states plainly that "agent count growth is a precursor to sales growth." Compensation changes were implemented at the start of Q2 2026 and management guides to mid-single-digit H2 growth in both agents and life sales. Renewal lapses remain above pre-pandemic levels, described as the new baseline.
- **Direct to Consumer:** net life sales −15%, premium −1% to $244M. Management identifies a structural cause: AI-mediated consumer search reducing paid-search volume and bidding up the price of remaining inventory. FY2026 guidance is for a single-digit decline in DTC net life sales.

Growth is concentrated in United American, which produced 2.0% of FY2025 underwriting margin on 13.6% of premium at a 6% margin (down from 11% in 2023). The Medicare Advantage → Medicare Supplement migration is real and Globe Life captures it cleanly; it markets no MA plans, so it is a pure recipient of displaced beneficiaries. Medicare Supplement net sales more than doubled from $70M (2024) to $126M (2025). But if UA grows 25-35% while total premium grows 6.5-7%, UA moves from 13.6% toward ~16% of premium in 2026 while contributing 2-3% of margin. Consolidated normalized health margin has already fallen 28% → 27% → 25%, guided to 23-25% in Q4 2026, which the 10-K attributes to claims experience and UA mix. Q2 2026 health policy obligations were 56.8% of premium versus 53.3% a year earlier, "higher than our estimates," driven by prior-period Medicare Supplement claims tied to a CMS physician-reimbursement correction, high-severity claims at Every Health, and adverse cancer claims at Liberty National.

Offsetting evidence matters: Liberty National agent count +8% and Family Heritage +7% in Q2 2026 argue the AIL decline is company-specific and remediable rather than market-wide, and DTC is expanding margin (33% of premium in 2025 vs 29% in 2024) as it declines in volume, a discipline choice. Management also flags a 2027 upside not in current estimates: the Nebraska regulator approved reciprocal jurisdiction for GL Re in Q2 2026, Indiana approval (AIL's domicile) is pending, a new cession is expected in Q3 2026, and 2026 excess cash flow estimates explicitly exclude any parent cash flow from that structure.

**Earnings surprise and revision context.** The four-quarter record is one beat and three misses: −4.2% (Q4'25), −4.1% (Q1'26), −4.7% (Q2'26) against the consensus series in the data. Source bases differ: the brief cites a Zacks operating consensus of $3.67 against reported operating EPS of $3.61, a 1.6% shortfall, while other coverage called the $3.65 GAAP figure in line. The magnitude is disputed; the direction is not. Estimate revisions are mixed and skew negative where it matters: current-quarter consensus $4.88 is up 8.0% over 90 days, but that quarter contains the guided $110-130M assumption unlock, so the revision is largely mechanical recognition of a known non-cash actuarial item. Next quarter is −1.3%, current year +1.0%, next year −0.6%. Consensus is not accelerating in a way that would justify treating targets as a floor.

**Growth Prospects: 4.5.** Mid-single-digit premium growth, 2-5% normalized earnings growth, and per-share growth dependent on a repurchase program whose accretion falls as the stock rises.

## Valuation Assessment

At $182.25:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on TTM GAAP EPS ($14.07) | ~13.0x |
| P/E on FY2026 guided operating EPS midpoint $15.75 | 11.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $16.52 | 11.0x |
| Price / BVPS ex-AOCI ($100.04) | 1.82x |
| Price / GAAP BVPS ($70.18) | 2.60x |
| Dividend yield ($1.32 annualized) | 0.72% |
| Operating ROE ex-AOCI | 16.0% FY25 / 18.4% YTD |

Peer medians in the data are P/E 19.9 and yield 2.0%, but the median is distorted: JXN is negative, UNM at 19.9 and CNO at 20.0 carry different earnings composition, and PFG sits at 15.7. The closest structural comparable is Primerica at 11.7x, a distribution-led middle-market life insurer that also compounds per share through buyback. GL at ~13.0x trailing sits modestly above PRI. So the apparent discount to the sector median narrows substantially against the one peer with a comparable business model.

The reason to be cautious about the low multiple is exactly the reason the multiple is low. Roughly 8-9% of operating EPS in a given year comes from a judgment-heavy annual actuarial exercise. Normalized earnings power is about $14.52 for 2026, implying 12.6x. Underlying earnings grow 2-5%, with the balance of per-share growth purchased, increasingly with debt, at rising prices. A re-rating mechanically reduces future EPS growth, which caps the multiple.

Sell-side targets: mean $193.91 (+6.4%), median $192, high $225, low $166. Analyst coverage is 4 strong buy / 4 buy / 3 hold, stable across three months with no recent changes. Because the stock is at 11.6x forward rather than a premium multiple, the argument that targets lag reality has some force, but it requires accelerating estimates, and next-year consensus is drifting down.

The bull case on absolute value is that Globe Life earns 16-18% ROE, has AA− subsidiaries, holds a genuinely conservative portfolio in a sector that has moved the other way, retires 4-5% of its shares a year, and has an unmodeled 2027 capital release from GL Re. The bear case is that 1.82x book ex-AOCI for a 2-5% earnings grower with a compressing high-margin channel mix is roughly fair, and that the post-short-seller trust discount has already closed (the proxy's TSR index moved from 121 to 154 in 2025 alone).

**Valuation: 5.0.** Fair rather than mispriced. Cheapness on headline P/E is offset by earnings-quality dependence on the unlock, buyback-manufactured growth, and litigation and classification tails that are not in the numbers.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat, where it exists, is distribution. Exclusive captive agency forces hold privileged access to labor-union and affinity membership lists (AIL) and worksite employers (Liberty National, Family Heritage); the DTC operation generates over one million leads a year, shared with the agencies. A 49% life underwriting margin at AIL is not achievable without genuine pricing power in a segment larger carriers find uneconomic to serve. Management's framing (same market, essentially the same products, 60+ years, accumulated underwriting data on that population) is supported by the margin structure.

Globe Life competes for agents rather than primarily for customers, which makes the intra-company divergence diagnostic: Liberty National agents +8% and Family Heritage +7% against AIL −7% in Q2 2026. Management uses that contrast to argue the AIL problem is internal and fixable, which is credible but unproven; management also notes an annual agent-count decline has happened only once or twice in 25 years.

On the investment side Globe Life is positioned favorably against the private-credit-heavy cohort: management states total BBB plus below-investment-grade exposure as a share of equity ex-AOCI is at its lowest in over 25 years. An insurer with ~1% in direct lending and asset-based finance has a relative-quality advantage that is not obviously reflected in a ~13x multiple.

Trajectory is the deciding factor, and it is negative in the highest-value channels. AIL sales and agents are down, DTC faces a structural lead-funnel compression from AI-mediated search, and mix is shifting toward the non-exclusive broker channel in a rate-regulated line at a 6% margin. AI cuts both ways: management expects the 7.3% administrative-expense-to-premium ratio to fall as AI is applied to high-volume application, service and claims processing, but the revenue-side impairment is arriving now and the cost-side benefit is prospective.

**Competitive Position: 4.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Independent-contractor misclassification (highest severity, unquantified).** The FY2025 10-K discloses that in September 2024 the EEOC notified Globe Life it had determined all sales agents affiliated with a single State General Agent (Simon Arias) were employees of Globe Life and/or AIL rather than independent contractors. The determination is not binding, but the 10-K states any civil action the EEOC brings "would likely include such an allegation," and that reclassification of a material portion of the agency force "could significantly increase our operating costs and negatively impact our insurance business." The exclusive agency forces total roughly 17,200 producing agents, and the entire 41-49% life underwriting margin structure is predicated on variable, commission-based, non-employee distribution. No accrual or exposure range is disclosed.

**Securities litigation past the dismissal stage.** One putative securities class action and five derivative suits remain from the April 2024 short-seller episode. Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, the motion to dismiss the securities class action was denied on September 29, 2025, putting the case into discovery with associated cost and settlement exposure. Directly attributable legal-proceedings expense was $17.1M (2025), $21.6M (2024), $0.9M (2023).

**Reputational feedback into recruiting.** The 10-K explicitly links short-seller reports and agent-misconduct allegations to the ability to recruit and retain agents and to policy persistency, the same variable driving the AIL agent decline.

**Assumption sensitivity.** A 1% mortality increase reduces net income by $47.2M; a 5% morbidity increase by $68.7M; a 10% decrease in lapses by $101.1M. With roughly 8-9% of operating EPS sourced from the annual review, these are live valuation inputs.

**Health claims and rate regulation.** Medicare Supplement carries structurally lower margins and depends on timely state rate approvals. Q2 2026 health policy obligations came in above management's estimates.

**Capital cushions.** ~$95M of subsidiary capital above the 300% RBC target and $50-60M targeted parent liquidity leave little slack for a claims shock or an adverse legal outcome without drawing the revolver or exercising the P-CAPS facility.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 22.8% against 19.9% one-year historical volatility, a 2.8-point spread the data characterizes as fair, and IV sits in the 5th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is flat (22.8% at 17 days, 23.1% at 45 days, 23.4% at 108 days), skew is described as balanced at 1.7%, and the nearest expiry prices only a ±4.2% move. Short interest is 0.1% of float with 0.1 days to cover. Neither the options market nor short sellers are positioned for a discontinuity. The EEOC determination and a securities class action in discovery are precisely the kind of unquantified binary that options and short interest tend to miss until a filing forces the issue. It also means there is no crowded-short fuel for a squeeze, and near-term optionality is cheap relative to the tail if one wants to hedge rather than trim.

**Risk Factors: 4.3.**

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AIL's Q2 compensation changes restore agent growth to the guided mid-single digits in H2, and net life sales follow with a two-to-three-quarter lag. Health margin stabilizes at the upper end of the 23-27% guided range as the $65M of approved Medicare Supplement rate increases fully earn in and prior-period claims development stops recurring. GL Re receives Indiana approval, the Q3 2026 cession executes, and 2027 parent excess cash flow rises above the $650M 2026 estimate, funding continued 4-5% annual share retirement without further leverage. Excess investment income compounds at 7%+ on 6.27% new-money yields against a portfolio yielding materially less. The conservative portfolio earns a relative-quality premium as private-credit-exposed peers face marks. On ~$17.00 of 2027 operating EPS at 13.5x, the multiple GL held before the 2024 episode, the shares reach roughly $222-230, near the $225 sell-side high.

**Bear case.** The AIL agent decline proves structural rather than compensation-driven, and the 2% sales decline widens; DTC declines exceed the guided single digits as AI-mediated search continues to compress paid-lead economics; mix shift to United American pulls consolidated health margin to the low end of guidance; the Q3 unlock lands below the $110-130M guided range, exposing the normalized 2-5% growth rate to full view; the EEOC files a civil action, or the securities class action settles materially. Consensus, having missed three straight quarters, moves down rather than up. At 9.5x $15.60 the shares are $148, or −18.8% from $182.25, roughly the level of the $166 low sell-side target only if the multiple compression is milder.

**Base case.** Guidance is delivered near the midpoint, mix headwinds and the AIL recovery roughly offset, and the multiple holds at approximately its current 11.0-11.6x forward. On next-year consensus of $16.52 at 11.6x that is $192, +5.3% from $182.25, plus a 0.72% dividend. That is consistent with the sell-side median of $192 and mean of $193.91.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $192** — 11.6x (the current forward multiple, held constant) on FY2027 consensus operating EPS of $16.52. **+5.3%** from the $182.25 basis.
- **Bull case target: $222** — 13.5x on approximately $17.00 of 2027 operating EPS, assuming AIL agent and sales recovery, health margin at the high end, and GL Re capital release funding buyback continuity. **+21.8%.**
- **Bear case target: $148** — 9.5x on $15.60, reflecting a mix-driven health margin at the low end, continued DTC and AIL sales declines, and a below-guidance Q3 unlock. **−18.8%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on three checkpoints: the Q3 2026 print (assumption unlock magnitude versus the $110-130M guide, and H2 AIL agent count), the Q4 2026 print (whether guided mid-single-digit AIL agent growth materialized), and any 8-K or 10-Q disclosure regarding EEOC action or securities-class-action developments.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. AIL average producing agent count returns to year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters with net life sales positive, the single most important operating variable, given AIL is ~50% of underwriting margin.
2. Consolidated normalized health underwriting margin stabilizes at or above 25% for two consecutive quarters, breaking the 28% → 27% → 25% trend, with Q2's 56.8% policy-obligation ratio reverting toward 53-54%.
3. Price near $150-158, which would put the shares at 9.5-10.0x guided 2026 operating EPS and restore a repurchase earnings yield above 10%.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. An EEOC civil action alleging employee status for a material portion of the agency force, or any disclosed accrual for agent reclassification.
2. Q3 2026 assumption remeasurement below $100M pretax, or FY2026 operating EPS guidance cut below $15.55.
3. FY2027 buyback guidance set below roughly $500M, or a further debt upsizing to fund repurchases while parent excess cash flow estimates fall, the combination that would confirm the capital-return engine is being sustained on the balance sheet rather than on earnings.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026), which contains the annual assumption unlock and the first full quarter of post-compensation-change AIL agent data.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on Globe Life, with the composite score having drifted from 7.0 in April to 4.86 now. The trajectory is not a reaction to price; the shares are up 29.7% over twelve months and sit 4.9% below their high. It reflects a specific, cumulative finding: the reported double-digit EPS growth is a 2-5% earnings grower plus a large repurchase program, and both halves of that equation have weakened in the past two quarters. The repurchase yield fell from ~11.5% at the 2025 average execution price to ~8.0-8.6% now, marginal buybacks are debt-supported, and the two channels generating ~67% of underwriting margin both posted declining net life sales while growth concentrated in the channel earning a 6% margin through brokers who also sell for competitors.

Against that, the franchise is real and the balance sheet is clean where it counts. A 49% underwriting margin at American Income Life, 16-18% operating ROE, AA− subsidiaries, $1.40B of annual operating cash flow, a portfolio with ~1% in private credit while much of the life sector went the other way, and an unmodeled 2027 capital release from GL Re are all genuine. At 11.6x forward and 1.82x book ex-AOCI, the shares are not priced for perfection.

Base case upside of 5.3% against bear case downside of 18.8% does not clear the bar for new capital, particularly in a sector where selection has historically added no alpha and where the largest identified risk, reclassification of the agency force, is unquantified by the company and unpriced by the options and short-interest markets. Existing holders are compensated by 4-5% annual share retirement, a 22% dividend increase, and a legitimate H2 recovery path in AIL. Own it if you own it; wait for either AIL agent growth to turn or a price in the $150s before adding.
