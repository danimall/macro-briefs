## Executive Summary

J&J Snack Foods has spent fiscal 2026 trading revenue for margin, and the trade is working at the gross-profit line. Nine-month revenue fell 5.0% to $1,114.6M while adjusted EBITDA held essentially flat at $123.1M versus $123.5M, and gross margin expanded 210bp to 31.1% (Q3 alone: +240bp to 35.5%). Food Service — 63% of the company — grew operating income 11.0% on a 7.3% sales decline, a 118bp margin gain that is the clearest evidence Project Apollo is real. Management raised the program's run-rate operating-income target to $25M from roughly $20M and now says full benefit arrives in fiscal 2027. Four plants have been closed with the cash cost essentially behind the company, three regional distribution centers opened, and the June 5, 2026 credit amendment extended the $225M revolver from December 2026 to June 2031, removing a near-term refinancing overhang.

Three things temper the recovery case. First, distribution expense has risen from 10.6% of sales in FY2025 to 11.6% for nine months FY2026, including roughly $5.0M of incremental Q3 fuel and freight cost excluding surcharge collections. Annualized crudely, that offsets roughly three-quarters of the entire $25M Apollo target. The cause is a supply-driven contraction in refrigerated truckload capacity — non-domiciled CDL restrictions effective March 16, 2026, tighter cabotage enforcement, carrier exits — which is external to JJSF and does not self-correct with volumes. Second, Retail Supermarket operating income fell 68.5% to $3.4M on exactly flat sales, with high-single-digit negative net pricing in Q3 and a $2M increase in slotting fees; whether that is a front-loaded innovation J-curve or brand erosion paying rent is genuinely unresolved. Third, the frozen-beverage service line declined 11% for nine months because customers are insourcing dispenser maintenance — the one revenue stream that most directly underpins the switching cost protecting syrup volumes across ~132,000 machines.

The rating is HOLD, unchanged for a fourth consecutive review, but the reasoning has shifted. The prior HOLDs reflected a business whose trajectory was unclear at a price in the $70s. The August 5 print resolved part of the uncertainty in the company's favor — and the shares rose 13% from the pre-print level and 29% from the May 12 low of $68.87 to capture it. At $89.02 the stock trades at roughly 20.9x TTM adjusted EPS of ~$4.26 and 9.9x lease-inclusive EV/adjusted EBITDA, against a base case of $97 (+9.0%), a bull case of $118 (+32.6%) and a bear case of $70 (-21.4%). Adding the 3.6% dividend, probability-weighted expected return is roughly 9-10% over twelve months, with meaningfully asymmetric downside if the freight offset persists and Retail does not recover. Existing holders should stay; new capital has no urgent entry signal here.

## Company Overview

J&J Snack Foods manufactures and markets niche frozen and refrigerated snacks and frozen beverages from Mount Laurel, New Jersey. FY2025 revenue of $1,583.2M split across three segments: Food Service ($1,001.4M, 63%), Frozen Beverages ($368.1M, 23%) and Retail Supermarket ($213.8M, 14%). Within Food Service, bakery was the largest line at $405.9M, followed by soft pretzels $230.1M, frozen novelties $149.9M, churros $97.9M and handhelds $92.0M.

Two structural features separate JJSF from a generic packaged-food company. The first is a service-and-equipment installed base: the company owns roughly 24,000 frozen-beverage dispensers and provides managed service and/or product to approximately 132,000 company- and customer-owned dispensers, supported by 172 company-managed warehouse and distribution locations across 45 states, Mexico and Canada. Equipment sales plus repair-and-maintenance service were 9% of FY2025 revenue. JJSF is paid both for syrup and, separately, for maintaining machines — including machines the customer owns. Syrup is bought primarily from Coca-Cola, Keurig Dr Pepper, PepsiCo and Jogue; those are suppliers, not customers.

The second is niche positioning rather than scale. JJSF holds high share of small pools — soft pretzels (SUPERPRETZEL, described as the #1 brand), churros, funnel cake, beaded ice cream (DIPPIN' DOTS), biscuits — categories too small to attract a Mondelez or General Mills. The 10-K concedes many competitors are substantially larger and specifically flags "one large retail chain which uses its own frozen beverage brand." Foreign sales were $69.6M, 4.4% of revenue; this is functionally a domestic U.S. business with ~4,600 employees, ~1,400 of whom are covered by collective bargaining.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was the trough.** Operating income fell 28.3% to $84.3M (5.3% margin) on 0.5% revenue growth, driven by record cocoa costs concentrated in the first half, a 14.4% churro decline as the company lapped a limited-time-offer with one major fast-food customer, and Mexican peso weakness in Frozen Beverages. Reported results absorbed $24.1M of plant-closure expense and $2.3M of intangible impairment, partly offset by a $10.6M insurance gain. Diluted EPS was $3.36 versus $4.45.

**FY2026 nine months show margin repair on a smaller base.** Revenue $1,114.6M (-5.0%), gross margin 31.1% (+210bp), GAAP operating income $48.7M versus $72.9M, adjusted operating income $65.7M versus $70.5M, adjusted EBITDA $123.1M versus $123.5M, adjusted diluted EPS $2.65 versus $2.68. Adjusted EBITDA margin therefore expanded from 10.53% to 11.04% on 5% less revenue. Q3 was the cleanest quarter: gross margin 35.5% (+240bp), adjusted EPS $1.96 against a non-GAAP consensus reported variously at $1.81 (Zacks) and $1.70 (TipRanks) — an 8-15% beat depending on compiler — with revenue of $426.0M modestly missing a ~$429M estimate. GAAP diluted EPS fell 16.8% to $1.88, so the beat is entirely a non-GAAP construct; the two bases should not be blended.

**Segment mechanics identify where value moves.** Food Service: sales -7.3% to $688.1M, operating income +11.0% to $49.0M, margin 5.95% → 7.13%. Frozen Beverages: sales -1.7% to $264.0M, operating income +1.9% to $31.5M, margin 11.52% → 11.93%, with product sales +6% (gallons +2%) offsetting machine sales -17% and service -11%. Retail Supermarket: sales flat at $162.4M, operating income -68.5% to $3.4M, margin 6.70% → 2.11%. General corporate expense rose 13.9% to $25.3M while consolidated revenue fell 5.0%; part is identified non-recurring legal ($1.4M) and restructuring ($1.5M), but not all of the increase is bridged in the filings.

**The revenue decline is mostly deliberate, though not verifiably so in full.** Management stated that anticipated bakery reductions accounted for approximately $16.0M of the $22.9M Q3 Food Service decline — roughly 57% of the consolidated Q3 shortfall. Nine-month bakery sales fell 17%. Pro-rating FY2025's $405.9M bakery base and applying that decline implies roughly $50-52M of the $58.4M nine-month consolidated revenue decline is bakery, an approximation since nine-month bakery dollars are not disclosed. Supporting context: the FY2025 10-K states the company bid on but did not retain certain seasonal pie business because of its "declining margin profile," and the Atlanta bakery/biscuit plant closed in September 2025. The Food Service margin response is consistent with exited volume earning below the corporate average. What the filings do not do is split voluntary exit from lost bids, and Food Service handhelds (-18%) and cookies are declining for reasons management attributes to demand rather than portfolio choice.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** At June 27, 2026: cash $63.1M (from $105.9M), AR $210.5M (from $184.1M), inventories $171.4M, total assets $1,355.9M, long-term debt $28.0M (from zero), operating lease liabilities $162.3M, equity $892.1M. Net cash is $33.5M ($63.1M less $28.0M revolver and $1.58M finance leases). Excluding operating leases there is no meaningful financial leverage; recomputed Altman Z on the June balance sheet with TTM flows is approximately 4.50, comfortably in the safe zone. An automated screen showing Z ≈ 2.81, operating margin of -0.0% and D/E of 1.43 is mis-tagged — actual FY2025 operating margin was 5.33% and total liabilities/equity 0.43.

Nine-month operating cash flow was $100.4M versus $98.7M despite net earnings falling from $54.2M to $37.9M, helped by non-cash closure charges ($4.5M) and the reversal of a prior-year inventory build. Capex fell to $53.3M from $61.3M, giving nine-month free cash flow of $47.2M versus $37.4M. Receivables consumed $26.0M versus $16.5M, and DSO on Q3 revenue works out to roughly 45 days versus 41 at fiscal year-end. Some is normal summer seasonality, but AR grew on falling sales and the direction warrants monitoring.

**Trailing-twelve-month figures** (Q4 FY25 plus 9M FY26): revenue $1,524.8M; GAAP operating income $60.2M; GAAP net earnings $49.3M; adjusted EBITDA $180.5M; operating cash flow $166.9M; capex $74.9M; free cash flow $92.0M.

**Capital allocation is the most consequential line.** Nine-month buybacks of $74.7M (854,208 shares at an $86.66 average) plus $45.8M of dividends totaled $120.5M against $47.2M of free cash flow. The $73.3M gap was funded by a $42.8M cash decline and a $28.0M net revolver draw. Share count fell 4.18%, from 19,440,000 to 18,628,000. Q3 purchases averaged $73.09 in late April/May and $76.25 in late May/June. FY2025 repurchases were only 66,776 shares at a $119.80 average — the pace accelerated after the price collapsed, which is the correct direction and a rare pattern among small-cap staples. The annualized dividend of ~$59.6M consumes roughly 65% of TTM FCF before any buyback, so the combined program is currently deficit-funded. Q4 is seasonally cash-generative (derived Q4 FY2025 operating cash flow was $66.4M), so the gap should narrow materially by fiscal year-end and the revolver draw could be repaid.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is negative and will remain so for FY2026: nine months at -5.0% with Q4 guided qualitatively to an "improving sales environment." Management states it expects a "return to top-line growth in fiscal 2027." No numeric revenue, EPS or margin guidance was provided, and the earnings-call transcript was not available, so tone is inferred from the press release alone.

The growth case is therefore an earnings-recovery case, not a revenue case, and it rests on four levers:

1. **Project Apollo run-rate.** The target moved from roughly $20M of run-rate operating income to $25M for the full program, with plant consolidation accounting for $20M and full benefit beginning in fiscal 2027. Four plants closed (Holly Ridge NC, Atlanta GA, Colton CA, New York NY soft-pretzel manufacturing), three regional DCs opened, six new production lines added, and the supply-chain transformation described as complete. Total closure charges were $24.1M in Q4 FY2025 plus $10.7M in nine-month FY2026, of which $6.2M was cash; the closure and severance liabilities have been drawn to essentially zero.

2. **Freight normalization.** This is the swing factor. Q3 fuel and freight cost roughly $5.0M more year over year excluding surcharge collections. A $5M quarterly delta annualizes to $19-20M against a $25M program — meaning three-quarters to four-fifths of the Apollo benefit is presently offset. Contract freight resets annually and surcharge recovery lags, so a single quarter's delta need not persist. But the driver is regulatory: non-domiciled CDL restrictions effective March 16, 2026 affecting roughly 194,000 licenses, tighter cabotage enforcement, and carrier exits, with refrigerated capacity tightening specifically (C.H. Robinson raised its 2026 reefer cost-per-mile forecast). JJSF's portfolio is overwhelmingly frozen and refrigerated, so it absorbs more of this than a shelf-stable peer. Both paths — multi-year regulatory tightness, or 2027 normalization releasing the full $25M — are live.

3. **Retail Supermarket recovery.** A 459bp nine-month margin loss on $162.4M of revenue is roughly $7.5M of forgone operating income, offsetting a third of Apollo. Slotting fees are typically front-loaded, so if the recent innovation (the Dippin' Dots sundae launch, ~$2.9M of nine-month new-product sales) achieves velocity, FY2027 keeps the volume without the fee. If it does not, this is a brand paying rent for shelf space.

4. **Product mix.** Frozen-beverage product sales +6% on +2% gallons, pretzels and churros stabilizing, bakery shrinking. The exited volume earned below average, so each dollar of revenue lost is worth less than each dollar retained — visible in the Food Service margin gain.

The counterweight to all four is structural demand risk newly emphasized in the FY2025 10-K: GLP-1 weight-loss drugs named explicitly against indulgent snack demand, and federal/state action on synthetic dyes, preservatives and front-of-package warnings. Frozen novelties and frozen beverages — roughly 30% of revenue, including ICEE, SLUSH PUPPIE, LUIGI'S, WHOLE FRUIT and DIPPIN' DOTS — sit near the center of the dye-restriction target, creating reformulation cost and retailer-delisting risk.

Earnings surprise history is mixed: two beats and two misses across four quarters, with the September 2025 quarter beating 30.2% and the December and March quarters missing 7.3% and 4.4%. Visibility on quarterly EPS is low; the seasonal skew (summer is the high season) amplifies estimate error.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantages are identifiable in the filings. The frozen-beverage installed base — ~24,000 owned dispensers, ~132,000 serviced, 172 company-managed locations across 45 states — is genuinely hard to replicate and generates a 21.4% Q3 segment operating margin. Institutional channel relationships in schools, theaters, stadiums and convenience stores put JJSF products in venues where they are often the only option. Brand recognition in categories too small for scale players to attack (SUPERPRETZEL, ICEE, DIPPIN' DOTS) supports pricing in Food Service.

The disadvantages are equally documented. Top-10 customer concentration was 46% of FY2025 sales, up from 45% and 43% in the two prior years, with the largest at 10% and no long-term contracts. Five of the top ten are food distributors, so the identity and stability of true end demand is partly opaque. The churro episode illustrates the exposure: FY2025 churro sales fell 14.4% purely from lapping one customer's limited-time offer. In retail grocery, high-single-digit negative net pricing in Q3 is direct evidence of no pricing power. Distribution cost intensity of 11.6% of sales is a structural disadvantage in a reefer inflation cycle. And the 10-K's reference to "one large retail chain which uses its own frozen beverage brand" describes a customer-turned-competitor in the highest-margin segment.

The trajectory question matters more than the level, and the answer is mixed. Food Service margin improved 118bp on lower volume, and frozen-beverage product volume grew — both consistent with a strengthening position in the core. Against that, the 11% nine-month decline in frozen-beverage service revenue is attributed to customers insourcing maintenance, and that line is the operational glue keeping JJSF inside machines it does not own. If insourcing spreads, JJSF loses recurring service revenue and a piece of the switching cost protecting syrup volumes. The company disclosed the cause but not the affected account base, so durability is unresolved. It may be one or two large accounts rebuilding field teams during a period of tight labor, and reversible. It is the most important competitive datapoint to have emerged this cycle.

Adjusted EBITDA margin progression frames the whole recovery: FY2023 11.65%, FY2024 12.71%, FY2025 11.42%, TTM 11.84%. The company gave back the FY2024 gain and is roughly halfway back. FY2024's $200.1M of adjusted EBITDA on $1,574.8M of sales is the benchmark against which Apollo should be judged.

## Risk Assessment

**Freight cost persistence.** Distribution expense at 11.6% of sales has fully reversed the FY2025 improvement to 10.6%. The Q3 delta of ~$5.0M, annualized, offsets 75-80% of the $25M Apollo target. The driver is regulatory supply contraction in refrigerated trucking, which does not correct with volume. This is the single largest determinant of whether FY2027 earnings power is $5.00+ or closer to $4.50.

**Retail Supermarket economics and goodwill headroom.** Operating income -68.5% on flat sales, with a $2M slotting-fee increase and trade-spending liabilities up $5.9M to $28.2M. Segment assets are only $31.2M with zero nine-month capex, so ROIC damage is contained. But the FY2025 10-K's disclosure that every reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by at least 50% is as of September 27, 2025 and is not re-tested at interim dates. With Retail operating income down two-thirds since, the Retail reporting unit's headroom at the FY2026 annual test could be materially lower. Indefinite-lived intangibles include $11.2M of Retail trade names within ~$142.0M total. This is a watch item, not a current impairment indicator.

**Customer concentration is worsening.** 43% → 45% → 46% top-10, largest at 10%, no long-term contracts. A shifting product mix does not constitute customer diversification, and the disclosed measure moved the wrong way.

**Regulatory demand risk on ~30% of revenue.** GLP-1 drugs and synthetic-dye/front-of-pack action are named risks in the FY2025 10-K bearing directly on frozen novelties and frozen beverages. Reformulation cost and retailer delisting are both plausible transmission channels; neither is quantified.

**Commodity pass-through lag.** Forward purchasing extends only 1-12 months with $133M of purchase commitments at FY-end. Input shocks flow through with a two-to-three-quarter lag, as the FY2025 cocoa episode demonstrated.

**Governance and related-party dependency.** Director Sidney R. Brown is CEO and an owner of NFI Industries, which received $50.8M in nine-month FY2026 payments (~$68M annualized, ~4.4% of revenue and ~39% of annualized distribution expense). NFI operates and labor-manages all three regional distribution centers, and an NFI subsidiary built and owns the Terrell, Texas DC that JJSF leases ($25.7M ROU remaining). Related-party trade payables rose from $0.6M to $4.8M. Using a specialist 3PL for DC labor is standard practice and may be cheaper than in-housing; no self-dealing is inferred. But the supply-chain transformation on which the entire Apollo thesis rests is built on infrastructure JJSF does not own and labor it does not employ, with a sitting director on the other side.

**Anti-takeover architecture removes the valuation floor.** New Jersey Shareholders' Protection Act, classified board, directors removable only for cause, a "Voting Threshold" limiting share holding and voting, special "Experienced Directors" rights on hostile board change, no shareholder right to call special meetings or act by written consent, and exclusive New Jersey forum. At 9.9x lease-inclusive EV/EBITDA with net cash and strong niche brands, JJSF would otherwise be an obvious strategic or sponsor target. Investors should not underwrite a takeout scenario.

**Unresolved items that should not be modeled.** An advisory jury returned a verdict in the company's favor on a long-standing matter in Q3 FY2026; final judgment is not entered and no gain or receivable was recognized. The counterparty and amount are undisclosed. Separately, $17.1M of Holly Ridge insurance proceeds were received in August 2026 (cumulative proceeds ~$51.8M against $17.5M of directly recovered costs), likely producing a material Q4 GAAP gain — but the company's own non-GAAP policy excludes PP&E insurance gains from adjusted EPS and adjusted EBITDA, so this is a cash and book-equity event rather than an earnings-power event. General counsel Michael A. Pollner's resignation is reported externally but unverified against the June 10, 2026 8-K, which was not retrievable.

**Macro overlay.** [unverified — no macro data in sources] JJSF's institutional channels (schools, theaters, stadiums, convenience) are more defensive than pure discretionary retail, but Retail Supermarket's negative net pricing is already consistent with a trade-down environment.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 43 contracts across three expirations — so signal quality is low and directional inference should be limited. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.2% against 35.0% one-year realized is fair-value pricing with no volatility risk premium worth harvesting in either direction. The term structure is in backwardation (36.2% at eight days versus 28.9% at 36 days), which typically indicates near-term event pricing; no scheduled catalyst sits in that window — Q4 FY2026 results are due in late November — so this most likely reflects post-earnings residual and thin-chain mechanics rather than informed positioning. Put/call skew of 1.0% is balanced. Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.07 is extremely call-tilted but on negligible absolute open interest. The prior snapshot's 419.9% ATM IV reading is a data artifact and carries no information.

The more informative market-structure datapoint is short interest: 15.6% of float, 5.1 days to cover, against a two-session post-earnings advance on 2.7x and 1.3x average volume. Elevated short positioning plus a sharp print-driven rally implies a meaningful covering component in the recent move. That neither validates nor invalidates the fundamental case; it argues against treating the 29% rally off the low as a clean fundamental re-rating, and it leaves an upside tail if FY2027 guidance in November confirms the recovery.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+32.6%)

Project Apollo delivers its full $25M of run-rate operating income in FY2027 as reefer capacity normalizes and the ~$19-20M annualized freight headwind reverses. Food Service margin, already up 118bp on lower volume, continues to expand as the low-margin bakery exit completes and the pretzel/churro core returns to growth. Retail Supermarket's slotting investment converts to velocity, recovering most of the $7.5M of forgone operating income without repeating the fee. Frozen-beverage product volume compounds at 2-6% while the machine-sales comp normalizes and service insourcing proves account-specific. Revenue returns to ~$1,570M and adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$205M, back above the FY2024 peak of $200.1M on a better-mix revenue base. Meanwhile the buyback continues at the demonstrated pace — 4.18% of shares retired in nine months at an $86.66 average — compounding per-share earnings against a 3.6% dividend. Adjusted EPS reaches $5.50-5.75; at 11.0x lease-inclusive EV/EBITDA the equity is worth ~$118, matching the analyst high target. The Q4 insurance gain and any judgment from the favorable advisory verdict add unmodeled cash.

### Bear Case — $70 (-21.4%)

The word "anticipated" in management's bakery commentary is doing more work than the filings support. A meaningful share of the ~$50M nine-month bakery decline, the 18% handheld decline and the softness in cookies is lost business rather than shed business, and FY2027 revenue falls another 3-4% instead of growing. Reefer capacity tightness proves multi-year because its cause is regulatory, permanently netting Apollo down to single-digit millions. Retail Supermarket's 459bp margin loss is brand erosion, not a J-curve, and the segment's goodwill and trade-name headroom compresses enough to force a write-down at the FY2026 test. Frozen-beverage service insourcing spreads, taking syrup volumes with it as the switching cost erodes. Customer concentration at 46% with no contracts produces another churro-style single-customer air pocket. GLP-1 penetration and state dye restrictions begin measurably suppressing frozen-novelty demand. Adjusted EBITDA settles near $165M, the multiple compresses to 8.5-9.0x, and the stock retests the May low of $68.87. The dividend at 65% of TTM FCF and a deficit-funded return program force the buyback to stop, removing the per-share offset.

### What Would Settle It

The decisive evidence arrives in two pieces. First, the Q4 FY2026 report in late November: whether revenue actually improved as guided, whether distribution expense as a percent of sales fell from 11.6%, and whether Retail Supermarket operating income recovered off the $3.4M nine-month base. Second, any numeric FY2027 guidance, which the company has so far withheld. Until both land, the range between $70 and $118 is genuinely wide, and $89.02 sits close to the center of it.

1. Q4 FY2026 distribution expense falls below 11.0% of sales, or management quantifies net Apollo flow-through of $15M or more for FY2027 after freight. This is the single largest swing factor in FY2027 earnings power.

## Conclusion

The August 5 print did what the prior three HOLD reviews were waiting for: it demonstrated in reported numbers that the restructuring converts. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA is flat on 5% lower revenue, gross margin is up 210bp, Food Service operating income grew 11% on a 7.3% sales decline, the Apollo target was raised to $25M, and the revolver was extended to 2031 on a balance sheet carrying $33.5M of net cash. Management is retiring shares at a 4.18%-per-nine-months pace at prices in the $73-87 range after buying almost nothing at $119.80 a year earlier — the correct pattern, and the strongest single management signal in the file.

The offsetting facts are equally concrete. Reefer freight inflation is currently absorbing roughly three-quarters of the Apollo benefit and its cause is regulatory rather than cyclical. Retail Supermarket operating income has fallen 68.5% on flat sales with promotional spend rising and its goodwill headroom untested since September 2025. Frozen-beverage service revenue is declining because customers are insourcing the maintenance that anchors JJSF inside 132,000 dispensers. Customer concentration is worsening to 46% with no long-term contracts. And the voluntary-versus-involuntary split of a ~$50M bakery revenue decline — the pivot on which the "return to growth in fiscal 2027" claim rests — is not disclosed.

At $89.02 the stock has risen 29% from the May low and 13% since the print, taking it to roughly 20.9x TTM adjusted EPS, 34.4x GAAP, and 9.9x lease-inclusive EV/adjusted EBITDA against a $97 base case. Part of that move reflects covering against 15.6% short interest rather than fresh conviction. The business has improved and the price has improved with it, leaving expected risk-adjusted return close to fair: roughly 9-10% probability-weighted total return against 21% downside if the freight offset persists and Retail does not recover. Overall score 5.53, stabilizing after three declining reviews — financial health improved on the revolver extension and Apollo raise, valuation modestly worse on the 19% price move since the last review, competitive position slightly weaker on service insourcing.

**HOLD.** Existing owners are compensated by a 3.6% dividend covered at 65% of free cash flow and a shrinking share count while the FY2027 evidence accumulates. New capital should wait for the late-November Q4 report to quantify net Apollo flow-through and Retail's trajectory, or for a re-entry in the mid-$70s where management itself was buying.
