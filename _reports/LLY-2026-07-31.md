## Executive Summary

Lilly has compounded revenue from $28.5B in FY2022 to $65.2B in FY2025 and $19.8B in the first quarter of 2026 alone, a 55.5% year-over-year gain. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $72.2B, net income $25.3B, and diluted EPS $28.15. Consensus expects $34.51 in CY2026 and $45.06 in CY2027, putting the shares at 33.5x and 25.6x respectively against a peer-group median trailing P/E of 20.0x for companies growing at low-single digits. Four consecutive earnings beats averaging roughly 19% support confidence in the forward numbers. On growth-adjusted terms the multiple is defensible: PEG on next-year consensus earnings growth of 30.6% is approximately 1.1.

What has changed is the composition of the growth. FY2025's revenue bridge was volume +50%, price −6%, FX +1%. Q1 2026 was volume +65%, price **−13%**, FX +4%, with ex-U.S. realized price down 25% on Mounjaro's addition to China's National Reimbursed Drug List and U.S. price down 7%. Gross margin, which expanded from 81.3% to 83.0% in FY2025, fell to 81.9% in Q1 2026 from 82.5%, attributed in the 10-Q to lower realized prices. The strategic question the market cares most about has begun resolving unfavorably: Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide launched roughly three months ahead of Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron, FDA-approved April 2026), won European approval first, and, per a JPMorgan note relayed through the Wall Street Journal in mid-July, was still leading Foundayo three months after Lilly's launch. Foundayo also carries a Chugai royalty in the mid-single to low-teens percentage range on worldwide net sales, so oral share, when it comes, arrives at structurally lower gross margin than tirzepatide.

We move to HOLD. The stock is essentially flat since our July 19 note (−2%) and up 52% over twelve months, and the revenue trajectory has not deteriorated. The downgrade reflects four specific items: realized-price erosion now visible in gross margin rather than only in the revenue bridge; the oral GLP-1 share evidence running against Lilly; business-development commitments escalating from under $3B in February to roughly $15B by mid-July against FY2025 free cash flow of $9.0B; and a base case of about $1,300 (analyst median; 29x CY2027 consensus) that offers ~13% upside against a bear case near $850. With near-term implied volatility at 61.4% versus 38.4% one-year realized and an 8.4% implied move into the August 5 second-quarter report, this is not the point at which to add.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a single-segment human pharmaceutical company, incorporated in Indiana in 1901, selling in roughly 90 countries with about 50,000 employees, including approximately 12,000 in R&D. Management reports one operating segment; the chief operating decision maker uses consolidated net income, with R&D disaggregated only into early-stage and late-stage buckets.

Four franchises: Cardiometabolic Health (Mounjaro, Zepbound, Trulicity, Jardiance through the Boehringer Ingelheim collaboration, insulins), Oncology (Verzenio, Jaypirca, Retevmo, Inluriyo, Cyramza, Erbitux, Tyvyt in China), Immunology (Taltz, Omvoh, Ebglyss, Olumiant), and Neuroscience (Kisunla, Emgality).

The defining structural fact is concentration. Mounjaro and Zepbound were 56% of FY2025 revenue and **65% of Q1 2026 revenue** ($8,662M + $4,160M of $19,799M). Cardiometabolic Health overall was $15,760M, or 79.6% of Q1 revenue. Six products together were 82% of FY2025 revenue. At the channel level, McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health each individually exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Two counterparty details matter for margin analysis and are frequently mis-stated. Boehringer Ingelheim owns the Jardiance family; Lilly receives a share of net sales, and that arrangement runs only through December 31, 2028 in the most significant markets. Chugai is the licensor of orforglipron and receives royalties from Lilly, not the reverse.

The principal asset is the tirzepatide compound patent: U.S. 2036, major European countries 2037, Japan 2040. U.S. regulatory data protection expires in 2027, but the compound patent is the binding constraint. A product at a greater-than-$36B annualized run rate carries a decade of composition-of-matter protection.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.**

| 2022 | $28,541M | — | $6,245M | 21.9% | — |
| 2023 | $34,124M | +19.6% | $5,240M | 15.4% | — |
| 2024 | $45,043M | +32.0% | $10,590M | 23.5% | $11.71 |
| 2025 | $65,179M | +44.7% | $20,640M | 31.7% | $22.95 |
| Q1 2026 | $19,799M | +55.5% | $7,396M | 37.4% | $8.26 |

FY2025 non-GAAP adjusted EPS was $27.06 versus GAAP $22.95, a $4.11 gap driven principally by acquired in-process R&D ($2,910M in FY2025, $3,280M in FY2024) which is expensed rather than capitalized. Any consensus comparison must hold basis constant.

Two Q1 optics adjustments deserve attention. The 10-Q attributes part of both Mounjaro's and Zepbound's U.S. results to "a favorable one-time adjustment to estimates for rebates and discounts," and separately discloses that prior-period true-ups to U.S. rebate and return estimates were under 3% of U.S. revenue in Q1 2026 versus under 1% in Q1 2025. On $12,119M of U.S. revenue, that band tops out near $360M. Separately, a one-time Jardiance collaboration benefit was $250M in Q1 2026 versus $370M in Q1 2025. The 37.4% Q1 net margin should not be treated as a run-rate baseline, and the underlying U.S. price decline was likely worse than the reported −7%. The company notes in the 10-K that such adjustments were below 2% of consolidated revenue in each of 2024 and 2025, so a sub-3% U.S. figure is within a normal band for a business whose channel mix is shifting rapidly toward cash-pay and LillyDirect.

**Cash conversion is the weak link.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $16,813M against net income of $20,640M — OCF below net income. The cause is on the balance sheet: trade receivables rose from $11,006M to $17,760M (+$6.75B) and inventories from $7,589M to $13,744M (+$6.16B). Capex of $7,841M produced free cash flow of $8,972M, a 13.8% FCF margin against a 31.7% net margin. At a ~$1.03T market capitalization that is a 0.87% FCF yield.

The most plausible reading is a growth signature: pre-building inventory into a demand ramp while financing 45% revenue expansion. Q1 2026 supports that — OCF tripled to $5,333M from $1,666M while inventories rose only $785M to $14,529M against 56% revenue growth. The alternative the 10-K itself flags is "excess inventory, and related financial charges" and "impairment of inventories" if demand is overestimated. Receivables growing 61% against 45% revenue growth in FY2025 is worth monitoring in the Q2 statements.

We do not annualize Q1 free cash flow ($5,333M OCF less $2,326M capex ≈ $3.0B). Q1 contained a $1,664M working-capital outflow, roughly $1.0B of employee-compensation liability drawdown (typical Q1 bonus payment), and a $1.1B decline in prepaid taxes. A normalized anchor is FY2025's $9.0B growing with EBIT but restrained by a capex program management describes as "meaningfully higher in the near term."

**Leverage and solvency.** Total debt moved $33.6B (12/31/24) → $42.5B (12/31/25) → $43.4B (3/31/26), with $6,461M of long-term debt issued in Q1 2026 alone. Net debt is approximately $35.0B at 3/31/26. Against FY2025 EBIT of roughly $26.9B and EBITDA near $28.9B, net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.0–1.1x and interest coverage is very high. Current ratio 1.58. There is no realistic near-term solvency question.

One nuance worth stating plainly, because automated screens get it wrong: book equity of $26,535M against $42,503M of debt is thin, a legacy of sustained buybacks and dividends, producing an equity multiplier of 4.24x (assets/equity) that is frequently mislabeled as debt/equity. Actual total debt/equity is 1.60x. Distress-score readings in the low single digits for this company are artifacts of an income statement that contains no "operating income" line; FY2025 pretax income was $25,731M. Recalculated properly, credit metrics are comfortable. The honest caveat is that most of that comfort comes from the market-capitalization term; on book capital alone, Lilly's balance sheet looks ordinary rather than fortress-like. Also note that the disclosed fair value of long-term debt ($38,233M) sits below carrying value ($41,595M), meaning the fixed-rate stack was issued below current market rates, an economic benefit invisible in GAAP equity.

**Capital allocation.** Dividends rose from $5.20/share (2024) to $6.00 (2025) to an indicated $6.92 for 2026 — approximately $6.2B annualized, or 69% of FY2025 free cash flow before buybacks and business development. Buybacks were $4.1B in FY2025 and $2.3B in Q1 2026 at an average of $987.15/share (January $1,024.22, February $1,029.80, March $947.93), all below the current price; $8.6B remains of the $15.0B December 2024 authorization. Diluted weighted-average shares fell from 900.6M to 895.9M, a 0.5% reduction, modestly accretive rather than an EPS driver.

Business development is the item that changed. Pending acquisition commitments went from "less than $3 billion" in the February 10-K to "up to approximately $12 billion" in the April 10-Q, and AtaiBeckley was announced July 16 at $2.8B upfront plus up to $1B in milestones, after the balance-sheet date and therefore incremental. Combined near-term BD exposure is on the order of $15B against $9.0B of FY2025 FCF, rising capex, $6.2B of dividends, and continuing repurchases. The debt trajectory is the arithmetic consequence.

**Financial Health: 7.2.** Earnings power, leverage against EBITDA, and coverage are strong. Cash conversion, the 0.87% FCF yield, the rising debt balance, the ~$10B take-or-pay contract-manufacturing contingency, and roughly $15B of near-term deal commitments keep this out of the 8+ range.

## Growth Analysis

**What is working.** Volume growth of 65% in Q1 2026 is extraordinary for a company of this size. Ex-U.S. revenue grew from $4,239M to $7,680M (+81%), with Rest-of-World nearly tripling from $997M to $2,771M. Foundayo (orforglipron) was approved in April 2026 for obesity via the FDA's Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pathway, and Phase 3 trials for orforglipron in type 2 diabetes and retatrutide in type 2 diabetes both met primary endpoints. New Phase 3 starts include eloralintide (obesity add-on, OSA, osteoarthritis pain), baricitinib in type 1 diabetes, and brenipatide in major depressive disorder. R&D was $13,337M in FY2025 (20.5% of revenue), up 21%, and $3,510M in Q1 (up 28%).

Externally reported FY2026 guidance of $82–85B, which we could not verify in an SEC filing, implies roughly 28% growth off $65.2B at the midpoint. Estimate revisions for next year are +7.0% over 90 days and +1.3% over 30 days, with 25 analysts contributing. The earnings-surprise record is 4-for-4: +14.5%, +22.0%, +11.9%, +27.0%.

**What is constraining.** Price is now the dominant variable. Consolidated realized price fell 13% in Q1, ex-U.S. 25%, U.S. 7% (flattered by rebate true-ups). China's NRDL inclusion is the template for international expansion: very large volume for very large price concessions, which grows revenue while compressing gross margin.

The U.S. pricing agreements, finalized in Q1 2026, commit Lilly to lower Medicaid and certain other U.S. prices, to launch new medicines with "a more balanced pricing approach across developed nations" (importing international reference pricing into future launches, structurally capping Foundayo's U.S. premium), and to a Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program running **July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027**. The 10-Q states directly that "the uptake from this expanded access is unknown" and that Lilly "continue[s] to engage with CMS on long-term Medicare access." The concession is contractual and immediate; the volume offset is unquantified and expires in eighteen months.

The non-incretin base is shrinking rather than stable. Trulicity loses U.S. compound patent and biologics data protection in 2027 and is already declining ($1,095M → $919M in Q1, −16%). Cyramza U.S. protection expires in 2026. HHS selected Trulicity and Verzenio for government-set prices effective 2028 — Verzenio generated $5,723M in FY2025 and $1,302M in Q1, with U.S. compound protection to 2031, so the IRA compresses roughly three years of protected life. The Jardiance collaboration ends December 31, 2028 in major markets.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** The current-quarter consensus was cut 25.8% (from $8.82 to $6.55) over 30 days while the next quarter rose 0.7%, next year rose 1.3%, and the current year fell 4.7% (but is up 0.8% over 90 days). That pattern — a single quarter reset with the out-years intact or higher — is most consistent with an accounting charge landing in Q2, plausibly acquired IPR&D from deals closing in the quarter and/or the undisclosed Emgality charge, rather than demand deterioration. We treat it as a basis/charge item requiring confirmation on August 5 rather than a growth warning. It does mean the strong revision-driven buy signal (>+30% over 90 days) is not present here.

**Growth Prospects: 8.0.** Volume growth and pipeline breadth are best-in-class; the deceleration from 55% toward roughly 28% and the shift of the growth mix toward price-concessionary channels prevent a higher mark.

Lilly trades at roughly double the peer median trailing multiple and a 68% premium on CY2026 forward earnings. That premium is justified on growth arithmetic — the peer set is growing low-single-digit revenue while Lilly grew 44.7% and 55.5% in the last two reported periods — but the premium is now large enough that it prices continued execution rather than offering a discount for it.

Twenty-eight to thirty times is a reasonable multiple for a company we expect to be growing high-teens to low-twenties percent beyond 2027 with composition-of-matter protection to 2036. That brackets the analyst median target of $1,300 and mean of $1,277, roughly 11–13% above spot. The analyst high is $1,600 and the low $850; JPMorgan's $1,400 was described as well above consensus when published July 9.

Estimate revisions are stable-to-positive on the out-years, and analyst targets on a stock at 33.5x forward with rising estimates are more likely to be revised up than to act as a ceiling. But the FCF yield of 0.87% and the dividend yield of 0.60% provide no valuation floor, and the multiple is high enough that any of the identified negatives — a Medicare Bridge with weak uptake, a second quarter of gross-margin compression, or a durable Novo lead in oral — compresses it toward the low-20s on CY2027 rather than the high-20s. Twenty-two times $38 (a modest miss scenario) is $836, close to the analyst low.

**Valuation: 5.5** (unchanged). Fairly valued on growth-adjusted terms, with roughly balanced-to-modestly-positive asymmetry rather than the mispricing that would support adding.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity market has moved from a supply-constrained duopoly, where both incumbents sold everything they could manufacture, to a contested oligopoly where the binding constraints are access, price, and share of new prescriptions.

**Where Lilly's position is strong.** Tirzepatide's U.S. 2036 compound patent on a >$36B run-rate product. Manufacturing scale as a moat — capex of $7.8B in FY2025 versus $5.1B in 2024, with new sites in North Carolina, Wisconsin, Indiana, Virginia, Texas, Alabama, Pennsylvania, Ireland, Germany, and the Netherlands. Owned-channel distribution through LillyDirect, described in the 10-K as "a growing portion of our business in 2025," plus direct-to-employer contracting and participation in a government direct-to-patient platform — all routes around PBM gatekeeping. And unusual mechanism breadth in the same space: retatrutide (triple agonist), eloralintide (amylin), efsitora (weekly insulin), lepodisiran and muvalaplin in Lp(a).

**Where the trajectory has turned.** On the oral axis, Novo is currently ahead: first to U.S. market by roughly three months, first to EU approval, reportedly past 3 million prescriptions on the Wegovy pill, and still leading Foundayo three months post-launch. Foundayo's small-molecule label with no food or water restrictions is a genuine product advantage over a constrained peptide, and the forthcoming type 2 diabetes indication (Phase 3 endpoint met) broadens the addressable base; neither has yet translated into share. This is the single highest-leverage unresolved question for the stock, and the available evidence is second- and third-hand: Lilly has disclosed no Foundayo revenue in any filing.

Three additional pressures. CVS Caremark stopped covering Zepbound as a preferred obesity medicine on some plans in July 2025, which the 10-K acknowledges "has negatively impacted access." Amgen's MariTide, a monthly-dosed candidate, is externally reported to be running six Phase 3 trials, attacking tirzepatide's convenience position. And the 10-K explicitly calls out "intensifying competition worldwide, including from China," where companies "are increasingly licensing products to multinational pharmaceutical companies… and competing directly in major markets," an unusual disclosure for a U.S. large-cap pharma.

There is also a structural asymmetry inside Lilly's own franchise: the 10-K notes that the IRA's nine-year timeline for small molecules approved under an NDA "reduces the attractiveness of investment in small molecule innovation." Foundayo is an NDA small molecule on that nine-year clock; tirzepatide is a peptide. And Foundayo carries the Chugai royalty. Oral share gains, when they come, are worth less per dollar of revenue than injectable share.

Concentration is worsening, not improving. Incretin share of revenue went from 56% to 65% in one quarter; distributor concentration is flat with three wholesalers each above 10%. The 2026 BD program (inflammation, gene editing, neuroscience, oncology, pain) is forward diversification with no revenue for years — BPL-003's Phase 3 data is not expected until roughly 2029. A broadening pipeline is not diversified revenue.

**Competitive Position: 7.4** (from 7.8). The level remains exceptional; the trajectory on oral share, payer access, and realized price is mildly negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-mechanism concentration.** Sixty-five percent of Q1 revenue derives from two brands of one molecule. The 10-K states that dependence on key products "exacerbates" the risk and could produce "significant and sudden declines or volatility in the trading price of our common stock and market capitalization." A tirzepatide safety signal, a quality event at a tirzepatide site, or a superior competing mechanism is the tail that matters most.

**Pricing architecture in flux.** Three simultaneous vectors: the finalized U.S. government agreements (lower Medicaid pricing, international reference pricing on new launches, an 18-month Medicare Bridge with unknown uptake); IRA selection of Trulicity and Verzenio for 2028 prices, with the 10-K stating Lilly "expect[s] additional of our significant products will be selected in future years" — Jardiance's announced IRA price was a 66% discount to 2023 list; and PBM formulary power as demonstrated by the CVS Caremark exclusion.

**Tariff-linked capex.** The 10-K discloses a three-year Section 232 tariff grace period conditional on meeting U.S. manufacturing investment commitments, which are not quantified. Part of the capex ramp is properly understood as the price of tariff exemption rather than purely return-seeking capacity. Lilly separately warns it "may not be able to share the burden of increased costs from tariffs… to any meaningful degree" and that it depends on China-based suppliers for chemical synthesis, reagents, and starting materials, with the BIOSECURE Act cited.

**Capacity asymmetry.** Under-build limits capitalizing on demand; over-build renders capacity "unnecessary" and triggers "contractual payment obligations, which may be significant," tied to roughly $10B of disclosed take-or-pay exposure. The $13B FY2025 working-capital build sits on the same fault line.

**Litigation, with movement.** In April 2026 the Federal Circuit reversed the district court's invalidity finding in the Emgality/Teva matter and remanded; Lilly recognized a charge in Q1 with no amount disclosed (Q1 "asset impairment, restructuring, and other special charges" rose from $35M to $279M, though attribution is inference). The AMP qui tam verdict was affirmed by the Seventh Circuit with a Supreme Court petition filed in March. Also live: 340B litigation, the Mosaic Health antitrust class action, the insulin pricing MDL, and Mounjaro/Trulicity/Zepbound product-liability MDLs covering alleged gastrointestinal injuries and NAION. Lilly notes it is "predominantly self-insured for litigation liability losses," a material qualifier when the products at issue generate more than $36B annually. Separately, Novo Nordisk sued Lilly on or about July 21 alleging false claims of superior efficacy in advertising, seeking a permanent injunction and corrective advertising; this is not yet reflected in any Lilly filing and is unquantified.

**Near-term event risk.** Second-quarter earnings are dated August 5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 61.4% against 38.4% one-year realized volatility — a 23-point spread at the 84th percentile of the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (61.4% near versus 37.5% at six months). The one-week tenor expires in one day with a 0.4% implied move and a −37.4% skew reading, which is same-day pinning mechanics and should be discarded. The informative tenor is the August 14 expiry: 54.7% IV, an **8.4% implied move**, and a put/call volume ratio of 3.58 — the market is pricing a substantial two-sided reaction to the August 5 print. Three- and six-month expiries show 11.0% and 17.7% implied moves with put/call volume ratios of 2.83 and 3.60.

Put/call open interest rose from 0.76 to 0.95 over twelve days while ATM IV went from 33.7% to 61.4% and the price fell 2.6%. Aggregate skew is reported as call-heavy at −4.3%, so directional inference is mixed; the reliable takeaway is that protection is being accumulated into the print and that volatility is expensive. Short interest is 1.1% of float with 3.3 days to cover — no crowded bearish positioning to squeeze.

For a fundamental holder, this argues for owning the position through the print rather than adding into rich vol. It prices the dispersion of outcomes around the growth thesis without contradicting it.

**Risk Factors: 5.2.** The stack is unusually specific and partly unquantified: 65% single-molecule dependence, an 18-month access mechanism with unknown uptake, IRA selections through 2028, self-insured product-liability MDLs on the flagship, a tariff grace period contingent on undisclosed investment commitments, and an undisclosed litigation charge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,600 (+38.5%)

Foundayo's no-food/no-water label and the pending type 2 diabetes indication reverse the early oral share gap; the three-month head start proves not to be durable in a market this large. The Medicare Bridge Program delivers volume that more than offsets the price concession, and CMS converts it into permanent coverage before December 2027, opening a previously closed population. Retatrutide and eloralintide Phase 3 data extend the franchise past tirzepatide's 2036 patent. Lilly beats CY2027 consensus by its four-quarter average — $45.06 becomes roughly $50 — and the multiple holds at 32x. Working-capital normalization converts a 31%+ net margin into 25%+ FCF margins, and the 0.87% FCF yield problem resolves. Guidance of $82–85B for 2026 proves conservative, as guidance has been in each of the last four quarters.

### Bear Case — $850 (−26.4%)

Realized price erosion continues to compound: consolidated price was −6% in FY2025 and −13% in Q1 2026, and each new international access expansion trades 20–25% price for volume. Gross margin, already down 60bp, continues sliding, and the Q1 rebate true-up reverses. Medicare Bridge uptake disappoints, the program lapses on December 31, 2027 without a successor, and Lilly is left holding contractual Medicaid concessions with no volume offset. Novo holds the oral segment, and Amgen's monthly MariTide erodes tirzepatide's convenience advantage. The $13B working-capital build becomes an inventory charge. Trulicity's 2027 LOE, Verzenio's 2028 IRA price, and the Jardiance collaboration's December 2028 expiry hit simultaneously while the ~$15B BD program has produced nothing revenue-generating. Twenty-two times a reduced $38 in CY2027 gives $836.

### Base Case — $1,300 (+12.6%)

Volume growth decelerates from 65% toward the high-twenties as the law of large numbers and price concessions bite; FY2026 lands within the reported $82–85B range; CY2027 EPS approximates consensus at $45; the multiple settles at 29x. Foundayo takes a credible second position in oral rather than leading it. Total return of roughly 13% including the 0.60% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** We see a positive twelve-month return path — approximately 13% to base case — but insufficient asymmetry to add at $1,155 into an 8.4% implied earnings move with IV 23 points above realized. The change from BUY reflects specific new evidence (Q1 gross-margin inflection, mid-July oral share reporting, BD commitments to ~$15B, and a base case now within 13% of spot) rather than the roughly flat price since July 19.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Q2 print (August 5) showing gross margin at or above 82.5% with U.S. realized price better than −7%**, demonstrating that the Q1 compression was mix/timing rather than structural. Confirm on a like-for-like basis (GAAP-to-GAAP or non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP) and check whether the current-quarter estimate cut from $8.82 to $6.55 was in fact charge-driven.
2. **Verified Foundayo prescription data showing it closing or reversing the gap to oral semaglutide** — script-level share, not commentary.
3. **Price below $1,000** (roughly 22x CY2027 consensus) with CY2027 estimates intact at $44+, which restores 30%+ upside to base case.
4. **Free cash flow inflection**: two consecutive quarters of FCF conversion above 60% of net income, taking the FCF yield above 1.5%.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A second consecutive quarter of gross-margin decline combined with a cut to FY2026 revenue guidance** below the low end of the reported $82–85B range.
2. **Medicare Bridge uptake disclosed as immaterial** through the Q3 or Q4 print, leaving the pricing concession without volume offset ahead of the December 31, 2027 expiry.
3. **An inventory or capacity impairment charge**, or receivables growth again exceeding revenue growth by more than 15 percentage points, indicating the $13B working-capital build is not demand-backed.
4. **A tirzepatide safety signal or a manufacturing/quality action at a tirzepatide site** — the step-function risk given 65% concentration.
5. **CY2027 consensus falling below $40** on demand rather than accounting grounds.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 5, 2026 (Q2 earnings) is the immediate checkpoint, with the Q3 print and the first meaningful Medicare Bridge uptake data (post-July 1, 2026 launch) as the substantive test. The Novo advertising litigation and the Emgality remand are monitorable but unquantified. Existing holders should carry the position; new capital should wait for the print or a better entry.

## Conclusion

Lilly is the highest-quality growth franchise in large-cap pharmaceuticals, with a decade of composition-of-matter protection on a product at a $36B-plus run rate, volume growth of 65% in the most recent quarter, and a pipeline (retatrutide, eloralintide, efsitora, Lp(a) assets) that plausibly extends the franchise beyond tirzepatide. Leverage is roughly 1.0x EBITDA, coverage is high, and management has repurchased stock in the high-$900s and raised the dividend 15%.

At 33.5x CY2026 and 25.6x CY2027 consensus, with a PEG near 1.1, the shares are not expensive against that growth. They are also not mispriced. The base case of $1,300 sits 13% above spot and coincides with the analyst median, while a credible bear case — accelerating realized-price erosion already visible in gross margin, an 18-month Medicare access mechanism with unknown uptake, a lost first-mover position in oral GLP-1, Trulicity's 2027 LOE, Verzenio's 2028 IRA price, and the Jardiance collaboration ending in December 2028 — supports $850. Free cash flow yield of 0.87% and a 0.60% dividend provide no floor. Near-term options pricing (61.4% IV against 38.4% realized, 8.4% implied move into August 5) makes this a poor moment to increase exposure.

**HOLD.** Own it for the twelve-month growth path; add on either the fundamental confirmation listed above or a price below $1,000.
