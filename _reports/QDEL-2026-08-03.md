## Executive Summary

QuidelOrtho is a $2.73B-revenue in-vitro diagnostics company whose operating franchise is materially more durable than its capital structure. The Labs and Transfusion Medicine businesses run on razor/razor-blade contracts that contractually oblige consumable purchases, with more than 75% of the flagship installed base e-connected and a 1,200-person service organization behind it. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $597.0M on $2,730.2M of revenue implies a 21.9% margin. That asset supports real enterprise value. The problem is that roughly two-thirds of that enterprise value belongs to creditors: net debt was ~$2,547.6M at March 29, 2026, and roughly $2,650M pro forma for the $100M delayed-draw term loan drawn April 13, 2026 and the ~$100M cash LEX Diagnostics purchase closed the same week. Against a $1.21B equity value, every 1x of EV/EBITDA multiple is worth approximately $8 per share, and every $50M of EBITDA erosion is worth roughly $5 per share at a 7x multiple.

The trajectory is moving toward the constraint rather than away from it. Company-level Adjusted EBITDA fell from $159.8M in Q1 2025 to $108.7M in Q1 2026, a 32% decline, in what is seasonally the respiratory quarter. Reconstructed on reported Adjusted EBITDA, trailing-four-quarter net leverage computes to 4.67x against a disclosed 4.50x maximum, and interest coverage to 2.89x against a 3.00x minimum. The company states it was in compliance at both December 28, 2025 and March 29, 2026, and both statements can be true — the Credit Agreement's defined "Consolidated EBITDA" almost certainly permits run-rate restructuring savings, pro-forma acquisition contribution and transaction-cost add-backs that reported Adjusted EBITDA does not. The correct conclusion is narrower and still consequential: compliance now depends on definitional add-backs rather than on reported profitability, and the covenant steps down to 4.25x after roughly the third anniversary of the August 2025 refinancing, making deleveraging contractual rather than discretionary.

We are reversing the July HOLD to AVOID. Three things changed. Consensus EPS was cut a further 4.0% (current year, to $1.84) and 6.1% (next year, to $2.39) over the last 30 days, with the current-quarter estimate flipping from +$0.02 to -$0.05. The share price rose 6.6% over the same window, compressing risk/reward into a Q2 print that near-dated options price at ±21.3% with 118.2% implied volatility in backwardation. And the median sell-side target is $14, 21% below the current price, with the $21.33 mean distorted by a single $38 outlier in a three-analyst coverage universe. Our base case of $14 sits below spot; the bull case of $30 depends on an unconfirmed asset sale. That is not an ownable configuration. It is also not shortable — 23.6% short interest with 7 days to cover, in a stock that rose 34.7% intraday on a single unconfirmed press report, is the wrong side of a squeeze.

## Company Overview

QuidelOrtho was formed by Quidel's 2022 acquisition of Ortho Clinical Diagnostics. It operates four business units reported across five geographic segments (North America, EMEA, China, JPAC, Latin America):

- **Labs** — the largest unit. Clinical chemistry on the proprietary VITROS dry-slide platform (covering approximately 24 therapeutic areas and ~90% of a typical lab's testing menu, with no water requirement) and immunoassay across 60+ chemiluminescent tests. Platforms include VITROS 5600 and VITROS XT 7600/3400.
- **Transfusion Medicine** — immunohematology (ORTHO VISION analyzers, ID-MTS gel cards in the U.S., BIOVUE cassettes internationally, ORTHO Sera reagents covering "more than 99% of most tested blood antigens") and donor screening for blood and plasma infectious disease.
- **Point of Care** — SOFIA/SOFIA 2 fluorescent immunoassay readers, QUICKVUE lateral flow including the at-home OTC COVID test, TRIAGE/TRIAGE METERPRO cardiometabolic panels, and eye-health assays.
- **Molecular Diagnostics** — LYRA (open-platform PCR reagents) and SOLANA. The SAVANNA syndromic multiplex PCR platform, which was CE-marked and 510(k)-cleared with an RVP4 assay, was discontinued in Q2 2025.

Approximately 6,500 employees, customers in 140+ countries, and manufacturing in Carlsbad, San Diego, Athens (OH), Raritan, Rochester (NY), Pompano Beach, Pencoed (Wales) and Changsha (China). Respiratory products were roughly 15% of FY2025 revenue, down from post-pandemic peaks — normalization is largely complete, which removes both a tailwind and most of the remaining roll-off risk.

Two deliberate portfolio exits are in progress: the U.S. donor screening wind-down initiated February 2024 (expected substantially complete in H1 2026), and the SAVANNA discontinuation. Both reduce revenue and are framed by management as margin-accretive mix improvements.

## Financial Analysis

### The impairment cycle is complete

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$3.0B | $2,782.9M | $2,730.2M |
| Net income | –$10.1M | –$2,052.0M | –$1,131.8M |
| Goodwill (period end) | $2,492M | $649.5M | **$0** |
| Adjusted EBITDA (company-selected) | $723.2M | $542.9M | $597.0M |
| R&D | $245.0M | $218.7M | $186.2M |

FY2025 operating income was –$919.2M, comprising ~$700.7M of goodwill impairment, ~$9.7M of other asset impairment and ~$263.6M of restructuring. Loss before tax –$1,107.7M; net loss –$1,131.8M. Cumulative FY2024–FY2025 net losses of roughly $3.18B are approximately 2.6x the current market capitalization.

Goodwill is now zero. That matters for what comes next. Total equity of ~$1,920.5M less identifiable intangibles of ~$2,563.8M gives **tangible book equity of approximately –$643M** (1,920.5 − 2,563.8 = −643.3). Retained earnings are roughly –$996M. Approximately 44% of the $5,769.3M asset base is still carried as amortizable or indefinite-lived intangibles from the Ortho purchase accounting. Those intangibles survived FY2025 impairment testing, which is the normal ASC ordering — goodwill is tested at the reporting-unit level and absorbs shortfalls first — so a zero goodwill balance does not imply the intangibles are overstated. It does mean that any further EBITDA shortfall, or a divestiture below allocated carrying value, attacks the intangibles directly and pushes reported equity toward zero.

### Leverage is the investment case

The August 2025 Credit Agreement comprises a $1.15B Term Loan A, a $100M delayed-draw term loan, a $1.45B Term Loan B and a $700M revolver, with a ~$5.1M loss on extinguishment recorded. Disclosed financial covenants: maximum Consolidated Leverage Ratio of 4.50x for approximately three years, stepping to 4.25x thereafter, and minimum Consolidated Interest Coverage of 3.00x.

Debt at Q1 2026: ~$2,688.0M against cash of ~$140.4M, so net debt ~$2,547.6M. Pro forma for the April 13 DDTL draw and the LEX cash outlay, net debt is approximately $2,650M with no immediate offsetting EBITDA.

Reconstructing trailing-four-quarter metrics on **reported** Adjusted EBITDA: Q1 2026 company-level Adjusted EBITDA of $108.7M (segment $242.3M less corporate/unallocated $133.6M) versus $159.8M in Q1 2025, so TTM = 597.0 − 159.8 + 108.7 = **$545.9M**. TTM interest expense = 177.6 − 40.0 + 51.1 = **$188.7M**. That yields net leverage of 2,547.6 ÷ 545.9 = **4.67x** and coverage of 545.9 ÷ 188.7 = **2.89x**.

These are not the covenant calculations. The Credit Agreement's defined terms were not available for review, and such definitions routinely permit run-rate cost savings from announced restructuring, pro-forma acquisition contribution, and non-recurring charge add-backs, while often excluding non-cash financing amortization from interest. If contractual Consolidated EBITDA is $700M, TTM leverage is closer to 3.6x and headroom is comfortable. What the reconstruction does establish is that **reported** metrics now sit at or through the covenant lines, so compliance rests on definitional cushion rather than on profitability, and that cushion shrinks with each quarter of EBITDA decline.

The interest exposure compounds this. TTM interest of $188.7M on ~$2.69B of largely floating-rate term debt implies an average cost near 7.0%. Each 100bp of additional base rate adds roughly $27M of annual interest, which alone moves reported coverage from 2.89x to approximately 2.53x. The debt is described as largely floating-rate term debt, but the fixed/floating mix and the amortization schedule were not verified, so the sensitivity to a given change in base rates is directional rather than precise. [unverified — actual: N/A] the current level and direction of policy rates were not supported by the provided sources; the sign of the sensitivity is unfavorable if rates rise.

### Earnings quality and cash flow

FY2025 D&A of ~$442M against operating income of –$919.2M means the loss is overwhelmingly non-cash. Q1 2026 D&A of $112.9M is roughly equal to Q1 Adjusted EBITDA of $108.7M — purchase-accounting amortization will suppress GAAP earnings for years regardless of operational performance. Free cash flow was not directly verified for FY2025 or Q1 2026, and we deliberately do not annualize Q1 cash flow: respiratory-season collections, annual incentive payouts and inventory build all distort the first half. Any quarterly run-rate should be treated as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

Screening metrics are consistent with the balance sheet: Altman Z ≈ 0.81, deep in the distress zone. The Z-Score is sensitive to the impairment-driven EBIT and retained-earnings terms, but the negative retained earnings and the market-value-of-equity-to-liabilities terms are independent of the accounting — the market has marked this equity down on its own.

### Earnings surprise pattern

Three beats then a large miss: Q2 2025 +689.5%, Q3 2025 +70.7%, Q4 2025 +6.6%, then Q1 2026 actual –$0.04 against a $0.40 estimate, a –109.9% miss. That sequence — decelerating beat magnitude followed by a decisive miss — is the classic signature of deteriorating forecast visibility, and it aligns with reports that 2026 EPS guidance was cut twice in three months.

The credible growth path is Labs share gain plus mix improvement from the two exits, producing low-single-digit revenue growth and Adjusted EBITDA stabilization near $550-600M. Nothing in the current data confirms that path is being executed; Q1 2026 points the other way.

The reported forward P/E field of –0.94 is unusable (it duplicates the trailing figure on GAAP EPS of –$16.69, which is impairment-driven). Enterprise value is the only coherent frame. The bear case is a further leg of EBITDA erosion, no asset sale, and multiple compression to 6x on $475M: $2.85B EV − $2.65B = $200M equity, or roughly **$3**. They are not a margin of safety: a single-digit equity multiple on a business where creditors own two-thirds of enterprise value, tangible book is negative $643M, and the earnings estimates themselves are being cut 4-6% per month, is a value trap signature rather than a mispricing. Valuation scores 4.0 — above the bottom band because the enterprise multiple is genuinely undemanding and the divestiture optionality is real, but well short of asymmetric when the base case sits 21% below spot.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own competitor list — Roche, Abbott, Danaher/Beckman Coulter, Siemens Healthineers, Thermo Fisher, bioMérieux, DiaSorin, Bio-Rad, Hologic, Qiagen, Revvity, and Waters Corporation (which the 10-K states acquired the BD Diagnostics division) — includes companies 10-50x QuidelOrtho's market capitalization, and the filing concedes they have "substantially greater financial, marketing and other resources." In central lab diagnostics, scale drives menu breadth, R&D amortization and tender competitiveness. Cutting R&D 24% while that cohort invests is the competitive crux.

The defensive case is legitimate and grounded in the filing rather than in management rhetoric. Instruments are placed under long-term contracts under which customers are contractually required to purchase assays, reagents and consumables. More than 75% of the installed base of VITROS 5600/XT 7600 and ORTHO VISION is e-connected, supporting a claimed 98% uptime for e-connected U.S. customers, with five technical solution centers resolving roughly two-thirds of issues remotely. Replacement cycles in central lab run 7-10 years. The ORTHO Sera menu covering more than 99% of most tested blood antigens sits in a concentrated global niche with few credible alternatives; transfusion medicine is the most durable franchise in the portfolio and the least exposed to commoditization.

Customer concentration is not improving. One customer represented 11% of FY2025 total revenue. Revenue *mix* has shifted meaningfully — respiratory down to ~15%, donor screening exiting, Labs growing — but mix change does not diversify the customer base, and on the direct measure disclosed, concentration remains material.

The strategic tension is unresolved. Management's stated logic is cross-care-setting integration: winning in the central lab creates POC opportunities within the same delivery network, and vice versa. Selling POC, which the 10-K names as one of five core competitive strengths, contradicts that. The reasonable reading is that the balance sheet, not the strategy, is now setting portfolio decisions. That is a rational response to a leverage problem and value-destructive relative to an unlevered counterfactual. The alternative — that POC's post-COVID volume profile and EUA regulatory dependency make it worth more to a private buyer than inside QuidelOrtho — is possible but cannot be tested without POC's standalone revenue and EBITDA, which we do not have.

Competitive position scores 4.0: the installed-base moat is real and understates in the multiple, but the trajectory across R&D, platform abandonment, sub-scale positioning and potential divestiture of a named core strength is negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant proximity is the dominant risk.** Reported metrics at or through 4.50x/3.00x, a step-down to 4.25x from approximately Q4 2028, and a $100M DDTL draw plus $100M cash acquisition that added debt without immediate EBITDA. A covenant amendment or waiver, if required, would arrive with repricing and likely equity-unfriendly conditions.

**Further intangible impairment.** With goodwill exhausted, $2,563.8M of identifiable intangibles is the next line of defense. A divestiture below allocated carrying value or a further EBITDA decline could push reported equity toward or below zero and interact with any net-worth-sensitive provisions.

**Grifols Joint Business termination.** A $65M charge payable over three years was recorded in December 2025 to reflect terms agreed "in principle" for early termination of a collaboration whose initial 50-year term runs to December 2039. The 10-K states explicitly that ultimate resolution "may differ from the estimated amount accrued, and such an amount could be material." The underlying economics are small (Grifols-supplied antigens under 2% of COGS, associated licensed-IP revenue under 1% of revenue, most patents expired), and QuidelOrtho is the payer. Grifols is a collaboration counterparty, supplier and licensor, not a customer.

**Regulatory and reimbursement.** EU IVDR transition deadlines run December 2027 (high-risk), 2028, 2029 and 2030, with recurring compliance cost and no public proposal yet from the 2025 Commission consultation. PAMA compresses Medicare lab reimbursement; the 2% Medicare sequester runs through 2030; OBBBA-driven Medicaid eligibility and financing changes appear as a fresh risk factor this cycle. Some respiratory products remain authorized under EUAs; termination of the relevant declaration would require 510(k) or PMA pathways for those products — a latent binary specific to POC.

**China VBP.** China is a standalone reportable segment with localized VITROS manufacturing in Changsha. Local content helps in tenders; it does not protect against government-driven reagent price cuts. Which provincial programs currently cover QuidelOrtho's menu, and the realized pricing impact, are unverified.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Three separate Item 5.02 8-Ks between February and June 2026, an Item 4.01 auditor change (March 2025) whose particulars are unverified, no retrievable earnings call transcript, and Item 3 cross-referenced only to a financial-statement note we did not read. Securities litigation is plausible given an 84% relative TSR decline but is unconfirmed — treat as unknown rather than absent.

**Verification gaps that materially change the answer.** The Credit Agreement's Consolidated EBITDA definition; POC's standalone economics; whether Q1 2026 EBITDA is seasonal or the new run-rate; the actual 2026 guidance ranges; and the debt amortization/fixed-floating mix. Each is decision-relevant and none is resolved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$30, +69%)

A Point of Care sale closes near the reported ~$1.5B. Net debt falls from ~$2.65B toward ~$1.15B, taking leverage from roughly 4.2-4.7x to 2.0-2.5x depending on the EBITDA sold. Covenant risk is removed, the interest burden roughly halves (freeing ~$90M+ of annual cash), and the remaining Labs plus Transfusion Medicine business — higher-margin, more recurring, less EUA-dependent — re-rates on a clean balance sheet. A more concentrated ~$400M-EBITDA business at 8x yields ~$2.05B of equity value. Layered on that: 23.6% short interest provides mechanical fuel, the new CFO arrives from Masimo with a 356,555-share RSU grant struck at a depressed price and therefore maximum leverage to a recovery, and Q1 2026's EBITDA decline proves partly seasonal and partly restructuring noise rather than the run-rate. Respiratory normalization is largely complete at ~15% of revenue, so the largest historical source of negative revenue surprise is behind the company.

### Bear Case (to ~$8, –55%)

No sale closes — sale processes frequently do not, and a buyer must clear diligence on POC's EUA dependency and post-COVID volume trajectory. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA settles near $450-475M as China VBP pricing, PAMA and OBBBA reimbursement compression, and a 24%-reduced R&D budget bite into an already-declining revenue line. The definitional add-back cushion supporting covenant compliance is exhausted within one to three quarters, forcing an amendment on terms that price in the distress. A 100bp move higher in base rates adds ~$27M of interest and takes reported coverage to ~2.53x. Any divestiture at proceeds below allocated POC intangible carrying value triggers a further impairment against $2,563.8M of remaining intangibles, pushing reported equity toward zero from an already-negative tangible base. At 6x on $475M EBITDA against $2.65B of net debt, the equity is worth roughly $3-9.

### Which is more likely

The base case sits between and below spot. On the evidence available, the operating trajectory is negative (revenue –1.9%, Q1 EBITDA –32%, estimates cut 14-17% over 90 days and still falling), the balance-sheet constraint is tightening, and the single identified upside path is unconfirmed by any filing. The 50% drawdown from the $35.58 fifty-two-week high is not a mispricing we can identify against; it is a reasonable market response to a levered small-cap whose EBITDA fell a third year over year while its covenant tightens. We are not arguing the sellers are wrong.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q2 2026 print, implied by the 17-day options tenor to fall within roughly two weeks, is the nearest dated event. It resolves three things simultaneously: whether Q1's 32% EBITDA decline was seasonal, whether 2026 guidance is cut a third time, and whether management addresses the POC process. A secondary catalyst is any 8-K disclosing a covenant amendment. Neither is a *reliably* bearish catalyst — the same print could confirm a sale process.

**Timing window.** Two to four weeks for the print; the covenant question is a one-to-three-quarter horizon on the add-back-exhaustion path. Carrying cost across that window is material.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 23.6% of float short, 11.8M shares, 7 days to cover. This is the disqualifying fact. The June 29 session produced a 34.7% intraday advance on an unsourced report — a live demonstration that a crowded short base plus asset-sale headlines produces violent upside. Institutional ownership narrowed further following removal from the Russell 3000/2000/2500 Growth indexes in late June, which reduces the natural lender pool.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis: front-month skew is balanced at –1.0% and open-interest put/call is 0.68, call-tilted. Front-month IV of 118.2% at the 91st percentile means put protection is expensive, and the 32.7pp premium to realized volatility makes defined-risk bearish structures poor value. Borrow cost is not directly observable but is unlikely to be cheap at 23.6% SI on a $1.2B float.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $8 (–55%); squeeze/deal upside to $30 (+69%) or beyond on a confirmed transaction. That is materially worse than 1:1 on a probability-weighted basis, before borrow cost.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis on capital structure is credible, but 23.6% short interest with demonstrated squeeze reflexivity, call-tilted open interest, expensive put protection, and a live asset-sale catalyst that would violently invalidate the trade make this unshortable. Avoid owning; do not press the short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (specific and checkable):**

1. **Covenant headroom quantified.** Disclosure — in a 10-Q, 8-K or credit-agreement exhibit — of contractual Consolidated EBITDA and the actual leverage ratio, showing headroom of at least 0.75x against the applicable maximum. Absent this, we cannot distinguish a going-concern deleveraging story from a pre-amendment situation.
2. **A completed, quantified divestiture.** A definitive agreement disclosing proceeds and the EBITDA transferred, with pro-forma net leverage below 3.0x and no impairment charge against POC intangibles. Confirmation of the ~$1.5B figure by the company, not by aggregated press reports.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA stabilization** — year-over-year declines of less than 10%, with the current-year consensus estimate ceasing to fall (a 30-day revision of 0% or better versus the current –4.0%).
4. **Valuation reset.** At the current EBITDA trajectory, a price in the **$9-11** range would place the equity at roughly 6.0-6.5x EV/EBITDA on $500M with the leverage risk substantially discounted, which is where the asymmetry turns favorable enough to compensate for the covenant tail.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — reimbursement compression, China VBP, IVDR compliance, and the mandated deleveraging path toward a 4.25x covenant from approximately Q4 2028 — persist for at least 12-24 months. The binary elements (Q2 print, divestiture, covenant disclosure) resolve much sooner, within one to two quarters, and we would rather pay up after resolution than guess ahead of a ±21% implied move.

**Next reassessment.** The Q2 2026 print, expected within the next two to three weeks based on the options term structure, and any 8-K disclosing a divestiture agreement or credit-agreement amendment.

**What would force a faster upgrade.** A company-confirmed POC sale at ≥$1.4B with disclosed transferred EBITDA below $175M. That combination alone would move the base case above $25 and warrant an immediate revisit regardless of the operating trend.

Overall score 3.1 (from 3.6 in July).** The downgrade from HOLD reflects three specific developments rather than price action: consensus EPS cut a further 4.0% for the current year and 6.1% for next year over 30 days with the current-quarter estimate flipping to a loss; a 6.6% price advance that compresses risk/reward into a print the options market prices at ±21.3%; and a median sell-side target of $14 that sits 21% below spot, with the $21.33 mean an artifact of one $38 outlier in a three-analyst universe.

The franchise underneath is not the problem. Razor/razor-blade consumable contracts, a >75% e-connected installed base, 1,200 service employees, a transfusion-medicine reagent menu covering more than 99% of most tested blood antigens, and 21.9% Adjusted EBITDA margins support genuine enterprise value at ~7x. The problem is that creditors own roughly two-thirds of that value, reported leverage and coverage metrics sit at or through the disclosed 4.50x/4.25x and 3.00x covenant lines, Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA fell 32% year over year in a seasonally favorable quarter, goodwill is exhausted, tangible book equity is approximately –$643M, and R&D has been cut 24% in two years against competitors the company concedes have substantially greater resources.

That configuration makes the common stock a levered option on balance-sheet repair rather than a claim on operating performance. The cleanest evidence is the market's own: a 34.7% single-day move on an unconfirmed press report of a ~$1.5B POC sale. Options are pricing an imminent binary; short interest at 23.6% with 7 days to cover makes the bear expression unattractive; and the base case of $14 is below the current $17.78. Wait for the covenant arithmetic to be disclosed, for a divestiture to be confirmed with quantified proceeds and transferred EBITDA, or for the price to reach $9-11 where the leverage tail is genuinely paid for.

**Base case $14 (–21%). Bull case $30 (+69%). Bear case $8 (–55%).** Analyst target range $12-$38, median $14.
