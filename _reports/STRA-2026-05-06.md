## Executive Summary

Strategic Education, Inc. operates a post-secondary education platform across three segments: U.S. Higher Education (USHE, 68% of revenue), Australia/New Zealand (ANZ, 20%), and Education Technology Services (ETS, 12%). The company's financial profile is strong: debt-free balance sheet, $154M in FY2025 free cash flow (12.1% FCF margin), and aggressive share repurchases ($138.9M in FY2025). The ETS segment grew 41.4% in FY2025 and 21% in Q1 2026, providing a legitimate growth engine alongside margin expansion from AI-driven productivity initiatives.

The stock declined 13.3% on April 23 following a Q1 2026 miss ($1.42 adjusted EPS vs. $1.52 consensus) on flat revenue. At $78.58, STRA trades at approximately 10.9x FY2026E EPS ($7.19) and 9.7x FY2027E ($8.09), modestly below the peer median P/E of 14.9x. However, the recent earnings miss, Strayer's precarious 89.64% federal revenue ratio (vs. the 90% regulatory threshold), and the incoming OBBBA legislation (effective July 2026, which eliminates PLUS loans for graduate students) create specific, quantifiable downside risks that prevent a high-conviction entry at current prices. The thesis is that ETS growth, declining Title IV dependence, and operating leverage will produce mid-teens EPS growth over the next 12-18 months, but the regulatory overhang requires a wider margin of safety than the current price offers.

## Company Overview

Strategic Education serves approximately 105,000 students globally through:

- **Capella University** (graduate-focused, 58% master's/doctoral students, FlexPath competency-based model)
- **Strayer University** (primarily undergraduate, working adults, employer partnerships)
- **Torrens University / Think Education** (Australia/NZ, 45% international students)
- **Workforce Edge** (B2B employer tuition benefits platform, 80 enterprise partners)
- **Sophia Learning** ($99/month unlimited general education courses)

The company generated $1.27B in FY2025 revenue with 13.7% operating margins (up from 6.6% in 2022). Total enrollment is roughly flat, but revenue per student is growing through mix shift toward higher-value programs and improved persistence (88.3% vs. 86.9% prior year).

Operating income grew at a 35% CAGR from 2022-2025 on 6% revenue CAGR, demonstrating clear operating leverage. The company moved from effectively breakeven to generating $174M in operating income through enrollment mix improvement, technology-driven cost reduction, and scale in ETS.

**Balance Sheet:** Zero long-term debt (paid down from $101M in 2022). $153.1M in cash. $250M undrawn revolver. Altman Z-Score of 4.59 (safe zone). The primary balance sheet risk is $1.24B in goodwill (61% of assets), split between USHE ($732M) and ANZ ($510M).

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 OCF of $198.2M less $44.3M capex = $154M FCF. At the current market cap of $1.76B, this implies an 8.8% FCF yield. Capital allocation in FY2025: $138.9M buybacks + $57.5M dividends = $196.4M returned to shareholders, exceeding FCF by drawing modestly on the cash balance.

**Q1 2026 (most recent quarter):** Revenue $305.9M (flat YoY), adjusted EPS $1.42 (missed $1.52 consensus by 6.8%). Management characterized this as the FY2026 trough quarter, with acceleration expected through the year driven by ETS ramp, margin expansion, and enrollment stabilization. ETS grew 21% to $42M in Q1.

## Growth Analysis

**High-growth ETS segment:** 41.4% revenue growth in FY2025, 21% in Q1 2026. Workforce Edge (80 employer partners and growing) and Sophia Learning represent a fundamentally different business model: recurring B2B revenue with high switching costs. At $148M annual revenue growing 20%+, this segment alone could be worth $1B+ on a standalone SaaS-like multiple. Currently it's embedded in a $1.76B enterprise.

**Flat-to-declining core USHE:** Enrollment fell 1.4% in FY2025, though revenue per student grew enough to deliver 1.2% segment revenue growth. Student persistence improvement (88.3%) and higher revenue per student from mix shift toward Capella's graduate programs provide a partial offset. The for-profit sector saw its first enrollment decline since 2021 in fall 2025 (-1.2%), suggesting headwinds aren't company-specific.

**Estimate revisions support continued growth:** Current-year EPS estimate of $7.19 has been revised up 11.8% over 90 days; next-year $8.09 is up 8.9%. This represents ~33% EPS growth in FY2026 ($7.19 vs. $5.41) and 12.5% in FY2027. The growth is driven more by margin expansion (management targets +200bps in FY2026 via AI productivity) and buyback accretion than revenue acceleration.

The +11.8% 90-day revision is constructive but below the 30% threshold that would trigger a strong BUY signal under an estimate-revision framework. Growth is real but measured rather than explosive.

## Competitive Landscape

STRA occupies a defensible but pressured position:

**Strengths:** (1) Capella's FlexPath competency-based model has limited direct competition among accredited institutions at scale; (2) Workforce Edge's 80-employer-partner network creates B2B switching costs; (3) Sophia Learning at $99/month is the lowest-cost accredited course option available, creating a funnel into degree programs; (4) Geographic diversification through ANZ.

**Pressures:** (1) Traditional universities rapidly expanding online offerings (post-COVID momentum); (2) For-profit sector enrollment declining for the first time since 2021; (3) Alternative credentialing (Google Career Certificates, corporate training) eroding the degree value proposition for some demographics; (4) Regulatory environment perpetually hostile to for-profit education (though slightly moderated under current administration).

The 32.3% employer-affiliated enrollment rate (up from 29.6%) suggests the corporate partnership strategy is working, which both reduces regulatory risk and creates stickier enrollment. However, this is slow-moving progress against a fast-approaching regulatory threshold.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Strayer 90/10 Rule (CRITICAL):** At 89.64% federal revenue, Strayer has 36 basis points of cushion before triggering provisional certification. One bad quarter of employer enrollment, a Workforce Edge partner loss, or a slowdown in Sophia Learning adoption could push this over. Loss of Title IV eligibility would destroy the business. The company's entire growth strategy (ETS, Workforce Edge, employer partnerships) is essentially a regulatory survival mechanism.

**2. OBBBA Legislation (HIGH):** Effective July 2026, eliminates PLUS loans for graduate/professional students. Capella is 58% graduate — a material portion of these students likely rely on PLUS loans. Management hasn't quantified the enrollment impact, but any reduction in available funding for graduate students directly pressures Capella enrollment and revenue.

**3. ANZ Goodwill ($510M):** Fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~17% at the October 2024 quantitative test. Australian international student caps, currency headwinds, and margin pressure could trigger impairment. A write-down wouldn't affect cash flow but would hit EPS and book value.

**4. Q1 Miss Pattern Risk:** The most recent quarter broke a 3-quarter beat streak. If Q2 also disappoints, the credibility of management's "Q1 is the trough" guidance erodes, and the estimate revision trend reverses.

**5. Consumer Macro:** Working adult enrollment correlates with job market conditions — in a recession, for-profit enrollment often increases (counter-cyclical to some degree as displaced workers seek credentials). However, the current stagflation environment with elevated costs could reduce discretionary education spending.

## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Assessment

(a) **Positive sequential comparable revenue growth:** Q1 2026 revenue was flat YoY ($305.9M). This does NOT pass the gate. Sequential growth decelerated from Q4 2025.

(b) **FCF yield >= 4%:** Yes — 8.8% FCF yield clearly passes this threshold.

(c) **Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** Michigan Consumer Sentiment at 53.3 (declining). This does NOT pass the gate.

(d) **Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** Yes — FY2026 EPS revised up 11.8% over 90 days. Passes.

**Result:** STRA passes 2 of 4 conditions. The flat revenue and declining consumer confidence prevent clearing the Consumer Discretionary BUY gate.

## Conclusion

STRA presents a mixed profile: strong balance sheet, legitimate growth in ETS, operating leverage story intact, and a cheap valuation vs. peers. These are offset by a recent earnings miss, the critical 90/10 regulatory constraint (36bps of cushion), and pending OBBBA legislation that directly threatens its graduate-heavy Capella segment.

The stock at $78.58 is reasonably valued but not compellingly cheap given the specific binary risks. The 8.8% FCF yield and 3.1% dividend provide downside support, while the ETS growth and margin expansion story provide a credible path to $93-101 over 12 months. Until Q2 confirms the trough thesis and OBBBA's actual enrollment impact becomes measurable, the prudent posture is to hold existing positions and wait for either price weakness ($68-72) or fundamental confirmation (Q2 beat) before adding.

Rating: **HOLD** with positive lean. The risk/reward improves materially at $68-72 or upon Q2 execution confirmation.
