## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is a pure-play natural gas E&P focused on the Haynesville/Bossier shale in North Louisiana and East Texas. The company sits at an uncomfortable intersection: declining production despite $1.35B in annual capex, chronic free cash flow deficits ($450M in 2025, likely $500-800M in 2026), rising debt ($2.95B), and a Q1 2026 earnings miss of 42% below consensus. The stock has fallen 55% from its June 2025 peak of $31.17 to $14.11, and consensus estimates are in freefall with current quarter EPS revised down 77% over 90 days.

The long-term thesis centers on the Western Haynesville development and a NextEra partnership for a 5.2 GW gas-fired power hub that could consume ~1 Bcf/day by 2031. This optionality is genuine — the Haynesville's proximity to Gulf Coast LNG terminals and data center power demand creates structural demand tailwinds. However, the power hub is 5+ years from generating revenue, and the company must fund $4.7B in development costs while burning cash at current rates. With only $24M in cash, a $440M Quantum Capital buyout closing in H1 2026, and a current ratio of 0.49, the path between here and that long-term prize is fraught with dilution, leverage, and commodity price risk.

At 6.7x forward P/E and with PV-10 reserves of $4.5-5.2B vs. a $4B market cap, the stock appears cheap on paper. But the market discount reflects real risks: the reserves valuation depends on gas prices remaining above $3/Mcf, the free cash flow deficit requires continuous external funding, and the controlling shareholder (Jerry Jones) has demonstrated willingness to dilute minority holders through private placements.

## Company Overview

Comstock Resources operates as one of the largest independent natural gas producers in the Haynesville shale, holding approximately 803,000 net acres across the Haynesville and Bossier formations. The company's asset base includes:

- 7.0 Tcfe of proved reserves (PV-10: $4.5B at SEC prices)
- 1,724 producing wells (960 net), operating 99% of its proved reserve base
- Pinnacle Gas Services midstream subsidiary (246 miles of high-pressure gathering)
- 2,561 net drilling locations in the Western Haynesville (exploration/growth phase)

The company is a controlled entity — Jerry Jones and affiliated entities hold a majority stake and have conducted private equity placements totaling $100.5M in 2024 alone. This governance structure means minority shareholders have limited influence over capital allocation, compensation, or strategic direction.

Revenue is almost exclusively tied to Henry Hub natural gas pricing, making CRK essentially a leveraged bet on domestic gas prices with a deep inventory of drilling locations.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Paradox:** CRK is spending more each year yet producing less. 2025 production of 450 Bcfe represents a 15% decline from 2024 (528 Bcfe), and Q1 2026 annualizes to ~392 Bcfe. Management attributes this to the Shelby Trough divestiture and weather, but the fundamental challenge is shale decline curves: Haynesville wells lose 60-70% of initial production in the first year, requiring continuous drilling to maintain volumes.

**Western Haynesville — The Growth Vector:** The company's primary growth thesis is the Western Haynesville, where only 30 wells have been turned to sales. This is a deep (14,000-18,000 foot), high-pressure play with higher well costs and development infrastructure still being built. The 2,561 net locations represent decades of inventory, but delineation is early-stage.

**NextEra Power Hub — A 2031 Call Option:** The U.S. Department of Commerce-selected 5.2 GW gas-fired power facility would consume ~1 Bcf/day, effectively doubling CRK's current production and providing dedicated demand. Requirements for realization: definitive supply agreement, $16B facility construction, ERCOT transmission buildout, Japan-U.S. trade execution. Timeline is 5+ years with multiple dependencies.

**LNG Export Demand:** Haynesville's proximity to Gulf Coast terminals (Sabine Pass, Cameron, Golden Pass) positions CRK for growing export demand. Multiple new terminals are expected online through 2028.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Natural Gas Price Collapse (HIGH probability, HIGH severity):** At $2/Mcf, CRK's entire financial model breaks. Operating cash flow would halve, borrowing base would be reduced, and $4.7B in future development costs become uneconomic. Hormuz-driven oil price dynamics create indirect support for gas (power switching, reduced associated gas drilling), but Henry Hub trades on domestic fundamentals.

2. **Liquidity Squeeze (MODERATE probability, HIGH severity):** Heavy reliance on the $1.3B revolver given the combination of minimal cash, quarterly FCF burn, and the Quantum buyout obligation. A borrowing base reduction at the next redetermination (triggered by gas price decline or reserve write-downs) could create covenant pressure.

3. **Production Continues Declining (MODERATE probability, MODERATE severity):** If Western Haynesville wells underperform or take longer to bring online, the production decline worsens capital efficiency metrics and erodes investor confidence.

4. **Controlling Shareholder Dilution:** Jerry Jones has repeatedly injected capital via private placements (12.5M shares in 2024). Future placements at distressed prices would further dilute minority holders.

5. **Hedge Losses:** Locking in below-market prices while gas trades higher directly reduces shareholder value ($80.4M impact in Q1 2026).

**Options Market Signal:**

ATM IV of 58.8% matches historical volatility — the options market prices CRK at fair value for its realized volatility. The contango term structure (58.8% near-term vs 65.3% 6-month) prices increasing uncertainty at longer horizons, consistent with the liquidity/production trajectory concerns. The put/call volume ratio of 1.62 indicates moderate protective positioning. The IV collapse from 513.9% to 58.8% over the past week reflects earnings event resolution, not a structural change in risk perception.

Short interest at 26.7% of float with 10.2 days to cover represents extremely crowded bearish positioning. This creates binary dynamics: if gas prices rally or Western Haynesville production materializes faster than expected, a violent short squeeze is possible. Conversely, this level of short interest from informed investors reflects genuine balance sheet and capital structure concerns.

## Conclusion

Comstock Resources has premier gas basin positioning, Western Haynesville optionality, and the NextEra power hub partnership, but these are wrapped in a deeply concerning near-term financial reality. The company is spending $1.5B+ annually while generating $900M in operating cash flow, creating a $500-800M annual funding gap that must be filled by debt, asset sales, or dilutive equity. Production is declining despite the spending. The controlling shareholder structure limits minority investor agency. Estimate revisions are collapsing at the fastest rate in the coverage universe.

At $14.11, the stock trades at 6.7x forward P/E and below PV-10 reserve value, which would normally signal value. But the discount is earned: the capital structure requires continuous external funding, the production trajectory is negative, governance is non-independent, and the growth catalysts (Western Haynesville ramp, NextEra hub) are 18-60 months from contributing. The 55% decline from the June 2025 peak creates optionality for risk-tolerant investors, but the 26.7% short interest from presumably informed institutional investors reinforces that the balance sheet risks are genuine and proximate.

The appropriate posture is to avoid CRK until the free cash flow equation demonstrates a path toward breakeven — likely requiring either gas prices above $5/Mcf sustained or Western Haynesville volumes inflecting meaningfully. Until then, minority shareholders are funding Jerry Jones's growth ambitions without receiving dividends, buybacks, or governance influence in return.
