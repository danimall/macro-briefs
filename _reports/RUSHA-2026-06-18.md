**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rush Enterprises operates the largest network of commercial-vehicle dealerships in North America (126 Rush Truck Centers across 23 U.S. states plus Ontario), selling new and used trucks while generating the majority of its profit from recurring parts, service, and collision work. The structural appeal of the model is the aftermarket business: only ~34% of revenue but 63.7% of gross profit, with an absorption ratio of 130.7% in FY2025, meaning aftermarket gross profit covers the entire fixed dealership overhead before a single truck is sold. This keeps Rush profitable through truck-sales recessions: net income held at $264M in 2025 against a 5.2% revenue decline, and Q1'26 net income rose to $61.5M despite a 9% revenue drop.

The complication is that the Q1'26 earnings growth is not operational. Operating income fell 10.5% year over year; the EPS gain came from a 50.6% collapse in interest expense (lower rates plus aggressive inventory de-stocking), SG&A cuts, a one-time-low 18.2% tax rate from option-exercise benefits, and a share count reduced by $194.9M of 2025 buybacks. New Class 8 unit sales fell 17.4% in 2025, U.S. Class 8 share slipped to 5.8%, and the absorption ratio has declined three straight years (135.3% → 132.2% → 130.7%) as lower miles driven finally reach the "stable" aftermarket segment. Estimates are drifting lower for the current quarter and year.

The stock has rallied 38% off its 52-week low of $45.67 to ~$68 and trades at roughly 18.3x the $3.71 current-year consensus, a premium to truck-cyclical peers (Greenbrier 10.1x, Trinity 10.8x) that prices in a freight recovery and a 2026 pre-buy ahead of the EPA 2027 Low NOx mandate that has not yet shown up in the operating line. The business is high quality and well managed, but at this multiple, with the operating line still contracting and the regulatory setup carrying a post-mandate 2027 air pocket, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate RUSHA a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Rush Enterprises is a full-service commercial-vehicle retailer founded in 1965 in New Braunfels, Texas, that became a Peterbilt dealer in 1966 and has consolidated into the scaled leader in a fragmented dealer industry. It reports a single "Truck Segment" spanning new and used truck sales, aftermarket parts and service, collision repair, leasing and rental, financing and insurance, and vehicle upfitting.

The franchise mix anchors on PACCAR's Peterbilt (26.1% of total revenue) and International (14.7%), with additional brands including Hino, Ford, Isuzu, IC Bus, Blue Bird, Blue Arc, Battle Motors. The company holds 80% of RTC Canada (12 International + 2 IC Bus dealerships in Ontario) and a 50% JV in Cummins Clean Fuel Technologies for CNG systems.

The competitive engine is the aftermarket business. Management's signature KPI, the absorption ratio, measures aftermarket gross profit against all fixed dealership overhead excluding vehicle selling costs. The moat rests on scale, geographic density, the ability to absorb multi-unit fleet trade-ins, one-stop service, and roughly 24-hour parts logistics, capabilities that scale with network size and favor the largest operators as the industry shifts competition from price toward uptime and service capability.

## Financial Analysis

**A cyclical downturn is in progress, well managed but unmistakable.** Revenue peaked in 2023 and has declined two consecutive years: $7.57B (2023) → $7.45B (2024) → $7.06B (2025). Q1'26 revenue was $1.68B. Net income followed the same path: $347M (2023) → $304M (2024) → $264M (2025). New Class 8 unit sales fell to 12,770 units, and U.S. Class 8 share slipped from 6.1%.

**Margins are compressing on the vehicle side and holding on aftermarket.** New Class 8 gross margin fell from 9.7% (2024) to 8.6% (2025) to 7.8% (Q1'26) in an oversupplied market. Used-truck gross margin dropped sharply to 13.6% from 18.9% on mix shift. Aftermarket gross margin held at ~36.9% (FY25) and 36.3% (Q1'26), supported by parts pricing and manufacturer rebates. Operating margin compressed from 6.5% (2023) to 5.9% (2024) to 5.6% (2025); net margin is 3.8%.

**The balance sheet is healthy, and headline leverage is misleading.** The reported debt/equity of 2.01 is dominated by $918M of floorplan notes payable, which is self-liquidating inventory financing rather than corporate leverage. True long-term debt is only ~$275–278M against $2.27B of equity, a debt/equity near 0.12x. Altman Z is 3.04 (safe zone), current ratio 1.4, ROE 12.1%, ROA 6.0%. Inventory fell from $1.79B to $1.53B as the company de-stocked into the downturn, which generated cash and cut financing costs. FY25 operating cash flow was $861.8M and FCF was $462M (6.5% FCF margin), though OCF is inflated by GAAP treatment of manufacturer-affiliated floorplan borrowings and by lease-fleet depreciation, so the FCF figure should be read with care.

**Interest expense is the dominant earnings swing.** Interest expense fell 34.7% in 2025 ($46M vs $71M) and another 50.6% in Q1'26 on lower rates plus disciplined inventory reduction. This is the single largest reason net income rose to $61.5M ($0.77 EPS), alongside a 2.5% SG&A cut and an 18.2% tax rate flattered by stock-option exercise benefits. The operating business is still contracting (operating income -10.5% YoY). Q1 EPS growth should not be read as evidence of an operational turn.

The earnings surprise record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters; +14.0% in Q4'25, +7.9% in Q1'26), which lends confidence to near-term consensus. But the estimate revision trend is unfavorable: current-quarter consensus has fallen 4.4% over 90 days and current-year 0.2%, with only next-year estimates rising (+2.7% over 90 days, presumably embedding a freight recovery).

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth at present. Revenue has declined two straight years, Q1'26 was down 9%, and the operating line is still contracting. The growth case is entirely about cyclical inflection plus M&A.

**The cyclical case.** Q1'26 industry Class 8 retail was down 21% YoY and Class 4–7 down 25.6%; the quarter showed no inflection. ACT Research (as cited in Rush's filings) forecasts a modest 2026 rebound of +5.7% in Class 8 (to 224,800 units) followed by a decline in 2027 (~205,900), consistent with a 2026 pre-buy ahead of the EPA 2027 Low NOx mandate and a post-mandate air pocket the year after. Backlog has rebuilt to $1.46B at 3/31/26 from a $1.11B December trough, a tentative positive that supports management's optimism for a second-half recovery.

**The aftermarket is resilient but not immune.** Aftermarket revenue was essentially flat (+0.3% FY25, +1.3% Q1'26), and the declining absorption ratio shows the freight recession is finally pressuring the stable segment through lower miles driven. Management identifies inflecting freight/miles-driven as the key 2026 watch item.

**M&A adds incremental footprint.** Rush acquired Leeds Transit ($25.6M, June 2025) and signed an agreement to acquire Peterbilt of Louisiana (~$35M plus inventory), expanding into Louisiana and Mississippi. These are sensible bolt-ons that consolidate a fragmented industry but are not large enough to offset the cyclical revenue decline.

The growth picture is muted-to-negative right now: falling near-term estimates, a still-contracting operating line, and a cyclical recovery that is forecast but not yet visible. The 2027 air pocket caps the durability of any 2026 rebound.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$68, RUSHA trades at roughly 20.5x trailing EPS of $3.27 and ~18.3x the $3.71 current-year consensus (~14.9x the $4.55 next-year estimate, which embeds a freight recovery). The dividend yield is modest at ~1.1%.

Against peers in Construction Machinery & Heavy Transportation Equipment, the multiple sits at the high end. Greenbrier trades at 10.1x, Trinity at 10.8x, GATX at 18.5x, Alamo at 18.5x, Federal Signal at 25.1x; peer median is 18.5x. Rush's better quality (durable aftermarket profit pool, disciplined balance sheet, active capital return) justifies a premium to the railcar-cyclical names like Greenbrier and Trinity, but the current price already captures that premium and then some. Analyst targets are above the current price (low $78, mean $83.75, median $84, high $89), implying ~12–27% upside if consensus is right.

The inflection-stock framework does not apply here. Estimate revisions are not running hot: current-quarter and current-year estimates are falling, not rising +30%, and revenue is contracting rather than accelerating >40%. This is a cyclical at a trough where the forward multiple is being supported by an anticipated recovery that has not yet shown in the operating line. On normalized mid-cycle earnings (the $4.55 next-year figure), ~15x is defensible, but realizing that requires the freight recovery and the 2026 pre-buy to materialize on schedule before the 2027 air pocket arrives.

The stock's 38% run off its low reflects genuine fundamental developments (backlog rebuild, interest-expense tailwind, buyback-driven EPS support), so prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark it down. At current price the asymmetric upside is limited: the bull case requires a clean cyclical recovery, the bear case is a stalled recovery into a known 2027 headwind, and the multiple no longer offers a margin of safety. We score valuation slightly below neutral and treat the apparent "discount to analyst targets" as a recovery bet rather than confirmed mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Rush is the scaled consolidator in a fragmented dealer industry, holding ~5.8% of the U.S. Class 8 market and ~5.7% of Class 4–7. Competition comes from Freightliner/Kenworth/Mack/Volvo dealer groups, independents, and parts wholesalers. The structural advantage is that industry differentiation has shifted from price toward uptime and service capability, which favors the largest networks with the best parts logistics and technician base.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Class 8 share slipped during the downturn, and new-truck and used-truck gross margins are compressing in an oversupplied market, so share and pricing both weakened. Against that, the aftermarket franchise is widening its relative advantage as scale-dependent service capability becomes the basis of competition, and M&A continues to consolidate the dealer base. Technician turnover improved to 35% in 2025 from 38% but remains the key operational constraint on the high-margin service business.

The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving over a full cycle, with the aftermarket moat intact even as near-term unit share and margins reflect cyclical pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Manufacturer dependence and change-of-control franchise risk (the defining structural risk).** The majority of revenue flows through Peterbilt/PACCAR and International. Peterbilt dealer agreements can be terminated if the Dealer Principals' voting power falls below 22% (currently ~40.8%), or if anyone other than Rusty Rush holds both Chairman and CEO/President. This ties the entire 26%-of-revenue Peterbilt relationship to one family's continued control and creates a material estate-tax/succession tail risk: a forced liquidation of Rusty Rush's Class B shares could trigger termination rights. This is the idiosyncratic risk that most investors, focused on the cycle, underweight.

**2027 emissions whipsaw.** The EPA 2027 Low NOx rule (expected January 2027) requires cleaner, more expensive engines, historically generating a pre-buy in the prior year followed by a post-mandate air pocket. Separately, the EPA's repeal of the GHG Endangerment Finding and Congress's rescission of CARB's preemption waivers have removed a major EV-mandate overhang, a net positive for the long-term parts/service model, but everything is under active litigation, creating genuine demand-timing uncertainty. Management blames CARB uncertainty for weaker 2024–25 California sales.

**Cyclicality.** U.S. Class 8 retail swings from ~196k (2020) to ~281k (2019). Q1'26 showed no inflection. A stalled recovery into the 2027 air pocket is the central bear scenario.

**Tariffs.** 25% commercial-vehicle tariffs on certain medium/heavy trucks and parts could raise prices and trigger backlog cancellations, though management expects realized price increases well below 25%.

**Long-term technology disruption.** Drivetrain electrification and autonomy could eventually erode the high-margin parts/service business (EVs have fewer wear parts). This is a multi-year tail, not a near-term threat, and the recent deregulatory shift has pushed it further out.

**Dual-class structure.** Class A carries 1/20th vote and Class B float is thin, limiting outside shareholder influence.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 42.2% against 30.3% historical, a rich 11.8-point spread, with the term structure in backwardation (1-month 42.2% vs 6-month 33.2%). The market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most plausibly the next earnings report, with an implied move of ±5.9% at the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is balanced at 3.0% and put/call OI is a benign 0.07, so there is no directional bearish positioning; the elevated near-term IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bet. The rich IV argues against buying near-term options and supports patience on entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

A 2026 freight recovery materializes on the ACT-forecast +5.7% Class 8 trajectory, amplified by a pre-buy ahead of the EPA 2027 mandate, driving a sharp second-half rebound in unit volumes. The aftermarket reaccelerates as miles driven inflect, lifting the absorption ratio back above 130%. The interest-expense tailwind persists, SG&A discipline holds, and the untouched $150M buyback authorization shrinks the share count further. Operating income returns to growth, next-year EPS of $4.55+ is achieved, and the stock re-rates toward analyst targets in the low-to-high $80s. Bull case ~$87 (~28% upside), roughly 19x the next-year estimate.

### Bear Case

The freight recovery stalls or proves shallow, new-truck margins keep compressing in an oversupplied market, and the 2027 post-mandate air pocket arrives without a meaningful 2026 pre-buy to bridge it. The aftermarket absorption ratio continues declining as miles driven stay depressed, and the interest-expense and tax tailwinds that flattered Q1'26 fade. The multiple compresses toward the truck-cyclical peer range (~12–14x), and EPS stagnates near $3.50. Bear case ~$50 (~26% downside), near the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business is high quality and well managed, but at ~18x forward EPS with a contracting operating line, falling near-term estimates, and a recovery that is forecast but not yet visible, neither the upside nor the downside is compelling enough for a directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of operating income growth (not interest/tax-driven EPS growth), confirming the cyclical turn is real.
- Class 8 retail volumes inflecting positive YoY alongside a sustained backlog above $1.5B, with the absorption ratio stabilizing or rising back above 130%.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that restores a margin of safety on the ~$4.55 normalized earnings power (forward P/E ~12x).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence the 2026 freight recovery is not materializing — Class 8 retail still declining double digits into the second half, or backlog rolling back below $1.2B.
- New Class 8 gross margin falling below ~7%, signaling structural rather than cyclical pricing erosion.
- Any concrete development around Rush family succession/estate planning that threatens the >22% voting threshold and Peterbilt franchise agreements.
- Next-year estimates reversing lower, removing the recovery that supports the current multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, late July) is the natural checkpoint; it will show whether the second-half recovery management is guiding toward is beginning, and whether operating income (ex-interest/tax) has inflected. The 2026/2027 emissions setup will progressively clarify through the year.

## Conclusion

Rush Enterprises is a well-run cyclical with a genuine structural advantage: an aftermarket profit pool that covers its entire fixed-cost base and keeps the company solidly profitable through a freight recession. Management has navigated the downturn with discipline, de-stocking inventory to cut financing costs, sustaining the dividend and buyback, and pursuing sensible bolt-on M&A. The balance sheet is sound once self-liquidating floorplan debt is stripped out, and the franchise is positioned to gain as the industry competes increasingly on service capability.

The case for patience rather than purchase rests on three points. The Q1'26 EPS growth was driven by halved interest expense, SG&A cuts, and a low tax rate, not operating recovery, and the operating line is still contracting. The stock has run to a forward multiple that already prices a freight recovery and 2026 pre-buy that have not shown up in volumes, while near-term estimates drift lower. And the 2027 post-mandate air pocket caps the durability of any 2026 rebound. The structural founder-control/franchise-termination linkage is a real tail risk that most investors underweight. At ~$68 the asymmetry is balanced; we would become buyers on operating-line confirmation of the cyclical turn or on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s. **HOLD.**
