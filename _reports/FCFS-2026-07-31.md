## Executive Summary

FirstCash operates 3,343 pawn stores across the U.S., Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Colombia and the U.K., plus American First Finance (AFF), a U.S. point-of-sale lease-to-own and installment lender. The pawn business is performing at a level that would justify a premium multiple in isolation: Q2 2026 revenue rose 29% to $1,074.7M, GAAP diluted EPS rose 58% to $2.12, adjusted EPS rose 40% to $2.50, consolidated pawn receivables were up 22% on a same-store basis, and the U.S. segment posted its twelfth consecutive quarter of double-digit same-store receivable growth. Management raised 2026 pawn guidance again on July 23 and completed the H&T point-of-sale migration (~300 stores) in under nine months, ahead of schedule.

The complication is the composition of that growth. Consolidated collateral is now 75% gold jewelry, up from 64% a year ago, and the U.K. business acquired in August 2025 is effectively a pure-play gold pawn operation (99% jewelry collateral). Wholesale scrap jewelry revenue — essentially a gold passthrough — rose 223% to $264.6M in the first half, more than all of FY2025 in six months, with scrap gross margins expanding (U.S. 19% versus 14%). Applying disclosed segment margins, consolidated scrap gross profit went from roughly $11.7M to roughly $68.8M, a $57M increase against a $79.8M increase in consolidated pre-tax income, or approximately 21% of that increase; disclosed currency translation added another $6.5M to Latin America pre-tax income (8%). Higher gold prices also raise the loan advanced per pledged item — average U.S. pawn loan $322 versus $286 — which inflates both pawn fees and the same-store receivable metric on identical transaction counts. A meaningful portion of the reported earnings acceleration is a gold and FX effect rather than unit growth.

Three things changed since our last review. AFF guidance was cut to gross transaction volume down ~10% and net revenue down 20-25%, with lease delinquencies at 25.6% versus 23.2%. The Ramsdens offer was raised 12.5% to 675 pence (~$307.5M), which on revolver funding takes pro forma net debt/adjusted EBITDA to roughly 2.95-3.12x against restricted-payment thresholds of 2.75x (2028 notes) and 3.0x (2030/2032/2034 notes). And sell-side gold forecasts were cut in late July for the first time since late 2023. The stock is down about 14% from its May high of $235.97, which prices some of this. We move to a lower overall score than the prior 6.7 not because the price fell but because the Q2 filings made the earnings-quality question quantifiable, the AFF trajectory worsened, and leverage headroom narrowed. HOLD, with a base case of $220 (+9%), a bull case of $255 (+26%) and a bear case of $150-160 (-21% to -26%).

## Company Overview

Three revenue streams flow from the same pledged collateral. Pawn loan fees are earned when a customer redeems; forfeited collateral becomes owned inventory resold at 35-44% retail gross margin; jewelry not sold at retail is melted and sold as bullion. The loans are non-recourse, so credit loss is not the exposure — collateral valuation is, and it moves with gold rather than with employment.

Store base at June 30, 2026: U.S. 1,212; Mexico 1,729; Guatemala 77; El Salvador 18; Colombia 12; U.K. 295. The company added 347 locations in the trailing twelve months, including 313 acquired for $453M, and has 174 more pending via Ramsdens. Management owns 466 pawn properties, 38% of the U.S. base.

AFF finances customers at roughly 16,700 active merchant partner locations, up 9% year over year. Management guides pawn to be over 90% of 2026 net revenue and segment pre-tax income; AFF has become a minority contributor with a shrinking profit stream.

We do not treat that as a solvency signal — the real collateral is $897.6M of over-collateralized short-duration non-recourse pawn loans, $570.5M of fast-turning inventory, and 466 owned parcels inside $855.0M of net PP&E. But it does mean a goodwill write-down would consume most of reported tangible equity, and $486.2M of that goodwill sits on AFF, the segment whose pre-tax income fell 39%. **Cash flow tells a different story than earnings.** Operating cash flow rose 36% to $330.4M in the first half, but company-defined free cash flow was $130.0M versus $131.1M — flat — because net pawn-loan funding absorbed $148.0M against $30.6M a year earlier. Outside that FCF definition, the company also spent $40.2M on real estate (23 locations), $11.5M on acquisitions and $37.9M of ordinary capex. TTM free cash flow $293.8M against $256M of dividends and buybacks leaves essentially nothing internally for the $307.5M Ramsdens purchase, which is why the $750M notes were issued and why the revolver will be drawn at close. We would not annualize first-half operating cash flow: pawn is seasonal, Q1 benefits from tax-refund redemptions, and the half included an $18.0M tax working-capital outflow. TTM OCF of $672.9M is the defensible anchor.

One data caveat worth stating: the standardized revenue tag for FCFS maps to retail merchandise sales only ($1,668.4M in FY2025) rather than total revenue ($3,661.0M), and a screen-derived Altman Z of ~1.7 rests on an EBIT/assets input inconsistent with actual FY2025 pre-tax income of ~$448M on $5.30B of assets. Neither is decision-relevant here.

## Growth Analysis

**What is genuinely operating growth.** Twelve consecutive quarters of U.S. double-digit same-store pawn receivable growth (+19% in Q2, +32% on a two-year stack) is not a gold artifact in its entirety, and 22% consolidated same-store receivable growth alongside a 10%+ larger store base is real franchise expansion. U.S. retail sales are guided +10-15% at 42-43% margins; Latin America retail sales up mid-20s% at ~35% margins; U.K. segment income was raised to $135-140M for 2026. Merchant doors at AFF grew 9%.

**What is price and currency.** Average U.S. pawn loan rose 12.6% to $322 and Latin America to $104 from $96, which management attributes to an increased mix of higher-value jewelry loans. Because receivable balances are a function of loan size as well as loan count, gold price appreciation mechanically inflates the same-store receivable metric that carries the bull case. Latin America revenue grew 41% in USD but 26% in local currency; the peso averaged 17.5 versus 20.0, a 13% favorable swing that added $6.5M to segment pre-tax income. Management quantifies the sensitivity at $0.10-0.12 of annual EPS per full point of USD/MXN and $0.07-0.09 for a comparable GBP move.

**What is contracting.** AFF gross transaction volume fell 14% in Q2 and 6% in the half; management guides full-year volume down ~10% and net revenue down 20-25%. Root cause is the late-2024 bankruptcies of American Freight and Conn's plus furniture-industry weakness. Credit metrics are worsening rather than stabilizing: lease delinquency 25.6% versus 23.2%, monthly net charge-off 6.4% versus 6.2%, finance receivable delinquency 22.3% versus 20.6%, provision rate 29.0% versus 27.9%. The diversification response into services verticals carries lower interest rates and is diluting portfolio yield — interest and fees on retail finance products fell 3% in Q2 despite a higher combined receivable-plus-off-balance-sheet balance.

**Estimates and delivery.** Consensus (adjusted basis, matching the company's non-GAAP EPS) stands at $11.29 for 2026 and $13.05 for 2027, revised up 3.6% and 5.1% respectively over 90 days, with the next-quarter figure up 8.7%. Four consecutive beats (2.1%, 14.3%, 3.1%, 14.1%) support confidence in the near-term numbers. These are healthy positive revisions but well short of the magnitude that signals a mispriced inflection; they read as consensus tracking a gold-supported quarter rather than discovering a new earnings regime.

The durability question is straightforward. Consensus 2026 EPS of $11.29 embeds gold near $4,000/oz. Third-party reporting places spot at roughly $4,010-4,045 in late July, with Reuters noting analysts cut forecasts for the first time since late 2023 after a pullback from January's record, and HSBC lowering 2026-27 estimates on a hawkish Fed tilt and stronger dollar. FirstCash does not disclose realized gold prices. It does disclose commitments to deliver 43,500 ounces between July 2026 and September 2027 at a weighted-average $3,920/oz (~$170.5M notional), below normal scrap production — a partial forward sale that provides certainty on that tranche and caps it modestly below current spot. We draw no inference about management's gold view from its existence; forward sales of scrap production are ordinary practice.

Peer medians in consumer finance sit at 10.6x P/E, but ALLY (10.6x), SLM (6.2x) and BFH (8.4x) are balance-sheet credit lenders whose multiples reflect charge-off cyclicality and capital constraints FirstCash does not carry. The relevant comparable is EZCORP at 16.6x trailing, against which FCFS at 20.0x trailing adjusted carries a premium justified by scale, geographic breadth and a materially better growth rate. Heartland Advisors' Q2 letter valued the company at 13x forward EBITDA, "in line with long-term averages," which is consistent with our 13.7x TTM figure.

On a headline basis, then, this is a fairly valued to modestly attractive stock: 17.9x forward earnings with a PEG of 1.15 and consensus targets of $220-252 all above the current price. The reason we score valuation at 5.5 rather than higher is that the denominator is gold-inflated. If gold settles 15-20% lower, scrap gross profit compresses toward the 2025 run rate, loan sizes and therefore pawn fees fall, and $570.5M of inventory (66% jewelry) requires markdowns. Normalized EPS in that scenario is closer to $9.50-10.50, on which the current price is 19-21x — not a discount for a levered, acquisitive, four-jurisdiction operator with a shrinking second segment. The market is not obviously wrong at $202; it is paying a fair multiple for earnings whose base is unusually dependent on one commodity.

**Base case: $220.** Roughly 19.5x 2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $11.29, equivalently ~14x an $865M full-year adjusted EBITDA estimate less net debt. That is a modest re-rating from the current 17.9x, supported by the raised U.K. guidance, Ramsdens closing, and continued double-digit same-store growth, and it sits below the $240.50 consensus mean because we discount the gold-supported portion of the earnings base.

## Competitive Landscape

FirstCash is the consolidator in a fragmented industry across three continents simultaneously — 313 stores acquired for $453M in twelve months, 174 pending, a stated pipeline of 35-40 more in the second half. The durable advantages visible in the filings are store density supporting a proprietary POS platform (FirstPawn), an owned real-estate portfolio of 466 properties that protects locations and limits future rent inflation, and demonstrated integration capability: ~300 H&T stores migrated onto FirstPawn in under nine months, ahead of schedule. That last point is the most decision-relevant evidence for the Ramsdens execution case.

The U.K. is the economically interesting front. First-half U.K. pre-tax margin of 37% exceeded U.S. (27%) and Latin America (20%), on an average pawn loan of $877 versus $322 and $104, with scrap jewelry at 44% of segment revenue at an implied ~43% gross margin against 19% in the U.S. That pattern is consistent with a direct gold-buying and refining operation, and it explains the strategic logic of doubling down. Two cautions: the segment has been consolidated for under a year with preliminary purchase accounting, its margin is being measured at peak gold, and its operating discipline is not yet at company standard — 13.7% of U.K. inventory is aged over one year versus 1.5% in the U.S. and 1.2% in Latin America, with 2.2x inventory turns versus 3.8x in Latin America. A 37% margin observed over two quarters at record gold prices is not a through-cycle margin.

AFF's competitive setting is the reverse. It competes in U.S. point-of-sale lease-to-own against Progressive Leasing, Katapult, Acima/Upbound and a wide BNPL field while its core furniture and appliance vertical contracts. The company's own risk language names merchant contraction or closure as a standing exposure, and American Freight and Conn's proved it. Merchant-level revenue concentration is not disclosed; growth in active doors from 15,300 to 16,700 while volume fell 14% establishes broader distribution but says nothing about whether concentration has been remedied.

Macro cross-currents cut both ways. Rising consumer stress raises pawn loan demand and lowers redemption rates, which pushes more collateral into higher-margin retail inventory. That same stress is precisely what is degrading AFF's subprime lease book. With pawn at over 90% of segment income, the net effect of a weakening consumer is favorable.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Gold is now the dominant single-factor exposure.** Seventy-five percent of collateral is jewelry, up 11 points in a year; scrap revenue tripled; inventory is 66% jewelry. A sustained gold decline compresses scrap revenue and margin, shrinks loan sizes and therefore pawn fees, and requires inventory markdowns simultaneously. The industry's counter-cyclical reputation obscures this, and the timing of the late-July sell-side gold downgrades against the stock's roughly 14% drawdown from its May high suggests the market has begun discounting it. We cannot establish causation.

**2. AFF deterioration and the $486.2M goodwill balance.** Guidance is for net revenue down 20-25% with delinquencies rising across both product lines. No impairment indicators were identified through June 30, but this is the most plausible location for a write-down. Such a charge would not touch cash, adjusted EBITDA or covenant ratios — it would consume most of tangible equity and mark the 2021 AFF acquisition as underdelivering.

**3. Off-balance-sheet credit retention at AFF.** Since Q3 2025 AFF has assisted customers in applying for unsecured installment loans underwritten and retained by a bank partner, while FirstCash reimburses the bank for principal plus accrued interest on any loan reaching 90 days past due. At June 30 the outstanding balance was $35.2M with a recognized liability of $18.3M, up from $13.8M; first-half provision $15.1M against $10.6M of charge-offs. The 52% liability-to-exposure ratio partly reflects lifetime loss recognition at inception on a young growing book, so it is not a clean coverage ratio, and bank-partnership structures are a standard response to state rate caps. The concrete items to track are that the program was ~6% of consolidated net revenues in 2025 and the underlying agreement expires August 2028. Note that the headline provision line now blends on- and off-balance-sheet exposure, so it is not comparable to the on-book receivable trend.

**4. Leverage headroom after Ramsdens.** Adding ~$307.5M of revolver borrowing with no EBITDA credit takes net debt/adjusted EBITDA to ~3.12x; crediting an illustrative $35-45M of Ramsdens EBITDA (a placeholder — we have no Ramsdens financials) gives ~2.95-2.99x. Restricted-payment thresholds are 2.75x and 3.0x with permitted baskets above, so this constrains unlimited buybacks rather than prohibiting them. Revolver availability drops to roughly $320M before the flagged 35-40 additional store acquisitions. The direction — less capital-return headroom in 1H 2027 than in 1H 2026 — is the durable conclusion. The 12.5% price increase to secure the Ramsdens board's agreement is unexplained in the filings.

**5. Currency and regulation.** A peso reversion toward 20.0 would reverse a visible portion of reported Latin America growth. The company operates under four regulatory regimes; an $11.0M CFPB litigation settlement was accrued in Q2 2025 ($0.21/share, excluded from adjusted results), and the underlying allegations, whether any consent-order obligations persist, and the substance of the June 2026 Texas reincorporation's changes to shareholder rights (filed under Item 3.03) are unverified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.8% against 29.7% one-year realized is rich, and the term structure is backwardated (40.8% at 20 days versus 36.0% at six months), which is the market pricing near-term stress. Put/call skew of +3.2% at the front expiry is described as elevated put demand, and it flattens to +0.3% at three months and -2.8% at six. The ±8.0% implied move for the August 21 expiry is notable because Q2 earnings have already passed; the plausible drivers are gold volatility and the Ramsdens scheme timetable. Two caveats limit the weight we place on this: the chain is thin (90 contracts across three expirations, zero reported put/call volume ratio), and the prior-snapshot comparison shows no IV change with the open-interest ratio dropping to zero, which reads as data sparsity rather than a positioning shift. Short interest at 5.3% of float and 4.1 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish position. The signal is directionally consistent with our thesis — the market is pricing a near-term event with mildly negative asymmetry — but it does not carry enough depth to drive the call.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $255

Gold holds above $3,900-4,000, supported by the structural reserve-asset bid and the Fed-independence premium now visible at the long end of the Treasury curve. The pawn franchise compounds: U.S. same-store receivable growth extends past twelve quarters, retail margins hold at 42-43%, the U.K. delivers $135-140M of 2026 segment income and Ramsdens closes by year-end at economics resembling H&T's (which, annualizing first-half segment pre-tax income of $72.8M against a ~$500M enterprise value, looks like a low-to-mid single-digit multiple of pre-corporate-allocation earnings). A weakening U.S. consumer raises pawn loan demand and forfeiture rates, feeding higher-margin retail inventory. AFF stabilizes at a smaller base and stops being a story. Consensus 2027 adjusted EPS of $13.05 at 19.5x gives $255. Add a re-rating toward peak multiples on demonstrated Ramsdens accretion and the number is higher.

### Bear Case → $150-160

Gold normalizes 15-20% lower as the dollar firms and real rates stay elevated. Scrap revenue reverts toward the FY2025 run rate, scrap gross margin compresses from 26% back toward 14%, average loan sizes fall so receivable balances and pawn fees decline on flat transaction counts, and $570.5M of 66%-jewelry inventory takes markdowns. The peso reverts to 20.0, removing $0.30-0.40 of annualized EPS. AFF's delinquency trend continues and the $486.2M goodwill balance is written down, producing a GAAP loss quarter and a visible marker on the 2021 acquisition. Post-Ramsdens leverage at ~3.0x limits the buyback offset. Adjusted EPS lands near $9.50 and the multiple compresses to 16-17x on lower earnings quality: $150-160, or 21-26% below the current price.

### What we would need to see to upgrade

Evidence that the pawn franchise's earnings are less gold-levered than they currently appear — specifically, two quarters of double-digit same-store receivable growth accompanied by stable-to-growing loan counts rather than rising average loan size, or U.K. segment margins holding near 37% through a gold pullback. Either would convert the current earnings base from cyclical to structural and justify paying 20x+ forward.

**Bear case $150-160 (-21% to -26%).** 16-17x normalized adjusted EPS of ~$9.50 after gold and peso normalization, with an AFF goodwill charge as the visible catalyst.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**

2. AFF gross transaction volume returns to flat or better year over year with lease delinquency below 24%, removing the impairment overhang on $486.2M of goodwill.
3. Price below $180 (under 16x 2026 consensus adjusted EPS, ~12x forward EV/EBITDA) with 2027 estimates still rising — that compresses the multiple enough to absorb a gold normalization scenario.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. Gold sustains below $3,300/oz for a quarter, which would push scrap gross profit back toward the ~$12M half-year 2025 level and require jewelry inventory markdowns.
2. Post-Ramsdens net debt/adjusted EBITDA prints above 3.0x with buybacks suspended, indicating the restricted-payment thresholds are binding.
3. AFF lease delinquency exceeds 28% or a goodwill impairment is taken, or the bank-partner off-balance-sheet liability rises above ~65% of stated maximum exposure.
4. Ramsdens closes materially above the revised 675p terms, or the company funds it with equity.

**Monitoring items with no threshold yet:** the substance of the Texas Certificate of Formation and new bylaws (filed as a material modification of securityholder rights); whether any CFPB consent-order obligations survive the $11.0M settlement; Ramsdens standalone financials, which would replace every placeholder in the leverage sensitivity above.

## Conclusion

FirstCash is a well-run consolidator whose operating execution — twelve straight quarters of U.S. double-digit same-store growth, four consecutive earnings beats, an integration delivered ahead of schedule, raised guidance — is not in question. The question is what the earnings are made of. The Q2 filings permit the decomposition, and it shows that gold-linked scrap gross profit and a 13% favorable peso account for a meaningful portion of the first-half pre-tax income increase (scrap gross profit alone ~21%, plus $6.5M of Latin America FX), with a further unquantifiable contribution running through larger loan sizes into pawn fees and the same-store receivable metric itself. Consensus 2026 EPS of $11.29 sits on gold near $4,000 at the same moment sell-side gold forecasts are being cut for the first time in almost three years.

At 17.9x that consensus, a PEG of 1.15 and 13.7x TTM EV/EBITDA, the stock is priced fairly for what it is delivering and expensively for what it would deliver at $3,300 gold. Layer on AFF net revenue guided down 20-25% with worsening delinquencies against $486.2M of allocated goodwill, pro forma leverage moving to roughly 3.0x against restricted-payment thresholds, an $18.3M and growing off-balance-sheet credit liability, and five insiders selling into $175-231 with no open-market purchases in six months, and the risk stack is heavier than the 9% upside to our base case compensates. Management quality and financial health — the dimensions that have most reliably separated winners in this framework — read as adequate rather than strong here: real execution credit for the H&T migration, offset by unidirectional insider selling including the incoming CEO, buybacks executed above the current price, incentive metrics that exclude acquisition costs and acquired-intangible amortization at a company acquiring $453M of stores a year, and cash generation that has stopped translating into distributable cash.

We maintain HOLD with a lower overall score than the prior review. This is a genuine growth hold rather than an uncertainty hold — the pawn franchise has identifiable catalysts (Ramsdens closing, $135-140M of U.K. segment income, continued store additions) and a base case above the current price. But the marginal buyer at $202 is underwriting the gold price, and we would rather pay under $180 for that exposure or wait for evidence that the same-store growth engine runs on loan counts rather than the gold fix.
