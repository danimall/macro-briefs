**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Standex has executed a deliberate portfolio rotation over the past two years, shifting its center of gravity from mature, cyclical industrial niches toward two secular-growth, high-margin end markets: electrical-grid instrument transformers (the ~$462M Amran/Narayan acquisition) and space/defense (McStarlite plus organic ramp). The strategy is working on the metrics that matter. Consolidated gross margin expanded from ~39.4% to 41.4% over the last nine months, Electronics operating margin runs ~26%, backlog under one year rose 22.1% YoY to $305M concentrated in the fastest-growing segments, and Adjusted EBITDA climbed ~21% to $170.6M in FY25 even as GAAP net income optically fell. Shares are up 86% over the trailing twelve months, with the stock re-rating to a ~$3.7B cap on roughly $790M of sales.

The fundamental case is credible, and the balance sheet has been de-risked faster than expected: funded debt/EBITDA sits at 2.18x versus a 3.5x covenant after the transformational deal, helped by the $68.3M Federal Industries divestiture. What holds this back from a BUY is valuation combined with the absence of forward momentum in estimates. At $303.65, SXI trades at ~37x trailing EPS and ~35x the $8.66 current-year consensus, a roughly 50% premium to the Industrial Machinery peer median of 23.6x. Estimate revisions are flat over 30 days and negative over 90 days (current-year -2.3%, next-year -4.0%), so the strong-inflection buy signal (rising revenue plus rising estimates) is absent. The premium is fully underwritten by expectations that grid and space growth persist and margins keep climbing — a reasonable base case, but one that leaves little cushion.

We rate SXI HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis is constructive: grid modernization, data-center power demand, and defense platform ramps are durable tailwinds, and Standex is a legitimate beneficiary, but the current price already discounts continued execution. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the mid-$200s or on a resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity following the July 30 Q4 FY26 print. Near-term options positioning (backwardation, 46.5% ATM IV, ±10.5% implied move) argues for patience through the print rather than chasing at record-adjacent levels.

## Company Overview

Standex is a diversified multi-industry manufacturer headquartered in Salem, NH, producing highly engineered components and systems under a "Customer Intimacy" model built on custom solutions and switching costs. Following a March 2026 reorganization (all periods restated), the company reports four segments:

- **Electronics** (~52% of 9M FY26 sales, ~25.9% operating margin) — magnetics, reed switches, and grid instrument transformers via the Amran/Narayan business. This is the growth and margin engine.
- **Aerospace & Defense** (formerly Engineering Technologies) — net/near-net formed components for aviation, space, defense, energy, and medical; includes McStarlite aerospace sheet metal. Fastest revenue grower at +37.7% over 9M FY26.
- **Scientific** — specialty temperature-controlled cold storage for medical/pharma/biotech/academic markets. High margin (~22.6%) but pressured by NIH funding cuts to research institutions.
- **Engraving & Hydraulics** — mold texturing, roll engraving, stealth-aircraft vents, and hydraulic cylinders. Mature, undergoing restructuring (four site closures).

The strategy is explicit: rotate the portfolio toward secular-growth, high-margin markets (grid, space, defense) and away from mature/cyclical ones (Hydraulics restructuring, Federal Industries divested). The company reports no customer above 5% of sales or receivables and a diversified, regionalized supply chain, which limits both credit and tariff concentration.

## Financial Analysis

The headline GAAP figures are distorted by M&A and must be read through. Revenue moved from $735M (FY22) to $790M (FY25), and momentum has accelerated: 9M FY26 sales rose 16.8% to $663M (organic +4.6%, acquisitions +11.8%, FX +0.9%), with Q3 FY26 up 8.1%.

Margin expansion is the single most important trend. Q3 gross margin ran 40.9%, driven by pricing discipline, productivity, and mix shift toward grid and space. The mechanism is straightforward: Electronics with grid runs ~26% operating margins versus mid-teens for legacy segments, so as grid and space grow and Hydraulics/Federal shrink, consolidated margins structurally rise. Adjusted EBITDA rose from $129M (FY22) to $170.6M (FY25), versus FY24's $140M, even as GAAP net income fell.

That GAAP decline is a head-fake. FY25 net income fell to $55.8M from $73.1M despite +10% revenue, entirely explained by $20.4M of deal costs and purchase-accounting step-ups plus a jump in interest expense to $23.9M (from $4.5M) on the debt-funded acquisition. On the other side, 9M FY26 net income attributable to common of $84.2M (vs. $40.9M) includes a one-time $56.8M pre-tax gain ($49.0M after-tax) on the Federal divestiture plus a $6M discrete tax benefit, so reported EPS should be quality-adjusted downward. The clean read is Adjusted EBITDA up ~21% and gross margins expanding, indicating the acquisitions are additive operationally.

Cash flow is solid: 9M FY26 operating cash flow from continuing operations rose to $49.4M (from $36.2M), with modest capex (~$20M over nine months, FY26 guide $31–34M). FY25 FCF of $41.3M was depressed by deal costs.

The balance sheet is the key watch item. Long-term debt rose from $149M (FY24) to $553M (FY25) to fund Amran/Narayan, then was paid down to $472.8M by 3/31/26. Interest coverage is 4.54x against a 2.75x minimum, with $188M available on the expanded $825M revolver. The concentration risk sits in goodwill ($585M) plus intangibles ($205M), which together are ~52% of total assets and exceed the $754M equity base — an impairment exposure if grid demand normalizes. $225M of debt is swapped to fixed at 3.48%; the blended effective rate is 5.80%.

Capital allocation is balanced and shareholder-aligned. Standex declared its 248th consecutive quarterly dividend in July 2026 and raised it ~6.3% to $0.34/quarter, a multi-decade streak. A $200M buyback authorization exists but repurchases have been minimal as cash was directed to M&A and deleveraging.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed rather than reassuring: two beats and two misses over four quarters, including a -2.8% miss in the most recent quarter (Q3 FY26, $2.21 vs. $2.27). That pattern argues against assuming consensus is conservative.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on secular tailwinds Standex has deliberately positioned against. Grid instrument transformers (Amran/Narayan) benefit from grid modernization, electrification, data-center power demand, and a utility capex super-cycle. Space and defense (A&D +37.7% over 9M FY26) ride commercial space commercialization and defense platform ramps.

Backlog is the strongest forward indicator, with Electronics at $177M and A&D at $126M of the $305M total — the growth concentrated exactly where margins are highest. The Narayan redemption mechanics provide a quantifiable, if expensive, signal of the underlying compounding: the 9.9% minority stake was redeemable at the greater of deal price or 12x trailing-twelve-month EBITDA, forcing an $18.8M re-measurement charge and a ~$64M final buyout in July 2026, implying Narayan's formula value roughly doubled within ~18 months of purchase. Operationally that validates the grid growth rate; financially it was a costly reminder that earn-out math can bite.

Against these positives, the estimate-revision picture is the constraint. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a mispriced inflection; it is a stable-to-slightly-softening consensus. Segment-specific drags (NIH funding cuts to Scientific, soft Hydraulics and automotive) partly offset the grid/space acceleration. The net is genuine mid-teens revenue growth carried by acquisitions and grid, but not a consensus in the process of being revised sharply higher.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the peer set — KAI 32.9x, MWA 19.4x, FELE 30.7x, KMT 19.4x, ESAB 23.6x, median 23.6x — Standex carries roughly a 50% premium. The dividend yield of ~0.44% offers no valuation support relative to the 1.1% peer median.

Part of the premium is justified. Standex's margin trajectory (gross margin +200bps YoY, mix shifting to ~26%-margin grid), higher revenue growth (16.8% 9M FY26 vs. flat-to-modest peers), and the deleveraging execution all warrant a multiple above the median. The closest structural comps by growth/quality profile (KAI 32.9x, FELE 30.7x) sit in the low-30s. But SXI trades above even those, and the PEG on ~15% forward EPS growth against a ~35x forward multiple is above 2, not the sub-1.5 that would flag cheapness on an inflection stock.

A stock that has appreciated 86% on genuine fundamental improvement is not "expensive" for that reason alone, and I do not penalize prior appreciation. But the valuation score measures price versus intrinsic value today. Analyst targets here (mean $290.80, median $290.00, high $323, low $260) sit below the current price, and estimates are drifting lower, so the "targets are floors on inflection stocks" logic does not apply. This is a stock at a premium multiple where both price and near-term estimates already reflect the growth story.

The valuation dimension is anti-predictive in the current regime — cheap sorts the wrong way — so I am not penalizing SXI for being expensive per se. What I am flagging is the absence of a specific reason the market is mispricing it upward from here. On a base case of $9.96 next-year EPS at a defensible 28-30x (a premium to peers for growth/margin, but below today's 35x), fair value lands around $280-300 — roughly the current price. The multiple, not the business, caps the score. I assign 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Standex holds niche market-leadership positions defended by custom-engineered products and switching costs, and its "Standex Value Creation System" provides a repeatable operational and acquisition playbook. The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable: the company is actively gaining share in structural-growth end markets (grid, space, defense) while divesting/restructuring lower-growth exposure. Grid instrument transformers position it as a scaling global player with a US-plus-India footprint via Narayan, and the backlog growth signals traction in space and defense.

The competitive risks are diffuse rather than acute. Management cites "lower-cost competition" as a generic risk, and the markets are fragmented and niche-specific, which cuts both ways: fragmentation limits any single competitor's ability to displace Standex, but also means no dominant moat. The absence of customer concentration (no customer >5%) reduces the risk of a single lost relationship. The margin trajectory — gross margin expanding faster than most peers — is the clearest quantitative evidence the competitive position is strengthening, driven by mix rather than pure pricing. I score competitive position 6.8, reflecting an improving trajectory in favorable end markets, tempered by the fragmented, niche nature of the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition integration and goodwill concentration.** Goodwill and intangibles from a still-fresh transformational deal exceed total equity. The entire bull thesis rests on Amran/Narayan executing; any integration stumble or grid-demand normalization risks impairment. This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and is not fully priced into a 35x multiple.

**Management transition mid-transformation.** The May 2026 promotion of CFO Ademir Sarcevic to EVP-Corporate & Group President, Electronics places the finance chief in operational command of the crown-jewel grid segment — a strong signal of grid's centrality, but it removes an experienced CFO from the finance seat during a transformation, with Danielle Rangel now signing as VP/Chief Accounting Officer and assuming CFO duties. Several other Item 5.02 filings across 2025-2026 warrant monitoring for continuity of financial discipline.

**End-market and policy drags.** Scientific is hurt by NIH funding cuts to academic/research institutions (a discrete, policy-driven headwind), and Hydraulics and general automotive are soft. These are specific, not sector-truism, risks.

**Tariffs and commodities.** MD&A flags tariff exposure across segments, Strait of Hormuz/Middle East supply risk, and rhodium/steel/copper inflation; the company is evaluating potential IEEPA tariff-refund implications following a Supreme Court decision.

**FX and pension.** The AOCI foreign-currency loss widened to ($112M) at 3/31/26 on larger Indian Rupee and Euro exposure post-Narayan. A frozen US pension carries an $88M unrecognized loss, though funded status is manageable.

**Redemption/earn-out mechanics.** The Narayan 12x-EBITDA redemption already forced its re-measurement charge and buyout; contingent-consideration structures elsewhere (SEPL earn-out) could similarly surprise.

The valuation itself amplifies risk asymmetry: at 35x forward with a mixed beat/miss record, a Q4 disappointment carries meaningful derating risk while a beat is largely expected. I score risk factors 5.3.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates near-term caution. ATM IV is 46.5% versus 38.6% historical volatility — rich, at the 65th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (46.5% near-term vs. 40.4% six-month), which the market uses to price imminent stress into the July 30 Q4 FY26 print. ATM IV has risen 9 points (37.5% to 46.5%) even as the stock fell 7.4% since early July, signaling growing uncertainty ahead of the catalyst. The ±10.5% implied one-month move is large. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.9%), so this is event-anticipation rather than directional bearishness. The takeaway for a HOLD: waiting through the print to buy post-IV-crush is the disciplined path, and the backwardation warns against establishing new positions immediately before earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($360-400).** Grid modernization and data-center power demand drive Electronics growth and margin above expectations; A&D backlog converts to accelerating space/defense revenue; consolidated gross margin pushes toward 42-43% as mix shifts further; deleveraging continues and the balance sheet supports a resumption of M&A or buybacks. Estimates inflect higher, and the multiple holds at 38-42x on next-year EPS approaching $10-11. The 22% backlog growth and Narayan's demonstrated doubling in value underpin this scenario.

**Bear Case ($200-230).** Grid demand normalizes from super-cycle levels, exposing the goodwill to impairment; NIH cuts deepen and Scientific weakens; a Q4 miss (plausible given the -2.8% Q3 print) triggers a multiple derating from 35x toward the peer median of 24x on ~$8.66 EPS, implying ~$210. The ±10.5% implied move shows the market recognizes this downside is live. Represents roughly -30% from current.

**Base Case ($295-310).** Grid and space growth continue at a healthy but not-accelerating pace; margins hold their gains; consensus stays roughly stable. Applying ~29x to $9.96 next-year EPS yields ~$290-300 — approximately the current price plus dividend. The business compounds, but the multiple leaves little room for the stock to re-rate further from here without upward estimate revisions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$200s (roughly 27-28x forward EPS), restoring asymmetric risk/reward on an unchanged business.
- Resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity — current-year and next-year consensus turning up rather than down — following the July 30 Q4 FY26 print, ideally alongside a clean beat and continued backlog growth above 15% YoY.
- Confirmation that Electronics/grid margins are sustaining or expanding beyond ~26% with no integration issues surfacing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge or evidence grid demand is normalizing (backlog growth decelerating below high-single-digits, Electronics organic revenue rolling over).
- A Q4 miss combined with a guidance cut, which would validate the bear multiple-compression scenario.
- Leverage rising back above ~3x on an acquisition without a clear ROIC case, or a dividend-coverage deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 30, 2026 Q4 FY26 print is the immediate reassessment point — it resolves the near-term earnings uncertainty the options market is pricing and will show whether estimate revisions turn. A secondary checkpoint is the next backlog and grid-margin disclosure in the subsequent quarter.

## Conclusion

Standex has earned a premium multiple through genuine transformation: a portfolio now anchored in grid electrification and space/defense, structurally rising margins, controlled post-deal leverage, and a 248-quarter dividend streak. The business is fundamentally stronger than the GAAP optics suggest, and the growth tailwinds are durable. Financial health has improved as deleveraging progressed, and the stock's 86% trailing-twelve-month gain reflects real fundamental progress rather than multiple speculation alone.

What keeps this a HOLD is the entry point. At a premium multiple with estimates flat-to-negative and a mixed beat/miss record, the price already discounts continued execution, and the recent price weakness reflects a modest de-rating rather than a mispricing to exploit. Analyst targets sit below the current price. The near-term options backwardation and implied move into the July 30 print argue for patience. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the mid-$200s or on confirmed upward estimate revisions after the next quarter. For now, hold for the growth thesis, but do not chase.
