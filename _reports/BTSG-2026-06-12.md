**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BrightSpring Health Services is a home- and community-based healthcare platform serving roughly 475,000 high-need Senior and Specialty patients daily across two segments: Pharmacy Solutions (~88% of revenue) and Provider Services (~12%). The investment story over the past nine months has been a clean, well-executed transformation: the Community Living divestiture closed March 30, 2026 for $810.9M net cash, leverage fell from 3.31x (Sept 2025) to 2.27x (March 2026), adjusted EBITDA grew 44.8% YoY in Q1'26 to $189.8M, and operating income rose 139% to $121.4M. The stock has tracked that improvement, moving from roughly $28 in October 2025 to ~$63, a 188% twelve-month gain.

The fundamentals are real and improving, and prior framing that treated the Inflation Reduction Act as a thesis-killer overstated its scope. The IRA headwind is concentrated in Home & Community Pharmacy (long-term-care/institutional pharmacy under PharMerica), which declined 9.2% YoY. The growth engine, Infusion & Specialty Pharmacy (~73% of the Pharmacy segment and growing 35%+), is not subject to the same Medicare Part D negotiation dynamics. The core driver of results is intact, and the Street reflects this with a unanimous 17-0 buy/strong-buy rating stable across four monthly periods.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation and downside asymmetry, not a bearish view of the business. The revenue growth is overwhelmingly low-margin specialty-drug pass-through: prescriptions dispensed actually fell 1.4% YoY, with all growth coming from revenue-per-script (+27%) via drug mix at ~9.5% Pharmacy gross margin. The durable signal is EBITDA dollars and deleveraging, not margin expansion. After the rerating, the stock trades at ~30x next-year consensus EPS ($2.11) and ~37x current-year ($1.68), with the analyst mean price target ($62.50) sitting essentially at the current price and the low target ($49) implying roughly -22%. This is a high-quality, accelerating, deleveraging business fully priced for that quality today.

## Company Overview

BrightSpring operates a home- and community-based delivery model built around two reportable segments:

- **Pharmacy Solutions (~88% of revenue):** Comprises (a) **Infusion & Specialty Pharmacy** (~73% of segment revenue, growing 35%+), driven by specialty branded and oncology drugs dispensed under owned patient relationships; and (b) **Home & Community Pharmacy** (institutional/LTC pharmacy, PharMerica brand), declining under IRA-driven Part D pricing pressure.
- **Provider Services (~12% of revenue):** Home Health, Rehab, Personal Care, plus hospice/primary care. Now the strategic growth focus following the March 2026 exit of the Community Living (intellectual/developmental disability services) business.

The company was formed via KKR and Walgreens Boots Alliance's 2017 PharMerica purchase and 2019 BrightSpring acquisition, IPO'd in January 2024, and as of June 2026 no longer has a controlling shareholder — both WBA and KKR have fully exited. The differentiation thesis is integrated pharmacy plus provider services delivered in lower-cost home settings, with cross-sell across services (roughly 9,000 patients receive multiple integrated services) framed as the long-term value-based-care opportunity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth (continuing operations):**
- Q1'26 revenue **$3.61B, +25.6% YoY**. Pharmacy +25.2% to $3.17B; Provider +27.9% to $442M, of which roughly 22.7 points came from the Amedisys/LHC home-health/hospice branch acquisition and ~5 points organic.
- 9M'25 continuing-ops revenue $9.36B, +28% YoY.

**Profitability — the central nuance.** This is a razor-thin-margin, high-volume distribution model. Consolidated gross margin is ~13.3% (Q1'26); the Pharmacy segment runs ~9.5%. Revenue per script rose 27% to $295.56 while gross profit per script rose to $28.03, a mix shift toward expensive specialty drugs that inflates revenue more than profit. Revenue growth of 25%+ is largely branded-drug pass-through, not unit volume growth; prescriptions dispensed fell 1.4% YoY.

Despite thin margins, operating leverage is genuine:
- Q1'26 operating income +139% to $121.4M; net income from continuing operations $74.3M (vs. $9.2M).
- **Adjusted EBITDA +44.8% to $189.8M.** FY2025 adjusted EBITDA guidance was $605–615M.
- Reported GAAP net income of $148.6M is flattered by the divestiture; total net income includes $74.3M from discontinued operations, which carried a $103.4M pre-tax gain on the Community Living sale. Adjusted EPS of $0.39 is the cleaner run-rate figure (vs. $0.73 GAAP).

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- Q1'26 operating cash flow $122.9M (vs. $101.6M). Working capital is volatile given specialty-drug inventory stocking.
- Cash rose to **$888.8M** (from $88.4M) entirely from the Community Living sale proceeds.
- Total debt ~$2.56B (First Lien Term Loan B-5 at SOFR+2.50%, ~6.17%, maturing Feb 2031), with a $475M revolver fully undrawn and $1.5B notional of rate swaps hedging variable exposure. A 1% rate move equals ~$10.1M annual pre-tax impact.
- **Leverage fell to 2.27x** (from 2.99x in Dec 2025), driven by both the divestiture and EBITDA growth. The first-lien covenant (≤6.90x, tested only when the revolver is >35% drawn) is comfortably clear.
- **TEU overhang:** 8M Tangible Equity Units mandatorily convert Feb 1, 2027 into 26.2M–30.8M new shares — material dilution, though largely already in the diluted share count (221M diluted vs. 193M basic).

**Earnings track record:** Three beats and one miss over four quarters — Q1'26 +23.3%, Q3'25 +41.6%, Q2'25 +26.3%, with Q4'25 missing by 7.6%. The pattern supports reasonable confidence in forward estimates while flagging that the model is not a metronomic beater.

Financial Health scores 6.8: the deleveraging, cash position, covenant headroom, and EBITDA growth are strong; the constraints are structurally thin margins and the debt load against a low-margin model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars, two of them durable and one optical.

First, the **infusion and specialty pharmacy engine** benefits from aging demographics, payor preference for lower-cost home settings, and growth in home infusion and specialty oncology. This is genuine BrightSpring volume — the company dispenses and owns the patient relationships rather than acting as a conduit for a larger PBM.

Second, **EBITDA-dollar growth and deleveraging** are the figures that matter for an inherently thin-margin business. Consensus estimate revisions are positive across all horizons: current quarter +9.4% over 90 days, current year +9.6%, next year +11.2% (to $2.11). These are solid, broad-based upward revisions consistent with continued execution.

Third — and where caution is warranted — the headline revenue growth is **margin-light mix shift**, not unit expansion. The entire revenue lift came from revenue-per-script. The framework's strongest inflection signal (revenue >40% with expanding margins, or estimate revisions >30% in 90 days) is not met here: revenue is +25.6% with structurally flat/thin margins, and revisions are +9–11%, not +30%+. The growth is real but qualitatively different from a margin-inflecting story.

The Amedisys/LHC branch acquisition (110 branches, ~$246M, contributing $78.5M revenue and $7.4M operating income in Q1'26) shows disciplined bolt-on M&A, but it also means a large share of Provider growth is inorganic (~22.7 of 27.9 points). Organic Provider growth was ~5 points.

Growth Prospects score 7.2: durable demographic and specialty tailwinds plus genuine EBITDA-dollar acceleration, tempered by the margin-light composition of revenue and the inorganic share of Provider growth.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the dimension that determines the HOLD.

On next-year EPS growth of ~25.6%, the PEG is roughly 1.17, which clears the framework's reasonableness threshold (forward P/E <30x, PEG <1.5) and means the stock should not be dismissed as expensive simply because it has run. Peer P/Es are dispersed: DaVita 18.5x, Chemed 24.1x (peer median), Sotera Health 38.2x, with two peers (HIMS, RDNT) at negative earnings. BTSG at ~30x next-year sits above the peer median for materially faster EBITDA growth, which the growth premium can support.

The reason the valuation score is 5.0 rather than 7+ is the absence of asymmetric upside at the current price. Three points anchor this:

1. **The rerating has captured the deleveraging/divestiture story.** The move from $28 to $63 coincided with the leverage drop, the divestiture close, and the EBITDA acceleration. Those are the catalysts the framework treats as fundamental, and they are now largely in the price.

2. **The consensus mean target equals the current price.** Analyst mean $62.50, median $63.50, low $49, high $71. The ratings are unanimously bullish (17-0), but price views bracket the current price with the mean at parity. On a genuine cheap-vs-rising-estimates inflection, consensus targets lag and act as floors; here the mean target sits at spot, which signals fair value with continued execution expected, not a discount.

3. **The margin-light model caps the multiple.** A business growing revenue 25% at 9.5% Pharmacy gross margin with falling unit volume cannot durably command a high-growth multiple on revenue. The valuation must rest on EBITDA dollars, and at ~$700M+ forward EBITDA against a ~$12.3B market cap and the debt load (EV ~$14B), the EV/EBITDA is ~19–20x — full for the category.

Valuation scores 5.0: fairly valued on forward EBITDA with a defensible PEG, but no identifiable mispricing to exploit and the consensus mean already at spot.

## Competitive Landscape

BrightSpring is one of the largest independent (non-retail-chain) providers in its pharmacy niches and a top home-based services player. The infusion/specialty business is gaining share into demographic and site-of-care tailwinds, and the integrated pharmacy-plus-provider model is a credible value-based-care positioning. Competition is real: Option Care Health and others in home infusion, Optum and Enhabit in home health, and intense reimbursement competition across government payors. The legacy LTC pharmacy book is in structural decline. Net trajectory is improving in the segments that matter (specialty/infusion, home health) and declining in the minority legacy slice. Competitive Position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement and government-payor dependence.** Roughly 40% of revenue is government (Medicare A–D plus Medicaid), with Part D alone ~30%. The IRA is a present, management-confirmed headwind, but its current scope is the Home & Community Pharmacy book, not the growth engine. IRA is a contained drag on a declining portion of the business today, with a multi-year watch as Part D negotiation lists expand to potentially touch a broader set of drugs. It does not impair the infusion/specialty engine now.

**Specialty drug concentration.** Growth is concentrated in low-margin, often limited-distribution branded drugs. Loss of access to a limited-distribution drug, adverse PBM contract terms, or drug tariffs (newly emphasized in risk disclosures) would hit the engine directly. This is the single most underappreciated tail risk.

**Dilution.** TEU mandatory conversion in Feb 2027 adds 26–31M shares, largely already in the diluted count but a known supply event.

**Leverage and rates.** Debt is partially hedged; the deleveraging trajectory and cash provide cushion, and the covenant is comfortably clear.

**Regulatory/legal.** Standard Medicare/Medicaid exposure to post-payment audits and False Claims Act actions; PharMerica has litigation history (the Silver matter settled in 2024). No single material case is currently flagged.

**Governance.** KKR's full exit removes both the controlling-shareholder overhang and the controlling-shareholder support; heavy Item 5.02 director/officer turnover during the transition warrants monitoring.

Risk Factors score 5.5: specialty-drug-access and government-payor concentration are genuine and not fully priced, and the IRA list expands over time, offset partly by the cash cushion and the contained current scope of the IRA hit.

The options market is not flagging downside risk the equity is missing, and confirms the constructive-but-not-cheap read. The 3m implied move of ~22% frames the volatility a position-taker should expect around the next earnings print (likely early August).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$71, +13% from ~$63):** Infusion/specialty continues at 35%+, adjusted EBITDA compounds in the mid-teens-plus on operating leverage, leverage falls below 2x, the cash funds accretive bolt-ons or buybacks at sensible prices, and value-based-care cross-sell begins to show in margins. Estimate revisions keep ticking up, and the Street's high target ($71) is reached as next-year EPS approaches $2.30+. The 17-0 consensus and rising estimates support the multiple.

**Bear case (~$49, -22% from ~$63):** Margin-light revenue growth (scripts already falling) decelerates as specialty mix matures; the IRA negotiation list expands beyond LTC pharmacy into the broader Part D book; a limited-distribution-drug access loss or PBM contract reset hits the engine; and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (~24x) on $2.11, landing near the Street's own low target of $49. The TEU conversion adds visible supply into Feb 2027.

**Base case (~$66, +5%):** Continued execution at the guided ~29–34% total growth / ~23.5–28% organic, adjusted EBITDA toward the high end of guidance, and a multiple that holds near current levels as estimates rise modestly. The stock tracks earnings growth rather than rerating further, given the consensus mean already sits at spot.

The genuine debate is whether EBITDA-dollar growth and cross-sell can keep outrunning structural margin compression. The evidence to date says yes, but the price already pays for that outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2'26 earnings report (expected early August 2026), with the TEU conversion (Feb 1, 2027) as the next structural event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence of adjusted EBITDA-*margin* expansion (not just dollar growth) sustained over two quarters, demonstrating that the integrated/value-based-care model is improving unit economics rather than only adding low-margin specialty revenue.
2. A renewed PEG gap — either continued estimate-revision velocity (next-year EPS revised toward $2.30+) that re-opens upside versus the multiple, or a price pullback that takes forward P/E meaningfully below ~25x while fundamentals hold.
3. The TEU conversion clearing without disruption, plus disciplined deployment of the cash into accretive M&A or buybacks at reasonable prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Sequential deceleration in infusion/specialty growth below ~20%, or a loss of limited-distribution drug access that impairs the growth engine.
2. IRA Part D negotiation expanding materially beyond the legacy LTC book into the broader Pharmacy revenue base, or adverse drug-tariff developments.
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin compression below current levels alongside a guidance cut, signaling the mix shift is eroding economics faster than volume can offset.

This is a growth_hold: a high-quality, accelerating, deleveraging business worth holding for continued EBITDA-dollar growth, where the price already reflects that quality and the asymmetry does not favor adding at ~$63.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold, constructive lean).** BrightSpring has executed a clean transformation: divestiture closed, leverage cut, adjusted EBITDA and operating income up sharply, and a substantial cash position. Prior framing that treated the IRA as a thesis-killer was overstated: the headwind sits on the declining LTC pharmacy book, while the infusion/specialty engine drives the business and is not IRA-impaired. The Street is unanimously bullish (17-0), justified on the business quality.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the valuation, after a 188% twelve-month move, is full-to-fair rather than cheap. Revenue growth is margin-light specialty pass-through (scripts down 1.4%), so the durable signal is EBITDA dollars, not margin inflection; the stock trades at ~30x next-year EPS with the consensus mean target essentially at spot; and the bear case to the Street's own low target ($49) is roughly -22%, well beyond the downside threshold that would justify adding here. The overall score of 6.3 — up from 6.1, reflecting the further deleveraging and the resolution of the KKR overhang — places this among quality holds with a bullish lean rather than a conviction long. Own it for the continued EBITDA-dollar growth; add on either demonstrated margin expansion or a pullback that re-opens a PEG gap.
