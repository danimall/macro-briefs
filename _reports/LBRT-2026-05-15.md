## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is a hydraulic fracturing services company undergoing a high-stakes strategic transformation into distributed power generation for data centers. The core frac business generates ~$600-650M annual Adjusted EBITDA but has seen operating margins collapse from 16% (2023) to 1.8% (2025) as service pricing compressed faster than costs. Management raised $1.3B in zero-coupon convertible notes in Q1 2026 to fund a new Liberty Power Innovations (LPI) division targeting 3 GW of deployments by 2029, with 400 MW committed to Vantage Data Centers for 2027 delivery.

The stock has risen 167% from its September 2025 low of $9.90, driven by three factors: the convertible raise signaling power ambitions, the Q1 2026 earnings beat ($0.14 actual vs. -$0.13 estimate), and WTI surging to $100+ on the Strait of Hormuz closure. Estimate revisions have been dramatic (+240% current quarter, +345% next quarter over 30 days), but these revisions represent a shift from expected losses to barely-positive EPS rather than a true earnings inflection from scale. At $32.69, the stock trades at 36.7x trailing earnings on a frac business that was operationally breakeven in 2025 before investment gains, with the power business entirely pre-revenue.

The 12-month thesis centers on two catalysts: (1) oil at $100+ drives E&P spending recovery, lifting frac margins through H2 2026, and (2) LPI milestones de-risk the power pivot as 2027 equipment deliveries approach. Both are credible but neither is assured — and at current prices, meaningful execution on both fronts is required to justify the valuation.

## Company Overview

Liberty Energy operates approximately 40 active hydraulic fracturing fleets across all major North American shale basins plus Australia's Beetaloo Basin. The company differentiates through proprietary digiFleet electric/hybrid pump technology (~500 patents), integrated proppant supply (two Permian sand mines), and data analytics capabilities. Revenue was $4.01B in FY2025, down from the $4.75B 2023 peak.

The transformative development is Liberty Power Innovations (LPI), announced January 2025 and now funded with $1.3B in zero-coupon convertibles. Key commitments include 400 MW to Vantage Data Centers (2027 delivery) and a 330 MW preliminary reservation with a Texas developer (phases in Q4 2027 and Q2 2028). The technology platform (Forte™ modular power, Tempo™ AI load management, Chorus™ grid optimization) leverages Liberty's existing gas compression and field logistics capabilities.

Founder Christopher Wright departed to become U.S. Secretary of Energy in February 2025. Ron Gusek, 20-year Liberty veteran and former President, serves as CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue declined 7.2% in FY2025 to $4.01B and continued compressing on a quarterly basis before Q1 2026 showed a 4.5% YoY improvement to $1.02B. The margin profile is the critical concern: operating income fell 81% from $389M (2024) to $73M (2025) on just a 7% revenue decline. Cost of services is sticky ($3.44B in 2025 vs. $3.47B in 2024 on $310M less revenue), reflecting ~$500M in annual depreciation as a fixed cost base. Q1 2026 operating income was $22M (2.2% margin).

**The Investment Gains Distortion:** FY2025 net income of $148M included $162.6M in gains on strategic investments (primarily Oklo and Tamboran). Excluding these, core operating earnings were approximately negative. Q1 2026 included $17.3M in investment gains. Reported EPS ($0.89 trailing) materially overstates operating profitability.

**Balance Sheet:** Following the convertible raises, Liberty holds $699M cash against $1.315B total debt ($1.295B zero-coupon converts + $20.5M term loan). The zero-coupon structure eliminates ~$65-80M in annual cash interest expense versus traditional debt. However, if shares remain below conversion prices ($34.50 and $37.44), the company must repay $1.3B principal at maturity (2031-2032). The credit facility remains undrawn with $489.5M available, and an amendment permits a $600M bridge loan for power expansion.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $610M, but capital expenditures of $595M left only $14M of free cash flow. Q1 2026 operating CF was a weak $8.4M (working capital absorption of $113M). Capital deposits and construction-in-progress grew from $295M to $385M in the quarter, reflecting accelerating power equipment investment. This business currently generates negligible free cash flow.

**Adjusted EBITDA:** $634M in FY2025, annualizing at roughly $500M based on Q1 2026's $126M run-rate. At a $5.3B market cap, this implies ~8.4x trailing EBITDA and ~10.6x annualized Q1 run-rate EBITDA, which is expensive for a North American frac services business (peers like TDW at 13.7x P/E, OII at 11.3x P/E represent much lower multiples on profitable operations).

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth vectors exist:

**Core Frac Business — Cyclical Recovery Thesis:** With WTI averaging $99.85 in April 2026 (versus $65.45 average in FY2025), E&P capital spending should accelerate through H2 2026. Liberty's operating leverage means a 10-15% revenue increase could drive 50-100%+ operating income growth given the high fixed-cost base. Management expects sequential revenue growth in Q2. Completion intensity continues rising (25M lbs proppant/well in 2025 vs. 6M in 2014), structurally supporting per-fleet economics.

**Power Business — Multi-Year Platform Build:** The 3 GW target by 2029 represents potentially transformative economics. At industry power margins of $300-500K per MW annually once operational, a fully-deployed 3 GW platform could generate $900M-$1.5B in annual revenue. But this is 3-4 years out, pre-revenue today, with acknowledged execution risk ("we have not directly engaged in the distributed power solutions business before"). The 400 MW Vantage commitment with 2027 delivery is the nearest credible milestone.

**Estimate Revisions:** The +240% current quarter and +345% next quarter revision velocity over 30 days is dramatic but misleading. Estimates moved from -$0.06 to +$0.08 (current quarter) and -$0.05 to +$0.11 (next quarter). These are tiny absolute numbers. Full-year consensus is $0.31 for FY2026 and $0.36 for FY2027, implying continued minimal profitability. The revision trajectory confirms the cyclical bottom is in, but does not signal rapid earnings acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.69 with trailing EPS of $0.89 (inflated by $163M+ investment gains), the trailing P/E of 36.7x significantly overstates the multiple on operating earnings. Stripping investment gains, the frac business earned approximately zero in FY2025. The forward P/E based on consensus FY2026 EPS of $0.31 is over 100x.

Against peers: Archrock (20.2x P/E), Tidewater (13.7x), Oceaneering (11.3x). LBRT trades at a substantial premium to the oilfield services sector, reflecting the market pricing in power optionality.

The implied valuation math: if the frac business is worth $4-5B (6-8x normalized EBITDA of $600-700M at full utilization with $100 oil), the remaining $0-1.3B of implied value represents the market's assessment of LPI power optionality minus the $1.3B debt. At current prices, the market assigns approximately zero to negative value to the power business after netting convertible debt — which means the upside case (power executes successfully) could be substantial, but also that the downside case (power fails, frac cyclically weak) has $1.3B of debt to service.

The stock sits within 5% of both convertible conversion prices ($34.50, $37.44). This creates a technical zone where the converts act as a soft ceiling on the equity (arbitrageurs short stock against convert positions at/above conversion).

## Competitive Landscape

**Frac Services:** Liberty competes with Halliburton, Patterson-UTI/NexTier (merged), ProFrac, and ProPetro. Liberty differentiates through digiFleet technology (25% lower emissions, fuel cost savings for clients), integrated supply chain, and operational efficiency. Market share is approximately 10-12% of active U.S. frac capacity. Equipment attrition across the industry creates a gradual tightening dynamic.

**Power Generation:** This is a greenfield entry against established competitors (utilities, independent power producers, other distributed generation specialists). Liberty's differentiation rests on gas logistics expertise and a technology platform purpose-built for AI workload variability. The ERCOT demand quadrupling projection by 2032 suggests the addressable market is large enough for multiple winners.

**Competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving:** The digiFleet technology continues gaining share, the power pivot opens an entirely new TAM, and equipment attrition across the frac industry should support pricing recovery. However, Liberty has no proven track record in power generation.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Power Pivot Execution (HIGH):** $1.3B invested in a business the company explicitly acknowledges it hasn't operated in before. Equipment deposits are non-refundable. Contracts are developmental agreements, not firm purchase agreements with end-users. Failure scenario: equipment ordered but contracts don't convert → stranded capital.

2. **Debt Maturity Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** If shares remain below $34.50-$37.44 through 2031-2032, $1.3B in principal repayment comes due. FY2025 FCF was $14M. The company cannot organically repay this debt without either share appreciation (triggering conversion) or refinancing.

3. **Core Business Cyclicality (MEDIUM):** Despite $100 oil today, the Strait of Hormuz closure is a geopolitical event that could resolve. A diplomatic resolution bringing oil to $85-95 would reverse much of the expected E&P spending recovery. At $65 oil (2025 average), this business generates negligible operating profit.

4. **Customer Concentration (MEDIUM):** Top 5 customers = 39% of revenue. Occidental and XTO Energy (ExxonMobil) each exceed 10%. Loss of either would materially impair revenues.

5. **Dilution Risk (MEDIUM):** If stock reaches $65-72 range (above capped call strikes), dilution is fully exposed. At conversion prices ($34.50-$37.44), the initial 36.3M shares represent 22% dilution to existing holders.

## Investment Thesis

- Oil sustains $95-110 through H2 2026 → frac margins recover to 8-10% operating margin → $350-450M operating income
- LPI secures additional contracts; Vantage 2027 milestone remains on track
- FY2027 EPS reaches $1.50-2.00 as both businesses contribute
- Multiple expands to 20-25x forward on hybrid services/power platform valuation
- Stock converts above $34.50, removing debt overhang

**Bear Case ($18-22, -33% to -45%):**
- Oil retreats to $75-85 on Hormuz resolution → E&P spending flat/down → frac margins stay compressed at <3%
- LPI contracts fail to convert from development to purchase agreements; equipment costs escalate
- FY2027 EPS remains below $0.50; market de-rates from 36x to 15-20x
- Convert conversion unlikely; $1.3B maturity risk overhangs equity

**Base Case ($30-35, -8% to +7%):**
- Oil remains $90-105; gradual frac margin recovery to 4-6% operating margin
- LPI makes incremental progress (first equipment deliveries, additional small contracts) but remains pre-revenue through 2026
- FY2026 EPS of $0.30-0.50; stock consolidates near current levels while execution unfolds

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $24-26 range (7-8x normalized EBITDA on frac alone, providing free optionality on power business)
- LPI announces a finalized power purchase agreement (not development agreement) with credit-worthy counterparty at specified pricing
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating margins exceed 6% demonstrating cyclical recovery at $100+ oil

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Oil falls below $80/bbl sustained (Hormuz resolution) without corresponding cost reduction
- LPI contract cancellations or material delivery delays on 2027 commitments
- Operating margins remain below 3% for two consecutive quarters despite $95+ oil
- Insider selling accelerates (3+ executives, >50% holdings)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) is the next critical data point — will reveal whether $100 oil is flowing through to margins and provide LPI updates. The May 7, 2026 8-K material agreement filing may contain power business contract details worth monitoring.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy presents a genuine optionality situation: a cyclically-depressed frac business (barely profitable in 2025) combined with an aspirational but potentially transformative power generation platform, funded with $1.3B in zero-coupon debt. Oil at $100+ should drive frac margin recovery, and LPI milestones provide a specific catalyst runway into 2027.

However, at $32.69 the stock already prices considerable success on both fronts. Trailing P/E of 36.7x (inflated by investment gains), forward P/E >100x on consensus estimates, and a premium to all oilfield services peers mean the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The power business remains pre-revenue with acknowledged execution risk, the core business has no demonstrated profitability at current pricing absent $100 oil, and $1.3B in convertible debt creates a binary dynamic: success triggers conversion (dilution capped by capped calls), failure creates a maturity wall.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth thesis. The specific 12-month catalysts (frac margin recovery at $100+ oil, LPI contract milestones, 2027 delivery timelines) justify maintaining exposure rather than avoiding the name. But initiating a position at current levels does not offer sufficient margin of safety given execution uncertainty across both businesses and a valuation that requires both to succeed.
