## Executive Summary

First Solar is America's dominant thin-film solar manufacturer, trading at 15x trailing earnings ($14.21 EPS) and approximately 13.4x consensus FY2026 estimates ($17.45). The stock sits 18% below its 52-week high of $286 but has recovered 72% from its June 2025 low of $136. The company delivered 24% YoY revenue growth in Q1 2026, beat consensus EPS by 10%, maintains $2.3B net cash, and holds a 47.9 GW contracted backlog valued at approximately $14B through 2030.

The fundamental case is attractive at this valuation. The problem is a specific, identified, and time-bounded policy risk: the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs removed First Solar's primary competitive protection against Chinese crystalline silicon imports. The Section 122 replacement tariffs are capped at 15% for a maximum of 150 days — expiring approximately August 2026. If no new tariff authority is established before expiration, First Solar's pricing power and market position face material impairment. This is a falsifiable, binary event that will resolve within 3 months and prevents an upgrade to BUY today despite otherwise compelling fundamentals.

## Company Overview

First Solar manufactures utility-scale photovoltaic modules using proprietary cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film technology. Unlike conventional crystalline silicon panels (dominated by Chinese manufacturers), CdTe uses approximately 2-3% of the semiconductor material, employs a fully integrated continuous manufacturing process, and does not depend on Chinese polysilicon supply chains. The company operates 6 U.S. facilities (5 operational, 1 commissioning H2 2026) plus plants in India, Malaysia, and Vietnam.

The company's market position in the U.S. is defined by a multi-layered trade protection regime: AD/CVD orders (14-3,400% duties on SE Asian crystalline silicon), Section 45X manufacturing credits (~$1.6B annually), FEOC restrictions under the OBBBA, and — until the Supreme Court ruling — IEEPA tariffs of 19-50%. With the IEEPA tariffs struck down and replaced by weaker Section 122 authority, one layer of this protection structure has been materially weakened.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth faces headwinds despite strong fundamentals:

1. **Volume growth remains robust:** 16.1 GW produced and 17.5 GW sold in 2025. Capacity continues expanding with the 6th U.S. plant commissioning H2 2026.

2. **Backlog provides visibility:** 47.9 GW contracted through 2030 at average ASP of ~$0.30/watt. This provides approximately 3 years of revenue visibility at current production rates.

3. **Technology advancement:** CuRe modules in commercial production; one Ohio facility permanently converting to CuRe in 2026. Perovskite development line under construction. These improve energy yield and degradation rates, supporting premium pricing.

4. **AI/data center demand:** Utility-scale solar is a primary capacity addition for hyperscaler electricity demand.

**Offsetting factors:** FY2026 guidance implies flat revenue growth, reflecting tariff uncertainty on international operations. The company is consolidating toward U.S. manufacturing (higher cost, higher 45X benefit) and reducing international production due to Chinese pricing pressure. Consensus EPS deterioration is driven by policy uncertainty rather than demand weakness, but the impact on near-term earnings is real.

## Competitive Landscape

First Solar's competitive moat has multiple layers, but one has been structurally weakened:

**Intact protections:**
- AD/CVD orders with duties up to 3,400% on SE Asian crystalline silicon (issued June 2025)
- Section 45X manufacturing credits ($1.6B/year, available through 2032 with phase-down from 2030)
- FEOC restrictions under OBBBA limiting competitor access to IRA credits
- Technology differentiation (CdTe vs. crystalline silicon, non-Chinese supply chain)
- Patent portfolio offensive (suits against JinkoSolar, Canadian Solar, USITC petition against 10 entities)

**Weakened protection:**
- IEEPA tariffs struck down (Feb 2026); Section 122 replacements capped at 15% for 150 days max
- If no new tariff authority is established by ~August 2026, this layer disappears

**Emerging threats:**
- China tellurium export controls (February 2025) create a single-point-of-failure supply risk
- India MNRE efficiency threshold proposal could exclude First Solar from ALMM qualification in 2027
- N-type TOPCon technology gaining efficiency share globally
- Chinese manufacturers producing below cost with unlimited state support

The competitive position is stable in the medium term due to the multi-layered protection, but the trajectory of protection is concerning. The IEEPA expiration without replacement would be the first material weakening of the trade protection structure since its construction.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks ranked by probability × impact:**

1. **Tariff authority expiration (HIGH — binary, 3-month resolution):** If no new authority (Section 301 reinstatement, new legislation, or executive action under alternative statute), Chinese crystalline silicon modules flood U.S. market at 30-50% below First Solar's pricing. This compresses both volume and ASP.

2. **45X credit reduction or elimination (MEDIUM — longer-dated):** Credits are legislatively available through 2032 but OBBBA introduced FEOC restrictions. Any further Republican legislative action to curtail clean energy credits would be devastating given 45X represents ~75% of gross profit. Phase-down from 2030 is already legislated.

3. **Tellurium supply disruption (MEDIUM — binary):** China controls significant tellurium production. February 2025 export controls are in effect. A full embargo would halt CdTe production. U.S. strategic stockpile ($12B critical minerals fund) provides partial mitigation.

4. **Series 7 quality issues (LOW-MEDIUM):** $35M-$75M remaining estimated liability. Reputational risk ongoing but contained given settlements underway.

5. **BP/Lightsource litigation (LOW):** $323.6M sought; $175M counterclaim. Demonstrates backlog execution risk — contracted does not mean collected.

## Conclusion

First Solar presents an unusual analytical configuration: objectively cheap (13.4x forward), fundamentally strong (24% growth, net cash, massive backlog), but facing a specific, identified policy risk that will resolve within 3 months. The IEEPA Supreme Court ruling struck down the tariff regime that had provided FSLR with strong competitive protection, and the Section 122 replacement is temporary and weaker.

The HOLD recommendation reflects an inability to quantify the probability of tariff replacement with sufficient precision. Estimated probability: 55-65% that some form of protection is maintained (administrative action, new legislation, or political imperative given manufacturing jobs at stake in Ohio). But the consequence of the 35-45% adverse scenario is severe enough — potentially 30%+ downside — that the asymmetry does not cleanly favor BUY at $233. At $200 or below, the risk/reward shifts decisively toward BUY regardless of policy outcome (backlog + net cash provide floor value). The current price requires policy clarity to unlock further upside with acceptable risk.
