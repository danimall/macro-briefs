## Executive Summary

Highwoods has delivered the first genuinely encouraging quarter in four years. Q2 2026 revenue rose 7.9% to $216.4M, NOI rose 6.8% to $146.2M, FFO of $0.90 beat the $0.86 consensus, period-end occupancy inflected from 85.3% at year-end to 85.7%, and the company signed 1,026,262 square feet of second-generation leases at a +20.9% GAAP re-leasing spread — roughly double Q4 2025's volume at a wider spread. Leverage fell from 43.7% to 42.1% of undepreciated book value, the $750M revolver is entirely undrawn, and cash stood at ~$195M as of July 21. Reported 2026 FFO guidance of $3.46–$3.70 (midpoint $3.58, secondary sources only — see the verification note below) would be the first per-share increase since 2022, ending a run of $3.90 → $3.75 → $3.57 → $3.40 in Core FFO.

Three facts constrain how much to pay for that. First, the growth is bought rather than grown: the 10-Q's own attribution of the $15.8M Q2 revenue increase is acquisitions +$15.6M, same-property +$5.4M, development +$1.2M, dispositions −$6.4M. Same-property *cash* NOI was +0.3% in the quarter and **−0.2% for the first half**; the +1.4% GAAP figure carries $5.2M of straight-line and lease-intangible adjustments. Second, the headline GAAP EPS of $0.85 contains $79.0M of disposition gains ($0.70/share on 112.3M diluted shares); ex-gain operating EPS was approximately $0.15. Third, and most important, FFO overstates free cash. First-half FFO of $194.7M less $84.6M of tenant-improvement and leasing capital and $26.8M of building improvements leaves $83.3M against $113.3M of distributions — 73.5% coverage. Routed through the cash flow statement: $188.1M of operating cash flow less $111.4M of real-estate capex less $113.3M of distributions equals **−$36.6M**, before any development or acquisition dollar. The gap was bridged with $297.5M of net disposition proceeds and $197.2M of joint-venture partner equity.

At $31.45 the stock trades at 9.04x trailing FFO, 8.79x the reported 2026 midpoint, and an implied nominal cap rate of ~8.2% (~7.6–7.9% adjusting for non-cash NOI and G&A) against a ~5.43% marginal cost of unsecured debt. That is a mid-range office-REIT valuation, not a distressed one, and consensus sits at $31.22 mean / $31.00 median — essentially spot. The prior two AVOID ratings on this name, issued when the stock traded near $21.50 and $26.27, proved directionally wrong; the shares rallied 73% off the March 27 low of $20.45 to a July 29 high of $35.44 before settling 11.3% lower. The current setup is different from either: the operating news has improved and the valuation has re-rated with it, leaving a modest positive expected return that is almost entirely the dividend. **HOLD, on income grounds.**

## Company Overview

Highwoods is a self-managed Sunbelt/Southeast office REIT operating through an UPREIT structure (98.2% ownership of Highwoods Realty LP). The portfolio was 27.7 million rentable square feet in service at June 30, 2026, plus 0.6 million square feet under development and land supporting roughly 3.5 million square feet of additional office build-out. There is no diversification into industrial, residential, or data centers — this is a pure office bet.

The revenue base at December 31, 2025 was Raleigh 21.6% (89.3% occupied), Nashville 18.7% (81.8%), Atlanta 16.9% (86.4%), Charlotte 12.8% (87.1%), Tampa 10.3% (88.8%), Orlando 6.2%, Richmond 4.8%, and a residual "Other" bucket at 8.7% on 75.7% occupancy. Dallas appeared as a reported segment for the first time in Q2 2026 ($3.4M revenue, $2.2M NOI). The strategic direction over the last eighteen months has been unambiguous: exit Richmond and the Pittsburgh/Kansas City residue, trim Nashville and Atlanta, and concentrate capital in Raleigh, Charlotte, and Dallas.

Tenant granularity is genuinely unusual for an office REIT. Only two customers exceed 3% of annualized GAAP rent at June 30, 2026 — Bank of America at 4.2% and Asurion at 3.3% — with the top 20 at 28.3% of revenue and a 7.3-year weighted-average remaining term. The capital structure is predominantly unsecured: of $3,515.6M total debt, only $699.2M is secured, at a 4.44% weighted-average rate versus 4.45% unsecured, with just $350.0M (10%) floating and unhedged.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Rental and other revenue declined from $834.0M (2023) to $825.9M (2024) to $806.1M (2025), a 2.4% fall in the most recent year attributed by management to dispositions (−$18.8M), lower same-property revenue (−$13.9M), and out-of-service properties (−$6.2M), partly offset by Charlotte and Raleigh acquisitions (+$17.8M). NAREIT FFO per share went $3.83 → $3.61 → $3.48; average occupancy went 89.2% → 88.0% → 85.5%. Consolidated same-property NOI fell 1.8% in 2025.

**Leasing economics are the strongest datapoint in the file.** Q2 2026 second-generation signings: 1,026,262 sf at a 6.0-year average term, $40.97/sf GAAP rent, a +20.9% spread over the prior lease on the same space, with $4.43/sf/year of tenant improvements and $1.37/sf/year of commissions. Combined leasing capital is roughly 11% of GAAP rent — modest for Class A office. Annualized GAAP rent per occupied square foot rose from $31.89 (2022) to $34.80 (2025), +9.1%, while average occupancy fell 5.3 points. Highwoods is renting less space at materially higher rents, which is what the winning side of a flight-to-quality looks like.

One definitional caution: these spreads are disclosed as *GAAP* comparisons, embedding straight-line and concession amortization. A +20.9% GAAP spread does not establish a +20.9% cash spread, and Highwoods does not disclose cash-over-cash spreads in these tables.

**Cash coverage is the central financial fact.** The FFO payout ratio reads a comfortable 55.9% on the guided midpoint ($2.00 ÷ $3.58). It does not survive a deduction for the capital required to produce that FFO, as shown above: 73.5% coverage on an FFO-less-capex basis, −$36.6M on the operating-cash-flow route. Two offsets deserve weight. Conventional AFFO deducts only recurring second-generation capital, and part of the first-half spend is unambiguously growth — the Bloc 83 ($210.5M) and Terraces ($109.3M) total-investment figures explicitly include planned near-term building improvements. And the quarter's exceptional leasing volume front-loaded TI commitments (roughly $27M from Q2 signings alone) that should convert into NOI on a 12–24 month lag. Normalizing half of first-half leasing capital as growth spend produces approximately break-even coverage rather than a 27% shortfall. The direction of the finding survives either treatment; the magnitude does not.

The practical implication: the dividend is not at imminent risk — $195M of cash, a fully undrawn $750M revolver, falling leverage, no disclosed covenant issue — but there is no cushion, and the dividend plus portfolio-maintenance capital is currently part-funded by shrinking the asset base. An FFO-payout screen will not detect this.

**Balance sheet and maturities.** Leverage of 42.1% of undepreciated book value genuinely improved (from 43.7%) on dispositions and JV equity; the debt-plus-preferred-to-total-capitalization improvement from 55.3% to 51.1% is mostly a share-price effect and should be discounted. Coverage: NOI/interest of 3.51x in Q2 and 3.57x for FY2025; an adjusted-EBITDA proxy gives ~3.4x; net debt to annualized first-half EBITDA is ~6.3x (annualized from one half-year — treat as a sensitivity), which is mid-range for office and offers limited cushion if occupancy resumes falling.

The only meaningful near-term maturity is $289.1M of 3.875% notes due March 2027 (reduced from $300M by an $11.0M open-market repurchase). Refinancing at the ~5.43% yield on the most recent unsecured issue (the $350M January 2033 notes) adds roughly $4.5M of annual interest, about $0.04 of FFO per share. Manageable. Term loans were pushed out twice — $200M to January 2029 and $150M to June 2029. On the other side of the ledger, $350M of 7.650% notes due 2034 carry a $26.8M coupon; refinancing those at market would save ~$7.9M annually (~$0.07/share) but not before 2034 absent a premium tender.

A small unrecognized positive: mortgages and notes payable carry a fair value of $3,414.2M against $3,515.6M book, a $101.4M (2.9%) discount that widened from $83.2M at year-end. Below-market assumable debt is worth roughly $0.90/share in an NAV or acquisition context and is invisible in book equity. It should not be double-counted, since the same rate environment that discounts the debt pressures asset values.

**On the earnings-surprise record.** The reported track record (three beats, one miss, including a 46.3% Q2 beat) is built on GAAP EPS and is dominated by disposition gains. The correct like-for-like comparison is FFO of $0.90 versus the $0.86 consensus — a genuine but modest $0.04 operating beat. Do not extrapolate the GAAP surprise magnitudes.

## Growth Analysis

The bull path is mechanical and identifiable: occupancy guided to 86.0–87.0% average for the remainder of 2026 from 85.7%, with 1.0 million square feet of Q2 signings at +20.9% GAAP spreads commencing over the next four to six quarters. Same-property cash NOI is a lagging construct by design — it reflects leases already in place, not leases just signed. Flat cash SPNOI concurrent with record leasing volume at record spreads is consistent with the trough of a commencement cycle. If that read is correct, organic cash NOI growth appears in 2027, not 2026.

The bear path on growth is equally identifiable. Nashville — 18.7% of rent at 81.8% occupancy — saw segment NOI fall 9.2% year over year in Q2 and 5.3% for the half, and remains the largest single lever on organic performance; the FY2025 10-K discloses a concrete cause in the cancellation of a 110,000 sf backfill lease in the former Tivity building. The "Other" bucket runs at 75.7% occupancy and is the most likely source of further impairment (following $8.8M in 2025 and $24.6M in 2024). And 31 leases with 24 federal agencies covering 748,955 sf and $21.6M of annualized rent (2.71%) carry only a 3.6-year weighted-average remaining term, the shortest of any top-20 relationship, into an environment of active federal space consolidation.

The company guides to up to $400M of new development starts in the balance of 2026 (2028+ deliveries) and up to $250M of additional acquisitions, against $100–250M of additional dispositions beyond $73.5M under contract, a $250M buyback authorization, and ~$195M of cash — all while intending to grow on a leverage-neutral basis. These cannot all be funded. Which of them Highwoods actually executes over the next two to four quarters is the most informative forward signal available on this name.

There is a real risk embedded in the development plan. Highwoods' own risk factors state that undeveloped land in its markets "is generally more readily available and less expensive than in higher barrier-to-entry markets" and that competitors — or Highwoods itself — could build product that competes with the existing portfolio. Sunbelt office is a demand-growth story and an easy-supply story simultaneously. The entitled, utility-served land bank (4.2 million sf of core office capacity) is an asset in an upcycle and an admission of a thin moat in a downcycle.

**A note on the estimate-revision data.** The revision table shows a single analyst trimming GAAP EPS estimates (−3.3% current year, −7.9% next year). For a REIT where depreciation of $295.0M exceeds net income by ~1.8x and disposition gains swing quarterly results by $0.70/share, GAAP EPS estimates carry little information. The direction nonetheless conflicts with reported FFO guidance having been raised, and analyst sentiment is degrading (buy ratings 2 → 1 against 9 holds). Neither signal is strong; together they argue against paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

The closest listed comparables are Piedmont Office Realty Trust and Cousins Properties in the Sunbelt/Southeast, with the coastal-gateway names as sector reference points rather than asset-level competitors. What the filings establish:

Pricing power at the asset level is real, evidenced by +15.4% (Q4 2025) and +20.9% (Q2 2026) GAAP re-leasing spreads and a 9.1% rise in rent per occupied square foot since 2022. Occupancy has stopped falling. Tenant granularity — only two customers above 3% — is a durability feature that most office REITs cannot claim. And third-party institutional capital is willing to underwrite these markets alongside Highwoods: the North Carolina Investment Authority committed $189.5M of the $210.5M Bloc 83 investment with HIW as the 10% common-equity partner and controlling operator, and Granite Properties partnered on The Terraces in Dallas.

That Bloc 83 structure warrants a reading caveat. It is a consolidated VIE, so consolidated revenue, NOI, total assets, in-service square footage (26.83M → 27.7M sf) and portfolio occupancy statistics all reflect an asset Highwoods owns one-tenth of. FFO per share is *not* distorted — HIW's definition subtracts income attributable to noncontrolling interests in consolidated affiliates, and the first-half deduction was $(1.8)M. The accounting is standard; top-line consolidated metrics simply must be read with the 90% third-party interest in mind. Obtaining control and operating economics of a trophy CBD Raleigh asset for 10% of the capital, with a retained option to increase to 50%, is a sensible use of scarce equity.

The offsetting structural point is the low-barrier admission quoted above, plus a tightening acquisition environment: the 10-K notes competition from investors with greater capital resources and reluctance among owners to sell. That Highwoods paid $441.5M for three assets in 2025 and needed a 90/10 structure to secure Bloc 83 is consistent with a firmer bid for quality Sunbelt office — good for existing portfolio value, adverse for external growth math.

The $110.2M Legacy Union parking acquisition (3,057 spaces, ~$36,000/space) serves the 1.2 million square feet of HIW-owned office at Legacy Union, including Bank of America Tower, connected by skybridge. Removing a third-party control point over the largest tenant relationship (620,790 sf, 8.6-year remaining term) ahead of eventual renewal or refinancing has strategic logic, though the purchase cap rate is undisclosed and the economics cannot be independently assessed.

## Risk Assessment

**23Springs and the March 2027 cluster.** The 50%-owned 23Springs joint venture in Dallas had $192.0M outstanding on a construction loan maturing March 2027 at June 30, 2026, on a building completed in Q1 2025 that has not stabilized; HIW's carrying value is $107.6M and reduced JV earnings are attributed partly to 23Springs and Granite Park Six. This coincides with the $289.1M unsecured maturity. Recourse to wholly owned assets is disclosed as absent, and maximum exposure is generally the carrying value plus advances — but an unstabilized asset refinancing into a 5.5%+ market may require incremental equity.

**Federal lease roll.** $21.6M of annualized rent across 31 leases and 24 agencies with a 3.6-year average remaining term, in a policy environment actively consolidating civilian federal footprint. The 10-K's hedge is unusually explicit about termination risk.

**Nashville and "Other."** 18.7% of revenue at 81.8% occupancy with NOI down 9.2%, plus 8.7% of revenue at 75.7% occupancy carrying live impairment risk.

**JV put obligations.** NCIA may put its Bloc 83 interest to HIW at fair market value any time after January 2031 (contributed $189.5M ≈ 5.5% of current equity market value); Granite has a put on its 20% Terraces interest after January 2029; unquantified IRR-promote participations exist on certain developments. All contingent and distant, none a present liability.

**Rate sensitivity.** With ~6.3x net debt/EBITDA, a 6.36% yield, and 42.1% leverage, the equity is a levered long-duration instrument. Only 10% of debt floats, so the transmission is through the discount rate and cap rates rather than interest expense.

**Smaller items.** Net credit losses on operating lease receivables rose to $1.857M in 1H26 from $0.139M in 1H25 — 0.43% of revenue and immaterial to earnings, but directionally contrary to the recovery read and unexplained. The straight-line rent receivable stands at $305.0M with $16.8M of new accrual in the half, the mechanism by which GAAP SPNOI (+1.4%) exceeded cash SPNOI (+0.3%). Ground leases cover 1.2M sf with $79.8M of future obligations. And management elevated the $73.5M pending non-core disposition into the Q2 forward-looking-statement list — mitigated by expired diligence and nonrefundable deposits, but an unusual signal that the disposition program matters to the 2026 plan.

**Verification gap worth stating plainly.** The $3.46–$3.70 FFO guidance range is sourced to secondary outlets (MarketBeat, Zacks). No Q2 earnings 8-K exists in the EDGAR record reviewed; the most recent 8-K is dated June 4, 2026. The 10-Q corroborates the direction (revenue, NOI, occupancy, operating cash flow all guided higher; 86–87% average occupancy) but states no FFO range. The 8.79x forward multiple therefore rests on an unconfirmed number. Also unverified: acquisition and disposition cap rates for the $551.7M of 2025–1H26 purchases against $507.0M of sales, which leaves the central question — whether the inorganic growth creates value — unresolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.1% against 28.1% one-year realized is fair, sits in the 8th percentile within the current chain, and has crushed from 37.5% pre-earnings as the price fell 6.5% — post-event normalization, not stress. The near-dated skew is call-heavy (−10.6%) with a ±5.9% implied move. The informative reading is the term structure: the September 18 tenor prices 44.5% IV with a +10.0% put skew and a ±12.0% implied move, spanning the September FOMC, while the six-month tenor sits at 38.1%. The market is complacent about the next two weeks and is paying for downside protection around the rate decision. That is consistent with the fundamental view that the dominant near-term variable for this equity is the long end, not the operating trajectory.

Short interest of 14.7% of float with 4.5 days to cover is elevated for a REIT of this size. It cuts both ways: a genuine bearish institutional stance, and enough crowding that positive surprises can be amplified.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($38, +20.8% price / ~+27% total).** Occupancy has bottomed and the 1.0 million square feet signed in Q2 at +20.9% GAAP spreads commences through 2027, converting flat cash SPNOI to +2–3% and lifting FFO toward $3.70–3.80. Leverage continues to fall toward 40% on the disposition program, the $250M buyback is executed with sale proceeds rather than reinvested at compressed cap rates, and the March 2027 refinancing clears at ~5.4% for a $0.04 drag. A wide implied cap rate of 7.6–8.2% against 5.43% marginal debt compresses as the market accepts Sunbelt Class A as a functioning asset class, taking the multiple to 10.25x. Five-year TSR of −9.3% against a −24.3% peer group establishes that this management team has been the relative winner in the sector.

**Bear case ($24.50, −22.1% price / ~−15.7% total).** The +20.9% GAAP spread proves to be substantially concession-funded, cash spreads land near flat, and cash SPNOI stays negative through 2027. Federal agencies give back space as their 3.6-year average term rolls, Nashville stays below 82%, and "Other" produces another impairment. Development starts of up to $400M into markets Highwoods itself describes as easy to build in add supply just as demand normalizes. The dividend plus real-estate capex continues to exceed operating cash flow, forcing further asset sales that shrink the earnings base — four consecutive years of Core FFO decline resumes after a one-year acquisition-funded pause. The multiple compresses to 7.5x on $3.25.

The honest synthesis: the operating evidence has improved enough to end the AVOID case, and the valuation has re-rated enough to prevent an accumulation case. What is missing is proof that the organic engine works — one quarter of positive cash SPNOI on the same-property pool, or a disclosed acquisition yield that demonstrates the external growth is accretive to the ~5.43% marginal debt cost and 6.36% dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.**

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following three:**
1. Same-property **cash** NOI positive by 1.5% or more for two consecutive quarters (Q3 and Q4 2026), confirming that signed leases are converting to cash rather than concessions.
2. Actual repurchases under the $250M authorization at prices below $30, funded from disposition proceeds — demonstrating that management will shrink the equity rather than buy assets at compressed cap rates, and putting a floor under the NAV discount.
3. Price toward **$27.50–28.50** with fundamentals intact, which would restore a 7.0%+ yield and roughly 8.0x forward FFO — the level at which the bear-case downside compresses to about 11% and the total-return math becomes compelling.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. FY2027 FFO guidance below $3.45, indicating the 2026 improvement was a one-year acquisition effect rather than an inflection.
2. Federal lease non-renewals or Nashville occupancy below 80% in either of the next two quarters — the two largest identified organic drags moving the wrong way.
3. Development starts exceeding $400M funded with revolver draws or ATM issuance while dispositions fall short of the $100–250M guided range, breaking the stated leverage-neutral discipline; equivalently, a rise in leverage back above 44% of undepreciated book.
4. Any indication that 23Springs requires incremental HIW equity at its March 2027 loan maturity beyond the $107.6M carrying value.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report (late October) for cash SPNOI, occupancy against the 86–87% guide, and buyback execution; then the FY2026 10-K (February 2027) for full-year real-estate capex — the single number that resolves the dividend-coverage question I could only assess on half-year data — and initial 2027 guidance. The March 2027 refinancing of the $289.1M unsecured notes and the 23Springs construction loan is the hard event on the calendar.

**On the trajectory.** Scores have improved 4.4 → 5.2 → 5.0 → 5.24, and the two AVOID calls at $21.50 and $26.27 underestimated both the leasing recovery and the sector's willingness to re-rate. The current Q2 data confirms the improving direction on occupancy, leasing spreads, leverage, and FFO, which is why Financial Health holds at 5.5 and Valuation recovers modestly from 4.0 as the stock trades 11% below its July high on better operating news. Risk Factors are marked down slightly to 4.8 because the March 2027 refinancing cluster and federal-lease roll are now dated, identifiable events rather than generic concerns.

## Conclusion

Highwoods is a competently run, granularly leased Sunbelt office portfolio at an inflection that is real but shallower than the headline numbers suggest. Occupancy has bottomed, re-leasing spreads are wide, leverage is falling, and 2026 should produce the first FFO-per-share increase since 2022. The offset is that essentially all of the Q2 revenue increase came from acquisitions, same-property cash NOI was negative for the first half, and after the capital required to hold occupancy, the dividend is currently part-funded by selling buildings. At 8.8x reported forward FFO, a 6.36% yield, and a ~7.6–8.2% implied cap rate — with consensus at spot and analyst sentiment drifting toward hold — the shares are priced approximately for what they are.

Expected 12-month total return of roughly 8% on probability-weighted scenarios, essentially all dividend, against a 22% price decline in the bear case, is a fair-return proposition without a margin of safety. Existing holders should collect the distribution and monitor cash SPNOI and buyback execution. New capital has no urgent entry signal here; the $27.50–28.50 range, or two quarters of positive same-property cash NOI, would change that.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Base $32.50 · Bull $38.00 · Bear $24.50.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FY2025 payout ratio of 57.5% cited in the brief was replaced by the report with a 55.9% figure on the unconfirmed guided midpoint (the FFO-payout screen the report critiques should be read against the verified 57.5% trailing figure); the implied cash cap-rate net of G&A was corrected from ~7.3% to ~7.6% (per the brief), modestly tightening the stated spread over marginal debt cost; and the off-low rally figure was corrected from 54% to ~73% ($20.45 → $35.44) — none of these individually overturns the HOLD, but the valuation looks marginally less cheap after correction.
