**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Duke Energy is one of the largest fully-regulated US electric utilities, serving ~8.6 million electric customers across the Carolinas, Florida, Ohio, Indiana, and Kentucky, plus a gas distribution business anchored by Piedmont Natural Gas. The investment case rests on a single, durable structural advantage: Duke's service territory sits in the path of the fastest-growing US load centers for population, reshored manufacturing, and AI data centers. Management guides to 5-7% EPS growth through 2030 on roughly 7%+ rate-base growth, funded by a "once-in-a-generation" capital cycle. Q1 2026 results were solid: revenue +11.3% YoY to $9,178M, adjusted EPS $1.93 (+9.7%), with a three-of-four beat record and a high-performing nuclear fleet that set a systemwide capacity-factor record.

The complication is the funding mechanics. Duke is FCF-negative by design: Q1 2026 capex of $4.1B against operating cash flow of $1.5B. To fund ~$90B+ of multi-year capex without over-diluting common equity or over-levering the balance sheet, management is executing a multi-pronged financing program: the $6B staged Brookfield minority investment in Florida Progress, the ~$2.5B Piedmont Tennessee sale to Spire, a $3.1B tax-credit monetization, and a $6B ATM forward equity program. The strategy is well-conceived and protects credit metrics, but it caps per-share upside relative to enterprise-level growth and introduces execution risk across multiple discrete transactions.

We rate DUK a HOLD with a growth lean. At ~$123.70 the stock trades at ~18.5x the $6.70 current-year EPS estimate and ~17.3x next year's $7.17, a discount to the electric-utility peer median of ~23.8x. The 3.4% yield with a ~65% payout provides downside support and the data-center load growth is confirmed across the regulated-utility complex. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: ~12% upside to the $138 consensus mid-point against a higher-for-longer rate path that directly pressures a financing-heavy negative-FCF model, plus emerging affordability politics over who pays for the data-center buildout. We need confirmation that the data-center load converts to recoverable rate base without ratepayer backlash, and that the financing plan executes on schedule, before moving to a BUY.

## Company Overview

Duke operates as a set of regulated monopolies organized into two segments:

- **Electric Utilities & Infrastructure (EU&I)** — ~85% of segment income. Operating subsidiaries are Duke Energy Carolinas, Duke Energy Progress (merging into Carolinas effective Jan 1, 2027), Duke Energy Florida, Duke Energy Ohio, and Duke Energy Indiana. Q1 2026 EU&I segment income was $1,254M on $7,878M revenue. The segment includes a large nuclear fleet that achieved an all-time systemwide capacity-factor reliability record in early 2026.
- **Gas Utilities & Infrastructure (GU&I)** — Piedmont Natural Gas plus Ohio/Kentucky gas LDCs and midstream/RNG investments.

The value-creation engine is regulated rate-base growth earning authorized ROEs of ~9.75%-10.95% on ~53% equity ratios. The moat is geographic: the Carolinas, Florida, and Indiana rank among the fastest-growing US regions for the demand drivers (AI data centers, advanced manufacturing, population in-migration) that translate into rate-base expansion. Constructive multi-year rate plans, decoupling, and rider mechanisms, reinforced by recent legislation (NC SB266, SC Energy Security Act, Indiana HB1002, Ohio SB103) expanding forward-looking test years and faster cost recovery, make this one of the more favorable regulatory backdrops in the sector.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** GAAP net income to common was $1,536M, or $1.97 EPS (vs $1.76 prior). The GAAP figure was flattered by the $171M after-tax Piedmont Tennessee gain (+$0.22), partly offset by a $150M legal/regulatory settlement (-$0.19); adjusted EPS of $1.93 is the cleaner number. FY net income has grown from $2,735M (2023) to $4,402M (2024) to $4,912M (2025), and adjusted EPS from $5.24 (2021) to $6.31 (2025). The earnings drivers are textbook regulated-utility: rate-case recovery of infrastructure investment, weather-normal volume growth, and improved weather, partly offset by higher O&M (storm costs), depreciation on a growing asset base, and rising interest expense.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets $198B; total debt ~$90B ($80.5B long-term + $7.4B current maturities + $2.4B commercial paper); common equity $56.5B. Goodwill is $19.0B, ~34% of equity. The Altman Z-Score reads ~0.8 ("distress"), which is a false signal: this leverage is structural to the regulated-utility model, backed by ~$5B/year of regulated net income and investment-grade access to ~$10B in committed credit. The meaningful watch-item is credit-metric maintenance (FFO/debt and the rating agencies' tolerance for the capex program), not solvency.

**Cash flow — the crux of the thesis.** Operating cash flow of $1.5B (down from $2.2B) against the quarter's capex. The OCF dip is partly a timing item (deferred fuel and Winter Storm Fern restoration costs that are regulatory assets pending recovery, $809M fuel/purchased-power recovery filings), but the structural point stands: Duke is deeply FCF-negative and will remain so through the build cycle. Interest expense rose to $968M for the quarter. The Fed's 2025-26 easing (75bps of cuts, upper bound 3.75%) is a tailwind for this rate-sensitive name, but the balance sheet remains highly sensitive to the long-end rate path. **Dividend.** $1.065/quarter ($4.26 annualized), ~3.4% yield, ~65% payout, well-covered by adjusted earnings and a core component of the total-return case.

## Growth Analysis

Duke's growth thesis is among the more credible in the regulated-utility space because it is anchored in confirmed demand. CEO Sideris frames US power demand as growing "10x the historic pace," driven by AI data centers and reshored manufacturing concentrated in Duke's footprint; the DOE projects data centers could reach ~12% of US electricity demand by 2028. The data-center load surge is now visible across the regulated-utility complex (commercial KWh growth running well above historical norms at multiple operators), which raises the durability of the demand signal.

The mechanism is straightforward: load growth → capacity and transmission buildout → rate-base expansion → earnings on authorized ROE. Duke has authorized a large generation pipeline (hydrogen-capable gas CC/CT plants — Anderson SC ~$3.2B, Cayuga IN ~$3.3B, Person County NC, Marshall/Buck CTs) with in-service dates 2028-2030, plus the Robinson nuclear license renewal to 2050. The EPS growth guidance is achievable if the load materializes and financing stays on plan.

The estimate-revision picture is stable rather than accelerating: current-quarter EPS revised +3.2% over 90 days, current-year flat at $6.70, next-year flat at $7.17. This is not the +30% revision signal that would mark a re-rating candidate; it is steady, mid-single-digit compounding. The growth is above the utility-sector average, which supports a 7.0 score, but the velocity is not changing.

The key caveat that prevents a higher growth score: the 10-Q forward-looking disclosures explicitly flag risk "particularly if usage of electricity by data centers is less than currently projected." The elevated capex and EPS-growth narrative rests on this demand converting to billed, recoverable load. Hyperscaler capex durability is being stress-tested elsewhere in the AI complex, and a slowdown in data-center buildout would directly hit the load forecast underpinning Duke's plan.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the electric-utility peer median P/E of ~23.8x, DUK's ~18.5x current-year multiple is a notable discount. Same-sector comparables:

|--------|-----|-----------|
| CEG | 21.1 | 0.7% |
| SO | 23.8 | 3.2% |
| AEP | 19.0 | 3.0% |
| ETR | 28.1 | 2.3% |
| VST | 24.7 | 0.7% |
| **DUK** | **~18.5** | **3.4%** |
| **Median** | **23.8** | **2.3%** |

DUK is the cheapest in the group on P/E and offers the highest yield. The discount is partly justified by Duke's high leverage (below-average FFO/debt cushion), the negative-FCF/dilution funding model, and authorized ROEs that sit at sector-typical levels rather than premium. AEP, the closest comparable on both growth profile and multiple (19.0x), confirms the market is applying a similar discount to high-load-growth utilities that fund capex through dilution.

A reasonable base case applies ~19x to next-year EPS of $7.17, yielding ~$136, broadly in line with the $138.6 consensus mean (median $138) and ~10% above the current price. The bull case (the discount closes toward the peer median as data-center rate base de-risks) gets to ~$146 (the high analyst target); the bear case (rate pressure and affordability backlash compress the multiple to ~16x) sits near $115.

There is real, growing rate base behind the multiple, but this is not an asymmetric bargain. The 5.5 score reflects a modest discount that is largely warranted by the leverage and dilution profile, with ~10-12% upside to base case against a comparable downside. The cheap headline P/E does not, on its own, signal mispricing; the discount is the market correctly pricing the financing-heavy negative-FCF structure in a rate-sensitive environment.

## Competitive Landscape

Duke's competitive position is structurally strong and improving at the margin. As a regulated monopoly there is no direct competitor for retail load; the relevant competition is for capital and regulatory goodwill, and on both Duke is well-positioned. Its territory geography is the differentiator: the Carolinas/Florida/Indiana load-growth concentration gives it a higher organic rate-base growth runway than most peers. The Carolinas utility combination (Duke Energy Progress into Duke Energy Carolinas, effective Jan 1, 2027, with 14-year guaranteed customer savings) simplifies the corporate structure and should lower cost-to-serve.

The trajectory is positive: load growth is accelerating, the regulatory framework is becoming more constructive (forward test years, faster recovery riders), and the nuclear fleet's reliability record supports rate-case credibility. The 7.5 score reflects a strong and improving position, capped below 8 by the affordability counter-current: consumer advocates in Florida and North Carolina are challenging whether data centers, rather than residential ratepayers, should bear infrastructure costs. If regulators side with advocates on cost allocation, Duke captures less of the data-center upside than the bull case assumes.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Financing execution.** The dominant financial risk. The layered program (Brookfield minority stake, asset sales, tax-credit monetization, ATM forwards) is designed explicitly to "displace previously planned issuances of long-term debt and common equity through 2029." Any stumble (a delayed Brookfield closing, a credit downgrade forcing more equity, ATM issuance at depressed prices) compresses per-share growth. The next Brookfield closing (+$200M by 12/31/26) is a near-term execution milestone.

2. **Data-center load shortfall.** The entire elevated-capex/EPS-growth narrative rests on data-center demand materializing. A broader AI-capex deceleration would hit the load forecast directly.

3. **Coal ash (CCR) liabilities.** EPA's 2024 CCR Rule (expanding scope to legacy impoundments) is in litigation/abeyance with a revised rule expected Q4 2026. Duke states impacts "could be material." The Mooresville coal ash class action survived a motion to dismiss in Feb 2026.

4. **Affordability/cost-allocation politics.** Live political risk to cost recovery during a period of rising bills, the inverse of the data-center upside.

5. **PJM transmission cost reallocation.** FERC's March 2026 DFAX de-minimis ruling could require retroactive surcharges back to 2015 for Duke Energy Ohio; impact "unable to reasonably estimate."

6. **Pending ROE appeals.** NC Supreme Court decision (expected Q2 2026) on DEC/DEP rate-case ROEs.

7. **Convertible dilution overhang.** The new $1.5B 3.0% converts (due 2029, ~$161 effective conversion price) plus the ATM forwards create a modest future share-count overhang if the stock rallies materially.

The 5.5 risk score reflects a cluster of identifiable, not-fully-priced tails (CCR rule, cost-allocation rulings, financing execution) layered on a rate-sensitive balance sheet — above a distressed read, but not the clean profile that would warrant a 7+.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term (1-week) ATM IV reading of 154% against ~15% historical vol, in steep backwardation, is a corrupted thin-options artifact for a low-vol utility and should be disregarded; the one-week implied move of ±3.8% is not credible for DUK and reflects a near-dated expiry pricing distortion. The informative tenors are the 1-month (19.5% IV), 3-month (19.9%), and 6-month (21.0%), all modestly elevated versus the 14.9% one-year realized vol and showing slight put skew at longer dates (-2.1% at 6 months). The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.54 to 0.85 over six weeks. Net read: the longer-dated options market is pricing mildly elevated downside hedging consistent with the rate-sensitivity and regulatory-ruling calendar (NC Supreme Court, CCR rule), not acute stress. Short interest is benign at 2.2% of float. This confirms market positioning for moderate two-sided risk into the pending regulatory decisions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$146).** Data-center load materializes as forecast across the footprint; rate base compounds at 7%+ and EPS at the high end of the range; the financing program executes cleanly, protecting credit metrics and limiting common dilution; the rate path moderates; and the P/E discount to the peer median narrows as the data-center rate base de-risks. The stock re-rates toward 20x next-year EPS, and the dividend delivers a mid-teens total return.

**Bear case (~$115).** Data-center buildout slows, undercutting the load forecast; affordability politics push regulators to shift more cost to large-load customers or disallow recovery, capping the captured upside; the rate path stays elevated, pressuring the negative-FCF balance sheet and forcing more dilutive equity issuance; and the multiple compresses toward 16x. Total return turns negative on price, partly cushioned by the dividend.

The recommendation is a HOLD because the risk/reward is genuinely balanced: ~10-12% to base case and ~18% to the bull against ~7% downside to the bear, with the principal upside driver (data-center load conversion to recoverable rate base) gated by the principal risk (affordability politics and financing execution). This is a quality defensive total-return name with an above-peer growth kicker, but the asymmetry required for a BUY — a specific, falsifiable mispricing — is not present. The market is correctly discounting the financing-heavy, dilution-capped structure; the cheap headline P/E is warranted. The score sits at 6.1 rather than crossing the BUY threshold because the valuation discount is justified by leverage/dilution rather than mispriced, and the growth velocity is steady (no +30% revision) rather than accelerating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD with a growth lean. The next natural reassessment points are the Q2 2026 earnings release, the NC Supreme Court ROE decision (expected Q2 2026), the next Brookfield staged closing (by 12/31/26), and the revised EPA CCR rule (expected Q4 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Data-center large-load tariffs or take-or-pay agreements approved that explicitly assign infrastructure cost to large-load customers, de-risking the rate-base plan and removing the affordability overhang — combined with the stock trading below ~$118.
- The financing program demonstrably ahead of plan (Brookfield closings on schedule, credit ratings affirmed, ATM issuance minimized) such that 5-7% EPS growth looks achievable with materially less common dilution than feared.
- A favorable NC Supreme Court ROE ruling plus positive estimate revisions of >5% on the out-year, signaling the load forecast is converting to higher authorized earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit-rating downgrade or a forced shift to materially larger common-equity issuance, signaling the financing engineering has failed and dilution will cap per-share growth.
- Evidence the data-center load forecast is being cut (management lowering rate-base or EPS-growth guidance below 5%).
- An adverse CCR rule (Q4 2026) or PJM DFAX ruling quantified at a materially negative level, or a cost-allocation ruling that disallows recovery of data-center infrastructure from large-load customers.

## Conclusion

Duke Energy is a constructively-regulated large-cap utility sitting in attractive load-growth territory, guiding to mid-single-digit EPS growth through 2030 with a well-covered yield. Management is funding the capex cycle through a multi-pronged financing program that protects credit and limits dilution. The score has improved modestly across prior reviews (6.4 → 6.6 → 6.1 this read, with the slight step down reflecting a sharper accounting of the negative-FCF/dilution structure against a rate-sensitive backdrop), and the underlying competitive position remains strong.

The recommendation stays HOLD. The growth and quality are real, but the valuation discount to peers is largely warranted by the leverage and dilution profile, the upside to consensus is moderate (~10-12%), and the principal growth driver is gated by affordability politics and financing-execution risk that the market is only beginning to price. We would become buyers below ~$118 with confirmation that data-center load is converting to recoverable rate base, or upgrade outright on evidence the financing plan is running ahead of schedule. For now, own it for the yield and the steady mid-single-digit compounding, not for asymmetric appreciation.
