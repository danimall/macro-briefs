## Executive Summary

Centrus is two businesses stacked on one balance sheet: a shrinking, imported-supply enrichment trading operation that generates today's revenue, and a first-of-a-kind domestic enrichment build-out that generates nothing until 2029. The Q2 2026 10-Q, filed August 6, made the gap between them wider than the headline suggests. Reported revenue rose 14% to $176.1 million, but separative work unit (SWU) revenue — the recurring, contracted core — fell 20% to $100.0 million on a 23% volume decline, and the entire offset was $53.4 million of lower-margin uranium sales against zero a year earlier. LEU segment gross margin compressed from 40.3% to 33.6%. Average SWU unit cost rose 13% in the quarter against realized price growth of 3%. The Technical Solutions segment turned to a $1.7 million gross loss.

The income statement is being carried by the treasury. H1 2026 pretax income was $34.1 million; H1 2026 investment income was $33.3 million. Operating income of $11.2 million on $252.8 million of revenue (4.4% margin) was down 79% year over year, and even management's own adjusted operating income declined, from $63.1 million to $56.9 million. Free cash flow was negative $111.5 million in the half (operating cash flow −$16.7 million, capex $94.8 million), with capex explicitly guided higher by "approximately several hundred million." Meanwhile DOE's FY2027 budget proposal contains no funding for HALEU cascade operation, DOE has said it does not intend to exercise further options, and the June 30 Modification 27 replaced a two-year production option with a three-month, $15.0 million maintenance-and-storage period with zero production. That single disclosure impairs $0.8 billion of the $4.5 billion headline backlog.

None of this makes the long-term thesis wrong. Non-Russian enrichment supply genuinely does not cover non-Russian reactor demand; Russia holds 43% of world capacity; TradeTech's SWU spot indicator sits at an all-time-high $200 versus a $34 trough in 2018; Congress appropriated ~$3.4 billion to restart domestic fuel production. Centrus won a $900 million firm-fixed-price DOE task order on July 1, 2026, and on completion its subsidiary takes title to the deployed capacity. The problem is price and sequencing. At $190.27 the stock trades at roughly 51x next-year consensus EPS of $3.75 on estimates that have been cut 9.6% in 90 days, 4.6x tangible book, and ~6.6x EV to guided FY2026 revenue, while consensus for the current quarter has collapsed 78.9% to $0.11. The DOE award pays roughly 40% in 2028 and 30% in 2031, leaving only ~30% across the heaviest construction years, and management has told investors in unusually direct language that it "may opportunistically raise capital through large follow-on offerings of our common stock, warrants, or a combination thereof, which could result in substantial dilution." Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Centrus operates two segments. The **LEU segment** (87% of Q2 2026 revenue) buys the enrichment component of nuclear fuel from third parties and resells it to utilities, along with natural uranium hexafluoride and concentrates. Centrus does not currently produce the enrichment it sells commercially. Its two principal supply contracts are the 2011 TENEX contract with Russia's state-owned Rosatom subsidiary (running to 2028) and the 2018 Orano agreement with France (to 2030). The average individual SWU order fulfilled in H1 2026 was ~$11.7 million, which is why quarterly results are lumpy.

The **Technical Solutions segment** (13% of Q2 revenue) operates the 16-centrifuge HALEU demonstration cascade at Piketon, Ohio under DOE contract, plus a centrifuge manufacturing and technology center at Oak Ridge. Cumulative HALEU output exceeded 1,900 kg, with the final 900 kg of the demonstration contract completed mid-June 2026 — two weeks early.

The strategic pivot is to become a vertically integrated domestic enricher. Centrus holds an NRC construct-and-operate license at Piketon running to April 2037, amended in 2021 to permit HALEU. Announced commitments include >$560 million at Oak Ridge for high-rate centrifuge manufacturing and a Piketon expansion targeting an initial 12 metric tons/year of HALEU plus LEU cascades, with first new capacity online in 2029.

One structural nuance deserves preservation: Centrus's rights to American Centrifuge technology are conditional. It holds a non-exclusive license in DOE centrifuge inventions with 1–2% SWU royalties, and DOE holds a commercial-purpose license exercisable if Centrus misses milestones under the 2002 DOE-USEC Agreement or abandons commercial deployment. DOE is simultaneously the largest single customer, the Piketon landlord, the technology licensor, and the holder of a contingent claw-back right. Rights and remedies on missed milestones are preserved and tolled by agreement.

The GICS tag "Energy / Coal & Consumable Fuels" is an artifact. The supplied peer set (Helmerich & Payne, Patterson-UTI, Crescent Energy, Kinetik, Solaris) shares no revenue driver with a nuclear fuel enricher; peer median P/E of 62.4x carries no information for this security, and I have not used it as a valuation anchor.

**The revenue growth is a mix artifact.** Consolidated gross margin fell from 34.9% to 28.3% in Q2. LEU segment cost of sales rose 36% while segment revenue rose 22%. Volume, not price, drove the SWU decline: −23% in Q2 and −31% in H1, against realized price gains of +3% and +16%. Because Centrus's book was signed in prior years, realized prices lag spot by several years — so the $200/SWU spot indicator is a multi-year tailwind, not a current one, and it is flat over the last six months. On the cost side, average unit SWU cost rose faster than realized price in both periods.

**Earnings quality.** H1 pretax income of $34.1 million against H1 investment income of $33.3 million means essentially the whole reported pretax profit was economically equivalent to interest on the ~$1.87 billion cash balance, itself the product of $1.2 billion of convertible issuance and ~$578 million of cumulative ATM sales. The countervailing reading is legitimate: operating income is depressed by $27.6 million of H1 non-capitalizable "Growth Costs" and $18.1 million of stock compensation including a one-time $17.2 million reclassification charge, which management strips out to reach adjusted H1 operating income of $56.9 million and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.81. Two cautions on that bridge. First, adjusted results fell year over year as well (operating income −10%, EPS −23%), so the non-GAAP presentation narrows the deterioration rather than removing it. Second, the Growth Costs adjustment is a timing item: management states these costs "stop being expensed as costs related to our expansion projects become capitalizable," meaning they migrate to the balance sheet and reappear as depreciation.

**Cash flow inflected hard.** Operating cash flow swung from +$89.3 million to −$16.7 million; capex went from $5.7 million to $94.8 million; free cash flow was −$111.5 million versus +$83.6 million. Inventories consumed $92.5 million; deferred revenue and customer advances declined $38.4 million net. An additional $21.0 million of PP&E sat in payables at quarter-end, so the true H1 capital commitment exceeded the cash figure. Net PP&E jumped from $29.5 million to $142.5 million against accumulated depreciation of only $7.5 million — this company has barely begun to own fixed assets. Annualizing a single half is inappropriate for an order-timing-driven business, but the direction is unambiguous and capex is guided materially higher.

Backing out the 2.25% conversion price from the incremental-share arithmetic implies ~$97.50, which is a derived rather than verified input (the 10-Q cross-references a 10-K note not retrieved). On that basis, full conversion at $190.27 would produce roughly 2.0 million share-settled shares — about 10% of current shares outstanding, or ~9% post-issuance ownership dilution. The 0% notes' conversion price is not established in the documents reviewed and the disclosed "excluded" share figures do not reconcile to a simple out-of-the-money reading; treat that as an open verification gap, not settled.

Separately, the November 2025 shelf had ~$554.5 million remaining at June 30 and the ATM authorization runs to $1.0 billion. The Q2 10-Q added a new risk factor on the need for additional capital. To management's credit, the CFO publicly declined to use the ATM in Q1 2026 because pricing "just didn't feel it provided the right shareholder value," and the 2025 ATM sales of 2.87 million shares for $523.7 million net were executed at prices far above today's — price-sensitive, opportunistic issuance rather than forced funding.

## Growth Analysis

The industry setup is the strongest in four decades. Roughly 80 reactors are under construction worldwide, 94 U.S. reactors operate, 15 Japanese units have restarted with 10 more in approval, and the IEA projects nuclear generation +38% by 2035 under Stated Policies. Nine of ten initially selected ARDP designs require HALEU. Management's framing is the crux: with Russian supply included the enrichment market is oversupplied; without it, undersupplied — and replacing 43% of world capacity "will take a number of years and significant funding."

Centrus's participation in that setup is where the analysis turns cautious.

**Near term the numbers point down.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $450–500 million was reaffirmed, implying H2 revenue of $197–247 million against H1's $252.8 million — flat to down sequentially at the midpoint, and only ~5.9% full-year growth on FY2025's $448.7 million. Consensus has moved against that: current-quarter EPS cut 78.9% to $0.11, next quarter −28.2% to $1.46, current year −17.1%/−18.4% to $3.44, next year −5.6%/−9.6% to $3.75. Falling estimates of this magnitude are a genuine warning signal, and the pattern here is the mirror image of an inflection: revenue guidance held flat while earnings estimates collapsed, meaning the cut is about margin and mix, not volume timing.

**Backlog is three assets, not one.** Headline backlog is $4.5 billion (up from $3.8 billion) extending to 2040. ASC 606 remaining performance obligations are approximately $0.8 billion, extending only to 2030. The reconciliation: LEU backlog $3.7B versus ~$0.7B RPO, with the ~$3.0B gap disclosed as contingent LEU and HALEU commitments ($2.4B under definitive agreements) tied to "achievement of operational milestones" for capacity that does not yet exist; Technical Solutions backlog $0.8B versus $40.4M RPO, the gap being unexercised options on the contract DOE has defunded. Backlog grew $0.7 billion in six months, entirely in the contingent bucket. That $2.4 billion of definitive contingent agreements is real evidence of demand and is exactly what a developer needs to underwrite a plant — but it is not near-term revenue, and it converts only if construction succeeds.

**The 2029 start date is the binding constraint.** First new capacity comes online in 2029; the DOE task order requires 1 MTU of HALEU UF6 at 19.75% by July 2032 (the 10-Q date; secondary reporting says March 2032 — the filing governs). Between now and 2029, revenue growth depends on the same imported-SWU trading business that just shrank 31% by volume, plus whatever commercial arrangement replaces the defunded cascade contract. Centrus announced on July 1 that it is working with DOE to operate the cascade privately on a commercial basis, with a long-term ACP site lease extension as the gating item. If that lands, Centrus becomes the only privately produced U.S. HALEU source — the single most important near-term swing factor in the growth case.

## Competitive Landscape

Centrus's four named enrichment competitors — Orano (France), Rosatom/TENEX (Russia), Urenco (Netherlands/UK/German utilities), and CNEIC (China) — are all wholly or substantially government-owned. Centrus's differentiation is ownership nationality and technology provenance: it is the only U.S.-owned company with a licensed, operating enrichment facility using U.S.-origin centrifuge technology, and per POWER Magazine the first U.S.-owned, U.S.-technology enrichment plant to begin production in roughly 70 years. That is a policy-derived moat.

The trajectory of that moat is the concern. In January 2026 DOE allocated three $900 million task orders: HALEU to Centrus's ACO subsidiary, HALEU to **General Matter**, and LEU to **Orano Federal Services** for "Project IKE" in Oak Ridge — on the same footprint as Centrus's own manufacturing base. The 10-K confirms from the primary side that ACO was notified another bidder won the LEU production task order. Urenco USA operates the only currently producing commercial U.S. enrichment plant (Eunice, NM) and is expanding it. The 10-Q notes additional DOE funding "has not been requested or announced," and the IDIQ structure carries a $2.0 million contract minimum per awardee — future federal support is a competition, not an entitlement.

Management itself flags two further pressures: rising Chinese enriched-uranium imports into the U.S. that "will likely have a negative impact on our business," and state-backed competitors that "may elect to increase their production or exports... even when not justified by market conditions, thereby depressing prices." Trade policy is two-sided: uranium import tariffs were removed September 8, 2025, but Proclamation 11001 (January 14, 2026) contemplates an additional sectoral tariff under the Section 232 critical-minerals investigation. Because Centrus imports nearly all the SWU it sells, a sectoral tariff raises its input costs while possibly raising domestic prices — the 10-K states the net effect is ambiguous.

Customer concentration moved the wrong way. FY2025: top ten ~77% of revenue, top two ~31%. Q2 2026: two LEU customers at $78.9 million and $39.1 million plus DOE at $22.0 million equal $140.0 million, or 79.5% of quarterly revenue from three counterparties, versus a materially more dispersed $35.7M/$32.8M/$19.9M in Q2 2025. The geographic mix also shifted sharply — foreign SWU-and-uranium revenue rose from $68.5M to $118.0M while U.S. revenue fell from $57.2M to $35.4M. That is a change in mix, not diversification, and it adds Euratom's ≤20% foreign-enrichment policy and euro-denominated payment exposure.

**Competitive position score: 5.0** — a genuine and defensible policy moat, with a deteriorating near-term trajectory on award allocation, entrant count, and concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Russian supply dependency with a 2027 cliff.** The 10-K states that through 2027, "well over one-half" of LEU deliveries come from TENEX material and that other sources "are not sufficient to replace the TENEX supply." Three independent legal regimes must each permit every shipment: U.S. DOE waivers under the Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act (a third waiver application filed December 11, 2024 covering 2026-2027 imports for *future* sales is still pending — more than 20 months); Russian specific export licenses under Decree No. 1544 as extended to 12/31/2027; and Canadian ocean-carrier permits extended only to March 2027, using Chinese-built vessels whose current USTR port-fee exemption the 10-K says would otherwise be "cost prohibitive." Payment friction from the January 2025 Treasury determination has already caused agreed delays. Compounding it, the TENEX contract obliges Centrus to pay for all SWU in its annual minimum purchase obligation "even if it fails to submit orders." Centrus can be contractually obliged to pay for material it may be barred from importing. A newly surfaced friction: one facility notified Centrus it will no longer receive natural uranium for TENEX, constraining Russian Suspension Agreement compliance.

**2. DOE defunding.** FY2027 budget proposal excludes cascade operation funding; DOE has stated it does not intend to exercise further options; Modification 27 cut the near-term option to three months and $15.0 million with no production. DOE previously acknowledged the Option 1b cost estimate was "insufficient to support full performance," and the extended Phase 2 fee remains not definitized. A budget proposal is not an enacted appropriation, and privatization negotiations are live — but the segment already ran a gross loss.

**3. Execution and overrun risk.** Fluor's EPC contract is time-and-materials, cost-reimbursable-plus-margin: Centrus bears overrun risk. Mitigants are real — funds authorized incrementally against stage gates, and the termination-for-convenience fee started at $24.0 million on February 9, 2026 and declines $2.0 million monthly to zero (~$12 million now, zero by roughly February 2027). The contract signings are a genuine strategic option, not an irreversible commitment. Geiger Brothers construction is capped in aggregate at $900 million through January 2031. The company also needs at least 150 additional employees in 2026 against security-clearance lead times.

**4. Legal and legacy.** Five Price-Anderson actions arising from the Portsmouth GDP site (McGlone 2019, Lykins 2022, Rose 2023, Shaw 2023, Dunham filed 11/24/2025); Centrus asserts Price-Anderson indemnification and has notified DOE. DOE has asserted ~$9.6 million for Joppa Power Plant D&D costs with no accrual recorded.

**5. Governance.** The board-RSU reclassification is a real finding: because of a history of withholding shares "to fund the grantees' income tax liabilities beyond the Company's legal requirement," awards granted from 2014 to 2021 lost equity classification and moved to liabilities, adding $17.2 million to Q2 SG&A and creating an $18.0 million Level 1 liability marked quarterly to the share price. Mechanically, SG&A now moves with the stock. Disclosure controls were concluded effective and there was no restatement or material-weakness disclosure. Compensation performance measures are Revenue, Gross margin and Cash balance — no return-on-capital, project-milestone, schedule, or per-share metric for a company deploying billions; a new Executive Incentive Supplemental Plan effective June 15, 2026 was filed but not reviewed. The Section 382 rights agreement was extended to June 30, 2029 with the exercise price raised 7.1x, and charter provisions permit redemption of foreign-held shares above 5% thresholds, in some cases at the lesser of fair market value and purchase price.

**Two unexamined filings** should be noted for completeness: an 8-K filed 12/1/2025 tagged Item 3.01 (which covers listing transfers as well as deficiencies; nothing elsewhere suggests a listing problem) and an 8-K filed 3/16/2026 tagged Item 5.03. Neither was retrieved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $310 (+63%)

Non-Russian enrichment demand exceeds non-Russian supply, and 43% of world capacity cannot be replaced quickly. Spot SWU at $200 rolls into Centrus's realized book over the next several years as legacy contracts expire — H1 realized prices already rose 16%. The TENEX waiver is granted, letting Centrus place 2026-2027 material into a tight market. DOE and Centrus reach a commercial arrangement with an ACP lease extension, making Centrus the only privately produced U.S. HALEU source at better margins than the cost-reimbursable demonstration contract. The $2.4 billion of definitive contingent agreements begins converting as milestones are hit, with the Oklo letter of intent (up to five Aurora reactors, deliveries from 2029) formalized. The $900 million DOE award funds capacity to which ACO takes title on completion, and management's stated ~$300 million of identified partnership savings plus non-dilutive alternatives (a potential NNSA sole-source award, third-party or foreign direct investment) bridge the 2026-2028 funding gap without a large equity raise. On 2030-2032 earnings power from owned capacity, $310 is achievable, and 26.6% short interest amplifies any positive catalyst.

### Bear Case — $120 (−37%)

The TENEX waiver is not granted or Russian/Canadian permits lapse in 2027, and Centrus is left paying minimum purchase obligations for material it cannot import or place — the 10-K's own framing that it "may be unable to sell all of the LEU we are required to purchase... for prices that cover our costs." SWU volume declines continue; unit costs keep outrunning realized prices; the uranium trade that filled the Q2 gap is not repeatable. Cascade privatization stalls on the DOE lease, and Technical Solutions runs continued gross losses with $40.4 million of enforceable obligations. Capex accelerates as guided, free cash flow runs to −$200 million or worse, and the funding gap to the 2028 milestone payment is closed with a large equity offering at depressed prices — precisely as MD&A warns. On Fluor's cost-reimbursable structure, an overrun lands on Centrus. In that world the equity is worth cash plus a small option premium: roughly 2.9x tangible book and ~1.3x cash per share, or about $120, versus a 52-week low of $142.13.

### Base Case — $185 (−2.8%)

Guidance is met at roughly $475 million of FY2026 revenue with adjusted operating income near $110-115 million and negative free cash flow. The waiver eventually arrives but too late to place incremental 2026 material. Cascade privatization is agreed in some form but on modest terms. Construction proceeds on schedule with moderate cost creep and one equity raise of $300-500 million in 2027, diluting existing holders ~8-12%. The market continues valuing the equity as a 2029 option: roughly 4.4x tangible book and ~30x annualized adjusted operating income, or about $185.

Probability-weighted (25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear): ~$197, or roughly +3.4% from $190.27. For a security with 92.6% realized volatility and a binary regulatory dependency, that is not adequate compensation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $185 (−2.8%), bull $310 (+63%), bear $120 (−37%). Probability-weighted return ~+3% over 12 months against realized volatility near 90%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than dumping into weakness. The stock has traded between $142 and $232 over the last six months; scale out on rallies above $210-220, targeting a retained position no larger than one-third to one-half of a normal-sized speculative allocation, sized so that a move to the $120 bear case is tolerable. Given near-term IV at the 4th percentile and below realized vol, holders who want to keep the 2029 optionality should price September and December puts as a cheaper alternative to selling the whole position. Do not short: 26.6% short interest at 5.3 days to cover with call-tilted flow makes the mechanics unfavorable, and a TENEX waiver grant or a cascade-privatization announcement are both plausible squeeze catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. DOE grants the third TENEX import waiver covering 2026-2027 future sales, *and* Centrus discloses incremental SWU contracting against it. This removes the highest-severity risk and would restore volume visibility.
2. A definitive agreement to operate the Piketon cascade commercially, including the ACP long-term lease extension, with disclosed pricing that returns Technical Solutions to positive gross margin.
3. Two consecutive quarters of SWU *volume* growth with unit cost growth below realized price growth — the specific reversal of the H1 pattern.
4. Funding the 2026-2028 gap without common equity: an NNSA sole-source award, a strategic third-party investment at or above market, or conversion of contingent commitments into prepaid customer advances.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. TENEX permitting fails — waiver denial, non-renewal of the Canadian carrier permit beyond March 2027, or loss of Russian specific export licenses — while minimum purchase obligations remain in force.
2. An equity offering of $400 million or more priced at a material discount, or shelf/ATM utilization that takes cumulative dilution beyond ~15% without corresponding contracted revenue.
3. A disclosed cost overrun or schedule slip on the Piketon/Oak Ridge program that pushes first capacity beyond 2029, or a stage-gate stop under the Fluor arrangement.
4. FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $450 million, or Technical Solutions gross losses widening with no privatization agreement.

## Conclusion

Centrus owns something genuinely scarce: a licensed, operating, U.S.-owned enrichment facility using U.S.-origin technology, in a market that structurally cannot replace 43% of world capacity quickly. That scarcity is worth paying for at the right price. It is not worth 51x next-year consensus on estimates cut 18%, 4.6x tangible book, and 6.6x EV/revenue while the recurring business shrinks 31% by volume, unit costs outrun realized prices, DOE defunds the segment backlog, three counterparties supply 79.5% of revenue, the Russian supply chain requires three separate legal permissions that all expire in or before 2028, and management has pre-announced the possibility of "large follow-on offerings" causing "substantial dilution."

The balance sheet — $1.87 billion of cash, $661 million net cash, no near-term maturities — means this is not a solvency story and there is no reason to short it, particularly against 26.6% short interest. It is a sequencing and price story. The equity is an option on 2029-2032 capacity, and between now and the 2028 DOE milestone payment the most likely news flow is negative operating leverage, continued negative free cash flow, and a capital raise. The stock is already 59% below its high, and the fundamentals moved in the same direction as the price, which argues that the derating was earned rather than mistaken.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength above $210-220. The rating changes to HOLD on a granted TENEX waiver with disclosed incremental contracting, or a definitive commercial cascade agreement with the ACP lease extension. It changes to AVOID on TENEX permitting failure or a large discounted equity offering. Overall score 4.55/10.
