## Executive Summary

Nokia is two businesses moving in opposite directions inside one guidance range. Network Infrastructure — optical transport and IP routing, now including Infinera — grew 12% in Q2 2026 with gross margin up 240 basis points to 42.7%, and management doubled its FY2026 growth guidance for the segment from 6–8% to 12–14% (IP plus Optical from 10–12% to 18–20%). Net sales to AI & Cloud customers rose 105% to €446m, and AI & Cloud order intake in the quarter alone was €2.8bn, of which Nokia expects roughly half to convert to revenue within twelve months. Mobile Infrastructure, which now houses radio, core software and the patent licensing annuity and supplies roughly two-thirds of segment operating profit, produced exactly flat operating profit of €310m on 6% revenue growth, with gross margin down 70bps.

The consequence shows up in guidance. Nokia raised comparable operating profit guidance from €2.0–2.5bn to €2.1–2.6bn while stating explicitly that "operationally, Nokia's outlook is unchanged," attributing the €0.1bn to reclassifying two businesses to discontinued operations. It did this while also benefiting from a FX assumption that moved from EUR:USD 1.18 to 1.14 — a translation tailwind. Incremental AI/cloud revenue is arriving at accretive gross margins and being consumed below the gross line by R&D and go-to-market spend, plus zero operating leverage in Mobile Infrastructure. Meanwhile FY2026 restructuring charges were raised from €250m guided in January to €800m in July, Q2 IFRS operating profit was a €50m loss against €434m comparable, and net cash and interest-bearing financial investments have fallen from €4,854m (Dec 2024) to €2,776m (Jun 2026).

At $9.36 the stock is 46% below its 3 June high of $17.45 and 56% above Nvidia's late-October entry at $6.01, having returned roughly 123% over twelve months. The derating is multiple compression from a euphoric peak rather than fundamental deterioration — the company beat comparable consensus in all four of the last four quarters. But at ~24x FY2026E comparable EPS, ~18x EV/comparable operating profit and ~20x Nokia's own FY2028 target EPS, the valuation still requires Nokia to beat a plan whose midpoint implies only 3–5% annual comparable-profit growth from FY2024. Probability-weighted expected return over twelve months is roughly flat against 60%+ implied volatility and a Q4 that must deliver ~51% of full-year profit. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Nokia is a Finnish foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F annually and Form 6-K for interim disclosure. This has two practical effects on the evidence base. There is no 10-K XBRL, so standard distress and quality scores (Altman Z, Piotroski F) cannot be computed for this issuer from SEC data. And Section 16 does not apply, so no Form 4 record exists — the absence of insider-transaction data is structural and carries no directional information. Third-party claims that "Nokia insiders are buying" cannot be verified through SEC channels and, critically, cannot be separated from share-award vesting. Governance should be treated as unassessed rather than clean: there is no DEF 14A, so compensation structure, pay-for-performance alignment, board composition and related-party transactions are unavailable.

From the start of 2026 Nokia collapsed four segments into two plus a residual. Q2 2026:

| Segment | Net sales | YoY | Gross margin | Op. profit | Op. margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Network Infrastructure | €2,037m | +12% | 42.7% (+240bp) | €166m | 8.1% (+170bp) |
| Mobile Infrastructure | €2,680m | +6% | 49.3% (−70bp) | €310m | 11.6% (−60bp) |
| Portfolio Businesses | €94m | +6% | 28.7% | €0m | 0.0% |
| **Group** | **€4,815m** | **+8%** | **46.0% comparable** | **€434m comparable** | **9.0%** |

Mobile Infrastructure's 49.3% gross margin is materially supported by patent licensing, which Nokia has disclosed at approximately 100% gross margin with a €1.4bn contracted annual run-rate. Backing out roughly €375m of quarterly licensing (a derived estimate: €1.4bn ÷ 4, cross-checked against €384m of reported Q4'25 Nokia Technologies revenue) leaves radio plus core software at approximately 41% gross margin. Nokia does not disclose this split, so treat the 41% as an estimate. The point stands regardless of precision: the segment with two-thirds of group profit has equipment economics roughly 800bps below its reported blended margin.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is recovering; IFRS profit is not.** Revenue fell from €23,761m (FY2022) to €19,220m (FY2024) before rising 3.5% to €19,889m in FY2025 and 5.9% on a TTM basis to €20,265m. Reported IFRS operating profit went the other way: €1,970m (FY2024) → €885m (FY2025), a 55% decline, and €791m TTM June 2026.

**The comparable-to-reported gap is the central earnings-quality issue.**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1'26 | Q2'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable operating profit | €2,584m | €2,024m | €735m | €434m |
| Reported operating profit | €1,970m | €885m | €33m | **−€50m** |
| Gap | €614m | **€1,139m** | €702m | €484m |
| Comparable diluted EPS | €0.39 | €0.29 | €0.13 | €0.07 |
| Reported diluted EPS | €0.23 | €0.12 | €0.02 | €0.00 |

FY2026 restructuring guidance of €800m — versus €250m guided in January — plus Infinera and China purchase-accounting amortisation makes a FY2026 gap above €1.3bn plausible. Three overlapping programmes drive it: €250m from the 2023–2026 programme (tracking to the high end of €800m–1,200m gross savings), €350m of Nokia Shanghai-Bell integration charges pulled from a three-year into a two-year plan for €200m of savings, and €200m of newly initiated European programmes. These are real cash actions with identified savings attached, and compressing an integration timeline is a defensible operating choice. I do not infer presentational motive. The investor consequence is unambiguous: Nokia has taken large "non-recurring" charges in every year from 2023 through 2026, and the 24x multiple on comparable earnings corresponds to roughly 72x trailing IFRS earnings.

**FY2025's profit collapse was mis-read by many observers.** Comparable operating profit fell €560m while revenue rose 3.5%. The mechanism was not equipment deterioration. Nokia Technologies revenue fell €427m (€1,928m → €1,501m) at 65–100% operating margins, plausibly removing €300–400m of operating profit and accounting for 55–70% of the group decline (derived). FY2024 licensing was roughly €500m above the €1.4bn contracted run-rate, consistent with catch-up settlement income in the base. Nokia separately cut FY2025 guidance by up to €310m on currency and tariffs in July 2025. Comparable R&D intensity rose from 22.4% to 23.2% of sales, roughly 80bps of margin. Q4'25 comparable gross margin actually expanded 90bps to 48.1%. So the base year was distorted by licensing normalisation, FX and deliberate reinvestment — a more favourable read than the headline.

**Cash flow is the weak link.** Operating cash flow: €2,493m (FY2024) → €2,071m (FY2025) → €1,133m TTM June 2026. Nokia states FY2025 free cash flow of €1.5bn, a 72% conversion of comparable operating profit, at the top of its 55–75% range. The TTM series implies H1'26 operating cash flow roughly €0.9bn worse year over year — a derivation that assumes consistent basis across the Q2'26 discontinued-operations recast, which I could not verify, so treat it as indicative. Identified drivers are all evidenced: €700–800m of guided restructuring cash outflows, €500m of cash taxes, and working-capital build against the AI order book. I do not annualise any single quarter; Nokia's cash generation is structurally Q4-weighted.

FY2026 free cash flow implied by guidance is €1.16–1.95bn (55–75% of €2.1–2.6bn); at €2.4bn operating profit and 65% conversion, ~€1.56bn. The €0.14 dividend costs roughly €790m annually on ~5.65bn shares, or about 53% of FY2025 free cash flow.

**Balance sheet: still net cash, cushion halved.** Nokia's own measure of net cash and interest-bearing financial investments: €4,854m (Dec 2024) → €3,378m (Dec 2025) → €2,776m (Jun 2026). A third-party series computes lower absolute levels (€3,534m / €2,010m / €1,473m) because it deducts total debt including lease liabilities, but the decline is nearly identical in magnitude (~€2.06bn versus ~€2.08bn). Gross debt fell from €4,750m to €3,359m over the same span — Nokia is deleveraging, not levering. The €2.1bn net-cash erosion is explained by the Infinera acquisition, a €0.5bn net outflow for the Shanghai-Bell buyout (which Nokia notes largely equates to acquiring minority-held joint-venture cash, a near-value-neutral transaction), ~€0.79bn/year of dividends, and restructuring cash. This is not distress. It does mean the optionality a €4.9bn net cash position provided has narrowed materially at the moment Nokia commits to multi-site semiconductor manufacturing with an undisclosed Arizona purchase price.

**Beat record supports the comparable line.** Four consecutive comparable EPS beats: +16.7%, +5.0%, +22.9%, +26.4%. Q2'26 comparable operating profit of €434m beat LSEG consensus of €382m by 13.6% — a non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP comparison whose IFRS counterpart was a €50m loss.

## Growth Analysis

**The optical/AI-cloud engine is real and confirmable.** Optical Networks +20% and IP Networks +16% in Q2'26. Network Infrastructure growth guidance doubled to 12–14% and IP plus Optical to 18–20% against a Capital Markets Day long-term target of only 10–12%. AI & Cloud net sales rose 105% to €446m. AI & Cloud order intake was €2.8bn in the quarter with roughly half expected to convert within twelve months, implying ~€1.4bn of AI & Cloud revenue from one quarter's orders. Note precisely what this is: period order intake, not defined backlog, deferred revenue or remaining performance obligations. Nokia disclosed no group backlog figure, and no statement about total contracted backlog can be supported from it.

Nokia's own framing is that "supply continues to be the main industry constraint, prompting our customers to place longer-term orders." That reframes the question from whether demand exists to whether Nokia can convert capacity into share.

**Group earnings growth is the problem.** Consider what did not move. Nokia doubled NI growth guidance, gained a ~3.4% favourable FX translation assumption, and raised comparable financial income guidance by €100m — and still described its operational outlook as unchanged. Mobile Infrastructure operating profit was flat at €310m on 6% revenue growth. Nokia's own FY2028 target for that segment is to "grow from a base of EUR 1.5 billion," approximately the current run-rate. The group FY2028 comparable operating profit target of €2.7–3.2bn (midpoint €2.95bn) compares with FY2024 actual of €2,584m: +14.2% over four years, a 3.4% CAGR on raw numbers. That comparison is unfair in one direction — the target is struck at EUR:USD 1.17, a stronger EUR than FY2024 actuals were earned at, so on constant FX the plan is closer to +20–24%, roughly 5% CAGR. Either way, management's own plan for group profit is modest.

**Estimate revisions do not signal inflection.** Current-quarter consensus fell 17.9% over 30 days and 18.2% over 90 days, consistent with Nokia guiding Q3 comparable operating profit "largely flat from Q2" on software revenue-recognition phasing. Next-quarter is −3.5%. Current-year is +1.2% and next-year +2.4%. This is a stable-to-mildly-positive full-year picture with near-term timing cuts, not the +30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection. I explicitly note that the strong-buy revision signal is **absent** here.

**AI-RAN contributes nothing in 2026.** Nokia launched what it calls the industry's first commercial AI-RAN platform in mid-July 2026 on a software subscription model, claiming more than 100% spectral efficiency gains by 2028. Pilots run at the end of 2026 with commercial availability in 2027. Independent coverage is cooler than Nokia's framing, questioning whether operators will pay a subscription for efficiency gains projected two years out. This is a 2027–2028 revenue question.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived at $9.36 (≈€8.21), using ~5,646m shares (cross-checked: a third-party P/S of 2.23 on €20,265m TTM revenue implies €8.00/share against the €8.01 computed from the prior close, internally consistent):

- **Market capitalisation ≈ €46.4bn (~$52.8bn)**
- **Enterprise value ≈ €43.6bn** (less €2,776m net cash)
- **FY2026E comparable EPS ≈ €0.339** (€2,400m operating profit + €200m financial income, taxed at 26.5%, ÷ 5,646m). Consensus current-year of $0.41 (€0.36) is slightly above this.
- **P/E on FY2026E comparable EPS ≈ 24.2x**
- **EV / FY2026E comparable operating profit ≈ 18.2x**
- **P/E on FY2027 consensus $0.50 ≈ 18.7x**
- **P/E on Nokia's own FY2028 target EPS (€0.410) ≈ 20.0x**
- **Trailing IFRS P/E ≈ 72x** (TTM reported diluted EPS ~€0.113); GAAP and non-GAAP multiples differ by roughly 3x
- **Dividend yield ≈ 1.71%** (€0.14 ≈ $0.160), flat for two consecutive years
- **P/S 2.3x versus 0.81x in FY2023 and 1.19x in FY2024**

The re-rating from 0.81x to 2.3x sales has already happened. Two observations decide the valuation score.

First, consensus FY2027 of $0.50 (€0.44) implies net income of ~€2.48bn, pre-tax ~€3.38bn, and therefore comparable operating profit of roughly €3.18bn — at the top of Nokia's FY2028 target range, one year early. The $15.02 mean price target (+60% from spot) rests on both that above-plan estimate and a multiple expansion on top of it. Those targets were largely struck before the July derating; the low target of $8.50 sits below spot. I do not treat the mean as an independent floor here.

Second, if FY2027 lands in-plan at €2.7–2.8bn operating profit, comparable EPS is €0.38–0.39 ($0.43–0.45), and at 22–23x the ADR is worth $9.75–10.30. That is the honest base case: modest upside, contingent on delivery.

Against peers, Ericsson's slower-growth radio franchise trades meaningfully below 20x, while optical pure-plays with faster growth trade higher. Nokia at 24x comparable forward on a portfolio that is one-third accelerating optical and two-thirds flat-profit mobile plus a lumpy licensing annuity is neither obviously mispriced nor obviously stretched. Score: 5.0 — fair, with the asymmetry not yet favourable.

**On the 46% drawdown.** The stock fell from $17.45 (3 June) to $8.41 (29 July), including a ~30% July decline, and the 23 July comparable beat did not arrest it. I do not read this as fundamental deterioration: revenue guidance for the growth segment was doubled in the same release, the beat streak is intact, and net cash remains positive. But the burden of proof at this magnitude runs against the bulls, and what the sellers are reacting to is identifiable rather than mysterious: restructuring charges tripled, IFRS operating profit turned negative, group profit guidance was operationally unchanged despite the NI raise, and the run from $4.08 to $17.45 in nine months had built in a level of AI-narrative expectation that Q2's group P&L did not support. The derating removed euphoria; it did not create a discount.

## Competitive Landscape

**Optical and IP: improving trajectory, challenger position.** Optical +20% and IP +16% with NI gross margin up 240bps suggests the Infinera combination is accretive rather than dilutive. Nokia is explicitly pushing "inside the data center," which puts it against Cisco, Arista and Juniper/HPE in a market where it is not incumbent, and against Ciena, Cisco/Acacia and Huawei in transport. The vertical integration into Indium Phosphide fabrication — San Jose ramping in Q4 2026, Pennsylvania advanced test and packaging up 10x from Q3 2026, and a definitive agreement to lease part of NXP's Chandler, Arizona campus from early 2027 with full acquisition closing expected Q1 2029 — is an attempt to own a supply chokepoint in a capacity-constrained component market. The lease-then-buy structure defers cash cost beyond the planning horizon; the purchase price is undisclosed, so the capital commitment is unquantified for investors today.

**Mobile and RAN: stable-to-contested.** Nokia's own framing is "a stable market environment" with the priority being "efficiency and improving profitability" rather than share gain. Gross margin fell 70bps in Q2. The risk factor about competitors seeking to take share persists in both 2026 releases. Open RAN compliance lowers switching costs in both directions. Against Ericsson, Huawei, Samsung and ZTE, Nokia is defending rather than advancing.

**R&D intensity is the binding constraint.** Reported FY2025 R&D of €4,855m is 24.4% of revenue, funding optical components, IP routing, RAN, mobile core and now semiconductor manufacturing on a €19.9bn base — against Huawei, which spends multiples of that. This explains, better than any single product decision, why revenue growth does not drop to the profit line.

**On the Nvidia relationship, precision matters.** Nokia's AnyRAN software is powered by Nvidia's Aerial AI-RAN platform: Nokia is a **customer** of Nvidia for GPUs and software, and Nvidia is separately a ~2.95% equity holder and go-to-market partner following a $1bn investment at $6.01/share (166.4m shares, ~3.0% of pre-existing shares; externally reported, not filing-verified in the releases I read). Nvidia does not develop Nokia's RAN products, and the relationship is non-exclusive — the AI-RAN Alliance had roughly 130 members in January 2026. Named engagements (T-Mobile US innovation centre, Indosat Ooredoo Hutchison trials, Taiwan Mobile, an Ooredoo/Nvidia/Nokia Southeast Asia compute platform) carry no disclosed contract values, and in the Ooredoo case the telco is the buyer and Nokia the supplier.

## Risk Assessment

**Q4 concentration is the near-term binary.** Derived: at €2,400m FY comparable operating profit ("somewhat above the midpoint" of €2.1–2.6bn) less €735m H1 actual and ~€434m Q3 (Nokia's own "largely flat" language), Q4 must deliver ~€1,231m, or ~51% of the full year, +16% versus Q4'25's €1,058m. On a Q4 revenue base near €6.1bn that implies ~20.1% comparable operating margin versus 17.3% in Q4'25, +280bps. The revenue base is a sensitivity, not a forecast. Mitigating context: Nokia has always been Q4-heavy (Q4'25 was 52% of FY2025), so this is seasonal-normal. That cuts both ways — familiar, but one weak December quarter breaks the year, and the stated Q3 constraint is software revenue-recognition timing, a notoriously slippable item.

**Licensing lumpiness.** Nokia Technologies revenue swung €1,595m → €1,085m → €1,928m → €1,501m across FY2022–FY2025 at 65–100% margins. A single renewal cycle can move group comparable operating profit €300–400m, roughly 15% of the FY2026 midpoint, with no operational change. The €1.4bn contracted run-rate is a floor that must be periodically renegotiated.

**New fab execution risk.** Nokia amended its own risk factor between January and July 2026 to read "our ability to procure **or manufacture** certain components (e.g. semiconductors), the costs thereof **and our ability to align our inventory levels and supplier commitments with customer demand**." Both additions are substantive: Nokia now carries InP yield and process risk it did not previously carry, and it is placing long-dated supply commitments against an order book that assumes AI capex persists. Three simultaneous build-outs, in a material with demanding yield characteristics, in a company with no history as a volume compound-semiconductor operator.

**FX.** The guidance assumption moved from 1.18 to 1.14 within six months; the FY2028 target is struck at 1.17, a headwind to a EUR-reported target. Nokia cut FY2025 guidance by up to €310m on currency and tariffs alone.

**Finnish deferred tax assets.** Retained in both risk lists is "our ability to utilize our Finnish deferred tax assets and their recognition on our balance sheet." A write-down would be a large non-cash charge. I could not size the DTA balance — a genuine verification gap.

**Disclosure gaps.** No proxy statement means compensation quantum, pay-for-performance alignment, board composition and related-party transactions are unassessed. The Arizona purchase price is undisclosed. The Enterprise Campus Edge disposal has no signed agreement — Nokia only "deems it highly probable."

**Governance/quality residual.** Serial restructuring across four consecutive years and a comparable-to-reported gap that widened from €614m to €1,139m mean the earnings on which the 24x multiple rests have not converted to IFRS profit at anything close to face value.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 63.8% against 58.3% one-year realised — rich by 5.5 points, with the term structure flat (near 63.8% ≈ far 63.7%), so uncertainty is spread evenly rather than clustered on a dated catalyst. The data source characterises skew as call-heavy at every tenor (−3.4% at one week widening to −5.8% at six months), and put/call volume is 0.35 against balanced open interest of 0.96. That combination reads as fresh speculative call demand layered on a roughly neutral existing book — dip-buying into the July decline rather than institutional hedging. The six-month implied move of ±31.2% is the number to size against: the market is pricing a wide two-sided distribution, consistent with my flat expected return and wide scenario band. Short interest of 0.9% of float and 0.6 days to cover means there is no crowded bear position and no squeeze fuel; sceptics have expressed their view by selling stock, not borrowing it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($12.00, +28%).** Optical supply scarcity persists, and Nokia's fab programme converts capacity into share before Ciena, Coherent, Lumentum or Marvell expand. The €2.8bn quarterly AI & Cloud order intake proves representative rather than lumpy, delivering €1.4bn+ of incremental annual revenue at NI's expanding gross margin. Q4 2026 delivers the ~€1,231m implied and management's "somewhat above midpoint" language proves conservative, as it has in each of the last four quarters (beats of 17%, 5%, 23%, 26%). The 2023–2026 restructuring programme lands at the high end of €800m–1,200m gross savings, the China integration adds €200m, and the FY2027 comparable operating profit approaches the €3.0–3.2bn consensus implies — pulling the FY2028 target forward a year. AI-RAN converts subscription revenue in 2027. Licensing renews at or above the €1.4bn run-rate. At $0.50 FY2027 comparable EPS and 24x, the ADR is $12.00.

**Bear case ($6.75, −28%).** Q3 software revenue-recognition slippage pushes even more into Q4, and Q4 misses the ~20.1% margin requirement; FY2026 comparable operating profit lands near €2.1bn. A licensing renewal cycle takes €300m out of the annuity. Restructuring charges rise again from €800m, and the IFRS operating loss of Q2 recurs, forcing investors to value the company on reported earnings where it trades at 72x. Working-capital build against inventory commitments continues to depress cash conversion below the 55% low end, and the net cash position — down €2.1bn in 18 months — approaches zero as Arizona capital commitments crystallise. Mobile Infrastructure remains flat, validating Nokia's own modest FY2028 plan, and the multiple compresses toward Ericsson-like mid-teens on €0.36–0.38 comparable EPS. $6.75.

**Base case ($10.00, +6.8%).** FY2026 lands inside guidance near €2.4bn with a Q4 that delivers most but not all of the implied step-up. FY2027 comparable operating profit reaches €2.7–2.8bn — in-plan, below consensus — for comparable EPS of €0.38–0.39 ($0.43–0.45). At 22–23x, the ADR is $9.75–10.30. Optical growth continues; Mobile Infrastructure stays flat; licensing holds the €1.4bn floor. Probability-weighted across 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives roughly +2% expected return over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (hold rationale: uncertain).** I have a positive segment-level growth thesis in optical/AI-cloud with dated catalysts, and I do not have a confident group-level earnings thesis that supports appreciation from $9.36. Expected return of roughly +2% against a ±31% six-month implied move is not adequate compensation for the Q4 binary, and the fundamentals are not deteriorating enough to justify REDUCE.

- **Base case target: $10.00** (22.5x FY2027 in-plan comparable EPS of €0.385 ≈ $0.44), +6.8% from $9.36
- **Bull case target: $12.00** (24x FY2027 consensus $0.50), +28%
- **Bear case target: $6.75** (16x €0.37 ≈ $0.42), −28%
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored on the Q3 print (late October 2026), the Q4/FY2026 report (late January 2027), and the San Jose fab production ramp beginning in Q4 2026

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q4 2026 comparable operating profit of €1,150m or better with comparable operating margin at or above 19.5%, confirming the implied H2 step-up, combined with FY2026 free cash flow at or above €1.5bn (65%+ conversion).
2. A second consecutive quarter of AI & Cloud order intake at €2bn or more, with AI & Cloud net sales run-rating above €700m per quarter — evidence the €2.8bn was representative rather than a one-off.
3. Mobile Infrastructure operating profit growing at all on positive revenue growth — any break from the flat €310m — or a price below $7.50, at which the base case offers 33% upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 2026 comparable operating profit below €400m, or any language extending the software revenue-recognition delay into Q4.
2. FY2026 restructuring charge guidance raised above €800m, or FY2026 free cash flow conversion guided below the 55% floor.
3. Net cash and interest-bearing financial investments below €2.0bn, or disclosure of an Arizona purchase price that materially changes the capital plan; alternatively, a licensing renewal announced at below the €1.4bn contracted run-rate.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which resolves the Q3 software-timing question and tests whether the Q4 concentration is achievable.

## Conclusion

Nokia's optical and IP franchise is inflecting on genuine, supply-constrained AI infrastructure demand — 20% optical growth, 240bps of gross margin expansion, doubled segment guidance, and €2.8bn of quarterly AI & Cloud order intake. That part of the story is confirmed by the company's own numbers. The problem is what happens between the gross line and the group P&L: Mobile Infrastructure delivers zero operating leverage on two-thirds of segment profit, restructuring charges tripled within six months, Q2 IFRS operating profit was negative, net cash has halved in eighteen months, and management's own FY2028 plan implies only 3–5% annual comparable-profit growth.

At $9.36 — 46% below the June peak but 56% above Nvidia's entry and roughly 123% above the level of a year ago — the ADR trades at ~24x FY2026E comparable EPS, ~18x EV/comparable operating profit, ~20x Nokia's own FY2028 target EPS and ~72x trailing IFRS earnings, with a 1.7% dividend that has been flat for two years. The drawdown removed the AI-narrative premium without creating a margin of safety, and consensus FY2027 estimates already embed a result at the top of Nokia's 2028 target range. The single most consequential near-term fact is that roughly 51% of full-year comparable operating profit must arrive in Q4 2026, at a margin 280bps above the prior year, with Q3 explicitly constrained by software revenue-recognition timing. Own it if you hold it; wait for either Q4 delivery or a price closer to $7.50 before adding.
