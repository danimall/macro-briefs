**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corporation is a diversified media and information-services business whose market value sits well below the sum of its identifiable parts. The central debate concerns whether and when the conglomerate discount narrows, not financial durability — the balance sheet carries roughly $2.17B cash against $1.99B debt and the business generated $535M of free cash flow in the first nine months of FY2026. The most important asset, a 61.6% stake in ASX-listed REA Group, is independently worth a large share of (and at times more than) the company's entire $9.6B equity capitalization. Layered on top are a high-margin Dow Jones data/subscription franchise, HarperCollins, News UK/Australia, and net cash. The market is effectively assigning modest-to-negative value to the non-REA assets.

The post-Foxtel portfolio is cleaner and more recurring than at any point in the company's history. Dow Jones and Digital Real Estate Services together produced about 83% of segment EBITDA in the nine months ($1,001M of $1,204M), both growing EBITDA double-digits. Total segment EBITDA rose 10% YoY to $1,204M even as headline net income from continuing operations declined 9%, a distortion created by a prior-year $87M one-time gain on REA's PropertyGuru sale. The legacy News Media segment is the structural drag, and AI-driven disintermediation of news traffic is a real long-term threat. Against that, News Corp has begun monetizing its content moat directly: licensing deals with OpenAI and Meta turn proprietary content into a payable AI input rather than only a disruption victim.

Management's accelerated buyback — $456M repurchased in nine months versus $114M a year earlier, executed at $24–26, roughly the current price — is a credible statement that insiders view the equity as cheap, and it is funded by FCF and net cash rather than leverage. The September 2025 Murdoch succession settlement removed a multi-year governance overhang. We rate NWSA a BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $32, acknowledging that the dual-class control structure both explains the persistent discount and limits the catalysts that could fully close it.

## Company Overview

Following the April 2025 divestiture of Foxtel to DAZN (now reported as discontinued operations), News Corp operates in five reportable segments. The nine-month FY2026 mix:

| Segment | 9M FY26 Revenue | 9M FY26 Segment EBITDA | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dow Jones | $1,853M (28%) | $482M | WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch; B2B data — Risk & Compliance, Energy (OPIS), Factiva, Newswires |
| Digital Real Estate Services | $1,463M (22%) | $519M | 61.6% of REA Group (realestate.com.au, Housing.com India) + 80% of Move (Realtor.com) |
| Book Publishing | $1,722M (26%) | $230M | HarperCollins — #2 global consumer publisher |
| News Media | $1,653M (24%) | $115M | News Corp Australia, News UK (The Times, The Sun, talkSPORT), New York Post |
| Other (corporate) | — | $(142)M | Overhead + UK Newspaper Matters costs |

The strategic direction has been a deliberate shift toward recurring digital, subscription, and data revenue. Dow Jones digital is now 84% of segment revenue, and WSJ digital-only subscriptions grew 11% YoY to 4.33M. The two crown-jewel segments — Dow Jones (EBITDA margin ~26%) and Digital Real Estate (~35%) — drive the value, while News Media and Book Publishing are lower-margin and slower-growing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Total revenue from continuing operations rose 9% YoY in Q3 FY26 ($2,185M vs $2,009M) and 5% for the nine months ($6,691M vs $6,343M). Roughly four points of Q3 growth (+$88M of the $176M increase) came from a weaker USD; underlying organic growth is mid-single-digit, led by Digital Real Estate (+17% Q3) and Dow Jones (+8% Q3). The FX tailwind is a quality caveat — it can reverse and inflates the reported trajectory.

**Profitability.** Margin expanded to roughly 18%, with growth concentrated in the high-margin segments. Pressure points: News Media EBITDA fell 8% over nine months and 55% in Q3 on California Post launch costs and weaker News UK; Book Publishing EBITDA fell 7% on a $16M inventory write-off and a $13M receivable write-off tied to a distributor's closure. Net income from continuing operations was $513M (9M) vs $562M; adjusting for the prior-period PropertyGuru gain, underlying operations improved.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $815M for the nine months. Management guided to strong full-year FCF growth. Conversion is healthy and earnings quality is high — the FCF is real and recurring, anchored in subscription and data revenue.

**Balance sheet.** Near-zero net leverage. Two nuances matter: $245M of the cash sits inside REA Group and is not freely accessible to the parent (REA must declare a dividend to release it), and REA's debt is non-recourse to News Corp. In March 2026 the company refinanced into a new $1.5B unsecured facility extended to 2031, with a revolver up to $1B and a 3.5x net-leverage covenant — substantial headroom against effectively zero net debt.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks accelerated 4x YoY. The 2021 $1B program is complete, and a new $1B program launched in July 2025 has roughly $851M remaining. The dividend is a modest $0.10 semi-annual ($0.20 annual, ~0.8% yield); REA is also running its own A$200M buyback. The capital return is shareholder-friendly, accelerating, and funded by FCF rather than debt.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is bifurcated. The compounders — Dow Jones Risk & Compliance (+18–19%), Energy/OPIS (+10–12%), and Digital Real Estate — are reframing what was historically a newspaper company into an information-services and property-portal business. REA Group holds clear pricing power in Australian residential portals, and Move/Realtor.com is shifting to premium products with lead volumes up 5–6%. Dow Jones "Other" revenue grew 55% in Q3, partly reflecting the emerging AI content-licensing stream (OpenAI, ~$250M over five years per press reports; a new Meta deal announced around March 2026). These are deals where AI companies pay to use News Corp's content; News Corp is the licensor and owns the IP.

Against this, News Media remains in structural print decline, only partly offset by digital subscriptions, FX, and audio. Book Publishing is cyclical with hit-title dependence.

The estimate-revision picture tempers the growth score. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $1.08, essentially flat over 30 days and up only 2.1% over 90 days; next-year EPS at $1.29 is up 3.1%/3.9%. But the next-quarter estimate was cut 10.7% over both windows, and the current quarter was trimmed 2.8% over 30 days. The aggregate revision trend is mildly negative, which falls short of the strong forward signal that would warrant an aggressive growth rating. Earnings surprises have been positive (3 beats, 1 in-line over four quarters, beats of 12–17%), supporting confidence in near-term consensus, but the revision data argues against extrapolating acceleration. We score growth at 6.2 — solid in the crown jewels, dragged by the legacy segments and soft near-term revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline multiple — 33x trailing/forward P/E on $0.84 EPS — overstates expensiveness because it is a consolidated number that captures the low-margin legacy segments and excludes the look-through value of REA. The proper frame is sum-of-the-parts.

News Corp's $9.6B equity value compares against:
- A 61.6% stake in publicly traded REA Group. REA's standalone market capitalization is large enough that the stake alone has historically been valued in the ballpark of, or above, News Corp's entire equity. This is the single most important valuation fact a screening multiple misses.
- Dow Jones, a high-margin data/subscription compounder that comparable standalone information-services businesses would value richly.
- HarperCollins, the #2 global consumer publisher.
- ~$2.2B cash against $1.99B debt (net cash).

Stacked together, the market is assigning little-to-no value to Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and News Media after crediting REA and net cash. The discount has narrowed historically when management surfaces value (the prior Fox/News split structure, asset sales) but persists because the dual-class Murdoch structure suppresses break-up and activist optionality.

Against publishing peers, the picture is mixed: NYT trades at 32.1x, NWS (the Class B) at 38.7x, IAC at 25.3x, WLY at 14.6x, SCHL at 16.8x; peer median P/E is 25.3x. NWSA at 33x is above median on a consolidated basis but the comparison is not apples-to-apples — none of the peers carry an embedded controlled stake in a separately listed market-leading property portal. The analyst price-target set is constructive: low $29.40, mean $35.18, median $35.00, high $43.00, all above the current $26.10. With shares roughly 17% below the 52-week high of $31.61 and fundamentals intact, the risk/reward has improved on the pullback. We score valuation 6.9 — the SOTP discount is real and the buyback validates it, but the structural reasons the discount persists (Murdoch control) keep this from a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

**Digital Real Estate:** REA Group is the clear #1 in Australian residential property portals, with demonstrated pricing power (Australian residential revenue up on price increases plus add-ons). REA's earlier (withdrawn) Rightmove bid signals consolidation ambitions. Move/Realtor.com is the #2 US portal behind Zillow, repositioning toward premium products.

**Dow Jones B2B:** Competes against Bloomberg, S&P/Moody's, LSEG, and Thomson Reuters in financial data and compliance. Risk & Compliance and Energy/OPIS are the highest-growth pieces and represent a genuine information-services moat in recurring enterprise data.

**Book Publishing:** HarperCollins competes with Penguin Random House, Hachette, and Macmillan; performance is hit-driven.

**News Media:** Structurally declining print, partly offset by digital subscriptions and audio (talkSPORT). This is the segment most exposed to AI-driven disintermediation of search and news traffic. The AI licensing deals partially convert that threat into revenue, but they do not eliminate the traffic-erosion risk. The franchise content (WSJ, Barron's, The Times) remains scarce and increasingly valuable as a payable AI input. Trajectory is improving in the high-value segments and deteriorating in News Media; net, we score 6.6.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Conglomerate discount may not close.** The dual-class Murdoch control structure entrenches family control (capped at 44% of Class B under the new agreement, with a right of first refusal), which limits hostile activist pressure or break-up scenarios. The discount could persist indefinitely. This is the primary risk to the thesis — the value is visible but the unlock mechanism is constrained.

2. **FX reversal.** Roughly half of recent revenue growth was currency-driven. A stronger USD would compress reported AUD/GBP results.

3. **REA cash trap and minority structure.** News Corp consolidates 100% of REA's revenue but owns 61.6%; minority interests took $119M of net income over nine months, and REA's cash is not freely accessible to the parent.

4. **Litigation overhang.** The OPIS PVC-pipe antitrust matter includes a DOJ Antitrust grand-jury subpoena (Jan 2025) and a state AG civil investigative demand (Apr 2025); a US civil class settlement received preliminary approval (July 2025) but a Canadian class action followed (Sept 2025). A criminal antitrust inquiry is unusual and worth monitoring. UK Newspaper Matters (phone-hacking) carries ~$16M accrued (indemnified by Fox) plus reputational tail risk.

5. **AI disruption is double-edged.** AI assistants could erode news traffic and content value even as licensing creates incremental high-margin revenue. Net direction is uncertain over a multi-year horizon.

6. **News Media secular decline** continues, with EBITDA down despite FX help.

Most of these are partly priced. The unpriced asymmetry skews modestly positive (discount closure, AI licensing upside) against a real but bounded downside (litigation, FX). We score risk 6.2.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.6% is rich relative to 24.0% one-year historical volatility (a 9.6pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.6% at one month vs 29.0% at six months), which prices imminent stress around the next catalyst window. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.2% near-term) and put/call open-interest ratio is negligible (0.03), so there is no directional bearish positioning — the elevated near-term vol reflects event premium rather than informed downside bets. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.5%. The richness of near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing or for selling premium against a position rather than chasing it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The conglomerate discount narrows as the high-margin Dow Jones data franchise and REA's portal dominance get re-rated, aided by continued double-digit EBITDA growth in the crown jewels, accelerating AI licensing revenue, and an aggressive buyback shrinking the share count at depressed prices. Segment EBITDA compounds at high-single-digit/low-double-digit rates; FCF growth funds further repurchase. The stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $43.

**Bear case.** The Murdoch control structure keeps the discount permanently in place; FX reverses and erases reported growth; News Media decline accelerates and AI disintermediates news traffic faster than licensing offsets; litigation (OPIS DOJ inquiry) produces a material adverse outcome. The stock drifts back toward the low-$22 area.

The base case sits between: steady EBITDA growth in the compounders, continued buyback support, no full discount closure but partial narrowing, and a re-rating toward the analyst low-to-mean range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $32 (12 months).** Built on roughly 5–7% segment EBITDA growth, continued ~$1B buyback reducing share count, partial narrowing of the conglomerate discount, and the analyst median target of $35 acting as a re-rating anchor. We discount the median for the structural reasons the discount persists, arriving at $32, ~23% upside from $26.10.

**Bull case target: $43.** Matches the analyst high — full re-rating of Dow Jones plus REA stake recognition. ~65% upside.

**Bear case target: $22.** Near the 52-week low; discount entrenched, FX reverses, News Media accelerates downward. ~16% downside.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with key checkpoints at quarterly earnings (next reporting cycle) and any update on the OPIS DOJ matter.

**Upside/downside:** +23% to base, +65% to bull, -16% to bear. The asymmetry favors the long.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Segment EBITDA growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (crown-jewel compounding is the core of the thesis).
- Management slows or halts the buyback while the stock remains below $28, signaling reduced confidence in undervaluation.
- A material adverse development in the OPIS DOJ antitrust inquiry (indictment or large reserve).
- Current-year EPS estimates are cut more than 10% over a 90-day window, signaling fundamental deterioration rather than mix noise.

## Conclusion

News Corp is a value-realization story backed by a fortress balance sheet. The net-cash position, nine-month FCF, accelerating buyback at the current price, and resolved succession overhang support a constructive view, while the sum-of-the-parts discount provides the upside. The constraint is structural: the dual-class Murdoch control that explains the discount also limits the catalysts that would close it, and near-term estimate revisions are soft. This is a measured BUY rather than a maximum-conviction one. We rate NWSA **BUY** with a $32 base-case target over 12 months, with the buyback and crown-jewel EBITDA growth as the monitorable anchors of the thesis.
