**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AdaptHealth is one of the largest national providers of home medical equipment (HME), medical supplies, and "healthcare-at-home" services, serving ~4.5 million patients annually across ~670 locations. It is a 2019 SPAC-origin company that grew through a debt-funded acquisition roll-up, and it now carries the consequences of that strategy: $2.54B of goodwill against a ~$1.28B equity market value, ~$1.82B of total debt, and a balance sheet that screens in the distress zone on Altman Z (1.64). The investment question is whether a pivot toward at-risk capitation and higher-margin Respiratory can offset a structurally challenged Diabetes segment and a thicket of reimbursement and litigation overhangs.

The fundamental picture deteriorated through FY2025 and into Q1 2026. Revenue was roughly flat (−0.5% to $3,245M in FY2025), the company posted a −$70.8M net loss after a $128M Diabetes goodwill impairment, Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 19.0% from 21.1%, and Q1 2026 free cash flow turned negative −$27.5M as capex surged to fund a new capitation contract. The earnings track record is poor: four consecutive misses, including a −786% surprise in the March 2026 quarter, and consensus EPS estimates have been cut −16% to −32% over 90 days for the current period and year. These are genuine signs of deterioration, not noise.

Against that, several counter-signals argue the bearish case is largely priced. The stock trades at ~$9.60, between its $8.06 52-week low and $13.43 high, and Phase 1 reflects a −5.3% move on the latest session. A major-holder director, Richard Cashin, deployed ~$24M in open-market purchases at $9.73–9.94 in March 2026, with two other insiders buying at $8.91–8.96 in February — informed capital deployed above any reasonable bear-case price. The Street's lowest target is $11 (mean $14) with zero sell ratings. An April 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031, and ~$350M of revolver capacity remains undrawn. The balance of evidence supports a HOLD: the downside is supported by insider buying and the maturity extension, while the upside requires the capitation bet to prove margin-accretive and the DOJ probes to resolve without material liability, neither of which is yet visible. The classification is an uncertain hold; the evidence does not support high conviction in either direction.

## Company Overview

AdaptHealth provides patient-centered home medical equipment, supplies, and related services across all 50 states, with ~10,900 employees plus ~4,400 offshore back-office FTEs in India and the Philippines handling billing and administration. The business is organized into four reportable segments:

- **Sleep Health** (~$1,378M, 42.5% of revenue; ~22.5% segment margin) — CPAP/BiLevel therapy for sleep apnea. The largest and a profitable, resupply-driven franchise, though margin has compressed from 25.8% in 2024.
- **Respiratory Health** (~$691M, 21.3%; ~30% margin) — oxygen and home ventilation for COPD and respiratory failure. The highest-margin segment and growing.
- **Diabetes Health** (~$592M, 18.3%; ~4.4% margin, down from 9.9%) — CGMs and insulin pumps. The problem segment, recently impaired by $128M.
- **Wellness at Home** (~$583M, 18.0%; ~12% margin) — post-acute HME, actively being shrunk via divestitures.

Revenue mix is approximately 63% one-time sales/resupply, 33% fixed monthly equipment reimbursement, and ~4% at-risk capitation (rising rapidly). Payor mix in Q1 2026 was 59% insurance, 25% government, 16% patient-pay. The company's stated competitive advantage is an integrated, technology-enabled centralized platform (e-prescribing, proprietary mobile delivery) versus a fragmented field of mom-and-pop suppliers. It competes with Lincare, Rotech, Cardinal, Quipt, Viemed, and Inogen, and increasingly with CVS and Amazon at the consumable/retail edge.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is stalling.** FY revenue moved from $2,971M (2022) to $3,200M (2023) to $3,261M (2024) to $3,245M (2025), a −0.5% decline reflecting ~$92M of disposition drag (incontinence, infusion, custom rehab) partly offset by +1.7% organic and +0.6% acquisition. Q1 2026 revenue rose +5.4% to $819.8M, but that headline is flattered by a single large at-risk capitation contract signed in Q3 2025: capitated revenue jumped from $32.8M to $74.9M year-over-year, and organic growth of +9.1% is heavily capitation-driven. Stripping out the contract ramp, the durable organic trend is far more modest.

**Profitability deteriorated.** FY2025 net loss of −$70.8M reversed a +$90.4M profit in 2024, driven by the Diabetes goodwill impairment, partly offset by a $32.6M gain on business sales and burdened by higher tax. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $616.7M from $688.7M. Q1 2026 continued the slide — net loss −$16.0M, Adjusted EBITDA margin 14.8% versus 16.4% — with management attributing ~$12M of the gap to transition labor for the new capitation contract.

**Cash flow quality is the central concern.** FY2025 reported FCF was healthy at $219.4M (OCF $601.8M less $382.4M capex), but Q1 2026 FCF turned negative as capex surged to fund equipment for the capitation ramp. Cash fell to $48.0M from $106M, working capital went negative −$55.8M, and the company drew $100M on its revolver. This is a capital-intensive model: patient-equipment depreciation alone was ~$341M in 2025, and capex is incurred ahead of capitated revenue. The mechanism: signing an at-risk capitation contract requires AdaptHealth to deploy equipment and onboarding labor up front, while revenue and margin normalize only after the transition phase. Whether margins normalize is the swing variable on the entire growth thesis.

**The balance sheet is leveraged and goodwill-heavy.** Total assets are $4.32B, of which goodwill is $2.54B (~59%) even after impairments of $831M in 2023 and $128M in 2025. Total debt is ~$1.82B, D/E is 1.84, and stockholders' equity of $1.52B sits against a −$649M accumulated deficit. The debt stack comprises ~$311M secured term loan plus $1,425M senior unsecured notes (6.125% due 2028 [$335M], 4.625% due 2029 [$500M], 5.125% due 2030 [$600M]). Interest expense was $105.8M in 2025, down from $126.7M as debt was paid down ($218.8M of voluntary term-loan repayments). A SPAC-legacy Tax Receivable Agreement carries a $238.9M liability requiring AdaptHealth to pay 85% of certain tax savings to former LLC members, a recurring ~$25–27M/yr cash leak not captured in standard leverage metrics.

**On the liquidity question, the distress framing is overstated.** The April 2026 refinancing established a new credit agreement ($325M term + $325M delayed-draw term + $450M revolver) maturing April 2031, with the delayed-draw tranche earmarked to retire the 2028 notes. With only $100M drawn, roughly $350M of revolver capacity remains available. Combined with $602M of FY25 operating cash flow, there is no near-term maturity wall or covenant breach pressuring the equity. The Altman Z of 1.64 is dragged down by the goodwill-heavy, equity-light SPAC structure and post-impairment EBIT; it overstates bankruptcy risk in isolation. The more accurate near-term concern is reimbursement, litigation, and the cash-consumption of the capitation ramp, not imminent insolvency, though $48M of cash and negative working capital do bear watching.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is bifurcated and currently low-quality. Respiratory Health (~30% margin, growing) and the capitation pivot are the genuine forward drivers; Diabetes is in structural decline and Wellness at Home is being deliberately shrunk. The reported Q1 2026 acceleration is real in dollars but concentrated in one capitation contract that is currently dilutive to both cash and margin — capitated revenue more than doubled while FCF went negative and EBITDA margin fell ~160 bps.

The estimate-revision picture is unambiguously negative, which is the dominant forward signal here. Current-quarter consensus EPS was cut −31.9% over 30 days and −31.3% over 90 days; current-year EPS was cut −16.4%/−15.8%. Estimates are falling, not rising, alongside four consecutive earnings misses, which argues against treating a high trailing multiple as cheap. Next-year EPS of $1.18 is down −6.4% over 30 days and −5.5% over 90 days but still implies a return to meaningful profitability if the capitation contract matures and Respiratory continues to grow. The durability of that recovery is unproven.

Diabetes deserves explicit treatment because multiple independent red threads converge on it: EBITDA margin fell from ~10% to ~4% (2.9% in Q1'26), the segment took the $128M impairment, CGM census is declining, and CGMs and insulin pumps are explicitly in the 2026–2028 DMEPOS Competitive Bidding categories. This is structural impairment, not a cyclical trough, and it caps the consolidated growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative (−17.2x) on a loss-making trailing year, so earnings multiples are not directly usable. The peer set in Health Care Equipment is similarly distorted — ENOV (−1.1x), IART (−2.7x), STAA (−68.3x), TNDM (−11.3x) all carry negative or meaningless P/Es, with INSP the lone positive at 9.5x. The peer median P/E of 9.5x is not a clean comparable given the cohort's losses.

On forward earnings, next-year consensus of $1.18 against a $9.60 price implies ~8.1x forward P/E, and the current-year $0.84 implies ~11.4x. Those are undemanding multiples in absolute terms, but a low multiple alone is not a buy signal here: the compression reflects genuine structural headwinds (Diabetes decline, reimbursement risk, two DOJ probes, negative Q1 FCF, falling estimates), not a clean mispricing. This is closer to a value-trap profile than an asymmetric-upside setup, and the falling estimate trend argues against rewarding the cheapness.

What anchors the downside and prevents an AVOID is the cluster of priced-in counter-signals. The Street's lowest of six targets is $11, mean $14, with zero sell ratings — the entire sell-side floor sits above the current price. The major-holder director and two other insiders bought above any defensible bear-case level. When informed insiders deploy eight figures of personal capital above where a bearish thesis would target, the bearish view is substantially reflected in the price. The equity sits with the negatives largely discounted; the valuation neither offers asymmetric upside nor screens as a clean short.

## Competitive Landscape

The HME market is fragmented, consolidating, and reimbursement-pressured. Scale players with integrated technology (AdaptHealth, Lincare, Rotech) gain share and better vendor pricing as smaller suppliers struggle to fund technology and absorb rate cuts. AdaptHealth's competitive position is mixed: it retains scale and platform advantages in Sleep and Respiratory, but its historical growth-by-acquisition playbook is now constrained by new CMS rules — a February 2026 six-month nationwide enrollment moratorium on certain DMEPOS supplier types, a 36-month change-of-majority-ownership rule applying to DMEPOS from January 2026, and annual rather than three-year re-surveys.

Two structural forces dominate the trajectory. First, Medicare DMEPOS Competitive Bidding resumes in 2026 (bids late-2026, contracts ~2028) and now extends to CGMs and insulin pumps plus urological/ostomy supplies, directly threatening the already-weak Diabetes segment. Second, OBBBA tightens Medicaid eligibility, adds work requirements (2027), caps state-directed payments, and lets ACA subsidies expire, which could reduce covered lives and reimbursement across the 25% of revenue tied to government payors. The capitation pivot is a defensible strategic response — moving up the value chain to lock in payer relationships — but it transfers utilization risk to AdaptHealth and front-loads capex and labor. The net competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-deteriorating, with the Respiratory franchise the clearest bright spot.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is elevated and partly unquantified:

1. **Two active DOJ False Claims Act investigations** strike the profit engines — South Carolina (July 2024, billing for humidifiers integrated with PAP devices, 2017–present) and Eastern District of Pennsylvania (March 2025, respiratory devices and supplies, 2018–present). FCA exposure can include treble damages and program exclusion. Both are early-stage with no reserve taken. This is the largest unquantified tail risk.

2. **Securities and derivative litigation** tied to diabetes billing and acquisition integration. The Allegheny County securities class action settled for $35M ($34M insurer-funded), with final-approval motion in April 2026; three related derivative suits reached agreement-in-principle in March 2026, with the Myers complaint alleging insider trading. The securities themes overlap the segment that was just impaired.

3. **NC Debt Collection Practices Act class action (Ray)** settled for $14.5M company-funded, expensed in 2025, reflecting billing/refund process weaknesses.

4. **Reimbursement/regulatory overhang** — Competitive Bidding extending to CGMs/insulin pumps, OBBBA Medicaid tightening, and the new CMS enrollment moratorium and ownership-change rules.

5. **Goodwill impairment risk persists** — goodwill against a ~$1.28B market cap, with the methodology reconciling to market cap, so a sustained price decline is itself a triggering event, as in 2023 and 2025.

These risks justify the low risk-factor score. They are partly offset by the maturity extension to 2031, the litigation-resolution wave reducing some overhang, and the insider buying that suggests informed parties view the equity as undervalued near current levels.

### Options Market Signal

The options data shows a large shift since the prior snapshot: ATM IV moved from 50.4% to 277.0% (+226.6pp) while the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 2.99 to 71.56, on a slightly lower price. An IV reading near 277% likely reflects a thin, illiquid options book around a small-cap name rather than a clean directional signal — single large positions can distort both IV and the P/C ratio in low-volume chains. Taken at face value, the readings indicate elevated near-term uncertainty and heavy put positioning, consistent with the binary overhangs (DOJ probes, reimbursement). The signal is a caution flag on near-term volatility, not a confirmation of either thesis; it argues against deploying fresh capital ahead of catalyst resolution and supports the HOLD stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The capitation contract proves margin-accretive once transition labor rolls off, Respiratory continues to grow at ~30% margins, and the company returns to the ~$1.18 next-year EPS consensus. Litigation overhangs resolve without material new liability, the DOJ probes settle within insurance/reserve ranges, and the refinancing plus deleveraging path improves the equity cushion. Insider buying at ~$9.7–9.9 proves prescient, and the stock re-rates toward the $14 Street mean and beyond to the $18 high target. Bull case ~$14–16, roughly +46% to +67% from $9.60.

**Bear Case.** Capitation margins stay compressed, Diabetes continues to bleed share into Competitive Bidding, one or both DOJ FCA probes produce a material settlement, and OBBBA reduces covered lives. Negative FCF persists, liquidity tightens, and a sustained price decline triggers another goodwill impairment. Estimates continue to fall and the stock retests the $8.06 52-week low. Bear case ~$8, roughly −17% from $9.60, below where the major-holder director deployed $24M, which is the key reason this scenario looks less probable than the price action alone would suggest.

**Resolution.** The base case is a stock that grinds in a $9–12 band while the market waits for capitation economics and DOJ outcomes to clarify. The bearish fundamentals are real but substantially priced; the bullish catalysts are plausible but unproven. This is the textbook profile for a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August 2026), which should show whether capitation transition labor is rolling off and whether the ~14.8% Adjusted EBITDA margin recovers toward the prior ~16%+ range. The Competitive Bidding bid window in late 2026 and any DOJ developments are the other natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin recovers above ~17% for two consecutive quarters and FCF turns positive, confirming the capitation contract is accretive rather than dilutive.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year EPS revised up rather than the recent −16%), signaling the earnings cuts have bottomed.
- Resolution of at least one DOJ FCA probe within insurance/reserve ranges, removing a material tail risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse DOJ FCA settlement or an enforcement action threatening program participation.
- Cash falls further while FCF stays negative and revolver draws climb, pointing toward genuine liquidity stress rather than ramp-related investment.
- A third goodwill impairment, or Diabetes/Sleep margins compressing further, indicating the structural decline is broadening into the profit engines.
- Sustained price decline below ~$8 on deteriorating fundamentals (not market beta), which would invalidate the insider-buying support.

## Conclusion

AdaptHealth presents a balanced risk/reward at ~$9.60. The fundamental deterioration is genuine: four straight earnings misses, sharply falling consensus estimates, a structurally impaired Diabetes segment, two open DOJ False Claims Act investigations targeting the profit engines, and negative Q1 free cash flow as a single capitation contract consumes capital. These are real and they cap the upside.

The AVOID case does not clear the bar, because the bearish thesis is substantially priced. The Street floor target ($11) is above the current price with zero sell ratings, a major-holder director and two other insiders bought aggressively above any defensible bear-case level, the April 2026 refinancing pushed maturities to 2031, and ~$350M of revolver capacity plus $602M of FY25 operating cash flow undercut the distress framing. For the stock to fall meaningfully below where informed insiders deployed $24M of personal capital, the market would need a structural-impairment-at-any-price catalyst that the current data does not support. The recovery case is equally unproven: capitation margins must normalize, Respiratory must keep growing, and the DOJ probes must resolve benignly. With neither thesis carrying conviction, the appropriate call is **HOLD (uncertain)**, monitored against the Q2 2026 print and the capitation/DOJ checkpoints above.
