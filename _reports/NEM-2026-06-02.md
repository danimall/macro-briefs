**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Newmont is the world's largest gold producer and the only gold miner in the S&P 500. Its 2025–Q1 2026 financial transformation is real and largely driven by one variable: the gold price. FY25 revenue rose 21% to $22.67B, net income more than doubled to $7.09B, free cash flow hit $7.3B, and average realized gold reached $3,498/oz against a co-product AISC of $1,609/oz. Q1 2026 set records: $4,900/oz realized gold, $3.14B FCF, by-product AISC down to $1,029/oz. The balance sheet swung from $5.3B net debt at YE2024 to $3.2B net cash by Q1 2026, and management has paired a $1.1B/yr sustainable dividend with $6B of completed buybacks and another $6B authorized in April 2026.

The difficulty is that almost none of this is operational. Attributable production is drifting lower (5.89 Moz FY25; 1.30 Moz in Q1 2026, down 10% sequentially on bushfire, rain, and maintenance disruptions), and 2026 guidance points to AISC rising back toward $1,680/oz on Ghana royalty escalation and tailings capex. The equity is a high-beta call on the gold price wearing an improved-balance-sheet narrative. With gold at record levels and the stock having appreciated ~95% over the trailing year, the relevant question is whether the current price embeds asymmetric upside. It does not. The 14x trailing multiple looks cheap only because trailing earnings sit at a cyclical peak built on a $3,498–$4,900/oz gold price that the company itself stress-tests at $2,500/oz for impairment purposes.

We rate NEM a HOLD. The financial inflection and shareholder-return discipline are genuine, but they are offset by commodity-price dependence at a cyclical high, the active Nevada Gold Mines dispute with Barrick, Ghana fiscal escalation, and a near-term options structure pricing imminent stress. This is a defensible core position for investors who want gold exposure, but the entry does not offer the risk-adjusted upside required for a fresh purchase.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1921 and headquartered in Denver, Newmont produces roughly 5% of the world's mined gold across a portfolio spanning the U.S., Australia, Papua New Guinea, Ghana, Canada, Peru, Mexico, Suriname, Argentina, and equity stakes elsewhere (Lundin Gold's Fruta del Norte). At YE2025 it held 118.2 million attributable proven and probable gold reserves.

The November 2023 Newcrest acquisition (~$13.5B, all-stock) roughly doubled the share count from ~841M to ~1,146M and added Lihir, Cadia, Telfer, Brucejack, and Red Chris. Newmont then ran a deliberate post-deal optimization, divesting six non-core mines plus the Coffee project (Telfer, CC&V, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine, Akyem) for $4.6B+ after-tax. The result is a leaner 13-segment structure: 12 managed mines plus a 38.5% non-operated interest in Nevada Gold Mines, the JV Barrick operates. Gold is ~85% of sales; copper, silver, lead, and zinc (from Peñasquito, Cadia, Boddington, Red Chris) round out the mix.

The strategy contrasts with Barrick's. Where Barrick is pursuing a restructuring/IPO of its North American assets, Newmont is shrinking via divestitures and buybacks to maximize per-share metrics rather than chasing M&A.

## Financial Analysis

The trailing financials are the strongest in the company's history.

| Revenue | $11.8B | $18.7B | $22.7B | $7.3B |
| Net income (attrib.) | –$2.49B | $3.35B | $7.09B | $3.26B |
| Diluted EPS | –$3.00 | $2.92 | $6.39 | $3.00 |
| Adj. EBITDA | $4.2B | $8.7B | $13.5B | $5.15B |
| Free cash flow | $0.09B | $2.9B | $7.3B | $3.14B |
| Avg realized gold | $1,954 | $2,408 | $3,498 | $4,900 |

FY25 net margin was 31.6%, FCF margin ~32%, ROE 21.2%, ROA 12.5%. The balance sheet is the standout. Newmont repaid $3.4B of debt in 2025 (including a $2B tender), ending the year with $7.65B cash against $5.1B debt — a $2.06B net cash position, extending to $3.2B by Q1 2026 with cash of $8.8B. Net debt/EBITDA is roughly –0.2x. Current ratio is 2.29. The Altman Z of 2.26 ("grey zone") reflects the capital-intensive asset base in the trailing calculation, not distress; the company comfortably clears its net-debt/total-cap covenant (≤62.5%).

The mechanical driver is plain: each $100/oz move in realized gold is roughly $505M of revenue. Costs moved favorably too — Q1 2026 by-product AISC benefited from surging silver/copper by-product credits — but 2026 guidance signals upward cost pressure from Ghana royalties, elevated tailings capex at Cadia and Boddington, and lower-grade quarters.

FCF conversion is high-quality (FCF roughly tracks net income), and the capital-return framework is well-designed: a fixed $1.1B/yr dividend dollar commitment translating into rising per-share dividends as the count shrinks (1,146M in 2024 → 1,085M in Q1 2026). The buyback pace — $2.4B repurchased since the prior call — is consistent with a management team prioritizing per-share compounding over volume growth.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the thesis weakens. The earnings surge is price-led, not volume-led. Attributable production has been drifting lower (1.54 → 1.48 → 1.42 → 1.45 Moz across recent quarters, then 1.30 Moz in Q1 2026, down 10% QoQ on Boddington bushfire, Tanami rain/grade, and Lihir/Cerro Negro maintenance). FY26 guidance of 5.26 Moz is below FY25's 5.89 Moz.

Estimate revisions are positive but their composition matters. Current-quarter EPS revised +4.8% over 30 days, next-quarter +5.1% (and +18.8% over 90 days), current-year +11.0% over 90 days. These are meaningful, but they track the gold price higher rather than reflecting a structural volume or cost inflection. The earnings beat track record is strong (four straight beats: +20.4%, +18.3%, +24.7%, +31.7%), which raises confidence in near-term consensus, but that beat pattern is driven by realized gold prices running ahead of conservative estimates.

The growth project pipeline (Tanami Expansion 2, commercial H2 2027; Cadia Panel Caves to 2029; Ahafo North, commercial Oct 2025; Red Chris Block Cave; Wafi-Golpu in PNG) supports volume stabilization over a multi-year horizon, not near-term acceleration. Sustaining capex is elevated for tailings work. The honest characterization is flat-to-modestly-declining volume with rising unit costs, where the earnings line is wholly dependent on the commodity holding near record levels.

Per the estimate-revision signal threshold, the +30%+ velocity that flags a genuine inflection buy is not present here on the full-year numbers (+11.0% current-year, +4.8% next-year over 90 days), and what revision momentum exists is commodity-driven rather than an operating turnaround. This is a peak-cycle commodity producer, not the cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates inflection setup.

## Valuation Assessment

NEM trades at 14.1x trailing earnings ($6.39 EPS, $106.61 reference price) and roughly 10.3x the current-year consensus of $10.40. Against the Materials peer median P/E of 29.4 (FCX 34.8, SHW 29.2, CRH 20.1, ECL 34.6, APD 29.4), Newmont looks deeply cheap. That comparison is misleading. The peer set is dominated by specialty chemicals and base-metals names with mid-cycle or recurring earnings; Newmont's trailing earnings sit at a cyclical peak built on a gold price the company values at $2,500/oz for its own impairment testing.

The correct lens is cycle-adjusted. On the current $4,900/oz realized price, 10x forward earnings is the multiple a high-quality producer fetches at the top of a commodity cycle, where the market correctly discounts the durability of trough-to-peak earnings. If gold normalizes toward, say, $3,000/oz, FY27 EPS would compress materially below the $11.40 next-year consensus, and the "10x" becomes 15–18x on normalized earnings — fair, not asymmetric.

Analyst targets (mean $142.8, median $145, range $72–$175) imply ~34% upside to the mean. On a high-momentum stock with a compressed forward multiple, targets often lag. Here, though, the targets are themselves a leveraged bet on gold staying elevated; they are not independent confirmation of mispricing. The $72 low target frames the downside if gold reverses.

The valuation score reflects a genuine but conditional case: cheap on trailing/forward earnings if and only if gold holds near record levels, with a clearly identifiable mechanism (commodity reversion) that would invalidate the cheapness. That is a 6.0 — supportive but not the asymmetric setup a high valuation score requires.

## Competitive Landscape

Newmont is one of two gold supermajors alongside Barrick. Its competitive position rests on scale, reserve quality across favorable-to-mixed jurisdictions, and disciplined cost management. The trajectory is mixed. The post-Newcrest portfolio cleanup improved asset quality and the balance sheet, and the per-share capital framework is shareholder-friendly. Against that, attributable production is declining, the largest single asset (Nevada Gold Mines, ~999 koz attributable in FY25) is in active dispute with its operator, and relative TSR (195 vs. a peer group at 330 over the measurement period) shows the market has rewarded peers more.

The Barrick dynamic cuts both ways. Barrick's planned North American spin-off hinges on Newmont's approval because Newmont holds a right of first refusal on Barrick's NGM stake — negotiating leverage that could resolve into improved NGM governance, a larger Newmont interest, or a value-unlocking transaction. Until resolved, it is an overhang on Newmont's biggest contributor, where Q1 2026 NGM attributable production fell 19% QoQ to 236 koz. Stable-to-slightly-deteriorating competitive trajectory warrants a 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Gold-price dependence.** The dominant risk. Earnings and FCF are overwhelmingly price-driven against flat-to-declining volume. The operating leverage that produced record FCF works violently in reverse if gold reverts. Gold is not a one-way bet even amid geopolitical stress, and a rising-real-yield path would pressure the metal.

**Nevada Gold Mines / Barrick dispute.** Newmont accused its JV operator of diverting NGM resources to benefit Barrick's wholly-owned Fourmile deposit, exercised audit rights (Jan 26, 2026), and issued a notice of default (Feb 3, 2026). Material, unusual, and unresolved.

**Ghana fiscal escalation.** The Revised Investment Agreement expired Dec 31, 2025; Ghana enacted a sliding 5–12% royalty tied to gold price (Mar 10, 2026), a 35% max corporate tax, and a Growth & Sustainability Levy (3%, cut to 1% Apr 1, 2026). Estimated ~$185/oz AISC impact to Ghana ops (~$25/oz to total Newmont). Ahafo South/North exposed.

**Reclamation overhang.** ~$9.9B undiscounted obligations ($6.8B accrued); Yanacocha water-treatment plants ~$1.8B ($938M spent). Elevated reclamation spend through 2027.

**Asset-specific operating risks.** Q4'25 $779M Yanacocha Sulfides impairment; Conga (Peru) idled since 2011; Cadia magnitude-4.5 earthquake (Apr 2026, limited damage, Q2 production hit); Cerro Negro (Argentina) fatalities, safety shutdowns, currency controls; Peñasquito tailings-cost increases.

**FX/cost.** 59% of costs non-USD (27% AUD), partially hedged.

**Margin of safety partially offsetting.** Management's $2,500/oz impairment assumption (vs. ~$4,900 spot) means asset values are stress-tested far below market — a $100 decrease causes no impairment. This limits downside impairment risk and signals conservative reserve marks.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that the dominant risk (commodity reversion at a cyclical peak) plus two specific, unresolved situations (NGM dispute, Ghana fiscal) are not fully discounted in the trailing multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure is cautionary. Near-term ATM IV of 52.9% sits rich to 46.7% historical vol (+6.2pp), at the 67th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (52.9% near-term vs. 44.1% at six months), which prices imminent near-term stress, typical ahead of a binary event or earnings volatility. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±4.0%, widening to ±20.2% by September. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-3.2%), and put/call OI of 0.99 is balanced, so positioning is not outright bearish. Since the prior snapshot, the stock fell 7.4% while IV held flat and P/C OI ticked up from 0.89 to 0.99 — consistent with hedging into a gold-price-sensitive window rather than a directional collapse. The market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, which supports patience over a fresh purchase at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Gold holds or extends above $4,000–$5,000/oz on persistent geopolitical risk, fiscal deficits, and central-bank demand. Newmont compounds per-share FCF via the buyback at a shrinking share count, the NGM dispute resolves favorably (improved governance, larger stake, or a value-unlocking transaction leveraging the right of first refusal), and the multiple re-rates toward the analyst mean. Bull case ~$160–$175, broadly in line with the analyst high of $175 (~50–64% upside), predicated on sustained record gold and successful capital return.

### Bear Case

Gold reverts toward $3,000/oz as real yields rise (a hawkish FOMC, easing geopolitical premium). FY27 EPS compresses well below the $11.40 consensus, the "cheap" multiple re-rates higher on lower earnings, Ghana royalties and tailings capex push AISC up, production keeps drifting lower, and the NGM dispute drags. Bear case ~$72 (the analyst low, ~-32% from current), with the 52-week low of $51.80 a tail outcome on a sharper gold reversion.

The base case sits between: gold holds in a $3,500–$4,500/oz range, FCF stays strong, buybacks support per-share metrics, but the multiple does not expand because the market correctly recognizes peak-cycle earnings. Base case ~$120 (~13% upside), reflecting modest appreciation from continued capital return against a leveling commodity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The fundamental story is strong but the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the dominant variable (gold) is outside the company's control and near a cyclical high.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$80s/low-$90s (forward P/E compressing below ~9x on stable estimates) while gold holds above $4,000/oz — improving the entry without fundamental deterioration.
- Favorable resolution of the NGM/Barrick dispute that materially improves Nevada production economics or expands Newmont's stake on accretive terms.
- Evidence of volume stabilization/growth (FY27 production guidance above 5.5 Moz) that shifts the thesis from purely price-led to operationally supported.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gold breaks decisively below $3,000/oz with rising real yields, threatening the earnings base.
- Production guidance cut below 5.0 Moz combined with AISC guidance above $1,800/oz.
- NGM dispute escalates into protracted litigation that impairs Nevada cash flow or forces a writedown.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the next FOMC meeting (a key input to the gold/real-yield path). The Cadia Q2 production update and any NGM dispute developments are the operational checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Newmont has executed a genuine balance-sheet and capital-allocation turnaround, swinging to net cash and committing to disciplined per-share returns through the buyback and a sustainable dividend. The trailing financials are the best in company history. But the entire earnings surge rests on a gold price at multi-decade highs against flat-to-declining production, and the company's own $2,500/oz impairment assumption underscores how far current realized prices sit above through-cycle norms. The 14x trailing multiple is cheap only on peak-cycle earnings; on normalized gold it is fair, not asymmetric.

Layered on top are two specific unresolved situations — the Barrick/NGM dispute over Newmont's largest single asset, and Ghana's fiscal escalation — plus a near-term options term structure pricing imminent stress and no open-market insider buying (only sales into record prices). For an investor seeking gold exposure within a quality producer, NEM is a defensible core hold. For new capital seeking risk-adjusted upside, the entry does not clear the bar at current levels. We rate NEM a **HOLD** and would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$80s/low-$90s or a favorable NGM resolution.
