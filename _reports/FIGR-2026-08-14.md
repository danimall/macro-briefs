## Executive Summary

Figure Technology Solutions is a blockchain-native HELOC origination and distribution platform that has produced one of the more striking revenue trajectories in specialty finance: net revenue of $340.9M (FY2024) → $506.9M (FY2025) → $392.6M in the first half of 2026 alone, with 1H growth of 106% year over year and Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding from 29.9% in FY2024 to 54.6% in Q2-2026. Consumer Loan Marketplace volume reached $4.259B in Q2, up 132%, with Figure Connect — the third-party secondary marketplace — reaching 65% of that volume versus a target of roughly 70% in Q3. The operating story is real and is corroborated by hard-to-fake artifacts: 23 securitizations totaling roughly $7B with AAA ratings from both S&P and Moody's, DART registry adoption moving from 2% to 87% of loans on Figure's origination system in a single year, and 489 active origination partners at 6/30/26 versus 307 at year-end.

The reason we are not owners at $31.49 is the composition of the earnings that support the multiple. Three items materially inflate reported net income. First, the LTM effective tax rate is **negative 18.2%**: derived LTM pretax income of $200.8M produced reported LTM net income of $237.4M, implying a $36.6M net tax *benefit* from a post-Recombination deferred-tax-asset reassessment plus stock-option exercise windfalls. Normalized at a 25% blended rate, LTM net income is approximately $150.6M rather than $237.4M — roughly 36% of reported profit is discrete rather than recurring, and the option-windfall component is reflexive, shrinking if the share price falls. Second, Q2 gain on sale of loans ($57.6M) plus gain on servicing asset ($29.1M) equal $86.7M, or 38.4% of net revenue, recognized at the moment of sale on Level 3 inputs; Level 3 assets were $664.0M at 3/31/26, or 24.3% of total assets. Third, stock-based compensation ran $26.1M in Q2 (11.6% of revenue) with $221.0M unrecognized, all added back to Adjusted EBITDA.

On a tax-normalized basis the stock trades at roughly 38x current-year and 34x next-year earnings, and at 18.9x LTM Adjusted EBITDA on all-class capitalization of $7.13B — approximately fair for a business growing volume triple digits but conceding price as it scales. Against that fair-to-full valuation sits an unusually stacked risk ledger: 44.1% of total debt owed to a single related party (Signum Ltd., dba Hastra) redeemable hourly, whose ownership and affiliation we could not verify; four customers representing 81.6% of trade receivables; a TILA open-end recharacterization risk that by the company's own disclosure "would affect all HELOCs originated to date" across more than $30B of cumulative production; 74% of security instruments notarized by Nevada remote online notaries in a practice the 10-K concedes "has not been tested in court"; controlled-company and emerging-growth-company status that exempts Figure from majority-independent-board requirements and SOX 404(b) attestation; and forward-looking-statement language in the Q2 release referencing "previously identified material weaknesses." Insiders — CEO, CFO, Chief Capital Officer and both founders — sold into the $31–$44 range between April and July with no offsetting open-market purchases, including none at $27–$28 in late July when the stock was near its trailing low.

## Company Overview

Figure operates a two-sided lending marketplace. On the supply side, partner lenders (retail and wholesale mortgage banks, depositories, servicers, fintechs) originate HELOCs under their own brands on Figure's Loan Origination System, paying volume-based technology and ecosystem fees; Figure also originates directly under its own brand at a higher take rate. On the demand side, Figure Connect (launched June 2024) distributes those loans to whole-loan buyers and securitization investors. HELOCs were more than 98% of FY2025 originations, so despite the blockchain framing this is a mortgage-adjacent origination and distribution business with an on-chain settlement and registry layer.

The infrastructure is load-bearing rather than decorative. DART, the eNote and lien registry that replaces MERS-style intermediation, went from 2% to 87% of LOS loans in one year. Provenance, the public chain on which assets are recorded, carried roughly $14B of real-world-asset TVL at year-end 2025 with approximately 75% share of tokenized private credit (company-sourced to RWA.xyz; note the Provenance Blockchain Foundation is a related party, not a subsidiary). YLDS, an SEC-registered face-amount certificate marketed as a yield-bearing stablecoin alternative, pays holders SOFR minus 35bps with Figure retaining the 35bp spread. Democratized Prime, launched June 2025, is an on-chain lend/borrow venue where YLDS is supplied against HELOC, auto and SMB collateral pools.

The regulated perimeter is the least replicable asset: more than 180 lending and servicing licenses, 48 money-transmitter licenses, an SEC-registered broker-dealer operating an ATS, a registered transfer agent, a registered face-amount certificate company, a money-transmitter subsidiary, and a registered investment adviser.

Governance is concentrated. Class B carries 10 votes; 37.9M Class B shares deliver 378.9M votes against 185.7M Class A and Blockchain votes, or 67.1% of voting power held by co-founders Mike Cagney (chairman) and June Ou (director, spouse). Michael Tannenbaum is CEO; Macrina Kgil is CFO. Figure has elected controlled-company status.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** Net revenue: $340.9M FY2024 → $506.9M FY2025 (+48.7%) → $167.0M Q1-26 (+97.6%) → $225.6M Q2-26 (+112.7%). Operating income moved from $9.2M (FY2024) to $117.5M (FY2025) to $119.7M in 1H-2026 alone. Adjusted EBITDA margin: 29.9% → 48.8% → 52.4% in 1H-26, with Q2 at 54.6%. Operations and processing cost fell to roughly 67bps of marketplace volume in Q2 from 79bps a year earlier — operating leverage is demonstrable.

**The revenue-mix shift is the strategic center.** Ecosystem and technology fees went $28.3M (FY24) → $120.8M (FY25) → $72.9M in Q2-2026 alone, or 32.3% of quarterly revenue. Balance-sheet-linked revenue (origination fees, gain on sale, servicing, interest income) fell from 82% of net revenue in FY2024 to 71% in FY2025. The direction is toward higher-quality fee revenue.

**Take rate is compressing, and the mechanism is disclosed.** The company's own Net Take Rate fell from 4.0% to 3.6% year over year in Q2 (1H: 3.9% → 3.7%). Derived revenue-to-volume: $225.6M ÷ $4,259M = 5.30% in Q2-26 versus $106.1M ÷ $1,838M = 5.77% in Q2-25. Volume grew 132% while revenue grew 113%; the gap is the take rate. Two disclosed drivers are structural, not cyclical: ecosystem fees use "a sliding scale that decreases as higher volume tiers are reached," so success with large partners mechanically lowers realized pricing; and the weighted-average servicing fee rate fell 34bps (FY24) → 31bps (FY25) → 28bps at 3/31/26 versus 33bps a year earlier. Reaching the guided Q3 volume of $4.8–5.2B with a ~70% Connect mix should compress the take rate further.

**Earnings quality.** The tax item is the largest single adjustment. FY2025 recorded a $20.6M benefit (−18.1% rate) from reassessing deferred-tax-asset realizability; Q1-26 recorded $6.9M attributed to "excess windfall benefit due to stock exercises"; Q2-26 a further $4.4M. Net deferred tax assets rose from $26.0M to $56.8M in six months. Derived LTM: pretax $200.8M, reported net income $237.4M, implied net benefit $36.6M, effective rate −18.2%. This is correct accounting — a valuation-allowance release once realizability becomes more-likely-than-not is the required treatment, and option windfalls are real cash tax savings. Our point is narrowly about recurrence: roughly 36% of reported LTM profit will not repeat at a normalized rate, and the option-windfall portion is a function of the share price.

The second adjustment is gain-on-sale geometry. Of Q2's $29.1M servicing-asset gain, roughly $20.7M was "additions" — non-cash recognition of retained servicing value at the instant of sale — and $8.4M was valuation-assumption change, which management itself strips from Adjusted Net Revenue. Combined with $57.6M of gain on loan sales, 38.4% of Q2 revenue is fair-value recognition on Level 3 inputs (HELOC discount rate 6.3%, CPR 15.4%, loss severity 27.9%).

**Cash flow cannot be read conventionally, and should not be annualized.** FY2025 operating cash flow was +$62.6M against $134.3M of net income; Q1-2026 OCF was −$37.8M against $45.0M of net income. For a held-for-sale originator, originations (−$1.20B), loan purchases (−$1.03B) and sale proceeds (+$1.99B) all run through operating activities, so quarterly OCF is dominated by origination-and-sale timing. The 1H-2026 cash-flow statement was not available; no normalized free-cash-flow figure should be attributed. Capitalized internally developed software ($20.6M FY25, $6.4M Q1-26) is running ahead of amortization ($16.3M, $4.7M).

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: total assets $3,017.7M; cash and equivalents $1,437.5M; loans held for sale $597.4M; retained securitization interests $354.0M; servicing asset $155.0M; total liabilities $1,604.6M; equity attributable to Figure $1,413.1M. Two corrections are needed to read the cash line: $506.7M of "payables to third-party loan owners" is borrower cash collected as servicer and owed onward, and roughly $38.9M of "cash and cash equivalents" is USDC stablecoin. Unencumbered corporate liquidity is closer to **$930M** than $1.44B.

Total debt at 6/30/26 is $963.0M: $222.5M current third-party, $315.9M non-current, and **$424.6M current to related parties**. That related-party line grew $166.1M → $376.5M → $424.6M across three quarters and equals 26.5% of total liabilities and 44.1% of total debt. At 3/31/26 it comprised $73.6M of FCC certificates and $302.9M of Democratized Prime YLDS held by Signum Ltd. (dba Hastra), a counterparty that also pays Figure a 0.50% royalty under a December 2025 software license. Redemption terms make this funding demand-callable: Democratized Prime YLDS "can be redeemed each hour" and FCC certificates "may be surrendered at any time by the holder at face amount." Approximately $544.7M of debt is redeemable at the holder's option, roughly 78% of it related-party.

**Warehouse structure.** Committed capacity $1,905.3M at 3/31/26 with only $318.6M drawn (16.7% utilization) — deep headroom, and total capacity fell $200M only because the REIT warehouse left the perimeter on deconsolidation. The ladder is short: roughly $740M, or 39% of capacity, required renewal within about three months of the balance-sheet date, and the 10-Q confirms all maturing obligations were refinanced, extended or repaid by the filing date. Short tenors are industry-standard for originators; the exposure is that renewal terms reprice annually and tighten precisely when HELOC credit or crypto sentiment sours. Two small facilities carry punitive pricing (MSR Lender 1 at 16.5%, Digital Asset Loan Facility at 13.5% with a disclosed 26.8% weighted-average funding cost) — roughly $70M combined, informative rather than solvency-relevant. Covenants were in compliance at 12/31/25 and 3/31/26, but the FY2025 10-K states the company has "in the past" failed to comply with certain financial and other covenants, and covenants include an unusual "required range of net income or loss during specified periods."

**Off-balance-sheet.** Unconsolidated securitization VIEs held $6.77B of collateral UPB at 3/31/26 with disclosed maximum exposure to loss of $377.6M. Contingent loan-repurchase commitments run to $2.0B of aggregate UPB against a recorded obligation of $16.8M plus a $5.9M reserve; realized losses have been small ($47K in Q1-26). A securitization reserve-replenishment obligation is described in the 10-Q as "not limited."

**Credit.** The representative sample is clean: weighted-average 60+ day delinquency on the $6.77B of securitized collateral was 0.8%, flat quarter over quarter, on prime borrowers (FICO 750–754, average balances $98–101K). Loans held for sale show 30+ day delinquency of $32.2M on $492.7M (6.54%) versus $20.9M on $396.5M (5.27%), with non-accruals rising $2.4M → $14.0M. The most likely reading is composition — the held-for-sale bucket accumulates repurchased early-payment-default loans and unsellable legacy paper while performing loans exit in three to four weeks — but one quarter cannot distinguish that from the leading edge of vintage normalization.

## Growth Analysis

The volume engine is working. Consumer Loan Marketplace volume $4.259B in Q2 (+132%); Figure Connect volume $2.773B (+262%), 65% of marketplace volume; servicing portfolio UPB $8.1B → $12.9B → $14.56B across 12/24, 12/25 and 3/26; partners 307 → 489 in six months. Q3 guidance is $4.8–5.2B of marketplace volume, implying 13–22% sequential growth into a seasonally strong quarter.

The demand backdrop is structural rather than cyclical. Large tapped-able home equity coexists with a stock of low-rate first mortgages borrowers will not refinance, which channels equity extraction into second-lien HELOCs. Weighted-average customer rates fell from 9.6–9.8% (Q1-25) to 8.4–8.7% (Q1-26). This positions Figure differently from first-lien originators: the Q1-2026 MD&A notes "an increasing rate environment during the three months ended March 31, 2026," which cut unrealized gains on unsold loans by $8.3M — a reminder that gain-on-sale is rate-path-sensitive in both directions, even as the refinance-substitution logic favors HELOC volume.

**Kiavi is the largest strategic event.** Announced June 10, 2026 at $717M total consideration, structured with Figure acquiring the technology and operating platform while a Sixth Street–Figure joint venture acquires the balance-sheet assets. Kiavi is described as the leading U.S. residential transition lender, adding more than $7B of annual first-lien volume plus DSCR rental loans. Strategically this addresses three weaknesses simultaneously: it moves Figure from junior-lien into first-lien collateral, adds asset classes for Democratized Prime pools, and repeats a JV template Figure already used with Sixth Street in February 2025. The Q2 release says the deal "remains on track to close in the second half of 2026"; a spokesperson indicated August. **Figure's own cash outlay, the consideration mix, the JV ownership split and the financing are not disclosed in any source we reviewed** — a material gap against roughly $930M of unencumbered liquidity, and one reason the July 14, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01 and 2.03, material agreement plus creation of a direct financial obligation, contents unretrievable) matters.

Product expansion is early: SMB volume +57% sequentially; SMB pools added to Democratized Prime alongside auto and home equity; third-party borrowing on Democratized Prime reached roughly $170M at August 6, 2026, "a ~23x increase" since year-end. That last figure cuts both ways — 23x growth is the right direction, but it implies roughly $7M of third-party borrowing at 12/31/25 against $206M of reported "matched offers," meaning the platform's headline adoption metrics have been substantially seeded by the related-party YLDS supply rather than independent demand.

Digital assets are strategically useful but financially small: Q1-26 marketplace volume $818M (+285%), but digital assets held excluding collateral were only $45.1M, and FY2025 fair-value changes were a $12.4M drag added back to Adjusted EBITDA.

**Estimate revisions warrant a discount.** Consensus current-year EPS rose 14.4% over 90 days to $1.29 and next-year 8.3% to $1.44. Normally revisions of that direction and velocity are a constructive signal. Here they are contributed by **a single analyst**, and the quarterly surprise record is 2 beats and 2 misses with a −58.6% miss in Q4-2025 followed by a +49.4% beat in Q2-2026 — a dispersion consistent with fair-value and gain-on-sale accounting and inconsistent with high forecast visibility. We treat the revision trend as supportive but not decision-grade.

The headline ~25x forward multiple against 100%+ revenue growth reads cheap. Adjusted for a normalized tax rate it becomes roughly 38x current-year and 34x next-year — a full multiple that requires the volume-versus-take-rate race to keep resolving in Figure's favor. Adjusting Adjusted EBITDA for stock-based compensation (1H SBC $52.0M against 1H Adjusted EBITDA $202.0M) puts SBC-inclusive cash EBITDA nearer $300–320M annualized, or 22–24x — again, fair rather than cheap.

Our base case triangulates three approaches. At 16x forward Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $430M, equity value is $6.9B, or $27.9 per diluted-adjusted share. At 8x forward revenue of roughly $830M, $6.6B, or $26.7. At 40x tax-normalized 2026 EPS of roughly $0.76 (25% rate on approximately $250M of pretax income across 247M diluted shares), $30.4. **Base case $30**, 4.7% below the current price.

The bull case requires the take rate to stabilize near 3.6%, Kiavi to close on non-dilutive terms and accrete, and the tokenized-credit multiple to re-rate on digital-asset market-structure legislation. At 10x 2027 revenue of roughly $1.05B, equity is $10.5B, or **$47** per diluted share (+49%). The bear case is a spread-widening or credit-normalization event that hits gain-on-sale revenue (38% of Q2) and warehouse renewal terms simultaneously — the exposures are correlated, not independent — producing roughly 8x depressed Adjusted EBITDA or approximately 2.8x book: **$18** (−43%).

Probability-weighted at 45% base, 25% bull, 30% bear gives approximately **−3%** expected return with a very wide distribution. Analyst targets of $31 low and $70 high (mean $51.14, median $55) sit well above spot, but several of the constructive views predate both Kiavi and Q2, the coverage panel is only seven firms with one at strong sell, and at a fair-to-full normalized multiple analyst targets are not independent confirmation — they embed the same growth extrapolation as the price.

## Competitive Landscape

Figure does not compete on credit selection — borrowers are prime, FICO 750–754. It competes on cost-to-produce and speed-to-fund, and on being the distribution rail. Company-computed figures cite median 10 days to fund versus an industry ~43 days, and production cost of roughly $717 per loan versus an MBA industry average of $11,109. The scope comparison is arguably unlike-for-unlike (HELOC versus full first-lien production), so treat the magnitude as directional. The credit corroboration is stronger: losses on loan and ABS sales excluding repurchases and charge-offs have historically run approximately 1% of all loans originated since inception, and 23 securitizations totaling roughly $7B carry AAA ratings from both S&P and Moody's.

The defensible assets are (1) the regulated-entity stack — broker-dealer with ATS authority, transfer agent, face-amount certificate company, 48 money-transmitter licenses, 180+ lending and servicing licenses; (2) rating-agency and ABS-investor acceptance of blockchain-native collateral; and (3) the two-sided network, 489 origination partners against a diversifying buy side (top five purchasers fell to 51.6% of UPB sold from 73.6%). A competitor could replicate the software; replicating licenses plus rating-agency acceptance plus investor acceptance simultaneously is materially harder.

Where the moat looks thinner is pricing. If Figure had durable power over partners, tiered pricing would not be compressing realized fees as partners scale. The 10-K describes partners as having no long-term volume commitments, paying de minimis fixed fees, and free to leave at any time — a low-switching-cost relationship. Concentration is worsening on the supply side even as it improves on the buy side: top 10 partners were 56% of origination volume in FY2025 versus 52% in FY2024; top five loan originators were 77.8% of UPB purchased in Q1-2026 versus 73.9%; a single customer (Toledo Bend Investments) was 13% of Q1-2026 net revenue; and four customers accounted for 81.6% of trade receivables at 3/31/26.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-product, single-legal-theory concentration.** HELOCs were more than 98% of FY2025 originations, and Figure treats its HELOC as open-end credit under TILA. The 10-K warns that recharacterization as closed-end or variable-rate credit "would affect all HELOCs originated to date" — more than $30B of cumulative production. Low probability, extraordinarily wide blast radius.

**Documentation and perfection novelty.** 74% of security instruments were notarized by Nevada remote online notaries, and the 10-K concedes out-of-state remote online notarization "has not been tested in court." Figure uses automated valuation models instead of appraisals and lien data instead of title searches or title insurance. The $717-per-loan cost advantage and the legal-novelty exposure are the same fact from two sides.

**Demand-callable, related-party-concentrated funding.** Roughly $545M of hourly or on-demand redeemable certificate debt at 6/30/26, approximately 78% related-party. Withdrawal would require funding redemptions from roughly $930M of unencumbered cash while simultaneously replacing financing for the ~$360M of balance-sheet loans the CFO says support Democratized Prime. We could not verify Hastra/Signum's ownership or whether it is affiliated with the controlling party; we make no assertion of insider control, but the gap is material to reading 44% of the debt stack.

**Correlated spread exposure.** Loan-purchaser commitments generally run under six months. A widening of HELOC credit spreads would hit gain-on-sale revenue immediately and pressure warehouse renewal terms at the same time — a correlated, not independent, exposure, and the most plausible path to the bear case.

**Collateral position.** At 12/31/25 Figure did not hold the first lien on 83% of balance-sheet HELOCs; 9% of FY2025 originations went to non-occupant borrowers. As a non-depository lender Figure lacks DIDMCA and National Bank Act rate-exportation preemption, and subordinate-lien HELOCs do not receive Title V preemption. Geographic concentration: California 24.7%, Florida 11.9% of serviced UPB.

**Governance and controls.** 67.1% of voting power in Class B; controlled-company election removes majority-independent-board, independent-compensation-committee and independent-nominating-committee requirements; EGC status removes SOX 404(b) auditor attestation and pay-versus-performance disclosure (the first DEF 14A returned nulls on CEO compensation and TSR). The Q2 release lists among its risks "our ability to remediate previously identified material weaknesses"; we could not locate the originating disclosure or confirmation of remediation, though management concluded disclosure controls were effective at 3/31/26 with no ICFR changes.

**Dilution.** 42.4M options and RSUs against 219.4M shares at 3/31/26 — 19.3% of existing shares, or 16.2% post-issuance ownership dilution — plus an evergreen provision adding up to 5% of outstanding shares annually through 2035 and an ESPP up to 1%. Q2 diluted shares of 247.0M already exceed basic 221.5M by 11.5%.

**Structural items on the monitoring list rather than the red-flag list.** Figure holds 55% of VS Evergreen Financing Fund LP and does not consolidate it, having concluded it is not the primary beneficiary — technically supportable under VIE guidance if power over significant activities rests elsewhere, and the amounts are small ($9.75M equity-method, no gain recognized), but it moved an asset pool and a $200M warehouse out of the disclosed perimeter. The Altman Z-score of 2.34 reflects low asset turnover and EBIT/assets on a financial balance sheet rather than distress; equity/liabilities of 1.14x and a 2.2x current ratio are the solvency-relevant inputs and both are sound.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 137.6% is a one-day-expiry artifact and should be discounted; the informative readings are the 13-day tenor at 79.8% and the 41-day at 77.2%, against one-year realized volatility of 98.5%. Term structure is in backwardation from the front, with the 3-month tenor pricing a ±21.4% move — the market expects a large move around the Kiavi close and the Q3 print, without directional conviction. Positioning has become notably more call-tilted: put/call open interest fell from 0.79 in early June to 0.32, and skew is call-heavy across every tenor (−5.2% at 1w to −0.3% at 6m). Downside protection has been removed rather than accumulated. For a fundamental holder that is a caution, not a comfort — speculative call demand into a high-beta, low-float, dual-class name with a pending unquantified acquisition is exactly the configuration that amplifies drawdowns when a catalyst disappoints. Short interest at 7.5% of float and 4.1 days to cover is unremarkable and provides no squeeze support.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($47, +49%)

Volume growth continues to outrun take-rate compression: Q3 delivers $5.0B+ of marketplace volume with Connect at 70%, 2027 revenue reaches $1.0–1.1B, and Adjusted EBITDA margin holds above 50% as operations cost per dollar of volume keeps falling faster than fees. Kiavi closes on modest cash consideration with the Sixth Street JV absorbing the balance-sheet assets, adding $7B of annual first-lien volume, diversifying away from the 83%-junior-lien exposure and seeding Democratized Prime with a new asset class. Third-party Democratized Prime borrowing continues compounding from $170M, reducing related-party funding dependence and validating the platform independently. Digital-asset market-structure legislation passes, and the market re-rates the only SEC-registered, AAA-rated tokenized-credit platform toward 10x forward revenue. At that point the tax-normalization critique becomes moot because pretax earnings power has doubled.

### Bear Case ($18, −43%)

HELOC or ABS credit spreads widen. Gain-on-sale revenue — 38.4% of Q2 — compresses immediately while warehouse renewal terms tighten on the 39% of capacity that reprices annually, and Level 3 marks on $664M of assets move against the company. Take-rate compression continues from 3.6% while volume growth decelerates from 132%, producing negative operating leverage on revenue. The tax benefit reverses as the valuation allowance is exhausted and option windfalls shrink with the share price, moving the effective rate from −18% to +25% and cutting reported EPS by roughly a third with no change in operations. The related-party YLDS holder reduces exposure, forcing Figure to fund hourly redemptions and replace $360M of Democratized Prime funding at once. Any adverse development on TILA characterization or remote notarization enforceability becomes an existential rather than a cost item.

### Base Case ($30, −4.7%)

Volume grows into the guided range, revenue grows more slowly than volume as the take rate compresses toward 3.4–3.5%, and margins hold. Kiavi closes and integrates without incident but with modest 2026 accretion. Reported EPS growth flatters underlying pretax progress as the tax benefit persists into 2026 and normalizes in 2027. The stock trades in a wide range around fair value on tax-normalized earnings, with the multiple capped by earnings-quality discount, governance discount and the funding-structure opacity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Probability-weighted expected return of approximately −3% against a 43% bear case and a stacked left tail is less than fair on a risk-adjusted basis.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into current strength. The stock has recovered roughly 30% from the $24.11 August 3 low in eight sessions on the Q2 print, which provides a serviceable exit level. We would reduce to a residual position — no more than one-third of a full weight — ahead of the Kiavi close, whose consideration mix and financing remain undisclosed and whose funding may be the subject of the unretrievable July 14 8-K. Do not add on weakness before the disclosure gaps close.

## Conclusion

Figure is a genuinely differentiated business executing at an unusual pace. Revenue more than doubled year over year in the first half, Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 54.6%, and the on-chain infrastructure is demonstrably load-bearing — 87% DART adoption, AAA ratings on blockchain-native securitizations, 489 partners, 65% of volume flowing through a third-party marketplace that did not exist two years ago. The regulated-entity estate is the hardest asset in the story to replicate and is not something a competitor can build quickly.

What we cannot underwrite at $31.49 is the price paid for that execution once the earnings are put on a normalized footing. Roughly 36% of LTM reported net income is discrete tax benefit; 38.4% of Q2 revenue is inception-dated fair-value gain on Level 3 inputs; 11.6% of Q2 revenue is stock-based compensation added back to the headline margin; the disclosed take rate is compressing structurally as the business scales; 44% of debt is owed to a single related party redeemable hourly whose affiliation we could not establish; four customers hold 81.6% of trade receivables; and the controlling shareholders, who together with the CEO, CFO and Chief Capital Officer sold into the $31–$44 range, bought nothing at $27. On tax-normalized earnings the stock is at roughly 38x forward and 19x LTM Adjusted EBITDA — approximately fair for a high-growth marketplace, and not enough margin of safety against a left tail that includes correlated spread-and-warehouse exposure and a legal-characterization risk the company itself says would touch every loan it has ever originated.

REDUCE. Trim into the post-print recovery, retain a small residual for the Kiavi and legislation optionality, and require the 10-Q disclosures and two quarters of take-rate stability before re-engaging.
