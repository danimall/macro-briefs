**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Astrana Health (formerly Apollo Medical Holdings) is one of the largest independent risk-bearing value-based care platforms in California, coordinating care for roughly 1.55 million patients through 20,000-plus contracted physicians organized into 29 risk-bearing organizations. The July 2025 acquisition of select Prospect Medical assets transformed the company's scale: revenue rose 56% to ~$3.18B in FY2025, and Q1 2026 revenue reached $965.1M, up 56% year-over-year with $300.2M from Prospect. Adjusted EBITDA grew 82% to $66.3M in Q1, operating cash flow surged to $68.1M from $16.6M, and free cash flow hit $64.1M. The stock has appreciated about 50% over the trailing twelve months and trades near its 52-week high of $40.13.

The central analytical tension is that the headline growth is acquisition-fueled and the core risk-bearing margin is contracting. Care Partners segment operating income, which represents the economic engine at ~94% of revenue, declined 11% in Q1 ($39.5M vs $44.2M) as medical cost growth outpaced revenue under the transition to full institutional plus professional risk. The consolidated beat was carried by Care Enablement, the MSO/technology segment, where operating income rose 470% to $20.2M largely on Prospect's administrative contracts. The market is rewarding consolidated EBITDA, but the underlying total-cost-of-care margin is under pressure from the same elevated Medicare Advantage utilization cycle that hit larger MA insurers in 2024-2025.

We rate ASTH a HOLD. The forward valuation (~11.6x forward P/E against rising estimates and a +30.9% 90-day current-year revision) is genuinely attractive, cash generation is improving, and the Street is constructive (Baird $45/Outperform, Barclays upgraded to Overweight). But an unremediated material weakness in business-combination accounting at a serial acquirer with ~$1.13B of goodwill and intangibles (48% of assets), single-payer concentration at 20.9% of revenue and 39.2% of receivables, $1.04B of debt near covenant-relevant leverage, and a dense related-party web cap our conviction near the 52-week high. A defensible 12-month appreciation case exists if Prospect integration restores Care Partners margins, but the balance sheet and governance overhangs make a high-conviction buy unwarranted at the current price.

## Company Overview

Astrana is a physician-centric, technology-enabled, risk-bearing value-based care company headquartered in Alhambra, California. It contracts with health plans across Medicare Advantage, Medicaid (California Medi-Cal), Commercial, Exchange, and Medicare fee-for-service, taking professional and institutional risk on total cost of care. The model is vertically integrated across three layers:

- **Care Partners (~94% of revenue):** Builds and manages risk-bearing provider networks (IPAs, ACOs, Restricted Knox-Keene licensed plans). This is where the company takes capitated risk and is the primary economic driver.
- **Care Delivery:** Owns an acute-care hospital, primary and specialty clinics, urgent care, imaging, ambulatory surgery, labs, and a specialty pharmacy.
- **Care Enablement:** The proprietary MSO/technology platform (population health, claims, utilization management), sold internally and to third-party payers and providers. Highest-margin segment.

The competitive logic is that Astrana captures economics at the network, facility, and administrative layers simultaneously, so it can monetize even when pure risk margins compress. Q1 2026 demonstrated this: Care Enablement's profit surge offset Care Partners' margin decline. The model is increasingly branded as "AI-powered," though the substance remains the proprietary care-management platform and physician incentive design.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale-up via acquisition.** Revenue grew from ~$2.03B (2024) to ~$3.18B (2025), up 56%. The mechanism is the Prospect close (7/1/2025).

**Earnings quality is mixed.** FY2025 net income to Astrana fell to ~$22-24M from ~$43M in 2024 despite the revenue surge, compressed by higher medical utilization, integration costs, intangible amortization, and a near-doubling of interest expense. Q1 2026 showed recovery: net income to Astrana rebounded to $14.4M (vs $6.7M), and Adjusted diluted EPS reached $0.74 (vs $0.42). The beat reflects segment composition: Care Partners operating income declined 11% while Care Enablement operating income rose 470%. The consolidated story improved; the core risk-bearing economics did not.

**Cash flow is the genuine bright spot.** Operating cash flow of $68.1M and FCF of $64.1M (vs $13.6M) in Q1 materially exceed GAAP net income, supporting debt service. The cash story is stronger than the earnings story, which partly reflects working-capital timing but also the cash-generative nature of the MSO contracts.

**Balance sheet carries real leverage.** Total debt is $1,040.3M (Term Loans $918.3M plus a $122M revolver), up from ~$425M pre-acquisition, financed via a $745M delayed-draw term loan ($707.3M drawn). Covenants cap total net leverage at 5.0x (stepping to 4.5x after Q1 2027) with a 2.5x minimum interest coverage. The blended rate is ~5.67%, with a $200M notional swap fixing one tranche at 3.179%; a 1% rate move equals ±$8.4M of annual interest. Goodwill of $874.8M plus intangibles of $257M totals ~$1.13B, about 48% of $2.36B total assets, a high intangible load that carries impairment risk if value-based economics deteriorate. Cash is $478.4M, working capital $258.7M, and $138M of revolver availability remains. Most debt matures in 2030 ($777M), so there is no near-term refinancing cliff. Medical liabilities (IBNR) grew to $439.3M from $335.7M at year-end, and IBNR estimation is a critical accounting estimate that becomes more sensitive when margins are under pressure.

**Capital allocation.** The buyback authorization was doubled to $100M in February 2026 ($83.1M remaining), but actual Q1 repurchases were modest (~$2.9M). No common dividend. Capital is directed to debt service, integration, and selective buybacks.

**Earnings surprise track record warrants caution.** Over four quarters, ASTH posted one beat and three misses, including a -97.8% miss in Q3 2025 ($0.01 vs $0.45 estimate) and a -48.7% miss in Q2 2025. The most recent quarter (Q1 2026) was a narrow miss (-4.9%, $0.29 vs $0.30). The volatility in earnings delivery reduces confidence in point estimates, even though estimates are being revised up.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile splits into two distinct components that deserve separate treatment.

**Inorganic scale is real but financed.** The revenue growth is dominated by Prospect, a deal that more than doubled debt (from ~$425M to ~$1.04B). This is genuine market expansion in Southern California density, but it is not organic operating leverage.

**Estimate revisions are positive and meaningful.** Current-year EPS consensus has risen to $2.92 from $2.23 ninety days ago (+30.9%) and from $2.69 thirty days ago (+8.6%). Next-year consensus is $3.41, up 14.1% over 30 days. This combination of >30% 90-day current-year revision with >50% revenue growth has historically preceded continued estimate re-rating. We weight it as a positive, with two caveats specific to this name. First, only two analysts cover the estimates, so the revision sample is thin and idiosyncratic. Second, the revisions reflect the consolidation of Prospect's contribution rather than organic margin recovery; they are a function of the deal closing, not of the core risk business improving.

**The durability question is Care Partners margin.** The bull case requires Prospect integration synergies and a normalizing MA utilization cycle to restore Care Partners operating margin. The favorable 4/6/2026 CMS final 2027 MA rate notice (which retained the 2024 risk model) is a sector tailwind management expects to benefit from. The bear case is that elevated MA medical utilization is structural for the next several quarters and the core margin keeps eroding while interest expense and amortization weigh on net income.

We score Growth Prospects 6.5: strong top-line and rising estimates, tempered by the fact that growth is acquisition-driven and the underlying risk margin is contracting.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~64.8x is meaningless here because trailing earnings reflect the integration-depressed trough. Forward P/E of ~11.6x on consensus is the relevant metric, and on a rising estimate base ($2.92 current year, $3.41 next year) it implies a PEG well below 1. On its face, this is inexpensive for a company growing revenue 56% with improving cash flow.

The peer set provided is unhelpful (BridgeBio is a development-stage pharma with negative earnings, not a comparable). Against value-based care peers (Agilon, P3 Health), the relevant context is that the entire subsector de-rated through the 2024-2025 MA utilization cycle, and Astrana has executed better than most, which is why it trades at a premium to the most distressed names while still appearing cheap on forward earnings.

The discount to consensus targets is wide: the stock at $39.51 sits below the analyst low of $41, mean of $46, and high of $50, implying roughly 16% upside to mean. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, analyst targets typically lag and revise upward. The tempering factor is that the forward P/E embeds the assumption that Care Partners margins stabilize; if they do not, the "cheap" forward multiple is being applied to estimates that themselves drift lower despite the recent upward revisions.

We score Valuation 6.0. The forward multiple and target discount are genuinely supportive, but we apply a discount for the earnings-quality split (consolidated beat carried by one segment), the leverage that magnifies any margin disappointment, and the thin analyst coverage behind the estimates. This is a reasonably-priced inflection story with real overhangs, not a clear mispricing that warrants a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Astrana competes against Agilon Health, P3 Health Partners, Optum-affiliated groups, and other California IPAs/MSOs. Its differentiator is multi-layer vertical integration plus the proprietary tech/MSO platform, which lets it earn at the administrative layer when pure risk margins compress. Care Enablement's Q1 profit surge is the proof point of that diversification.

On trajectory, the picture is mixed. The company is gaining scale and patient count (1.0M to 1.55M patients year-over-year) and deepening California density. The dual analyst upgrades suggest the Street views Astrana as navigating the MA utilization cycle better than peers. Against that, the core risk-bearing margin is contracting, which is the layer of the business with the most defensible economics if it works. The competitive moat (owns the rails, proprietary platform, physician incentive design) remains intact, but the demonstrated value of that moat in the current cycle has been to redistribute profit toward the MSO layer rather than protect total risk margin.

We score Competitive Position 6.5: improving scale and a defensible integrated model, capped by the margin pressure in the core segment.

## Risk Assessment

This is the dimension where the bull case is most constrained.

1. **Unremediated material weakness in M&A accounting.** Disclosed in the FY2025 10-K and still not remediated as of Q1 2026, specifically related to accounting for business combinations. For a company whose entire strategy is serial acquisition, with ~$1.13B of goodwill and intangibles and Prospect purchase accounting still preliminary (a +$9.5M measurement-period goodwill adjustment in Q1 alone), this is the highest-priority red flag. It intersects directly with the riskiest part of the balance sheet.

2. **Core margin compression.** Care Partners operating income is declining as members move to full risk. If the MA utilization cycle persists, the headline EBITDA growth could prove transitory.

3. **Customer concentration.** Payer A is 20.9% of revenue and 39.2% of net receivables; historically the top five payers exceeded 70% of revenue. Loss or repricing by a single plan would be highly material.

4. **Leverage and rate exposure.** $1.04B of debt near covenant-relevant leverage (5.0x cap, stepping to 4.5x post-Q1 2027), with floating-rate exposure beyond the hedged $200M.

5. **Prospect seller distress.** Prospect Medical Holdings has documented financial distress; the deal was an asset/equity purchase ($674.9M) with limited recourse and still-preliminary accounting.

6. **Complex VIE / corporate-practice-of-medicine structure.** Care is delivered through nominee-physician-owned professional corporations consolidated as VIEs; ~$1.32B of assets sit in VIEs usable only to settle VIE obligations, and APC carries a mezzanine deficit of $(237.7)M.

7. **Related-party density.** Material transactions with AHMC (a director is an officer there; $41.4M net Q1 expense), Allied Pacific Holdings real estate (a board member is the lessor's CEO), and Third Way Health (an officer is a board member), layered on a founder/family executive chairman structure.

8. **Regulatory.** Heavy CMS (Medicare/MA risk adjustment) and Medi-Cal dependence, with potential Medicaid funding cuts flagged in MD&A. Partly offset by the favorable 2027 MA rate notice.

We score Risk Factors 4.5: several specific, not-fully-priced idiosyncratic risks (the control weakness above all), partly offset by the absence of a near-term debt maturity cliff and the favorable CMS rate backdrop.

This is moderate: high enough to indicate a real bearish constituency (likely focused on the leverage, the control weakness, and the core-margin pressure), but not at squeeze-risk levels (>20%). ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Prospect integration synergies materialize, Care Partners margins recover as the MA utilization cycle normalizes and the favorable 2027 CMS rate model flows through, and the MSO/Care Enablement layer continues to scale at high margin. Estimates keep rising, cash flow funds deleveraging, and the stock re-rates toward the $50 analyst high as the forward multiple expands toward peers. Cash generation demonstrates the model produces real cash even at a depressed GAAP margin. Bull target ~$50 (+27%).

**Bear Case.** Elevated MA utilization proves structural, Care Partners margin keeps eroding, and the consolidated beat's reliance on one segment is exposed. The unremediated material weakness produces a restatement or impairment on the goodwill/intangible base. Leverage near covenants limits financial flexibility, and a repricing by Payer A hits hard. The stock de-rates back toward the low-$20s where it traded in early 2026. Bear target ~$24 (-39%).

**Base Case.** Integration proceeds, cash flow supports debt service, Care Partners margin stabilizes without dramatic recovery, and estimates hold near current levels. The stock works toward the analyst low-to-mean range as the Street's bullish case partially plays out. Base target ~$45 (+14%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The forward valuation, rising estimates, and improving cash flow support a constructive 12-month view, but the stock sits near its 52-week high with material balance-sheet and governance overhangs that prevent a high-conviction buy. Hold existing positions for the integration thesis while requiring evidence of core-margin recovery and control remediation before adding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), which will show a full year of Prospect under one roof and reveal whether Care Partners margin is stabilizing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Care Partners segment operating income returns to year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the core risk margin is recovering rather than the beat resting on the MSO layer.
- Remediation of the material weakness in business-combination accounting, disclosed in a 10-Q or 10-K.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that restores a wider margin of safety to the $45-46 analyst mean.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment or restatement tied to the control weakness.
- Care Partners operating income decline accelerating beyond the Q1 -11% pace, or a covenant-relevant leverage breach approaching the 5.0x cap.
- Loss or material repricing by Payer A, or a guidance cut on the integration synergies.

## Conclusion

Astrana has executed a transformational, debt-funded scale-up and now generates real cash, which the market is rewarding with a ~50% trailing-twelve-month gain and a forward multiple that looks inexpensive against rising estimates. The Street is constructive, and the estimate revisions are genuine. But the quality of the beat is the crux: consolidated EBITDA grew 82% while the core risk-bearing segment's operating income fell 11%, and the company carries $1.04B of debt, ~$1.13B of goodwill and intangibles, single-payer concentration at 20.9% of revenue, and an unremediated material weakness in M&A accounting, the area its acquisition-led strategy depends on. Those overhangs, near a 52-week high, justify a growth-oriented HOLD rather than a buy. We would become more constructive on confirmed Care Partners margin recovery, remediation of the control weakness, or a pullback that widens the margin of safety.
