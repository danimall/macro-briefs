**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hormel Foods is a Dividend King (60 consecutive years of increases, 391 consecutive quarterly payments since 1928) that has spent the past three fiscal years destroying relative shareholder value while its core packaged-meats economics deteriorated under commodity inflation, turkey-supply disruption, and a botched Planters integration. FY2025 was a trough: GAAP EPS of $0.87 was cut roughly in half by $234M of non-cash impairments (Garudafood $164M, Planters/Chi-Chi's trade names $71M), operating income fell 33%, and the FY2026 dividend raise was a token 1%, the smallest in living memory and a signal that the streak is now being defended rather than comfortably funded. The stock sits at $25.11, down ~13% since the prior review and ~16% below its 52-week high of $29.78.

The counterweight is a real recovery. Q2 FY2026 (ended April 26) showed all three segments growing both sales and profit, gross margin recovering to 17.4% from 16.7%, adjusted EPS up 14% to $0.40, and operating cash flow up 44% to $528M for the half. Management has moved decisively to prune the portfolio, with three divestitures in roughly seven months (Justin's 51% stake, the whole-bird turkey business, and the Brazil/Ceratti operations) plus a corporate restructuring, shedding low-margin, impairment-prone, and drag assets in favor of branded, value-added Foodservice and Retail. FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided to $1.43–$1.51, which restores dividend coverage on an adjusted basis.

The recovery is running through a leadership vacuum. Both the CEO (interim Jeffrey Ettinger, a recalled former chief) and CFO (interim Paul Kuehneman) are unconfirmed as of the Q2 filing, the prior CFO left under a separation agreement, and the prior CEO is on a paid consulting contract through April 2027. President John Ghingo is the likely successor but is not formally named permanent CEO in the filings. Layered on top are an October 8, 2026 turkey antitrust trial, $683M of intangibles flagged at heightened impairment risk, and a dividend now covered only on adjusted earnings with no buyback cushion. We rate HRL a HOLD on income grounds: the ~4.6% yield and margin recovery justify holding for the payout, but there is no clean appreciation catalyst until leadership and margin durability are confirmed.

## Company Overview

Hormel is a ~$12.1B-revenue global branded food manufacturer based in Austin, Minnesota, with a late-October fiscal year. It operates three reportable segments (FY2025 net sales):

- **Retail — $7.46B (62%):** grocery, mass, club, and e-commerce. Brands include SPAM, Jennie-O turkey, Applegate, Planters, Skippy, Hormel Black Label bacon, Herdez/Mexican foods (via the 50%-owned MegaMex JV), Chi-Chi's, and Hormel Gatherings.
- **Foodservice — $3.94B (33%):** restaurants, healthcare, K-12, and hospitality through a direct-selling force. Brands include Austin Blues, Fontanini, and branded pepperoni/bacon, plus a fast-growing customized-solutions business.
- **International — $709M (6%):** SPAM exports, China (Jiaxing ambient meat-snack facility), and equity investments in the Philippines (Purefoods-Hormel), Indonesia (Garudafood), and Japan (Okinawa Hormel).

The moat rests on category-leading, shelf-stable branded staples (SPAM is effectively a niche monopoly) and a high-touch Foodservice arm that competes on solutions and service rather than price. Walmart is ~16% of gross sales; the top five customers are ~38%. The Hormel Foundation controls a large voting stake, which stabilizes governance while entrenching insiders.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat; the story is margin recovery, not top-line growth.**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.46B | $12.11B | $11.92B | $12.11B (+1.6%) |
| Operating income | $1.31B | $1.07B | $1.07B | $0.72B (−33%) |
| Net income | $1.00B | $0.79B | $0.81B | $0.48B (−41%) |
| GAAP diluted EPS | ~$1.82 | $1.45 | $1.47 | $0.87 |
| Adjusted EPS | — | — | $1.58 | $1.37 (−13%) |
| Gross margin | 17.4% | 16.5% | 17.0% | 15.6% |

FY2025 GAAP results were distorted by the Q4 impairments (Planters $59M, Chi-Chi's $3M within the $71M trade-name figure). Underlying operations were squeezed by pork-belly, beef, and nut inflation that the Transform & Modernize (T&M) cost program only partially offset. Adjusted EPS of $1.37 is the cleaner read on earnings power.

Q2 FY2026 confirms the recovery is operational rather than cosmetic. Net sales rose 2.5% (3.3% organic). Total segment profit rose 12.6%, with Foodservice +10.8%, Retail +13.5%, and International +20.3%. The six-month operating cash flow was aided by lapping a prior-year inventory build. GAAP EPS fell 12% to $0.29 solely because of a $61M loss on the whole-bird turkey divestiture, a portfolio action rather than operational deterioration.

The balance sheet is investment-grade despite a misleading Altman Z-score of 1.26. That reading is a data artifact: the equity terms register as zero, but Hormel carries $7.96B of equity against $13.34B of assets (~60% equity-to-assets), a current ratio near 1.9–2.5, $827M cash, and an undrawn $750M revolver. Long-term debt of $2.85B is entirely fixed-rate senior notes with interest coverage near 9x EBIT. This is not a leverage or solvency story. One item to monitor: $500M of 4.8% notes mature March 2027 and now sit in current maturities.

Capital allocation is where the strain shows. FY2025 dividends of $633M (a record) were paid against GAAP EPS of $0.87 versus the ~$1.16 declared per share, a payout above 100% on GAAP and ~85% on adjusted. FY2025 free cash flow was roughly $534M (a 4.4% margin). There were no buybacks in FY2025 or 1H FY2026 despite an authorization, confirming that cash is being conserved to protect the streak.

## Growth Analysis

Hormel is a low-single-digit organic grower with the recovery concentrated in margin and mix rather than volume. FY2026 guidance calls for organic sales of +1–4% with segment-profit growth across all three units.

The durable growth engine is Foodservice, which has compounded mid-single-digit organic sales for several years (FY2025 +5.5% organic, 1H FY2026 +7%) on customized solutions, pepperoni, and premium proteins, and enjoys structurally better pricing power than Retail. Retail is the battleground: FY2025 volumes fell 1.4% on consumer trade-down to private label and Hormel's own exit from low-margin private-label snack nuts. The retail thesis leans on Jennie-O ground turkey, Applegate, Black Label bacon, and the Herdez/MegaMex Mexican-foods portfolio.

Estimate revisions are flat, which caps the growth score. Current-quarter consensus has been trimmed 5% over 90 days to $0.35; full-year FY2026 is stable-to-slightly-up (+1.6% over 90 days to $1.49); next year is essentially unchanged at $1.56. There is no positive revision velocity of the kind that would signal a systematic underestimation of the recovery. The earnings-surprise record is supportive but not decisive: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the most recent two quarters beating by 5–11% and the September 2025 quarter missing by 15%. The protein-demand tailwind that management cites is real for SPAM and Jennie-O, but the GLP-1 headwind on overall consumption is a genuine offsetting secular risk the company acknowledges.

## Valuation Assessment

At $25.11, Hormel trades at ~29.6x trailing GAAP EPS of $0.87, which is meaningless given the trough was distorted by impairments. On the cleaner adjusted base, the stock trades at ~17x the FY2026 adjusted midpoint of $1.47 and ~16x the FY2026 next-year consensus of $1.56. The dividend yield is ~4.6%.

Peer comparison is genuinely difficult here because the packaged-foods complex is heterogeneous and several peers carry their own distortions:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| SJM | −89.9 (impaired) | 3.8% |
| MKC | 8.1 | 3.9% |
| TSN | 44.0 | 3.6% |
| GIS | 8.3 | 7.2% |
| PPC | 8.0 | — |
| **Peer median** | **8.3** | **3.8%** |

On trailing multiples HRL screens expensive against a peer median of 8.3x, but that comparison is contaminated by trough GAAP earnings on Hormel's side and by a mix of impairment-distorted (SJM) and commodity-peak (TSN, PPC) prints on the peer side. On the more defensible forward-adjusted basis of ~16–17x, Hormel trades at a premium to the low-multiple staples (MKC, GIS) that is not clearly earned given its inferior five-year TSR (55.7 versus a 103.3 peer benchmark).

Two disciplines apply. First, a low headline multiple is not a buy signal on its own: the cheap-looking peers screen cheap for structural reasons, and cheapness in packaged foods has repeatedly proven to be a value trap rather than a mispricing. Second, this recovery does not deserve forward-earnings generosity, because revisions are flat rather than rising +30% and revenue growth is 2.5% rather than 40%. The cycle-adjusted framework that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple does not clearly apply because the recovery is modest and already reflected in the ~16–17x forward multiple.

The yield provides a floor: at ~4.6% with an adjusted payout near 80–85% and covered by FY2026 guidance, downside is bounded so long as margins hold. But there is no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion until leadership is resolved and margin durability is confirmed. Analyst targets ($25 low, $27 median, $30 high) bracket a modest 7% median upside; on a slow-growth staple with flat estimates, these targets are not the floor they would be on an accelerating recovery name, and they reflect the same wait-and-see posture we hold. We assign a below-neutral valuation score: fairly-to-slightly-richly priced on forward earnings, floored by yield, with no asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Hormel's competitive position is mixed and, on balance, has weakened over the trajectory of recent reviews. The Foodservice arm is a structural winner, differentiated on solutions and service, growing organic sales mid-single digits, with better pricing power than Retail. SPAM's niche dominance and the Applegate/Herdez brands are defensible.

Retail is deteriorating: volumes are falling, private-label trade-down is pressuring share, and Planters has been a persistent drag (integration issues, a FY2024 Listeria recall, a FY2025 Little Rock facility fire, and a $59M trade-name impairment). The five-year cumulative TSR of 55.7 versus a peer 103.3 is a stark, company-specific signal: peers materially outperformed, which points to Hormel-specific missteps (turkey/HPAI exposure, Planters, commodity sensitivity, and portfolio errors) rather than a purely sector-wide malaise.

The portfolio cleanup is a credible step toward re-focusing on higher-return branded businesses, and the retained Jennie-O brand plus the exit from commodity whole-bird turkey improves the Retail mix. But the trajectory over the past several reviews has been down (competitive position dropped from 6.0 to 4.5), and nothing in the current data reverses that; it merely arrests further decline. Customer concentration (Walmart ~16%) remains a structural vulnerability against a retailer with growing private-label ambition.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment risk is elevated and explicitly flagged.** After the FY2025 write-downs, management identified $683M of indefinite-lived intangibles at heightened risk, including the Justin's trade name and the International reporting-unit goodwill ($259M with a sub-20% cushion). The Garudafood equity investment ($247M carrying value versus ~$224M market value) was re-tested in Q2 and not impaired further, but another other-than-temporary-impairment charge is possible. This is the single largest near-term earnings-quality risk.

**Commodity input volatility** with limited pass-through speed drives quarter-to-quarter earnings sensitivity to pork, beef, turkey feed, and nut prices. **Avian influenza** was detected in Hormel's turkey supply chain in FY2024 and FY2025, directly relevant to Jennie-O. **Food-safety/recall exposure** is recurring (Q4 FY2025 Class 1 chicken recall, FY2024 Planters Listeria recall, Little Rock fire). **Turkey antitrust litigation** has a first trial set for October 8, 2026 with no liability accrued, a genuine binary event within the horizon. Customer concentration and the GLP-1 consumption headwind round out the specific risks.

The dividend is the crux. The 60-year streak is defensible on FY2026 adjusted guidance, but coverage is thin, the raise was a token 1%, and there is no buyback cushion. A margin relapse or a fresh impairment could force a harder choice than management wants to make.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% is fair against 1-year historical vol of 30.5% (spread −1.0%), and the IV percentile within the current chain is a middling 34%. The put/call skew of 3.9% shows modestly elevated put demand, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 29.5% versus long-term 25.5%), which points to some near-term event pricing consistent with an upcoming earnings print and the October antitrust trial. Short interest is moderate at 6.8% of float and 4.3 days to cover, not a crowded position. The 6-month tenor shows a −3.6% put skew and a ±14.4% implied move, reflecting genuine uncertainty around the October trial and the possibility of further impairment. Options positioning neither strongly confirms nor refutes the income-hold thesis; it flags manageable near-term event risk rather than a directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($30–32).** The margin recovery proves durable: FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $1.51 high end, Foodservice keeps compounding mid-single digits, the portfolio cleanup lifts blended returns, a permanent CEO/CFO is named (Ghingo) and stabilizes governance, the turkey antitrust trial resolves without material liability, and no further impairments hit. The stock re-rates toward 20x forward adjusted EPS as the market gains confidence the trough is behind it, with the ~4.6% yield providing a floor along the way.

**Bear case ($19–20, roughly −20% to −24%).** A commodity relapse or a fresh Garudafood/International/Justin's impairment pressures adjusted earnings back below the FY2026 range, dividend coverage tightens further, the antitrust trial produces an adverse October verdict, and the leadership vacuum persists into 2027. The stock retests its 52-week low near $19.70 as multiple compression meets a dividend the market begins to question.

**Base case ($25–27).** Margins hold at the recovered level, FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near the $1.47 midpoint, the dividend is defended, and leadership is resolved incrementally. The stock trades roughly flat to modestly higher, delivering total return primarily through the ~4.6% yield rather than capital appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 6–12 month income hold. The reassessment points are the Q3 FY2026 earnings print (late summer), the October 8, 2026 turkey antitrust trial, and any permanent CEO/CFO announcement.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A permanent CEO (likely Ghingo) and CFO are confirmed, removing the governance overhang, AND FY2026 adjusted EPS delivers at or above $1.47 with gross margin holding ≥17%.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity emerges (full-year consensus rising >3% over 90 days) alongside sustained all-segment profit growth, indicating the market is underpricing the recovery.
- The turkey antitrust matter resolves without material liability and no new impairment is taken on the $683M at-risk intangibles.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or a suspension of the increase streak, or FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance withdrawn/cut below $1.40.
- A material new impairment (Garudafood, International goodwill, or Justin's) or an adverse antitrust verdict with a quantified liability.
- Gross margin relapses below 16% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the recovery has stalled.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 print; the October 8 trial is the key binary event within the horizon.

## Conclusion

Hormel presents a genuine but capped situation. The operating trough of FY2025 was overstated by one-time impairments, the Q2 FY2026 recovery is real, and the portfolio cleanup is a credible strategic pivot. The ~4.6% yield, floored by an adjusted payout the company can currently cover, provides downside support and justifies holding for income.

The appreciation case does not clear the bar. Revisions are flat, revenue growth is low-single-digit, the forward multiple already reflects the recovery at ~16–17x, and the five-year relative TSR confirms company-specific underperformance rather than a temporary sector dislocation. The dual-interim C-suite is an unresolved governance red flag, the dividend is stretched with no buyback cushion, the at-risk intangibles remain a live threat, and the October antitrust trial is a live binary. The trajectory across recent reviews (competitive position 6.0→4.5, management 5.0→4.0) reflects these deteriorating qualitative signals, and current data arrests rather than reverses them.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Hold for the Dividend King yield and the confirmed margin recovery; do not underwrite appreciation until leadership is resolved and margin durability is proven across another quarter or two.
