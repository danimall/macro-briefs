## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the world's largest private-sector power producer following its $22B acquisition of Calpine Corporation in January 2026, operating approximately 55 GW of generation capacity dominated by a 22 GW nuclear fleet — the largest in the United States. The company sits at the intersection of two structural trends: AI-driven electricity demand growth and decarbonization mandates requiring 24/7 clean power. PJM capacity prices rose 246-445% in 2025, and these elevated prices are consistent with tighter supply-demand conditions that provide revenue visibility into future delivery years, though future prices remain subject to PJM market design, FERC approval, demand growth, and new supply.

At $303.63 (as of early May 2026), the stock trades 27% below its October 2025 52-week high of $412.70. This decline reflects three identifiable concerns: Calpine integration execution, Crane (Three Mile Island) restart transmission delays, and FERC co-location regulatory uncertainty. The fundamental earnings trajectory remains intact — FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus of $11.62 (within company guidance of $11.00–$12.00) implies the stock trades at ~26x forward earnings on a growth rate of 15%+ (consensus FY2027 EPS of $13.59 represents 17% growth). For a company with irreplaceable assets, 20-year hyperscaler PPAs, and elevated capacity price visibility, this valuation is reasonable and the risk/reward favors accumulation.

Investors are applying a mature utility multiple framework to a company experiencing structural demand growth more comparable to infrastructure capex beneficiaries. The 246-445% capacity price increases reflect tightening supply-demand conditions from generation retirements meeting data center load growth, though their persistence remains subject to PJM market reforms and FERC proceedings. Post-Calpine, approximately 50% of the combined company's MWh production is in PJM, making Constellation a primary beneficiary.

## Company Overview

Constellation Energy operates the nation's largest nuclear fleet (14 stations, 25 units, 22 GW) alongside approximately 27 GW of natural gas capacity (post-Calpine), 730 MW of geothermal, and 2.6 GW of other renewables. The company serves approximately 2.5 million customer accounts including more than 80% of Fortune 100 companies through its competitive retail platform — the largest in the U.S.

Standalone Constellation generated $25.5B in revenue in 2025, with adjusted operating earnings of $9.39 per share (+8% YoY). On a pro forma combined basis including Calpine, 2025 revenue was approximately $36.8B. The nuclear fleet operates at a 94.7% capacity factor, consistently 4 points above industry average, with 22-day average refueling outages versus 33-38 days industry-wide.

Key contracted revenue streams include: a 20-year Microsoft PPA for the Crane (TMI) restart, a 20-year Meta PPA for Clinton, New York ZEC extensions through 2049, and forward capacity commitments at substantially elevated prices.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** FY2025 standalone revenue of $25.5B with adjusted operating EPS of $9.39 (+8% YoY). GAAP earnings declined from 2024's $3.7B to $2.3B, but this reflects a mechanical reduction in Nuclear Production Tax Credits ($2.08B → $320M) as market prices rose above phase-out thresholds. The adjusted metric better captures economic reality: higher market prices more than offset PTC reductions.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $4.2B in FY2025 (vs. negative $2.5B in 2024, which was distorted by AR facility accounting). CapEx of $2.9B generated approximately $1.3B in free cash flow. The 2026-2027 capex run rate will be elevated ($5.7B and $4.7B respectively) due to Crane restart, nuclear fuel buildup, and co-location infrastructure investment.

**Balance Sheet:** Post-Calpine pro forma total debt of approximately $21.5B (including project finance), with pro forma cash of approximately $1.0B (reflecting the $4.5B cash consideration paid for Calpine) and a $7.0B revolving credit facility. Credit ratings affirmed at BBB+/Baa1 post-acquisition — investment grade with no immediate refinancing cliff. Current ratio 1.53x.

**Leverage Context:** Pro forma debt-to-equity of approximately 0.67x (using $21.5B debt against $32.3B pro forma equity) is appropriate for this asset base. Nuclear plants and gas CCGTs are infrastructure assets with 30-60 year operational lives and contracted/quasi-contracted cash flows. Credit agencies confirmed this view by maintaining investment-grade ratings through the Calpine transaction.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Third-party consensus estimates project FY2026 EPS of $11.62 (within company guidance of $11.00–$12.00, representing ~24% growth from $9.39 adjusted) and FY2027 EPS of $13.59 (17% growth). Growth drivers: full-year Calpine contribution, capacity price escalation flowing through, power price increases in competitive markets, and targeted 10% annual dividend growth.

## Growth Analysis

**Structural Demand Growth:** PJM capacity prices rose 246-445% in 2025 versus 2024 reference prices. This quantifies a fundamental supply-demand imbalance: generation retirements are accelerating while data center load in the PJM footprint is growing at double-digit rates. Because capacity auctions clear forward, Constellation has multi-year revenue visibility at elevated levels, though future delivery years remain subject to administrative price caps, FERC proceedings, and evolving market design.

**Data Center/AI Demand:** Constellation has secured two 20-year hyperscaler PPAs (Microsoft for Crane, Meta for Clinton) validating the nuclear-for-AI demand thesis. The company's 22 GW nuclear fleet provides 24/7 carbon-free power — the only generation technology that satisfies both the reliability and sustainability requirements of major cloud providers at this scale. While competitors such as Vistra and Talen have also signed large nuclear PPAs with hyperscalers, Constellation's fleet scale remains unmatched.

**Calpine Integration:** The acquisition adds approximately 23 GW of predominantly gas-fired capacity, creating a combined platform that can serve the full spectrum of load needs: nuclear for baseload, gas for peaking/ramping. Management has stated the acquisition is expected to be accretive and add free cash flow, though the pro forma financials explicitly do not reflect expected cost savings or synergies. Integration will take 12-18 months to fully realize.

**Crane Restart:** 835 MW nuclear restart with a 20-year Microsoft PPA. DOE has finalized a $1B loan guarantee. Originally targeted for 2028, later materials indicate potential restart as early as 2027, subject to regulatory and transmission/interconnection approvals. Transmission line delays create execution uncertainty.

**Nuclear Uprates:** 30 MW Clinton uprate by 2029, plus potential uprates across the existing fleet. Each additional MW of nuclear capacity at current power prices generates approximately $0.5-1.0M in annual revenue.

**Growth Rate:** 15-17% EPS growth from FY2025 adjusted base through FY2027 on consensus estimates. This is driven by identifiable sources (capacity prices, Calpine contribution, power price escalation) rather than speculative assumptions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $303.63 (as of early May 2026) with consensus FY2026 EPS of $11.62, CEG trades at approximately 26.1x forward earnings. Against FY2027 consensus of $13.59, the forward P/E compresses to 22.3x.

**Peer Comparison:** Traditional utility peers trade at 19-24x P/E (SO 23.6x, DUK 19.2x, AEP 19.5x, ETR 28.6x). CEG commands a premium because:
1. Growth rate of 15-17% annualized vs. 4-6% for regulated utilities
2. Unique exposure to AI/data center demand
3. Irreplaceable nuclear assets with insurmountable barriers to entry
4. Competitive/merchant model captures upside from power price increases (regulated utilities do not)

Among merchant power peers, Vistra (VST) trades at approximately 16x forward P/E per Yahoo Finance data, reflecting its different asset mix and growth profile. CEG's premium to VST on a forward basis reflects its larger nuclear fleet, contracted hyperscaler revenue, and higher near-term EPS growth rate.

**Valuation Math:** 28x FY2027 EPS of $13.59 = $380 (base case). This is a reasonable multiple for 15%+ growth with structural visibility. At 32x FY2027 (bull case with Crane on-time + additional hyperscaler PPAs), price target = $435. Bear case of 22x FY2026 EPS = $256.

**Why the market is wrong:** The stock has corrected 27% from highs on three concerns (Calpine integration, Crane delays, FERC co-location ruling) while the structural demand thesis has only strengthened — capacity prices are elevated, power demand growth continues to accelerate, and the combined platform provides greater diversification. The market is pricing execution risk at an implied discount that overstates the probability of permanent impairment.

## Competitive Landscape

Constellation's competitive position is among the strongest in the U.S. power sector:

**Barriers to Entry:** Nuclear generation has effectively insurmountable barriers. No new merchant nuclear plant has been built in the U.S. in decades. The combination of regulatory, capital ($15B+ per new unit), and expertise requirements means Constellation's 22 GW fleet is irreplaceable. Post-Calpine, the addition of ~27 GW of gas generation creates the only private-sector platform capable of serving both baseload and peaking needs at massive scale.

**Hyperscaler Relationships:** Microsoft and Meta PPAs demonstrate that Constellation is a leading counterparty for the world's largest electricity consumers seeking long-term carbon-free power. Competitors include Vistra (Meta PPA for 2,609 MW) and Talen (Amazon PPA for up to 1,920 MW), but Constellation's fleet scale and operational track record across 14 nuclear stations provide an unmatched platform for additional contracts.

**Operational Excellence:** 94.7% nuclear capacity factor (4 points above industry average). 22-day refueling outages vs. 33-38 industry average. 77% C&I renewal rate. These metrics compound over decades given the long-lived nature of nuclear assets.

**Customer Platform:** 2.5M accounts, more than 80% of Fortune 100 companies. Natural hedge between generation and load serving. Largest competitive retail electric supplier in the U.S.

**Trajectory:** Improving. Each hyperscaler PPA signed, each capacity auction clearing at elevated prices, and each data center interconnection agreement widens Constellation's competitive moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Calpine Integration (Moderate):** $22B acquisition with complex integration of 72 generation assets. Credit ratings affirmed, suggesting manageable financial risk. Operational integration will take 12-18 months. Customer retention during transition is a quantifiable risk. Mitigation: experienced management team, complementary asset profiles (nuclear + gas), and commercial optimization opportunities.

**Crane Restart Execution (Moderate-High):** Transmission line delays disclosed in April 2026 threaten the restart timeline. Company has proposed FERC workarounds. If delayed beyond 2028, financial impact includes continued capex without revenue ($1B+ invested). However, the $1B DOE loan guarantee and 20-year Microsoft PPA limit downside — even delayed, the project has positive economics once operational.

**Nuclear PTC Volatility (Moderate Impact on GAAP, Low on Adjusted Earnings):** Higher market prices mechanically reduce PTC revenue ($2.08B → $320M in 2025). While this creates GAAP earnings volatility (GAAP EPS fell from $11.89 to $7.40 in 2025), the economic outcome is positive — higher market revenues more than compensate, as reflected in adjusted operating EPS growth from $8.67 to $9.39. Investors focused on GAAP earnings may misread results, but PTC changes represent a large reported revenue swing that affects GAAP comparability.

**FERC Co-Location Regulatory Risk (Moderate):** December 2025 ruling found PJM's tariff unjust regarding co-located load. Could complicate behind-the-meter data center arrangements. Constellation's existing Microsoft and Meta contracts are structured as utility-scale PPAs rather than behind-the-meter arrangements, providing some insulation, though future contract economics and interconnection arrangements remain subject to regulatory developments.

**PJM Market Reform Uncertainty (Moderate):** PJM proposed extending administrative price caps for 2028-2029 and 2029-2030 delivery years, subject to FERC approval. Reliability-backstop proposals, large-load cost allocation, and evolving market design introduce uncertainty around future capacity revenues. Constellation frames PJM regulatory clarity as "coming into view" with multiple active workstreams.

**Higher-for-Longer Rates (Low-Moderate):** Elevated long-term yields increase the discount rate applied to long-duration assets. However, CEG's growth rate (15%+) and merchant model differentiate it from regulated utilities with bond-proxy characteristics. The correlation between CEG and Treasury yields has been weak relative to traditional utilities.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter consensus down 5.1% over 90 days; next quarter down 8.0%. Full-year FY2026 essentially flat (-0.1% over 90 days). The near-term revision weakness reflects Calpine integration timing uncertainty rather than structural deterioration. FY2027 estimates are up 0.5% over 90 days.

### Options Market Signal

As of early May 2026, ATM IV of approximately 80.5% vs. 45.2% historical volatility represents extreme richness. The term structure is in steep backwardation (near-term vs. ~48.4% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a near-term binary event. Put/call OI ratios have increased alongside recent price declines.

This options positioning reflects broad market stress rather than CEG-specific informed selling. The 6-month IV (closer to historical volatility) suggests the market views near-term uncertainty as transient. For fundamental buyers with a 12-month horizon, elevated near-term IV creates an opportunity to sell puts or buy calls at outer tenors where IV/HV premium is minimal.

Short interest at approximately 3.2% with several days to cover is low — no crowded short thesis or meaningful squeeze risk. The selling pressure is fundamental repositioning, not speculative shorting.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy represents a structural growth story trading at a cyclically depressed level. The 27% decline from October 2025 highs has created reasonable entry for a business with elevated capacity price visibility, contracted hyperscaler revenue, and irreplaceable nuclear assets. At 26x forward earnings growing at 15%+, the valuation is justified by identifiable, already-secured revenue sources rather than speculative projections.

The specific risks — Calpine integration timing, Crane restart delays, FERC regulatory evolution — are real but manageable. None represents permanent capital impairment given investment-grade credit, $7B revolver, and a 55 GW diversified generation portfolio. The score trajectory (declining from 7.1 to 6.0 over prior reports) reflected legitimate uncertainty accumulation during the selloff from $412 to $243. The stock has since recovered to $303, and the capacity price structural thesis has been supported by PJM auction results. The fundamental trajectory has stabilized while the price remains depressed, creating improving risk/reward.

The negative estimate revision trend in near-term quarters (-5.1% current quarter, -8.0% next quarter) is the primary counterargument. This reflects Calpine integration timing uncertainty and needs monitoring. However, full-year FY2026 estimates are essentially flat and FY2027 estimates are up 0.5% over 90 days — the structural earnings power is not being revised down, only the quarterly distribution is shifting. Temporal estimate migration within a year is far less concerning than structural downward revision.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The Vistra valuation comparison has been corrected (VST forward P/E is lower than CEG's, not higher), PJM exposure is ~50% post-Calpine (not ~70%), capacity-price "lock-in" language has been softened, and post-Calpine pro forma cash is ~$1.0B (not $3.6B). These corrections reduce the margin-of-safety argument but do not eliminate the growth thesis underpinning the recommendation.
