## Executive Summary

AIG has completed a multi-year transformation from a sprawling, poorly-rated conglomerate into a focused pure-play property & casualty insurer with AA-/A1-rated insurance subsidiaries. The company has indicated the sale of remaining Corebridge shares is imminent, marking the final step in this separation. Core operating book value per share has compounded at 14-15% annually ($52.74 → $61.75 → $69.12 over 2023-2025), driven by a combination of underwriting improvement (combined ratio from 92.0 to 90.1), aggressive buybacks (31% share reduction since 2022), and investment income growth. The stock trades at $78.42, or approximately 1.10x Q1 2026 core operating BVPS of $71.54 — a discount to high-quality P&C peers like Chubb (>1.5x) and Travelers (~1.4x).

The primary risk is long-tail casualty reserve adequacy in a social inflation environment. U.S. Excess Casualty showed $85M in adverse development in 2025, and sensitivity analysis reveals potential $900M-$1.15B reserve increases from modest assumption changes. The NICO adverse development cover ($25B limit) provides a backstop, but it is being consumed ($16.5B utilized). Catastrophe exposure (particularly wildfire, at $440M in 2025) adds volatility. At current valuation — trading near adjusted book with EPS growing via buybacks to an estimated $7.97 in FY2026 — investors are compensated for bearing these risks.

## Company Overview

AIG operates as a global P&C insurer across three segments: North America Commercial (approximately 37% of net premiums written), International Commercial (approximately 37%), and Global Personal (approximately 26%). The company provides insurance solutions in over 200 countries through operating subsidiaries including National Union Fire Insurance, Lexington Insurance, American Home Assurance, AIG Europe S.A., and Talbot (Lloyd's). Total assets stand at $161.3B with stockholders' equity of $41.1B. The business employs approximately 22,100 people across 45 countries.

Following the Corebridge Financial separation, AIG is now predominantly a P&C insurer — a cleaner, more focused entity that should trade at a higher multiple than the former conglomerate structure warranted. As of March 31, 2026, AIG retained a 5.6% equity stake in Corebridge ($607M fair value), which management has indicated it intends to fully divest.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** FY2025 was AIG's best underwriting year in its post-crisis era. The combined ratio of 90.1 (vs. 91.8 in 2024) reflects disciplined rate adequacy and AIG 200 operational improvements. Underwriting income reached $2.33B (+22% YoY). Adjusted after-tax EPS of $7.09 was up 43% from $4.95 in 2024, though much of this was driven by share count reduction rather than absolute earnings growth. Net investment income from General Insurance grew 12% to $3.4B, benefiting from the higher-rate environment and 3.8-year fixed income duration.

**Capital Return:** AIG returned $6.8B to shareholders in 2025 — $5.8B in repurchases (73M shares, -11% count) and $1.0B in dividends. Share count has declined from 779M (end-2022) to 538M (end-2025). As of April 24, 2026, approximately $3.2B remained under the current buyback authorization, with additional proceeds expected from the final Corebridge sale. Another ~8-9% share count reduction is feasible at current prices. The dividend was increased 12.5% in 2025 with management signaling >10% growth in 2026.

**Balance Sheet:** Book value per share is $76.44; adjusted BVPS is $78.02; core operating BVPS is $69.12 (all year-end 2025). As of Q1 2026, core operating BVPS increased to $71.54. The company maintains $9.3B in parent liquidity with a $3.0B undrawn revolving credit facility. The investment portfolio ($71B AFS bonds, 91% investment grade) is conservatively positioned with a manageable 3.8-year duration, though below-investment-grade holdings increased from 6% to 8% in 2025.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $3.3B supports both the dividend and buyback programs. Insurance subsidiaries distributed $3.0B to the parent in 2025.

**Earnings Consistency:** AIG has beaten estimates in recent quarters, including Q1 2026 (adjusted EPS of $2.11 vs. approximately $1.87 consensus, a beat of roughly 13%). This pattern suggests consensus estimates remain conservative.

## Growth Analysis

AIG's organic growth is modest. Net premiums written were essentially flat in 2025 (+1% earned, -1% written), with North America Commercial and International Commercial each growing 4%, offset by a 12% decline in Global Personal (primarily from the travel insurance disposition).

Forward estimates reflect mid-single-digit EPS growth: $7.97 for FY2026E and $8.86 for FY2027E. The majority of per-share growth comes from continued buybacks rather than top-line expansion. Estimate revisions are stable — FY2026 estimates rose +1.5% over 90 days while current quarter estimates drifted -2.0%.

The growth pivot is evident in strategic deployments: $2.1B for a 35% Convex Group stake (specialty reinsurance), $646M for 9.9% of Onex, $301M for Everest renewal rights, and a partnership with CVC Capital Partners involving up to $1.5B contributed from AIG's existing private equity portfolio and up to $2B intended for SMAs/funds (with an initial $1B through 2026). Combined, these represent approximately $6.5B in growth-oriented capital allocation, though a portion reflects reallocation of existing investments rather than entirely fresh deployment. Convex is well-positioned in fast-growing specialty lines, but the shift from pure capital return to investment-driven growth adds execution risk.

Organic growth catalysts include: continued rate adequacy in commercial lines (though industry pricing is moderating from peak hard-market levels), expansion in Programs and Global Specialty, and enhanced investment income from CVC and alternative investment partnerships.

## Valuation Assessment

At $78.42, AIG trades at:
- 13.8x trailing P/E (GAAP EPS basis); approximately 9.8x forward adjusted P/E (consensus $7.97 adjusted FY2026E EPS)
- ~1.10x Q1 2026 core operating book value ($71.54)
- ~1.00x Q1 2026 adjusted book value ($78.55)
- ~2.6% dividend yield

Relative to peers: the multi-line insurance median P/E is 13.7x (AIG at 13.8x trailing is in-line). However, this comparison is misleading because AIG's combined ratio (90.1) and ROE trajectory (11.1%, targeting mid-teens) are superior to most multi-line peers. Higher-quality P&C peers trade at 1.4-1.5x book, implying $100-$107 per share on current core operating BVPS — 28-37% upside.

The mean analyst target is $87.65 (median $86.00), representing 12% upside. The high target of $101 implies ~1.4x core operating book, consistent with the re-rating thesis toward quality P&C peer multiples.

At ~9.8x adjusted EPS ($7.97E for FY2026), AIG is cheap for a company compounding book value at 14-15% with subsidiary financial-strength ratings at the AA-/A1 level and improving underwriting. The market appears to be pricing in either (a) a reserve charge from long-tail casualty or (b) skepticism about sustaining the combined ratio below 91. If neither materializes, the stock re-rates.

## Competitive Landscape

AIG competes against Chubb, Zurich, Allianz, Travelers, Hartford, and Berkshire Hathaway specialty units. The 2025 rating upgrades (S&P AA-, Moody's A1, Fitch AA- — all referring to insurance subsidiary financial strength ratings; AIG Parent senior debt is rated A-/Baa1/A-) bring AIG's financial strength closer to best-in-class peers (Chubb is rated AA), which matters for winning large multinational accounts where counterparty strength is a procurement criterion.

The company's global reach (200+ countries) is a genuine competitive differentiator that few insurers can match. Multinational program capabilities (seamless coverage across jurisdictions) create switching costs and justify price premiums.

Combined ratio is declining, ratings are upgrading, and ROE is rising. The Convex acquisition and Everest renewal rights add distribution and specialty capabilities that should accelerate growth in 2026-2027 without the integration complexity of a full acquisition.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Long-Tail Casualty Reserve Adequacy (HIGH).** The $85M adverse development in U.S. Excess Casualty (mass tort) and $216M in UK/Europe are early indicators of potential under-reserving in a social inflation environment. Sensitivity tables show $900M-$1.15B potential reserve increases from modest assumption changes in U.S. Excess Casualty alone. The NICO cover ($25B limit, $16.5B utilized) provides a buffer but is finite.

**2. Catastrophe Exposure (MODERATE).** $440M in wildfire losses in 2025 (up from $41M in 2024). The 1-in-250 worldwide PML is $2.5B (4.8% of equity). Climate risk intensification creates a secular headwind for the Personal lines high-net-worth book.

**3. Strategic Execution Risk (MODERATE).** Approximately $6.5B in recent investments/acquisitions (including some reallocation of existing portfolio assets) marks a meaningful shift from capital return to growth. Convex, Onex, and CVC each require different execution capabilities. Returns won't be visible for 2-3 years.

**4. Social Inflation.** Accident year loss ratio ex-CAT rose to 57.2% from 56.2% — a 1pt deterioration that could signal the beginning of margin erosion if litigation trends continue.

**5. Investment Portfolio.** Below-investment-grade holdings rose from 6% to 8%; real estate fund impairments ($403M in 2025) suggest more aggressive yield-seeking. Office-heavy CML portfolio ($986M of $2.5B) is exposed to CRE stress.

**6. Pricing Environment.** While commercial pricing remains adequate, industry commentary from S&P Global and others indicates a moderating/softening pricing environment in several lines, with increased competition. The broad hard-market tailwind that benefited AIG's recent results may not persist at the same intensity.

### Market Sentiment

Short interest at 1.6% of float is negligible and not a concern. The consistent earnings beat pattern and improving fundamentals contrast with a valuation that appears to reflect lingering skepticism about the sustainability of underwriting improvement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-101, 21-29% upside):** AIG's combined ratio stabilizes below 91, continued buybacks reduce shares by another 8-10% annually, core operating BVPS compounds to $80+ by end-2027, and the market re-rates the stock to 1.3-1.5x book (consistent with P&C peers with AA-level subsidiary ratings). FY2027 EPS of $8.86 at 11-12x = $97-106. Convex and CVC investments begin generating visible returns.

**Bear Case ($65-70, 11-17% downside):** Social inflation accelerates, requiring a material reserve charge ($500M-$1B) that resets the loss ratio higher. Combined ratio reverts above 93. Market de-rates to 0.9-1.0x book as confidence in underwriting improvement erodes. Catastrophe losses compound the problem in a heavy hurricane or wildfire year.

**Base Case ($88-92, 12-17% upside):** Combined ratio around 91, buyback-driven EPS growth to $8.00-8.50 in FY2026, gradual re-rating toward 1.25-1.3x core operating book as the pure-play P&C identity becomes fully established. The Corebridge overhang is expected to be completely eliminated upon final sale.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $90 — derived from 1.25x core operating BVPS of ~$72 by mid-2027, or alternatively 11.3x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $7.97. This represents 15% upside from current levels.

**Bull case target:** $101 — 1.4x core operating BVPS growing to $72 (FY2026E), or 11.4x FY2027E adjusted EPS of $8.86. Represents 29% upside.

**Bear case target:** $67 — 0.95x current core operating BVPS of $69.12, reflecting a major reserve charge and combined ratio deterioration. Represents 15% downside.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q2-Q3 2026 combined ratio confirmation (~August/November), continued buyback execution, and absence of material reserve charges.

**Upside/downside from current:** +15% to base case, +29% to bull case, -15% to bear case. Reward/risk ratio: approximately 2:1 on base case probability.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Calendar year combined ratio exceeds 94 for two consecutive quarters (would indicate underwriting deterioration is structural, not episodic)
2. Net adverse reserve development exceeds $500M in any single quarter (would signal NICO cover is insufficiently wide and social inflation is overwhelming)
3. Core operating BVPS declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters (would indicate capital destruction)

## Conclusion

AIG at approximately 1.10x core operating book with improving fundamentals, AA-/A1-level subsidiary financial-strength ratings, and aggressive capital return represents a reasonable buy for investors with a 12-18 month horizon. The company has completed its transformation from conglomerate to pure-play P&C insurer, and the market hasn't yet fully re-rated the stock to reflect this improved profile.

The long-tail casualty reserve risk is real and warrants position sizing discipline — this is a 2-3% position, not a high-conviction overweight. At current valuation, investors are getting the improving business at approximately book value while waiting for the multiple expansion that typically accompanies sustained ROE improvement in the insurance sector. The 2.6% dividend yield provides downside support, and the remaining ~$3.2B buyback authorization (subject to market conditions and board discretion) supports continued capital return.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The P&C pricing environment is moderating (not broadly hardening as originally framed), and remaining buyback authorization is $3.2B (not $3.8B), which modestly reduces the near-term capital return tailwind supporting the thesis.
