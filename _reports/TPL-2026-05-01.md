## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation is one of the most unusual and highest-quality businesses in U.S. public markets: a legacy landowner controlling ~882,000 surface acres and ~224,000 net royalty acres in the Permian Basin, the most productive oil basin in the world. The company generates revenue from oil & gas royalties, water services, easements, and surface operations with virtually no capex requirement for its royalty streams. In 2025, TPL posted record revenue of $798M, record free cash flow of $498M at a 62.4% FCF margin, and operated at a 74% operating margin with zero debt. There is no direct public market peer.

The fundamental challenge is that this exceptional quality is reflected — and arguably over-reflected — in the current valuation. At ~$444 and ~64x trailing earnings, TPL trades at nearly 6x the median P/E of its nominal E&P peer group (11.2x). The stock has rallied significantly from its December 2025 lows, driven by a blowout Q4 earnings report and enthusiasm around the Bolt Data & Energy data center partnership. Meanwhile, 3 of the last 4 quarters missed consensus EPS estimates, and earnings growth (+6% YoY) substantially lagged revenue growth (+13%) due to higher depletion charges from the $451M royalty acquisition. The options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV of 52.9% in backwardation, short interest sits at 8.9% of float with 7.4 days to cover, and consensus analyst opinion is evenly split (1 Buy, 1 Sell). The business deserves a premium multiple, but the magnitude of that premium leaves minimal margin of safety at current levels.

## Company Overview

TPL traces its origins to 1888, when it was formed to manage the land grants of the Texas and Pacific Railway Company. Today it operates two segments: Land and Resource Management (62% of 2025 revenue, $491M) and Water Services and Operations (38%, $308M). The royalty business — the core value driver at $412M in oil & gas royalties alone — requires zero capex. TPL collects 1/128th and 1/16th non-participating royalty interests on production across its acreage. Water services involves sourcing and selling water for well completions and collecting royalties on produced water disposal across its surface. The company employs just 114 people generating $7.0M in revenue per employee.

Two emerging growth vectors merit attention. First, the December 2025 Bolt Data & Energy partnership ($50M minority investment) positions TPL to monetize its land for data center campuses, leveraging proximity to power generation, water for cooling, and cheap acreage. This is pre-revenue and binary. Second, the Transmissive produced water desalination project ($45.5M invested, Phase 2B facility under construction, expected completion 1H 2026) offers an alternative to subsurface disposal as seismicity regulations tighten. Also pre-revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** 2025 revenue of $798.2M grew 13.1% YoY, driven by a 29% surge in oil & gas royalty production volumes (34.6 MBoe/d vs. 26.8 MBoe/d), primarily from the $451M Midland Basin royalty acquisition. Realized prices fell 14.3% ($34.18/Boe vs. $39.87/Boe), but volume growth overwhelmed the price decline. Water services revenue grew on both volume (+3.4%) and pricing (+8.8%). Operating income of $592.2M (+9.8%) and net income of $481.4M (+6.0%) grew more slowly than revenue due to a $37.4M increase in DD&A charges from the new royalty assets. Diluted EPS was $6.97 vs. $6.57 prior year.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $545.9M and free cash flow of $498.3M represent a 62.4% FCF margin. Capex was just $59.5M (7.5% of revenue), primarily water infrastructure and the Transmissive project. FCF/Net Income was 103%, indicating clean, high-quality earnings conversion.

**Balance Sheet:** Zero debt drawn on the $500M revolving credit facility (established October 2025, first formal debt capacity in company history). Cash of $144.8M, down from $370M pre-acquisition. Debt-to-equity of 0.11x. Current ratio of 4.4x. Altman Z-Score of 8.46. ROA of 29.7% and ROE of 33.0%. This is among the strongest balance sheets in U.S. public equities.

**Capital Returns:** $156.2M returned in 2025 (31% of FCF) via $147.8M in dividends and $8.4M in buybacks. A 12.5% dividend increase was announced in February 2026 ($0.60/share quarterly from $0.53). The yield at current prices is ~0.5%. Management targets rebuilding cash to ~$700M before resuming larger returns.

**Q4 2025 Run Rate:** Q4 revenue of $211.6M annualizes to ~$846M. Record quarterly production of 37.5 MBoe/d, record water sales of 1.0M bbl/d, and record produced water royalties of 4.8M bbl/d indicate continued acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects hinge on three drivers operating at different time horizons:

**Near-term (0-12 months):** The full-year benefit of the royalty acquisition (closed November 2025) should sustain production volume growth through 2026. Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026E EPS of $9.56 (+37% YoY) with positive revision momentum (+15.7% over 30 days, +16.8% over 90 days). However, with only 2 analysts covering, estimate reliability is low, and the Q2-Q3 2025 misses of -73% and -10% respectively (largely due to estimate methodology transitions post-split) reduce confidence in consensus accuracy.

**Medium-term (1-3 years):** Organic volume growth depends on Permian drilling activity. Rig counts declined to 257 in 2025 from 296, and DUC inventory fell to 3,946 from 4,536 — both negative leading indicators for completion activity. However, drilling efficiency improvements allow production growth with fewer rigs, and the current oil price environment should incentivize stable-to-higher activity levels. Water services revenue benefits from the structural trend toward higher water intensity per well completion. Produced water volumes grow proportionally with basin production.

**Long-term (3+ years):** The Bolt data center partnership and Transmissive desalination technology represent optionality that could materially expand TPL's addressable market. If large-scale data centers are built on TPL's acreage leveraging proximity to natural gas power generation and water supply, the land value proposition diversifies beyond oil dependency. This is speculative but structurally plausible.

Estimate revisions are uniformly positive: current quarter +7.6% (30d), next quarter +25.0% (30d), FY2026 +15.7% (30d), FY2027 +8.5% (30d). This positive momentum is the strongest fundamental signal for near-term appreciation.

## Valuation Assessment

TPL's valuation is the binding constraint on the investment thesis. At $444 and ~64x trailing earnings, the stock trades at a premium that exceeds even the most generous royalty/streaming company multiples:

| Metric | TPL | E&P Peer Median | Premium |
|--------|-----|-----------------|---------|
| P/E (trailing) | 63.7x | 11.2x | 5.7x |
| Dividend Yield | 0.5% | 2.4% | -1.9pp |
| EV/EBITDA (est.) | ~43x | ~5-7x | ~7x |

The premium is partly justified: TPL carries zero operational risk, zero debt, requires virtually no capex for its royalty streams, and owns an irreplicable land position. Franco-Nevada or Royal Gold trade at 30-50x earnings, and TPL's asset quality arguably merits comparable-to-higher multiples given its Permian monopoly and multi-revenue-stream model. But 64x trailing EPS of $6.97 prices in substantial future growth. On FY2026E consensus EPS of $9.56, the forward P/E is ~46x — still expensive relative to even royalty comps.

The analyst consensus mean target of $445.50 is essentially at the current price, implying no upside. The range ($252-$639) is extraordinarily wide, reflecting genuine uncertainty about appropriate valuation methodology for a company with no true peers.

Using $9.56 FY2026E EPS and a 40x forward multiple (generous but defensible for a zero-capex royalty business): fair value = ~$382. At a 50x multiple (reflecting data center optionality): fair value = ~$478. The current price of $444 sits in the middle of this range, suggesting the market is partially pricing in the Bolt optionality without requiring proof of execution.

**The specific concern preventing a BUY: at $444, the stock prices in successful execution of the royalty acquisition at full commodity price assumptions AND partial credit for pre-revenue growth vectors, while 3 of 4 recent quarters missed estimates and the P/E is ~46x forward.** The downside to a bear case where oil prices revert to strip (~$63 WTI) is 30-40%, while the upside to the bull case ($639 high target) is ~44%. This asymmetry is insufficiently favorable at current levels given the earnings miss pattern.

## Competitive Landscape

TPL has no direct public market peer. The 882,000-acre Permian Basin land position cannot be replicated — it is a legacy asset from 19th century railroad land grants. Competitive advantages include:

- **Monopoly land position:** Operators drilling on or near TPL's acreage have no alternative landowner. Royalty interests are contractually perpetual.
- **Multi-stream revenue extraction:** A single well generates royalty income, water sales, produced water royalties, easement fees, and material sales — all flowing to TPL simultaneously.
- **Asset-light model:** The royalty business has near-zero marginal cost. Operating expenses are predominantly water infrastructure and G&A.
- **Scale in water services:** TPL's water infrastructure network across the Permian gives it a logistical advantage over smaller competitors.

The competitive position is improving: the royalty acquisition added 17,306 NRA in the most productive Midland Basin counties, and the Bolt partnership opens a new monetization channel. The only competitive risk is regulatory (seismicity restrictions on disposal wells), which TPL is partially hedging through its Transmissive technology investment.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Dependency (HIGH):** Despite the asset-light model, 100% of TPL's revenue streams are downstream of Permian Basin drilling activity. A sustained commodity downturn (WTI below $60) would simultaneously compress royalty revenue, water demand, and easement activity. Mean-reversion toward strip pricing (~$63 WTI) would materially impair earnings.

**Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** ~40% of 2025 revenue came from three customers. Capital allocation decisions by 2-3 major Permian operators directly determine TPL's volume trajectory.

**Earnings Estimate Reliability (MODERATE):** Only 2 analysts cover TPL. The recent miss pattern (partly reflecting post-split estimate methodology confusion) creates legitimate uncertainty about consensus accuracy.

**Valuation Compression Risk (HIGH):** At the current multiple, any disappointment in growth or commodity prices triggers disproportionate multiple contraction. The stock declined from its February 2026 high ($547) to its December 2025 low ($269), demonstrating the volatility inherent in premium-multiple stocks during commodity weakness.

**Pre-Revenue Venture Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** $95.5M committed to Bolt ($50M) and Transmissive ($45.5M) — both pre-revenue and binary. Immaterial to the balance sheet but could become narrative-driven catalysts (positive or negative).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 52.9% vs. 1-year historical volatility of 46.4% indicates the options market is pricing 6.5pp of excess uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (52.9% near-term vs. 43.6% at 6 months), consistent with pricing of a near-term catalyst — likely the upcoming Q1 2026 earnings report. Put/call skew of 4.2% shows moderately elevated put demand. The put/call OI ratio of 0.72 is not extreme but directionally cautious. IV has declined 2.1pp from the April 13 snapshot (55.1% → 52.9%) alongside a 2.6% price increase, suggesting modest de-risking.

Short interest at 8.9% with 7.4 days to cover is elevated for a $30B market cap company. This creates both squeeze potential on positive catalysts and reflects informed bearish positioning on the valuation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($550-640):**
- Sustained high oil prices drive continued Permian production growth, with TPL's royalty volumes reaching 40+ MBoe/d in 2026
- Bolt Data & Energy secures an anchor customer and begins construction on TPL land, creating a credible data center revenue stream narrative
- Transmissive desalination achieves commercial viability, opening a new produced water treatment market
- FY2026 EPS reaches $10-11, supporting a 50-60x forward multiple on growth optionality
- Buyback acceleration after cash target achieved

**Bear Case ($270-320):**
- Oil prices revert toward strip (~$63 WTI) as current geopolitical premiums fade
- Permian rig count declines further, compressing royalty volumes and water demand
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $7-8 (below current consensus)
- Multiple compresses to 35-40x on lower growth and commodity reversion
- Bolt and Transmissive fail to generate meaningful revenue, removing optionality premium

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price pullback to $350-370 range, representing ~37-39x FY2026E consensus EPS — a more defensible entry point with 15-20% upside to fair value
2. Bolt Data & Energy announces a binding anchor tenant agreement or breaks ground on a data center on TPL land, converting optionality premium from speculative to fundamental
3. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats exceeding 5%, re-establishing estimate credibility

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. WTI oil price sustained below $65 for more than one quarter while stock remains above $400 (would imply >50x on declining earnings)
2. Permian rig count falls below 220 (signaling structural activity decline on TPL acreage)
3. Bolt Data & Energy investment impaired or partnership dissolved

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (expected May 2026 for Q1 FY2026). This will provide the first full-quarter look at post-acquisition run rate production and the first data point on 2026 volume trajectory.

## Conclusion

TPL is an exceptional business — zero debt, 62% FCF margins, irreplicable Permian land position, asset-light royalty model — but the current valuation at ~64x trailing and ~46x forward earnings already incorporates much of this quality and a portion of speculative optionality that has yet to generate revenue. The 3-of-4 quarterly earnings miss pattern, despite positive estimate revision momentum, creates uncertainty about whether consensus accurately captures the post-acquisition, post-split financial trajectory. With the options market pricing near-term stress and short interest at 8.9%, the risk/reward at $444 is balanced rather than asymmetric. HOLD with a growth thesis: the business will likely compound intrinsic value at double-digit rates over multi-year periods, but the entry point matters for a 64x P/E stock, and $350-370 offers a substantially more favorable risk/reward setup.
