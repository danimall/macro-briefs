**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cactus has spent the first half of 2026 executing the largest transaction in its history. On January 1, 2026, it acquired 65% of Baker Hughes' surface pressure control business for $344.5M cash, converting itself from a predominantly U.S.-onshore wellhead and spoolable-pipe supplier into a global pressure-control player with Middle East manufacturing, $537.5M of remaining performance obligations, and a revenue base that grew 38.5% year-over-year in Q1 2026. The strategic logic is clear: legacy Cactus was a short-cycle, call-out business with little forward visibility, and the JV adds longer-cycle contracted backlog plus exposure to the one part of the global rig market that is still growing (international land rigs +1% in Q1, led by the Middle East and Latin America).

The transaction also imported three problems that did not exist a year ago. First, Baker Hughes can put its retained 35% stake to Cactus starting January 1, 2028 at 6x trailing EBITDA, capped at $660M (with a $530M floor if Cactus initiates), a material contingent claim that may force debt or equity issuance. Second, the put is carried as redeemable mezzanine equity (~$240.6M) and remeasured each quarter; the Q1 remeasurement produced an $81.5M non-cash deemed dividend that turned reported EPS negative ($(0.70)/share) even though underlying attributable net income was a positive $32.9M and operating cash flow was $128.3M. Third, the UAE and Saudi plants Cactus just bought to gain Middle East exposure are now being directly disrupted by the Iran/Israel/U.S. conflict that broke out in February 2026: impeded Hormuz traffic, supply-chain disruption, and delayed customer orders that management explicitly warns may impair the anticipated synergies of the deal.

We rate WHD a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The business is genuinely high quality (~23% operating margins, ~20% FCF margin, Z-Score 4.12, current ratio 5.81), the competitive position is improving on the JV, and the strategic direction is defensible. But at ~54x trailing and ~17x next-year EPS the stock has already re-rated through a 40% run off its October low to near consensus targets, near-term estimates are being cut (current-quarter EPS -13.2% over 90 days), and the founders have accelerated selling, including a new 1.2M-share 10b5-1 plan adopted in March 2026. The risk/reward is balanced, and the dominant near-term swing factor (Middle East disruption versus synergy capture) is genuinely two-sided.

## Company Overview

Cactus designs, manufactures, sells, and rents highly engineered pressure-control and spoolable-pipe equipment for onshore oil and gas wells. Founded in 2011 by the Bender family (wellhead operators since the late 1970s), it IPO'd in February 2018. The company runs two segments:

- **Pressure Control (Cactus Wellhead, ~74-77% of revenue):** wellheads, production trees, and frac/pressure-control equipment for drilling, completion, and production. Cactus is one of the largest U.S. wellhead suppliers, competing with Vault, SLB, and TechnipFMC. Manufacturing in Bossier City (Louisiana), Suzhou (China), and Vietnam.
- **Spoolable Technologies (FlexSteel, ~23% of revenue):** spoolable pipe and fittings for production, gathering, and takeaway lines, acquired in February 2023 for $658.6M. Manufactured in Baytown (Texas). Competes with Baker Hughes, Mattr, NOV, and steel-line-pipe makers.

Revenue mix is roughly 76% products, 8% rentals, 16% field service, across 300+ customers. A single customer represented 17% of total revenue in 2025, up from 15% in 2024 and 10% in 2023, a rising concentration worth monitoring.

The defining structural feature is the "Up-C" holding-company arrangement. Cactus Inc. (the public Class A entity) owns ~86.6% of operating subsidiary Cactus Companies; Bender-controlled entities and other holders own the remaining ~13.4% through CC Units / Class B shares redeemable for Class A stock. This produces a meaningful non-controlling interest drag on net income and a substantial Tax Receivable Agreement liability (~$264.8M at Q1 2026), under which 85% of realized tax savings flow to pre-IPO owners.

## Financial Analysis

The pre-deal balance sheet was strong. Altman Z-Score of 4.12 sits comfortably in the safe zone, current ratio 5.81, debt/equity 0.36, and the company was effectively net-cash before funding the Baker Hughes acquisition. Cash fell from $343M at end-2024 to $124M at end-2025 (with $371M parked in escrow for the deal). Cash generation is high quality: operating cash flow consistently exceeds net income, capex is light ($39M in 2025, $40-50M guided for 2026), and FCF margin runs around 20% ($220M FCF in 2025).

The multi-year picture shows a strong franchise that had a soft 2025:

| Metric (FY) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 688 | 1,097 | 1,130 | 1,079 |
| Operating income ($M) | 175 | 264 | 290 | 251 |
| Operating margin | 25.4% | 24.1% | 25.6% | 23.2% |
| Net income to Cactus Inc. ($M) | 110 | 169 | 185 | 166 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | 340 | 316 | 258 |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | — | 220 |

2025 was a down year: revenue -4.5%, operating income -13.4%, net income to Cactus -10.3%. Both segments declined (Pressure Control -0.9%, Spoolable -9.5%) as U.S. land rigs fell ~6% and average oil prices dropped ~15%. Margins compressed on Section 232 50% steel tariffs and elevated tariff costs on China/Vietnam imports, a recurring company-specific headwind given the Asian manufacturing footprint that management is only partially offsetting through cost cuts and price increases.

Q1 2026 was the first consolidated quarter including Cactus International (the Baker Hughes JV). The headline numbers require careful reading:

- Revenue **$388.3M, +38.5% YoY**, entirely driven by Cactus International's $126.9M contribution (~33% of consolidated revenue).
- Operating income **fell to $49.5M (-27.8% YoY)** on purchase-accounting drag (inventory step-up, $12.5M intangible amortization), tariffs, and integration costs.
- Reported EPS was **$(0.70)**, an accounting artifact. A non-cash $81.5M accretion of redeemable non-controlling interest to redemption value (the Baker Hughes put/call mezzanine equity) is treated as a deemed dividend. Underlying net income attributable to Cactus was a positive $32.9M (down from $44.2M a year earlier), and operating cash flow was $128.3M. A naive data pull flags WHD as suddenly unprofitable; it is not. This accretion will recur each quarter and will scale with JV EBITDA: higher JV value means a larger deemed dividend and more EPS distortion. Investors and screening models will need to look through it.

Capital allocation has been conservative outside the deal: a $0.14/quarter dividend (raised from $0.13), and a $150M buyback authorization with $146.3M still available, barely used, only RSU tax-withholding repurchases in Q1 2026. The December 2025 ABL amendment extended maturity to December 2030 and added a $100M undrawn delayed-draw term loan, pre-positioning liquidity for the deal and the eventual put. Maximum leverage covenant is 2.50x.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is now bifurcated between organic cyclicality and the JV step-change.

Organically, the backdrop is mixed. U.S. land rigs fell from 580 (2024) to 545 (2025) to ~530 in Q1 2026 as E&P capital discipline held activity flat even through the oil-price spike; elevated prices do not translate into proportional equipment demand during a capital-discipline regime. The partial offset is natural-gas-directed drilling, which is strengthening on AI/data-center power demand and supports both segments.

The JV transforms the growth profile. Cactus moves from a U.S.-onshore supplier to a global wellhead supplier with direct Middle East exposure, where international land rig counts are growing. The deal adds a backlog feature the short-cycle legacy business never had, improving revenue visibility and bringing longer-cycle, technically complex equipment contracts into the model.

The forward estimate picture is the key tension. Estimate revisions are split by horizon:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Rev. |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $0.65 | -13.2% |
| Next Quarter | $0.71 | -10.0% |
| Current Year | $2.99 | -2.3% |
| Next Year | $3.45 | -1.1% |

Near-term estimates are being cut materially, consistent with Middle East disruption, integration drag, and tariffs. Full-year and next-year estimates are roughly stable. This is the opposite of the high-conviction inflection signal (estimate revisions +30% over 90 days) that would argue for a more aggressive stance. The earnings beat track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, beats of 8.6-13.7%), which lends modest confidence to the stabilized annual numbers, but the direction of near-term revisions is a caution flag, not a tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58-60, WHD trades at ~54x trailing EPS ($2.41) and roughly 17x next-year consensus EPS ($3.45). The trailing multiple is distorted by the Q1 NCI accretion and is not the right anchor; forward is the relevant metric, and ~17x forward for a high-margin, FCF-generative oilfield-equipment franchise with a transformative international acquisition is full rather than cheap.

Against peers in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, the picture is unflattering on a headline basis. Peer median P/E is 18.2 (OII 11.4, TDW 12.3, AROC 18.2, LBRT 32.2, AESI negative). WHD's ~17x forward is near the median, and its trailing 54x is an outlier. The premium is partly justified by superior margins (~23% operating versus the asset-heavy services peers) and the backlog/visibility improvement from the JV, but it is not a discount that the market has failed to recognize.

The analyst target distribution frames the asymmetry. Mean $63.22, median $66.00, low $50.00, high $72.00, against a current ~$58-60. The high case (~$72) reflects optimism on JV integration; the low ($50, near Citi's $55) reflects the cyclical and integration risk. At current price the stock sits below the mean and median but well within the band: implied upside to consensus is modest and downside to the low target is comparable. Analyst targets here are not floors: the stock is not cheap relative to its growth (PEG well above 1.5 on the ~17x forward against low-single-digit organic growth), so we treat the consensus band as a genuine range rather than a base that will be revised up.

The valuation score reflects a multiple stretched against modest organic growth, near-term estimate cuts, and no identifiable specific mispricing the market is missing. The cycle-adjusted argument cuts both ways: 2025 was a soft year, but U.S. activity remains in capital-discipline mode with no clear path back to peak rig counts, and the JV earnings are encumbered by purchase-accounting drag and the 2028 put overhang. We score valuation at 4.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus is a premium, differentiated player in a fragmented oilfield-equipment market. Its edge rests on product performance, rapid made-to-order delivery from Bossier City, safety, and a low-cost Asian manufacturing base, though that same base now exposes it to the Section 232 and China/Vietnam tariffs that compressed 2025 margins. The competitive trajectory is improving: the JV vaults Cactus from predominantly U.S.-onshore into global pressure control, expands into the growing Middle East land-rig market, and turns Baker Hughes, previously listed as a competitor in FlexSteel's spoolable market, into a 35% partner in pressure control.

The industry backdrop is split. Oil-directed U.S. activity is soft on E&P capital discipline, while natural-gas-directed drilling is strengthening on data-center power demand, which benefits both segments. The competitive position scores well (7.0) on improving trajectory: gaining international scale, adding backlog visibility, and converting a competitor into a partner. The main offset is the new geographic and operational stretch: Cactus had little Middle East infrastructure before the deal and now depends on Baker Hughes transition services, with shared governance (Baker Hughes board veto rights, transfer restrictions) limiting flexibility.

## Risk Assessment

**Baker Hughes JV integration and put liability (dominant new risk).** Cactus may lack the liquidity to fund the put and be forced into unfavorable debt or equity financing. The put is carried as ~$240.6M mezzanine equity, remeasured each quarter, the source of the recurring EPS distortion. Shared governance and non-compete/transfer restrictions further constrain flexibility.

**Iran/Israel/U.S. conflict.** The newly acquired UAE and Saudi plants face impeded Strait of Hormuz traffic, supply-chain disruption, customer order delays, and safety concerns. Management explicitly warns this may delay or impair the synergies that justify the deal. This is brand-new (only in the Q1 10-Q risk factors) and is the single most important near-term swing factor: the bull case (Middle East expansion) and the largest new risk (the Middle East war) are the same exposure.

**Tariffs.** A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down certain tariffs and Cactus has filed for limited refunds, but the most material Section 232 tariff is unchanged; refund optionality is small upside, not a thesis driver.

**Customer concentration.** One customer at 17% of revenue, up from 10% in 2023.

**Commodity cyclicality.** Demand tracks U.S. rig count and completions, which are in a flat-to-declining capital-discipline regime even with elevated oil prices.

**TRA and Up-C overhang.** ~$264.8M TRA liability; an early-termination/change-of-control trigger could accelerate to ~$251M+, a structural takeover deterrent and liquidity risk.

**Litigation.** Ongoing Cameron International (SLB) patent suit over the SafeLink frac system; trial delayed, outcome unpredictable. 2025 Pressure Control income was hit by increased legal expenses and reserves.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing moderate, evenly distributed uncertainty rather than acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 37.5% against 40.7% one-year historical vol, cheap on an IV-vs-HV basis (-3.1% spread), at the 23rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is flat (near 37.5% ≈ far 38.9%), implying the market does not see a single dated catalyst dominating. Put/call skew is elevated at 8.7% in the front month with a 6.4% implied move, signaling some hedging demand into the near term, but the 6-month skew is negative (-4.0%), so the downside-protection bid is short-dated. Put/call OI ratio is low at 0.23. The combination reads as a market that views WHD as moderately risky but not facing a binary near-term event, consistent with our balanced HOLD. The 6% short interest with 4.8 days to cover is unremarkable and does not create meaningful squeeze dynamics in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$72).** The Baker Hughes JV proves transformative: Middle East disruption fades, synergy capture accelerates, and the backlog converts into durable, higher-visibility revenue. Natural-gas-directed drilling strength offsets soft oil activity, tariffs ease (further SCOTUS relief or sourcing shifts), and the franchise's margins reassert as purchase-accounting drag rolls off. The market re-rates the now-global supplier toward the ~$72 high target on improved earnings power and visibility. This requires the conflict overhang to clear and the 2028 put to be refinanced without dilution.

**Bear Case (~$50).** The Middle East conflict persists, impairing the acquired plants and the very synergies that justified the deal, while near-term estimate cuts continue. U.S. capital discipline keeps domestic activity flat-to-down, tariffs grind on margins, and the 2028 put forces dilutive financing as it approaches. Accelerating founder selling proves prescient. The stock de-rates toward the Citi/low-target $50-55 zone, roughly -14% to -17% from current levels.

**Base Case (~$60).** The JV delivers gradual but uneven integration with the conflict as a persistent drag, organic growth stays modest, and the stock holds near its current level and consensus mean as competing forces (strategic upside, integration/conflict/dilution overhangs) offset. ~17x forward on ~$3.45 next-year EPS, adjusted for the put and TRA overhangs, supports roughly $60.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the key checkpoint: it will show a second consolidated quarter, clarify the conflict's revenue impact, and reveal whether near-term estimate cuts have stabilized. The 2028 Baker Hughes put window is the structural medium-term checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation that Middle East disruption is contained and synergy capture is on track, with near-term estimate revisions turning positive (current/next-quarter EPS revised up rather than down).
- A clear, non-dilutive financing path for the 2028 put (e.g., term-loan capacity expansion or demonstrated FCF accumulation toward the $530-660M obligation).
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores asymmetric upside to the consensus mean/high without fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material escalation of the conflict that forces sustained plant shutdowns and impairs the JV (goodwill/intangible impairment on the $228.8M intangibles + $45.3M goodwill).
- Continued near-term estimate cuts spilling into full-year and next-year numbers (current-year EPS revised below ~$2.80).
- A leverage covenant approach (toward the 2.50x cap) or a forced dilutive equity raise tied to the put.
- Acceleration of founder selling beyond the disclosed 1.2M-share plan, or signs the buyback remains idle while management distributes.

## Management & Governance

Founder-led by the Bender family — Scott Bender (Chairman/CEO), Joel Bender (President), Steven Bender (COO) — controlling ~12.1% of voting power via Cactus WH Enterprises. Stephen Tadlock runs both Spoolable Technologies and Cactus International; Jay Nutt is CFO. The board is being de-staggered (classified structure phased out by 2027), and director Andy O'Donnell is a former Baker Hughes executive, relevant to the JV.

The forward-looking management signal is cautionary. In March 2026, Scott and Joel Bender each sold large blocks (~$10M+ each), and on March 11, 2026 Bender Investment Company adopted a new 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 1.2M Class A shares (expiring June 2027). Directors Semple and McGovern also sold in May 2026. This is net insider distribution beginning right as the stock recovered toward the $50s/$60s, against a $150M buyback authorization that remains almost entirely unused outside RSU tax-withholding. Founder selling can reflect diversification and estate planning rather than a view on value, but the combination of accelerating, programmatic insider sales with an idle buyback at elevated prices is not the alignment signal a higher score would require. We score management quality at 4.5.

## Conclusion

Cactus has executed a transformative deal that improves its competitive trajectory, adds backlog visibility, and opens the growing Middle East market. The underlying franchise remains high quality: strong margins, robust FCF, a clean balance sheet, and a misunderstood Q1 EPS "loss" that is an accounting artifact rather than operational deterioration. Those are the reasons this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

The reasons it is not a BUY are equally concrete. The stock has already run 40% off its October low to near consensus targets, with no specific mispricing the market is missing. Near-term estimates are being cut, the dominant near-term swing factor (Middle East synergy capture versus active-conflict disruption) is genuinely two-sided and unresolved, the 2028 Baker Hughes put creates a structural financing overhang, and the founders are distributing stock while the buyback sits idle. The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 → 5.8), driven by valuation compression as the price re-rated, partly offset by improving growth and competitive-position reads, and our current analysis confirms that pattern rather than reversing it. Risk/reward is balanced. We rate WHD HOLD (uncertain), and would look to a pullback toward the low-$50s with stabilized estimates and conflict containment as the path to a more constructive stance.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward P/E was overstated (~20x → ~17x on $3.45 next-year EPS), which places WHD near—not above—the peer median P/E of 18.2 and modestly weakens the "stock is not cheap / no mispricing" argument underpinning the HOLD.
