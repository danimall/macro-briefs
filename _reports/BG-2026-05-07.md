## Executive Summary

Bunge Global SA has undergone a transformative year. The July 2025 completion of the $7.2 billion Viterra acquisition nearly doubled the company's asset base and cemented its position as the world's largest publicly-traded oilseed processor. The stock has rallied 61% over the past twelve months, driven by a massive Q1 2026 earnings beat (+103% surprise), a guidance raise to $9.00-$9.50 adjusted EPS, and positive estimate revisions across all time horizons. Consensus is overwhelmingly bullish (8/9 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy, mean target $142).

Material risks remain unresolved. The company generated negative free cash flow of approximately $880 million in 2025. Total debt has surged to $14.1 billion post-acquisition, and a lock-up expiration on July 3, 2026 will release approximately 59 million shares (30% of the float) held by Glencore and CPP Investments. Grain Merchandising goodwill has only a 9% cushion above carrying value. The Spruce Point short report, while hyperbolic in its price targets, raises legitimate structural questions about FCF generation capacity and PP&E marking that deserve ongoing monitoring rather than dismissal.

At ~13.6x forward earnings with estimates rising, the stock is neither expensive enough to warrant avoidance nor cheap enough relative to its risk profile to warrant a high-conviction buy. The lock-up expiration creates a defined catalyst window in approximately 8 weeks that will resolve a major source of uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Bunge Global SA is a Swiss-domiciled agribusiness company connecting farmers to consumers through oilseed processing, grain merchandising, and food manufacturing across 50+ countries with approximately 34,000 employees. Post-Viterra, the company operates in four segments:

- **Soybean Processing & Refining** (47% South America) — 2025 EBIT: $1,225M (+40% YoY)
- **Softseed Processing & Refining** (53% Europe, 30% North America) — 2025 EBIT: $521M (-21%)
- **Other Oilseeds Processing & Refining** — 2025 EBIT: $118M (-45%)
- **Grain Merchandising & Milling** — 2025 EBIT: $465M (+14%, includes $155M divestiture gain)

The Viterra acquisition added grain merchandising volume (+83% in 2025), expanded geographic reach (particularly in Canada, Australia, and the Black Sea region), and created approximately $250M in expected annual run-rate synergies.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**

Revenue grew 32% to $70.3 billion in 2025, entirely attributable to the Viterra consolidation. Gross profit remained flat at $3.4 billion despite the revenue surge, meaning the acquired revenue carries significantly lower margins than the legacy business. Net income declined to $816 million ($4.91 diluted EPS), down 64% from the 2023 peak of $2.24 billion.

Operating margins are structurally razor-thin: 0.4% operating, 1.2% net. This is characteristic of commodity processing businesses but leaves minimal buffer against margin compression.

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure:**

The most concerning data point is the 2025 free cash flow profile:
- Operating cash flow: $844M (down 56% from $1.9B in 2024)
- CapEx: $1.72B
- Free cash flow: -$879M

The company returned $1.05B to shareholders ($551M buybacks + $502M dividends) while generating negative FCF, funded entirely by new debt issuance. Total debt reached $14.1B (from $6.2B in 2024), and interest expense surged 33% to $628M. Cash declined from $3.3B to $1.1B.

The debt load is partially structural for an ag-commodity business (working capital financing fluctuates with commodity prices), and the $9.1B in unused committed credit facilities provides meaningful liquidity. All three credit agencies upgraded Bunge in 2025 (S&P to A-, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+), indicating institutional comfort with the post-acquisition leverage trajectory.

**Recent Earnings Trajectory:**

Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.83 vs. $0.90 consensus (+103%). Management raised full-year guidance to $9.00-$9.50 from $7.50-$8.00. The beat was driven by soybean processing margins in South America and global trade flow disruption that benefited Bunge's merchandising network (tariff-driven rerouting creates margin opportunities for origin-agnostic processors).

Estimate revisions are materially positive: FY2026 consensus up +14.7% in 30 days and +11.7% over 90 days. FY2027 up +6.1% in 30 days. This is genuine upward momentum, not noise.

## Growth Analysis

**Structural Tailwinds:**
- Biofuels demand continues growing (Bunge-Chevron JV for low-carbon transportation fuels)
- Protein meal demand in emerging markets tied to rising animal protein consumption
- Trade flow complexity rewards scale and geographic diversification (tariff environment favoring ABCD giants)
- Viterra synergy realization ($250M annual target, early stages)

**Structural Headwinds:**
- Crush margin normalization from 2022-23 supercycle
- Overcapacity risk from renewable diesel-driven processing additions
- China demand uncertainty (both trade policy and economic slowdown)
- Very minimal R&D spend limits differentiation capacity

The Q1 2026 guidance raise suggests synergy capture is tracking ahead of plan, and trade disruption is creating near-term margin opportunities. However, Bunge's original 2022 mid-cycle target of ~$11 EPS by 2026 has been effectively abandoned — even the raised guidance of $9.00-$9.50 is 14-18% below that promise. This represents a meaningful undershoot relative to the business case presented to shareholders who endorsed the Viterra deal.

Growth prospects score at 5.5 reflecting genuine near-term momentum tempered by structural commodity cycle normalization and capacity concerns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $126.05, Bunge trades at approximately 13.6x the midpoint of 2026 guidance ($9.25) and 11.6x FY2027 consensus ($10.89). This is below the Consumer Staples peer median of 31.3x but that comparison is misleading — Bunge is a commodity processor, not a branded staples company. The appropriate comparison is to ADM (trading at roughly 15-17x forward earnings) and grain/oilseed processor peers.

Relative to ADM, Bunge trades at a modest discount, which is reasonable given the higher leverage, integration risk, and lock-up overhang. The forward P/E of 13.6x is not demanding for a business with rising estimates and a recent 103% earnings beat.

The analyst consensus mean target of $142 implies 13% upside, with the low target at $116 (-8%) and high at $150 (+19%). No sell-side analyst has a target below $116.

The Spruce Point target of $24.50-$55.85 is not credible — it implies the stock should trade at 3-6x forward earnings, below distressed commodity processors. All three credit agencies upgraded the company with full access to financial details, directly contradicting the thesis of hidden leverage endangering solvency.

The valuation is reasonable but not compelling. At 13.6x forward, the stock prices in moderate execution on synergies without demanding optimism about margin expansion or cycle recovery. There is no obvious mispricing on either side.

## Competitive Landscape

Post-Viterra, Bunge is now the largest publicly-listed pure-play oilseed processor globally and a member of the "ABCD" oligopoly (ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus) that dominates global agricultural commodity trading. The acquisition meaningfully strengthened competitive position:

- Geographic diversification (added Canada, Australia, Black Sea)
- Volume scale in grain merchandising (+83%)
- Cross-selling between Viterra origination and Bunge processing

Competitive threats include: (1) COFCO's aggressive expansion in South America (47% of Bunge's soybean capacity is in South America), (2) petroleum companies entering biofuels feedstock competition, and (3) ADM's own restructuring efforts. The lack of R&D spend is a structural weakness — Bunge competes on scale, asset position, and logistics rather than technology or brand.

The current trade disruption environment (tariffs, China-US tensions) specifically advantages large-scale, geographically diversified processors who can arbitrage origin-to-destination flows. This is a near-term tailwind that partially explains the Q1 beat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Lock-Up Expiration (July 3, 2026) — HIGH PROBABILITY, MODERATE MAGNITUDE:**
Approximately 59 million shares (~30% of outstanding) become freely tradable. BC Investment Management already sold its 6.5M shares, establishing a precedent. Glencore is a commodity trader with no historical pattern of long-term strategic equity holdings. While CPP has longer holding horizons and Glencore has board seat incentives (contingent on 10% ownership), the probability of material selling pressure is significant.

Counter-argument: This is publicly known. The stock has rallied to within 6% of its 52-week high despite this knowledge, suggesting the market has partially priced the overhang. Glencore selling 31M shares in a stock that trades ~1.8M shares/day would require months of orderly selling or a block trade — either way, the magnitude of any single-day impact is contained.

**Assessment:** The lock-up is a real catalyst with defined timing but uncertain magnitude. It warrants position management (avoiding adding before July 3) rather than avoidance of the stock entirely.

**2. Negative Free Cash Flow and Capital Intensity:**
CapEx guidance of $1.5-1.7B for 2026 combined with $628M interest means the company needs approximately $2.5B in operating cash flow to achieve positive FCF. This is achievable if FY2026 EPS guidance is met (implying $1.7-1.8B net income on higher asset base), but it requires working capital discipline and commodity price cooperation.

**3. Integration Execution:**
Measurement period for Viterra purchase accounting remains open. Systems consolidation, SOX compliance, and talent retention (particularly Viterra's commercial trading teams) create execution risk. The Grain Merchandising goodwill cushion of only 9% is fragile.

**4. Commodity Cycle Normalization:**
Crush margins have normalized from 2022-23 extremes. The Q1 2026 beat was partially driven by trade disruption (which may be temporary if tariff tensions moderate). Structural overcapacity in oilseed processing from renewable diesel investments threatens long-term margin recovery.

**5. China/Trade Policy:**
US-China tariff tensions directly threaten soybean trade flows. While Bunge's geographic diversification partially insulates (South American origin to China avoids US tariffs), any prolonged disruption to global trade reduces overall system throughput.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term IV of 35.9% sits 4.8pp above the 31.1% one-year HV — modestly rich, consistent with the market pricing the lock-up expiration and Q2 earnings uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.41 is low, indicating limited institutional hedging despite the known lock-up catalyst. The flat term structure (35.9% near vs. 36.6% far) suggests no acute near-term event is being priced beyond normal volatility. The 6-month put skew of -5.2% shows mild downside protection demand for October, capturing the lock-up window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (Target: $140-150):**
- Viterra synergies accelerate beyond $250M target
- Trade disruption persists, benefiting multi-origin merchandising margins
- Q2-Q4 2026 EPS track above $9.50 guidance midpoint
- Lock-up selling is orderly and limited (Glencore holds for board seats, CPP holds for yield)
- Biofuels JV with Chevron generates visible incremental earnings
- Multiple re-rates from 13x toward 15x as execution is demonstrated

**Bear Case (Target: $95-110):**
- Lock-up expiration produces aggressive selling by Glencore (block trades at discount)
- Crush margins compress further as capacity additions outpace demand
- Grain Merchandising goodwill impaired ($593M at risk with 9% cushion)
- Working capital demands prevent positive FCF in 2026
- Trade normalization (US-China deal) removes merchandising margin tailwind
- Stock drifts toward 10-11x forward earnings on reduced confidence

Note: The bear case target of $95-110 is above the low analyst target of $116 on all but the most extreme scenarios. A $95 price would require 10x forward earnings on reduced estimates — possible but requiring multiple simultaneous negative developments. The prior AVOID recommendation's bear case of $85-100 implied a degree of confidence in thesis deterioration that the current evidence does not support, given three credit upgrades, positive estimate momentum, and 8/9 bullish analysts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Lock-up expiration passes (post-July 3, 2026) with less than 20M shares sold by former Viterra holders — eliminates the largest known overhang
2. FY2026 FCF turns positive (likely visible in Q2/Q3 reporting) — demonstrates Viterra acquisition can be self-financing
3. Stock pulls back to $105-115 on lock-up selling — would compress multiple to 11-12x rising estimates, creating asymmetric entry

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Glencore announces block sale of >15M shares at discount — signals no confidence and creates multi-week selling pressure
2. Q2 2026 results miss, forcing guidance cut back toward $8.00-$8.50 — would invalidate the Q1 beat thesis
3. Grain Merchandising goodwill impairment — signals Viterra overpayment and destroys earnings credibility
4. Working capital blow-up requiring emergency debt issuance beyond existing facilities

**Reassessment timeline:** Post-July 3, 2026 lock-up expiration (8 weeks) is the primary decision point. Secondary: Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July).

## Conclusion

Bunge presents a balanced risk/reward at current levels. The positive case — rising estimates, strong Q1 beat, scale advantages in a disrupted trade environment, reasonable forward valuation — is offset by concrete near-term risks including the lock-up expiration, negative free cash flow, elevated leverage, and fragile goodwill cushions.

The prior AVOID recommendation was too aggressive given that: (1) estimates are demonstrably rising (+14.7% in 30 days), not falling; (2) three independent credit agencies upgraded the company with full access to post-Viterra financials; (3) the Q1 2026 beat was substantial and management raised guidance; and (4) the stock trades at only 13.6x forward earnings — a level that doesn't require optimism to justify.

A BUY recommendation is premature given the July 3 lock-up expiration — a defined, near-term catalyst that could create material selling pressure and a better entry point. The rational positioning is to wait for this catalyst to resolve. If the stock pulls back to $105-115 on lock-up selling, or if the lock-up passes without material dislocation, the thesis should be reassessed for upgrade.

HOLD at current levels. The 2.2% dividend yield provides modest compensation for waiting.
