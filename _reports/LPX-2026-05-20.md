## Executive Summary

Louisiana-Pacific Corporation is a building materials company in the middle of a multi-year strategic transformation from commodity OSB production to differentiated engineered wood siding. The Siding segment (62% of 2025 revenue) generates 26-28% EBITDA margins and shows pricing power, while OSB (31% of revenue) has collapsed to breakeven or losses. The company is spending $390-400M in 2026 capex to convert mills, compressing free cash flow during a housing downturn.

The stock has declined 29% over the trailing year to $70.11, now trading at 57x trailing earnings with a $4.9B market cap. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen sharply: FY2026 EPS cut from $3.53 to $1.95 over 90 days (-45%), and FY2027 from $4.97 to $3.84 (-23%). At 36x forward FY2026 earnings or 18x FY2027, the stock is not cheap relative to its current earnings trajectory, though the long-term Siding transformation retains strategic merit.

The fundamental question is whether the market is correctly pricing cyclical weakness or whether the transformation story offers asymmetric upside once housing recovers and new Siding capacity comes online (2027-2028). With negative operating cash flow in Q1 2026, accelerating estimate downgrades, OSB losses, and elevated mortgage rates suppressing starts, the near-term risk/reward does not favor new capital deployment.

## Company Overview

Louisiana-Pacific operates 20+ manufacturing facilities across North and South America, producing engineered wood building products. The business has two primary segments:

**Siding (62% of revenue):** LP SmartSide engineered wood siding, the largest such platform in North America. Products include primed trim & siding, pre-finished ExpertFinish, and outdoor building solutions. Over 50% of demand comes from repair & remodeling and off-site structures (sheds), providing some insulation from new construction cycles.

**OSB (31% of revenue):** Oriented strand board structural panels. While LP offers value-added products (fire-rated, radiant barrier, weather barrier sheathing), pricing is predominantly commodity-driven.

**Other (7%):** South American operations in Brazil and Chile.

The strategic narrative centers on converting OSB mill capacity to Siding production, permanently shifting the earnings mix toward higher-margin, less cyclical revenue streams.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue peaked at $3.85B during the 2022 lumber supercycle, declined to $2.58B in 2023, recovered to $2.94B in 2024, then fell again to $2.71B in 2025 (-8% YoY). Q1 2026 revenue of $574M implies an annualized run rate of ~$2.3B (-21% vs prior year).

The earnings collapse is stark:
- FY2024 net income: $420M (~$5.80/share)
- FY2025 net income: $146M (~$2.08/share)
- Q1 2026 net income: $27M (annualized ~$1.50/share)

Trailing EPS of $2.08 at a $70.11 price yields the 57x P/E. Forward consensus of $1.95 for FY2026 puts the stock at 36x forward, and $3.84 for FY2027 at 18x.

### Segment Economics

The dual-business dynamic is critical:

**Siding:** 2025 revenue $1.69B (+8%), EBITDA $444M (+14%), margin 26.3%. In Q1 2026, pricing grew +9% but volumes fell -18% (weather + macro), yielding $101M EBITDA at a 28% margin. Full-year 2026 guidance: $1.64-1.66B revenue. Siding is holding margins through pricing power even as volumes compress.

**OSB:** 2025 revenue $832M (-30%), EBITDA $7M. Q1 2026 revenue $168M (-37%), EBITDA -$12M (operating loss). OSB pricing declined 19-31% through the period. At current pricing, OSB destroys value.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is conservative relative to the cyclical nature of the business:
- Cash: $164M (March 2026)
- Long-term debt: $348M (3.625% Senior Notes due 2029)
- Net debt/equity: ~0.20x
- Undrawn revolver: $750M (extended to 2032)
- Current ratio: 2.78

However, the $350M notes maturing in 2029 will need refinancing at materially higher rates (likely 5-6% vs current 3.625%), adding ~$7-9M annually in interest expense.

### Cash Flow Compression

2025 FCF: $91M (3.4% margin), depressed by $291M capex. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was -$38M (seasonal inventory build + low earnings). 2026 capex guided at $390-400M (2.8x depreciation) means FCF will be minimal or negative for the full year absent an OSB price recovery.

Share repurchases: paused in Q1 2026. Dividend: $1.20/year (1.7% yield), appears safe given revolver liquidity but barely covered by current earnings.

## Growth Analysis

### The Siding Conversion Thesis

The core growth thesis is sound in concept: each OSB-to-Siding mill conversion permanently shifts ~$100-200M of revenue from near-zero-margin commodity production to 26-28% EBITDA margin differentiated product. The Hines, Oregon conversion and other projects, when complete (likely 2027-2028), should meaningfully expand Siding capacity.

Long-term Siding growth drivers:
- Market share gains from vinyl and fiber cement (easier installation in a labor-constrained market)
- ExpertFinish mix-up (16% of revenue, 10% of volume — price/mix opportunity)
- Carbon-negative positioning as building codes tighten
- R&R demand less cyclical than new construction

### Near-Term Growth Impairments

However, near-term growth is severely impaired:
- Single-family housing starts -6% in both 2025 and Q1 2026
- Siding volumes -18% in Q1 2026 despite pricing gains
- OSB at loss levels with no recovery catalyst visible while housing remains weak
- Estimate revisions are catastrophically negative: current quarter -30% (30 days), -36% (90 days); next quarter -46% (30 days), -52% (90 days)

The speed of downward revisions indicates analysts are still catching down to reality. Consensus likely remains too optimistic even at current depressed levels.

### Housing Dependency

With 30-year mortgage rates elevated and affordability at multi-decade lows, the single-family construction cycle appears unlikely to recover in the near term. LP's management acknowledged "increasingly volatile macro backdrop" and geopolitical uncertainty in Q1 guidance.

The FY2027 estimate of $3.84 requires an OSB recovery and full contribution from new Siding capacity. Both are plausible but uncertain. At $70.11, you're paying 18x uncertain recovery earnings — reasonable if the recovery materializes, expensive if it doesn't.

## Competitive Landscape

### Siding Market Position (Strong)

LP SmartSide holds the leading position in engineered wood siding in North America with meaningful advantages:
- Proprietary treatment technology (moisture, termite, fungal resistance)
- Carbon-negative manufacturing (differentiator as ESG codes tighten)
- Installation ease vs fiber cement (James Hardie) — critical advantage given persistent labor shortages
- Growing pre-finished capability reduces on-site labor
- Consistent share gains against vinyl, fiber cement, and traditional wood

### Competitive Threats

- **James Hardie (JHX.AX):** Dominant in fiber cement, investing heavily in North American capacity. Larger company with established brand.
- **Vinyl siding:** Cheaper alternative, though losing share on aesthetics and durability.
- **New entrants:** Barriers exist (proprietary treatment, mill investment, distribution relationships) but are not insurmountable.

### OSB Market Position (Weak)

OSB is a commodity market with minimal differentiation. LP's Structural Solutions (FlameBlock, WeatherLogic) provide some premium, but pricing is dictated by supply/demand. Industry capacity additions have pressured pricing. LP's strategy of converting mills out of OSB effectively concedes this market to competitors over time.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Housing cycle deterioration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** Further weakening beyond current -6% starts would compress Siding volumes below management's $1.64-1.66B guidance range.

2. **Estimate revisions haven't bottomed (MEDIUM-HIGH probability):** With consensus still cutting (-45% over 90 days and accelerating), the downgrade cycle may have further to run. Q2 guidance of $80-85M Siding EBITDA implies continued sequential weakness.

3. **Capex cycle execution risk (MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact):** $680M invested over 2025-2026 in conversions. If new capacity ramps into weak demand, utilization rates will be poor and ROI impaired. Worst case: converted capacity sits underutilized while the company has lost OSB optionality.

4. **Refinancing risk in 2029 (LOW-MEDIUM probability):** Not existential given the $750M undrawn revolver, but incrementally negative to earnings.

5. **Tariff escalation (MEDIUM probability):** Current $8M tariff cost (2025) is manageable. However, the current trade environment could escalate. Canadian operations face potential retaliatory actions.

## Conclusion

Louisiana-Pacific's long-term strategy of converting from commodity OSB to differentiated Siding is sound and well-executed at the segment level. The Siding business alone, generating $444M in EBITDA at 26%+ margins with consistent share gains, would command a premium multiple. The problem is timing: the stock sits at 36x depressed forward earnings with estimates still falling, OSB losing money, housing in decline, rates elevated, and a massive capex cycle consuming all cash flow.

The director's $1.7M open-market purchase at $85.49 (February 2026) provides some conviction signal, but the stock has fallen another 18% since then as the fundamental picture deteriorated further. The transformation thesis requires patience — likely 12-18 months before new capacity contributes and housing potentially recovers. At current levels, the risk/reward is approximately symmetrical: ~30-36% upside to bull case vs ~17-22% downside to bear case. This does not meet the threshold for conviction in either direction given the severe estimate deterioration, ongoing cash consumption, and elevated macro uncertainty in the housing-exposed Materials sector.

HOLD — wait for estimate stabilization or further price compression before committing capital.
