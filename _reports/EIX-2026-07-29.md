**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Edison International is a holding company for Southern California Edison (SCE), a regulated electric transmission and distribution utility serving roughly 15 million people across a 50,000-square-mile territory in central, coastal, and Southern California. On the surface it is a conventional rate-base growth story: management has reaffirmed a $38–41B capital plan through 2030, a 5–7% long-term core EPS growth rate, 2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90–$6.20, and no new equity issuance through 2030. At ~$79 the stock trades near 13x the midpoint of 2026 core EPS, a wide discount to the electric-utility peer median of ~21.7x. That discount is not a mispricing; it is the market's estimate of the still-unquantified Eaton Fire liability plus the political fragility of California's wildfire liability backstop.

The single fact that has shifted since prior reviews is that causation of the January 2025 Eaton Fire is now largely conceded: new evidence points to SCE's idle transmission line as the ignition source, and SCE has moved toward acknowledging it. The debate is no longer whether SCE is at fault but how large the bill is, how much flows through the AB 1054 Wildfire Fund versus shareholders, and whether Sacramento replenishes that Fund without punitively penalizing utility equity holders. SCE's voluntary compensation program has already offered more than $700M to 10,000+ participants, a partial down payment against total plausible damages that could run into the many billions. Under California's inverse-condemnation doctrine, SCE bears strict liability for property damage regardless of negligence, which is what makes the total exposure so large and so hard to bound.

We maintain a HOLD. The ~13x forward multiple reasonably compensates a patient holder for the tail risk while the ~4.5% dividend provides carry, but the setup lacks the clean asymmetry a BUY requires. Rising political hostility in Sacramento, including a revealed utility-funded advocacy campaign and editorial pressure against "rewarding utilities," is trending the wrong way for a clean Fund replenishment, and the valuation discount already reflects the more benign outcomes. The stock has recovered roughly 53% from its July 2025 low near $51 as fault-recovery optimism has firmed, which has compressed the risk/reward rather than improved it.

## Company Overview

SCE is a rate-regulated monopoly electric T&D utility. Earnings are driven by the rate base — invested capital on which SCE earns a return authorized by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) for distribution and by FERC for transmission. Growth comes from capital deployment into grid hardening (wildfire mitigation), electrification and EV-driven load growth, and reliability upgrades. The unregulated subsidiary Trio (formerly Edison Energy) provides commercial and industrial decarbonization advisory services and is immaterial to the consolidated earnings story.

The competitive "moat" is the regulatory franchise: SCE faces no direct competition for electricity delivery within its territory. What distinguishes EIX from every other large U.S. investor-owned utility is its jurisdiction. California combines the nation's most severe climate-driven wildfire exposure with inverse condemnation, a legal doctrine imposing strict liability on utilities for property damage caused by their equipment even absent negligence. PG&E's 2019 bankruptcy is the direct precedent and the reason the state created the AB 1054 wildfire fund. That fund (~$21B) is meant to backstop utility wildfire liabilities, subject to a prudency standard and a liability cap tied to prudent conduct and retention of a safety certification.

## Financial Analysis

The reported GAAP earnings for EIX are noisy and, for this name, misleading. Year-over-year swings in GAAP net income are dominated by wildfire-related charges, Wildfire Fund and insurance accounting, and associated tax effects. Core EPS is the operationally meaningful metric. Q1 2026 core EPS came in at $1.42, described as on track. Reuters attributed the quarter's profit growth primarily to higher authorized electricity rates, consistent with a rate-base-driven model.

The earnings-surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters, including a 26.3% beat in Q4 2025 ($1.86 vs. $1.47 estimate) and a 7.1% beat in Q1 2026. That gives reasonable confidence in near-term consensus. Estimate revisions are essentially flat — current-year consensus of $6.12 has not moved over 30 or 90 days, and next-year ($6.51) is likewise static. Next-quarter estimates have been trimmed 10% over 90 days to $1.94. There is no positive revision momentum here; the growth is steady rather than inflecting.

The balance sheet is the core financial-health concern. EIX carries very high leverage — holding-company debt stacked on top of utility debt — which is structurally normal for the sector but elevated even by utility standards given the wildfire liabilities. The negative-free-cash-flow reality of a heavy-capex utility is standard for the model, but here it collides with a potential multi-billion-dollar cash outflow for Eaton Fire settlements. Management's commitment to no new equity through 2030 is the linchpin claim. If Eaton cash outflows materially exceed Wildfire Fund reimbursements, that commitment, the dividend, and credit-rating stability all come under simultaneous pressure. The SCE Wildfire Recovery Compensation Program has ramped from $100M paid in April to more than $700M offered by June, which indicates management is proactively settling to control litigation costs — a signal it views direct settlement as cheaper than protracted inverse-condemnation litigation, but also a signal that cash is flowing out ahead of any confirmed recovery.

We score Financial Health 4.5. The rate-base model and reaffirmed guidance provide a floor, but the combination of high structural leverage, negative FCF, an unquantified multi-billion-dollar liability, and a "no equity through 2030" pledge that is contingent on favorable Fund recovery keeps this below neutral.

## Growth Analysis

The regulated growth engine is genuine. The capital plan supports rate-base expansion and the 5–7% core EPS growth target. California's electrification agenda, EV load growth, and mandated wildfire-mitigation spending all provide durable, recoverable rate-base drivers — the same data-center-and-electrification demand tailwind visible across the regulated-utility sector, though for SCE the dominant capex driver remains grid hardening. Load growth from data centers is less of a specific catalyst here than at some Midwestern utilities.

Per-share growth quality is capped by the financing risk the wildfire liability creates. The 5–7% target assumes the no-equity commitment holds; a dilutive raise to fund settlements would immediately compress per-share growth. Flat-to-slightly-negative consensus revisions argue the market does not yet see acceleration. The four-beat track record supports the near-term guidance, but the guidance itself is mid-single-digit growth. We score Growth Prospects 5.5 — a credible, recoverable rate-base plan, discounted for the financing overhang that could interrupt per-share compounding.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$79 and ~13x 2026 core EPS midpoint, EIX trades at a roughly 40% discount to the electric-utility peer median P/E of 21.7x (FE 25.4x, PPL 21.7x, ES 14.6x, WEC 22.5x, PEG 17.4x). The dividend yield of ~4.5% is above the peer-median 3.4% and near the high end of the group (ES 4.6%). On headline metrics the stock looks cheap.

The discipline required here is to recognize that the discount is doing exactly what it should. A cheap multiple on a regulated utility with a specific, unquantified, multi-billion-dollar tail liability is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, unless there is an identifiable catalyst that resolves the overhang favorably. The catalyst exists in theory — a clean AB 1054 Fund replenishment and a favorable prudency/cost-recovery determination would re-rate the stock toward peer multiples — but its resolution is a political variable subject to intensifying public backlash, not a mechanical rate-case outcome. JPMorgan's $75 target (raised from $74 in May) sits below the current price, and analyst consensus mean is $76.46 versus a $79 spot, meaning the stock trades above the average sell-side estimate. The sentiment trend is degrading, and the analyst book (1 strong buy, 5 buy, 8 hold, 3 sell) is defensive.

The valuation is not a floor here because this is a static-estimate regulated utility whose discount reflects a genuine, quantifiable-in-principle liability rather than a high-momentum stock with rising estimates. The consensus median of $79.50 essentially matches the current price. We score Valuation 5.0 — the discount fairly compensates for the tail risk but does not offer the asymmetric upside a higher score requires. Cheapness alone, absent a catalyst with a defined timeline and a favorable base case, does not warrant reward.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, SCE's competitive position is defined by its regulatory franchise rather than market share. Within its territory it faces no delivery competition. The relevant comparison is against other California IOUs (PG&E, Sempra/SDG&E), all of which share the inverse-condemnation exposure, and against the broader regulated-utility group, against which SCE's fundamentals are sound but its jurisdiction is uniquely penalized.

Relative to PG&E, SCE has stronger financial footing and has avoided bankruptcy, but the Eaton Fire has placed it in the same category of scrutiny. The entire California IOU investment case now hinges on whether the state maintains a functioning liability backstop. On the operational side SCE is executing — grid hardening is progressing, load growth is recoverable, and rate-case outcomes have supported the profit trajectory. But the franchise's political durability is deteriorating: the utility-funded advocacy controversy, editorial hostility toward utility dividends post-fire, and survivor protests all raise the risk that the CPUC and legislature turn less accommodative on ROE, cost recovery, and Fund terms. We score Competitive Position 5.5, in line with the prior review — a durable regulated franchise offset by a worsening political environment that directly threatens the terms of that franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by a single idiosyncratic overhang, and it is the reason the stock scores poorly on this dimension.

**Eaton Fire liability.** The fire was one of the most destructive in California history. Under inverse condemnation, SCE faces strict liability. The quantum is unknown and could easily exceed early estimates.

**Wildfire Fund adequacy and prudency.** The Fund backstops liabilities only if SCE retains its safety certification and is judged to have acted prudently. Two questions remain open: whether SCE's conduct meets the prudency standard (complicated by reports that SCE withheld investigation documents under attorney-client privilege, drawing regulator criticism), and whether the Fund is large enough given the Eaton claims plus future fire risk. Legislative debate over replenishing the Fund is active in 2026 and is the single most important catalyst for the entire California IOU group.

**Political backlash.** This is escalating, not fading. A "fire-survivor" advocacy campaign was revealed to be utility-funded, and public/editorial hostility toward utility profits and dividends post-fire raises the risk of a politically punitive Fund reform. Because the AB 1054 backstop that underpins the valuation is fundamentally a political variable, this is a genuine idiosyncratic risk a standardized screen would miss.

**Financing risk.** Large capex plus large potential settlement outflows create refinancing and equity-dilution risk. The higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the negative-FCF financing model. If the no-equity pledge breaks, the dividend and credit rating are exposed.

We score Risk Factors 3.5. The dominant overhang is specific, material, unquantified, and tied to a deteriorating political variable with binary outcomes — the profile that warrants a sub-4 score. Much but not all of the risk is priced in the discount; the tail (a punitive Fund outcome or a settlement that forces dilution) is not.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$100+).** Sacramento replenishes and expands the AB 1054 Wildfire Fund without punitively penalizing shareholders; SCE retains its safety certification and secures a favorable prudency determination, capping its Eaton exposure and enabling Fund reimbursement; the no-equity-through-2030 commitment holds; and the stock re-rates from ~13x toward the peer-median ~20x on the reaffirmed 5–7% growth plan. A move to ~18x on $6.05 core EPS implies roughly $109. This requires the political environment to improve and the settlement to land within Fund-absorbable bounds.

**Bear case (~$55–62).** Eaton settlement quantum materially exceeds Fund reimbursement; a prudency dispute (aggravated by the document-withholding friction) forces shareholders to absorb costs; political backlash produces a punitive Fund reform or ROE pressure; and SCE is forced into dilutive equity, pressuring the dividend and credit rating. The stock re-rates toward the low end of its 52-week range and the JPMorgan-style skeptical valuation, roughly the $55–62 zone that analyst low targets ($62) anchor.

**Base case (~$79–84).** The overhang persists unresolved through 2026; core EPS grows at the low end of the target; the multiple stays discounted; and total return is carried primarily by the ~4.5% dividend plus modest rate-base-driven appreciation. This is essentially where the stock trades today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction, and the setup is a bet on a politically-determined liability outcome that is not yet resolvable.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoints are (1) each quarterly print for wildfire-charge accruals and Wildfire Fund receivable recognition, (2) the 2026 California legislative session outcome on Fund replenishment/reform, and (3) any CPUC prudency/cost-recovery determination on the Eaton Fire.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed AB 1054 Fund replenishment/reform that preserves shareholder protection, removing the primary overhang.
- A defined Eaton settlement quantum that lands within Fund-absorbable bounds with a favorable prudency finding, allowing the stock to re-rate toward peer multiples.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that widens the discount enough to price the tail risk with genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A punitive legislative outcome or an adverse prudency determination that shifts material Eaton costs to shareholders.
- Abandonment of the no-new-equity-through-2030 commitment (a dilutive raise), or a dividend cut / credit-rating downgrade.
- Eaton settlement disclosures that materially exceed Fund reimbursement capacity in the quarterly financials.

## Conclusion

Edison International is a fundamentally sound regulated utility trapped inside a single, dominant, unquantified idiosyncratic risk. The forward multiple and yield fairly compensate a patient holder for that risk, but they do not offer the asymmetric upside that a BUY requires, and cheapness on its own is a warning rather than a signal when the discount reflects a genuine multi-billion-dollar tail liability. The developments of the past six months have clarified fault without resolving quantum, and the political environment — the true determinant of whether the AB 1054 backstop holds — is deteriorating.

The score trajectory (5.6 → 5.7 → 5.2, now 4.8) has been drifting lower, driven by declines in valuation, financial health, and competitive position. New data confirms rather than reverses that trend: causation is now conceded, cash is flowing out through the compensation program ahead of any confirmed recovery, and political hostility is escalating. This is the third consecutive HOLD, and we do not upgrade because the resolving catalyst — Fund reform and settlement quantum — remains unresolved and increasingly politicized. We would turn constructive on either a clean legislative/prudency outcome or a pullback into the low-$60s that restores asymmetry. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
