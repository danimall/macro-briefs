## Executive Summary

Insulet reported Q2 2026 on August 5 and the stock fell approximately 19% intraday. The results themselves were fine — revenue $801.7M, up 23.5%, ahead of roughly $787M expected, with EPS of $1.66 against a consensus near $1.44-1.45. What moved the stock was guidance: management cut full-year US Omnipod growth to 17%-19% from 20%-22%, attributing the change to a softer second half specifically in US sales, and guided Q3 revenue to $833.4M, below consensus. Against an estimated Q3 2025 revenue base that is not a filing fact (only FY2025 total and Q1 2025 are filing facts; the Q2 2025 figure is back-solved and approximate), the $833.4M Q3 guide implies a marked step down from Q2's 23.5% growth. Management simultaneously raised adjusted EPS growth guidance to above 30%.

That combination — revenue guidance down, earnings guidance up — is the central analytical puzzle. One reading is benign: a slower ramp in US type 2 adoption, with international growth of 35.5% and cost discipline more than covering it. The competing reading is not benign: two voluntary Medical Device Corrections ten weeks apart (March 12, internal tubing tear in Omnipod 5; May 26, insulin leaking and dose under-delivery spanning Omnipod 5, DASH and Classic), escalated by the FDA to Class I on approximately July 6 after injury reports, suppressing new US patient starts. For a business whose primary competitor is multiple daily injection therapy and whose growth depends on first-time pump adopters, a Class I designation attacks the new-starts funnel directly while leaving the installed base of 600,000-plus users relatively sticky. That is the pattern the guidance describes: US growth slowing but still 17%-19%, international accelerating.

No evidence available distinguishes the two hypotheses. The Q1 10-Q was filed May 6, three weeks before the second correction, so the only quantified recall cost in any filing ($11.7M accrued, roughly $30M total) covers the March event only. The scope and cost of the May correction, and the financial consequence of the Class I designation, are not quantified anywhere I can verify. At $135.20 the stock trades at 20.9x current-year consensus EPS of $6.48, 16.8x next-year $8.05, roughly 15.2x trailing EV/adjusted EBITDA and about 12x a forward EBITDA estimate — inexpensive for a business compounding revenue above 20% with 24% EBITDA margins and a recurring consumable model. The reason it is inexpensive is an unresolved operational control failure, not a mispricing I can specify. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Insulet sells a single platform. The Omnipod is a tubeless, disposable, self-adhesive insulin pump worn up to three days and then replaced. Because there is no durable pump to purchase, the consumable Pod carries the entire economics, and the company distributes in the US through the pharmacy benefit channel rather than durable medical equipment, a structural advantage in reimbursement breadth (coverage in all 50 states) and recurring revenue predictability.

The product line comprises Omnipod 5 (automated insulin delivery with the dosing algorithm embedded in the Pod itself, receiving glucose values by Bluetooth from a third-party CGM), Omnipod DASH (non-automated, handheld controller) and Classic Omnipod, which the FY2025 10-K states is being phased out. Omnipod products are sold in 25 countries, with Omnipod 5 available in 19 following February 2026 Middle East launches. Headcount reached roughly 5,400 at year-end 2025, a 38% increase in one year. The patent estate runs to more than 1,000 issued patents and 700-plus applications with expirations from 2026 to 2047, covering the drive system, cannula insertion and algorithms.

Penetration is the growth engine. The 10-K estimates roughly 6 million people with type 1 diabetes in served countries, 6 million with insulin-requiring type 2, and 3 million US basal-only type 2 patients — against pump penetration of roughly 40% of the US type 1 population and 25% internationally, and "an even smaller portion" of type 2. FDA clearance for an expanded Omnipod 5 type 2 indication in adults arrived in August 2024, and Insulet abandoned the separate basal-only Omnipod GO product to concentrate the type 2 push on Omnipod 5.

The critical structural dependency: the CGM is sold separately by Dexcom (G6/G7) and Abbott (Libre 2 Plus, with Libre 3 Plus in limited market release). Insulet's automated system is non-functional without a sensor it does not own, and the 10-K states plainly that loss of those integration rights "could impair the functionality of our products."

### The GAAP net income decline is an accounting artifact

GAAP net income fell from $418.3M (FY2024) to $247.1M (FY2025) while operating income rose from $308.9M to $473.8M. Two discrete items explain the reversal entirely: FY2024 included a $182.5M one-time tax benefit from releasing the deferred tax asset valuation allowance (FY2024 effective rate was a 39.3% benefit; FY2025 a 27.2% expense), and FY2025 included a $123.9M loss on extinguishment from retiring the convertible notes. Neither reflects operating deterioration.

Two data-integrity points that materially affect screening: the XBRL feed tags the $123.9M extinguishment loss as FY2025 interest expense. The 10-K MD&A states interest expense was $59.4M in 2025 versus $42.7M in 2024, with net interest expense of $24.7M after $34.7M of interest income. Any model built on the raw tag shows interest expense nearly tripling on a falling debt balance — a false distress signal. Separately, the commonly reported "debt/equity of 1.11" is total liabilities divided by equity ($1,675.2M / $1,515.2M). Interest-bearing debt to equity is $949.2M / $1,515.2M = 0.63.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is unit conversion, not price. Penetration of 40% in US type 1 and 25% internationally against a served type 1 population of roughly 6 million, plus a barely-tapped insulin-requiring type 2 population of similar size, is a long runway on Insulet's own estimates. International is delivering it — +44.1% in FY2025, +59.4% in Q1 2026 (though 14.2 points was FX), +35.5% in Q2 — and now represents more than a quarter of revenue.

Pipeline: limited market release of Omnipod 5 algorithm enhancements including a 100 mg/dL target set point; limited market release with Abbott Libre 3 Plus; first patient enrolled in the EVOLVE pivotal study for a fully closed-loop type 2 system supporting a planned 510(k) submission in 2027; Omnipod Discover data analytics platform launched in 2026. R&D at 11.8% of revenue and rising funds this.

Two headwinds temper the picture. First, gross-to-net erosion. US pharmacy rebate provisions charged against gross sales went $367.3M (2023) → $452.7M (2024) → $654.7M (2025). Derived rebate rates: roughly 23.1% of gross US sales in FY2024 rising to roughly 25.4% in FY2025 — about 230bps of annual deterioration, with rebates growing 44.6% against 27.2% net US revenue growth. Unit and patient growth is therefore stronger than headline net revenue suggests, but Insulet is conceding an increasing share of gross price to PBMs and payors, and consolidated gross margin only expanded because international mix and manufacturing efficiency outran the drag. That offset is finite. The estimate is also judgmental — Medicare Part D benefit redesign is a plausible external driver, and prior-period true-ups can distort a single year.

Second, the US deceleration itself. Guidance of 17%-19% US growth against 20%-22% previously, on approximately 71% of revenue, is roughly a 2-point haircut to consolidated growth. Estimate revisions as captured were essentially flat before the print — current year +2.1% over 90 days to $6.48, next year −0.2% to $8.05, next quarter −2.1% — so consensus had not anticipated the reset. The current-year figure is broadly consistent with the raised adjusted EPS guidance (management's ">30% growth" on a reconstructed FY2025 adjusted base of roughly $4.99 implies ≥$6.49, though that base is my reconstruction, not a company-disclosed figure). The next-year $8.05 is more exposed: it requires roughly 24% EPS growth on a revenue base likely growing mid-to-high teens, which demands continued margin expansion against a cost-of-quality overhang that extends into 2027.

Drug Delivery is running off — FY2025 $34.1M (−12.3%), Q1 2026 $3.3M versus $14.9M — with the Amgen agreement expiring December 2028. At 1.3% of revenue this is immaterial to valuation, but the "second platform" optionality should carry no weight.

**Growth Prospects: 6.5.** Low-20s revenue growth in 2026 with 30%-plus adjusted earnings growth is good in absolute terms and the TAM runway is real. The score is held down by the first guidance cut in years, an unresolved cause, a 230bp annual pricing concession, and 2027 consensus that looks exposed.

Peer context therefore provides no constraint in either direction, and the valuation case has to stand on absolute arithmetic.

On absolute arithmetic it is inexpensive. 16.8x next-year on 24% consensus EPS growth is a PEG of roughly 0.7. Roughly 12x forward EV/EBITDA for a razor/razorblade consumable business with 24% EBITDA margins, 600,000-plus recurring users, a patent moat and net leverage below 0.75x is a multiple ordinarily associated with low-growth med-tech.

Three qualifications keep this from being a high valuation score. First, next-year consensus of $8.05 predates the guidance cut and is likely to drift toward $7.50 as analysts absorb 17%-19% US growth carrying into 2027; at $7.50 the forward multiple is 18.0x, not 16.8x. Second, the analyst target set — mean $235.54, median $240, high $360, low $172 against a $135.20 price — is stale. These are pre-print numbers, and Evercore had already cut to $180 on July 6. A consensus target well above spot is not independent confirmation of value; it is a lagging distribution that has not yet been marked. Third, a compressed multiple following a quality crisis is the classic value-trap configuration. Cheapness here is explained by a live, unresolved operational failure with an unquantified remediation cost. That is a reason the multiple is low, not evidence that the market is wrong.

What would make it a mispricing: evidence that the US deceleration is type 2 ramp timing rather than recall-driven suppression of new starts. That evidence is not available.

**Valuation: 6.5.** Attractive absolute multiples on a durable model, but the discount is explained rather than anomalous, and the forward estimates anchoring it are pre-revision.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat has three verifiable components and one that has just been damaged.

**Form factor and IP.** Tubeless with no durable pump is a differentiated architecture, protected by more than 1,000 issued patents, and the in-Pod algorithm is a design choice competitors cannot easily copy without infringing.

**The EOFlow injunction.** On December 3, 2024 a unanimous jury found four Insulet trade secrets valid and misappropriated, awarding $452M ($170M compensatory plus $282M exemplary). On April 24, 2025 the district court entered final judgment with a worldwide permanent injunction barring defendants from using, selling, distributing or seeking regulatory approval for products built on Insulet's trade secrets, requiring assignment of certain patent applications to Insulet, disgorgement of break-up fees EOFlow received from Medtronic in connection with a contemplated acquisition, and compliance audits. Because the injunction removed double recovery, the monetary award was reduced to $59.4M, which Insulet has not recorded given EOFlow's appeal and uncertain ability to pay. The economically significant element is the injunction, not the cash: it removed a low-cost tubeless patch-pump competitor from the global market and blocked its regulatory pathway. Oral argument was heard January 5, 2026, making a decision a plausible near-term catalyst in either direction. A reversal would be a material negative not currently priced.

**Pharmacy channel.** Coverage across all 50 states through the pharmacy benefit rather than DME reduces payor friction and shortens the path from prescription to first Pod.

**What has deteriorated.** The 10-K is explicit that the primary competitor is multiple daily injection therapy, since most new Omnipod users come from injections. Growth therefore depends on persuading naive patients and their endocrinologists to adopt a device that delivers a drug where under-delivery causes hyperglycemia and over-delivery causes hypoglycemia. An FDA Class I designation — the agency's most serious, applied after injury reports, warning that continued use could result in serious injury or death — attacks that conversion decision directly. The mechanism is concrete: Class I recall → prescriber hesitancy on new starts → fewer US first-time adopters → US revenue growth decelerating in H2 while the installed base, which faces high switching friction, continues reordering. That is the shape of the guidance revision.

The competitive set is also becoming more focused. Medtronic's diabetes division is being spun into an independent public company, which historically produces a more aggressive competitor than a division inside a diversified conglomerate. Tandem Diabetes remains, and Beta Bionics — named explicitly in the 10-K as an emerging competitor — is now public and reporting. Vertex is named by name as a well-capitalized company pursuing a cure, a long-dated obsolescence risk for a type 1-centric franchise. GLP-1s are framed in the 10-K as a demand-delay risk (potentially slowing type 2 progression) rather than demand destruction, but Insulet's type 2 strategy and the GLP-1 wave target overlapping populations.

Two dependencies compound the picture: the CGM licensing relationships with Dexcom and Abbott, without which the automated system does not function, and the Glooko data-platform agreement expiring December 2026. Omnipod Discover, launched in 2026, plausibly insources part of that data layer, though it is described as providing "supplemental data," which is narrower than Glooko's clinician-facing function. Renewal remains the likelier base case.

**Competitive Position: 5.8.** Trajectory is deteriorating: a Class I recall in a trust-dependent consumable, US growth decelerating faster than international can offset in the largest market, 230bps of annual payor price concession, and a competitive set becoming more focused — against the offsetting positives of the EOFlow injunction and continued international share capture.

## Risk Assessment

**Manufacturing quality — the dominant risk.** The Q1 10-Q discloses a March correction for Omnipod 5 Pods with "a tear in their internal tubing that delivers insulin," with $11.7M accrued and roughly $30M total estimated, "more than half" in 2026 and the remainder in 2027. The 2027 portion is the informative detail: it "relates to incremental manual quality inspections expected to be performed until automated inspection systems are implemented." Insulet is bridging with manual inspection because automated detection for this failure mode does not exist on its lines. A second, broader correction followed ten weeks later on May 26, covering Omnipod 5, DASH and the Omnipod Insulin Management System for an issue that "could lead to the leaking of insulin and the under-delivery of doses" — a different root cause spanning three product generations, which is harder to attribute to a single lot or line. The FDA reportedly escalated to Class I on July 6. Neither the scope nor the cost of the second correction is quantified in any filing available, and the Class I designation itself is not confirmed in a filing. The unpriced tail is further FDA action — warning letter or consent decree — which would extend the new-starts overhang well beyond 2026.

**Securities litigation.** Class actions have been filed for purchasers from February 21, 2025, with an August 31, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline, alleging inadequate disclosure of manufacturing controls. The existence of the litigation is credible given the volume of consistent filings; the "$90-plus per share" damage characterization comes entirely from plaintiff-firm promotional material and is not evidence. Base case is a multi-year process settling within insurance plus some cash — a distraction and a modest tail, not existential.

**Concentration.** Nearly all revenue comes from one product platform. FY2025 revenue ran 27%/26%/25% through three distributors — 78% of total from three counterparties. Q1 2026 disclosed 21%/25%/19%, but a quarterly percentage is not comparable to an annual one, letter labels need not map to the same entities across filings, and receivable concentration for "A" actually rose to 28%. I will not characterize this as improving diversification. Substantially all US inventory sits at a single Massachusetts location, with European inventory primarily at one Netherlands third-party site.

**Section 232 tariffs.** Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security has an open national-security investigation into medical equipment imports "including medical devices such as insulin pumps." Management's stated position is no material 2026 gross-margin impact, but that if the current medical-device exemption is eliminated, tariffs "would have a material impact on our results of operations in future years." Insulet manufactures in Malaysia and on its own lines at a contract manufacturer's facility in China. This is a live binary controlled externally.

**Rate environment.** Insulet is well insulated on the debt side — $460M of the term loan swapped at 3.47%, no maturity before 2028, and 6.5% notes already termed to 2033 — so the exposure is multiple compression rather than cash interest.

**Risk Factors: 4.0.** The stack of unpriced or partially-priced binaries is dense: unquantified second-recall cost, possible further FDA escalation, the EOFlow appeal (two-sided), Section 232, Glooko expiry in four months, three-distributor concentration, single-site inventory, CGM licensing dependency. A drawdown from the 52-week high near $355 to $135 (roughly 62%) has priced the known recall and litigation; it has not priced the unquantified pieces.

### Options Market Signal

The options market corroborates caution on timing rather than accumulation. Near-term ATM IV of 67.5% sits 27 points above one-year realized volatility of 40.4%, at the 70th percentile within the chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (67.5% at one month versus 53.4% at six months) — the standard signature of imminent, event-driven stress rather than a structural regime shift. Nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.9%.

Two details matter more. Put/call open interest ratio has moved to 3.56 from 0.03 at the mid-July snapshot while the price fell 17.7% and ATM IV rose 11.8 points; the prior 0.03 reading looks anomalously low and I would not read the delta literally, but a 3.56 ratio means substantial downside protection is held in size. And the six-month tenor carries the most negative skew reading in the term structure at −10.9%, against a balanced −2.0% near-term — protection is concentrated at the tenor that spans Q3 and Q4 prints and the automated-inspection implementation window, which is where the recall thesis resolves.

Short interest of 7.6% of float with 3.1 days to cover is modest; this is not a crowded short, meaning neither a squeeze setup nor evidence that informed capital has broadly taken the bear side.

Rich near-term IV has one constructive implication: for an investor who wants exposure, selling cash-secured puts into 67.5% implied volatility is a better entry mechanic than buying stock outright.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $230 (+70%)

The August 5 revision is mix and timing: US type 2 adoption ramping slower than modeled, with the core type 1 base intact. Management raised adjusted EPS growth guidance above 30% in the same breath as cutting revenue growth, which is difficult to reconcile with a franchise losing patients. International compounds above 30% and crosses 30% of revenue. Automated inspection systems come online during 2027, the 2027 cost-of-quality line falls away, and warranty expense normalizes from the $14.2M quarterly run rate. The EOFlow injunction is upheld on appeal, permanently removing a low-cost tubeless competitor. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $8.50 on continued SG&A leverage; at 28x — still less than half the multiple the stock carried in 2025 — that is $238. Management deploys the remaining $115.4M repurchase authorization at prices near $135 rather than the $239.89 average paid in February.

### Bear Case — $98 (−28%)

The Class I recall has structurally damaged the US new-starts funnel. Endocrinologists route naive patients to Tandem, Beta Bionics or a newly independent Medtronic Diabetes while Insulet demonstrates a clean quality record over multiple quarters. US growth decelerates from 17%-19% to low teens in 2027. The FDA escalates to a warning letter, adding remediation cost and management distraction. Gross-to-net erosion continues at 200-plus basis points annually as PBMs press a supplier with a compliance problem. The second correction's cost, still unquantified, materially exceeds the $30M booked for the first. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at $6.50 against $8.05 consensus; at 15x that is $98. The Glooko renewal fails and the EOFlow judgment is reversed on appeal, restoring a low-cost competitor.

### What decides it

Whether US new patient starts recovered in Q3 and Q4. The available data does not distinguish the hypotheses — no transcript, no Q2 10-Q, no quantification of the second correction. That is why this is a HOLD at a valuation that would otherwise support a BUY.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 6.05, a modest decline from 6.2 and the fourth consecutive HOLD, but on a materially changed information set rather than a repeated thesis: the first US guidance cut in years landed today alongside a 19% price decline.

The price decline is not itself the reason for caution — at $135.20 the stock is demonstrably cheaper against unchanged-to-better earnings guidance than it was at $166.82. Management raised adjusted EPS growth guidance above 30% in the same release that cut revenue growth, and the balance sheet is unambiguously strong: net debt of $467.7M against roughly $800M of 2026 adjusted EBITDA, floating exposure hedged at 3.47%, nothing due before 2028, an undrawn $500M revolver, and springing-only covenants. The franchise economics — a disposable consumable, 600,000-plus recurring users, pharmacy-channel reimbursement in all 50 states, a competitor removed by permanent worldwide injunction — remain intact on the evidence available.

What prevents a BUY is that the reason for the discount is identifiable but not resolvable. Two voluntary corrections ten weeks apart, spanning three product generations, remediated by manual inspection until automated systems arrive in 2027, escalated by the FDA to its most serious classification after injury reports — followed six weeks later by a guidance cut attributed specifically to softer H2 US sales. That sequence is at least as consistent with a damaged new-patient funnel as with slower type 2 ramp timing, and nothing in the available filings, transcripts or reporting distinguishes them. The cost and scope of the second correction are quantified nowhere. Management quality carries the operational failure, the $300M accelerated repurchase executed at an average of $239.89 weeks before the first correction, and the absence of any executive open-market buying against two directors' constructive but weaker $900K of purchases at roughly $144.

A cheap multiple on a business with an unresolved quality problem and a pending regulatory escalation path is the configuration that most often disappoints. The valuation is real; so is the reason for it. The Q3 print in early November will resolve which one dominates.
