## Executive Summary

Martin Marietta is in the middle of the largest portfolio reconstruction in its history, and the market is charging it for the transition. Over eighteen months the company exited cement and Texas ready-mix via the QUIKRETE asset exchange, bought aggregates platforms in Missouri, Kansas, Virginia and British Columbia plus New Frontier Materials in St. Louis, and on June 27, 2026 signed a $13.5 billion cash-and-stock acquisition of Lhoist North America. Regulatory approvals for LNA were announced on August 5, 2026, which converts the leverage step-up from a probability into a near-certainty: management guides to approximately 3.7x combined net leverage at close against an amended covenant ladder of 4.75x / 4.25x / 3.75x, with Fitch (BBB+ Negative), Moody's (Baa2 under review) and S&P (BBB+ CreditWatch Negative) all in negative posture.

Simultaneously, the pricing power that earned MLM a mid-teens EV/EBITDA multiple has decelerated. Reported aggregates average selling price fell 2.0% year over year in Q2 2026; organic ASP rose 2.1%; the company's most favorable framing, organic mix-adjusted, was +3.7%. Cost of goods sold per ton rose 3.6% (roughly 2.1% excluding pass-through freight). Even the flattering measure produces the thinnest price/cost spread in several years and roughly half of FY2025's realized 6.9%. Organic tonnage grew only 2.3% — essentially all volume growth was purchased. Revenue rose 21.1% in the quarter while gross profit was flat (-0.2%) and diluted continuing-operations EPS fell 12%, the mechanical result of a $52M non-cash inventory step-up charge and aggregates depreciation, depletion and amortization rising from $125M to $166M ($2.70/ton versus $2.37). On a cash basis, aggregates gross profit per ton was $10.32 versus $10.53, down 2.0% — so the cash economics are close to intact and the GAAP damage is largely non-cash. Both readings matter: the cash franchise is fine, but the GAAP earnings power of an acquired ton is structurally lower than a legacy ton because the asset base keeps being re-based upward.

The stock has already derated 22.8% from its February 10 high of $710.97 while FY2026 revenue guidance was raised. That derating has moved valuation from expensive to approximately fair rather than to cheap. At $548.57, MLM trades at ~29.8x FY2026 guided adjusted continuing-operations EPS of ~$18.40, ~16.0x guided EV/EBITDA of $2.43B, and roughly 15.2-15.5x on pro forma combined EBITDA of ~$3.35B against ~$12.4B of pro forma net debt. Consensus next-year EPS has been cut 7.8% in the last thirty days and 6.8% over ninety. That combination — a fair multiple, negative revision momentum, rising leverage into a rising long end, and an undisclosed purchase-price allocation that determines whether LNA is +9% accretive or -9% dilutive to first-year GAAP EPS — supports HOLD rather than an accumulation call. We would not deploy new capital here.

## Company Overview

MLM produces construction aggregates (crushed stone, sand and gravel) across 29 states, Canada and The Bahamas, and, increasingly, upstream specialty minerals (magnesia, dolomitic lime, high-purity magnesia). The economics are freight-bound: aggregates rarely travel more than 30-50 miles by truck economically, which creates local quasi-monopolies around permitted reserve positions. Permitting friction is both the moat and a disclosed risk.

Segment reporting was re-cut in Q1 2026 following the QUIKRETE close, with all prior periods recast. Q2 2026: East Group revenue $972M / earnings from operations $295M; West Group $823M / $81M; Specialties $152M / $42M. By product line, aggregates generated $1,533M of revenue and $418M of gross profit; Other Building Materials (ready-mix, asphalt, paving) $303M / $34M; Specialties $152M / $50M. Specialties is now the highest-margin line at ~33% gross margin, which is the strategic logic behind the LNA pivot. Other Building Materials, at 4.3% H1 gross margin ($18M on $420M), is a plausible future divestiture candidate.

The stated strategy, "SOAR 2030," is to grow aggregates and build an upstream specialties business with "aggregates-like characteristics." LNA is the flagship execution: 20 quarries and production facilities, 45 distribution terminals, more than 2 billion tons of Sun Belt limestone reserves, TTM gross sales of $1.8B and Adjusted EBITDA of $786M at a 43.7% margin — well above MLM's ~33%.

## Financial Analysis

**The reported revenue series is misleading.** Unadjusted filings show $6.777B (FY2023) → $6.536B (FY2024) → $6.150B (FY2025), which screens as two years of contraction. The FY2025 10-K recast prior periods for the cement and Texas ready-mix discontinued operations; on a continuing-operations basis FY2025 revenue of $6,150M compares to $5,662M, growth of 8.6%. The same recast inflates FY2024 net income (which included the South Texas cement divestiture gain), which is why year-over-year change-based screens on this name are unreliable. Growth is real; it is also almost entirely acquired.

**FY2025 baseline:** revenue $6,150M, earnings from operations $1,437M (23.4% margin), net earnings attributable $1,137M, company-selected Adjusted EBITDA $2,302M, operating cash flow ~$1,800M against ~$800M of capex, aggregates shipments 198.5M tons (+3.8%) at +6.9% pricing and ~34% gross margin. Total debt $5,323M against $67M cash. Buybacks $450M at an average $494.04 — below today's price, so well-timed. Dividends ~$197M.

Management's disclosed 2.27x net leverage reconciles ($5,256M net debt ÷ $2,302M = 2.28x) but uses a denominator that includes the cement operations owned through February 2026. On a continuing-operations basis, using an estimated FY2025 continuing-ops Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2,110M (a derived figure, not disclosed), year-end leverage was nearer 2.5x.

**Q2 and H1 2026.** Revenue $1,947M (+21.1%), gross profit $495M (-0.2%), earnings from operations $372M (-10%), diluted continuing-operations EPS $4.26 (-12%), adjusted continuing-operations EPS $5.00 (+3%), Adjusted EBITDA $638M (+13%). Aggregates shipments 61.6Mt (+17.0% total, +2.3% organic) at $22.74 ASP (-2.0%). COGS-embedded DD&A rose 36% to $191M.

The West Group is where the acquired assets and the step-up land: revenue +28% to $823M but earnings from operations down to $81M from $92M, margin 14.4% → 9.8%. H1 West earnings were $38M versus $50M on revenue of $1,208M versus $958M. Corporate expense also expanded materially: -$46M in Q2 versus -$12M, and -$107M in H1 versus -$62M, including $18M of acquisition/divestiture/integration expense and a portfolio rationalization charge. Specialties is the offset: record Q2 revenue $152M and gross profit $50M (+39%), lime ASP +4.0% (+5.0% mix-adjusted) on +0.9% shipments — pricing power currently better than aggregates.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $339M versus $605M (-44%), driven by a step-up in cash taxes on the taxable gain from the February 2026 Midlothian/Texas ready-mix disposition and a $269M receivables build against 21% revenue growth, partly offset by inventories providing $61M versus consuming $42M. H1 capex was $314M versus $412M. H1 free cash flow of $25M should not be annualized: MLM's generation is heavily H2-weighted and H1 absorbed a one-time cash tax on a $1.98B divestiture gain. As a labeled sensitivity, if H2 operating cash flow matches last year's implied ~$1.2B against ~$350M of capex, FY2026 free cash flow lands near $875M — below FY2025 but not impaired.

Management disclosed a $350M annualized cash-flow improvement program targeted to run-rate by exit-2027, of which "more than $200 million" was said to be unlocked year to date. The H1 statements reconcile that figure almost exactly to capex deferral (-$98M) plus the inventory swing (+$103M) = ~$201M. That is legitimate cash management ahead of a $7B funding need, but it is timing and investment deferral, not margin. A portion of the broader program may be structurally durable given the smaller post-divestiture footprint; no disclosure separates the durable from the timing component.

**Balance sheet at 6/30/2026.** Cash $120M, PP&E net $13,101M (up $2.8B in six months at stepped-up fair values — the source of the DD&A drag), intangibles $4,524M, total assets $21,305M, equity $11,548M. Current maturities of long-term debt jumped to $860M from $30M, reconciling to the $560M trade receivable securitization facility maturing September 16, 2026 plus $300M of notes; unused facility capacity was $742M. Total debt $5,951M less cash gives ~$5,839M net debt; against TTM continuing-operations Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2,228M, net leverage is ~2.62x — already above the top of the historical 2.0-2.5x target range before LNA. Interest coverage is ~5.9x (TTM EBIT $1,379M ÷ ~$232M interest), falling to roughly 3.25x pro forma under reasonable LNA assumptions.

A screening Altman Z of 1.92 is unreliable — its EBIT/assets component was returned as 0.0 against $1,437M of operating income. Recomputing on 6/30/26 data yields Z ≈ 3.10, but 65% of that comes from the market-equity term, making it a share-price barometer rather than a solvency test. Pro forma for ~$7B of new debt plus a likely step-up deferred tax liability, Z falls to roughly 2.2-2.4. For a reserve-backed extractive business the relevant tests are net leverage, coverage and covenant headroom, all of which degrade materially.

**FY2026 guidance (reaffirmed July 30).** Revenue raised to $7.20-7.40B; Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations reaffirmed at $2.36-2.50B (midpoint $2.43B), excluding any LNA contribution. Revenue raised while EBITDA was only reaffirmed implies a modest expected margin reduction, consistent with acquisition mix. The company's own guidance bridge implies GAAP continuing-operations EPS of roughly $17.30 and adjusted EPS of roughly $18.40 — the latter matching the current-year consensus of $18.40.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth is strong and mostly bought. Q2 revenue +21.1% decomposes into +17.0% aggregates tonnage of which only 2.3 points were organic, and reported pricing that was negative. The FY2025 to FY2026 revenue path ($6.15B → $7.30B midpoint, +18.7%) is dominated by the QUIKRETE-acquired assets, Premier Magnesia and NFM.

Three growth vectors deserve separate treatment:

**Aggregates pricing.** This is the thesis. FY2025 delivered +6.9%. Q2 2026 delivered -2.0% reported, +2.1% organic, +3.7% organic mix-adjusted, against core COGS/ton of roughly +2.1%. The best-case spread is ~160bps; the reported spread is deeply negative. Management attributes the gap to acquisition mix (lower-priced Missouri, Kansas, Virginia, BC and St. Louis assets). That explanation is plausible and partly verifiable through the organic series, but two considerations argue at least part of the deceleration is sector-wide: MLM's own cost inflation decelerated to ~2.1% core, removing the cost-push justification for aggressive increases, and residential softness reduces the mix of higher-priced small-load business. We could not obtain comparable current-period Vulcan pricing, so company-specific versus sector-wide remains unresolved — the single most important open question on the name.

**Specialties.** Genuinely executing, with better pricing (+4.0% ASP, +5.0% mix-adjusted) and superior margin. LNA adds $786M of Adjusted EBITDA at a 43.7% margin plus ~$85M of run-rate cost synergies, roughly a 32% step-up to the consolidated EBITDA base. That is real EBITDA growth. Whether it is per-share growth depends on financing cost and purchase accounting.

**End-market demand.** Public infrastructure and heavy non-residential are supporting shipments; residential is weak with single-family starts down and risk language citing sustained high mortgage rates. The AI/data-center framing applied to MLM by press coverage is external commentary — the company has not quantified data-center revenue, and 2.3% organic volume growth does not currently corroborate a visible buildout tailwind. IIJA reauthorization is the largest exogenous demand variable and is not resolved.

**Estimate momentum is the clearest negative.** Current-year consensus fell from $19.50 (90 days ago) to $19.14 (30 days) to $18.40; next year from $23.06 to $23.31 to $21.49 — a 7.8% cut in thirty days. The Q2 print itself beat on adjusted EPS ($5.00 versus $4.79, +4.3%) and raised revenue, yet the stock fell to $540.00 on July 30 from $569.66 (-5.2%) and to $525.14 on July 31 (-2.8%). Analysts are cutting for the earnings drag rather than for demand: Wolfe $692→$648, UBS $739→$715, RBC $615→$610, all still well above spot. Against the calibration that large positive revision velocity is a powerful buy signal, the inverse applies here — falling estimates alongside a stable multiple is a warning, and it is why we score growth at 5.2 rather than at the 7.0 that prevailed in February.

Peer trailing multiples: VMC 33.2x, KNF 29.6x, CRH 19.4x, EXP 16.5x; median 29.6x. MLM at 33.6x trailing sits alongside Vulcan and above the median, with the discount names (CRH, EXP) reflecting different mixes and geographies. On the pure aggregates comparison MLM is not cheap relative to VMC, and VMC does not carry a pending 3.7x-leverage event.

**The LNA multiple arbitrage is roughly neutral.** MLM is paying ~15.5x post-synergy and 17.2x pre-synergy ($13.5B ÷ $786M) for LNA while trading at ~16.0x itself. Funding is 52% cash/debt and 48% stock issued at an implied $593.42 (10,953,543 shares valued at $6.5B on a 15-day VWAP through June 26), a premium to today's price — helpful on the equity leg. But the headline multiple paid is not below MLM's own, so value creation depends on LNA's 43.7% margin lifting consolidated mix, on reserve scarcity supporting a durable terminal multiple, and on synergies beyond the disclosed $85M. That $85M equals 10.8% of LNA EBITDA and 0.63% of purchase price — a thin assumption, consistent with genuinely minimal overlap (which is also why antitrust clearance came quickly).

**Dilution, precisely.** The 10,953,543 consideration shares represent an 18.24% increase on 60,050,859 shares outstanding, taking the count to 71,004,402; existing holders go from 100% to 84.57% of the company, i.e., 15.43% of ownership transferred. The larger figure describes share-count growth; the smaller describes ownership given away. If the share count is fixed rather than floating (the 10-Q language implies fixed, though we have not read the Securities Sale Agreement), MLM is delivering equity currently worth ~$5.94B, an effective consideration nearer $12.94B, with the seller bearing the mark.

**The accretion question is unresolved and it is the crux.** Management states LNA is accretive to earnings and margins in the first full year, footnoted as excluding one-time transaction costs *and purchase accounting impacts* — precisely the item that has been suppressing reported results all year. Two reproducible scenarios on identical operating assumptions:

- *Excluding step-up DD&A:* $786M + $85M synergies − $76M existing DD&A − $31M assumed group overhead = ~$764M EBIT; less $368M interest (assumed 5.25% blended on $7.0B); taxed at 23% → ~$305M net. Combined ($1,043M + $305M) ÷ 71.25M shares = **$18.92**, versus standalone $17.30 → **+9.4% accretive**.
- *Including $300M of incremental step-up DD&A:* EBIT ~$464M; less $368M interest → ~$74M net. Combined **$15.68** → **-9.4% dilutive**.

Both the 5.25% rate and the $300M step-up are assumptions; no preliminary purchase price allocation has been disclosed. If the $13.5B allocates predominantly to reserves depleted on units-of-production over 200+ years, incremental non-cash charges could be far smaller and Scenario A is closer to reality. Cash accretion is considerably more likely than GAAP accretion in either case. An investor should treat the range, not either endpoint, as the finding — and should note that a stock at ~30x adjusted earnings has limited room for a -9% GAAP outcome.

Consensus targets (mean $663.74, median $672.00, low $440, high $800) sit 21% above spot. Those targets are being cut, not raised, and MLM does not qualify for treating consensus as a floor: this is not a cheap inflection stock with rising estimates, it is a ~30x-adjusted-earnings name with estimates falling 6-8%. Valuation scores 5.0 — fair, not mispriced in either direction, with the derating having removed the excess rather than created an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

U.S. aggregates is a consolidated oligopoly with freight-capped competitive radii, which historically delivered price increases through recessions. MLM's permitted Sun Belt reserve positions, terminal network and water/rail long-haul capability are difficult to replicate. That level of advantage is intact.

Trajectory is what has changed, and trajectory is what matters. The clearest measure of pricing power — realized price per ton against cost per ton — has compressed from a wide spread in FY2025 to roughly neutral on organic figures and negative on reported. Organic volume growth of 2.3% is not evidence of share gain. Corporate expense has expanded faster than revenue. Other Building Materials is deteriorating on its own merits (Q2 gross profit -14% on revenue +12%).

Post-LNA, MLM claims it will be the largest U.S. producer of lime and limestone solutions, a position neither Vulcan nor CRH holds. The bull framing is a consolidated, 43.7%-margin, reserve-scarce business with 200+ year lives. The bear framing is that lime is a different business with different customers — LNA's largest disclosed end market is domestic steel manufacturing — and different carbon exposure, since calcination releases process CO₂ chemically. MLM just exited cement, the classic process-CO₂ business, while entering one that shares that profile. Management's distinction (lime is upstream and reserve-driven, cement is manufacturing-driven) is defensible but not costless. Lime's other applications — water treatment, flue-gas desulfurization, soil stabilization, agriculture — are genuinely non-cyclical and may substantially offset steel exposure, but LNA's end-market split was not disclosed, so the diversification claim cannot be verified. Revenue mix shifting toward Specialties is not the same evidence as reduced end-market concentration. Competitive position scores 6.5, down from 7.5 previously, on pricing-spread deterioration rather than on any loss of structural advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Pricing deceleration (highest conviction).** The FY2026 forward-looking list explicitly names "a widespread decline in aggregates pricing, including reduced shipment volume negatively affecting price." If mix-adjusted pricing settles at 3-4% rather than 6-9%, a ~30x adjusted multiple is difficult to defend.

**Leverage and covenants — newly material.** Opening 3.7x, an amended ladder of 4.75x / 4.25x / 3.75x, and three agencies on negative watch. The pre-emptive amendment on July 10 is the most candid disclosure about expected leverage; you do not negotiate 4.75x headroom expecting 3.0x. The terminal 3.75x test arrives roughly six quarters after close with almost no cushion above the 3.7x opening level. Getting from ~$12.4B net debt to <2.5x on a ~$3.6B combined base implies ~$3.4B of reduction, or ~$1.7B annually, against combined free cash flow we estimate at $1.3-1.8B before a ~$210M dividend. The plan works only if buybacks are essentially suspended through 2028. H1 repurchases were already cut 56% to $200M from $450M. Covenant baskets may permit synergy and pro forma add-backs that make the tested ratio more permissive than a simple calculation; we have not read the amended definitions.

**Funding mechanics.** $860M of current maturities against $742M of unused capacity, with the $560M trade receivable securitization maturing September 16, 2026 — a renewal falling in the same window as a $7B acquisition financing. Goldman Sachs provided fully committed financing and a $1.5B three-year term loan commitment was secured July 15, so the path exists; a one-notch downgrade to BBB/Baa3 would remain investment grade but raise the cost.

**Purchase-accounting and integration drag.** DD&A guided to $800M for FY2026 versus a lower FY2025-equivalent base, plus $67M of inventory step-up in H1 alone. The gap between "Adjusted EBITDA +13%" and "EPS -12%" is the mechanical expression, and it widens with LNA.

**Public-funding dependency.** Disclosed state concentrations include Texas, North Carolina, Colorado, California, Georgia, Florida, South Carolina, Arizona, Iowa and Minnesota. Risk language names transportation funding timing, a prolonged federal shutdown, and fuel-tax reduction or suspension. IIJA reauthorization risk is a disclosed exogenous demand variable.

**Rate regime.** MLM is adding roughly $7B of debt into an environment that raises the refinancing cost of the deleveraging plan and pressures the residential end market that leads light non-residential by 12-24 months. [unverified — no macro rate data in sources]

**Governance and succession.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks in fifteen months (July 2025; April 27, May 14 and August 4, 2026 — the last filed five days after earnings) alongside a newly added executive-succession risk factor. The contents of the August 4 filing could not be retrieved and should be checked. Two of three disclosed incentive metrics (Adjusted EBITDA and Total Revenues) increase mechanically with acquisitions regardless of price paid or shares issued; relative TSR is the third and does capture per-share outcomes, and five-year TSR of ~226 versus a peer ~139 is strong evidence the framework has not so far destroyed value. We draw no inference about motive, but note the architecture does not penalize overpayment.

**Goodwill.** $4.5B of intangibles pre-LNA with a large step-up coming; the FY2026 risk list explicitly names share-price pressure affecting impairment evaluations.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $670 (+22%)

Reported ASP weakness is genuinely acquisition mix, and organic mix-adjusted pricing reaccelerates toward 5%+ in 2027 as the acquired books are repriced toward MLM's list discipline. LNA's purchase-price allocation lands predominantly in units-of-production reserves, incremental step-up DD&A is well below $300M, and the deal is accretive on GAAP as well as cash. The $350M cash-flow program proves partly structural, combined free cash flow runs at the upper end of $1.8B, net leverage falls through 3.0x by late 2027 and toward 2.5x by mid-2028, ratings stabilize, and buybacks resume in 2028. Specialties at a 43.7% margin lifts consolidated mix, MLM re-rates toward 17.5x on ~$3.45B of combined EBITDA, and the equity clears $670. Sell-side targets of $610-715 are consistent with this path.

### Bear Case — $440 (-20%)

Pricing normalization is sector-wide rather than mix-driven, and mix-adjusted realizations settle near 2-3% against 2-3% cost inflation, eliminating the spread that justifies the multiple. Residential weakness propagates into light non-residential in 2027, and infrastructure reauthorization slips. The LNA purchase-price allocation produces $300M+ of incremental DD&A, first-year GAAP EPS falls ~9%, and the deleveraging plan slips against the 3.75x covenant step-down arriving in mid-2028. A one-notch downgrade raises financing cost; LNA's steel end-market exposure turns cyclical; goodwill testing becomes a live issue at a depressed share price. The stock de-rates to ~13.5x on ~$3.25B of combined EBITDA, or roughly $440 — matching the low end of the analyst target range.

### Base Case — $575 (+4.8%)

Organic mix-adjusted pricing holds in the 3-4% range with cost inflation near 2-3%, LNA closes in 2H 2026 at ~3.7x with a mid-range step-up that makes the deal cash-accretive and roughly GAAP-neutral, and the market continues to apply ~16x to ~$3.35B of combined EBITDA. EV of $53.6B less ~$12.4B net debt over 71.0M shares gives ~$580; cross-checking on earnings, 27x a 2027 pro forma adjusted EPS near $21 gives ~$567. We anchor base at $575. Adding the 0.60% dividend, a 50/25/25 base/bull/bear weighting produces an expected return near +3.5% over twelve months — approximately fair, below the return we would require to fund a leveraging cyclical in this rate regime.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The unresolved items — purchase-price allocation, whether pricing deceleration is company-specific or sector-wide, and deleveraging execution against a 3.75x covenant step-down — are material enough that we cannot support a stronger call in either direction. Existing owners are compensated by an irreplaceable reserve base and a ~$575 base case; prospective buyers have no entry signal at $548.57.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with three dated checkpoints: the LNA close in 2H 2026 and its purchase-price allocation disclosure; the trade receivable securitization renewal on or before September 16, 2026; and the Q3 2026 print (late October), which is the first clean read on whether mix-adjusted pricing is stabilizing or eroding further.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE/BUY if:**
1. Organic mix-adjusted aggregates pricing prints ≥5% for two consecutive quarters with core COGS/ton inflation at or below 3% — restoring a spread consistent with the historical thesis.
2. The LNA purchase-price allocation discloses incremental step-up DD&A below roughly $150M annually, making GAAP accretion credible, and combined net leverage prints at or below 3.5x within two quarters of close.
3. Price near $470-500 (roughly 13.5-14.0x pro forma EBITDA) on unchanged guidance, which would restore a double-digit margin of safety to the base case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. FY2027 consensus EPS is cut a further 5%+ from $21.49 without an offsetting guidance raise, or FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance is lowered below the $2.36B floor.
2. Combined net leverage prints above 4.0x at or after close, or any agency downgrades below BBB/Baa2, or covenant compliance requires reliance on synergy add-backs.
3. Reported and organic aggregates ASP both turn negative in a quarter, indicating deceleration is sector-wide rather than mix.

**Downgrade to AVOID if** a covenant amendment beyond the current 4.75x ladder is required, the securitization facility fails to renew on comparable terms, or a goodwill/intangible impairment is taken against the acquired asset base.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report and the LNA closing 8-K with preliminary purchase accounting, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory on this name has declined from 7.0 in May to 6.0 in June, and the current data confirm rather than reverse that direction. The stock is 9.9% lower than at the last review, and unlike many drawdowns this one is accompanied by identifiable fundamental deterioration: consensus next-year EPS cut 7.8% in thirty days, reported aggregates pricing negative for the first time in years, gross profit flat on 21% revenue growth, corporate expense up 73% year over year in H1, and a leverage step-up from ~2.6x to ~3.7x that regulatory clearance on August 5 made effectively certain.
