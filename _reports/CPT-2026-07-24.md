**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Camden Property Trust is a Texas-based, internally-managed multifamily REIT with 174 communities and roughly 59,400 apartment homes concentrated in Sunbelt and Texas markets, plus a D.C. Metro minority. The balance sheet is strong: A-/A3/A- rated, ~90% unencumbered assets, weighted-average debt maturity extended to 5.4 years, and 6.0x interest coverage. The problem is not solvency; it is that CPT's operating fundamentals have turned negative at the same time the stock trades near the top of its 52-week range ($113.50 vs. a $96.53–$119.81 band). Same-store revenue growth compressed from +0.8% in FY2025 to +0.2% in Q1 2026, and same-store NOI turned negative at -0.7% as operating expenses grew ~1.9% against roughly flat revenue. Core FFO per share declined year over year in Q1 ($1.70 vs. $1.72). This is the sector-wide Sunbelt supply-absorption story showing up in Camden's numbers, not a company-specific execution failure.

The reported earnings picture is flattered by asset sales. FY2025 net income jumped to $384.5M from $163.3M (a 135% increase), but $260.9M of that was disposition gains; on a Core FFO basis, growth was essentially flat at +0.4%. Q1 2026 repeats the pattern: a $68.1M gain on an Irving, TX sale masked a Core FFO decline and was partly offset by ~$59M in RealPage settlement and technology-investment impairment charges. On a Core FFO/AFFO basis rather than GAAP net income, the trajectory is stagnant-to-declining. Management is responding rationally: buybacks below NAV (over 6M shares since early 2025 at $100–111) and a pared-back development pipeline (three projects, $176.6M to complete). That is a credible countercyclical value signal, but it is being partly funded with debt (commercial paper, the $600M 2036 notes) alongside a new $500M ATM program, making it capital arbitrage rather than free upside.

We maintain HOLD, classified as an income hold. The ~3.8% dividend is well covered (~71% of Core AFFO) and the balance sheet gives Camden flexibility that lower-rated peers lack heading into a refinancing-heavy environment. The stock offers no asymmetric upside at ~31x trailing GAAP earnings and ~16.5x Core FFO with fundamentals at or near a cyclical trough, negative same-store NOI, a live multi-front RealPage legal overhang, and a founder-era leadership handoff. The prior recommendation streak (four consecutive HOLDs) and a declining score trajectory reflect a business whose quality is intact but whose earnings momentum and valuation support have both eroded.

## Company Overview

Camden was formed in 1993 and is internally managed, with in-house property management and development/general-contracting capabilities. As of March 31, 2026, it owned interests in, operated, or was developing 174 communities / 59,416 apartment homes across 15 U.S. markets, employing roughly 1,640 people. The portfolio strategy centers on high-growth Sunbelt and Texas markets selected for job and household formation.

Geographic concentration by real-estate book value (YE2025) is led by Houston at 13.8% (being actively trimmed from 28 to 23 properties), Washington D.C. Metro at 11.9%, Dallas/Ft. Worth at 7.8%, then a diversified spread across Atlanta, Charlotte, Orlando, Tampa, Phoenix, Austin, and Raleigh, each in the 5.8–6.9% range. No single community exceeds 1.5% of consolidated revenue. Roughly two-thirds of the book sits in Texas and the Sunbelt, with a minority in D.C. Metro and coastal California.

The competitive advantages are real: an investment-grade balance sheet, in-market operating scale for cost leverage, and a well-regarded corporate culture (18 consecutive years on FORTUNE's "100 Best Companies to Work For"). The current challenge is cyclical: Camden's core markets absorbed the heaviest apartment-delivery wave in decades, which has suppressed rent growth precisely where the company is most exposed.

## Financial Analysis

A data-quality note first: the automated XBRL "revenue" figure of $12.97M reflects only fee/management income, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.39 ("distress") is a false signal, structurally inappropriate for a leveraged, low-asset-turnover REIT carrying A-/A3 credit ratings and 6.0x interest coverage. Actual FY2025 total property revenue was ~$1.573B.

**FY2025 (audited).** Total property revenue was $1.573B (+1.9% YoY, with most of the growth from acquisitions rather than organic gains). Same-store revenue rose just +0.8% and same-store NOI +0.3% — effectively flat. Property NOI reached $1.007B (+2.2%). GAAP net income to common was $384.5M versus $163.3M in 2024, but that ~135% jump was driven by $260.9M of property-sale gains (against $43.8M the prior year) and a smaller land impairment, not operating improvement. Core FFO was $757.2M (~$6.88/diluted share) and Core AFFO $649.1M (~$5.90/share). Operating cash flow was $826.6M against recurring capex of ~$108M. The FY2025 dividend of $4.20/share, raised to $4.24 annualized for 2026, is covered at ~71% of AFFO.

**Q1 2026 — the turn.** Property revenue declined 0.5% YoY to $388.8M as the portfolio shrank via dispositions. Same-store revenue grew only +0.2%, but same-store NOI fell -0.7% because operating expenses (real estate taxes, salaries) grew ~1.9% against ~0.2% revenue. This is operating deleveraging: when a fixed-cost-heavy business sees costs outrun revenue, margins compress mechanically. Core FFO fell to $181.1M ($1.70/sh) from $189.8M ($1.72/sh); Core AFFO fell to $164.9M from $173.7M. GAAP net income was again boosted by a $68.1M gain on the Irving, TX sale, offset by the RealPage settlement and a $4.9M technology-fund impairment.

**Balance sheet and capital structure.** Total debt is ~$4.25B (Q1 2026), with ~90.6% of properties unencumbered and weighted-average maturity extended to 5.4 years (from 4.5 at YE2025). Interest coverage of ~6.0x is solid but slipping from 6.7x. Floating-rate debt is ~$900.8M (~21%), including the $500M 5.85% 2026 notes swapped to floating. Management has been terming out proactively: it issued $600M 4.90% notes due 2036 in February 2026, removed a $300M term loan, extended the revolver to March 2030, and runs a $600M commercial paper program ($358.8M drawn at Q1). About $664M of debt matures in the next 12 months plus commercial paper, manageable given the credit profile and unencumbered pool.

**Capital allocation.** The standout is repurchasing below NAV/book: 2.53M shares at an average $106.92 ($270.7M) in FY2025, then 1.10M in January 2026 ($110.03), 1.54M in February–March ($102.91), and 1.43M in April ($100.78). A new $600M buyback was authorized in February 2026 (~$298M remaining after Q1). Development is being pared to three projects. The signal is that management views its own equity as cheaper than new development or acquisitions — accretive and defensive. The caveat is that buybacks are partly funded with debt and coexist with a new $500M ATM, so this is two-sided capital flexibility rather than one-directional conviction.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth outlook is weak, and this is the most important fundamental thread. Same-store revenue decelerated from +0.8% (FY25) to +0.2% (Q1 2026), and same-store NOI turned negative (-0.7%). Core FFO/share is declining year over year. The mechanism is straightforward: the post-2021 Sunbelt supply wave delivered record apartment units in Texas, Florida, and the Carolinas, which suppressed rent growth in exactly Camden's core footprint. Camden's 14-month lease terms mean rents reprice quickly when markets soften — helpful on the way up, painful on the way down.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration. Current-year consensus is stable (+0.8% over 90 days), but the next-year figure has been cut sharply: -37.3% over 30 days and -44.9% over 90 days (note: next-year estimate reflects only 2 analysts, so precision is limited). That magnitude of downward revision on the forward year is a clear negative-momentum signal and contradicts any recovery thesis. The earnings-surprise history shows large positive surprises in recent quarters (308.9% in Q4 2025, 243.1% in Q3 2025), but those are driven by non-operating disposition gains against low GAAP estimates, not operating outperformance; they should not be read as evidence of accelerating fundamentals.

The bull framing, articulated by management, is that supply is now moderating while Sunbelt demand persists (population and job in-migration, elevated mortgage rates keeping households renting rather than buying), setting up a 2026–2027 rent-growth recovery as deliveries fall. That is plausible and is the base-case path to a re-rating. But it is a 2027 story; the 2026 data currently in hand shows fundamentals at or near a trough with no confirmed reacceleration. We are not paying up for a recovery that has not yet appeared in the numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

CPT trades at ~$113.50, roughly 31x trailing GAAP EPS of $3.54 and ~16.5x Core FFO of ~$6.88/share. On the more relevant Core FFO/AFFO basis, the multiple is not extreme for a high-quality apartment REIT, but there is no earnings recovery to justify multiple expansion: Core FFO is flat-to-declining, and the forward estimate cuts argue against treating current consensus targets as floors.

Against peers, the picture is mixed and does not create a clear mispricing. Peer P/E medians are inflated by REIT-specific GAAP distortions (MAA at 41.9x, ESS at 31.6x, IRT at 84.0x, CSR at 123.1x), so the trailing-P/E comparison is largely noise. The cleaner comparison is dividend yield: CPT at ~3.8% sits below the peer median of ~4.4% (UDR and MAA both 4.4%). That lower yield implies the market is already awarding Camden a quality premium for its balance sheet and culture, which limits the case that the stock is cheap. The analyst consensus reinforces the range-bound read: mean target $116.13, median $113.50 (essentially spot), low $102, high $134. With the median target at the current price and forward estimates falling, consensus is not signaling upside.

The calibration lesson here is directly relevant: a "cheap-looking" multiple on a REIT with structurally distorted GAAP earnings is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and cheapness without a catalyst has historically sorted the wrong way. Camden's genuine support is absolute — the covered dividend and the buyback-below-NAV floor — not a relative-value case. We score valuation below neutral: the stock is fairly-to-slightly-richly priced against deteriorating operating fundamentals, and the trough-recovery optionality is not yet visible enough to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Camden competes with other multifamily REITs (MAA, Equity Residential, AvalonBay, UDR, Essex) as well as single-family rentals, condos, for-sale homes, and short-term rentals. Its structural advantages — investment-grade rating, in-house development/management, diversified in-market scale — are durable and differentiate it from lower-rated peers heading into a refinancing-heavy period.

The competitive-position trajectory, however, has flattened rather than improved. The prior score history shows this dimension falling from 7.0 to 5.3, and the current data supports a neutral read. Camden's flat-to-negative same-store metrics track peers, confirming this is a sector-wide supply problem, not a share-loss problem: Camden is neither gaining nor losing meaningful ground. The May 2026 Scotiabank double-downgrade of CPT and MAA to Sell-equivalent ratings reflects the sector's supply/absorption concern rather than a Camden-specific verdict. Camden is holding its position in a soft cycle, which is a neutral (not improving) trajectory. The proactive Houston trimming (28→23 properties) is sensible portfolio management to reduce energy-economy concentration, but it does not widen the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**RealPage antitrust litigation** is the most concrete idiosyncratic risk. Camden is a defendant in consolidated multidistrict class-action litigation alleging rent-fixing via RealPage revenue-management software. In April 2026 it agreed to a binding term sheet to settle the class action for $53.0M (two $26.5M installments), with prospective data-sharing/software-use commitments. Separate cases remain unresolved: DC and Arizona AG suits and a DOJ + 10-state civil suit filed January 2025. Camden recorded $58.8M in aggregate loss contingencies at Q1 2026. This is an industry-wide overhang (MAA, Equity Residential, and others are named), and an adverse outcome or forced changes to revenue-management practices could pressure sector-wide pricing power, a risk not fully quantifiable today.

**Operating deceleration and supply absorption** is the dominant fundamental risk, discussed above: negative same-store NOI, 14-month lease terms that reprice quickly, and a Sunbelt footprint that absorbed heavy deliveries. **Rent control/stabilization** is flagged explicitly in several of Camden's markets. **Interest-rate/refinancing risk** persists with ~$664M maturing in the next 12 months and ~$911M floating; a higher-for-longer path pressures both refi costs and cap rates (asset values). **Development and land impairment** risk remains ($12.9M land impairment in FY2025, two parcels held for future development). **Concentration** in Houston/Texas carries energy-economy sensitivity and hurricane/storm exposure in TX/FL.

The founder-era leadership handoff (effective March 24, 2026) is orderly and internal — Campo to Executive Chairman, Jessett to CEO, Baker to President/COO, Fraker to EVP-CFO — but a June 11, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K flags a further director/officer change whose details are not yet confirmed and warrant monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing modest, evenly-distributed uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 25.3% is rich versus 20.0% one-year historical vol (a 5.4pp spread), but sits at only the 29th percentile within the current chain, so it is not signaling an imminent binary event. Put/call skew of 6.3% shows mildly elevated put demand, and the near-term put/call volume ratio of 3.00 in the 1-month tenor leans defensive, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.53 (down from 0.76 a month ago) has eased on the price rally. The flat term structure (near 25.3% ≈ far 27.1%) indicates the market sees no concentrated catalyst. Net read: mildly cautious positioning consistent with a range-bound, income-oriented name, not a signal of an approaching sharp move in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130–134).** Sunbelt apartment deliveries have peaked and are moderating; as new supply falls through 2026–2027, occupancy firms and rent growth reaccelerates against durable in-migration demand and elevated mortgage rates that keep households renting. Same-store NOI turns back positive, Core FFO/share resumes growth, and the market re-rates Camden's quality balance sheet back toward the top of its range. Buybacks executed at $100–111 prove accretive, and the RealPage overhang resolves within the recorded contingency. This is the analyst-high scenario and roughly matches consensus upside.

**Bear Case ($95–100, roughly -12% to -16%).** Supply absorption takes longer than management expects, expense growth (taxes, insurance, labor) continues to outrun flat rents, and same-store NOI stays negative through 2026. Higher-for-longer rates lift refinancing costs on the near-term maturities and pressure cap rates, compressing NAV. The DOJ/state RealPage cases produce an adverse outcome or forced practice changes that dent sector pricing power. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low and the ~3.8% yield does the heavy lifting on total return. Camden's buyback pace at $100–104 in the spring effectively marks where management itself found value support.

**Base Case ($110–116).** The most likely outcome is a range-bound year. Fundamentals bottom but do not visibly reaccelerate in 2026; the dividend is covered and grows modestly; buybacks continue below NAV; and the stock oscillates around consensus (~$113–116 mean) as investors wait for supply data to confirm the recovery. Total return is dominated by the ~3.8% yield plus low-single-digit price drift.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD classified as an income hold. The reassessment framework:

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-store NOI returns to positive growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the supply-absorption trough has passed and rent growth is reaccelerating.
- Forward-year Core FFO estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the current -44.9% 90-day trend), signaling the Street sees an operating inflection.
- A pullback toward the low-$100s (where management itself bought back stock at $100–104), which would restore a genuine value entry with the covered yield as downside support.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI decline accelerates beyond -1.5% with further expense-outpacing-revenue deleveraging, or occupancy deteriorates materially.
- An adverse DOJ/state RealPage ruling or forced revenue-management changes that impair sector pricing power beyond the recorded contingency.
- Dividend coverage erodes toward or above ~85% of AFFO, or leverage climbs materially as buybacks are debt-funded into a still-declining FFO base.
- Confirmation that the June 2026 Item 5.02 officer change reflects instability in the newly-installed leadership team.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) is the natural checkpoint for the same-store NOI trend and updated supply commentary, followed by year-end for the 2027 delivery-pipeline read.

## Conclusion

Camden is a high-quality, well-financed apartment REIT caught in a soft point of its operating cycle. The balance sheet, dividend coverage, and countercyclical buybacks are genuine positives, and management's decision to repurchase stock below NAV rather than chase development is a rational, shareholder-aligned response to a difficult market. The fundamentals have turned the wrong way — same-store NOI is negative, Core FFO/share is declining, and forward-year estimates have been cut nearly 45% over 90 days — while the stock sits near the top of its 52-week range and offers no asymmetric upside. GAAP earnings are flattered by asset sales that mask stagnant operating results, so the trailing multiple overstates earnings power quality.

The recommendation is HOLD as an income hold. Own Camden for the ~3.8% covered yield and the balance-sheet flexibility, with the recognition that a re-rating depends on a 2027 Sunbelt supply recovery that is not yet visible in the data. This marks a fifth consecutive HOLD against a declining score trajectory; the case for continuing it is that the quality floor and covered dividend limit downside, not that a growth catalyst is at hand. A more constructive stance requires either confirmation of an operating inflection (positive same-store NOI, rising forward estimates) or a better entry in the low-$100s.
