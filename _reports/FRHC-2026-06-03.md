**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Freedom Holding Corp. is a Nevada-incorporated, New York-headquartered financial holding company whose operations are concentrated in Kazakhstan (~84% of revenue, ~63% of assets). It is controlled by founder and CEO Timur Turlov, who owns roughly 69% of the shares. The business has grown into an integrated "SuperApp" ecosystem spanning brokerage, banking, insurance, and a set of newer ventures (payments, e-commerce, plus loss-making telecom, media, and AI infrastructure). FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) results were strong on the surface: total net revenue of $2,191.3M (+9% YoY) and net income of $153.3M (+101% YoY), with underlying franchise metrics that look healthier than the headline — banking deposits +46%, loan book +29%, and 858K brokerage customers feeding a SuperApp with 2.59M monthly active users.

The problem is what sits beneath the reported earnings. On March 11, 2026, both the company and Turlov personally received Wells Notices from the SEC, signaling staff intent to recommend a civil enforcement action over settlement practices, accounting for internalized trades, disclosures, internal controls, and relationships with institutional market-maker customers of the non-US broker-dealer subsidiaries. A single non-US institutional market-maker customer generated $345.5M, equal to 71% of total fee and commission income. The line item that powers the earnings narrative is the same line item under federal investigation. This is the defining fact of the FRHC thesis.

We rate FRHC AVOID. The franchise is not failing — customer, loan, and deposit growth are real, and the company carries $2.69B of aggregate excess regulatory capital. The stock carries an unresolved binary regulatory event layered on extreme concentration (single customer, single country, single controlling shareholder), lower-quality trading-driven earnings, and a capital-allocation pivot into unproven, debt-funded verticals. The risk asymmetry is poor at $157.84, and the trailing P/E of ~3,500x and reported EPS of $1.40 reflect a denominator problem (heavy share count math and non-recurring earnings volatility) rather than a clean earnings stream we can underwrite with confidence.

## Company Overview

Freedom Holding traces to a 2015–2017 reverse merger combining shell company BMB Munai with Turlov's Freedom Finance brokerage. It now reports four segments:

- **Brokerage** (FY26 net revenue $831.5M): 858K retail customers with access to US/EU markets, plus investment-banking activity through US subsidiary FCM (an NYSE floor broker).
- **Banking** (net revenue $689.2M): Freedom Bank KZ — 5.0M customers (doubled YoY), a $2.05B loan portfolio, $2.52B in deposits, with digital mortgages, auto/SME/cash loans, and multi-currency cards.
- **Insurance** (net revenue $497.8M): Freedom Life and Freedom Insurance, leaders in online and general insurance in Kazakhstan.
- **Other** (net revenue $172.8M): payments, e-commerce (Arbuz), ticketing, and developmental telecom, cloud, and media businesses that are loss-making.

The ecosystem is anchored by the Freedom SuperApp (5.2M registered users, 2.59M MAU, +154% YoY) and the Tradernet trading platform. The genuine differentiation is deep integration with Kazakhstan government databases enabling loan onboarding in minutes, plus cross-border market access that domestic competitors lack. That same integration concentrates the company in one country's macro and regulatory fate.

FRHC is a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules: Turlov controls board elections and shareholder votes, and the company uses governance exemptions accordingly.

## Financial Analysis

The standardized data fields require careful handling. The XBRL "revenue" tag of $97.4M captures only "sales of goods and services"; the actual total net revenue is $2,191.3M. The trailing P/E of ~3,500x and EPS of $1.40 conflict with reported net income of $153.3M on ~61.2M shares (which would imply EPS closer to $2.50), reflecting timing and share-base inconsistencies in the standardized feed. Quarterly EPS prints of $1.25, $0.63, and $0.50 across recent quarters confirm that reported per-share earnings are volatile.

**Revenue and earnings trajectory:**
- Total net revenue: $2,191.3M (FY26), up from $2,004.2M (FY25) and $1,647.3M (FY24) — multi-year growth, decelerating to +9%.
- Net income: $153.3M.
- Revenue mix: interest income 41% ($882M), fee & commission 22% ($490M), net insurance 18% ($402M), trading gains 7% ($159M), FX 3%, derivatives 3%, goods/services 5%.

**Earnings quality is the central concern.** The doubling of net income was substantially driven by volatile, non-recurring items. Net trading gains swung +$216.6M YoY (from a $57.8M loss to a $158.8M gain, mostly realized sales of Kazakhstan sovereign and corporate debt), and derivatives/FX added roughly $70M. Approximately 83% of trading income derives from Kazakhstan government and quasi-government debt — cyclical and rate-sensitive. Insurance revenue fell 29% ($168.8M) on regulatory caps on agent commissions. Operating cash flow collapsed to $185M from $1.68B (driven by margin-lending and trading-book swings), and free cash flow was negative $13.5M after $199M of capex. The durable parts of the franchise (brokerage fees +17%, loan book +29%, deposits +46%) are healthier than the bottom line; the trading-driven portion of the earnings jump is what the company itself warns may not recur.

**Balance sheet:**
- Total assets $13.2B (+33%); equity $1.49B; total liabilities $11.7B including $7.1B of customer liabilities.
- Long-term debt (Freedom SPC bonds) jumped to $1.26B from $470M, issued at 9%–12% coupons to fund telecom and AI ambitions — a rising debt-service drag.
- Aggregate excess regulatory capital across subsidiaries: $2,688M; all subsidiaries exceed minimums.
- No dividend. A Kazakhstan common-stock offering (Reg S) was announced for consideration on April 9, 2026 — a potential dilution overhang.

A naive quant screen will misfire here: the Altman Z-score of 0.25 and a "157% net margin" are artifacts of a bank/broker balance sheet (customer liabilities inflate leverage) combined with the XBRL revenue mislabel. These are not bankruptcy signals against a company with $2.69B of excess regulatory capital and doubling reported profit. The real risk is regulatory and governance, not solvency. We score Financial Health at 5.5 — the franchise is well-capitalized, but the rapid debt ramp, negative FCF, and collapsing operating cash flow temper the picture.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying franchise growth is the strongest part of the story, evident in the deposit, loan, customer, and MAU metrics above. Brokerage fee income grew 17%. The company sits at the intersection of Central Asian financial deepening, digital banking adoption, and Kazakhstan's policy ambition to become a regional fintech and AI hub.

The +9% total revenue figure understates franchise momentum because insurance fell 29% on regulatory commission caps and trading gains are lumpy. The customer-acquisition and balance-sheet metrics point to a structurally growing core. But the company is now diverting capital into unproven, capital-hungry verticals: a non-binding ~$2B AI data-center MOU with NVIDIA and Kazakhstan's AI Ministry (Nov 2025), a separate "Freedom AI" entity created May 5, 2026, the pending acquisition of 99.32% of Turkish Bank A.S., and developmental telecom and media that are expected to lose money for years. These are funded with high-coupon debt and potentially new equity, which raises execution and dilution risk while diluting the clean fintech growth narrative. We score Growth Prospects at 6.0 — real momentum in the core, offset by deceleration in reported totals and capital being redirected into unproven businesses.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is difficult to anchor cleanly because the earnings base is contaminated by non-recurring trading gains and the standardized P/E is distorted. On reported net income of $153.3M and a $9.64B market cap, the implied P/E is roughly 63x. Strip out the ~$160M+ of non-recurring trading, derivatives, and FX gains, and normalized earnings are materially lower, pushing the effective multiple well above 100x on recurring earnings power. The company pays no dividend, so there is no yield support.

There is no published analyst price-target distribution to anchor against; the lone reference point is a current price-target marker near $158.56, essentially spot. Independent sell-side coverage is thin, which the company itself flags as a risk and which removes a normal check on consensus.

Peers within Kazakhstan/CIS fintech (Kaspi.kz being the most relevant) and global brokerage ecosystem players (Interactive Brokers, eToro) trade on cleaner, more durable earnings streams. FRHC's premium-to-normalized multiple is not supported by a specific identifiable catalyst that resolves favorably — the most consequential near-term catalyst (the SEC matter) is a downside event. A low headline P/E is absent here, and the high effective multiple on recurring earnings, combined with the regulatory overhang on the highest-margin revenue line, leaves no margin of safety. We score Valuation at 4.0: the price discounts continued franchise growth without discounting the probability and severity of an adverse enforcement outcome.

## Competitive Landscape

Domestically, FRHC is strong. Its integrated brokerage-plus-banking-plus-insurance-plus-lifestyle SuperApp with government-database integration is difficult to replicate cross-border, and it competes credibly against Halyk, BCC, and Kaspi on cross-border market access. The MAU and customer growth indicate the ecosystem flywheel is working.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. In core Kazakhstan payments and banking, Kaspi.kz is a larger, better-capitalized ecosystem incumbent. In brokerage outside the home market, Interactive Brokers and eToro dwarf FRHC in scale and capital. The new telecom and AI verticals put FRHC against entrenched incumbents (Kazakhtelecom) and require capabilities it has not demonstrated. The reputational baggage of the market-maker model, the Russia-adjacent history, and the Ukraine sanction on Turlov constrain its ability to expand into Western markets. We score Competitive Position at 6.0 — a strong and improving domestic franchise, but with a moat that is geographically captive and a set of new bets where it is the challenger, not the leader.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the decisive factor in this rating.

**SEC Wells Notice (the single most material item).** SEC staff made a preliminary determination to recommend a civil enforcement action alleging federal securities-law violations. The investigation has run since 2021, with subpoenas to officers and directors, and concerns settlement practices, relationships with institutional market-maker customers of non-US broker-dealer subsidiaries, accounting for internalized trades, disclosures, and internal controls. Outcomes could include monetary and non-monetary penalties and severe reputational damage. This is a binary event with asymmetric downside and no clear resolution date.

**Customer concentration intertwined with the probe.** The single non-US institutional market-maker customer's share of fee and commission income rose from 56% to 71%. One "major customer" accounts for 17% of total revenue. These are the very arrangements under SEC scrutiny. If the enforcement action forces changes to these relationships, the highest-margin revenue line is directly impaired.

**Single-country macro fragility.** Inflation ran 11% (Mar 2026), the NBK base rate reached 18%, the tenge fell 21.6% before partial recovery, and the country depends on oil exports (the CPC pipeline was attacked in Nov 2025). A new tax code added a 10% tax on Kazakh sovereign-securities income, lifting the effective tax rate to 32.1% from 27.2% and directly taxing the trading-gain engine.

**Governance and related-party web.** Controlled-company status concentrates all voting power with Turlov. Related-party arrangements include FFIN Credit microfinance loan purchases (discontinued Sept 2025, loans still on balance sheet), $10M to the Kazakhstan Chess Federation (Turlov holds a management role), the Freedom Youth Football League (99.9% Turlov-owned), and an A-Telecom prepayment. The company explicitly flags that Turlov may need to sell stock to fund outside ventures. There is no key-man insurance.

**History of accounting and control friction.** A prior material weakness (FY2023, remediated FY2024), three "non-reliance"/restatement 8-Ks in 2022–2023, a Hindenburg short report (Aug 2023), Ukraine sanctions on Turlov (Oct 2022), and OFAC inquiries regarding ~$187.8M (3% of customer liabilities) tied to sanctioned/under-review persons in omnibus accounts where FRHC lacks beneficial-owner visibility. The pattern indicates regulatory overhang is structural to the model, not a one-off.

We score Risk Factors at 2.5. The Wells Notice is a specific, undated, asymmetric downside catalyst directly tied to the company's most important revenue line, layered on concentration risks that could each independently impair the franchise.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates the cautious view without screaming distress. Near-term ATM IV is 42.0% versus 38.8% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich (3.2% spread), with the IV percentile at the 26th percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew of 7.3% indicates elevated near-term put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 1.15 leans bearish, though the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.22 is light. The term structure is in contango (1m 42.0% < 6m 45.6%), consistent with the market pricing rising uncertainty into the medium term, the window in which the SEC matter could escalate. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±6.8%. The options market is pricing event risk building over the next several months rather than an imminent shock, which fits an enforcement matter with no fixed resolution date.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The core franchise is genuinely growing — deposits +46%, loans +29%, bank customers doubled, SuperApp MAU +154%. The company holds $2.69B of excess regulatory capital, and the quant screens that flag "bankruptcy risk" are mechanical artifacts. If the SEC matter resolves with a manageable monetary settlement and no forced restructuring of the market-maker relationships, the regulatory overhang lifts, the trading-driven earnings volatility normalizes around a structurally growing core, and the stock re-rates on franchise growth. The Turkish bank acquisition and AI/telecom optionality could add upside if execution holds. In this scenario the stock recovers toward its 52-week high of $194.01 (+23%).

### Bear Case
The SEC enforcement action forces changes to the market-maker relationships that generate 71% of fee income, impairing the highest-margin revenue stream while imposing monetary penalties and reputational damage. Non-recurring trading gains do not repeat, exposing a lower-quality recurring earnings base. Kazakhstan macro stress (high rates, tenge volatility, the new securities tax) compresses the trading engine, and the debt-funded pivot into telecom/AI/Turkish banking strains the capital position while a Kazakhstan equity offering dilutes. In this scenario the stock revisits its 52-week low of $107.98 (-32%) or lower.

The asymmetry favors the bear case because the same line item drives both the earnings narrative and the gravest risk, and because there is no near-term catalyst that resolves the SEC matter favorably on a known timeline.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The operative catalyst is the SEC enforcement matter following the March 11, 2026 Wells Notices. A formal complaint, settlement terms, or any forced change to the market-maker arrangements would drive the stock materially lower. The critical weakness for a short is that this catalyst is undated — Wells Notice responses and SEC enforcement timelines commonly span many months to over a year.

**Timing window**: Indeterminate. The Wells Notice formalizes staff intent but does not set a resolution date. Carry would be open-ended.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 5.3% of float (984,314 shares) with days-to-cover of 13.4. The float is thin and Turlov controls ~69% of shares, so the genuinely tradeable float is small and illiquid (average volume ~121K shares). The 13.4-day cover ratio against a 5.3% SI is a function of low liquidity, not crowding; this combination makes a position hard to exit and elevates squeeze and slippage risk despite uncrowded SI.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options confirm a mild bearish lean (put skew 7.3%, P/C volume 1.15, contango term structure pricing rising medium-term uncertainty), but the signal is moderate, not extreme. IV at the 26th percentile of its chain indicates the market is not yet pricing an imminent binary, which limits the payoff from puts purchased today and reflects an undated catalyst.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A bear outcome targets the $107.98 low (-32%) or lower. Upside risk includes a favorable SEC resolution (re-rate toward $194.01, +23%), continued franchise-driven beats, or a squeeze in a thin float. The reward:risk on the directional move is roughly 1:1 against an undated catalyst, well below the 2:1 threshold that would justify a short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is well-supported, but the catalyst is undated, the float is thin and illiquid (controlled company, ~121K average daily volume), open-ended carry erodes the trade, and the reward:risk does not reach 2:1. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The thesis rests on an unresolved binary regulatory event tied directly to the company's most concentrated and highest-margin revenue stream, layered on single-country macro exposure, controlled-company governance, and lower-quality trading-driven earnings.

**Re-entry criteria** (any of the following would prompt a reassessment toward HOLD or better):
- The SEC matter resolves with a defined, manageable monetary settlement and no forced restructuring of the market-maker relationships that generate 71% of fee income.
- Recurring earnings demonstrate durability for two-plus quarters with materially reduced reliance on Kazakhstan sovereign-debt trading gains (trading income falling below ~10% of total revenue while net income holds).
- Customer concentration in fee income falls below ~40% from a single market-maker, reducing the structural risk regardless of enforcement outcome.
- Valuation compresses to a normalized multiple (sub-25x recurring earnings) that prices in the regulatory and concentration risks.

**Timeline**: The SEC enforcement matter is likely to persist for many months and potentially over a year. The macro overhang (Kazakhstan inflation, rates, tenge, the new securities tax) is a multi-quarter condition. We do not see a near-term catalyst that improves the risk/reward, so the AVOID stance is appropriate for at least the next 6–12 months pending regulatory clarity.

## Conclusion

Freedom Holding is a real and growing fintech ecosystem with strong domestic positioning, doubling customer counts, and a well-capitalized balance sheet that the standard quant screens mischaracterize as distressed. The issue is that the reported earnings — particularly the 71% of fee income from a single market-maker customer and the trading gains on Kazakhstan sovereign debt — sit on a base that is simultaneously concentrated, cyclical, and under active federal investigation. The arrangements driving the earnings narrative are the precise subject of that investigation.

At $157.84, the stock prices in continued franchise growth without adequately discounting the probability and severity of an adverse enforcement outcome, the dependence on non-recurring earnings, single-country macro fragility, or the capital-allocation pivot into debt-funded, unproven verticals. The risk/reward is unattractive and the most consequential near-term catalyst is a downside event with no fixed resolution date. We rate FRHC **AVOID** and would revisit only on a favorable resolution of the SEC matter or on demonstrated durability of recurring earnings independent of the contested revenue lines.
