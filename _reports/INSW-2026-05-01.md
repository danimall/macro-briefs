## Executive Summary

International Seaways is a pure-play crude oil and refined petroleum products tanker company operating 70 vessels (8.4M dwt) with 4 LR1 newbuilds scheduled for delivery by Q3 2026. The company's Q4 2025 earnings showed VLCCs more than doubling to $75,566/day from $34,809 in Q3, driving a 25.4% EPS beat. Consensus estimates have responded with extraordinary upward revisions: current-year EPS now $9.92 (up 70% in 30 days, up 89% in 90 days), and next-quarter estimates up 178% in the past month alone.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) the structural supply constraint in crude tanker tonnage (orderbook at 17% of fleet, but concentrated in 2028-2030 deliveries, with the current fleet aging rapidly), (2) the geopolitical demand tailwind from Red Sea rerouting and Russia sanctions increasing ton-mile demand, and (3) a balance sheet strong enough (0.27x D/E, $724M liquidity, 31 unencumbered vessels) to both weather cyclical downturns and opportunistically deploy capital at the trough. At ~8.4x FY2026E earnings, the stock trades at a 67% discount to the peer median P/E of 25.4x, a gap that reflects cyclical earnings skepticism but appears too wide given the multi-year supply/demand setup.

The primary risk is geopolitical normalization: any diplomatic resolution or Red Sea normalization would compress ton-mile demand and tanker rates simultaneously. With 82% of revenues from the spot market, INSW has maximum operating leverage to rate moves in both directions.

## Company Overview

INSW operates a diversified tanker fleet spanning crude carriers (12 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes) and product carriers (1 LR2, 7 LR1s, 33 MRs). The company participates predominantly in the spot market via third-party commercial pools (82% of TCE revenues), supplemented by selective time charters (18%). The January 2026 acquisition of 100% ownership of Tankers International (TUKA) gives INSW direct commercial control over its VLCC pool and a newly launched Suezmax pool.

The operating model is capital-efficient: 66 shoreside employees manage 2,697 seafarers through outsourced technical and commercial management. This lean structure produces operating margins of 41-57% through the cycle. INSW also operates a lightering business in the U.S. Gulf, providing ship-to-ship transfer services that generate ancillary revenue with minimal capital intensity (though this segment declined from $23.3M to $7.8M operating income in FY2025).

The company redomiciled vessel-owning subsidiaries to Bermuda in Q4 2025 to comply with OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax rules, taking advantage of Bermuda's shipping income exclusion.

### Balance Sheet Strength

INSW maintains a strong balance sheet for a shipping company:

- **Debt/Equity:** 0.27x (down from 0.58x in 2022)
- **Net Debt/Capital:** 16.5%
- **Total Liquidity:** $724M ($117M cash + $557M undrawn revolver + other available capacity)
- **Unencumbered Vessels:** 31 (44% of fleet)
- **Current Ratio:** 3.71x
- **Altman Z-Score:** 3.79 (safe zone)

Debt maturities are well-distributed from 2030 to 2037, with no near-term refinancing pressure. The September 2025 $250M bond issuance at 7.125% was used to retire more expensive lease financing on 6 VLCCs, a net-positive capital structure transaction.

### Cash Flow and Capital Returns

OCF was $380M in FY2025 on $843M revenue (45% conversion). The company has returned over $1.0B to shareholders since 2020 through dividends and buybacks. The February 2026 dividend included a $2.03/share supplemental payment, the largest of the year, signaling management confidence in near-term cash generation.

Fleet renewal activity in 2025 generated ~$246M in vessel sale proceeds (recognizing $42.5M gains) while deploying ~$426M in vessel purchases and newbuild progress payments. Post year-end, 5 MRs and 2 VLCCs were agreed to be sold for $216M, continuing the strategy of selling older tonnage at elevated asset values.

### Structural Demand Drivers

1. **Geopolitical rerouting:** Tanker rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope maximizes ton-mile demand. Each VLCC cargo rerouted adds 20-30 days to the voyage, equivalent to removing tonnage from the market.

2. **Russia sanctions:** Continued enforcement forces Russian crude onto longer trade routes (India, China via Cape), absorbing Aframax and Suezmax capacity.

3. **Red Sea avoidance:** Houthi attacks continue to force product carriers around the Cape of Good Hope, increasing ton-mile demand by 15-20% on affected routes.

4. **OPEC+ production increases:** Q4 2025 OPEC production at 28.5M b/d (+1.8M b/d YoY) directly increases seaborne crude volumes.

5. **China crude imports:** Up 4.4% in 2025, with December 2025 at 13.2M b/d (+17% YoY).

### Supply Constraints

The tanker orderbook at 17% of fleet is elevated versus 7% in 2023, but deliveries are concentrated in 2028-2030. For 2026-2027, net fleet growth remains modest as scrapping of older vessels (particularly pre-2010 tonnage approaching 20+ year age limits) partially offsets deliveries. The average age of the global VLCC fleet continues to rise, and environmental regulations (CII ratings, EU ETS) are effectively removing older, less efficient vessels from premium trades.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.08, INSW trades at:
- **8.4x FY2026E EPS** ($9.92)
- **14.7x FY2027E EPS** ($5.64)
- **~4.1x trailing EV/EBITDA** (using FY2025 EBITDA of $475M, EV ~$4.5B)

The peer median P/E of 25.4x suggests substantial discount, though direct comparison is imperfect: LPG (Dorian LPG, 13.7x) is the closest comp and trades at a similar multiple, suggesting the market applies a consistent cyclical discount to spot-exposed tanker names.

The critical valuation question is whether FY2026E earnings of $9.92 are sustainable or represent a cyclical peak. The FY2027 consensus of $5.64 (43% decline) explicitly models rate normalization. At a mid-cycle earnings power of $6-7/share and a 10-12x multiple (appropriate for a well-capitalized tanker with this balance sheet), fair value ranges from $60-84. At peak earnings of $9.92 and 10x, fair value is $99.

The stock is trading near the lower bound of the consensus target range ($56-$90), with the median target at $88, implying ~6% upside to median. However, given the velocity of estimate revisions, target revisions likely lag.

The market is pricing INSW as if rates will compress to FY2027 levels within 12 months. Given the structural demand from geopolitical disruption and supply constraints, this appears overly conservative for the next 6-12 months.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW operates in a highly fragmented industry where no single owner influences rates. Competitive advantages are structural rather than economic moats:

1. **Fleet diversification** across crude (VLCCs, Suezmaxes, Aframaxes) and products (LR1s, MRs) provides revenue diversification. Product carriers declined while crude carriers surged in Q4 2025, smoothing the overall trajectory.

2. **TI platform ownership** (post-January 2026 acquisition) provides commercial control and potential cost savings versus paying third-party pool commissions. This is a genuine competitive advantage versus smaller operators who lack pool influence.

3. **Balance sheet flexibility** enables counter-cyclical capital deployment (buying at troughs, selling at peaks). INSW has executed this strategy effectively, selling 12 older vessels at elevated prices in 2025.

4. **Fleet age profile:** An active renewal program (buying 2020+ built vessels, selling pre-2010 tonnage) positions the fleet favorably for tightening environmental regulations.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Geopolitical Normalization (CRITICAL — HIGH impact)**
A diplomatic resolution to the conflicts driving tanker rerouting would compress tanker ton-mile demand immediately. VLCC rates could fall from current $75K-100K+/day to $40-50K/day within weeks, cutting EPS by 50%+. The 10-K explicitly warns that normalization of geopolitical instability "could cause tanker rates to decline significantly."

**2. Cyclical Rate Compression (ELEVATED — orderbook driven)**
The 17% orderbook represents medium-term supply risk. If geopolitical demand tailwinds fade simultaneously with 2028-2030 deliveries, rates could fall below mid-cycle levels.

**3. China Port Fee Risk (NEW — company-specific)**
13 Chinese-built vessels face potential fees when entering U.S. ports under new regulations. Currently suspended for one year (effective November 2025). Reimposition would increase operating costs and reduce fleet flexibility.

**4. Vessel Carrying Value Risk (MODERATE)**
9 MR vessels have carrying values exceeding market values by $43.3M. A sustained rate downturn could trigger impairments.

**5. Large Shareholder Selling (MONITOR)**
Famatown Finance (BW Group/Sohmen-Pao) sold 456,362 shares ($34.5M) in March 2026. While they retain ~15.9% ownership, continued selling could pressure shares.

### Options Market Signal

IV compression from 70.3% to 60.0% over the past two weeks (alongside a 9.9% price rally) indicates declining uncertainty as earnings momentum becomes more widely recognized.

## Conclusion

INSW is a well-positioned beneficiary of the current geopolitical environment at a valuation that implies excessive skepticism about earnings durability. The combination of: (1) extraordinary estimate momentum (+70% in 30 days), (2) 4 consecutive earnings beats averaging ~20%, (3) structural supply constraints limiting fleet growth through 2027, and (4) confirmed sustained rate strength into Q1 2026 provides a compelling risk/reward setup at 8.4x forward earnings.

The stock has rallied 144% from its April 2025 low of $33.50, which normally warrants caution. However, the rally reflects a fundamental transformation in the earnings profile (from $5.42 in FY2025 to $9.92 consensus for FY2026), not multiple expansion. At 8.4x, the multiple remains compressed versus both the peer median and INSW's own trailing 13.3x P/E.

BUY with 2-3% portfolio sizing. The position is explicitly geopolitical-conflict-dependent: a diplomatic resolution would unwind the thesis. The option-implied 9.6% move over the next month and backwardated term structure confirm this is a high-volatility, event-driven name. Size accordingly.
