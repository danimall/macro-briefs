**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Southwest Gas Holdings completed a multi-year corporate simplification in September 2025, exiting Centuri (utility infrastructure services) after divesting MountainWest (pipeline/storage) in early 2023. What remains is a pure-play regulated natural gas distribution utility serving roughly 2.29 million customers across Arizona (53% of margin), Nevada (35%), and California (12%), plus the FERC-regulated Great Basin Gas Transmission pipeline in northern Nevada. All three states operate under decoupled rate structures, which sever the link between volumetric sales and revenue and give the earnings stream low weather- and conservation-sensitivity. Continuing-operations EPS has grown steadily — $1.98 (2023), $3.08 (2024), $3.25 (2025) — driven by Arizona rate relief and 1.6% annual customer growth in the Phoenix and Las Vegas markets.

The forward story rests on two legs: a $6.3B 2026–2030 capital program that grows rate base at roughly 7% per year, and a Great Basin pipeline expansion whose April 2026 open season drew 2.5 Bcf/d of expressions of interest against the ~0.9 Bcf/d currently being built. That oversubscription, consistent with large northern Nevada load additions likely data-center-driven, is the under-modeled call option in the story. Against that, management confirmed no equity issuance is expected in 2026 and raised the dividend 4% to $2.58 annualized, both shareholder-friendly signals enabled by ~$1.3B of Centuri proceeds.

The reasons for a HOLD rather than a BUY are three: the stock trades at a full valuation (~28x trailing, ~22x forward) at a 12-month high with limited margin of safety versus a peer-median P/E of 15.6; an unremediated material weakness in internal controls, tied to a deferred-tax apportionment error dating to the 2017 holding-company formation, forced a $36.7M cumulative retained-earnings restatement and remained open as of Q1 2026; and the company sits in an unusually heavy rate-case cycle across three states whose outcomes are not yet resolved. The business quality and growth optionality are real, but the current entry price already discounts most of the good news.

## Company Overview

Southwest Gas Corporation is the largest regulated natural gas distributor in both Arizona and Nevada. Approximately 85% of operating margin comes from residential and small commercial customers, with ~11% from transportation, a stable and granular revenue base. The core competitive dynamic is a one-time installation decision (gas vs. electric appliance), after which the customer relationship is sticky for the equipment's life. Arizona law prohibits municipal natural-gas bans, and Nevada's SB 281 (2023) establishes a triennial resource-planning framework with pre-approval of investments, both of which structurally reinforce gas's role in those territories.

The Great Basin Gas Transmission pipeline, a FERC-regulated interstate asset in northern Nevada, is the growth vehicle that differentiates SWX from a plain local distribution company. The initial expansion is a ~$1.7B rate-base project; the open season signals a potential multi-phase runway through 2035 if northern Nevada load growth materializes as the interest levels suggest.

Following the Centuri exit, SWX is 100% regulated gas, with total assets of $10.4B (down from $12.0B in 2024 as Centuri was removed) and roughly balanced total capitalization ($3.43B long-term debt against $3.96B equity).

## Financial Analysis

The headline financials require care because the Centuri deconsolidation distorts year-over-year comparisons and prior periods have been recast with Centuri in discontinued operations.

**Earnings power.** FY2025 reported net income of $439.8M ($6.09 basic EPS) is inflated by a one-time $343.1M gain on the Centuri deconsolidation (including a $222.9M remeasurement gain), which contributed $2.84 of the $6.09. The cleaner figure is continuing-operations EPS of $3.25 (2025) vs. $3.08 (2024) vs. $1.98 (2023). The Natural Gas Distribution segment contributed $300.3M in 2025, up ~15% from $261.2M in 2024, driven by roughly $95.2M of incremental Arizona rate relief plus ~$11.5M from customer growth. Utility operating margin (non-GAAP) rose to $1.44B (2025) from $1.33B (2024) and $1.25B (2023). Any screen that treats the $6.09 as run-rate EPS will materially misjudge the company.

**Revenue optics.** Reported regulated revenue fell from $2.48B (2024) to $1.94B (2025), but this reflects lower pass-through gas costs (net cost of gas sold fell from $1.15B to $498M), which are earnings-neutral. Gas-cost pass-through produces large but margin-irrelevant revenue swings.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** The company paid off the entire $550M term loan and $159M of credit facility in 2025 using Centuri proceeds, a material deleveraging. Actual leverage is normal for a utility: long-term debt of $3.43B against equity of $3.96B (Q1 2026: $4.10B) is a D/E near 0.87. A standardized health-score screen reporting D/E of 0.03 and an Altman Z of 21.15 is a mis-mapping artifact and should be disregarded. Credit ratings are solidly investment grade and stable: Holdings at Baa2 / BBB+ / BBB and Southwest Gas Corp at Baa1 / BBB+ / A-, reaffirmed through 2025.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $594M against $808M capex, producing negative FCF of roughly -$214M. This is normal and expected for a growth-phase rate-base utility funding heavy capex, not a distress signal. Management expects operating cash flow to fund roughly 60% of the 2026–2030 capex-plus-dividend program. Q1 2026 OCF was $162M against $209M capex; cash stood at $485M with ~$1.2B total liquidity. The ~$800M YoY OCF decline in 2025 was driven substantially by refunds of over-collected PGA balances ($248M at Q1 2026, down from $305M), earnings-neutral but a liquidity drag.

**Capital plan and dividend.** The ~$6.3B capex program, including ~$1.7B for Great Basin, drives rate-base and thus earnings growth; gas plant in service has grown from $8.4B to $11.5B over five years. The dividend was raised 4% in February 2026 to $0.645/quarter ($2.58 annualized), paid continuously since 1956, at a yield of ~2.8% — below the peer median of 3.5%. Management stated it does not expect to issue equity in 2026, though the plan and $1.5B of debt maturities over five years leave substantial external financing needs.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two identifiable, quantifiable drivers. First, rate-base growth: 37,000 first-time meter sets in 2025 driven by Sun Belt population inflows, combined with the capital plan, supports a mid-single-digit EPS compounding trajectory typical of a constructive-jurisdiction LDC. The heavy rate-case cycle currently in progress converts that capex into recoverable rate base:

- **Arizona** (GRC filed Feb 27, 2026): requesting +$101M (+10.4%), 10.25% ROE, 50.08% equity, plus a formula-based Rate Adjustment Mechanism to reduce regulatory lag; rates expected April 2027.
- **Nevada** (GRC filed March 18, 2026): +$71.3M (+10.8%), 10.0% ROE; rates expected October 2026.
- **California**: $39.5M settlement (before cost-of-capital litigation), rates expected Q2 2026.

The prior Arizona award came in at 9.84% ROE on 48.5% equity, below the 10.15% request, so the pending requests will likely be trimmed. The estimate revision picture is muted: current-year consensus EPS of $4.22 is essentially flat over 30 days and up 1.9% over 90 days, next-year $4.88 is flat-to-slightly-down, and the current quarter was cut 16.5% in 30 days. This is a stable, not accelerating, estimate profile; it does not clear the threshold that would flag a genuine earnings inflection.

Second is the Great Basin optionality. The open season drew 2.5 Bcf/d of expressions of interest — roughly 1.0 Bcf/d targeting 2028–2029 service and ~1.5 Bcf/d for phased 2030–2035 — against the ~0.9 Bcf/d currently being built. This points to a potential multi-phase expansion program well beyond the initial project, driven by what appears to be data-center-and-electrification demand in northern Nevada. Financing authority is being secured: the ACC approved (April 8, 2026) a waiver allowing $1.7B of funding to Great Basin, and a California CPUC application seeks incremental debt authority up to ~$1.15B (total ~$2.1B), with a decision expected end-2026. FERC construction approval remains pending and initial construction is not expected until Q4 2027, so this is a multi-year, not near-term, earnings contributor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.07, SWX trades at ~28x trailing continuing-ops EPS and roughly 22x forward consensus. Against a Gas Utilities peer median P/E of 15.6, this is a substantial premium: UGI 12.0x, NFG 10.3x, NJR 16.1x, SR 15.6x, OGS 17.0x. The dividend yield is also below the peer median, so the stock offers less income support than comparables.

The premium is partly defensible: SWX's decoupled, pure-play Sun Belt structure, above-average customer growth, and the Great Basin optionality justify some multiple above a mature, volume-exposed LDC. But at 22x forward earnings for a business growing EPS at a mid-single-digit regulated pace, the PEG is well above 2.0, and the current price sits within ~1.5% of the 52-week high of $94.47 after an 18.8% run over the trailing year. Analyst targets bracket the price at $94 (low) to $107 (high), mean $100.5, median $100, implying roughly 8% upside to the mean before dividends, with the low target essentially at spot. That is the target dispersion of a fairly-valued utility trading toward the top of its analyst range.

A superficially "quality" story does not translate into an attractive valuation score here. The stock is expensive, not cheap, and a premium regulated-utility multiple carries asymmetric risk: rate-case outcomes below request, a higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the cost of the negative-FCF financing model, or any escalation of the internal-controls issue would compress the multiple faster than the growth can offset. The Great Basin optionality is real and under-modeled, but it is not yet contracted at scale, is subject to FERC approval, and does not begin contributing until 2028+. Buying it at 22x forward means paying full price today for an option that resolves years out. The valuation score reflects a stretched multiple relative to fundamentals with limited near-term margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

SWX's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its structural advantages — largest distributor in Arizona and Nevada, decoupled rates in all three states, and statutory/regulatory protection of gas use in its two largest jurisdictions — insulate margin from weather, conservation, and volume risk. Sun Belt population growth provides an organic customer-addition tailwind that most mature LDCs lack. Management sees no material near-term margin threat from electrification or fuel-switching, and the primary competitive threat (electric utilities at the point of appliance installation) is slow-moving.

California is the more challenging jurisdiction, with electrification policy, cap-and-invest, and AB 1207 gradually shifting free allowances from gas to electric utilities representing a longer-term cost headwind, but California is only 12% of margin. The five-year cumulative TSR of 157.14 versus a peer group at 171.29 shows the stock modestly lagged its comparables through the simplification, but the trajectory is improving as the pure-play structure matures. The Great Basin oversubscription strengthens the competitive positioning of the FERC pipeline segment, turning what was a modest transportation contributor into a potential multi-phase growth vehicle. On balance, trajectory is favorable, though the premium multiple already prices much of that improvement.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness and restatement (the key differentiated risk).** In January 2026 the company identified a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting relating to estimating future state apportionment rates for deferred taxes. This triggered an Item 4.02 "Non-Reliance" 8-K (Jan 9, 2026) and a restatement of errors dating back to the 2017 formation of the holding company. Cumulative impact was a $36.7M reduction in retained earnings (as of March 31, 2025) with an offsetting deferred-tax-liability increase; individual-period effects were small (Q1 2025 net income revised down just $0.3M). The material weakness remained unremediated as of March 31, 2026, and remediation is being layered onto a mid-2026 financial-consolidation software implementation that introduces its own control-transition risk. This is a non-cash, tax-estimate-driven issue rather than operational fraud or a cash-flow problem, but it is a genuine quality-of-earnings and governance flag that warrants monitoring and is the single most important item not fully priced.

**Rate-case concentration.** With three simultaneous general rate cases and one prior award already below request, outcomes below request would compress returns during the peak-capex phase. This is a near-term, dated risk with resolution across Q2 2026 (California), October 2026 (Nevada), and April 2027 (Arizona).

**Great Basin execution.** The expansion (and any multi-phase follow-on) depends on scarce skilled pipeline labor, certified welders, and long-lead-time steel/compression equipment in a tight market; FERC approval is pending. Cost overruns or delays could impair projected returns.

**Executive turnover.** The 10-K explicitly elevated 2025 executive turnover to a named risk factor, with multiple Item 5.02 filings across 2025–2026. Leadership transition during a peak-capex, controls-remediation, and system-migration phase is a real, compounding operational risk.

**Interest-rate sensitivity and geographic concentration.** As a capital-intensive, negative-FCF, dividend-yield security, both the equity valuation and the financing cost of the capital plan are rate-sensitive; a higher-for-longer path is a direct headwind. And 88% of margin comes from just Arizona and Nevada, concentrating exposure to those two economies, weather patterns, and regulators.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 18.9% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 18.0% (a 0.9pp spread, fair), and IV percentile within the current chain is a low 6%. The options market is not pricing an imminent shock, and the sharp drop in ATM IV from 68.3% to 18.9% since mid-June reflects the passing of the earnings event. The term structure is in contango (18.9% near-term rising to 23.3% at six months), a normal-to-complacent structure. The one notable signal is a 12.7% put/call skew at the near tenor (elevated put demand), which flips to a -4.1% call-skew at six months, consistent with modest hedging into the near-term rate-case calendar and controls overhang rather than a directional bearish conviction. Short interest is low at 3.9% of float (3.3 days to cover), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor a crowded bearish view. Net, the options market is broadly neutral-to-complacent, offering no strong contradiction to the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SWX is a cleanly transformed, decoupled Sun Belt gas utility compounding EPS at a mid-single-digit rate on the capital plan, with above-peer customer growth and no expected 2026 equity dilution. The Great Basin open season is a materially under-modeled multi-phase growth option that, if it converts, transforms the FERC segment into a major earnings contributor by 2028–2035. Constructive Arizona and Nevada regulation, a resolved Icahn overhang, and investment-grade credit support a re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull target ~$107 (analyst high; ~22x on rising forward estimates plus Great Basin optionality being priced in), roughly +15% from current.

**Bear case.** The stock is expensive (22x forward, 2.8% yield vs. 3.5% peer median) at a 12-month high, offering limited margin of safety. The unremediated material weakness escalates, or a system-migration misstep produces a further restatement; rate-case awards come in below request during peak capex; a higher-for-longer rate environment raises financing costs on a negative-FCF model and compresses the premium multiple toward the peer group. Multiple compression toward ~18x on $4.22 would imply roughly $76, near the 52-week low — bear target ~$76–80, roughly -14% to -18% from current.

**Base case.** Steady rate-base-driven EPS growth with incremental Great Basin optionality gradually recognized, offset by a full starting multiple and unresolved governance/rate-case overhangs. Base target ~$97 (roughly the analyst median area, ~20x forward on modest estimate progression), approximately +4% plus a ~2.8% dividend over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD, classified as a growth_hold; the positive thesis rests on rate-base compounding and Great Basin optionality, but the entry offers insufficient upside to warrant a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Remediation of the material weakness confirmed complete with a clean control attestation, removing the governance overhang.
- Great Basin open-season interest converting into signed, binding precedent agreements materially above the initial ~0.9 Bcf/d, plus FERC construction approval — de-risking the multi-phase optionality.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $80s (roughly 18–19x forward, near the peer-adjusted premium), restoring a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further restatement or expansion of the internal-controls issue, or a failed consolidation-system implementation.
- Rate-case awards materially below request in Arizona or Nevada (e.g., authorized ROE below ~9.5% or large disallowances) that compress the forward earnings trajectory.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration or a forced return to equity issuance that dilutes per-share growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** The California rate decision (Q2 2026), the Nevada rate decision (October 2026), the mid-2026 consolidation-system go-live and controls-remediation status, and next earnings are the natural checkpoints. The Great Basin FERC approval and end-2026 CPUC financing decision are the key optionality milestones.

## Conclusion

Southwest Gas is a higher-quality business today than it was as a conglomerate: a focused, decoupled Sun Belt gas distributor with genuine rate-base growth and an under-appreciated Great Basin pipeline call option. Current data broadly confirms the risk profile: the material weakness remains unremediated, the rate-case cycle is unresolved, and the stock has climbed to a full multiple near its 52-week high while estimate revisions stay flat. The Q1 miss ($1.91 actual vs. $2.12 estimate, -10.0%) and the reported ~5.6% post-earnings decline reflect the rate-sensitivity backdrop and controls overhang, not a fundamental break, but they also do not create a compelling entry.

The right posture is to hold for the growth and the Great Basin optionality while waiting for either a cleaner risk profile (controls remediation, binding pipeline contracts) or a better price. There is limited margin of safety at 22x forward and a yield below peers, and the burden of proof for paying up sits on optionality that resolves years out. **HOLD.**
