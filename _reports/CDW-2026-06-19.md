**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CDW is the leading multi-brand IT value-added reseller (VAR) in the US, UK, and Canada, sitting between 1,000+ vendor partners and 250,000+ customers. The business is asset-light, generates ~$1.1B of free cash flow on minimal capex, and returns nearly all of it through buybacks (~4% of shares annually) and a ~2% dividend. The investment debate has narrowed to a single tension: revenue has re-accelerated to +9.2% in Q1 2026, but that growth is concentrated in lower-margin AI hardware (servers, storage, devices), which is compressing gross margin (21.9% FY24 → 21.0% Q1 2026) and leaving operating income flat. EPS growth is being manufactured by share-count reduction, not operating leverage.

The most actionable signal in the data is open-market insider buying into weakness. CEO Christine Leahy bought ~$499K at $103.40 on May 18, and Director David Nelms bought ~$2.0M at $111.43 on May 27, both into the post-Q1 selloff that took the stock to its 52-week low of $97.12. Two C-suite/board members committing personal capital near the bottom, immediately preceding a ~30% bounce, is a genuine conviction tell and the strongest piece of bullish evidence here. It is balanced against a multi-year stretch of peer underperformance (cumulative TSR 109.7 vs. peer group 182.3 per the proxy) and the structural question of whether a VAR can defend margins as hyperscaler marketplaces and direct OEM selling encroach on the channel.

We rate CDW a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. At ~12.0x current-year EPS (consensus $10.66) and ~11x FCF, the stock is no longer expensive after the drawdown, and the capital-return floor plus reaccelerating revenue provide downside support. The bull case is a yield-plus-buyback story without operating-leverage proof, and the secular margin/disintermediation threat is real and unresolved. The score trajectory has declined (6.4 → 5.8 → 5.5 over four months); our current read stabilizes rather than extends that decline, recognizing that a ~25% lower price on broadly rising estimates improves risk/reward without changing the structural questions.

## Company Overview

CDW procures more than 100,000 products and services from over 1,000 vendor partners (Apple, Cisco, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, Nvidia) and two large wholesale distributors (Ingram Micro, TD SYNNEX), then resells them while layering on advisory, integration, configuration, and managed services. It is a reseller, not a manufacturer. Roughly 10,400 customer-facing coworkers serve a base of 250,000+ customers; more than half of US sales come from account managers with 7+ years of tenure, which underpins the "trusted advisor" positioning.

The economics are characteristic of distribution: ~$22.4B of gross net sales translates to a 21.7% gross margin and a 7.4% operating margin. A growing share of software and services is recorded on a net (agent) basis, which is why ~72% of revenue is hardware but software and services contribute a disproportionate share of gross profit. That accounting detail matters for the margin debate: as hardware mix rises, both the revenue line and the margin profile move in ways that can look contradictory.

Effective January 1, 2026, CDW realigned into three reportable segments, Commercial (~63% of sales), Government (~11%), and Education (~12%), plus Other (UK/Canada, ~14%). End-market diversification has historically helped the company absorb sector-specific demand swings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The top line is cyclical and lumpy: FY2022 $23.75B, FY2023 $21.38B (-10% on post-COVID hardware digestion), FY2024 $21.00B (-1.8%), and FY2025 $22.42B (+6.8%, a clear recovery). Q1 2026 revenue was $5.68B, +9.2% YoY (+8.4% constant currency), broad-based and led by data storage, servers, netcomm, software, and notebooks. Momentum is accelerating.

**Margins — the central issue.** Gross margin slipped from 21.9% (FY24) to 21.7% (FY25) to 21.0% in Q1 2026, down 60 bps YoY, driven by mix shift toward lower-margin AI hardware and a smaller contribution from high-margin netted-down revenue. Operating margin fell from 7.9% (FY24) to 7.4% (FY25) to 6.6% in Q1 2026; SG&A rose 9% in FY25 on performance comp, transformation costs, and AI investment. Non-GAAP operating margin was 8.9% in FY25 (adding back ~$170M intangible amortization, ~$84M SBC, ~$58M transformation costs).

**Profitability and returns.** Net income was essentially flat at ~$1.07B in FY25 despite the revenue recovery; operating leverage was absent because margin compression and rising SG&A offset volume. Non-GAAP EPS grew to $10.02 (FY25) from $9.52 (FY24), with most of the gain attributable to buybacks. ROE of 40.9% is flattered by a thin equity base; ROA of 6.7% is the cleaner read on asset productivity.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** This is the heart of the bull case. FY25 operating cash flow was $1.21B and FCF was $1.09B (~4.9% FCF margin) on just ~$117M of capex. FY25 capital return was $653M of buybacks (4.0M shares) plus ~$330M of dividends; Q1 2026 added $201M of buybacks and $81M of dividends. Diluted share count fell from 135.2M (FY24) to 129.5M (Q1 2026), a ~4%/year reduction. The dividend is $0.63/quarter ($2.52 annualized), roughly a 2% yield, raised modestly in late 2025.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$5.6B, net debt ~$5.0B. Reported D/E of 6.15x and equity/assets of 16.3% look alarming on a screen and produce an Altman Z-Score of 2.16 (grey zone), but this is structural: retained earnings are negative (-$1.27B) from years of buybacks tied to the LBO heritage, not operating distress. Interest coverage is comfortable at ~7.3x (operating income $1.66B vs. interest expense $227M), and the Piotroski F-Score is 5/5 on available signals. The genuine constraint is reduced flexibility and rate sensitivity, not solvency.

The watch item is a $1.0B tranche of 2.670% senior notes due December 2026, against only ~$619M of cash. CDW will refinance via revolver capacity (a new 5-year $2.25B revolver, +$650M capacity, established December 2025, plus a $634.5M term loan, both maturing 2030) or new issuance. Rolling a 2.67% coupon at current market rates is a modest but real interest-expense headwind. Separately, DSO rose to 96 days from 84 as customers shift to multi-year software subscriptions (growing unbilled receivables), a structural change worth monitoring, partly offset by rising DPO; the cash conversion cycle remains a tight 16 days.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided. Revenue has reaccelerated to +9.2%, and estimate revisions are positive across all horizons: current-year EPS consensus moved to $10.66 (+1.4% over 90 days), and next-year to $11.64 (+2.9% over 90 days). The 90-day next-year revision of +2.9% is positive but well below the velocity that would flag a true earnings inflection; this is steady improvement, not a step-change.

The problem is the quality of growth. Revenue acceleration is led by AI-capable hardware, which is dilutive to margin. Operating income has not grown alongside revenue; net income was flat-to-down across FY23–FY25 despite the top-line recovery. The +9% revenue print and the flat operating profit tell the same story from two angles: CDW is selling more of a lower-margin mix. The earnings track record supports modest confidence in estimates (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters), though the most recent quarter (Q1 2026, reported May 6) was a 1.2% miss, the first miss in the series, and it coincided with the selloff.

The strategic response is a pivot up the value chain into higher-margin advisory, security, and managed services (Digital Velocity), signaled most concretely by the August 2025 hire of Mukesh Kumar (ex-President of Slalom Consulting) as Chief Services & Solutions Officer. If that re-mix works, it would convert revenue growth into margin and operating leverage. It is unproven, carries near-term transformation costs ($58M in FY25), and is the single most important variable for the durability of the growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $128.37, CDW trades at ~12.0x current-year consensus EPS of $10.66 and ~11x FCF (per the Argosy framing). That is a discount to the Technology Distributors peer median P/E of 19.3x. The peer set is heterogeneous, however: ARW at 15.9x and NSIT at 19.3x are the most relevant comparables; SNX (23.1x) and AVT (34.6x) carry different business mixes and cycle positions. Against ARW specifically, CDW's discount is modest, and CDW's higher-margin services-led model arguably warrants a premium, which argues there is some unrecognized value here.

The case against paying up is that the discount may be appropriate given flat operating income, margin erosion, and the disintermediation overhang. A "cheap" multiple on a business with no operating-leverage proof and a secular margin question is the classic value-trap profile, and we treat the low multiple as a reason for caution rather than an automatic buy signal. The bull case is a shareholder-yield story: a ~2% dividend plus ~4%/year buybacks gives a ~6% base shareholder yield, which provides downside support and a path to mid-single-digit EPS growth even with flat fundamentals.

Analyst targets bracket the range: low $123, mean $147.3, median $144.5, high $195.0, against the current $128.37. The median implies ~13% upside. Sentiment is degrading (the analyst sentiment trend is flagged as such), and the recommendation distribution (1 strong buy, 6 buy, 4 hold) shows no conviction either way. We do not treat the consensus median as a floor here because the growth profile is not a clear inflection and margins are still compressing; the conditions for "targets lag reality" do not apply when operating profit is flat.

Our base-case fair value is ~$140, applying ~13x to FY26 consensus EPS of $10.66, a modest re-rating from the current ~12.0x as revenue growth and the buyback compound, but no margin recovery assumed. That is ~9% above the current price, roughly in line with the median analyst target.

## Competitive Landscape

CDW's moat is scale, vendor certifications (favorable pricing and co-op marketing dollars), a deep tenured sales force, and vendor-neutral trusted-advisor positioning, particularly valuable to slower-adopting government, education, and mid-market customers who need integration help. These are real advantages, and they have allowed CDW to hold a leadership position in a fragmented market.

The competitive trajectory is the concern that has driven the score down (competitive position fell from 7.0 to 5.5 across prior reports; we mark it 5.0 here). The FY2025 10-K materially expanded language on two structural threats. First, hyperscaler marketplaces (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Marketplace) and direct OEM selling could disintermediate the reseller channel, limiting access to offerings, pressuring margins, and restricting participation in certain channels. This is the existential question for the VAR model and applies to the entire industry. Second, AI-driven mix shift toward lower-margin hardware, compounded by memory and storage shortages as OEMs prioritize datacenter/AI workloads, is lifting revenue while compressing margin and lengthening lead times.

The proxy's disclosure that CDW's cumulative TSR (109.7) has materially lagged its peer group (182.3) is the market's verdict on this trajectory over a multi-year window. The bull rebuttal is that scale, the services pivot, and the trusted-advisor role for less cloud-native customers preserve relevance, and that disintermediation has been predicted for the channel for years without materializing at scale. Both can be true; the trajectory currently points sideways-to-down.

## Risk Assessment

**Vendor and channel concentration / disintermediation (top risk).** Vendor agreements are mostly short-term and terminable on notice; the three largest vendors each represent >$2.0B of sales, and two distributors (Ingram Micro, TD SYNNEX) account for >25% of purchases. The secular disintermediation threat from cloud marketplaces and direct OEM selling sits underneath the entire model.

**AI-driven margin/mix risk.** Surging AI hardware demand is the proximate cause of the gross-margin decline and the operating-margin slide. Memory/storage shortages are supportive of ASPs and revenue but a risk to device availability and a drag on mix quality.

**Government/public-sector exposure.** Government plus Education is ~23% of sales. Federal budget uncertainty, shifting administration priorities, and shutdown risk could delay or reduce spending; Government segment gross margin already fell 260 bps YoY in Q1 2026.

**DOJ E-Rate investigation.** CDW received a Civil Investigative Demand from the DOJ on June 11, 2024 regarding bids for the Schools and Libraries (E-Rate) Program. CDW responded in November 2024 and says it is unaware of further activity, but cannot assess probability or impact. This is an unresolved False Claims Act overhang.

**Refinancing at higher rates.** The December 2026 note will be rolled at materially higher current rates, and there is a $634.5M variable-rate term loan. Manageable given FCF and revolver capacity, but a real interest-expense headwind.

**Execution risk on the transformation.** A nearly-new C-suite (the Slalom hire, a new CHRO, expanded CFO/CLO/CCO roles dated March 2025) is reshaping the company around a higher-margin services agenda. The capability is promising; execution is unproven and adds near-term cost.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 41.3% — fairly priced, with IV in the 5th percentile of the current chain (no elevated event premium near-term). The put/call volume ratio of 3.15 and put/call OI ratio of 2.38 look bearish at first glance, but the term structure is in contango (near-term 41.6% < long-term 47.1%), a normal/complacent structure, and the put/call skew is essentially balanced (-0.7% at 1m, widening to -4.3% at 6m). The 6-month put skew suggests modest demand for downside protection further out, consistent with the unresolved structural questions, but the near-term market is not pricing acute stress. The OI-ratio data point that jumped from 0.61 to 2.38 day-over-day, paired with a corrupted 664% IV print that reset to 41.6%, indicates a thin-options data artifact; the clean reads (fair IV, contango, balanced near-term skew) are the informative signals. The implied ±9.2% move into the July expiry is roughly in line with the stock's realized volatility and does not flag a specific catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$160–$195).** Revenue reacceleration to +9% holds and broadens, the services/solutions pivot begins to re-mix margin upward, and the ~6% base shareholder yield (2% dividend + 4% buyback) compounds into mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth. A re-rating from ~12x toward the 15–16x ARW-area multiple, applied to rising EPS, supports $160+; the high analyst target of $195 assumes both margin recovery and multiple expansion. Insider buying near the lows lends credibility that management sees the recent weakness as a mispricing.

**Bear case (~$95–$105).** AI-driven hardware mix continues to compress gross margin below 21%, the services pivot fails to offset it, operating income stays flat or declines, and disintermediation begins to show up in slower growth or further margin pressure. The DOJ E-Rate matter escalates, government spending softens, and the multiple stays compressed at 11–12x on flat-to-down EPS. That returns the stock to its 52-week low region near $97–$105, roughly 20–24% below the current price.

The probability-weighted center sits close to the current price. The capital-return floor and the insider conviction support the downside; the absence of operating leverage and the secular margin question cap the upside. This is a balanced setup, not an asymmetric one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The thesis is genuinely balanced: neither a confident appreciation case nor a clear value-trap, but a yield-supported business facing an unresolved structural margin question.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint, specifically gross-margin trajectory and any evidence the services re-mix is taking hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands (back above 21.5%) while revenue growth holds above mid-single digits, indicating the services pivot is converting volume into margin.
- Operating income grows YoY (not just EPS via buybacks), demonstrating genuine operating leverage for the first time in three years.
- Resolution of the DOJ E-Rate investigation with no material liability, removing a binary overhang while the stock remains below ~13x forward EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin falls below ~20.5% with no offsetting services contribution, signaling structural rather than cyclical erosion.
- Revenue growth decelerates back toward flat while margins stay compressed, indicating the demand recovery was transient.
- The DOJ E-Rate matter escalates to a quantified material liability, or a major vendor/distributor relationship is disrupted.
- The December 2026 refinancing proves more expensive or constrained than expected, materially raising interest expense relative to FCF.

## Conclusion

CDW is a high-quality, asset-light distributor that returns nearly all of its ~$1.1B FCF to shareholders, and after a ~25% twelve-month decline it trades at ~12.0x forward EPS and ~11x FCF, no longer expensive. The clearest positive signal is open-market insider buying by the CEO and a director (~$2.5M combined) into the May lows, which preceded a sharp bounce and is a credible contrarian tell. Estimate revisions are positive, and revenue has reaccelerated to +9%.

The offsetting reality is that growth is being delivered in a lower-margin AI-hardware mix that has compressed gross margin five quarters running and left operating income flat, so EPS growth is buyback-engineered rather than operating-leverage-driven. The secular disintermediation threat from cloud marketplaces and direct OEM selling, multi-year peer underperformance (TSR 109.7 vs. 182.3), the DOJ E-Rate overhang, and a $1B note to refinance at higher rates round out a balanced risk profile. The services pivot under a largely new C-suite is the swing factor, and it is unproven. We rate CDW a HOLD, with a fair-value estimate of ~$140 (~9% upside), and would look to upgrade on evidence of margin stabilization and genuine operating leverage at the August quarter.
