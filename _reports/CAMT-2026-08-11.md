## Executive Summary

Camtek sells optical inspection and 3D metrology tools into advanced interconnect packaging, heterogeneous integration and HBM — the highest-growth pocket of semiconductor capital equipment. The demand backdrop is confirmed rather than speculative: management states roughly 50% of FY2025 revenue was AI-related, the company has booked approximately $600M of orders since January for 2026-2027 delivery, and Q3 2026 guidance of $158-160M implies +25% to +27% year-over-year growth after a Q1 trough of +2.6%.

The tension is that Camtek's reported profitability is moving in the opposite direction from its revenue records. Q2 2026 revenue rose 8.0% to $133.2M while GAAP operating income fell 15% and non-GAAP operating margin compressed 330bp to 27.0%. For the first half, revenue grew 5.4% while total operating expense grew 25.0% (R&D +42.0%), producing a 15.7% decline in GAAP operating income and a 5.7% decline in non-GAAP diluted EPS. Cash conversion deteriorated alongside it: Q2 operating cash flow was $12.2M against $23.3M of GAAP net income, with trade receivables rising from $90.8M at year-end to $153.9M at June 30 — a DSO extension from roughly 65 to roughly 105 days in six months.

At $161.21 the stock trades at 46.1x current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS of $3.50 and 35.7x next-year consensus of $4.52, with enterprise value near $7.20B against guided FY2026 revenue of approximately $586M (~12.3x sales). That multiple requires H2 revenue of at least $331M versus $254.9M in H1, with Q4 implied near $172M against the $133.2M just delivered, *and* a recovery to roughly 30% non-GAAP operating margin that no quarter of 2026 has produced. Balance-sheet strength (net cash ~$327M, current ratio 7.4x, zero-coupon debt maturing 2030) removes financing risk from the equation but does not create margin of safety at this multiple. Probability-weighting a $178 base, $220 bull and $108 bear produces a low-single-digit expected return. That is a HOLD, and the honest classification is uncertainty rather than conviction: the two most decision-relevant questions — whether the R&D step-up is pre-investment or structural, and why DSO extended 40 days — cannot be resolved from disclosed data.

## Company Overview

Camtek (Migdal Ha'Emek, Israel; NASDAQ and TASE) designs and manufactures inspection and metrology systems that examine integrated circuits and measure IC features on wafers from the front end through mid-end and into the beginning of assembly (post-dicing). Served markets, as the company enumerates them, are advanced interconnect packaging, heterogeneous integration, memory and HBM, CMOS image sensors, compound semiconductors, MEMS and RF. Customers are described as global IDMs, OSATs and foundries. Manufacturing sits in Israel and Germany, with eight sales and support offices.

The commercial franchise runs on two platforms: **Eagle G5** (2D optical inspection, positioned on flexibility and cost) and **Hawk** (throughput and accuracy). Management guided these two to at least 50% of 2026 revenue on the February 2026 call; a secondary account of the Q1 2026 call put the two platforms at roughly 30% of 2025 revenue with revenue expected to double in 2026 (the two data points come from different calls and could not be reconciled against a filing). In Q2 2026 the company completed the acquisition of **Visual Layer**, which a tier-3 secondary source describes as adding AI-based defect detection and classification software layered on the existing hardware. Consideration was not disclosed; goodwill rose $38.4M and intangibles $6.6M over H1, implying mid-$40M-plus, with $2.6M of one-time M&A cost recorded in the quarter.

The structural mechanism worth understanding: as HBM stacks get taller and 2.5D/3D chiplet assemblies get more complex, the number of yield-critical inspection and metrology steps per package rises. If Camtek qualifies for more steps, tool intensity per unit of customer capacity rises and revenue can outgrow packaging capacity itself. That is the bull mechanism, and it is a step-coverage argument rather than a share-gain argument.

A note on data hygiene: Camtek is a 20-F filer, not a 10-K filer, and standardized quality/distress screens are unavailable. The trailing and forward P/E of 149.38 shown in the market-data feed uses FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.045, which was depressed by a one-time $100.9M pre-tax charge. That figure is mechanically misleading and should be disregarded.

## Financial Analysis

### The FY2025 GAAP break was a refinancing charge

FY2025 revenue was $496.1M (+15.6%) with operating income of $128.2M (25.8% margin), yet GAAP net income was only $50.7M. The company's own GAAP-to-non-GAAP reconciliation attributes this to $(100.9)M of "other expenses" from the extinguishment of Capital Notes, offset by a $12.3M tax benefit — an $88.7M net charge. The income-statement figures tie exactly to the XBRL data from the 20-F filed March 19, 2026. FY2025 non-GAAP net income was $159.0M (32.1% margin) on non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.26.

The transaction: in September 2025 Camtek priced an upsized $425M of 0.00% convertible senior notes due 2030 (a secondary summary indicates the final size reached $500M; carrying value at June 30, 2026 was $488.5M). Total debt rose from $198.5M at June 30, 2025 to $519.1M at September 30 (+$320.6M) while cash and investments rose only $203.5M, consistent with the legacy notes having been substantially cash-settled at a premium to carrying value. The refinancing simultaneously added roughly 4.9M shares of if-converted dilution.

The charge is genuinely non-recurring, but it was not costless: it consumed cash and expanded the diluted share count. Non-GAAP figures also exclude $16.8M of FY2025 share-based compensation, a real economic cost.

### 2026: record revenue, declining profit

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $133.2M | $123.3M | +8.0% |
| Gross margin (GAAP) | 50.1% | 50.8% | -70bp |
| R&D | $16.68M | $11.47M | +45.4% |
| SG&A | $22.79M | $19.16M | +18.9% |
| Operating income (GAAP) | $27.23M | $31.97M | -15% |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 20.4% | 25.9% | -550bp |
| Operating margin (non-GAAP) | 27.0% | 30.3% | -330bp |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $0.46 | $0.69 | -33% |
| Diluted EPS (non-GAAP) | $0.78 | $0.79 | -1% |

For H1 2026: revenue $254.9M (+5.4%), GAAP operating income $54.5M (-15.7%), non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.48 versus $1.57 (-5.7%). Gross margin held at 50-51%, so the operating-margin decline is entirely an operating-expense phenomenon — H1 opex $73.1M versus $58.5M, +25.0% against 5.4% revenue growth. Diluted shares rose from 49,327K to 51,520K (+4.4%), which is why non-GAAP net income grew 2% in Q2 while non-GAAP EPS declined 1%.

### Cash conversion is the near-term quality flag

Annual figures are healthy: FY2025 operating cash flow of approximately $132M against $14.4M of capex (FCF ~$118M), and FY2024 OCF of approximately $68.6M against $10.1M capex. These annual OCF figures are sums of third-party quarterly data rather than read directly from the audited cash flow statement. FY2025 OCF against $50.7M of GAAP net income independently confirms the extinguishment charge was not an operating cash cost.

Q2 2026 broke the pattern. Operating cash flow was $12.2M against $23.3M of GAAP net income. Trade receivables rose $63.1M (+69%) over six months while H1 revenue grew 5.4%; inventories declined $12.4M. Derived DSO: $153.9M ÷ $133.2M × 91 ≈ 105 days at June 30 versus $90.8M ÷ $128.1M × 92 ≈ 65 days at December 31. Plausible explanations include revenue landing in the final weeks of a quarter that "came ahead of expectations," a mix shift toward large HBM/IDM accounts with longer terms, or slower collection. None can be distinguished from disclosed data, and a single quarter's DSO in a business shipping $133M of tools is not evidence of credit deterioration. I have deliberately not annualized the $12.2M — equipment working capital is lumpy and a 40-day DSO extension is precisely the case where a run rate misleads. Two consecutive quarters above 100 days would change the read materially.

### Balance sheet

At June 30, 2026: cash, deposits and marketable securities of $815.8M; convertible notes of $488.5M as the only interest-bearing debt at a 0.00% coupon maturing 2030; net cash approximately $327M; total current assets $924.4M against current liabilities $125.3M (current ratio ~7.4x); shareholders' equity $710.2M. The investment portfolio out-earns the debt: net financial income was $7.0M in Q2 and $25.1M in FY2025. Solvency risk is remote and the maturity is four years out. No dividends were paid and no shares repurchased in H1 2026 — retained earnings rose exactly the $55.0M of H1 net income and treasury shares were unchanged.

One unresolved item: convertible carrying value *declined* $31.3M in H1 while additional paid-in capital rose $40.8M against only $8.0M of SBC and shares outstanding rose 840,044. Partial conversion or equity settlement is the most consistent hypothesis; a zero-coupon note at amortized cost would normally accrete upward. The mechanism is undisclosed.

### Tax

FY2025 income tax expense of $1.6M on $52.3M pre-tax includes the $12.3M extinguishment benefit. Excluding it, the effective rate was approximately 9.1% ($13.9M on $153.3M). Q2 2026 reported 31.9%, but includes a $7.7M one-time tax expense added back in non-GAAP; ex-item the rate is approximately 9.4%. Roughly a fifth to a quarter of Camtek's net margin versus a normally-taxed peer traces to Israeli preferential tax status — a legitimate but policy-dependent input to the multiple.

**Financial health: 7.0.** Fortress liquidity, zero effective interest cost, distant maturity, and positive annual FCF support a high score. Weak Q2 cash conversion, the 40-day DSO extension, 4.4% annual diluted-share creep, and earnings leverage to a 9% tax rate keep it out of the 8s.

## Growth Analysis

Year-over-year quarterly revenue growth traces a clear deceleration and a guided reversal:

- FY2024: +33.9%, +39.1%, +39.6%, +32.3%
- FY2025: +22.3%, +20.2%, +12.2%, +9.2%
- FY2026: **+2.6%, +8.0%**
- Q3 2026 guide: $158-160M → **+25.4% to +27.0%**, and +20% sequentially

Management guides H2 2026 revenue "more than 30%" above H1. On $254.9M of H1, that implies H2 ≥$331.4M and FY2026 ≥$586.3M (+18.2%). With Q3 at the $159M midpoint, Q4 must reach approximately $172M — a +20% sequential step followed by another +8%. The CEO cites approximately $600M of orders received since January with deliveries scheduled across 2026 *and* 2027, and expects "phenomenal growth in our AP business of 45% half over half."

Definitional discipline matters here. The $600M is an issuer statement of orders received year-to-date spanning two delivery years. It is not disclosed as total backlog, is not a remaining-performance-obligation figure, and its cancellability and scope are undefined. It cannot be read as 2026 revenue: FY2026 revenue on the company's own guidance is approximately $586M, of which $254.9M was recognized before most of those orders convert. Separately, the HBM pipeline management has described — "order and forecast from two HBM manufacturers... representing expected revenue exceeding $260 million for 2026 and 2027" — explicitly blends firm orders with customer forecasts and derives from two counterparties.

Consensus supports the direction without the velocity that would flag an inflection: current-year EPS $3.50 (revised +0.7% over 90 days, unchanged over 30), next-year $4.52 (+3.8% over 90 days, unchanged over 30). That is stable-to-modestly-positive, not the +30% revision velocity that historically precedes sustained re-rating. The earnings track record is similarly unremarkable: over the last four quarters, one beat (+0.7%), two in-line, one miss (-2.4%). Camtek does not systematically clear consensus, so I would not assume conservatism embedded in the H2 guide.

**Growth prospects: 6.5.** The demand mechanism (rising inspection steps per package as HBM stacks and chiplet complexity increase) is structural and corroborated by upstream capex confirmation across the equipment complex. But the near-term growth case is back-half loaded, order disclosure is not backlog-grade, and per-share growth is being suppressed by both opex and dilution. Consensus next-year growth of ~29% is credible but not yet demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $161.21 basis:

- Basic market cap: 46,668,177 × $161.21 = **$7.52B**; fully diluted 51,520K × $161.21 = **$8.30B**
- Enterprise value: $7.52B − $327M net cash ≈ **$7.20B**; on an if-converted basis, $8.30B − $815.8M gross investments ≈ **$7.49B**
- **EV/Sales on FY2026E ~$586M: 12.3x to 12.8x**
- **TTM non-GAAP diluted EPS ~$3.17 → 50.9x**
- **Current-year consensus $3.50 → 46.1x**
- **Next-year consensus $4.52 → 35.7x**; implied earnings growth of 29% gives a **PEG of ~1.23 on the next-year basis**
- TTM GAAP diluted EPS of $0.64 gives ~252x and is not usable; adding back the $88.7M net extinguishment charge (~$1.77/diluted share) gives ~$2.41, or ~67x

The bridge from H1 actuals to consensus is where the valuation debate sits. H1 non-GAAP EPS was $1.48. If H2 revenue reaches $331M at management's approximately 30% non-GAAP operating margin, H2 non-GAAP operating income is roughly $99M; adding roughly $15M of financial income and taxing at the historical ~9-10% ex-item rate gives roughly $103M, or approximately $2.00 per diluted share — FY2026 near $3.48. Holding the H2 margin at Q2's actual 27% instead produces roughly $1.82, or FY2026 near $3.30. Consensus at $3.50 therefore embeds both the revenue ramp and a full margin recovery. There is no cushion in the estimate.

Peer context cuts both ways. Onto Innovation, the closest listed comparable in advanced-packaging inspection and metrology, carried roughly double Camtek's market capitalization at a trailing P/E near 143 in early July. Camtek's ~51x TTM non-GAAP multiple is not an outlier within its niche. But an entire cohort priced on the same 2027 AI-capex assumption de-rates together, and the brief's observation that KLA fell roughly 40% from its highs in the thirty days to early August despite five consecutive beats illustrates the mechanism: semi-cap multiples are currently being marked on the 2027 capex debate rather than on delivered results.

Analyst targets: low $160.00 (-0.7% from spot), mean $187.58 (+16.4%), median $187.50, high $220.00 (+36.5%). Twelve covering analysts split 2 strong buy / 5 buy / 5 hold, unchanged for three months. At 46x current-year earnings, consensus targets are not independent confirmation of value — the target and the price rest on the same H2 margin-recovery assumption. The stock is 25.4% below its May high of $215.99 and 113% above its September low of $75.75.

**Valuation: 5.0.** The multiple is defensible against niche peers and against next-year growth (PEG ~1.2), which is why this is not a low score. But there is no identified mispricing: the stock is priced for the H2 recovery to happen, downside on a delivery slip is severe at 46x, and the drawdown from the peak reflects a legitimate reassessment of exactly the risk that remains unresolved rather than an overreaction I can demonstrate the market got wrong.

## Competitive Landscape

Camtek is a mid-end and packaging-focused inspection and metrology specialist, not a front-end process-control incumbent. Its franchise depends on HBM stacking and 2.5D/3D assembly where bump defects, warpage and micro-defects are yield-critical.

Evidence on trajectory is mixed. Management stated directly on the February 2026 call that it had "not lost any market share to competitors" in high-performance-computing sockets and expects to increase share in 2026 through R&D that lets it "penetrate more inspection and metrology steps." That is assertion, not verified data. Supporting it: Hawk and Eagle G5 scaling from roughly 30% of 2025 revenue toward at least 50% of 2026 revenue, and the Visual Layer acquisition adding AI-based classification software on top of installed hardware — the logical route to raising content per tool. Management claims TAM above $2B in 2027 against FY2026E revenue of approximately $586M, a company-sourced figure I cannot verify.

Cutting the other way: gross margin was flat to 70bp lower year over year, R&D rose 45% in the quarter, and management's own call summary lists competitive pressure in advanced packaging among negatives. Flat gross margin is consistent with a competitive but not deteriorating pricing environment; it is not evidence of widening moat. And the pipeline behind the growth narrows rather than broadens the customer base — the disclosed HBM figure comes from two manufacturers, and no quantified customer or geographic concentration data was obtainable. A revenue mix shifting toward AI and advanced packaging does not establish customer diversification; those are different measures.

**Competitive position: 6.5.** Structurally advantaged niche with a credible step-coverage expansion mechanism and a sensible software tuck-in, offset by flat gross margin, rising R&D intensity to hold position, and a narrowing rather than broadening counterparty base.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The H2 hockey stick is the thesis.** H2 revenue must reach $331M against H1's $254.9M, with Q4 near $172M versus $133.2M just delivered, and non-GAAP operating margin must recover roughly 300bp to 30%. Management conceded on the February call that "the exact timing between Q3 and Q4 depends on customer schedules." At 46x current-year earnings, a one-quarter delivery slip is a direct multiple risk.

**2. Israel manufacturing and Middle East disruption, with newly specific language.** The Q2 release names "increased risk and disruption around the Strait of Hormuz, and broader impacts on energy and freight markets." Principal manufacturing is in Israel with Germany secondary, making this a production-continuity risk rather than only a logistics one. This is an escalation in specificity over prior generic supply-chain language.

**3. Export controls.** The company cites uncertainty around the U.S. entity list and the ability to sell products incorporating U.S.-originated technology, changes to which "can be made without prior notice." For an Israeli toolmaker selling into Chinese OSATs, this is a live binary. Management expects stable China revenue with "no signs of weakness" — an expectation, not a hedge.

**4. Margin compression is real and its cause is not yet distinguishable.** R&D +45% and SG&A +19% in Q2. The bull reading (deliberate pre-investment for a known H2/2027 ramp plus Visual Layer integration, including $2.6M of one-time M&A cost) and the bear reading (structurally higher R&D intensity required to defend position) cannot be separated from disclosed data. Note also that management guided H1 gross margin of 50.5-51.5% and delivered 50.1% GAAP / 51.2% non-GAAP — met on non-GAAP, missed on GAAP.

**5. Working capital.** The $63.1M H1 receivables build and 40-day DSO extension alongside $12.2M of Q2 OCF, immediately preceding a guided 20% sequential revenue step that will itself consume working capital.

**6. Cyclicality with a recent precedent.** FY2023 revenue declined 1.7% with gross margin at 46.8% — a genuine down year two years before the AI packaging boom. The current multiple prices no down year.

**7. Tax.** Approximately 9% effective rate ex-items; a change in Israeli preferential status would compress net margin materially with no operating cause.

**8. Governance gaps.** As a 20-F filer, Camtek furnishes meeting materials via 6-K; no proxy was read. There is no evidence on executive compensation structure, board independence, related-party transactions, or founder/executive share ownership. Non-executive director holdings are de minimis (1,891 to 6,734 shares; the annual grant is 254 shares plus 571 options, roughly $41K), which is weak board-level economic alignment, though smaller equity grants are common on Israeli-domiciled boards and management holdings are unknown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 78.3% sits 8.9 points above 69.4% one-year realized volatility, with the term structure in backwardation (78.3% near versus 73.2% at three and six months) and the nearest expiry pricing a ±10.5% move over nine days despite earnings having just been reported on August 10. Put/call open interest is 1.68 and near-term skew is balanced at 2.6%. The September 18 tenor shows an extreme put/call volume ratio of 34.50 against a 19.1% implied move — a single-tenor flow observation worth noting without over-interpreting. ATM IV rose 1.1 points intraday on a 0.2% price move.

The read: the options market is pricing size, not a clear direction, and pricing near-term uncertainty above realized. That is consistent with a stock whose next two prints determine whether a 46x multiple is defensible. Short interest at 8.7% of float with 4.7 days to cover is meaningful but not crowded, and does not create squeeze mechanics in either direction. Rich near-term vol argues against paying up for upside optionality here and modestly favors patience over immediate entry.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** The concentration of specific, dated, binary risks — H2 delivery, export-control rule changes, Israeli production continuity, an unquantified customer base behind the growth, and a 9% tax rate — is high relative to what a 46x multiple accommodates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $220 (+36%)

Q3 lands at or above the $159M midpoint and Q4 reaches $172M-plus, confirming that H1's weakness was delivery-schedule timing rather than demand. The R&D step-up proves to be pre-investment: with opex roughly flat sequentially against a 30%-plus revenue increase, non-GAAP operating margin recovers to 30% and FY2026 non-GAAP EPS reaches $3.48-3.60, with FY2027 pushing toward $5.00 as the $600M order book converts and Visual Layer raises content per tool. Receivables unwind toward 70 days, releasing a meaningful H2 cash tailwind and restoring FCF conversion. Applying 44x to $5.00 gives $220, matching the high end of published targets. Optionality: $816M of liquid assets against an unlevered balance sheet supports M&A or the first buyback, and the stock's 25% drawdown from the peak would make repurchase economically sensible.

### Bear Case — $108 (-33%)

H2 revenue lands at $300-315M rather than $331M as one or two large HBM tool deliveries push into 2027, and non-GAAP operating margin holds near Q2's 27% because R&D intensity is structural. FY2026 non-GAAP EPS comes in near $3.20-3.30 and FY2027 consensus resets from $4.52 toward $3.60-3.90. DSO stays above 100 days through the ramp, absorbing cash and raising questions about revenue timing quality. Simultaneously, the semi-cap cohort de-rates on 2027 AI-capex doubts — the pattern already visible in peer drawdowns. 30x on $3.60 gives $108. A China export-control change or an Israeli production disruption would take this lower; the 52-week low of $75.75 is only nine months old.

### Base Case — $178 (+10%)

H2 revenue lands close to guidance but margin recovery is partial (28-29% non-GAAP operating margin rather than 30%), producing FY2026 non-GAAP EPS near $3.40 and FY2027 near $4.50. Applying 39x to $4.52 gives $178, below the $187.58 consensus mean. Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately +4%: (0.45 × 10.4%) + (0.25 × 36.5%) + (0.30 × −33.0%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The expected return is approximately fair from the current price. Existing holders have a coherent reason to stay — a structurally advantaged niche, a fortress balance sheet, and a guided reacceleration with order support. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 46x current-year earnings ahead of the two prints that decide the thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (expected early-to-mid November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint. Q4/FY2026 results in February 2027 is the confirmation checkpoint.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Q3 revenue at or above $158M **with** non-GAAP operating margin at or above 29% — confirming the opex ramp was pre-investment.
2. DSO returns below 85 days at September 30 with quarterly operating cash flow above $40M.
3. Price of $130-138 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $4.50 — approximately 29-31x next-year earnings, a level at which the H2 execution risk is compensated. The stock traded to $126.97 on July 29, so this is not hypothetical.

**Upgrade to BUY if** management defines the $600M as firm, non-cancellable backlog **and** discloses quantified customer concentration showing the top counterparty below 20% of revenue, at a price implying under 30x FY2027 earnings.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Q3 revenue below $150M, or FY2026 guidance of "more than 30% H2 over H1" is withdrawn or softened.
2. Non-GAAP operating margin below 27% in Q3 — a third consecutive quarter of compression, which would establish the R&D step-up as structural.
3. DSO above 100 days for a second consecutive quarter alongside quarterly operating cash flow below $20M.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 8% from $4.52.

**Downgrade to AVOID if** an export-control or entity-list change materially restricts China OSAT sales, or if Israeli manufacturing continuity is disrupted for more than one quarter.

## Conclusion

Camtek occupies a genuinely advantaged position in advanced-packaging inspection, with a demand driver — inspection steps rising faster than packaging capacity as HBM stacks and chiplet assemblies grow more complex — that is confirmed across the equipment complex rather than company-specific. Its balance sheet is among the cleanest in semi-cap: $816M of liquid assets, $488.5M of zero-coupon debt maturing in 2030, net cash of roughly $327M, and an investment portfolio that out-earns the cost of that debt.

Against that, the two most recent quarters delivered record revenue with declining operating profit on every basis, cash conversion weakened sharply, and the entire 2026 growth and margin case rests on an H2 step that requires Q4 revenue roughly 29% above the quarter just reported. Consensus of $3.50 for the current year already embeds full delivery of both the revenue ramp and the margin recovery, leaving no cushion in the estimate at 46x. The 25% drawdown from May's high reflects the market pricing that dependency, not overlooking it.

The base case offers roughly 10% upside against roughly 33% downside if the ramp slips, with the bull case dependent on outcomes visible only in November. HOLD. Own it if you own it; wait for either the Q3 confirmation or a price in the $130-138 range before adding.
