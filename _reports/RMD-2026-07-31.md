**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ResMed presents an unusually clean separation between reported operating performance and market pricing. Through nine months of fiscal 2026 (ending June 30, 2026), revenue grew 10% reported and 8% in constant currency to $4,189.8M, GAAP gross margin expanded to 61.8% from 58.8%, operating income rose 17%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS reached $8.22 versus $7.00. Management raised full-year gross margin guidance mid-year to 62%–63% from the 61%–63% range given in August 2025. Over that same window the shares fell from roughly $274 in February to $180.27 at the June low, and now trade at $208.56, down 29% from the 52-week high of $293.81.

The de-rating rests on three forward risks, each disclosed by the company and none yet visible in results. First, GLP-1 substitution: injectable weight-loss drugs are now approved for OSA in obese patients with moderate-to-severe disease, and oral formulations are in development. ResMed's own FY2025 10-K frames pharmaceuticals both as a competitive threat and as a diagnosis catalyst, without resolving which dominates. Second, Philips Respironics' return to US flow-generator sales, for which the 10-K states ResMed "cannot predict the timing or nature of their substantial return"; a material portion of ResMed's 2021–2025 device growth was share capture during Philips' absence. Third, and least discussed, the DMEPOS competitive bidding gap period has ended with new regulations reinstating the program; the 10-K states plainly that prior implementation "resulted in reduced Medicare payment for CPAP and respiratory assist devices, and related supplies and accessories."

At 18.8x consensus FY2026 EPS of $11.12 and 17.3x the $12.08 FY2027 estimate, against a Health Care Equipment peer median of 25.6x, the market is pricing a durable deceleration that has not appeared in the financials. That is either a mispricing or an early read on a structural change that lags in the data by construction. I cannot resolve it with the evidence available: no primary clinical or prescription-trend data on GLP-1 effects on CPAP starts, and no timeline for Philips from any source. Combined with an August 6 earnings event carrying FY2027 guidance, the MatrixCare divestiture accounting, and a ±9.2% implied move, I move to HOLD with an overall score of 6.3, down from 6.5. The balance sheet and cash generation deserve a high score; the risk stack does not.

## Company Overview

ResMed designs and manufactures cloud-connected devices and software for obstructive sleep apnea, COPD and other chronic respiratory conditions. Founded in 1989 by Dr. Peter Farrell, it employs approximately 10,600 people, sells in more than 140 countries, and dual-lists on the NYSE and ASX. Two segments:

**Sleep and Breathing Health** — FY2025 revenue $4,504.9M, +10%. Devices (AirSense 10/11, AirCurve, AirMini, Astral/Stellar/Lumis ventilators) represented approximately 52% of total company revenue in each of FY2023–FY2025; masks, diagnostics and accessories approximately 36%. Segment operating margin in Q3 FY2026 was 45.6%.

**Residential Care Software (RCS)** — FY2025 revenue $641.4M, +10%, approximately 12% of total revenue. Four brands: Brightree (US HME/pharmacy), HEALTHCAREfirst (home health/hospice), MatrixCare (skilled nursing, senior living) and MEDIFOX DAN (Germany). Sold only in the US and Germany. Q3 FY2026 segment operating margin 34.1%.

The business model matters for reading reimbursement risk. In the US, Canada and Latin America ResMed sells to home medical equipment (HME) providers who supply patients and bill payors; ResMed is generally not the biller. In Germany and Korea it receives payments directly from payors and operates vertically integrated home healthcare businesses. Following the May 2025 VirtuOx acquisition, ResMed bills payors directly for diagnostic testing as a Medicare-enrolled independent diagnostic testing facility.

The structural opportunity, per third-party literature cited in the 10-K: 936 million people worldwide with mild-to-severe OSA, more than 424 million with moderate-to-severe disease, with management estimating fewer than 20% of US sufferers diagnosed or treated and 10% or less outside the US. Installed base of more than 30 million patients on cloud-connected devices via AirView, over 10 million registered myAir users, and approximately 10,000 pending or granted patents and designs.

**Gross margin is the central operating fact.** Roughly 600bps of expansion in three years on a business with 52% of revenue in hardware, attributed by management to procurement, manufacturing and logistics efficiencies, lower acquired-intangible amortization, and the non-repeat of $14.3M in FY2024 field safety notification costs. Momentum continued into Q3 FY2026: GAAP gross margin 62.2%, non-GAAP 62.8%, up 290bps year over year. On $5.6B of revenue, each 100bps of gross margin is roughly $56M of pre-tax income, so this line has been worth more to earnings than volume growth.

**FY2025 earnings and cash flow were flattered, and the flattery is unwinding on schedule.** The 16.5% FY2025 effective tax rate benefited from an IRS refund of interest and penalties, a $21.4M benefit from a business cessation, and a $30.0M income tax effect of interest income on refunds. FY2025 operating cash flow included $124.4M of tax refunds and associated interest. Q3 FY2026's effective rate was 20.6% versus 12.6% a year earlier, reflecting Pillar Two global minimum tax (effective for ResMed from July 1, 2024) and the absence of the prior-year refund. The consequence is that FY2026 GAAP EPS growth understates operating progress: Q3 GAAP diluted EPS rose 10% to $2.74 while non-GAAP rose 21% to $2.86.

**Q3 FY2026 detail.** Revenue $1,431.4M, +11% reported and +8% constant currency, with FX contributing roughly three points. Sleep and Breathing Health $1,260.5M (+8% cc); RCS $170.9M (+4% cc). Masks and other outgrew devices materially: +12% cc to $524.8M versus devices +6% cc to $735.7M; in US/Canada/LatAm the gap was 14% versus 6%. Operating income $499.8M, +17%. SG&A moved the wrong way at 20.0% of revenue versus 19.0%, or 19.5% excluding $5.9M of acquisition and portfolio-review expenses — small relative to the gross margin gain but worth monitoring, since it partly reflects VirtuOx and marketing spend rather than one-time items. R&D 6.6% of revenue.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026).** Cash $1,660.5M, of which $944.0M is US-domiciled; total debt $664.1M, comprising $250M 3.24% notes that matured July 10, 2026, $250M 3.45% notes due 2029, and $165.0M drawn on facilities at 4.5% variable. The $1,500M revolver is fully undrawn; total disclosed liquidity $3,160.5M. Net cash of approximately $996M implies enterprise value of roughly $29.5B at the current price (144,239,563 shares × $208.56 = $30.08B market cap, less $0.996B net cash). Equity $6,491.9M; current ratio 3.44; debt/equity 0.37; FY2025 ROE 23.5%, ROA 17.1%; Altman Z-Score 4.32. Inventories declined to $911.9M from $927.7M while revenue grew ~10% — genuine working capital improvement. The revolving and term facilities terminate June 29, 2027, so a refinancing decision arrives within twelve months; with net cash and $1.6B of annual free cash flow, this is administrative rather than a risk.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 free cash flow of $1,661.7M on $5,146.3M revenue is a 32.3% FCF margin, exceptional for a hardware-weighted medtech; excluding the $124.4M of tax refunds the underlying base was approximately $1,537M. Nine-month FY2026 FCF was $1,246.0M ($1,351.2M OCF less $105.2M capex). Q3 OCF of $554.1M was down 4.2% year over year despite net income up 9%, driven by a $127.7M swing in prepaid expenses and deferred taxes that maps to the prior-year IRS refund receivable — a comparison artifact rather than deteriorating conversion, with nine-month OCF up 11.4%. Capex is stepping up sharply (9M $105.2M versus $59.3M) for the Greenwood, Indiana distribution center opening in 2027, still only ~2.5% of revenue. On $30.1B of market capitalization, the underlying FCF yield is roughly 5.1%–5.5% depending on whether one uses the normalized FY2025 base or the annualized nine-month figure; neither reconciles Q4 seasonality or cash-tax timing, so treat these as sensitivities.

**Capital returns.** Nine-month FY2026 repurchases of $500.0M for 1,900,332 shares (average $263.11) were 2.5x the $200.0M spent in the prior-year period, but shares outstanding fell only 0.92%, from 146,385,350 to 145,040,224 — roughly 555,000 shares of equity-compensation issuance offset about 29% of gross repurchases, with $76.9M of stock-based compensation over the period. The Q3 tranche of 673,418 shares for $175.0M at an average $259.87 was executed entirely in February 2026 and is marked roughly 20% below cost. Remaining authorization is 8.9M shares, about 6.1% of outstanding. The dividend was raised 13% to $0.60 quarterly in July 2025, an indicated yield of $2.40 / $208.56 = 1.15%.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is mid-single-digit to high-single-digit and decelerating at the margin; earnings growth is running at roughly double that on gross margin expansion. Nine-month constant-currency revenue growth of 8% compares with FY2025's underlying pace, with FX adding about 3 points that will not persist at that magnitude if the dollar stabilizes.

Three composition points matter more than the headline:

**The resupply mix shift is the highest-quality element.** Masks and accessories rose from ~34% of revenue in FY2023 to ~36% in FY2025 while devices fell from ~54% to ~52%, and masks are growing at roughly twice the device rate. Masks are payor-reimbursed consumables on a replacement schedule, so this converts installed base into recurring, higher-margin revenue and plausibly explains a meaningful share of the 600bps margin gain. It does not, however, establish customer or channel diversification: US demand still flows through a consolidated HME channel, and no customer-concentration disclosure was located.

**RCS has been the persistent underperformer and is being partly divested.** Segment growth decelerated to +4% constant currency in Q3 from +10% in FY2025, at a 34.1% operating margin versus 45.6% in the core. Capital allocated to it is disproportionate: RCS carried $2,144.3M of the company's $3,043.1M of goodwill at 3/31/2026 — about 70% of goodwill supporting 12% of revenue and roughly 11% of segment operating profit. The 10-K itself concedes "low barriers to entry" and that many customers use in-house systems.

**The portfolio reset changes the FY2027 revenue base.** On July 7, 2026 ResMed announced the sale of MatrixCare, HEALTHCAREfirst, Citus and home health/hospice solutions to Frazier Healthcare Partners for $490M cash (price per Reuters; the press release text does not state it), closing in Q1 FY2027. Brightree and MEDIFOX DAN are retained. Separately, ResMed agreed in May 2026 to acquire Noctrix Health for $340M, adding Nidra, a wearable neuromodulation device for restless legs syndrome — a transaction I could not verify in any filing and which should not be treated as closed. Net capital movement across the two is a ~$150M inflow. The divestiture likely removes some portion of RCS revenue at ~34% segment margin; the exact figure is unknown because ResMed does not disclose brand-level RCS revenue, so any revenue-loss estimate is a hypothesis rather than a calculation. After redeploying $490M into buybacks at current prices, the EPS effect is modest, but consensus FY2027 of $12.08 may not fully reflect it. Next-year estimates have drifted down 0.8% over 90 days.

Consensus revisions do not signal an inflection: current-year EPS is $11.12, up 0.9% over 90 days; next-year $12.08, down 0.8%. This is a stable-estimate situation, not the sharp upward revision pattern that would justify overriding valuation concerns. The earnings surprise record — three beats and one miss in four quarters, with beats of 1.3% and 2.1% — supports modest confidence in consensus rather than a pattern of systematic conservatism.

ResMed earns 45.6% segment operating margins in its core, 23.5% ROE, and a 32% FCF margin — metrics superior to every name in that table — yet trades at a 27% discount to the median forward multiple. Historically it carried a premium. Dispersion in the peer set is wide (15.5x to 32.2x), so the median is a loose anchor, but the direction is unambiguous.

The question is whether the discount is a mispricing or an accurate discount rate applied to a lower terminal growth rate. Arguments for mispricing: reported results are accelerating on margin, guidance was raised mid-year, the balance sheet holds net cash, and total-return arithmetic of 5.3% FCF yield plus ~1% share count reduction plus mid-single-digit organic growth clears 9–10% before any multiple recovery. Arguments against: the three structural threats are all forward effects that would appear in device volumes with a lag and cannot yet be visible; analyst sentiment is degrading (Buy ratings fell from 8 to 6 over one month while Holds rose from 8 to 10, with one Sell); short interest sits at 10.8% of float with 7.8 days to cover, elevated for a $30B medtech; and the mean price target of $248.60 sits well above the $234 median, indicating a wide split rather than consensus conviction.

I score valuation 6.0 — moderately attractive without being asymmetric. A compressed multiple on a high-quality business with three unresolved structural threats and decelerating organic growth is the configuration that produces value traps. The cheapness is real; the catalyst to close it is not identifiable independent of the GLP-1 and Philips questions resolving favorably, which I cannot underwrite with the data available.

Base case: 20x an FY2027 EPS band of $11.85–$12.10 (adjusting consensus for the MatrixCare divestiture net of redeployment) supports roughly $238, which I round to **$235**, or +12.7% from current. Bull case: 24x $12.50 if GLP-1 fears fade and Philips remains constrained, approximately **$300**. Bear case: 14x $11.00 if competitive bidding, Philips share recapture and GLP-1 attrition compress the growth rate toward low single digits, approximately **$154**, or -26%.

## Competitive Landscape

ResMed is one of two global-scale PAP players, with the other operating under a consent decree that has kept it largely out of US flow-generator sales since 2021. The FY2025 10-K names eight direct competitors including Fisher & Paykel Healthcare, DeVilbiss, React Health, Lowenstein, and lower-cost Asian manufacturers (BMC Medical, Apex Medical, Jiangsu Yuyue). The filing also notes that "some of our competitors are affiliates of our customers," a reference to vertical integration among HME distributors that constrains pricing power in parts of the channel.

The moat is genuine on installed base: 30 million connected patients on AirView, 10 million myAir users, and a mask franchise where fit and comfort drive resupply loyalty. The trajectory is what is uncertain. Philips' re-entry mathematically reverses some portion of 2021–2025 share gain, and no source I reviewed provides a timeline. Competitive bidding reinstatement compresses HME economics, which historically transmits back to manufacturers as price concessions, and would hit the fast-growing mask/resupply line as directly as devices. Against that, ResMed is building the diagnostic funnel: VirtuOx plus the NightOwl home sleep test made nationally available in April 2025 addresses the under-diagnosis bottleneck that gates the entire category, and Nidra (if the transaction closed) adds a sleep-adjacent therapeutic modality structurally immune to obesity-drug substitution.

Two analyst downgrades landed within ten days in June 2026 — Morgan Stanley citing GLP-1 headwinds and potential Philips re-entry, RBC cutting to Sector Perform — against Buy reaffirmations from Macquarie, BofA, J.P. Morgan, Canaccord and UBS through early July. These are aggregator-sourced ratings; treat them as directional sentiment only.

I hold competitive position at 6.5. The level is high; the trajectory is genuinely two-sided, and the RCS admission of "low barriers to entry" plus its deceleration and partial divestiture weigh against the composite.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement — the most concrete disclosed negative.** The 10-K states the DMEPOS competitive bidding gap period "has recently ended with the announcement of new regulations reinstating competitive bidding," with CMS expected to initiate the next round after rulemaking. CPAP and respiratory assist devices have been outside the program since January 1, 2019. Prior implementation "resulted in reduced Medicare payment" for these products both inside and outside bidding areas. Timing is rulemaking-gated, so the earliest financial impact is likely several years out, and prior rounds excluded CPAP entirely — but this is the risk with the clearest documented mechanism and it receives the least attention.

**GLP-1 substitution.** Named in the 10-K as a competitive risk, with the filing confirming that injectable weight-loss drugs are approved for OSA in obese patients with moderate-to-severe disease and that oral versions are reportedly under development. The same document frames pharmaceuticals as a tailwind through enhanced diagnosis. No independent clinical or prescription data was obtainable. Reported results through March 2026 show no device-side damage.

**Litigation, four live matters.** Fractus S.A. (S.D. Cal., filed 10/9/2025) asserts five antenna patents against AirSense 10/11, AirCurve 10/11 and the Connectivity Module, seeking a permanent injunction — the injunction request against flagship platforms distinguishes this from routine patent noise, though NPE injunctions are historically difficult and antenna claims are typically designable-around. Cleveland Medical's first suit is on Federal Circuit appeal with PTAB final written decisions on five instituted IPRs expected around July 2026. A second Cleveland Medical suit (11/5/2025) targets ApneaLink with AirView and VirtuOx's Sleepifi platform — the first litigation arriving through the VirtuOx acquisition. A qui tam False Claims Act complaint unsealed in December 2025 names ResMed alongside Respironics, Philips and Fisher & Paykel over alleged overcharging of the VA through a distributor; the United States declined to intervene and ResMed has not been served, both materially mitigating. ResMed cannot estimate a loss or range for any matter.

**Compliance surface from VirtuOx.** ResMed is now a Medicare-enrolled IDTF and a HIPAA covered entity, newly subject to the Civil Monetary Penalties beneficiary-inducement prohibition, and VirtuOx operates under its own five-year Corporate Integrity Agreement from a May 2022 $3.2M settlement running through 2027, with the 10-K noting VirtuOx "experiences frequent government payor audits." ResMed exited its own 2019 DOJ settlement CIA ($40.6M total) in December 2024 and inherited a fresh one almost immediately.

**Supply chain and tax.** China MOFCOM licensing and volume caps on rare-earth magnets are a named single-jurisdiction chokepoint for components integral to ResMed assemblies. Substantially all manufacturing is outside the US, with 36% of FY2025 revenue coming from the Europe/Asia/other region; April 2025 CBP guidance confirmed continuing tariff relief for these products, but broad reciprocal tariffs remain a stated material risk. Singapore tax holidays expire progressively through June 30, 2030; the ATO is auditing 2018 with years through 2024 open; Pillar Two is already lifting the effective rate.

**Event risk into August 6.** The Q4/FY2026 print will carry FY2027 guidance, the MatrixCare accounting treatment, and any Noctrix confirmation. The MatrixCare disclosure warrants attention: $490M against RCS goodwill of $2,144.3M, with goodwill tested only qualitatively and no impairment recorded in FY2023–FY2025. If the divested business is 40%–50% of RCS revenue, $490M implies roughly 1.5–2.0x revenue, low for healthcare software. A non-cash loss on sale or held-for-sale writedown is a live possibility. Any such charge would not touch the $490M of cash proceeds, operating cash flow or solvency, but it would be a headline event and a retrospective verdict on a decade of RCS capital allocation. I do not have brand-level revenue, so the multiple estimate is a hypothesis.

**Governance.** Four Item 5.02 officer/director change 8-Ks in nine months (8/1/2025, 8/18/2025, 11/20/2025, 4/30/2026), with an April 24, 2026 employment agreement for Aaron Bloomer filed as an exhibit and no confirmable role — Brett Sandercock still signed the Q3 10-Q as CFO, so this should not be read as a CFO transition. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows ResMed TSR trailing its self-selected peer group index in all five fiscal years, compounding to +34.4% versus +49.5%, alongside net income up 195% — consistent with persistent multiple compression rather than execution failure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 47.3% sits 19.7 points above one-year realized volatility of 27.6%, in the 77th percentile of the current chain, with a pronounced backwardation (47.3% at 20 days versus 32.8% at 167 days). That structure is consistent with the August 6 earnings event carrying more information than usual rather than a broad regime shift. Directional positioning is not one-sided: the reported put/call skew of -1.4% is characterized as balanced, put/call volume is 1.12 and put/call open interest is 0.44, meaning existing open positions skew toward calls even as near-dated volume tilts modestly to puts. The six-month tenor carries a wider skew reading of -3.9% and a 0.50 volume ratio. Read alongside the 10.8% short interest and 7.8 days to cover, the picture is elevated event risk with genuinely divided views, which argues against establishing or adding to a position immediately ahead of the print. For holders, the richness of near-dated volatility makes covered-call overwriting economically reasonable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300, +44%).** Realized results are the truth and the narrative is wrong. Constant-currency revenue growth holds at 7%–8% as diagnosis expands — helped, per management's framing, by GLP-1 marketing raising OSA awareness — while gross margin settles at 63% and the mask/resupply mix keeps drifting toward consumables. FY2027 EPS reaches $12.50 as the MatrixCare proceeds fund buybacks at prices 20% below February's average, and the multiple re-rates toward the 25.6x peer median as Philips' return proves gradual and competitive bidding rulemaking excludes CPAP again. 24x $12.50 = $300. The pattern would mirror the 2021–2025 record, where net income rose 195% while the shares underperformed a peer index every year.

**Bear case ($154, -26%).** The market is early rather than wrong. Oral GLP-1 approval for OSA arrives, new-patient device starts decelerate through FY2027–FY2028, and Philips recaptures share into a channel whose economics are simultaneously compressed by reinstated competitive bidding, transmitting price concessions to ResMed and reversing the gross margin gain that is currently carrying earnings. A MatrixCare loss on sale confirms that RCS capital allocation destroyed value, and the FY2027 guide lands below the $12.08 consensus. Growth settles at low single digits and a 14x multiple on $11.00 produces roughly $154, below the June low of $180.27.

**My assessment.** The bull case requires no heroic assumptions and is supported by every reported number through March 2026. The bear case requires a specific mechanism that is documented in the company's own filings but unquantified anywhere. I cannot assign confident probabilities without primary GLP-1 prescription and CPAP-start data or a Philips timeline, neither of which is available. The balance sheet — net cash, 5%+ FCF yield, 6.1% of shares authorized for repurchase — means downside is bounded by cash generation rather than solvency, which is why the bear target sits at a 14x multiple rather than a distressed one. That asymmetry favors holding over selling, and the unresolved central variable plus a ±9.2% event six days out favors holding over buying.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2027 guidance issued August 6 that implies constant-currency revenue growth of 6% or better ex-MatrixCare, with gross margin guided at 62.5% or higher. That combination would demonstrate that neither GLP-1 attrition nor channel pricing has begun to bite and would make the 18.8x multiple indefensible.
2. Evidence that device new-patient starts are holding: US/Canada/LatAm device constant-currency growth of 5% or better in Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027, alongside continued mask growth above 10%.
3. Price below $185 (approaching the $180.27 low) on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the shares near 15x FY2027 EPS and a 6%+ FCF yield — a level at which the bear case is substantially discounted.
4. Clustered open-market insider buying, which has been entirely absent through a 29% drawdown.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2027 guidance below $11.75 EPS or gross margin guided below 62%, indicating the pricing environment has turned.
2. Any quarter of negative constant-currency device growth in the US/Canada/LatAm region, or Sleep and Breathing Health constant-currency growth below 4%.
3. A CMS proposed rule that explicitly includes CPAP and respiratory assist devices in the next DMEPOS competitive bidding round, with a defined implementation date.
4. Confirmation of Philips Respironics' substantial US flow-generator return with an accompanying share-recapture disclosure.
5. A MatrixCare loss on sale exceeding roughly $400M, which would indicate the RCS goodwill balance of $2,144.3M is broadly impaired rather than the divested unit alone.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 6, 2026 (Q4/FY2026 results, FY2027 guidance, MatrixCare accounting, Noctrix confirmation) is the immediate reassessment point and will resolve more of this thesis than any other single event. Secondary checkpoints: PTAB final written decisions in the Cleveland Medical IPRs, expected around July 2026; the Q1 FY2027 print for post-divestiture run-rate economics; and any CMS notice-and-comment publication on DMEPOS bidding scope.

## Conclusion

**HOLD, overall score 6.3.** ResMed's financial condition is close to best-in-class for medtech: ~$996M net cash, 45.6% core segment operating margins, a 32% free cash flow margin, an Altman Z-Score of 4.32, and $3.16B of liquidity against $664M of debt. Gross margin execution — 600bps in three years, guidance raised mid-year — has been the company's strongest form of delivery, and nine-month non-GAAP EPS growth of 17% is real.

The score has declined from 7.2 in May to 6.3 today, and the decline is not a function of the share price. It reflects three identifiable changes: the arrival of a concrete regulatory negative in competitive bidding reinstatement, the accumulation of near-term binary events (Fractus injunction request, PTAB decisions, an unsealed FCA complaint, the MatrixCare accounting), and continued weakness in the management-quality evidence — $500M of buybacks executed at an average $263 now marked 20% below cost, net share reduction of only 0.92%, no open-market insider purchases through a 29% drawdown, four unexplained officer-change filings in nine months, and a divestiture price that may reveal a decade-old capital allocation error. Financial health remains an 8.5. Risk factors at 4.5 is the binding constraint.

At 18.8x forward earnings against a 25.6x peer median with superior returns on capital, the stock is cheap enough that selling is unattractive and the bounded downside supports holding. It is not cheap enough to buy in front of an August 6 print with a ±9.2% implied move that will supply FY2027 guidance, the divestiture accounting and the first clean look at whether GLP-1s and Philips have begun to affect device volumes. Own the position, do not add before August 6, and use the richness of near-dated volatility if overwriting is available.
