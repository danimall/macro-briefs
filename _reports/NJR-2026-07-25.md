**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

New Jersey Resources is a diversified energy-services holding company built around a regulated New Jersey gas utility (New Jersey Natural Gas, ~589,000 customers), supplemented by commercial solar (Clean Energy Ventures), wholesale gas marketing (Energy Services), and a fast-growing midstream storage and transportation business (Leaf River salt-dome storage, the Adelphia pipeline, and a 50% interest in Steckman Ridge). Fiscal 2025 (year ends September 30) delivered net income of $335.6M and net financial earnings per share (NFEPS) of $3.29, up from $2.95 in FY24, extending a steady ~8-10% compounding record. The durable driver was the November 2024 rate case at NJNG, which added $157M of base rates at a 9.6% allowed ROE and lifted utility gross margin roughly $140M year over year.

The story has two attractive threads and one persistent constraint. The attractive threads are the utility rate-base growth (high single digits through 2030, funded by a $4.8-5.2B five-year capital plan with no block equity needed) and an under-appreciated Storage & Transportation improvement: recontracting has moved average Leaf River storage rates from roughly $0.09 to $0.20/Dth/month on signed contracts, and management guides S&T net financial earnings to more than double by 2027 with no incremental capital, on top of a FERC-filed 70% capacity expansion that drew 3x oversubscription in its open season. These are concrete, contract-backed sources of upside that a headline screen misses.

The constraint is valuation and risk/reward. At $60.05 the stock trades near its 52-week high ($60.69) at roughly 18-19x the FY26 NFEPS guidance midpoint of $3.10, a premium to the gas-utility peer median of ~15.6x, while offering a below-median 3.2% dividend yield. Consensus mean target ($60.43) implies essentially no upside; the median ($63) implies ~5%. The FY26 guidance of $3.03-$3.18 is optically below FY25's $3.29 (a normalization for the absence of a $56.2M one-time solar gain, not a deterioration), but the market already understands this. Layered on top are OBBBA solar-credit phase-out risk at CEV and long-term New Jersey decarbonization pressure on the core franchise. We rate NJR HOLD (income), with the four consecutive HOLDs in this name reflecting a stable, correctly-priced business.

## Company Overview

NJR operates four reportable segments:

- **Natural Gas Distribution (NJNG)** — the regulated core, contributing $213.5M of FY25 NFE (65%, or ~70% excluding the one-time solar gain). NJNG serves ~589,000 customers across six central/coastal New Jersey counties under a monopoly franchise. New Jersey's regulatory framework is constructive: the Conservation Incentive Program (CIP) decouples revenue from weather, and multiple riders provide timely recovery. The November 2024 rate case set a 9.6% ROE, 54% equity ratio, and 7.08% overall rate of return.
- **Clean Energy Ventures (CEV)** — unregulated commercial solar, 479 MW in service across seven states, contributing $61.2M of FY25 NFE (18%), inflated by a $56.2M pre-tax gain on the November 2024 sale of its residential solar portfolio for $132.5M. Economics depend on investment tax credits and REC/SREC/TREC markets.
- **Energy Services (ES)** — wholesale gas marketing and optimization of transportation/storage contracts. Highly volatile: FY25 NFE fell to $34.9M from $111.5M in FY24 as asset-management-agreement (AMA) revenue recognition dropped from $137.2M to $19.7M.
- **Storage & Transportation (S&T)** — Leaf River (32.2 Bcf salt-dome storage in Mississippi), Adelphia (FERC-regulated pipeline in Pennsylvania), and 50% of Steckman Ridge. Only $18.5M of FY25 NFE (6%) but the fastest-growing segment.

The strategic tension is that New Jersey is simultaneously the source of NJR's stable regulated returns and of long-term decarbonization pressure (Global Warming Response Act target of 80% GHG cuts by 2050, Energy Master Plan for 100% clean energy, and an open BPU proceeding on the "future of natural gas utilities"). Management's differentiated hedge is midstream and solar optionality: Leaf River sits in the Gulf Coast demand center benefiting from LNG and data-center gas demand, and CEV positions NJR for PJM/New Jersey electric-capacity shortfalls.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** GAAP net income progressed FY23 $264.7M → FY24 $289.8M → FY25 $335.6M; NFEPS $2.70 → $2.95 → $3.29. This is a consistent high-single-digit to low-double-digit grower, aligned with the 7-9% long-term NFEPS target and five straight years of exceeding initial guidance.

**Quality of earnings.** FY25's headline strength blended two non-durable items with the durable driver. The $56.2M / ~$0.30 solar-sale gain at CEV and the ES normalization (NFE $111.5M → $34.9M on AMA timing) both distort the year-over-year comparison. The genuine, recurring growth came from the utility rate case, which is why FY26 guidance sits below FY25 — a normalization for the vanished solar gain, not an operational step-down. Consensus current-year EPS of $3.58 (higher than management's NFEPS midpoint, reflecting definitional differences and a +2.7% upward revision over 30 days / +6.3% over 90 days) confirms the market reads this as continued growth off a normalized base.

**Cash flow and capital.** Operating cash flow rose to $466.3M from $427.4M. Consolidated capex was $660M in FY25, and the new five-year plan (up ~40%) directs roughly 60% to the utility. Management asserts this is fundable with no block equity issuance while holding FFO/adjusted-debt near 20%. One structural nuance: NJR uses solar and gas-meter sale-leasebacks as recurring financing (CEV received $251.2M in FY25), which keeps reported balance-sheet debt below true economic obligations, worth normalizing when assessing leverage.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $7.58B; equity $2.39B; long-term debt $3.25B; short-term debt $195.6M — a ~40% equity / 60% debt structure typical for a rate-regulated utility. The Altman Z-Score of 0.36 and current ratio of 0.73 look alarming but are false signals; both are designed for industrials, not regulated utilities that intentionally run high leverage against stable, allowed-return cash flows. NJNG carries A1/A+ ratings (Moody's/Fitch), stable, investment-grade; NJR at the holdco level is unrated. Both are in covenant compliance (leverage caps of 70% at NJR, 65% at NJNG). The recorded MGP environmental remediation liability of $167M (range $144-200M) is recovered via a regulatory asset, probable but dependent on continued constructive BPU treatment.

**Earnings surprise record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with recent beats of 14.0% (March 2026) and 12.5% (December 2025) supporting confidence in near-term consensus. The one miss (September 2025, -10.4%) fell in a seasonally trivial quarter (EPS $0.16). This is a company that generally delivers.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars of differing quality:

1. **Utility rate base (durable, high-confidence).** High-single-digit rate base growth through 2030, funded by ~60% of the capital plan. This is the bedrock and underpins the 7-9% NFEPS growth target. Regulatory support (CIP decoupling, rider recovery, a completed constructive rate case) makes this the most reliable component.

2. **Storage & Transportation (the underappreciated surprise).** S&T is only 6% of NFE today but guided to more than double by 2027 on already-signed recontracting with no incremental capital. On top, the October 2025 FERC filing would expand Leaf River working capacity 70% to 43 Bcf by 2028 (with a fourth cavern taking it to 55 Bcf), backed by long-term fee-based contracts. Adelphia's third rate case settled and was FERC-approved November 4, 2025. This is the most concrete, contract-backed upside in the story and is easy to overlook in a small segment.

3. **Clean Energy Ventures (growth with policy overhang).** Management expects ~50% capacity growth over two years, but this depends on preserving ITC eligibility under the OBBBA phase-out (see Risk Assessment). The aggressive safe-harboring of a large pipeline is a direct response, but this segment is the most likely source of variance versus plan.

The offsetting drag is Energy Services, which is inherently mark-to-market and weather/spread dependent, with the $500M AMA program (2020-2030) providing only ~$34M/year of cash through FY31 on lumpy recognition. Estimate revisions are mixed at the margin: current-year up (+6.3% over 90 days), but next-quarter down (-17.6% over 90 days) and next-year roughly flat (+0.2% over 90 days), consistent with a business whose durable growth is steady rather than accelerating. This does not meet the threshold of an inflection story where estimate revisions of +30%+ would warrant treating consensus targets as floors. Growth is real and dependable, not accelerating.

Analyst targets confirm the constrained setup: mean $60.43 (essentially spot), median $63 (~5% upside), low $53 (-12%), high $64 (+7%). The recommendation mix (2 strong buy, 4 buy, 1 hold, 1 strong sell) is favorable but stable, with no recent upgrades. Because NJR is a steady grower rather than an inflection stock trading below its growth rate, the principle that analyst targets are stale floors does not apply here — the targets and the price both reflect the same modest-growth consensus.

Cycle-adjustment does not rescue the multiple: there is no trough in earnings to normalize away, no estimate-revision velocity exceeding 30%, and no revenue acceleration. This is a fully-valued quality utility. The valuation score of 4.6 reflects a premium multiple against peers with limited upside to consensus and no identifiable catalyst for the market to re-rate the stock higher. The anti-predictive history of "cheap" utility screens does not apply, because NJR is not screening cheap — it is screening expensive, and the score reflects that directly rather than a value-trap concern.

## Competitive Landscape

NJNG faces no direct utility competition within its franchise; the only competitive pressure is inter-fuel (electricity, oil, propane), where management leans on natural gas being the cheapest home-heating option — a strategically important narrative given New Jersey's electrification agenda. The regulated moat is stable-to-widening in the near term: the completed rate case, decoupling, and rider mechanisms support returns, and rate base is growing at high single digits.

The competitive-position trajectory is two-sided. Near-term, the S&T recontracting and Leaf River expansion strengthen NJR's position in the Gulf Coast demand center at a time of rising LNG and data-center gas demand, a structural tailwind that the market has not fully priced. Long-term, the core franchise faces terminal-value pressure from New Jersey's decarbonization framework and the open BPU "future of gas" proceeding, a slow-moving but real headwind to the regulated moat's durability. The competitive-position score of 5.7 reflects a stable regulated base with a genuine midstream tailwind, offset by long-dated structural decarbonization risk to the core product, consistent with the recent decline in this dimension from 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Solar/ITC policy risk (elevated, newly acute).** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) phases out clean-energy tax credits: solar must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 (unless construction begins before July 4, 2026) and must satisfy "prohibited foreign entity" material-assistance rules. CEV's growth depends on ITC eligibility; management has safe-harbored a large pipeline, but this is the single largest company-specific uncertainty and the thread most likely to create earnings variance versus plan. REC price declines would compound the risk.

**Energy Services volatility.** ES swung ~$77M in NFE year over year. The segment is mark-to-market and spread/weather dependent, introducing quarter-to-quarter noise that management smooths by steering to NFEPS.

**New Jersey decarbonization / "future of gas."** GWRA, EMP, and the open BPU proceeding create long-term terminal-value risk. Building-electrification and gas-hookup-ban templates from other jurisdictions are a threat, and affordability was front-and-center in the recent gubernatorial election.

**Regulatory-asset dependency and holdco structure.** Utility returns depend on continued constructive BPU treatment; any disallowance flows to earnings. NJR relies on subsidiary dividends and loans to service holdco debt and fund its 30-year dividend-growth streak.

Most of these risks are partly priced (the sector's regulatory and decarbonization overhangs are well understood). The OBBBA/ITC timeline is the most acute idiosyncratic risk not fully reflected. The risk score of 5.5 balances a stable, investment-grade regulated core against the specific ITC-timeline catalyst and long-term decarbonization overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($66-70).** The S&T improvement compounds faster than the Street models — Leaf River recontracting doubles the segment's NFE by 2027, the 70% capacity expansion clears FERC and adds contracted fee-based earnings by 2028, and CEV successfully safe-harbors its full ITC pipeline. Utility rate-base growth stays on the high-single-digit track, NFEPS compounds at the high end of 7-9%, and the market awards NJR a fuller premium (~20-21x) for the diversified growth mix and 30-year dividend record. This gets the stock to $66-70 over 18 months, ~10-17% above spot.

**Bear Case ($48-53).** OBBBA timelines slip or foreign-entity rules disqualify part of CEV's pipeline, forcing impairments or lost growth. New Jersey's BPU "future of gas" proceeding produces adverse signals on terminal franchise value. A hawkish rate environment raises the cost of the capital plan and pressures the utility multiple. The stock de-rates toward the peer-median multiple (~15.6x on $3.10-3.20 = $48-50) and revisits the analyst low of $53, a decline of 12-20% from spot.

**Base Case ($60-63).** Utility rate base and S&T recontracting deliver steady 7-9% NFEPS growth; CEV grows within its safe-harbored envelope; the multiple holds near current levels. The stock tracks earnings growth plus the 3.2% dividend, landing near the consensus median of $63 over 12 months — total return in the high-single-digits, dominated by the dividend.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable at the current price. The durable growth is real, but the multiple already reflects it, and the near-term risk/reward to consensus is capped at ~5% upside (to median) against ~12% downside to the analyst low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** NJR is a well-run, investment-grade gas utility with a 30-year dividend-growth record and genuine S&T growth optionality, but at ~19x the FY26 NFEPS midpoint with a below-median 3.2% yield and essentially no upside to the consensus mean target, the appreciation case is thin. Own it for the dividend and steady compounding, not for a re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s (peer-median multiple of ~15.6x on FY26 NFEPS), which would restore an attractive entry with a >3.6% yield.
- Confirmation that the Leaf River 70% expansion clears FERC on schedule with contracted fee-based revenue quantified, materially raising the S&T earnings trajectory beyond current guidance.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity on current-year NFEPS combined with resolution of the OBBBA/ITC safe-harboring (removing the CEV overhang).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse BPU signals from the "future of natural gas utilities" proceeding that impair long-term franchise terminal value.
- OBBBA disqualification of a meaningful portion of the CEV pipeline, forcing solar impairments or a cut to the capital plan.
- Evidence the no-block-equity funding plan is breaking (a large equity raise, FFO/debt slipping below the high-teens, or a rating outlook change at NJNG).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q (Q3 FY26 print, seasonally minor) for guidance confirmation, and the FY26 year-end call for the full NFEPS delivery and updated S&T/CEV progress against the capital plan. The FERC ruling on the Leaf River expansion is the key idiosyncratic catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

New Jersey Resources is a stable, well-managed diversified gas utility with a constructive regulator, a genuine and under-modeled midstream growth story in Storage & Transportation, and a 30-year record of raising its dividend. The fundamentals are intact and modestly improving; the FY25 earnings quality was mixed (a one-time solar gain and volatile ES results masking a solid utility rate-case-driven core), but the FY26 normalization is well understood by the market. The problem is price: near its 52-week high, with a below-median yield and ~5% upside to the consensus median, the stock offers a balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable risk/reward. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on this name, and the thesis has not changed. We would become more constructive on a pullback into the low-to-mid $50s or on confirmation that the Leaf River expansion and S&T recontracting are compounding faster than guided. Until then, hold for the dividend and the steady 7-9% NFEPS growth, not for capital appreciation.
