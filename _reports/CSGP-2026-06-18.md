**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CoStar Group trades at $30.46, down roughly 69% from its July 2025 high of $97.43 and sitting just above a 52-week low of $29.53. The headline trailing P/E of 508x is mechanically useless: GAAP net income collapsed from $369M (2022) to $7M (2025), and the company posted a $72M operating loss in 2025. The collapse was not a business failure. The dominant commercial real estate data franchise generated +$672M of segment Adjusted EBITDA in 2025 and is healthier than ever. Management chose to spend that cash flow building a residential marketplace business (Homes.com), which carried Adjusted EBITDA losses of -$361M (2024) and -$230M (2025). The entire investment debate reduces to whether that residential bet pays off.

Q1 2026 is the first quarter that supports the bull case. Revenue grew 23% YoY to $897M, the company returned to GAAP profitability (net income $3M vs -$15M; operating income +$3M vs -$43M), residential Adjusted EBITDA losses narrowed to -$29M from -$85M a year earlier, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA doubled to $132M. Operating cash flow rebounded to $152M from $53M. Founder-CEO Andrew Florance bought roughly $2.5M of stock in the open market at ~$35 on May 1, 2026, near multi-year lows, the most credible conviction signal in the file. The stock now trades below the lowest published analyst target ($33).

The reasons this is a hold rather than a buy: the residential monetization remains unproven, estimate revisions are positive but modest (+3.8% on the current-year number over 90 days, not the +30% velocity that marks a clean inflection), the cash cushion that once propped earnings has been spent down from $4.7B to $1.3B in 15 months, AI-driven changes to search and marketplace traffic are a structural threat to exactly the residential portals CoStar is funding, and $6.7B of goodwill against a depressed market cap creates impairment tail risk. The improving score trajectory (5.4 → 5.2 → 6.2 over the prior three reports) and the Q1 inflection justify holding for appreciation, but the binary nature of the residential outcome caps conviction.

## Company Overview

CoStar is the dominant provider of commercial real estate (CRE) information, analytics, and online marketplaces, now expanding aggressively into residential and international markets. Following a Q4 2025 recast, the business reports in two product segments:

- **Commercial Real Estate (~55% of revenue, the profit engine):** the flagship CoStar subscription platform (FY25 $1.26B), the LoopNet marketplace ($312M), and Other CRE ($216M), which now includes Matterport 3D digital twins, BizBuySell, Ten-X auctions, and STR hospitality benchmarking.
- **Residential Real Estate (~45%, loss-making growth bet):** Apartments.com (mature, profitable), Homes.com (monetization launched Feb 2024, heavy investment), Land.com, and the international portals Domain (Australia) and OnTheMarket (U.K.).

The commercial moat rests on the largest CRE research department in the industry and the most comprehensive CRE database, built over 35-plus years and distributed through auto-renewing annual subscriptions that make up roughly 90% of revenue at a steady ~89% renewal rate. That is a genuinely wide moat with pricing power. The residential side is a contested, capital-intensive land grab against the entrenched Zillow/Realtor.com portal model, and its economics are not yet proven at scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and consistent; GAAP profitability has been deliberately sacrificed.**

- Revenue: $2.18B (2022) → $2.46B (2023) → $2.74B (2024) → $3.25B (2025, +19%), aided by the Domain and Matterport acquisitions.
- Net income: $369M (2022) → $375M (2023) → $139M (2024) → $7M (2025). Operating income swung from +$282M (2023) to a -$72M loss (2025).
- The driver is residential spending. The commercial segment's segment Adjusted EBITDA effectively subsidizes the residential losses.

**The balance sheet remains strong; capital efficiency has weakened.** Altman Z of 4.76 (safe zone), current ratio 2.84, debt/equity 0.21, only $1.0B of 2.8% senior notes due 2030, a $1.1B undrawn revolver, and a 4.5x net-leverage covenant with ample headroom. The concern is the cash drawdown from $4.7B (end-2024) to $1.7B (end-2025) to $1.3B (Q1 2026), roughly $2.8B deployed into acquisitions plus buybacks. The consequence is real: interest income fell from $213M (2024) to $110M (2025), a direct EPS headwind, and FY25 free cash flow was only $123M ($430M operating cash flow less $307M capex, much of it the Richmond campus). Q1 2026 operating cash flow rebounded, the encouraging signal in an otherwise compressed cash-generation profile.

**Capital allocation has been active but poorly timed.** The company completed a $500M buyback in December 2025, authorized a new $1.5B program, and executed a $500M accelerated share repurchase in Q1 2026 at ~$44.27 (11.4M shares) with ~$995M remaining. With shares now near $30, those repurchases were executed roughly 45% above the current price. The remaining authorization gives management the option to buy back materially cheaper now.

## Growth Analysis

The top line is the cleanest part of the story. Revenue is compounding in the high-teens to low-twenties, the commercial engine grows ~9% organically on inflation-linked price increases against the ~89% renewal rate, and the residential and international additions (Domain closed August 2025 at ~$1.9B; Matterport February 2025 at ~$1.9B) push reported growth higher. Management guides residential to accelerate in 2026 on a full year of Domain while commercial moderates.

The earnings inflection matters more than the revenue line. The Q1 narrowing in residential losses and the doubling of consolidated Adjusted EBITDA are the key signals. If residential reaches breakeven, the consolidated margin re-rates materially because the commercial engine's profitability has been masked by the residential drag.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction but not a violent inflection: current-quarter EPS sits at $0.29 (+5.5% over 90 days), current-year at $1.36 (+3.8%), and next-year at $1.80 (+1.3%). These are positive, broad-based, and supported by four consecutive beats (27.7%, 25.5%, 10.9%, 22.5% for Q2'25 through Q1'26), which warrants confidence in the forward numbers. But +3.8% on the year is steady upgrading, not the +30% revision velocity that signals a market systematically underestimating a turn. The growth case is durable but incremental rather than explosive.

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted/consensus earnings the stock is not expensive. At $30.46 against a current-year consensus EPS of $1.36 and next-year $1.80, the forward multiple is ~22x and ~17x respectively, reasonable for a high-teens revenue grower with a wide commercial moat. The stock trades below the lowest analyst target ($33) versus a mean of $48.85 and median of $49, which is the kind of dislocation that occasionally flags genuine mispricing.

Two cautions temper that read. First, the consensus EPS figures are adjusted numbers that exclude the residential losses, which are real cash outflows; the GAAP picture remains near breakeven. A "cheap" forward multiple on a number that strips out the company's largest cash use is only as good as the assumption that residential losses keep narrowing. Second, the peer set is heterogeneous and unhelpful as a clean anchor: JLL at 16x, Cushman at 41.5x, Kennedy-Wilson at 96x, and two names with negative trailing earnings. CoStar is more an information-services franchise than a brokerage, so peer medians carry little signal.

The asymmetry is real but conditional. If residential reaches breakeven over the next 12-18 months and commercial holds its ~9% organic growth, normalized earnings power well exceeds today's depressed base and a re-rating toward the analyst mean ($48.85, ~60% upside) is plausible. If residential losses persist or AI erodes marketplace traffic, the "cheap" forward multiple is a value trap on optimistic adjusted estimates. The presence of a credible catalyst, the Q1 inflection plus the founder's open-market purchase, is what separates this from a pure cheapness call, but the catalyst still has to deliver.

## Competitive Landscape

In commercial real estate data, CoStar is the clear category leader. The proprietary research operation, the comprehensive database, inflation-linked pricing, and high renewal rate constitute a structural moat that competitors cannot easily replicate. This is the part of the business that is improving or, at minimum, stably dominant.

In residential, CoStar is the challenger. Homes.com is a direct assault on the Zillow/Realtor.com lead-generation model, built on a "your-listing-your-lead" agent value proposition. The competitive question is whether subscription-based portal economics plus AI features can win agents at scale before investor patience with the cash burn runs out. The risk factors now explicitly flag that AI-powered search and platform changes could bypass CoStar's marketplaces and erode the SEO-driven traffic that produces Apartments.com and Homes.com leads. CoStar is countering with "Homes AI," but this is a genuine structural threat to the residential thesis. The competitive trajectory is bifurcated: a widening moat in commercial and a contested, uncertain position in the segment absorbing all the capital.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Residential monetization is unproven and expensive.** The single largest swing factor. Residential remains EBITDA-negative; if agent and consumer adoption stalls, the path to profitability lengthens and the equity premium erodes.
2. **Goodwill and intangible impairment.** $6.7B of goodwill plus intangibles, roughly 64% of assets, concentrated in recently acquired Matterport ($1.1B goodwill) and Domain ($1.24B goodwill, $994M in residential). A sustained market-cap decline is itself a stated impairment trigger. CoStar tested at the Q4 2025 reporting-unit change and found no impairment, but this is an underappreciated near-term tail given the depressed price.
3. **AI disruption to marketplace traffic.** A structural threat to the residential lead-generation economics, explicitly elevated in the risk factors.
4. **Litigation.** The Matterport "Brown" case — the Delaware Supreme Court substantially affirmed a $79M damages award in April 2025; $99M accrued with up to ~$17M more possible. An STR hospitality antitrust class action (information-sharing allegations) with unestimable outcome. Multifamily algorithmic-pricing regulatory scrutiny.
5. **Spent cash cushion.** CoStar is no longer the cash-rich acquirer it was; interest income is no longer a meaningful earnings prop, raising the stakes on residential reaching profitability organically.
6. **FX exposure rising.** ~14% of revenue is now foreign (Domain in A$, OnTheMarket in £); a 10% USD move is roughly a $12M quarterly revenue swing, and there is no hedging in place.
7. **Macro/rate sensitivity.** Revenue tracks CRE leasing and transaction activity, apartment vacancy, and agent spending — all rate- and cycle-sensitive. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, weaker CRE transaction volume pressures the high-margin commercial engine that funds residential, while higher mortgage rates dampen the residential demand CoStar is spending to capture.

Much of the headline risk is arguably priced after a 69% drawdown. The risks that are less fully discounted are the goodwill impairment trigger (mechanically tied to the depressed market cap) and the binary residential outcome.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 53.0% against one-year historical volatility of 39.8%, so options are pricing elevated uncertainty (rich by ~13 points), though the IV percentile within the current chain is low at 6%, suggesting the absolute level is not extreme by recent standards. The nearest expiry implies a ±12.2% move. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (-0.6%) but steepens to -3.5% at the six-month tenor, indicating modest demand for downside protection further out. The put/call open-interest ratio of 6.51 looks alarming in isolation but the put/call volume ratio of 1.27 is far more balanced, so the OI figure likely reflects legacy hedging positions rather than fresh directional bearishness. (The options-change feed showing IV jumping to 1,565% is a clear data artifact and is disregarded.) The market is pricing meaningful two-sided uncertainty into the residential inflection, consistent with the fundamental view, without a decisive directional tilt.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The earnings collapse is self-inflicted and reversing. The commercial franchise generates segment EBITDA at a high renewal rate with pricing power; residential losses halved year-over-year in Q1 2026 and the company returned to GAAP profit. As residential approaches breakeven over the next 12-18 months, consolidated margins re-rate sharply because the commercial engine's profitability has been masked. Revenue compounds in the high-teens, the founder bought near lows, and the stock trades below the lowest analyst target. A re-rating toward the analyst mean of ~$48.85 implies roughly 60% upside, with the high target at $70.

**Bear case.** Residential is a money pit whose payoff is unproven, and AI-driven search and marketplace changes threaten the traffic that produces the leads Homes.com is built to monetize. The cash cushion that propped earnings is spent, interest income has fallen by ~$100M, and FY25 free cash flow was only $123M. The $6.7B of goodwill against a depressed market cap creates a live impairment trigger. Management collected $36.4M in CEO pay through a net-income decline to ~$6.5M and peer-lagging TSR (112.4 vs 122.5), repurchased $500M of stock at ~$44, and shuffled the executive ranks during the trough. If residential losses persist, the "cheap" forward multiple is a trap on optimistic adjusted estimates, and the stock drifts toward the high-$20s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth orientation. The improving score trajectory and the Q1 inflection support owning the position for appreciation, but the binary residential outcome and the absence of a decisive near-term catalyst keep it short of a buy. The price decline since the prior review (from ~$33.59 in May to ~$30.46) reflects continued de-rating against stable-to-improving fundamentals rather than fresh deterioration, which improves the entry risk/reward at the margin but does not by itself convert the call to a buy. After three consecutive holds, the bar to continue is whether the inflection is on track, and Q1 says it is, modestly.

**Reassessment timeline:** the next earnings report (covering Q2) is the key checkpoint, with the trajectory of residential Adjusted EBITDA losses the single most important line to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Residential Adjusted EBITDA reaching breakeven or a credible quarter-over-quarter path that points to breakeven within two quarters (Q2'26 loss materially better than -$29M).
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +10% over 90 days alongside sustained 20%+ revenue growth, with the stock still below ~20x forward.
- Confirmation that Homes.com agent monetization is scaling (disclosed memberships/ARPU acceleration), de-risking the residential bet.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or management language signaling an impending one, particularly on the Domain or Matterport units.
- Residential Adjusted EBITDA losses widening sequentially, or management extending the timeline to residential profitability.
- Evidence that AI-driven traffic loss is materially compressing Apartments.com/Homes.com lead volume, or a guidance cut on consolidated revenue growth below ~12%.

## Conclusion

CoStar is a wide-moat commercial data franchise wrapped around an unproven, cash-hungry residential bet. The GAAP earnings trough is a deliberate choice, and Q1 2026 is the first hard evidence that the choice is beginning to pay off: revenue +23%, a return to profitability, and residential losses cut by roughly two-thirds year-over-year. The founder buying near multi-year lows and the stock trading below the lowest analyst target both argue the discount may be overdone.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD rather than BUY is the conditional nature of the upside. The valuation looks reasonable only on adjusted earnings that strip out the residential losses; the revision velocity is steady rather than explosive; the cash cushion that once cushioned earnings is gone; AI is a real and rising threat to the residential traffic model; the goodwill against a depressed market cap is a live impairment risk; and the pay-versus-performance disconnect plus an executive reshuffle during the trough are governance cautions. The improving trajectory and the inflection-in-motion warrant holding the position with a constructive lean, but the residential outcome remains binary enough that adding aggressively requires confirmation in the next one or two quarters.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented). Reassess at Q2 2026 earnings, with residential Adjusted EBITDA the decisive metric.**
