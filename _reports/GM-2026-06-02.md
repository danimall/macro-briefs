**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Motors enters mid-2026 having round-tripped its own story twice. The trailing GAAP picture — net income down from $6.0B (2024) to $2.7B (2025), GAAP EPS of $3.27, a headline trailing P/E of ~32x — reads like a deteriorating business, but it is not. Roughly $9.8B of one-time charges, dominated by a $7.9B EV strategic realignment (tooling impairments, supplier settlements, battery JV exits) plus China JV restructuring and OnStar legal accruals, drove the GAAP collapse. EBIT-adjusted only slipped from $14.9B to $12.7B, and Q1 2026 confirmed an operating recovery: GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin recovered to 10.1% (top of the 8-10% target band), adjusted EPS rose 33% to $3.70, and management raised FY26 guidance to $11.50-13.50 adjusted EPS and $9.9-11.4B GAAP net income. On forward adjusted numbers, GM trades at roughly 6-7x — cheap by any absolute or historical standard.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the cheapness is inseparable from what makes the earnings fragile. GM's profit is concentrated in full-size ICE trucks and SUVs (variable profit weighting ~160% of the GMNA portfolio average), which makes the cash flow unusually exposed to a fuel-price shock or a mix shift toward smaller vehicles. The earnings stream is now extraordinarily policy-sensitive: tariffs cost $3.1B of 2025 EBIT-adjusted and are guided at $2.5-3.5B for 2026, while a favorable Supreme Court IEEPA ruling delivered a $0.5B refund and the EPA endangerment-finding repeal simultaneously removes a future compliance cost and forces an emissions-credit impairment of up to $1.0B. China, historically a meaningful equity-income contributor, is in secular decline (share ~6.9-7.1%) with the JV agreement expiring in 2027 and under renegotiation. These are specific, live, binary swing factors.

The buyback is doing real work — the share count fell from 1,364M (2023) to 902M (Q1'26), roughly a third retired in three years, with $6.3B reauthorized in January 2026. That mechanically supports per-share metrics and signals that management views the stock as cheap. But buyback-driven EPS growth on a policy-contingent, cyclically-elevated earnings base is not the same as durable franchise growth. The score trajectory has improved (5.4 in March to 6.5 in May, with valuation re-rating from 5.5 to 7.5 as the GAAP head-fake was understood), and the current data confirms that improvement. We hold with a constructive growth lean.

## Company Overview

GM designs and sells trucks, crossovers, cars, and parts, supported by software-enabled services and a captive finance arm. It reports three segments:

- **GM North America (GMNA)** — the profit engine. Chevrolet, GMC, Cadillac, and Buick, with full-size ICE pickups and SUVs generating the bulk of profit. Disclosed variable profit weighting confirms the concentration: trucks ~160%, cars ~60%, crossovers ~40% of the GMNA portfolio average.
- **GM International (GMI)** — small (~$13B revenue) and historically marginal. China runs through equity-method JVs (SAIC-GM, SGMW-Wuling) whose vehicle sales are not consolidated; only equity income flows through.
- **GM Financial** — the captive lender, with $90B+ receivables and $114B debt, driving ~33-34% of U.S. retail finance penetration.

GM holds #1 or near-#1 U.S. market share at 16.5-17.2%, with ~32% U.S. truck share and ~14% crossover share. In December 2024 it abandoned the Cruise robotaxi program, acquired the noncontrolling interest in February 2025, wound it down, and folded remaining AV/ADAS work into GMNA, a substantial reduction in capital intensity that is underappreciated in the trailing numbers.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year picture separates the operating reality from the GAAP noise:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $144.0B | $157.7B | $171.6B | $168.0B (-2.1%) |
| Operating income | $10.3B | $9.3B | $12.8B | $2.9B |
| Net income | $9.9B | $10.1B | $6.0B | $2.7B |
| EBIT-adjusted | $14.5B | $12.4B | $14.9B | $12.7B |
| GAAP diluted EPS | — | $7.32 | $6.37 | $3.27 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | — | $7.68 | $10.60 | $10.60 |

The 2025 GAAP collapse is driven by special charges, not operations: $7.9B of EV strategic realignment ($3.2B non-cash tooling impairment plus $4.7B cash charges for supplier settlements, contract cancellations, and battery JV settlements), $0.8B China JV restructuring, $0.66B OnStar privacy legal, and $0.2B Cruise wind-down. Separately, tariffs hit even the adjusted figure.

Cash generation remains strong. 2025 automotive operating cash flow was $18.7B against $9.2B capex, leaving free cash flow near $9.5B (FCF margin ~10%). Cash stood at $20.9B with total automotive liquidity of $35.7B. Corporate credit is investment grade at all four agencies (S&P BBB/stable, Moody's Baa2/stable, Fitch BBB/positive, DBRS BBB-high).

The Altman Z-Score of 1.17, nominally signaling distress, is a false read. GM Financial's ~$114B of debt and the captive-finance asset structure sit on the consolidated balance sheet, mechanically inflating D/E (3.57) and depressing the Z-Score. This is normal for automakers with captive lenders and does not indicate bankruptcy risk; GM is investment grade with $35.7B liquidity. We weight FCF margin and the investment-grade rating over the screen.

Capital allocation is heavily shareholder-return-oriented. Share count fell from 1,364M (2023) to 1,115M (2024) to 955M (2025) to 902M (Q1'26). In 2025 GM spent ~$6.0B on buybacks ($2B ASR plus $4B open market) and $0.5B on dividends; in January 2026 the board reauthorized $6.3B and raised the quarterly dividend to $0.18 from $0.15. The shrinking base is a structural EPS tailwind independent of operating performance.

Two quieter negatives merit attention. The warranty balance jumped to $13.6B from $10.6B a year earlier, a meaningful cost-trend deterioration partly masked by the EV/tariff noise, with open exposures including Takata, the ARC airbag inflators (unquantified, potentially material), and 8-speed transmission class actions. At GM Financial, subprime is 13.2% of the retail book, 30+ day delinquency rose to 3.4% from 3.0%, and there is residual-value risk on a ~$25B lease portfolio if used-car prices weaken.

## Growth Analysis

Q1 2026 is a clean recovery from the depressed 2025 base. GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin recovered to 10.1% (versus 8.8% in Q1'25), back inside the 8-10% target. Adjusted EPS was $3.70, up 33% year-over-year. China JV equity income turned positive (+$165M versus +$45M). Management raised FY26 guidance to $13.5-15.5B EBIT-adjusted, $9.9-11.4B GAAP net income, and $11.50-13.50 adjusted EPS, implying a near-quadrupling of GAAP net income off the 2025 trough.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in the guidance: four consecutive beats, including +37.3% in Q1'26 ($3.70 versus $2.69 estimate), +10.2%, +17.8%, and +0.5% in the prior three quarters. The estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than a clear positive momentum signal. Near-term quarterly estimates have drifted down (current quarter -4.0% over 90 days, next quarter -7.6%), while full-year estimates rose modestly (+3.5% current year, +1.5% next year). This is consistent with timing shifts (tariff and EV-charge cadence) rather than a fundamental deceleration, but it does not constitute the rising-estimate inflection that would justify a more aggressive call. The +30% revision velocity that flags a high-conviction buy is absent here.

The growth source matters. Much of the EPS uplift is buyback-driven on a normalized earnings base that is itself elevated by the regulatory reversal extending the profitable life of high-margin ICE trucks and SUVs. Volume signals softened in Q1'26: U.S. share slipped to 16.5%, GMNA wholesale fell 4.1%, and fleet mix rose to 20.6% of sales (lower margin). The growth thesis is real but is a recovery-and-buyback story, not a secular expansion. We hold Growth Prospects at 5.5, consistent with the recent step-down from 6.5 as the China secular decline and softening U.S. share became clearer.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP EPS of $3.27, the ~32x P/E is meaningless because the trailing period embeds the one-time charges. The relevant lens is forward adjusted earnings. At the $81.76 reference (and a ~$82.68 recent close) against FY26 guidance, GM trades at roughly 6.1-7.1x forward. Full-year consensus of $12.88 (2026) and $14.08 (2027) implies similar 5.8-6.3x multiples. Even on a more conservative normalized basis stripping the policy tailwind, the stock is in the high single digits.

Peer comparison is awkward because the cohort is heterogeneous: Ford carries a negative trailing P/E, Tesla trades at ~400x, and Winnebago at ~20x. The cleaner read is absolute: a ~6-7x forward multiple, ~10% FCF margin, investment-grade balance sheet, and a one-third reduction in shares over three years is statistically cheap. The mean analyst target of $94.81 and median of $96.50 sit 16-18% above the reference price, with a range of $59-131.

What keeps the valuation score at 6.5 rather than 7.5+ is that the low multiple reflects legitimate concern about earnings durability rather than a pure mispricing. The market is pricing GM's earnings as policy-contingent and cyclically elevated, and it has a defensible reason: tariffs, emissions rules, and the China JV expiry are all live binary variables. The specific reason the multiple could re-rate is that buyback-driven per-share compounding continues regardless of these swing factors, and the EV right-sizing structurally lowers future capital intensity. That is a credible path to a 7-8x re-rate, but it is not asymmetric enough on its own to override the risk concentration. The prior score-trajectory re-rating from 5.5 to 7.5 on valuation looks slightly aggressive given the policy contingency; we moderate to 6.5.

## Competitive Landscape

GM's domestic franchise is durable: ~32% U.S. truck share and category leadership in full-size pickups and SUVs, the highest-margin segments. The captive finance arm at ~33-34% retail penetration is a structural advantage. The 2025-26 regulatory reversal (end of U.S. EV consumer credits, relaxed CAFE/GHG rules under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and the EPA endangerment-finding repeal) extends the profitable life of GM's ICE truck/SUV franchise, which is a near-term competitive positive.

The trajectory is mixed rather than improving. China is a secular, not cyclical, decline — share has eroded as domestic EV makers take the market, and the JV agreement expires in 2027 under renegotiation with outcomes solely subject to Chinese government control. U.S. share slipped to 16.5% in Q1'26 with a rising lower-margin fleet mix. The Cruise exit removes a capital sink but also forecloses an autonomy optionality that competitors retain. Net, GM holds a strong but not widening domestic position against a deteriorating international one, which supports a 5.5 — a leader without clear share gains, offset by a structural China loss.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score is held at 5.0 because GM's idiosyncratic, partly binary exposures are unusually high for a company at this valuation:

1. **EV strategy whipsaw and stranded costs.** $7.9B of 2025 charges plus $1.1B in Q1'26, with more expected (though "significantly less" than 2025). Battery raw-material offtake agreements obligate purchases that hit cash and inventory if EV volumes stay low; EV-related NRV inventory reserves remain $1.5B.
2. **Tariffs.** USMCA is under review and the broader regime is dynamic and hard to predict.
3. **Profit concentration in ICE trucks/SUVs** (~160% relative variable profit). A new Q1'26 risk factor explicitly ties this to Middle East conflict and oil prices — an oil-price spike or mix shift toward smaller vehicles would disproportionately hurt earnings. Given the elevated geopolitical backdrop around energy, this exposure is more live than usual.
4. **China JV expiry in 2027** under renegotiation; equity income swung from +$1.1B (2023) to -$4.5B (2024) to roughly breakeven (2025).
5. **Warranty/recall escalation.** $13.6B balance, with the ARC airbag inflator exposure unquantified and potentially material.
6. **Litigation/indirect tax.** OnStar privacy ($0.5B accrued) and indirect-tax exposure up to $5.7B above accruals.
7. **GM Financial credit normalization** (subprime 13.2%, delinquency 3.4%, lease residual risk on a ~$25B portfolio).
8. **Pension** modestly underfunded ($0.7B), with the return assumption cut to 6.0%.

An unconfirmed thread: a May 26, 2026 8-K reported an Item 5.02 officer departure that could not be retrieved. Senior departures during a strategic pivot can be material; this is flagged for follow-up.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.9% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 34.0% (a 13.9-point premium, 77th percentile), with the term structure in backwardation (47.9% near-term versus 40.8% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with a near-dated catalyst (next earnings or a tariff/regulatory headline). Positioning is call-heavy rather than fearful: put/call volume ratio 0.37, put/call OI 0.40, near-term skew -7.9% (call-tilted). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.6%. The options market is pricing a binary near-term event without a clear bearish lean; the rich IV argues against chasing the stock here and favors patience on entry. Short interest is low at 2.4% of float (3.2 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$110-131):** FY26 adjusted EPS lands in the upper guidance half ($12.50-13.50), the $6.3B buyback retires another meaningful slice of the float, tariffs settle at the low end of the 2026 guide or are further refunded, and the regulatory reversal durably extends ICE truck/SUV profitability while cutting future EV capital intensity. A re-rate to 8-9x forward on $13+ EPS, plus continued share shrinkage, supports the high analyst target of $131. The EV right-sizing structurally lifts normalized margins.

**Bear Case (~$59-62):** A fuel-price spike or mix shift away from full-size trucks compresses the ~160%-weighted profit pool, tariffs run hot, the 2027 China JV renegotiation goes poorly, warranty and GM Financial credit costs continue rising, and EV realignment charges exceed expectations. Earnings revert toward the low end of guidance or below, the multiple stays at ~5x on lower normalized EPS, and the stock retests the analyst low of $59 (roughly -28% from current).

**Base Case (~$95):** Adjusted EPS near the guidance midpoint (~$12.50), continued buyback support, tariffs and emissions costs roughly as guided, and a modest re-rate toward 7.5x. This aligns with the analyst mean ($94.81) and median ($96.50), ~16% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July) is the primary checkpoint, with the tariff/USMCA review and any China JV renegotiation milestones as secondary triggers over the following 6-12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance raised toward or above $13.50 with GMNA margin sustained at or above 10%, alongside full-year consensus revisions turning clearly positive (+5% or more over 90 days).
- A favorable, durable resolution of the tariff regime (USMCA clarity or further material refunds) that removes the policy contingency from the earnings base.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives (currently absent), which would shift the management signal from neutral to bullish.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin falling back below 8% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the truck/SUV profit pool is compressing.
- Tariff costs exceeding the $3.5B upper guide, or a punitive China JV renegotiation outcome materially reducing equity income.
- GM Financial 30+ day delinquency rising above ~4.5% combined with falling used-car prices pressuring lease residuals, or warranty accruals continuing to escalate sharply.

## Conclusion

GM is a statistically cheap stock (~6-7x forward adjusted EPS) whose 2025 GAAP collapse was charge-driven, confirmed by a clean Q1 2026 operating recovery and raised FY26 guidance. The buyback is compounding per-share value at a meaningful clip, and the balance sheet is investment grade with $35.7B liquidity. Those facts justify the improved score trajectory and a constructive lean.

The reasons it stops short of a BUY are concrete. The earnings base is elevated by a regulatory reversal that could itself reverse, concentrated in ICE trucks/SUVs that are exposed to an oil-price shock at a time of elevated energy-market risk, and the China franchise is in secular decline with a 2027 JV cliff. Estimate revisions are mixed rather than rising, near-term options pricing signals a binary catalyst with no clear directional edge, and there is no insider buying to confirm the cheapness thesis from the inside. We rate GM a HOLD with a growth lean and a ~$95 base-case fair value, and would become more constructive on confirmation that the operating recovery is durable and the policy overhangs are resolving favorably.
