**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carnival's investment case has moved from balance-sheet repair to demonstrated earnings power. FY2025 produced record revenue of $26.6B (+6.4%), operating income of $4.5B (+25%), net income of $2.76B, and the highest adjusted ROIC in 19 years. Q2 FY2026 (reported June 23) extended the run with record revenue of $6.7B and a 12th consecutive quarter of record net yields, achieved despite roughly 30% higher fuel costs and a $73M fuel/FX headwind. The mechanism driving results is high quality: revenue growth comes from price and yield (FY25 ticket +5.8%, onboard +7.5%) on just +1.0% capacity, not from adding ships into a soft market.

The dominant value driver remains deleveraging. Carnival has cut total debt by more than $10B from the January 2023 peak to $26.0B, completed a $19B refinancing in under a year, brought net debt/adjusted EBITDA to 3.1x (more than half a turn better year-on-year), and earned a Moody's upgrade. Management states it surpassed its investment-grade leverage threshold and pivoted to capital return: a $0.15 quarterly dividend reinstated in December 2025 and a $2.5B buyback authorized in March 2026, of which over $450M has been executed. Each turn of deleveraging mechanically shifts enterprise value from creditors to equity holders, and interest expense down ~23% year-on-year flows straight to the bottom line.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is timing and asymmetry. The stock has rerated roughly 19% off its $22.58 52-week low back to ~13x forward EPS, narrowing the upside that existed at the trough. Estimate revisions are mildly negative (current-year EPS -10.2% over 90 days, next-year -6.1%) as European itinerary disruption from Middle East conflict and higher fuel weigh on the near-term outlook. Carnival sits in Consumer Discretionary against a developing H2 2026 consumer-spending backdrop, and the sector carries the worst sector-relative return profile in our framework. The bull thesis — transitory headwinds fading while the underlying yield-and-deleverage engine fires — is credible and well-supported by 2027 booking data, but it requires fuel and Europe to normalize on schedule. We hold for the 12-month appreciation thesis while acknowledging the risk/reward is no longer the steep discount it was in March.

## Company Overview

Carnival is the world's largest cruise operator, carrying ~13.6 million guests in FY2025 across nine brands: Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America, Seabourn (North America), and AIDA, Costa, P&O Cruises, Cunard (Europe). The business reports four segments: North America cruise (the profit engine, ~$3.3B FY2025 operating income, $31.3B of $51.6B total assets), Europe cruise (~$1.7B operating income), Cruise Support (owned ports and exclusive destinations), and Tour and Other (Alaska land/hotel operations).

Revenue mix runs ~65% passenger ticket and ~35% higher-margin onboard and other (beverage, casino, excursions, specialty dining, internet). The working-capital model is structurally favorable: customers pay deposits well in advance, generating a $9.0B record customer-deposit float as of Q2 FY2026 that funds operations and produces a perpetual, benign ~$8.7–8.9B working-capital deficit.

On structure: following the May 7, 2026 unification and redomiciliation, the company is now a single Bermuda-incorporated entity, Carnival Corporation Ltd., with Carnival plc as a wholly-owned UK subsidiary. The stated benefits (single global share price, simplified governance, lower admin costs, increased liquidity, and higher weighting in major U.S. indices) include a potential passive-inflow catalyst distinct from operating fundamentals.

## Financial Analysis

The recovery from COVID is complete and the financial profile has inflected to durable profitability. Revenue: $1.9B (FY21) → $12.2B (FY22) → $21.6B (FY23) → $25.0B (FY24) → $26.6B (FY25). Net income: -$9.5B → -$6.1B → -$74M → +$1.9B → +$2.76B over the same span.

**FY2025 detail.** Operating margin ~16.8% versus ~14.3% in FY24, lifted by onboard revenue growing faster than tickets. Operating cash flow was $6.2B against capex of $3.6B (down from $4.6B as the newbuild wave moderates), generating free cash flow that now funds deleveraging and capital return. Fuel was a $610/ton-versus-$665 tailwind in FY25 that reversed sharply (~+30% YoY) in FY2026.

**Q2 FY2026.** Record revenue $6.7B, net income $537M, record adjusted net income $569M (+>20% YoY), adjusted EPS $0.41 (+>15%), record adjusted EBITDA $1.6B. The quality of this print is the key data point: it beat the company's own March guidance by $100M despite the fuel and FX headwinds, with fuel consumption per ALBD improving 5.6% and ex-fuel costs roughly flat. The yield engine is performing better than headline GAAP suggests because fuel is masking it.

**Balance sheet — the central story.** Stockholders' equity has rebuilt to $13.0B. Moody's upgraded the credit, and management states it surpassed its investment-grade leverage threshold (covenants include min interest coverage 3.0x, debt/capital ≤65%, min liquidity $1.5B). This is textbook balance-sheet repair, and the de-risking versus 2023 is real.

**Earnings quality flag.** In December 2025 Carnival extended ship depreciable lives from 30 to 35 years and cut residual values, applied prospectively. Management calls the impact immaterial, but lengthening useful lives lowers annual depreciation and flatters GAAP EPS as a non-cash, estimate-driven change. Free cash flow is unaffected; cross-period earnings comparisons should normalize for it.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in management's guidance: four consecutive beats (7.3%, 37.2%, 7.0%, 42.7%), with the company beating yield/cost/EBITDA/EPS every quarter since Q1 2025. This pattern suggests current guidance may again prove conservative.

## Growth Analysis

Carnival's growth is yield-led rather than capacity-led, the higher-quality form of growth in a cyclical business. The 12 consecutive quarters of record net yields rest on a thesis that a persistent price-to-value gap versus land-based vacations is being closed, which would re-rate yields structurally rather than cyclically.

The forward booking picture is the strongest growth evidence. As of Q2, full-year 2026 is 93% booked with less remaining inventory than the prior year, and 2027 bookings are running ahead on both price and occupancy, including a "substantial increase" in European bookings for 2027. This supports management's framing of current European softness as transitory: rather than discounting to fill ships, Carnival leaned on a pre-built occupancy cushion and held pricing integrity.

The exclusive-destination strategy is the differentiated growth lever. Celebration Key (Grand Bahama), opened July 2025, hosted >1M guests in its first ~5 months. Management expects >9M guest visits across its exclusive-destination portfolio (Celebration Key, RelaxAway, Half Moon Cay, Isla Tropicale, the new Ensenada Bay Village) in 2027. These are owned, high-margin assets that both differentiate the product and drop incremental margin to the bottom line.

Newbuild discipline is a feature, not a constraint: three Princess ships ordered for 2035–2039 delivery, ~$11.8B total commitments largely funded by $10.9B of undrawn export credit facilities through 2033. Limited industry-wide shipyard capacity through 2033 keeps supply growth measured, supporting pricing power.

The qualifier on growth is the estimate-revision trend. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 10.2% over 90 days and next-year by 6.1%, driven by European disruption and fuel. The 30-day revisions have stabilized (current quarter +0.5%, current year +0.4%), suggesting the cuts may be done, but the trajectory does not yet show rising-estimate momentum. This is a growth story with a clear path but with two transitory headwinds (fuel, Middle East) currently obscuring the underlying acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

CCL trades at ~13x forward EPS (forward P/E 12.87 on the data) against a Hotels/Resorts/Cruise Lines peer median P/E of 30.8. The cruise comps are the more relevant benchmark: NCLH at 15.8x is the closest analog, and Carnival's discount to both NCLH and the broader leisure set is partly justified by its higher absolute leverage and partly an artifact of where it sits in the deleveraging arc.

The cleanest way to value Carnival is as a deleveraging equity. With $26B of debt and net leverage falling toward and below 3.0x, each turn of deleveraging shifts enterprise value from creditors to equity holders. On a ~$2.22 FY2026 adjusted EPS and ~$2.61 FY2027 consensus, the stock at ~$28.72 trades at ~13x current-year and ~11x next-year earnings, reasonable for a business generating >$9B of free cash flow over 2026–2027 with a credible path to >50% EPS growth by 2029 and ~16% ROIC. The PEG on next-year growth is well below 1.0, which on this growth profile would normally argue for a higher valuation score.

Two factors hold the valuation score at a moderate level rather than high. First, the stock has already rerated ~19% from its 52-week low; while prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, the rerating has compressed the discount that existed at the trough, and the current price sits below the analyst mean target of $35.05 but no longer at a deep absolute discount. Second, our framework consistently finds that a low headline multiple, absent a confirmed rising-estimate catalyst, is a weak buy signal, and here the estimate trend is mildly negative on a 90-day basis. The valuation is fair-to-attractive for the deleveraging-plus-yield thesis, but it is not the steeply mispriced setup it was three months ago. Analyst targets (Jefferies $35, Susquehanna $33, William Blair Outperform, mean $35.05, high $45) imply ~22% upside to the mean, which functions as a floor only if estimate revisions turn positive.

## Competitive Landscape

Carnival's competitive position is improving, which is what supports the above-trajectory score. It holds the largest global berth capacity and a multi-brand portfolio spanning price points and geographies that competitors cannot match. The moat is widening on two fronts: exclusive owned destinations (a structural, high-margin yield driver competitors are racing to replicate at smaller scale) and fleet modernization through the Evolution program at AIDA and Holland America.

Industry dynamics favor incumbents. Shipyard capacity constraints through 2033 cap supply growth across the entire industry, which supports pricing power and is itself a moat for operators with existing scale and order books. The booking curve is the furthest out on record, and 12 straight quarters of record yields evidence genuine pricing power rather than promotional volume.

The competitive risks are real but bounded. Port "overtourism" countermeasures (fees, ship limits) and adverse-weather concentration in the Caribbean — where the exclusive-destination investments sit — are the most company-specific. Overcapacity is a perennial industry risk but is muted by the supply constraint. Against NCLH and Royal Caribbean, Carnival's value-segment positioning and onboard-revenue mix are durable advantages, though it carries more leverage than [unverified — Royal Caribbean comparison not in sources].

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical/itinerary exposure — active and material.** Middle East conflict forced redeployment away from planned Arabian Gulf voyages and trimmed the FY2026 yield outlook by ~1 percentage point. This is the single biggest near-term swing factor. Management's "transitory" framing depends on the conflict de-escalating.

**Leverage and debt service.** Despite the paydown, $26B remains, with cross-default/cross-acceleration provisions and covenants. Materially de-risked versus 2023, but still the dominant structural risk.

**Fuel.** The ~30% YoY increase in FY2026 directly compressed gross-margin yields (down 3.9% in Q2). Fuel is the key external variable masking stronger underlying performance; lapping high fuel in 2027 is central to the accelerating-profit bull case.

**Emissions regulation (escalating).** EU ETS cost ramped $46M (2024) → $91M (2025) → 100% of in-scope emissions in 2026. Net-zero-by-2050 depends on alternative fuels not yet available at scale.

**Consumer cyclicality.** Carnival sits in Consumer Discretionary against a developing H2 2026 consumer-spending backdrop. The 93%-booked FY2026 position cushions near-term demand, but the 2027 book and onboard spending are exposed if the consumer weakens.

**Earnings quality.** The 30→35 year ship-life extension flatters GAAP earnings and should be normalized.

### Options Market Signal

The options chain is pricing near-term stress: ATM IV of 59.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 47.3% (a 12pp spread), at the 79th percentile of the current chain, with a term structure in backwardation (near-term 59.3% > long-term 46.2%). This reflects event risk around the just-reported Q2 print and the Middle East situation rather than a directional bearish bet: the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.28 and skew is balanced at 1.0%, while the 6-month tenor shows a -4.4% (call-favored) skew and a P/C OI ratio of 1.14. Short interest is light at 3.3% of float and 1.2 days to cover, so there is no crowded-bear positioning. The market expects volatility, not a one-directional collapse, and the elevated near-term IV is consistent with the binary nature of the Europe/fuel outlook.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$40–45)

Two transitory headwinds (fuel, Middle East itineraries) fade while the underlying engine fires. Carnival laps high FY2026 fuel costs in 2027, European bookings normalize as already-strong 2027 data suggests, and yields continue their record streak. Deleveraging continues toward and below 3.0x, the Moody's upgrade is followed by a full investment-grade restoration, interest expense keeps falling, and free cash flow of >$9B over 2026–2027 funds buybacks that shrink the share count after years of dilution. EPS compounds toward >50% growth by 2029 with ~16% ROIC. Index-inclusion inflows from the Bermuda redomiciliation add a structural, non-fundamental bid. The stock rerates toward the leisure-peer multiple. The $45 analyst high anchors this scenario.

### Bear Case (target ~$22–24, roughly -16% to -23% from current)

Middle East disruption proves persistent rather than transitory, forcing yield cuts and discounting that breaks the record-yield streak. Fuel stays elevated through 2027. The H2 2026 consumer cliff materializes, softening the 2027 book and onboard spending — the highest-margin revenue. Estimate revisions, already negative on a 90-day basis, continue lower. Deleveraging stalls if EBITDA growth slows, keeping the equity tethered to a heavy debt load. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $22.58.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — deleveraging is working faster than expected and the yield engine is intact — but the rerating off the trough has narrowed the asymmetry, and mildly negative estimate revisions plus a soft consumer backdrop keep this short of a BUY. The score trajectory (4.5 → 5.95) has improved on confirmed deleveraging progress, and Q2 results confirm rather than reverse the improving trend; the earlier declines in the growth, valuation, and management sub-scores reflected the European/fuel guidance cut and routine (non-buying) insider activity, both of which are now well understood and stabilizing.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive on a 90-day basis, confirming the European/fuel headwinds are fading; (2) net debt/adjusted EBITDA falls below 3.0x with a formal investment-grade rating restored; (3) buyback execution visibly outpaces convert/equity dilution, shrinking the diluted share count quarter-on-quarter.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) the record-net-yield streak breaks, signaling Carnival is discounting to fill ships; (2) 2027 booking momentum reverses (price or occupancy falling below prior year); (3) the consumer cliff hits onboard spending materially, with onboard yield growth turning negative.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 print (late September 2026), which will test whether European softness is transitory and whether 2027 bookings hold. The Middle East/Hormuz situation is a near-continuous monitor given its direct effect on both fuel and Mediterranean deployment.

## Conclusion

Carnival has executed a textbook balance-sheet repair: $10B+ of debt cut, a $19B refinancing in under a year, net leverage to 3.1x, a Moody's upgrade, and a pivot from survival to capital return via a reinstated dividend and a $2.5B buyback. The operating business is producing record yields for a 12th straight quarter on disciplined capacity growth, and the exclusive-destination strategy adds a structural, high-margin yield lever.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the steep discount that existed at the trough has compressed with the ~19% rerating, estimate revisions remain mildly negative as fuel and European itinerary disruption obscure the underlying acceleration, and the Consumer Discretionary positioning carries genuine cyclical risk into an uncertain H2 2026 consumer backdrop. The bull case is credible and well-supported by 2027 booking data, and the ~13x forward multiple is reasonable for the growth-and-deleverage profile, but the path to higher upside requires the two transitory headwinds to fade on schedule. We hold for the 12-month appreciation thesis and would upgrade on positive estimate-revision velocity, a formal investment-grade restoration, or evidence the buyback is shrinking the share count.
