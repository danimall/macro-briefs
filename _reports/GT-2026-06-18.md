**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Goodyear is the dominant US-listed pure-play tire manufacturer, midway through the most aggressive portfolio reshaping in its history. The "Goodyear Forward" plan, a ~$1.5 billion cost-out program paired with roughly $2.3 billion of divestiture proceeds (Off-the-Road tires to Yokohama, the Dunlop brand to Sumitomo, and the chemical business), was designed to leave a smaller, less-leveraged, higher-margin company anchored to premium 18-inch-plus consumer tires. The strategy is logically sound, but execution is faltering. Full-year 2025 total segment operating income margin came in at 5.8%, below the company's own 6.91% target, and Q1 2026 saw segment operating income roughly halve year-over-year on an 8.7% revenue decline, driven by a collapse in Americas margins to low single digits.

The headline GAAP figures overstate distress: the $1.72 billion 2025 net loss is dominated by ~$1.45 billion of tax valuation allowances, a $674 million goodwill impairment, and rationalization/pension charges, against ~$796 million of operating cash flow and ~$305 million of adjusted net income. The underlying business still generates cash, but the financial structure is fragile: debt-to-equity near 4.6x, an Altman Z-score of ~1.36 squarely in the distress zone, and $445 million of annual interest expense against thin and declining margins. The entire equity case is a deleveraging-versus-deterioration race: if margins recover toward the 7%+ target, a $1.8 billion market cap on $18 billion of revenue is deeply undervalued; if Americas import-share loss is structural and tariffs escalate, the leverage works against shareholders.

We maintain an AVOID rating. The decisive data point is the direction of estimate revisions: current-year consensus EPS has swung from +$0.54 ninety days ago to -$0.36 today, next-quarter estimates are down 80.7% over 90 days, and next-year estimates down 35.7% over 90 days. Falling estimates against record-low absolute earnings power, in a sector with poor relative performance, leave no asymmetric setup. The stock is down 40.5% over twelve months, and we do not see a specific, falsifiable thesis for why the sellers are wrong. The $1.4 billion deferred-tax valuation allowance is itself a bearish internal signal: management concluded it may not generate enough future taxable income to use those assets.

## Company Overview

Goodyear operates across three reporting segments (Americas, Europe/Middle East/Africa (EMEA), and Asia Pacific), selling into both the original equipment (OE) channel (automakers) and the larger, higher-margin replacement market through a global dealer network and company-owned retail outlets. Full-year 2025 revenue was $18.3 billion, down year-over-year on both divestitures and a 4.7% volume decline.

The company's competitive assets remain a globally recognized brand, an extensive distribution and retail footprint, and longstanding OE relationships. It competes in a brutally cyclical, capital-intensive, commoditizing industry against Michelin, Bridgestone, Continental, and Pirelli at the premium end, and against an accelerating wave of low-cost Asian import tires in the US replacement market. Management's stated escape route from commodity competition is the 18-inch-and-larger rim-size segment, which carries higher specifications and pricing as it rides SUV and EV adoption.

Leadership is relatively new. Mark W. Stewart became CEO in January 2024, joining from Stellantis where he ran North American operations; Christina Zamarro is CFO. The board separated the Chair and CEO roles in January 2024 (a governance positive) and refreshed its membership with auto and industrial executives. Ownership is concentrated in index funds (BlackRock 11.5%, Vanguard 10.2%, Wellington 8.8%).

## Financial Analysis

**2025 results.** Revenue of $18.3 billion produced total segment operating income (SOI) of $1,057 million, a 5.8% margin and below the 6.91% target. The GAAP net loss of $1,721 million is largely non-cash and one-time: approximately $1,448 million of tax valuation allowances and discrete tax items, a $674 million goodwill impairment, $321 million of rationalizations and asset write-offs, and $200 million of pension curtailment/settlement, partly offset by $816 million of net gains on asset sales. Adjusted net income was approximately $305 million. Operating cash flow was $796 million against $826 million of capex, leaving free cash flow roughly breakeven before adjustments.

**Balance sheet.** Debt-to-equity sits near 4.6x with $445 million of 2025 interest expense. The Altman Z-score of ~1.36 places the company in the distress zone (below 1.81), reflecting high leverage and thin margins rather than imminent insolvency. The deleveraging story is the central thesis-saver: divestiture proceeds funded roughly $1.76 billion of debt reduction, and in June 2026 the company issued new senior notes to refinance maturities and extend its profile. That refinancing extends the runway but also underscores continued dependence on capital-markets access.

**Q1 2026 — the most important recent data point.** Revenue fell 8.7% year-over-year to $3.88 billion. GAAP net loss was $249 million (-$0.86/share); the adjusted loss of $0.39 narrowly beat the -$0.41 estimate (a 4.1% surprise). Segment operating income roughly halved versus the prior-year quarter, with the deterioration concentrated in the Americas (historically the profit engine), where margins compressed to low single digits on double-digit volume declines and tariff pressure. EMEA and Asia Pacific strengthened, and Goodyear Forward delivered $107 million of in-quarter benefits, but neither offset the Americas decline. The stock fell roughly 17.5% on the print.

**A control-environment flag.** In 2025 the company restated 2022, 2023, and 2024 financials for errors in Turkey hyperinflation remeasurement (2024 net income revised to ~$35 million). A multi-year restatement, alongside a relatively fresh CFO tenure, is a yellow flag worth monitoring even though no clawback was triggered.

The clean read: the operating business is modestly profitable and cash-generative, the balance sheet is improving, but margins are well below plan, volumes are falling, leverage is high, and the profit-center region is deteriorating.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here in the conventional sense; there is a margin-recovery thesis layered on a shrinking revenue base. Reported revenue is contracting on both divestitures and a 4.7% volume decline in 2025, accelerating to an 8.7% revenue decline in Q1 2026. OE demand is soft, particularly commercial truck, and the replacement market is being eroded by low-cost imports in the US.

The estimate revision trend is unambiguously negative and is the single most important forward indicator in the data. Current-quarter consensus has fallen 12.4% in 30 days and 481.7% over 90 days; next-quarter is down 52.5% (30d) and 80.7% (90d); current-year EPS has swung from +$0.54 ninety days ago to -$0.36; and next-year has fallen from $1.19 to $0.77. Every forward period is being cut, with the 90-day moves far larger than the 30-day moves, indicating the deterioration is broad and ongoing. Estimates are collapsing rather than rising, and this carries decisive weight against the bull case.

The one durable forward lever is Goodyear Forward's ~$1.5 billion run-rate cost benefit. The $107 million Q1 contribution is real and recurring, and the 18-inch-plus mix shift offers a structural margin tailwind if it holds. But cost benefits that are being more than offset by volume declines, tariffs, and pricing pressure slow the bleed rather than produce a growth story. Until volumes stabilize and Americas margins recover, the cost program is a defensive lever, not an earnings engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $6.16, Goodyear carries a $1.8 billion market cap on $18 billion of revenue. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-0.85x), so multiple-based valuation must rest on normalized earnings power. If the company hits a 7% through-cycle SOI margin on a ~$18 billion base, that is roughly $1.26 billion of SOI; net of interest and a normalized tax rate, the equity at $1.8 billion would screen cheap. The peer median P/E in the Consumer Discretionary comparable set is 19.5x, and a recovered Goodyear at even $1.00-$1.20 of normalized EPS would imply meaningful upside.

The problem is that the cycle-adjusted bull case requires assuming a recovery the current data is actively contradicting. The principle that trailing multiples mislead at cyclical troughs cuts both ways: it warrants a higher valuation score only when the turn is visible in rising estimates and stabilizing fundamentals. Here, estimates are falling at double-digit-to-triple-digit rates, the trough is deepening rather than reversing, and the deferred-tax valuation allowance signals management's own doubt about near-term taxable income. A "cheap" multiple on a deteriorating, highly leveraged business is the textbook value-trap profile, and we treat the apparent cheapness with skepticism rather than as a buy signal.

Analyst targets bracket a low of $6.60 and high of $9.00, mean $7.46: modest upside to the mean but a narrow band that reflects the binary nature of the call rather than conviction in a re-rating. We assign a valuation score of 4.0: the absolute discount-to-asset-value optionality keeps it above the floor, but the lack of any visible catalyst and the structural-impairment risk in the Americas prevent a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

Goodyear's competitive position is stable-to-deteriorating, with trajectory the more important read. The brand, distribution, and OE relationships remain genuine assets, and management correctly notes no US-based tire peer matches its scale. But the structural pressure point is the US replacement market, where low-cost Asian imports are taking share and compressing both volume and price. This dynamic is the most likely explanation for the 4.7% 2025 volume decline and the Q1 2026 Americas margin collapse, and it is the pivotal unresolved question: is Americas weakness cyclical and tariff-driven (recoverable) or structural import-share loss (persistent)?

The 18-inch-plus premium strategy is a credible attempt to anchor to higher-spec, less commoditized demand, and EMEA/APAC improvement in Q1 suggests the playbook can work outside the most import-pressured market. But a market leader losing share in its core profit region scores lower than a challenger gaining it, and the evidence currently points to share erosion in the Americas. We score competitive position 4.0, consistent with the prior reading and reflecting deterioration in the region that matters most.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by leverage interacting with operating cyclicality. Substantial fixed costs mean segment operating income swings disproportionately with sales, the dynamic that halved Q1 SOI on an 8.7% revenue decline. With D/E ~4.6x and a distress-zone Z-score, the equity is a thin slice of a heavily indebted capital structure, and any further margin erosion is magnified at the equity level.

Specific, underpriced risks include: (1) tariffs, which cost ~$148 million of 2025 SOI and which management/industry commentary points to as a materially larger headwind in 2026, with the compensation committee excluding tariff impact from incentive metrics; (2) the deferred-tax valuation allowance, which flags management's own concern about future taxable income; (3) the multi-year Turkey hyperinflation restatement, a control-environment yellow flag; (4) execution risk on Goodyear Forward, now listed as the first risk factor; (5) USW labor and strike risk; and (6) ongoing transition/indemnity obligations to the buyers of the divested businesses. Against these, the deleveraging from divestitures and the June 2026 refinancing reduce near-term solvency risk, which is why we do not score this lower. We assign 3.0.

On governance, the 98%-of-target 2025 bonus payout in a year of GAAP net loss and below-target margin (enabled by excluding tariffs and divestiture impacts from metrics) sits uncomfortably against relative TSR at the 30th percentile of the peer group. The compensation structure is otherwise shareholder-friendly (90% at-risk, clawback, double-trigger, ownership guidelines), but the metric exclusions insulate management from the headwinds hurting shareholders.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates the cautious view. Near-term ATM IV of 57.7% is rich relative to 46.8% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term vol above long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress rather than complacency. Near-term put/call skew of 3.2% shows modest elevated put demand, and the three- and six-month tenors carry rising put/call volume ratios (1.29 and 1.89), showing the market is buying downside protection further out. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.5%. Short interest is 14.7% of float at 3.6 days to cover, elevated but not extreme. The collective read: institutions are positioned bearishly and hedging into expected volatility, which both confirms the AVOID and constrains the short case (a crowded-ish short with low days-to-cover and live squeeze potential on any positive surprise).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($9.00+).** The Americas margin collapse proves cyclical and tariff-driven rather than structural. Goodyear Forward's cost benefit lifts through-cycle SOI margin toward 7%+, divestiture proceeds and the June refinancing complete the deleveraging, and the equity re-rates as normalized EPS recovers toward $1.00+. Estimates inflect upward, the deferred-tax allowance reverses, and the market re-prices the equity as deeply undervalued against revenue. EMEA/APAC strength generalizes to the Americas.

**Bear Case ($4.00, -35%).** Americas import-share loss is structural and persistent; tariffs escalate further in 2026; volumes continue to decline double digits in the core region. Cost benefits are more than offset by pricing and volume pressure, margins stay below 6%, and the highly leveraged equity bears the full force of operating deleverage. The deferred-tax allowance proves prescient, refinancing costs rise, and consensus continues cutting. At sub-6% margins and 4.6x leverage, the equity value compresses materially.

The estimate revision evidence currently weights the bear case. We do not have a falsifiable thesis for why the market, which has marked the stock down 40.5% over twelve months, is wrong, and the burden of proof at a drawdown of this magnitude rests with the bull. Until estimates stop falling and Americas margins stabilize, the asymmetry favors avoiding.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalyst is the next quarterly print (Q2 2026), which would test whether Americas margins continue deteriorating and whether 2026 tariff impact runs materially above the 2025 ~$148 million figure. Estimate revisions are already collapsing, so a continued cut cycle could pressure the stock further. But there is no single hard, dated event (no near-term debt cliff post-refinancing, no binding regulatory ruling) that forces a re-rating lower on a defined timeline.

**Timing window.** The Q2 print is the next natural resolution point. A short held into that event carries roughly a quarter of borrow and opportunity cost without an exceptional setup.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 14.7% of float with 3.6 days to cover, elevated but below the >25% crowded-short threshold, and the low days-to-cover means modest squeeze capacity on any positive surprise. Institutional ownership is heavily indexed (BlackRock/Vanguard/Wellington), which dampens forced-selling dynamics.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms a bearish lean (rich near-term IV, backwardation, rising put/call volume in outer tenors), but near-term put/call skew is only modestly elevated and near-dated put/call volume ratios are low (0.14-0.22), indicating no acute panic positioning at the front of the curve.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case target ~$4.00 (-35%) against a credible squeeze/beat upside toward $7.50-$9.00 (+22% to +46%) on any margin stabilization, deleveraging milestone, or M&A/take-private interest at this depressed valuation. That is roughly 1:1 risk/reward, below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a deteriorating, over-leveraged turnaround, not an actively collapsing business with a dated trigger. The deleveraging narrative, the positive next-year (2027) consensus earnings recovery still embedded in estimates, and the modest squeeze risk against a sub-2:1 payoff make this one to avoid rather than to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: AVOID.** Re-entry would require evidence that the margin-recovery thesis is materializing rather than receding.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year and next-year consensus EPS stop falling and begin rising over consecutive 30-day windows.
- Americas segment operating margin stabilizes and recovers toward mid-single digits in a reported quarter, demonstrating the weakness was cyclical/tariff-driven rather than structural import-share loss.
- Total SOI margin progresses back toward the 6.91%+ target, confirming Goodyear Forward benefits are flowing through to the bottom line rather than being absorbed by volume and price erosion.
- Concrete evidence of further deleveraging (net debt reduction, improved Z-score above the distress threshold) without renewed reliance on dilutive or expensive financing.

**Timeline.** We expect the Americas and tariff headwinds to persist through at least the next two to three quarters. The next-year consensus recovery (next-year EPS $0.77, down from $1.19) is the implied turnaround window, but it is being cut, not confirmed. Reassess at the Q2 2026 print and again at year-end 2026 results.

## Conclusion

Goodyear is a coherent self-help story executing into the teeth of deteriorating fundamentals. The deleveraging from $2.3 billion of divestiture proceeds is real, the GAAP loss is largely optical, and the underlying business still generates cash. But the equity is a thin, highly leveraged claim on a business whose core profit region is contracting, whose volumes are falling, whose tariff exposure is rising, and whose forward estimates are being cut across every period. The $1.4 billion deferred-tax valuation allowance is management's own statement of doubt about near-term earnings power. The apparent cheapness against asset value is the classic value-trap configuration absent a visible catalyst, and the options and short-interest data confirm the market is positioned for continued stress without offering an attractive short.

We maintain **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.6, broadly consistent with the prior reading. The path back to a constructive view runs through estimate stabilization and an Americas margin recovery, neither of which is present today. Until then, the asymmetry favors staying out.
