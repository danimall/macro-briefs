**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Terex has spent roughly eighteen months executing a wholesale reconstruction of its business. Management sold cyclical tower and rough-terrain cranes, bought Dover's Environmental Solutions Group (ESG) for over $2B in late 2024, closed the $3.38B REV Group merger in February 2026, and put its historically largest segment (Genie-branded Aerials) under strategic review for divestiture. The intended result is a company weighted ~80% to North American revenue and ~85% U.S.-manufactured, with demand tilted toward waste/recycling, electric-grid utilities, and fire/emergency vehicles rather than rate-sensitive construction and aerial platforms. The strategic logic is coherent and, if it lands, lowers operating cyclicality in exchange for higher financial and integration risk.

The near-term financials are difficult to read cleanly because of the timing. Q1 2026 showed a GAAP operating loss of $(82)M and a net loss of $(89)M / $(0.93) EPS, but these are almost entirely purchase-accounting artifacts: a $112M REV inventory step-up, $47M of intangible amortization, $17M of transaction costs, and ~$25M of accelerated stock comp. Underneath, adjusted EBITDA rose 35% to $173M, pro-forma revenue grew 10.8%, every segment grew organically, and backlog rose to $7.1B from $2.35B at year-end 2025. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $7.5–8.1B revenue, $930M–$1.0B EBITDA, and $4.50–5.00 EPS. The inventory step-up should roll off within a quarter or two, sharply improving reported margins in 2H 2026.

We rate TEX a HOLD (growth_hold). This is the fourth consecutive HOLD at a roughly flat overall score (5.6 → 5.9 → 5.6 → 5.65), and the bar for continuing that call is appropriately higher now. The thesis holds because two swing factors remain unresolved: the Aerials divestiture (~27% of Q1 sales, the lowest-margin segment) and the roll-off of purchase accounting that would confirm the ~12.4% guided margin. The stock at ~$70 sits above the low analyst target ($66.50) and below the middle of the target range, trades at ~15x the midpoint of guided 2026 EPS, and is priced fairly for a business still proving its transformation. We would upgrade on a clean Aerials sale at a reasonable multiple plus confirmed margin normalization, and downgrade on integration slippage, a failed divestiture process, or covenant pressure.

## Company Overview

Terex is a global manufacturer of specialized capital equipment. Post-REV it reports four segments:

- **Environmental Solutions (ES)** — refuse/recycling bodies (Heil), compactors, balers, plus electric-utility equipment (digger derricks, insulated bucket trucks) and the 3rd Eye AI camera / Soft-Pak software franchise. Built from the ESG acquisition. 2025 sales $1.69B at a 13.8% operating margin; operating profit rose ~185% YoY on the full-year ESG add. This is the strongest segment in the portfolio.
- **Materials Processing (MP)** — crushers, screens, washing systems, material handlers, and concrete mixers (Powerscreen, Fuchs, Finlay). 2025 sales $1.68B at a 13.9% margin. Terex divested tower/rough-terrain cranes here in 2025 to reduce cyclicality.
- **Specialty Vehicles (SV)** — new from the REV merger: fire apparatus, ambulances, terminal trucks, industrial sweepers, and Class A/C recreational vehicles. Contributed $436M and a 14.2% EBITDA margin in its first two partial months.
- **Aerials** — Genie aerial work platforms and telehandlers, historically the largest business, now under review for possible sale. Its 2025 operating margin collapsed to 5.0% from 11.2% in 2024, the direct reason the portfolio is being reshaped.

Customer concentration is low: no single customer exceeds 6% of sales and the top 10 are under 28%. The company employs ~10,700, with roughly 1% of U.S. staff unionized.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (three-segment, pre-REV).** Revenue was $5.42B, up 5.7%, driven entirely by the full-year ESG contribution offsetting Aerials (-14.5%) and MP (-11.6%). Operating profit was $475M, but margin compressed to 8.8% from 10.3% in 2024 and 12.4% in 2023, the combined effect of mix shift, Aerials volume deleverage, and tariffs. Net interest expense nearly doubled to $165M from the ESG deal debt. Net income was ~$221M and free cash flow $322–325M, a strong 147% FCF conversion; the company returned $98M to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

**Q1 2026 (four-segment, post-REV).** Reported revenue jumped 41% to $1.73B, or +8.1% organic. Adjusted EBITDA was $173M (+35%), and management-reported EPS was $0.98 (which includes roughly $0.10 of one-time tax benefit). The balance sheet nearly doubled: total assets $6.1B → $10.2B, goodwill $1.1B → $2.5B, intangibles $1.0B → $3.0B (including $758M of indefinite-lived trade names and $819M of customer relationships), and long-term debt $2.58B → $2.75B. Share count rose from ~65M to ~113.5M as 47.9M new Terex shares were issued to REV holders.

Net leverage stands at 2.4x, down and trending lower. Working capital improved markedly to 16.7% of sales from ~26% a year earlier, helped by REV's less-seasonal profile and $359M of assumed customer advances. The backlog is boosted by REV's roughly two-year fire/ambulance order book plus healthy MP and Aerials bookings.

**Health metrics.** The Altman Z-Score of 2.23 places Terex in the "grey zone," but this is an artifact of the debt-financed transformation rather than a distress signal — leverage is already declining and coverage is comfortable. The first-lien net leverage covenant of 3.00x is tested only if the revolver is drawn beyond 30%, and consolidated cash-flow coverage must stay above 2.0x. ROIC of 11.7% (management-adjusted), ROE 10.5%, and a 2.3 current ratio round out a picture of moderate but manageable financial stress.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten in two of the last four quarters (Q3 2025 +23.3%, Q1 2026 +25.2% on the management-adjusted number) with one in-line print. That gives reasonable confidence in the reaffirmed 2026 guidance, though the current-quarter estimate has been trimmed 8.5% over 90 days, a sign that near-term visibility on the transition quarters is imperfect.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three structurally supported end markets plus deal synergies:

- **Utilities** was flagged as the fastest-growing business, tied to grid and data-center electricity demand, with 8–15% industry capex growth guided through 2030 and Terex adding ~30% capacity. This ties into the broad data-center power buildout theme and is likely underappreciated relative to the headline Aerials weakness.
- **Waste/recycling (ES)** benefits from population growth and a 2027 EPA regulatory cycle driving pre-buy activity.
- **Fire/emergency (SV)** is anchored by municipal budgets and a ~two-year backlog that provides revenue visibility unusual for a capital-goods maker.

Synergies add a defined layer: $28M in 2026 and a $75M run-rate target within 24 months. Management's on-schedule, within-budget execution of the ESG integration lends credibility to the REV plan.

2026 guidance implies EBITDA margin expanding to ~12.4% from the FY2025 operating margin, largely as purchase-accounting drag rolls off and synergies flow through. Estimate revisions are stable rather than a strong tailwind: current-year consensus is +1.7% over 90 days and next-year +1.3%, while the current-quarter number slipped 8.5%. This is a steady, transitional pattern consistent with a company whose reported numbers are temporarily distorted, not the >30% revision velocity that would flag a decisive buy signal.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline trailing multiples are misleading here. The ~47x trailing P/E reflects transitional GAAP earnings depressed by non-cash step-up amortization, not underlying earnings power. The relevant frame is forward: on the guided 2026 EPS, the stock at ~$70 trades at roughly 14–15x. On the ~$4.87 current-year and ~$5.81 next-year consensus, it is ~14.3x and ~12.0x respectively.

Against peers in construction machinery and heavy transportation (median P/E 18.5x), Terex screens cheaper on forward earnings, but the discount is defensible given elevated leverage, integration risk, and the unresolved Aerials process. Oshkosh, the closest competitor in aerials/emergency, trades at ~14.9x; GATX at ~18.5x; Trinity at ~10.8x. Terex sits in the lower half of that range, appropriately.

The analyst target range of $66.50 (low) to $100.00 (high), with a $79.46 mean and $80.00 median, implies ~14% upside to the mean. At the current price the stock offers modest asymmetric upside if the transformation delivers, but the mispricing case is not compelling: the discount to peers is largely earned by risk, and forward earnings already assume clean margin normalization that has not yet been demonstrated in reported results. We score valuation neutral (5.0). The tariff-refund optionality from the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling — for which Terex has filed refund claims but booked zero benefit — represents un-modeled upside worth monitoring, but it is not bankable today.

## Competitive Landscape

Terex has no single peer; it competes segment by segment against Oshkosh (JLG/McNeilus), Astec, Sandvik, Metso, Caterpillar, Liebherr, Deere/Kleeman, Manitou, Skyjack (Linamar), Altec, and Chinese OEMs (Dingli, Sinoboom, XCMG, Zoomlion). In Aerials, low-cost Chinese competition is a structural threat, partly mitigated by U.S. and EU anti-dumping/countervailing duties that Terex helped pursue.

The competitive trajectory is improving where it matters. The pivot toward ES and SV shifts revenue mix toward defensible, less-contested municipal and utility demand and away from the aerials arena where Chinese pricing pressure is most acute. The utilities franchise, riding grid and data-center capex, is a genuine structural tailwind. Divesting cranes and marketing Aerials removes the segments where Terex's competitive position was weakest. Level of dominance is unremarkable segment by segment, but the direction of the portfolio's competitive quality is positive, which supports a score above the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and leverage (the central risk).** Two large deals in ~15 months lifted debt roughly 4x from 2023 levels. While net leverage and the covenant structure (3.00x first-lien cap tested only above 30% revolver draw) are manageable, missing synergy or deleveraging targets would tighten flexibility. The 10-K names REV integration as its lead risk factor.

**Aerials divestiture uncertainty.** The review is "progressing" with multiple parties, but management is deliberately vague on timing and price. A clean sale de-risks cyclicality and funds deleveraging; a failed or lowball process crystallizes the segment's depressed value and would disappoint.

**Cyclicality and rate sensitivity** persist in Aerials and MP, tied to rental-company capex, rent-to-own conversions, and used-equipment residuals — all pressured by higher-for-longer rates.

**Impairment exposure.** With $2.5B goodwill and $3.0B intangibles, a demand downturn in Aerials, SV, or the RV business could force write-downs.

**RV exposure is a subtle inconsistency** in the low-cyclicality thesis: SV includes Class A/C motorhomes, a discretionary consumer product highly sensitive to rates and confidence, folded into an otherwise defensive municipal segment.

**Contingent liabilities from REV** are new: $830M in performance/bid bonds, $430M in dealer repurchase agreements, and $241M in customer-owned chassis exposure — customary for specialty vehicles but new to Terex's risk profile.

**Governance overhang.** A standing 2009 SEC injunction barring future securities-law violations heightens the consequences of any internal-control failure, given the pace of acquisition-driven accounting complexity. Early-stage antitrust class actions name Terex as a defendant (company says without merit).

These are meaningful, partly binary risks not fully priced, which supports a risk score below the midpoint (4.8), a modest improvement from the prior 5.0 reflecting the lower leverage and reaffirmed guidance offsetting the still-open divestiture question.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 59.0% is rich versus 48.7% one-year historical vol and sits at the 72nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (59.0% near-term vs 47.5% six-month), which typically prices imminent event risk — most plausibly the upcoming earnings print and possible Aerials news. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1%) and put/call volume/OI ratios are near zero, so there is no directional bearish tilt in positioning. The implied ±12.7% move at the nearest expiry is wide, signaling the market expects a catalyst-driven repricing in either direction. For a fundamental holder, the elevated near-term IV argues against paying up for short-dated exposure and favors patience until the vol crush that typically follows the earnings/divestiture resolution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($90–100).** The Aerials divestiture closes at a reasonable multiple, funding deleveraging below 2x and validating the resilient-portfolio narrative. The REV inventory step-up rolls off in 2H 2026, revealing the guided ~12.4% EBITDA margin; utilities and waste/recycling compound at high single to low double digits; the $75M synergy run-rate lands ahead of schedule; and the IEEPA tariff refund provides un-modeled upside. On ~$5.81 next-year EPS at a re-rated ~16–17x (peer median), the stock reaches the low-to-mid $90s to $100.

**Bear case ($48–52, ~26–31% downside).** The Aerials process stalls or clears at a lowball price; integration frictions and RV/consumer-cyclical softness pressure margins; a demand downturn forces goodwill/intangible impairment; and rate-sensitive Aerials/MP end markets weaken. Reported margins fail to normalize on schedule, the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range on trough earnings, and the stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $41.70 to the low-$50s.

**Base case ($78–82).** The transformation proceeds roughly on plan: purchase accounting rolls off, 2026 EPS lands in the guided range, and the Aerials review resolves at an acceptable outcome within 12 months. At ~15–16x forward EPS the stock reaches the high-$70s to low-$80s, consistent with the current analyst mean/median of ~$79–80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored on (1) 2H 2026 earnings prints that should reveal clean margins as the inventory step-up rolls off, and (2) resolution of the Aerials strategic review.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Aerials sale announced at a multiple that funds material deleveraging, with proceeds directed to debt reduction below 2x net leverage.
- 2H 2026 reported operating margin confirms the ~12.4% EBITDA guidance as step-up amortization rolls off, alongside estimate revisions turning meaningfully positive (>+5% current-year).
- IEEPA tariff refund recognized, adding un-modeled EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Aerials process abandoned or clears at a lowball multiple that fails to reduce leverage.
- Integration slippage or a synergy shortfall pushing net leverage back toward the 3.0x covenant zone, or a goodwill/intangible impairment charge.
- Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue contraction across MP/Aerials as rate-sensitive demand rolls over, or EPS guidance cut below the $4.50 floor.

## Conclusion

Terex is a credible transformation story priced fairly for its stage of execution. The operational trajectory is positive: a defensible pivot toward utilities, waste, and emergency vehicles, and a management team with a clean ESG-integration track record. But the reported financials remain distorted by purchase accounting, leverage is still bedding in two large deals, and the single largest value swing (the Aerials divestiture) is unresolved and deliberately opaque. At ~$70 and roughly 14–15x forward EPS, the stock offers modest upside to the analyst mean of ~$79 but no compelling mispricing, and the risk/reward is roughly balanced pending two catalysts that have not yet resolved.

We are comfortable maintaining the fourth consecutive HOLD as a growth_hold: the appreciation case is real but contingent, and the prudent posture is to wait for the Aerials outcome and clean 2H 2026 margins before adding conviction. Accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$50s where the peer discount and forward-earnings support turn more asymmetric; take the upgrade signal on a favorable divestiture plus confirmed margin normalization.
