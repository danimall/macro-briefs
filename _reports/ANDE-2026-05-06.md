## Executive Summary

The Andersons is a mid-cap agricultural supply chain and renewable fuels company undergoing a meaningful earnings inflection. The company has delivered three consecutive earnings beats (56.9%, 28.5%, and 79.0% surprises) and management has reaffirmed a $7.00 exit EPS target by 2028, up from $2.79 in FY2025. The primary earnings catalyst is the 45Z clean fuel production tax credit, which contributed $35M in 2025 and is guided to $90-100M in 2026, combined with full ownership of four ethanol plants (acquired Marathon's 49.9% stake in July 2025) and Agribusiness cyclical recovery.

The stock has already moved significantly — up 114% from its May 2025 low of $31.03 to $79.29 — and now trades at 28.4x trailing EPS on depressed 2025 earnings, or approximately 16x management's $4.88 consensus for 2026. The earnings growth path to $7.00 by 2028 is credible but policy-dependent: the 45Z credit alone accounts for approximately $2.00/share of the bridge and faces regulatory, political, and compliance risks that management itself highlights as a new risk factor. Persistent insider selling by the former CEO ($5.8M liquidated in 2 months) creates an additional confidence gap that prevents a full-conviction BUY at current levels.

## Company Overview

The Andersons operates through two segments following a January 2025 restructuring:

**Agribusiness (~75% of revenue):** Merchandises agricultural commodities (corn, wheat, soybeans), operates ~271 million bushels of grain storage across 180 locations, manufactures/distributes fertilizers, and provides premium ingredients to CPG customers. Geographic footprint spans Eastern and Western Grain Belts plus international trading.

**Renewables (~25% of revenue):** Four 100%-owned ethanol plants (Iowa, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio) with 405 million gallons combined nameplate capacity. Also merchandises ethanol, renewable feedstocks, and biofuels.

The company completed two transformative transactions in 2024-2025: acquisition of Marathon Petroleum's 49.9% stake in TAMH (ethanol JV) for $425M in August 2025, and the Skyland Grain acquisition extending operations into Kansas/Texas panhandle.

Gross profit remained relatively stable at $684M-$745M across this period, confirming that merchandising spreads — not headline revenue — drive profitability.

**Segment Profitability (2025):**
- Agribusiness pre-tax income: $57M (vs. $109M in 2024, -48%) — cyclical trough from oversupplied grain markets
- Renewables pre-tax income: $116M (vs. $83M in 2024, +40%) — driven by full ownership (+$40M) and $35M in 45Z credits

**Recent Earnings Trajectory:**

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on five identifiable earnings bridges from $2.79 (2025) toward $7.00 (2028 exit target):

1. **45Z Tax Credits:** $35M in 2025 → $90-100M guided for 2026 → potentially $100M+ through 2029 extension. This is approximately $2.00/share incremental at full run-rate.

2. **Full Ethanol Ownership:** TAMH acquisition added ~$40M incremental pre-tax in 2025 (partial year). Full-year benefit crystallizes in 2026.

3. **Capacity Expansion:** Clymers, Indiana debottlenecking adds 30M gallons (completion late 2027), worth approximately $0.50-0.75/share at current margins.

4. **Agribusiness Cyclical Recovery:** 2025's $57M pre-tax was a trough. Normalized contribution is $100M+. Q1 2026 showed improvement as market volatility returned.

5. **Export Infrastructure:** Port of Houston grain elevator upgrades (Q2 2026) and soybean meal export facility (Q3 2026) provide new margin contribution.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter EPS estimates have risen 42.7% over 90 days ($0.85 → $1.21), and current year estimates are up 10.9% ($4.41 → $4.88). This positive momentum is driven heavily by 45Z credit recognition and Agribusiness recovery that may not compound indefinitely.

- Forward P/E on 2028 target ($7.00): 11.3x
- EV/EBITDA on 2025: ~7x; on estimated 2026 (~$425M): ~5.5x

**Peer comparison:** Food distributor peers trade at a median P/E of 39.9x (CHEF 45.1x, PFGC 39.9x, USFD 31.0x, SYY 20.5x). At 28.4x trailing and 16.2x forward, ANDE trades at a discount to direct peers. However, ANDE's earnings mix is now heavily skewed toward ethanol/renewables and commodity merchandising, which warrant lower multiples than stable food distribution.

The stock already reflects meaningful improvement from its 2025 trough. At 11-12x the $7 target, the market appears to assign roughly 60-70% probability to full achievement — reasonable given the policy dependencies.

**Analyst consensus target is $75.00** — the stock has already exceeded this level at $79.29. All three covering analysts rate it Buy, but their targets suggest limited near-term upside and may require revision.

## Competitive Landscape

ANDE competes against much larger grain merchandisers (ADM, Bunge, Cargill) in Agribusiness and against ethanol peers (Green Plains, Rex Energy) in Renewables. Competitive advantages include:

- **Nimbleness:** 180 locations with regional density create basis arbitrage opportunities unavailable to larger, more diversified players
- **Vertical integration:** Ethanol plants source corn from ANDE's own grain network, reducing basis risk and transport costs
- **Premium ingredients:** CPG customer relationships provide higher-margin revenue than pure commodity merchandising
- **Carbon intensity:** Investments in carbon sequestration (Class 6 well at Clymers) will reduce lifecycle emissions scores, improving 45Z credit rates

Competitive weaknesses:
- Scale disadvantage vs. ADM/Bunge in capital markets access, international origination, and customer breadth
- Skyland acquisition underperforming initial targets (~$20M EBITDA vs. $30-40M range)
- Geographic concentration in Eastern Grain Belt creates weather/basis correlation risk

## Risk Assessment

**1. 45Z Policy Risk (HIGH):** The single largest risk to the thesis. Management explicitly added this as a new risk factor in the 2025 10-K. The credit could be reduced if: (a) lifecycle emissions modeling changes, (b) IRS/Treasury guidance shifts, (c) prevailing wage requirements aren't met, (d) political environment changes. At full qualification, the credit contributes ~$2.00/share. At 1/5th of the enhanced rate, the contribution drops to approximately $0.30-0.40/share — a $55-70M annual swing.

**2. Commodity Cycle Risk:** Agribusiness profitability varies 2-3x across cycles. Oversupplied grain markets (as in 2025) compress merchandising margins regardless of management execution.

**3. Integration Execution:** $425M+ deployed on TAMH and Skyland in 2024-2025. Skyland's $20M EBITDA vs. $30-40M target in year one signals integration is tracking below plan.

**4. Capital Intensity:** Negative FCF and $200-225M annual capex creates funding pressure if credit markets tighten. The March 2026 8-K filing (new material debt obligation) suggests ongoing external financing needs.

**5. EV Transition (Long-term):** Electric vehicle penetration threatens biofuels demand structurally over a 10-15 year horizon. Year-round E15 and sustainable aviation fuel provide medium-term mitigation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-110, +20% to +39%):**
- 45Z credits achieve $100M+ annually without policy disruption
- Agribusiness recovers to $100M+ pre-tax on normalized grain volatility
- Clymers expansion and Port of Houston come online on schedule
- Market re-rates toward 14-15x $7.00 EPS as 2028 target becomes visible
- At 15x $7.00 = $105 fair value

**Bear Case ($50-55, -37% to -31%):**
- 45Z credit reduced or eliminated under political/regulatory change
- Grain markets remain oversupplied, compressing Agribusiness margins
- Ethanol crush margins narrow on elevated corn prices
- Market assigns 10-12x reduced forward EPS of ~$4-5
- At 11x $4.50 = ~$50

**Base Case ($75-85, -5% to +7%):**
- 45Z credits largely achieved at $80-90M (slight haircut to guidance)
- Agribusiness partial recovery to $80-90M pre-tax
- Stock consolidates around 14-15x $5.00-5.50 mid-cycle EPS
- Limited upside from current levels until 2027 catalysts materialize

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price retracement to $60-65 range (forward P/E compressed below 13x on maintained 2026 estimates), representing a valuation reset with meaningful asymmetry
2. Formal Treasury/IRS guidance confirming 45Z credit structure for corn ethanol through 2029, removing the largest binary risk
3. Multiple insider purchases at open market prices, resolving the confidence gap created by Bowe's selling

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. 45Z credit rate reduced below 50% of current level via regulation, legislation, or model change
2. Two consecutive quarters of estimate revisions turning negative
3. Leverage exceeding 3.0x Debt/EBITDA, suggesting growth projects consuming more capital than planned

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (likely August 2026 for Q2 results). Key datapoints: Agribusiness margin trajectory, 45Z credit recognition pace, Port of Houston operational start, and any 45Z regulatory developments.

## Conclusion

The Andersons is executing a credible earnings inflection with multiple growth vectors and a 42.7% upward revision in current-quarter estimates. The path from $2.79 to $7.00 EPS by 2028 is arithmetically sound if 45Z credits persist, Agribusiness normalizes, and expansion projects deliver on schedule. At $79.29 — already 114% above its 2025 low and 4% above the analyst consensus target — the stock prices in substantial forward improvement. The 45Z credit dependency creates binary policy risk that management itself identifies as material, and aggressive insider selling by the former CEO adds uncertainty that a strong fundamental thesis alone doesn't fully offset.

The risk/reward is approximately balanced. Bull scenario offers 20-39% upside, bear scenario implies 31-37% downside, and base case suggests the stock is near fair value with modest single-digit movement potential over the next 6 months. HOLD with a growth thesis contingent on 2027 catalysts (Clymers expansion, full Port of Houston contribution) providing the next leg of re-rating. Entry would become attractive at $60-65 where the forward multiple compresses sufficiently to compensate for policy risk.
