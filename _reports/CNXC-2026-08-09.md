## Executive Summary

Concentrix is the cheapest large-cap-adjacent services business in our coverage on nearly every cash-based metric, and it is cheap for reasons that are specific, disclosed, and not yet resolved. On trailing twelve-month figures the company generated $1,433M of adjusted EBITDA and $514M of free cash flow. Against an enterprise value of roughly $5.95B, that is 4.2x EV/EBITDA and an 8.6% FCF/EV yield; against a $1.6B equity stub it is 2.3x trailing non-GAAP EPS of $10.97 and a ~31% trailing FCF yield on market cap. The dividend yields ~5.5% and is covered roughly 5.6x by trailing free cash flow.

The reason those multiples exist is a change in the pricing structure of the business rather than a collapse in demand for it. Management attributed the June 29 guidance reduction primarily to an acceleration in the mix shift toward offshore delivery — now quantified at nearly a 300 basis point revenue headwind versus a prior 200bp assumption — plus roughly 100bp from clients reducing support for certain customer segments in high-cost markets. Netting ~400bp of headwind against constant-currency growth guidance of +0.25% to +1.25% implies underlying activity growth of roughly 4–5%. Concentrix is winning work and being paid less per unit of it. The FY2025 10-K states, in past tense, that "some lower complexity services currently performed by our game-changers have been and may continue to be replaced by tools deployed by clients," which describes disintermediation by client-deployed automation, the structural bear case for the entire outsourced-CX complex.

The investment problem is arithmetic. With $4.34B of net debt against a $1.6B equity value, a 15% decline in EBITDA at an unchanged 4.2x multiple roughly halves the equity, while stabilization plus $500M of annual debt paydown compounds it quickly. Our probability-weighted outcome is only modestly positive (+6% against a base case of $30, a bull case of $45, and a bear case of $13) on an equity with 67% realized volatility. That is a poor risk-adjusted proposition, and near-term catalysts skew negative: the September 1 goodwill test, a late-September Q3 print against a guide already framed ~2.4% below prior consensus, and next-year EPS estimates that fell 7.4% in the last 30 days alone. We upgrade from AVOID to REDUCE. The valuation and the resolved 2026 refinancing narrow the downside relative to three months ago, and the stock is not shortable at 21.1% short interest, but we do not yet see a case for owning it.

## Company Overview

Concentrix provides outsourced customer experience services: contact-center operations across voice, chat, email and social, technical support, sales and retention, back-office processing, trust-and-safety/content moderation, and increasingly software deployed into those workflows. FY2025 revenue was $9,825.8M, generated by approximately 455,000 employees across 74 countries. The company reports as a single operating segment.

Roughly 99% of revenue is recognized as services are performed, and client contracts are generally terminable for convenience on 30 to 90 days' notice. There is no conventional multi-year backlog and no contractual buffer against volume reduction. Revenue by client vertical in FY2025: technology and consumer electronics $2,666.1M (27.1%, −0.3% YoY), retail/travel/e-commerce $2,433.9M (24.8%, +3.0%), communications and media $1,592.4M (16.2%, +4.2%), BFSI $1,536.2M (15.6%, +5.5%), healthcare $725.3M (7.4%, −0.3%), other $871.9M.

Client concentration is low by industry standards: no client exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue and the top five accounted for approximately 19%. That figure has been broadly stable, so vertical-mix shifts should not be read as improving concentration.

The structural feature that dominates the P&L is currency mismatch: 89% of FY2025 revenue came from non-U.S. operations while 54% was priced in U.S. dollars (52% in H1 FY2026). Dollar revenue against peso-, rupee-, euro- and real-denominated cost is hedged explicitly; at May 31, 2026 the company held forwards and options covering PHP 43,620M at a fixed $737.2M and INR 33,240M at $359.7M, both running through May 2028.

The current strategic identity rests on the "iX Suite" of proprietary AI and CX software. Management stated on the Q2 call that iX Suite deal count rose 400% year over year, that it closed nearly 100 iX Suite deals in the quarter, that it will surpass $120M of annual recurring revenue, and that 11% of company revenue is "influenced by" iX Suite deployments with roughly 350bp better margin. These figures come from earnings-call commentary relayed by third parties, are not audited, and "revenue influenced by" has no disclosed definition. The scale check matters more than the growth rate: $120M of ARR against a ~$10B revenue base is approximately 1.2% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (fiscal year ended November 30, 2025).** Revenue $9,825.8M, +2.2%. Gross profit $3,435.0M (35.0% margin, down from 35.9%). SG&A $2,825.5M (28.8% of revenue, improved from 29.7%). Impairment charges of $1,527.7M, of which $1,523.3M was goodwill, drove GAAP operating income to −$918.2M and a net loss of $1,278.9M (−$20.36 diluted EPS). Non-GAAP operating income was $1,253.5M (12.8%), adjusted EBITDA $1,469.3M (15.0%), non-GAAP diluted EPS $11.22. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge reconciles: −$918.2M + $1,527.7M impairment + $434.3M intangible amortization + $97.9M stock compensation + $10.3M step-up depreciation + $101.5M acquisition/integration/restructuring = $1,253.5M. The two largest adjustments are non-cash and Webhelp-acquisition-derived, which is a more defensible construction than most non-GAAP presentations.

Cash flow improved in FY2025 even as GAAP collapsed: operating cash flow $807.0M (from $667.5M), capex $234.5M, free cash flow $572.5M (+34%), adjusted FCF $626.4M. The write-off was an accounting event.

**The impairment mechanics carry forward.** Goodwill is tested annually as of September 1. The FY2025 annual test passed; a sustained decline in the share price then triggered an interim test as of November 1, 2025, which produced the $1,523.3M charge. Methodology is a 50/50 weighting of DCF and a peer-EBITDA-multiple market approach, reconciled to market capitalization, applied to a single reporting unit (the consolidated company). Carrying equity at May 31, 2026 was $2,701.3M against a market capitalization near $1.6B, and remaining goodwill of $3,653.5M is roughly 2.2x market cap. A further write-down at the September 1, 2026 test is plausible and arguably probable, though not mechanical, since the income approach carries half the weight and is not price-dependent. Any such charge would not consume cash, would not affect the adjusted-EBITDA-based covenants, and would not threaten the dividend. It would deepen tangible book value, already at approximately negative $2.70B.

**Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended May 31, 2026) shows operating deterioration independent of the impairment.** Revenue $2,462.5M, +1.9%, with FX contributing $29.7M (+1.3%), leaving organic growth near +0.6%. Gross margin 33.4% versus 35.1%. SG&A rose to 29.6% of revenue from 28.9%. GAAP operating income fell 36% to $95.4M (3.9% margin). Non-GAAP operating income $292.0M (11.9% versus 12.6%); adjusted EBITDA $347.4M (14.1% versus 14.8%); non-GAAP diluted EPS $2.63 versus $2.70. GAAP net income of $55.3M was higher than the prior year's $42.1M only because other income swung materially, including $45.0M of FX gains, versus $21.2M of other expense a year earlier.

Two adjustments improve the read on gross margin. H1 severance of $42.7M was charged to cost of revenue, equal to 86bp of H1 revenue; adjusting for it, H1 gross margin was approximately 34.6% versus 35.6%, a ~100bp underlying decline rather than the reported 190bp. The asymmetry matters: that severance depresses GAAP gross margin but is excluded from non-GAAP operating income, which fell 130bp on the half.

The restructuring is large in absolute terms. Severance was $42.4M in Q2 and $60.2M in H1, with actions affecting approximately 20,000 employees, roughly 4.4% of the workforce in six months. Total acquisition/integration/restructuring expense was $65.5M in Q2 against $16.8M a year earlier; management guided to $175M for FY2026.

**Half-year cash flow deteriorated.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $174.7M versus $237.9M; free cash flow $72.6M versus $131.5M; adjusted FCF $97.7M versus $160.5M (−39%). Management publicized a record *second-quarter* adjusted FCF of $242.3M, which implies Q1 adjusted FCF of approximately −$144.6M. Both statements are true and the shape is consistent with normal Q1 seasonality (annual incentive payouts, working-capital build); FY2025 followed the same pattern, with H1 FCF of $131.5M and H2 of $465.9M. The forward requirement is what matters: the FY2026 adjusted FCF guide of $630–650M requires H2 delivery of $532–552M against $465.9M last year, a 14–18% improvement while revenue is guided roughly flat-to-down and margins compress. In dollars that is $66–86M of incremental H2 cash generation, sourced from restructuring benefits and the roll-off of duplicate costs from moving work offshore. It is plausible, and it is the single most load-bearing assumption in the story.

**Capital structure.** At May 31, 2026: cash $255.6M, total debt $4,600.0M ($650.0M current), total equity $2,701.3M, liquidity $1,504.6M ($1,100M undrawn revolver, $137.5M undrawn securitization, plus cash). The debt stack: $200M of 6.650% notes due August 2026 (residual), $600M 6.500% due 2029, $800M 6.600% due 2028, $550M 6.850% due 2033, term loans of $1,837.5M (the Continued facility maturing December 27, 2026; the New facility and 3-year delayed-draw in September 2028), and $612.5M drawn on a $750M securitization facility terminating March 2028.

The 2026 wall has largely been addressed. In February the company issued $600M of 2029 notes and redeemed $600M of the 2026 notes at a $6.3M extinguishment cost; in March it upsized the securitization facility to $750M and extended termination to 2028. Remaining near-term items are the $200M August 2026 notes and the December 2026 term loan tranche, against $1.5B of available liquidity and management's plan to repay more than $550M of debt in FY2026.

Covenants are a leverage ratio ≤3.75x (4.25x following a qualified acquisition) and interest coverage ≥3.00x. On reported figures: TTM adjusted EBITDA of $1,433.4M (FY2025 $1,469.3M − H1 FY2025 $731.5M + H1 FY2026 $695.6M), net debt of $4,344.4M, giving net leverage of 3.03x and coverage of about 5.0x on TTM interest of $285.3M. Holding net debt constant, a breach at 3.75x requires adjusted EBITDA below $1,158.5M, a ~19% decline from the TTM level. The credit agreement's defined ratio likely permits add-backs we have not read, so as-defined headroom is probably wider. The ~19% cushion is meaningful but not comfort, for a company whose adjusted EBITDA margin has fallen 220bp across six quarters. Interest-rate sensitivity is disclosed at $24.5M of additional annual expense per 100bp on variable-rate debt.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue trajectory is the crux. H1 FY2026 revenue of $4,962.9M (+3.6%, of which FX added 230bp) implies H1 FY2025 of $4,790.4M and therefore H2 FY2025 of $5,035.4M. The FY2026 guidance range of $9.93–10.03B leaves implied H2 FY2026 revenue of $4,967–5,067M, or −1.4% to +0.6%, with a midpoint of −0.4%. Management's own guidance implies the first reported half-year revenue decline since the Webhelp combination closed in September 2023. FX is the swing factor; the guide is framed in constant currency, so a weaker dollar could keep reported H2 revenue marginally positive.

The composition of the shortfall is more informative than its size. Roughly 300bp of headwind is offshore-mix repricing and roughly 100bp is clients withdrawing support from lower-value customer segments in high-cost markets. Against constant-currency guidance of +0.25% to +1.25%, gross underlying activity is growing 4–5%. Two readings are defensible. The benign reading: offshore delivery structurally carries higher margins once transition duplicate costs roll off, so revenue deflation with stable or better gross profit dollars is an acceptable trade at 4.2x EV/EBITDA. The adverse reading: the offshore savings are being passed to clients permanently. The evidence does not yet separate them; the severance-adjusted ~100bp H1 gross margin decline is consistent with either transitional cost overlap or price give-back. One more quarter of gross-margin erosion after restructuring completes favors the adverse interpretation.

Vertical detail is deteriorating at the edges. Healthcare revenue fell 13.9% in Q2 and 9.7% in H1 after being flat in FY2025; technology and consumer electronics, the largest vertical at 27% of revenue, declined 5.8% in Q2. Those are client-vertical declines, distinct from demand for Concentrix's own software.

Consensus reflects the reset and is still moving down. Current-year EPS stands at $11.20 (−4.4% in 30 days, −6.5% in 90 days) and next year at $11.89 (−7.4% in 30 days, −9.1% in 90 days). The bulk of the next-year cut landed in the past 30 days, meaning the revision cycle did not stop with the June 29 guidance event. The company has missed non-GAAP consensus in each of the last four quarters, by 0.6% to 5.0%, which argues for treating the FY2026 FCF and leverage targets as upper-bound outcomes.

The iX Suite is the only genuine growth vector disclosed, and at a targeted $120M of ARR it cannot offset service-revenue deflation on any horizon relevant to the current capital structure. Growth Prospects score: 3.0.

Peer medians in the group provided are a P/E of 9.8 (Genpact 9.8, Maximus 9.3, ExlService 18.2, CSG 35.9, Verra 5.5), a comparison of limited use, since those are GAAP multiples on far less levered balance sheets while CNXC's GAAP earnings are negative on the impairment. The relevant point is that Concentrix trades at roughly a quarter of the peer P/E median on a non-GAAP basis, and the gap is explained almost entirely by leverage and by the market's assessment of terminal revenue-per-interaction risk. We did not obtain peer EV/EBITDA multiples, so we cannot say how much of the 4.2x is company-specific.

Two things must be held simultaneously. On equity multiples the stock is extraordinarily cheap. On enterprise value it is merely cheap. The difference is the $4.34B of net debt, which makes the equity a levered residual claim on EBITDA. Small changes in EBITDA produce very large changes in equity value in both directions. There is also a mechanical tailwind: at ~$500M of annual free cash flow directed to debt reduction, roughly 8 points of enterprise value per year transfers from creditors to the equity stub, which can outrun modest margin erosion. Run the sensitivity: if adjusted EBITDA drifts to $1,330M in FY2027 while net debt falls to ~$3,450M, a constant 4.2x multiple implies equity of $2,136M, or roughly $33 per share.

The reason we do not score valuation above 5.0 despite these figures is that the multiple is a solvency-and-terminal-value discount, not an oversight. Three of the four dimensions of the discount are documented in the filings: contract terminability inside 90 days, client-deployed automation already displacing lower-complexity work, and an EBITDA margin declining for six consecutive quarters. Cheapness plus a disclosed structural erosion mechanism is the classic profile of a value trap, and the past four quarters of misses and continued downward estimate revisions have not yet broken that pattern.

Analyst targets provide the counterweight: low $26, mean $35.25, median $35.00, high $45.00, with 1 strong buy, 2 buys and 1 hold. Barrington cut its target to $30 from $38 while maintaining Outperform. Every published target sits at or above spot. On a stock where estimates are falling, we treat those targets as sentiment context rather than independent confirmation.

**Scenario framework (per-share, ~63M shares):**
- **Base $30 (+17%):** FY2026 adjusted EBITDA ~$1,400M at 4.2x = $5,880M EV, less FYE net debt of ~$3,900M = $1,980M equity.
- **Bull $45 (+75%):** EBITDA stabilizes near $1,450M, net debt falls to ~$3,800M, multiple re-rates to ~4.7x as offshore mix proves margin-accretive and iX Suite ARR compounds.
- **Bear $13 (−49%):** EBITDA compresses to ~$1,250M as offshore savings are passed through and healthcare/tech verticals decline further; multiple derates to ~3.9x on covenant proximity; net debt near $4,000M.

Probability-weighted at 45%/20%/35% gives roughly +6%. On a 67%-volatility equity, that is an inadequate risk premium.

## Competitive Landscape

Concentrix competes against Teleperformance and Foundever among global pure-plays, against IT-services and consulting firms extending into CX (Accenture, TCS, Infosys BPM, Genpact, Cognizant), against offshore-native regional providers, and against its clients' own in-house operations and self-service automation. The 10-K identifies the live threat as displacement of lower-complexity work by tools that *clients* deploy. That is disintermediation rather than share loss to a named rival, and it is why a business generating over $500M of free cash flow trades at 2.3x non-GAAP earnings.

The industry dynamic is a three-way compression of revenue per interaction: automation removes the simplest high-volume tickets from the human queue; surviving work migrates from higher-priced onshore to lower-priced offshore seats (quantified by the company at nearly 300bp annually); and clients under their own cost pressure buy less service for lower-value customer segments (~100bp). Net of that, Concentrix still guides to marginally positive constant-currency growth, which is evidence that share and wallet are holding.

Scale is a real asset in one specific sense: 455,000 employees across 74 countries with multilingual delivery is not easily replicated, and it is what wins vendor-consolidation mandates. The proxy's five-year TSR series provides useful calibration: the selected peer index stood at 25.8 versus Concentrix at 36.9 (base 100, December 2020). Concentrix has outperformed its own peer group while losing roughly 63% of its value, which supports the view that the derating is sector-wide rather than idiosyncratic execution failure. Outperforming a collapsing peer group does not establish that the terminal economics are sound.

Trajectory, which is what this dimension measures, is deteriorating: gross margin down ~100bp underlying, pricing per unit falling by disclosure, two of the largest verticals declining, and the offsetting software business at ~1.2% of revenue. Score: 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Pricing deflation becoming permanent.** The single most important variable. If gross margin declines again in Q3 after $100M of H1 restructuring, the "transitional duplicate cost" explanation fails and the market will re-underwrite terminal margins on a lower base.

**Leverage against a compressing EBITDA base.** $4.60B gross debt at 6.5–6.9% on the bond stack, ~3.0x net leverage against a 3.75x covenant, and a ~19% EBITDA cushion on reported figures. The cushion is the binding constraint on how much operating deterioration the equity can absorb.

**Contract terminability.** 30–90 day termination for convenience with ~99% of revenue recognized as delivered. Combined with disclosed client behavior of cutting support for certain segments, downside would not be gradual.

**FY2026 cash-flow guidance.** The $630–650M adjusted FCF target requires $532–552M in H2 versus $465.9M last year. Deleveraging, the sub-2.6x leverage target and the ~$92M annual dividend all depend on it. Our own math suggests the 2.6x target requires the top of the FCF range: at ~$1,450M FY2026 EBITDA, 2.6x implies ~$3,770M of net debt, or ~$570M of H2 net-debt reduction against $532–552M of H2 FCF less dividends.

**Goodwill and negative tangible equity.** $3,653.5M of goodwill at 2.2x market cap; carrying equity exceeds market cap by roughly $1.05B; September 1 annual test pending. Non-cash, non-covenant, but it will produce another headline GAAP loss and further negative tangible book.

**Governance and register changes.** Groupe Bruxelles Lambert sold 6,000,000 shares at $22.25 on April 29, 2026, cutting a ~14.2% strategic stake to 2,773,667 shares (~4.6% of shares outstanding). Block sales are conventionally priced at a discount and diversified holding companies rotate capital for their own reasons, so we draw no inference about information advantage. What is established is the loss of the anchor holder that arrived via Webhelp, a residual overhang, and coincident board turnover (two new directors in March 2026, Item 5.02 filings in March, May and July). Separately, CEO total compensation rose from $11.64M to $13.35M in a year with a $1.28B net loss, and incentive metrics are adjusted EBITDA, non-GAAP operating income and revenue, with no leverage, return-on-capital or cash-flow component in a company defined by $4.6B of debt. Offsetting this: the SEC-mandated "compensation actually paid" measure ran $2.2–2.8M in FY2022–FY2024 against $8.6–11.6M granted, so the equity component has in fact been heavily devalued.

**Unverified items.** FY2026 guidance figures, the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS range, and all iX Suite metrics come from earnings-call commentary relayed by third parties rather than filings; a July 7 plaintiffs'-firm investigation announcement is a solicitation, not a filed complaint, and no securities litigation is confirmed. Our 2.3x multiple uses TTM non-GAAP EPS of $10.97, not guided FY2026 EPS; if FY2026 non-GAAP EPS is guided materially below that level, the forward multiple is higher.

**Not shortable.** Short interest is 21.1% of float at 4.0 days to cover, with the stock 56% below its 52-week high and 34% above its June 30 intraday low of $19.12. The bearish thesis is well populated and the squeeze mechanics are unfavorable, which is part of why we upgrade from AVOID rather than maintain it.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.3% against 66.8% one-year realized, rich by 5.4 points, though only in the 15th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is flat (72.3% near, 73.5% at six months), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single event. Skew is call-heavy (−4.5% at the front tenor), put/call volume is 0.35 and put/call open interest is 0.20. The most informative change is the shift in open-interest ratio from 3.26 on June 30 to 0.20 now, alongside a 14.8% price recovery from $22.41: the heavy protective/bearish structure that existed around the guidance cut has rolled off and positioning is now tilted toward calls. For a deep-value contrarian case, that is unhelpful, because the recovery trade is already populated, and the September 18 tenor prices a ±20.3% move, spanning the Q3 print. Options positioning corroborates neither the bull nor the bear thesis on fundamentals; it does indicate that the market is charging a full price for near-term optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($45, +75%).** Demand is stable and underlying activity is growing 4–5%; the revenue headwind is repricing, and offshore delivery carries structurally higher margins once duplicate transition costs roll off. Restructuring of ~20,000 positions and $175M of FY2026 expense lands on schedule, H2 adjusted FCF delivers $532–552M, net debt falls toward $3.8B and net leverage under 2.6x. Adjusted EBITDA stabilizes near $1,450M and the multiple re-rates modestly to ~4.7x as investors recognize that a levered, deleveraging cash generator at 8.6% FCF/EV is mispriced. iX Suite passes $120M ARR and gives the market a reason to underwrite a terminal margin above 14%. Both the CEO (three open-market purchases in FY2026, including 1,000 shares at $21.25 on July 8, eight days after the collapse) and the CFO (2,500 shares at $27.95 in April) have bought. At that point the equity roughly doubles from a $1.6B base on modest enterprise-value change.

**Bear case ($13, −49%).** The offshore savings are passed through to clients rather than retained, and gross margin declines again in Q3 after restructuring. Healthcare (−13.9% in Q2) and technology (−5.8%) continue contracting; contracts terminable in 90 days convert client cost programs into step-function volume losses. Adjusted EBITDA drifts to $1,250M, taking net leverage toward 3.3x on reported figures and putting the ~19% covenant cushion in view. The H2 FCF inflection under-delivers, the sub-2.6x target slips, and the ~$92M dividend becomes a candidate for reallocation. The multiple compresses to ~3.9x on solvency concern and the equity halves. In that path, a second goodwill impairment, cosmetically irrelevant but reputationally corrosive, arrives alongside a fifth consecutive earnings miss.

The bear case does not require a collapse. It requires the current rate of margin decline to continue for another year, which is the trend that four consecutive quarters and six consecutive quarters of margin data already describe.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $30 (+17%), bull $45 (+75%), bear $13 (−49%); probability-weighted expected return of roughly +6% against 67% realized volatility.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should use strength toward $28–30 to reduce exposure to a residual, non-core position sized for the option-like payoff rather than the yield. We would not hold this for the 5.5% dividend alone: the dividend costs ~$92M annually against a deleveraging plan that already requires the top of the FCF guide, and it is the most obvious source of funds if H2 cash generation misses. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (monitor at the Q3 print, expected late September 2026):**
1. Q3 gross margin flat or up sequentially versus Q2's 33.4% *after* the ~$45M of guided Q3 restructuring, which would validate the transitional-cost explanation over permanent price give-back.
2. Q3 adjusted free cash flow tracking to the FY2026 $630–650M guide (i.e., Q3 adjusted FCF above ~$230M) with net debt below $4.1B.
3. FY2026 constant-currency revenue guidance reaffirmed or raised, with the offshore headwind quantified at no more than 300bp, plus stabilization or upward revision in FY2027 consensus EPS from $11.89.
4. Resumption of share repurchases at prices near current levels, the clearest signal management believes the cash guide and views the equity as the highest-return use of capital.

**Downgrade triggers back to AVOID:**
1. A further widening of the offshore or client-segment revenue headwind, or FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $9.93B.
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin below 13.5% in Q3, which would put TTM EBITDA on a path toward the ~$1,160M covenant threshold and take reported net leverage above 3.3x.
3. Any dividend reduction, or failure to retire the $200M August 2026 notes and the December 2026 term loan tranche from liquidity and internal cash flow.
4. A fifth consecutive non-GAAP EPS miss combined with continued downward FY2027 revisions.

**Reassessment timeline.** Two near-term events: the September 1, 2026 goodwill test (expect a further non-cash write-down; do not treat it as economic) and the Q3 FY2026 print in late September, which is the first clean read on whether restructuring converts to margin. We would revisit constructively at $19–21, where the base case implies 45%+ upside and the bear case is largely realized.

## Conclusion

Concentrix is a genuinely cheap, genuinely impaired equity. The cash economics are intact: $514M of trailing free cash flow, $1.5B of liquidity, a refinanced 2026 maturity profile, ~19% of reported EBITDA cushion to the leverage covenant, and open-market buying by both the CEO and CFO including after the June collapse. Against that, the business is being repriced downward by its own clients at nearly 400bp per year, management's guidance implies the first post-Webhelp half-year revenue decline, adjusted EBITDA margin has fallen 220bp across six quarters, consensus has been cut 9.1% for next year with most of the reduction in the last 30 days, and the company has missed in each of the last four quarters. With $4.34B of net debt behind a $1.6B equity stub, those two sets of facts do not offset; the leverage multiplies the downside.

We upgrade from AVOID to REDUCE. The price has fallen 44% over twelve months and the refinancing risk that most concerned us in the spring has been addressed, which narrows the left tail; and at 21.1% short interest with call-heavy options positioning, this is not a stock to be short. But a +6% probability-weighted return with a 49% bear case, on an equity where the next two catalysts (goodwill test, Q3 print) skew negative, is not an argument for owning it. The decisive evidence is one line in the Q3 release: sequential gross margin. If it holds after $100M of first-half severance, the deflation-with-margin-accretion case becomes investable and we will move quickly. Until then, trim into strength.
