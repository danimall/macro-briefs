**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valvoline is a pure-play automotive preventive-maintenance retailer, the scaled leader in fast oil changes, operating 2,409 stores across the VIOC, Great Canadian Oil Change, and newly acquired Oil Changers banners as of March 31, 2026. The business has compounded same-store sales for 19 consecutive years, a consistency record few retailers can match, and the most recent quarter accelerated: Q2 FY2026 revenue rose 25% to $503.8M, system same-store sales grew 8.2%, adjusted EBITDA rose 28% to $133.6M, and adjusted EPS of $0.41 beat the $0.34 consensus by 18%. Management raised full-year guidance on same-store sales, EBITDA, and EPS, and JP Morgan upgraded the stock from Underweight to Neutral on May 29.

The fundamental story is genuinely improving, and the trajectory in our scoring reflects that. The improvement is now substantially recognized in the price. The stock has rallied from a low-$30s trough in May to $37.75, recovering most of the ground lost during the late-2025 derating, and now trades within roughly 11% of the $42.2 consensus mean target. On a forward basis the multiple is approximately 21x current fiscal-year EPS of $1.75, a premium to where it sat at the May lows and a premium to the automotive-retail peer set, justified partly by superior growth and unit economics but leaving thinner asymmetry than existed six weeks ago. Layered on top are three concrete overhangs: post-Breeze leverage that lifted total debt to $1.66B and paused buybacks, an internal-controls material weakness that remains open, and the structural dependence on Aramco/VGO for substantially all lubricant supply.

We rate VVV a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The franchise quality and insider-buying cluster argue against avoiding the name; the valuation and the leverage/controls/supply cluster argue against chasing it at $37.75. The cleanest entry already happened in the low $30s, where three insiders, including the new CFO, were buying.

## Company Overview

Following the March 2023 sale of its Global Products lubricant-manufacturing business to Aramco for a $1.57B pre-tax gain, Valvoline is a focused retail-services company. Its model is the roughly 15-minute "stay-in-your-car" oil change, supplemented by battery, bulb, wiper, tire-rotation, and OEM-recommended maintenance services. Of the 2,409-store system at quarter-end, 1,210 are company-operated and 1,199 are franchised, with another 240-plus Express Care locations. Roughly half the footprint is franchised, producing an asset-light royalty and product-supply revenue stream alongside the higher-capital company-operated base.

A structural feature investors must understand: the Valvoline-branded lubricant products are now owned and manufactured by Valvoline Global Operations, an Aramco subsidiary. VVV owns the Valvoline brand for retail services (ex-China/MENA) and purchases substantially all of its lubricants from VGO under a long-term supply agreement. The two are separate companies sharing a brand. This is the single most distinctive risk in the equity, discussed below.

The competitive moat rests on brand (the first trademarked U.S. motor oil, established 1866), convenience and speed, the proprietary SuperPro service system, data-driven marketing, and the 19-year same-store-sales streak. The market, U.S. "do-it-for-me" oil changes, is large and fragmented, favoring a scaled consolidator with three growth levers: franchise expansion, opportunistic M&A, and new-build.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (year ended September 30):** Revenue of $1,710M grew 5.6% (from $1,619M in FY24 and $1,444M in FY23). Operating income was $390M at a 22.8% margin, with a 38.5% gross margin. Net income from continuing operations was $214.8M, essentially flat against FY24's $214.5M. Adjusted EBITDA rose 5.5% to $466.8M, holding a 27.3% margin. Same-store sales grew 6.1% and the company added 170 net new stores, 8.5% unit growth.

The headline free-cash-flow figure of $47.9M (a 2.8% margin) looks alarming in isolation but is misleading. Of $259M in capex, roughly $193M was discretionary growth capex. FCF excluding growth capex was approximately $241M, a more accurate gauge of underlying cash generation. This is a company reinvesting aggressively at high returns on invested capital, not one starved of cash. Operating cash flow was $307M.

**Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended March 31):** The Breeze acquisition and organic acceleration combined to lift revenue. Revenue mix was $368M oil changes, $111M non-oil-change services, and $25M franchise fees, with non-oil-change penetration a deliberate lever. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.35.

Six-month GAAP results are distorted: YTD operating income fell to $104M from $211M, and YTD GAAP EPS dropped to $0.10. Almost all of this is the $57.9M pre-tax loss on the FTC-mandated divestiture of 45 stores plus transaction costs, compounded by nondeductible goodwill that drove a $20M unfavorable tax effect and a 75.5% YTD effective rate. On an adjusted basis, YTD EBITDA rose 21% to $251M. The operational story is accelerating; the GAAP earnings noise is one-time.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt jumped from $1.07B to $1.66B after the $740M Term Loan B (SOFR+2.0%, 7-year, ~5.67% effective) funding Breeze. Goodwill and intangibles nearly doubled to $1.28B, with Breeze adding $524M of goodwill. Book equity is thin at $353M. The leverage target was reset and tightened from "2.5–3.5x adjusted EBITDA net leverage" to "1.5–2.5x net debt/adjusted EBITDA," and buybacks have been paused since Q2 FY25 to prioritize de-levering, with $325M remaining on the $400M authorization. Net debt of roughly $1.57B against run-rate adjusted EBITDA in the high-$400M to ~$500M range places leverage near 3x, above the new target band, the reason management is prioritizing debt paydown over capital return.

The Altman Z-score of 3.08 sits in the safe zone, and the 0.7 current ratio is normal for a low-inventory, fast-turn retail model rather than a distress signal.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest part of the thesis. The 19-year same-store-sales streak demonstrates durable pricing and traffic power, and Q2's 8.2% comp marked an acceleration from FY25's 6.1%. The three-pronged unit-growth engine drove roughly 50% system growth over five years (+718 net stores), and Breeze adds 159 net stores (204 acquired minus 45 divested) plus valuable California density. Non-oil-change services and fleet/commercial offerings are deliberately under-penetrated levers, and the NuBrakes mobile-service acquisition extends the addressable footprint.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in delivery: three beats in the last four quarters, including the 18.1% Q2 beat. Management's full-year guidance raise on same-store sales, EBITDA, and EPS is a confirming signal.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and is the reason I do not treat this as a clean inflection BUY. Current-year EPS estimates rose 2.9% over 90 days and next-year rose 3.3% — positive, but well short of the velocity that would flag a true earnings inflection. The near-term quarters are drifting the other way: the current quarter is down 0.3% over 30 days and the next quarter down 1.8% over 90 days. The longer-dated optimism reflects unit growth and Breeze contribution; the near-term softness reflects rising base-oil costs (management flagged a $0.50–0.60 per-oil-change sensitivity per $1/gallon move and warned of higher Q3 costs) and rising interest expense ($10–13M of incremental drag). The growth thesis is intact and durable, but it is steady high-single-digit organic compounding plus M&A, not an acceleration the market is failing to see.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.75 the trailing P/E of approximately 50x is meaningless because trailing earnings are depressed by the Breeze divestiture loss. The relevant metric is forward: on consensus current-year EPS of $1.75 and next-year EPS of $2.06, the stock trades at roughly 21.6x and 18.3x respectively. On EV/EBITDA, ~$6.4B enterprise value against run-rate adjusted EBITDA puts the stock around 13x.

Against the automotive-retail peer set, VVV screens expensive on headline multiples: peer median P/E is 12.5x (GPI 12.5, ABG 7.1, AN 10.6), with AAP at 54.1x and PHIN at 22.9x. But those comps are dealership and parts-distribution models with very different growth, margin, and capital profiles. VVV's 27%+ EBITDA margin, high-single-digit organic comps, and ROIC-accretive unit growth justify a substantial premium to a low-growth dealer trading at 7–12x. The honest read is that VVV is a high-quality business priced as one: roughly 18–22x forward earnings for a mid-teens earnings grower (PEG near 1.3–1.5). That is fair, not cheap.

The asymmetry argument has weakened materially since the May lows. At the low-$30s, where insiders were buying, the stock offered a clear margin of safety against a $42.2 consensus mean. At $37.75 the stock sits within ~11% of that mean and ~27% below the high target of $48. Consensus targets on a fairly valued steady compounder are not reliable floors the way they are on a cheap inflection name; here the price already reflects most of the recovery. I score valuation a 5.0: the multiple is defensible but offers no identifiable mispricing to reward. A low-13x EV/EBITDA entry exists in the low $30s; it does not exist here.

## Competitive Landscape

Valvoline's competitive position is improving, which supports a 7.0. The 19-year comp streak, scaled brand, proprietary service systems, and consolidation runway in a fragmented market are durable advantages. Breeze adds California density and accelerates the M&A flywheel. The refranchising tilt (10 company stores sold to a franchisee in Q1 FY26 for a $14.8M gain) shifts the mix toward higher-return, asset-light royalty income.

The principal competitive dynamic is Take 5 (Driven Brands), the most aggressive growth rival, against which Valvoline differentiates on the trusted-brand, "see-the-service" experience. Industry tailwinds are real: an aging vehicle parc (~12+ years average age), rising miles driven, and increasing vehicle complexity. The long-duration structural headwind is EV adoption, which reduces oil-change frequency, compounded by lengthening OEM drain intervals that reduce visit frequency even among internal-combustion vehicles. Management frames EVs as an opportunity (coolants, fluids, greases) and is hedging via non-oil-change penetration and fleet, but this is the existential industry question and caps how high the competitive-position score can run.

## Risk Assessment

Several concrete, idiosyncratic risks are not fully priced and justify a risk score of 5.0:

1. **Single-source supply dependence on VGO/Aramco.** VVV buys substantially all of its lubricants from the company it spun off. Any interruption, quality issue, or pricing dispute would hit the core service directly. This is the most structurally unique risk in the equity, and it has no obvious near-term mitigant.

2. **Open internal-controls material weakness.** Tied to the January 2024 ERP implementation, ITGC deficiencies were remediated in FY25, but business-process control deficiencies remained a material weakness as of March 31, 2026, with remediation expected to conclude in FY26. Breeze controls are also excluded from assessment for one year. No misstatements have resulted, but a multi-year open material weakness is a governance overhang and a derating risk if remediation slips.

3. **Leverage and integration.** Net leverage sits above the new 1.5–2.5x target band, buybacks are paused, and Breeze is now ~21% of total assets. Integrating 159 acquired stores under a new brand carries execution risk, and rising interest expense already pressures GAAP EPS.

4. **Base-oil/commodity inflation.** Management flagged rising Q3 costs and a $0.50–0.60 per-oil-change sensitivity per $1/gallon base-oil move, partly offset by pricing power but a live margin pressure feeding the negative near-term revisions.

5. **Franchisee dependence and off-balance-sheet exposure.** With 53% of the system franchised, VVV has guaranteed up to $87M of assigned lease obligations and $12.6M of franchisee debt.

6. **Underfunded pension** (~$71M) and mark-to-market remeasurement volatility (a $26.6M loss in FY25).

### Options Market Signal

The options market has shifted decisively away from fear since the May lows. ATM implied volatility collapsed from 143.9% to 100.1% (down 43.8 points) as the price rose 17.5%, and the put/call open-interest ratio fell from a heavily-hedged 3.31 to 0.20. The pre-earnings hedging that dominated in mid-May has unwound, consistent with the post-beat relief rally. Short interest remains elevated at 14.1% of float (6.4 days to cover), so bearish institutional positioning persists despite the rally, a source of squeeze potential on continued beats, but also a signal that informed money has not capitulated on the leverage/supply concerns. IV has normalized and puts have unwound while short interest remains high, pointing to a stock that has repriced the good news while the structural skeptics remain in place.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($45–48):** Organic comps hold at 7–8%, Breeze integrates cleanly and adds California density, non-oil-change and fleet penetration lifts mix, the ERP material weakness is remediated in FY26 (removing a governance overhang and enabling a re-rate), de-levering completes and buybacks resume on the $325M remaining authorization, and the stock re-rates toward the $48 high target on ~$2.06+ EPS. The insider cluster — CFO Willis buying 10,000 shares at $31.80, plus two directors the same week — is a credible vote of confidence at prices ~15% below today's.

**Bear case ($30–32):** Base-oil inflation compresses margins faster than pricing offsets, near-term estimate erosion continues, Breeze integration disappoints or the material weakness lingers, leverage and rising interest expense weigh on GAAP earnings, and the still-elevated short base presses the stock back toward its trough. EV adoption and lengthening drain intervals provide a slow-bleed structural backdrop. A return to the low-$30s low-13x EV/EBITDA level is plausible on any execution stumble.

**Base case (~$40–42):** Steady high-single-digit comps and unit growth deliver ~$2.00+ EPS, the stock holds an 18–21x forward multiple, and it converges toward the $42.2 consensus mean over twelve months, roughly 12% upside from $37.75, before any buyback resumption.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q3 FY2026 earnings (early August), where base-oil cost pressure and Breeze integration will be visible, followed by the FY26 year-end report and the status of material-weakness remediation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$30s (sub-$33), restoring the asymmetry that existed at the May insider-buying levels and compressing the forward multiple toward ~16–17x.
- ERP material weakness formally remediated and buybacks resumed, removing the two clearest overhangs and supporting a re-rate.
- Same-store sales sustaining 8%+ with positive near-term estimate revisions (current/next-quarter revisions turning positive rather than the current -0.3%/-1.8% drift).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store sales decelerating below ~4% or a guidance cut.
- Material-weakness remediation slipping beyond FY26 or any restatement.
- Net leverage rising rather than falling, or a supply/pricing dispute with VGO/Aramco.
- Gross margin compressing materially on base-oil costs without pricing offset.

## Conclusion

Valvoline is a high-quality, share-gaining quick-lube consolidator with a remarkable comp streak, accelerating Q2 results, and a credible insider-buying signal. The trajectory has been improving, and the most recent data confirm rather than reverse that trend. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: at $37.75 the stock trades at roughly 21x forward earnings and ~13x EV/EBITDA — fair value for a mid-teens grower, within ~11% of the $42.2 consensus mean — with no identifiable mispricing to reward, while carrying three concrete overhangs (post-Breeze leverage above the target band with buybacks paused, an open internal-controls material weakness, and substantially-all-supply dependence on Aramco/VGO). The asymmetry that existed in the low $30s, where insiders were buying, has largely closed after a 17.5% rally. Owners should hold for the durable compounding; new capital is better deployed on a pullback toward the low $30s or on remediation of the material weakness and resumption of buybacks. The 12-month thesis is constructive but the current entry does not clear the bar for a BUY in a sector where company-specific outperformance has been difficult to achieve.
