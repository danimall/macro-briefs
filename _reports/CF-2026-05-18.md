## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, currently benefiting from a massive earnings inflection driven by the Strait of Hormuz closure, which has removed substantial Middle Eastern and North African nitrogen supply from global markets. The stock has appreciated 45% over 12 months from ~$86 to $125 and trades at approximately 7.1x consensus FY2026 EPS of $17.55 — cheap versus the peer median of 24.3x and inexpensive relative to the current earnings trajectory.

The fundamental case is strong: CF sits far below the marginal cost producer on the global nitrogen cost curve, generates $1.8B in annual FCF, has no debt maturities until 2034, and has a $2B buyback authorization in place. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $983M included a $170M litigation settlement gain; excluding this one-time item, underlying quarterly EBITDA of approximately $813M annualizes to roughly $3.25B, still well above the $2.9B FY2025 level and reflecting genuine pricing strength from the Hormuz disruption. The central challenge is that the geopolitical premium embedded in the stock creates a binary risk profile — a ceasefire, Hormuz reopening, or diplomatic resolution could trigger a rapid 15-25% drawdown. With the stock trading above consensus analyst price targets (estimated at approximately $112–$121), the asymmetric upside required for a BUY recommendation is absent at current levels.

## Company Overview

CF Industries operates nine nitrogen manufacturing complexes — six in the U.S., two in Canada, and one in the UK. Its flagship Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex is the world's largest ammonia production facility, representing approximately 40% of total capacity. The company converts natural gas and atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia via the Haber-Bosch process, then upgrades it into granular urea (47% gross margin), UAN solution (largest volume), ammonium nitrate, and other products.

CF holds a 50% interest in PLNL (Trinidad JV) and a 40% stake in the Blue Point joint venture with JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%), a greenfield low-carbon ammonia plant in development in Louisiana targeting 2029 production. The company completed its Donaldsonville CCS (carbon capture and sequestration) unit in July 2025, enabling ~1.9M tons/year of low-carbon ammonia capacity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**
FY2025 revenue of $7.1B represented a 19% YoY recovery driven entirely by pricing ($372/ton average, +19%), with volumes essentially flat (+1%). Gross margin expanded from 34.6% to 38.5% despite a 38% increase in natural gas costs ($2.40 → $3.31/MMBtu), demonstrating the company's pricing power when global supply tightens.

Q1 2026 results accelerated materially — reported adjusted EBITDA of $983M includes a $170M litigation settlement gain. Excluding this non-recurring item, underlying quarterly EBITDA was approximately $813M, implying an annualized run-rate of roughly $3.25B versus $2.9B for full-year 2025. This reflects meaningful pricing improvement from the Hormuz disruption.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**
- Operating cash flow: $2.75B (FY2025)
- Free cash flow: $1.79B (25.4% FCF margin); TTM FCF through Mar. 31, 2026 was $1.65B reflecting higher capex and Blue Point-related spending
- Cash on hand: $2.04B (as of Mar. 31, 2026, including $254M related to Blue Point)
- Total debt: $3.25B (all unsecured, maturities 2034-2044)
- Interest coverage: >12x
- Undrawn revolver: $750M
- Current ratio: approximately 3.5x

The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.6x based on $3.25B in debt against approximately $5.3B in stockholders' equity as of Q1 2026. With no maturities until 2034, fixed-rate debt, and FCF covering interest obligations more than 12 times, this is comfortable leverage for a commodity producer.

**Capital Allocation:**
CF paid $1.37B for share repurchases in 2025 and retired 17.1M shares (including previously repurchased stock) — approximately a 10% reduction in diluted share count. Since 2022, share count has fallen 20% (204M → 162M). A $2B buyback authorization runs through 2029, though Q1 2026 repurchases slowed significantly to approximately 155,000 shares ($15M), compared to 5.4M shares ($434M) in Q1 2025, suggesting management is exercising price discipline at elevated levels. Dividends of $2.00/share ($326M) bring total shareholder returns to ~$1.7B in FY2025 on $1.8B FCF.

2026 capex guidance is elevated at $1.3B ($550M maintenance/existing + $600M Blue Point + $150M infrastructure), temporarily absorbing FCF but creating future capacity.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $125 with consensus FY2026 EPS of $17.55, CF trades at roughly 7.1x forward earnings — dramatically below the Materials peer median of 24.3x. Even on a more conservative normalized earnings assumption ($11-12 EPS, reflecting partial geopolitical premium fade), CF trades at ~10-11x, still well below peers.

The key valuation question is which earnings level to normalize against:
- **Peak-disruption (current annualized):** ~$17-18 EPS → ~7x forward P/E
- **Moderate disruption persists:** ~$13-14 EPS → 9-10x
- **Geopolitical premium fully fades:** ~$8-9 EPS → 14x (still below peer median)
- **Trough (2024 base):** ~$6.75 EPS → 18.5x

Even in the bearish scenario where the geopolitical premium fully reverses, CF's trough valuation multiple remains below peers because of its structural cost advantage.

However, the issue for a BUY recommendation at current price is the asymmetry profile: the stock already trades above consensus analyst price targets (approximately $112–$121), while downside on a ceasefire/resolution could reach $100-105 (-18-20%) rapidly, as demonstrated by the April 2026 ceasefire rumor selloff. The risk/reward is approximately balanced rather than asymmetric.

**FCF Yield:** Based on FY2025 FCF of $1.79B, the yield is approximately 9.3% on ~$19.2B market cap. However, TTM FCF through Q1 2026 declined to $1.65B due to rising capex (particularly Blue Point), and 2026 capex guidance of $1.3B suggests FCF may remain under pressure even with elevated pricing.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive moat is structural and widening:

1. **Cost curve position:** North American Henry Hub gas (~$3.50/MMBtu in 2025) versus European TTF (~$12-18) and Asian LNG ($14-20) gives CF a structural per-ton cost advantage of $100-200+ versus marginal producers. The Hormuz closure has amplified this advantage by pushing international gas prices higher while North American supply remains relatively insulated.

2. **Scale:** Largest ammonia producer globally. Donaldsonville complex alone produces more than many competitors' entire networks.

3. **Distribution infrastructure:** North American pipeline and terminal network unmatched by competitors for domestic nitrogen delivery.

4. **Low-carbon first-mover:** Donaldsonville CCS operational (July 2025), Blue Point in development moving toward construction. JERA/Mitsui partnership validates Japanese demand for low-carbon ammonia in power generation. Japan's Ministry certified both as low-carbon hydrogen suppliers.

5. **EU Anti-dumping duties:** Extended through 2031 on Russian and Trinidadian UAN, protecting North American pricing.

CF's competitive position is improving as global supply disruptions concentrate pricing power among low-cost producers with domestic gas access.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Geopolitical Resolution Risk (HIGH):** The single largest risk. A Hormuz reopening would likely trigger a 15-25% stock decline within days, as demonstrated by prior ceasefire rumor selloffs. At $125, the stock prices in sustained disruption.

2. **Natural Gas Cost Escalation:** Henry Hub spiked to $7.72/MMBtu in January 2026 before declining to $3.62 in February and $3.04 in March. CF's Q1 2026 average daily Henry Hub reference price was $4.90/MMBtu, with its actual cost of natural gas used for production at $4.57/MMBtu. While CF benefits more than competitors when international prices rise faster than domestic costs, elevated or volatile North American gas compresses absolute margins versus the $3.31/MMBtu 2025 base.

3. **Blue Point Execution Risk:** $3.7B project ($1.5B CF share over 4 years) using ATR technology not widely deployed at this scale for ammonia. Louisiana moratorium on new Class VI well applications could delay carbon sequestration. Leadership transition during most complex capital program in CF's history.

4. **Yazoo City Production Shortfall:** 6% capacity reduction through at least Q4 2026. While pricing offsets volumes, this represents lost earnings potential.

5. **Cyclicality:** Post-peak risk if new global capacity additions (various low-carbon projects announced) coincide with demand normalization.

6. **Leadership Transition:** New CEO Christopher Bohn inherits the Blue Point mega-project and elevated expectations. W. Anthony Will's departure removes continuity during a critical execution period.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is fundamentally well-positioned: lowest-cost producer in the world's most structurally advantaged gas market, generating massive FCF, reducing share count, and building first-mover optionality in low-carbon ammonia. The Q1 2026 earnings beat confirms a genuine earnings inflection driven by global supply disruption.

At $125, the stock already reflects the geopolitical premium, trading above consensus analyst price targets. The near-term probability of diplomatic resolution, combined with the demonstrated selloff velocity on ceasefire rumors, creates a risk profile where the expected value of initiating a new position today is approximately neutral.

The constructive path is to maintain exposure if already owned (the structural thesis strengthens with each week the disruption persists) and to establish new positions on pullbacks to $105-110 where the risk/reward becomes genuinely asymmetric. The company is worth owning directionally, but entry price determines the margin of safety against the primary geopolitical risk.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to Q1 EBITDA annualization (excluding $170M litigation gain reduces run-rate from ~$3.9B to ~$3.25B), buyback pace slowdown in Q1 2026, and stock trading above rather than near/below analyst consensus targets collectively reduce the near-term upside case supporting the entry-on-strength component of the thesis.
