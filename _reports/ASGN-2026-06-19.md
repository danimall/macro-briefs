**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ASGN Incorporated, which legally rebranded to Everforth, Inc. (NYSE: EFOR) effective April 24, 2026, is an IT staffing and consulting provider serving commercial Fortune 1000/mid-market clients (70% of revenue) and federal government clients (30%). The investment case has deteriorated materially. The stock fell roughly 52% in a single session on April 23, 2026 following Q1 results released April 22, and is down 56.7% over the trailing twelve months, trading at $21.02 against a 52-week high of $60.75. This was a fundamental repricing: Q1 2026 net income collapsed to $5.5M ($0.13 EPS) from $20.9M ($0.48) a year earlier, a 74% decline, on compressing gross margins and a soft Q2 guide.

The structural picture is worse than the rebrand suggests. Revenue has declined three consecutive years ($4.58B in 2022 to $3.98B in 2025), operating margin has compressed from 8.9% to 5.8%, and net income is down roughly 58% since 2022. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut 27.8% (current year) and 21.0% (next year) over the past 90 days, the opposite of the rising-estimate setup that would justify owning a beaten-down cyclical. The federal segment faces a named, quantified DOGE-driven contraction with book-to-bill at 0.7 (TTM), a leading indicator of further declines. Management is funding a $290M acquisition on the revolver (cutting availability from $455M to $160M) and continuing buybacks ($39M in Q1 at $47.69, now worth $21) into a downturn, raising leverage to $1.46B.

The stock screens cheap at roughly 5.8x current-year EPS and ~6.6x annualized Q1 adjusted EBITDA, and a third-party note has framed it as "deep value." This is the textbook profile of a value trap: a compressed multiple applied to a declining earnings base with no identified positive catalyst, while concrete downside catalysts (further federal erosion, potential goodwill impairment, tightening covenant headroom) remain in front of the company. We rate ASGN/EFOR **AVOID**. It is not an active short; the stock has already de-rated 57% and sits near 52-week lows, leaving limited residual downside relative to squeeze risk.

## Company Overview

ASGN/Everforth operates two segments. The **Commercial segment** ($2.79B, ~70% of 2025 revenue) provides IT consulting and staffing to Fortune 1000 and mid-market clients across six solution areas (Cloud/Infrastructure, Data & AI, Software Development/Engineering, Customer Experience, Cybersecurity, Enterprise Platforms) under the Apex Systems, Creative Circle, CyberCoders, GlideFast, TopBloc, and Quinnox brands. The **Federal Government segment** ($1.19B, ~30%) delivers all-consulting work in defense, intelligence, national security, and federal civilian IT modernization, primarily through the ECS brand, with a $2.9B contract backlog and roughly 1,000 cybersecurity consultants (500 cleared) plus ~900 cleared AI/data professionals.

The strategic thesis is a pivot from low-margin staffing toward higher-margin IT consulting. In 2025, consulting reached 62% of consolidated revenue (up 5.1% YoY) while staffing ("Assignment") revenue fell 13.8%. Management cites a ~$680B total addressable market. The November 2025 rebrand to Everforth unifies seven brands under a single digital-engineering identity, with transition completed in H1 2026. Recent M&A (TopBloc, $304M, 2025; Quinnox, $290M cash, closed March 2026) is consistent with shifting toward platform and engineering consulting, with Quinnox adding offshore/India delivery.

A note on the reported market-cap figure: at $21.02 and roughly 41M shares outstanding, market capitalization is approximately $860M, broadly consistent with the data feed's $834M. Earlier confusion arose from anchoring on the Q1 buyback price of $47.69; the company has been repriced by more than half since.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year top-line and margin erosion.** Revenue and profitability have declined consistently:

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Operating Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $4.58B | $268.1M | 8.9% |
| 2023 | $4.45B | $219.3M | 8.2% |
| 2024 | $4.10B | $175.2M | 7.4% |
| 2025 | $3.98B | $113.5M | 5.8% |

Revenue is down ~13% cumulatively from 2022 and net income is down ~58%. Operating margin has compressed by 310bps.

**Q1 2026 confirms continued deterioration rather than a trough.** Revenue was flat YoY at $968.3M but down sequentially from $980.1M in Q4'25. Gross margin compressed 90bps to 27.5%, with Commercial gross margin down 140bps to 31.0% on unfavorable mix (lower contribution from the higher-margin solutions that anchor the bull thesis) and Mexico FX. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 8.6%, from 9.7% a year earlier and 11.0% in Q4'25. Q2'26 guidance is soft: revenue $970M–$1,000M and net income of just $8.0M–$13.7M.

**Cash flow is weakening.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $327.9M (down from ~$400M in 2024) and FCF was $288.1M (a 7.2% margin). Q1'26 OCF was only $18.5M and FCF $9.1M, partly seasonal but thin against a $39M buyback in the same quarter.

**Leverage is rising into the downturn.** Long-term debt has grown from $1.03B (2024) to $1.17B (2025) to $1.46B (Q1'26) after the Quinnox deal was funded entirely on the revolver. The structure includes Term Loan A/B (with variable-rate Term Loan B exposure) and $550M of unsecured notes at 4.625% due 2028. Debt/equity sits around 1.04. The Altman Z-Score of 2.71 places the company in the grey zone, driven by declining EBIT/assets.

**Goodwill exceeds equity.** Goodwill plus intangibles of ~$2.9B post-Quinnox stands against ~$1.8B of equity. If the consulting-margin pivot continues to underdeliver, an impairment is a realistic non-cash event that would dent reported equity and could pressure covenant cushions.

**Capital allocation is pro-cyclical.** Buybacks of $170M (2025), $327M (2024), and $39M in Q1'26 have reduced share count from 51.3M (2022) to ~41.4M, but funding the Quinnox acquisition on the revolver while continuing repurchases in a quarter with $5.5M of net income and $9.1M of FCF raises leverage and depletes liquidity into a weakening environment. Roughly $934M remains authorized. Adjusted EBITDA add-backs ("acquisition, integration, and strategic planning" of $26.5M in 2025 and $12.8M in Q1'26 alone) are inflating the adjusted figure relative to GAAP, which warrants caution when evaluating any "EBITDA multiple" framing of cheapness.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis on the current data. Revenue has fallen three straight years, and the forward signal is negative across every horizon. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut sharply over 90 days: current quarter -35.5%, next quarter -22.8%, current year -27.3% (to $3.62 from ~$4.97), and next year -21.5% (to $4.56). Revision velocity of this magnitude in the negative direction is among the most reliable predictors of continued underperformance, and it co-occurs with falling revenue, the inverse of the setup that would support a contrarian buy.

The federal segment is the clearest forward warning. Management explicitly attributes federal revenue declines to DOGE-related spending initiatives and a government shutdown. Federal revenue fell 3.3% in 2025, and federal book-to-bill deteriorated to 0.9 (2025) and 0.7 (TTM Q1'26); below 1.0 means the backlog is shrinking faster than it is being replaced, signaling further federal revenue declines through 2026. With 26% of consolidated revenue coming directly from federal agencies, this is a material drag. Within federal, Defense/Intelligence and Federal Civilian both declined; only National Security grew.

The one constructive data point is commercial consulting book-to-bill of 1.1–1.2x, indicating the commercial consulting pipeline is still expanding on volume even as overall revenue shrinks. The pivot is working on volume but failing on margin, with Q1'26 explicitly citing "lower-than-expected contribution from higher-margin solutions." Until that margin promise shows up in reported gross margin, the strategic narrative is not supported by results.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.02, ASGN trades at roughly 5.8x current-year consensus EPS ($3.62), ~4.6x next-year ($4.56), and ~6.6x annualized Q1 adjusted EBITDA. On absolute multiples, this is unambiguously cheap. The peer comp provided (Figma at a negative P/E) is not informative; ASGN is better understood against IT staffing/consulting peers and against larger consultancies facing the same AI-disintermediation pressure, where this name sits at a steep discount.

The problem is that cheapness here reflects structural impairment, not a temporary dislocation. Three conditions argue against rewarding the low multiple:

1. **The earnings base is itself falling.** A 5x multiple on an EPS number that has been cut 27% in 90 days and continues to be revised lower is not a margin of safety; it is a moving target. If FY26 EPS settles closer to current-quarter run-rates, the "cheap" multiple expands.
2. **There is no identified positive catalyst.** The catalysts on the calendar skew negative: continued federal erosion, potential goodwill impairment, tightening covenant headroom, and an unproven margin pivot. Compressed multiples re-rate higher only when a credible turn is visible; nothing in the Q1 print or guidance provides one.
3. **The buyback "floor" is unreliable.** Repurchases at $47.69 did not arrest a 57% decline, and continuing them now consumes liquidity the balance sheet increasingly needs.

Analyst price targets (low $28, high $38, mean $33) sit far above the $21 market price, but the recommendation mix (1 buy, 4 hold, 2 sell) and the stale targets relative to the magnitude of the estimate cuts suggest the sell-side has not fully repriced. With estimates falling sharply, those targets are more likely to be revised down toward the stock than to pull the stock up. We do not treat them as a reliable anchor.

The valuation score is low not because the multiple is stretched, but because the cheapness is a trap absent a catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

The IT services and staffing market is, per the company's own 10-K, "highly competitive and fragmented with limited barriers to entry." ASGN competes against staffing firms, traditional consultancies, and boutiques, and its competitive trajectory is deteriorating on two structural axes:

**Federal IT retrenchment under DOGE.** Government-efficiency initiatives are cutting and renegotiating federal IT/consulting work across the contractor base, with civilian and discretionary modernization budgets most exposed. ASGN's 0.7 TTM federal book-to-bill is the cleanest evidence that this is reducing its forward federal revenue, and the company has limited ability to offset it given that federal work is all-consulting and tied to discretionary agency budgets.

**AI disintermediation of staffing and consulting.** The 10-K added an expanded risk factor acknowledging that clients may build internal AI capabilities (disintermediating ASGN) and that an AI-investment pullback could cut project demand. Staffing revenue, the first thing clients cut in a slowdown, fell 13.8% in 2025. ASGN's response is to co-sell and co-deliver alongside hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud) and platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, Databricks, Snowflake), but these are partner/alliance relationships, not owned technology, so the company captures services economics that are themselves vulnerable to automation.

The offsets are real but insufficient to reverse the trajectory: a $2.9B federal backlog, ~1,000 cybersecurity and ~900 cleared AI/data consultants (a genuine barrier in classified work), and a growing commercial consulting pipeline. The competitive position is stable-to-eroding with the negative vectors currently dominant.

## Risk Assessment

The material, company-specific risks that are not fully reflected even after the drawdown:

1. **Goodwill impairment.** $2.9B of goodwill and intangibles against $1.8B of equity. Continued earnings deterioration would likely trigger a non-cash write-down that hits book equity and could pressure covenant cushions.
2. **Federal cliff.** Book-to-bill at 0.7 implies the federal segment keeps shrinking through 2026; the DOGE driver is policy-driven and outside management control.
3. **Leverage and covenant risk.** Revolver availability down to $160M, variable-rate Term Loan B exposure, and covenants tied to deteriorating operating results. Headroom is narrowing as EBITDA falls.
4. **Margin-pivot execution failure.** The entire bull narrative rests on mix shift to higher-margin solutions; Q1'26 showed those solutions under-delivering and gross margin compressing.
5. **Low revenue visibility.** Most client and talent contracts are terminable at will; outside the federal backlog, forward revenue is thinly visible.
6. **New operational dependencies.** A new risk factor discloses outsourcing of back-office functions to a single third-party vendor, plus a concurrent ERP implementation and workforce optimization, restructuring under cost pressure.

Anti-takeover provisions (classified board, blank-check preferred, DGCL §203) reduce the probability of a takeout premium that might otherwise cushion a beaten-down equity.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The stock is genuinely cheap on absolute multiples and the company remains FCF-generative ($288M FY25). The consulting pivot is working on volume (commercial consulting book-to-bill 1.1–1.2x), and if the higher-margin solutions begin to convert, gross margins could re-expand off a depressed base. New commercial and federal leadership has been installed to execute a "Next Wave Growth Strategy." A stabilization of federal spending (book-to-bill back above 1.0), combined with even modest margin recovery, could support a re-rating toward the low end of the analyst range ($28). The CEO's "comp actually paid" tracking the stock down indicates pay-for-performance alignment is functioning. Bull case: ~$30 (toward the analyst target range, ~+43%).

### Bear Case
Revenue and margins continue to erode, federal revenue keeps falling on the 0.7 book-to-bill, and the consulting-margin pivot fails to convert. Leverage stays elevated with covenant headroom tightening, and a goodwill impairment hits book equity. Estimates keep getting cut. The stock revisits and undercuts its 52-week low of $18.50, toward $15–16 (~-25%). Continued buybacks at the wrong prices consume liquidity the balance sheet needs.

The base case sits closer to the bear: structural decline, falling estimates, and no catalyst dominate the modestly cheap multiple. Base case ~$18–20 (roughly flat to -10%), with risk skewed to the downside.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The most plausible negative catalysts are the Q2 2026 earnings report (~late July) confirming the soft guide and continued estimate cuts, and a potential goodwill impairment at a future quarter-end. Neither is a clean, dated binary, and the soft Q2 guidance is already public.

**Timing window.** Q2 results land ~late July; an impairment could come at any quarter close but is not scheduled. The federal book-to-bill erosion is a slow grind, not a single-event trigger.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is elevated but not crowded. The stock has shown sharp counter-trend rallies (~+34% from the $17.35 low to $23.20), so squeeze risk on any stabilization or buyback support is meaningful.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data provided; SI alone suggests moderate borrow demand. The absence of confirming put-skew data leaves the bearish mechanics unconfirmed.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Remaining downside to ~$15–16 is roughly 25%; upside risk on a stabilization bounce or takeout interest is comparable given the volatility profile. Reward:risk is below 2:1 after a 57% decline, with much of the bad news already priced.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is correct (a structurally declining, leveraging business), but the stock has already de-rated 57% and trades near 52-week lows, leaving insufficient asymmetry for a new short relative to squeeze and bounce risk. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** (any combination would change the view):
- Federal book-to-bill recovers above 1.0 for two consecutive quarters, signaling the DOGE drag is stabilizing.
- Reported gross margin expands sequentially, demonstrating the higher-margin solution mix is finally converting (the central unproven element of the strategy).
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive, confirming the earnings base has troughed.
- Net leverage declines toward prior levels with revolver availability rebuilt above ~$300M, and buybacks are paused in favor of deleveraging.
- No goodwill impairment through FY26, removing the equity-write-down overhang.

**Timeline.** The federal and AI-disruption headwinds are structural and likely to persist through at least 2026. Reassess after Q2 2026 results (~late July) for evidence of margin conversion and federal stabilization; absent that evidence, the headwinds extend into 2027.

## Conclusion

ASGN/Everforth is a structurally challenged IT staffing and consulting business priced cheaply for good reasons. Revenue has declined three straight years, Q1 2026 net income collapsed 74%, consensus EPS has been cut 21–36% over 90 days, federal book-to-bill at 0.7 signals further declines under DOGE-driven spending cuts, and leverage is rising into the downturn via revolver-funded M&A and pro-cyclical buybacks. The strategic pivot to higher-margin consulting, the entire bull thesis, is currently failing to show up in reported margins. Goodwill now exceeds equity, adding impairment risk.

The ~5x forward earnings multiple is a value trap, not an entry point: a compressed multiple on a falling earnings base with no identified positive catalyst and several concrete downside catalysts ahead. We rate ASGN/EFOR **AVOID**. It is not an active short given the stock is already down 57% and near 52-week lows, which limits residual downside relative to squeeze risk. The path to reconsideration runs through federal stabilization, demonstrated margin conversion, positive estimate revisions, and deleveraging, none of which is visible in the current data.
