## Executive Summary

Arcus Biosciences is a late clinical-stage oncology company whose investment thesis underwent fundamental restructuring following the failure of its TIGIT program (STAR-121 and STAR-221) in late 2025 and early 2026. The company's value now rests primarily on casdatifan, an oral HIF-2α inhibitor with Phase 1/1b data showing 15.1-month median PFS versus belzutifan's 5.6 months in similar renal cell carcinoma populations. At $24.78, the stock prices in partial probability-weighted success of this asset against a $2-3B+ peak revenue opportunity.

The stock has appreciated approximately 220% from its 52-week low of $7.06, driven by strengthening casdatifan data (ARC-20 update Feb 2026) and recognition that Arcus solely owns global commercial rights to this asset. Cash of $876M provides operational runway through H2 2028, covering the critical Phase 3 PEAK-1 enrollment (YE 2026) and PRISM-1 pancreatic cancer readout (H1 2027). The 12-month forward thesis depends on whether these catalysts generate incremental de-risking events that justify holding through a period of elevated cash burn ($400-500M annually) and continued losses.

The investment merits a HOLD based on two specific catalysts within 12 months: (1) PEAK-1 full enrollment confirmation by YE 2026, which converts the trial from execution risk to readout risk and typically re-rates clinical biotechs by 15-25%, and (2) PRISM-1 data in H1 2027 for pancreatic cancer, which if positive would establish a second independent value driver and meaningfully reduce single-asset concentration risk.

## Company Overview

Arcus Biosciences is a Hayward, California-based clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on cancer immunotherapy and immunology. Founded in 2015 by Terry Rosen, Ph.D. (CEO) and Juan Jaen, Ph.D. (President), the company has 601 employees, 81% in R&D.

**Key Assets:**
- **Casdatifan (HIF-2α inhibitor):** Phase 3 PEAK-1 trial in 2L+ clear cell RCC; Phase 3 in 1L RCC planned for initiation by YE 2026. Arcus owns global rights (ex-Taiho territories in Japan/Asia).
- **Quemliclustat (CD73 inhibitor):** Phase 3 PRISM-1 in first-line metastatic pancreatic cancer. Enrollment completed September 2025. Co-developed with Gilead. Results expected H1 2027.
- **Domvanalimab (anti-TIGIT):** Effectively dead after STAR-121 and STAR-221 futility. Only PACIFIC-8 (AstraZeneca collaboration) remains.
- **Inflammation/Immunology Portfolio:** Early-stage MRGPRX2 antagonist (CSU, clinical entry Q3 2026), TNF inhibitor (IND late 2026/early 2027).

**Partnership Structure:**
Gilead Sciences owns 25.1% of shares outstanding, has 3 board seats, and co-develops quemliclustat. Gilead has progressively disengaged following TIGIT failures, surrendering options to CCR6, CD89, and CD40L programs. The $100M continuation payment due in 2026 represents a near-term test of Gilead's ongoing commitment.

## Growth Analysis

Growth for Arcus is measured in clinical milestones and probability-weighted pipeline value creation.

**Casdatifan — The Primary Growth Driver:**
Phase 1/1b ARC-20 data at the PEAK-1 dose/formulation (100mg QD) show:
- Confirmed ORR: 45.2%
- Median PFS: 15.1 months
- Versus belzutifan's Phase 3 LITESPARK-005 mPFS of 5.6 months

This ~3x improvement in PFS, if confirmed in randomized Phase 3, would position casdatifan as best-in-class in 2L+ ccRCC, a market with $2B+ peak revenue potential. The company targets an additional $3B+ opportunity in first-line RCC (TKI-free approach), where Phase 3 initiation is planned by YE 2026.

**Key Catalyst Timeline:**
- Q3 2026: MRGPRX2 clinical entry (I&I)
- YE 2026: PEAK-1 full enrollment
- YE 2026: 1L RCC Phase 3 initiation
- H1 2027: PRISM-1 data readout (pancreatic cancer)
- 2027-2028: PEAK-1 primary endpoint (PFS) readout

**Growth Challenges:**
The TIGIT class-wide failure (domvanalimab in STAR-121 and STAR-221) eliminated what was previously a major value driver. Gilead's progressive disengagement narrows the partnership's financial contribution. Management itself warns in the 10-K that Phase 2 data may not predict Phase 3 outcomes, citing the EDGE-Gastric → STAR-221 failure pattern as directly relevant.

**Peer Context:** The relevant comparison is risk-adjusted pipeline value per dollar of market cap. At $3.1B with a single Phase 3 readout potentially worth $5B+ if successful, the risk/reward is balanced but not compellingly asymmetric at current prices.

## Competitive Landscape

**HIF-2α Market:**
Merck's belzutifan is the established first-mover, FDA-approved for VHL disease (2021) and advanced RCC (2023). Casdatifan's Phase 1/1b data (15.1-month mPFS vs. 5.6 months) represent a large efficacy advantage if confirmed in Phase 3, which would likely displace belzutifan as the preferred HIF-2α inhibitor.

Other competitors include Novartis/NiKang (small molecule) and Arrowhead (RNA-based), both earlier in development. Casdatifan appears best-positioned based on available data.

**Pancreatic Cancer (Quemliclustat):**
First-line pancreatic cancer has seen minimal therapeutic innovation in decades. Quemliclustat's CD73 inhibition combined with standard-of-care chemotherapy addresses genuine unmet need. Orphan drug designation was received in June 2025.

**Critical Caveat:** Cross-trial comparisons (casdatifan Phase 1/1b vs. belzutifan Phase 3) are unreliable. Patient selection, prior therapy lines, and assessment frequency can create apparent differences that vanish in randomized settings. The TIGIT experience, where Phase 2 data looked impressive but Phase 3 failed, is directly relevant.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Binary Phase 3 Outcome (Casdatifan):** With TIGIT dead, virtually all equity value rests on casdatifan's Phase 3 success. PEAK-1 failure would likely result in 60-70% stock decline given the concentrated thesis.

2. **Phase 2 → Phase 3 Translation Risk:** Management's own 10-K cites the STAR-221 failure as evidence that strong Phase 2 data may not replicate. The ~120-patient single-arm ARC-20 study, while impressive, faces the standard limitations of uncontrolled data.

3. **eVOLVE-RCC02 Safety Signal:** The AstraZeneca-sponsored first-line study combining casdatifan with volrustomig was paused after immune-mediated adverse events. This creates uncertainty about casdatifan's combinability in first-line settings (the larger opportunity).

4. **Cash Runway and Dilution:** At $400-500M annual burn, additional raises are near-certain. Shares outstanding grew 49% over 3 years. Each 10% dilution at current prices would add ~$300M in proceeds.

5. **Gilead Disengagement:** The progression from active co-development partner to passive equity holder reduces strategic optionality. If Gilead eventually exits its 25.1% stake (~31M shares), that overhang could pressure the stock.

6. **BIOSECURE Act / WuXi Dependency:** WuXi Biologics is the sole manufacturer for zimberelimab and domvanalimab. While these assets are diminished post-TIGIT, supply chain risk exists for any remaining programs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35-47):**
Casdatifan's Phase 3 data replicates Phase 1/1b efficacy. PEAK-1 demonstrates statistically significant PFS benefit over standard of care. PRISM-1 generates positive pancreatic cancer data. Two blockbuster-potential assets would justify $5-7B market cap. I&I pipeline provides upside optionality. Gilead's $100M continuation payment confirms ongoing support.

**Bear Case ($10-15):**
Casdatifan Phase 3 fails to replicate Phase 1/1b data — the STAR-221/EDGE-Gastric pattern repeats. Market re-rates to cash-per-share plus residual pipeline option value. PRISM-1 disappoints. Additional dilution at depressed levels creates equity value destruction loop. Gilead further disengages or exits stake.

**Base Case ($28-33):**
PEAK-1 enrollment completes on schedule, maintaining clinical momentum. No additional safety signals emerge. PRISM-1 generates mixed-to-positive data. Stock trades at ~$30 reflecting incrementally higher probability weights on casdatifan success as enrollment and investigator site expansion validate feasibility.

## Conclusion

Arcus Biosciences presents a classic clinical-stage biotech investment proposition: concentrated binary risk around a potentially best-in-class asset (casdatifan), partially offset by adequate near-term liquidity and a clear catalyst timeline. The 12-month forward view includes two specific de-risking events — PEAK-1 enrollment completion (YE 2026) and PRISM-1 readout (H1 2027) — that provide rational basis for continued ownership rather than exit.

The stock is not cheap enough to warrant a BUY given the elevated clinical risk (14.6% short interest and 80.4% IV confirm informed investors see material downside probability), the TIGIT failure precedent that management itself highlights as relevant, and the ~220% appreciation from lows that has already priced in substantial casdatifan optimism. The fundamental thesis (casdatifan's competitive differentiation, adequate runway, two near-term catalysts) remains intact and the analyst consensus targets 34-38% upside from current levels.

HOLD with a growth thesis. Position sizing should reflect binary biotech risk — smaller than core positions in diversified companies.
