## Executive Summary

Kinder Morgan is experiencing growth driven by structural demand for natural gas from data center power generation and LNG exports. Q1 2026 results demonstrated this clearly: Adjusted EBITDA rose 18% YoY to $2.54B, EPS grew 38%, and the company is tracking 3%+ above its full-year budget. The $10.1B project backlog at 5.6x first-full-year EBITDA multiples implies ~$1.8B of incremental annual EBITDA by 2027-2030, a material acceleration from the 2-3% annual growth of 2019-2023.

The problem is price. At $33.49, KMI trades at 22.7x trailing and forward EPS — a 43% premium to ONEOK (15.8x) and a level that already capitalizes the growth acceleration visible in estimates. Consensus targets cluster at $35 (median), offering only 4.5% upside. The stock has appreciated 20% over the past year and trades within 4% of its 52-week high. While fundamentals are strong and improving, the current valuation leaves insufficient margin of safety for a high-conviction entry. The 3.5% dividend yield compensates holders adequately, and the stock warrants accumulation on pullbacks to the $29-30 range where the risk/reward rebalances.

## Company Overview

Kinder Morgan operates approximately 78,000 miles of pipelines, 136 terminals, and 706 Bcf of natural gas storage capacity across North America. The Natural Gas Pipelines segment generates over 70% of Adjusted EBITDA, with Products Pipelines and Terminals each contributing roughly 14%, and the CO2 segment adding 7%.

The business model is predominantly fee-based with long-term take-or-pay contracts. Weighted average remaining contract life is ~7 years for gas transport and ~12 years for LNG-related agreements. This provides highly predictable cash flows largely independent of commodity prices — approximately 91% of distributable cash flow is insulated from direct commodity exposure.

## Growth Analysis

KMI's growth profile has materially shifted. The $10.1B project backlog consists primarily (~92%) of natural gas infrastructure serving three demand vectors:

1. **Data center power generation (~60% of backlog):** Take-or-pay contracts with utilities and power generators serving hyperscaler facilities. This is structural demand driven by AI infrastructure buildout, non-discretionary and backed by creditworthy counterparties.

2. **LNG export capacity:** Record U.S. LNG exports in March 2026. KMI's Tennessee Gas Pipeline and Southern Natural Gas systems serve multiple Gulf Coast LNG terminals. Geopolitical disruption to Middle East energy flows is accelerating long-term contracting for U.S. LNG.

3. **Coal-to-gas switching:** TVA and other utilities converting baseload generation from coal to gas, with dedicated pipeline projects (SSE4, MSX) receiving FERC environmental approval.

Key projects: South System Expansion 4 ($1.8B), Trident ($1.8B), Mississippi Crossing ($1.7B), FGT expansion ($700M). FERC Draft EIS issued for SSE4 and MSX in January 2026; certificates expected July 2026. At 5.6x first-full-year EBITDA multiples, these projects imply ~$1.8B of incremental annual EBITDA upon completion (primarily 2027-2029).

Estimate revisions confirm the growth trajectory: current-year EPS revised up 8.3% over 90 days, next-year up 4.4%. Q1 2026 delivered a 19.3% earnings surprise. However, these are moderate revision rates — well below the 30%+ threshold that signals systemic underestimation.

## Competitive Landscape

KMI occupies the #1 position among independent natural gas pipeline operators in North America by mileage. Its coast-to-coast connectivity and combined transport/storage offering differentiate it from more regionally focused competitors.

Competitive advantages:
- Five major gas pipeline systems operating at 90% utilization (up from 74% in 2016), indicating structural tightness that supports pricing power on contract renewals
- Existing rights-of-way enabling brownfield expansion at lower cost and faster permitting than greenfield competitors
- Integrated storage/transport offering captures value from volatility events (e.g., Winter Storm Fern in Q1 2026)

KMI's specific positioning — serving Southeast/Gulf Coast power generation markets where most new gas-fired capacity is being built — provides above-peer exposure to data center demand. The Monument Pipeline acquisition ($505M, Houston metro gas distribution) further strengthens local demand capture.

Disadvantage: KMI's C-corp structure creates a tax drag relative to MLP-structured peers, partially explaining its historical valuation discount.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk on Mega-Projects:** The $10.1B backlog requires FERC approvals (SSE4 and MSX certificates expected July 2026), steel procurement (subject to tariff uncertainty), and construction execution across multiple geographies. The company's 10-K added a new risk factor specifically addressing tariff/trade policy impact on project costs. Fixed-price contracts limit ability to pass through cost increases.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With ~$32B total debt and ~$3.5B at variable rates, higher-for-longer rates directly compress distributable cash flow. ~$1.1B matures within 12 months. KMI's declining leverage (3.6x) and credit upgrades mitigate refinancing risk.

**Natural Gas Demand Concentration:** 92% of the backlog targets natural gas infrastructure. A severe recession, accelerated renewable buildout, or policy reversal on gas generation would impair the growth thesis.

**Q1 Weather Benefit Not Repeatable:** Winter Storm Fern contributed meaningfully to Q1 outperformance. The "tracking 3%+ above budget" statement needs to be verified against Q2-Q3 results to confirm structural vs. weather-driven growth.

**CO2 Segment in Secular Decline:** Oil production declining ~2% annually with hedges providing temporary cushion (crude hedged at ~$64/bbl through 2028). This segment will become a larger drag as hedges roll off into potentially lower oil prices.

## Conclusion

Kinder Morgan is a well-positioned midstream infrastructure company entering a structural growth phase driven by data center power demand and LNG exports. The fundamentals are strong and improving: credit upgrades confirm balance sheet quality, the backlog provides multi-year earnings visibility, and Q1 2026 results demonstrated the operational leverage inherent in 90%-utilized pipeline systems.

However, the current price of $33.49 reflects much of this improvement. At 22.7x forward earnings, within 4% of the 52-week high, and with only 4.5% upside to median analyst targets, the stock offers income (3.5% yield) but limited asymmetric upside. The appropriate posture is to hold existing positions for income and dividend growth, with a clear intent to add exposure on any pullback to $29-30 — a level last seen in late April 2026 — where the risk/reward becomes materially more attractive.
