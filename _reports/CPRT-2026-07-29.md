**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Copart runs the dominant online salvage-vehicle auction platform, a two-sided marketplace connecting insurance-company sellers with roughly a million buyers across 185+ countries through its proprietary VB3 auction system. For most of the past two decades it was a reliable double-digit compounder with fortress economics: ~33% net margins, essentially no funded debt, and a ~$4.2B pile of cash and Treasuries. The current investment debate is narrower than that history suggests. Growth has stalled. U.S. service revenue fell 2.1% over the first nine months of FY26 and total revenue is roughly flat, with all incremental growth carried by International (+11.4% nine-month) and higher revenue-per-car. The premium multiple that Copart carried through 2025 was built on secular unit-volume growth, and that thesis is now being tested.

The stock has fallen roughly 39% from its September 2025 high of $50.11 to the current ~$30.69, and the decline compounds two distinct shocks: the growth scare visible in flattening U.S. volume, and the unexpected June 29 CEO transition, in which Jeff Liaw departs (effective July 31) and founder Jay Adair returns from Executive Chairman to the CEO seat. That abrupt reversal at a company otherwise this stable is a governance signal worth weighing, even if management has framed the change as permanent and growth-oriented. Layered on top is an unresolved DOJ money-laundering investigation into Copart's anti-money-laundering practices around auction members, open since October 2023 with no quantified reserve and no resolution timeline.

At ~19x forward EPS following the drawdown, the multiple has compressed from a ~25x secular-growth premium toward a level that no longer prices in double-digit growth, appropriate given that the growth has evaporated. The balance sheet remains genuinely exceptional and the duopoly structure is intact. But the fundamentals that historically justified a premium have deteriorated, and the near-term picture carries three unresolved binary-ish items (DOJ, CEO strategy execution, whether U.S. volume is a cyclical or structural inflection). We rate CPRT a HOLD. The declining score trajectory (7.5 → 5.7 across the prior four reports) reflects a genuine narrative shift from compounder to show-me story, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend.

## Company Overview

Copart operates the world's leading digital total-loss and used-vehicle auction marketplace. The model is asset-light and toll-based: the company earns roughly 85% of revenue as consignment/agent "service revenues" and ~15% as principal "vehicle sales," collecting fees from both sellers and buyers on each transaction. Sellers are primarily insurance companies disposing of vehicles declared total losses after accidents, supplemented by dealers, banks, rental fleets, charities, and individuals. Buyers are licensed dismantlers, rebuilders, exporters, used-car dealers, and increasingly the general public.

The company reports two segments: United States (~82% of sales) and International (~18%, growing double digits). Its competitive position rests on a two-sided network effect: buyers follow inventory, insurers follow the buyer liquidity that maximizes salvage recovery, reinforced by ownership of most of its ~$3.7B of yard and land. Zoning difficulty for new salvage yards is a structural entry barrier that protects incumbents. The U.S. market is effectively a duopoly between Copart and IAA, now owned by RB Global (Ritchie Bros.).

## Financial Analysis

Copart's balance sheet and margin structure are among the strongest in the industrials universe. For the nine months ended April 30, 2026:

- **Revenue: $3.514B (–0.2% YoY)**; net income $1.157B, a ~33% net margin that has held stable through the growth stall.
- **Operating margin ~37%**; U.S. segment ~38%, International ~28% and improving.
- **Operating cash flow $1.247B** (nine months), with capex now declining.
- **Liquidity: ~$3.35B cash + $846M held-to-maturity Treasuries = ~$4.2B**, against total equity of $8.77B.
- **Funded debt: effectively none** — interest paid was $1.6M for nine months. The Altman Z-score sits deep in the safe zone (~9+).

Two dynamics deserve attention beyond the headline resilience.

First, a **fading interest-income prop**. Copart earned ~$142M of "other income" over nine months, largely Treasury interest on idle cash. Q3 interest income fell 9% YoY as rates declined, and this tailwind reverses further as rates fall and as cash is redeployed into buybacks and M&A. This pressures reported pre-tax income even if operations hold flat, a mechanical drag the market may under-model.

Second, a **capital-allocation regime change**. After roughly 15 years of hoarding cash and reinvesting only organically, Copart repurchased $1.63B of stock in nine months of FY26 (43.4M shares at an average of $37.63), cutting shares outstanding from 967M to 926M. It simultaneously replaced its secured facility with a new $1.25B unsecured revolver (January 2026, matures 2031), currently undrawn. Management explicitly flagged additional buybacks, acquisitions, and potential dividends. The read-through is that management itself sees fewer high-return organic reinvestment opportunities and is redirecting the cash pile toward per-share value. The buyback average of $37.63 sits above today's ~$30.69, so the repurchases so far have been executed above the current price.

The earnings-surprise record is unremarkable but stable: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the March 2026 quarter missing by 10% ($0.36 vs. $0.40 estimate) before a modest 3.8% beat in the June quarter. Estimate revisions are flat: current-year consensus of $1.58 has barely moved over 90 days (+0.6%), and next-year of $1.68 is essentially unchanged. This is the profile of a business that has stopped surprising to the upside, not one collapsing.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the crux of the bear case, and the data is clear. U.S. service revenue declined 2.1% over nine months; total service revenue fell 0.4%. All growth is being carried by International (+18% in Q3) and by higher revenue-per-car masking declining U.S. unit volume. Management attributes part of the U.S. softness to lapping FY25 Hurricane Helene/Milton catastrophe revenue, a legitimate one-time comp, but the underlying trend of softer U.S. volume is real and is not fully explained by weather comps.

The structural debate underneath this matters for whether the stall is cyclical or secular. Copart's volume depends on accident frequency and total-loss frequency (the share of accidents where insurers scrap rather than repair). The forces are two-sided: rising used-car prices make totaling a vehicle less economical (fewer salvage cars), and ADAS/accident-avoidance technology reduces accident rates over time; against that, rising vehicle complexity and EV repair costs push total-loss frequency up. Current U.S. softness suggests the bearish forces are winning at the margin right now. Whether that persists is the unanswerable question at the center of the thesis.

International is the only durable growth engine, and it is improving margins as it scales. But it is replicating the U.S. model in markets — Germany, Brazil, the Middle East — where insurers do not traditionally drive salvage disposition, an unproven path with regulatory sensitivity (Brazil proposals were specifically flagged). At 18% of sales, International can support low-single-digit total growth but cannot offset a prolonged U.S. volume stall.

We do not see the estimate-revision inflection signal here: 90-day revisions are essentially flat, not the +30% velocity that would flag an accelerating fundamental turn. The growth thesis is neither collapsing nor recovering; it is stalled. Score: 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

Copart trades at ~19x forward EPS ($1.58 current-year consensus against $30.69), down from a mid-20s multiple through 2025. Against the Diversified Support Services peer set, the picture is mixed: peer median P/E is 21.7 (RBA 49.3, CACI 21.7, AMTM 36.8, LDOS 11.2, SAIC 12.9). On that basis Copart now sits modestly below the peer median, which is defensible given that the peer set is heterogeneous (government-services names like LDOS and SAIC trade at ~12-13x on structurally lower growth and margins, while RBA — the closest true comparable as the owner of IAA — trades at 49x).

Copart's ~19x forward multiple is no longer a secular-growth premium; it is closer to a fair price for a high-margin, fortress-balance-sheet business with low-single-digit growth. That is neither a value trap nor an obvious bargain. The compression to ~19x has already done the work of derating from the growth narrative. Analyst targets span $26–$55 with a $40.3 mean and $41 median, implying meaningful upside to consensus, but on a name where growth has stalled and estimates are flat, we would not treat those targets as reliable floors; they lag the repricing rather than lead it.

There is no cycle-adjusted case for a materially higher normalized earnings power here; this is a high-margin business at low-single-digit growth, not a trough-earnings semiconductor. The buyback provides a per-share tailwind (43.4M shares retired, ~4% of the count), and the ~$4.2B cash provides optionality for accretive M&A, both of which support a floor. But absent a re-acceleration in U.S. volume or a resolution of the DOJ overhang, there is no specific catalyst identifying why the market is currently mispricing the stock. A merely "reasonable" multiple is not itself a buy signal without an independent catalyst, and we do not see one. Score: 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Copart's competitive position remains structurally strong but its trajectory has softened. The U.S. salvage-auction market is a rational duopoly with IAA (now part of RB Global), a consolidation that removed a standalone competitor and generally supports pricing discipline. Copart's moat — network effects, owned real estate with zoning barriers, proprietary VB3 technology, and multi-decade insurer relationships — is intact and difficult to replicate.

The trajectory concern is twofold. First, RB Global's scale and capital could intensify competition for insurer contracts, which are not long-term and have been terminated in specific markets before. Second, the U.S. volume softness appears to be an industry-wide demand headwind (soft claims frequency, used-car price dynamics affecting total-loss economics) rather than share loss, but Copart's premium positioning leaves little room for a prolonged volume stall. Additional competitive pressure comes from large dismantlers buying directly from insurers and bypassing the auction, and from potential new entrants including OEMs and software companies. The AI thread cuts both ways: a product opportunity in pricing and buyer matching, but also an obsolescence and cyber risk the company candidly flags.

The competitive level is high; the trajectory is stable-to-softening. Score: 6.0, down from the 9.0 assigned in early 2026: the moat has not eroded, but the demand backdrop has stopped reinforcing it.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ money-laundering investigation (idiosyncratic, unresolved).** The DOJ Consumer Protection Branch is investigating Copart's practices for preventing and detecting money laundering by auction members. Copart received a letter in October 2023 indicating it "may have exposure," is cooperating, and states it "cannot predict the range of possible loss." No reserve is quantified and no resolution timeline exists. For a company otherwise this clean, this is an under-appreciated tail risk that could produce fines, penalties, or reputational harm, and it is not obviously priced.

**CEO transition.** The June 29 departure of CEO Jeff Liaw and return of founder Jay Adair (from Executive Chairman) was described as unexpected, pressured the stock, and prompted plaintiff-firm "investigations" (routine after unexpected leadership changes plus a drop). Adair has emphasized the change is permanent and signaled a renewed push on growth, M&A, and AI. Founder involvement can be a positive alignment signal, but an unexpected reversal at a stable business raises execution and strategy-continuity questions.

**Structural demand.** Total-loss frequency and accident rates drive volume; the net long-term effect of ADAS/AVs (bearish) versus rising repair costs (bullish) is genuinely uncertain, and current U.S. softness suggests the bearish forces lead at the margin today.

**Customer concentration and weather lumpiness.** No single customer exceeds 10%, but a limited number of insurers drive a large share on short-term contracts. CAT events create surge revenue and tough comps, making quarterly results lumpy.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 34.0% against 27.7% one-year historical vol — modestly rich (6.3pp spread), with IV sitting at only the 11th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (–0.5%), put/call volume and OI ratios are both 0.95, and the term structure is flat (near 34.0% ≈ far 33.8%). This is not a market pricing a directional catastrophe or an imminent binary; uncertainty is evenly distributed. The drop in the delta snapshot (ATM IV 719.5% → 34.0%) reflects the collapse of a distorted prior reading around the CEO/earnings window rather than a fundamental change. The absence of elevated put skew is mildly reassuring for the HOLD thesis: the options market is not flagging acute downside risk beyond what fundamentals imply. Short interest is low at 5.1% of float (2.7 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

Score: 5.0 — the DOJ investigation and CEO-transition uncertainty are genuine idiosyncratic risks not clearly priced, offset by an otherwise clean, low-leverage business.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$40–45).** U.S. volume softness proves cyclical — a combination of lapping FY25 hurricane comps and soft claims frequency that normalizes as accident and total-loss trends re-accelerate. International compounds at double digits with expanding margins. Founder Adair's return catalyzes accretive M&A that redeploys the cash pile at attractive returns, and continued buybacks compound per-share value. The DOJ matter resolves with an immaterial settlement. On restored mid-single-digit-plus growth, the multiple re-rates back toward the low-20s, supporting the analyst mean of $40.3.

**Bear Case (~$24–26).** The U.S. volume stall is structural — ADAS-driven accident-rate declines and used-car-price dynamics permanently compress salvage supply. Interest income fades as a hidden earnings prop reverses, pressuring pre-tax income. The DOJ investigation produces a material fine and reputational damage. Adair's M&A push destroys value at premium multiples. The multiple compresses further toward the government-services peers (12-13x), and EPS stagnates near $1.60, pulling the stock toward the analyst low of $26 or below.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided. The balance sheet caps the downside and the duopoly caps how bad the competitive story can get, but there is no identified catalyst to drive re-rating, and three overhangs (growth inflection direction, DOJ, CEO execution) sit unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The business is high-quality and the price has come down, but the fundamental deterioration (stalled U.S. growth) is real, and the near-term overhangs are unresolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- U.S. service revenue returns to positive YoY growth for two consecutive quarters (would signal the volume stall is cyclical, not structural).
- DOJ AML investigation resolves with an immaterial or clearly-bounded outcome.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days), confirming a fundamental turn.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. volume decline accelerates below –5% YoY, or International growth decelerates below high-single-digits.
- A material DOJ fine, or evidence of a large-scale dilutive/overpriced acquisition under the new CEO.
- Current-year EPS estimates revised down more than 5% over a 90-day window.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q1 FY27, expected roughly late November 2026), which will provide the first read on U.S. volume under the new CEO and on the pace of buyback/M&A deployment. Monitor 8-K filings for DOJ updates and any M&A announcements in the interim.

## Conclusion

Copart is a structurally excellent business trading at a fair, no-longer-premium multiple after a ~39% drawdown, and the fortress balance sheet plus duopoly position genuinely limit the downside. But the reasons to own it aggressively have weakened materially. The core U.S. growth engine has stalled, the interest-income tailwind is fading, an unexpected founder-return CEO change has introduced strategy-continuity questions, and an unresolved DOJ investigation sits without a quantified reserve. Insiders show deep founder alignment (Adair's ~14.2M shares) but no open-market buying near multi-month lows, a neutral-to-cautious signal. The ~19x forward multiple prices the business roughly fairly for its current low-single-digit growth trajectory; it is neither a compelling bargain nor an obvious short.

The declining score trajectory across prior reports reflects this genuine narrative shift from compounder to show-me story, and today's data confirms it. We rate CPRT a HOLD, with a bar for upgrading to BUY that requires either a return to positive U.S. growth or resolution of the DOJ overhang, and a bar for downgrading to AVOID that requires accelerating volume declines or value-destructive capital deployment.
