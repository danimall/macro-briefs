**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a Delaware Basin–focused independent E&P that has grown its scale over three years (131,813 BOE/d in 2023 to 207,070 BOE/d in 2025) through the Advance and Ameredev acquisitions plus organic delineation, while building a separately-financed midstream business (San Mateo) that contributes a second cash-flow stream. The stock trades at $50.38, down from a March high of $66.84, a decline driven almost entirely by oil-price compression and a Waha natural-gas basis blowout rather than any deterioration in the underlying business. Forward consensus estimates have moved the opposite direction from the share price: current-year EPS estimates are up +56.0% over 90 days to $7.78, next-year up +35.4% to $8.79, and the company has beaten estimates four straight quarters, most recently by 18.8% in Q1 2026.

The valuation gap is the core of the case. At $50.38, Matador trades at roughly 6.5x current-year and 5.7x next-year consensus EPS, against a peer-group median P/E of 32.5x. The lowest of the analyst price targets ($62) sits ~26% above the current price; the mean ($73) implies ~45% upside. The most informative qualitative signal is a cluster of open-market insider purchases in May–June 2026 by the CEO, CFO, COO, CAO, and multiple directors at $52–56, above today's price, executed as management committed $1.143B to new "core-of-the-core" acreage and the stock was weakening.

This report upgrades to BUY. The setup meets the inflection profile: estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days combined with rising production volumes, at a forward multiple far inside the threshold where rising estimates reliably precede continued re-rating. The two reasons most often cited for caution, oil-price sensitivity and gas-egress timing, are generic commodity-beta exposures shared across the E&P sector and, in Matador's specific case, are explicitly time-bound (Hugh Brinson pipeline online Q3/Q4 2026) and partially mitigated (most Delaware gas sold at Houston Ship Channel, not Waha). They do not constitute a Matador-specific structural impairment that justifies forgoing the documented upside. Sizing should be disciplined given the direct unhedged commodity leverage and the debt-funded acquisition, but the rating should reflect the evidence.

## Company Overview

Matador (Texas-incorporated, founded 2003 by Joseph Wm. Foran, still Chairman and CEO) operates two segments:

- **Exploration & Production:** ~99% of proved reserves and ~98% of production from the Delaware Basin (Wolfcamp/Bone Spring) in SE New Mexico and West Texas, with a small Haynesville/Cotton Valley position in NW Louisiana. The asset base is oil-weighted (~58% oil). FY2025 scale: 207,070 BOE/d (+21% YoY), 667 MMBOE proved reserves (+9%), 354,600 gross (212,500 net) Delaware acres, 5,295 gross identified drilling locations (~3,800 miles of net lateral).
- **Midstream (San Mateo, 51% Matador / 49% Five Point):** Gas processing (720 MMcf/d), oil gathering/transport, and produced-water disposal (475,000 Bbl/d), serving Matador under 15-year fixed-fee dedications plus third-party volumes. San Mateo generated $136.7M in distributions to Matador in 2025.

The competitive structure rests on concentrated, contiguous, largely held-by-production (81%) Delaware acreage that enables long laterals (~10,400 ft average) and large batch developments, plus an owned midstream that provides flow assurance and incremental margin not available to pure-play E&Ps.

## Financial Analysis

**Headline GAAP vs. underlying cash:** Q1 2026 reported a GAAP net loss of $35.9M (–$0.29/sh), but this is almost entirely a non-cash $255.5M unrealized mark-to-market derivative loss: rising WTI (intraday $95–102 in late Q1) pushed prices above Matador's collar ceilings (~$66.36). The underlying metrics were solid: Adjusted EBITDA of $577.2M and operating cash flow of $470.5M. FY2025 net income to shareholders was $759M. Trailing reported EPS of $6.09 frames the valuation against forward consensus.

**The defining dynamic — natural gas/Waha:** Realized natural gas price collapsed to $0.64/Mcf in Q1 2026 from $3.56 a year earlier (–82%) as the Waha–Henry Hub basis differential blew out to ~($5.99)/MMBtu. Gas revenue fell from $160.6M to $30.4M YoY. Oil revenue rose 5% to $788M on higher volumes, masking the gas weakness. Management has at times voluntarily shut in high-GOR wells in response. This is the most acute near-term headwind, but it is time-limited: 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transport on Energy Transfer's Hugh Brinson Pipeline (expected online Q3/Q4 2026) will route gas to Gulf Coast/LNG markets, and most Delaware gas is already sold at Houston Ship Channel rather than Waha.

**Balance sheet:** Total long-term debt was ~$3.47B at 3/31/26 ($185M RBL + $918M non-recourse San Mateo facility + $2.4B senior notes). Reported D/E (~2.0) and Altman Z (~1.6) look distressed but are artifacts of the capital-intensive full-cost model (net PP&E ~$10.9B) and a $306M current derivative liability distorting working capital. The company was covenant-compliant (≤3.5x net debt/EBITDA, current ratio ≥1.0), is FCF-positive, and Fitch upgraded it from BB- to BB in 2025. The March 2026 refinancing redeemed $500M of 6.875% 2028 notes and issued $750M of 6.0% 2034 notes: lower coupon, longer maturity, opportunistic liability management.

**Capital allocation:** Dividend raised to $0.375/qtr (from $0.25 in Q4'24, ~3.0% yield). A $400M buyback was authorized in April 2025 but only ~$56M used in 2025 and $0.7M in Q1'26, deprioritized in favor of the acquisition and deleveraging. 2026 D/C/E capex budget is $1.35–1.44B plus $100–110M midstream. The CEO compensation structure is well-aligned: FY2025 total comp $9.8M but "comp actually paid" fell to $6.8M, tracking lower TSR/EBITDA, with cumulative TSR of 354.8 vs peer group 193.0 (indexed).

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest of the six dimensions and rests on hard, multi-source evidence:

- **Estimate revisions meet the inflection threshold.** Current-quarter EPS estimates are +76.6% over 90 days. The 30-day revisions remain positive (+8.4% current-quarter, +6.2% current-year), indicating momentum is continuing rather than rolling over. Revisions of this magnitude combined with rising production volumes are among the most reliable forward indicators of continued outperformance, and the forward multiple is far inside the range where rising estimates precede re-rating rather than being already discounted.
- **Beat track record.** Four consecutive beats — Q1 2026 +18.8%, Q4 2025 +6.2%, Q3 2025 +5.8%, Q2 2025 +6.6% — supports confidence in forward consensus.
- **Volume growth.** Production grew 21% YoY in 2025; 2026 production guidance was raised after a Q1 beat (~3% above guidance midpoint).
- **Inventory depth.** The $1.143B BLM lease-sale acquisition adds 5,154 net "core-of-the-core" Delaware acres with 141+ net 2-mile-equivalent locations, 87.5% NRI, and 10-year terms supporting 3-mile laterals adjacent to existing San Mateo infrastructure. Management cites the 2018 State Line/Rodney Robinson deals that generated $1.9B of returns as the template.

The principal growth uncertainty is gas egress timing, which compresses near-term cash realizations but is bounded by the Hugh Brinson startup and new ET marketing agreements, not a permanent constraint on the resource.

## Valuation Assessment

This is an inflection setup, and trailing multiples understate cheapness because they capture trough realized pricing on gas. The appropriate metrics are forward and growth-adjusted:

- **Forward P/E.** At $50.38, Matador trades at ~6.5x current-year ($7.78) and ~5.7x next-year ($8.79) consensus. The peer median P/E is 32.5x; the smaller-cap Delaware comps (MGY 15.8x, CNX 4.4x) bracket a wide range, but Matador's multiple sits at the low end despite superior production growth and an owned-midstream cash stream.
- **PEG.** With next-year EPS growth of ~13% off a rising base and forward P/E under 6x, the PEG is well below 1.0.
- **Analyst targets as a floor.** On a stock this cheap relative to rising estimates, consensus targets lag reality. The lowest target is $62 (~26% above spot); the mean is $73 (~45% upside); the high is $97. Truist upgraded to Buy with a $67 target on 5/12/26. When estimates are rising, current targets are stale and revise upward as consensus catches up.
- **Yield support.** A ~3.0% dividend, raised 50% over the prior year and well-covered by FCF, provides downside support.

The price decline from the high-$60s to $50 does not weaken the thesis: forward estimates are higher today than 90 days ago, so a lower price on rising estimates improves risk/reward. The standard caution that "cheap" energy names are value traps applies where cheapness reflects structural impairment with no catalyst; here there are concrete catalysts (Hugh Brinson startup, RBL deleveraging by H1 2027, continued beats) and the cheapness reflects commodity-beta compression that is partially reversing. I score valuation 7.5.

## Competitive Landscape

The Delaware Basin is the most productive U.S. shale sub-basin and the center of industry consolidation, where larger majors and independents compete for acreage, rigs, and services. Matador's differentiators:

- **Concentrated, contiguous, HBP acreage** (81% held by production) enabling long-lateral, large-batch development that lowers per-foot cost.
- **Owned midstream (San Mateo)** providing flow assurance and a second, non-recourse-financed cash stream, a structural advantage versus pure-play E&Ps facing the same Waha egress problem with no transport optionality.
- **Targeted bolt-on M&A discipline** rather than dilutive corporate mergers; the $1.143B lease deal at ~$222k/net acre is a premium price, justified only if the 3-mile-lateral inventory and adjacent infrastructure deliver the returns management projects.

The trajectory is improving (share gains via volume growth and inventory additions, midstream scaling to 720 MMcf/d), tempered by the basin-wide gas-egress headwind that affects all operators and the high price paid for the latest acreage. Competitive position scores 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Waha gas basis (most acute, near-term, time-limited).** Mitigated by Houston Ship Channel sales mix, NGL extraction before reporting, and the Hugh Brinson firm transport. Until then, gas realizations remain exposed.
2. **Commodity price / hedging asymmetry.** Costless collars cap 2026 oil at ~$66.36 (18.4M Bbl), producing GAAP losses and realized oil losses (–$4.79/Bbl in Q1) when oil rallies. This is direct, largely unhedged downside exposure to oil and a sector-wide trait, not a Matador-specific flaw.
3. **Federal-lands exposure (~33% of Delaware leasehold).** BLM permitting delays, NEPA litigation, the 2024 BLM waste rule, and NMED permit-deadline suspensions are genuine operational risks for a concentrated operator.
4. **Customer concentration.** Three purchasers = 72% of O&G/NGL revenue.
5. **Acquisition/leverage risk.** The debt-funded lease deal levers up just as gas prices are depressed; the deleveraging path to H1 2027 depends on $1.2B FCF holding, which depends on oil firmness and gas-egress relief. The RBL draw is small ($185M against $1.2B projected FCF), which limits the balance-sheet stress.
6. **Minimum volume commitments.** ~$2.29B third-party deficiency exposure plus $701M to San Mateo, a fixed-cost overhang in a downturn.

The macro backdrop adds commodity-price two-sidedness: lower crude compresses upstream realizations, while supply disruptions support prices. Matador's low breakevens and oil weighting make it resilient across a range of price environments without impairment risk. Given the direct unhedged commodity leverage and the debt-funded acquisition into weak gas, risk factors score 5.5: these are real and partially binary, but most are already reflected in the compressed multiple and the elevated short interest.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($73–97):** Hugh Brinson comes online on schedule, gas realizations recover toward more normal Houston Ship Channel pricing, and the gas drag that depressed Q1 reverses. Oil holds firm, the RBL is repaid by H1 2027, and the new acreage adds premium long-lateral inventory that compounds production growth. Estimates continue rising; the stock re-rates toward the peer median and the analyst mean ($73) or high ($97) as consensus targets catch up to the revision wave. Insider buying above spot is vindicated.

**Bear Case ($37–40):** Oil falls materially, compressing upstream realizations; Waha egress relief slips; the debt-funded acquisition leaves leverage elevated into a softer commodity environment; and the deleveraging path stalls. The stock revisits the 52-week low of $37.14. The hedge collar limits the downside on cash realizations but does not protect reported earnings.

**Base Case ($66):** 8.5x forward P/E on $7.78 FY26 EPS = $66, consistent with partial gas-egress relief, continued production growth, and a re-rating partway toward the peer cohort. This implies ~31% upside from $50.38, between the bear ($37–40) and bull ($73–97) scenarios.

The evidence cluster — +56%/+35% estimate revisions, four straight beats, ~6.5x forward P/E versus a 32.5x peer median, a broad C-suite insider buy cluster above spot, and an analyst target floor 26% above price — meets the inflection BUY archetype. The decision to wait for catalyst confirmation that the sources already evidence is the failure mode this rating corrects.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target:** $66 (8.5x forward P/E on $7.78 FY26 EPS), ~31% upside from $50.38.
- **Bull case target:** $73–97 (peer-median re-rating / analyst mean-to-high), +45% to +93%.
- **Bear case target:** $37–40 (52-week low retest on oil compression + delayed gas relief), –21% to –26%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Hugh Brinson startup (Q3/Q4 2026), the H1 2027 RBL repayment milestone, and the next two earnings prints testing the beat streak.
- **Upside/downside:** +31% to base, up to +93% to bull high; –21% to –26% to bear.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:**
  1. Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn negative for two consecutive months (signaling the revision momentum has reversed).
  2. Hugh Brinson startup slips materially beyond Q4 2026, or the Waha basis fails to narrow as the line comes online, leaving gas realizations depressed into 2027.
  3. Net debt/EBITDA breaches 3.0x or the RBL deleveraging path to H1 2027 is abandoned, indicating the acquisition is straining the balance sheet rather than self-funding.

## Conclusion

Matador is a BUY. The stock trades at a low single-digit forward multiple while estimates have risen sharply over 90 days, the company has beaten four straight quarters, and the C-suite has been buying open-market above the current price. The decline from the high-$60s to $50 reflects oil-price compression and a transient Waha gas-egress problem that is bounded by the Hugh Brinson pipeline and ET marketing agreements, not a structural impairment specific to Matador. The principal risks — unhedged oil sensitivity, gas-egress timing, and the debt-funded acquisition — are real and warrant disciplined position sizing, but they are largely sector-wide, partially mitigated, and substantially reflected in the compressed multiple. The base case implies ~31% upside, the analyst target floor sits ~26% above spot, and the insider signal is the strongest form of management conviction. Accumulate, with awareness that the position carries direct commodity beta and should be sized accordingly.
