**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum is a small, high-cost primary aluminum producer that has become one of the cleanest equity expressions of U.S. trade policy. The 50% Section 232 aluminum tariff has driven the U.S. Midwest Premium (MWP) from $427/tonne in 2024 to roughly $2,294/tonne in Q1 2026, a more than three-fold move that transforms the economics of Century's domestic Sebree and Mt. Holly smelters, which sell at LME-plus-premium and cannot pass through input cost increases. Adjusted EBITDA stepped from ~$171M in Q4 2025 to $231M in Q1 2026, with Q2 2026 guidance of $315-335M. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up +43.7% for the current year and +120.3% for next year over 90 days. This revision velocity and revenue acceleration typically precedes continued re-rating as analysts catch up.

The stock has appreciated roughly 200% over the past year, from the high-teens to the mid-$50s, but currently trades about 23% below its $70.43 52-week high and below every analyst price target ($75 low, $86 high). On forward earnings, the multiple is undemanding: the current-year consensus of $10.88 puts the stock near 5x forward EPS, and the next-year $13.69 estimate implies under 4x. If the tariff regime and premium hold, the stock is cheap. That "if" carries unusual weight here.

We rate CENX a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The fundamental trajectory is strong enough and the forward valuation undemanding enough to justify holding for appreciation toward the analyst range over twelve months. We stop short of a BUY because the thesis reduces almost entirely to a single, binary policy variable, sustained Section 232 protection, and because the people closest to the company have been distributing. The entire C-suite sold into the run, and Glencore, simultaneously the largest shareholder and largest customer, cut its stake from 36.4% to 30.0% near multi-year highs. Layered on top is an unremediated material weakness and "not effective" disclosure controls at Q1 2026, plus a hedge book generating large mark-to-market losses that partly cap upside.

## Company Overview

Century Aluminum produces primary aluminum and alumina, managed as a single "mine-to-metal" segment. Its principal assets are the Sebree (Kentucky) and Mt. Holly (South Carolina) U.S. smelters, the Grundartangi smelter in Iceland (~545 MW, roughly 70% LME-indexed power, a partial natural hedge), the 55%-owned Jamalco bauxite/alumina joint venture in Jamaica (consolidated as a VIE, providing alumina integration), and the Vlissingen carbon anode plant in the Netherlands supplying Iceland.

Profitability is overwhelmingly a spread: LME aluminum price plus regional premiums (above all the U.S. Midwest Premium), less power, alumina, carbon, and labor costs, which together comprise roughly 84% of COGS. Century is structurally higher-cost and smaller than the vertically integrated majors (Alcoa, Rio Tinto, Rusal, Chinese state producers) and sells at market-indexed prices, so it has no ability to pass through cost inflation. The current earnings power is a direct function of the 50% Section 232 tariff inflating the premium it captures on domestic metal.

## Financial Analysis

The trailing GAAP numbers are distorted by one-time items and should not be used as the run-rate.

| Year | Revenue | Operating Income | Net Income (attrib.) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $2,777M | -$150M | -$14M | High power costs |
| 2023 | $2,185M | $32M | -$43M | Jamalco acquired |
| 2024 | $2,220M | $121M | $337M | Includes $246M bargain purchase gain |
| 2025 | $2,528M | $158M | $42M | Tariff tailwind begins, $94.7M derivative loss |

The 2024 net income of $337M was inflated by a $245.9M Jamalco bargain purchase gain. Cleaner 2025 net income of $42M absorbed a $94.7M non-affiliate derivative loss as MWP/LME hedges moved against the company during the premium spike. The relevant signal is the premium and EBITDA trajectory:

- Average MWP: $427/t (2024) → $1,295/t (2025) → ~$2,294/t (Q1 2026), versus $729 a year prior.
- Average LME: $2,419 → $2,630 → ~$3,191 (Q1 2026).
- Adjusted EBITDA: ~$171M (Q4 2025) → $231.4M (Q1 2026) → guided $315-335M (Q2 2026).

Q1 2026 reported net income of $337.5M, but $287.9M was the Hawesville sale gain and ~$33M was an insurance gain. Q1 GAAP EPS was $1.63, missing the $1.84 estimate by 11.2%, part of a four-quarter miss streak (-10.9%, -34.6%, -5.2%, -11.2%). The misses warrant caution on the precision of forward consensus, though the operational EBITDA ramp is real and management-guided rather than analyst-extrapolated.

The balance sheet has materially improved. Total debt is ~$546M ($400M 6.875% secured notes due 2032, $85.5M 2.75% converts due 2028, ~$66M Iceland revolver), proactively termed out in July 2025. Liquidity rose to $611M at Q1 2026 ($244M cash + $88M restricted Hawesville proceeds + $279M revolver availability) from $418M at year-end 2025. Equity jumped to $1.15B from $806M on the Hawesville gain. Leverage is modest; net debt is well covered by guided EBITDA.

2025 operating cash flow was $185M and FCF $84.8M, a genuine swing from negative 2024 OCF (-$24.6M). FCF in 2026 will be pressured by $170-180M of capex (Grundartangi transformer repairs, Mt. Holly restart, Jamalco), partly insurance-reimbursed. One embedded earnings prop deserves flagging: the Section 45X advanced manufacturing credit is recognized as a reduction to COGS, $25M in Q1 2026 alone, with a $173M+ manufacturing credit receivable on the balance sheet. This is material and policy-dependent; it phases out 25%/yr from 2031 to zero in 2034 under the July 2025 budget law.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is being driven by price/premium rather than volume, with three identifiable volume levers layered on top:

1. **Premium-driven margin expansion.** The MWP flows almost directly to operating margin on domestic tonnes. This is the dominant Q1-to-Q2 EBITDA driver behind the $315-335M guide.
2. **Mt. Holly full restart** (Line 2 / last 90 pots, initiated April 2026; Santee Cooper power extended to 2031) adds domestic volume into the high-premium environment.
3. **Grundartangi recovery** from the November 2024 and October 2025 transformer failures, with full production expected end-July 2026.

The estimate-revision picture: current-year consensus EPS revised from $7.57 to $10.88 (+43.7% over 90 days), next-year from $6.21 to $13.69 (+120.3%), and next-quarter +58.2%. Revisions of this magnitude alongside revenue acceleration are among the strongest forward indicators available, and they are consistent with management's own EBITDA guidance rather than being a pure analyst artifact. We weight this signal heavily in the Growth Prospects score.

Longer-dated optionality comes from two transformational projects, both still subject to final decisions. The EGA joint venture (announced January 2026) would build the first new U.S. primary smelter since 1980 in Inola, Oklahoma — 750,000 tonnes/yr, EGA 60% / Century 40%, backed by a $500M DOE cooperative agreement. Construction is targeted for end-2026 but is contingent on a final investment decision, an Oklahoma power deal, and definitive JV terms. Separately, the Hawesville sale to a TeraWulf affiliate left Century with a 6.8% stake in Raylan Data Holdings plus a put option to force repurchase of that stake for $300M once the planned data center delivers ≥375 MW (combined fair-valued at $97M today). This is real, quantifiable optionality, but Century is a minority equity holder and former landowner, not a data-center operator; the figure belongs to the developer.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing earnings the stock looks expensive (15x reported EPS of $0.42 on a distorted base), but trailing P/E is meaningless at this point in the cycle. On forward consensus the picture inverts: current-year EPS of $10.88 puts CENX near 5.0x, and next-year $13.69 implies under 4x. Even haircutting next-year materially for the four-quarter miss streak and the policy risk, the multiple is undemanding for a business guiding to a near-doubling of EBITDA.

Peer comparison is of limited use here because the listed Materials peers (SXT 36.6x, LPX 63.9x, BCPC 33.9x, HCC 36.0x, MTRN 67.4x; median 36.6x) are stable-margin specialty/industrial names, not commodity smelters at a cyclical/policy-driven earnings peak. The market is correctly applying a low multiple to peak-looking, policy-contingent earnings. The relevant question is not the headline multiple but whether the premium-driven earnings power is durable. If the tariff regime holds through 2026-2027, the current price discounts a meaningful reversion that may not occur, and analyst targets of $75-86 (mean $81.33, median $83) imply roughly 38-58% upside from the current ~$54.31-$54.55 price. If the tariff is reduced or exempted, the MWP collapses toward historical levels and the cheap forward multiple was a trap.

We resist treating the analyst targets as firm floors despite the cheap forward multiple, because those targets embed the same tariff assumption that the price does; they are not independent confirmation when the entire thesis is one policy variable. We also resist scoring valuation high purely on cheapness; a compressed forward multiple on policy-contingent peak earnings is a classic value-trap profile absent confidence in durability. We land at a 5.5: genuinely cheap if the regime holds, but with no independent catalyst to de-risk the central assumption.

## Competitive Landscape

Century's competitive position is structurally weak but situationally advantaged. It is a high-cost, sub-scale producer that cannot pass through costs and competes against far larger integrated players and Chinese overcapacity. Its current advantage is entirely policy-conferred: the 50% tariff inflates the premium it captures, and the 45X credit subsidizes domestic production cost. Vertical integration via Jamalco (alumina) and Vlissingen (anodes) partially insulates input costs, and Mt. Holly's restart plus the EGA JV would expand domestic capacity into a protected market.

The trajectory is improving in the sense that domestic volume and protected premiums are both rising, and the EGA JV positions Century as the vehicle for U.S. primary capacity expansion. But the improvement is borrowed from trade policy rather than from a widening company-specific moat. Global LME inventories near record lows and constrained supply growth provide a supportive backdrop independent of tariffs, which tempers the downside, but Chinese overproduction remains the standing structural threat. We score competitive position at 5.5 — a small, high-cost operator whose position has improved on policy support rather than durable advantage, with Materials a sector where company-specific outperformance must clear a high bar.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is heavily idiosyncratic and not fully priced.

**Single-variable policy dependence.** The entire earnings inflection rests on the 50% Section 232 tariff sustaining the elevated MWP. The 10-K explicitly notes that any exemption, reduction, or repeal could lessen or eliminate the benefit. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down IEEPA tariffs but did not touch Section 232, a favorable distinction that underscores how exposed the company is to a single legal/political mechanism.

**Glencore concentration.** Glencore was ~54% of 2025 sales (47.1% in Q1 2026) and owns 30.0% of the stock with one of seven board seats. This combines customer-concentration, related-party, and governance risk. Glencore sold 6.3M shares in March 2026 (at $51.75 on 3/4/2026, ~$326M), reducing its stake near highs.

**Material weakness and restatement.** A prior-period restatement (incorrect Jamalco consolidation; Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K filed 3/3/2026), an unremediated material weakness in Jamalco controls, and disclosure controls deemed "not effective" as of Q1 2026 are uncomfortable for a company this exposed to commodity accounting and related-party transactions.

**Operational fragility.** Grundartangi transformer failures cut that smelter's output by ~two-thirds; a single equipment failure materially dents results even with insurance recovery. Sebree's market-based MISO power produced a $35.7M unfavorable swing in Q1 2026 from winter weather.

**Hedge book.** Because Century hedges LME/MWP, the premium spike that boosts operating results simultaneously generates large derivative losses ($94.7M in 2025, $65.3M in Q1 2026). Open positions (59,500t LME, 99,400t MWP through Dec 2027) cap upside partially and create latent margin-call liquidity risk if premiums keep rising.

**Convertible dilution.** The converts (strike ~$18.74) are deeply in the money; if-converted value exceeded principal by $158.8M at Q1 2026, ~4.6M dilutive shares.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 129.5% versus 63.4% historical volatility (a 66pp spread, rich), with the term structure in backwardation (129.5% near-term vs 81.6% six-month). ATM IV jumped from 80.6% to 129.5% over the past month even as the stock fell 5.9%, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.27 to 0.38 with an 8.8% near-term put skew. Short interest sits at 11.7% of float (4.8 days to cover). The combination of rising put demand, elevated short interest, and a stock 23% off its high signals that sophisticated participants are positioning for downside or hedging a binary event, consistent with the policy-dependence risk and the next earnings print. This argues against chasing the stock here and supports the HOLD rather than BUY conclusion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75-86, +38% to +58%)
Section 232 tariffs hold through 2026-2027, sustaining the MWP near $2,000+/tonne. Q2 EBITDA of $315-335M confirms the run-rate, Mt. Holly and Grundartangi volume ramps on schedule, and full-year EPS lands near or above the $10.88 consensus. Analysts revise targets upward as consensus catches up to the guided EBITDA, the stock re-rates toward $80+, and the Raylan put option and EGA JV provide longer-dated optionality. At under 5x forward EPS, modest multiple expansion plus earnings delivery drives the upside.

### Bear Case (~$30, -45%)
A tariff exemption, reduction, or repeal collapses the MWP toward historical levels, compressing the margin spread that drives 2026 earnings. The 45X credit's eventual phase-out compounds the structural pressure. The hedge book continues to bleed mark-to-market losses and triggers margin calls if premiums spike before reverting. The unremediated material weakness surfaces further accounting issues. The four-quarter miss streak continues, and the broad insider/Glencore distribution proves prescient. The stock reverts toward its cost-of-production-driven floor in the low-$30s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 results confirm Adjusted EBITDA in/above the $315-335M guide AND management signals durability of the MWP environment, with the miss streak broken.
- A material reduction in policy binary risk — e.g., codification or extension of Section 232 protections, or a long-term offtake structure that locks in domestic premium economics.
- Remediation of the material weakness with disclosure controls deemed effective, removing the governance overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any credible move to reduce, exempt, or repeal the Section 232 aluminum tariff, or a sustained MWP decline back below ~$1,000/tonne.
- A hedge-driven liquidity event (margin calls) or a further accounting restatement.
- EBITDA delivery materially below guidance for two consecutive quarters, signaling the inflection is not converting to the run-rate.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint; it tests whether the guided EBITDA step-up materializes and whether the miss streak breaks. The Grundartangi full-production milestone (end-July 2026) and any EGA final investment decision (targeted end-2026) are secondary monitoring points.

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum offers a genuine, well-documented earnings inflection: Adjusted EBITDA guided to roughly double from Q4 2025 to Q2 2026, consensus EPS revisions of +43.7%/+120.3%, and a forward multiple under 5x that is undemanding if the underlying premium environment holds. That combination supports holding for appreciation toward the $75-86 analyst range over twelve months, and on a price decline alone we would not lower our scores; the fundamentals have improved, not deteriorated, alongside the recent pullback.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the unusual concentration of the thesis in one policy variable, reinforced by the behavior of the best-informed sellers. The entire C-suite distributed into the run with no offsetting open-market buying, and Glencore cut its stake near the highs. The options market is pricing imminent stress, and short interest is elevated. An unremediated material weakness and "not effective" disclosure controls compound the caution. The earnings power is real but borrowed from trade policy, and the recurring four-quarter miss streak tempers confidence in the precise forward numbers. We would upgrade on confirmation of the EBITDA run-rate plus any structural de-risking of the tariff dependence, and downgrade on any erosion of the Section 232 regime. **HOLD.**
