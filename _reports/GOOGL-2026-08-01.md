## Executive Summary

Alphabet's Q2 2026 results were the strongest operational quarter in the company's history and the stock fell sharply the following session. Both facts are correct, and reconciling them is the analytical work.

The operations are excellent. Consolidated revenue grew 24.2% to $119,796M, operating income grew 30.4% to $40,770M, and operating margin reached 34.0%. Google Search & other, the franchise most exposed to generative-AI substitution, grew 16.8% year over year to $63,271M on paid clicks +13% and cost-per-click +3%, an *acceleration* versus FY2025's pace. Google Cloud grew 81.8% to $24,768M with segment operating margin of 35.6%, up from 23.7% in FY2025 and 5.2% in FY2023. Cloud remaining performance obligations stand at $513.9B. On the revenue line, the bear case that AI assistants are eroding Search monetization is not visible.

What deteriorated is the earnings basis and the funding model. Other income of $97,983M in Q2 2026 included $99,031M of gains on equity securities, of which only $278M was realized; $77,354M was unrealized markup on non-marketable securities and $21,399M on marketable equity holdings. Across the trailing twelve months, equity-security gains of $148,982M represent 49.8% of $299,269M of pretax income. Strip them out and TTM core EPS is approximately $10.08 rather than the $19.93 GAAP figure — a multiple of roughly 35x, or about 33x on the operating business after netting out the investment portfolio, versus the ~17.8x the headline implies. Free cash flow has been flat for three consecutive years ($69.5B → $72.8B → $73.3B on revenue up 31%) and was approximately −$5.8B in Q2 2026. Alphabet raised $105.8B of external capital in the first half — $56.2B of notes, $30.5B of common stock, $19.1B of mandatory convertible preferred — and repurchased zero shares against a $69.5B remaining authorization.

The recommendation is HOLD. The operating business justifies a premium multiple and the Cloud/TPU trajectory has a visible 2027 tailwind. But at $356 the stock already carries roughly 33x core earnings and a ~1.2% trailing FCF yield, the incremental depreciation from $122,814M of assets not yet in service is a $20–31B annual charge not yet in run-rate earnings, the DOJ ad-tech remedies ruling has been pending for eight months with structural relief on the table, and management is issuing equity at $351–355 while suspending buybacks.

## Company Overview and a Data Reconciliation

Alphabet reports three segments plus an unallocated corporate cost center.

**Google Services** ($342,721M FY2025 revenue; $184,177M in 1H 2026) contains three very different businesses. Search & other ($224,532M FY2025; $63,271M in Q2 2026, +16.8%) is compounding faster than it did last year. YouTube ads reached $11,055M (+12.9%). Subscriptions, platforms and devices reached $12,911M (+15.2%). Google Network — third-party publisher monetization — is in structural decline: $31,312M (FY2023) → $30,359M (FY2024) → $29,792M (FY2025) → $7,303M in Q2 2026 (−0.7% YoY), with impressions down 12% against cost-per-impression up 13%.

**Google Cloud** grew from $43,229M (FY2024) to $58,705M (FY2025) to $24,768M in Q2 2026 alone. The definition of what Cloud sells changed this quarter: the 10-Q amended its revenue-recognition policy to add "Product sales, primarily the sale of TPU systems," and inventory rose 4.1x from $2,439M to $9,991M, consisting primarily of TPU hardware. Management states a limited number of TPU supply agreements have been signed with customers requiring on-premises infrastructure, with "the significant majority to be recognized in 2027." Alphabet is entering the AI systems hardware market as a merchant vendor, a business with working-capital intensity, warranty obligations, customer return rights and cyclicality that its services segments lack.

**Other Bets** lost $7,515M in FY2025 and $3,899M in 1H 2026, with losses widening. Waymo remains consolidated; a $16.0B round announced February 2026 is "significant majority funded by Alphabet." GFiber is being divested for $1.5B cash plus a $2.0B note and a 49.99% retained stake, with $7.1B of PP&E held for sale.

**Alphabet-level activities**, where frontier-model development spend sits, consumed $10,541M (FY2024) → $16,760M (FY2025) → $11,180M in 1H 2026 alone versus $6,399M in 1H 2025, +74.7%. This is the fastest-growing cost line in the company.

**Data reconciliation required before any multiple is meaningful.** The supplied market capitalization of $2.078T reconciles to Class A shares alone (5,868M × $354.20 = $2,078B). Alphabet had 12,230M shares outstanding across Class A (5,868M), Class B (835M) and Class C (5,527M) at June 30, 2026. At $356.13 the all-class capitalization is approximately $4.36 trillion. The supplied trailing/forward P/E of 16.74x is also internally inconsistent with the supplied EPS of $10.80 (which would give 33x); it approximates the ~17.8x computed on TTM *GAAP* EPS of $19.93. The peer table compounds this — GOOG Class C is shown at 27.2x P/E against GOOGL at 16.74x for identical underlying earnings. Every multiple below is computed from the all-class share count and stated on an explicit earnings basis.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is strong and specific.

**Search is accelerating, not eroding.** 16.8% growth with paid clicks +13% means volume, not just pricing, is driving the line. Whatever query share generative assistants are capturing, monetizable Google search engagement grew double digits. The genuine advertising weakness sits in Google Network (impressions −12%), which is publisher-side disintermediation and the same business the DOJ ad-tech case targets — limiting the revenue at risk from that case while not limiting the disruption.

**Cloud has an unusually visible forward book.** RPO of $513.9B at June 30 compares to $242.8B at December 31, 2025, but the figures are *not* comparable: in Q1 2026 Alphabet elected to include contracts with original expected terms of one year or less. This is ASC 606 remaining performance obligations, not pipeline or total backlog, and it excludes cancellable contracts. Taken at face value, more than 50% recognizing within 24 months implies more than $259.8B, or above $129.9B annually, against Cloud TTM revenue of $77,617M. That embeds substantial contracted growth and is the strongest single argument that the capex is demand-led rather than speculative.

**TPU systems create a disclosed 2027 tailwind.** The "significant majority" of signed TPU supply agreements recognizes in 2027, and inventory of $9,991M is already staged. Hardware carries lower and lumpier margins than consumption-based cloud, so the 35.6% Q2 segment margin should not be read as a clean run rate — MD&A attributes growth "primarily" to GCP infrastructure and platform services and "in addition" to TPU systems without quantifying the split.

**Supply, not demand, is currently binding.** Management framed the capex raise as a response to capacity shortage, and the Financial Times reported (June 28, 2026) that Google capped how much Gemini compute Meta — a purchasing customer — could buy because Google could not supply it. Favorable for near-term pricing; a warning about utilization if demand normalizes before $122.8B of not-yet-in-service assets is absorbed.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Current-year consensus EPS was revised +44.7% in 30 days, to $20.58. This does not qualify as an inflection signal; it is consensus mechanically absorbing the $99,031M non-cash markup into FY2026 GAAP EPS. Next-year consensus was revised only +1.7% over 30 days and +4.9% over 90 days, to $14.77 — consensus expects GAAP EPS to *fall* about 28% in 2027 as the markup does not repeat. Next-quarter revisions of +1.5%/+1.8% are the honest read on operating momentum: positive, modest, not an inflection.

**Score: 7.5.** Top-decile growth among megacaps, with contracted visibility. Offsets: Network erosion, Other Bets losses widening, Alphabet-level costs +74.7%, and the fact that growth is being purchased with roughly $200B of annual capex whose depreciation has not yet arrived in earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

| Basis | Computation | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| TTM GAAP EPS | $356.13 ÷ $19.93 | 17.9x |
| TTM core EPS (ex equity gains) | $356.13 ÷ ~$10.08 | ~35.3x |
| Enterprise value / TTM EBIT | ~$4,301B ÷ $147,628M | 29.1x |
| Operating EV / TTM EBIT (ex investment portfolio) | ~$4,116B ÷ $147,628M | 27.9x |
| Operating business per share / core EPS | ~$336.60 ÷ $10.08 | 33.4x |
| TTM FCF yield | $53.3B ÷ $4,356B | 1.22% |
| Dividend yield | | 0.26% |

The rigorous approach is to value the investment portfolio separately and the operating business on core earnings. Marketable and non-marketable equity holdings total roughly $211B gross — approximately $180–190B after the deferred tax claim, or about 4% of market capitalization. Netting that out leaves the operating business at roughly 33x core earnings and 28x EBIT.

Is 28x EBIT demanding for a business growing EBIT 30%? Not obviously. On a growth-adjusted basis it is defensible, and the 2027 TPU recognition and Cloud RPO give the forward estimates unusual support. But three adjustments push against the multiple. First, $122,814M of assets not yet in service (up from $78,592M in six months) will add roughly $20.5B of annual depreciation at a six-year blended life and $30.7B at four years — 14–21% of TTM operating income, and management modified a risk factor specifically to flag that useful lives could change. Second, trailing FCF yield is 1.22% and the forward figure is plausibly zero or negative; a business generating no distributable cash cannot be valued on an earnings multiple alone without accepting that the earnings are being reinvested at an unproven return. Third, next-year consensus of $14.77 appears to include some non-operating income; against a core 2027 estimate closer to $14.00–14.80 the stock is already at 24–25x.

**The strongest valuation evidence is management's own capital allocation.** Alphabet repurchased $45,709M of stock in FY2025 and $0 in each of Q1 and Q2 2026, with $69.5B of authorization remaining. In the same period it sold 86M shares at $355.20 (Class A) and $351.80 (Class C), placed $10.0B privately with National Indemnity (a Berkshire Hathaway affiliate) at the same pricing, issued $19.0B of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred, and registered a $40.0B at-the-market program. The company purchased $1.0B of capped calls struck at $532.67/$527.80 — 50% above the offering price — an anti-dilution measure and a statement about where management sees the ceiling. Alphabet is selling stock near $353, not buying it. The Berkshire participation is a genuine counterweight — an informed outside buyer paid $10B at this level — but it is a purchase of newly issued shares, i.e., Alphabet raising capital, not returning it.

Dilution, sized properly: the June offering added 86M shares, a 0.71% increase and a 0.70% reduction in existing proportional ownership. Preferred conversion adds 43.0M shares at the minimum rate to 53.8M at the maximum (0.35%–0.44%). A fully drawn $40B ATM at $356 adds ~112.3M (0.92% increase; 0.91% ownership dilution). Maximum contingent dilution from ATM plus maximum conversion is ~1.36% of share count. Material but not dramatic. Separately, a 294M-share unvested RSU/PSU pool (2.40% of shares, $59.1B unrecognized cost over 2.7 years) is an ongoing stream, and the ATM's stated purpose is funding the tax withholding on those vestings, which consumed $10,835M of cash in 1H 2026 alone.

**Peer context.** Meta trades at 20.7x and GOOG Class C is shown at 27.2x in the supplied table; the sector median is 20.7x. Given the earnings-basis inconsistencies in that table, the more useful comparison is Alphabet's ~33x core operating multiple against peer megacaps also in heavy capex. Alphabet's TPU vertical integration justifies some premium; its zero free cash flow does not.

**Score: 5.5.** Not a stretched multiple relative to growth, but not asymmetric. The GAAP optic of 17.9x — which is what the Phillip Securities upgrade citing "16x trailing P/E" rests on — embeds $149B of non-cash markups as if recurring. Analyst mean target of $426.95 implies about 20% upside, but those targets appear anchored to the same GAAP arithmetic; I do not treat them as independent confirmation. Consensus low is $340, essentially at spot.

## Competitive Landscape

**Improving:** Cloud grew 81.8% — clear share capture at scale, with margin expanding roughly 1,190bp year over year (14.1% FY2024 to 23.7% FY2025; 35.6% in Q2 2026). Alphabet designs and now sells its own accelerator, which is a structurally better position than pure GPU buyers when three or four hyperscalers are simultaneously committing ~$200B each to the same accelerator, memory, power and construction supply chains. The Intersect acquisition ($5.9B, closed March 10, 2026; $5,129M of PP&E) vertically integrates renewable power development for data centers. Wiz ($29.5B, closed March 11, 2026) adds cloud security, though goodwill of $22,705M is 77% of consideration, which makes future impairment testing consequential. No customer exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025, and goodwill plus intangibles are only 7.3% of assets — reported returns are not an artifact of acquisition accounting.

**Compressing:** The December 2025 final judgment in the DOJ Search case restricts how Google distributes its services and *requires Google to share certain search data with, and offer syndication services to, certain competitors*. That is a mandated reduction in the Search moat, now a live judgment on appeal rather than a hypothetical. Separately, the EU Digital Markets Act will require interoperable Android access for third-party AI companies — forced distribution for Gemini's competitors on Alphabet's own operating system. Google Network continues to erode.

**Score: 7.5.** The trajectory in Cloud and silicon is strongly positive and quantified; the trajectory in Search distribution rights is negative and legally mandated. Net improving, with the caveat that "no customer above 10%" is a threshold disclosure that says nothing about whether the top ten Cloud/TPU counterparties are concentrating — and large TPU contracts with "a limited number" of counterparties could concentrate Cloud revenue materially without breaching that threshold.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Contingent support for third-party infrastructure went from zero to ~$95B in eighteen months.** At June 30, 2026: $43,785M notional of credit derivatives backstopping *third parties'* data center payment obligations, up from $16,940M in December 2025 and $0 in December 2024, with terms to 15 years and an $815M fair-value liability; approximately $24.1B of additional agreed backstops pending finalization; $7,600M of financial guarantees supporting counterparties' power-equipment procurement; a $20,000M milestone-contingent equity funding commitment carried at a $457M loss; $85.2B of leases not yet commenced (from $58.5B); and $811.0B of purchase commitments (Note 10 separately cites $707.0B of expected fixed or guaranteed commitments on contracts beyond one year — different scopes, not interchangeable). Alphabet is guarantor, third-party developers are primary obligors, and lessors and equipment vendors are beneficiaries. A meaningful slice of the AI build therefore sits off Alphabet's balance sheet with the credit risk retained, so reported capex understates total economic exposure and reported leverage understates contingent leverage. The benign reading is that these are market-standard credit-support terms for smaller developers and that Alphabet's retained right to assume the leases converts a default into capacity it wants; the $815M carrying value against $43.8B notional implies management assesses default probability as low.

**2. Antitrust — one live judgment, one unresolved binary.** The Search final judgment (December 2025) is on appeal with DOJ and state cross-appeals. The ad-tech remedies decision has been pending since November 2025 closing arguments — eight months as of the July 2026 filing — with the 10-Q stating the DOJ's proposal "includes structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect" and that Alphabet "cannot estimate a possible loss." The EC's €3.0B ad-tech fine ($3.5B charged, satisfied with bank guarantees) is on appeal. Resolved against Google: the €4.1B Android fine became final in July 2026 and Alphabet paid $5.2B in cash (a Q3 2026 outflow); the Stockholm court awarded PriceRunner ~$2.1B, accrued in Q2 and under appeal. Accrued fines and settlements total $17,356M.

**3. Depreciation timing.** The $122,814M of assets not yet in service currently generate no revenue and carry no depreciation. Depreciation already rose to $13,586M in 1H 2026 from $9,485M. Presented as a sensitivity: $20.5B (six-year life) to $30.7B (four-year) of incremental annual charge. The offsetting case is that Cloud growing 82% absorbs it.

**4. Reversibility of the investment marks.** Marketable equity securities include $80.0B of SpaceX shares under short-term sale restrictions plus $14,126M restricted through Q3 2027 — $94.1B, none monetizable today. Non-marketable securities under the measurement alternative carry at $124,259M and "primarily consist of our investment in a private company," with $87.9B remeasured in Q2 2026 on Level 2 inputs. External reporting identifies the second position as Anthropic; the filing does not name it and that attribution should be treated as unconfirmed. Level 2 marks derived from a single observable financing round are inherently reversible, and roughly half of trailing pretax income depends on them.

**5. Management's own new risk language.** Three additions to the Q2 10-Q at a company that historically carried almost no financing risk disclosure: a new risk factor on disruptions in capital-markets access and managing indebtedness; a new dilution risk factor covering the ATM and preferred conversion; and a modified counterparty risk factor conceding that in the event of nonperformance "or industry challenges" Alphabet "may incur additional liabilities, have excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy, and not receive payments from our counterparties or customers." That phrasing is management acknowledging overbuild risk.

**6. Governance.** Class B shares (835M, 6.8% of shares) carry ten votes each — approximately 58.7% of voting power, held principally by the founders. The disclosed executive incentive framework contains a single performance measure, relative total shareholder return versus the S&P 100, with no ROIC, capital-efficiency or free-cash-flow metric. At the moment Alphabet is committing roughly $200B of annual capex, has taken FCF to approximately zero and is raising external capital at scale, pay is levered to share price only, and public Class A holders lack the votes to change it. Alphabet's TSR index of 360.95 versus a peer 138.27 indicates the framework has not misfired historically, and founder control enabled the long-horizon TPU and DeepMind bets now driving Cloud. It remains an alignment gap worth naming.

**Score: 5.0.** The ad-tech structural remedy is an unquantified binary with a timeline outside anyone's control. The contingent exposure structure is new, large, and not reflected in leverage metrics. The depreciation wave is arithmetically certain in direction and uncertain in magnitude.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $465 (+31%)

Search compounds at mid-teens indefinitely because AI features increase query volume rather than cannibalizing it, which is what paid clicks +13% already shows. Cloud converts its RPO at better than 40% growth through 2027 while TPU system revenue — the significant majority of which recognizes in 2027 — arrives at scale, making Alphabet the only hyperscaler that both consumes and sells its own accelerator. Owned power development via Intersect compounds the cost advantage. Capex peaks in 2026; operating cash flow reaches $220B+ in 2027 against $180B of capex, restoring $40B+ of free cash flow and reinstating buybacks. The ad-tech remedy lands behavioral rather than structural, and the business at issue is the one already shrinking. On 30x a 2027 core EPS of $15.50 the stock is $465. The Berkshire placement near $353 supports the level.

### Bear Case — $270 (−24%)

The $122.8B of not-yet-in-service assets is placed in service through 2027 on a four-to-five-year life, adding $25–30B of annual depreciation just as Cloud growth decelerates from an 82% comparison, compressing consolidated operating margin back toward 30%. Free cash flow stays negative through 2027, the ATM is drawn, and the equity issuance stream continues. The DOJ ad-tech remedies ruling requires divestiture of publisher tools, and the Search data-sharing and syndication mandates survive appeal, giving competitors access to the index that funds everything. Non-marketable marks reverse — the SpaceX position cannot be sold and a single Level 2 private-company mark of $124B is one bad financing round from a large writedown — and GAAP EPS collapses from $20.58 toward $12, which repricing on a 22x multiple puts the stock at $265. The absence of any capital-efficiency metric in compensation means nothing internally constrains the spend.

### What Would Change My Mind

The bull case requires one observable: free cash flow inflecting positive with capex growth decelerating. That is a Q4 2026 / Q1 2027 datapoint. If Q3 delivers positive FCF and management guides 2027 capex growth to single digits, this becomes a BUY at almost any price near $356, because the growth is contracted and the multiple is not extreme. Absent that, an investor is paying 33x core earnings for a business whose cash generation is fully committed and whose incremental returns are unmeasurable from outside.

**Bear case target: $270 (−24%).** 22x approximately $12.30, on margin compression from depreciation, structural ad-tech remedy, and mark reversals.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to three catalysts — the Q3 2026 print (late October) for the first read on whether Q2's negative FCF was seasonal or structural and on TPU inventory conversion; the DOJ ad-tech remedies ruling, which is already eight months overdue and could land at any time; and Q4 2026 guidance for 2027 capex.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow with capex growth guided below 15% for 2027.
2. Resumption of share repurchases at scale while the ATM remains undrawn — the cleanest signal that management views internal cash as no longer fully committed and the stock as a use of funds rather than a source.
3. Ad-tech remedies ruling limited to behavioral relief, combined with the price below $320 (roughly 30x core 2027 EPS).

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Cloud segment operating margin falling below 28% as TPU hardware mix dilutes, or Cloud revenue growth decelerating below 40% year over year.
2. Search & other revenue growth decelerating below 10% year over year — the first genuine evidence of AI query substitution, versus none today.
3. Credit derivative notional exceeding $70B, or the recognition of a material loss on the existing $43.8B, which would convert contingent exposure into realized cost.
4. A writedown exceeding $25B on the non-marketable measurement-alternative portfolio.

**Next natural reassessment: the Q3 2026 report in late October**, or immediately upon the ad-tech remedies ruling.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Alphabet is executing better operationally than at any point in its history: Search accelerating to 16.8%, Cloud at 81.8% growth with 35.6% segment margins, consolidated operating income +30.4%, and $513.9B of contracted Cloud performance obligations that make the capex demand-led rather than speculative. Nothing in the Q2 data supports the thesis that generative AI is eroding Search monetization.

The reason this is not a BUY at $356 is that the earnings basis and the funding model both changed this quarter, and neither change is reflected in the headline multiple. Half of trailing pretax income is a non-cash markup on two positions that cannot be sold, so the real multiple is roughly 33x core operating earnings rather than 18x GAAP. Free cash flow has been flat for three years and turned negative; $105.8B of external capital replaced $45.7B of annual buybacks; approximately $95B of contingent support for third-party data centers appeared from a base of zero eighteen months ago; and $122.8B of assets awaiting service will add $20–31B of annual depreciation to earnings that are already being valued at a premium. Management is selling shares near $353 and buying none.

Score of 6.4, down from 6.7, reflects the disclosed deterioration in cash generation, capital allocation and contingent exposure — not the share price, which is 12.8% below its 52-week high on fundamentals that improved. Own the position; do not add here. Add on evidence that free cash flow inflects, or below $320.
