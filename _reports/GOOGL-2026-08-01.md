## Executive Summary

Q2 2026 was the first period in which Alphabet's AI build was fully visible in the cash flow statement, and the picture differs from the one that supported four consecutive BUY calls. Revenue accelerated to $119.8B (+24.2% year over year), Google Cloud grew 82% with a 35.6% segment margin, and Google Services expanded to a 41.8% margin (approximately 43.4% excluding the $1.5B PriceRunner damages charge). Against that, operating cash flow of $39.1B was exceeded by capex of $44.9B, producing negative free cash flow of $5.8B. For the half, FCF was $4.3B versus $24.3B a year earlier. Capex ran at 35.1% of revenue in H1 2026 versus 22.7% in FY2025 and 15.0% in FY2024.

The financing response is the more informative signal. Alphabet repurchased zero shares in both Q1 and Q2 2026 (versus $28.3B in H1 2025), with $69.5B of authorization untouched, while issuing $30.5B of common equity in June (including a $10.0B private placement to a Berkshire Hathaway affiliate) plus $19.0B of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred, a $40B at-the-market program, and $56.2B of debt in the half. The arithmetic dilution is small: the June common issuance was 0.71% of shares, and maximum preferred conversion adds roughly 0.44%. A company holding ~$162.5B of genuinely liquid securities against ~$101.5B of debt chose a 6.25% preferred rather than drawing down liquidity. The most economical reading is that management wants maximum optionality for a capex program whose terminal size it cannot yet bound. Alphabet's own new risk factor concedes the possibility of "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy."

Reported GAAP EPS of $9.11 is not a measure of operating performance: $99.0B of the $112.2B quarterly net income was gains on equity securities, of which realized gains on securities actually sold were $278M. On an operating basis the quarter produced roughly $2.76–2.85 of EPS, up approximately 24% year over year, and it *missed* consensus by 4.4% following a 3.1% miss in Q1. At $356, the stock trades 24.1x FY2027 consensus of $14.77 against ~25% operating-earnings growth, a defensible multiple, but one that assumes the depreciation ramp from $122.8B of not-yet-in-service assets does not compress the 2027 earnings base. Moving to HOLD with an add zone in the low $300s.

## Company Overview

Alphabet reports three segments. **Google Services** (Q2 2026 revenue $94.5B, 79% of total; operating income $39.5B) comprises Search & other ($63.3B), YouTube ads ($11.1B), Network ($7.3B), subscriptions, platforms and devices. **Google Cloud** ($24.8B, +82%; operating income $8.8B) now includes product sales of TPU systems for on-premises deployment. **Other Bets** ($382M revenue, $1.8B operating loss) is principally Waymo, with GFiber held for sale pending a late-2026 close. A fourth bucket, Alphabet-level activities, carries shared frontier-AI R&D and absorbed $5.8B in the quarter and $11.2B in the half, up 74.7% year over year.

Two structural changes are new. First, Alphabet has become a merchant silicon vendor, recognizing initial TPU system revenue in Q2 2026 with "the significant majority to be recognized in 2027." Inventory rose from $2.44B to $9.99B in six months, primarily TPU hardware. Second, Alphabet has become a credit provider to third-party infrastructure: credit derivative backstops of $43.8B notional (from $16.9B at year-end), $7.6B of financial guarantees, and an additional estimated $24.1B of backstops agreed subject to finalization with data center providers.

Advertising fell from 74% of consolidated revenue in Q2 2025 to 68% in Q2 2026 as Cloud rose from 14% to 21%. Control remains concentrated: Page and Brin hold approximately 52.7% of voting power.

**A market-data note.** The supplied market capitalization of $2.078T corresponds to Class A shares alone (5,868M × ~$354). Total equity value across A, B and C is approximately $4.36T at $356.13. All multiples below use the full 12,230M share count (12,309M diluted).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 +8.7%, FY2024 +13.9%, FY2025 +15.1%, Q1 2026 +21.8%, Q2 2026 +24.2%. The acceleration is a Cloud story. Decomposed:

| Line | Q1 2026 YoY | Q2 2026 YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Google Cloud | +63.4% | +81.8% |
| Search & other | +19.1% | +16.8% |
| Google Services | +16.0% | +14.5% |
| Google Network | — | −0.7% |

Search decelerated roughly 230bps sequentially, and its composition changed: FY2025 was price-led (paid clicks +6%, CPC +7%); Q2 2026 was volume-led (paid clicks +13%, CPC +3%). More engagement at lower average value per interaction is consistent with AI-driven surfaces expanding ad-eligible inventory, but management attributes growth to mobile query volume, advertiser spend, and ad-format improvements without AI-specific attribution. Treat the mix shift as fact and the causation as unverified.

**Margins.** Consolidated operating margin was 32.1% (FY2024), 32.0% (FY2025), 36.1% (Q1 2026), 34.0% (Q2 2026). Segment margins improved more than consolidated because Alphabet-level AI R&D is absorbing the difference at a ~$23B annualized run rate. Cloud margin went 14.1% → 23.7% → 35.6%; Services 39.8% → 40.7% → 41.8%. The Q2 operating margin missed sell-side expectations even as revenue beat, which follows from that arithmetic.

**Free cash flow — the decisive item.**

| Period | OCF | Capex | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $125,299M | $52,485M | $72,814M |
| FY2025 | $164,713M | $91,447M | $73,266M |
| H1 2025 | $63,897M | $39,643M | $24,254M |
| H1 2026 | $84,859M | $80,598M | $4,261M |
| Q2 2026 | $39,100M | $44,900M | −$5,800M |

Two points deserve emphasis. FY2025 FCF was only 0.6% above FY2024 despite 15% revenue growth and 32% reported net income growth — the deterioration began in 2025. And operating cash flow quality is not impaired: the H1 statement properly reverses $135.8B of securities gains and adds back $27.5B of deferred tax, leaving OCF up 32.8% against revenue up 23.1%. The FCF deterioration is a capex phenomenon, not an accrual-quality one. H1 also absorbed a $7.7B inventory build for a brand-new hardware business and $6.9B of receivables growth, so the −$5.8B quarter should not be extrapolated as a run rate. Full-year capex guidance of $195–205B is externally reported; the 10-Q says only "significantly increase, relative to 2025."

**The depreciation cliff.** H1 depreciation of property and equipment was $13.6B (~$27.2B annualized) against $80.6B of capex, capex running ~5.9x depreciation. Assets not yet in service reached $122,814M at June 30, up from $78,592M at December 31. Depreciation begins only on placement in service. As a labeled sensitivity, commissioning that pool over two years at the ~9.5% implied in-service depreciation rate would add roughly $12B of annual depreciation before any further 2026–2027 additions, equal to roughly $0.75 of pre-tax EPS on 12.3B diluted shares. Offsets exist: Alphabet has historically extended server useful lives, buildings and land depreciate over far longer schedules, and revenue may grow into the charge. The FY2025 risk factors explicitly flag useful-life revision risk. This is the single largest quantifiable threat to the FY2027 consensus of $14.77.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets grew from $595.3B to $922.0B in six months. Current ratio 2.72; equity/assets 69.5%; total liabilities/equity 0.44; debt/equity ~15.8%. One caveat matters: of $242.5B in "cash and marketable securities," $80.0B is sale-restricted SpaceX stock, with a further $14.1B restricted through Q3 2027 in other non-current assets. Genuinely liquid cash and securities are approximately $162.5B, against ~$101.5B of debt, a net liquid position near $61B rather than the headline $141B. Debt trades below par ($101.1B face versus ~$94.9B fair value), and the company drew $1.3B on credit facilities at SOFR +1.5–2.25% having previously carried nothing.

**Commitments.** Purchase and contractual obligations rose from $149.1B to $811.0B in two quarters ($200.7B short-term), with $707.0B in contracts longer than one year, generally fulfilled through 2030. Add $85.2B of leases not yet commenced, $43.8B of credit derivative notional, $7.6B of guarantees, $24.1B of agreed additional backstops, and a $20.0B milestone-contingent funding commitment. The components approach $1.0 trillion with some overlap and are largely contingent, amortizing, or offsettable. The velocity is the point.

**Earnings quality.** Q2 net income of $112.2B included $99.0B of equity securities gains: $77.5B unrealized on non-marketable holdings under the measurement alternative, $21.4B unrealized on marketable securities, $278M realized on sales. Non-marketable carrying value of $124.3B sits against $47.6B of initial cost, valued using option-pricing and market-comparable methods (Level 2/3). The 10-Q names SpaceX; the second large holding is described only as "a private company" (widely reported as Anthropic, unconfirmed in filings). Marks are GAAP-mandated on observable transactions, not discretionary, and a down round would reverse through the income statement with equal force.

## Growth Analysis

The top line is accelerating and the mix is improving. Cloud at 82% growth with a 35.6% margin is outgrowing Azure (reported at ~40% in the comparable quarter) and carries a $513.9B remaining performance obligation. That figure requires care: Alphabet changed the definition in Q1 2026 to include contracts with original terms of one year or less, so it is not comparable to prior periods, it excludes cancellable contracts, and it is not "total backlog." Management expects just over 50% to be recognized in 24 months, roughly $130B/year against Cloud revenue annualizing at $99.1B. Deferred revenue, a distinct and far smaller measure, was $10.1B.

TPU systems add a second growth vector with 2027-weighted recognition. This monetizes a decade of silicon investment and reaches customers who will not use public cloud. It also introduces inventory risk (already +$7.6B), a lower-margin hardware structure, return rights, and, per the new risk factor, increased "costs and operational complexity."

On the earnings line, the picture is more mixed than headline revisions suggest. Consensus FY2026 EPS jumped from $13.90 to $20.58 over 90 days (+48.1%), but that is a mechanical restatement for unforecastable mark-to-market gains, not operating-earnings momentum. The relevant revision is FY2027, up 4.9% over 90 days to $14.77. Summing quarterly operating-basis actuals and estimates gives FY2026 of approximately $11.80 ($2.62 + $2.85 + $3.00 + $3.33), implying ~25% growth into FY2027. The large-revision buy signal that normally accompanies +30% 90-day moves does not apply here, because the revision reflects balance-sheet marks rather than an operating inflection, and the operating line has now missed twice consecutively (−3.1% in Q1, −4.4% in Q2).

Growth durability is high at the segment level and less certain at the consolidated EPS level, because two costs are scaling faster than revenue: Alphabet-level AI R&D (+74.7% in H1) and, prospectively, depreciation. Score: 7.5.

Against Q2 operating income annualized ($40.7B × 4 = $163B) that is ~26.4x EV/EBIT; using a full-year estimate of $175–180B that includes seasonally stronger Q4 advertising, roughly 24x. On unadjusted annualized GAAP EPS the multiple is ~12x, which is not a meaningful figure.

Two considerations temper reliance on that: the low target sits *below* spot, and the consensus mean is being set against an FY2026 EPS number inflated by marks. On a stock at 24x forward with a specific, quantifiable cost headwind (depreciation) not yet embedded in the 2027 number, consensus targets are corroboration at best, not a floor.

My base case applies 27x to FY2027 $14.77 = **$399**, roughly in line with the multiple the market currently pays for the Class C shares and consistent with a business compounding operating EPS in the mid-20s with expanding segment margins. That is 12% above spot. Bull case: 30x on $15.50 (TPU revenue exceeding plan, ad tech remedy resolving behaviorally) = **$465**. The asymmetry from $356 is +12% to base, +31% to bull, −27% to bear. That distribution does not support a BUY. Score: 6.5 — fairly valued for the quality and growth, with no identifiable mispricing large enough to overcome the risk set.

## Competitive Landscape

Cloud is gaining share on the filings' own numbers, with 82% growth, a margin that has more than doubled in eighteen months, and the Wiz acquisition ($29.5B, 77% of consideration allocated to goodwill) adding security capability. The vertically integrated silicon stack is a genuine differentiator, and selling TPU systems externally puts Alphabet into NVIDIA's addressable market with a cost structure competitors cannot replicate quickly.

Search is the weaker leg. Growth decelerated from 19.1% to 16.8%; Network revenue is shrinking (impressions −12%, cost-per-impression +13%); and the December 2025 DOJ Search judgment requires sharing search data with, and offering syndication to, certain competitors. The EU Digital Markets Act now also contemplates mandated search-data sharing and interoperable Android access for third-party AI companies. Those are moat-narrowing obligations, not fines.

At the model layer, external reporting (Bloomberg via multiple secondary outlets) says Gemini 3.5 Pro is months behind schedule with particular weakness on coding, with Alphabet shipping Gemini 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite instead in July. One analyst characterized the perception shift as "leading edge to trailing edge." None of this appears in Alphabet's filings, and it sits against Gemini approaching a billion users. Separately, OpenAI cut per-token pricing by up to 80% in July, with Google responding via lower prices plus reduced token use. Commoditizing inference pricing is directly relevant to whether ~$200B of annual capex earns its cost of capital.

Net trajectory: Cloud improving materially, Search under simultaneous regulatory and AI-substitution pressure, model leadership contested. Score: 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Ad tech structural remedy (largest binary).** The Eastern District of Virginia found in April 2025 that Google's publisher tools unfairly excluded rivals. Remedies were argued in September 2025, closing arguments held November 2025, and as of the July 23, 2026 10-Q Google was still awaiting final judgment. The filing states directly: "The DOJ's remedy proposal includes structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect on our business," with no estimable loss. A Texas state AG trial follows. This is the highest-value fact to refresh before adding capital.

**Litigation as a recurring expense line.** Accrued fines and settlements reached $17.4B (from $15.6B). The €4.1B Android fine became final and Google paid $5.2B in cash in July. The Stockholm Patent and Market Court awarded PriceRunner ~$2.1B in a private follow-on action ($1.5B to Services G&A, $581M to other income). The 10-Q notes pending private claims across several jurisdictions, including cases where liability is already fully determined and only damages remain. PriceRunner is a template.

**Newly added risk-factor language.** The Q2 10-Q added disclosures on TPU supply increasing "costs and operational complexity"; on backstops to third-party data centers and power infrastructure where nonperformance could leave Alphabet with "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy"; on access to debt and equity markets "including through our ATM Program," warning that obligations "combined with the dilutive impact of current or future equity issuances, could harm our financial condition, depress our stock price"; on dilution from the ATM and preferred; and softened buyback language ("no assurances that we will make repurchases in the near term or at all").

**Undisclosed concentration in the growth lines.** TPU supply rests on "a limited number of agreements," and neither TPU nor the $513.9B Cloud backlog is broken out by counterparty. No customer has ever exceeded 10% of revenue, but segment diversification (advertising 74% → 68%) says nothing about customer concentration in the newest lines, which may have worsened. If a large equity investee is also a large Cloud/TPU customer, that circularity would be worth quantifying; the filings do not permit it.

**Depreciation and energy commitments.** Covered above; add take-or-pay energy agreements with substantive termination fees running to 2054.

**Governance.** Page and Brin hold ~52.7% of voting power; Class C is non-voting. Compensation is measured on relative TSR with no disclosed return-on-capital or FCF metric during the largest capex program in company history. A reasonable counter-reading is that multi-year relative TSR will ultimately capture whether the AI build earns its cost of capital.

Score: 5.0. The remedy ruling alone is a live, unquantified binary with structural-divestiture language in the filing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $465 (+31%)

Cloud sustains 50%+ growth on a $513.9B contracted pipeline while margins continue climbing from 35.6% toward the 40s, and TPU system revenue — with the majority of signed agreements recognizing in 2027 — establishes Alphabet as the credible second merchant AI silicon vendor at materially better gross margins than the market assumes for hardware. Search reaccelerates as AI surfaces monetize (paid clicks already +13%), Services margin holds above 43% ex-charges, and the depreciation ramp is absorbed by revenue growth plus useful-life extensions. The ad tech remedy resolves behaviorally rather than structurally, removing the largest overhang. FY2027 EPS reaches $15.50 and the multiple expands to 30x as free cash flow inflects positive in 2027 when capex growth decelerates against a $250B+ revenue base. Buybacks resume against the $69.5B authorization.

### Bear Case — $260 (−27%)

The court orders divestiture of the publisher ad stack, forcing a multi-year restructuring of a business embedded in Services' 41.8% margin, while DMA search-data-sharing and Android interoperability mandates erode query share. Depreciation from the $122.8B not-yet-in-service pool plus 2026–2027 additions adds $15–20B of annual expense into a decelerating advertising line, compressing consolidated margin below 30%. Model-layer price competition (OpenAI's 80% token cuts) commoditizes inference, so ~$200B of annual capex earns a return below cost of capital and the $43.8B of backstops plus $811B of purchase commitments become sources of loss rather than optionality, with Alphabet's own risk factor language on "excess capacity we cannot easily redeploy" proving prescient. A down round at a major private investee reverses tens of billions of unrealized gains through the income statement, and FY2027 EPS lands near $13.00 on a derated 20x multiple.

### Why HOLD rather than BUY

The four prior BUY calls rested on a combination that has partly broken: 20%+ Cloud growth, expanding margins, and self-funded capex generating $70B+ of annual free cash flow while buybacks shrank the share count. The first two remain intact. The third has reversed: FCF is now negative on a quarterly basis, buybacks are zero, and the share count is rising modestly rather than falling. That is a fundamental change confirmed by the cash flow statement, not an inference from price action. The score decline from 7.7 (February) to 6.7 now has hard-data support in financial health (7.5 → 7.0), management quality (6.5 → 6.0), and risk (→ 5.0), while competitive position holds at 7.5 on Cloud's strength.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — 2027 TPU revenue recognition, Cloud margin expansion, and remedy resolution are identifiable catalysts — but the entry price does not compensate for the binary.

**Base case: $399 (+12%).** 27x FY2027 consensus EPS of $14.77, in line with the multiple currently paid for the Class C shares and appropriate for mid-20s operating-EPS growth with expanding segment margins.

**Bull case: $465 (+31%).** 30x on $15.50 FY2027 EPS.

**Bear case: $260 (−27%).** 20x on $13.00 FY2027 EPS.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the ad tech final judgment (pending; could issue at any time), the Q3 2026 print (late October, when 2027 capex is likely to be framed), and Q1–Q2 2027 results, when the majority of TPU system revenue recognizes.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price in the low $300s (roughly 21x FY2027 consensus) with FY2027 estimates holding at or above $14.50 — that combination restores 25%+ upside to base case.
2. The ad tech remedy resolving without structural divestiture of the publisher ad stack.
3. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow with capex growth decelerating, or resumption of buybacks against the $69.5B authorization — evidence the funding regime shift was cyclical rather than open-ended.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A structural divestiture order covering publisher ad tech, with Services margin guidance revised below 38%.
2. Search & other growth decelerating below 12% year over year in any quarter, indicating AI substitution is reaching the monetized query base.
3. Cloud growth falling below 40% year over year, or disclosure that TPU/Cloud revenue depends on a customer exceeding 10% of segment revenue combined with evidence that customer is also an Alphabet equity investee.
4. Any drawdown on the $43.8B credit derivative notional or a write-down on the $122.8B not-yet-in-service asset pool.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediately upon the ad tech final judgment; otherwise the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October 2026), with particular attention to Q3 free cash flow, the not-yet-in-service balance, depreciation expense, and whether repurchases resume.

For holders who want exposure through the ruling, near-term implied volatility at 25.4% against 31.7% realized makes long optionality cheaper than usual relative to owning the stock outright.

## Conclusion

Alphabet is executing well on the two things it controls: Cloud is compounding at 82% with a 35.6% margin, Services margin reached 41.8% (43.4% ex-charge), operating cash flow grew 32.8% against 23.1% revenue growth, and operating-basis EPS grew roughly 24%. The balance sheet remains strong in absolute terms: 69.5% equity/assets, ~$162.5B of genuinely liquid securities against ~$101.5B of debt.

What changed in Q2 2026 is the cost of that execution. Capex at 35.1% of revenue turned free cash flow negative, buybacks stopped, $49.5B of common and preferred equity was issued including an expensive 6.25% preferred, purchase commitments grew from $149.1B to $811.0B in two quarters, off-balance-sheet backstops to third-party data center and energy developers rose to $43.8B, and $122.8B of assets sits waiting to begin depreciating. Alphabet added risk-factor language acknowledging it could end up with capacity it cannot easily redeploy. Layered on top is an ad tech remedy ruling with structural-divestiture language in the filing and no estimable loss.

At 24x FY2027 consensus with 12% upside to a base case and 27% downside to a bear case that hinges on a pending court ruling, the risk/reward no longer supports adding. Own the position; add in the low $300s, on resolution of the ad tech remedy without structural relief, or on demonstrated free-cash-flow inflection. **HOLD, 6.7/10.**
