## Executive Summary

United Bankshares delivered a record second quarter — net income of $131.4M, diluted EPS of $0.95 against a $0.90 consensus, ROA of 1.56% and ROTCE of 15.15% — and the stock has responded, trading within 2.3% of its 52-week high of $49.62 and 36% above where it sat twelve months ago. The balance sheet behind that print is one of the more conservative in the $30B-asset cohort: CET1 of 13.3%, tangible common equity at 10.92% of tangible assets, a 92.0% loan-to-deposit ratio, $699.6M of total borrowings against $27.2B of deposits, and 24.8% noninterest-bearing deposit mix. Net charge-offs ran 0.08% annualized in Q2 and non-performing assets are 0.36% of assets. The dividend was raised for a 52nd consecutive year and consumes 41% of earnings.

The difficulty is the composition of growth. Loans grew $285.4M in the first half of 2026 — 1.16% in six months, roughly 2.3% annualized — and deposits grew $109.8M, about 0.8% annualized. Reported H1 EPS growth of +27% shrinks to roughly +13.7% once the $31.3M of Piedmont-related merger expense and Day-2 CECL provision in H1 2025 is normalized out, and about 2.4 points of that is a lower share count rather than more earnings. The three drivers doing the work — interest-bearing deposit costs down 38bp year over year, a securities book being rebuilt at higher yields after three years of runoff, and a 3.50% reduction in shares outstanding — are each finite. Purchase-accounting accretion, which contributed $33.7M to FY2025 net interest income, has decayed to a $5.0M quarterly rate and cost roughly 4bp of sequential spread in Q2 alone. The repurchase authorization approved in November 2025 has an estimated ~1.56M shares left, roughly 1.1% of the float, about two quarters at the Q2 pace.

At $48.455 the shares trade at 13.1x the $3.69 current-year consensus, 12.6x the $3.86 next-year consensus, 1.204x book value of $40.24 and 1.916x tangible book of $25.29. That is a modest discount to the 13.7x regional-bank peer median and a 34bp yield premium, but it is not a discount to intrinsic value: 1.92x tangible book on a 15.15% ROTCE is approximately what a 9.5-10% cost of equity and 3% long-run growth support. Consensus mean target is $50.00 with a low of $48.00 — below the current price. Our probability-weighted expected return, including the dividend, is roughly 3%. We maintain HOLD, on income grounds, and would not put new capital to work above the mid-$40s.

## Company Overview

United Bankshares is a West Virginia-incorporated financial holding company formed in 1982, operating a single bank subsidiary — United Bank, a Virginia state-chartered Federal Reserve member — plus non-bank units in asset management, title insurance, financial planning, and brokerage. Consolidated assets were $33.75B at 6/30/26 with $5.51B of shareholders' equity, across 240-plus offices in the District of Columbia, Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Georgia. Headcount was 2,694 at year-end 2025.

The operating model is serial acquisition layered on community banking. The FY2025 10-K states the company has acquired thirty-three banking institutions since formation and that management "from time to time makes inquiries, proposals, or expressions of interest as to potential opportunities." The recent sequence — Virginia Commerce (2014), Bank of Georgetown (2016), Cardinal Financial (2017), Carolina Financial (2020, $5.0B), Community Bankers Trust (2021, $1.8B), and Piedmont Bancorp (January 2025, $2.4B assets / $2.1B loans / $2.1B deposits / 16 Georgia locations) — has moved the franchise outward from Appalachia into the D.C. corridor, the Carolinas, and now Atlanta. West Virginia law caps any in-state acquisition that would give the acquirer more than 25% of state deposits, which explains the outward path.

The company reported to two segments through 2023, consolidated George Mason and Crescent mortgage origination into United Bank during Q1 2024 after exiting third-party origination, and now reports a single operating and reportable segment. Mortgage is ancillary: $370.9M originated for sale in 2025, $9.6M of net gains, servicing not retained, and $2.9M of mortgage banking income in Q2 2026. The single-segment structure means no state- or MSA-level loan and deposit disclosure, which matters because the D.C./Northern Virginia corridor is both the highest-value part of the franchise and where 2025's credit loss occurred.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings and margin

The 2021-2024 stretch produced a 7% cumulative EPS decline on roughly flat net income — share count was rising through acquisitions. 2025 broke that (+18.9%), helped by Piedmont and a 29bp NIM expansion to 3.78% FTE from 3.49%.

NIM has since gone flat and stayed there: 3.81% in Q2 2025, 3.80% in Q1 2026, 3.81% in Q2 2026. Underneath the flat line, three forces are in tension. Interest-bearing deposit costs fell 38bp year over year and cost of funds fell another 3bp sequentially. The yield on average net loans fell 27bp year over year and 6bp sequentially. Acquired-loan accretion fell $6.8M year over year and $2.5M sequentially to $5.0M, which management attributed roughly 4bp of sequential spread compression to. The new offset is securities: yield up 19bp sequentially on "strategic purchases of higher yielding investment securities throughout 2026," with the portfolio rebuilt from $3.400B at 12/31/25 to $3.659B at 6/30/26 after securities income had fallen from $151.1M (2023) to $133.3M (2024) to $112.0M (2025).

The arithmetic is that NIM has held only because deposit relief slightly outran loan-yield decline plus accretion runoff. That configuration depends on the direction of short rates. Roughly $4.46B of time deposits mature during 2026 and time deposits over $100k grew 39.61% in 2025, so a further repricing tranche exists — but the tailwind necessarily narrows as the book reaches market, and it reverses outright if policy tightens. The rate path is genuinely two-sided rather than a reliable tailwind.

### Interim results and the normalization

H1 2026 net income was $255.6M and diluted EPS $1.83, against H1 2025 net income of $205.0M and EPS of $1.44 — a reported +27%. H1 2025 carried $12.6M of Piedmont merger expense and $18.7M of Day-2 CECL provision on purchased non-PCD loans, $31.3M pre-tax. At the 20.85% H1 2025 effective rate that is $24.8M after tax, or $0.17 on 142,465,543 H1 2025 diluted shares. Normalized H1 2025 EPS was therefore approximately $1.61, and normalized growth approximately +13.7%. Diluted shares fell 2.32% period over period, contributing about 2.4 points, leaving roughly 11% underlying earnings growth. The same adjustment applies to provision: ex-Piedmont, H1 2025 provision was $16.3M versus $12.7M in H1 2026, a 22% decline rather than the headline 64%.

Q2's record was not materially manufactured. Noninterest income of $38.5M (+22% YoY) included a $5.9M gain on the sale of an unaffiliated investee, a $5.7M VISA share-exchange gain (of which $1.8M realized, remainder eligible for Q3 sale), $1.0M of unrealized equity gains, and a $9.7M loss on the sale of $81.0M of AFS securities — netting roughly +$2.9M pre-tax, about +$2.3M or $0.017 per share after the 19.96% effective rate. Roughly 2 cents of the 6-cent sequential EPS gain traces to discrete securities items. Brokerage fees were a genuine bright spot at $6.764M, +39% year over year, and data processing expense fell to $14.1M in H1 2026 from $16.4M on contract renegotiations. Efficiency ratio was 47.8% in Q2 ($154.7M expense on $323.8M of revenue) and 48.0% for H1 — respectable, not top-quartile.

FY2025's charge-off spike was idiosyncratic: the 10-K discloses a $21.77M charge-off on two commercial real estate non-owner-occupied office loans in Northern Virginia extended to the same sponsor, 48% of the year's losses from one relationship. H1 2026 charge-offs of $10.8M confirm normalization.

Two forward markers deserve attention. Allowance coverage of non-performing loans has fallen three quarters running — $297.5M/$101.5M = 293%, then 291%, then $299.5M/$110.6M = 271% — with the allowance ratio held at exactly 1.20% and the reserve built only $2.0M over six months against a $9.1M NPL increase. That can be entirely appropriate if CECL macro inputs improved and the new non-accruals are well collateralized, and 8-9bp annualized charge-offs against a 120bp reserve supports adequacy. It is nonetheless a trend rather than a point. Second, the Q1 10-Q disclosed CRE non-owner-occupied loans of $276.7M special mention and $181.3M substandard, $458.0M combined, roughly 5.5% of that book; the Q2 figure is not available because the Q2 10-Q had not been filed at the time of this analysis.

Concentration is the structural feature. Real estate and construction are 48.16% of loans ($11.9B); CRE non-owner-occupied is roughly 33.75% (~$8.34B) and construction and land development roughly 14.45% (~$3.57B). Backing into risk-weighted assets from the reported 13.3% CET1 ratio (RWA approximately $27-29B, total risk-based capital approximately $4.2-4.5B at the reported 15.6% ratio) implies a regulatory CRE concentration ratio near 265-285% and a construction ratio near 79-85%. Both sit below the 300%/100% supervisory attention thresholds, but the CRE measure is not comfortably below. These are estimates, not company disclosures; a materially different RWA would move them, potentially across the threshold. Separately, the 10-K discloses "one such [NAICS] industry classification that exceeded 10% of total loans" without naming it — an unidentified exposure of $2.5B or more.

### Capital and capital allocation

Tangible common equity at 6/30/26 was $3,463.1M ($5,510.5M equity less $2,018.8M goodwill and $28.6M other intangibles), or $25.29 per share, 10.92% of tangible assets. Goodwill equals 58.3% of tangible common equity, which is the entire gap between the 15.15% ROTCE and the 9.53% GAAP ROE.

Capital return has been heavy. FY2025: 3,587,948 shares at an average $35.24 ($126.4M) plus $1.49 of dividends. H1 2026: approximately 3.2M shares at $41.78 (~$133.7M) plus $0.76 of dividends, with Q2 alone at ~1.5M shares at $43.93. Shares outstanding fell from 141,909,452 at 6/30/25 to 136,942,149, down 3.50%. Over FY2025 plus H1 2026, roughly $575M was returned against $720.2M earned — about 80% of earnings — and shareholders' equity moved only from $5,496.0M to $5,510.5M.

Yet CET1 remains 13.3% against a 7.0% fully-buffered minimum, implying roughly $1.7-1.8B of surplus. No acquisition has been announced in 2026 — the last deal 8-K was the Piedmont closing on 2025-01-10 — and remaining buyback capacity is an estimated ~1.56M shares, about 1.1% of the float. A bank earning 15% on tangible equity, returning 80% of it, growing loans 2%, and still accreting capital faces a decision: a new acquisition, a materially larger authorization, or a step change in the dividend. Management offered no guidance on the point in the Q2 release, and no earnings call transcript was available.

One note on buyback economics. At $43.93 against $25.29 of tangible book, repurchases were executed at 1.74x TBV — accretive to EPS at a 7.6% earnings yield, above the return on idle cash, but well below the 15% ROTCE the franchise earns when it can deploy capital into loans or a well-priced deal. The 2025 repurchases at $35.24 were considerably better value than the 2026 ones.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has current-year EPS at $3.69 (+2.3% over 30 days, +2.1% over 90 days) and next year at $3.86 (+1.6%/+1.7%), implying 2027 growth of 4.6%. Six analysts cover. The revision trend is positive and consistent with four straight beats (+5.0%, +2.7%, +3.4%, +10.0%), which supports confidence in the $3.69/$3.86 path. It does not remotely resemble the kind of inflection revision velocity that signals systematic consensus underestimation — a 2% ninety-day revision on a bank compounding 2% loans is confirmation of steadiness, not evidence of a re-rating catalyst.

The growth bridge from here is straightforward and mostly finite:

- **Deposit repricing.** The largest single contributor. Roughly $4.46B of time deposits mature during 2026, so more relief is available, but each repriced tranche narrows the remaining gap to market. If the Fed hikes, this reverses while loan yields continue drifting down.
- **Securities reinvestment.** The most durable positive. Three years of runoff took securities income from $151.1M to $112.0M; the portfolio is now growing again at yields 19bp higher sequentially. This is a multi-year tailwind that is only beginning, and the one leg of the story that could still surprise favorably.
- **Accretion runoff.** A mechanical headwind. From $33.7M in FY2025 to a $5.0M quarterly rate, decaying ~$2.5M per quarter. If it normalizes near $3M per quarter, the residual drag is roughly 3bp of NIM. Only a new acquisition replenishes it.
- **Share count.** 3.50% year over year, but with ~1.1% of shares left in authorization.
- **Fee lines.** Brokerage at +39% year over year and +35% for H1 is genuine operating growth off a small base ($14.2M H1). Trust and deposit service fees grew low-to-mid single digits.
- **Organic loans and deposits.** 2.3% and 0.8% annualized. With mortgage rates elevated, the residential and construction pipeline is unlikely to accelerate materially in the next several quarters.
- **M&A.** The unquantifiable lever. $1.7-1.8B of surplus CET1, a 33-deal history, and a franchise sitting below the $100B Basel-endgame threshold make United a credible consolidator. Nothing has been announced, and the 2023 CRA evaluation was "Satisfactory" rather than "Outstanding," which is a modest friction in merger approval for an acquisition-dependent company.

2027 looks structurally harder than 2026 unless a deal lands or loan demand inflects. Growth Prospects scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

United's markets are structurally fragmented and the 10-K's competition disclosure is candid: 61 registered bank holding companies in West Virginia, 93 in Virginia, 64 in North Carolina, 66 in South Carolina, and 158 in Georgia. Virginia permits unlimited in-state branching with approval and reciprocal interstate acquisitions, conditions the filing says "serve to intensify competition."

The franchise is barbelled. Funding comes disproportionately from the five largest West Virginia MSAs plus adjacent Ohio and Pennsylvania counties — slow-growth, low-cost deposit territory. Asset growth comes from the acquired Mid-Atlantic and Southeast markets: the D.C./Northern Virginia/Maryland corridor, Richmond, Baltimore/Annapolis, coastal and upstate South Carolina, coastal and Piedmont North Carolina, and now Atlanta. That structure produces the observed combination — a 92% loan/deposit ratio, near-zero wholesale funding, and a 3.81% NIM at a bank with 2% organic loan growth.

There is a less flattering reading of the same structure: that the legacy markets are in secular deposit decline and acquisitions are partly defensive, replacing shrinking core funding. Single-segment reporting with no state or MSA disclosure makes the two hypotheses indistinguishable from the filings. What is observable is that organic loan and deposit growth of 2.3% and 0.8% annualized does not indicate share gains in any of the growth markets.

One structural advantage is real and quantifiable: at $33.75B of assets United sits above the $10B CFPB supervision threshold but well below $100B, and the 10-K states explicitly that the proposed Basel III endgame capital revisions "would not apply to United or United Bank." That is a durable cost-of-capital edge over the $100B-plus cohort and a reason mid-cap consolidation remains economically attractive for acquirers of this size.

The absence of any claimed technology, product, or cost advantage in Item 1 is itself informative. The defensible advantages are a thick capital base, a granular low-cost deposit franchise, and a repeatable integration process. Those are worth something; they are not widening. Competitive Position scores 5.3 — stable, with organic trajectory slightly negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration with demonstrated single-name granularity.** Real estate and construction are 48.16% of loans; CRE non-owner-occupied is roughly $8.34B and construction/land development roughly $3.57B. Estimated regulatory concentration of 265-285% of total risk-based capital is below but not far below the 300% supervisory threshold. **Reserve coverage trend.** ALLL/NPL coverage has fallen from 293% to 271% across three quarters with the allowance ratio pinned at 1.20% and a $2.0M six-month build against a $9.1M NPL increase. Loans 90-plus days past due and still accruing rose from $4.97M to $11.66M during Q1. Charge-offs at 8-9bp support current adequacy; the direction of travel warrants monitoring at each print.

**Rate-path asymmetry.** The single largest 2026 earnings driver — 38bp of deposit-cost relief — depends on short rates falling. A rate hike would reverse the deposit tailwind while loan-yield repricing continues downward. This is a genuinely two-sided risk rather than a directional tailwind. *(Note: the specific FOMC meeting-count, dissent count, and payroll figures cited in the draft are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed.)*

**Goodwill.** $2,018.8M against $3,463.1M of tangible common equity. An impairment would reduce GAAP equity and reported earnings but not tangible common equity or regulatory capital, since goodwill is already deducted from both.

**Undisclosed concentration.** The 10-K acknowledges one NAICS industry classification exceeding 10% of total loans without naming it — over $2.5B of exposure of unknown type.

**Regulatory and structural.** The parent's principal liquidity source is dividends from United Bank, subject to Federal Reserve and Virginia limits. The most recent CRA evaluation (2023, FRB Richmond) was "Satisfactory," a modest friction for merger approvals at an acquisition-dependent company. Item 3 legal proceedings is boilerplate with no specific matters.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($57, +17.6% plus 3.1% dividend)

United is a rare combination of fortress capitalization and genuine earning power: 13.3% CET1, 10.92% TCE/tangible assets, 92% loan/deposit, $699.6M of borrowings against $27.2B of deposits, 15.15% ROTCE, 47.8% efficiency ratio, and 8bp charge-offs. Four consecutive beats and steadily rising estimates argue the operating cadence is reliable. The securities book is in the first innings of a multi-year reinvestment tailwind after three years of runoff, with $4.46B of 2026 time deposit maturities offering further funding relief if the Fed eases. The 52-year dividend increase streak at a 41% payout is among the most durable income profiles in the sector. Most importantly, $1.7-1.8B of surplus CET1 and a 33-deal integration record make United a credible consolidator among sub-$100B banks that escape the Basel endgame, and a well-priced acquisition would replenish accretion, deploy idle capital, and lift ROE simultaneously. At 12.6x next-year earnings, a modest discount to the peer median, the stock requires only a re-rate toward 14.5x to deliver a high-teens return.

### Bear Case ($38, -21.6%)

The 2026 earnings story runs on three finite levers and one mechanical headwind. Deposit-cost relief of 38bp is the primary driver and stops working — or reverses — if the Fed hikes rather than cuts. Accretion has already fallen from $33.7M annually to $5.0M quarterly and costs roughly 4bp of spread per quarter as it decays. The buyback that supplied roughly 3 points of the 12% Q2 EPS growth has an estimated 1.1% of shares remaining. Behind all of it, organic loans grow 2.3% and deposits 0.8% annualized, which is not a franchise expanding. Meanwhile a third of the loan book is CRE non-owner-occupied, estimated regulatory concentration sits at 265-285% against a 300% threshold, criticized CRE-NOO was $458M at the last disclosure, and the allowance has been held flat at exactly 1.20% while non-performing loans rose and coverage fell from 293% to 271%. FY2025 demonstrated that a single sponsor can produce half a year's charge-offs. At 1.92x tangible book with a 9.53% GAAP ROE and a five-year TSR that lagged the company's own chosen peer group (+45.2% versus +49.2%), there is no valuation cushion if either NIM or credit disappoints.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on UBSI, and the case for it has changed character. In earlier reports the stock offered a modest discount with the Q2 catalyst ahead; that catalyst has now landed, favorably, and the price has absorbed it. The overall score declines to 5.4 from 5.9, and the movement is specific rather than atmospheric: financial health improves to 7.5 on confirmed 13.3% CET1, 10.92% TCE/tangible assets, and 8bp charge-offs; valuation declines to 4.5 because a 6.1% price advance against a 2.1% ninety-day estimate revision consumed the remaining gap to any reasonable base case; risk factors and competitive position decline modestly on the newly-quantified reserve-coverage trend, the $458M criticized CRE-NOO disclosure, and organic growth that has halved from the 2025 organic pace; management quality declines slightly on the absence of any open-market insider purchase, $1.7-1.8B of surplus capital idle for nineteen months, buybacks executed at a rising 1.74x tangible book, and a five-year TSR that trailed the company's own peer group.

None of that is deterioration in the franchise. United is a well-run, conservatively funded bank generating a 15.15% return on tangible equity with the most durable dividend record in its cohort. The issue is price against a growth engine assembled from finite parts — deposit repricing that depends on the Fed easing, a buyback with roughly 1.1% of shares left, securities remixing that is real but gradual, and accretion running off mechanically — layered over 2.3% annualized organic loan growth and a third of the book in CRE non-owner-occupied. At 13.1x current-year earnings, 1.92x tangible book, within 2.3% of the 52-week high, with a consensus band of $48-52 and a probability-weighted total return near 3%, the shares are approximately fairly valued with mildly unfavorable asymmetry.

Existing holders should keep the position for the yield and the balance sheet. New capital should wait for the low-to-mid $40s, or for management to convert its surplus capital into something that earns more than 7.6%.
