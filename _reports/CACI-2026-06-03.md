**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CACI International is a defense-IT and technology services prime that has spent the past decade moving up the value chain from labor-based staff augmentation toward IP-rich technology: software-defined signals intelligence and electronic warfare, space-based sensors, and optical communications. That pivot accelerated with the March 2026 close of the ~$2.64B ARKA Group acquisition, the largest deal in the company's history, which brought space-based imaging sensors, ground processing, and agentic-AI tooling with existing government authorizations. The strategic logic aligns directly with the funded portions of the FY26 defense budget (GFY26 defense at ~$838.7B plus ~$156B of reconciliation funding including $25B for Golden Dome missile defense).

The financial profile shows consistent execution: FY25 revenue grew 12.6% to $8.63B, net income rose 19%, operating margins expanded to 8.9%, and the company has raised FY26 revenue guidance to $9.5–9.6B with EBITDA margin of 11.8–11.9%. Backlog stands at $33.4B (+6.4% YoY), and the company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters. This is a well-run business in a structurally supported end market.

The reasons to hold rather than buy at $520 are concrete. Long-term debt more than tripled from $1.48B to $5.18B in roughly 18 months to fund seven acquisitions plus ARKA, leaving the balance sheet ~74% goodwill and intangibles with an Altman Z-Score in the grey zone (2.49). Near-term consensus EPS revisions are negative across the current and next quarter (current-quarter EPS cut 8.3% over 90 days, next-quarter down 5.4% over 30 days). The Fourth Circuit affirmed a $42M Abu Ghraib jury verdict in March 2026 — immaterial in dollars but carrying reputational risk with the IC/DoD customers that generate 78% of revenue and a precedent for the stayed 46-plaintiff Abbass case. The options term structure is in backwardation, pricing near-term stress. At ~18.5x forward EPS the stock is reasonably valued but not asymmetrically cheap, and the recommendation upgrade to BUY in the prior report is not supported by the current near-term revision trend.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1962, CACI provides "Expertise and Technology" to U.S. national security customers across intelligence, defense, and federal civilian agencies. The company reports two segments: Domestic Operations (~97% of revenue) and International Operations (~3%, primarily UK/Netherlands).

The business splits roughly into Technology (~57% of domestic revenue) — software-defined SIGINT/EW, optical communications, space-based sensors and ground processors, photonics, ERP, agile software/DevSecOps, and AI-augmented data platforms — and Expertise (~43%), which is technical and functional talent across software development, intelligence and special-operations support, naval architecture, and network exploitation analysis.

Customer concentration is extreme but stable. Approximately 95.7% of revenue comes from the federal government as prime or sub. DoD is ~55%, the Intelligence Community ~25% (combined ~78% per the latest 10-Q), federal civilian ~18%, and commercial/other ~4%. The top 10 contracts represent 46.4% of revenue (~$4.0B). The contract mix is favorable from a risk standpoint: roughly 60% cost-plus-fee, 26% fixed-price, and 13% time-and-materials, meaning most cost inflation is passed through to the customer.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily: $6.20B (FY22), $6.70B (FY23), $7.66B (FY24), $8.63B (FY25). FY25 growth was 12.6% (7.2% organic plus acquisitions), with net income up 19% to $500M and operating margin expanding to 8.9%. Through nine months of FY26, revenue reached $6.86B (+8.5%, 6.8% organic), nine-month net income was $379M (+10.8%), and operating income rose 16% as acquisition synergies flowed through. Q3 FY26 revenue was $2.35B (+8.5%).

Returns are solid for an asset-light services firm: ROE ~12.8%, ROA ~5.8%, net margin ~5.8%. One caveat on FY25 reported profitability: the effective tax rate fell to 17.4% from 22.9% on resolution of a federal tax audit, a non-recurring benefit. The Q3 FY26 rate normalized back to ~26%, so year-over-year EPS comparisons that lean on the FY25 tax tailwind overstate underlying earnings momentum.

Cash flow is healthy and improving. FY25 operating cash flow was $547M and FCF $481M (FCF margin ~5.6%). Nine-month FY26 operating cash flow rose to $508M from $391M, aided by OBBBA restoring immediate domestic R&D expensing, which reverses the TCJA five-year capitalization headwind that cost ~$47M in FY25. The R&D tax tailwind is real and supports the deleveraging math.

The balance sheet is where the investment debate concentrates. The ARKA financing added an $800M Term Loan B-2 (matures 2033) and $500M of additional 2033 notes (6.375%, issued at a premium for $516.5M proceeds). The credit facility was upsized to $3.25B (Nov 2025), and a $1.3B bridge facility was arranged in December 2025 and terminated undrawn at close. The Altman Z-Score of 2.49 sits in the grey zone, which for a government-backed, asset-light services firm with predictable cash flows overstates genuine distress risk but correctly flags the elevated leverage. Management has guided that net debt/EBITDA returns to the "low threes" within about six quarters, a credible path given strong FCF and EBITDA growth. Roughly $900M of floating-rate debt is hedged via swaps; each 1% rate move equals ~$8.9M of annual interest on remaining variable debt.

Capital allocation shifted defensively. Share repurchases were paused in 9M FY26 ($12.7M versus $164M the prior year) as cash was redirected to ARKA and deleveraging, with $187M remaining on the 2023 authorization. There is no dividend. Diluted share count is ~22.1M and gradually shrinking.

## Growth Analysis

The growth setup has two layers. The structural layer is the funded defense budget, which overlaps directly with CACI's space, EW, and homeland-security capabilities. The reconciliation funding is available regardless of the appropriations cycle, which partly insulates the company from the recurring shutdown and continuing-resolution dynamics documented in the 10-Q. Backlog is resilient at $33.4B total, with $5.0B funded; remaining performance obligations of $12.3B, of which ~45% is recognized within 12 months.

The company-specific layer is the space/multi-INT pivot. ARKA brings space-based imaging sensors, ground processing, and agentic-AI tools with existing geospatial intelligence authorizations. The technology and resulting capabilities belong to CACI outright. Management has flagged revenue synergies not yet embedded in guidance, and the May 2026 advance to Phase 3 of the U.S. Space Force EST program with CACI's optical communications terminal validates the strategic direction. ARKA is expected to contribute ~$150M of revenue in FY26.

The qualification on growth is the near-term estimate trend. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut to $7.33, next-quarter is down to $7.22, and current-year and next-year estimates have drifted slightly lower (-1.9% and -2.4% over 90 days). Management itself flagged that ARKA revenue is delivery/milestone-based hardware and software, so quarter-to-quarter growth and margins will be lumpier than CACI's historically smooth services revenue. The combination of falling near-term estimates and an upcoming run of "non-linear" reported results means the next two prints carry more variance than the four-quarter beat streak (2.7%, 3.6%, 9.0%, 25.9% surprises) would suggest. The FY25 25.9% beat in particular was flattered by the tax-audit resolution and should not be extrapolated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $520, CACI trades at ~18.5x consensus current-year EPS ($28.16) and ~16.7x next-year ($31.14). The trailing P/E of ~21.6x is less informative given the lumpy tax and ARKA-integration distortions.

Against peers in Diversified Support Services, CACI's multiple is at the high end: Leidos 11.7x, SAIC 13.5x, KBR 10.3x, with a peer median of 13.5x. Amentum at 38.8x is an outlier. The premium to Leidos/SAIC/KBR is partly justified by CACI's higher organic growth (6.8–7.2% versus those names' more mature profiles) and its differentiated technology mix, but the gap is wide enough that the market is already pricing CACI's growth premium. This is not a deep mispricing.

Analyst price targets — low $550, mean $681, median $700, high $800 — sit meaningfully above the current price, with the entire distribution above spot. On a high-quality name with rising long-term backlog and a credible deleveraging story, these targets carry some weight. The caution is that current-year and next-quarter estimates are falling, which historically precedes target trims rather than the upward revisions you see on accelerating-estimate names. The targets are credible as a 12–18 month frame but should not be read as imminent given the soft near-term revision trend.

The valuation score reflects a reasonable, not cheap, multiple on a quality franchise where the bull thesis depends on un-guided ARKA synergies materializing. There is asymmetric upside if the space/multi-INT pivot generates differentiated growth and deleveraging proceeds on schedule, but no specific near-term catalyst forcing a re-rate, and the near-term revision trend is the wrong direction.

## Competitive Landscape

CACI competes against larger primes — Leidos, Booz Allen, SAIC, Northrop's IT units, Parsons — in a market where "no single competitor is dominant." Its differentiation is increasingly technology- and product-led rather than commodity staff augmentation, which is the more defensible position as DOGE-style efficiency initiatives threaten pure services spend. The industry is consolidating, and CACI is an active consolidator (seven acquisitions in three years plus ARKA), deliberately shifting toward higher-value, IP-rich offerings in software-defined SIGINT/EW, space ISR, and optical comms.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. Management notes LPTA (lowest-price/technically-acceptable) procurement pressure has moderated, and the EST Phase 3 win demonstrates the company is winning differentiated, technology-led programs rather than re-competing commodity contracts. The risks to this trajectory are rising bid-protest frequency and lengthening procurement timelines, which can delay revenue conversion. On the competitive read, five-year TSR of 219.8 essentially matches the peer-group TSR of 220.29, meaning CACI has delivered strong absolute returns but no demonstrable alpha versus the defense-IT cohort. The forward case rests on whether the space pivot generates that differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**Abu Ghraib litigation (Al Shimari).** On March 12, 2026, the Fourth Circuit affirmed (2-1) a $42M jury verdict against CACI Premier Technology for conspiring with the U.S. military in detainee abuse in 2003–04. CACI has booked no reserve, considers the claim "completely without merit," and will seek en banc rehearing and Supreme Court review (pending Cisco v. Doe). The dollar amount is immaterial to an $11.5B company, but the appellate affirmance moves it materially closer to a payable judgment, and the stayed Abbass case (46 plaintiffs) means the precedent could multiply exposure. The larger dimension is reputational risk with the IC/DoD customers that generate the majority of revenue. This is a binary, headline-driven risk that a standardized screen would miss.

**Leverage.** Debt was funded at elevated rates (new notes at 6.375%, term loans ~5.4%). The deleveraging path to "low threes" within six quarters is credible given FCF, but it depends on continued strong cash conversion and uninterrupted EBITDA growth. A funding shock or integration stumble would leave CACI carrying $5B+ of debt against a balance sheet that is already heavily weighted to goodwill and intangibles.

**Government funding instability.** The 10-Q documents an October 2025 shutdown, a CR through January 2026, a partial shutdown, and a DHS shutdown as of February 2026. The reconciliation funding partly insulates CACI, but the appropriations environment is genuinely disrupted.

**Customer and contract concentration.** Termination-for-convenience, declined option years, and rising bid protests are structural to the model. DCAA cost audits remain open through FY24.

**Goodwill and integration.** Goodwill is now ~$6.5B. Seven deals in three years plus ARKA create integration execution risk and potential impairment if reporting-unit cash flows deteriorate, though the FY25 annual test passed with fair value "significantly exceeding" carrying value.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 41.5% is rich versus 32.0% historical volatility (a 9.5pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.5% near-term versus 37.7% longer-dated), consistent with the June 18 expiry sitting near the next earnings window and ongoing budget/litigation headlines. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.13 to 1.19 over the past month, indicating a shift toward defensive positioning, though the put/call skew remains balanced at -2.0%. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.9%. Short interest is moderate at 8.2% of float with 3.9 days to cover — not a crowded short, but the recent OI shift toward puts argues against adding fresh long exposure ahead of the near-term event window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CACI executes the space/multi-INT pivot, ARKA delivers the un-guided revenue synergies management has flagged, and the EST Phase 3 win scales into a multi-year optical-comms franchise. Strong FCF deleverages to the low-threes net-debt/EBITDA within six quarters, the R&D tax tailwind boosts cash conversion, and the company resumes buybacks. Backlog converts steadily, organic growth holds at 6–7%, and the multiple re-rates toward the analyst median as the leverage overhang clears. Bull case ~$680–700 (analyst mean/median, ~31–35% upside).

**Bear case.** ARKA integration stumbles, the lumpy milestone-based revenue produces a near-term miss against already-falling estimates, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (13.5x) on the leverage overhang and the Abu Ghraib reputational headline. Continued government funding disruption delays contract conversion. Bear case ~$410 (-21%), roughly the lower end of the 52-week range.

The base case sits between these: a quality franchise with a real structural tailwind, growing into its multiple over 12–18 months while it deleverages, but without a near-term forcing catalyst and against a soft near-term revision trend that caps upside in the next two prints.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive 12-month growth thesis (growth_hold). The franchise quality, backlog, and structural budget tailwind support continued ownership, but the falling near-term estimates, tripled leverage, and options-implied stress argue against deploying fresh capital at $520.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Near-term EPS revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter estimates stop falling and begin rising), confirming the ARKA-distorted period is past.
- Net debt/EBITDA demonstrably tracks toward the low-threes on the next one to two prints, de-risking the leverage overhang, OR management quantifies the un-guided ARKA revenue synergies.
- A pullback toward the low-$400s that compresses forward P/E below ~14x while backlog and organic growth remain intact, creating asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Q4 FY26 or Q1 FY27 revenue/EPS miss driven by ARKA integration or contract losses, combined with continued estimate cuts.
- Adverse resolution of the Abbass case or evidence of customer-relationship damage from the Abu Ghraib affirmance affecting award rates.
- Deleveraging stalls (net debt/EBITDA flat or rising) or a goodwill impairment is taken.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q4 FY26, expected August 2026, given the June 30 fiscal year-end), which will be the first full quarter of ARKA contribution (ARKA closed March 9, 2026, partway through Q3 FY26) and a clearer read on revenue lumpiness and the deleveraging trajectory.

## Conclusion

CACI is a high-quality defense-IT consolidator executing a coherent move up the value chain into space and multi-INT capabilities, supported by a funded defense budget and a large long-term backlog. The strategic direction is sound and the EST Phase 3 win plus the ARKA capabilities give the forward thesis real substance. The prior report's upgrade to BUY reflected the legitimate strategic story, but the current data tilts the balance back toward HOLD: near-term consensus estimates are falling across the current and next quarter, the balance sheet carries newly tripled debt heavily weighted to goodwill and intangibles, the Abu Ghraib appellate affirmance adds a reputational tail risk, and the options market is pricing imminent stress through backwardation and a sharp shift toward puts. The valuation is reasonable but not asymmetrically cheap, and the stock sits roughly mid-range after a drawdown from January highs (peaking at ~$683 intraday in late January) without a clear near-term catalyst to force the re-rate the analyst targets imply.

The appropriate posture is to hold existing positions for the 12–18 month growth and deleveraging thesis while waiting for near-term revisions to stabilize and the ARKA integration to prove out before committing fresh capital. Recommendation: **HOLD**.
