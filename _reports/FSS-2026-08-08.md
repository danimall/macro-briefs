## Executive Summary

Federal Signal is executing well and the market knows it. Q2 2026 delivered $670.2M of net sales (+18.7%), 17.6% operating margin, diluted EPS of $1.40, and a fourth consecutive earnings beat (adjusted $1.42 versus $1.30 estimated, +9.3%). Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.58–2.67B and adjusted EPS to $5.12–$5.30 (both externally reported rather than filing-verified). Consensus estimates for the current year have risen (Yahoo/yfinance: +4.8% over 90 days) and next year (+3.4% over 90 days). The stock rallied 18% from $111.86 on July 29 to $132.51 on August 4 before settling at $127.83, which is 4.96% below the July 6 high of $134.51.

Two facts sit uncomfortably against that print. First, orders are not keeping pace with shipments: consolidated H1 book-to-bill was 0.97 and deteriorated to 0.95 in Q2, backlog fell 7.5% year over year to $1,002.1M, and Safety & Security orders declined 10% in Q2 with backlog down 19.1% in a segment with no acquisition distorting the comparison. Second, the growth is substantially bought rather than generated: Mega contributed $23.9M of sales in 5.5 months, New Way's H1 contribution is undisclosed but plausibly $120–140M, and Environmental Solutions MD&A attributes growth to "acquisitions and pricing actions" in every period discussed. Strip a reasonable acquisition estimate out and organic ES growth is roughly 9–11% with organic ES orders approximately flat.

The valuation leaves no room for that ambiguity to resolve badly. At $127.83 the shares trade 27.6x trailing GAAP EPS of $4.64, 24.5x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, 22.3x FY2027 consensus of $5.73, and 16.3x estimated TTM adjusted EBITDA — against a peer median P/E of 18.5x and FY2026-to-FY2027 consensus earnings growth of about 10%, a PEG of roughly 2.4. Management, which has the best information, repurchased 500 shares for $0.1M in H1 2026 against 531,497 shares for $39.7M in H1 2025, with ~$157M of authorization unused. That is defensible as post-acquisition deleveraging; it is also not the behavior of a board that considers the stock cheap. HOLD, fourth consecutive.

## Company Overview

Federal Signal manufactures specialty vehicles and safety equipment from 21 U.S. plants, three in Canada, two in Europe, and one in South Africa. Two segments:

**Environmental Solutions (ES)** — 85.7% of H1 2026 sales ($1,110.4M) and 81.6% of segment operating income. Sewer cleaners, hydro-excavation "safe-digging" trucks, industrial vacuum loaders, dump bodies, street sweepers, waterblasting equipment, refuse collection vehicles (New Way), road-marking and line-removal equipment (Hog), metal extraction support equipment (Mega), plus a $234.7M H1 aftermarket stream of parts, service, rental, and training.

**Safety and Security Systems (S&S)** — 14.3% of sales ($185.4M). Lightbars and sirens for public safety ($125.8M), industrial signaling ($37.1M), public warning and PA ($22.5M).

The segments have inverted cost structures. S&S earns a 43.4% gross margin but carries 18.7% SEG&A, netting 24.6% operating margin. ES earns 27.3% gross on 7.8% SEG&A, netting 18.3%. S&S is the higher-return business per revenue dollar; ES receives all of the growth capital. Funding the lower-margin, faster-growing segment is rational only if the ES installed base and aftermarket annuity compound, which is the core of the strategic thesis.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (10-K):** net sales $2,180.5M (+17.1%), operating income $340.9M (15.6% margin), net income $246.6M, diluted EPS $4.01, free cash flow $227.1M (10.4% of sales, 92% of net income), company-reported adjusted EBITDA $438.9M.

**H1 2026 (10-Q):** net sales $1,295.8M (+26.0%), operating income $217.9M (16.8% margin versus 15.9%), diluted EPS $2.54 versus $1.91.

The margin story deserves precision because it changed direction mid-year. Derived from the reported Q2 and H1 columns, Q1 2026 sales were $625.6M (+34.9%) with operating income of $99.7M (+51.8%) and a 15.9% margin versus 14.2% — roughly 176 bps of expansion. Q2 expanded only 30 bps (17.6% versus 17.3%). The segment tables locate the cause: S&S operating margin *fell* 170 bps to 23.9% on a 160 bp gross-margin decline the company attributes to "unfavorable sales mix and higher material costs," offsetting a 60 bp ES improvement. Two quarters is not a trend, but the appearance of explicit material-cost language on the S&S gross line, in a year when the 10-K flags trade policy as a live risk, is the most concrete evidence of input-cost pressure in the filings.

**Balance sheet.** A screening metric of 0.73 "debt/equity" is total liabilities to equity, not borrowings. Actual borrowings plus finance leases fell from $566.6M at 12/31/2025 to $455.0M at 6/30/2026, against $1,507.6M of equity — 0.30x. Net debt is $392.2M ($455.0M less $62.8M cash), down $110.7M in six months. Against estimated TTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$503M (bridging FY2025's reported $438.9M with H1 EBITDA deltas; an approximation, not a company figure), net leverage is roughly 0.78x. H1 interest coverage is 16.9x ($217.9M operating income / $12.9M interest). Current ratio 3.02, Altman Z 3.3, FY2025 ROE 17.8%, ROA 10.3%.

The 2025 Credit Agreement (Fourth Amended and Restated, 10/29/2025) provides $1.5B of capacity — a $1.1B revolver plus a $400M delayed-draw term loan — maturing 10/29/2030, replacing an $800M facility. At 6/30/2026: $52.7M revolver drawn, $400.0M term loan outstanding, $1.04B net availability, covenants (net leverage, interest coverage) in compliance. The accordion permits incremental debt to 2.75x pro-forma net leverage, implying roughly $990M of theoretical additional capacity, with the $1.04B undrawn revolver the nearer practical constraint.

One specific exposure: both interest-rate swaps ($75M notional each) matured in 2025 and were not replaced, leaving effectively the entire $452.7M of credit-agreement borrowings floating and unhedged. A 100 bp move is roughly $4.5M pre-tax, ~$0.06 per diluted share, or about 1.1% of guided adjusted EPS. Not thesis-critical, but letting all hedges roll off in the same year borrowings more than doubled is a treasury choice with no stated rationale.

**ROIC.** NOPAT of roughly $262.2M (FY2025 operating income $340.9M at a 23.1% effective rate) against year-end invested capital of $1,884.9M gives 13.9% on ending capital and roughly 16.4% on average capital. Both clear any plausible cost of capital. The denominator rose ~43% in one year because of New Way, which makes 2026–2027 ROIC the cleanest available test of whether the $436.8M price was justified.

**Cash flow — and why H1 must not be annualized.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $214.2M versus $96.4M, with capex of $18.6M, for $195.6M of free cash flow (15.1% of sales). More than half of that improvement is timing rather than earnings. "Changes in operating assets and liabilities" consumed only $1.7M versus $68.1M a year earlier, a $66.4M favorable swing: receivables *declined* to $288.8M from $292.2M and inventories rose only to $479.8M from $471.6M despite 26% revenue growth. New Way arrived carrying $60.0M of receivables and $86.0M of inventory that have since been worked down. Capex is guided to $45–55M for the year against $18.6M spent, so H2 carries $26–36M of additional drag. Management describes Q1 as the weakest equipment quarter with rental and parts peaking in Q2–Q3.

A defensible normalized frame: FY2025 converted 10.4% of sales to FCF; on ~$2.625B of guided FY2026 revenue at 9.5–11.4% conversion, FY2026 FCF of $250–300M is reasonable. That is a 3.2–3.8% FCF yield on the current $7.80B market capitalization, and it supports roughly $200M+ of annual discretionary capital after the ~$37M dividend — enough for a $200–400M acquisition every 12–18 months without increasing leverage.

**Dilution.** Diluted shares of 61.5M against 60.9M basic in Q2, with 0.6M of dilutive equivalents (~1.0% of basic). No convertibles or warrants. The New Way ($54M maximum) and Standard ($4.8M) earn-outs are cash-settled, carried at a combined $15.0M fair value — the company is ascribing low probability to full achievement, and maximum incremental cash exposure above carrying value is roughly $43M. Contingent equity dilution is negligible.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is strong and decelerating. FY2026 guidance of $2.58–2.67B implies +20.4% at the $2.625B midpoint. H1 actual of $1,295.8M is 49.4% of that midpoint, implying H2 revenue of ~$1,329M, +15.4% over H2 2025's $1,152.1M — a step down from H1's +26% as acquisition anniversaries roll through (Hog anniversaried February 2026, New Way anniversaries November 2026, Mega January 2027). Consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.73 against FY2026's $5.20 implies about 10% earnings growth.

**Organic composition is the unresolved question.** Disclosed: Mega generated $23.9M of sales and $5.3M of operating income from 1/16/2026 to 6/30/2026; New Way generated $21.2M and $1.4M from 11/25/2025 to 12/31/2025; Hog contributed $65.6M of FY2025 sales. New Way's H1 2026 contribution is not disclosed. Scaling the 36-day stub with adjustments for holiday shutdowns and Q1 seasonality suggests $120–140M, which would put acquisition contribution at roughly $145–165M of the $242.5M ES sales increase and organic ES growth at approximately 9–11% on the prior-year $867.9M base. That estimate has wide error bars and could be lower still if pricing actions carry more of the increase than assumed.

**Orders are the harder number.** Consolidated H1 book-to-bill was 0.97 ($1,259.5M orders / $1,295.8M sales) and Q2 was 0.95. ES orders rose 24% in Q2 and 17.5% in H1, but MD&A states this is "inclusive of the effects of acquisitions," naming New Way refuse trucks and Mega metal extraction as drivers; applying the same acquisition estimate leaves organic ES orders roughly flat to modestly down. S&S orders fell 10.0% in Q2 and 4.8% in H1, with non-U.S. public safety equipment orders down $12.7M, and S&S backlog down 19.1% to $67.3M, with no acquisition in that segment's comparison period until Western Technology closed 7/1/2026.

The FY2025 order figures reconcile internally (beginning backlog $997.1M + orders $2,221.5M − sales $2,180.5M = $1,038.1M versus $1,042.4M reported, a $4.3M FX gap), and the FY2025 order total explicitly included ~$146M of New Way and ~$20M of Hog acquired backlog. Excluding those, organic FY2025 orders were roughly $2,056M, +11% on FY2024's ~$1,848M, not the 20% the headline implies.

Two readings compete and cannot be fully separated from the filings. Demand may be softening in municipal and non-U.S. public safety channels. Alternatively, backlog is normalizing from the 2021–2023 supply-chain era when lead times stretched multiple quarters; a shrinking backlog alongside rising shipments is what throughput recovery looks like, and the FY2025 10-K stated the year-end backlog was expected to be "substantially completed during 2026." The chassis-supply mechanism means backlog inflates and deflates independently of demand, which is what makes the signal ambiguous. The S&S decline, however, is not explained by lead-time normalization and is a filing fact.

**Aftermarket.** ES aftermarket revenue (parts $166.5M, rental $40.6M, service/other $27.6M) totaled $234.7M in H1 2026, up 22.4% from $191.8M. But as a share of ES revenue it fell from 22.1% to 21.1%. The "pivot to recurring revenue" framing circulating in contributor research is not supported by current mix data — acquiring equipment manufacturers grows the vehicles line faster than the aftermarket line. The offsetting consideration is real: aftermarket revenue lags equipment sales by years, so a declining share during an acquisition wave is consistent with a *future* mix shift. What the data establishes is strong absolute aftermarket growth, not a current mix change.

## Competitive Landscape

Federal Signal competes in fragmented, application-specific niches rather than broad-line equipment. The substantiable advantages are an installed base large enough to generate $234.7M of H1 aftermarket revenue, a rental fleet carried at $210.0M net of $73.5M accumulated depreciation that both smooths demand and seeds equipment sales, and $177.9M of indefinite-lived trade names. Pricing power is evident: MD&A cites "benefits from pricing actions" as a gross-margin driver in every period for both segments, and consolidated gross margin of 29.6% with 17.6% Q2 operating margin is strong for this industry classification.

The New Way acquisition changed the competitive posture. Refuse collection bodybuilding is the most consolidated market FSS has entered, with established U.S. players including Heil (a Dover subsidiary), McNeilus (Oshkosh), Labrie, and Wayne Engineering. *These competitor identifications are general industry knowledge, not filing-sourced, and no market-share data was obtainable.* Where FSS historically competed against smaller private manufacturers, in refuse it competes against divisions of larger diversified industrials with their own dealer networks and chassis relationships. The adjacency logic is credible — a municipality buying a sewer cleaner and a street sweeper through the FSS dealer network is the same buyer with the same procurement cycle as the one buying a refuse packer — and the rental arm is a distribution asset most competitors lack. But the competitive intensity assessment here is weak on evidence.

Mega marks the first move into a mining-capex end market, materially more cyclical than municipal maintenance. At $45M the position is too small to be thesis-relevant, but the direction trades demand stability for higher-beta revenue.

Two trajectory negatives temper the score. S&S is losing ground internationally: orders down 10% in Q2, non-U.S. public safety orders down $12.7M in H1, backlog down 19.1%. Geographic concentration is worsening — U.S. revenue rose from 78.7% to 81.5% of sales H1-over-H1 (82.8% in Q2), Canada revenue fell 12.2% in Q2, and every acquisition since 2024 (Standard, Hog, Kinloch, New Way, Mega, Western Technology) is U.S.-domiciled. Product-line breadth is genuinely expanding; exposure to U.S. municipal and industrial capex is concentrating. Those are different measures and should not be conflated. Competitive position 6.2: stable-to-slightly-improving in ES, deteriorating in S&S, with the prior 7.0-to-6.5 decline confirmed rather than reversed.

## Risk Assessment

**Order and backlog deterioration (primary).** At 24.5x forward earnings there is no cushion for a quarter in which orders visibly fail to support the shipment rate. This is the risk most capable of moving the stock and the one least reflected in the current multiple.

**Trade policy and input costs.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors reference a February 2026 U.S. Supreme Court decision striking down certain tariffs and the resulting refund/replacement uncertainty. *That legal specific is filing-sourced but not independently verified.* The economic consequence is already visible in the 160 bp S&S gross-margin decline attributed partly to "higher material costs," and in ES cost of sales. Whether FSS nets out as a beneficiary (protection on imported competing equipment) or a loser (steel and component inflation) is unresolved.

**Chassis dependence.** A large share of ES products are built on third-party truck OEM chassis from a concentrated supplier set. This constrains revenue recognition timing, creates pass-through cost exposure, and is the mechanism that makes the backlog signal genuinely ambiguous in both directions.

**Municipal funding concentration.** ES customers are heavily municipal; S&S customers are police and fire. Management sized one channel on the Q2 call — U.S. water taxes affecting "less than 15% of total net sales" — but that quote is third-party reported and unverified, and addresses one funding source rather than aggregate municipal-budget sensitivity.

**Acquisition integration and control scope.** Item 4 of the Q2 10-Q discloses that management has not yet fully assessed internal controls over financial reporting at Mega, New Way, or Kinloch. This is a permitted one-year accommodation, not a finding of weakness, but it places the largest acquisition in company history outside the assessed ICFR perimeter through at least Q4 2026. Purchase-price allocations for New Way and Mega remain preliminary with third-party intangible valuations unfinalized; New Way goodwill already rose $10.9M in H1 on true-ups.

**Quantified contingencies (6/30/2026).** Letters of credit and bid/performance bonds $33.3M; equipment repurchase guarantees with maximum cash exposure $11.8M; affiliate lease guarantees $3.8M. Individually immaterial against $1.5B of facility capacity, though the repurchase guarantees are pro-cyclical.

**Legacy litigation.** The firefighter hearing-loss suits (filed 1999–2017) were resolved by a global settlement executed 11/4/2019 at $700 per filed claimant and $300 per eligible non-filer, with no company-paid attorney fees. A liability is accrued; excess loss is "reasonably possible" but not expected to be material. Largely defused.

**Floating-rate exposure.** $452.7M unhedged; ~$0.06 per share per 100 bp.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $158 (+23.6%)

Backlog compression is lead-time normalization, not demand loss. The $1,002.1M backlog covers roughly 4.6 months of guided revenue and, per management's framing, supports a strong H2 and early 2027. ES organic growth of 9–11% persists, Western Technology and further bolt-ons add accretion at Mega-like economics (Mega produced a 22.2% operating margin on a $45M price), and the M&A engine keeps compounding: $250–300M of normalized FCF plus $1.04B of revolver availability funds a $200–400M deal every 12–18 months without leverage increase. ROIC in the compensation plan disciplines price paid. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.73 proves conservative given four straight beats; $6.05 on continued accretion at 26x gives $157. Analyst targets to $155 corroborate.

### Bear Case — $100 (−21.8%)

Q3 and Q4 orders confirm that S&S weakness is spreading and ES organic orders are flat-to-negative. As acquisition anniversaries roll through (New Way in November 2026, Mega in January 2027), reported growth converges toward organic single digits while the H2 margin comparison faces the material-cost pressure already visible in S&S. A company growing high-single-digit organically with ~10% EPS growth does not hold 24.5x. Compression to the peer median 18.5x on FY2026 adjusted $5.20 gives $96; 19.2x on $5.20 gives $100, near the $101.19 52-week low. The 9.7% short interest and 4.13 put/call OI ratio suggest positioning is prepared for this. Working-capital reversal in H2 makes reported FCF look worse than H1 implied, and the buyback pause removes the support that cushioned the 2025 drawdowns.

### Base Case — $140 (+9.5%)

Orders stabilize near 1.0x book-to-bill without accelerating; H2 revenue lands in the guided range; FY2027 EPS comes in modestly above the $5.73 consensus at $5.80–5.85 on continued small-deal accretion. The multiple holds roughly where it is — 24x FY2027 $5.83 = $140 — because the balance sheet, ROIC, and beat record justify a premium to peers, but nothing forces expansion. Total return with the 0.47% dividend is approximately 10% over twelve months.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 22% bull / 28% bear yields expected return of roughly +3.8%. That is fair-to-slightly-positive, which is a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE.

**On the trajectory.** This is a fourth consecutive HOLD. The evidence set did change: the Q2 print added a guidance raise, a fourth beat, and a $110.7M reduction in net debt (all positive), against a Q2 book-to-bill of 0.95, a 30 bp margin-expansion print versus 176 bp in Q1, and S&S orders down 10% (all negative). The stock also re-rated 18% off the pre-print low. Financial health improved as prior reports indicated (0.30x borrowings/equity, 16.9x coverage), and management quality remains high on the ROIC-gated plan and absent discretionary C-suite selling. What compressed is the risk/reward: the same business at a higher multiple with a book-to-bill below 1 in both segments. Overall score declines from 6.5 to 6.08 on that arithmetic, not on the price path; the price is essentially unchanged from the early-July report.

## Management Quality Detail

Compensation performance measures are adjusted pre-tax income, adjusted EBITDA margin, adjusted EPS, and **return on invested capital**. For a company that has spent roughly $580M on seven deals in two years, the ROIC gate is the single most important governance feature in the proxy: a pure adjusted-EPS structure would actively reward leveraged acquisition of any business earning more than the after-tax cost of debt. Adjusted pre-tax income compounded from $128.0M (2021) to $343.0M (2025), a 27.9% CAGR, while CEO total compensation rose from $4.67M to $8.42M and "compensation actually paid" *declined* from $15.67M to $14.80M in 2025 as net income rose 14% — consistent with genuine mark-to-market rather than a ratchet. Cumulative TSR indexed to $339 versus $243 for the peer group.

Insider activity is mixed but not alarming. One open-market purchase: director Richard Maue bought 2,000 shares at $110.766 on 3/10/2026 (~$222K), consistent with a new director building a required stake. The largest sales were GC Diane Bonina exercising 14,856 options struck at $35.80 and $51.81 and selling ~19,264 shares at $128.75–$131.05 for ~$2.5M (ending with 15,689 shares), and director Brenda Reichelderfer selling 6,070 shares at $131.02. Bonina's activity is the standard monetization of deep-in-the-money expiring-value instruments and is weakly informative. More relevant: Sherman, Hudson, and Weber made no discretionary open-market sales — every 2026 disposition was Code F tax withholding on vesting. Sherman retains ~641,724 shares, roughly $82M at the current price against $8.4M of annual compensation. That is meaningful alignment.

Capital allocation in 2026 was coherent: $109.8M of debt repaid, ~$60M deployed on Mega and the Hog earn-out, $9.2M on Western Technology, dividend raised from $0.14 to $0.15 quarterly (11.5% payout on guided adjusted EPS), buybacks paused. The unexplained element is the buyback pause coinciding with the stock at 24–28x earnings — defensible as deleveraging, but also an implicit valuation view from the best-informed party. Score 7.0, unchanged from the prior report.
