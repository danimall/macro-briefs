## Executive Summary

Chemed Corporation operates two distinct businesses — VITAS Healthcare (hospice, ~64% of revenue) and Roto-Rooter (plumbing/drain services, ~36%) — that historically provided a combination of recession-resistant healthcare demand and steady consumer services cash flow. The investment case has been complicated over the past year by three consecutive quarterly earnings misses (Q2-Q4 2025), structural erosion of Roto-Rooter's organic lead generation due to Google algorithm changes, and a stock price decline of approximately 24% from its 52-week high.

The Q1 2026 report provided genuine stabilization: adjusted EPS of $5.65 beat consensus by 5.5%, VITAS admissions grew 6.9% YoY, and management raised full-year ADC growth guidance to 4.5%-5.5%. The stock responded with a 10%+ gap higher on earnings day. However, Roto-Rooter revenue contracted 0.9% with EBITDA margins compressing 218 bps as the shift from organic to paid digital leads structurally increases customer acquisition costs. This headwind is durable and accelerating.

At $440/share and approximately 18x forward adjusted earnings ($24.35 consensus FY2026), the valuation is neither compelling nor stretched for a business of this quality and defensive characteristics. The resolution of the $50.3M Medicare overpayment dispute and aggressive share repurchases ($197.7M in Q1 alone) are positives. But with 95%+ Medicare dependence at VITAS, a deteriorating Roto-Rooter competitive position, and only moderate estimate revision momentum (+2.0% 30-day, -4.2% 90-day for full year), there is insufficient asymmetric upside to justify a BUY recommendation.

## Company Overview

Chemed Corporation is a Cincinnati-based holding company with two wholly unrelated operating subsidiaries:

**VITAS Healthcare** is one of the largest for-profit hospice providers in the United States, serving terminally ill patients through interdisciplinary care teams across multiple states with significant concentration in Florida. Revenue is derived almost entirely from Medicare/Medicaid per diem payments, with rates set annually by CMS. Q1 2026 average daily census was 22,723 patients.

**Roto-Rooter** is the most recognized plumbing and drain cleaning brand in the U.S., operating company-owned branches and franchise networks covering over 90% of the population. Services include plumbing, drain cleaning, excavation, and water restoration for residential and commercial customers.

The corporate structure functions as a capital allocator, deploying cash flows from both businesses toward share repurchases, dividend growth, and selective acquisitions.

## Growth Analysis

**VITAS (positive trajectory):**
- ADC growing 2.2% with admissions up 6.9% — management raised guidance to 4.5%-5.5% ADC growth
- Revenue guidance: 6.5%-7.5% growth for FY2026
- Secular tailwind from aging baby boomer demographics provides multi-year demand support
- Medicare rate increase of 2.6% for FY2026 provides pricing floor

**VITAS (offsetting headwinds):**
- Average length of stay declining (102.7 days vs. 118.7 YoY) — faster turnover means more admissions needed to sustain ADC growth
- Continuous care days declined 26.4% — lower acuity mix reduces revenue per patient day
- Implicit price concessions more than doubled ($5.1M vs. $2.3M)
- The 2.6% Medicare rate increase barely keeps pace with wage inflation

**Roto-Rooter (structural challenges):**
- Revenue declining 0.9% in Q1 with water restoration down 11.7%
- Organic search leads down 16% due to Google algorithm changes — management acknowledges this is permanent
- Paid lead mix now 53-54% vs. historically lower — $15.5M+ annualized advertising cost headwind
- Guidance for 3-3.5% revenue growth relies on franchise acquisitions (Fort Worth, San Francisco for $20.6M) rather than organic demand

**Consolidated growth outlook:** Mid-single-digit revenue growth (4-6%) driven primarily by VITAS volume, with EPS growth enhanced by 6-7% annual share count reduction through buybacks. This produces low-double-digit EPS growth potential ($24-25 in FY2026 rising toward $27 in FY2027) but is not an acceleration story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $440/share with consensus FY2026 EPS of $24.35, CHE trades at 18.1x forward earnings. The peer comparison is limited — the closest Health Care Services peers trade at median 37.6x P/E, but most are earlier-stage growth companies (HIMS, RDNT, VCYT) with negative or minimal earnings. A more appropriate comparison set would be mature healthcare services names.

**Valuation framework:**
- Forward P/E: 18.1x on FY2026, 16.4x on FY2027 consensus ($26.91)
- FCF yield: approximately 5-6% normalized
- Dividend yield: 0.55%
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 11-12x (based on ~$470M annualized EBITDA)

For a business with recession-resistant demand characteristics, consistent FCF generation, and a 13.2M-share count declining at 6%+ annually, a 16-20x forward P/E range is defensible. The stock sits at the midpoint of this range. Management's aggressive repurchase activity at $395/share (Q1 average) suggests their internal view of fair value is above current levels, though management buyback signals are imperfect predictors.

The stock is not cheap enough to represent a compelling entry (no specific catalyst for re-rating to 22-24x), nor expensive enough to suggest meaningful downside risk from current levels. The analyst consensus target range ($400-$500) brackets the current price, with the mean at $446.50 — only 1.5% above current.

## Competitive Landscape

**VITAS strengths (stable but not improving):**
- Scale advantages in a fragmented 5,000+ provider industry
- Certificate of Need protections in key markets (Florida, California)
- Established referral networks and 25+ year relationships with healthcare systems
- Successfully resolved $50.3M billing dispute — validates compliance infrastructure

**VITAS vulnerabilities:**
- 95%+ Medicare dependence creates single-payer concentration risk
- CMS halted Special Focus Program but could reinstate at any time
- Southern California enforcement scrutiny specifically mentioned on earnings calls
- UnitedHealth's Amedisys acquisition and Humana's CenterWell Home Health create well-capitalized competitors

**Roto-Rooter strengths (deteriorating):**
- Brand recognition covering 90%+ of U.S. population
- Company-owned branch network enables quality control and margin capture
- Water restoration and excavation services provide ancillary revenue streams

**Roto-Rooter critical weakness — Google algorithm erosion:**
The 16% decline in organic search leads reflects Google's strategic monetization of local service searches through Google Local Services Ads and AI-generated responses. This is a structural shift that permanently increases Roto-Rooter's customer acquisition cost. The brand's historical moat — consumers typing "Roto-Rooter" directly into search engines — is being intermediated by Google's platform changes. At $22M quarterly advertising (up from $18.2M), this cost inflation directly compresses margins and will continue. Management's acknowledged 53-54% paid lead mix going forward represents a new, higher operating cost baseline.

Private equity investment in home services competitors (noted as a new risk factor in the 10-K) compounds this by bringing well-capitalized competitors with sophisticated digital marketing capabilities into fragmented local markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Medicare reimbursement changes (HIGH):** Any material reduction in hospice per diem rates directly impairs VITAS profitability with no ability to offset through pricing. The 2% sequestration continues through FY2027. With 95%+ revenue concentration, this is a permanent structural vulnerability.

2. **Roto-Rooter digital acquisition cost inflation (MODERATE-HIGH):** The Google algorithm headwind is structural and accelerating. Each percentage point shift from organic to paid leads adds approximately $1-2M in annual advertising expense with no corresponding revenue benefit.

3. **VITAS labor market (MODERATE):** Running 100 FTEs below budget benefited Q1 margins but is "not sustainable" per management. Hiring 60 FTEs/month for the rest of 2026 will pressure EBITDA margins back toward guidance range (18-18.5%), suggesting Q1's 16.3% may not represent run-rate.

4. **Tariff exposure (MODERATE):** New risk factor in 2025 10-K. Steel costs affect Roto-Rooter equipment; pharmaceutical costs affect VITAS (which cannot pass along increases under Medicare per diem). Management downplays 2026 impact but flagged uncertainty for 2027.

5. **Acuity mix deterioration at VITAS (LOW-MODERATE):** Declining length of stay and shift away from continuous care toward lower-acuity routine home care reduces revenue per patient day. Partially offset by volume growth.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($500-520, 14-18% upside):**
- VITAS continues to execute on 4.5-5.5% ADC growth, demonstrating the secular aging tailwind
- Roto-Rooter stabilizes with franchise acquisitions and water restoration recovery
- Aggressive buybacks (6-7% annual share reduction) mechanically lift EPS
- Stock re-rates from 18x to 20-21x forward as consecutive beats restore confidence after 2025's misses
- Potential Medicare rate increases under favorable policy environment

**Bear Case ($365-380, 14-17% downside):**
- Medicare rate compression in FY2027 budget (2% sequestration + potential reduction to base rate)
- Google algorithm changes accelerate Roto-Rooter margin compression beyond guidance
- VITAS acuity mix continues deteriorating, requiring admissions growth to outpace per-patient-day revenue decline
- Tariff-driven cost inflation at VITAS (pharmaceuticals) with no pass-through mechanism
- Return to earnings misses if labor hiring exceeds revenue growth

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarterly EPS beats of 5%+ demonstrating sustained execution recovery
- Roto-Rooter organic revenue growth returning to positive territory (would signal Google headwind stabilization)
- Stock declining to $380-400 on no fundamental deterioration (would create 20%+ upside to base case)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Roto-Rooter EBITDA margin falling below 20% for two consecutive quarters (structural competitive impairment confirmed)
- CMS announcing material hospice per diem rate cuts or reinstating Special Focus Program targeting VITAS
- Revenue growth turning negative at both segments simultaneously

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected late July 2026). Q2 results will reveal whether Q1's beat was genuine stabilization or a temporary benefit from understaffing.

## Conclusion

Chemed presents a quality-defensive business profile trading at a reasonable valuation, with the Q1 2026 beat providing genuine stabilization after a difficult 2025. However, the stock lacks a specific catalyst for meaningful re-rating. The VITAS segment benefits from secular demographic tailwinds but operates in a structurally constrained Medicare reimbursement environment. Roto-Rooter faces durable competitive erosion from Google's platform changes that management has acknowledged will persist.

The prior declining score trajectory (6.2 → 5.5) reflected the three consecutive earnings misses and emerging recognition of Roto-Rooter's structural challenges. The Q1 beat and raised guidance partially address the earnings miss pattern, justifying a modest score recovery from the April trough. However, Roto-Rooter's competitive position deterioration is confirmed and accelerating rather than stabilizing, preventing a return to prior levels.

At 18x forward earnings with mid-single-digit organic revenue growth enhanced by buybacks, CHE offers approximately 10% total return potential (EPS growth + dividend yield) — adequate for a defensive holding but insufficient to justify new capital deployment given the identified structural headwinds and limited upside to consensus targets.
