## Executive Summary

Perdoceo Education Corporation operates three accredited postsecondary institutions — Colorado Technical University (CTU), the American InterContinental University System (AIUS), and the recently acquired University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS). The company generated $225M of free cash flow on $846M of revenue in FY2025, a 26.6% FCF margin driven by an asset-light online delivery model with CapEx at just 1% of revenue. The balance sheet holds $643M in cash and short-term investments against $11.5M of total debt, resulting in a DOE Composite Score at the maximum 3.0.

At $32.35, the stock trades at ~9x trailing FCF and ~13x trailing earnings, a meaningful discount to education services peers (median P/E 15.1x). This discount reflects regulatory risk: approximately 76% of tuition cash receipts flow from federal Title IV programs, making PRDO's revenue stream a function of government policy. The discount has been partially warranted: the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" signed July 2025 eliminates Grad PLUS loans for new graduate borrowers effective July 1, 2026, directly threatening USAHS, which was acquired for $138M just seven months before the legislation passed.

Given the severity and imminence of the Grad PLUS change, the lack of clarity on its impact to USAHS enrollment, and heavy insider selling patterns, the correct posture is to wait for visibility on USAHS enrollment trends post-July 2026 before committing capital.

## Company Overview

Perdoceo serves approximately 44,400 students across three institutions, with 91% enrolled in fully online programs and 68% over age 30. The student base is overwhelmingly non-traditional adult learners pursuing career-oriented degrees.

**CTU** is the core profit engine: $462M revenue, 39.1% operating margin, ~29,950 students. Corporate engagement programs represent 38.3% of CTU enrollment and serve as a critical non-federal revenue diversifier for 90-10 compliance. CTU's proprietary intellipath® adaptive learning platform is a genuine differentiator in the online education market.

**AIUS** ($226M revenue, 15.9% operating margin, ~10,560 students) includes AIU, Trident University International, and California Southern University. Trident's military-focused student body creates sensitivity to government shutdowns, which weighed on FY2025 results.

**USAHS** ($157.6M revenue, 2.0% operating margin, ~3,890 students) was acquired December 2024 for $138M. It offers graduate health sciences degrees (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, nursing) via campus-based and hybrid delivery in California, Florida, and Texas. The thin operating margin reflects $30.3M in acquired asset depreciation and amortization; adjusted for this, the segment is meaningfully profitable, but the campus-based model carries a structurally higher cost base than CTU or AIUS.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

Revenue grew 24.2% YoY to $846M in FY2025, though nearly all of the $165M increase came from the USAHS acquisition ($147.5M contribution). Organic performance was mixed: CTU grew 4.1%, AIUS declined 0.4%. Adjusted operating income rose to $237.6M (28.1% adjusted margin), up from $188.9M, demonstrating continued compounding in the legacy business.

Consolidated operating margin compressed from 25.6% (FY2024) to 23.2% (FY2025) because USAHS operates at a thin margin. This is mechanical dilution from the acquisition, not deterioration in the base business. Bad debt expense improved from 4.9% to 3.5% of revenue, a meaningful credit quality indicator.

Diluted EPS reached $2.42 ($2.61 adjusted), up from $2.19 ($2.26 adjusted) in FY2024 — an increase of 10.5% on a reported basis and 15.5% adjusted. The earnings beat streak is 4 consecutive quarters with an average surprise of ~4.2%.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is fortress-grade:
- Cash, short-term investments, and restricted cash: $643.5M
- Current ratio: 5.06x
- Total debt: $11.5M
- Debt-to-equity: 0.01x
- Altman Z-Score: 3.29 (safe zone)

Notable obligations include $154M in failed sale-leaseback liabilities (related to USAHS campus properties), $62.9M in operating leases, and $12.2M in finance leases. Even inclusive of these, total obligations are modest relative to the $972M in stockholders' equity.

The DOE Composite Score sits at the maximum 3.0 for both FY2024 and preliminary FY2025.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow of $224.9M (26.6% margin) on just $8.6M of CapEx demonstrates the capital efficiency of the online education model. Operating cash flow of $225.2M was 1.41x net income, indicating high-quality earnings with no accrual concerns.

### Capital Allocation

Management returned ~$158M to shareholders in FY2025: $120.8M in buybacks (4.1M shares at $29.17 average) and $36.9M in dividends. This represents ~70% of FCF. A new $100M repurchase authorization was approved January 2, 2026. The buyback execution at $29.17 average is below the current $32.35 price — modestly favorable timing.

### Consumer Discretionary Validation Gates

PRDO is technically classified as Consumer Discretionary / Education Services. Addressing the three conditions explicitly:

**(a) Positive sequential comparable revenue growth:** CTU grew 4.1% organically in FY2025; total enrollment grew 7.3% to 44,400 students. AIUS enrollment surged 11.2% (partly boosted by an extra session start). Management guides that "full year adjusted operating income is expected to be higher for 2026 as compared to 2025." This condition is met.

**(b) FCF yield ≥ 5%:** At a $2.03B market cap and $225M trailing FCF, FCF yield is approximately 11.1%. This condition is met by a wide margin.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** Consumer confidence is in a declining trend across broad measures (record-low sentiment readings, trucking fleet bankruptcies, mortgage stress). This condition is NOT met. However, PRDO's student base is structurally counter-cyclical — enrollment in career-oriented education programs historically increases during economic weakness as displaced workers seek credentials. The company's 10-K explicitly notes that enrollment growth is supported by recessionary labor market dynamics. The consumer confidence gate is designed for businesses whose revenue depends on consumer spending willingness; PRDO's revenue depends on adults seeking degree completion, which correlates positively with economic distress. The condition fails on technical grounds but the economic mechanism runs counter to the gate's intent.

Despite meeting two of three gates and having a credible counter-cyclical argument on the third, the regulatory risk profile described below prevents a BUY recommendation.

## Growth Analysis

Enrollment momentum is constructive: total students grew 7.3% YoY to 44,400 at December 31, 2025. CTU added ~1,870 students (+6.6%), driven by corporate engagement expansion and healthcare-adjacent programs. AIUS added ~1,070 students (+11.2%), though part of this was from an additional session start that won't recur. USAHS grew a more modest 2.6%.

Management's FY2026 guide — adjusted operating income higher than FY2025 — is vague but directionally positive. The single covering analyst has current-year EPS at $3.05 (up 9.7% from 30 days ago) and next-year at $3.27. At $3.05, the forward P/E is 10.6x; at $3.27, it is 9.9x.

If USAHS enrollment declines materially post-July 2026, consolidated growth could stall despite CTU and AIUS momentum. USAHS currently represents ~8.8% of total enrollment but 18.6% of consolidated revenue, so the revenue impact is disproportionate to headcount.

CTU's corporate engagement program expansion (now 38.3% of enrollment) is the most durable organic growth vector. These employer-sponsored students carry lower default rates, improve 90-10 compliance, and generate revenue independent of individual student borrowing decisions.

## Competitive Landscape

PRDO operates in a competitive and politically sensitive subsector. Traditional universities have expanded online offerings aggressively, eroding the for-profit sector's historical advantage in flexibility and convenience. Several competitors (Purdue Global, SNHU) operate as non-profits with lighter regulatory burdens.

CTU's competitive advantages are specific and measurable: (1) the intellipath® adaptive learning platform, which personalizes instruction and has been a retention driver; (2) the corporate engagement program, which provides employer-sponsored demand that competitors lack at this scale; and (3) strong retention/completion metrics that support gainful employment compliance.

AIUS faces more competitive pressure — the 0.4% revenue decline in FY2025 (pre-session adjustment) suggests market share pressure in the general online degree market. Trident's military niche provides some insulation but creates government shutdown vulnerability.

USAHS competes in healthcare education where demand is structurally strong (aging demographics, healthcare worker shortages), but faces capacity-constrained campus models and accreditation requirements that limit scalability. The Grad PLUS elimination is a competitive headwind specific to programs that previously offered students uncapped borrowing.

The competitive position is stable at CTU, slightly deteriorating at AIUS, and uncertain at USAHS. The regulatory environment creates asymmetric competitive risk for the for-profit subsector relative to traditional institutions.

## Risk Assessment

### Grad PLUS Elimination (Severity: HIGH, Probability: CONFIRMED)

USAHS students in physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech-language pathology programs previously relied on Grad PLUS for program costs that exceed the new $20,500/year Direct Unsubsidized cap. Under current consensus language, these programs are NOT classified as "professional programs" (which would allow $50,000/year). Management acknowledges they "cannot currently predict the extent to which reduced federal loan availability may influence prospective student demand."

The magnitude of impact depends on classification decisions not yet finalized, USAHS's ability to offer institutional financing alternatives, and price elasticity of demand for health sciences degrees. A 20-30% enrollment decline at USAHS would reduce consolidated revenue by 3.7-5.6% and compress earnings disproportionately given USAHS's high fixed-cost campus model.

### 90-10 Rule Compliance (Severity: HIGH, Probability: ONGOING)

With 76% of cash receipts from federal sources, PRDO operates with limited margin above the 90% ceiling (now inclusive of VA/DoD benefits). Management states all institutions passed preliminary FY2025 calculations with improvement, but compliance depends on student mix that PRDO cannot fully control. Corporate engagement programs are the primary compliance lever. Any future regulatory tightening of the threshold would be existential.

### Gainful Employment / Earnings Premium Accountability (Severity: MODERATE, Probability: UNCERTAIN)

The new earnings-premium test under the Reconciliation Act could revoke loan eligibility for underperforming programs. The Department has not published initial rates. PRDO's career-oriented programs should perform reasonably well on earnings metrics, but the uncertainty is non-trivial until initial data is released.

### Political Regime Change Risk (Severity: HIGH, Probability: STRUCTURAL)

The current Trump Administration is favorable to for-profit education: certifications renewed, provisional status removed, investigations closed. A future administration could reverse all of this. PRDO's 10-K explicitly warns of "dramatic shifts in regulatory approaches across different presidential administrations."

### Qui Tam Litigation (Severity: UNCERTAIN)

A pending False Claims Act qui tam action is disclosed without magnitude details. This is a standard sector risk but creates tail uncertainty.

The backwardation and elevated IV argue against taking new directional positions until the event resolves.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($40-42, +24-30%)

CTU enrollment growth accelerates through corporate engagement expansion. USAHS programs receive "professional program" classification under the Reconciliation Act, preserving $50,000/year borrowing access and neutralizing the Grad PLUS elimination impact. AIUS stabilizes with Trident military recovery. The company deploys $100M buyback authorization, reducing share count by 5%+. Adjusted EPS reaches $3.20+ in FY2026. The regulatory discount narrows as favorable classification decisions are published. Multiple re-rates toward 13-14x earnings, in line with Strategic Education (STRA, 15.1x).

### Bear Case ($22-24, -26-32%)

USAHS programs are confirmed as "non-professional" under the Reconciliation Act, capping borrowing at $20,500/year. New graduate student enrollment at USAHS drops 30-40% as students cannot fund program costs. The campus-based fixed cost structure creates operating deleverage, dragging USAHS to operating losses. Goodwill impairment on the $266M balance becomes probable. 90-10 ratios tighten industry-wide. A future administration re-intensifies for-profit regulatory pressure. Consolidated EPS declines to $2.00-2.20. The multiple compresses to 10-11x earnings on higher perceived risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **USAHS program classification clarity:** If USAHS programs receive "professional program" designation under the Reconciliation Act (allowing $50K/year borrowing), the primary regulatory risk to the acquisition thesis is neutralized. Monitor DOE implementation guidance, expected before July 2026.
2. **USAHS enrollment stability post-July 2026:** Two consecutive quarters of USAHS enrollment decline <5% following the Grad PLUS effective date would confirm the market has over-discounted the risk. The first actionable data point will be Q3 FY2026 enrollment (reported ~February 2027).
3. **Insider buying:** Open-market purchases by C-suite executives would signal confidence in the thesis. Current activity is entirely vest-and-sell, which is neutral to mildly negative.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **USAHS enrollment decline >15% in any quarter post-July 2026**, indicating Grad PLUS elimination is materially impairing demand.
2. **90-10 ratio approaching 85% for any institution**, indicating eroding compliance buffer.
3. **DOE Composite Score falling below 2.5**, indicating regulatory financial health concerns.

### Reassessment Timeline

The next natural reassessment points are: (1) any DOE implementation guidance on Reconciliation Act program classifications (expected Q2-Q3 2026); (2) the July 1, 2026 Grad PLUS effective date; and (3) Q3 FY2026 earnings (likely February 2027), which will be the first quarter reflecting full impact.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo is a fundamentally strong business with 27% FCF margins, $643M cash, a 4-quarter beat streak, accelerating buybacks, and the maximum DOE financial health score, trading at a meaningful discount to peers. The discount exists because ~76% of revenue flows from federal programs, creating binary regulatory dependency that the market correctly penalizes.

The critical uncertainty is the Grad PLUS elimination impact on USAHS, which was acquired seven months before the legislation passed. Until program classification decisions are finalized and post-July 2026 enrollment data is available, the USAHS overhang prevents full confidence in the forward earnings trajectory.

The appropriate posture is HOLD. The valuation is attractive enough that the stock could be a strong BUY if USAHS survives the regulatory change intact, but the magnitude and proximity of the Grad PLUS risk prevent committing capital today. The $643M cash balance provides substantial downside protection (cash alone is ~$10/share, or 31% of the stock price), limiting the bear case to ~$22-24. Monitor DOE classification guidance and USAHS enrollment trends for re-entry signals.
