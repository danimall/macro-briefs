**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida is a ~$21.1 billion-asset Florida community bank that reshaped its balance sheet in the second half of 2025 through two acquisitions (Heartland Bancshares in July and Villages Bancorporation/VBI in October, $829M), lifting assets roughly 37% in a single year. The core earnings franchise is strong and improving: adjusted EPS reached $0.62 in Q1 2026 (up 111% year-over-year), adjusted ROA hit 1.31%, adjusted ROTE 16.26%, and the efficiency ratio improved to 55.3% from ~63% a year earlier. Net interest margin expanded to 3.83% (FTE) as deposit costs fell to 1.54% and the January 2026 securities repositioning lifted asset yields. The headline GAAP figure (EPS $0.29, down from $0.37) is depressed entirely by a deliberate $39.5M pre-tax securities loss that is accretive to forward net interest income, not a credit event.

The central tension in the stock is credit quality set against the balance-sheet expansion. Nonperforming assets rose 30% quarter-over-quarter to $99.3M (0.47% of assets) and nonaccruals rose 32% to $95M, concentrated in the acquired Heartland/VBI commercial real estate and residential books, while the allowance-for-credit-losses ratio declined to 1.39% and Q1 provisioning was minimal at $0.8M. Management attributes the rise to collateral values that exceed loan balances (81% is real-estate-secured), which is plausible for acquisition-seasoning, but the combination of rising NPAs, a falling reserve ratio, and near-zero provisioning is the thread most likely to determine the next 12 months. CRE is ~49% of the loan book, and the VBI system conversion is not expected to finish until Q3 2026.

At $33.49 the stock trades near ~21x trailing EPS (distorted by the securities loss) but ~13x the $2.51 FY26 consensus and roughly 1.05x tangible book. Consensus estimates are essentially flat over 90 days, and analyst targets ($34-$38) sit only modestly above the current price. This is a fairly-valued regional bank mid-integration with a real core-earnings inflection offset by a legitimate credit watch item, aggressive buybacks near tangible book that signal management conviction, and no open-market insider buying. We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold), with the July 28 earnings report and its credit disclosures as the key near-term reassessment point.

## Company Overview

Seacoast operates almost entirely through its subsidiary Seacoast National Bank, running 104 full-service branches across Florida and Georgia with ~$16.6 billion in deposits and ~$2.8 billion in wealth-management AUM. It reports as a single segment spanning commercial and consumer banking, wealth management, mortgage, and insurance. The strategy is explicitly "balanced growth": organic deposit and loan generation and talent hiring from larger regionals, layered with a serial-acquisition engine that consolidates smaller Florida franchises.

The 2025 acquisitions define the current story. Heartland added four Central Florida branches and ~$823M in assets; VBI added 19 branches in North Central Florida, including The Villages retirement community, and ~$4.45B in assets with $3.45B of deposits. Combined, the deals added 23 branches, $5.3B in assets, and $4.2B in deposits, moving Seacoast from ~$15.2B to ~$21B in assets. The Villages franchise is strategically valuable: it brings a demographically resilient, deposit-rich customer base in one of the wealthiest, fastest-growing retirement markets in the country, strengthening an already low-cost funding base (87% non-time deposits, average loan size only ~$439K reflecting a granular, diversified book).

## Financial Analysis

**Core profitability is the positive story.** Adjusted net income was $67.8M in Q1 2026, up 111% year-over-year and 42% sequentially, evidence that the acquired scale is converting to operating leverage. FY2025 GAAP net income was $144.9M. The GAAP Q1 EPS is the single most misread number in the file: it reflects the January 2026 sale of ~$277M of ~1.9%-yield securities, reinvested into ~4.8%-yield agency MBS. That trade permanently raises forward net interest income and is not a credit signal.

**NIM expansion is real but partly transient.** Net interest margin (FTE) rose to 3.83% in Q1 2026 from 3.66% in Q4 2025 and 3.48% a year earlier. The mechanism: deposit costs repriced down to 1.54% (from 1.93%) faster than asset yields fell, and the securities repositioning lifted the earning-asset yield. Purchase-accounting accretion on acquired loans contributed $12.1M in the quarter; excluding accretion, NIM was 3.57% (still up from 3.24% year-over-year). As acquired loans pay down, the ~$12M/quarter accretion tailwind decays, so the ex-accretion 3.57% is the truer run-rate and the honest gauge of margin durability.

**Balance sheet and capital are conservative.** Loans stand at ~$12.6B (roughly flat sequentially, as organic loan growth is modest), securities ~$5.6B, and the loan/deposit ratio is ~76%, leaving ample room to fund growth with deposits. Capital ratios are comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds: total RBC 16.01%, Tier 1 14.60%, CET1 11.66%, leverage 10.40%, and tangible common-plus-preferred equity/tangible assets of 9.24%. Goodwill and intangibles total ~$1.22B after the deals, roughly 45% of tangible common equity, a structural consideration for an acquisitive bank and a latent impairment vector if acquired franchises underperform.

**Cash flow and rate risk.** Operating cash flow was $71.8M in Q1 2026, up from $31M. The bank pays a $0.19 quarterly dividend (raised from $0.18) and repurchased 317,628 shares in Q1 plus another 320,763 shares post-quarter at ~$31.18 under a $150M authorization expiring December 31, 2026. Accumulated other comprehensive loss was ~$78.6M against $2.7B of equity, modest and much improved from $158M a year ago, with an additional $98M unrecognized loss in the HTM book. The balance sheet is modestly asset-sensitive (+100bp = +1.2% NII in year one, +3.2% year two), so Fed rate cuts are a mild headwind to net interest income but a tailwind to securities marks. Management added $300M of interest-rate caps and portfolio-layer swaps to dampen directional exposure.

The earnings surprise record is respectable but not pristine: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the December 2025 quarter missing by 13.5%, a reminder that the acquisition-driven period carries genuine estimation noise.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a two-part story. The near-term earnings inflection is real but largely mechanical: the securities repositioning and full-quarter contribution from Heartland/VBI drive the year-over-year adjusted EPS more-than-doubling, and cost synergies are compressing the efficiency ratio. This is acquisition math and margin management, not organic revenue acceleration. Deposits are growing ~7% annualized organically (with 29% annualized growth in noninterest-bearing), which is genuinely healthy, but organic loan growth is modest and the reported balance-sheet expansion reflects deals rather than underlying demand.

The forward growth engine depends on three things: (1) clean completion of the VBI integration, which determines whether the promised synergies and deposit retention materialize; (2) continued M&A in a fragmented Florida market where Seacoast is a proven consolidator; and (3) the durability of the funding-cost advantage as rates fall. Consensus captures this as steady rather than accelerating growth: FY26 EPS of $2.51 rising to $2.71 in FY27, roughly 8% growth. Estimate revisions are flat: current-year is -0.3% over 90 days, next-year -1.3%, with 30-day movement essentially zero. There is no positive revision velocity that would signal a fundamental inflection the market is missing; the estimates have absorbed the acquisition accretion and settled.

The Growth Prospects score reflects this: the core-earnings improvement is real and the deposit franchise is attractive, but organic loan growth is modest, the accretion tailwind fades, and the growth trajectory is steady-compounding rather than accelerating. Given the pattern that growth signals only add reliable value alongside financial health confirmation, we weight this dimension directionally but do not treat the acquisition-driven year-over-year jump as durable momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

Against Regional Banks peers (SFNC, PFS, BANC, PRK, FBK), the peer-median P/E is 16.3x with a 2.5% dividend yield. SBCF's trailing P/E of ~21x is inflated by the securities loss and is not the right lens. On forward earnings, the stock trades at ~13.3x FY26 consensus and ~12.4x FY27, a discount to the forward multiples implied for most of the peer group and reasonable for a bank generating a 1.31% adjusted ROA and 16%+ adjusted ROTE. On tangible book, at ~1.05x, the valuation is undemanding for those return metrics.

Cheap on forward earnings requires a reason the market is wrong, and here the market's skepticism is well-grounded. The pay-vs-performance disclosure shows Seacoast's cumulative TSR (119.79) materially trailing its peer group (187.40), meaning the discount has persisted despite strong operating metrics. That discount prices dilution (diluted share count rose from ~85.4M to ~97.8M year-over-year), CRE/Florida concentration, integration execution, and, most acutely, the credit-quality question. Analyst price targets ($34 low, $38 high, $34.83 mean) sit only modestly above the current price, and with flat estimate revisions there is no catalyst for consensus to re-rate upward in the near term. Cheapness without a specific, falsifiable mispricing catalyst is a value-trap risk, and the credit watch item is precisely the kind of factor that can justify a persistent discount.

The valuation score lands at 5.0: the stock is fairly priced. It is not stretched (forward multiple below peers, decent tangible-book support), but it is not a clear asymmetric entry either. The re-rating case is real only if credit holds and integration lands cleanly; the discount is justified if acquired credit is weaker than modeled. That two-sidedness is the definition of a HOLD-quality valuation.

## Competitive Landscape

Seacoast competes in one of the most attractive U.S. banking geographies, Florida's migration-fueled, growing markets, against money-center banks, super-regionals, other Florida community banks, credit unions, and fintechs. Its edge is a granular, low-cost core deposit franchise (87% non-time deposits, cost of deposits 1.54%) and a relationship-based community-bank model, complemented by a demonstrated M&A engine consolidating a fragmented state. The VBI/Villages deal materially strengthens the funding franchise by adding a sticky, deposit-heavy, demographically resilient customer base.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the funding and scale dimensions: deposit costs are falling faster than peers in many cases, and the acquired branches expand density in desirable markets. The offsetting factor is that Seacoast's differentiation sits squarely in the sector's most-scrutinized credit vector: CRE at ~49% of loans (224% of bank risk-based capital versus the 300% regulatory soft guideline; construction 35% versus the 100% guideline). Office exposure ($550M) is characterized as suburban/low-rise with limited CBD concentration, a relative positive. On balance, the competitive position is solid and improving on funding, with the moat's credibility contingent on the acquired credit books performing as modeled. That yields a competitive score in the mid-6s, better than a market leader losing share, but not a clean structural winner given the concentration overhang.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit quality is the dominant near-term risk and it is flashing early yellow.** The ACL ratio *fell* to 1.39% (from 1.42%). Management leans on collateral values exceeding balances. If that optimism proves wrong, provisions could spike sharply; Q4 2025 already carried a $29.3M provision (largely the $22.7M day-one CECL double-count on VBI non-PCD loans, an accounting artifact, but a reminder of the acquired book's size). This is the most important item to press at the July 28 report.

**CRE and Florida concentration** compound the credit risk: nearly all lending and collateral is in Florida, exposing the book to hurricane/climate risk and the state's dislocated property-insurance market, which can impair collateral values and stress borrower cash flow independent of the macro cycle.

**Integration and dilution risk** remain live: deposit/customer attrition and synergy realization are still to be proven; $138M of remaining fair-value loan discount (4.0%) is a preliminary estimate subject to revision. The serial-acquirer model depends on stock issuance, and the $1.22B of intangibles carries goodwill-impairment risk. Beginning June 2027, ~$72.8M of trust preferred securities begin phasing from Tier 1 to Tier 2 capital, a modest, known headwind.

**Governance thread:** No open-market insider buying is present. Legacy-family director Dennis Hudson III has sold steadily (8,000 shares in March at $30.04, 8,000 in June at $31.41, 4,000 in July at $34.00), the CEO sold 10,367 shares in May (post-vesting), and the Chief Credit Officer also sold in May. Individually these are routine diversification/estate-planning transactions; collectively, alongside two unexplained Item 5.02 officer/director-change 8-Ks (March 26 and May 29, 2026, contents unconfirmed), they form a cluster worth monitoring given the credit backdrop. The CCO selling during a period of rising nonaccruals is the one worth watching most.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure is pricing an imminent event: 1-month ATM implied volatility is 84.0% against 26.7% one-year historical vol (a 57-point spread), with backwardation collapsing to 26.3% at six months and a ±15.3% implied move on the nearest expiry. This is almost entirely the July 28 earnings report, where the credit disclosures will resolve the key uncertainty. Put/call skew is elevated at 26.4%, signaling defensive positioning ahead of the print, consistent with the market's concern about the credit-quality thread rather than any structural bearishness (ATM IV collapsed from an anomalous 350.5% reading in mid-June, so the level should be read as event-pricing, not a persistent stress signal). Short interest is moderate at 6.4% of float (4.2 days to cover), not a crowded bearish position. The market is bracing for a binary credit update, which reinforces waiting for the July 28 report before adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-42):** Q2 credit disclosures show the NPA rise was acquisition-seasoning with collateral coverage intact and provisioning normalizes without a spike. VBI integration completes cleanly in Q3, synergies land, and ex-accretion NIM holds near 3.57% or expands as deposit costs continue repricing down. Adjusted ROA sustains ~1.3%, the market re-rates the forward multiple toward the peer median (16x) on the $2.71 FY27 estimate, and continued buybacks at ~1x tangible book accrete value. This is the re-rating path, roughly $38-42, or 13-25% upside, if the credit fears prove unfounded and integration lands.

**Bear Case ($26-28):** Acquired-portfolio credit deteriorates beyond seasoning, forcing a provisioning catch-up that the 1.39% reserve does not cover; office and retail CRE marks weaken; a Florida hurricane or insurance-market shock impairs collateral. NIM compresses as accretion runs off faster than deposit repricing helps, rate cuts pressure the asset-sensitive book, and the market extends the persistent TSR discount. Downside to ~$26-28, roughly 15-22% below current levels.

**Base Case ($34-36):** Credit is a manageable yellow flag rather than a red one, integration completes on schedule, core earnings compound at high-single digits, and the stock grinds toward the low end of analyst targets with the dividend and buyback providing support. This is the most-likely outcome and sits between the scenarios above, consistent with the flat estimate-revision backdrop and the modest analyst-target premium.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The core-earnings inflection is real and the franchise is attractive, but the credit watch item and the absence of positive estimate-revision velocity leave the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. The score trajectory (6.6 in May → 5.8 in June → 5.75 now) reflects genuine deterioration in the growth and valuation dimensions as the acquisition accretion was absorbed into estimates and the credit question surfaced; the current reading confirms rather than reverses that trend, though the price is roughly flat over the period so the risk/reward has not worsened materially. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the bar for changing the call rests on the July 28 credit disclosures.

**Base case target: $35.** ~14x the FY26 consensus, a modest premium to current forward multiple reflecting integration progress but capped by the persistent peer discount. ~4.5% upside plus a 2.3% dividend.

**Bull case target: $40.** ~15x the FY27 estimate with a re-rating toward peer median if credit fears clear. ~19% upside.

**Bear case target: $27.** ~17% downside if acquired credit deteriorates and provisioning spikes.

**Timeframe: 6-12 months**, with the July 28, 2026 earnings report (and the Q3 VBI conversion completion) as the decisive catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 shows NPA/nonaccrual stabilization or decline with provisioning proportionate to reserve adequacy, confirming the credit rise was acquisition-seasoning.
- Ex-accretion NIM holds at/above 3.57% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating durable margin power beyond the accretion tailwind.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (FY26/FY27 estimates rising) combined with clean VBI conversion, or a pullback toward tangible book (~$32 or below) that widens the discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Nonaccruals continue rising with a material provision catch-up that erodes the sub-1.4% reserve, signaling the acquired book is weaker than modeled.
- Adjusted ROA falls below ~1.1% as accretion runoff outpaces deposit-cost relief, undermining the core-earnings thesis.
- Confirmation that the Item 5.02 filings involve a credit-function departure, or accelerating clustered insider selling alongside deteriorating credit metrics.

## Conclusion

Seacoast is a well-run, well-capitalized Florida community bank with a genuinely improving core-earnings profile that a screen misreads through the lens of a deliberate, accretive securities-repositioning loss. The funding franchise is a real advantage, the VBI/Villages deal strengthens it, and management is buying back stock near tangible book while capital ratios sit comfortably above requirements. Against that, the acquired-portfolio credit metrics are rising while reserves and provisioning are not keeping pace, CRE is ~49% of the book, integration is unfinished, and no insider is buying in the open market. The forward valuation (~13x, ~1.05x tangible book) is fair rather than cheap, and estimates are flat.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the appreciation case exists if credit holds and integration lands, but it is contingent, and the near-term options market is pricing a binary event at the July 28 report. A clean print upgrades the risk/reward toward a BUY; a provisioning catch-up validates the market's persistent discount and moves the stock toward the bear case.
