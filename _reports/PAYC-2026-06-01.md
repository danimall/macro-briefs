**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Paycom is a founder-led, cloud-based Human Capital Management (HCM) provider built on a single-database architecture, with payroll as the mandatory anchor application and an attached suite of talent, HR, and time-and-labor modules. The business generates roughly 43% adjusted EBITDA margins, $408M of FY25 free cash flow (~20% margin), and 91% annual revenue retention. After a peak near $268 in mid-2025, the stock has fallen 45% to ~$148, and now trades at ~13.6x forward consensus EPS ($10.92 CY26), a steep discount to both its own history and to industrial-sector software peers.

The central analytical issue is reading past GAAP noise. Headline net income fell from $502M (2024) to $453M (2025), and operating margin compressed from 33.7% to 27.6%. That decline is mechanical: 2024 included a $117.5M stock-comp reversal when CEO Chad Richison forfeited a large performance award during the brief Co-CEO transition, while 2025 SBC normalized, a ~$140M swing in the year-over-year comparison. On a cleaner basis, adjusted EBITDA grew 13.8% to $882M, non-GAAP net income grew 12% to $519M, and Q1 FY2026 operating margin expanded to 36.8% from 34.9%. Estimate revisions are rising (+6.7% current-year, +8.8% next-year over 90 days), and Q1 EPS beat by 3.3%.

The offsetting concerns are substantive and not artifacts. Revenue growth has decelerated from ~25-30% historically to high-single digits, with FY26 guidance of ~$2.19B implying ~7%, softer than HCM peers. The automation strategy (Beti, IWant) cuts Paycom's own costs but structurally suppresses the per-transaction fees that once fueled growth, the same dynamic that triggered the 2023 guidance cut. Float income (~5-6% of revenue) is falling with rates. And the company has undergone a capital-allocation regime change, drawing $675M on its revolver to repurchase ~16% of its float in a single quarter, loosening its leverage covenant from 3.0x to 3.5x. The cheap multiple and margin discipline argue one direction; the structural deceleration, governance instability, and new leverage argue the other. We rate PAYC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Paycom delivers a comprehensive HCM platform as SaaS, spanning payroll, talent acquisition, talent management, HR management, and time and labor on a single system-of-record. The unified database is the core differentiator: there is no integration friction between modules, the incremental cost to add applications is low, and switching costs are high once a client adopts payroll plus attached modules. Revenue is earned on a per-employee, per-billing-period basis, supplemented by interest on client payroll funds held in float (~$2.7B average in FY25, $3.1B in Q1'26).

Scale metrics: 39,199 clients in FY25 (~20,300 on a parent-company basis), with annual revenue retention improving to 91% from 90%. The business operates as a single segment, overwhelmingly North America, with no client exceeding 10% of revenue. Flagship products are Beti, an employee-driven self-service payroll engine, and IWant, a command-driven AI interface. The strategic thesis that systems should automate tasks rather than have people perform them is now reducing Paycom's own headcount and operating expenses, a margin tailwind that also reflects a more mature growth posture.

Competitors range from far larger incumbents (ADP, Paychex, Workday, Oracle, SAP, UKG, Intuit) to nimbler mid-market rivals (Paylocity, Dayforce). An emerging structural threat is white-label and embedded payroll, which could erode the standalone HCM model over time.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $1,375M (2022) → $1,694M (2023) → $1,883M (2024) → $2,052M (2025, +9%). FY26 guidance of ~$2.19B implies ~7% growth. The deceleration from historical 25-30% rates is the defining fundamental fact about this business.

**Profitability past the GAAP noise:** The reported operating-margin decline and 9.7% net income drop are distorted by the ~$140M SBC swing between the 2024 forfeiture reversal and 2025 normalization. The cleaner picture: adjusted EBITDA grew 13.8% to $882M (~43% margin), non-GAAP net income grew 12% to $519M, and non-GAAP diluted EPS rose to $9.24 from $8.21. Q1 FY2026 confirmed the underlying health: revenue $571.9M (+7.8%), GAAP net income $155.7M (+11.8%), and operating margin expanding to 36.8% as R&D and operating expenses fell year-over-year. The automation cost discipline is visible in the income statement.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** FY25 operating cash flow was $679M against $271M capex, yielding ~$408M free cash flow. The heavy internal-software capitalization ($153M in FY25) flatters expensed R&D and is worth monitoring, since it shifts development cost from the income statement to the balance sheet and depreciation. The widely-screened Altman Z-Score of 1.09 ("distress") and debt/equity of 3.39 are mechanical distortions caused by the client funds obligation (~$5.1B at Dec-25), a pass-through liability fully matched by funds held for clients on the asset side. The operating business is highly profitable and cash-generative.

**Float income headwind:** Interest on client funds fell 9.6% to $113M in FY25 (5.5% of revenue) as rates declined, despite average float rising to $2.7B. Sensitivity is approximately $26M per 100bps. With the macro path tilted toward eventual easing, though the near-term rate picture is genuinely two-sided, this high-margin revenue line carries downside that float-agnostic models tend to overlook.

**The new leverage:** In Q1 FY2026, Paycom borrowed $675M on its revolver and repurchased 8.375M shares (~16% of float) for ~$1.06B at an average $126.61. Shares outstanding fell from 54.8M to 46.6M; equity dropped from $1.73B to $0.81B. The revolver was expanded from $1.0B to $1.46B (Mar 12) to $2.125B (Apr 23), maturity extended to 2031, and the leverage covenant loosened from 3.0x to 3.5x. A new $2.0B buyback (no expiration) was authorized May 4. This is the first meaningful debt the company has carried in years.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct components that pull in opposite directions. On the positive side, recurring revenue grew 9% in Q1, retention improved to 91%, and consensus estimates are rising: current-year EPS up 6.7% over 90 days to $10.92, next-year up 8.8% to $12.30. The 90-day revision momentum is real and constructive, and the margin expansion (Q1 operating margin +190bps) demonstrates that automation is converting to profitability.

On the negative side, the top-line deceleration to high-single digits is structural, not cyclical. Beti and the broader automation push cannibalize the unscheduled-payroll and error-correction fees that historically drove growth, the precise dynamic that forced the 2023 guidance cut. Management is betting that automation improves client ROI and retention enough to offset the suppressed transaction fees, and the retention data supports that bet at the platform level. But attrition remains concentrated in smaller clients, and FY26 guidance runs below HCM peers (Paylocity, Dayforce). The estimate-revision signal here does not meet the threshold of an inflection BUY: revenue is decelerating, not accelerating from a depressed base, and the EPS revisions are driven largely by margin and buyback-aided share-count reduction rather than top-line reacceleration. We treat this as a margin-and-capital-return story layered on a maturing growth franchise, not a growth re-acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$148 against CY26 consensus EPS of $10.92, PAYC trades at ~13.6x forward earnings; on next-year $12.30 it is ~12.1x. Against its own history of 25-40x and against the peer set provided (Industrials median P/E 51.2, though that cohort is heterogeneous and not a clean HCM comparison), the multiple is genuinely depressed. The software peers in HCM (ADP, Paychex, Paylocity, Dayforce) trade meaningfully higher on forward earnings.

The specific case for mispricing: the market appears to be anchoring on the GAAP earnings decline and the slowing top line, while underpricing the ~43% adjusted EBITDA margin, ~20% FCF margin, and the aggressive buyback shrinking the share count. The management team is voting with the balance sheet, repurchasing 16% of the float at an average price (~$127) well below current levels and far below the poorly-timed 2024 repurchases at $213.81. For a debt-free company to lever up specifically to buy stock is a clear conviction signal on undervaluation.

The case against treating this as deeply asymmetric: at ~12-14x forward, a 7%-growth business with falling float income and automation-driven fee cannibalization is not obviously cheap on a growth-adjusted basis. The PEG is reasonable but not compelling, since much of the EPS growth is buyback-engineered rather than organic. A 12-14x multiple is a defensible steady-state for a maturing, founder-controlled software company that has lagged its peer group on TSR (35.95 vs. 180.77 over the measurement window). We see the valuation as supportive rather than asymmetric: it limits downside and provides a floor, but does not on its own force a re-rating. We score valuation a 6.5: cheap enough to matter, not cheap enough to be a standalone BUY case.

Analyst targets span $120 (low) to $195 (high), with a median of $144.50 and mean of $151.44, bracketing the current price almost symmetrically, consistent with a balanced setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Paycom's moat rests on its single-database architecture, which produces low integration friction, low incremental module cost, and high switching costs. Retention at 91% and rising confirms the stickiness. The automation depth (Beti, IWant) is a genuine differentiator and a structural margin advantage as it reduces Paycom's own service headcount.

The trajectory, however, is the concern the scoring framework weights most heavily. Paycom's growth has fallen behind Paylocity and Dayforce, suggesting share loss at the margin in the mid-market. The embedded/white-label payroll trend threatens the standalone HCM model over a multi-year horizon, allowing software platforms to offer payroll natively without a Paycom-style standalone relationship. Against far larger incumbents (ADP, Workday), Paycom remains a focused, well-run niche player, but the competitive trajectory is flat-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving. We score competitive position 5.5: a solid moat with a decelerating relative growth profile.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth deceleration and automation self-cannibalization.** The per-employee, per-transaction model means efficiency gains can suppress near-term revenue. Beti automates away the very fees that drove historical growth. This is the structural risk underpinning the depressed multiple and is not fully resolved.

**Leverage regime change.** The shift from debt-free to drawn on a $2.125B facility, with the covenant loosened to 3.5x, introduces balance-sheet risk that did not exist a year ago. If buybacks continue at this pace and float income compresses with rates, the interest burden and leverage become a more material consideration. This is conviction-signaling and risk-creating simultaneously.

**Float/rate sensitivity.** ~$26M revenue sensitivity per 100bps; already down 9.6% in FY25. Eventual rate cuts would pressure this high-margin line.

**Cybersecurity and data sensitivity.** Paycom holds vast PII and payroll data, discloses having been targeted, and a third-party vendor breach previously exposed certain client/employee data. The new licensed LLM running on internal data adds breach and compliance surface.

**Governance instability.** Richison ceded then re-took sole CEO/President/Chairman within roughly two years, accompanied by a cluster of Section 5.02 officer changes (Aug'25, Sept'25, Jan'26, Mar'26) and a new Feb 2026 letter agreement that re-established a $22.8M FY25 comp package (up from ~$3.5M). Founder-controlled, no key-man insurance. The severe TSR underperformance frames the deep-value narrative but also reflects a multi-year governance and execution gap.

**Regulatory.** EU AI Act substantive provisions take effect Aug 2026; expanding US state privacy laws; money-transmitter/BSA regimes (partially mitigated by the OCC-approved Paycom National Trust Bank, which lets it exit state money-transmitter licensing); FCRA background-check liability.

The options market reads as neutral-to-slightly-constructive, neither confirming a strong bull case nor flagging an imminent downside catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$185-195)

The market has misread a stock-comp-driven GAAP decline as fundamental deterioration. Adjusted EBITDA grew ~14%, Q1 operating margin expanded to 36.8%, and estimates are rising. At ~13.6x forward on a ~43% EBITDA-margin, ~20% FCF-margin franchise with 91% retention, the multiple compresses far below software peers. Management's aggressive, leverage-funded buyback of 16% of the float at ~$127, well below current price, drives EPS accretion and signals deep conviction. As the GAAP comparison normalizes through 2026 and the Street recognizes the margin trajectory, the multiple re-rates toward the high-teens, supporting the $195 analyst high. Falling share count compounds the per-share recovery.

### Bear Case (~$120)

Revenue growth settles into a structural ~7% with no path back to double digits, as automation cannibalizes legacy fees and embedded/white-label payroll erodes the standalone model. Float income keeps falling with rates, removing a high-margin tailwind. The leverage-funded buyback proves to be the same mistimed capital allocation that produced 2024 repurchases at $213.81, now layering interest expense and covenant risk onto a decelerating business. Governance instability and severe TSR underperformance keep the multiple anchored at 12-13x. The stock drifts toward the $120 analyst low, ~19% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The competing forces — a genuinely cheap multiple with improving margins and a conviction-signaling buyback against structural top-line deceleration, governance instability, and new leverage — do not net to a confident directional call. We do not have a differentiated insight that the market is clearly wrong in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic recurring-revenue growth reaccelerating above 10% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the automation-cannibalization drag has been outgrown.
- FY26/FY27 revenue guidance revised upward toward double digits, paired with continued operating-margin expansion above 37%.
- Open-market insider buying by Richison or the CFO/COO (absent to date — only routine grants and minor sales), which would corroborate the buyback's undervaluation signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage approaching the loosened 3.5x covenant while free cash flow weakens, indicating buybacks are straining the balance sheet.
- Annual revenue retention falling below 90%, signaling the automation strategy is costing clients rather than retaining them.
- A guidance cut on revenue (the 2023 pattern recurring), or a material cybersecurity incident affecting client funds or PII.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026, ~early August), which will show whether the margin expansion holds, whether the buyback pace continues, and whether retention and recurring-revenue growth are stabilizing. The June 16-17 FOMC also bears on the float-income trajectory.

## Conclusion

Paycom is a well-run, high-margin HCM franchise whose GAAP optics overstate a decline that is largely a stock-comp artifact; the underlying business grew adjusted EBITDA ~14% and expanded operating margins in the most recent quarter. At ~13.6x forward earnings after a 45% drawdown, with rising estimates and a management team aggressively repurchasing stock, the downside is reasonably contained and the valuation provides support. Against that, the growth deceleration to ~7% is structural, the automation strategy suppresses the fees that once drove growth, float income is falling, and the company has taken on meaningful new leverage to fund buybacks, introducing balance-sheet risk and amplifying the consequences of any execution stumble in a founder-controlled company that has badly lagged its peer group on total return. The prior report's upgrade to BUY rested on the deep-value-plus-buyback thesis; we step back to HOLD because the structural growth and governance concerns are sufficient to offset the valuation appeal, and because the EPS-revision momentum is buyback- and margin-driven rather than evidence of a top-line inflection. Owning this stock requires a clear view that automation-led retention will eventually reaccelerate growth, which the current data does not yet confirm.
