**Research Date: August 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TechnipFMC has delivered one of the cleanest operating turnarounds in oilfield services. Gross margin expanded 833 basis points in three years (13.07% in FY2022 to 21.40% in FY2025), adjusted EBITDA margin roughly doubled from 8.8% in 2021 to 18.5% in 2025, long-term debt fell 60% from $999.3M to $395.7M, and three rating agencies moved the company two notches above investment grade. Free cash flow of $764.8M in the first half of 2026 funded $684.9M of buybacks, cutting the share count 7.1% in eighteen months. Second-quarter adjusted EPS of $0.91 beat consensus by 13%, the fourth straight beat, and consensus current-year EPS has risen 5.1% in ninety days to $3.10.

None of that is in dispute. The problem is price. At the frozen close of $78.61 the equity is worth $30.9B and the enterprise $30.3B against net cash of $590M. That is 25.4x current-year consensus, 22.0x next-year consensus $3.58, 13.6–14.4x our guidance-implied FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $2.11–2.23B, and a 4.2–4.7% free-cash-flow yield on guided FCF of $1.3–1.45B. The peer median forward P/E in oil and gas equipment and services is 18.1x. The stock sits 2.3% below its 52-week high, 3.9% above the $75.67 mean analyst target, and management's own last disclosed repurchases were at $72.01 in May and $68.35 in June, with none in April and $1.5B of authorization still available.

The more consequential issue is what the order book says about 2027. First-half inbound orders fell 17.6% year over year to $4,879.0M, Subsea inbound fell 17.4%, and book-to-bill was 0.93x for the half and 0.99x in Q2. Total backlog of $16,440.0M is down 1.2% year over year, with Surface backlog down 27.4%. Only $5,405.8M of backlog is scheduled for recognition in 2027 — roughly 51% of a $10.6B revenue run rate. Management's stated confidence in $10B of 2026 Subsea inbound requires $5,589M in the second half, 26.7% above the first half. The second half also carries a tax rate that must rise to roughly 32-33% to hit the 27-31% full-year guide, capex nearly doubling from $115.7M to about $224M, and $93.2M of AOCI FX losses scheduled to reclassify into earnings over the next twelve months. Guidance was explicitly not raised at Q2. We move to REDUCE: trim into strength, revisit below the mid-$60s or on confirmed order acceleration.

## Company Overview

TechnipFMC is a UK-incorporated plc headquartered in Houston with approximately 22,000 employees, operating two segments. Subsea, roughly 90% of revenue, designs and manufactures subsea production systems and performs engineering, procurement, construction and installation for offshore developments. Its distinguishing construct is iEPCI, a single integrated contract combining the subsea production system, the SURF scope (umbilicals, risers, flowlines) and vessel installation. The company is one of two players able to self-perform that full scope at scale, the other being SLB's OneSubsea joint venture, because it owns both the product lines and an installation fleet. Subsea 2.0 is the pre-engineered product platform sold under a configure-to-order model; management quantifies the benefit as up to 25% lower product cost and up to twelve months less delivery time.

Surface Technologies, roughly 10% of revenue, supplies wellhead and pressure-control equipment. The mix has been deliberately internationalized: 65% of segment revenue was international in 2025 and US land was under 5% of total company revenue, with segment backlog concentrated in ADNOC and Saudi Aramco.

Backlog spans TotalEnergies Mozambique LNG and GranMorgu, bp Tiber, Kaskida and the Northern Endurance Partnership, Petrobras Mero 3 HISEP and Global 24, Shell Orca, Equinor Raia, Vår Energi Ofelia and Gjøa Nord, Eni Coral North, Woodside Great Western Flank Phase 4 and Energean Katlan — a genuinely diversified operator list. Two new-energy scopes sit in backlog rather than in a pipeline: Mero 3 HISEP (seabed CO2 separation and reinjection) and the Northern Endurance Partnership, described as the first all-electric subsea integrated EPCI for carbon capture in the UK.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement trajectory.** Revenue grew 16.4% in 2023, 17.1% in 2024 and 9.0% in 2025 to $9,640.5M. Pretax income moved $68.9M → $206.6M → $940.4M → $1,270.0M. Net income was $963.9M in FY2025 on 419.7M diluted shares, roughly $2.30 of GAAP diluted EPS. Effective tax rates were erratic — 74.9% in 2023, 9.0% in 2024, 23.9% in 2025 — so pretax income is the cleaner comparison across the period.

**H1 2026.** Revenue rose 10.2% to $5,255.8M; net income attributable rose 51.4% to $623.2M; adjusted EBITDA rose to $1,047.9M from $864.6M, a margin of 19.9% versus 18.1%. In Q2 specifically, revenue rose 9.0% to $2,763.1M, Subsea revenue rose 12.2% to $2,486.9M with operating margin of 19.6% (17.2% a year earlier, 15.8% in Q1) and adjusted EBITDA margin of 23.2%. Surface revenue fell 13.3% to $276.2M but operating margin improved to 14.1% from 7.3% against a prior-year quarter carrying $19.0M of restructuring.

**Quality of the margin gain.** Q2 2026 carried a net $17.2M unfavorable impact from changes in contract estimates ($73.3M unfavorable, $56.1M favorable offsets) versus $123.3M favorable in Q2 2025. Adjusting reported gross profit for those items — an analyst construct, since ASC 606 cumulative catch-up adjustments are not cleanly separable and a favorable revision may relate to margin properly earned in earlier periods — implies roughly 25.4% underlying gross margin this year against roughly 18.5% last year. Reported gross margin expanded only 140bp because a ~$140M adverse revision swing absorbed the rest. Directionally this argues the margin improvement is pricing and execution rather than reserve release. The counter-reading, that 2025's large favorable revisions pulled margin forward and 2026 is normalizing, still leaves the underlying rate higher.

**Below-the-line help.** Q2 net interest expense fell to $3.6M from $14.4M and the tax rate to 24.0% from 28.4% (H1: 25.2% versus 32.0%). Full-year guidance is 27-31%. With H1 pretax of roughly $832M and H1 tax of roughly $210M, hitting 29% on approximately $1.70B of full-year pretax requires an H2 rate near 32-33%. Each 100bp on roughly $870M of H2 pretax is about $8.7M, or $0.02 per share. That is a mechanical second-half headwind the Q2 print does not display.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $991.8M against total debt of $401.9M leaves net cash of $589.9M at 6/30/26. Debt is legacy private placements: 4.00% due 2027 ($85.5M), 4.00% due 2032 ($114.0M), 3.75% due 2033 ($114.0M) plus $91.9M of bank borrowings. The $1.25B revolver is fully undrawn with no letters of credit outstanding, a $1.0B commercial paper program established June 2025 is unused, and a $500M performance letter-of-credit facility expandable to $1.0B supports $2,412.9M of outstanding performance guarantees. Ratings moved to Moody's Baa2 (Sept 2025), Fitch BBB (June 2026) and S&P BBB (June 2026); collateral was released and certain negative covenants fell away on reaching investment grade.

Book equity of $3,363.8M is small relative to market capitalization because of a $(3,761.4)M accumulated deficit and $(1,357.4)M of AOCI, legacy of post-merger goodwill impairments. Any book-value or Altman-style screen will misread this company; the cash-flow, coverage and liquidity measures are the relevant ones and they are strong.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $880.5M against capex of $115.7M, giving $764.8M of free cash flow. Working capital was a net $53.7M drag in H1 2026 versus a $44.6M benefit in H1 2025, so the $94.6M increase in operating cash flow came from earnings rather than balance-sheet timing — a favorable quality signal. Two offsets deserve attention. Trade receivables rose 31.8% to $1,487.4M while H1 revenue grew 10.2%, stretching days sales outstanding from roughly 43 to roughly 52 on annualized period revenue; payables rose in parallel (+32.2% to $1,560.2M) and the supplier finance balance rose from $123.2M to $143.7M. The most likely explanation is milestone timing on a ramping Subsea book partly funded by extending payables. Mitigating evidence against a collectability concern: the allowance for credit losses fell from $45.3M to $42.2M with a $3.1M release, versus an $8.1M provision a year earlier.

**The structural working-capital engine, and its reverse gear.** Contract liabilities of $2,358.9M exceed contract assets of $1,295.5M, a net customer-advance position of $1,063.4M, about 11% of FY2025 revenue in interest-free funding. This is why a 1.08 current ratio is not a liquidity warning. It also creates a specific downside asymmetry: in a period of declining awards, backlog burns off advances without new advances replacing them, and free cash flow falls faster than earnings. With H1 inbound already down 17.6%, this is the channel through which an order slowdown would appear first — in cash flow, before revenue or margin.

**Capital allocation.** H1 buybacks of $684.9M (Q2: $420.1M, 5,869,638 shares at averages of $72.01 in May and $68.35 in June, none in April) against $39.7M of dividends. Cumulative repurchases since 2022 total $2.3B; the board added $2.0B in October 2025 taking authorization to $3.8B with $1.5B remaining, equal to 19.1M shares or 4.9% of the 392.8M outstanding at $78.61. Share count went 423.0M → 400.7M → 392.8M. The dividend of $0.20 annualized yields 0.25%. Debt repayment was only $15.0M in H1 2026 versus $241.6M a year earlier; the deleveraging phase is finished and cash now goes to repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

The forward picture divides cleanly between confirmed 2026 delivery and unconfirmed 2027 growth.

FY2026 guidance, issued 19 February 2026 and explicitly not updated at Q2, calls for Subsea revenue of $9.2–9.6B at 21-22% adjusted EBITDA margin, Surface revenue of $1.15–1.3B at 16.5-18%, corporate expense of $115–125M, capex of about $340M and free cash flow of $1.3–1.45B. Management said Subsea revenue and margin are both tracking toward the high end. Our guidance-implied adjusted EBITDA is $2,112M at the midpoint and $2,231M at the high end, requiring $1.06–1.18B in H2 against $1,047.9M delivered in H1. The FCF guide implies H2 free cash flow of $535–685M, below the H1 run rate, consistent with capex nearly doubling. We deliberately do not annualize Q2's $487.9M of FCF, which reflects milestone-driven advances, a $338.4M H1 payables inflow and a cash tax rate below the guided full-year rate.

The 2026 delivery is largely locked. 2027 is not. Backlog scheduled for recognition is $4,043.0M for the remainder of 2026, $5,405.8M for 2027 and $6,991.2M for 2028 and beyond. Even the current half requires roughly $1,326M — about 25% of H2 revenue — to be booked and burned within the period, normal for Subsea Services and Surface but a reminder that backlog is a less complete forward indicator than its size implies. For 2027, $5,405.8M covers roughly 51% of a $10.6B run rate. The rest must be won.

Against that, H1 inbound fell 17.6%. Management targets $10B of 2026 Subsea inbound, which needs $5,589M in H2, 26.7% above H1, followed by a "step-up in orders in 2027" extending through the end of the decade. The nuance favoring management: weakness was concentrated in Q1. Q2 Subsea inbound rose 31.7% sequentially and Q2 Subsea book-to-bill was about 1.0x. Q2 awards include Azule Energy Greater PAJ in Angola ($75–250M), Vår Energi Ofelia and Gjøa Nord iEPCI ($500M–1B, following a five-year collaboration agreement), Eni Baleine Phase 3, and an Equinor Norway portfolio of $250–500M. The commercial pattern matters more than the amounts: direct awards and multi-year collaboration frameworks rather than competitively bid one-offs, and brownfield tie-backs alongside greenfield. If clients genuinely move to portfolio contracting, intake lumpiness declines and switching costs rise.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest — current year +3.8% over 30 days and +5.1% over 90 days, next year +0.8% and +3.0%. This is steady upward drift on a well-covered name, not the inflection velocity that would justify overriding valuation discipline. Consensus implies EPS of $3.10 this year and $3.58 next, roughly 15% growth, against revenue growth decelerating from 17.1% to 9.0% to a guided ~10%.

Offshore FIDs are driven by multi-year planning rather than spot prices, so a normalization of the geopolitical premium would likely do more damage to FTI's multiple than to its order book.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural picture is improving. Saipem and Subsea7 announced a merger in February 2025; through 2026 it cleared Brazil, Turkey and the US HSR waiting period, but the European Commission opened a full Phase II investigation on 22 July 2026, warning the deal may lead to price increases and less innovation. Either outcome helps TechnipFMC. Completion consolidates the SURF and installation market from three credible integrated players toward two-plus, supporting pricing — the Commission's own stated concern is effectively a regulator confirming that thesis. A prolonged Phase II absorbs senior management bandwidth at the merging parties and can impose remedies, plausibly moderating their commercial aggression on new tenders across several quarters.

The offsetting evidence is that the iEPCI advantage is a lead rather than a monopoly. SLB's OneSubsea was awarded an EPC contract by bp for a subsea boosting system at Thunder Horse in June 2026, described as a milestone in subsea standardization. bp appears in both companies' award lists — FTI's backlog includes Tiber, Kaskida and the Northern Endurance Partnership — so major operators are deliberately dual-sourcing integrated scopes rather than granting exclusivity. OneSubsea is pursuing the same standardization-plus-integration playbook, and Aker Solutions remains a credible Norwegian-centered competitor. There is also a longer-term tension in the Subsea 2.0 logic: standardization compresses operators' cycle time and project breakeven, which expands the sanctionable set, but it also commoditizes hardware over time and transfers savings to customers. That is consistent with the observed pattern where FTI's advantage shows up in volume, utilization and margin rate rather than headline product pricing.

The balance sheet has become a competitive input rather than a constraint. In integrated EPCI, the ability to post large performance bonds cheaply is a bid-qualification requirement. With $2,412.9M of performance guarantees outstanding supported by an undrawn $1.25B revolver, an unused $1.0B commercial paper program and a $500M dedicated performance-LC facility expandable to $1.0B, FTI qualifies for scopes that more leveraged contractors cannot underwrite at the same cost.

Demand framing per the company: offshore is taking a growing share of upstream capex, with the greatest potential acceleration in the Gulf of America, North Sea and West Africa and frontier growth in Suriname, Namibia and Mozambique. We discount this framing — it is the same narrative every offshore contractor uses and could not be corroborated with independent FID-pipeline data. The falsifiable version is narrower: does Subsea inbound reach roughly $10B in 2026, and does a genuine step-up appear in 2027 announcements.

## Risk Assessment

**Order intake versus revenue is the central tension.** H1 inbound of $4,879.0M was 17.6% below prior year; Subsea inbound down 17.4%; H1 book-to-bill 0.93x; total backlog down 1.2% year over year with Surface down 27.4%. The 2026 revenue and margin strength is being delivered out of a book won in 2023-25. Q2's sequential improvement is encouraging but does not yet establish the trend management describes.

**Contract-estimate and fixed-price exposure.** The Q2 swing from $123.3M favorable to $17.2M unfavorable illustrates how much reported gross margin can move on estimate revisions. Management states no project had an individually material impact and no 8-K has disclosed a project charge in two years, which is meaningful corroboration for an EPC contractor. The exposure remains structural.

**Customer-advance unwind.** The $1,063.4M net advance position is the specific mechanism by which softer awards would hit free cash flow ahead of revenue. This is the least appreciated element of the story.

**Geographic and counterparty concentration.** Latin America was 39.4% of Q2 Subsea revenue and 39.6% of H1, with Petrobras projects named in backlog. We do not have customer-concentration percentages — Item 1 of the FY2025 10-K was not retrievable — and we explicitly decline to infer diversification from a shifting regional mix. Brazil exposure is amplified off-balance-sheet through the Dofcon Brasil 50/50 JV with DOF Subsea, where FTI guarantees $228.9M of vessel debt owed to third-party lenders and is owed $95.6M by the JV under a loan due June 2028.

**Middle East cuts both ways, measurably.** Surface Middle East revenue fell 34% to $77.8M in Q2, attributed in the filing to regional conflict, with Surface backlog down 27.4% and concentrated in ADNOC and Saudi Aramco. Management separately argues that a re-rating of Middle East risk would accelerate capital flows toward offshore basins where Subsea operates. Item 1A includes an explicit piracy and maritime-conflict risk, unusual and directly relevant to an installation-fleet owner.

**FX.** Notional hedge positions extend into 2H2029 across EUR, GBP, NOK and AUD, with approximately $93.2M of AOCI losses expected to reclassify into earnings over the next twelve months. Q2 carried a $19.3M FX loss versus a $12.1M gain, a $31.4M swing that suppressed reported adjusted EBITDA (hence management's emphasis on the $601.2M ex-FX figure). The AOCI reclassification is a quantified near-certain headwind.

**Corporate form.** Item 1A flags additional restrictions on dividends or repurchases as an English plc. UK distributable-reserves rules interact awkwardly with a $3,761.4M accumulated deficit, and buybacks are now the dominant return mechanism. We could not verify distributable-reserve headroom.

**Governance and insider signal.** The shareholder-approved Value Creation Plan, capped at 3.6M PSUs on a ROIC vesting condition, vested approximately 2.3M PSUs in July 2026 after the Committee determined ROIC was 60% higher than before adoption. The CEO received 1,626,240 shares, 72.9% of the total, worth roughly $128M at $78.61; the CFO and CTO received 302,016 each. Aggregate dilution is 0.57% of outstanding shares, or 0.56% post-issuance ownership dilution — modest against the headline dollar figure, and below the 0.92% the full cap would have represented. H1 share-based compensation was $71.4M versus $31.5M. Process was sound: shareholder approval, a capital-efficiency metric rather than share price alone, and a VWAP mechanic that reduced shares earned precisely because the price was high. Concentration in one individual is nonetheless an outlier that standard compensation screens will not capture, and the combined Chair/CEO role compounds it.

Across the Forms 4 reviewed through 3 August 2026 there were no open-market purchases. The March 2026 cluster is best read as concentrated diversification after a price that had roughly doubled — the CEO sold 733,424 shares for about $45.65M and retained 2,411,852 (4,038,092 after the VCP vesting), the CFO sold about $7.93M, the CTO about $10.70M, the Subsea president about $7.23M, with directors selling uniform 6,350-share tranches consistent with annual-award liquidation. This is not evidence of deteriorating fundamentals; the concurrent guidance issuance and subsequent beat argue against that reading. The complete absence of buying at any point, including during the June dip when the company itself repurchased at $68.35, leaves the aggregate insider signal negative. Rising estimates and a four-quarter beat streak are the dominant signals; the selling adds to a picture in which nobody with inside information is adding exposure at these levels.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.6% against 31.6% one-year realized (82nd percentile within the chain), with the term structure in backwardation (43.6% near versus 33.0% long-dated), prices near-term event risk rather than a directional view. The near-term put/call skew of 15.9% indicates elevated put demand at the front, but the one-, three- and six-month tenors show negative skew readings and open interest is heavily call-tilted at a 0.17 put/call ratio. Volume put/call is 0.49. Implied volatility rose from 38.0% to 43.6% while the price rose 11.4% since the prior snapshot, so the market is paying more for optionality into a higher price. Short interest is only 3.4% of float at 3.4 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short cushion and no positioning tailwind. The composite read: expensive near-term protection, unhurried longer-dated positioning, no confirmation from the options market that the next move is up. For an owner trimming into strength, richly priced near-term volatility argues for outright sales over collars.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($100, +27.2%)

Second-half Subsea inbound reaches $5.6B, validating the $10B 2026 target, and 2027 awards step up as management projects. Adjusted EBITDA reaches the high end of the guidance-implied range this year and moves toward $2.6B by 2028 as the 2024-26 award vintage burns at the higher pricing visible in the ex-revision margin analysis. The Saipem–Subsea7 combination either completes, consolidating installation capacity, or grinds through Phase II while both parties bid less aggressively. Portfolio contracting frameworks with Equinor and Vår Energi reduce intake lumpiness and raise switching costs, justifying a structurally higher multiple for a project business. The remaining $1.5B of buyback authorization retires another 4.9% of shares, and the investment-grade balance sheet with net cash lets FTI underwrite scopes competitors cannot. At 14.5x EV/EBITDA on a $2.6B path with 380M shares, the equity is worth roughly $100.

### Bear Case ($60, -23.7%)

The 17.6% decline in H1 inbound proves to be trend rather than timing. Second-half Subsea inbound falls short of $5.6B, the $10B target is missed, and the 2027 revenue gap — $5.4B of scheduled backlog against a $10.6B run rate — becomes visible when 2027 guidance is issued in February 2027. Free cash flow decelerates faster than earnings as the $1,063.4M customer-advance position unwinds without replacement. Reported EPS growth stalls as the tax rate normalizes to 32-33%, $93.2M of AOCI FX losses reclassify into earnings, and capex steps up. Contract-estimate revisions stay negative. Meanwhile the geopolitical premium in the offshore complex compresses if the Middle East risk backdrop normalizes, removing the sentiment component of the re-rating. A high-beta cyclical at 22x next-year earnings with flat orders derates to 18x on $3.30, or roughly $60.

The asymmetry is the point. The bull case requires new information that does not yet exist in the disclosed order data. The bear case requires only that the existing trend in inbound orders persist for two more quarters.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $84 (+6.9%), bull $100 (+27.2%), bear $60 (-23.7%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +3% at 25/45/30 weights is below the cost of equity for this risk profile, with downside 3.4x the base-case upside.

**De-risking plan.** Trim materially into current strength, at the frozen $78.61 basis or better, targeting a reduction to roughly one-third of a full position. Do not exit entirely: the operating franchise is high quality, the balance sheet is genuinely investment grade with net cash, and the Saipem–Subsea7 consolidation is a real structural tailwind. Rich near-term implied volatility (43.6% versus 31.6% realized) argues for outright sales rather than paying for downside protection. The residual position exists to participate if H2 inbound confirms the $10B target.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) Q3 or Q4 disclosure of Subsea inbound tracking to roughly $10B for 2026 — specifically H2 Subsea inbound at or above $5.4B, versus $4,410.8M booked in H1. (2) FY2027 backlog scheduled for recognition rising above $7.0B from $5,405.8M, restoring coverage above roughly 65% of a $10.6B run rate. (3) A price near $64-66, which would put the shares at roughly 18x next-year consensus $3.58 — in line with the peer median while retaining the balance-sheet and margin advantages. (4) Resumption of buybacks at or above $78, signaling management views current levels as below intrinsic value.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Book-to-bill below 0.85x for a second consecutive half, indicating structural rather than timing-driven order weakness. (2) FY2026 free cash flow tracking below the $1.3B guidance floor, which would confirm the customer-advance unwind mechanism is active. (3) An individually material project charge disclosed in an 8-K or a net unfavorable contract-estimate impact above $75M in any quarter. (4) FY2027 revenue guidance below $10.0B when issued.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 print, expected late October, is the first hard test — it must show Subsea inbound of roughly $2.7B or more to keep the $10B target credible with one quarter left. The decisive event is February 2027, when FY2027 guidance is issued against a backlog that currently covers about half of the run rate. ## Conclusion

TechnipFMC is a better company than it was three years ago by every measure that matters: 833bp of gross margin expansion, four ratings upgrades to two notches above investment grade, net cash of $590M, an undrawn $1.25B revolver plus an unused $1.0B commercial paper program supporting $2.4B of performance guarantees, four consecutive earnings beats, and a 7.1% reduction in share count with $1.5B of authorization remaining. Guidance was not raised despite a 13% beat. And the forward order book — inbound down 17.6%, book-to-bill 0.93x, 2027 backlog covering roughly half the run rate — has not yet confirmed the growth the multiple embeds.

Management's own repurchases stopped at $72.01 and $68.35, with $1.5B unused. Insiders sold across the spring and bought nothing, including during the June dip. Neither is decisive on its own, and both are consistent with rational diversification after a price that doubled. Together with the valuation arithmetic and the order data, they support taking profits into strength. REDUCE, with a path back at $64-66 or on confirmed H2 order acceleration.
