## Executive Summary

Par Pacific Holdings operates four refineries in logistically isolated markets across Hawaii, Montana, Washington, and Wyoming with total throughput capacity of ~219 Mbpd. The company's primary competitive advantage is geographic: each facility serves an infrastructure-constrained market where pipeline, rail, and shipping economics create natural barriers to competition. This structural positioning allows PARR to capture above-benchmark crack spread margins in its niche markets.

The stock has appreciated approximately 359% from its April 2025 low of $13.95 to $64.62, driven by a steep upward revision cycle in analyst estimates (current-year EPS consensus has risen from $3.72 to $11.29 in 90 days, a 203% increase). This rally reflects both strengthening refining margins in PARR's operating regions and a favorable macro backdrop for US refiners. However, FY2025 net income of $369M included a $199.5M one-time Small Refinery Exemption windfall covering six compliance years. Normalizing for this, underlying earnings were roughly half the reported figure. Broad insider selling across executive ranks, 11% short interest, and the inherent cyclicality of refining margins introduce material risk at current levels.

At ~9x trailing earnings and with consensus targeting ~$70 (mean), the stock is fairly valued if current-year estimates prove achievable. The geographic moat is real and durable. But the risk/reward from $65 is balanced rather than asymmetric; the margin of safety has been consumed by the rally.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific Holdings is a Houston-headquartered energy company organized across three segments: Refining (~91% of revenue), Logistics, and Retail. The refining segment operates four facilities:

- **Hawaii (Kapolei)**: 84.1 Mbpd, the largest facility. Sources crude from the Asia-Pacific region and serves the isolated Hawaiian market.
- **Montana (Billings)**: 51.7 Mbpd. Acquired June 2023. Processes Canadian crude for the northern Rockies.
- **Washington (Tacoma)**: 38.7 Mbpd. Sources Bakken and Canadian crude for the Pacific Northwest.
- **Wyoming (Newcastle)**: 13.3 Mbpd. Smallest and most operationally challenged facility, which experienced an explosion in February 2025 that idled it for 66 days.

The Logistics segment provides intercompany transportation and storage infrastructure, while Retail operates fuel stations and convenience stores primarily in Hawaii. A new Hawaii Renewables joint venture with Mitsubishi Corporation and ENEOS Corporation (63.5% PARR ownership) is expected to commence renewable fuels manufacturing in H1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

FY2025 revenue of $7.46B declined 6.4% from $7.97B in FY2024, though operating income surged from $48M to $539M. The disconnect reflects the SRE benefit: $199.5M of the $539M operating income was non-recurring. Adjusted gross margin per barrel improved to $11.64 from $9.05 in FY2024, a meaningful recovery but still 27% below the $16.01 peak in FY2023. The Combined Index (PARR's blended benchmark crack spread indicator) stood at $12.40/bbl in FY2025 versus $9.37 in FY2024 and $15.46 in FY2023.

**Balance Sheet**

The balance sheet improved materially. Long-term debt declined 28% from $1.11B to $798M. Stockholders' equity rose to $1.51B from $1.19B. The current ratio of 1.61 provides adequate liquidity, supplemented by $914.6M in total available liquidity ($164M cash + $750.5M ABL facility). The Altman Z-Score of 3.57 is firmly in the safe zone. D/E of 1.51 is moderate for a capital-intensive refiner and is trending favorably. The December 2025 term loan repricing reduced the interest margin by 50 bps. PARR also carries approximately $700M in NOL tax carryforwards.

**Cash Flow and Capital Allocation**

Operating cash flow of $445M and free cash flow of $296M were strong, though the FCF figure is inflated by the SRE benefit. Capital expenditures were $149M in FY2025 with a guided range of $190-220M for FY2026 (including turnarounds at Hawaii and Wyoming). Share repurchases totaled $124M in FY2025 and $142M in FY2024, reducing the diluted share count from ~61M (FY2023) to ~51.6M (FY2025), a cumulative ~15% reduction. A new $250M repurchase authorization was approved in February 2025 with no expiration date. No dividend is paid.

**Quarterly Earnings Pattern**

The earnings surprise record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses over the past 4 quarters. The most recent quarter (Q4 2025, reported Feb 24, 2026) missed by 9.9% ($1.17 vs. $1.30 expected), while Q2 2025 delivered a massive 96.5% upside surprise. This volatility is characteristic of refining earnings, which swing with crack spreads on a quarterly basis.

## Growth Analysis

Current-year EPS estimates have risen from $3.72 to $11.29 in 90 days (+203%). Next-quarter estimates surged from $1.05 to $5.15 (+390%). This implies that analysts expect a sustained period of elevated crack spreads, consistent with the current oil market environment where structural refining supply constraints are widening margins.

The sustainability of this earnings trajectory is uncertain. Several factors could sustain elevated margins: tight global refining capacity (post-COVID capacity rationalization has reduced the global refinery count), PARR's geographic isolation insulating it from competitive displacement, and the Billings acquisition providing operational diversification. The Hawaii Renewables JV adds a modest growth vector with renewable diesel credits and potentially more stable cash flows.

Factors that could compress margins: OPEC production decisions affecting crude costs, a resolution to current supply disruptions (which would narrow crack spreads), and the possibility that FY2026 represents a cyclical peak rather than the beginning of a new plateau. FY2025 adjusted gross margin per barrel ($11.64) was still well below the FY2023 peak ($16.01), which suggests room for improvement but also highlights that current estimates embed a significant margin expansion assumption.

Long-term organic growth is limited by the fixed nature of refining capacity. PARR's growth has historically come through acquisitions (Billings in 2023) and now through the renewables JV. Throughput expansion at existing facilities is incremental at best.

## Valuation Assessment

At $64.62, PARR trades at approximately 9.0x trailing P/E ($7.16 EPS) and 5.7x forward P/E based on current-year consensus of $11.29. Against the peer group median P/E of 21.4x, PARR appears inexpensive. However, this comparison requires context:

- The peer group includes deeply distressed names (PBF at -31x, WKC at -2.6x) and a sub-scale compounder (REX at 19.4x), making the median less informative.
- CVI at 127x is in a trough earnings year; DINO at 21.4x is the most directly comparable and trades at roughly 2.4x PARR's trailing P/E.
- The trailing $7.16 EPS includes the one-time $199.5M SRE benefit. Normalizing for ~$3.87/share in SRE gains (using ~51.6M diluted shares), underlying trailing EPS was approximately $3.29, which puts the stock at ~19.6x, essentially in line with DINO.

The consensus mean target of $70.29 implies 8.8% upside. The median of $77 implies 19.2% upside. The low target of $58 implies 10.2% downside. Goldman Sachs upgraded to Buy with a $77 target in April 2026.

The 359% rally from the 52-week low has consumed most of the margin of safety. At $65, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric around consensus expectations. The forward P/E of 5.7x on $11.29 consensus looks attractive, but that estimate embeds substantial margin expansion assumptions. If crack spreads normalize toward FY2024 levels, EPS could revert to $3-5, putting the stock at 13-22x, fairly valued to expensive.

## Competitive Landscape

PARR's geographic moat is genuine and durable. The Hawaii refinery is effectively the sole significant refiner for the Hawaiian Islands; importing refined product from the mainland or Asia involves substantial shipping costs that provide a structural pricing advantage. The Rockies refineries (Montana, Wyoming) serve landlocked markets where pipeline constraints limit product imports. The Washington refinery benefits from West Coast capacity rationalization.

Post-Billings acquisition, PARR approximately doubled its throughput capacity and improved geographic diversification. The company's asset turnover of 1.95x is strong for a refiner. The Billings facility specifically provides exposure to the WCS-WTI differential, adding a different margin driver from the Singapore crack that drives Hawaii.

The competitive position is stable rather than improving. PARR is a geographic beneficiary of supply-side constraints rather than an active share-gainer. The renewable fuels JV could provide modest differentiation if it executes on schedule, but the competitive implications are marginal relative to the core refining business.

## Risk Assessment

**Crack Spread Cyclicality**: The primary risk. The swing from FY2023 net income of $729M to a $33M loss in FY2024 illustrates the magnitude of cyclical exposure. Current consensus estimates embed elevated spreads persisting through FY2026. Any normalization would significantly impair earnings.

**SRE Non-Recurrence**: The $199.5M SRE benefit covering 2019-2024 is definitionally non-recurring. No SRE has been granted for the 2025 compliance year. Full RVO compliance costs would represent a material ongoing burden, particularly for the smaller refineries.

**Operational Risk**: The February 2025 Wyoming explosion (66-day shutdown, main crude heater replacement) illustrates the risk of operating aging, small-scale refineries. Environmental remediation liabilities at Wyoming total $27.4M accrued ($15.8M plus $11.6M for wastewater impoundments), with consent decrees dating to the 1970s.

**Labor Risk**: 22% of the workforce (395 employees across three refineries) is unionized. Collective bargaining agreements expired January 31, 2026, with negotiations ongoing under 24-hour extensions. A work stoppage would immediately impair throughput.

**Tariff/Trade Policy**: PARR imports crude and feedstocks; tariff changes can directly affect input costs. The 10-K details multiple tariff changes in 2025-2026, including a 100% tariff on certain Chinese goods, a Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs, and a subsequent 15% global tariff under Section 122. The current policy environment adds ongoing uncertainty.

**Insider Selling**: Broad-based selling across the executive team: CEO Monteleone sold ~109K shares at $53-55, Director Pate exercised and disposed ~690K options at $42.67, and multiple other officers sold in Q1 2026. While these transactions are largely option exercises near expiration (at prices well below the current $65), no open-market purchases have occurred.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case** ($77-85): Crack spreads remain elevated through FY2026 driven by structural refining supply tightness and favorable macro conditions. PARR captures above-index margins in its isolated markets. The Hawaii Renewables JV launches on schedule in H1 2026, providing incremental EBITDA and renewable fuel credits. Aggressive buybacks continue (at $65, the $250M authorization covers ~7.8% of the market cap). If $11.29 EPS is achieved and the market assigns 7-7.5x forward earnings, the stock reaches $79-85.

**Bear Case** ($30-40): A diplomatic resolution to current geopolitical supply disruptions or OPEC production increases compress crack spreads sharply. Alternatively, global demand weakness normalizes spreads to FY2024 levels. Without the SRE benefit, normalized EPS reverts to $3-5. At 8-10x normalized earnings, the stock trades at $24-50. Labor stoppage at one or more unionized refineries compounds the impact. Renewable fuels JV experiences delays or cost overruns.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- Confirmation of FY2026 Q1 earnings at or above consensus ($0.99), validating that the estimate revision cycle has fundamental support

**Downgrade triggers** (to AVOID):

- Failure to reach consensus for 2 consecutive quarters (the last quarter already missed by 9.9%)
- Management reduces or eliminates the buyback program, signaling reduced confidence in forward earnings
- Labor stoppage at any of the three unionized refineries lasting more than 2 weeks

**Reassessment timeline**: Next quarterly earnings (estimated late May / early June 2026). This will be the first report covering the period when the stock was trading near current levels and will provide critical validation of whether the estimate revision cycle is tracking.

## Conclusion

Par Pacific Holdings possesses a genuine competitive advantage through its geographic positioning in isolated refining markets. The balance sheet is in good shape, debt is declining, and buybacks are shrinking the share count meaningfully.

The challenge for new capital at $65 is that much of this positive narrative is priced in. FY2025 earnings were flattered by the one-time SRE windfall, broad insider selling suggests executives have been taking profits, short interest at 11% reflects meaningful institutional skepticism, and the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Refining is inherently cyclical, and the current consensus assumes elevated margins persist, an assumption that has historically proven fragile.

HOLD with a bias toward accumulation on pullbacks to $45-50, where the risk/reward becomes more favorable.
