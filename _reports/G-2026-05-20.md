## Executive Summary

Genpact is a $5.4B market cap business process and technology services company undergoing a material transformation from labor-arbitrage BPO toward AI-enabled "agentic" solutions. The stock has declined 35% from its December 2025 highs ($48.64) to $31.67, driven by weak Q2 2026 revenue guidance and broader macro concerns around tariffs affecting outsourcing spending. At 7.8x forward earnings ($4.07 FY26E), the stock trades at a 58% discount to its peer median P/E of 18.5x despite superior growth (ATS segment at 24% YoY), higher margins (14.5% FCF margin), and a consistent earnings beat record (4/4 quarters).

The central tension is between an unusually attractive valuation on improving fundamentals — ATS growth accelerating, margins expanding, buybacks at prices 45% above current — and a $900M+ contingent liability from Indian tax authorities and the Enforcement Directorate that represents 36% of total equity. The favorable appellate record (Genpact has won at every level) suggests resolution in the company's favor, but a Supreme Court reversal or ED enforcement action would be devastating. This binary risk, combined with the absence of a specific near-term catalyst for resolution, keeps the recommendation at HOLD with a growth thesis intact.

## Company Overview

Genpact operates as a technology and business process services company with 146,500+ employees across 35+ countries, serving clients primarily in financial services (27% of revenue), consumer/healthcare (34%), and high tech/manufacturing (39%). Originally the global capability center for GE, the company has evolved over 25+ years into a provider of AI-enabled operational solutions.

The company is executing a deliberate pivot from headcount-driven revenue (FTE-based billing) to outcomes-based pricing. Non-FTE revenue now represents 48% of the total, up materially over the past two years. The Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS) segment — encompassing AI, data analytics, and agentic solutions — reached $1.2B in FY2025 revenue (24% of total) growing 17% YoY, accelerating to 24% in Q1 2026.

Key partnerships with Google Cloud, Databricks, Microsoft, and ServiceNow provide channel scale. Partner-related revenues grew 35% YoY in Q1 2026, reaching 13% of total revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $4.37B (2022) → $4.48B (2023) → $4.77B (2024) → $5.08B (2025) → $1.30B Q1 2026 (6.7% YoY). Full-year 2026 guidance calls for at least 7% growth, implying ~$5.44B.

**Profitability metrics:**
- Gross margin: 36.0% in 2025, guided to 36.5% in 2026 (50bp expansion)
- Adjusted operating margin: 17.5% (up from 17.1%)
- Net margin: 10.9%
- ROE: 21.7%
- FCF margin: 14.5% ($735M on $5.08B revenue)

**Cash flow quality:** FCF of $735M exceeds net income of $552M by 33%, indicating high-quality earnings. Operating cash flow benefited from $170M in one-time advance client payments; adjusting for this, underlying OCF was still ~$643M, up meaningfully from $615M in 2024.

**Balance sheet:** $1.2B in cash/investments against ~$1.54B in debt. Net debt/EBITDA well within the <3x covenant. The April 2026 maturity ($350M at 1.75%) was pre-funded via November 2025 issuance ($350M at 4.95%). No near-term refinancing risk.

**Capital return:** $283M in buybacks during 2025 at an average of $46.16/share — 45% above today's price. Dividend increased 10% to $0.75/share ($0.1875 quarterly). Since 2015, the company has retired 70.9M shares at an average of $33.65, reducing share count from 184M to 174M.

**Earnings quality:** Four consecutive quarterly beats (0.7% to 5.9% surprise). EPS estimates trending modestly higher: FY2026 at $4.07 (up 1.4% over 90 days), FY2027 at $4.48 (up 0.9%). The revision trend is positive but modest.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on two pillars:

**1. ATS acceleration:** 17% growth in FY2025 accelerated to 24% in Q1 2026. Agentic solutions bookings in Q1 nearly doubled the total 2025 contract value. Pipeline grew 30% in 90 days. Management targets ATS to grow at 2x the company rate (i.e., 14%+ on a 7% company rate), with 70% annuitized and 70% non-FTE pricing. If ATS reaches 35-40% of revenue within 2-3 years at 20%+ growth, blended company growth rises toward 9-10%.

**2. Revenue model transformation:** Non-FTE revenue at 48% and rising decouples revenue from headcount. ATS revenue per employee runs at >2x the company average, meaning each incremental ATS dollar is higher margin. This should drive continued margin expansion beyond the guided 36.5% gross margin.

**Growth risks:** The weak Q2 guidance ($1.33B below expectations) reflects tariff-driven macro uncertainty elongating sales cycles. This is likely a timing issue — the pipeline and bookings data contradict structural demand deterioration — but could persist for 2-3 quarters if trade policy remains unstable.

## Valuation Assessment

**Peer comparison:**
- Peer median P/E: 18.5x (EXLS at 18.5x, MMS at 9.0x, VRRM at 16.3x)

**Why is the market discounting Genpact?**

2. Macro/tariff uncertainty on outsourcing spend
3. AI disruption narrative (existential threat to traditional BPO)
4. Weak Q2 guidance despite Q1 beat

**Why the discount appears excessive:**

- Dividend yield: 2.4% with 10% growth rate
- Management was buying back stock at $46.16 — 45% premium to current
- Even at a conservative 12x FY26 EPS, fair value is $48.84 (54% upside)
- At peer-median 18.5x, the stock would be $75+ — though that requires legal resolution

The 52-week low of $28.78 (reached May 14) represents a near-term floor. Analyst consensus at $42.45 mean / $40.00 median implies 34-42% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Genpact occupies a distinctive position between traditional IT services firms (Accenture, Infosys, Wipro) and pure-play AI companies. Its competitive advantage is deep process expertise accumulated over 25+ years of managing complex business operations for Fortune 500 clients.

**Moat characteristics:**
- High switching costs: clients have deeply integrated operational processes
- Knowledge asymmetry: understanding "how things actually work" in complex enterprises requires years of embedded presence
- Data flywheel: operational data feeds AI models that improve outcomes, generating more data
- Client attrition: 24% — meaningful but not catastrophic

**Competitive threats:**
- Client GCCs (in-house capabilities) represent a growing threat as enterprises build captive AI teams
- AI commoditization of simple BPO tasks (data entry, basic processing) will compress traditional margins
- Traditional IT services firms (Accenture, Infosys) investing heavily in AI capabilities
- EXLS (closest peer) pursuing similar transformation with arguably similar or better execution

ATS growing 24% and now 27% of revenue suggests Genpact is successfully transitioning. The key variable is whether the transition pace exceeds the erosion rate of traditional services.

## Risk Assessment

**1. India Tax & Enforcement Liability (HIGH — Binary):** Combined ITA ($792M + $98M) and ED (building lien, no demand yet) exposure exceeds $900M against $2.5B in equity. Genpact has won at every appellate level, including Delhi High Court dismissal in December 2024. The ITA has appealed to the Supreme Court. If lost, this wipes approximately 36% of book equity. If won, it removes the largest single overhang. No timeline for Supreme Court resolution.

**2. Macro/Tariff Headwinds (MODERATE):** 71% of revenue from North American clients creates direct exposure to U.S. tariff-driven budget uncertainty. Extended sales cycle elongation could persist through H2 2026.

**3. AI Displacement (MODERATE-TO-HIGH, Long-Term):** Management explicitly acknowledges that "some services we currently perform will be replaced by AI." The bet is that Genpact can move up the value chain faster than AI erodes the base. The 24% ATS growth vs. 3.7% core services growth creates a race condition.

**4. Client Concentration (LOW-MODERATE):** Top 5 at 15.2% (rising from 14.1%). Manageable but trending wrong.

**5. India Geopolitical (LOW):** India-Pakistan escalation in May 2025 is noted. Delivery workforce concentrated in India. Low probability of sustained disruption but acknowledged.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

3. Management announces accelerated buyback program at current levels (would signal board confidence in legal outcome)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. ED issues monetary demand exceeding $200M
2. Supreme Court accepts ITA arguments and orders payment
3. ATS growth decelerates to below 10% for two consecutive quarters (would indicate competitive displacement)
4. Revenue growth turns negative organically (excluding currency)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (August 2026 for Q2), India Supreme Court scheduling (monitor case status quarterly), or any 8-K related to ED/ITA proceedings.

## Conclusion

Genpact presents a genuine valuation anomaly at 7.8x forward earnings for a company generating 13.6% FCF yield, growing its highest-value segment at 24%, and buying back stock 45% above the current price. The AI transformation narrative is backed by real numbers — ATS acceleration, margin expansion, non-FTE mix shift — and the company's domain expertise provides a defensible position in deploying AI for complex business processes.

The reason this is not a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the $900M+ India tax/enforcement liability represents a catastrophic binary outcome with no firm timeline for resolution. While favorable appellate history and management behavior (raising dividends, buying back stock at $46) suggest confidence in eventual resolution, the Supreme Court appeal introduces genuine uncertainty. A ruling against Genpact would impair 36% of equity and likely drive the stock to the low $20s or below.

The HOLD recommendation reflects a 12-month growth thesis with 26-42% base-case upside, conditional on the legal overhang not materializing. The stock offers compelling reward for investors willing to accept this binary risk, but the magnitude of the downside scenario (~$25 loss per share on adverse ruling vs. ~$10-15 gain in base case) creates an asymmetry that requires resolution before deploying fresh capital.
