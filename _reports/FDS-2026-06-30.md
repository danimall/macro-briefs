**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FactSet has lost roughly half its equity value over the past twelve months, sliding from a 52-week high of $453 to $234, a drawdown that has compressed the trailing P/E to ~15x from the high-20s the market historically assigned this franchise. The decline is not driven by demand deterioration. Organic annual subscription value (ASV) growth accelerated from 5.7% in FY25 to 6.7% in the February 2026 quarter, and organic revenue growth accelerated from 4.4% to 6.8% over the same span. The pain sits in two places: margin compression (operating margin fell to 30.3% from 32.5% YoY in Q2 FY26) driven by one-time CEO make-whole awards, rising cloud hosting costs, higher software amortization, India labor-code changes, and an equity-investment impairment; and sentiment, where the market is discounting a structural thesis that generative AI commoditizes aggregated financial data and that shrinking client headcounts cap the seat-based subscription model.

This is a quality-compounder de-rating, and the bull case is real: FactSet generates a 26.6% FCF margin, retains >95% of ASV, holds an exclusive monopoly in CUSIP identifiers, and is buying back stock aggressively into weakness (~$1B authorized, $697M remaining, share count down to 37.3M). But the burden of proof at a ~48% drawdown is on the bull, and the offsetting concerns are concrete. A material weakness in internal controls over the revenue recognition process has persisted unremediated for six-plus quarters, an unusual and specific governance red flag for a data-integrity business. Leadership is in broad transition: an external transformation-oriented CEO (ex-JPMorgan) arrived September 2025, a second CFO change appears underway, and multiple officer-departure 8-Ks have been filed. The core AI-disruption debate is genuinely unresolved.

We rate FDS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The stock is not expensive on forward earnings and the demand data contradicts the bear narrative, but the combination of unremediated control weakness, C-suite churn, and an open-ended AI question keeps risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on remediation confirmation plus evidence the AI monetization pivot is defending seat economics; we would downgrade on a repeated control-weakness disclosure or a deceleration in ASV growth.

## Company Overview

FactSet is a global financial-data and analytics platform serving ~9,100 clients and 241,000+ users across institutional asset management, hedge funds, banks, wealth management, private equity, and corporates. Founded in 1978, public since 1996, and added to the S&P 500 in December 2021, the company runs a predominantly recurring subscription model measured internally as ASV. Client retention is 91% and ASV retention exceeds 95%, reflecting deep workflow entrenchment.

Revenue is reported geographically (Americas ~65%, EMEA ~25%, Asia Pacific ~10%) and organized by firm type into Institutional Buyside, Dealmakers, Wealth, and Market Infrastructure (renamed from "Partnerships and CGS" in FY2026). Approximately 82% of organic ASV is buy-side. Within Market Infrastructure sits CUSIP Global Services (CGS), where FactSet is the exclusive issuer of CUSIP/CINS identifiers in the U.S. and substitute ISIN agency for 30+ countries, an effective monopoly generating high-margin recurring revenue.

The competitive moat rests on integration into client workflows, proprietary plus aggregated third-party data, and possession of clients' own portfolio data. Principal competitors are Bloomberg, S&P Global Market Intelligence, and LSEG Data & Analytics, with adjacent pressure from BlackRock Aladdin, MSCI, and Morningstar. About 68% of the ~12,840-employee base sits in low-cost centers in India and the Philippines, supporting the structurally high margins.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily: $1.84B (FY22) → $2.09B → $2.20B → $2.32B (FY25, +5.4%; organic +4.4%). FY25 operating margin was 32.2% (36.3% adjusted), net income $597.0M (+11.2%, though ~$17.2M was a one-time divestiture gain), diluted EPS $15.55, and free cash flow $617.5M, a 26.6% FCF margin consistent with capital-light cash generation. ROE was 27.3% and ROA 13.9%.

The February 2026 quarter (Q2 FY26) is the central story. Revenue reached $611.0M (+7.1%; organic +6.8%) with organic ASV up 6.7%, led by data solutions, workstations, and portfolio/trading solutions (aided by the LiquidityBook acquisition). But operating margin fell to 30.3% from 32.5% YoY, net income declined 8.1% to $133.1M, and GAAP diluted EPS fell 4.5% to $3.59 (adjusted EPS rose 4.2% to $4.46). The margin bridge is composed of identifiable items: higher employee compensation including one-time new-CEO make-whole cash and equity, rising cloud hosting costs (+40–60 bps), higher amortization of capitalized internal-use software, India Labor Codes Reform effective November 2025, a $10.2M equity-investment impairment, and a tax rate rising to 17.4% on lost executive-comp deductibility and lower stock-comp benefits.

The distinction matters for scoring. The top line is healthier than the headline EPS, and several of the margin drivers are one-time (CEO make-whole, impairment) or investment-driven (cloud, capitalized software). The recurring structural pressures, cloud hosting cost inflation and the India labor changes, are real but modest relative to the franchise's margin cushion.

On the balance sheet, long-term debt is $1.37B ($1.0B fixed Senior Notes at 2.9%/3.45% maturing 2027/2032, plus a $375M floating term loan) against $268M cash (Feb 2026). D/E is ~0.97 and the Altman Z of 2.5 sits in the "grey zone," which is normal for a levered, intangible-heavy data business rather than a distress signal. The company remains compliant with its 3.75x leverage covenant. The earnings surprise record is mixed, two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent two quarters beating by 0.9% and 2.2%, indicating tight but not deteriorating visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth setup is better than the tape suggests. Growth was led by the LiquidityBook/Irwin acquisitions extending front/middle-office and Dealmakers reach. Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not dramatic: current-year consensus EPS of $17.71 is up 1.2% over 90 days, and next-year consensus of $19.42 is up 2.1%. These do not clear the +30% velocity threshold that would flag a strong inflection buy signal; this is steady mid-single-digit growth with stable-to-slightly-rising estimates, not an earnings inflection.

Growth durability rests on the AI monetization pivot. FactSet's "AI Blueprint" leans on three pillars: expanding proprietary and third-party data (the scarce, defensible asset), embedding deeper in client workflows (front/middle office, wealth prospecting, pitch automation), and monetizing AI through embedded persona-specific solutions. The Google Cloud partnership announced around June 30, 2026 is an offensive move on the third pillar: FactSet is a Google Cloud customer embedding Google AI into FactSet-owned products, not a loss of product ownership. It also raises near-term cloud hosting costs, the margin headwind already visible in Q2. The strategic logic is sound; the financial payoff is unproven.

The structural counter-argument is genuine. Roughly 82% of ASV is buy-side and tied to seat counts. If financial-services headcount continues to shrink and if generative AI compresses the value of an aggregator/redistributor, seat growth stalls. The bull rebuttal, that FactSet's proprietary connected data and workflow entrenchment position it as an AI beneficiary selling trusted data to feed models, is plausible but not yet demonstrated in the numbers beyond the ASV acceleration itself. Growth prospects earn a middling score: the trajectory is positive and accelerating, but weighed against an unresolved structural threat, we cannot yet treat the growth as durable in the way we would a business without the seat-model overhang.

## Valuation Assessment

At $234, FDS trades at ~15x trailing earnings and ~13.2x forward. Against a peer set in Financial Exchanges & Data with a median P/E of 18.1x (Morningstar 18.1x, MarketAxess 13.8x, CBOE 25.3x, Virtu 9.2x), FactSet trades at a discount to the median despite superior retention economics and a higher-margin subscription model than the transaction-levered exchange names. On the mean analyst target of $248 the implied upside is modest; the median target of $234 sits essentially at spot, and the high target of $380 reflects a full re-rating back toward historical multiples.

The valuation discipline here requires caution in both directions. A low P/E is not, on its own, a buy signal; cheapness has repeatedly proven anti-predictive as a value-trap indicator, and a compressed multiple can reflect structural impairment as easily as mispricing. The question is whether the market is wrong. There is a specific, falsifiable case that it is: the stock halved while organic ASV and revenue growth accelerated, and the margin compression is substantially one-time or investment-driven. If margins normalize as the one-time items roll off and the AI monetization begins contributing, mid-cycle earnings power supports a re-rating toward the peer median, implying a fair value in the high-$200s to low-$300s.

Against that, the estimate revisions are only modestly positive, which means analyst targets here are not the "lagging floor" they would be on a genuine inflection stock; consensus is not chasing rapidly rising numbers. And the ~48% drawdown means many sophisticated investors are selling; the material weakness and AI overhang give them a coherent reason. We assign a moderately positive valuation score: the forward multiple is genuinely cheap relative to the franchise quality and the peer group, and there is an identifiable reason the market may be wrong (mistaking one-time margin drag and sentiment for structural decline). But the absence of a strong estimate-revision catalyst and the real structural questions prevent a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

FactSet is the smallest of the "big three" workflow/data incumbents, behind Bloomberg and S&P Global and roughly comparable to LSEG's data unit, while facing adjacent pressure from portfolio/risk platforms (Aladdin, MSCI) and analytics tools (Morningstar). Its moat (workflow entrenchment, proprietary and aggregated data, and possession of clients' portfolio data) remains intact, evidenced by the 91% client and >95% ASV retention. CGS is a genuine monopoly asset generating high-margin recurring revenue.

The competitive trajectory is where the concern lies, and it is why the competitive-position score has declined from 7.8 in February toward 5.3 now. The industry's central 2025–26 debate is whether generative AI commoditizes aggregated financial data and shrinks the seat-based model. FactSet's counter-strategy is credible, but the company is competing against far larger balance sheets (Bloomberg, S&P Global) with more resources to build proprietary AI, and its own 5-year TSR of 111.53 badly lags its peer group's 231.96, a signal of relative competitive underperformance that likely drove the board to reach outside for a change agent. The accelerating ASV suggests the moat is holding for now, but whether FactSet gains or cedes share as AI reshapes the workflow is unresolved, and the current level of entrenchment does not settle it.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are specific and not fully priced, which is why the risk-factors score sits at 4.5.

**Material weakness in internal controls.** IT general controls over the revenue, accounts receivable, and deferred revenue processes were first disclosed for FY2024 and remain unremediated as of February 28, 2026, six-plus quarters. Management concluded disclosure controls were "not effective." For a company whose core competency is data integrity, persistence this long is a concrete governance concern, and a repeated disclosure in the FY26 10-K would be a meaningful negative.

**AI-driven data commoditization and headcount pressure.** The dominant market fear, flagged directly in the 10-K: free/cheap information accelerated by generative AI may reduce demand, competitors with AI capabilities could out-innovate, and client headcount reductions threaten the per-seat model. The shift from active to passive investing compounds the seat-count erosion.

**Leadership discontinuity.** An external CEO (September 2025), an apparent new CFO transition around spring 2026 (equity grants to a new CFO in May 2026 while the Q2 10-Q was still signed by Helen Shan, herself only reinstated in July 2024), and multiple officer-departure 8-Ks amount to broad C-suite churn during a strategic transition. Execution risk is elevated.

**CGS antitrust litigation.** *Dinosaur Financial Group et al. v. S&P Global et al.*, a purported class action over the acquisition and pricing of the CUSIP monopoly, is an open legal liability against a jewel asset.

**Vendor concentration.** One cloud supplier provides the majority of compute and one data supplier exceeds 10% of data cost.

Mitigants: the Massachusetts sales-tax dispute (~$66.2M) is settled and behind them; the balance sheet carries manageable maturities; and FX/floating-rate exposures are modest relative to cash generation.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 68.2% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 43.3% (a rich 24.9-point spread, 77th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (68.2% near-term vs 50.4% long-term); the market expects a near-term catalyst, consistent with the Q3 FY26 print due around July 1, 2026. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.5%. Near-term skew is call-heavy (-10.2%) with a put/call volume ratio of 0.96, so positioning ahead of the print is not uniformly bearish; the P/C open-interest ratio fell from 1.50 to 1.20 in the latest snapshot. Against that, short interest is elevated at 16.9% of float (5.8 days to cover), reflecting institutional bearish positioning. The combination of rich near-term IV, backwardation, call-heavy near-term flow, and high short interest describes a stock at a binary event with two-sided positioning. A clean Q3 print with sustained ASV acceleration could squeeze the short base; a margin miss or a repeated control-weakness disclosure would validate the bears.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($300–380)

The selloff is a sentiment-driven de-rating of a high-quality compounder whose demand is accelerating. The margin compression is substantially one-time and investment-driven and normalizes over the next several quarters. The AI monetization pivot, backed by proprietary connected data and the Google Cloud partnership, turns FactSet into an AI beneficiary rather than a victim. Management is retiring stock aggressively at depressed prices ($302.9M in 1H FY26, $697.1M remaining). At ~13x forward on a >95%-retention subscription base, a re-rating toward the 18x peer median implies the high-$200s to low-$300s, and a full reversion toward historical multiples supports the $380 high target.

### Bear Case ($185–200)

Generative AI structurally compresses the value of an aggregated-data business, and client headcount reductions cap the seat-based model, turning today's ASV acceleration into a plateau. Margin pressure proves recurring rather than one-time as cloud costs and AI investment scale. The material weakness signals deeper operational fragility and produces a repeated adverse disclosure. C-suite churn disrupts execution during the AI pivot. The multiple stays compressed at ~13x on flat-to-declining EPS, and the stock revisits the 52-week low near $185, roughly 21% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not yet support a confident call in either direction, and the next earnings report plus the FY26 10-K are the natural adjudicators.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation in the FY2026 10-K that the material weakness in revenue-process controls has been remediated.
- Q3/Q4 FY26 prints showing organic ASV growth sustained at or above 6.5% with operating margin stabilizing (30%+ and no longer compressing) as one-time CEO costs roll off.
- Tangible evidence the AI monetization pivot is contributing to ASV (disclosed AI-product revenue or ASV attribution), demonstrating the seat-model threat is being offset.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A repeated "not effective" disclosure-controls conclusion in the FY26 10-K, indicating the control weakness cannot be remediated.
- Organic ASV growth decelerating below ~5%, confirming the seat-count/AI-commoditization bear thesis.
- Further unplanned senior-officer departures suggesting the strategic transition is destabilizing, or a materially adverse ruling in the CGS antitrust litigation.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY26 report (~July 1, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; the FY2026 10-K (expected October 2026) is the decisive one for the control-weakness question. Given the ±11.5% implied move and event-driven backwardation, near-term volatility is expected regardless of the fundamental trajectory.

## Conclusion

FactSet is a genuinely high-quality franchise trading at a discount to its peer group on forward earnings, with demand accelerating even as the stock has halved. Under the framework that a price decline on intact or improving fundamentals is a stronger buy case, the ~48% drawdown against accelerating ASV is compelling on its face. But at this magnitude of drawdown the burden is on the bull to identify what the market is missing, and here the offsetting concerns are specific: a six-quarter-plus unremediated material weakness in the revenue-recognition process, broad C-suite churn during a strategic transition, and an AI-commoditization question that the accelerating ASV supports but does not settle. Estimate revisions are only modestly positive, so this is not the rapid-inflection setup that would override valuation caution.

The result is a two-sided setup rather than an asymmetric one. We rate FDS a HOLD (overall score 5.6), consistent with the recent trajectory of declining competitive-position, management-quality, and risk-factor scores. The path to a BUY runs through control remediation and demonstrated AI monetization; the path to an AVOID runs through a repeated adverse controls disclosure or ASV deceleration. Both are testable at the next two reporting checkpoints.
