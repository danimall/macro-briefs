**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carlyle is one of the world's largest alternative asset managers, with $475.4 billion of AUM as of March 31, 2026, spread across Global Private Equity, Global Credit, and AlpInvest. The investment debate is narrow and well-defined: whether the firm's multi-year pivot toward private credit, insurance solutions, secondaries, and perpetual capital re-rates the stock toward its faster-growing peers (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, Ares), or whether a stagnating legacy private-equity fee base and high earnings volatility from carry marks keep it stuck at a discount. The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of ~$69.85 to ~$44.50, a roughly 36% drawdown, and now trades below the analyst target range.

The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(0.37) per share looks alarming on a standardized screen but is largely a quality-of-earnings illusion. A $681M unrealized reversal of carried interest, almost entirely from public-equity marks in Carlyle Partners VII, drove the headline loss, while Fee Related Earnings held resilient at ~$300M (-3%) and the company-only operating cash flow stayed positive ($34.9M). The durable fee machine is intact; the swing was mechanical mark-to-market on accrued carry. Any Altman Z-Score near 0.6 or negative free cash flow from a standardized pull is an artifact of consolidating ~$11-12B of non-recourse CLO and fund debt, and should be disregarded for an asset manager.

The harder problem is that the forward setup is genuinely soft. Consensus has cut current-year EPS by 11.8% over 90 days and 5.8% over the last 30, the company has missed EPS in all four reported quarters, known fee tailwinds (AlpInvest catch-up fees) are rolling off, and the GPE fee base is flat while peers grow. The stock is cheap relative to peers like Blackstone and KKR, but that discount has a fundamental cause. With no open-market insider buying to signal conviction at a depressed price, the risk/reward is roughly balanced. We rate CG a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Carlyle operates as a general partner and manager across three reportable segments, earning recurring management fees plus episodic carried interest realized on fund exits. It co-invests roughly 1-3% alongside limited partners. The firm converted from a limited partnership to a C-corporation on January 1, 2020.

- **Global Private Equity (GPE) — $159.0B AUM / $99.1B fee-earning.** Buyout, growth, real estate, infrastructure, natural resources, and NGP energy carry funds. The legacy core, and now the slowest-growing segment.
- **Global Credit — $209.5B AUM / $166.4B fee-earning.** The largest and fastest-growing segment, roughly quadrupled over five years. Includes the Fortitude insurance relationship ($85.7B), liquid credit/CLOs (Carlyle is among the largest global CLO managers), direct lending (BDCs CGBD/CARS), opportunistic credit, asset-backed and aviation finance. This is the strategic centerpiece of the transformation.
- **Carlyle AlpInvest — $106.9B AUM / $67.9B fee-earning.** Secondaries, portfolio finance, co-investments, primary fund investments. Fastest fee-earning AUM growth (+25% YoY) on the secondaries boom.

Perpetual Capital now stands at $115.8B, a structurally meaningful shift toward permanent, fee-stable assets across insurance, evergreen/wealth, and BDCs. This is the part of the business that justifies the highest multiple, because it removes fundraising cyclicality from a portion of the fee base.

## Financial Analysis

GAAP figures are distorted by fund consolidation; the relevant metrics are segment-level Distributable Earnings (DE), Fee Related Earnings (FRE), fee-earning AUM growth, and net accrued carry.

**FY2025 (full year):**
- Distributable Earnings: $1,691M (+11% YoY)
- Fee Related Earnings: $1,236M (+12% YoY), up from the "low-$800M range" cited by management at the start of the transformation
- DE by segment: GPE $891M (-7%), Global Credit $481M (+27%), AlpInvest $319M (+67%)
- Capital inflows $53.7B (+32%); ~$88B dry powder; carry funds appreciated 8%
- GAAP net income to common $809M (down from $1,020M, on lower unrealized carry)
- Dividend $1.40/yr ($505M paid); 7.5M shares repurchased ($400M); buyback authorization reset to $2.0B effective Feb 26, 2026

**Q1 2026 (a clear miss, but quality-distorted):**
- GAAP net loss to common of $(132.2)M, or $(0.37)/share, vs. +$130M/+$0.36 a year ago
- Driver: performance allocations of $(681.1)M, overwhelmingly from Carlyle Partners VII (-$723M) as public holdings were marked down in the Q1 software/equity selloff. This is a mark-to-market reversal of unrealized carry, not a cash loss.
- DE fell 28% to $327M; FRE was resilient at ~$300M (-3%)
- Realized net performance revenue collapsed to $20.5M (vs. $127M in Q1'25), with exits skewed to funds not yet in carry
- Segment DE: GPE $149.9M (-44%), Global Credit $98.2M (-11%), AlpInvest $78.9M (flat)
- Fund management fees roughly flat YoY (~$544.5M segment); strong new-fund activations offset GPE step-downs and a $13.4M drop in AlpInvest catch-up fees (which do not recur, as secondaries fundraising concluded Q3'25)

**Balance sheet (company-only, ex-Consolidated Funds):**
- Company-only total assets $15.4B; cash ~$1.7B (~$1.0B free after earmarks)
- Total debt ~$3.0B (laddered senior notes 2029-2048 plus $500M sub notes 2061); issued $800M 5.05% notes due 2035 in Sept 2025; $1.0B revolver undrawn; in covenant compliance
- Net accrued performance revenue attributable to CG: ~$2.59B, an off-income-statement reservoir of future realized carry (~$1.67B GPE, $643M AlpInvest)
- Carry-related comp liability ~$4.6B (moves with carry marks)

The financial health picture is solid for a scaled alt manager: a laddered debt stack with no near-term wall, an undrawn revolver, positive company-only operating cash flow, and a large accrued-carry reservoir. It is not pristine, because realized DE and therefore dividend coverage depend on PE exit markets that are currently slow.

## Growth Analysis

The growth mix has flipped. Global Credit and AlpInvest now drive earnings growth, while GPE is a flat-to-declining fee contributor. Management itself flags that smaller corporate-PE fund sizes, fee step-downs, and realizations "may continue to result in lower fund management fees." GPE fee-earning AUM is flat YoY while peers grow their PE engines.

The forward indicators are negative. Consensus EPS revisions are falling across every horizon:
- Current quarter: $0.93, down 6.3% over 30 days and 14.3% over 90 days
- Next quarter: $1.11, down 7.3% / 10.8%
- Current year: $4.09, down 5.8% / 11.8%
- Next year: $5.22, down 2.1% / 4.0%

This is the opposite of the rapidly-rising-estimates signal that would support a more aggressive stance. The revision velocity is squarely negative, and the company has missed EPS four consecutive quarters (-8.4%, -2.1%, -7.6%, -0.6%). Two known 2026 headwinds are already disclosed and partly visible: AlpInvest catch-up fees rolling off and continued GPE step-downs. Investors should not extrapolate 2025's FRE growth rate without adjusting for these.

The bullish counterweight is structural. Perpetual Capital and a fundraising "super cycle" framed by the CEO support a durable, less cyclical fee base over multiple years. But the near-term trajectory of estimates does not confirm that thesis converting to reported numbers yet. Growth prospects are real but moderating, and the burden of proof now sits with management to show fee growth reaccelerating against the catch-up-fee and GPE drags.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$44.50, CG trades at a trailing P/E near 30x on $2.18 of GAAP EPS, but that metric is meaningless for an alt manager because GAAP swings with unrealized carry. The more relevant lens is forward DE/share. On current-year consensus of ~$4.09, the stock trades at roughly 10.9x; on next-year $5.22, roughly 8.5x. That is genuinely cheap on an absolute basis and consistent with the "cheaper than peers" framing from the sell side, where CG persistently trades at a discount to Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, and Ares.

The peer-comp table provided is a heterogeneous mix of traditional and alternative managers (P/E median 15.0x, yield 1.2%), which is not the right comparison set; the relevant peers are the listed alt managers, against which CG trades at a discount on fee-related multiples. The 3.1% dividend yield ($1.40/yr) provides yield support, with the dividend held flat and well within DE coverage in normal years, though Q1's depressed realized DE is a reminder that coverage flexes with exit markets.

The reason this is not an asymmetric long: the discount has a fundamental cause. CG is the diversified-but-slower-growth name with a stagnant PE fee base, higher carry-mark volatility (~16% of accrued carry in a single fund), and a smaller, less-captive insurance platform than Apollo's Athene or KKR's Global Atlantic. Estimates are falling, not rising, so the "analyst targets are floors" logic does not apply here; falling estimates typically drag targets down, not up. The consensus mean target of $60.5 and median of $63.0 sit well above spot, but those targets predate the full force of the recent revision cycle and the stock now sits below the $43.0 low target. The valuation is fair: cheap enough to floor the downside, but without a specific, dated catalyst to force convergence to peers, the multiple discount can persist.

## Competitive Landscape

Carlyle is a scaled, global top-5 alternative manager. Its genuine differentiators are the AlpInvest secondaries franchise and the Fortitude insurance flywheel. Its relative weaknesses are well understood by the market: slower growth in the corporate PE engine, and a smaller insurance balance sheet than peers. Carlyle owns only ~10.5% of Fortitude, so the fee stream flexes with Fortitude's general-account value and is less captive than the wholly-owned insurance platforms its largest competitors built.

The industry is consolidating around private credit, insurance balance sheets, and retail/wealth and perpetual-capital channels. Carlyle is competitive in credit and secondaries and is building perpetual capital, but it is a follower rather than a leader in the insurance-balance-sheet race that has driven the strongest re-ratings among peers. Competitive trajectory is mixed: improving in credit and secondaries, stagnant in PE, structurally behind in captive insurance. That nets to a stable-to-slightly-mixed position rather than a clearly widening or eroding moat.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Earnings volatility from carry marks**, demonstrated in Q1'26. GPE carry is sensitive to public-equity marks; ~16% of accrued carry sits in Carlyle Partners VII. Reversals can produce GAAP losses even when the cash business is healthy.
2. **Structural GPE management-fee headwind.** Smaller fund sizes, step-downs, and realizations are pressuring the legacy fee base while peers grow.
3. **Non-recurring fee tailwinds rolling off.** AlpInvest catch-up fees, which boosted 2025, are concluding — a known 2026 headwind already visible in Q1.
4. **Realization/exit dependence.** Slow PE exits and weak IPO markets defer the realized carry that funds DE and the dividend. Q1'26 LBO volume was -3.5% YoY and exits -15% QoQ, with the public-private valuation gap the widest in a decade.
5. **Fortitude/insurance concentration** in a rate-sensitive, only ~10.5%-owned platform.
6. **Rising equity-based comp** ($736M unrecognized, ~2.4-year recognition; ~$136M+ performance RSU grants), which pressures GAAP but is excluded from FRE/DE.
7. **TRA litigation (Delaware Chancery)** challenging payments tied to the 2020 conversion's TRA elimination; most claims survived a 2024 dismissal motion, with a renewed motion filed Jan 2026. Company views direct exposure as limited but will incur defense costs.
8. **Regulatory:** AIFMD II (effective April 2026), EU Market Integration Package (~2H'27), routine SEC/FCA examination exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty without a clear directional bet. Near-term ATM IV is 50.3% versus 35.6% one-year historical vol — rich by ~14.7 points — with a flat term structure (50.3% near vs. 51.0% far), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a single near-dated catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.2%. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-3.2%), but the put/call OI ratio jumped to 3.03 from 0.63 over the past month as the stock fell 17.8%, consistent with downside hedging into the decline. IV has compressed sharply (70.1% → 50.3%) since the May earnings event, suggesting the binary earnings-reaction risk has resolved. The net read is balanced-to-cautious: rich IV signals the market still sees meaningful two-sided risk, and the OI shift shows hedging demand, neither of which contradicts a neutral fundamental stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The FRE transformation (low-$800M to >$1.2B) is real and continuing, carried by Global Credit and AlpInvest. Perpetual capital de-risks the fee base. The stock trades at ~8.5x next-year DE versus higher-multiple peers, leaving room for a re-rating if the credit/secondaries/perpetual-capital engine proves out and PE exit markets thaw, releasing the accrued-carry reservoir into realized DE. The $2.0B buyback (~13% of the cap) and held dividend signal management confidence at a depressed price. Bull target ~$60-63, in line with consensus mean/median, on re-rating toward peers as estimates stabilize and exits recover.

### Bear Case
Every consensus estimate line is being cut, the firm has missed EPS four straight quarters, and the two known fee tailwinds are rolling off into a stagnant GPE fee base. The peer discount reflects genuine inferiority on growth and captive insurance, not a temporary mispricing. Slow exits keep realized DE and dividend coverage depressed; carry-mark volatility produces further headline shocks. Without exit-market recovery or open-market insider buying to corroborate conviction, the multiple discount persists or widens. Bear target ~$36-38 (roughly -15% to -19%), toward the 52-week-low zone, on continued estimate cuts and an extended exit drought.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on uncertain grounds: a fairly-valued turnaround where the structural fee story is intact but the near-term estimate trajectory and PE-exit backdrop are unfavorable, and there is no insider or estimate-revision signal to justify a more aggressive call.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive over a 30-90 day window, confirming FRE growth reaccelerating past the AlpInvest catch-up-fee and GPE step-down drags.
- A clear thaw in PE exit markets (rising realized performance revenue back toward $100M+/quarter), releasing accrued carry into DE.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, which would corroborate management's stated conviction at the depressed valuation.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or sustained DE-coverage shortfall on the $1.40/yr payout.
- Evidence that Global Credit or AlpInvest fee-earning AUM growth is decelerating materially, removing the engine carrying the FRE story.
- An adverse TRA litigation outcome with quantified material exposure, or a credit-cycle event impairing the BDC/CLO/Fortitude credit book.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, on/around early August), with the estimate-revision trend and realized-performance-revenue line as the key monitorables.

## Conclusion

Carlyle is a high-quality, scaled alternative manager whose Q1 GAAP loss badly misrepresents an intact and growing fee business. The transformation toward credit, secondaries, and perpetual capital is genuine, the balance sheet is sound, and the stock is cheap on forward distributable-earnings multiples relative to listed alt-manager peers. But the discount is at least partly deserved: the legacy PE fee engine is stagnating, consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, the company has missed EPS four straight quarters, known fee tailwinds are rolling off, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal that management views the current price as a bargain. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CG a **HOLD**, with a constructive bias that would convert to a more positive stance on estimate stabilization, an exit-market recovery, or insider buying.
