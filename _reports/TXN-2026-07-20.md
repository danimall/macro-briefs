**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Instruments is the world's largest analog and embedded-processing semiconductor maker, and it sits at a turning point in its capital cycle. After six years of elevated capex to build three new 300mm fabs (Sherman, Richardson, Lehi), spending is guided to fall from ~$4.55B in 2025 to $2-3B in 2026. The mechanical result is a sharp free-cash-flow inflection: trailing-twelve-month FCF through Q1 2026 reached $4.35B (23.6% of revenue), up 154% year-over-year, against FY2025 FCF of $2.94B. Because TI runs most of its own manufacturing, the fixed-cost base that punished margins during the 2023-2024 downturn now amplifies profitability as factory loadings recover; Q1 2026 operating profit rose 37% YoY on 19% revenue growth. This is a credible cash-flow story, and it is TI's explicit north-star metric (long-term FCF per share).

The problem is that the market has already largely priced it. TXN trades at roughly 37x current-year consensus EPS ($7.77) and ~48x trailing, after appreciating ~86% off its November 2025 low of $152.73 and briefly touching a 52-week high of $334.03 in June. Estimate revisions have been strongly positive (+20.5% current-year over 90 days), which supports the direction of travel, but the stock's forward multiple already embeds continued beats. Analyst consensus sits at a $301.50 median against the current $284.02, so the stock is effectively at fair value on the sell-side view, with a wide $200-$400 target dispersion signaling low conviction.

Three factors keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY: (1) valuation offers little asymmetric upside at ~37x forward on a recovery that is well-telegraphed; (2) the ~50% China shipment exposure is the dominant, unhedged risk in a period of escalating US-China semiconductor tension and Chinese domestic analog substitution; and (3) the pending $7.5B all-cash, debt-funded Silicon Labs acquisition adds leverage and integration risk atop an already historic-high $13.5B debt load. The scores have declined across three consecutive reports (6.7 → 6.5 → 6.0), driven by re-ratings of competitive position, management quality, and risk. Current data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory: the fundamentals are improving, but the price has moved faster than the risk-adjusted case warrants.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1930 and headquartered in Dallas, TI sells more than 80,000 products to over 100,000 customers across 30+ countries. Its revenue base is unusually diversified: roughly half comes from customers outside its top 50. FY2025 revenue was $17.68B across three segments:

- **Analog — $14.01B (79% of revenue), 38.6% operating margin.** Power management and Signal Chain. High-margin, long-lived, sticky products; the core of the franchise.
- **Embedded Processing — $2.70B (15%), 11.3% operating margin.** Microcontrollers, processors, wireless connectivity, radar. Margins depressed by the under-loaded Lehi (LFAB) fab.
- **Other — $979M (~6%).** DLP, calculators, custom ASICs.

End markets after a 2025 realignment: Industrial 33%, Automotive 33%, Personal Electronics 21%, Data Center 9%, Communications 3%. Management prioritizes industrial, automotive, and data center as its best long-term growth markets.

TI's moat rests on four self-described advantages: in-house 300mm manufacturing giving a ~40% per-chip cost advantage over 200mm; catalog breadth; direct market reach (>80% of revenue now direct or via TI.com); and product/customer longevity. The relatively low data-center exposure (9%) is the reason TI captured less of the AI capex wave than peers, a structural gap that explains five years of TSR underperformance.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (cyclical trough to recovery):** 2022 $20.0B → 2023 $17.5B → 2024 $15.6B (trough) → 2025 $17.7B (+13%). Q1 2026 revenue was $4.83B, +19% YoY and +9% sequential, led by industrial and data center. Q2 2026 guidance is $5.00-5.40B revenue with EPS $1.77-2.05.

**Margins bottoming and beginning to inflect.** Gross margin fell from 68.8% (2022) to 57.0% (2025), driven by new-fab startup and depreciation costs plus under-utilized loadings. Q1 2026 gross margin recovered to 58.0% with operating margin at 34.1% for FY2025 and net margin 28.3%. ROE 30.7%, ROA 14.5%, still elite for the sector. Because TI owns most of its fabs, roughly fixed costs compress margins on the way down and amplify them on the way up; Q1's 37% operating-profit growth on 19% revenue growth is that leverage turning positive.

**The core narrative is the capex-to-harvest transition.** Capex ran $4.82B (2024) and $4.55B (2025), roughly 26-31% of revenue. Guidance halves that to $2-3B in 2026. PP&E has grown from $6.9B (2022) to $12.3B (2025), so depreciation is rising (Q1'26 $541M vs $424M a year earlier) and will cap near-term gross-margin recovery, but the cash outflow is ending. FCF went from $1.50B (9.6% of revenue) in 2024 to $2.94B (16.6%) in 2025 to a TTM $4.35B (23.6%) through Q1 2026. Operating cash flow was $7.15B in 2025 (40.5% of revenue).

**Balance sheet, solid but at historic leverage.** Long-term debt nearly doubled from $8.2B (2022) to $13.5B (2025), funding the fabs and dividends; interest expense rose to $543M against roughly 11x interest coverage. Cash and short-term investments were $4.88B at YE2025 (down $2.7B YoY as capex plus returns exceeded FCF). Current ratio 4.35, D/E ~1.13. The Altman Z of 2.65 ("grey zone") reflects fixed-asset intensity and the debt-funded buildout, not distress; coverage and cash generation are robust. Inventory of $4.80B (222 days, down from 241) is a deliberate service-level choice. The relevant caution is that the pending $7.5B Silicon Labs deal will be funded with cash plus new debt, pushing leverage to a modern high.

**Capital allocation.** TI returned $6.48B to shareholders in 2025 ($5.0B dividends + $1.48B buybacks) and raised the dividend to $1.42/quarter. Buybacks slowed to $158M in Q1'26, consistent with conserving capacity for Silicon Labs. The dividend yield is ~2.0%, well-covered by operating cash flow.

**Earnings quality.** The beat/miss record is mixed: two beats, two misses over the last four quarters, but the most recent print (Q1 2026) was a decisive 21.6% beat ($1.68 vs $1.38), the strongest in the sample and consistent with operating leverage kicking in faster than consensus modeled.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a cash-flow inflection more than a top-line-growth story. Revenue is recovering off a 2024 trough but remains below the 2022 peak of $20.0B, and management describes the current upturn as "slower than prior upturns." The durable growth argument rests on rising semiconductor content per system: power management for AI servers, ADAS in automotive, industrial automation, which supports mid-cycle unit demand across TI's core industrial/auto markets.

Estimate revisions are the most encouraging signal. Current-year consensus rose from $6.45 (90 days ago) to $7.77, up 20.5%; next-year from $7.71 to $9.01, up 16.9%. The 30-day revisions have decelerated to roughly +1%, so the sharp upgrade cycle has already happened and momentum is now incremental rather than accelerating. This matters for scoring: the +30%-plus revision velocity that would flag a strong inflection BUY is not present at the 30-day cadence; the 90-day figure captures a re-rating that has largely played out.

Segment-level growth is uneven but improving. Embedded Processing operating profit rose 205% YoY in Q1 2026 off a low base as LFAB loadings improve, an early sign the underperforming segment is inflecting. The Silicon Labs acquisition, if it closes in H1 2027, directly targets the Embedded/wireless-connectivity gap. Weighting the confirmed revision trend, the FCF inflection, and the segment recovery against the below-peak revenue and slow-upturn characterization, growth prospects earn a moderate score.

## Valuation Assessment

TXN trades at ~48x trailing and ~37x current-year consensus EPS ($7.77), or ~32x next-year ($9.01). Against semiconductor peers, the picture is mixed: peer-median P/E is 61.4 (skewed by MRVL 97.0, MPWR 113.8, ADI 61.4), while QCOM sits at 22.2 and NXPI at 28.9. TI is not obviously expensive versus the analog cohort's high-multiple members, but it is not cheap versus the diversified names either, and its lower growth profile (recovery, not AI-secular) argues it should trade closer to QCOM/NXPI than to the AI-levered peers.

Applying the cycle-adjusted framework: TI is a recovery-inflection stock, so forward and normalized earnings are the right anchor, not trailing P/E. On that basis ~37x forward is not a trough multiple being misread; it is a full multiple on visibly rising estimates. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument; the relevant question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside. It does not. The consensus median sits above the current price, but the guardrail here matters: on a stock at ~37x forward, consensus targets are not independent confirmation of undervaluation; they reflect the same recovery extrapolation as the price, and the wide dispersion signals genuine disagreement rather than a mispricing the market is slow to correct.

Cheapness historically sorts the wrong way in this environment, but that is not the issue here: TXN is not cheap. It is a quality franchise at a fair-to-full price on a well-telegraphed recovery, with the FCF inflection (TTM FCF $4.35B against ~$265B market cap = ~1.6% FCF yield, rising toward the low-to-mid single digits as capex normalizes) providing the strongest support to the multiple. That yield trajectory keeps the valuation from being stretched, but it does not create the asymmetric upside a BUY requires. Valuation earns a below-average score.

## Competitive Landscape

The analog/embedded market is "highly fragmented" despite consolidation; TI competes with Analog Devices, Infineon, STMicro, NXP, ON Semiconductor, and Microchip, plus Asian niche and emerging entrants. TI's differentiation is real: the in-house 300mm scale gives a structural per-unit cost advantage that peers relying more on foundries and 200mm cannot match, and US-based "dependable" capacity is an increasingly valuable selling point to Western auto and industrial customers seeking supply-chain security.

The trajectory, however, is not unambiguously improving, which is why competitive position has been marked down from 8.5 to 6.8 to the current 6.3 across prior reports. Two structural pressures: first, TI under-indexed to the AI/data-center boom (9% of revenue), so it has captured less of the highest-growth demand than peers, contributing to five years of TSR underperformance (121.78 vs peer 258.38). Second, and more durably, China is executing a national push to build a domestic analog industry aimed directly at TI's ~50% China shipment exposure via local substitution. The cost advantage and breadth remain a wide moat, but the moat is being tested on its largest geography and TI is a laggard in the fastest-growing end market. The company is pruning weaker areas (closing its final two 150mm fabs, a $117M 2025 restructuring, and a goodwill impairment on custom ASICs), which is disciplined but signals portfolio strain rather than pure strength.

## Risk Assessment

**China concentration is the dominant risk.** The 10-K discloses ~50% of revenue from products shipped into China and ~20% from China-headquartered end customers. Any escalation in US-China trade measures, retaliatory tariffs on US chips, or accelerating Chinese domestic analog substitution is a direct threat to TI's largest revenue source. This risk is not fully priced because its timing is unknowable and its base rate of realization is rising given the broader US-China tech-conflict trajectory.

**High operating leverage cuts both ways.** With most manufacturing in-house and rising depreciation from the new fabs, margins swing hard with loadings. If the recovery stalls, under-loaded fabs compress margins sharply; the same mechanism now working in TI's favor would reverse.

**Silicon Labs integration and leverage.** The debt-funded deal (announced Feb 4, 2026, expected to close H1 2027) adds integration risk and pushes debt to a modern high. The long close timeline (regulatory approval plus a shareholder vote) leaves an extended window of execution uncertainty.

**CHIPS Act dependency.** TI received $335M in CHIPS proceeds in 2025 and is eligible for up to $1.6B direct funding plus a 35% ITC on qualifying post-2025 investments. The 10-K flags that incentives could be reduced, modified, clawed back, or terminated, a swing factor for both reported earnings and cash taxes.

**Cyclicality.** TI is a pure play on the analog/embedded cycle; management notes the recovery is running slower than prior upturns and shipments remain below the prior peak.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 117.7% against 43.4% one-year historical vol, richly priced, in the 91st percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 117.7% collapsing to 58.6% at six months). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.4%, consistent with the imminent Q2 earnings print. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3% near-term) and short interest is negligible (2.1% of float, 1.6 days to cover), so this is event-driven hedging around earnings rather than a directional bearish bet. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.47 to 0.73 as the price fell 12% from the June high, indicating some incremental protective positioning but nothing extreme. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and suggests the earnings reaction is a live binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$400).** Capex halves while factory loadings recover, driving FCF/share sharply higher, the harvest phase management has spent six years building toward. Operating leverage restores gross margin toward the low-to-mid 60s, Embedded Processing inflects further as LFAB loads, and rising semiconductor content per system in auto/industrial/data-center sustains mid-cycle demand. OBBBA tax expensing and the 35% CHIPS ITC lower cash taxes from 2026. The market re-rates TI on a full-cycle FCF-yield basis, and Silicon Labs strengthens the wireless-connectivity franchise. This is the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case (~$200).** US-China tension escalates into tariffs or export controls, or Chinese domestic analog substitution accelerates against the China shipment exposure, cutting the largest revenue source. The recovery stalls, under-loaded fabs compress margins hard given rising depreciation, and the debt-funded Silicon Labs deal proves dilutive or integration-troubled. The ~37x forward multiple derates toward the low-20s of the QCOM/NXPI cohort. This is the analyst low target.

The base case sits closer to the current price than either tail: a quality franchise executing a real cash-flow inflection that is already substantially priced, held back from re-rating higher by China risk and leverage, and supported from below by the FCF yield trajectory.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings print is the next natural checkpoint; the near-dated options ±10.4% implied move confirms it is a live binary. Beyond that, the Silicon Labs regulatory/shareholder-vote path (H1 2027 close) and quarterly gross-margin trajectory are the key monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward or below ~$250 (roughly 32x current-year / 28x next-year EPS) that restores asymmetric upside without any fundamental deterioration.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross-margin recovery toward the low-60s alongside continued capex normalization, confirming the FCF/share ramp is durable rather than a one-quarter loading benefit.
- Meaningful de-risking of the China exposure (tariff/export-control clarity, or evidence local substitution is not eroding share).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A concrete US-China escalation directly hitting analog chips (new tariffs/export controls on TI products, or confirmed Chinese local-substitution share loss).
- Gross margin failing to recover or re-compressing below ~56% on stalled loadings.
- Silicon Labs deal proving materially dilutive, closing at a worse structure, or pushing leverage to a level that pressures the dividend/buyback.

## Conclusion

Texas Instruments is a high-quality analog franchise executing a genuine free-cash-flow inflection as its 300mm fab cycle transitions from investment to harvest: capex halving while TTM FCF has risen 154% to $4.35B and operating leverage turns positive. The fundamentals are improving, and the estimate-revision trend confirms it. But at ~37x forward and ~48x trailing, after an ~86% run off the low and with the sell-side median target barely above the current price, the recovery is already substantially in the multiple. The China shipment exposure is the dominant unhedged risk in a deteriorating US-China backdrop, and the debt-funded Silicon Labs deal adds leverage and integration risk. The scores have declined across three prior reports, and the current data confirms that trajectory: better fundamentals, but a price that has outrun the risk-adjusted case.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Own it for the cash-flow inflection and dividend, but the entry math and China/leverage risks argue against adding here. A pullback toward ~$250 or confirmation the FCF/share ramp is durable through a second quarter would convert this to a BUY.
