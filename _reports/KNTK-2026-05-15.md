## Executive Summary

Kinetik Holdings is an integrated midstream operator concentrated in the Delaware Basin (Permian), providing gas gathering, processing, compression, NGL transportation, and produced water services. The company has grown through acquisitions (Durango 2024, Barilla Draw January 2025) and organic buildout, achieving record Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $251M and guiding to $950M–$1.05B for FY2026. At $51.17, the stock trades near its 52-week high, yielding 6.3% on a $3.24/year annualized dividend.

The fundamental case rests on volume growth from New Mexico expansion (ECCC pipeline Q2 2026 in-service, Kings Landing processing), declining capital intensity post-2026, and a structural position as the 4th-largest gas processor in the Delaware Basin. Against this, the company carries $3.79B in total debt (3.8x EBITDA), faces ongoing Waha gas price curtailments reducing throughput by 170–220 MMcf/d, has only $112M in organic FCF (well below dividend requirements), and is experiencing sustained selling pressure from I Squared Capital (~$225M liquidated in Feb–Apr 2026 with ~17M convertible units remaining).

The 6.3% yield provides a defensible reason to hold existing positions, particularly given predictable pipeline equity distributions (~$200M+/year) that supplement organic cash flow for dividend coverage. However, at the 52-week high with analyst consensus at $50.86 (essentially flat) and 11.8% short interest, the stock is not offering entry value for new capital.

## Company Overview

Kinetik Holdings operates exclusively in the Delaware Basin portion of the Permian Basin. Formed in February 2022 through the combination of BCP Raptor (Blackstone-backed) and Altus Midstream (Apache spinoff), the company provides:

- **Gas gathering & processing:** 4,200+ miles of pipeline, 2.4 Bcf/d processing capacity across 8 complexes, 825,000+ HP compression
- **Crude oil gathering:** ~280 miles of pipeline, 90,000 bbl storage
- **Water gathering & disposal:** ~370 miles pipeline, 580,000 bbl/d disposal capacity
- **Pipeline transportation (equity method):** 55.5% of Permian Highway Pipeline (2.65 Bcf/d), 33.0% of Shin Oak NGL pipeline (600 MBbl/d)

The organizational structure is complex: Kinetik Holdings Inc. owns the non-economic GP interest and ~40% LP interest in the partnership. Blackstone and I Squared Capital retain board designation rights and significant ownership stakes.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Margins:**
- FY2025 revenue: $1.76B (+19% YoY), driven by Durango/Barilla Draw acquisitions and NGL volume growth (+41%)
- FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA: $988M (+1.7% YoY), a significant deceleration from +15.7% in FY2024
- Margin compression (EBITDA growth far below revenue growth) resulted from 38% operating expense increases for acquired asset integration, utility cost inflation, and lost EPIC/GCX equity income

**Cash Flow Position:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $604M
- FY2025 capex: $492M (growth-heavy)
- FY2025 FCF: $112M
- Dividends paid: ~$500M/year
- The dividend is funded through operating cash flow ($604M) minus maintenance capex (~$50-80M estimated), plus $200M+ in pipeline equity distributions — the math works, but without much margin for error

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $3.79B (Debt/EBITDA ~3.8x)
- Negative stockholders' equity: -$565M (structural for MLPs)
- Current ratio: 0.69 (below 1.0)
- Available liquidity: $1.22B (mostly undrawn revolver)
- Variable rate exposure: $1.77B (with only $500M hedged post-December 2025)
- Interest expense: $233M/year (12.4% of revenue)

The May 2025 refinancing extended maturities (term loan to 2028, revolver to 2030), reducing near-term refinancing risk. But 3.8x leverage in a rising-rate environment with $1.27B in unhedged variable-rate debt is a real cost headwind.

**Q1 2026:**
- Record Adjusted EBITDA: $251M (annualizing to ~$1.0B, within guidance)
- Waha curtailments offset by Gulf Coast marketing gains
- FY2026 guidance reaffirmed: $950M–$1.05B EBITDA, $450–$510M capex

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):**
1. ECCC Pipeline in-service Q2 2026: ~150 MMcf/d rich gas capacity connecting Eddy County NM to Culberson County TX
2. Kings Landing Acid Gas Injection (year-end 2026): enables processing of higher H₂S/CO₂ gas in northern Delaware Basin
3. ~70% of 2026 capex directed at New Mexico expansion — relatively undeveloped midstream area

**Growth rate expectations:**
- FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint ~$1.0B represents ~7% growth vs. 2025's $988M
- Consensus EPS: $1.47 (FY2026), $2.12 (FY2027) — implying ~44% earnings growth into 2027 as capex declines and new infrastructure generates returns
- However, only 1-2 analysts provide estimates, limiting confidence in consensus

**Structural growth drivers:**
- Delaware Basin remains the most active drilling sub-basin in the Permian
- Upstream consolidation (Diamondback/Endeavor, COP/Marathon) typically leads to sustained, disciplined development of dedicated acreage
- PHP pipeline (55.5% ownership) benefits from any resolution of Waha basis blowouts

**Growth headwinds:**
- Waha curtailments directly reduce throughput volumes today
- NGL/condensate prices declined 13-16% YoY in FY2025
- Capital intensity remains high ($450-510M/year) vs. $112M organic FCF
- Post-2026 capex is expected to decline materially, but this remains guidance rather than confirmed

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik is the 4th-largest gas processor in the Delaware Basin, behind Targa Resources, Enterprise Products, and Energy Transfer/ONEOK. Its competitive advantages include:

- **Integrated service offering:** gas, oil, water, and NGL services from a single provider reduces producer complexity
- **New Mexico early-mover advantage:** ~70% of 2026 capex targets relatively undeveloped Eddy County midstream infrastructure
- **PHP ownership (55.5%):** provides both an earnings stream and gives customers integrated wellhead-to-Gulf-Coast connectivity

Competitive vulnerabilities:
- Pure Delaware Basin concentration vs. diversified peers
- Smaller scale means less bargaining power on procurement and less ability to absorb volume swings
- Complex corporate structure (LP/GP, multiple unit classes) reduces investor accessibility vs. pure C-corp midstream peers

The competitive position score remains stable at 6.5 — the New Mexico expansion provides a positive trajectory, but geographic concentration limits the ceiling.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Waha curtailment persistence (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact):** 170-220 MMcf/d of curtailed volumes represents roughly 7-9% of processing capacity sitting idle due to basin-level gas price dynamics. Resolution requires additional takeaway pipeline capacity (WhiteWater Midstream's Blackcomb pipeline not online until late 2026).

2. **I Squared Capital monetization overhang (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact):** ~17M convertible partnership units remain, worth ~$870M at current prices. The selling pace of $225M in 3 months suggests 12-18 months of continued supply pressure. Each conversion and sale creates a technical headwind.

3. **Interest rate sensitivity (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** $1.27B in unhedged variable-rate debt. Each 100 bps increase adds ~$12.7M in annual interest expense (after existing hedges).

4. **Dividend sustainability (LOW probability but HIGH impact if cut):** The $500M annual dividend is not covered by $112M organic FCF. Coverage depends on EMI pipeline distributions (~$200M+) and declining growth capex. Any disruption to pipeline distributions (PHP operational issues, Shin Oak volume weakness) would stress coverage immediately.

5. **11.8% short interest (6.2 days to cover):** Elevated for a midstream name, indicating institutional skepticism. Could produce a squeeze on positive catalysts, but also signals informed investors see downside.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** Dividend cut or suspension; Debt/EBITDA rises above 4.5x; Waha curtailments expand beyond 300 MMcf/d; ISQ selling accelerates beyond current pace

The income thesis: 6.3% yield from a midstream company with visible asset growth and declining future capital intensity, backed by contracted pipeline equity investments generating $200M+ in annual distributions. This justifies holding for income-focused portfolios despite elevated leverage and geographic concentration, provided the investor established a position at lower cost basis. At the 52-week high, new capital is better deployed on a pullback.

## Conclusion

KNTK is a HOLD for income. The 6.3% dividend yield — funded through a combination of operating cash flow, equity method distributions, and declining growth capex — provides a defensible reason to maintain existing positions. The company's Delaware Basin assets are well-positioned for sustained Permian activity, and the Q2 2026 ECCC pipeline represents a near-term catalyst for incremental EBITDA.

Against this, the stock near its 52-week high offers minimal price appreciation potential (consensus target $50.86), carries 3.8x leverage with significant variable-rate exposure, faces persistent Waha curtailments, and has a quantifiable sponsor selling overhang. The 11.8% short interest and recent analyst downgrades (Jefferies to Hold post-rally) validate the view that current prices incorporate the near-term growth.

For investors seeking midstream income exposure, KNTK at $44-45 represents a materially more attractive entry point where the yield exceeds 7% and the risk/reward shifts favorably. At $51, the recommendation is to hold for the income stream and wait for either a pullback or fundamental improvement (EBITDA guidance raise, leverage reduction below 3.5x, capex declining to $300M) before adding.
