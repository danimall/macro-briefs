## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is a pure-play ethanol producer that has rallied 154% over the past twelve months, driven by a record Q4 FY2025 earnings beat ($1.32/share vs. $0.14 consensus) largely attributable to $28.1M in newly recognized 45Z clean fuel tax credits. The stock now trades at ~20x trailing EPS of $2.50, which would be approximately ~30x normalized earnings excluding the 45Z benefit — a premium multiple for a commodity-spread business with no diversification.

The company's balance sheet is genuinely exceptional: $376M in cash and short-term investments against zero bank debt, funding a self-financed capacity expansion (150M → 200M gallons at One Earth) and a carbon capture and sequestration project that could structurally improve the business. However, three material binary risks — EPA Class VI well permitting (Q3 2026), Treasury finalization of 45Z regulations, and an $82.7M IRS audit exposure — create a distribution of outcomes too wide to warrant chasing at current levels.

The market is pricing in successful execution of the CCS + 45Z strategy. At current levels, investors are paying for the upside without adequate compensation for the downside scenarios where permits are denied, regulations change, or crush spreads revert.

## Company Overview

REX American Resources, headquartered in Dayton, Ohio, operates ethanol production facilities through majority-owned subsidiaries. Despite its NYSE classification under Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing, REX is entirely concentrated in corn-based ethanol and by-products (dried distillers grains, distillers corn oil).

**Operational Footprint:**
- One Earth Energy (Gibson City, IL) — 76.1% ownership, ~150M gallon/year capacity (expanding to 200M)
- NuGen Energy (Marion, SD) — 99.7% ownership
- Big River Resources (multiple IA/IL/WI plants) — 10.3% equity interest

Effective capacity under REX's consolidated and equity-method accounting: ~333M gallons/year across six plants. Revenue mix: ethanol 77.5%, dried distillers grains 13.5%, distillers corn oil 8.0%.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**

1. **One Earth Expansion (150M → 200M gallons):** Testing and commissioning underway; full operations targeted FY2026. At current crush margins, 50M incremental gallons could add $10-15M in annual gross profit.

2. **45Z Clean Fuel Credits:** Extended through 2029. At $0.10-$1.00/gallon (depending on carbon intensity score and prevailing wage compliance), the credit could be worth $20-50M/year at current capacity, potentially $30-70M at expanded capacity. This is the primary growth catalyst the market is pricing.

3. **Carbon Capture & Sequestration:** If EPA issues the Class VI well permit (expected Q3 2026) and Illinois lifts its moratorium (July 2026), CCS would generate: (a) 45Q credits at $85/ton for 12 years, and (b) lower CI scores that increase 45Z credit values. Total project budget: $220-230M, self-funded.

**Growth headwinds:**

- Electric vehicle penetration structurally reduces long-term gasoline/ethanol demand
- Crush spreads are cyclical and the current wide-margin environment may not persist
- Tariff risk to the 36% of U.S. ethanol exports going to Canada and 20% of distillers grains to Mexico
- EIA projects declining domestic ethanol demand beyond 2030

Growth is binary rather than gradual. If CCS + 45Z play out, EPS power could reach $4-5/share. If they don't, normalized earnings remain $1.50-2.00/share.

## Competitive Landscape

REX occupies a mid-size position in U.S. ethanol with ~333M effective gallons against industry capacity of ~18.5B gallons. Competitors include POET (private, largest U.S. producer), Green Plains (public, larger but leveraged), and divisions of ADM, Cargill, Valero, and Marathon.

**Competitive advantages:**
- Zero-debt balance sheet enables self-funding of CCS (competitors rely on troubled third-party pipelines like Summit Carbon Solutions)
- Early mover on 45Z compliance and CI score verification
- Conservative capital allocation with active buyback

**Competitive vulnerabilities:**
- No scale advantage (2% of industry capacity)
- Geographic concentration in Midwest
- Single-product dependence makes REX a spread-trade, not a structural compounder
- Larger competitors with diversified operations can absorb margin cycles better

Capacity expansion and CCS could widen the competitive gap against leveraged peers, but REX remains price-taking on both inputs (corn) and outputs (ethanol) with no pricing power.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CCS Permitting Binary (Q3 2026):** $58.9M already invested. EPA Class VI well permit pending. Illinois CO2 pipeline moratorium until July 2026. South Dakota banned eminent domain for CO2 pipelines. If permits are denied, write-off plus competitive disadvantage. Probability of denial: 30-40%.

**2. IRS Audit Exposure ($82.7M):** $58.2M in refined coal production credits + $24.5M in R&E credits under IRS challenge. Only $19M reserved. Full adverse outcome = ~$80M+ in back taxes plus penalties, representing ~5% of market cap.

**3. 45Z Regulatory Fragility:** Treasury regulations still proposed, not final. CI score methodology changes could disqualify plants. Political risk through 2029 extension. The $28.1M recognized in FY2025 may not recur at the same magnitude.

**4. Crush Spread Reversion:** REX's gross margins expanded from 6.4% to 14.4% over two years. This reflects favorable corn/ethanol price dynamics that are cyclical. A return to FY2023 margins would roughly halve operating income.

**5. Tariff/Trade Risk:** Canada (36% of U.S. ethanol exports) and Mexico (20% of distillers grains exports) are targets of active tariff policy. Retaliatory measures could reduce export demand and depress domestic prices.

## Conclusion

REX is a well-managed, conservatively financed ethanol producer that has rallied on the back of regulatory optionality (45Z credits, CCS potential). The balance sheet provides genuine downside protection, and the growth thesis is coherent if binary events resolve favorably. However, at $51, the stock is pricing in successful execution across multiple uncertain regulatory processes while offering no dividend yield and trading on earnings inflated by first-time tax credit recognition. The risk/reward is balanced; investors who want exposure should wait for a pullback to the $35-38 range where the margin of safety improves, or for de-risking via permit approvals that would validate the growth premium.
