## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is the world's largest aerospace and defense company with $88.6B in 2025 revenue, a $268B backlog, and unique dual exposure to both commercial aerospace and defense spending. The stock has declined approximately 19% from its early March peak of ~$214.50 to $172.87 following a Q1 2026 earnings report that actually beat consensus by approximately 16% ($1.78 vs. consensus estimates). This disconnect between improving fundamentals and declining price creates a more attractive entry point than at any time since October 2025.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) structurally locked defense spending — $156B+ in supplemental DoD funding, Golden Dome missile defense, and European rearmament that produce multi-year demand visibility independent of any single geopolitical outcome; (2) commercial aerospace aftermarket recovery as Pratt & Whitney's Powder Metal Matter normalizes (remaining accrual shrunk to $0.7B from $2.8B); and (3) a deleveraging trajectory that has reduced long-term debt (net of current portion) by $8.1B in two years while generating $10.6B in operating cash flow. At ~25x FY2026 adjusted EPS, the stock trades at a valuation that reflects its balanced commercial-defense positioning but does not fully capture the structural defense spending tailwinds or Powder Metal normalization trajectory.

The primary risks are the Airbus dispute over late GTF engine deliveries, tariff passthrough constraints on fixed-price contracts, and the defense contractor executive order that conditionally limits capital return flexibility. These are real but bounded: the Powder Metal accrual is nearly depleted, tariff impacts are acknowledged but not yet "material" per management, and buybacks were already effectively paused.

## Company Overview

RTX Corporation operates through three segments: Collins Aerospace ($30.2B revenue, 16.3% operating margins — avionics, aerostructures, mission systems), Pratt & Whitney ($32.9B, 7.9% margins — GTF engines for A320neo, military engines including F135 for F-35), and Raytheon ($28.0B, 11.5% margins — Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk, SPY-6 radars). Revenue splits approximately 52% defense/government and 48% commercial aerospace.

The $268B backlog (up 23% from $218B at year-end 2024) provides exceptional revenue visibility. Approximately 25% of total remaining performance obligations are expected to be recognized over the next 12 months, with roughly 45% relating to long-term Pratt & Whitney commercial maintenance contracts realized over periods of up to 20 years. Defense backlog grew from $93B to $107B in a single year. The company employs more than 180,000 people globally and maintains the only large-cap business that simultaneously addresses commercial aerospace engine demand and the highest-priority missile defense spending categories.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**
Revenue has grown from $67.1B (2022) to $88.6B (2025), representing 10% reported growth in 2025 with $8.9B in organic contribution. Operating margins recovered from 5.2% (2023, depressed by the $2.9B Powder Metal charge) to 10.5% (2025). Diluted EPS more than doubled from $2.23 to $4.96 over the same period, while adjusted EPS (as reported in earnings releases) grew from $5.06 (2023) to $6.29 (2025).

Q1 2026 demonstrated continued momentum: $1.78 adjusted EPS versus consensus estimates (approximately 16% beat), the fourth consecutive quarterly beat.

**Cash Flow Quality:**
Operating cash flow of $10.6B in 2025 (47% YoY increase) converts to $7.9B free cash flow — a 9.0% FCF margin and approximately 118% of net income. At the current share price, trailing FCF yield on enterprise value is approximately 3.0% (based on $7.9B FCF and an EV of approximately $263B, reflecting market cap plus approximately $30.5B in net debt).

**Balance Sheet and Deleveraging:**
Long-term debt (net of current portion) has declined from $42.4B (2023) to $34.3B (2025), a reduction of $8.1B in two years. Total debt stood at $37.9B at year-end 2025, down from $41.3B at year-end 2024. The company repaid $3.35B in 2025 specifically and has $3.4B in current maturities due in 2026, fully serviceable from operating cash flow. Credit ratings have been upgraded to Baa1/stable (Moody's, March 2025) and BBB+/stable (S&P, June 2025). The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 is misleading — it reflects $85.2B in goodwill/intangibles from the 2020 merger, not operational distress. A company generating $10.6B in OCF with $7.4B cash, $5B in undrawn revolving credit, and no commercial paper outstanding has low near-term liquidity risk.

**Capital Allocation:**
Current returns are dividend-only ($2.72/share annualized, ~1.6% yield). Buybacks collapsed from $12.9B (2023 ASR) to $50M (2025) with only $0.6B remaining under authorization. The defense contractor executive order could further constrain buyback flexibility under certain conditions. All excess cash is directed toward debt reduction and manufacturing capacity investment ($2.6B CapEx in 2025, including a $200M Columbus, GA investment in February 2026).

## Growth Analysis

**Defense Growth — Multi-Year Structural Tailwind:**
Defense bookings of $61B in 2025 sustain the pace set in 2024. The Golden Dome missile defense initiative alone represents $24.4B in initial funding, with RTX explicitly positioned across Patriot, LTAMDS, SM-3, and space-based sensors. Supplemental DoD funding of $156.2B+ through 2029 directly targets categories where RTX holds market-leading positions: air and missile defense, munitions replenishment, and supply chain resiliency.

European rearmament represents an additional growth vector independent of U.S. budgets. NATO allies increasing defense spending to 2.5-3.0% of GDP creates years of international demand for Patriot systems, AMRAAM, and advanced radars.

**Commercial Aerospace — Normalization from Disruption:**
Pratt & Whitney's Powder Metal Matter is a diminishing headwind: the remaining accrual of $0.7B (down from $2.8B at peak) represents an estimated ~$0.7B cash impact in 2026, though RTX acknowledges that assumptions remain subject to variability and potential changes could materially affect results. Aircraft-on-ground levels have declined from their 2025 peak (reduced by more than 20% per Bloomberg reporting) but are expected to remain elevated through 2026. The GTF Advantage-powered A320neo-family aircraft received EASA certification in April 2026 (following FAA engine certification in February 2025 and EASA engine validation in October 2025), clearing the path for production deliveries and entry into service. AirAsia's order of 150 A220 aircraft in May 2026 supports the aircraft program that relies on Pratt & Whitney GTF engines.

Commercial aftermarket revenues (maintenance, repair, spare parts) are growing at double-digit rates as the GTF fleet ages and returns from inspections, providing high-margin recurring revenue.

**Consensus Estimates and Company Guidance:**
RTX's own FY2026 guidance (raised after Q1 2026) calls for adjusted EPS of $6.70–$6.90, adjusted sales of $92.5–$93.5B, and FCF of $8.25–$8.75B. Using 2025 adjusted EPS of $6.29 as the base, the midpoint of guidance ($6.80) implies approximately 8% adjusted EPS growth year-over-year, reflecting continued operational improvement and Powder Metal charge normalization. FY2027 consensus of approximately $7.55 (+10-11% growth) reflects the expected further normalization post-Powder Metal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $172.87, RTX trades at approximately 25x the midpoint of FY2026 company guidance ($6.80) and approximately 23x FY2027 consensus of $7.55. Relative to aerospace and defense peers:

- GE Aerospace: ~35-38x forward P/E — primarily commercial aerospace exposure with a smaller Defense & Propulsion Technologies segment
- Lockheed Martin: ~20x forward P/E — defense-only, lower growth, no commercial upside
- General Dynamics: ~21x forward P/E — lower growth, less backlog
- Howmet Aerospace: premium growth multiple reflecting materials/components positioning
- Boeing: recovery/turnaround valuation, not directly comparable

RTX's positioning as a company with both significant commercial aerospace and defense exposure at scale warrants consideration of a premium over pure-defense names (LMT, GD). GE Aerospace does have defense exposure through its Defense & Propulsion Technologies segment, but its revenue and profit mix skews more heavily toward commercial engines and aftermarket than RTX's more balanced split. The stock's decline from $214.50 to $172.87 compresses the forward multiple while fundamentals (Q1 beat, backlog growth, GTF certification) have actually improved.

On an FCF yield basis: assuming FY2026 FCF of $8.25-8.75B (per company guidance), forward FCF yield on current EV rises to approximately 3.1-3.3%. This is reasonable for a company with mid-to-high single-digit organic growth and defense spending locked in.

The valuation is not extreme enough to be the primary investment thesis — the thesis is structural defense demand + Powder Metal normalization at an adequate (not bargain) price. The 19% correction from highs creates a more favorable entry than existed in March.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX occupies a unique competitive position as the largest company meaningfully straddling both commercial aerospace and defense.

1. **Patriot dominance:** The Patriot system is the global standard for integrated air and missile defense. Every Middle Eastern and European conflict reinforces demand. Production capacity — not demand — is the binding constraint.

2. **GTF platform lock-in:** Pratt & Whitney's GTF is one of two engine options on A320neo (alongside CFM's LEAP). The installed base creates 20+ years of aftermarket revenue per engine. The GTF Advantage certification strengthens the competitive position against CFM's RISE engine program (still in development).

3. **Missile systems breadth:** RTX's Raytheon segment produces AMRAAM, SM-3, Stinger, Tomahawk, and LTAMDS radar — essentially the full spectrum of air defense from short-range to strategic interceptors. No single competitor matches this breadth.

4. **Collins Aerospace avionics:** Market-leading positions in avionics, environmental control, and power systems across virtually all commercial and military platforms.

Defense backlog growth of 15% ($93B to $107B in one year), GTF Advantage certification, and Golden Dome positioning across multiple technology domains all reinforce this competitive position.

## Risk Assessment

**Airbus Dispute Over Engine Deliveries (Moderate, Uncertain Magnitude):** Bloomberg and other outlets have reported that Airbus has faulted Pratt & Whitney for engine delivery shortfalls that are "significantly behind" commitments, with Airbus indicating the shortages are holding back its annual aircraft delivery targets. The remaining Powder Metal accrual of $0.7B may not fully cover potential claims if Airbus pursues damages aggressively. The magnitude of any potential additional liability remains uncertain and unquantified at this time. This represents the single largest uncertain risk.

**Tariff Exposure (Moderate, Bounded):** RTX imports materials subject to U.S. tariffs and faces counter-tariffs on exports. Fixed-price contracts limit cost passthrough ability. Management states tariffs won't have "material adverse effect" under current conditions but acknowledges uncertainty. Net EAC adjustments were already negative ($386M in 2025, $473M in 2024) — tariffs could widen these losses on specific programs.

**Defense Contractor Executive Order (Low-Moderate):** The January 2026 EO directs the Secretary of Defense to identify underperforming defense contractors and ensure future contracts prohibit buybacks and corporate distributions during underperformance periods. This is conditional on underperformance determinations and applies to future contract provisions — it is not a blanket current ban on RTX dividends or buybacks. The practical near-term impact is limited given buybacks were already effectively paused, but it constrains future capital allocation flexibility.

**DOJ/SEC Compliance Monitorship (Low):** Three-year monitorship creates operational overhead but is unlikely to disrupt core business operations. Any compliance breach could theoretically trigger debarment, but this outcome has extremely low probability for a company of RTX's scale and government dependency.

**Geopolitical Scenario Risk (Low-Moderate for RTX specifically):** Even if geopolitical tensions de-escalate in certain theaters, defense spending is structurally locked by Golden Dome appropriations, NATO commitments, and multi-year contract awards already booked. RTX's $107B defense backlog doesn't unwind on a single geopolitical resolution.

## Conclusion

RTX remains a BUY with a base case target of $198 (15% upside). The score trajectory shows declining scores over recent months (6.8 → 6.4), driven primarily by competitive position, management quality, and risk factor deterioration. I partially confirm this trajectory: the Airbus dispute, executive order, and tariff exposure are genuine new risks that justify lower risk scoring. However, the fundamental picture has improved since March (Q1 beat, GTF certification, continued backlog growth), and the 19% price decline creates better risk/reward than at higher prices.

RTX's $268B backlog, $10.6B operating cash flow, and positioning across the highest-priority defense spending categories create a structural earnings floor that most geopolitical scenarios reinforce rather than threaten. At ~25x forward earnings with mid-to-high single digit growth, active deleveraging, and FCF conversion exceeding 100% of net income, the stock offers adequate compensation for the identifiable risks.
