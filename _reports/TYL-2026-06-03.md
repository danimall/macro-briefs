**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tyler Technologies is the largest U.S. software vendor focused exclusively on the public sector, supplying systems-of-record to courts, public safety, ERP/financials, property tax, K-12 administration, permitting, and government payments across one of the most fragmented IT markets in the country (~3,000 counties, ~36,000 municipalities, ~12,600 school districts). The franchise is high quality: recurring revenue is 87% of total, client attrition runs ~2% annually, and the multi-year migration from on-premise licenses to SaaS plus a parallel data-center-to-AWS transition is structurally lifting margins. FY2025 revenue grew 9.1% to $2.332B, but the composition matters more than the headline: subscriptions rose 18.1% to $1.586B (68% of revenue), SaaS fees rose 21%, and ARR reached $2.15B (+10% YoY) by Q1 2026. Gross margin expanded from 43.8% to 48.3% over the past year, and free cash flow was $637.5M (27.3% FCF margin).

The stock has fallen ~50% from its August 2025 high of $621.34 to $313.56, with roughly 10% of that decline coming after the Q1 2026 print. The repricing is anchored in two developments: headline growth decelerated into single digits, and the Platform Technologies (PT) segment — payments and digital government — is in active deterioration, with operating income down 41% YoY in Q1 after a state payment-processing contract wound down (-$12.3M of PT transaction revenue in the quarter). The de-rating is a legitimate adjustment to a slower growth rate, not a liquidity-driven dislocation.

We rate TYL a HOLD with a constructive 12-month bias (growth_hold). The forward multiple is no longer demanding for the quality, and the stock trades below the lowest published analyst target ($335). What prevents a fresh long is the absence of the conditions that have historically separated winning software longs from value traps: estimate revisions are flat (not the +30% revision velocity that signals systematic underestimation), there is no identifiable catalyst inside the next 60 days, the PT segment is more likely to drag than to re-accelerate at the late-July print, and a credible bear case (multiple compression plus a recurrence of contract reserves) puts the stock 15–17% lower, beyond the downside we require to underwrite a new long. We would upgrade on Payments stabilization or a lower entry.

## Company Overview

Tyler operates two reportable segments. **Enterprise Software (ES)** is the back-office systems-of-record business, the high-quality growth engine, selling integrated suites that are deeply embedded in government operations and rarely re-bid. **Platform Technologies (PT)** comprises payments (a platform processing roughly half a billion transactions per year, anchored by the 2021 NIC acquisition), digital solutions, and the data platform; it is more partner-dependent, has lower switching costs, and is currently the soft spot.

The competitive structure is favorable. Switching costs on government systems-of-record are very high; once a county's court, tax, or ERP system runs on Tyler, displacing it requires a multi-year migration with significant operational risk. Tyler's edge over enterprise giants (Oracle, SAP, Workday, Infor) is public-sector domain depth and an integrated suite that wins RFPs; its edge over small regional players (CentralSquare and many local vendors) is scale, financial strength, and breadth. The NIC cross-sell engine — selling Tyler software into NIC's payments client base and NIC payments into Tyler's installed base — adds a second growth vector.

Management is led by CEO Lynn Moore, with founder/long-time CEO John Marr as Executive Chair and Brian Miller as CFO. The company carries ~7,800 employees with average tenure of ~8 years and 7% voluntary turnover, unusually low retention for a software firm.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2025 revenue was $2.332B, up 9.1% (FY2024 $2.138B; FY2023 $1.952B). The mix shift dominates the story:
- Subscriptions +18.1% to $1.586B (68% of revenue, up from 62.8%), split between SaaS fees +21% to $777.8M and transaction-based fees +16% to $808.4M.
- Maintenance declined 4% to $445.6M and software licenses fell 51%, both intentional as on-prem clients convert to SaaS. In 2025, 488 on-prem clients converted to SaaS and 612 net-new SaaS clients were added; new-contract mix is ~89% subscription.
- ARR reached $2.06B at year-end 2025 (+11%).

**Margins.** The SaaS shift plus the AWS migration lifted gross margin from 43.8% (FY2024) to 46.5% (FY2025). GAAP operating margin rose to 15.3% in FY2025 and 16.3% in Q1 2026. The mechanism is straightforward: lower-margin maintenance and perpetual revenue is rolling into higher-margin SaaS, and the migration off proprietary data centers consolidates hosting onto AWS, though hosting costs rose ~$21M in FY2025 as duplicate environments run during the transition. That duplication is a temporary drag that should ease.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $653.5M (up from $624.6M) and FCF was $637.5M on minimal capex (~$16M). OCF consistently exceeds net income, helped by $138M of D&A, $151M of stock-based comp, and favorable deferred-revenue dynamics. SBC at ~6.5% of revenue is a real economic cost that should be netted against the GAAP margin improvement.

**Balance sheet, now re-levered.** As of FY2025, Tyler held $1.0B cash plus $142M investments against $3.7B equity, with goodwill of $2.59B and intangibles of $780M reflecting an acquisition-heavy history. The Altman Z of 2.71 sits in the grey zone, but it is mechanically depressed by the intangible base and a convert classified as current at year-end, not by distress; the underlying cash flow is robust. The material change since the prior review is leverage. The $600M 0.25% converts due 2026 were repaid entirely in cash at maturity on March 15, 2026, with zero dilution because the stock never approached the $493.44 conversion price. In May 2026 the company then issued $1.4375B of 0.50% convertible notes due 2031 (including the greenshoe), with capped calls raising the effective conversion price to ~$655.77, and earmarked proceeds for buybacks. This converts a previously near-debt-free balance sheet into a modestly levered one at a 0.50% coupon, trading cash flexibility for repurchase firepower.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. Q1 2026 repurchases were $250.1M against a new $1.0B authorization (Feb 3, 2026), with ~$653M remaining as of late April. The effective tax rate rose to 19.1% (FY2025) and 23.7% (Q1 2026) as excess SBC tax benefits and R&D credits shrank.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth is in ES, where subscriptions grew 27% in FY2025 and 29% in Q1 2026, and segment operating income rose 21% (FY2025) and 17% (Q1 2026). That is the franchise the long thesis rests on: large jurisdictions adopting cloud, on-prem conversions, and net-new SaaS logos at ~2% attrition.

Two offsets temper the headline. First, maintenance and perpetual revenue is rolling off by design, holding consolidated growth to ~9%. Second, PT is shrinking in profitability: the Q1 operating-income decline followed a state payment-processing contract that wound down in Q4 2025, compounded by ~$10.7M of PT loss reserves on two state contracts in FY2025. Payments is a more contestable, partner-dependent business than the ES systems-of-record, and its lumpiness is now visible.

The R&D line rose 73% to $204.6M (8.8% of revenue) in FY2025, but the filing is explicit that most of this is reclassification — support and engineering staff freed up by SaaS migration and version consolidation were redeployed to development (R&D headcount rose from 870 to 1,368), with incremental genuine AI investment. Margins expanded despite the reclassification, which is evidence of operating leverage rather than a spending shock.

Consensus EPS is $12.64 for the current year and $14.33 for next year, implying ~13% growth. Estimate revisions are flat: current-year EPS rose only +0.8% over 90 days and -0.1% over 30 days; next quarter is +0.1% over 90 days. There is no inflection signal here. The earnings track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters; Q1 2026 beat by 1.8% on $3.09 vs $3.04), but the magnitude of beats has compressed and the December 2025 quarter missed by 4.1%.

## Valuation Assessment

At $313.56, TYL trades at ~24.8x current-year EPS ($12.64) and ~21.9x next-year EPS ($14.33), and roughly 21x trailing FCF. The headline 43.3x trailing P/E reflects a depressed GAAP/trailing EPS figure ($7.20) and is not the right anchor; the forward multiple is.

Against peers, TYL is not expensive. The Application Software peer-median P/E is 37.4x (DT 78.9, GWRE 70.4, DOCU 35.5, BSY 37.4, PTC 13.3), and TYL's forward multiple sits below it. The group is heterogeneous, but Tyler's recurring-revenue quality and attrition profile justify a premium to the median, not a discount. The stock also trades below the lowest published analyst target ($335), against a mean of $445 and a median of $440.

The constraint is growth-adjusted. A ~25x forward multiple on ~13% EPS growth is a PEG near 1.9 — full for the growth on offer, even allowing for the recurring-revenue quality. On inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates, analyst targets lag reality and proximity to them is not evidence of full value. That principle does not apply here: estimates are flat, not rising, so the consensus targets are not stale upside indicators in the way they would be for an accelerating name. The ~50% drawdown is not, by itself, a reason for a low valuation score, and we do not treat it as one. At this magnitude the burden of proof is on the bull, and what the sellers identified is real — headline deceleration to single digits and a deteriorating PT segment. The de-rating is largely justified, leaving the current price reasonable rather than asymmetric.

We score valuation 5.5: a high-quality compounder at a below-peer multiple with some downside support from the analyst-target floor and the buyback, offset by a PEG near 1.9, flat revisions, and no specific catalyst to force a re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Tyler's competitive position is strong and, in the ES franchise, improving. The combination of 87% recurring revenue, ~2% attrition, and an integrated suite spanning multiple government functions creates a moat that widens as the company wins larger jurisdictions on cloud and consolidates fragmented local vendors via tuck-in acquisitions (MyGov, Emergency Networking, CloudGavel, Edulink in 2025). Digital-government modernization is a multi-year secular tailwind, and government revenue bases (property/sales tax, fees) are comparatively recession-resilient.

The watch-item is PT. Payments and digital government is more contestable than systems-of-record: it depends on third-party processors (some of which raised prices in 2025, which partly helped revenue), and it is exposed to specific state contracts that can be lost or repriced. The recent state-contract wind-down and loss reserves illustrate that this segment is lumpier and structurally lower-quality than ES. The competitive trajectory is bifurcated: ES strengthening, PT contested, which is why we score competitive position 8.0 on the weight of the core franchise while flagging PT as the segment to monitor.

## Risk Assessment

- **Government budget and appropriation dependence.** Substantially all revenue is public-sector. Funding lapses, contract terminations for lack of appropriation, and long open-bid cycles are structural and largely priced, but a prolonged recession or a shift in federal/state IT spending would compress growth.
- **PT segment deterioration (concrete, ongoing).** The decline in PT operating income and the lost transaction revenue from a state contract are current results, not abstract risks. Recurrence of contract reserves (FY2025 carried ~$9.7M ES and ~$10.7M PT) would further pressure the print.
- **Goodwill impairment now a live test.** With $2.59B of goodwill and the stock down ~50% from its high, the impairment indicator the 10-K names (a sustained market-cap decline) is active. A non-cash impairment would not affect FCF but would dent reported earnings and sentiment.
- **New leverage.** The 2031 convert re-levers a previously near-debt-free balance sheet. The coupon is trivial and capped calls push effective dilution out, but the risk profile is higher than it was, and the buyback at ~$313 depends on PT stabilizing.
- **AWS dependence and cybersecurity.** The cloud migration runs on AWS under a strategic agreement; concentration and non-renewal risk are hard to switch away from. As custodian of sensitive government and citizen data, breach risk is material, and the 10-K notes prior breaches.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 48.6% against a one-year historical vol of 37.0%, rich by ~11.7 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (48.6% at one month versus 45.3% at six months), which prices imminent event stress rather than a longer-horizon concern. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.2%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7% near-term), and the P/C open-interest ratio fell from 1.02 to 0.77 over the past week as the stock rose 3.6%, tilting positioning modestly toward calls. The options market is not pricing a directional bearish bet, but it is pricing elevated short-term uncertainty, consistent with a stock that has been volatile around recent prints and faces an unresolved PT story. The rich front-end vol argues against chasing here and favors patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The ES franchise is the core of Tyler — 87% recurring revenue, ~2% attrition, subscriptions compounding at 27–29%, and a margin runway as the AWS migration completes and maintenance/perpetual revenue finishes rolling into higher-margin SaaS. PT softness is a discrete, identifiable contract event, not a structural break; once it laps the state-contract wind-down, consolidated growth and PT profitability re-accelerate. The company is buying back stock aggressively ($250M in Q1, $653M remaining authorization) at ~25x forward earnings, below its peer multiples, while funding it at a 0.50% coupon. If PT stabilizes and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median on resumed double-digit consolidated growth, the stock returns to the $440 analyst-median area, roughly +40%. Bull case ~$520–550 (low-30s forward multiple on accelerating estimates).

**Bear case.** Headline growth stays in the high single digits, PT continues to drag, and a contract-reserve recurrence or ES dispute pressures the print. Estimate revisions, already flat, tip negative. The goodwill impairment test triggers a non-cash charge that, while economically irrelevant, hits sentiment. The multiple compresses toward 20x forward next-year EPS, putting the stock at ~$285, and a worse scenario (18x on flat estimates plus an impairment headline) reaches ~$260, roughly 15–17% below the current price.

The HOLD reflects three concrete, falsifiable conditions that prevent a fresh long:

1. **No 60-day catalyst, and the next print likely drags.** The next earnings release is late July. PT is in active deterioration, and the quarter is more likely to show continued PT headwind than re-acceleration. Falsifiable: a Q2 print showing PT operating income flat-to-up YoY, or management guiding PT recovery, would remove this objection.

2. **No estimate-revision inflection.** The +30% revision velocity that historically signals systematic underestimation is absent. Paying ~25x forward (PEG ~1.9) for ~13% growth is full without rising estimates. Falsifiable: a 90-day revision acceleration above +5% on the current or next year would materially change the valuation calculus.

3. **Bear-case downside exceeds the threshold for a new long.** Options backwardation prices imminent stress, the goodwill impairment indicator is live, and a credible multiple-compression/reserve-recurrence scenario reaches $260–285, a 15–17% decline. Falsifiable: a pullback into the high-$200s with fundamentals intact would bring the bear-case downside inside the range we require and convert this to a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — ES SaaS compounding, margin runway, below-peer multiple, and a large buyback at depressed prices — but the entry and catalyst timing argue for patience rather than fresh capital today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PT segment operating income flat-to-positive YoY in the Q2 2026 print (late July), or explicit management guidance for PT recovery, removing the largest active drag.
- Current-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +5% over a rolling 90-day window, confirming the consensus is catching up to ES strength.
- A pullback into the high-$200s with fundamentals intact, which would push the bear-case downside inside ~10–12% and restore an asymmetric setup.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of declining PT operating income beyond the state-contract lap, signaling structural rather than discrete weakness.
- Recurrence of material contract loss reserves in ES, indicating the dispute issue is not contained.
- A goodwill impairment charge accompanied by negative estimate revisions, or a guidance cut to consolidated growth below ~7%.
- ARR growth decelerating below ~8% YoY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release in late July, which laps the state-contract wind-down and is the first clean read on whether PT has bottomed. Secondary checkpoint is any update to the buyback pace against the remaining ~$653M authorization.

## Conclusion

Tyler Technologies is a high-quality, recession-resilient compounder with a widening moat in its Enterprise Software franchise and a structural margin tailwind from the SaaS and AWS transitions. After a ~50% drawdown, the forward multiple is reasonable relative to peers and below the lowest analyst target. The reason to wait rather than buy is specific: estimate revisions are flat, there is no catalyst inside 60 days, the Payments segment is in active decline and likely to drag the late-July print, and a credible bear case lands 15–17% lower while the goodwill impairment test sits live against the depressed share price. This is a name to own on confirmation of PT stabilization or on a lower entry, not at the current setup. **HOLD**, with a constructive 12-month bias and a clear path to upgrade.
