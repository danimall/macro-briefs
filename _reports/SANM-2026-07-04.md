**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sanmina is a top-tier global Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) provider that has made the most consequential strategic move in its four-decade history. The October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems' manufacturing business from AMD gave the company U.S.-based, rack-scale AI-server integration capacity at the moment hyperscalers are prioritizing domestic supply. Q2 FY26 revenue rose 102% year-over-year to $4.01B, the Communications/Cloud+AI segment grew 278% to $2.77B, and the stock has appreciated roughly 117% over the trailing twelve months. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 11.1% for FY26 and 7.5% for FY27 over the past 90 days, and the company has beaten estimates in four consecutive quarters (most recently by 29%). The AI-infrastructure demand driving this is concentrated in the hyperscaler/AI end market, and Sanmina's growth is currently dependent on that single demand vector.

The same deal that doubled revenue concentrated the risk profile. Top-10 customer concentration jumped from roughly 50% to 70-72%, and the company now has two customers each exceeding 10% of revenue where it previously had one. The entire ZT thesis rests on Sanmina being AMD's manufacturing partner of choice, which makes SANM an implicit leveraged bet on AMD's Instinct/MI-series accelerators winning share against a dominant NVIDIA. The balance sheet moved from net cash to roughly $2.2B of variable-rate term debt. Q3 FY26 guidance of ~$3.35B is a sequential step-down from $4.01B, signaling that AI-server revenue is lumpy and program-timing-driven rather than a clean run-rate. Insiders have sold uniformly into the rally with no open-market buys.

Our recommendation is HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. Early execution is beating plan, and the AI-infrastructure theme has durable multi-year legs. But at roughly 20x forward EPS the market is pricing the growth fairly rather than mispricing it, the concentration and AMD-dependence risks are not adequately priced given their magnitude, and the sequential guidance moderation warrants caution on run-rate durability. We would upgrade on evidence that the Q2 spike is a sustainable run-rate and that customer concentration is broadening; we would downgrade on a demand-driven (rather than timing-driven) sequential decline or an AMD share stall.

## Company Overview

Sanmina is one of the EMS industry's "big six" alongside Flex, Jabil, Celestica, Foxconn (Hon Hai), Benchmark, and Plexus. It manufactures complex electronics on an outsourced basis for OEMs across five end markets: industrial/energy, medical, defense & aerospace, automotive/transportation, and communications networks/cloud+AI infrastructure. The company employs roughly 39,000 people across 20 countries and historically produced about 80% of sales offshore.

The business runs as two segments. Integrated Manufacturing Solutions (IMS) covers PCB assembly and test, high-level assembly, and direct-order fulfillment; it is the larger, lower-margin unit (7-8.5% gross margin) and now represents roughly 90% of revenue post-ZT. Components, Products & Services (CPS) is the higher-margin unit (12-14% gross) housing vertically integrated components plus proprietary product lines: Viking Technology (memory/SSD/CXL), Viking Enterprise Solutions (hyperscale storage), SCI Technology (defense/aerospace), 42Q (cloud manufacturing software), and Advanced Microsystems (optical, RF, photonics).

Sanmina deliberately targets higher-mix, mission-critical, regulated products (medical, defense, complex compute) rather than commodity consumer EMS, seeking above-industry margins through vertical integration. The distinction matters for competitive positioning, but the core IMS business remains a thin-margin, price-competitive operation with no firm backlog — customer orders extend only 30-90 days out.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a scale step-change from the ZT Systems acquisition, which closed October 27, 2025.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q2 FY26 | 6mo FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $8.94B | $7.57B | $8.13B | $4.01B (+102% YoY) | $7.20B (+80.5%) |
| Gross margin | 8.3% | 8.5% | 8.8% | 8.8% | 8.3% |
| Operating margin | 5.1% | 4.4% | 4.4% | 3.9% | 3.2% |
| Net income (to common) | $310M | $223M | $246M | $93.6M | $142.9M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | — | $1.70 | $2.58 |
| Operating cash flow | $235M | $340M | $621M | — | $577M (6mo) |

Revenue doubled almost entirely on ZT's contribution, with ZT alone contributing roughly $1.9B (47% of total company revenue). The legacy Industrial/Medical/Defense/Auto segment was essentially flat-to-down (-0.7% in Q2), meaning nearly all growth is AI/cloud-driven.

Margins remain structurally thin at roughly 3% net. IMS gross margin rose to 8.5% post-ZT, but ZT is high-volume server/rack integration that sits within the lower-margin segment. The GAAP operating margin compressed to 3.9% in Q2, partly driven by a $59M non-cash charge from an increase in the ZT earnout liability (booked in "acquisition, integration and others"). That charge is an operational positive: it signals ZT is tracking above the acquisition forecast toward the $450M maximum earnout, meaning the headline operating-margin read understates underlying performance.

Total assets rose from $5.86B to $9.67B. The company moved from net cash to roughly $2.2B of term-loan debt (Term Loan A $1.4B, Term Loan B $800M), against $1.58B cash and $2.61B equity — gross debt/equity around 0.85x. Covenant maximum net leverage is 4.0x with a 3.0x interest coverage requirement; the company reports compliance with comfortable headroom. Roughly $1.45B notional of the variable-rate debt has been swapped to fixed at ~5.1%.

Inventory ballooned from $1.99B to $3.03B (raw materials $2.72B), reflecting ZT's working-capital-heavy server-build model. This is partly funded by $889M of customer advances (hyperscalers pre-funding materials) and elevated payables ($2.5B), which is favorable for cash conversion but ties the company's liquidity to continued hyperscaler order flow. Operating cash flow of $577M over the first six months of FY26 demonstrates the model self-funds working capital through customer deposits and receivables factoring (RPA capacity up to $1.3B post-deal). FCF conversion looks solid given the customer-funding structure.

Capital allocation has stayed aggressive despite the new leverage. Sanmina repurchased $239M in the first six months of FY26, exhausting its $300M program, and the board approved a new $600M authorization after Q2. There is no dividend. The accumulated deficit of -$2.32B is a legacy of historical impairments (dot-com era and later) rather than a current operating concern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the AI-infrastructure demand cycle. Sanmina's ZT acquisition positions it directly in the rack/cluster-scale AI-server integration layer, and the reshoring dimension is a genuine tailwind: U.S. rose to roughly 71% of Americas revenue in Q2 FY26 from about 30% a year earlier, precisely as hyperscalers seek domestic supply and AMD seeks a scaled NPI partner after divesting ZT's manufacturing arm.

The estimate-revision and surprise data support the growth case. FY26 EPS has been revised up to $11.20 and FY27 to $13.02. Current-quarter estimates rose 13.8% over 90 days. The combination of accelerating revenue and rising estimates is the pattern that typically precedes further re-rating as consensus catches up.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, revisions have flattened over the trailing 30 days (0.0% across all periods), suggesting consensus has largely caught up to the near-term reality rather than continuing to chase. Second, the Q3 FY26 guidance step-down is meaningful. Management frames AI-server revenue as lumpy and program-timing-driven, and the pro-forma combined run-rate points toward ~$10B annually. But the burden is on the company to prove the sequential decline reflects program cadence rather than demand normalization. A demand-driven step-down would materially change the thesis.

We weight the growth dimension heavily given its demonstrated reliability as a winner-selection signal, and Sanmina scores well: 80%+ revenue growth, an AI-infrastructure tailwind, and a genuine strategic pivot. The 90-day revision plateau and the guidance moderation keep this below the top tier.

## Valuation Assessment

At $219.77, Sanmina trades at roughly 20x the current-year EPS estimate and roughly 17x the FY27 estimate. This is a substantial discount to the EMS peer median P/E of 50.0x, though that median is distorted by IPG Photonics (163.6x) and Benchmark (94.6x). The more comparable AI-EMS peers — Fabrinet at 50.0x and Plexus at 42.1x — trade at roughly double Sanmina's forward multiple.

The discount is partly justified and partly an opportunity. It is justified by Sanmina's thinner margin profile, its heavier reliance on a single end-market for growth, and its sharply elevated concentration. It is an opportunity to the extent the market is under-rewarding a company delivering comparable AI-driven growth at a lower absolute multiple. On a growth-adjusted basis, a ~20x forward multiple against 80%+ revenue growth and double-digit estimate revisions is not stretched.

We do not score valuation higher because we cannot identify a specific catalyst for the market to close the peer discount, and the discount arguably reflects the genuine incremental risk (AMD dependence, two 10%+ customers) rather than a mispricing. Cheapness relative to peers is a weak standalone signal — it sorts the wrong way absent an independent catalyst, and here the "cheapness" is at least in part a rational risk premium. The trailing P/E of ~46.6x is not the relevant metric given the earnings inflection; forward metrics are appropriate, and on those the stock is fairly valued rather than clearly mispriced.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $185, median $215, mean $223.75, high $280. With the stock trading essentially at the mean and median, and with 30-day revisions flat, the consensus targets are not signaling a stale, lagging-reality dynamic that would justify treating them as floors. The valuation is balanced: modest upside to the high case, modest downside to the low case.

## Competitive Landscape

The EMS industry is intensely competitive with a history of overcapacity and commodity pricing pressure. Every major peer — Celestica, Jabil, Flex — has pivoted toward AI/data-center infrastructure, which is the sector's dominant growth driver. Sanmina's competitive trajectory is improving on the strength of the ZT deal, a direct and aggressive move to capture AI-server integration demand and deepen its AMD and hyperscaler relationships.

The distinctiveness of the bet is the U.S.-based reshored rack-scale capacity plus the AMD NPI partnership. AMD retained ZT's design and R&D and divested the manufacturing arm to Sanmina, positioning Sanmina as AMD's scaled manufacturing partner of choice. That relationship is the moat, and it is a relationship rather than proprietary IP. The purchase-price allocation reflects this: only $49M of intangibles and roughly $328M of goodwill on a $1.9B deal. Sanmina bought scale, a workforce, and hyperscaler relationships, not defensible technology. This limits future impairment risk but also means the competitive advantage is execution and the AMD tie, both of which can erode.

The competitive-position trajectory is improving — Sanmina moved from a differentiated-but-niche high-mix EMS player toward a scaled AI-infrastructure manufacturer. We hold this dimension below the trajectory implied by the prior read (which lifted it to 7.5) because the recent signal on competitive position has been unreliable as a standalone predictor, and because the "moat" here is a customer relationship that is inherently less durable than proprietary technology. The legacy segments show flat-to-declining share, so the improvement is concentrated in one vector.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile shifted materially and adversely with the ZT deal, and the magnitude of these idiosyncratic risks is not fully reflected in the ~20x forward multiple.

**Customer concentration.** This hyperscaler/AI-driven concentration is the dominant risk and it worsened by design; the slowdown or loss of a single mega-customer would be severe.

**AMD dependence.** The company's own risk factor states success depends in part on AMD's products achieving acceptance in the AI marketplace. AMD is the number-two AI accelerator player behind a dominant NVIDIA; a stall in AMD's Instinct/MI-series adoption directly impairs ZT volumes. Owning SANM is an implicit leveraged bet on AMD's AI competitiveness.

**Leverage and rate risk.** The variable-rate debt is partly swapped to fixed at ~5.1%, plus up to $450M in cash earnout ($170M booked). Covenants are comfortable but constrain flexibility, and a higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the floating tranche.

**Revenue lumpiness.** The sequential guidance step-down is the clearest near-term uncertainty. If it reflects demand normalization rather than program timing, the run-rate assumption underpinning the valuation is too high.

**Litigation and tax.** The SCI defense qui tam suit (Eckert) alleges roughly $100M in False Claims Act damages, with theoretical treble damages and debarment exposure; DOJ declined to intervene, but the suit survived a partial motion to dismiss in September 2025. An IRS Revenue Agent's Report proposes disallowing a $503M worthless-stock deduction from FY2009, which could reduce NOL carryforwards and create back-tax liability. Multiple California wage-and-hour class/PAGA actions are unquantified. The company deems the qui tam loss "not probable or estimable."

**Governance and succession.** Founder-CEO Jure Sola is 74 with no named heir apparent among the executive officers, a risk layered on top of the company's largest-ever integration.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is priced for elevated near-term uncertainty but is not directionally bearish. Near-term ATM IV of 77.8% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 69.2% (an 8.6pp rich spread), reflecting the pricing of continued volatility around AI-server order cadence and earnings. The put/call skew is balanced at 1.2%, the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.48, and the OI ratio is 0.22 — none of which signal aggressive hedging or bearish positioning. The term structure is in contango (near-term 77.8% below long-term 82.6%), a normal, complacent structure. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±12.4%, which is wide and consistent with a stock that has moved 15%+ on recent prints. The absence of elevated put demand does not contradict our fundamental caution, but it does indicate the market is not currently positioned for an imminent downside catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The AI-infrastructure demand cycle proves durable, and the Q3 sequential step-down is confirmed as program-timing lumpiness rather than demand deterioration. ZT continues to track above the earnout forecast, pushing toward the $450M maximum and confirming integration success. The AMD partnership deepens as MI-series accelerators gain share, and reshoring tailwinds sustain U.S. capacity utilization. Consensus continues to revise FY27 EPS above $13, and the peer discount narrows toward Fabrinet/Plexus multiples. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the $280 analyst high and beyond, roughly 27% upside.

**Bear Case.** The Q3 guidance step-down proves demand-driven as hyperscaler capex cadence slows or AMD's AI share stalls against NVIDIA. Customer concentration bites — a single mega-customer reduces orders — and the working-capital-heavy inventory build ($3.03B) becomes a liability if pre-funding advances slow. The newly levered balance sheet compresses flexibility in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and thin ~3% net margins leave no cushion. The SCI qui tam or IRS matter escalates. In this scenario the stock retraces toward the $185 analyst low, roughly 16% downside, with further downside if the AI-EMS multiple compresses.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $240**, roughly 9% upside. Reasoning: ~20x the FY27 EPS estimate approaches $260, but we discount for the sequential-guidance uncertainty and concentration risk, anchoring near the analyst mean of $223.75 with modest upside as FY26 delivery confirms. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $280** (analyst high), roughly 27% upside — durable AI demand, run-rate confirmation, peer-discount narrowing.

**Bear case target: $185** (analyst low), roughly 16% downside — demand-driven sequential decline or AMD share stall.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, keyed to the next two earnings prints (Q3 FY26 near-term, then Q4 FY26) that will resolve whether the sequential step-down was timing or demand.

**Upgrade triggers:** (1) Q3 FY26 revenue lands at or above ~$3.35B guidance and management frames the sequential move as timing with a re-acceleration path; (2) FY27 EPS estimates resume rising above $13.50; (3) customer concentration broadens (a third named 10%+ customer or top-10 concentration stabilizing/declining).

**Downgrade triggers:** (1) a demand-driven sequential revenue decline below ~$3.2B with negative estimate revisions; (2) evidence of an AMD AI-share stall or a lost mega-customer; (3) covenant headroom deterioration or a leverage increase beyond 3.0x net; (4) material escalation of the SCI qui tam or IRS matter.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY26 report), the pivotal test of run-rate durability.

## Conclusion

Sanmina has made a transformational, well-timed leveraged bet on AI/data-center infrastructure through the ZT/AMD acquisition, doubling revenue and re-rating the stock roughly 117% over twelve months. Early execution is beating plan (four consecutive beats, +11% FY26 estimate revisions, ZT tracking above the earnout forecast), and the AI-infrastructure demand behind it is a durable multi-year theme. On growth and forward valuation the stock screens attractively — roughly 20x forward EPS against 80%+ revenue growth is not demanding.

The offsetting concerns keep this a hold rather than a buy. Customer concentration jumped to 70-72% with two 10%+ customers; the thesis embeds an implicit leveraged bet on AMD winning AI share against NVIDIA; the balance sheet moved from net cash to $2.2B of levered debt; the Q3 sequential step-down raises legitimate run-rate questions; and insiders have sold uniformly into strength with no open-market buys. The peer discount is at least partly a rational risk premium rather than a clear mispricing, and 30-day estimate revisions have flattened, suggesting consensus has caught up to the near-term reality.

We rate SANM a HOLD (growth-hold). We would move constructive on confirmation that the Q2 revenue level is a sustainable run-rate and that concentration is broadening; we would turn negative on a demand-driven decline or an AMD share stall. At the current price the risk/reward is balanced — modest upside to base and bull cases, modest downside to the bear case — and the appropriate posture is to hold through the next print rather than add.
