## Executive Summary

Darden Restaurants is the largest publicly traded full-service restaurant operator in North America with 2,196 company-owned locations across 11 brands. The company generates $12B+ in annual revenue, produces ~$1B in free cash flow, and returns virtually all of it to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. LongHorn Steakhouse (+7.2% same-restaurant sales in Q3 FY26) is outperforming the casual dining industry by a wide margin, and the Chuy's acquisition ($649M) adds a growth vehicle in Tex-Mex.

The investment challenge is timing. Darden has missed earnings estimates in each of the last four quarters, a clear departure from its historical pattern. Estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative. The consumer environment is deteriorating — savings rate at 3.6%, gas approaching $5/gallon, mortgage rates near 7.5%, and multiple independent data points confirming spending contraction [unverified — actual: N/A]. At 20.7x forward earnings with a 3.1% dividend yield, the stock offers some downside cushion relative to peers but insufficient margin of safety to warrant a buy given the magnitude of macro headwinds facing the casual dining segment.

The options market confirms near-term uncertainty: IV is running 35.9% vs. 25.1% historical vol (a 10.8pp premium), the term structure is in backwardation, and put demand is elevated at a 13.63x put/call volume ratio. This pricing is consistent with incremental consumer stress being priced into restaurant equities ahead of key data releases.

## Company Overview

Darden operates four reportable segments:

- **Olive Garden** (944 restaurants, ~43% of revenue): The flagship casual Italian chain averaging $5.6M in annual sales per unit. Same-restaurant sales growth of +3.2% in Q3 FY26, driven by check increases (+3.6%) offset by slight traffic declines (-0.4%).
- **LongHorn Steakhouse** (608 restaurants, ~25% of revenue): The clear growth engine, with +7.2% SRS in Q3 FY26 including +3.3% traffic — one of the few full-service brands consistently gaining guests.
- **Fine Dining** (185 restaurants): Ruth's Chris, The Capital Grille, Eddie V's. Average sales of ~$7.2M per unit. Recovering from FY25 weakness (+2.1% SRS in Q3).
- **Other Business** (~460 restaurants): Cheddar's, Yard House, Chuy's (acquired Oct 2024), and Seasons 52. Bahama Breeze is being shuttered entirely.

The company owns no franchised restaurants domestically, operates with centralized purchasing and shared services from Orlando headquarters, and maintains investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/Baa2).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY25 revenue of $12.08B grew 6.0% YoY, decelerating from 8.6% in FY24. Nine-month FY26 revenue of $9.49B reflects +7.8% growth, aided by the Chuy's acquisition. Organic same-restaurant sales growth of 4.4% for the nine-month period is solid but reliant on check increases rather than guest count gains at most brands.

**Margins under modest pressure:** Operating margin of 11.2% (9-month FY26) is approximately flat YoY but below FY23-24 levels of 11.5%. Food and beverage costs have risen from 30.4% to 30.8% of sales (Q3 YoY), driven by 1.2-1.5% ingredient inflation that pricing is not fully offsetting. Labor costs remain stable at ~30.6%. G&A leverage from Chuy's synergies partially offsets.

**Cash generation remains strong:** FY25 operating cash flow of $1.71B and free cash flow of $1.06B represent an 8.8% FCF margin. FY26 capex is guided to $750-775M (up from $645M) to fund ~70 new restaurant openings. The company returned $1.08B to shareholders in FY25: $659M in dividends + $418M in buybacks. In FY26, the pace of buybacks has accelerated to $534M in the first 9 months.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $2.43B with an adjusted Debt/EBITDAR of 2.1x (well within the 3.5x covenant). The structurally negative working capital (current ratio 0.39) and high lease liabilities ($5.69B) are industry-standard for full-service restaurant companies. Liquidity is ample: $1.25B revolving credit facility fully available plus $290M in commercial paper outstanding.

**Earnings misses:** Darden has missed EPS estimates in all four reported quarters, by margins of -0.7% to -2.6%. This pattern, while individually small, suggests either persistent optimism in the consensus or underlying execution challenges the company hasn't fully resolved.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth (FY26-27):** Consensus estimates $10.62 in FY26 EPS (+11.2% vs FY25 adjusted of $9.55) and $11.37 in FY27 (+7.1%). These estimates have been essentially flat over the past 90 days, with next-quarter estimates actually declining 1.2%. The +1.6% 90-day revision to the current quarter is modest.

**Unit growth:** ~70 new restaurants planned in FY26 represents a 3.2% unit growth rate. Combined with 4-5% same-restaurant sales growth, this supports high-single-digit revenue growth. LongHorn's unit expansion (591 to 608 in 9 months) is the primary growth vector.

**Structural growth drivers:**
- LongHorn continues taking share in casual dining with consistent positive traffic
- Chuy's acquisition adds ~103 units with room for 300+ over time (management's stated opportunity)
- Casual dining industry consolidation (competitor closures) benefits survivors with scale advantages

**Growth constraints:**
- Olive Garden traffic is negative (-0.4%), meaning growth is check-driven and limited by price sensitivity
- Fine Dining recovery remains modest after a meaningful FY25 decline
- Consumer macro headwinds (detailed below) create a ceiling on near-term traffic improvement
- FY26 guidance implies ~$10.60-10.70 EPS, which at current run rate appears achievable but not beatable

## Valuation Assessment

At $195.24, DRI trades at 20.7x trailing EPS of $8.88 (reported) and approximately 18.4x FY26 consensus EPS of $10.62. On FY27 estimates of $11.37, the forward P/E is 17.2x.

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 25.9x (skewed by CAVA at 156x and BROS at 84x). Among comparable full-service operators: Texas Roadhouse trades at 25.9x (justified by superior growth), EAT at 14.2x (reflecting leverage and execution risk), and DPZ at 19.1x. DRI's 18-21x multiple is in line with DPZ and at a justified discount to TXRH given slower traffic growth.

**Yield support:** The 3.1% dividend yield provides income support. Combined with ~2-3% annual buyback yield, total shareholder return is approximately 5-6%, which offers a reasonable floor for the stock absent fundamental deterioration.

**DCF perspective:** At $1.0B FCF growing at 6-7% annually, a 9% discount rate and 3% terminal growth rate implies fair value of approximately $190-210. The current price is roughly at fair value on this basis.

The valuation is neither compelling nor obviously expensive. The stock is priced for continued mid-single-digit growth execution, which is achievable but offers limited upside unless consumer conditions improve or the company demonstrates consistent estimate beats.

## Competitive Landscape

Darden's competitive position is stable to modestly improving, primarily driven by LongHorn's share gains and the company's scale advantages in a consolidating industry.

**Strengths:** Purchasing power across $12B in revenue creates 2-3% cost advantages vs. smaller operators. Portfolio diversification across price points provides flexibility. The company's owned-restaurant model (no franchisee conflicts) allows rapid execution of corporate strategy.

**Threats:** Delivery aggregators (DoorDash, Uber Eats) are creating new competitive dynamics by unbundling the dining experience and shifting consumer habits toward at-home consumption. Grocery "convenient meals" and fast-casual (Chipotle, CAVA) continue taking share from traditional full-service dining. The 10-K explicitly identifies these as persistent competitive threats.

**Industry structure:** Full-service casual dining is a challenged category structurally. Industry traffic has been declining for over a decade (pre-COVID trends showed 1-2% annual traffic erosion). Darden's outperformance is relative — even LongHorn's +3.3% traffic growth occurs against this secular headwind, making it genuinely impressive but not a sector tailwind.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer spending deterioration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** With savings rate at 3.6%, gas approaching $5/gallon, and confidence at multi-year lows [unverified — actual: N/A], full-service dining is among the first discretionary categories cut. DRI's Q3 FY26 results show Olive Garden traffic already negative — further macro deterioration would likely accelerate this trend.

**Food cost inflation from tariffs (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** The 10-K explicitly identifies imported produce and seafood tariff risk. With tariffs on various import categories and potential for expansion, Darden's food cost ratio (already rising from 30.4% to 30.8%) could face additional pressure. Each 100bps of F&B ratio deterioration reduces EPS by approximately $0.60-0.70.

**Consecutive earnings misses (ACTIVE risk):** Four consecutive misses — even at small magnitudes (-0.7% to -2.6%) — typically indicate either structural visibility problems or consensus anchored to management guidance that proves optimistic. The next earnings report is critical: a fifth miss would likely trigger meaningful multiple compression.

**Red Lobster lease guarantees:** $87M in residual guarantee obligations from the 2014 disposition. Red Lobster filed for bankruptcy in 2024. The company has reserved $10.6M, but total exposure could be higher if lease rejection claims exceed reserves.

**Energy cost pass-through:** Oil above $100/barrel [unverified] increases both direct costs (utilities, delivery) and indirect costs (consumer wallet share diverted to gasoline). Darden's locations are overwhelmingly suburban, serving customers who drive to restaurants — elevated gas prices are a direct tax on their customer base.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term risk. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term vol > long-term vol), suggesting the market expects a stress event or catalyst in the near term. The put/call open interest ratio (1.17x) is more moderate than the volume ratio, suggesting recent hedging activity rather than structural positioning. This aligns with the consumer spending thesis — the market is pricing the possibility that upcoming consumer data catalyzes further restaurant sector selling.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($225-240, +15-23%)
- Consumer proves more resilient than feared; LongHorn continues gaining traffic
- Chuy's integration delivers above-expected synergies, driving EPS upside in FY27
- Casual dining consolidation accelerates, reducing competitive pressure and improving pricing power
- FY26 comes in at $10.80-11.00, above current $10.62 consensus
- Multiple re-rates to 21-22x as execution track record is restored

### Bear Case ($155-170, -13-21%)
- Consumer recession materializes; DRI traffic turns negative across all brands
- Tariff-driven food cost inflation adds 100-150bps to F&B ratio, compressing margins meaningfully
- Fifth consecutive earnings miss triggers multiple compression to 15-16x
- Fine Dining segment deteriorates as high-income confidence falters
- Dividend coverage becomes a concern as FCF declines with negative operating leverage

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines to $170-175 (~16x FY26 EPS) providing genuine margin of safety
- Two consecutive quarters of positive earnings surprises (reversing the 4-quarter miss streak)
- Same-restaurant traffic turns positive at Olive Garden (currently negative)
- Consumer macro data stabilizes (savings rate improvement, gas prices decline)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two additional quarters of negative traffic at Olive Garden AND LongHorn traffic decelerates below +1%
- Food cost ratio breaches 32% with no pricing offset visible
- Management cuts dividend or suspends buybacks
- Stock rallies above $225 without fundamental improvement (compresses risk/reward to unfavorable)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely June 2026 for Q4 FY26), May 19-21 consumer earnings cluster (HD/TGT/WMT provide read-through), and any tariff policy developments affecting food imports.

## Conclusion

Darden is a well-managed, scale-advantaged operator navigating an increasingly difficult consumer environment. LongHorn's traffic gains and the Chuy's acquisition provide growth optionality, and the 5-6% total shareholder yield provides support. However, four consecutive earnings misses, flat estimate revisions, and an acutely hostile consumer backdrop create meaningful near-term risk at 20.7x earnings. The stock is approximately fairly valued for its growth rate in a normal environment, but the current environment is decidedly abnormal for consumer discretionary.

The appropriate posture is to wait for either a better entry point (~$170-175, 16x earnings) or evidence that the consumer is stabilizing (positive Olive Garden traffic, resumed earnings beats). At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced — reasonable downside protection from the dividend and buyback program, but limited upside catalyst absent macro improvement.
