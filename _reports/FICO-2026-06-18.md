**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fair Isaac is a near-monopoly toll-booth business — the FICO® Score is the de facto standard for US consumer credit risk and remains required by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac for conforming mortgages — bolted onto a slower-growing decisioning-software segment. The company is performing strongly. Q2 FY26 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) delivered revenue of $691.7M (+39% YoY), Scores revenue of $475.0M (+60%), net income of $264.5M (+63%), and diluted EPS of $11.14 (+69%), with the Scores segment operating margin reaching 91%. FCF margins run near 39%. On the headline numbers, this is one of the highest-quality compounders in software.

The complication, and the reason this is not a straightforward buy, is that those record numbers were produced by years of mortgage-score price hikes at the exact moment regulators and the credit bureaus are dismantling that pricing lever. On April 22, 2026, FHFA and HUD approved VantageScore 4.0 — a joint venture owned by Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax, FICO's own distributors — for GSE mortgages. FICO responded by cutting its mortgage score price to roughly match VantageScore and shifting to per-pull pricing plus a ~$65 success fee on funded loans. That trades guaranteed royalty pricing power for volume defense and housing-cycle exposure. The +60% Scores growth is partly a harvest-before-the-storm dynamic, combining higher unit price with a ~127% surge in mortgage origination volume that will not repeat at that pace.

We rate FICO a HOLD. The stock at ~$1,127 sits ~38% below its December 2025 high of ~$1,810 and ~44% below the October 2025 high of $1,998, trading at roughly 26x current-year consensus earnings versus a 40.8x peer median. The VantageScore threat is heavily discounted, and consensus estimates rose after the April approval (current year +2.9%, next year +2.0% over 90 days) following a +11.7% EPS beat the same quarter. There is no fundamental deterioration in the trailing data — four straight beats, no guidance cut, rising estimates. What has changed is the multiple and the forward distribution of outcomes, which is now genuinely two-sided: the franchise is intact and cheap relative to peers, but the pricing model has been structurally altered and adoption data does not yet exist to quantify the impact. That is the analytical signature of a HOLD pending evidence, not a directional call.

## Company Overview

Fair Isaac operates two segments. **Scores** (~59% of FY25 revenue, ~88-91% segment operating margin) is the crown jewel: the FICO Score (300-850 scale) is used in most US credit decisions and is GSE-required for conforming mortgages. B2B scores are distributed almost entirely through the three national credit bureaus, who pay FICO a royalty per score pulled. A smaller B2C business (myFICO.com) sells directly to consumers. FICO carries only ~3,811 employees (40% in India); the moat, not headcount, drives the profitability.

**Software** (~41% of FY25 revenue, ~29-30% segment margin) sells decisioning and analytics tools — fraud detection via the Falcon network, originations, customer management, optimization — increasingly through the modular, cloud-based FICO Platform on multi-year subscriptions. The business serves financial services (92% of revenue) and is heavily US/Americas concentrated (~87-90%). CEO William Lansing has led since 2012.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and profitability are strong and accelerating.** FY25 revenue was $1,990.9M (+16% YoY), with Scores +27% and Software +3%. FY25 operating income reached $924.9M (46.5% margin, up from 43%), net income $651.9M (+27%), and diluted EPS $26.54 (+30%). The Q2 FY26 quarter was a step-change higher.

**Cash generation is excellent.** FY25 FCF was $769.9M (38.7% margin) on operating cash flow of $778.8M, against trivial capex of roughly $9M/year. First-half FY26 operating cash flow was $397.4M (+48% YoY). This is a capital-light cash machine.

**The balance sheet is where the nuance sits.** Stockholders' equity is negative: -$1.75B at FY25, deepening to -$2.10B at Q2 FY26. This is not distress; it is the cumulative result of years of buybacks, with retained earnings strongly positive ($4.98B) and treasury stock at -$8.3B. The Altman Z-Score of 2.61 ("grey zone") is misleading for an asset-light cash monopoly because its equity-based components are distorted by the buyback-driven negative book value. Leverage, however, is real and rising: total debt grew from $3.06B (FY25) to $3.64B (Q2 FY26). FICO issued $1.5B senior notes (6.00%, 2033) in May 2025 and $1.0B (6.25%, 2034) in March 2026. Interest expense rose to $133.6M in FY25 and $86.6M in 1H FY26 alone. The credit agreement caps leverage at 3.5x (step-up to 4.0x). The current ratio of 0.83 is normal for the deferred-revenue model.

**Capital allocation is a leveraged share-shrink machine.** Essentially all FCF plus incremental debt funds buybacks: $1.4B in FY25, $773.9M in 1H FY26, with a new $1.5B authorization in February 2026 and $611M repurchased in Q2 alone. Share count fell from ~26M (FY22) to ~23.2M (April 2026). This is hugely accretive while the moat holds and the multiple stays elevated, and it amplifies downside if mortgage Scores economics deteriorate, since the buybacks are funded partly at 6%+ coupons against a structure with zero equity cushion.

The four-quarter earnings record is clean: four straight beats (Q2 FY26 +11.7%, Q1 +1.8%, Q4 FY25 +3.4%, Q3 +9.1%). This warrants reasonable confidence in near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is almost entirely a Scores story, and that is both its strength and its vulnerability. Scores grew +27% in FY25 and +60% in Q2 FY26, driven by higher unit pricing and the mortgage origination surge. The price component reflects historical hikes that are now being unwound; the volume component is cyclical and conversion-dependent under the new pricing model.

The Software segment is quietly weak. Headline ARR growth of +10% masks an -8% YoY decline in non-platform ARR. Total Software revenue grew just 3% in FY25 and 7% in Q2 FY26, and Software operating income declined in Q2 FY26 on rising data-center hosting costs. The Platform pivot is the diversification bet: Platform ARR grew +49% YoY and now represents 44% of software ARR, with DBNRR recovered to 109%. For the Software story to matter to the thesis, the Platform must keep compounding ~40-50% to offset legacy erosion and reduce mortgage dependence. So far it works at the margin.

Estimate revisions are stable-to-positive. Current-year EPS consensus stands at $43.07, up +2.9% over 90 days; next-year at $54.43, up +2.0%. The 30-day moves are flat (-0.2% / +0.2%). Estimates rose after the April 22 VantageScore approval, which argues against terminal franchise impairment in the near term — the sell-side does not yet model a sharp Scores revenue erosion. What the rising estimates do not capture is the changed cyclical character of mortgage Scores revenue that the new per-pull-plus-success-fee model introduces.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,127, FICO trades at roughly 26x current-year consensus EPS of $43.07 and ~21x next-year of $54.43. Against the Application Software peer median P/E of 40.8x (WDAY 40.7x, TYL 40.8x, DT 74.8x, OKTA 85.1x, with PTC the outlier at 11.4x), FICO trades at well below the peer multiple. The trailing P/E of 35.8x in the headline data understates the forward picture because trailing earnings predate the Q2 FY26 acceleration.

The consensus price target backbone is meaningful: 21 analysts with a mean of $1,534.50 and median of $1,546 imply ~40% upside, with ratings of 5 strong buy / 11 buy / 4 hold / 1 sell strengthening from -3m (4/10/5/1). Only one of 21 analysts is bearish. BofA cut its target to $1,400 from $1,550 in May 2026 but kept its Buy and wide-moat framing. We do not dismiss this — the burden on a contrary view is to explain why ~20 of 21 analysts are wrong. The honest answer is that they may not be wrong on the franchise, but the consensus targets were largely set before the new pricing model could be observed in adoption data, and they embed an assumption that the VantageScore channel shift is slow and that FICO's pricing concession defends volume without materially compressing dollar revenue. That assumption is reasonable but unproven.

The reason this earns a 6.0 rather than a higher valuation score: the discount to peers is real and the absolute multiple is undemanding for a 39%-FCF-margin franchise, but the discount exists for an identifiable reason — the pricing lever that produced the 91% Scores margin is being actively dismantled, and we cannot yet quantify the steady-state Scores revenue under the new model. A ~26x multiple is not obviously cheap if mortgage Scores revenue becomes meaningfully more cyclical and lower-priced. The valuation is supportive but not asymmetrically so until adoption data arrives.

## Competitive Landscape

The Scores moat is the most entrenched in financial software: GSE-required status, decades of proprietary models, the Falcon fraud network with 10,000+ contributing institutions, and deep workflow integration. The threat is structural and newly materialized. After the July 2025 FHFA decision letting originators choose the score submitted to the GSEs, FHFA and HUD approved VantageScore 4.0 on April 22, 2026, and VantageScore launched a 21-lender pilot in May 2026. VantageScore is owned by the three credit bureaus that distribute FICO; those distributors are now also its competitor, and they are co-defendants in the antitrust case.

FICO's defensive response — matching VantageScore on per-pull price and adding a ~$65 funded-loan success fee — concedes the pricing lever in exchange for volume defense and upside leverage to a housing recovery. The competitive position trajectory has clearly deteriorated from the near-monopoly that existed two years ago, which is why this dimension scores 5.5 rather than the 9.0 it would have earned historically. The franchise remains dominant and approved for now, but the trajectory has turned.

In Software, FICO is a smaller player against Pegasystems, SAS, IBM, Experian, Equifax, Nice Actimize, Feedzai, Featurespace, Salesforce, and Adobe, competing on analytics depth and the Platform's land-and-expand model.

## Risk Assessment

1. **VantageScore GSE competition is the defining risk** — now real, not hypothetical, and directly aimed at the highest-margin, pricing-power-driven part of Scores.
2. **The pricing pivot changes the revenue model** — per-pull plus success fee makes mortgage Scores revenue more dependent on loan conversion and the housing cycle, and more variable.
3. **Customer concentration is structurally adverse and worsening** — the three credit bureaus were 64% of total revenue in Q2 FY26, up from 52% a year earlier. These same partners own the competing VantageScore and are antitrust co-defendants.
4. **Surviving antitrust claim** — a Sherman Act Section 2 monopolization claim (plus state claims) against FICO survived a November 2024 motion to dismiss and proceeds to discovery, an overhang amid political scrutiny of credit-score pricing.
5. **Regulatory pressure on mortgage closing costs** could permanently cap the pricing lever.
6. **Software stagnation** — non-platform ARR is in outright decline (-8% YoY); the Platform turnaround must succeed to diversify away from mortgage.
7. **Rising interest burden** from debt-funded buybacks at 6.0-6.25% coupons against negative book equity amplifies downside if Scores economics deteriorate.

The risk_factors score of 4.5 reflects that several of these are specific, binary, and not fully quantifiable — the steady-state Scores revenue under the new model is genuinely unknown, and the antitrust claim is a live discovery-stage matter. These are company-specific catalysts capable of moving the stock.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is meaningfully bearish and pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 56.0% sits above 1-year HV of 51.1% (rich by ~4.9pp), the put/call volume ratio is 1.72 and OI ratio 1.45, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 56.0% > 6-month 53.7%), consistent with a near-term catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±12.3%. The reported put/call skew figure (590.5%) and the 334.9% ATM IV in the delta snapshot are thin-options data artifacts and should be treated as non-informative; the directionally reliable signals are the elevated put volume, the backwardation, and the rich IV. For a HOLD, this confirms that the market sees a two-sided, event-driven setup rather than a one-way trade, and that the next earnings print and early VantageScore adoption data are the catalysts being hedged. Short interest of 9.7% of float with 5.2 days to cover is moderate — uncrowded enough that a positive surprise could squeeze, but not so heavy as to signal consensus capitulation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The FICO Score is too embedded, too trusted, and too deeply integrated into lender workflows to be dislodged quickly; VantageScore adoption proves slow, and FICO's price cut plus success fee defends volume while capturing upside in a housing recovery. The origination surge points to a mortgage cycle that, if sustained, makes the success-fee model accretive. The company keeps compounding EPS at 20%+, the Platform diversifies the revenue base, buybacks shrink the share count meaningfully, and the multiple re-rates back toward the peer median as the threat proves manageable. Upside target $1,400-1,550 (consensus territory, ~24-37% above current).

**Bear case.** VantageScore takes share faster than expected; the conceded pricing lever permanently compresses Scores dollar revenue; the housing cycle turns, gutting the now-cyclical mortgage Scores line; the antitrust discovery surfaces damaging facts; and the negative-equity, 6%+-coupon balance sheet amplifies the de-rating as buyback accretion reverses. Bear target $850-950 (~15-25% below current).

The asymmetry is roughly balanced — call it 1:1 to modestly favorable on the consensus upside, against a bear case that is real but partially discounted given the ~38% drawdown from the December high already absorbed. That balance, on a still-dominant, GSE-approved, cash-generative franchise trading below peer multiples, supports a HOLD. A BUY is hard to defend given the conceded pricing power and the housing-cycle re-exposure that consensus has not yet stress-tested. An AVOID is equally hard to defend: there is no fundamental deterioration, the structural threat is heavily discounted in the current-year multiple, and the bear case offers limited incremental downside relative to squeeze and buyback support. The decisive evidence — VantageScore adoption rates and the dollar impact of the new pricing model — does not yet exist.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two earnings prints (Q3 FY26 expected late July 2026, Q4 FY26 in November) and any early VantageScore pilot adoption data are the decisive catalysts. The pilot launched in May 2026; meaningful adoption signal should emerge over the next two to three quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- VantageScore adoption stalls or remains de minimis in GSE mortgage volume through year-end FY26, AND FICO mortgage Scores dollar revenue holds or grows under the new per-pull-plus-success-fee model (i.e., the price cut is more than offset by volume/success fees).
- Forward P/E compresses below ~20x ($850-900 area) while estimates hold, restoring clear asymmetric upside.
- Platform ARR sustains ~40%+ growth and crosses ~55% of software ARR, demonstrably diversifying revenue away from mortgage dependence.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A measurable Scores revenue miss or guidance cut tied to VantageScore share loss or housing-cycle reversal — the first quarter of genuine fundamental deterioration.
- The three-CRA concentration rises further alongside evidence the bureaus are actively steering volume to VantageScore.
- An adverse development in the Sherman Act Section 2 discovery, or leverage approaching the 3.5x covenant cap amid declining EBITDA.

**Reassessment point:** Q3 FY26 earnings (late July 2026), with VantageScore pilot data as the wildcard input.

## Conclusion

Fair Isaac is a wide-moat, hyper-profitable, capital-light franchise posting its best-ever results at the precise moment the regulatory and competitive structure that produced those results is being deliberately altered. The financial momentum is real and the trailing data shows no deterioration. The structural threat is also real and newly materialized, and FICO's own price concession confirms management takes it seriously. With the stock off its December high, trading below the peer median, and consensus implying meaningful upside, much of the bad news is discounted, but the steady-state economics of the post-VantageScore Scores business cannot yet be quantified, and the per-pull-plus-success-fee model re-introduces housing-cycle dependency that consensus targets do not appear to stress-test.

We rate FICO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The thesis does not predicate on terminal impairment — the franchise is intact, GSE-approved, and dominant — nor does it support a confident growth call given the conceded pricing lever and the two-sided forward distribution. The decisive evidence is VantageScore adoption data and the dollar impact of the new pricing model, neither of which exists yet. The appropriate posture is to wait for that evidence at the next one to two earnings prints, with clear, measurable triggers to move to BUY (adoption stalls, dollar Scores revenue holds, or valuation compresses below ~20x) or to AVOID (the first quarter of Scores revenue deterioration or adverse antitrust development).

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward valuation was overstated — FICO trades at ~26x current-year consensus EPS ($43.07), not ~21x as originally claimed (~21x applies only to next-year EPS of $54.43); the "below ~17x" upgrade trigger was internally inconsistent with the corrected multiple and has been revised to ~20x. The valuation discount to peers remains real but is less pronounced than the report originally implied.
