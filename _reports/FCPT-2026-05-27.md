**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Four Corners Property Trust is a net-lease REIT with 1,303 properties across 48 states, operating at 99.6% occupancy with a 5.8% dividend yield at current prices. The company generates stable cash flows from triple-net leases, primarily to restaurant tenants, with Darden Restaurants still representing ~45% of annualized base rent. At $24.97, the stock trades at approximately 14x trailing AFFO ($1.78/share) and offers a yield well above the net-lease peer median of 4.8%.

The investment case hinges on two competing forces: (1) income generation and portfolio quality are strong — zero bad debt, 5.1x EBITDAR coverage, and a CEO buying stock in the open market — while (2) AFFO/share growth is structurally limited to 2-4% annually due to dilutive equity issuance, estimates are declining (-7.4% over 90 days), the company has missed or matched earnings estimates for four consecutive quarters, and the critical Darden lease renewal window (extension notices due October 2026) creates near-term binary uncertainty.

The stock is fairly valued as a high-yield, low-growth income vehicle, but the risk/reward profile does not support a BUY given the deteriorating earnings trajectory and pending Darden catalyst. It warrants holding for income while monitoring the October 2026 renewal outcome.

## Company Overview

Four Corners Property Trust was spun off from Darden Restaurants in November 2015 and owns single-tenant properties leased on a triple-net basis. The portfolio spans restaurant, auto service, medical retail, and QSR categories. The company has systematically diversified from its Darden legacy, reducing concentration from near-100% at spin-off to ~45% today, while growing from the initial ~400-property portfolio to 1,303 properties.

The business model is straightforward: FCPT acquires properties at cap rates averaging 6.8% (cash), funds them with a mix of ~4% cost debt and ATM equity issuance, collects triple-net rent (tenants pay taxes, insurance, and maintenance), and distributes ~80% of AFFO as dividends.

The company also operates seven LongHorn Steakhouse restaurants directly under franchise agreements with Darden, contributing $31.5M in annual revenue (11% of total). This segment provides modest additional income but is not the core thesis.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Cash Flow Trajectory:**
Total revenue grew 9.7% in 2025 to $294.1M, driven by $325.5M in acquisitions (105 properties). Rental revenue specifically grew 10.8% to $262.6M. Q1 2026 cash rental income reached $70M (+10% YoY), maintaining the aggregate growth trend.

**Per-Share Metrics Tell a Different Story:**
AFFO/share grew from $1.73 (2024) to $1.78 (2025), a 2.9% increase. FFO/share grew from $1.65 to $1.68. The gap between aggregate growth (~12%) and per-share growth (~3%) is entirely attributable to equity dilution: shares outstanding grew 32% over three years (82M to 108M), with 8.2M shares issued via ATM in 2025 alone at a weighted average price of $27.95.

**Earnings Miss Pattern:**
Four consecutive quarters of misses/in-line results: -12.6% (Q1 2026), -5.3% (Q4 2025), -4.0% (Q3 2025), -1.3% (Q2 2025). The misses are worsening in magnitude. This suggests either consensus estimates are stubbornly optimistic or operational execution is slipping. Given the simplicity of the business model (collect rent), the more likely explanation is that acquisition timing, transaction costs, and interest expense are coming in slightly worse than modeled.

**Balance Sheet:**
Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDAre stands at 5.0x — conservative for a net-lease REIT and below many larger peers (NNN at ~5.5x, O at ~5.6x). The $200M term loan raised in April 2026 at 4.9% all-in provides clear acquisition capacity. Total liquidity is ~$402M. Debt maturities are well-staggered with the nearest being $100M (extendable) in November 2026 and $50M in December 2026.

**Dividend Coverage:**
At $1.4315/share annualized versus $1.78 AFFO, the payout ratio is 80.4%. This leaves reasonable headroom. Current yield of 5.8% at $24.97 is attractive relative to net-lease peers.

## Growth Analysis

FCPT's growth trajectory is constrained by its capital structure decisions. Aggregate AFFO is growing 10-12% annually through acquisitions, but the ATM equity issuance required to fund those acquisitions limits per-share growth to 2-4%. This is a structural feature of the business model for REITs at this scale.

Estimate revisions are negative: current-year EPS consensus has fallen 7.4% over 90 days (from $1.26 to $1.17). Next year shows modest positive revision (+1.3% over 90 days to $1.22). The negative momentum is concentrated in the near-term, likely reflecting the Q1 2026 miss flowing through models.

Growth drivers include:
- Continued acquisitions at 6.8% cap rates with ~200 bps spread to cost of capital
- 1.5% average annual contractual rent escalations
- Lease renewals capturing mark-to-market (27 renewals at +6% above prior rent)
- Diversification into auto service, medical retail, and QSR segments

Growth constraints include:
- Dilutive equity issuance limits per-share accretion
- Cap rate compression in the broader market could narrow acquisition spreads
- Rising interest rates increase the cost of new debt (April 2026 loan at 4.9% vs. older swapped debt at ~4.0%)

Net assessment: low-single-digit AFFO/share growth is the realistic trajectory absent a material improvement in equity market pricing (which would make ATM issuance less dilutive).

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.97, FCPT trades at:
- 14.0x trailing AFFO ($1.78)
- ~22.8x trailing P/E (but P/FFO or P/AFFO are the appropriate metrics for REITs due to depreciation)
- 5.8% dividend yield

**Relative to peers:**
The peer median P/E of 18.1x (GLPI at 15.1x, EPR at 18.1x) suggests FCPT at 14x AFFO is not expensive on a funds-flow basis. GLPI offers 6.9% yield at 15.1x; EPR offers 6.3% at 18.1x. FCPT's positioning between these — higher quality tenants than EPR (theaters, education) but lower growth — seems approximately fair.

**Relative to recent issuance:**
The stock trades 10.7% below its 2025 weighted ATM price of $27.95. While this doesn't guarantee reversion, the company's own capital allocation (issuing shares) implied a higher fair value as recently as 12 months ago.

**Analyst consensus:** Median target $28.00, mean $28.11, range $25-$30. Consensus implies 12% upside from current levels. With 2 Strong Buys, 2 Buys, and 5 Holds, the street is neutral-to-modestly constructive.

The valuation is not demanding, but the negative estimate revision trend and earnings miss pattern prevent a higher score. A 14x AFFO multiple is reasonable for 2-4% growth.

## Competitive Landscape

FCPT operates in the competitive net-lease acquisition market alongside Agree Realty (ADC), NNN REIT, Realty Income (O), and others. Its differentiation lies in:

1. **Restaurant/service-retail specialization** — management claims proprietary underwriting expertise in restaurant credit
2. **Lower average property cost ($2.6M)** — enables volume acquisitions below the radar of larger REITs
3. **Disciplined cap rate targeting (6.8%)** — higher than the 5-6% trophy-asset market
4. **Avoidance of structurally impaired categories** — no theaters, pharmacies, or experiential retail

The 5.1x EBITDAR coverage ratio (portfolio-wide) is genuinely strong and provides a buffer against tenant distress. Same-store sales performance among major tenants is positive (Olive Garden +3%, LongHorn +7%, Chili's +4% in Q1 2026).

However, the competitive position is not improving meaningfully. FCPT underperformed its peer group by 25 percentage points in cumulative TSR over 5 years (102% vs. 127%). The company is growing its portfolio but not creating visible competitive separation in cost-of-capital advantages or acquisition pipeline exclusivity.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Near-Term Risk — Darden Lease Renewal (October 2026):**
Darden represents 44.7% of ABR with extension notices due by October 2026. Average remaining term on Darden properties is only 4.8 years. While 5.8-6.0x rent coverage makes renewal economically rational, the binary nature of this event creates concentration risk. A failure to renew (unlikely but non-zero probability) would be catastrophic; even partial non-renewal would materially impact estimates. Management expressed confidence, but until the decision is formalized, the market will not fully credit the stock.

**Dilution Risk:**
32% share count growth in 3 years. At current prices (~$25), issuing equity to fund 6.8% cap rate acquisitions is barely accretive on a per-share basis after transaction costs. If the stock remains depressed, the acquisition model's per-share economics deteriorate.

**Bahama Breeze Closures:**
10 properties (1.3% of ABR) affected. 6 converting to other Darden brands, 4 being re-tenanted. Manageable but illustrates brand-specific vulnerability within the concentrated Darden relationship.

**Interest Rate Environment:**
The April 2026 term loan at 4.9% is already 90 bps above the blended cost of existing hedged debt. FCPT faces headwinds on two fronts: (1) higher refinancing costs as swaps expire in November 2027, and (2) rate-sensitive equity valuation compression.

**Earnings Deterioration Pattern:**
Four consecutive misses with worsening magnitude (-1.3% → -4.0% → -5.3% → -12.6%) is concerning. While net-lease REITs have simple revenue models, the consistency of misses suggests either higher interest expense, slower deployment timing, or elevated G&A relative to expectations.

**Short Interest:**
4.6% of float short with 5.8 days to cover — modest but not negligible. This represents a small contingent betting against the stock, likely focused on the Darden renewal risk or rising-rate thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($28-30, +12-20% upside):**
- Darden exercises lease extensions in October 2026, removing the overhang and triggering a relief rally
- Fed pivots dovish, compressing cap rates and boosting net-lease REIT valuations
- Diversification progress continues, with Darden falling below 40% of ABR within 12-18 months
- Zero bad debt track record validates portfolio construction thesis
- Stock reverts toward $27-28 ATM issuance price level

**Bear Case ($21-22, -12-20% downside):**
- Darden signals partial non-renewal or seeks materially below-market rent resets
- Rising rates compress REIT valuations further
- Earnings misses continue, pushing estimates further negative
- ATM issuance at depressed prices accelerates dilution
- Bahama Breeze situation expands to other underperforming Darden brands

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Darden exercises lease extensions (October 2026) — removes the single largest risk overhang
2. AFFO/share growth accelerates to 5%+ (would require less dilutive funding or faster deployment)
3. Stock declines below $22 (would push yield above 6.5% and P/AFFO below 12.5x — compelling income entry)

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Darden signals non-renewal or significant rent reduction on more than 10% of its ABR exposure
2. EPS estimates fall more than 15% from current levels (below $1.00 annual)
3. Leverage exceeds 6.0x Net Debt/EBITDAre without corresponding portfolio growth
4. Occupancy drops below 97% indicating broader tenant stress

**Reassessment timeline:** October 2026 (Darden extension notices), Q2 2026 earnings (July), and any material rate policy shifts.

## Conclusion

FCPT is a well-run net-lease REIT generating reliable income (5.8% yield, 80% payout ratio) from a portfolio with best-in-class rent coverage metrics. The business model is working as designed at the aggregate level, but per-share value creation is structurally limited by dilutive equity issuance, and the earnings trend is clearly deteriorating. Four consecutive misses and falling estimates demand patience rather than conviction.

The Darden lease renewal decision — due in months, not years — represents the single most important catalyst for the stock. A positive outcome would likely push shares back toward the $27-28 range; a negative outcome would be severely damaging. Until that binary resolves, the stock is an income vehicle worth holding for its yield but not worth adding to at the current risk/reward balance.

Rating: HOLD for income. Monitor the October 2026 Darden extension notice window as the key reassessment catalyst.
