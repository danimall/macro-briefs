## Executive Summary

Instacart reported Q1 2026 results on May 6 that showed revenue of $1.02B (+14% YoY) and GTV growth of 13%. Q2 GTV guidance of $10.1–10.25B implies 11–13% year-over-year growth, a modest sequential deceleration from Q1's 13% pace. The company is generating $910M in annual FCF (24% margin), buying back shares at a pace that retires a significant portion of float annually, and growing its topline at a rate that meaningfully exceeds the ~25x forward P/E multiple.

Adjusted EBITDA margins expanded from 21% (2023) to 26% (2024) to 29% (2025) on relatively stable revenue growth. At $40.16, the stock trades at approximately 13.9x consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.89 — a valuation that implies little growth premium for a platform with 74% gross margins and structural operating leverage. Analyst consensus median target of $50 implies 24% upside, and estimate revisions are positive across all timeframes (+9.6% on current year over 90 days, +14.7% on next year).

The key risk that prevents maximum conviction is the retailer concentration: 43% of GTV from 3 retailers means a single partnership loss would be catastrophic. Secondary concerns include advertising revenue sensitivity to brand spending pullbacks, the CEO also serving as Board Chair, and the declining cash balance as buybacks consume available liquidity.

## Company Overview

Maplebear Inc., operating as Instacart, is the dominant third-party grocery delivery and enablement platform in North America. The company operates a three-sided marketplace connecting consumers, 2,200+ retail banners, 9,000+ advertising brands, and approximately 600,000 gig-worker shoppers. Revenue splits approximately 72% transaction-based and 28% advertising, with the advertising segment driving disproportionate profitability.

The platform reaches 98%+ of North American households and processed 338.8M orders on $37.2B of GTV in FY2025. Instacart's strategic pivot from pure marketplace to technology partner (via its Enterprise Platform, Connected Stores hardware, and Wynshop acquisition) aims to make the company indispensable to retailer e-commerce operations regardless of whether orders flow through Instacart's consumer app or retailers' own digital storefronts.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
FY2025 revenue of $3.74B grew 11% YoY, consistent with the prior year's pace. Q1 2026 showed acceleration to 14% YoY ($1.02B), driven by 10% order growth and continued expansion of advertising revenue (up 16% YoY, the fastest advertising growth since Q3 2023). GTV reached $37.2B for FY2025 (+11%) with Q1 2026 tracking at ~$10.2B (+13% YoY).

**Profitability:**
Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 21% to 26% to 29% over three years. GAAP operating margin reached 13.3% in FY2025. Gross margins are 74%, reflecting the asset-light marketplace model. FCF of $910M (24.3% margin) significantly exceeds GAAP net income ($447M), though the gap is partly driven by non-cash items including $352M of stock-based compensation and $98M in deferred tax benefits included in operating cash flow.

**Balance Sheet:**
Cash declined from $2.14B (end 2023) to $637M (end 2025) as the company deployed $2.7B+ in buybacks. In May 2026, the company entered a $500M revolving credit facility for working capital and general corporate purposes, though no amounts were drawn at closing. Liabilities-to-equity of approximately 0.39 and Altman Z-Score of 4.39 indicate the balance sheet remains healthy, with minimal conventional financial debt. However, the cash depletion trajectory bears monitoring as the company's liquidity cushion has narrowed significantly.

**Capital Return:**
The buyback program is the defining capital allocation decision. With approximately $1.3B repurchased in FY2025, the company is retiring a significant portion of shares outstanding per year. This mechanically supports EPS growth even in modest revenue environments and signals management confidence in the stock's undervaluation.

## Growth Analysis

Order growth accelerated from 9% (2024) to 15% (2025), suggesting the platform has not saturated its addressable market. Online grocery penetration remains approximately 15% of total U.S. grocery spend, leaving substantial runway. Key growth vectors include:

1. **Instacart+ membership expansion** — driving order frequency and basket consistency
2. **Enterprise Platform** — powering retailers' own e-commerce (380+ retailers), with Wynshop acquisition expanding capabilities
3. **Advertising monetization** — at 2.9% of GTV, still below Amazon's advertising take rate on its marketplace
4. **Uber Eats partnership** — adding restaurants to the platform and driving engagement without capital expenditure
5. **Connected Stores (Caper Carts, Carrot Tags)** — hardware + software for physical stores

Q2 2026 GTV guidance implies 11–13% year-over-year growth, a modest deceleration from Q1's 13%. Instacart's 10-K identifies macro uncertainty and trade policy as risk factors for brand spending. Q1 2026 advertising revenue grew 16% YoY with over 9,000 active brand partners. FY2027 EPS estimates rose approximately 14.7% over the past 90 days, from $2.52 to $2.89.

## Competitive Landscape

Instacart's competitive position is defensible but not invulnerable. The 2,200+ retail banner network creates a breadth advantage that no competitor replicates — Amazon owns one banner (Whole Foods + Fresh), DoorDash partners with limited grocers, and Walmart operates only its own stores. This breadth matters because consumers want selection across multiple retailers.

The advertising flywheel is the key competitive moat: 9,000+ brands spend on Instacart's platform because it offers closed-loop attribution (ad impression → purchase → delivery confirmation) across the broadest retailer footprint. This data advantage compounds as more brands and retailers join.

The strategic risk is disintermediation: retailers building their own technology stacks, reducing dependency on Instacart's marketplace while potentially maintaining the Enterprise relationship. The Enterprise Platform strategy (powering retailers' own sites) is the hedge against this, but it generates lower margins than the marketplace.

DoorDash's grocery expansion is the most credible competitive threat, particularly for impulse/convenience orders. However, Instacart's average basket size (~$110) and weekly-shop use case differentiate it from DoorDash's smaller-basket, faster-delivery model.

## Risk Assessment

**Retailer Concentration:** Three retailers represent 43% of GTV. If Kroger, Costco, or another major partner departed, the revenue impact would be 15-20% minimum, with advertising revenue proportionally affected. The market likely under-appreciates this at current multiples.

**Advertising Cyclicality:** At 28% of revenue and a disproportionate share of profit, advertising is the margin engine. Instacart's 10-K identifies macro uncertainty and trade policy as risk factors for brand spending. A recession-driven cut in CPG advertising budgets would compress margins faster than the market expects, though Q1 2026 results showed continued strong advertising growth.

**Regulatory/Legal:** The FTC $60M settlement (December 2025), the KSF investigation (March 2026), and ongoing independent contractor classification challenges create a persistent overhang. NYC minimum earnings legislation (September 2025) signals regulatory expansion.

**Governance:** CEO Rogers also serving as Board Chair since November 2025 concentrates authority during a transition period. No insider purchases observed to offset this concern.

**Liquidity Trajectory:** Cash declining from $2.14B to $637M due to buybacks, with a new $500M revolving credit facility available but undrawn, leaves a narrower buffer for acquisitions, competitive responses, or cyclical downturns. The $910M annual FCF provides capacity to sustain buybacks from operating cash flow, but the margin for error has decreased.

## Conclusion

CART presents a favorable risk/reward at $40.16. The stock trades at a valuation that under-appreciates the quality of the underlying business: 14% revenue growth in Q1, 29% EBITDA margins, $910M in FCF, and a disciplined buyback program. At 13.9x FY2027 consensus EPS with positive estimate revisions and significant annual share retirement, the stock is priced as an ex-growth grocery stock rather than a high-margin technology platform with operating leverage.

Investors appear focused on the modest Q2 GTV guidance deceleration (11–13% vs. Q1's 13%) and macro risks to advertising, while underweighting the structural margin improvements and the buyback program's mechanical EPS support. The reported short interest of approximately 10% reflects lingering skepticism that should unwind as estimates continue rising.

The retailer concentration risk (43% from top 3) is genuine and prevents maximum conviction sizing. This is a standard-sized position (2-3%) rather than a high-conviction overweight.

**Recommendation: BUY at $40.16 with a 12-month base case of $50 (+24%).**
