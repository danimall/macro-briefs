## Executive Summary

Cable One's equity has become a residual claim rather than an operating investment. At $37.32 the 5.673 million shares outstanding are worth $211.7M (the $214.4M market cap in the data feed reflects the $37.80 extended-hours print; the arithmetic below uses the frozen close). Against that sits $3,055.4M of principal debt and $166.2M of cash at 6/30/2026, and a contractual obligation to acquire the remaining ~55% of Mega Broadband Investments for approximately $480M in cash while consolidating roughly $920M of MBI debt maturing November 2027. Pro forma net debt of approximately $4,289M against combined LTM EBITDA of roughly $908M implies 4.72x, or 5.11x if the Q2 2026 run rate is the right base. The equity is 6.4% of enterprise value at par debt values and 8.6% at market debt values. Its value is determined almost entirely by the outcome of a creditor negotiation that has already gone badly once.

The second-quarter report changed the operating story, not just the balance-sheet arithmetic. Through FY2025 the revenue decline was largely deliberate — video PSUs down 22.1% at programming costs equal to 59-63% of video revenue, while residential data ARPU still rose 0.6% to $80.84. In Q2 2026 residential data units fell 6.6% to 870.0k *and* ARPU fell 0.8% to $80.56, passings contracted from 2,899.4k to 2,847.0k, revenue fell 8.4%, and Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed approximately 360bp to 49.7%. Losing price and volume simultaneously on a fixed cost base against fixed debt is the mechanism by which a 4.1x levered cable asset becomes a 6x levered one within three years. Management's own filing language now describes targeting customers "who are more value-conscious," which is what a premium-priced incumbent says when it begins defending share on price.

Three prior reports rated this AVOID, with the composite score declining from 2.9 to 2.5. The bar for a fourth unchanged call should be higher, and the new evidence clears it: the MBI option flipped from a $31.8M asset to a $426.0M liability, the MBI equity stake was written down $349.8M to $31.8M, a further $597.7M of franchise and goodwill impairment was recorded, a deferred tax valuation allowance was established, and the June MBI term-loan exchange offer failed at ~34% participation with the company stating it expects not to consummate it. The stock's ~72% twelve-month decline is not a mispricing to exploit; it tracks identifiable deterioration. Composite score moves to 2.15. **AVOID** — and, for reasons detailed below, not a short.

## Company Overview

Cable One operates rural and small-market broadband under the single Sparklight brand following a 2025 consolidation of acquired system names. As of 12/31/2025 the footprint covered 24 states, approximately 2.9 million passings and roughly 1.0 million residential and business customers, of which ~999,000 were data subscribers, ~88,000 video and ~94,000 voice. Concentration is high: about 75% of customers sit in seven states (Arizona, Idaho, Mississippi, Missouri, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Texas). Headcount fell 8.0% during 2025 to 2,592.

FY2025 revenue mix was residential data 60.1%, business data 15.3%, residential video 12.5%, residential voice 1.8%. The network is 750 MHz+, DOCSIS 3.1 across the footprint with gigabit available to 100% of passings and multi-gig in 53% of markets; DOCSIS 4.0 is being deployed. Q4 2025 average residential data usage was ~835 GB/month with more than 30% of customers above 1 TB, while peak bandwidth utilization stayed at or below 20%.

Beyond the consolidated business, Cable One holds minority equity-method stakes in other rural operators — Point Broadband (~15%, carried at $135.0M), Nextlink (~22%, $114.5M), CTI Towers, Visionary — totaling $298.6M of carrying value at 6/30/2026, down from $613.8M. MBI, held at ~45% since 2020, is the exception: the other holders exercised their put on January 2, 2026, compelling Cable One to buy the rest.

In the worst mechanical case, roughly $3.5B of obligations cluster inside about seven months. That, rather than FY2026 operations, is what a $211.7M equity value discounts.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis. Residential data PSUs fell 6.6% YoY to 870.0k, total PSUs 8.1%, customer relationships 6.3%. Passings fell from 2,899.4k to 2,847.0k, so the denominator is shrinking too; penetration slipped from 31.0% to 30.6%. ARPU turned negative for the first time in the recent record. Video is being deliberately harvested at -22.1% annually. MBI, the acquisition that will be consolidated, is declining faster than the parent with expenses rising 7.8%.

Estimate revisions require careful handling. Current-year consensus moved from +$23.59 thirty days ago to -$188.31, an artifact of the Q2 impairments rather than an operating revision, on a two-analyst sample. Next-year consensus rose 24.8% to $27.34, implying roughly $155M of net income on 5.67M shares. That is the bull framing: at $37.32 the stock trades at 1.4x that number. The problem is that the estimate almost certainly assumes no further impairment, does not reflect the full step-up in interest expense from consolidating approximately $1.4B of incremental debt, and says nothing about who owns the equity when the 2028 stack is addressed. Earnings power exists in this asset. The open question is whose it is.

The two potential offsets are both late. The MVNO/MVNE pilot launched in Q4 2025 — the lever Comcast, Charter and Altice used to defend broadband ARPU and churn — arrives five to nine years behind peers, at roughly 1/50th of Charter's scale, through an intermediary rather than a direct MVNO agreement. It is unlikely to be a margin contributor within the window that matters for the 2027-2028 refinancing, though it is low-capital and could measurably reduce churn even subscale, which is the stated objective. DOCSIS 4.0 and multi-gig in 53% of markets add capacity the network does not need: peak utilization is at or below 20%.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer P/E comparison is not usable — CABO's reported EPS is -$63.21, and the -0.20 "P/E" in the data feed is meaningless. The relevant sector context is that the entire group has derated: Charter at 3.8x earnings and 68% below its 52-week high, Comcast at 4.7x and 34% below, Versant at 6.7x. Cable multiples have compressed on the same FWA and fiber-overbuild dynamics that afflict Cable One, so a low absolute multiple here carries no relative signal.

On enterprise value, 3.27x LTM EBITDA at market debt prices is genuinely below where cable assets clear in private transactions, and Cable One is paying 9.0x for MBI, which is a live data point on private-market value. That is the strongest bull argument. It fails as an equity argument for a specific reason: the multiple is applied to EBITDA declining 12-15% annually against debt that is fixed in dollar terms. At a 10% annual EBITDA decline, combined EBITDA falls from $908M to roughly $662M by 2029 while net debt stays near $4.2B — 6.4x, at which point the equity is a formality. At flat EBITDA, 5.5x on $908M gives $4.99B of EV and roughly $700M of equity, or $123 per share. The entire equity value sits in the gap between those two paths, and Q2 delivered evidence for the first.

Free cash flow yield looks spectacular and is misleading. Annualized H1 FCF of approximately $193M against $211.7M of market cap is a 91% yield — before $480M of cash leaves for the put, and before the stack reprices. Post-MBI, a rough build: combined EBITDA of $908M, combined capex of approximately $361M ($285.3M plus MBI's $75.3M), and interest rising from FY2025's $130M to roughly $235M as the put draw and MBI debt are added, leaves about $312M. If the whole $4.3B stack eventually refinances near 10% — which is what a 2030 note at 53.8 cents implies — interest becomes roughly $430M and FCF falls to about $117M. Positive, but insufficient to de-lever a 5x structure on a declining base.

**Scenario framework.** Because the equity is an option on a creditor negotiation, point precision is inappropriate. Probability-weighting:

- **Restructuring or coercive exchange (≈45%)** — the 5.00x covenant step-back collides with the November 2027 MBI maturity; unsecured holders at 53.8 cents convert to equity; existing common heavily diluted or eliminated. **$0-8, midpoint $5.**
- **Amend-and-extend with dilutive financing (≈35%)** — maturities pushed with higher coupons, warrants, structurally senior new money or asset sales from the $298.6M minority portfolio. Equity survives but is diluted and carries higher interest. **$30-45, midpoint $35.**
- **Clean refinancing and operating stabilization (≈20%)** — MBI closes early at a discount, ARPU stabilizes, mobile reduces churn, term loans extend near current pricing. **$85-110, midpoint $90.**

Weighted value: 0.45($5) + 0.35($35) + 0.20($90) = **$32.5**, roughly 13% below the $37.32 close, with a 45% branch that loses nearly everything. Analyst targets bracket this widely — low $38, mean $54.25, median $46.50, high $86 — and the consensus rating distribution (five hold, one sell, zero buys) alongside a mean target 45% above spot is exactly the wide-dispersion pattern that scenario-driven stub valuations produce. Those targets are not independent confirmation of value here; they are the same scenario tree with different weights.

Valuation score of 3.0 reflects a real discount to private-market asset value offset by a declining numerator, a fixed and imminent claim ahead of the equity, and an absence of any identified mechanism by which shareholders capture the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The rural cable thesis rested on three premises stated plainly in the 10-K: in over 40% of the footprint there is no wired competitor offering ≥100 Mbps; rural density makes overbuild returns difficult; and technology arrives late in these markets. Each has been attacked.

Fixed wireless access from national carriers is named explicitly, and the company's rebuttal — that its wired plant delivers "speeds, capacity and reliability far in excess of what any cell phone internet offering can provide" — is technically correct and commercially incomplete. Rural households consuming a fraction of the 835 GB average do not need DOCSIS 4.0 headroom; they respond to price, and Cable One's $80.56 ARPU sits far above typical FWA pricing. Units down 6.6% with ARPU down 0.8% is what price competition looks like at the start. The genuine counter-evidence: peak utilization at or below 20% and more than 30% of customers above 1 TB means the heavy-usage cohort cannot be served by FWA, which is consistent with erosion concentrated in light users and therefore modest ARPU decline rather than collapse.

Subsidized overbuild directly negates the density premise. RDOF, ARPA, the Infrastructure Act and BEAD direct billions into "unserved" and "underserved" areas; Cable One is both a recipient and a challenger of awards in territories it already serves, and the 10-K concedes challenge efforts "may not always be successful." Municipal utilities may compete without obtaining a franchise. Video is structurally lost, with programming at 59-63% of video revenue and broadcaster consolidation strengthening retransmission leverage after a July 2025 decision vacated much of the FCC's 2018 ownership review.

What remains real is the cost structure. Even after 360bp of compression, a 49.7% Adjusted EBITDA margin is at or above most cable peers on a business that is 60% residential data, and FY2025 capex was 19.0% of revenue. The asset throws off cash. Trajectory, which is what matters for scoring, is clearly negative: price and volume both falling, footprint contracting, and the defensive product (mobile) arriving too late to matter before the refinancing.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing at the 2027-2028 wall (highest).** Up to roughly $3.5B of obligations cluster in a seven-month window, including a springing provision on $936M. The June 2026 MBI term-loan exchange offer — cash plus new Cable One debt — drew approximately 34% participation, and the company expects not to consummate it. Management's own new disclosure language is that it is "analyzing strategic options available to us, including raising additional funds, amending existing debt agreements, among other financing transactions." That is direct evidence that lenders will not extend on the terms offered. One honest alternative reading: exchange offers routinely carry high minimum-participation conditions and get relaunched on improved terms, and lenders declining an exchange can signal confidence in *par* recovery on a November 2027 maturity — bullish for asset value, bearish for Cable One's negotiating leverage.

**The MBI put is a non-discretionary, out-of-the-money forward.** Cable One did not choose to buy at this price; the counterparties put it. Approximately $480M leaves (or revolver capacity is consumed) for an asset the company itself marks at a fraction of the price, and approximately $920M of November 2027 maturities arrive with it. No walk-away option is disclosed. The 12% annualized early-close discount could reduce the outlay somewhat; whether closing has occurred and at what final price is unverified as of the cutoff.

**Covenant compression.** The 5.50x accommodation lasts four quarters. If combined EBITDA continues declining at the H1 2026 pace, the step-back to 5.00x arrives at roughly the same time as the first major maturity, handing lenders control of the negotiation.

**Further impairment.** Only $326.4M of book equity remains against $769.1M of goodwill and approximately $1.1B of franchise value, with an implied control premium already at 15-20% and the shares down about 70% since the measurement date. Non-cash and covenant-irrelevant, but it removes any book-value floor.

**Portfolio marks.** $298.6M of remaining equity-method value (Point $135.0M, Nextlink $114.5M). MBI's carrying value fell 92% in six months; the durability of the remaining marks is not established. These stakes are also the most likely source of emergency liquidity, which cuts both ways.

**Deferred tax valuation allowance.** Recorded in Q2 2026, with the effective benefit rate falling to 11.1%. The mechanical explanation — capital-loss-character DTAs requiring future capital gains — is the more likely driver than a deterioration in taxable-income forecasts. It should not be read as a going-concern signal on its own.

**Concentration and regulation.** Roughly 75% of customers in seven states; RDOF and Enhanced ACAM build-out milestones carry explicit clawback risk. Broadband remains lightly regulated Title I after the Sixth Circuit vacated the 2024 Open Internet Order, but open FCC inquiries cover data caps, outage reporting, service standards, early-termination and billing-cycle fees, plus proposals to extend USF contributions to broadband revenue. Pole-attachment rate changes are flagged as potentially increasing annual costs significantly.

**Screening context.** An Altman Z-Score of 0.62 on FY2025 data sits in the distress zone, corroborated independently by unsecured paper at 53.8-65 cents. The score is depressed partly by items structural to cable (negative working capital, asset intensity) and partly by impairment-driven negative retained earnings — but D/E has risen from 2.9x at FY2025 to approximately 9.4x at 6/30/2026, so the screen is if anything stale in the bearish direction.

No options data was provided for this name, so no options-market read is included.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($85-110).** The asset generates approximately $117-310M of annual free cash flow depending on the interest and capex path, holds a 49.7% EBITDA margin, and faces no wired ≥100 Mbps competitor across more than 40% of its footprint. Management has the right skill mix for this moment: a CEO hired from Astound Broadband, an operator with rural overbuild experience, and a CFO who ran TMT leveraged finance at Truist. Capital allocation since the crisis began has been rational — dividend suspended (preserving roughly $67M a year), $403.4M of debt repaid in 2025, $79.4M of Senior Notes retired for $51.9M in H1 2026 (about 65 cents blended, 60 cents in Q2), $42.0M raised from the tower-contract sale, and the Clearwave Fiber exit removing a $124.5M annual equity-method drag. Underlying business data is declining only 2.5-3% once the divested tower revenue is removed. If maturities are termed out and ARPU stabilizes, 5.5x on flat combined EBITDA of $908M supports roughly $700M of equity, or about $123 per share. The unsecured paper at 53.8 cents is also an opportunity: every dollar of face retired at that price is accretive to the residual.

**Bear case ($0-8).** The 5.00x covenant step-back lands in late 2027 alongside a $920M MBI maturity that Cable One's own lenders declined to extend at 34% participation. Combined EBITDA declining 10-15% annually against approximately $4.29B of fixed net debt means the leverage ratio deteriorates even if the company pays down debt with all its free cash flow. The 53.8-cent price on the 2030 notes says the market expects unsecured principal impairment; in any such exchange, holders who take a haircut take equity, and $211.7M of existing common is the cheapest currency in the structure. Meanwhile the operating base is eroding on price as well as volume, mobile arrives too late to defend ARPU inside the refinancing window, and further franchise impairment is likely at the next measurement date given a 70% decline in the market-based input. Cable One is simultaneously required to spend approximately $480M acquiring an asset it marks at a fraction of the price, consuming most of its remaining revolver capacity, seven weeks before the fiscal quarter in which it must begin negotiating with its lenders.

**Why the bear case dominates.** The disagreement is not about whether the asset generates cash — it does — but about who owns the residual. Two facts settle the weighting. First, the 34.7-cent spread between first-lien term loans (88.5) and Senior Notes (53.8) is a market-derived statement that a substantial creditor class expects to be impaired, and impaired creditors are paid in equity. Second, the failed exchange offer is a completed experiment: on terms Cable One was willing to offer in June 2026, roughly two-thirds of the relevant lenders said no. Everything about the sequencing — put closing by October 1, 2026; covenant relief expiring roughly four quarters later; $920M due November 2027; $2B-plus due in 2028 — puts the negotiation on the creditors' clock.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Three dated items exist. The MBI put closes on or before October 1, 2026, at which point pro forma leverage becomes reported leverage and roughly $220M of revolver capacity is all that remains. The Q3 2026 report (late October/early November) is a probable further franchise impairment date given the collapse in the market-based input. The binding event is the covenant step-back to 5.00x roughly four quarters after closing — approximately Q4 2027 — which is more than a year out.

**Timing window.** The nearest catalysts are 7-12 weeks away but are ambiguous in direction: an early MBI close captures the 12% annualized discount and removes uncertainty, which the market could read positively. The decisive catalyst is 15+ months out, which is a long carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 18.7% of float short, 919,884 shares, 2.9 days to cover against 166,406 average daily volume. Total shares outstanding are 5.67 million, one of the smallest floats on the NYSE for a company of this enterprise value. The price record demonstrates the consequence: 6/26/2026 opened at $40.42 and closed at $52.87 (+30% in one session on 573,844 shares); 8/3/2026 gained approximately 12% intraday from the prior close; the stock has traded between the low $40s and high $50s repeatedly within three months. A restructuring headline framed as "agreement reached" would produce a large move against a short in a single session.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data was available for this name, so there is no confirmation from put skew or implied volatility. On a $211.7M market cap with 18.7% short interest and a 5.67M share count, borrow cost should be assumed to be materially above the sector norm, adding meaningful carry against a 15-month thesis.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $5 (-87%). Realistic squeeze or amend-and-extend upside to $75-90 (+101% to +141%), with the analyst high at $86. Weighting the scenarios above, expected return on a short is only modestly positive at roughly +13%, against a 55% combined probability of paths that produce $30-90 prints. Reward:risk on the tails is approximately 0.6:1 to 0.9:1, well below the 2:1 threshold, before borrow.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish analysis is right, and the position is still not worth taking. The decisive catalyst is too far out relative to carry, the near-term catalysts are two-sided, the float is small enough that a single financing headline can move the stock sharply in a session, and the equity is already trading as a low-priced option where most of the loss has occurred. Investors who own it should exit; investors who do not should stay away and express any cable-sector view elsewhere.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** Three conditions, any two of which would justify a fresh look:

1. **A completed, non-coercive maturity extension.** Specifically, term loans B-2/B-3/B-4 and the revolver extended beyond 2029 without warrants, equity issuance or structurally senior new money, and with the springing provision on B-2/B-3 removed. The measurable tell is the 4.00% Senior Notes trading above 80 cents; at 53.8 today the credit market says this has not happened.
2. **Operating stabilization on two axes.** Residential data ARPU flat or positive year over year for two consecutive quarters (versus -0.8% in Q2 2026) *and* residential data PSU decline improving to better than -4% annualized (versus -6.6%). Either alone is insufficient — the change in regime was that both turned negative together.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Composite score 2.15, down from 2.5, and the fourth consecutive AVOID. The downgrade is warranted by new information rather than repetition: the MBI option flipped from a $31.8M asset to a $426.0M liability, roughly $947.5M of additional impairment was taken across franchise, goodwill and the MBI stake ($597.7M plus $349.8M), residential data ARPU turned negative for the first time alongside a 6.6% unit decline, EBITDA margin compressed approximately 360bp, and a liability-management exercise failed at 34% participation, prompting management to disclose it is analyzing options including amending existing debt agreements.

The business generates cash and holds a defensible cost structure and a genuinely difficult-to-overbuild footprint across part of its territory. None of that reaches the common equity on a reliable path. Approximately $4.29B of pro forma net debt sits in front of $211.7M of market value, roughly $3.5B of obligations may cluster in a seven-month window in 2027-2028, the covenant relief expires just as the first major maturity arrives, and the unsecured tranches trade at 53.8 to 65 cents while first-lien paper holds near 88.5 — the market's own statement about which claims get paid. Probability-weighting the restructuring, amend-and-extend and clean-refinancing paths produces roughly $32.5 of value against a $37.32 close, with a 45% branch near zero. That combination — negative expected return with a large probability of near-total loss — is what AVOID is for. It is also, because of an 18.7% short position in a 5.67-million-share float that has produced repeated 30%-plus single-session rallies, not a short. The correct action is no position.
