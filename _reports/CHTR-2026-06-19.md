**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Charter Communications is the second-largest U.S. cable operator, marketing residential and commercial connectivity under the Spectrum brand across 41 states. The equity has lost roughly two-thirds of its value over the past twelve months, falling from a 52-week high of $422 to $126, with one ~25% one-day crash on April 24, 2026 following a Q1 broadband-subscriber miss and a guidance cut. The decline reflects the market repricing a structurally challenged, highly-levered franchise whose core profit engine, residential broadband, has entered net-subscriber decline as fixed-wireless access (FWA) and fiber overbuilders take share.

The defining financial feature is leverage: ~$94 billion of total debt against ~$16 billion of book equity, net-debt/EBITDA of 4.15x, and interest expense consuming roughly 40% of operating income. Adjusted EBITDA was flat year-over-year and declined 2.2% in Q1 2026. With equity representing a thin slice atop the debt stack, even modest EBITDA erosion produces outsized equity moves, which is why the stock crashes 25% on a quarterly subscriber print. The 3.4x trailing P/E that screens as "cheap" is a structural-impairment signal: the market is discounting a business whose forward earnings power is being eroded by competition and creeping refinancing costs.

We rate CHTR AVOID with an overall score of 3.8. The bull case rests on convergence (bundling mobile with broadband to defend retention), the Cox transaction adding scale, and tax tailwinds from the OBBBA. These are real but do not address the central problem: there is no visible catalyst to reverse residential broadband losses, and management is attempting to close two mega-deals while the core business bleeds. We do not view the stock as an active short candidate given the 44.3% short interest, meaningful squeeze risk, and post-crash insider buying.

## Company Overview

Charter operates as a holding company whose principal asset is a controlling interest in Charter Communications Holdings, LLC. It passes roughly 58-59 million homes and businesses and reports as a single "broadband connectivity" segment. Revenue composition for 2025:

- **Internet**: $23.8B (43% of revenue, +1.7%) — the profit engine
- **Mobile**: $3.8B (+22%) — the growth line, an MVNO on Verizon's network supplemented by owned CBRS spectrum
- **Video**: $13.7B (−9.4%) — secular decline
- **Voice**: $1.35B (−6%); **Commercial**: $7.3B; **Advertising**: $1.47B (−17.6% on lower political spend)

Approximately 89% of revenue is recurring monthly subscription. The competitive position rests on an upgraded fiber-coax hybrid network (the multi-year "network evolution" to symmetrical multi-gig), a clustered footprint, and a converged Internet+Mobile bundle strategy. The balance sheet is dominated by indefinite-lived intangibles: $67.5B of franchises (44% of assets) plus $29.7B of goodwill (19%), so roughly 63% of the asset base is intangible and supported only by annual impairment tests.

## Financial Analysis

**The top line is stagnant-to-declining.** Revenue moved from $55.1B (2024) to $54.8B (2025), −0.6%, with Q1 2026 down 1.0% YoY. The flat aggregate masks a damaging mix shift: high-margin video and voice subscribers are eroding while growth comes from lower-ARPU mobile. Connectivity revenue grew 4.1%, but the company-wide top line still shrank, so the mix is working against margins.

**EBITDA has turned the wrong direction.** Adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat at $22.7B (2025) vs. $22.6B (2024), but Q1 2026 declined 2.2% YoY ($5.64B vs $5.76B). For a business where the entire equity thesis depends on stable-to-growing EBITDA to service ~$94B of debt, a negative EBITDA inflection is the most important data point in the filing.

**Reported EPS is engineered, not earned.** Net income was ~$5.0B (2025) and diluted EPS of $36.21 looks robust, but it is substantially a function of share-count reduction: diluted shares fell to 137.7M (2025) from 145.4M (2024), and the basic count is down to ~125M by Q1 2026. The buybacks driving this were executed at ~$200-232 per share in Q1 2026, far above the current $126, which is value-destructive in hindsight. Only ~$179M of buyback authorization remained at quarter-end (ex-Liberty), so this lever is largely spent.

**Free cash flow is shrinking as capex rises.** FCF was ~$5.0B (company definition) / $4.4B (per scoring), but Q1 2026 FCF fell to $1.37B from $1.56B as capex rose to $2.9B from $2.4B. Capex is guided to ~$11.4B for 2026 (ex-Cox).

**Leverage is the defining feature.** Total debt of ~$94.3-94.8B, reported D/E of 9.6x, and equity of just ~$16B. The Altman Z-score of 0.49 sits in the "distress zone," though this is a structural artifact of capital-intensive, intangible-heavy cable economics rather than an imminent-bankruptcy signal. Liquidity is thin on cash ($517M) but supported by ~$4.6B revolver availability and an investment-grade-secured / high-yield-unsecured split rating.

**Refinancing creep is a slow drag.** January 2026 issuance ($1.75B at 7.0% due '33; $1.25B at 7.375% due '36) refinances older 4-5% paper at 7%+, giving interest expense a structural upward bias even as total debt stays roughly flat. About 13% of debt is floating, adding rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is broken at the level that matters. Residential Internet customers fell 393K in 2025 and another 455K YoY by Q1 2026, with 120K lost in Q1 alone — worse than estimates and the trigger for the April crash. Residential monthly revenue per customer slipped to $118.44 (Q1 2026) from $120.07, reflecting the low-promotional-price bundle strategy and unfavorable bundle revenue allocation.

Mobile is the only genuine growth line (+22% revenue, +1.9M lines in 2025, +368K in Q1 2026), but it carries far lower margins and higher device and working-capital costs than the eroding base. Growth here is dilutive to blended economics rather than accretive. Management's framing — trading ARPU for lifetime value and retention through convergence — is coherent strategically, but it has not yet stabilized subscriber counts, and the financial output (negative EBITDA inflection, falling FCF) is the relevant scorecard.

Estimate revisions tell a deteriorating story. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates fell 6.2% (to $42.08), next-year fell 10.7% (to $45.74), and current-quarter fell 5.2%. The 30-day figures are marginally positive (+0.3% to +0.9%), but this is noise against the 90-day decline, not a reversal. The earnings track record reinforces deteriorating visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including −11.8% in Q1 2026 and −12.4% in Q3 2025.

The mechanism behind the decline is straightforward and confirmed in both the 10-K and trade press. Fixed wireless (T-Mobile and Verizon 5G home Internet) offers simple, aggressive pricing and is the primary driver of cable's industry-wide net-add reversal. Fiber overbuild (AT&T, Frontier) pressures both subscriber counts and margins within Charter's footprint. LEO satellite (Starlink) is a longer-term rural/edge threat. None of these is cyclical; they are structural entrants into a market cable previously dominated.

## Valuation Assessment

CHTR trades at 3.4x trailing earnings and ~3.0x forward earnings (current-year EPS of $42.08 against $126). Against a Communication Services peer median P/E of 31.8, this looks extraordinarily cheap. The discount is justified, and the low multiple is a warning rather than an opportunity.

Three reasons the cheapness is a trap rather than a mispricing. First, the reported EPS is structurally inflated by buybacks executed above current prices; the run-rate earnings power is lower than the $36-42 figures suggest once the financial engineering is normalized. Second, the equity is a thin, levered slice — ~$16B of book equity atop ~$94B of debt — so the appropriate valuation lens is enterprise value, where Charter trades at roughly 5x EV/EBITDA on EBITDA that is now declining. A 5x multiple on a shrinking EBITDA base is not obviously cheap; it is the market pricing forward erosion. Third, the trailing period does not represent trough earnings; it represents a peak that is rolling over, the inverse of the setup that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple.

The cycle-adjustment principle that rewards high trailing multiples on troughing earnings cuts the other way here: estimates are falling, not rising, and the business is decelerating. Analyst targets span $124 to $413 with a $215 median, but that dispersion reflects deep disagreement about whether broadband stabilizes, not a confident floor. The current price sits essentially at the analyst low, which tells you the most bearish analysts are now closest to correct.

We assign a 5.0 to valuation — neutral rather than low. The score is not lower because at $126 a great deal of bad news is priced, and a genuine stabilization in broadband net-adds could produce a sharp re-rating off a depressed base. But there is no identified catalyst for that stabilization, and absent one, the multiple is cheap for cause.

## Competitive Landscape

Charter's moat has compressed materially. For two decades cable enjoyed a near-duopoly in residential broadband within its footprint, with telco DSL as the only meaningful competitor. That structure has broken on two fronts simultaneously. Fixed wireless converts excess 5G mobile capacity into a home-broadband substitute at simple, low prices, and it is winning the marginal subscriber. Fiber overbuilders are extending FTTH into Charter's territory, offering symmetrical speeds that pressure both share and pricing.

Charter's defensive bet is convergence: bundle Verizon-network mobile (plus CBRS offload) with owned-network broadband at persistently low prices, trading ARPU for retention and lifetime value. The Cox transaction adds scale and clustered density to help defend against overbuilders. The strategy is rational, but the trajectory of competitive position is negative — share is eroding, ARPU is declining, and the company is reacting to entrants rather than setting terms. Trajectory matters more than level for this dimension, and Charter's is deteriorating, warranting a 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Broadband subscriber erosion** is the central thesis-breaker, and it is actively occurring rather than hypothetical. Continued FWA and fiber share-taking compounds directly into EBITDA, which compounds into the levered equity.

**Leverage and refinancing risk.** Rising coupons, covenant constraints at CCO Holdings (restrictions on dividends, distributions, and investments), and cross-default exposure. The Cox deal adds ~$12.4B of assumed net debt plus ~$4.2B of cash funded by new debt.

**M&A execution and integration risk.** Charter is attempting to close the Cox transactions (a $34.5B headline deal: $3.5B cash for the commercial fiber/managed-IT business; $650M cash + $6.0B of 6.875% convertible preferred units + ~33.6M common units for the residential cable business; ~$12.4B assumed net debt) and absorb Liberty Broadband (all-stock, outside date Aug 31, 2027) while the core business is in crisis. The Cox deal dilutes via the preferred and common units, hands Cox Enterprises ~25% of the combined entity and the chairmanship (Alex Taylor, 3-year term), and demands management attention precisely when the operating business needs it.

**Intangible impairment risk.** With $97B+ of franchises plus goodwill and a franchise fair-value cushion of only ">10%" at the last test (Oct 31, 2025), sustained competitive pressure on future cash flows could force impairments.

**Programming and video economics.** Programming (~$8.8B) is the largest cost, and programmers shifting content to their own streaming apps create lower-priced substitutes that net hundreds of millions out of video revenue.

**Regulatory.** Universal Service Fund litigation, RDOF build-out compliance, potential broadband reclassification, and an evolving FCC/SpaceX spectrum posture.

These risks are largely priced into a stock down roughly two-thirds over the past twelve months, but they are not fully resolved, and several (subscriber erosion, EBITDA inflection, refinancing creep) are still deteriorating rather than stabilizing. The combination of an active negative trajectory and a leveraged equity warrants a low risk score of 3.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.3% sits essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 52.4% (1.0% spread), so the options market is not pricing acute near-term stress beyond what has already realized. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (−4.2%), and the near-term put/call volume ratio is low at 0.45, suggesting traders are not aggressively positioning for further immediate downside, consistent with a stock that has already crashed and where some see a bounce. The term structure is in contango (long-term IV 64.5% vs near-term 53.3%) and the 3-month tenor shows a steep IV jump to 73.0% with a 2.18 put/call volume ratio, indicating the market expects elevated uncertainty around the next earnings print and deal milestones. The prior-snapshot ATM IV reading of 439.8% was a corrupted thin-options artifact and is non-informative. Net read: the options market is complacent near-term but pricing real event risk three months out.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($175-215)
Broadband net losses stabilize as the convergence bundle gains traction and the network-evolution upgrade closes the speed gap against fiber. Mobile continues to scale, eventually reaching margin parity. The Cox transaction adds clustered density and synergies that defend against overbuilders. OBBBA tax tailwinds (100% bonus depreciation, EBITDA-based §163(j); ~$669M FCF benefit in 2025) sustain free cash flow. On a stabilized FCF base and a modest EV/EBITDA re-rating, the levered equity recovers sharply off a depressed base. Post-crash insider buying (CEO Winfrey ~$1.2M, three directors including Ramos ~$1.4M, totaling ~$4M+ on the open market) signals management conviction that the stock is oversold.

### Bear Case ($90-110)
FWA and fiber continue taking share, broadband losses accelerate, and ARPU keeps declining. EBITDA erosion compounds against the debt stack, with refinancing creep adding interest drag. The Cox and Liberty integrations distract management and add leverage during the crisis. EBITDA falls a few points, the EV/EBITDA multiple compresses further on a smaller base, and the thin equity slice absorbs a disproportionate share of the value destruction. Buyback support is exhausted, removing the EPS-engineering crutch. With ~63% of assets intangible and a >10% franchise cushion, a sustained cash-flow deterioration risks an impairment that crystallizes the structural decline.

The probability balance favors the bear-to-base path. The subscriber and EBITDA trends are negative and the catalyst that would reverse them — broadband stabilization — is not yet visible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID. Timeline: 12-18 months of persistent competitive headwinds.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. **Broadband net-add stabilization** — two consecutive quarters where residential Internet losses narrow materially or turn flat. This is the single most important signal; it would confirm convergence is working and de-risk the EBITDA base.
2. **EBITDA inflection** — a return to flat-or-positive YoY Adjusted EBITDA growth, reversing the Q1 2026 −2.2% print.
3. **Clean Cox/Liberty integration path** — deals closing on terms without leverage covenant stress, and evidence the integrations are not distracting from the core operational turnaround.
4. **Estimate-revision reversal** — 90-day EPS revisions turning positive, reversing the current −6% to −11% trend.

**Why not own it now:** The business is decelerating, the equity is leveraged to that deceleration, the buyback support is spent, and there is no dated catalyst to reverse the broadband bleed. The cheapness is a function of structural impairment, not mispricing.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The clearest bearish driver is the next quarterly broadband-subscriber print (Q2 2026, likely late July), where continued net losses worse than the ~120K Q1 pace would extend the derating. The Cox/Liberty deal milestones (regulatory approvals, dilution mechanics) are slow-moving and not sharply dated. The structural EBITDA-erosion thesis lacks a single dated event that forces a step-change lower.

**Timing window**: The Q2 print is roughly six weeks out. Beyond that, the thesis is a slow grind on EBITDA and refinancing creep over 12-18 months — a long carry for a structural short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 44.3% of float (21.4M shares), with 6.3 days to cover. This is dangerously crowded. A stabilization surprise, a deal development, or a broad market rally could trigger a violent squeeze, and the post-crash insider buying ($4M+ from the CEO and three directors) is the kind of signal that fuels short-covering rallies.

**Borrow and options signal**: The options market is not confirming acute near-term bearishness — near-term put/call volume is low at 0.45 and skew is call-heavy (−4.2%). The 3-month tenor shows elevated event-risk pricing (73% IV, 2.18 put/call), but the near-term complacency and call-heavy positioning are warning signs for a short. High short interest implies elevated borrow cost, adding to carry.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$90-110 (roughly −13% to −29% from $126). Upside-squeeze risk on any positive surprise could easily be +30-40% given the short interest. The reward:risk is worse than 1:1 on a squeeze-adjusted basis; the asymmetry favors the bears being squeezed, not the stock falling further in an orderly fashion.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT** — The fundamental thesis (structural broadband decline, levered equity) is correct, but short interest at 44.3% with meaningful insider buying and call-heavy near-term options positioning makes the remaining downside small relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Conclusion

Charter is a cash-generative but structurally challenged business whose equity sits as a thin, volatile slice atop ~$94B of debt. The April crash repriced a franchise that has lost its broadband moat to fixed-wireless and fiber entrants. The 3.4x trailing P/E is a value-trap signal driven by buyback-engineered EPS; on an enterprise basis (~5x EV/EBITDA on falling EBITDA) the stock is cheap for cause. Management is attempting two mega-deals during an operational crisis, the buyback cushion is largely spent, and 90-day estimate revisions are running −6% to −11%.

We rate CHTR **AVOID** with an overall score of **3.8**, consistent with the declining score trajectory (6.2 → 4.3 over four months) and the three prior AVOID calls. The further price decline since the last report does not change the recommendation: the fundamentals (subscriber losses, negative EBITDA inflection, refinancing creep) have continued to deteriorate alongside the price, so this is a confirmed thesis rather than a price move detached from fundamentals. We do not recommend an active short given the squeeze risk and post-crash insider conviction buying. Re-entry requires concrete evidence of broadband stabilization, which is not yet visible.
