## Executive Summary

Chubb reported record net income of $10.3 billion in 2025, driven by a 85.7% P&C combined ratio and $6.5 billion in net investment income. The company's Q1 2026 results showed core operating EPS of $6.82, continuing the pattern of disciplined underwriting delivery.

At $326/share and approximately 12.0x FY2026 forward earnings, Chubb trades at a premium to P&C peers but below its own historical average. The premium is defensible: Chubb operates in 54 countries with unmatched product breadth, generates $12.8B in operating cash flow, and has an investment income tailwind as its $168.8B portfolio rolls into higher new-money yields. The declining score trajectory in prior analyses reflects real headwinds — long-tail casualty reserve uncertainty from social inflation, Bermuda's new 15% CIT reducing after-tax margins, and a 71-year-old CEO with no disclosed formal succession plan — but none of these individually or collectively impair the core earnings power of the franchise.

The current risk environment (elevated geopolitical vol, rising catastrophe frequency, hardening casualty rates) is structurally favorable for large, well-capitalized incumbents like Chubb that can absorb volatility and reprice risk, while competitors face capital constraints.

## Company Overview

Chubb Limited is among the world's largest publicly traded P&C insurers by market capitalization (~$127B). Swiss-incorporated with operations across 54 countries, the company underwrites commercial P&C (38% of net premiums), overseas general insurance (27%), life insurance (14%), personal P&C (13%), agricultural insurance (5%), and global reinsurance (3%). Total assets stand at $272 billion with shareholders' equity of $73.8 billion.

The company's competitive moat rests on three pillars: (1) geographic and product diversification unmatched by any pure-play peer, (2) two decades of disciplined underwriting culture under CEO Evan Greenberg that consistently prioritizes combined ratio over premium volume, and (3) a $168.8B investment portfolio (83% investment-grade, average rating A/A) generating an ever-growing income floor as legacy low-coupon bonds mature into higher rates.

## Competitive Landscape

Chubb's competitive position is stable-to-improving across most segments:

- **Commercial P&C:** Rate adequacy in casualty lines (+5.7% growth with rate above loss trend). Large, well-capitalized incumbents gain share during hardening cycles as smaller competitors cannot absorb volatility.
- **Personal Lines:** The high-net-worth niche has fewer competitors and higher barriers (specialized claims handling, broad coverage forms). 19.6% auto growth reflects repricing power.
- **International:** 54-country admitted-basis licensing creates a regulatory moat that takes decades and billions to replicate. Huatai (87.2% ownership) provides unique China access.
- **Reinsurance:** Chubb Tempest Re is small (3% of premiums) but strategically important for retaining risk and managing volatility.

**Threats:** Insurtech in small commercial (not Chubb's core market), alternative capital in cat reinsurance (Chubb reduced reinsurance exposure), and potential regulatory restriction of Western ownership in China. None represent near-term material impairment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Long-Tail Casualty Reserve Uncertainty (Moderate-High):**
76% of Chubb's $69.7B net reserves are casualty-related. Social inflation — nuclear verdicts, litigation funding, aggressive plaintiff strategies — has driven adverse development in commercial excess, umbrella, and auto liability. Workers' comp: 1% tail factor change = $1.1B impact. This is an industry-wide problem, and Chubb's reserve position appears adequate given consistent favorable development overall ($1.13B in 2025), but the casualty-specific deterioration warrants monitoring.

**2. Catastrophe Risk (Demonstrated):**
2025 included $1.47B in California wildfire losses. The 1-in-100 year aggregate PML is $5.86B (7.9% of equity). Climate-driven frequency increases are structural. Chubb is actively reducing California wildfire exposure and repricing personal lines, but elevated catastrophe years will periodically compress earnings.

**3. Bermuda CIT & Tax Headwinds (Structural):**
ETR increased from 15.8% to 18.6% due to Bermuda's new 15% CIT. OECD minimum tax guidance may add further pressure after 2026. The exact steady-state impact remains uncertain as ETR also depends on jurisdictional earnings mix and discrete items; Q1 2026 ETR was 21.6% vs. 19.3% in Q1 2025, suggesting ongoing variability.

**4. CEO Succession (Long-term):**
Evan Greenberg (71) has led Chubb for 21 years. Internal bench development is visible (Ortega 51, McNamee 50, Johns 50), and the promotion of younger executives in July 2024 suggests active development of next-generation leadership. However, no formal succession announcement has been made. The stock likely trades down 5-10% on any retirement announcement given Greenberg's identification with underwriting discipline.

**5. China/Geopolitical Risk:**
87.2% Huatai ownership exposes Chubb to Chinese regulatory, political, and currency risk. $8.5B in Chinese corporate bonds. This is a real risk that could crystallize through regulatory restriction or currency controls.

## Conclusion

Chubb remains a BUY at $326, though conviction is lower than at $283 (May 2025) or $313 (February 2026). The investment case rests on durable mid-single-digit premium growth, structural investment income accretion, consistent capital return, and a combined ratio that demonstrates genuine underwriting skill rather than pricing-cycle luck. At approximately 12.0x forward earnings, the valuation is full relative to P&C peers but not stretched relative to Chubb's own history or quality.

The declining score trajectory observed in prior reports is accurate: the Bermuda tax headwind, casualty reserve deterioration at the industry level, and approaching CEO succession all incrementally reduce the margin of safety. These factors argue for moderate position sizing (2-3%) rather than maximum conviction. Elevated geopolitical volatility, rising catastrophe frequency, and credit stress in other financial subsectors (private credit, CRE) are structurally favorable for well-capitalized insurers who can absorb shocks and reprice business — Chubb is the canonical beneficiary of this dynamic.
