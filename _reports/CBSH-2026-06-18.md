**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commerce Bancshares is among the highest-quality franchises in U.S. regional banking: a Kansas City-based, Kemper-family-stewarded super-regional with $35.7 billion in assets, a fee-heavy revenue mix (non-interest income ~37% of total revenue), and a fortress balance sheet (CET1 17.09%, loans/deposits ~73%, core deposits 91.4% of total). Q1 2026 produced $141.6M net income ($0.96 diluted EPS, +7.6% YoY), an ROA of 1.62%, and net interest income up 11.4% YoY, the last lifted by the January FineMark acquisition. The credit-rating stack (S&P A-/A, Moody's A3/a2) and absence of long-term debt place it in the flight-to-quality tier of the group.

The investment question is whether the price offers asymmetric upside, not whether the franchise is good. At $54.72 the stock trades at roughly 13.2x current-year consensus EPS ($4.16) and 12.5x next-year ($4.38), essentially in line with the regional-bank peer median P/E of 13.0, while its 2.0% dividend yield sits below the peer median of 3.1%. Earnings growth is structurally modest: net income compounded from $541M (2021) to $571M (2025), roughly 1.4% annually, and estimate revisions, while positive, are small (+2.7% current-year over 90 days, but next-year down 1.0%). This is a steady compounder priced as one. The near-term tailwinds (a ~$99M Visa Class B gain funding a securities repositioning into higher-yielding bonds, and FineMark scaling the Wealth segment) are real but incremental.

We rate CBSH a HOLD. The franchise quality and capital return (a ~5% dividend raise, a buyback authorization lifted to 7.5M shares) make it a sensible defensive position, and the balance sheet limits downside in a stressed regional-bank tape. The absence of a mispricing catalyst, a low single-digit earnings growth trajectory, and the negative selection record for regional-bank longs argue against an upgrade. The prior trajectory (6.2 in March, 7.0 in May) reflected appropriate recognition of balance-sheet and management strength; we trim modestly on a more sober growth and valuation read, with no fundamental deterioration implied.

## Company Overview

Commerce Bank operates ~150 retail branches concentrated in the central U.S. (Missouri, Kansas, Illinois, Oklahoma, Colorado), now extended into Florida, Arizona, and South Carolina via FineMark. The company reports three segments:

- **Commercial** ($84.0M Q1'26 pre-tax): corporate lending, leasing, international, treasury/cash management, merchant and corporate card, and the Commercial Tradable Products fixed-income/municipal underwriting unit.
- **Wealth** ($63.2M Q1'26 pre-tax, fastest-growing): trust, estate planning, discretionary investment management, brokerage, private banking. Trust fees ran $71M in the quarter, up 25.5% YoY.
- **Retail Banking** ($49.5M Q1'26 pre-tax): consumer deposits, mortgage, indirect/auto, debit and credit card.

The distinguishing feature is the fee weighting. Non-interest income near 37% of revenue (trust fees, corporate-card fees ~$26M/quarter, deposit and cash-management fees) is well above the regional-bank norm and reduces reliance on spread income. Combined with a conservative underwriting culture, this produces best-in-class credit metrics and capital. The FineMark deal signals where management is steering: it allocated 100% of the deal's goodwill to the Wealth segment, confirming a deliberate scaling of fee-based private banking into Sun Belt high-net-worth markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** Annualized ROE was 13.22%, strong in absolute terms but down from 15.82% a year earlier, mechanically diluted by the equity issued for FineMark (9.9M shares) and a larger equity base. The efficiency ratio rose to 60.0% (from 55.6%) on FineMark onboarding and ~$14M of one-time acquisition costs, so the underlying run-rate efficiency is better than the headline.

**Net interest income / margin.** The FTE net yield on earning assets improved to 3.59% (from 3.56%). FineMark added ~$2.7B of average loans and $6.9M of purchase-accounting accretion. Deposit costs declined on a rate basis (1.61% cost of interest-bearing deposits vs. 1.72%), reflecting the strength of the core-deposit franchise as the rate cycle turns.

**Balance sheet and funding.** Loans were $20.5B against $28.4B of deposits, an exceptionally liquid, deposit-rich balance sheet. Non-interest-bearing deposits are 28% of total, a meaningful funding advantage. Liquid assets totaled $12.8B plus $6.9B of untapped FHLB/Fed capacity.

**Capital.** Total risk-based capital is 17.90%, Tier 1 leverage 12.60%, and tangible common equity/tangible assets 11.07%, all well above well-capitalized thresholds and at the top of the peer set.

**The AOCI overhang.** The AFS securities book carries a $711M gross pre-tax unrealized loss, with AOCI at –$539.6M, concentrated in agency MBS (–$579M) purchased at low rates. This is rate-driven, not credit, and unrealized; the bank has the liquidity to hold to maturity, but it depresses tangible book value. Management is actively addressing it (see Visa repositioning below). The earnings simulation shows mild asset sensitivity (+200bps → +2.63% NII; –200bps → –2.45% NII), reduced post-FineMark by acquired rate-sensitive deposits.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was $684.8M. The dividend is $0.275/quarter (+5% YoY), the company repurchased 1.6M shares in Q1'26 at an average $51.56 (below the current price), and the buyback authorization was raised to 7.5M shares on 4/24/2026. An annual 5% stock dividend is also paid.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the weakest element of the case, and the reason this is not a higher score. Net income moved from $541M (2021) to $500M (2022) to $485M (2023) to $534M (2024) to $571M (2025), steady but barely compounding. Consensus EPS is $4.16 for the current year and $4.38 for next year, roughly 5% growth, and estimate revisions are modest: +2.7% for the current year over 90 days but –1.0% for next year. This does not approach the inflection signature (revenue acceleration >40% with rising margins, or estimate revisions >30% over 90 days) that would warrant treating the stock on normalized rather than trailing earnings. CBSH is correctly evaluated on its current/forward multiple.

The genuine growth levers are incremental. First, the FineMark wealth bet: $8.7B of added assets under administration and the trust-fee gain show the Wealth segment compounding faster than the bank overall, and the deal is structured as a fee-business expansion rather than a balance-sheet play (management immediately moved $1.0B of FineMark's high-cost money-market deposits off-balance-sheet to protect its funding-cost advantage). Second, the securities repositioning (below) lifts forward NII. Third, as the rate cycle turns, a deposit-rich bank with falling deposit costs gets margin relief without taking credit risk. None of these are large enough individually to re-rate the stock; collectively they support mid-single-digit EPS growth.

The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in consensus: two beats, one in-line, one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 7.1% (and ~16.5% above the headline consensus per news reports). Visibility is decent; the ceiling is low.

## Valuation Assessment

The regional-bank peer median P/E is 13.0 (VLY 13.0, PB 13.0, WAL 9.6, COLB 12.2, CFR 14.2). On earnings multiple, CBSH sits at a slight premium to the median, which its superior capital, fee mix, and credit quality justify, but there is no discount to exploit. On yield, the dividend trails the peer median materially; investors are not being paid a yield premium for the quality.

The bull would point to the Visa gain and securities repositioning as a mispriced catalyst. The mechanics are attractive: CBSH tendered 411,723 Visa Class B-2 shares carried at $0 cost basis, generating an estimated ~$99M pre-tax gain, and is using it to absorb an ~$95M pre-tax loss on the sale of ~$911M of ~2.5%-yielding AFS securities, reinvesting into ~4% paper. This is a well-executed, roughly capital-neutral maneuver that lifts forward NII and partially addresses the AOCI drag. The NII uplift on $911M of a $35.7B balance sheet (a ~150bp yield pickup, roughly $13-14M annualized pre-tax) is modest relative to a ~$571M net income base, supportive of the steady-compounder thesis rather than a re-rating catalyst.

A low or peer-line multiple is not, by itself, a reason to expect outperformance; cheapness without a catalyst tends to persist. CBSH is fairly valued for what it is, a defensive, high-quality bank with low single-digit growth. We score valuation at 5.5: no clear mispricing, no compelling discount, and a below-peer yield offsetting the quality premium. Analyst targets bracket the case: low $53 (essentially current price), median $57.75, mean $58.69, high $69. The consensus mean implies ~7% upside, consistent with a fairly-priced compounder rather than an underpriced one.

## Competitive Landscape

CBSH's positioning is deliberately counter-cyclical: a low loan/deposit ratio, fortress capital, heavy fee income, and a sticky low-cost deposit base. In the post-2023 regional-banking-stress environment, this profile is a differentiator: an A-tier-rated regional with minimal wholesale-funding reliance and trivial non-performing assets (0.03% of assets). The Wealth segment is the area of clearest competitive momentum, with FineMark extending the franchise into faster-growing Sun Belt HNW markets.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than sharply gaining share. The principal qualification is relative performance: five-year cumulative TSR of 111.04 versus a peer group at 152.69 shows that the conservatism that protects the franchise also cost relative returns in a cycle that rewarded riskier regionals. The competitive position is sound and the moat (low-cost deposits, fee diversification, brand in core markets) is durable, but the structural ceiling on growth caps how much the moat translates into shareholder return. We score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are second-order rather than existential, consistent with the fortress balance sheet, but several deserve monitoring:

1. **FineMark integration.** At ~12% of assets, FineMark's internal controls are excluded from management's ICFR assessment for up to a year, and the 10-Q flags deposit/customer/employee attrition and cost-savings shortfall risk. The $1.0B off-balance-sheet move of FineMark's high-cost deposits suggests its funding base was more rate-sensitive than Commerce's; disciplined, but a sign integration carries friction.

2. **Credit normalization.** Potential problem (substandard) loans rose 11.8% QoQ to $296.2M, and consumer credit-card net charge-offs annualized 5.21% (up from 4.55% prior quarter). Office CRE ($611M, 3.0% of loans, with $51.7M substandard) and multi-family construction ($529.5M) are the areas where cracks would appear first. Total NCOs remain low at 0.30% and NPAs trivial at 0.03%, but the trend is the watch item, particularly against a backdrop of national CRE/multifamily stress.

3. **AOCI / rate risk.** The unrealized AFS loss on a 4.2-year-duration portfolio depresses tangible book; the repositioning addresses part of it but the residual mark moves with rates.

4. **Macro overhang.** A higher-for-longer rate path, a possible H2 consumer slowdown, and broad private-credit stress are sector-level headwinds. They are largely priced for a name with this capital profile, but they cap upside.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options surface shows backwardation: 28-day ATM IV of 50.8% against one-year historical volatility of 22.4% and three-/six-month IV of ~21-25%. Taken at face value this would imply the market is pricing imminent stress. We treat it with caution: the put/call open-interest ratio is just 0.02 (overwhelmingly call-side, not a bearish signal), the cross-snapshot IV reading (a jump to "1323%") is a corrupted thin-options artifact, and the most plausible explanation for elevated near-term vol is positioning into the July Q2 earnings release, which will book the Visa gain and securities repositioning. We do not read the options surface as a credible bearish tell, but it does confirm the next print is an event. Short interest is unremarkable at 4.3% of float (5.5 days to cover). Net, the options data neither confirms nor materially challenges the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$62-69)

CBSH compounds tangible book and EPS in the mid-single digits while the rate cycle turning lower relieves deposit costs and the securities repositioning lifts NII. The Wealth segment, supercharged by FineMark, drives fee growth above the bank average and earns a higher multiple for the mix shift. Capital return (buybacks below intrinsic value) compounds per-share metrics. In a regional-bank flight to quality, the A-tier balance sheet attracts a scarcity premium and the stock re-rates toward 15-16x on $4.38+ EPS, reaching the analyst-high $69. Probability: lower than the base case given the structural growth ceiling.

### Bear Case (~$47-50)

Credit normalization accelerates: office CRE and the rising substandard book convert to charge-offs, consumer card losses keep climbing past 5%, and reserves build. FineMark integration disappoints on cost or deposit retention. The macro consumer cliff and CRE stress weigh on the central-U.S. and newly-entered Sun Belt markets. The stock de-rates toward the 52-week low ($46.99), roughly 10x next-year EPS, a ~14% decline from current levels. The fortress capital and trivial NPAs make this scenario shallow, not catastrophic.

The base case sits between: a steady compounder at a fair multiple, modestly supported by the Visa/NII tailwind and wealth-fee growth, returning capital while the franchise quality limits downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, classified as an income/quality hold: the durable reasons to own are the dividend (2.0% yield, +5% raise), steady cash generation, ongoing buybacks, and a defensive franchise, not a growth re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A genuine discount opens: forward P/E compressing below ~11x (roughly $48 on $4.38 next-year EPS) with fundamentals intact, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Wealth-segment fee growth durably accelerates (trust fees sustaining >20% YoY post-FineMark) and consensus next-year EPS revisions turn positive and exceed +5% over 90 days, signaling a faster compounding rate than currently priced.
- Clean evidence the securities repositioning and rate relief lift the FTE net yield above ~3.75% without credit deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Substandard loans continuing to rise (next reading materially above $296M) combined with total NCOs breaking above ~0.60% of loans, or a step-up in office-CRE classified loans, signaling genuine credit deterioration rather than normalization.
- FineMark integration problems surfacing: deposit attrition, a goodwill impairment indication, or guidance acknowledging cost-savings shortfalls.
- A dividend or buyback pullback signaling capital stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the July 2026 Q2 earnings release, which will book the Visa gain and securities repositioning and provide the first full quarter of FineMark in the Wealth segment, plus updated credit trends.

## Conclusion

Commerce Bancshares is a textbook quality-compounder bank: fortress capital, a deposit-rich and fee-heavy model, conservative underwriting, and aligned, low-cost family stewardship reinforced by a director's open-market purchase and buybacks executed below the current price. These attributes make the score's strength reside in financial health and a credible, well-aligned management team. The constraint is equally clear: low single-digit earnings growth, a multiple in line with peers, a below-peer dividend yield, and the absence of a catalyst large enough to re-rate the stock. The Visa monetization and securities repositioning are well-executed and accretive at the margin, but modest against the earnings base.

We rate CBSH a HOLD with an overall score of 6.15, trimming modestly from the prior 7.0 on a more sober growth and valuation read rather than any fundamental deterioration; the ~9% price decline over the past year on improving capital and stable credit is better risk/reward, but not yet an entry point. Own it for the defensive franchise and capital return; do not expect it to lead in a rising market, and do not pay up for it.
