**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Palomar Holdings is a specialty property & casualty insurer that has compounded gross written premiums from $16.6M at inception in 2014 to $2.0B in FY2025, a roughly 55% annualized rate, while sustaining elite returns on equity. The stock has fallen 28% from its 52-week high of $162.65 to $114.42, with most of the decline occurring after the May 6 Q1 print despite a 5% adjusted EPS beat and a reaffirmed FY2026 guidance. The market sold the headline that GAAP net income was flat year-over-year while net earned premiums grew 59%, reading the rising loss ratio as the onset of structural margin compression.

My analysis concludes the market has the mechanism wrong, but the debate is real and the burden of proof at a 28% drawdown sits with the bull case. GAAP net income was flat because of identifiable one-time and non-cash growth costs ($7.4M of Gray Surety deal fees, a $5.4M step-up in intangible amortization, a $4.1M increase in stock-based comp, and $3.2M of new interest expense) combined with a deliberate decision to retain a higher share of Crop and Casualty premium and the associated attritional losses. Adjusted net income grew 23% to $63.1M, adjusted EPS rose 24% to $2.31, and annualized adjusted ROE held at 26.6% versus 27.0%. The loss-ratio increase from 23.6% to 33.3% reflects mix migration away from ultra-low-attrition earthquake toward higher-loss-ratio but still-profitable specialty lines, not deterioration within any line.

At $114.42, the stock trades at roughly 11.6x the current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $9.88 and 10.2x the $11.18 FY27 estimate, against a five-quarter history where the multiple sat in the high teens to low twenties. Estimate revisions are positive across current-quarter (+4.5% over 90 days) and current-year (+3.2%), the company has beaten four straight quarters, and the analyst target range of $132-$168 sits 15-47% above the current price. The asymmetry favors a BUY. The score sits at 6.8, above my BUY threshold, and the concrete reason I am not defaulting to HOLD is that the specific concern the market is pricing, structural margin erosion, is contradicted by the line-item bridge, the maintained adjusted ROE, and the positive estimate revisions occurring concurrently with the price decline.

## Company Overview

Palomar underwrites specialty P&C risk across five product lines, recategorized in 2026 to reflect its diversification strategy. Q1 2026 gross written premium of $629.8M split as follows: Casualty $206.3M (32.8% of GWP, +55% YoY), Inland Marine & Property $166.6M (26.4%, +47%), Earthquake $137.3M (21.8%, +2.7%), Crop $87.8M (13.9%, +82%), and Surety & Credit $31.9M (5.1%, +131%).

The defining transformation is the migration away from earthquake concentration. Earthquake, historically Palomar's identity, has fallen from roughly 30% to 22% of GWP and grows in the low single digits, while Casualty, Crop, and Surety drive growth. This is deliberate diversification away from peak-zone catastrophe risk, trading the ultra-low loss ratios of earthquake for the higher attritional loss ratios (but lower tail volatility) of specialty casualty and crop lines.

Distribution runs through retail agents, wholesale brokers, program administrators, and fronting partnerships. The underwriting entities are PSIC (admitted, 51% of GWP), PESIC (E&S, 43%), Laulima (a consolidated Hawaii reciprocal exchange), and the surety carriers FIA and PCSC. A.M. Best rates the main carriers "A" (Excellent), with PCSC rated "A-". The competitive edge rests on proprietary data analytics, a modern technology platform, granular pricing, and specialty underwriting expertise rather than balance-sheet scale.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue roughly tripled from approximately $327M in 2022 to $876M in 2025, with net income up about 4x. The FY model produces ROE around 21% on a GAAP basis and roughly 27% adjusted, net margin around 22%, and FCF margin near 47%.

Q1 2026 showed the central tension. GWP rose 42.4% to $629.8M and net earned premiums rose 59.3% to $261.4M, yet GAAP net income was essentially flat at $42.9M with diluted EPS of $1.57. Adjusted net income grew 23.1% to $63.1M and adjusted diluted EPS rose 24% to $2.31, beating consensus by roughly 3% ($2.31 vs $2.24 estimate). Net investment income grew 49% to $18.0M at a 4.89% book yield and 4.21-year duration on an A1/A+ rated portfolio.

The bridge from 59% NEP growth to flat GAAP earnings is the analysis that matters:

- **Loss ratio rose to 33.3% from 23.6%.** Two drivers: a deliberate decision to retain a higher percentage of Crop premium (and its losses) in 2026, and higher attritional losses as the mix shifts toward Casualty/Crop/IM&P and away from low-attrition earthquake. Catastrophe losses were negligible at a 0.1% cat loss ratio.
- **Below-the-line growth costs:** $7.4M Gray Surety transaction costs, $6.1M intangible amortization (up from $0.7M), $8.8M stock-based comp (up from $4.7M), and $3.2M interest expense on the new term loan (up from near zero).
- **Favorable prior-year reserve development of $10.3M** in Inland Marine & Property provided a tailwind, indicating reserves remain conservative.

The GAAP combined ratio was 84.5%; the adjusted combined ratio was 76.0%, a healthy underwriting profit but a roughly 750bp deterioration from 68.5% a year earlier. This is the margin story analysts are tracking. The combination of a maintained 26.6% adjusted ROE, a 76% adjusted combined ratio still well within underwriting-profit territory, and favorable prior-year development is inconsistent with the "structural margin collapse" thesis the price action implies. A company experiencing genuine reserve deterioration does not post favorable development; one experiencing genuine margin erosion does not hold ROE at 27%.

On the balance sheet, total assets reached $3.6B, investments $1.56B, equity $959M. Tangible equity fell to $712.9M from $881.6M, entirely due to Gray Surety goodwill and intangibles (which jumped to $246M from $61M). The new $297.4M term loan is the first meaningful leverage in the company's history, carrying roughly $2.9M of quarterly interest. AOCI of -$16.5M reflects rate-driven unrealized bond losses on an investment-grade portfolio (71.9% A-rated or better, 3.8% below IG), not credit impairment. Operating cash flow was $47M in Q1, down from $87M on timing.

A note on screening artifacts: an Altman Z-Score reading near 1.0 flags PLMR as distressed, but the Z-Score model is invalid for insurers given their structurally high-liability, low-asset-turnover balance sheets. The underlying health — 27% adjusted ROE, conservative reserves, an A1/A+ portfolio, $1.6B of cash and securities — does not warrant penalizing the company for this false positive.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the strongest dimension and the primary basis for the BUY. Four of five lines posted 47-131% growth, with only earthquake (deliberately throttled) growing low single digits. Net investment income grew 49% as the portfolio compounds at elevated reinvestment yields.

The estimate revision picture is consistent with continued momentum: current-quarter consensus rose 4.5% over 90 days, current-year rose 3.2% to $9.88, and FY27 rose 1.4% to $11.18. These revisions are occurring while the stock has fallen, which is the configuration that historically precedes re-rating as price catches up to fundamentals. The earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats, including a 21.4% beat in Q3 2025, supporting confidence in the forward estimates.

Crop seasonality distorts interim comparisons — the majority of crop premium is recognized in Q3 — so quarterly crop trends should not be annualized. This argues for patience through the year rather than reading any single quarter as definitive.

The durability question is whether the mix shift toward attritional lines structurally compresses returns over time. My read, supported by the maintained 27% adjusted ROE and management's reaffirmed guidance, is that Palomar is intentionally accepting higher loss ratios on more profitable retained business while net investment income scales as a second earnings pillar. The growth thesis has high durability because it is broad-based across four independent lines rather than dependent on a single product.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing GAAP P/E of 15.96x is the wrong lens here, because the trailing period embeds the one-time Gray Surety costs and the intangible amortization step-up that depress GAAP earnings without reflecting earnings power.

The peer table requires interpretation. The listed P&C comparables (AGO 8.7x, HCI 7.1x, STC 14.6x, PRA 19.6x, RDN 7.9x) carry a median 8.7x, but these are mature, low-growth or run-off-oriented franchises (financial guaranty, title, mortgage, medical malpractice) growing in the low single digits or shrinking. PLMR is growing GWP at 42% with a 27% adjusted ROE. A specialty grower at that ROE compounding at that rate should command a premium to an 8.7x median, not a discount. On a growth-adjusted basis, with EPS growing in the low-20s and a forward multiple near 12x, the PEG is roughly 0.5, which is clearly cheap if the growth holds.

The more relevant anchor is PLMR's own history: the multiple sat in the high teens to low twenties across the past five quarters when the stock traded $120-$160. The de-rating to ~12x reflects the market's fear of structural margin compression. If that fear is misplaced (my base case), the multiple should normalize toward the mid-teens, implying meaningful re-rating on top of earnings growth.

This is a configuration where a low multiple is backed by an identifiable reason the market is mispricing the stock — it has misread a deliberate, ROE-neutral mix shift as margin deterioration — rather than a value trap. Analyst targets of $132 low and $168 high function as a floor that lags reality and will likely be revised as the mix-shift debate resolves favorably through the year.

I am tempering this against the discipline that cheap multiples in Financials have, in aggregate, sorted the wrong way. The distinction is that PLMR's cheapness is paired with accelerating top-line growth and rising estimates, not declining fundamentals, which is the profile that distinguishes mispricing from a trap.

## Competitive Landscape

Palomar competes against larger, better-capitalized specialty and standard insurers, underwriting agencies, and state entities (California Earthquake Authority, NFIP, Texas Windstorm). Its edge is niche specialization, analytics, and underwriting speed rather than scale.

The trajectory is improving on peril diversification: California fell to 25% of GWP (from 30.9% in FY25) and earthquake to 22%, reducing peak-zone concentration. The expansion into Casualty, E&S, Crop, Surety, and Environmental addresses both the concentration risk and the opportunity in dislocated specialty markets where pricing remains firm.

Two offsetting concerns hold the score below 7. Distribution concentration persists: the top two program administrators represent roughly 30% of GWP, and the largest (Arrowhead, distributing the Value Select Residential Earthquake program at 16.1% of Q1 GWP) is terminable on 180 days' notice for cause. The moat rests on analytics and speed rather than durable scale advantages, so share gains depend on continued execution against better-resourced competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin/mix risk (the central debate):** As Palomar retains more attritional-loss business, loss ratios structurally rise. The flat GAAP Q1 is the first visible sign. The bull case requires this to be a profitable mix artifact (ROE-neutral) rather than the start of return compression. This is the single most important risk and the reason the risk score sits at 5.5.

**Distribution concentration:** Top two PAs at ~30% of GWP; loss of Arrowhead would dent earthquake premium materially. California at 25% of GWP carries regulatory rate-approval constraints and indirect wildfire-related non-renewal exposure.

**Catastrophe tail:** Earthquake remains 22% of GWP. Cat retention is $20M (EQ) / $11M (hurricane); reinsurance exhausts at $3.5B for EQ. A San Andreas-scale event beyond the 1-in-250-year PML could impair capital. Fire-following-earthquake is excluded.

**Reinsurance dependence:** Palomar cedes roughly 46% of GWP across 100+ reinsurers plus $1.2B of Torrey Pines Re cat bonds. Hard reinsurance markets raise costs/retentions; reinsurer default leaves Palomar primarily liable.

**New leverage and M&A integration:** The first-ever $297M term loan introduces covenants including a minimum A.M. Best rating, and a downgrade would breach. Three acquisitions in 13 months (FIA, AAP, Gray Surety) raise integration risk; surety is a newer line and PCSC posted a $1.2M stub-period loss. Goodwill and intangibles of $246M now carry impairment risk if surety underperforms.

**Social inflation / reserve risk:** The casualty migration introduces longer-tail reserve and social-inflation exposure over time, currently masked by favorable IM&P development.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 36.2% sits essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 37.0% (spread -0.8%), with the IV percentile within the current chain at 3% — options are not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, suggesting this is not an unfavorable entry on a volatility basis. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.4%, and the put/call OI ratio of 0.15 is light, indicating no concentrated bearish positioning. The term structure is roughly flat (36.2% near, 38.1% far). The one anomaly is the reported jump in a delta-snapshot ATM IV reading to 151.6%, which is inconsistent with the detailed term-structure table and reads as a thin-options data artifact rather than a genuine signal; I weight the term-structure data, which shows a calm, balanced market. The options market neither confirms nor contradicts the thesis strongly; it indicates a stock the derivatives market views as roughly fairly volatile with no acute directional fear.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($160-$175)

The mix shift proves to be ROE-neutral profitable diversification, not margin erosion. Adjusted EPS compounds at low-20s toward $11+ in FY27, the loss ratio stabilizes as the retained Crop/Casualty book seasons, and the multiple re-rates from ~12x back toward the mid-teens as the structural-compression fear dissipates. Net investment income continues compounding at high-double-digit rates as a second earnings pillar. At 15x FY27 adjusted EPS of $11.18, the stock reaches roughly $168, matching the high analyst target.

### Bear Case ($85-$95)

The rising loss ratio is the leading edge of genuine return compression as Palomar trades low-volatility earthquake economics for competitive, attritional, social-inflation-exposed casualty business. Adjusted ROE drifts from 27% toward the high teens, the surety and crop acquisitions disappoint, goodwill is impaired, and the multiple stays compressed at 9-10x on now-lower earnings power. A major California earthquake or covenant breach would accelerate the downside. At 9x a flattened ~$10 EPS, the stock reaches roughly $90, about 21% below current.

The asymmetry: roughly 47% upside to the high target versus roughly 21% downside to the bear case, with the maintained adjusted ROE and favorable reserve development tilting probability toward the bull mechanism.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $140.** At 13x the FY27 adjusted EPS estimate of $11.18, reflecting a partial re-rating from ~12x toward the historical mid-teens as the mix-shift debate resolves favorably, plus continued mid-single-digit estimate revision. This sits between the $90 bear and $168 bull cases. Roughly 22% upside from $114.42.

**Bull case target: $168** (47% upside) — 15x FY27 adjusted EPS, full multiple normalization.

**Bear case target: $90** (21% downside) — 9x a compressed ~$10 EPS on confirmed margin erosion.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the key checkpoints being the Q2 (August) and Q3 (November) prints, where the Q3 crop-seasonal results will materially inform whether the retained-Crop strategy is accretive.

**Upside/downside from current ($114.42):** +22% to base, +47% to bull, -21% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Adjusted ROE falls below 22% on a trailing-four-quarter basis, signaling the mix shift is compressing returns rather than diversifying risk.
2. The adjusted combined ratio deteriorates beyond 80% with no offsetting investment-income growth, confirming structural margin erosion.
3. Adverse prior-year reserve development emerges in Casualty or Crop, reversing the favorable-development pattern and signaling under-reserving in the longer-tail lines.
4. Loss of the Arrowhead PA relationship or a material A.M. Best downgrade (the latter also a covenant trigger).

## Conclusion

Palomar is a high-growth specialty insurer trading at a multiple that prices in margin compression the fundamentals do not yet support. The 28% drawdown is explained by a market misreading of a deliberate, ROE-neutral loss-ratio mix shift as deterioration, compounded by one-time Gray Surety costs that suppressed GAAP earnings without affecting earnings power. With GWP growing 42%, adjusted EPS up 24%, adjusted ROE holding at 27%, four consecutive beats, positive estimate revisions, and a forward multiple near 12x against an analyst target range of $132-$168, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.

The recommendation is **BUY**, with the explicit caveat that the thesis hinges on the margin debate resolving in favor of profitable diversification, monitored quarterly against the invalidation triggers above. The score of 6.8 reflects strong growth and valuation support, tempered by a 5.5 risk score that captures the unresolved margin question and the distribution and catastrophe concentrations. This is a position to size with discipline given the binary nature of the margin debate, not an unqualified high-conviction holding.
