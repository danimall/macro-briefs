## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest independent natural gas producer in the United States, formed through the October 2024 merger of Chesapeake Energy and Southwestern Energy. The company produces 7.2 Bcfe/d across the Haynesville and Appalachian basins, holding proved reserves with a PV-10 of $19.4 billion. At $96.90, the stock trades at approximately 10.6x forward earnings (based on consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.10), a ~13% discount to the E&P peer median P/E of 12.2x, despite superior scale, investment-grade credit, and dual-basin positioning adjacent to both Gulf Coast LNG terminals and Northeast data center power demand.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) rapid balance sheet deleveraging (~$1.3B in debt redeemed in April 2026 after exceeding the full-year target, now pivoting to buybacks), (2) a $500M/year margin improvement program showing early execution ($90M captured in Q1 from volatility monetization), and (3) structural demand growth from LNG exports and AI/data center power consumption that EXE's asset base is positioned to serve. The primary risk is natural gas price volatility, partially mitigated by >60% hedge coverage through 2026. The CEO transition to an interim leader introduces governance uncertainty, though multiple insider purchases at these levels provide some comfort.

The stock has declined 23% from its December 2025 high of $126.62 to $96.90 currently, while fundamentals have improved (Q1 2026 beat, debt target achieved, LNG deal signed). This price-fundamental divergence creates an attractive entry point.

## Company Overview

Expand Energy operates approximately 6,600 gross wells across three core areas:
- **Haynesville (Louisiana/Texas):** 3,000 MMcf/d, adjacent to Gulf Coast LNG export infrastructure. Pure gas.
- **Northeast Appalachia (Pennsylvania):** 2,624 MMcf/d, Marcellus Shale, serving growing data center power demand. Pure gas.
- **Southwest Appalachia (WV/Ohio):** 1,559 MMcfe/d, Marcellus/Utica, includes oil and NGL production.

The company holds approximately 4.2 million gross acres, maintains a goal of 100% RSG (Responsibly Sourced Gas) certification across the portfolio, and was added to the S&P 500 in March 2025. It achieved investment-grade ratings (BBB-) from all three major agencies.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term:** Consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.10 reflects approximately 20% growth from the $7.57 trailing figure, driven by higher gas realizations and full-year merger synergies. FY2027 estimates at $9.85 suggest continued modest growth.

**Estimate revision context is nuanced.** Current and next quarter estimates have been revised downward (-23.4% and -22.2% over 90 days respectively), reflecting seasonal gas price weakness. However, full-year 2026 estimates are essentially stable (+4.1% over 90 days) and FY2027 is flat (-0.5%). The quarterly weakness is a gas pricing phenomenon, not a structural deterioration signal.

**Medium-term growth drivers:**
1. **$500M margin improvement program:** Management targets $0.20/Mcf additional realization through premium market access, LNG sales, and volatility monetization. Q1 execution ($90M from volatility alone) suggests the near-term half is achievable. If fully realized: ~$2.10/share additional FCF.
2. **LNG export growth:** Delfin LNG offtake (1.15 Mtpa, 20-year, targeted ~2031 start, subject to FID). Haynesville proximity to 5+ Gulf Coast LNG facilities under construction or expansion provides volume placement advantage.
3. **Data center/AI power demand:** Management estimates 4-6 Bcf/d of potential Northeast demand growth. Appalachian assets directly serve this load.
4. **Western Haynesville exploration:** First well online March 2026, already on the lower end of the cost curve. Second well in progress.

**Production guidance:** 7.5 Bcfe/d for 2026 (up from 7.2 Bcfe/d in 2025; +4%).

## Competitive Landscape

EXE holds structural advantages vs. gas-focused peers:

1. **Scale:** Largest U.S. independent gas producer by volume. Lowest G&A per unit ($0.07/Mcfe) in the peer group.
2. **Dual-basin optionality:** Haynesville for Gulf Coast LNG (structural growth market) + Appalachia for Northeast power demand (AI/data centers). Among pure-gas peers, this dual-basin positioning across both demand growth vectors appears differentiated.
3. **LNG proximity:** Physical adjacency to Sabine Pass, Calcasieu Pass, Delfin, and other Gulf Coast export facilities. This provides a marketing advantage that landlocked Appalachian producers lack.
4. **RSG certification:** The company maintains a goal of 100% Responsibly Sourced Gas certification, providing premium access to European and Asian buyers with ESG mandates.
5. **Investment-grade balance sheet:** BBB- with $5.7B liquidity (as of March 31, 2026). Most mid-cap E&P peers lack investment-grade access.

**Competitive trajectory: improving.** The merger created a gas-focused entity with sufficient scale to compete for LNG offtake agreements (the Delfin deal demonstrates this). Management's claim that "nearly 90% of expected U.S. demand growth can be served by our assets" reflects a uniquely diversified gas position.

**Key competitive risk:** EQT is the primary Appalachian competitor and is pursuing similar LNG/power strategies. However, EXE's Haynesville position provides a differentiated Gulf Coast angle EQT lacks.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Natural gas price sensitivity (HIGH).** Revenue is highly correlated to gas prices. Current economics work well at $3.50+/Mcf but stress at $2.50 or below. Mitigation: >60% hedged through 2026 via collars.

2. **CEO transition (MODERATE-HIGH).** Dell'Osso was replaced in February 2026; his subsequent appointment as CEO of competitor Gulfport Energy raises questions about the nature of his departure, though SEC filings indicate he would serve as an external advisor during a transition period. Interim CEO Wichterich is experienced but a permanent hire (timing not formally disclosed in SEC filings reviewed) introduces execution uncertainty during a critical post-merger period.

3. **GP&T cost structure (MODERATE).** $0.91/Mcfe transport costs and $9.6B in undiscounted midstream commitments represent fixed obligations that compress margins. The $500M improvement plan addresses this but execution is multi-year.

4. **Gas price correlation to Iran/geopolitical developments.** Natural gas prices are less directly affected by the Iran conflict than oil, but elevated diesel costs increase drilling expenses. Conversely, any sustained move toward LNG demand (Europe seeking alternatives to Russian pipeline gas) benefits EXE structurally.

5. **LNG project execution risk.** The Delfin LNG facility (targeted ~2031 start, subject to FID) is subject to construction, permitting, and regulatory risk. A delay extends the timeline for margin improvement from this channel.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Hedge book covers >60% of volumes through 2026
- Investment-grade balance sheet with $5.7B liquidity (as of March 31, 2026)
- Breakeven appears around $2.00-2.50/Mcf all-in
- Multiple insider purchases in recent months signal management confidence

## Conclusion

Expand Energy represents a compelling value opportunity within the E&P space. The stock trades at a ~13% discount to gas-focused peers despite holding the largest domestic gas production base, an investment-grade balance sheet, and structural positioning for the two dominant U.S. gas demand growth vectors (LNG exports and data center power). The near-term quarterly estimate revisions reflect seasonal gas pricing, not structural deterioration — full-year estimates are stable.

The CEO transition is a legitimate risk that partially explains the discount, but the severity is mitigated by: an experienced interim leader with skin in the game (open-market buyer), a seasoned new CFO, operational continuity from a COO with 20 years at COP, and a Board that has already demonstrated willingness to act decisively. Multiple insider purchases across different executives at $92-108 provide additional conviction.

At 10.6x forward earnings with a 2.4% base dividend, >60% hedge coverage, and $500M/year margin improvement upside, the risk/reward is favorable for a 12-month holding period. Maintain **BUY** with a base case target of $120-125.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation discount to peers is approximately 13% (not 39% as originally calculated), which narrows the margin-of-safety argument supporting the BUY recommendation; the thesis now relies more heavily on margin improvement execution and commodity price support.
