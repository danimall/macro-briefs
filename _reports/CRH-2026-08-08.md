## Executive Summary

CRH is down 23.6% from its January 9, 2026 high of $131.55 and sits 6.8% above the July 31 low of $94.115. This drawdown has a clean causal chain. On June 21, 2026 CRH agreed to acquire Arcosa (NYSE: ACA) for $150.00 per share in cash, an enterprise value of roughly $8.5bn, funded with approximately $8.75bn of incremental debt. In the same announcement CRH suspended its share buyback — a program that returned $1.3bn in FY2024, $1.2bn in FY2025 and $0.7bn year-to-date 2026, and shrank the share count 7.2% over two years — with no restart date beyond "will reevaluate at a later date." Pro forma net debt/EBITDA moves from 1.94x today to roughly 2.4-2.9x. On my arithmetic the transaction is close to GAAP EPS-neutral in its first full year even assuming full delivery of the $175m synergy figure reported from the earnings call (a figure that does not appear in CRH's own press release or 10-Q), and it lowers group return on invested capital, which had already fallen 130bps to 12.1% in FY2025.

The offsetting evidence is the reason this is not a REDUCE. FY2025's entire revenue growth was inorganic — organic revenue declined in all three segments — while H1 2026 organic revenue turned positive in the Americas (+$381m in Americas Materials in H1, +$145m in Americas Building in Q2). Q2 pricing held: Americas aggregates +5%, asphalt +6%, International cement +4%. FY2026 guidance of $8.1-8.5bn Adjusted EBITDA and $5.60-6.05 diluted EPS was reaffirmed on July 30. The balance sheet is defensively constructed: 91% fixed-rate debt, 8.2-year weighted-average maturity, a €3.5bn undrawn revolver, no financial covenants on the revolver or the new term loan, and three stable investment-grade ratings as of June 30, 2026.

At $100.48 the stock is 17.2x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $5.83 and 15.0x next-year consensus of $6.70, versus peer P/Es of 33.2x (VMC), 35.4x (MLM), 29.6x (KNF) and 16.5x (EXP). Consensus targets ($105 low, $138.65 mean, $165 high) sit entirely above spot, but estimates are being trimmed — the current-quarter number is down 6.2% in 30 days — which makes those targets stale rather than corroborating. Base case $112, bull $140, bear $82: a probability-weighted return near +11% before a 1.55% dividend, with the principal uncertainty (antitrust clearance and synergy delivery) unresolvable before Q1 2027. That is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

CRH operates roughly 3,961 locations across 28 countries with about 83,000 employees, reporting in three segments. FY2025: Americas Materials Solutions $17,029m revenue at a 23.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin; Americas Building Solutions $7,122m at 20.7%; International Solutions $13,296m at 16.6%. Group revenue $37,447m, Adjusted EBITDA $7,681m (20.5%). Approximately 75% of net income and 71% of Adjusted EBITDA came from North America. End markets were 40% infrastructure, 32% residential, 28% non-residential, split 60% new build and 40% repair and remodel.

The asset base is the business. CRH holds 1,334 extractive properties across 252,791 owned and 128,651 leased acres, shipping 380.7Mt of aggregates, 59.0Mt of cementitious materials, 39.5m cubic yards of readymix and 62.8Mt of asphalt in FY2025. The economics of the moat are geological rather than brand- or technology-based: transport cost is high relative to product value, so quarry proximity to the demand center determines who can compete, and permitting new reserves is slow and increasingly contested. Arcosa's own filings independently document the same mechanism, and note more than 5,000 US aggregates producers — a fragmentation that has justified CRH's serial consolidation strategy.

That strategy has accelerated. CRH completed 40 acquisitions for $5.0bn in 2024, 38 for $4.1bn in 2025, and 16 for $1.2bn in H1 2026 (plus $0.2bn in July), roughly $10.5bn of M&A in 30 months against a $67bn market cap — before Arcosa. Concurrently it has been selling: $1.8bn of divestiture proceeds in H1 2026 alone, exiting construction accessories ($0.7bn), lawn and garden ($1.1bn) and MoistureShield decking ($0.1bn), while buying Axius Water ($0.7bn, closed May 29, 2026). The direction of travel is out of consumer-adjacent building products and into aggregates, cement, roads, water and grid-adjacent infrastructure.

Two things stand out. First, margin quality: 280bps of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in two years, with FY2025 operating cash flow at 148% of net income and FY2025 free cash flow (OCF less total capex of $2,713m) of $2,912m, a 4.3% FCF yield on the current market cap. Second, the composition of that expansion: FY2025 organic revenue was negative in every segment (−$73m Americas Materials, −$102m Americas Building, −$90m International) while organic Adjusted EBITDA was positive in all three. FY2025 profit growth came from price/cost spread and self-help on a flat-to-declining volume base. That is good execution with a finite runway.

**Q2 and H1 2026.** Q2 revenue $10,777m (+5.6%), Adjusted EBITDA $2,627m (+6.7%) at a 24.4% margin (+30bps), diluted EPS $2.21 versus $1.94. The reported 8.7% EPS beat against a $2.03 consensus is flattered by a $266m pre-tax divestiture gain in other non-operating income, partly offset by an effective tax rate that rose to 31% from 24% specifically because of divestiture taxes. Underlying, the picture is 30bps of margin expansion delivered against disclosed cost inflation of energy +16%, haulage +21%, labor +4% and D&A +8%. The market reaction: the stock fell from $99.71 to $96.00 on results day and to $95.01 the following session, a 4.7% two-day decline on a headline beat.

H1 operating cash flow of $513m versus $719m a year earlier should not be annualized. CRH is heavily seasonal — H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $3,213m is 38.7% of the FY2026 guidance midpoint, almost exactly the 38.5% H1 2025 share of FY2025 actual — and the H1 drag is dominated by a $1,824m seasonal receivables build (AR from $5,178m at December 31 to $6,777m at June 30) plus $404m of cash taxes. FY2025 generated roughly $4.9bn of operating cash in H2 alone. H2 2026 should be judged against that pattern.

**Segment divergence.** Americas Building Solutions is deteriorating: Q2 revenue −2%, Adjusted EBITDA −8%, margin −140bps to 21.8%, organic EBITDA −$22m. Inside it, Building & Infrastructure Solutions revenue rose 10% on energy and data-center demand while Outdoor Living Solutions fell 7% on divestitures and subdued residential. CRH has been selling the weak half. Americas cement pricing was −1% in both Q2 and H1 with volumes −2%, which CRH attributes to geographic mix; cement is the most capital-intensive, highest-fixed-cost part of the portfolio and its profit is the most volume-sensitive.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt was $15,416m at June 30, 2026 against TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $7,936m (FY2025 $7,681m + H1 2026 $3,213m − H1 2025 $2,958m), or 1.94x. Enterprise value of roughly $82.5bn puts EV/TTM Adjusted EBITDA at 10.4x. Structure is conservative for the leverage level: 91% fixed rate, 8.2-year weighted-average maturity, maturities of $1,526m in the balance of 2026 and $2,207m in 2027, an undrawn €3.5bn revolver to May 2030, and no financial covenants on either the revolver or the July 2026 term loan. Ratings at June 30: S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+, all stable, though all predate the deal announcement and I found no agency commentary on the financing.

The leverage trajectory is the concern rather than the current level. Net debt has grown from $5.4bn (FY2023) to $15.4bn, with interest expense from $376m to $810m, and is headed toward roughly $21-24bn. With the long end of the Treasury curve at multi-decade highs, refinancing the $3.7bn maturing through 2027 at 5.0-5.5% against retired coupons like the 3.875% 2025 notes adds roughly $16m of pre-tax interest per $1bn refinanced. Two smaller items warrant monitoring: CRH plc guarantees $16.9bn of subsidiary borrowings (up from $13.1bn at end-2024), and the consolidated Philippines venture, which lost $28m in H1, saw its current portion of long-term debt jump from $13m to $318m.

## Growth Analysis

**FY2026 guidance (reaffirmed July 30, 2026):** net income $3.9-4.1bn, Adjusted EBITDA $8.1-8.5bn, diluted EPS $5.60-6.05, capex $2.7-2.9bn. Stated assumptions: ~$0.7bn net interest, ~26% tax rate, ~674m average diluted shares. The midpoint implies +8% Adjusted EBITDA and +5.8% EPS. Two changes accompanied the reaffirmation: capex guidance was lowered from $2.8-3.0bn "to reflect project timing and lower maintenance spend," and the tax expense midpoint was raised from $1.3bn to $1.4bn on divestiture impacts. Lower capex plus higher tax within an unchanged EPS range is a mixed signal — the capex reduction supports near-term free cash flow but is not a demand indicator.

**The organic inflection is the strongest positive in the file.** After FY2025's negative organic revenue in all three segments, H1 2026 delivered +$381m organic in Americas Materials and Q2 organic gains of $170m (Americas Materials), $145m (Americas Building) and $53m (International). Q2 Americas aggregates volumes +2% with pricing +5%; asphalt volumes +3% with pricing +6%. This suggests the 2025 volume trough was cyclical. The caveat is that one favorable half-year is thin evidence, and weather cut both ways — Arcosa reported wet weather hurting Texas and New Jersey aggregates and asphalt volumes in the same quarter.

**Consensus is drifting the wrong way.** Current-quarter estimates are down 6.2% over 30 days and 7.2% over 90 days ($2.43 → $2.26); current year −1.1%/−0.7% to $5.89; next year −1.2%/−0.5% to $6.70. Consensus current-quarter ($2.26) plus next-quarter ($1.59) added to H1 actual EPS of $1.93 implies FY2026 of $5.78, marginally below the guidance midpoint. The revisions are modest in the aggregate but the direction is unambiguous, and it materially weakens the case for treating the $138.65 mean target as a floor.

**Where growth comes from over 2027-2029.** Three distinct drivers, with different confidence levels.

*Electricity and data infrastructure — highest confidence.* CRH's Building & Infrastructure Solutions revenue rose 10% on data-center and energy demand. Arcosa's utility and related structures backlog rose to $648.1m at June 30, up 49% year-to-date and 44% year-over-year, which Arcosa attributes to grid hardening and AI-driven electricity demand. This corroborates independently across two filers and is consistent with the broader grid buildout. Axius Water and Eco Material Technologies point the same direction.

*Federal infrastructure — highest swing factor.* IIJA provided roughly $350bn of federal highway funding with CRH stating approximately 50% yet to be deployed, plus ~$48bn water, ~$79bn energy and ~$65bn technology allocations. Arcosa notes IIJA funds highway programs through 2026 and expects either reauthorization by end-2026 or an extension. Reauthorization is the single largest external variable for the combined entity.

*Residential — the drag.* Thirty-two percent of end markets, with new build "expected to remain subdued" per guidance and repair and remodel described only as "resilient." Arcosa reports Texas housing permits down roughly 8% in 2025. This argues against a 2027 residential volume recovery in either company.

## Valuation Assessment

On the frozen $100.48 basis:

| Metric | CRH |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2025 diluted EPS $5.51 | 18.2x |
| P/E on FY2026 guidance midpoint $5.83 | 17.2x |
| P/E on next-year consensus $6.70 | 15.0x |
| EV / TTM Adjusted EBITDA ($82.5bn / $7,936m) | 10.4x |
| FY2025 FCF yield (OCF − total capex) | 4.3% |
| Dividend yield ($1.56 annualized) | 1.55% |
| Payout on FY2025 EPS | 28% |

(The data feed reports identical trailing and forward P/E of 17.21x, which cannot both be right given trailing EPS of $5.51; I have used computed values throughout.)

Against peers, CRH at 17.2x forward is roughly half the multiple of Vulcan (33.2x) and Martin Marietta (35.4x) and close to Eagle Materials (16.5x). The discount to VMC/MLM is structural and long-standing rather than newly created: those are pure-play US aggregates businesses with higher margins, lower leverage, no international exposure and no large-deal overhang. Eagle Materials is the more honest comparable, and CRH trades at a small premium to it. The relevant question is whether 15-17x forward is too low for a business earning 20.5% EBITDA margins with pricing power of 5-6% in its core Americas product lines; CRH does not deserve 33x.

There is a case that it is too low. The multiple has compressed roughly 25% since January while Adjusted EBITDA grew 8% and organic volumes turned positive. Applying 16.7x to next-year consensus of $6.70 gives $112. Historically CRH has re-rated as its US mix increased; the January peak of $131.55 was roughly 22x forward.

There is also a case that the compression is warranted and durable, and I give it comparable weight. The market is pricing four things: (1) an acquisition at 13.5-16.5x pre-synergy EBITDA against CRH's own 10.4x, i.e. a 30-55% premium multiple; (2) the removal of $1.2-1.3bn per year of buyback accretion for an unspecified period; (3) leverage moving from 1.94x to 2.4-2.9x into a rising long-end regime; and (4) declining incremental returns on a rapidly growing capital base — Adjusted NOPAT grew 23.8% from FY2023 to FY2025 while invested capital grew 48.2%. A company that reinvests at falling marginal returns should trade at a lower multiple of current earnings, not the same one.

I therefore score valuation moderately positive at 6.2 rather than strongly positive. The forward multiple is undemanding relative to the asset quality, but the discount has an identifiable cause that will not resolve before the deal closes, estimates are being trimmed rather than raised, and cheapness in Materials has historically been a poor standalone reason to buy. The sell-side setup — 21 analysts split 3 strong buy / 16 buy / 2 hold, lowest target $105, mean $138.65, spot below the low target — reads as targets that have not been marked down for the June 21 deal and the July 30 tax-rate change, not as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is intact and both CRH's and Arcosa's filings describe it identically: aggregates cannot travel far economically, asphalt plants must be co-located because the mix hardens, cement rarely moves more than a few hundred miles, and new permits are difficult. The result is durable local market structures where price typically runs above general inflation. Q2 2026 evidence supports this — Americas aggregates +5%, asphalt +6%, International cement +4%, International aggregates +2%.

Two qualifications. Americas cement pricing was −1% in Q2 and H1 with volumes −2%; CRH attributes this to geographic mix, but cement is where fixed-cost leverage works hardest in both directions. And International asphalt pricing of +20% is disclosed as mix rather than underlying price, a reminder to read the percentage disclosures as mix-affected.

On trajectory, CRH is consolidating share in a market with more than 5,000 US aggregates producers, and the reserve position (1,334 extractive properties; Arcosa adds 1.3bn tons concentrated in Texas at ~35% and New Jersey at ~20% of its Construction Products revenue) is genuinely scarce and getting scarcer. The unresolved issue is price. The consolidation runway exists; the cost of tenancy has risen, and the Arcosa multiple is the clearest evidence of that.

The competitive repositioning toward electricity, water and data infrastructure is real and reduces residential dependence. It does not reduce customer concentration — the deal imports a segment-level 12.2% customer relationship (GE Vernova, which purchases wind towers from Arcosa), though at roughly $352m that is under 1% of a combined ~$40bn revenue base.

## Risk Assessment

**Arcosa completion and antitrust.** Required clearances are HSR plus Australia (CRH owns Adbri), Mexico (Arcosa manufactures there) and possibly the Investment Canada Act. Arcosa's definitive proxy was filed August 3 with a September 4, 2026 special meeting; the outside date is June 21, 2027 with up to six months' extension. CRH's reverse termination fee is approximately $372.0m, or 5.05% of the ~$7.37bn equity consideration, at the upper end of market practice; Arcosa's fee to CRH is $260.4m. Texas is the plausible locus of any remedy given CRH's $2.1bn 2024 Texas cement and readymix acquisition alongside Arcosa's ~35% Texas Construction Products exposure. A high reverse fee is consistent with a seller pricing regulatory risk, though sellers routinely extract high fees in all-cash, financing-condition-free deals for certainty alone. I am not asserting a remedy will be required.

**Deal economics.** Using Arcosa TTM continuing-operations EBIT of roughly $296m (EBITDA ~$521m less ~$225m D&A), $8.75bn of incremental debt at 5.0-5.5% costs $440-480m of annual interest. Pre-synergy that is roughly −$164m pre-tax; with the reported $175m of synergies fully realized it is approximately +$11m pre-tax, about a cent per share, before purchase-accounting amortization step-up. First-year incremental ROIC is roughly 2.6% pre-synergy or 4.1% with fully-taxed synergies, against group Adjusted ROIC of 12.1%. Three mitigants deserve weight: first-year accounting returns understate quarry economics because depletion front-loads the capital charge; Arcosa's utility backlog is up 49% year-to-date so 2027 EBIT should exceed the TTM base; and CRH's divestiture engine ($1.8bn in H1 2026) can retire part of the debt. The burden of proof has shifted to synergy delivery, and the $175m figure is not in CRH's own filings.

**Imported policy cliff.** Inside Arcosa, Engineered Structures operating profit rose 37.7% in H1 while Construction Products fell 9.0%. Within Engineered Structures, utility structures revenue rose 13.7% while wind towers fell 20.2% with backlog down 14% year-to-date. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminates the AMP credit for wind towers sold after 2027 and removes the PTC for projects beginning construction after July 4, 2026 that are not in service by end-2027. Sixty-six percent of remaining wind backlog is scheduled for 2027 recognition, so this is plausibly a 2027 tailwind followed by a step-down. Arcosa has already converted one idled wind plant to utility structures.

**Leverage into a higher-for-longer curve.** Pro forma 2.4-2.9x with $16.9bn of parent guarantees. No agency has commented on the financing; CRH's intention to maintain a strong investment-grade rating is management expectation. Interest coverage on FY2025 figures is roughly 6.7x (EBIT $5,440m / interest $810m); the pro forma level will fall as incremental interest is added.

**Governance and continuity.** Seven Item 5.02 officer/director change filings in about 19 months. The CFO took the seat May 12, 2026 (an internal promotion with 14 years at CRH) and the CEO is new within the last 6-18 months, both executing the largest transaction in company history. A February 28, 2025 Item 4.01 auditor change was filed and its reason is unverified. Mine safety Item 1.04 filings were made in December 2024, March 2026 and May 2026.

**Other.** Goodwill of $13,099m is 22% of assets, with two reporting units at only 13% aggregate headroom — but their combined goodwill is $249m, so the concentrated impairment risk is small. Energy and energy-linked raw materials equal roughly 10% of revenue, with Q2 energy costs +16%. CRH discloses people and assets in Ukraine and neighboring countries. A $435m redeemable non-controlling-interest put on two North American subsidiaries is currently exercisable by the holder while CRH's call rights begin only after 2035/2040.

### Options Market Signal

Post-earnings volatility has fully normalized: ATM IV fell from 78.3% on July 30 to 30.5%, against 31.7% one-year realized — fairly priced, at the 7th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure (30.5% near, 32.5% at six months) and a ±3.5% implied move on the nearest expiry. Put/call open interest fell from 1.46 to 0.94 over the same window, which is not consistent with an accumulation of downside protection. The one datapoint worth flagging is the September 18 tenor, where put/call volume ratio is 6.07 with an 8.4% skew reading — the expiry that brackets both the September 4 Arcosa shareholder vote and the September FOMC. Short interest is 2.4% of float at 3.0 days to cover, so the bear case is uncrowded. On balance the options market is pricing an ordinary risk profile with localized event hedging around September, which neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($140, +39%).** Arcosa clears all four jurisdictions without material remedy and closes in Q1 2027. CRH quantifies synergies in its own disclosure at or above $175m, and Arcosa's utility-structures backlog (+49% YTD) converts so that 2027 segment EBIT runs well above the TTM base I used, lifting incremental ROIC toward group average. Divestiture proceeds continue at the H1 2026 pace of $1.8bn per half, holding pro forma leverage near 2.4x and allowing the buyback to resume in 2027. IIJA is reauthorized at or above current levels, releasing the ~50% of highway funds still undeployed. Americas organic volumes build on the H1 inflection with pricing continuing at 5%+. FY2027 EPS reaches $7.00-7.20 and the multiple recovers to 19-20x as the deal overhang clears. This is consistent with the $138.65 consensus mean.

**Bear case ($82, −18%).** Antitrust review requires Texas divestitures, delaying close past mid-2027 or triggering the $372m reverse fee. Higher long-end yields keep residential demand weak, single-family starts fall through 2027, and the 32% residential exposure plus Outdoor Living drag more than offsets infrastructure. IIJA receives only a short extension, freezing state lettings. Leverage sits near 2.9x, one agency moves to negative outlook, and the buyback stays suspended into 2028. FY2027 EPS lands near $6.20 and the multiple compresses to 13x on the combination of leverage and falling ROIC. This is a de-rating scenario, not a distress scenario — covenant-free facilities, 91% fixed-rate debt and an 8.2-year maturity ladder make a liquidity event unlikely.

**Base case ($112, +11%).** Deal closes in Q1 2027 with minor or no remedies. Synergies are delivered on a lag, making the transaction roughly EPS-neutral in year one and modestly accretive by 2028. Americas organic revenue growth continues in the low single digits with pricing above cost inflation; Building Solutions stays weak; International is flat. FY2026 lands within guidance near $5.80-5.85 and FY2027 near $6.70. The multiple holds at 16-17x forward as the deal overhang persists through most of the period. Base case = 16.7x $6.70 = $112.

Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear: +11% price return plus a 1.55% dividend. That is approximately fair compensation for the dispersion involved, and there is no dated catalyst that resolves the central uncertainty inside twelve months.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have moved 7.2 → 7.2 → 6.1, and I take them to 5.91. The reduction is not a function of the lower price. It reflects specific developments since the June 18 review: an acquisition agreed at a materially higher multiple than CRH's own with first-year incremental ROIC of roughly 2.6-4.1%; the indefinite suspension of a buyback that had been removing roughly 3.5% of shares annually; leverage guided from 1.94x to 2.4-2.9x; and negative estimate revisions across every forecast period. Growth Prospects stays near the prior level rather than falling further because the H1 organic inflection is a genuine improvement that partially offsets the negative revisions. Management Quality moves down marginally on the capital-allocation trade — pausing repurchases at a 5.8% forward earnings yield to deploy $8.5bn at a first-year return in the low single digits — tempered by the disciplined divestiture program and a 5% dividend increase at a 28% payout.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive information arriving in three tranches — the September 4, 2026 Arcosa shareholder vote and subsequent HSR/international clearance news; the Q3 print in late October or early November 2026, which tests whether the H1 organic inflection persisted through the seasonal peak; and surface-transportation reauthorization by end-2026.

**Base case target: $112** (16.7x next-year consensus EPS of $6.70), +11.5% from $100.48.
**Bull case target: $140** (19.5x FY2027 EPS of ~$7.15), +39.3%.
**Bear case target: $82** (13.2x FY2027 EPS of ~$6.20), −18.4%.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. HSR and the Australian/Mexican clearances obtained without a material divestiture remedy, *and* CRH quantifying Arcosa synergies in its own filings at or above $175m.
2. Announcement of buyback resumption, or a stated leverage path to below 2.5x at close funded by divestitures rather than retained cash.
3. Next-year consensus stabilizing or rising for two consecutive monthly observations alongside Americas Materials organic revenue growth sustained above 3%.
4. Price toward $88-92 (13.1-13.7x $6.70), which would restore a margin of safety wide enough to absorb the bear case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. A cut to FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA or EPS guidance at the Q3 print.
2. A negative outlook or downgrade from S&P, Moody's or Fitch on the Arcosa financing, or pro forma leverage disclosed above 3.0x at close.
3. Americas Materials organic revenue turning negative again, or aggregates pricing decelerating below 3%.
4. Surface-transportation reauthorization failing to pass by year-end 2026 with only a short-term extension, combined with state lettings guidance moving lower.

**Next scheduled reassessment:** the Q3 2026 results release. Interim checkpoints: the September 4 Arcosa vote and any HSR second-request disclosure.

## Conclusion

CRH owns one of the better asset bases in global building materials — 1,334 extractive properties, 20.5% group EBITDA margins, 5-6% pricing in its core Americas products, and a moat grounded in transport economics and permitting scarcity rather than anything competitors can replicate with capital. The H1 2026 organic inflection, after a FY2025 in which every segment's organic revenue declined, is the most encouraging operating datapoint in two years, and FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed on July 30.

Against that, the company has committed $8.5bn to an acquisition at 13.5-16.5x pre-synergy EBITDA versus its own 10.4x, funded with $8.75bn of new debt in a regime where the long end of the curve is at multi-decade highs, and has suspended a buyback that was retiring roughly 3.5% of shares annually to do it. First-year incremental returns on that capital are in the low single digits against a group Adjusted ROIC that has already fallen 130bps to 12.1%. Estimates are being trimmed, one segment is contracting, and 32% residential exposure faces a subdued new-build outlook through 2027.

At 15.0x next-year consensus and 10.4x EV/EBITDA, the price already reflects most of this. Base-case upside of roughly 11% plus a 1.55% dividend against 18% downside, with the central uncertainty unresolvable before Q1 2027, is a fair-value proposition. Existing holders should stay invested and collect the dividend while the organic recovery and utility-infrastructure exposure build. New capital has a better entry available either at $88-92, or after antitrust clearance and a company-quantified synergy number remove the two largest unknowns.
