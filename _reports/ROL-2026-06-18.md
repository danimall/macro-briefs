**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rollins is the global leader in pure-play pest control, a recurring-revenue, capital-light franchise that has compounded revenue for 98 consecutive quarters and earns a 38% return on equity on minimal capital expenditure. The business itself is not impaired. The debate is narrower: whether operating margins re-inflate toward the company's reaffirmed 30–35% incremental target after Q1 2026 broke the operating-leverage story, and whether a ~36x forward earnings multiple is defensible during a CFO transition that has removed the architect of the investor-transparency story.

The stock has fallen roughly 32% from its 11 February 2026 high of $66.14 to $45.13, with the decline accelerating after the 27 May 8-K disclosing CFO Kenneth Krause's abrupt departure (effective 15 June) and a Bernstein downgrade to Market Perform with the price target cut from $70 to $52. Three linked developments explain the drawdown: Q1 2026 operating margin compressed 120bps to 16.1% as insurance, fleet, labor, and selling costs pressured the model simultaneously; the CFO who built the modernization and capital-structure narrative left for an unrelated industry only two weeks after appearing as a spokesperson in a defensive "growth algorithm" press release; and the controlling Rollins family sold 17.4M shares at $57.50 in November 2025, planting a known seller and a reference price ~22% above today's level.

We rate ROL a HOLD, a "show-me" stance rather than a conviction call in either direction. The franchise quality is real, but at this multiple the market still prices Rollins as a clean compounder while its near-term earnings growth is being carried by a falling tax rate (from purchased transferable tax credits) rather than operating leverage, and there is no insider open-market buying to signal conviction at the lows. We need either multiple compression to a level that prices the margin risk, or evidence from the new CFO that the 30–35% incremental margin target is being delivered, before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

Rollins operates ~850 company-owned and franchised locations serving 2.8M+ residential and commercial customers across roughly 70 countries. It runs as a single reportable segment across three offerings: Residential (~45% of revenue), Commercial (~33%), and Termite & Ancillary (~21%). The flagship is Orkin (acquired 1964), supported by 20+ brands including HomeTeam Pest Defense, Clark Pest Control, Fox Pest Control, Northwest Exterminating, Trutech, and Saela.

The core of the thesis is the recurring, contracted, capital-light model. Roughly 75% of revenue is recurring through scheduled service agreements, often multi-year, with 10% ancillary and 15% one-time. This produces strong forward revenue visibility and pricing power. The moat rests on scale and route density, proprietary routing and scheduling technology (the BOSS platform; InSite for commercial), the brand portfolio, and a serial acquisition machine that consolidates a fragmented market. Principal competitors are Rentokil (the global leader, which has struggled to integrate Terminix in North America), Ecolab, and Anticimex, alongside a long tail of regional and local operators in a market with low barriers to entry.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is consistent and high-quality. Q1 2026 marked the 98th consecutive quarter of revenue growth. The progression: $2.70B (2022), $3.07B (2023), $3.39B (2024), and $3.76B (2025), the last representing 11.0% growth split into 6.9% organic and 4.1% acquisitions. Within 2025, organic growth was residential +5%, commercial +8%, and termite/ancillary +10%. Net income rose from $369M in 2022 to $527M in 2025, with EPS of $1.09 (+13.5%).

Profitability and cash generation are excellent. 2025 gross margin was 52.8%, operating margin 19.3% (20.0% adjusted), net margin 14.0%, and adjusted EBITDA margin 22.7%. ROE was 38.3% and ROA 16.8%. Free cash flow of $650M (17.3% margin) converted at 123% of net income, with capex below 1% of revenue (~$28M), confirming the capital-light model.

The balance sheet is conservative, though leverage has crept up to fund M&A. Net leverage is ~0.9x adjusted EBITDAR. Long-term debt is anchored by the $500M 5.25% Senior Notes due 2035 (issued February 2025), with $164M of commercial paper outstanding at Q1 2026. The current ratio of 0.6 and Altman Z of 2.92 (grey zone) are artifacts of the prepaid-service model (a $194M unearned-revenue balance) and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet ($1.38B goodwill plus $0.57B intangibles equal ~62% of total assets), not signs of distress.

The key emerging negative sits in Q1 2026. Operating margin fell 120bps to 16.1% (16.9% adjusted, −100bps) and gross margin fell 60bps to 50.8%. The drivers stacked up: less favorable insurance/claims experience, higher selling and marketing, higher fleet costs, and rising labor as a percent of revenue. Adjusted EPS still grew 9.1% to $0.24, but the growth came from a lower tax rate (21.3% vs 23.5%, helped by purchased transferable tax credits) and a lower share count, not operating leverage. Q1 operating cash flow fell 19.4% on timing of tax and interest payments, which management expects to normalize.

On capital allocation, Rollins is dividend-forward (quarterly dividend raised to $0.1825, +10.6% YoY; ~$328M paid in 2025, +80% since 2022). There were no open-market buybacks in 2024 or 2025 despite 11.4M shares authorized and $650M of FCF; the only 2025 repurchase was a $200M buyback executed alongside the family's secondary at $57.50. With the stock now ~22% below that level and still at a premium multiple, management's continued preference for dividends and M&A over buybacks is a reasonable signal that they do not view the shares as obviously cheap.

## Growth Analysis

The medium-term framework management reaffirmed in the 14 May "growth algorithm" release is 7–8% organic growth, 2–3% M&A growth, 30–35% incremental margins, >100% FCF conversion, and double-digit EPS/FCF growth. The organic and M&A components are credible and supported by the structure of the market: a >$20B fragmented U.S. industry with secular tailwinds (urbanization, climate-driven pest range expansion, rising commercial regulatory requirements in food service and healthcare). The roll-up machine remains active, with 94 deals in three years (26 in 2025), Saela ($207M, April 2025) and Romex ($90M plus $10M earnout, April 2026) the largest recent.

The stalled component is margin. The reaffirmed incremental-margin target is precisely the line item Q1 2026 called into question, and the timing of the press release, after a margin miss and a family sell-down, reads as a defensive confidence communication rather than a routine update. Consensus estimate revisions corroborate a stall rather than an inflection: current-year EPS is $1.25 (essentially flat over 30 and 90 days, −0.3% on the 90-day look) and next-year $1.40 (−1.0% over 90 days). There is no positive revision velocity here; estimates are stable-to-slightly-soft. This is a steady compounder where the growth algorithm is being tested on the cost side, not an earnings-inflection stock where rising estimates argue for re-rating.

Quality of earnings deserves emphasis. Both 2025 and Q1 2026 EPS growth was meaningfully aided by a falling tax rate from purchased transferable federal tax credits (24.9% in 2025, 21.3% in Q1 2026) and a modestly lower share count. Adjusted operating margin was roughly flat-to-down. The tax tailwind should not be extrapolated indefinitely, and stripping it out leaves the underlying operating-leverage story looking softer than the headline EPS suggests.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.13 against trailing EPS of $1.088, ROL trades at ~41x trailing GAAP earnings and roughly 36x the $1.25 current-year consensus. Against same-sector environmental/facilities peers — Veralto at 21.3x, Republic Services at 29.8x, Waste Management at 31.7x (peer median 29.8x) — Rollins commands a clear premium. The premium is historically justified by superior organic growth, higher returns on capital, and a more capital-light model. The question is whether that premium is defensible while operating leverage has stalled and management is in transition.

We do not penalize Rollins for having fallen ~32% from its high; price action is not a valuation input. What matters is current price versus current and forward fundamentals. On that basis, the multiple still embeds compounder-quality expectations (high-30s ROE, double-digit EPS growth) at a moment when the proximate evidence — margin compression, tax-aided EPS, flat estimate revisions — argues those expectations carry more risk than the multiple acknowledges. A 36x forward multiple on a business growing EPS in the low double-digits implies a PEG well above 2.5, and the analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply: revisions are not rising, and at a premium multiple consensus targets tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation.

The analyst price-target band is wide: low $50, median $66.5, high $72, mean $62.9. The Bernstein bear case at $52 cites the CFO departure clouding the margin path; the upper end reflects a clean return to the growth algorithm. The dispersion itself signals genuine uncertainty about which scenario prevails. The ~1.56% dividend yield offers modest absolute support and sits above the peer median of 1.2%, yet remains thin enough that it does not anchor the stock the way a 3%+ yield would. We score valuation at 5.0: the premium is not absurd for the franchise, but it offers no asymmetric upside today and embeds margin recovery that is not yet evidenced.

## Competitive Landscape

Rollins' competitive position remains strong and is best described as stable rather than improving. Scale and route density are genuine cost advantages over the fragmented local long tail, and the recurring-contract base creates switching inertia. Against Rentokil, Ecolab, and Anticimex, Rollins has historically been the most consistent organic grower and the highest-return operator, and Rentokil's North American integration difficulties have, if anything, widened Rollins' execution advantage.

The competitive risk is less about share loss than about margin. The same tight technician labor market, fleet and insurance inflation, and rising customer-acquisition costs that pressured Q1 2026 are industry-wide cost dynamics that compress the economics of the model. The FTC consent order restricting non-compete enforcement, while immaterial financially, removes one talent-retention tool in a tight labor market. We trim competitive position to 7.0 from the prior 7.0/8.5 trajectory: the franchise advantage is intact, but the trajectory is no longer clearly improving when the cost side is eroding incremental returns.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin compression** is now front-of-mind. Multiple cost lines pressured margins at once in Q1 2026, and the reaffirmed incremental-margin target is being tested in real time. This is the central swing variable for the stock.

**Controlling-shareholder overhang.** The Rollins family held ~38% at year-end 2025, down after the November 2025 secondary. The 10-K explicitly warns that future family sell-downs could destabilize the stock and that minority holders have limited effective voice. With the stock below the $57.50 offering price, the November buyers are underwater, and the family remains a potential future seller.

**Management transition.** The CFO departure is the pivotal recent event. Krause, credited with the capital-structure optimization and improved investor transparency, left abruptly for an unrelated industry. His successor, William Harkins (former Mohawk CAO, joined Rollins as CAO March 2025), is an accounting-heavy internal appointment. The abruptness — Krause was a quoted spokesperson two weeks before announcing his exit — is what most spooked the market.

**Insurance/self-retention.** Rollins self-insures auto, workers' comp, and general liability on a high-deductible basis ($84.6M in letters of credit). Reserves are actuarial estimates, and Q1 2026 already saw less favorable claims experience against a ~22,000-employee field fleet.

**Acquisition/goodwill.** The 94 deals in three years leave goodwill at ~44% of assets; underperformance could trigger impairment. Seasonality and weather remain recurring swing factors, as both Q4 2025 and early Q1 2026 demonstrated.

**Governance watch.** Cumulative indexed TSR of 163 trails the peer group's 190 even as CEO "compensation actually paid" spiked to $15.3M, and there is no open-market insider buying at the lows.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 25.7% sits roughly in line with one-year historical volatility of 24.7% (a 1.0% spread, fair value), and IV is in the 3rd percentile of its own chain; the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst-driven move. The 1-month put/call volume ratio of 4.10 and a 4.7% put skew indicate elevated near-term put demand, consistent with hedging into the leadership transition, though the 6-month skew flips negative (−3.0%) and the term structure is in contango, a complacent rather than alarmed structure. The reported jump in ATM IV to anomalous levels in the delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Net read: the options market reflects near-term caution without pricing a binary event, which fits the "show-me" fundamental stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($66–72)

The franchise is unimpaired, supported by a fragmented market that sustains both 7–8% organic growth and a steady tuck-in M&A pipeline. Q1 2026 margin pressure proves transitory: insurance/claims normalize, fleet and S&M costs are absorbed as the year progresses, and incremental margins return to the 30–35% range. The new CFO re-establishes guidance credibility within two to three quarters, the family overhang clears, and the stock re-rates back toward its historical premium as a clean compounder. At 40x a recovering $1.65–1.75 forward EPS, the stock returns to the high-$60s to low-$70s.

### Bear Case ($46–52)

Margin compression proves structural rather than cyclical: technician labor inflation, fleet and insurance costs, and rising customer-acquisition spend permanently lower incremental margins below the target, breaking the operating-leverage narrative that justified the premium. EPS growth, stripped of the tax-credit tailwind, decelerates toward high-single digits, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (~30x) on a lower earnings base. The family resumes selling, and the new CFO's accounting-heavy profile fails to restore the growth-story credibility Krause provided. The stock settles in the high-$40s to low-$50s, validating the Bernstein $52 target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), the first full quarter of margin data under the incoming CFO and the cleanest test of whether Q1's compression was transitory.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating margin stabilizes or re-expands toward 30–35% incremental margins, demonstrating the Q1 compression was cyclical, with the recovery visible *ex* the tax-credit benefit.
- The new CFO reaffirms and begins delivering against the growth algorithm, accompanied by positive consensus estimate revisions (current-year EPS revised up >3%).
- Multiple compression to ~28–30x forward (low-$40s on current estimates) that prices the margin risk and restores asymmetric upside, ideally with clustered open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of operating-margin compression confirming structural rather than cyclical pressure.
- A guidance cut to the organic-growth or incremental-margin targets, or a goodwill impairment charge.
- Renewed family selling that signals a larger overhang, or further senior-management departures.

## Conclusion

Rollins is a high-quality, capital-light compounder that has stumbled into a credibility gap. The business mechanics — recurring revenue, route-density moat, 38% ROE, 123% FCF conversion — remain intact, and the drawdown has genuinely improved the entry point relative to February. But the decline is grounded in real developments: Q1 margin compression that broke the operating-leverage story, tax-aided rather than organically-driven EPS growth, an abrupt CFO departure that removed the investor-transparency architect, and a controlling-family overhang with an underwater reference price. At a premium forward multiple with flat estimate revisions and no insider conviction-buying at the lows, the multiple still prices a clean compounder while the near-term evidence argues for caution.

We rate ROL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The franchise could re-rate higher if margins recover and the new CFO restores credibility, or it could grind lower toward the peer multiple if the cost pressure proves structural. Neither outcome has enough evidence behind it today to justify a directional call. We want the first clean post-transition margin print before adding, and would turn more constructive on either a margin recovery confirmation or a further multiple compression toward the high-$30s/x range.
