**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lantheus is the leading U.S. radiopharmaceutical-focused diagnostics company, built around three franchises: prostate-cancer PSMA PET imaging (PYLARIFY, the majority of revenue), echocardiography contrast (DEFINITY, >80% U.S. share), and an expanding Alzheimer's/neurology diagnostic portfolio (Neuraceq plus a pipeline of tau/amyloid agents). The investment case turns on a single tension: the flagship product that generated the company's outperformance is now shrinking — management guides PYLARIFY net revenue to decline 8–10% in 2026 — while the company simultaneously spends ~$906M of upfront cash on acquisitions to diversify away from that concentration before the erosion accelerates. Whether the new franchises backfill the PYLARIFY decline fast enough is the central question.

The near-term fundamentals are not deteriorating in the way the price action of 2025 implied. Q1 2026 revenue was $377.3M (+8.6% ex-SPECT), adjusted EPS of $1.46 beat consensus by roughly 16%, gross margin held at 67.0%, and full-year guidance was reaffirmed. The stock has recovered from a 2025 trough near $47 back to $108, a ~46% move over the trailing year and a full round-trip to its 52-week high. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (current-year EPS +3.4% over 90 days, next-year +0.9%), which reads as stabilization rather than the +30% velocity that would flag a decisive buy signal.

We rate LNTH a HOLD. The valuation is reasonable at ~20x guided 2026 EPS and well below the (distorted) health-care-supplies peer median, but there is no independent catalyst that makes cheapness a buy rather than fair value for a business with a declining core product, an unfilled CEO seat, a live securities class action, and a May 2026 exclusivity cliff. The options market is pricing a ±15.7% near-term move with rich, backwardated implied vol, which indicates event risk over the next several weeks. We prefer to wait for resolution of the TruVu reimbursement decision and the August 13 MK-6240 PDUFA before changing our view.

## Company Overview

Lantheus (founded 1956 as New England Nuclear; public since 2015) manufactures and distributes diagnostic and therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals and imaging agents. The portfolio is organized into three reporting categories:

- **Radiopharmaceutical Oncology** — dominated by PYLARIFY (piflufolastat F-18), an F-18 PSMA-targeted PET agent for prostate cancer launched June 2021, which crossed $1B in revenue in 2024. Its distribution moat rests on a national network of PET manufacturing facilities that manage the 110-minute half-life logistics of F-18, which is genuinely hard to replicate.
- **Precision Diagnostics** — DEFINITY (ultrasound-enhancing microbubble for echocardiography, the most-used agent of its kind in the U.S.) and Neuraceq (F-18 amyloid PET for Alzheimer's, acquired July 2025 via Life Molecular).
- **Strategic Partnerships & Other** — royalties on flurpiridaz/Flyrcado (out-licensed to GE HealthCare, which launched April 2025), RELISTOR milestones, AI software (aPROMISE, aBSI), and a new CDMO business from the Evergreen acquisition.

The company reshaped itself materially in 2025–26: it acquired Evergreen Theragnostics (April 2025, $276.4M upfront + up to $727.5M milestones) and Life Molecular Imaging (July 2025, $352.9M upfront + up to $400M earnouts), and divested its legacy SPECT business to SHINE (closed January 2026, up to $155M). The strategic direction is a retrenchment toward PET radiodiagnostics and microbubbles, with management explicitly exploring "value-maximizing alternatives" for its radiotherapeutics assets after shelving the late-stage PNT2002 (SPLASH) program.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has plateaued.** FY2025 revenue was ~$1.542B, essentially flat versus $1.534B in 2024 after several years of growth. The stall reflects PYLARIFY price erosion, which is the leading edge of the concern rather than a one-off.

**Margins compressed.** Operating income fell from ~$457M (2024) to ~$311M (2025), and net income declined from $312M to $234M. The drivers were PYLARIFY pricing, a step-up in R&D ($168M in 2024 to $177M in 2025, up from $77.7M in 2023), and acquisition/integration costs. The business is still profitable, but the direction of both revenue and operating margin turned down in 2025.

**The balance sheet was transformed by M&A.** Goodwill rose from ~$61M to ~$240M and intangibles from ~$162M to ~$723M in 2025. Cash fell from ~$913M to ~$359M after funding the upfront deal consideration. Leverage remains modest and solvency is not in question, but the large contingent earnouts (up to $400M on Life Molecular, $727.5M on Evergreen) create a future call on capital that constrains flexibility. There is no dividend.

**Guidance points down.** Management guides 2026 worldwide revenue of $1.4–1.45B — a decline from 2025, partly attributable to the SPECT divestiture — and adjusted EPS of $5.00–$5.25.

**The beat/miss record is uneven.** Over the last four quarters, Lantheus posted two beats (Q1 2026 +15.9%, Q4 2025 +40.2%) and two misses (Q3 2025 -1.5%, Q2 2025 -8.0%). The pattern argues for moderate rather than high confidence in forward consensus. The Q1 2026 beat ($1.46 adjusted EPS against a $1.26 estimate) sits against a full-year guide that still implies a step-down.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a race between decline and diversification. On the decline side, PYLARIFY, the majority of revenue and the highest-margin product, faces generic/follow-on entry risk after its 5-year New Chemical Entity exclusivity expires May 26, 2026, and a structural reimbursement disadvantage (detailed below) versus competitors.

On the growth side, three levers matter:
1. **Neurology/Alzheimer's diagnostics** — Neuraceq (amyloid PET) plus a pipeline of MK-6240 (tau PET, PDUFA August 13, 2026) and NAV-4694 (amyloid). As anti-amyloid Alzheimer's therapies scale and require PET confirmation, this is a genuine secular tailwind, but the market is early and reimbursement economics are unproven for these agents.
2. **Flyrcado royalties** — GE HealthCare's cardiac PET agent launched April 2025; Lantheus collects milestones and royalties. This is a clean, capital-light revenue stream ramping off a partner's commercial engine.
3. **PYLARIFY TruVu** — the March 2026 approved enhanced formulation, for which Lantheus intends to seek 3 years of fresh Transitional Pass-Through (TPT) status, is the defensive lever to reset the flagship's reimbursement and supply economics.

Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-positive, with 30-day moves marginally negative. This does not meet the threshold for an earnings-inflection buy signal and reads as consensus settling around a stabilizing but low-growth profile. Growth prospects rate a below-average 5.3: the tailwinds are real but the flagship decline and the unproven pace of backfill keep this a show-me story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108, LNTH trades at roughly 20x the midpoint of guided 2026 adjusted EPS and ~17x consensus next-year EPS of $6.35. Against the reported peer set in Health Care Supplies (MMSI 28.8x, NVST 60.1x, ICUI 77.1x, HAE 38.9x; median 60.1x), LNTH looks deeply discounted, but that peer median is distorted by trough-earnings multiples at NVST and ICUI and a negative print at XRAY, so it is not a clean comparable. On its own merits, ~20x forward for a company whose largest product is shrinking is neither cheap nor stretched; it is fair for the risk profile.

The equity lessons from this system's own history apply here: a compressed multiple is not a buy signal absent a specific catalyst, and cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive. There is a plausible catalyst path (TruVu TPT approval, MK-6240 launch, therapeutics divestiture crystallizing value), but none is confirmed, and the near-term guidance points down. We therefore treat the valuation as roughly fair rather than mispriced — a 5.2.

Analyst targets bracket the current price closely: low $94, mean $111.58, median $110, high $129. With the stock at $108, consensus sees limited upside to the median and modest downside to the low. That tight dispersion around spot is consistent with a fairly-valued, event-dependent name rather than an asymmetric opportunity. We would not treat these targets as stale floors, because this is a fairly-valued name with flat estimates and a declining core product rather than a low-multiple inflection stock with rising estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

Lantheus holds two genuinely strong positions: PSMA PET imaging (PYLARIFY) and echo microbubbles (DEFINITY, >80% U.S. share, patent method-of-use to 2037). The PMF distribution network for short-half-life isotopes is a durable structural advantage. But the trajectory of the competitive position is deteriorating at the flagship even as it improves at the periphery.

Three forces are pressing:
- **Reimbursement disadvantage.** PYLARIFY's CMS Transitional Pass-Through status expired December 31, 2024, moving it to a mean-unit-cost (MUC) reimbursement basis that Lantheus argues understates true cost. Several competing PSMA agents retain TPT status and are paid at the more favorable ASP+6% — a structural handicap on Medicare outpatient economics. CMS declined to shift to ASP in the 2026 OPPS rule. Neuraceq faces the same MUC disadvantage.
- **Intensifying competition.** PYLARIFY competes with Telix, Blue Earth (Bracco/Novartis), and emerging Ga-68 and Cu-64 PSMA tracers. DEFINITY faces GE HealthCare and Bracco and future generics. Neuraceq competes with Lilly and GE amyloid agents.
- **Better-resourced rivals.** The industry is consolidating around Novartis/AAA, Lilly/POINT, BMS/RayzeBio, GE HealthCare, Bracco, Telix, and Curium — all larger and more diversified than a mid-cap pure-play.

The competitive-position score of 5.3 reflects this split: leadership in two niches, but with the largest niche facing exclusivity loss and a reimbursement penalty its competitors do not share.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and partly binary, which is why this dimension scores below neutral at 4.5.

1. **PYLARIFY concentration + exclusivity cliff.** The majority of revenue sits in one product whose NCE exclusivity expired May 26, 2026. Composition-of-matter patents run to 2037 (with potential Hatch-Waxman 30-month stays), but ANDA/505(b)(2) entry risk is now live.
2. **Reimbursement.** The MUC-vs-ASP disadvantage is a persistent structural drag, and the TruVu TPT decision is the key unresolved lever.
3. **Litigation.** A securities class action (Margolis/Indiana PRS) tied to 2025 PYLARIFY guidance disclosures is active. Separately, Novartis (AAA) brought a Hatch-Waxman suit over Lantheus's PNT2003 ANDA; trial was held December 2025 with a decision pending — a binary outcome.
4. **Drug-pricing policy.** Radiopharmaceuticals are not excluded from Medicare price negotiation; Part B eligibility begins 2028 with identification around 2026. OBBBA Medicaid changes and most-favored-nation pricing concepts add pressure.
5. **Governance.** CEO Brian Markison retired December 31, 2025; former board chair Mary Anne Heino is interim CEO, and the search for a permanent CEO is unresolved.
6. **Integration and pipeline attrition.** Two large 2025 acquisitions carry hundreds of millions in contingent milestones, and the abandonment of PNT2002 (SPLASH) is a reminder that late-stage programs fail.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 64.1% versus 49.9% trailing realized — rich by a 14.1-point spread and in the 89th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (64.1% near-term vs. 51.8% at six months), which prices an imminent event. The nearest expiry implies a ±15.7% move. Put/call skew of 3.6% shows modestly elevated put demand, and ATM IV has jumped from 30.7% to 64.1% since May while price rose 12%; rising vol on a rising price signals the market anticipating a catalyst rather than complacency. This is consistent with the August MK-6240 PDUFA and the TruVu/TPT reimbursement path, and it argues against paying up at the current level ahead of those resolutions. Short interest at 12.9% of float (6.3 days to cover) reflects genuine institutional skepticism about the PYLARIFY trajectory.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$129).** TruVu secures fresh 3-year TPT status, resetting PYLARIFY's reimbursement economics and blunting the generic threat; the PYLARIFY decline is milder than the guided 8–10%. MK-6240 approves in August and the Alzheimer's diagnostic market scales faster than expected as anti-amyloid therapies drive PET confirmation demand. Flyrcado royalties ramp materially. The company monetizes its radiotherapeutics portfolio at an attractive value. A permanent CEO is named, removing the governance overhang. On these outcomes, the market re-rates toward the high analyst target of $129, ~20% above current price.

**Bear Case (~$80).** Generic/follow-on PSMA entry accelerates PYLARIFY erosion beyond guidance; the TruVu TPT bid fails and the MUC disadvantage persists. MK-6240 disappoints or the Alzheimer's diagnostic market monetizes slowly. Contingent earnouts and integration costs weigh on FCF. The securities class action or the Novartis patent decision surprises negatively. The leadership vacuum lingers. On these outcomes the stock retraces toward the low-$80s, roughly 25% below current price and above the 2025 trough given the diversified base now in place.

**Base Case (~$110).** PYLARIFY declines roughly in line with guidance while neurology and Flyrcado partially backfill; adjusted EPS lands near the guide with next-year consensus around $6.35. The stock holds a ~17–20x forward multiple and trades roughly in line with the analyst median of $110 — flat-to-modestly-higher from current levels, dependent on catalyst outcomes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as **uncertain** — the setup is genuinely two-sided and the near-term binary events (TruVu reimbursement, August PDUFA, patent decision, CEO search) prevent a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CMS grants TruVu fresh TPT status (ASP+6% reimbursement restored), materially improving PYLARIFY's forward economics.
- MK-6240 approval on/around the August 13, 2026 PDUFA with a credible Alzheimer's-diagnostic ramp, plus estimate revisions turning decisively positive (+10%+ over a quarter).
- Announcement of a permanent CEO with a clear capital-allocation mandate, and/or a value-crystallizing divestiture of the radiotherapeutics portfolio.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence PYLARIFY erosion is running materially worse than the guided 8–10% (e.g., accelerated generic launch, a subsequent quarter guiding PYLARIFY down double digits).
- Adverse Novartis patent decision or a materially adverse turn in the securities class action.
- A guidance cut on 2026 revenue or EPS, or a break of the $80 area on fundamental (not sentiment) deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release and the CMS TruVu reimbursement determination, followed by the August 13, 2026 MK-6240 PDUFA. We would revisit the rating on any of these.

## Conclusion

Lantheus is a fundamentally healthy, profitable radiopharmaceutical franchise navigating a deliberate and expensive pivot away from a declining flagship. The Q1 beat and TruVu approval were real positives, and the equity has recovered fully from its 2025 trough, but the recovery reflects sentiment repair and the diversification story rather than an earnings inflection — estimate revisions are flat and 2026 guidance points down. The valuation at ~20x forward is fair, not compelling, and cheapness alone is not a reason to buy when the core product is shrinking and multiple binary events sit within the next quarter. The rich, backwardated implied vol and the short interest corroborate the event risk. We stay on the sidelines pending the TruVu reimbursement decision and the August PDUFA, either of which could resolve the thesis in a direction that justifies a change of view. HOLD.
