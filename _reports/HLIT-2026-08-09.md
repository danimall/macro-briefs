## Executive Summary

Harmonic completed a structural reset on June 17, 2026, closing the $145M cash sale of its Video business to MediaKind and leaving behind a single-segment broadband access company built around cOS, a virtualized CMTS that runs on commercial off-the-shelf servers, plus Remote PHY/MAC-PHY nodes and a growing fiber-to-the-home line. Nearly every trailing metric on a standard screen describes a different company: reported trailing P/E of 156x reflects FY2025 continuing-operations net income of roughly $1.3M and a $57.5M non-cash goodwill impairment on the divested unit, neither of which characterizes the entity trading today.

On forward numbers the picture is more defensible and considerably less exciting than the headline growth rate suggests. FY2026 guidance of $475–495M revenue represents +32% to +37% growth, but the $485M midpoint sits 0.7% *below* FY2024 continuing-operations revenue of $488.2M — a two-year CAGR of about −0.3%. The intervening FY2025 collapse (−26.2%, driven by a $148.0M decline in US appliance revenue on one tier-1 operator's DOCSIS 4.0 timing) was an air pocket, and FY2026 is the refill. At $11.84 the stock trades at 19.1x guided non-GAAP EPS ($0.62 mid), 29.2x guided GAAP EPS ($0.405 mid), and roughly 2.4x pro forma EV/revenue after netting an estimated $140M of post-close net cash. That is not expensive for a business with 50% gross margins and $582.1M of backlog and deferred revenue, of which the disclosed 60%-within-twelve-months portion (~$349M) covers approximately 96% of guided Q2–Q4 revenue.

The offsetting evidence is specific rather than generic. Management's own guide implies second-half gross margin of roughly 49% against Q1's actual 52.3%, because low-margin outside-plant and professional services scaled from $0.175M to $12.1M year over year in a single quarter. The Q1 10-Q added new language on AI-driven component demand and pricing — a direct threat to a vendor whose entire cost advantage is buying commodity servers into a market where DRAM is scarce and pricing above prior peaks. One customer was 54% of FY2025 revenue and the top two were 58% of Q1 FY2026 revenue; concentration percentages improved while absolute dollars from those two accounts grew 26%. Q1 book-to-bill was 0.95x after a 3.53x fourth quarter. And $140M of cash sits with no disclosed use against an M&A record that includes a fully impaired 2016 video acquisition. Q2 results land August 12 with options implying a ±21% move. We rate HLIT HOLD with a $14.00 base case, $18.00 bull and $8.50 bear.

## Company Overview

Post-divestiture Harmonic sells into one end market: broadband access infrastructure for cable and fiber operators. Revenue is reported as "Appliance and integration" (hardware plus integration and professional services) and "SaaS and service" (recurring software, support, subscription). The commercial argument is that a software-defined CMTS on commodity x86 servers, paired with distributed access architectures that push intelligence out to the node, is cheaper and more flexible than purpose-built chassis from CommScope, Cisco, Nokia, Vecima, Vantiva and ATX. The disclosed traction supports the argument: 150 cOS customers serving 45.7 million cable modems, expansion across all tier-1 accounts, and additional DOCSIS 4.0 wins in Q1 FY2026. Named customers include Comcast, Charter, Cable One, Astound, Mediacom, GCI and Midcontinent domestically, plus América Móvil, Millicom, Vodafone, Tele2, Telia Norge, DNA and Telecentro internationally.

The company is asset-light and R&D-heavy: FY2025 capex was $11.1M company-wide against R&D of $76.3M (21.2% of continuing revenue). Manufacturing is outsourced entirely to Plexus in Malaysia under an agreement auto-renewed only through October 2026. Headcount is roughly 520–535, of whom 198 (38%) are in Israel, where core R&D and the CEO are based; additional engineering is outsourced through GlobalLogic including engineers in Kyiv. Geographic revenue is 89% Americas (FY2025 continuing ops: $320.6M of $360.5M).

The asset purchase agreement includes a three-year covenant not to compete with the Video business, locking Harmonic out of video delivery until roughly June 2029. The broadband bet is now irreversible in the medium term.

## Financial Analysis

**The trailing decline is smaller than it looks, and the FY2025 trough was real.** Continuing-operations revenue went $388.5M (FY2023) → $488.2M (FY2024) → $360.5M (FY2025); the $210.3M of FY2025 Video revenue moved below the line. So the apparent fall from $678.7M to $360.5M is a 26.2% decline in the retained business plus reclassification, not a 47% collapse. The cause is documented in MD&A: Americas revenue fell $128.8M, driven by a $148.0M decline in US appliance and integration revenue attributed to customer deployment timing delays around DOCSIS 4.0 and network readiness, partially offset by $19.2M of Latin American FTTH growth.

**Operating leverage cuts both ways.** Harmonic held R&D and SG&A roughly flat in dollars through the 26% decline ($72.6M → $76.3M and $79.2M → $81.4M), preserving capability while operating margin fell from 15.3% to 3.9% (operating income $74.7M → $14.1M). The FY2025 continuing-operations effective tax rate was approximately 85% ($7.6M on $8.9M pretax), leaving roughly $1.3M of continuing net income — corroborated by the proxy pay-versus-performance net income figure of $1,216,000. Despite a $2.11B accumulated deficit, FY2026 is guided at a 33.0% GAAP / 24.5% non-GAAP rate, with full valuation allowances already against California ($32.7M gross) and Swiss deferred tax assets. The historical losses are not an efficient shield; jurisdictional mix and repatriation friction on $46.2M of offshore cash are a persistent drag on cash conversion.

**Q1 FY2026 was a large volume beat with adverse mix.** Revenue of $121.7M was +43.4% year over year and well above the ~$100–105M guided in February; non-GAAP EPS of $0.17 beat a $0.11–0.12 guide. But gross margin of 52.3% was 250bp below the prior-year quarter and roughly 200–250bp below guidance, because professional services within Appliance & integration went from $0.175M to $12.1M (outside-plant services +$7.5M). GAAP continuing EPS was $0.09 per the 10-Q, revised from $0.10 in the preliminary release via a ~$1.58M reallocation of tax expense from discontinued to continuing operations; total net income was identical at $7.309M in both. That reallocation is mechanically ordinary in held-for-sale structures, but $0.09 is the correct anchor.

**Cash flow: FY2025 flattered, Q1 FY2026 normal.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $107.97M less $11.1M capex gave $96.9M of FCF, but that figure spans continuing and discontinued operations and was driven by a receivables unwind — AR fell from roughly $178M to $85.9M as the very large Q4 FY2024 shipments were collected, contributing +$79.6M in Q1 FY2025 alone — plus $14.5M lower cash tax payments and the $57.5M non-cash impairment add-back. Against total FY2025 revenue of $570.8M, the FCF margin was 17.0%, not the ~26.9% a screen computes by dividing total-company FCF by continuing-operations revenue. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $31.7M versus $83.6M confirms the normalization (AR contributed +$3.2M versus +$79.6M), and that $31.7M itself includes a +$10.1M working-capital contribution and essentially zero cash tax. A normalized FY2026 build — guided GAAP net income $40–50M, plus ~$23M SBC and ~$10M depreciation, less ~$8–10M capex, working capital neutral — points to roughly $63–73M of FCF, a 4.9%–5.7% yield on market capitalization and 5.5%–6.4% on pro forma EV. That is the defensible base, not the FY2025 headline.

**Balance sheet is the clearest strength.** At April 3, 2026: cash $109.0M, total debt $111.5M ($75M revolver + $36.5M term loan), net debt ~$2.5M, current ratio ~2.3–2.5x, $82.0M of undrawn revolver, covenant compliant (max net leverage, min fixed charge coverage), and no maturity before December 21, 2028. Adding $145M of gross proceeds against ~$2.5M of net debt implies pro forma net cash on the order of $140M, or about $1.29 per share and 10.9% of the current price — an approximation, since the working-capital/cash/debt/selling-expense adjustment, transaction taxes and residual advisory fees are undisclosed. Stockholders' equity was $355.2M, including a $102.1M net deferred tax asset equal to 14.5% of total assets whose realizability depends on sustained jurisdictional profitability.

**Capital returns have been material.** Against a $200M authorization through February 2028, roughly $79M was deployed in FY2025 and $43.0M in Q1 FY2026 (4.22M shares at an average of about $10.19, 14% below the current price), leaving $78.0M — 6.1% of market capitalization. Shares outstanding went 116.7M → 111.2M → 108.5M, a 7.1% reduction in 15 months. Diluted weighted-average shares were 110.6M in Q1; FY2026 guidance assumes 110.0M at a $9.91 assumed stock price, so at $11.84 actual diluted shares likely run modestly higher — a small headwind to guided per-share figures. Outstanding RSUs of 3.99M are roughly 3.7% of the current base (about 3.5% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully vested); no warrants or earn-outs are disclosed.

Financial health scores 7.0: near-zero net debt moving to net cash, no maturity wall, undrawn liquidity, asset-light capex, and real if overstated cash generation. The offsets — high structural tax rate, a large DTA whose value depends on continued profits, single-source manufacturing with an October 2026 renewal, and a history of destroyed capital in the accumulated deficit — keep it out of the 8s.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is +32% to +37% revenue growth, which sounds like an inflection and is better understood as recovery. Three derived observations frame it:

**The midpoint is a round trip.** $485M versus FY2024's $488.2M is a −0.3% two-year CAGR. Guided GAAP operating margin of 13.5%–15.8% brackets FY2024's actual 15.3%, but FY2024 carried a $10.9M lease impairment; cleaned up, FY2024 operating margin was ~17.5%. FY2026 guided GAAP opex of $173–176M is 12%–14% above FY2024's $154.5M ex-impairment. About $10M of that gap is stranded cost from the divestiture that should fade, and $2.3M is tariff cost in COGS; the remainder is real cost growth on flat revenue.

**The guide embeds a sequential plateau and a second-half margin step-down.** Q2 is guided to $115–125M, a $120M midpoint *below* Q1's $121.7M actual. At the full-year midpoint, implied H2 revenue is $243.3M against H1's ~$241.7M — flat. On margin: FY gross profit midpoint ~$245.5M less Q1's $63.6M less Q2's implied ~$63.0M leaves ~$118.9M on ~$243.3M, an implied H2 gross margin near 49% versus Q1's 52.3%. Management is telling investors that continued mix shift into outside-plant services plus tariffs costs roughly 340bp. The alternative reading is conservatism in an unusually wide annual range, supported by an 18% Q1 revenue beat.

**Backlog coverage is high but the leading indicator turned.** RPO at April 3 was $582.1M with 60% expected within twelve months (~$349M) against $363.3M of guided Q2–Q4 revenue — roughly 96% coverage, though over a window that does not align exactly with the fiscal periods and using a metric that is not total backlog (the 10-K noted only ~53% of the year-end $573.8M "backlog and deferred revenue" was expected to convert within a rolling year). More telling: bookings were $72.9M in Q1 FY2025, $346.9M in Q4 FY2025, and $115.9M in Q1 FY2026. Book-to-bill went from 3.53x to 0.95x, and backlog grew only $8.3M in Q1 despite record revenue. Infrastructure frame agreements are lumpy and one sub-1.0 quarter after a 3.5x quarter is not evidence of demand weakness, but bookings — not backlog — is the metric that matters from here.

The genuinely encouraging diversification datapoints: fiber products exceeded 14% of trailing Appliance & integration revenue with multiple international wins, Latin American FTTH added $19.2M in FY2025, and Rest-of-Market revenue grew from $28.4M to $50.6M (+78%). Consensus estimate revisions are mildly supportive — next-year EPS $0.78 is +7.1% over 90 days, and the current-quarter $0.17 matches the Q2 non-GAAP guide midpoint — though the reported +40.4% current-quarter and −42.0% next-quarter 90-day moves are largely period-roll artifacts and the current-year figure of $0.38 is only covered by three analysts on an ambiguous basis. This does not constitute the kind of clean, large-magnitude upward revision cycle that signals systematic underestimation.

Growth prospects score 6.0, down from the prior cycle's 7.5. The downgrade is not about the FY2026 number, which is on track and backlog-covered; it is that FY2026 restores rather than extends, the guide itself implies a flat sequential second half at lower margin, and there is no disclosed visibility on whether $485M is a plateau or a waypoint.

Valuation scores 6.2. ## Competitive Landscape

The DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade cycle is a real, multi-year capex program driven by HFC operators defending against fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless. Harmonic's position within it looks strong on adoption metrics — 150 cOS customers, 45.7M modems, presence in every tier-1 account — and was likely helped by Casa Systems' 2024 Chapter 11 (externally reported, not verified in a primary document here), which removed a direct vCMTS competitor.

Pricing power is the weaker part of the story. Gross margin ranged 45.9%–49.2% across FY2023–FY2025 and is guided to ~50% in FY2026: respectable for communications equipment, not software-like, and currently trending the wrong way as deployment labor scales. The IP position is modest (65 issued US patents, 39 pending). The moat rests on incumbency inside operator networks and multi-year deployment lock-in rather than defensible technology.

FTTH is the most important competitive development and the most double-edged. It hedges the existential question of whether HFC survives — Harmonic can sell into either architecture — but it puts the company into a fiber access market against Nokia, Adtran, Calix and Chinese vendors where its cable incumbency does not transfer. Q1 mix actually shifted *toward* the Americas (international fell from 27% to 20% of revenue) even as international fiber wins were announced, meaning the US DOCSIS ramp is currently outgrowing fiber in absolute dollars.

Competitive position scores 5.8: share is being won in the current cycle and the customer base is broadening in percentage terms, but margin trajectory, patent breadth and the single-end-market structure argue against a wide-moat characterization.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (highest materiality).** One customer was 54% of FY2025 continuing revenue; top ten were 84%. In Q1 FY2026 the top two were 58% of revenue ($71.1M) versus 67% ($56.5M) prior year — the *share* improved nine points while the *dollars* grew 26%. The FY2025 experience is the proof: one tier-1 operator's schedule change removed $148.0M of US appliance revenue in a single year. Rest-of-Market dollar growth sustained through a top-customer downcycle is the metric that would demonstrate reduced fragility, and it is untested.

**Component cost inflation.** The 10-Q's newly added AI-driven component demand and pricing language, against a business model built on commodity servers, is the most underappreciated margin threat and directly compounds the mix-driven H2 compression already in guidance.

**Geopolitical concentration.** 198 employees (38%) and core R&D in Israel, with the CEO based there; the Q1 10-Q added "Iran war" to its geopolitical risk enumeration. Additional outsourced engineering sits in Kyiv.

**Supply chain.** Plexus in Malaysia is the sole contract manufacturer under an agreement auto-renewed only through October 2026, with $76.6M of purchase commitments outstanding.

**Tariffs, two-sided.** Guidance embeds ~$2.3M of tariff cost; the 10-Q notes certain tariff actions were invalidated by subsequent Supreme Court determinations with refund timing and availability uncertain — an unquantified potential credit.

**Capital allocation.** $140M of undisclosed-use cash, a three-year non-compete that eliminates video optionality, and a single end market create a plausible strategic rationale for acquisition. The 2016 Thomson Video Networks purchase, whose goodwill was ultimately impaired and whose legacy produced a (since-closed) OFAC subpoena, is the cautionary precedent. This is the primary downside scenario for the cash.

**Deferred tax asset.** $102.1M net DTA, 14.5% of total assets, with valuation allowances already against California and Swiss attributes. A return to losses would put a materially larger write-down in play.

Note that screening outputs (Altman Z ≈ 2.49 grey zone, weak Piotroski) are mechanically depressed by the $57.5M non-cash disposal impairment and the $2.11B accumulated deficit. Direct solvency evidence — net cash, 2.3–2.5x current ratio, $82M undrawn revolver, no maturity before December 2028 — indicates low near-term distress risk. The accumulated deficit does, however, reflect real destroyed capital and is relevant history for any future acquisition.

Risk factors score 4.2. Several of these are specific, dated and not obviously priced: the August 12 print, the October 2026 Plexus renewal, and the undisclosed use of proceeds.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.00 (+52%)

Q2 delivers within or above the $115–125M revenue and 52.0%–53.0% gross margin guide, demonstrating that the H2 margin step-down embedded in the annual guide was conservatism rather than forecast. DOCSIS 4.0 volumes from the tier-1 base continue through FY2027, fiber compounds off a 14%-of-A&I base with international wins converting to revenue, and stranded costs of ~$10M plus the $4.6M one-off foreign withholding charge fall away, lifting FY2027 non-GAAP EPS toward $0.75–0.80 against consensus $0.78. Management applies the $145M plus remaining $78M authorization to buybacks rather than acquisitions, taking share count below 105M. At 24x $0.75, the shares are worth $18.00; the 52-week high of $17.68 and the $20 high analyst target bracket the same zone. The four-quarter beat record (79%, 39%, 160%, 231% surprises) supports the view that guidance is set to be exceeded.

### Bear Case — $8.50 (−28%)

Component cost inflation and services mix take FY2026 gross margin to the low end or below, and the implied ~49% second half proves optimistic. Non-GAAP EPS lands near $0.45–0.50. Simultaneously, bookings stay below 1.0x book-to-bill as the Q4 FY2025 frame agreement is drawn down, making the record backlog a depleting asset rather than a growing one, and investors mark the business back toward a $460–480M plateau with mid-teens operating margin. At 17x $0.48 plus $1.29 of net cash, the shares are worth roughly $9.45; a repeat of the FY2025 tier-1 pause, or a large acquisition at a full price, takes them to the $8.00 low analyst target and toward the 52-week low of $8.465. Use $8.50.

### Base Case — $14.00 (+18%)

Q2 lands in guidance, the second half comes in between Q1's 52.3% and the implied 49% — call it 50.5% — and FY2026 non-GAAP EPS finishes near the $0.62 midpoint with FY2027 at $0.70. Applying 13x pro forma EV to ~$100M of FY2027 non-GAAP operating profit gives $1.30B of EV; adding $140M of net cash and dividing by ~106M shares after continued buybacks yields approximately $13.60. Rounded to $14.00, that is 20x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $0.70 — a modest discount to the peer median and a reasonable multiple for a company with high customer concentration and flat multi-year revenue.

Probability-weighting 45% base, 20% bull and 35% bear produces an expected return of roughly +9%, consistent with fair compensation for the risk rather than an asymmetric opportunity. That is the arithmetic behind HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint three days out.

**Targets from $11.84:** base $14.00 (+18.2%); bull $18.00 (+52.0%); bear $8.50 (−28.2%). Consensus mean $15.29 and median $15.00 sit between base and bull; the $8.00 low target aligns with the bear case.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Q2 (August 12) delivers revenue at or above $120M *with* gross margin at or above 52.0%, and management raises or reaffirms the FY range without widening the margin band — this would falsify the H2 compression read that is the core of the bear case.
2. Bookings return above 1.0x book-to-bill for two consecutive quarters, confirming the backlog is being replenished rather than drawn down.
3. Management commits the $145M to buyback and/or debt paydown rather than acquisition, and the shares trade at or below $10.50 (roughly 17x guided non-GAAP EPS, near management's own Q1 average repurchase price of $10.19).

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. Q2 or Q3 gross margin prints below 50%, or FY2026 gross-margin guidance is cut, confirming that component cost inflation plus services mix is structural rather than transitional.
2. The top customer's revenue contribution declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters, or the company discloses a renewed deployment pause.
3. Harmonic announces an acquisition above roughly $150M or above 3x revenue, particularly outside broadband access.
4. The Plexus manufacturing agreement (auto-renewed only to October 2026) is not extended, or a supply interruption is disclosed.

**Reassessment date:** August 12, 2026 (Q2 FY2026 results and the first pure-play quarter), with a secondary checkpoint at the October 2026 Plexus renewal window.

## Conclusion

Harmonic has completed a real simplification: a cleaner single-segment business, near-zero net debt moving to roughly $140M of net cash, a $582.1M backlog and deferred revenue balance that covers most of the guided year, and a demonstrated willingness to shrink the share count by 7.1% in 15 months at prices below where the stock trades today. At 19x guided non-GAAP and 2.4x pro forma EV/sales, none of that is priced aggressively.

What holds the rating at HOLD is that the FY2026 growth headline restores FY2024 revenue rather than extending it, management's own guide implies roughly 340bp of second-half gross-margin decline, the Q1 10-Q newly flagged the exact cost input (AI-driven component pricing) that would make that compression worse, one customer remains over half of revenue, and $140M of cash has no announced destination in the hands of a company whose last major acquisition was written off. Options pricing a ±21% move into an August 12 print that resolves the central margin question is a reasonable summary of the state of knowledge. The scores move from an overall 6.0 to 5.9 — financial health improves on the closed divestiture, growth prospects step down on the round-trip framing and sub-1.0 book-to-bill, valuation improves on a 21.5% lower price, and risk deteriorates on the imminent binary and the component-cost channel. Existing holders should stay; new capital has a clearly dated reason to wait three days, and a specific price ($10.50 or below, with margins holding) at which the arithmetic becomes compelling.
