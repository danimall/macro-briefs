## Executive Summary

Core Laboratories is a reservoir-measurement and completion-diagnostics business whose most stable profit pool has been disabled by a mechanism most oilfield-services screens do not capture. Roughly 65% of revenue sits in Reservoir Description, and a meaningful portion of that segment is levered not to drilling but to the physical movement of crude cargoes: buyers and sellers pay Core Labs to certify cargo quality at points of maritime transfer in Kuwait City, Dammam, Doha, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Following the February 28, 2026 escalation between the United States/Israel and Iran, global cargo movement of crude and derived products fell 15-20% year over year in Q2 2026, with a substantial portion of the roughly 20% of global crude production that transits Hormuz stranded. Reservoir Description's operating margin went from 14% in Q2 2025 to 5% in Q2 2026, and 3% for the half. Management chose to retain the regional cost base intact, which produced a year-over-year decremental margin above 100% in the segment — costs rose in absolute dollars while revenue fell.

Two accounting facts compound the operating picture. First, FY2025 results included roughly $8.30M of Aberdeen fire insurance recoveries (FY2024: $8.4M), with the final settlement reached in September 2025. Ex-recoveries, FY2025 operating income was approximately $48.2M (9.2% margin) versus roughly $50.2M (9.6%) in FY2024, so the underlying two-year trend was margin contraction on flat revenue, and 2026 mechanically loses about $0.13 of EPS. Second, H1 2026 book tax expense was $0.256M on $5.301M of pre-tax income — a 4.8% rate — while cash taxes paid rose to $6.546M from $4.874M. At a normalized 25% rate, H1 EPS would have been roughly $0.07-0.08 rather than the reported $0.11. Q3 guidance assumes 25%, so this tailwind reverses.

The bull case rests on convexity: because the Middle East cost base is already absorbed, returning assay volumes should carry very high incremental margins. That case is genuine, and the sequential trend supports a trough — consolidated operating income moved from $1.887M in Q1 to $9.151M in Q2 to a guided $10.5-15.0M in Q3, with Production Enhancement margin recovering from 2% to 11%. But the equity is not priced as a distressed option on that recovery. At $11.25 the shares trade at 26x FY2026 consensus EPS of $0.43, roughly 11-12x forward EBITDA, 1.85x book value, and a 2.9% trailing free cash flow yield, while consensus current-year EPS has been cut 38.1% in 90 days and next-year EPS 22.0%. Gulf transit conditions have not verifiably improved since guidance was issued on July 29. We upgrade from four consecutive AVOID ratings to REDUCE — the trough evidence, roughly 48% of covenant headroom and the CEO's July 31 open-market purchase are real — but the expected return from this price is modestly negative with asymmetric downside.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1936 and redomesticated from a Netherlands/Curaçao structure to Delaware in 2023, Core Labs operates from more than 70 offices in over 50 countries with roughly 3,300 employees. Management's stated primary financial objective is return on invested capital rather than growth, and the model is deliberately asset-light.

**Reservoir Description** (H1 2026 revenue $160.7M, 65.2% of total) performs core analysis, fluid phase behavior work, and crude-oil and derived-product assay testing, and sells the laboratory instruments used for that work. Roughly 80% of segment revenue originates outside the United States. Its technical backbone is Advanced Technology Centers in Abu Dhabi, Australia, Aberdeen and Houston, plus the Gulf assay laboratories. Proprietary assets include the Advanced Digital Imaging System, Dual Energy CT scanning and the RAPID reservoir-data database.

**Production Enhancement** (H1 2026 revenue $85.7M) sells perforating systems and energetics, tubing-conveyed perforating packages, and stimulation diagnostics including SpectraStim proppant tracing and SpectraScan spectral gamma logging. Demand skews to North American unconventional completions with a growing international component.

The revenue triggers therefore run on three separate cycles: new-well core and fluid analysis tied to exploration and appraisal drilling; assay work tied to physical cargo movement; and completion products tied to North American activity. In a normal year that is diversification. In H1 2026, the first two were impaired simultaneously — exploration by a 20-year low in international offshore success rates, and assay by Hormuz — while only the third improved.

### Segment margins have inverted

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reservoir Description | 14% | 1% | 5% | 9% | 3% |

Reservoir Description was historically the higher-margin, internationally diversified anchor and Production Enhancement the volatile North American piece. That relationship has reversed. On the Q2 year-over-year comparison, Reservoir Description revenue fell $7.533M ($86.280M to $78.747M) while segment operating income fell $8.525M ($12.203M to $3.678M) — a decremental margin above 100%, or roughly 80% after adjusting the prior-year base for approximately $2.5M of embedded insurance recovery. That is the arithmetic signature of holding fixed cost in place through a demand air pocket.

The mix-shift point predates Hormuz and matters for the recovery multiple: Reservoir Description margin had already fallen from 14.9% in FY2024 to 12.6% in FY2025, which management attributed to a shift toward lower-margin assay services and instrument sales. Whether that shift is cyclical or structural is the central unresolved question in this business, and the filings do not settle it.

One caution on reading the segment mix: the improving Production Enhancement share of profit is a denominator effect from Reservoir Description's collapse, not demonstrated diversification. Segment revenue mix barely moved, and no customer-concentration disclosure was available to test whether the customer base broadened.

The facility is secured by first-priority liens on substantially all tangible and intangible personal property and subsidiary equity, and carries springing maturities — October 14, 2027 if the $15M 2021 Series B notes remain outstanding, March 30, 2028 if the $25M 2023 Series A notes remain outstanding — unless liquidity covers the respective principal. Covenant headroom is the reassuring part of the file. At June 30 interest coverage was 6.53x against a 3.00x minimum and leverage 1.30x against a 2.50x maximum. If leverage is net debt over TTM Adjusted EBITDA, 1.30x on $93.64M implies roughly $72.0M of Adjusted EBITDA, and the 2.50x test on constant net debt would tolerate a decline to about $37.5M — roughly 48%. Available revolver capacity was $85.6M after $12.4M of letters of credit. Solvency is not the near-term issue; earning power is.

Two screening caveats. A vendor debt-to-equity reading of 2.2x cannot be reconciled to the filed balance sheet — total liabilities over equity is 1.10x and gross debt over equity 0.42x — and should be disregarded. A Piotroski output of "5" reflected five of five computable single-filing signals, not five of nine, and should not be read as a mid-range F-Score. The Altman Z-Score of 2.35 was calculated on FY2025 inputs; with TTM EBIT now around $47.8M and the share price down from $16.03 at year-end, the current reading is directionally lower, though we could not reproduce the vendor's formula variant.

## Growth Analysis

Trailing revenue is $519.2M and declining. Consensus has moved sharply against the company: current-quarter EPS $0.15 (−13.9% in 30 days, −22.5% in 90), next quarter $0.18 (−11.9%/−13.9%), current year $0.43 (−20.4%/−38.1%), next year $0.68 (−21.1%/−22.0%). Coverage is thin at two contributing analysts, and the revisions are largely catching up to disclosed reality — the current-quarter $0.15 sits inside management's $0.12-0.20 guide — but the direction and magnitude are unambiguous.

Q3 2026 guidance is the most useful forward anchor: revenue $128.5-135.5M, operating income $10.5-15.0M (roughly 10% at the midpoint), EPS $0.12-0.20, excluding FX and assuming a 25% tax rate. Because Q2's realized rate was about 8%, the guided sequential EPS improvement is more substantive than it looks. Management attributes support to the South Atlantic Margin (Guyana, Suriname, Namibia), Asia-Pacific, and modest improvement in US land completions, and expects to outperform US land completion activity through diagnostics and energetics adoption. Middle East recovery timing is described as difficult to predict; guidance does not appear to depend on it.

The credible medium-term growth items are specific and modest: UAE regulatory approval to deploy SpectraStim and SpectraScan in a country where the company already has infrastructure; conventional core analysis on a Namibian offshore exploration well; a reservoir characterization program supporting Murphy Oil's Côte d'Ivoire discovery; an Australian onshore gas study loaded into RAPID. Frontier conventional exploration is the highest-intensity use case for core analysis, and Core Labs is positioned in that flow through its Aberdeen and Houston centers.

Against that sit two structural drags independent of Hormuz. The FY2025 10-K states that the success rate for international offshore exploration and appraisal wells is at a 20-year low, driving project cancellations. Core Labs' highest-value work is triggered by successful new wells generating follow-on appraisal and development programs, so a lower hit rate shrinks the funnel regardless of rig count or oil price — which helps explain flat revenue since 2023 despite a supportive commodity backdrop. Separately, one disclosed Q2 win warrants a careful reading: the company supplied a complete tubing-conveyed perforating package and training to an Asia-Pacific national oil company to build that NOC's in-house perforating capability. That is one-time product and training revenue against a potential reduction in the recurring service opportunity. The alternative reading — a deeper strategic relationship pulling through consumables and upgrades — is plausible; we flag it as interpretation, not a filing conclusion.

Growth prospects score 3.2. The trough evidence and UAE approval are real positives; declining revenue, a 38% current-year estimate cut and a structurally smaller exploration funnel dominate.

## Competitive Landscape

The genuine moat is the accumulated proprietary dataset (RAPID) plus a small number of high-capital-intensity technology centers that are hard to replicate. The same concentration is a liability: fixed costs sit in specific geographies that cannot be flexed when one region goes offline, which is precisely what H1 2026 demonstrated.

The more consequential competitive dynamics are two forms of disintermediation rather than traditional rivalry. The first is customer insourcing, exemplified by the NOC that bought a perforating package and training to build internal capability. The second is computational substitution: the FY2025 10-K explicitly names generative AI enabling enhanced computer modeling of reservoir rock and fluid properties as something that "could displace some of our current products or services." It is unusual for a physical-measurement business to name simulation as a displacement threat to its core service rather than as an efficiency tool. The counter-argument — that models require physical calibration data, which makes RAPID more valuable — is at least as plausible, and no financial evidence of AI-driven displacement appears in the filings. We treat this as management signaling genuine long-term uncertainty, weighted alongside the disclosed Reservoir Description mix shift toward lower-margin assay and instrument work, which is the kind of shift a substitution thesis would predict.

Industry signals are negative near term and constructive medium term: the IEA, EIA and OPEC have all cut 2026 global crude demand forecasts while projecting a rebound in demand growth in 2027. Core Labs' entire strategic posture — retaining Middle East capability and cost through the disruption — is a bet on that 2027 thesis.

Competitive position scores 4.2. The asset base is differentiated but the trajectory is negative, and the pre-Hormuz margin decline in the flagship segment suggests part of the erosion is not exogenous.

## Risk Assessment

**Hormuz duration is the dominant variable and has no company-provided timeline.** The FY2025 10-K added a dedicated risk factor naming the Abu Dhabi technology center and the Doha, Dammam and Kuwait City laboratories specifically. Management states in the guidance section that the timing of Middle East recovery is difficult to predict. Higher crude prices do not help Core Labs' assay franchise — cargo movement does. Any assessment of a Q4 recovery should require evidence of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit rather than headline de-escalation. [Note: whether Gulf conditions have changed since the July 29 guidance is not established in the provided sources; the report should not assert a post-guidance directional change either way.]

**Impairment is a live FY2026 audit issue.** Management performed long-lived-asset impairment assessments for both the Middle East and Russia/Ukraine and identified no triggering events as of June 30, 2026 — a judgment that implies undiscounted future cash flows still support carrying values, which is only consistent with a normalization assumption. Against $105.8M of goodwill and $70.6M of net deferred tax assets (63% of book equity), a slipped recovery makes non-cash write-downs a genuine Q4/FY2026 possibility. Note that the FY2025 deferred-tax valuation allowance already rose from $8.8M to $15.8M.

**Refinancing and structure.** The January 2026 shift to secured floating-rate bank debt with springing maturities means Core Labs must access refinancing markets in 2027-2028 while EBITDA is falling. Covenant headroom of roughly 48% and $85.6M of undrawn capacity mean this is a 2027 problem, not a 2026 one.

**Capital return coverage is thinner than it looks.** Dividend of roughly $1.84M annually plus H1-annualized buybacks totals approximately $8.5M against TTM FCF of $15.1M. There is no publicly announced repurchase program, so buybacks are discretionary and can stop without announcement, and the FY2025 risk factors explicitly name the quarterly dividend and senior executive base salaries as cost-reduction candidates.

**Contained and disclosed.** Russia represented $26.2M, or 5.0% of FY2025 revenue, with $16.8M of assets and roughly $2.5M of estimated exit costs — quantified and small. The 2023 redomestication's intended Section 368(a) tax treatment is described as "not free from doubt," a low-probability tail.

**Verification gaps that should not be scored as resolved.** The FY2025 10-K discloses "Prior Period Immaterial Corrections" adjusting beginning retained earnings and total assets, with the December 31, 2024 current ratio revised from 2.32 to 2.16, with no impact on net income or cash flows; the underlying line-item detail and root cause were not obtainable. Mitigating: no Form 8-K Item 4.02 non-reliance filing was made, and disclosure controls were concluded effective at June 30, 2026. A March 5, 2026 Item 5.03 filing amending articles and bylaws was not reviewed. No customer-concentration disclosure was obtained, so we make no claim about single-customer exposure.

### Options Market Signal

At-the-money implied volatility on CLB moved from 68.8% on July 20 to 159.6% on August 7 while the price was essentially unchanged ($11.35 to $11.30 on the quote series). Some of that jump reflects the thin options book on a $516M-cap name and post-earnings quote behavior, so the level should not be read literally, but the direction is consistent with the market pricing extreme uncertainty around the next several quarters rather than a directional view. Put/call open interest moved from 0.14 to 0.21 — still call-tilted, which sits oddly beside 21% short interest and 3.1 days to cover. The most defensible reading is that some short exposure is being paired with upside protection while speculative recovery bets accumulate. We do not treat this positioning as independent confirmation of either case. For a REDUCE, the practical implication is that expensive volatility and squeeze mechanics both argue for trimming stock rather than expressing the view through short exposure.

Risk factors score 3.5: the binary Hormuz outcome, the impairment question against a book equity that is 63% intangible and tax assets, and the springing-maturity structure are material and not obviously priced at 26x current-year earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$14.50, +29%)

Q1 2026 was the trough. Operating income has moved $1.887M to $9.151M to a guided $10.5-15.0M, and the Q3 guide assumes a 25% tax rate against Q2's realized 8%, so the guided EPS improvement understates the underlying operating recovery. Production Enhancement margin at 11% is the best in years, and the segment is now earning more than the historically dominant Reservoir Description. Because management retained the Middle East cost base, returning assay volumes carry very high incremental margins — the same operating leverage that destroyed H1 works in reverse. If Reservoir Description normalizes to a 14% margin, EPS approaches $0.89, and at 16-17x that supports $14-15. South Atlantic Margin exploration (Guyana, Suriname, Namibia) is the industry's most active conventional theatre and Core Labs is winning work there; UAE approval for SpectraStim and SpectraScan expands the addressable market where infrastructure already exists. Covenant headroom of roughly 48% and $85.6M of undrawn capacity remove any near-term solvency question, and the CEO's purchase of 5,000 shares at $10.78 on July 31 — two days after a weak print, the only open-market purchase in the transaction record with no offsetting open-market sales — is directionally consistent with an internal view that the shares are undervalued.

### Bear Case (~$7.75, −31%)

The recovery is not in the company's control, and management explicitly declines to forecast the timing of Middle East normalization. If disruption persists into 2027, Core Labs will have absorbed multiple additional quarters of negative operating leverage, will face pressure to restructure the regional footprint, and will confront a live impairment question against $105.8M of goodwill and $70.6M of deferred tax assets. The persistence case produces roughly $0.37 of EPS — 30x at today's price — with leverage rising to about 1.84x and free cash flow near zero. Separately, three headwinds are Hormuz-independent: roughly $8.3M of annual insurance recoveries expired in September 2025 (about $0.13 of EPS); the 4.8% H1 tax rate reverses to a guided 25%; and international offshore exploration success rates at a 20-year low shrink the funnel for the highest-value core work. Reservoir Description's margin was already falling before the conflict (14.9% to 12.6%) on a mix shift toward lower-margin assay and instrument sales, which is what a structural erosion thesis — customer insourcing plus computational substitution, both named in the 10-K — would look like in its early stages. Five-year total shareholder return of $61.22 against a company-selected peer index of $180.69, a combined Chairman/CEO/President role, and CEO compensation equal to roughly 0.96% of market capitalization argue against paying a premium multiple for management's judgment on when to normalize costs.

### Where we land

Base case is roughly flat. FY2027 EPS of $0.60 — below consensus $0.68, reflecting the revision trend and unresolved transit conditions — at 19x gives $11.40; a cross-check at 10x on approximately $56M of FY2027 EBITDA gives an EV of $560M, or about $10.16 per share after net debt. Averaging the approaches puts base value near $11.00. Probability-weighting 35% bear / 40% base / 25% bull produces an expected return of roughly −4%, with 31% downside against 29% upside. That is a REDUCE profile: no margin of safety, negative estimate momentum, and a catalyst the company does not control.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into strength rather than sell indiscriminately. The stock has traded between $9.87 and $11.29 since the July 29 print; use rallies toward $12.00-12.50 — roughly where management itself repurchased in Q2 at a $12.53 average — to reduce exposure meaningfully. Retain no more than a starter position pending evidence on Gulf transit. Do not express this view as a short: 21% short interest with 3.1 days to cover, at-the-money implied volatility near 160%, and a guided sequential recovery make the borrow-and-squeeze mechanics unfavorable relative to a 31% downside case.

**Targets from the $11.25 basis.** Base $11.00 (−2%); bull $14.50 (+29%); bear $7.75 (−31%), approximately 1.3x book value of $6.08 with a dividend cut and restructuring charges assumed.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) Verified, sustained resumption of commercial crude cargo movement through the Strait of Hormuz, evidenced in Reservoir Description revenue recovering above $85M in a quarter with segment operating margin back above 10%. (2) Two consecutive quarters in which consolidated operating income lands at or above the top of guidance with no offsetting non-recurring benefit, alongside a stabilization or upturn in FY2027 consensus EPS from $0.68. (3) Free cash flow returning above $10M in a single quarter, restoring coverage of the roughly $8.5M annualized capital-return run rate.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Any goodwill or long-lived-asset impairment charge, or the disclosure of a triggering event in the Middle East or Russia/Ukraine regions at Q3 or FY2026 reporting. (2) A dividend suspension or announced regional restructuring, which the FY2025 risk factors already contemplate. (3) Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 operating income below the Q2 2026 level of $9.151M, indicating the sequential trough thesis has failed. (4) Leverage above 2.00x, which would put the 2.50x covenant and the 2027-2028 springing-maturity mechanics into play simultaneously.

**Reassessment timeline.** Late October 2026 (Q3 report and Q4 guidance) is the next decision point, with the Gulf transit situation monitored continuously. FY2026 results in early February 2027 carry the impairment and audit-judgment risk and are the more consequential checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Core Labs is a differentiated measurement franchise going through a shock that its own cost decisions have amplified. The operating trough appears to be behind it — $1.887M of operating income in Q1, $9.151M in Q2, $10.5-15.0M guided for Q3 at a normalized tax rate — and the balance sheet has enough covenant headroom (roughly 48% of Adjusted EBITDA) and undrawn capacity ($85.6M) that solvency is not the question. That evidence, plus the CEO's first open-market purchase in the record, is why this moves off four consecutive AVOID ratings.

It does not clear the bar for owning. At $11.25 the shares carry 26x FY2026 consensus EPS of $0.43, roughly 11-12x forward EBITDA — no de-rating from the multiple it commanded at $16.03 in December — 1.85x book value and a 2.9% trailing free cash flow yield, on estimates that have been cut 38% for this year and 22% for next in 90 days.
