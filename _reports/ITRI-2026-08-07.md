## Executive Summary

Itron is three years into a margin transformation that is real and well documented in the filings, and roughly eighteen months into a revenue contraction that the transformation has masked. From FY2022 to FY2025, revenue grew 31.8% to $2,367.2M while GAAP operating margin went from −0.4% to 13.2% and operating cash flow reached $406.0M. But FY2025 revenue fell 3%, 1H26 fell 5.3%, and Q2 2026 fell 7.2%, with the entire decline located in North America (1H26 US/Canada revenue −8.5%) and concentrated in Networked Solutions, which dropped 17% in Q2 to $339.2M. Earnings growth has come from mix and efficiency, not volume.

The July 28 print was the fifth consecutive quarter of the same pattern: revenue at or below consensus, a margin-driven non-GAAP EPS beat ($1.59 vs $1.31 estimated, +21.1%), and a raised full-year guide. The stock rose ~22% on the day and sits at $102.83, up 19.5% from $85.99 at our prior review. The market re-rated on management's "structurally better earnings power" framing, and it was probably helped along by 20% short interest and a collapse in bearish options positioning (put/call open interest ratio fell from 2.24 to 0.36 across the event).

Read carefully, the FY2026 guide is more informative than the raise headline. Revenue of $2.37–2.41B against FY2025's $2,367.2M is flat to +1.8%. Non-GAAP EPS of $6.30–6.50 is 8.8–11.6% *below* FY2025's $7.13. The EPS decline is entirely non-operating — the effective tax rate moved from 11.4% in FY2025 to 27.4% in 1H26, and interest income is halving as cash funded $877M of acquisitions — while non-GAAP operating income rose 6.8% and adjusted EBITDA rose 6.2% in 1H26. That distinction matters, but so does its corollary: FY2025's $7.13 was flattered by a tax artifact (valuation-allowance dynamics plus one-time OBBBA elections) and never represented normalized earnings power. Anchoring on $7.13 overstates the base; anchoring on $6.40 at a ~27% rate is closer to correct. Meanwhile the guide requires implied 2H26 revenue of $1,240.1M (+7.5%) against 1H26's −5.3%, with an implied Q4 of roughly $645M — up 12.8% YoY and 8.4% sequentially. Twelve-month backlog of $1,680M (+8.8% YoY) is genuine support for that. Book-to-bill of 0.89 for six consecutive quarters is genuine evidence against it.

## Company Overview

Itron sells grid-edge and industrial-IoT infrastructure to utilities and municipalities: communicating meters and endpoints, network infrastructure and headend software, distributed-energy management, distribution automation, and increasingly, software and managed services layered on the installed base. Headcount was ~5,550 at December 31, 2025. R&D was $207M in FY2025, 8.7% of revenue.

Four reportable segments as of Q4 2025, when Resiliency Solutions was created to house two acquisitions:

| Segment | Q2'26 Revenue | % of total | Adj. gross margin | Adj. op. margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Networked Solutions | $339.2M | 60.3% | 42.8% | 33.0% |
| Device Solutions | $111.4M | 19.8% | 34.8% | 28.3% |
| Outcomes | $96.4M | 17.1% | 38.8% | 21.3% |
| Resiliency Solutions | $15.8M | 2.8% | 75.3% | 27.7% |

Networked Solutions is both the franchise and the problem — 60% of revenue, the largest profit pool ($112.1M adjusted operating income in Q2 alone), and the source of the entire consolidated revenue decline. Customer concentration is unusually low for a company selling into a concentrated buyer base: no customer above 10% of revenue in FY2025 or 1H26, and top-10 concentration improving from 36% (2023) to 32% (2025). Itron pays no dividend and returns capital exclusively through buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**The margin story is verified and substantial.** Q2'26 GAAP gross margin was 41.0% versus 36.9% a year earlier; adjusted gross margin of 41.4% was a company record, up 460bps. Every segment expanded in 1H26: Device Solutions 35.1% from 29.9% (+520bps), Networked Solutions 41.8% from 37.7% (+410bps), Outcomes 40.2% from 38.8% (+140bps). Management attributes this to customer/product mix and operational efficiency.

Quality-of-earnings check on that margin: Q2 warranty expense swung to negative $(290)K from $1,812K, a $2.1M favorable move worth roughly 37bps on $562.9M of revenue — about 8% of the 460bps expansion. Stock-based compensation fell to $13.5M from $18.7M, a genuine tailwind that flows through non-GAAP because Itron does not add SBC back (more conservative than typical software-peer practice). A $3.249M equity-method gain ran through GAAP and was correctly excluded from non-GAAP. The great majority of the margin gain is mix and efficiency, not accrual release.

**Operating leverage is running against volume, and volume is winning at the EPS line.** Q2 GAAP operating income was $76.1M, flat on 7.2% less revenue. Non-GAAP operating income rose 8.3% to $89.1M and adjusted EBITDA rose 7.8% to $96.8M. GAAP diluted EPS fell 19.0% to $1.19; non-GAAP fell 1.9% to $1.59. The gap between the operating improvement and the EPS decline is tax and interest income, as described above.

**Cash generation is the strongest part of the story.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $406.0M against $301.1M of net income is 1.35x conversion; FCF of $383.1M on $2,367.2M of revenue is a 16.2% FCF margin, enabled by capex of just $22.9M (1.0% of revenue). In 1H26, OCF rose 2.8% to $173.6M while net income fell 20% — conversion of 1.62x. The drivers are visible: unearned revenue +$50.9M and receivables +$23.3M, partly offset by inventory and wage outflows. The unearned-revenue build is a legitimate signal of the software/maintenance mix shift, but it is a working-capital source, not a substitute for revenue growth, and cannot compound indefinitely. FY2025's $406.0M also benefited from one-time OBBBA R&D deduction elections and should not be treated as a clean run rate. A defensible normalized FCF range is $300–340M, or 6.7–7.6% of market cap.

**The balance sheet was materially de-risked in Q1 2026, and this deserves credit.** At 6/30/26: cash $745.2M, current portion of debt $0, current ratio 2.75x (from 1.80x), working capital $985.3M. Debt is $805M of 1.375% notes due 2030 (conversion price $131.24) and $805M of **zero-coupon** notes due 2032 (conversion price $123.77). The $460M 2021 notes were repaid in cash on March 16, 2026 with no equity issued. The $750M revolver is undrawn with $706.9M available to September 2030. Cash interest paid in 1H26 was $6.3M on $1.61B of debt — a blended cash coupon of roughly 0.7%. Net debt is $864.8M, or 2.31x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA. There are no maturities before 2030.

Two offsets. Tangible book value is negative $340.7M: equity of $1,616.2M less goodwill of $1,690.8M and intangibles of $266.1M. Goodwill is 42.3% of total assets and 104.6% of shareholders' equity. And $1.61B of convertible principal must be settled in cash under both indentures, a long-dated but real claim on liquidity; the revolver maturity springs forward to April 2030 if the converts are not settled or extended. Separately, surety bonds in force rose 26% to $658.1M in six months — plausibly a forward-revenue positive (more contracts entering the performance phase, consistent with rising 12-month backlog) or tougher customer guarantee demands. The filings do not distinguish.

The grey-zone Altman Z-Score (2.34) computed on FY2025 data reflected the $460M then-current notes and systematically penalizes goodwill-heavy asset-light balance sheets. With zero current debt at 6/30/26, near-zero cash interest, and $1.45B of liquidity, I do not read it as a solvency signal. It correctly flags that the equity value rests on intangibles.

## Growth Analysis

**The bookings picture is the crux, and it is genuinely two-sided.**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bookings | $2,155M | $2,698M | $2,101M |
| Total backlog | $4,511M | $4,734M | $4,501M |
| 12-month backlog | $2,032M | $1,767M | $1,632M |

FY2025 book-to-bill was 0.89. 1H26 bookings of $1,026M against $1,149.9M of revenue is 0.89 again. Total backlog fell $104M YoY to $4,410M. Twelve-month backlog, however, inflected: $1,680M at Q2'26 versus $1,544M at Q2'25 (+8.8%) and $1,632M at year-end 2025. These two series answer different questions — total backlog speaks to the long-tail pipeline, 12-month backlog to the next four quarters — and should not be aggregated into a single directional claim. The 12-month figure is the concrete evidence underwriting the guided 2H26 acceleration.

A necessary definitional caution: "total backlog" is a company-defined operating measure. The audited ASC 606 remaining-performance-obligation figure is approximately $0.9B for the next 12 months and $0.9B beyond — about $1.8B, or 41% of stated backlog. The 10-Q explains the two "will not agree" because backlog includes contracts with termination-for-convenience clauses, and warns that RPO itself is not a complete measure of future revenue. Neither number is a firm contractual obligation. Itron does note it has no history of significant cancellations.

**Mix is shifting toward software, and this is the most durable part of the growth case.** Service revenue reached 19.0% of 1H26 revenue from 14.3% a year earlier, growing 25.9%. Outcomes grew 13% in Q2 and 18% in 1H. Reported ARR reached $417M from $343M (+21.6%). But that ARR growth is substantially acquisition-funded: Resiliency Solutions exists only because of Urbint and Locusview and annualizes to roughly $63M of revenue, of which the ARR-qualifying portion (excluding professional services) plausibly falls in a $40–55M range. That implies organic ARR growth of roughly $19–34M, or +5.5% to +9.9%. A reader treating 21.6% as an organic software growth rate would materially overvalue the transition. Itron does not disclose the organic/acquired split, and it changed this headline metric (from endpoints under management) precisely as the acquisitions closed, which reduces the ability to verify the trend against prior disclosure.

**The guide requires a sharp Q4.** Q1'26 revenue was $587.0M, Q2 $562.9M, Q3 guided to $590–600M. The FY midpoint of $2,390M implies Q4 revenue of roughly $645M — +12.8% YoY off a Q4'25 that was itself down 6.7%, and +8.4% sequentially. Implied 2H26 revenue is $1,240.1M, +7.5% against 1H26's −5.3%. Implied 2H26 non-GAAP EPS is $3.33 versus $3.99 a year ago, and implied Q4 EPS is roughly $1.77. This is the single largest execution variable in the story.

**Estimate revisions have turned positive across all four horizons for the first time in several quarters** — current quarter +2.0%, next quarter +6.5%, current year +6.7%, next year +3.6% over 30 days, with 11–12 contributors. Combined with four consecutive beats of increasing magnitude (2.5%, 10.2%, 17.0%, 21.1%), this raises confidence that the FY2026 guide is achievable and possibly beatable. It falls well short of the +30%/90-day revision velocity that signals a fundamental inflection; this is a modest positive-momentum read on a low-growth base.

**Is there a specific reason the market is wrong at $102.83?** The strongest candidate is that screens ranking on EPS growth will mis-rank Itron in FY2026, because the guided 10% EPS decline is entirely tax and interest income while operating earnings are improving. That is a real mechanism. But it is substantially offset: FY2025's 11.4% effective tax rate was itself abnormal, so $7.13 was never sustainable earnings power, and the loss of interest income is a permanent economic cost of spending $877M of cash — not an accounting artifact. Most of the "mispricing" was corrected by the 19.5% move since our prior review.

The second candidate is the 8.5% trailing FCF yield. On normalized FCF of $300–340M the yield is 6.7–7.6%, which is respectable for a business with 41% gross margins and 1% capex intensity, but not compelling absent a dividend and with buybacks now executing above the $89.81 blended price Itron paid in 1H26.

Analyst targets: mean $129.6, median $130.0, range $115–150. Notably, the low target sits above spot, which normally reads as consensus support — but the trend is negative. ## Competitive Landscape

Itron names LM Ericsson, Landis+Gyr, Advanced Energy Industries and Xylem as primary competitors. The durable advantage is the installed AMI network and the switching costs around it: a utility deploying Itron infrastructure commits to multi-year, regulatory-approved capital programs and is difficult to displace mid-cycle.

The specific competitive threat is stated in Itron's own risk disclosure with unusual precision: some utilities may purchase meters separately from the connectivity technology, with interchangeability specifications driving meter commoditization and unfavorable price/margin effects. If the meter unbundles from the network, the end-to-end bundling that produces Networked Solutions' 42.8% gross margin erodes. Itron's strategic response is visible and coherent — shift revenue and margin away from the meter toward the network and the software on it. Service revenue at 19.0% of revenue, Networked Solutions down to 60.0% of 1H26 revenue from 65.8% of FY2025, and Resiliency's 75.3% gross margin all point that direction.

**The unresolved question is whether Networked Solutions' 17% decline is deployment timing or share loss.** Management's language — "the timing of project deployments and lower volumes" — is consistent with either, and the second clause is easy to overlook. I have no independent AMI market-share data or competitor financials to adjudicate this, and the two explanations carry very different terminal values. One piece of circumstantial evidence favors the timing explanation: 410bps of gross margin expansion within Networked Solutions during a 17% volume decline is not the signature of a vendor losing share on price, which would normally show margin compression. That is an inference, not a finding.

The demand backdrop is supportive. Load growth from data centers and electrification is creating interconnection-queue urgency, pushing utilities to extract capacity and visibility from existing infrastructure rather than build new. Itron's grid-edge sensing, DERMS and distribution-automation products sit directly in that path, and management's "time-to-power" framing is a plausible explanation for 12-month backlog rising while total backlog erodes. Regulated utilities across the sector are guiding to large multi-year capital programs driven by data-center load, and Itron is a credible beneficiary. The constraint is that higher-for-longer rates pressure the financing side of those programs and utility affordability politics can slow approvals — a real timing risk on the demand that underwrites the 2H26 guide.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment in Resiliency Solutions is the clearest binary.** Itron deployed $877.0M of cash — Urbint at $330.6M (November 2025) and Locusview at $546.4M (January 2026) — into a segment annualizing roughly $63.3M of revenue, or about 13.9x revenue. Segment goodwill is $611.4M (36% of consolidated goodwill) plus most of the $266.1M intangible balance, against ~$17.5M of annualized segment operating income. The 10-K's impairment risk factor expressly names "changes in our organization or management reporting structure, which could result in additional reporting units" as a trigger, and the FY2025 conclusion that no reporting unit was "at risk" predates the Locusview close. Locusview's purchase-price allocation remains preliminary. Mitigating: 75.3% gross margin and 27.7% operating margin are unusual for recently acquired software and indicate the assets are not cash-burning, and ~14x revenue is not out of line for high-margin vertical software with defensible utility relationships. Two quarters is not a track record.

**Q4 execution.** The implied +12.8% YoY Q4 revenue step-up is the largest single variable. A miss of $40–50M in Q4 revenue would likely take FY2026 EPS below the guided range and reset the multiple.

**Tax-rate volatility.** The effective rate moved from 11.4% (FY2025) to 27.4% (1H26) to 33% in Q2 standalone, driven by an intra-entity asset transfer, valuation allowances against a $271.5M deferred-tax-asset balance, earnings mix, and non-deductible executive compensation. There are $69.0M of unrecognized tax benefits outstanding. Forward EPS is more sensitive to the tax line than to operating performance in the near term.

**Contractual and supply-chain exposure.** Itron relies on limited or sole sources for most circuit boards, and discloses that certain backlog contracts include previously committed pricing that may not be recoverable through pricing actions. With $4.4B of stated backlog, committed-price contracts are a live tariff and input-cost exposure. Contracts carry liquidated damages, supported by $658.1M of surety bonds and $72.0M of letters of credit; a loss-making contract component requires immediate revenue reduction in the period identified.

**Spectrum dependence — genuinely idiosyncratic and not captured by any standard screen.** A significant portion of US products are unlicensed FCC Part 15 devices that must accept interference from licensed users. If unlicensed bands become crowded, restrictive, or subject to changed rules, or if the FCC requires product modifications, the business could be materially affected.

**Contingent dilution and convertible mechanics.** Maximum contingent share obligation is 12.6M shares (6.1M + 6.5M), equal to 28.8% of shares outstanding or 22.3% post-issuance ownership dilution. Both conversion prices ($123.77 and $131.24) require +20.4% and +27.7% moves from spot, neither convertible contributed shares to Q2 diluted EPS, and reported diluted shares of 44,608K are the correct current capitalization. Three mechanisms sit between the maximum and shareholders: principal must be cash-settled under both indentures; capped calls cover identical share counts at identical strikes to caps of $190.42 and $205.86; and the legacy 2021 warrants (3.7M shares at $180.00) fully expire in October 2026. One asymmetry: capped calls settle with bank counterparties, not noteholders, so GAAP diluted EPS would reflect conversion dilution without the offsetting benefit, and Itron bears uncollateralized counterparty credit risk on those calls.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** Three 8-Ks in a two-week window carry Item 8.01 (Other Events): April 24, 2026, a May 1 8-K/A, and May 8 (paired with the annual-meeting vote). Their substance could not be verified. Explanations range from routine supplemental proxy disclosure to litigation-related disclosure. This is a verification gap, not evidence of a problem.

**Short interest.** 20.0% of float short with 9.5 days to cover. The 22% post-earnings move likely involved short covering; remaining crowding cuts both ways — it can amplify a further re-rating on a Q3 beat, and it means a Q4 disappointment meets a lighter bid.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.7% sits 14.6 points below trailing one-year realized volatility of 47.4%, at the 9th percentile of the current chain — a standard post-event vol crush, with the nearest expiry pricing only a ±5.2% move. The term structure is in contango, with the six-month tenor at 47.1% implying a ±19.3% move and a negative skew reading at that tenor. The market has stopped pricing near-term risk and is pricing meaningful uncertainty around the FY2026 delivery window, which aligns with the view that Q4 is the crux.

The most informative change is positioning: put/call open interest collapsed from 2.24 to 0.36 alongside the 19.5% price move. The bearish options structure that existed into the print has been unwound. That removes one source of mechanical upside fuel and means the current price reflects a materially less skeptical options market than the last review. Put/call volume at 2.00 against an OI ratio of 0.36 suggests some fresh short-dated hedging without a structural build. Cheap near-term vol makes optionality inexpensive for holders who want to protect the Q3 print; it is not a directional signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$145, +41%)

The margin transformation is structural, not cyclical: 460bps of adjusted gross margin expansion in one year across all four segments, driven by mix and efficiency rather than accrual releases (the warranty swing accounts for only ~8% of the gain). Twelve-month backlog inflected to $1,680M (+8.8%), validating the guided 2H26 acceleration; book-to-bill crosses 1.0 as "time-to-power" urgency converts utility interconnection pressure into grid-edge orders. Four consecutive beats of increasing magnitude (2.5% → 21.1%) and the first positive revision cycle across all four horizons suggest the $6.40 guide is conservative. Tax normalizes near 26%, removing the year-over-year EPS headwind, and FY2027 delivers $7.50+ as revenue returns to growth on a 41%+ gross margin base. Liability management is genuinely excellent — no maturities to 2030, a 0.7% blended cash coupon, $1.45B of liquidity — so nearly all incremental operating income accrues to equity holders, and $297.8M of remaining buyback authorization shrinks the count against roughly $300–340M of normalized FCF. At 19x $7.65, $145.

### Bear Case (~$75, −27%)

Book-to-bill has been 0.89 for six consecutive quarters and total backlog has fallen $104M YoY. Networked Solutions — 60% of revenue and the largest profit pool — declined 17% in Q2 with the cause unresolved; if it is share loss to Landis+Gyr or Ericsson rather than deployment timing, the margin expansion is a harvest, not a franchise improvement. The implied Q4 revenue step-up of +12.8% is a demanding assumption in a business where management itself discloses that sales cycles are "long and unpredictable" and purchases "may be deferred." Growth in ARR is substantially acquired; organic ARR growth is plausibly 5.5–9.9%. The company spent $877M at ~13.9x revenue into a segment with two quarters of history, creating $611.4M of goodwill in a new reporting unit whose "not at risk" assessment predates the larger deal, against negative $340.7M of tangible book. Compensation metrics — adjusted EBITDA, revenue, non-GAAP EPS — all exclude the amortization and deal costs that debt-funded acquisitions generate, an incentive asymmetry (not an allegation of motive) that a scoring analyst should weigh. Five-year TSR is negative while CEO total compensation rose from $3.90M (2022) to $11.21M (2025), and 2025 alone saw ~39 points of relative TSR underperformance. A Q4 shortfall plus a Resiliency impairment takes the stock to 12.5x $6.00, or $75, near the 52-week low of $77.77.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. What changed since the prior review is real but modest: estimate revisions turned positive across all four horizons (previously negative in every window), 12-month backlog inflected up 8.8%, adjusted gross margin set a record at 41.4%, and guidance was raised. What did not change is the stated upgrade trigger — book-to-bill remains 0.89, not above 1.0 — and revenue is still declining. Against that, the price rose 19.5%, from below the prior base-case range to above it. Improved fundamentals and a higher price roughly offset, which is why the rating holds rather than moves.

**Base case: $115** — 17x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.80, assuming 2H26 delivers approximately as guided, a normalized ~26–27% tax rate, and low-single-digit revenue growth resuming in FY2027. **+11.8% from $102.83.**
**Bull case: $145** — 19x $7.65. **+41.0%.**
**Bear case: $75** — 12.5x $6.00. **−27.1%.**
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 print (late October 2026) and the Q4/FY2026 print (mid-February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Book-to-bill above 1.0 for a single quarter, with total backlog stabilizing or rising — this would resolve the medium-term pipeline concern that has driven four consecutive HOLDs.
2. Q3 revenue at or above the $600M high end of guidance, plus reaffirmation of the implied ~$645M Q4, with management providing a Networked Solutions order-book detail that distinguishes deployment timing from share loss.
3. Price weakness toward **$88–92** on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 25%+ upside to a $115 base case and would put the entry below the ~$89.81 blended price at which Itron repurchased 1,694,497 shares in 1H26.
4. Disclosure of organic versus acquired ARR showing organic growth above 10%, which would materially strengthen the software-transition valuation case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 revenue below $580M, or any reduction in the implied Q4 step-up — the 2H26 acceleration is the load-bearing assumption in the guide.
2. Networked Solutions declining more than 10% YoY in Q3, indicating the 1H pattern is not deployment timing.
3. Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge in Resiliency Solutions, or a change in the interim impairment assessment language regarding that reporting unit.
4. Twelve-month backlog rolling back below $1,600M, reversing the single piece of hard evidence supporting the near-term guide.
5. Clustered insider selling by three or more C-suite executives, or a buyback pause, either of which would suggest management does not view current prices as attractive.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release (expected late October 2026). ## Conclusion

Itron has done the hard operational work: adjusted gross margin at a record 41.4%, all four segments expanding, operating margin up more than 13 points over three years, 16% FCF margins on 1% capex intensity, and a balance sheet with no maturities until 2030 and a 0.7% blended cash coupon. Management earned credit for the February 2026 refinancing and for repurchasing shares at $95.21 and $81.07 rather than at the highs.

What it has not done is grow. Revenue fell 3% in FY2025 and 5.3% in 1H26, Networked Solutions fell 17% in Q2 with the cause unresolved, book-to-bill has been 0.89 for six consecutive quarters, and the FY2026 guide implies flat revenue and a 10% EPS decline that requires a Q4 revenue step-up of nearly 13% to hit. The July 28 raise was genuine and the market's 19.5% response was defensible — but it moved the stock from a price where the risk/reward favored patience to one where 16.1x forward earnings and 14.4x EV/EBITDA capture most of the improvement. Base case $115 offers +11.8%; the bear case at $75 costs 27%. Expected return of roughly +7% with unfavorable asymmetry, on a business where the single most consequential question (share loss versus deployment timing) cannot be resolved from available evidence, is a HOLD.

The next four to six months should resolve it. A Q3 print at the high end with 12-month backlog holding above $1,680M and book-to-bill crossing 1.0 would make this a straightforward ACCUMULATE at a materially higher base case. A Q3 miss with Networked Solutions down another double digit, or an impairment in Resiliency, would make it a REDUCE.
